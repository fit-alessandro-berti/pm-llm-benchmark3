## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

The flag is not underlying risk; it is a label produced from `risk_score`, possibly `complexity` and unobserved information, which then triggers a policy bundle: priority triage, near-mandatory manual review, and downstream escalation exposure. Hence “set flag=0” is ambiguous. It could mean (i) hide the label while staff retain the score/judgement, (ii) recalculate the threshold, (iii) disable the routing rules attached to the label, or (iv) change the underlying risk – the last is not a flag intervention at all. **P1** is closest to a display/separable effect; **P2–P4** are separable policy-component interventions on routing/capacity. I therefore avoid natural direct/indirect effects (NDE/NIE) as primary: they require cross-world counterfactuals such as “the manual review this flagged case would have had if unflagged,” plus no exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounding – implausible here because manual review affects escalation, queue state affects later waits, and reviewer behaviour is affected by the flag. Primary estimands are **total policy effects** and **interventional (stochastic) indirect effects / controlled effects under explicitly set routing or capacity regimes**, which only require single-world interventions on policy components. NDE/NIE are reported, if at all, as sensitivity objects.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | P0 vs P2: keep priority triage, remove automatic mandatory manual review for flagged cases | Case, among flagged-eligible arrivals in a defined period; system-level version preferred | Total cycle time, arrivalclose | Total policy effect | Yes, as policy bundle | Interference via shared queues; secular load changes |
| E2 | P1 vs P0: suppress flag display, retain risk_score, complexity and human information | Case/cluster of cases handled by same staff | Triage completion time: arrivaltriage_done, split into wait/service | Total effect of display component; separable effect of label visibility | Yes if display can be hidden without changing score access | Staff may infer flag from score; Hawthorne/behaviour change |
| E3 | P2 vs P0, with manual-review occurrence for flagged cases set by human judgement distribution as in comparable unflagged cases | Flagged cases | Cycle time | Interventional indirect effect via manual-review occurrence: shift mediator distribution, not each case’s cross-world mediator | Partly; stochastic intervention well-defined | Exposure-induced confounding: triage outcome, reviewer, queue state affect both review and cycle time |
| E4 | P3 vs P0: add protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases, routing unchanged | System-period / queue-cluster | Manual queue wait: triage_donemanual_start among reviewed; cycle time | Interventional effect mediated by manual-review waiting, separating occurrence from wait conditional on occurrence | Yes as capacity policy | Queue interference; protected capacity may starve unflagged cases |
| E5 | P4 vs P0: change escalation criteria and/or senior capacity, manual review retained | Reviewed cases reaching manual_done | Escalation occurrence, escalation_queue_wait, cycle time | Interventional indirect effect via escalation occurrence and wait | Yes as policy on criteria/capacity | Escalation is post-review, selected on review findings (U) |
| E6 | P0 vs P1/P2 combined component contrasts | All arrivals | Total cycle time distribution, e.g. median/90th percentile, not only mean | Total policy effect on cycle time; component separable effects | Yes at cluster/system level | SUTVA violation: one case’s priority/review consumes others’ capacity |
| E7 | P2/P3/P4 vs P0 | All arrivals, with audit subsample | Decision quality: outcome vs audited correct decision, Reject errors, compliance with mandatory-review rules | Total policy effect on quality; not a mediation effect unless review is set by intervention | Outcome observed, quality only partly | `outcome` (Approve/Reject) is not correctness; unobserved underlying_risk and review findings confound quality |
| E8 | Controlled: flagged cases assigned manual_review=0 vs =1 regardless of judgement, priority retained | Flagged cases where review is discretionary (positivity) | Cycle time and decision quality | Controlled direct effect of flag label with review fixed; and controlled effect of review | Mathematically yes; operationally may violate compliance | No overlap: most flagged are reviewed, few unflagged high-risk are not; safety risk |

Interference arises because shared triage, review, and senior queues mean one case’s priority or review changes others’ waits, violating SUTVA; `arrival_load` is only a coarse snapshot, not time-varying queue state. Exposure-induced confounding arises because flag-induced manual review, triage findings, and reviewer caution affect escalation and also cycle time/quality, so escalation is both mediator and confounder for later paths. Treatment-confounder feedback occurs over time: early priority changes queue state, which changes later waits and possibly human review/escalation decisions for subsequent cases. Resource assignment (T1–T4, M1–M3) may be caused by flag/complexity and affect service times, making it a post-exposure confounder rather than a simple baseline covariate.

## 3. Stage decomposition

Values are **means in hours**, computed from the timestamps as: wait = start  previous event; service = done  start; gaps only among cases where the next stage occurs. If a stage is absent, its wait/service are `NA`, not zero; `residual-to-close` = close  last observed completion (triage/manual/escalation, whichever is last), so non-reviewed cases are not assigned a fake manual interval. Total = close  arrival.

Flagged cases start triage about 2.2 hours sooner and have slightly shorter triage service, yet finish about 8.3 hours later on average. The reversal coincides with near-universal manual review for flagged cases, a ~6.5-hour triage-done-to-manual-start gap versus ~1.9 hours among the few reviewed unflagged cases, and escalation in 6/10 flagged cases adding roughly 7 hours of queue plus ~2 hours of senior service. Manual and escalation *service* times are similar by flag, suggesting waiting and occurrence, not slower work, dominate. These contrasts are not automatically causal: flagged cases have higher `risk_score` and more High complexity, reviewed unflagged cases are human-selected, queue waits depend on other cases, and conditioning on review/escalation selects on post-flag events. Small cells, especially escalated unflagged cases (n=2), make several means fragile.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Is the flag label itself, near the threshold, causing routing/delay? | Within-risk-score / regression-discontinuity around flag threshold if flag = 1{scorec} plus complexity rule; compare narrow bandwidth | risk_score running variable, complexity strata, arrival hour/load; no adjustment for post-flag review | No manipulation of score around cutoff; continuity of U_risk at cutoff | Density test at cutoff; covariate balance; placebo cutoffs; donut RD | Jump in review but no jump in pre-determined complexity/outcome proxies supports label effect; score bunching invalidates it |
| Does manual queue wait, not review occurrence, drive delay? | Queue-state-aware analysis: reconstruct time-varying queue length/resources busy from starts/dones; model wait as function of queue at triage_done | Baseline score, complexity, arrival_load + time-varying queue state, resource on duty; stratify by review occurrence | Queue reconstruction from timestamps valid; no unmeasured expedited handling | Wait should rise with contemporaneous queue, not only arrival_load; service times should not rise similarly | If waits unrelated to actual queue overlap, look for administrative batching/holding instead |
| What is the total policy effect of removing mandatory review? | Policy-change interrupted time series or randomized encouragement: randomly encourage reviewers to use discretion (P2) in flagged cases, cluster by team/week | Cluster, calendar time, load, score, complexity; intention-to-treat primary | Encouragement affects cycle mainly via review uptake; no spillover across clusters (use cluster design) | First stage: encouragement reduces review occurrence; check unflagged spillover waits | Weak first stage or large unflagged spillover means case-level estimates are not policy effects |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 keep current | Fast flagged triage (~0.2h wait) | High manual/escalation queues | Long flagged cycle (~16.5h mean) | Lowest compliance risk; quality unknown | Burdens unflagged via shared queues | Descriptive only | Do not expand without pilot |
| P1 suppress flag display | Priority may weaken if lane is label-driven; triage wait likely rises for flagged | Review may fall if humans rely on label | Uncertain: triage slower, downstream faster | Risk if score misunderstood; staff may reconstruct flag | May relieve queues generally | Weak; tests label salience | Useful mechanistic sub-study, not standalone fix |
| P2 retain priority, remove mandatory review | Triage speed retained | Manual volume and queue fall sharply | Largest plausible reduction, towards flagged-no-escalation profile, if review was discretionary | Highest: missed mandatory review, false Approves | Frees capacity for unflagged/human-selected reviews | Moderate rationale, no causal estimate yet | Best pilot candidate with audits/guardrails |
| P3 add protected flagged review capacity | No triage change | Flagged manual wait falls; unflagged wait may rise unless capacity truly additional | Moderate reduction, limited by escalation queue (~7h wait) | Low compliance risk | Senior/review staff pulled from elsewhere | Weak-moderate; addresses wait not occurrence | Consider if review is legally mandatory |
| P4 change escalation criteria/capacity | No triage/manual change | Senior queue falls if criteria tightened or capacity added | Reduction concentrated in escalated cases (~9h added per escalation) | Under-escalation of true high risk | Senior capacity may be diverted | Weak; escalation highly selected | Pair with review audit; not first alone |

**Pilot choice: P2.** Eligibility: flagged cases meeting score/complexity criteria where manual review is currently automatic but not legally mandated; exclude pre-specified safety-critical High-complexity subtypes. Randomization: cluster-randomize by reviewer-team-week to P2 (priority retained, review at documented human discretion) versus P0, to limit queue contamination; stratify by expected load. Queue-capacity guardrail: pause and revert a cluster if unflagged manual wait or flagged triage wait exceeds P0 median by >50% for two consecutive days, or senior queue exceeds safe threshold. Primary outcome: total cycle time (median/90th percentile). Secondary: triage wait, review occurrence/wait, escalation, unflagged spillover waits, audited decision quality on random plus all Reject/override cases, compliance exceptions. Minimum process data: full start/done timestamps, time-varying queue length, staffing/resources, review rationale/findings, escalation rationale, flag-display exposure, audit outcome. Stopping conditions: any serious missed-risk event plausibly linked to skipped review; audited false-Approve rate above pre-set margin; guardrail breach; review occurrence does not fall (failed first stage). Limit: decision-quality non-inferiority is binding, not secondary in importance.

```python
w = df.copy()
w["triage_wait"] = h(w["triage_start"] - w["arrival"])
w["triage_service"] = h(w["triage_done"] - w["triage_start"])
w["manual_wait"] = h(w["manual_start"] - w["triage_done"])       # NaN if no manual
w["manual_service"] = h(w["manual_done"] - w["manual_start"])
w["esc_wait"] = h(w["escalation_start"] - w["manual_done"])      # NaN if no escalation
w["esc_service"] = h(w["escalation_done"] - w["escalation_start"])
last_done = w[["triage_done","manual_done","escalation_done"]].max(axis=1)
w["residual_to_close"] = h(w["close"] - last_done)
w["total_cycle"] = h(w["close"] - w["arrival"])
w["has_manual"] = w["manual_start"].notna(); w["has_esc"] = w["escalation_start"].notna()

cols = ["triage_wait","triage_service","manual_wait","manual_service",
        "esc_wait","esc_service","residual_to_close","total_cycle"]
def summary(data, label):
    print(f"\n{label} n={len(data)} manual_n={int(data['has_manual'].sum())} esc_n={int(data['has_esc'].sum())}")
    print(data[cols].mean(numeric_only=True).round(2).to_string())
    if len(data) < 5 or data["has_esc"].sum() < 5:
        warnings.warn(f"Small subgroup/support for {label}: interpret descriptively only.")

for f, sub in w.groupby("flag"): summary(sub, f"flag={f}")
for c, sub in w.groupby("complexity"): summary(sub, f"complexity={c}")
summary(w[(w.flag==1)&(w.arrival_load=="High")], "flagged High load")
summary(w[(w.flag==1)&(w.arrival_load=="Normal")], "flagged Normal load")
summary(w[(w.flag==1)&w.has_esc], "flagged with escalation")
summary(w[(w.flag==1)&~w.has_esc], "flagged without escalation")
warnings.warn("DFG performance differences are descriptive process associations under case-mix, "
              "selection into stages, and shared-queue interference; they do NOT identify natural "
              "direct/indirect effects, which need cross-world assumptions implausible here.")
```

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk:** If richer measures of `U_underlying_risk` (or audit findings) show flagged/reviewed/escalated cases are genuinely more complex and a plausible unobserved-risk sensitivity analysis explains the ~8-hour gap, the “mandatory review causes delay” interpretation reverses to case mix.
2. **Queue interference:** If time-varying queue reconstruction or a cluster pilot shows unflagged cases slow as much as flagged cases when flag density rises, or flagged waits do not fall when protected capacity is added, the bottleneck is system congestion/staffing, not flag routing per se, and P2’s case-level benefit will not materialise at system level.
3. **Decision quality:** If audited false-Approve / missed-risk rates rise under discretionary review (P2) beyond the non-inferiority margin, cycle-time gains are not acceptable and P0/P3 becomes preferable despite delay.
4. **Timestamp semantics:** If timestamps are batch-entered, backdated, in mixed local timezones, or `manual_start` records assignment rather than work start, the wait/service decomposition inverts and apparent queue delay may be recording delay.
5. **Positivity or lack of overlap:** If, within score×complexity×load strata, there are effectively no comparable reviewed/unreviewed or escalated/non-escalated cases, mediation contrasts are extrapolations; recommendations must revert to policy-level trials rather than adjusted decompositions.
6. **Behavior change by reviewers:** If suppressing or retaining the flag changes reviewer caution, documentation, or gaming (e.g. inferring the flag from score under P1, defensive escalation under scrutiny), then component effects are not separable and pilot results will not transport to routine practice.

## 9. Final synthesis

Directly observed: flagged cases reach and finish triage much faster (wait ~0.24 vs 2.48 hours), are universally sent to manual review versus 4/10 unflagged, wait far longer for that review (~6.5 vs 1.9 hours among reviewed), escalate more often (6/10 vs 2/10), and ultimately take about twice as long overall (~16.5 vs 8.2 hours), while manual and escalation service durations themselves are broadly similar by flag. Plausible mechanism: the flag is a policy bundle in which a small triage gain is swamped by adding a review stage, injecting volume into a shared review queue, and then exposing more cases to a scarce senior queue; complexity and imperfectly measured underlying risk amplify all three. Unidentified: how much delay would have occurred anyway for these risks under human judgement, the cross-world natural direct/indirect effects, the effect on decision correctness (Approve/Reject is not correctness), and system-level effects under interference, because treatment of one case changes others’ waits and review findings are unobserved post-treatment confounders. The most useful next evidence is not another adjusted decomposition but the cluster-randomized P2 pilot – retain priority triage, make manual review discretionary for eligible flagged cases, with audited quality, queue guardrails for unflagged cases, and time-varying queue/staffing capture – because it directly tests the largest suspected pathway while measuring spillovers and safety.
