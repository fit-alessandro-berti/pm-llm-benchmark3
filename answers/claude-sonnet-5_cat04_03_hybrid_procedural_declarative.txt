# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

**Material assumptions**

- A1. One trace = one vendor onboarding case; `concept:name`, `time:timestamp`, `org:resource` are always present; case-level attributes (risk class, emergency flags) are generally **not** present unless stated otherwise.
- A2. "Accepted case"  `Activate Vendor` occurs; "rejected case"  `Reject Vendor` occurs. A case containing both (T8) is a data-quality/control-flow error, not a third outcome.
- A3. "Eventually" (R3, R4, R6) means "at some later position in the same trace," not "within the same case instance across systems."
- A4. The stable backbone (Section 2 of the scenario) defines the **only** activities that belong in the canonical control-flow model. `Sanctions Escalation`, `Obtain Legal Opinion`, `Change Bank Details`, `Suspend Vendor` are **exception/extension activities**: real, but not part of the block-structured backbone, and are deliberately modeled declaratively.
- A5. No activity `Reactivate Vendor` / `Resume Vendor` exists in the visible label set. Any requirement that implies a second activation/payment cycle after `Suspend Vendor` (part of R11) cannot be realized with the given vocabulary and is treated as a **documented blind spot**, not silently enforced.
- A6. Concurrency of `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, `Verify Bank Account` is true interleaving: no fixed directly-follows relation exists from `Receive Onboarding Request` to any single one of them.

**The R5/R6 conflict**

- R5 (Legal): `Obtain Legal Opinion` **must always** precede `Activate Vendor` once `Sanctions Escalation` has occurred — a hard, unconditional control-flow precedence.
- R6 (Ops): activation **may** precede the opinion if jointly authorized *outside the event log*, provided the opinion follows within 24h. The authorization is, by construction, **not observable** in the data we are given.

These two statements are not simultaneously satisfiable as a single global rule over the visible event log: R5 says "never", R6 says "sometimes, but the evidence for 'sometimes' is invisible."

**Two possible resolutions**

- **Resolution A — Legal wins (strict, default-deny).** Treat every case where `Activate Vendor` precedes `Obtain Legal Opinion` after a `Sanctions Escalation` as a violation **unless** an explicit case attribute (e.g. `emergency_authorization_id`) is present and the opinion arrives within 24h. Absent that attribute, the case fails.
- **Resolution B — Ops wins (permissive, default-allow).** Treat the same pattern as a warning only, on the theory that authorization commonly happens outside the system and the log's silence should not be held against operations; only the 24h SLA is checked.

**Chosen implementation:** **Resolution A**, enforced as a conservative default-deny rule in the resource/data-aware layer (Section 7), with an explicit override path if/when an authorization attribute is added to the log.

**Compliance risk of this choice:** Resolution A will generate **false positives** against genuinely authorized emergency activations that are never retrofitted into the log (operational friction, manual-review backlog). The alternative (Resolution B) would generate **false negatives**: an unauthorized premature activation would sail through as a mere warning, which is the materially worse regulatory outcome for a sanctions-adjacent control. We accept the operational cost of A to avoid the compliance exposure of B. Both variants are implemented in Section 7 so the choice is switchable, and Section 8 shows T6 evaluated under **both**.
