1. Current-process diagnosis
- Speed and cost concentrate on one dominant happy path: Intake  Auto eligibility screen (3600/5000)  Fast approval (2550)  Dispatch replacement (2300)  Quality/compliance close. Performance DFG shows this path is operationally attractive (AutoFast approval median 0.4h; Fast approvalDispatch median 1.8h; Dispatchclose median 3.2h). Manual and fraud branches are an order of magnitude slower (Manual eligibility median 5h from intake; ManualRepair quote median 15h; Request missing evidenceManual median 26h).
- Quality and CX contradict that attractiveness. Customer complaint/reopen hits 9.2% overall but 14.1% after Fast approval versus 3.3% on Repair quoteCustomer approvalRepair device. Fast-approval economics erode from “31% cheaper before rework” to “only 9% cheaper” after complaints, reshipments, and credits. Dispatchcomplaint is a long-tail failure (mean 96h, p90 180h) linked to wrong replacement selection; wrong-replacement expected cost is EUR 95 (accessories) vs EUR 410 (high-value).
- Compliance is structurally weak on the frequent path. R1 requires Manual or Fraud/risk before Fast approval for claims >EUR 400; R2 forbids dispatch before evidence completeness except Premium low-risk accessories; R3 requires Manual eligibility on any reopen before close/denial. Token replay: fitness 0.91 but only 68% fitting traces. Alignment diagnostics flag Fast approval before evidence completeness, dispatch without risk review on high-value devices, and complaint/reopen after close—exactly the high-frequency route.
- Segment outcomes show the average hides risk. Premium/Standard low-risk accessories tolerate the fast path (complaints 6–8%, strong 24h Premium SLA pressure). Premium/Standard high-value devices do not (fast-path complaints 22%/18% vs 5–6% on reviewed/repair; high R1/R2 exposure). Unclear-defect/missing-evidence cases (22% share) have median 140h, repeated evidence in 26% of evidence cases, and highest abandonment (Request missing evidenceabandoned 320).
- Resources amplify the contradiction: auto screen is nearly free but “outdated and not segment-aware”; 7 manual reviewers cliff above 82% utilization; 2 fraud specialists bottleneck after campaigns; repair-quote delay is part-price wait; quality check is underused except after repair campaigns; escalation board is a single daily 45-minute slot. Precision of the discovered model is low because low-frequency mixes of auto, risk, and repair violate policy—so “optimize the average path” would industrialize non-compliance.
- Core contradiction: the most frequent path is locally optimal on cycle time and pre-rework cost, yet globally inferior on rework, compliance, and high-value CX. The slower reviewed/repair path is not universally better—it is better for high-value and unclear-defect work, and often wasteful for Premium low-risk accessories that need sub-24h completion.

2. Redesign A: Segment-aware guarded fast path
- Redesigned flow: Intake  segment/defect/value-aware auto screen (rules rewritten)  (i) Guaranteed fast path only for low-risk accessories with evidence-complete or Premium-low-risk R2 exception  Fast approval  lightweight pre-dispatch part-match gate (photos/SKU checksum)  Dispatch  close; (ii) mid/high-value or incomplete evidence  Manual eligibility and/or Fraud/risk before any approval; (iii) reopen always forced through Manual eligibility (hard R3 gate) before close/deny; unclear defect forced into structured evidence request once, then Manual—not back into blind auto.
- Targets: Premium/Standard low-risk accessories (keep speed); high-value devices and >EUR 400 claims (remove illegal Fast approval); alignment deviations “Fast approval before evidence” and “dispatch without risk review.”
- Mechanism: Converts the auto screen from a volume optimizer into a policy-and-risk router. Preserves the cheap fast path where complaint rates are already moderate (6–8%) and SLA credits dominate, while starving the fast path that drives 18–22% high-value complaints and R1/R2 breaches. Pre-dispatch gate attacks the Dispatchcomplaint long tail and EUR 410 mis-ship cost without putting every case through full repair. Uses existing auto capacity (unlimited, EUR 0.20) and only modest quality-gate capacity.
- Deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved: Does not fix repeated evidence loops or digital-literacy abandonment; may raise manual inflow 15–35% (rule-update risk stated in prompt); does not rescale escalation board; unclear-defect cycle time remains weak; no structural preference for repair vs replace on high-value beyond correct routing.

3. Redesign B: Evidence-first portal with single-loop completion
- Redesigned flow: Intake  immediate defect/segment triage questionnaire  Evidence portal (guided uploads, part/serial OCR, symptom tree) with completeness score  only when score passes: Auto or Manual eligibility (segment-aware)  technical decision (Fast approval only if completeness + low-risk; else Fraud/risk or Repair quote)  fulfillment  close. Incomplete cases get one timed portal cycle with proactive status; second failure routes to assisted Manual outreach, not another blind Request missing evidence. Abandonment recovery offer (callback) for low-digital-literacy flags. Reopens enter Manual eligibility with portal packet attached (R3).
- Targets: the 22% unclear-defect segment; Request missing evidence loops (26% 2 times); RequestManual mean 34h / p90 88h; Claim abandoned (390 ends); R2 evidence-before-dispatch violations.
- Mechanism: Moves evidence completeness from a mid-process rework activity to a gated precondition, cutting the multi-hop Manualevidence cycle visible in the DFG (RequestManual 890, ManualRequest 780). Prompt estimates portal can cut repeated evidence 30–45%. Eligibility decisions then operate on fuller information, reducing wrongful Fast approval and later complaint/reopen (which today returns 310 cases to Manual). Status visibility matches Standard customers’ tolerance of ~72h when informed. Reduces fraud/manual load caused by thin files.
- Deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved: Risk of higher abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers (explicit prompt trade-off); does not by itself add pre-dispatch part QA; Premium low-risk sub-24h SLA can worsen if portal friction is mandatory for all—so Premium low-risk keeps a thin-evidence exception under R2; implementation is product/UX heavy; fraud Monday spikes and repair part-price waits remain.

4. Redesign C: High-value repair-preferring fulfillment with capacity-tiered quality gates
- Redesigned flow: After eligibility (retain auto for true low-risk complete cases only), split fulfillment by value/defect: (1) low-risk accessories  Fast approval  Dispatch with sampling QA (e.g., statistical or first-time-shipper checks); (2) high-value devices  prefer Repair quoteCustomer approvalRepairQuality check (full), with replacement only after repair infeasibility or Premium opt-in; (3) mandatory full pre-dispatch QA when replacement is chosen on high-value; (4) complaint/reopen  Manual eligibility then tiered: technical fix path vs Escalation board reserved for Premium or repeated failure (board no longer default). Quality check capacity actively scheduled for high-value repair, not left underused.
- Targets: high-value complaint asymmetry (22%/18% fast vs 5–6% repair path); rework repair 12.5% on high-value devices; wrong-replacement EUR 410 exposure; underused Quality check vs overloaded Escalation board; Fast approvalcomplaint and Dispatchcomplaint edges.
- Mechanism: Uses segment outcome data showing repair path quality dominance on high-value, while not imposing repair latency on accessories that need speed. Shifts quality effort upstream of customer failure (pre-dispatch gate / full QC) instead of downstream credits and escalation. Reduces board congestion by filtering reopens through Manual first (R3) and limiting board to high-sensitivity cases. Aligns resource rates: technicians/inspectors absorb value-protective work; reviewers handle policy, not every CX failure.
- Deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved: High-value median cycle time will stay much higher than fast replace (96–120h class vs 20–28h)—accepted for quality/compliance; repair-quote part-price waits and technician capacity become critical; Standard high-value customers may perceive “slower” unless messaging frames repair as value protection; does not fully modernize auto rules or evidence portal (can coexist later); Premium accessory credit risk if any high-value logic leaks into low-risk routing.

6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks
- Redesign A: (1) Over-tuning auto rules floods Manual eligibility (prompt +15–35%), utilization >82%, queues explode, Premium accessories miss 24h credits. (2) Lightweight pre-dispatch gate becomes rubber-stamp or adds p90 delay without catching SKU/part errors if checksum data are poor. Bottleneck migration: from invisible compliance debt and complaint tail  Manual reviewers and gate operators; fraud specialists still spike post-campaign.
- Redesign B: (1) Portal increases abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers, shifting loss from cycle time to lost claims/NPS. (2) “One evidence loop” policy without assisted outreach creates hard denials/complaints that hit Escalation board. Bottleneck migration: from Request missing evidenceManual ping-pong  portal support staffing and (if completeness too strict) Manual assisted completion; eligibility latency moves earlier rather than disappearing.
- Redesign C: (1) Repair-prefer without part-price process redesign lengthens Repair quote waits (already median 21h to customer approval) and starves technicians. (2) Full HV QA without staffing turns Quality check into a new queue; sampling on accessories may miss clusters of wrong parts. Bottleneck migration: from Dispatchcomplaint long tail and Escalation board  Repair technicians, part-price confirmation, and Quality check; Manual still absorbs R3 reopens.

7. Recommended experiment sequence
- First pilot: Redesign A rule rewrite + optional light pre-dispatch gate on Standard high-value replacements only (2-week rule deploy path), with explicit capacity buffer (temporary reviewer overtime or deferred non-SLA work) before enabling stricter routing. Hold Premium low-risk fast path constant as control for SLA credits.
- Metrics to monitor: % fitting traces / R1–R3 violation rate; complaint/reopen rate by segment and by path; cycle-time median and p90 by segment (not global mean); manual utilization and queue wait; cost after rework (replacements, credits, reships); abandonment rate; wrong-replacement incidents; Premium credit exposure >24h.
- Stop/go thresholds (qualitative/ranges): Go expand A if HV complaint rate falls materially toward reviewed-path levels (directionally toward mid-single digits) AND R1/R2 violations drop sharply AND manual utilization stays under ~80–82% with stable Premium accessory median near fast-path baseline. Stop/rollback rules if manual queue wait doubles or Premium credit rate rises sustainably. Do not go wide if only average cycle time improves while HV complaints flat.
- Sequencing logic: (1) Stabilize legal routing and stop hemorrhaging on HV fast path (A). (2) Pilot B evidence portal on the unclear-defect cohort and on cases that would have entered Request missing evidence—measure repeat-evidence rate (30–45% target band) and abandonment before mandating portal for Premium low-risk. (3) Layer C repair-prefer + full QA on high-value once front-end files are cleaner (B) and auto routing is trustworthy (A), so repair capacity is not wasted on avoidable thin-file cases. Escalation board policy tightening only after Manual R3 gate is reliable.
- Learning real improvement vs downstream shift: Use paired segment-level dashboards and case-level linkage—track whether reopen reduction is offset by higher abandonment, later board volume, repair rework, or credit substitution. Require same-segment before/after plus holdout geography or randomized rule exposure where feasible. Declare success only if post-rework cost and compliance move with CX, not if work simply migrates IntakeManual or DispatchQuality check queues. Revisit fraud capacity after any marketing campaign during pilots so Monday specialist overload is not misread as redesign failure.
