## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

Raw fitness collapses from 0.92–0.93 in January–February to 0.51–0.55 in March–April, while mapped fitness remains 0.90–0.91. Distinct labels rise from 9 to 11, duplicate `Open Case` appears in 4–5% of cases, and missing resources rise from about 1% to about 5%. That timing and recovery pattern strongly indicate a semantic/provenance change in labels and recording, not a commensurate 40-point collapse in business conformance. Mapping recovers alignments by treating `Eligibility Screen` as `Validate Eligibility` and deleting duplicate technical events; therefore it tests whether traces fit after a declared equivalence. It does not prove behaviour was unchanged: mapping could also erase a genuinely new screening activity, conceal changed ordering or attributes, or be over-fitted to the old model. Mapped fitness still drifts modestly, from 0.92–0.93 to 0.86–0.88 during May–October, suggesting residual behavioural, population, or recording differences, but aggregate fitness cannot locate them.

Duplicated `Open Case` events inflate starts, variants, DFG edges such as `Open Case->Register`, and can reduce raw fitness; missing resource identifiers, concentrated in migrated activities, bias handover, workload, and resource-specific performance, but unless cases are filtered they need not alter control-flow frequencies. Conversely, post-May patterns cannot plausibly be logging alone: normalized `Assess->Request Info` rises from 12 to 24–29 per 100 cases, rework variants rise similarly, supplier-Z share surges, and cycle times—especially p90 and reconstructed queue waiting—deteriorate. A relabelling of validation alone would not generate extra `Request Info->Collect Documents` loops or waiting. Still, variant metrics from operational tables were not normalized identically, so event-level recomputation is required before quantifying the behavioural share.

### 3.5 One global change point or multiple?

The data strongly favour **multiple mechanism-specific change points**, not one global break. March is a near-pure logging/semantic break: raw fitness collapses but the normalized DFG fragment is essentially unchanged (L1 = 3). May is a behavioural/population break: supplier share and rework jump while March-style label artefacts are already stable. Mid-June/July is a performance/capacity break: cycle tails keep worsening while supplier share already falls. October is a recurring seasonal population break with a possible additional senior-assessment recording/routing component. A single change-point model fitted to, say, median cycle time would smear these together and misattribute the March logging shock to process performance. Monitoring and causal analysis should therefore be artifact-specific: label/schema series, normalized DFG/variant series, stratified waiting/service series, and case-mix series each get their own change-point detection, followed by a joint timeline synthesis.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |

| Level 6: document-quality proxy | First submission complete vs incomplete (zero vs 1 RequestInfo before first decision; bundle completeness field if available) | Test mediation supplier  incompleteness  rework | 40 cases per cell; if completeness field missing, treat RequestInfo proxy as outcome, not mediator | Mediation and campaign effectiveness | Using RequestInfo as both proxy and outcome is circular; obtain true bundle metadata if possible |

The **primary monitoring population** should be Level 1—**all started cases in the normalized log, stratified by high-complexity × Supplier Z, reported monthly (weekly in season) with case-start cohorts**—because the two largest confounded mechanisms (supplier rework and complexity/seasonality) are separated there, cell sizes remain viable (smallest expected cell is roughly 15–45% of 600–800 monthly cases, i.e. usually 50 after pooling rules), and both behavioural rates (loops per case) and performance (waiting/service) can be standardised back to the Jan–Feb mix. The whole-log Level 0 series should be displayed alongside for context but never alerted on alone.

| Outcomes & quality | Approve/Reject mix, decision after SeniorAssessment, reopen/appeal rate if available, outcome by stratum | Baseline outcome rates by complexity × supplier | Outcome shift >4pp within stratum or senior-path outcome divergence | Case audit sample; check whether rework improves decision quality | Mix within broad strata; delayed outcomes | Quality / compliance owner |

Seasonality and multiple testing: (1) Every behavioural and performance monitor is computed both crude and **directly standardised to the Jan–Feb complexity × supplier mix**, and alerting uses the standardised series outside known seasonal windows; during October–November, references switch to a seasonal expectation (prior-year season if obtainable, otherwise the pre-registered Oct–Nov uplift from 2026 with wide intervals), and a monitor must exceed the *seasonal* reference, not the annual baseline, to alert. (2) The renewal season is declared a surveillance regime in advance: thresholds widen, but within-season stratified divergence (e.g. supplier-specific rework) still alerts. (3) With ~12 monitors × multiple strata, false alarms are controlled by requiring two consecutive windows or a CUSUM exceedance, applying Benjamini–Hochberg FDR control (q = 0.10) across the family of weekly edge/family tests, and using monitor-specific effect-size floors (above) rather than one universal threshold or naked p-values. (4) Recurrence detection: each alert is compared against the drift fingerprint library (March logging fingerprint, May supplier fingerprint, June queueing fingerprint, October seasonal fingerprint) by edge-contribution profile; matching a known seasonal fingerprint routes to seasonal playbook rather than new-drift escalation.

def load_and_validate():
    m = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(MONTHLY_CSV), parse_dates=["month"])

def baseline_relative(m, baseline_months=("2026-01", "2026-02")):
    # 2. Baseline-relative changes vs the Jan-Feb mean.
    base = m[m["month"].dt.strftime("%Y-%m").isin(baseline_months)].drop(
        columns=["month"]).mean(numeric_only=True)
    out = m.copy()
    for c in base.index:
        out[c + "_vs_base_abs"] = m[c] - base[c]
        out[c + "_vs_base_rel"] = (m[c] - base[c]) / base[c] * 100.0
    return out, base

def dfg_distances(d, reference="2026-01"):
    # 3. Transparent distances on the supplied DFG *fragment* (rates per 100 cases).
    edges = list(d.columns[1:])
    ref = d.loc[d["month"].dt.strftime("%Y-%m") == reference, edges].iloc[0].astype(float)
    rows = []
    for _, r in d.iterrows():
        diff = r[edges].astype(float) - ref
        rows.append({
            "month": r["month"].strftime("%Y-%m"),
            "L1_pp_summed": float(diff.abs().sum()),
            "L2": float(np.sqrt((diff ** 2).sum())),
            "top_contributor": diff.abs().idxmax(),
            "top_contribution_pp": float(diff.abs().max()),
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

def fitness_recovery(m):
    # 4. Raw-to-mapped recovery: absolute gap and share of the March-style deficit
    #    relative to the baseline raw fitness that mapping restores.
    out = m[["month"]].copy()
    base_raw = m.loc[m["month"].dt.strftime("%Y-%m").isin(["2026-01", "2026-02"]),
                     "raw_model_fitness"].mean()
    out["mapped_minus_raw"] = m["mapped_model_fitness"] - m["raw_model_fitness"]
    out["raw_deficit_vs_baseline"] = base_raw - m["raw_model_fitness"]
    out["share_of_deficit_recovered"] = np.where(
        out["raw_deficit_vs_baseline"] > 0,
        out["mapped_minus_raw"] / out["raw_deficit_vs_baseline"], np.nan)
    return out

def flag_change_points(m):
    # 5. Simple explainable rule: flag a month when a metric-specific,
    #    pre-declared effect-size threshold is crossed month-over-month.
    #    Thresholds are operational floors, NOT a universal significance test.
    rules = {  # metric: (threshold, unit) applied to absolute month diff
        "raw_model_fitness": 0.10, "mapped_model_fitness": 0.03,
        "supplier_z_pct": 10.0, "rework_variant_pct": 5.0,
        "high_complexity_pct": 5.0, "median_cycle_h": 3.0,
        "p90_cycle_h": 8.0, "missing_resource_pct": 2.0,
        "duplicate_open_case_pct": 2.0, "distinct_activity_labels": 1.0,
    }
    flags = []
    for metric, thr in rules.items():
        diffs = m[metric].diff()
        for i in m.index[diffs.abs() >= thr]:
            flags.append({"month": m.loc[i, "month"].strftime("%Y-%m"),
                          "metric": metric,
                          "change": round(float(diffs.loc[i]), 3),
                          "rule": f"|MoM change| >= {thr}"})
    return pd.DataFrame(flags).sort_values(["month", "metric"])

import pm4py

CASE, ACT, TS, RES = "case_id", "activity", "timestamp", "resource"
LABEL_MAP = {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}

def format_log(df, case=CASE, act=ACT, ts=TS, res=RES):
    """Format a raw dataframe as a PM4Py event log without mutating the input."""
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(df.copy(), case_id_key=case,
                                  activity_key=act, timestamp_key=ts)

def normalized_log(df):
    """Return (raw_log, mapped_log); the original dataframe/log are untouched.
    Mapping: rename the migrated eligibility label and drop duplicated technical
    'Open Case' events (exact duplicate case/activity/timestamp rows plus any
    second 'Open Case' per case, keeping the first)."""
    raw_log = format_log(df)
    d = df.copy()
    d[ACT] = d[ACT].replace(LABEL_MAP)
    d = d.drop_duplicates(subset=[CASE, ACT, TS], keep="first")
    oc = d[ACT] == "Open Case"
    d = d[~(oc & d.duplicated(subset=[CASE, ACT], keep="first"))]
    return raw_log, format_log(d.sort_values([CASE, TS]))

def monthly_slices(log, freq="MS"):
    """Yield (period_start, sublog) using pm4py.filter_time_range on events."""
    df = pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(log)
    starts = pd.date_range(df["time:timestamp"].min().normalize().replace(day=1),
                           df["time:timestamp"].max(), freq=freq)
    for s in starts:
        e = s + pd.offsets.MonthEnd(0) + pd.Timedelta(days=1)
        yield s, pm4py.filter_time_range(log, str(s.date()), str(e.date()),
                                         mode="events")

def monthly_dfg(log):
    """DFG per month: returns {period: (dfg, start_activities, end_activities)}."""
    return {str(p.date()): pm4py.discover_dfg(sub) for p, sub in monthly_slices(log)}

def monthly_variants(log, top_k=20):
    """Top variants per month via pm4py.get_variants."""
    out = {}
    for p, sub in monthly_slices(log):
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(sub)
        out[str(p.date())] = sorted(variants.items(),
                                    key=lambda kv: len(kv[1]), reverse=True)[:top_k]
    return out

def monthly_temporal_profile(log, resolution="hours"):
    """Temporal profile (activity-pair mean durations) per month."""
    return {str(p.date()): pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(sub, resolution=resolution)
            for p, sub in monthly_slices(log)}

def optional_models(log):
    """Optionally discover a log skeleton and a DECLARE model, if supported."""
    result = {"log_skeleton": pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(log)}
    discover_declare = getattr(pm4py, "discover_declare", None)
    result["declare"] = discover_declare(log) if callable(discover_declare) else None
    return result

def compare_raw_vs_normalized(df):
    """Side-by-side DFG/variant comparison of raw vs mapped logs (no overwrite)."""
    raw_log, mapped_log = normalized_log(df)
    return {
        "raw_dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(raw_log),
        "mapped_dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(mapped_log),
        "raw_variants": pm4py.get_variants(raw_log),
        "mapped_variants": pm4py.get_variants(mapped_log),
        "raw_temporal": pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(raw_log),
        "mapped_temporal": pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(mapped_log),
    }

def segment_log(log, **equality_filters):
    """Filter a formatted log on case/event attributes, e.g.
    segment_log(log, supplier_z=True, high_complexity=False), if present."""
    df = pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(log)
    for col, val in equality_filters.items():
        if col in df.columns:
            df = df[df[col] == val]
    return format_log(df.rename(columns={"case:concept:name": CASE,
                                         "concept:name": ACT,
                                         "time:timestamp": TS}) if
                      "case:concept:name" in df.columns else df)

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest conclusion is that the March fitness collapse is primarily a **logging/semantic drift**, with high confidence (about 90–95%). Raw fitness falls by roughly 0.38 while mapped fitness falls by only about 0.03, the normalized March DFG fragment is nearly identical to January (summed absolute distance 3 points across seven relations), and the label count, duplicate `Open Case` rate, and resource missingness all step at the migration. This does not prove the underlying business step was unchanged—only that, under the declared label equivalence, conformance and the supplied control-flow fragment were stable. Resource missingness after March is likewise confidently a measurement/provenance problem, concentrated in migrated activities, and it invalidates naive resource and handover comparisons thereafter.

It is highly plausible (about 80–85%) that the May break is **supplier-driven rework on a non-random subpopulation**: supplier-Z share jumps by about 29 points while normalized `Assess->Request Info` doubles, rework variants double, and all three partially reverse after the reported September recovery. However, supplier-Z cases were already more complex, and the aggregate data cannot separate bundle incompleteness from region or complexity mix; the supplier attribution needs within-stratum, case-level confirmation. The mid-June/July performance deterioration is plausibly (about 75–80%) dominated by **queueing after the staffing loss, amplified by rework load**, because `Assess` service time rises only about 0.6 hours while waiting and the p90 tail rise far more. The July priority rule probably redistributed waiting toward standard cases (about 50–60% confidence in direction, low confidence in size) but is invisible in monthly aggregates. The October–November deterioration is most confidently a **seasonal population drift** (about 85%): volume and high-complexity share surge together, with senior assessment and rework rising as expected consequences, though a senior-assessment recording or routing change remains a live alternative given historically inconsistent capture.

Mediation prevents clean additive attribution: incomplete supplier bundles create rework, rework consumes scarce assessor capacity, capacity loss lengthens queues, priority reassigns that queueing pain, and seasonality then stresses the same depleted system. December's improvement therefore cannot be split among lower seasonal load, overtime, and the document-quality campaign.

I would revise this account if event-level data showed: within supplier × complexity strata, rework did not rise in May; queue waiting did not diverge by priority class after July; `Eligibility Screen` cases had materially different successors, attributes, or outcomes from `Validate Eligibility`; waiting rose before the staffing loss after backlog adjustment; or prior-year seasonal data reproduced October exactly, including the senior-assessment rate.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw fitness falls by more than 0.05 while mapped fitness and the normalized DFG fragment remain within baseline variation, THEN open a logging/schema investigation and freeze process-change conclusions, BECAUSE a raw–mapped divergence is the fingerprint of semantic drift rather than behavioural change.
2. IF duplicated technical events exceed 1% of cases or resource missingness exceeds 3% overall or 8% in any activity × team cell, THEN route to data engineering for source-capture remediation and flag all resource-based analyses as biased, BECAUSE provenance defects concentrated by activity or team are missing-not-at-random and mimic organisational drift.
3. IF mix-standardised rework rises by more than 5 points for two consecutive windows, THEN audit document completeness by supplier within complexity strata before changing assessor guidance, BECAUSE a standardised rework break indicates changed loop behaviour while stratification identifies whether supplier bundle quality mediates it.
4. IF waiting before `Assess` rises by more than 25% while `Assess` service time stays within 15% of baseline, THEN treat the alert as a capacity/queueing incident and review roster, backlog age, and work-in-process, BECAUSE stable service with rising waiting is the signature of insufficient capacity rather than slower work.
5. IF high-complexity waiting improves while standard-case waiting or p90 worsens after a priority-rule change, THEN audit the priority rule's distributional impact and consider queue safeguards for standard cases, BECAUSE priority policies redistribute delay and aggregate cycle time can conceal harm to deprioritised classes.
6. IF crude cycle time and rework worsen in October–November but mix-standardised, within-stratum metrics remain within seasonal reference bands, THEN invoke the seasonal staffing playbook without declaring new process drift, BECAUSE recurring population drift changes aggregate metrics even when underlying behaviour is stable.
7. IF mapped fitness falls by more than 0.03 and the DFG fragment distance exceeds its baseline bootstrap band for two consecutive windows after logging monitors are clean, THEN escalate as candidate behavioural drift and run variant, alignment, and DECLARE/log-skeleton comparisons by segment, BECAUSE persistent normalized conformance loss with clean provenance is unlikely to be explained by labels or duplicates alone.
