# Independent Review: Supplier-Onboarding Conformance: M0/CS-9 versus Pilot, Emergency, Data, and Practice

## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### 1.1 What M0 actually says (model semantics, stipulated)

M0 is a strict partial order Register → checks → decision → Notify → Close. `checks` contains Sanctions_Check (S) and Quality_Review (Q) with no internal edge, so they are concurrent. `decision` is XOR(PO[Manual_Procurement_Approval (MPA) → Activate_Supplier (ACT)], Reject_Supplier). So the language of M0 is exactly four traces:

- ⟨Register, S, Q | Q, S, MPA, ACT, Notify, Close⟩
- ⟨Register, S, Q | Q, S, Reject, Notify, Close⟩

M0 has no optional activities, no silent steps, no loop, and no labels for Auto_Approve, Request_More_Info, QA_Clearance, reactivation, withdrawal or master-data validation. It has no resource, data, time or amount semantics.

Compared with CS-9 as quoted, M0 is:

- **Stricter than CS-9 in five places.** CS-9 does not require:
  - Register_Supplier to be the first event;
  - Q before a rejection (CS-9 explicitly allows an abbreviated pre-screen for rejected leads);
  - S and Q before MPA (CS-9 only requires them before activation);
  - Notify_Requester at all;
  - Notify before Close.
- **Weaker than CS-9 in two places.** M0 does not encode the requester ≠ approver rule (CS-9 says so explicitly). It also cannot express the data conditions that make an exception admissible.
- **Silent** on pilots, emergencies, reactivation, withdrawal and information requests.

The consequence is that an M0 deviation and a CS-9 violation are different things. Some M0 deviations are not CS-9 violations: notification, pre-screen rejection, and possibly registration. Some CS-9 violations produce no M0 deviation: segregation-of-duties (SoD) breaches.

### 1.5 Burden-of-proof approach

**To declare NONCOMPLIANT, the reviewer must show four things:**

1. An applicable norm with authority at the time of the case. CS-9 qualifies. A model-only requirement in M0 does not, unless it traces to an authoritative source.
2. The case is in the norm's scope.
3. A deviation established on reliable evidence.
4. For a HIGH or CRITICAL severity, that the deviation touches a preventive control, not merely its logging.

Absence of an event counts as evidence that it did not happen only where the log is complete for that activity: the closed-world assumption. That holds for notifications before 2026-07-01 but not after. Standard: more-likely-than-not for classification; clear evidence for HIGH or CRITICAL.

**To accept JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, the burden shifts to Operations as the party invoking the exception.** It must show five things:

1. an authority source;
2. that the source was in force at the event timestamp;
3. that the case met the eligibility predicate, captured at decision time;
4. that the exception's retained and compensating conditions were met;
5. that this was recorded at or near the time.

If the eligibility or authority evidence is missing, the exception is *not established*, and the base norm applies.

**Retroactivity.** Retroactive *evidence* of what happened is admissible. The SUP-713 mapping confirms that the content performed was equivalent. Retroactive *authorization* of what was not permitted does not change the classification; it can only reduce severity. That applies to a hoped-for amendment (SUP-714) or a later ratification of SUP-705.

**Claims that M0 is defective** succeed where M0 contradicts or exceeds CS-9 or another authoritative source. Stakeholder desirability alone justifies a *model change request*. It excuses a deviation from M0 only where CS-9 is silent (RMI). It never excuses a deviation from CS-9 (permanent auto-approval).

## 2. Prosecution Brief

**The operation is materially noncompliant, and CS-9, operationalized by M0, must be enforced.**

**P1: CS-9 is the only binding norm in the file.** It is "controlled and formally approved". Everything the defense relies on sits lower in the hierarchy of authority:

- PD-3 is minutes plus an email and was never incorporated into CS-9.
- The Compliance observer's own later statement is that she did not approve weakening CS-9.
- FD-7 was self-limiting and "expired automatically".
- The permanent auto-approval branch Operations wants "has no permanent policy amendment".

A norm lower in the hierarchy cannot silently displace the controlled standard. At best it creates a narrow, strictly conditioned exception whose conditions the exception's proponent must prove.

**P2: The exception machinery had no off switch.**

- **SUP-705** was activated on Monday 2026-07-13, the first minute of business after FD-7 expired. Operations' answer that "the emergency still factually existed" concedes the point: anyone who believed the emergency continued could have sought a written extension over the weekend and did not.
- **SUP-714** (Sunday 2026-08-02) and **six post-pilot Auto_Approve cases** in the first week of August show that the pilot flow had become the default: normalization of deviance. "Believed permanent approval was imminent" describes a *belief about future authority*, which is precisely what CS-9 control exists to exclude.

An organization whose exceptions do not expire in practice does not have exceptions. It has an unapproved policy.

**P3: The eligibility gate failed, and it failed in the most dangerous direction.**

- Of 312 Auto_Approve cases, 14 are clearly ineligible and 9 more have no eligibility attributes: 23 cases (7.4%) in which the exception is not established.
- **SUP-703** is the paradigm. A HIGH-risk, new, foreign supplier failed every PD-3 criterion, yet the operator selected "pilot", no manual approval took place, quality review came after activation, and the first purchase was EUR 180,000. That is nine times FD-7's emergency cap, with no emergency.
- PD-3 was permitted only "as a monitored exception". Monitoring that lets 14 clearly ineligible cases through (plus 9 unverifiable ones) did not fulfil the condition on which the Compliance observer's consent rested.

**P4: M0 understates misconduct; it does not just overstate it.** Operations' claim is that M0's deviation rate is inflated. The opposite error is just as real:

- **SUP-712** fits M0 perfectly, yet the requester and approver are the same human under two aliases. That is a direct breach of an explicit CS-9 rule, and fitness analysis cannot see it.
- **SUP-715** fits perfectly, yet the quality reviewer and approver are spouses in the same reporting line with no COI disclosure recorded.
- For 13 of 15 cases the file does not establish SoD at all (U5).

A "fitting" trace therefore proves nothing about resource compliance. Any argument that "most deviations are artifacts" must be offset by the violations that produce no deviation.

**P5: High-risk preventive controls were breached.** **SUP-708** is a HIGH-risk supplier activated before its sanctions check, and before MPA, with no pilot or emergency. Sanctions screening is a *preventive* control: activation creates the capability to order and pay, which is the exposure. "No transactions occurred" is outcome luck, not a control. Internal Audit independently flagged SUP-703 and SUP-708 as potentially severe.

**P6: Desirable outcomes do not amend policy.**

- That 289 pilot cases were eligible, or that Operations likes auto-approval, does not change CS-9. The legitimate route is a controlled amendment.
- Stakeholder approval of RMI (SUP-707) is conceded but irrelevant: RMI never conflicted with CS-9.
- SUP-713's mapping was approved *after* the case. At case time no approved control recognized QA_Clearance as satisfying CS-9's quality-review requirement.

**P7: Evidence failures are the organization's failures.** The organization owns its log. A post-migration missingness of 29% means nearly a third of notification obligations cannot be evidenced. SUP-706's only evidence is a mutable text link entered two days later. The organization also chose to reactivate SUP-710 after six months without Register or Q, on the strength of a prior certificate that nobody re-reviewed in the log.

**Prosecution concessions (required and genuine):**

- M0 cannot express RMI. The 47 RMI cases, including SUP-707, are not misconduct.
- M0's requirement of Q before rejection contradicts CS-9's abbreviated pre-screen, so SUP-709 is not a violation.
- Notification is an M0 requirement, not a CS-9 requirement.
- SUP-711's duplicate is a migration artifact.
- SUP-713's content was equivalent.
- M0 has no resource layer.
- Where PD-3 or FD-7 validly applied and all conditions are proved, a case is not a violation.

These concessions remove noise. They do not touch SUP-703, 705, 708, 712, 714 or the systemic expiry and eligibility failures.

## 3. Defense Brief

**M0 is obsolete, incomplete and incorrectly scoped, and it was applied to data it cannot interpret. Its deviation rate is not a compliance measure.**

**D1: M0 is a model of CS-9, not CS-9, and it is wrong in both directions.** Five of M0's ordering and existence requirements have no basis in CS-9:

- mandatory Register;
- Q before rejection;
- checks before MPA;
- mandatory Notify;
- Notify before Close.

Every trace that deviates only on these points (SUP-706, 709, 710 in part) is an M0 deviation with no CS-9 content.

**D2: Concurrency is right, but the block is at the wrong granularity.** M0 correctly makes S and Q concurrent. It then puts the whole `checks` block before the whole `decision`. That forces a quality review before a sanctions-driven rejection and before approval, neither of which CS-9 requires. SUP-709 is the direct casualty: under CS-9 a lead that fails sanctions pre-screen "may use an abbreviated pre-screen process". If SUP-709 is in scope, M0 contradicts CS-9. If it is out of scope, it should not be scored. Either way, it is not misconduct.

**D3: The missing loop penalizes the most careful cases.** M0 has no loop and no RMI label, so the two RMI events in SUP-707 are two log moves. Yet all 47 RMI cases waited for the requested evidence before activation (F8), and Compliance agrees the behaviour is desirable. A conformance oracle that scores diligence as deviation is miscalibrated. CS-9 is silent on RMI, so there is no norm to violate.

**D4: Overlays are legitimate governance instruments.** CS-9 is a standard, and standards are routinely administered with time-boxed, monitored exceptions that do not amend them.

- **PD-3.** A steering-committee decision had the Compliance observer's *contemporaneous written* consent: "pilot may proceed as a monitored exception". Her later statement ("had not approved weakening CS-9") is fully consistent with that: a time-boxed exception is not a weakening. 289 of 312 cases (92.6%) appear eligible.
- **SUP-702** meets every observable condition. It was LOW-risk, DE and in an approved group; eligibility fields were complete; it fell inside the window (Monday 06-15); sanctions preceded activation; Q came the next business day (limit 2); and the CRM shows a notification with a stable ID.
- **SUP-704** meets every FD-7 condition in evidence. It was a critical pump supplier activated inside the window (Saturday 07-11), with sanctions first, initial spend of EUR 18,000 (under the 20,000 cap), daily monitoring, and Q and retrospective approval on Monday 07-13, one business day later.

Scoring SUP-702 and SUP-704 as deviations measures M0's ignorance of the overlays, not misconduct.

**D5: Most of the notification gap is explained by the CRM migration.** The rise from 2% to 29% matches the roughly 30% expected from 70% mirroring (F6). If notifications had actually stopped, one would expect missingness *above* the mirroring gap. Treating a missing log event as a missing notification after 07-01 applies the closed-world assumption to a log known to be open. SUP-702's notification is verified in the CRM by a stable ID. SUP-706 has a CRM message at +3h.

**D6: Reactivation is a different case notion.** SUP-710 is the reactivation of a supplier closed six months earlier. CS-9 has no reactivation procedure. The missing Register is expected, because the supplier already exists, and the prior quality certification remains valid. The truly preventive controls were done, and fresh: sanctions was rechecked and manual approval preceded activation. The "deviation" comes from the case notion, not from behaviour.

**D7: Labels and migration artifacts.**

- **SUP-713.** The *procedure and UI* renamed Quality_Review to QA_Clearance, and audit documents show equivalent content. The later mapping approval recognizes the equivalence; it does not create it.
- **SUP-711.** The duplicate Register rows carry the same source transaction ID, so this is deduplication, not a double registration.

**D8: A trace deviation is not business misconduct.**

- **SUP-708.** No transactions occurred before sanctions clearance.
- **SUP-705.** The flood emergency still factually existed, the spend was EUR 12,000 (under the cap FD-7 would have applied), and Q and approval came the next business day.
- **SUP-714.** The supplier was fully eligible and LOW-risk, and it was processed in good faith during an expected transition.

None of these involved concealment, personal gain, or harm.

**Defense concessions (required and genuine):**

- **SUP-703 is indefensible.** An operator overrode ineligibility on a HIGH-risk foreign supplier and EUR 180,000 of spend followed. No reading of PD-3 covers it, and the same goes for the other 13 clearly ineligible cases.
- **SUP-712** is a real SoD breach if the aliases resolve correctly; delegation cannot cure one human acting on both sides.
- **SUP-708** is a genuine preventive-control failure. The absence of transactions mitigates harm, not the breach.
- **Informal practice cannot become policy.** Minutes, belief that approval is imminent, and operational habit cannot create a permanent auto-approval route. Expiry dates mean what they say.
- **A factual emergency without a written extension is an argument for mitigation or ratification, not authority.**
- **Nine cases with missing eligibility attributes cannot be presumed eligible.**
- **SUP-715's missing disclosure is a real gap in a stated duty.**

### 5.2 System-level judgment

**Operational compliance: materially noncompliant, but bounded and diagnosable.** The failures are not scattered; they cluster in four control-design weaknesses:

1. **Eligibility gate.** A free operator "pilot" selection with no system validation produced 14 clearly ineligible cases and 9 unverifiable ones, including one CRITICAL.
2. **Expiry enforcement.** Neither the PD-3 nor the FD-7 route was disabled at its end date: SUP-705, SUP-714 and the 6 August cases.
3. **Preventive sequencing.** Activation was possible before sanctions (SUP-708).
4. **Resource and COI controls.** These are not monitored at all (SUP-712, SUP-715; U5 for everything else).

Governance of PD-3 is itself deficient. A derogation from a controlled standard was run on minutes and an email, and its "monitored" condition was not effectively met. That is a finding against the steering committee and the Compliance function, not against operators processing eligible cases.

**Model validity: M0 is not valid as the sole conformance oracle.** Among the 15 cases, M0 flags 12 deviations. Only 4 of those (SUP-703, 705, 708, 714) are genuine noncompliance. Two are justified exceptions, four are model or scope defects, and two are data artifacts. Meanwhile 2 of the 5 noncompliant cases (SUP-712, 715) fit M0 perfectly.

So Operations is right about counts: most M0 deviations are artifacts. It is wrong about risk: the residual genuine deviations and the invisible resource violations include the most severe findings in the file. M0 should be retired as the compliance oracle and kept only as the standard-route component of the layered architecture in §6.

### 5.3 Where I reject each side

**Rejected prosecution arguments:**

- **Counting M0-only requirements as noncompliance** (SUP-706, 707, 709, 711, 713). This confuses a model with a norm (§1.1).
- **Treating in-window eligible PD-3 cases as violations.** The observer's contemporaneous written consent to a *monitored exception* is authority for a time-boxed exception. Her later statement denies a *weakening* of CS-9, which is a different act and is honoured by denying permanence. The stronger reading of her position defeats SUP-714, not SUP-702.
- **Treating missing post-migration notifications as non-occurrence.** The 29% figure matches the mirroring gap.
- **Treating SUP-710 as clear noncompliance.** CS-9's text does not settle whether reactivation is onboarding, and the prosecution cannot fill a normative gap by analogy.
- **Treating SUP-713's later mapping as fatal.** It is retroactive evidence of equivalence, not retroactive authorization.
- **Invalidating the 289 eligible cases because monitoring failed.** Failed monitoring is a pilot-level governance breach. It does not convert individually eligible, condition-satisfying cases into violations.

**Rejected defense arguments:**

- **"The emergency factually continued" (SUP-705).** FD-7 expired automatically by its own terms, and the power to extend it was never used.
- **"Approval was imminent" and normalization (SUP-714 and the six August cases).** An expected future authority is not authority.
- **"No transactions occurred" (SUP-708).** The control is preventive; the outcome only mitigates.
- **"The deviation rate is mostly obsolescence" as exculpation.** It is true by count and false by risk, and it ignores violations M0 cannot see.
- **"289/312 shows the pilot worked."** A 7.4% gate-failure rate that includes a CRITICAL case shows the gate did not work.
- **Presuming the 9 missing-attribute cases eligible.** The burden is on the party invoking the exception.
- **Treating desirability as grounds for a permanent auto-approval route.** That needs a CS-9 amendment.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The architecture separates *what may happen in what order* (control flow) from *when a route is admissible* (data and time), *who may do it* (resources), *who may authorize deviations* (governance) and *what counts as a case* (scope). Each layer references rule IDs, so any conformance verdict can be traced to a specific norm version.

### Layer 0: Norm registry and overlay governance (organizational control plus data)

**Norm registry.** Every norm is a versioned record: `rule_id`, `version`, `source_doc`, `authority_level`, `approver`, `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `status`. CS-9 is decomposed into atomic rules:

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| CS9-R1 | S before ACT |
| CS9-R2 | Q before ACT |
| CS9-R3 | MPA before ACT |
| CS9-R4 | Requester ≠ approver (different humans) |
| CS9-R5 | Abbreviated pre-screen allowed for rejected leads |

**Overlays.** A pilot or emergency is an **overlay**. It must:

- name the base rules it relaxes;
- name the controls it retains;
- add compensating obligations;
- define a machine-checkable scope predicate;
- carry a hard `effective_to`.

**Minimum authority.**

- A pilot requires a signed waiver from the CS-9 owner (Compliance). Minutes alone are insufficient; the waiver may cite the minutes.
- An emergency requires an issuing authority named in a standing emergency procedure.
- Extensions must be written and issued *before* expiry.
- Ratification after the fact is recorded as `RATIFIED_DEVIATION`. It is never recast as compliance.

**System enforcement.** The workflow engine reads the registry. The Auto_Approve and provisional-activation buttons exist only while an overlay is active *and* its scope predicate is true on the frozen eligibility snapshot. An operator override requires a second, independent approver and creates an `override_id`.

**Sunset.** Each overlay's owner files a review before `effective_to`. After expiry the overlay can only be revived as a new, separately approved version; it cannot silently roll over.

### Layer 1: Scope and case-notion rules

A case is identified by (`supplier_id`, `onboarding_episode_id`) and has a `case_type`:

| case_type | Starts with | Must include | Scored against |
|---|---|---|---|
| NEW_ONBOARDING | Register_Supplier | Standard, overlay or reject route | M1 plus all CS-9 rules |
| PRESCREEN_LEAD | Register_Supplier | S → Reject_Supplier with `rejection_basis=SANCTIONS_PRESCREEN` (Q optional per CS9-R5) | M1 reject route. Reported separately from onboarding KPIs, so pre-screen volume does not distort conformance rates. |
| REACTIVATION | Request_Reactivation (`prior_case_id` mandatory) | Fresh S; MPA; Q in `mode=FULL` or `mode=CERT_REVALIDATION` | M1 plus CS-9. **Interim rule:** a reactivation needs a Q event; certificate revalidation counts only if the certificate is valid on the activation date and a reviewer records it. A permanent rule needs a CS-9 amendment (proposed REACT-1). |
| WITHDRAWN | Register_Supplier or Request_Reactivation | Withdraw_Request before any ACT; Notify | M1 withdraw route. Withdrawal after activation is a deactivation, a separate process out of scope. |

Scope resolution for the SUP-709 dispute: include rejected leads in the lifecycle model (the process owner's view), because excluding them would let a case escape scrutiny by being labelled a "lead". Report them in a separate stratum (addressing the model owner's concern).

### Layer 2: Control flow (POWL, revised model M1)

The skeleton is: entry XOR(Register_Supplier, Request_Reactivation) → XOR of five routes → Close_Case. Notify_Requester sits inside every route, so it always precedes Close_Case.

| Route | Structure |
|---|---|
| STANDARD | {S ∥ Q ∥ RMI-loop} → MPA → ACT → Notify |
| AUTO (overlay-gated) | {S ∥ Validate_Master_Data ∥ RMI-loop} → Auto_Approve → ACT → Notify. Q is unordered relative to ACT; its deadline is a data rule. |
| EMERGENCY (overlay-gated) | S → ACT → {Q ∥ retrospective MPA ∥ Notify} |
| REJECT | {S ∥ optional Q ∥ RMI-loop} → Reject → Notify |
| WITHDRAW | {optional S ∥ optional Q ∥ RMI-loop} → Withdraw_Request → Notify |

POWL is used here only for what it expresses well: ordering, concurrency, choice and repetition.

### Layer 3: Declarative constraints (DECLARE / log-skeleton, including metric-time MP-Declare)

These cross-route invariants hold regardless of how the POWL choice resolves:

**Preventive ordering and existence.**

- `precedence(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier)` on every route (CS9-R1).
- `precedence(Quality_Review, Activate_Supplier)` unless `activation_mode ∈ {PILOT_PROVISIONAL, EMERGENCY_PROVISIONAL}` (CS9-R2).
- `precedence({Manual_Procurement_Approval, Auto_Approve}, Activate_Supplier)` unless `activation_mode = EMERGENCY_PROVISIONAL` (CS9-R3).
- `exactly1(Activate_Supplier)` on activation routes; `absence(Activate_Supplier)` on reject and withdraw routes.

**Deadlines after provisional activation.**

- `response(Activate_Supplier, Quality_Review, ≤ 2 business days)` when activation is provisional (PD3-QR, FD7-QR).
- `response(Activate_Supplier, Manual_Procurement_Approval[retrospective])` when `activation_mode = EMERGENCY_PROVISIONAL`, with deadline FD7-RA. FD-7 set no deadline (U9); the proposed default is 2 business days, pending the issuing authority.

**Information requests and notification.**

- `alternate_response(Request_More_Info, Receive_Requested_Info)`, and no decision event while a request is open. This is checked on request IDs because legacy logs lack the receive event.
- `precedence(Notify_Requester, Close_Case)` and `exactly1(Close_Case)`. Notification may be satisfied by a CRM record joined on a stable `notification_id`.

### Layer 4: Data checks (pandas)

**Eligibility.**

- PD-3 style eligibility on a *frozen* snapshot taken at the Auto_Approve timestamp: `risk_rating = LOW`, `country = DE`, `corporate_group_id` approved on that date, all fields non-null.
- A null field yields `EXCEPTION_NOT_ESTABLISHED`, never a pass.

**Time windows.**

- Overlay validity uses the immutable `event_ts` against `effective_from`/`effective_to` in the overlay's time zone.
- Business-day arithmetic uses a versioned holiday calendar.

**Spend and monitoring.**

- FD-7 cumulative spend: the sum of AP and PO commitments from ACT until the retrospective MPA must be ≤ EUR 20,000.
- One monitoring record per calendar day while provisional.

**Data hygiene.**

- Deduplicate on `source_txn_id`.
- Map labels through a versioned `label_map` with `valid_from`.
- Classify notification evidence as `LOG_EVENT`, `CRM_STABLE_ID`, `CRM_WEAK_LINK` (indeterminate) or `NONE`.

### Layer 5: Resource and conflict-of-interest rules

**SoD (CS9-R4).**

- `person_id(requester) ≠ person_id(approver)`, resolved through a versioned identity registry that maps every alias (logins, service accounts, email aliases) to a human. A delegation can transfer an approval right, but never to the requester.
- Proposed CS9-R4b, subject to policy approval: the Q performer should differ from the approver.

**COI.**

- If a relationship-register entry (spouse, partner or family, or a direct reporting line) links any two performers in the case, a `disclosure_id` dated before the later of the two events is required. Otherwise the case is `COI_UNDISCLOSED`.
- Proposed rule COI-2, not yet in force: a declared close relationship between the Q performer and the approver requires an independent second approver. No reviewer may impose this retroactively on SUP-715.

### Placement summary

| Concern | POWL | DECLARE / log skeleton | pandas / data | Organizational |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S ∥ Q; route choice; RMI repetition; Notify before Close | ✔ | (invariants duplicated) | | |
| Sanctions-before-activation across all routes | ✔ (per route) | ✔ (global) | | |
| Q within 2 business days; retrospective approval deadline | | ✔ (metric time) | ✔ (holiday calendar) | |
| Eligibility, amount caps, windows | | | ✔ | Snapshot freezing |
| SoD and COI | | (resource-aware DECLARE optional) | ✔ (identity/relationship joins) | Registers, disclosures |
| Authority, extension, ratification, sunset | | | Registry lookup | ✔ |
| Scope and case types | Entry XOR; route XOR | | `case_type` validation | Scope document |

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

### 7.1 Executable model and rule layer

The code builds M1 from fresh node objects. The same label may occur on several routes as distinct `Transition` objects. It converts M1 to a Petri net and aligns the 15 observed traces after the two deterministic repairs: deduplication by `source_txn_id` and the approved label map. It then evaluates the data-aware rules on facts taken only from the case table; `None` means not evidenced. It reads no external files. Synthetic timestamps carry order only.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Layer 2: revised control-flow skeleton M1 (POWL).
# Route admissibility (dates, risk, amount, authority) is NOT encoded here; see Layers 3-5.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

def T(label):
    return Transition(label=label)


def tau():
    return SilentTransition()


def xor(*children):
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=list(children))


def optional(node):
    return xor(node, tau())


def info_loop():
    # LOOP(do=tau, redo=Request_More_Info): zero or more information requests
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[tau(), T("Request_More_Info")])


def po(nodes, edges=()):
    p = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for a, b in edges:
        p.order.add_edge(a, b)
    return p

def business_days_after(start, end, holidays=frozenset()):
    """Business days from start (exclusive) to end (inclusive): Mon-Fri minus a versioned holiday set."""
    n, d = 0, start
    while d < end:
        d += timedelta(days=1)
        if d.weekday() < 5 and d not in holidays:
            n += 1
    return n


def active(overlay_id, ts):
    o = OVERLAYS[overlay_id]
    return o["from"] <= ts <= o["to"]


def verdict(rule, status, note=""):
    return {"rule": rule, "status": status, "note": note}

def check_sod(c, alias_registry):
    req, app = alias_registry.get(c["requester_alias"]), alias_registry.get(c["approver_alias"])
    if req is None or app is None:
        return [verdict("CS9-R4-SOD", "UNPROVEN", "alias not resolvable")]
    return [verdict("CS9-R4-SOD", "PASS" if req != app else "FAIL", f"{req} vs {app}")]


def check_coi(c):
    if not c.get("relationship"):
        return [verdict("COI-DISCLOSE", "PASS", "no registered relationship")]
    return [verdict("COI-DISCLOSE", "PASS" if c.get("disclosure_id") else "FAIL",
                    f"relationship={c['relationship']}")]


def check_notification(c):
    link = c.get("notification_link")
    if link in ("LOG_EVENT", "CRM_STABLE_ID"):
        return [verdict("NOTIFY-EVIDENCE", "PASS", link)]
    if link == "CRM_WEAK_LINK":
        return [verdict("NOTIFY-EVIDENCE", "UNPROVEN", "mutable text link entered after the fact")]
    return [verdict("NOTIFY-EVIDENCE", "FAIL", "no notification evidence")]

            print(f"{cid:16s} {v['rule']:22s} {v['status']:9s} {v['note']}")
```

**What running the code produced.** It was run against PM4Py 2.7. M1 converts to a workflow net that passes the soundness check.

- **Fit M1 with no deviations:** SUP-701, 704, 705, 707, 709, 711 (after deduplication), 712, 713 (after label mapping) and 715.
- **SUP-702:** missing Validate_Master_Data and, in the log, Notify. The rule layer then clears Notify via the CRM stable ID; master-data validation stays UNPROVEN.
- **SUP-703 and SUP-714:** missing Validate_Master_Data only.
- **SUP-706:** missing Notify.

This is by design. Control-flow fitness for SUP-704/705 and 712/715 is correct, because their verdicts belong to the time, authority and resource layers.

**Stated limitations and approximations:**

1. **Route admissibility is not a POWL concept.** M1's XOR lets *any* case take the auto-approval or emergency route. Admissibility is decided in Layer 4 against the overlay registry. Including a route in M1 is capability, not authorization. The auto route is currently *inactive*: PD-3 expired and CS9-A1 is only proposed.
2. **Non-local dependencies are handled by duplication.** The dependency between route choice and later obligations (e.g. retrospective Q only on provisional routes) is expressed by giving each route its own copies of S, Q, MPA and Notify. A shared post-activation block with its own XOR would let a pilot case "choose" to skip Q.
3. **The RMI loop has no receive event.** The loop is LOOP(τ, Request_More_Info) placed concurrently with the checks. It does not model the Receive_Requested_Info event, because legacy logs lack it. "No decision while a request is open" is checked on request IDs.
4. **Withdrawal is an approximation.** It is a route with optional checks, not a true cancellation region; POWL cannot express "abort at any point before activation" directly.
5. **Metric time is outside POWL.** Deadlines, spend caps and daily monitoring are Layer 3/4 checks; the date-only window check in the code must use full event timestamps in production.
