# 1. Executive Memo

**To:** Telecom Executive Leadership Team
**From:** Process Optimization & Decision Support
**Date:** [Insert Date]
**Subject:** Strategic Recommendation on Fiber-Installation Redesign R

**Key Decision:** Adopt a **hybrid approach (Alternative E)**—scale Redesign R *only* for simple, low-risk houses while implementing targeted controls for apartments and older buildings, and invest in immediate measurement to validate segment-specific risks before broader rollout. **Pause** the survey-skipping rule and post-billing activation for all other segments, and **run a controlled experiment (M1)** to refine the building-risk predictive model.

### **Why This Decision?**
Redesign R delivers **clear wins for simple houses** (34% of cases), reducing median cycle time by 6 days, improving first-time-right rates, and cutting visible costs—all while simplifying handovers. However, the **worsened tail duration, rework, and compliance risks** (e.g., billing before activation) disproportionately harm apartments, older buildings, and business customers. These segments face **higher complaint rates (+5–7pp), second visits (+7pp), and manual activations (+8pp)**, eroding trust and increasing downstream costs.

The **conflicting metrics** reflect a **segment-specific trade-off**: fewer handovers improve accountability for straightforward cases but **reduce resilience** when exceptions arise (e.g., coordinator absences, permit issues). The process-mining artifacts—**high fitness but low precision**—confirm that the redesign fits observed behavior *too loosely*, allowing unsafe paths (e.g., skipped permits, premature billing). Meanwhile, **incomplete cost data** (missing complaint handling after week 4) and **high coordinator utilization (93%)** suggest scalability risks.

### **Trade-Offs and Rationale**
1. **Avoid National Scaling (Alternative A):**
   - Risks amplifying tail duration and rework nationally, harming customer experience and operational stability.
   - Premature scaling could lock in compliance violations (e.g., billing before activation) and regulatory exposure.

2. **Avoid Full Rollback (Alternative B):**
   - Sacrifices proven benefits for simple houses (faster, cheaper, fewer handovers).
   - Ignores that the coordinator-ownership model *reduced handovers by 22pp*—a structural improvement worth preserving.

3. **Targeted Experimentation (Alternative D) vs. Hybrid (E):**
   - A pure experiment (D) delays benefits for simple houses and risks losing momentum.
   - The hybrid approach **captures low-risk wins immediately** while containing risks to high-risk segments.

### **Immediate Actions**
- **Scale:** Deploy Redesign R *only* for simple, low-risk houses (34% of cases), retaining coordinator ownership and merged validation/scheduling.
- **Pause:** Suspend survey-skipping and post-billing activation for all other segments (66% of cases).
- **Measure:** Launch **M1 (randomized survey-skipping experiment)** and **M4 (60-day downstream cost tracking)** within 4 weeks.
- **Monitor:** Implement **M7 (conformance checks for billing before activation and missing permits)** in real time.
- **Threshold for Broader Rollout:** Expand to apartments/old buildings only if:
  - Tail duration (p90) improves by 15% in experiments.
  - First-time-right rates recover to 80%.
  - Downstream costs (including complaints) fall below baseline.

**Next Steps:** Approve this plan within 1 week to avoid further drift in pilot regions. Allocate resources for M1/M4 and assign a cross-functional team (Operations, Customer Service, Compliance) to oversee segment-specific controls.

# 3. Explanation of Metric Conflicts

1. **High Fitness but Low Precision:**
   - *Fitness* measures how well the discovered model explains observed behavior (high = most cases fit the model).
   - *Precision* measures how much the model *overgeneralizes* (low = model allows too many unrealistic paths).
   - **Conflict:** Redesign R simplified the process (e.g., merged tasks, skipped surveys), so the model fits the *observed* cases well (high fitness). However, it also allows *unsafe* paths (e.g., billing before activation, skipped permits) that didn’t occur in the baseline, lowering precision.

2. **Improved Median but Worse Tail Duration:**
   - **Median improvement:** Coordinator ownership and merged tasks streamlined simple cases (e.g., low-risk houses), reducing median duration.
   - **Tail deterioration:** Exceptions (e.g., apartments, permit issues) now face **longer delays** due to:
     - Survey-skipping leading to second visits.
     - High coordinator utilization causing rescheduling bottlenecks.
     - Missing permit checks creating rework.

3. **Fewer Handovers but Lower Resilience:**
   - **Fewer handovers:** Single coordinator ownership reduced context-switching delays.
   - **Lower resilience:** When coordinators were absent or overloaded, cases stalled because **no backup context** existed (e.g., no shared team ownership). The process became *less adaptable* to exceptions.

4. **Lower Visible Cost but Higher Rework/Downstream Cost:**
   - **Visible cost (EUR 184  EUR 161):** Fewer handovers and skipped surveys reduced labor costs.
   - **Hidden costs:** Second visits, manual activations, and complaints **increased**, offsetting savings. The EUR 207 estimate is likely *understated* (cost data incomplete after week 4).

5. **Improvement for Simple Houses but Deterioration for Apartments/Old Buildings:**
   - **Simple houses:** Merged tasks and coordinator ownership worked well for predictable cases.
   - **Apartments/old buildings:** Survey-skipping and permit omissions **backfired**, requiring rework. Business customers faced **billing disputes** from post-billing activation.
