# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

**Increase concern:**

- Authenticated source logs show the same human performed both prohibited actions, with no valid authorization or independent compensating control; actor IDs agree across ERP, access and approval records.
- A changed V-77x bank account received payment soon after the change, but the vendor denies the account, independent callback is absent or failed, or the maintenance ticket does not cover that account or date.
- Approval comments, access logs or version histories show no substantive review despite recorded approval, or an override and justification were actually completed by the same human or after the deadline.
- Cases repeatedly bypass normal queues or controls after accounting for urgency, amount, category, regional staffing and source system; unsupported manual edits or unexplained changes to audit logs corroborate the pattern.
- Vendor ownership, beneficiary, bank-account, address or access records reveal an undisclosed connection relevant to a transaction, corroborated by independent records.

**Reduce concern:**

- Original ERP, identity and bot logs demonstrate separate authenticated humans, or show that apparent same-person matches arose from the shared account, bot mapping or migration.
- Approved platform-migration documentation links each relevant V-77x change to verified old and new accounts, independent vendor confirmation and the subsequent payment destination.
- Contemporaneous emergency tickets, delegated authority and review records support urgent routing, while original justifications show a different human acted within the applicable three working days.
- Substantive correction histories explain loops, and matched comparison groups show similar timing and pair rates under the same urgency, workload, category and staffing conditions.
- Reconciled source and upload timestamps show that apparent lateness or proximity came from batch entry, while independent review occurred at the recorded original time.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Our review identified potential control exceptions and patterns requiring follow-up in the procurement-to-payment event log. The observations do not establish misconduct or collusion. Repeated working relationships can reflect team assignments and specialization, and short approval intervals can reflect legitimate emergency procedures.

The most concerning subgroup involves vendor-master and accounts-payable activity associated with VendorMaster_Lena and AP_Kim. We recommend tracing the affected changes through independent vendor confirmation, maintenance tickets, approval records and actual payment destinations.

Emergency medical supply and weekend repair cases show rapid approvals, recurring clarification loops and apparent segregation exceptions. These may reflect legitimate urgent handling, but the applicable approval authority and independent, timely retrospective justification should be checked against original records. The 17 apparent same-person override and justification cases also warrant review; a recorded control exception is not, on its own, evidence of misconduct.

The log has material attribution and timing limitations. Shared approval accounts, spreadsheet migration, missing maintenance-ticket links and legacy bot-to-human mappings may distort both actors and timestamps. Audit should reconcile these records with authenticated source logs and original documents, classify unresolved cases separately, and report confirmed exceptions with their scope and operational context.
