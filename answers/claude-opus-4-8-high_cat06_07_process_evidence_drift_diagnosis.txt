## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

**Jan–Mar (baseline).** Stable regime: AUROC ~0.81–0.82, Brier ~0.13, ECE ~0.02–0.025, mean predicted risk ≈ observed breach rate (~0.18–0.19), 100% maturity, negligible unknown/missing. Variant mix steady (V1 45 / V2 35 / V3 20). Matured per-variant breach rates are the reference bands: V1 ~0.15–0.16, V2 ~0.10–0.11, V3 ~0.30–0.32. This is the "known-good" anchor for all later comparisons.

**April (schema/pipeline incident — not real drift).** The 1 Apr migration renamed the diagnosis activity; the pipeline recognized only the old label, so prediction prefixes were built at the wrong event or not built, plus duplicates and `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`. Symptoms are the fingerprints of a *data* failure, not a *world* change: unknown-activity 17%, missing-queue-feature 14%, unmapped variant 18%, AUROC 0.67, ECE 0.110, and mean predicted risk 0.28 while the *true* outcome (matured breach by variant) stays near baseline. April should be read as an ingestion/feature-construction outage.

**May (partial recovery, residual pipeline debt).** The 6 May mapping patch backfilled most records: unknown-activity 2.0%, missing-queue 3%, unmapped 1%, AUROC 0.78, ECE 0.050. But not all derived features were recomputed (the cached "events-before-diagnosis" for April), so metrics are close to but not fully back to baseline. May = convalescence from the April incident, not a new phenomenon.

**June (benign process/routing mix change).** The 1 Jun remote-routing policy pushed V2 from ~34% to 52%. Because V2 is the fast, low-breach path, the *overall* observed breach rate falls to 0.15 purely by composition. Discrimination/calibration are near baseline (AUROC 0.79, ECE 0.060). This is genuine **process drift** in the *mix*, but it does **not** imply concept drift — per-path outcome relationships are unchanged. Treating June as "improvement" or as failure would both be wrong.

**July (onset of supplier-driven degradation).** The 10 Jul supplier miss appears on the `Order Part→Part Received` edge (42h→65h). V3 matured breach rate ticks up (0.35→0.46). Overall metrics only slightly soft (AUROC 0.76) because V3 is still a minority and the effect is early. First real-world signal that the parts path is decoupling from the model's expectations.

**August–September (overlapping performance, process, concept, and population shifts).** Several distinct things co-occur and must be separated:
- Performance/process: part-edge latency becomes severe (130h Aug, 148h Sep); V3 share climbs (35%→42%).
- Concept: conditional on V3, breach rate jumps to 0.64/0.61 — the outcome relationship for the *same path* changed because the driving variable (part wait) is invisible to the model → under-prediction (mean risk ~0.19–0.21 vs reality). 
- Population: from 1 Sep, Partner Z (24% of cases) adds rural/older-device cases routed disproportionately (58%) through V3, with higher observed breach (0.45), i.e., **covariate + population shift** layered on top.
- Maturity begins to bite (Aug 93%, Sep 82%; delay 38→42d), so the Aug–Sep observed numbers already need maturity correction. August/September are the genuinely hard, multi-cause months.

**October (dominated by label maturity/censoring — do not over-read).** Maturity collapses to 55% and label delay to 52d, and the backlog hits long resolution paths hardest. The observed breach rate paradoxically *drops* to 0.16 even though the underlying process is worse than ever — a textbook censoring artifact (the slow, breach-prone cases simply haven't returned labels). October's AUROC/Brier/ECE and breach rate are **not comparable** to mature months and must not drive any performance verdict or retraining. Underlying Sep drivers (supplier, Partner Z, V3) almost certainly persist, but October cannot confirm their magnitude yet.

The point of the narrative: **April = data/schema; June = benign routing; Jul = onset; Aug–Sep = layered real drift (process + concept + population/covariate); Oct = measurement artifact.** Collapsing these into one "the model degraded" story would misdiagnose four different mechanisms with four different remedies.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

For each test I give the setup and the expected result under competing hypotheses (H).

**(a) Label-maturity curves + fixed-vintage backtesting.** Build, per start-month, the cumulative fraction of labels realized as a function of days-since-start; then recompute all metrics on a *fixed vintage* (e.g., only cases with ≥60d maturity, using labels as of a frozen cutoff).
- H = label delay/censoring drives Oct (and part of Sep): fixed-vintage metrics for Oct/late-Sep move substantially (observed breach rises toward the process-implied level; AUROC/Brier stabilize).
- H = real degradation independent of maturity: fixed-vintage metrics stay degraded (V3 under-prediction persists) even on fully matured cohorts.

**(b) Recomputation after activity-label normalization + duplicate cleaning.** Re-run April–May from raw with `Diagnostic Assessment Completed`↔`Initial Diagnosis Completed` unified, `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` resolved, and dedup applied to *both* sources; rebuild prefixes and the "events-before-diagnosis" feature.
- H = April is pipeline failure: recomputed April metrics recover toward baseline (unknown share ~0, AUROC back to ~0.8), confirming a data incident.
- H = April had real drift too: metrics stay depressed after clean recompute (unlikely given matured-by-variant April looks normal).

**(c) Calibration + discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month.** Compute AUROC, Brier, ECE, reliability curves separately for V1/V2/V3, Partner Z vs others, each month (matured cohorts only).
- H = concept drift localized to V3: V1/V2 calibration stays good while V3 shows systematic under-prediction growing Jul→Sep.
- H = global model failure: all variants degrade together (not seen — argues against global concept drift).

**(d) Process-variant + DFG / performance-DFG comparison.** Compare `discover_dfg` variant frequencies and `discover_performance_dfg` edge times month over month, focusing on `Order Part→Part Received` and `diagnosis→Remote Fix`.
- H = supplier-driven process drift: part-edge latency rises sharply (42→155h) while remote edge is flat — degradation isolated to the parts path.
- H = generalized slowdown/migration artifact: many edges shift together or new spurious edges appear (seen in April as unmapped variants, not in Aug–Oct).

**(e) Test whether outcome-given-path changed (concept-drift test).** Fit/compare a simple conditional breach model P(breach | variant, prefix) on Jan–Jun vs Jul–Sep (matured), same features.
- H = concept drift: P(breach | V3, same path) is significantly higher in Jul–Sep than Jan–Jun.
- H = pure composition/covariate: conditional-on-path relationship is stable; the shift is only in the *mix* of paths/covariates.

**(f) Population reweighting / transport checks for Partner Z.** Reweight (importance weighting / covariate-shift correction on travel distance, device age, variant) so Partner-Z-inclusive months match the training population; recompute metrics. Also compare Partner Z vs matched non-Partner-Z cases.
- H = degradation is population/covariate shift: after reweighting to the old population, metrics recover much of the loss.
- H = genuine concept drift or unfair model behavior: reweighting does *not* restore performance; residual gap remains, and it is explained by observable features (travel/age/part-wait) rather than by protected-group membership per se.

**(g) Feature missingness + lineage audit.** Trace `missing_queue_feature` and "events-before-diagnosis" provenance across April backfill; quantify missingness by month/source; check whether backfilled-but-not-recomputed derived features exist.
- H = pipeline/lineage debt: missingness concentrated in April/May and in specific derived features; fixing lineage removes it.
- H = new features simply absent for Partner Z (structural, not a bug): missingness is systematic for the new segment and requires model change, not a backfill.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Stage 0 — Immediate containment (days).**
- Quarantine April scores; attach data-quality flags to any month with unknown-activity or missing-feature spikes.
- Suppress October (and low-maturity late-September) from all *performance* reporting; report only fixed-vintage matured metrics.
- Add a guardrail on V3/parts-path cases (e.g., manual review or a conservative risk floor tied to observed part-wait) since the model demonstrably under-predicts V3 breaches.

**Stage 1 — Short-term recalibration (1–2 weeks).**
- Recalibrate on **matured** data only, stratified by variant and source. Global recalibration is insufficient because the miscalibration is concentrated in V3 and Partner Z; a single scalar/Platt/isotonic fit would smear a V3-specific bias across V1/V2.
- Do **not** recalibrate using April (pipeline bug) or October (censoring). Recalibrating around either would bake an artifact into the model.

**Stage 2 — Feature repair (parallel, 2–4 weeks).**
- Fix lineage: recompute all derived features for April–May; unify activity labels upstream; make dedup source-agnostic.
- Add the covariates that now carry signal: part-wait / `Order Part→Part Received` duration (or a live parts-SLA feature), travel distance, device age. Much of the Jul–Sep "concept drift" is really an omitted-variable problem that feature repair can convert back into a well-specified prediction.

**Stage 3 — Full retraining (only after labels + pipeline are clean).**
- **Safe for training:** Jan, Feb, Mar, and **recomputed** May–Jul (after label normalization/dedup and once maturity ≥ chosen threshold). June is safe (benign routing shift) once treated as normal variation.
- **Unsafe until fixed:** **April** (schema/feature corruption) — exclude or use only after verified clean recompute. **October** and immature **September** (censored labels) — exclude until matured; using them teaches the model the *inverse* of reality (Oct's deflated breach rate).
- **August** is borderline: usable for training only on its matured subset with maturity weighting.
- Retrain with maturity-aware sampling (survival-style or fixed-vintage labels) and the repaired feature set.

**Segment/variant-specific model?** Justified in a limited form. A single global retrain with the new covariates is preferred first, because the drift is largely explained by omitted variables (part-wait) and population mix that features can capture. A dedicated **V3/parts-path calibration head** (or segment-specific calibration for Partner Z) is warranted because the residual bias is concentrated there and matures slowly. Fully separate V2/V1 models are not justified — those paths remain stable.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

Layered monitors, each emitting a typed signal; **alerts have dependencies so a label-delay alarm gates (not triggers) retraining.**

- **Predictive monitor:** rolling AUROC/PR-AUC on matured, fixed-vintage cohorts, stratified by variant/source. Fires only on matured data.
- **Calibration monitor:** ECE, reliability curves, mean-predicted vs observed, per variant/source. Detects V3-style under-prediction.
- **Label-maturity monitor (gatekeeper):** per-start-month maturity %, median label delay, backlog age from the outcome API. Publishes a `maturity_ok` flag per cohort.
- **Data-quality monitor:** unknown-activity share, missing-feature rate, unmapped-variant share, duplicate rate, schema/label-name changes. Publishes `data_quality_ok`.
- **Process-variant monitor:** variant mix (V1/V2/V3/unmapped) drift vs baseline; flags routing changes (e.g., June, Aug–Sep V3 rise).
- **Performance-DFG monitor:** median/percentile edge times, especially `Order Part→Part Received` and `diagnosis→Remote Fix`; alerts on SLA-breaking latency (July onset).
- **Population monitor:** source mix (Partner Z share), covariate distributions (travel distance, device age); PSI/covariate-shift stats.
- **Intervention/deployment monitor:** log policy/onboarding/migration events (1 Apr migration, 1 Jun routing, 1 Sep Partner Z) so metric shifts are attributed to known changes, not mystery drift.

**Alert dependency logic (to avoid needless retraining):**
1. Any performance/calibration alarm is first checked against `maturity_ok`. If maturity is low → classify as **"deferred: awaiting label maturity,"** do **not** page for retraining.
2. Then check `data_quality_ok`. If data quality is bad (April-type) → route to **pipeline incident**, not model retrain.
3. Only if maturity is OK **and** data quality is OK **and** the degradation persists on fixed-vintage cohorts do we escalate. Even then, branch: if process-DFG/population monitors explain it → feature repair/recalibration first; **full retraining is the last resort**, requiring matured labels and clean pipeline.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

RENAME_MAP = {
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed",
    # UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY intentionally left as-is so it stays visible in DQ audits
}

def load_and_prepare(path="field_service_events.csv"):
    df = pd.read_csv(path)
    # Normalize the renamed diagnosis label BEFORE any feature/variant extraction.
    df["activity"] = df["activity"].replace(RENAME_MAP)
    df["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["timestamp"], utc=True, errors="coerce")

    # Duplicate handling: exact-duplicate events (same case/activity/timestamp)
    # arose from the migration. Drop exact dups; keep a count for auditing.
    before = len(df)
    df = df.sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp"])
    df = df.drop_duplicates(subset=["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"], keep="first")
    dupes_removed = before - len(df)

    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp"
    )
    return log, dupes_removed

def month_bounds(year, month):
    start = pd.Timestamp(year=year, month=month, day=1, tz="UTC")
    end = (start + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1))
    return start.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"), end.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S")

def monthly_summary(log, year, month):
    lo, hi = month_bounds(year, month)

    # Cohort A: EVENT-CALENDAR — any event occurring in the month.
    # Interpretation: reflects operational activity/throughput in-month,
    # but mixes cases started earlier (biased for start-conditioned metrics).
    event_cohort = pm4py.filter_time_range(log, lo, hi, mode="events")

    # Cohort B: CASE-START — cases whose FIRST event falls in the month.
    # Interpretation: the correct denominator for start-conditioned prediction
    # and for label-maturity accounting; comparable across months.
    start_cohort = pm4py.filter_time_range(log, lo, hi, mode="traces_contained")

    dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(start_cohort)
    perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(start_cohort)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(start_cohort)

    # Temporal features for auditability (durations between events), NOT model fitting.
    temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(start_cohort)

    part_edge = ("Order Part", "Part Received")
    remote_edge = ("Initial Diagnosis Completed", "Remote Fix")

    return {
        "month": f"{year}-{month:02d}",
        "n_cases_start_cohort": start_cohort["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
        "n_events_calendar_cohort": len(event_cohort),
        "n_variants": len(variants),
        "unknown_activity_events": int(
            (start_cohort["concept:name"] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").sum()
        ),
        "perf_order_to_part_received": perf_dfg.get(part_edge),
        "perf_diag_to_remote_fix": perf_dfg.get(remote_edge),
        "dfg_edges": len(dfg),
        "temporal_rows": len(temporal),
    }

if __name__ == "__main__":
    log, dupes = load_and_prepare()
    rows = [monthly_summary(log, 2026, m) for m in range(1, 11)]
    summary = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    summary["dupes_removed_total"] = dupes
    # Auditable monthly process-evidence table (no model is trained here).
    print(summary.to_string(index=False))
```

Note: PM4Py has no built-in "concept-drift detector"; the code deliberately produces auditable monthly process evidence (variant mix, DFG/performance-DFG edges, unknown-activity counts, dual cohorts) that a human/analyst combines with the maturity and calibration monitors above.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

1. **Retraining on immature labels (October/late-September).** Because censoring hides slow, breach-prone long-path cases, October's observed breach rate is deflated (0.16). Retraining on it teaches the model that breaches are *less* likely exactly when they are becoming *more* likely, and would suppress risk on V3/parts cases — the opposite of what's needed. *Mitigation:* train only on fixed-vintage matured cohorts; use survival/maturity-aware labeling; gate any retrain on the label-maturity monitor.

2. **Calibrating around the April pipeline bug.** If April's inflated predicted risk and ECE (0.110) are treated as a calibration problem, a recalibration fit would compensate for a *data* defect (wrong prefixes, missing features), corrupting the model for correct future data. *Mitigation:* route April via the data-quality branch; recompute features after label normalization + dedup; exclude April from any calibration/training until the clean recompute matches baseline.

3. **Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift (Jul–Sep V3).** If Aug–Sep degradation is dismissed as "just maturity" or "just mix," the real, persistent under-prediction on V3 (matured breach 0.64) goes unaddressed and customers keep getting missed breach warnings on parts cases. *Mitigation:* confirm with the conditional-outcome test (e) on matured data; add part-wait/parts-SLA features; recalibrate/retrain the V3 segment; monitor the `Order Part→Part Received` edge.

4. **Attributing Partner Z's population shift to unfair model behavior without stratified evidence.** Partner Z's high observed breach (0.45) is confounded by slower label maturity, heavier V3 routing, and missing covariates (rural travel, device age). Declaring the model "biased against Partner Z" and applying a group-level fix could mask the true drivers and introduce new distortions. *Mitigation:* stratify by source on matured cohorts; run reweighting/transport checks (f); explain residual gaps via observable features before any fairness intervention; add the missing covariates rather than patching outputs.

5. (Bonus) **Treating June's benign routing shift as a defect.** Retraining or "fixing" the model because the overall breach rate dropped in June would chase a composition effect. *Mitigation:* the process-variant and intervention monitors attribute June to the 1 Jun policy; verify per-path calibration is intact before acting.
