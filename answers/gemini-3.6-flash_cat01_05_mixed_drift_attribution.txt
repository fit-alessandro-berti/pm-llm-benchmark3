## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The raw-versus-mapped fitness gap ($\Delta \approx 0.35$ to $0.38$) measures syntactic label mutation and event log schema noise, rather than operational non-compliance. When `raw_model_fitness` collapsed in March 2026 while `mapped_model_fitness` remained high ($0.90$), it proved that the underlying sequential execution of business tasks remained compliant with normative logic. However, restoring conformance via label mapping (`Eligibility Screen` $\rightarrow$ `Validate Eligibility`) and event deduplication (`Open Case`) does not imply the business process was unchanged. A label mapping resolves semantic mapping failure, but cannot detect underlying behavioral, performance, or resource changes. 

Duplicated technical events inflate trace lengths and corrupt directly-follows graphs (DFG), artificially creating loops or parallel splits in mined models. Missing resource identifiers ($>6\%$ post-migration) severely impair resource-centric analyses, preventing accurate social network mining, individual work-allocation tracking, and queue waiting-time attribution across teams.

Crucially, the post-May process degradation cannot be dismissed as logging drift. While March logging artifacts were purely syntactic, the post-May changes exhibit real behavioral and operational drift: rework rates doubled (`Request Info` rising from 12% to 29%), P90 cycle time nearly doubled (67h to 121h), and formal routing to `Senior Assessment` quadrupled (4% to 22%). Because mapped model fitness remained high while cycle times and DFG relation rates severely deteriorated, the post-May drift reflects genuine external disruptions (Supplier Z), capacity loss (assessor departures), and seasonal volume surges, rather than system logging anomalies.

Distance Measure: L1 Manhattan Distance on normalized edge frequencies (per 100 cases) across the 7 tracked relations ($E = 7$). Omitted edges are assumed unchanged at 0 relative shift.

### 5. Global vs. Mechanism-Specific Change Points

The empirical evidence strongly rejects a single global change point. The process experienced multiple mechanism-specific change points across distinct functional axes. Logging drift changed abruptly on March 1, but left business control-flow intact. Behavioral control-flow drift changed abruptly on May 5 (Supplier Z) and October 1 (Seasonality). Performance queueing drift changed continuously starting June 15 due to staffing deficits, modulated by the July 1 priority dispatch policy. Treating these events as a single global change point would obscure causal attribution and lead to inappropriate operational remediation.

### Seasonality and Multiple Testing Handling
- **Seasonality Handling**: Reference windows for population and DFG monitors use historical Year-Over-Year (YoY) seasonal baselines (e.g., comparing October against prior October cycles) rather than trailing 30-day windows. Dampened seasonal scaling factors are applied to volume and high-complexity threshold triggers during Q4.
- **Multiple Testing Control**: To prevent false-positive alerts across 10 simultaneous monitors, alert thresholds apply a False Discovery Rate (FDR) control using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure at $\alpha = 0.05$. Alerts require a sustained signal across a minimum 3-day trailing window before escalating to management intervention.

    df_formatted = dataframe_utils.convert_timestamp_columns_in_df(df_formatted)
    df_formatted = df_formatted.rename(columns={
        case_id_col: 'case:concept:name',
        activity_col: 'concept:name',
        timestamp_col: 'time:timestamp'
    })
    return df_formatted

def slice_time_window(df_log: pd.DataFrame, start_date: str, end_date: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Slices event log within specified time window."""
    filtered_df = pm4py.filter_time_range(
        df_log, 
        dt1=start_date, 
        dt2=end_date, 
        mode='events'
    )
    return filtered_df

def analyze_process_artifacts(df_log: pd.DataFrame):
    """Computes DFGs, variants, temporal profiles, and skeletons for an event log slice."""
    # Compute Directly-Follows Graph (DFG)
    dfg, start_act, end_act = pm4py.discover_dfg(df_log)
    
    # Compute Process Variants
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(df_log)
    
    # Compute Temporal Profile
    temporal_profile = pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(df_log)
    
    # Compute Log Skeleton
    try:
        log_skeleton = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(df_log)
    except AttributeError:
        log_skeleton = None
        
    return {
        'dfg': dfg,
        'start_activities': start_act,
        'end_activities': end_act,
        'variants_count': len(variants),
        'temporal_profile': temporal_profile,
        'log_skeleton': log_skeleton
    }

def compare_raw_vs_normalized_mapping(df_log: pd.DataFrame, mapping_dict: dict) -> dict:
    """Compares raw vs normalized activity mappings without overwriting original log."""
    df_raw = df_log.copy()
    df_mapped = df_log.copy()
    
    # Apply activity mapping to normalized copy
    df_mapped['concept:name'] = df_mapped['concept:name'].replace(mapping_dict)
    
    # Remove duplicated technical events (e.g. repeated 'Open Case')
    df_mapped = df_mapped.drop_duplicates(subset=['case:concept:name', 'concept:name', 'time:timestamp'])
    
    # Discover DFGs for comparison
    raw_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(df_raw)
    mapped_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(df_mapped)
    
    return {
        'raw_dfg_edge_count': len(raw_dfg),
        'mapped_dfg_edge_count': len(mapped_dfg),
        'raw_distinct_activities': df_raw['concept:name'].nunique(),
        'mapped_distinct_activities': df_mapped['concept:name'].nunique()
    }

## 9. Provisional attribution

The observed process drift across 2026 is driven by four interacting mechanisms rather than a single root cause. 

The **strongest attribution** belongs to the March 1 system migration logging artifact. The sudden drop in raw model fitness (0.92 to 0.55) coupled with stable mapped model fitness (0.90) and flat baseline cycle times (31.2h) conclusively proves that March performance deterioration was a syntactic reporting artifact (`Eligibility Screen` label mutation and `Open Case` duplication) rather than operational failure. 

Equally strong is the attribution of the May–August rework surge to Supplier Z’s API/staffing disruption. The doubling of rework variant share (12% to 27%) and `Request Info` DFG edge frequency (12 to 24 per 100 cases) tracks Supplier Z’s intake proportion ($r = 0.94$), resolving as Supplier Z recovered in September. 

The October–November cycle time peak (49.0h) is strongly attributed to seasonal population drift. Intake volume (+27%) and high-complexity cases (+114%) expanded concurrently, driving a quadrupling of formal `Senior Assessment` routing (4% to 22%).

Attributes that remain **only plausible** involve the exact breakdown between staffing loss and priority dispatch rules. While losing four assessors on June 15 created an unreplaced throughput deficit that built structural queues, the July 1 priority rule redistributed waiting times non-linearly. In aggregate monthly data, mediation between overall queuing accumulation and priority-induced waiting redistribution prevents clean isolation of whether priority rules improved high-complexity throughput at an acceptable cost to standard cases.

We would **revise this account** if event-level timestamp analysis reveals that:
1. Active handling time during `Assess` expanded significantly within fixed complexity tiers (indicating unobserved procedural complexity or system latency);
2. Non-Supplier Z channels experienced identical rework spikes in May (indicating an internal policy change rather than external vendor disruption); or
3. Disaggregated queue profiles show that priority dispatch rules exacerbated total workflow variance without reducing high-complexity cycle times.
