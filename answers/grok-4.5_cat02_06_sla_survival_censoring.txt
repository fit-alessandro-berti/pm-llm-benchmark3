# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

**Time origin.** \(t = 0\) is the `Submit` event for every case (the SLA clock start).

**Horizon.** Fixed administrative horizon \(L = 72\) hours of elapsed time from Submit.

**Primary event of operational success.** Reaching `Resolve` (internal resolution).

**Other terminal dispositions.** `Withdraw`, `Reject`, and `Transfer` end internal observation. `open` means no terminal disposition by the extract cutoff; `duration_or_age_h` is then age at cutoff, and `last_event_gap_h` is inactivity since the last recorded activity.

**Right censoring vs competing events vs already-observed breaches.**
- **Administrative right censoring:** case is still `open` at cutoff and age may be \(<\) or \(>\) 72h; the Resolve time is only known to exceed current age.
- **Competing events:** Withdraw, Reject (and, under some policies, Transfer) remove the case from the internal Resolve risk set; they are not the same as non-informative censoring.
- **Already-observed breach:** a case with Resolve time \(> 72\), or still open with age \(> 72\) without Resolve, has already demonstrated failure to resolve by the SLA horizon (for estimands that care about “resolved by 72h”).

**Assumption A1.** Elapsed time in `duration_or_age_h` is measured on the same clock as the 72h SLA (no business-hour adjustment). Needed because only elapsed hours are supplied.

---

### Estimand E1 — Net probability of not being resolved by 72h  
\[
\theta_{E1} = \Pr(T_{\text{Resolve}} > 72)
\]  
This is a **marginal** probability that ignores *why* resolution failed (still open, withdrawn, rejected, transferred, or resolved late). It answers: “What fraction of submitted cases fail the operational success clock by 72h?”

**Decision match:** executive SLA % reporting, capacity planning for “% resolved within SLA,” customer-facing success rate. It does **not** isolate preventable delay among cases that remain eligible for Resolve.

### Estimand E2 — Cause-specific / cumulative incidence of late or never-internal-resolve pathways  
Define the competing terminal cause \(D \in \{\text{Resolve}, \text{Withdraw}, \text{Reject}, \text{Transfer}, \text{AdminCensor}\}\).  
\[
\theta_{E2,r} = F_r(72) = \Pr(T \le 72,\ D = r)
\]  
especially \(F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)\) (CIF of timely resolve) and \(1 - F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)\) as the probability of not having resolved by 72h in the presence of competing exits. A related tail object is the **subdistribution** mass of Resolve after 72h among those who eventually resolve, or  
\[
\theta_{E2\text{-tail}} = \Pr(T_{\text{Resolve}} > 72,\ D=\text{Resolve}) + \Pr(\text{still open at }72)
\]  
depending on whether transfers are treated as competing.

**Decision match:** operations triage and root-cause SLA governance—how much of non-resolution is withdrawal/rejection (demand/quality), transfer (hand-off), vs delay while still in-house.

### Estimand E3 (supporting) — Probability of no terminal disposition at 72h  
\[
\theta_{E3} = \Pr(\text{no terminal event by }72) = \Pr(T_{\text{any terminal}} > 72)
\]  
**Decision match:** backlog and aging WIP alerts; staffing of cases still sitting in the internal system past SLA.

### Estimand E4 (supporting) — Cause-specific hazard of Resolve among cases still internally active  
\[
h_{\text{Resolve}}(t) = \lim_{\Delta\to 0}\frac{\Pr(t \le T < t+\Delta,\ D=\text{Resolve}\mid T\ge t)}{\Delta}
\]  
**Decision match:** process control—whether hazard of resolution stalls after Review/Escalate; feeds multi-state and Cox models with time-varying stage.

# 2. Event and censoring encoding

**Coding legend.**  
- \(\delta_R = 1\): Resolve observed.  
- Competing-risk cause \(D\): 0=censor/admin, 1=Resolve, 2=Withdraw, 3=Reject, 4=Transfer.  
- For binary E1 at horizon: \(Y_{E1}=1\{T_R>72\}\) with careful handling when \(T_R\) is censored before 72.

### Master mapping (primary encoding)

| observed_status | \(T\) used | \(\delta_R\) | \(D\) (primary) | Censoring rule (primary) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| resolved | duration_or_age_h | 1 | 1 | Not censored | If \(T\le72\): timely; if \(T>72\): late resolve (E1 breach, post-SLA CIF mass) |
| withdrawn | duration_or_age_h | 0 | 2 | Competing event at \(T\), not censored for CIF | Not non-informative censoring for E1 marginal |
| rejected | duration_or_age_h | 0 | 3 | Competing event | Same as withdraw |
| transferred | duration_or_age_h | 0 | 4 (primary) | See three assumptions below | Informative exit plausible |
| open | duration_or_age_h (age) | 0 | 0 | Right-censored at age for survival/CIF | If age>72, E1 breach already known |

### Transfers — at least three plausible assumptions

| ID | Assumption | Encoding | When defensible | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **TR-A** | Transfer = **competing terminal** (internal Resolve impossible / not credited) | \(D=4\), \(\delta_R=0\), event time = transfer time | SLA is *internal* resolve; external org not counted | Understates true customer resolution if externals often succeed quickly |
| **TR-B** | Transfer = **non-informative administrative censoring** | \(D=0\), censor at transfer | Transfer timing independent of residual Resolve hazard (rarely true) | Bias if slow/complex cases are preferentially transferred (informative censoring) |
| **TR-C** | Transfer = **worst-case / failure for internal SLA** (or latent Resolve \(>\) transfer time, and count as not resolved by 72 if transfer \(\le72\) or if transfer \(>72\) still open path) | For E1: if transfer time \(\le72\), count as not resolved by 72; if transfer \(>72\), already breached before transfer | Conservative SLA governance, hand-off treated as internal failure | Pessimistic; mixes hand-off policy with delay |
| **TR-D** (sensitivity) | Transfer = **delayed Resolve with unknown lag** — impute or bound external outcome | Needs external data (missing) | Only with follow-up feed | Not identifiable from extract alone |

**Primary recommendation:** TR-A for E2 operational CIF; report E1 under both TR-A/TR-C (aligned) vs TR-B (optimistic independence).

### Withdrawal / rejection sensitivities

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| **WR-1** | Competing risks (primary for E2) |
| **WR-2** | Exclude from cohort (“not eligible for resolve SLA”) — changes denominator; use only if policy says withdrawn/rejected cases are out of SLA scope |
| **WR-3** | Censor at withdraw/reject — generally **not** recommended (informative; underestimates non-resolution) |

### Open cases and horizon indicator construction

For **E1 known-by-72** pseudo-outcome:
- Resolve with \(T\le72\): \(Y=0\) (success).
- Resolve with \(T>72\): \(Y=1\).
- Open with age \(\ge72\): \(Y=1\) (known breach).
- Open with age \(<72\): **unknown** \(Y\) — remains censored for the survival curve; for a binary complete-case \(Y\) at 72 these must **not** be dropped naively nor coded as success; use IPCW, landmark, or survival estimators.
- Withdraw/Reject/Transfer at \(t^*\): under TR-A/WR-1, \(Y=1\) for “not resolved by 72” if we define resolve-only success; under WR-2 excluded.

### Sensitivity alternatives column (summary)

| Status | Primary | Alt-1 | Alt-2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| resolved | event R | — | — |
| withdrawn | compete W | exclude | censor (discouraged) |
| rejected | compete J | exclude | censor (discouraged) |
| transferred | compete X (TR-A) | censor (TR-B) | SLA fail (TR-C) |
| open | admin censor at age | if age>72 force E1=1 | gap-weighted informative-censoring models |

**Informative censoring concern.** Long `last_event_gap_h` on open cases (S11: 55h, S25: 48h, S26: 72h, S09: 31h) suggests stall or silent abandonment; treating these as ordinary independent censoring is questionable. Transfers after Escalate (S08, S21, S32) look like structured exits of hard cases.

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

**Naive approach to avoid:** keep only `observed_status == resolved`, then compute fraction with `duration  72` or mean duration among resolves.

### Survivor bias (conditioning on Resolve)
- Resolved cases in the extract: S01, S02, S03, S04, S07, S13, S16–S20, S27, S28 (and late S03=76, S13=110, S20=74, S28=88).
- Dropping S05/S14/S23 (reject), S06/S12/S22/S30 (withdraw), S08/S21/S32 (transfer), and all opens removes paths that **never** or **not yet** resolve.
- Among resolves only, SLA pass rate looks better than the submission-population rate: e.g. early easy paths S01 (18h), S16 (16h), S18 (12h) dominate the completed set, while S09/S11/S25/S26 sit aged past or near SLA without credit as failures if dropped.

### Already-open below vs above 72h (active risk set)
- **Below 72h open (still can meet SLA):** S15 (35h, gap 2), S10 (47h), S24 (67h), S29 (51h), S31 (23h). Dropping them as “incomplete” removes cases still entitled to contribute survival time and possible timely resolve; if one waits only for completion, short successful cases finish first (**length bias / slow-case under-representation**).
- **Above 72h open:** S09 (92h), S11 (81h), S25 (79h), S26 (104h). These are **known E1 breaches** even without Resolve. A completed-case analysis ignores them entirely—systematic underestimation of tail risk.

### Competing risks misread as “no problem”
- S05 rejected at 9h, S23 at 11h, S12 withdrawn at 15h: short durations if mistakenly averaged into “cycle time” look like good performance; they are **not** operational successes.
- S06 withdraw 27h, S22 44h, S30 70h: mid-horizon exits. KM that censors them as non-events **inflates** apparent Resolve probability (classic competing-risk bias).

### Informative transfer / censoring
- Transfers S08 (68h, A, P1, escalated), S21 (26h, B, P1), S32 (96h, B, P1)—all **P1 and escalated**. Censoring them as independent (TR-B) likely removes high-severity tail mass preferentially  optimistic Resolve survival for P1.
- Open escalated with huge gaps: S11 (A, P1, age 81, gap 55), S25 (B, P1, age 79, gap 48) — residual time to resolve is probably longer than for hot cases with gap 2–6h (S15, S29, S31). Independent censoring assumption is strained.

### Cohort imbalance and non-randomized policy
- Cohort B: automated-screening / escalation-policy change; **not randomized**, incomplete compliance.
- Prefixes show structural shift: B often `Submit>AutoCheck>...` (S16, S18, S19, …); A often `Submit>Screen>...` and AutoCheck later or absent.
- Naive pool of completed resolves confounds policy era with mix of priority, escalation, and path. B has several quick AutoCheck resolves (S16, S18) **and** transfers S21/S32 and opens S24–S26, S29, S31—completed-case B vs A contrasts are not causal ATEs.

### Concrete distortion sketch (illustrative counts, not a formal estimator)
- Internal resolves: 14 cases; among them durations >72: S03(76), S13(110), S20(74), S28(88)  4/14  29% late among completes.
- But known non-resolves by 72 from opens aged 72: S09, S11, S25, S26 (4 more), plus all withdraw/reject/transfer that never resolved — E1 population failure rate is **materially higher** than 29%. Late opens still below 72 can add more failures. **Hypothesis H1:** completed-case late rate understates submission-level SLA breach by a wide margin.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

| Method | Role here | Limitation on these data |
|---|---|---|
| **Kaplan–Meier** of time-to-Resolve, censoring open (+ optionally transfer) | Quick marginal survival \(S_R(t)\); 1S(72) related to E1 under pure censoring | **Biased for Resolve probability** if withdraw/reject/transfer compete; small \(n=32\) |
| **Aalen–Johansen CIF** | Primary for E2: \(F_R(t), F_W(t), F_J(t), F_X(t)\) | Needs clear cause coding; TR assumption sensitivity |
| **Cause-specific Cox hazards** | Effects of cohort/priority/stage on instantaneous Resolve vs exit rates | CS hazard ratios  CIF ratios; time-varying stage needs counting process |
| **Fine–Gray subdistribution** | Direct covariate effects on CIF of Resolve | Interpret carefully with many competes; TR coding changes meaning |
| **Restricted mean time to Resolve / RMST difference to 72** | \(\int_0^{72} S(t)dt\) — expected hours resolved “in SLA window” under a chosen censor coding | Still depends on competing-risk handling; good for communication |
| **Landmarking at e.g. 24h, 48h, 72h** | P(resolve by 72  alive/active at landmark, stage at landmark) — operational alerts | Needs stage at landmark from prefixes+gaps; small strata |
| **Multi-state model** (SubmitScreen/AutoCheckReviewEscalateResolve/Withdraw/Reject/Transfer) | Process-aware intensities; handles rework loops | Sparse transitions; timestamps between activities **not fully supplied** (only terminal duration and last gap) |

### Primary approach (recommended)
**Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence for Resolve and for each competing exit, stratified by priority and by cohort**, with **TR-A** (transfer competes). Report:
- \(F_R(72)\), \(1-F_R(72)\), \(F_{\text{exit}}(72)\), and mass still active at 72.
- Companion **KM only as sensitivity** under TR-B, clearly labeled “requires independent transfer censoring.”

**Why:** The business question is SLA tail risk in a system with multiple dispositions; AJ matches E2 and decomposes E1-type failure into causes. \(n=32\) is small—stratified descriptive CIFs and exact-case accounting at 72h matter more than complex multivariable models.

### Secondary approaches
1. **Counting-process cause-specific hazards** with **time-varying stage** derived from prefix order (coarse), cohort and priority as baseline; landmark logistic at 48h for P(breach) among still-open-internal cases for **alerting**.
2. **RMST / area under AJ or KM to 72** for a single “hours of successful resolution capacity” KPI by cohort.
3. **Multi-state** as a **process diagnostic** (not primary inferential engine) once inter-event times exist; with current data, use prefix variants and rework/escalate flags as proxies.

### What not to do as primary
- Logistic regression on resolved-only SLA pass/fail.
- Cox with `escalated` or `rework_count` as baseline covariates without time alignment (immortal time / leakage—§5).
- Pooling cohorts as RCT evidence of the policy change.

**Uncertainty.** With \(n=32\) and sparse P1 cells, use Greenwood/AJ variance cautiously; prefer bootstrap case resampling and qualitative robustness across TR-A/B/C over p-value claims. **Assumption A2:** cases are independent given covariates (no batch queue dependence visible in data).

**Stage-hazard hypotheses (not proven):**
- **H2:** Hazard of Resolve is higher from AutoCheck-success paths (S01, S16, S18) than from prolonged Review loops.
- **H3:** After Escalate, competing hazard of Transfer rises (S08, S21, S32), especially P1—so Escalate is both treatment and selection into hand-off.

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

### Analysis grid (minimum three)

| Analysis | Transfer | Withdraw/Reject | Censoring | Cohort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S0 Primary** | TR-A compete | Compete | Open = admin censor; gaps ignored | Stratify A vs B (descriptive) |
| **S1 Optimistic independence** | TR-B censor | Compete | Open admin censor | Stratify |
| **S2 Conservative SLA** | TR-C fail internal | Compete as non-resolve for E1 | Open age72  breach; age&lt;72 censored in curve | Stratify |
| **S3 Eligible-only** | TR-A | WR-2 exclude W/J from SLA denom | Open censor | Stratify |
| **S4 Informative-gap** | TR-A | Compete | Weight or stratify opens by gap&gt;24h vs 24h; or censor only with copula bound | Stratify |
| **S5 Policy** | TR-A | Compete | Open censor | Compare B vs A within priority; no causal claim; discuss compliance confounding |

### What could reverse qualitatively

1. **P1 tail risk ranking under TR-B vs TR-A:** If transfers (S08, S21, S32) are censored (S1), P1 Resolve CIF rises and P1 may look acceptable; under TR-A/TR-C, P1 shows heavy competing hand-off and poor internal \(F_R(72)\). **Conclusion “P1 meets SLA” can reverse.**
2. **Cohort B better than A:** B has fast AutoCheck resolves but also S20=74, S28=88, S32 transfer 96, opens S25/S26 past SLA. Under completed-case or TR-B, B may look better; under S0/S2 population E1, advantage may shrink or vanish. **Policy-success claim can reverse.**
3. **Excluding withdraw/reject (S3):** Removes quick “failures” from denominator  SLA % among “eligible” improves; governance that uses eligible-only can paint a rosier picture than customer-level resolve rate.
4. **Informative censoring bounds (S4):** If stalled opens (S11, S25, S26) would have very long resolve times, upper-bound breach rates exceed primary AJ; if they resolve immediately after cutoff, lower bound improves. **Point estimates are not tight without follow-up.**
5. **Treating late Resolve (S03, S13, S20, S28) as partial success in “soft SLA” KPIs** changes operational ranking vs hard E1.

**Stable expectations (hypothesis, data-consistent):** non-trivial mass fails internal resolve by 72h once opens72 and competes are counted; escalation co-occurs with transfer and long ages; naive completed-case is optimistically biased in all scenarios above.

# 7. Operational interpretation

### Converting outputs to action (non-causal)
- **Alerting:** Landmark risk at 48h: if still internal and \(1-\hat F_R(72\mid \text{state at 48})\) above threshold, page owner. Use **stage + gap + priority**, not a black-box score presented as “causing” breach.
- **Workload:** E3 and open aged72 (S09, S11, S25, S26)  immediate queue purge / senior review. Opens near 72 with Escalate (S10, S25, S29)  surge capacity.
- **SLA governance:** Report dashboard as **stacked CIF at 72h**: Resolved in SLA | Still open | Withdrawn | Rejected | Transferred | Resolved late. Avoid single KM “survival” as the sole SLA %.
- **Transfer policy:** High P1 transfer incidence is a **hand-off KPI**, tracked separately from delay KPI (TR-A decomposition).
- **Do not** interpret cohort B vs A CIF differences as causal effects of automation; use for monitoring and targeted audit of non-compliant paths.

### Calibration and fairness
- **Calibration:** Within priority×cohort cells, compare predicted landmark breach rates to realized E1 when cases mature past 72 (holdout in time once cutoff advances). Small n  Bayesian smoothing or pooled hierarchical rates.
- **Fairness / equity-style checks:** Separate \(F_R(72)\) and transfer rates for P1 vs P2 vs P3; ensure P1’s lower internal resolve is not hidden by averaging with P3 quick rejects. Check that B’s AutoCheck path is equally available across priorities (compliance).
- **Stability:** Recompute S0–S2 weekly as cutoff moves; flag if TR-B and TR-A dashboards disagree on red/amber status.

### Communication template
“Among submissions, estimated probability of internal resolve by 72h is \(F_R(72)\) (AJ, transfers as competing). Probability still open at 72h is …. These are **association/monitoring** figures under stated coding, not effects of escalation or of the cohort-B policy.”

df["acts"] = df["prefix"].map(activities)
df["n_acts"] = df["acts"].map(len)
df["current_stage"] = df["acts"].map(lambda a: a[-1])
df["n_review"] = df["acts"].map(lambda a: sum(x == "Review" for x in a))
df["has_rework_path"] = df["acts"].map(lambda a: "Rework" in a)
df["early_autocheck"] = df["acts"].map(
    lambda a: len(a) > 1 and a[0] == "Submit" and a[1] == "AutoCheck"
)
df["variant"] = df["prefix"]

CAUSE = {"resolved": 1, "withdrawn": 2, "rejected": 3, "transferred": 4, "open": 0}

def encode(df, transfer_mode="TR-A"):
    """
    transfer_mode: TR-A compete, TR-B censor, TR-C treat as internal SLA failure
    Returns columns for two estimands readiness:
      E1-related: time, event_resolve_cs, known_breach_72, e1_not_resolved_by_72 (partial)
      E2-related: cause D, time_cif
    """
    out = df.copy()
    t = out["duration_or_age_h"].astype(float)
    status = out["observed_status"]

    # Cause under TR-A default then adjust transfer
    d = status.map(CAUSE).astype(int)
    time = t.copy()
    # Resolve event indicator for cause-specific / KM-with-competes-as-censor variants
    event_r = (status == "resolved").astype(int)

    if transfer_mode == "TR-A":
        # transfer remains cause 4; for KM-to-resolve, treat as censored (no resolve)
        event_r_km = event_r  # transfers already 0
        censor_for_km = status.isin(["open", "withdrawn", "rejected", "transferred"])
    elif transfer_mode == "TR-B":
        # transfer = administrative censor
        d = d.where(status != "transferred", 0)
        event_r_km = event_r
        censor_for_km = status.isin(["open", "withdrawn", "rejected", "transferred"])
    elif transfer_mode == "TR-C":
        # transfer counts as non-resolve failure for E1; still cause 4 for CIF
        event_r_km = event_r
        censor_for_km = status.isin(["open", "withdrawn", "rejected", "transferred"])
    else:
        raise ValueError(transfer_mode)

    # E1: not internally resolved by 72 (marginal success = resolve with T<=72)
    # known value when: resolved, or any terminal non-resolve, or open with age>=72
    y = np.full(len(out), np.nan)
    # resolved
    m = status == "resolved"
    y[m] = (t[m] > L).astype(float)
    # withdraw/reject: never resolved
    m = status.isin(["withdrawn", "rejected"])
    y[m] = 1.0
    # transfer
    m = status == "transferred"
    if transfer_mode in ("TR-A", "TR-C"):
        y[m] = 1.0  # not internal resolve by 72 (if t<=72 fail; if t>72 also not resolved by 72)
        # if transfer time > 72, still Y=1 for not resolved by 72
    elif transfer_mode == "TR-B":
        # only known if transfer time > 72 (then T_R > 72) else unknown (censored before horizon)
        y[m] = np.where(t[m] > L, 1.0, np.nan)
    # open
    m = status == "open"
    y[m] = np.where(t[m] >= L, 1.0, np.nan)

    out["time"] = time
    out["cause_D"] = d  # 0 censor, 1 R, 2 W, 3 J, 4 X
    out["event_resolve"] = event_r  # observed resolve
    out["event_resolve_km"] = event_r  # KM event
    out["e1_not_resolved_by_72"] = y
    out["known_e1"] = ~np.isnan(y)
    out["open_past_sla"] = (status == "open") & (t >= L)
    out["open_pre_sla"] = (status == "open") & (t < L)
    out["transfer_mode"] = transfer_mode
    return out

# --- 2) Encode two estimands × three transfer scenarios ---
encodings = {m: encode(df, m) for m in ["TR-A", "TR-B", "TR-C"]}

def e1_summary(enc, label):
    y = enc["e1_not_resolved_by_72"]
    known = enc["known_e1"]
    # Bounds: unknown opens pre-72 could finish either way
    n = len(enc)
    n_known = int(known.sum())
    n_breach_known = int((y == 1).sum())
    n_success_known = int((y == 0).sum())
    n_unk = n - n_known
    # worst case: all unknown become breaches; best: all become success
    p_hat = n_breach_known / n  # treats unknown as 0 in numerator only — report bounds
    p_lo = n_breach_known / n
    p_hi = (n_breach_known + n_unk) / n
    return {
        "mode": label,
        "n": n,
        "known": n_known,
        "unknown_pre72_open_or_TRB": n_unk,
        "known_breaches": n_breach_known,
        "known_timely_resolve": n_success_known,
        "E1_breach_rate_lower": round(p_lo, 3),
        "E1_breach_rate_upper": round(p_hi, 3),
    }

print("=== E1 bounds Pr(not internal resolve by 72h) ===")
for m, enc in encodings.items():
    print(e1_summary(enc, m))

def crude_cif_table(enc):
    """Nonparametric cause counts at event times; opens contribute to risk set until age."""
    work = enc.copy()
    # For CIF construction: event if cause_D>0, else censor at time
    work["event_any"] = (work["cause_D"] > 0).astype(int)
    times = np.sort(work.loc[work["event_any"] == 1, "time"].unique())
    rows_out = []
    for tau in times:
        at_risk = (work["time"] >= tau).sum()
        for cause, name in [(1, "Resolve"), (2, "Withdraw"), (3, "Reject"), (4, "Transfer")]:
            d_c = ((work["time"] == tau) & (work["cause_D"] == cause)).sum()
            if d_c:
                rows_out.append({"t": tau, "cause": name, "n_events": int(d_c), "n_risk": int(at_risk)})
    return pd.DataFrame(rows_out)

print("\n=== Crude risk-set event table (TR-A, all cases retained) ===")
print(crude_cif_table(primary).to_string(index=False))

# Horizon snapshot decomposition (E2-style accounting)
def horizon_decomp(enc, L=72.0):
    st = enc["observed_status"]
    t = enc["time"]
    d = enc["cause_D"]
    # status at 72 along observed data
    timely_resolve = ((d == 1) & (t <= L)).sum()
    late_resolve = ((d == 1) & (t > L)).sum()
    w = ((d == 2) & (t <= L)).sum()
    j = ((d == 3) & (t <= L)).sum()
    x = ((d == 4) & (t <= L)).sum()
    # still active at 72: open with age>72 OR terminal after 72 OR open age==? 
    # open age >= 72; also paths with first terminal >72
    still_active = (
        ((st == "open") & (t >= L))
        | ((d > 0) & (t > L))
    ).sum()
    # open pre-72 still unresolved unknown beyond age - count separately
    open_pre = ((st == "open") & (t < L)).sum()
    return {
        "timely_resolve_<=72": int(timely_resolve),
        "late_resolve_>72": int(late_resolve),
        "withdraw_<=72": int(w),
        "reject_<=72": int(j),
        "transfer_<=72": int(x),
        "observed_still_nonterminal_at_72": int(still_active),
        "open_censored_before_72": int(open_pre),
        "F_resolve_hat_crude": round(timely_resolve / len(enc), 3),
        "note": "crude proportions n=32; open_pre not allocated",
    }

print("\n=== Horizon decomposition E2-style (TR-A) ===")
print(horizon_decomp(primary))

# 9. Limitations and additional data

**Priority missing data for stronger inference**

1. **Exact activity timestamps** (not only terminal duration and last gap) — required for true multi-state hazards, time-to-escalate, immortal-time-safe covariates, and gap trajectories.
2. **External outcomes after `Transfer`** — only way to tighten TR-C vs customer-true resolution; without this, internal SLA and customer SLA diverge unidentifiably.
3. **Observation cutoff time and each case’s Submit calendar time** — staggered entry, calendar effects, and whether open ages are comparable; freeze-time bias.
4. **Time-varying workload / queue length / assignee** — to model informative gaps and shared frailty (violations of A2).
5. **Assignment mechanism and policy rollout compliance** — who got AutoCheck-first in cohort B; partial compliance makes cohort contrasts confounded.
6. **Reason codes** for Withdraw/Reject/Transfer and whether SLA contract excludes them (chooses WR-1 vs WR-2).
7. **Business-hour vs elapsed-hour SLA definition** — could re-rank borderline cases (S03=76, S20=74).
8. **Larger sample and longer follow-up** of opens (S10, S15, S24, S29, S31 especially) — narrow E1 bounds.
9. **Prior cases / customer history** and channel — heterogeneity beyond priority.
10. **Explicit censoring audit** — whether “open” includes abandoned silent cases vs active WIP.

**Methodological limitations of this extract.** \(n=32\) precludes stable multivariable Fine–Gray models; prefix order without times blocks precise stage-entry clocks; non-randomized cohort B blocks causal policy claims; long gaps make independent censoring doubtful; PM4Py variants here are control-flow descriptive only.

**Bottom line.** Define SLA tail risk as **population non-resolution by 72h (E1, with bounds)** and **cause-specific cumulative incidence of Resolve vs exits (E2, TR-A primary)**. Do not analyze resolved cases only. Treat transfers under competing-risk and conservative failure assumptions in parallel; treat escalation/rework as time-aligned process states, not baseline regressors. Use AJ/CIF + landmark alerts for operations; reserve KM-with-everything-censored as an explicitly optimistic sensitivity check.
