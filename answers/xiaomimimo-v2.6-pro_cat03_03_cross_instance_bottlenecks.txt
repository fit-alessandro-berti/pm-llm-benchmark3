**Machine view.** *Visible:* utilisation, activity sequences, gaps. *Hidden:* which job blocked which; tool-mediated coupling. *Duplicated:* `Setup`/`Release` per job. *Misleading:* **the "longest recorded gap" metric is contaminated.** OVEN-1 is non-productive 12:55–14:50 = 115 min, yet its longest gap is 73 min, because cleaning/quarantine events are logged as machine events and cut the gap. TEST-1 is non-productive 16:35–17:25 = 50 min, yet its longest gap is 39 min, because `Calibrate` counts as a machine event. Managers therefore see two short gaps where there are two long stalls.

**Batch view.** *Visible:* the barrier and its synchronisation cost. *Hidden:* *why* the last member is late (FIX-A, L7, rework) — the batch's max is a symptom aggregator. *Duplicated:* the single cure event's effects, re-expressed per job. *Misleading:* B1 looks like "one 50-min cure for three jobs"  "ovening is cheap," which drives exactly the batching policy that created 435 min of waiting.

**Tool view.** *Visible:* hold windows, contention, CAL-3 maintenance. *Hidden:* machine-side consequences (42 min CNC-2 idle) and shipment-side consequences. *Misleading:* **tool utilisation is an inverted metric** — FIX-A held 09:00–11:40 would report as 100 % utilised and "productive," while generating up to 95 min of J4's block.

**ComponentLot view.** *Visible:* quarantine windows and containment. *Hidden:* job-level effects (dropped relations; the override in a separate system). *Misleading:* "L7 cost 210 min" — the marginal cost on B2 is ~5 min, while the policy-level effect (override, risk exposure) is unmeasured. Impact is simultaneously over- and under-stated depending on the counterfactual.

**Shipment view.** *Visible:* `CreateLoadDispatch` (0.8 h, 0.3 h). *Hidden:* the entire pre-dispatch wait of already-packed jobs. *Misleading:* "shipment lead time 1.1 h"  "shipping is not a problem," while shipping contributes 53 % of J1's elapsed time. S1 has the *fastest* tail (15 min) and the *worst* hidden delay.

**Why low utilisation coexists with long waiting.** Waiting is stored as *queue-before-resource*, not as *resource-busy*. The binding constraints here are synchronisation rules (batch all-of, shipment all-of) and exclusive non-machine state (FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3, L7), none of which occupy machine time. With an all-of batch rule, the oven runs only when the **max** readiness is reached, so idle time is attributed to "no work available" rather than to J3. And the machine gap metric is biased downward by setup/cleaning/calibration events. Utilisation of 44 % on OVEN-1 is fully compatible with J1 and J2 each losing ~4 h to that oven.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**Narrative A – J3  (FIX-A)  J4  (S1)  J1.** Objects: Job J3, J4, J1; Tool FIX-A; Machine CNC-2; Shipment S1. *Temporal precedence:* J3 acquires FIX-A 09:00, releases 11:40; J4 arrives at CNC-2 09:50; CNC-2 idle 10:05–10:47; J4 `Machine Process` 11:45; J4 packed 19:10; S1 dispatches 19:25; J1 was already packed 13:30. *Resource feasibility:* one FIX-A, one CNC-2, S1 loads both members  J1 cannot leave before J4. *Policy:* shipment membership is fixed at planning; no re-routing of J1. *Causal speculation:* J3's 160-min FIX-A hold is plausibly inflated by a rework loop (`InspectReworkInspect` exists for 2 jobs) — unproven. Net: J1 is damaged twice by two different partners, at two different barriers, via two different resource types.

**Narrative B – L7 override  B1  OVEN-1/RACK-7  S2.** Objects: ComponentLot L7, Components C2/C3, Jobs J2/J3, Batch B1, Machine OVEN-1, Tool RACK-7, Shipment S2. *Temporal precedence:* J2 ready 08:25; `Quarantine Lot` 10:20 (L7, C2, C5, J2, J5); J3 ready 11:55; `Batch Cure` 12:05 (9 objects); `Release Lot` 13:50. *Resource feasibility:* C2 must physically be in the rack at 12:05 — so the batch ran on a quarantined lot. *Policy:* lot release should gate production; a supervisor override recorded elsewhere permitted it. *Causal speculation:* the override may have seeded a downstream quality event — nothing in the log shows one. The same `Quarantine Lot` event has different effective force on J2 (null) and J5 (blocking): coupling is *policy-mediated*, not purely structural.

**Narrative C – L7  J5  RACK-7  B2  CAL-3  S3.** Objects: L7, C5, Jobs J5/J6, Tools RACK-7 and CAL-3, Machines OVEN-1 and TEST-1, Batch B2, Shipment S3. *Temporal precedence:* quarantine 10:20  release 13:50  J5 batch-ready 14:40 (50 min later); RACK-7 "cleaning/quarantine" 12:55–14:35; B2 14:50; J5 ready for test 16:00, tests 16:10–16:35; CAL-3 recalibrated 16:35–17:20; J6 (ready 16:05) tests 17:25–17:50; S3 dispatches 35 min after its last pack. *Resource feasibility:* one RACK-7 serves both batches; one CAL-3 serves all final tests. *Policy:* mandatory rack cleaning between batches (100 min) and recalibration after a test (45 min). *Causal speculation:* whether recalibration is per-test or per-N-tests is unknown; if per-test, J6 was simply unlucky. Note J6 is the only job with **no** batch coupling — yet it still loses 40–45 min, showing that removing one coupling does not free a job.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

**I1 – Split/partial batches (reduce synchronisation).** *Target:* all-of batch barrier. *Benefit:* ~435 min of aggregate waiting removed in this episode (225 for J1, 210 for J2); 90 min for J4 if B2 splits. *Helped:* J1, J2, J4, downstream S1/S2. *Harmed:* OVEN-1 and RACK-7 (more cycles; RACK-7 turnover is already 100 min), J5 (its slot may be displaced), energy. *New cost/risk:* second rack (RACK-8) + cleaning labour; possible cure non-uniformity if partial loads change thermal mass. *Perverse metric:* "batch fullness %" or "ovens cycles per shift" — both push straight back to large batches. *Evaluation:* counterfactual replay over object-linked readiness timestamps computing  job waiting and # of `Batch Cure` hyperedges under min-size-1 and min-size-2 policies; report `Batch–Machine` and `Batch–Tool` interaction counts alongside.

**I2 – Fixture pooling and hold-discipline (tools).** *Target:* FIX-A-type exclusive tools. *Benefit:* up to 95 min for J4; removes the rework-spanning hold pattern. *Helped:* J4 and any job queued behind a long-hold job. *Harmed:* J3 (must release during rework and re-acquire  re-fixturing error/quality risk). *New cost/risk:* capital for a second fixture + traceability/calibration of two fixtures. *Perverse metric:* "tool utilisation %" or "changeovers per job" — both reward long holds, which is exactly the pathology. *Evaluation:* per-Tool hold-duration distribution; count of `Job–Tool–Job` 2-paths; regress job waiting on overlap with foreign tool holds (object-centric features).

**I3 – Shipment re-bucketing / partition alignment (shipment policy).** *Target:* shipment consolidation — and, more deeply, the cross-cutting partition motif M4. *Benefit:* aligning the shipment partition to the batch partition ({J1,J2,J3},{J4,J5},{J6}) lets the second barrier inherit the first's synchronisation: J1's barrier wait falls from 565 min to ~225 min, **~340 min saved**, without reducing truck fill. *Helped:* J1 (and any early-packed job). *Harmed:* J4 (if left as a singleton it still dispatches ~19:25, but if forced into a new pair it may wait longer), freight planners. *New cost/risk:* partial trucks if sizes mismatch (3/3 vs 2/2/2), customs paperwork per shipment. *Perverse metric:* "truck fill rate" rewards re-consolidation and recreates the delay; "shipments/day" rewards trivially small shipments. *Evaluation:* per-Shipment per-member `Dispatch  Pack` distribution (max and mean), plus a bin-packing-with-due-dates re-solve over the object graph; report max member wait, not shipment cycle time.

**I4 – Object-centric logging (logging, not operations).** *Target:* observability artefacts. *Benefit:* converts "unpredictable" into attributable; makes e14 (Job–ComponentLot) pipeline-invariant. *Helped:* planners, analysts, and indirectly J5 (which was blocked silently). *Harmed:* operators (documentation load  risk of fabricated or defaulted reason codes). *New cost/risk:* MES change, training, data-quality drift. *Perverse metric:* "% of delays coded" pushes operators to pick the fastest code, not the true one. *Evaluation:* before/after comparison of explained variance in job lead time using `pm4py.extract_ocel_features` + regression; count edges that are currently *inferred-only* as a defect metric.

**I5 – Lot-splitting / scoped quarantine (component-lot).** *Target:* L7-type whole-lot quarantine. *Benefit:* decouples J2 from J5; under the B2 counterfactual this alone saves only ~5 min, but it removes the *override* pressure and the 3.5-h coupling window. *Helped:* J5, J2, and compliance. *Harmed:* quality/traceability (partial-lot release may breach regulatory rules), inspection cost. *Perverse metric:* "quarantine hours per lot" incentivises early release — the very override we observed. *Evaluation:* count `ComponentLot–Job` edges with heterogeneous downstream status and measure the induced job-waiting delta between the blocked and unblocked member.

**I6 – RACK-7 turnover reduction (tool/changeover).** *Target:* the 12:55–14:35 cleaning/quarantine window. *Benefit:* B2 could start at 14:45 (5 min) today, but under the I5 counterfactual (J5 ready 11:10) the rack becomes binding and B2 could start at 13:20, saving J4 90 min. *Helped:* J4, J5. *Harmed:* contamination/quality risk; cleaning labour. *Perverse metric:* "cleaning minutes per rack" or "racks in service." *Evaluation:* measure `Unload  next Load` gaps per Tool and attribute batch-start delay to that gap via object-linked events.

*(The ordering matters: I5 alone buys ~5 min; I5 + I6 buys ~90 min. This is the clearest spillover in the data.)*

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations that would weaken "batch synchronisation" as the top mechanism:**

1. Physical-readiness evidence showing J1/J2 were not kitted until ~11:50 (i.e. the 08:10/08:25 stamps are event-creation times) — the 435-min "tax" would collapse to ~0.
2. A cure recipe specification stating that OVEN-1/RACK-7 requires a full 3-job load (thermal mass / chemistry) — the waiting would be unavoidable and the mechanism would remain but cease to be a *fixable* bottleneck.
3. Evidence that RACK-7 could not have hosted a second cure between 08:20 and 12:05 (single rack, 100-min turnover) — splitting B1 would then save ~5 min, exactly as in the B2 counterfactual.
4. Dispatch-punctuality data showing all six shipments left on time — in which case 355 min of J1's wait is harmless and shipment consolidation drops out of contention entirely.
5. Measurement that B1's cure occupied 12:05–15:00 rather than 12:05–12:55, shrinking the sync share of J1's lead time below 35 %.
6. Repeated episodes where batches start within minutes of the **first** readiness rather than the last — contradicting an all-of rule and implying the 12:05 start had another cause (e.g. an oven booking slot).
7. Independent evidence that J4's `Machine Process` could not have started before 11:45 for CNC-2-side reasons (the unexplained 10:47–11:45 events being another job), which would demote tool contention and raise the relative weight of batch/shipment.

**Additional fields / event types that would discriminate among hypotheses:**

1. **`Wait for <Tool|Batch|Lot|Shipment>` events** with start, end and the blocking object ID (currently absent — the single highest-value addition).
2. **Physical readiness / kitting timestamps** distinct from log-creation timestamps.
3. **Batch recipe constraints:** min/max load, per-job rack slots, cure parameters (tests partial-batch feasibility).
4. **Tool state events** (`Acquire`, `Release`, `Calibrate`, `Clean`) with planned/unplanned flag, reason code, and whether recalibration is per-test or per-N-tests.
5. **Override/exception records** as first-class events: approver, timestamp, scope, reason (currently in a separate system).
6. **Shipment plan:** planned members, planned dispatch, per-job due date and priority class (enables the partition-alignment counterfactual).
7. **Rework reason codes and per-job rework duration**, plus whether the fixture is held across the rework loop.
8. **Full per-job post-cure timestamps** (`Inspect`, `Final Test`, `Pack`) with object links — J4's unexplained ~210 min is currently unattributable.
9. **Queue-entry timestamps and queue position** at each machine and tool.
10. **Operator / shift identifiers** (to test shift-boundary and handover effects, e.g. the 10:05–10:47 CNC-2 idle).
