## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

- **January–March: stable baseline.**  
  Labels are fully mature, unknown activity and missing queue are near baseline, AUROC/Brier/ECE are stable, and observed breach rates match mean predicted risk. These months are the best reference period for the original model and process.

- **April: primarily a schema and pipeline incident, not real performance deterioration.**  
  Observed breach remains 0.19 and label maturity is 100%, so the outcome distribution is not showing a real breach surge. However, mean predicted risk jumps to 0.28, AUROC falls to 0.67, ECE rises to 0.110, unknown activity reaches 17%, missing queue reaches 14%, and 18% of cases are unmapped. The migration from `Initial Diagnosis Completed` to `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` broke prediction prefixes and feature construction. This is best explained by **data-pipeline failure**, with some artificial covariate distortion caused by missing/unknown values. It should not be treated as evidence of model concept drift or process failure.

- **May: partial pipeline recovery, not yet fully clean.**

- **June: routing/process-mix change, not necessarily concept drift.**

## 3. Diagnostic tests

1. **Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting**

Compute breach rate, AUROC, Brier, and ECE only on cases with a fixed maturity window, e.g., 60, 90, or 120 days. Compare the raw cutoff metrics with fixed-vintage metrics.  
   - **If label delay/censoring dominates:** October and September metrics will change materially as maturity increases; the apparent October breach rate of 0.16 will rise, and performance metrics will stabilize when restricted to mature vintages.  
   - **If real concept/process drift dominates:** August–September fixed-vintage cohorts will still show elevated breach rates and degraded calibration relative to January–June, even at equal maturity.

2. **Recomputation after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning**  
   - **Run:** Rebuild April and May prediction prefixes using canonical `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, remove exact duplicates, flag near duplicates, recompute cached features such as “number of events before diagnosis,” and re-evaluate metrics.  
   - **If pipeline failure explains April:** unknown/missing rates fall, mean predicted risk returns toward 0.18–0.20, AUROC/Brier/ECE approach baseline, and errors concentrate only in unrecoverable records.  
   - **If genuine drift also exists:** April metrics remain degraded after clean recomputation, especially among records with complete features.

3. **Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month**  
   - **Run:** Compute AUROC, Brier, ECE, calibration slope/intercept, and calibration curves separately for V1/V2/V3, old/new source, Partner Z/non-Z, and missing/non-missing queue.  
   - **If covariate/population mix dominates:** global metrics degrade, but within-variant or within-source metrics remain stable; reweighting by variant/partner restores global performance.  
   - **If concept drift or pipeline failure dominates:** within-V3 metrics degrade in Aug–Sep, or within-new-source/missing-queue metrics degrade in April. Persistent within-segment degradation indicates more than mix shift.

4. **Comparison of process variants and DFG/performance-DFG edges**  
   - **Run:** Monthly, compute variant shares, unmapped share, DFG frequencies, and performance DFG edge durations. Focus on `Order Part -> Part Received`, `Initial Diagnosis Completed -> Remote Fix`, and `Dispatch Technician -> Repair Completed`.  
   - **If process drift explains outcome changes:** variant shares and relevant edge durations change in the same months as breach rates change. August–September breach growth should align with the part-received edge and V3 share.  
   - **If label delay alone explains changes:** process edges and variant mix would remain stable while apparent outcomes change. This is contradicted by the observed June V2 jump and August–September part-delay explosion.

5. **Test whether the outcome relationship conditional on the same path changed**  
   - **Run:** Within V3, and separately within V1/V2, model breach as a function of baseline features plus month indicators. Then add part lead time, supplier identity/region, Partner Z, rural distance, and device age. Test whether month effects remain.  
   - **If concept drift is real:** month effects, especially August–September, remain significant after conditioning on observed process and population variables.  
   - **If the change is mediated by omitted process/population variables:** month effects attenuate or disappear after adding part lead, Partner Z, rural distance, and device age. That would favor process/population drift with missing features over intrinsic concept drift.

6. **Population reweighting or transport-style checks for Partner Z**  
   - **Run:** Compare September non-Partner-Z cases reweighted to the Partner Z covariate distribution against Partner Z cases. Train or calibrate on pre-Partner-Z data and evaluate on Partner Z; then repeat after adding partner/rural/device-age features. Compute standardized breach gaps.

   - **If model bias, missing features, or concept drift remains:** Partner Z performance remains worse after reweighting/conditioning, indicating the model does not transport to the new population without structural changes.

7. **Feature missingness and lineage audits**  
   - **Run:** Audit lineage from raw source to model features: activity-label mapping, duplicate removal by source/date, queue-feature missingness, cached feature freshness, prediction-prefix correctness, and unknown activity rates. Correlate missingness with prediction error.

   - **If real drift is the cause:** errors persist in clean, complete records after pipeline repair.

**Safe or unsafe months for training until issues are resolved:**

- **Safe:** January–March are safe as baseline but stale. June–July are generally safe for mature-label training, provided the routing change is represented. May is safe only after recomputing derived features.
- **Conditionally safe:** August can become safe after label maturity is verified and supplier-lead/partner features are added. It is valuable for representing the supplier disruption if that regime is expected to continue.
- **Unsafe:** April raw data is unsafe because of schema/pipeline failure. September is unsafe until label maturity and Partner Z label bias are addressed. October is unsafe until outcome labels mature or delayed-feedback methods are used.

**Segment-specific or variant-specific model justification:**  
A segment-specific approach is justified for V3 and Partner Z because their base breach rates, process paths, and missing covariates differ materially. However, the first step should be a single model with stronger variant/partner/supplier features and separate calibrators. Fully separate models are warranted only if stratified evaluation shows persistent miscalibration or discrimination failure and if each segment has enough stable, mature data. ## 5. Monitoring architecture

| Monitor layer | What it watches | Example metrics / checks | Dependency and action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data-quality monitor | Activity schema, unknown labels, duplicates, missing queue, source counts, feature lineage | Unknown activity share, missing queue share, duplicate rate, unmapped variant share, cached-feature freshness | If red, mark downstream model metrics provisional and block retraining. Trigger pipeline repair first. |
| Label-maturity monitor | Outcome completeness by cohort, variant, partner, and path | Maturity at 30/60/90 days, median label delay, unresolved long-path share, Partner Z maturity gap | If maturity is below threshold, suppress performance alerts for that cohort or label them provisional. Do not trigger retraining. |
| Predictive-performance monitor | Discrimination and overall error on mature cohorts only | AUROC, Brier, calibration slope, PSI on predictions | Alert only after data-quality and label-maturity gates pass. Use fixed-vintage comparisons. |
| Calibration monitor | Global and segment calibration | ECE, calibration curves by variant, partner, source, month | Segment miscalibration triggers recalibration/feature investigation, not automatic global retraining. |
| Process-variant monitor | Variant mix and unmapped/rare paths | V1/V2/V3 shares, new variants, unmapped share | Mix shift triggers operational and model-segment review. It does not by itself trigger retraining. |
| Performance-DFG monitor | Edge durations and bottlenecks | `Order Part -> Part Received`, `Diagnosis -> Remote Fix`, `Dispatch -> Repair Completed` | Edge-duration alarms trigger supplier/process intervention. If mature model performance also degrades, consider concept-drift investigation. |
| Population monitor | Partner Z, rural customers, device age, source mix | Partner Z share, rural-distance PSI, device-age PSI, fairness/segment outcome gaps | Population shift triggers feature/segment review and possible reweighting, not automatic retraining. |
| Intervention/deployment monitor | Model versions, patches, mapping changes, outcome API incidents | Deployment dates, mapping patch, outcome backlog status | Annotate monitoring windows. Prevents misattributing deployment artifacts to model drift. |

**Alert dependency rules:**

- A **label-delay alarm** should suppress or downgrade recent-month predictive-performance alerts and should never directly trigger retraining.
- A **data-quality alarm** should block training pipelines until the feature lineage is repaired.
- A **process-drift alarm** should trigger operational review and segment analysis; retraining is considered only if mature, stratified evidence shows changed outcome relationships.
- A **population-shift alarm** should trigger reweighting, fairness/segment checks, and feature-gap analysis.
- A **concept-drift alarm** should require: clean data, sufficient label maturity, fixed-vintage degradation, and within-segment evidence.

import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# 2) Duplicate handling: remove exact duplicates; flag consecutive repeats for audit.
df = df.sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp", "activity"], kind="mergesort")
exact_dup = df.duplicated(subset=["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"], keep="first")
duplicate_audit = df.loc[exact_dup].copy()
df_clean = df.loc[~exact_dup].copy()

df_clean["next_activity"] = df_clean.groupby("case_id")["activity"].shift(-1)
df_clean["consecutive_repeat_flag"] = df_clean["activity"] == df_clean["next_activity"]

# 3) Case-start cohort metadata.
case_start = df_clean.groupby("case_id")["timestamp"].min().rename("case_start")

df_clean["case_start_month"] = df_clean["case_start"].dt.to_period("M")

    df_clean,
    case_id="case_id",
    activity_key="activity",
    timestamp_key="timestamp",
)

def median_part_lead_hours(frame: pd.DataFrame):
    order = frame.loc[frame["activity"] == "Order Part"].groupby("case_id")["timestamp"].min()
    recv = frame.loc[frame["activity"] == "Part Received"].groupby("case_id")["timestamp"].min()
    both = pd.concat({"order": order, "recv": recv}, axis=1).dropna()
    if both.empty:
        return None
    return ((both["recv"] - both["order"]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600).median()

def monthly_audit(period: pd.Period, cohort: str):
    start = period.start_time
    end = (period + 1).start_time

    if cohort == "event_calendar":
        # Calendar cohort: events occurring in the month.
        # Interpretation: workload and process activity happening in that calendar month.
        df_m = df_clean[(df_clean["timestamp"] >= start) & (df_clean["timestamp"] < end)]
        log_m = pm4py.filter_time_range(log, start, end)
    else:
        # Case-start cohort: cases whose first event is in the month, followed through all later events.
        # Interpretation: longitudinal cohort suitable for outcome maturity and breach analysis.
        cases_m = df_clean.loc[df_clean["case_start_month"] == period, "case_id"].unique()
        df_m = df_clean[df_clean["case_id"].isin(cases_m)]
        log_m = pm4py.format_dataframe(
            df_m,
            case_id="case_id",
            activity_key="activity",
            timestamp_key="timestamp",
        )

    if df_m.empty:
        return {
            "month": str(period),
            "cohort": cohort,
            "cases": 0,
            "events": 0,
            "unknown_activity_share": None,
            "missing_queue_share": None,
            "consecutive_repeat_share": None,
            "distinct_variants": 0,
            "dfg_edges": 0,
            "performance_dfg_edges": 0,
            "temporal_feature_rows": 0,
            "median_order_to_part_received_h": None,
        }

    variants = pm4py.get_variants(log_m)
    dfg, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_dfg(log_m)
    perf_dfg, perf_start_activities, perf_end_activities = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log_m)
    temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(log_m)

    unknown_share = float((df_m["activity"] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean())
    missing_queue = float(df_m["queue"].isna().mean()) if "queue" in df_m.columns else None
    consecutive_repeat_share = float(df_m["consecutive_repeat_flag"].mean())

    return {
        "month": str(period),
        "cohort": cohort,
        "cases": int(df_m["case_id"].nunique()),
        "events": int(len(df_m)),
        "unknown_activity_share": unknown_share,
        "missing_queue_share": missing_queue,
        "consecutive_repeat_share": consecutive_repeat_share,
        "distinct_variants": len(variants),
        "dfg_edges": len(dfg),
        "performance_dfg_edges": len(perf_dfg),
        "temporal_feature_rows": int(len(temporal)),
        "median_order_to_part_received_h": median_part_lead_hours(df_m),

months = pd.period_range("2026-01", "2026-10", freq="M")

# Auditable monthly summaries; no predictive model is fitted here.
summaries = pd.DataFrame(

    [monthly_audit(m, "case_start") for m in months]

duplicate_by_month = duplicate_audit.groupby("duplicate_month").size().rename("exact_duplicates_removed")
summaries = summaries.merge(duplicate_by_month, left_on="month", right_index=True, how="left")

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

| Failure mode | Consequence | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Retraining on immature October labels | The model learns that long, unresolved cases do not breach, because breach labels are missing for exactly the riskiest cases. Future predictions will understate risk for V3, Partner Z, and supplier-delayed cases. | Gate training on label maturity. Use fixed-vintage cohorts, delayed-feedback modeling, or survival analysis. Exclude October until labels mature. |
| Calibrating around the April pipeline bug | The model may become calibrated to missing queue values, unknown activities, or wrong prediction prefixes. When the pipeline is fixed, that calibration will become wrong and may harm later months. | Treat April as a pipeline incident. Repair labels, deduplicate, backfill features, and recalibrate only after recomputed April/May metrics are validated. |
| Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept/process drift | The model will continue to underpredict breach risk for parts-dependent cases, causing missed escalations, breached SLAs, and poor operational prioritization. | Monitor `Order Part -> Part Received` and V3 breach rates. Add supplier lead-time features. Retrain or apply risk adjustments if fixed-vintage evidence confirms persistent change. |
| Attributing Partner Z differences to unfair model behavior without stratified evidence | The organization may apply fairness corrections or model penalties without understanding that the gap is driven by rural distance, device age, V3 routing, or label maturity. This can obscure real operational issues. | Stratify by partner, variant, rural distance, device age, and label maturity. Run reweighting/transportability checks. Add missing covariates before concluding bias or unfairness. |
| Treating June’s routing mix shift as concept drift and retraining unnecessarily | Retraining on a short post-policy window may destabilize a model that was otherwise well calibrated, especially if the new V2 regime is stable and low-risk. | Use variant-specific calibration and process-mix monitoring first. Retrain only if within-variant outcome relationships change or if the new mix persists and is not captured by features. |
