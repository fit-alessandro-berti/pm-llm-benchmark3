## 9. Provisional attribution

The March fitness collapse is attributed to logging semantic drift with 95% confidence, evidenced by the immediate recovery in mapped fitness and the appearance of new labels. This is a technical artifact, not a process failure. The May surge in rework (12% to 24%) and cycle time is attributed to Supplier Z disruption with 90% confidence, correlating perfectly with the supplier volume spike. This is a population-driven behavioral change. The July acceleration in cycle time (44.2h median) despite stable rework suggests staffing-driven queueing drift (70% confidence), supported by the assessor departure timeline. However, the priority rule introduced simultaneously mediates this; it likely reduced waiting for high-complexity cases while inflating it for standard ones, masking the true queueing impact in aggregates. The October complexity spike (41%) is attributed to seasonal population drift with 85% confidence, consistent with historical renewal patterns. The December recovery is plausibly linked to interventions (60% confidence), though reduced seasonal load also contributes. Interaction effects prevent clean attribution between staffing and supplier issues; incomplete documents from Supplier Z likely exacerbated the workload for remaining staff, creating a compounded delay effect not visible in isolated metrics. The `Senior Assessment` surge in October reflects both seasonal complexity and the priority rule forcing complex cases through specific paths. Conclusions on staffing are weakest due to confounding priority rules. Conclusions on supplier impact are strongest due to direct correlation. I would revise the staffing attribution if resource-level event logs revealed unchanged individual productivity despite higher queue times. Similarly, if Supplier Z cases showed normal cycle times despite incomplete docs, the supplier hypothesis would weaken. Currently, the data supports a multi-mechanism drift model where technical, population, and organizational factors overlap sequentially and interactively. The distinction between behavioral adaptation and population mix is critical; rework increased because documents were incomplete (population), not because assessors changed habits (behavior), though prolonged queueing may have induced rushed assessments later. Future analysis must isolate Supplier Z cases to confirm if the rework rate is intrinsic to that population or contagious across the process.
