# 2. Benign alternative explanations

**A. Create PO + Approve PO exceptions (Carla/Dan/Gita)**
- *Shared account or assistant entry:* The Approver_Gita account may be recorded as creator when an assistant entered the PO. Supported if `entered_by` differs from `approved_by`; refuted if sign-in logs show a single individual using the account for both steps.
- *Bot/human mapping defect:* Bot_PO-created POs may carry the human approver as org:resource. Supported if the affected cases are legacy_erp and the bot-created events are the same as the flagged cases; refuted if the flagged cases are non-bot, non-legacy records.
- *Sanctioned emergency practice:* A delegated approver may initiate and approve in a rush under a documented exception. Supported by a written emergency delegation covering these cases; refuted if no delegation exists.

**B. Bank change + payment approval exceptions (Lena/Kim, V-77x)**
- *Mass maintenance after platform migration:* Bulk bank-detail updates followed by normal payment runs. Supported by migration batch IDs, bulk file uploads, and maintenance tickets linked to the changes; refuted if changes are individual, unticketed and not in migration batches.
- *Legitimate, verified changes:* Bank changes were callback-verified before payments were approved. Supported by verification logs timestamped before payment; refuted if verification is absent or after payment.
- *Attribution artefact:* The "same person" link may come from a shared or mapped identity rather than one person. Supported if the 9 cases have identical `entered_by` on both steps through a shared account; refuted if the two steps have distinct, verified logins.

**C. Unusually fast approvals**
- *Pre-authorised emergency channel:* Approvals made by phone or message, then recorded. Supported by an emergency authorisation record preceding the approval; refuted if no authorisation exists.
- *Timestamp reflects data entry, not decision:* Fast medians may come from late keying of a decision made earlier. Supported if workflow-engine timestamps differ from ERP entry times; refuted if the two match.
- *Genuinely urgent, low-complexity orders:* Emergency medical supplies and weekend repairs are often small and routine. Supported by amount bands and item lists; refuted if fast approvals occur on high-band orders with no urgency evidence.

**D. Subcontracting loops**
- *Clarification loops (GitaCarlaGita; Aline/Elena; Boris/Felix):* Ordinary back-and-forth after comments. Supported by comment text and loops occurring *before* approval; refuted if loops occur after approval and change amounts or vendors.
- *Escalation loops (HugoDanHugo):* Managerial oversight of repairs. Supported by repair work orders and escalation notes; refuted if escalations are not documented.
- *Correction loops after payment approval (LenaKimLena):* Ordinary correction of vendor records already approved. Supported by correction logs showing typo fixes; refuted if corrections change bank details or amounts after approval. *This is the one loop whose ordering warrants attention.*
- *Automated exception feedback (Bot_MatchKim):* Expected for exception handling. Supported by exception codes; this is the least concerning loop.

**E. Vendor concentration in V-77x**
- *Supplier-mix effect:* The family is simply large or serves a specific sector. Supported by vendor-master counts and spend before the migration; refuted if concentration is new.
- *Migration artefact:* Concentration reflects which records were touched by the migration. Supported by migration logs listing the same vendors.

**F. Manual source-system concentration**
- *Migration or backfill:* Manual upload is the migration channel. Supported by upload batch metadata and dates; refuted if manual entries persist after the migration window.
- *Assistant entry during end-of-quarter peak:* Temp_Nina-style support. Supported by `entered_by` values; refuted if entries are attributed to people with no role in the process.

**G. Shared account (Approver_Gita)**
- *Delegated-approver convenience:* One account used by a delegation team. Supported by the delegation policy and assistant `entered_by` values; refuted if the account has no formal owner.

**H. Batch-stamped justifications**
- *Quarter-close back-filling:* Justifications keyed in after the fact from paper or spreadsheets. Supported by the upload log and paper or email evidence dated earlier; refuted if no source record exists before the batch date.

# 3. Case prioritization

**High priority: review first (verification, not accusation)**

- **H1. VendorMaster_Lena + AP_Kim, V-77x bank change  payment approval (44 cases; 9 SoD exceptions; 11 correction loops; 16 fast approvals; 83% manual upload).**
  - *Reason:* Highest financial exposure. It combines a same-person-style exception that cannot yet be reproduced, correction loops after payment approval, and manual channels. It is also the cluster most likely to be explained by mass maintenance.
  - *Inspect first:* Bank-change tickets and migration batch IDs; callback verification logs and their timestamps versus payment approval timestamps; `entered_by` and `approved_by` for the 9 exception cases; the `source_system` value for each event; payment file contents for the 16 fast cases; and vendor-master audit history for V-77x before and after migration.

- **H2. Approver_Gita shared account with Buyer_Carla emergency POs (23 shared-account approvals; part of the 42 Create+Approve exceptions; 84 fast approvals).**
  - *Reason:* Attribution is impossible while the shared account persists, and SoD breaches cannot be tested. Emergency medical supplies carry high stakes.
  - *Inspect first:* Delegation matrix and emergency authorisation records; sign-in logs for the Approver_Gita account; `entered_by` versus `org:resource` for the 23 cases; and whether Create and Approve events come from the same login.

- **H3. Manager_Hugo same-person override/justification (17 cases; 12 with identical quarter-close timestamps).**
  - *Reason:* A direct breach of the stated emergency exception. Batch entry means the justification timing and authorship are unreliable.
  - *Inspect first:* Original workflow timestamps before migration; quarter-close upload logs and the source spreadsheet's author and creation metadata; whether any justification was entered by someone other than the override actor; and whether the 3-working-day window was met on the original workflow date.

**Medium priority**

- **M1. Buyer_Dan + Manager_Hugo weekend repairs (104 cases; 79% emergency; 21% late justification; 62 fast approvals; 29 escalation loops).**
  - *Reason:* Substantial emergency volume with lateness and speed. Weekend timing needs confirmation.
  - *Inspect first:* Weekend emergency authorisation records; repair work orders and goods-receipt evidence; time of override versus time of repair.

- **M2. Create PO exceptions involving bot-mapped legacy_erp records.**
  - *Reason:* Mapping defect could generate or conceal exceptions in either direction.
  - *Inspect first:* Mapping table; Bot_PO execution logs; a sample of 20 flagged cases with raw source records.

- **M3. Bot_Match  AP_Kim exception handling (210 cases; 52 feedback loops).**
  - *Reason:* Human payment approval of automated exceptions is a legitimate control point, and volume is high.
  - *Inspect first:* Exception codes; approval timing for exceptions versus clean matches; whether any AP_Kim approvals bypass the exception queue.

- **L2. Temp_Nina  Buyer_Carla request/assignment (65 cases).** Check whether Temp_Nina is entering records on behalf of Buyer_Carla; this is an entry-attribution question.
- **L3. Aline/Elena and Boris/Felix routine pairs (1,005 cases; 1% late justification; ~5% manual).** Use as the baseline and control group. Monitor only.
- **L4. VendorMaster_Marc + AP_Iris (41 cases; ordinary onboarding/payment).** Monitor as a comparison group for H1.

def actor_sets(d, activity, col):
    s = d.loc[(d[ACT] == activity) & (~d["is_bot"]), [CASE, col]].dropna()
    return s.groupby(CASE)[col].agg(set)

def sod_violations(d, act_a, act_b, col_a, col_b):
    A, B = actor_sets(d, act_a, col_a), actor_sets(d, act_b, col_b)
    common = A.index.intersection(B.index)
    rows = [(c, sorted(A[c] & B[c])) for c in common if A[c] & B[c]]
    return pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=[CASE, "shared_actor"])

# Run three attribution variants; disagreement between variants points to mapping/shared-account artefacts

**4e. Retrospective justification: rule check and declarative model**

```python
def check_justification(d):
    ov = (d[d[ACT] == "Emergency Override"][[CASE, RES, TS]]
          .rename(columns={RES: "ov_res", TS: "ov_ts"}))
    rj = (d[d[ACT] == "Retrospective Justification"][[CASE, RES, TS, "source_system"]]
          .rename(columns={RES: "rj_res", TS: "rj_ts"}))
    m = ov.merge(rj, on=CASE, how="left").dropna(subset=["ov_ts"])
    ts_counts = rj["rj_ts"].value_counts()
    m["batch_stamp"] = m["rj_ts"].map(ts_counts).fillna(0) > 1
    m["same_person"] = m["ov_res"] == m["rj_res"]
    ok = m["rj_ts"].notna()
    m.loc[ok, "wd_gap"] = np.busday_count(
        m.loc[ok, "ov_ts"].values.astype("datetime64[D]"),
        m.loc[ok, "rj_ts"].values.astype("datetime64[D]"))
    m["within_3wd"] = m["wd_gap"].between(0, 3)
    m["violation"] = m["same_person"] | ~m["within_3wd"].fillna(False) | m["rj_ts"].isna()
    return m

just = check_justification(df)
print(just.groupby(["batch_stamp", "violation"]).size())
```

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

**Would increase concern**

- **Bank changes:** Callback verification logs absent or timestamped *after* the payment approval for any of the 9 exception cases; bank-account numbers that recur across multiple V-77x vendors or match employee accounts; bank changes not linked to any migration batch or maintenance ticket.
- **Identity:** Sign-in logs showing one individual using both the change and payment-approval steps (or both emergency override and justification steps) under different display names; same device or session identifiers across "different" approvers.
- **Timing:** Approvals outside working hours or on weekends that have no emergency authorisation; approvals clustered in seconds across unrelated cases.
- **Justifications:** Batch-stamped justifications whose source spreadsheet metadata shows creation after the 3-working-day window and authorship by the override actor.
- **Pattern:** Reciprocal approvals between the same two individuals on high-band orders outside any emergency flag; amounts clustered just below approval thresholds.
- **Correction loops:** LenaKimLena corrections that change bank details or amounts after payment approval.

**Would reduce concern**

- **Bank changes:** Migration batch IDs and maintenance tickets linked to all 44 Lena/Kim cases; callback verification logged before each payment; the 9 exceptions resolved as mapping errors.
- **Identity:** Distinct verified logins for each step; Approver_Gita account sign-in logs showing individual access, or the account retired and replaced by named delegation.
- **Emergency:** Written emergency authorisations (clinical or facilities lead) that precede the fast approvals; workflow timestamps matching ERP timestamps.
- **Justifications:** Original workflow timestamps showing timely justification by a different person, with the quarter-close batch only re-keying records.
- **Mapping:** Corrected legacy_erp mapping that eliminates the 42 Create+Approve exceptions or reassigns them to bot and human identities.
- **Baseline:** Routine pairs (Aline/Elena, Boris/Felix) showing similar loop and approval patterns, which would suggest the emergency and V-77x patterns are workflow features rather than exceptions.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

**Procure-to-Pay Event Log Review: Preliminary Observations**

This review identified control exceptions and data-integrity limitations in last quarter's procure-to-pay event log. The observations are preliminary. They describe control exceptions for follow-up and do not establish misconduct, fraud, or collusion, and no individual has been found to have acted improperly.

Four-eyes exceptions appear in four activity-pair categories, totalling roughly 99 flagged cases (about 4% of cases, with possible overlap). The most concerning subgroup is the cluster of bank-detail changes followed by payment approval for vendor family V-77x (44 cases, handled through manual upload, with 9 flagged exceptions). This may reflect legitimate mass maintenance following a banking platform migration. However, the maintenance tickets are not linked to the procurement cases in the log, and two different named resources appear in exceptions that cannot be reproduced from the resource field alone.

Emergency purchase approvals by two buyer/approver pairs are unusually fast but are consistent with documented emergency channels. Their attribution is limited, because one approver account is shared across 23 approvals.

Data-quality limitations affect the analysis: 12 of 17 retrospective justifications share identical timestamps from a quarter-close migration, a bot/human mapping defect affects legacy_erp records, and summary tables report inconsistent case counts.

We recommend targeted follow-up: (1) reconcile bank-change records with maintenance tickets and callback verifications; (2) obtain sign-in logs and original workflow timestamps for shared-account approvals and retrospective justifications; (3) correct the legacy_erp mapping and re-run four-eyes tests; and (4) confirm authorisation of emergency approvals against delegation records.
