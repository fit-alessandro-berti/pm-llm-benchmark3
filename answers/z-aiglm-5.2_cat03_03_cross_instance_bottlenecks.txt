## 4. What flattening misses

### Job flattening

- **Makes visible:** The sequence ReleaseStageMachine ProcessInspectPackDispatch, with median durations. No job exceeds 1.8 in total duration.
- **Hides:** Batch waits (no `Wait for Batch` event), lot quarantine events, tool-acquisition delays, and shipment consolidation waits. The FIX-A hold on J3 and J4 is invisible because the flattening uses only Job-at-source events.
- **Misleading conclusion:** "All jobs are broadly normal; no outlier." The 3h55m wait of J1 in B1 is absorbed into the 1.1 h median of StageMachine Process, inflating that edge's variance without flagging it as anomalous.

### Machine flattening

- **Makes visible:** Utilization percentages, activity sequences, longest inter-event gaps.
- **Hides:** Why machines are idle. OVEN-1's 73-min gap and CNC-2's 48-min gap are presented as utilization deficits, but the actual causes are RACK-7 quarantine and FIX-A contention, respectively. TEST-1's idle 16:35–17:25 is caused by CAL-3 recalibration.
- **Misleading conclusion:** "Low utilization means no bottleneck." This conflates capacity utilization with flow efficiency. The machines are underutilized *because* upstream synchronization barriers starve them, not because demand is low.

### Batch flattening (hypothetical, not provided)

- **Would make visible:** Batch start/end times, batch membership.
- **Would hide:** Per-job readiness times within the batch, so the variance in individual waiting is invisible. It would show that B1 starts at 12:05 but not that J1 waited 3h55m while J3 waited 10 min.
- **Misleading conclusion:** "Batching is efficient because batch cure time is short." The cure time is not the problem; the synchronization delay before the cure is.

### Tool flattening (hypothetical)

- **Would make visible:** Tool hold durations (FIX-A 09:00–11:40, CAL-3 usage and recalibration).
- **Would hide:** Which job or machine the tool hold blocks. FIX-A's hold by J3 would be visible, but its effect on J4 at CNC-2 would not, because the job-machine-tool triangle is flattened to one dimension.
- **Misleading conclusion:** "FIX-A is busy only 2h40m, so it is not a bottleneck." The tool's utilization is low, but it is a gating resource during a critical window.

### ComponentLot flattening (hypothetical)

- **Would make visible:** Quarantine and release events on L7 and L8.
- **Would hide or duplicate:** The link to jobs is inconsistent. L7 quarantine affects J2 and J5, but the job flattening omits these events. An alternative pipeline might link them, but the current one does not.
- **Misleading conclusion:** "No lot-level delays affect jobs" (if lot events are absent from the job view) or "all lot delays affect all contained-component jobs equally" (if lot events are naively propagated).

### Shipment flattening

- **Makes visible:** CreateLoadDispatch with 0.8 h and 0.3 h medians.
- **Hides:** The interval between a job's pack time and the shipment's creation or load. J1 is packed at 13:30 but S1 dispatches at 19:25; the flattening records only the shipment's own lifecycle, not the per-job consolidation wait.
- **Misleading conclusion:** "Shipments dispatch quickly (LoadDispatch = 0.3 h)." This is true for the shipment object but hides 5+ hours of waiting for already-packed jobs.

### Why low utilization coexists with long waiting

Machine utilization measures busy time divided by calendar time. When synchronization barriers (batches, lots, tools) delay job readiness, machines sit idle waiting for inputs. The idle time is attributed to the machine in the flattening, but the root cause is upstream coupling. Utilization is a necessary but not sufficient condition for bottleneck identification; in this system, the binding constraints are synchronization policies and shared non-machine resources, not machine hours.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

### Narrative 1 — FIX-A contention propagates to shipment delay (6 object types)

**Objects:** J3, FIX-A, CNC-2, J4, S1, J1.

- **Temporal precedence:** J3 acquires FIX-A at 09:00 and releases at 11:40. J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50 (before FIX-A is free). J4's process starts at 11:45. J4 is packed at 19:10. S1 dispatches at 19:25. J1 was packed at 13:30.
- **Resource feasibility:** FIX-A is a single physical fixture. J3's hold makes it unavailable to J4. CNC-2 was idle 10:05–10:47, consistent with waiting for FIX-A.
- **Policy constraint:** S1 consolidates J1 and J4, so J1's dispatch is gated by J4's pack time.
- **Causal speculation:** If J3 had released FIX-A earlier (or if a second fixture existed), J4 could have started CNC-2 at ~10:05, finished and packed earlier, and S1 could have dispatched earlier, reducing J1's consolidation wait. This is plausible but not proven—the 10:47–11:40 CNC-2 activity may involve another job.

### Narrative 2 — L7 quarantine couples J2 and J5 across batches (5 object types)

**Objects:** L7, C2, C5, J2, J5.

- **Temporal precedence:** L7 quarantined at 10:20. J2 had already entered B1 via supervisor override (timing unspecified but before 12:05). L7 released at 13:50. J5 did not proceed until after release. J5 ready for B2 at 14:40.
- **Resource feasibility:** C2 and C5 are physically in L7. A quarantine on the lot restricts all contained components.
- **Policy constraint:** The override for J2 demonstrates that the quarantine policy is not uniformly enforced—supervisors can bypass it in a separate system. J5 had no such override.
- **Causal speculation:** The override for J2 but not J5 suggests that the quarantine was known to be precautionary for C2 but blocking for C5, or that J2's urgency was higher. Without the quarantine, C5 would have been available from ~10:20, potentially allowing J5 to reach B2 readiness earlier. However, we cannot confirm that J5's 14:40 readiness was solely determined by the 13:50 release; other processing steps may have been gating.

### Narrative 3 — CAL-3 recalibration gates J6 through TEST-1 (4 object types)

**Objects:** J5, CAL-3, TEST-1, J6.

- **Temporal precedence:** J5 tests 16:10–16:35. CAL-3 recalibrated 16:35–17:20. J6 ready at 16:05, tests 17:25–17:50. TEST-1 idle 16:35–17:25.
- **Resource feasibility:** Every `Final Test` requires both TEST-1 and CAL-3. TEST-1 is physically free at 16:35, but CAL-3 is in recalibration. J6 cannot test without CAL-3.
- **Policy constraint:** Recalibration is triggered after J5's test (whether by usage count, quality threshold, or scheduled interval is unknown).
- **Causal speculation:** If recalibration were not triggered by J5 (or could be deferred to a non-critical window), J6 could test at ~16:35, saving 50 min. The 30 min before 16:35 (J6 ready 16:05, TEST-1 busy with J5) is TEST-1 occupancy, not CAL-3-specific. This narrative cleanly separates tool-induced delay from machine-induced delay.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

### Intervention 1 — Dynamic batch-minimum policy for OVEN-1

- **Target mechanism:** Batch synchronization (B1, B2).
- **Expected benefit:** Allow batches to start when 2 jobs are ready rather than waiting for all assigned jobs. J1 and J2 (both ready by 08:25) could cure at ~08:30 instead of 12:05.
- **Jobs helped:** J1 (~3h35m saved), J2 (~3h30m saved).
- **Jobs potentially harmed:** J3 would cure in a later sub-batch, adding its own wait. OVEN-1 may run more cure cycles, increasing setup overhead.
- **New cost/risk:** More frequent oven cycles increase energy and setup labor. Quality may differ for smaller batches.
- **Perverse incentive metric:** "Average batch size" could incentivize over-batching if managers are penalized for small batches.
- **Object-centric evaluation:** Compare per-job waiting time in B1 vs. per-job waiting in sub-batches, stratified by job readiness order. Track OVEN-1 cycle count and energy per job.

### Intervention 2 — Duplicate or schedule FIX-A

- **Target mechanism:** Shared-tool contention (FIX-A).
- **Expected benefit:** Eliminate the ~1h40m delay for J4.
- **Jobs helped:** J4 (direct), J1 (indirect via S1 consolidation, if J4 packs earlier).
- **Jobs potentially harmed:** None directly, but capital cost is non-trivial. If FIX-A demand is low overall, the second fixture may sit idle.
- **New cost/risk:** Procurement and storage cost; calibration/maintenance of a second fixture.
- **Perverse incentive metric:** "FIX-A utilization" could incentivize over-use to justify the purchase, creating unnecessary contention elsewhere.
- **Object-centric evaluation:** Measure FIX-A hold duration vs. job wait-for-FIX-A duration across all jobs. Track whether J4's pack time improvement propagates to S1 dispatch.

### Intervention 3 — Split S1 into two shipments or allow rolling dispatch

- **Target mechanism:** Shipment consolidation (S1).
- **Expected benefit:** J1 dispatches ~13:48 instead of 19:25, saving ~5h37m.
- **Jobs helped:** J1.
- **Jobs potentially harmed:** None operationally, but customer may require consolidated delivery; shipping cost increases with two shipments.
- **New cost/risk:** Higher freight cost; possible customer dissatisfaction if partial deliveries are undesired.
- **Perverse incentive metric:** "Shipment count per order" could incentivize splitting all shipments, increasing total cost.
- **Object-centric evaluation:** For each Shipment object, compute max(pack time)  min(pack time) across consolidated jobs. Flag shipments where the spread exceeds a threshold (e.g., 2 h) as candidates for splitting.

### Intervention 4 — Automated lot-release linkage to job events

- **Target mechanism:** Data-integration artifact (L7 events not in Job flattening).
- **Expected benefit:** `Quarantine Lot` and `Release Lot` events on L7/L8 would appear in job-centric views, making lot-induced delays visible to managers.
- **Jobs helped:** All jobs whose components are in shared lots (J1, J2, J3, J5).
- **Jobs potentially harmed:** None operationally, but increased event volume may clutter dashboards.
- **New cost/risk:** Integration engineering effort; risk of false attribution if lot-to-job links are ambiguous (e.g., L7 contains C2 and C5, but only J5 was truly blocked).
- **Perverse incentive metric:** "Number of lot events per job" could incentivize over-linking, diluting causal signal.
- **Object-centric evaluation:** Compare job DFGs with and without lot-event integration. Measure the delta in total job duration attribution. Verify that quarantine events correctly link to blocked jobs (J5) and not to overridden jobs (J2).

### Intervention 5 — Deferred or parallel CAL-3 recalibration

- **Target mechanism:** Shared-tool contention (CAL-3).
- **Expected benefit:** J6 tests at ~16:35 instead of 17:25, saving 50 min. TEST-1 utilization improves.
- **Jobs helped:** J6 (and any subsequent jobs queued behind J6).
- **Jobs potentially harmed:** If recalibration is quality-critical, deferring it risks false test results. A backup CAL-3 unit would avoid this but adds cost.
- **New cost/risk:** Quality risk if recalibration is skipped or deferred past a critical threshold.
- **Perverse incentive metric:** "TEST-1 utilization" could incentivize skipping calibration to keep the machine busy.
- **Object-centric evaluation:** Track CAL-3 recalibration triggers (usage count vs. scheduled vs. quality-driven). Compare J6's test-start delay before and after policy change. Audit test results for any quality regression.

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd

# Assume ocel is a valid OCEL object loaded from the event log

interactions = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)

print("ETOT edges:", list(etot.edges(data=True))[:20])

# --- 3. OC-DFG with two frequency metrics ---
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
# Compare act_count (event frequency) vs. unique_obj_count (distinct objects)
# We extract both from the OC-DFG's internal representation
for act in ocdfg["activities"]:
    e_freq = ocdfg["activities"][act].get("event_count", 0)
    o_freq = ocdfg["activities"][act].get("unique_objects", 0)
    if e_freq != o_freq:
        print(f"Activity {act}: events={e_freq}, unique_objects={o_freq}")

# --- 4. Flattening comparisons ---
job_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Job")
machine_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Machine")
shipment_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Shipment")
lot_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "ComponentLot")

# Performance DFGs for each flattening
job_dfg, job_dfg_perf = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(job_flat)
machine_dfg, machine_dfg_perf = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(machine_flat)
shipment_dfg, shipment_dfg_perf = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(shipment_flat)

# Identify edges with high variance or high median in job flattening
print("\nJob DFG performance (sorted by median):")
for (a, b), perf in sorted(job_dfg_perf.items(), key=lambda x: x[1].get("median", 0),
                          reverse=True)[:10]:
    print(f"  {a} -> {b}: median={perf.get('median'):.2f}, "
          f"min={perf.get('min'):.2f}, max={perf.get('max'):.2f}")

# --- 5. Extract OCEL features for bottleneck discrimination ---
features = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel, obj_type="Job")
print("\nOCEL features (Job):")
print(features.head())

# --- 6. Convert to NetworkX for motif analysis ---
nx_graph = pm4py.convert_ocel_to_networkx(ocel)
# Find jobs connected through shared batches
shared_batches = []
for job_node in [n for n in nx_graph.nodes
                 if n[0] == "Job"]:
    neighbors = list(nx_graph.neighbors(job_node))
    batch_neighbors = [n for n in neighbors if n[0] == "Batch"]
    for bn in batch_neighbors:
        co_jobs = [n for n in nx_graph.neighbors(bn) if n[0] == "Job" and n != job_node]
        for cj in co_jobs:
            shared_batches.append((job_node[1], cj[1], bn[1]))
print("\nJobs sharing batches:", shared_batches)

# --- 7. Waiting estimation from object co-occurrence, not machine gaps ---
# For each Batch object, compute readiness spread of member jobs
# using event timestamps from the flattened Job log
for batch_id in ["B1", "B2"]:
    batch_jobs = [j for (j, b) in shared_batches if b == batch_id]
    ready_times = {}
    for j in batch_jobs:
        j_events = job_flat[job_flat["case:concept:name"] == j]
        stage_events = j_events[j_events["concept:name"] == "Stage Component"]
        if len(stage_events) > 0:
            ready_times[j] = stage_events["time:timestamp"].min()
    if ready_times:
        spread = max(ready_times.values()) - min(ready_times.values())
        print(f"\n{batch_id} readiness spread: {spread}")
        print(f"  Per-job ready times: {ready_times}")
```

This code compares event-frequency and unique-object-frequency per activity (revealing activities where a single event touches many objects, such as `Batch Cure`), computes readiness spreads per batch from job-flattened timestamps (rather than relying on machine idle gaps), and extracts per-job features for discriminative analysis.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

### Observations that would weaken the top-ranked explanations

1. **If OVEN-1 had a scheduled maintenance window 08:00–12:00**, the 3h55m wait for J1 in B1 would be machine availability, not batch synchronization.
2. **If a quality record showed that J1 or J2 required a specific cure profile incompatible with J3**, then batching B1 together would be technically necessary, not merely a policy choice.
3. **If CNC-2 was processing another job from 10:47 to 11:40**, the 53 min would be machine capacity, not FIX-A contention, reducing the FIX-A delay estimate from 1h40m to ~47 min.
4. **If the S1 consolidation was customer-mandated (single delivery location)**, splitting S1 would be infeasible and the consolidation delay would be an external constraint, not an internal policy.
5. **If CAL-3 recalibration was triggered by a failed calibration check (not routine usage)**, deferring it would be unsafe and the 50 min delay would be quality-driven, not tool-scheduling-driven.
6. **If the supervisor override for J2 was recorded before the quarantine event at 10:20**, then J2's entry into B1 was not a bypass of quarantine but a pre-quarantine decision, weakening the "inconsistent enforcement" hypothesis.
7. **If RACK-7's "cleaning/quarantine" until 14:35 was triggered by the L7 lot quarantine itself**, then mechanisms 1 and 4 are not independent—RACK-7 quarantine is a downstream effect of lot quarantine, not a separate barrier.

### Additional fields or event types that would discriminate among hypotheses

1. **Per-job `Wait for Batch` event** with timestamp and reason code—would directly measure synchronization delay and distinguish batch-policy waits from machine-availability waits.
2. **Tool request timestamp** (when a job first needs a tool) vs. tool acquire timestamp—would quantify tool contention precisely without inferring from machine idle gaps.
3. **Batch policy parameters** (minimum jobs, maximum wait time, override authority)—would reveal whether B1's 12:05 start was policy-driven or resource-driven.
4. **Calibration trigger code** for CAL-3 (usage-count, scheduled, quality-fail)—would determine whether recalibration is avoidable or mandatory.
5. **Shipment constraint metadata** (customer requirement, carrier cutoff, consolidation rule)—would distinguish internal policy from external mandate for S1.
6. **Lot quarantine reason** (quality hold, documentation gap, supplier issue)—would determine whether L7's 3h30m quarantine was precautionary or substantive.
7. **Supervisor override log entry** for J2 with timestamp, author, and justification—would clarify whether the override preceded or followed the quarantine and whether it reflects a systemic policy gap or a one-off decision.
8. **RACK-7 cleaning/quarantine reason**—would determine whether it is tied to L7 or to an independent sanitation protocol.
