The shared mechanism is narrow and plausible: where two verification tasks depend on genuinely independent inputs, removing an artificial sequencing convention converts serial waiting into overlapped waiting, so `true_overlap` reduces `waiting_before_assessment` and hence `cycle_time`. That structural claim — a partial order with fewer arcs cannot lengthen the critical path when capacity is unconstrained — travels between sites because it is a property of the ordering, not of the site.

Site-specific mechanisms do not travel. Site A's registry `API_latency` upgrade, its two extra document reviewers, its mix shift toward simple digital claims, and its `queue_huddles` are separate causes of the same observed drop; none is entailed by parallelization. Conversely Site B has causes A lacks: statutory `originals_required`, mandatory `Legal Review` with only two reviewers, and the `IT_case_lock`.

The effect modifiers are `originals_required`, `digital_documents`, `IT_case_lock`, `role_specialization`, `case_complexity`, `legal_capacity` and `assessment_capacity`. Each sits on the path from treatment to overlap or from overlap to cycle time, and each takes a different value at B — precisely the selection-diagram condition under which the causal effect is not directly transportable and must be re-estimated within strata where the modifiers match.

A similar control-flow diagram guarantees nothing because the process tree encodes only ordering, not why the ordering exists. At A the arc `Identity Check -> Document Check` was convention; at B the equivalent arc is entailed by law through `Originals Receipt` for 62% of cases. Two identical-looking arcs with different causes respond differently to the same intervention. The tree also omits capacity, locks, data dependencies and durations, which is where B's binding constraints live.

## 5. Quantitative transport scenarios

Reasoning that maps A's evidence onto B's constraints:

- A's verification service times barely moved (identity −0.4 h, document −0.7 h, both plausibly the API upgrade and mix shift), so the mechanism must act on *waiting*, not work. A's pre-assessment wait fell 6 h (18→12). B's is 20 h.
- Under a serial order, the pre-assessment path is roughly `identity_wait + identity_service + document_wait + document_service`; under a partial order it is roughly the maximum of the two branches. With B's medians (3.4 h and 8.1 h service) the theoretical ceiling on saving per eligible case is about the identity branch, i.e. ~3-4 h of service plus its queueing, call it 4-8 h when queues are not the binding constraint.
- Eligibility: only 38% of B cases (≈1,292 of 3,400) can start `Document Check` at registration. For the 62% originals stratum the only concurrency available is `Identity Check` alongside `Originals Receipt` — but the median originals delay is 18 h, comfortably longer than identity service, so identity work is already effectively hidden; the incremental saving there is small (0-2 h) and comes only from cases where identity currently starts *after* receipt. The DFG supports this: `Identity Check -> Originals Receipt` occurs 1,020 times, i.e. sequencing is already partly overlapped in practice.
- Site-wide median effect ≈ eligible share × per-case saving, damped by any downstream bottleneck. With 38% eligibility and 4-8 h saving, a naive upper bound is 1.5-3 h on the site median — far from A's 9 h, and that is before assessment and legal congestion claw some back.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

**Name:** Conditional partial-order verification with receipt-triggered document work.

**Eligibility rules**
- Wave 1: cases with no statutory originals requirement (38%) **and** no public-sector/high-value flag (i.e. no mandatory `Legal Review`) **and** complexity band low/medium.
- Wave 2: no-originals cases **with** `Legal Review` required, only after legal queue stability is demonstrated.
- Wave 3: originals-required cases, treated with the *partial* variant only (identity concurrent with originals receipt).
- Permanently excluded from full parallelism: cases on the dependency-exception list where document evidence is needed to frame identity queries.

**Concurrent creation and execution**
- Always at registration: `Identity Check` and, when originals are required, `Originals Receipt`.
- For eligible no-originals cases: `Identity Check` and `Document Check` created simultaneously at registration and assigned to different pools.
- For originals-required cases: `Document Check` is created only by the `Originals Receipt` completion event, never at registration.
- `Legal Screening` (a new lightweight triage task determining whether `Legal Review` will be required, and flagging likely supplements) runs concurrently with verification for flagged cases, so legal is not first informed after `Assessment`.

**Statutory prerequisites that remain mandatory**
- `Originals Receipt` strictly precedes `Document Check` whenever originals are legally required.
- Both `Identity Check` and `Document Check` strictly precede `Assessment`.
- `Legal Review` strictly follows `Assessment` for public-sector/high-value cases and strictly precedes the final decision for those cases.
- `Request Supplement` re-opens `Document Check` and forces re-`Assessment`; no decision may be taken with an open supplement.
- `Notify` strictly follows the decision.

**Queue and WIP controls**
- Per reviewer: maximum 6 open verification tasks (mirroring A's rule).
- Per case: maximum 2 concurrently active verification tasks.
- Global: cap on cases in `Assessment`-ready state; if the assessor queue exceeds a threshold, throttle new parallel creation (parallelization is only released when the downstream can absorb it).
- Legal admission control: cap concurrent open `Legal Review` items at a level the 2-reviewer pool can clear.
- Daily 15-minute queue huddle per pool, plus a cross-pool huddle — transferred from A as an explicit, separately-evaluated component.

**Resource responsibilities**
- Identity pool (4) owns `Identity Check`; document pool (7) owns `Originals Receipt` chasing and `Document Check`; assessors (3) own `Assessment` and re-assessment; legal (2) owns `Legal Screening` and `Legal Review`.
- 2 identity and 2 document reviewers cross-trained for surge coverage only.
- A named case coordinator per parallel case reconciles findings before `Assessment` (the reconciliation checkpoint that protects against rework drift).

**Legal-review and supplement loops**
- `Legal Screening` at registration marks supplement-prone cases; for those, `Document Check` is executed in a "scope-limited" first pass, deferring the expensive evidence work until legal has confirmed scope.
- Supplements re-enter `Document Check` with a distinct activity label (`Document Check (supplement)`) so loop iterations are separable in the log.
- A case may not exceed two supplement iterations without escalation.

**Logging requirements**
- System-generated `start` and `complete` timestamps for every task, immutable, with `resource`, `pool`, and `case_lock_bypass` flags.
- Explicit attributes: `originals_required`, `legal_required`, `complexity_band`, `channel`, `wave`, `treatment_arm`, `supplement_count`.
- Manual/back-entered events flagged as such; all overlap and waiting analyses reported both with and without them.

**POWL-style / partial-order specification**

```text
ROOT = StrictPartialOrder(
  nodes = { Register, IdentityCheck, OriginalsGate, DocumentCheck,
            Assessment, LegalBlock, Decision, Notify }
)

# OriginalsGate = XOR( OriginalsReceipt , skip )        # taken iff originals_required
# DocumentCheck = LOOP( DocumentCheck_body , Redo )      # Redo fired by Request Supplement
# LegalBlock    = XOR( LEGAL , skip )                    # taken iff legal_required
#   LEGAL       = StrictPartialOrder( LegalReview -> RequestSupplementXorNothing )
# Decision      = XOR( Approve , Reject )

order edges (irreflexive, transitively closed):
  Register        -> IdentityCheck
  Register        -> OriginalsGate
  OriginalsGate   -> DocumentCheck        # statutory when originals required; vacuous when skipped
  IdentityCheck   -> Assessment           # required
  DocumentCheck   -> Assessment           # required
  Assessment      -> LegalBlock
  Assessment      -> Decision
  LegalBlock      -> Decision
  Decision        -> Notify

deliberately absent:
  IdentityCheck   -> DocumentCheck        # the artificial arc removed by the intervention
  DocumentCheck   -> IdentityCheck

supplement loop (outside the plain partial order, modelled as an OperatorPOWL LOOP):
  LegalReview --RequestSupplement--> DocumentCheck --> Assessment --> LegalReview
```

For a no-originals case the `OriginalsGate` collapses to a silent step, so `IdentityCheck` and `DocumentCheck` become genuinely unordered — that is exactly Site A's `+` construct, but reached only through a data-dependent choice rather than unconditionally.

**How the pilot separates mechanism failure from implementation failure (≤220 words).** The design makes fidelity a measured quantity rather than an assumption. For every eligible treated case the log yields *true overlap* computed only from immutable, system-generated start and complete timestamps, with back-entered events flagged and excluded in a sensitivity run. This splits outcomes into four cells. If overlap is high and waiting before `Assessment` falls as predicted, the mechanism transported. If overlap is high but waiting does not fall, the mechanism is real but irrelevant at B — the constraint lies downstream in the assessor or legal pool, and the bottleneck-shift scenario is confirmed; the remedy is capacity, not sequencing. If overlap stays low despite eligibility, this is implementation failure: the case lock, role hand-offs, or dispatching rules prevented concurrent work, and the causal question remains untested. The fourth cell — low overlap on immutable timestamps but high overlap on manually entered ones — is the specific signature of the IT-lock workaround, and it is exactly the artefact that contaminated Site A's 8%→71% figure.

Version arms sharpen this further: V1 isolates the structural change, while V2 and V3 add WIP caps, reconciliation, and huddles. If V3 improves but V1 does not, the benefit at A was largely coordination discipline, not parallelization — a conclusion that changes what Site B should invest in.

## 8. PM4Py model-construction code

```python
"""
Site A (before / after) and a Site B candidate model.

Site A is expressible as a process tree; Site B is not, because its concurrency is
conditional on data (originals_required, legal_required). We therefore build Site B
with POWL classes so that a strict partial order can encode "Assessment waits for
BOTH verification branches" without forcing an ordering between the branches.
"""

import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    POWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
    OperatorPOWL,
    StrictPartialOrder,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Site A: before and after, as process trees
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

site_a_before_str = """
-> (
    'Register',
    'Identity Check',
    'Document Check',
    'Assessment',
    X ( 'Approve', 'Reject' ),
    'Notify'
)
"""

site_a_after_str = """
-> (
    'Register',
    + ( 'Identity Check', 'Document Check' ),
    'Assessment',
    X ( 'Approve', 'Reject' ),
    'Notify'
)
"""

site_a_before = pm4py.parse_process_tree(site_a_before_str)
site_a_after = pm4py.parse_process_tree(site_a_after_str)

print("Site A before:", site_a_before)
print("Site A after :", site_a_after)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Convert both Site A trees to accepting Petri nets
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

net_a_before, im_a_before, fm_a_before = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(site_a_before)
net_a_after, im_a_after, fm_a_after = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(site_a_after)

for label, (net, im, fm) in {
    "A_before": (net_a_before, im_a_before, fm_a_before),
    "A_after": (net_a_after, im_a_after, fm_a_after),
}.items():
    print(f"\n[{label}] places={len(net.places)} "
          f"transitions={len(net.transitions)} arcs={len(net.arcs)}")
    print(f"[{label}] is_workflow_net = {pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net)}")
    print(f"[{label}] soundness = {pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm)[0]}")


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Site B candidate as a POWL model
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
#
# Structure:
#
#   Register
#     |-> Identity Check                      (always startable after Register)
#     |-> Originals Gate = XOR(Originals Receipt, tau)
#            |-> Document Block               (loop: Document Check + optional redo)
#   Identity Check -> Assessment
#   Document Block -> Assessment              (both required: explicit order edges)
#   Assessment     -> Legal Gate = XOR(Legal Review sub-order, tau)
#   Legal Gate     -> Decision = XOR(Approve, Reject)
#   Decision       -> Notify
#
# The absence of an order edge between Identity Check and the Document Block is the
# intervention itself: they are unordered, hence concurrent when both are enabled.

register = Transition(label="Register")
identity_check = Transition(label="Identity Check")
originals_receipt = Transition(label="Originals Receipt")
document_check = Transition(label="Document Check")
redo_documents = Transition(label="Document Check (supplement)")
assessment = Transition(label="Assessment")
legal_review = Transition(label="Legal Review")
request_supplement = Transition(label="Request Supplement")
approve = Transition(label="Approve")
reject = Transition(label="Reject")
notify = Transition(label="Notify")

# --- Originals gate: statutory receipt for 62% of cases, skipped otherwise -----
originals_gate = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[originals_receipt, SilentTransition()],
)

# --- Document block: receipt strictly precedes document checking --------------
# Modelled as its own strict partial order so the statutory arc is explicit.
document_block = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[originals_gate, document_check])
document_block.order.add_edge(originals_gate, document_check)

# --- Supplement loop: Document Check, then optionally redo and re-check --------
# OperatorPOWL LOOP semantics: children[0] is the "do" part, children[1] the "redo".
supplement_redo = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[request_supplement, redo_documents])
supplement_redo.order.add_edge(request_supplement, redo_documents)

legal_loop = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[legal_review, supplement_redo],
)

# --- Legal gate: mandatory for public-sector / high-value, skipped otherwise ---
legal_gate = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[legal_loop, SilentTransition()],
)

# --- Decision -----------------------------------------------------------------
decision = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[approve, reject])

# --- Root strict partial order ------------------------------------------------
root = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[register, identity_check, document_block, assessment, legal_gate,
           decision, notify]
)

# Registration enables both verification branches.
root.order.add_edge(register, identity_check)
root.order.add_edge(register, document_block)

# (4) Assessment waits for BOTH verification branches — set explicitly.
root.order.add_edge(identity_check, assessment)
root.order.add_edge(document_block, assessment)

# Downstream statutory ordering.
root.order.add_edge(assessment, legal_gate)
root.order.add_edge(legal_gate, decision)
root.order.add_edge(decision, notify)

# NOTE: no edge is added between identity_check and document_block. That omission
# is exactly the Site A intervention, transported only where it is legal.

print("\nSite B POWL model:")
print(root)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Convert the POWL model to an accepting Petri net
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

net_b, im_b, fm_b = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(root)
print(f"\n[B_candidate] places={len(net_b.places)} "
      f"transitions={len(net_b.transitions)} arcs={len(net_b.arcs)}")


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Structural checks
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

print("[B_candidate] is_workflow_net =", pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net_b))
try:
    sound_b = pm4py.check_soundness(net_b, im_b, fm_b)
    print("[B_candidate] soundness =", sound_b[0])
except Exception as exc:  # soundness checking can be expensive on POWL conversions
    print("[B_candidate] soundness check unavailable:", exc)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. Optional playout: inspect the variants the model permits
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

log_b = pm4py.play_out(net_b, im_b, fm_b)
variants = pm4py.get_variants(log_b)
print("\nSample Site B variants (first 8):")
for i, variant in enumerate(sorted(variants, key=lambda v: len(v))[:8]):
    print(f"  {i + 1}. " + " -> ".join(variant))

log_a_after = pm4py.play_out(net_a_after, im_a_after, fm_a_after)
print("\nSample Site A (after) variants (first 5):")
for i, variant in enumerate(list(pm4py.get_variants(log_a_after))[:5]):
    print(f"  {i + 1}. " + " -> ".join(variant))


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 8. What this model cannot encode
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
#
# - DATA-DEPENDENT LEGAL REQUIREMENTS. The originals gate and the legal gate are
#   plain XOR choices. Nothing in the model states that the receipt branch is taken
#   iff originals_required is true (62% of cases) or that legal review is taken iff
#   the case is public-sector/high-value (40%). Playout will therefore generate
#   traces that skip a statutorily mandatory step. Enforcing this needs a data
#   Petri net / DPN with guards, or a decision model mined per gate.
#
# - RESOURCE CAPACITY. The 4/7/3/2 identity/document/assessor/legal pools are
#   invisible here. The model says Identity Check and Document Check MAY overlap;
#   it cannot say whether staff are available to make them overlap. Every queueing
#   effect in the transport analysis — the assessment bottleneck, legal saturation,
#   the bottleneck-shift scenario — lives outside this formalism and needs a
#   simulation model with resource pools and service-time distributions.
#
# - CASE LOCKING. The legacy one-active-owner lock is an implementation constraint
#   that would collapse the partial order back to a sequence at runtime while the
#   model still claims concurrency. Conformance checking against this model would
#   PASS a locked, sequentially-executed case, because a partial order permits any
#   linearization. Detecting lock failure requires start/complete timestamp overlap
#   analysis, not control-flow conformance.
#
# - DURATIONS AND DELAYS. The 18 h median originals delay, the heavy tail, and the
#   waiting times that carry the entire causal argument are not representable. The
#   model is a feasibility statement about ordering, not a performance model.
#
# - WIP LIMITS AND LOOP BOUNDS. The six-open-task cap, the two-supplement escalation
#   rule, and the legal admission cap are policies over multiple cases; POWL is a
#   single-case formalism and cannot express cross-case constraints.
#
# - MEASUREMENT QUALITY. Back-entered timestamps are indistinguishable from genuine
#   ones at the model level; only log-quality attributes can flag them.
```

## 9. Recommendation

**`conditional go`**

**What should be transferred.** Only the structural core: where an ordering arc between `Identity Check` and `Document Check` is a convention rather than a statute, delete it. That claim is defensible on partial-order grounds alone and does not depend on Site A's confounded numbers. Transfer alongside it the two cheap enablers that Site A quietly relied on and that carry no legal or IT risk: the per-reviewer WIP cap of six open verification tasks, and the daily queue huddles. Both are testable as separate arms, and there is a real chance the huddles account for more of Site A's improvement than parallelization does.

**What should not be copied.** Do not copy management's proposal as written. Creating a `Document Check` task at registration for every case is legally incoherent at Site B: for 62% of volume the check cannot lawfully begin before `Originals Receipt`, so the task would sit blocked, inflating WIP and reviewer churn while producing no overlap. Do not copy Site A's numbers. The nine-hour median reduction is jointly produced by parallelization, an 11% FTE increase, a registry API upgrade, daily huddles, and an eight-point drop in high-complexity share — with no control and no negative control. Per-FTE throughput at Site A actually fell slightly. Do not copy the intervention into the legal-review stratum first; with two legal reviewers covering 40% of volume, faster verification simply relocates the queue.

**Which subgroup enters first.** Cases with no statutory originals requirement, no mandatory legal review, and low-to-medium complexity — roughly 38% of volume before the legal exclusion, on the order of 20-25% after it. This is the stratum where Site B most closely resembles Site A and where the mechanism can actually fire.

**Expected benefits and risks.** Realistically a 4-7 hour median reduction inside the treated stratum and 1.5-3 hours site-wide — meaningful but far short of Site A's headline. The two serious risks are bottleneck shift (the median improves while p90 worsens because the three-assessor pool absorbs the gain and complex cases queue behind faster ones) and fake parallelism under the case lock, where measured overlap rises through back-entered timestamps while waiting time does not move at all.

**Triggers.** Expand to wave 2 if true overlap in eligible treated cases exceeds 60% on immutable timestamps, pre-assessment waiting falls, and rework and appeal stay within 2 and 1 points of the concurrent control. Modify — capacity first, sequencing second — if overlap is high but waiting is flat, or if p90 worsens while the median improves. Abandon parallelization and redirect effort to originals-channel digitization and legal capacity if overlap stays below 40% after the lock fix, or if rework exceeds 19% or appeals exceed 6%.

## 10. Assumption register

1. **The `Identity Check -> Document Check` arc at Site B is artificial for no-originals cases** — needed because the entire mechanism is the removal of that arc; if the arc is legally or informationally required even without originals, there is nothing to remove — *test:* review the statutory basis and interview senior reviewers on whether document evidence informs identity queries; check the log for cases where identity was reopened after document findings — *if false:* the eligible share collapses toward zero and the recommendation becomes no-go.
2. **The no-originals stratum is genuinely 38% and identifiable at registration** — needed because eligibility must be decidable before tasks are created — *test:* audit 200 registrations for the originals flag against the eventual `Originals Receipt` event; measure misclassification rate — *if false:* blocked document tasks appear anyway and the IT-lock/WIP failure modes activate; requires a receipt-triggered creation rule with a re-classification step.
3. **Site B's 20 h pre-assessment wait is substantially verification-queue time, not assessor-capacity time** — needed because parallelization only compresses the former — *test:* decompose the 20 h into waiting-for-identity, waiting-for-documents, waiting-for-originals, and waiting-for-assessor from start/complete timestamps — *if false:* the bottleneck-shift scenario dominates; the correct intervention is assessor capacity or arrival smoothing, not sequencing.
4. **The case lock can be resolved or reliably bypassed with genuine, system-generated concurrent timestamps** — needed because without it "parallelization" is unobservable and probably unreal — *test:* run a two-week technical spike; measure the share of treated cases with two simultaneously open, system-stamped tasks and audit manual entries — *if false:* the pilot cannot distinguish mechanism from implementation failure and should be deferred until the IT change lands.
5. **The three-assessor and two-legal pools have enough headroom to absorb faster upstream arrivals in the pilot stratum** — needed because a saturated downstream converts upstream gains into downstream waiting — *test:* compute utilization and queue-length series for both pools over the pre-period; simulate a 20% arrival-rate increase — *if false:* stage entry more slowly, apply admission control, or fund capacity before scaling; expect the tail to worsen.
6. **Parallel verification does not materially degrade decision quality** — needed because Site A already shows adverse drift (rework 11.8→12.4, appeals 4.1→4.4) and Site B starts higher (17%, 5.0%) — *test:* track rework, supplement rate, duplicate external queries, and 90-day appeals by arm with a 12-week follow-up — *if false:* add the reconciliation checkpoint, restrict to low-complexity cases, or abandon; quality losses at 40% public-sector volume outweigh hours saved.
7. **Case mix at Site B stays stable during the pilot** — needed because Site A's eight-point complexity shift is the leading alternative explanation for its results and would corrupt B's too — *test:* monitor weekly complexity, channel, and legal-required shares against the pre-period; pre-register covariate adjustment — *if false:* rely on randomized within-stratum contrasts rather than before/after comparisons, and report mix-adjusted effects only.
8. **Case-level randomization is not fatally undermined by shared-queue interference** — needed because treated and control cases draw on the same identity, document, assessor, and legal pools — *test:* compare control-arm cycle time against its own pre-pilot baseline; run one fully-treated team as a cluster check; inspect queue-length series for arm-correlated shifts — *if false:* switch to team-level or stepped-wedge-only inference, accept lower power, and lengthen the pilot rather than trusting a biased case-level estimate.
