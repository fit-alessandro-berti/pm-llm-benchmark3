## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### Stipulated Facts
1. **CS-9 is the current normative standard**: Effective 2025-01-01, requires Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review before activation, and Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier (except where explicitly overridden).
2. **PD-3 had a defined window**: 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31, with specific eligibility criteria (LOW-risk, DE supplier, approved corporate group).
3. **FD-7 had a defined window**: 2026-07-10 00:00 to 2026-07-12 23:59, with specific scope (critical relief suppliers), caps (EUR 20,000), and post-hoc requirements.
4. **M0 lacks loops**: No Request_More_Info activity exists in the model.
5. **M0 lacks resource constraints**: The different-human rule between requester and approver is not encoded.
6. **CRM migration caused data loss**: Notify_Requester missingness rose from 2% to 29% after 2026-07-01.
7. **Reactivation (SUP-710) and rejected leads (SUP-709) have ambiguous scope**: Neither CS-9 nor M0 explicitly address these lifecycle states.
8. **Label equivalence exists**: QA_Clearance = Quality_Review per audit documentation.
9. **Operations has normalized expired exceptions**: Both PD-3 patterns post-July and FD-7 patterns post-July-12 appear.
10. **SUP-703 and SUP-708 represent material control failures**: High-risk supplier incorrectly processed under pilot; activation preceded sanctions check with no authorization.

### Disputed Propositions
1. **Scope boundary**: Whether rejected leads (SUP-709) and reactivations (SUP-710) fall within M0's governance scope or constitute separate processes.
2. **Authority of PD-3 post-expiry**: Whether the Compliance observer's "monitored exception" language constitutes authority for post-pilot continuation, and whether operations' normalization constitutes de facto amendment.
3. **Authority of FD-7 post-expiry**: Whether factual emergency continuation (SUP-705) justifies extension absent written authorization.
4. **Notification completeness**: Whether CRM-mirrored notifications with mutable links constitute adequate evidence of compliance.
5. **Model vs. behavior gap**: Whether Request_More_Info loops represent noncompliance or merely unmodeled desirable behavior.
6. **Resource rule violations**: Whether SUP-712 (same human via aliases) and SUP-715 (spouses, no disclosure) constitute noncompliance given M0's silence on resources.
7. **Data quality vs. process deviation**: Whether duplicated registration (SUP-711) is a logging defect or indicates something about process execution.

### Burden of Proof Approach
**For declaring noncompliance**: 
- The party asserting noncompliance must demonstrate either (a) violation of an explicit, in-force normative rule where no authorized exception applies, or (b) circumvention of controls resulting in material risk (financial, regulatory, or reputational) even where the formal rule is ambiguous.
- Evidence standard: preponderance of evidence for operational findings; clear and convincing evidence for individual sanctions or retrospective penalties.

**For accepting an exception**:
- The party asserting an exception bears the burden of demonstrating (a) valid authority at time of execution, (b) eligibility criteria satisfied, (c) compensating controls active, and (d) timely documentation.
- Post-hoc rationalization without contemporaneous authority records does not satisfy this burden.
- Emergency exceptions require evidence of exigency, proportional response, and prompt regularization.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

### Core Argument
The operation exhibits systematic noncompliance with CS-9 through unauthorized use of expired exceptions, deviation from mandatory control sequences, normalization of unapproved practices, and material failures in high-risk cases. M0 and CS-9 remain in force; their incompleteness does not license ungoverned deviation.

**PD-3 violations** (SUP-702, SUP-703, SUP-714, SUP-715 via aggregate):
The steering committee explicitly limited PD-3 to 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31. Operations' post-pilot use of Auto_Approve (six cases in first week of August, including SUP-714) has no authority. M0's lack of risk-gating does not excuse executing a pilot-prohibited path. Internal Audit correctly identified this as potentially severe.

**SUP-708** (activation 40 minutes before sanctions check):
No emergency, no pilot, no authorization of any kind. The fact that no transactions occurred before sanctions clearance is fortuitous, not controlled. This represents a breakdown of the fundamental protective sequence: Sanctions_Check  evaluation  activation. Forty minutes of unvetted activation for a HIGH-risk supplier is a regulatory exposure in any sanctions regime.

**SUP-714** (post-pilot Auto_Approve on 2026-08-02):
Operations "believed permanent approval was imminent." Belief is not authority. This represents normalization of deviance: a temporary exception becomes routine practice through inertia and wishful thinking rather than governance action. The 47 Request_More_Info cases show desirable behavior that M0 should capture, but the prosecution does not assert these as noncompliance—they represent a model defect, not a control failure.

The defense will argue that many deviations produced acceptable outcomes. This confuses outcome with process. A pilot that worked well argues for policy change through proper channels. ## 3. Defense Brief

### Core Argument
M0 and CS-9 are materially obsolete, incorrectly scoped, and blind to legitimate operational patterns. The prosecution conflates model deviation with business misconduct, treats data-quality artifacts as process failures, and applies a static model to a dynamic operating environment with multiple authorized (and some quasi-authorized) overlays. The high deviation rate reflects model inadequacy, not operational lawlessness.

### Concurrency and Missing Loops: M0 is Structurally Inadequate

M0 models Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review as concurrent within a `StrictPartialOrder`. This is correct as far as it goes, but PD-3 explicitly relaxes Quality_Review timing (up to two business days after provisional activation) and FD-7 relaxes both Quality_Review and Manual_Procurement_Approval timing. M0 cannot express "Quality_Review may follow activation under specified conditions." The prosecution treats these as sequence violations; the defense argues they are M0's inability to encode authorized temporal flexibility.

The Request_More_Info loop (SUP-707, 47 aggregate cases) is universally regarded as desirable. M0 cannot represent it. Operations has invented a necessary activity that the model omits. Calling 47 cases "deviations" when every stakeholder endorses the behavior reveals the model as the problem, not the operation.

SUP-713 (QA_Clearance = Quality_Review) shows that even label changes break naive activity matching. The model's brittleness to UI/procedure renaming is a data-governance problem, not an operational failure.

### Pilot and Emergency Overlays: Authorized at Time of Execution

**PD-3 cases within window (SUP-702)**: SUP-702 is fully compliant with PD-3: LOW-risk DE supplier in approved corporate group, Quality_Review next business day, CRM notification with stable ID. This is not a deviation from CS-9; it is compliance with an authorized exception to CS-9. The prosecution's framing treats CS-9 as the exclusive standard when the governance framework explicitly created PD-3 as a concurrent authority.

**FD-7 cases within window (SUP-704)**: SUP-704 is textbook emergency compliance: sanctions before activation (as FD-7 requires), EUR 18,000 under the EUR 20,000 cap, daily monitoring, retrospective quality and approval next business day. This is not noncompliance; it is the system working as designed under stress.

**SUP-705 (FD-7 pattern one day after expiry)**: The defense acknowledges this is technically outside authority. However, context matters: flood recovery continued, the supplier was critical, spend was EUR 12,000 (well under the cap), and all retrospective controls were executed. This is a JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION or minor procedural lapse, not material noncompliance. The prosecution's bright-line approach ignores that emergency conditions did not magically resolve at 23:59 on July 12.

**SUP-714 (post-pilot)**: The defense concedes this lacks formal authority. However, the 289 eligible pilot cases demonstrated safety and effectiveness. Operations' belief that permanent approval was imminent, while legally insufficient, reflects organizational reality: the governance body had 312 cases of evidence that the pilot worked and had not acted to either codify or terminate it. This is a governance failure (failure to decide) as much as an operational failure (anticipating decision). The six post-pilot cases should be viewed as the governance body's latency problem, not operations' recklessness.

### CRM Migration: Data Quality, Not Process Failure

The 29% notification missingness is an integration defect. SUP-702 demonstrates the CRM link works when the notification_id is stable. SUP-706's mutable text-field link is concerning but likely reflects the CRM transition's incomplete technical implementation, not suppression of notifications. The prosecution should not equate "event not in the log" with "event did not occur" where independent CRM evidence exists.

SUP-706: CRM contains a message at +3 hours. The link is a mutable text field. This is a data-reliability question, not evidence of missing notification. Corroborating timestamps and message content would resolve this.

### Reactivation and Rejected Leads: Scope, Not Deviation

**SUP-710 (reactivation)**: This case lacks Register_Supplier and Quality_Review. CS-9 has no reactivation procedure. The defense argues reactivation is a separate process outside M0's scope. A supplier with valid prior certification being reactivated is not the same as a new supplier being onboarded without quality review. The prosecution's insistence that all supplier events fit M0's sequence is a scope error.

**SUP-709 (rejected lead)**: The process owner says rejected leads are intake cases; the model owner says M0 begins only after pre-screen acceptance. The scope document is ambiguous. The defense argues SUP-709 is a valid execution of a pre-screening process that M0 does not claim to govern. Sanctions_Check  Reject_Supplier  Notify_Requester  Close_Case is rational for an intake case: the lead arrived, was screened, failed, and was rejected with notification. M0's decision-xor assumes both branches start from the same state, but the intake/onboarding boundary is a scope question, not a compliance question.

### Label Equivalence and Data Artifacts

**SUP-713 (QA_Clearance)**: Audit documents confirm equivalent content. The mapping was approved, albeit one week post-hoc. This is a data harmonization issue, not a process deviation. The operation executed Quality_Review; the log recorded QA_Clearance. The model should accept label mappings.

**SUP-711 (duplicated Register_Supplier)**: Same source transaction ID, migration artifact. This is a data-quality issue requiring deduplication logic, not evidence of registering the supplier twice.

### Distinguishing Trace Deviation from Business Misconduct

The prosecution's strongest cases deserve careful parsing:

**SUP-703 (HIGH-risk, ineligible, EUR 180,000)**: The defense does not defend this case. It represents operator error or misconduct. However, it is one case (plus 13 other ineligible), not evidence of systemic failure. The pilot's eligibility controls failed for 4.5% of cases (14/312). This warrants control improvement, not condemnation of the entire pilot or post-pilot pattern.

**SUP-708 (activation before sanctions check)**: Indefensible. The defense does not claim otherwise. However, the fact that this is the only case of its kind among the evidence suggests it is an outlier, not a pattern.

### Resource Rules: Organizational Controls, Not Process Model

SUP-712 and SUP-715 involve rules M0 does not encode. The defense argues these are properly addressed through organizational controls (identity management, conflict-of-interest disclosure workflows, audit), not by declaring the process model violated. M0's silence on resources means resource violations are not process-model violations. They may be policy violations, but the prosecution's framing conflates the two.

### Aggregate Picture

The 312 pilot cases with 93% apparent eligibility, the 47 Request_More_Info cases universally endorsed, the emergency cases with proper compensating controls, and the successful flood response all paint a picture of an operation that generally achieves control objectives even when formal model compliance is imperfect. The prosecution's focus on bright-line rule adherence ignores that control objectives (prevent sanctioned entities, ensure appropriate vetting, limit financial exposure) were substantially achieved except in identified outliers.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

### Layered Architecture Design

**Layer 1: Revised Control-Flow Model (POWL)**
- Core process structured around concurrent checks, decision points for standard vs. auto-approve vs. emergency vs. rejection paths
- Request_More_Info as a repeatable loop before decision
- Notification before closure
- Silent transitions for scope boundaries (lead intake vs. onboarding; reactivation entry points)
- See Section 7 for executable POWL

**Layer 2: Data-Aware Eligibility, Amount, and Time Rules (DECLARE / log-skeleton / pandas checks)**
- **Auto-approve eligibility**: risk_class = LOW, country = DE, corporate_group in approved_groups, activated during valid_policy_window
- **Spend caps**: Emergency path  EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval; auto-approve path  EUR 50,000 (proposed, to be ratified)
- **Temporal constraints**: 
  - Standard route: Quality_Review within 5 business days before activation
  - Emergency route: Quality_Review within 2 business days after provisional activation
  - Pilot/auto-approve route: Quality_Review within 2 business days after activation
- **Sequence constraints**: Sanctions_Check always before activation (hard constraint, no overlay can relax)
- DECLARE templates: `Precedence(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier)`, `Response(Activate_Supplier, Quality_Review)` with time windows, `Chain_Precedence(Manual_Procurement_Approval, Activate_Supplier)` for standard route only

**Layer 3: Resource and Conflict-of-Interest Rules (Organizational Controls)**
- Requester  Manual_Procurement_Approver (identity resolution with alias management)
- Conflict-of-interest disclosure required for familial, financial, or reporting-line relationships
- Auto-approve requires automated risk classification, not operator selection
- Emergency activation requires designated emergency approver role with documented delegation
- Identity unification across aliases (SUP-712 prevention)

**Layer 4: Policy-Overlay Governance**
- **Policy registry**: Each overlay (PD-3, FD-7, future amendments) recorded with:
  - `policy_id`: unique identifier
  - `effective_start`, `effective_end`: timestamps (null for permanent)
  - `authority`: approving body and record reference
  - `relaxed_constraints`: explicit list of constraints relaxed
  - `eligibility_predicate`: machine-readable conditions
  - `compensating_controls`: required additional checks
- **Case-to-policy binding**: Each case records `applied_policy_id` with immutable timestamp
- **Post-hoc ratification path**: Emergency cases past expiry must be ratified within 48 hours by designated authority or revert to noncompliant status
- **Sunset enforcement**: Automated rejection of expired policy references at execution time

**Layer 5: Explicit Scope Rules**
- **Lead intake vs. onboarding**: 
  - Intake: Register  Sanctions_Check  (Pass  transition to onboarding | Fail  Reject_Lead  Notify  Close)
  - Onboarding: begins after intake acceptance
  - M0 governs onboarding; intake is a separate pre-process
- **Reactivation**: 
  - Defined as separate entry point
  - Prior certifications valid if within 12 months and no material change
  - Requires Sanctions_Check re-execution
  - Skip Quality_Review if prior certification valid and within window
- **Withdrawals**: Supplier or requester may withdraw before activation; requires withdrawal reason and notification

**Which parts belong where**:
| Layer | Technology | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Revised control-flow | POWL (PM4Py) | Core process structure, concurrency, loops |
| Eligibility/amount/time | DECLARE constraints + pandas data validation | POWL cannot enforce data-aware rules; DECLARE for temporal constraints, pandas for attribute checks |
| Resource/COI | Organizational policy + IAM system + audit checks | Not a process-model concern; requires identity management and disclosure workflows |
| Policy-overlay governance | Policy registry database + case-policy binding table | Authorization and eligibility require structured data, not process-model encoding |
| Scope rules | Process architecture documentation + entry-point routing | Distinguishes intake, onboarding, reactivation as related but distinct flows |

  approximated with a parallel branch that defers, not skips, Quality_Review
  and Manual_Procurement_Approval.
- Resource segregation and COI rules are handled in Layer 3.
"""

from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition

# --- Activities ---
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
request_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
provide_info = Transition(label="Provide_More_Info")
sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
activate_standard = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
activate_provisional = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier_Provisional")
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

# Silent transitions for routing and merging
tau_checks_done = SilentTransition(label="tau_checks_done")
tau_info_loop_entry = SilentTransition(label="tau_info_loop_entry")
tau_info_loop_exit = SilentTransition(label="tau_info_loop_exit")
tau_decision_entry = SilentTransition(label="tau_decision_entry")
tau_standard = SilentTransition(label="tau_standard")
tau_auto = SilentTransition(label="tau_auto")
tau_emergency = SilentTransition(label="tau_emergency")
tau_reject_path = SilentTransition(label="tau_reject_path")
tau_provisional_merge = SilentTransition(label="tau_provisional_merge")
tau_activation_done = SilentTransition(label="tau_activation_done")
tau_pre_close = SilentTransition(label="tau_pre_close")

# --- Concurrent checks block (standard and auto routes) ---
# Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review must both complete before proceeding
# to manual/auto approval in standard/auto routes
checks_block = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])
# No explicit ordering between sanctions and quality; they are concurrent

# --- Request_More_Info loop ---
# After checks, before decision: optionally request more info, receive it,
# then loop back to re-execute any needed checks or proceed.
# We model this as: checks -> (info_loop)* -> decision
# The loop body: Request_More_Info -> Provide_More_Info
info_loop_body = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[request_info, provide_info])
info_loop_body.order.add_edge(request_info, provide_info)

# Loop construct: XOR with loop-back
# tau_info_loop_entry -> (info_loop_body -> tau_info_loop_exit) XOR (skip -> tau_decision_entry)
# tau_info_loop_exit loops back to tau_info_loop_entry
info_loop_choice = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=OperatorPOWL.XOR,
    children=[
        StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[
            info_loop_body,
            tau_info_loop_exit
        ]),
        tau_decision_entry
    ]
)
# Note: POWL's OperatorPOWL.XOR does not natively support loops.
# The loop-back from tau_info_loop_exit to tau_info_loop_entry requires
# graph-level edge addition in the outer StrictPartialOrder.
# This is the most defensible approximation: an XOR choice between
# "do one info cycle then loop" and "proceed to decision."

# --- Decision: standard, auto, emergency, or reject ---
# Four exclusive paths
decision = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=OperatorPOWL.XOR,
    children=[
        tau_standard,
        tau_auto,
        tau_emergency,
        tau_reject_path
    ]
)

# --- Standard route: Manual_Procurement_Approval -> Activate_Supplier ---
standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[manual_approval, activate_standard])
standard_route.order.add_edge(manual_approval, activate_standard)

# --- Auto-approve route: Auto_Approve -> Activate_Supplier ---
auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[auto_approve, activate_standard])
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_approve, activate_standard)

# --- Emergency route ---
# Sanctions_Check must occur before provisional activation.
# Quality_Review and Manual_Procurement_Approval are deferred but still required.
# We model: Sanctions_Check -> Activate_Supplier_Provisional
# Then in parallel: Quality_Review, Manual_Procurement_Approval
# Then merge before notification.
emergency_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
# Reuse sanctions check; in the emergency branch we sequence it explicitly
emergency_pre_activation = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[emergency_sanctions, activate_provisional])
emergency_pre_activation.order.add_edge(emergency_sanctions, activate_provisional)

emergency_post_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[quality, manual_approval])
# Quality and manual approval concurrent post-activation

# Emergency route: activation -> post-checks -> merge
emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[emergency_pre_activation, emergency_post_checks])
# Edges added in outer graph

# --- Reject route: reject -> notify -> close ---
reject_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[reject])

# --- Merge activation paths ---
# All activation paths converge before notification
activation_merge = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=OperatorPOWL.XOR,
    children=[activate_standard, activate_provisional, reject]
)

# --- Notification and closure ---
notification_block = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[notify, close])
notification_block.order.add_edge(notify, close)

# --- Assemble the full model ---
M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[
    register,
    checks_block,
    tau_checks_done,
    info_loop_body,
    tau_info_loop_entry,
    tau_info_loop_exit,
    tau_decision_entry,
    decision,
    tau_standard,
    tau_auto,
    tau_emergency,
    tau_reject_path,
    standard_route,
    auto_route,
    emergency_route,
    reject_route,
    tau_provisional_merge,
    tau_activation_done,
    tau_pre_close,
    notify,
    close
])

M1.order.add_edge(register, checks_block)
M1.order.add_edge(checks_block, tau_checks_done)
M1.order.add_edge(tau_checks_done, tau_info_loop_entry)

# Info loop structure: tau_info_loop_entry -> info_loop_choice
M1.order.add_edge(tau_info_loop_entry, info_loop_choice)

# info_loop_choice children:
#   child[0]: info_loop_body -> tau_info_loop_exit
#   child[1]: tau_decision_entry
# Loop-back: tau_info_loop_exit -> tau_info_loop_entry
M1.order.add_edge(tau_info_loop_exit, tau_info_loop_entry)

# From info_loop_choice (via tau_decision_entry) to decision
M1.order.add_edge(tau_decision_entry, decision)

# Decision routing
M1.order.add_edge(tau_standard, standard_route)
M1.order.add_edge(tau_auto, auto_route)
M1.order.add_edge(tau_emergency, emergency_route)
M1.order.add_edge(tau_reject_path, reject_route)

# Standard route to activation merge
M1.order.add_edge(standard_route, activation_merge)

# Auto route to activation merge
M1.order.add_edge(auto_route, activation_merge)

# Emergency route: post-checks after pre-activation
M1.order.add_edge(emergency_pre_activation, emergency_post_checks)
M1.order.add_edge(emergency_post_checks, activation_merge)

# Reject route to activation merge
M1.order.add_edge(reject_route, activation_merge)

# Activation merge to notification
M1.order.add_edge(activation_merge, notify)

# Notification to close
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)

"""
MODEL LIMITATIONS (explicit):
1. The loop-back edge (tau_info_loop_exit -> tau_info_loop_entry) creates a cycle
   that POWL's strict partial order may not natively support. In practice, this
   requires a process-tree loop operator or an XOR-loop construct. The current
   StrictPartialOrder does not formally support cycles; a production implementation
   should use OperatorPOWL(LOOP) or a state-machine pattern instead.
   
2. Quality_Review temporal flexibility is not captured: in auto and emergency routes,
   quality review may occur POST-activation, but the POWL places it in a block
   before the decision. Layer 2 temporal constraints handle the actual timing
   relaxation.

3. The emergency branch uses a separate Sanctions_Check transition
   ('emergency_sanctions') for structural clarity; semantically it is the same
   activity. A real implementation would use the same Transition object reference
   or accept the duplication with documentation.

4. Activate_Supplier_Provisional and Activate_Supplier are distinct labels
   for the same business event under different authority. This is intentional:
   it allows post-hoc audit to distinguish activation paths. A merged label
   would obscure which route was taken.

5. Auto_Approve eligibility (LOW-risk, DE, corporate group) and emergency
   spend caps (EUR 20,000) are NOT enforced by this POWL. They require:
   - Layer 2 data checks before allowing the tau_auto or tau_emergency branch
   - Layer 4 policy-overlay validation of effective dates and authority
"""

# ============================================================

### Summary of the Architecture

The revised model M1 addresses the identified deficiencies while explicitly acknowledging where POWL's expressiveness ends:

1. **Request_More_Info loop**: Approximated via XOR-loop pattern; production implementation should use a proper loop operator. The mechanism allows repeated information requests before the activation decision.

2. **Concurrent checks**: Preserved from M0; Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review are concurrent in standard and auto-approve routes.

3. **Multiple activation routes**: Standard (manual approval), auto-approve, emergency (provisional), and reject paths are all represented as exclusive choices, with appropriate sequencing within each.

4. **Emergency route**: Sanctions_Check before provisional activation (hard constraint), with Quality_Review and Manual_Procurement_Approval deferred but still mandatory, merging before notification.

5. **Policy-overlay table**: Separates authority, eligibility, temporal flexibility, and spend caps from the control-flow model, enabling data-layer enforcement that the POWL cannot provide.

6. **Event collection**: Mandates stable notification IDs, actor identity unification, immutable timestamps, policy references, and eligibility snapshots—directly addressing the data-quality issues in SUP-706, SUP-711, SUP-712, and the CRM migration.

7. **Scope boundaries**: Explicit case typing for intake vs. onboarding vs. reactivation, resolving the SUP-709 and SUP-710 ambiguities through architecture rather than ad-hoc interpretation.
