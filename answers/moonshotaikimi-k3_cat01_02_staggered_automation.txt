```python
rows = []
for b, t0 in ADOPT.items():
    g = df[df.branch == b]
    pre = g[g.month < t0]; post = g[g.month >= t0]
    # calendar-matched controls (never treated) over same windows
    ctl = df[~df.branch.isin(ADOPT)]
    cpre = ctl[ctl.month < t0]; cpost = ctl[ctl.month >= t0]
    for oc in OUTCOMES:
        d_adopt = post[oc].mean() - pre[oc].mean()
        d_ctl = cpost[oc].mean() - cpre[oc].mean()
        rows.append({"branch": b, "outcome": oc, "pre": round(pre[oc].mean(),2),
                     "post": round(post[oc].mean(),2), "change": round(d_adopt,2),
                     "control_change": round(d_ctl,2), "did": round(d_adopt - d_ctl,2)})
contrast = pd.DataFrame(rows)
```

*Causal interpretation (requires §3 assumptions):* medians improved ~3–6h (~7% to 12% of pre means) relative to controls, consistently across three adopters; p90 rose ~6–7h *less* than controls' but remains 7–11% above adopters' own pre levels — relative mitigation, absolute worsening. Escalation shows the cleanest pattern: +0.8–1.0pp steps at adoption in all three adopters at two different calendar dates, with controls flat in those same months.

**5.3 Treatment-intensity analysis.** Within-branch endpoint slopes: Alpha (25%48%, 48.343.3h)  0.22 h/pp; Beta (27%50%, 47.345.0h)  0.10 h/pp. But intensity is almost collinear with calendar time, hence with shock decay — the gradient cannot be separated from "later months were calmer." The August cross-section is non-monotone (Gamma at 32% is fastest at 36.0h; Alpha at 48% is 43.3h), so between-branch dose–response is unusable. Also, non-adopters show a 4–6% fast-path floor, so intensity is not pure new-system use. Panel intensity models (§9) yield small negative coefficients with unreliable cluster-robust SEs.

**5.4 Pre-trends and regression to the mean.** Only three pre-adoption months exist for A/B: Beta drifts up (45.446.4, ~+0.5h/month), Alpha is noisy-flat (50.446.949.4). Controls drift upward JanApr (Delta +4.2h, Epsilon +0.7h) before any treatment — common demand pressure. A formal pre-trend test has near-zero power at T=3. On RTM: adopters were selected on *pre-2026* history we cannot see; their 2026 Q1 values are not local maxima within the observed window, which tempers (but does not remove) the concern. The shared May–June spike-and-reversion among never-adopters demonstrates how much apparent "improvement" a window choice can manufacture.

**5.5 Sensitivity / bounded conclusions (arithmetic  scenarios).** *Median:* (a) naive DiD: 3.4 to 5.6h; (b) adjust for complexity (control-estimated slope 1.5h/pp) and Alpha's 2 FTE: Alpha more negative, Beta  2h  range  2 to 8h; (c) pure shock-decay/RTM scenario (own-baseline only, no control adjustment):  0 on average. Bound: **[8h, 0h], i.e., roughly 15% to 0%**, central  4h. *p90:* (a) DiD: 5 to 8h relative (less worsening); (b) absolute levels: +7% to +11% above pre; (c) mixture story (fast path cuts median, extended path + fraud desk lengthens tail) is consistent with both. Bound: **relative 8 to 5h; absolute level effect unsigned.** Median and p90 must not be collapsed into one number.

## 6. Threats, tests, and decision consequences

| threat | why it matters here | direction of likely bias | test or additional evidence | consequence if threat is severe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Selection into adoption | A/B chosen for poor historical cycle times | Toward overstating benefit (RTM) or understating (permanent disadvantage) | Pre-2026 history; match on levels and trends | DiD invalid; only randomization rescues the claim |
| Nonparallel trends | Controls are smaller, lower-complexity | Unknown; can flip signs | Pre-event coefficients; placebo dates; HonestDiD bounds | Reported effects are artifacts of trend gaps |
| Time-varying confounding | April routing (+10% to adopters), May product change | Toward harm for adopters (demand/mix), or benefit if routed cases are simpler | Covariate-adjusted event study; arrivals-by-class data | Median/p90 changes unattributable |
| Treatment intensity | Binary flag ignores the 2550% ramp | Toward null for availability estimand | Intensity models; event-time profile | Understated effects; wrong dose conclusion |
| Fraud-desk spillovers | Adopters send more borderline cases to a shared desk; desk waits unrecorded | Controls' outcomes worsen  overstates benefit | Desk queue/wait by sending branch | SUTVA fails; DiD contrasts contaminated |
| Staffing | Alpha 2 FTE in May; others ±1 | Toward harm for Alpha (conservative for benefit claims) | Utilization-adjusted models; hours/overtime data | May–June worsening misattributed to automation |
| Case mix | May complexity +3–8pp everywhere | Toward harm post-May; masks benefit | Mix adjustment with stable risk definitions | Benefit overstated as harm or vice versa |
| Anticipation | Gamma pilot; managers prepare | Toward null (contaminated pre-period) | Pilot logs; pre-period practice-change audit | Attenuated event-study coefficients |
| Survey nonresponse | Response rates 20–45%, no counts | Unknown | Response counts; weighting; non-survey proxies (complaints, repeat contacts) | All CSAT claims vacuous |
| Measurement aggregation | Branch-month medians/p90 from ~600–1,400 cases | No systematic direction; inflated uncertainty; ecological fallacy | Case-level data; bootstrap CIs for p90 | Distributional effects masked or overconfident |
| Small number of branches | 5 clusters | SEs too small  overconfidence | Wild cluster bootstrap; randomization inference | False positives drive a bad rollout decision |
| Dynamic treatment effects | Learning ramp; pooling post months | Toward null for mature effect | Event-time profile; per-version metrics | Premature negative (or overstated early) verdict |
| Local co-interventions | Managers changed work practices during rollout | Toward benefit (effort/Hawthorne) | Practice-change logs; qualitative inquiry | Effect not transportable to Delta/Epsilon |
| Regression to the mean | Selection on a bad 2025 | Toward benefit | Longer pre-history; compare to 2025 baseline | "Improvement" is mean reversion, not automation |

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**`expand with an embedded evaluation`**

Next units: Delta and Epsilon — but not as a simple flip. Randomize adoption order (Delta in September, Epsilon in November, or the reverse by coin flip), and, more powerfully, randomize case-level triage assignment within each adopting branch (e.g., 50% of eligible new cases to automated triage, stratified by registration risk class). The staggered branch contrast preserves the E1-style estimand; the within-branch randomization identifies the per-path effect (E2) that branch-month aggregates cannot.

Measurement: keep the existing 8 months of branch-month history, but require at least 6 months of event-level baseline before each switch and at least 6 months post. Collect case-level event logs (activity timestamps, path assignment as intended vs. actual, rework loops), queue states (specialist-review queue length and wait; fraud-desk referral, queue, and wait per sending branch), productive hours/overtime/absence rather than FTE alone, arrivals by a frozen risk-class definition, and CSAT with response counts and denominators.

Spillover monitoring: weekly fraud-desk wait per sending branch; routing-share audits; a cap on borderline referrals if desk wait exceeds an agreed threshold; mandatory logging of local work-practice changes as co-interventions.

Guardrails: a weekly dashboard with pre-registered tripwires — p90 more than 15% above the branch's seasonally matched baseline for two consecutive weeks, escalation above +2pp, rework above baseline +2pp, or CSAT below baseline 0.1 with n  100 — triggers throttling of the automated share and review of triage thresholds; a kill switch returns a branch to manual triage within 24 hours.

Decision rule (pre-registered): scale to remaining branches and raise the automated share if, at 6 months, the case-level randomized contrast shows median cycle time at least 3 hours (~5%) lower with a wild-bootstrap 90% interval excluding +1h; p90 no worse; escalation no more than +1.5pp; rework non-increasing; CSAT non-inferior. If the median improves but the tail degrades, retune triage thresholds and re-pilot rather than scale. If nothing is detectable, redirect investment to staffing where utilization is highest.

## 10. Final claim ladder

- Observed: At adoption, escalation stepped up ~+1pp in each adopter at two different calendar dates while never-adopters were flat in those same months; adopters' automated fast-path share ramped from ~25% to ~50%; and by August adopters' median cycle times were at or below their Jan–Mar baselines while never-adopters remained ~3–5h above theirs.
- Suggestive: Difference-in-differences against never-adopters yields 3.4 to 5.6h on median cycle time and 5–8h less p90 worsening, consistent across three adopters — but confounded by targeted routing, staffing cuts, case-mix shocks, and possible regression to the mean.
- Conditionally causal: Under short-window (April/July) comparisons assuming no other adopter-specific shocks in those months, automation plausibly caused a ~+1pp escalation increase and a transient ~2pp rework reduction; a modest median-cycle benefit (3 to 6h) is causal only if conditional parallel trends hold.
- Not supported: Any single statement of the form "automation reduces cycle time by X%" — median and tail move differently, effects vary over the adoption ramp, identification is confounded, and no CSAT claim is defensible with current measurement.
