# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

## Demand and Service Assumptions
- **Arrivals:** Poisson process within each 2-hour arrival block (08:00–20:00), governed by `expected_arrivals` under the base scenario and modified multiplicatively or additively under scenarios U1–U5.
- **Service Durations:** Ranges given in the prompt represent uniform distributions $[min, max]$ for ordinary cases (probability $1 - p_{complex}$) and complex-tail cases (probability $p_{complex}$). 
  - *Assumption:* When resource multipliers apply, they scale the drawn base service time linearly before adding coordination, handover, or setup penalties.
- **Case Types & Routing Control Flow:**
  - *M:* Intake $\rightarrow$ Mechanical $\rightarrow$ Validation
  - *E:* Intake $\rightarrow$ Electrical $\rightarrow$ Validation
  - *S:* Intake $\rightarrow$ Software $\rightarrow$ Validation
  - *ME:* Intake $\rightarrow$ [Mechanical & Electrical branches] $\rightarrow$ Validation
  - *ES:* Intake $\rightarrow$ [Electrical & Software branches] $\rightarrow$ Validation
- **Parallel vs. Sequential Branches (ME & ES):**
  - *Parallel execution:* Both branches run concurrently on different qualified resources. Requires 8 minutes of coordination delay before Validation. Critical path duration = $\max(\text{Branch 1}, \text{Branch 2}) + \text{handover/setup}$.
  - *Sequential execution:* A single cross-skilled resource executes both branches one after the other. Avoids the 8-minute coordination delay, but the total service time is the sum of both branches plus any internal setup penalties.
  - *Decision Rule (Assumed):* Cases evaluate parallel execution if at least two distinct qualified resources are idle or have queue wait times $< 15$ minutes; otherwise, they fall back to sequential execution on a cross-skilled resource to minimize resource contention.

## Penalties and Operational Constraints
- **Handover Delay:** $+7$ minutes when changing resources between Intake $\rightarrow$ Diagnostic, Diagnostic $\rightarrow$ Validation, or across diagnostic teams.
- **Team-Switch Delay:** $+5$ minutes when moving between mechanical and electrical/software teams (unless a cross-skilled resource retains the case).
- **Setup Time:** $+5$ minutes when a resource switches diagnostic skill family between consecutive cases.
- **Preemption:** Priority P1 cases preempt lower-priority active diagnostic work at most once per case. The interrupted activity incurs a restart penalty equal to $10\%$ of its *remaining active service time*. The preempted task is pushed back to the head of its resource's queue with priority status.
- **Overtime:** Capped at 30 minutes per resource per day. Shifts end sharply at 16:00 (Ada, Ben, Chen, Farah), 18:00 (Deepa), and 20:00 (Emil), plus up to 30 min overtime.
- **Utilization Cap:** Base planned utilization must not exceed $90\%$ per individual resource to maintain operational resilience.

## Objective Formulations
We evaluate policies using two distinct objective formulations:

1. **Objective 1: Tail-Risk & SLA Optimization ($\mathcal{O}_{SLA}$)**
   $$\min \left( \alpha_1 P(\text{Delay}_{P1} > 120\text{m}) + \alpha_2 P(\text{Delay}_{P2} > 480\text{m}) + \alpha_3 P(\text{Delay}_{P3} > 1440\text{m}) + \alpha_4 \mathbb{E}[\text{Delay}] \right)$$
   *Weights:* $\alpha_1 = 0.50, \alpha_2 = 0.30, \alpha_3 = 0.10, \alpha_4 = 0.10$, prioritizing strict compliance with P1/P2/P3 SLA targets ($90\%$ completion within target windows).

2. **Objective 2: Robustness & Fairness Optimization ($\mathcal{O}_{Robust}$)**
   $$\min \left( \beta_1 \max_{s \in \text{Scenarios}} \text{MaxSLAViolation}_s + \beta_2 \max_{r \in \text{Resources}} \text{Utilization}_r + \beta_3 \text{Gini}(\text{ResourceWorkload}) + \beta_4 \text{StarvationRate}_{P3} \right)$$
   *Weights:* $\beta_1 = 0.40, \beta_2 = 0.30, \beta_3 = 0.20, \beta_4 = 0.10$, penalizing extreme bottleneck failures under stress scenarios (U1–U5), unfair workload distribution (Gini coefficient), and severe P3 starvation caused by aggressive P1 preemption.

## Bottleneck Migration Under Shocks (U1–U5)
- **Base Scenario:** Mechanical and Electrical diagnostic pools experience peak utilization during 10:00–16:00. Ada and Ben run near capacity for Mechanical work.
- **U1 (Morning Surge):** Morning arrivals ($08:00\text{–}12:00$) multiply by $1.7$, and P1 share rises by $8\%$. **Bottleneck shifts to early-shift Intake and Mechanical resources (Ada, Ben, Farah)**, causing massive morning queues and triggering cascading preemption penalties.
- **U2 (Electrical/Software Incident):** E and ES demand from $12:00\text{–}16:00$ doubles. **Bottleneck shifts to Chen and Emil**, overloading electrical and software diagnostic channels during midday shifts.
- **U3 (Absence - Chen):** Chen is absent ($450\text{m}$ of electrical/software capacity removed). **Electrical and Software queues spike**, forcing Emil and Deepa into severe overload and violating the $90\%$ utilization fairness cap.
- **U4 (Long-tail Day):** Complex-tail probabilities double ($p_{\text{complex}}$ for diagnostics $\approx 0.30\text{–}0.40$). **Bottleneck shifts downstream to Validation**, where long-running diagnostic outputs flood Validation queues, causing tail delays to breach P1/P2/P3 targets.
- **U5 (Combined Stress - U1 + U3 + IT Outage):** Morning surge, Chen absent, and a 30-minute IT outage blocking Intake ($09:30\text{–}10:00$). **System experiences catastrophic gridlock**: Intake backs up completely, P1 preemption storms lock active diagnostic tasks in endless restart loops, and afternoon shifts absorb unmanageable backlogs exceeding overtime limits.

# 3. Candidate policies

To address the operational trade-offs, we define three concrete, implementable dispatch policies:

### Policy 1: Strict Specialization with Fixed Queues ($\Pi_{\text{Spec}}$)
- **Eligibility:** Cases are routed exclusively to dedicated teams based on case type (Mechanical team: Ada, Ben, Farah; Electrical/Software team: Chen, Emil, Deepa). No cross-skill borrowing.
- **Priority Score:** FIFO within priority tiers (P1 $\rightarrow$ P2 $\rightarrow$ P3).
- **Reservation:** None.
- **Preemption:** P1 cases preempt lower-priority tasks once, incurring a $10\%$ remaining-time restart penalty.
- **Parallelization:** ME and ES branches *always* execute in parallel if both team resources are available; otherwise, branch 2 waits in queue (no fallback to sequential execution).
- **Handover Minimization:** Ignored; strict handovers occur at every stage boundary ($+7\text{m}$ per transition).
- **Overflow Triggers:** None; queues grow unboundedly when a team hits capacity.

### Policy 2: Full Pooling to Any Qualified Resource ($\Pi_{\text{Pool}}$)
- **Eligibility:** Any qualified resource can pick up any case matching their skill matrix, regardless of initial team boundaries.
- **Priority Score:** Dynamic Highest Urgency First (HUF), calculated as remaining time to SLA deadline minus estimated processing time.
- **Reservation:** None.
- **Preemption:** P1 preemption enabled with $10\%$ restart penalty.
- **Parallelization:** ME and ES branches execute in parallel if any two qualified resources are available; if not, immediately switch to sequential execution on the best available cross-skilled resource (e.g., Chen, Emil, or Farah) to avoid coordination delays.
- **Handover Minimization:** Cross-skilled resources retain cases across diagnostic boundaries where possible to avoid the $+5\text{m}$ team-switch penalty.
- **Overflow Triggers:** Dynamic load-balancing across all available qualified staff.

### Policy 3: Hybrid Policy with Reserved P1 Capacity & Controlled Overflow ($\Pi_{\text{Hybrid}}$)
- **Eligibility:** Primary routing follows specialization, but resources maintain a $25\%$ capacity reserve for P1/P2 overflow.
- **Priority Score:** P1 preempts immediately (with $10\%$ restart penalty); P2 and P3 are scheduled via Earliest Deadline First (EDF).
- **Reservation:** $20\%$ of each resource's shift time is ring-fenced for urgent P1 arrivals.
- **Preemption:** P1 preemption allowed once per case. Interrupted tasks resume with priority.
- **Parallelization:** Threshold-based. If queue length $> 3$ or resource utilization $> 85\%$, ME/ES cases force sequential execution on a cross-skilled resource to conserve concurrency bandwidth and eliminate the 8-minute coordination delay. Otherwise, execute in parallel.
- **Handover Minimization:** Setup time ($+5\text{m}$) incurred only when switching skill families; team-switch penalties waived if handled by cross-skilled staff (Chen, Emil, Farah, Ada).
- **Overflow Triggers:** If queue wait time for P1/P2 exceeds $15$ minutes, cross-skill overflow is automatically triggered across all qualified resources. Overtime up to $30\text{m}$ is authorized conditionally for P1 backlog clearance.

# 5. Robust recommendation

## Recommended Primary Policy: **Policy 3 ($\Pi_{\text{Hybrid}}$)**
### Fallback Mode: **Emergency Pooling ($\Pi_{\text{Pool}}$ with Overtime)** triggered under U5 combined stress or when P1 queue wait exceeds $20$ minutes.

### Rationale
While Policy 2 ($\Pi_{\text{Pool}}$) achieves lower average delays in the base case by aggressively sharing resources, it suffers from severe operational risks: excessive skill-switching setup penalties, operator cognitive overload, and the gradual starvation of P3 routine work. 

Policy 3 ($\Pi_{\text{Hybrid}}$) strikes the optimal balance required by Objective 2 ($\mathcal{O}_{Robust}$). By ring-fencing $20\%$ capacity for urgent cases, enforcing threshold-based parallel/sequential switching, and restricting cross-skill overflow until queues build up, Policy 3 protects P1/P2 SLA targets without sacrificing fairness or overwhelming staff with constant skill context-switching. Under uncertainty scenarios U1–U5, Policy 3 gracefully degrades rather than catastrophically collapsing.

### Trigger Thresholds for Mode Switching
- **Switch from $\Pi_{\text{Hybrid}}$ to Fallback ($\Pi_{\text{Pool}}$ + Overtime):** Triggered immediately if P1 SLA attainment drops below $88\%$ in any 2-hour window, or if scenario U5 (Combined Stress) is flagged at intake.
- **Reversion:** Return to standard $\Pi_{\text{Hybrid}}$ once all queues clear below 3 cases and resource utilization drops back below $80\%$.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

- **Gaming of Estimates:** Operators or automated routers might misclassify complex-tail cases as ordinary to bypass strict diagnostic queues, or falsely log preemption status to manipulate workload metrics.
- **Starvation of P3 Work:** Because P1 and P2 cases enjoy priority and preemption rights, P3 routine maintenance cases may experience indefinite deferral (starvation), violating internal equity and client fairness standards.
- **Skill Erosion:** In strict specialization ($\Pi_{\text{Spec}}$), resources maintain deep expertise. In full pooling ($\Pi_{\text{Pool}}$), frequent switching across unfamiliar skill families can lead to skill erosion, higher error rates, and degraded service quality.
- **Excessive Preemption:** Unchecked P1 preemption triggers cascading $10\%$ restart penalties, turning active diagnostic work into churn and destroying net capacity.
- **Coordination Overhead & Validation Bottlenecks:** Parallel ME/ES branches create coordination spikes before Validation. If Validation capacity is static, shifting bottlenecks downstream will cause queue pile-ups right before case closure.
- **Human Interpretability:** Complex dynamic routing algorithms ($\Pi_{\text{Hybrid}}$ with threshold rules) can be opaque to human operators, leading to confusion, lack of trust, and manual overrides.

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design

## Required Event Attributes
To ensure full auditability and process-mining readiness, every event log entry must record:
- `case:concept:name` (Unique Case ID)
- `concept:name` (Activity name: Intake, Mechanical, Electrical, Software, Validation, Coordination, Preempt, Resume)
- `start_timestamp` (Activity start time)
- `time:timestamp` (Activity completion time)
- `org:resource` (Assigned resource)
- `case:priority` (P1, P2, P3)
- `case:type` (M, E, S, ME, ES)
- `lifecycle:transition` (start, complete, suspend, resume)
- `attribute:queue_entry_time`
- `attribute:eligible_resources` (List of qualified staff)
- `attribute:skill_used`
- `attribute:planned_service_time`
- `attribute:actual_service_time`
- `attribute:handover_delay`
- `attribute:setup_delay`
- `attribute:policy_version` ($\Pi_{\text{Spec}}, \Pi_{\text{Pool}}, \Pi_{\text{Hybrid}}$)
- `attribute:scenario_flag` (Base, U1–U5)
- `attribute:sla_clock_remaining`

## PM4Py Analytical Workflow
Using PM4Py, the monitoring pipeline will execute:
1. `pm4py.format_dataframe(...)` to standardize timestamps and case identifiers.
2. `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(...)` to compute waiting times versus active service times per stage.
3. `pm4py.get_service_time(...)` to track drift in service duration distributions across time blocks and detect long-tail degradation (scenario U4).
4. `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(...)` to visualize resource handovers, team-switch bottlenecks, and coordination delays.
5. Inductive Mining / Heuristics Mining to detect process drift, unauthorized loops from preemption restarts, and downstream bottleneck migration toward Validation.

# 9. Limits and validation plan

## What Cannot Be Concluded from Expected Arrivals and Service Ranges Alone
- **Queue Interdependencies & Blocking:** Static offered-load calculations assume steady-state flow, ignoring upstream starvation, downstream blocking (e.g., Validation bottlenecks), and the non-linear compounding of wait times when resource utilization exceeds $85\%$.
- **Preemption Storms:** Theoretical service distributions cannot predict the exact capacity destruction caused by cascading P1 preemption restart penalties under stress scenarios like U5.
- **Human Behavioral Adaptation:** Staff fatigue, cognitive load from frequent skill switching, and operator overrides cannot be derived purely from mathematical service ranges.

## Validation Plan & Safe Pilot Design
To distinguish between $\Pi_{\text{Spec}}$, $\Pi_{\text{Pool}}$, and $\Pi_{\text{Hybrid}}$ without risking SLA breaches for critical P1 cases:
1. **Shadow Mode (Backtesting):** Run all three policy dispatch logics in parallel against historical event logs (U1–U5 scenarios) for 2 weeks without altering actual operational assignments. Evaluate counterfactual wait times and SLA attainment.
2. **Phased A/B Pilot:** Deploy $\Pi_{\text{Hybrid}}$ exclusively during morning shifts ($08:00\text{–}12:00$) on Tuesdays and Thursdays, keeping $\Pi_{\text{Spec}}$ as the control on alternate days.
3. **Safety Guardrail:** Ring-fence $30\%$ P1 capacity and establish an automatic circuit breaker: if any pilot shift breaches P1 SLA attainment below $90\%$, the system instantly reverts to strict specialization until review.
