# 2. Benign alternative explanations

## 2.1 Fast emergency approvals and the same-person justifications

Two plausible benign explanations are:

1. **Documented urgent operations.** Medical supply shortages and safety-critical repairs may justify a pre-established rapid channel with authorized decision-makers and appropriate review.
2. **Delegation and administrative batching.** An assistant may have entered a justification for an authorized human decision-maker. Similarly, 12 justifications may have been uploaded after decisions were already made, without those uploads representing new decisions.

**Supporting evidence:** emergency-request documentation, staffing rosters, delegation records, original approval timestamps, policy-compliant response-time requirements, and evidence of substantive review.

**Evidence that would weaken these explanations:** the same authenticated human actually performed both prohibited actions, emergency requests were unsupported, the override lacked authority, or the required independent justification was absent or late. These findings would still indicate control exceptions rather than prove misconduct.

## 2.2 Lena–Kim rapid bank changes, V-77x concentration, and manual uploads

Two plausible benign explanations are:

1. **A genuine banking-platform migration.** The mass-maintenance activity may have been authorized, linked to supplier records outside the event log, and followed by standard payment processing.
2. **A centralized high-risk supplier process.** AP_Kim already handles higher-risk suppliers. Short bank-to-payment intervals could reflect an established exception workflow rather than preferential treatment.

**Supporting evidence:** maintenance tickets, before-and-after bank records, independent approvals, effective payment dates, a link between each change and the correct supplier master record, and independent confirmation of beneficiary details through a previously trusted channel.

**Evidence that would weaken these explanations:** changes outside the announced migration scope, missing or retrospective authorizations, the same human controlling bank change and payment approval after identity resolution, unverified new beneficiaries, or payment to an account inconsistent with the authoritative supplier master. These signals would warrant investigation, not an allegation of collusion.

## 2.3 Recurring human and automated loops

Two plausible benign explanations are:

1. **Workflow-driven clarification or correction.** Emergency clarification, failed-payment correction, and approval comments can naturally produce return paths.
2. **Logging or automation artifacts.** Shared accounts, migrated events, and automated exception feedback can create apparent two-step loops that do not represent direct interpersonal coordination.

**Supporting evidence:** reason codes for each return transition, failed-payment records, post-approval edits linked to business corrections, and loop rates comparable to other exceptions after source and identity normalization.

**Evidence that would weaken these explanations:** loops produced no documented work, repeatedly bypassed substantive review, or concealed unauthorized edits. A loop itself would not establish why the underlying conduct occurred.

## 2.4 Shared accounts, legacy mappings, and vendor-check/bank-change pairs

Two plausible benign explanations are:

1. **Authorized credential sharing or pooled approval accounts.** A shared account may have been used under an approved delegation policy.
2. **Deliberate role design plus source mapping defects.** Vendor checking and bank maintenance may be assigned to the same operational role while an independent authorization occurs elsewhere. Separately, `legacy_erp` may incorrectly assign a bot resource to a human or vice versa.

**Supporting evidence:** authenticated session logs, workflow approval tokens, delegation start/end times, source-system mapping history, role descriptions, and independent bank authorization.

**Evidence that would weaken these explanations:** no identifiable human approver, delegation outside its effective period, an assistant’s entry being mistaken for the approver’s identity, or a mapping that merges distinct human identities. A bot’s involvement alone is not a human four-eyes violation; existence of independent human approval remains a separate question.

## 2.5 Routine buyer–approver repetition and Bot_Match–AP_Kim

Two plausible benign explanations are:

1. **Stable organizational routing.** Aline–Elena and Boris–Felix may simply be high-volume Team A and Team B assignments with broadly consistent two-hour approval times.
2. **Automated exception handling.** Bot_Match–AP_Kim may reflect AP_Kim processing a high volume of automated matching exceptions rather than personally controlling both sides of a transaction.

**Supporting evidence:** assignment rules, approval thresholds, caseload-adjusted routing, bot execution logs, exception reason codes, and evidence that humans retain decision authority over exceptions.

**Evidence that would weaken these explanations:** unlogged manual changes to bot outcomes, unauthorized overrides, or routing designed to avoid independent review. High frequency alone provides no such evidence.

## 4.1 Filtering the two defined four-eyes rules

```python
import pm4py

raw_log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    resource_key="org:resource",
    lifecycle_key="lifecycle:transition",
)

activity_pairs = [
    ("Create Purchase Order", "Approve Purchase Order"),
    ("Change Vendor Bank Details", "Payment Approval"),
]

raw_four_eyes = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
    raw_log, activity_pairs
)

# Independently verified identity view:
# resolved_log retains raw_log plus org:resource and actor_id.
resolved_four_eyes = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
    resolved_log, activity_pairs
)
```

Then:

- Report **distinct violating cases**, not just matching rows, separately for each rule.
- Stratify by emergency status, amount band, purchase category, region, and source system.
- Compare raw and resolved actors. Different account names are not conclusive proof of different humans; identical shared-account names are not conclusive proof of the same human.
- Keep Vendor Check  Change Vendor Bank Details outside this rule unless policy explicitly defines it as a four-eyes pair.
- Treat the same-person retrospective-justification requirement as a separate explicit check.

## 4.5 Declarative and explicit retrospective-justification checks

```python
# Pseudocode wrapper around the release-specific pm4py.MultiVariants API.
# Include variants containing both activities and variants containing
# either activity alone, so missing justification is visible.
retro_variants = make_multi_variants(
    pm4py.get_variants(raw_log),
    relevant_activities={
        "Emergency Override",

    },
    include_single_activity_variants=True,

retro_model = pm4py.discover_declare(raw_log, retro_variants)

```

The learned model can test existence, co-occurrence, and ordering of override and justification. It does **not** establish the business requirements by itself. Apply these explicit checks:

| Requirement | Test |
|---|---|
| Relevant override has a retrospective justification | Link the actual override and justification instances. If several exist per case, obtain an event or ticket reference rather than arbitrarily joining them. |
| Different human | Compare independently resolved human actors, not merely account labels, entry helpers, or shared logins. |
| Within three working days | Calculate the deadline using the relevant region’s working-day and holiday calendar and distinguish decision time from data-entry time. |
| Emergency timing | Confirm the override was authorized and test whether PO approval occurred on the permitted same working day. Emergency status alone is not a waiver. |

A nine-minute interval may satisfy the three-working-day rule, while a late-looking batch upload may or may not. Missing calendar information or migrated timestamps should produce an **unresolved/incomplete** result, not an assumed pass or failure.

| Domain | Evidence increasing concern | Evidence reducing concern |

Evidence should be evaluated together. A maintenance ticket alone does not resolve a control exception if the payment beneficiary remains unverified; conversely, a shared account alone does not establish collusion if authenticated logs demonstrate properly separated, authorized decisions.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Internal audit reviewed procurement-to-payment events for control exceptions, operational patterns and data reliability. This is not a finding of fraud or collusion, neither of which is established by the supplied log. The defined same-human four-eyes exceptions comprise 42 purchase-order cases (1.8%) and nine bank-change/payment cases (0.4%). The reported 17 retrospective justifications were also recorded by the override actor, contrary to the independent-review requirement; 12 share batch-entry timestamps, requiring source-time verification.

The most concerning subgroup is VendorMaster_Lena–AP_Kim. Its 44-case pair cohort combines 72% V-77x concentration, high-value purchases, 83% manual uploads, rapid approvals and reported two-step payment loops. These signals justify targeted testing, but the reported nine normative violations must not be equated with all 44 cases. A banking-platform migration and missing maintenance-ticket links provide credible benign explanations.

The rapid emergency medical and facilities segments are more readily consistent with specialized urgent work, although emergency status does not automatically waive the two four-eyes rules. Routine buyer–approver repetition and automated matching feedback also have operational explanations.

Review should obtain original maintenance approvals, independent vendor-bank verification, beneficiary details, access and delegation logs, and effective approval identities. Emergency cases should be tested against documented need, authorised same-day handling, substantive review and timely independent justification. Shared-account and legacy-system mappings must be corrected or explicitly treated as unresolved; migrated timestamps must be compared with source records. Broader testing should use corrected identities, complete populations and suitable working-day/holiday calendars before drawing conclusions.
