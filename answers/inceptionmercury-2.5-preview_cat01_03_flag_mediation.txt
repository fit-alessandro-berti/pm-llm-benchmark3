

## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | P1 vs P0 | Flagged cases | Cycle time | Total policy effect | Yes | Flag is post-treatment |
| E2 | Flagged vs Unflagged (at intake) | All cases | Triage duration | Association | Yes | Risk/complexity confounding |
| E3 | Flagged cases (Review on) vs Flagged (Review off) | Flagged cases | Time to manual start | Controlled direct effect | No | Counterfactual unobserved |
| E4 | Flagged vs Unflagged (in review) | Cases entering review | Manual queue wait | Interventional indirect effect | Yes | Shared queue interference |
| E5 | Flagged cases (Escalate) vs Flagged (No Esc) | Escalated flagged | Senior service time | Controlled direct effect | No | Escalation selection bias |
| E6 | Policy P2 vs P0 | Entire system | Total cycle time | Separable effect | No | Interference between cases |
| E7 | Flagged vs Unflagged | All cases | Decision outcome (Reject) | Association | Yes | Unobserved underlying risk |

We rely on interventional indirect effects rather than natural effects because cross-world assumptions fail under interference: one case’s triage changes queue congestion for others.

                |                |                 |

Interference arises because `triage_service` and `manual_queue_wait` depend on system congestion shared across cases. Exposure-induced confounding occurs since the flag dictates entry to queues; queue length affects subsequent wait times for all cases, violating the stable unit treatment value assumption. Treatment-confounder feedback exists because earlier decisions affect later resource availability. Unobserved reviewer capacity limits identification of direct service duration effects.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Within-risk-score | Local threshold (RDD) around 0.70 | Complexity, arrival_load | Continuity of outcomes | Pre-treatment balance check | Discontinuity in unflagged group |
| Queue-state-aware | Stratify by queue length at arrival | Timestamp, arrival_load | Queue length not confounded | Compare wait times conditional on length | No difference after adjustment |
| Policy-change | Introduce P2 in subset of cases | Day of week, load | No spill-over effects | Compare flagged cycles in new region | Flagged cycle time decreases |
| Mediation-oriented | Decompose cycle time into segments | Flag status, risk score | Mediation model correctly specified | Sequential mediation test | Manual review accounts for 80% delay |
| Negative-control outcome | Use non-time outcome | Arrival_load, complexity | Outcome not affected by flags | Compare rejection rates | Rejection rate shifts significantly |
| Unobserved risk | Sensitivity analysis using proxy | Risk score | Risk score correlates with true risk | Bound the bias | Significant change in causal effect |
| Interference-aware | Instrumental variables for congestion | Case order, load | Valid instrument | Weak instrument F-test | Weak instrument reduces precision |

Pilot Selection: P2 (Conditional Review)
**Eligibility:** Cases with `risk_score` between 0.60 and 0.80 and `complexity` Medium/High.
**Randomization:** Randomly assign 50% to auto-review (P0) and 50% to human decision (P2).
**Queue-Guardrail:** Limit manual queue depth to 10 cases; overflow bypasses manual review.
**Primary Outcome:** Total cycle time from arrival to close.
**Secondary Outcomes:** Manual review rate, escalation rate, and rejection rate.
**Data Needed:** Timestamps for triage, review start/end, and final decision.
**Stopping Conditions:** 1,000 cases completed or 30 days passed; if flagged case rejection drops >10% stop.
**Evaluation:** Compare median cycle times; adjust threshold if P2 delays unflagged cases >5%.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. If unobserved underlying risk for flagged cases is lower than for unflagged cases, the flag may be protecting rather than delaying.
2. If manual reviewers work faster on flagged cases due to clearer priorities, the observed longer service time is confounded by selection, not policy.
3. If queue length is unrelated to cycle time, then the interference hypothesis is false, and delays stem from individual case complexity.
4. If timestamps represent clock time and not business hours, the observed delays may be due to night-time processing rather than queue congestion.
5. If flagged cases with low risk scores are still reviewed due to policy error, the effect reverses when conditioning only on true high-risk cases.
6. If reviewers delay unflagged cases intentionally, the flag's benefit may be overstated in observed data compared to true potential.

## 9. Final synthesis

The data directly shows flagged cases receive triage faster (0.2h wait vs 2.5h) but enter longer manual queues and escalation paths. The observed mechanism is plausible: the flag triggers routing decisions that force cases into congested, high-latency stages. However, this interpretation remains unidentified because unobserved case complexity drives both flagging and service time. If flagged cases are intrinsically harder, the flag itself may not be the cause of delay.

Queue interference complicates identification; flagged cases might block unflagged ones, making direct effects hard to isolate. To improve both performance and knowledge, the most useful intervention is P2 modified: conditional manual review for medium-risk scores. This pilot decouples triage routing from mandatory review. It requires randomization by risk band and guards on manual queue depth. By comparing cycle times conditional on risk, we can estimate whether review time is truly redundant or necessary for quality. This design minimizes interference while maximizing causal insight into downstream bottlenecks.
