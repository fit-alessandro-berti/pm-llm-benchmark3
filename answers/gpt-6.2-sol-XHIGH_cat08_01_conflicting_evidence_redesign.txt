# 1. Current-process diagnosis

The fast path is valuable for some claims and costly for others. The frequency DFG shows 3,600 of 5,000 intakes going to auto eligibility screening, 2,550 transitions from that screen to Fast approval, and 2,300 transitions from Fast approval to Dispatch replacement. Their median elapsed times are 0.5, 0.4, and 1.8 hours respectively. Fast approval costs 31% less before rework, but the reported advantage falls to 9% after complaints, reshipments, and service credits. Complaint or reopen occurs in 14.1% of fast-approval cases, compared with 9.2% overall and 3.3% on the completed quote–approval–repair sequence. Claim mix and selection into completed repair can explain part of the gap, so these figures cannot establish the effect of sending the same claim down a different path.

- **Speed has different value by segment.** Premium low-risk accessories take a median 8 hours on the fast path versus 46 hours on the manual path, with 6% versus 3% complaint or reopen. The manual median crosses the 24-hour service-credit threshold. Standard low-risk accessories take 13 versus 52 hours, with 8% versus 4% complaint or reopen. Their tolerance for 72-hour completion with good status visibility gives the operation more room to resolve exceptions. For Premium high-value devices the comparison is 20 versus 96 hours and 22% versus 5% complaint or reopen. For Standard high-value devices it is 28 versus 120 hours and 18% versus 6%. The expected cost of a wrong replacement is EUR 410 for high-value devices and EUR 95 for low-risk accessories.
- **Several delays surface after the apparent win.** Dispatch to complaint or reopen has a median of 70 hours and a p90 of 180 hours, with incorrect replacement parts implicated in the long tail. Reopen to manual review or the escalation board has a p90 of 100 or 130 hours respectively. Before fulfillment, 26% of claims entering Request missing evidence repeat the activity, and the return edge to manual review has a p90 of 88 hours. The unclear-defect or missing-evidence group has a 140-hour median and the highest abandonment. Manual review to quote has a p90 of 60 hours across the reported edge and often violates the temporal profile for Premium cases. Part-price confirmation is a known source of that delay. Repair also has a quality problem of its own, with 12.5% rework for repaired high-value devices versus 4.0% for repaired low-risk devices.
- **Aggregate fitness masks material policy exceptions.** Replay fitness is 0.91, while only 68% of traces fit. Alignments flag fast approval before evidence completeness, complaints after close, and high-value dispatches without a model-expected risk review. Audit those traces against the actual predicates. R1 requires manual eligibility or fraud/risk review before fast approval above EUR 400. R2 governs dispatch and has a specific Premium low-risk accessory exception. A complaint after initial close calls for tracking the next episode and checking whether manual review occurred before the subsequent close or denial under R3. Low precision also means the discovered auto–risk–repair combinations should be checked against policy before being adopted as routes.
- **Use case records to size the problems the graphs locate.** The listed terminal edges sum to 3,625 against 5,000 starts. Reconcile trace coverage, open cases, event ordering, and linked reopens before calculating rates from counts. Directly-follows counts describe adjacent events, and elapsed edge times can combine queueing, customer wait, and work. Use linked traces to measure end-to-end percentiles and time in each queue.

# 2. Redesign A: Segment-aware accessory replacement

## Flow

At intake, use the updated screen to verify customer tier, product, claim value, risk, and evidence status. Route clearly identified low-risk accessories through fast approval when policy permits. Before releasing a replacement, match the approved device identifier, revision, and region to the replacement SKU and the item being picked. Send ambiguous matches to a fulfillment specialist for a compatibility check. Route uncertain eligibility, unresolved value or risk, and claims outside the approved accessory rules to the appropriate reviewer. Require a qualifying human review before any fast approval above EUR 400. Confirm evidence before dispatch for Standard claims and log any use of the R2 exception for verified Premium low-risk accessories. Give Premium cases priority in the compatibility queue when the 24-hour window is at risk. Link complaints to the original case and route reopens to manual review before any new close or denial.

## Target

The two low-risk accessory segments represent 43% of the reported mix. Start with clearly identified products. A mid-risk device can use this route only after its defect and compatibility rules have been validated. High-value devices stay on a governed review route.

## Mechanism

Keep the sub-hour median auto-screen steps and put the additional work where the wrong-part failure arises. Deterministic matches can pass inside fulfillment. The proposed pre-dispatch gate could add 6–18 hours depending on its design, so measure elapsed time and catch rate separately for automatic matches and manual exceptions. Standard customers get a useful status and next update time when a check delays dispatch.

## Trade-offs

Some claims that previously moved straight to dispatch will wait for evidence, review, or a matched part. Updated rules can raise manual-review inflow by 15–35%, and even an accessory gate can push Premium cases over 24 hours. The design depends on reliable catalog and pick data. It leaves repeated evidence collection and repair quality largely untouched.

# 3. Redesign B: High-value technical triage with parallel quote preparation

## Flow

Give high-value claims a human case owner. With sufficient information for preliminary diagnosis, start eligibility review and technical triage in parallel. The technician identifies the likely repair, checks parts and inventory, and prepares a provisional quote while eligibility is being resolved. Use current part-price data for routine quotes and seek supplier confirmation for missing or stale prices. Route fraud indicators and claims subject to specialist review to the risk specialists. Once the required checks clear, choose repair or verified replacement by defect, feasibility, total expected cost, and the customer's service commitment. Issue a confirmed repair quote for customer approval before starting repair. Verify replacement compatibility and confirm evidence before releasing a replacement. Require a quality check that retests the reported failure mode before releasing a repaired device, with failed checks routed back for repair and retest. Reopens return to manual eligibility review before subsequent close or denial.

## Target

Premium and Standard high-value devices and defects with meaningful misdiagnosis or repeat-repair risk. Reserve bounded early triage capacity for Premium cases and provide a decision time and updates. Keep Standard work in a visible queue with age-based priority to prevent starvation. Simple accessory claims retain their accessory route.

## Mechanism

Preserve human judgment on EUR 410 wrong-replacement exposure while taking sequential waiting out of the reviewed path. Parts pricing, diagnosis, and eligibility can progress concurrently before commitment. A verified replacement remains available when it is technically preferable. The repair release check addresses failures that otherwise escape to customers and may initially raise recorded rework by detecting more defects internally.

## Trade-offs

Preliminary technical work can be wasted on claims later denied or abandoned. Skilled labor, supplier data, and inspection capacity are required, and some high-value claims will take longer than historical fast approval. The customer approval wait and physical repair time remain. Priority for Premium work consumes capacity that must be accounted for in Standard tail times.

# 4. Redesign C: Assisted evidence completion with a single case owner

## Flow

Classify missing evidence and unclear defects at first contact. Use a defect- and product-specific checklist, prefill purchase or warranty details when already available, and consolidate known gaps into a single request. Offer guided upload and a phone or agent-assisted route into the same record. Assign a case owner to validate submissions as they arrive, explain any remaining gaps, and give customers a next update time. Keep the evidence record and request history attached to the claim so reviewers can reuse it without sending duplicate requests. Once enough evidence and defect detail are available, send the claim to the authorized eligibility, risk, or technical route. Use documented reminders and distinguish customer withdrawal, nonresponse, and cases still awaiting action. Record evidence confirmation for dispatch, apply the narrow R2 exception only where eligible, and send any reopened claim through manual review before a later close or denial.

## Target

The 22% unclear-defect or missing-evidence group, including customers who need help gathering or submitting evidence. A complete, clear intake progresses immediately through the relevant downstream route.

## Mechanism

Interrupt the Request missing evidence → Manual eligibility review → Request missing evidence loop by resolving known gaps together and making evidence reusable. Better defect information can also reduce misroutes and improve the quality of the first quote. A shared record, assisted access, and proactive updates address the abandonment and customer effort that a digital-only portal could worsen.

## Trade-offs

Assisted intake moves labor earlier and can create a callback queue. Some claims will still require additional evidence as diagnosis progresses. Successfully recovering abandoned claims can increase legitimate downstream review and fulfillment workloads and raise spend per intake even as processing cost per resolved claim falls. This design leaves high-value repair inspection and replacement compatibility to the downstream process.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

- **A.** Stale SKU mappings or a mismatch between the system and the picked item can produce false passes. Overly broad exception rules can swamp fulfillment checks and manual eligibility, particularly if updated screening raises manual inflow toward the upper end of 15–35%. Monitor errors on audited passes and capacity on exceptions. The likely bottleneck shifts from near-unlimited auto screening to compatibility resolution and, for value or eligibility uncertainty, the manual queue.
- **B.** Stale part prices can produce revised quotes and a new approval loop. Work released too early can fill the technicians' queues with claims that will never reach repair. Inspection may queue after repair campaigns, and specialist availability remains vulnerable on Mondays and after promotions. The likely bottleneck shifts from waiting for quote confirmation toward technician or inspector capacity, then to customer approval if quote preparation improves faster than acceptance.
- **C.** Customers with limited digital access may drop out if assisted options are difficult to reach. A broad checklist can front-load unnecessary requests, and inconsistent owner decisions can recreate repeated requests across channels. More complete cases can then queue at eligibility or risk review. The likely bottleneck shifts from repeated evidence handoffs to callbacks and validation first, then to downstream review and fulfillment as completion improves.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

1. **Establish a compliant baseline before live assignment.** Put the same minimum R1–R3 controls in every pilot and comparison arm. Record claim value and the qualifying review before fast approval above EUR 400, evidence confirmation or the explicit Premium accessory exception before dispatch, and a new manual review after each reopen before close or denial. Reconcile model diagnostics with written policy for high-value risk review. Link original claims, reopen episodes, and fulfillment attempts, and audit event order against case records. Set queue limits using measured active handling times and arrival patterns. Seven reviewers provide 252 productive hours per week, and 82% utilization is about 207 hours. Use that as an expansion guardrail, with daily monitoring for bursts. Keep the escalation board for cases ordinary review cannot resolve given its one daily slot.

2. **Pilot A first on Standard low-risk accessories with clear product identifiers.** Randomly assign eligible cases at intake to A or the current accessory flow with the shared controls, blocking by defect group and week. Start with Standard because its 72-hour tolerance gives room to observe gate delays before exposing Premium cases to the 24-hour credit threshold. Expand if the 72-hour completion share and p90 hold or improve, confirmed wrong-part shipments fall, and mature complaint and cost outcomes show no material deterioration. Sample cases that pass the gate to check for missed errors. Add Premium in a capped wave only when elapsed gate times support a stable or improved share within 24 hours, with no meaningful rise in service credits. Pause affected routing on a verified policy breach or sustained queue growth at the manual guardrail.

3. **Pilot C next on claims classified as evidence-deficient or unclear at intake.** Compare the combined checklist, shared record, and assisted channel with compliant usual intake. An initial go criterion could be a 20–30% relative reduction in repeated requests among comparable evidence-deficient starts, with stable or lower abandonment and improved durable resolution time. Count requests made through assisted channels as well as the portal, and check results by channel and need for assistance. A fall in requests accompanied by more abandonment, longer callback waits, or a growing downstream backlog calls for revising or capping the pilot before expansion.

4. **Pilot B on high-value cases with adequate intake evidence and reliable parts coverage.** Compare parallel preparation and defect-specific release checks with usual compliant reviewed handling. Include Premium and Standard strata and cap work in progress across technicians and inspectors. Expand where quote p90 falls, durable resolution time improves or stays within an agreed tolerance, and mature wrong-part, repeat-repair, and complaint outcomes improve without eroding Standard service. Use total cost per intake and per resolved claim to decide whether avoided failures justify additional labor. Include ordinary weeks and a representative demand spike before scaling specialist-dependent work. Test C feeding into B in a capped cohort once each change has been measured on its own.

5. **Follow the claim beyond the first close.** Report intake-to-decision, intake-to-fulfillment, and intake-to-last-resolution time using medians, p90s, and SLA attainment by segment. Track pending age, abandonment, denials and their audit results, customer contacts, gate catches, actual wrong parts, internal rework and repeat repairs after release, repeat requests, service credits, and labor hours by role. Use a common follow-up window, initially 30 days after first disposition, and extend it if late complaints continue to materially change the result. Keep all assigned starts in the analysis, show unresolved claims and disposition mix, and compare like cohorts across the same demand periods. Set materiality margins before assignment and report uncertainty alongside effects. Include reshipments, credits, and repeat handling in fully loaded cost. If time saved at dispatch or quote reappears in customer effort, downstream queues, repeat repair, or reopened-case age, the end-to-end result determines whether to expand.
