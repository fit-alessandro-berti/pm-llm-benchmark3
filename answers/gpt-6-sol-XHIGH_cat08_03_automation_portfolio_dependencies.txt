## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

- **Data and artifact chain:** A1 must identify the right case; A2 must choose the right product and exposure path. A5 reduces incomplete submissions before A6 extracts figures. A6 needs source-document provenance and analyst validation before its output can support credit analysis. A8 can assemble a useful pack only after KYC, normalized statements, credit notes, and any required valuation reconcile. A9 needs the authoritative committee decision (or approved decision for cases without committee), final terms, and clearance status. A11 depends on all of these plus valid signing. A fast registration or pack bot cannot repair defects earlier in this chain.
- **Controls and legal order:** A4 and A3 may assist KYC, but a named compliance owner makes the clearance decision. A9 must compare contract terms with the decision, prevent signing before KYC clearance, and retain evidence of every rule result. A10 must enforce identity and consent as well as the KYC gate; A11 must refuse activation while any issue is open. The 380 mismatch-to-contract directly-follows instances warrant checking clearance evidence, not assuming the arc itself proves a breach. A12 needs agreed remediation policy. For High-exposure SME, system roles must keep credit analysis and committee packaging with different people; software is not a second approver.
- **Human escalation capacity:** Three compliance specialists, with work concentrated on two seniors, are a hard capacity constraint for A3/A4. Queue and triage rules, reserved escalation slots, and a manual fallback must precede volume expansion. A5 can send more complete cases to nine credit analysts; A6 should therefore follow closely. Analysts retain judgment on High-exposure cases and poor PDFs. Legal ops' four FTE shift toward rule maintenance and exceptions, especially at month end; RMs need to own clearer document instructions rather than simply send more nudges.
- **External synchronization:** A7 can identify collateral need early, route to available valuers, and chase late work, but cannot automate the valuer's 58-hour median wait away. In Equipment finance, where collateral is required in 71% of cases, valuation and KYC/document work should proceed in parallel where policy permits. Committee packaging must still wait for the final accepted valuation. Weekly High-exposure committee cadence remains even with A8.
- **Integration budget:** Reserve the two deep core-banking integrations in months 1–6 for A9's authoritative contract/control data and A6's validated financial-data flow. Run A4 through a read-only case/KYC view with human disposition recorded in the existing workflow, and A5 through the portal and RM process. Any deeper A4 writeback waits until after month six. This plan treats portal and case-view connections as light interfaces; IT must confirm that classification before committing the schedule.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

| Portfolio (four modules each) | What it optimizes | What it sacrifices |
|---|---|---|
| **Quick win: A1, A5, A9, A10** | Visible high-volume registration saving, fewer document chases, contract control, and a shorter signing wait. A9 is the required control-strengthening module. | Leaves the 31-hour mismatch queue, analyst normalization load, and external valuation wait largely intact; rapid clean inflow may worsen them. A10 must wait for a reliable KYC signing gate. |
| **Bottleneck/synchronization: A4, A5, A6, A9** | Removes blockers before KYC clearance and credit, reduces document loops, protects analyst capacity, and prevents bad contracts/signing. A9 strengthens controls. | Does not directly change the 58-hour external valuation wait or customer signing behavior; requires manual valuer routing and RM adoption. |
| **Compliance first: A2, A3, A9, A12** | Correct exposure routing, reviewed KYC triage, decision-to-contract checks, and visible remediation. A9/A12 strengthen controls. | Slower labor and cycle-time gains: A3 remains human reviewed, missing documents and normalization still delay credit, and valuation/signing waits remain. Poorly tuned A2/A3 could increase specialist work. |

These are alternative allocations, not a sequence of twelve modules. For an Equipment-finance-heavy pilot with reliable valuer partners, A7 can replace A5 in the bottleneck portfolio, but only with a manual missing-document checklist and measurement of whether external wait actually falls.

## 4. Expected process-level effects

**Quick win (A1, A5, A9, A10).**

- **Cycle time:** A1 trims the early receive-to-register step (11,880 directly-follows instances) but is unlikely to change the longest gated paths by itself. A5 can prevent repeat document cycles; A10 can reduce signing delay after contract preparation. Mismatch, normalization, and valuation variants remain slow.
- **Waiting time:** Registration's 1.5-hour median queue and signing's 44-hour median pre-activity wait are targets; the latter depends on customer response. KYC/mismatch, the 28-hour pre-credit queue, and external valuation may become more prominent.
- **Rework:** A5 should reduce financial-review-to-request-to-review cycling; A9 should reduce decision/contract mismatch, especially the 14% contract-remediation rate in Credit-line renewal. A1 duplicate errors could create new rework.
- **Handovers:** Fewer RM/customer document chases and legal remediations, but faster registration increases handoffs into KYC and review. A10 reduces manual signing reminders.
- **Resource demand:** Back-office effort falls where utilization already drops below 65% after 15:00; analysts and compliance may receive work faster. Legal ops shifts to exceptions and rule upkeep; RMs help customers who cannot use e-sign.
- **Control violations:** A9's term and KYC checks should reduce unsafe contract/signing paths, provided A10 refuses provisional signing. Four-eyes violations still need separate role enforcement.

**Bottleneck/synchronization (A4, A5, A6, A9).**

- **Cycle time:** Best broad-based prospect because it removes repeated or blocking work before credit and legal. A4 targets 1,750 KYC-to-mismatch cases, 1,160 mismatch-to-KYC returns, and a step critical in 82% of affected cases. A5/A6 improve the review-to-normalize-to-credit chain. Equipment-finance and weekly High-exposure committee paths retain residual waits.
- **Waiting time:** Aim to reduce the 31-hour wait before mismatch resolution, 20 hours before normalization, 28 hours before credit analysis, and 22 hours before contract preparation. The 58-hour valuation and 44-hour signing waits remain; these medians must not be summed as a predicted case-time saving.
- **Rework:** Better document instructions reduce the 3,300 review-to-request and 2,800 return arcs; verified entity evidence reduces KYC cycling. A9 addresses the 650 contract-to-control-remediation and 510 return arcs. Poor extraction is routed to review rather than silently creating credit rework.
- **Handovers:** Fewer RM/customer and KYC/specialist repeat handovers; structured financial data and reconciled terms simplify analyst/legal handoffs. The compliance and credit decisions still have named human owners.
- **Resource demand:** A4 concentrates difficult residual cases among three specialists, so queue capacity must be protected. A5 raises clean analyst arrivals while A6 releases analyst time; QA for difficult PDFs remains. Legal ops shifts to exceptions and month-end rule oversight. Monitor whether committee or valuation becomes the new constraint.
- **Control violations:** A4 records human KYC resolution; A9 blocks uncleared KYC and mismatched terms before signing. Separate staffing and access controls must still address the 5.8% High-exposure four-eyes violation rate. No expected reduction is credited to automation until sampled cases show it.

**Compliance first (A2, A3, A9, A12).**

- **Cycle time:** May shorten misrouting and remediation tails, but broad improvement is limited by missing-document loops, normalization, customer waits, and external valuation. High-exposure committee cadence remains.
- **Waiting time:** Correct classification can avoid wrong queues after register-to-classify (11,680 instances). KYC pre-classification may reduce routine-review time, while false positives could lengthen the specialist queue. The 18-hour wait before document review and 58-hour valuation wait persist.
- **Rework:** Fewer wrong-path cases and more explicit fixes for contract-remediation loops are plausible; A12 also reveals policy ambiguity that must be resolved by people. A3 false negatives could conceal rework rather than eliminate it.
- **Handovers:** More structured routing among back office, compliance, legal, and remediation owners; human KYC escalations remain and may grow. A2 should not force KYC then financial review into a serial workflow merely because the log records them sequentially.
- **Resource demand:** Compliance specialists and policy owners face more review and rule maintenance; analysts still spend time normalizing statements. The two senior specialists must not become the hidden bottleneck.
- **Control violations:** Strongest direct coverage of exposure routing, KYC exceptions, and contract-term matching. A9 can stop the 6.5% provisional-signing pattern, subject to system enforcement; the 1.1% needing remediation must still be worked. Four-eyes requires explicit separate-user enforcement, not just a cleaner pack.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Risk introduced or exposed | Compensating control |
|---|---|
| KYC false negative, mistaken entity match, or wrongly cleared signing | A3/A4 suggest and assemble evidence only; compliance signs off. A hard system gate requires recorded KYC clearance before signing. Review all high-risk cases and sample apparent negatives by segment. |
| False positives and exception overload | Calibrate thresholds against specialist capacity; show reason codes; route by severity; cap work in progress and retain a manual path. Track specialist queue age and overrides, not only automation rate. |
| Model or document-format drift | Version models and rules, monitor errors by applicant/product/document type, run periodic labeled reviews, and roll back a failing version. Send unsupported or low-confidence PDFs to analysts. |
| Bad source data propagating through A6, A8, or A9 | Keep source-document links and field-level provenance; reconcile KYC, amounts, collateral, decision, and terms; block incomplete artifacts rather than fabricate values. |
| Outdated contract rules or policy ambiguity | Legal/compliance own an effective-dated rule library, test every policy change on representative cases, and approve documented overrides. A12 records root cause and owner for repeated exceptions. |
| Lost auditability or blurred responsibility | Immutable case trail of input, model/rule version, suggestion, confidence or reason, human override and rationale, final decision, timestamps, and actor identity. An automated recommendation never becomes the accountable approver. |
| Four-eyes failure during month-end rush | Enforce distinct user identities and role separation for High-exposure credit analysis and committee packaging; block same-person completion, staff a second reviewer, and report violations daily. |
| Faster flow shifting queues to analysts, committee, legal, or operations | Track arrivals, WIP, utilization, and p90 queue age at every gate; release cohorts gradually, reserve exception capacity, and rebalance staffing before scaling. |
| Valuer quality or customer friction | Set valuer SLAs and quality checks without accepting a deficient valuation; test A5 wording with RMs/customers, permit assisted submission, and measure response and complaint rates. |

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

1. **Weeks 0–4: baseline and design.** Segment Micro, SME, High-exposure SME and product variants. Reconstruct case-level elapsed times, median/p90 waits, loop counts, clearance/signing order, terms mismatches, four-eyes breaches, specialist and analyst WIP, and month-end behavior. Check timestamps and actual overlap of KYC and financial review before assigning causal savings. Name the compliance, credit, legal, RM, and IT owners; confirm which interfaces count as deep core integrations.
2. **Months 1–3: A9 control pilot, then A4 assisted resolution.** First run A9 rules in shadow against decisions and contracts, including Credit-line renewals; release generation only for cases with reconciled terms and recorded KYC clearance. Run A4 with read-only evidence, specialist disposition in the existing workflow, and a manual fallback. This first wave visibly strengthens controls. **Gate:** no contract or signing release without clearance and terms match; no increase in control-remediation or specialist p90 wait; legal/compliance sign off on sampled outputs. A9 is deep integration **1**; A4 has no core writeback in this period.
3. **Months 4–6: A5, then A6 on supported documents.** Train RMs on product-specific instructions and test portal nudges for response quality and complaints. Pilot A6 after completeness checks, with analyst validation of extracted fields and explicit handling of unreadable PDFs. A6 is deep integration **2**. **Gate:** fewer repeated document requests and normalization corrections in comparable cohorts, stable or lower analyst p90 queue age, and no rise in credit or committee artifact errors. If clean inflow grows faster than analyst capacity, slow A5 expansion until A6 and staffing catch up.
4. **Months 7–12: expand by variant and stabilize.** Extend A4/A5/A6/A9 only to segments that pass the gates. Use non-module process changes to flag collateral early, route valuers by capacity, chase late valuations, enforce separate High-exposure reviewers, and set committee cutoffs. Review Equipment-finance valuation waits and signing delays for a second-year A7 or A10 decision. **Scale/stop gate:** compare matched variant cohorts on end-to-end median and p90 cycle time, critical-path waiting, rework loops, handovers, exception aging, resource load, complaints, and control breaches. Stop or roll back any cohort with a KYC-before-sign event, an unapproved terms mismatch, or a four-eyes breach attributable to the new workflow; investigate every exception before resuming.

## 7. Final recommendation

Choose **A4 + A5 + A6 + A9** for year one. It addresses the KYC exception that is often critical, the document loop that repeatedly returns cases upstream, the normalization work that constrains nine analysts, and the legal/control gate that protects signing. The pairing of A5 with A6 matters: cleaner customer submissions without faster normalization would simply move the queue to credit analysis. A9 makes the portfolio control-strengthening and protects the decision-to-contract transition while KYC remains a human responsibility.

Do **not** spend the first scarce module slot on A1 solely because registration occurs almost everywhere. Its eight-minute service time and 1.5-hour pre-activity wait are material labor opportunities, but back-office capacity is not the dominant constraint and faster registration could feed the specialist and analyst queues. A1 is a sensible later low-cost win if capacity and budget permit. The main first-year trade-off is leaving the long external valuation and customer-signing waits to operational management; if Equipment-finance cases dominate and valuer SLAs prove actionable, substitute A7 for A5 only after protecting document quality with an RM checklist and checking the effect on the whole case.
