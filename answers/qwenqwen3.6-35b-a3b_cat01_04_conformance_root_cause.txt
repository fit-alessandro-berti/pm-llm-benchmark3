The UI release renamed `Classify` to `Risk Screen`, and the event gateway introduced `Queue Routed`, inflating log cardinality and breaking label alignment. Simultaneously, the ETL change to warehouse-ingestion time for `Decision` and `Notify` caused timestamp inversions, reversing the control-flow order for a significant subset of cases. These data changes alone reduced fitness from 93% to 32%. Furthermore, a policy update mandated `Supervisor Sign-off` for high-value claims, but the normative model was not updated, causing systematic deviations for those cases.

Stable business outcomes (cycle time, appeal rate) suggest the underlying operational process has not fundamentally deteriorated; reviewers are likely still performing work as intended, but the recorded events no longer match the model due to logging semantics, technical artifacts, and timing errors. The residual deviations after data normalization and model extension may reflect minor real changes (e.g., `Manual Override` for routing) or undetected noncompliance, but the primary driver of the collapse is the divergence between the recorded log and the normative model, exacerbated by the model's inability to capture data-aware constraints. Better fitness via overpermissive models (optional sign-off) confirms that the process is largely conforming once the model and data are aligned.

## 7. Provisional conclusion

The measured conformance collapse is primarily a measurement artifact resulting from the convergence of three data-model mismatches and one model obsolescence. The UI release renamed `Classify` to `Risk Screen`, and the event gateway injected `Queue Routed`, breaking label alignment. The ETL change to warehouse-ingestion time caused timestamp inversions, reversing `Decision` and `Notify` for a significant subset of cases. These changes reduced fitness from 93% to 32%. Additionally, a policy update requiring `Supervisor Sign-off` for high-value claims was not reflected in the normative model, causing systematic deviations.

Stable business outcomes (cycle time, appeal rate) strongly suggest that the underlying operational process has not deteriorated. Reviewers are likely performing work as intended, but the recorded events no longer match the model due to logging semantics, technical artifacts, and timing errors. The massive fitness recovery after label mapping, technical-event removal, and source ordering confirms that the collapse was driven by data quality and model misalignment rather than a real process breakdown.

However, a real compliance problem cannot be ruled out. Residual deviations, such as `Enhanced Review` without `Compliance Check` and `Manual Override` usage, require investigation. The stability of outcomes suggests noncompliance is not systemic or impactful on results, but the incomplete detection of incidents means undetected bypasses may exist.

Confidence is high for data/model causes (90%+). Confidence is low for noncompliance (30%), as evidence is ambiguous and detection is incomplete. Immediate actions: IT must fix the ETL ordering and update the pipeline to map `Risk Screen` and filter `Queue Routed`. Process owners must update the normative model to include optional `Supervisor Sign-off` and clarify `Request Info` semantics. Compliance must conduct a manual audit of missing checks and overrides, stratified by reviewer and high-value cases. It must not be claimed that noncompliance is absent, nor that the model is fully adequate without data-aware constraints.

## 8. Model-governance recommendation

1. **Model Versioning:** Link normative process models to specific UI, API, and policy versions. Automate model updates when policy changes are detected, ensuring the model reflects current requirements before conformance checks.
2. **Event-Semantic Contracts:** Define strict contracts for event labels, including aliases and mappings. Implement label normalization in the ingestion pipeline to handle UI changes without breaking conformance checks.
3. **Technical-Event Policy:** Classify events as business or technical. Automatically filter or tag technical events (e.g., routing, system actions) to prevent them from inflating log cardinality or distorting control-flow analysis.
4. **Timestamp Provenance:** Mandate the use of source-system sequence numbers or event IDs for ordering events. Disable ingestion-time ordering for conformance checking to prevent timestamp inversions caused by pipeline delays.
5. **Data-Aware Constraints:** Extend process models to capture critical data conditions (e.g., high-value thresholds) that trigger different control-flow paths. Use data-aware process mining to validate compliance with data-dependent rules.
6. **Post-Release Validation:** Establish a validation protocol that runs conformance checks on a sample log immediately after UI or pipeline releases. Compare results against baselines to detect regressions in data quality or model fit before they impact operations.
