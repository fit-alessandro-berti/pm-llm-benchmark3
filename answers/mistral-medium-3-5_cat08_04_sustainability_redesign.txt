# Sustainable Process Redesign for Industrial Spare-Parts Distribution

---

## 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Boundary:**
- **Inside:** All activities from order receipt to delivery confirmation, including production (CNC, heat treatment, inspection), packaging, transport, and rework loops. Environmental impacts considered: energy use (kWh), transport emissions (kgCO2e), material scrap (kg), and packaging material (kg).
- **Outside:** Upstream material extraction, end-of-life disposal, and customer-side impacts (e.g., downtime costs). Supplier logistics and third-party carrier operations are partially included via transport_kgco2e but not fully controlled.

**Assumptions:**
- Transport emissions (kgCO2e) are proportional to route mode and distance (implied by the data).
- Energy use (kWh) is dominated by production steps (CNC, heat treatment) and scales with batch size as described.
- Rework and scrap are directly tied to process paths (e.g., inspection failures, packaging incompatibility).
- Service level is measured by late-delivery risk and cycle time against promised lead time.
- Resilience is maintained if alternative routes (per R1) and emergency production capacity exist.
- Customer behavior (e.g., tolerance for longer lead times) is segment-dependent (Regular vs. Service-contract).

**Uncertainty:**
- Exact carbon intensity of energy sources (e.g., grid mix for CNC machines) is unknown.
- Obsolescence risk (6% for large batches) may vary by product type and demand volatility.
- Rebound effects (e.g., increased demand due to faster service) are not quantified.

## 3. Intervention options

### **S1. Green shipping recommender**
- **Target:** Standard-stock Service-contract orders (16% of cases) and Made-to-order Regular orders (22%).
- **Mechanism:** Replace air/van transport with ground or consolidated truck when promised lead time can be met with 85% probability (per SP1). Uses route reliability data and customer segment tolerance.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:**
  - **Service:** May increase late deliveries for ~22% of Standard-stock Service-contract orders (current SP1 violation rate). Mitigated by priority picking.
  - **Quality:** No direct impact.
  - **Resilience:** Consolidated trucks may reduce redundancy (risk to R1), but ground options remain.
- **Monitoring evidence:**
  - Track % of Standard-stock Service-contract orders switched from air to ground.
  - Monitor late-delivery rate for switched orders vs. baseline.
  - Validate SP1 conformance (ground/consolidated truck usage when slack allows).

---

### **S2. Dynamic batching with waiting-time caps**
- **Target:** Made-to-order orders (34% of cases) using CNC_A or heat treatment.
- **Mechanism:** Batch CNC_A and heat treatment jobs dynamically, but cap batch wait time by segment:
  - **Critical-downtime:** Max 2h batch wait (emergency production path).
  - **Service-contract:** Max 8h batch wait.
  - **Regular:** Max 24h batch wait.
  - Disband batches if wait time exceeds cap; process immediately.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:**
  - **Service:** Reduces late deliveries for Service-contract (currently 13-18%) by limiting wait times.
  - **Quality:** No direct impact, but may reduce rework if rushed batches are avoided.
  - **Resilience:** Maintains flexibility to prioritize urgent orders.
- **Monitoring evidence:**
  - Median batch wait time by segment.
  - % of batches disbanded due to time caps.
  - Energy per item (should improve for larger batches within caps).

---

### **S3. Shift Made-to-order production from CNC_B to CNC_A**
- **Target:** Made-to-order orders (34%) currently routed to CNC_B (higher rework rate: 18% vs. 9% for CNC_A).
- **Mechanism:** Route to CNC_A when queue length and due-date slack allow it. CNC_A has lower rework but longer wait times.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:**
  - **Service:** May increase cycle time for some orders (CNC_A is slower), but reduces rework loops (currently 5% of MTO orders).
  - **Quality:** Reduces scrap (CNC_B has higher rework  more material_scrap_kg).
  - **Resilience:** No direct impact; CNC_A and CNC_B are both available.
- **Monitoring evidence:**
  - % of MTO orders shifted to CNC_A.
  - Rework rate and scrap kg for shifted orders vs. baseline.
  - Cycle time impact for shifted orders.

---

### **S4. Replace air for Standard-stock Service-contract with priority pick + ground**
- **Target:** Standard-stock Service-contract orders (16%) shipped by air (currently ~5% of all orders use air for stock).
- **Mechanism:** Use priority picking (reduce pick time by 50%) + ground transport for orders where promised lead time allows it. Air is only used if ground + priority pick cannot meet the deadline.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:**
  - **Service:** Late deliveries may increase slightly (current air late rate: 3%; ground: 8%), but priority picking mitigates this.
  - **Quality:** No impact.
  - **Resilience:** Ground transport is more reliable during disruptions (per R1).
- **Monitoring evidence:**
  - % of Standard-stock Service-contract orders switched from air to ground.
  - Late-delivery rate for switched orders.
  - Transport kgCO2e reduction.

---

### **S5. Consolidate truck departures twice daily for non-urgent stock orders**
- **Target:** Standard-stock Regular orders (39%) shipped via ground or consolidated truck.
- **Mechanism:** Schedule consolidated truck departures at fixed times (e.g., 10 AM and 4 PM) for non-urgent orders. Non-urgent = promised lead time > 48h.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:**
  - **Service:** Adds 12-24h to cycle time for consolidated orders (current median: 34h for ground). Acceptable for Regular customers with longer lead times.
  - **Quality:** No impact.
  - **Resilience:** Consolidated trucks may be less flexible during disruptions (risk to R1), but ground options remain.
- **Monitoring evidence:**
  - % of Standard-stock Regular orders using consolidated trucks.
  - Transport kgCO2e reduction (35-55% vs. individual ground).
  - Cycle time increase for consolidated orders.

---

### **S6. Improve packaging-route compatibility checks**
- **Target:** All orders with reinforced packaging (3470 + 620 = 4090 orders) or packaging rework (260 + 360 = 620 orders).
- **Mechanism:** Add a pre-shipment check to ensure packaging type (standard/reinforced) matches route_mode and part geometry. Flag incompatibilities before shipment.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:**
  - **Service:** No direct impact.
  - **Quality:** Reduces packaging rework (currently 5% of orders) and damage-related returns (40% reduction for reinforced packaging).
  - **Resilience:** No direct impact.
- **Monitoring evidence:**
  - % reduction in Rework packaging paths.
  - Damage-related return rate for Made-to-order heavy parts.

---
### **S7. Add inspection quality gate for high-rework combinations**
- **Target:** Made-to-order orders (34%) using CNC_B or heat treatment (high rework rates: 18% for CNC_B, 1.7x late-delivery risk for heat treatment batches).
- **Mechanism:** Introduce a mandatory secondary inspection for:
  - All CNC_B-produced parts.
  - Heat treatment batches >10 items.
  - Parts with historical rework rates >10%.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:**
  - **Service:** Adds inspection time (may increase cycle time by ~5-10h).
  - **Quality:** Reduces rework loops (currently 5% of MTO orders) and scrap.
  - **Resilience:** No direct impact.
- **Monitoring evidence:**
  - Rework rate for targeted combinations vs. baseline.
  - Cycle time impact for orders with secondary inspection.

---
### **S8. Replace dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes**
- **Target:** Critical-downtime Service-contract orders (11%) using dedicated_van (620 orders).
- **Mechanism:** Partner with a courier service offering shared emergency lanes (e.g., multi-customer vans) for Critical-downtime orders. Dedicated vans are only used if shared lanes are unavailable.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:**
  - **Service:** May increase cycle time slightly (shared lanes may have minor delays), but dedicated vans are retained as backup.
  - **Quality:** No impact.
  - **Resilience:** Shared lanes may be less reliable during peak demand (risk to R1), but dedicated vans remain for true emergencies.
- **Monitoring evidence:**
  - % of Critical-downtime orders using shared lanes vs. dedicated vans.
  - Transport kgCO2e reduction (dedicated van: 52 kgCO2e; shared lane: ~30-40 kgCO2e estimated).
  - SP2 conformance (justification for dedicated van usage).

---
### **S9. Split Critical-downtime into true downtime and precautionary urgent**
- **Target:** Critical-downtime Service-contract orders (11%).
- **Mechanism:** Classify Critical-downtime orders into:
  - **True downtime:** Machine is down; requires immediate delivery (use dedicated van/air).
  - **Precautionary urgent:** Risk of downtime; can tolerate slightly longer lead times (use ground + priority).
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:**
  - **Service:** Precautionary urgent orders may have longer cycle times, but true downtime orders are unaffected.
  - **Quality:** No impact.
  - **Resilience:** Reduces overuse of dedicated vans, freeing capacity for true emergencies.
- **Monitoring evidence:**
  - % of Critical-downtime orders classified as precautionary.
  - Transport kgCO2e reduction for precautionary orders.
  - Late-delivery rate for precautionary orders.

---
### **S10. Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular MTO customers**
- **Target:** Made-to-order Regular customers (22%).
- **Mechanism:** During order intake, offer longer lead times (e.g., +24-48h) in exchange for:
  - Ground transport instead of air.
  - Larger batch inclusion (reducing energy per item).
  - Consolidated truck shipping.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:**
  - **Service:** Longer lead times for Regular customers (acceptable if communicated).
  - **Quality:** No impact.
  - **Resilience:** No direct impact; may reduce pressure on urgent paths.
- **Monitoring evidence:**
  - % of Regular MTO customers accepting longer lead times.
  - Transport kgCO2e and energy kWh reduction for accepted orders.

---
### **S11. Reduce partial shipments by changing availability-check rules**
- **Target:** Partial shipment path (7% of orders: 1010 cases).
- **Mechanism:** Modify availability-check to:
  - Only allow partial shipment if the backordered items can be shipped within 48h.
  - Otherwise, wait for full availability or cancel (per customer preference).
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:**
  - **Service:** Reduces cycle time for partial shipment orders (currently 79h median) but may increase cancellations.
  - **Quality:** Reduces packaging rework (partial shipments often require repackaging).
  - **Resilience:** No direct impact.
- **Monitoring evidence:**
  - % reduction in partial shipment orders.
  - Late-delivery rate for orders that were previously partial shipments.

---
### **S12. Sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides**
- **Target:** All orders violating SP1, SP2, or Q1.
- **Mechanism:** Require explicit justification (e.g., customer contract, downtime risk) for:
  - Air/van transport when ground is feasible (SP1).
  - Dedicated van without Critical-downtime justification (SP2).
  - Non-reinforced packaging for heavy parts (Q1).
  Log all exceptions for audit.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:**
  - **Service:** No direct impact; ensures overrides are intentional.
  - **Quality:** Reduces Q1 violations (correlated with returns/rework).
  - **Resilience:** No direct impact.
- **Monitoring evidence:**
  - % of SP1/SP2/Q1 violations with valid justifications.
  - Reduction in Q1-related rework/returns.

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching rebound:**
   - **Risk:** Dynamic batching (S2) with time caps may reduce energy per item but increase the number of small batches, leading to higher total energy use if caps are too strict.
   - **Displacement:** More small batches  higher setup energy per batch, offsetting per-item gains.

2. **Transport displacement:**
   - **Risk:** Replacing air with ground (S1, S4) may increase late deliveries, prompting customers to switch to faster (but higher-emission) competitors, negating emissions savings.
   - **Displacement:** Emissions are displaced to competitors’ logistics.

3. **Packaging rebound:**
   - **Risk:** Reinforced packaging (Q1) reduces damage but doubles material use. If S6 reduces rework, customers may demand even more reinforced packaging for non-heavy parts, increasing material waste.
   - **Displacement:** Material savings from reduced rework are offset by overuse of reinforced packaging.

4. **Rework displacement:**
   - **Risk:** Shifting production to CNC_A (S3) reduces rework but may increase cycle time, leading to more expedited transport (air/van) to meet deadlines, increasing transport emissions.
   - **Displacement:** Energy savings from lower rework are offset by higher transport emissions.

5. **Obsolescence rebound:**
   - **Risk:** Larger batches (S2, S10) reduce energy per item but increase obsolescence risk (6% for large batches). Obsolescent parts may be scrapped, increasing material waste.
   - **Displacement:** Energy savings are offset by material waste from unsold inventory.

6. **Customer behavior rebound:**
   - **Risk:** Carbon-aware lead times (S10) may lead Regular customers to order earlier or in larger quantities, increasing overall demand and energy use.
   - **Displacement:** Emissions are displaced to upstream production or downstream usage.

7. **Resilience displacement:**
   - **Risk:** Consolidated trucks (S5) reduce emissions but may reduce route redundancy (R1), making the system more vulnerable to disruptions. During a carrier outage, more orders may need emergency transport, increasing emissions.
   - **Displacement:** Emissions are displaced to emergency transport modes.

8. **Inspection rebound:**
   - **Risk:** Secondary inspections (S7) reduce rework but add time, leading to more expedited transport to meet deadlines, increasing emissions.
   - **Displacement:** Quality improvements are offset by higher transport emissions.

## 6. Balanced recommendation

### **Redesign Package: "Segment-Aware Green Lean"**
Combine interventions that target high-impact, low-risk opportunities while protecting service, quality, and resilience. The package prioritizes:
1. **Transport optimization** (S1, S4, S5, S8, S9) for the largest emissions reductions.
2. **Production efficiency** (S2, S3, S7) to reduce energy and scrap.
3. **Packaging and rework reduction** (S6, S11) to cut hidden waste.
4. **Governance** (S12) to ensure exceptions are justified.

#### **Core Interventions:**
| **Intervention**               | **Rationale**                                                                                     | **Trade-off Mitigation**                                                                                     |
|--------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **S1 + S4**                    | Replace air/van with ground for Standard-stock Service-contract and Regular orders where feasible. | Use priority picking (S4) to offset ground transport delays. Monitor late-delivery risk (target: <10%). |
| **S5**                         | Consolidate trucks for non-urgent stock orders.                                                  | Limit to orders with promised lead time >48h. Retain ground options for resilience (R1).                   |
| **S8 + S9**                    | Replace dedicated vans with shared emergency couriers; split Critical-downtime into true/precautionary. | Dedicated vans remain for true downtime. Shared lanes must meet 95% on-time performance.                     |
| **S2**                         | Dynamic batching with time caps for MTO orders.                                                 | Caps aligned with segment tolerance (Critical: 2h, Service-contract: 8h, Regular: 24h).                  |
| **S3 + S7**                    | Shift MTO from CNC_B to CNC_A; add secondary inspection for high-rework combinations.             | Accept slight cycle-time increase for CNC_A. Secondary inspections target only high-risk parts.            |
| **S6 + S11**                   | Improve packaging-route checks; reduce partial shipments.                                        | Reduces rework and damage returns without service impact.                                                   |
| **S12**                        | Sustainability exception logging for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides.                                        | Ensures overrides are intentional and auditable.                                                             |

#### **Why This Balances Impact and Constraints:**
- **Environmental impact:**
  - **Transport:** Largest reductions from S1, S4, S5, S8, S9 (transport is ~30-50% of emissions for many segments).
  - **Energy:** Reductions from S2, S3, S7 (production is dominant for MTO orders).
  - **Materials:** Reductions from S3, S6, S7, S11 (scrap and packaging waste).
- **Service:**
  - Late-delivery risk is managed via priority picking (S4), batch wait caps (S2), and segment-specific rules (S9).
  - Critical-downtime orders retain dedicated vans (S8) and emergency production.
- **Quality:**
  - Rework and scrap are reduced via S3, S6, S7, S11.
  - Reinforced packaging is retained for heavy parts (Q1).
- **Resilience:**
  - Route redundancy (R1) is preserved by retaining ground options and not over-relying on consolidated trucks.
  - Emergency capacity (dedicated vans, emergency production) is reserved for true downtime (S9).

#### **Excluded Interventions:**
- **S10 (Carbon-aware lead times):** Excluded due to risk of customer behavior rebound (earlier/larger orders) and complexity of negotiation.
- **Full elimination of air/van transport:** Excluded because Critical-downtime and some Service-contract orders require fast transport to meet SLAs.

## 7. Validation and governance plan

### **Validation Approach:**
1. **Pre-post comparison:**
   - Compare the **sustainability indicators** (Section 2) for the 12-month period before and after implementation.
   - Segment results by case_type, customer_type, and route_mode to isolate effects.

2. **Conformance monitoring:**
   - **SP1 (Green transport):** Track % of orders using ground/consolidated truck when promised lead time allows it. Target: >90% compliance (current: 78% for Standard-stock Service-contract).
   - **SP2 (Dedicated van justification):** Track % of dedicated van orders with explicit Critical-downtime justification. Target: >95% compliance (current: 69%).
   - **Q1 (Reinforced packaging):** Track % of heavy parts (>8 kg) using reinforced packaging or with low-damage-risk certification. Target: >98% compliance.

3. **Segment-level service monitoring:**
   - Monitor late-delivery rate and cycle time by segment. Alert if:
     - Late-delivery rate exceeds 10% for any segment (current: 4-18%).
     - Cycle time p90 increases by >20% for any segment.

4. **Exception governance:**
   - **S12 (Exception log):** Audit all SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides monthly. Require manager approval for >5% of orders in any segment.
   - **Rebound tracking:** Monitor for:
     - Increase in air/van transport for segments not targeted by S1/S4 (e.g., Made-to-order).
     - Increase in small batches (batch_size < 4) for MTO orders (S2 rebound).
     - Increase in obsolescence (unsold inventory >14 days) for batched orders.

5. **Process-mining evidence:**
   - **Path analysis:** Use DFG to detect new rework loops or transport mode shifts (e.g., increase in Inspection -> Rework production).
   - **Bottleneck detection:** Monitor queue times at CNC_A, CNC_B, heat treatment, and inspection_cell. Alert if batch wait times exceed caps (S2).
   - **Transport mode shifts:** Track % of orders switching from air to ground (S1/S4) and from dedicated van to shared courier (S8).

6. **Pilot testing:**
   - Roll out the package to 10% of orders (e.g., one region or customer segment) for 3 months. Validate:
     - No >5% increase in late deliveries.
     - No >10% increase in cycle time for any segment.
     - 15% reduction in transport kgCO2e for targeted segments (S1, S4, S5, S8).
     - 10% reduction in energy kWh for MTO orders (S2, S3, S7).

### **Governance Structure:**
- **Sustainability Committee:** Monthly reviews of:
  - Conformance to SP1/SP2/Q1.
  - Exception log (S12) and override justifications.
  - Rebound/displacement risks (Section 5).
- **Process Owners:** Responsible for:
  - Transport: Monitor S1, S4, S5, S8, S9.
  - Production: Monitor S2, S3, S7.
  - Quality: Monitor S6, S11, Q1.
- **IT/Analytics:** Maintain process-mining dashboards for real-time monitoring of:
  - Sustainability indicators (Section 2).
  - DFG changes (e.g., new rework loops).
  - Segment-level performance.

### **Continuous Improvement:**
- **Quarterly deep dives:** Analyze root causes of:
  - SP1/SP2/Q1 violations.
  - Late deliveries or cycle-time increases.
  - Rebound effects (e.g., increased air transport in untargeted segments).
- **Annual review:** Update batch wait caps (S2), transport rules (S1/S4), and packaging standards (Q1) based on new data.
