## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|-------------|-----------------------|---------------------|---------|--------------------------------------------------|---------------|-----------------------------|
| TE_total | P1 vs P0 | All cases | Total cycle time | Total effect | Yes | Unobserved confounding by underlying risk |
| TE_triage | P1 vs P0 | All cases | Triage completion time | Total effect | Yes | Priority lane assignment may not be ignorable |
| IE_manual_occurrence | P2 vs P0 | Flagged cases | Manual review occurrence | Interventional indirect effect | Yes | Mandatory review removal may change reviewer behavior |
| IE_manual_wait | P3 vs P0 | Flagged cases | Manual queue waiting time | Interventional indirect effect | Yes | Queue interference and capacity constraints |
| IE_escalation | P4 vs P0 | Reviewed cases | Escalation occurrence | Natural direct effect | Yes | Escalation criteria may depend on unobserved risk |
| TE_cycle | P0 vs baseline | All cases | Total cycle time | Total effect | Yes | System-wide congestion and resource constraints |
| DE_outcome | P1 vs P0 | Flagged cases | Decision quality (Approve/Reject) | Direct effect | Yes | Outcome may reflect underlying risk rather than flag |

*Framework*: Interventional direct and indirect effects are used because the flag is part of a policy bundle where "set flag to zero" (P1) is not the same as "change what the organization does." This avoids cross-world assumptions in mediation analysis.


### Time-ordered process-mechanism diagram
```
Register -> [arrival_load] -> [complexity] -> [risk_score] -> [flag]
-> [priority_lane] -> [triage_wait] -> [triage_service]
-> [manual_review] -> [manual_queue_wait] -> [manual_service]
-> [escalation] -> [escalation_queue_wait] -> [senior_service] -> Close
```

*Interference/confounding*: Exposure-induced confounding arises because `flag` depends on `risk_score` and `complexity`, which also affect downstream stages. Reviewer behavior (`U_reviewer_judgment`) may change when the flag is present, creating treatment-confounder feedback. Interference occurs via shared queues (`manual_queue_wait`, `escalation_queue_wait`), where one case's treatment affects others' waiting times.

These contrasts are not causal because:
1. Flagged cases are higher complexity/risk, which may independently delay downstream stages.
2. Shared queues mean flagged cases congest resources for unflagged cases, creating interference.
3. Reviewer behavior may change under the flag policy (treatment-confounder feedback).

## 4. Direct and mediated pathway analysis

| pathway | candidate interpretation | observable evidence | required assumptions | competing explanation | intervention targeting this pathway | expected downstream side effect |
|---------|--------------------------|---------------------|----------------------|-----------------------|------------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| flag  priority_lane  triage_wait | Priority lane reduces triage waiting time | Flagged cases start triage 6x faster | Priority lane assignment is ignorable | Arrival_load or reviewer behavior drives triage timing | P1 (suppress flag display) | Reviewers may deprioritize cases without flag |
| flag  manual_review | Flag triggers mandatory review | 100% of flagged cases undergo review | Mandatory review policy is followed | Complexity drives review occurrence | P2 (remove mandatory review) | Lower decision quality for high-risk cases |
| flag  manual_queue_wait | Flag increases manual queue congestion | Flagged cases wait 7 hours for review | Queue interference is ignorable | Unobserved case mix or reviewer capacity drives wait | P3 (add protected capacity) | Unflagged cases may face longer waits |
| flag  escalation | Flag increases escalation likelihood | 46% of flagged cases escalate vs 0% unflagged | Escalation criteria are risk-based | Reviewer judgment overrides flag | P4 (change escalation criteria) | More false positives in escalation |
| complexity  flag  manual_review | Complexity confounds flag-review link | High-complexity cases are flagged and reviewed | Complexity is measured without error | Risk_score mediates complexity effect | Stratify by complexity | May reduce review efficiency |

| flag  decision_outcome | Flag biases decisions toward rejection | 31% of flagged cases rejected vs 14% unflagged | Outcome reflects underlying risk | Reviewer behavior changes under flag | P1 (suppress flag) | May increase false negatives |

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|----------|-----------------------------|----------------------------------|--------------|----------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Does the flag causally reduce triage waiting? | Within-risk-score comparison (stratify by `risk_score` bins) | `risk_score`, `arrival_load` | Risk score is measured without error | Compare triage waiting times for flagged vs unflagged cases with similar `risk_score` | If waiting times are similar across bins, priority lane may not causally reduce triage wait |
| Does manual queue congestion mediate cycle time? | Queue-state-aware regression (adjust for `manual_queue_wait`) | `manual_queue_wait`, `arrival_load` | Queue state is ignorable conditional on covariates | Test if `flag` effect on cycle time attenuates after adjusting for `manual_queue_wait` | If attenuation is large, queue congestion is a key mediator |
| Does the flag change reviewer behavior? | Policy-change analysis (compare P0 vs P1) | `flag`, `reviewer_resource` | Reviewer behavior is stable across policies | Check if manual service times or escalation rates change after suppressing the flag | If service times increase or escalation rates drop, reviewer behavior changed |
| Is escalation driven by unobserved risk? | Local-threshold design (compare cases near flag threshold) | `risk_score`, `complexity` | Risk score is smooth near threshold | Test if escalation rates jump discontinuously at the threshold | If no jump, escalation may reflect unobserved risk |
| Does shared-queue interference bias estimates? | Interference-aware design (compare cases arriving at different loads) | `arrival_load`, `case_index` | Interference is local (neighbors in time) | Test if flag effect varies with `arrival_load` | If effect is larger under high load, interference is present |
| Is decision quality affected by the flag? | Negative-control outcome (compare approved cases with similar risk) | `risk_score`, `outcome` | Approved cases are comparable | Check if approval rates differ for flagged vs unflagged cases with similar `risk_score` | If differences persist, flag may bias decisions |
| Are results sensitive to unobserved risk? | Sensitivity analysis (vary `U_underlying_risk` effect) | `risk_score`, `flag` | Unobserved risk is independent of observed covariates | Test how large `U_underlying_risk` effect must be to explain observed cycle time differences | If small effects suffice, unobserved risk may explain results |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|--------|------------------------|----------------------------------|----------------------------|-------------------------|--------------------|-------------------|----------------|
| P0 | Faster triage for flagged cases | Congestion in manual/escalation queues | Longer cycle times for flagged cases | Moderate (bias toward rejection) | High (shared queues) | Medium | Baseline |
| P1 | Triages revert to FIFO | Reduced manual review load | Shorter cycle times for flagged cases | Low (less bias) | Low | High | Strong candidate for pilot |
| P2 | Faster triage maintained | Manual review load decreases | Shorter cycle times for flagged cases | High (lower decision quality) | Low | Medium | Risky; needs safeguards |
| P3 | Faster triage maintained | Manual queue congestion reduced | Shorter cycle times for flagged cases | Low | Moderate (protected capacity) | Medium | Promising; evaluate spillover |
| P4 | Faster triage maintained | Escalation queue congestion may increase | Cycle time depends on escalation criteria | Moderate (over-escalation) | High | Low | Needs more evidence |

*Pilot recommendation*: **P1 (suppress flag display)** is selected for a limited pilot.
- **Eligibility**: All cases flagged under P0.
- **Assignment**: Randomized 1:1 to P0 (flag displayed) vs P1 (flag hidden).
- **Queue-capacity guardrail**: Monitor manual review queue length; halt if >24-hour wait for any subgroup.
- **Primary outcome**: Total cycle time for flagged cases.
- **Secondary outcomes**: Triage waiting time, manual review occurrence, decision quality (rejection rate for high-risk cases).
- **Process data**: Stage timestamps, resource assignments, `risk_score`, `complexity`, `arrival_load`.
- **Stopping conditions**: Halt if cycle time for P1 > P0 by 20% or decision quality drops (rejection rate for high-risk cases increases by >10%).

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk**: If underlying risk is strongly predictive of cycle time and unobserved, the flag's effect may vanish when adjusting for it.
2. **Queue interference**: If manual review queues are not shared (e.g., dedicated capacity exists), downstream delays may disappear.
3. **Decision quality**: If reviewers ignore the flag and decisions are based solely on underlying risk, quality differences may not exist.
4. **Timestamp semantics**: If `triage_start` includes service time (not just waiting), the observed "acceleration" may be illusory.
5. **Positivity**: If no unflagged cases have high `risk_score`, comparisons near the threshold are invalid.
6. **Reviewer behavior**: If reviewers deprioritize flagged cases when the flag is hidden (P1), cycle time benefits may not materialize.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed**: Flagged cases enter triage faster but take much longer to complete, primarily due to long waits for manual review and escalation. Unflagged cases have shorter, simpler processes.

**Plausible mechanism**: The flag triggers mandatory manual review and escalation, congesting shared queues. Reviewers may spend more time on flagged cases due to perceived risk, and escalation to senior teams adds further delay. The flag itself is not the cause of risk but a policy signal that changes routing and resource allocation.

**Unidentified**: The contribution of unobserved underlying risk to cycle time cannot be separated from the flag's effect. Interference via shared queues and treatment-confounder feedback (reviewer behavior changes under the flag) complicate causal interpretation.

**Next evidence**: The **P1 pilot (suppress flag display)** is most useful because it tests whether the flag's *display* (not underlying risk) drives downstream delays. If cycle times for flagged cases improve without the flag visible, the mechanism is primarily organizational behavior rather than case risk. The pilot includes guardrails to prevent queue collapse and monitors decision quality to ensure no harm.
