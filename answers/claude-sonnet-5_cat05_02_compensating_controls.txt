## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 is not one objective; it is a bundle of threat scenarios that "different preparer/releaser" imperfectly proxies for. I decompose into seven, mapped to which control can **P**revent, **D**etect, **C**orrect, or **N**ot-address it.

| # | Objective / Threat Scenario | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| O1 | One person both initiates and irrevocably authorizes a transfer (raw segregation-of-duties breach) | N (system, not human independence) | P if before release/settlement, else D | D only, C only within recall window | Defines bounded conditions under which O1 may be knowingly suspended |
| O2 | Account-takeover / fraudulent bank-account redirection | P/D — checks ownership and 7-day change, but overridable | P/D if manager actually re-verifies bank details; N if rubber-stamped | D, C only pre-settlement | Precondition "no bank change in 7 days" is itself a **P** gate |
| O3 | Fictitious invoice / goods-not-received fraud | Partial P/D — duplicate-invoice and PO-match only; **N** for receipt authenticity or commercial reasonableness | P/D if manager reviews receipt/PO substantively; N otherwise | D (can uncover fabricated receipts after the fact, as in AP-204), C only if pre-recall-window | N — not addressed |
| O4 | Rubber-stamped "independent" approval defeats the intent of segregation | N | Central — quality-dependent, can be P, D, or N | D over time via pattern audit (approval-cycle-time monitoring) | N (ED-1 assumes MG-4 is substantive) |
| O5 | Loss of recoverability once settlement is irrevocable | N (pre-settlement only) | P/C if before settlement, N after | C only within ~24h recall window; D after | Condition 7 (audit within 1 business day) exists precisely to preserve O5 |
| O6 | Collusion between nominally-independent actors (shared workstation, repeated pairing, borrowed credentials) | N | N (independence is assumed, not verified) | N — a clean audit does not disprove collusion | N |
| O7 | Emergency exception used as routine convenience or retro-justified after the fact | N | Partial — manager can question necessity | Partial — can flag late/absent emergency justification | Central — the 7 conditions exist to bound genuine necessity, but nothing verifies the *token issuance timing itself* is honest |

Key takeaway: AV-2, MG-4, RA-7, ED-1 collectively cover O1, O2, O5, O7 reasonably well; O3 (receipt authenticity) and O6 (collusion) are **structural blind spots** in the whole control package, not just gaps in a single case.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating package is scored on seven dimensions, not on activity-count:

1. **Timing** — pre-release (best) > post-release/pre-settlement (hybrid) > post-settlement (forensic only).
2. **Independence** — different individual, no reporting-line conflict, no shared/borrowed credentials, no evidence of coordinated behavior (shared workstation, repeated pairing).
3. **Coverage** — does the specific risk in *this* case (bank change, receipt authenticity, commercial reasonableness, sanctions) fall inside what AV-2/MG-4 actually checked?
4. **Overrideability** — was a warning overridden, by whom, and was the override itself independently gated?
5. **Recoverability** — did detection occur inside the bank's practical recall window (~24h), or after settlement is treated as irrevocable?
6. **Evidence quality** — substantive documented review (invoice + bank + receipt) vs. a rubber stamp; corroborated emergency justification vs. self-reported token.
7. **Collusion resistance** — structural safeguards (rotation, different workstations, no repeated pairing) beyond mere role labels.

**Classification ladder:**

- **FULLY_EQUIVALENT** — preventive timing, genuine independence, full coverage of the case's actual risk vector, no unresolved override, recoverable if needed, high evidence quality, no collusion indicators (e.g., AP-203).
- **PARTIALLY_EQUIVALENT** — one dimension weaker but compensated elsewhere (e.g., approval after release but before settlement, with substantive review and low inherent risk) (AP-201, AP-207).
- **MERELY_MITIGATING** — a material gap in a critical dimension (ambiguous emergency precondition, unresolved independence pattern) with no confirmed loss (AP-206, AP-209).
- **INEFFECTIVE** — override defeats prevention, conflicted approver, detection outside the recovery window, or a confirmed anomaly/loss (AP-202, AP-204, AP-208).

**Why "same expected loss" is not the sole standard:** Expected loss (probability × magnitude) ignores (a) recoverability — a low-probability, unrecoverable EUR 900k exposure (AP-208) is qualitatively worse than a higher-probability but recoverable EUR 2k exposure; (b) accountability value — the ability to reconstruct who exercised independent judgment matters for deterrence, regulatory defensibility, and legal recovery even when no loss materializes; (c) collusion resistance — two events with identical modeled "expected loss" can differ enormously in tail risk if one has genuine independence and the other does not (AP-204 vs AP-209 illustrate this — same actors, same nominal conformance, different confirmed-anomaly outcome).

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control | Primary nature | Secondary nature | Can it stop loss after Release but before Settlement? | Overridable? | Coverage limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AV-2 | Preventive (blocking mode) | Detective (warning mode) | No — acts only before/at release | Yes, by "authorized manager" | No goods-receipt, no commercial reasonableness, no collusion detection |
| MG-4 (before release) | Preventive | — | N/A (already prevented) | Manager can rubber-stamp (silent failure) | Quality depends entirely on reviewer diligence |
| MG-4 (after release, before settlement) | **Hybrid** | Detective-of-process, Preventive-of-loss | **Yes** — can trigger a halt/recall before irrevocability | Same rubber-stamp risk | Same |
| MG-4 (after settlement) | Detective / forensic | — | No | — | Cannot undo loss; only informs future controls |
| RA-7 | Detective | Corrective only within recall window | Sometimes (if audit lands before 24h elapses) | N/A | Cannot recover after window closes |
| ED-1 | Governance/permission | Frames when P/D/C controls substitute for four-eyes | N/A | Its conditions can be partially satisfied yet still fail the spirit (AP-206) | Does not itself detect or correct anything |

**Semantics for "manager action after Release but before Settlement":** I treat this as **hybrid**, not purely detective. Relative to the *segregation-of-duties step* (Release_Payment), it is detective — the single-operator act already occurred. But relative to the *loss event* (irrevocable settlement), it retains preventive/corrective power: a diligent manager can still halt, question, or force a recall before money is gone. Labeling it purely "detective" would understate its value in AP-201/AP-207; labeling it purely "preventive" would overstate it, since the four-eyes breach at Release_Payment has already happened and cannot be undone. This is why timing relative to *settlement*, not merely relative to *release*, is the operative variable in ED-1 condition 6.

## 5. Counterfactual and Residual-Risk Analysis

**AP-201** — Loss mechanism: u17 makes an erroneous entry (wrong amount/account) with no one else looking before release. Causal chain broken by: AV-2 (clean pass on the checks it covers) and MG-4 pre-settlement review. Remaining exposure: goods-receipt/commercial-reasonableness fraud, since AV-2/MG-4 as described don't confirm receipt. Under a truly independent second releaser: risk drops from low to very-low; marginal benefit here is small given the low amount and established vendor — illustrating that four-eyes has diminishing marginal value for low-risk, low-amount cases when compensations are strong.

**AP-202** — Loss mechanism: account-takeover via a bank-detail change shortly before payment, executed by the same person who can silence the resulting warning using a borrowed managerial credential. Causal chain **not** broken — AV-2's warning was overridden by a non-independent party, MG-4 was conflicted and late, RA-7 was past the recall window. Remaining exposure: total — this is close to a worst-case realization. Under a truly independent second releaser (different person, no override rights, no reporting-line tie to preparer): the override step itself would likely have required a second independent human, very plausibly stopping this payment before settlement — the qualitative risk reduction here is large (from "near-certain loss" to "likely blocked or delayed for review").

**AP-204** — Loss mechanism: collusive fabrication of a service receipt by two habitually-paired individuals sharing a workstation, with formal segregation providing no actual friction because the two act as a de facto single unit. Causal chain not broken by nominal four-eyes (it was satisfied and still failed); RA-7 eventually detected the fabrication but likely too late for recall. Remaining exposure: repeat-transaction fraud risk across the other 36+ payments this pair processed. Under a *truly* independent second releaser (different reporting line, rotated pairing, separate workstation, no persistent dyad): the opportunity for coordinated fabrication drops substantially — this case shows that nominal FEP-1 conformance without organizational independence gives a false sense of security.

**AP-206** — Loss mechanism (if any): a single operator initiates a payment during a genuine emergency, but the emergency token is logged after Prepare_Payment, raising the possibility that "emergency" status is being applied retroactively to legitimize a decision already made rather than genuinely required in advance. Causal chain: MG-4 (independent, pre-release) and fast RA-7 largely break the chain even if the emergency framing is questionable; what remains exposed is a governance/precedent risk — normalization of after-the-fact emergency justification, which erodes ED-1's bounding function over time even if this specific instance caused no loss. Under a truly independent second releaser, the emergency-exception question becomes moot entirely, since four-eyes would apply nominally.

**AP-208** — Loss mechanism: payment to an account that changed ownership 3 days earlier, released by a single operator while the automated validation service — the one control designed exactly to catch this — was down, with manager sign-off occurring only after irrevocable settlement. Causal chain: **not broken by any control** in this instance; only the (unverified) legitimacy of the underlying vendor prevented an admitted loss. Remaining exposure: total, and by far the most severe in the set given amount and freshness of the bank change. Under a truly independent second releaser: a second independent human, seeing a fresh bank change and an unavailable validation system on a EUR 900k payment, would very plausibly have refused to proceed — the qualitative risk reduction here is the largest in the entire case set, underscoring that this case represents the clearest ED-1/compensating-control failure regardless of the (currently unverified) benign outcome.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

Recommended standing metrics (each is an **alert**, not proof of misconduct, and must be paired with case-level review before any conclusion is drawn):

- **Same-user rate** — % of completed payments with Prepare=Release resource; trend over time and by business unit.
- **Override rate** — % of AV-2 warnings overridden, broken out by whether the overriding credential belongs to an independent party.
- **Late manager approval** — distribution of MG-4 timestamp relative to Release_Payment and Bank_Settlement; flag any approval after settlement as a hard exception (as in AP-208).
- **Audit-before-recall-window** — % of RA-7 audits completed within 24h of settlement (not just release); flag misses (AP-202, AP-208).
- **Repeated resource pairs** — frequency count of (preparer, releaser) or (preparer, approver) dyads over rolling 30/90-day windows; flag pairs exceeding an organization-defined threshold (AP-204/AP-209's u12/u13 pattern).
- **Shared workstation / IP / login-session correlation** — cross-reference infrastructure logs (not present in this event log) to test whether "independent" actors are in fact co-located or co-logged-in.
- **Bank-change proximity** — days between last bank-detail change and payment release; flag any value inside the 7-day window regardless of AV-2 outcome (AP-202, AP-208).
- **Control outages** — track AV-2 availability incidents and whether any payment was released during an outage without an equivalent compensating manual control (AP-208).
- **Emergency-token timing integrity** — flag any token activation timestamp occurring after Prepare_Payment (AP-206), and separately track corroboration (e.g., outage tickets) for the underlying emergency claim.

An alert on any of the above should trigger a targeted case review (bank confirmation call, receipt verification, HR/org-chart check) — it is evidence for a judgment process, not itself a finding of fraud or negligence.

```python
        bank_change_within_7d=(facts["bank_change_days"] is not None and facts["bank_change_days"] < 7)
                              if facts is not None else None,
        ed1_all_conditions_satisfied_per_available_data=emergency_eligible,
    ))

flags_df = pd.DataFrame(flags).set_index("case")

# Repeated preparer/releaser pairs across cases (collusion-monitoring proxy)
pair_counts = pr.dropna(subset=["releaser"]).groupby(["preparer", "releaser"]).size()
repeated_pairs = pair_counts[pair_counts > 1]

print("=== NOMINAL FOUR-EYES VIOLATIONS (completed payments) ===")
print(nominal_violations)
print("\n=== BLOCKED / NOT COMPLETED (e.g., AP-205) ===")
print(blocked_cases)
print("\n=== REPEATED PREPARER/RELEASER PAIRS (monitoring flag, not proof) ===")
print(repeated_pairs)
print("\n=== PRELIMINARY EVIDENCE FLAGS (per-case) ===")
print(flags_df.to_string())

print("""
--------------------------------------------------------------------
LABEL: The tables above are PRELIMINARY EVIDENCE for analyst/judge
review (Sections 1-6). They are NOT a final compliance verdict and
must not be used to auto-classify cases as conforming or violating.
--------------------------------------------------------------------
""")
```

**Notes on PM4Py behavior that cannot be guaranteed across versions:**
- `pm4py.format_dataframe` argument names (`case_id`, `activity_key`, `timestamp_key`) and its requirement/optionality of a `timestamp_key` sorting step have changed between pm4py 2.x minor releases.
- `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` (and its underlying `pm4py.algo.filtering.log.attributes` equivalent) has, in some releases, returned the **compliant** sub-log (cases where the two activities have different resources) and in others been documented as returning **violations**; the exact semantics also depend on whether events with a missing second activity (e.g., blocked releases) are silently dropped. For this reason the code treats pm4py's output as a cross-check only and relies on a manual, version-independent groupby-based computation as the authoritative source for the nominal-violation list and the blocked-case list.
- `pm4py.convert_to_event_log` / `convert_to_dataframe` round-tripping can reorder or rename columns in some versions; case/activity/timestamp keys are re-verified before use rather than assumed.
