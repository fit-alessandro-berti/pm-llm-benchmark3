# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

Neither submission is presently justified as a compliance model. Model S demonstrably permits forbidden behavior; Model C confuses a small collection of observed variants with the policy. The reported metrics help identify these problems but cannot resolve them.

I distinguish four uses:

- **Exploration:** Favor coverage and interpretable deviations. An explicitly permissive model can help investigators form hypotheses, provided its accepted behavior is not presented as approved behavior.
- **Conformance enforcement:** Enforce agreed rules and distinguish violations from insufficient evidence. High average fitness cannot compensate for admitting a missing mandatory check. Conversely, rejecting an unseen but authorized combination is an enforcement defect.
- **Simulation:** Require credible routing probabilities, attribute distributions, correction dynamics, durations, and resources. Neither a permissive language nor a variant catalogue supplies these. Structural play-out alone is not a calibrated operational simulation.
- **Stakeholder communication:** Favor a compact explanation of obligations, choices, and uncertainty. Comprehensibility must include correctly understanding exceptions and corrections, not merely finding a diagram approachable.

I interpret `->` as sequence, `+` as parallel composition allowing every interleaving of its children, `X` as exclusive choice, and `tau` as an invisible, optional path. Acceptance below means an exact, complete visible execution, with silent transitions allowed; it does **not** mean an alignment can repair the trace at positive cost. Neither supplied expression contains a loop.

The policy also needs an interpretation register:

1. Supplier Review must occur before approval when sourcing is external. P3 states an external-order obligation; I do not infer that internal orders are prohibited from receiving a supplier review. Its timing relative to the mandatory checks is otherwise unrestricted. The preapproval boundary and any prohibition on internal reviews need owner confirmation.
2. Each mandatory check initially needs completion. A correction invalidates the affected check results; approval requires all outstanding checks to be completed. Expedite Review requires both check results to be current when it occurs.
3. For the proposed implementation, correction affects Credit Check, Technical Review, or both, following F6. Correction can occur repeatedly anywhere between registration and approval, including while a previous recheck remains outstanding. Pending obligations accumulate.
4. Supplier Review and Expedite Review occur at most once. A correction does not invalidate either under this initial interpretation. P6's broader word “checks” leaves that issue unresolved: corrections affecting supplier or expedite decisions, corrections affecting no mandatory check, and repeated supplier/expedite reviews require explicit policy decisions before deployment in those scenarios.
5. No additional rule about eligibility for Auto Approval or urgency is supplied. I will not invent one. A trace judged compliant here satisfies only the stated and explicitly adopted rules.

These assumptions are visible configuration decisions, not discoveries from the activity log. The missing attributes prevent some trace-level compliance conclusions even when control flow is completely observed.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

**Strongest case for S.** It compactly represents independent work and accommodates both approval types. Its optional branches can avoid flagging every missing event as a process violation. Its coverage, runtime, and comprehension results make it a plausible exploratory summary of incomplete observations. It preserves registration first, release last, exactly one approval, and at most one occurrence of each review. Under the stated semantics it admits 130 distinct visible traces: two approval choices multiplied by the 65 ordered subsets of four reviews. It does not admit arbitrary behavior.

**Strongest case against S.** It encodes missing mandatory checks as legitimate executions. Concurrency also removes the required dependencies into Expedite Review. These are inspectable semantic defects, not speculative objections to a low precision score.

For compact traces below: `R` = Register Dossier, `C` = Credit Check, `T` = Technical Review, `S` = Supplier Review, `E` = Expedite Review, `D` = Correct Dossier, `M` = Manager Approval, `A` = Auto Approval, and `O` = Release Order.

All of the following are accepted by Model S:

| Admitted trace | Problem |
|---|---|
| `R M O` | Skips both mandatory checks. |
| `R C A O` | Skips Technical Review. |
| `R T M O` | Skips Credit Check. |
| `R E C T M O` | Expedite Review precedes both mandatory checks. |
| `R C E T A O` | Expedite Review precedes Technical Review even though Credit Check has finished. |
| `R C T A O`, with `external=True` | Omits the required Supplier Review. The same activity sequence can be valid for an internal order. |

Allowing absent observations can be intentional noise tolerance, but it belongs in an observation/error model or an explicitly labeled exploratory view. A silent branch means the process may skip the activity; it is not evidence that the activity occurred but was unrecorded. Premature Expedite Review is a separate accidental ordering flaw: optionality alone did not require eliminating its prerequisites.

S also omits Correct Dossier entirely. It rejects every correction trace, including legitimate selective rechecks, and cannot explain why repeating a check becomes authorized. Rejecting B8 is useful but only results from allowing each mandatory check once. S therefore combines serious overacceptance with serious underacceptance.

F7 is defensible only with a clear exploratory boundary. A diagram understandable because it hides obligations is unsuitable as the communicated operating policy or an enforcement engine.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

**Strongest case for C.** Its eight alternatives are easy to relate to specific observed examples. Every listed execution registers first, performs both mandatory checks before approval, has exactly one approval, and releases last. Its expedite paths respect the mandatory-check prerequisite. It rejects B3, B4, and B8. A narrow catalogue can be appropriate when an independently authorized, closed list of procedures actually exists.

**Strongest case against C.** The supplied policy authorizes combinations, not just eight complete strings. C reproduces some combinations while arbitrarily omitting others:

- `R T C A O` is explicitly valid B2, but C rejects it.
- `R T C S E A O` combines known choices and is plausible valid B5, but C rejects it.
- Auto Approval appears only with `C T`, without supplier review, expedite review, or correction.
- Expedite Review never appears with the alternate initial check order, and its supplier combination permits only `S E`, despite P4 allowing both orders.
- Supplier Review is placed after both mandatory checks in every branch containing it, although the stated policy does not impose that dependency.

The correction branch permits exactly `D C T`, once, after the initial `C T`, with Manager Approval and no supplier or expedite review. It cannot express correction affecting just one check, reverse recheck order, or multiple corrections. Worse, its accepted `R C T D C T M O` is excessive rework when only one check was affected. Without attributes, the same accepted string can represent appropriate or inappropriate rechecking.

Branch enumeration scales poorly as independent dimensions multiply. A change to an activity's rule must be propagated across copies, and an omitted copy can create a silent inconsistency. The repeated labels are not intrinsically unsound; they expose the absence of a shared business rule and complicate attribution of alignments and ownership. The undocumented count makes a numerical penalty premature.

High training precision is consistent with memorizing frequent variants. That explanation is supported by the visible enumeration, F4, and the 22.9 percentage-point holdout drop, but it is not proved by precision alone. Holdout loss could also contain real violations, missing supplier observations, population change, or evaluation inconsistencies. Each must be separated.

F8 would be defensible if governance had independently authorized precisely these eight workflows and explicitly prohibited all others. No such authorization is supplied, and B2 contradicts the proposed rejection rule. “Unseen in training” is not an adequate compliance rule. Audit readiness requires policy provenance, controlled exceptions, reproducible evaluation, and explainable evidence; similarity to common traces supplies none of those by itself.

The models' languages are incomparable: S accepts seven of C's eight branches but rejects its correction branch; C rejects many S executions, including both invalid shortcuts and valid combinations.

# 7. Third Alternative

I propose a **procedural obligation-state model with data guards and explicit unresolved-evidence outcomes**. The source consists of shared transition rules. It generalizes independent choices without enumerating observed full traces. The Petri net represents possible control flow; the data checks are a required part of the governed model.

**Compact formal description.** After `R`, state is `(P, s, e)`, where `P` is the set of mandatory checks still needed, and `s,e` record whether supplier and expedite review occurred. Initially `P={C,T}` and `s=e=0`.

- A check `x∈{C,T}` can execute exactly when `x∈P`; it removes itself from `P`.
- `D(Affected)` sets `P := P ∪ Affected`, with a nonempty affected set drawn from `{C}`, `{T}`, `{C,T}`. It can repeat. When the affected set is unknown, the structural net exposes those alternatives without claiming which is true.
- `S` changes `s=0` to `s=1` anywhere before approval.
- `E` changes `e=0` to `e=1` only when `P` is empty. There is no precedence edge between `S` and `E`.
- Exactly one of `M,A` can execute when `P` is empty. Its data guard additionally requires `s=1` for an external order.
- Only `O` follows approval, and `O` terminates the case.

Thus a check cannot repeat unless a correction has reopened its obligation. An unaffected check result remains valid. Corrections before both initial checks finish do not waive unfinished initial obligations. Multiple corrections can accumulate pending work without forcing an irrelevant repeat after every correction.

The following code is self-contained apart from PM4Py. `model_third` is the conventional `(net, initial_marking, final_marking)` tuple. The finite net has one token representing the current obligation state. The accompanying verifier uses the same successor rules and must run with conformance evaluation or enforcement.

```python
from itertools import product
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import PetriNet, Marking
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.utils import petri_utils

R = "Register Dossier"
C = "Credit Check"
T = "Technical Review"
S = "Supplier Review"
E = "Expedite Review"
D = "Correct Dossier"
M = "Manager Approval"
A = "Auto Approval"
O = "Release Order"
CHECK_BITS = {C: 1, T: 2}
WORK = (C, T, S, E, D)

# Executable guard functions below implement this documented contract.
third_policy = {
    "external_attribute": "external",             # case-level bool, or unknown
    "affected_attribute": "affected_checks",      # on each correction event
    "affected_domain": ((C,), (T,), (C, T)),
    "supplier_rule": "external implies supplier before approval",
    "internal_supplier_allowed": True,
    "expedite_rule": "all mandatory obligations clear at expedite",
    "approval_rule": "one approval, all obligations clear, supplier guard",
    "correction_scope": "mandatory checks only; pending sets accumulate",
    "supplier_expedite_max_occurrences": 1,
    "unknown_policy": "review evidence; do not infer missing attributes",
}


def successors(state, label, affected_mask=None):
    pending, supplier, expedite = state
    if label in CHECK_BITS:
        bit = CHECK_BITS[label]
        return {(pending & ~bit, supplier, expedite)} if pending & bit else set()
    if label == S:
        return {(pending, 1, expedite)} if not supplier else set()
    if label == E:
        return {(pending, supplier, 1)} if pending == 0 and not expedite else set()
    if label == D:
        masks = (1, 2, 3) if affected_mask is None else (affected_mask,)
        return {(pending | mask, supplier, expedite) for mask in masks}
    return set()


def build_third():
    net = PetriNet("obligation_state_backbone")

    def place(name):
        p = PetriNet.Place(name)
        net.places.add(p)
        return p

    def edge(source, label, target):
        t = PetriNet.Transition("t_%d" % len(net.transitions), label)
        net.transitions.add(t)
        petri_utils.add_arc_from_to(source, t, net)
        petri_utils.add_arc_from_to(t, target, net)

    source = place("source")
    sink = place("sink")
    approved = place("approved")
    states = list(product(range(4), range(2), range(2)))
    places = {q: place("q_%d_%d_%d" % q) for q in states}
    edge(source, R, places[(3, 0, 0)])
    for q in states:
        for label in WORK:
            for q_next in sorted(successors(q, label)):
                edge(places[q], label, places[q_next])
        if q[0] == 0:
            edge(places[q], M, approved)
            edge(places[q], A, approved)
    edge(approved, O, sink)
    return net, Marking({source: 1}), Marking({sink: 1})


model_third = build_third()
net_third, im_third, fm_third = model_third


def assess_case(events, external=None):
    """events: ordered dictionaries with concept:name and optional affected_checks.

    PASS = supplied sequence satisfies the adopted policy.
    FAIL = no interpretation of missing attributes satisfies it.
    REVIEW = viable interpretation, but required attribute evidence is unresolved
             (or an attribute is malformed).
    These are trace assessments, conditional on event-capture completeness.
    """
    if external is not None and type(external) is not bool:
        return {"status": "REVIEW", "reason": "external must be bool or unknown"}
    labels = [event.get("concept:name") for event in events]
    if (len(labels) < 3 or labels[0] != R or labels[-1] != O
            or labels.count(R) != 1 or labels.count(O) != 1):
        return {"status": "FAIL", "reason": "registration/release boundary"}
    if labels[-2] not in (M, A) or sum(x in (M, A) for x in labels) != 1:
        return {"status": "FAIL", "reason": "exactly one final approval required"}

    possible = {(3, 0, 0)}
    unknown_correction = False
    for index, event in enumerate(events[1:-2], start=1):
        label = event.get("concept:name")
        mask = None
        if label == D:
            affected = event.get("affected_checks")
            if affected is None:
                unknown_correction = True
            else:
                if (not isinstance(affected, (list, tuple, set, frozenset))
                        or not affected
                        or any(x not in (C, T) for x in affected)):
                    return {"status": "REVIEW", "reason": "malformed affected_checks"}
                mask = sum(CHECK_BITS[x] for x in set(affected))
        next_possible = set()
        for q in possible:
            next_possible.update(successors(q, label, mask))
        possible = next_possible
        if not possible:
            return {"status": "FAIL", "reason": "disallowed event at index %d" % index}

    possible = {q for q in possible if q[0] == 0}
    if not possible:
        return {"status": "FAIL", "reason": "mandatory obligations outstanding"}
    supplier_seen = S in labels
    if external is True and not supplier_seen:
        return {"status": "FAIL", "reason": "external order lacks Supplier Review"}
    if unknown_correction or (external is None and not supplier_seen):
        return {"status": "REVIEW", "reason": "source or affected-check evidence missing"}
    return {"status": "PASS", "reason": "satisfies adopted policy on supplied evidence"}


# Example: B6 with its required attribute evidence.
b6 = [
    {"concept:name": R}, {"concept:name": C}, {"concept:name": T},
    {"concept:name": D, "affected_checks": [T]},
    {"concept:name": T}, {"concept:name": M}, {"concept:name": O},
]
assert assess_case(b6, external=False)["status"] == "PASS"
```

**What the hybrid does and does not certify.** The unguarded net permits supplier omission because sourcing is not an activity label. It also permits alternative correction transitions because affected sets are not visible labels. For a given fully attributed case, the verifier selects the actual correction sets and applies the supplier guard. Structural net acceptance alone is never the deployment decision. Attribute filtering must also constrain online enabled actions; a plain PM4Py token game is insufficient for enforcement.

With missing affected data, the verifier tracks possible pending states. It returns `FAIL` when none can explain the trace, and conservatively returns `REVIEW` when a possible explanation depends on unknown correction scope. It does not infer “Technical Review alone was affected” just because only Technical Review repeated. If sourcing is unknown and supplier review is absent, it returns `REVIEW`; when supplier review is present, the external-order obligation is satisfied regardless of sourcing under the chosen implication interpretation.

These statuses are conditional on the supplied events being complete and correctly ordered. An observed omission may be a capture problem: operational handling must separately establish evidence completeness before turning a trace failure into a finding about actual execution. Alignment can suggest an investigation, but cannot manufacture evidence that a check occurred.

The generated net contains repeated labels, deliberately. They encode current obligations and review flags, rather than separate ownership of each observed full variant. Its source rules, documentation, and event-to-rule mapping are the maintained artifacts. I do not claim that its Petri-net duplicate count, arc-degree simplicity, or runtime must beat C. For this construction, 16 work states yield 19 places and 68 visible transitions; compilation can expand a compact policy. This is a useful counterexample to treating duplicate count or one simplicity proxy as the design objective.

The finite one-token construction supports either order of independent completions as interleavings; it does not represent simultaneous in-progress work or resource contention. Duration/resource simulation would need start/complete lifecycles and calibrated parameters. Repeated corrections are possible indefinitely: workflow soundness should establish the option to complete, not promise that people eventually stop correcting a dossier.

# 9. Validation Experiments

This is a proposed evaluation program, not a claim that it has been executed on the unavailable logs or that the third model has achieved particular scores.

1. **Reconstruct and freeze the evaluation.** Obtain the exact model strings, converted nets, PM4Py and dependency versions, hardware details, evaluation code, classifiers, costs, preprocessing, and original case manifests. Preserve raw event provenance. Publish stable pseudonymous case IDs, split seeds, data/preprocessing hashes, and tie-breaking rules. Do not delete incomplete cases or duplicate events merely to improve fitness. Publish any justified exclusions and their counts.
2. **Use reproducible, appropriately separated data.** Start with an 80/20 case split using seed `20260905`. Split by stable dossier lineage/customer-order group where related cases would leak information; never split a single case's events. Use additional development seeds, for example `20260906`–`20260909`, to estimate split sensitivity, and a separate forward-time holdout to test change over time. Since F4 already informs this redesign, reserve a fresh, untouched confirmation set for the final decision. Do not select the best seed.
3. **Stratify attributes and evidence quality.** Report internal, external, and unknown source cohorts; correction scope `{C}`, `{T}`, `{C,T}`, and unknown; correction count; approval type; supplier/expedite presence and order; mandatory-check order; and verified versus uncertain capture. Recover attributes through independent business records or owner adjudication for a validation sample. Do not derive “external” solely from supplier presence or “affected” solely from observed rechecks. Report small-cell counts and uncertainty rather than treating sparse strata as reliable estimates.
4. **Build a signed policy test suite.** Include B1–B8, A1–A6, and attribute variants of identical strings. Add omissions of each mandatory check, expedite after only one check, missing external supplier, both approvals, no approval, release before approval, events after release, duplicate registration, unaffected rechecks, and approval with an outstanding affected check. Include valid controls for each mutation. Curate expected `PASS`, `FAIL`, or `REVIEW` with operations and compliance, and keep genuinely disputed cases separate. Require zero acceptance of agreed forbidden cases and zero rejection of agreed valid, fully evidenced cases in this suite.
5. **Test missing and duplicate observations.** From independently verified complete cases, delete one event at a time, then at fixed seeded rates such as 1%, 5%, and 10%; stratify by activity rather than only deleting uniformly. Duplicate events with and without the same immutable event ID. Distinguish a duplicated record from a genuinely repeated activity: a documented ingestion rule may collapse the former, while the latter remains evidence for P7. Measure structural fit, policy status, and erroneous compliance certification separately. An incomplete trace should not become certified just because a repair is cheap.
6. **Hold out combinations, not only cases.** Deliberately withhold combinations of check order, sourcing, supplier/expedite order, approval type, and correction scope/count while retaining their component behaviors in training. B2, B5, A1–A3, and A6 are explicit examples. Use only combinations independently authorized under the policy. A model's inability to accept them is undergeneralization even if they were statistically rare.
7. **Vary alignment assumptions.** Compare equal visible deviation costs with profiles penalizing an unobserved mandatory check more strongly and with a calibrated observation-error profile. Vary invisible routing cost separately. Record raw costs, normalized fitness, exact fitting-case fraction, and event-level repair suggestions. Examine whether rankings change and whether the chosen explanation changes for ambiguous duplicated labels. Data guards and hard policy tests remain mandatory in every profile.
8. **Check structure and constraints.** Convert all models using the same version and reduction policy. Check workflow-net soundness, final-marking reachability, proper completion, dead transitions, and boundedness. For the third model, independently check the finite guarded state space for internal/external contexts, all affected sets, and both optional-review flags. Its one-token invariant establishes boundedness; from any work state, completing pending checks and, if necessary, supplier review should allow approval and release. Verify that no forbidden approval or unsupported recheck is reachable. Soundness alone does not establish these policy invariants, and unrestricted correction loops do not guarantee inevitable termination.
9. **Report disaggregated performance and usability.** Report training and holdout alignment fitness, precision, token generalization, arc-degree simplicity, exact acceptance, rule-specific error rates, unresolved-evidence rate, and stratum counts separately. Report guarded hybrid outcomes separately from unguarded-net metrics. Add size, label multiplicities, correction-loop coverage, latency quantiles, and timeout counts. Use paired case/group bootstrap intervals where suitable; report deterministic structural counts directly.
10. **Run a documented stakeholder study and change exercise.** Recruit operations, compliance, IT, and process-owner representatives; predeclare sample size and report limitations. Counterbalance model order and provide equal instruction and diagram conventions. Ask participants to classify unseen valid/invalid cases, explain B6's missing evidence, identify approval prerequisites, and update a rule such as allowing the alternate recheck order. Score correctness, time, errors, and update consistency; collect subjective 1–5 ratings separately. Assess whether expanded Petri-net detail hides the third model's simpler source rules.

def frozen_case_split(log, manifest_path, seed=20260905):
    ids = [str(trace.attributes["concept:name"]) for trace in log]
    if len(ids) != len(set(ids)) or len(ids) < 5:
        raise ValueError("Need unique stable case IDs and enough cases to split")
    shuffled = sorted(ids)
    random.Random(seed).shuffle(shuffled)
    cut = int(0.8 * len(shuffled))
    train_ids = set(shuffled[:cut])
    holdout_ids = set(shuffled[cut:])
    manifest = {
        "seed": seed,
        "train": sorted(train_ids),
        "holdout": sorted(holdout_ids),
        # Add raw-data hash, preprocessing version, ordering rule, and group IDs.
    }
    Path(manifest_path).write_text(json.dumps(manifest, indent=2), encoding="utf-8")
    train = EventLog([t for t in log if str(t.attributes["concept:name"]) in train_ids])
    holdout = EventLog([t for t in log if str(t.attributes["concept:name"]) in holdout_ids])
    return train, holdout


def as_net(model):
    if isinstance(model, tuple) and len(model) == 3:
        return model
    return pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)


def standard_metrics(eval_log, model):
    net, im, fm = as_net(model)
    return {
        "alignment_fitness": pm4py.fitness_alignments(eval_log, net, im, fm),
        "alignment_precision": pm4py.precision_alignments(eval_log, net, im, fm),
        "token_generalization": pm4py.generalization_tbr(eval_log, net, im, fm),
        "arc_degree_simplicity": pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(
            net, im, fm, variant="arc_degree"),
    }


def exact_visible_fit(result):
    # Plain alignment moves: (log_label, model_label).
    # A model-only invisible routing move is compatible with exact visible fit.
    if result is None or result.get("alignment") is None:
        return None  # timeout/error is neither a fit nor a completed rejection
    return all(
        (left == right and left != ">>") or (left == ">>" and right is None)
        for left, right in result["alignment"]
    )


def align_with_costs(trace, net, im, fm,
                     mandatory_model_cost=10000, silent_cost=1):
    params = {
        "trace_cost_function": [10000] * len(trace),
        "model_cost_function": {
            t: (silent_cost if t.label is None else
                mandatory_model_cost if t.label in CHECK_BITS else 10000)
            for t in net.transitions
        },
        "sync_cost_function": {t: 0 for t in net.transitions},
        "ret_tuple_as_trans_desc": False,
    }
    started = time.perf_counter()
    result = align_algo.apply_trace(
        trace, net, im, fm, parameters=params,
        variant=align_algo.Variants.VERSION_STATE_EQUATION_A_STAR,
    )
    return {
        "seconds": time.perf_counter() - started,
        "exact_visible_fit": exact_visible_fit(result),
        "diagnostic": result,
    }

**Decision rule.** Reject a candidate for enforcement if it accepts any agreed critical violation, rejects any agreed valid fully evidenced policy test, treats unresolved mandatory attribute evidence as proven compliant, or fails soundness/guard-reachability checks. # 10. Governance Recommendation

Keep S, if useful, as a clearly labeled exploratory observation model. Its low precision is tolerable for that bounded purpose; its permission to skip checks must be visible in every explanation. Retire it as a policy reference, compliance gate, or calibrated simulation model.

Retire C as the general process or compliance model. Retain its eight variants as historical examples and regression fixtures, with their attribute-dependent validity documented. A separately approved closed workflow catalogue could justify a narrow use, but discovery frequency and F8 do not establish that authority.

Neither original should currently certify compliance. Pilot the third alternative in shadow mode, producing rule-specific findings and `PASS`/`FAIL`/`REVIEW` outcomes with evidence provenance. It addresses the stated structural defects, but it is a proposed governed model, not a demonstrated metric winner.

Before enforcement deployment, require:

- Owner approval of the interpretation register, especially supplier timing, internal supplier behavior, correction scope, and whether supplier/expedite decisions become stale after correction.
- Reliable source and affected-check attributes, documented capture completeness, and an explicit review workflow for unresolved evidence. Existing logs without those attributes remain eligible for analysis with qualified conclusions.
- Reproducible evaluation on a fresh confirmation set, passing the policy suite and guarded structural checks, with acceptable measured runtime and review workload.
- A versioned policy source, stable event-to-rule ownership, readable generated views, approved change control, and a documented comprehension study showing that users understand the obligations and uncertainties.
- Monitoring of deviations, evidence gaps, valid new combinations, and policy drift, with a controlled exception mechanism and rollback procedure.

The policy source, executable net, data guards, and evidence contract must be released together. Deploying the unguarded net alone would recreate the supplier and correction ambiguities this design is intended to resolve.

# 11. Dissenting View

The following is the strongest rebuttal to this recommendation:

> Your proposal risks replacing a visible, bounded catalogue with a larger system whose apparent rigor hides disputed policy. Model C has eight inspectable paths; your compiled net has dozens of transitions, latent correction choices, and a separate verifier. Audit teams must now establish that the implementation, explanatory view, attribute extraction, and enforcement integration agree. That creates failure modes which a small authorized catalogue avoids.
>
> You also make decisions the stakeholders have not actually settled: freshness at expedite, the scope of affected checks, and whether earlier supplier and expedite decisions survive correction. Calling these assumptions explicit does not authorize them. The high holdout rejection rate could reflect operating drift that governance ought to stop, rather than combinations it should accommodate automatically.
>
> Most seriously, the evidence required by your alternative does not exist in the discovery log. A review outcome may be intellectually honest but operationally costly, especially if nearly every correction case needs manual investigation. Collecting trustworthy affected-check attributes could cost more than standardizing approved correction routes. Retaining S for exploration and converting C into a formally approved, versioned whitelist may therefore offer a safer and cheaper interim arrangement. Require prospective evidence that selective rechecks reduce workload without weakening control before introducing a more expressive engine.

I would change my recommendation if policy owners formally authorized a restricted catalogue and explained why presently valid B2 or other combinations should lose authorization; if independently adjudicated holdout cases showed C's rejections were predominantly genuine violations; or if a controlled study showed that C's approved-route workflow met coverage and change-management needs at substantially lower risk and cost. I would also withdraw or revise the third design if supplier/expedite invalidation rules contradicted its state assumptions, affected-check evidence proved unreliable, its guarded implementation failed equivalence checks, or its unresolved-evidence workload was unacceptable. Those findings could favor a smaller family of explicitly authorized, attribute-conditioned workflows. They would still not justify S's mandatory-check shortcuts as compliance behavior.
