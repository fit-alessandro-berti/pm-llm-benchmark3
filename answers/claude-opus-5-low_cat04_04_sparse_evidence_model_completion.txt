**Why both DFG directions do not prove concurrency.** A directly-follows relation is computed over a total order of *completion* events. Observing `A→B` and `B→A` proves only that the completion order varied across cases. That variance is produced identically by: (i) true overlapping execution, (ii) a choice between two rigid sequences (e.g. two clerks with different habits), (iii) a short loop, and (iv) tie-breaking by ingestion index (S7). Distinguishing them requires *start* timestamps (interval overlap) or a resource/queue attribute — neither is available. Therefore "α-style" concurrency detection here is an inductive bias, not an inference.

# 2. Shared Assumptions

- **A1 — Event semantics.** Every event is a `complete` lifecycle event (S7). All orderings discussed are orderings of completions. No model claims anything about start times or overlap.
- **A2 — Tie handling.** Events sharing a timestamp are ordered arbitrarily (ingestion index). Any single observed ordering of two activities separated by 0 duration is treated as uninformative.
- **A3 — Fixed skeleton.** `Register Order` is the unique start, `Archive Case` the unique end, and `Approve Configuration → Release Production → Archive Case` is a rigid tail (D1, D2, 30/30 support).
- **A4 — Both checks mandatory.** CC and ER appear in 30/30 cases and are required by D3; no candidate makes them optional.
- **A5 — SC optionality is an abstraction.** SC is modelled as `XOR(Supplier Confirmation, τ)`. The τ branch **conflates three causes**: (a) legitimate skip for stocked components (S4), (b) a lost event due to the integration defect (S5, ~5–10% of SC events ⇒ roughly 1–2 of the 30 cases), and (c) any unknown further cause. This is a *control-flow representation of a data condition plus a data-quality defect*, adopted only because the external/stocked attribute was not exported (S5). It is a deliberate abstraction, not a claim that skipping SC is a free choice. Consequence: none of the three models can be used to *audit* SC compliance; all of them will silently accept a case whose SC event was merely dropped.
- **A6 — EX optionality is genuine.** EX is modelled as `XOR(Expedite Review, τ)`, at most once (D6). No loop is introduced; two EX events in one case are rejected by every candidate.
- **A7 — No loops anywhere.** No repetition is observed for any activity; introducing a loop would be unsupported even in the permissive model.
- **A8 — Silent transitions are free.** τ transitions carry no evidence and are ignored by all playout/trace comparisons.
- **A9 — Alphabet is closed.** Only the eight given labels may occur.
- **A10 — Frequencies are not encoded.** All three are control-flow models; the 12/7/4/3/2/1/1 distribution informs *choice of hypothesis*, not model semantics. A stochastic extension is out of scope but is recommended in §9.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

**Inductive-bias statement.** This model adopts the strongest possible *closed-world* bias: the language of the model is exactly the set of the seven observed variants. It refuses to generalize any interleaving, any unobserved combination of the SC/EX options with the check orders, and any concurrency claim, on the grounds that D7 and S7 make concurrency unidentifiable and that a single observation (V6, V7) is too weak to license a family of behaviours. Its only inductive step is that the observed 30 traces are a complete enumeration of the legal behaviour. It is the right hypothesis if the process is in fact a small, tightly governed set of checklists; it is badly wrong if the sample is simply small — with 20 traces containing SC and only 2 containing EX, that is a real possibility.

**Compact formal expression.**

```
→( RegisterOrder,
   ×( →(CC, ER, SC),
      →(ER, CC, SC),
      →(CC, ER),
      →(ER, CC),
      →(CC, SC, ER),
      →(CC, ER, SC, EX),
      →(ER, CC, EX, SC) ),
   ApproveConfiguration, ReleaseProduction, ArchiveCase )
```

**PM4Py code.**

```python
import pm4py

CONSERVATIVE_TREE_STR = (
    "->( 'Register Order', "
    "    X( ->('Credit Check','Engineering Review','Supplier Confirmation'), "
    "       ->('Engineering Review','Credit Check','Supplier Confirmation'), "
    "       ->('Credit Check','Engineering Review'), "
    "       ->('Engineering Review','Credit Check'), "
    "       ->('Credit Check','Supplier Confirmation','Engineering Review'), "
    "       ->('Credit Check','Engineering Review','Supplier Confirmation','Expedite Review'), "
    "       ->('Engineering Review','Credit Check','Expedite Review','Supplier Confirmation') ), "
    "    'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case' )"
)

model_conservative = pm4py.parse_process_tree(CONSERVATIVE_TREE_STR)
```

*Duplicate labels and scalability.* This construction requires **duplicate labels**: `Credit Check` and `Engineering Review` each appear 7 times, `Supplier Confirmation` 5 times. Consequences: (i) alignment-based conformance becomes more expensive because the synchronous-move search space branches over indistinguishable transitions; (ii) precision metrics that use "escaping edges" behave optimistically — the model is maximally precise by construction; (iii) any downstream analysis keyed by transition identity (bottleneck overlay, decision mining) must first fold duplicates back to labels; (iv) the structure scales **linearly in the number of observed variants and combinatorially in the underlying behaviour**: if CC/ER are truly concurrent and SC/EX truly optional and freely positioned, the full behaviour would need dozens of branches, and every newly observed variant forces a model edit. This is the classic overfitting signature of variant enumeration ("flower-model's opposite"): perfect fitness and precision on the sample, zero generalization.

**Behaviour deliberately excluded.**
1. `ER, CC, SC, EX` and `CC, ER, EX, SC` — the two EX-bearing check-order/SC-position combinations not observed, although D6/S6 permit them.
2. EX without SC (e.g. `CC, ER, EX`) — permitted by D6 + S4 but never seen.
3. `ER, CC, SC` with SC before ER's completion (i.e. `ER, SC, CC` / `CC, SC, ER` mirrored) — only the `CC, SC, ER` variant is admitted.
4. Any interleaving of EX between the two checks, and any occurrence of SC before both checks.
5. All repetitions of any activity.

**Underfitting risks.**
- **Rejects legal cases.** Any of the ~8–20 further interleavings that D3–D6 clearly permit would be flagged as non-conforming; used as a compliance model it would generate a high false-alarm rate.
- **Brittle to the rare tail.** EX-cases have n=2; the model canonizes those two accidental positions as the only legal ones.
- **Blind to the missing-data hypothesis.** It treats `CC, ER` (no SC) as a first-class variant rather than as possibly a corrupted `CC, ER, SC`, so a case whose SC event is dropped is silently "conforming" while a case with SC in a new position is rejected — exactly backwards from a data-quality standpoint.
- **Maintenance load.** Model must be re-cut whenever a new variant appears; concept drift is indistinguishable from noise.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

**Inductive-bias statement.** This model generalizes only along dimensions where the evidence is *structurally* consistent, and refuses to generalize where it is not. It accepts S2's concurrency reading for CC/ER (unordered), accepts the invariant that held in 20/20 SC-cases (`CC` completes before `SC`), keeps SC and EX mutually unordered because both orders were observed (V6, V7), and enforces the compliance rule that EX follows both checks (D6/S6). A strict partial order (POWL) is used precisely because the required constraint set — "EX after both checks, SC after CC, SC and EX incomparable, everything before AC" — is *not* block-structured and cannot be written as a process tree without either losing a constraint or duplicating labels.

**Compact formal expression.** `PO(N, <)` embedded in the fixed skeleton:

```
Nodes:  RO, CC, ER, SCopt = ×(SC, τ), EXopt = ×(EX, τ), AC, RP, AR
Edges:  RO<CC, RO<ER,
        CC<SCopt,                       # 20/20 empirical invariant, and D5's "one check suffices"
        CC<EXopt, ER<EXopt,             # D6 / S6
        CC<AC, ER<AC, SCopt<AC, EXopt<AC,   # D3, D4, D6
        AC<RP, RP<AR                    # D2, D1
(no edge between ER and SCopt; none between SCopt and EXopt; none between CC and ER)
```

**PM4Py code.**

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

ro = Transition(label="Register Order")
cc = Transition(label="Credit Check")
er = Transition(label="Engineering Review")
ac = Transition(label="Approve Configuration")
rp = Transition(label="Release Production")
ar = Transition(label="Archive Case")

# Optionality as an explicit XOR with a silent branch (see assumption A5/A6).
sc_opt = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                      children=[Transition(label="Supplier Confirmation"), SilentTransition()])
ex_opt = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                      children=[Transition(label="Expedite Review"), SilentTransition()])

model_balanced = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[ro, cc, er, sc_opt, ex_opt, ac, rp, ar])
for s, t in [
    (ro, cc), (ro, er),
    (cc, sc_opt),
    (cc, ex_opt), (er, ex_opt),
    (cc, ac), (er, ac), (sc_opt, ac), (ex_opt, ac),
    (ac, rp), (rp, ar),
]:
    model_balanced.order.add_edge(s, t)
```

**How sequence vs. concurrency vs. partial order was decided, activity by activity.**

- **Credit Check vs. Engineering Review → unordered (concurrent).** Decision rule: both completion orders observed (19 vs. 11 — not a rare-noise ratio), no evidence of a data-driven switch, and a domain expert (S2) asserts parallelism. An XOR-of-two-sequences would fit equally well but would postulate an unobservable decision variable with no candidate attribute behind it; parsimony (one construct instead of a hidden choice + duplicated labels) favours "unordered". S1 contradicts this and is the main risk (§10). Note: in a POWL, "unordered" means *interleaving allowed*, which is exactly the observationally justified claim; it does not assert genuine temporal overlap.
- **Supplier Confirmation vs. Credit Check → `CC < SC` (ordered).** Decision rule: an ordering is imposed when it holds in **100% of the cases where both occur** and is compatible with the domain rule. `CC` precedes `SC` in all 20 SC-cases; D5 explicitly says one check suffices to release SC. Imposing it costs nothing on fitness and buys precision.
- **Supplier Confirmation vs. Engineering Review → unordered.** Decision rule: an ordering is *not* imposed when a counterexample exists. V5 (`CC, SC, ER`, 2 cases) is that counterexample; D5 supports it. This is the one place where the model is strictly more permissive than a "majority" reading of the DFG (`ER→SC` 13 vs. `SC→ER` 2).
- **Expedite Review vs. both checks → `CC < EX`, `ER < EX` (ordered).** Decision rule: a compliance rule stated by two independent sources (D6, S6) with no observed counterexample is encoded as a hard constraint, even at n=2.
- **Expedite Review vs. Supplier Confirmation → unordered.** Decision rule: both orders observed (V6 and V7, one case each) *and* S6 explicitly declares them order-free. Two weak signals pointing the same way are treated as sufficient to refuse an ordering. This pair is the reason the model is a POWL and not a tree.
- **Optionality (SC, EX) → local XOR with τ, not a partial-order feature.** POWL has no "skippable node", so optionality must be a child operator. Both are placed *inside* the partial order so that the precedence edges apply whichever branch is taken (with the τ branch the edges are vacuous).

**Behaviour deliberately excluded.**
1. `SC` before `CC` (e.g. `RO, SC, CC, ER, ...`) — despite D5's "either check", no case supports SC preceding CC.
2. `EX` before either check completes (e.g. `RO, CC, EX, ER, ...`) — forbidden by D6/S6.
3. `EX` or `SC` after `AC`.
4. Two `EX` events, or two `SC` events.
5. Any case ending before `Archive Case` or releasing production without approval.

**Underfitting risks.**
- If D5 truly means "after CC **or** ER", the model wrongly rejects `RO, ER, SC, CC, ...`. A strict partial order cannot express a disjunctive precedence at all; the choice of `CC` as the enabling check is an empirical bet on 20 cases with no *causal* backing.
- If EX is in reality permitted after a single check under time pressure, the two `< EX` edges over-constrain a rare, high-stakes path.
- The τ-branch for SC means a genuinely non-conforming case (external component, SC skipped) is accepted; the model cannot detect the S3 violation. This is underfitting *of the compliance question*, deliberately traded for fitness on a log that cannot distinguish skip from loss.
- Concurrency of CC/ER over-generalizes if S1 holds: 11 traces would then be data-quality artifacts rather than legal behaviour, and the model would legitimize them.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

**Inductive-bias statement.** This model keeps only the constraints that are high-confidence or structurally invariant — the start/end skeleton, the mandatory presence of both checks, and the `AC → RP → AR` tail — and lets the middle phase be a free interleaving of the two checks with an optional SC and an optional EX. Its bias is *open-world*: any ordering inside the pre-approval phase is assumed legal unless a high-confidence rule forbids it, on the reasoning that completion-only timestamps under-report the true interleaving space and that 30 cases cannot enumerate it. It is expressed as a **ProcessTree** so that the trade-off is visible: block structure buys simplicity and readability but forces the loss of D6's precedence constraint.

**Compact formal expression.**

```
→( RegisterOrder,
   ∧( CC, ER, ×(SC, τ), ×(EX, τ) ),
   ApproveConfiguration, ReleaseProduction, ArchiveCase )
```

**PM4Py code.**

```python
import pm4py

PERMISSIVE_TREE_STR = (
    "->( 'Register Order', "
    "    +( 'Credit Check', "
    "       'Engineering Review', "
    "       X('Supplier Confirmation', tau), "
    "       X('Expedite Review', tau) ), "
    "    'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case' )"
)

model_permissive = pm4py.parse_process_tree(PERMISSIVE_TREE_STR)
```

**Disclosed structural compromise.** Because `EX` must be unordered w.r.t. `SC` (V6/V7) yet ordered *after* both checks (D6), and because a process tree cannot place a node "after two siblings but concurrent with a third" without duplicating labels, the parallel block **drops the `CC < EX` and `ER < EX` edges**. The permissive tree therefore admits `RO, EX, CC, ER, AC, ...`, which violates D6/S6. This is accepted as the price of the block-structured, maximally-general hypothesis; if that specific violation is unacceptable, the same permissive language minus the violation is obtainable as a POWL identical to `model_balanced` but with the `CC < SC` edge removed and the `< EX` edges retained.

**Boundary between reasonable generalization and forbidden behaviour.** Reasonable generalization = *reordering within the pre-approval phase* and *presence/absence of the two optional activities*, because neither is identifiable from completion timestamps and both are consistent with D3–D6 read broadly. Forbidden = anything touching (i) the case skeleton — no case may start other than with RO or end other than with AR; (ii) the approval gate — no CC, ER, SC or EX after AC, and no RP without AC (D2, high confidence); (iii) cardinality — no activity repeats, in particular EX at most once (D6) and no loops; (iv) omission of a mandatory check — both CC and ER must occur (D3). In short: the *order* inside the middle phase is free, its *membership* and the *gate* around it are not.

**Underfitting risks.**
- Severe precision loss: the middle block alone admits 4! / (symmetry) — 24 orderings with both options present, ×4 option combinations, i.e. **40 distinct complete traces**, of which 7 are observed. Anomalies such as SC completing before any check would pass conformance unnoticed.
- It legitimizes the D6 violation described above, so it cannot be used for compliance reporting on expedited orders.
- It cannot express that SC-absence is data-driven; the `×(SC, τ)` is a free choice, so the model gives no leverage at all on the S3/S4/S5 question.
- Being frequency-blind and maximally permissive, it will make any drift detection insensitive: a shift from `CC,ER` to `ER,CC` dominance produces no conformance signal.

# 7. Comparative Model Matrix

| Criterion | Conservative | Balanced | Permissive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expected fitness on the 30 observed cases | Perfect (1.0 by construction — the log *is* the language). | Perfect (every observed variant satisfies all imposed edges; verified in §8). | Perfect (every observed variant is an interleaving of the block). |
| Expected precision risk | Minimal; precision at or near the maximum an alignment-based measure can report. Overfitting risk instead is maximal. | Moderate: language ≈ 12–16 complete traces vs. 7 observed → precision plausibly in the high-0.7 to low-0.9 band, not a computed value. | High: language ≈ 40 complete traces vs. 7 observed → precision likely in the 0.5–0.7 band. |
| Generalization potential | Effectively none; any new legal variant is rejected. | Good and *directed* — it generalizes exactly along the dimensions the evidence says are free. | Maximal within the phase, but undirected — it generalizes into behaviour no rule endorses. |
| Structural simplicity | Poor: 7 branches, ~23 leaves, 12 duplicate labels; grows with every new variant. | Moderate: 8 nodes, 11 edges, 2 small XOR blocks, no duplicates; but a non-block-structured formalism that some tooling handles less well. | Best: 8 leaves, 3 operators, no duplicates; a one-line model. |
| Explainability | Easy to read case-by-case ("these are the seven approved checklists"), hard to explain *why* — every constraint is "because we saw it". | Best: each edge maps to a named piece of evidence (D3/D4/D6, S6, the 20/20 CC-before-SC invariant); each omitted edge maps to a named counterexample. | Easy to state ("do the checks and the optional steps in any order, then approve"), but one of its behaviours (EX first) is an admitted artifact rather than a claim. |
| Robustness to missing SC events (S5) | Poor: a dropped SC turns V1 into V3 — still accepted, but the model records that as a distinct legitimate variant, so the defect is invisible and inflates the "no-SC" branch. If a drop produced an *unseen* residual variant it would be rejected outright. | Moderate: the τ branch absorbs drops without rejection; the model is stable under 5–10% loss, but by design cannot distinguish loss from a legitimate skip. Adding the missing attribute would let this XOR be converted into a data-conditioned choice. | Moderate-to-poor in a different sense: absorbs drops trivially, but is so permissive that no missingness signal could ever surface through conformance. |
| Handling of unseen interleavings | Rejects them — high false-alarm rate on legal behaviour. | Accepts those the rules permit, rejects those they forbid — the intended behaviour. | Accepts all of them, including D6-violating ones. |
| Suitability: compliance vs. exploration | Neither, really: too brittle for compliance (false alarms), too closed for exploration. Useful only as a *variant catalogue*/baseline. | **Compliance**: encodes D2, D3, D4, D6 and S6 as enforceable constraints while tolerating the known data defect. | **Exploration**: a safe over-approximation for discovering what the process *could* do, for playout-based simulation scaffolding, and as a null model against which to test precision. Unusable for compliance because of the D6 relaxation. |

All assessments are qualitative or interval-valued; no exact metric values are asserted here — the numbers must come from running §8 on the actual log.

```python
"""
Validation harness for the three candidate models.
Self-contained; requires pm4py (>= 2.7 for POWL support).
"""

import itertools
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 1. the log
VARIANTS = [
    (12, "Register Order,Credit Check,Engineering Review,Supplier Confirmation,"
         "Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case"),
    (7,  "Register Order,Engineering Review,Credit Check,Supplier Confirmation,"
         "Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case"),
    (4,  "Register Order,Credit Check,Engineering Review,"
         "Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case"),
    (3,  "Register Order,Engineering Review,Credit Check,"
         "Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case"),
    (2,  "Register Order,Credit Check,Supplier Confirmation,Engineering Review,"
         "Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case"),
    (1,  "Register Order,Credit Check,Engineering Review,Supplier Confirmation,"
         "Expedite Review,Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case"),
    (1,  "Register Order,Engineering Review,Credit Check,Expedite Review,"
         "Supplier Confirmation,Approve Configuration,Release Production,Archive Case"),
]
TRACE_STRINGS = [s for n, s in VARIANTS for _ in range(n)]
assert len(TRACE_STRINGS) == 30

log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(TRACE_STRINGS, sep=",")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 2. models
CONSERVATIVE_TREE_STR = (
    "->( 'Register Order', "
    "    X( ->('Credit Check','Engineering Review','Supplier Confirmation'), "
    "       ->('Engineering Review','Credit Check','Supplier Confirmation'), "
    "       ->('Credit Check','Engineering Review'), "
    "       ->('Engineering Review','Credit Check'), "
    "       ->('Credit Check','Supplier Confirmation','Engineering Review'), "
    "       ->('Credit Check','Engineering Review','Supplier Confirmation','Expedite Review'), "
    "       ->('Engineering Review','Credit Check','Expedite Review','Supplier Confirmation') ), "
    "    'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case' )"
)
model_conservative = pm4py.parse_process_tree(CONSERVATIVE_TREE_STR)

PERMISSIVE_TREE_STR = (
    "->( 'Register Order', "
    "    +( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', "
    "       X('Supplier Confirmation', tau), X('Expedite Review', tau) ), "
    "    'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case' )"
)
model_permissive = pm4py.parse_process_tree(PERMISSIVE_TREE_STR)


def build_balanced():
    ro = Transition(label="Register Order")
    cc = Transition(label="Credit Check")
    er = Transition(label="Engineering Review")
    ac = Transition(label="Approve Configuration")
    rp = Transition(label="Release Production")
    ar = Transition(label="Archive Case")
    sc_opt = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                          children=[Transition(label="Supplier Confirmation"),
                                    SilentTransition()])
    ex_opt = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                          children=[Transition(label="Expedite Review"),
                                    SilentTransition()])
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[ro, cc, er, sc_opt, ex_opt, ac, rp, ar])
    for s, t in [(ro, cc), (ro, er), (cc, sc_opt),
                 (cc, ex_opt), (er, ex_opt),
                 (cc, ac), (er, ac), (sc_opt, ac), (ex_opt, ac),
                 (ac, rp), (rp, ar)]:
        po.order.add_edge(s, t)
    return po


model_balanced = build_balanced()

CANDIDATES = {
    "conservative": model_conservative,
    "balanced": model_balanced,
    "permissive": model_permissive,
}

# ------------------------------------- 3. conversion + soundness + 4. metrics
nets = {}
for name, model in CANDIDATES.items():
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    nets[name] = (net, im, fm)
    print(f"\n=== {name} ===")
    print("places=%d transitions=%d arcs=%d" %
          (len(net.places), len(net.transitions), len(net.arcs)))
    try:
        sound = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)
        print("soundness:", sound[0] if isinstance(sound, tuple) else sound)
    except Exception as e:                      # pragma: no cover
        print("soundness check failed:", e)

    for label, fn in [
        ("fitness", lambda: pm4py.fitness_alignments(log, net, im, fm)),
        ("precision", lambda: pm4py.precision_alignments(log, net, im, fm)),
        ("generalization", lambda: pm4py.generalization_tbr(log, net, im, fm)),
        ("simplicity", lambda: pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm)),
    ]:
        try:
            print(f"{label}: {fn()}")
        except Exception as e:
            print(f"{label}: unavailable in this pm4py build ({e})")

# --------------------------------------------------- 5. bounded/sampled playout
PLAYOUT_CASES = 2000
for name, (net, im, fm) in nets.items():
    try:
        pl = pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm, parameters={"noTraces": PLAYOUT_CASES})
    except TypeError:
        pl = pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm)
    seen = {tuple(e["concept:name"] for e in t) for t in pl}
    print(f"\n{name}: {len(seen)} distinct complete traces in a "
          f"{PLAYOUT_CASES}-case sampled playout")
    for tr in sorted(seen):
        print("   ", " | ".join(tr))

# LIMITATION (stated explicitly, per the task): a sampled playout enumerates a
# *subset* of the model language. For these three models the language happens to
# be finite (no loops), so a sufficiently large sample very likely enumerates it
# fully -- but "very likely" is not a proof. Nothing below should be read as
# establishing language inclusion or equivalence between two candidates; it only
# reports which traces the sampler happened to produce. Exhaustive comparison of
# two Petri net languages would require a reachability-graph / state-space
# construction, which pm4py's playout API does not perform.

# ------------------------------------------- 6. positive / negative test suite
PREF, SUFF = ["Register Order"], ["Approve Configuration", "Release Production",
                                  "Archive Case"]
def T(*mid):
    return PREF + list(mid) + SUFF

CC, ER, SC, EX = ("Credit Check", "Engineering Review",
                  "Supplier Confirmation", "Expedite Review")

OBSERVED = [T(CC, ER, SC), T(ER, CC, SC), T(CC, ER), T(ER, CC),
            T(CC, SC, ER), T(CC, ER, SC, EX), T(ER, CC, EX, SC)]

SUITE = {
    # name: (trace, {model: expected_accept})
    "observed_v1":      (T(CC, ER, SC),
                         {"conservative": True,  "balanced": True,  "permissive": True}),
    "observed_v5":      (T(CC, SC, ER),
                         {"conservative": True,  "balanced": True,  "permissive": True}),
    "observed_v7":      (T(ER, CC, EX, SC),

    "ex_other_order":   (T(ER, CC, SC, EX),
                         {"conservative": False, "balanced": True,  "permissive": True}),
    "ex_without_sc":    (T(ER, CC, EX),
                         {"conservative": False, "balanced": True,  "permissive": True}),
    "sc_before_cc":     (T(SC, CC, ER),
                         {"conservative": False, "balanced": False, "permissive": True}),
    "ex_before_checks": (T(CC, EX, ER),
                         {"conservative": False, "balanced": False, "permissive": True}),
    "sc_after_ac":      (PREF + [CC, ER, "Approve Configuration", SC,
                                 "Release Production", "Archive Case"],
                         {"conservative": False, "balanced": False, "permissive": False}),
    "rp_before_ac":     (PREF + [CC, ER, SC, "Release Production",
                                 "Approve Configuration", "Archive Case"],
                         {"conservative": False, "balanced": False, "permissive": False}),
    "missing_er":       (T(CC, SC),
                         {"conservative": False, "balanced": False, "permissive": False}),
    "double_ex":        (T(CC, ER, SC, EX, EX),
                         {"conservative": False, "balanced": False, "permissive": False}),
}


def accepts(net, im, fm, trace):
    """Exact replay via alignments: accepted iff a perfectly fitting
    alignment exists (fitness == 1.0 for that single trace)."""
    one = pm4py.parse_event_log_string([",".join(trace)], sep=",")
    res = pm4py.fitness_alignments(one, net, im, fm)
    return res["log_fitness"] >= 1.0 - 1e-9


print("\n=== positive / negative suite ===")
failures = 0
for tname, (trace, expected) in SUITE.items():
    for mname, exp in expected.items():
        net, im, fm = nets[mname]
        got = accepts(net, im, fm, trace)
        ok = (got == exp)
        failures += (not ok)
        print(f"{'OK ' if ok else 'FAIL'}  {mname:12s} {tname:18s} "
              f"expected={exp} got={got}")
print(f"\n{failures} suite mismatch(es).")

# Sanity: all three candidates must accept all 7 observed variants.
for mname, (net, im, fm) in nets.items():
    assert all(accepts(net, im, fm, t) for t in OBSERVED), \
        f"{mname} does not fit an observed variant"
print("All candidates fit all observed variants.")
```

Notes on the harness: `check_soundness` should report all three nets as sound workflow nets (they are block-structured trees, or a POWL whose conversion yields an acyclic sound net). Alignment-based acceptance (`fitness == 1.0` on a single-trace log) is used instead of token replay because token replay can under- or over-report on nets with silent and duplicate transitions. The `precision_alignments` value for the conservative model is inflated by construction and should not be compared naively across candidates with very different label multiplicities.

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

**Recommend for immediate use: `model_balanced` (POWL).** It fits all 30 cases, encodes every medium-or-higher-confidence rule (D1–D4, D6, S6) as an enforceable constraint, generalizes only where a concrete counterexample or an explicit expert statement licenses it, and each of its 11 edges is traceable to a named piece of evidence. It is the only candidate usable for conformance checking without either flooding reviewers with false alarms (conservative) or silently condoning a compliance violation (permissive).

**Recommend for sensitivity analysis: `model_permissive` (ProcessTree).** Running the same log and any future log against both gives a direct read on how much of the behaviour space is actually exercised: cases accepted by permissive but rejected by balanced are exactly the candidate "new legal behaviour vs. genuine deviation" set, and that difference set is the cheapest queue of cases to hand to the domain experts. The conservative model is retained only as a variant catalogue and as a precision upper-bound baseline, not as a deployed model.

**Data-collection plan to resolve the ambiguities.**

1. **Start timestamps (lifecycle `start` + `complete`) for Credit Check and Engineering Review** — the single highest-value item. This resolves D7 and adjudicates S1 vs. S2 directly: if any case shows `start(ER) < complete(CC)`, S1 is false and concurrency (or at least overlap) is real; if `start(ER) ≥ complete(CC)` in 100% of cases, S1 holds and both the balanced and permissive models are wrong to leave CC/ER unordered — the conservative XOR-of-sequences reading becomes correct, restricted to the `CC, ER` order only.
2. **The external/stocked component attribute at order level** — resolves the SC XOR. With it, `×(SC, τ)` becomes a *data-conditioned* choice: SC mandatory when `external=true`, skippable otherwise, and every `external=true` case without an SC event becomes a detectable violation or a detectable data-quality incident.
3. **Fix and then backfill the SC integration defect (S5), and add an ingestion audit flag** — separates missingness from optionality. Concretely: reconcile SC events against the supplier system's own records for the same 30 cases; the reconciliation delta tells you how many of the 7 SC-absent cases are artifacts. Until then, no model can answer "is SC really optional?".
4. **Start timestamps for Supplier Confirmation** — resolves D5. If `start(SC)` follows `complete(CC)` in all cases but sometimes precedes `complete(ER)`, the balanced model's `CC < SC` edge is confirmed as causal, not coincidental. If some case has `start(SC)` after `complete(ER)` but before `complete(CC)`, the disjunctive form of D5 is confirmed and the balanced model must be relaxed (and no strict partial order can capture it — a POWL with a `×` over two ordered alternatives, or a Petri net with an OR-join, would be required).
5. **The expedite trigger attribute** (e.g. requested delivery date, priority flag, customer segment) — with n=2, EX behaviour is essentially unmodelled. Even 20 more EX cases without an explanatory attribute would only sharpen the ordering question, not the *when* question.
6. **Resource / queue attributes for CC and ER** — if the two checks are performed by the same resource in every case, the "true concurrency" hypothesis is undermined regardless of what timestamps say.

**Maximally informative future traces**, ranked by how much each would move the posterior:

| Future trace observed | Discriminates |
|---|---|
| `RO, ER, SC, CC, ...` (SC completes before CC) | Kills the balanced model's `CC < SC` edge; confirms the disjunctive reading of D5; forces a POWL-with-XOR or an OR-join. **Highest information value.** |
| Any case with `start(ER) < complete(CC)` | Refutes S1; settles concurrency vs. XOR-of-sequences for the check pair. |
| `RO, CC, ER, EX, AC, ...` (EX without SC) | Confirms EX and SC are independently optional — currently only assumed. |
| `RO, ER, CC, SC, EX, ...` and `RO, CC, ER, EX, SC, ...` | Confirms EX/SC are genuinely unordered rather than the two observed cases being coincidence; both are already admitted by the balanced model. |
| A case with EX before a check completes | Would refute D6/S6 and vindicate the permissive tree's relaxation — currently believed impossible, so this is a high-value falsification test. |
| A run of `CC, ER` (no SC) cases whose orders reconcile to *present* SC in the supplier system | Directly quantifies the S5 loss rate and shrinks the τ branch's ambiguity. |

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

| Decision | Confidence | Basis and what would change it |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed skeleton: RO start, AR end, `AC → RP → AR` | ~0.98 | 30/30 support plus two high-confidence rules (D1, D2). Only a logging change would alter this. |
| Both CC and ER mandatory before AC | ~0.95 | 30/30 support plus D3 (medium-high). Would change only if a legitimate "no engineering review" order type exists and simply did not appear in 30 cases. |
| CC and ER left **unordered** (balanced, permissive) | ~0.6 | Supported by 11 counter-ordered cases and S2, contradicted by S1 and unidentifiable per D7/S7. This is the weakest load-bearing decision in the recommended model. |
| SC modelled as optional (`×(SC, τ)`) | ~0.85 that *some* form of optionality is needed; only ~0.4 that all 7 absences are legitimate | S4 asserts real optionality; S5 says 5–10% of SC events may be lost (≈1–2 cases). The construct is right; its *interpretation* is uncertain. |
| `CC < SC` edge (balanced) | ~0.65 | Empirically perfect (20/20) but D5 says "either check", so the edge may be an artifact of CC usually finishing first. Explicitly flagged as an empirical bet. |
| SC left unordered w.r.t. ER | ~0.85 | Two direct counterexamples (V5) plus D5. Strong. |
| EX after both checks (balanced) | ~0.8 | Two independent normative sources (D6, S6), no counterexample — but only n=2 of behavioural support. |
| EX unordered w.r.t. SC | ~0.8 | Both orders observed (n=1 each) *and* S6 states it explicitly; the agreement of weak evidence with a normative statement carries the confidence. |
| EX at most once, no loops anywhere | ~0.9 | D6 explicit for EX; zero repetition observed for anything else. |
| Permissive tree's loss of the `< EX` edges | Known-wrong, ~1.0 | Deliberate representational compromise, disclosed in §5; it is the reason the permissive model is unfit for compliance. |
| Recommendation of `model_balanced` | ~0.7 | Conditional on the CC/ER concurrency reading and the `CC < SC` edge both surviving. |

**Plausible alternative interpretation that would materially change the recommendation.** If **S1 is correct** — Credit Check must finish before Engineering Review starts — then the 11 traces showing `ER` before `CC` are not evidence of concurrency at all but evidence of a *process violation, a timestamp-tie artifact, or a systematic recording lag on Credit Check completions*. Under that reading the true model is `→(RO, CC, ER, ×(SC, τ), ×(EX, τ)-appropriately-placed, AC, RP, AR)` with a rigid check order, roughly one third of the log is non-conforming rather than legal, and the balanced model would be actively harmful: it would legitimize the very violations Finance wants surfaced. My recommendation would flip to a *stricter-than-conservative* normative model plus a deviation report on the 11 `ER`-first cases. Note that the conservative model, despite its overfitting, is the only candidate that at least keeps the two check orders as *distinguishable named branches*, which is why it is worth retaining as a catalogue. A second, milder alternative: if the S5 reconciliation shows that most or all SC absences are lost events, then SC is effectively *mandatory*, `×(SC, τ)` should be replaced by a plain `SC` node, and the "SC skipped" branch becomes a data-quality alert rather than a control-flow path. Both alternatives are resolvable by items 1 and 3 of the data-collection plan, which is why those are ranked first.
