## 1. Conformance semantics

**Closed-world vs. open-world.** The norm is closed-world only for (a) relations explicitly marked `forbidden` and (b) the `mandatory` relations *within their stated scope*. Under closed-world reading, an observed instance of a forbidden relation is a violation by construction, and the *absence* of a mandatory relation inside its scope is a deviation by construction. Everything else is open-world: absence of a normative edge is **not** evidence of anything. This is why `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer`, `Package object_codeath Delivery`, and `Reopen Order -> Close Order` cannot be labelled violations — the norm simply has no coverage (N2 even states intermediate events are "incompletely modeled"; N6 is `unspecified`).

Deviation classes I use:

- **Forbidden behavior** – an observed instance of a relation with status `forbidden`, inside scope. Closed-world: no appeal to silence elsewhere can excuse it. (N3, T6, E10.)
- **Missing mandatory behavior** – a `mandatory` relation inside its stated scope with no supporting observation. Not automatically a process failure: scope applicability and abstraction must be tested first. (N1, N4, E2, E6, E8.)
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior** – observed behavior the norm does not cover and does not sanction. Neutral by default. (`Validate -> Issue Invoice` 72; `Create -> Manual Compliance Review` 3; `Reopen -> Close` 1; OTG Customer/RiskCase edges.)
- **Typical-pattern absence** – a `typical` relation under-observed relative to expectation. Never a violation; at most a monitoring signal. (N2: 83 of ~98–100; N5: 92 of 110.)
- **Likely data-quality error** – a relation whose existence is better explained by extraction, join, clock, or identity failure than by real business action. The *observation* may still be forbidden while the *root cause* is technical; these must be reported separately so that process owners are not sanctioned for pipeline defects. (E10 `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` 1, stated join-on-reused-identifier.)
- **Abstraction mismatch** – the norm states an eventual/epistemic expectation ("should eventually enter validation") but the observation is computed as a directly-follows relation on a projected object lifecycle. Gap here proves only "not directly followed", not "never happened". (N1; plausibly N4.)
- **Insufficient evidence** – the summary cannot decide. Includes snapshot censoring (14 invoices with no successor), counting ambiguity (72 + 83 = 155 directly-follow successors for 98 validated orders), and cross-family semantic drift (OTG `(Package, interaction, Delivery)` = 200 vs. ET–OT `Dispatch Package -> Delivery` = 198).

Severity is calibrated on *business exposure*, not on norm status alone: a forbidden relation with real fulfilment consequences outranks a forbidden relation that is almost certainly a join artifact.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG
**Can establish:** which activity pairs are adjacent in an object's projected lifecycle, and how often. It is the right instrument for N1–N6 and shows the N3 signature directly (2 Orders). **Cannot establish:** causality, event-time vs. record-time order, whether an activity ever happened at all (only whether it was *adjacent*), or cross-object facts.
**Counts and scopes:** N2 is scoped to Orders reaching `Validate Order`; the denominator is 98 validated, not 100 created. The 72 + 83 = 155 tally exceeds that denominator, so the counts cannot all be per-Order adjacent pairs — repetition or a different counting unit is present (finding 10). N5's denominator is 110 invoices; the 4 write-offs and 14 silent invoices sit inside N5's own sanctioned-exception language (write-offs, credit notes, open credit terms), so 18 non-payments are **not** 18 deviations.
**Conforming here, suspicious elsewhere:** `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` at 198/200 looks like a mandatory gap, but E4/E5 show all 200 packages had both events — so both activities happened; only *adjacency* failed. That is an abstraction mismatch unless intermediate events are ruled out.

**The "eventually" trap.** N1 says a created order "should eventually enter validation", and the summary itself "approximates this with a directly-follows expectation." Eventual (reachability) and directly-follows (adjacency) are different predicates: `eventually(P)` is implied by `directly(P)`, but its negation is **not** implied by `not directly(P)`. Judging 2 orders as missing validation on adjacency evidence alone is exactly the error of reading a business rule as a control-flow rule. The same trap threatens N4 (intermediate QC/relabel activities) and N2/N5 (intermediate invoice/review/fulfilment events are explicitly declared out of model). Any mandatory gap in this family must first survive a "does an intervening event plausibly exist?" test.

### OTG
**Can establish:** structural coupling between object lifetimes — interactions and co-birth/co-death — independent of activity order. Good for T1–T6 and for detecting identity-level anomalies. **Cannot establish:** which event produced the coupling, in what order, or by whom.
**Counts and scopes:** T1 and T2 are satisfied in aggregate (1450 and 200), but aggregate satisfaction can mask event-level gaps — precisely the 200 vs. 198 tension in finding 9. If `object_interaction` is a lifetime-level relation while ET–OT requires an event-level link, both numbers can be correct and the "mandatory satisfied" verdict is only true at the coarser abstraction. T3 is `typical`, so 92 of 93 payments linked to invoices is normal, not a deficit. T4/T5 are `unspecified`, so weights 100 and 3 carry no normative signal at all.
**Conforming at one abstraction, suspicious at another:** T2 satisfied (200 edges) is reconcilable with E6 failing on 2 events — the same two dispatches have a Delivery *relationship somewhere in the log* but not on the dispatch event. Only the finer abstraction exposes the traceability gap.

### ET–OT
**Can establish:** which object types an event instance is required to touch — the purest test of E1–E10, because the scope ("every `X` event") is stated per event. **Cannot establish:** ordering, or whether a missing link is a missing business action or a missing database relation.
**Counts and scopes:** E2 (1450 vs 1448) and E6 (200 vs 198) are genuine per-event mandatory gaps with explicit scopes ("every Add Item event", "every Dispatch Package event") — these cannot be waved away as typicality. E8 is subtler: its scope contains an explicit exception ("except events explicitly typed as unapplied cash; none are so typed in this snapshot"), so the exception is *not available* on this evidence and the 1 event remains an E8 deviation, even though finance plausibly says the underlying business is legitimate. That is a classification-correct, business-benign finding — the remedy is labelling, not sanction. E10's forbidden link must be reported as a forbidden observation first (closed-world) and as a suspected join artifact second; the second explains the cause, it does not un-observe the first.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

**Mandatory – satisfied:** T1 (1450); T2 aggregate (200); E1 (100); E3 (1450); E4 (200); E5 (200); E7 (93).
**Mandatory – deviations:** N1 (2 orders), N4 (2 packages), E2 (2 events), E6 (2 events), E8 (1 event).
*Flip conditions:* N1 and N4 become **conforming** if intervening/unrecorded events are confirmed (abstraction mismatch) or if the norm is restated as eventual/reachability. E6 becomes **conforming** if a carrier reference is accepted as a Delivery surrogate and E6's scope is amended. E8 becomes **conforming (typical exception)** the moment the event is explicitly typed as unapplied cash; typed, it is not a deviation at all. E2 has no flip condition other than genuine object-link loss being repaired.

**Forbidden:** N3 (2 Orders); T6 (1 cobirth); E10 (1 `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice`).
*Flip conditions:* N3 becomes a **data-quality artifact** (not a business violation) if cancellation was recorded after the physical dispatch or the Order–Package link is wrong — it does not become "unregulated". T6 flips to a **log-hygiene finding** only if the record is proven synthetic/test data. E10 stays an observed forbidden relation but its *class of remedy* changes from process sanction to data repair if the join artifact is confirmed.

**Typical:** N2 (`Validate -> Close`, 83, with 15 without = typical-pattern absence); N5 (`Issue Invoice -> Record Payment`, 92/110); T3 (92, 1 payment unlinked = allowed unapplied cash).
*Flip conditions:* N5's 14 no-successor invoices escalate to a **credit-risk finding** (still not a norm violation) if aging shows them overdue beyond terms. T3's unlinked payment flips to the **mandatory bucket (E8)** precisely because it is *not* labelled unapplied cash.

**Unregulated / unspecified:** `Validate -> Issue Invoice` (72); `Create -> Manual Compliance Review` (3); `Reopen -> Close` (1, N6); `(Order, interaction, Customer)` (100, T4); `(Order, interaction, RiskCase)` (3, T5); `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer` (3); `Issue Invoice -> Write Off` (4, sanctioned inside N5's typical scope); `Package object_codeath Delivery` (7); `Issue Invoice` with no successor (14 = insufficient evidence).
*Flip conditions:* codeath becomes **suspicious** if it is shown to hide an unrecorded dispatch or unconfirmed delivery. `Reopen -> Close` becomes **suspicious** if reopening reversed a recorded payment or write-off (currently unobservable). `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer` becomes a **data-protection finding** under a separate policy — never a finding under *this* norm.

## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch

```python
import pm4py
from dataclasses import dataclass, field
from typing import Any, Dict, Tuple

# =====================================================================
# NORM METADATA LIVES OUTSIDE PM4Py's GRAPH STRUCTURES.
# PM4Py's discover_/conformance_ outputs carry counts and edge presence
# only: they have no notion of mandatory / forbidden / typical / unspecified,
# no scope language, and no open-world semantics. The status+scope table
# (norm_meta below) must be maintained as an external artifact (YAML/CSV/
# service) with its own versioning and owner. NEVER encode status into the
# edge keys or overwrite the raw diagnostics with a verdict.
# =====================================================================

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class NormClause:
    nid: str
    family: str            # "ocdfg" | "otg" | "etot"
    signature: Tuple[Any, ...]
    status: str            # "mandatory" | "forbidden" | "typical" | "unspecified"
    scope: str             # free-text applicability condition
    closed_world: bool     # True only for forbidden + in-scope mandatory

norm_meta: Dict[str, NormClause] = load_norm_meta()   # external, versioned
assert all(
    c.closed_world == (c.status == "forbidden" or c.status == "mandatory")
    for c in norm_meta.values()
), "closed-world flag may only be set for forbidden and mandatory clauses"

# ---------- 1. RAW DISCOVERY (kept verbatim) -------------------------
ocdfg_obs = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)    # {(act1, act2): {otype: count}}
otg_obs   = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)      # {(t1, relationship, t2): weight}
etot_obs  = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)     # {(event_type, otype): weight}

raw_models = {"ocdfg": ocdfg_obs, "otg": otg_obs, "etot": etot_obs}

# ---------- 2. RAW PM4Py CONFORMANCE (preserved, not overwritten) ----
diag_ocdfg = pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(ocel, norm_ocdfg)
diag_otg   = pm4py.conformance_otg(ocel, norm_otg)
diag_etot  = pm4py.conformance_etot(ocel, norm_etot)

diagnostics = {"ocdfg": diag_ocdfg, "otg": diag_otg, "etot": diag_etot}
# `diagnostics` is write-protected output: it answers "does the observed
# graph contain / follow these edges?", nothing about legitimacy.

# ---------- 3. SEMANTIC CLASSIFICATION LAYER (additive) --------------
DQ_HINTS = {
    "E10": "join on reused numeric identifier",
    "T6":  "timestamp granularity / record merge",
    "E6":  "carrier reference in attribute instead of Delivery object",
    "N1":  "unrecorded intervening review event",
}

def classify(clause: NormClause, obs_weight: int, expected_weight: int,
             within_scope: bool) -> Dict[str, Any]:
    """Purely additive verdict; never mutates `diagnostics`."""
    out = {"nid": clause.nid, "status": clause.status, "scope": clause.scope,
           "observed": obs_weight, "expected": expected_weight,
           "data_quality_hint": DQ_HINTS.get(clause.nid)}

    if not within_scope:
        out.update(deviation_class="out_of_scope", severity="none",
                   confidence="high",
                   note="open-world: silence or excess is not a deviation")
        return out

    if clause.status == "forbidden":
        # closed-world: an observation is a violation regardless of cause
        out.update(deviation_class="forbidden_behavior",
                   severity="critical" if obs_weight else "none",
                   confidence="high",
                   note="root cause may still be data quality -> report both")
    elif clause.status == "mandatory":
        if obs_weight >= expected_weight:
            out.update(deviation_class="none", severity="none", confidence="high")
        else:
            out.update(deviation_class="missing_mandatory_behavior",
                       severity="high" if expected_weight - obs_weight > 2 else "medium",
                       confidence="medium",   # abstraction may explain the gap
                       note="test reachability, not just directly-follows adjacency")
    elif clause.status == "typical":
        deficit = max(0, expected_weight - obs_weight)
        out.update(deviation_class="typical_pattern_absence",
                   severity="informational" if deficit else "none",
                   confidence="high",
                   note="no tolerance band is defined in the norm; deficit is descriptive only")
    else:  # unspecified
        out.update(deviation_class="unexpected_but_unregulated",
                   severity="informational", confidence="high",
                   note="absence from the norm is NOT noncompliance")
    return out

findings = [classify(cl, w_obs, w_exp, in_scope)
            for cl, w_obs, w_exp, in_scope in compute_clause_weights(raw_models)]

# Cross-family consistency is a separate, non-PM4Py check:
cross_family = compare_abstractions(otg_obs, etot_obs)   # e.g. T2=200 vs E6=198
audit_trail  = {"raw_models": raw_models, "pm4py_diagnostics": diagnostics,
                "semantic_findings": findings, "cross_family": cross_family}
store(audit_trail)   # immutable evidence bundle for review and appeal
```

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 eventual vs. directly-follows.** Risk: orders with intervening review events are wrongly scored as control bypasses, generating false positives and eroding trust in conformance reporting. Clarification: "every created Order must enter `Validate Order` at least once within 5 business days," measured as reachability. **mandatory** (reachability, with time bound).
2. **N3 ordering semantics.** Risk: a backdated or late-recorded cancellation makes a lawful dispatch look forbidden, or a real post-cancel dispatch hides behind record-time ordering; goods cannot be recovered either way. Clarification: dispatch is forbidden after the **effective cancellation timestamp**, with a defined tie-break rule for same-timestamp cases and a 24-hour dispatch-interlock requirement. **mandatory** (plus the interlock as a **mandatory** control).
3. **T6 "same business event" undefined.** Risk: one cobirth at second granularity is sanctionable, while a deliberate millisecond merge is invisible. Clarification: define cobirth as the same activity instance and specify timestamp granularity + tolerance (e.g.  1 s and same event ID). **forbidden** (status retained, definition added).
4. **E8 unapplied-cash typing mechanism.** Risk: legitimate unapplied cash is permanently untypeable, so E8 is unenforceable in practice and every unapplied payment is a false deviation. Clarification: require attribute `payment_type  {applied, unapplied_cash}` and treat `unapplied_cash` as out of E8's scope. **mandatory** (the typing itself), with the exception restated as **explicitly unspecified** within scope.
5. **Write-off / credit-note event types ungoverned.** Risk: write-offs (4 observed) sit inside N5's narrative but nowhere in the ET–OT or OTG tables — a write-off with no Invoice link, or an actor approving their own write-off, is invisible. Clarification: `Write Off -> Invoice` link and an approval attribute. **mandatory**.
6. **No tolerance / frequency policy.** Risk: 98/100 and 198/200 cannot be adjudicated — is 98% compliance or 2 breaches? Different reviewers will disagree. Clarification: state 100% within scope with a formal exception/waiver process, or define an explicit residual-error SLA per clause. **mandatory**.
7. **Codeath and other lifecycle-coupling relations (7 observed).** Risk: lifecycle coupling that closes a Delivery without a confirmed receipt could hide under "codeath", or benign sharing of a confirmation event gets flagged as noise. Clarification: state codeath as benign coupling of closing confirmations. **explicitly unspecified** (with a monitoring note).
8. **`Reopen Order` downstream effects (N6, 1 observed).** Risk: reopening can re-invoice, reverse write-offs, or re-close with different amounts — revenue-recognition and audit-trail risk — while N6 stays silent. Clarification: reopening may not reverse a recorded payment or write-off, and must be logged with a reason and approver. **forbidden** (reversal) + **mandatory** (audit fields), leaving plain reopen **explicitly unspecified**.
9. **OTG `object_interaction` semantics vs. ET–OT linkage (200 vs. 198).** Risk: aggregate compliance reports hide event-level traceability gaps. Clarification: define both derivations formally and require a reconciliation check. **mandatory** (documentation + check).
10. **Telemetry event types other than the one forbidden edge.** Risk: `Sensor Heartbeat` linked to Order, Package, or Payment is not forbidden by E10 as written. Clarification: forbid linking any telemetry event type to any financial object. **forbidden**.
