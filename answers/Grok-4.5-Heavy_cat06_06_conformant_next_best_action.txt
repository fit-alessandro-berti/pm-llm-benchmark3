**3. Benefit and harm reasoning**

Deadline-related benefit is bounded (20–35 k€ avoided operational loss). Customer harm from false rejection or prolonged hold spans 15–80 k€. Regulatory/compliance harm from unlawful execution is highly asymmetric (plausibly 100–500 k€ plus non-monetary consequences: audit findings, sanctions exposure, license risk, reputational damage).  

Dominance argument: any action with material probability of nonconformant execution (A, B, or an invalid G) carries expected harm that swamps the entire operational-benefit range. Therefore numerical forecasts of lower SLA-breach probability cannot elevate A or B.  

For conformant paths the relevant comparison is:  
- P(timely execution and true-positive approval) × 20–35 k€  
versus  
- P(false reject/hold) × 15–80 k€  
plus spillover expected loss on P-901 (roughly 0.12–0.28 × comparable high-value stakes)  
plus residual uncertainty that observational approval probabilities (0.55–0.75) and SLA predictions (0.48–0.64) do not transport unchanged under intervention.  

Information-gathering (alternate screen) has positive option value by potentially reducing false rates without violating process. Pure rejection (F) eliminates regulatory tail risk but incurs high expected customer harm given the approval probability. Long evidence cycles (E) dominate the deadline-loss term. Exact monetization is inappropriate; ranges and first-order dominance suffice for ranking.
