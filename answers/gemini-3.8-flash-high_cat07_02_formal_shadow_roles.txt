# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

Organizational role discovery cannot rely on raw activity frequencies alone. A robust distinction requires cross-referencing activity volume with case complexity, structural substitution episodes, and organizational delegation of authority.

### Automated Intake & Digital Pre-Validation (R14_Nora)
- **Core Activities:** `A1 Register Request` (760 events; 55.5% of its volume), `A2 Validate Entitlement` (610 events; 44.5% of its volume).
- **Peripheral Activities:** None. Zero counts across the remaining 12 activities.
- **Activity Frequency & Concentration:** 100% of workload is confined to initial intake and pre-validation. Nora processes 51.5% of all incoming registrations across the entire service process.
- **Case Complexity:** Strictly low complexity (median 0.11; 690 Low, 85 Medium, 0 High, 0 Critical). The bot is programmed to drop or route cases above standard complexity thresholds to human agents.
- **Substitutions:** Nora does not substitute for human agents and cannot be substituted by human agents at equivalent scale or speed; it operates 24/7 without shift constraints.
- **Organizational Authority:** Purely automated, zero-discretion execution. Performs algorithmic entitlement matching; holds no authority to grant policy exceptions, configure solutions, or close requests.

### Front-Office General Service (Tier-1 Core: R01_Amira, R02_Ben)
- **Core Activities:** `A1 Register Request` (430 combined), `A2 Validate Entitlement` (805 combined), `A4 Provide Standard Response` (645 combined), `A12 Close Request` (540 combined).
- **Peripheral Activities:** `A3 Diagnose Issue` (75 for Amira, 60 for Ben), `A8 Customer Negotiation` (42 for Amira, 25 for Ben), `A5 Configure Solution` (30 for Amira, 18 for Ben), `A7 Pricing Exception Review` (18 for Amira, 5 for Ben), `A10 Implement Solution` (12 for Amira, 9 for Ben), `A13 Reopen Request` (8 for Amira, 4 for Ben).
- **Activity Frequency & Concentration:** Core activities account for 85.8% of Amira's workload (1,180 / 1,375) and 90.8% of Ben's workload (1,240 / 1,366).
- **Case Complexity:** Centered on Low to Medium complexity (Amira median 0.34; Ben median 0.29). Amira absorbs noticeably more Medium (225) and High (58) cases than Ben (205 Medium, 31 High), reflecting her tenure (4.2y vs 3.7y).
- **Substitutions:** Act as operational absorbers when temporary agents introduce errors. In Weeks 8–10, when seasonal agents created validation errors, Amira and Ben handled subsequent rework and case reopens.
- **Organizational Authority:** Certified in entitlement validation. Authorized to validate standard service contracts, provide binding standard responses, and formally close resolved customer requests. No sign-off authority for pricing exceptions or technical architectural modifications.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

Informal and bridging roles represent adaptations developed by an organization to bypass operational bottlenecks, bridge departmental silos, and absorb workload volatility. When analyzing event logs, these roles must be examined through both their positive buffering contributions and their associated organizational risks.

### 1. R07_Giorgio as a Commercial Shadow Role
- **Empirical Evidence & Profile:** Giorgio's formal title is Senior Service Agent within Front Office, yet he executed 72 `A7 Pricing Exception Review` and 88 `A8 Customer Negotiation` events over the 10-week window. In Weeks 3–4, during Fatima's 6-day absence, Giorgio absorbed the commercial desk, executing 31 A7 and 18 A8 events. His activity similarity with Fatima is 0.71, while his similarity with front-office peer Amira is 0.84.
- **Structural Mechanism:** Giorgio possesses dual certifications in entitlement and pricing. His 7.4 years of tenure provide deep institutional knowledge. Rather than transferring complex customer requests across departmental boundaries to Commercial, Giorgio handles commercial exceptions directly at the front-office perimeter (reflected in Sequence 4: `Validate → Giorgio:Negotiation → Hana:Approval`).
- **Operational Performance Impact:** During normal operations under Fatima, pricing exceptions have a median service time of 38 minutes. Under Giorgio's substitution in Weeks 3–4, median duration surged to 74 minutes (+94.7% increase). While Giorgio possesses the formal certification, pricing exceptions are not his primary day-to-day focus, introducing substantial friction.
- **Benefits:**
  - Prevents total process stagnation when Fatima is absent or backlogged.
  - Reduces customer friction by minimizing handoffs between front-office intake and commercial departments.
  - Provides tactical elasticity during seasonal commercial campaigns.
- **Risks:**
  - **Severe SLA Degradation:** Near doubling of cycle times for pricing reviews damages customer responsiveness.
  - **Margin Risk:** An informal substitute may lack real-time market pricing intelligence, leading to suboptimal concession structuring.
  - **Masking Structural Capacity Deficits:** By absorbing pricing overflow informally, Giorgio conceals the reality that Commercial is a single-point-of-failure team with only one dedicated specialist (Fatima).

### 2. R13_Mateo as a Cross-Functional Escalation Broker
- **Empirical Evidence & Profile:** Formally titled Incident Coordinator within the Coordination team (5.9y tenure, escalation certification). Mateo exhibits the highest activity entropy across the entire event log: he executes events across all 14 activities, ranging from 10 A7 events to 52 A12 events. His median case complexity is 0.77, handling 76 critical cases.
- **Structural Mechanism:** In the social network of the process, Mateo demonstrates dominant betweenness centrality. He is the structural bridge connecting front-office intake, technical configuration, risk review, and supervisory approval (Sequences 3 and 5). He acts as a router, triage manager, and expediter on reopens and complex failures.
- **Substitution & Intervention Episodes:**
  - *Week 5 (Audit Workshop Substitution):* Covered for Supervisor Hana (R08), executing 21 preliminary `A9 Supervisor Approval` events. Crucially, 13 of these cases (61.9%) were rechecked by Hana upon her return. This proves Mateo acts as an operational pre-filter rather than a legally empowered replacement.
  - *Weeks 7–8 (Enterprise Security Incident Surge):* Mateo actively filtered low-risk security referrals away from Erik (R05). While this filtering successfully reduced false escalation noise, it introduced a catastrophic failure: Mateo misclassified two critical security cases, causing them to sit in queue for over 2 days.
- **Benefits:**
  - Prevents high-cost specialists (Erik, Hana, Chen) from drowning in trivial or misrouted escalations.
  - Serves as the primary operational engine for recovering failed or reopened requests (Sequence 5: `Reopen → Mateo:Coordinate → Specialist → Approval/Close`).
  - Eliminates deadlocks between isolated departmental silos (Front Office vs Technical vs Commercial).
- **Risks:**
  - **Single Point of Failure (SPOF):** The entire coordination layer relies on a single human node. If Mateo is unavailable, cross-functional flow collapses.
  - **Filtering Blind Spots:** When a coordinator without specialized technical certifications filters referrals, critical risks are misclassified (e.g., the 2-day security delay).
  - **Authority Dilution:** Performing 44 A9 approvals blurs regulatory lines, necessitating expensive rework and rechecking.

### 3. R05_Erik as a Rare but Critical Specialist
- **Empirical Evidence & Profile:** Security Specialist in Risk (6.8y tenure, security certification). Total event footprint is only 170 events over 10 weeks, yet he performs 96 of the process's 148 `A6 Security Review` events (64.9%). Median case complexity is 0.86, with 55 critical cases and 82 high-complexity cases (82.5% of his volume).
- **Structural Mechanism:** Erik does not engage in routine process flow. # 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

The following Python script illustrates how to discover organizational roles using PM4Py, enrich the discovered roles with case complexity and substitution metrics, and construct guardrails to prevent misinterpreting raw frequency clusters as formal organizational authority.

```python
"""
Organizational Role Discovery & Boundary Analysis using PM4Py.
Combines activity profiles, social networks, case complexity, and substitution filters.
"""

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.algo.organizational_mining.roles import algorithm as roles_discovery
from pm4py.algo.organizational_mining.resource_similarity import algorithm as resource_similarity
from pm4py.algo.organizational_mining.sna import algorithm as sna
import networkx as nx


def analyze_organizational_roles(log_csv_path: str):
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 1. Load and Format DataFrame
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    raw_df = pd.read_csv(log_csv_path)

    # Ensure required PM4Py columns exist and types are correct
    raw_df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw_df["time:timestamp"])
    raw_df["case_complexity"] = pd.to_numeric(raw_df["case_complexity"], errors="coerce")
    raw_df["temporary_substitution"] = raw_df["temporary_substitution"].astype(bool)

    # Format dataframe using standard PM4Py utility
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        raw_df,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        resource_key="org:resource"
    )

    print(f"Loaded event log with {len(df)} events across {df['case:concept:name'].nunique()} cases.")

    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 2. Baseline PM4Py Role Discovery (Unweighted Activity Clustering)
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # Discover default roles using standard PM4Py algorithm
    discovered_roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(df)
    print("\n=== Standard PM4Py Discovered Roles ===")
    for idx, role in enumerate(discovered_roles):
        members = list(role.roles)
        activities = list(role.activities)
        print(f"Role {idx + 1}: Members: {members} | Core Activities: {activities}")

    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 3. Activity-Based Resource Similarity Matrix
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # Compute resource similarity matrix based on relative activity profiles
    similarity_matrix = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(df)
    print("\n=== Resource Activity-Based Cosine Similarity (Sample) ===")
    # Convert to DataFrame for readable inspection
    sim_df = pd.DataFrame(similarity_matrix)
    print(sim_df.round(2))

    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 4. Social Network Analysis (Handover & Working Together)
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # Handover of work network reveals directional execution flow and brokerage
    hw_values = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(df)
    
    # Build NetworkX directed graph to compute betweenness centrality (brokerage metric)
    G_hw = nx.DiGraph()
    for (u, v), weight in hw_values.items():
        if weight > 0:
            G_hw.add_edge(u, v, weight=weight)

    betweenness = nx.betweenness_centrality(G_hw, weight="weight")
    
    # Working together network shows co-occurrence within the same case
    wt_values = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(df)

    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 5. Join Role Discovery with Complexity and Substitution Metadata
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # Compute resource complexity profiles
    complexity_profile = df.groupby("org:resource").agg(
        total_events=("concept:name", "count"),
        median_complexity=("case_complexity", "median"),
        mean_complexity=("case_complexity", "mean"),
        critical_cases=("case_complexity", lambda x: (x >= 0.85).sum()),
        substitution_events=("temporary_substitution", "sum")
    ).reset_index()

    # Add network betweenness centrality
    complexity_profile["brokerage_centrality"] = complexity_profile["org:resource"].map(betweenness).fillna(0.0)

    print("\n=== Resource Multi-Dimensional Behavioral Profile ===")
    print(complexity_profile.sort_values(by="median_complexity", ascending=False).to_string(index=False))

    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 6. Guardrails: Filtering Substitutions & Detecting Authority Discrepancies
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    print("\n=== Guardrail Checks: Preventing Misclassification ===")

    # A. Baseline vs Substitution Filtering: Compare Giorgio (R07) with/without substitutions
    df_clean = df[~df["temporary_substitution"]]
    roles_clean = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(df_clean)
    print(f"Discovered roles after removing temporary substitutions: {len(roles_clean)} roles.")

    # Check pricing exception service time during normal ops vs substitution
    pricing_cases = df[df["concept:name"].isin(["A7. Pricing Exception Review", "A8. Customer Negotiation"])]
    pricing_perf = pricing_cases.groupby(["org:resource", "temporary_substitution"]).agg(
        event_count=("concept:name", "count"),
        avg_complexity=("case_complexity", "mean")
    ).reset_index()
    print("\nPricing Activity Execution under Normal vs Substitution:")
    print(pricing_perf.to_string(index=False))

    # B. Authority Audit: Recheck loops on Supervisor Approvals (A9)
    # Detect instances where A9 was followed by another A9 in the same case (indicates pre-check)
    df_sorted = df.sort_values(by=["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])
    df_sorted["next_activity"] = df_sorted.groupby("case:concept:name")["concept:name"].shift(-1)
    df_sorted["next_resource"] = df_sorted.groupby("case:concept:name")["org:resource"].shift(-1)

    approval_rechecks = df_sorted[
        (df_sorted["concept:name"] == "A9. Supervisor Approval") &
        (df_sorted["next_activity"] == "A9. Supervisor Approval")
    ]
    
    print("\nDetected Supervisor Approval Recheck Loops (Pre-checks):")
    if not approval_rechecks.empty:
        recheck_summary = approval_rechecks.groupby(["org:resource", "next_resource"]).size().reset_index(name="recheck_count")
        print(recheck_summary.to_string(index=False))
        print("ALERT: Resources appearing in 'org:resource' followed by a supervisor recheck hold operational screening roles, NOT formal sign-off authority.")
    else:
        print("No direct sequential A9->A9 loops detected in the sample.")

    # C. Downstream Rework Audit: Track closures leading to reopens (A12 -> A13)
    closure_reopens = df_sorted[
        (df_sorted["concept:name"] == "A12. Close Request") &
        (df_sorted["next_activity"] == "A13. Reopen Request")
    ]
    print(f"\nTotal premature closures followed by reopen: {len(closure_reopens)}")
    if not closure_reopens.empty:
        print("Premature closures by resource:")
        print(closure_reopens["org:resource"].value_counts())

    return {
        "roles": discovered_roles,
        "profiles": complexity_profile,
        "betweenness": betweenness
    }


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # Example execution (path provided as illustrative)
    # analyze_organizational_roles("event_log_order_to_resolution.csv")
    pass
```

### Explanatory Commentary on Code Logic
1. **Multi-Attribute Enrichment:** Naïve role mining relies exclusively on `pm4py.discover_organizational_roles` (which performs agglomerative clustering on normalized activity frequencies). The script rectifies this by joining activity profiles with case complexity distributions (`median_complexity`, `critical_cases`) and network centrality (`brokerage_centrality`).
2. **Centrality vs Frequency:** By constructing a directed handover graph (`pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network`) and calculating NetworkX betweenness centrality, the code formally distinguishes high-dispersion brokers (Mateo: high betweenness, high complexity) from transactional frontline generalists (Amira/Ben: low betweenness, moderate complexity).
3. **Filtering Masked Substitutions:** The script isolates `temporary_substitution == False` to discover baseline operational roles, preventing emergency coverage (like Giorgio in Weeks 3–4 or Mateo in Week 5) from permanently distorting formal role clusters.
4. **Authority and Quality Guardrails:** The code explicitly scans for sequential pattern anomalies:
   - `A9 → A9` loops identify non-authoritative approvals that require managerial ratification.
   - `A12 → A13` loops identify premature closures and trace rework back to unqualified frontline staff.

Every reopened case required expensive coordination from Mateo (median complexity 0.77) and rework from Amira and Ben. Because his time is fragmented across hundreds of micro-interactions, he is vulnerable to cognitive overload. - **Mandatory Entitlement Certification for Front-Office Staff:**

  - If Mateo is to act as an assistant supervisor, management must formally establish a tiered DOA where Mateo is legally authorized to sign approvals below a defined financial/risk threshold (e.g., complexity < 0.50).
  - Approvals above that threshold must be routed to a designated peer supervisor, rather than relying on retroactive rechecking after cases have already advanced.
- **Automated Failsafes on Security Escalations:**
  To prevent multi-day backlogs like the Weeks 7–8 incident, any case flagged with `product_line="Enterprise Security"` or complexity > 0.80 must automatically bypass intermediary human triage and route directly to Erik's queue or trigger an immediate SLA breach alert after 4 hours of inactivity.

- **Transition from Informal Shadowing to Formal Redundancy:**
  The organization currently relies on informal shadow roles (Giorgio shadowing Fatima; Mateo shadowing Hana) to survive absences. This creates extreme fragility:
  - If Fatima and Giorgio are simultaneously unavailable, the commercial review capability completely halts.
  - If Hana is absent, formal approvals enter legal limbo.
  Management must establish formal primary and secondary cross-functional backup charters with defined SLA standards, system access privileges, and workload backfill plans.

### 5. Cautions Against Changing Staffing Based Solely on Mined Roles

Management must heed the following specific cautions before making restructuring, re-banding, or staffing decisions based on process-mined organizational models:

> #### Caution 1: The "Activity Volume Fallacy" (Mistaking Low Frequency for Low Value)
> A naïve analysis of the event log might conclude that Erik (R05_Erik) is heavily underutilized because he executed only 170 events over 10 weeks (17 events/week), compared to front-office agents who execute 1,300+ events. Downsizing or consolidating Erik's role into general technical support would be catastrophic: Erik handles a median complexity of 0.86 and accounts for 64.9% of all enterprise security reviews. In specialized, highly regulated processes, **event count does not correlate with business impact or risk exposure**. Eliminating or diluting low-volume, high-complexity specialist roles destroys the organization's defensive risk barrier.

> #### Caution 2: The "Shadow Competence Fallacy" (The Substitution Cost Illusion)
> Observing that Giorgio (R07) performed 31 pricing reviews and 18 customer negotiations during Fatima's absence might lead an executive to conclude: *"We do not need a dedicated Commercial Specialist; Front Office can handle commercial exceptions."* This conclusion ignores the **hidden operational cost**: Giorgio's cycle time was double that of Fatima (74m vs 38m), and diverting him degraded front-office throughput. Mined substitution events prove that a resource *stepped in under emergency conditions*; they do **not** prove that the resource possesses equivalent efficiency, strategic domain depth, or long-term competence.

> #### Caution 3: The "Homogeneous Cluster Fallacy" (Overlooking Structural Defect Rates)
> Agglomerative and cosine-based clustering algorithms assign seasonal temporary agents (R11, R12) and permanent core service agents (R01, R02) to the exact same cluster due to their identical relative activity proportions (>0.91 similarity across A1, A2, A4, A12). Restructuring frontline operations under the assumption that these workers are interchangeable commodities ignores that the uncertified temporary workforce caused 17 downstream case reopens. Naïve clustering captures **what activity was performed**, but remains completely blind to **how well, under what authority, and with what downstream quality** it was completed. Staffing decisions must never decouple activity profiles from complexity, certifications, and downstream defect metrics.
