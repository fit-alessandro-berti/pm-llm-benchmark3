## 1. Conformance semantics

Because the norm is only closed-world for relations marked **forbidden** and for **mandatory** columns within their stated scope, and open-world everywhere else, I use seven distinct classifications. Getting these apart is the whole point: absence from the norm is not evidence of a violation.

- **Forbidden behavior.** An observation matches a relation whose status is `forbidden` (N3, T6, E10). This is the only case where *presence* of behavior is itself a violation, because the closed-world assumption over forbidden relations means "we said this must never happen." Even here, presence must be established as *genuine* before it is called a violation, because a forbidden edge produced by a bad join is a data-quality artifact, not a business breach.

- **Missing mandatory behavior.** A relation marked `mandatory` is *absent* for an object that falls inside the relation's stated scope (N1, N4, T1, T2, E1–E8). The closed-world guarantee applies to the mandatory *column* within scope, so a genuine in-scope absence is a real gap. Two qualifiers dominate: (a) does the object actually fall in scope, and (b) is the "absence" real or an artifact of the abstraction used to summarize the log?

- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior.** An observation exists that the norm never mentions and never forbids (Validate Order -> Issue Invoice, Manual Compliance Review -> Customer, Package object_codeath Delivery, Reopen Order -> Close Order, Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review). Under the open-world assumption these are **not violations**. They may still be *interesting* (worth documenting or later governing), but the correct default is "permitted."

- **Typical-pattern absence.** A relation marked `typical` (N2, N5, T3) is absent for some objects. Typical means "common and desirable, exceptions may be legitimate." Absence lowers a soft expectation; it is a candidate for review, never a violation. Severity is calibrated by whether a plausible legitimate exception exists (credit note, write-off, open credit terms, unapplied cash).

- **Likely data-quality error.** An observation is best explained by a logging/integration defect rather than by real-world behavior (Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice from a reused numeric identifier; possibly the cobirth edge; possibly the two missing Delivery relations that carry a carrier reference). The tell is a mechanism that produces the edge without any corresponding business act.

- **Abstraction mismatch.** The observed summary uses an operator (directly-follows, cobirth, codeath) that does not faithfully represent the underlying *eventually*-style rule (N1 especially). The "deviation" is an artifact of the summarization, not of the process. This is the most dangerous category to misread, discussed at length in §3.

- **Insufficient evidence.** The graph summary cannot settle the classification without an attribute, a label, or trace-level replay (the single unlabeled unapplied-cash Payment; whether the two N1-missing Orders truly skipped validation). The honest output is "undecidable at this abstraction."

**Open- vs closed-world impact.** For forbidden and in-scope-mandatory relations I reason *closed-world*: silence in the observed data about a required edge is meaningful (a gap), and presence of a forbidden edge is meaningful (a breach). For everything else I reason *open-world*: silence means "not covered," so I must not manufacture violations from unmodeled or merely-typical relations. The constraints in the task enforce exactly this discipline.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

**Can establish.** Ordering/flow relations *per object type*: how often one activity directly follows another for Orders, Packages, Invoices. It gives the 98/100 for N1, the 198/200 for N4, the 83 for N2, the 92/110 for N5, plus ungoverned flows.

**Cannot establish.** Whether a "directly-follows" gap reflects a real skip or an intervening, unmodeled event. It cannot see cross-object causality, attributes (carrier reference), or whether an Order is *in scope* (standard vs non-standard). It cannot prove intent behind the Cancel -> Dispatch edge (direction/timing).

**Counts and scope.** N4 at 198/200 is scoped to "packages intended for external shipment"; if the 2 exceptions are not external-shipment packages, there is no gap at all. N1 at 98/100 must be read as "of *newly created standard* Orders." Counts are denominators for severity but the norm sets no tolerance band (see §6), so 98/100 is not automatically "acceptable"—it is 2 in-scope candidates to investigate, not 2 confirmed violations.

**Conforming here, suspicious elsewhere.** Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package (N3) is an OC-DFG edge that looks like an ordinary flow at count level, but semantically it is a forbidden edge—suspicious precisely because the norm's closed-world clause elevates it. Conversely Validate Order -> Issue Invoice is a busy, healthy OC-DFG edge and is *not* a violation despite being absent from the norm.

**The "eventually" vs "directly-follows" danger.** N1 says every newly created standard Order should *eventually* enter validation; the summary "approximates this with a directly-follows expectation." A directly-follows edge is broken by *any* intervening event (e.g., an unrecorded review), even when the eventual rule holds perfectly. Treating the 2 missing edges as N1 violations therefore risks a **false positive born entirely of abstraction**. The context note that these 2 Orders "may have an unrecorded review event between creation and validation" is exactly this trap. The right move is to replay eventually-follows at trace level before calling a violation.

### OTG (object-type graph)

**Can establish.** Which object types interact and with what weight: Order–Item (1450), Package–Delivery (200), Invoice–Payment (92), plus the cobirth/codeath structural relations.

**Cannot establish.** *Which* individual interactions are missing or spurious, or *why*. A single anomalous edge (cobirth weight 1) is visible but its cause (real event vs bad join) is invisible at type level.

**Counts and scope.** T1 (Order–Item) at 1450 aligns with Add Item -> Item (1450), so the interaction backbone is healthy. T2 (Package–Delivery) at 200 *matches Dispatch Package count 200*, which is interesting: the OTG type-level edge shows 200 even though the ET–OT Dispatch Package -> Delivery is 198—evidence that the 2 missing Deliveries may be a *linking* gap, not a truly absent Delivery object (consistent with the carrier reference). T6 (cobirth Order–Payment) is forbidden; weight 1 is a single candidate breach, but low confidence as a real breach.

**Conforming here, suspicious elsewhere.** The codeath Package–Delivery edge (7) is a perfectly ordinary OTG structural relation and is explicitly ungoverned—benign at every abstraction. But the *cobirth* edge, structurally similar, is forbidden and thus suspicious. Same graph family, opposite verdicts, driven purely by norm status.

### ET–OT (event-type to object-type)

**Can establish.** Whether each event type carries the mandated object links: Create Order->Order 100 (E1 fully met), Add Item->Item 1450 (E3 met), Add Item->Order 1448 (E2 short by 2), Pack/Dispatch->Package 200 (E4/E5 met), Dispatch->Delivery 198 (E6 short by 2), Record Payment->Payment 93 and ->Invoice 92 (E7/E8), and the forbidden Sensor Heartbeat->Invoice 1 (E10).

**Cannot establish.** Whether a missing link is a real absence or a dropped association, and whether the one Invoice-less payment is genuine unapplied cash (E8's own exception clause) or an error. It cannot read the "not labeled as such" nuance—that requires an attribute.

**Counts and scope.** E8 explicitly excludes events typed as unapplied cash, and states "none are so typed in this snapshot." So by the letter of E8 the single Payment-without-Invoice *is* an in-scope mandatory gap—yet finance says unapplied cash is possible. This is the cleanest example of **insufficient evidence**: the norm's exception exists but the *label* is missing. The correct action is to obtain/confirm the label, not to record a violation.

**Conforming here, suspicious elsewhere.** Record Payment->Payment (93) is conforming and healthy at ET–OT level; the *derived* mismatch with Record Payment->Invoice (92) is what surfaces the one anomalous event. And Sensor Heartbeat->Invoice is a single ET–OT edge that is forbidden (E10) yet almost certainly a join artifact—suspicious as a rule but benign as reality.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

**Mandatory (in-scope gaps to resolve).**
- N1: 2/100 Orders missing Create->Validate. *Condition:* reclassifies to abstraction mismatch (benign) if an intervening event exists and eventually-validation holds; stays a gap only if the Order truly never validates and is standard/in-scope.
- N4/E6/T2: 2/200 Dispatch Package lacking Delivery. *Condition:* reclassifies to data-quality linking gap if the carrier reference resolves to an existing Delivery; a true gap only if the package is external-shipment scope and no Delivery exists.
- E2: 2 Add Item events lacking an Order link (1448 vs 1450). *Condition:* data-quality if the association was dropped.
- E8: 1 Record Payment lacking Invoice. *Condition:* becomes a legitimate typical exception once labeled unapplied cash; otherwise an in-scope gap.
- (E1, E3, E4, E5, E7, T1 are fully satisfied and require no action.)

**Forbidden (closed-world; presence is the concern).**
- N3: Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package (2). *Condition:* remains a genuine breach unless timestamps show dispatch preceded cancellation (then a direction/data error).
- T6: Order object_cobirth Payment (1). *Condition:* genuine breach only if a real single business event births both; otherwise data-quality.
- E10: Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice (1). *Condition:* treated as data-quality error given the reused-identifier join; would be a serious control breach only if the link is real.

**Typical (soft; absence reviewed, not violated).**
- N2: Validate->Close present for 83 (intermediate events legitimately intervene).
- N5: Issue Invoice->Record Payment 92/110; the 4 write-offs and 14 open/no-successor invoices are legitimate or window-truncated exceptions.
- T3: Invoice–Payment interaction 92; unapplied cash tolerated.

**Unregulated / unspecified (open-world; document only).**
- Validate Order -> Issue Invoice (72), Manual Compliance Review -> Customer (3), Package object_codeath Delivery (7), Reopen Order -> Close Order (1), Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review (3), Order–Customer interaction (100, T4), Order–RiskCase (3, T5), Manual Compliance Review->RiskCase (3, E9). None are violations; per the constraints they must not be flagged merely for being absent from a normative edge set.

## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch

```python
import pm4py

# --- 1. Discover the observed models from the log (raw, untouched) -----------
disc_ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
disc_otg   = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
disc_etot  = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# --- 2. Run PM4Py conformance against the normative structures ----------------
#     norm_ocdfg : normative OC-DFG dictionary  (bare graph structure only)
#     norm_otg   : normative OTG tuple
#     norm_etot  : normative ET-OT tuple
# NOTE: these bare structures encode WHICH edges exist, NOT their status.
conf_ocdfg = pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(ocel, norm_ocdfg)
conf_otg   = pm4py.conformance_otg(ocel, norm_otg)
conf_etot  = pm4py.conformance_etot(ocel, norm_etot)

# Preserve raw diagnostics verbatim; never overwrite them.
raw_diagnostics = {
    "ocdfg": conf_ocdfg,
    "otg":   conf_otg,
    "etot":  conf_etot,
    "discovered": {"ocdfg": disc_ocdfg, "otg": disc_otg, "etot": disc_etot},
}

# --- 3. Semantic metadata lives OUTSIDE the graph structures ------------------
# PM4Py graphs cannot store mandatory/typical/forbidden/unspecified status,
# scope, or the "eventually vs directly-follows" caveat. Maintain it here.
NORM_STATUS = {
    "N1": ("mandatory",   "Create Order->Validate Order",  "standard orders; EVENTUALLY, not directly-follows"),
    "N2": ("typical",     "Validate Order->Close Order",   "intermediate events allowed"),
    "N3": ("forbidden",   "Cancel Order->Dispatch Package",""),
    "N4": ("mandatory",   "Pack Package->Dispatch Package","external-shipment packages only"),
    "N5": ("typical",     "Issue Invoice->Record Payment", "credit notes/write-offs/open terms ok"),
    "T2": ("mandatory",   "(Package,object_interaction,Delivery)", ""),
    "T6": ("forbidden",   "(Order,object_cobirth,Payment)",""),
    "E8": ("mandatory",   "Record Payment->Invoice",       "except events typed unapplied_cash"),
    "E10":("forbidden",   "Sensor Heartbeat->Invoice",     ""),
    # ... plus every remaining ID, including 'unspecified' ones kept explicit
}

# --- 4. Separate semantic-classification layer (does NOT mutate raw output) ---
def classify(norm_id, observed_present, in_scope=True, evidence=None):
    status, _, _ = NORM_STATUS[norm_id]
    if status == "forbidden":
        return "FORBIDDEN_BREACH" if observed_present else "OK"
    if status == "mandatory":
        if not in_scope:
            return "OUT_OF_SCOPE"                 # scope gate before verdict
        if observed_present:
            return "OK"
        # mandatory gap: could be abstraction mismatch or missing evidence
        return "MANDATORY_GAP_REVIEW"
    if status == "typical":
        return "OK" if observed_present else "TYPICAL_ABSENCE_REVIEW"
    return "UNREGULATED"                          # open-world default

semantic_layer = {
    nid: classify(nid, observed_present=lookup_presence(raw_diagnostics, nid))
    for nid in NORM_STATUS
}

# Data-quality and abstraction caveats are attached, never folded into raw_diagnostics:
semantic_layer_notes = {
    "E10": "reused-identifier join -> suspected data-quality artifact",
    "T6":  "single cobirth; verify real business event before treating as breach",
    "N1":  "replay EVENTUALLY-follows at trace level before calling a violation",
    "T2":  "carrier reference in event attribute -> possible linking gap not true absence",
    "E8":  "confirm unapplied_cash label before treating as mandatory gap",
}

result = {"raw": raw_diagnostics, "semantic": semantic_layer, "notes": semantic_layer_notes}
```

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **No frequency/tolerance bands.** The norm sets no minimum coverage, yet mandatory edges show 98/100, 198/200. *Risk:* every isolated exception forces manual triage; no basis to say "acceptable." *Clarification:* define a tolerance or an exception-handling workflow for mandatory edges. *Status:* **typical** (a soft band, since true mandatory items should still be individually reconciled).

2. **"Directly-follows" vs "eventually" for N1.** The summary approximates an eventual rule with a DFG edge. *Risk:* systematic false positives whenever benign intermediate events occur. *Clarification:* state that N1 is satisfied by eventually-follows and must be replayed at trace level. *Status:* **mandatory** clarification of semantics.

3. **Scope of "external shipment" (N4/T2/E6).** No rule defines which packages are external-shipment. *Risk:* wrongly counting internal/returns packages as mandatory-Delivery gaps. *Clarification:* define an attribute that marks external-shipment scope. *Status:* **mandatory**.

4. **Unapplied-cash labeling (E8/T3).** E8 excepts unapplied cash but nothing mandates the label; finance says it happens unlabeled. *Risk:* legitimate exceptions look like mandatory gaps, or real gaps hide behind "unapplied cash." *Clarification:* require an explicit `unapplied_cash` flag on such Record Payment events. *Status:* **mandatory**.

5. **Cobirth/codeath governance (T6 vs codeath).** Cobirth is forbidden but codeath is entirely unmentioned. *Risk:* the 7 Package–Delivery codeath edges get debated forever with no rule. *Clarification:* explicitly state codeath is outside scope (or govern it). *Status:* **explicitly unspecified** (make the silence intentional and documented).

6. **Direction/temporal semantics of forbidden flows (N3).** N3 forbids dispatch *after* cancellation, but a DFG edge does not by itself prove temporal order or same-object identity robustly. *Risk:* a timing/direction artifact is escalated as a shipping breach, or a real breach is missed. *Clarification:* define N3 in terms of per-Order event timestamps, not just a directly-follows edge. *Status:* **forbidden** (keep the prohibition, sharpen its detection).

7. **Reopen lifecycle (N6).** Marked unspecified, yet reopening interacts with N2's Close expectation. *Risk:* ambiguity about whether reopened Orders must re-validate/re-close. *Clarification:* state whether N1/N2 re-apply after reopen. *Status:* **explicitly unspecified** now, with a note to revisit if reopen volume grows.

8. **Multi-object interactions of review events (E9).** Manual Compliance Review touches both RiskCase and Customer, but only RiskCase is mentioned. *Risk:* the Customer link is debated as a violation (it is not). *Clarification:* declare Review–Customer explicitly unspecified. *Status:* **explicitly unspecified**.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Prioritized on the two highest-severity findings:** Rank 1 (N3, Cancel->Dispatch, real forbidden behavior) and Rank 2 (E10, Sensor Heartbeat->Invoice, forbidden edge suspected data-quality). Benign rare behavior (codeath, review->Customer, reopen->close, write-offs) is *not* escalated merely for rarity.

**Immediate containment.**
- N3 (rank 1): place a hold/recall on the 2 dispatched packages tied to cancelled Orders; freeze those 2 Orders; verify per-Order timestamps to confirm dispatch truly followed cancellation.
- E10 (rank 2): quarantine the Sensor Heartbeat->Invoice edge so no downstream process treats a sensor stream as financially linked; do not act on it as a real link pending provenance.

**Data repair.**
- E10: trace the reused numeric identifier join and correct the mapping so heartbeats cannot bind to Invoices.
- T2/E6: resolve the carrier references on the 2 Dispatch events to existing Deliveries and re-link, or create the missing Delivery objects.
- E2: reconcile the 2 Add Item events missing an Order link.
- E8: apply the `unapplied_cash` label to the 1 qualifying Payment once finance confirms.
- T6: inspect the single cobirth event; if spurious, correct the object-creation semantics.

**Process investigation.**
- N3: root-cause why dispatch occurred post-cancellation (system race, manual override, missing cancellation propagation to the warehouse).
- N1: replay eventually-follows for the 2 Orders; confirm whether an intervening review event explains the missing directly-follows edge (abstraction, not violation).
- N5: age the 14 open Invoices; distinguish window-truncation from genuinely stalled receivables. Treat the 4 write-offs as documented, legitimate exceptions.

**Norm revision.** Implement §6 clarifications: define external-shipment scope (N4), re-express N1 as eventually-follows, mandate the unapplied-cash label (E8), declare codeath/reopen/review-Customer explicitly unspecified, and define N3 temporally.

**Monitoring.** Stand up recurring checks: any N3/T6/E10 forbidden edge alerts immediately; mandatory-edge coverage tracked with the (to-be-defined) tolerance; open-invoice aging as a KPI rather than a violation; a data-quality monitor on identifier-join integrity to catch future E10-style artifacts.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

**Robust conclusions.** The forbidden statuses are closed-world, so the *existence* of Cancel Order->Dispatch Package (N3), the Sensor Heartbeat->Invoice link (E10), and the Order–Payment cobirth (T6) are genuine flags worth acting on—independent of counts. The fully-satisfied mandatory edges (E1, E3, E4, E5, E7, T1) are conforming with high confidence. The explicitly ungoverned observations (Validate->Issue Invoice, Review->Customer, codeath, Reopen->Close, review interactions) are **not** violations; this is robust because it follows directly from the open-world rule and the task constraints.

**Depends on the norm's incompleteness.** Whether the 2 missing-Delivery dispatches and the 2 missing N1 edges are truly gaps hinges on undefined scope ("external shipment," "standard Order") and on N1 being an eventually rule mis-summarized as directly-follows. The 14 open invoices and 92/110 payment rate depend on the absence of tolerance bands and on the extract's cut-off.

**Depends on suspected data-quality issues.** The E10 heartbeat link is very likely a reused-identifier join artifact; the T6 cobirth (weight 1) and the 2 unlinked Add Item / 2 unlinked Delivery events are plausibly association defects rather than real behavior. Their final classification flips between "breach" and "artifact" once provenance is checked.

**Cannot be decided from graph summaries alone.** Whether the single Invoice-less Payment is legitimate unapplied cash (E8's own exception) versus an unmapped payment; the true temporal ordering behind the N3 edges; and whether the N1 Orders eventually validate. These require trace-level replay, event attributes, or finance confirmation. PM4Py diagnostics locate the anomalies but do not, by themselves, constitute a complete legal or business interpretation.
