**Retrospective-justification conformance**

`discover_declare` and `discover_log_skeleton` can learn common constraints but cannot, by themselves, reliably express “within three working days by a different human.” Use them for structural screening and add an explicit Python rule:

```python
model = pm4py.discover_declare(df)
declare_conf = pm4py.conformance_declare(df, model)

skeleton = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(df)
skeleton_conf = pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton(df, skeleton)

def check_emergency_case(g, holidays=set()):
    g = g.sort_values("time:timestamp")
    overrides = g[g["concept:name"] == "Emergency Override"]
    justifs = g[g["concept:name"] == "Retrospective Justification"]
    findings = []

    for _, o in overrides.iterrows():
        later = justifs[justifs["time:timestamp"] >= o["time:timestamp"]]
        # Replace with a holiday-aware business-day calculation.
        valid = later[
            later["time:timestamp"].map(
                lambda t: len(pd.bdate_range(
                    o["time:timestamp"].date(), t.date()
                )) - 1 <= 3
            )
            & (later["org:resource"] != o["org:resource"])
        ]
        findings.append({
            "override_time": o["time:timestamp"],
            "compliant": not valid.empty
        })
    return findings
```

Repeat the actor comparison using authenticated IDs, not just `org:resource`, and retain separate findings for late, missing, and non-independent justification.

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

**Evidence that would increase concern**

- Raw authentication records showing the same human account, device, session, or IP performed both bank maintenance and payment approval.
- `entered_by`, `approved_by`, or immutable ERP audit tables confirming dual control rather than a display-name mapping error.
- Bank accounts changed to an employee-related, unrelated, newly created, or otherwise unexplained beneficiary.
- Payments released shortly after changes without call-back verification, bank confirmation, sanctions screening, or payment hold.
- V-77x vendors absent from the approved platform-migration population or changes outside the ticket’s authorized dates and fields.
- Reused bank accounts across apparently unrelated vendors, regions, or tax identifiers.
- Approval timestamps preceding vendor checks, quote receipt, bank verification, or creation of emergency support.
- Emergency flags or justifications added only after approval or after audit extraction.
- Repeated identical justification text, document metadata identifying the original approver as author, or missing independent signatures.
- Gita shared-account logs showing credential sharing without delegation, or assistants approving beyond authority.
- High-risk cases continuing after mapping defects and control gaps were corrected.
- Unexplained overrides of approval limits, vendor blocks, duplicate-payment warnings, or bot-match exceptions.

**Evidence that would reduce concern**

- A formally approved V-77x migration ticket listing every affected vendor, old/new bank details, dates, authorized makers/checkers, and reconciliation results.
- Independent bank verification completed before payment approval, with recorded call-back number and verifier.
- Raw system IDs demonstrating distinct humans despite the same `org:resource` label.
- Bot_PO logs and corrected legacy mappings eliminating apparent PO creator/approver identity matches.
- Contemporaneous emergency requests, incident records, on-call rosters, and documented delegated authority.
- Independent retrospective justifications authored within three working days, even if uploaded later in a batch.
- Field-level histories showing loops were genuine corrections and no payment-relevant bank data changed after approval without reverification.
- Statistical analysis showing fast approvals are explained by documented emergency/category routing and do not correlate with vendor, bank account, or exception outcomes.
- Access reviews showing least-privilege permissions and no user capable of both bank maintenance and payment release.
- Complete reconciliation confirming that all resulting goods/services were received and payments went to the vendors’ independently verified accounts.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

The event-log review identified control exceptions and concentrated processing patterns that warrant targeted follow-up, but the available evidence does not establish fraud or collusion. The overall pattern appears more consistent with a combination of emergency handling, informal specialization, control-design or execution gaps, and data-quality limitations.

The most concerning subgroup is the VendorMaster_Lena/AP_Kim cluster involving vendor family V-77x. Nine cases are recorded as violating the bank-change/payment-approval segregation rule, and 16 bank-change-to-approval paths occurred in a median of 17 minutes, compared with 7.8 hours overall. This cluster also has high-value and vendor concentration and is predominantly associated with `manual_upload`. These features increase control risk; however, V-77x was subject to a banking-platform migration, and missing links between maintenance tickets and procurement cases prevent a definitive interpretation.

Fast Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo approvals are substantially associated with medical-supply and weekend-repair emergencies. This supports a legitimate rush-processing explanation, although late or same-resource retrospective justifications indicate exceptions to the required compensating control. Routine Aline/Elena and Boris/Felix collaboration does not appear unusual solely because of its frequency.

Data limitations materially affect attribution. Shared accounts, quarter-close spreadsheet migration, and a known `legacy_erp` bot-to-human mapping error may create apparent same-person actions or unreliable timestamps. Audit should first review the V-77x bank-change cases, then emergency cases lacking timely independent justification. Follow-up should reconcile raw user identities, access logs, bank-verification records, migration tickets, delegation records, and original justification documents before drawing conclusions about individual conduct.
