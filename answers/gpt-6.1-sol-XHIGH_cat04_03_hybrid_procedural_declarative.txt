# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

The hybrid model is the intersection of an accepting Petri net, authoritative declarative and count constraints, and explicit external checks. Temporal-profile deviations add warnings. Passing one layer does not compensate for failing another.

R5 and R6 conflict for a case with an outstanding sanctions opinion: Legal forbids activation before the opinion, whereas Operations permits it with external joint authorization. A later opinion does not retroactively satisfy R5.

Two possible resolutions are:

1. **Legal-first policy, chosen for implementation.** R5 governs every activation. An outstanding sanctions escalation blocks activation regardless of emergency authorization. R6 is a proposed exception that has not been adopted. This avoids activating while legal review is outstanding, but can delay emergency onboarding and reject cases Operations considers legitimate.
2. **Formally adopted emergency exception.** Amend R5 to permit activation while an opinion is outstanding only with verified joint authorization, obtained before activation, and require the subsequent opinion within 24 hours of activation. This adds exposure during the review interval and depends on auditable authorization evidence. Missing evidence makes the exception unverified; timing alone cannot establish authorization.

A third operational option would be to hold affected cases for policy adjudication until the departments agree. Silently enforcing both statements as unconditional rules is not a resolution.

Material assumptions:

- The eight traces are completed cases. All visible events are completion events, with a reliable total order. Production records need a stable event sequence when timestamps tie, and timezone-aware timestamps for elapsed-time checks.
- “Final outcome” in R3 means either **Activate Vendor** or **Reject Vendor**. Clarification cycles must finish before either. Each response answers one outstanding request; unsolicited or duplicate responses are excluded. This one-to-one interpretation strengthens the wording slightly and supports count equivalence.
- Accepted means activated; rejected means rejected. The procedural choice implements exactly one disposition. An accepted completed case must reach first payment and then closure.
- No risk-class value is supplied. The low/high route can be checked structurally, but its consistency with the vendor's actual classification cannot be verified.
- Escalations and clarification events are allowed after receipt and before the disposition, including during checks or between approvals and activation. Escalations are additionally subject to R7.
- Every escalation creates an opinion obligation. The first later legal opinion discharges all currently outstanding escalations. A rejected case still owes its outstanding opinion before closure: rejection does not cancel R4. Additional opinions are allowed after a prior escalation.
- Post-activation bank changes, suspension, and repeat bank verification may occur before or after first payment, up to closure. The model excludes pre-activation bank changes and suspension because their workflow is unspecified. Initial bank verification and later re-verification have the same visible label.
- Suspension does not imply another activation or a second payment. R11 requires eventual suspension and verification before a later payment; it does not explicitly require suspension before that payment or specify a resumption activity.
- R9 compares all bank-verification resources, including later re-verifications, with the applicable approval resource. It also compares all master-data capture resources with activation resources. Resource identifiers must refer to people consistently.
- A verification or suspension after multiple changes can discharge all changes preceding it. A verification preceding a change cannot discharge that change.
- Under the exception policy, the 24-hour deadline runs from activation, inclusive. “Joint authorization” means verified authorization by the required independent parties; the policy owner must specify those parties. A timestamp, an approval activity, or a resource name cannot substitute for that evidence.
- All hard rules concern completed cases. Outstanding eventual obligations in a running case are pending; a forbidden outcome with an outstanding prerequisite is an immediate violation.
- Every encoded DECLARE rule has confidence 1.0 because these are authored rules. Support and confidence are metadata, not a legal basis for enforcement.

# 2. Requirement-to-Formalism Mapping

For DECLARE, a pair `(A, B)` uses conventional source/target order. For the Log Skeleton, `always_before[(A, B)]` means **every A has an earlier B**; `always_after[(A, B)]` means **every A has a later B**.

| Requirement | Primary formalism | Exact encoding | Why this formalism | Redundancy or limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | DECLARE, authoritative | `noncoexistence[(Reject Vendor, Activate Vendor)]` | Forbids both labels anywhere in one case. | The net's exclusive disposition and skeleton's `never_together` duplicate it. |
| R2 | Procedural net, authoritative | From classification choose rejection, Manager Approval, or Enhanced Due Diligence → Compliance Approval. Only the two approval paths enable activation. Each path can be traversed once. | Expresses the conditional, exclusive route choice directly. | DECLARE excludes the two approvals together and either approval with rejection; skeleton limits each approval to counts {0,1}. Neither supplement alone requires an accepted case to have one of the approvals. |
| R3 | DECLARE, authoritative | `altresponse[(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)]`, `altprecedence` on the same pair, and `nonsuccession[(Activate Vendor, Receive Clarification)]` / `[(Reject Vendor, Receive Clarification)]`. | Alternate response requires a reply before another request; banning replies after a disposition forces it to precede that disposition. | Alternate precedence adds the stated one-to-one assumption. Net restricts clarification events to the pre-outcome phases; skeleton checks equal counts and weaker ordering. |
| R4 | DECLARE, authoritative | `response[(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)]` | Every escalation needs some strictly later opinion, even in a rejected case. | Skeleton `always_after[(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)]` duplicates it. An opinion before an escalation does not satisfy it. |
| R5 | External order monitor, authoritative | At every activation, no earlier escalation may remain without an intervening later legal opinion. | Tracks a conditional outstanding obligation exactly without requiring an opinion in ordinary cases. | Unconditional `precedence[(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)]` would incorrectly require an opinion in every accepted case. R4 plus `nonsuccession[(Activate Vendor, Obtain Legal Opinion)]` is a conservative alternative, but also bans harmless additional later opinions; it is not used. |
| R6 | External authorization/time validator, authoritative if the exception is adopted | For activation with outstanding opinions: verify joint authorization before activation and a subsequent opinion with `0 ≤ opinion_time − activation_time ≤ 86400` seconds. | Authorization and the conditional deadline need evidence absent from activity labels. | Disabled as an exception under the chosen Legal-first policy. The log cannot establish joint authorization. |
| R7 | DECLARE, authoritative | `precedence[(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)]` | Each escalation requires a strictly earlier screening. | Skeleton `always_before[(Sanctions Escalation, Run Sanctions Screening)]` duplicates it. The net deliberately leaves this cross-cutting check to these layers. |
| R8 | DECLARE, authoritative | `noncoexistence[(Enhanced Due Diligence, Manager Approval)]` and `[(Compliance Approval, Manager Approval)]` | Case-wide exclusions tolerate intervening events. | Duplicated by route choice and skeleton exclusions. |
| R9 | External resource validator, authoritative | Bank-verifier resource set ∩ applicable-approver resource set = ∅; master-data resource set ∩ activation resource set = ∅. | Requires resource identities across occurrences. | Activity-only models cannot verify it. Missing relevant resource data gives “not evaluated,” unless a violation is already proved from known resources. |
| R10 | Log Skeleton, authoritative for explicit counts | `activ_freq` gives Activate Vendor, Reject Vendor, and Release First Payment allowed counts {0,1}. | Represents optional activities with a maximum count directly. | The net also traverses these transitions at most once. DECLARE `exactly_one` would incorrectly make optional activities mandatory. |
| R11 | External state monitor, authoritative | A post-activation change sets suspension pending and bank verification stale. A later suspension clears pending suspension; a later verification clears stale status. Payment while stale fails; closure with pending suspension fails. | Associates obligations with successive changes and distinguishes initial verification from later verification. | Net permits the events but does not enforce their conditional ordering. Generic response/precedence constraints would also affect cases without a post-activation change. |
| R12 | Temporal profile, authoritative for warnings | Pair-specific `abs(elapsed − mean) > 2.0 × standard_deviation`, with seconds as the unit. | Expresses statistical timing expectations. | Warning only. Missing target events are handled by hard existence/response rules, not by imputing a temporal deviation. Repeated occurrences require an explicit pairing policy. |

# 3. Procedural Backbone

Let:

- `Checks = Capture Master Data || Run Sanctions Screening || Verify Bank Account`;
- `Accepted = (Manager Approval XOR (Enhanced Due Diligence ; Compliance Approval)) ; Activate Vendor ; Release First Payment ; Close Onboarding`.

The backbone is:

`Receive Onboarding Request ; Checks ; Classify Risk ; (Reject Vendor ; Close Onboarding XOR Accepted)`.

Here `||` is concurrency, `;` is precedence, and XOR selects one branch. Non-consuming phase self-loops add clarification and escalation before the disposition, legal opinions through the remaining case, and bank-change handling after activation. These loops are permissive opportunities; the other layers enforce their obligations.

The following self-contained PM4Py construction assigns an accepting Petri net to `procedural_model`. Internal transition names distinguish transitions sharing the same visible label.

```python
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import PetriNet, Marking
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.utils import petri_utils

net = PetriNet("controlled_vendor_onboarding")
place_names = [
    "source", "pre_classification",
    "capture_ready", "screen_ready", "bank_ready",
    "capture_done", "screen_done", "bank_done",
    "classified", "edd_done", "manager_done", "compliance_done",
    "activated", "paid", "rejected", "sink",
]
places = {}
for name in place_names:
    places[name] = PetriNet.Place(name)
    net.places.add(places[name])

def add_transition(name, label, inputs, outputs):
    transition = PetriNet.Transition(name, label)
    net.transitions.add(transition)
    for place_name in inputs:
        petri_utils.add_arc_from_to(places[place_name], transition, net)
    for place_name in outputs:
        petri_utils.add_arc_from_to(transition, places[place_name], net)
    return transition

add_transition(
    "receive", "Receive Onboarding Request", ["source"],
    ["pre_classification", "capture_ready", "screen_ready", "bank_ready"]
)
add_transition(
    "capture", "Capture Master Data", ["capture_ready"], ["capture_done"]
)
add_transition(
    "screen", "Run Sanctions Screening", ["screen_ready"], ["screen_done"]
)
add_transition(
    "initial_bank_check", "Verify Bank Account", ["bank_ready"], ["bank_done"]
)
add_transition(
    "classify", "Classify Risk",
    ["pre_classification", "capture_done", "screen_done", "bank_done"],
    ["classified"]
)
add_transition(
    "manager_approval", "Manager Approval", ["classified"], ["manager_done"]
)
add_transition(
    "edd", "Enhanced Due Diligence", ["classified"], ["edd_done"]
)
add_transition(
    "compliance_approval", "Compliance Approval",
    ["edd_done"], ["compliance_done"]
)
add_transition(
    "reject", "Reject Vendor", ["classified"], ["rejected"]
)
add_transition(
    "activate_low", "Activate Vendor", ["manager_done"], ["activated"]
)
add_transition(
    "activate_high", "Activate Vendor", ["compliance_done"], ["activated"]
)
add_transition(
    "first_payment", "Release First Payment", ["activated"], ["paid"]
)
add_transition(
    "close_accepted", "Close Onboarding", ["paid"], ["sink"]
)
add_transition(
    "close_rejected", "Close Onboarding", ["rejected"], ["sink"]
)

pre_outcome_phases = [
    "pre_classification", "classified", "edd_done",
    "manager_done", "compliance_done",
]

def add_phase_loops(label, phase_names, prefix):
    for phase_name in phase_names:
        add_transition(
            prefix + "_" + phase_name, label,
            [phase_name], [phase_name]
        )

add_phase_loops(
    "Request Clarification", pre_outcome_phases, "request_clarification"
)
add_phase_loops(
    "Receive Clarification", pre_outcome_phases, "receive_clarification"
)
add_phase_loops(
    "Sanctions Escalation", pre_outcome_phases, "sanctions_escalation"
)
add_phase_loops(
    "Obtain Legal Opinion",
    pre_outcome_phases + ["activated", "paid", "rejected"],
    "legal_opinion"
)
for label, prefix in [
    ("Change Bank Details", "change_bank"),
    ("Suspend Vendor", "suspend"),
    ("Verify Bank Account", "repeat_bank_check"),
]:
    add_phase_loops(label, ["activated", "paid"], prefix)

initial_marking = Marking({places["source"]: 1})
final_marking = Marking({places["sink"]: 1})
procedural_model = (net, initial_marking, final_marking)
```

Receipt creates three independent work tokens and a phase token. Classification consumes all four, so any order of the three completed checks is legal, but none can be skipped. Timestamp-interleaved traces demonstrate allowable orders; they do not prove actual execution overlap.

The classified token permits exactly one primary route. High-risk approval requires due diligence; activation requires the selected approval. Rejection and activation cannot both be reached in one execution. Accepted closure requires payment; rejected closure requires rejection.

Clarification loops permit arbitrary request/response occurrences and interleaving with the backbone. A request can occur in one phase and its response in a later pre-outcome phase. The net itself does not count unanswered requests: R3 supplies that memory. Similarly, it deliberately permits an opinion after activation so that the same structural net supports either conflict resolution; the chosen policy monitor rejects an activation with an outstanding opinion.

The activated and paid states allow repeated bank changes, suspension, and re-verification. R11 supplies the conditional obligations. No second activation or payment is possible. Nothing follows closure. There are no block-structure approximations: this is an explicit Petri net, with the stated separation of permissive structure from cross-cutting obligations.

| `response`: (Activate Vendor, Release First Payment); (Release First Payment, Close Onboarding); (Reject Vendor, Close Onboarding) | Each source needs its later completion activity. Intervening events are allowed. |
| `altresponse`: (Request Clarification, Receive Clarification) | Every request gets a later response before the next request, if there is another request. Arbitrary other activities may intervene. |
| `altprecedence`: (Request Clarification, Receive Clarification) | Every response has a preceding request since the previous response. Together with alternate response this yields paired alternating cycles. |
| `nonsuccession`: (Activate Vendor, Receive Clarification); (Reject Vendor, Receive Clarification) | No response may occur after either disposition. Combined with alternate response, an outstanding request cannot be carried through an outcome. |

**Response versus precedence:** response is triggered by the source and looks forward; precedence is triggered by the target and looks backward. Neither implies the other.

**Alternate versus chain response:** alternate response permits intervening checks, approvals, or other events while disallowing a second request before its response. Chain response would require the response to be the immediately next event and would incorrectly reject T2.

**Noncoexistence versus nonsuccession:** noncoexistence bans both labels in either order. Nonsuccession bans a source-before-target ordering while allowing target-before-source. It is not an immediate-adjacency restriction.

There is intentionally no unconditional legal-opinion-to-activation precedence rule. R5 belongs to the exact outstanding-obligation monitor.

There is deliberately no `always_after[(Verify Bank Account, Classify Risk)]`: later re-verification need not precede another classification. There is no finite allowed-count set for bank verification or clarification cycles because no maximum was specified.

The skeleton overlaps strongly with the net and DECLARE, but cannot select the correct approval route, enforce alternate pairing, detect an outstanding opinion specifically at activation, inspect resources, or establish the R11 change-to-verification dependency. Equal clarification counts and ordinary before/after relations are necessary but insufficient for valid cycles. T6 therefore passes this skeleton even under the strict legal policy.

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"):
        (48 * 3600, 24 * 3600),
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"):
        (12 * 3600, 6 * 3600),
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"):
        (72 * 3600, 36 * 3600),
}
zeta = 2.0
```

For a measured elapsed time `d`, flag a warning when `abs(d - mean) / standard_deviation > zeta`. Equality at the boundary is not a deviation. The resulting nonnegative acceptable bands are:

| Pair | Mean seconds | Standard deviation seconds | Two-standard-deviation band |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Request Clarification → Receive Clarification | 172800 | 86400 | 0–96 hours |
| Sanctions Escalation → Obtain Legal Opinion | 43200 | 21600 | 0–24 hours |
| Activate Vendor → Release First Payment | 259200 | 129600 | 0–144 hours |

These are statistical warnings by R12, not hard deadlines or automatic rejection criteria. In particular, a 2-hour payment is within the specified band despite being much earlier than its 72-hour mean.

A profile check needs both events and usable timestamps. A missing response or payment does not generate a measured duration; the appropriate hard rule catches that absence. “pass” for such a temporal check means no measured deviation, not proof of fulfillment.

For repeated activities, define which occurrences belong together. In this design, clarification uses its matched request/response cycle; each escalation uses its first subsequent opinion; activation uses its unique first payment. Generic PM4Py activity-pair evaluation can consider additional forward pairs across repeated cycles and produce extra warnings. Those diagnostics must be labeled with their actual event pairs and reconciled against the obligation pairing rather than silently treated as matched-cycle delays. All eight supplied traces have at most one occurrence of each temporal-profile endpoint, so this distinction does not change their verdicts.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

The validator below operates on one case in its established event order. It uses `concept:name`, `org:resource`, and, when needed, `time:timestamp`. Timestamp values are timezone-aware Python `datetime` objects. The authorization callback is a trusted external-evidence adapter, not a classifier inferred from the trace.

```python
from datetime import datetime, timezone

def validate_external(
    events, *, complete=True, policy="legal_first",
    authorization_lookup=None
):
    """
    policy: "legal_first" or "emergency_exception".

    authorization_lookup(activation_event, outstanding_escalation_events)
    returns "authorized", "denied", or "unknown".
    The adapter verifies the joint authorization, its scope, authenticity,
    and validity before activation. Missing evidence returns "unknown".
    """
    if policy not in {"legal_first", "emergency_exception"}:
        raise ValueError("Unknown policy")
    if any("concept:name" not in event for event in events):
        return {
            "status": "not evaluated",
            "failures": [],
            "unknowns": [("input", None, "Missing activity label")],
        }

    failures, unknowns = [], []

    def fail(rule, index, detail):
        failures.append((rule, index, detail))

    def unknown(rule, index, detail):
        unknowns.append((rule, index, detail))

    def timestamp(event):
        value = event.get("time:timestamp")
        if (
            isinstance(value, datetime)
            and value.tzinfo is not None
            and value.utcoffset() is not None
        ):
            return value.astimezone(timezone.utc)
        return None

    indexed = list(enumerate(events))

    # R9: compare every relevant occurrence, including re-verifications.
    def check_separation(left_label, right_labels, rule):
        left = [(i, e) for i, e in indexed
                if e["concept:name"] == left_label]
        right = [(i, e) for i, e in indexed
                 if e["concept:name"] in right_labels]
        if not left or not right:
            return  # No applicable pair; activity obligations live elsewhere.
        for i, event in left + right:
            if event.get("org:resource") in (None, ""):
                unknown(rule, i, "Missing relevant resource identifier")
        for i, first in left:
            first_resource = first.get("org:resource")
            if first_resource in (None, ""):
                continue
            for j, second in right:
                second_resource = second.get("org:resource")
                if (
                    second_resource not in (None, "")
                    and first_resource == second_resource
                ):
                    fail(
                        rule, max(i, j),
                        "Same resource on events " + str(i) + " and " + str(j)
                    )

    check_separation(
        "Verify Bank Account",
        {"Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval"}, "R9-bank"
    )
    check_separation(
        "Capture Master Data", {"Activate Vendor"}, "R9-master"
    )

    outstanding_escalations = []
    emergency_activations = []
    activated = False
    suspension_pending = False
    bank_stale = False

    for i, event in indexed:
        activity = event["concept:name"]

        if activity == "Sanctions Escalation":
            outstanding_escalations.append(event)

        elif activity == "Obtain Legal Opinion":
            # This is the first subsequent opinion for every pending exception.
            for activation_index, activation in emergency_activations:
                start = timestamp(activation)
                finish = timestamp(event)
                if start is None or finish is None:
                    unknown(
                        "R6-deadline", i,
                        "Missing or unusable activation/opinion timestamp"
                    )
                else:
                    elapsed = (finish - start).total_seconds()
                    if elapsed < 0 or elapsed > 24 * 3600:
                        fail(
                            "R6-deadline", i,
                            "Opinion outside the 24-hour activation deadline"
                        )
            emergency_activations.clear()
            outstanding_escalations.clear()

        elif activity == "Activate Vendor":
            if outstanding_escalations:
                if policy == "legal_first":
                    fail("R5", i, "Activation with outstanding legal opinion")
                else:
                    if authorization_lookup is None:
                        auth = "unknown"
                    else:
                        auth = authorization_lookup(
                            event, list(outstanding_escalations)
                        )
                    if auth == "denied":
                        fail("R6-authorization", i,
                             "Joint authorization absent or invalid")
                    elif auth != "authorized":
                        unknown("R6-authorization", i,
                                "Joint authorization cannot be established")
                    # Check timing even if authorization is unresolved/invalid.
                    emergency_activations.append((i, event))
            activated = True

        elif activity == "Change Bank Details" and activated:
            suspension_pending = True
            bank_stale = True

        elif activity == "Suspend Vendor" and activated:
            suspension_pending = False

        elif activity == "Verify Bank Account" and activated:
            bank_stale = False

        elif activity == "Release First Payment" and bank_stale:
            fail("R11-verification", i,
                 "Payment after bank change without subsequent verification")

    if suspension_pending:
        if complete:
            fail("R11-suspension", len(events),
                 "Completed case has a bank change without later suspension")
        else:
            unknown("R11-suspension", len(events),
                    "Suspension obligation still pending")

    if emergency_activations:
        for i, activation in emergency_activations:
            if complete:
                fail("R6-deadline", i,
                     "Completed emergency case has no later legal opinion")
            else:
                unknown("R6-deadline", i,
                        "Opinion pending; monitor the activation deadline")

    status = (
        "fail" if failures else
        "not evaluated" if unknowns else
        "pass"
    )
    return {"status": status, "failures": failures, "unknowns": unknowns}
```

| Check | Required attributes/evidence | Missing-data treatment |

| Master data versus activation | `org:resource` on master-data capture and activation. | Same treatment. No activation means this separation rule is vacuous. |
| Strict legal ordering | Activity labels and event order; escalation/opinion obligation IDs if opinions are scoped separately in production. | The supplied model uses the documented “one opinion clears all outstanding escalations” semantics. | Emergency authorization | A trusted record linked to the case and activation, required joint signatories, emergency designation and authorization scope covering the outstanding escalations, evidence identifiers, and an authorization time no later than activation. | No record or unverifiable scope/signature gives unknown, not inferred authorization. Verified absence or invalid authorization gives fail. Activity labels and approval resources cannot fill this gap. |

Bank-version IDs are recommended if parallel updates or scope-specific verification are possible. | Ambiguous order or unknown version coverage is not evaluated. # 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

**Duplicated enforcement.** The net, DECLARE, and skeleton all exclude contradictory outcomes and approval combinations. Counts, classification prerequisites, and eventual completion also overlap. Preserve one authoritative rule identifier and report one finding with multiple supporting diagnostics rather than three separate penalties. The duplication helps detect implementation drift.

**Loops and post-activation behavior.** Setting bank verification frequency to {1}, equating its count with classification, or requiring every bank verification to have a later classification would contradict legitimate R11 re-verification. A chain-response clarification rule would conflict with permitted interleaving. Directly-follows constraints between payment and closure would conflict with post-payment changes or opinions. A global change-to-suspension response would also regulate pre-activation changes beyond R11's condition. The net's exclusion of those pre-activation changes is an explicit workflow-scope assumption to revisit if they become legitimate.

Clarification count equivalence is valid only under the stated one-response-per-request assumption. Matching counts alone cannot detect incorrectly paired cycles. Opinion count equivalence with escalations is intentionally absent: one later opinion may discharge several escalations, and additional opinions are allowed.

**Policy conflict.** Do not label T6 an authorized emergency because an opinion arrives within 24 hours. The strict policy forbids it; the amended policy requires independent evidence. Statistical legal-review timing and the emergency deadline are different rules, even though the supplied two-standard-deviation legal-review upper bound happens to be 24 hours. They use different anchors: escalation versus activation.

**Vacuity.** Response is vacuously satisfied without its source; precedence is vacuously satisfied without its target; noncoexistence allows both activities to be absent. Temporal checks cannot measure missing target events. The net and mandatory counts prevent empty traces and skipped core workflow, but conditional pass results still need to identify inapplicable obligations. None of these formalisms alone establishes the whole policy. Batch conformance on a running prefix must not turn still-pending eventual obligations into completed-case failures.

**Missing case and event attributes.** The log supplies no actual risk class, emergency flag, authorization record, bank version, opinion scope, or independent-person identifier beyond resource strings. The model cannot recover them from activity sequences. A true low/high classification mismatch therefore remains invisible. Shared accounts and inconsistent resource aliases can defeat segregation checks unless identity data is normalized.

**Timestamp limitations.** Completion timestamps and their total order do not establish real concurrency, activity start times, working-time calendars, or causation. Clock skew, rounding, and ties can change apparent precedence and measured elapsed time. Require an event sequence for order and normalized timestamps for deadlines. Do not infer that equal timestamps mean a prerequisite was satisfied first.

**Statistical versus normative truth.** A profile mean is not a service-level deadline. Support/confidence values are not policy authority: 100% prevalence in historical logs can still encode a historical violation or an accidental regularity. Conversely, rare compliant exception behavior may disappear during discovery. Keep authored policies versioned and separate from discovered candidates.

**Composite limits.** Passing the skeleton is weaker than passing the net and all obligation monitors. The net is deliberately permissive for legal review, clarification memory, and changed-bank handling. A change followed by verification and payment can satisfy the verification part of R11 while still violating eventual suspension. No supplied trace exercises R11, so the eight cases do not validate that implementation branch.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

Use the accepting Petri net as the canonical procedural representation. Maintain a versioned policy catalog that assigns authority as in Section 2: route choice to the net, cross-cutting activity obligations to DECLARE, optional maximum counts to the skeleton, and conditional legal ordering, authorization, resource separation, and bank-change state to explicit validators. Keep the chosen Legal-first decision visible in that catalog; adopting an emergency exception requires a policy-version change.

Run checks in this order:

1. Validate case grouping, event labels, order, identities, completion status, and timestamps. Record missing evidence separately from proven violations.
2. Check exact procedural acceptance and produce alignment diagnostics for failures.
3. Evaluate DECLARE and skeleton constraints independently, even for procedurally invalid cases, to expose additional and duplicated violations.
4. Run the external monitors with the selected policy and available evidence. For live cases, maintain outstanding clarification, opinion, suspension, and bank-verification obligations; block forbidden outcomes immediately and schedule emergency deadlines only under an adopted exception.
5. Evaluate temporal warnings on identifiable occurrence pairs and compute an overall result: hard violation → fail; otherwise unresolved required evidence → not evaluated or policy-dependent; otherwise temporal deviation → warning; otherwise pass.

The PM4Py diagnostic calls can use the model dictionaries directly:

```python
# log is an appropriately formatted PM4Py event log or dataframe.
import pm4py

declare_diagnostics = pm4py.conformance_declare(log, declare_model)
skeleton_diagnostics = pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton(log, log_skeleton)
temporal_diagnostics = pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(
    log, temporal_profile, zeta=zeta
)
```

These diagnostics require the policy and external-monitor results before becoming an overall compliance decision. Record the policy version, authoritative requirement, decisive event, supporting layer findings, and any missing evidence. A screen should explain “activation while legal opinion outstanding under Legal-first policy” for T6, even though its activity-only layers pass. Under the exception policy it should instead show “authorization unverified; opinion arrived within 10 hours.” Contradictory layer findings should remain visible with their differing scopes; they should never be combined by majority vote.
