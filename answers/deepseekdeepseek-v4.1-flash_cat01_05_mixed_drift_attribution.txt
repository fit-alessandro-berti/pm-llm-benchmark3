## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The raw/mapped fitness gap is mainly a semantic and provenance signal. In March, raw fitness falls from about 0.93 to 0.55 while mapped fitness stays near 0.90–0.91 after mapping `Eligibility Screen` to `Validate Eligibility` and removing duplicated `Open Case` events. That does not imply the underlying business process was unchanged. It implies the old normative model and raw labels stopped matching the recorded log. A label mapping can recover conformance because conformance checking is label-sensitive; if the same activity is renamed, the model sees a missing or illegal move. But renamed labels, duplicated technical events, and missing resources can coexist with real behavioral drift, such as more `Request Info` loops or longer queues.

Duplicated technical events inflate variants and DFG edges, making process discovery and variant analysis noisier. Missing resources weaken resource/team/organizational mining but may not affect control-flow conformance after deduplication. The post-May DFG and performance patterns cannot be dismissed as logging drift alone because normalized `Assess->RequestInfo` rises from 12 to 24–29 per 100 cases, `RequestInfo->CollectDocs` rises similarly, rework variant share rises from 12% to 27–29%, and p90 cycle time rises from 69h to 110h+. Logging migration is strongest in March–April; supplier, staffing, priority, and seasonal mechanisms better explain May onward.

        out["concept:name"] = out["concept:name"].replace(mapping)
        if remove_duplicate_open_case:
            open_mask = out["concept:name"].eq("Open Case")
            open_df = out[open_mask]
            dup_idx = open_df[open_df.duplicated(
                subset=["case:concept:name"], keep="first"
            )].index
            out = out.drop(index=dup_idx)
    else:
        out["activity"] = out["activity"].replace(mapping)
        if remove_duplicate_open_case:
            open_mask = out["activity"].eq("Open Case")
            open_df = out[open_mask]
            dup_idx = open_df[open_df.duplicated(
                subset=["case_id"], keep="first"
            )].index
            out = out.drop(index=dup_idx)
    return out

def compare_raw_normalized(df, start, end):
    """Compare raw and normalized label mappings without overwriting the original."""
    raw = slice_time_window(df, start, end)
    norm = normalize_log(raw)
    return {
        "raw_dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(raw),
        "normalized_dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(norm),
        "raw_variants": pm4py.get_variants(raw),
        "normalized_variants": pm4py.get_variants(norm),
    }

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest conclusion is that the March raw-fitness collapse is primarily logging/semantic/provenance drift. The raw fitness drop from 0.93 to 0.55 while mapped fitness remains near 0.90 is difficult to explain otherwise. The migration introduced `Eligibility Screen`, duplicated `Open Case`, and missing resources. Confidence: high.

The May deterioration is plausibly supplier-driven rework and population/case-mix drift. Supplier Z rises from 16% to 46%, rework variant share from 12% to 27%, and p90 cycle time from 68h to 95h. Supplier Z cases were already somewhat more complex, so this is not pure supplier causation; the supplier disruption may amplify pre-existing complexity. Confidence: medium-high.

The June–August queueing and cycle-time worsening plausibly reflects staffing loss and capacity redistribution. Service time for `Assess` changes only modestly, while reconstructed queue waiting before `Assess` rises much more. That points to queueing rather than task execution. However, Supplier Z backlog and the July priority rule mediate this effect. Confidence: medium.

The July priority rule likely redistributed delay: high-complexity cases may move faster while standard cases wait longer. Monthly aggregates cannot confirm this because they hide priority-specific queues. The October senior-assessment jump and high-complexity rise are consistent with seasonal renewal load, but the priority rule and staffing shortage amplify the impact. Confidence: medium for priority redistribution, high for seasonal population drift.

December improvement is real but cannot be cleanly attributed to overtime and document-quality campaign because seasonal load also falls. Persistent post-May rework and missing resources suggest that some adaptation or measurement issues remain even after supplier recovery. I would revise the account if event-level analysis showed non-Z, non-high-complexity cases with unchanged rework, no priority-specific queue shift, or mapped DFG changes explained entirely by remaining label/schema drift.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw fitness drops >0.05 but mapped fitness recovers within 0.02 of baseline, THEN treat as label/schema drift and audit mappings, BECAUSE conformance models are label-sensitive and a rename can mimic illegal behavior.

2. IF Supplier Z share rises >10 pp and Z cases show higher `Assess->RequestInfo`, THEN open supplier-quality and backlog review, BECAUSE incomplete bundles predict recollect loops and longer cycle time.

3. IF reconstructed queue waiting before `Assess` rises >20% while `Assess` service time rises <10%, THEN investigate staffing and queue capacity, BECAUSE waiting dominates service-time deterioration.

4. IF high-complexity wait falls while standard wait rises after a priority rule, THEN audit priority triage and standard-case aging, BECAUSE aggregate cycle time can hide redistribution of delay.

5. IF high_complexity_pct and volume exceed same-season bands, THEN stratify conformance and performance by complexity before declaring process drift, BECAUSE renewal season changes population mix.

6. IF missing_resource_pct exceeds 3% and is concentrated in migrated activities, THEN pause resource/team mining and fix ETL/resource entry, BECAUSE missing resources invalidate organizational analysis.

7. IF rework remains above baseline after Supplier Z recovery and after seasonal adjustment, THEN treat as persistent behavioral adaptation and review document collection/assessment rules, BECAUSE supplier backlog alone no longer explains the loop rate.
