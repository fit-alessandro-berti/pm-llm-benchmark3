# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

## Handover-of-work network
- **What it captures**: Directed, sequential transfers of “work ownership” (or at least event responsibility) between resources inside the same case.
- **What Mira’s centrality implies**:
  - Mira is a frequent *broker* in exception flows: many cases move from `Bot_Rules → Mira_Coordinator → (adjuster/specialist/team)` and back from specialists to Mira.
  - High betweenness (0.77) is consistent with a role that *routes*, *reopens*, *escalates*, or *requests information*—actions that sit between many other steps.
- **What it does not imply**:
  - It does not mean Mira “works closely” with others, nor that she does most of the substantive claim work.
  - It does not prove she is the *cause* of long cycle times; high centrality can reflect *selection* (she touches inherently difficult cases) and *event granularity* (many short routing events create many edges).

## Working-together network
- **What it captures**: Undirected co-occurrence—two resources are linked when they both appear on the same case (regardless of order, intensity, or time spent).
- **What Mira’s peripheral position implies**:
  - Mira touches a *minority* of cases (22%), so her maximum possible co-case rate with any individual is capped by that coverage; pairwise rates (4–8%) are plausible even with many handovers.
  - She likely interacts with a *wide variety* of partners across different exception cases rather than forming stable dyads (contrast: `Alice—Carla`, `Bruno—Diego`).
- **What it does not imply**:
  - It does not imply she is unimportant. A dispatcher/exception desk can be “everywhere” in *transitions* while rarely co-working repeatedly with the *same* person across the *entire portfolio*.
  - It does not measure gatekeeping intensity (one brief routing touch counts the same as hours of joint handling).

## Subcontracting network
- **What it captures**: Short A→B→A patterns (“I delegate to you, then I resume”), within a small event window (here n=2).
- **What Mira’s centrality implies**:
  - Frequent `Mira → Omar_Medical → Mira` and `Mira → Priya_Legal → Mira` fragments suggest Mira is a *case manager* for specialist exceptions: she hands off for expert review and then decides the next step (reopen/assign/complaint handling).
  - High subcontracting betweenness (0.69) supports “delegation-and-return” coordination rather than deep collaboration.
- **What it does not imply**:
  - It does not prove Mira controls specialist conclusions—only that the process returns to her after their event.
  - It may also reflect rework loops (e.g., repeated missing info) rather than purposeful delegation.

## Performance/time statistics
- **What the stats suggest**:
  - Mira’s median service time per event (4.2 min) is modest; her impact is more likely through *queueing and gating* than slow per-event handling.
  - “High variance” cycle-time contribution and the absence-period effect (exception p90 rising from 8.4 to 13.9 days) point to a *capacity-sensitive coordination function* for exceptions.
  - Vendor steps (`Vendor_Verify`, `Vendor_Pay`) have large service times (hours) and high waiting-time components, so exception delays may be a *composition* of vendor latency, specialist availability, and coordinator routing—rather than a single bottleneck.
- **What it does not prove**:
  - It does not prove Mira is “the bottleneck” in all claims; ordinary low-value claims were unaffected during her absence.
  - It does not distinguish service time from waiting time without explicit waiting-time insertion and queue analysis.

## Role-discovery or activity-profile similarity
- **What it implies if roles are discovered from activity profiles**:
  - Mira’s activity mix (missing info, reopen, escalate, specialist assignment) should cluster into a distinct “exception coordination” role, separate from adjusters (assess/negotiation) and team leads (triage/approve).
  - Similarity analysis may reveal partial overlap with temporary staff (missing info/coverage validation), indicating possible load shifting or shared responsibilities.
- **What it does not imply**:
  - Role clustering does not establish authority or compliance adequacy; it only shows behavioral similarity in the log.
  - If resource IDs are reused (shared inbox accounts), role discovery can conflate multiple people into one “resource.”

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

## Hypothesis 1: Formal coordinator / exception desk
- **Mechanism**: `Bot_Rules` routes exception-prone cases to Mira; she requests missing information, reopens cases, escalates complaints, and assigns the right downstream handler (adjuster or specialist), then hands back to a team workflow.
- **Evidence supporting it**:
  - Strong `Bot_Rules → Mira` handover (920, 3.5 min) matches automated-to-manual exception routing.
  - Mira touches 64% of reopened claims and 71% of complaints; her activity list aligns with exception handling.
  - High subcontracting with `Omar_Medical`/`Priya_Legal` suggests delegated expert review with return-to-coordinator.
  - Absence causes exception queues to accumulate and p90 to worsen materially.
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it**:
  - Mira touches only 22% of all claims, so she is not a universal coordinator—her “desk” likely applies to a subset (exceptions/complaints).
  - Working-together edges with team leads are low (4–5%), which may indicate limited formal integration with team leadership or that her function is orthogonal to routine team work.
- **Observable predictions in future event logs**:
  - Mira’s presence should be strongly enriched in variants containing `Reopen Claim`, `Escalate Complaint`, repeated `Request Missing Information`, and specialist reviews.
  - Temporal position: Mira events should cluster shortly after `Bot_Rules` for exception_flag=True cases and near complaint/reopen points later in the case.

## Hypothesis 2: Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency (selective bottleneck)
- **Mechanism**: Exception cases cannot progress without Mira’s routing/clarification decisions; when she is absent or overloaded, exception work stalls, even if her own per-event service time is short.
- **Evidence supporting it**:
  - Very high handover betweenness (0.77) and subcontracting betweenness (0.69) indicate “bridge” dependency.
  - Absence period increases exception p90 cycle time (8.4 → 13.9 days) while ordinary claims remain stable—classic sign of a *segment-specific* bottleneck.
  - Qualitative reports: “person who knows where hard cases should go.”
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it**:
  - Median service time is only minutes; delays may come from waiting/queues around her, not her handling speed.
  - Vendors and specialists have long service times and may dominate latency; Mira may be the visible “switch” but not the limiting server.
- **Observable predictions in future event logs**:
  - Waiting time between `Bot_Rules` (or reopen/complaint events) and the next downstream handler will increase as Mira’s workload rises.
  - Adding backup coordinators or codified routing rules should reduce exception waiting times and reduce the p90 sensitivity to Mira’s absence.

## Hypothesis 3: Organizational boundary spanner / triage broker
- **Mechanism**: Mira spans organizational boundaries (automation ↔ humans, North ↔ South, adjusters ↔ specialists, internal ↔ vendors), resolving mismatches in information and responsibility, and selecting the next accountable actor.
- **Evidence supporting it**:
  - Mira hands off to many downstream roles (multiple adjusters, both specialists) and receives escalations from temporary staff.
  - Her “disappearing” after routing is consistent with brokerage: intervene at boundary points, then exit the case until the next boundary crossing.
  - High out-degree and in-degree centrality support a hub that connects different resource communities.
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it**:
  - Low working-together co-occurrence with each specific partner suggests she does not repeatedly collaborate with the same individuals, though this can be an expected consequence of boundary spanning across many cases.
  - If team routing is mostly stable (`Alice→Carla`, `Bruno→Diego`), Mira’s boundary role may be more about *exceptions* than routine cross-team coordination.
- **Observable predictions in future event logs**:
  - Mira involvement should be higher for cases with attributes that increase boundary crossings (e.g., certain `region`, `channel`, higher `amount_band`, or exception_flag=True).
  - Community detection on networks should place Mira at the interface between bot/vendor nodes and human specialist/adjuster clusters.

## Hypothesis 4: Automation monitor / human-in-the-loop quality gatekeeper
- **Mechanism**: Mira validates or corrects automation outcomes (routing and fraud screening), triggers re-evaluation, and ensures complex exceptions are handled consistently.
- **Evidence supporting it**:
  - Subcontracting fragment `Bot_Rules → Mira → Bot_Rules` (62) suggests cases where automation is revisited after manual intervention.
  - High exception_flag share among Bot_Rules-handled cases and Mira’s exception-heavy portfolio is consistent with “review-and-correct” work.
  - Compliance reviewer concern indicates her decisions may materially affect control outcomes.
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it**:
  - Mira’s main recorded activities are not “Fraud Screen” or “Triage Claim”; monitoring may be partial or under-recorded.
  - The strongest evidence also fits simpler “exception desk” routing without implying deep automation governance.
- **Observable predictions in future event logs**:
  - Cases with repeated `Fraud Screen` or routing changes should disproportionately include Mira.
  - After Mira events, Bot_Rules events should recur more often than in cases without Mira (a “manual correction loop” signature).

## Hypothesis 5: Data/label artifact or role conflation (shared coordinator identity)
- **Mechanism**: `Mira_Coordinator` may represent a shared inbox, a queue role, or mixed responsibilities (multiple humans logging under one ID), inflating centrality metrics while diluting stable co-working ties.
- **Evidence supporting it**:
  - Many short “routing/assignment” events and the pattern “appears then disappears” can occur when logging captures queue moves rather than sustained handling.
  - Peripheral working-together edges can arise if the resource label is used sparsely per case but across many different cases/partners.
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it**:
  - The operational absence effect and adjusters’ qualitative statements suggest there is a real, capacity-constrained coordination function (even if the ID is shared).
- **Observable predictions in future event logs**:
  - If conflated, Mira events may show unusually high concurrency (overlapping timestamps), broader-than-human working hours, or multiple distinct behavioral clusters within the same resource label.
  - Splitting the resource into real individuals (or shifts) should reduce apparent betweenness and reveal more meaningful working-together patterns.

# 3. Risk assessment

- **Bottleneck risk (segment-specific)**:
  - **Risk**: Exception_flag=True, complaints, and reopened claims are sensitive to Mira’s availability; queues build when she is absent.
  - **Calibration**: This is not evidence she delays cases intentionally; it is consistent with capacity limits and exception complexity.
  - **Mitigation**: Add backup coverage, clarify routing rules, and reduce unnecessary routing touches (e.g., automate low-risk missing-info requests).

- **Coordination risk (misrouting, rework, escalation loops)**:
  - **Risk**: Frequent `Request Missing Information` and `Reopen Claim` patterns can create rework loops; misrouting can overload specialists or cause repeated reopenings.
  - **Mitigation**: Standardize decision criteria for specialist assignment and reopen triggers; add feedback loops from specialists to improve routing rules.

- **Key-person risk (knowledge concentration)**:
  - **Risk**: “Person who knows where hard cases should go” implies tacit knowledge; losing it increases exception cycle-time variance.
  - **Mitigation**: Codify heuristics into playbooks, cross-train at least one backup coordinator, and maintain a transparent exception triage queue.

- **Compliance/control risk (discretion at sensitive decision points)**:
  - **Risk**: Mira is both (a) detecting/flagging problems and (b) selecting the next specialist; this can reduce auditability and create uncontrolled discretion.
  - **Calibration**: No direct evidence of wrongdoing; the concern is about *process control design*.
  - **Mitigation**: Add auditable decision fields (reason codes), lightweight approvals for high-risk routing (e.g., legal/medical), and periodic sampling reviews.

- **Workload fairness risk (unequal case complexity allocation)**:
  - **Risk**: Central routing can unintentionally concentrate difficult cases on particular adjusters or specialists (e.g., repeatedly sending to the same senior adjusters), affecting burnout and SLA performance.
  - **Mitigation**: Monitor complexity-adjusted load per resource (by `amount_band`, exception_flag, complaint/reopen indicators) and use balanced assignment rules.

- **Data-quality risk (measurement artifacts)**:
  - **Risk**: Short routing events, shared accounts, missing lifecycle transitions, or inconsistent logging can distort SNA metrics and misattribute delays.
  - **Mitigation**: Validate `org:resource` semantics (person vs role), check event ordering and lifecycle completeness, and separate “system routing events” from “human decision events” if needed.

# 4. Further process-mining analyses

## Segment and compare networks (exception vs non-exception)
- **Handover-of-work network**: Filter to `exception_flag=True` and `exception_flag=False`, then compute `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` and compare centrality via `pm4py.discover_network_analysis`. Check whether Mira’s betweenness is concentrated in exception cases.
- **Working-together network**: On the same segments, compute `pm4py.discover_working_together_network`. Evaluate whether Mira’s low weighted degree is driven by low case coverage or by highly diverse partnering (many low-strength ties).

## Analyze subcontracting patterns and rework loops
- **Subcontracting network**: Compute `pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network` for n=2 and a larger n (e.g., 5) to see whether `Mira → specialist → Mira` is a clean delegation or part of longer rework loops.
- **Variant-based checks**: Use `pm4py.filter_variants` to isolate high-frequency exception variants that contain `Reopen Claim` or repeated `Request Missing Information`, then quantify how often Mira is the “return-to” node.

## Role and similarity discovery
- **Activity-based similarity**: Run `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` and inspect where Mira clusters (with temps, team leads, or a distinct “exception coordination” role).
- **Organizational roles**: Use `pm4py.discover_organizational_roles` to derive roles and then validate whether specialist assignment is concentrated in a single discovered role (control-risk indicator).

## Time-window and absence-period analysis
- **Filtering by time ranges**: Filter the 6-day absence period and adjacent weeks, recompute networks and waiting times. Test whether exception queue growth is explained by missing coordination capacity vs changes in incoming exception volume or vendor delays.
- **Filtering by attributes**: Use `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` (and case-level filters) for `region`, `channel`, `amount_band`, `priority`, and `case_type` to see where Mira’s brokerage is most pronounced.

## Service and waiting-time decomposition
- **Waiting-time analysis**: Use `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` and `pm4py.get_service_time` to separate (a) Mira’s own service time from (b) waiting time before/after her events.
  - Compare “waiting after Mira” to “waiting after team leads” for exception cases to identify whether the delay is downstream (specialist/vendor capacity) or at the coordination gate.
  - Compute distributions for time from `Bot_Rules` to first human action, and from specialist completion back to the next routing decision.

## Automation and vendor interaction checks (don’t ignore bots/vendors)
- Recompute networks including bots and vendors explicitly; quantify whether `Bot_Rules` is the primary broker for exceptions and whether Mira reduces or increases the number of specialist/vendor touches per case (proxy for rework).
- Compare cycle time components attributable to `Vendor_Verify` and `Vendor_Pay` vs internal routing; assess whether improving vendor SLAs would dominate gains relative to changing coordination.

# 5. Recommendation

- **What should be changed immediately**:
  - **Add resilience for exception coordination**: establish backup coverage for Mira’s exception-desk function (cross-train a second coordinator or rotate duty) to reduce p90 sensitivity to absence.
  - **Standardize and log routing decisions**: require reason codes for `Assign Specialist`, `Reopen Claim`, and complaint escalations; define clear criteria for medical vs legal routing.
  - **Reduce low-value manual touches**: where safe, automate or template the most common missing-information requests (especially those currently pushed through Mira) while keeping human override for high-risk exceptions.

- **What should be monitored**:
  - **Exception SLA health**: p90 cycle time for `exception_flag=True`, complaints, and reopened cases; queue buildup during peak periods.
  - **Control/fairness indicators**: distribution of complex cases across adjusters/specialists; specialist assignment rates conditioned on case attributes; frequency of reopen loops after specialist review.
  - **Network drift**: weekly centrality of `Bot_Rules`, Mira, and vendors; sudden increases can indicate policy changes, staffing shortfalls, or logging shifts.

- **What should not be changed until more evidence is available**:
  - **Do not remove Mira_Coordinator** without a designed replacement (rules + staffing) for exception routing and specialist scheduling; otherwise expect more formal escalations and specialist overload.
  - **Do not over-interpret centrality as blame**: validate data quality (resource identity, lifecycle completeness) and perform waiting-time decomposition before reorganizing teams or rewriting routing automation.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira_Coordinator appears to function as an exception desk and routing broker between automation, adjuster teams, specialists, and vendors. In handover and subcontracting networks she is central because many exception cases pass through her for short “assign/reopen/request info” actions, and she often delegates to Medical/Legal and then resumes the case. She looks peripheral in the working‑together network because she touches only ~22% of all claims and spreads that work across many different partners, so no single pair co-occurs in many cases. The main operational risk is not “too much work” per event (her median handling time is minutes) but key-person and control risk: exception queues build up during her absence and her discretion in selecting the next specialist may be hard to audit. Recommendations: formalize routing rules, add backup coverage, audit specialist-selection decisions, and monitor exception cycle-time and workload distribution before making structural changes.
