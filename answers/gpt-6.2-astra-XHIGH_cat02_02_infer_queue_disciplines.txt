# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

**Reading the tables.** Each eligible set is for the named resource immediately before the observed choice. It doubles as the prediction of a skill-only rule, which permits every member. The sets assume a scanned, unfinished job is ready unless the log gives a constraint. A question mark adds a job that could already have arrived if physical entry preceded its scan by up to two minutes. If dispatch sees jobs only after scanning, omit it. Minute ties can conceal event order. Start time stands in for decision time because commitment times are missing. Each prediction holds the prior service history fixed, since changing an earlier choice could change a later candidate set.

F means oldest original arrival. P means highest priority with F breaking ties. S means shortest dispatch-visible estimate. At a free server, preemptive and non-preemptive priority give the same P ranking. W illustrates appointment preference within a window, with F as an explicit fallback. “F” in the W column means the appointment rule adds no preference at that time. The columns compare mechanisms separately. A hard release time for a booking would also remove it from F, P and S until release. In the tables, job numbers inherit the queue's letter, so 03 in QA means A03.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

- **QA.** The walk-in subsequence A01, A02, A04, A05, A07, A08 is exactly FIFO. In particular A02 starts before shorter, priority-1 A04, and A07 before priority-1 A08. A03's pass over A02 is explained by its recorded slot. The four-minute separation between their arrival scans exceeds the scan-delay bound. A06 challenges any claim that A1 always reserves enough capacity for an exact start. A due-booking preference applied only when A1 becomes free explains both bookings without requiring an interruption or manual override.
- **QB.** B02 provides direct evidence of urgent preemption. B05 overtaking older, shorter B04 at 10:00 also supports a role for priority. B01's two resumptions ahead of waiting priority-2 work and B04's start ahead of B06 contradict uniform highest-priority-first dispatch. The recorded reassignment explains why B01 stops the second time. It does not identify why B03 gets the server ahead of B05. A prior commitment to B03, FIFO among the fresh jobs at that stop, or a manual instruction could do so. B07's booking has little visible force even at the first release after 10:30.
- **QC.** SEPT picks the observed job at three of the six starts with a genuine choice (10:10, 10:18, 11:34). C04 over C06 is a clear exception to live SEPT. A sequence prepared before C06 arrived could explain it, but a completely frozen initial batch would also have served C01 before C05 and C06. C05 over C06 at 10:54 is compatible with a priority or overdue-work override. With 40 minutes of estimated work, C05's latest on-time start was 10:10, so a rule that acts as soon as estimated slack turns negative should already have favored it at 10:18. C01 over C07 at 11:40 is consistent with protecting a job that had waited 100 minutes. No aging threshold or override is recorded. C07's eventual completion 34 minutes late also weakens a general deadline-protection diagnosis.
- **QD.** D04 over D08 on D-M and D07 over D10 on D-X both favor local FIFO over priority and SEPT. D06 starting on time supports appointment handling, but it was also the oldest candidate on X at 11:30. Holding X from 11:20 is stronger evidence of planning, although on the estimates even D08 could have finished by 11:28. The skill constraint holds in every assignment. Neither the skill matrix nor the two documented availability windows explain all the idle time. For example, D-F could even have run D05 before the meeting had it selected D05 at 11:21.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked by support in this trace. The ranking distinguishes the evidence that an event occurred from the uncertainty about its effect on dispatch.

1. **Recorded interruptions and resource calendars.** A1's calibration, C1's prior job, D-X's opening and D-F's meeting remove real opportunities. QB gives separate reasons for B01's two stops. Treating these as ordinary free-server choices would overstate the evidence for an ordering rule. The calendar explains only part of D-F's idle interval. An unrecorded setup or hold is possible during the remainder. An empty reason field cannot establish its cause.
2. **Appointment windows and non-preemptive service already in progress.** A03 and D06 start exactly on time. A06 starts at the first release after its booking. A tolerance of at least five minutes on the late side would accommodate A06. B07 is still bypassed at +6 and starts at +26. Its booking could have expired, carried little weight, or had a wider window. Exact-time reservation across all bookings has weak support.
3. **Scan delay and prior dispatch commitments.** A two-minute lag can add C05 to the 10:10 physical backlog and D03 to D-F's 11:01 choice. It cannot remove a job scanned well before a start, such as C06 at 10:32. A distinct, earlier commitment time could explain C04 over C06 or B03 over B05. That is a hypothesis about decision timing, independent of scan lag.
4. **Resume preference, setup costs and resource-specific assignments.** B01 receives 45 active minutes (14 + 17 + 14) over 86 elapsed minutes from first start to completion. The two suspensions account for 41 minutes of elapsed delay. That measures B01's wait during other service. Any labor spent switching or restoring context is unmeasured. Such costs could favor continuing or resuming B01. A prior assignment of D10 to X could similarly remove an apparent opportunity on D-F.
5. **Escalation or aging changes the ranking.** C05's selection after its deadline and C01's long wait support testing conditional overrides. A breach trigger could act later than a predicted-breach trigger. An aging threshold could protect C01 despite C07's impending deadline. Neither threshold can be estimated from one apparent activation.
6. **Revised, noisy or strategically distorted estimates.** Dispatch may rank expected remaining time differently after inspection, interruption or an estimate update. For example, selecting C04 over C06 by live SEPT would require C04's then-visible estimate to rank at or below C06's despite arrival values of 22 and 6. Ordinary forecast error alone does not change the ranking of the numbers originally displayed. The recorded active durations agree with the listed estimates here. There is no direct evidence of revisions or strategic reporting.

# 7. Final synthesis

**QD is most identifiable at the routing level.** Its skill matrix makes specific assignments impossible and every observed service respects it. **QC is least identifiable.** Estimate order explains part of the trace, but a live SEPT rule fails on clearly waiting jobs, and the pivotal C05 choice aligns priority with deadline. QA gives the clearest evidence for an ordering rule among comparable jobs, namely FIFO among walk-ins with a due booking able to pass them.

The most dangerous operational inference would be to promise **automatic preemptive priority in QB**. B02 shows that an urgent arrival *can* displace ongoing work. B05 was already waiting when B01 stopped for reassignment, yet B03 received the server first. That decision contradicts a guarantee that the highest-priority waiting job will get the next available service.
