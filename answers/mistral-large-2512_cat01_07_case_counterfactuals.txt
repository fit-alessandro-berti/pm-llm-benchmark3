## 1. Feasibility model

### Partial-order relation set
```
# K-104 (high-risk, high-value, no originals, complex docs)
Register  Triage  {Identity Check, Document Check}  Assessment  Escalation Advice  Compliance Review  Decision  Notify
(Identity Check || Document Check)  // concurrent allowed
Compliance Review > Assessment      // final compliance must follow final assessment
Escalation Advice may precede or follow Assessment

# K-219 (standard, originals required, handwritten)
Register  Triage  Identity Check  Originals Receipt  Document Check  Assessment  Decision  Notify
Identity Check || Document Check   // only after originals arrive

# K-337 (high-risk, high-value, originals, slow supplier)
Register  Triage  Identity Check  Originals Receipt  Document Check 1  Assessment 1  Request More Info 1  Document Check 2  Assessment 2  [intervention: no Request More Info 2]  Compliance Review  Decision  Notify
Compliance Review > Assessment 2   // final compliance must follow final assessment

# K-411 (standard, no originals, digital)
Register  Triage  {Identity Check, Document Check}  Assessment  Decision  Notify
(Identity Check || Document Check)  // concurrent allowed
```

### Conditional and declarative constraints
```
- Four-eyes: final Assessment.resource  final Compliance Review.resource
- High-risk/high-value: Compliance Review mandatory after latest Assessment
- Originals required: Originals Receipt  Document Check
- No originals: Identity Check || Document Check allowed
- Rework: Request More Info  new Document Check  new Assessment  (new/reaffirmed Compliance Review if high-risk/high-value)
- Resource pools: each activity must be assigned to a resource from its pool
- No overlapping service intervals for the same resource
- Queue estimates are forecasts, not guarantees
```

### Intervention types
- **K-104 (earlier escalation)**: alters *timing* (earlier provisional compliance work) and *routing* (preliminary escalation packet to S1 before Assessment).
- **K-219 (different document resource)**: alters *routing* (D2 instead of D1) and may alter *timing* (earlier start but longer duration).
- **K-337 (suppress rework loop)**: alters *decision policy* (prohibits second rework) and *timing* (avoids external delay).
- **K-411 (parallelize verification)**: alters *timing* (earlier completion of checks) and *routing* (controlled workaround for concurrency).

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104
**Factual mechanism:**
Escalation and compliance work began only after Assessment, when the sanctions ambiguity was rediscovered. This delayed the final Compliance Review, which had to read evidence from scratch.

**Minimal intervention:**
At Triage (Mon 08:50), send a preliminary escalation packet to S1 and reserve a provisional C1 slot for Wed 08:00–14:00. S1 reviews the packet (Mon 16:00–18:00) and flags the sanctions ambiguity. C1 begins preliminary compliance work (Tue 08:00–12:00), reducing the final Compliance Review duration by 2–3 hours.

**Temporal propagation:**
- Identity Check and Document Check proceed as factual (I1: Mon 10:40–12:50; D1: Mon 21:40–Tue 05:40).
- Assessment (A1) starts at Tue 15:10 (unchanged) but now includes S1’s escalation advice, reducing ambiguity.
- Compliance Review (C1) starts at Wed 08:00 (vs. factual 13:10) and completes by Wed 12:00–14:00 (vs. factual 17:40). Decision and Notify occur on Wed afternoon.

**Process-feasibility check:**
- Four-eyes rule: A1  C1 (satisfied).
- Compliance Review follows final Assessment (satisfied; preliminary work does not replace final review).
- No resource overlaps: S1’s advisory work (Mon 16:00–18:00) does not conflict with other activities; C1’s provisional slot is reserved.

**Best/base/worst case:**
- Best: Preliminary work is accurate; C1 completes by Wed 12:00; cycle time = 3.5 days.
- Base: Preliminary work saves 2 hours; C1 completes by Wed 14:00; cycle time = 4.0 days.
- Worst: Preliminary packet is incomplete; C1 requires full 6 hours; cycle time = 5.5 days.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:**
- If C1’s provisional slot is preempted by a higher-priority case, the intervention fails.
- If S1’s preliminary advice is incorrect, the final Compliance Review may take longer than factual.

### K-219
**Factual mechanism:**
Document Check was assigned to D1 due to handwriting concerns, starting at Tue 09:30 (12 hours after originals arrived) and completing at Tue 13:30.

**Minimal intervention:**
Assign Document Check to D2 as soon as originals arrive (Mon 17:30). D2 starts at Mon 17:30 (vs. Tue 09:30 for D1) but takes longer (8–14 hours vs. D1’s 4–8 hours).

**Temporal propagation:**
- Identity Check (I2) completes at Mon 16:20 (unchanged).
- Document Check (D2) starts at Mon 17:30 and completes at Tue 01:30–07:30 (vs. D1’s Tue 13:30).
- Assessment (A2) starts at Tue 09:30–15:30 (vs. factual Tue 20:00) and completes by Tue 12:00–18:30.
- Decision and Notify occur on Tue afternoon or Wed morning.

**Process-feasibility check:**
- Originals Receipt  Document Check (satisfied).
- No resource overlaps: D2 is available at Mon 17:30.
- Rework: If D2 requests clarification, a new Document Check and Assessment are required.

**Best/base/worst case:**
- Best: D2 completes by Tue 01:30; Assessment starts at Tue 09:30; cycle time = 2.0 days.
- Base: D2 completes by Tue 04:30; Assessment starts at Tue 12:00; cycle time = 2.5 days.
- Worst: D2 requests rework; cycle time = 3.5–4.5 days.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:**
- If D2’s rework probability exceeds 30%, the intervention may increase cycle time.
- If D1 is idle at Mon 17:30, assigning to D1 would be faster.

### K-337
**Factual mechanism:**
Two rework loops due to supplier inconsistencies, extending cycle time to 8.5 days.

**Minimal intervention:**
After Assessment 2 (Fri 10:30), prohibit the second Request More Info and require a decision based on available evidence.

**Temporal propagation:**
- Document Check 2 completes at Thu 17:00; Assessment 2 starts at Fri 08:00 and completes at Fri 10:30.
- Compliance Review (C1) starts at Fri 11:00 (vs. factual Tue 15:30) and completes by Fri 15:00–18:00.
- Decision and Notify occur on Fri afternoon or Mon morning.

**Process-feasibility check:**
- Compliance Review follows final Assessment (satisfied).
- Four-eyes rule: A1  C1 (satisfied).
- Decision policy: Must address unresolved discrepancy (approve with conditions or reject).

**Best/base/worst case:**
- Best: Compliance Review completes by Fri 15:00; cycle time = 5.0 days.
- Base: Compliance Review completes by Fri 18:00; cycle time = 5.5 days.
- Worst: Decision is appealed; cycle time = 6.5+ days.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:**
- If the unresolved discrepancy is material, the intervention may increase compliance risk.
- If the supplier later provides exculpatory evidence, the decision may be overturned.

### K-411
**Factual mechanism:**
Case lock prevented Document Check until Identity Check completed, delaying Assessment.

**Minimal intervention:**
At Triage (Tue 09:35), create both verification tasks and allow concurrent execution via a controlled workaround.

**Temporal propagation:**
- Identity Check (I2) starts at Tue 11:30 and completes at Tue 13:20 (unchanged).
- Document Check (D2) starts at Tue 11:30 (vs. factual Tue 15:00) and completes at Tue 15:30–19:30.
- Assessment (A2) starts at Tue 16:00–20:00 (vs. factual Wed 09:30) and completes by Tue 18:30–22:30.
- Decision and Notify occur on Tue evening or Wed morning.

**Process-feasibility check:**
- Identity Check || Document Check (satisfied).
- No resource overlaps: I2 and D2 are distinct.
- Workaround must log timestamps correctly to avoid false concurrency.

**Best/base/worst case:**
- Best: Document Check completes by Tue 15:30; Assessment starts at Tue 16:00; cycle time = 1.2 days.
- Base: Document Check completes by Tue 17:30; Assessment starts at Tue 18:00; cycle time = 1.5 days.
- Worst: Assessment queue is congested; cycle time = 1.8 days.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:**
- If the workaround creates false timestamps, the intervention may fail compliance audits.
- If the assessment queue is congested, earlier completion may not reduce cycle time.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **K-104** | +S1 (1–2 h), +C1 (2–4 h) | Compliance (C1) | Identity, Document, Assessment | High-risk cases needing C1 | Negative if C1 is scarce | C1 reservation may delay other high-risk cases |
| **K-219** | +D2 (8–14 h), -D1 (4–8 h) | Document (D1) | Document (D2) | Cases needing D1 for complex docs | Mixed; depends on rework probability | D2’s slower speed may offset earlier start |
| **K-337** | -D1 (8–14 h), -A1 (2–5 h) | Document, Assessment | None | Cases needing rework | Positive; avoids external delays | Decision quality may degrade |
| **K-411** | +I2 (1.5–3.5 h), +D2 (4–8 h) | None | Assessment | Cases needing A2 | Negative if assessment queue is congested | Earlier completion may push cases into peak load |

**100-case weekly rollout:**
- **K-104**: Reserving C1 for 100 high-risk cases would monopolize ~200–400 hours of C1’s weekly capacity (C1 has ~40 hours/week). This would delay other high-risk cases by 2–5 days, creating a compliance bottleneck. Preliminary escalation work by S1 (100–200 hours) may also strain the Escalation pool.
- **K-219**: Substituting D2 for D1 on 100 standard cases would shift ~800–1400 hours of work from D1 to D2. D1’s queue would shrink, but D2’s queue would grow, and rework probability (20–40%) could add 20–40 rework loops, increasing total document-check demand by 160–560 hours.
- **K-337**: Suppressing rework on 100 high-risk cases would save ~800–1400 hours of document/assessment work and 800–3600 hours of external response time. However, compliance risk would increase, and appeals could offset savings.
- **K-411**: Parallelizing verification on 100 standard cases would shift ~150–350 hours from sequential to concurrent execution. Assessment queues would grow by ~200–500 hours, as cases complete earlier but arrive at the same congested queue. Weekend effects would amplify delays.

**Key nonlinearities:**
- **C1 scarcity**: K-104’s benefits are highly sensitive to C1’s availability. If C1 is already at 90% utilization, the intervention may fail.
- **Rework probability**: K-219’s success depends on D2’s rework rate. If rework exceeds 30%, the intervention may increase cycle time.
- **Assessment queue**: K-411’s benefits are limited by the assessment queue. If the queue is already congested, earlier completion may not reduce cycle time.
- **External response**: K-337’s savings depend on external response times. If responses are fast, the intervention’s impact is muted.

## 6. Pilot designs

| pilot element | **K-411 (parallelize verification)** | **K-219 (different document resource)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Eligibility** | Standard cases, no originals, digital documents | Standard cases, originals required, handwritten documents |
| **Assignment rule** | Randomly assign 50% of eligible cases to intervention | Randomly assign 50% of eligible cases to D2 (vs. D1) |
| **Sample/support logic** | 100 cases over 2 weeks; power to detect 0.5-day cycle time reduction | 100 cases over 2 weeks; power to detect 0.3-day cycle time reduction or 10% rework rate increase |
| **Primary outcome** | Cycle time reduction | Cycle time reduction |
| **Process-path outcome** | Proportion of cases with true concurrency (timestamps logged correctly) | Proportion of cases requiring rework |
| **Quality outcome** | Decision accuracy (appeals or errors) | Document accuracy (rework requests) |
| **Queue-spillover outcome** | Assessment queue length and wait time | Document queue length and wait time for D1/D2 |
| **Logging validation** | Audit timestamps for concurrency; verify no false overlaps | Audit rework requests and document accuracy |
| **Stopping rule** | Stop if >10% of intervention cases have false concurrency or if assessment queue grows by >20% | Stop if rework rate exceeds 30% or if D1’s queue shrinks by >50% |

## 7. Final synthesis
The **most robust intervention** is **K-411 (parallelize verification)**, as it relies on a feasible workaround for concurrency, has minimal compliance risk, and its benefits are insensitive to external factors. Its principal risk—assessment queue congestion—is measurable and reversible.

The **most dangerous intervention** is **K-337 (suppress rework loop)**, despite its potential for significant cycle time reduction. The decision policy change is irreversible and may increase compliance risk, appeals, or loss exposure. Its benefits are highly sensitive to the materiality of unresolved discrepancies.

The **least transportable counterfactual** is **K-104 (earlier escalation/compliance preparation)**. While locally beneficial, its reliance on scarce C1 capacity makes it unscalable. A system-wide rollout would create a compliance bottleneck, delaying other high-risk cases.

**Additional data needs:**
1. **Queue arrival patterns**: To model how interventions shift congestion (e.g., K-411’s impact on assessment queues).
2. **Rework probabilities**: To quantify K-219’s trade-offs between D1 and D2.
3. **Compliance risk metrics**: To assess K-337’s impact on decision quality.
4. **Resource utilization**: To evaluate K-104’s feasibility under C1 scarcity.
5. **External response times**: To model K-337’s savings from avoided rework.

## 8. Assumption audit
1. **Structural**: The partial-order relations (e.g., Compliance Review > Assessment) hold for all cases. *(Violation would break compliance rules.)*
2. **Temporal**: Queue estimates are forecasts, not guarantees, and service times follow the provided ranges. *(Violation would invalidate cycle time predictions.)*
3. **Resource**: Resource pools are static, and no resources are added or removed during the analysis window. *(Violation would alter capacity.)*
4. **Behavioral**: Resources do not strategically delay or expedite cases based on interventions. *(Violation would bias queue effects.)*
5. **Quality**: Preliminary compliance work (K-104) is sufficiently accurate to reduce final review duration. *(Violation would negate benefits.)*
6. **Interference**: Non-target cases are unaffected by single-case interventions. *(Violation would require system-wide modeling.)*
7. **Quality**: D2’s rework probability for handwritten documents is 20–40%. *(Violation would alter K-219’s trade-offs.)*
8. **Interference**: Assessment queue congestion does not worsen nonlinearly with earlier case completion (K-411). *(Violation would amplify adverse effects.)*
