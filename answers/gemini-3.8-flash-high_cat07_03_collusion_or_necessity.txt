# 2. Benign alternative explanations

Evidential rigor requires evaluating alternative operational and technological hypotheses before concluding that anomalies stem from collusion or intentional misconduct. Below are benign explanations for each major suspicious pattern, along with the evidence required to support or refute them.

### Pattern 1: Rapid Vendor Bank Modification Followed Immediately by Payment Approval (VendorMaster_Lena & AP_Kim on Vendor Family V-77x)
* **Benign Explanation 1: Vendor Banking Migration with Escalated Payment Clearance.**
  * *Context:* Vendor family V-77x underwent an enterprise-wide consolidation of its banking platforms across multiple operating subsidiaries. Existing scheduled automated payment runs failed due to invalid beneficiary details, halting critical supply deliveries. Lena executed an authorized mass master data update using standardized upload templates, and Kim (assigned specifically to higher-risk/complex vendor portfolios) immediately approved the pending disbursements to clear the backlog and avoid supply chain disruption.
  * *Evidence to Support:* IT/treasury change tickets referencing V-77x's banking migration; official notification letters on vendor bank letterhead signed by authorized corporate officers; documented dual-custody callback verification logs conducted prior to the file upload; clearing house (SEPA/SWIFT) return/rejection error logs preceding the bank update.
  * *Evidence to Refute:* Beneficiary bank account names differing from registered vendor corporate entities; routing numbers associated with high-risk offshore jurisdictions or employee personal accounts; absence of external vendor change requests; lack of callback logs; bank accounts reverted to prior details immediately following payment disbursement.
* **Benign Explanation 2: Division of Labor in High-Risk Supplier Remediation via Manual Integration.**
  * *Context:* Kim is formally assigned to handle complex, high-risk suppliers who frequently require manual exception processing due to legacy invoicing formats, while Lena is the primary master data clerk handling banking updates. Their high co-occurrence (44 cases) and use of `manual_upload` stems from legacy system integration deficits rather than personal collusion.
  * *Evidence to Support:* Formal job descriptions and organizational charts showing Kim designated as the primary AP specialist for the V-77x vendor group; standard operating procedures (SOPs) permitting manual template uploads for legacy multi-subsidiary accounts; matching 3-way documentation (valid POs, delivery receipts, verified tax invoices).
  * *Evidence to Refute:* Other AP specialists handling V-77x payments without manual uploads; unrecorded bank modifications lacking supporting master data tickets; invoice line items with inflated pricing or vague service descriptions.

### Pattern 2: Accelerated PO Approvals and Four-Eyes Exceptions (Buyer_Carla & Approver_Gita; Buyer_Dan & Manager_Hugo)
* **Benign Explanation 1: Operational Necessity in Life-Safety and Critical Facilities Emergencies.**
  * *Context:* Carla specializes in emergency medical supplies where an 8-minute turnaround is essential for patient care; Dan handles emergency facilities repairs (e.g., HVAC failure, structural damage, water pipe bursts on weekends) requiring immediate contractor dispatch. Gita (delegated urgent approver) and Hugo (manager handling emergency overrides) prioritize these urgent queues via direct telephone or radio communication, authorizing POs immediately upon creation.
  * *Evidence to Support:* High correlation with `emergency_flag = True` (88% for Carla/Gita, 79% for Dan/Hugo); facilities work-order logs and hospital department clinical incident tickets matching case timestamps; Dan’s approvals concentrated during off-hours, weekends, or severe weather events; physical proof of urgent delivery.
  * *Evidence to Refute:* PO requisitions for routine consumable stock, discretionary capital equipment, or non-urgent services; delivery dates occurring weeks or months after "emergency" approval; price markups significantly above master contract rate sheets without emergency surcharge clauses.
* **Benign Explanation 2: Data-Quality Distortion from Legacy ERP Interface Mapping and Account Sharing.**
  * *Context:* The apparent 42 four-eyes violations (`Create Purchase Order` + `Approve Purchase Order`) are technical artifacts. In `legacy_erp`, `Bot_PO` generated purchase orders from approved requisitions, but the interface mapped the human approver's user ID into `org:resource` for both creation and approval. Furthermore, Gita's shared administrative profile was utilized by assistants to pre-screen and submit orders, which Gita then authorized.
  * *Evidence to Support:* Audit trail field mismatches where `entered_by = "Bot_PO"` or assistant usernames, while `approved_by` lists Gita or Hugo; distinct IP addresses and workstation IDs for creation and approval actions; technical change logs or defect tickets documenting legacy ERP field mapping errors.
  * *Evidence to Refute:* Identical IP addresses, identical Windows user session tokens, and identical MAC addresses for both creation and approval actions under human logins; manual textual comments entered by the same individual across both events; absence of bot activity logs in the backend ERP database.

### Pattern 3: Self-Justification of Emergency Overrides (Manager_Hugo & Approver_Gita)
* **Benign Explanation 1: Bulk Migration of Offline Governance Records at Quarter-Close.**
  * *Context:* During emergency operations, managers authorized overrides verbally or via paper logs. At quarter-end, compliance personnel or administrative assistants performed a bulk data entry clean-up from a central spreadsheet (`quarter_close_upload`) to reconcile compliance records. The upload script defaulted `org:resource` to the uploading manager, resulting in 12 of the 17 cases sharing identical minute timestamps.
  * *Evidence to Support:* Identical minute timestamps across 12 cases; batch ID metadata linking records to a single bulk upload file; attached physical emergency memos bearing contemporaneous signatures of secondary reviewers; quarter-end compliance reconciliations explicitly noting the bulk digitization effort.
  * *Evidence to Refute:* Individual, non-batch entries where override and justification were executed sequentially within minutes during regular operating hours (the remaining 5 cases); absence of underlying offline emergency documentation; generic or copied justification text without case-specific incident details.
* **Benign Explanation 2: Process Ambiguity in ERP Workflow Task Assignments.**
  * *Context:* Operating procedures directed authorizing managers to "document the business rationale for emergency overrides." Managers interpreted the subsequent "Retrospective Justification" task as a self-documentation step to record why they initiated the override, rather than recognizing it as a control gate requiring independent managerial ratification.
  * *Evidence to Support:* ERP workflow configuration routing the justification task back to the initiator’s inbox by default; internal training decks instructing managers to "enter your retrospective justification notes within 3 days"; substantive, detailed incident notes entered in justification fields.
  * *Evidence to Refute:* Explicit ERP alert messages requiring secondary sign-off that were bypassed; uninformative justification comments (e.g., "approved", "ok", "urgent"); prior written audit reprimands issued to Hugo or Gita regarding self-justification.

### Pattern 4: Recurrent Subcontracting Loops ($A \rightarrow B \rightarrow A$)
* **Benign Explanation 1: Legitimate Interactive Negotiation, Quote Clarification, and Specification Tuning.**
  * *Context:* In emergency procurement (Gita → Carla → Gita, 36 loops; Hugo → Dan → Hugo, 29 loops), initial emergency requisitions frequently lack required attachments, contractor rate breakdowns, or delivery confirmations. Approvers reject or return the PO to the buyer to update terms, attach quotes, or reduce quantities, followed by re-submission and final approval.
  * *Evidence to Support:* System field change logs showing PO line item edits, dollar amount adjustments, or newly attached vendor quote documents between the first and second approver touchpoints; audit trail comments containing substantive back-and-forth dialogue.
  * *Evidence to Refute:* Zero changes in PO line items, unit prices, or total amounts between iterations; loop durations of under 30 seconds with no textual comments (suggestive of robotic clicking or scripted approval bypass).
* **Benign Explanation 2: Standard Operational Pod Alignment and Automated Matching Workflows.**
  * *Context:* For routine pairs (Aline/Elena, 33 loops; Boris/Felix, 30 loops), rework occurs in ~5% of their ~650 cases, representing the natural baseline error rate in procurement operations. For `Bot_Match` → `AP_Kim` → `Bot_Match` (52 loops), this is the intentional system design: when automated 3-way matching detects price/quantity variances exceeding tolerance, it routes an exception to AP_Kim, who enters credit notes or verifies freight surcharges, returning the case to the bot for automated posting.
  * *Evidence to Support:* Exception codes generated by `Bot_Match` matching standard invoice variance categories; formal team charters establishing Team A (Aline/Elena) and Team B (Boris/Felix) operating pods; variance rates aligning with industry baseline benchmarks.
  * *Evidence to Refute:* Invoices cleared by Kim where variance thresholds were overridden without supporting supplier credit notes or PO amendments.

### Pattern 5: Dual Role in Master Data: Vendor Check + Bank Details Modification (Lena & Marc)
* **Benign Explanation 1: Small-Team Operational Cross-Coverage and Capacity Bottlenecks.**
  * *Context:* The master data team comprises only two individuals (Lena and Marc). During annual leave, sick leave, or month-end volume surges, single analysts handle both supplier onboarding checks and initial banking setups to prevent vendor onboarding paralysis.
  * *Evidence to Support:* HR absence and leave calendars showing one analyst on leave during the 31 dual-role occurrences; formal management coverage delegation memos; 100% presence of complete onboarding packets in the Master Data Management (MDM) repository.
  * *Evidence to Refute:* Dual-role actions executed when both analysts were present and active in the system; concentration of dual-role actions exclusively on specific high-risk vendors.
* **Benign Explanation 2: Co-Location of Duties within Master Data Role Architecture.**
  * *Context:* Organizational role definitions inside the MDM system assign both tasks to the "Master Data Specialist" role, relying on secondary verification downstream in Accounts Payable rather than intra-team segregation.
  * *Evidence to Support:* MDM security matrix and role-based access control (RBAC) documentation defining `Vendor Check` and `Bank Maintenance` under a unified permission profile; formal policies assigning SoD enforcement to the AP payment release gate.
  * *Evidence to Refute:* Corporate policies explicitly mandating two distinct master data personnel for onboarding vs. banking data entry.

### High-Priority Case Clusters (Immediate Substantive & Forensic Testing)

#### Cluster 1A: VendorMaster_Lena + AP_Kim Bank Modification & Rapid Disbursement Cluster
* **Population:** All 44 cases involving Lena and Kim, with top priority on:
  1. The 16 fast-approval cases (median turnaround of 17 minutes via `manual_upload`).
  2. The 9 direct SoD violations where the same user performed bank change and payment release.
  3. The 11 subcontracting rework loops (`Lena` → `Kim` → `Lena`).
* **Reason for Priority:** Represents the single greatest financial risk in the log. Combines vendor master modification, rapid cash disbursement, 68% high-value band, 72% concentration in vendor family V-77x, and 83% ingestion via manual spreadsheet uploads. Directly circumvents disbursement defense layers.
* **What Documents & Audit-Trail Fields to Inspect First:**
  * *Banking Documents:* External bank confirmation letters; signed vendor bank change requests; telephone callback verification forms (inspecting the recorded phone number against independent public registry data, call timestamps, and named vendor contacts).
  * *Audit-Trail & System Fields:* Source spreadsheet file metadata (author, creation date, modification history); server logs for `source_system = "manual_upload"`; Active Directory session logs, user IP addresses, workstation MAC addresses, and multi-factor authentication (MFA) transaction IDs for Lena and Kim during the 17-minute windows; `entered_by` vs. `approved_by` database fields.
  * *Treasury Records:* Electronic funds transfer (EFT/SWIFT) output logs to verify beneficiary account owner name, clearing bank IBAN, and confirmation that funds cleared to the corporate vendor rather than an intermediary account.

#### Cluster 1B: Confirmed Human Four-Eyes Violations in Purchase Order Approval
* **Population:** Cases within the 42 `Create Purchase Order` + `Approve Purchase Order` violations where backend database logs confirm creation by a human buyer rather than `Bot_PO`.
* **Reason for Priority:** Direct breakdown of preventative commitment controls. Authorizing one's own purchase order enables unauthorized expenditure, contract splitting, and potential vendor favoritism without supervisory approval.
* **What Documents & Audit-Trail Fields to Inspect First:**
  * *Audit-Trail Fields:* Reconcile `entered_by`, `approved_by`, and `org:resource` against `source_system`. If `source_system = "legacy_erp"` and `entered_by = "Bot_PO"`, divert case to Cluster 3B. If `entered_by` matches the human approver's personal corporate login ID, retain in Cluster 1B.
  * *Commercial Documents:* Original purchase requisition forms; competitive quotes or tender waiver forms; proof of independent goods receipt (`Goods Receipt` notes signed by warehouse or departmental receiving staff); inventory receipt ledgers.

### Medium-Priority Case Clusters (Operational Compliance & Control Effectiveness Testing)

#### Cluster 2A: Emergency Overrides with Overdue Retrospective Justifications
* **Population:** Emergency cases handled by Carla/Gita (18% late, ~23 cases) and Dan/Hugo (21% late, ~22 cases) where `Retrospective Justification` occurred >3 working days post-override, or remains missing.

* **What Documents & Audit-Trail Fields to Inspect First:**
  * *Audit-Trail Fields:* Calculate exact business duration: `time:timestamp` of `Retrospective Justification` minus `time:timestamp` of `Emergency Override`. Check whether the resource executing the justification differed from the override author.
  * *Operational Documents:* Hospital clinical incident logs or facilities maintenance emergency call logs; third-party emergency call-out dispatch sheets; formal written justifications.

#### Cluster 2B: Real-Time Self-Justification of Emergency Overrides
* **Population:** The 5 cases where `Emergency Override` and `Retrospective Justification` were performed by the same resource (Hugo or Gita) in real-time (median 9 minutes), excluding the 12 batch-uploaded cases.
* **Reason for Priority:** Indicates intentional bypass of secondary oversight during live transactional execution, converting supervisory controls into meaningless self-authorization.
* **What Documents & Audit-Trail Fields to Inspect First:**
  * *System Logs:* User interaction clickstream logs; session tokens; justification text content (evaluating whether detailed notes were provided or generic boilerplate text was entered).
  * *Governance Documents:* Delegation of Authority (DOA) schedules to verify whether single-signature emergency authority limits were exceeded.

#### Cluster 2C: Shared Administrative Account Usage ("Approver_Gita")
* **Population:** The 23 emergency approval cases where `org:resource = "Approver_Gita"` but detailed audit trail fields record assistant user IDs in `entered_by`.

* **What Documents & Audit-Trail Fields to Inspect First:**
  * *Identity & Access Management (IAM) Logs:* Concurrent Active Directory logins under "Approver_Gita" from disparate IP addresses or physical locations; workstation terminal IDs; formal delegation-of-authority forms granting assistants temporary approval proxy rights.

#### Cluster 2D: Master Data Dual Onboarding & Banking Modifications
* **Population:** The 31 cases where Lena or Marc performed both `Vendor Check` and `Change Vendor Bank Details`.
* **Reason for Priority:** Concentration of master data lifecycle custody. Creates a single point of failure in vendor onboarding and maintenance.
* **What Documents & Audit-Trail Fields to Inspect First:**

# 4. Network and conformance analyses

To support continuous auditing and forensic investigation, the following Python analyses leverage PM4Py (version 2.7+) and Pandas to programmatically detect control violations, extract organizational networks, compare process variants, evaluate timing performance, and isolate source-system distortions.

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

# ==============================================================================
# 0. DATA PREPARATION & LOG FORMATTING
# ==============================================================================
def load_and_prepare_log(file_path: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Loads procurement event log, standardizes column types, and formats
    dataframe for PM4Py processing.
    """
    df = pd.read_csv(file_path)
    df['time:timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(df['time:timestamp'])
    
    # Ensure boolean and categorical types are properly cast
    df['emergency_flag'] = df['emergency_flag'].astype(str).str.lower().isin(['true', '1'])
    
    # Format dataframe using PM4Py standard naming
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id='case:concept:name',
        activity_key='concept:name',
        timestamp_key='time:timestamp'
    )
    return df

# ==============================================================================
# 1. FILTERING FOUR-EYES PRINCIPLE VIOLATIONS
# ==============================================================================
def analyze_four_eyes_violations(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """
    Identifies cases violating four-eyes segregation of duties using
    pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle.
    """
    # Check 1: PO Creation vs PO Approval
    po_violations = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
        df,
        activity1="Create Purchase Order",
        activity2="Approve Purchase Order",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        resource_key="org:resource",
        keep_violations=True
    )
    po_violating_cases = po_violations['case:concept:name'].nunique()
    print(f"[!] Four-Eyes PO Violations Detected: {po_violating_cases} cases")
    
    # Check 2: Vendor Bank Detail Change vs Payment Approval (CRITICAL RISK)
    bank_payment_violations = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
        df,
        activity1="Change Vendor Bank Details",
        activity2="Payment Approval",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        resource_key="org:resource",
        keep_violations=True
    )
    bp_violating_cases = bank_payment_violations['case:concept:name'].nunique()
    print(f"[CRITICAL] Four-Eyes Bank Detail + Payment Approval Violations: {bp_violating_cases} cases")
    
    # Display resource and vendor breakdown for critical violations
    if bp_violating_cases > 0:
        breakdown = bank_payment_violations[
            bank_payment_violations['concept:name'].isin(['Change Vendor Bank Details', 'Payment Approval'])
        ].groupby(['case:concept:name', 'vendor_id', 'org:resource'])['concept:name'].count()
        print("\nCritical Bank/Payment Violation Details:\n", breakdown)
        
    return po_violations, bank_payment_violations

# ==============================================================================
# 2. DISCOVERING HANDOVER, WORKING-TOGETHER, & SUBCONTRACTING NETWORKS
# ==============================================================================
def analyze_organizational_networks(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """
    Extracts social network metrics to evaluate organizational relationships,
    handover dependencies, and potential collusion patterns.
    """
    # A. Handover of Work Network
    handover_net = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
        df,
        beta=0,
        resource_key="org:resource",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name"
    )
    
    # B. Working-Together Network (Co-occurrence within cases)
    working_together_net = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(
        df,
        resource_key="org:resource",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name"
    )
    
    # C. Subcontracting Network (n=2 Ping-Pong loops: A -> B -> A)
    subcontracting_net = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(
        df,
        n=2,
        resource_key="org:resource",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name"
    )
    
    print("\nSocial Network Analysis Completed:")
    print(f" - Handover Network Nodes: {len(handover_net.nodes)}")
    print(f" - Working-Together Network Nodes: {len(working_together_net.nodes)}")
    print(f" - Subcontracting Network Nodes: {len(subcontracting_net.nodes)}")
    
    return handover_net, working_together_net, subcontracting_net

# ==============================================================================
# 3. COMPARING EMERGENCY AND NON-EMERGENCY COHORTS
# ==============================================================================
def compare_emergency_cohorts(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """
    Splits log into emergency vs. non-emergency cohorts and compares
    process throughput, duration, and variant structures.
    """
    # Use pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values with level="case"
    emergency_df = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        df,
        attribute_key="emergency_flag",
        values=[True, "True", "true", 1],
        level="case",
        retain=True,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name"
    )
    
    non_emergency_df = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        df,
        attribute_key="emergency_flag",
        values=[True, "True", "true", 1],
        level="case",
        retain=False,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name"
    )
    
    # Throughput and case duration comparison
    for name, cohort in [("Emergency", emergency_df), ("Non-Emergency", non_emergency_df)]:
        cases = cohort['case:concept:name'].nunique()
        durations = cohort.groupby('case:concept:name')['time:timestamp'].agg(lambda x: (x.max() - x.min()).total_seconds() / 3600.0)
        median_dur = durations.median() if not durations.empty else 0
        variants = len(pm4py.get_variants(cohort))
        print(f"Cohort [{name}]: Cases={cases}, Median Duration={median_dur:.2f} hours, Variants={variants}")
        
    return emergency_df, non_emergency_df

# 5. CONFORMANCE CHECKING FOR EMERGENCY RETROSPECTIVE JUSTIFICATION
# ==============================================================================
def audit_emergency_retrospective_justification(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """
    Builds declarative and log-skeleton checks to enforce that:
    1. Every 'Emergency Override' is followed by 'Retrospective Justification'.
    2. Justification occurs within 3 working days (72 hours).
    3. Justification is executed by an INDEPENDENT resource (Four-Eyes on Governance).
    """
    # A. Log Skeleton Conformance: Co-existence / Equivalence
    skeleton = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(df, noise_threshold=0.0)
    conformance_skel = pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton(df, skeleton)
    
    # B. Programmatic Declarative Temporal & SoD Rule
    cases = df.groupby('case:concept:name')
    audit_results = []
    
    for case_id, group in cases:
        acts = group['concept:name'].tolist()
        if "Emergency Override" in acts:
            override_events = group[group['concept:name'] == "Emergency Override"]
            just_events = group[group['concept:name'] == "Retrospective Justification"]
            
            for _, o_row in override_events.iterrows():
                o_time = o_row['time:timestamp']
                o_res = o_row['org:resource']
                
                # Check for corresponding justification
                subsequent_just = just_events[just_events['time:timestamp'] >= o_time]
                if subsequent_just.empty:
                    audit_results.append({
                        'case_id': case_id,
                        'violation': 'Missing Retrospective Justification',
                        'severity': 'Critical',
                        'hours_elapsed': np.nan,
                        'override_res': o_res,
                        'just_res': None
                    })
                else:
                    j_row = subsequent_just.iloc[0]
                    j_time = j_row['time:timestamp']
                    j_res = j_row['org:resource']
                    delta_hours = (j_time - o_time).total_seconds() / 3600.0
                    
                    # Rule 1: Must be within 3 working days (72 hours)
                    if delta_hours > 72.0:
                        audit_results.append({
                            'case_id': case_id,
                            'violation': 'Overdue Retrospective Justification (>72h)',
                            'severity': 'Medium',
                            'hours_elapsed': round(delta_hours, 2),
                            'override_res': o_res,
                            'just_res': j_res
                        })
                    
                    # Rule 2: Must be different human resource
                    if o_res == j_res:
                        audit_results.append({
                            'case_id': case_id,
                            'violation': 'Self-Justification (Four-Eyes Breach on Emergency)',
                            'severity': 'High',
                            'hours_elapsed': round(delta_hours, 2),
                            'override_res': o_res,
                            'just_res': j_res
                        })
                        
    results_df = pd.DataFrame(audit_results)
    print("\nEmergency Governance Conformance Summary:\n", results_df['violation'].value_counts())
    return results_df

def isolate_source_system_and_mapping_effects(df: pd.DataFrame):

    gita_events = df[df['org:resource'] == 'Approver_Gita']
    shared_account_usage = gita_events[
        gita_events['entered_by'].notnull() &

    ]
    print(f"[Identity Governance] 'Approver_Gita' Events Executed by Assistants ({shared_account_usage['entered_by'].unique()}): {len(shared_account_usage)} events")
    
    # D. Detect Quarter-End Bulk Justification Uploads
    quarter_close = df[df['source_system'] == 'quarter_close_upload']

    print(f"[Data-Quality Artifact] Quarter-Close Upload Justifications Sharing Identical Timestamps: {bulk_timestamp_count} events")
    
    return system_profile, bot_mismatches, shared_account_usage
```

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

During our continuous audit testing of the procure-to-pay process, we identified several significant control exceptions across segregation of duties (SoD), emergency authorizations, and master data management. Crucially, these anomalies reflect control design and operating effectiveness deficiencies rather than substantiated fraudulent misconduct.

The most concerning pattern involves the VendorMaster_Lena and AP_Kim subgroup, where rapid succession between vendor bank detail modifications and subsequent payment approvals occurred (median 17 minutes), alongside nine direct SoD violations and eleven subcontracting loops. This concentration is heavily focused on vendor family V-77x (72%) and executed via manual spreadsheet uploads (83%), presenting heightened financial exposure and control circumvention risks.

Conversely, apparent four-eyes exceptions and accelerated approval cycles within emergency procurement (Buyer_Carla/Approver_Gita and Buyer_Dan/Manager_Hugo) are substantially consistent with operational urgency in clinical supplies and off-hours facility repairs, rather than illicit collaboration. However, post-incident governance is compromised by recurring delays in retrospective justifications and instances of self-justification.

These analytical findings are subject to notable data-quality limitations. Specifically, shared credential usage under Approver_Gita, legacy ERP interface mapping errors that conflate automated bot creation with human approver identities, and quarter-end batch upload migrations obscure genuine event attribution and inflate synthetic violation rates. Furthermore, legitimate corporate bank migrations for V-77x lack explicit tracking links within the procurement log.

We recommend targeted follow-up: immediately freeze manual upload privileges for vendor master data and disbursements; perform substantive audit procedures—including independent vendor callbacks, physical receiving verification, and banking confirmation—on all V-77x disbursements; remediate ERP identity mapping scripts; enforce system-level technical controls preventing self-justification of emergency overrides; and eliminate shared administrative user accounts.
