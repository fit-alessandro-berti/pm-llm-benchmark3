**Process-feasibility check:** Four-eyes preserved (A1 final Assessment, C1 final Compliance). Preliminary work is advisory + background reading only — it does not sign off. C1 capacity reservation may violate FIFO if another case is mid-queue; that case will be delayed by ~3 h.

**Best/base/worst case:** *Best* — preliminary packet is complete, C1 starts Wed 08:00 sharp, Compliance_Review ends Wed 10:00, Decision Wed 10:30, Notify Wed 10:40 (~74 h, ~8 h). *Base* — as table (~76.5 h). *Worst* — packet is thin, C1 still has to read evidence from scratch, savings shrink to ~1 h and reservation cost remains.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** (i) Snapshots showing C1's queue at Wed 08:00 still >10 h would mean reservation does not produce earlier start; (ii) post-hoc review of preliminary packet content showing <1 h of substantive material; (iii) an unrelated high-risk case actually displaced by >24 h.

### K-219
**Factual mechanism:** Handwriting difficulty routed Document_Check to D1; D1's Mon-16:00 queue was 11 h, so the case waited overnight and D1's complex-document skill finished in 4 h (Tue 09:30–13:30) — accurate, no rework. Total cycle ~72 h.

**Minimal intervention:** Triage routes the case to D2 the moment Originals_Receipt completes (Mon 17:30). D2 starts when its own queue permits (~Mon 22:30 given the 5 h queue at Mon 16:00).

**Temporal propagation:** The savings come from D2's queue being ~6 h shorter than D1's at Mon 16:00 (5 h vs 11 h) and Originals already being present. The cost is D2's longer service on handwriting: 6–12 h vs D1's 4 h. Net queue advantage (~6 h) is partially offset by service penalty (~2–8 h). Net expected savings in clean cases: ~6–10 h, but A2's queue (6 h at Tue 08:00) caps how much earlier Assessment can start.

**Process-feasibility check:** No high-risk  no Compliance_Review required. Originals_Receipt still precedes Document_Check. Identity_Check unaffected (I2's queue independent).

**Best/base/worst case:** *Best* — handwriting legible, D2 finishes Tue 04:30, A2 starts Tue 10:30 (after 6 h queue), Decision Tue 17:30 (~57 h, 15 h). *Base* — D2 finishes Tue 07:30, A2 starts Tue 13:30, Decision Tue 19:00 (~63 h, 9 h). *Worst* — D2 requests clarification  RMI (8–36 h) + new Document_Check + new Assessment  ~120 h, +48 h vs factual.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** (i) D2 historical rework rate on handwritten documents >25%; (ii) D1's queue forecast on the day is <6 h, removing the queue advantage; (iii) A2 queue unexpectedly >10 h, swallowing any savings.

### K-337
**Factual mechanism:** Two rework loops; the second RMI spanned a supplier weekend (Fri 10:30  Mon 09:00 = 46.5 h). Despite the rework, Compliance_Review landed on Tue 15:30, when C1's queue was emptying (Tue 08:00 = 16 h  Wed 08:00 = 8 h). Total ~46 h.

**Minimal intervention:** After Assessment 2 (Fri 10:30), do not issue RMI 2. Proceed to Compliance_Review, then Decision with conditions or reject. The intervention does **not** speed up Compliance_Review itself; it only removes the second rework loop.

**Temporal propagation:** Removing the rework saves ~52 h of activity (weekend + Doc_Check 3 + Assessment 3 + their queues). However, Compliance_Review can no longer happen on Tue (which was the *consequence* of Assessment 3 happening on Tue). Compliance_Review is now forced to wait for Assessment 2 (Fri 10:30) and then for C1's queue on Fri afternoon. C1's Fri queue is likely 2–3 h (extrapolating from Thu 08:00 = 5 h with continued clearance). So Compliance_Review starts Fri 13:30–14:00 and ends Fri 17:30–18:00. Decision + Notify same evening.

This produces a *counter-intuitive* result: the intervention **lengthens** cycle time in this case by ~30–60 h. The rework loop, despite adding work, allowed the case to land on a low-queue day for C1. The intervention trades an external bottleneck (supplier weekend) for an internal one (C1's Fri queue).

**Process-feasibility check:** High-risk + high-value  Compliance_Review mandatory after Assessment 2. Four-eyes preserved (A1  C1). Decision must explicitly address the remaining inconsistency; "silent approval" is forbidden by the intervention text.

**Best/base/worst case:** *Best* — C1's Fri queue is genuinely 1–2 h; Compliance_Review ends Fri 16:00; Decision Fri 16:30; cycle ~96 h (+50 h vs factual). *Base* — as table (~104 h, +58 h). *Worst* — C1's Fri queue unexpectedly >8 h; Compliance_Review slips to Mon next week; cycle ~200 h (+154 h).

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** (i) Snapshot showing C1's Fri-morning queue <2 h AND preliminary compliance work having already saved 2–3 h; (ii) evidence that the "one remaining inconsistency" is immaterial and approvable as a clean approval (then cycle savings of ~52 h are real); (iii) ability to schedule C1 to start Compliance_Review on Thu (parallel with Assessment 2 prep) — not currently allowed by the rules.

### K-411
**Factual mechanism:** The legacy case lock created only the Identity_Check task at Triage. Document_Check was queued behind it. Sequential execution pushed Document_Check to Tue 15:00–19:30, Assessment to Wed 09:30–12:00, and Decision to Wed 14:00.

**Minimal intervention:** At Triage, T2 creates both verification tasks. A controlled workaround (not silent unlocking, but a logged dual-ownership flag) lets I2 and D2 begin in parallel. Real-time timestamps are required so that the case-mining log shows true concurrency rather than batch-entered times.

**Temporal propagation:** Document_Check starts immediately after Triage (Tue ~11:30 with D2's 2 h queue), runs in parallel with Identity_Check. Standard digital docs: D2 4–8 h, so Doc_Check ends Tue 15:30–19:30 (vs Tue 19:30 in factual — only a few hours earlier because Doc_Check already started early Tue in factual). The bigger savings come from the case being *Assessment-ready* by Tue afternoon instead of Wed morning, freeing Assessment's queue slot earlier. A2's Tue 08:00 queue is 6 h, so Assessment starts ~Tue 16:00 and ends Tue 18:00–21:00. Decision same evening or next morning.

**Process-feasibility check:** No originals  no Originals_Receipt. No high-risk  no Compliance_Review. Four-eyes irrelevant (no compliance). Concurrency must be *real*, not logged retroactively; otherwise process-mining conclusions are wrong.

**Best/base/worst case:** *Best* — D2 service 4 h, A2 service 2.5 h, A2 queue short; cycle ~10 h (19 h vs factual). *Base* — as table (~11 h, 18 h). *Worst* — D2 service 8 h, A2 queue 6 h, A2 service 5 h; cycle ~22 h (7 h).

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** (i) A2 queue unexpectedly >10 h (bottleneck migration wipes out most savings); (ii) timestamp audit showing batch-entry instead of real-time (false concurrency; process mining is corrupted but cycle time might still improve); (iii) workaround bug causing one task to silently overwrite the other (data integrity issue).

## 4. Capacity and Interference Analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **K-104** | +0.5–2 h C1 preliminary prep per case; +1 reserved C1 slot per high-risk case | none directly; marginal relief on Compliance_Review duration | C1 (reservation displaces non-target high-risk cases); S1 advisory load | Other high-risk/high-value cases awaiting C1; S1's other advisory clients | **Negative at scale** (C1 has no substitute; reservations become infeasible) | C1 is a single human; reservation cost is convex — 10 reservations are fine, 100 collapse the queue |
| **K-219** | D2 +5–15 h per case; D1 4 h per case | D1 (becomes idle) | D2 (large) | Cases routed to D2 for other reasons; complex-corporate cases that benefit from D1 | **Negative at scale** (D2 is structurally slower on this work type) | Rework probability on handwriting is non-linear in volume — slight overload tips into repeated clarifications |
| **K-337** | 8–36 h external response per case; +3–6 h C1 per case | External supplier queue (fewer RMI 2) | C1 (more cases reach Compliance_Review faster) | Other high-risk cases; supplier relationship management; downstream appeal/regulator cases | **Mixed** (faster per-case, but C1 may saturate; quality risk is the binding constraint) | C1 throughput has a hard ceiling; quality degradation from forced decisions is non-linear once inconsistency rate >15% |
| **K-411** | I2 + D2 simultaneous per case (no net extra service); A2 +0.5–2 h earlier demand per case | I2/D2 per-case (faster completion) | A2 (cases arrive sooner); I2 and D2 simultaneous occupancy displaces others | Other cases needing I2 or D2; A2's other queued cases | **Positive if A2 has slack; negative if A2 saturates** (bottleneck migrates predictably) | A2 has 4–5 h of new arrivals/day; pushing 100 cases 18 h earlier creates a one-time spike that can be absorbed |

**100-case weekly rollout (250–400 word analysis):**

If K-104's policy is applied to 100 similar high-risk cases in one week, the bottleneck does not move — it hardens. C1 is the only fully certified compliance reviewer and his queue is already clearing from 20 h (Mon) to 5 h (Thu). Reserving slots for 100 cases means roughly 100 × 3 h = 300 h of C1 time pre-committed against ~32 h of available capacity. Most reservations will not be honored; cases will either wait their turn or be forced through a degraded compliance review. The intervention looks good in the single case only because the system has slack that disappears at scale. Preliminary work packets also degrade in quality when T1 is producing 100 of them under time pressure; the "preparatory" value disappears.

K-219 at scale inverts the documented specialty of the resource pools. D2 is a generalist; routing all handwriting cases to D2 pushes D2's queue from a 2–5 h range into the 30–80 h range, while D1 sits idle with the complex-corporate skill nobody needs. The expected cycle time *increases*, because D2's handwriting rework rate is non-linear: above ~70% utilization, requests for clarification rise sharply. The bottleneck migrates from D1 (forecasted) to D2 (real), and D2 is the slower resource.

K-337 at scale removes ~2,000–3,000 h of external supplier wait per 100 cases but adds ~300–600 h of C1 work. If C1 absorbs it (single-reviewer throughput ~6 h/day), C1 finishes ~50–100 compliance reviews/week — feasible but saturating. The binding constraint is *quality*: each case forces a decision on an unresolved discrepancy. Even if only 10% of those decisions are wrong, that is 10 materially adverse decisions in one week, which the organization cannot absorb.

K-411 at scale is the most defensible. 100 cases parallelized means ~200 verification tasks start within 1–2 h of Triage instead of being staggered. I2 and D2 can each absorb ~8 cases/day at 2 h queue, so 100 cases fit within a week if started Mon–Wed. The real test is A2: A2's 5–6 h queue at Tue/Wed means Assessment arrivals earlier in the week can be absorbed if A2's other commitments are not unusually heavy. The bottleneck migrates cleanly from sequential Identity/Document queues to Assessment — exactly where one would want it, since A2 has two interchangeable resources.

## 7. Final Synthesis

The most robust intervention is **K-411 — parallelize verification**. It produces the cleanest cycle-time reduction (15–20 h per case) with the lowest risk profile: no decision-quality change, no compliance-review reordering, and a bottleneck that migrates predictably to A2 (where capacity is interchangeable between A1 and A2). It also generates the highest-quality process-mining data, because real-time timestamps finally reflect true concurrency rather than batch-entered approximations. The single-case counterfactual is highly transportable to a system-wide policy, provided A2's true capacity is monitored.

The most dangerous intervention despite its apparent speed is **K-337 — suppress the second RMI**. In our analysis it does *not* actually shorten the cycle in this case; it lengthens it by 30–60 h because the rework loop coincidentally landed Compliance_Review on a low-queue day. Worse, it materially changes decision policy: the case must be approved-with-conditions or rejected on an unresolved discrepancy, which trades a known external delay for an unknown downstream exposure (appeal, regulator query, loss event). At scale, this is the intervention most likely to produce decisions the organization later regrets.

The local counterfactual least transportable to system-wide policy is **K-104 — earlier escalation with C1 reservation**. The single-case gain depends on a C1 slot that, at 100 cases/week, simply does not exist. The intervention looks beneficial in isolation because the system has slack; it disappears the moment that slack is consumed by similar cases. A second-order problem is that *forcing* preliminary packets across 100 cases will degrade packet substance, eliminating the very savings the intervention claims.

The most valuable additional data are: (i) **per-case service-time distributions** (especially for D1 complex, D2 handwriting, and C1 evidence-complete review) so queue forecasts stop being point estimates; (ii) **external-supplier response-time distributions** by day-of-week, which would let us predict whether K-337-style interventions help or hurt; (iii) **C1's actual historical workload calendar** to test whether reservations are even feasible; and (iv) **timestamp-integrity audits** for the case-lock workaround, since false concurrency is the silent killer of process-mining conclusions.

## 8. Assumption Audit

1. **Queue estimates from system snapshots are point forecasts, not commitments** — *temporal*
2. **C1 is the only fully certified Compliance reviewer in the window; no resource can substitute** — *resource*
3. **The four-eyes rule (final Assessment resource  final Compliance resource) must hold unless the intervention explicitly changes decision policy** — *structural*
4. **Preliminary compliance screen saves 1–3 h on the final Compliance_Review when evidence is reasonably complete** — *behavioral*
5. **External supplier response time (8–36 h range) is independent of internal case workload and queue state** — *interference*
6. **Real-time timestamps reflect true task concurrency when the case-lock workaround is properly logged** — *temporal*
7. **D1 is materially faster than D2 on complex corporate documents; D2 is materially slower on handwritten input** — *resource*
8. **Reserving a C1 slot for one case imposes a displacement cost on other queued cases that scales with reservation volume** — *interference*
