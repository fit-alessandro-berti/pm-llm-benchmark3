## 2. Dependency and Prerequisite Map

**Upstream Data Quality Dependencies:**
- **A8** (Committee pack) depends on clean outputs from **KYC** (via A3/A4), **normalized financial statements** (A6), **collateral valuation** (A7), and **credit analysis**.
- **A9** (Contract generation) depends on **KYC clearance** and **Committee decision** data integrity.
- **A11** (Loan activation) depends on all control checks being complete upstream.
- **A6** (Financial-statement normalization) performance degrades if input documents are poor quality (handled partially by **A5**).

**Legal/Compliance Controls:**
- **Four-eyes principle** affects **A8** (Committee packaging) and **A9** (Contract generation) – automation must enforce role separation for High-exposure SME.
- **KYC-clearance-before-signing** rule constrains **A10** (E-signature) – system must block signing if KYC not cleared.
- **Audit trail requirement** applies to **A3**, **A2**, **A9** – all model suggestions/overrides must be logged.

**Human Escalation Capacity:**
- **A3** (KYC pre-classifier) requires sufficient **Compliance specialists** (currently only 3 FTE) for hit resolution.
- **A4** (Entity-mismatch resolution) also depends on these specialists.
- **A6** (Financial normalization) may increase workload for **Credit analysts** (9 FTE) if not paired with upstream quality improvement.

**External Valuation Dependency:**
- **A7** (Collateral triage) affects **Wait for external valuation** (58h median wait) but cannot control valuer performance.
- **A8** (Committee packaging) depends on valuation completion for required cases.

**Core-Banking Integration Constraints (max 2 deep integrations in first 6 months):**
- **A1** (Auto-registration) would benefit from deep integration.
- **A2** (Classification recommender) might require integration with product database.
- **A9** (Contract generation) needs deep integration with legal clauses database.
- **A11** (Loan activation) needs deep integration with loan system.

## 3. Portfolio Alternatives

### Portfolio A: Quick-Win Portfolio
**Modules:** A1, A5, A10, A12  
**Optimizes:** Political visibility (high-frequency A1), customer experience (A10), immediate cycle time reduction (A5 reduces document loops), control visibility (A12).  
**Sacrifices:** Does not address major bottlenecks (KYC entity mismatch, normalization, collateral wait). May increase downstream volume without improving capacity.

### Portfolio B: Bottleneck/Synchronization Portfolio
**Modules:** A4, A5, A6, A7  
**Optimizes:** Addresses root causes of delay: entity-mismatch blockage (A4, 31h wait), document loops (A5), normalization bottleneck (A6, 20h wait), external valuation variability (A7). Targets synchronization points (KYC-signing, committee packaging).  
**Sacrifices:** Lower political visibility (lower frequency activities). Requires change management for compliance specialists and credit analysts. Does not directly automate high-frequency registration.

### Portfolio C: Compliance-First Portfolio
**Modules:** A3, A4, A9, A12  
**Optimizes:** Control effectiveness: KYC accuracy (A3+A4 reduces 11% rework, addresses provisional signing risk), contract-term compliance (A9 with rule checks addresses 8.5% remediation), auditability (A12). Strengthens controls as required.  
**Sacrifices:** Limited cycle time reduction for non-compliance bottlenecks. May not reduce waiting times significantly. Requires significant specialist involvement.

## 4. Expected Process-Level Effects

### Portfolio A (Quick-Win):
- **Cycle time:** Moderate reduction in early activities; may increase in mid-process due to bottlenecks.
- **Waiting time:** Reduced at registration and signing; increased at KYC, document review, and normalization due to volume.
- **Rework:** Slight reduction in document loops (A5); no change in entity-mismatch or contract remediation.
- **Handovers:** Reduced at registration and signing; unchanged elsewhere.
- **Resource demand:** Reduced for back-office (A1) and customer-facing (A10); increased for compliance and credit analysis.
- **Control violations:** Unchanged or slightly improved (A12 provides visibility).

### Portfolio B (Bottleneck/Synchronization):
- **Cycle time:** Significant reduction, especially for Equipment finance and complex cases.
- **Waiting time:** Major reduction at entity mismatch (from 31h), normalization (from 20h), document loops (from 8h+), and collateral wait (if A7 reduces variability).
- **Rework:** Reduced for entity mismatch (from 38%), document loops (from 52%), and normalization (from 16%).
- **Handovers:** Reduced between KYC and specialists, between back office and credit analysts.
- **Resource demand:** Reduced demand on compliance specialists and credit analysts; may require new skills for exception handling.
- **Control violations:** Improved because KYC clearance is more reliable (fewer entity mismatches), reducing provisional signing risk.

### Portfolio C (Compliance-First):
- **Cycle time:** Moderate reduction due to fewer remediation loops.
- **Waiting time:** Reduced for contract remediation and control issues.
- **Rework:** Significant reduction for KYC (A3+A4) and contract remediation (A9).
- **Handovers:** Unchanged or slightly reduced.
- **Resource demand:** Reduced for legal ops and compliance specialists; may increase for model oversight.
- **Control violations:** Significant reduction in provisional signing, contract-term mismatches, and four-eyes violations (if A9 enforces rules).

## 6. Recommended Phased Rollout

**Phase 1 (Months 1-3): Foundation & Quick Win**
- Deploy **A5** (Missing-document checklist) – immediate impact on document loops (52% rework), no integration needed.
- Deploy **A12** (Control remediation workbench) – satisfies compliance requirement for control strengthening, provides visibility.
- Deep integration #1: **A1** (Auto-registration) – politically visible, requires core banking integration.
- *Monitoring metrics:* Document resubmission rate, registration cycle time, control violation detection rate.
- *Gate criteria:* 20% reduction in missing-document loops; registration error rate <2%.

**Phase 2 (Months 4-6): Bottleneck Addressal**
- Deploy **A4** (Entity-mismatch resolution assistant) – addresses critical 31h wait, uses existing compliance specialist capacity.
- Deploy **A6** (Financial-statement normalization) – addresses 20h wait, reduces analyst manual work.
- Deep integration #2: **A2** (Classification recommender) – reduces misrouting, builds on A1.
- *Monitoring metrics:* Entity-mismatch resolution time, normalization cycle time, classification accuracy.
- *Gate criteria:* 30% reduction in entity-mismatch wait; 25% faster normalization; classification error rate <5%.

**Phase 3 (Months 7-9): Control & Synchronization**
- Deploy **A9** (Contract generation with rule checks) – addresses 12% contract remediation, enforces compliance.
- Deploy **A7** (Collateral valuation triage) – reduces external wait variability.
- *Monitoring metrics:* Contract remediation rate, valuation wait time variance, four-eyes violation rate.
- *Gate criteria:* 40% reduction in contract remediation; 20% reduction in valuation wait time variance; zero new four-eyes violations.

**Phase 4 (Months 10-12): Adoption & Scaling**
- Deploy **A10** (E-signature adoption) – now safe because KYC clearance is improved.
- Pilot **A3** (KYC hit pre-classifier) with enhanced audit trail.
- *Monitoring metrics:* E-signature adoption rate, KYC false-negative rate, overall cycle time.
- *Gate criteria:* 50% e-signature adoption; KYC false-negative rate <0.5%; 25% overall cycle time reduction.

## 7. Final Recommendation

**I recommend Portfolio B (Bottleneck/Synchronization) with elements of C, implemented in the phased rollout above.**

**Rationale:**  
The highest-frequency activity (Receive application, 12,000) is already 72% digital, and automating registration (A1) further would primarily increase volume into bottlenecks without addressing root causes of delay and rework. Process-mining evidence shows:

1. **Critical path bottlenecks:** Entity mismatch (31h wait, 38% rework), document loops (8h wait + customer delay, 52% rework), normalization (20h wait, 16% rework), and collateral wait (58h) dominate cycle time. These are lower-frequency but create synchronization delays.

2. **Dependency structure:** Committee packaging and contract preparation cannot be safely automated until upstream data (KYC, financials, collateral) is clean. Portfolio B addresses these prerequisites directly.

3. **Control alignment:** While Portfolio C strengthens controls directly, Portfolio B indirectly improves controls by making KYC clearance more reliable (A4 reduces entity mismatches that cause provisional signing) and reducing document loops that lead to incomplete files. The phased rollout includes A9 (contract rule checks) and A12 (control workbench) to meet compliance requirements.

4. **Resource impact:** Portfolio B avoids overloading credit analysts (by improving normalization before increasing volume) and uses compliance specialist capacity efficiently (A4 targets their specific bottleneck).

5. **Process-level thinking:** The directly-follows graph shows loops (KYCentity mismatch, document reviewmissing documents) that consume significant time. Portfolio B breaks these loops at their source, whereas automating high-frequency activities would only accelerate entry into these loops.

**Trade-off argument:**  
Automating registration first is a "local optimization" that appears attractive by frequency but ignores "global" process dynamics. The process-mining evidence shows that end-to-end performance is dominated by a few synchronization points and exception-handling loops. By addressing these first, we create a more stable foundation for subsequent automation, avoid amplifying downstream bottlenecks, and achieve larger cycle-time reductions for complex cases (which likely carry higher loan values and strategic importance). This approach aligns with the management's request for a process-level recommendation rather than a simple frequency-based one.
