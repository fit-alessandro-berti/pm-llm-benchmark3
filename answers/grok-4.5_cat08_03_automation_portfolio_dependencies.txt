## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

**Upstream data quality dependencies**  
- A1 (registration)  A2 (classify)  correct routing into KYC vs financial-document paths (DFG: ClassifyKYC 7250, ClassifyFin doc 3940).  
- A5 (missing docs) and A6 (normalization) form a chain before Credit analysis (NormalizeCredit 5000). Automating credit-adjacent steps without A5/A6 increases dirty inflow to the 9 FTE analyst pool.  
- A8 (committee pack) requires reconciled outputs of KYC clearance, normalized statements, credit notes, and valuation when required; packaging without these produces GIGO and committee rework.  
- A9 (contract) and A11 (activation) inherit any upstream identity, decision, or collateral defects.

**Legal/compliance controls**  
- KYC clearance must precede Customer signing (control finding: 6.5% provisional signing, 1.1% remediation). A10 must hard-gate on KYC status; A12 should capture violations.  
- Contract terms must match Committee decision (8.5% remediation overall). A9 rule checks are a control-strengthening module.  
- Four-eyes: Credit analysis and Committee packaging must not be the same person on High-exposure SME (5.8% violations in month-end rush). Automation of A8 must preserve segregation-of-duties checks.  
- A3/A4 touch regulated identity resolution; every model suggestion, override, and final decision needs an audit trail.

**Human escalation capacity**  
- Compliance specialists = 3 FTE with high handover concentration from KYC ops. A3 and A4 increase or reshape escalation demand; without capacity planning they lengthen the 31h mismatch queue.  
- Credit analysts are central in the working-together network. A6 frees capacity but A1/A2/A5 that only accelerate inflow without normalization will overload them.  
- Back office (12 FTE) is visible but utilization drops after 15:00—candidate for exception handling rather than more headcount.  
- RMs trigger missing-document loops via incomplete instructions; A5 must improve RM-facing guidance or friction resistance will block adoption.

**External valuation dependency**  
- Wait for external valuation (median 58h, 19% late) is a synchronization point into Committee packaging (DFG: WaitPack 1980). A7 can triage/parallelize/route but cannot fully eliminate partner latency; committee and contract automation must tolerate residual external wait.  
- Required in ~71% Equipment finance and ~18% Working-capital—variant-aware routing (depends on A2 quality) matters.

**Core-banking integration constraints**  
- IT can deeply integrate only two modules in the first six months. Prefer deep integration for modules that write control-critical state (e.g., A9 contract rules, A2 classification, or A4 mismatch disposition) and lighter integration (portal, RPA, document AI side-car) for A5, A6, A10, A7.  
- A11 activation bot should not be deeply integrated until control completion signals are reliable.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

### Quick-win portfolio
**Modules:** A1, A5, A10, A9  
**Optimizes:** Visible volume reduction, early political win, customer-wait reduction, and one control-strengthening module (A9) to satisfy compliance.  
**Sacrifices:** Entity-mismatch critical path (31h), external valuation sync (58h), analyst normalization burden, and specialist capacity. Risk of pushing volume into unchanged KYC/credit bottlenecks. Limited structural attack on synchronization delays.

### Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio
**Modules:** A4, A5, A6, A7  
**Optimizes:** Long waits and rework loops—entity mismatch critical path, missing-doc ping-pong, normalization before credit, external valuation triage. Strongest expected cut to end-to-end cycle time and analyst/compliance waiting.  
**Sacrifices:** Immediate high-frequency labor optics (no A1), weaker direct contract/signing control automation, only partial compliance “strengthen controls” story unless A4 is framed with audit trail as control support. Committee packaging and four-eyes not directly addressed. A7 partner dependency remains.

### Compliance-first portfolio
**Modules:** A12, A9, A3, A2  
**Optimizes:** Control posture—remediation workbench, contract rule checks, KYC hit governance, correct product/exposure routing (prevents wrong control path). Meets “at least one control-strengthening module” emphatically. Reduces provisional signing and contract-decision mismatch.  
**Sacrifices:** Lower direct impact on 58h valuation wait, 44h signing wait, and doc-review loops. Customer-facing cycle time improvement slower. RM friction lower than pure nudge designs, but specialist escalation (A3) may intensify load on 3 FTE unless tuned.

## 4. Expected process-level effects

### Quick-win (A1, A5, A10, A9)
- **Cycle time:** Moderate improvement; registration and signing tails shrink; mid-process (KYC mismatch, valuation, credit) largely unchanged.  
- **Waiting time:** Register wait (1.5h) and Customer signing (44h) down; Financial doc review (18h) improves via A5; Resolve entity mismatch (31h) and external valuation (58h) untouched.  
- **Rework:** Request-missing-doc loops (3300/2800 DFG) decline; contract-remediation loops (650/510) decline via A9; KYC mismatch loops (1750/1160) remain.  
- **Handovers:** Fewer portalback-office registration handovers; RMcustomer doc handovers cleaner; compliance specialist handovers unchanged.  
- **Resource demand:** Back office load down early day; Legal ops smoother (less remediation); Credit analysts may see *more* clean-enough cases sooner  risk of overload without normalization.  
- **Control violations:** Contract–decision match improves; KYC-before-sign still needs hard gate with A10; four-eyes unchanged; provisional signing should fall if A10 enforces KYC status.

### Bottleneck/synchronization (A4, A5, A6, A7)
- **Cycle time:** Largest expected reduction for cases on mismatch and valuation paths; Equipment finance and mismatch-affected cases benefit most.  
- **Waiting time:** Resolve entity mismatch 31h and its critical-path presence targeted; doc-review 18h and normalize 20h targeted; external 58h partially reduced via triage/parallel route; committee pack still waits on weekly cadence for high exposure.  
- **Rework:** Missing-doc repeated rate 52% down; mismatch rework 38% down; cleaner normalizecredit (5000) reduces credit rework.  
- **Handovers:** KYC opsspecialist concentration eased if A4 covers common mismatch patterns; fewer Fin-docRequest-missing cycles; valuer routing reduces ad-hoc collateral team chase.  
- **Resource demand:** Compliance specialists: lower queue time per case but need escalation design; Credit analysts: capacity freed by A6—**necessary** before any inflow acceleration; Collateral team more triage, less firefighting; Back office shifts to exception handling on docs.  
- **Control violations:** Indirect improvement (faster clean KYC path reduces provisional-sign temptation); four-eyes and contract-match not directly enforced—residual control gap vs compliance-first.

### Compliance-first (A12, A9, A3, A2)
- **Cycle time:** Modest overall; gains on remediation loops and misrouted cases; less effect on valuation/signing waits.  
- **Waiting time:** Remediatecontract loops shrink; misrouting-induced waits fall with A2; entity mismatch wait only indirectly helped if A3 reduces bad hits.  
- **Rework:** Contract remediation 8.5% (14% renewals) down; classification-driven rework (8% classify exception) down; KYC hit handling more standardized.  
- **Handovers:** Clearer escalate-to-compliance paths; fewer wrong-pool handovers after classify; Legal ops and committee see fewer defective packs from wrong exposure path.  
- **Resource demand:** Compliance ops + 3 specialists: A3 must be tuned to avoid escalation floods; Legal ops utilization more even (less month-end contract fix firefighting); Credit analysts less harmed by misrouted high-exposure work.  
- **Control violations:** Strongest reduction—four-eyes monitoring via A12, KYC-before-sign enforcement support, contract–decision match via A9, auditable KYC suggestions via A3, correct control path via A2. Addresses 5.8% four-eyes and 6.5% provisional-sign findings most directly.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Risk | Compensating controls |
|------|------------------------|
| False negatives in KYC hit pre-classification (A3) | Human escalation mandatory on uncertain scores; dual threshold (auto-clear / review / hard-stop); sample QA by compliance; immutable audit trail of score, features, override, actor |
| False positives / over-blocking in mismatch or KYC | SLA on specialist queue; A4 assists but does not auto-close high-risk entities without four-eyes for high-exposure |
| Misclassification of product/exposure (A2) | Shadow mode then recommend-only; mandatory human confirm for High-exposure SME; downstream control-path simulator; periodic confusion-matrix review |
| Model drift in extraction/normalization (A6) | Confidence thresholds; analyst acknowledgment of extracted fields used in credit; gold-set regression tests; monitor credit-rework rate post-deploy |
| GIGO in committee pack (A8) if used later | Completeness checklist gates (KYC status, valuation present if required, normalize flag, credit note ID); do not auto-submit to committee on failed gate |
| Stale contract rule library (A9) | Rule ownership in Legal; versioned rule packs tied to product codes; pre-production difference reports vs last committee templates; block generate on missing decision ID |
| E-sign identity/consent and provisional signing (A10) | Hard technical gate: no sign package without KYC clearance flag; RM override only with A12 case and dual approval; reminder copy reviewed for regulatory language |
| Activation bot on incomplete controls (A11) | Do not deploy until control completion API is reliable; checklist: KYC, committee, contract match, collateral if required, four-eyes flag |
| Four-eyes bypass under month-end rush | SoD system checks on High-exposure; A12 alerts when same user touches credit analysis + packaging; freeze auto-packaging when violation detected |
| Specialist overload (3 FTE) | Cap auto-escalation volume; playbooks for top mismatch types; surge staffing gate metric on mismatch wait |
| RM/customer friction from rigid doc nudges (A5) | Co-design checklists with RMs; allow structured “exception reason”; measure abandonment and complaint rate |
| External valuer quality disputes (A7) | Routing scorecards; dual-valuer trigger on low confidence or high exposure; retain human collateral-team release |
| Auditability gaps across all models | Standard event log: model_id, version, input hash, suggestion, human decision, timestamp, case_id; retain for exam cycle |
| Shifted resource demand onto credit analysts | Pair any inflow-accelerating module with A6 (or analyst capacity gate); monitor WIP and wait before credit analysis |

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

### Phase 0 – Foundation (Weeks 0–6)
- Instrument control events: KYC clearance timestamp, four-eyes pair IDs, contract–decision match flag, provisional-sign flag.  
- Baseline metrics: median/P90 cycle time by segment (Micro/SME/High-exposure; Working-capital/Equipment/Renewal); waits on mismatch, valuation, signing, doc-review; rework rates; specialist utilization; control violation rates.  
- Gate to Phase 1: baselines agreed with Compliance, Credit, and Internal Audit; audit-trail schema approved.

### Phase 1 – First six months (two deep core-banking integrations only)
**Deep integration (count = 2):**  
1. **A2 Classification recommender** (writes product/exposure path used by controls)  
2. **A9 Contract generation with control-rule checks** (writes contract artifacts; enforces decision match)

**Light / side-car integration:**  
3. **A5 Missing-document checklist & portal nudging** (portal + RM desktop)  
4. **A12 Control remediation workbench** (case management on top of process events; satisfies “strengthen controls”)

*Note: Portfolio shape = hybrid of compliance-first + bottleneck doc loop, within 4-module cap.*  
If steering committee insists on one synchronization specialist play, substitute A4 (light integration + specialist UI) for A5 only if doc-loop metrics are already improving operationally—default keeps A5.

**Monitoring metrics:** classify override rate & misroute rate; missing-doc loop count (RequestReview DFG); contract-remediation rate; control violations (KYC-before-sign, four-eyes, decision match); back-office and legal utilization; customer complaint/abandonment on nudges.  
**Gate criteria to Phase 2:**  
- Contract remediation  5% overall and declining on renewals  
- No increase in provisional signing  
- Misroute-related rework down  30% vs baseline  
- A5: repeated missing-doc rate down without RM revolt (complaint SLA)  
- Audit trail complete on 100% of A2/A9 decisions  
- Specialist and analyst WIP not above agreed red lines

### Phase 2 – Second six months (next integration capacity)
5. **A6 Financial-statement extraction & normalization** (deep or document-AI platform integration)  
6. **A4 Entity-mismatch resolution assistant** (specialist workbench + KYC system)  
Parallel light: **A7** valuer triage rules; **A10** e-sign reminders with KYC hard gate (if not pulled earlier).  
Defer **A1** until after A6/A4 so accelerated registration cannot flood analysts/specialists. Defer **A8** until artifact completeness gates are green. Defer **A11** until control-completion signal reliability > agreed threshold. **A3** only after A4 capacity model stabilizes (avoid double escalation shock to 3 FTE).

**Monitoring add-ons:** wait before Resolve entity mismatch; % mismatch cases still critical path; normalize rework; credit analyst utilization & wait 28h trend; external valuation P90; four-eyes violations at month-end.  
**Gate criteria to expand:** mismatch median wait cut materially (target directionally  40%); credit wait not increased; extraction field-error rate within tolerance; no SoD regression at month-end.

### Phase 3 – Stabilize / optional backlog
- A8 committee pack auto-assembly with completeness gates  
- A3 KYC pre-classifier under strict thresholds  
- A1 auto-registration (political win once downstream stable)  
- A11 activation bot  
- Continuous model-drift and rule-library reviews  

## 7. Final recommendation

**Recommend a sequenced hybrid: Phase-1 portfolio {A2, A5, A9, A12}, then Phase-2 {A6, A4} with A7/A10 parallel light—not the pure quick-win and not frequency-first A1.**

**Why this portfolio**  
- **Process-level leverage over frequency:** Entity mismatch (freq 1750) and external valuation waits dominate calendar time; missing-doc loops (52% repeated) and normalization block credit. A5 and later A4/A6/A7 attack those synchronization and rework structures. A1’s ~12k frequency offers labor optics but, per resource-network evidence, would expose classify/KYC/doc-review bottlenecks and raise clean inflow to already-central credit analysts without A6.  
- **Dependencies respected:** Classification quality (A2) precedes correct control path and valuation necessity; document completeness (A5) precedes normalization (A6); contract rules (A9) and remediation (A12) precede any safe activation automation (A11). Deep integrations in the first six months are spent on path-correctness and contract integrity—the two writes that protect legal outcomes—while A5/A12 deliver value without consuming both deep slots alone.  
- **Compliance mandate met:** A12 + A9 explicitly strengthen controls (decision match, remediation, auditability), not only labor. Hard gates for KYC-before-sign and SoD monitoring address measured 6.5% provisional signing and 5.8% four-eyes breaches.  
- **Resource demand reshaped, not ignored:** A6 in Phase 2 is the safety valve for analyst capacity; A4 is scoped to the 3 FTE specialist reality with assistive—not fully autonomous—design; back-office afternoon spare capacity absorbs doc exceptions; RM friction is managed via co-designed A5.  
- **Exceptions and variants:** Equipment-finance valuation path, High-exposure committee cadence, credit-line renewal contract remediation, and concurrent KYC/financial-doc practice are reflected in prioritization (A2 routing, later A7, A9 renewal rules, not assuming sequential IS logs equal real concurrency).

**Trade-off argument on highest-frequency first**  
Register application is high frequency, very high feasibility, and politically attractive (A1). Process-mining evidence shows it is **not** the dominant constraint on end-to-end performance: median waits and critical-path blockage sit at Resolve entity mismatch, Wait for external valuation, Customer signing, Financial document review loops, and Committee packaging synchronization. Automating A1 first without A2/A5/A6 would move the queue downstream onto Compliance specialists and Credit analysts—the constrained, high-centrality resources—while leaving control gaps untouched. Therefore A1 should be **delayed until after** control path integrity (A2), missing-doc and normalization quality (A5/A6), and mismatch assistance (A4) stabilize inflow. A simple high-frequency win is preserved as a Phase-3 option once the process can absorb it without creating larger hidden queues or control debt.

**Defensible summary:** Maximize reduction in synchronization delay, rework, and control violations per scarce integration slot and specialist capacity—not transactions automated per se—while keeping a compliance-strengthening module in wave one and protecting analysts from dirty accelerated volume.
