## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

**Assets at stake, ranged not point-estimated:**

* Deadline breach ($P$-884): €20k–€35k avoided operational loss. Baseline breach probability under “continue normal processing” 0.48–0.64  baseline expected operational loss  €9.6k–€22.4k.
* Customer harm from false rejection/prolonged hold: €15k–€80k (high variance: SME liquidity crunch vs large corporate resilience).
* Case-interdependent harm ($P$-901): high-value payment, deadline 70m, interruption +0.12–0.28 breach prob  if valued similarly, expected spillover €~2.4k–€9.8k (but reputational contagion non-linear).
* Regulatory/compliance harm from unlawful execution: €100k–€500k quantified + non-monetary (correspondent-bank de-risking, licence conditions, personal liability, criminal referral) that is **asymmetric, convex and partially irreversible**. Even at 10% enforcement probability, EV = €10k–€50k, already eclipsing operational gain; at plausible 30-60% detection for sanctions-adjacent €480k wire, EV = €30k–€300k. Non-monetary component cannot be traded off.

**Dominance logic:**

1. **Lexicographic compliance constraint.** Process rules 6,7,9 are mandatory controls for sanctions-adjacent payments. Utility is not €-linear beyond them: a reduction in breach probability cannot compensate for creating an illegal execution state. Therefore any ranking that puts `A`/`B` above a feasible conformant path is invalid *even if* point-EV favours them — classic compliance-aware dominance.
2. **Prediction non-transportability.** The quoted 0.48-0.64 breach probability and 0.55-0.75 ultimate approval are observational, trained on historically *compliant* traces (self-selection, earlier evidence loops, reviewer assignment). Under intervention “skip second review”, the data-generating process changes (no review duration, but downstream audit distribution shifts dramatically). Treat predicted breach reduction for `A`/`B` as **upper bound** and regulatory risk increase as **under-observed**.
3. **Value of information.** Alternate screening (€ marginal API cost, 15-30m parallel time) reduces Type I/II error for the second officer: distinguishing $0.46$ false positive from corroborated match. Given €80k false-reject upside and €100k-€500k regulatory downside, resolving uncertainty dominates the modest delay *unless* $S4$ delay alone guarantees breach. Hence D* with parallelization weakly dominates D-sequential and, for system welfare, dominates `C` alone despite `C` being faster for $P$-884 isolated.
4. **Spillover-adjusted comparison.**  
   * Isolating $P$-884: $C$ (EV loss ~ €4k-14k) beats $D*$ with slow $S4$ ($75$-$155m$ total, EV loss ~ €11k-30k) on breach cost *alone*.  
   * System view ($P$-884 + $P$-901): $D*$ avoiding $P$-901 preemption saves €2.4k-9.8k expected + reduces correlated deadline failure risk across portfolio. When you add error-reduction value of screening (cutting expected regulatory loss by even 0.05×€300k = €15k), $D*$ dominates $C$.

**Why not simply maximize operational gain?**  
Naïve EU($A$)  +€20k-€35k (breach avoided) – p(detect)×€100k-€500k. Break-even detection probability for indifference is ~ 0.04-0.35 — implausibly low for sanctions-border destination with unresolved name match and audit-logged bypass of four-eyes. Add unquantifiable licence/criminal tail and the inequality becomes strict in all realistic worlds.

**Handling uncertainty:** Use minmax/ranges rather than point estimates. Conformant paths have bounded worst-case (breach + customer harm, both capped, insurable). Nonconformant paths have heavy right-tail (regulatory escalation, network exclusion) — dominance under ambiguity aversion.

## 4. Conformance constraints

Process rules applicable:

* R1-2: Registration & Sanctions Screening done.
* R3: Auto-Clear permitted *only* if no alert and risk <40  **inapplicable** (risk 72/78, alert = unresolved fuzzy match).
* R4: Otherwise Analyst Review mandatory  satisfied ($R17$ completed).
* R5: Evidence loop may repeat (current loop completed).
* **R6:** **Risk 70 OR any unresolved fuzzy sanctions match  Second Officer Review mandatory before Approve or Execute.** Present state: risk 72 (and initial 78) **and** match 0.46 unresolved  both triggers fire. Stale timestamp (12m) does not suspend; if anything requires refresh *within* second review.
* **R7:** **Analyst (first reviewer)  second officer (four-eyes).**
* R8: After required review(s) case may be Approved/Rejected/Held + SAR. SAR alone  authorization to execute.
* R9: **Payment Executed only after valid approval path.**
* **R10:** Emergency override allowed *only* with Compliance Director authorization **AND** valid incident identifier. Neither satisfied now.
* R11: SAR must not be disclosed to customer (tipping-off prohibition).

Mapping:

* **Action A – Execute immediately:** Violates **R6+R9** directly; **R10** not satisfied to excuse. Speed (10-15m) irrelevant. Trace missing required `Second Officer Review` event  replay fitness failure, auditable as control bypass. Cannot be repaired by any outcome prediction.
* **Action B – $R17$ self-certify:** Violates **R6+R7+R9**. Even if labeled “second review”, identity equality fails R7. Creating a second log entry by same user is falsification, elevating non-monetary sanction vs A. Independence requirement is substantive, not formal — second pair of eyes must bring uncorrelated judgment.
* **Action C – Interrupt $S2$:** Satisfies **R6+R7+R9 IF** $S2\neq R17$ and conducts independent validation (evidence, refreshed risk score, match disposition rationale). No rule prohibits prioritization/preemption per se; resource contention is operational, not prohibitive. Must ensure $S2$ not rubber-stamps to protect $P$-901 timeline.
* **Action D – Alternate screening  Second Review:** **Rule-compliant augmentation, not substitution.** Alternate vendor result *does not* waive R6; even if match clears, risk 72 still mandates Second Review; even if risk drops to <70 on refreshed score, match unresolved until formally dispositioned. D is valuable precisely because it informs but does not replace the mandatory human control. Requires same R7 independence.
* **Action E – Request additional evidence:** Fully `CONFORMANT NOW`; R5 permits looping. Downside is purely operational.
* **Action F – Reject now:** Potentially conformant under R8, **iff** documented evidence threshold for rejection is met. An unresolved 0.46 fuzzy match alone may be insufficient per policy — many regimes require enhanced due diligence before adverse action to avoid discriminatory/economic harm and to preserve appeal rights. What would validate it: written policy clause for risk70+fuzzy>threshold+EDD attempts documented, prior evidence invalidated with reasons, customer informed without disclosing SAR considerations (R11), and retention of right to re-present. Absent that proof, rejection risks being retroactively deemed arbitrary (procedural nonconformance).
* **Action G – Emergency override:** `EXCEPTION PATH NOT YET ENABLED`. R10’s conjunctive condition (Director + incident ID) must *actually* be satisfied, not merely requested. Seeking approval is permissible activity; **executing** before satisfaction reverts to A-nonconformance. Genuine enablement would require: verifiable incident (system outage, humanitarian emergency, market-stability event), Director written authorization naming case and expiry, incident register entry, and mandatory post-override second review + audit. None present. Waiting for G without progressing conformant path is operationally risky.

**Why favorable forecasts cannot rehabilitate A/B:** Predictive monitoring estimates $P(\text{breach}\mid\text{history})$ under observance of the process. Intervention $do(\text{Execute})$ mutilates the causal graph: it removes the control nodes. Compliance risk lives off-distribution — historically rare because controls worked — so model has no data on regulator response to intentional bypass. Expected regulatory harm is therefore **externally estimated**, not learned from log. Conformance is a hard filter preceding utility maximization.

## 5. Resource spillovers

**Second officer scarcity trade-off:**

* $S2$ now: immediate, but externality +0.12–0.28 breach prob for $P$-901 (high-value, 70m deadline, overlap >50%). If $P$-901’s breach cost is order-magnitude similar (€20k-€35k), expected spillover  €2.4k–€9.8k + relational damage with that customer + possible correlated regulatory view if both payments share correspondent corridor.
* $S4$ in 35–65m: stochastic ETA. At 35m, queuing plus parallel screening still allows $T_{exec}\approx 35+35+1080m$ (best) to $65+75+15155m$ (worst). Probability of meeting $90m$ rises from ~0.30 to ~0.65 as ETA moves left. Spillover near-zero (no preemption) and protects $P$-901 service level.

**Decision rule accounting for S2 vs S4:**
Do not block-preempt $P$-901 at $T_0$. Use the 15-30m alt-screening window to **acquire credible ETA** for $S4$. Set timed fallback at $T_{0}+25$-$30m$: if $S4$ not committed to start $T_{0}+40m$, escalate to $S2$ and accept spillover transparently (document necessity, mitigate $P$-901 via delegation or partial automation). This *option value* is why Rank1 dominates statically committing to Rank2.

Sensitivity:

* **If $S4$ becomes available in 35m (optimistic):** Rank1 is strictly best — total $75$-$95m$ likely beats $90m$ without harming $P$-901, plus quality gain from screening. Fallback never triggered. Gap to Rank2 widens.
* **If $S4$ in 65m (pessimistic) and no fallback:** Rank1 becomes worst conformant ($105$-$155m$, breach ~0.75-0.90). Ranking flips to **C (Rank2)  D*-sequential  F**. With the designed fallback, Rank1 *converges* to Rank2 performance at $T+30m$ decision point (you pay only the 25-30m information-gathering delay). Expected delay cost of 25m  +0.15 breach prob  €3k-€5k, still cheaper than *certain* immediate preemption cost for $P$-901 in expectation — so fallback-equipped D* remains weakly preferred even when $S4$ is slow, unless $P$-901 preemption cost is declared negligible by business leadership.

**Alternate screening API quota:** Three expedited calls left today. Opportunity cost low relative to €480k payment, but not zero. Policy: reserve one for post-execution true-hit clarification if payment later held for investigation. Using one now is justified because it simultaneously (a) reduces second-officer cognitive load, (b) creates defensible artefacts for audit (“two-vendor discrepancy review”), (c) may be decisive for SAR filing decision without tipping customer. Consuming call speculatively *without* pairing to mandatory second review would be waste — hence D without subsequent review is nonconformant shortcut.

**Exception waiting risk:** Pursuing G consumes the most constrained resource — *time* — with lowest success probability. If run **sequentially**, it cannibalizes the conformant window and converts a manageable breach risk into near-certain breach plus rushed review errors. If pursued at all, it must be **purely parallel background task** that never blocks assignment of $S2$/$S4$, and abandoned by fallback deadline.
