## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

Decision-making for P-884 requires comparing four qualitatively distinct loss domains across different probability and severity scales:

1. **Lexicographic Admissibility (Hard Compliance Constraints Over Operational Utility)**:
   In compliance-governed financial workflows, mandatory controls (Rules 6–11) are **hard admissibility boundaries**, not priced externalities. An action that violates the four-eyes principle (Action B) or skips mandatory second review (Action A) cannot be justified by saving €20,000–€35,000 in SLA penalties. Even before considering monetary fines, nonconformant execution is inadmissible by institutional governance.

2. **Asymmetric Regulatory Exposure and Non-Monetary Harm (€100,000–€500,000+ plus Tail Consequences)**:
   * **Monetary asymmetry**: The observational probability that P-884 is *not* ultimately approved after a conformant review is $1 - (0.55\text{–}0.75) = 0.25\text{–}0.45$. Executing without a valid independent review (Actions A, B) exposes the institution to a $0.25\text{–}0.45$ risk of executing an unapprovable €480,000 payment to a sanctioned-border region. Even in raw expected value terms, $(0.25\text{–}0.45) \times (\text{€100,000–€500,000}) = \text{€25,000–€225,000}$, which already overlaps and dominates the maximum €20,000–€35,000 deadline benefit.
   * **Non-monetary dominance**: Cross-border sanctions violations and willful control circumvention (such as R17 self-certifying under Action B) carry non-linear, uninsurable harms: regulatory consent orders, loss of correspondent banking relationships, mandatory lookback audits across historical transactions, and personal/officer liability. No bounded operational saving (€20,000–€35,000) can compensate for this tail exposure.

3. **Balancing P-884 Deadline Loss (€20,000–€35,000) Against False Rejection / Prolonged Hold Harm (€15,000–€80,000)**:
   * **Why Action F (Reject Now) is dominated by Conformant Review (Actions D and C)**:
     If P-884 is rejected immediately, the institution forfeits the €20,000–€35,000 on-time execution benefit on all legitimate transactions AND incurs €15,000–€80,000 in customer harm whenever the rejection is false ($P(\text{Approve}) = 0.55\text{–}0.75$). Thus, Action F carries an expected false-rejection + lost-execution penalty of roughly $(0.55\text{–}0.75) \times (\text{€35,000–€115,000}) \approx \text{€19,250–€86,250}$. By contrast, conducting a conformant second review (Actions D or C) takes 40–155 minutes: even if the 90-minute deadline is missed by 15–40 minutes (risking €20,000–€35,000 in SLA loss), an approved payment still executes shortly thereafter, avoiding the up to €80,000 false-rejection harm.
   * **Why Action D (Composite Plan) dominates Action E (6–24h Customer Evidence Request) as an immediate action**:
     Action E guarantees missing the 90-minute deadline ($P(\text{breach}) \approx 1.0$, locking in the €20,000–€35,000 loss on valid cases) and imposes a 6–24 hour hold (triggering prolonged-hold harm toward the €15,000–€80,000 range). Running alternate screening in parallel with waiting for S4 (Action D) resolves match uncertainty in 15–30 minutes without customer delay, preserving a realistic chance of meeting the 90-minute deadline while keeping Action E available if the second officer still needs customer documentation.

4. **Interventional Uncertainty and Non-Transportability**:
   The baseline predictions—$P(\text{SLA breach}) = 0.48\text{–}0.64$ and $P(\text{Approve}) = 0.55\text{–}0.75$—are observational estimates from historical logs. Under intervention:
   * Preempting S2 (Action C) introduces time pressure and context switching without resolving the 0.46 fuzzy match, which can depress approval probability (more defensive holds) or lengthen review time.
   * Running alternate screening (Action D) actively injects new information during the S4 wait window, reducing epistemic uncertainty for the second officer and shrinking both false-rejection risk and review duration variance.

### Why Actions A and B Cannot Become Recommended Solely Through a Favorable Numerical Forecast
1. **Deontic Constraints vs. Predictive Probabilities**: Rules 6, 7, and 9 define what constitutes a *legally and procedurally valid payment execution*, not a heuristic for estimating risk. Even if a predictive model output $P(\text{Approve}) = 0.999$ and $P(\text{SLA breach}) = 1.0$, executing without an independent second officer (Action A) or falsifying four-eyes review (Action B) is a **100% certain compliance breach**.
2. **Observational Conditioning on Control Existence**: The historical approval rate ($0.55\text{–}0.75$) was generated *under* the conformant regime where second officers scrutinized evidence. Using a forecast conditioned on intact controls to abolish those very controls invalidates the causal basis of the forecast.
3. **Auditability and Regulatory Liability**: Bank supervisors and sanctions regulators audit process conformance (presence of independent sign-off on alerts $\ge 70$ and fuzzy matches) independently of transaction outcomes. Circumventing separation of duties (Action B) converts an operational delay into deliberate control fraud.

* **To validate Action F (Reject Now)**:
  1. *Procedural closure of Rule 5*: R17 must formally log the post-validation `Analyst Review` step and refresh the 12-minute-old risk score (72).
  2. *Substantive evidentiary threshold*: Written verification that the customer's already-validated evidence failed to supply a mandatory disambiguating attribute (e.g., registration number, tax ID, or beneficial ownership proof) required by policy to rebut the 0.46 sanctions match.
  3. *Governance rule confirmation (Rule 8)*: Confirmation in the bank's control matrix that a first-line analyst is authorized to issue a final `Rejected` disposition when the evidentiary threshold fails, without requiring a Second Officer Review (or, if Rule 8 requires Second Officer concurrence for all dispositions on risk $\ge 70$ cases, obtaining a rapid S2/S4 rejection sign-off).
  4. *Sanitized customer communication (Rule 11)*: Verification that the rejection notice cites generic policy/cut-off grounds and contains zero reference to sanctions alerts or SAR contemplation.
* **To validate Action G (Emergency Manual Override)**:
  1. *Genuine recorded Incident Identifier (Rule 10)*: Creation of a verifiable incident ticket in the enterprise incident log documenting a qualifying systemic/technical failure (e.g., screening platform outage or staffing system failure—not routine SLA pressure).
  2. *Authenticated Compliance Director Authorization (Rule 10)*: Explicit, time-stamped written/system authorization from the Compliance Director referencing P-884 and the recorded incident ID *before* payment execution.
  3. *Documented sanctions disposition*: Because statutory sanctions liability cannot be waived by internal workflow overrides, the override record must also include a compliance determination explaining why executing despite the 0.46 fuzzy match does not breach external sanctions law.
