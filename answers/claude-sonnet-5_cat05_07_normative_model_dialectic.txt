## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Stipulated (evidence-grounded, not seriously contestable):**
- M0's structure is as coded: `checks` is a genuine concurrent block (no edge between Sanctions_Check/Quality_Review), both must precede `decision`; the approved branch requires Manual_Procurement_Approval strictly before Activate_Supplier; `decision` is XOR(approved_branch, Reject_Supplier); Notify_Requester precedes Close_Case.
- CS-9 is a controlled, formally approved standard effective 2025-01-01, mandating Sanctions_Check + Quality_Review before activation, Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier, and requester  approver.
- PD-3's effective window is 2026-06-01–2026-07-31 with named eligibility conditions; it exists only as minutes/email, not incorporated into CS-9/M0.
- FD-7's effective window is 2026-07-10 00:00–2026-07-12 23:59, with an explicit EUR 20,000 cap and mandatory two-day Quality_Review/daily monitoring; it "expired automatically."
- CRM migration (2026-07-01) caused notification missingness to rise from 2% to 29%; older CRM links are mutable text fields.
- Aggregate Auto_Approve figures: 312 total, 289 eligible, 14 ineligible, 9 indeterminate; 47 Request_More_Info cases with no premature activation; 6 post-pilot Auto_Approve cases in early August.

**Disputed / authority-dependent (not resolvable from the record alone):**
- Whether PD-3 was ever *validly* authorized to deviate from a "controlled and formally approved" CS-9 — the Compliance observer's retraction ("had not approved weakening CS-9") is directly contested testimony.
- Whether rejected leads (SUP-709) fall inside M0's scope at all — both the process owner and the model owner disagree, and CS-9 is silent.
- Whether reactivation (SUP-710) is governed by CS-9 by default, or is an uncovered gap.
- Whether a factually-continuing emergency (SUP-705) can extend FD-7 without a written act — this is not merely disputed, it is resolvable by policy logic (see §2/§3), but the *underlying fact* of continued flood risk is asserted, not documented.
- Whether QA_Clearance is truly Quality_Review (SUP-713) — supported by later audit mapping, but the mapping postdates the case.
- Whether missing Notify_Requester events (SUP-706) reflect real process failure or CRM logging attrition.

**Burden of proof adopted:**
- **To declare a case NONCOMPLIANT**, the prosecution must show (a) a deviation from M0/CS-9 semantics, and (b) the absence of a valid, in-scope, time-bounded overlay authority covering that specific deviation, with eligibility contemporaneously documented. Ambiguity in scope (not in conduct) is not resolved in favor of noncompliance; it is resolved as MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT pending clarification.
- **To accept an exception (JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION)**, the defense must show a competent authority issued the overlay, the case falls within the overlay's effective window, the case meets the overlay's own documented eligibility criteria, and evidence of eligibility is contemporaneous rather than reconstructed after the fact. Any one failure defeats the exception and reverts the case to the default noncompliance test above, unless the failure is purely evidentiary (logging), in which case it is DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

**Formal authority.** CS-9 is "controlled and formally approved"; PD-3 is minutes and an email. Under any normal change-control hierarchy, a lower-form instrument cannot suspend a higher-form one. The Compliance observer's own retraction ("had not approved weakening CS-9") means the *strongest* evidence that PD-3 licenses a CS-9 deviation is contested by the very person cited as approving it. Even accepting PD-3 arguendo, its authority is bounded exactly by its stated window and eligibility text — nothing more.

**Expiry and normalization.** FD-7 expired automatically at 2026-07-12 23:59. SUP-705 occurred 2026-07-13 with no written extension — "operations says the flood emergency still factually existed" is precisely the self-authorized extension that emergency-directive regimes exist to prevent. If informal operational judgment can extend a directive, the directive has no fixed boundary at all. Similarly, SUP-714 uses Auto_Approve two days after PD-3 lapsed, justified only by a "belief that permanent approval was imminent" — belief is not authority, and no permanent amendment exists. The aggregate fact that **six** Auto_Approve cases appear in the first week of August is not noise; it is evidence of *institutional drift* from a lapsed pilot into an unauthorized permanent practice, exactly the risk that time-boxing exists to prevent.

**Deviations hidden by M0's abstraction.** SUP-712 and SUP-715 replay M0's control flow perfectly, yet both violate rules CS-9 states but M0 does not encode: same-human approval-via-alias (712) defeats the entire purpose of the approval gate, and an undisclosed spousal relationship between reviewer and approver (715) breaches conflict-of-interest disclosure duty. "The trace fits M0" cannot be treated as proof of compliance; it only proves M0 is blind to resource-level misconduct — the misconduct itself stands.

**High-risk, uncontested violations.** SUP-708 activates a HIGH-risk supplier 40 minutes *before* Sanctions_Check — the single most safety-critical ordering rule in CS-9 — with no emergency or pilot cover at all. That no transactions occurred before clearance is a fortunate outcome, not a compliance fact; the control exists to prevent the *possibility*, not to be excused after the fact by good luck. SUP-703 is worse: an operator affirmatively selected the pilot flow *despite* known ineligibility (HIGH-risk, new, foreign), and the first purchase was EUR 180,000 — nine times FD-7's own emergency cap, on a route that has no cap at all. Internal Audit's flagging of exactly these two cases as "potentially severe" corroborates the prosecution's selection, not Operations' claim that deviations are mostly obsolescence noise.

**Desirability does not amend policy.** SUP-707's Request_More_Info loop may be operationally desirable and evidentially safe (47/47 cases show no premature activation), but stakeholder approval of an outcome is not a policy amendment. Until CS-9/M0 is formally revised, the activity is ultra vires with respect to the controlling standard, and treating it as compliant by informal consensus sets the same precedent that produced the PD-3/FD-7 drift above. The same logic bars Operations' desire for a *permanent* low-risk auto-approval branch: desire, however reasonable, is not authority, and no amendment exists.

**Aggregate exposure.** Of 312 Auto_Approve cases, 14 are confirmed ineligible and 9 are of unknown eligibility — at minimum 23 cases (7.4%) are confirmed-or-indeterminate noncompliant, a materially significant control failure rate for an activity gating financial and sanctions exposure.

## 3. Defense Brief

**Concurrency is not disorder.** M0's `checks` block is an explicit `StrictPartialOrder` with no edge between Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review — the model itself declares their relative order immaterial. Prosecutorial arguments that hinge on *sequence* within `checks` (as opposed to timing relative to `decision`/activation) misread M0's own semantics.

**Missing loop is a model defect, not misconduct.** M0 has no representation whatsoever for Request_More_Info; a fully desirable, evidence-gated practice (SUP-707, and 47/47 cases showing no premature activation) is technically "unreplayable" only because the model was never extended. This is squarely a MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT, not a governance violation — the correct remedy is to add a loop, not to prosecute the practice.

**Pilot/emergency overlays are legitimate governance instruments, imperfectly integrated.** PD-3 is a *documented*, steering-committee-level, time-boxed decision — the kind of instrument organizations routinely use to pilot policy before formal codification. The Compliance observer's later disclaimer is a retrospective interpretive dispute, not proof the pilot was unauthorized *at the time*; the correct response is to resolve the governance ambiguity going forward (§6), not to retroactively convict 289 documented-eligible cases. SUP-702 is the model case for how the pilot was *supposed* to work: complete eligibility fields, correct sequencing per PD-3's own text, quality review within the allowed window, and a stable `notification_id` link — this is compliance with a real, if informally instantiated, rule set. FD-7 is even stronger: it is an emergency directive with an explicit cap, explicit monitoring requirement, and explicit sequencing permission; SUP-704 satisfies every element.

**CRM migration is a logging problem, not a process failure.** Missingness jumped from 2% to 29% coincident with the CRM cutover — a textbook data-collection artifact. SUP-706's absent Notify_Requester event alongside a CRM message at +3 hours (albeit on a mutable link) is far more plausibly a linkage/collection gap than an actually-skipped notification. Convicting on this record substitutes absence-of-evidence for evidence-of-absence.

**Reactivation and rejected leads sit outside CS-9's stated scope.** CS-9 has no reactivation procedure at all; SUP-710 reusing a still-valid quality certification is defensible operational judgment absent any written rule to violate. CS-9 explicitly permits an abbreviated pre-screen for rejected leads and explicitly declines to say whether such leads are M0 cases; SUP-709 cannot be convicted under a scope rule that its own source document says is undecided.

**Label change is not substance change.** SUP-713's QA_Clearance is audit-confirmed as content-equivalent to Quality_Review; the mapping's approval lagging the case by one week is a documentation-timing issue, not a control failure.

**Duplicate events are migration noise, not double execution.** SUP-711's duplicate Register_Supplier rows share one source transaction ID — definitionally a single business event recorded twice.

**Conceded ground.** The defense does not dispute that SUP-708 (activation before sanctions clearance) and SUP-703 (known-ineligible override, grossly oversized first purchase) are genuine, serious violations with no overlay to excuse them. It also concedes SUP-705 and SUP-714 lack any written authority and cannot be certified compliant on operational belief alone — informal practice has limits, and an expired instrument does not self-renew by operational conviction or "normalized" habit. Six August Auto_Approve cases indicate a governance gap requiring immediate correction, not retroactive validation. Finally, the defense agrees that resource-level failures (SUP-712 alias self-approval; SUP-715 undisclosed COI) are real risks that a control-flow-only model cannot excuse and must not be waved away merely because the trace "fits."

**System-level judgment.** Operational compliance is **partially sound but materially compromised at the margins that matter most**: the standard route (SUP-701-type) and the two properly-scoped overlays (PD-3, FD-7 used within their own terms) function as intended, and the aggregate eligibility rate (289/312, ~93%) supports the defense's claim that the pilot is not, in the main, being abused. However, three failure modes are real and not curable by informal appeal to good outcomes: (1) deliberate or negligent overrides of known eligibility limits (SUP-703), (2) unauthorized extension of expired time-boxed instruments (SUP-705, SUP-714, plus the six-case August pattern), and (3) resource/COI failures invisible to control-flow checking (SUP-712, SUP-715). M0 itself is **materially obsolete and incomplete** as a sole normative artifact — it cannot express the loop, the pilot, the emergency route, or any resource condition, and CS-9 is silent on reactivation and rejected-lead scope. Neither side should prevail wholesale: the prosecution is right that authority and expiry are being treated too casually in practice, and the defense is right that M0/CS-9's silence is being wrongly treated as automatic noncompliance rather than as a drafting gap.

**Where each side is rejected:**
- Prosecution's claim that PD-3 is *categorically* unauthorized is rejected as unproven — a steering-committee minute is a recognized (if imperfect) governance instrument, and 289 fully-eligible, in-window cases cannot be convicted on an unresolved authority dispute alone.
- Prosecution's implicit claim that Request_More_Info (SUP-707) is a compliance violation is rejected — this is a model gap, not a behavioral fault, and the evidence (47/0) affirmatively supports safety.
- Defense's claim that "the operation is basically obsolescence noise" is rejected as too broad — SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712 are not model artifacts; they are conduct failures independent of any drafting gap.
- Defense's implicit claim that factual continuation of an emergency can substitute for written extension (SUP-705) is rejected — this is exactly the erosion mechanism that time-boxed authority is designed to prevent, regardless of sympathetic facts.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

**Layer 1 — POWL control-flow (M1):** encodes the *shape* of legitimate routes: concurrent checks, standard manual-approval route, low-risk auto-approval route, emergency/provisional route, a bounded Request_More_Info loop, rejection, and notify-before-close. POWL cannot express eligibility, amounts, dates, or identities — these are deliberately left as gateway conditions resolved by Layer 2.

**Layer 2 — Data-aware rules (pandas / DECLARE-log-skeleton style):**
- `Auto_Approve` eligible iff `risk=='LOW' and country=='DE' and corporate_group_approved==True and rule_version=='PD-3' and effective_date in [2026-06-01,2026-07-31]`.
- Emergency route eligible iff `directive=='FD-7' and effective_date in window and spend_cumulative<=20000 until retrospective_approval==True`.
- DECLARE-style `Response(Activate_Supplier, Quality_Review, 2 business days)` for both overlay routes.
- DECLARE `Precedence(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier)` as an absolute, overlay-independent constraint (violated by SUP-708 in all routes).
- Notification-linkage rule: `notification_id` must be present and immutable; missingness above a threshold triggers DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE, not NONCOMPLIANT, classification.

**Layer 3 — Resource/COI rules (organizational control + log check):**
- `requester_canonical_id != approver_canonical_id` after alias resolution (defeats SUP-712-style evasion).
- Mandatory COI disclosure record required whenever reviewer/approver share HR-reporting-line or declared-relationship attributes (SUP-715); absence of a disclosure record is itself a control failure, independent of proven bias.

**Layer 4 — Policy-overlay governance (organizational control):**
- Overlay registry: `overlay_id, source_doc, authorizing_body, effective_start, effective_end, ratification_status, superseding_amendment_id`.
- System-enforced hard stop: any activity requiring an overlay is rejected/flagged automatically once `now > effective_end` unless a new `overlay_id` with `ratification_status='approved'` exists (prevents SUP-705/714/August drift).
- Explicit two-tier ratification: "monitored exception noted" (observer comment) is insufficient; only a signed compliance sign-off changes `ratification_status`.

**Layer 5 — Explicit scope rules (organizational control, documented in CS-9 amendment):**
- Rejected leads: define an `Intake_PreScreen` sub-process outside M0's activation scope; log it separately with its own conformance target.
- Reactivations: define `Reactivate_Supplier` activity requiring certification-validity check (`cert_expiry_date > today`) in lieu of full Register/QR, logged with reference to prior `case_id`.
- Withdrawals: define an explicit `Withdraw_Application` terminal activity distinct from Reject_Supplier, to avoid conflation in future logs.

**Summary of what each layer covers and why it is not all in POWL:** the control-flow skeleton (concurrency, route selection, loop, rejection, notify-before-close) is expressed in POWL because it is genuinely about *ordering and choice*. Eligibility, spend caps, timing windows, and overlay validity are pandas/DECLARE-style data checks because they are quantitative/temporal predicates over case attributes, not sequencing facts, and forcing them into POWL would either be impossible (amount thresholds) or would hard-code a temporary policy into a supposedly durable process model (the exact defect that made M0 brittle against PD-3/FD-7 in the first place). Resource/COI rules and overlay-ratification governance are organizational controls because POWL and DECLARE both model *event* behavior, not *authority and identity* relationships; encoding "requester  approver" or "ratification_status='approved'" as a control-flow constraint would misrepresent an authorization fact as an activity-ordering fact.
