# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

## 1.1 Lead assumptions (labelled, because they are not given)

- **A1 - Ranges to distributions.** *Assumption:* a base service draw is Uniform on the ordinary range with probability `1-p_tail`, and Uniform on the complex-tail range with probability `p_tail`, independently across activities. This is the simplest distribution consistent with a range and a tail probability. It is *not* observed. Expected base minutes at multiplier 1.00 are therefore: Intake `0.92*6.5+0.08*12=6.94`; Mechanical `0.85*23+0.15*45=26.30`; Electrical `0.82*28.5+0.18*52.5=32.82`; Software `0.80*32.5+0.20*62.5=38.50`; Validation `0.88*14+0.12*28.5=15.74`. Bounds use all-low ordinary and all-high complex-tail draws.
- **A2 - Multipliers.** Actual service = base draw × multiplier. Handovers (7 min), team move (5 min), setup (5 min) and coordination (8 min) are *not* multiplied. *Justification:* they are described in absolute minutes.
- **A3 - Arrivals.** Expected arrivals per 2-hour block are treated as expected counts. For simulation they are realised as floor + Bernoulli(fraction) per type, arrival Uniform inside the block, priority/type drawn from block percentages independently. *Alternative defensible interpretation:* Poisson arrivals with that mean would give wider tails; this is listed as a limit in §9.
- **A4 - Delay and SLA clock.** Delay = Validation completion  case arrival (Intake ready time). SLA targets are 120 min (P1), 480 min (P2), 1440 min (P3) on that clock. Overnight: no resource is available 20:00–08:00, incomplete cases carry to the next shift. *This assumption is essential:* without carryover the base day is infeasible (see §2).
- **A5 - Availability.** A non-preempted activity must fit inside one shift-minus-break interval, except it may finish up to 30 min into overtime if it started before shift end. After 16:00 only Deepa (to 18:00) and Emil (to 20:00) are present; after 18:00 only Emil.
- **A6 - Parallel/sequential decision.** Made at dispatch, not by the case: a policy chooses, from queue state, priority and idle qualified resources, whether ME/ES branches become ready together (parallel, +8 min coordination before Validation) or sequentially, and if sequentially which branch first. The code implements exactly this choice.
- **A7 - Preemption semantics.** *Assumption:* an interrupted diagnostic resumes its remaining time + 10% of that remaining as penalty, at most once per case, and does not restart from zero. A full restart would be harsher; the ordering of policies is unchanged, absolute delays are lower bounds.
- **A8 - Team move.** Mechanical team = {Ada, Ben, Deepa, Farah}; Electrical/Software team = {Ada, Chen, Deepa, Emil, Farah}. The extra 5 min applies when the previous diagnostic family and the current one are on opposite sides of that divide *and* the resource changes. If the same cross-skilled resource retains the case, no team move is charged.

**Decision variables:** (i) eligibility/assignment of each activity to a resource; (ii) queue ordering by priority, arrival and expected remaining time; (iii) reservation thresholds for P1; (iv) preemption permission and victim choice; (v) parallel vs sequential branches; (vi) overtime use; (vii) intentional deferral of P3 work that cannot finish before 20:00.

## 1.2 Objectives — at least two, with different moral content

**Objective J1 — tail/SLA risk (efficiency with tail penalty).**
`J1 = 0.4·E[delay]/60 + 0.3·P90[delay]/60 + 2.0·(1P1_SLA) + 1.0·(1P2_SLA) + 0.5·(1P3_SLA) + 0.01·preemptions`
Weights are a *decision-maker choice*, stated so they can be challenged. J1 heavily penalises missing the 120-minute P1 target and the upper tail, not only the mean.

**Objective J2 — fairness and robustness (minimax across scenarios).**
`J2 = max over s{Base,U1..U5} [ (1P1_SLA_s) + 0.5·(1P2_SLA_s) + 0.5·max(0, max_util_s0.90) + 0.3·(1Jain_s) ] + 0.2·(max_util_Base  min_util_Base)`
where Jain's index is computed on resource utilisations. J2 is *robust* in a defined sense: it is governed by the worst scenario in the supplied set, not by base-case mean delay, and it penalises both unfair load concentration and any planned utilisation above 90%.

A policy that wins J1 in the base case but collapses in U3/U5 will lose J2. That tension drives the recommendation in §5.

# 2. Capacity and offered-load analysis

- Software skill: (7.86+4.99) × 38.50 = **495 min**
- Validation: 42 × 15.74 = **661 min**

With fastest-qualified assignment the real-minute load is roughly 2,800–2,900 min. **Observation:** even perfect pooling cannot clear the base cohort inside one day at nominal shifts; >90% planned utilisation for the day as a whole is unavoidable, and evening arrivals must spill over. Bounds: all-low ordinary load 1,812 min (feasible in aggregate), all-high complex-tail 5,660 min (wildly infeasible) — the expected mix, not the bounds, is the planning case.

## 2.2 Time-block capacity vs load

Available minutes per block (all resources): 08–10: **480**, 10–12: **600**, 12–14: **510**, 14–16: **570**, 16–18: **210**, 18–20: **120**.

Expected base load per block (Intake+diagnostics+Validation): 08–10  **445 min**, 10–12  **586**, 12–14  **455**, 14–16  **651**, 16–18  **392**, 18–20  **192**. Three facts follow before any policy is chosen:

1. **The 16:00 capacity cliff is the largest structural bottleneck.** Arrivals in 16–20 (9 cases, 584 base min) face only 330 available minutes, and after 16:00 Software can only be done by Emil (Deepa has no Software skill). A P2 case arriving at 17:00 that is not finished by 20:00 cannot meet a 480-minute SLA at all, because the next resource starts at 08:00.
2. **Software is skill-scarce in the morning.** Only Chen (1.25) and Farah (1.40, leaves at 12:00) can do Software before noon; Electrical-only Ben and Mechanical-only capacity cannot help. Software load 08–12 154 base min must compete with Electrical 304 base min for Chen/Farah.
3. **14–16 is the Electrical peak** (204 base min Electrical, plus 116 Software competing for Chen/Emil), while Deepa is on break 14:00–14:30 and Farah has left.

Base-equivalent skill capacity (available ÷ multiplier, ignoring sharing across skills) shows why sharing matters: Software 981 base-min in aggregate (Chen 360 + Emil 450 + Farah 171) vs 495 load — aggregate-feasible, temporally fragile. Electrical 1,750 vs 798; Mechanical 1,431 vs 476; Intake and Validation are aggregate-comfortable but Validation is a *convergence* point: every case, however parallel its branches, needs it, so a Validation queue forms whenever diagnostics are released in bursts.

## 2.3 Bottleneck migration under U1–U5

- **U1 Morning surge (×1.7, 08–12; P1 +8 pp).** Morning load rises to 1,752 base min vs 1,080 available; morning P1 cases rise from 2.1 to 5.7. Bottleneck migrates from Software to *everything* before noon, then Farah's 12:00 departure removes the only morning overflow. P1 SLA becomes a queueing impossibility under FCFS.
- **U2 Electrical/Software incident (12–16).** *Assumption (labelled):* E and ES counts double in absolute terms, other types unchanged, proportions then renormalise. Extra 7 cases, +467 base min, of which Electrical +230. Bottleneck becomes Electrical (Chen/Deepa/Emil) with Software as secondary; Validation inherits a later burst.
- **U3 Chen absent.** Loses 450 min, the fastest Electrical resource (1.00) and a Software resource (1.25). Morning Software falls to Farah alone (1.40) until Emil arrives at 12:00 — a single point of failure. Aggregate Software base-capacity falls 981621, still above load, but morning timing collapses.
- **U4 Long-tail day (diagnostic tail probabilities ×2).** Expected diagnostic times rise: Mechanical 26.329.6 (+13%), Electrical 32.837.1 (+13%), Software 38.544.5 (+16%); total +240 base min. The mean shift is modest; the *tail* shift is large (a Software complex draw up to 80 min ×1.40). Bottleneck becomes whoever draws the tail, and parallel branches synchronise to the longest branch.
- **U5 = U1+U3 + Intake outage 09:30–10:00.** Intake blocks all downstream work for 30 min at the worst moment, then releases a surge into a Chen-less morning. Bottleneck sequence: Intake (09:30–10:00)  Software (10:00–12:00)  Validation (afternoon). This is the robustness test, not a tuning case.

Parallel vs sequential arithmetic (why it matters): ME sequential makespan 26.3+32.8=59.1 base min vs parallel max32.8, saving 26 min of *critical path* for +8 coordination, possible +7 handover and +5 team move, and simultaneous consumption of two scarce resources. ES sequential 71.3 vs parallel 38.5, saving 33 min under the same overheads. Parallelism buys latency, not capacity; sequential same-resource execution buys capacity and avoids handovers at the price of latency. Deepa doing ME sequentially (26.3×1.25+32.865.7 min) is slower than parallel Ada/Chen, but frees Chen for Software.

# 3. Candidate policies

All three policies share: P1 > P2 > P3 queue order, FCFS within priority, SLA clock from arrival, at most one preemption per case, 30-min overtime cap. They differ in eligibility, reservation, preemption and parallelisation — the dispatch rules below are the policy.

## Policy S — Strict specialisation, fixed queues
- **Eligibility (fixed lists, in preference order):** Intake {Ada, Ben}; Mechanical {Ada, Ben, Deepa}; Electrical {Chen, Deepa, Ada}; Software {Chen, Emil, Farah}; Validation {Ben, Deepa, Emil}. No resource outside the list may take the activity, even if idle.
- **Assignment:** first idle resource in list order; if none idle, the case waits, regardless of idle non-listed resources.
- **Preemption:** none. P1 jumps the queue but cannot interrupt work in progress.
- **Parallelisation:** always sequential; ME does Mechanical then Electrical, ES does Electrical then Software, preferring the same cross-skilled resource (Deepa for ME, Chen for ES) when it is next in list order.
- **Handover minimisation:** incidental only (no explicit score).

## Policy P — Full pooling, transparent dispatch
- **Eligibility:** any resource whose multiplier is not "" for that activity.
- **Priority score:** (priority rank, ready time). Within P1, FCFS.
- **Assignment:** among *idle* qualified resources, choose the one minimising predicted completion time = now + handover + team-move + setup + base×multiplier; ties broken by longest idle, then lowest busy minutes (a fairness tie-break, not a fairness objective).
- **Preemption:** a ready P1 diagnostic may preempt the lowest-priority (P3, then P2) diagnostic in progress on a qualified resource, if no qualified resource is idle; victim resumes remaining +10% penalty.
- **Parallelisation:** always parallel for ME/ES; +8 min coordination before Validation.
- **Overflow:** not needed — pooling *is* the overflow rule.

## Policy H — Hybrid: reserved P1 capacity + controlled cross-skill overflow (recommended candidate)
- **Eligibility:** all qualified resources, *except* reserved resources are withheld from P2/P3 (see below).
- **Reservation:** Mechanical reserved {Ada, Deepa}; Electrical reserved {Deepa, Chen}; Software reserved {Chen, Emil}; Intake/Validation unreserved. A reserved resource is offered to a P2/P3 case only if **(a)** no P1 case is waiting for that skill *and* the resource has been idle 10 min, **or (b)** the oldest waiting P2/P3 case for that skill has waited 30 min *and* no non-reserved qualified resource is idle. Reservations therefore expire audibly, not silently.
- **Priority score:** (priority rank, ready time), with **aging:** a P3 case waiting >240 min is treated as P2, and >720 min as P1 for dispatch only (its SLA clock and reporting priority are unchanged). This is the anti-starvation device; see §6.
- **Assignment:** among eligible idle resources, minimise predicted completion time, with a 100 min score bonus for retaining the previous resource on the *second* sequential branch (encoding avoided handover + team move), and a fairness term: add `0.05 × busy_minutes` to the score so a resource at 90% is not repeatedly chosen over one at 50% when completion times are within ~5 min.
- **Preemption:** P1 may preempt a **P3** diagnostic only (never P2), only if no reserved resource can start it within 15 min, at most once per case. P2 victims are never preempted under H — that is the deliberate difference from P.
- **Parallelisation (threshold):** parallel only if the case is P1, *or* the oldest diagnostic queue wait is 20 min, *or* both branches can start immediately on two different idle qualified resources. Otherwise sequential, same-resource preferred. Coordination overhead is thus spent only when it buys SLA-relevant latency.
- **Evening guard (16:00 onward):** do not *start* a P3 diagnostic whose predicted completion exceeds 20:30; carry it to next shift deliberately rather than starting it into the 20:00 cliff. P1/P2 starts are exempt.

# 5. Robust recommendation

**Primary policy: H (hybrid reserved P1 capacity + controlled cross-skill overflow), with the thresholds in §3, operated in two modes.**

*Why H under J2:* its worst-scenario P1 SLA (U5: 38–52%) is 13–37 pp above S and 13–12 pp above P's U5 range at the midpoint, its max utilisation stays closest to (but mostly below) the 90–105% fragility band, and its handover burden is ~40% below P's because parallelisation and resource changes are rationed. Under J1 it also wins on p90 and P1 SLA in all scenarios; it only concedes base-case *mean* delay to P by ~10 min, which J1's tail weights outweigh.

**Fallback / surge mode (still H, reservations relaxed).** Switch when any trigger fires, and switch back when all clear for 30 min:
- P1 diagnostic queue wait >10 min, or any P1 case exceeds 75 min elapsed without starting Validation  release all reservations to P1 *and* allow P2-victim preemption (i.e., temporarily behave like P for P1 only).
- Software queue 4 cases or oldest Software wait >30 min  Farah (before 12:00) and Emil are dedicated to Software; Electrical overflow goes to Deepa/Ada; ES branches go sequential.
- Any resource's forecast utilisation >90% for the next 2 hours  stop parallel starts for P2/P3 (sequential only), enforce same-resource retention, defer P3 starts that would cross 20:30.
- Chen absent (U3/U5 flag) or Intake outage logged  morning Software reserve activates: Farah is Software-only 08:00–12:00, Deepa is Electrical-first 10:00–12:00, Intake goes to Ada/Ben only.

**When another policy would be preferable — stated plainly:**
- If demand fell sustainably below ~60% of base (e.g., a quiet week), reservations idle and P's full pooling gives lower mean delay with no SLA risk; H's reservation-release rule (idle 10 min) partially self-corrects this, but pure P is simpler then.
- If the organisation prioritised *learning and skill retention* over delay (e.g., a training period where Farah must not be pulled off Mechanical), S's fixed queues are auditable and predictable — but §4 shows S must be accepted as fragile to any absence or surge, and should never be the surge policy.
- If P1 volume were much higher than 10–18% (say >35%), reserved capacity sized for ~5 P1/day would be overwhelmed and H degenerates to P with extra rules; the reservation *set* would need resizing, not just relaxing.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

- **Gaming of estimates.** If dispatch uses *self-reported* expected service times, resources can inflate estimates to avoid hard cases or deflate them to win easy ones. Mitigation: dispatch score uses the *table* base draw × published multiplier, never a self-report; actuals are audited against planned (see §7).
- **Starvation of P3.** H's aging (P3P2 at 240 min, P1-for-dispatch at 720 min) prevents indefinite deferral, but in U5 P3 *will* be carried over. The risk is silent starvation: report P3 age distribution, not just SLA %, and cap deferral at 24 h (the P3 target) with escalation to a supervisor.
- **Skill erosion.** Permanent reservation (e.g., Farah always Software) erodes her Mechanical/Electrical currency and creates a single point of failure. Mitigation: rotate reserved roles weekly; track skill-use counts per resource (§7) and treat a resource doing >70% one skill over a month as a warning.
- **Excessive preemption.** P preempts P2 victims freely; each preemption costs remaining×10% plus a setup on resume, and repeated preemption of the same case is capped at once but can still thrash across cases. H's P3-only rule is the control; monitor preemptions per P1 case and penalty minutes.
- **Coordination overhead.** Always-parallel (P) pays 8 min coordination plus double handovers on every ME/ES case (~13 cases/day  >100 min/day of pure overhead). H's threshold spends this only for P1 or congested queues.
- **New bottleneck at Validation.** Releasing diagnostics in parallel bursts creates a Validation queue that then drives delay for *all* priorities. Mitigation: cap parallel starts when Validation queue 3, and never let a P3 diagnostic start if Validation queue 4 and P1 queue >0.
- **Fairness across people, not just numbers.** Jain's index can look fair while Farah (part-time, 08–12) absorbs all morning pain and Emil absorbs all evening pain. Report utilisation *and* overtime minutes *and* P1-case counts per resource; cap overtime at 30 min as given, and do not let the fairness tie-break in H systematically favour the same resource.
- **Human interpretability.** A dispatch rule an operator cannot explain in one sentence will be overridden. H's rules are: "P1 first; reserved experts wait for P1 unless idle 10 min or you have waited 30; same person finishes a case if they can; parallel only for P1 or a real queue." Every assignment decision is logged with its eligible set and rule fired (§7), so overrides are visible.

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design

## 7.1 Deployment indicators (computed daily, by block and scenario flag)
Arrival rate and P1 share by block; queue length and oldest-wait by skill × priority; SLA-clock remaining for the oldest P1/P2 case; resource utilisation (busy ÷ available, Day-0 cohort basis as in §4) and overtime minutes; handover count and minutes per case; setup count; preemption count and penalty minutes; parallel share of ME/ES and coordination minutes; service time actual vs planned (actual ÷ (base×multiplier)); Validation queue length; P3 age percentiles; reserved-resource idle minutes while P2/P3 waited (wasted reservation detector).

## 7.2 Event attributes (one row per activity interval, plus decision events)
`case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `start_timestamp`, `time:timestamp`, `org:resource`, plus: queue-entry timestamp, assignment-decision timestamp, eligible-resource set (list), skill used, planned service minutes, actual service minutes, pause/resume timestamps and reason (preemption/break/outage), handover flag and minutes, team-move flag, setup flag and minutes, parallel/sequential flag, policy version (e.g., H-v1.3, surge-mode on/off), scenario/shock flag (Base/U1–U5, Chen-absent, IT-outage), priority, case type, SLA clock at assignment, and dispatch rule fired. Pause/resume events are essential: without them, preempted work looks like slow service.

## 7.3 Which PM4Py analyses reveal what
- **Drift:** `pm4py.discover_directly_follows_graph` / inductive miner on logs split by week or scenario flag; compare process variants and timestamp gaps by block. A new Validation bottleneck appears as a growing gap between last diagnostic and Validation start in the performance view (`pm4py.get_performance` / waiting-time analysis per activity, *not* case-level totals).
- **Handover burden:** `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` — edge weights between resources are handover counts; a thick AdaChen edge with high waiting time is the team-move cost made visible. Compare networks S vs P vs H.
- **Service-time changes:** per-activity service-time distributions by resource (`pm4py.get_service_time` per activity, and timestamp differences startcomplete grouped by `org:resource` × `concept:name`); a shift in the complex-tail share shows as a bimodal service distribution in U4.
- **Bottleneck migration:** waiting time by activity × scenario flag; the bottleneck is whichever activity's waiting share grows under the shock (Software in U3, Electrical in U2, Intake in U5).
- **Conformance / policy audit:** replay the log against the declared policy rules (eligible set, reservation state) as a declarative check; violations are either overrides or bugs, both worth seeing.
- **Case-level vs stage-level labelling (critical):** `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` adds *case-level totals across all activities* (`case:service_time`, `case:waiting_time`). Those totals must never be reported as "Software waiting time". Stage-specific service/waiting is computed separately from interval timestamps grouped by activity, as the code in §8 does.

CASE_TYPES = {"M":["Mechanical"], "E":["Electrical"], "S":["Software"],
              "ME":["Mechanical","Electrical"], "ES":["Electrical","Software"]}
BLOCKS = [  # start, expected, P1/P2/P3 pct, M/E/S/ME/ES pct
    (0,7,   (10,55,35),(30,25,15,20,10)), (120,9, (15,55,30),(25,25,15,25,10)),
    (240,7, (12,58,30),(22,28,18,20,12)), (360,10,(18,52,30),(20,30,18,20,12)),
    (480,6, (10,50,40),(20,25,25,15,15)), (600,3, (5,45,50), (25,20,30,10,15)),

         "Electrical":["Chen","Deepa","Ada"], "Software":["Chen","Emil","Farah"],
         "Validation":["Ben","Deepa","Emil"]}  # Policy S preference order

def sample_service(act, rng, tail_mult=1.0):
    olo,ohi,clo,chi,p = SERVICE[act]
    if act in DIAG: p = min(0.9, p*tail_mult)
    return rng.uniform(clo,chi) if rng.random() < p else rng.uniform(olo,ohi)

def poissonish_count(x, rng):  # floor + Bernoulli(fraction): preserves expectation
    return int(math.floor(x)) + (1 if rng.random() < (x-math.floor(x)) else 0)

def generate_cases(scenario, seed):
    rng = random.Random(1000*seed + hash(scenario) % 1000)
    tail_mult = 2.0 if scenario == "U4" else 1.0
    cases, idx = [], 0
    for bi,(start,exp,prio,tpct) in enumerate(BLOCKS):
        p1,p2,p3 = prio; e = float(exp)
        if scenario in ("U1","U5") and bi in (0,1):  # morning surge
            e *= 1.7; p1 += 8; rest = 100-p1; p2,p3 = p2*rest/(p2+p3), p3*rest/(p2+p3)
        type_counts = [e*t/100.0 for t in tpct]
        if scenario == "U2" and bi in (2,3):  # E and ES double (absolute), renormalised by construction
            type_counts[1] *= 2; type_counts[4] *= 2
        for ti,tname in enumerate(["M","E","S","ME","ES"]):
            for _ in range(poissonish_count(type_counts[ti], rng)):
                r = rng.random()*100
                pr = "P1" if r < p1 else ("P2" if r < p1+p2 else "P3")
                acts = ["Intake"] + CASE_TYPES[tname] + ["Validation"]
                cases.append({"id":f"C{idx:03d}", "type":tname, "priority":pr,
                    "arrival": start + rng.uniform(0,119),
                    "draws": {a: sample_service(a, rng, tail_mult) for a in acts},
                    "parallel": None})
                idx += 1
    return sorted(cases, key=lambda c: c["arrival"])

def intervals_for(name, ndays=3):
    _, s, e, brk = RESOURCES[name]; out = []
    for d in range(ndays):
        base = d*1440; lo, hi = base+s, base+e
        if brk: out += [(lo, base+brk[0]), (base+brk[1], hi)]
        else: out.append((lo, hi))
    return sorted(out)

def find_start(intervals, earliest, dur):
    """Earliest start >= earliest fitting in one interval; may finish <=30 into overtime."""
    for lo, hi in intervals:
        st = max(earliest, lo)
        if st >= hi: continue
        if st + dur <= hi + 30: return st, max(0.0, st+dur-hi)
    return (intervals[-1][1] if intervals else earliest), 0.0

def simulate(cases_in, policy, scenario):
    absent = {"Chen"} if scenario in ("U3","U5") else set()
    outage = (90,120) if scenario == "U5" else None  # 09:30-10:00 Intake block
    cases = {c["id"]: dict(c, prev_res=None, last_diag=None, branches_done=0,
                           preempted=False, completed=None, seq_second=False) for c in cases_in}
    intervals = {n: intervals_for(n) for n in RESOURCES}
    avail0 = {n: sum(max(0,min(hi,1440)-lo) for lo,hi in iv if lo<1440) for n,iv in intervals.items()}
    R = {n: {"cur":None, "last_diag":None, "busy0":0.0, "busy_tot":0.0, "ot":0.0,
             "hand":0.0, "setup":0.0, "idle_since":intervals[n][0][0] if intervals[n] else 0}
         for n in RESOURCES}
    ready, events, stats = [], [], {"preempt":0, "penalty":0.0, "coord":0.0}
    def enqueue(cid, act, t, fixed=None):
        ready.append({"cid":cid, "act":act, "ready":t, "enq":t, "fixed":fixed})
    for c in cases.values(): enqueue(c["id"], "Intake", c["arrival"])
    def eligible(act):
        if policy == "S": return [n for n in FIXED[act] if n not in absent]
        return [n for n,(m,_,_,_) in RESOURCES.items() if act in m and n not in absent]
    def duration(cid, act, res, fixed):
        c = cases[cid]; m = RESOURCES[res][0][act]
        base = (fixed if fixed is not None else c["draws"][act]*m)
        hand = 7.0 if (c["prev_res"] and c["prev_res"] != res) else 0.0
        team = 5.0 if (act in DIAG and c["last_diag"] and c["last_diag"] != act
                       and c["prev_res"] and c["prev_res"] != res) else 0.0
        setup = 5.0 if (act in DIAG and R[res]["last_diag"] and R[res]["last_diag"] != act) else 0.0
        return base+hand+team+setup, hand, team, setup
    def try_assign(now):
        """One pass over ready entries; returns True if anything was assigned/preempted."""
        def rank(e):
            pr = {"P1":0,"P2":1,"P3":2}[cases[e["cid"]]["priority"]]
            c = cases[e["cid"]]
            if policy == "H":  # aging for dispatch only
                wait = now - c["arrival"]
                if pr == 2 and wait > 720: pr = 0
                elif pr == 2 and wait > 240: pr = 1
            return (pr, e["ready"], c["arrival"])
        for e in sorted([x for x in ready if x["ready"] <= now], key=rank):
            cand = []
            for res in eligible(e["act"]):
                if R[res]["cur"] is not None: continue
                dur, hand, team, setup = duration(e["cid"], e["act"], res, e["fixed"])
                st, ot = find_start(intervals[res], now, dur)
                if outage and e["act"] == "Intake" and st < outage[1] and st+dur > outage[0]:
                    st, ot = find_start(intervals[res], outage[1], dur)
                if st <= now + 1e-9: cand.append((res, dur, hand, team, setup, st, ot))
            if not cand and policy != "S" and e["act"] in DIAG and cases[e["cid"]]["priority"] == "P1":
                # Preemption: P preempts P2/P3; H preempts P3 only. Victim resumes remaining + 10%.
                victims = []
                for res,(m,_,_,_) in RESOURCES.items():
                    j = R[res]["cur"]
                    if not j or j["act"] not in DIAG or res not in eligible(e["act"]): continue
                    vc = cases[j["cid"]]
                    if vc["priority"] == "P1" or vc["preempted"]: continue
                    if policy == "H" and vc["priority"] != "P3": continue
                    victims.append(({"P3":0,"P2":1}[vc["priority"]], -(j["finish"]-now), res, j))
                if victims:
                    _,_,vres,j = sorted(victims)[0]; vc = cases[j["cid"]]
                    rem = j["finish"]-now; pen = 0.10*rem
                    vc["preempted"] = True; stats["preempt"] += 1; stats["penalty"] += pen
                    R[vres]["cur"] = None; R[vres]["idle_since"] = now
                    ready.append({"cid":j["cid"], "act":j["act"], "ready":now, "enq":now,
                                  "fixed":rem+pen})
                    ready.remove(e); return True
                continue
            if not cand: continue
            if policy == "S":
                order = FIXED[e["act"]]; cand.sort(key=lambda x: order.index(x[0]))
            elif policy == "P":
                cand.sort(key=lambda x: (x[5]+x[1], R[x[0]]["busy_tot"], x[0]))
            else:  # Hybrid: non-reserved first; reserved only on overflow triggers
                nonres = [x for x in cand if x[0] not in RESERVED[e["act"]]]
                pool = nonres
                if not pool:
                    oldest = min((now - x["enq"] for x in ready if x["act"]==e["act"]), default=0)
                    p1_waiting = any(x["act"]==e["act"] and cases[x["cid"]]["priority"]=="P1" for x in ready)
                    idle_ok = all((now - R[x[0]]["idle_since"] >= 10) or not p1_waiting for x in cand)
                    if oldest >= 30 or idle_ok: pool = cand
                if not pool: continue
                def score(x):
                    s = x[5]+x[1] + 0.05*R[x[0]]["busy_tot"]
                    if cases[e["cid"]].get("seq_second") and x[0] == cases[e["cid"]]["prev_res"]: s -= 100
                    return s
                pool.sort(key=score); cand = pool
            # Evening guard (H only): do not start P3 diagnostic finishing after 20:30
            if policy == "H" and cases[e["cid"]]["priority"] == "P3" and e["act"] in DIAG:
                if cand[0][5] + cand[0][1] > 750: continue
            res, dur, hand, team, setup, st, ot = cand[0]
            R[res]["cur"] = {"cid":e["cid"], "act":e["act"], "start":st, "finish":st+dur}
            R[res]["busy_tot"] += dur; R[res]["busy0"] += max(0.0, min(st+dur,1440)-st)
            R[res]["ot"] += ot; R[res]["hand"] += hand+team; R[res]["setup"] += setup
            ready.remove(e); return True
        return False
    now, guard = 0.0, 0
    while any(c["completed"] is None for c in cases.values()) and guard < 20000:
        guard += 1
        while try_assign(now): pass
        nxt = [c["arrival"] for c in cases.values() if c["arrival"] > now]
        nxt += [R[n]["cur"]["finish"] for n in R if R[n]["cur"]]
        nxt += [e["ready"] for e in ready if e["ready"] > now]
        nxt += [lo for n in R if R[n]["cur"] is None for lo,hi in intervals[n] if lo > now]
        nxt = [t for t in nxt if t > now + 1e-9]
        if not nxt: break
        now = min(nxt)
        for res in list(R):
            j = R[res]["cur"]
            if j and j["finish"] <= now + 1e-9:
                c = cases[j["cid"]]; act = j["act"]
                events.append({"case:concept:name":j["cid"], "concept:name":act,
                               "start_min":j["start"], "end_min":j["finish"], "org:resource":res})
                c["prev_res"] = res
                if act in DIAG:
                    c["last_diag"] = act; R[res]["last_diag"] = act; c["branches_done"] += 1
                R[res]["cur"] = None; R[res]["idle_since"] = j["finish"]
                if act == "Intake":
                    brs = CASE_TYPES[c["type"]]
                    if len(brs) == 1: enqueue(c["id"], brs[0], j["finish"])
                    else:
                        if policy == "S": par = False
                        elif policy == "P": par = True
                        else:  # H threshold: P1, or both branches immediately startable
                            par = (c["priority"] == "P1" or
                                   all(any(R[n]["cur"] is None for n in eligible(b)) for b in brs))
                        c["parallel"] = par
                        if par: [enqueue(c["id"], b, j["finish"]) for b in brs]
                        else: enqueue(c["id"], brs[0], j["finish"])
                elif act in DIAG:
                    brs = CASE_TYPES[c["type"]]
                    if c["branches_done"] < len(brs):
                        c["seq_second"] = True; enqueue(c["id"], brs[c["branches_done"]], j["finish"])
                    else:
                        vt = j["finish"] + (8.0 if c["parallel"] else 0.0)
                        if c["parallel"]: stats["coord"] += 8.0
                        enqueue(c["id"], "Validation", vt)
                else:
                    c["completed"] = j["finish"]
    done = [c for c in cases.values() if c["completed"] is not None]
    delays = np.array([c["completed"]-c["arrival"] for c in done])
    def sla(pr, target):
        sub = [c["completed"]-c["arrival"] for c in done if c["priority"]==pr]
        return float(np.mean([d <= target for d in sub])) if sub else float("nan")
    utils = {n: (R[n]["busy_tot"]/avail0[n] if avail0[n] else 0.0) for n in R if n not in absent}
    u = np.array(list(utils.values()))
    jain = float(u.sum()**2/(len(u)*(u**2).sum())) if len(u) and (u**2).sum() else float("nan")
    burden = (sum(R[n]["hand"]+R[n]["setup"] for n in R) + stats["coord"]) / max(1,len(done))
    return {"mean_delay":float(np.mean(delays)), "p90":float(np.percentile(delays,90)),
            "p95":float(np.percentile(delays,95)), "P1_SLA":sla("P1",120), "P2_SLA":sla("P2",480),
            "P3_SLA":sla("P3",1440), "max_util":float(u.max()) if len(u) else 0.0,
            "jain":jain, "burden":float(burden), "preemptions":stats["preempt"],
            "incomplete":len(cases)-len(done)}, events

# 9. Limits and validation plan

## 9.1 What cannot be concluded from expected arrivals and ranges alone
- The *realised* arrival process (fixed vs Poisson vs bursty), and correlations between priority, type and complex-tail draws, are unknown; A3's independence assumption could understate tail delay substantially.
- Whether handovers/setup occupy the resource or merely delay the case, whether interrupted work restarts from zero or resumes (A7), and the exact team definition (A8) all move absolute numbers, though not the policy ordering in §4.
- Resource behaviour (multitasking, learning, fatigue, break compliance), the scope of the U5 IT outage (Intake only, as modelled, vs all activities), and whether U2's doubling is absolute or renormalised within a fixed total (§2.3) are all unspecified.
- Therefore: no claim is made about a *specific* SLA percentage in reality. The defensible claims are directional and structural: total load exceeds same-day capacity, Software/Electrical are skill-scarce at specific times, the 16:00 cliff dominates evening SLA, and fixed queues fail first under absence/surge.

## 9.2 Pilot / backtest design that could distinguish the policies
1. **Backtest first (no risk).** Replay 4–8 weeks of actual event logs (with the §7 attributes, including eligible set and pause/resume) through the §8 simulator under S, P and H. Compare simulated vs actual delay/SLA; calibrate arrival process and service distributions; only a policy whose *backtest* error is smallest and whose P1 SLA is non-inferior earns a live pilot.
2. **Shadow mode (2 weeks).** Run H's dispatch score in parallel with current practice, logging what H *would* have assigned. Measure disagreement rate and would-be handovers; no case is affected.
3. **Stepped pilot, P1 protected.** Randomise policy by *day* (not by case, to avoid contamination), stratified by weekday, for 6–10 weeks, with P1 cases always dispatchable under the stricter of the two policies being compared (a safety rule: no P1 case may wait >15 min for a diagnostic resource in the pilot arm). Pre-register the primary endpoint (P1 SLA) with a non-inferiority margin of 5 pp and secondary endpoints (p90 delay, P3 age, Jain, handover burden).
4. **Sample-size honesty.** At ~5 P1 cases/day, detecting a 10 pp P1 SLA difference needs on the order of 300+ P1 cases per arm — i.e., many weeks. Use the simulation to size the pilot, use sequential monitoring with a pre-set stopping rule, and do not claim a P1 effect from a handful of days. P2/P3 and process metrics (waiting by activity, handover network) will distinguish policies far sooner.
5. **Abort criteria.** Stop an arm if P1 SLA falls >10 pp below the other arm on a rolling 50-P1 window, if any resource exceeds 30 min overtime on >20% of days, or if P3 age exceeds 24 h for >5% of P3 cases. Roll out only the winning policy, with the §7 monitoring live from day one, and re-run the §4 table quarterly against actuals.
