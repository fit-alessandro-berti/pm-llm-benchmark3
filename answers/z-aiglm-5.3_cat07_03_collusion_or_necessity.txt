FE_PAIRS = [
    ("Create Purchase Order",   "Approve Purchase Order"),          # normative rule
    ("Change Vendor Bank Details", "Payment Approval"),             # normative rule
    ("Emergency Override",      "Retrospective Justification"),     # normative rule (different person)
    ("Vendor Check",            "Change Vendor Bank Details"),      # candidate rule - may be role design
]

def four_eyes_report(log, label=""):
    """filter_four_eyes_principle keeps cases where the SAME resource
    performed both activities, i.e. the violations."""
    rows = []
    for a, b in FE_PAIRS:
        viol = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, a, b)
        rows.append({
            "pair": f"{a} -> {b}",
            "violating_cases": viol["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
            "top_resources": viol.groupby("org:resource")["case:concept:name"]
                                  .nunique().sort_values(ascending=False).head(3).to_dict(),
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

print(four_eyes_report(df, "as-recorded"))

- *Benign 1 — genuine rush channel:* pre-negotiated framework pricing with standing quotes; approval is confirmation of a pre-agreed exception. **Supports:** 88%/79% emergency flags, framework contracts on file, quotes predating approval, mostly on-time justifications. **Refutes:** approvals precede quote receipt; prices outside framework terms; late justifications clustering on high amounts.
- *Benign 2 — on-call/weekend triage:* genuine facilities emergencies handled by an on-call manager (Hugo's stated role). **Supports:** weekend timestamps, work orders, on-call rosters. **Refutes:** equally fast approvals on ordinary weekdays without emergency flags.
- *Benign 3 — verbal-then-batched entry:* approvals given verbally, entered later in batches. **Supports:** clustered identical timestamps, different `entered_by`. **Refutes:** naturally spread timestamps.

**D. Same-person retrospective justification (17 cases)**
- *Benign 1 — quarter-close batch migration:* the spreadsheet was uploaded after the fact; the actual justifier differed from the overrider, and "same person" reflects data entry, not performance. **Supports:** 12/17 identical-to-the-minute timestamps, `quarter_close_upload` provenance, a spreadsheet author column naming different people. **Refutes:** `entered_by` = overrider on distinct sessions; the 5 non-batch cases unexplained.
- *Benign 2 — proxy entry under a shared login:* an assistant entered the justification under the manager's account. **Supports:** access logs showing assistant sessions; documented temporary access. **Refutes:** no alternative sessions on the account.

**E. High co-working frequency (Aline/Elena 510, Boris/Felix 495)**
- *Benign 1 — team-based routing:* Team A / Team B assignment rules. **Supports:** stable routing rules, near-median timings, low amounts, low manual share (4–6%). **Refutes:** would require evidence of routing-rule overrides concentrating risk cases — frequency itself proves nothing.
- *Benign 2 — bot ownership artifact:* Bot_PO creates most POs (1,730 cases) while the buyer remains case owner, making buyer–approver co-occurrence structurally inevitable. **Supports:** `entered_by = Bot_PO` on the relevant cases.

**F. Vendor Check + bank change by same person (31 cases)**
- *Benign 1 — role design:* both are vendor-master team activities. **Supports:** pattern uniform across the team; no normative rule requiring segregation; documented procedures. **Refutes:** control matrix explicitly requires separation.
- *Benign 2 — mass-maintenance campaign:* vendor-master staff executed both steps for the platform migration. **Supports:** migration ticket; uniform change to one new banking platform.

**G. Bot_Match  AP_Kim approvals at ~6 min (210 cases)**
- *Benign 1 — exception queue cleared in working sessions:* 6-minute median reflects bothuman handoff timestamps, not review duration. **Supports:** exception codes resolved with notes; actual review times visible in the AP system. **Refutes:** approvals within seconds with no exception resolution, concentrated on high amounts.

**4.1 Four-eyes filtering (as-recorded vs. reattributed)**

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

df = pd.read_csv("p2p_events.csv")
df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"])
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id="case:concept:name",
                            activity_key="concept:name",
                            timestamp_key="time:timestamp")
# all other columns (org:resource, vendor_id, source_system, ...) are preserved

FE_PAIRS = [
    ("Create Purchase Order", "Approve Purchase Order"),          # normative rule
    ("Change Vendor Bank Details", "Payment Approval"),           # normative rule
    ("Emergency Override", "Retrospective Justification"),        # normative rule
    ("Vendor Check", "Change Vendor Bank Details"),               # candidate rule (role design?)
]

def four_eyes_report(log):
    # filter_four_eyes_principle KEEPS cases where the SAME resource
    # performed both activities -- i.e., the violations.
    rows = []
    for a, b in FE_PAIRS:
        v = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, a, b)
        rows.append({
            "pair": f"{a} -> {b}",
            "violating_cases": v["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
            "top_resources": v.groupby("org:resource")["case:concept:name"]
                               .nunique().sort_values(ascending=False).head(3).to_dict(),
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

print(four_eyes_report(df))          # as recorded

# --- Reattribute identities before concluding anything ---
# legacy_erp conflated Bot_PO with the human approver; assistants used Gita's account.
def reattribute(r):
    if r["source_system"] == "legacy_erp":
        if r["concept:name"] == "Create Purchase Order" and pd.notna(r.get("entered_by")):
            return r["entered_by"]
        if r["concept:name"] == "Approve Purchase Order" and pd.notna(r.get("approved_by")):
            return r["approved_by"]
    return r["org:resource"]

df["org:resource_raw"] = df["org:resource"]
df["org:resource"] = df.apply(reattribute, axis=1)
print(four_eyes_report(df))          # after reattribution -> how many violations survive?
```

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

## Evidence that would increase concern

- The beneficiary account at `Release Payment` differs from both the pre-migration verified account and any documented vendor notification; or V-77x bank details were changed and then **reverted within days** after payment (classic diversion signature).
- New IBANs shared across multiple `vendor_id`s within V-77x, or new vendor contact phone/email matching an employee or another vendor.
- The migration work order's dates and vendor list do **not** cover the 16 fast cases, or fast bank-changepayment cases exist for non-V-77x vendors.
- Access logs show the VendorMaster_Lena or AP_Kim account active outside the holder's working hours, from atypical IPs/devices, or Lena's bank change and Kim's approval originating from the **same session/IP** within the 17-minute window.
- No call-back verification records exist for any V-77x bank change, despite policy requiring them.
- After reattribution, a residual set of same-person create+approve POs with distinct session IDs, `emergency_flag=False`, and high `amount_band`; or approvals timestamped before quote receipt.
- The 5 non-batch same-person justifications show `entered_by` = overrider, distinct timestamps, and no assistant sessions on the account.
- `emergency_flag` set by the same buyer who later features in the fast approval, correlated with vendor or amount rather than genuine urgency.
- AP_Kim match-exception approvals completed within seconds with no exception codes resolved, concentrated on V-77x.

## Evidence that would reduce concern

- A migration work order covering exactly the 16 fast cases' vendors and dates; signed bank-confirmation letters from vendor contacts; call-back logs to independently verified phone numbers.
- New IBANs belong to the same legal vendor (bank records match vendor name); no account sharing across vendor_ids.
- After reattribution, most of the 42 PO violations disappear (`legacy_erp` mapping), and the survivors are covered by a documented emergency delegation with on-time, different-person justifications.
- The quarter-close spreadsheet contains an "actual justifier" column naming different people, with pre-upload timestamps.
- Approver_Gita account sessions map one-to-one to pre-approved temporary access for named assistants.
- Fast emergency approvals carry complete quotes, framework-contract pricing, and mostly on-time justifications; late ones coincide with documented workload peaks.
- Temp_Nina's access window matches approved onboarding/deprovisioning, with activity limited to data entry.
- `manual_upload` cases show separate, sequential sessions with sensible durations, confirming the upload-time artifact explanation.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

> Internal Audit reviewed the quarter's procurement-to-payment event log using process-mining techniques. The review identified control exceptions and data-quality weaknesses; it did not identify direct evidence of misconduct, and nothing in this report should be read as a finding of fraud or collusion against any individual.
>
> Three matters stand out. First, 42 purchase orders (1.8% of cases) were created and approved by the same recorded resource, and 17 retrospective justifications were entered by the same resource that performed the emergency override. However, a legacy-system mapping error conflates bot and human identities, one approver account is shared with assistants, and 12 of the 17 justifications were batch-loaded from a quarter-close spreadsheet with identical timestamps. Several exceptions may therefore be recording defects rather than actual control failures, and attribution must be resolved before conclusions are drawn.
>
> Second, most unusually fast approvals occur in the emergency medical-supplies and facilities-repair channels. High emergency-flag rates, low vendor concentration, and normal correction patterns are consistent with a genuine rush channel; the 18–21% rate of late retrospective justification nonetheless indicates weak documentation discipline.
>
> Third, and of most concern, a cluster of 44 cases centred on vendor family V-77x combines bank-detail changes followed within minutes by payment approval, high amounts, and predominantly manual submissions. This is consistent with a legitimate banking-platform migration executed with weak process linkage, but the combination warrants priority verification given its payment-diversion risk profile.
>
> We recommend targeted follow-up: reconcile V-77x bank changes to maintenance tickets, vendor confirmations, and payment beneficiary records; confirm the delegation-of-authority position on emergency self-approval; remediate shared credentials and the legacy mapping defect; and re-run segregation-of-duties analytics after data correction.
