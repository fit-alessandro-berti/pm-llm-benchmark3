# 2. Scenario forecasts

## Optimistic scenario

- **Handover pattern:** Local specialist loops drop meaningfully. Shared-service edges are clean and rule-based. Cross-team ad hoc handovers fall because the central queue provides cover. Total handovers stay flat or fall slightly.
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** Technical Shared Service waits remain near 4h median and do not grow. Legal/Risk waits rise only modestly despite broader detection. Shared approval median wait is below current supervisor wait. East benefits because E_Tech overload is pooled.
- **Cycle-time distribution:** Average cycle time improves slightly. p90 improves in East and remains stable in North/South. High-complexity p90 does not worsen.
- **Rework and quality:** Routine rework falls due standardization. Complex cases retain enough local context, so rework does not rise. Legal/risk detection catches real risks with limited over-referral.
- **Role clarity and accountability:** Routing rules are clear; each shared queue has an owner. `reassignment_count` stays stable. Role discovery shows fewer overlapping roles.
- **Fairness implications:** East improves; North/South not disadvantaged. Language/regional customer segments still receive local contact and context-aware specialist advice.
- **Early warning signs:** If shared-service wait times trend upward, legal/risk wait exceeds baseline, or high-complexity rework rises, the optimistic path is failing.

## Central scenario

- **Handover pattern:** Local embedded tech loops are replaced by shared-service edges. Total handovers remain similar or increase slightly because of queue pull, return, and shared approval. Cross-team handovers decrease.
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** Technical Shared Service wait rises for high-complexity cases. Legal/Risk worsens because demand rises from 9 to 12 per 100 and capacity is already near 93%/91%. Shared approval is moderate but adds a new queue.
- **Cycle-time distribution:** Average cycle time may improve or stay flat due to pooling and simple-case standardization. Tail latency worsens for high-complexity, legal/risk, and regional/language cases. East p90 improves; North/South p90 may worsen slightly.
- **Rework and quality:** Routine rework stable or lower. Complex/regional rework rises modestly due to context loss and more handovers. Over-referral increases specialist load.
- **Role clarity and accountability:** Rules help at the front door, but accountability at queue boundaries is murky. `reassignment_count` and “waiting for owner” states increase.
- **Fairness implications:** East gains; North/South complex cases and certain customer segments lose. Legal/risk segments wait longer. Team-level variation narrows except in tails.
- **Early warning signs:** Technical wait >4h, legal/risk wait >10.5h, p90 high-complexity rising, local tech informal advice edges disappearing, rework after shared service > baseline.

## Adverse scenario

- **Handover pattern:** Central queues create many extra pull/return handoffs. Local caseworkers route to Technical Shared Service, then to shared approval, then back to implementation. Total handovers increase despite fewer local tech loops.
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** Central bottleneck becomes severe. Technical Shared Service and Legal/Risk wait times dominate cycle time. Shared approval backlog grows. High-complexity cases queue longest.
- **Cycle-time distribution:** Average may still look stable if simple cases are fast, but p90 and p99 blow out. East may improve initially, then worsen as central queue saturates. North/South lose local loops and gain central waits.
- **Rework and quality:** Context loss increases rework. Difficult QC queue creates rework loops. Legal/risk over-referral and delay worsen outcomes for complex/regional cases.
- **Role clarity and accountability:** Ownership is unclear. Removed local supervisors create escalation gaps. `reassignment_count` rises; cross-team cover may return informally.
- **Fairness implications:** Customer segments needing language/regional accommodation face more handovers and longer waits. North/South lose local expertise. Legal/risk cases are unfairly delayed. East may benefit only temporarily.
- **Early warning signs:** Central resource utilization >90% while waits rise, variants show extra review loops, rework after shared activities increases, local_expertise_flag cases have worse SLA misses.

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1. **Tail latency:** Average cycle time can improve while p90/p99 worsen, especially for high-complexity or legal/risk cases stuck behind a central queue. Performance DFG and case-duration quantiles by segment reveal this.
2. **High-complexity cases:** These are a small share, so they barely move the average. If they wait longer, rework more, and miss more SLAs, the process may be less reliable even if averages look better.
3. **Local knowledge loss:** Removing embedded tech may eliminate informal advice that prevented unnecessary referrals. This appears as fewer local tech  caseworker edges, more specialist referrals, and higher rework for `local_expertise_flag` cases.
4. **Fairness:** Standardization may reduce team-level variation, but central routing can disadvantage language/regional customer segments or teams with complex local conventions. Average metrics hide segment-level wait and rework gaps.
5. **Learning erosion:** If hard cases are routed away, local staff stop developing technical skill. Resource-activity similarity and role discovery can show local caseworkers becoming less technically diverse over time.
6. **Accountability:** Shared queues can make ownership unclear. Average cycle time may not show blame-shifting, but `reassignment_count`, waiting states between queues, and role overlap can.
7. **Exception handling:** Centralization often works for standard cases but fails for exceptions. Variant analysis and filtered DFGs for rare/high-complexity cases can reveal extra loops and manual workarounds.
8. **Resilience:** Pooling can improve cover, but a central bottleneck creates a single point of failure. Absence or peak-load analysis can show wait-time spikes hidden by monthly averages.
9. **Over-referral:** Broader legal/risk detection may catch more risks, but also overload specialists. The average cycle time may not show increased low-value referrals and queue congestion.

# 5. Pilot design

**Pilot scope**
- Phase 1: Create a Technical Shared Service queue only for `case_complexity=high` technical cases. Keep local embedded tech for low/medium cases and informal advice.
- Do not broaden Legal/Risk detection immediately. Run a separate limited pilot for `required_specialty in {legal, risk, security}` with capacity guardrails.
- Introduce shared approval queue only for medium/high-value cases in pilot teams. Keep local supervisor for exceptions and appeals.
- Start with East plus a matched subset of North/South high-complexity cases, or use staggered team-week rollout to avoid confounding.

**Comparison group**
- Business-as-usual cases in the same teams before the pilot.
- If possible, cluster-randomize by team-week or case: high-complexity cases routed to shared service vs local embedded tech.
- For East, compare against its own pre-pilot baseline and against North/South non-pilot high-complexity cases.

**Pre/post observation window**
- Pre-pilot baseline: 8–12 weeks.
- Washout: 2 weeks.
- Pilot post: 8–12 weeks for leading indicators; extend to 6 months for outcomes, rework, fairness, and learning.

**Segmentation**
- Team: North, South, East.
- Complexity: low, medium, high.
- Specialty: technical, legal, risk, security.
- Customer segment: language/regional need, priority, location.
- Flags: `local_expertise_flag`, `rework_flag`, `priority`, `customer_segment`.

**Guardrail metrics**
- p90/p99 cycle time for high-complexity cases.
- Median wait before Technical Shared Service and Legal/Risk.
- Rework rate after shared-service activities.
- SLA miss rate by team, complexity, specialty, customer segment.
- `reassignment_count`.
- Local tech  local caseworker handover frequency.
- Central resource utilization and queue WIP.
- Fairness gap: max-min wait/rework/SLA across customer segments.

**Staff feedback collection**
- Weekly short survey: role clarity, workload, perceived bottleneck, ability to get informal advice.
- Focus groups with local caseworkers, embedded tech, central specialists, supervisors.
- Case-note quality audit before/after.
- Queue-owner interviews: accountability, escalations, exception handling.

**Rollback or adjustment triggers**
- Shared-service median wait exceeds baseline by >20% for two consecutive weeks.
- High-complexity p90 exceeds baseline by >15%.
- Legal/Risk wait exceeds agreed threshold.
- Rework after shared service increases by >2 percentage points.
- Fairness gap widens beyond pre-defined limit.
- Central utilization >90% while waits rise.
- Local informal advice edges drop sharply and complex-case rework rises.
- If triggered: pause expansion, add capacity, restore local tech time, adjust routing rules, or revert to local embedded model for complex cases.

# 6. Recommendation

**Pilot first, then modify cautiously; do not proceed to full rollout without monitoring.**

The restructuring has plausible benefits: pooled specialist capacity may help East, reduce local tech idle time, and standardize routine decisions. But the current data already show scarce Legal/Risk specialists near capacity, East tech overload, and strong North/South local expertise. Centralizing all specialist work at once risks turning a local handover problem into a central queue problem.

Management should not infer from early averages that the change is working. Average cycle time can improve because simple cases become faster while high-complexity, legal/risk, and language/regional cases suffer longer tails. Higher specialist utilization can mean queue saturation, not efficiency. Fewer local tech loops can mean lost informal advice and learning. Standardization can hide unfair waits across customer segments.

Proceed only if guardrails pass: stable or improved p90/p99 for high-complexity cases, no worsening of Legal/Risk wait beyond threshold, no rework increase, no fairness gap widening, and preserved local technical learning/advice. If guardrails fail, modify by keeping local tech for triage/informal advice, capping central queue referrals, adding specialist capacity, and using shared queues only for truly standardized cases.
