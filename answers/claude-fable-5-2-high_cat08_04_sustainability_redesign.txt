## 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Scope used: operational "order-to-delivery" boundary, from `Receive order` to `Confirm delivery` / `Close` (or `Cancel due to delay`).**

Inside the boundary (measurable from the enriched log):
- Production energy (`energy_kwh`) on CNC_A, CNC_B, heat_treat and inspection_cell, including emergency production and rework loops.
- Outbound transport (`transport_kgco2e`) by `route_mode`, including second shipments after `Partial shipment` / `Backorder wait`.
- Material losses: `material_scrap_kg` and `package_material_kg`, including `Rework packaging`.
- Waste that the log shows only indirectly: batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days (obsolescence), production already spent on cancelled orders, damage-related returns.

Outside the boundary (not claimed as savings or costs):
- Embodied emissions of raw material and purchased stock parts, inbound logistics, warehouse building energy, capital equipment, packaging end-of-life.
- The customer's side: the energy, scrap and idle capacity caused by a machine standing still. This matters for Critical-downtime orders: a late part can cause environmental and economic damage at the customer that is larger than the van emissions saved, so a low-emission route for a true downtime case is not automatically the "greener" outcome.

Assumptions and remaining uncertainty:
- **No single footprint number.** `energy_kwh`, `transport_kgco2e` and kg of scrap/packaging are in different units. Without a grid emission factor and material factors they cannot be added, so I treat them as separate criteria and look for dominated options instead of a weighted score.
- **Flattened log.** Line-item events are collapsed to the order, so batch energy allocation, partial shipments and scrap per order are approximations. An object-centric view (order, line, batch, package, shipment) is needed for attribution.
- **Counts do not fully reconcile.** 11,200 closed + 530 cancelled = 11,730 of 12,000 orders; shipping edges sum to about 9,550 against 11,200 closes; `Cancel due to delay` has 470 incoming edges but 530 outgoing. Path shares (e.g. 8% critical/van ≈ 960 orders) also differ from DFG edges (620 `Dedicated van`). All quantities below are therefore indicative.
- **Low model precision** for emergency and partial-shipment paths during disruptions: variant-level conclusions for those paths are weaker than for the main stock and MTO paths.
- **Observational evidence.** CNC_B's 18% rework versus 9% on CNC_A may partly reflect a harder part mix; air's low late rate may reflect selection. Causal claims need controlled pilots (section 7).
- **Unknown measurement conventions:** whether `transport_kgco2e` includes the empty van return leg, and whether returns and obsolete items are logged as events at all.
- **Missing attributes:** part weight (needed for Q1), return events, downtime confirmation, and load factor of trucks.

**Baseline hot spots** (prompt averages multiplied by shares; orders of magnitude only):
- Transport: Critical-downtime is 11% of orders but roughly 40% of transport kgCO2e (38.0 per order). The three air/van variants (stock -> air 5%, MTO immediate -> air 6%, critical -> van 8%) are about 19% of orders and roughly 60% of transport emissions.
- Energy and scrap: Made-to-order is 34% of orders but roughly 85-87% of both energy and scrap.
- Rework: the `CNC_B -> inspection -> rework` variant is 5% of orders but roughly a fifth of production energy and a quarter of scrap (32 kWh and 1.8 kg per order), with 32% late delivery. This is the clearest case where environment and service point the same way.
- Standard-stock Regular on ground (42% of orders, 3.6 kgCO2e, 0.9 kWh) is already low-impact; little should be changed there except consolidation where slack exists.

Two composite views are useful: **impact per on-time, defect-free delivered order** (so that late, reworked or returned orders do not look cheap), and **slack consumption** (how many hours of `promised_lead_time_hours` are spent in batch wait, consolidation wait, inspection queue and transport).

## 3. Intervention options

A design principle links the options: **promised-lead-time slack is one budget that batching, consolidation and transport mode all draw on.** The data suggest it is worth most when spent on avoiding air (about 24h of slack avoids a 4-6x transport penalty), and less when spent on very large batches (about 34h of extra wait saves about 1.5 kWh per item on CNC_A). This ranking depends on the grid factor and items per order and should be verified, but it argues against maximising batch size first and expediting later.

**O1. Slack-based ground shipping for Standard-stock Service-contract (S4, first use case of the S1 recommender)**
- Target: `Pick from stock -> Package standard -> Ship air` (5% of orders, 18.5 vs 3.6 kgCO2e on ground); specifically the 22% of Standard-stock Service-contract cases (about 420 orders) violating SP1.
- Mechanism: at `Availability check`, compute remaining slack; if ground plus priority picking meets the promise, release with pick priority and ship ground. Air stays available when slack is insufficient.
- Service/quality/resilience: late risk rises slightly on shifted orders (ground variant 8% late vs 3% for air). SP1's 85% threshold alone would allow a much higher late rate than the segment's current 5%, so the priority pick is essential and the realised on-time rate for contract customers should be held near today's level. Air remains a live alternative route (R1).
- Evidence to monitor: SP1 conformance at the ship decision; calibration of predicted versus realised on-time by lane; late rate and p90 for the segment; `expedite_flag` after a ground assignment (rescue shipments); queue-jumping effects of priority picks on Standard-stock Regular.

**O2. Extend the recommender to Made-to-order and decide mode at planning time (S1)**
- Target: `MTO -> CNC immediately -> inspection -> reinforced pack -> air` (6%, 24 kWh and 23 kgCO2e). Reinforced packages go by air 800 times and never by consolidated truck in the DFG.
- Mechanism: decide batch release and route jointly at `Plan production`; avoid paying twice for speed (immediate small-batch production and air) when one lever would meet the promise; open consolidated truck for heavy reinforced parts.
- Service/quality/resilience: neutral if slack is estimated correctly; MTO is already 13-18% late, so the recommender must use route reliability and not average transit time.
- Evidence: share of cases with both small batch and air; slack at ship decision; late rate by recommended versus overridden mode.

**O3. Twice-daily consolidated truck departures for non-urgent stock orders (S5)**
- Target: `Package standard -> Ship ground` (5,100) for Standard-stock Regular (median 31h, p90 62h); today only 850 go consolidated.
- Mechanism: 35-55% less transport kgCO2e than individual ground parcels; two departures keep the wait near the lower end of the 12-24h range. Backordered lines can join the next departure instead of a separate parcel.
- Service/quality/resilience: adds wait, so only orders with slack above the wait plus a buffer are eligible; excluded for Critical. Parcel ground remains the fallback, which supports R1.
- Evidence: consolidation wait distribution; load factor per departure (a poorly filled truck can be worse than parcels); late rate of consolidated versus matched ground orders; cancellations.

**O4. Dynamic batching with slack-based waiting caps (S2)**
- Target: `Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC production` (3,020 cases) and heat-treatment batches; the 13% variant with 118h median and 21% late.
- Mechanism: release a batch when it reaches the efficient size (12 or more on CNC_A, above 10 for heat treatment) or when the oldest order's remaining slack falls below a threshold. Caps differ by segment: longest for Regular MTO, shorter for Service-contract MTO, none for Critical. Fill batches by grouping firm orders with the same setup or heat recipe, not by adding forecast items.
- Service/quality/resilience: reduces the long-wait tail and the 1.7x late risk of heat-batch waits; some batches will be smaller, costing energy per item. The prompt gives only the endpoints (2.8 kWh at size 1-3, 1.3 kWh at 12-20); if most of the gain arrives by mid-size batches, tight caps cost little energy.
- Evidence: energy per item by `batch_size`; batch wait p90 by segment; share of cap-triggered releases; obsolescence rate for firm-order versus forecast-filled batches; expedites following batch waits.

**O5. Route MTO work to CNC_A when queue and slack allow, and fix CNC_B causes (S3)**
- Target: `MTO -> batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground` (5%, 160h, 32% late, 32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap).
- Mechanism: 9% versus 18% rework means fewer repeated CNC passes, less scrap, fewer late orders. CNC_B's speed advantage disappears when one in five or six parts loops back. In parallel, mine rework by part family, material and program on CNC_B to find which combinations fail, and keep CNC_B for the combinations where it performs well.
- Service/quality/resilience: CNC_A queues grow, so routing must be slack-conditional. CNC_B must stay qualified as alternative and emergency capacity; a single-machine dependency would be a resilience loss.
- Evidence: rework rate by machine x part family (controls for part mix); CNC_A queue time and utilisation; total cycle time including rework of matched cases.

**O6. Risk-based inspection quality gate (S7)**
- Target: `Inspection -> Rework production` (470) and defects that escape rushed inspections into `Rework packaging` or customer returns.
- Mechanism: full inspection and first-article checks for high-rework combinations (CNC_B, heat-treated parts), lighter sampling for proven low-risk combinations to free inspection_cell capacity. Catching a defect on the first piece of a batch avoids scrapping the batch; catching it before packaging avoids the return transport, repackaging and remanufacture.
- Service/quality/resilience: the inspection queue is already a cause of rushed checks, so the gate needs capacity levelling, otherwise cycle time rises. Measured in-process scrap may rise at first because hidden waste becomes visible.
- Evidence: inspection queue time and duration versus later rework or return; first-pass yield; return rate by inspection depth.

**O7. Packaging-route-geometry compatibility check, with Q1 built in (S6)**
- Target: `Package ... -> Rework packaging` (620 cases); `Rework packaging -> Package reinforced` (420) exceeds the 360 that came from reinforced packs, so some orders were first packed standard and repacked reinforced, consistent with late-detected Q1 violations.
- Mechanism: choose packaging after the route is fixed and from part weight and geometry, so each order is packed once. Keep reinforced packaging for MTO parts over 8 kg (40% fewer damage returns justifies doubled material, since a return costs remanufacture energy, scrap and two transport legs). Allow lighter packaging only with the inspection certificate Q1 requires.
- Service/quality/resilience: removes a delay loop; no service downside. Risk lies only in over-optimistic low-damage certification.
- Evidence: packaging rework rate by `route_mode` x packaging type; Q1 conformance; damage returns by packaging type and weight class; `package_material_kg` per order.

**O8. Split Critical-downtime into true downtime and precautionary urgent (S9)**
- Target: `Critical -> emergency production -> reinforced pack -> dedicated van` (8%, 52 kgCO2e, 3% late) and the SP2 gap (31% of van cases without a justification event).
- Mechanism: true downtime keeps the fastest path unconditionally. Precautionary urgent orders get priority handling but the lowest-emission mode that meets their promise, with instant escalation if the customer reports actual downtime. The existing `Critical -> stock/ground exception` path (4.8 kgCO2e) shows the potential but also the limit: 9% late versus 3%.
- Service/quality/resilience: protects the cases where delay is most damaging; the risk is misclassification. The split should be rolled out with service-contract customers, not imposed.
- Evidence: late rate per sub-category; escalation events; SP2 justification completeness; category mix over time (drift towards "true downtime" signals gaming).

**O9. Shared emergency courier lanes and backhaul for vans (S8)**
- Target: `Dedicated van` (620) trips that return empty.
- Mechanism: pool nearby critical orders, use shared emergency lanes for precautionary urgent orders, and load returns or reusable packaging on the way back. Emissions per order fall without slowing the outbound leg for true downtime.
- Service/quality/resilience: shared lanes add stops and possible delay, so dedicated vans remain for true downtime. A second emergency option improves R1 as long as it is a separate provider or lane.
- Evidence: orders per trip, empty-return share, delivery time of shared versus dedicated, late rate of critical orders.

**O10. Availability-check rules to reduce partial shipments (S11)**
- Target: `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment` (1,010) and `Backorder wait` (220, of which 60 cancel); the variant shows 79h, 24% late and 7.2 kgCO2e, twice the single-shipment figure.
- Mechanism: ship complete by default when the missing line's expected availability is within the order's slack; split only when a line is urgent or the promise would be broken. Promise dates should come from line-level availability.
- Service/quality/resilience: fewer second shipments, customer contacts and cancellations; the risk is delaying lines the customer needs now, so Critical and customer-requested splits are exempt.
- Evidence: partial-shipment and backorder-cancel rates; shipments per order; late rate for held-complete orders; needs line-item (object-centric) data.

**O11. Carbon-aware promise dates for Regular Made-to-order (S10)**
- Target: Made-to-order Regular (22%, 94h median, 18% late).
- Mechanism: at order entry, offer an optional date aligned with the next efficient batch and ground or consolidated transport, next to the standard date. This creates slack before production starts instead of consuming it afterwards. With 18% late today, a more realistic promise may also improve reliability.
- Service/quality/resilience: opt-in only; not offered where it would push the order past the customer's stated need date.
- Evidence: uptake rate, promised versus actual lead time, order size and frequency changes, cancellation and reclassification rates.

**O12. Sustainability exception log (S12)**
- Target: every SP1, SP2 and Q1 override.
- Mechanism: no direct saving. A mandatory event with reason code, approver, slack at decision time and estimated extra kgCO2e separates logging gaps from real non-compliance (the 31% SP2 gap may be either) and makes legitimate air and van use visible as legitimate.
- Service/quality/resilience: must be a quick step so that true downtime shipments are never delayed by paperwork (pre-authorised for confirmed downtime).
- Evidence: exception rate by segment, reason, planner and customer; time added by the justification step.

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

| # | Area | Risk mechanism | Signal in the log | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Batching -> transport | Waiting for batches of 12 or more saves kWh per item but uses up slack (wait 4h to 38h); the order is then expedited by air, which can cost more than the batch saved. | `expedite_flag` or `parcel_air` after long `Batch wait`. | Slack-based caps (O4); joint batch and mode decision (O2). |
| 2 | Batching -> obsolescence | Filling batches with forecast demand lowers energy per item produced but 6% of batch items are not shipped within 14 days; their full energy and material are wasted. | Unshipped items per `batch_id` after 14 days. | Batch firm orders only, or forecast items only for proven fast movers; measure energy per shipped item. |
| 3 | Batching -> quality | A process fault in a large batch multiplies scrap; a failed heat-treatment batch repeats the most energy-intensive step. | Scrap and rework per batch by `batch_size`. | First-article inspection before the batch runs (O6). |
| 4 | Transport | Moving air to ground lowers kgCO2e per shipment but more late deliveries cause rescue re-shipments, duplicate orders, cancellations, and for critical parts downtime impacts at the customer that lie outside the boundary. | Second shipments, `Cancel due to delay`, late rate by mode. | Probability-based mode choice with segment guardrails; never apply to true downtime. |
| 5 | Transport consolidation | Twice-daily trucks with low fill can emit more per order than the parcel network; fixed departures can also pull orders into waiting that had no slack. | Load factor per departure; wait before `Consolidated truck`. | Minimum-fill rule with parcel fallback; eligibility by slack. |
| 6 | Transport -> resilience | Shifting volume away from air and vans can lead to cancelled carrier capacity, so no alternative exists during a ground disruption (R1 breach). | Distinct route modes used per Service-contract lane. | Keep minimum contracted capacity and periodic use of alternatives. |
| 7 | Packaging | Reducing reinforced packaging saves material but loses the 40% damage-return reduction; each return costs remanufacture, scrap and two more transport legs. | 8 | Rework displacement | Moving work to CNC_A lengthens its queue; overflow and emergency jobs then land on CNC_B under time pressure, or orders are expedited. ## 6. Balanced recommendation

I recommend a package built around one rule: **use slack where it avoids the most impact, remove waste that also hurts service, and keep the fast paths for the cases that need them.**

**Step 1 - Measurement and control (first):** O12 exception log, plus the missing attributes (part weight, return events, downtime confirmation, truck load factor) and line/batch-level logging. Without this, later results cannot be validated and the SP2 gap cannot be interpreted.

**Step 2 - Changes where environment and service agree (low risk, start immediately):**
- O5 + O6: route MTO work away from failing CNC_B combinations and add the risk-based inspection gate. This addresses the variant with the highest energy, scrap and late rate at once.
- O7: packaging compatibility check with Q1 embedded; reinforced packaging stays for heavy MTO parts.
- O10: ship-complete rules based on line-level availability.

**Step 3 - Slack-conditional transport changes:**
- O1: ground plus priority pick for the Standard-stock Service-contract orders that violate SP1 today.
- O3: twice-daily consolidated departures for Standard-stock Regular with a minimum-fill rule.
- O2: joint batch and mode decision for MTO, including consolidated truck for reinforced packages.

**Step 4 - Trade-off management in production:** O4 dynamic batching with segment caps and firm-order batches, sized after mining the energy curve between batch sizes 4 and 11.

**Step 5 - Critical-downtime, done with customers:** O8 split and O9 shared lanes and backhaul. True downtime keeps emergency production and dedicated vans without restriction other than a logged justification.

**Step 6 - Optional:** O11 carbon-aware promise dates as an opt-in pilot for Regular MTO.

What I explicitly do not recommend:
- A general ban or quota on air and dedicated vans. Air is justified when slack is insufficient; vans are justified for confirmed downtime.
- Making `Critical -> stock/ground exception` the default for critical orders: 9% late versus 3% is not acceptable for true downtime.
- Always waiting for batches of 12 or more: it worsens an already 13-21% late MTO flow and raises obsolescence.
- Removing reinforced packaging for parts over 8 kg, or retiring CNC_B.

Why this is balanced:
- **Environment:** it targets the paths that carry most of the impact (air and van variants for transport; MTO rework for energy and scrap) and leaves the already efficient stock/ground path largely alone.
- **Service:** every slower option is conditional on slack and segment. Suggested guardrail: no segment's late rate or p90 cycle time may worsen beyond an agreed small tolerance against its baseline (7%, 5%, 18%, 13%, 4% late), and the Critical true-downtime late rate must not rise at all.
- **Quality:** rework and returns are treated as environmental issues, not separate ones; the quality steps come before the transport steps.
- **Resilience:** alternative routes and CNC_B capacity are preserved, which satisfies R1 and keeps options open in the disruption periods where the discovered model is least reliable.

## 7. Validation and governance plan

**Validation design**
- Freeze a baseline per segment and variant (the tables in the prompt, recomputed at line and batch level).
- Introduce each step as a pilot with a comparison group: by lane for O1 and O3, by part family for O4-O6, by customer group for O8 and O11. Compare with matched cases or difference-in-differences, since case mix and season change.
- Evaluate on impact per on-time, defect-free delivered order, with absolute totals alongside, over a period long enough to see returns and 14-day obsolescence.
- Define stop rules in advance: a pilot is paused if its segment breaches the service guardrail or if rescue expedites exceed the avoided air shipments.

**Conformance monitoring**
- **SP1:** at each `Package -> Ship` decision, record predicted probability of meeting `promised_lead_time_hours` by ground or consolidated truck. A violation is air or van use with probability above 85% and no exception event. Also check calibration: predicted versus realised on-time by probability band and lane. Target: the 22% violation rate in Standard-stock Service-contract falls substantially while the segment's late rate stays near 5%.
- **SP2:** every `Dedicated van` event must be preceded by a justification event with category (true downtime) and approver. Target: missing justifications fall from 31% towards zero; van trip counts and empty-return share are reported with it, so that better logging is not mistaken for fewer trips.
- **Q1:** for Made-to-order parts over 8 kg, `Package reinforced` or a low-damage-risk certificate from inspection must exist before shipping. Track violations against `Rework packaging` and return events to confirm the correlation weakens.
- **R1:** monthly check that each Service-contract lane has at least one alternative route mode used or contractually available; test during real disruption periods.

**Segment-level service monitoring**
- Weekly dashboard for the five segments, with Critical split into true downtime and precautionary: late rate, median and p90 cycle time, cancellation rate, partial-shipment rate, expedite rate.
- Variant and drift monitoring: new or growing variants such as ground followed by expedite, order splitting, or a rising Critical share per customer indicate rebound effects from section 5.
- Bottleneck monitoring for CNC_A queue, inspection_cell queue, batch wait and consolidation wait, since these are where the redesign moves waiting time.

**Sustainability monitoring**
- Energy per good shipped item by `machine_group` and batch-size band; rework energy share; scrap per order; packaging per order including repacks; transport kgCO2e per order by mode; obsolescence per batch; truck load factor.
- Reconcile log-based figures periodically with meter readings and carrier reports to check the `energy_kwh` and `transport_kgco2e` attributes.

**Exception governance**
- Each override carries reason code, approver, slack at decision time and estimated additional impact. Confirmed downtime is pre-authorised so that logging never delays shipment.
- Monthly review by operations, quality, logistics and customer service: exceptions by reason, lane, customer and decision owner. Recurring justified exceptions lead to a rule change (for example a lane where ground is unreliable); recurring unjustified ones lead to training or system blocks.
- A named owner for each policy (SP1, SP2, Q1, R1) and a quarterly review of thresholds, including whether 85% in SP1 is appropriate for Service-contract customers.
- Rules, thresholds and caps are versioned so that changes in results can be traced to changes in the redesign.
