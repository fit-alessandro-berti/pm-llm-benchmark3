## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

The following are established by the supplied materials, subject to ordinary questions about the reliability of the underlying records:

- CS-9 is a formally approved, controlled standard effective from 2025-01-01. Its express baseline is sanctions and quality review before activation, manual procurement approval before activation, and separation between requester and manual approver.
- M0 represents registration followed by concurrent sanctions and quality checks, then an exclusive choice between a manual-approved activation branch and rejection, then notification and closure. It does not represent identity, risk, monetary, effective-date, authority, data-provenance, or conflict-of-interest conditions.
- PD-3 was an approved-looking steering-committee decision with a stated window of 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31, but it was not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. Its stated pilot route is limited to low-risk German suppliers already in an approved corporate group; sanctions and automated master-data validation remain pre-activation, and quality may be up to two business days after provisional activation.
- FD-7 is a time-bounded emergency directive for critical flood-relief suppliers. It permits provisional activation before quality review and manual approval, but retains the pre-activation sanctions requirement and adds a EUR 20,000 cap, daily monitoring, and quality review within two business days. It expired at the end of 2026-07-12.
- The CRM migration changed the evidence path for notification, and only approximately 70% of notifications are mirrored in the onboarding log. A stable notification_id is useful evidence; a mutable text link entered later is materially weaker evidence.
- The observed trace orders and the stated case contexts are facts about the supplied observations. In particular, SUP-703 is high-risk and had a EUR 180,000 first purchase, SUP-708 activated before sanctions clearance, SUP-704 occurred during FD-7, and SUP-705 occurred after its expiry.
- The aggregate figures establish a control signal, not a complete population-level finding: 312 Auto_Approve cases were observed in June and July, 14 appear clearly ineligible, 9 lack eligibility attributes, 47 cases used information requests, notification missingness rose to 29% after migration, and six post-pilot Auto_Approve cases occurred in the first August week.

The following propositions remain disputed or require additional evidence:

- Whether a steering-committee decision and follow-up email can authorize a temporary exception to a formally controlled standard, or merely authorize a pilot subject to CS-9, is an authority and governance question. The observer's statement that she had not approved weakening CS-9 is evidence against treating PD-3 as an unqualified policy amendment.
- Whether each case is in M0's scope is unresolved for rejected pre-screen leads and reactivations. CS-9 mentions abbreviated rejected-lead processing but does not define whether those leads are M0 cases. It says nothing explicit about reactivation.
- “Appears eligible” is not the same as proven eligible. Supplier risk, German jurisdiction, approved-group membership, the relevant policy version, and the eligibility snapshot must be tied to immutable case-time evidence.
- Whether a CRM message belongs to a case depends on the strength and timing of the link. A stable identifier and append-only source record are stronger than a later mutable text association.
- Whether QA_Clearance is equivalent to Quality_Review depends on the approved mapping and substantive audit evidence. The stated evidence for SUP-713 supports equivalence, but a mapping approved after the event does not automatically amend the historical policy.
- Whether two aliases resolve to one human, whether the requester and approver were different humans, and whether a delegation was valid are identity and resource-control questions, not visible in M0's event order.
- Whether a flood emergency factually continued after FD-7 expired does not itself establish that the directive's legal effect continued. A written extension or new authority would be needed.
- Whether the spouse relationship in SUP-715 violates a policy prohibition is not established: the supplied CS-9 rule is about requester/approver separation, while the conflict-of-interest rule requires disclosure and the supplied facts say no disclosure was recorded.

For a finding of noncompliance, I would require proof that the case is within the applicable scope, that the relevant rule was effective and authoritative at the event time, that the observed or reliably reconstructed event sequence violates the rule, and that no valid exception applies. A high-confidence violation of a safety ordering rule, such as activation before sanctions, can satisfy that burden from the trace itself. For an exception, the burden should be affirmative: the operator must show eligibility, scope, authority, effective window, and any compensating controls. A missing field should not silently become permission. Where applicability or event provenance cannot be established, the proper result is an indeterminate or model/scope finding rather than an invented violation.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest prosecution position begins with authority. CS-9 is the only expressly controlled and formally approved normative source. M0 is its stated control-flow representation. Neither operational convenience nor a decision that was never incorporated into CS-9 can silently repeal the baseline. PD-3 may have created a monitored pilot, but the compliance observer expressly said she had not approved weakening CS-9. At minimum, the organization needed a documented exception mechanism that identified eligible cases, authority, dates, and compensating controls. A frequent practice is not a policy amendment.

On that view, the operation has both direct violations and a normalization problem. M0's XOR and partial order do not encode the resource rule, so apparent control-flow conformance can conceal failures. They also do not encode policy version, risk, amount, emergency scope, or authority, so the fact that a trace can be replayed by M0 is not evidence that the business was compliant.

The clearest cases are:

- SUP-703 used Auto_Approve for a high-risk new foreign supplier, despite the pilot being limited to low-risk eligible German suppliers in an approved corporate group. The EUR 180,000 first purchase makes the departure from the intended low-risk containment especially serious. Selecting “pilot” in the interface cannot create eligibility. This is a high-confidence, severe noncompliance finding.
- SUP-708 activated a high-risk supplier 40 minutes before the sanctions check, with no emergency or pilot. CS-9 states the ordering requirement directly. The absence of transactions before clearance reduces realized exposure, but it does not make the control sequence compliant. This is a severe preventive-control failure with high confidence.
- SUP-705 followed the FD-7 pattern on 2026-07-13, after the directive had expired. The continued factual emergency is not a written extension. Without new authority, the operation had no basis to use the emergency route. Its EUR 12,000 spend was below the old cap, but the cap is not the only condition and an expired directive supplies no authority.
- SUP-714 used Auto_Approve on 2026-08-02, after PD-3 expired. The belief that permanent approval was imminent is operational expectation, not approval. The six post-pilot cases in the aggregate indicate normalization rather than an isolated date error.
- SUP-712 follows M0's visible sequence but violates CS-9's explicit requester/approver independence rule because aliases resolve to the same human. M0 cannot exonerate an activity it does not model.
- SUP-715 follows the M0 sequence, but the undisclosed spouse relationship and common reporting line create a plausible conflict-of-interest control failure. At a minimum, the required disclosure was absent. The prosecution should not overstate this as a violation of CS-9's separate-human rule unless the requester, reviewer, approver, or applicable conflict rule establishes that proposition.

SUP-702 and SUP-704 do not weaken the prosecution's general case. SUP-702 has unusually good evidence of PD-3 eligibility, stable CRM notification linkage, and timely quality review, so it is best treated as a justified, monitored exception if PD-3 had sufficient authority. SUP-704 occurred within FD-7, kept sanctions first, stayed below the EUR 20,000 cap, had daily monitoring, and completed quality and approval the next business day. Those facts support a properly controlled emergency exception. They are exceptions to the baseline, not proof that the baseline no longer applies.

SUP-701 is a straightforward compliant case. SUP-713 may be substantively compliant, but the prosecution can still say that a later mapping approval does not automatically repair a historical naming and governance gap. SUP-706 cannot be cleared merely because a CRM message exists: the mutable link is weak evidence, and the increase from 2% to 29% missingness materially impairs auditability. SUP-711's duplicate registration event may be a migration defect, but until deduplication is proven it creates uncertainty about the event history.

SUP-709 and SUP-710 expose scope arguments, but ambiguity is not an operational license. If M0 begins after accepted pre-screen, SUP-709 is outside the model; if it begins at registration, the missing quality review is a departure expressly contemplated only for a properly scoped rejected-lead path. Similarly, prior certification may make SUP-710 a sensible reactivation process, but CS-9 does not authorize an undocumented alternative. The organization should have defined those scopes before relying on them.

The 47 information-request cases demonstrate a second prosecution point. The behavior may be good, but a desirable activity that M0 cannot express is still a model deviation until the model is revised. The same is true of the 14 clearly ineligible Auto_Approve cases and the six post-pilot cases. Outcome quality does not retroactively amend a controlled normative process. A control system that tolerates undocumented exceptions and then calls them normal has lost the ability to distinguish authorized flexibility from unauthorized bypass.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense is that the comparison is being made against an under-specified abstraction, not against the complete operational policy. M0 is a control-flow skeleton. It does not encode the distinction between an intake lead and an accepted onboarding, a prior certification and a new supplier, a provisional activation and a final activation, or a temporary policy overlay. It also omits resource identities and evidence provenance. A high apparent deviation rate therefore mixes genuine violations with scope errors, legitimate overlays, label changes, and logging defects.

M0 correctly makes Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review concurrent in the standard route. Their order in SUP-701, SUP-702, and SUP-713 should not be read as a prescribed total order; the partial-order semantics allow either order among the concurrent checks. A replay implementation that insists on one sequence would itself be wrong. Likewise, the missing Request_More_Info loop is a genuine model omission. Operations used it in 47 cases, and none activated before the requested evidence arrived. Treating those cases as misconduct would penalize a desirable evidence-gathering control merely because the model has no state for it.

There are also credible policy overlays:

- SUP-702 is a strong example of the pilot operating as described: low-risk DE supplier, approved corporate group, complete eligibility attributes, sanctions before activation, automated approval, quality the next business day, and a stable CRM notification identifier. If PD-3 was authorized as a monitored exception, the M0 deviation is justified rather than noncompliant.
- SUP-704 is within FD-7's exact effective period and scope as described. Sanctions preceded provisional activation; spend was EUR 18,000; monitoring existed; quality and approval followed on the next business day. The trace is not a CS-9 trace, but the emergency directive expressly changed that ordering for the specified population.
- SUP-705 shows the model's inability to represent emergency continuation and expiry as separate policy states. The facts support an operationally real emergency and a low spend, even though formal extension evidence is absent. That is at least a governance/scope defect and not automatically proof of bad intent or excessive operational risk.

The data problem is substantial. After the CRM migration, 29% of cases lack a notification event in the onboarding log, compared with 2% before migration. SUP-706 has an actual CRM message, and the weakness is the mutable late association entered two days later. That is an evidence-integrity failure and should lower confidence in a missing-notification finding; it should not be converted into proof that notification never happened. The same reasoning supports treating SUP-711's duplicate registration as a migration duplication when both rows share a source transaction ID.

Several apparent deviations are terminology or scope defects. SUP-713 has approved-equivalent QA evidence and a later mapping; this is not the same as omitting quality review. SUP-709 failed sanctions pre-screen and was rejected, so no quality review may be rational for an intake lead. CS-9 expressly permits abbreviated pre-screen processing, while the model owner says M0 begins only after acceptance. The ambiguity belongs in scope governance. SUP-710 is a reactivation with a still-valid quality certification; forcing a fresh Register_Supplier and quality event through an onboarding model could be wasteful and semantically wrong. CS-9's silence on reactivation is a specification defect.

SUP-712 and SUP-715 show why process replay cannot answer all compliance questions. The former is a real resource-control violation if the alias resolution is reliable, but it is not a flow-model failure. The latter may violate a conflict-of-interest disclosure obligation, but the supplied facts do not establish a rule that spouses may not review and approve the same supplier. The correct defense is to distinguish M0 conformance from organizational-control conformance, not to deny the need for the latter.

The defense nevertheless has limits. A successful outcome, no pre-clearance transaction, or widespread practice cannot cure a forbidden ordering rule. SUP-708 still activated before sanctions without an applicable overlay. A UI selection cannot make SUP-703 eligible, and high-risk status plus EUR 180,000 makes the case more than a harmless model mismatch. Operations' belief that permanent Auto_Approve was imminent cannot extend PD-3 beyond 2026-07-31. Informal normalization is evidence that the model and governance lagged practice, but it is not evidence of authority.

## 5. Final Judgment

The primary case classifications are as follows. Severity describes the potential control impact, while confidence describes confidence in the classification from the supplied evidence.

1. SUP-701 — COMPLIANT; low severity; high confidence. It satisfies the M0/CS-9 flow, and the supplied identity evidence satisfies the stated separation rule.
2. SUP-702 — JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION; moderate severity; medium-high confidence. The case satisfies the stated PD-3 eligibility and compensating-control facts, but confidence is below high because the authority of PD-3 to displace CS-9 is not conclusively established.
3. SUP-703 — NONCOMPLIANT; critical severity; high confidence. The case is expressly outside the pilot's risk and eligibility conditions, and the high purchase amount makes the unauthorized route materially consequential.
4. SUP-704 — JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION; high inherent severity but medium residual severity; high confidence. It fits FD-7's dates, scope facts, sanctions ordering, amount cap, monitoring, and two-day retrospective controls. The remaining confidence caveat is documentary proof of directive authority and timestamps.
5. SUP-705 — NONCOMPLIANT; high severity; medium-high confidence. The dominant reason is use of an expired emergency authority. The continued emergency is a genuine governance defect and mitigation context, not a valid extension.
6. SUP-706 — DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE; moderate severity; high confidence in the data finding. A message likely exists, but the mutable late association does not establish a reliable case-level notification event.
7. SUP-707 — MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT; low severity; high confidence. The dominant reason is that M0 omits a repeatable, desirable evidence-gathering loop; no evidence shows harmful early activation.
8. SUP-708 — NONCOMPLIANT; critical severity; high confidence. The activation-before-sanctions ordering directly violates an unqualified CS-9 safety rule, and no overlay applies.
9. SUP-709 — MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT; low-to-moderate severity; medium confidence. The dominant reason is unresolved scope between rejected intake leads and accepted onboarding, not proof that the rejection handling was substantively unsafe.
10. SUP-710 — MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT; moderate severity; medium confidence. The dominant reason is the absence of a defined reactivation procedure. The case still carries stale-certification risk, which must be tested by the new reactivation controls.
11. SUP-711 — DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE; low process severity and moderate auditability severity; high confidence. The identical source transaction ID makes migration duplication the leading explanation, but the raw event lineage must confirm it.
12. SUP-712 — NONCOMPLIANT; high severity; high confidence, conditional on the stated alias resolution. The explicit CS-9 resource rule is violated even though M0's control flow passes.
13. SUP-713 — MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT; low substantive severity; medium-high confidence. The evidence supports an equivalent quality activity; the defect is label/effective-mapping governance. It would become noncompliant if the artifacts were not actually equivalent.
14. SUP-714 — NONCOMPLIANT; high severity; high confidence. The dominant reason is operation of a pilot route after its fixed end date without a permanent amendment or extension. Normalization and expected policy change do not confer authority.
15. SUP-715 — MIXED; moderate severity; medium confidence. The dominant issue is a likely organizational conflict-of-interest disclosure failure, while the M0/CS-9 control-flow and distinct requester/approver requirements are not shown to fail. “Mixed” here identifies the separate control domains; it is not a substitute for deciding that the flow itself is compliant and the disclosure control is unresolved or breached.

At system level, the operation is operationally noncompliant in a material but not uniform sense. The high-confidence severe findings in SUP-703 and SUP-708, the expired-authority pattern in SUP-714, the explicit independence failure in SUP-712, and the aggregate 14 clearly ineligible and six post-pilot Auto_Approve cases prevent a conclusion that the organization merely has a stale model. The controls are being bypassed in at least some cases, and informal normalization has become a governance risk.

M0 is nevertheless not a valid complete oracle. It is a useful standard-route control-flow core, particularly its concurrency and notify-before-close ordering, but it is materially incomplete for the actual operating domain. It omits information-request loops, auto and emergency routes, case scope, policy overlays, resource identity, conflict controls, and the data lineage needed after the CRM migration. The 47 information-request cases, SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-711, SUP-713, and SUP-706 show why raw M0 replay would overstate noncompliance. The correct system judgment is therefore: enforce CS-9 as the default immediately, quarantine unauthorized exceptions, and revise M0 into a versioned layered architecture rather than discard it or treat it as sufficient.

I reject the prosecution's claim wherever it treats every M0 deviation as substantive misconduct: SUP-702 and SUP-704 have strong exception facts, SUP-707 is desirable omitted behavior, and SUP-706/SUP-711 are materially affected by collection defects. I reject the defense's claim wherever it treats model obsolescence or operational belief as authority: SUP-703 is ineligible, SUP-708 violates a core safety ordering, SUP-714 postdates the pilot, and absence of realized loss does not satisfy a preventive control.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The revised design should separate four layers that M0 currently conflates:

1. A versioned control-flow core. For an accepted onboarding, begin with registration and an information-resolution state that may repeat Request_More_Info until required evidence is present. Then choose among a standard route, an authorized low-risk auto route, an authorized emergency/provisional route, rejection, or withdrawal. The standard route runs Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review concurrently, requires Manual_Procurement_Approval, then activates. Notification must precede closure on every terminal route that requires requester communication. The auto route requires pre-activation sanctions and master-data validation, an Auto_Approve decision, and a defined quality timing rule. The emergency route requires sanctions before provisional activation and has post-activation monitoring, quality review, and retrospective approval as obligations before final closure.

2. A data-aware policy decision layer. Every case receives a policy snapshot containing case type, risk, jurisdiction, corporate-group status, policy/rule version, eligibility result, authority, effective start and end timestamps, provisional status, and monetary cap. The route selector must evaluate the snapshot at the relevant event's effective time, not infer permission from the activity label. A permanent auto route should be introduced only by a controlled amendment that specifies whether quality is pre-activation or a monitored post-activation obligation. PD-3 and FD-7 should remain historical overlay versions and should not be rewritten to fit later practice.

3. Resource and integrity controls. Canonical actor IDs must be resolved from aliases using an effective-dated identity table. Requester and manual approver must be different humans. Approval authority, delegation, reviewer/approver relationships, reporting lines, conflict disclosures, recusals, and independent review must be checked separately. A spouse relationship should produce a disclosure/recusal requirement, not an unstated prohibition invented by replay.

4. Policy-overlay governance. An overlay is valid only if it has a unique rule/version ID, named issuing authority, scope predicate, effective and expiry timestamps, permitted substitutions, compensating controls, amount limits, and a closure/retrospective obligation. Pilot and emergency overlays must be selected by an immutable eligibility snapshot and linked to the authorization artifact. Expiry should fail closed. A new extension must be a new version, not an informal continuation of the old one.

The POWL should express durable control-flow and permitted structural alternatives: concurrency, ordered activities, XOR route choices, loops, and terminal notification/closure. DECLARE or log-skeleton-style constraints should express event relations that are naturally cross-case or declarative, such as Sanctions_Check precedes any activation, every activation eventually has quality review, no activation is final before approval unless a named overlay is present, and Notify_Requester precedes Close_Case. POWL and declarative constraints should not be expected to compare human identities or evaluate a currency amount.

Pandas or an equivalent data-check layer should evaluate risk, jurisdiction, approved-group membership, amount caps, dates, policy version, eligibility completeness, event-time ordering, duplicate source IDs, stable notification joins, and two-business-day deadlines. Organizational controls must approve policy changes, maintain the authority register, resolve identities, certify mappings, manage emergency extensions, investigate conflicts, and review exceptions. This division preserves a meaningful model while making the important predicates auditable.

Scope should be explicit:

- Rejected pre-screen leads are an intake scope with a short Register -> Sanctions pre-screen -> Reject -> Notify -> Close model. They enter the full onboarding model only after an acceptance event. If the organization wants all leads governed by the same case ID, the scope marker must still distinguish the abbreviated route.
- Reactivations are a separate case type. They require a reactivation assessment, fresh sanctions and risk screening, verification that prior quality certification remains valid for the supplier and current product, current authority approval, and a documented decision on whether full quality review is waived. They should not be judged by missing initial-registration events unless policy expressly says so.
- Withdrawals are a distinct terminal route, with reason, actor, and requester notification requirements. A withdrawal before activation must not be confused with rejection.
- A notification event is complete only when the immutable notification_id, case ID, message status, effective send time, source system, and append-only recording time are present. A mutable text link is a reconciliation lead, not conclusive proof.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The following code constructs a POWL control-flow skeleton. Operator.LOOP is used with silent entry and exit around Request_More_Info, giving zero or more information-request iterations before route selection. The model deliberately represents both a normal low-risk auto route, in which quality is pre-activation, and a pilot auto route, in which activation is provisional and quality is post-activation. The policy layer must decide which one is enabled; the POWL does not authorize either route by itself. It also approximates ongoing emergency monitoring as one required monitoring activity; deadline and frequency checks belong in data rules.

`
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def t(label):
    return Transition(label=label)


def sequence(*nodes):
    \"\"\"A total order over the supplied POWL nodes.\"\"\"
    result = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for left, right in zip(nodes, nodes[1:]):
        result.order.add_edge(left, right)
    return result


def concurrent(*nodes):
    \"\"\"A partial order with no order among the supplied children.\"\"\"
    return StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))


# Registration is followed by zero or more evidence-request iterations.
# LOOP(do=tau, redo=Request_More_Info, exit=tau) is the usual process-tree
# encoding of an optional, repeatable activity before the next state.
request_more_info_loop = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[SilentTransition(), t(\"Request_More_Info\"), SilentTransition()],
)

# Standard route: sanctions and quality are concurrent; both finish before
# manual procurement approval, which precedes final activation.
standard_checks = concurrent(
    t(\"Sanctions_Check\"),
    t(\"Quality_Review\"),
)
standard_manual_route = sequence(
    standard_checks,
    t(\"Manual_Procurement_Approval\"),
    t(\"Activate_Supplier\"),
)

A normalized policy-overlay table should be maintained as controlled data, for example:

| rule_version | authority and status | scope predicate | effective interval | allowed route/substitution | mandatory compensating controls | expiry behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CS-9:v1 | formally approved standard | accepted supplier onboarding | 2025-01-01 to successor | standard manual route; sanctions and quality before activation | requester and approver are distinct humans | remains default until formally replaced |
| PD-3:v1 | steering decision; authority to override CS-9 must be confirmed | low-risk DE supplier in approved corporate group with complete snapshot | 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31 | Auto_Approve instead of manual approval; provisional activation may precede quality | sanctions and master-data validation before activation; quality within two business days; monitoring and pilot log | fail closed at expiry |
| FD-7:v1 | emergency directive | critical flood-relief supplier needed to prevent shutdown | 2026-07-10 00:00 through 2026-07-12 23:59 | provisional activation before quality and manual approval | sanctions first; EUR 20,000 cap; daily monitoring; quality and retrospective approval within two business days | automatic expiry; extension requires new signed version |
| PROPOSED-AUTO:v1 | future formally approved amendment | explicitly defined low-risk population | explicit future interval | permanent Auto_Approve route, with the chosen quality timing stated | independent eligibility snapshot, master-data validation, monitoring, audit sampling, fallback manual review | fail closed if eligibility or version is missing |

The table should be accompanied by an authority register that records issuer, approver, signature, delegation, publication time, and superseded version. A case must store the exact rule_version selected, not merely the string “pilot” or “emergency”.

The event collection should use an append-only event schema with at least these fields: case_id, case_type, event_id, immutable activity, source activity label, immutable event_time, immutable recorded_at, source system, source_transaction_id, event status, canonical actor ID, actor alias and alias-map version, requester ID, approver ID, reviewer ID, approval/delegation artifact, notification_id, CRM message ID, message status, and predecessor/causal reference where available. event_time says when the business action occurred; recorded_at says when it was ingested. Neither should be overwritten during reconciliation.

For each case, store an immutable eligibility snapshot containing risk classification, country/jurisdiction, approved-group identifier and validity interval, master-data validation result, case type, provisional flag, policy and rule versions, authority artifact, effective start/end timestamps, and the reason for any exception. Monetary controls need transaction ID, currency, amount, exchange-rate version if aggregation is required, and the exact amount-time used for the cap. Emergency cases need incident ID, critical-supplier designation, monitoring records, and retrospective approval timestamps. Identity data needs canonical human ID, alias history, role and reporting-line validity intervals, conflict disclosure, recusal, and delegation evidence.

For migration resilience, CRM should emit an immutable notification event keyed by a stable notification_id and include case_id as a foreign key at creation time. Historical mutable links should be retained as provisional reconciliation evidence, never silently rewritten into authoritative joins. A deduplication check should collapse or flag repeated source transaction IDs without deleting raw events. Data checks should then test coverage and timing: every applicable route reaches notification before closure, every activation has the required pre- or post-activation controls for its selected overlay, every emergency spend remains under the effective cap, every identity rule is evaluated against canonical IDs, and every deadline is calculated from immutable business event times.
