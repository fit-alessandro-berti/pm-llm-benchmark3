Conventions used below: **[O]** = observed in the supplied evidence; **[I]** = arithmetic inference from the supplied numbers; **[H]** = hypothesis that still needs a test. "Q1 rates" means the Jan–Mar matured breach rates by variant (V1 ≈ 0.16, V2 ≈ 0.11, V3 ≈ 0.31).

One structural fact drives much of the reading: the model scores at `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, while the variant (V1/V2/V3) is decided *after* that event. Variant is therefore a diagnostic stratifier, not a feature the model had. A change in routing or in part lead times can leave P(breach | path) intact and still change P(breach | prefix features), which is what the model experiences.

Reference decomposition [I] (variant mix × variant breach rate):

## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

**Jan–Mar: stable baseline.** [O] AUROC 0.81–0.82, ECE 0.022–0.025, predicted ≈ observed, variant mix 45/35/20, part lead time 28–35 h. The lead time is already creeping up (+25% in the quarter), which is worth noting as a slow precursor but has no visible effect on outcomes.

**Apr: an input incident, not a change in the world.** [O] The outcome side is untouched: breach rate 0.19, labels 100% mature. The input side is broken: 17% unknown activities, 14% missing queue feature, 18% unmapped variants. The model over-predicts by 0.09 and loses discrimination (0.67). [H] The competing reading is that the migration really disrupted operations. The small bumps in variant breach rates allow a minor real effect, but it cannot explain a jump in *predicted* risk with a flat observed rate. April's 0.67 is a measurement of corrupted prefixes; it says little about the model.

**May: partial recovery.** [O] The patch arrived on 6 May, so 5 of 31 days were scored on broken inputs. [I] That alone is consistent with the residual gap (predicted 0.21 against 0.18, AUROC 0.78). May is therefore the tail of the April incident, with the un-recomputed derived features as a second possible contributor [H].

**Jun: real process change, benign for outcomes.** [O] Remote routing lifts V2 to 52%; per-variant breach rates and the remote-fix edge time are unchanged. [I] The fall in observed breach rate to 0.15 is fully reproduced by the new mix at old rates. AUROC holds at 0.79 while the model over-predicts by 0.05 (ECE 0.06). Because routing is decided after the prediction point, the model could not see the change: its prefix features map to a lower breach probability than before. That is a calibration-level shift in P(Y given X) caused by policy, with no evidence that the outcome mechanism on any path changed. Labelling June "concept drift" without that qualification would overstate it.

**Jul: transition.** [O] Supplier misses begin on 10 July: the parts edge reaches 65 h and V3 breach reaches 0.46. [I] The aggregate looks healthy (0.19 observed, 0.18 predicted, ECE 0.042) only because the June over-prediction on V2 and the new under-prediction on V3 offset each other. AUROC already slips to 0.76. Aggregate calibration hides the onset; the stratified view shows it.

**Aug: genuine outcome-relationship change, with a mix change on top.** [O] Parts edge 130 h, V3 breach 0.64, V3 share 35%, no Partner Z yet. Under-prediction of about 0.09–0.12, AUROC 0.68. [I] About 85% of the rise is within-V3. This is the clearest month for supplier-driven concept drift because population shift is absent and maturity is 93%.

**Sep: the August mechanism persists and a new population is added.** [O] Partner Z brings 24% of cases with a 58% V3 share; the parts edge reaches 148 h. [I] Non-Z cases look like August, so the September increment is Partner Z. V3's rate reads 0.61, slightly below August, although lead times lengthened. [H] At 82% maturity the missing labels are probably long V3 cases, so 0.61 and the overall 0.31 are likely lower bounds.

**Oct: mostly a measurement problem.** [O] 55% maturity with selection against long paths; mix and parts edge (155 h) are essentially September's. [I] The reported 0.16 is what a V1/V2-only labelled sample would give. The reported AUROC (0.64), Brier (0.205) and ECE (0.13) are computed on a selected, range-restricted subset in which predicted risk and labelled outcomes are mismatched by construction. [H] October's true performance is probably similar to September's; nothing in the table can confirm or refute that yet.

**Why these are four different failures.** They differ in which side of the model moved, in what repairs them, and in whether the metric can be trusted:

| Period | What moved | Metric trustworthy? | Repair |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apr (and early May) | Inputs, through a schema break | Yes for labels; scores are invalid | Data engineering: rebuild, re-score |
| Jun | Post-prediction routing mix | Yes | Recalibrate (intercept); no relearning needed |
| Aug–Sep | Outcome mechanism on V3, then case population | Mostly; biased low | New information (lead time, source features), then retrain |
| Oct | Label availability | No | Wait, or correct for censoring |

AUROC values of 0.67, 0.68, 0.66 and 0.64 look alike across April, August, September and October, yet each has a different cause. Treating them as one decline would lead to a single retraining on data that includes a broken month and an unlabelled month.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Stage 0: immediate containment (this week).**
- Suspend any automatic retraining or recalibration triggered by aggregate metrics. Label October and September dashboards "provisional, immature".
- Add a fail-closed scoring guard: if the diagnosis event cannot be located, the unknown-activity share exceeds a threshold, or a required feature is missing, the case gets a fallback score (segment base rate) and a flag.
- Add an operational overlay for the supplier problem that does not depend on breach labels: when `Order Part` occurs, re-score or escalate using the rolling 7–14 day median of `Order Part -> Part Received`. That edge is observable within days, long before outcomes mature. The business response (alternative supplier, revised promise dates) matters more than any model change here.
- Report every metric by variant and by source.

**Stage 1: short-term recalibration (1–3 weeks).**
- Recalibrate only on mature, clean vintages, using information available at prediction time: an intercept/slope update with terms for source (Partner Z) and for the current parts lead-time index. June–July support the routing-related correction; August (93%, with the missing 7% treated as censored, not negative) supports the supplier correction.
- A single global intercept is not enough: the June error (over-prediction) and the August error (under-prediction on V3) have opposite signs.
- Recalibration preserves ranking, so it can repair ECE but cannot restore AUROC from 0.66. It is a stopgap. Version it and set an expiry tied to the lead-time monitor so it is withdrawn when the supplier recovers.

**Stage 2: feature repair (2–4 weeks).**
- Rebuild April and 1–5 May from raw events with normalisation and deduplication over all sources and dates; rebuild the cached feature; re-score and confirm the result of test 3.
- Put the activity mapping under version control with contract tests.
- Add features that address the diagnosed mechanisms: part-required likelihood from the diagnosis, current supplier lead time (exogenous, time-varying), source/partner, rural travel distance and device age (collected for all sources), remote-eligibility.

**Stage 3: full retraining (when gates are met).**
Gates: April rebuilt and verified; August at or above about 98% maturity and September at or above about 95%, or a time-to-event formulation that handles censoring; new features populated for at least two months; Partner Z has enough matured labels. Validate out-of-time by vintage and by segment. Weight recent regimes more heavily but keep Q1 for structure.

**Training-data status.**

| Months | Status | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Jan–Mar | Safe | Clean and mature, though they represent the pre-routing, pre-supplier regime |
| Apr | Unsafe until rebuilt | Labels are fine; features and prefixes are corrupted. Usable after recomputation and verification |
| May | Conditional | 1–5 May need recomputation; the rest is safe |
| Jun | Safe | Clean, mature, new routing regime |
| Jul | Safe with a caveat | 98% mature; contains the supplier onset mid-month |
| Aug | Conditional | 93% mature; missing labels probably V3 breaches. Use only with censoring handled, or wait |
| Sep | Unsafe as-is | 82% mature, with Partner Z maturing more slowly |
| Oct | Unsafe for labels | 55% mature with informative selection. Usable only for unlabelled input and process monitoring |

**Segment-specific or variant-specific model?** A model split by realised variant is not deployable at this prediction point, because the variant is unknown when the score is produced. Two alternatives are justified by the evidence:
- A single model with the new features plus segment-aware calibration (by source, and by predicted parts-dependence). This is the default. Partner Z has roughly 1,200 cases a month with immature labels: enough for a calibration layer or hierarchical/partial-pooling terms, too few for a separate model.
- A two-stage structure: P(parts required given prefix) combined with P(breach given parts path, current lead time). This isolates the unstable component, the supplier-dependent one, so it can be updated without touching the rest, and a second scoring point at `Order Part` becomes natural.
A separate Partner Z model is warranted only if test 7 shows a residual within-variant difference after the new features are added.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

**Monitors.**
1. **Data-quality and schema** (daily, no labels needed): activity vocabulary against a contract, unknown-activity share, duplicate rate, per-feature missingness and default rates, prefix-construction success rate, source and pipeline-version lineage.
2. **Label-maturity**: share labelled by vintage and age, label-delay distribution with censoring taken into account, outcome-API backlog, labelled-versus-unlabelled composition gap.
3. **Process-variant**: variant shares, new or unmapped variants, distance from a reference window, by source.
4. **Performance-DFG**: median and p90 per edge (especially `Order Part -> Part Received`, diagnosis → `Remote Fix`, `Dispatch Technician -> Repair Completed`), edge frequencies, in event-calendar and case-start views.
5. **Population**: source/partner shares, region, device age, travel distance, volume, domain-classifier AUC against the reference.
6. **Input and score drift**: per-feature PSI, score distribution, mean predicted risk by segment.
7. **Predictive**: AUROC, PR-AUC and Brier skill against the month's base rate, on vintages above a maturity threshold only, with an "early" view at fixed age for comparison with past vintages at the same age.
8. **Calibration**: ECE, calibration intercept and slope, observed-to-expected ratio by variant, source and score decile; the mix-reweighted breach rate.
9. **Intervention and deployment**: a change log (migrations, routing policies, partner onboarding, supplier incidents, model, calibrator and mapping versions) and a record of actions taken on predictions, since interventions triggered by high scores alter the outcomes that later evaluate the model.

**Alert dependencies (evaluated in this order).**
- Data-quality red → predictive and calibration alerts for the affected window are suppressed and tagged "invalid inputs". Action: pipeline incident, fallback scoring. Retraining is blocked.
- Label-maturity below threshold, or a labelled-versus-unlabelled composition gap → predictive and calibration metrics are shown as provisional and cannot open a retraining ticket. Action: fix the backlog, wait, or apply censoring correction.
- Variant-mix alert with stable within-variant calibration → recalibration candidate (prior shift), not retraining.
- Performance-DFG alert on an edge → early warning of outcome drift on paths using that edge. Action: operational escalation and time-limited recalibration. A retraining review opens only when mature stratified calibration confirms it.
- Population alert → stratified evaluation and transport check before any fairness or retraining conclusion.
- Retraining is proposed only when all of these hold: data quality green, vintage mature, degradation persisting over at least two mature vintages, degradation present within strata, and no explaining entry in the deployment log that a cheaper action would resolve.
- Every alert is annotated with change-log entries from the preceding 30 days.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

DIAG = "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
RENAMES = {"Diagnostic Assessment Completed": DIAG}   # governed, versioned mapping table in practice
EDGES = [("Order Part", "Part Received"), (DIAG, "Remote Fix")]
C, A, T = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"

def load(path="field_service_events.csv"):
    raw = pd.read_csv(path)   # assumed columns: case_id, activity, timestamp[, resource]
    raw["activity_raw"] = raw["activity"]                             # keep lineage
    raw["activity"] = raw["activity"].str.strip().replace(RENAMES)    # normalise BEFORE prefixes/features
    raw["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["timestamp"], utc=True)
    raw["month"] = raw["timestamp"].dt.strftime("%Y-%m")
    n_raw = raw.groupby("month").size()
    # Duplicates: (1) exact replays; (2) same case+activity re-emitted within 60 s (double writes).
    # Applied to every source and date, unlike the production rule (new source, after 20 April).
    raw = raw.sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp"]).drop_duplicates(["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"])
    gap = raw.groupby(["case_id", "activity"])["timestamp"].diff()
    raw = raw[~(gap <= pd.Timedelta(seconds=60))]
    dup_share = (1 - raw.groupby("month").size() / n_raw).rename("dup_removed_share")
    raw["org:resource"] = raw["resource"] if "resource" in raw else "NA"
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(raw, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp")
    return log, dup_share

def family(trace):   # UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY is counted, never silently imputed
    s = set(trace)
    if "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY" in s or DIAG not in s: return "unmapped"
    if "Order Part" in s: return "V3"
    if "Remote Fix" in s: return "V2"
    return "V1" if "Dispatch Technician" in s else "open_or_other"

def summarise(sub):
    out = {"cases": sub[C].nunique(), "events": len(sub),
           "unknown_activity_share": (sub[A] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean()}
    fam = {}
    for v, n in pm4py.get_variants(sub).items():           # {activity tuple: case count}
        n = n if isinstance(n, int) else len(n)            # older releases return the traces
        fam[family(v)] = fam.get(family(v), 0) + n
    out.update({f"share_{k}": n / sum(fam.values()) for k, n in fam.items()})
    dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(sub)
    perf, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(sub)       # seconds; dict of aggregates per edge
    for a, b in EDGES:
        out[f"n[{a}->{b}]"] = dfg.get((a, b), 0)
        out[f"median_h[{a}->{b}]"] = perf.get((a, b), {}).get("median", float("nan")) / 3600
    return out

def prefix_audit(sub):                                     # recomputed from events, never read from the cache
    sub = sub.sort_values([C, T])
    sub = sub.assign(pos=sub.groupby(C).cumcount())
    first_diag = sub[sub[A] == DIAG].groupby(C)["pos"].min()
    n = sub[C].nunique()
    return {"diag_found_share": len(first_diag) / n,
            "median_events_before_diag": first_diag.median(),
            "closed_share": sub.loc[sub[A] == "Case Closed", C].nunique() / n}   # right-censoring indicator

def monthly_evidence(path="field_service_events.csv"):
    log, dup_share = load(path)
    start_month = log.groupby(C)[T].transform("min").dt.strftime("%Y-%m")
    rows = []
    for m in sorted(start_month.unique()):
        lo = pd.Timestamp(m + "-01")
        hi = lo + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1) - pd.Timedelta(seconds=1)
        # (a) Event-calendar view: what the operation did in month m, whatever the case age.
        #     Shows supplier/queue conditions early. Traces are fragments here (read variant shares
        #     from view b) and edges crossing the month boundary are cut, so long waits are understated.
        ev = pm4py.filter_time_range(log, f"{lo:%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S}", f"{hi:%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S}", mode="events")
        # (b) Case-start cohort: full history of cases created in month m. Matches the model's scoring
        #     population and label vintages; recent cohorts are right-censored (see closed_share).
        co = log[start_month == m]
        rows.append({"month": m, "view": "event_calendar", **summarise(ev)})
        rows.append({"month": m, "view": "case_start", **summarise(co), **prefix_audit(co)})
    summary = pd.DataFrame(rows).merge(dup_share, left_on="month", right_index=True, how="left")
    shares = [c for c in summary if c.startswith("share_")]
    summary[shares] = summary[shares].fillna(0.0)
    temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(      # arrivals, cases, waiting/sojourn per month
        log, grouper_freq="MS", activity_key=A, timestamp_key=T,
        case_id_key=C, start_timestamp_key=T, resource_key="org:resource")
    # No drift detector is invoked: these tables feed ordinary tests (chi-square/JS distance on shares,
    # control charts on edge medians) and are joined to prediction metrics by month and vintage.
    return summary, temporal

if __name__ == "__main__":
    summary, temporal = monthly_evidence()
    summary.to_csv("monthly_process_evidence.csv", index=False)
    temporal.to_csv("monthly_temporal_features.csv", index=False)
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

| # | Wrong action | What goes wrong | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Retraining on immature labels (Sep–Oct).** | Labelled cases are mostly short paths, so the model learns a breach prior near 0.16 and learns that parts-dependent or Partner Z profiles are low-risk. It then under-predicts on exactly the cases that breach most. Treating unlabelled cases as negatives is worse still. | Maturity gate on training data; fixed-vintage evaluation; a survival or inverse-probability-of-censoring formulation if recent data must be used; early positive labels from "open past promised date". |
| 2 | **Calibrating or retraining around the April pipeline bug.** | A calibrator fitted to April lowers scores by about 0.09. Once inputs are repaired it under-predicts in every later month. A model trained on April learns relationships between corrupted event counts and outcomes. | Fix inputs first; re-score; quarantine April until test 3 passes; data-quality alerts block model actions. |
| 3 | **Dismissing August–September as noise, label delay or "just mix".** | Under-prediction of about 0.10 continues on the highest-risk path; breaches go unflagged while the parts edge is at 150 h. The Aug–Sep bias from immaturity runs the other way, so mature labels will make these months look worse. | Use the performance-DFG edge as a leading indicator; lead-time-indexed recalibration; operational escalation; add the lead-time feature. |
| 4 | **Declaring the model unfair to Partner Z, or declaring it fair, from aggregates.** | The 0.45 against 0.27 gap is largely path mix (about 0.10 of 0.18) and sits on less mature labels. A blanket Z adjustment could over-correct, and when the supplier recovers it becomes a standing bias against rural customers. Ignoring a real within-variant gap leaves Z customers with systematically wrong promises. | Stratify by variant, score and maturity; run the transport check; collect rural distance and device age for all sources; prefer features that describe the mechanism over a partner flag; review per-segment error rates on mature vintages. |
| 5 | **Reading June as model failure and retraining.** | Cost and risk are spent on a shift that an intercept update fixes, and the Q1 structure may be discarded. Conversely, a global downward recalibration left in place after July deepens the V3 under-prediction. | Mix-reweighted checks before any decision; segment-aware, versioned calibrators with expiry and re-validation each mature vintage. |
| 6 | **Baking the supplier crisis into a permanent model.** | A retrain on Aug–Sep without a lead-time feature encodes "parts-dependent means about 0.6 breach" as a constant. When the supplier recovers the model over-predicts, technicians are over-escalated and trust falls. | Represent the cause explicitly (lead-time feature or two-stage model); monitor the edge for recovery; a rollback plan for calibrators. |
| 7 | **Treating October's metrics as comparable with mature months in reporting.** | Management sees a breach rate of 0.16 and concludes the supplier problem is over, or sees AUROC 0.64 and orders an emergency rebuild. Both conclusions come from the same selected sample. | Publish maturity next to every metric; show projected mature rates with ranges from maturity curves; suppress headline numbers below a maturity threshold. |
| 8 | **Ignoring feedback from interventions.** | If high-risk flags lead to expediting, prevented breaches appear as false positives and later evaluation or retraining penalises the model for working. | Log interventions; evaluate on untreated or randomly held-out cases; include treatment in the retraining design. |
