## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

Stipulated facts (evidence sufficient, parties do not contest)  
• CS-9 is the only formally approved compliance standard in force for all of 2026.  
• CS-9 requires (1) Sanctions_Check, (2) Quality_Review, (3) Manual_Procurement_Approval, all before Activate_Supplier.  
• PD-3 (pilot) and FD-7 (emergency) were time-bound directives signed by competent steering-committee or crisis authority.  
• PD-3 expired 2026-07-31; FD-7 expired 2026-07-12.  
• Notification logging became incomplete after 2026-07-01.  
• SUP-703, SUP-708 violated at least one condition written in CS-9 and in the pilot / emergency directives.  
• M0 expresses no loop and no resource rule.  

Disputed propositions (need interpretation or additional authority)  
1. Whether rejected leads (SUP-709) and reactivations (SUP-710) are “onboarding cases” governed by CS-9.  
2. Whether post-flood recovery on 2026-07-13 (SUP-705) is still covered by FD-7.  
3. Whether PD-3 created a de-facto permanent low-risk branch once widely adopted.  
4. Whether missing Notify_Requester events constitute a process deviation or only a logging error.  
5. Whether CS-9 may be flexibly interpreted to permit Request_More_Info loops.  

Burden-of-proof policy proposed  
• To declare NONCOMPLIANT: preponderance of evidence that the case violates an in-force written rule AND the rule applies to the case.  
• To accept a JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION: evidence of an authorized directive that (a) overrides CS-9 for the case period, scope, or risk class, and (b) the case satisfies all conditions of that directive.  
• When scope or data quality is unclear, classify as MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT or DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE rather than guilty by default.


## 2. Prosecution Brief

1. Formal authorityCS-9 is a board-approved compliance standard. Pilots and emergencies are temporary and explicitly time-bounded. Once they lapse, CS-9 re-asserts full force.

2. High-risk deviations  
   • SUP-703 used Auto_Approve for a high-risk foreign supplier, breaching both CS-9 and PD-3 eligibility rules. It spent EUR 180,000—nine times the FD-7 emergency cap.  
   • SUP-708 activated before Sanctions_Check with no emergency in force—an absolute breach of CS-9, PD-3, and FD-7.  

3. Normalisation of exceptionsThe aggregate data show 312 Auto_Approve cases; 14 clearly ineligible and 9 missing attributes. Six more occurred in August after PD-3’s sunset. The organisation is institutionalising the pilot as if permanent without authority.

4. Expired emergency misuseSUP-705 follows the FD-7 pattern but on 2026-07-13, after expiry. Mere operational hardship does not extend the directive.

5. Hidden deviations through model omissions  
   • Resource separation (requester  approver) is silently violated in SUP-712.  
   • Conflict-of-interest issues (spouses) in SUP-715 escape detection.  
   • Missing notifications after 2026-07-01 (29 % rate) hide potential failures to inform requesters.

6. Data incompleteness is not exculpatory  
   The organisation had a duty to maintain evidence (CS-9 §9 “records shall be complete and tamper-resistant”). Moving Notify_Requester to CRM without ensuring traceability is itself non-compliant record-keeping.

ConclusionM0 correctly represents CS-9’s core flow. Deviations result from undisciplined practice, not model error. Systemic enforcement and disciplinary measures are warranted.


## 3. Defense Brief

1. Model obsolescence and incompleteness  
   • M0 lacks Request_More_Info, a business-sanctioned loop present in 47 cases and endorsed by Compliance.  
   • It omits concurrency tolerance for post-activation Quality_Review allowed under PD-3 and FD-7.  
   • Resource rules, amount caps, and risk categories are data constraints, not control-flow; their absence causes false “fits-but-violates” findings.

2. Scope ambiguity  
   • Rejected leads (SUP-709) arguably exit the funnel before CS-9 obligations; the governing scope document is ambiguous.  
   • Reactivations (SUP-710) reuse prior due-diligence; CS-9 is silent, so applying the full onboarding flow is questionable.

3. Authorised overlays override CS-9  
   • PD-3 and FD-7 were properly issued and recorded. SUP-702 and SUP-704 comply with every condition (risk class, amount caps, timing of Quality_Review).  
   • SUP-705 occurred one day after expiry but during the same flood incident; a good-faith reading of “critical relief suppliers” should extend until operations stabilise.

4. Data defects masquerade as non-compliance  
   • 70 % CRM integration loss explains missing Notify_Requester in SUP-706; the message exists in CRM with a reasonable link.  
   • Duplicate Register_Supplier in SUP-711 is a migration artefact, not misconduct.

5. Evolving terminology  
   • QA_Clearance in SUP-713 is formally mapped to Quality_Review. Label drift should not be treated as a breach.

6. Continuous improvement  
   • The high uptake of Auto_Approve reflects operational efficiency. Management intends to amend CS-9; enforcement now would penalise initiative and customer value.

ConclusionThe main issue is that policy, model, and data collection have not kept pace with controlled pilots and emergencies. Update the model and finish the policy amendment rather than punish operations.

System-level judgment

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

Layer 1 – Control-flow (POWL)  
A base tree containing:  
• Register_Supplier  
• concurrent Sanctions_Check || Quality_Review (but Quality may also happen after activation in approved overlays)  
• Decision XOR { Manual_Procurement_Approval, Auto_Approve, Provisional_Activate } governed by data attributes rule_set_id  
• Activate_Supplier (or Provisional_Activate)  
• Repeatable Request_More_Info loop before decision and/or approval nodes  
• Notify_Requester (mandatory before Close_Case, but implemented as a SilentTransition proxy because logging may be external)  
• Close_Case  
• Reject_Supplier branch, explicitly in scope

Layer 2 – Declarative / constraint overlay (Declare or log-skeleton)  
• Sanctions_Check BEFORE any activation (standard & pilot), except if rule_set_id == ‘FD-7’ allows 0 minutes lag.  
• Quality_Review BEFORE Activate_Supplier for rule_set_id == ‘CS-9’; WITHIN 2 business days AFTER for PD-3 or FD-7.  
• Max spend 20 000 EUR until retrospective approval when rule_set_id == ‘FD-7’.  
• Manual_Procurement_Approval required when approval_type == ‘Manual’.  
• Separation-of-duty constraint requester != approver.  
• Conflict-of-interest rule invoking HR relationship data.

Layer 3 – Data checks (pandas/sql)  
• Eligibility snapshot for Auto_Approve: risk_class == ‘LOW’ AND supplier_country == ‘DE’ AND corporate_group_approved == True.  
• Effective/record timestamp checks for authority window.  
• Stable notification_id present within 4 h OR raised exception.

Layer 4 – Governance metadata  
• Table policy_versions(rule_set_id, start_ts, end_ts, authorising_body, supersedes).  
• Each event carries rule_set_id frozen at execution time.  
• Emergency and pilot IDs require sign-off records stored immutably.

Layer 5 – Organisational controls  
• Automated blocker for Activate_Supplier if Sanctions_Check is missing or failed.  
• Delegation and COI disclosure workflow.  
• Periodic trace conformance with PM4Py + declarative rules.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal
```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import Transition, SilentTransition, StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# Transitions
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")  # QA_Clearance mapped in ETL
request_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")

manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
provisional_activate = Transition(label="Provisional_Activate")  # FD-7 route
activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")

reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")  # could become SilentTransition if logged elsewhere
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

# --- Sub-structures ---

# 1. Concurrent mandatory checks (standard)
checks_parallel = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.AND, children=[sanctions, quality])

# 2. Approval alternatives
manual_path = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[manual_approval, activate])
manual_path.order.add_edge(manual_approval, activate)

auto_path = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[auto_approve, activate])
auto_path.order.add_edge(auto_approve, activate)

provisional_path = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[provisional_activate])  # activation is provisional
# quality and manual approval will be re-checked later by declarative rules

approval_choice = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[manual_path, auto_path, provisional_path])

# 3. Loop for Request_More_Info before approval_choice
# POWL has no native LOOP; approximate via a self-loop silent transition
loop_entry = SilentTransition(label="Loop_Entry")
loop_back = SilentTransition(label="Loop_Back")

loop_order = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[loop_entry, request_info, loop_back, approval_choice])
loop_order.order.add_edge(loop_entry, approval_choice)
loop_order.order.add_edge(loop_entry, request_info)
loop_order.order.add_edge(request_info, loop_back)
loop_order.order.add_edge(loop_back, loop_entry)

# 4. Decision: approve (via loop_order) or reject
approve_branch = loop_order
decision = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[approve_branch, reject])

# 5. Main flow assembly
M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, checks_parallel, decision, notify, close])
M1.order.add_edge(register, checks_parallel)
M1.order.add_edge(checks_parallel, decision)
M1.order.add_edge(decision, notify)
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)

# M1 is the revised control-flow model
```

Limitations of the POWL approximation  
• The Request_More_Info “loop” uses silent transitions because POWL lacks an explicit LOOP operator; conformance queries must recognise multiple visits to request_info before approval.  
• Amount caps, risk categories, actor separation, and timing constraints live in declarative/data layers, not POWL.  
• Provisional_Activate requires post-hoc checks enforced outside the control-flow model.
