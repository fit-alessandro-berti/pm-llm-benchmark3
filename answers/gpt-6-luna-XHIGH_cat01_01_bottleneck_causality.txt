## 1. Executive causal assessment

Provisional verdict: **not identifiable from the supplied evidence** (confidence 85/100 in that identification judgment, not in a zero or harmful Orion effect). Orion’s crude mean is 21.96 hours versus Nova’s 15.24, a +6.72-hour gap. But Orion has 42.9% high-complexity cases versus Nova’s 12.5%, and its mean is lower in every observed complexity stratum: 7.50 versus 9.64 hours for low, 17.17 versus 19.38 for medium, and 31.57 versus 35.00 for high. Standardizing both groups to the pooled complexity mix (40%, 33.3%, 26.7%) gives about 17.14 versus 19.65 hours, or Orion −2.51 hours. This reversal is the strongest evidence that the crude gap is compositional; it argues against treating that gap as Orion’s causal effect.

It does not establish that Orion helps. Assignment favors Orion for difficult cases, almost all Orion work is at night, arrival load is imbalanced, and there is little common support in shift-by-complexity cells. Only completed cases are observed. For a total effect, assignment includes reviewer skill and downstream queue, service, and rework pathways; a direct effect holding those pathways fixed is a different estimand and is not identified here. Stratified wait and review-service means favor Orion, while its overall rework rate and exposure to compliance work are higher; each comparison remains confounded. The two appeals, one per group, say little about quality.

Moving half of Orion’s cases may help if Nova has spare capacity and comparable certification; it may fail or backfire if Nova queues grow or difficult cases lose access to experienced reviewers. A small, eligibility-limited routing pilot is safer and more informative than a blanket transfer.

**Recommendation.** Do not reassign 50% of Orion’s cases on the basis of its crude mean. The aggregate comparison mixes assignment with severity, night coverage, load, and compliance mix, while the complexity-stratified pattern points in the opposite direction and still lacks adequate overlap. First instrument all routed cases, including cases not yet completed, and measure queue and step times so wait, compliance, review, and rework are distinguishable. In parallel, target the largest measured queue or compliance-capacity bottleneck without changing case labels. Then run a small randomized routing pilot only among cases both groups are certified and staffed to handle, blocked on complexity, shift/load, and compliance status. Track total elapsed time, its process components, backlog spillovers, and an adjudicated quality measure under pre-set rollback limits. Expand only if the pilot’s benefit replicates without worsening queue tails or quality. This learns whether routing to the group bundle matters while protecting complex cases and avoiding a shift of congestion from Orion to Nova.

        pieces.append(

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Effect of a routing-policy change:** Current allocation is selective and observational. The most valuable design change is randomized assignment among cases both groups can safely handle, with overrides recorded.
2. **Risk differences within complexity strata:** The supplied three-level complexity field may leave important severity differences. Add a pre-routing calibrated case-risk score.
3. **Queue, compliance, and rework pathways:** Wait combines queueing and compliance-related delay, while service includes repeat reviews. Capture event-level timestamps for every activity and queue entry.
4. **Group label versus reviewer skill:** Certification is known only at aggregate group level. Record the assigned reviewer ID linked to certification and experience.
5. **Selection and quality measurement:** Open and abandoned cases are missing, and the 30-day outcome is coarse. Use prospective follow-up of every assigned case through completion or censoring and an adjudicated 30-day outcome.
