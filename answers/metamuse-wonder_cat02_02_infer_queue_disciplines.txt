# 1. Queue-by-queue diagnosis

**Definitions and assumptions used throughout.** *Observation* means directly read from the tables. *Eligible* means: arrived, not completed, required skill is covered by an idle resource, and the resource is not in a stated exception. **Assumption A1 — non-preemptive unless a semicolon occurs:** needed because only QB shows split segments; QA/QC/QD show one segment per job. **Assumption A2 — appointment times may be targets with an unknown tolerance, not exact instants:** needed because QA A06 and QB B07 start after their appointments without any stated penalty rule. **Assumption A3 — a job cannot be dispatched before its recorded arrival, but arrivals within 2 minutes of a dispatch are ambiguous** because scans may be delayed by up to 2 minutes. Realised duration equals `est_min` for essentially every job in the data (e.g., B01 segments sum to 45; C02–C04 run 8, 14, 22), so SPT-on-estimates and SPT-on-realised-times are *observationally equivalent here* — estimate noise cannot be assessed from these rows.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

**QA — FIFO + appointment exception.**
Supporting: A05, A06, A07 (09:05–09:41) — three consecutive FIFO choices against both priority (A08 = P1 waiting throughout) and SPT (A07 = 9 min). A02 at 08:30 also supports FIFO over priority/SPT (A04, P1, 7 min, was waiting). A04 at 08:58 is consistent with FIFO (oldest waiting) though priority agrees.
Contradicting: A03 jumping A02 at 08:20 is a genuine FIFO violation **explained by a known mechanism** (appointment at exactly 08:20, start on the minute) — an exception *within* the hybrid diagnosis, not evidence against FIFO as the base rule. A06's appointment (09:15) starting at 09:20 is explained by non-preemption: A05 ran 09:05–09:20; no undocumented exception required if appointments have a tolerance window. The 2-minute idle gap 08:30–08:32 is unexplained by any rule (see §5). A08 (P1) waiting until last is the clearest evidence that priority is *not* operative in QA.

**QB — preemptive priority with limits.**
Supporting: B02 preempting B01 at 09:16 (reason recorded); B03 (P2) starting at 09:48 over resuming B01 (P3); B05 (P1) at 10:00 jumping B01 and B04. B01's total service (14+17+14 = 45 = `est_min`) shows preemption carried no visible resumption cost in the timestamps.
Contradicting: (i) B05 did **not** preempt B03 at ~09:46–09:48 — under pure preemptive priority it should have. Scan delay (2 min) cannot explain this: B05 arrived 09:46, B03 ran until 10:00. This requires either a priority-gap threshold, a no-preempt grace rule, or dispatcher discretion — an *undocumented exception or a refined rule*. (ii) B04 (P3) beating B06 (P2) at 10:28 violates priority; FIFO and SPT both explain it, so it contradicts priority but cannot arbitrate between the alternatives. (iii) B01 resuming at 09:31 ahead of B03 (P2) violates strict priority-ordering of the waiting set, though "resume-before-new" is a known variant. (iv) B07's appointment (10:30) was not honoured (started 10:56) — explained by B06's non-preemptive run, so appointments appear *inoperative* in QB rather than merely soft.

**QC — SEPT with overrides.**
Supporting: C02C03C04 (81422) at 10:10–10:32; C06 (6) at 11:34 jumping C01 (36) and C07 (18). C02 (P3) beating C03 (P2) at 10:10 is the single most discriminating episode in QC: it rules out pure priority and pure FIFO simultaneously.
Contradicting: C05 at 10:54 (40 min chosen over 6-min C06) violates SEPT — explained by priority or deadline, which are equivalent here; no *undocumented* exception needed if the hybrid includes an override, but the override itself is undocumented. C01 at 11:40 violates SEPT, priority *and* deadline order (C07: 18 min, P2, deadline 12:00, all "better") — this one genuinely requires an additional mechanism: aging/max-wait rescue, an SLA switch (C01's own 13:00 deadline becoming the focus), or manual intervention. It cannot be explained by skill, availability (C1 idle at 11:40), scan delay (arrivals were 100 and 60 minutes earlier), or appointments (none in QC).

**QD — skill-based routing + within-pool FIFO.**
Supporting: D05D-F only (note confirms unique qualification); D10D-X with note citing D-F's meeting; D01/D02 simultaneous starts on the only eligible resources; D08 sequence on D-M in arrival order; D06's exact 11:30 appointment start on D-X (D-X idle 11:20–11:30 — consistent with holding for the appointment, *hypothesis*, or with D-X simply having no other eligible job until D07 was routed there at 11:48).
Contradicting: D05 not starting on idle D-F at 11:36 — explained *if* dispatch avoids starting a 30-min non-preemptive job 24 min before a known 12:00 meeting; this look-ahead is plausible but **undocumented**, and the alternative (D-F was doing unrecorded work 11:36–12:00) cannot be excluded. D04 over D08 (11:25) and D07 over D10 (11:48) contradict priority and SPT but *support* the within-pool FIFO component. No QD episode contradicts skill routing itself.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

**Test 1 — QC at 11:40: C01 (36 min, P3, dl 13:00, waited 100 min) vs C07 (18 min, P2, dl 12:00).**
- *Explanation H1 (SEPT + aging/max-wait cap):* predicts C01 because its waiting time crossed a threshold; predicts that in future, any job waiting beyond the cap jumps regardless of estimate.
- *Explanation H2 (one-off manual exception / unrecorded operational reason):* predicts C07 under the stated rule, with C01 as noise; predicts no systematic pattern for long-waiting jobs in future data.
- *Discriminating observable:* the **dispatch-decision timestamp and the waiting-time/age field (or policy-version flag) recorded at 11:40**, plus, prospectively, whether the *next* long-waiting long job is again rescued at a consistent waiting-time threshold. A repeatable threshold supports H1; a dispatcher-override code on C01 alone supports H2. Absence of an interruption reason does not prove no reason existed, so H2 cannot be excluded from current data.

**Test 2 — QA at 08:20: A03 (appt 08:20) vs A02 (waiting since 08:04).**
- *Explanation H1 (appointment scheduling with priority over FIFO):* predicts the appointed job starts at its appointment even over older waiting jobs; predicts A06 should likewise have jumped A05 at 09:15 — it did not (started 09:20, in FIFO position), which already strains H1 unless appointments are non-preemptive-targets.
- *Explanation H2 (FIFO with a reserved-slot/soft-appointment convention):* predicts appointed jobs are started at their time only if the resource frees exactly then (as at 08:20, when A01 ended), and otherwise slot into FIFO order — consistent with both A03 and A06.
- *Discriminating observable:* the **appointment tolerance window / slot-reservation flag** (e.g., "hold resource idle up to X min before an appointment" or "appointment class = hard/soft"). Under H1-hard, a future episode should show the resource *idling* to wait for an imminent appointment or preempting/queue-jumping for one; under H2 it never idles for an appointment. One recorded idle-for-appointment episode would settle it.

**Test 3 (brief) — QB at 09:46: B05 (P1) not preempting B03 (P2).**
Preemptive-priority-with-gap-threshold predicts P1 preempts P3 but not P2; dispatcher-discretion predicts preemption correlates with who is on shift. Discriminator: the **preemption-reason/decision log with author (system vs dispatcher)** across future P1-over-P2 arrivals.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked by plausibility (1 = most plausible), with effect on diagnosis:

1. **Non-preemption plus known resource exceptions (calibration, meetings, prior job).** *Observation-based:* QA's idle 08:52–08:58 and A06's late appointment start, QD D05's 12:20 start and D10's routing, QC's 10:10 start all align with stated exceptions. Effect: *strengthens* all four diagnoses; removes several apparent violations without new mechanisms. The QD D05 11:36–12:00 idle gap additionally requires meeting look-ahead (not starting a job that cannot finish) — plausible, but it is an inference, not a recorded fact.
2. **Appointments are soft targets with an unknown tolerance, honoured only when the resource frees in time.** Explains QA A03 (exact) vs A06 (5 min late) and QB B07 (26 min late, effectively ignored). Effect: QA stays "FIFO + soft appointment"; if appointments were instead hard, A06 and B07 would be strong contradictions and QA's diagnosis would drop toward pure FIFO with one anomaly.
3. **Delayed arrival scans (2 min) and 1-minute timestamp rounding.** Can reorder only near-simultaneous arrivals: relevant to QB at 09:48 (B05 arrived 09:46 — could have been known, so it *cannot* excuse the non-preemption, but could affect whether B05 was in the 09:48 eligible set) and to QA's 08:30–08:32 gap (setup/scan artefact). Effect: low — no queue's primary diagnosis flips, because the decisive episodes (QC 10:10, QA 09:05, QD skill routing) involve arrivals separated by far more than 2 minutes.
4. **Manual dispatcher overrides ("dispatcher reassignment", QB 09:48).** The recorded reason for B01's second stop is not a rule name; a human may have imposed priority order opportunistically. Effect: if overrides are frequent, QB's "preemptive priority" may be an artefact of dispatcher behaviour rather than the documented discipline — this is the main reason QB confidence is capped at 65.
5. **Policy switch under SLA/aging pressure.** QC C01 at 11:40 (waited 100 min) and the note "deadline already at risk when selected" for C05 suggest the centre reacts to endangered jobs. Effect: converts QC from "SEPT with two anomalies" to "SEPT + priority/deadline override + aging rescue" — a three-component hybrid that fits everything but is weakly identified because each component rests on roughly one episode.
6. **Strategically inaccurate or noisy estimates.** *Not supported by these data:* realised durations equal `est_min` throughout, so there is no observed noise; but if estimates are gamed in general, QC's SEPT could really be "shortest *true* job" routing, indistinguishable here. Effect: none on the ranking of episodes, but it blocks any claim that dispatch uses the recorded `est_min` specifically rather than a correlated internal estimate.
7. **Unrecorded resource work / setup times.** QA idles 08:30–08:32; D-F idles 11:36–12:00; D-X idles 11:20–11:30. Unlogged setups or side-tasks could fill these. Effect: would weaken the QD meeting-look-ahead inference (rank-1 exception) and the QA appointment-reservation reading, mildly lowering QD/QA confidence.

# 7. Final synthesis

**Most identifiable: QD.** Skill eligibility is a hard, checkable constraint, corroborated by routing notes (D05, D10) and by parallel starts that only make sense per-resource; within-pool FIFO then explains the order. Its one soft spot — D05's 44-minute wait for D-F — needs a meeting look-ahead assumption but does not threaten the primary label. **QB is second**: the 09:16 preemption with a recorded "urgent arrival" reason is nearly decisive for preemptive priority, but the non-preemption of B03 by B05 and the "dispatcher reassignment" label leave real doubt about whether the rule is systematic.

**Least identifiable: QC.** SEPT is strongly supported by the 81422 opening run, yet C05 requires a priority-or-deadline override (the two are observationally equivalent here) and C01 requires a third mechanism (aging, SLA rescue, or manual exception). Each hybrid component rests on about one episode, so several quite different policies fit the same eight jobs. QA sits between: FIFO is well supported after 08:30, but the appointment component rests on a single clean episode (A03) partly undercut by A06.

**Most dangerous policy to infer from these observations alone: pure (preemptive) priority** — e.g., generalising QB's preemptions, or reading QC's C05 and QD's D08 as "priority scheduling". Priority agrees with the observed choice at many decision points only because it coincides there with FIFO, SPT, deadline order, or skill eligibility (QD 11:35: all three agree; QC 10:54: priority  deadline). Inferring it as *the* documented rule would mispredict exactly the discriminating episodes already in the data — A08 waiting last in QA, B04 beating B06 in QB, C02 beating C03 and C01 beating C07 in QC, D04 and D07 beating higher-priority jobs in QD — and, operationally, would license preemptions (with their unmeasured resumption costs) that the centre demonstrably does not always perform. The safe cross-queue conclusion is narrower: *each queue's base order is visible only at decision points where the candidate rules disagree, and at those points the data support FIFO+soft appointments (QA), priority-with-limits (QB), SEPT-with-overrides (QC), and skill-pooled FIFO (QD) — all as hybrids, none as pure rules.*
