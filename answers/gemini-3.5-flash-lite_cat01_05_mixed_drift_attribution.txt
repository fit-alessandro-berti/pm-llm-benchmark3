## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The sharp divergence between raw model fitness (collapsing from 0.93 to ~0.50 in March 2026) and mapped model fitness (remaining stable between 0.86 and 0.91) highlights the profound distinction between *syntax/provenance drift* and *behavioral drift*. The raw fitness drop does not imply that operational execution collapsed overnight; rather, it reflects a nomenclature and schema shift introduced by the system migration on March 1, 2026. Specifically, renaming `Validate Eligibility` to `Eligibility Screen` and introducing duplicated technical `Open Case` events invalidated the old normative process model’s trace checking rules.

Applying a label mapping and projection (removing duplicate events, renaming activity strings) recovers structural conformance (mapped fitness ~0.90). However, this mathematical recovery *does not* prove that the business process was unchanged. A conformance score measures only whether control-flow execution sequences match the permitted graph paths; it is entirely blind to processing speed, resource allocation, rework frequency, and payload quality. 

Duplicated technical events inflate event log sizes and distort transition frequencies if unfiltered, whereas missing resource identifiers (which surged from 1.0% pre-migration to over 6% by summer) truncate organizational social network analyses and obscure workload distribution patterns. 

Crucially, the post-May deterioration in performance metrics—such as the doubling of the `Assess -> Request Info` loop, the jump in rework variants from 12% to 29%, and the p90 cycle time blowout from 67 hours to 121 hours—cannot be dismissed as mere logging drift. These shifts persist even after complete label normalization, confirming genuine operational degradation driven by external supplier disruptions, staffing shortages, and queueing bottlenecks.

### Threshold Strategy, Seasonality, and Multiple Testing
To prevent alert fatigue and false positives caused by annual seasonality, all thresholds (except raw schema checks) must utilize **adaptive seasonal baselines** (comparing the current month against the same month in the prior year, or using a seasonally adjusted Holt-Winters exponential smoothing model). For multiple testing across 10 concurrent monitors, apply the **Benjamini-Hochberg procedure** to control the false discovery rate (FDR) at $q = 0.05$, ensuring that alerts represent statistically significant structural breaks rather than random noise.

## 9. Provisional attribution

The observed drift in the document-eligibility process is best explained as a multi-causal compound evolution rather than a single systemic failure. Our strongest conclusions relate to the **March 1 system migration**, where the collapse of raw model fitness from 0.93 to 0.55 accompanied by the emergence of `Eligibility Screen` and duplicated `Open Case` events is unequivocally a logging and semantic artifact. The math proves that applying a label mapping fully restores structural conformance (mapped fitness ~0.90), confirming that the underlying control-flow syntax was modified by software deployment rather than operational breakdown.

Equally strong is the attribution of the **May–June rework surge** to **Supplier Z's API and staffing disruption**. The precise temporal alignment between Supplier Z's volume jump (from 18% to 46%) and the doubling of `Assess -> Request Info` loops (from 12 to 29 per 100 cases), coupled with a surge in rework variants, demonstrates that external supplier data quality directly degraded internal process efficiency.

Our conclusions regarding **July–August queueing and performance degradation** are moderately strong. While the June 15 loss of four assessors is historically documented, the precise quantification of queueing delay versus service time increase relies on aggregate P90 and median metrics. The data confirms that median service time for `Assess` increased only modestly (4.8h to 5.4h), whereas P90 cycle times exploded from 95h to 110h (and later 121h in November). This signature proves that **resource scarcity and queueing bottlenecks**—exacerbated by the July 1 priority scheduling rule—generated waiting time inflation rather than direct assessor inefficiency.

Mediation and multicollinearity complicate absolute attribution during the **October–December renewal season**. The convergence of peak annual case volume (790 cases), a jump in high-complexity share (45%), and lingering Supplier Z backlogs creates severe overlap. We cannot cleanly separate how much of the November cycle-time peak was driven by seasonal population mix versus cumulative staffing exhaustion without granular case-level event logs. 

We would revise this account if event-level analysis reveals that missing resource identifiers are systematically correlated with specific assessors, or if priority-class stratification shows that standard-complexity cases experienced zero wait-time inflation, which would invalidate our hypothesis of queue starvation.
