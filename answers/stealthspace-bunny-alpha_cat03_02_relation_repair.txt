## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

The repair treats the supplied log as evidence, not as a lifecycle template that must be completed.

- **Hard invariant — referential integrity:** Every E2O or O2O endpoint must identify an existing event or object. No repair may invent an event, object, or duplicate an already recorded relation.
- **Hard invariant — direction and non-duplication:** O2O qualifiers are directional. An exact duplicate of an existing E2O or O2O edge is not new evidence. “O2O” does not mean every qualifier is globally one-to-one; one invoice may bill several orders.
- **Strong default — activity cardinality:** Packing normally identifies one item and one package; dispatch normally identifies one package and one delivery; payment normally identifies one payment and one invoice. These defaults support additions but do not override explicit evidence of an outage or an aggregate acknowledgement.
- **Strong default — lifecycle sequence:** Creation before item addition, packing before dispatch, and invoice/payment activities before their respective records are expected. Shared packages and split deliveries are permissible.
- **Weak heuristic — identifiers:** `PK8-2` favors PK-8, while `N-421?` favors DEL-Y. `N-42*` cannot distinguish DEL-X from DEL-Y. The payment’s `B-772` supplies no direct evidence for a particular invoice.
- **Weak heuristic — time, customer, and route:** Ordering, C17, NORTH, and similar timestamps provide context. Simultaneous dispatch does not prove that two packages share a delivery; a common customer does not make two orders identical.
- **Weak heuristic — amount:** The declared values total 230, matching the invoice total, and payments total 230. This supports a combined-invoice interpretation but does not prove either invoice-to-order or payment-to-invoice linkage. Fees, discounts, taxes, or unapplied cash remain possible.
- **Evidence against a repair:** A proposed edge can be weakened by explicit uncertainty, a lifecycle conflict, competing identifiers, or an already sufficient association. For example, forcing ev08 into PK-7 conflicts with the stronger `PK8-2` clue; replacing ev11’s order association with one delivery ignores its permitted aggregate/fallback behavior.

I combine these as weighted evidence. Existing E2O/O2O structure supplies a semantic prior, not independent confirmation of an edge inferred from that same structure. Confidence numbers below are interpretive scores, not calibrated probabilities or assertions of uniqueness.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

### Bundle A — conservative default completion

Bundle A adopts A1–A10 while retaining all observed relations, including ev11  ORD-A.

- **ev08/PK-8:** Treat the item-only row as an outage subsequently reconciled through its scan reference. PK-8 becomes a plausible two-item package.
- **ev10/DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Attach PK-8 provisionally to DEL-Y, but leave DEL-X available as the weaker carrier-reference-error interpretation.
- **ev11/deliveries:** Keep the order-level link and provisionally associate the acknowledgement with both deliveries. This is an aggregate confirmation, not proof of either carrier identity individually.
- **INV-44/ORD-B:** Add ORD-B as a second billed order. The resulting combined commercial interpretation is coherent, although numerically supportive rather than conclusive.
- **PAY-2/INV-44:** Add the second payment to the same invoice. Together with PAY-1, this supplies a complete but not proven settlement interpretation.

The package and invoice aggregates are intentional: PK-8 has two items, INV-44 bills two orders, and one confirmation may cover two deliveries.

### Bundle B — preserve unresolved lifecycle gaps

Bundle B accepts A2, A5, and A6, plus their O2O counterparts, but rejects A1, A3, and A4.

- **ev08/PK-8:** Leave ev08 item-only. A missing scan target is an allowed, complete record—not an objectless event.
- **ev10/deliveries:** Still favor DEL-Y for PK-8.
- **ev11/deliveries:** Retain only the observed ORD-A association. The carrier identities remain unresolved, so neither DEL-X nor DEL-Y gains a confirmation edge.
- **INV-44/ORD-B:** Retain the combined invoice interpretation.
- **PAY-2/INV-44:** Retain the second payment association.

This yields two dispatched deliveries but only an order-level confirmation record. That incompleteness is acceptable under the portal-fallback rule. IT-B1 still has its Add Item and Pack Item events, but no package target is asserted for ev08.

### Bundle C — incomplete financial reconciliation

Bundle C uses A1–A4 and A7–A8, but leaves financial completion unresolved.

- **ev08 and ev10:** Use PK-8 for ev08 and DEL-Y for ev10.
- **ev11:** Keep both provisional delivery associations.
- **INV-44/ORD-B:** Retain only the observed ORD-A link. ORD-B can remain uninvoiced or have documentation outside this extract.
- **PAY-2/INV-44:** Leave settlement unresolved; PAY-2 could be unapplied cash.

The invoice total exceeding either order’s declared value prevents arithmetic from settling the issue. This bundle deliberately leaves ORD-B without an invoice and PAY-2 without a settlement target.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Order.** Bundle A gives both orders creation and item-addition histories. ORD-A has confirmation and invoice links; ORD-B gains an invoice link. Items lead to packages through E2O/O2O paths, but those paths are not direct Order-to-Package O2O relations. Bundle C leaves ORD-B without invoicing.

**Item.** IT-A1 follows a clear add–pack–dispatch path. IT-A2 has a clear packing and dispatch path. IT-B1 is the uncertainty point: Bundle A places it in PK-8, while Bundle B preserves its item-only packing event. None of the items is an orphan object in either bundle.

**Package.** PK-7 has a coherent single-item path to DEL-X. In Bundle A, PK-8 packs two items and dispatches through DEL-Y. This one-to-many packing relationship is plausible, not a uniqueness violation. Bundle B gives PK-8 a less complete item history.

**Delivery.** Bundle A gives each delivery a package and a provisional confirmation. The two provisional confirmation links form an aggregate event-to-delivery pattern, not evidence that one delivery uniquely identifies one package. Bundle B leaves both delivery confirmation identities unresolved.

**Invoice.** INV-44 bills two orders in A/B and one in C. The first case is a plausible one-invoice-to-many-orders relationship; the second remains a plausible incomplete or differently scoped invoice.

**Payment.** PAY-1 has a complete observed path. PAY-2 is provisionally linked to INV-44 in A/B; in C it remains an object with a Record Payment event but no asserted settlement target. An unresolved settlement is not an orphan object.

There is **no circular reasoning intended**: A7–A10 are object-level paraphrases of provisional E2O additions, not independent corroboration. For example, deriving `DEL-Y carries PK-8` from ev10 and then citing that O2O edge to prove ev10 would be circular. By contrast, the existing `DEL-X carries PK-7`, scanner evidence, and carrier reference provide separate recorded support for that existing lifecycle.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**OC-DFG.** Raw event counts and ordinary event-only directly-follows edges remain unchanged in both bundles: there are still 14 events. `Add Item  Pack Item`, `Pack Item  Dispatch Package`, `Dispatch Package  Confirm Delivery`, and `Confirm Delivery  Issue Invoice  Record Payment` already exist temporally. Differences appear in object-associated activity paths, not necessarily in the raw event DFG. A version or filtering option that restricts events by object association could nevertheless change discovery output.

**OTG.** OTG aggregates by object type, so some additions may be topologically invisible. Pack Item already relates to Package through ev06/ev07, and Dispatch Package already relates to Delivery through ev09. A may increase multiplicities or weights rather than introduce new vertices. A does add a second Issue-Invoice-to-Order association and a first Record-Payment-to-Invoice association at the relevant type level. Exact behavior depends on the PM4Py implementation and whether O2O edges are incorporated.

**ET–OT graph.** Event nodes remain fixed, but A can add or strengthen Pack Item–Package, Dispatch Package–Delivery, Confirm Delivery–Delivery, Issue Invoice–Order, and Record Payment–Invoice links. A binary implementation may only increase edge weights; a multigraph implementation may retain parallel associations. These should not be mistaken for new events.

**Plain flattenings.** `pm4py.ocel_flattening` primarily exposes E2O-connected events; it does not automatically traverse every O2O edge.

- ORD-A retains ev01, ev03, ev04, ev11, and ev12. ORD-B has ev02 and ev05, plus ev12 in A/B but not C.
- PK-7 retains ev06 and ev09. PK-8 has ev07 and ev10, plus ev08 in A but not B.
- DEL-X retains ev09, plus ev11 in A/B. DEL-Y has ev10 in A/B, plus ev11 only in A.
- INV-44 has ev12–ev14 in A/B, but ev12–ev13 in C.
- PAY-1 and PAY-2 each still have their respective payment event. Losing PAY-2’s invoice edge removes settlement context, not its Record Payment event.

**Frequencies and clustering.** Event-based frequencies are always 2 Create Order, 3 Add Item, 4 Pack Item, 2 Dispatch Package, 1 Confirm Delivery, 1 Issue Invoice, and 2 Record Payment. There are always 12 unique objects. Object-based activity counts change: Package is associated with two packing events in A but only one uniquely associated object in B; Delivery has two confirmation-associated objects in A but one in B; Invoice has two payment-associated objects in A/B but one in C.

Order-flattened traces become more similar in A because both orders reach the shared invoice and its payments. That similarity may improve equivalent-execution clustering, but it does not prove ORD-A and ORD-B are the same order. C makes their invoice histories diverge. Manually expanding O2O paths can further align traces, but may also duplicate shared-package events and create artificial many-to-many behavior.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Forcing ev08 into PK-7** merely to give IT-B1 a package would contradict the stronger scan clue and could create a false repacking or cross-order package history.
2. **Correcting `N-421?` silently to N-420** would change an uncertain record into a supposedly known fact and could attach PK-8 to two deliveries at the same timestamp.
3. **Deleting ev11  ORD-A after adding delivery links** would erase a legitimate portal fallback and weaken the order-level delivery-confirmation trace.
4. **Treating DEL-X and DEL-Y as interchangeable** would erase meaningful carrier and package distinctions. Conversely, requiring exactly one confirmation object would ignore aggregate acknowledgement.
5. **Using the 230 total as proof of ORD-B billing** could turn fees, discounts, taxes, or unrelated commercial activity into a false order lifecycle.
6. **Using the remaining 130 as proof of PAY-2 settlement** could suppress genuine unapplied cash and distort object-centric payment conformance.
7. **Adding reverse O2O edges** such as `PK-7 carries DEL-X` would create directionally incorrect behavior and potentially duplicate dispatch evidence during enrichment.
8. **Counting an E2O edge and its inferred O2O counterpart as two independent facts** would double-count support and encourage unjustified confidence.
9. **Inventing a second invoice to accommodate PAY-2** would manufacture an object rather than represent uncertainty using the supplied data.
