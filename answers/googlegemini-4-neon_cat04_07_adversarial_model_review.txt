# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

This review evaluates two candidate process models—**Model S** (simple/permissive) and **Model C** (complex/precise)—submitted for the custom-order dossier process, and synthesizes a defensible third alternative for the model-governance board. Because no single process model optimizes every analytical objective simultaneously, evaluation criteria must be separated by **intended use case**:

- **Exploratory discovery and hypothesis generation:** The goal is to summarize dominant control-flow patterns, surface unexpected paths, and guide follow-up data investigation. Slightly higher fitness and simplicity are prioritized; moderate under-precision is tolerable *only* if users understand the model is descriptive rather than normative.
- **Conformance enforcement and compliance auditing:** The goal is to detect policy breaches reliably without drowning operations in false alarms. **Hard policy boundaries (forbidden behavior rejection)** and **diagnostic precision** are paramount. High fitness achieved by relaxing mandatory controls is actively harmful, while overly rigid memorization creates alert fatigue and shadow processes.
- **Simulation and capacity/what-if analysis:** The goal is to generate realistic synthetic executions for bottleneck and SLA testing. This requires **compositional generalization** (generating valid combinations of checks, approvals, and rework), calibrated branching probabilities, and sound termination; neither a permissive over-approximator nor an 8-variant lookup table suffices.
- **Stakeholder communication and governance alignment:** The goal is shared operational truth across Compliance, Operations, IT Architecture, and Audit. This demands structural clarity, low label duplication, traceable ownership per activity, and explicit visibility of unresolved policy rules.

**How use case shifts the fitness–precision trade-off:** In exploration, favoring recall (fitness $\approx 0.90\text{–}0.98$) over precision ($\approx 0.60\text{–}0.75$) can be defensible if coupled with variant inspection. In compliance enforcement, **precision on forbidden behavior** (rejecting unauthorized skips, inverted precedence, and unjustified repeats) is a hard constraint that cannot be traded away for higher alignment fitness. Conversely, high training precision achieved by memorizing observed variants destroys **holdout fitness** and **compositional generalization**, rendering a model unusable for operations and simulation.

**Working assumptions and epistemic boundaries:**
1. **Unobserved attributes:** Neither `order_source` (external vs. internal, needed for P3) nor `affected_checks` (checks invalidated by `Correct Dossier`, needed for P6) exists in the current control-flow discovery log. No purely control-flow model can certify P3 or P6 completely.
2. **Incomplete experimental provenance:** Both teams omitted random seeds, split stratification logic, preprocessing scripts, alignment-cost functions, and holdout case identifiers. Reported metrics are treated as **unverified preliminary claims**, not ground truth.
3. **Unresolved policy details:** Stakeholders have not yet formally decided (a) whether `Supplier Review` may run concurrently with `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` or must follow them, (b) whether `Correct Dossier` can invalidate `Supplier Review` alone, and (c) whether `Auto Approval` carries business-rule restrictions after corrections or expediting.
4. **Formal semantics:** Process trees are interpreted under standard PM4Py semantics: `->` (strict sequence), `+` (concurrent interleaving / AND), `X` (exclusive choice / XOR), `*` (loop, absent from both inputs), and `tau` (silent skip).

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

### Strongest Case FOR Model S
Model S is compact, acyclic, free of duplicate labels, sound by construction, and fast to align (`0.018 s` median). It naturally captures **compositional concurrency** without enumerating permutations: it accepts both orderings of `Credit Check` and `Technical Review`, accepts both `Manager Approval` and `Auto Approval` across all upstream combinations, and naturally accommodates `Expedite Review` occurring before or after `Supplier Review`. As an **early exploratory sketch** (F7), it immediately conveys the process phases (Registration $\rightarrow$ Check Pool $\rightarrow$ Approval $\rightarrow$ Release) and avoids the catastrophic holdout collapse seen in Model C (`0.981` vs. `0.744`). Where event logs suffer from occasional missing `Supplier Review` events (F4), optionalizing that check prevents noisy logging gaps from triggering spurious structural failures.

### Strongest Case AGAINST Model S
Model S is **unfit for compliance, audit, or operational governance** (F1) and fails even as a complete descriptive model:
1. **Violation of core mandatory controls (P2):** Wrapping `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` in `X(..., tau)` allows approval and order release with *one or zero* mandatory checks performed.
2. **Violation of precedence controls (P4):** Placing `Expedite Review` inside the same parallel `+` block as the mandatory checks allows expediting *before* or *between* `Credit Check` and `Technical Review`, or without either check occurring at all.
3. **Complete omission of rework/correction (P6):** Contrary to the claim that Model S is "robust," `Correct Dossier` **does not appear anywhere in Model S**, nor does any loop operator (`*`). Consequently, Model S rejects *every* valid correction trace (including B6) and incurs silent alignment penalties on all real-world rework cases.
4. **Accidental rather than principled handling of P7:** Model S rejects B8 (duplicate check without correction) only as an accidental side effect of forbidding *all* activity repetitions—throwing out legitimate P6 rework alongside suspicious P7 repeats.
5. **Deceptive simplicity:** A `4.4/5` informal comprehension score is hazardous if business stakeholders assume the diagram enforces P2 and P4 when the formal tree does not.

### Concrete Undesirable or Policy-Ambiguous Traces Admitted by Model S
Model S admits $2^4$ subsets across all permutations ($65$ middle sequences $\times 2$ approvals $= 130$ traces). The following six admitted traces expose concrete policy breaches:

1. **Complete bypass of all checks (B3 — violates P2):**
   `Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order`
2. **Partial omission of mandatory review (violates P2):**
   `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Auto Approval, Release Order` *(skips `Technical Review` silently via `tau`)*
3. **Premature expedite review before mandatory checks (B4 — violates P4):**
   `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order`
4. **Expedite review interleaved between mandatory checks (violates P4):**
   `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Auto Approval, Release Order` *(violates "must follow **both** mandatory checks")*
5. **Expedite review with zero mandatory checks (violates P2 and P4):**
   `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Auto Approval, Release Order`
6. **Internal/external ambiguity combined with missing mandatory check (violates P2, ambiguous on P3):**
   `Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` *(admitted even though `Credit Check` never occurred, and `Supplier Review` cannot be audited against external status)*

### Intentional Noise Tolerance vs. Accidental Semantic Flaws
- **Defensible (conditional) permissiveness:** Making `Supplier Review` optional in a purely control-flow model is a *temporarily defensible* concession to missing `order_source` data and missing `Supplier Review` logging (P3, F4)—*provided* it is explicitly flagged for attribute-based post-hoc verification rather than treated as compliant. Allowing `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` in parallel, and `Expedite Review` concurrent with `Supplier Review`, reflects genuine policy flexibility (P2, P4).
- **Accidental / indefensible semantic flaws:** Optionalizing mandatory `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` is **not** noise tolerance; it destroys the ability to distinguish logging drops from compliance breaches. Placing `Expedite Review` concurrently with mandatory checks is an outright structural modeling error (violating P4). Omitting `Correct Dossier` entirely contradicts the claim of robustness and leaves rework completely unmodeled.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

### Strongest Case FOR Model C
Model C represents a **strict allow-list philosophy** (F8): every one of its 8 branches respects P1, P2, P4, P5, and P7, and it decisively rejects severe compliance breaches such as B3 (skipping mandatory checks), B4 (premature `Expedite Review`), and B8 (unjustified repeat of `Credit Check`). Unlike Model S, Model C acknowledges the existence of `Correct Dossier` (Branch 7) and demonstrates high alignment precision on the training log (`0.941`). In an extreme short-term regulatory freeze where *only* pre-approved historical trace templates may pass without manual escalation, an explicit variant catalog has clear, unambiguous membership semantics.

### Strongest Case AGAINST Model C
Model C is **not a process model in the structural sense**; it is a hard-coded XOR enumeration of 8 frequent training variants. This creates severe operational, analytical, and architectural failures:

1. **Branch enumeration and combinatorial non-scalability:** The model explicitly lists 8 fixed sequences. Even *without* any corrections, the policy permits $2$ (`CC`/`TR` orders) $\times 2$ (`Supplier` absent/present) $\times \text{(if present: } 1 \text{ without Exp} + 2 \text{ with Exp ordering, else } 2)$ $\times 2$ (approvals) $= 20+$ valid variants (and infinitely many once repeatable `Correct Dossier` under P6 is included). Enumerating variants explicitly causes combinatorial explosion whenever one new check or rule is added.
2. **Severe label duplication and ownership failure (F3):** The submitted tree contains **48 visible transitions across 9 activity labels** (`Register Dossier`: 8, `Credit Check`: 9, `Technical Review`: 9, `Supplier Review`: 3, `Expedite Review`: 2, `Manager Approval`: 7, `Release Order`: 8, `Correct Dossier`: 1, `Auto Approval`: 1)—i.e., **39 excess label occurrences** (and an unresolved discrepancy with the undocumented reported count of `33`). In IT architecture and governance, changing SLA rules, system integrations, or ownership for `Credit Check` requires coordinating 9 separate transition instances.
3. **Rejection of legitimate unseen combinations (F2, F4):** Model C rejects valid recombinations of behaviors it already knows individually. For example, it knows `Technical Review -> Credit Check` (Branches 2, 4) and it knows `Auto Approval` (Branch 8), yet it rejects their combination **B2**. It knows `Supplier Review`, `Expedite Review`, `Auto Approval`, and `Technical Review -> Credit Check`, yet it rejects **B5**. It also violates P4 by only allowing `Supplier Review` *before* `Expedite Review` (Branch 6), rejecting `Expedite Review` before `Supplier Review`.
4. **Broken correction semantics (P6, F6):** Branch 7 permits at most **one** `Correct Dossier`, forces **both** `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` to repeat in one rigid order (`CC -> TR`), forbids single-check rechecks (**B6**), forbids repeated corrections (P6 explicitly states "`Correct Dossier` may repeat"), and forbids corrections in any case involving `Supplier Review`, `Expedite Review`, `Auto Approval`, or initial `TR -> CC` order.
5. **Holdout degradation and false-alarm risk:** Alignment fitness collapses from `0.973` (training) to `0.744` (holdout). Treating unseen valid recombinations as noncompliant (F8) will overwhelm Compliance and Operations with false positives, eroding trust in process monitoring.
6. **Precision as a memorization artifact:** Model C’s `0.941` training precision is largely an artifact of **memorizing the training variant head**. Because alignment precision compares model-enabled transitions against observed prefixes *in the training log*, an XOR of the most frequent training traces automatically yields near-1.0 precision on that same log—even while failing holdout validity and lacking any negative-suite test (F5).
7. **Maintainability, comprehension, and runtime penalties:** Arc-degree simplicity (`0.281`), generalization (`0.412`), stakeholder comprehension (`1.9/5`), and alignment runtime (`0.440 s`, 24× slower than Model S) all degrade sharply as direct consequences of XOR branch proliferation.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

Ten structural and methodological pathologies likely distort the reported benchmark table. They are **ranked from highest to lowest expected decision impact** on the governance board:

1. **Fitness rewarded by optionalizing mandatory behavior (Impact: Critical — Rank 1):** Wrapping mandatory checks (`Credit Check`, `Technical Review`) in `X(a, tau)` turns compliance failures (missing checks) into zero-cost silent skips. This inflates Model S’s training (`0.992`) and holdout (`0.981`) fitness while completely defeating P2.
2. **Positive-log-only precision without a negative behavior suite (Impact: Critical — Rank 2):** Measuring precision solely against observed positive training traces (F5) creates a dual blind spot: it rewards Model C (`0.941`) for memorizing training variants while failing to penalize it for rejecting valid unseen variants, and it does not distinguish *benign* unobserved behavior (like B5) from *catastrophic* forbidden behavior (like B3/B4) in Model S’s `0.584`.
3. **Train/holdout split sensitivity and missing provenance (Impact: High — Rank 3):** Without random seeds, stratification rules, preprocessing code, or held-out case IDs, we cannot verify that both teams evaluated the exact same split or avoided temporal/variant leakage. A random case split can mask concept drift, whereas an unstratified split where certain combinations (`Supplier Review + Auto Approval`, F4) land primarily in holdout can swing Model C’s holdout fitness wildly.
4. **Silent-transition effects (Impact: High — Rank 4):** Model S relies on four `tau` bypasses inside a parallel block. Silent transitions create invisible shortcuts that suppress move-on-model penalties during alignments, distort prefix-automaton escaping-edge counts depending on silent-transition reduction, and mask non-compliant traces behind clean fitness numbers.
5. **Generalization rewarded by excessive permissiveness (Impact: High — Rank 5):** Token-based generalization (`generalization_tbr`) estimates generalization from transition visit frequency ($1 - \text{mean}(1/\sqrt{\text{visits}})$). Model S achieves `0.907` not because it captures true process structure, but because funneling all cases through a tiny pool of transitions maximizes visit counts—a completely meaningless flower model scores near `1.0`.
6. **Missing-event bias (Impact: Medium–High — Rank 6):** The analyst notes that some `Supplier Review` events may be missing from the log (F4). In Model S, missing events are silently absorbed by `X('Supplier Review', tau)`, hiding logging defects. In Model C, missing events penalize fitness identically to genuine policy violations, confounding data-quality errors with noncompliance.
7. **Duplicated-label effects and undocumented metric definition (Impact: Medium–High — Rank 7):** Model C’s 39 excess label occurrences fragment transition visit counts (depressing `generalization_tbr` to `0.412`), bloat place degrees (depressing `arc_degree` simplicity to `0.281`), and multiply A* alignment paths. Worse, the reported duplicate count (`33`) cannot be reproduced from the submitted tree, undermining trust in the entire benchmark table.
8. **Alignment-cost sensitivity (Impact: Medium — Rank 8):** Standard unit costs treat a skipped `Credit Check` (major compliance breach), a missing `Supplier Review` (possible logging gap), and an unmodeled `Correct Dossier` (structural model omission) identically. Model S still achieves `0.992` fitness despite lacking `Correct Dossier` entirely—either because corrections are rare in the log or because undocumented low move-on-log costs masked them.
9. **Stakeholder-score methodology bias (Impact: Medium — Rank 9):** An informal 1–5 rating (`4.4` vs. `1.9`) without a documented protocol, blind labeling, or comprehension test questions measures **visual neatness**, not **semantic accuracy**. Stakeholders frequently rate overly permissive diagrams highly because they do not notice subtle `tau` skips or missing ordering edges.
10. **Runtime confounding by model size and unaligned traces (Impact: Low–Medium — Rank 10):** Model C’s `0.440 s` median runtime reflects combinatorial A* search over 48 transitions and 8 branches—and potentially higher alignment search cost on non-fitting holdout traces (the table does not even specify whether median runtime was measured on training or holdout cases). While operationally relevant, runtime is a downstream symptom of branch enumeration rather than an independent quality dimension.

# 7. Third Alternative

### Chosen Strategy: Two-Layer Hybrid (Balanced POWL Backbone + Declarative/Attribute Policy Guard)
Neither a permissive control-flow tree nor an enumerated variant catalog can simultaneously satisfy compliance, compositional generalization, maintainability, and missing-attribute transparency. We therefore design a **two-layer hybrid**:

- **Layer 1 (`model_third` — Balanced POWL Control-Flow Backbone):** Enforces all **control-flow-decidable** rules (P1, P2, P4, P5, P7, and the structural part of P6) in a sound, compact, partial-order workflow model with **13 visible transitions** (only 4 excess occurrences across 9 labels, vs. 39 in Model C).
- **Layer 2 (Declarative & Attribute-Aware Post-Hoc Policy Layer):** Explicitly evaluates **data-dependent rules** (P3 external/internal mandate and P6 affected-check verification) and **audits missing-attribute uncertainty** rather than silently hiding missing data behind unrestricted optionality.

### 1. Compact Formal Description of `model_third`
`model_third` is a `StrictPartialOrder` over 8 top-level POWL nodes:

- `reg`: `Register Dossier`
- `cc_init`: `Credit Check` *(mandatory initial check)*
- `tr_init`: `Technical Review` *(mandatory initial check)*
- `corr_loop`: `LOOP(do = tau, redo = corr_body)` *(0 or more correction cycles)*, where:
  - `corr_body` = `PO(Correct Dossier -> recheck_choice)`
  - `recheck_choice` = `XOR(Credit Check, Technical Review, PO(Credit Check, Technical Review))` *(structurally non-empty recheck of Credit Check, Technical Review, or both in either order)*
- `sup_opt`: `XOR(Supplier Review, tau)` *(attribute-conditioned optionality; governed by Layer 2 Rule D1)*
- `exp_opt`: `XOR(Expedite Review, tau)` *(optional)*
- `approval`: `XOR(Manager Approval, Auto Approval)` *(exactly one approval)*
- `rel`: `Release Order`

**Strict partial-order precedence edges (DAG):**
- `reg -> cc_init`, `reg -> tr_init`, `reg -> sup_opt`
- `cc_init -> corr_loop`, `tr_init -> corr_loop`
- `corr_loop -> exp_opt`
- `exp_opt -> approval`, `sup_opt -> approval`
- `approval -> rel`

*(No edge between `cc_init` and `tr_init` $\Rightarrow$ concurrent. No edge between `sup_opt` and `{cc_init, tr_init, corr_loop, exp_opt}` $\Rightarrow$ `Supplier Review` may occur before or after `Expedite Review` and concurrently with check execution.)*

### 2. Self-Contained PM4Py Code Constructing `model_third`

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
    OperatorPOWL,
    StrictPartialOrder,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def build_model_third() -> StrictPartialOrder:
    # Distinct node instances for every POWL element (never reuse node objects)
    reg = Transition(label="Register Dossier")
    cc_init = Transition(label="Credit Check")
    tr_init = Transition(label="Technical Review")

    # Post-correction targeted recheck: Credit Check, Technical Review, or both (concurrent)
    recheck_both = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[
            Transition(label="Credit Check"),
            Transition(label="Technical Review"),
        ]
    )
    recheck_choice = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [
            Transition(label="Credit Check"),
            Transition(label="Technical Review"),
            recheck_both,
        ],
    )

    corr_act = Transition(label="Correct Dossier")
    corr_body = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[corr_act, recheck_choice])
    corr_body.order.add_edge(corr_act, recheck_choice)

    # Zero or more correction-recheck cycles: do=tau, redo=corr_body => (corr_body)*
    corr_loop = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.LOOP,
        [SilentTransition(), corr_body],
    )

    # Attribute-conditioned optional Supplier Review (verified in Layer 2)
    sup_opt = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [Transition(label="Supplier Review"), SilentTransition()],
    )

    # Optional Expedite Review (must follow mandatory checks and any corrections)
    exp_opt = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [Transition(label="Expedite Review"), SilentTransition()],
    )

    # Exactly one approval before release
    approval = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [
            Transition(label="Manager Approval"),
            Transition(label="Auto Approval"),
        ],
    )
    rel = Transition(label="Release Order")

    # Top-level Strict Partial Order
    root = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[reg, cc_init, tr_init, corr_loop, sup_opt, exp_opt, approval, rel]
    )
    root.order.add_edge(reg, cc_init)
    root.order.add_edge(reg, tr_init)
    root.order.add_edge(reg, sup_opt)
    root.order.add_edge(cc_init, corr_loop)
    root.order.add_edge(tr_init, corr_loop)
    root.order.add_edge(corr_loop, exp_opt)
    root.order.add_edge(exp_opt, approval)
    root.order.add_edge(sup_opt, approval)
    root.order.add_edge(approval, rel)

    return root


model_third = build_model_third()

# Convert to Petri net and verify soundness
net_third, im_third, fm_third = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)
is_sound, diagnostics = pm4py.check_soundness(net_third, im_third, fm_third, print_diagnostics=True)
assert is_sound, f"model_third failed soundness check: {diagnostics}"
```

### 3. Declarative Dictionaries and External Post-Hoc Checks (Layer 2)

```python
DECLARATIVE_POLICY_RULES = {
    "P3_supplier_review_external_mandate": {
        "required_case_attribute": "order_source",  # expected values: "external" | "internal"
        "when_external": {"template": "Existence", "activity": "Supplier Review", "min_count": 1},
        "when_internal": {"template": "Absence", "activity": "Supplier Review", "max_count": 0},
        "when_attribute_missing": "FLAG_UNVERIFIED_P3",
    },
    "P6_correction_affected_checks": {
        "required_event_attribute": "affected_checks",  # subset of {"Credit Check", "Technical Review", "Supplier Review"}
        "segment_rule": "Between Correct Dossier and next Correct Dossier or Approval, executed checks must equal affected_checks.",
        "when_attribute_missing": "FLAG_UNVERIFIED_P6_SCOPE",
    },
    "P7_no_unjustified_mandatory_repeat": {
        "enforced_structurally_in_layer_1": True,
        "declarative_backup": "Every repeat of Credit Check or Technical Review must have an intervening Correct Dossier.",
    },
}


def evaluate_layer2_policy(case_events: list[dict], case_attrs: dict) -> dict:
    """
    case_events: ordered list of dicts with keys 'concept:name' and optional 'affected_checks' (set/list).
    case_attrs: dict with optional 'order_source' ('external' or 'internal').
    Returns structured compliance status separating violations from missing-data uncertainty.
    """
    activities = [e["concept:name"] for e in case_events]
    violations = []
    audit_flags = []

    # Rule D1: P3 Supplier Review conditioned on order_source
    order_source = case_attrs.get("order_source")
    sup_count = activities.count("Supplier Review")
    if order_source == "external":
        if sup_count == 0:
            violations.append("P3_VIOLATION: External order missing Supplier Review (or logging drop).")
    elif order_source == "internal":
        if sup_count > 0:
            violations.append("P3_VIOLATION: Internal order executed unnecessary Supplier Review.")
    else:
        audit_flags.append("P3_UNVERIFIED: Missing case attribute 'order_source'; cannot certify Supplier Review rule.")

    # Rule D2: P6 Affected-check recheck verification per correction segment
    corr_indices = [i for i, a in enumerate(activities) if a == "Correct Dossier"]
    approval_idx = next(
        (i for i, a in enumerate(activities) if a in {"Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"}),
        len(activities),
    )
    for pos, idx in enumerate(corr_indices):
        end_idx = corr_indices[pos + 1] if pos + 1 < len(corr_indices) else approval_idx
        segment_checks = {
            a for a in activities[idx + 1 : end_idx]
            if a in {"Credit Check", "Technical Review", "Supplier Review"}
        }
        affected = case_events[idx].get("affected_checks")
        if affected is None:
            audit_flags.append(
                f"P6_UNVERIFIED_SCOPE: Event #{idx} (Correct Dossier) lacks 'affected_checks'; "
                f"observed rechecks={sorted(segment_checks)}."
            )
        else:
            affected_set = set(affected)
            missing = affected_set - segment_checks
            extra = segment_checks - affected_set
            if missing:
                violations.append(f"P6_VIOLATION: Event #{idx} missed required recheck(s): {sorted(missing)}.")
            if extra:
                audit_flags.append(f"P6_OVER_RECHECK: Event #{idx} repeated unaffected check(s): {sorted(extra)}.")

    # Rule D3: P7 Defensive check (also enforced structurally by model_third)
    for check in ("Credit Check", "Technical Review"):
        positions = [i for i, a in enumerate(activities) if a == check]
        for prev_i, next_i in zip(positions, positions[1:]):
            if "Correct Dossier" not in activities[prev_i + 1 : next_i]:
                violations.append(f"P7_VIOLATION: Repeated '{check}' without intervening Correct Dossier.")

    status = "NON_COMPLIANT" if violations else ("PROVISIONALLY_COMPLIANT_PENDING_DATA" if audit_flags else "COMPLIANT")
    return {"status": status, "violations": violations, "audit_flags": audit_flags}
```

### 4. Explicit Semantics of `model_third`
- **Mandatory checks (P2):** `cc_init` and `tr_init` are non-optional nodes with no edge between them, placed strictly after `Register Dossier` and strictly before `corr_loop`, `exp_opt`, and `approval`. Both must complete (in either order) before approval; skipping either is structurally impossible.
- **Optional `Supplier Review` (P3):** Modeled as `XOR(Supplier Review, tau)` strictly between `Register Dossier` and `approval`, concurrent with checks and `Expedite Review`. Crucially, this optionality is **not** a silent waiver: Layer 2 enforces `Existence` for external orders, `Absence` for internal orders, and emits `P3_UNVERIFIED` whenever `order_source` is absent.
- **`Expedite Review` ordering (P4):** Placed strictly after `corr_loop` (hence transitively after both initial mandatory checks and any post-correction rechecks) and strictly before `approval`, with no precedence edge to `sup_opt`. Thus it must follow both mandatory checks, precedes approval, and may occur before or after `Supplier Review`.
- **Exactly-one approval (P5):** `XOR(Manager Approval, Auto Approval)` sits strictly before `Release Order`, guaranteeing mutual exclusivity and mandatory execution.
- **Correction and recheck behavior (P6, P7):** `corr_loop` permits zero or more `Correct Dossier` events after initial checks. Every `Correct Dossier` must be followed by a **non-empty** subset of `{Credit Check, Technical Review}` (single check or both in either order), preventing both vacuous corrections (0 rechecks) and unprompted repeats without `Correct Dossier` (P7).

### 5. Limitations Caused by Missing Attributes (and Unresolved Policy Questions)
- **Cannot certify P3 without `order_source`:** Control flow alone cannot distinguish a valid internal order without `Supplier Review` from an external order that skipped `Supplier Review` (or suffered a logging drop per F4). Layer 2 makes this epistemic gap explicit via `PROVISIONALLY_COMPLIANT_PENDING_DATA` instead of hiding it.
- **Cannot certify P6 scope without `affected_checks`:** Control flow alone cannot verify whether `Correct Dossier -> Technical Review` (B6) was sufficient or illegally skipped an affected `Credit Check`. Layer 1 prevents empty rechecks and unprompted repeats, while Layer 2 flags the scope gap until the dossier system logs `affected_checks`.
- **Documented design assumptions pending stakeholder sign-off:**
  1. `model_third` currently allows `Supplier Review` to run concurrently with initial mandatory checks; if stakeholders mandate that `Supplier Review` wait for both mandatory checks, adding two edges (`cc_init -> sup_opt`, `tr_init -> sup_opt`) enforces it cleanly.
  2. `model_third` places `Expedite Review` after `corr_loop` (so expediting evaluates the final corrected dossier) and does not yet allow `Correct Dossier` to re-trigger `Supplier Review` alone. Both are modular 2–4 line adjustments in POWL once stakeholders resolve those edge cases.

# 9. Validation Experiments

Before approving `model_third` (or reconsidering either original), the discovery and audit teams must execute a reproducible validation protocol with published code, fixed seeds, and disaggregated metrics.

### Evaluation Program Design
1. **Reproducible case splits & published preprocessing:** Freeze raw event log hash, publish deterministic cleaning code (timestamp sorting, incomplete-case handling, duplicate-event rules), and evaluate **both** a fixed-seed 5-fold case split (`seed=42`) and a **chronological 80/20 split** (earlier cases train, later cases test) to detect concept drift and eliminate split cherry-picking.
2. **Attribute-stratified & leave-variant-out holdout tests:** Once ERP attributes (`order_source`, `affected_checks`) are joined, stratify splits by `(order_source, approval_type, has_correction, has_expedite)`. Additionally run a **leave-combination-out test** holding out specific valid combinations (e.g., all cases with `Supplier Review + Auto Approval` or `TR -> CC + Expedite`) to directly measure compositional generalization.
3. **Curated negative trace suite:** Co-author a versioned suite of at least 30–50 forbidden traces with Compliance and Audit covering violations of P1–P7 (including B3, B4, B8, A3, A4, double approvals, post-release checks, and reversed start/end). Measure **Forbidden-Trace Rejection Rate (True Negative Rate)** via exact replay/alignment.
4. **Missing-event and duplicate-event perturbation tests:** Take verified valid holdout traces and inject controlled synthetic noise: (a) drop `Supplier Review` (10%–30%), (b) drop `Credit Check` or `Technical Review` (5%–15%), and (c) inject unprompted duplicate mandatory checks (5%–15%). Verify that a compliance model **flags** dropped mandatory checks and unprompted duplicates rather than absorbing them with fitness `1.0`.
5. **Unseen-combination tests:** Construct a synthetic **positive compositional suite** (including B2, B5, A1, A2, and all valid policy-conformant recombinations) and measure **Valid-Combination Acceptance Rate (Compositional Recall)**.
6. **Alignment-cost sensitivity analysis:** Run alignments under at least three explicit cost profiles: (a) standard unit costs (`1` model move, `1` log move), (b) compliance-weighted costs (`10` for missing mandatory checks or inverted precedence, `1` for optional logging omissions), and (c) asymmetric rework-penalty costs. Report whether model rankings reverse under realistic compliance costs.
7. **Soundness, reachability, and play-out checks:** Verify workflow-net soundness (boundedness, option to complete, proper completion, no dead transitions), inspect exact duplicate-label counts with a **standardized formula** (`visible_transitions - distinct_labels`), and sample 1,000+ traces via `pm4py.play_out` to audit generated traces against Layer 2 rules and stakeholder expectations.
8. **Disaggregated quality metrics (train & holdout):** Report alignment fitness (train + holdout), alignment precision (train + holdout), token-based generalization, arc-degree simplicity, duplicate-label count (explicitly defined), and p50/p95 alignment runtime **separately**—never as a single composite average.
9. **Documented stakeholder comprehension protocol:** Conduct a blinded, randomized, **task-based** study ($n \ge 16$ across Compliance, Operations, IT, and Audit) where participants answer 10 concrete behavior questions ("Does this model permit Trace X?") using Model S, Model C, and `model_third` (rendered consistently with a concise legend for Layer 2 rules). Measure **answer accuracy** and **time-to-answer**, not merely subjective 1–5 liking.
10. **Hard governance rejection gates (veto rules):** Reject any candidate model for compliance deployment—**regardless of high aggregate fitness, precision, or simplicity**—if it violates any of the following:
    - Fails workflow-net soundness (`is_sound == False`);
    - Accepts **any** critical forbidden probe (e.g., B3, B4, B8, A4 $\Rightarrow$ Forbidden-Trace Rejection Rate $< 100\%$ on P1/P2/P4/P5/P7 hard violations);
    - Rejects $> 5\%$ of verified valid unseen combinations (compositional recall $< 95\%$);
    - Silently absorbs injected missing mandatory checks with fitness $= 1.0$;
    - Fails to publish reproducible seeds, preprocessing scripts, alignment costs, and standardized duplicate-label counts.

### PM4Py Validation Code Skeleton

```python
import random
import time
import statistics
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import EventLog, Trace, Event
from pm4py.algo.conformance.alignments.petri_net import algorithm as align_algo
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.utils import align_utils


def make_reproducible_splits(df: pd.DataFrame, seed: int = 42, test_frac: float = 0.2):
    """Deterministic case-level random split + chronological split."""
    case_starts = (
        df.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"]
        .min()
        .sort_values()
    )
    ordered_cases = list(case_starts.index)
    split_idx = int(len(ordered_cases) * (1 - test_frac))

    # Chronological split
    chrono_train_ids = set(ordered_cases[:split_idx])
    chrono_test_ids = set(ordered_cases[split_idx:])

    # Seeded random split
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    shuffled = ordered_cases[:]
    rng.shuffle(shuffled)
    rand_train_ids = set(shuffled[:split_idx])
    rand_test_ids = set(shuffled[split_idx:])

    return {
        "chrono": (
            df[df["case:concept:name"].isin(chrono_train_ids)].copy(),
            df[df["case:concept:name"].isin(chrono_test_ids)].copy(),
        ),
        "random": (
            df[df["case:concept:name"].isin(rand_train_ids)].copy(),
            df[df["case:concept:name"].isin(rand_test_ids)].copy(),
        ),
    }


def traces_to_event_log(trace_dict: dict[str, list[str]]) -> EventLog:
    """Convert {probe_id: [activity_names]} into a PM4Py EventLog."""
    log = EventLog()
    for case_id, activities in trace_dict.items():
        tr = Trace(attributes={"concept:name": case_id})
        for act in activities:
            tr.append(Event({"concept:name": act}))
        log.append(tr)
    return log


def count_duplicate_labels_standardized(net) -> dict:
    """Explicit, reproducible duplicate-label accounting on a Petri net."""
    visible_labels = [t.label for t in net.transitions if t.label is not None]
    distinct_labels = set(visible_labels)
    return {
        "visible_transitions": len(visible_labels),
        "distinct_labels": len(distinct_labels),
        "excess_label_occurrences": len(visible_labels) - len(distinct_labels),
        "silent_transitions": sum(1 for t in net.transitions if t.label is None),
    }


def evaluate_model(model, train_log, holdout_log, positive_suite, negative_suite):
    """Evaluate one model with disaggregated metrics and hard probe checks."""
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    is_sound, sound_diag = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=False)

    # Disaggregated conformance metrics (train & holdout)
    fit_train = pm4py.fitness_alignments(train_log, net, im, fm)
    fit_holdout = pm4py.fitness_alignments(holdout_log, net, im, fm)
    prec_train = pm4py.precision_alignments(train_log, net, im, fm)
    prec_holdout = pm4py.precision_alignments(holdout_log, net, im, fm)
    gen_holdout = pm4py.generalization_tbr(holdout_log, net, im, fm)
    simp_arc = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm, variant="arc_degree")
    dup_stats = count_duplicate_labels_standardized(net)

    # Per-case alignment runtime (p50 and p95) on holdout
    runtimes = []
    for trace in holdout_log:
        single_log = EventLog([trace])
        t0 = time.perf_counter()
        pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(single_log, net, im, fm)
        runtimes.append(time.perf_counter() - t0)

    # Positive compositional suite & Negative forbidden suite
    pos_diags = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(positive_suite, net, im, fm)
    neg_diags = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(negative_suite, net, im, fm)
    pos_accept_rate = sum(1 for d in pos_diags if d["fitness"] == 1.0) / len(pos_diags)
    neg_reject_rate = sum(1 for d in neg_diags if d["fitness"] < 1.0) / len(neg_diags)

    # Play-out audit sample (for manual/declarative screening of generated traces)
    simulated_log = pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm)

    return {
        "is_sound": is_sound,
        "soundness_diagnostics": sound_diag,
        "train_alignment_fitness": fit_train,
        "holdout_alignment_fitness": fit_holdout,
        "train_alignment_precision": prec_train,
        "holdout_alignment_precision": prec_holdout,
        "holdout_generalization_tbr": gen_holdout,
        "simplicity_arc_degree": simp_arc,
        "duplicate_label_stats": dup_stats,
        "holdout_runtime_p50_s": statistics.median(runtimes) if runtimes else None,
        "holdout_runtime_p95_s": (
            sorted(runtimes)[int(0.95 * (len(runtimes) - 1))] if runtimes else None
        ),
        "positive_suite_acceptance_rate": pos_accept_rate,
        "negative_suite_rejection_rate": neg_reject_rate,
        "playout_trace_count": len(simulated_log),
    }


def evaluate_alignment_cost_sensitivity(model, eval_log, high_penalty_activities=("Credit Check", "Technical Review")):
    """Compare standard unit costs against compliance-weighted model-move costs."""
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    model_cost_fn = {}
    for t in net.transitions:
        if t.label is None:
            model_cost_fn[t] = 0
        elif t.label in high_penalty_activities:
            model_cost_fn[t] = 10 * align_utils.STD_MODEL_LOG_MOVE_COST
        else:
            model_cost_fn[t] = align_utils.STD_MODEL_LOG_MOVE_COST

    params = {
        align_algo.Variants.VERSION_STATE_EQUATION_A_STAR.value.Parameters.PARAM_MODEL_COST_FUNCTION: model_cost_fn
    }
    weighted_alignments = align_algo.apply_log(eval_log, net, im, fm, parameters=params)
    return weighted_alignments
```

# 11. Dissenting View

### Rebuttal to This Recommendation
This recommendation underestimates delivery risk and regulatory conservatism. Rejecting both submitted models to build a custom POWL-plus-declarative hybrid delays compliance monitoring while introducing unproven operational complexity. First, the Model C advocate’s core premise (F8) deserves more weight in regulated custom-order environments: when an unseen combination such as `Technical Review -> Credit Check -> Supplier Review -> Expedite Review -> Auto Approval` (B5) appears for the first time, automatically declaring it compliant without governance review can expose the firm to unvetted control interactions—for instance, whether `Auto Approval` should ever bypass `Manager Approval` on expedited external orders. A strict allow-list that escalates novel combinations to human audit is a defensible risk-control posture, not merely overfitting. Second, `model_third` still contains duplicate labels inside its recheck block, still cannot enforce P3 or P6 without upstream ERP changes that may take quarters, and relies on POWL plus custom Python rules that business stakeholders and standard BPMN tools may struggle to maintain. If Compliance needs immediate protection against skipped checks (B3) and premature expediting (B4), deploying an expanded, parameter-governed variant allow-list—or running Model C as an immediate exception-flagging monitor while collecting attribute data—could deliver faster, safer risk reduction than waiting for an untested hybrid architecture.

### What Additional Evidence Would Change My Decision
I would revise my recommendation in favor of a **strict allow-list / phased Model C variant** or a **simplified procedural alternative** if empirical validation produced any of the following evidence:
1. **Regulatory mandate for explicit pre-approval of combinations:** Written Compliance/Audit policy establishing that *every* previously unseen combination (e.g., `Auto Approval` combined with `Expedite Review` or `Correct Dossier`) is legally prohibited until individually approved—meaning Model C’s `0.744` holdout fitness actually reflects genuine policy breaches rather than false positives.
2. **Low real-world compositional diversity:** Chronological holdout analysis showing that $>95\%$ of *legitimate* holdout cases actually fall into a small, stable set (e.g., $\le 12$ variants) and that the remaining holdout mismatches in Model C were genuinely unauthorized workarounds.
3. **Permanent infeasibility of ERP attribute capture:** Confirmation from IT Architecture that `order_source` and `affected_checks` cannot be logged or joined within a realistic horizon, making a Layer 2 data-aware architecture permanently inoperable and shifting preference toward separate external/internal workflow queues or manual-audit gates.
4. **Comprehension failure of `model_third`:** Blinded task-based comprehension testing showing that Compliance, Operations, and IT stakeholders achieve significantly lower decision accuracy with the POWL + declarative hybrid than with a small family of simple, attribute-separated process trees.
