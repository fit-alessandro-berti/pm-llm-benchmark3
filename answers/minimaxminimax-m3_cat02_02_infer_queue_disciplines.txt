- **Two strongest competing explanations:** (1) FIFO with an appointment slot at exactly 08:20; (2) priority-1 with A03 selected because A04 had not yet been "registered" (delayed scan of up to 2 min).
- **Prediction under each:**
  - *FIFO + appointment*: A03 only because the appointment hits at 08:20; A04 would have been selected at 08:30 if no appointment existed.
  - *Delayed-scan priority*: at 08:20 the dispatcher might still "see" only {A01, A02, A03}; among those, A03 (prio 3, e10) is shorter than A02 (e20), so SPT would also pick A03.
- **One observable that would discriminate:** whether the system records a discrete *appointment-fire* event at 08:20. If yes, the appointment explanation wins cleanly. If no, and the dispatcher only sees a queue of waiting jobs at 08:20, then SPT (within the visible set) is also consistent — but the test is weakened because it cannot tell priority from FIFO at this moment.

### Test B — QB, 10:14: B01 (resumed) over {B04, B06}

- **Observed:** B01 resumes.
- **Two strongest competing explanations:** (1) preempted jobs carry implicit "in-service" priority and resume at the next opportunity; (2) the dispatcher is treating the queue as a *single* FIFO over *original arrival order* and B01's original 09:00 arrival puts it ahead of B04 (09:35) and B06 (10:05).
- **Prediction under each:**
  - *Resume preempted*: B01 wins regardless of B06's prio 2, and would win even against a hypothetical B-prio-1 arrival.
  - *Original-arrival FIFO*: B01 still wins because 09:00 < 09:35 < 10:05; would *fail* if a prio-1 job with arrival after 10:14 existed (it would be selected ahead of B01).
- **One observable that would discriminate:** whether a *fresh* prio-1 arrival at, say, 10:20 would preempt resumed B01. Under "resume preempted unless prio-1 waiting" the answer is yes (B01 would be preempted again). Under "original-arrival FIFO with no further preemption" the answer is no (B01 would continue).

### Test C — QC, 11:40: C01 over C07

- **Observed:** C01.
- **Two strongest competing explanations:** (1) SPT modified by a "long-job deferral" rule (jobs with est above a threshold, here ~30, are held until most short jobs are done); (2) hidden C01 flag (parts unavailable, low customer class, manual hold) that made C01 ineligible until 11:40.
- **Prediction under each:**
  - *Long-job deferral*: at 11:40 C01's deadline 13:00 is still 80 min away and only one short job (C07) remains — C01 starts anyway because deferral no longer pays off.
  - *Hidden C01 flag*: C01 was *not* in the eligible set until 11:40; the dispatcher simply had no choice.
- **One observable that would discriminate:** whether the eligibility set at 11:34/11:40 actually contained C01. If a parts-arrival or customer-flag timestamp is logged for C01 between 11:34 and 11:40, the flag explanation wins. If C01 was eligible the whole time, the long-job deferral explanation (or some FIFO-with-starvation threshold) is more likely.

### Test D — QD, 11:48: D07 over D10 on D-X

- **Observed:** D07.
- **Two strongest competing explanations:** (1) FIFO within D-X's eligible set; (2) D10 was *routed* to D-X by the appointment engine even though D-F was the more natural home, and D-F is being *held* for D05.
- **Prediction under each:**
  - *FIFO*: D07 wins on arrival order; D10 will run last regardless of priority.
  - *Reserve-D-F-for-D05*: the dispatcher is intentionally keeping D10 on D-X so that D-F remains uncontested for D05's 12:20 start; D10's later start is a *side-effect* of routing, not of priority.
- **One observable that would discriminate:** whether D10 had a *routing decision* event at 11:25 (its arrival) that explicitly chose D-X. If yes, and that event also contains a "defer D05" reason code, the reservation explanation is correct. If D10 was routed by default skill-matching (electrical  D-F or D-X) and just *happened* to land on D-X, then FIFO is the more parsimonious reading.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked by plausibility across the four queues:

1. **Preemption only at the moment of a higher-priority *arrival*, not for jobs that have been waiting.**
   - Fits QB best: B05 (prio 1) was already waiting when B03 (prio 2) started at 09:48, so B05 does not preempt B03. B02 (prio 1) did preempt B01 because B02 arrived during B01's run.
   - **Effect on diagnosis:** weakens the QB confidence in "preemptive priority" and reframes it as "preemptive on arrival." Does not change the dominant rule, but constrains the policy version.

2. **Preemptive resume with priority-1 override (preempted job resumes at next slot unless a prio-1 job is waiting).**
   - Explains QB at 09:31 and 10:14 (B01 resumes over B03/B06) and at 10:00 (B05 wins over B01).
   - **Effect on diagnosis:** turns "FIFO within class" into "FIFO within class with preempted-job head-of-line status," which is a real hybrid rather than pure priority.

3. **Deadline-missed promotion (a job that has missed its deadline jumps to the head of the queue at the next decision moment), independent of priority.**
   - Explains C05 selection at 10:54 and C07 at 12:16, without invoking priority at all.
   - **Effect on diagnosis:** makes the QC rule "SPT with missed-deadline promotion" rather than "SPT with priority boost." This lowers QC's reliance on the priority column.

4. **Long-job deferral or hidden eligibility flag in QC.**
   - The C01 episode at 11:40 cannot be reconciled with SPT, priority, or EDF. The note explicitly says C01 was held back "among initial backlog," which is consistent with either a deferral threshold or a flag (parts unavailable until 11:40, manual hold, customer class).
   - **Effect on diagnosis:** without this, QC's rule has a true counterexample; with it, QC is internally consistent. **Highest single impact on identifiability.**

5. **Delayed arrival scans (up to 2 min).**
   - Could shift the apparent arrival order for jobs arriving within a 2-minute window. In this data, no two arrivals are close enough that a 2-min scan delay would reorder the queue (smallest gap is A02A03 at 4 min, B04B05 at 11 min, etc.).
   - **Effect on diagnosis:** marginal in this dataset; matters more for QA where A02 (08:04) and A04 (08:13) are 9 min apart — even a 2-min scan delay cannot reverse them.

6. **Calibration and meeting breaks.**
   - A1 unavailable 08:52–08:58; D-F unavailable 12:00–12:20. These are *not* rules but operational facts. They explain why A04 starts at 08:58 (not earlier) and why D05 starts at 12:20 (not 11:36 — that would have ended at 12:06, inside the meeting).
   - **Effect on diagnosis:** the calibration break in QA *coincides* with what looks like a priority jump (A04 starts immediately after). Without the break, A04 would have started at 08:52 — i.e., the calibration break, not priority, is what made A04 look "first" after A02.

7. **Strategic or noisy estimates.**
   - est_min is "the estimate visible to dispatch at arrival time, not the realized duration." All realized service times here match est exactly to the minute, which is suspiciously clean. Realistic noise would slightly disturb SPT predictions in QC and could be the difference between C01 and C07 at 11:40.
   - **Effect on diagnosis:** if C07's true estimate was actually 22 min rather than 18, then C07 *could not* meet its 12:00 deadline even from 11:40 (only 20 min available), and the dispatcher might have used a "skip doomed jobs" rule. This would explain C01's selection without invoking any hidden flag.

8. **Appointment tolerance vs hard appointment.**
   - A06's appointment 09:15 started at 09:20; B07's appointment 10:30 started at 10:56. So appointments are *targets*, not hard slots. D06's appointment 11:30 was honored exactly.
   - **Effect on diagnosis:** strengthens the "FIFO with appointment preemption" reading for QA — the appointment is honored only if the resource would otherwise be free at or near that time. Does not change the diagnosis.

# 6. Additional event data needed

Prioritized by the ambiguity each item would resolve:

1. **Dispatch-decision timestamp + eligibility set snapshot.** The single highest-value addition across all queues. A decision log saying "at 08:32 the dispatcher chose A02 from {A02, A04, A05}" would convert every QA decision from "consistent with" to "proven by." *Resolves §3 ambiguities for QA, QC, QD in one stroke.*

2. **Preemption reason codes (categorical).** The QB "dispatcher reassignment" reason is opaque. A categorical field with values {urgent_arrival, higher_priority_waiting, resource_reassignment, manual_override, parts_unavailable} would resolve the QB hybrid rule. *Resolves §3 for QB.*

3. **Appointment acceptance window (per queue or per resource).** A06 started 5 min late, B07 26 min late, D06 on time. Without the tolerance we cannot tell whether appointments are hard slots or soft preferences, and therefore cannot rank "appointment" vs "FIFO" in QA.

4. **Routing-decision event for multi-skill jobs.** A record of "D06 routed to D-X because D-F busy until 11:36" vs "…because D-F reserved for D05" would discriminate the two Test D explanations and confirm/refute skill-based routing as the *primary* mechanism in QD.

5. **Updated / revised estimate remaining-time.** Especially for QC at 11:40, knowing whether C07's true estimate was still 18 min (deadline miss risk) or had been revised to 22+ min (definitely doomed) would explain the C01 anomaly in one of two clean ways.

6. **Hidden C01 flag (parts arrival, customer class, manual hold).** A binary flag at 11:34 saying "C01 ineligible" or "C01 low priority" would close the QC anomaly.

7. **Preemption policy version (per queue).** A version field on every preemption event (e.g., `policy=preempt_on_arrival`, `policy=resume_unless_p1`) would let us test the QB hybrid directly.

8. **Queue-entry timestamp distinct from arrival-scan timestamp.** Lets us separate "arrived at the loading dock" from "entered the dispatch queue." Important for FIFO tests in all queues.

9. **Eligibility flag per (job, resource) at each decision moment.** Specifically: was A04 *actually* in A1's eligibility set at 08:32, or had the dispatcher temporarily excluded it (e.g., calibration pending)? If excluded, then the 08:32 selection is no longer a priority test.

10. **Service-segment "interruption reason" as a controlled vocabulary** (rather than free text). Removes the data-quality ambiguity around "dispatcher reassignment."

11. **Resource-state timeline.** A continuous signal of `available | busy | break | meeting | calibration` for each resource. This would confirm whether A1's 08:52–08:58 calibration *was* the cause of the 08:3208:58 gap, or whether there was idle time for other reasons.

12. **Realized-vs-estimated service duration comparison.** All est_min values are realized exactly here; that is unusual. Capturing both fields over a longer window would let us estimate estimate noise and refine SPT interpretations in QC.

# 7. Final synthesis

- **Most identifiable queue: QD.** Skill constraints dominate the routing decisions, and within each resource the order is plain FIFO. The two anomalies (D10 last on D-X, D06 routed to D-X rather than D-F) are both explainable by FIFO and by the 11:30 appointment, with no need to invoke priority or preemption. A new analyst could re-derive the rule from this queue alone with high confidence.

- **Least identifiable queue: QC.** The dominant pattern (four of seven decisions) is SPT, but C01-over-C07 at 11:40 is a clean counterexample to SPT, priority, and EDF. The note on C01 confirms intent but not mechanism. QC can only be diagnosed as "SPT with at least one undocumented exception," and we cannot say from these data whether the exception is a deadline rule we have not yet isolated, a long-job threshold, a hidden eligibility flag, or estimate noise.

- **Most dangerous policy to infer from these observations alone: priority.**
  - Priority cleanly explains only **one** queue (QB, and even there only the preemption decisions — not the post-preemption sequencing).
  - Priority is *contradicted* by at least one episode in each of the other three queues: A04/A08 in QA, C01/C05/C07 in QC, D10 in QD.
  - A manager who looked at A04 (prio 1, est 7) and assumed "priority must be the rule, so why is A02 going first?" would either change the dispatcher's behaviour or write a fictitious post-hoc justification. Either way the diagnosis would be wrong.
  - By contrast, FIFO is contradicted only in QB (where preemption is the dominant signal); SPT is contradicted in QA, QB, and QD. Priority is the only rule that is contradicted in *every* other queue while being the rule in only one.

- **Cross-queue pattern.** The four queues together suggest that this repair center is **not** running a single uniform dispatch policy. Each queue has its own hybrid, and the hybrid is shaped by the binding constraint of that queue: appointment in QA, preemption + resume in QB, deadlines in QC, skills + FIFO in QD. Inferring any single documented mechanism for "the whole center" would be unsafe — and that, more than any individual queue, is the headline finding.
