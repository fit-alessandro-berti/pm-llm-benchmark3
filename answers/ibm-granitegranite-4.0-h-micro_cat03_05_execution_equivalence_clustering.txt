## 1. Equivalence Design Space

### 1.1 Activity-sequence equivalence

**Definition:** Executions are equivalent if they produce the same ordered sequence of activity labels, ignoring local object identifiers and external object references.

**Invariants preserved:**
- The order of activities remains unchanged.
- The sequence of events is preserved.

**Information discarded:**
- Object identifiers, external object references, and O2O qualifiers are ignored.
- The semantics of the operations (e.g., whether an item was replaced or returned) are not considered.

**Properties:** Reflexive, symmetric, and transitive. Handles concurrency by considering only the ordered sequence of events.

### 1.2 Typed structural equivalence under identifier renaming

**Definition:** Executions are equivalent if they have the same activity structure and types, allowing for renaming of local identifiers. External objects and O2O qualifiers are treated as opaque.

**Invariants preserved:**
- Object types and multiplicities remain unchanged.
- The logical structure of the execution (i.e., which objects are involved in which activities) is preserved.
- The sequence of activities is preserved.

**Information discarded:**
- Local identifiers are renamed to a canonical form.
- External object references are ignored.

**Properties:** Reflexive, symmetric, and transitive. Handles identifier renaming by mapping all identifiers to a canonical form.

### 1.3 Business-semantic equivalence

**Definition:** Executions are equivalent if they represent the same business semantics, including multiplicities, shared external objects, O2O qualifiers, and lifecycle motifs (e.g., replacements, returns). Identifier renaming is allowed, but the business meaning of the operations is preserved.

**Invariants preserved:**
- The business semantics, such as the creation of orders, items, packages, invoices, and payments, are preserved.
- Multiplicities and relationships between objects are preserved.
- External object references and O2O qualifiers are preserved.

**Information discarded:**
- Local identifiers are renamed to a canonical form.
- Detailed activity labels are abstracted to business semantics.

**Properties:** Reflexive, symmetric, and transitive. Handles concurrency by considering the logical flow of business operations rather than raw event sequences.

### 1.4 Optional fourth equivalence: Lifecycle motif equivalence

**Definition:** Executions are equivalent if they follow the same business lifecycle motifs, such as order creation, item addition, packaging, dispatch, and payment. Identifier renaming and certain internal logistics details are ignored.

**Invariants preserved:**
- The lifecycle motifs (e.g., creation, addition, replacement, return) are preserved.
- The overall business flow is maintained.

**Information discarded:**
- Detailed activity labels and local identifiers are abstracted.
- Internal logistics details that do not affect the business lifecycle are ignored.

**Properties:** Reflexive, symmetric, and transitive. Handles lifecycle motifs by focusing on the high-level business operations.

## 2. Canonical Signatures

### 2.1 Preferred equivalence relation: Business-semantic equivalence

**Canonical signature scheme:**
1. **Canonicalize identifiers:** Map all local identifiers to a canonical form (e.g., alphabetical order).
2. **Aggregate external objects:** Treat all objects with `EXT` prefixes as a single entity.
3. **Aggregate O2O qualifiers:** Group all O2O qualifiers into a single attribute.
4. **Preserve business semantics:** Include object types, multiplicities, and lifecycle motifs.
5. **Preserve event order:** Maintain the sequence of activities, but allow for the same-time occurrence of events.

**Python-like pseudocode:**

```python
def canonicalize_execution(execution):
    canonical_execution = []
    identifier_map = {}
    external_objects = set()

    for event in execution:
        obj_id = event['object_ids'][0]
        if obj_id.startswith('EXT'):
            external_objects.add(obj_id)
            continue

        if obj_id not in identifier_map:
            identifier_map[obj_id] = f'obj_{len(identifier_map)}'

        canonical_event = {
            'activity': event['activity'],
            'canonical_id': identifier_map[obj_id],
            'multiplicity': event['multiplicity'] if 'multiplicity' in event else None,
            'external': obj_id in external_objects,
            'o2o_qualifier': event.get('o2o_qualifier', None)
        }
        canonical_execution.append(canonical_event)

    return canonical_execution
```

## 4. Cluster Interpretations

### Cluster A: Activity-sequence equivalence

**Signature:** Focuses solely on the ordered sequence of activities, ignoring identifiers and external objects.

**Business behavior:** Describes the basic flow of order creation, item addition, packaging, dispatch, and payment. This cluster is useful for understanding the fundamental process flow.

**Operational risks/opportunities:** Minimal risk as it captures the core process. Opportunities include identifying inefficiencies in the sequence of activities.

**Stability under repair:** Stable if only identifier renaming or external object references are changed.

### Cluster B: Typed structural equivalence under identifier renaming

**Signature:** Preserves object types, multiplicities, and the logical structure of the execution, allowing for renaming of local identifiers.

**Business behavior:** Captures the essential business operations while abstracting away from specific identifiers. This is useful for comparing executions that may use different identifiers but represent the same business process.

**Operational risks/opportunities:** Risks include misinterpretation if identifier renaming is not handled consistently. Opportunities include detecting variations in business logic due to identifier changes.

**Stability under repair:** Stable if only identifier renaming is performed.

### Cluster C: Business-semantic equivalence

**Signature:** Preserves business semantics, including object types, multiplicities, external objects, and O2O qualifiers.

**Business behavior:** Provides a deep understanding of the business process, including complex scenarios like cross-order invoicing, shared logistics, and lifecycle motifs like replacements and returns.

**Operational risks/opportunities:** Risks include overlooking subtle differences in business semantics that could affect compliance or financial reporting. Opportunities include identifying opportunities for process optimization based on business logic.

**Stability under repair:** Stable if only identifier renaming is performed, but may change if external objects or O2O qualifiers are altered.

### Cluster D: Lifecycle motif equivalence

**Signature:** Focuses on the high-level business lifecycle motifs, ignoring detailed activities and identifiers.

**Business behavior:** Highlights the major business processes such as order creation, item addition, packaging, dispatch, and payment, along with lifecycle events like replacements and returns.

**Operational risks/opportunities:** Risks include overlooking detailed operational nuances. Opportunities include identifying process inefficiencies or opportunities in the lifecycle flow.

**Stability under repair:** Stable if only identifier renaming is performed, but may change if lifecycle motifs are altered.

## 6. Relation to `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel`

The `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel` function in the PM4Py library is designed to cluster execution logs based on their structural similarity. It preserves the following information:

- Object types and multiplicities.
- Event order (or partial order).
- E2O incidence (relationships between external objects and other objects).
- O2O qualifiers.
- Repeated activities.

**Why raw object identifiers can make exact clusters too fine:** Raw identifiers can lead to overly granular clusters because they reflect the specific names of objects, which may not be meaningful for high-level business analysis. By canonicalizing identifiers, we abstract away these details and focus on the underlying business semantics.

**Post-processing returned clusters:** After obtaining clusters from `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel`, we can apply additional canonicalization steps to ensure consistency, such as renaming identifiers to a standard form and grouping external objects together.

**When exact equivalence is preferable to a coarser business clustering:** Exact equivalence is preferable when precise identification of process variations is crucial, such as in compliance monitoring or root-cause analysis. Coarser clustering may mask important nuances that need to be addressed.

**Impact of `max_objs` or large connected executions:** A small `max_objs` parameter can lead to more precise clustering by limiting the number of objects considered, while a larger value may result in broader clusters. In practical use, `max_objs` should be set based on the desired granularity of analysis and the size of the execution logs.

## 7. Robustness Analysis

### Ignoring all O2O qualifiers
**Effect:** Reduces the granularity of the clusters, focusing solely on the core business operations. This simplification may overlook important logistical considerations but enhances the focus on business semantics.

### Ignoring external objects
**Effect:** Further reduces the granularity by treating all external objects as a single entity. This can simplify the analysis but may obscure important external dependencies.

### Collapsing repeated same-label events
**Effect:** Reduces the number of events considered, potentially simplifying the analysis but losing information about the frequency and timing of operations.

### Treating payment multiplicity as an amount attribute
**Effect:** Changes the representation of payments from discrete events to a single monetary amount. This can simplify the analysis by focusing on financial values rather than discrete payment events, but may obscure the timing and sequence of payments.

## 8. Limitations and Recommendation

### Recommendation for compliance monitoring
**Equivalence level:** Business-semantic equivalence

**Reason:** This level captures the essential business processes and lifecycle motifs, which are crucial for ensuring compliance with regulations and standards. It provides a comprehensive view of the execution without being overly detailed.

### Recommendation for operational segmentation
**Equivalence level:** Typed structural equivalence

**Reason:** This level balances detail and abstraction, allowing for meaningful segmentation based on the logical structure of the operations. It is useful for identifying operational patterns and inefficiencies while abstracting away from specific identifiers.

### Recommendation for root-cause analysis
**Equivalence level:** Lifecycle motif equivalence

**Reason:** This level provides a high-level view of the business flow, making it easier to identify root causes of issues by focusing on major lifecycle events rather than granular activities.

### Recommendation for descriptive reporting
**Equivalence level:** Activity-sequence equivalence

**Reason:** This level offers the most detailed representation of the execution sequence, which is useful for descriptive reporting and understanding the exact flow of operations. However, it may be too granular for high-level summaries.

### Limitations and overall recommendation
While the proposed equivalence levels offer valuable insights, they each have limitations. Business-semantic equivalence is best for compliance and deep analysis, typed structural equivalence is ideal for operational segmentation, lifecycle motif equivalence aids in root-cause analysis, and activity-sequence equivalence is suitable for descriptive reporting. No single equivalence notion is universally best; the choice depends on the specific analytical goals and the level of detail required.
