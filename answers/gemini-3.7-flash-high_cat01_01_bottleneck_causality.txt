## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict:** `mainly confounded`
**Confidence level:** 85/100

**Strongest evidence for the verdict:**
The apparent +6.71-hour aggregate cycle-time deficit of resource group Orion (mean 21.96h vs Nova's 15.24h) represents a textbook case of Simpson's Paradox caused by confounding by indication. Routing rules assign Orion a severely disadvantaged case mix: 42.9% High complexity (vs 12.5% for Nova), 64.3% compliance checks (vs 25.0%), and 92.9% night shift (vs 25.0%). Once stratified by case complexity, Orion's cycle-time performance completely reverses: Orion finishes High-complexity cases 3.43 hours faster than Nova (31.57h vs 35.00h), Medium cases 2.21 hours faster (17.17h vs 19.38h), and Low cases 2.14 hours faster (7.50h vs 9.64h). Standardizing against a common population complexity distribution demonstrates that Orion is 2.51 hours faster overall (17.14h vs 19.65h).

**Strongest evidence against the verdict:**
Unconfoundedness and positivity cannot be fully established from the observational data. Severe empirical overlap deficits exist: Orion handles only one Day case, while Nova handles only two High-complexity and four Night cases. Furthermore, Orion holds more certified reviewers; if routing selects unobserved high-liability sub-cases to Orion, or if night-shift arrival surges induce unmeasured queueing externalities, residual confounding remains. Finally, conditioning strictly on completed cases risks selection/attrition bias.

**Why the proposed 50% reassignment will backfire:**
Management's proposal to reassign 50% of Orion's workload to Nova will backfire. Because Nova displays longer review service times and longer pre-review queue delays across every complexity stratum, routing complex, night-arriving claims to Nova will overload uncertified staff, trigger increased information requests and rework, inflate pre-review queue backlogs, and ultimately lengthen enterprise cycle times rather than improving throughput.

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Phase 1 | Upstream Compliance Queue Decoupling and Parallel Triage | Decouple compliance checks from review queueing by initiating compliance screening in parallel with classification; add dedicated compliance support. | All incoming claims where `compliance_required = Yes` | 100% compliance audit check; no changes to reviewer assignment rules. | Pre-review wait time (`wait_before_review_h`), compliance stage throughput time, queue length at Review handoff. | Directly tests whether pre-review wait is an addressable upstream process bottleneck without risking reviewer performance confounding. | Roll back if compliance audit failure rate > 0.0% or if pre-review queue wait increases by > 1.0 hour. |
| Phase 2 | Micro-Randomized Routing Pilot on Overlap Strata (Medium Complexity) | Randomly assign 20% of Medium-complexity claims equally between Orion and Nova across both Day and Night shifts. | Incoming Medium-complexity claims at `Classify` stage | High-complexity claims remain 100% with certified Orion reviewers; individual reviewer caseload limits enforced. | True unconfounded ATE on `review_service_h`, `rework_count`, within-shift `cycle_time_h`, and DFG transition latencies. | Eliminates routing confounding and achieves empirical overlap/positivity to identify the true causal effect of resource group on medium claims. | Halt immediately if Nova rework rate on Medium claims exceeds 25% or if Nova cycle time exceeds Orion by > 3.0 hours. |
| Phase 3 | Reviewer Skill Certification Transfer and Dynamic Load Balancing | Certify selected Nova reviewers for High complexity; transition from static group assignment to dynamic queue-depth load balancing across shifts. | Claim routing engine dispatcher and reviewer scheduling rules | Mandatory peer review / shadowing on first 20 High-complexity claims per newly certified Nova reviewer. | Stratified review service duration, rework loop frequency (`Review -> Request Info -> Review`), 30-day appeal rate. | Identifies the causal mechanism of reviewer certification (skill portability) and tests the system-wide effect of dynamic load balancing. | Revert to static routing if 30-day appeal rate exceeds 7.5% or reviewer overtime/burnout markers exceed operational thresholds. |

### Final Recommendation
We strongly advise the operations committee to reject the proposed 50% blanket workload shift from Orion to Nova. The apparent aggregate delay in Orion is an artifact of severe confounding by indication (Simpson's Paradox): Orion handles 85.7% medium/high-complexity claims, 92.9% night shifts, and 64.3% compliance checks. Within every complexity stratum, Orion is actually 2.1 to 3.4 hours faster than Nova, and standardized performance shows Orion is 2.51 hours faster overall. Blindly transferring complex night cases to Nova—which lacks certified reviewers and works primarily day shifts—will overwhelm staff, trigger rework, and lengthen cycle times.

Instead, implement our three-phase learning plan. First, target the primary physical bottleneck by streamlining compliance queueing, which accounts for over 55% of total elapsed time. Second, run a micro-randomized routing pilot strictly on Medium-complexity claims to obtain unconfounded causal benchmarks. Third, expand reviewer certifications across groups and transition from static group assignment to dynamic, queue-aware load balancing. This structured approach preserves decision quality, eliminates structural waiting waste, and optimizes operational throughput based on rigorous causal evidence.

    grp_log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(grp_cases['trace'].tolist(), sep='>')
    dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(grp_log)
    
    print(f"\n--- Resource Group: {grp} (Total Cases = {len(grp_cases)}) ---")
    print(f"Start Activities: {dict(start_acts)}")
    print(f"End Activities  : {dict(end_acts)}")
    print("Directly-Follows Edges (Activity -> Activity : Count):")
    for (source, target), count in sorted(dfg.items()):
        print(f"  {source:18s} -> {target:18s} : {count:2d}")

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Unobserved within-stratum claim liability and case risk:**
   *Uncertainty:* The discrete 3-tier `complexity` variable may mask significant sub-stratum severity; routing algorithms may assign the highest-liability or litigation-prone claims within 'High' complexity exclusively to certified Orion reviewers.
   *Mitigating data field / design change:* Collect continuous pre-assignment claim risk covariates, specifically `claimed_loss_amount_usd` and `claim_severity_index`, to enable fine-grained regression adjustment or propensity matching.

2. **Positivity violation and extreme shift-group collinearity:**
   *Uncertainty:* Orion handles 92.9% night work while Nova handles 75.0% day work, preventing the clean empirical separation of diurnal queueing dynamics, staffing levels, and arrival surges from intrinsic reviewer productivity.
   *Mitigating data field / design change:* Implement a balanced fractional shift-rotation schedule where certified Orion reviewers work daytime shifts and Nova reviewers work nighttime shifts under comparable arrival loads.

3. **Sample truncation and survivorship bias from completed-case sampling:**
   *Uncertainty:* The dataset contains only completed cases (`case_completed = 1`), potentially concealing stalled, abandoned, or long-open cases that could distort Nova's or Orion's apparent speed.
   *Mitigating data field / design change:* Ingest complete operational lifecycle records including open and abandoned claims (`case_status` = 'open' / 'abandoned' / 'completed' and `in_flight_duration_h`), analyzing throughput via right-censored survival models.

4. **Conflation of compliance queue wait and compliance service duration:**
   *Uncertainty:* `wait_before_review_h` bundles post-classification queueing, compliance queue waiting, and active compliance check execution, obscuring whether delays stem from compliance backlog or review queueing.
   *Mitigating data field / design change:* Capture discrete lifecycle event timestamps for compliance activities (`compliance_start_timestamp` and `compliance_complete_timestamp`) in the event log.

5. **Quality measurement noise and long-term settlement validity:**
   *Uncertainty:* The binary 30-day appeal indicator (`outcome_30d`) has only two observed events (n=1 in Orion, n=1 in Nova), making it impossible to detect whether faster review speed causes downstream settlement leakage, underpayment, or legal disputes.
   *Mitigating data field / design change:* Implement comprehensive post-process quality audit scoring (`audit_error_score` from 0 to 100) and track `post_settlement_reopened_180d` over an extended six-month evaluation window.
