## 2. Dependency and Prerequisite Map

Process flow and performance depend on key operational, data, regulatory, and technical relationships:

```
[A2 Classification] > [A5 Doc Portal] > [A6 Normalization] > [A8 Pack Assembly]
                                                                         
                                                                         
  [A3 KYC Pre-Class] > [A4 Mismatch Assist] > [Credit Analysis] > [Committee Decision]
                                                                          
                                                                  
                                                                          
                                                       [A9 Contract Generation]
                                                              
                                                              
                                                    [A10 E-Signature / Gate]
```

### Upstream Data Quality Dependencies
*   **Classification (A2) $\rightarrow$ Downstream Routing:** Incorrect initial classification misroutes cases between High-exposure SME and Micro/SME paths, applying incorrect credit risk, collateral requirements, and committee review rules.
*   **Normalization (A6) $\rightarrow$ Credit Analysis & Committee Packaging (A8):** Financial-statement extraction (A6) feeds directly into credit risk models and Committee Pack Assembly (A8). If A6 generates unverified data, it risks propagating errors into Credit Analysis (110 min service time) and Committee Decisions.

### Legal and Compliance Control Prerequisites
*   **KYC Clearance before Customer Signing / Contract Prep:** Legal rules strictly prohibit loan execution prior to full KYC clearance. DFG analysis shows provisional signing in 6.5% of cases (with 1.1% requiring severe remediation). Systems executing Contract Preparation (A9) and E-Signatures (A10) must be programmatically gated by verified KYC status (clearing both A3 and A4).
*   **Four-Eyes Principle Enforcement:** In High-exposure SME cases, the system must enforce strict segregation of duties: the individual performing Credit Analysis cannot assemble or approve the Committee Package (violating policy in 5.8% of cases during month-end surges).
*   **Term Alignment:** Contract Generation (A9) must automatically reconcile contract terms against Committee Decision outputs to eliminate the 8.5% overall contract rework rate (which surges to 14% for Credit-line renewals).

### Human Escalation Capacity and Resource Constraints
*   **Compliance Specialist Constraint (3 FTE):** *Resolve entity mismatch* (A4) carries a 31.0h median wait time and a 38% exception/rework rate. Pushing higher upstream volumes from *Register application* (A1) or *KYC check* (A3) without implementing A4 will overwhelm these 3 FTEs, increasing overall process cycle time despite local intake speedup.
*   **Credit Analyst Constraint (9 FTE):** Credit analysts spend 75 minutes per case on *Normalize financial statements* (5,200 cases/year = 6,500 hours) before conducting actual Credit Analysis (110 min/case). Accelerating application intake (A1) without implementing A6 creates a major bottleneck at Credit Analysis.

### External Valuation Dependency
*   *Collateral valuation* introduces a 58.0h external waiting period affecting 71% of Equipment Finance cases. Because this external dependency cannot be eliminated internally, *Committee packaging* (A8) must run concurrently on internal tasks (KYC clearance and financial statement normalization) while tracking external valuation status in parallel.

### Core-Banking Integration Constraints
*   IT limits core-banking integration to **two deep integration modules in the first six months**. Core integrations involve direct write/read access to primary ledgers and legal customer records (e.g., A1 Auto-registration, A9 Contract Generation, A11 Activation Bot). Light-touch/wrapper integrations (e.g., A5 Customer Portal, A6 Financial Statement Extraction, A4 Entity Mismatch Assistant) can be deployed without consuming core integration quota.

## 3. Portfolio Alternatives

Three distinct 4-module portfolios are evaluated based on differing strategic priorities:

### Portfolio 1: Quick-Win / Volume Portfolio
*   **Modules:** A1 (Auto-registration), A5 (Missing-Doc Portal), A9 (Contract Generation), A10 (E-Signature Automation).
*   **Optimizes:** High-frequency, repetitive manual tasks; immediate direct labor savings in back-office registration and contract drafting.
*   **Sacrifices:** Structural bottlenecks (Entity Mismatch A4, Financial Normalization A6); compliance control enforcement at intake; credit analyst capacity.
*   **Process Dynamics:** Accelerates front-end ingestion, causing applications to stack up at entity mismatch (31.0h wait) and credit analyst review (20.0h wait). Fails to address the 5.8% four-eyes breach or upstream misclassifications.

### Portfolio 2: Bottleneck & Synchronization Portfolio (Process-Optimized)
*   **Modules:** A2 (Product/Exposure Classification), A4 (Entity-Mismatch Assistant), A5 (Missing-Doc Portal & Nudging), A6 (Financial Statement Normalization).
*   **Optimizes:** Critical path end-to-end cycle time, resolution of major wait times (31.0h, 18.0h, 20.0h), analyst capacity protection, reduction of document loops (52%).
*   **Sacrifices:** High-frequency back-office clerical automation (A1 registration labor remains manual), end-of-pipe automated loan activation (A11).
*   **Process Dynamics:** Smooths application flow across the system, alleviates pressure on scarce resources (3 FTE Compliance, 9 FTE Analysts), and prevents misrouting early in the process.

### Portfolio 3: Compliance-First Portfolio
*   **Modules:** A2 (Product/Exposure Classification), A3 (KYC Pre-classifier), A9 (Contract Generation with Control Checks), A12 (Control Remediation Workbench).
*   **Optimizes:** Regulatory control, elimination of contract terms rework (14% in renewals), prevention of provisional signing breaches, audit trails.
*   **Sacrifices:** End-to-end cycle time reduction; relief for high waiting times in doc review (18.0h) and financial statement normalization (20.0h).
*   **Process Dynamics:** Provides strong audit trails and control guarantees, but leaves core operational queues (credit analysis, document loops) largely unaddressed.

## 5. Risks and Compensating Controls

Deploying automation across loan origination introduces specific operational, legal, and systemic risks:

```
Risk Architecture & Controls:

  Automated Input > [Confidence Threshold Check] > High Confidence > Direct Processing
                               
                                Low Confidence > [Audit Logged] > Human Escalation
                                                                              
                                                                              
                                                                 [Four-Eyes Rule Check]
```

### Auditability & Lineage Tracking
*   **Risk:** Automated decision engines (e.g., classification, extraction, rule checks) operating as "black boxes," preventing internal/external auditors from verifying decision logic.
*   **Compensating Control:** Implement immutable audit logging for every automated action. Log input payload, model/rule version, confidence score, recommendation output, human override flag, and final decision timestamp.

### False Positives / False Negatives in Compliance Screening
*   **Risk:** Unchecked automation in KYC (A3) or Entity Mismatch (A4) generating false negatives (missing sanctioned entities) or false positives (overwhelming compliance specialists).
*   **Compensating Control:** Establish deterministic, tiered risk thresholds. High-confidence entity matches automatically resolve routine technical typos; low-confidence hits mandatory route to human Compliance Specialists. False negatives are mitigated by running deterministic sanction matching rules in parallel with fuzzy logic AI models.

### Model Drift & Outdated Regulatory Rules
*   **Risk:** Regulatory shifts, tax law changes, or economic updates render contract rule libraries (A9) or extraction models (A6) inaccurate over time.
*   **Compensating Control:** Institute quarterly rule-library reviews owned jointly by Legal Ops and Compliance. Implement continuous model validation comparing extracted financial statement values against audited human samples (5% random sampling).

### Exception Handling & Shifted Resource Demand
*   **Risk:** Upstream automation (A1/A5) increasing downstream processing speeds, leading to severe resource bottlenecks at Credit Analysis and Compliance.
*   **Compensating Control:** Implement dynamic workload management and queue throttling. Direct automated notifications to RMs to ensure applications meet minimum data quality standards before entering analyst queues.

### Four-Eyes Principle Compliance
*   **Risk:** Automated workflow systems assigning Committee Pack Assembly (A8) or decision prep to the same analyst who completed Credit Analysis, breaching policy in High-exposure SME cases (current breach rate: 5.8%).
*   **Compensating Control:** Programmatic segregation-of-duties enforcement built into workflow tools. The system validates user IDs at state transitions and blocks approval actions if `Analyst_ID (Credit Analysis) == Analyst_ID (Committee Pack)`.

## 6. Recommended Phased Rollout

A 12-month, four-phase rollout plan targeting bottleneck elimination and control enforcement while staying within technical constraints (maximum 2 core-banking deep integrations in H1):

```
Rollout Timeline (12 Months):

Q1: [Phase 1: Foundation & Controls]
    - A2: Product & Exposure Classification
    - A5: Missing-Document Portal

Q2: [Phase 2: Bottleneck Resolution]  (Core Integration #1: A9)
    - A6: Financial Statement Extraction
    - A4: Entity-Mismatch Assistant
    - A9: Contract Generation with Rules

Q3: [Phase 3: Cycle-Time Optimization]  (Core Integration #2: A1)
    - A1: Auto-Registration & Dup Detection
    - A10: E-Signature Integration & Reminders

Q4: [Phase 4: Optimization & Refinement]
    - A8: Committee Pack Auto-Assembly
    - A3/A11: Targeted Refinements / Scaling
```

### Phase 1: Foundation & Early Control (Months 1–3)
*   **Modules Deployed:**
    *   **A2: Product/Exposure Classification Recommender** (Lightweight API)
    *   **A5: Missing-Document Portal & Customer Nudging** (Web portal/SMS integration)
*   **Core Integrations Used:** 0
*   **Compliance Control Target:** Enforces routing rules early, preventing high-exposure applications from bypassing committee workflows.
*   **Gate Criteria:** Classification accuracy > 98% against historical manual labels; missing-document response loop time reduced by > 30%.

### Phase 2: Core Bottleneck Unblocking (Months 4–6)
*   **Modules Deployed:**
    *   **A6: Financial Statement Extraction & Normalization** (OCR/AI service)
    *   **A4: Entity-Mismatch Resolution Assistant** (Compliance UI wrapper)
    *   **A9: Contract Generation with Control Checks** (Core Integration #1)
*   **Core Integrations Used:** 1 (A9 Contract Generation writing to Core Banking document/loan repository).
*   **Compliance Control Target:** Hard gating to eliminate contract rework (14% in renewals) and prevent contract generation without confirmed KYC clearance.
*   **Gate Criteria:** Financial statement normalization field extraction accuracy > 92%; entity mismatch resolution queue time reduced from 31.0h to < 4.0h; zero policy breaches on contract generation rules.

### Phase 3: Intake Acceleration & E-Signatures (Months 7–9)
*   **Modules Deployed:**
    *   **A1: Auto-registration & Duplicate Detection** (Core Integration #2)
    *   **A10: E-signature Adoption & Reminder Automation** (E-sign integration)
*   **Core Integrations Used:** 1 (A1 Registration integration with Core Banking customer records). Cumulative Total: 2 (Limit reached).
*   **Compliance Control Target:** Mandatory KYC clearance lock prior to executing e-signature release, preventing provisional signing breaches (6.5%).
*   **Gate Criteria:** Registration processing time reduced from 8 min to < 30 seconds; 0% e-signed contracts executed without verified KYC clearance.

### Phase 4: Assembly & Process Refinement (Months 10–12)
*   **Modules Deployed:**
    *   **A8: Committee Pack Auto-Assembly** (Workflow reconciliation tool)
    *   Targeted refinements to A3 (KYC Pre-classification) or A11 (Loan Activation Bot), subject to operational readiness.
*   **Core Integrations Used:** 0 additional core integrations (leveraging existing APIs).
*   **Compliance Control Target:** Programmatic enforcement of segregation of duties (Four-Eyes principle) during Committee Packaging.
*   **Gate Criteria:** Committee pack assembly waiting time reduced from 26.0h to < 2.0h; 0% Four-Eyes policy breaches across all High-exposure SME applications.

### Key Performance Tracking Metrics across Rollout
*   End-to-end median cycle time (Target: >45% reduction from baseline).
*   Entity Mismatch waiting time (Target: <4 hours).
*   Credit Analyst hours spent on manual statement normalization (Target: >70% reduction).
*   Control breach rate (provisional signing, four-eyes, term mismatches) (Target: 0%).

## 7. Final Recommendation

### Recommended Portfolio Selection
The bank should adopt **Portfolio 2 (Bottleneck & Synchronization Portfolio)**, expanded in the rollout plan to include **A9 (Contract Generation with Control Checks)**.

This portfolio selection directly addresses the bank's operational bottlenecks and compliance requirements:
1.  **A2 (Classification Recommender):** Prevents misrouting and ensures correct control enforcement upfront.
2.  **A4 (Entity-Mismatch Assistant):** Targets the process's single largest non-external waiting time (31.0 hours) and relieves the 3 FTE Compliance Specialist bottleneck.
3.  **A5 (Missing-Doc Portal & Nudging):** Eliminates the 52% repeated missing-document loop, cutting the 18.0-hour document review wait time.
4.  **A6 (Financial Statement Normalization):** Frees ~6,500 hours/year of manual processing time for 9 FTE Credit Analysts, eliminating the 20.0-hour normalization queue before credit analysis.
5.  **A9 (Contract Generation with Control Checks):** Fulfills the compliance mandate by programmatically enforcing contract terms alignment and mitigating contract rework loops (up to 14%).

### Strategic Trade-Off Argument: Why NOT Automate the Highest-Frequency Activity (A1) First

Senior management's initial instinct to automate *Register application* (A1) first based on frequency (11,880 cases) represents a classic localized optimization trap:

```
Automating High-Frequency Intake First (The Trap):

[Automated Ingestion (A1)] > Accelerates Flow > [Entity Mismatch (31h Wait)] > Massive Queue Backup
                                                 > [Analyst Normalization (20h)] > Overwhelms 9 FTE Analysts
                                                 > [Unchecked Controls]       > High Risk Exposure
```

1.  **Local Optimization vs. Global Flow:** *Register application* has a service time of only 8 minutes and a median wait time of 1.5 hours. Furthermore, Back Office utilization drops below 65% after 15:00, indicating that registration capacity is not a constraining bottleneck. Automating A1 first yields minimal end-to-end cycle time reduction.
2.  **Downstream Queue Flooding:** Accelerating intake without resolving major downstream bottlenecks—specifically *Resolve entity mismatch* (31.0h wait, 3 FTEs) and *Normalize financial statements* (20.0h wait, 9 FTEs)—simply shifts work rapidly into queues that cannot absorb it. This increases in-flight inventory, creates operational chaos, and extends total cycle times.
3.  **Ignoring High-Impact Critical Path Bottlenecks:** Process-mining data confirms that *Resolve entity mismatch* sits on the critical path in 82% of cases where it occurs, generating a massive 31.0-hour delay. Eliminating this synchronization delay yields vastly superior process velocity compared to automating an 8-minute registration step.
4.  **Risk Exposure and Control Governance:** Automating registration first accelerates flow into downstream stages without resolving control vulnerabilities (such as the 5.8% four-eyes violations and 8.5% contract terms rework). Deploying classification (A2), normalization (A6), mismatch resolution (A4), and contract control checks (A9) establishes proper control governance, unblocks critical paths, and stabilizes operational capacity before intake volume is accelerated in Phase 3.
