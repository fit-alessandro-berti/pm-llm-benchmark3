## 8. Reversal conditions

1.  **Hidden Risk:** If unobserved risk factors (not in `risk_score`) drive both the flag assignment and the need for lengthy manual investigation, the flag effect is confounded.
2.  **Queue Interference:** If removing the flag reduces total system load enough to speed up unflagged cases significantly, the net system benefit might outweigh individual delays.
3.  **Decision Quality:** If faster processing of flagged cases (via P2) leads to a >5% increase in false approvals (fraud), the time savings are negated by loss.
4.  **Timestamp Semantics:** If `manual_start` represents assignment rather than actual work start, waiting times are overestimated, misleading the bottleneck analysis.
5.  **Positivity/Overlap:** If no unflagged cases have high complexity, we cannot estimate the effect of the flag on complex cases without extrapolation.
6.  **Behavior Change:** If reviewers know a case was "unflagged" due to policy P2 but suspect high risk, they may unofficially escalate, recreating the bottleneck without the data trail.

## 9. Final synthesis

Directly observed is a stark trade-off: flagged cases experience significantly reduced triage waiting times (14 vs 141 minutes) but incur substantial downstream delays in manual review and escalation queues, resulting in longer total cycle times (1054 vs 454 minutes). A plausible mechanism is that the flag triggers a policy bundle (priority lane + mandatory review) that shifts bottleneck location from intake to processing. However, what remains unidentified is the counterfactual duration: would these cases have been delayed anyway due to inherent complexity, or did the mandatory review policy create the delay? Furthermore, the impact on decision quality is unobserved in the event log. The most useful next evidence would come from a randomized encouragement design (Pilot P2) on borderline risk scores. This would isolate the policy effect from the underlying risk, allowing us to determine if the delay is structural (capacity) or procedural (policy rules), and whether safety can be maintained with fewer mandatory reviews.
