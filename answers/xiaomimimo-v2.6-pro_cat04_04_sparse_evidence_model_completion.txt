def _po(nodes):
    try:
        return StrictPartialOrder(nodes=nodes)
    except TypeError:
        sp = StrictPartialOrder()
        for n in nodes:

Note 7/30 = 23%  5–10%  **most** absences are probably genuine skips, but not provably | Medium | Forces the conflation disclosure in §2 (A4) |
| **S6** (Compliance: `XR` only after both checks; before **or after** `SC`; before approval) | `cc  XR`, `er  XR`, `XR  AC`, and **no** edge between `SC` and `XR` | That `XR` needs `SC` at all | Medium–high | Encoded in all three models; the `SC`/`XR` non-ordering is confirmed by V6 and V7 |
| **S7** (Logging note: completion timestamps only; ties ordered by ingestion index) | That **every** order in the log is a completion order and that ties are arbitrarily broken | Any enablement, start/finish separation, or true precedence | High (a fact about the data) | Primary source of spurious DFG directions; forbids interpreting `CCER` as "`CC` caused `ER`" |

## 1.4 Why a two-directional DFG does **not** prove concurrency

A frequency DFG is a multiset of *directly-follows-in-the-completion-sequence* pairs. The pair `(CC, ER): 17` says *"in 17 traces, `ER`'s completion was recorded immediately after `CC`'s"*. It does **not** say that `CC` enabled `ER`, that `CC` finished before `ER` started, or that the two were disjoint. At least four distinct data-generating mechanisms produce the observed 17/11 split:

1. **True concurrency + tie-breaking (S2, S7).** `CC  ER`; the log shows the first *finisher* first; ties are broken by ingestion index. Predicts both directions with a ratio driven by duration distributions.
2. **Case-level exclusive choice of two chains (S1 for one segment).** `(CC·ER, ER·CC)` depending on an unexported attribute (risk tier, work-package split). Predicts both directions with a ratio driven by the case mix.
3. **Globally sequential but time-varying order** (e.g., "the check that is cheaper this month goes first"). Predicts both directions with a ratio driven by policy drift.
4. **Logging artifact.** Long-running, overlapping activities whose completion timestamps are equal or near-equal, reordered by ingestion index (S7). Predicts both directions with a ratio driven by the tie rate.

Mechanisms 1 and 2 generate the **identical trace language** `{CC·ER, ER·CC}`; only start/finish lifecycle data (or resource calendars) can separate them. Mechanism 3 and 4 differ from 1/2 in what they predict about *inserted* activities.

The decisive evidence is therefore not the 17/11 ratio itself but **V5** (`CC·SC·ER`, 2 cases): whatever the mechanism for `CC`/`ER`, `SC` is not placed in a fixed position in the chain. Under mechanism 2 restricted to two fixed chains `CC·ER·SC` and `ER·CC·SC`, V5 is impossible; it requires either a third chain (`CC·SC·ER`) or, more parsimoniously, a partial order `CC  SC` with `ER` unordered w.r.t. `SC`.

Conversely, note that a DFG *is* informative about **absent** pairs at the directly-follows level: the given DFG has no `(SC, CC)`, no `(SC, RO)`, no `(XR, CC)`, no `(XR, ER)`, no `(XR, AC)`-after-`AC`, no `(RP, AC)`. These absences support `CC  SC`, `cc  xr?`, `er  xr?`, `ac  rp`, but they only rule out *directly*-follows orderings; the variants (higher-order evidence) are what rule out `SC`-before-`CC` outright.

# 2. Shared Assumptions

These hold for **all three** candidates and are not re-argued in §3–§5.

**A1 — Event/completion semantics.** One event per activity per case, and the event timestamp is the activity's **completion** (S7). Everywhere in this document `u  v` means *"u's completion precedes v's completion in the trace"*. It does **not** mean `u` finished before `v` started, does not mean `u` causally enabled `v`, and does not mean the two were disjoint. All three models are models of a **completion-order language**. (D7 is honoured: no model asserts overlap or true precedence.)

**A2 — Case and multiplicity.** A trace is one custom production order. Across 30 cases, every activity occurs **at most once**; therefore all three candidates contain **no** `LOOP`, `OR`, or repetition construct. If rework/rollback exists in reality it is invisible to this log and to these models. "At most once" is structural, not an extra guard.

**A3 — Alphabet and silence.**  = {`Register Order`, `Credit Check`, `Engineering Review`, `Supplier Confirmation`, `Expedite Review`, `Approve Configuration`, `Release Production`, `Archive Case`}. `` denotes a silent step. No new labels are invented.

**A4 — Missing-data abstraction for `Supplier Confirmation` (mandatory disclosure).** Optionality is written `x?  x  ` (`OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [Transition(x), SilentTransition()])`, or `X(x, )` in a ProcessTree). The `` branch deliberately **conflates two different realities**:
 (a) `SC` genuinely not required and never executed (S4 — stocked components), and
 (b) `SC` executed but not delivered to the log (S5 — integration fault).
Because the `external/stocked` attribute is not exported and there is no `sc_expected` / `sc_delivered` flag, these two are **observationally equivalent** in this log. Consequence: a compliant stocked case and a *rule-violating* external case with a lost `SC` event are replayed by exactly the same `` branch. **None of the three models can distinguish them**; this is an abstraction, not a finding. (§9 specifies the attribute that would break the tie. A control-flow-only representation of missingness is a *tolerance* semantics, not a compliance semantics.)

**A5 — Optionality is never a loop.** "Optional" and "at most once" are encoded as `x  `, which admits 0 or 1 occurrences. Nothing in the evidence licenses 2.

**A6 — Common spine.** All three models share `RO · body · AC · RP · AR`. The three candidates differ **only** in `body` and in the ordering edges from `body` to `AC`. `CC`, `ER`, `AC`, `RP`, `AR`, `RO` are mandatory (D1, D2, D3).

**A7 — `Expedite Review` ordering.** `cc  xr?`, `er  xr?`, `xr?  ac` (D6, S6), and **no** edge between `SC` and `XR` (S6, confirmed by V6 and V7).

**A8 — Overfitting / underfitting terminology.** We use the mapping that is consistent across the process-mining and ML literatures once "model complexity  language size" is aligned:
 * **Overfitting** = the model language is unnecessarily **narrow** / sample-specific  it *rejects* legitimate behaviour. Symptoms: low generalization, high false-reject rate.
 * **Underfitting** = the model language is unnecessarily **wide** / the model is too coarse  it *admits* behaviour the process does not allow. Symptoms: low precision, silent rule violations.
(The ML reader should note that "overfitting = too complex" and "underfitting = too simple" map onto these exactly: a lookup table is maximally complex and has a minimal language; a "any permutation" model is maximally simple and has a maximal language.) Every candidate gets **both** risk lists so the reading cannot be mis-construed.

**A9 — Confidence handling.** Rules D1–D3 and S6 are treated as *hard constraints*. D4–D6 are treated as *hard constraints only in the permissive model* and as *empirically-resolved constraints* in the balanced model. D5 is the main axis of divergence between balanced and permissive. S1/S2 are competing hypotheses (D7) and are never both encoded.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

## 3.1 Inductive bias

`model_conservative` embodies a **memorise-and-abstain** bias: the model language is the set-union of the observed variants and nothing else, and generalisation is treated as an *unlicensed* act rather than as a goal. Its implicit prior is "the near future is exchangeable with the observed sample, and any linearisation not yet observed is more likely to be an error than a legitimate new behaviour." This inverts structural parsimony on purpose: it accepts a 25-leaf tree with seven copies of `Credit Check` in exchange for **zero** false accepts. It is the correct choice only under an asymmetric loss in which admitting one illegal trace is catastrophically worse than rejecting a legal one — i.e. forensic reconstruction of what has already happened, not planning or enforcement. It deliberately makes **no** claim about causality, concurrency or enablement (D7).

## 3.2 Compact formal expression

```
M_c := ro · ( _{w  W} w ) · ac · rp · ar

W := { cc·er·sc , er·cc·sc , cc·er , er·cc , cc·sc·er ,
       cc·er·sc·xr , er·cc·xr·sc }              (|W| = 7)

L(M_c) = { ro·w·ac·rp·ar : w  W }              |L(M_c)| = 7
```

This factored form is **language-identical** to the flat enumeration `_{wW} (ro·w·ac·rp·ar)`; factoring removes 28 duplicate leaves (`RO`,`AC`,`RP`,`AR` × 6 extra copies) and is used below. The remaining duplicates are 7× `Credit Check`, 7× `Engineering Review`, 5× `Supplier Confirmation`, 2× `Expedite Review` (21 body leaves + 4 spine leaves = 25).

## 3.3 PM4Py code

```python
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# model_conservative -- PM4Py ProcessTree: XOR enumeration of observed bodies
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

RO, CC, ER, SC = "Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review", "Supplier Confirmation"
XR, AC, RP, AR = "Expedite Review", "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"

def _task(label):
    return ProcessTree(label=label)

def _op(operator, children):
    node = ProcessTree(operator=operator, children=list(children))
    for c in node.children:
        c.parent = node
    return node

def _seq(*children):
    return _op(Operator.SEQUENCE, children)

def _xor(*children):
    return _op(Operator.XOR, children)

# the seven observed bodies, between Register Order and Approve Configuration
OBSERVED_BODIES = [
    (CC, ER, SC),        # V1  (12x)
    (ER, CC, SC),        # V2  ( 7x)
    (CC, ER),            # V3  ( 4x)
    (ER, CC),            # V4  ( 3x)
    (CC, SC, ER),        # V5  ( 2x)
    (CC, ER, SC, XR),    # V6  ( 1x)
    (ER, CC, XR, SC),    # V7  ( 1x)
]

body_choice = _xor(*[_seq(*[_task(a) for a in body]) for body in OBSERVED_BODIES])

model_conservative = _seq(_task(RO), body_choice,
                          _task(AC), _task(RP), _task(AR))
```

**Duplicate labels and scalability (required discussion).**
* *Duplicates.* The tree contains 7 syntactic copies of `Credit Check` etc. `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net` emits one transition per leaf, so the net is **language-correct at the transition level** (only one branch is ever entered). The danger appears the moment any step identifies activities by **label rather than by transition identity** — a common "deduplicate labels" refactoring, or any label-folding used to compute activity-level escaping edges, heuristic dependency matrices, or a merged DFG. Merging is **not** language-preserving here: once the five `Supplier Confirmation` transitions share a single transition with the union of their input and output places, a token can enter through one branch's place and leave through another's. The merged net then admits e.g. `RO, CC, SC, ER, SC, XR, AC, RP, AR` (a trace with a **repeated** `Supplier Confirmation`) by walking `b5`'s prefix and `b6`'s suffix — a trace contained in no branch. Exact alignments also degrade: every optimal alignment becomes one of up to `(k_i!)` equivalent optima over same-label transitions, inflating runtime and making the reported cost non-identifiable.
* *Scalability.* The leaf count is `(_{wW} |w|)`, i.e. linear in the number of *variants*, which grows combinatorially in the number of independent optional/interleaving points (k independent binary options  up to `2^k` branches; adding free interleaving of two pairs multiplies by up to `4!/(2!·2!)`). 30 cases already produced 7 variants (variant entropy  2.0 bits); at 300 cases one should expect dozens. The model is non-parametric: nothing is shared between branches, so it cannot be updated incrementally and cannot explain *why* a case took a given branch.

## 3.4 Behaviour deliberately excluded

Everything outside the seven enumerated bodies, in particular:

1. `Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Expedite Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case` (`XR` without `SC`) — never observed.
2. `Register Order, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case` (`SC` between the two checks with `ER` first) — never observed; licensed by D5 but with 0 support.
3. `Register Order, Engineering Review, Credit Check, Supplier Confirmation, Expedite Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case` (`XR` after `SC` with `ER` first) — never observed even though V7 is its sibling.
4. `Register Order, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check, Engineering Review, …` (`SC` before both checks) — never observed and rule-forbidden (D5).
5. `Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Expedite Review, Expedite Review, …` (repetition) — structurally impossible.
6. Any trace omitting `Credit Check`, `Engineering Review`, `Approve Configuration`, `Release Production` or `Archive Case`, or starting with anything but `Register Order`.
7. Any interleaving of the middle block with `Approve Configuration` (e.g. `SC` after `AC`), forbidden by D4.

## 3.5 Known underfitting risks (language still wider than the plausible process)

1. **Label-merge language inflation.** As shown in §3.3, the moment duplicate labels are merged the language grows to include traces with repeated activities and cross-branch splices. The model *as compiled* is exact; the model *as typically consumed* may not be. This is genuine over-permissiveness introduced downstream, and it is invisible to a fitness score of 1.0.
2. **Missingness conflation (A4).** A case whose `Supplier Confirmation` was executed but lost (S5) is replayed as a legitimate `SC`-skipping branch. The model therefore admits — as *conforming* — cases that may violate S3 (external component, no `SC`). It cannot raise a compliance flag, because "absent `SC`" and "skipped `SC`" are the same token in its language.
3. **Attribute blindness.** Because `external/stocked` is not in the model, the model admits `SC`-absent traces for **any** case profile. A rule engine with the attribute would reject a subset of the model's own language. The model is thus strictly weaker than the business rule set it claims to encode.
4. **Semantic over-reading of linear order.** The tree asserts a total order on each body (`CC·ER·SC` claims `CC` completes before `ER` *and* before `SC` starts nothing at all — but a reader will infer causality). At the level of true executions with overlap (D7), the model's asserted total orders are fabrications; several *distinct* true executions map onto the same modelled trace. The model therefore admits (as "the same trace") behaviour it never intended to license.
5. **Zero control outside the sample support.** If the real process admits a partial order whose linearisations happen to be only partly sampled, the model has no notion of "near-miss"; a legal trace one transposition away from an observed one is rejected with the same confidence as a nonsense trace.

### Overfitting (over-restriction) risks — complementary list
By construction this model has **maximal** over-restriction: it rejects 58 of the 65 traces in the bounded search space of §8 (see §6). Every legitimate new interleaving is a false reject; 30 cases are far too few to have sampled the support of a process with even one optional activity and one free interleaving.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

## 4.1 Inductive bias

`model_balanced` embodies **structural parsimony subject to empirical adequacy**: among all models whose language contains the seven observed variants, prefer the one with (i) the fewest choice points, (ii) the fewest *total-order* constraints that the evidence does not license, and (iii) the coarsest partial order that still reproduces every *absent* directly-follows relation we care about. Concurrency (more precisely: leaving two nodes **unordered** in a partial order) is selected exactly where a strict order has observed counterexamples *and* where a case-level XOR would add a choice point the data does not demand; optionality is selected exactly where an activity is absent in some complete cases. Domain rules D1–D4, D6 and S6 are treated as hard constraints; where a rule (D5) conflicts with a strong empirical regularity (23/23), the empirical regularity wins and the conflict is recorded in the uncertainty register below. This is the model of choice when the cost of false rejects and the cost of false accepts are comparable.

## 4.2 Compact formal expression

```
M_b := PO(N, )

N   := { ro, cc, er, sc?, xr?, ac, rp, ar }        sc? = sc   ,  xr? = xr  
   :=  rocc , roer ,
         ccsc? ,
         ccxr? , erxr? ,
         sc?ac , xr?ac , erac ,
         acrp , rpar         (transitive closure taken)

(no edge between er and sc?)  (no edge between sc? and xr?)  (no edge between cc and er)

L(M_b) = LinExt(PO) = 12 completion sequences:
  SC present, XR absent (3):  cc·er·sc , er·cc·sc , cc·sc·er
  SC present, XR present (5): cc·er·sc·xr , er·cc·sc·xr , cc·sc·er·xr , cc·er·xr·sc , er·cc·xr·sc
  SC absent,  XR absent (2):  cc·er , er·cc

## 4.3 PM4Py code

```python

                                    Transition, SilentTransition)

        return StrictPartialOrder(nodes=nodes)
    except TypeError:                       # older builds expose add_node only
        sp = StrictPartialOrder()
        for n in nodes:
            sp.order.add_node(n)
        return sp

def _opt(label):
    """'label, zero-or-one occurrence' ->  label (+) tau   (see assumption A4)."""
    return OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [Transition(label), SilentTransition()])

ro, cc, er       = Transition(RO), Transition(CC), Transition(ER)
ac, rp, arc      = Transition(AC), Transition(RP), Transition(AR)
sc_opt, xr_opt   = _opt(SC), _opt(XR)          # SC? , XR?

model_balanced = _po([ro, cc, er, sc_opt, xr_opt, ac, rp, arc])
o = model_balanced.order

o.add_edge(ro,  cc)      # D1
o.add_edge(ro,  er)      # D1
o.add_edge(cc,  sc_opt)  # empirical: 23/23 SCs follow CC   (see decision table)
o.add_edge(cc,  xr_opt)  # D6 / S6 : XR after BOTH checks
o.add_edge(er,  xr_opt)  # D6 / S6
o.add_edge(sc_opt, ac)   # D4 : SC before approval when it occurs
o.add_edge(xr_opt, ac)   # D6 : XR before approval when it occurs
o.add_edge(er,  ac)      # D3
o.add_edge(ac,  rp)      # D2
o.add_edge(rp,  arc)     # D1
# deliberately NO edge: (cc, er), (er, sc_opt), (sc_opt, xr_opt)
```

4. **`er` unordered w.r.t. `sc?` licenses `SC` before `ER`** (`CC·SC·ER` observed 2×; `ER·SC·CC` is *not* what this edge licenses — see §5 — but `CC·SC·ER` is). 5. **No attribute guard on the `` of `XR`.** If `Expedite Review` is triggered by an order-type attribute, the model admits `XR` on standard orders. Control-flow alone cannot express the guard, so the model is wider than the business rule by exactly the cases whose trigger attribute is not met.

Residual risk is small but not zero: (a) 11 observed traces are *forced* through the `cc  sc?` edge, so if D5's "or" is literally true, the model will mis-represent the enabling structure (though it will still fit the log); (b) if `SC` is in fact mandatory (S3) and all 7 absences are data loss, the model's `` branch is an artefact and the "correct" model has `SC` mandatory plus a missing-event tolerance — which this model cannot express.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

## 5.1 Inductive bias

`model_permissive` embodies **rule-satisficing with maximal tolerance**: the language is *all* linear extensions of the coarsest partial-order family that satisfies every domain rule at its stated confidence level (D1–D7 as written, S6 as written), subject only to the fixed spine, the mandatory status of `CC`/`ER`/`AC`, and at-most-once. Empirical frequency is used to **rank** explanations but never to **restrict** the language below what the rules license: a behaviour that D5 permits is admitted even with 0/23 empirical support, on the grounds that a low-confidence rule is still a rule and a 30-case sample cannot be treated as the support of the distribution. The prior is "the observed sample under-covers the support; rare but licensed behaviour is the norm, not the exception." This is the right choice for exploratory discovery, for pre-filtering candidate traces before a human review, and as an **upper bound** in a sensitivity analysis; it is the wrong choice for compliance enforcement.

## 5.2 Compact formal expression

```
M_p := ro · ( PO(N, )    PO(N, ) ) · ac · rp · ar

N = N := { cc, er, sc?, xr? }        sc? = sc   ,  xr? = xr  

 :=  ccsc? , ccxr? , erxr?          ("SC is enabled by Credit Check")
 :=  ersc? , ccxr? , erxr?          ("SC is enabled by Engineering Review")   -- D5's "or"

(no edge in either branch between er and sc? / cc and sc?  respectively,
 no edge between sc? and xr?)

L(M_p) = L(ro·PO·ac·rp·ar)  L(ro·PO·ac·rp·ar)

  SC present, XR absent (4):  cc·er·sc , er·cc·sc , cc·sc·er , er·sc·cc
  SC present, XR present (6): cc·er·sc·xr , er·cc·sc·xr , cc·sc·er·xr ,
                              cc·er·xr·sc , er·cc·xr·sc , er·sc·cc·xr
  SC absent,  XR absent (2):  cc·er , er·cc
  SC absent,  XR present (2):  cc·er·xr , er·cc·xr

                                       |L(M_p)| = 14   (7 observed + 7 unobserved)
```

Note the deliberate **language duplication**: `cc·er·sc` and `cc·er·xr·sc`-style traces occur in *both* branches. As a *set* of traces this is harmless; it does mean the model is not a "one execution, one path" model and any frequency statistic derived from it would double-count. Disclosed.

## 5.3 PM4Py code

```python
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# model_permissive -- PM4Py POWL: XOR of two partial orders (D5's "or" gate)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL,
                                    Transition, SilentTransition)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

RO, CC, ER, SC = "Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review", "Supplier Confirmation"
XR, AC, RP, AR = "Expedite Review", "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"

def _po(nodes):
    try:
        return StrictPartialOrder(nodes=nodes)
    except TypeError:
        sp = StrictPartialOrder()
        for n in nodes:
            sp.order.add_node(n)
        return sp

def _opt(label):
    return OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [Transition(label), SilentTransition()])

def _branch(sc_gate_label):
    """One arm of D5: SC is enabled by sc_gate_label (CC or ER), XR by BOTH checks."""
    cc, er   = Transition(CC), Transition(ER)
    sc_opt   = _opt(SC)
    xr_opt   = _opt(XR)
    body     = _po([cc, er, sc_opt, xr_opt])
    o        = body.order
    gate     = cc if sc_gate_label == CC else er
    o.add_edge(gate, sc_opt)     # D5  : SC enabled by ONE of the principal checks
    o.add_edge(cc,   xr_opt)     # D6/S6: XR after BOTH checks
    o.add_edge(er,   xr_opt)     # D6/S6
    # deliberately NO edge between (sc_opt, xr_opt)          -- S6
    # deliberately NO edge between (the non-gate check, sc_opt) -- D5
    return body

middle = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [_branch(CC), _branch(ER)])

ro, ac, rp, arc = Transition(RO), Transition(AC), Transition(RP), Transition(AR)
model_permissive = _po([ro, middle, ac, rp, arc])
o = model_permissive.order
o.add_edge(ro, middle)      # D1
o.add_edge(middle, ac)      # every branch completes before approval (D3, D4, D6)
o.add_edge(ac, rp)          # D2
o.add_edge(rp, arc)         # D1
```

*(A ProcessTree rendering of the same middle block is possible — `((CC, X(SC,))  ER, (ER, X(SC,))  CC)` — but it forces `XR` to run after the whole block and therefore wrongly forbids `CC·ER·XR·SC`, which S6 explicitly permits. POWL is used because "after both checks, unordered w.r.t. a third node" is a genuine partial-order constraint that no ProcessTree operator expresses without a combinatorial blow-up of XOR branches.)*

## 5.4 Behaviour deliberately excluded

1. `Register Order, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case` and `Register Order, Supplier Confirmation, Engineering Review, Credit Check, …` — `SC` before **both** principal checks. D5 requires that at least one check has produced information first; neither branch can produce `SC` first.
2. `Register Order, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, …` — `XR` before `ER`; violates `er  xr?` (D6, S6).
3. `Register Order, Engineering Review, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Supplier Confirmation, …` — `XR` before `CC`; violates `cc  xr?`.
4. `Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Expedite Review, Expedite Review, Approve Configuration, …` — a second `Expedite Review`; violates "at most once" (D6).
5. `Register Order, Credit Check, Supplier Confirmation, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case` — `Engineering Review` omitted (D3).
6. Any trace in which `Approve Configuration` does not precede `Release Production`, in which `Archive Case` is not last, or in which `Register Order` is not first (D1, D2).

### Boundary: "reasonable generalization" vs. behaviour that stays forbidden

A trace is a **reasonable generalization** iff *all* of:
 **(G1)** it is a linear extension of `PO(N,)` or `PO(N,)` above;
 **(G2)** it contains each of the eight labels at most once;
 **(G3)** it changes only the relative order of node pairs that the evidence leaves **unordered** — namely (`CC`,`ER`), (`ER`,`SC?`) in branch 1 / (`CC`,`SC?`) in branch 2, and (`SC?`,`XR?`);
 **(G4)** it invents no label, no repetition, and no new control point.

A trace is **forbidden** iff it violates any of (G1)–(G4): `SC` before both checks; `XR` before either check or after `Approve Configuration`; more than one `XR` or any repetition; anything outside the `Register Order … Archive Case` envelope; omission of `Credit Check`, `Engineering Review`, `Approve Configuration`, `Release Production` or `Archive Case`; `SC` after `Approve Configuration`.

A trace is **"licensed but undesirable"** (the grey zone) iff it satisfies (G1)–(G4) but has 0 empirical support *and* has a competing explanation in terms of data quality. Concretely: `…,CC,ER,XR,AC,…` (`XR` with no `SC`) is licensed by D6 yet is also exactly what a lost `SC` event looks like; `…,ER,SC,CC,…` is licensed by D5 yet has 0/23 support and would be indistinguishable from an ingestion-index tie artifact (S7). These are admitted but **flagged**; a monitoring rule should raise them for human review rather than accept them silently.

*What is explicitly **not** done*: a fully parallel `(CC, ER, SC?, XR?)` body is **not** used. It is simpler and would be defensible under an ML-style "maximum entropy" bias, but it admits `SC`-first traces, which D5 forbids and which 0/23 SC-bearing cases support. That is where "permissive" stops and "unprincipled" begins.

## 5.5 Known underfitting risks (language wider than the plausible process)

1. **The `er·sc·cc` family is admitted on the strength of a low-confidence rule alone.** D5 is "low-to-medium" and its "after `ER`" arm has 0/23 empirical support. If `SC` is in fact enabled only by `Credit Check` (e.g. it is priced against the credit-approved quantity), the model licenses an entire class of impossible traces and will silently absorb ingestion-index tie artifacts as legitimate.
2. **`XR`-without-`SC` traces are licensed** (same risk as balanced U4, but here they are joined by `er·sc·cc·xr` and friends). As an expedite decision presumably concerns an external supplier, this may be not merely rare but incoherent.
3. **Missingness conflation is at its most dangerous here.** The `` branch of `SC?` combined with the disjunctive gate means a data-loss trace can be explained post hoc by whichever branch is convenient. The model therefore has the *lowest* ability to detect the S5 integration fault — precisely the failure mode a permissive model should not enable.
4. **Branch duplication (§5.2) destroys frequency semantics.** Six of the 14 traces are generated by both branches. Any simulated case count, throughput estimate or APTN-style frequency conformance computed on this model double-counts those traces, biasing capacity planning towards the `XR`-present and `SC`-present variants.
5. **The disjunction is a modelling device, not a claim about the world.** "Enabled by `CC`" and "enabled by `ER`" are alternatives in a control-flow sense; in reality a single case has one true trigger. The model cannot say which, so it cannot be used to *assign responsibility* or to *predict* which check gates procurement.

### Overfitting (over-restriction) risks — complementary list
Low: the model only hard-codes rules with high/medium–high confidence (D1, D2, D3, D6, S6) and at-most-once. The main residual over-restriction is the at-most-once assumption on every activity (A2) — if rework exists, all three candidates fail and none of the current evidence can detect it.

# 6. Unobserved Behavior Audit

## 6.1 Bounded search space used for the audit

Bound **B**: `Register Order` exactly first, `Approve Configuration · Release Production · Archive Case` exactly last and in that order, each of `Credit Check`, `Engineering Review`, `Supplier Confirmation`, `Expedite Review` occurring **at most once** in between and in any order (including absent). The middle block then ranges over all permutations of all subsets of a 4-element set: `_{k=0..4} P(4,k) = 1 + 4 + 12 + 24 + 24 = 65` candidate traces.

Under bound B the audit is **exact, not sampled** (see §8): `|L(model_conservative)| = 7`, `|L(model_balanced)| = 12`, `|L(model_permissive)| = 14`. All observed variants lie in all three languages (fitness on the 30 cases is 1.0 for all three), so the audit below is entirely about the extra behaviour.

#  §8  Validation — self-contained
#  Reconstructs the 30-case log, rebuilds the three candidates, converts them to
#  accepting Petri nets, checks soundness, computes fitness / precision /
#  generalization / simplicity, performs play-out, and checks a manual suite.
#

# ------------------------------------------------------- 1. rebuild the log --
def _manual_log():
    log, n = EventLog(), 0
    for cnt, acts in VARIANTS:
        for _ in range(cnt):
            tr = Trace()
            tr.attributes["concept:name"] = "c%03d" % n
            for a in acts:

    return log

    for sep in (",", ";", "|", " > ", ">", "\t"):

        if len(cand) == 30 and sorted(len(t) for t in cand) == want:
            return cand
    return _manual_log()                                      # deterministic fallback

def _op(op, ch):

    for c in n.children: c.parent = n

        sp = StrictPartialOrder()
        for n in nodes: sp.order.add_node(n)

_b_ro, _b_cc, _b_er = Transition(RO), Transition(CC), Transition(ER)

model_balanced = _po([_b_ro, _b_cc, _b_er, _b_sc, _b_xr, _b_ac, _b_rp, _b_ar])
_o = model_balanced.order
_o.add_edge(_b_ro, _b_cc); _o.add_edge(_b_ro, _b_er)
_o.add_edge(_b_cc, _b_sc); _o.add_edge(_b_cc, _b_xr); _o.add_edge(_b_er, _b_xr)
_o.add_edge(_b_sc, _b_ac); _o.add_edge(_b_xr, _b_ac); _o.add_edge(_b_er, _b_ac)

    body = _po([cc, er, scq, xrq]); o = body.order
    o.add_edge(cc if gate == CC else er, scq)   # D5
    o.add_edge(cc, xrq); o.add_edge(er, xrq)    # D6

_p_mid = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [_branch(CC), _branch(ER
