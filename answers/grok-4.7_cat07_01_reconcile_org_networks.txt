# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

Mira_Coordinator is a cross-team, single-touch router on the exception, complaint, reopen, and specialist-assignment path. She is central wherever the metric counts **transfers**, and peripheral wherever it counts **shared caseload inside a stable team**. Those results describe one position, not two people. They are directional. Several frequency tables do not add up under a strict reading of one 12-week log, so magnitudes should be recomputed before anyone treats a number as a headcount, a capacity, or a control finding.

North and South are near mirrors (Alice–Carla–Elise versus Bruno–Diego–Farid). Bot_Ingest, Bot_Rules, Vendor_Verify, and Vendor_Pay sit on the main path in volumes no human matches. Any reading of Mira that ignores that designed split and those non-human resources will overstate her scope.

## Handover-of-work network

This network counts direct resource succession: the resource of one event and the resource of a following event. It records the **flow position**, not effort, competence, or ownership of outcomes.

What it supports about Mira:

- She is the main human destination of Bot_Rules (about 920 handovers, roughly 3.5 minutes after the bot). Alice and Bruno receive the routine regional split (about 610 and 590).
- She then fans out to both senior adjusters, both junior adjusters, Omar_Medical, and Priya_Legal. Specialists and both temps hand work back to her.
- If the reported centralities use the usual degree/(n−1) scaling on these 15 resources, an out-degree of 0.82 means she hands work to most of the others, and an in-degree of 0.51 means several distinct roles hand work to her. Betweenness of 0.77, with Bot_Rules second at 0.41, is what a star-shaped exception router looks like beside an automated first split.
- Mean elapsed times on her outgoing edges (about 16–19 minutes to adjusters, 42 minutes to Omar, 51 minutes to Priya) line up with the **next** resource’s median service time. Those edges look like ordinary next-step duration, not a multi-day wait inserted at the handover itself.

What it does not support:

- It does not show that she stays responsible after the handover. The narrative evidence says she often appears just after Bot_Rules and then drops out for several events.
- It does not show that high betweenness is delay. A router is central even when each touch is minutes.
- The top-edge list is not the whole graph. Bot_Rules has about 4,750 events and only about 2,120 of its outgoing mass is in the three named edges, so many bot exits are elsewhere.
- Outgoing frequencies named for Mira (on the order of 1,640 on the listed edges alone) exceed her 960 recorded events. A directly-follows count cannot do that if both tables are raw counts from the same rows. Treat the pattern as real and the absolute edge weights as unreconciled.

## Working-together network

Classical working-together counts pairs of resources that both appear in the same case. It is **joint presence**, not a handover and not a duration. Edge strength then depends entirely on the denominator: shared cases over all cases, over one resource’s cases, or a Jaccard-style normalization. The reported “co-case rates” must be read only as the study’s normalized strengths.

What it supports:

- The dense ties are the regional teams: Alice–Carla 27%, Alice–Elise 24%, Carla–Elise 20%, and the South mirror at 26%, 22%, and 19%. Weighted degree follows that clique structure (seniors about 0.43–0.49; Mira 0.16).
- Mira’s pairwise rates are weak with everyone she hands work to (about 4–8%, including 5% and 4% with the two team leads). Absolute handovers to Carla (about 350) resemble Alice’s handovers to Carla (about 320), yet the normalized tie is much weaker. That is the signature of a **bridge**: many counterparts, each shared case a small slice of either person’s portfolio, rather than a partner who works the same book repeatedly.
- Dispersion explains the paradox without calling the metric broken. Handover centrality rises because many paths pass through her. Working-together degree stays low because those paths are spread across North, South, temps, medical, and legal, while Alice and Carla co-occupy the same cases. Short four-minute events explain why colleagues experience the contact as routing rather than co-handling. They do not, under the classical definition, erase co-presence: one event in a case is still co-presence. Low weighted degree here is a **normalization and dispersion** result.

What it does not support:

- It does not mean she is unimportant, rarely involved, or a logging ghost. She is on about 905 cases (about 22% of claims).
- It does not mean the handover edges are false. A one-off transfer creates a handover and only a small normalized collaboration score.
- It does not identify a team. Omar–Priya at 4% shows that even the two specialists rarely share cases; Mira is not embedded with them either.

## Subcontracting network

With distance n=2, subcontracting counts a local sandwich: resource A, then B within a short window, then A again. In the classical social-network construction that pattern means A passed a piece of work out and resumed. It is **local resumption**, not proof of case ownership through close.

What it supports:

- The strongest sandwiches are Mira→Omar→Mira (126) and Mira→Priya→Mira (98), with fragments that match “send to specialist, then assign, reopen, or continue the exception.” Bot_Rules→Mira→Bot_Rules (62) fits a manual look at a fraud screen and a return to the rules engine.
- Her subcontracting betweenness (0.69) is far above the team leads (0.21 and 0.19), who show the same pattern only inside one dyad (approval / negotiation / approval, 86 and 82).
- The same motif exists outside her role: Temp_1→Vendor_Verify→Temp_1 (68) on coverage checks. Subcontracting is a structural pattern of this process, not a Mira-only anomaly.

What it does not support:

- It does not show she does medical or legal work. The middle event is the specialist’s.
- It does not show she holds the case for its whole life. n=2 is a very short window.
- The counts conflict with the activity table. She has 960 events in 905 cases, a surplus of about 55 events, while the three named return-to-Mira patterns alone sum to 286. Each real sandwich needs at least two of her events. Until both metrics are rebuilt on one dataframe, with one lifecycle rule, “how often she resumes” is not a reliable magnitude. The direction (specialist work is wrapped by her coordination events when it happens) can still be tested.

## Performance and time statistics

Service time, handover delay, and case cycle time answer different questions.

- Median service time of 4.2 minutes, with about 1.06 events per case, means her **own handling** is a short clerical act. She is not a slow processor. Rough touch-time over 12 weeks is on the order of 960 × 4.2 minutes, far below Carla or Diego and far below Omar or Priya.
- “High variance” in cycle-time contribution is what we should expect if she is **selected onto** hard cases: 48% of her cases carry exception_flag=True, versus about 21–26% for the team leads and about 62–71% for the specialists. Composition inflates her average. It does not, by itself, mean she causes the length.
- The six-day absence is the strongest evidence in the pack, and it is narrow. Median cycle time of ordinary low-value claims was unchanged. For exception_flag=True, the 90th percentile moved from 8.4 to 13.9 days. A rise of about 5.5 days across a six-day stop is the size of a **gate left unattended**, not the size of her four-minute touch. Cases that needed a routing decision waited; routine Bot_Rules→team-lead flow did not.
- That absence is a dam, not a simulation of deleting the role. Work accumulating in queues would, if anything, **withhold** arrivals from Omar and Priya during the absence and release a backlog afterward. It does not test the claim that a redesigned flow with no coordinator would flood specialists.
- Vendor_Verify (median 2.1 hours, about 1,500 cases) and Vendor_Pay (median 1.3 hours, about 2,840 cases, handover in-degree 0.26) dominate elapsed time on coverage and payment. Bot_Ingest and Bot_Rules contribute seconds. Main-path waiting and exception-tail waiting are different problems.

What these figures do not support: a process-wide bottleneck located in her labor, a judgment that exception cases are long only because of her, or a claim that ordinary claims would speed up if she were removed.

## Role discovery and activity-profile similarity

No similarity matrix and no `discover_organizational_roles` result are in the evidence. What follows is a profile prediction, not a mined role.

Her stated activities — Request Missing Information, Reopen Claim, Escalate Complaint, Assign Specialist — do not match the other profiles:

- Bots: Receive Claim, Triage Claim, Fraud Screen.
- Team leads: Triage Claim, Approve Settlement, and for Alice also Reopen Claim, then release to Vendor_Pay.
- Adjusters: Assess Damage and Negotiate Settlement, with juniors also on Request Missing Information.
- Specialists: a single long activity each.
- Temps: Validate Coverage, Request Missing Information, Close Claim.
- Vendors: Validate Coverage or Issue Payment.

She would very likely fall in her **own cluster**, not with Alice and Bruno. The only overlap with a team lead is Reopen Claim, and she is absent from Approve Settlement and from the payment-release edges.

Two catalog problems block a stronger claim. “Assign Specialist” is one of her main activities and appears in the subcontracting fragments, but it is not in the 14-activity process list. Either the catalog is incomplete or that label is sitting outside the controlled activity concept. Request Missing Information is also done by Temp_1, Elise, and Farid, so an activity-only similarity will pull her somewhat toward juniors and temps even if the rest of the profile is distinct. Role mining has to be read on the full multiset, after the activity dictionary is fixed.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

These are overlapping mechanisms. The credible reading is a formal exception desk that also acts as a broker and, because it is staffed by one person, a key-person dependency. None of the labels below is established as the single ground truth.

## H1. Formal coordinator and exception desk

**Mechanism.** Bot_Rules performs the first split. Regional, ordinary work goes to Alice or Bruno. A defined residue — missing information, complaints, reopens, fraud-screen fallout, messy vendor or temp results — is queued to Mira, who classifies it and sends it on. She is a desk, not a second team lead.

**Supporting evidence.** Largest human inbound edge from the rules bot; activity mix aligned with exception handling; 48% exception-flag rate; presence on 64% of reopened claims and 71% of complaints while touching only 22% of all claims; four-minute service time; near one event per case; routine claims insensitive to her absence; the process owner’s “queue cleaner” description; North/South symmetry left intact beside her.

**Limits.** About half of her cases are not exception-flagged, so either the flag is incomplete or the desk also does ordinary rerouting. “Assign Specialist” may be undocumented. She receives work from temps on validation and closure, which may be operational noise rather than a formal exception product. The owner’s phrase describes status, not the design history. We do not have the routing rules, the queue name, or the role description from the system.

**Predictions.** On a fresh log, filtering to exception_flag=True, Escalate Complaint, or Reopen Claim raises her handover share further; filtering to exception_flag=False and to low amount-band, low priority, no reopen and no complaint drops it sharply. A resource–activity matrix shows Assign Specialist and Escalate Complaint concentrated on her. Cases that reach Alice or Bruno through Bot_Rules rarely visit her afterward. If the organization publishes a RACI, this desk is named on it.

## H2. Hidden bottleneck and key-person dependency

**Mechanism.** Exception and complaint cases cannot move to the next competent person until she performs a short but personal routing act. While she is available, the mean handover looks fast because the act is short. When she is absent, the tail of exception cycle time grows by about the length of the absence. The dependency is the **gate**, not the touch time.

**Supporting evidence.** Absence result confined to exception cases; betweenness and in-degree; specialist return edges that need her to be there to receive them; adjusters’ statement that she knows where hard cases should go; concentration of 71% of complaints in one resource.

**Limits.** Mean edge times do not show chronic delay at her hop. Ordinary medians did not move. Vendors dominate ordinary sojourn. A six-day window can be confounded by volume, staffing of Omar and Priya, or a channel incident in the same week. High betweenness is implied by any star router and is not independent evidence of harm. The absence created a queue dam; it did not measure steady-state overload after a redesign. Her labor hours do not show personal overload.

**Predictions.** Waiting time **before** her event, not her complete−start service time, rises in the exception tail. Repeating the absence test, or a planned coverage gap with a deputy, moves exception p90 only when the deputy is also missing. Specialist start times shift by roughly the coverage gap. Queue age at the exception desk predicts exception cycle time better than her service time does. If a second trained person staffs the same queue, betweenness of the **queue** stays high and betweenness of the **named person** falls.

## H3. Boundary spanner and triage broker

**Mechanism.** She bridges groups that otherwise work in separate cliques: the rules bot, two regional teams, junior and senior adjusters, temps, an external verifier’s fallout, medical review, and legal review. She fills structural holes. Value, if any, is matching, not processing. The operations view that she protects specialists is a broker claim: of the large inbound stream, only a minority is sent to Omar or Priya, and the rest is pointed back at adjusters.

**Supporting evidence.** Fan-out across both teams and both specialists; weak working-together ties to all of them, especially to both team leads; Omar–Priya co-presence only 4%; subcontracting wrapped around both specialists; temps escalating into her rather than into Alice or Bruno; adjusters describing tacit knowledge of placement. She is outside the approval–payment dyads, so she spans the organization without substituting for the team leads.

**Limits.** A formally designed desk **is** a broker. H1 and H3 can be the same job described from the org chart and from the network. Brokerage is not yet shown to be tacit skill. If assignment is a simple function of region, case_type, amount_band, and a medical/legal keyword, Bot_Rules could carry it and she is a pass-through. We lack the decision rules and the outcome of her routing (rework, complaint upheld, specialist overturn). Low working-together with team leads also limits the “informal team lead” variant: an informal lead of North or South would sit inside that clique and would show approval or payment-release behavior. She does neither.

**Predictions.** Her outbound destination, stratified by region, case_type, priority, amount_band, and channel, is predictable if the broker role is mostly a rule, and noisy but associated with better specialist yield if it is judgment. During coverage by a deputy who only follows a script, the mix of destinations shifts and specialist rework changes. Working-together inside North and inside South stays high whether she is present or not. Medical and legal arrival rates fall during an absence and rebound when the queue is cleared, rather than rising because unfiltered work hits them immediately.

## H4. Thin queue identity, with a real person behind it, and a measurement problem on top

**Mechanism.** Part of the centrality is a logging and dictionary effect: a shared queue user, lifecycle start/complete counted as two handovers, an uncontrolled activity label (“Assign Specialist”), and exception_flag set on the same path that routes to her, so “she handles exceptions” becomes partly circular. Part of it is still a real person, because a system account does not take a six-day holiday and leave a human queue behind.

**Supporting evidence.** Event-to-case ratio as thin as the temps and the vendors; service time closer to clerical than to adjuster work; handover and subcontracting frequencies that cannot be raw directly-follows counts on her 960 events; an activity name outside the official catalog; the process owner’s reluctance to call her a team lead.

**Limits.** The absence effect is difficult to produce with a pure artifact. Specialists return work to this identity specifically. Her activity mix differs from both bots. A ghost user would not accumulate judgment descriptions from adjusters.

**Predictions.** Rebuilding the log with one lifecycle policy (for example complete-to-next-start only), a repaired activity dictionary, and a documented timestamp for when exception_flag is written will shrink the numerical gap between event counts and SNA weights. Her rank order — high handover betweenness, low working-together degree, absence effect limited to exceptions — will remain if H1–H3 are right. If the rank order disappears after deduplication, H4 dominates and the organizational story should be rewritten from the cleaned log.

# 3. Risk assessment

Centrality is a position, not a verdict. The risks below are localized, and none is a finding of misconduct.

**Bottleneck risk — medium, and only on the exception tail.** The failure mode is an unstaffed gate for complaints, reopens, and specialist assignment. Mean handover latency at her edges does not show a chronic processing bottleneck, and ordinary low-value medians were stable while she was away. Vendor_Verify and Vendor_Pay remain the larger waiting-time masses on the main path. Confidence is moderate: one six-day window, no published volume control, and a plausible size match between the gap and the p90 shift.

**Coordination risk — medium-high.** Ownership is split across a bot that triages, a coordinator who routes and then disappears, an adjuster who assesses, a team lead who approves, and a vendor that pays. Request Missing Information is performed by Mira, Temp_1, Elise, and Farid, so the same case can collect repeated clarification from different roles. The low working-together scores with the people she hands off to are consistent with weak feedback: she may not see the downstream result, and they may not see her as a partner. That is a design exposure for dropped and duplicated work, not evidence that duplication is already widespread.

**Key-person risk — high for exception routing, low for the rest of the process.** One person is on most complaints and most reopens, and the exception tail moved when she was away. The tacit piece, if the adjusters are right, is knowing which hard case goes where. Irreplaceability is not proven. The test was “this person was not there,” not “no competent deputy exists.” A second person on the same desk would retire most of this risk without retiring the function.

**Compliance and control risk — medium as a design gap, not a conduct finding.** She is involved both in detecting issues (missing information, complaints, a return into fraud screening) and in choosing the next specialist or adjuster. That combination can be uncontrolled discretion: similar cases could be assigned differently, and the person who records the problem also steers its review. There is no audit sample, no override log, no outcome by assignee, and no evidence of biased selection, suppressed complaints, or inappropriate influence. Medical and legal cases are **supposed** to look exceptional; their high exception rates are not suspicious by themselves. The proportionate response is traceable assignment and sampling, not an inference of wrongdoing. Segregation should be tightened first for high amount-band, legal, and complaint cases.

**Workload-fairness risk — low for Mira’s hours, worth watching for specialists and juniors.** Her touch time is short. The heavy human effort sits with Carla, Diego, Omar, and Priya. Temps are shielded from exceptions (about 13–14% of their cases). That shield can be fair design or quiet dumping of residue onto Mira’s queue and then onto a few adjusters. Fairness cannot be judged from event counts. It needs hours, case difficulty (priority, amount_band, exception, reopen), and after-hours load, stratified by team. The North/South mirror is a built-in control: a large difference in exception load between Elise and Farid, or between Carla and Diego, would be a fairness signal; symmetry would be evidence of even design.

**Data-quality risk — medium, and it contaminates the other risks.** Specifically: handover and subcontracting frequencies are not consistent with her event total; “Assign Specialist” is outside the stated activity list; exception_flag may be written by the same routing path under study; lifecycle:transition can double SNA edges if start and complete are both counted; service time is only interpretable if start and complete exist and the calculation uses them. Decisions based on the unreconciled weights could target a logging artifact or miss a real queue. This risk is about measurement, not about suspected falsification.

# 4. Further process-mining analyses

Recompute everything from one `pm4py.format_dataframe` extract. Fix the case id, activity, timestamp, resource, and lifecycle columns. Decide a single handover semantics, preferably the resource of a `complete` followed by the next resource’s `start`. Keep Bot_Ingest, Bot_Rules, Vendor_Verify, and Vendor_Pay in a first run, then repeat without them, because bots inflate paths and vendors inflate time. Use the North/South mirror as a negative control: a real Mira effect should be cross-regional and should not appear as a difference between Alice’s clique and Bruno’s clique.

**Handover-of-work.** Run `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` on the full log and on four slices: exception_flag true, exception_flag false, the absence window, and the adjacent baseline weeks. Report raw counts beside any normalized centrality so edge weights cannot exceed the source resource’s event count. Add a resource–activity handover view (who performs which activity immediately before Mira, and which activity the receiver performs). Compare betweenness with bots included and excluded. Stratify Bot_Rules’ exits by region, case_type, priority, amount_band, and channel to see whether Mira’s inbound stream is a rule or a residue.

**Working-together.** Run `pm4py.discover_working_together_network` and publish three numbers per pair: shared-case count, share of each resource’s cases, and Jaccard. The present paradox cannot be interpreted further until those denominators are explicit. Restrict a second run to human resources and to cases with at least two human resources, so bots do not create universal weak ties. Separately, compute working-together only on the suffix **after** Mira’s event. If she truly drops out, adjusters and team leads still co-occur with each other in that suffix, and they co-occur with her only at the single routing event.

**Subcontracting.** Run `pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network` at n=2 and at a longer distance. n=2 tests the local sandwich seen here. A longer distance tests whether she ever resumes the case much later, which is the difference between a dispatcher and a case owner. Require the result to be case-level as well as occurrence-level, and reconcile it to the surplus of events over cases. Break sandwiches by the middle activity (Medical Review, Legal Review, Fraud Screen, Negotiate Settlement). Compare Mira’s sandwiches with Temp_1–Vendor_Verify and with Alice–Carla so the motif is benchmarked inside this same log.

**Activity-based similarity and roles.** Build the resource–activity matrix and run `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` plus `pm4py.discover_organizational_roles`. Expect a bot cluster, a vendor pair that may **not** cluster together because their activities differ, a team-lead pair, a senior-adjuster pair, a junior-adjuster pair, two specialist singletons, and Mira alone if Assign Specialist and Escalate Complaint are truly hers. Before mining, resolve Assign Specialist against the activity catalog. Repeat the clustering on exception cases only and on routine cases only. If she clusters with temps and juniors solely because of Request Missing Information, report that instability instead of forcing a role name.

**Filtering.** Use `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` on exception_flag, org:resource, concept:name (especially Escalate Complaint, Reopen Claim, Fraud Screen, Medical Review, Legal Review), region, and amount_band. Use `pm4py.filter_variants` to compare the variant mix of cases that touch Mira with those that do not. Time-filter to the six absence days and to matched non-absence days, and keep volume, mix, and staffing beside the cycle-time comparison. Define the flag’s write-time before interpreting her 48% exception share. A useful cut is “flag already true at Bot_Rules” versus “flag becomes true only at or after Mira.”

**Service and waiting time.** Use `pm4py.get_service_time` only where start and complete both exist; otherwise label the statistic as sojourn and do not call it labor. Use `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` and decompose each case into bot time, queue time until Mira, Mira service, customer wait after Request Missing Information, specialist service, adjuster service, team-lead approval, and vendor wait. The actionable test is whether exception p90 is queue time before Mira and idle time during her absence, rather than her service time. Repeat the decomposition for Vendor_Verify and Vendor_Pay so management does not “fix” the coordinator while document and payment waits dominate ordinary claims. `pm4py.view_sna` or `pm4py.save_vis_sna` is useful only after the raw and normalized networks are labeled, with bots either drawn or explicitly omitted.

# 5. Recommendation

**Change now.** Keep the desk, and stop staffing it with one person and no deputy. Name a backup who can perform the same routing acts, and put both names on a coverage calendar. Write a one-page assignment rubric for the choices that currently sit in her head: when a case goes back to a junior, when it goes to Carla or Diego, when it goes to Omar, when it goes to Priya, and when it returns to Bot_Rules. Log Assign Specialist as a real activity if the work is real, and log routing events with a single lifecycle rule so the next SNA extract is countable. For high amount-band, legal, and complaint cases, record the reason for the specialist choice and review a sample. That addresses segregation without accusing anyone. Tell the operation, in the same breath, that ordinary-claim waiting is still largely Vendor_Verify and Vendor_Pay; a coordinator rota will not move those clocks.

**Monitor.** Exception, complaint, and reopen queue age and WIP, not her personal betweenness. Exception-cycle-time p90 against coverage, not against her median service time. Destination mix to Omar, Priya, and the four adjusters, by region, priority, and amount_band, so discretion becomes visible as variation. Specialist queue time and arrival rate during coverage gaps, to separate a dam effect from overload. Repeat missing-information loops by resource, to see whether Mira, temps, and juniors are asking the same thing. North versus South outcome symmetry as a fairness control. Whether the frequency tables reconcile after the lifecycle fix; if they do not, pause further organizational interpretation.

**Leave unchanged until the evidence is better.** Do not remove Mira_Coordinator, and do not fold the queue into Alice and Bruno, on the current pack. The lost function would be the cross-team gate that routine regional leads do not appear to hold: complaint and reopen concentration, specialist wrapping, and temp fallout. Removal is only reasonable once a replacement is running — a staffed second coordinator, or a Bot_Rules policy that has been shown on historical cases to reproduce her destinations and to keep exception p90 stable. The six-day absence does not provide that demonstration. Do not push her work onto Omar and Priya to “cut a handoff”; their service times are already the long human activities, and a short absence is not a test of unfiltered specialist demand. Do not use handover betweenness as a KPI to be driven down. Someone, or some rule, has to sit between the bot and the exception path. Reduce **single-person** dependency; keep the coordination function until a compared alternative matches it.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira_Coordinator is the cross-team desk where automated triage sends claims that need a human routing decision: missing information, complaints, reopened files, and specialist review. She touches about 22% of claims and most complaints and reopened claims, yet each touch lasts about four minutes. Handover and subcontracting maps place her in the middle of many transfers. The working-together map places her on the edge because she does not keep a standing caseload inside one regional team, unlike the Alice–Carla and Bruno–Diego pairs. The two pictures describe one routing job. During a six-day absence, ordinary low-value claims kept their usual duration, while the slowest exceptional claims moved from 8.4 to 13.9 days. That is a localized dependency. It does not show that she slows the whole process, and it does not show misconduct. Document checks and payments still wait mainly on external vendors. Keep the desk staffed, name a deputy, and make specialist assignment reviewable. Defer removing the role until a second person or a clearer rule covers the same exception paths.
