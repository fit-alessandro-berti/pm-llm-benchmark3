# 1. Evidence Ledger

The 30 cases contain seven distinct completion sequences. The notation below uses **R** for Register Order, **C** for Credit Check, **E** for Engineering Review, **S** for Supplier Confirmation, **X** for Expedite Review, **A** for Approve Configuration, **L** for Release Production, and **Z** for Archive Case. In the variant rows, each middle word has the same prefix R and suffix A L Z.

| Evidence ID | What it supports | What it does not prove | Reliability | Modeling implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1 · C E S · 12 | The most frequent route has S after both checks. | A mandatory C-before-E or E-before-S rule. | High for the recorded word, strongest count. | Admit this sequence in every candidate. |
| V2 · E C S · 7 | E can appear first with S still following both checks. | That the checks overlapped, or that order was freely chosen. | High for the recorded word. | A universal C-before-E completion sequence would lose these cases. |
| V3 · C E · 4 | A can immediately follow E with no S recorded. | That all four orders were stocked or that S was unnecessary. | High for the word, uncertain for the omission. | Provide a visible no-S route. |
| V4 · E C · 3 | The no-S pattern also occurs with E first. | Why S is absent, or that its absence is statistically independent of check order. | High for the word, uncertain for the omission. | Let the no-S route replay both check orders. |
| V5 · C S E · 2 | S can complete between C and E in the export. | That C enabled S, or that E had yet to start. | Two observations, timing resolution matters. | Do not require both check completions before every visible S. |

Both directions in a directly-follows graph are consistent with concurrency, two strictly sequential routes chosen by order type, or ingestion ordering of ties. For example, V1 and V2 alone would produce both C→E and E→C even if every individual order followed a predetermined sequence. Nineteen cases have C before E, including two with S in between, so even the count of C-before-E cases differs from the C→E edge count.

# 2. Shared Assumptions

- A word lists one case's visible **completions** in exported order. We model completed, registered cases over the eight supplied labels. A precedence edge compares visible events. An unordered pair permits both linearizations. Neither supplies start times or durations.
- All models use one R, C, E, A, L, and Z per completed word. All middle work lies between R and A, and the suffix is A L Z. This combines D1–D4 and D6 with an empirical one-pass, fixed-suffix assumption. D2 alone would allow intervening work.
- There are 23 visible S events, 21 after both checks and two between them. Seven cases have no visible S. An order with no external component might legitimately skip S. An external order might undergo S and lose its event. The missing external/stocked attribute prevents assigning these explanations per case. The estimated 5–10% concerns S **events** at risk of loss, whereas seven out of 30 concerns **cases** with no visible event.
- Each optional label appears zero or one time. Optionality is encoded by alternative words, route choice, or an exclusive choice with a silent step τ. A visible bypass permits replay of an incomplete record. It conveys no authorization to bypass a required check in production. There are no inferred probabilities on branches. The counts weight conformance measurements.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

**Inductive bias.** Treat the seven observed words as a catalogue of admissible records. Sharing the boundaries avoids repeating identical work at the ends. Keeping each entire middle word intact preserves the association between check order, S position, and X position, even when the association comes from just one case.

**Expression.** R · XOR(C E S, E C S, C E, E C, C S E, C E S X, E C X S) · A · L · Z. Juxtaposition and · denote sequence.

```python
import pm4py

def make_conservative():
    labels = {"C": "Credit Check", "E": "Engineering Review",
              "S": "Supplier Confirmation", "X": "Expedite Review"}
    words = ("CES", "ECS", "CE", "EC", "CSE", "CESX", "ECXS")
    branches = ["->(" + ",".join(repr(labels[k]) for k in w) + ")" for w in words]
    choice = "X(" + ",".join(branches) + ")"
    return pm4py.parse_process_tree(
        f"->('Register Order',{choice},'Approve Configuration','Release Production','Archive Case')"
    )

model_conservative = make_conservative()
```

The XOR branches have distinct transition instances with duplicate activity labels. For example, replay may need to consider several occurrences of C after R. The labels describe the same business activity across alternative routes. One branch per variant grows with the number of distinct words, and independent activities can generate factorially many such words. Here, seven branches are still auditable.

**Behavior deliberately excluded**

- X in any context beyond its two observed words, including C E X S.
- S immediately after an initial E, any repeat, and any altered boundary or approval suffix.

**Known underfitting risks** concern constraints the model fails to express. Its complementary **overfitting risk** is rejecting a legitimate new word merely because it was absent from 30 cases.

- All seven words are available to every order. A real guard might authorize the no-S route only for certain component sources.
- An ingestion artifact or policy violation observed once becomes an admissible record. The catalogue cannot tell an exception from an authorized execution.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

**Inductive bias.** Preserve the directional regularity with the most supplier evidence and generalize the ordering that has explicit support from compliance. In all 23 supplier records C precedes S, while S can be on either side of E. Both X cases contain S. I provisionally retain that co-occurrence and relax the accidental association between check order and S/X order. A well-audited X case without S would challenge the co-occurrence guard.

**Expression.** R · XOR(C ∥ E, PO({C,E,S,opt(X)}, {C≺S, C≺opt(X), E≺opt(X)})) · A · L · Z. Here opt(X) = XOR(τ,X), ∥ admits either interleaving, and PO admits every linear extension of its edges. On the supplier branch, even when X is skipped the silent alternative adds no visible event.

- **C and E** are unordered in both branches. Both occur in every word and both orders recur with and without S. This gives the completion language of a parallel block while leaving actual overlap open under D7.
- **S** has C as a predecessor on the supplier branch. Requiring E too would reject V5. Allowing S after only E extrapolates beyond every supplier case. An unordered E/S pair captures the distinction without parallelizing all three activities.
- **X** follows both C and E by D6/S6. On the supplier branch there is no edge between S and X, so either can complete first when their own predecessors are satisfied. X is unavailable on the no-S branch. That branch coupling is a low-confidence precision choice, separate from the compliance prerequisites.
- **A** follows the entire chosen branch. If S or X is visible it must finish before approval.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

def make_balanced():
    def checks():
        return Transition("Credit Check"), Transition("Engineering Review")

    no_supplier = StrictPartialOrder(list(checks()))
    c, e = checks()
    s = Transition("Supplier Confirmation")
    opt_x = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [SilentTransition(), Transition("Expedite Review")])
    with_supplier = StrictPartialOrder([c, e, s, opt_x])
    for before, after in [(c, s), (c, opt_x), (e, opt_x)]:
        with_supplier.order.add_edge(before, after)

    mid = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [no_supplier, with_supplier])
    stages = [Transition("Register Order"), mid, Transition("Approve Configuration"),
              Transition("Release Production"), Transition("Archive Case")]
    root = StrictPartialOrder(stages)
    for i, before in enumerate(stages):
        for after in stages[i + 1:]:
            root.order.add_edge(before, after)
    return root

model_balanced = make_balanced()
```

**Behavior deliberately excluded**

- E S C, because C has yet to complete when S is recorded.
- An X case without a visible S, even when X otherwise satisfies D6. This can reject a legitimate record with a lost S event.

**Known underfitting risks**

- The three new combinations in section 6 assume that timing and expedite eligibility do not impose a further route-specific order. The two X observations are too few to establish that freedom.
- The branches omit component-level sourcing and urgency guards. They can accept a missing required S as an ordinary no-S record. Conversely, the C-before-S rule and the X/S coupling can overfit the sample.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

**Inductive bias.** Generalize symmetrically from D5. Once either principal check has completed, S may appear while the other check is pending or later. Decide independently whether S and X are visible. This includes both an expedite on a stocked order and an expedite on an external order whose S event was lost. The same visible word can represent either explanation.

**Expression.** Let F(i,j) = i · ((j · opt(X)) ∥ opt(S)). The model is R · XOR(F(C,E), F(E,C)) · A · L · Z. The XOR identifies the first **recorded completion**. It requires the other check later in the word. After the first check, S can interleave with the other check and its optional X. This implements “at least one check before visible S” without accidentally enabling S at registration.

```python
import pm4py

def make_permissive():
    def first_then_rest(first, other):
        after_first = (f"+(->('{other}',X(tau,'Expedite Review')),"
                       "X(tau,'Supplier Confirmation'))")
        return f"->('{first}',{after_first})"

    c, e = "Credit Check", "Engineering Review"
    mid = f"X({first_then_rest(c, e)},{first_then_rest(e, c)})"
    return pm4py.parse_process_tree(
        f"->('Register Order',{mid},'Approve Configuration','Release Production','Archive Case')"
    )

model_permissive = make_permissive()
```

**Behavior deliberately excluded**

- S as the first middle completion. The chosen proxy for “enough information” is one visible check completion. D5 could also describe information produced earlier, a possibility outside this model.
- X while either required check is still outstanding, a repeated S or X, and approval before outstanding visible work. Either check finishing first, S occurring on either side of the second check, and X appearing without visible S are the extent of the generalization.

**Known underfitting risks**

- It admits S after E alone despite all 23 visible S events following C. Finance sign-off or a supplier-specific milestone could make this ordering impossible.
- It admits X without visible S for either check order. Expedite work might be supplier-specific, and the model has no way to require loss evidence or an urgency designation for such a record.

# 7. Comparative Model Matrix

| Criterion | Conservative | Balanced | Permissive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expected fitness on the 30 cases | Full visible replay by enumeration | Full visible replay, including C S E | Full visible replay through the appropriate first-check branch |
| Expected precision risk | Lowest against this log, but a logged error becomes allowed | Intermediate, adds three recombinations | Highest, adds seven words including S after E alone and X without S |
| Generalization potential | Low for new words | Moderate, recombines supported relative orders | Broadest within the one-pass assumptions |
| Structural simplicity | Simple XOR concept, duplicated leaves across seven branches | Two branches and three internal precedence edges on the supplier branch | Compact symmetric rule, two mirrored subtrees with optional parallel work |
| Explainability | Easy to match an example, little explanation for its constraints | Each edge has a stated rationale and each provisional guard can be challenged | Clear eligibility rules, with wider uncertainty about actual eligibility |
| Robustness to missing S | Fits the two observed no-S words, rejects any X word that loses S | Same limitation when X is present | Deleting S from any accepted word preserves acceptance, which can conceal a genuine breach |
| Handling unseen interleavings | Rejects all | Admits those preserving C-before-S and the X/S coupling | Admits either check as S predecessor and either S/X order subject to X's prerequisites |
| Compliance versus exploration | Historical reference and novelty detection. Rare legitimate behavior can raise alarms. | Provisional conformance triage with human review of uncertain guards | Sensitivity analysis for timing and event loss. Acceptance gives weak evidence of compliance. |

All three can explain the training cases. Net simplicity and token-replay generalization depend on the encoding, including duplicate or silent transitions. Neither estimates the probability of an unseen word directly.

# 8. Validation Code

This standalone script includes the three constructors. It reconstructs and checks the case multiplicities, converts each candidate to an accepting net, runs the four metrics, and plays out all enabled visible and silent transitions through the stated bound. The manually specified suite checks both shared requirements and the points where the candidates disagree.

```python
import pm4py

def make_conservative():

from collections import Counter, deque
from pm4py.objects.petri_net import semantics

names = dict(zip("RCESXALZ", ["Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review",
                              "Supplier Confirmation", "Expedite Review", "Approve Configuration",
                              "Release Production", "Archive Case"]))
symbols = {name: k for k, name in names.items()}
counts = {"RCESALZ": 12, "RECSALZ": 7, "RCEALZ": 4, "RECALZ": 3,
          "RCSEALZ": 2, "RCESXALZ": 1, "RECXSALZ": 1}
strings = [",".join(names[k] for k in word) for word, n in counts.items() for _ in range(n)]
log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(strings)
event_log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(log)
reconstructed = Counter("".join(symbols[ev["concept:name"]] for ev in case) for case in event_log)
assert len(event_log) == 30 and reconstructed == counts

def bounded_playout(net, im, fm, limit=8):
    queue = deque([(im, "")])
    visited, complete = set(), set()
    while queue:
        marking, word = queue.popleft()
        state = (frozenset(marking.items()), word)
        if state in visited:
            continue
        visited.add(state)
        if marking == fm:
            complete.add(word)
            continue
        for t in semantics.enabled_transitions(net, marking):
            if t.label is not None and len(word) == limit:
                continue
            nxt = semantics.execute(t, net, marking)
            extension = "" if t.label is None else symbols[t.label]
            queue.append((nxt, word + extension))
    return complete

# Expected memberships in conservative, balanced, permissive order.

    "RECALZ":   (True,  True,  True),    # no recorded supplier
    "RECXSALZ": (True,  True,  True),    # observed X before S
    "RCEXSALZ": (False, True,  True),    # X before S with C first
    "RECSXALZ": (False, True,  True),    # X after S with E first
    "RCSEXALZ": (False, True,  True),    # S before E, X after E
    "RESCALZ":  (False, False, True),    # S after only E
    "RCEXALZ":  (False, False, True),    # X without visible S
    "RSCEALZ":  (False, False, False),   # S first
    "RCXESALZ": (False, False, False),   # X too early
    "RCALZ":    (False, False, False),   # approval without E
    "RCELAZ":   (False, False, False),   # release before approval
    "RCESSALZ": (False, False, False),   # repeated S
    "RCEXXALZ": (False, False, False),   # repeated X
    "RCEAL":    (False, False, False),   # incomplete suffix

          ("permissive", model_permissive)]
languages = []
for i, (name, model) in enumerate(models):
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    sound, diagnostics = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=False)
    print(name, "sound:", sound)
    assert sound, diagnostics
    for metric, f, args in [
        ("fitness", pm4py.fitness_alignments, (event_log, net, im, fm)),
        ("precision", pm4py.precision_alignments, (event_log, net, im, fm)),
        ("generalization", pm4py.generalization_tbr, (event_log, net, im, fm)),
        ("simplicity", pm4py.simplicity_petri_net, (net, im, fm)),
    ]:
        print(name, metric, f(*args))
    accepted = bounded_playout(net, im, fm)
    assert set(counts) <= accepted, (name, set(counts) - accepted)
    for word, expected in suite.items():
        assert (word in accepted) == expected[i], (name, word, expected[i])
    print(name, "additional complete words:", sorted(accepted - set(counts)))
    languages.append(accepted)
assert languages[0] == set(counts) and languages[0] < languages[1] < languages[2]
assert [len(lang) for lang in languages] == [7, 10, 14]
```

With PM4Py 2.7.23.4, all three nets passed the soundness check, all 30 cases were classified as fitting by alignment, and all membership assertions passed. Playout counts only words reaching the final marking. It keeps the prefix as well as the marking so distinct words reaching the same state survive. The structures are acyclic, every case has at most eight visible events, and the nets have finite reachable markings. The bound therefore covers these entire visible languages. For a model with unbounded visible repetition the same finite horizon would answer only bounded questions. Sampled playout alone cannot establish language inclusion.

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

Use **balanced** immediately for conformance triage. It replays the early-supplier cases and respects the documented prerequisites for approval and expedite. Flag a novel order separately from an evidenced policy breach. In particular, a no-S record needs sourcing and integration evidence for a compliance verdict. Use **permissive** for sensitivity analysis. Its four additional words beyond balanced test whether S can finish after E alone and whether an X case can have no visible S.

| Ambiguity | What to collect | Observation that would discriminate |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed C→E sequence, route-specific XOR, or overlapping checks | Synchronized start and complete times, precision and tie flags, work-item IDs, enable/dispatch times, routing decision, policy version, order type and team. Separate ingestion time from event time. | A verified E start before C finishes contradicts S1 as a universal rule. Positive-duration intersection of the C and E execution intervals establishes overlap in that case. Disjoint intervals in opposite orders predicted by a pre-existing route attribute support XOR over sequences. Disjoint intervals alone can also result from scheduling independent checks. |
| Which check enables S | Supplier request, dispatch and confirmation times, prerequisite sign-offs, and the time partial specifications became available. Link the records to the same component and order. | A verified R E S C A L Z challenges balanced. An S request after an E milestone but before C completion tests the start rule even if S finishes last. A verified S completion before both check completions challenges the proxy in permissive as well. |
| Legitimate bypass versus missing S | Per-component sourcing, inventory and external purchase orders, required-S decision, source-system confirmation ID and timestamp, export receipt, failure/retry status. Reconcile source to export. | A stocked-only case with an authorized bypass supports true optionality. For an external case, a confirmation present at source and absent in the export establishes event loss. An external case with neither record warrants review of actual performance. Estimate loss among source confirmations, stratified by integration path. |
| X/S coupling and S/X order | Expedite trigger, reason, eligibility policy, sourced components, and both teams' source timestamps. Target expedited cases prospectively because only two are present. | An audited stocked case R C E X A L Z with a legitimate supplier bypass supports operational independence. The same export with S recovered at source supports a measurement explanation. R C E X S A L Z or R E C S X A L Z distinguishes balanced from enumeration. |

Freeze the candidates before examining a new chronological batch. Test the rare distinctions in targeted cases and report their prevalence separately on the full batch. Compare held-out replay, the first offending event, and source-audited false alarms by order type and tie status. Use the audited cases to decide whether to remove an edge, add a routing guard, or repair the export.

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

| Modeling decision | Confidence and basis |
|---|---|
