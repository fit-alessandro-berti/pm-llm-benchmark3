# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

**Recommendation in brief:** use the hybrid policy as a **provisional P1-protection policy**, not as evidence that the supplied staffing can meet the targets. Under the midpoint service model, even the base workload exceeds available capacity before speed, setup, and restart penalties. Full pooling is preferable under an objective that places more weight on overall delay and P2 performance. Neither policy has demonstrated SLA compliance.

## Assumptions

**A1 — Demand.** For the simulation, arrivals in each block are Poisson with the supplied expectation; arrival times are uniform within that block. Priority and case type are independent conditional on the arrival block. These are modelling assumptions, not supplied observations.

For U1/U5, the extra eight percentage points of P1 demand are taken proportionally from P2 and P3. For U2, the primary interpretation is **unchanged arrival counts with doubled E/ES weights followed by renormalization**. A second, materially different interpretation—doubling absolute E/ES demand—is analysed separately.

**A2 — Service distributions.** Each activity independently draws an ordinary/tail indicator with the supplied probability, then draws uniformly from the applicable range. Thus,

$$
\mu_a=(1-p_a)\frac{l_a+u_a}{2}
       +p_a\frac{l_a^{tail}+u_a^{tail}}{2}.
$$

A resource multiplier multiplies active service, not the seven-minute handover delay. Independent activity tails are an important assumption: correlated complexity across a case could materially worsen end-to-end tails.

**A3 — SLA clock and experimental horizon.** Completion means finishing Validation. The SLA clock starts at **external arrival**, including waiting for Intake, outages, breaks, and overnight delays.

The simulation is a **one-day arrival-cohort experiment**: no initial backlog; shocks affect that day; subsequent normal shifts drain unfinished work without new arrivals. Chen returns on the next day in U3/U5. Consequently, these are neither steady-state results nor realistic recovery estimates under continuing demand. Unfinished cases are not discarded.

**A4 — Coordination semantics.** Handover delays consume elapsed time but not worker service. Each changed-resource predecessor edge incurs seven minutes; parallel transfer delays can overlap rather than being summed onto the critical path. Because home-team assignments are unspecified, Intake and Validation are treated as team-neutral; a split-resource ME diagnosis incurs the extra five minutes once at its join. Same-resource sequential ME avoids it.

Setup consumes worker time when their diagnostic family differs from the previous case’s diagnostic family. Intake/Validation do not reset that history. Switching branches within the same sequential case does not incur the *between-cases* setup penalty.

**A5 — Interruptions and fairness.** Breaks, shift endings, and the IT outage pause affected work without the P1 restart penalty. Only a P1-induced diagnostic interruption invokes that penalty. Paused work remains with its assigned worker. The simulator uses a one-minute grid, rounding sampled activity durations and restart additions upward.

Utilization is active service, restart work, and setup divided by **regular availability net of breaks**. Overtime remains in the numerator, so utilization can exceed 100%. The 90% threshold is a planning warning and routing penalty in the prototype—not a falsely claimed hard constraint.

## Objectives

Let \(F_i\) be arrival-to-completion time, \(T_{p_i}\) the applicable target, and \(Z_i=F_i/T_{p_i}\).

A **delay-and-tail objective** could be:

$$
J_{\text{flow}}(\pi)=
\sum_p w_p\left[
E_\pi[Z\mid p]
+\lambda_pP_\pi(Z>1\mid p)
+\eta_p\operatorname{CVaR}_{.95}\big((Z-1)^+\mid p\big)
\right].
$$

This avoids choosing a policy solely on mean delay.

A **robust, P1-first objective**, used for the provisional recommendation, is lexicographic:

$$
\operatorname{lexmin}_{\pi}
\left(
\max_{s\in\{B,U1,\ldots,U5\}}P_{\pi,s}(Z>1\mid P1),
\quad
\max_s \operatorname{CVaR}_{.95}(Z),
\quad
\max_s \Phi_{\pi,s}
\right),
$$

where, for example,

$$
\Phi_{\pi,s}=
\sum_r[(u_{r,s}-.90)^+]^2+
\gamma\,CV(u_{\cdot,s})^2.
$$

The scenario probabilities are unspecified, making minimax comparison more defensible than inventing scenario weights. Fairness could instead be imposed as a hard base-case constraint; under the midpoint model, that would require a staffing/capacity remedy rather than merely different routing.

**Decision variables** are stage-resource assignment, queue order, sequential versus parallel diagnosis, protected P1 capacity, overflow authorization, preemption, and overtime use. All three simulated policies use the same supplied roster.

## Skill availability matters more than aggregate headcount

A useful single-skill capacity envelope is

$$
C_{a,b}^{upper}=\sum_{r:\,r\text{ qualified for }a}
\frac{\text{available minutes}_{r,b}}{m_{r,a}}.
$$

These envelopes **cannot be added across skills**: doing so would count a cross-skilled worker repeatedly.

For example, before noon Software capacity is Chen’s \(120/1.25=96\) plus Farah’s \(120/1.4=85.7\) base minutes per block. Allocating either person to Intake, Validation, or Electrical reduces that capacity.

Chen’s absence exposes an additional coupling. In U3, the 08–10 Software cohort needs about \(67.4(1.4)=94.3\) Farah-minutes. Her remaining 25.7 minutes provide only 18.3 Electrical base minutes. Even dedicating all Ada’s time to Electrical adds only \(120/1.35=88.9\), giving **107.2 versus 126.4 required Electrical minutes**—before any Mechanical, Intake, or Validation work. Separate skill totals would conceal this infeasibility.

Late coverage is equally important. After 18:00, Emil is the only regular-shift worker and cannot perform Mechanical work. Deepa’s overtime can extend Mechanical coverage only to 18:30. Intake plus Validation alone represent about **204 of the 330 regular staff-minutes available from 16:00–20:00**, before diagnostics and earlier backlog.

# 3. Candidate policies

## Common execution rules

Assignment occurs when a task becomes enabled and is binding until completion. Forecasts use mean remaining work, current queues, calendars, multipliers, handover delay, and setup. The dispatcher does not inspect the simulated latent service draw.

For all policies, only active lower-priority diagnostics can be preempted. The prototype triggers preemption when a waiting P1 task’s **coarse urgency proxy**—current task, Validation, and two handovers—has less than 15 minutes of slack, the victim has not previously been preempted, and its estimated remaining work exceeds 5.5 minutes. Actual restart work is 10% of actual remaining active service, rounded upward. This proxy is explicitly not a calibrated end-to-end tail forecast.

## S — Strict diagnostic specialization with fixed skill queues

Mechanical is restricted to Ada/Ben; Electrical to Chen/Deepa; Software to Emil. Separate Intake and Validation queues can use any qualified worker. Sharing these mandatory stages avoids constructing an artificially crippled policy with no front/back coverage after Farah leaves.

Cases are ordered P1, P2, P3, then arrival order. Within an eligible pool, choose the earliest predicted completion. There is no diagnostic cross-skill overflow or P1 reservation. ME/ES are split between their specialist queues and incur parallel coordination, even when queues prevent actual overlap.

Its strength is efficient specialist service and limited skill switching. Its weaknesses are unusable cross-skills, no morning Software specialist, and late Electrical work that cannot use Emil’s Electrical qualification.

## P — Full pooling with earliest predicted completion

Every task can use any qualified resource. Dispatch remains P1/P2/P3 with earliest deadline within class; with the supplied fixed class targets, this is equivalent to FIFO within class.

Choose the resource with earliest predicted completion, including speed, queue, calendar, handover, and setup effects. For ME/ES, compare same-resource sequential alternatives against different-resource parallel alternatives, including coordination and ME team delay. Choose the lower forecast. There is no reservation or fairness surcharge.

Pooling can reduce stranded capacity, but “qualified” does not mean “equally productive.” Slow overflow can consume the minutes it was intended to save.

## H — Hybrid with borrowable P1 reserve and controlled overflow

Use the specialist groups as primary pools. Open all qualified resources when the predicted primary-pool wait exceeds **30 minutes**, or immediately for P1.

The fastest available Software-qualified worker is the protected P1 cover: normally Chen before noon and Emil after noon; Farah supplies morning cover during Chen’s absence. After becoming idle, this worker can hold off starting non-P1 diagnostic work for up to **10 minutes**. The reserve is then released rather than left permanently idle. Intake/Validation remain possible.

For non-P1 assignments, add a fairness surcharge of **three forecast minutes per percentage point of planned utilization above 90%**. P1 bypasses that surcharge. P1 always ranks first; non-P1 work uses deadlines, with a two-hour ageing credit for P3 after four elapsed hours. This reduces, but does not guarantee elimination of, starvation.

Choose parallel diagnosis only if its routing score improves by at least **15 minutes**, unless no eligible same-resource sequential option exists. The score includes coordination, team delay, and fairness penalties.

## Parallelism is not free—and sequential ownership is not always cheaper

At plug-in mean service times, sequential ES on Chen requires

$$
32.82+1.25(38.5)=80.95\text{ active minutes}.
$$

Electrical on Chen and Software on Emil gives a diagnostic critical-path estimate of

$$
\max(32.82,38.5)+8=46.5\text{ minutes},
$$

before handovers and Validation. It also uses less active labor than slow sequential Software on Chen, but occupies both scarce resources simultaneously.

For ME, sequential work on Deepa takes about **65.70 active minutes**. Fast specialist branches have a plug-in join time of \(32.82+8+5=45.82\) minutes before transfer effects.

These are **comparative planning approximations**, not expected parallel durations: generally, \(E[\max(X,Y)]\neq\max(E[X],E[Y])\). The simulator uses realized branch completion times.

# 5. Robust recommendation

**Choose H provisionally when the explicit objective is worst-scenario P1 protection.** Its worst observed scenario P1 attainment is about 59%, versus 52% for P and 44% for S. It also avoids the large utilization imbalance of strict specialization. However, the paired uncertainty interval does not establish statistical superiority over pooling.

**Do not approve any of these configurations as a demonstrated SLA-compliant normal operating policy.** Under A2, the base roster is already under-capacity, and none of the simulated policies reaches the P1 target.

To support the midpoint workload while preserving a 90% allowance, the speed-free capacity calculation alone requires at least

$$
2720.6/.90-2490 \approx 533
$$

**additional net available staff-minutes per day**, before speed, setup, restart, and temporal-coverage effects. This is a lower-bound planning calculation, not a complete staffing prescription. Extra capacity must include morning E/S coverage, late Mechanical coverage, and sufficient Intake/Validation capacity; adding only early Mechanical capacity would not resolve the principal gaps.

The operational thresholds should be explicit:

| Trigger                                                            | Action                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Primary-pool predicted wait exceeds 30 minutes                     | Authorize qualified cross-skill overflow                                       |
| P1 task becomes eligible                                           | Immediately consider all qualified resources                                   |
| Protected cover has waited 10 minutes without starting urgent work | Release the borrowable reserve                                                 |
| Parallel score improves by at least 15 minutes                     | Permit a split diagnosis, subject to actual availability                       |
| Any projected individual utilization exceeds 90%                   | Raise a capacity/fairness exception; do not silently normalize overload        |
| Work requires an unavailable skill before its deadline             | Escalate immediately rather than repeatedly reprioritizing an infeasible queue |
| Overtime requirement exceeds 30 minutes for a resource             | Reject that plan and escalate capacity/commitments                             |

The base forecast already activates the capacity exception; it is not something to wait for U5 to reveal.

**Fallback:** use P when overall delay, P2 attainment, or recovery speed has higher weight, or when reservation demonstrably consumes scarce capacity without protecting an imminent P1 need. P has lower mean delay in most tested scenarios and better P2 performance than H in several scenarios.

Strict specialization is defensible where specialist service efficiency dominates and coverage is adequate. Its better U1 mean delay illustrates that pooling is not automatically superior under overload. Its morning/late coverage deficiencies make it unsuitable as the general robust choice here.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

**Estimate gaming.** Short-looking cases may obtain faster service or overflow permission. Freeze estimates at assignment, retain every revision, and compare prediction errors by activity, type, resource, and policy. Do not interpret a worker’s adverse case mix as poor performance.

**P3 starvation.** Ageing credits improve ordering but cannot manufacture capacity. Track P3 deadline slack and unfinished cohorts explicitly. Escalate cases approaching their 24-hour target rather than allowing repeated P1 overrides to remain invisible.

**Skill erosion and learning.** Strict specialization preserves speed but can weaken backup capability. Conversely, indiscriminate pooling can impose a sustained 25–40% service penalty. Development assignments should be deliberately identified, supervised, and separated from urgent overflow; no learning benefit has been assumed in the simulation.

**Excessive preemption.** One preemption per victim case limits repeated damage, not total disruption across many victims. Monitor restart minutes, setup after interruption, victim SLA changes, and P1 benefit. The prototype’s coarse trigger needs replacement with a validated remaining-case forecast before operational reliance.

**Nominal rather than useful parallelism.** A split whose second branch waits until the first finishes pays coordination without gaining overlap. Record actual overlap and the fraction of splits that reduce predicted completion time.

**Validation migration.** Validation contributes more expected work than either Mechanical or Software. Completing more diagnoses can merely move the queue downstream. Reserve rules and dashboards must include Validation, not stop at diagnostic completion.

**Fairness and interpretability.** Publish both utilization level and dispersion. A worker performing complex overflow should not be compared through raw completion counts. Each assignment should expose a small explanation: qualification, deadline/priority, predicted completion, continuity cost, and any overflow or fairness exception.

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design

Use three linked datasets: an **interval execution log**, an **assignment/lifecycle audit**, and explicit **causal handover edges**. One event per completed activity is insufficient for this process.

The execution log should contain the required case/activity/resource timestamps plus `task_id`, `branch_id`, `skill_used`, case type/priority, planned service, actual active service, setup intervals, and the diagnostic mode. Each uninterrupted active segment should be identifiable, so a pause is not counted as service.

The decision audit should retain queue-entry time, eligibility time, assignment-decision time, the complete eligible-resource set, exclusion reasons, candidate scores, selected resource, workload/calendar snapshot, reserve status, assignment reason, policy version, scenario/shock flag, and override author. Pause/resume records need their cause, remaining service estimate, actual restart addition, and case-level preemption count.

Handover records need source/target task and resource, initiation/completion timestamps, seven-minute charge, team-crossing charge, and whether overlapping transfers occurred. SLA attributes should include external arrival, absolute deadline, elapsed clock, and slack **at the decision**, not just final outcome.

**Deployment indicators** should distinguish:

* arrival-to-completion distributions and SLA attainment by priority, type, arrival block, and shock;
* task queue waiting from eligibility to resource acquisition, including subsequent queue episodes;
* active service, setup, handover, coordination, calendar interruption, and restart burden;
* individual planned/actual utilization, overtime, reserve use, and overflow;
* unfinished workload, oldest deadline slack, actual branch overlap, and recovery under continuing arrivals.

`pm4py.format_dataframe` should be given both completion and start timestamp keys; simply formatting completion timestamps does not reconstruct missing lifecycle intervals. ([GitHub][1])

`pm4py.get_service_time` is appropriate for interval duration summaries. On a segmented execution log its mean is a **mean segment duration**, not a whole-task mean. Aggregate segments by `task_id` separately to obtain total active service per task. ([GitHub][2])

A critical semantic warning concerns `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time`: its case service quantity sums event interval durations, while sojourn spans the earliest included start to latest completion; its waiting quantity is their difference. With parallel branches, summed labor can exceed elapsed time, making that difference negative. It also excludes external-arrival-to-first-start waiting unless that boundary is explicitly represented. These outputs are **not branch-specific queue waits**. ([GitHub][3])

A separate union-of-active-intervals calculation can measure elapsed time with no case activity, but that still is not queue waiting: one branch may queue while another works. Queue metrics require the explicit eligibility and acquisition timestamps.

`pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` is useful as an exploratory completion-order resource network, not as definitive evidence of physical transfers. Parallel siblings and resumed segments can create misleading succession. Validate it against the explicit causal handover table. ([GitHub][4])

For drift and bottleneck migration, compare type/priority/time-block cohorts using service summaries, queue distributions, and performance DFGs on logical-task views. Use working-together networks for collaboration and activity-based similarity/organizational roles for changes in work allocation; inferred roles must not authorize new skills. Batch discovery can identify setup-saving opportunities, but apparent batches may also reflect release bursts after the outage. These are diagnostic analyses, not causal proof that a routing policy produced an improvement. ([GitHub][5])

# Minutes relative to 08:00. -1 denotes no break.
SHIFTS = pd.DataFrame(
    [[0, 480, 255, 285], [0, 480, 240, 270], [0, 480, 270, 300],
     [120, 600, 360, 390], [240, 720, 480, 510], [0, 240, -1, -1]],
    index=SPEED.index,
    columns=["start", "end", "break_start", "break_end"]
)
DEMAND = pd.DataFrame(
    [[7, 10, 55, 35, 30, 25, 15, 20, 10],
     [9, 15, 55, 30, 25, 25, 15, 25, 10],
     [7, 12, 58, 30, 22, 28, 18, 20, 12],
     [10, 18, 52, 30, 20, 30, 18, 20, 12],
     [6, 10, 50, 40, 20, 25, 25, 15, 15],
     [3, 5, 45, 50, 25, 20, 30, 10, 15]],
    columns=["arrivals", "P1", "P2", "P3", "M", "E", "S", "ME", "ES"]
)
SCENARIOS = pd.DataFrame(
    [["Base", 1, 0, False, 1, 1, False],
     ["U1", 1.7, 8, False, 1, 1, False],
     ["U2", 1, 0, False, 2, 1, False],
     ["U3", 1, 0, True, 1, 1, False],
     ["U4", 1, 0, False, 1, 2, False],
     ["U5", 1.7, 8, True, 1, 1, True]],
    columns=["scenario", "morning_factor", "P1_extra_pp", "Chen_absent",
             "E_ES_weight", "diagnostic_tail_factor", "IT_outage"]
).set_index("scenario")

RULE = dict(
    handover_delay=7, ME_team_delay=5, diagnostic_setup=5,
    parallel_coordination=8, restart_fraction=.10, max_preemptions=1,
    overtime=30, fair_utilization=.90, overflow_wait=30,
    parallel_gain=15, reserve_hold=10, urgent_slack=15
)
TYPES = {
    "M": ["Mechanical"], "E": ["Electrical"], "S": ["Software"],
    "ME": ["Mechanical", "Electrical"],
    "ES": ["Electrical", "Software"]
}
PRIMARY = {
    "Mechanical": ["Ada", "Ben"],
    "Electrical": ["Chen", "Deepa"],
    "Software": ["Emil"]
}
SLA = {1: 120, 2: 480, 3: 1440}
POLICIES = ["Spec", "Pool", "Hybrid"]
EPOCH = pd.Timestamp("2026-09-07 08:00", tz="Europe/Berlin")


def inputs(scenario, u2_volume=False):
    """u2_volume=True implements the alternative absolute-demand interpretation."""
    d = DEMAND.astype(float).copy()
    s = SERVICE.copy()
    z = SCENARIOS.loc[scenario]
    d.loc[:1, "arrivals"] *= z.morning_factor
    for b in [0, 1]:
        old = d.loc[b, "P1"]
        d.loc[b, ["P2", "P3"]] *= (100-old-z.P1_extra_pp)/(100-old)
        d.loc[b, "P1"] += z.P1_extra_pp
    kinds = list(TYPES)
    for b in [2, 3]:
        d.loc[b, ["E", "ES"]] *= z.E_ES_weight
        total = d.loc[b, kinds].sum()
        if u2_volume:
            d.loc[b, "arrivals"] *= total/100
        d.loc[b, kinds] *= 100/total
    s.loc[DIAG, "tail_p"] *= z.diagnostic_tail_factor
    s["mean"] = (
        (1-s.tail_p)*(s.lo+s.hi) +
        s.tail_p*(s.tail_lo+s.tail_hi)
    )/2
    return d, s, z


def simulate(scenario, policy, seed, u2_volume=False, days=5, emit_log=True):
    d, svc, shock = inputs(scenario, u2_volume)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    speed = SPEED.to_dict("index")
    mu = svc["mean"].to_dict()
    cases, tasks, segments, edges = [], [], [], []
    last_segment, audit = {}, []
    shift_end = SHIFTS["end"].to_dict()

    for b, row in d.iterrows():
        for _ in range(rng.poisson(row.arrivals)):
            arrival = int(120*b + rng.integers(120))
            priority = int(rng.choice(
                [1, 2, 3],
                p=row[["P1", "P2", "P3"]].to_numpy(float)/100
            ))
            kind = rng.choice(
                list(TYPES), p=row[list(TYPES)].to_numpy(float)/100
            )
            draws = {}
            # Draw all latent work before routing: matched work across policies.
            for a in ACTS:
                v = svc.loc[a]
                lo, hi = (
                    (v.tail_lo, v.tail_hi)
                    if rng.random() < v.tail_p else (v.lo, v.hi)
                )
                draws[a] = rng.uniform(lo, hi)
            cases.append(dict(
                id=str(len(cases)), arrival=arrival, p=priority, kind=kind,
                due=arrival+SLA[priority], draws=draws, finish=None,
                preemptions=0, mode="single", diag=[], seq_next=None
            ))
    cases.sort(key=lambda c: (c["arrival"], c["id"]))

    horizon = days*1440
    calendars, available, regular, busy, overtime = {}, {}, {}, {}, {}
    running, last_diag, idle_since = {}, {}, {}
    for r, sh in SHIFTS.iterrows():
        intervals = []
        av = np.zeros(horizon, dtype=bool)
        for day in range(days):
            if day == 0 and shock.Chen_absent and r == "Chen":
                continue
            raw = (
                [(sh.start, sh.end+RULE["overtime"])]
                if sh.break_start < 0 else
                [(sh.start, sh.break_start),
                 (sh.break_end, sh.end+RULE["overtime"])]
            )
            for x, y in raw:
                x, y = int(day*1440+x), int(day*1440+y)
                intervals.append((x, y))
                av[x:y] = True
        calendars[r], available[r] = intervals, av
        regular[r] = (
            0 if shock.Chen_absent and r == "Chen" else
            int(sh.end-sh.start-max(0, sh.break_end-sh.break_start))
        )
        busy[r], overtime[r] = 0, 0
        running[r], last_diag[r], idle_since[r] = None, None, 0

    def advance(r, start, work):
        """Calendar-aware forecast; no hidden access to latent actual duration."""
        for lo, hi in calendars[r]:
            start = max(start, lo)
            if start >= hi:
                continue
            if work <= hi-start:
                return start+work
            work -= hi-start
            start = hi
        return float("inf")

    def rank(c, t):
        if policy == "Spec":
            return (c["p"], c["arrival"], c["id"])
        if policy == "Pool":
            return (c["p"], c["due"], c["id"])
        credit = 120 if c["p"] == 3 and t-c["arrival"] >= 240 else 0
        return (0 if c["p"] == 1 else 1, c["due"]-t-credit, c["id"])

    def ahead(r, c, t):
        return sum(
            max(1, j["estimate"]) for j in tasks
            if j["r"] == r and j["state"] != "done"
            and (j["state"] == "run" or rank(j["c"], t) <= rank(c, t))
        )

    def transfer(parents, r):
        # Parallel predecessor transfers overlap; edge burdens are logged separately.
        return (
            RULE["handover_delay"]
            if any(x["r"] != r for x in parents) else 0
        )

    def estimate_finish(r, acts, c, parents, t):
        work = sum(mu[a]*speed[r][a] for a in acts)
        old = last_diag[r]
        switched = (
            acts[0] in DIAG and old
            and old[0] != c["id"] and old[1] != acts[0]
        )
        setup = RULE["diagnostic_setup"] if switched else 0
        return advance(
            r, t+transfer(parents, r), ahead(r, c, t)+work+setup
        )

    def eligible(a, c, parents, t):
        all_r = [r for r in speed if not np.isnan(speed[r][a])]
        if t < 1440 and shock.Chen_absent:
            all_r = [r for r in all_r if r != "Chen"]
        if a not in DIAG or policy == "Pool":
            return all_r
        primary = [r for r in PRIMARY[a] if r in all_r]
        if policy == "Spec":
            return primary
        primary_wait = min(
            (estimate_finish(r, [a], c, parents, t)
             -t-mu[a]*speed[r][a] for r in primary),
            default=float("inf")
        )
        return (
            all_r
            if primary_wait > RULE["overflow_wait"] or c["p"] == 1
            else primary
        )

    def cost(r, acts, c, parents, t):
        f = estimate_finish(r, acts, c, parents, t)
        if policy == "Hybrid" and t < 1440 and c["p"] > 1:
            planned = busy[r] + sum(
                max(1, j["estimate"]) for j in tasks
                if j["r"] == r and j["state"] != "done"
            )
            planned += sum(mu[a]*speed[r][a] for a in acts)
            # One percentage point above 90% adds three routing-score minutes.
            f += 300*max(
                0, planned/max(1, regular[r])-RULE["fair_utilization"]
            )
        return f

    def add(c, a, r, parents, t, lag=0):
        hand = transfer(parents, r)
        j = dict(
            id=len(tasks), c=c, a=a, r=r, q=t, ready=t+lag+hand,
            estimate=mu[a]*speed[r][a],
            remaining=math.ceil(c["draws"][a]*speed[r][a]),
            state="queue", setup=0, first_dispatch=None, first_active=None,
            queue_wait=0, queued_since=t+lag+hand,
            service=0, setup_total=0,
            eligible=",".join(eligible(a, c, parents, t)),
            finish=None, parents=[x["id"] for x in parents]
        )
        tasks.append(j)
        if emit_log:
            audit.append(dict(
                task_id=j["id"], minute=t, event="assignment", reason=policy
            ))
        for parent in parents:
            if parent["r"] != r:
                edges.append(dict(
                    case=c["id"], source=parent["r"], target=r,
                    source_task=parent["id"], target_task=j["id"],
                    minutes=RULE["handover_delay"],
                    start_minute=t+lag,
                    end_minute=t+lag+RULE["handover_delay"]
                ))
        return j

    def route_one(c, a, parents, t, lag=0):
        rs = eligible(a, c, parents, t)
        r = min(
            rs, key=lambda x: (cost(x, [a], c, parents, t+lag), x)
        )
        return add(c, a, r, parents, t, lag)

    def route_diagnostics(c, parent, t):
        aa = TYPES[c["kind"]]
        if len(aa) == 1:
            c["diag"] = [route_one(c, aa[0], [parent], t)]
            return
        a, b = aa
        e1 = eligible(a, c, [parent], t)
        e2 = eligible(b, c, [parent], t)
        seq = [
            (cost(r, aa, c, [parent], t), r)
            for r in set(e1) & set(e2)
        ]
        par = [
            (max(cost(r, [a], c, [parent], t),
                 cost(s, [b], c, [parent], t))
             + RULE["parallel_coordination"]
             + (RULE["ME_team_delay"] if c["kind"] == "ME" else 0),
             r, s)
            for r, s in product(e1, e2) if r != s
        ]
        best_s = min(seq, default=(float("inf"), None))
        best_p = min(par, default=(float("inf"), None, None))
        gain = RULE["parallel_gain"] if policy == "Hybrid" else 0
        if best_s[1] is None or best_p[0]+gain < best_s[0]:
            c["mode"] = "parallel"
            c["diag"] = [
                add(c, a, best_p[1], [parent], t),
                add(c, b, best_p[2], [parent], t)
            ]
        else:
            c["mode"], c["seq_next"] = "sequential", b
            c["diag"] = [add(c, a, best_s[1], [parent], t)]

    def finish(j, t):
        if emit_log:
            audit.append(dict(
                task_id=j["id"], minute=t,
                event="complete", reason="service finished"
            ))
        j["state"], j["finish"] = "done", t
        c = j["c"]
        if j["a"] == "Intake":
            route_diagnostics(c, j, t)
        elif j["a"] in DIAG:
            if c["seq_next"]:
                a, c["seq_next"] = c["seq_next"], None
                # Existing sequential commitment retains its qualified resource.
                c["diag"].append(add(c, a, j["r"], [j], t))
            elif all(x["state"] == "done" for x in c["diag"]):
                parents = c["diag"] if c["mode"] == "parallel" else [j]
                lag = 0
                if c["mode"] == "parallel":
                    lag = RULE["parallel_coordination"]
                    if c["kind"] == "ME":
                        lag += RULE["ME_team_delay"]
                route_one(c, "Validation", parents, t, lag)
        else:
            c["finish"] = t

    def record(j, phase, t):
        if not emit_log:
            return
        previous = last_segment.get(j["r"])
        if (
            previous is not None
            and segments[previous]["task_id"] == j["id"]
            and segments[previous]["phase"] == phase
            and segments[previous]["end"] == t
        ):
            segments[previous]["end"] = t+1
        else:
            last_segment[j["r"]] = len(segments)
            segments.append(dict(
                task_id=j["id"], phase=phase, start=t, end=t+1
            ))

    n_arrived = 0
    for t in range(horizon):
        while n_arrived < len(cases) and cases[n_arrived]["arrival"] <= t:
            route_one(cases[n_arrived], "Intake", [], t)
            n_arrived += 1
        outage = bool(shock.IT_outage and 90 <= t < 120)
        for r in speed:
            j = running[r]
            if not available[r][t]:
                if emit_log and j and t and available[r][t-1]:
                    audit.append(dict(
                        task_id=j["id"], minute=t,
                        event="pause", reason="calendar"
                    ))
                continue
            if emit_log and j and t and not available[r][t-1]:
                audit.append(dict(
                    task_id=j["id"], minute=t,
                    event="resume", reason="calendar"
                ))
            if j and outage and j["a"] == "Intake":
                if emit_log:
                    audit.append(dict(
                        task_id=j["id"], minute=t,
                        event="pause", reason="IT outage"
                    ))
                j["state"], j["ready"], j["queued_since"] = "queue", 120, 120
                running[r], j = None, None

            ready = [
                x for x in tasks
                if x["r"] == r and x["state"] == "queue"
                and x["ready"] <= t
                and not (outage and x["a"] == "Intake")
            ]
            ready.sort(key=lambda x: rank(x["c"], t))

            # Only P1 interrupts lower-priority active diagnostic service.
            if (
                j and j["a"] in DIAG and j["setup"] == 0
                and j["c"]["p"] > 1 and ready
            ):
                p1 = ready[0]
                # Deliberately coarse proxy, not a remaining-case p90 forecast.
                slack = (
                    p1["c"]["due"]-t-max(1, p1["estimate"])
                    -mu["Validation"]-2*RULE["handover_delay"]
                )
                allowed = (
                    j["c"]["preemptions"] < RULE["max_preemptions"]
                )
                benefit = max(1, j["estimate"]) > (
                    RULE["diagnostic_setup"]*(1+RULE["restart_fraction"])
                )
                if (
                    p1["c"]["p"] == 1 and slack < RULE["urgent_slack"]
                    and allowed and benefit
                ):
                    if emit_log:
                        audit.append(dict(
                            task_id=j["id"], minute=t,
                            event="pause", reason="P1 preemption"
                        ))
                    j["remaining"] += math.ceil(
                        RULE["restart_fraction"]*j["remaining"]
                    )
                    j["estimate"] *= 1+RULE["restart_fraction"]
                    j["c"]["preemptions"] += 1
                    j["state"], j["ready"], j["queued_since"] = "queue", t, t
                    running[r], j = None, None

            if j is None and ready:
                pick = ready[0]
                sw = [
                    x for x in speed
                    if available[x][t] and not np.isnan(speed[x]["Software"])
                ]
                reserve = (
                    min(sw, key=lambda x: (speed[x]["Software"], x))
                    if sw else None
                )
                hold = (
                    policy == "Hybrid" and r == reserve
                    and pick["c"]["p"] > 1 and pick["a"] in DIAG
                    and t-idle_since[r] < RULE["reserve_hold"]
                )
                if not hold:
                    j = pick
                    running[r], j["state"] = j, "run"
                    if emit_log:
                        audit.append(dict(
                            task_id=j["id"], minute=t,
                            event=("start" if j["first_dispatch"] is None
                                   else "resume"),
                            reason="dispatch"
                        ))
                    j["queue_wait"] += t-j["queued_since"]
                    if j["first_dispatch"] is None:
                        j["first_dispatch"] = t
                    old = last_diag[r]
                    switched = (
                        j["a"] in DIAG and old
                        and old[0] != j["c"]["id"] and old[1] != j["a"]
                    )
                    j["setup"] = RULE["diagnostic_setup"] if switched else 0

            if j is None:
                continue
            phase = "Setup" if j["setup"] > 0 else "Service"
            record(j, phase, t)
            if t < 1440:
                busy[r] += 1
                if t >= shift_end[r]:
                    overtime[r] += 1
            if phase == "Setup":
                j["setup"] -= 1
                j["setup_total"] += 1
            else:
                if j["first_active"] is None:
                    j["first_active"] = t
                if j["a"] in DIAG:
                    last_diag[r] = (j["c"]["id"], j["a"])
                j["remaining"] -= 1
                j["estimate"] = max(0, j["estimate"]-1)
                j["service"] += 1
                if j["remaining"] == 0:
                    running[r] = None
                    idle_since[r] = t+1
                    finish(j, t+1)

        if (
            n_arrived == len(cases)
            and all(c["finish"] is not None for c in cases)
        ):
            break

    if any(c["finish"] is None for c in cases):
        raise RuntimeError(
            "Censored cohort: increase days; never drop unfinished cases."
        )
    if not cases:
        raise RuntimeError("Zero-arrival seed: omit or resample explicitly.")
    assert max(overtime.values()) <= RULE["overtime"]
    assert all(
        c["preemptions"] <= RULE["max_preemptions"] for c in cases
    )

    case_df = pd.DataFrame([
        {k: c[k] for k in
         ["id", "arrival", "p", "due", "finish", "mode", "preemptions"]}
        for c in cases
    ])
    case_df["delay"] = case_df.finish-case_df.arrival
    util = pd.Series({
        r: busy[r]/regular[r] for r in speed if regular[r] > 0
    })
    metrics = dict(
        scenario=scenario, policy=policy, seed=seed, n=len(cases),
        mean_delay=case_df.delay.mean(),
        p95_delay=case_df.delay.quantile(.95),
        max_util=util.max(),
        utilization_CV=util.std(ddof=0)/util.mean(),
        overhead_per_case=(
            sum(x["minutes"] for x in edges)
            +sum(j["setup_total"] for j in tasks)
        )/len(cases),
        close_fraction=(case_df.finish <= 750).mean(),
        recovery_minutes=max(0, case_df.finish.max()-750),
        preemptions=case_df.preemptions.sum(),
        overtime_minutes=sum(overtime.values())
    )
    for p in [1, 2, 3]:
        cohort = case_df[case_df.p == p]
        metrics[f"P{p}_n"] = len(cohort)
        metrics[f"P{p}_ok"] = int((cohort.delay <= SLA[p]).sum())

    if not emit_log:
        return metrics, None, None, None, case_df

    task_df = pd.DataFrame([
        {k: j[k] for k in
         ["id", "a", "r", "q", "ready", "first_dispatch", "first_active",
          "finish", "queue_wait", "service", "setup_total"]}
        for j in tasks
    ])
    rows = []
    for seg in segments:
        j = tasks[seg["task_id"]]
        c = j["c"]
        rows.append({
            "case:concept:name": c["id"],
            "concept:name": j["a"] if seg["phase"] == "Service" else "Setup",
            "start_timestamp": EPOCH+pd.Timedelta(minutes=seg["start"]),
            "time:timestamp": EPOCH+pd.Timedelta(minutes=seg["end"]),
            "org:resource": j["r"],
            "task_id": j["id"], "phase": seg["phase"], "skill_used": j["a"],
            "queue_entry": EPOCH+pd.Timedelta(minutes=j["q"]),
            "assignment_decision": EPOCH+pd.Timedelta(minutes=j["q"]),
            "eligible_resource_set": j["eligible"],
            "planned_service": mu[j["a"]]*speed[j["r"]][j["a"]],
            "actual_task_service": j["service"],
            "case_priority": c["p"], "branch_mode": c["mode"],
            "preemption_count": c["preemptions"],
            "policy_version": policy+"-illustrative-v1",
            "scenario": scenario,
            "sla_deadline": EPOCH+pd.Timedelta(minutes=c["due"])
        })
    log = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    log.attrs["audit_events"] = audit
    return metrics, log, task_df, pd.DataFrame(edges), case_df


def sla_interval(g, priority):
    """Day-cluster bootstrap: sampling uncertainty, not model uncertainty."""
    ok = g[f"P{priority}_ok"].to_numpy()
    n = g[f"P{priority}_n"].to_numpy()
    if n.sum() == 0:
        return "NA (n=0)"
    ix = np.random.default_rng(934).integers(
        len(g), size=(5000, len(g))
    )
    denominator = n[ix].sum(axis=1)
    rates = (
        ok[ix].sum(axis=1)[denominator > 0]
        /denominator[denominator > 0]
    )
    lo, hi = np.quantile(rates, [.025, .975])
    return (
        f"{100*ok.sum()/n.sum():.0f}% "
        f"[{100*lo:.0f},{100*hi:.0f}] n={n.sum()}"
    )


def main(seeds=12):
    results, sample = [], None
    for scenario, policy, seed in product(
        SCENARIOS.index, POLICIES, range(seeds)
    ):
        keep_log = (scenario, policy, seed) == ("Base", "Hybrid", 0)
        out = simulate(scenario, policy, seed, emit_log=keep_log)
        results.append(out[0])
        if keep_log:
            sample = out

    df = pd.DataFrame(results)
    columns = [
        "mean_delay", "p95_delay", "max_util", "utilization_CV",
        "overhead_per_case", "close_fraction"
    ]
    print("\nRepeated-seed mean metrics; time units are minutes:")
    print(
        df.groupby(["scenario", "policy"])[columns]
        .mean().round(2).to_string()
    )
    sla_rows = []
    for (scenario, policy), g in df.groupby(["scenario", "policy"]):
        sla_rows.append(dict(
            scenario=scenario, policy=policy,
            **{f"P{p}_SLA": sla_interval(g, p) for p in [1, 2, 3]},
            mean_delay_seed_p10=g.mean_delay.quantile(.1),
            mean_delay_seed_p90=g.mean_delay.quantile(.9)
        ))
    print("\nSLA rates with 95% day-bootstrap intervals:")
    print(pd.DataFrame(sla_rows).round(1).to_string(index=False))
    df.to_csv("policy_seed_metrics.csv", index=False)

    _, log, task_df, edge_df, case_df = sample
    audit_df = pd.DataFrame(log.attrs.pop("audit_events"))
    audit_df["timestamp"] = (
        EPOCH+pd.to_timedelta(audit_df.minute, unit="m")
    )
    audit_df.to_csv("simulated_lifecycle_audit.csv", index=False)

    # Merge any remaining contiguous fragments on the same task/resource/phase.
    log = log.sort_values(["org:resource", "start_timestamp", "task_id"])
    keys = ["org:resource", "task_id", "phase"]
    new_segment = (
        (log[keys] != log[keys].shift()).any(axis=1)
        | (log.start_timestamp != log["time:timestamp"].shift())
    )
    log["_segment"] = new_segment.cumsum()
    agg = {
        c: "first" for c in log.columns
        if c not in ["_segment", "time:timestamp"]
    }
    agg["time:timestamp"] = "max"
    log = (
        log.groupby("_segment", as_index=False)
        .agg(agg).drop(columns="_segment")
    )
    log.to_csv("simulated_interval_log.csv", index=False)
    task_df.to_csv("simulated_tasks.csv", index=False)
    edge_df.to_csv("simulated_handover_edges.csv", index=False)

    try:
        import pm4py

# 9. Limits and validation plan

Expected arrivals and service ranges do not identify arrival burstiness, within-range distributions, case-level dependence, resource calendars beyond the stated day, initial backlog, diagnostic quality, or workers’ actual ability to switch context. They also do not determine scenario probabilities. Exact operational SLA rates cannot be inferred from these inputs alone.

The simulation establishes that **these particular implementations, under explicit assumptions, exhibit severe capacity and coverage problems**. It does not establish that every possible routing algorithm has the displayed SLA rates, or that the hybrid is statistically superior.

A staged validation should proceed as follows.

**First, validate the event model and capacity accounting.** Check resource non-overlap, qualification, break/overtime compliance, fork/join precedence, unique Validation, at-most-one priority preemption per case, and conservation of active service plus restart work. Reconcile external arrivals with Intake queues and ensure unfinished cases remain in SLA denominators.

**Second, backtest competing rules with stronger uncertainty treatment.** Replay multiple-day arrival streams with initial backlog and continuing post-shock demand. Test both U2 interpretations, positively correlated activity tails, alternative within-range distributions, and uncertain residual-time estimates. Use common random numbers and paired, day-clustered comparisons. Include recovery time, accumulated backlog, P3 attainment, and the probability of exceeding the 90% planning threshold.

**Third, run shadow dispatch before intervention.** Log what each policy would have chosen alongside actual decisions. Investigate disagreements involving scarce morning Software capacity, late Mechanical work, and Validation. Shadow decisions alone cannot reveal their causal outcomes, but they can expose infeasible or uninterpretable rules.

**Finally, use a guarded switchback pilot at team/day or suitably isolated episode level**, rather than independently randomizing cases that share the same workers and queues. Preserve an experienced dispatcher’s P1 override, immediate skill-coverage escalation, and the overtime limit. Resolve the identified capacity deficit before using a pilot to justify normal operation.

Pre-register acceptable noninferiority margins for P1, improvements sought in delay/P2, and fairness limits. Require sufficient independent demand episodes and priority-specific outcomes; an observed 90% success rate is not itself evidence that the underlying rate is at least 90%.

**The defensible decision is therefore conditional:** use the hybrid to prioritize P1 risk containment, pooling when broader flow performance dominates, and a separate capacity intervention to make the operating commitments credible. Routing should expose the shortage—not conceal it through overtime, postponed P3 cases, or misleading aggregate utilization.
