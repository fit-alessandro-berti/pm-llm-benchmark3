## 1. Assumptions and notation

All times are expressed in minutes unless otherwise stated.

- **A1 (Rounded timestamps as central points)**: Each reported clock time is treated as the central point of an interval of width 5 minutes, i.e. a logged time \(t\) represents an actual time in \([t-2.5, t+2.5]\). For the **central scenario**, I use the logged times as exact; the rounding interval is used only to justify \(\pm 2.5\)-minute robustness bands, not to re-round every value.
- **A2 (Queue entry and service discipline)**: A case enters the **S1** queue at `arrival_s1` and leaves S1 when `s1_end` occurs. A case enters the **S2** queue when `s1_end` occurs and leaves S2 when `s2_end` occurs. Both stations are modeled as single-queue/multi-server with FCFS discipline within each station; I ignore any priority effects because there is no evidence for them.
- **A3 (Event ordering and interval convention)**: Time intervals are treated as half-open \([start, end)\). When two events at the same station share the same reported time, I assume the following order for simulation purposes: external arrival \(\rightarrow\) station completion \(\rightarrow\) station start. This avoids artificial overlaps from rounding while keeping queue lengths conservative.
- **A4 (Capacity and resources)**: S1 has two identical single-task resources (I1, I2) available for the full observation window. S2 has two single-task resources (C1, C2) with C2 unavailable from 10:55–11:10; C1 is fully available. Resources never work on more than one case at a time.
- **A5 (Missing starts – MISSING)**: For activities with `MISSING` start times but known resource and completion time, I derive **earliest** and **latest** feasible start bounds from (i) case arrival or upstream completion, (ii) resource non-overlap with its previous and next tasks, and (iii) non-negative service time. The **central** imputed start is the midpoint of this feasibility window (rounded to the nearest 5 minutes). I also report the full range as an uncertainty interval.
- **A6 (Open activities – OPEN)**: For `OPEN` activities at 14:00, I distinguish **elapsed service time so far** (from recorded start to 14:00) from **eventual service time**, which is only bounded below. Waiting times before the start of an open activity are fully observed and are treated as exact (subject to rounding).
- **A7 (Blank times)**: A blank start and end time at S2 for a case with completed S1 means the case has entered S2’s queue and is still waiting; blank S1 times with nonblank arrival mean the case is waiting for S1. These are treated as right-censored waiting times (lower bounds only).
- **A8 (Aggregation intervals)**: For arrival/throughput rates I split the observation window into three non-overlapping intervals: \([08{:}00,10{:}00)\), \([10{:}00,12{:}00)\), and \([12{:}00,14{:}00)\). Times on the boundary belong to the later interval.
- **A9 (Notation)**: For case \(i\):
  - \(W_{1,i}\): waiting time in S1 queue.
  - \(S_{1,i}\): service time at S1.
  - \(T_{1,i} = W_{1,i} + S_{1,i}\): sojourn time at S1 (arrival to S1 completion).
  - \(W_{2,i}\): waiting time in S2 queue.
  - \(S_{2,i}\): service time at S2.
  - \(T_{2,i} = W_{2,i} + S_{2,i}\): sojourn time at S2 (S1 completion to S2 completion).
  - \(T_{i} = T_{1,i} + T_{2,i}\): total sojourn time from external arrival to S2 completion (for completed cases).
- **A10 (Scenarios for missingness)**:
  - **Optimistic scenario O**: all MISSING starts take their **earliest** feasible start times (minimizing waiting and maximizing service duration); open activities complete immediately after 14:00 (minimal additional service).
  - **Central scenario C**: all MISSING starts take the **midpoint** of their feasible windows; rounding uncertainty is ignored in the point estimates but discussed qualitatively.
  - **Pessimistic scenario P**: all MISSING starts take their **latest** feasible start times (maximizing waiting and minimizing service duration); open activities are assumed to require one additional full typical service duration beyond 14:00.


## 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

### 2.1 Case-level waiting, service, and sojourn

For a case \(i\) with fully observed S1:
- **S1 waiting**: \(W_{1,i} = \max(0, s1\_start_i - arrival\_s1_i)\).
- **S1 service**: \(S_{1,i} = s1\_end_i - s1\_start_i\).
- **S1 sojourn**: \(T_{1,i} = s1\_end_i - arrival\_s1_i\) (this is known even when S1 start is MISSING, as long as S1 completes).

For S2 with fully observed start and end:
- **S2 waiting**: \(W_{2,i} = \max(0, s2\_start_i - s1\_end_i)\).
- **S2 service**: \(S_{2,i} = s2\_end_i - s2\_start_i\).
- **S2 sojourn**: \(T_{2,i} = s2\_end_i - s1\_end_i\) (again known even if S2 start is MISSING).

For **completed cases** (S2 completed), total sojourn time is:
\[
T_i = s2\_end_i - arrival\_s1_i
\]
which is exactly known (up to rounding) for all completed cases, including those with missing starts.

For **OPEN** activities:
- Waiting time before the start is treated as observed and computed as above.
- Service time is decomposed into:
  - **elapsed service** by 14:00: \(S^{\text{elapsed}} = 14{:}00 - \text{start time}\);
  - **eventual service** \(S\) satisfying \(S \ge S^{\text{elapsed}}\), with no upper bound from the data.

For **blank S2 starts/ends on completed S1** (P16–P20), the S2 waiting time is **right-censored**:
\[
W_{2,i} \ge 14{:}00 - s1\_end_i, \quad S_{2,i} = 0\ \text{within the window}.
\]

### 2.2 Bounds for MISSING start times

For each MISSING start (S1 for P07, P15; S2 for P05, P11) I construct:
- **Earliest feasible start**:
  - For S1: \(\max(\text{arrival}_i, \text{end of previous job on same resource})\).
  - For S2: \(\max(s1\_end_i, \text{end of previous job on same resource}, \text{end of C2 outage if on C2})\).
- **Latest feasible start**:
  - For S1: at most the logged `s1_end` (allowing zero-length central-logged intervals, but I also discuss a more realistic 5-minute minimal service).
  - For S2: at most the logged `s2_end`, with the additional constraint of not overlapping the next job on that resource (here the end time already respects that).

For this dataset the resulting feasibility windows (in logged time) are:
- **P07 S1 (I1)**: start in \([09{:}25, 10{:}00]\), because I1 finishes P05 at 09:25 and completes P07 at 10:00 before starting P09 at 10:05.
- **P15 S1 (I1)**: start in \([11{:}40, 12{:}15]\), because I1 finishes P13 at 11:40 and must finish P15 by 12:15 before P17 starts at 12:20.
- **P05 S2 (C1)**: start in \([09{:}45, 10{:}15]\), because C1 finishes P03 at 09:45 and completes P05 at 10:15 before P07 starts at 10:20.
- **P11 S2 (C2)**: start in \([12{:}00, 12{:}25]\), because C2 finishes P08 at 12:00 (after its 10:55–11:10 outage) and completes P11 at 12:25 before P13 starts at 12:30.

Waiting and service bounds then follow mechanically; for example, for P05 S2:
- \(W_{2,\text{P05}} \in [09{:}45-09{:}25, 10{:}15-09{:}25] = [20, 50]\) minutes.
- \(S_{2,\text{P05}} \in [0, 30]\) minutes.

In the **central scenario C**, I use the midpoint of each interval (rounded to the nearest 5 minutes) for imputation:
- P07 S1 start \(\approx 09{:}45\) (service \(\approx 15\) min, wait \(\approx 60\) min).
- P15 S1 start \(\approx 12{:}00\) (service \(\approx 15\) min, wait \(\approx 110\) min).
- P05 S2 start \(\approx 10{:}00\) (service \(\approx 15\) min, wait \(\approx 35\) min).
- P11 S2 start \(\approx 12{:}10\) (service \(\approx 15\) min, wait \(\approx 65\) min).

### 2.3 Station-level flows, WIP, and utilization

For a station \(s \in \{1,2\}\) over an interval \(I\):
- **Arrivals** \(A_s(I)\): number of cases entering station \(s\) during \(I\).
  - For S1, arrivals are the external `arrival_s1`.
  - For S2, arrivals are S1 completions (`s1_end`).
- **Completions** \(C_s(I)\): number of cases completing station \(s\) during \(I\) (S1 completions or S2 completions).
- **Throughput rate**: \(\lambda_s(I) = C_s(I) / |I|\) (cases per hour), where \(|I|\) is interval length in hours.
- **Mean sojourn time at station \(s\)**:
  - For completed cases in \(I\): \( \bar{T}_s(I) = \frac{1}{C_s(I)} \sum_{i\in I^{\text{completed}}} T_{s,i}\).
  - For S2, I also consider ranges due to MISSING starts, but note that \(T_{2,i}\) is known even when S2 start is missing.
- **Instantaneous WIP** at station \(s\) at time \(t\):
  \[
  N_s(t) = \#\{i : \text{case } i \text{ has entered station } s\ \text{and not yet completed station } s \text{ at time } t\}.
  \]
  This counts both queued and in-service cases.
- **Total WIP in the system** at time \(t\):
  \[
  N_{\text{sys}}(t) = \#\{i : arrival\_s1_i \le t < s2\_end_i \text{ (or 14{:}00 if incomplete)}\}.
  \]
- **Mean WIP** over interval \(I\): approximated by discrete-time averaging of \(N_s(t)\) (e.g. at each 5-minute grid point).
- **Resource busy time** for station \(s\) over \(I\):
  \[
  B_s(I) = \sum_{i} S_{s,i}^{(I)},
  \]
  where \(S_{s,i}^{(I)}\) is the portion of case \(i\)'s service time that lies in \(I\).
- **Utilization range**:
  \[
  U_s(I) = \frac{B_s(I)}{\text{available capacity}_s(I)},
  \]
  where `available capacity` is the sum, over resources, of their available minutes in \(I\) (e.g. for S2, \(360\) minutes for C1 plus \(345\) minutes for C2 due to its 15-minute outage, totaling \(705\) minutes).
  Using the start-interval bounds from Section 2.2 yields optimistic/central/pessimistic utilization ranges.

### 2.4 Little’s Law checks

Where station behavior is roughly stable, I apply Little’s Law at the station level:
\[
L_s \approx \lambda_s W_s,
\]
with:
- \(L_s\): mean number of cases in station \(s\) (queued or in service).
- \(\lambda_s\): effective departure rate from station \(s\) in the interval.
- \(W_s\): mean sojourn time in station \(s\) in the same interval.

Because the system clearly accumulates backlog (more arrivals than completions in some periods), strict steady-state assumptions do not hold globally. I therefore treat Little’s Law as a **consistency check** only on coarser windows (e.g. the full 08:00–14:00 period) and explicitly acknowledge the mismatch due to ramp-up and backlog growth.

## 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

### 5.1 Early morning (08:00–10:00)

- **S1**: From 08:00–09:00, S1 behaves like a mildly loaded two-server station. P01–P04 have S1 waits of only 5–20 minutes and S1 service around 20–30 minutes. There is some early queueing (already visible in P03’s 20-minute wait), but WIP at S1 remains modest (roughly 1–3 cases) and both inspectors are not continuously busy.
- **S2**: S2 starts work almost immediately after S1 finishes its first cases. Between 08:30 and 09:45, C1 and C2 alternate jobs with reasonably short S2 waits (5–15 minutes) and service times of 30–40 minutes. S2 completes 3 cases in 2 hours (1.5/h), compared with 6 S1 completions (3/h), so some S2 backlog is created, but still at a low level (queue length around 1–2).
- **Bottleneck interpretation**: In this period, S1 is not clearly the bottleneck (it has capacity headroom) but S2’s lower throughput relative to S1 begins to build a small downstream queue. However, both stations are far from the extreme congestion seen later.

### 5.2 Mid-morning (10:00–12:00) and C2 outage

- **S1 behavior**:
  - S1 completions accelerate: 7 S1 completions vs 5 S2 completions in 10:00–12:00.
  - Waiting times for S1 grow sharply: P08–P14 have S1 waits from 50 up to 95 minutes, with P15 and P16–P20 eventually experiencing 90–130+ minutes of S1 queueing.
  - This indicates that by mid-morning the S1 arrival rate from the environment (up to ~6.5/h in the first interval) has pushed S1 into a heavily loaded regime.
- **S2 behavior and C2 outage**:
  - P06 (C2) completes S2 at 10:50, immediately before the outage; P07 (C1) runs 10:20–11:00; C2 is then unavailable 10:55–11:10.
  - The next C2 job (P08) starts at 11:15, just after the outage window, consuming backlog that accumulated on S2.
  - At the start of the outage window, S2 likely has at least P07 in service and P06 just completed, with another case (P08) ready or nearly ready. The 15-minute C2 outage therefore removes some potential service capacity that could otherwise reduce the S2 queue.
  - However, the data show that the outage coincides with increasing S1 completions: more and more cases arrive at S2 between 10:00 and 12:00, and S2 completes only 5 of them. So while the outage contributes to S2 congestion, the dominant driver is rising S1 throughput.
- **Bottleneck interpretation**:
  - In mid-morning, **S1** is heavily loaded (long S1 waits) but also passes many cases to S2, while **S2’s throughput** lags behind the incoming S2 arrival rate, especially during and after the C2 outage.
  - The bottleneck thus shifts toward **S2**: S2 cannot keep up with S1 completions, and a persistent downstream queue starts to form.

### 5.3 Late morning to early afternoon (12:00–14:00)

- **S1**:
  - S1 continues to complete 7 cases in 12:00–14:00 (P14–P20), while two more cases (P21, P22) are still in or waiting for S1 at 14:00.
  - Most late cases (P16–P22) experience very long S1 waits (100–130+ minutes). Even if S1 utilization is similar to S2 in percentage terms, the fact that arrivals persist while S1 still has a big backlog marks S1 as operationally critical for late arrivals.
- **S2**:
  - S2 completes another 6 cases (P08, P10–P14) in 12:00–14:00 but accumulates a queue of 4 waiting cases plus 2 in service at 14:00.
  - Typical S2 waits for late completed cases (P12–P14) are 45–65 minutes, and for unfinished cases P16–P20 the S2 waiting time is at least 15–85 minutes.
  - At 14:00, the system’s WIP is 8 cases: 2 in S1 (P21 in service, P22 waiting) and 6 at S2 (4 waiting, 2 in service), so **S2 holds the majority of the in-process work**.
- **Bottleneck interpretation**:
  - By the end of the window, **S2 is the dominant bottleneck**: more cases are stuck at or after S2 than at S1, and S2’s backlog is growing.
  - S1 remains heavily utilized and does create long waits, but given the state at 14:00, the limiting factor for throughput in the near future is S2 capacity.

### 5.4 Overall bottleneck conclusion

- Early in the shift (08:00–09:30), neither station is clearly bottlenecked; S2 is slightly slower than S1 but queues remain small.
- As arrivals ramp up, S1 begins to show long waiting times, but because S1 still clears many cases, the S2 queue becomes critical from around 10:30 onward.
- The C2 outage contributes to S2 congestion but is **not the sole cause**; even without it, the sustained high inflow from S1 and long S2 service times would still produce a substantial downstream queue.
- Across all missingness scenarios, the data are most consistent with a **time-varying bottleneck**: S1’s congestion is important mid-morning, but **S2 becomes the main bottleneck by the end of the observation window**, where most WIP resides.


## 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

### 6.1 Optimistic scenario O

- **MISSING start times**:
  - P07 S1 start set to earliest 09:25 → \(W_{1,\text{P07}} = 40\) min, \(S_{1,\text{P07}} = 35\) min.
  - P15 S1 start set to earliest 11:40 → \(W_{1,\text{P15}} = 90\) min, \(S_{1,\text{P15}} = 35\) min.
  - P05 S2 start set to earliest 09:45 → \(W_{2,\text{P05}} = 20\) min, \(S_{2,\text{P05}} = 30\) min.
  - P11 S2 start set to earliest 12:00 → \(W_{2,\text{P11}} = 55\) min, \(S_{2,\text{P11}} = 25\) min.
- **Open activities**:
  - P15 S2 and P17 S2 are assumed to finish almost immediately after 14:00, so only the elapsed service times of 30 and 5 minutes are counted.
  - P21 S1 is similarly assumed to finish soon after 14:00 (no extra service counted beyond the 30 elapsed minutes).
- **Implications**:
  - This scenario **minimizes queueing and average waiting** and **maximizes average service times** for the affected activities.
  - S1 busy time is at the upper end of its feasible range (~605 minutes), and S2 busy time is near its upper bound (~570 minutes).
  - Even under this optimistic interpretation, S1 and S2 utilizations remain high (~0.80–0.85), and late-arriving cases still experience very long waits (e.g. P22 has ≥110 minutes of S1 waiting regardless of imputation).

### 6.2 Central scenario C

- **MISSING start times**:
  - Use midpoints of the feasibility windows (P07 S1 ≈09:45, P15 S1 ≈12:00, P05 S2 ≈10:00, P11 S2 ≈12:10).
  - This yields mid-range splits between waiting and service while preserving exact sojourn times for the stations.
- **Open activities**:
  - Only elapsed service within 08:00–14:00 is counted (30 minutes for P15 S2, 5 minutes for P17 S2, 30 minutes for P21 S1); eventual completions are left unspecified.
- **Implications**:
  - S1 and S2 utilizations settle near the midpoints of their ranges (about 0.79 for S1 and 0.77 for S2).
  - Average S1 sojourn \(T_1\) for completed S1 cases is about **1.5 hours**, and average S2 sojourn \(T_2\) for completed S2 cases is about **1.2 hours**.
  - The qualitative picture from Sections 3–5 holds: both stations are highly loaded, S1 waits grow over time, and S2 holds most WIP by 14:00.

### 6.3 Pessimistic scenario P

- **MISSING start times**:
  - P07 S1 start set to latest 10:00 → \(W_{1,\text{P07}} = 75\) min, \(S_{1,\text{P07}} = 0\) min (practically interpreted as a very short S1 service).
  - P15 S1 start set to latest 12:15 → \(W_{1,\text{P15}} = 125\) min, \(S_{1,\text{P15}} = 0\) min.
  - P05 S2 start set to latest 10:15 → \(W_{2,\text{P05}} = 50\) min, \(S_{2,\text{P05}} = 0\) min.
  - P11 S2 start set to latest 12:25 → \(W_{2,\text{P11}} = 80\) min, \(S_{2,\text{P11}} = 0\) min.
- **Open activities**:
  - P15 S2, P17 S2, and P21 S1 are assumed to require an additional full typical service time beyond 14:00 (about 30–40 minutes), implying that actual busy times for S2 and S1 are higher than those observed in-window.
- **Implications**:
  - This scenario **maximizes queueing times** and **minimizes logged service times**, thereby pushing average waiting times up, especially at S2 (P05 and P11).
  - S1 busy time remains within the previously stated 535–605 minute range, while S2 busy time stays within 515–570 minutes in-window but with more unobserved work beyond 14:00.
  - Despite these pessimistic assumptions, the **relative ordering** of bottlenecks is unchanged: S2 still holds more WIP than S1 at 14:00, and late cases still face extreme S1 and S2 waiting.

### 6.4 Conclusions stable across scenarios vs. sensitive conclusions

**Stable conclusions across O, C, and P:**
- Both S1 and S2 operate at high utilization (roughly 75–80% or more).
- S1 waiting times increase strongly over the morning; late arrivals routinely experience **90–130+ minutes** of S1 waiting.
- S2 accumulates a significant downstream backlog, with at least 6 cases at or past S2 at 14:00 in every scenario.
- The bottleneck is **time-varying**, with S1 and S2 both stressed but **S2 clearly dominating as bottleneck near 14:00**, where most WIP resides.
- Completed-case total sojourn times from arrival to S2 completion range from about **1 hour** early to nearly **4 hours** later; this ordering is independent of how missing starts are split.

**Conclusions sensitive to imputation and rounding:**
- The **exact split** between waiting and service for P05, P07, P11, and P15 can vary by up to **30–35 minutes** in either direction.
- Point estimates of **utilization** (e.g. 0.77 vs 0.82) move within the stated ranges, especially if open activities are assumed to continue longer beyond 14:00.
- Instantaneous **peak queue lengths** at specific timestamps can shift by ±1 case depending on the event-order convention and rounding jitter, although the broad shapes of S1 and S2 queues (rising over time) do not change.

def parse_time_with_status(df, day="2026-05-12"):
    # 2. Parse status-aware timestamps without collapsing MISSING, OPEN, and blank
    def parse_field(value):
        if value == "MISSING":
            return pd.NaT, "MISSING"
        if value == "OPEN":
            return pd.NaT, "OPEN"
        if value == "" or pd.isna(value):
            return pd.NaT, "BLANK"
        # normal timestamp
        return pd.Timestamp(f"{day} {value}"), "OK"

    for col in ["arrival_s1", "s1_start", "s1_end", "s2_start", "s2_end"]:
        ts_col = f"{col}_ts"
        status_col = f"{col}_status"
        parsed = df[col].map(parse_field)
        df[ts_col] = parsed.map(lambda x: x[0])
        df[status_col] = parsed.map(lambda x: x[1])
    return df


def build_interval_events(df):
    # 3. Create PM4Py-compatible interval-event DataFrame (usable observations only)
    rows = []
    for _, r in df.iterrows():
        if r["s1_start_status"] == "OK" and r["s1_end_status"] == "OK":
            rows.append({
                "case:concept:name": r["case"],
                "concept:name": "S1",
                "start_timestamp": r["s1_start_ts"],
                "time:timestamp": r["s1_end_ts"],
                "org:resource": r["s1_resource"],
                "station": "S1",
            })
        if r["s2_start_status"] == "OK" and r["s2_end_status"] == "OK":
            rows.append({
                "case:concept:name": r["case"],
                "concept:name": "S2",
                "start_timestamp": r["s2_start_ts"],
                "time:timestamp": r["s2_end_ts"],
                "org:resource": r["s2_resource"],
                "station": "S2",

    events = pd.DataFrame(rows)

    events = pm4py.format_dataframe(

        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
        resource_key="org:resource",
    )
    return events


def add_missing_bounds(df):
    # Bounds derived analytically in the report for MISSING starts
    bounds = {
        ("P07", "s1_start"): ("09:25", "10:00"),
        ("P15", "s1_start"): ("11:40", "12:15"),
        ("P05", "s2_start"): ("09:45", "10:15"),
        ("P11", "s2_start"): ("12:00", "12:25"),
    }

    def to_ts(t):
        return pd.Timestamp(f"2026-05-12 {t}")

    df["s1_start_earliest"] = pd.NaT
    df["s1_start_latest"] = pd.NaT
    df["s2_start_earliest"] = pd.NaT
    df["s2_start_latest"] = pd.NaT
    for (case, field), (lo, hi) in bounds.items():
        mask = df["case"] == case
        if field == "s1_start":
            df.loc[mask, "s1_start_earliest"] = to_ts(lo)
            df.loc[mask, "s1_start_latest"] = to_ts(hi)
        else:
            df.loc[mask, "s2_start_earliest"] = to_ts(lo)
            df.loc[mask, "s2_start_latest"] = to_ts(hi)
    return df


def compute_case_times(df, scenario="central"):
    # 5. Use pandas to calculate station-specific queue/service/WIP quantities and uncertainty scenarios
    # Scenario controls how MISSING starts are imputed
    assert scenario in {"optimistic", "central", "pessimistic"}

    def choose_start(row, col):
        status = row[f"{col}_status"]
        if status == "OK":
            return row[f"{col}_ts"]
        if col == "s1_start":
            lo = row["s1_start_earliest"]
            hi = row["s1_start_latest"]
        else:
            lo = row["s2_start_earliest"]
            hi = row["s2_start_latest"]
        if pd.isna(lo) or pd.isna(hi):
            return pd.NaT
        if scenario == "optimistic":
            return lo
        if scenario == "pessimistic":
            return hi
        # central
        return lo + (hi - lo) / 2

    df = df.copy()
    df["s1_start_imputed"] = df.apply(lambda r: choose_start(r, "s1_start"), axis=1)
    df["s2_start_imputed"] = df.apply(lambda r: choose_start(r, "s2_start"), axis=1)

    # S1 waiting and service
    df["S1_wait"] = (df["s1_start_imputed"] - df["arrival_s1_ts"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
    df["S1_service"] = (df["s1_end_ts"] - df["s1_start_imputed"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
    df.loc[df["s1_end_status"] != "OK", "S1_service"] = None  # open or missing end

    # S2 waiting and service
    df["S2_wait"] = (df["s2_start_imputed"] - df["s1_end_ts"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
    df["S2_service"] = (df["s2_end_ts"] - df["s2_start_imputed"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
    df.loc[df["s2_end_status"] != "OK", "S2_service"] = None

    # For blank S2, S2_wait is right-censored at 14:00
    end_of_window = pd.Timestamp("2026-05-12 14:00")
    mask_waiting_s2 = (df["s1_end_status"] == "OK") & (df["s2_start_status"] == "BLANK")
    df.loc[mask_waiting_s2, "S2_wait_censored_min"] = (
        (end_of_window - df.loc[mask_waiting_s2, "s1_end_ts"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
    )

    # Total case sojourn for completed cases
    completed_mask = df["s2_end_status"] == "OK"
    df["total_sojourn"] = None
    df.loc[completed_mask, "total_sojourn"] = (
        (df.loc[completed_mask, "s2_end_ts"] - df.loc[completed_mask, "arrival_s1_ts"])
        .dt.total_seconds()
        / 60.0
    )

    return df


def summarize_stations(df):
    # Station-level summaries from the central scenario
    s1_completed = df[df["s1_end_status"] == "OK"]
    s2_completed = df[df["s2_end_status"] == "OK"]

    s1_wait_mean = s1_completed["S1_wait"].mean()
    s1_sojourn_mean = (
        (s1_completed["s1_end_ts"] - s1_completed["arrival_s1_ts"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
    ).mean()

    s2_sojourn_mean = (
        (s2_completed["s2_end_ts"] - s2_completed["s1_end_ts"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
    ).mean()

    print("Station-level central scenario (case-level means)")
    print(
        pd.DataFrame(
            [
                {
                    "station": "S1",
                    "completed": len(s1_completed),
                    "mean_wait": round(s1_wait_mean, 1),
                    "mean_sojourn": round(s1_sojourn_mean, 1),
                },
                {
                    "station": "S2",
                    "completed": len(s2_completed),
                    "mean_wait": None,  # depends on imputation; use sojourn as robust metric
                    "mean_sojourn": round(s2_sojourn_mean, 1),
                },
            ]
        )
    )
