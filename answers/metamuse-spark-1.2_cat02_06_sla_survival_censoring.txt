# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

**Time origin:** `t=0` = `Submit` event timestamp (first element of `prefix`). Common origin for all cases. **Horizon:** `=72h`. **Observation window:** Right-truncated at study cutoff. Each case contributes `X = min(T_term, C)` where `T_term` is time to first terminal disposition and `C` = age at cutoff for `open` rows, and `duration_or_age_h` is that value.

**Observation vs. latent event:** For `open` rows we observe `X=C` and `=0`; we do not observe future `T_resolve`. For `transferred` rows we observe `T_transfer` but `T_resolve` (external) is unobserved. `duration_or_age_h` is *elapsed* time, not remaining time. `last_event_gap_h` is time since last activity to terminal/cutoff – a proxy for staleness/informative censoring.

We define at least three operationally distinct estimands:

*   **E1 – Marginal non-resolution by  (survival / breach risk):** `S() = Pr(T_resolve > )`. `1-S()` = prob. resolved *by* . Under competing events this is the net/Hypothetical probability if competing events could be prevented, estimated by treating competing events as censored (cause-specific). Operational meaning: **workload/alerting**: among cases still alive at `t<`, what is the chance they will miss the SLA if nothing else stops them? Treats `Withdraw/Reject/Transfer` as censoring.
    *   Strong assumption: competing events are non-informative for `T_resolve`.

*   **E2 – Cumulative incidence (CIF) of (late) resolution in presence of competing risks:** `F_resolve(t) = Pr(T_resolve  t  resolve precedes any competing event)`. Derived partition: `F_resolve()` = resolved *on time*; `F_resolve()-F_resolve()` = resolved *late* (observed breach); `1- _k F_k()` = never resolved due to censoring. For SLA breach reporting, two useful contrasts:
    *   `F_late = Pr(resolve after )` and
    *   `F_breach-inclusive = 1 - F_resolve()` = probability not resolved on time for *any* reason (late + competing + censored tail).
    E2 is the **governance/accountability** estimand: what fraction of *all submitted cases* actually resolves late vs. never resolves internally because of withdrawal/rejection/transfer.

*   **E3 – Event-free survival (no terminal disposition by ):** `S_EF() = Pr( no Resolve  no Withdraw  no Reject  no Transfer by )`. Estimates `Pr(still pending at )`. Useful for **capacity / backlog** forecasting, independent of resolution definition. Uses composite event; only `open` = censored.

**E1 vs E2 vs E3:** E1 answers "if the case were to continue, would it breach?" E2 answers "what proportion of cases will be counted as a (late) resolution in the presence of attrition?" E3 answers "how many cases remain open past SLA?" A case `Withdrawn at 27h` *lowers* E1 risk set but is *absorbing* in E2/E3.

# 2. Event and censoring encoding

Primary analysis recommends two parallel encodings for the two estimands. All times use `duration_or_age_h`.

**Estimand E1 – Cause-specific (marginal) resolution hazard:**

| `observed_status` | Time | Event indicator `_resolve` | Censoring rule | Role in risk set |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `resolved` | `duration` | 1 at `duration` (0 beyond) | event | at-risk until event |
| `withdrawn` | `duration` | 0 | censored at `duration` (competing treated as non-informative) | removed after `duration` |
| `rejected` | `duration` | 0 | censored at `duration` | removed |
| `transferred` | `duration` | 0 | censored at `duration` (primary) | removed – see sensitivity |
| `open` | `age` | 0 | censored at `age` | at-risk until `age`; if `age>72` breach status at 72 is known, but future beyond `age` censored |

**Estimand E2/E3 – Competing-risk / composite:**

| `observed_status` | Competing-risk code `J` | CIF interpretation | Censoring rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| `resolved` | 1 = `Resolve` | event of interest at `duration` | – |
| `withdrawn` | 2 = `Withdraw` | competing absorbing at `duration` | – |
| `rejected` | 3 = `Reject` | competing absorbing | – |
| `transferred` | 4 = `Transfer` | competing / censoring per scenario (see below) | – |
| `open` | 0 = censored | right-censored at `age` | non-event |

**Transfer – three plausible assumptions (sensitivity mandatory):**

*   **T-A Censoring (primary for E1, non-informative):** Transfer time = independent censoring. Assumption: external resolution hazard = internal. Justification needed by exchangeability; violated if transfers are higher-risk or SLA-escape. Overestimates `S()` if transfers are sicker.

*   **T-B Competing risk (primary for E2 governance):** Transfer = absorbing internal failure (case leaves system). Mass to `F_transfer`. Implies internal SLA will *never* be met for that case. Operationally corresponds to "transferred = not resolved internally by SLA". Decreases `F_resolve()`. Conservative for resolution rate.

*   **T-C Informative / worst-best bounds:**
    *   C-worst: transfer = imminent breach if had stayed (all transfers would have breached). Encode as `1` for `Pr(T_resolve>72)` at  (or impute event after ). Gives upper bound on `S(72)`.
    *   C-best: transfer = would have resolved on time (impute censored as success). Gives lower bound.
    *   C-IPCW/pattern: weight transfers by `last_event_gap_h` or priority; stalled transfers (>48h gap) get higher breach probability. Formally requires time-varying covariates.

**Withdrawal/Rejection sensitivity alternatives:**

*   **W-A Competing (primary):** As absorbing.
*   **W-B Censoring (curve of what-if preventable):** Treat as censoring to estimate net resolution hazard *if* withdrawal could be prevented – relevant for interventions on retention.
*   **W-C Composite failure (SLA governance strict):** Pool `Withdraw/Reject` with `not resolved` as failure – then `1-F_resolve()` includes them as breach-like attrition.

Note: No case is censored before origin. All censoring is right-censoring. Already-observed breach  censoring at breach time – for `open` with `age>72`, censoring *after* horizon does not obscure breach status at horizon.

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

**Definition of naive:** Restrict denominator to `resolved` rows only, compute `Pr(duration>72 | resolved)`. In these data: 13 resolved, breaches = S03 76, S13 110, S20 74, S28 88 = 4/13 = 30.8% breach. Cohort A 2/6 =33%, B 2/7=29%.

**Bias 1 – Survivor (selection) bias / truncation:**

*   Conditions on the outcome. Cases S05 (reject 9h), S12 (withdraw 15h), S06 (27h), S22 (44h), S30 (70h), S14 (63h), S23 (11h) are deleted despite removing 7/32 =22% of submitted cases from the resolvable pool. This inflates apparent resolution probability. E2 true `F_resolve() =13/32 41%` uncensored, far below 100% of completed sample. Likewise E2 `F_resolve(72)=9/32 observed on-time resolutions` vs 9/13=69% on-time among completers overstates success.

**Bias 2 – Informative censoring / ignoring `open` tails:**

*   9 open cases (S09 92, S10 47, S11 81, S15 35, S24 67, S25 79, S26 104, S29 51, S31 23). Naive drops all. Yet 4/9 (S09, S11, S25, S26) have `age>72` – they are *known breaches* of `S(72)` and `S_EF(72)`. Counting only resolved, we miss 4 additional breaches already observed. 5 cases with `age<72` (S10 47, S15 35, S24 67, S29 51, S31 23) are still at-risk before horizon; dropping them underestimates denominator of `S(72)` and artificially stabilizes tail, violating risk-set principle. E.g., S24 (67h, gap 4h) is about to breach in 5h but naive ignores it.

**Bias 3 – Competing risks misclassified as censoring:**

*   `Withdraw/Reject` are *not* just incomplete follow-up; they prevent future resolution. Naive censoring-equivalent (dropping them) estimates a *net* probability that is not realizable. Example: S06 withdrawn 27h would have been censored in E1 but competes in E2; if withdrawal correlates with low priority P3 (S12 P3 withdraw 15h), censoring overstates resolution for P3.

**Bias 4 – Transfer handling & cherry-picking:**

*   S08 transferred 68h (<72), S21 26h, S32 96h (>72). Naive drops them. Under T-B, S08 should *reduce* on-time resolution probability (it left just before SLA). Under T-A, it should remain at-risk until 68. Either way, exclusion biases comparison of cohorts with different transfer rates (A:1/15, B:2/17) and priorities (P1 enrichment: S08 P1, S21 P1, S32 P1 – all P1 transfers).

**Bias 5 – Cohort / priority imbalance & non-random assignment:**

*   Cohort B has more P1 (5 vs 3) and includes `AutoCheck>Screen` reorder (S16, S19...). Naive resolved-only mix confounds time-trend with priority. Example: B has 2 P1 open stalled (S25 gap48, S32 transfer) vs A has S11 gap55. Resolved P1 cases S04 29h (A) vs S18 12h (B) suggest improvement, but open P1 tail is worse in both cohorts – naive misses the tail.

**Bias 6 – Active below vs above horizon:**

*   Below 72: S10 (47h, gap19), S24 (67h gap4), S29 (51h gap6), S15 (35h gap2), S31 (23h gap3). These contribute to uncertainty about `S(72)`; censoring is *uninformative only if gap small*. Above 72: S09 (92h gap31), S11 (81h gap55), S25 (79h gap48), S26 (104h gap72) have *stale* gaps indicating possible informative dropout (abandoned cases). Treating both groups identically as "open = censored" understates breach risk for stale group and overstates precision for at-risk group.

Numerical illustration: Naive breach 31%; proper `1-S_KM(72)` = 1-0.64 =36% (E1) but `F_resolve(72)` 28% (9/32 empirical, AJ 30% accounting for competing)  `Pr(not resolved on time)` = ~70% vs naive 31% – qualitative reversal.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

**Estimand first, estimator second.** Primary governance estimand is E2 (CIF of resolution by/without breach); primary alerting estimand is E1 (conditional survival). Both require handling competing risks and right censoring without dropping `open`.

| Method | What it estimates | How it handles competing/open | Fit for these data |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kaplan-Meier (KM) 1-S** (cause-specific) | `S_resolve(t)=Pr(T_resolve>t)` net, treating competing as censored | Censors competing at event time; open censored at age; Greenwood variance | Simple for E1 alerts; biased for E2 if competing not independent. Needs IPCW if transfer/withdraw informative. n=32  wide CI, step function. |
| **Aalen-Johansen (AJ) CIF** | `F_k(t)` for k=Resolve/Withdraw/Reject/Transfer | Competing = absorbing increments; open = censored; decomposes total probability | **Primary for E2** governance. Correctly shows late-resolution mass vs competing attrition. Handles S03 76 vs S09 92 distinction. Requires competing coding. |
| **Cause-specific hazards (Cox CSH)** | `_k(t|X)` for each cause | Models each hazard separately; competing events censored for `k`=Resolve | For covariate effects (e.g., `Escalate` timing). Needed to explain *why* breach occurs via stage. Not directly probability without AJ recombination. |
| **Subdistribution hazards (Fine-Gray)** | `_resolve(t|X)` directly on `F_resolve` | Withdraw/Transfer kept in risk set with weighting | Gives direct effect on `Pr(resolve on time)`. Useful for policy: "does Cohort B increase on-time resolution probability?" But n=32 overfits; proportional subdistribution may not hold with crossing due to early rejects. |
| **Restricted mean time (RMST / RMST-lost)** | `()=0^ S(t)dt`, `-` = mean time lost to non-resolution/competing | Integrates KM or AJ `S_EF`; no proportional hazards | **Secondary** for communicating SLA burden: mean hours to resolution within 72h window. Robust with competing via `F`. Insensitive to tail beyond  where n small. |
| **Landmarking (dynamic prediction)** | `Pr(T_resolve   | alive at s, H(s))` for landmark s (e.g., 24h, 48h) | Conditions on being still open at landmark; re-estimates S from s forward | **Primary for operational alerts.** Solves immortal-time: `Escalate` evaluated only for those still at-risk at escalation time. E.g., S10 escalated then open 47h – evaluated at s=48h landmark, not baseline. |
| **Multi-state model** | Transition intensities `q_ij(t)` between states; `P_ij(0,t)` probabilities | Unified: transient states = stages (Screen, Review, Escalate, Rework); absorbing = Resolve/Withdraw/Reject/Transfer; censoring = still transient | **Recommended framework** for process-aware analysis. Embeds prefixes as states. Allows `Escalate` as time-varying state, not baseline. AJ is special case with 2 states. |

**Recommended Primary + Secondary (justified for n=32, process data):**

*   **Primary: Aalen-Johansen CIF for `F_resolve(t)` + Kaplan-Meier cause-specific `S(t)` for `E1`, both stratified by `cohort` and `priority`, with Transfer scenario T-A (censor) and T-B (competing) as sensitivity.** Why: Governance needs CIF decomposition; alerting needs conditional S. AJ avoids naive paradox where `KM_Resolve()>0` sums >1 with competing. With only 13 events, point estimate at 72 is estimable but CI wide; AJ still valid with small n using Greenwood/Aalen variance.

*   **Secondary: Landmarked cause-specific hazards with time-varying covariates (multi-state view). Landmarks at 24h and 48h using `current stage` and `rework_count`, and RMST at =72.** Why: Immortal-time for `Escalate` (e.g., S03 escalated before 76, S28 88) would bias Cox if treated baseline. Landmarking and multi-state clock-reset (semi-Markov on entry to Review) respect temporal ordering without fabricating timestamps. RMST translates survival curves into "expected hours resolved within SLA window" for workload – less sensitive to unstable tail at 110h (S13).

**Not recommended as primary:** Fine-Gray alone (over-parameterized for 32 cases, misinterprets competing mechanism); naive complete-case logistic on `breach|resolved` (excludes pending).

**Inference:** Use non-parametric bootstrap (or Greenwood) for CI; report bounds not p-values. Cohort effect is observational – use stratification, not pooled HR, due to non-random rollout and incomplete compliance; present unadjusted and priority-stratified curves.

# 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards

**From `prefix` parsing:** Sequence is observed path; no timestamps except total duration, so we cannot infer sojourn times per activity. Features must be defined as **current state at time t** (time-varying) or **baseline path-invariant** attributes.

| Feature | Derivation from prefix / data | Type | Bias / leakage risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| `current_stage` at `t` | Last element of prefix before `t` (e.g., `Review` in S09, `Escalate` in S10) | **Time-varying** state | Must be modeled as multi-state or time-dependent; not baseline |
| `path_pattern` variant | `pm4py.get_variants_as_tuples` e.g., `Submit>Screen>Review>Rework>Review>Resolve` (S07) vs `Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Review` (S19) | Baseline *only if* pattern defined as *intended* route, else post-treatment if variant determined by downstream events | Leakage if uses `Rework`, `Escalate` that occur *after* origin to predict breach at origin – includes future. Use only prefix up to landmark `s`. |
| `escalated` flag + **`escalation_timing`** | `escalated=yes` and position in prefix: early (S04 after Screen) vs late (S13 after Rework) | **Time-varying** (`Escalate` transition at unobserved time) | **Immortal-time bias** if treated baseline: cases must survive to escalate (e.g., S03 survives 76h to include Escalate). Must use `t_esc` as time-dependent or landmark. |
| `rework_count` | Count of `Rework>Review` loops (S07 1, S13 1, S27 1) | **Time-varying** (01 during case) | Leakage if counted at baseline for cases censored before rework opportunity (S31 23h). Use as counting process. |
| `screen_order` | `Screen>AutoCheck` vs `AutoCheck>Screen` (cohort B change) | Baseline-ish but **post-policy** assignment proxy – confounded | Not causal; treat as cohort interaction, compliance incomplete |
| `inactivity_gap_h` (`last_event_gap_h`) | Time since last event to cutoff/terminal (S26 72h, S11 55h, S25 48h, S09 31h) | Baseline at prediction time *and* indicator of informative censoring | Strong leakage if gap used to define censoring without joint model; but valuable as staleness predictor at landmark. |
| `review_repeated` | `Review` appears 2 times (S03, S07, S13) | Time-varying | Indicates loop, correlates with delay |
| `priority` P1/P2/P3 | Given | **Baseline** (assigned at Submit) | Fairness stratification; not time-varying |
| `cohort` A/B | Given | **Baseline policy era** | Non-random, confounded |
| `num_steps_observed` | Length of prefix | Time-varying cumulative | Correlated with duration by construction – leakage if used baseline |

**Classification:** Baseline safe: `priority`, `cohort`, `screen_order` at Submit. Time-varying (require counting process / landmark / multi-state): `current_stage`, `escalated` after its occurrence, `rework_count>0`, `review_repeated`, `inactivity_gap`. Post-treatment: full variant using downstream nodes beyond landmark `s`. Potential leakage: using whole `prefix` to predict `Pr(breach)` at `t=0` – peeks into future (e.g., knowing S13 will have Rework+Escalate).

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

We propose 4 analyses (minimum 3) spanning transfer, competing-risk, and informative censoring:

**A – Primary (governance): AJ competing-risk, T-A censor for E1 / T-B competing for E2**

*   Resolve = interest, Withdraw/Reject = competing, Transfer = censored (E1) vs competing (E2). Open = censored.
*   Expect: `S_KM(72)=0.64`, `F_resolve(72)0.30`, `F_withdraw+reject0.22`, `F_transfer(72)0.06` (only S08 68, S21 26 inside 72), remainder0.42 pending/censored. Cohort stratified shows B slightly higher early resolve but similar tail.

**B – Strict internal SLA (transfer = failure / competing + composite failure):**

*   Encode Transfer as competing failure that contributes to `1-F_resolve(72)` as bad outcome (worst-case governance). Also pool Withdraw/Reject as failure for `Pr(not resolved on time)`.
*   Effect: `F_resolve(72)` unchanged but `Pr(failure by 72)=F_withdraw+F_reject+F_transfer+F_late` increases from ~0.40 to ~0.45 at 72. The 68h transfer (S08) now moves mass *before* horizon, lowering apparent `S_EF(72)` earlier, making A vs B cohort breach gap larger (B has 1 transfer <72, A has 1 – balanced – so conclusion stable). If instead transfer best-case (resolve on time), `F_resolve` would rise. **Reversal risk:** Conclusion that "B improves on-time resolution" could reverse to "no improvement" if transfers are counted as successes.

**C – Informative censoring / staleness weighting (IPCW & gap threshold):**

*   Treat open cases with large `last_event_gap_h` (>30h: S09 31, S11 55, S25 48, S26 72) as likely abandoned / never-to-resolve. (i) Impute them as censored with IPCW weights inversely to gap, or bound analysis: (C-low) keep as censored, (C-high) reclassify as competing `Withdraw` at age. (ii) RMST sensitivity excluding stale vs including.
*   Expect: S(72) upper bound (keep censored) =0.64; lower bound (reclassify 4 stale as competing at 72)  `n` at 72 drops, `F_censored` mass shifts to failure, `S(72)` estimate conditional on not abandoning 0.55. Breach risk increases 8-10 pts. **Reversal risk:** Stale-heavy cohort A (S09,S11) vs B (S25,S26) balanced, but priority P1 stale (S11,S25) would drive priority-fairness conclusion: P1 breach appears worse under informative assumption; naive non-informative understates P1 tail risk.

**D – Policy/Immortal-time sensitivity (landmark vs baseline Escalate):**

*   (D1) Naive Cox with `escalated` baseline: HR for escalation appears protective (early escalations S04 29h, S18 12h resolve quickly). (D2) Time-varying / landmark at 24h: only cases still at-risk at 24h evaluated; escalation after 24h (S03, S13, S28) shows *higher* late hazard.
*   Expect: D1 yields RR 0.6 (escalation seems good), D2 yields HR 1.4 for being in Escalate state (longer remaining). **Reversal risk:** Operational recommendation ("escalate early to meet SLA") reverses to "escalation marks at-risk cases needing intervention, not a treatment effect" – causal interpretation invalid without randomization/compliance data.

Across A–D, **stable conclusions:** Late-resolution rate nontrivial (25-35% on-time resolve, 15% late among resolvers); ignoring competing/open overstates success. **Fragile conclusions:** Cohort B superiority, escalation effectiveness, and P1 vs P3 ordering flip under informative transfer/staleness assumptions. Report bounds, not point claims.

# 7. Operational interpretation

**From survival curves to actions:**

*   **Alerting (E1, landmark):** At each landmark `s=24,48h`, compute `Pr(T_resolve 72 | at-risk at s, history H(s))` from cause-specific S or multi-state `P_ij(s,72)`. If `Pr(breach | H(s)) > threshold` (e.g., >60% predicted `>72`), trigger: case review, workload re-prioritization, or SLA extension negotiation. Use *conditional* S, not CIF, because action is for still-open cases (S10 47h in Escalate, S24 67h in Review with gap4). Display as predicted median remaining time vs 72 – `RMST_lost`.

*   **Workload / capacity (E3):** `S_EF(72)` forecasts pending backlog at SLA horizon. Multiply by intake rate  expected cases requiring overtime. Use stratified by priority: P1 pending at 72 (S11, S25) gets dedicated escalation queue.

*   **Governance / SLA reporting (E2):** Report `F_resolve(72)` (on-time resolve), `F_late = F_resolve()-F_resolve(72)` (breach), `F_withdraw/reject/transfer` as competing. Do not report naive `4/13`. Provide AJ stacked CIF plot with uncertainty band. Use RMST: "on average, 38h of the 72h window is spent unresolved" – more intuitive for stakeholders than hazard ratios.

**Avoid causal misinterpretation:**

*   Risk scores are *prognostic*, not causal. `Escalate` flag predicts longer tail because sicker cases get escalated (confounding by indication). Cohort B vs A difference is *association* under non-random rollout and incomplete compliance; do not claim "AutoCheck reduces breach by X%". Present stratified curves and state Assumption: no unmeasured confounders required for causal claim – violated here.
*   Do not use future path (e.g., future Rework) as baseline predictor for staffing decisions at Submit – leakage would allocate resources based on unknowable future.

**Calibration & fairness checks:**

*   **Calibration:** At landmark s, bucket predicted `Pr(breach)` deciles vs observed breach among cases with known status at 72 (9 resolved on/off + 4 open>72 known breaches + 7 competing <72). For small n, show reliability diagram with bootstrap CI; expect miscalibration if transfers/stale censored non-informatively.
*   **Priority fairness:** Stratified `S(72)` and `F_resolve(72)` for P1 vs P2 vs P3. Check: Are P1 alerts thresholds equally calibrated? Data shows P1 has 2 transfers, 2 open stale, 2 fast resolves (S04 29, S18 12) – small n but risk of over-escalating P1 while under-serving P3 (S26 104 stalled P3). Compute `1-S(72)` per priority: P1 n=6 (A3+B5 but small), observe heterogeneity.
*   **Cohort fairness / drift:** Compare calibration in A vs B; automated screening change may shift feature distribution (AutoCheck missing in early A). Monitor `screen_order` performance drift; do not deploy B-trained model to A without rec calibration.

# 9. Limitations and additional data

**Missing data that would strengthen inference (prioritized):**

1.  **Exact event timestamps (not just total `duration_or_age_h`):** Needed for `time-to-Escalate`, sojourn in `Review`, cause-specific hazard timing, and to avoid fabricating order-only durations. Without them, semi-Markov sojourn distributions and stage-specific hazards are unidentifiable beyond prefix order. Assumption: ordering equals temporal order – needed for transition diagram.

2.  **External outcomes after `Transfer`:** Internal system is blind. To distinguish T-A vs T-B, need follow-up from external org (resolved externally and when) or at least transfer reason and SLA responsibility. Currently 3 transfers (S08 68, S21 26, S32 96) are sensitivity drivers.

3.  **Observation cutoff / calendar dates (Submit date, cutoff date):** `age` censoring depends on entry time; without it we cannot adjust for staggered entry, left truncation, or time-varying workload seasonality. Assumption: censoring time independent of hazard – violated if cutoff coincides with cohort rollout.

4.  **Time-varying workload / capacity (queue length, staffing):** Confounder for both resolution speed and transfer/withdraw decisions. Needed for IPCW for informative censoring (stale gaps 31-72h) and for cohort policy effect (non-random assignment).

5.  **Assignment mechanism & policy rollout compliance logs:** Cohort B began after automated-screening change but compliance incomplete. Need individual `AutoCheck` actually applied flag, eligibility, and propensity score variables (priority, channel) to estimate policy effect without immortal-time confounding. Currently `prefix` shows compliance proxy (e.g., S20 B without AutoCheck) but not intention-to-treat.

6.  **Reasons for `Withdraw/Reject` and their timing relative to Review:** To judge competing vs censorable (customer-initiated vs error). Needed for W-A vs W-B sensitivity.

7.  **Rework/escalation timestamps and actor:** To model `rework_count` and `last_event_gap_h` as time-varying covariates without leakage. Gap 72h (S26) suggests abandonment but we lack activity log between last event and cutoff to confirm dormancy vs unlogged updates.

8.  **Priority assignment rules and changes over time:** P1–P3 may be reassigned after screening; without it priority fairness checks confound.

9.  **Censoring reason for `open` (still active vs lost to follow-up):** Distinguish administrative censoring (cutoff) from loss to follow-up; the latter requires informative censoring model.

10. **Sample size & rare events:** n=32, only 13 resolves, 9 opens; all estimates have wide uncertainty (Greenwood CI for `S(72)`  ±0.12). Larger sample required for Fine-Gray or multi-state transition intensities beyond descriptive.

Until 1–5 are available, report AJ/KM with bounds for transfer and stale-gap assumptions, use landmarking for any escalation covariate, and avoid causal claims about cohort policy effectiveness.
