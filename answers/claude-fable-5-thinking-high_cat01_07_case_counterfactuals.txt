## 1. Feasibility model

Partial-order relation set (per case; `<` = must precede, `||` = may be concurrent):

```
GLOBAL CORE (all cases)
  Register < Triage
  Triage < IdentityCheck              (task must exist before service)
  Triage < DocumentCheck              (task creation; timing conditional below)
  IdentityCheck < Assessment_final
  DocumentCheck_latest < Assessment_final
  Assessment_final < Decision
  Decision < Notify

K-104 (high risk/value, no originals, complex docs)
  IdentityCheck || DocumentCheck
  Assessment_final < ComplianceReview_final < Decision
  EscalationAdvice || {IdentityCheck, DocumentCheck, PrelimCompliance}
  PrelimCompliance || {IdentityCheck, DocumentCheck}; PrelimCompliance < ComplianceReview_final

K-219 (low risk/value, originals required)
  OriginalsReceipt < DocumentCheck
  IdentityCheck || {waiting for originals}   (identity may finish before receipt)
  Assessment < Decision                      (no compliance review required)

K-337 (high risk/value, originals required, rework loops)
  OriginalsReceipt < DocumentCheck_1
  DocumentCheck_k < Assessment_k
  Assessment_k --RMI_k--> RMI_k < DocumentCheck_{k+1} < Assessment_{k+1}
  Assessment_last < ComplianceReview < Decision

K-411 (low risk/value, no originals)
  IdentityCheck || DocumentCheck             (allowed by rules; blocked only by system lock)
  {IdentityCheck, DocumentCheck} < Assessment < Decision
```

Conditional and declarative constraints:

```
C1. If originals required: OriginalsReceipt must precede DocumentCheck (K-219, K-337).
C2. If no originals required: IdentityCheck and DocumentCheck may run concurrently (K-104, K-411).
C3. If high-risk or high-value: ComplianceReview_final after the latest Assessment and
    before Decision (K-104, K-337).
C4. After any RequestMoreInfo: a NEW DocumentCheck and a NEW Assessment are mandatory before
    Decision; high-risk/value cases additionally need a new or reaffirmed compliance review.
C5. A preliminary compliance screen never substitutes for the final ComplianceReview.
C6. Four-eyes: performer(Assessment_final) != performer(ComplianceReview_final).
C7. Resource exclusivity: no resource serves two intervals simultaneously (C1 is a singleton
    pool; S1 advisory work is the only activity allowed to overlap other checks).
C8. Queue figures are forecasts with error; any counterfactual start time derived from a
    queue estimate carries that uncertainty.
C9. Decision requires M1; observed decisions occur in business hours, so evidence that
    becomes ready overnight or on weekends waits for the next M1 window.
C10. Timestamps must reflect true service intervals; batch-entered timestamps may not be
     used to claim concurrency (relevant to K-411).
```

Classification of the four interventions:
- **Timing only:** K-104 (moves escalation/compliance preparation earlier and pre-books C1; the activity set, routing to resources, and the decision rule are unchanged) and K-411 (creates both verification tasks at Triage so the already-permitted concurrency can actually occur; no routing or policy change).
- **Routing:** K-219 (reassigns Document Check from D1 to D2; sequence and policy unchanged, but service-time distribution and rework probability change with the resource).
- **Decision policy:** K-337 (prohibits the second Request-More-Info loop; this removes an allowed control-flow branch and forces a decision under residual uncertainty — it changes what the process is allowed to decide, not merely when).

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism: The sanctions ambiguity was visible at Triage (Mon 08:50) but travelled silently through Identity and Document Check until A1 rediscovered it at Assessment (Tue 19:10). That triggered a strictly serial tail: Escalation Advice Tue 19:20–21:20, then a ~16 h wait for the only certified reviewer C1 (queue 16–18 h), then a 4.5 h review in which "most evidence had to be read from scratch." The critical path after Assessment was therefore advice + queue + cold review = ~23 h.

Minimal intervention: One change at one moment: at Triage, ship a preliminary packet to S1 and book a provisional C1 slot. No activity is removed, no resource substituted, no rule relaxed; rules 3, 5, 10 explicitly permit the parallelism, and constraint C5 keeps the final review mandatory.

Temporal propagation: Identity, Document Check, and Assessment keep their factual timings — their queues are untouched by the intervention. Three tail components shrink: (a) Escalation Advice moves off the critical path entirely (S1 advises Mon morning in parallel, per the overlap allowance); (b) the C1 queue wait collapses from ~16 h to near zero because the slot was reserved just after the predicted Assessment finish; (c) the final review shortens 1–3 h because C1 has pre-read the file. Downstream, Decision moves earlier only insofar as M1 has a signing window: if C1 finishes Tue ~22:00, Decision realistically lands Wed 08:30–09:00, not overnight — earlier readiness is not identical to earlier completion.

Process-feasibility check: Register<Triage<{Identity‖Doc‖Advice‖Prelim}<Assessment<FinalCompliance<Decision<Notify holds. Four-eyes holds (A1 assesses, C1 reviews). C1 performs prelim and final work in disjoint intervals (C7). The prelim screen does not replace the final review (C5).

Best/base/worst case: Best: C1 slot lands Tue 19:30, 2 h review, Decision Wed 08:30, Notify Wed 08:40 (~10–12 h saved). Base: slot Wed 08:00–11:00, Decision ~11:30 (~7 h saved). Worst: the early packet is incomplete, C1 must re-read anyway, the reservation slips because the two urgent Monday cases pre-empt it, and the path collapses to roughly factual (~0–2 h saved) while other cases were still delayed.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Logs showing preliminary screens historically save no final-review time when evidence arrives after the screen; C1 reservation-breakage rates above ~50 %; or evidence that the two urgent Monday compliance cases carried higher penalty costs than K-104's delay, making the reservation net-negative system-wide.

### K-219

Factual mechanism: Triage optimized for first-pass accuracy by routing handwritten documents to specialist D1, accepting D1's long backlog (11–13 h forecast). Originals arrived Mon 17:30 but Document Check only ran Tue 09:30–13:30. The wait for D1, not D1's service time, dominated: ~16 h of idle originals.

Minimal intervention: Change only the resource assignment: D2 takes the check the moment originals arrive. Sequence, prerequisites (C1: OriginalsReceipt < DocumentCheck), and decision rules are untouched.

Temporal propagation: D2's forecast queue near receipt was ~5 h (Mon 16:00 snapshot), so service could begin ~Mon 22:30 (overnight service is evidenced by D1 working Mon 21:40–Tue 05:40 on K-104). D2 on difficult handwriting plausibly runs 6–8 h, finishing Tue 04:30–06:30 — still ~7–9 h before the factual finish. That pulls Assessment forward: A2's Tue 08:00 queue was ~6 h, giving a start ~Tue 11:00–14:00 and finish ~Tue 14:00–16:30, so Decision could sign Tue afternoon or Wed 08:30 at the latest. The whole gain comes from converting idle waiting-for-D1 into slower-but-earlier D2 service; the gain survives even though D2's service time exceeds D1's.

Process-feasibility check: OriginalsReceipt (Mon 17:30) precedes D2's start; Identity already complete (Mon 16:20); D2 serves one interval; no compliance review is required (low risk/value); Decision precedes Notify. The rework branch, if triggered, correctly regenerates DocumentCheck_2 and Assessment_2 per C4.

Best/base/worst case: Best: no clarification needed, Notify Tue ~16:30 (−16 h). Base: no rework but M1 signs Wed 08:30, Notify Wed 08:45 (−¼ day, mostly readiness gain). Worst: D2 requests clarification (plausibly 20–40 % on handwriting); external response 8–36 h plus a second Document Check and Assessment pushes Notify to Wed evening–Thu (+8 to +40 h vs factual). The expected value hinges almost entirely on that rework probability.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Historical first-pass-yield data showing D2's clarification rate on handwritten files above roughly 30–40 % (the breakeven region given the asymmetry of gains and losses); or evidence D2's realized queue at receipt was far above the 5 h forecast (C8).

### K-337

Factual mechanism: Two sequential evidence loops. The second loop (RMI 2, Fri 10:30) bought one confirmation at the cost of a weekend of external silence, DocumentCheck_3, Assessment_3, and a push of Compliance and Decision into the next week — roughly 4 calendar days for one residual inconsistency.

Minimal intervention: Minimal in edit distance (one branch pruned after Assessment 2) but not minimal in kind: it rewrites the decision policy. The process must now conclude under acknowledged uncertainty — approve with conditions or reject — rather than resolve it. Rule 7's rework machinery for loop 1 stays intact; only loop 2 is forbidden.

Temporal propagation: Assessment 2 (final) ends Fri 10:30. C1's queue was ~5 h Thursday and falling, so Compliance plausibly starts Fri ~15:30. The review likely runs long within or above its 3–6 h band (unresolved discrepancy means more judgement and documentation), finishing Fri evening. M1 signs Monday 08:30 (C9: no observed weekend decisions), Notify ~09:00 Monday — about 2 days earlier than factual Wed 09:10. Note the timing chain: the saving comes from deleting {weekend RMI wait + DocCheck_3 + Assessment_3 + Tuesday compliance queue}, partially given back to the Friday-evening/Monday-morning boundary.

Process-feasibility check: Assessment_2 becomes Assessment_final; C3 forces ComplianceReview after it and before Decision — satisfied. Four-eyes: A1 vs C1 — satisfied. The genuine feasibility risk is rule 6: C1 may lawfully issue its own Request More Info, which would resurrect the forbidden loop through another door; the intervention as stated cannot bind C1 without a further policy change.

Best/base/worst case: Best: C1 accepts, approve-with-conditions Monday, −2 days, discrepancy later proves immaterial. Base: −1.5 to −2 days with tighter conditions and modest residual loss exposure. Worst: either C1 refuses and issues RMI (time saving ≈ 0, plus wasted Friday review), or the case is rejected/approved wrongly — appeal costs, re-entry, or realized losses that dwarf the 2-day gain.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Data showing second-loop RMIs frequently change decisions or catch material errors (i.e., loop 2 has high information value); high appeal-overturn rates on decisions made under residual discrepancies; or regulator guidance that conditional approvals with open inconsistencies are indefensible.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: A system defect, not a rule, serialized the checks: Triage created only the identity task; a case lock blocked document-task ownership until Identity completed (Tue 13:20), so D2 started Tue 15:00 and the case reached the assessment queue at Tue 19:30, landing in a congested Wednesday.

Minimal intervention: Create both tasks at Triage and apply a controlled lock workaround with real-time timestamps. Nothing about routing, resources, or policy changes; the intervention merely lets rule 3's already-permitted concurrency happen.

Temporal propagation: Identity is unaffected (I2 queue governs; start Tue 11:30 as before). Document Check's start moves from "after Identity + D2 queue" to "after Triage + D2 queue": with D2's ~4 h forecast, start ~Tue 13:35, finish ~18:05 given the factual 4.5 h service. Readiness for Assessment improves ~1.4 h. But the binding constraint downstream is A2's queue plus the overnight boundary: factually Assessment started Wed 09:30. An 18:05 arrival instead of 19:30 might improve Wednesday queue position slightly (Assessment ~08:30–11:00), pulling Decision from 14:00 to ~11:30 — or might change nothing. This is the cleanest illustration that earlier readiness ≠ earlier completion when the next stage is the bottleneck.

Process-feasibility check: Rule 3 permits concurrency (no originals). I2 and D2 are distinct resources, so C7 holds even with overlapping intervals. C10 requires the workaround to log true service times; batch entry would fabricate concurrency and invalidate exactly the measurement the pilot needs.

Best/base/worst case: Best: assessment slot found Tue evening or first Wed slot, Notify Wed ~11:45 (−2 to −3 h). Base: same Wednesday pattern, Notify ~Wed 13:30 (−1 h). Worst: no completion gain at all (queue absorbs everything), plus a poorly executed workaround creates data-integrity or task-ownership conflicts. Downside on this single case is near zero if logging is done properly.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Timestamps revealing the workaround's "concurrency" is batch-entered fiction; incident reports of lock conflicts corrupting case state; or evidence that assessment congestion makes readiness gains systematically worthless while the workaround carries operational cost.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 early escalation + C1 reservation | +2 h S1 early; +1–3 h C1 prelim, −1–3 h C1 final (≈ net C1 neutral per case, but time-shifted and reserved) | Compliance queue for escalated cases | Compliance queue for non-reserved cases; possibly S1 advisory backlog | Urgent compliance cases displaced by reservations | Positive at low adoption; turns negative as reservations saturate the singleton C1 | C1 is a single server: reservation utilization above ~80–90 % makes waits for unreserved cases explode |
| K-219 D1→D2 substitution | −4 h D1, +6–8 h D2 per case; +rework load on D-pool and A2 when clarification triggers | D1 queue | D2 queue; Assessment queue when rework doubles arrivals | Standard cases relying on D2's short queue; complex cases if later pushed to D2 | Mixed: positive while D2 has slack, negative once D2 saturates or rework rate is high | Rework probability × 8–36 h external delay: a small rate change swings expected value across zero |
| K-337 suppress second rework loop | −1 DocCheck, −1 Assessment, −1 external wait per case; +1–2 h per compliance review (harder judgement) | Document and Assessment queues (fewer rework re-entries) | Compliance queue (longer, harder reviews); downstream appeals/re-entry stream | Cases behind the longer compliance reviews; future cases if appeals re-enter | Positive on throughput, negative tail risk on quality/loss; net sign depends on error costs | Decision-error cost is discontinuous: one large wrongful approval can outweigh hundreds of saved days |
| K-411 parallel task creation | No net service-time change; work time-shifted earlier; small coordination overhead | Document-stage waiting (cases stop queueing behind their own identity check) | Assessment queue (earlier, burstier arrivals) | All cases in the assessment queue on heavy days | Positive to neutral; benefit capped by assessment capacity | Synchronized earlier arrivals create batch surges at Assessment; queueing delay grows nonlinearly with utilization |

Rollout of each policy to 100 similar cases in one week changes the analysis qualitatively, because single-case counterfactuals held queues fixed and a policy does not. Reserving C1 is the starkest failure: C1 offers roughly 40–60 h of weekly capacity, while 100 high-risk cases demand 300–600 h of review plus preliminary screens. Reservations cannot create capacity; they only re-order a hopeless queue, so unreserved and urgent cases are displaced, the Monday 20 h backlog becomes structural, and the bottleneck deepens exactly where the organization is thinnest. The policy is only viable with strict eligibility (a small sanctioned-ambiguity subset) or a second certified reviewer. Wholesale D1→D2 substitution migrates the document bottleneck rather than removing it: D2's 2–5 h queues exist because D2 receives overflow-sized demand; 100 redirected cases at 4–8 h each saturate D2 within days, while D1 idles — and every extra clarification on handwriting injects an 8–36 h external wait plus a duplicated Document Check and Assessment, so rework can consume more capacity than the substitution saves. Suppressing second rework loops is the one policy that reduces total work (fewer document checks, fewer assessments, fewer external waits, fewer weekend-crossing stalls), but it exports load to Compliance — reviews become longer and harder — and creates a delayed rework stream in the form of appeals and re-entries that the event log will not show for weeks. Parallelizing task creation adds no work but synchronizes arrivals: 100 cases reaching assessment half a day earlier and closer together turn A1/A2's 4–12 h forecast queues into a standing morning surge; the bottleneck migrates from a software lock to human assessors, and M1's signing window becomes visible as the next constraint. Finally, mandated real-time timestamps matter at scale: if workarounds batch-enter times, measured "concurrency" and queue statistics become fiction, and every subsequent capacity model inherits the error.

## 5. Robustness comparison

| rank | intervention | robustness across plausible assumptions | expected benefit | principal risk | reversibility | information value of a pilot |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | K-411 parallel task creation | High: removes a system defect; benefit ≥ 0 under almost any queue realization; no policy or quality channel | Small per case (0–3 h completion, ~1.5 h readiness), but applies to every no-originals case at near-zero cost | False concurrency from bad logging; assessment-queue surges at scale | Fully reversible (configuration change) | High: cleanly measures readiness-vs-completion gap and true assessment bottleneck |
| 2 | K-104 early escalation / compliance prep | Medium-high for a narrow eligible subset: gains rely on reservation integrity and packet completeness, but no quality degradation channel and four-eyes preserved | Moderate (4–12 h on escalated high-risk cases), plus fewer late compliance surprises | Displacement of urgent cases at the singleton C1; wasted prelim effort at scale | Reversible (stop reserving); some sunk prelim effort | High: reveals how much prelim work truly saves in final review, and reservation-breakage rates |
| 3 | K-219 D2 substitution | Medium: sign of benefit flips with the handwriting-rework rate and D2 queue realization | Moderate when it works (10–18 h) on low-risk standard cases | Rework cascade (8–36 h external waits, duplicated checks); D2 saturation at scale | Fully reversible (routing rule) | Medium-high: first-pass-yield by document type is cheap to measure and decisive |
| 4 | K-337 suppress second rework loop | Low: outcome depends on unobserved information value of loop 2, C1's willingness to conclude, and error costs that dwarf time savings | Large per case (~2 days) on multi-loop cases | Wrong decisions, appeal exposure, compliance defensibility; C1 can lawfully reopen the loop | Hard to reverse per case (a signed decision stands); policy itself revocable | Medium: a pilot observes time but decision-quality errors surface too slowly to learn safely |

Ranking rationale: cycle time alone would favor K-337, but robustness weights feasibility (K-337 can be vetoed by C1's own RMI right), quality/compliance (K-337 and K-219 carry the only degradation channels), spillovers (K-104's reservation displaces the scarcest resource), and learnability (K-411 and K-104 pilots produce fast, safe measurements; K-337's key outcome — decision error — is slow and costly to observe).

## 6. Pilot designs

| pilot element | intervention 1 (K-411 parallel task creation) | intervention 2 (K-104 early escalation / compliance prep) |
|---|---|---|
| eligibility | Cases with no originals required and digital standard documents (both checks executable at Triage) | High-risk or high-value cases where Triage flags a concrete ambiguity (e.g., sanctions) at registration |
| assignment rule | Random 50/50 at Triage: parallel task creation vs legacy serial creation, stratified by weekday and document type | Alternate-week (or random-by-case) assignment: prelim packet + provisional C1 slot vs status quo; cap concurrent reservations at 1–2 to protect urgent cases |
| sample/support logic | ~100–200 cases (2–3 weeks): powered to detect a 1–2 h mean completion change given high queue variance; stratification guards against Monday-surge confounding | Eligible cases are rare (~5–15/week), so run 4–6 weeks; support limited to flagged-ambiguity cases — do not extrapolate to unflagged high-risk cases |
| primary outcome | Register-to-Notify cycle time; secondarily Register-to-assessment-ready time (separates readiness from completion) | Assessment-complete-to-Decision interval (the tail the intervention targets), plus total cycle time |
| process-path outcome | Fraction of cases with genuinely overlapping Identity/Document service intervals; conformance of task-creation events at Triage | Fraction of cases where Escalation Advice precedes Assessment; fraction where final compliance starts within 2 h of its reserved slot |
| quality outcome | Assessment first-pass acceptance rate (checks that earlier, concurrent verification does not degrade inputs) | Final compliance review duration and rework/RMI rate out of compliance; audit rating of decision documentation |
| queue-spillover outcome | A1/A2 queue length and wait distribution for non-pilot cases, especially morning surge on high-arrival days | Waiting time of non-reserved compliance cases and count of urgent cases displaced past their target; S1 advisory backlog |
| logging validation | Compare event timestamps to system-session telemetry; flag batch-entered blocks (identical or end-of-day clustered timestamps) as protocol violations | Verify prelim-screen and final-review intervals for C1 are disjoint and real-time; verify reservation creation/consumption events exist |
| stopping rule | Stop early if lock-workaround incidents corrupt any case state, or if false-concurrency rate exceeds ~10 % of pilot cases | Stop early if any urgent compliance case breaches its regulatory deadline due to a reservation, or if >50 % of reservations are broken/rescheduled |

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-411's parallel task creation. It deletes an accidental serialization that no rule requires, its benefit is non-negative under essentially any realization of queues and service times, it touches no decision or quality channel, and it is reversible by configuration. Its honest limitation — that assessment congestion may absorb the readiness gain — is itself valuable knowledge that the pilot measures cheaply.

The most dangerous intervention despite its speed is K-337's suppression of the second rework loop. It buys roughly two days on the clock, but it does so by changing what the process is allowed to conclude, not how fast it concludes. The unresolved discrepancy does not disappear; it is converted into loss exposure, appeal risk, or compliance indefensibility, and one materially wrong approval can outweigh hundreds of saved case-days. It is also fragile in feasibility terms: the compliance reviewer retains the right to request more information, which can silently resurrect the loop.

Local counterfactuals are least transportable for K-104's compliance reservation. For a single case, holding the system fixed, reserving C1 is nearly free; as a policy over 100 weekly cases it is arithmetically impossible, because a singleton reviewer with tens of hours of weekly capacity cannot honor hundreds of hours of reservations — the local gain is precisely the displacement of someone else's wait.

The most valuable additional data are: true queue-length and wait-time event streams (arrival-to-start per station) to replace point forecasts; first-pass-yield and clarification rates by document type and by D1 versus D2; the historical information value of second-loop RMIs (how often they changed decisions); appeal and post-decision loss outcomes linked to case IDs; and session-level telemetry to distinguish genuine concurrency from batch-entered timestamps.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural` — The normative rules (prerequisites, four-eyes, compliance-after-final-assessment) are binding and were not violated in the factual logs; counterfactuals must satisfy the same partial orders.
2. `temporal` — Queue snapshots are unbiased but noisy forecasts; counterfactual start times derived from them carry at least ±2–3 h of error, and all savings are reported as ranges accordingly.
3. `temporal` — Overnight service is feasible for Identity/Document pools (observed for D1, Mon 21:40–Tue 05:40), but M1 decisions occur only in business-hour windows, so overnight readiness waits for the next signing slot.
4. `resource` — C1 is the sole certified compliance reviewer all week (no substitute is certified mid-week), making compliance a single-server queue whose reservation or elongation directly displaces other cases.
5. `resource` — In single-case counterfactuals, all other cases keep their factual resource bookings; queue positions freed or taken by the focal case shift other cases by at most the focal case's service time.
6. `behavioral` — D2 requests clarification on difficult handwriting in roughly 20–40 % of cases versus a materially lower rate for D1; this parameter dominates the sign of the K-219 counterfactual.
7. `quality` — The residual discrepancy in K-337 is of uncertain materiality: with meaningful probability it is immaterial (conditions suffice), and with non-trivial probability it signals an error whose cost exceeds the two-day time saving.
8. `interference` — Single-case interventions leave queues approximately fixed, but at 100-case scale arrival synchronization, C1 reservation saturation, D2 overload, and rework-stream feedback make queue behavior nonlinear and the local estimates non-transportable.
