## 1. Materiality Framework

Raw alignment cost is an **edit distance**: how many log/model moves were needed to reconcile a trace with the process tree under a uniform cost of 1 per visible move. It is not a measure of money lost, control failure, customer harm, or evidential certainty. Severity and confidence must therefore be assessed separately.

**Severity 1–5, considering jointly:**

| Dimension | Question asked |
|---|---|
| Control objective | Was a preventive control bypassed — fraud screen, approval before payment, coverage/loss assessment — or only a recording/notification step affected? P3 makes pay-before-approval severe by definition. |
| Amount / exposure | EUR exposure at risk when the control failed (480k  800), and whether exposure was actually paid out. |
| Reversibility | Payment released to a (possibly changed) beneficiary is largely irreversible; a missing log record is fully reversible/correctable. |
| Timing | Did the deviation occur *before* the irreversible step (preventive failure) or after it (detective/recording issue)? Notification within 24h (P4) is a timing test, not just presence test. |
| Customer / regulatory harm | Wrong-payee fraud, unpaid legitimate claimant, late notification, denied-claim handling. |
| Recurrence | A one-off differs from a systemic pattern (74 retry duplicates; 4 synchronous-but-noncompliant auto-approvals). Recurrence raises severity of otherwise small items because it indicates a broken mechanism, not an accident. |
| Evidence reliability | Signed external documents and bank timestamps are strong; verbal recollection and mutable text-field links are weak. Weak evidence raises *assurance* severity even where the underlying act may have occurred. |
| Model scope | The tree covers payable claims (P6 disputed). Moves "missing" because a claim was legitimately denied/withdrawn are model-scope artefacts, not operational failures. |

**Severity scale:** 5 = preventive control bypassed on high exposure / irreversible payment without required fraud+manual gate; 4 = data-aware policy gate bypassed or payment sequencing failure with material exposure; 3 = policy failure or control-evidence gap with moderate exposure; 2 = probable activity-performed-but-unrecorded, or notification-evidence weakness, low direct loss; 1 = harmless technical noise, label/scope artefact, or fully fitting behaviour.

**Confidence differs from severity.** Severity asks *"how bad if true?"*; confidence asks *"how sure are we, and of what exactly?"* A missing event (`M`) is **not proof of non-occurrence** — it proves absence from this log. Confidence is high where independent evidence converges (bank timestamp 10:03 vs approval 10:20 in CL-604; identical message ID + transport retry in CL-603), medium where plausible external performance exists but linkage is weak (CL-606, CL-608), and low where the only support is verbal (CL-602's claimed fraud check — low confidence it occurred, high confidence the *evidence* is absent). A case can be high-severity/low-confidence (CL-602: investigate urgently precisely because we cannot yet prove the screen happened) or low-severity/high-confidence (CL-601 telemetry).

**Deviation taxonomy used below:** (a) harmless technical noise, (b) event-label mismatch, (c) missing evidence / logging-integration gap, (d) process-model mismatch / scope, (e) actual operational deviation, (f) data-aware policy failure (model-conformant, policy-violating).

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

A missing event is **absence from this log, not proof of non-occurrence**. Each hypothesis set below states what would discriminate.

**CL-602 — missing Fraud Screen (EUR 480k, HIGH, bank change).**
H1: Screen never performed; verbal claim is post-hoc rationalisation. H2: Screen performed off-system (phone/desktop tool) and never recorded. H3: Screen performed in a fraud tool whose feed failed.
*Discriminators:* fraud-platform query logs by claim ID/approver/timestamp; telephony records; payee-change audit trail and subsequent account behaviour; whether the approver's other cases show the same `M(Fraud Screen)` signature (pattern  systemic H2/H3; isolated  H1 concern); any loss event on the changed account.

**CL-604 — payment before approval.**
H1: Payment deliberately released early (override). H2: Payment interface releases on assessment completion, racing the approval recording (systemic race). H3: Approval actually granted earlier verbally/systemically and recorded late at 10:20 (documentation lag, payment genuinely authorised).
*Discriminators:* payment-release user/system ID and trigger event; approval-workflow audit trail (created vs committed timestamps); recurrence scan for other L(Pay)…M(Pay) alignments; approver interview corroborated by contemporaneous messages.

**CL-606 / Pattern B — external adjuster assessment.**
H1: Assessment fully performed externally, equivalent to Assess Loss; pure integration gap. H2: External report is narrower than the internal Assess Loss standard (e.g., no quantum validation), so the control is partially missing. H3 (for the 2 unlinked cases): document belongs to a different claim.
*Discriminators:* content checklist of signed reports vs Assess Loss procedure; stable document-to-case ID presence; adjuster engagement letter timestamps vs downstream Fraud Screen (ordering already supports H1 here).

**CL-608 — notification evidence.**
H1: Claimant notified at +3h via portal; core-log gap only. H2: CRM record linked to the wrong claim or created retrospectively.
*Discriminators:* portal message ID immutably bound to claim ID; claimant open/read receipt or subsequent contact history; CRM record creation vs modification timestamps (mutable text field makes modification history decisive).

**CL-609 / CL-610 / Pattern E — synchronous auto-approval.**
H1: Routing rules never encoded amount/bank-change conditions (design gap). H2: Rules encoded but a risk-flag feed (bank change) failed for CL-610. H3: Staff manually selected the auto path (workaround).
*Discriminators:* approval-engine rule configuration and decision logs showing which attributes were evaluated; bank-change event timestamp vs the engine's flag state at decision time; distribution — the 4 synchronous violations sharing attributes points to H1, isolated anomalies to H2/H3.

**CL-611 / Pattern C — label mismatch.**
H1: Pure relabelling; same activity, same performers, same timestamps distribution. H2: "Assess Damage" is a genuinely different (e.g., vehicle-only) sub-activity, and true Assess Loss is sometimes missing behind it.
*Discriminators:* performer/resource and form fields behind each label; whether any case contains *both* labels (would support H2); timestamp/duration profile comparison.

**CL-607 / CL-612 / Pattern D — scope.**
H1: Cases are in scope and the model is incomplete (add denial/withdrawal branches). H2: Cases are out of scope and should be filtered from the conformance population.
*Discriminators:* this is a governance decision, not an empirical one — but intake volumes, regulatory reporting obligations for denials, and whether denial handling has its own controls determine the answer; the process owner's vs model owner's mandates must be reconciled in writing. Empirically, check whether denied/withdrawn cases show anomalies *within* their own lifecycle (e.g., payment after withdrawal), which would justify modelling them.

**CL-603 / Pattern A — duplicate notification.**
H1: One send, double-recorded via retry. H2: Claimant actually received two notifications (customer-impacting).
*Discriminators:* already strong (same message ID); confirm at the message-broker level that one dispatch occurred and check claimant-contact complaints across the 74 cases.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

**1. The cost function is uniform and business-blind.** Every visible move costs 1 by configuration, with no business weighting. A missing fraud screen on EUR 480,000 (CL-602, cost 1) therefore scores identically to a duplicated notification retry (CL-603, cost 1) and *lower* than UI telemetry on a flawless EUR 1,200 claim (CL-601, cost 3) and lower than a mere synonym (CL-611, cost 2). Any ranking built on these numbers inverts true risk.

**2. One semantic event can cost 2; one catastrophe can cost 1.** CL-611's single activity, recorded under a variant label, generates both an `L` and an `M` move — cost 2 for zero operational deviation. CL-602's absent fraud gate generates a single `M` — cost 1 for a triple-trigger P2 failure. Cost counts *edit operations*, not *things that went wrong*.

**3. Data conditions are invisible to control-flow alignment.** The tree's `X` branch permits Auto Approve unconditionally, so CL-609 (EUR 18,000 > EUR 5,000 limit) and CL-610 (bank change one hour before payment) align with **cost 0 — perfect fitness — while violating P1 and, for CL-610, P2**. Zero cost certifies only that the *sequence* is model-legal; it is silent on amount, risk, and recency attributes that carry the actual policy.

**4. Hidden moves and scope decide what cost can even see.** CL-605's `T(tau)` is fitting by construction — correctly cost 0, but a reminder that "synchronous" includes silent model plumbing, not observed business events. Conversely, CL-607 and CL-612 incur the maximum cost 4 because the model lacks denial/withdrawal exits: the "missing" approvals and payments are missing *because staff correctly stopped*. Cost here measures model scope (P6), not performance.

**5. Cost cannot distinguish "not done" from "done but not logged here."** CL-606's `M(Assess Loss)` (cost 1) coexists with a signed external adjuster report predating approval; CL-608's `M(Notify Claimant)` (cost 1) coexists with a portal message at +3 hours satisfying P4's window and channel. The alignment faithfully reports log absence; the business meaning depends entirely on evidence outside the log — strong for CL-606, weaker (mutable link) for CL-608. Identical cost, different residual risk: auditability rather than loss.

**6. Irreversibility and timing are not priced.** CL-604's cost 3 buys, in alignment terms, a tidy repair: the early real payment is discarded as an `L` move and the required payment marked `M`. In business terms, EUR 90,000 left the bank at 10:03, before the 10:20 approval, with no logged fraud screen — a severe, largely irreversible P3 failure. The alignment *understates* it precisely because reordering is cheap for an aligner and impossible for a bank. Likewise CL-602 vs CL-604: cost ranks them 1 < 3, while both are severity 5 for different evidentiary reasons.

**7. Duplicated telemetry inflates cost without any customer effect.** CL-601 and CL-603 (and the 74-case retry pattern) show cost accumulating from instrumentation, not operations. Activity granularity — what the logger chooses to emit — is a logging-design decision (P5), yet it directly moves the metric.

In short: alignment cost is a *diagnostic signal about log–model distance under an arbitrary cost function*. Business risk requires the overlay of control criticality, exposure, timing relative to irreversible steps, data attributes, external evidence, recurrence, and model scope. Used alone, it would have investigators start with denied claims (CL-607) and workbench telemetry (CL-601) while the two zero-cost policy failures and the EUR 480,000 unevidenced payment sat at the bottom of the queue.
