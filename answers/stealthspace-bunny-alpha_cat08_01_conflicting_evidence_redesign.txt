# 1. Current-process diagnosis

## Performance: the frequent route is fast, but its exceptions are also frequent

- **Intake is predominantly automated:** 3,600 of 5,000 cases go to the auto screen and 1,400 to manual eligibility review. Of the 2,550 auto-screen-to-fast-approval transitions, 2,300 proceed to replacement dispatch. The combination of **2,790 observed fast approvals and 2,300 dispatches** explains why this route dominates operational volume.
- The fast-route activity durations are short: Intake  Auto eligibility screen has a **0.5-hour median**, Auto screen  Fast approval **0.4 hours**, and Fast approval  Dispatch replacement **1.8 hours**. This supports retaining automation for genuinely straightforward claims.
- But elapsed-time distributions are right-skewed, particularly on exception paths:
  - Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility review: **26-hour median, 88-hour p90**.
  - Repair quote  Customer approval: **21-hour median, 80-hour p90**.
  - Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen: **70-hour median, 180-hour p90**.
  - Customer complaint/reopen  Escalation board: **42-hour median, 130-hour p90**.
- These edge medians must not be added to estimate total claim time: they are conditional elapsed times, potentially on different cases, not a decomposition of a common case’s total cycle time.

## Quality: apparent low cost substantially narrows after downstream failure

The main contradiction is explicit:

- Fast approval is **31% cheaper before rework but only 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and service credits**.
- Its reported complaint/reopen rate is **14.1%**, versus **3.3%** for Repair quote  Customer approval  Repair device.
- Fast approval generates both **140 direct complaint/reopen transitions** and **260 complaint/reopen transitions after dispatch**.
- High-value devices have a **12.5% repair-rework rate**, compared with **4.0%** for low-risk devices. The high-value incorrect-replacement exposure is **EUR 410**, versus EUR 95 for accessories.
- The temporal profile identifies **incorrect replacement-part selection** as a driver of the long dispatch-to-complaint path.

Thus, the fast route should not be eliminated wholesale. Its dispatch controls and part-selection logic need to change. Conversely, the reviewed/repair route is not a proven universal solution: it may contain better risks, better diagnostics, and more appropriate fulfillment choices. The observed 3.3% complaint rate is a useful comparator, **not proof that elapsed review time itself causes quality**.

## Evidence completion and technical resolution are recurring sources of delay

- The DFG shows **1,430 entries to Request missing evidence**:
  - 890 return to Manual eligibility review;
  - 320 abandon;
  - 220 move to Fraud/risk review.
- Separately, **26% of cases entering missing evidence experience it at least twice**. These are different statistics: one describes transitions; the other describes repeated requests within cases.
- The unclear-defect/missing-evidence segment is **22% of cases**, has a **140-hour median cycle time**, and has the highest abandonment and repeated-request rates.

The 34-hour mean and 26-hour median for evidence-request waiting show that adding more downstream eligibility reviewers would not necessarily address the principal delay. Customers may still be waiting for evidence, a decision, or part-price confirmation while a reviewer remains available.

## Compliance risk is concentrated, but its exact incidence is not established

Three concrete deviations require control redesign:

1. **Fast approval before evidence completeness**, potentially preceding a noncompliant dispatch.
2. **Complaint/reopen after close**, indicating that a terminal state has not necessarily meant the claim’s downstream obligations are finished.
3. **Dispatch without risk review for high-value devices**, threatening the R1 control.

The normative rules should remain binding:

- Claims above EUR 400 require Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review before Fast approval.
- Dispatch requires confirmed evidence completeness, except documented Premium low-risk accessory cases.
- Reopened claims require Manual eligibility review before final close or denial.

The **0.91 log fitness and 68% of traces fitting** the normative net are not a measured compliance rate. They show that the discovered log and the normative model do not align well enough to infer exact violations from the fit summary. Low model precision further warns against interpreting every discovered combination as an authorized or optimal route.

## Customer and resource trade-offs are segment-specific

- **Premium low-risk accessories:** fast path median 8 hours versus 46 manually, but complaints are 6% versus 3%. Service-credit exposure rises beyond 24 hours. Blanket manual review would exchange a modest complaint improvement for a substantial SLA and cost disadvantage.
- **Standard low-risk accessories:** 13 versus 52 hours, 8% versus 4% complaints, with stated tolerance up to 72 hours if status visibility is good.
- **Premium high-value devices:** 20 versus 96 hours, but 22% versus 5% complaints. This is a strong case for selective review, not an argument for preserving fast approval.
- **Standard high-value devices:** 28 versus 120 hours, 18% versus 6% complaints. Slower handling can be justified by lower failure exposure, but not by assuming every repair must be manual.
- Complaints are an outcome signal, not a pure defect-quality measure or a compliance measure. They can reflect part correctness, expectation setting, repair durability, or service recovery.

Resource constraints reinforce this segmentation:

- Seven manual reviewers become queue-sensitive above **82% utilization**. A rule update that increases manual inflow by **15–35%** must therefore be capacity-tested.
- Two Fraud/risk specialists are vulnerable to Monday and campaign peaks.
- Three repair-quote technicians are delayed primarily by **part-price confirmation**, suggesting that adding technician hours alone may not solve the wait.
- Two quality inspectors have spare capacity outside repair campaigns, creating an opportunity to redeploy some capacity to prevention.
- The escalation board has only one daily 45-minute slot. Premium service recovery cannot simply be added to that capacity without disrupting other work.

There is also a measurement limitation: the DFG displays **5,000 starts but 3,625 terminal observations**, and complaint/rework activity can overlap or repeat. The artifacts are therefore not a reconciled case-level accounting. Counts should guide investigation; reported segment/path outcome rates should not be mechanically recomputed from transition counts.

# 2. Redesign A: Policy-enforced risk routing with a narrow pre-dispatch gate

## Redesigned process flow

1. **Create a risk profile at intake.** Record claim value, Premium/Standard status, defect type, evidence status, and replacement-part fit. Missing or uncertain attributes default to review, not auto approval.
2. **Replace the current branching rules with a versioned policy engine.**
   - Complete, low-risk Standard accessory claims and qualifying Premium accessory claims remain eligible for the fast route.
   - A claim above EUR 400 cannot enter Fast approval without the required Manual eligibility or Fraud/risk review.
   - Fraud/risk review is also triggered by risk signals independent of value.
   - Repair fulfillment cannot be used to bypass a required review merely because it avoids the “Fast approval” node.
3. **Use one structured evidence request.** Reuse evidence already accepted elsewhere in the case, identify exactly what is missing, and distinguish unchanged information from facts requiring a new eligibility or fraud decision. Do not automatically recycle the entire case through Manual eligibility review.
4. **Add a pre-dispatch correctness gate.**
   - Low-risk claims use catalog/model compatibility checks.
   - High-value claims receive a human confirmation of device identity and replacement-part fit.
   - The gate records evidence completeness and approval provenance.
   - Premium low-risk exceptions to R2 are explicitly identified; they are not treated as general exceptions.
5. **Preserve the downstream control loop.** Repairs pass Quality check before close. A complaint or newly discovered defect changes the case to an active reopened state; the case cannot receive a final close or denial until the required Manual eligibility review is complete.

## Segments, paths, and activities targeted

This design principally targets the **46% of dispatches reached through Fast approval**, especially:

- The 35% high-value-device portfolio, where compliance and incorrect-part exposure are greatest.
- Standard low-risk accessories that are genuinely complete and low risk.
- Premium low-risk accessories where a lightweight check is preferable to a full 46-hour manual route.
- Uncertain claims whose risk is currently misclassified by outdated, non-segment-aware auto rules.

It does not route every high-value device through a full repair workflow; it separates **approval assurance** from the later choice between replacement and repair.

## Mechanism of improvement

The redesign spends the inexpensive auto screen where it is valuable and places scarce review capacity on explicit risk criteria. It addresses the discovered-policy mismatch rather than treating the frequent path as inherently correct.

The pre-dispatch gate attacks the identified wrong-part mechanism before dispatch. Its expected six-to-18-hour delay can use some of the underutilized inspection capacity, although peak-period availability must be checked.

Reusing accepted evidence should reduce unnecessary 34-hour evidence-to-review cycles. The two-week auto-rule update is feasible, but the two-week implementation estimate should not be misrepresented as the timeline for integrating evidence history, part validation, and reopen-state controls.

## Deliberate sacrifices and unresolved issues

- Some claims currently completed in eight hours will take longer. A six-to-18-hour gate can push a Premium low-risk case beyond its 24-hour credit-sensitive window.
- More selective rules could increase manual inflow by 15–35%, potentially moving the bottleneck immediately to the seven reviewers.
- The design does not resolve the underlying causes of quote delay or missing-evidence abandonment. Those require Redesign B’s operating model.
- Part validation catches errors but does not by itself improve the original diagnostic decision.
- The cost effect cannot be quantified from the available 31%/9% comparison because that comparison does not isolate routing selection from downstream failure.

# 3. Redesign B: Resolve evidence and part fit before committing fulfillment

## Redesigned process flow

1. **Create a provisional case immediately after intake.** Intake records the required evidence, defect, value, segment, and likely replacement/repair route. It does not prematurely bank Fast approval merely because a front-end completeness indicator is green.
2. **Open two bounded resolution workstreams where applicable.**
   - **Evidence workstream:** obtain missing proof, explain the claim in customer-appropriate language, and identify any eligibility or fraud implications.
   - **Technical workstream:** validate the device identity, likely part or repair, and part-price requirements.
   
   Evidence collection and technical preparation can proceed concurrently; neither requires a full manual eligibility restart simply because the other workstream is waiting.
3. **Introduce an evidence-resolution hub with assisted and digital channels.**
   - Provide a targeted checklist and example documents.
   - Allow an immediate “missing/not applicable” response rather than forcing repeated identical requests.
   - Offer telephone or assisted completion for customers with low digital literacy.
   - Give customers a clear next action, expected response, and status.
   - Reassess eligibility only when the response changes relevant facts or risk signals.
4. **Separate repair authorization from part-price waiting.**
   - Request prices early and prepare the rest of the repair decision in parallel.
   - Do not hold the entire claim for a price lookup when the technical remedy and required approvals are already established.
   - Use an explicit fallback when the part cannot be confirmed: correct the diagnosis, choose an authorized alternative, or explain the delay.
5. **Validate fulfillment before dispatch.**
   - R1 and R2 are recorded as machine-checkable preconditions.
   - Catalog and model-compatibility checks govern routine replacements.
   - High-value or ambiguous compatibility receives human confirmation.
   - Repairs proceed to Quality check, with rework linked to the original defect and technician rather than treated as an unrelated new claim.

## Segments, paths, and activities targeted

This design primarily targets:

- The unclear-defect/missing-evidence segment, which is 22% of cases and has a 140-hour median.
- Claims entering Request missing evidence, where repeated requests and abandonment are observed.
- High-value devices subject to incorrect replacement selection.
- Repair cases delayed by the 21-hour median / 80-hour p90 quote-to-customer-approval interval.
- Low-digital-literacy customers, for whom an unassisted portal would be a poor redesign.

For already-complete low-risk accessory claims, the hub is largely bypassed. This avoids imposing evidence friction where none is needed.

## Mechanism of improvement

The portal’s **30–45% reduction in repeated evidence requests** is treated as a hypothesis to test, not an automatic case-level saving. Those customers must retain an assisted route because the same intervention may increase abandonment.

The more fundamental change is organizational: one case owner coordinates evidence and technical resolution, rather than transferring the customer between disconnected queues. In particular, part-price confirmation should stop being the sole reason three technicians’ repair work remains idle.

Part compatibility validation addresses the specific wrong-part failure mechanism and its EUR 95/EUR 410 exposures. The expected benefit is fewer late exceptions, not merely faster first responses.

## Deliberate sacrifices and unresolved issues

- There is an upfront investment in portal support, data integration, and a maintained device/part catalog.
- Assisted evidence completion consumes scarce human capacity and may increase cost per resolved case even if abandonment falls.
- More complete front-end resolution can make initial disposition slower for complex cases.
- The portal does not remove fraud, diagnostic, or repair-capability constraints. A confident but incorrect customer submission can still create downstream work.
- A compatibility model that is stale or too coarse can misclassify unfamiliar devices; uncertainty must trigger human review, not a forced replacement.
- This design is especially valuable where missing information or wrong parts drive cost. It is not automatically preferable for every clean, low-risk fast case.

# 4. Redesign C: Priority case ownership and closed-loop service recovery

## Redesigned process flow

1. **Route simple cases normally and give selected cases a named owner.** Routine, complete, low-risk claims can use the fast route. Premium high-value, ambiguous high-value, and repeated-evidence cases receive end-to-end ownership.
2. **Bound work in progress.** The case owner receives a complete risk packet rather than an unfiltered queue. Review capacity is reserved before additional cases are accepted.
3. **Create one case plan across eligibility, risk, repair, and fulfillment.**
   - Record the evidence status once.
   - Start part-price and diagnostic preparation early.
   - Maintain one decision history across handoffs.
   - Use authorized repair/replace choices and service-credit rules rather than improvising remedies.
4. **Provide differentiated service levels without differentiated safety rules.**
   - Premium low-risk cases retain rapid handling and proactive status updates.
   - Premium complex cases receive a faster, visible first disposition than the current 96-hour reviewed/repair median.
   - Standard cases are not unnecessarily upgraded, but high-value correctness and compliance remain mandatory.
   - Premium status never exempts a claim from R1 or R3.
5. **Replace the single daily bottleneck with protected recovery capacity.**
   - Assign dedicated overflow or cross-trained board capacity rather than placing all exceptions in the existing one-slot queue.
   - Allow decisions outside a daily batch when the case is already blocked on board review.
   - Require Manual eligibility review for reopened claims before close or denial.
6. **Close the loop after apparent completion.**
   - Monitor delivery, first-use confirmation, complaints, and repeat repair within a predeclared follow-up window.
   - A complaint reopens the existing case with retained evidence and decision history.
   - Root-cause analysis links wrong parts, technician rework, and repeated contacts to the responsible decision.

## Segments, paths, and activities targeted

The initial portfolio should be:

- Premium high-value devices: 14% of cases, 96-hour reviewed/repair median, 5% complaints, and high R1/R2 exposure.
- Selected Standard high-value devices: 21%, 120-hour reviewed/repair median, 6% complaints, and EUR 410 incorrect-replacement exposure.
- Complex or repeatedly failing cases already visible in the complaint/reopen population.

Premium low-risk accessories should stay largely on a controlled fast route. Their eight-hour fast median and high service-credit exposure make extended concierge handling inappropriate.

## Mechanism of improvement

The mechanism is **whole-case coordination and selective service differentiation**, rather than adding another automated filter. It reduces repeated eligibility work, waiting across ownership boundaries, and the delay before a complaint receives a decision.

Repair-quote preparation can use quality and technical expertise before the repair is completed. Protected board/overflow capacity attacks the daily 45-minute constraint directly. Named ownership also improves the customer’s ability to see status without restarting the process.

The objective is not to reproduce the full reviewed/repair duration. It is to obtain its substantially better complaint outcomes without leaving customers waiting for repeated reviews or a daily board.

## Deliberate sacrifices and unresolved issues

- This is the most touch-intensive and potentially expensive option.
- It consumes senior eligibility, risk, technical, and recovery capacity that is already scarce.
- Without explicit capacity funding and work-in-progress limits, Premium prioritization can simply move the queue to Standard cases.
- Faster first disposition must not become an incentive to offer unauthorized credits or replacements.
- Close, complaint, and cost measures can improve merely by reclassifying work. Persistent case identifiers and downstream follow-up are essential.
- The available evidence does not establish that the full Premium 96-hour path can be compressed to the fast-path 20 hours while retaining the 5% complaint rate. The service commitment should therefore be tested explicitly.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

| Redesign | Ways it could fail | Likely bottleneck migration |
|---|---|---|
| **A: Policy-enforced routing** | **1. Rule misclassification:** an incomplete or high-value claim is treated as low risk, repeating the discovered noncompliant shortcut. **2. Review overload:** the estimated 15–35% manual-review inflow pushes the seven-reviewer pool beyond its 82% queue-sensitive utilization. **3. SLA deterioration:** a poorly staffed pre-dispatch gate adds up to 18 hours to a case whose current fast median is only eight hours. | Eligibility review becomes the first bottleneck, followed by the pre-dispatch gate for high-value devices. A gate that depends on the same scarce specialists as repairs could displace work into repair scheduling. |
| **B: Resolution hub** | **1. Digital exclusion:** an unassisted portal increases abandonment, especially for the already vulnerable unclear-evidence group. **2. Incorrect compatibility automation:** a stale part catalog validates the wrong replacement for unfamiliar models, transferring rather than removing risk. **3. New evidence creates new eligibility/fraud questions without an owner:** a single case is resubmitted through several reviews. | Fraud/risk review, part-data clarification, or assisted-evidence support becomes the bottleneck. If technical preparation is prioritized over customer evidence, the waiting queue moves to the customer-contact team. |
| **C: Case ownership** | **1. Priority overload:** named owners become a queue, and the Premium promise remains blocked by the existing 45-minute board slot. **2. Cost gaming:** service credits or rapid replacement are used to suppress complaints, raising fully loaded cost. **3. Weak ownership boundaries:** technical, eligibility, and recovery teams maintain separate histories, so the customer still restarts the process. | The escalation board or senior reviewer becomes the new approval queue. Part-price confirmation can still emerge as a bottleneck if case ownership does not give technical staff authority to resolve it early. |

The displaced-bottleneck point is important: shortening one edge is not an improvement if the case simply waits elsewhere. Especially for A and C, apparent cycle-time gains should be checked against **case age, queue age, and touch time**, not only activity-level elapsed time.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

## Preparation before the first pilot: establish a comparable case baseline

First obtain a case-complete extract linking:

- Segment, defect type, value band, evidence completeness, and risk-review reason.
- Claim start, actual decision, dispatch/repair, complaint/reopen, final resolution, and downstream follow-up.
- Part-selection correctness, actual repair outcome, rework, reshipment, and credits.
- Labor and nonlabor costs, including queue delay and downstream service recovery.
- R1, R2, and R3 status, including documented Premium exceptions.

Reconcile activity-event counts with case-level outcomes. Preserve repeated requests and reopens as events, but avoid treating them as additional claims. Use the 0.91/0.68 replay results as **model-quality warnings**, not compliance denominators.

A short historical review should validate the proposed routing against actual device and part records, especially the high-value cases implicated in dispatch-without-review deviations. The current segment statistics are not a randomized comparison of otherwise identical fast and reviewed claims.

## First pilot: Redesign A, with a narrow scope

**Pilot population:** complete, low-risk accessory claims, stratified by Premium and Standard status. Deploy the two-week rule update for these cases, but retain mandatory R1/R2/R3 controls and human confirmation for high-value dispatches.

Use a concurrent, risk-matched control group where feasible. Test the incremental pre-dispatch check against the existing process rather than comparing selected fast-approval completers with selected reviewed/repair completers. That comparison would reproduce the current selection bias.

**Illustrative go criteria:**

- No known R1/R2/R3 violation; any material control breach triggers an immediate routing pause and case review.
- Complaint/reopen rates do not worsen by more than roughly **2 percentage points**, with a plausible improvement band of **25–40% relative reduction** within the relevant risk/segment strata.
- Premium low-risk median time remains within about **two hours of the current eight-hour benchmark**, and the design establishes whether the p90 can remain within the 24-hour credit-sensitive objective.
- Standard low-risk completion remains comfortably compatible with the stated 72-hour tolerance.
- Fully loaded cost is evaluated after complaints and credits; a small increase can be acceptable if quality and compliance gains are credible, but it is not automatically an economic success.

The important learning is whether the gate prevents wrong parts before dispatch and preserves low-risk speed. A lower complaint rate caused only by a different case mix is not a successful gate test.

## Second experiment: Redesign B’s evidence hub and parallel technical resolution

**Pilot population:** unclear/evidence-heavy cases and a separate sample of part-sensitive device claims. Learn from A’s instrumentation before scaling the routing rules.

For customers with adequate digital access, test a structured portal with immediate feedback. Keep an assisted channel and compare outcomes separately for customers with lower digital literacy. Do not force the portal and then attribute resulting abandonment to customer behavior.

In parallel, test early part-price lookup and part-compatibility resolution on cases that would otherwise wait at Repair quote  Customer approval.

**Illustrative go criteria:**

- Repeated evidence requests fall toward the portal’s proposed **30–45% reduction**, measured per case rather than per request.
- Abandonment does not rise by more than approximately **1–2 percentage points**, with particular scrutiny of vulnerable customers.
- There are no dispatches lacking required evidence confirmation, except documented Premium low-risk exceptions.
- Part-related complaint/reopen incidence and the long dispatch-complaint tail decline on matched defects, rather than merely moving to another claim category.
- Total cost per successfully resolved claim improves after support effort, repeat evidence work, and downstream recovery are included.

The experiment should report both portal and assisted-channel results. An aggregate improvement that hides deterioration for less digital customers is not an acceptable rollout.

## Third experiment: Redesign C’s ownership and recovery model

**Pilot population:** Premium high-value claims, selected Standard high-value claims, and cases repeatedly entering complaint/reopen. Do not place every Premium case into a high-touch model.

Fund protected board/overflow capacity and establish work-in-progress limits before claiming a service-level improvement. A “priority case” without available ownership and decision capacity is only a label.

**Illustrative go criteria:**

- First-disposition p90 for complex cases falls meaningfully from the current 96/120-hour reviewed/repair benchmarks; a **20–30% reduction** is a reasonable initial hypothesis, not a promised outcome.
- Complaint/reopen performance remains near the reviewed-path 5–6% outcome levels rather than returning toward the 18–22% fast-path levels.
- Premium service-credit exposure and the agreed Premium service commitment improve together.
- Standard-case queue age and completion time do not deteriorate by more than approximately **20%** relative to the comparison period.
- Recovery cost, credits, and reshipments do not rise enough to erase the customer and quality benefits.
- No reopened case is closed or denied before the required manual review.

## Shared metrics and the “real improvement” test

| Dimension | Metrics to monitor | What would indicate displacement rather than improvement? |
|---|---|---|
| **End-to-end time** | Start-to-resolution median/p90 by segment and defect; queue age; touch time; active-case age | Only first-response time improves while resolution, repeat contact, or reopen time worsens. |
| **Cost** | Eligibility/risk labor, evidence support, quote wait, gate labor, replacement errors, reshipments, credits, and total cost per resolved claim | Pre-rework cost falls but downstream cost and exposure rise. |
| **Quality** | First-time-correct part, repair rework, confirmed repeat defect, repeat repair, dispatch complaints | Complaints fall because customers cannot report problems or cases are reclassified. |
| **Compliance** | R1, R2, and R3 pass rates; documented exceptions; reopened-before-close sequencing | Overall fit improves while high-risk exceptions remain hidden in unaligned logs. |
| **Customer experience** | Premium/Standard and digital/non-digital outcomes; abandonment; repeat requests; status visibility; credits | Average improves while Premium SLA exposure or vulnerable-customer abandonment worsens. |
| **Resources** | Reviewer/fraud/inspection utilization, part-price wait, board backlog, work-in-progress aging | Local utilization improves but queues simply move to another team. |
| **Downstream validation** | Costs and outcomes after a stable post-delivery observation window | Savings appear only because complaints or credits are recorded after the measurement cutoff. |

**Decision rule:** advance a redesign only when compliance gates are met and improvements survive the full customer journey, including downstream cost and outcomes. Lower median cycle time with worse complaints, credits, or exception aging is not a Pareto gain. Equally, a slower path is not automatically superior: retain the fast route where it is safe and genuinely uncomplicated, and reserve reviewed, higher-touch handling for the risks and segments where the evidence supports it.
