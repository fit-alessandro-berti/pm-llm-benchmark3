# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

### 1.1 Material Assumptions
1. **Lifecycle Scope and Case Boundaries:** Every vendor onboarding instance starts strictly with `Receive Onboarding Request` and reaches terminal completion at `Close Onboarding`. A case cannot terminate without reaching `Close Onboarding`.
2. **Pre-Classification Concurrency:** `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, and `Verify Bank Account` execute concurrently after receipt. All three activities must complete before `Classify Risk` can execute.
3. **Clarification Dynamics (R3):** Clarification requests (`Request Clarification`) and responses (`Receive Clarification`) form an alternating transactional sub-cycle. A clarification cycle can occur multiple times, but every open request must be resolved by a corresponding response before a subsequent request can be made and before any terminal outcome (`Activate Vendor`, `Reject Vendor`, or `Close Onboarding`) is executed.
4. **Mutual Exclusivity of Outcomes (R1, R2, R8):** A case evaluates to exactly one terminal governance outcome:
   - **Rejection Route:** `Reject Vendor` is executed without any manager or compliance approvals.
   - **Low-Risk Accepted Route:** `Manager Approval` is executed, followed by `Activate Vendor`, `Release First Payment`, and `Close Onboarding`.
   - **High-Risk Accepted Route:** `Enhanced Due Diligence` is executed, followed by `Compliance Approval`, `Activate Vendor`, `Release First Payment`, and `Close Onboarding`.
5. **Post-Activation Lifecycle Separation (R11):** `Change Bank Details`, subsequent `Suspend Vendor`, and re-verification of the bank account represent post-activation lifecycle events. While governed by declarative integrity rules, they are separated from the core onboarding backbone to prevent recursive deadlock in case-completion semantics.
6. **Log Attribute Limitations (R6):** The event log contains standard attributes (`concept:name`, `time:timestamp`, `org:resource`, `case:concept:name`), but lacks native boolean flags for emergency authorization or cryptographic multi-party signatures.

---

### 1.2 Analysis and Resolution of the R5/R6 Policy Conflict

#### The Conflict
- **R5 (Legal Department Policy):** Strict control-flow invariant. If `Sanctions Escalation` occurs, `Obtain Legal Opinion` must strictly precede `Activate Vendor` under all circumstances. No exceptions are permitted.
- **R6 (Operations Department Policy):** Exception-driven business continuity policy. Emergency activation is permitted *before* `Obtain Legal Opinion` if and only if out-of-band joint authorization is obtained, provided the legal opinion is retroactively delivered within 24 hours of escalation/activation.

#### Resolution Options
1. **Resolution 1 (Strict / Conservative Legal Compliance):**
   - *Design:* Treat R5 as a non-negotiable control-flow invariant across all formalisms (Procedural, DECLARE, Log Skeleton). Encode `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)`.
   - *Behavior:* Any trace where `Activate Vendor` occurs before `Obtain Legal Opinion` is marked as an immediate control-flow violation (`fail`), regardless of subsequent legal opinion delivery or operational emergency claims.
2. **Resolution 2 (Permissive Control Flow with Delegated Resource/Data Audit):**
   - *Design:* Relax the strict precedence in control-flow models to `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` (ensuring the opinion arrives eventually), while delegating the enforcement of activation ordering, out-of-band joint authorization, and the 24-hour SLA window to a dedicated Resource/Data validation layer.
   - *Behavior:* Control-flow models pass the trace if the opinion is eventually received; the Resource/Data layer inspects out-of-band authorization logs and timestamp deltas.

#### Chosen Resolution for Implementation: Resolution 1 (Strict Legal Compliance)
- **Rationale:** Under international Anti-Money Laundering (AML), Counter-Terrorist Financing (CTF), and international sanctions enforcement (e.g., OFAC, EU Sanctions), activating a sanctioned or high-risk vendor prior to legal clearance carries strict liability, catastrophic financial penalties, and criminal exposure.
- **Compliance Risk of the Choice:**
  - *Risk under Resolution 1:* Legitimate operational emergencies executed under joint executive authority will trigger automated control-flow compliance alarms (`fail`), causing operational friction and requiring post-audit reconciliation.
  - *Risk under Resolution 2:* A rogue operator could bypass legal screening by claiming an unverified "emergency," leading to illicit fund disbursement and regulatory breach before legal counsel can intervene.
  - *Hybrid Architectural Compromise:* In this model, the formal control-flow backbones strictly enforce Resolution 1 (R5). Concurrently, the Resource/Data layer implements explicit audit evaluation for Resolution 2 (R6) so compliance officers can distinguish between an unauthorized bypass and a documented, time-compliant emergency activation.

# 3. Procedural Backbone

### 3.1 Formal Description
The procedural backbone is formally structured as a block-structured Process Tree $T$:
$$T = \rightarrow ( a_1, + ( a_2, a_3, a_4 ), * ( \tau, \rightarrow ( a_5, a_6 ) ), a_7, \times ( a_8, \rightarrow ( \times ( a_9, \rightarrow ( a_{10}, a_{11} ), \rightarrow ( a_{12}, + ( a_{10}, a_{13} ), a_{11} ) ), a_{14}, a_{15} ) ), a_{16} )$$
Where:
- $a_1$: `Receive Onboarding Request`
- $a_2$: `Capture Master Data`, $a_3$: `Run Sanctions Screening`, $a_4$: `Verify Bank Account` (Parallel block $+$)
- $*(\tau, \rightarrow(a_5, a_6))$: Optional clarification loop ($a_5$: `Request Clarification`, $a_6$: `Receive Clarification`)
- $a_7$: `Classify Risk`
- $\times$: XOR branch between Rejection ($a_8$: `Reject Vendor`) and Acceptance.
- Acceptance sub-tree: XOR choice between:
  - Low-Risk: $a_9$ (`Manager Approval`)
  - High-Risk Standard: $\rightarrow(a_{10}, a_{11})$ (`Enhanced Due Diligence` $\rightarrow$ `Compliance Approval`)
  - High-Risk Escalated (R5 compliant): $\rightarrow(a_{12}, +(a_{10}, a_{13}), a_{11})$ (`Sanctions Escalation` $\rightarrow$ Parallel(`Enhanced Due Diligence`, `Obtain Legal Opinion`) $\rightarrow$ `Compliance Approval`)
- Post-Approval Acceptance Sequence: $\rightarrow(a_{14}, a_{15})$ (`Activate Vendor` $\rightarrow$ `Release First Payment`)
- $a_{16}$: `Close Onboarding` (Joins all terminal branches)

### 3.2 Executable PM4Py Code

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

def build_procedural_backbone() -> ProcessTree:
    """
    Constructs the authoritative procedural ProcessTree for Controlled Vendor Onboarding
    under strict legal compliance (Resolution 1 / R5).
    """
    # Root sequence: Request -> Pre-checks -> Clarification -> Classify -> Outcome -> Close
    root = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE)

    # 1. Start: Receive Onboarding Request
    t_receive = ProcessTree(label="Receive Onboarding Request", parent=root)

    # 2. Parallel Pre-Classification Checks
    parallel_pre_checks = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.PARALLEL, parent=root)
    t_capture = ProcessTree(label="Capture Master Data", parent=parallel_pre_checks)
    t_screening = ProcessTree(label="Run Sanctions Screening", parent=parallel_pre_checks)
    t_verify_bank = ProcessTree(label="Verify Bank Account", parent=parallel_pre_checks)
    parallel_pre_checks.children.extend([t_capture, t_screening, t_verify_bank])

    # 3. Optional Clarification Loop: *(tau, Sequence(Request, Receive))
    clarification_loop = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.LOOP, parent=root)
    tau_clarification = ProcessTree(label=None, parent=clarification_loop)  # Exit/skip loop
    clarification_body = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, parent=clarification_loop)
    t_req_clarif = ProcessTree(label="Request Clarification", parent=clarification_body)
    t_rec_clarif = ProcessTree(label="Receive Clarification", parent=clarification_body)
    clarification_body.children.extend([t_req_clarif, t_rec_clarif])
    clarification_loop.children.extend([tau_clarification, clarification_body])

    # 4. Synchronization Gate: Classify Risk
    t_classify = ProcessTree(label="Classify Risk", parent=root)

    # 5. Primary Governance Outcome XOR (Reject vs Accept)
    outcome_xor = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR, parent=root)

    # 5a. Rejection Branch
    t_reject = ProcessTree(label="Reject Vendor", parent=outcome_xor)

    # 5b. Acceptance Branch: Approval Route -> Activation -> First Payment
    accepted_sequence = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, parent=outcome_xor)

    # Sub-choice for Approvals
    approval_xor = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR, parent=accepted_sequence)

    # Route A: Low-Risk
    t_mgr_app = ProcessTree(label="Manager Approval", parent=approval_xor)

    # Route B: High-Risk Standard
    high_risk_std = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, parent=approval_xor)
    t_edd_std = ProcessTree(label="Enhanced Due Diligence", parent=high_risk_std)
    t_comp_std = ProcessTree(label="Compliance Approval", parent=high_risk_std)
    high_risk_std.children.extend([t_edd_std, t_comp_std])

    # Route C: High-Risk Sanctions Escalation (Strict R5 Compliance)
    high_risk_esc = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, parent=approval_xor)
    t_sanc_esc = ProcessTree(label="Sanctions Escalation", parent=high_risk_esc)
    esc_parallel = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.PARALLEL, parent=high_risk_esc)
    t_edd_esc = ProcessTree(label="Enhanced Due Diligence", parent=esc_parallel)
    t_legal_op = ProcessTree(label="Obtain Legal Opinion", parent=esc_parallel)
    esc_parallel.children.extend([t_edd_esc, t_legal_op])
    t_comp_esc = ProcessTree(label="Compliance Approval", parent=high_risk_esc)
    high_risk_esc.children.extend([t_sanc_esc, esc_parallel, t_comp_esc])

    approval_xor.children.extend([t_mgr_app, high_risk_std, high_risk_esc])

    # Downstream Acceptance Milestones
    t_activate = ProcessTree(label="Activate Vendor", parent=accepted_sequence)
    t_payment = ProcessTree(label="Release First Payment", parent=accepted_sequence)
    accepted_sequence.children.extend([approval_xor, t_activate, t_payment])

    outcome_xor.children.extend([t_reject, accepted_sequence])

    # 6. Terminal Synchronization: Close Onboarding
    t_close = ProcessTree(label="Close Onboarding", parent=root)

    root.children.extend([
        t_receive,
        parallel_pre_checks,
        clarification_loop,
        t_classify,
        outcome_xor,
        t_close
    ])

    return root

procedural_model = build_procedural_backbone()
```

### 3.3 Structural and Behavioral Explanations
1. **Concurrency:** `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, and `Verify Bank Account` are enclosed in an `Operator.PARALLEL` ($+$) block immediately following `Receive Onboarding Request`. This models full interleaving concurrency; any sequence of these three checks is replayable.
2. **Synchronization Gates:** `Classify Risk` is sequentially positioned after both the parallel pre-checks and the clarification loop. It cannot execute until all three checks have completed and any pending clarification is resolved.
3. **Clarification Loops:** Modeled as an `Operator.LOOP` ($*$) where the loop's initial child is a silent transition ($\tau$) allowing the process to bypass clarification when unneeded, and the second child is a strict sequence `Request Clarification` $\rightarrow$ `Receive Clarification`. This guarantees that clarifications always occur in balanced request-response pairs prior to classification.
4. **Terminal Outcomes and Joins:** `Close Onboarding` is the root sequence's final node. Whether the process takes the Rejection branch or the multi-stage Acceptance branch, both paths converge at `Close Onboarding`.
5. **Deliberate Exclusion of R11:** `Change Bank Details`, `Suspend Vendor`, and subsequent bank re-verification are deliberately excluded from the procedural onboarding backbone. Onboarding terminates upon first payment and case closure. Treating bank modifications as part of onboarding would require unbounded post-activation loops, causing sound process models to become non-terminating and entangling master data maintenance with initial vetting.

### 4.2 Template Semantics and Directionality

| Template | Key / Pair | Direction & Formal Semantics | Context & Invariant Justification |
|---|---|---|---|
| `init` | `Receive Onboarding Request` | Unary: Trace must start with $t_0 = \text{Receive Onboarding Request}$. | Enforces canonical case initiation. |
| `absence2` | `Activate Vendor`, `Reject Vendor`, `Release First Payment`, etc. | Unary: Count of activity in trace must be $\le 1$. | Enforces R10 (cardinality constraints on key milestones). |
| `noncoexistence` | `(Activate Vendor, Reject Vendor)` | Symmetric: If $A$ occurs, $B$ cannot occur anywhere in the trace (and vice versa). $\neg (\lozenge A \land \lozenge B)$. | Enforces R1; absolute outcome mutual exclusion. |
| `noncoexistence` | `(Manager Approval, Compliance Approval)` | Symmetric: Enforces that low-risk and high-risk approval types never co-occur in the same case. | Enforces R2 and R8; prevents double approval or pathway pollution. |
| `noncoexistence` | `(Reject Vendor, Manager Approval)` | Symmetric: Rejection precludes any manager approval. | Enforces R2; no approvals in rejected cases. |
| `precedence` | `(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` | Target-driven ($B \to A$): If $B$ occurs, $A$ must have occurred earlier. $\lozenge B \implies (\neg B \mathbin{\mathcal{U}} A)$. | Enforces R5 (Resolution 1); guarantees activation never happens without prior legal clearance. |
| `precedence` | `(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` | Target-driven: Escalation requires prior screening check. | Enforces R7; escalation cannot be fabricated without screening. |
| `precedence` | `(Capture Master Data, Classify Risk)` | Target-driven: Classification requires all pre-checks to precede it. | Enforces pre-classification gate. |
| `response` | `(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` | Source-driven ($A \to B$): If $A$ occurs, $B$ must eventually occur at or after $A$. $\square (A \implies \lozenge B)$. | Enforces R4; unclosed sanctions escalations are prohibited. |
| `response` | `(Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor)` | Source-driven: If bank details change post-activation, suspension is mandatory. | Enforces R11; reactive safety mechanism. |
| `altresponse` | `(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` | Source-driven alternating: Every $A$ must be followed by $B$ before the next $A$ occurs. | Enforces R3; prevents sending multiple overlapping clarification requests without resolution. |
| `altprecedence` | `(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` | Target-driven alternating: Every $B$ must be preceded by $A$ with no other $B$ in between. | Enforces R3; prevents unsolicited or orphaned responses. |

#### Semantic Distinctions
- **`response(A, B)` vs. `precedence(A, B)`:** `response(A, B)` is activated by the occurrence of $A$ and requires future occurrence of $B$; if $A$ does not occur, it is vacuously satisfied. `precedence(A, B)` is activated by the occurrence of $B$ and requires past occurrence of $A$; if $B$ does not occur, it is vacuously satisfied.
- **`altresponse(A, B)` vs. `chainresponse(A, B)`:** `altresponse(A, B)` requires $B$ to occur before the next $A$, permitting arbitrary other activities in between. `chainresponse(A, B)` strictly requires $B$ to be the *immediate next event* ($t_{i+1}$) after $A$.
- **`noncoexistence(A, B)` vs. `nonsuccession(A, B)`:** `noncoexistence(A, B)` is bidirectional and symmetric (neither can appear if the other appears). `nonsuccession(A, B)` is unidirectional and asymmetric (if $A$ occurs, $B$ cannot occur *after* $A$, but $B$ could legally occur *before* $A$).

### 5.2 Pair Orientations, Overlap, and Boundary Disagreements

1. **Orientation of Pairs:**
   - In `always_before: {(A, B)}`, the pair semantics mean **$A$ must occur before $B$** whenever $B$ occurs. For example, `("Obtain Legal Opinion", "Activate Vendor")` requires that legal clearance is in place prior to activation.
   - In `always_after: {(A, B)}`, the pair semantics mean **if $A$ occurs, $B$ must eventually occur after $A$**. For example, `("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion")` enforces that escalation forces an eventual opinion.
2. **Equivalence Semantics:**
   - `("Receive Onboarding Request", "Close Onboarding")` asserts that every initiated case must terminate exactly once ($\text{count}(A) = \text{count}(B)$).
   - `("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment")` reflects the strict invariant in accepted onboarding cases that activation is tied $1:1$ to the release of initial payment.
3. **Disagreements and Special Accommodations:**
   - **Pre-Classification Frequency:** `activ_freq["Verify Bank Account"] = {1, 2}` deliberately accommodates R11. If this were restricted to `{1}`, any trace undergoing a post-activation bank modification would fail log-skeleton conformance.
   - **Directly Follows Conservatism:** In concurrent systems, `directly_follows` is highly brittle because independent activities (e.g. `Capture Master Data` and `Run Sanctions Screening`) can interleave arbitrarily. Thus, `directly_follows` is populated *only* for deterministic terminal joins: `("Release First Payment", "Close Onboarding")` and `("Reject Vendor", "Close Onboarding")`.

### 6.2 Temporal Deviations as Warnings vs. Control-Flow Violations
1. **Separation of Control Flow and Performance:**
   - Control-flow violations (e.g. activating a vendor without sanctions clearance or dual approval) represent **structural illegality** and regulatory non-compliance.
   - Temporal deviations represent **stochastic performance variance** (operational latency). Under Chebyshev's inequality and Gaussian distribution assumptions, a threshold of $\zeta = 2.0$ bounds the nominal interval to $[\mu - 2\sigma, \mu + 2\sigma]$ (encompassing $\approx 95.4\%$ of compliant operations).
2. **Operational Validity of Out-of-Bound Executions:**
   - In test case **T7**, `Obtain Legal Opinion` arrives at hour 40 following `Sanctions Escalation` at hour 6 ($\Delta t = 34\text{ hours}$). The upper threshold is $12 + 2(6) = 24\text{ hours}$.
   - While 34 hours is statistically late ($z = +3.67$), the opinion is fully valid, authentic, and obtained *prior* to activation. Rejecting the onboarding case on control-flow grounds would be a catastrophic false positive, misidentifying a slow legal review as an illegal activation.
3. **Governance Tiering:** Temporal profile deviations trigger automated **SLA Amber Warnings** routed to operational queue managers for root-cause analysis, whereas control-flow breaches trigger **Hard Red Blockers** that halt transaction processing.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

### 7.1 Executable Python Validation Layer

```python
import pandas as pd
from typing import Dict, List, Any, Tuple

def check_four_eyes_principle(df_case: pd.DataFrame) -> List[Dict[str, Any]]:
    """
    Validates R9 (Segregation of Duties / Four-Eyes Principle):
    1. Resource performing 'Verify Bank Account' must differ from resource performing Approval.
    2. Resource performing 'Capture Master Data' must differ from resource performing 'Activate Vendor'.
    """
    violations = []
    case_id = df_case["case:concept:name"].iloc[0] if "case:concept:name" in df_case.columns else "UNKNOWN"

    # Rule 1: Bank Verification vs Approvals
    bank_events = df_case[df_case["concept:name"] == "Verify Bank Account"]
    approval_events = df_case[df_case["concept:name"].isin(["Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval"])]

    if not bank_events.empty and not approval_events.empty:
        for _, b_row in bank_events.iterrows():
            b_res = b_row.get("org:resource")
            if pd.isna(b_res) or str(b_res).strip() == "":
                violations.append({
                    "case_id": case_id,
                    "requirement": "R9",
                    "type": "MISSING_RESOURCE_DATA",
                    "activity": "Verify Bank Account",
                    "severity": "CRITICAL",
                    "message": "Resource attribute missing on Verify Bank Account."
                })
                continue

            for _, a_row in approval_events.iterrows():
                a_res = a_row.get("org:resource")
                a_act = a_row.get("concept:name")
                if pd.isna(a_res) or str(a_res).strip() == "":
                    violations.append({
                        "case_id": case_id,
                        "requirement": "R9",
                        "type": "MISSING_RESOURCE_DATA",
                        "activity": a_act,
                        "severity": "CRITICAL",
                        "message": f"Resource attribute missing on {a_act}."
                    })
                elif b_res == a_res:
                    violations.append({
                        "case_id": case_id,
                        "requirement": "R9",
                        "type": "FOUR_EYES_VIOLATION",
                        "severity": "FAIL",
                        "conflicting_resource": b_res,
                        "activities": ("Verify Bank Account", a_act),
                        "message": f"Resource '{b_res}' performed both Verify Bank Account and {a_act}."
                    })

    # Rule 2: Capture Master Data vs Activate Vendor
    capture_events = df_case[df_case["concept:name"] == "Capture Master Data"]
    activate_events = df_case[df_case["concept:name"] == "Activate Vendor"]

    if not capture_events.empty and not activate_events.empty:
        for _, c_row in capture_events.iterrows():
            c_res = c_row.get("org:resource")
            for _, act_row in activate_events.iterrows():
                act_res = act_row.get("org:resource")
                if pd.notna(c_res) and pd.notna(act_res) and c_res == act_res:
                    violations.append({
                        "case_id": case_id,
                        "requirement": "R9",
                        "type": "FOUR_EYES_VIOLATION",
                        "severity": "FAIL",
                        "conflicting_resource": c_res,
                        "activities": ("Capture Master Data", "Activate Vendor"),
                        "message": f"Resource '{c_res}' performed both Capture Master Data and Activate Vendor."
                    })

    return violations


def check_emergency_activation_policy(
    df_case: pd.DataFrame,
    resolution_mode: str = "strict"
) -> List[Dict[str, Any]]:
    """
    Evaluates R5/R6 Emergency Policy:
    - Under 'strict' (Resolution 1): Any activation prior to legal opinion is an immediate FAIL.
    - Under 'permissive' (Resolution 2): Checks for joint authorization token and <= 24h SLA.
    """
    violations = []
    case_id = df_case["case:concept:name"].iloc[0] if "case:concept:name" in df_case.columns else "UNKNOWN"

    escalation = df_case[df_case["concept:name"] == "Sanctions Escalation"]
    activation = df_case[df_case["concept:name"] == "Activate Vendor"]
    opinion = df_case[df_case["concept:name"] == "Obtain Legal Opinion"]

    if not escalation.empty and not activation.empty:
        act_time = activation.iloc[0]["time:timestamp"]
        esc_time = escalation.iloc[0]["time:timestamp"]

        # Check if activation occurred before legal opinion
        is_premature_activation = False
        if opinion.empty:
            is_premature_activation = True
            op_time = None
        else:
            op_time = opinion.iloc[0]["time:timestamp"]
            if act_time < op_time:
                is_premature_activation = True

        if is_premature_activation:
            if resolution_mode == "strict":
                violations.append({
                    "case_id": case_id,
                    "requirement": "R5",
                    "severity": "FAIL",
                    "type": "CONTROL_FLOW_SANCTIONS_BREACH",
                    "message": "Activate Vendor executed prior to Obtain Legal Opinion (Strict R5 Violation)."
                })
            else:
                # Permissive / R6 Audit Evaluation
                # 1. Check out-of-band joint authorization attribute
                has_auth = df_case.get("case:emergency_authorized", pd.Series([False])).iloc[0]
                auth_signers = df_case.get("case:emergency_authorizers", pd.Series([[]])).iloc[0]

                if not has_auth or len(auth_signers) < 2:
                    violations.append({
                        "case_id": case_id,
                        "requirement": "R6",
                        "severity": "FAIL",
                        "type": "UNAUTHORIZED_EMERGENCY_ACTIVATION",
                        "message": "Emergency activation occurred without verified joint out-of-band authorization."
                    })

                # 2. Check 24-hour SLA window
                if op_time is not None:
                    delta_hours = (op_time - esc_time).total_seconds() / 3600.0
                    if delta_hours > 24.0:
                        violations.append({
                            "case_id": case_id,
                            "requirement": "R6",
                            "severity": "FAIL",
                            "type": "EMERGENCY_LEGAL_SLA_EXCEEDED",
                            "message": f"Legal opinion arrived {delta_hours:.1f}h after escalation (exceeds 24h deadline)."
                        })
                else:
                    violations.append({
                        "case_id": case_id,
                        "requirement": "R6",
                        "severity": "FAIL",
                        "type": "MISSING_LEGAL_OPINION",
                        "message": "Legal opinion never obtained following emergency activation."
                    })

    return violations


def validate_case_resource_and_data(df_case: pd.DataFrame, policy_resolution: str = "strict") -> Dict[str, Any]:
    """
    Master resource and data validation wrapper for a single case dataframe.
    """
    sod_violations = check_four_eyes_principle(df_case)
    emergency_violations = check_emergency_activation_policy(df_case, resolution_mode=policy_resolution)

    all_violations = sod_violations + emergency_violations
    status = "pass"
    if any(v["severity"] == "FAIL" or v["severity"] == "CRITICAL" for v in all_violations):
        status = "fail"
    elif all_violations:
        status = "warning"

    return {
        "status": status,
        "violations": all_violations
    }
```

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

### 9.1 Duplicated Enforcement Across Layers
There is intentional structural redundancy across the Procedural, DECLARE, and Log-Skeleton layers:
- The mutual exclusivity of `Activate Vendor` and `Reject Vendor` is simultaneously enforced by the procedural XOR operator, DECLARE `noncoexistence`, and Log Skeleton `never_together`.
- *Defense-in-Depth Justification:* While procedural models enforce global structural reachability via token replay, DECLARE and Log Skeleton act as rapid declarative assertion checks. In large event logs, declarative rule checking detects isolated local violations (e.g. T8 contradictory outcomes) in $O(n)$ time without calculating full state-space alignments.

---

### 9.2 Inconsistencies Under Loops and Post-Activation Modifications
1. **Activity Frequency Incompatibilities (R10 vs. R11):**
   - If Log Skeleton strictly defines `activ_freq["Verify Bank Account"] = {1}`, the model becomes fundamentally incompatible with R11 (`Change Bank Details` triggering a second `Verify Bank Account`). A valid post-activation bank update would cause a false-positive violation. Setting `activ_freq` to `{1, 2}` resolves this contradiction.
2. **Clarification Loops vs. Directly Follows:**
   - Defining `directly_follows: ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification")` creates an operational blind spot if intermediate logging events (e.g. `Log Note`, `Audit Ping`) occur between request and response. Utilizing `altresponse` in DECLARE provides robust alternation without brittle adjacency assumptions.

---

### 9.3 Vacuity in Declarative Rules
A critical vulnerability in declarative process mining is **vacuous satisfaction**:
- In DECLARE, `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` is formally expressed as:
  $$\square (\text{Activate Vendor} \implies \lozenge^{-1} \text{Obtain Legal Opinion})$$
- In rejected cases (such as **T3**), `Activate Vendor` never occurs. Consequently, the precedence rule evaluates to `True` (vacuously).
- Similarly, `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` evaluates to `True` in T1, T2, T3, T4, and T5 simply because `Sanctions Escalation` never fired.
- *Audit Implication:* Conformance tools must never report vacuous satisfaction as "positive proof" of sanctions vetting. Compliance reporting must explicitly distinguish between *actively validated constraints* and *untriggered (vacuous) constraints*.

---

### 9.4 Missing Case Attributes and Log Opacity
1. **Out-of-Band State Blindness:** As highlighted by R6, real-world organizations frequently execute emergency exceptions via out-of-band communication (verbal director sign-offs, secure messaging). Because standard event logs only record discrete activity strings, process mining engines suffer from **log opacity**—they cannot distinguish between an authorized emergency activation and a rogue compliance breach without external data integration.
2. **Resource Identity Obfuscation:** If resource strings are hashed, missing, or represent shared generic service accounts (e.g., `[Admin]`, `[ServiceAccount]`), Four-Eyes checks (R9) become completely blind, generating catastrophic false-positive passes (as demonstrated in T4).

---

### 9.5 Timestamp Limitations and Business Calendars
Temporal profiling based purely on epoch timestamp deltas ($\Delta t = t_j - t_i$) introduces significant distortion:
- Standard standard-deviation thresholds ($\zeta = 2.0$) assume continuous 24/7 calendar progression.
- If `Request Clarification` is sent on Friday at 17:00 and `Receive Clarification` arrives on Monday at 09:00, the elapsed calendar time is 64 hours (triggering a false warning against a 48h SLA), whereas the actual business working time was only 1 hour. Production temporal profiles must incorporate active business calendars and shift schedules.

---

### 9.6 Risk of Treating Discovery Statistics as Normative Truth
In standard process mining, support and confidence metrics reflect empirical frequencies in historical logs:
- If an organization discovers a DECLARE model where `support = 0.98` and `confidence = 0.95` for `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)`, this simply indicates that 5% of past historical cases violated sanctions screening.
- **Normative Engineering Rule:** In legal and regulatory governance, support and confidence must **never** be treated as probabilistic thresholds. Compliance constraints are deterministic policy invariants ($\text{support} = 1.0, \text{confidence} = 1.0$). Tolerating a 5% failure rate in sanctions clearance represents willful regulatory non-compliance.

### 10.1 Multi-Layered Architecture and Authoritative Layer
1. **Canonical Procedural Representation:** Deploy the block-structured **ProcessTree** (or equivalent Accepting Petri Net) as the foundational control-flow backbone to govern the standard lifecycle, branch synchronization, and clarification loops.
2. **Authoritative Policy Layer:** The **Resource/Data-Aware Governance Layer** serves as the authoritative arbiter of case validity. It sits hierarchically above pure control-flow replay, ensuring that structurally valid traces cannot clear governance if they violate Segregation of Duties (R9) or lack out-of-band authorization tokens (R6).

---

### 10.2 Recommended Conformance Pipeline Order
1. **Step 1: Schema and Data Completeness Gate:**
   - Ingest trace; verify non-null values for `concept:name`, `time:timestamp`, and `org:resource`. If resource attributes are absent on critical activities, halt processing immediately (`CRITICAL_DATA_MISSING`).
2. **Step 2: Control-Flow & Invariant Conformance Gate:**
   - Execute token-based replay against the procedural backbone and evaluate DECLARE / Log-Skeleton invariants (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5, R7, R8, R10). If any structural violation occurs, halt processing with a **Hard Red Blocker**.
3. **Step 3: Resource & Data-Aware Audit Gate:**
   - Execute programmatic Four-Eyes segregation checks on `(Verify Bank Account, Approval)` and `(Capture Master Data, Activate Vendor)`.
   - If an emergency activation occurred before legal opinion, check for authenticated joint authorization records. If unverified, halt processing with a **Segregation/Policy Breach Blocker**.
4. **Step 4: Temporal Profiling & SLA Queue:**
   - Evaluate temporal profile metrics at $\zeta = 2.0$. If $z > 2.0$, generate an **Amber Operational Warning** ticket for management review without halting payment processing.

---

### 10.3 User-Facing Diagnostics and Contradiction Surfacing
- **Tiered Incident Categorization:**
  - **Red Blocker (Regulatory Non-Conformance):** Structural bypass, sanctions breach, or Four-Eyes violation. Transaction is halted; vendor status set to `LOCKED`; immediate notification dispatched to Legal and Compliance Officers.
  - **Amber Alert (Operational Performance / SLA):** Temporal deviation or clarification backlog. Transaction proceeds; notification dispatched to Operations Desk for SLA tracking.
- **Root-Cause Diagnostic Reporting:**
  - The UI must surface the exact nature of the failure rather than a generic alignment score. For example, in **T4**, the system reports:
    `[BLOCKED: Four-Eyes Principle Violation (R9)] Resource 'Cora' executed both 'Verify Bank Account' (2026-08-14 03:00) and 'Manager Approval' (2026-08-14 08:00). Secondary approval must be re-executed by an independent manager.`
  - In **T6**, the diagnostic explicitly highlights policy dependency:
    `[BLOCKED: Premature Activation Prior to Legal Opinion (R5/R6)] Activation executed at hour 13 before Legal Opinion at hour 23. Out-of-band joint emergency authorization token missing from case payload.`
