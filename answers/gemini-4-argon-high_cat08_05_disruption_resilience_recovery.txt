# 1. Disruption timeline

### T0: Baseline Period (Day -30 to Day -1)
* **Operational State:** Stable, high-volume automated execution across 42,000 cases over 21 business days (~2,000 cases/day). Conformance against the normative process model is high (fitness **0.96**, **91%** fitting traces). Cycle times are predictable across segments: **Hospital-critical** median 8h (p90 15h), **Pharmacy-standard** median 23h (p90 49h), and **Wholesale-bulk** median 36h (p90 72h). Rework/correction is low at **3.5%**, and order cancellations sit at **2.1%**.
* **Dominant Process Variants:** 87.6% of cases (36,800 / 42,000) traverse the complete end-to-end normative backbone: `Receive order (42,000) -> Validate authorization (41,700) -> Inventory reservation (41,000) -> Pick (40,100) -> Pack (39,500) -> Quality/release check (38,900) -> Dispatch (38,200) -> Proof of delivery (37,400) -> Invoice close (36,800) -> END`. Exceptions are handled inside structured, system-enforced rework loops: `Compliance hold` (700 entries; 520 cleared back to validation), `Backorder wait` (900 entries; 600 fulfilled to pick, 300 canceled), and `Repack/recheck` (450 entries; 400 returned to release check).
* **Risk Posture:** Low, preventative, and real-time. System gates enforce compliance prior to physical movement, holding traceability exceptions to **0.8%**, missing cold-chain temperature documentation to **0.4%**, and missing controlled-substance dual-control to **0.2%**.

### T1: Incident Onset (Day 0 Morning)
* **Operational State:** Acute technological shock. A cyber incident disables the Warehouse Management System (WMS) and severs automated interfaces across the order-to-delivery chain (electronic customer/product authorization, real-time inventory allocation, barcode scanning at pick/pack/dispatch, and automated billing).
* **Dominant Process Variants:** Automated traces freeze mid-flight at reservation, picking, and release gates. Operations pivot to crisis containment by printing a point-in-time morning inventory snapshot and establishing an ad-hoc `Emergency triage` desk.
* **Risk Posture:** Extreme operational continuity and clinical safety risk (immediate threat of hospital stockouts and dockside cold-chain thermal excursions), catalyzing a rapid shift from system-enforced preventative controls to unstandardized human improvisation.

### T2: Manual Workaround Phase (Day 0 Afternoon to Day 2)
* **Operational State:** Degraded, labor-intensive manual operations covering 5,600 cases (~2,240 cases/day over ~2.5 business days). Normative conformance collapses (fitness **0.71**, fitting traces **44%**). Capacity is heavily skewed to protect acute patient care: **Hospital-critical** fill rate holds at **91%** with minimal cycle-time degradation (median 10h vs. 8h baseline; p90 21h vs. 15h). Conversely, **Pharmacy-standard** suffers severe service erosion (fill rate drops to **61%**, median cycle time more than doubles to 54h, p90 reaches 118h), and **Wholesale-bulk** is throttled (median 96h, p90 180h). Rework/correction surges **3.6x** to **12.5%**, and cancellations jump **3.7x** to **7.8%**.
* **Dominant Process Variants:** The normative backbone disappears, replaced by improvised move-on-log and move-on-model deviations:
  * *Intake & Triage:* `Receive order (5,600)` branches into `Emergency triage (5,100)` and immediate `Defer noncritical order (500)`. Triage splits into three authorization variants: `Manual authorization check (2,500)`, `Skip system authorization check (1,600)`, and `Hospital-critical fast lane (1,000)`.
  * *Stale Allocation & Deferred Backlog:* All three paths converge on `Manual inventory snapshot check (4,720 total: 2,300 + 1,500 + 920)`, which routes 3,920 cases to `Manual pick list` and diverts 580 additional cases to `Defer noncritical order`. Deferred orders enter `Backlog queue (960)` (whose delay exceeds baseline p95), trickling 420 cases back to `Manual pick list` and forcing 320 into `Cancel order` (430 total cancellations).
  * *Uncontrolled Release & Un-scanned Dispatch:* Physical fulfillment flows through `Manual pick list (4,340 total inflow) -> Manual pack (3,600)`. At pack, **52.2%** of orders (`Manual pack -> Dispatch without scan: 1,800`) bypass release checks entirely, while only 1,650 pass through `Temporary release control`. Crucially, even inspected orders proceed to `Dispatch without scan (1,450)`, meaning **100% of dispatched orders (3,250)** leave the warehouse without a system barcode scan.
  * *Deferred Close:* Post-dispatch relies on `Phone proof of delivery (2,600)` (with highly variable delay) and parks cases in `Spreadsheet invoice hold (2,200) -> END (2,100)`.
* **Risk Posture:** High concealed noncompliance and severe supply-chain blindness. While hospital life-safety supply is protected, regulatory integrity fractures: traceability exceptions rise **11.9x** to **9.5%**, missing cold-chain documentation increases **12x** to **4.8%**, and missing controlled-substance dual-control jumps **14.5x** to **2.9%**. Skipping authorization on 1,600 orders and dispatching 3,250 orders without scans breaks lot/serial chain-of-custody and creates a massive un-reconciled financial and compliance backlog.

### T3: Partial Recovery (Day 3 to Day 6)
* **Operational State:** Hybrid "split-brain" operations across 11,400 cases (~2,850 cases/day as teams process new demand alongside T2 backlog). Electronic order intake returns, but WMS inventory synchronization and handheld scanning remain unreliable. Conformance partially recovers (fitness **0.82**, fitting traces **59%**). Cycle times begin improving (**Hospital-critical** median 9h / p90 18h; **Pharmacy-standard** median 38h / p90 90h; **Wholesale-bulk** median 84h / p90 160h), while rework (**9.0%**) and cancellations (**5.2%**) remain more than double baseline.
* **Dominant Process Variants:** Coexistence of restored electronic intake/validation with manual warehouse workarounds. Because physical stock depleted during T2 via `Manual pick list` does not match digital WMS balances, orders passing automated intake collide with phantom inventory reservations, requiring ad-hoc manual overrides, intermittent un-scanned dispatches, and ongoing manual proof-of-delivery collection.
* **Risk Posture:** Elevated synchronization and data-integrity risk. The mismatch between digital order intake and un-reconciled physical stock generates reservation collisions and picking errors, keeping traceability exceptions (**5.4%**), missing cold-chain documentation (**2.3%**), and missing controlled-substance dual-control (**1.4%**) roughly **6x to 7x** above baseline.

### T4: Stabilization (Day 7 to Day 21)
* **Operational State:** Normal technical systems mostly return across 35,000 cases (~2,333 cases/day over 15 business days), raising conformance to **0.90** fitness (**76%** fitting traces). Surface-level cycle times for priority segments appear largely recovered (**Hospital-critical** returns to baseline 8h median / 16h p90; **Pharmacy-standard** reaches 28h median / 74h p90), though **Wholesale-bulk** remains sluggish (58h median / 120h p90 vs. 36h/72h baseline). However, rework (**5.8%** vs. 3.5% baseline), cancellations (**3.0%** vs. 2.1%), and compliance failures (traceability **2.2%**, cold-chain **0.9%**, controlled-substance **0.5%**) remain **2.2x to 2.8x** higher than baseline.
* **Dominant Process Variants:** While the main flow returns to the normative path (`Receive order: 35,000 -> Validate authorization: 33,500 -> Inventory reservation: 32,200 -> Pick: 30,900 -> Pack: 30,200 -> Quality/release check: 29,600 -> Dispatch: 28,800 -> Proof of delivery: 27,900 -> Invoice close: 27,000`), four distinct recovery and debt-remediation loops absorb the fallout of T2/T3:
  1. *Backlog Absorption:* `Receive order -> Backlog prioritization (1,500)`, clearing 1,050 to validation and terminating 450 via `Cancel order`.
  2. *Inventory State Repair:* `Inventory reservation -> Reconcile manual reservation (1,400)`, salvaging 900 cases to `Pick` and forcing 300 to `Cancel order` (with 200 still unresolved). Simultaneously, `Compliance hold` (1,300 vs. 700 baseline) and `Repack/recheck` (900 vs. 450 baseline) double in volume.
  3. *Delivery Evidence Recovery:* `Dispatch -> Proof-of-delivery remediation (1,200) -> Invoice close (800)`.
  4. *Post-Close Compliance Tail:* `Invoice close -> Audit remediation (2,300) -> END (2,100)`, leaving 200 cases open in a persistent remediation queue.
* **Risk Posture:** Latent regulatory exposure and hidden-workload burnout. Operational recovery is partially an accounting artifact: because cycle time is measured up to `Invoice close`, routing 2,300 cases into `Audit remediation` *after* `Invoice close` makes operational cycle times look healthy while masking a long tail of unresolved compliance exceptions (missing lot scans, missing thermal logs, and retroactive authorization reconstruction).

# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

### Conceptual Distinction in Pharmaceutical Distribution
In a regulated pharmaceutical supply chain, **true resilience** is the ability to sustain mission-critical service (delivering life-essential medicines to hospitals and patients) under degraded conditions by **dynamically reallocating capacity and substituting valid, bounded manual controls for unavailable digital controls**. Resilient adaptations make trade-offs explicit, preserve non-negotiable safety and legal invariants (chain of custody, thermal integrity, dual-control verification, recall traceability), and keep technical/compliance debt visible and bounded.

By contrast, **concealed noncompliance (brittle shortcutting)** preserves surface-level throughput and cycle-time metrics by **shedding mandatory verification and traceability gates without compensating controls**. In process mining terms, concealed noncompliance manifests as:
1. **Uncompensated Move-on-Model Deviations (Skipped Gates):** Bypassing `Validate authorization`, `Quality/release check`, outbound barcode scanning, or cold-chain temperature logging to keep boxes moving across the dock.
2. **Metric Boundary Gaming (The Post-Close Tail):** Recording `Invoice close` to stop the operational cycle-time clock (making T4 median cycle times appear recovered at 8h / 28h / 58h) while shunting **2,300 non-conforming cases** into `Audit remediation` and **1,200 cases** into `Proof-of-delivery remediation`.
3. **Irreversible Physical Control Loss vs. Retroactive Paperwork:** Under Good Distribution Practice (GDP), DSCSA lot serialization, and DEA/controlled-substance regulations, certain controls are **time- and state-bound physical observations**. If a cold-chain biologic leaves the dock without thermal verification (`Cold-chain temperature documentation missing: 4.8%` in T2) or an ambient/recall-sensitive lot leaves without a lot-to-order scan (`Dispatch without scan: 3,250`), after-the-fact `Audit remediation` in T4 cannot prove the product stayed within 2–8°C or definitively identify which hospital received which lot during a Class I recall. Treating post-hoc document reconstruction as equivalent to real-time compliance conceals permanent regulatory and patient-safety exposure.

### Classification of Observed Adaptations (A–L)

#### 1. True Resilience (Adaptations B, F [Controlled-Substance Core], I [Conceptually], J, K)
* **B (`Manual authorization check`: 2,500 cases):** Substituted trained compliance personnel and offline/phone verification for the down WMS gate. It accepted slower processing to preserve genuine preventative control prior to shipment.
* **F (`Temporary release control` — Controlled-Substance Scope: 1,650 cases):** Deploying pharmacists to physically inspect 100% of controlled-substance orders directly substituted human dual-control for electronic hard-stops, containing controlled-substance exceptions to **2.9%** in T2 despite complete system loss.
* **I (`Defer noncritical order`: 1,080 inflows) & J (`Backlog prioritization`: 1,500 cases):** Deliberately shedding non-urgent bulk load during acute capacity loss (I) and draining the resulting queue by clinical criticality and product risk rather than blind FIFO (J) represent textbook adaptive capacity management.
* **K (`Reconcile manual reservation`: 1,400 cases):** Explicitly confronting and repairing state divergence between paper picks and digital WMS records rather than ignoring inventory drift.

#### 2. Risky Shortcuts / Concealed Noncompliance (Adaptations C, G, the Uninspected Bypass around F, and Misuse of L)
* **C (`Skip system authorization check`: 1,600 cases):** A pure move-on-log shortcut that traded regulatory compliance for throughput on nearly a third of triaged T2 orders, exposing the wholesaler to shipping to customers with expired/revoked licenses or active compliance holds.
* **G (`Dispatch without scan`: 3,250 cases):** The single largest source of concealed noncompliance. Every single dispatched order in T2 bypassed outbound scanning (both the 1,800 orders directly from `Manual pack` and the 1,450 orders from `Temporary release control`), driving traceability exceptions up **11.9x** (from 0.8% to 9.5%) and blinding the firm to lot custody.
* **Bypassing Release Control (`Manual pack -> Dispatch without scan`: 1,800 cases):** Allowing 52.2% of packed T2 orders to skip `Temporary release control` completely—including cold-chain shipments whose missing temperature documentation spiked **12x** to **4.8%**—preserved dock speed by sacrificing product quality verification.
* **L (`Audit remediation` after `Invoice close`: 2,300 cases) as a Substitute for Control:** While investigating exceptions is necessary, routing 2,300 defective cases *after* `Invoice close` decoupled operational performance metrics from compliance reality and created a persistent, unclosed remediation tail past Day 21.

#### 3. Ambiguous Adaptations Depending on Risk Appetite and Execution Boundaries (Adaptations A, D, E, H, and the Execution of I)
* **A (`Hospital-critical fast lane`: 1,000 cases):** *Resilient intent, hazardous boundary.* Prioritizing hospital orders saved lives and maintained a 91% fill rate at a 10h median cycle time. However, allowing fast-lane orders to advance before authorization was confirmed is only acceptable if bounded by a formal executive risk appetite (e.g., pre-verified standing hospital licenses for non-controlled life-saving drugs) rather than ad-hoc floor discretion.
* **D (`Manual inventory snapshot check`: 4,720 cases):** *Temporally decaying resilience.* Highly resilient on Day 0 afternoon when the morning printout matched physical shelves; progressively reckless by Day 1–2 as thousands of manual picks invalidated the static snapshot, generating the **12.5%** rework and **7.8%** cancellation rates.
* **E (`Manual pick list`: 4,340 inflows):** *Operationally necessary, evidentiary weak.* Paper/spreadsheet picking was unavoidable without WMS RF terminals, but relying on free-text spreadsheets without physical lot-sticker capture caused the downstream traceability crisis.
* **H (`Phone proof of delivery`: 2,600 cases):** *Product-dependent validity.* Acceptable under commercial risk appetite to confirm arrival of low-risk **Ambient** goods, but legally noncompliant for **Controlled-substance** (requires signed physical chain-of-custody) and **Cold-chain** (requires arrival temperature/time acceptance).
* **I (`Defer noncritical order` impact on `Pharmacy-standard`):** *Over-rotation risk.* While deferring **Wholesale-bulk** (median 96h) was appropriate, starving **Pharmacy-standard** to a **61% fill rate** and **118h p90 cycle time** transferred acute disruption risk onto community retail patients.

# 4. Recovery process model

### Notation Explanation (POWL / Process-Tree Notation)
The redesigned recovery model below is expressed in **Process-Tree / POWL (Partially Ordered Workflow Language)** notation, supplemented with structured operational rules:
* $\rightarrow(A, B, C)$ : **Strict Sequence** — step $A$ must complete before $B$, and $B$ before $C$.
* $\times(A, B)$ : **Exclusive Choice (XOR)** — exactly one branch is taken based on explicit decision predicates.
* $\wedge(A, B)$ : **Parallel / Concurrent Execution (AND)** — all branches must be executed before advancing.
* $\circlearrowleft(A, B)$ : **Structured Loop** — execute body $A$; either exit or execute redo/remediation path $B$ and repeat $A$.

---

### Formal POWL Recovery Process Specification

$$\text{Disruption\_Recovery\_Process} = \rightarrow\big(\text{Phase\_1\_Trigger}, \text{Phase\_2\_Continuity\_Execution}, \text{Phase\_3\_PreCutover\_Sync}, \text{Phase\_4\_Stabilized\_Close}\big)$$

1. **$\text{Phase\_1\_Trigger}$ (Outage Detection & State Freeze):**
   $$\rightarrow\big(\text{Detect\_WMS\_Outage\_>30m}, \wedge(\text{Freeze\_Auto\_Allocations}, \text{Export\_Signed\_Offline\_Snapshots}, \text{Activate\_Manual\_Ledger\_Desk})\big)$$

2. **$\text{Phase\_2\_Continuity\_Execution}$ (Risk-Tiered Manual Operations during T1/T2):**
   $$\rightarrow\big(\text{Receive\_Order}, \text{Risk\_Matrix\_Triage}, \times(\text{Path\_Active\_Fulfillment}, \text{Path\_Bounded\_Deferral})\big)$$
   * **$\text{Path\_Bounded\_Deferral}$ (Low-Priority Load Shedding with Anti-Starvation):**
     $$\rightarrow\big(\circlearrowleft(\text{Hold\_in\_Priority\_Backlog\_Queue}, \text{Recalculate\_Dynamic\_Priority\_Score}), \times(\text{Release\_to\_Fulfillment}, \text{Proactive\_Customer\_Cancel\_with\_Notice})\big)$$
   * **$\text{Path\_Active\_Fulfillment}$ (Controlled Degraded Fulfillment):**
     $$\rightarrow\big(\text{Tiered\_Offline\_Auth\_Gate}, \text{Decrementing\_Inventory\_Ledger\_Check}, \text{Twin\_Sticker\_Manual\_Pick}, \text{Manual\_Pack}, \text{Mandatory\_Risk\_Release\_Gate}, \text{Offline\_Batch\_Scan\_Dispatch}, \text{Tiered\_POD\_Capture}\big)$$
     * Where $\text{Tiered\_Offline\_Auth\_Gate} = \times(\text{Cached\_Whitelist\_AutoPass}, \text{Compliance\_Staff\_Manual\_Verify}, \text{Compliance\_Hold\_Reject})$
     * Where $\text{Mandatory\_Risk\_Release\_Gate} = \times\big(\wedge(\text{Pharmacist\_Dual\_Sign}, \text{Seal\_Check})_{[\text{Controlled}]}, \wedge(\text{Thermal\_Logger\_Verify}, \text{Packout\_Time\_Sign})_{[\text{Cold-Chain}]}, \text{Lot\_Manifest\_Match}_{[\text{Recall-Sensitive}]}, \text{Sample\_QC\_10\%}_{[\text{Ambient}]}\big)$ *(Zero uninspected bypass permitted for Regulated/Cold/Recall items)*

3. **$\text{Phase\_3\_PreCutover\_Sync}$ (T2 $\rightarrow$ T3/T4 Transition Gate — Executed *Before* Resuming Automated WMS Allocation):**
   $$\rightarrow\big(\text{Upload\_Offline\_Scan\_&\text{Ledger\_Batches}}, \circlearrowleft(\text{Compare\_WMS\_vs\_Physical\_Ledger}, \text{Targeted\_Bin\_Cycle\_Count\_Adjustment}), \text{Release\_Clean\_Inventory\_to\_WMS}, \text{Score\_&\text{Inject\_Backlog\_Queue}}\big)$$

4. **$\text{Phase\_4\_Stabilized\_Close}$ (Pre-Invoice Verification & Transparent Exception Handling in T4):**
   $$\rightarrow\big(\text{Match\_Order\_Pick\_Release\_Dispatch\_POD}, \times(\text{Clean\_Match}, \text{Pre\_Invoice\_Exception\_Remediation}), \text{Invoice\_Close}, \times(\text{END}, \text{Formal\_CAPA\_Regulatory\_Disclosure})\big)$$

---

### Detailed Specification of Required Model Components

#### 1. Explicit Trigger Conditions
* **Activation Trigger (T0 $\rightarrow$ T1/T2):** Triggered automatically if WMS core allocation, RF scanning, or authorization verification fails for **$>30\text{ minutes}$**, OR if system-sync error rate exceeds **5%** of active picks over a 15-minute rolling window.
* **Immediate Automated Actions on Trigger:**
  1. Pull the latest **Rolling 15-Minute Offline Resilience Package** (kept continuously synced on an isolated, read-only local resilience server/appliance): (a) Customer/Product Authorization Whitelist, (b) Lot/Bin Inventory Balance Ledger, and (c) Pre-generated Barcode Order/Tote Label Ranges.
  2. Freeze automated order reservation to prevent split-brain allocations.
* **De-escalation Trigger (T2 $\rightarrow$ T3 $\rightarrow$ T4):** Automated `Inventory reservation` and `Pick` are **prohibited** from restarting immediately when the WMS boots up. Transition to T3/T4 requires completing the **Pre-Cutover Ledger Sync Gate** (reconciling all T2 paper/ledger decrements against WMS stock and achieving **$\ge 99\%$** bin balance agreement on top-200 velocity SKUs and **100%** on Controlled/Cold-chain SKUs).

#### 2. Triage Rules & Capacity Allocation
Every incoming order at `Receive order` is classified along a $3 \times 4$ **Customer Segment $\times$ Product Risk** matrix and assigned to one of three execution lanes:
* **Lane 1 — Immediate Protected Fast Lane (Target: 65% of degraded capacity):** All **Hospital-critical** orders and clinically urgent **Pharmacy-standard** orders (Cold-chain, Controlled-substance, Recall-sensitive, life-sustaining therapies). Processed immediately.
* **Lane 2 — Quota-Protected Standard Lane (Target: 30% of degraded capacity):** Standard **Pharmacy-standard** Ambient orders. Guaranteed at least 30% of hourly warehouse picking waves so pharmacy fill rates never drop below **80%** (preventing the T2 collapse to 61%).
* **Lane 3 — Bounded Deferral Lane (Target: 5% of degraded capacity / Deferred):** **Wholesale-bulk** Ambient orders and non-urgent stock replenishments. Routed to the managed `Backlog queue` with immediate customer notification of expected SLA delay.

#### 3. Minimum Controls by Product and Customer Risk
No order may bypass authorization or release checks unrecorded. Controls scale strictly by product risk rather than ad-hoc skipping:

| Product Type | Customer Segment | Authorization Control (Replaces Pattern C) | Release Check at Pack-Out (Fixes Pattern F Bypass) | Dispatch & POD Control (Replaces Patterns G & H) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

#### 4. Manual Evidence Capture Protocol
To eliminate spreadsheet transcription errors (`Manual pick list` driving 12.5% rework and 9.5% traceability exceptions) and un-scanned dispatches (`Dispatch without scan: 3,250`):
1. **Decrementing Inventory Ledger:** Instead of handing pickers static copies of a morning printout (Pattern D), a central **Inventory Control Desk** holds the master offline ledger (on an isolated local laptop/tablet or master board) and decrements stock *before* issuing each wave of pick tickets.
2. **Twin Peel-Off Barcode Stickers:** Stock bins and cases carry twin peel-off barcode/lot stickers (or pickers use battery-powered **offline batch-memory barcode scanners** that store `(Order_ID, SKU, Lot, Qty, Timestamp)` locally in CSV memory). When picking manually, the picker peels the duplicate lot/serial barcode sticker and affixes it directly onto the paper `Manual pick list` next to the line item.
3. **Dock Gate Pass:** Security at the loading dock is prohibited from releasing a pallet to a courier (`Dispatch`) without collecting the **Dock Gate Pass** copy of the manifest containing: (a) affixed lot stickers / offline scanner sync confirmation, (b) the Release Pharmacist/QC stamp (mandatory for Controlled, Cold-chain, Recall-sensitive), and (c) the courier driver's signature and timestamp.

#### 5. Backlog Prioritization Algorithm
Both during T2/T3 deferral draining and T4 system recovery (`Backlog prioritization`), orders in the backlog queue are ranked dynamically every 2 hours using an explicit **Priority Score ($S_i$)** to balance clinical urgency against customer fairness (preventing starvation):
$$S_i = (W_{\text{seg}} \times C_{\text{seg}}) + (W_{\text{prod}} \times R_{\text{prod}}) + \left(W_{\text{age}} \times \frac{\text{Age\_Hours}_i}{\text{SLA\_Hours}_{\text{seg}}}\right)$$
* **Segment Weight ($W_{\text{seg}} \times C_{\text{seg}}$):** Hospital-critical = $100$, Pharmacy-standard = $50$, Wholesale-bulk = $15$.
* **Product Risk Weight ($W_{\text{prod}} \times R_{\text{prod}}$):** Cold-chain / Controlled = $40$, Recall-sensitive = $25$, Ambient = $10$.
* **Anti-Starvation Aging Escalator ($W_{\text{age}} = 30$):** As an order's wait time ($\text{Age\_Hours}_i$) exceeds its baseline p90 SLA ($15\text{h}$ Hospital, $49\text{h}$ Pharmacy, $72\text{h}$ Wholesale), its aging term grows linearly. Once a **Pharmacy-standard** order reaches $1.5\times$ its p90 SLA ($73.5\text{h}$) or a **Wholesale-bulk** order reaches $2.0\times$ its p90 SLA ($144\text{h}$), it automatically elevates into the active picking wave or triggers a proactive customer consultation (confirming whether to hold or cancel without penalty).

#### 6. Reconciliation and Audit-Remediation Flow
The redesigned flow corrects the two major T4 structural flaws (`Reconcile manual reservation` occurring too late, and `Audit remediation` occurring *after* `Invoice close`):
1. **Stage 1 — Pre-Cutover Inventory Reconciliation (Before T4 Automated Reservation):** Before switching WMS order allocation back on, all offline scanner memory files and Decrementing Ledger logs from T2/T3 are batch-uploaded into the WMS staging table. Discrepancies trigger immediate physical bin verification (`Reconcile manual reservation` runs as a **pre-open batch job**, eliminating the 1,400 mid-stream reservation collisions and 300 late cancellations seen in T4).
2. **Stage 2 — Pre-Close Evidentiary Reconciliation (Before `Invoice close`):** Orders fulfilled during T2/T3 enter a `Pre-Invoice Compliance Sync` queue prior to `Invoice close`. Here, twin-sticker manifests, offline batch scans, thermal logger downloads, and signed courier PODs are matched to digital order records.
   * **Clean or Reconciled Cases:** Proceed to `Invoice close -> END`.
   * **True Unrecoverable Control Exceptions (CAPA Path):** If a real-time physical control was genuinely missed (e.g., a cold-chain logger failed or wasn't packed, or a controlled-substance signature is missing), the case is **blocked from standard `Invoice close`** and routed to `Quality & Regulatory Exception / CAPA`. Rather than fabricating after-the-fact paperwork to make compliance look whole, Quality assesses product stability/custody with the customer, initiates a targeted recall/quarantine if required, and logs a formal regulatory deviation.

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management must explicitly **accept** slower cycle times for non-critical orders (allowing Wholesale-bulk median cycle time to stretch to 72–96h and Pharmacy-standard to 30–36h), higher front-line supervisory labor at pack-out, and a temporary 24–48h billing delay (`Pre-Invoice Compliance Sync`) during a cyber disruption in order to protect both acute patient care and unbroken regulatory chain-of-custody. Conversely, management must **never accept** un-scanned dock dispatches (`Dispatch without scan`), ad-hoc skipping of customer/product authorization (`Skip system authorization check`), uninspected pack-out bypasses for Cold-chain, Controlled-substance, or Recall-sensitive medicines, or the use of post-invoice `Audit remediation` to retroactively paper over missing real-time physical controls—Nor should it accept starving Pharmacy-standard fill rates down to 61% to keep Hospital-critical median cycle times at 10h when Hospital p90 SLA is 15h. **What evidence would change this recommendation:** Only a formal, written emergency waiver from the national medicines regulator (e.g., FDA/EMA/DEA) during a declared public-health mass-casualty emergency—or empirical proof that Hospital-critical life-saving orders are breaching their 15h p90 clinical survival threshold despite 100% deferral of Wholesale-bulk volume—would justify relaxing pre-shipment authorization or transitioning Ambient pharmacy orders to post-shipment verification; even under those extreme conditions, physical lot sticker capture and cold-chain/controlled-substance release checks must remain non-negotiable.
