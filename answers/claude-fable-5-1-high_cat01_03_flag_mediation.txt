## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

Framework. I do not use natural direct/indirect effects as the primary estimands. Natural effects require cross-world independence (the mediator value a case *would* have had without the flag must be independent of the outcome it *would* have had with the flag, given covariates). That fails here for three structural reasons: (i) the flag is a policy bundle (priority lane + mandatory review + what reviewers do after seeing the flag), so "flag = 0 with the mediator held at its flag = 1 value" describes no operable state; (ii) there are exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounders (triage completion time fixes the time-of-day and queue state that drive both `manual_queue_wait` and `escalation_queue_wait`; `manual_review` occurrence itself confounds later waits and the decision); (iii) shared queues create interference, so a case's mediator depends on other cases' exposures. I therefore use, in order of preference:

- Separable effects (Robins–Richardson / Stensrud et al.): the flag is decomposed into operable components — display/lane component (`priority_lane`), routing component (mandatory `manual_review`), and reviewer-behaviour component — and each policy P1–P4 is a contrast that switches a component. These map one-to-one onto the policies and require no cross-world counterfactual.
- Interventional (randomized-interventional-analogue) indirect effects (VanderWeele–Vansteelandt–Daniel) when the question is "how much runs through mediator M": draw M from its covariate-conditional distribution under the comparison policy. These are well-defined under exposure-induced confounding, at the price of being population rather than individual quantities.
- Controlled direct effects only where the mediator is itself a feasible policy lever (`manual_review := 0`).
- Population-level total/overall/spillover effects under partial interference (Hudgens–Halloran) for any queue-wait outcome, with the shift or site-day as the interference cluster.

Interference arises because manual review and escalation queues are shared: every flagged case pushed into review lengthens `manual_queue_wait` for other cases, and the six flagged escalations compete with the two unflagged ones for the same senior team. Exposure-induced confounding arises at two points. First, the priority lane fixes `triage_done_clock`, which determines the queue state and shift the case meets; that clock state then drives both `manual_queue_wait` and `escalation_queue_wait`, so the mediator–outcome relation is confounded by a variable the exposure created. Second, `manual_review` occurrence is itself caused by the flag and confounds every downstream wait and the decision, which is why occurrence mediation and waiting mediation must be separated rather than read off one "review stage" duration. Treatment–confounder feedback appears over the shift: flagged arrivals raise queue state, queue state changes reviewers' willingness to escalate or to pull unflagged cases early, and that feeds back into the next arrivals' waits. Natural-effect decompositions are not identified under these structures; interventional and separable contrasts are.

Descriptive accounting of the 8.3 h mean gap (16.5 − 8.2): triage wait and service are 2.6 h shorter for flagged cases; the manual stage adds 7.4 h (10/10 reviewed versus 4/10, and reviewed flagged cases wait 6.5 h versus 1.9 h with identical 2.7 h service); escalation adds 3.6 h (6/10 versus 2/10 escalated, each costing ~7 h wait plus ~2 h service); residual is flat. Crudely, ~2.8 h of the manual stage is occurrence (60 extra points × 4.6 h at unflagged intensities) and ~4.6 h is extra waiting per reviewed case. The pattern is acceleration at triage and delay downstream, concentrated in queue waits, not service. These contrasts are not causal: complexity is unbalanced (no Low-complexity flagged case; 5/10 flagged versus 3/10 unflagged are High), reviewer M2 carries seven of eight escalations, unflagged reviews are reviewer-pulled so "decide to review" and "start" nearly coincide, and flagged inflow lengthens everyone's shared-queue waits. The direction persists within complexity strata (Medium 10.6 vs 6.0; High 22.4 vs 15.4), weakening pure case-mix confounding, but cells hold only 3–5 cases.

Pilot selection: P3, protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases, run as a shift-level stepped-wedge with within-shift randomization.

- Eligibility: all flagged cases arriving in participating shifts; Medium and High complexity (Low has no flagged support). Unflagged cases are enrolled for spillover measurement only.
- Assignment: shifts randomized to coverage 0%, 50%, or 100% in a rotating schedule; in 50% shifts, flagged cases are randomized 1:1 at `triage_done` to the protected slot versus the shared queue. Protected slot means a named reviewer (rotating, not always M2) whose hours are additional to baseline.
- Queue-capacity guardrail: if the unflagged reviewed 90th-percentile wait in a treated shift exceeds its baseline by more than 1 h, or the shared queue length at any `triage_done` exceeds 4, the shift reverts to 0% coverage for the next shift and the event is logged.
- Primary outcome: `triage_done → manual_start` for flagged cases (per-case), and shift-level mean cycle time for all cases (overall effect).
- Secondary: flagged cycle time; escalation rate and escalation wait; unflagged review wait (spillover); reject rate and a 10% blinded audit of approvals for decision quality; reviewer FIFO violations.
- Minimum data: per-event start and complete timestamps with time zone, reviewer id, protected-slot indicator, queue length at entry, shift id, complexity, `risk_score`, decision, audit result.
- Stopping: audited miss rate in any arm exceeds baseline by 2 points; guardrail triggers on three consecutive treated shifts; or 8 weeks / ~120 flagged cases reached.

TS_COLS = ["arrival", "triage_start", "triage_done", "manual_start", "manual_done",
           "escalation_start", "escalation_done", "close"]
MIN_SUPPORT = 5

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 1. parse
wide = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(CSV), na_values=["-"])
for c in TS_COLS:
    # "-" became NaN via na_values; errors="coerce" keeps absent stages as NaT
    wide[c] = pd.to_datetime(wide[c], format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M", errors="coerce")
wide["flag"] = wide["flag"].astype(int)

# ------------------------------------------------- 2. wide -> event level
# (activity, start column, complete column, resource column). Register and
# Close are instantaneous, so start == complete. A row is emitted only when
# the completion timestamp exists: absent stages produce NO event.
STAGES = [
    ("Register", "arrival", "arrival", None),
    ("Triage", "triage_start", "triage_done", "triage_resource"),
    ("Manual Review", "manual_start", "manual_done", "review_resource"),
    ("Escalation", "escalation_start", "escalation_done", None),
    ("Close", "close", "close", None),
]
CASE_ATTRS = ["risk_score", "flag", "complexity", "arrival_load", "outcome"]

rows = []
for _, r in wide.iterrows():
    for act, s_col, e_col, res_col in STAGES:
        if pd.isna(r[e_col]):
            continue
        rows.append({
            "case:concept:name": r["case"],
            "concept:name": act,
            "start_timestamp": r[s_col],
            "time:timestamp": r[e_col],
            "org:resource": (r[res_col] if res_col and pd.notna(r[res_col]) else None),
            **{f"case:{a}": r[a] for a in CASE_ATTRS},
        })
events = pd.DataFrame(rows).sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])

# ------------------------------------------------------------ 3. format
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    events,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
)

# --------------------------------------------- 4. DFGs per flag stratum
def dfgs_for(sub, label):
    freq_dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_dfg(sub)
    # edge value = mean seconds from completion of source to completion of target
    perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(sub, perf_aggregation_key="mean")
    print(f"\n=== {label}: {sub['case:concept:name'].nunique()} cases ===")
    print("start activities:", sa, "| end activities:", ea)
    for edge, f in sorted(freq_dfg.items(), key=lambda kv: -kv[1]):
        perf = perf_dfg.get(edge, float("nan"))
        if isinstance(perf, dict):  # older versions return {agg: value}
            perf = perf.get("mean", float("nan"))
        print(f"  {edge[0]:>14} -> {edge[1]:<14} freq={f:2d}  mean gap={perf / 3600.0:6.2f} h")
    return freq_dfg, perf_dfg

flagged = log[log["case:flag"] == 1]

dfg_f = dfgs_for(flagged, "FLAG = 1")
dfg_u = dfgs_for(unflagged, "FLAG = 0")

warnings.warn(
    "Performance-DFG edge times mix waiting and service (edge = completion of "
    "source to completion of target) and are conditional on the edge occurring. "
    "Differences between the flag=1 and flag=0 DFGs are DESCRIPTIVE. They are "
    "confounded by complexity/underlying risk, by which resources serve each "
    "lane, and by shared-queue interference; they are not causal effects of the flag."
)

# ------------------------------------ 5. explicit stage intervals (hours)
def hours(a, b):
    return (wide[b] - wide[a]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0

stage = pd.DataFrame({
    "case": wide["case"], "flag": wide["flag"], "complexity": wide["complexity"],
    "arrival_load": wide["arrival_load"], "risk_score": wide["risk_score"],
    "review_resource": wide["review_resource"], "outcome": wide["outcome"],
    "triage_wait": hours("arrival", "triage_start"),
    "triage_service": hours("triage_start", "triage_done"),
    "manual_queue_wait": hours("triage_done", "manual_start"),      # NaN if no review
    "manual_service": hours("manual_start", "manual_done"),
    "escalation_queue_wait": hours("manual_done", "escalation_start"),  # NaN if no escalation
    "senior_service": hours("escalation_start", "escalation_done"),
    "cycle_time": hours("arrival", "close"),
})
last_done = wide["escalation_done"].fillna(wide["manual_done"]).fillna(wide["triage_done"])
stage["residual_to_close"] = (wide["close"] - last_done).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0
stage["manual_review"] = wide["manual_start"].notna().astype(int)
stage["escalation"] = wide["escalation_start"].notna().astype(int)

# Optional PM4Py case-level decomposition. insert_case_service_waiting_time
# adds, per case: @@service_time = sum of (complete - start) over the case's
# OBSERVED events; @@sojourn_time = last complete - first start; and
# @@waiting_time = sojourn - service. It therefore only sums stages that
# occurred and never treats a missing stage as a zero-length activity.
try:
    svc = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
        log, start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp", timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )
    case_svc = (svc.groupby("case:concept:name")[["@@service_time", "@@sojourn_time", "@@waiting_time"]]
                .first() / 3600.0)
    stage = stage.merge(case_svc, left_on="case", right_index=True, how="left")
    print("\nPM4Py case-level totals (hours): service = sum of observed stage durations, "
          "waiting = sojourn - service")
    print(stage.groupby("flag")[["@@service_time", "@@waiting_time", "@@sojourn_time"]].mean().round(2))
except Exception as exc:  # keep the script runnable across pm4py versions
    warnings.warn(f"insert_case_service_waiting_time unavailable or failed: {exc}")

# ------------------------------------------------- 6. summaries + support
INTERVALS = ["triage_wait", "triage_service", "manual_queue_wait", "manual_service",

def summarize(sub, label):
    n = len(sub)
    if n < MIN_SUPPORT:
        warnings.warn(f"subgroup '{label}' has n={n} < {MIN_SUPPORT}; treat as anecdotal")
    print(f"\n--- {label} (n={n}; reviewed={int(sub.manual_review.sum())}, "
          f"escalated={int(sub.escalation.sum())}) ---")

        "mean_h": sub[INTERVALS].mean().round(2),
        "median_h": sub[INTERVALS].median().round(2),
        "support": sub[INTERVALS].notna().sum(),  # cases in which the stage occurred
    })
    print(out.to_string())

        if len(sub):
            summarize(sub, f"complexity={cx}, flag={f}")
        else:
            print(f"\n--- complexity={cx}, flag={f}: NO SUPPORT (positivity violation) ---")
for load in ("High", "Normal"):
    summarize(stage[(stage.flag == 1) & (stage.arrival_load == load)], f"flag=1, arrival_load={load}")
for esc in (1, 0):
    summarize(stage[(stage.flag == 1) & (stage.escalation == esc)], f"flag=1, escalation={esc}")

print("\nRouting rates by flag:")
print(stage.groupby("flag")[["manual_review", "escalation"]].mean().round(2))
print("\nOutcome by flag:")
print(pd.crosstab(stage.flag, stage.outcome))
print("\nReview resource by complexity (resource assignment is entangled with complexity):")
print(pd.crosstab(stage.review_resource.fillna("none"), stage.complexity))

    "occurrence; flag=1 cases are 100% reviewed while flag=0 cases are 40% reviewed, "
    "so 'flag effect on manual wait' is undefined without a routing intervention. "
    "No natural direct/indirect effect is identified here: the flag is a policy "
    "bundle (priority lane + mandatory review + reviewer behaviour), queues are "
    "shared (interference), and manual_review occurrence is an exposure-induced "
    "confounder of downstream waits and outcomes."
)

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. Hidden risk. If an audit or later adverse-event follow-up shows that flagged cases carry materially more true risk than unflagged cases of the same complexity and score band, then the longer review and escalation waits are appropriate handling of harder cases rather than a routing artefact; the within-complexity gap would be attributed to `U_risk`, P2 would become unsafe, and the recommendation would shift from capacity to accepting the cycle-time cost.
2. Queue interference. If the queue-state analysis shows that unflagged reviewed cases wait as long as flagged ones at equal reconstructed queue length, and that unflagged waits rise one-for-one when flagged cases get protected capacity, then P3 is a pure transfer of waiting; the mechanism is aggregate congestion and the lever becomes total reviewer capacity or fewer reviews (P2), not protection for flagged cases.
3. Decision quality. If the pilot audit finds that the four-to-nine hours of review for approved Medium-complexity flagged cases produces corrections, deterrence, or documentation that later prevents losses, or if reject rates under discretionary review fall, then occurrence mediation is buying quality and cutting it (P2) or speeding it (P3, if speed reduces care) would reverse from beneficial to harmful.
4. Timestamp semantics. If `manual_start` is a system assignment stamp for pushed (flagged) cases but a work-start stamp for pulled (unflagged) cases, the 6.5 h vs 1.9 h wait contrast is measurement, not queueing; likewise if the senior team's escalation timestamps mark shift hand-over rather than work start, the ~7 h escalation wait is a calendar artefact and P4 capacity changes would not shorten it. Overlapping M2 sessions already hint at assignment-style stamps.
5. Positivity or lack of overlap. No Low-complexity case is flagged and no flagged case is unreviewed. If the pilot population turns out to consist mostly of High-complexity flagged cases (where escalation, not review wait, dominates), P3's expected 4–5 h gain shrinks toward zero and P4 becomes the priority; any estimand for Low-complexity flagged cases remains undefined and should not be extrapolated.
6. Behaviour change by reviewers. If reviewers respond to protected capacity by reviewing flagged cases more slowly or escalating more (induced demand), or respond to hidden flags by reviewing everything, the separable-component logic fails because the reviewer-behaviour component is not held fixed; observed pilot waits would understate the policy effect and the decomposition into lane, routing, and behaviour would have to be re-estimated with reviewer fixed effects.

## 9. Final synthesis

Directly observed. In twenty cases over one and a half days, flagged cases reach triage in 0.2 h instead of 2.5 h and are triaged faster (0.6 vs 1.0 h), yet close 8.3 h later on average (16.5 vs 8.2 h). Every flagged case is reviewed (4/10 unflagged), reviewed flagged cases wait 6.5 h for a reviewer versus 1.9 h, service times are identical, 6/10 flagged versus 2/10 unflagged are escalated, and each escalation costs about 9 h of mostly overnight waiting. Reject counts are equal (2 and 2), all rejects are High complexity and escalated, and reviewer M2 carries all High-complexity work.

Plausible mechanism. The flag is a bundle: a lane that saves ~2.6 h, a mandatory-review rule that adds review occurrence (~2.8 h) and, more importantly, queue waiting behind pulled and High-complexity work (~4.6 h), and a reviewer-behaviour component that raises escalation into a scarce, shift-bound senior team (~3.6 h). Delay is in queues, not in service.

Unidentified. Whether the extra waiting is congestion, queue discipline, or timestamp semantics; how much of the within-complexity gap is hidden risk; whether review of Medium-complexity flagged cases changes decisions; and any natural direct/indirect decomposition, which cross-world assumptions cannot support here.

Most useful next evidence. A shift-randomized P3 pilot with protected reviewer slots, queue-length reconstruction, unflagged spillover measurement, and an approval audit. It is safe (no control removed), directly tests the largest mediated component, exposes interference, and generates the decision-quality baseline needed before any P2 or P4 change to routing or escalation criteria is contemplated.
