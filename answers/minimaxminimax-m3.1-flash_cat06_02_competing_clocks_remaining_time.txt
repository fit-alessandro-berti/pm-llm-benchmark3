## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

A useful shorthand is:

\[
T_{\text{terminal}}
=
\max(T_{\text{chemistry usable}},T_{\text{microbiology usable}})
+W_{\text{QA}}+S_{\text{QA}}
+R_{\text{QA}}+T_{\text{final}}.
\]

The stage times in the maximum are absolute calendar timestamps. Chemistry and microbiology are **not added**.

**Smooth path, routine Monday readout**

- **Chemistry waiting and service.**
  - The two active samples finish at approximately Friday 16:42–17:30 and 15:54–16:24.
  - Under FIFO, B-417 remains behind four waiting samples plus the active work. It is therefore unlikely to start before Monday; a working estimate is Monday 10:30–13:00.
  - Under risk priority, B-417 is normally selected after the urgent sample, around Friday 16:42–17:30. Even the 1.5-hour minimum service would normally extend past 18:00, so the sample pauses and resumes Monday. There is only a narrow low-probability edge in which it finishes before Friday closing.
  - With a typical 2.5-hour service, chemistry completion is approximately Monday 09:30–13:00 under risk priority, or Monday 12:00–17:30 under FIFO. The FIFO upper end is sensitive to the queue and service time.
- **Microbiology incubation and readout.**
  - Technical readiness spans Saturday 12:00 to Sunday 20:00.
  - Without on-call authorization, the result is read Monday 08:00–09:30.
- **Parallel join.**
  - Use the maximum of Monday 09:30–09:30 for routine microbiology and roughly Monday 09:30–17:30 for chemistry.
  - The join is therefore approximately Monday 09:30–17:30, not the sum of the two branch durations.
- **QA waiting and service.**
  - Use a future QA queue of about 3–6 batches. With two Monday reviewers, central waiting is approximately 0.5–3 hours, with a 4–8 hour congestion tail.
  - QA service is centered on 1.5 hours, with a 3.5-hour upper-quantile case.
- **Optional rework/investigation.** None in this scenario.
- **Final release.** Add 0.25–0.75 hours to QA acceptance. This gives the central Monday afternoon/evening release range and a possible Tuesday tail if chemistry or QA work runs late.

**Smooth path with on-call microbiology**

- **Chemistry waiting and service.** Assume the favorable risk-priority outcome: B-417 begins late Friday, pauses at 18:00, and completes around Monday 09:30–13:00.
- **Microbiology incubation and readout.**
  - The earliest technical readiness is Saturday 12:00, followed by 1–3 hours’ dispatch/readout, giving Saturday 13:00–15:00 at the very earliest.
  - Central technical readiness is around Sunday 16:00, making a central on-call result Sunday 17:00–19:00.
- **Parallel join.**
  - The microbiology result is available before Monday QA, but chemistry is not ready before roughly Monday 09:30–13:00. The join is therefore Monday morning, only modestly earlier than in the no-authorization case.
  - Saturday completion is not credible: the earliest result arrives as Saturday QA closes, and a normal QA review cannot finish without overtime.
- **QA waiting and service.** The future Monday queue remains the main uncertainty. A lighter queue or favorable position can produce release around 11:30–16:00; a heavy queue returns the outcome to the ordinary smooth range.
- **Optional rework/investigation.** None assumed.
- **Final release.** Add 0.25–0.75 hours.

**Chemistry rerun**

- **Chemistry waiting and service.** The initial chemistry result follows the smooth-path queue and service. The rerun occurs only after that result and has priority.
- **Rerun calendar.**
  - If the primary result is available by about Monday 15:00, a 1–3 hour rerun can finish before Monday 18:00.
  - If the primary result arrives later, the rerun pauses at 18:00 and resumes Tuesday 08:00, finishing approximately Tuesday 09:00–11:00.

- **Microbiology incubation and readout.** No contamination is assumed in the dominant rerun scenario; routine Monday 08:00–09:30 remains the fallback.

- **Optional rework/investigation.** A rerun is an adverse event, so an investigation has probability 0.30–0.55. That investigation is not added to the rerun automatically; it is an alternative branch, often overlapping with the Tuesday delay.

**Microbiology contamination confirmation**

**Confirmed temperature excursion with associated investigation**

- **Chemistry waiting and service.** A confirmed excursion raises the chance of a borderline result and rerun, but the increased conditional probability is not supplied. No precise joint probability is assigned.
- **Microbiology incubation and readout.** The excursion also raises contamination risk. These events are treated as correlated adverse outcomes: the terminal time is not calculated by adding independent chemistry and microbiology anomaly probabilities.
- **Parallel join.** The join may remain Monday if no actual rerun or contamination occurs, but moves to Tuesday when either branch requires additional work.
- **QA waiting and service.** Normal QA queueing still applies.
- **Optional rework/investigation.** A confirmed excursion gives investigation probability 0.30–0.55. A documentation-only investigation adds 8–20 working hours; if it is laboratory-based, use the 3–7 day path below.
- **Final release.** The central range is Tuesday through Thursday, but the uncertainty is driven by the downstream investigation type rather than by the excursion confirmation alone.

**QA documentation query or documentation-only investigation**

- **Chemistry waiting and service.** Both branches follow the smooth path; the join is Monday.
- **Microbiology incubation and readout.** Routine Monday readout.
- **QA waiting and service.** The batch first joins the approximately 3–6 batch queue and receives an initial review.
- **Optional rework/investigation.**
  - A documentation query, with probability 0.15–0.30, adds 4–12 working hours before QA resumes.
  - A normal-path deviation opens with probability 0.04–0.10; its documentation-only share adds 8–20 working hours.
  - A four-hour query issued early Monday can finish that day. A 12-hour query issued near midday extends into Tuesday. An 8–20 hour investigation generally shifts acceptance into Tuesday or Wednesday.
- **Final release.** Central Tuesday, with Wednesday possible. These durations include the rework itself; they should not also be interpreted as a full additional QA service unless the process explicitly requires another complete review.

**Laboratory deviation investigation**

- **Chemistry and microbiology.** The investigation is usually triggered by an adverse branch result, such as a rerun, contamination indication, or confirmed excursion.
- **Parallel join.** The branch result still has to reach QA before the investigation can be opened.
- **QA waiting and service.** Initial queueing and review occur before or at the start of the investigation.
- **Optional rework/investigation.** The additional 3–7 calendar days is sequential and is not subject to the laboratory weekend pause. If the investigation starts Monday, it ends approximately Thursday through the following Monday.
- **Final release/rejection.** Terminal release follows QA acceptance and the 0.25–0.75 hour release step. A confirmed critical defect could instead lead to rejection, potentially earlier than the end of a long investigation if the critical finding is already established.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. **Unresolved temperature excursion and the induced correlation — dependence uncertainty, with observation uncertainty.**  
   The missing sensor observation can simultaneously increase borderline chemistry, microbiology contamination, and investigation probability. It therefore affects both the central case and the correlated upper tail more than any isolated anomaly probability.

2. **QA queue size and age at the actual join — queue uncertainty.**  
   The current five-batch count cannot simply be carried forward or discarded. Friday/Saturday completions and weekend/Monday arrivals compete with B-417. This is the largest uncertainty around the smooth Monday release time.

3. **Microbiology readiness and staffed readout — calendar uncertainty, with service-time uncertainty.**  
   The 46–54 hour incubation band and weekend staffing determine when a usable result exists. The surprising limitation is that weekend authorization usually helps only modestly because QA is also closed.

4. **Chemistry queue discipline and service time — queue uncertainty and service-time uncertainty.**  
   FIFO can move the join into Monday afternoon, whereas risk priority can produce a late-Monday join. A 4.5-hour service can also cause a Tuesday spillover.

5. **QA rework type and duration — path uncertainty, with calendar and service-time uncertainty.**  
   A four-hour query may barely move the release; a 20-hour documentation investigation pushes it to Wednesday; a 3–7 day laboratory investigation moves it into the following week.
