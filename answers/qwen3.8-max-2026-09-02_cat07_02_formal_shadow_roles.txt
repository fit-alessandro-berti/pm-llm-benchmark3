def top_edges(network, n=15):
    edges = []
    for k, v in network.items():
        if isinstance(k, tuple) and len(k)==2:

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

## R07_Giorgio — commercial shadow role

Giorgio is a shadow commercial resource. His formal base appears to be senior front office, but he repeatedly performs pricing exception review and customer negotiation. The evidence is strong: 72 A7 events, 88 A8 events, similarity 0.71 with Fatima, and direct substitution during Fatima’s absence. During weeks 3–4, he performed 31 pricing reviews and 18 negotiations. However, median pricing exception duration rose from 38 to 74 minutes, suggesting that his commercial competence is real but not equivalent to Fatima’s routine expertise.

Benefits: Giorgio provides resilience, reduces handoffs for complex front-office cases, and allows commercial work to continue during absences. His hybrid profile can improve customer continuity because he already knows entitlement and front-office constraints.

Risks: authority may be unclear, especially if pricing exceptions require formal approval. If Giorgio is overloaded, front-office throughput may suffer. If management assumes substitution proves full competence, they may underinvest in formal commercial training or fail to define delegation limits. This shadow role is not improper; it is a useful organizational adaptation, but it should be made explicit with thresholds, approval rights, and training.

Mateo is the clearest bridging role. He appears across technical, commercial, security, quality, and supervisory activities. Risks: brokerage can become hidden authority. In week 5, Mateo performed 21 preliminary A9 approvals, but 13 were later rechecked by Hana. In weeks 7–8, his security filtering helped reduce noise but contributed to two critical cases waiting more than two days. Mateo’s role should be formalized as incident coordination with explicit triage rules, escalation thresholds, and limits on approval authority. He should not be treated as a supervisor simply because he performs A9-related events during absences.

## R05_Erik — rare but critical security specialist

Erik is a low-frequency, high-impact specialist. His core activity, A6 Security Review, has 96 events, which is modest compared with routine activities, but his complexity profile is extreme: median 0.86, 82 high-complexity cases, and 55 critical cases. Fragment 3 places Erik in the critical security path before coordination and implementation.

Benefits: Erik provides scarce expertise that protects the organization from high-risk security failures. His work likely prevents severe customer, compliance, or operational damage.

Risks: frequency-based mining can make Erik look peripheral. If staffing decisions rely only on event counts, management might under-resource him or assume Mateo can substitute. Nora is a boundary role between automated intake and human processing. She performs high-volume A1 and A2 events, but her “role” is not human work. ## R11_Kai and R12_Lina — temporary-workforce boundary role

Kai and Lina form a temporary surge role. They handle simple register-validate-respond-close work and provide seasonal capacity. The benefit is flexibility. They should not be treated as permanent front-office substitutes without additional training and complexity-based routing.

## R09_Ivan and R10_Julia — quality gatekeeping bridge

Ivan and Julia bridge execution and control. Their core work is quality checking and knowledge updating, but they also perform some implementation and closure. The benefit is that they can correct defects and convert recurring problems into knowledge. The risk is that if management counts their A10/A12 events as implementation capacity, the quality gate may be weakened. Their role should remain independent from the teams whose work they check, especially for high-complexity technical cases.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load and format the dataframe
# ------------------------------------------------------------------

df = pd.read_csv(
    "order_to_resolution_event_log.csv",
    parse_dates=["time:timestamp"]
)

# Expected columns include:
# case:concept:name, concept:name, time:timestamp, org:resource,
# case_complexity, customer_segment, product_line,
# temporary_substitution, escalation_level

df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    resource_key="org:resource"
)

# Optional: convert to event log for PM4Py organizational mining
log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Discover organizational roles
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Roles are behavioral clusters, not formal HR roles.
# Threshold should be tuned: lower threshold usually gives more roles.

role_groups = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(
    log,
    threshold=0.05
)

print("Mined organizational roles")
for i, role in enumerate(role_groups):
    print(f"role_{i}: {sorted(role)}")

# Create a resource-to-mined-role mapping
role_map = {}
for i, role in enumerate(role_groups):
    for resource in role:
        role_map[resource] = f"role_{i}"

resource_role = (
    pd.Series(role_map)
    .rename("mined_role")
    .to_frame()
)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Discover activity-based resource similarity
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# This is useful for finding similar activity mixes, but similarity
# does not prove equal authority, competence, or risk exposure.

similarity_groups = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
    log,
    threshold=0.05
)

print("\nActivity-based similarity groups")
for i, group in enumerate(similarity_groups):
    print(f"similarity_group_{i}: {sorted(group)}")

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Inspect handover, working-together, and subcontracting networks
# ------------------------------------------------------------------

handover_network = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
    log,
    threshold=0.05
)

working_together_network = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(
    log,
    threshold=0.05
)

subcontracting_network = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(
    log,
    threshold=0.05
)

def top_edges(network, n=20):
    """
    PM4Py network outputs are often dictionaries keyed by
    (source_resource, target_resource) with a numeric weight.
    """
    edges = []
    for key, value in network.items():
        if isinstance(key, tuple) and len(key) == 2:
            edges.append((key[0], key[1], value))
    return sorted(edges, key=lambda x: x[2], reverse=True)[:n]

print("\nTop handover-of-work edges")
for source, target, weight in top_edges(handover_network):
    print(f"{source} -> {target}: {weight:.3f}")

print("\nTop working-together edges")
for source, target, weight in top_edges(working_together_network):
    print(f"{source} <-> {target}: {weight:.3f}")

print("\nTop subcontracting edges")
for source, target, weight in top_edges(subcontracting_network):
    print(f"{source} => {target}: {weight:.3f}")

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Compute resource activity matrix and cosine similarity
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# This reproduces the kind of activity-profile similarity mentioned
# in the scenario, but should never be the only evidence.

activity_matrix = df.pivot_table(
    index="org:resource",
    columns="concept:name",
    values="case:concept:name",
    aggfunc="count",
    fill_value=0
)

row_norms = np.linalg.norm(activity_matrix, axis=1)
normalized_matrix = activity_matrix.div(row_norms, axis=0).fillna(0)
cosine_similarity = normalized_matrix @ normalized_matrix.T

print("\nSelected cosine similarities")
pairs_to_check = [
    ("R03_Chen", "R04_Dina"),
    ("R09_Ivan", "R10_Julia"),
    ("R11_Kai", "R12_Lina"),
    ("R01_Amira", "R02_Ben"),
    ("R06_Fatima", "R07_Giorgio"),
    ("R08_Hana", "R13_Mateo"),
    ("R05_Erik", "R13_Mateo"),
    ("R01_Amira", "R07_Giorgio"),
]

for r1, r2 in pairs_to_check:
    if r1 in cosine_similarity.index and r2 in cosine_similarity.columns:
        print(f"{r1} - {r2}: {cosine_similarity.loc[r1, r2]:.3f}")

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Join mined roles with complexity and substitution indicators
# ------------------------------------------------------------------

def critical_flag(value):
    """
    Handles both categorical complexity labels and numeric scores.
    Adjust threshold if complexity is numeric.
    """
    if isinstance(value, str):
        return value.lower() == "critical"
    try:
        return float(value) >= 0.75
    except Exception:
        return False

df["is_critical_case"] = df["case_complexity"].apply(critical_flag)

complexity_by_resource = df.groupby("org:resource").agg(
    median_complexity=("case_complexity", "median"),
    mean_complexity=("case_complexity", "mean"),
    critical_event_share=("is_critical_case", "mean"),
    event_count=("case:concept:name", "count")
)

# If temporary_substitution is boolean:
# True means the event occurred during a documented substitution episode.
if "temporary_substitution" in df.columns:
    substitution_by_resource = df.groupby("org:resource").agg(
        substitution_events=("temporary_substitution", "sum")
    )
else:
    substitution_by_resource = pd.DataFrame(
        index=activity_matrix.index,
        columns=["substitution_events"]
    ).fillna(0)

resource_summary = resource_role.join(
    complexity_by_resource,
    how="outer"
).join(
    substitution_by_resource,
    how="outer"
).join(
    activity_matrix,
    how="outer"
)

print("\nResource summary")
print(resource_summary.head(20))

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. Map mined roles onto events and cases
# ------------------------------------------------------------------

df["mined_role"] = df["org:resource"].map(role_map)

case_role_mix = (
    df.groupby(["case:concept:name", "mined_role"])
    .size()
    .unstack(fill_value=0)
)

case_attributes = df.groupby("case:concept:name").agg(
    case_complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
    customer_segment=("customer_segment", "first"),
    product_line=("product_line", "first"),
    escalation_level=("escalation_level", "first")
)

case_summary = case_attributes.join(case_role_mix, how="left")

print("\nCase-level role mix")
print(case_summary.head(20))

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 8. Analyze temporary substitution episodes temporally
# ------------------------------------------------------------------

df["week"] = df["time:timestamp"].dt.isocalendar().week.astype(int)

# Example: Fatima absent in weeks 3-4, Giorgio covers commercial work.
fatima_absence_weeks = [3, 4]

giorgio_commercial_substitution = df[
    df["week"].isin(fatima_absence_weeks)
    & (df["org:resource"] == "R07_Giorgio")
    & (df["concept:name"].isin([
        "A7 Pricing Exception Review",
        "A8 Customer Negotiation"
    ]))
].shape[0]

print("\nGiorgio commercial events during Fatima absence:",
      giorgio_commercial_substitution)

# Compare baseline commercial activity by Giorgio outside substitution weeks.
baseline_weeks = [w for w in df["week"].unique()
                  if w not in [3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10]]

giorgio_baseline_commercial = df[
    df["week"].isin(baseline_weeks)
    & (df["org:resource"] == "R07_Giorgio")
    & (df["concept:name"].isin([
        "A7 Pricing Exception Review",
        "A8 Customer Negotiation"
    ]))
].shape[0]

print("Giorgio commercial events in baseline weeks:",
      giorgio_baseline_commercial)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 9. Inspect service time and waiting time around substitutions
# ------------------------------------------------------------------

# PM4Py versions differ; use the available service-time helper.
service_time = pm4py.get_service_time(log)

# Add case-level service/waiting indicators if supported.
df = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df)

print("\nService time sample")
print(service_time)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 10. Filter critical escalations and inspect variants
# ------------------------------------------------------------------

critical_cases = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df,
    "escalation_level",
    ["Critical", "High"],
    level="case"
)

security_events = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    df,
    "concept:name",
    ["A6 Security Review"],
    level="event"
)

variants = pm4py.get_variants(df)

print("\nTop variants")
for variant, count in sorted(
    variants.items(),
    key=lambda x: len(x[1]) if isinstance(x[1], list) else x[1],
    reverse=True
)[:10]:
    print(variant)

# Optional case clustering: use only as a behavioral comparison,
# not as proof of formal authority.
clusters = pm4py.cluster_log(log, k=8)

print("\nCase clusters")
print(clusters)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 11. Avoid over-interpreting activity-frequency clusters
# ------------------------------------------------------------------

# Example: approval authority cannot be inferred from role clustering alone.
approval_events = df[
    df["concept:name"] == "A9 Supervisor Approval"
]

approval_share = (
    approval_events.groupby("org:resource")
    .size()
    .div(approval_events.shape[0])
    .sort_values(ascending=False)
)

print("\nA9 approval share by resource")
print(approval_share)

# Check whether Mateo's A9 events are followed by Hana rechecks.
a9_events = df[
    df["concept:name"] == "A9 Supervisor Approval"
```
