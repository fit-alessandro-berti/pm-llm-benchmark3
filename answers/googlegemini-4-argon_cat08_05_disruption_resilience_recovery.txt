# 1. Disruption timeline

* **T0 (Day -30 to Day -1): Baseline Period**
  * **Operational State:** Stable, high-throughput automated operations (42,000 cases). Median cycle times are tight: Hospital-critical (8h), Pharmacy-standard (23h), and Wholesale-bulk (36h).
  * **Dominant Process Variants:** Normative linear path (`Receive order` -> `Validate authorization` -> `Inventory reservation` -> `Pick` -> `Pack` -> `Quality/release check` -> `Dispatch` -> `Proof of delivery` -> `Invoice close`), with 91% fitting traces and 0.96 model fitness. Minor exception loops exist for compliance holds (700) and backorders (900).
  * **Risk Posture:** Low risk. Traceability exceptions (0.8%), missing cold-chain documentation (0.4%), and missing controlled-substance dual-control (0.2%) are minimal. Rework sits at 3.5% and cancellations at 2.1%.

* **T1 (Day 0 Morning): Incident Onset**
  * **Operational State:** Acute shock. Cyber incident disables the Warehouse Management System (WMS) and breaks the order-to-delivery workflow.
  * **Dominant Process Variants:** Automated flows halt; transition to emergency triage and ad-hoc manual coordination.
  * **Risk Posture:** Extreme operational and clinical risk (potential hospital stockouts) shifting rapidly toward compliance and inventory accuracy risk as blind workarounds begin.

* **T2 (Day 0 Afternoon to Day 2): Manual Workaround Phase**
  * **Operational State:** Degraded, bifurcated operations (5,600 cases). Hospital-critical median cycle time is fiercely protected at 10h (91% fill rate), while Pharmacy-standard degrades to 54h (61% fill rate) and Wholesale-bulk to 96h. Rework surges to 12.5% and cancellations hit 7.8%.
  * **Dominant Process Variants:** Model fitness drops to 0.71 (44% fitting traces). Dominant path shifts to `Emergency triage` (5,100) branching into `Manual authorization check` (2,500), `Skip system authorization check` (1,600), or `Hospital-critical fast lane` (1,000), converging on `Manual inventory snapshot check` -> `Manual pick list` -> `Manual pack` -> `Dispatch without scan` (3,250 total) -> `Phone proof of delivery` -> `Spreadsheet invoice hold`. Noncritical orders are routed to `Defer noncritical order` (1,080 total) and `Backlog queue` (960).
  * **Risk Posture:** High concealed noncompliance. Move-on-model deviations show widespread skipping of `Quality/release check` (1,800 bypassed `Temporary release control`) and complete loss of system dispatch scans. Traceability exceptions spike 12x to 9.5%, missing cold-chain docs hit 4.8%, and missing controlled-substance dual-control reaches 2.9%.

* **T3 (Day 3 to Day 6): Partial Recovery**
  * **Operational State:** Hybrid recovery (11,400 cases). Order intake returns, but inventory sync and scanning remain unreliable. Cycle times improve (Hospital-critical 9h, Pharmacy-standard 38h, Wholesale-bulk 84h), and rework drops to 9.0% (cancellations at 5.2%).
  * **Dominant Process Variants:** Model fitness rises to 0.82 (59% fitting traces) as front-end order intake normalizes, though warehouse execution still relies partially on manual pick/pack and unynchronized inventory workarounds.
  * **Risk Posture:** Elevated synchronization and compliance risk. While exception rates drop roughly in half from T2 (traceability at 5.4%, cold-chain at 2.3%, dual-control at 1.4%), unreliable inventory sync creates double-allocation and stock discrepancy risks.

* **T4 (Day 7 to Day 21): Stabilization**
  * **Operational State:** Superficial operational recovery with a heavy hidden remediation tail (35,000 cases). Hospital-critical returns to baseline (8h median), Pharmacy-standard nears baseline (28h median, though p90 lags at 74h vs. 49h baseline), and Wholesale-bulk remains elevated (58h median, 120h p90).
  * **Dominant Process Variants:** Normative flow largely resumes (0.90 fitness, 76% fitting traces), augmented by four heavy recovery loops: `Backlog prioritization` (1,500), `Reconcile manual reservation` (1,400), `Proof-of-delivery remediation` (1,200), and a post-close `Audit remediation` tail (2,300 cases after `Invoice close`).
  * **Risk Posture:** Latent regulatory and financial exposure. Operational metrics look healthy, but rework (5.8%) and compliance gaps (2.2% traceability, 0.9% cold-chain, 0.5% dual-control) remain more than double baseline levels, proving that post-hoc reconstruction (`Audit remediation`) cannot fully restore real-time physical controls.

# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

In crisis management, **true resilience** is the capacity to absorber shock, reallocate finite resources toward mission-critical outcomes, and degrade gracefully within defined safety and regulatory boundaries. **Concealed noncompliance**, by contrast, preserves superficial throughput (cases shipped, cycle times met) by silently shedding mandatory safety, security, and traceability controls—deferring the failure into an unmanageable compliance debt or an unmitigated patient safety hazard.

### True Resilience in this Case
* **Explicit Load Shedding and Triage (Patterns A, I, J):** Diverting 1,000 orders into the `Hospital-critical fast lane` while deferring 1,080 noncritical orders (`Defer noncritical order`) represents textbook graceful degradation. The organization consciously sacrificed Wholesale-bulk cycle times (36h -> 96h) to keep Hospital-critical supply lines near baseline (8h -> 10h, 91% fill rate).
* **Targeted High-Risk Control Preservation (Patterns B, F - Controlled):** Deploying pharmacists for `Temporary release control` on 100% of controlled substances and using compliance staff for `Manual authorization check` (2,500 cases) demonstrated risk-aware resource allocation.
* **Structured Post-Incident Reconciliation (Pattern K):** Routing 1,400 cases through `Reconcile manual reservation` in T4 acknowledged data corruption and systematically repaired state before normal picking resumed.

### Concealed Noncompliance (Risky Shortcuts)
* **Uncontrolled Control Bypass (Patterns C, G, and unscreened Pack->Dispatch):** Shipping 1,600 orders via `Skip system authorization check`, routing 1,800 orders directly from `Manual pack` to `Dispatch without scan` (bypassing `Temporary release control`), and handing off 3,250 total orders without dispatch scans preserved warehouse flow by sacrificing legal and safety guardrails. If a recall occurred on Day 4, the wholesaler could not know which hospital or pharmacy received specific lots (9.5% traceability exceptions).
* **Irreversible Physical Control Omissions (Cold-Chain & Dual-Control Gaps):** In T2, missing cold-chain documentation rose 12x (0.4% -> 4.8%) and missing controlled-substance dual-control rose 14.5x (0.2% -> 2.9%). Even in T4, after 2,300 `Audit remediation` cases, exceptions remained more than double baseline (2.2% traceability, 0.9% cold-chain, 0.5% dual-control). You cannot retroactively verify the departure temperature of a biologic vaccine or perform a physical two-person count on opioids two weeks after the truck has unloaded (`Invoice close` -> `Audit remediation`). Treating `Audit remediation` (Pattern L) as a substitute for real-time physical verification is concealed noncompliance.

### Ambiguous Adaptations (Depending on Risk Appetite and Execution Duration)
* **Manual Inventory Snapshot (Pattern D):** Resilient during the first 4 hours of Day 0 to maintain momentum; evolved into a hidden risk by Day 1–2 as unrecorded picks rendered the paper snapshot stale, driving rework to 12.5% and causing late cancellations in T4.
* **Manual Pick Lists & Phone POD (Patterns E, H):** Acceptable for standard **Ambient** products where late data entry (`Proof-of-delivery remediation`) merely delays invoicing (`Spreadsheet invoice hold`: 2,200). However, for **Controlled-substance**, **Cold-chain**, and **Recall-sensitive** items, paper picking without lot capture and verbal phone PODs breach regulatory chain-of-custody standards.

# 4. Recovery process model

### Notation Explanation
The redesigned recovery model uses a structured **Process-Tree / Guarded Workflow Notation**:
* `SEQ(A, B, ...)` : Sequential execution from left to right.
* `XOR( [Condition 1] -> Branch A, [Condition 2] -> Branch B )` : Exclusive choice based on explicit risk/system triggers.
* `AND( Branch A, Branch B )` : Parallel execution requiring both branches to complete.
* `LOOP( Body,Redo_Trigger )` : Structured rework/reconciliation loop.

---

### A. Explicit Trigger Conditions & Mode Switching
* **Trigger T-DOWN (Enter Continuity Mode):** WMS, Inventory Sync, or Barcode Scanning unavailable for **> 30 minutes**, OR conformance fitness drops below **0.85**.
  * *Immediate Action:* Freeze automated ATP promises; export/print timestamped Master Inventory Baseline & Pre-Approved Customer Standing Authorization List; initialize shared Offline Inventory Ledger.
* **Trigger T-PARTIAL (Enter Hybrid Sync Mode):** Order intake online, but warehouse scanning/inventory sync unreliable (error rate **> 2%**).
  * *Immediate Action:* Lock automated stock reservations until `Reconcile manual reservation` completes for active SKUs.
* **Trigger T-STABLE (Return to Normative Mode):** Core systems online and synced for **> 4 hours**, active physical reconciliation complete, and post-close remediation queue declining.

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

### C. Redesigned Recovery Process Flow

```text
SEQ(
  1. Receive Order,
  
  2. Emergency Triage & Load Shedding:
     XOR(
       [Tier 3: Wholesale-Bulk Ambient & Outage < 48h] -> 
         SEQ(Defer Noncritical Order, Enter Managed Backlog Queue),
       
       [Tier 1 or Tier 2 Active Fulfillment] -> 
         SEQ(
           3. Risk-Tiered Authorization:
              XOR(
                [Controlled OR Unlisted Buyer] -> Manual Compliance Staff Check (Phone/Offline Registry),
                [Hospital-Critical OR Known Buyer Ambient/Cold] -> Verify against Cached Standing Authorization List
              ),
           
           4. Dynamic Offline Inventory Reservation:
              LOOP(
                Check & Decrement Shared Offline Inventory Ledger,
                [Discrepancy / Zero Stock] -> Trigger Spot Cycle Count OR Route to Managed Backlog Queue
              ),
           
           5. Traceability-Preserving Manual Pick & Pack:
              SEQ(
                Generate Sequenced Paper Pick List (with Unique Offline Tracking ID),
                Physical Pick + Affix Peel-Off Lot/Expiry Sticker to Pick Sheet,
                Manual Pack + Attach Physical Manifest
              ),
           
           6. Mandatory Risk-Gated Release Check:
              XOR(
                [Controlled-Substance] -> Pharmacist 100% Dual-Control Sign-Off,
                [Cold-Chain] -> Pharmacist/QA 100% Temp-Logger Activation & Document Sign-Off,
                [Recall-Sensitive] -> QA Tech 100% Lot/Sticker Verification,
                [Ambient] -> QA Tech 10% Statistical Sample Check
              ),
           
           7. Controlled Dispatch (Zero Blind Handoffs):
              AND(
                Scan Package & Manifest into Offline Batch-Memory Scanner,
                Capture Courier Signature & Vehicle/Cooler ID on Paper Manifest
              ),
           
           8. Tiered Proof of Delivery & Hold:
              XOR(
                [Controlled OR Cold-Chain] -> Collect Signed Physical/Photo POD + Temp Readout,
                [Ambient / Recall-Sensitive] -> Collect Courier Electronic/Paper POD (Phone POD only as interim flag)
              ),
           
           9. Structured Staging for System Sync:
              Hold in Pre-Invoice Reconciliation Queue (Do NOT close invoice with missing controls)
         )
     ),

  10. Post-Disruption Synchronization & Backlog Recovery (Triggered at T-PARTIAL / T-STABLE):
      AND(
        Branch_A_Inventory_Sync:
          SEQ(
            Upload Offline Inventory Ledger & Batch-Scanner Logs to WMS,
            Reconcile Manual Reservations against Physical Stock (Spot-Count High-Variance SKUs),
            Unlock Automated WMS Inventory Reservation
          ),
        Branch_B_Backlog_Drain:
          LOOP(
            Prioritize Backlog Queue by Score = (Customer_CriticalityWeight * Order_Age_Hours),
            Release Batch to Normative WMS Validation & Picking
          ),
        Branch_C_PreClose_Audit_Gate:
          SEQ(
            Match Paper Manifests, Lot Stickers, Temp Logs, and PODs to WMS Order Records,
            XOR(
              [Evidence Complete] -> Execute Invoice Close -> END,
              [Missing Physical Control / Traceability Exception] -> 
                Route to Compliance Incident Investigation & Customer Quarantine Notice -> END
            )
          )
      )
)
```

# 7. Management trade-off statement

During a severe cyber disruption, management **must accept** slower cycle times and temporary load-shedding for Wholesale-bulk (up to 96h) and non-acute Pharmacy replenishment orders, as well as a short-term hold on invoicing (`Spreadsheet invoice hold`), in order to preserve a 90% fill rate and 12h cycle time for Hospital-critical supply lines and allocate scarce pharmacist capacity to high-risk release checks. Conversely, management **must never accept** blind throughput preservation—specifically shipping Controlled substances without dual-control verification, dispatching Cold-chain products without verified temperature documentation, skipping authorization for unvetted buyers, or handing off packages without lot/dispatch scan capture—because after-the-fact `Audit remediation` cannot retroactively verify physical safety or execute a life-saving product recall. **What evidence would change this recommendation:** We would only relax pre-dispatch physical documentation gates for Cold-chain or Controlled substances if an official public-health emergency declaration explicitly waives wholesale regulatory chain-of-custody requirements to prevent immediate mass casualties, OR if the company deploys automated IoT smart-packaging (e.g., cellular/RFID autonomous temp-and-location beacons inside totes) that captures continuous telemetry independently of the warehouse management system.
