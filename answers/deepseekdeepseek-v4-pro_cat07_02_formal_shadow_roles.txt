# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

## Automated Front-Line Processor (R14_Nora)
- **Core**: A1 Register Request, A2 Validate Entitlement—these constitute 100% of Nora's events. The activities are deterministic, rule-based, and operate without human judgment. Volume (1370 events total) indicates Nora is the primary entry point.
- **Peripheral**: None. Zero events in any other activity is the defining structural feature. Nora cannot diagnose, configure, negotiate, approve, or close.
- **Authority boundary**: Nora has *no organizational authority*. It performs precheck validation only—cases requiring judgment (medium/high complexity, all downstream activities) are automatically routed to human resources. The bot's "decisions" are fully auditable and reversible.
- **Substitution implications**: Nora cannot substitute for any human role. During high load, no failover to Nora for complex work exists. This is an automation frontier, not a flexible role.

## Front-Office Generalist (R01_Amira, R02_Ben)
- **Core**: A2 Validate (390/415), A4 Standard Response (310/335), A12 Close (260/280). These form the high-volume, low-complexity resolution path. Together with A1 Register, these four activities account for ~85% of their total events.
- **Peripheral**: A3 Diagnose (75/60), A5 Configure (30/18), A7 Pricing Exception (18/5), A8 Negotiate (42/25), A10 Implement (12/9). These constitute <15% of events but represent *capability reach* into technical and commercial domains.
- **Complexity gradient**: Amira's peripheral work skews toward medium/high cases (225 medium, 58 high), while Ben's stays in low/medium (205 medium, 31 high). This suggests Amira is the designated first-line escalations handler within the front-office team.
- **Authority**: Can close cases independently but cannot approve exceptions (zero A9 events). Their peripheral A7/A8 events likely occur on cases where pricing/commercial factors are straightforward enough not to require Fatima or Giorgio.
- **Why frequency  role**: The core activities are high-frequency but structurally simple. The peripheral activities are low-frequency but indicate cross-functional competence that distinguishes them from temps.

## Technical Analyst (R03_Chen, R04_Dina)
- **Core**: A3 Diagnose (260/255), A5 Configure (240/235), A10 Implement (215/220). These form a clear technical resolution triplet.
- **Peripheral**: A6 Security Review (Chen 24, Dina 6), A11 Quality Check (12/10), A14 KB Update (14/11). Chen's security-adjacent work is notable—24 events vs Dina's 6, indicating Chen functions as a *technical security screener* before Erik engagement.
- **Complexity-driven role differentiation**: Despite 0.94 cosine similarity, Chen handles critical cases (42 vs 9), more high cases (180 vs 82), and higher median complexity (0.71 vs 0.48). This is not random variation—it's systematic assignment reflecting seniority (5.1y vs 1.2y) and shift coverage (Chen on late shift handles escalated cases that accumulated during day).
- **Authority**: Technical analysts can independently implement solutions but cannot approve pricing exceptions or security sign-offs. Chen's security review events suggest advisory participation, not decision authority.
- **Substitution**: No evidence of formal substitution. Dina's profile mirrors Chen's but at lower complexity, suggesting Dina is being developed as Chen's backup/successor, not yet operating at full authority.

## Security Specialist (R05_Erik)
- **Core**: A6 Security Review (96). This single activity defines the role and constitutes ~60% of Erik's total events.
- **Peripheral**: A3 Diagnose (16), A5 Configure (10), A10 Implement (18), A11 Quality Check (8), A14 KB Update (9). These are *not* routine technical work—they represent Erik's involvement in end-to-end security-critical case resolution, where security review findings feed back into diagnosis and configuration.
- **Criticality vs frequency inversion**: Erik's median complexity is 0.86—the highest—and 55 of his cases are critical. Yet his total event count (~166) is among the lowest. This is the defining structural feature: Erik is a *rare but decisive* resource. Frequency-based clustering would undervalue him; complexity-weighted analysis reveals his centrality to the highest-stakes cases.
- **Authority**: Security sign-off authority. Cases cannot proceed to implementation without Erik's review when security review is triggered. The week 7-8 data (Mateo filtering low-risk referrals) shows Erik's time is actively protected from false positives.
- **Substitution risk**: No other resource has security certification. During weeks 7-8, Mateo's filtering reduced false escalations but two critical cases waited >2 days—indicating the filtering mechanism had imperfect sensitivity.

## Commercial Specialist (R06_Fatima)
- **Core**: A7 Pricing Exception Review (122), A8 Customer Negotiation (90). These form the commercial resolution path.
- **Peripheral**: A9 Supervisor Approval (16), A2 Validate (22), A12 Close (18). The 16 A9 events are significant—Fatima performs limited approvals, likely in pre-authorized pricing bands that don't require full Hana escalation.
- **Authority**: Pricing decision authority within defined parameters. The 16 A9 events suggest delegated approval for standard commercial exceptions. Full strategic pricing changes or escalations above threshold still go to Hana.
- **Substitution vulnerability**: Weeks 3-4 absence exposed a single point of failure. Giorgio covered 31 A7 and 18 A8 events, but median pricing exception duration rose from 38 to 74 minutes—a 95% increase. This indicates Giorgio has competence but not equivalent efficiency, likely due to needing secondary consultation or lacking Fatima's pre-built commercial relationships.

## Senior Front-Office / Commercial Hybrid (R07_Giorgio)
- **Core (dual)**: Front-office core—A2 Validate (190), A4 Standard Response (150), A12 Close (130). Commercial core—A7 Pricing Exception Review (72), A8 Customer Negotiation (88). Neither domain is clearly dominant; Giorgio operates in both.
- **Peripheral**: A9 Supervisor Approval (20)—more than any resource except Hana (138) and Mateo (44). This is the clearest indicator of Giorgio's elevated authority relative to standard front-office agents.
- **Hybrid authority**: Dual certification (entitlement + pricing) is unique among front-office resources. Giorgio's 72 A7 events are not occasional—they represent a structural commercial workload, not just Fatima's overflow. The 20 A9 events suggest Giorgio can approve certain commercial exceptions independently, likely within a delegated limit below Hana's threshold.
- **Substitution role**: During Fatima's absence, Giorgio's A7 events spiked. The 74-minute median duration (vs Fatima's 38) shows this is a *designated backup* arrangement, not seamless substitution. Management has accepted an efficiency penalty for continuity.

## Supervisor / Escalation Authority (R08_Hana)
- **Core**: A9 Supervisor Approval (138)—the single highest concentration of any activity-resource pair in the data. A13 Reopen (24)—highest count, indicating Hana is the final arbiter of reopened cases.
- **Peripheral**: A8 Negotiation (22), A11 Quality Check (12). These represent Hana's direct involvement in cases that escalated beyond specialist resolution.
- **Authority**: Definitive approval authority. No case requiring A9 can complete without Hana (or delegated authority during absence). The week-5 data is revealing: Mateo performed 21 preliminary A9 events, but 13 (62%) were rechecked by Hana, indicating the delegation was procedural (moving cases forward) rather than substantive (final decisions).
- **Critical case load**: 62 critical cases—highest of any resource. Hana sees the most complex and highest-risk cases in the organization.
- **Why clustering fails**: Hana's total event count (~245) is modest, and A9 is a single activity. Frequency clustering would produce a small "Approval" cluster or merge Hana with Mateo's coordination role. This conflates decision authority with coordination activity.

## Quality Assurance Analyst (R09_Ivan, R10_Julia)
- **Core**: A11 Quality Check (310/298), A14 Knowledge Base Update (96/104). These form a quality-gatekeeping and organizational learning function.
- **Peripheral**: A3 Diagnose (20/16), A5 Configure (26/22), A10 Implement (38/35). These represent QA involvement in cases requiring rework or deeper investigation.
- **Gatekeeping authority**: Quality checks occur before close on technical resolution paths. Ivan/Julia can block closure and trigger rework. Their KB update activity (combined 200 events) creates organizational memory, feeding insights back to technical and front-office teams.
- **Shift differentiation**: Ivan (late) and Julia (day) provide continuous QA coverage. This is operationally significant even though their activity profiles are nearly identical (cosine 0.97).

## Temporary Seasonal Support (R11_Kai, R12_Lina)
- **Core**: A1 Register (180/170), A2 Validate (270/250), A4 Standard Response (220/205), A12 Close (190/180). This mirrors the front-office core but with critical structural differences.
- **Peripheral**: A3 Diagnose (8/12), A8 Negotiate (3/4). These are negligible—temps are *not* developing cross-functional skills.
- **No complexity reach**: Kai median 0.18, Lina 0.19. Zero high cases, zero critical cases. Temps are explicitly restricted to low-complexity work.
- **No authority**: Zero certifications. Zero A5-A11, A13-A14 events. Temps cannot configure, review, approve, quality-check, or reopen.
- **Quality risk**: Weeks 8-10 data shows 17 reopened cases traced to incomplete Validate notes by temps. This is not individual error—it's a structural consequence of assigning untrained, uncertified temporary workers to an activity (Validate) that requires entitlement knowledge and downstream awareness.
- **Why frequency clustering is dangerous**: Temps look like front-office resources in activity counts. Treating them as equivalent for capacity planning or role assignment would push complex cases to unqualified resources, creating rework cascades.

## Incident Coordinator / Escalation Broker (R13_Mateo)
- **Core**: A13 Reopen (42—highest), A12 Close (52), A9 Supervisor Approval (44), A2 Validate (50), A4 Standard Response (45). Note the diversity: no single activity dominates.
- **Peripheral**: A1, A3, A5, A6, A7, A8, A10, A11, A14—Mateo touches every activity in the process. This is not role confusion; it's the structural signature of a *broker*.
- **Brokerage function**: Mateo does not specialize in executing any single activity. Instead, Mateo:
  - Triages reopened cases to appropriate specialists
  - Coordinates across technical, commercial, security, and quality functions
  - Provides preliminary approvals during supervisor absence
  - Filters escalations to protect specialist time (weeks 7-8 security filtering)
- **Critical case centrality**: 76 critical cases—second only to Hana (62). Mateo sees critical cases across all domains, acting as the organizational "glue" for complex, cross-functional incidents.
- **Authority**: Coordination authority, not decision authority. Mateo can route, prioritize, and preliminarily approve, but final decisions rest with specialists (Erik for security, Fatima for pricing, Hana for supervisory approval). The week-5 data (62% recheck rate by Hana) confirms this boundary.
- **Why clustering fails**: Mateo's profile diversity defeats frequency-based clustering. He might be assigned to a residual cluster or flagged as an outlier. But Mateo is arguably the most structurally important role for cross-functional case resolution—his value is in the *transitions* between activities, not in the activities themselves.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

## Shadow Role 1: R07_Giorgio — Front-Office / Commercial Bridging with Shadow Pricing Authority

**Nature of the shadow role**: Giorgio's formal title is "Senior Service Agent" in Front Office, but his activity profile reveals a substantial commercial workload: 72 A7 Pricing Exception Review events and 88 A8 Customer Negotiation events—volumes comparable to Fatima's core commercial work (122 A7, 90 A8). Giorgio is not formally in the Commercial team, yet performs commercial activities at a scale that cannot be explained as occasional overflow.

**Evidence for shadow role status**:
- Dual certification (entitlement + pricing) is unique among front-office resources—Amira and Ben have only entitlement certification, temps have none.
- 20 A9 Supervisor Approval events exceed any resource except Hana (138) and Mateo (44)—suggesting Giorgio holds delegated approval authority for certain commercial exceptions.
- During Fatima's 6-day absence (weeks 3-4), Giorgio seamlessly absorbed the commercial workload (31 A7, 18 A8), proving operational competence.
- Cosine similarity of 0.71 with Fatima is lower than the Chen-Dina (0.94) or Ivan-Julia (0.97) pairs, but reflects Giorgio's split identity rather than lack of commercial capability.

**Benefits of this shadow role**:
- **Resilience**: Provides a designated backup for the commercial function, preventing complete blockage during Fatima's absence. The accepted efficiency penalty (3874 minutes median duration) is a conscious trade-off for continuity.
- **Front-office/commercial integration**: Giorgio can handle cases that straddle entitlement and pricing issues without handoff delays between front-office and commercial teams. This reduces case duration for moderate-complexity commercial cases.
- **Career pathway**: The shadow role creates a development path from front-office to commercial specialist, visible in Giorgio's certification and tenure progression.

**Risks of this shadow role**:
- **Efficiency gap**: The 95% increase in pricing exception duration during substitution indicates Giorgio lacks Fatima's commercial specialization depth. Over-reliance on Giorgio as backup could create systemic delays during extended absences.
- **Authority ambiguity**: When Giorgio performs A7/A8 events, is he acting as a front-office agent with pricing knowledge, or as a delegated commercial authority? If a pricing decision is later challenged, the accountability line is unclear.
- **Workload fragmentation**: Giorgio's dual role may prevent deep specialization in either domain, creating a "jack of all trades, master of none" dynamic. The higher complexity cases Giorgio handles (high cases 120, critical 36) may push him beyond his optimal competence boundary.
- **Single point of hybrid failure**: If Giorgio leaves or is absent simultaneously with Fatima, the organization loses both commercial primary and backup, exposing a deeper resilience gap.

**Management recommendation**: Formalize Giorgio's commercial role as a defined "Commercial Liaison" or "Senior Service Agent – Commercial Specialization" with explicit pricing authority bands, rather than leaving it as an informal shadow arrangement. Train at least one other front-office agent (likely Amira, given her medium/high case reach) toward pricing certification to create a third-tier backup.

## Bridging Role 2: R13_Mateo — Cross-Functional Escalation Broker

**Nature of the bridging role**: Mateo's formal title is "Incident Coordinator" in the Coordination team, but "coordinator" understates the structural function. Mateo operates as a *broker*—a resource whose primary value is not executing activities but facilitating transitions between specialized resources. The evidence is in Mateo's unique profile: touches all 14 activities, highest reopen volume (42), second-highest critical case load (76), preliminary approval authority during supervisor absence, and active filtering of specialist escalations.

**Evidence for broker function**:
- Activity diversity: Mateo is the only resource touching every activity. No single activity exceeds 52 events (A12 Close). This is not role confusion—it's the structural signature of someone who *routes* cases rather than *resolves* them independently.
- Reopen centrality: 42 A13 Reopen events—highest of any resource. Reopened cases are by definition cross-functional failures requiring coordination. Mateo is the organizational "reset button."
- Preliminary approval during absence: 21 A9 events during Hana's week-5 absence, with 13 rechecked. Mateo kept cases moving procedurally while preserving Hana's decision authority.
- Security escalation filtering (weeks 7-8): Mateo actively reduced Erik's false-positive security referrals, improving specialist utilization. This required sufficient technical/security knowledge to triage without being a security specialist.
- Critical case involvement: 76 critical cases across all domains. Mateo sees the organization's most complex, cross-functional incidents and routes them to appropriate specialists.

**Benefits of this bridging role**:
- **Specialist protection**: Filtering low-risk referrals preserves Erik's time for genuine security-critical cases. The weeks 7-8 outcome (decreased false escalation complaints) validates this function.
- **Cross-functional continuity**: Mateo prevents cases from stalling at handoff boundaries between technical, commercial, security, and quality functions.
- **Absorption of organizational complexity**: Reopened cases (highest volume) are inherently messy—they involve prior failures, multiple specialties, and often customer dissatisfaction. Mateo absorbs this complexity so specialists can focus on domain-specific resolution.
- **Supervisory surge capacity**: Preliminary approvals during Hana's absence maintain case velocity without compromising decision quality (62% recheck rate indicates appropriate conservatism).

**Risks of this bridging role**:
- **Bottleneck potential**: Mateo is a single resource with the highest diversity and second-highest critical case load. If Mateo is overloaded, cross-functional coordination degrades across all domains simultaneously.
- **Filtering errors**: The weeks 7-8 security filtering had false negatives—two critical cases waited >2 days. Mateo's triage sensitivity is imperfect. Over-reliance on broker filtering creates hidden risk accumulation.
- **Knowledge breadth vs depth**: Mateo knows enough about all domains to coordinate, but may not recognize subtle indicators that a domain specialist would catch. This is inherent to brokerage—the broker's knowledge is wide but shallower than each specialist's.
- **Authority creep**: The preliminary approval pattern could drift toward de facto decision-making if Hana's recheck rate declines over time, creating ungoverned approval authority at the broker level.

**Management recommendation**: Define explicit broker protocols with escalation thresholds, required recheck rates, and maximum filtering sensitivity targets. Consider training a deputy broker (potentially Amira, given her cross-functional peripheral work) to prevent single-point broker failure. Monitor Mateo's false-negative rate in security and other filtering decisions as a key risk indicator.

## Rare Specialist Role 3: R05_Erik — Critical Security Specialist with Inverse Frequency-Criticality Profile

**Nature of the specialist role**: Erik is not a shadow or bridging role in the conventional sense—he is a *rare but decisive* specialist whose organizational value is inversely related to his event frequency. The data shows 96 A6 Security Review events—Erik's defining activity—but only ~166 total events across 10 weeks. Yet Erik handles the highest median complexity (0.86) and 55 of 76 total critical cases in the dataset. This profile violates the assumption (implicit in frequency-based clustering) that activity volume correlates with organizational importance.

**Evidence for rare-specialist status**:
- Highest median complexity score (0.86) with lowest total event count among permanent staff (excluding Nora the bot).
- 55 critical cases—Erik sees nearly three-quarters of all critical cases despite touching only a fraction of total case volume.
- Exclusive security certification—no other resource can perform A6 as a core activity (Chen's 24 events are peripheral/technical, not authoritative reviews).
- Zero front-office activities (A1, A2, A4, A12)—Erik is structurally disconnected from routine case flow, engaged only when security gates are triggered.
- Zero commercial activities (A7, A8)—security and commercial domains are cleanly separated.
- Week 7-8 filtering arrangement: The organization actively protects Erik's time by routing potential security cases through Mateo for pre-screening, acknowledging that Erik's attention is a scarce resource.

**Benefits of the rare-specialist model**:
- **Deep expertise concentration**: Erik's low volume enables depth—each security review receives focused attention from a 6.8-year specialist with dedicated security certification.
- **Risk containment**: Concentrating security decisions in one highly qualified resource creates clear accountability and reduces variability in security assessments.
- **Organizational learning**: Erik's peripheral activities (A14 KB Update—9 events, A11 Quality Check—8) feed security insights back into organizational knowledge and quality processes.

**Risks of the rare-specialist model**:
- **Single point of critical failure**: If Erik is unavailable (illness, departure, overwhelming surge), zero resources can authoritatively complete security reviews. Cases requiring A6 would stall indefinitely or proceed without security sign-off.
- **Filtering dependency**: The Mateo pre-screening arrangement means Erik only sees filtered cases. If filtering sensitivity degrades (as weeks 7-8 showed two false negatives), critical security cases may not reach Erik at all.
- **Knowledge isolation**: Erik's low interaction volume with other teams (zero front-office events, few commercial touchpoints) may limit organizational understanding of security requirements among non-specialists.
- **Succession vacuum**: No other resource shows progression toward security competence (Chen's 24 A6 events are not authoritative). If Erik leaves, security capability leaves with him.
- **Utilization measurement trap**: Standard productivity metrics (events/hour, cases closed) would show Erik as underutilized, creating pressure to assign non-security work that dilutes specialization or to reduce headcount in a role that appears "low volume."

**Management recommendation**: Actively develop a security deputy—Chen is the natural candidate given 24 security-adjacent events and technical depth. Formalize security review delegation for low/medium-complexity cases to the deputy, reserving Erik for high/critical cases. This reduces single-point failure risk while preserving Erik's deep specialization for the cases that need it. Measure Erik's performance by critical case outcomes and false-negative rates in the filtering process, not by event volume.

## Additional Structural Role: Automation Boundary (R14_Nora)

**Nature of the boundary**: Nora is not just an automated resource—it represents an *automation frontier* in the process. Nora handles 100% of its events (A1, A2) without human intervention, but cannot cross into any activity requiring judgment (A3 onward). This creates a hard structural boundary: cases are either fully automatable (and handled by Nora) or require human intervention (and are routed to human resources). The boundary is defined by case complexity and the nature of the activity, not by resource availability.

**Evidence**:
- 1370 total events, exclusively A1 and A2
- Zero events in any judgment-requiring activity
- Low-complexity cases only (690 low, 85 medium, 0 high, 0 critical)
- Median complexity 0.11—lowest in organization
- 24/7 operation—no human shift constraints
- Zero certifications listed—consistent with bot status

**Implications for role structure**: Nora should not be grouped with any human role. The automation boundary means that front-office resources (R01, R02, R11, R12) and Nora *complement* rather than *substitute* for each other. Nora handles the simplest, most repetitive cases; human front-office handles cases requiring judgment, even if the activities are nominally the same (A1, A2 performed by humans for medium-complexity cases).

## Additional Structural Role: Temporary Workforce with Explicit Competence Boundary

**Nature of the boundary**: R11_Kai and R12_Lina form a distinct structural group defined not by what they *do* but by what they *cannot do*. The critical distinction from permanent front-office (R01, R02) is:
- No certifications
- Zero high or critical cases
- Zero events in any activity beyond A4 (Standard Response)
- Temporary contracts (0.4y/0.3y tenure)

**Evidence for explicit boundary**: The weeks 8-10 data—17 reopened cases traced to incomplete Validate notes—demonstrates the consequences of the boundary. Temps performed A2 Validate without sufficient training or downstream awareness, creating rework. This is structural, not individual: the temporary workforce model accepts a quality penalty in exchange for seasonal capacity.

def enrich_roles_with_complexity(formal_roles, combined_profile, handover_network, subcontracting):
    """
    Augment frequency-based role clusters with complexity data,
    substitution indicators, and network centrality measures.
    This reveals where frequency clusters obscure structural differences.
    """
    enriched_roles = {}
    
    for role_id, members in formal_roles.items():
        member_data = combined_profile.loc[members] if all(m in combined_profile.index for m in members) else None
        
        if member_data is not None:
            # Check complexity heterogeneity within cluster
            complexity_range = member_data['median_complexity'].max() - member_data['median_complexity'].min()
            complexity_cv = member_data['median_complexity'].std() / member_data['median_complexity'].mean() if member_data['median_complexity'].mean() > 0 else 0
            
            # Check critical case heterogeneity
            has_critical_specialist = any(member_data['critical_cases'] > 30)
            has_no_critical = any(member_data['critical_cases'] < 5)
            
            # Check activity diversity (number of distinct activities per resource)
            activity_cols = [c for c in member_data.columns if c.startswith('A')]
            activity_diversity = (member_data[activity_cols] > 0).sum(axis=1)
            
            enriched_roles[role_id] = {
                'members': members,
                'complexity_range': complexity_range,
                'complexity_cv': complexity_cv,
                'heterogeneity_warning': complexity_cv > 0.3,  # Flag internally diverse clusters
                'has_critical_specialist': has_critical_specialist,
                'has_no_critical_resource': has_no_critical,
                'activity_diversity_range': (activity_diversity.min(), activity_diversity.max()),
                'potential_misclassification': (
                    complexity_cv > 0.3 or  # High complexity variance = different roles merged
                    (has_critical_specialist and has_no_critical) or  # Critical specialist merged with routine
                    activity_diversity.max() - activity_diversity.min() > 5  # Very different activity breadth
                )
            }
    
    return enriched_roles

# 6. Operational interpretation

## How Management Should Use the Discovered Role Structure

### 1. Staffing and Capacity Planning

**Right-sizing by role, not by activity volume**: The refined role structure reveals that staffing adequacy cannot be assessed by simple event counts. Erik's ~16 events/week may appear "underutilized" but his role is defined by *coverage of critical security cases*, not throughput. Staffing the security function requires ensuring 100% availability for critical cases, not maximizing Erik's event count. Conversely, the temporary workforce (R11/R12) handles high event volumes but cannot absorb any medium/high-complexity work—adding more temps will not increase capacity for complex cases.

**Single-point-of-failure identification**: The analysis reveals three critical single points of failure:
- **R05_Erik**: No other resource holds security certification. If Erik is unavailable, security reviews cannot be authoritatively completed. Mitigation: Begin cross-training Chen (already at 24 security-adjacent events) toward formal security certification.
- **R06_Fatima**: Sole commercial specialist. Giorgio provides backup but at 95% efficiency penalty and likely with narrower authority. Mitigation: Formalize Giorgio's commercial role, train Amira toward pricing certification as third-tier backup.
- **R13_Mateo**: Sole cross-functional broker. If Mateo is unavailable, the "glue" between specialized functions dissolves—reopened cases have no coordinator, specialist filtering stops, preliminary approvals during supervisor absence have no owner. Mitigation: Identify a deputy broker (Amira shows appropriate cross-functional reach) and document broker protocols explicitly.

**Temporary workforce boundaries**: The weeks 8-10 quality failures (17 reopened cases from incomplete Validate notes) demonstrate that temporary workers should not perform A2 Validate Entitlement without additional training or quality gates. Management options:
- Restrict temps to A1 Register and A4 Standard Response only
- Add mandatory quality check on all temp-performed A2 events (cost: increased QA load)
- Invest in pre-assignment certification-light training for temps on entitlement basics

### 2. Training and Development

**Formalize development pathways revealed by shadow roles**: Giorgio's hybrid profile and Amira's peripheral technical/commercial work are emergent development pathways. Management should formalize them:
- Create a "Senior Service Agent – Commercial" track with explicit pricing certification milestones, delegated authority bands, and a timeline to independent commercial case handling
- Create a "Technical Development" track for front-office agents (Amira currently, Ben potentially) with structured progression through A3 Diagnose  A5 Configure  A10 Implement
- Formalize Dina's relationship to Chen: is Dina in a structured mentorship with complexity progression targets, or in a permanently differentiated junior role? The answer determines training investment.

**Cross-training for resilience, not just efficiency**: Giorgio's 95% efficiency penalty during Fatima's absence shows that cross-training provides *continuity* but not *equivalence*. Management should set realistic expectations: the goal of cross-training is preventing case blockage during absences, not achieving identical performance. Document acceptable efficiency penalties for each backup arrangement.

### 3. Risk Control

**Monitor broker filtering sensitivity**: Mateo's security filtering during weeks 7-8 reduced false escalations but produced two false negatives (critical cases waited >2 days). This is an inherent trade-off in broker-mediated filtering. Management should:
- Track false-negative rate (critical cases that should have reached specialist but didn't) as a key risk indicator
- Establish maximum acceptable false-negative thresholds (e.g., zero critical cases waiting >1 day)
- Periodically audit filtered-out cases to verify triage accuracy
- Ensure filtering criteria are explicit and reviewed, not purely tacit in Mateo's judgment

**Formalize shadow role authority boundaries**: Giorgio's commercial decisions and Mateo's preliminary approvals operate in authority gray zones. Management should:
- Define explicit pricing authority bands for Giorgio (e.g., "up to $X discount without Fatima/Hana review")
- Codify Mateo's preliminary approval scope and mandatory recheck criteria
- Ensure audit trails distinguish Giorgio-as-front-office from Giorgio-as-commercial-delegate and Mateo-as-coordinator from Mateo-as-preliminary-approver

**Recognize that rare specialists create hidden risk concentration**: Erik's inverse frequency-criticality profile means his absence risk is invisible to standard utilization metrics. Management should track "critical case coverage availability" as a distinct metric from "resource utilization." A resource at 20% utilization who is the only person capable of handling 72% of critical cases is not "underutilized"—they are *indispensable and dangerously concentrated*.

### 4. Resilience

**Accept substitution efficiency penalties as a conscious trade-off**: The 3874 minute pricing exception duration increase during Fatima's absence is not a failure—it's a working resilience mechanism at a known cost. Management should document acceptable degradation levels for each critical role's backup arrangement and monitor whether degradation exceeds thresholds (indicating backup needs additional training) or stays within bounds (indicating acceptable resilience).

**Test resilience through planned absences, not just emergencies**: The week-5 Hana absence was planned (audit workshop) and the Mateo substitution was pre-arranged with a defined recheck process (62% recheck rate). This is good practice. The week 3-4 Fatima absence appears unplanned (6 working days, Giorgio stepped in). Management should periodically run planned resilience tests for all critical single-point resources to validate and refine backup procedures.

**Document broker protocols for Mateo's absence**: Mateo's role is the hardest to back up because it's defined by cross-functional knowledge and relationships, not by a specific activity. Management should document:
- Which resources handle reopened cases by domain in Mateo's absence (likely: Hana for escalated, Amira/Giorgio for front-office/commercial, Chen for technical)
- Who performs specialist filtering (likely requires temporary elevation of a senior resource)
- Whether preliminary approvals are suspended entirely during Mateo+Hana simultaneous absence

### 5. Two Critical Cautions About Changing Staffing Based Only on Mined Roles

**Caution 1: Do not "balance" utilization by redistributing rare-specialist work to generalists**

A naïve reading of the activity-frequency data would show Erik as dramatically underutilized (~16 events/week) and Chen/Dina as highly utilized. The "efficiency" response would be to train Chen to handle more security reviews, reducing Erik's role. This would be dangerous because:
- Erik's low volume enables depth. Each of his 96 security reviews receives focused attention from a 6.8-year security specialist. Increasing volume would dilute attention per review.
- Security errors are low-frequency, high-consequence events. The cost of one missed security vulnerability likely exceeds the "efficiency gain" of higher Erik utilization.
- Chen's 24 security-adjacent events are *peripheral and advisory*, not authoritative. Promoting Chen to independent security review without equivalent depth creates risk of false negatives.
- The Mateo filtering arrangement already optimizes Erik's utilization by reducing false referrals. Further "balancing" would undermine this triage system.

The role-mining output shows *what activities resources perform*, not *what consequences their errors have*. Erik's low frequency reflects high consequence, not low importance.

**Caution 2: Do not eliminate or merge shadow roles based on formal cluster cleanliness**

A role-mining purist might recommend:
- Moving Giorgio's commercial work entirely to Fatima and returning Giorgio to pure front-office (simplifies clusters)
- Eliminating Mateo's diverse profile by routing all escalations directly to specialists (reduces "outlier" complexity)
- Having Hana handle all A9 events with no preliminary approvals (clarifies authority)

This would destroy the resilience mechanisms that the "messy" structure provides:
- Giorgio's shadow role is the *only* backup for commercial cases. Eliminating it creates a hard single point of failure without reducing Fatima's workload.
- Mateo's broker role *protects* specialists from false escalations and prevents cross-functional cases from stalling. Direct specialist routing would overload Erik with false positives and leave multi-domain cases without an owner.
- Hana's preliminary approval delegation to Mateo and Giorgio prevents approval bottlenecks. Concentrating all A9 events on Hana would create delays for cases that could be provisionally advanced.

The "messiness" of shadow roles, brokers, and preliminary delegation is not process failure—it's organizational adaptation to complexity, specialization, and absence. Mining tools that reward clean formal clusters will systematically undervalue these adaptations. Management should use role mining to *understand* the structure, including its informal elements, not to *simplify* it into clean boxes that break resilience.
