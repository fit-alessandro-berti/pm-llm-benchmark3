Interference arises because manual and senior queues are shared: one flagged case’s queue time depends on other cases’ routing. Exposure-induced confounding can occur because flag status and mediators such as manual review are both influenced by unmeasured urgency and complexity. Treatment-confounder feedback can occur if reviewers see the flag, escalate differently, and thereby change later queue states that affect subsequent cases.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| Does flag affect cycle time near a risk-score threshold? | Regression discontinuity/local threshold on risk_score if flag rule has threshold | risk_score polynomial, complexity, arrival_load, time-of-day | Score not manipulated; continuity at threshold | McCrary density test; covariate smoothness at cutoff | Discontinuity in covariates or no threshold jump weakens interpretation |
| Is manual queue wait a bottleneck? | Queue-state-aware survival/Cox model of manual_starttriage_done | current manual queue length, reviewer load, arrival_load, complexity, flag | Queue state measured before wait | Check queue length predicts wait after adjustment | If queue length explains flag wait entirely, flag is not the main driver |
| What happens under policy change? | Randomized encouragement to P2/P3 by day/team/queue | team, shift, arrival_load, complexity, risk_score | Encouragement affects policy but not outcomes except through it | Balance and compliance checks | Noncompliance or spillover changes effect interpretation |
| Does manual review mediate cycle time? | Interventional mediation with manual_review occurrence and wait as sequential mediators | baseline risk, complexity, arrival_load, queue state | No unmeasured mediator-outcome confounding; positivity | Negative-control mediator; E-value sensitivity | Strong unmeasured confounding could reverse mediation |
| Are effects specific to flag? | Negative-control outcome: unrelated administrative field or future case outcome | same adjustment set | Negative control should be unaffected by flag | Test flag association with negative control | Association suggests confounding or measurement artifact |
| How sensitive to unobserved risk? | Sensitivity analysis adding U_underlying_risk with plausible strength | observed risk_score, complexity, outcome | Sensitivity model correctly specifies confounding | E-value or bias-factor curve | If modest unobserved risk explains effect, flag mechanism is weakened |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 current | Fast triage for flagged | High manual and escalation queue load | Long for flagged | Review/escalation may protect quality | Shared queues already congested | Observational only | Baseline |
| P1 suppress flag display | Removes priority triage; slower flagged triage | Fewer automatic reviews/escalations; shorter queues | Unclear; may shorten for some but miss risk | High: hidden high-risk cases | Reduces manual/senior load | Weak; depends on display semantics | Not for high-risk cases |
| P2 retain priority, remove automatic review | Fast triage retained | Less manual queue, fewer escalations | Likely shorter for flagged | Moderate-high: missed risk if review protective | Frees manual capacity | Plausible but untested | Pilot only with audit |
| P3 protected manual-review capacity for flagged | Fast triage retained | Shortens flagged manual wait; may spill to unflagged | Likely shorter for flagged; system effect depends on guardrail | Lower if review retained | Possible unflagged delay | Best targeted pilot | Preferred limited pilot |
| P4 change escalation criteria/capacity | No triage change | Alters senior queue and escalation rate | Mixed; helps if escalation is bottleneck | Depends on criteria | Senior capacity is scarce | Weak unless criteria precise | Test after queue diagnosis |

For a limited pilot, choose P3: retain current routing but add protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases. Eligibility: all newly arriving cases eligible for the flag, stratified by arrival_load and complexity, with no exclusions after flag assignment. Assignment: randomize by team-shift or sub-queue, not by individual case, to limit contamination; compare P3 to P0 within matched shifts. Queue-capacity guardrail: cap protected capacity at a prespecified fraction of total manual-review capacity and stop if unflagged manual queue wait increases beyond a pre-set threshold. Primary outcomes: manual queue wait for reviewed cases and total cycle time. Secondary outcomes: escalation rate, unflagged cycle time, override rate, audit-based decision appropriateness. Minimum process data: all timestamps, flag, risk_score, complexity, arrival_load, resources, queue length at manual/escalation entry. Stopping conditions: guardrail breach, evidence of harm to unflagged cases, or audit reversal rate increases materially.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. Hidden risk: if unflagged cases with low recorded `risk_score` have unmeasured high risk that drives manual review/escalation, then the apparent flag-related delay is partly severity confounding.
2. Queue interference: if protected manual capacity for flagged cases shifts delay to unflagged cases or increases total system cycle time, P3 is worse than P0 despite improving flagged cases.
3. Decision quality: if reducing manual review or accelerating triage increases missed high-risk approvals/rejections, then shorter flagged cycle time is not a better policy.
4. Timestamp semantics: if timestamps are batch-recorded, rounded to shift boundaries, or affected by local clock changes, then stage waits and services are mismeasured and the downstream-delay mechanism may reverse.
5. Positivity or lack of overlap: if there are too few flagged normal-load, non-escalated, or low-complexity cases, mediation contrasts are not estimable and observed effects may be driven by a few strata.
6. Behavior change by reviewers: if seeing the flag changes review depth or escalation thresholds, then suppressing the flag display is not equivalent to removing the underlying risk information or the organizational response.

## 9. Final synthesis

Directly observed: flagged cases have much shorter arrival-to-triage-start, 14.4 vs 148.8 minutes, and slightly shorter triage service, 36 vs 57 minutes. Yet they have much longer total cycle time, 990.6 vs 490.2 minutes. Almost all flagged cases enter manual review, 10/10 vs 4/10, and reviewed flagged cases wait far longer before manual review, 390 vs 112.5 minutes. Flagged cases also escalate more often, 6/10 vs 2/10, and escalation queues are long.

A plausible mechanism is that priority triage accelerates the front end, while automatic manual review and escalation route flagged cases into shared downstream queues. The flag may act as a policy bundle: it changes lane, review probability, escalation probability, reviewer behavior, and queue priority. Complexity, risk score, and arrival load confound these pathways, and shared queues create interference because one case’s treatment affects others’ waits.

What remains unidentified: natural direct and indirect effects, the causal effect of the flag independent of underlying risk, the effect of manual review versus mere selection into review, and decision quality without an audit gold standard. The observed data cannot separate “review causes delay” from “higher-risk cases are reviewed and take longer.”

The most useful next evidence comes from a P3 pilot: retain current routing but add protected, queue-aware manual-review capacity for flagged cases, randomized by team-shift or sub-queue with guardrails for unflagged wait times. This would test whether manual-review waiting is a causal bottleneck while monitoring quality and spillover.
