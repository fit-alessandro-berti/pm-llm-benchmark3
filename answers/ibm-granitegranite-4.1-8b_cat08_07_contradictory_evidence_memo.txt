# 1. Executive memo

**To:** Executive Steering Committee  
**From:** ProcessMining & Strategy Team  
**Subject:** Recommendation on the rollout of Redesign R for fiberinstallation  

**Situation:** The pilot of Redesign R (last 6 weeks, 9200 cases) cut median cycle time from 14.2days to 10.8days and reduced handovers dramatically, but it also produced troubling sideeffects: tail durations p90 and p95 grew, firsttimeright fell, complaints after billing rose, and manual activations and second visits increased—especially in apartments and olderbuilding segments. The process model shows high fitness (0.94) but low precision (0.51), indicating many adhoc variations that the model cannot cleanly distinguish.

**Key Tradeoffs:**  
* **Speed & cost savings** – evident for simple, lowrisk homes (6days, cheaper visible cost).  
* **Risk & quality** – severe deterioration for mediumrisk apartments and permitsensitive old buildings, reflected in higher rework, complaints, and longer tail times.  
* **Resource pressure** – coordinators run at 93% utilization, increasing risk of bottlenecks when unavailable.  

**Recommendation:** **Adopt a segmented, staged rollout (AlternativeE)** – implement Redesign R **only for simple, lowrisk houses** while **suspends the predictive skipsurvey rule and keeps activation checks before billing** for all other segments. Conduct a controlled regional experiment with enhanced measurement (M1–M6) to validate the buildingrisk rule and monitor downstream costs before any broader expansion.

**Rationale:**  
* Preserves the clear speedandcost benefits where they are strongest (simple houses, 34% of cases).  
* Protects highrisk segments from the adverse effects observed in apartments and old buildings.  
* Provides decisionrelevant data on the predictive rule’s accuracy, downstream complaint costs, and coordination resilience, reducing uncertainty before a national scaleup.  

**Immediate actions:**  
1. **Scale** Redesign R for simple lowrisk homes across all regions (coordinator ownership retained, survey skipping disabled).  
2. **Pause** the skipsurvey and postbilling activation changes for all other segments.  
3. **Launch** a controlled experiment in two additional regions (including mediumrisk apartments) with the following measurements: M1 (randomized surveyskip by risk strata), M2 (instrument start timestamps), M3 (structured reason codes), M4 (full downstream cost for 60days), and M6 (validate buildingrisk rule).  

**Future criteria for broader rollout:**  
* Median tail improvement (p90, p95) reaches parity with baseline in highrisk segments.  
* Firsttimeright 80% across all segments.  
* Downstream complaint cost baseline (M4).  
* Coordinator utilization remains 85% under normal operating conditions (M5).  

Implementing this cautious, datadriven approach balances the undeniable productivity gains against the emerging quality and operational risks, ensuring that any national rollout is evidencebacked and segmentaware.

# 3. Explanation of metric conflicts  

| Conflict | Explanation (linked to evidence) |
|----------|-----------------------------------|
| **High fitness but low precision** | Fitness measures how well the observed event log fits the discovered model; the redesign created many adhoc variations (e.g., frequent moveonlogs for billing before activation, varied handovers) that the model captures broadly  high fitness. However, the model’s inability to distinguish these variations results in low precision (many model paths not aligned with actual practice). |
| **Improved median but worse tail duration** | Median reflects the bulk of “simple” cases where the redesign’s streamlined coordinator path and merged tasks accelerate flow. The tail (p90/p95) includes “exceptional” cases—especially apartments and oldbuilding segments where the skipsurvey rule and shifted activation timing cause longer, more variable cycles, pulling the tail up. |
| **Fewer handovers but lower resilience** | Fewer handovers reduce coordination overhead and improve accountability, shown by the 34%12% reduction. Yet because all cases now funnel into a single coordinator, any absence or overload creates a single point of failure, reducing systemic resilience despite the apparent efficiency gain. |
| **Lower visible cost but higher rework or downstream cost** | Visible cost (ordertocompletion) dropped (EUR184161) due to reduced handover overhead and merged tasks. However, increased manual activations, second visits, and postbilling complaints raise hidden costs (rework, downstream handling), manifesting as the higher estimated total cost (EUR207) and rising complaint rates. |
| **Improvement for simple houses but deterioration for apartments/old buildings** | The redesign’s simplifications (merged tasks, skipsurvey) align well with lowrisk, simple houses, delivering speed gains (6days, +3pp firsttimeright). In contrast, mediumrisk apartments and permitsensitive old buildings require more nuanced handling; the predictive rule misclassifies them, leading to skipped surveys, delayed activations, and higher rework (8pp firsttimeright, +5pp complaints). |

# 4. Decision alternatives  

| Alternative | Advantages | Risks / Downsides | Conditions Favoring Use |
|-------------|-------------|--------------------|------------------------|
| **A – Scale nationally** | Full speed & cost benefits realized quickly; eliminates existing handover inefficiencies across all regions. | Severe quality degradation in highrisk segments; high coordinator utilization may cause bottlenecks; lowprecision model may misguide future process tweaks. | Not advisable given segmentspecific failures and low model precision. |
| **B – Roll back to baseline** | Restores known stable process; eliminates new sources of rework and complaints; avoids singlepoint failures of coordinator overload. | Loses the mediantime and cost savings achieved in simple houses; may miss opportunities for efficiency gains; organizational inertia around new coordinator ownership. | Could be justified only if pilot data conclusively showed no feasible way to mitigate highrisk segment issues. |
| **C – Scale only coordinator ownership, suspend survey skipping & postbilling activation** | Keeps the efficiency of reduced handovers while removing the two most problematic rule changes; retains benefits for simple houses. | May still leave some tailduration issues if other latent variations persist; requires retraining staff to manage potentially higher coordinator load. | Reasonable if the predictive skipsurvey rule is the primary driver of segment degradation and can be replaced with a more cautious gating mechanism. |
| **D – Run a controlled regional experiment (M1–M6)** | Provides highresolution data on segmentspecific impacts, validates buildingrisk rule, measures downstream costs, tests coordinator resilience. | Delays full productivity gains; experiment adds operational complexity; results may take several weeks to months. | Ideal when uncertainty about segmentlevel effects and downstream cost dominates decision confidence. |
| **E – Hybrid rollout (E for simple lowrisk houses, controls for others, plus measurement)** | Directly captures speed/cost gains where safest; protects highrisk segments from adverse redesign effects; measurement phase supplies evidence for future national scaleup. | Requires careful segmentation governance; coordination overhead to manage two parallel process tracks; potential shortterm confusion for field teams. | Best fit given mixedsegment outcomes and need for a phased, evidencedriven expansion. |

# 6. Final recommendation  

**Recommendation:** **Adopt AlternativeE – a hybrid rollout with targeted measurement.**  

- **Immediate actions:**  
  1. **Scale RedesignR for simple, lowrisk homes** (34% of orders) across all regions, preserving the coordinatorownership model and disabling the predictive skipsurvey and postbilling activation changes for all other cases.  
  2. **Pause** the skipsurvey and delayed activation adjustments pending the results of the controlled experiment.  

- **Measurement phase (next 8–12weeks):**  
  - Conduct **M1** (randomized skipsurvey by risk strata) and **M6** (full validation of the buildingrisk rule) in two additional regions that include mediumrisk apartments and oldbuilding sites.  
  - Deploy **M2** (starttimestamp instrumentation) and **M3** (structured reason codes) to capture detailed temporal and causal data.  
  - Initiate **M4** (full 60day downstream cost) and **M5** (coordinatorabsence resilience test) to assess hidden costs and system robustness.  
  - Run **M7** and **M8** concurrently to monitor conformance and separate speed vs. quality customer experience.  

- **Scale criteria for broader rollout (postexperiment):**  
  1. **Tailduration parity:** pilot p90 and p95 match baseline values for all risk segments.  
  2. **Firsttimeright 80%** across every segment.  
  3. **Downstream complaint cost  baseline** (M4).  
  4. **Coordinator utilization 85%** under normal operations (M5).  

- **If these thresholds are met,** proceed to a phased national expansion of the redesign, retaining the segmented guardrails. **If not,** consider reverting to the baseline for highrisk segments or further refining the predictive rule before any wider rollout.

This approach leverages the clear productivity gains in safe environments while safeguarding quality and operational resilience in higherrisk contexts, and it builds a robust evidence base to inform any future national deployment.
