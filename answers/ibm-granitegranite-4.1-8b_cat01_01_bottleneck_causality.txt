## 1. Executive causal assessment
**Provisional verdict:** *partly causal through mechanisms*  
**Confidence level:** 65  
**Strongest evidence for:**  
- Within each complexity stratum, Orion’s mean cycle time exceeds Nova’s (e.g., High complexity: 31.6h vs. 35h).  
- Orion handles night work and most complex cases, consistent with the routing rule that assigns highcomplexity cases to Orion.  

**Strongest evidence against:**  
- Crude overall comparison shows higher mean for Orion (21.96h vs. 15.24h), suggesting possible confounding by case mix (Orion sees more complex cases).  
- No direct measure of underlying reviewer skill or capacity; resource group may be a proxy for agent quality rather than an independent lever.  

**Why the 50% reassignment may succeed, fail, or backfire:**  
- *Success*: If Orion’s longer times stem from serving harder cases, shifting those to Nova could reduce overall cycle time, especially if Nova’s reviewer pool is equally capable but operates under less complex case mix.  
- *Failure*: If Orion provides a higherquality review that reduces rework and appeals (quality outcome), merely moving cases without improving Nova’s handling could increase cycle times and quality issues.  
- *Backfire*: Moving nightshift cases to Nova without adjusting staffing could create bottlenecks, increasing `wait_before_review_h` and potentially worsening cycle times disproportionately.  

Overall, the evidence points to a **partial causal effect mediated by case complexity and shift scheduling**, but the lack of unconfoundedness means definitive causation cannot be asserted without further analysis.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests  

| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
|------|-------------|-------------------|------------------------|---------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|-----------------------|
| 1 | **Orion’s higher times are mainly due to case mix (complexity)** | Orion receives a disproportionate share of highcomplexity cases, which require longer `review_service_h` and `wait_before_review_h`. | Withincomplexity comparisons show Orion lower than Nova only for low complexity; higher for medium/high. | Crude overall mean does not disappear when stratified; Orion’s mean remains elevated within high complexity (31.6h vs. 35h), indicating residual effect beyond mix. | Propensityscore matching on `complexity`, `arrival_load`, `shift` to create comparable Orion vs. Nova cases; contrast matched means. | Matched groups should have similar cycle times, weakening the causal claim for Orion’s direct harm. |
| 2 | **Orion’s group provides higherskill reviewers that reduce rework and appeals** | Skilled reviewers improve quality, lowering `cycle_time` despite longer service hours. | Orion has lower rework count (0 vs. occasional Nova) and similar appeal rates; highcomplexity Orion cases have fewer appeals than comparable Nova cases. | No direct measurement of reviewer skill; Orion’s mean cycle time remains higher even after matching. | Add a proxy for reviewer skill (e.g., prior appeal rate per reviewer) and include it as a covariate; test if Orion’s advantage persists. | If skill explains most of the cycletime gap, residual Orion advantage should shrink. |
| 3 | **Shift scheduling (night work) is the dominant driver of longer Orion times** | Night shift may cause longer queue waiting due to staffing limits; Orion covers night work, inflating wait times. | Night Orion cases show slightly lower median cycle time (20.2h) than day Orion (22.9h), suggesting shift explains part of the variance. | Overall Orion mean is still higher than Nova even within night shift (20.2h > Nova night N=1, 9.8h), indicating shift alone insufficient. | Withingroup (Orion vs. Nova) compare night vs. day subsamples after stratifying by `complexity`; assess interaction of `shift` × `resource_group`. | If shift explains a large share, night Orion vs. Nova night should be indistinguishable after adjusting for complexity. |
| 4 | **Compliance requirement acts as a mediator that inflates Orion’s cycle time** | Compliance check adds a mandatory step; Orion may perform it slower due to workflow differences. | Orion cases with compliance required have median cycle time 7.79.6h vs. without 3.43.9h; Nova compliance cases similarly increase time. | Crude overall mean difference persists after removing compliance cases only for high complexity (still 31.6h vs. 35h). | Conduct a withingroup comparison of Orion vs. Nova where compliance status is balanced (use negative controls where compliance is absent). | Balanced groups should show similar residuals, indicating compliance is not the sole driver. |
| 5 | **Selection bias from observing only completed cases skews the estimate** | Abandoned or stillopen cases may have systematically longer cycles, biasing Orion’s average upward. | No data on open cases; Orion’s high nightshift load may lead to more completions than Nova, altering distribution. | Overall Orion vs. Nova comparison includes only closed cases; stratified patterns still show excess for Orion. | Collect a random sample of *inprocess* cases (including pending) and compare their `wait_before_review_h` distributions by group. | If pending Orion cases exhibit markedly longer waits, the crude gap is likely inflated by selection. |

## 6. Intervention and learning plan  

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | processmining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|-------|---------------|------------------|------------------|------------|----------------------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| 1 | **Controlled Routing Pilot** – Randomly assign 20% of Orionclassified cases (complexity  Medium) to Nova for 4 weeks (maintaining nightshift coverage by coassigning Nova to a complementary day slot). | Test if moving highcomplexity cases to Nova reduces `cycle_time` without worsening `rework` or `quality_outcome`. | Individual cases (based on classification). | Keep Orion staff for complex cases where pilot fails; monitor appeal rates. | PM4Py DFG discovery per group to track added steps; compute `wait_before_review_h` and `review_service_h` distributions. | Provides direct evidence of causal effect of group assignment on cycle time. | If pilot shows >10% increase in average `cycle_time` or >5% rise in appeals, rollback to baseline. |
| 2 | **ShiftBalancing Adjustment** – Reallocate nightshift capacity from Orion to Nova while maintaining total reviewer count, ensuring no nightshift Orion cases remain for highcomplexity buckets. | Isolate the effect of nightshift staffing on waiting times. | Nightshift roster slots (fixed). | Conduct a prepost analysis of `wait_before_review_h` for nightshift Orion vs. Nova. | Track queue length per minute using event logs; compute correlation with `shift`. | Determines whether shift coverage, not group identity, drives the cycletime gap. | If nightshift Nova experiences overflow (queue > baseline), reinstate Orion nightshift capacity. |
| 3 | **ComplianceCapacity Experiment** – For a subset of compliancerequired cases, overstaff the compliance step with additional reviewers from Nova, while keeping the primary reviewer group Orion. | Separate the impact of compliance bottleneck from overall case handling. | Compliancecheck stage (case level). | Monitor `review_service_h` for compliance step alone; ensure no doublereviewing. | Use event timestamps to isolate compliancestep duration; overlay on DFG. | Clarifies whether compliance step mediates the perceived Orion advantage. | If compliance duration does not change despite extra staff, conclude compliance is not the primary driver. |

**Recommendation (120200 words)**  
The evidence suggests that Orion’s higher average cycle times stem partly from a mix of more complex cases and its responsibility for night work, while also benefiting from a pool of experienced reviewers that may reduce downstream rework. To proceed safely, we should first conduct a small, randomized routing pilot that selectively shifts highcomplexity cases to Nova, while preserving nightshift coverage through complementary assignments. Simultaneously, monitor queuewaiting and review service times with processmining tools to detect any unintended bottlenecks. If the pilot shows no adverse increase in cycle times or quality outcomes, we can expand the shiftbalancing adjustment to evaluate the contribution of nightshift staffing. If, however, the pilot reveals longer waits or more appeals, we should rollback and focus on capacity adjustments (e.g., bolstering compliance staffing) rather than wholesale reassignment. This staged approach respects the underlying process mechanisms, limits operational risk, and yields robust causal insights before committing to a 50% reassignment.

## 8. Residual uncertainty  

1. **Skill heterogeneity of reviewers** – collect reviewerlevel performance metrics (e.g., appeal rate per reviewer) to assess whether Orion’s advantage is due to higherskill staff.  
2. **Unobserved arrivalload dynamics** – record realtime queue lengths or system load indicators to quantify the contribution of waiting time.  
3. **Qualityoutcome linkage** – obtain postresolution satisfaction scores to determine if longer cycle times trade off against better outcomes.  
4. **Rework causality** – verify whether rework is a symptom or cause of longer reviews (e.g., measure decisionmaking errors per reviewer).  
5. **Selection of completed cases** – capture inception timestamps of all cases (completed and stillopen) to perform inverseprobability weighting and reduce bias from only observing finished work.  

Each additional data field would sharpen the causal inference by reducing either confounding, measurement error, or selection bias.
