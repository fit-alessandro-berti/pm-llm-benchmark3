## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

**Jan–Mar:** Stable baseline. Observed breach ~0.18–0.19, AUROC ~0.81, ECE <0.03, data-quality clean. This is the reference performance.

**April:** A *schema/pipeline incident*, not a real outcome change. Observed breach stayed 0.19, but prediction metrics collapsed (AUROC 0.67, mean pred 0.28). The 18% “Unmapped/other” variant and 17% unknown-activity share are artifacts of the renamed diagnosis event, duplicates, and missing queue features. The model literally saw the wrong prefix.

**May:** Partial remediation. Mapping patch reduced unknown to 2% and missing queue to 3%, but cached “events before diagnosis” was not fully backfilled. AUROC partially recovered to 0.78 and ECE to 0.05, yet residuals remain a pipeline problem, not drift.

**June:** A *real routing/process-mix change*. The remote-routing policy lifted V2 from ~35% to 52%. Because V2 breach is ~0.09, the overall observed breach fell to 0.15. The model’s mean pred (0.20) was too high, so ECE rose to 0.06, but within-variant rates (V1 0.16, V2 0.09, V3 0.35) were unchanged. This is benign process drift/covariate-prior shift, not concept drift or a model failure.

**July:** First signs of *genuine process + concept drift* for parts-dependent cases. Supplier slippage appears: `Order PartPart Received` jumps to 65 h. V3 matured breach climbs to 0.46 while V1/V2 stay flat. Overall breach remains ~0.19 only because V2 is still large. AUROC drops to 0.76.

**August:** Severe supplier delay (130 h). V3 share rises to 35% and V3 breach to 0.64; overall observed breach 0.28. Model underpredicts (mean pred 0.19). Here process drift, concept drift, and variant-mix shift overlap.

**September:** *Triple overlap*. Partner Z onboarding adds a population shift (24% of cases, rural, older devices, 58% V3). Supplier delay persists (148 h; V3 breach 0.61). Label maturity falls to 82% and Z labels are slower. Aggregate observed breach 0.31 is real but partially immature.

**October:** *Heavily confounded by label censoring*. Only 55% of labels matured and the backlog hides long-path breaches. The observed 0.16 breach is misleading; the model’s mean pred 0.23 is closer to the eventual truth. Underlying process/concept/population issues continue, but they cannot be measured from the observed rate.

**Why not one homogeneous failure:** April is a feature/pipeline break; June is a deliberate routing mix change with stable conditional rates; Aug–Sep are overlapping supplier-driven process/concept drift plus a new partner population; Oct is primarily an evaluation artifact from censoring. Treating them as a single deterioration would misallocate remediation (e.g., retraining instead of bug-fixing, or calibrating instead of restoring labels).

## 3. Diagnostic tests

1. **Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting**
   - *H1 (censoring):* Oct maturity curve flattens early for long-path cases; fixed-vintage backtest on cases aged 90 d shows true breach 0.30 and AUROC similar to Sep.
   - *H2 (real low breach):* Oct curve reaches the same asymptote as Jan by 52 d; backtest confirms 0.16.
   - *Falsifies H1* if complete-case Oct rate stays 0.16 at 90 d.

2. **Recomputation after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning**
   - *H1 (pipeline failure):* After relabeling `Diagnostic Assessment Completed`, dropping duplicates, and recomputing event-count/queue features, April AUROC returns to ~0.81, unknown <1%, mean pred ~0.19.
   - *H2 (early concept drift):* Even with clean features, April AUROC remains 0.67 and observed breach rises.
   - *Falsifies H1* if cleaned April remains degraded despite correct prefixes.

3. **Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month**
   - *H1 (mix shift only):* In June, within-V1/V2/V3 AUROC and ECE match March; only aggregate shifts.
   - *H2 (concept drift starting June):* Within-variant metrics degrade in June.
   - *Falsifies mix-only* if stratified V2/V3 AUC drops in June.

4. **Comparison of process variants and DFG/performance-DFG edges**
   - *H1 (process drift):* `Order PartPart Received` time correlates with monthly V3 breach; other edges stable.
   - *H2 (pure population shift):* Edge times stay ~40 h, but breach rises because Partner Z enters.
   - *Falsifies process drift* if edge time stays ~40 h yet V3 breach remains 0.64.

5. **Tests of whether the outcome relationship conditional on the same path changed**
   - Fit per-month logistic/discrimination on V3 prefixes only.
   - *H1 (concept drift):* V3 intercept shifts up from Jul, AUC/Brier degrades.
   - *H2 (covariate/mix shift):* V3 conditional relationship is stable; only the prior probability of V3 changes.
   - *Falsifies concept drift* if stratified V3 AUC/Brier is stable through Oct.

6. **Population reweighting or transport-style checks for Partner Z**
   - *H1 (population shift):* After reweighting non-Z cases to Z’s rural/device-age distribution (or matching), breach and calibration gaps disappear.
   - *H2 (model unfairness/concept for Z):* Residual gap remains after reweighting.
   - *Falsifies population shift* if reweighted non-Z still differs from Z.

7. **Feature missingness and lineage audits**
   - *H1 (pipeline failure):* April/May missing-queue and unknown-activity spikes align with metric drops; lineage shows stale cached counts.
   - *H2 (coincidental):* Metrics drop even where features are complete.
   - *Falsifies H1* if missingness does not correlate with April AUROC collapse.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Immediate containment**
- Freeze the current model; do **not** deploy a new version triggered by Oct metrics.
- Quarantine April predictions (and any May rows with unrepaired features) from production scoring or clearly flag them invalid.
- Suppress October from all training and from headline KPI reports; report only matured-cohort KPIs.

**Short-term recalibration**
- After May feature backfill, pool clean months **Jan–Mar + Jun + Jul** and fit a **variant-stratified isotonic/Platt calibration**. This corrects June’s overprediction (mean pred 0.20 vs obs 0.15) without absorbing April noise or Aug–Oct concept drift.
- Do **not** include Apr, unrepaired May, or Aug–Oct (immature/censored) in calibration.

**Feature repair**
- Fully backfill April activity mapping, deduplicate, and recompute “events before diagnosis” and queue features.
- Ingest Partner Z indicators, rural travel distance, and device age.
- Add a post-diagnosis supplier-lead-time monitor feature (even if only available later) to help the model anticipate V3 risk.

**Full retraining**
- Only after Aug–Sep reach 90% maturity and Oct backlog clears: retrain on **Jan–Mar, Jun–Jul, and matured Aug–Sep**, excluding Apr and unrepaired May.
- Include new covariates and variant-path probabilities. Start with a unified model plus variant interactions; a fully separate V3 or Partner-Z model is justified **only if** stratified error persists after feature repair.

**Training-month safety**
- *Safe:* Jan–Mar (clean, mature). Jun is mature but reflects a prior shift; safe if the model is allowed to learn new base rates. Jul is 98% mature but contains early V3 concept drift; include only if that drift is intended to be learned.
- *Use with caution:* May **only after** verifying missingness <2% and feature recomputation.
- *Unsafe:* Apr (exclude entirely); Aug–Sep (immature + drift; wait); Oct (censored; exclude).

A segment-specific or variant-stratified calibration is justified now; a separate model is not yet proportionate.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

**Monitors**
- *Predictive monitor:* monthly AUROC, Brier, ECE, mean predicted risk vs observed (computed only on matured labels).
- *Calibration monitor:* reliability curves stratified by variant and source; alert if ECE > 0.05.
- *Label-maturity monitor:* per cohort, proportion labeled by 30/60/90 d, median label delay, censoring-by-path check.
- *Data-quality monitor:* unknown-activity share, missing-queue share, duplicate rate, schema/label-mapping version.
- *Process-variant monitor:* V1/V2/V3 and unmapped distribution per case-start month.
- *Performance-DFG monitor:* edge durations, especially `Order PartPart Received`; alert on >50% increase.
- *Population monitor:* Partner/share, rural/device-age distributions, new-source onboarding.
- *Intervention/deployment monitor:* log mapping patch, routing policy, model versions to align with metric shifts.

**Alert dependencies**
- A *label-delay alarm* must **not** trigger retraining; it only switches reporting to matured-only and blocks training.
- A *data-quality spike* (unknown activity >5%) blocks deployment and forces feature lineage review.
- A *process-drift alarm* notifies operations and feeds a candidate feature, but recalibration is triggered only after confirming clean labels and stable data quality.
- A *calibration alarm* first checks label-maturity and data-quality; if either is red, it is treated as a measurement artifact, not a model defect.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Load raw events
df = pd.read_csv("field_service_events.csv", parse_dates=["timestamp"])

# Normalize renamed diagnosis label (1 Apr migration) BEFORE feature extraction
df["activity"] = df["activity"].replace({
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
})
# Keep UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY visible for data-quality audit; do not silently drop yet

# Format as PM4Py event log
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp"
)

# Duplicate-handling strategy: remove exact case/activity/timestamp duplicates
# (post-20-April source emitted duplicates)
df = df.drop_duplicates(subset=["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"])

# Case-start times for case-start cohort construction
case_start = (
    df[df["activity"] == "Case Created"]
    .groupby("case_id")["timestamp"].min()
)

monthly_summaries = []
for ym in pd.period_range("2026-01", "2026-10", freq="M"):
    start, end = ym.start_time, ym.end_time

    # Event-calendar cohort: all events occurring in the month (volume/quality view)
    ev_log = pm4py.filter_time_range(
        df, start.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"), end.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S")
    )

    # Case-start cohort: full traces of cases created in the month (variant/DFG view)
    ids = case_start[(case_start >= start) & (case_start < end)].index
    cs_log = df[df["case_id"].isin(ids)]

    variants = pm4py.get_variants(cs_log)
    dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(cs_log)
    perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(cs_log)

    # Extract temporal/prefix features (no model fitting here)
    try:
        feat = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(cs_log)
    except Exception:
        feat = None

    monthly_summaries.append({
        "month": str(ym),
        "event_calendar_events": len(ev_log),
        "cases_started": len(ids),
        "unknown_activity_share": ev_log["activity"].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean(),
        "variant_counts": {k: len(v) for k, v in variants.items()},
        "order_to_part_receipt_h": perf_dfg.get(("Order Part", "Part Received"), None),
    })

summary_df = pd.DataFrame(monthly_summaries)
print(summary_df)
```

The code returns auditable monthly summaries; it does **not** invent a PM4Py drift detector.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

1. **Retraining on immature October labels**
   - *Risk:* The backlog hides long-path breaches, so the model learns a falsely low base rate and underpredicts going forward.
   - *Mitigation:* Enforce a minimum maturity gate (e.g., 90%) and use fixed-vintage backtesting or survival-aware evaluation; never train on Oct’s 55% cohort.

2. **Calibrating around the April pipeline bug**
   - *Risk:* Including April (or un-repaired May) in recalibration makes the model adapt to corrupted prefixes and missing queue features, embedding the bug as “normal.”
   - *Mitigation:* Exclude Apr and unverified May; audit feature lineage; recompute before any calibration.

3. **Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift in V3**
   - *Risk:* Attributing Aug–Sep AUROC collapse solely to mix/population leads to no operational warning and continued underprediction of parts-dependent breaches.
   - *Mitigation:* Stratify monitoring by variant; add supplier-lead-time signals; allow a variant-specific intercept once drift is confirmed.

4. **Misattributing Partner Z population shift to model unfairness**
   - *Risk:* Without stratified evidence, Partner Z’s higher observed breach (0.45 vs 0.27, itself immature) could trigger fairness interventions or model rollback that are undeserved.
   - *Mitigation:* Acquire rural/device-age features; perform reweighting/transport checks; delay fairness conclusions until labels mature and covariates are controlled.

5. **Treating June’s lower observed breach as model improvement**
   - *Risk:* The routing policy inflated V2 share; if read as better modeling, needed recalibration for the prior shift is skipped and future V2/V3 rebalancing surprises the system.
   - *Mitigation:* Decompose every monthly rate by variant; recognize June as process-mix change and recalibrate the prior accordingly.
