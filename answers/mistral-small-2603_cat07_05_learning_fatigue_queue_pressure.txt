# 2. Integrated explanation

The observed patterns in service time and error rates emerge from the **interplay of multiple mechanisms**, none of which operates in isolation. A simplistic narrative—such as "workers get faster but make more mistakes due to fatigue"—ignores the context-dependent dynamics of the triage system.

### Early Shift: Complexity and Warm-Up
At the start of each shift, workers face a **complexity-driven backlog** (high WIP, high-priority cases) inherited from the previous shift. This inflates service times (18.8 min in the first 5 events) and error rates (3.4%) because:
- **Selection bias**: Early-shift cases are disproportionately complex (48% high-complexity in the first hour of Early shift) and urgent.
- **Learning/warm-up**: Workers take time to adapt to the new shift’s workload, though this effect is modest (service time drops to 15.2 min by sequence 6–15).
- **Circadian effects**: Early-morning workers (e.g., Early shift) may be less alert, but this is confounded with backlog.

The **stabilization phase** (sequence 6–30) reflects a balance between:
- **Learning**: Workers become more efficient (service time drops to 13.6 min).
- **Queue pressure**: WIP remains high (39 cases at sequence 6–15), preventing further speed-up.
- **Case-mix**: Complexity decreases (0.61  0.54), but not enough to offset pressure.

### Mid-Shift: Efficiency and Trade-offs
From sequence 31–50, service time reaches its nadir (12.9 min), but error rates remain low (2.7%). This suggests:
- **Queue pressure**: WIP is lower (31 cases), allowing workers to process cases more efficiently.
- **Case-mix**: Complexity is at its lowest (0.50), reducing cognitive load.
- **Circadian effects**: Workers are likely at peak alertness (mid-shift).

However, **hidden trade-offs** emerge:
- **Batching**: Workers may start grouping similar cases (e.g., Document Review by juniors) to speed throughput, but this can introduce errors if cases are misclassified.
- **Automation**: Bot_Risk handles simple cases, reducing human workload but creating hidden rework when humans correct Bot errors.

### Late Shift: Pressure, Fatigue, and Batching
After sequence 50, the system enters a **high-pressure zone**:
- **Queue pressure**: WIP rises sharply (62 cases at sequence 51–75, 88 at 76+), forcing workers to prioritize speed over accuracy.
- **Fatigue**: Error rates spike (4.6% at seq 51–75, 7.9% at 76+), especially for juniors (12.4% under high WIP) and mid-level staff (6.8%).
- **Batching/workarounds**: Workers batch cases (e.g., Quality Check delayed until the last 45 minutes) to meet shift-end deadlines, but this creates bursts of errors and rework.
- **Circadian effects**: Late-shift workers (e.g., Day shift’s final two hours) may experience circadian dip, exacerbating errors.

**Why service time continues to drop (11.4 min at seq 76+)** despite rising errors:
- **Speed-quality tradeoff**: Workers prioritize throughput to clear the queue before shift end, accepting higher error rates.
- **Case-mix**: Late-shift cases are simpler (complexity 0.43), enabling faster processing.
- **Selection bias**: Complex cases may be deferred to the next shift, leaving only low-complexity cases for late-shift workers.

### Shift-Level Patterns: Demand and Resource Allocation
Shifts differ in their **demand profiles** and **resource allocation**:
- **Early shift**: Clears night backlog efficiently (service time 12.7 min late) but inherits complex cases (48% high-complexity first hour).
- **Day shift**: Demand peaks late, leading to high WIP (75 at shift end) and elevated late-shift errors (7.5%).
- **Late shift**: Inherits Day shift’s backlog (70 queue at start), but workers are more experienced, mitigating errors (6.8%).
- **Night shift**: Fewer cases, more urgent exceptions, and higher service time (15.2 min), suggesting circadian effects or case complexity.

### Automation’s Role
Bot_Risk **reduces service time** for simple cases (e.g., Risk Classification) but introduces **hidden rework**:
- Humans correct Bot errors more often under high WIP (>70), suggesting automation quality degrades when pressure is high.
- Seniors may take over complex cases from bots, reducing their own throughput but improving accuracy.

### Key Takeaway
No single mechanism explains the patterns. Instead:
1. **Early shift**: Complexity and warm-up dominate.
2. **Mid-shift**: Efficiency peaks as queue pressure and case complexity balance out.
3. **Late shift**: Queue pressure and fatigue drive speed-quality tradeoffs, amplified by batching and automation interactions.
4. **Shift differences**: Demand patterns and resource allocation (e.g., seniors handling complex cases early) further shape outcomes.

# 3. Proposed analyses

### Statistical and Process-Mining Analyses

#### 1. **Within-Resource Comparisons**
- **Goal**: Isolate learning/fatigue effects for individual workers.
- **Method**:
  - For each resource, fit a mixed-effects model:
    ```
    service_time ~ sequence_position + minutes_since_shift_start + I(minutes_since_shift_start^2) + WIP_at_start + complexity_score + (1 | case)
    error_rate ~ sequence_position + minutes_since_shift_start + WIP_at_start * complexity_score + (1 | case)
    ```
  - Use `pm4py.format_dataframe` to prepare the log, then extract features with `pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe`.
- **PM4Py Code**:
  ```python
  from pm4py.objects.log.util import dataframe_utils
  from pm4py.objects.conversion.log import converter as log_converter
  from pm4py.algo.filtering.pandas import attributes_filter
  import pandas as pd

  # Format log and filter for a single resource (e.g., Junior_H)
  df = pm4py.format_dataframe(log, case_id='case:concept:name', activity_key='concept:name', timestamp_key='time:timestamp')
  df_junior = attributes_filter.apply_events(df, [Junior_H], parameters={attributes_filter.Parameters.ATTRIBUTE_KEY: "org:resource"})

  # Extract features
  features = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(df_junior, case_id='case:concept:name', activity_key='concept:name')
  ```

#### 2. **Within-Shift Position Effects**
- **Goal**: Decompose sequence position effects into learning, fatigue, and queue pressure.
- **Method**:
  - Stratify by shift and sequence bin, then run ANOVA:
    ```
    service_time ~ sequence_bin + shift + WIP_at_start + complexity_score
    error_rate ~ sequence_bin + shift + WIP_at_start * complexity_score
    ```
  - Visualize with `pm4py.view_events_distribution_graph`:
    ```python
    pm4py.view_events_distribution_graph(df, x_axis="sequence_position_within_shift", y_axis="minutes_since_shift_start")
    ```

#### 3. **WIP/Queue Stratification**
- **Goal**: Quantify the interaction between WIP and complexity.
- **Method**:
  - Fit a generalized additive model (GAM) with tensor product smooths:
    ```
    service_time ~ s(WIP_at_start, complexity_score) + s(sequence_position) + shift
    error_rate ~ s(WIP_at_start, complexity_score) + s(sequence_position) + shift
    ```
  - Use `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` to create WIP bins:
    ```python
    low_wip = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(df, "wip_at_start", [0, 30])
    high_wip = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(df, "wip_at_start", [70, float('inf')])
    ```

#### 4. **Complexity Adjustment**
- **Goal**: Control for case-mix effects when comparing performance.
- **Method**:
  - Regress service time and error rates on complexity score, then compute residuals:
    ```
    residuals_service_time = service_time - predicted_service_time_from_complexity
    residuals_error_rate = error_rate - predicted_error_rate_from_complexity
    ```
  - Compare residuals across sequence positions to isolate non-complexity effects.

#### 5. **Shift and Time-of-Day Controls**
- **Goal**: Separate circadian effects from shift-specific patterns.
- **Method**:
  - Fit models with hour-of-day fixed effects and shift random effects:
    ```
    service_time ~ hour_of_day + shift + WIP_at_start + complexity_score
    ```
  - Use `pm4py.filter_time_range` to isolate Night shift (22:00–06:00) vs. Day shift (09:00–17:00):
    ```python
    night_shift = pm4py.filter_time_range(df, "2023-01-01 22:00:00", "2023-01-02 06:00:00")
    day_shift = pm4py.filter_time_range(df, "2023-01-01 09:00:00", "2023-01-01 17:00:00")
    ```

#### 6. **Batching Detection**
- **Goal**: Identify batching patterns and their impact on errors.
- **Method**:
  - Use `pm4py.discover_batches` to detect consecutive events with the same activity and short service times:
    ```python
    batches = pm4py.discover_batches(df, activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp")
    ```
  - Compare error rates in batch vs. non-batch events:
    ```python
    batch_events = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(df, "is_batch", [True])
    non_batch_events = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(df, "is_batch", [False])
    ```

#### 7. **Resource-Experience Interactions**
- **Goal**: Test whether experience moderates learning/fatigue effects.
- **Method**:
  - Fit models with interaction terms:
    ```
    service_time ~ sequence_position * resource_experience_months + WIP_at_start + complexity_score
    error_rate ~ sequence_position * resource_experience_months + WIP_at_start * complexity_score
    ```
  - Group resources by experience (e.g., Juniors: <12 months, Mids: 12–36, Seniors: >36) and compare coefficients.

#### 8. **Rework/Error Downstream Analysis**
- **Goal**: Trace how errors propagate through the process.
- **Method**:
  - Use `pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity` to identify activities with high rework:
    ```python
    rework = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(df, activity_key="concept:name")
    ```
  - Compute downstream error rates:
    ```
    error_rate_after_activity_X = P(error_flag | activity_X occurred)
    ```
  - Visualize with `pm4py.view_dotted_chart` to see error clusters:
    ```python
    pm4py.view_dotted_chart(df, case_id='case:concept:name', activity_key='concept:name', timestamp_key='time:timestamp')
    ```

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

### Worker-Level Conclusions Can Be Unfair or Misleading
1. **Selection Bias**:
   - Workers are not randomly assigned to cases. Seniors handle complex cases early, while juniors get simpler cases late. Raw error rates may reflect case difficulty, not worker skill.
   - *Example*: A junior with a 12.4% error rate under high WIP may be unfairly penalized, even if the system’s queue pressure is the root cause.

2. **Measurement Error**:
   - `error_flag` and `rework_flag` may not capture all errors (e.g., silent failures, customer complaints not logged).
   - Service time may include waiting time (e.g., for approvals), inflating or deflating true processing time.

3. **Queue Pressure Created by Management**:
   - High WIP at shift start (e.g., Early shift’s 61 cases) is often a management decision (e.g., not staffing enough overnight). Blaming workers for errors under high WIP is punitive.
   - *Example*: Penalizing Night staff for high service time (15.2 min) ignores that they inherit backlog from previous shifts.

4. **Automation Quality**:
   - Bot_Risk’s errors are not measured. If Bot errors drive human rework, punishing humans for correcting bots is unfair.
   - *Example*: Juniors correcting Bot_Risk may be labeled "error-prone," but the root cause is automation failure under pressure.

5. **Punitive Use of Error Rates**:
   - Late-shift error spikes (7.9% at seq 76+) are often system-driven (queue pressure, fatigue). Using these as performance metrics without context is demoralizing and counterproductive.
   - *Example*: Penalizing a worker for a 12.4% error rate under high WIP may encourage them to slow down, reducing throughput further.

6. **Circadian and Shift Effects**:
   - Night shift workers have higher service time (15.2 min) and moderate error rates (5.1%), but this may reflect circadian misalignment (working overnight) rather than skill.
   - *Example*: Forcing night workers to adopt day-shift schedules may not improve performance.

### Recommendations for Fair Analysis
- **Stratify by WIP and complexity** before comparing workers.
- **Use residualized metrics**: Adjust error rates for case complexity, WIP, and sequence position before ranking workers.
- **Focus on system-level interventions** (e.g., queue management, automation quality) rather than worker-level punishments.
- **Involve workers in analysis**: Co-design interventions to address root causes (e.g., batching, breaks) rather than symptoms (e.g., late-shift errors).

### Dashboard Implementation (PM4Py-Oriented)
```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import seaborn as sns

# Load and format log
log = pm4py.read_xes("triage_log.xes")
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(log, case_id='case:concept:name', activity_key='concept:name', timestamp_key='time:timestamp')

# 1. Service Time by Sequence Position
service_time_seq = df.groupby(pd.cut(df['sequence_position_within_shift'], bins=[0,5,15,30,50,75,float('inf')]))['service_time'].mean()
service_time_seq.plot(kind='bar', title='Service Time by Sequence Position')
plt.ylabel('Mean Service Time (min)')
plt.show()

# 2. Error Rate by WIP and Complexity
df['wip_bin'] = pd.cut(df['wip_at_start'], bins=[0,30,70,float('inf')])
error_wip_complexity = df.groupby(['wip_bin', 'case_complexity_score'])['error_flag'].mean().unstack()
error_wip_complexity.plot(kind='bar', title='Error Rate by WIP and Complexity')
plt.ylabel('Error Rate')
plt.show()

# 3. WIP at Shift Start/End
shift_wip = df.groupby('shift_id').agg({'wip_at_start': 'mean', 'queue_length_at_start': 'mean'})
shift_wip.plot(kind='bar', title='Mean WIP at Shift Start by Shift')
plt.ylabel('WIP')
plt.show()

# 4. Rework Rate by Activity
rework = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(df, activity_key='concept:name')
rework.plot(kind='bar', title='Rework Rate by Activity')
plt.ylabel('Rework Cases')
plt.show()

# 5. Batch Detection
batches = pm4py.discover_batches(df, activity_key='concept:name', timestamp_key='time:timestamp')
df['is_batch'] = df['case:concept:name'].isin(batches['case:concept:name'])
batch_service_time = df.groupby('is_batch')['service_time'].mean()
batch_service_time.plot(kind='bar', title='Service Time: Batched vs. Non-Batched')
plt.ylabel('Mean Service Time (min)')
plt.show()

# 6. Worker Load Fairness (residuals from mixed model)
# (Assume residuals are precomputed in a DataFrame `residuals_df`)
sns.scatterplot(data=residuals_df, x='residual_service_time', y='residual_error_rate', hue='org:resource')
plt.title('Worker Load Fairness (Residuals)')
plt.show()

# 7. Circadian Effects
df['hour_of_day'] = df['time:timestamp'].dt.hour
circadian = df.groupby('hour_of_day').agg({'service_time': 'mean', 'error_flag': 'mean'})
circadian.plot(title='Service Time and Error Rate by Hour of Day')
plt.ylabel('Mean Service Time (min) / Error Rate')
plt.show()

# 8. Handover Sankey
handover_log = df[['case:concept:name', 'org:resource', 'time:timestamp']].drop_duplicates()
handover_log = handover_log.sort_values(['case:concept:name', 'time:timestamp'])
handover_log['next_resource'] = handover_log.groupby('case:concept:name')['org:resource'].shift(-1)
handover_df = handover_log.dropna(subset=['next_resource'])
pm4py.view_sankey(handover_df, activity_key='org:resource', case_id='case:concept:name')
```
