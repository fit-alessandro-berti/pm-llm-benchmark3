## 1. Feasibility model

Let R = Register, T = Triage, I = Identity Check, O = Originals Receipt, D_j = Document Check in evidence round j, A_j = Assessment in that round, C_j = Compliance Review, Q_j = Request More Info issued after round j, E_j = receipt of the requested evidence, V = Decision, and N = Notify. S and P denote optional Escalation Advice and preliminary compliance work. Here, x < y means x completes before y starts, allowing an immediate handoff; || means no required order, not guaranteed simultaneous service.

```text
R < T
T < I
T < D_0
I < A_0
D_0 < A_0

If originals are required:
    O < D_0
    No ordering between I and O or between I and D_0 is otherwise required.

For every information-request round j:
    A_j < Q_j                         [request issued by Assessment]
    OR A_j < C_j < Q_j                 [request issued by Compliance]
    Q_j(issue) < E_(j+1) < D_(j+1) < A_(j+1)
    I < A_(j+1)
    Any newly required originals receipt < D_(j+1)

For every terminal path:
    A_latest < V < N
    If high-risk OR high-value:
        A_latest < C_final < V

For K-104's preliminary preparation branch:
    T < S < P
    T < I; T < D_0; (I AND D_0) < A_0
    P may overlap I, D_0, or A_0 on distinct resources.
    P < C_final when its completed work is used in C_final.
    A_latest < C_final remains mandatory.

For K-411:
    T < (I || D_0); (I AND D_0) < A_0
```

```text
CONDITIONAL AND DECLARATIVE CONSTRAINTS
- Register is first; Triage is the next process activity.
- Each Assessment consumes the completed checks and evidence for its round.
- Request More Info is issued by Assessment or Compliance; a document checker
  may flag an ambiguity but does not bypass the authorized issuing activity.
- Request More Info creates mandatory new Document Check and Assessment tasks.
- A high-risk/high-value case requires final Compliance after its latest
  relevant Assessment, including after rework. Reaffirmation must be explicit,
  linked to the revised evidence, and occur after that Assessment.
- Preliminary screening never authorizes Decision by itself.
- actor(final Assessment) != actor(final Compliance).
- Each service task uses an eligible, actually available resource. All service
  intervals assigned to the same person are disjoint, including other cases.
- External response intervals and reservations are not continuous internal
  service intervals. Preparation, advice, and idle reserved capacity do count
  when they consume or withhold a resource.
- A provisional reservation is not guaranteed service and cannot permit final
  Compliance before Assessment. Readiness failure requires release/rescheduling.
- Queue forecasts do not establish exact start times, priorities, or staffing.
- Parallel tasks require separate ownership, safe evidence/version handling,
  an enforced completion join, and actual contemporaneous timestamps.
- K-337 prohibits the second information-request loop, including a replacement
  request from Compliance. The discrepancy must be recorded and resolved by an
  authorized conditional approval or rejection using existing evidence.
- Conditions cannot substitute for mandatory evidence or conceal deferred rework.
```

K-104 changes release timing, preparation, and scheduling priority, adding an early advisory/screening branch while retaining the terminal route. K-411 changes task release and permitted execution overlap. Neither changes decision authority. K-219 changes document-resource routing; any extra rework is an endogenous consequence, not part of the intended intervention. K-337 changes the allowed decision policy and removes one possible evidence-acquisition branch; it does not waive compliance or four-eyes requirements.

All durations below are elapsed Register-start-to-Notify-completion times. Illustrative schedules use April 2026 dates and require staffed, conflict-free slots; they are conditional executions, not duration predictions. Single-case analysis preserves the focal prefix and background behavior approximately, allowing only displacement caused by the intervention. K-219's factual D1 interval on April 7, 09:30–13:30 overlaps K-337's D1 interval, 08:30–14:30. The supplied traces therefore cannot constitute one feasible simultaneous roster. I use them as separate focal baselines; a joint schedule would first require correcting that resource conflict. Unknown calendars and other cases prevent certification of the illustrative slots from the snapshots alone.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:** Identity finishes Monday April 6 at 12:50, but complex documents remain on the critical path until Tuesday April 7 at 05:40. A1 then assesses from 15:10–19:10. The already visible sanctions issue triggers S1 advice at 19:20–21:20, followed by C1 on Wednesday April 8 at 13:10–17:40. Thus early identity completion alone cannot remove the document, assessment, or compliance waits.

**Minimal intervention:** At Monday 08:50, send the ambiguity and available evidence to S1 and request a provisional final-review reservation. An illustrative early S1 interval is 09:00–11:00, followed by C1 preparation at 16:00–17:30. Keep the factual I1, D1, and A1 intervals. Treat the early advice as replacing the late advice when still relevant; do not automatically repeat it. C1 must update the packet with verified documents and the completed assessment.

**Temporal propagation:** In the base execution, reserve C1 Wednesday 09:00–12:00, after A1's Tuesday 19:10 completion. Reusable preparation reduces the final review from 4.5 h to an illustrative 3 h; 1.5 h preliminary plus 3 h final equals the factual 4.5 h of C1 service. M1 signs Wednesday 13:00–13:30 and notifies 13:30–13:40. Notification advances by 5 h because both the review slot and final service improve and an earlier signature is available. The reservation changes priority; it does not create C1 capacity. If M1 still signs at 18:00, earlier compliance completion produces no cycle-time gain.

**Process-feasibility check:** The preliminary work uses unverified material provisionally and can overlap I1/D1 because resources differ. Assessment still follows both completed checks. Final C1 service follows the latest assessment; A1 and C1 satisfy four-eyes. Neither preparation nor a reservation substitutes for final authorization. The early C1 interval requires an actual free slot or explicitly accounted displacement, especially given Monday's urgent arrivals. Any new information request requires new documents, assessment, and final/reaffirmed compliance; that branch is outside the illustrated no-rework schedule.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best, a genuine C1 slot Tuesday 20:00–23:00 and M1 signing Wednesday 08:30–09:00, with notification by 09:10, yield 49 h, saving 9 h 30 min. Base is the Wednesday 13:40 notification above, yielding 53 h 30 min. Adverse preparation may save nothing; if a six-hour final review finishes Wednesday 19:10 and the next manager slot is Thursday 08:30, notification at 09:10 yields 73 h, 14 h 30 min slower. External rework can extend that tail. These outcomes depend on slot access, reuse, and evidence completeness, not a guaranteed preparation discount.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Actual C1 calendars showing no usable earlier slot, preparation logs showing duplicated rather than avoided reading, unchanged M1 signing batches, or extra rework caused by misleading early evidence would remove the benefit. Conversely, measured reusable preparation and available post-assessment slots would strengthen it. If urgent non-target cases miss deadlines because of reservations, a local gain would not justify the same policy at scale.

### K-219

**Factual mechanism:** Identity completes Monday April 6 at 16:20, but originals arrive only at 17:30. D1 starts Tuesday April 7 at 09:30 and finishes at 13:30; A2 starts at 20:00. M1 does not sign until Wednesday morning. D1's accurate handwriting interpretation prevents rework, so its queue delay has a quality benefit as well as a time cost.

**Minimal intervention:** Route the document task to D2 when originals arrive at Monday 17:30. Preserve registration, triage, identity, and external arrival. Immediate assignment means immediate eligibility for D2's queue, not immediate service. Keep the existing assessment and decision rules; D2 may flag an ambiguity for A2, which can issue Request More Info if needed.

**Temporal propagation:** A feasible conditional base has D2 serving Monday 21:30–Tuesday 03:30, using six hours rather than D1's four. Both verification prerequisites are then complete 10 h earlier than factually. With a realized A2 slot Tuesday 14:00–16:30, followed by M1 at 17:00–17:20 and notification at 17:20–17:30, the cycle becomes 32 h 30 min. Earlier readiness permits an earlier position in the assessment queue and catches a Tuesday signature. The five-hour D2 forecast at Monday 16:00 makes an evening start plausible but does not establish it; staffing and actual task releases must confirm the interval.

**Process-feasibility check:** D2 never starts before Originals Receipt; identity proceeds independently. Assessment waits for both checks. There is no mandatory compliance branch because neither risk nor value is high. If A2 requests clarification, a fresh Document Check and Assessment follow receipt of the response before Decision. Any newly necessary originals must also arrive before that recheck. An overnight D2 interval is conditional, since D1's factual overnight work does not prove that D2 is staffed overnight.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best, D2 finishes by Tuesday 00:30 and a realized A2 slot at 10:00–12:30 leads to notification at 13:30: 28 h 30 min, saving 19 h 30 min. Base saves 15 h 30 min. Without night staffing or earlier A2/M1 access, little or no gain survives. In a clarification scenario, A2 issues the request Tuesday 16:30, the response arrives Thursday April 9 at 04:30 after 36 h, D2 rechecks Thursday 10:00–16:00, and A2 reassesses Friday April 10 at 08:00–10:30. M1 signing at 11:00–11:20 and notification by 11:30 yield 98 h 30 min, 50 h 30 min slower. A misread document can instead preserve apparent speed while degrading the decision.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Actual D2 staffing without an evening slot, long D2 service times on handwritten originals, or increased clarification/error rates would favor retaining D1. Recorded document completion, A2 queue position, and manager availability could show that the apparent readiness gain never reaches notification. A controlled comparison showing accurate D2 interpretation and little extra rework would support rerouting; comparing only successful approvals would hide the relevant downside.

### K-337

**Factual mechanism:** Preserve the first response and rework: Request More Info 1 lasts Wednesday April 8, 11:30 to Thursday April 9, 08:00, followed by D1 checking 12:00–17:00 and A1 Assessment 2 on Friday April 10, 08:00–10:30. One inconsistency then triggers a second request. Its response arrives Monday April 13 at 09:00, after 70 h 30 min, exceeding the rough collection guidance because of the weekend. Document Check 3 and Assessment 3 postpone final compliance until Tuesday April 14 and notification until Wednesday April 15.

**Minimal intervention:** At Friday April 10, 10:30, prohibit another information request. Preserve all earlier activities and evidence. A1's second assessment remains the final assessment; record the discrepancy, its materiality, and the permissible decision alternatives. C1 must review that assessment before M1 chooses conditional approval or rejection. This intervention changes what evidence a terminal decision may use; it cannot retain factual approval merely by deleting three rows.

**Temporal propagation:** Removing the second request eliminates its external wait plus the subsequent D1 and A1 queue/service intervals. Final-review readiness moves from Tuesday April 14 at 10:30 to Friday April 10 at 10:30, a 96 h advance. In an illustrative base, C1 reviews Friday 14:00–18:00, but M1's next available slot is Monday April 13, 08:30–09:00, followed by notification at 09:10. The resulting 166 h 10 min cycle saves 48 h, substantially less than the readiness advance. Compliance queues and a weekend manager gap absorb the remainder.

**Process-feasibility check:** Document Check 2 and Assessment 2 satisfy the first request's rework obligations. Final C1 service must follow Assessment 2 and consider the unresolved issue; A1 and C1 remain distinct. A preliminary or old compliance finding cannot stand in for this review. A condition is admissible only when existing authority permits the residual risk; it cannot require the very missing confirmation as disguised post-decision rework. If approval needs the missing evidence, reject using the existing evidence and documented reason. If neither approval nor rejection is legally permissible without further information, the specified intervention is infeasible rather than a license to bypass requirements.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best, C1 works Friday 12:00–16:00, M1 signs 16:00–16:30, and notification completes 16:40: 101 h 40 min, saving 112 h 30 min. Base saves 48 h with Monday signing. If C1 remains unavailable until Tuesday April 14 and M1 until Wednesday April 15, the omitted loop produces no notification gain; prolonged dispute over decision authority can make the case slower. Conditional approval retains residual loss risk, while rejection can prevent that loss but cause false rejection and appeal. The fastest scenario is not necessarily the best outcome.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** The actual discrepancy's materiality, the evidence required for an authorized decision, C1's ability to defend a rejection or conditional approval, and Friday signing availability are decisive. If the second confirmation routinely changes decisions or reveals serious discrepancies, suppression becomes unattractive even when it saves days. Conversely, evidence that these confirmations are immaterial, combined with defensible terminal decisions and no adverse appeal outcomes, would support a narrowly defined policy.

### K-411

**Factual mechanism:** The legacy system creates only I2's task at Tuesday April 7, 09:35. The identity lock delays document-task ownership until identity completes at 13:20; D2 then serves 15:00–19:30. Assessment waits until Wednesday April 8, 09:30–12:00, with notification at 14:30. The delay combines a technical release constraint and downstream queues; eliminating the former does not eliminate the latter.

**Minimal intervention:** Create both tasks at Tuesday 09:35 and allow separate, controlled ownership by I2 and D2. Keep I2's factual 11:30–13:20 interval, service requirements, and assessment/decision policy. Record actual starts and completions as they occur, use stable evidence versions, and retain the join requiring both completed checks before A2 begins.

**Temporal propagation:** A conditional base D2 interval is Tuesday 12:00–16:30, overlapping I2 for 1 h 20 min. Both checks finish at 16:30, three hours earlier than factually. If this earlier arrival secures A2 Wednesday 08:00–10:30, M1 Wednesday 11:00–11:20, and notification by 11:30, the cycle becomes 26 h 30 min, saving three hours. Assessment begins only 1 h 30 min earlier; shorter waiting for M1 supplies the remaining gain. If A2 and M1 remain at their factual slots, verification readiness improves but notification remains Wednesday 14:30.

**Process-feasibility check:** No originals are required, so independent verification is allowed. I2 and D2 are distinct resources; each must still have a free, staffed interval. A2 starts after the later true completion and reads consistent versions. No final compliance review is required for this low-risk, low-value case. A timestamp inserted later to look like Tuesday 12:00 does not establish overlap; resource/task ownership and contemporaneous event provenance must corroborate actual service.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best, after Tuesday 16:30 readiness, A2 has a genuine 17:00–19:30 slot and M1 an evening 19:45–20:05 slot, with notification by 20:15. The cycle is 11 h 15 min, saving 18 h 15 min, conditional on evening availability. Base saves three hours. With unchanged downstream priority and slots, the clean intervention saves zero; earlier readiness alone cannot force a faster completion. Actual update conflicts, task duplication, or corrective work could make completion worse, but those are implementation harms rather than an inherent benefit of serial verification.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** D2's actual roster might show no earlier interval; A2/M1 calendars might show fixed downstream access. Either would erase the projected cycle benefit. Lock traces revealing a necessary shared-state invariant would invalidate the proposed workaround until that invariant is protected. Conversely, independently corroborated overlap, unchanged verification quality, earlier queue admission, and no extra duplicate work would support the intervention. Backdated timestamps would invalidate an apparent measured gain.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104: early escalation/preparation and reservation | S1 work moves earlier; C1 gains preliminary work but may save final reading. Base C1 total is unchanged at 4.5 h; reservations can withhold idle capacity. | Late advice wait and target final-review wait; compliance workload only if reuse exceeds preparation/duplication cost. | Early S1/C1 demand and urgent C1 waits; downstream assessment remains a constraint. | Other high-risk/high-value cases, especially urgent arrivals that share sole reviewer C1. | Mixed; target improvement can be pure priority displacement. | Incomplete packets miss reserved slots; urgent arrivals and near-full C1 utilization make small reservations costly. |

| K-411: concurrent verification | Nominal I/D service unchanged; tasks release earlier. Coordination adds overhead only if the workaround requires it. | Document-task creation/lock wait and case age awaiting verification. | D2 earlier in the day, then assessment and manager queues as cases become ready together. | D2 standard cases and A2 cases arriving at the same time. | Likely favorable with spare downstream capacity; modest or mixed under congestion. | The later branch controls readiness; assessment/signing cutoffs turn small readiness gains into large or zero cycle gains. |

Treat each rollout as a separate policy applied to 100 existing similar cases entering during a week, not as 400 additional arrivals or a promise of completion within that week. Single-case schedules approximate a fixed background; rollout changes that background. Gains must therefore include the waiting and quality outcomes of untreated cases sharing the same pools.

For K-104, 100 packets using 1–3 hours of preliminary C1 work move 100–300 reviewer-hours upstream. Savings in final reading may offset this, but not automatically: incomplete packets require rereading. Reserving scarce C1 capacity can leave idle gaps or displace urgent cases. Even 100 complete-evidence reviews at 3–6 hours require 300–600 hours, exceeding one reviewer's 168-hour absolute weekly calendar ceiling. Actual staffed capacity is lower. Reservation changes access, not that limit.

K-219 substitutes D2 for D1. This releases valuable D1 capacity for complex corporate work but exposes standard cases to D2 congestion. One hundred initial D2 checks at 4–8 hours require 400–800 hours before rework. Higher clarification rates add document and assessment visits; lower accuracy can conceal harm without increasing recorded queues. The relevant comparison includes both pools and their displaced cases.

K-337 avoids an external response and downstream repeat work. If all 100 cases otherwise replicated its third loop, it would avoid 350 D1-hours and 250 A1-hours. Actual savings scale with loop incidence. Earlier closures feed C1/M1 sooner and may create appeal or remediation work. Avoiding a Friday request can remove a weekend discontinuity; faster notification does not remove unresolved risk.

K-411 mainly advances releases rather than reducing service demand. Large cohorts can migrate the bottleneck from task ownership to assessment, particularly with training and already growing queues, then to manager signing. External responses and weekends also bunch rework arrivals for the other policies. Measure genuine service overlap using contemporaneous ownership and timestamps: batch entry can manufacture apparent concurrency without releasing capacity. Monitor utilization, queue age, urgent deadlines, rework, decision mix, and spillovers through backlog clearance rather than only during the enrollment week.

## 6. Pilot designs

Intervention 1 is K-411; intervention 2 is K-104. Begin with limited operational support, then vary treatment saturation to identify shared-queue effects. Do not infer system-wide benefits from a comparison that measures only treated cases.

|---|---|---|
| eligibility | Low-risk, low-value, digital standard cases without originals, using the same lock mechanism. Independent task ownership and protected evidence versions must be technically supported. | High-risk/high-value cases with ambiguity recognized at triage and an available preliminary packet; retain full final review and four-eyes. Include incomplete packets rather than selecting only easy successes. |

A small successful pilot supports technical feasibility, not blanket rollout.

## 7. Final synthesis

K-411 appears most robust because it removes an artificial task-release dependency while retaining both verification checks, the assessment join, and the existing decision policy. Its strongest defensible claim is earlier readiness. Earlier notification follows only when assessment and manager slots are available. A controlled pilot can distinguish those effects and verify that apparent concurrency reflects actual work.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite its potentially dramatic speed. Eliminating the second request avoids a weekend and repeated processing, but it also changes the evidence available for decision. Final compliance and four-eyes remain mandatory. Conditional approval needs an authorized basis for accepting the unresolved risk; otherwise rejection may be appropriate. Neither faster approval nor faster rejection establishes a better outcome, and subsequent appeals or remediation can reverse the operational benefit.

K-104's local result is least transportable to a weekly policy. A single reservation can favor one case, while widespread reservations compete for the same sole certified reviewer. Preparation helps only when reused; unused slots and duplicated reading worsen congestion and urgent-case access. K-219 similarly trades D1 relief for D2 demand and possible rework, so its benefit depends on handwriting performance and displaced standard cases.

The most valuable additional data are actual staffed resource calendars, queue positions and priority changes, reservation usage, task creation and ownership events, real service timestamps, and evidence-version links across assessment and compliance. Obtain handwriting-specific clarification/error rates, discrepancy materiality, external response distributions including weekends, manager signing availability, and outcomes after notification.
