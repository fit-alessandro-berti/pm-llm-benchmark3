# 2. Integrated explanation

Service time falls and error rates rise together only when queue pressure and adverse case-mix interact with resource experience.

Early in a shift, especially Early and Night, workers inherit backlog from previous periods. Positions 1-5 have mean complexity 0.72, high-priority share 41% and queue 46. Seniors selectively take complex carry-over, inflating early service time ~15.9 min for Seniors and 19.6 min for Juniors, and error rates 3.4-5.1%. This is selection/case-mix, not pure warm-up.

Positions 6-30 show genuine warm-up/learning within a relatively stable queue 33-39 and falling complexity 0.610.54. Service time drops 15.213.6 min and error rate 2.8%2.5% with rework falling. Under low WIP the effect persists: Seniors later error 2.2% under low WIP vs 2.1% first 20, confirming skill stabilization.

After position ~30, case-mix continues to simplify - complexity 0.500.43 and high-priority share 25%19% - which would predict further speed and quality gains. Instead error rises from 2.7% at 31-50 to 4.6% at 51-75 and 7.9% at 76+. The inflection coincides with rising queue length 316288 and WIP pressure. High WIP >70 forces speed-quality trade-off: low complexity service time 9.4 min vs 12.7 baseline, error 5.8% vs 2.1%. The effect is amplified for high complexity: error 10.9% under high WIP.

Batching/workarounds appear late Day shift, e.g., Document Review by Junior_H/I in 9-18 event runs with very short service times, and Quality Check bursts in last 45 minutes. This is a pressure-induced workaround, not learning.

Circadian effects are present but secondary. Night staff are slower 14.9 min later and handle 56% high-complexity first hour, yet their last-two-hours error 5.1% is lower than Day 7.5%. The dominant driver late shift is inherited queue from Day peak 3875 and demand-driven speed-up.

Resource experience moderates all effects. Seniors maintain error ~2.2% under low WIP later and only 4.9% under high WIP. Juniors degrade from 5.1% first 20 to 4.4% later under low WIP but to 12.4% under high WIP. Thus service time fall is partly learning, partly selection of simpler cases, and partly pressure-induced speeding. Error rise is pressure and batching mediated by experience and case mix, not fatigue alone.

# 4. Intervention options

1. Strictly cap shift length for all workers
Benefit: limits exposure to high WIP late shift. Risk: increases handover load, Night shift already handles urgent exceptions, may increase queue at start for next shift. Monitor: mean service time and error rate by minutes_since_shift_start, queue at shift start/end, handover cases with rework.

2. Move complex cases away from the first hour of each shift
Benefit: reduces early service time inflation and error from backlog carry-over. Risk: delays urgent complex cases, may increase waiting time for high priority. Monitor: high-complexity share in first hour, first hour error rate, waiting time for high priority cases.

3. Add a queue-pressure alert when WIP > 70
Benefit: triggers staffing or triage before speed-quality trade-off. Risk: alert fatigue, no action if capacity fixed. Monitor: proportion of events processed with WIP>70, service time and error rate pre/post alert, batching indicator.

4. Force random assignment of cases to reduce selection bias
Benefit: improves fairness and observability of true resource performance. Risk: seniors lose ability to triage, may increase errors on complex cases assigned to juniors. Monitor: case_complexity_score distribution per resource, error rate by complexity-resource interaction.

5. Introduce mandatory micro-breaks in the final two hours
Benefit: may counter fatigue component. Risk: reduces capacity during peak Day late demand, may increase queue. Monitor: error rate last two hours under low vs high WIP, service time variance, self-reported batching.

6. Defer low-priority quality checks to the next shift
Benefit: frees capacity for frontline triage. Risk: delays detection, may increase downstream rework. Monitor: Quality Check waiting time, rework within 7 days, error rate of deferred cases.

7. Add an extra senior reviewer during day-shift demand peaks
Benefit: absorbs high complexity and high WIP, protects juniors. Risk: cost, senior availability. Monitor: Day shift queue at shift end, error rate last two hours Day, senior utilization and service time.

8. Penalize workers with higher late-shift error rates
Benefit: none expected. Risk: punishes workers under pressure and selection bias; incentivizes hiding errors or faster shallow processing. Do not implement. Monitor if implemented: error flag under-reporting, increase in rework within 7 days, increase in batching.

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

Worker-level conclusions are confounded by assignment policy. Seniors select complex carry-over early, juniors receive simpler later cases but also face high WIP. Raw late-shift error rates reflect queue pressure created by management scheduling and Day demand peak, not individual competence.

Measurement error: error_flag may be recorded only when Quality Check occurs. Deferring Quality Check to end of shift creates bursts of corrections that inflate late error rates without reflecting real-time mistakes.

Queue pressure is endogenous. High WIP >70 is caused by staffing levels, shift overlap, and batching of Document Review. Blaming workers for speed-quality trade-off ignores system design.

Automation quality: Bot_Risk instant classifications increase human correction burden when queue is high. Error rates attributed to humans may reflect poor automation handover.

Punitive use of error rates will distort behaviour: workers may rush, avoid complex cases, or under-report errors. Performance metrics must be adjusted for case_complexity_score, priority, wip_at_start, sequence_position_within_shift and resource_experience_months before any individual feedback.
