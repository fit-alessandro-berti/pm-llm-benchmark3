# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

### Queue QA
* **Supporting Cases (`A01`, `A02`, `A04`, `A05`, `A07`, `A08`, `A03`):**
  * The dispatch order of all non-appointment jobs (`A01` [arr 08:00] $\rightarrow$ `A02` [arr 08:04] $\rightarrow$ `A04` [arr 08:13] $\rightarrow$ `A05` [arr 08:21] $\rightarrow$ `A07` [arr 08:35] $\rightarrow$ `A08` [arr 08:40]) perfectly matches arrival timestamp order.
  * Priority is unequivocally ignored: at 08:32, `A02` (Priority 3) starts ahead of `A04` (Priority 1); at 09:05 and 09:32, `A05` and `A07` (both Priority 3) start ahead of `A08` (Priority 1).
  * `A03` demonstrates exact-time appointment insertion at 08:20, interrupting the pure FIFO backlog without preempting running work (since `A01` completed at 08:20).
* **Contradicting / Challenging Cases (`A06`):**
  * `A06` (appointment 09:15) started late at 09:20.
  * *Explanation:* This violation is fully explained by an operational constraint: `A05` was dispatched at 09:05 under non-preemptive FIFO. Because QA does not utilize lookahead lead-time reservation or preemption, `A1` was locked into `A05` until 09:20. `A06` was dispatched immediately upon `A1` becoming free.

### Queue QB
* **Supporting Cases (`B02`, `B05`, `B06`):**
  * `B02` (Priority 1) arrived at 09:14 and successfully preempted `B01` (Priority 3) at 09:16 under explicit "urgent arrival" rules.
  * `B05` (Priority 1) was selected at 10:00 ahead of waiting Priority 3 jobs `B01` and `B04`.
  * `B06` (Priority 2) was selected at 10:36 ahead of Priority 3 job `B07`.
* **Contradicting / Challenging Cases (`B01` resumption at 09:31, `B04` at 10:28, `B07` appointment):**
  * At 09:31, `B01` (Priority 3) resumed instead of starting `B03` (Priority 2). *Explanation:* Requires an undocumented operational behavior—either physical workbench inertia (the technician resumed the disassembled unit on the bench) or dispatch latency. This was explicitly corrected at 09:48 via manual "dispatcher reassignment".
  * At 10:28, `B04` (Priority 3, est 8m) started ahead of `B06` (Priority 2) and delayed appointment `B07` (appt 10:30). *Explanation:* Undocumented hybrid rule where short jobs / FIFO backlog took precedence, or B1 operated under decentralized dispatch without checking appointment calendars.

### Queue QC
* **Supporting Cases (`C02`, `C03`, `C04`, `C06`):**
  * At 10:10, `C02` (est 8m) was selected over older arrival `C01` (arr 10:00, est 36m) and higher-priority / earlier-deadline job `C03` (Priority 2, ddl 12:30, est 14m).
  * The initial execution order (`C02` [8m] $\rightarrow$ `C03` [14m] $\rightarrow$ `C04` [22m]) strictly follows ascending processing times ($8 < 14 < 22$), leaving the longest job `C01` (36m) for last.

* **Contradicting / Challenging Cases (`C05`, `C01` at 11:40):**
  * `C05` (est 40m, longest job) was dispatched at 10:54 ahead of `C06` (est 6m). *Explanation:* Explained by a dynamic policy shift triggered by an endangered SLA ("deadline already at risk when selected", deadline was 10:50). Priority 1 / zero-slack overrides SPT.
  * `C01` (est 36m) started at 11:40 before `C07` (est 18m, Priority 2, ddl 12:00). *Explanation:* Explained by batch commitment (the 10:10 backlog batch was frozen) or maximum wait-time aging limits preventing infinite starvation under SPT.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

### Episode 1: Queue QC at 10:10 (Dispatch of `C02` vs `C03` / `C01`)
* **Competing Explanations:**
  * **Hypothesis A (SEPT / SPT):** The queue operates under Shortest Expected Processing Time. `C02` is selected because $\text{est\_min}(C02) = 8 < 14 < 22 < 36$.
  * **Hypothesis B (Earliest Deadline First / Minimum Slack Time):** The queue prioritizes jobs by deadline urgency or slack ($S = \text{deadline} - \text{now} - \text{est\_min}$).
* **Counterfactual Predictions:**
  * *Counterfactual Scenario:* Suppose a new job $C_{test}$ arrives at 10:09 with $\text{est\_min} = 5\text{m}$ but a distant deadline of 18:00 (slack = 466m), while $C_{urgent}$ has $\text{est\_min} = 30\text{m}$ and deadline 11:00 (slack = 20m).
  * Under **Hypothesis A (SPT)**, the system will select $C_{test}$ at 10:10 because $5\text{m} < 8\text{m} < 30\text{m}$.
  * Under **Hypothesis B (EDF/Slack)**, the system will select $C_{urgent}$ or $C03$ because their deadlines/slack are far tighter than $C_{test}$ and $C02$.
* **Discriminative Observable:**
  * Logging the **dispatch calculation metric** (a numerical sort key field in the dispatch event log indicating whether sorting is based on `expected_duration_sec` vs `slack_seconds`).

### Episode 2: Queue QD at 11:20 (`D-X` Idle Period: 11:20–11:30)
* **Competing Explanations:**
  * **Hypothesis A (Lookahead Appointment Reservation):** `D-X` is fully online at 11:20 but intentionally withheld from starting `D07` (est 28m) or `D04` (est 10m) to preserve an unfragmented slot for appointment `D06` at 11:30.
  * **Hypothesis B (Resource Availability / Scan Latency):** `D-X` experienced a delayed check-in / workstation startup latency and was not operationally ready to receive work until 11:30.
* **Counterfactual Predictions:**
  * *Counterfactual Scenario:* Suppose `D06`'s appointment was scheduled for 12:15 instead of 11:30, with all other arrivals identical.
  * Under **Hypothesis A (Lookahead Reservation)**, `D-X` would immediately start `D07` (mech+elec, est 28m) at 11:20, completing it at 11:48 well before the 12:15 appointment.
  * Under **Hypothesis B (Startup Latency)**, `D-X` would remain idle until 11:30 regardless of the appointment schedule.
* **Discriminative Observable:**
  * The **resource state heartbeat log** (distinguishing between `RESOURCE_IDLE_RESERVED_FOR_APPT` and `RESOURCE_OFFLINE_INITIALIZING`).

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Here we evaluate plausible operational and data-quality exceptions, ranked by plausibility, and state how each would alter the queue diagnosis:

1. **Delayed Arrival Scans (Up to 2-minute latency bound) [Rank 1 — Highest Plausibility]**
   * *Mechanism:* Physical arrival occurs at $T$, but barcode intake scanning is recorded up to 2 minutes later ($T+2$).
   * *Impact on Diagnosis:* Explains why `B05` (arr 09:46, P1) was not selected at 09:48 when the dispatcher assigned `B03`. If `B05` was scanned at 09:48:01, `B03` was the only visible high-priority job. Eliminates the need to hypothesize an arbitrary priority bypass in QB.
2. **Work-in-Progress (WIP) Physical Bench Retention [Rank 2 — High Plausibility]**
   * *Mechanism:* When a preempted technician (B1) finishes an urgent task (`B02`), they instinctively resume the partially disassembled workpiece (`B01`) already sitting on their bench, rather than querying the digital dispatch queue.
   * *Impact on Diagnosis:* Changes QB from being viewed as an erratic priority algorithm to a standard preemptive priority system paired with human bench inertia that requires supervisory intervention (`09:48` dispatcher reassignment) to realign.
3. **Uncoordinated / Myopic Appointment Scheduling without Lookahead [Rank 3 — High Plausibility]**
   * *Mechanism:* Schedulers allow customers to book exact times (e.g., `A06` at 09:15, `B07` at 10:30), but dispatchers operate non-preemptively on a myopic next-available basis without blocking capacity prior to the slot.
   * *Impact on Diagnosis:* Explains why appointments in QA (`A06`) and QB (`B07`) were delayed, whereas QD successfully honored `D06`. Confirms QA/QB use unbuffered appointment overlays, while QD uses lookahead capacity blocking.
4. **Static Batch Scheduling at Queue Initialization [Rank 4 — Moderate Plausibility]**
   * *Mechanism:* In QC, when resource C1 became available at 10:10 with four backlog items, the system generated a static batch sequence (`C02` $\rightarrow$ `C03` $\rightarrow$ `C04` $\rightarrow$ `C01`) sorted by SPT and committed to it sequentially.
   * *Impact on Diagnosis:* Explains why `C04` started at 10:32 even if `C06` (est 6m) arrived at 10:25, and why `C01` executed at 11:40 before `C07` (est 18m). Alters QC diagnosis from dynamic real-time SPT to batch-committed SPT with SLA exception handling.
5. **Strategic or Biased Processing Time Estimates (`est_min`) [Rank 5 — Moderate Plausibility]**
   * *Mechanism:* Initial estimates visible to dispatch are rounded, noisy, or systematically underestimated for complex tasks.
   * *Impact on Diagnosis:* In QC, if `C01` was estimated at 36m but historically took longer, dispatchers might deliberately defer it. If estimates are noisy, true SPT might diverge from nominal SPT.
