## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

The flag is an intake classification based on a risk score. Hiding it changes what staff see while leaving the case’s underlying risk intact. Compare policies for the same arrival population, preserving the joint distribution of risk, scores, complexity and other intake information. Define “flagged” for every comparison by the **original intake rule**, even if its output is hidden. Compare differences in case-weighted means (first policy minus second). For system policies, assign the whole connected set of queues, start from a common distribution of backlog, shifts and arrivals, and follow an entry cohort through closure. Other cases respond to the assigned policy too.

P0 uses current display, priority, review and senior-referral rules, including human referrals and existing exceptions. P1 masks the flag on staff screens while the backend still applies P0 priority and automatic routing. Staff retain the score, case file and any clues from lane assignment. Disabling backend routing as well would define a different policy. P2 keeps display and priority, turns off the flag-triggered review mandate, and lets staff use the existing discretionary referral criteria. Independently required reviews still occur. P3 keeps P0 rules and adds one continuously staffed, equally qualified manual-review slot serving the oldest ready flagged request whenever one is waiting. Existing reviewers keep their dispatch rules. P4 here adds one continuously staffed **shared senior** slot at the current dispatch priority, with review and escalation criteria fixed.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | P3 minus P0 | Whole queue system assigned, effects reported across all arrivals and by original flag | Hours from arrival to close | Total policy effect, including spillovers | Yes, for the specified incremental staffing | Only P0 is observed. Senior congestion can change |
| E2 | P0 priority dispatch minus one pooled first-come, first-served triage queue feeding the same crew, with hours, case information and downstream rules fixed | All arrivals, reported separately by original flag | Hours from arrival to triage completion | Total effect of triage dispatch on an early endpoint | Yes, if both dispatch disciplines are implemented as specified | Flag, lane and triage crew coincide in this log |
| E3 | P1 minus P0 | All originally flagged arrivals in assigned systems | Hours from arrival to close | Total effect of the display component, including human responses | Yes for masking the screen. Staff may infer flag status | No display variation independent of routing |
| E4 | Replace only the P0 review-entry decision with a draw from the P2 referral probability given the current eligible history, versus a draw under P0 | All originally flagged arrivals, including those who skip review under the replacement | Hours from arrival to close | Interventional analogue through review **occurrence**, including resulting changes in queues and senior referrals | Given a specified discretionary decision rule or measured referral probabilities | All ten flagged cases were reviewed. No discretionary-only regime observed |
| E5 | Substitute the joint manual-start schedule achievable with the P3 extra slot for P0 dispatch, keeping P0 request rules, review work requirements and assessment protocol | All arrivals, with waiting incurred only by cases requesting review | Hours from arrival to close | Interventional analogue through review **waiting** and subsequent timing | Yes for a feasible schedule and exchangeable service slots | Ready times, staffing and the counterfactual schedule are unknown. Escalated cases are selected |

Correctness and missed risk are unobserved |

E4 draws a review decision at the history generated under each regime, then allows subsequent work and queues to evolve. E5 changes feasible starts across the pool. Drawing independent waits for each case could allocate the same slot twice. E5 would equal the corresponding P3 time effect only if the extra slot acts entirely by changing starts. E9 isolates a narrow direct effect by timing the intervention after the upstream mediators. A natural indirect effect would require, for example, a current-policy outcome evaluated with the review decision that the same case would have received without the mandate. Identification by the usual mediation formula would require cross-world independence that these data cannot justify, particularly when priority changes referral-time workload. The proposed contrasts therefore use interventional analogues. They can interact and depend on which rule changes first. Holding every realized interval fixed would already fix their sum, the cycle time.

## 2. Mechanism graph

The graph gives candidate mechanisms for a focal case. The `U_` prefix denotes unobserved states or actions. Policy settings are assigned inputs, and `priority_lane` is the lane prescribed by policy. Stage indicators and times are derived from the log. Queue waits in the graph have timestamp proxies described in §3.

```text
Arrive with score, complexity, human cues and a system backlog
  -> apply flag rule -> display/automated actions -> priority or ordinary lane
  -> wait -> triage start -> triage service -> triage done
  -> review decision (automated rule and human judgment)
       skip  -------------------------------------------------------> closing
       enter -> handoff/wait -> manual start -> service -> findings
                 -> escalation decision
                      skip  ----------------------------------------> closing
                      enter -> handoff/wait -> senior start -> service -> closing
  closing -> remaining decision/administrative work -> close timestamp
  Previous starts and completions change the slots available to the next arrival.
  Each new referral changes the workload facing later cases.
```

Earlier triage changes the clock time and shift at which a case seeks review. The backlog encountered then can affect both the decision to refer and the time to close. Reviewer assignment can change findings, which affect both escalation and the ultimate decision. Conditioning on workload or findings after treatment can remove part of the effect under study. Conditioning on review can also select on a common consequence of the flag and unrecorded human concerns. Across cases, referrals accumulate and completions free capacity. A rule that speeds one flagged case can delay another case or move congestion to the senior team. The load recorded at arrival is only a proxy for the state then and is even less informative about the backlog several hours later.

## 3. Stage decomposition

Entries are arithmetic means in local elapsed hours, rounded after calculation. In subgroup labels, m/e gives the number reviewed/escalated. Optional-stage columns average over their entrants and show NA when nobody entered. Residual runs from the last actual stage completion (triage, manual or senior) to close. Service is elapsed start-to-completion time and can include interruptions. Entry gaps include any handoff or off-shift time between previous completion and next start. For additive accounting, multiply each optional-stage mean by its entry fraction. A skipped stage contributes no elapsed interval to that case.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| What does crossing the flag boundary do under P0? | Obtain the versioned intake rule and a larger cohort. Compare local trends on each side of the actual cutoff. A sharp discontinuity estimates a local bundle effect at prevailing system load. A fuzzy rule also needs a first stage, monotonicity and exclusion for an effect of receiving the bundle | Running score centered at the rule-specific cutoff, rule/complexity stratum, narrow time window, pre-arrival covariates | Smooth mean potential outcomes under each policy and smooth peer exposure at the cutoff, with no sorting or other rule change | Check flag/lane first stage, score density and heaping, baseline balance, placebo cutoffs, bandwidths. Observed scores end at 0.61 for unflagged cases and begin at 0.64 for flagged cases | Without a verified cutoff and support on both sides, a local estimate is unjustified. A triage jump without a review-entry jump weakens the local mandate mechanism |
| Is the long manual gap a capacity wait? | At each ready timestamp, reconstruct outstanding eligible requests, in-service cases, qualified free slots, queue order and the next shift. Estimate time to start at comparable states and validate a dispatch replay on held-out days | Ready time, required skill, current priorities, shift and observed backlog at that decision. Standardize over evolving states when predicting policy totals | Complete queue census including carry-in and other work, reliable readiness and calendars | Check starts against slot releases and predicted order. R01 and R02 overlap on T1, so a resource ID alone cannot specify a single-server calendar | If slots sit available while cases await documents, target readiness. Favor P3 if delay tracks slot exhaustion and falls when slots open |
| Does ending the review mandate help? | Randomize P2 versus P0 by independent pool or pool-period among systems with the same eligibility rule. Measure intention-to-treat changes in review entry, time and audited errors | Block on baseline demand, staffing and case mix. Use assignment-level inference | Documented adherence, independent capacity pools, controlled carryover and common audit follow-up | Check entry-rate first stage, discretionary referrals, priority adherence and incident reporting | Little entry-rate change despite verified mandate removal suggests staff would refer these cases anyway. A cycle gain with excess errors argues against P2 |
| How much delay responds to entry, waiting or senior referral rules? | Fit sequential decision, service and dispatch models to richer multi-regime logs. Use longitudinal g-computation in a capacity-constrained queue replay. Replace one rule at a time as in E4–E6 and propagate subsequent histories | Baseline score, complexity, arrival and workload, followed by triage finish, readiness, queue state, staff assignment and findings as available **before** each decision | Sequential exchangeability, supported histories/actions, consistent interventions, adequate queue and interference model | Reproduce held-out route frequencies, stage waits and tail times by pool/shift. Check support for each substituted action | No observed skips at relevant flagged histories or a replay missing overnight tails makes a numerical pathway estimate extrapolative |
| Is the apparent flag contrast partly selection? | Use submission-to-registration delay at a separately staffed intake service as a negative-control outcome. This interval ends before the case is scored | Same cutoff strata, submission channel, shift and baseline load | Current flag cannot affect this earlier interval, and the intake service shares no downstream resources or anticipatory routing | Run the same local discontinuity analysis on this pre-flag interval | A jump suggests sorting, clock differences or another rule. A null result alone cannot establish comparability |
| Could omitted severity account for the gap? | Blindly reabstract pre-flag severity in a validation sample. Vary the prevalence imbalance and incremental delay of a latent binary risk factor. Compare the bias with the observed or standardized gap | Supported intake strata, with prevalence and delay calibrated against audited severity and human cues | An additive delay and specified imbalance in each scenario | An unmeasured factor with a 60-percentage-point prevalence gap and a 13.9 h delay would account for the crude 8.34 h gap. Compare those inputs with audits | Plausible imbalances this large, or a much smaller gap after risk standardization, shift attribution toward case mix |
| Does changing one case's access help or delay its neighbors? | Randomize whole connected pools or switch the whole pool across blocks to P3/P0. Estimate effects on flagged and unflagged arrivals and the all-arrival mean. Relate senior tail waits to the assigned capacity | Block on weekday, shift pattern and starting backlog. Record previous assignments and all competing traffic | Independent pools or sufficient switchback washout, stable arrivals or a defined arrival response, enough assignment units | Test for carryover into control blocks, staff borrowed from control pools, and changes in unflagged and senior tails | Flagged gains matched by losses elsewhere or sustained carryover would change the recommendation or require longer blocks |

Baseline adjustment for the total effect should use information available before assignment. Conditioning a total-effect regression on realized reviewers, escalation or backlog **after** priority has operated can block a pathway or open a selection bias. A network replay would generate those quantities under each policy. Twenty cases from one arrival day cannot validate that model.

**P3 pilot.** Enroll every arrival to the connected review/senior pool during prespecified enrollment windows. Only ready, originally flagged manual requests can use the extra slot. Randomize matched multi-day blocks of the whole pool to P0 or P3, balancing weekdays and planned staffing. Set fixed run-in, enrollment and follow-through windows using measured backlog clearance before launch. Track previous assignments and cases spanning blocks. Estimate the block-assignment effect with uncertainty at the block level.

Keep every usual slot staffed and fund separate qualified coverage throughout P3 blocks. The primary endpoint is mean arrival-to-close time for originally flagged arrivals. Secondary endpoints include all-arrival and unflagged cycle time, manual and senior readiness-to-start waits and their 90th percentiles, service times, referral rates, cost and blinded 30-day error rates in both groups.

Log all arrivals and carry-in work, eligibility, score, complexity, display, routing reasons, ready/request/start/end times, holds, rosters, concurrent slots, reviewer, findings, disposition and follow-up. Check whether earlier manual starts translate into earlier closure. Pause immediately for a missed compulsory check, a serious attributable decision error or borrowing a baseline reviewer. As a proposed capacity guardrail, pause if senior or unflagged manual 90th-percentile wait exceeds matched P0 by two hours in two monitoring windows. Set an acceptable audited-error margin before assignment. Stop expansion if monitoring crosses it.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. If blinded intake audits show that omitted severity explains the cycle gap and accounts for the added preparation time before review readiness, favor better risk and readiness measurement over protected staffing.
2. If P3 shortens flagged manual waits but the resulting senior backlog or displacement of unflagged work worsens system-wide time or breaches the pilot guardrail, withdraw P3 and test the binding shared constraint.
3. If independent audits show materially more false approvals or rejections under the extra-slot protocol, stop P3 even if cases close sooner.
4. If request logs show that most of the apparent manual wait is a document hold, or that “start” records delayed data entry, reverse the queue-delay interpretation and correct the timing measure before allocating staff.
5. If larger logs still lack comparable cases at a verified cutoff or in relevant review-decision histories, abandon flag-effect and mediation estimates based on observational overlap. Use assigned policy variation.
6. If reviewers respond to faster access by widening referrals, repeating checks or reserving ordinary slots, and these responses erase the time gain, change the referral or dispatch rule before expanding capacity.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Observed.** Flagged cases completed triage in 0.84 hours on average, compared with 3.43 hours for unflagged cases. They then spent 15.67 hours from triage completion to close, compared with 4.74 hours. All ten flagged cases entered manual review and six reached seniors. Of ten unflagged cases, four entered review and two reached seniors. Mean manual service among entrants was close to 2.7 hours in both groups.

**Plausible mechanism.** Priority advances triage, while the review rule sends a larger share of flagged arrivals into a stage with a long gap before service starts. Senior referrals add a second such interval. The largest difference in elapsed time per arrival is at manual entry. Its operational significance depends on whether cases were ready and a reviewer was unavailable.

**Unidentified.** The groups occupy different score ranges, and human concerns and review findings can influence routing. One case's start also depends on other cases competing for staff. These records cannot establish how long the same intake cohort would take under discretionary review or whether extra scrutiny improved decisions.

**Next evidence.** A randomized whole-pool P3 pilot preserves the review rule while changing access to a qualified reviewer. Earlier starts followed by earlier closure, stable audited decisions and acceptable waits for unflagged and senior cases would support expansion. Earlier starts without earlier closure would locate the next constraint downstream.
