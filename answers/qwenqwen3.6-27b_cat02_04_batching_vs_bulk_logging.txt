# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

**Resource ID trust vs. shared accounts:**
- If `analyst_pool` (G5) is trusted as a single resource, the overlapping intervals violate physical concurrency constraints, suggesting a data quality failure or impossible multitasking. If treated as a shared account masking 1–3 analysts, the overlaps become plausible concurrent work. Utilization metrics shift from >100% (invalid) to ~60–90% (realistic).
- If `RBT-7` (G1) were a shared manual station instead of an automated cell, identical timestamps would strongly imply end-of-shift bulk entry or timestamp coarsening. The automation context is the critical disambiguator.

**Exact timestamps vs. interval-censored by precision:**
- Under exact interpretation, G7’s identical 14:00/14:15 timestamps imply a true 15-minute batch. Under interval-censoring (±7.5 min rounding), the true starts could span 13:52–14:08 and completes 14:07–14:23, fully supporting sequential work masked by legacy MES rounding. Service time bias shifts from 0% to ±30%.
- G6’s 17:00 stamps become interval-censored to [16:55–17:05] under 1-min precision. This slightly reduces waiting time inflation but does not resolve the bulk-submission artifact.

**Three cases with multiple defensible explanations:**
1. **G1 (Identical start/complete):** Could be genuine simultaneous batch (automated fixture) OR start batching/common setup (operator loads 4 items, presses run, PLC stamps batch completion). Distinguished by fixture capacity context and PLC behavior.
2. **G5 (Overlapping intervals):** Could be concurrent work (multiple analysts) OR shared-account artifact (single analyst logging multiple cases with rounded timestamps). Distinguished by session/badge tracking and precision analysis.
3. **G7 (Identical start/complete with 15-min precision):** Could be timestamp coarsening (legacy MES rounding) OR genuine sequential batching (operator processes 5 items in a fixed 15-min cycle). Distinguished by precision metadata and queue arrival spread.

# 6. Data-collection redesign

**Priority 1: Execution vs. Recording Timestamps**
- Decouple physical execution times from database insertion times. Capture `actual_start` and `actual_complete` at the point of work, not at form submission. Store `recorded_at` separately for audit trails.

**Priority 2: Resource/Badge Identity**
- Replace shared logins (`analyst_pool`, `manual_bulk_form`) with badge-scanned or SSO-authenticated sessions. Log `session_id` alongside `resource_id` to enable concurrency validation and accurate utilization calculation.

**Priority 3: Group/Batch Identifiers**
- Introduce explicit `batch_id` or `group_id` fields for operations that naturally process multiple items (e.g., fixture loads, inspection groups). This prevents algorithmic batch discovery from misinterpreting setup/completion artifacts as physical concurrency.

**Priority 4: Source Clock Synchronization**
- Implement NTP synchronization across PLCs, scanners, tablets, and legacy MES. Log `clock_offset` metadata. Address the T-H safety system vs. scanner clock skew by aligning to a central time authority.

**Priority 5: Pause/Resume & Downtime Events**
- Capture explicit `pause`, `resume`, and `downtype` events (e.g., safety check, shift change, bulk submission). This prevents queue accumulation artifacts (G6, G8) from inflating waiting time metrics.

**Priority 6: Workstation Capacity & Fixture Metadata**
- Store `max_parallel_items` and `fixture_type` per resource. Enables process miners to distinguish true parallelism from serial processing and adjust concurrency constraints accordingly.

**Priority 7: Timestamp Precision Metadata**
- Embed `precision_seconds` and `rounding_rule` in every event record. Enables interval-censoring and bias correction during analysis rather than post-hoc guessing.
