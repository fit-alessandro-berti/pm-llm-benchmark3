## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The sharp raw-to-mapped fitness gap beginning in March is primarily a provenance/semantic effect. Mapping `Eligibility Screen` to `Validate Eligibility` and deleting duplicated `Open Case` technical events restores conformance from roughly 0.55 to 0.90. This does **not** prove that the business process was unchanged. Conformance checking after label normalisation only shows that the old normative model can still be followed when event names are coerced. Activity names are not the whole process: durations, rework loops, queue waiting, and resource assignments can all change while trace fitness remains acceptable.

Technical duplicates and missing resources affect different analyses. Duplicated `Open Case` events create artificial self-loops and inflate variant diversity, so they must be removed before control-flow and variant comparisons. Missing resource identifiers do not necessarily change the control-flow footprint, but they seriously contaminate organisational mining, workload analysis, and any conformance variant that includes resource attributes. Missingness concentrated in migrated activities suggests a logging defect rather than actual absence of resources.

The post-May changes cannot be dismissed as logging drift. The mapped fitness series declines from 0.92 to around 0.86–0.88, and the normalised DFG shows large increases in `Assess -> Request Info`, rework, and eventually `Senior Assessment`. These changes coincide with supplier disruption, staffing loss, and seasonal volume. A pure logging event would not create longer cycle times, more document rework, and queue growth.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temporal cohort | Calendar week/month, known events | Detect change points and recovery trends | 30 cases and 500 events per cohort | Pre/post migration, supplier, staffing, season | Longer cohorts hide within-period effects |
| Supplier/complexity cross | Supplier Z vs other × high vs standard complexity | Isolate supplier rework from mix | 30 cases per cell | Rework and cycle time by supplier and complexity | Supplier Z is non-random; cells may correlate with region |
| Priority/queue class | High-priority vs standard, reconstructed queue wait | Test priority redistribution | 50 cases per class per month | Waiting time distributions by priority | Priority label may be missing or only proxied by complexity |
| Resource/team | Assessor/team, assigned hours, missing resource indicator | Capture staffing and organizational effects | 20 assessed cases per assessor per period | Team workload and queue waiting | Work assignment is not random; small teams may disappear |
| Variant group | Mapped variants: fast, rework, Senior Assessment | Monitor control-flow changes | 25 cases per variant per period | Variant share and cycle time | Technical duplicates inflate variants unless removed |

Primary monitoring population: **non-Supplier-Z, standard-complexity cases**, aggregated by week. This is the most stable reference population because it is less affected by supplier disruption and seasonal complexity; changes there are more likely to reflect genuine process or resource drift. High-complexity and Supplier-Z strata are tracked separately as risk groups rather than combined into one average.

## 7. Monitoring architecture

| monitor | statistic/artifact | reference window | alert logic | diagnostic follow-up | likely false-positive source | owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Label/schema drift | Distinct activity labels and unknown transitions | Pre-migration 6 months | New label or transition rate outside 99% control limit for two consecutive weeks | Map new labels, check provenance, update taxonomy | One-off system release | Data governance |
| Technical duplicate rate | Duplicated `Open Case` events per 100 cases | Pre-migration baseline | Exceeds baseline + 2 for two weeks | Verify migration pipeline; remove duplicates only after confirmation | Replay jobs or failed writes | IT/event log owner |
| Resource completeness | Missing resource identifier % per week | Pre-migration baseline | Exceeds baseline + 3 percentage points for 3 consecutive weeks | Audit activity-level resource extraction; use missingness-aware resource analysis | Staff transitions are not recorded in source system | HR/IT |
| DFG/variant distribution | Normalised DFG edge rates or top-variant shares | 4-week rolling baseline | Manhattan distance > 25 from baseline after mapping | Drill into variant differences and affected segments | Supplier Z share changes | Process mining team |
| Rework/loop monitor | `Assess -> Request Info -> Collect Documents` occurrences per 100 cases | Pre-supplier baseline | Rework rate > 20 per 100 for two weeks; or CUSUM crosses limit | Inspect document quality and supplier completeness | Seasonal complexity increase | Operations |
| Queue waiting | Reconstructed waiting time from `Collect Documents` end to `Assess` start | Staffing baseline | Median wait exceeds baseline + 20% for two weeks | Model assessor capacity, compare high vs standard priority | Timestamp imprecision in migrated logs | Workforce management |
| Service time | Assess service time distribution, median and 90th pct | Pre-staffing baseline | Median service time exceeds baseline + 1 h; or p90 drift | Distinguish slow service from backlog effects | Case mix with more complex assessments | Assessor team lead |
| Case-mix monitor | High-complexity share, Supplier-Z share, volume | Seasonal baseline from prior year | Monthly share outside seasonal prediction interval for two periods | Re-weight metrics by mix; adjust rework and queue targets | Annual renewal season | Business analytics |
| Senior Assessment label rate | `Assess -> Senior Assessment` per 100 cases | Pre-migration/seasonal baseline | Rate jumps beyond complexity-based expected rate | Check whether Senior Assessment is real policy or label improvement | Historically inconsistent labelling | Compliance |
| Mapped conformance | Mapped fitness by month/segment | Baseline 6 months | Drop > 0.03 from rolling mean for same segment | Inspect alignments for non-conforming traces | New but legitimate process variant | Process excellence |
| Outcome monitor | Approve/reject distribution and `Request Info` final outcomes | Baseline, seasonally adjusted | Shift > 5 points in approval rate | Investigate policy or document-quality effect | Population complexity changes | Business owner |
| Throughput/cycle time | Median/p90 cycle time by segment | Seasonal baseline | p90 > seasonal upper bound for two periods | Separate queueing vs service vs rework effects | Annual volume spike | Operations lead |

No universal threshold will work because each monitor has different dispersion, and seasonality is strong. Use rolling baselines with seasonal adjustment where enough history exists. For multiple testing, require the same monitor to trigger for two consecutive periods and require a corroborating monitor before operational action; otherwise use a Benjamini-Hochberg or family-wise alert budget rather than individual p-value thresholds.

# Part B: Reusable PM4Py functions for event-level analysis

import pm4py

def load_log(df):
    """Format raw dataframe for PM4Py."""
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        start_timestamp_key=None
    )

def slice_time(log, start, end):
    """Filter event log to [start, end)."""
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(log, start, end)

def discover_monthly_dfg(log, months):
    """Return a dict of monthly DFGs."""
    result = {}
    for month, (start, end) in months.items():
        window = slice_time(log, start, end)
        dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(window)
        result[month] = {"dfg": dfg, "start": start_acts, "end": end_acts}
    return result

def mapped_log(df, mapping, duplicate_filter=None):
    """Return a copy of the log with mapped labels and optional duplicate removal."""
    mapped = df.copy()
    mapped["concept:name"] = mapped["concept:name"].replace(mapping)
    if duplicate_filter is not None:
        mapped = mapped.loc[~duplicate_filter]
    return mapped

def get_variant_summary(log):
    """Return variant counts."""
    return pm4py.get_variants(log)

def get_temporal_profile(log):
    """Return temporal profile between activities."""
    return pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(log)

def discover_log_skeleton_or_declare(log):
    """Optional discovery; fall back gracefully if not available."""
    try:
        return pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(log)
    except AttributeError:
        from pm4py.algo.discovery.log_skeleton import algorithm as lsk
        return lsk.apply(log)

def compare_raw_and_mapped(df, mapping, duplicate_filter=None):
    """Compare raw and mapped variants without overwriting original log."""
    raw = load_log(df)
    mapped_df = df.copy()
    mapped_df["concept:name"] = mapped_df["concept:name"].replace(mapping)
    if duplicate_filter is not None:
        mapped_df = mapped_df.loc[~duplicate_filter]
    mapped = load_log(mapped_df)
    return {"raw_variants": pm4py.get_variants(raw),
            "mapped_variants": pm4py.get_variants(mapped)}

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest conclusion is that the March raw fitness collapse is logging/provenance drift, not a real process collapse. The mapped fitness series recovers almost entirely at that point, and the control-flow metrics tied to rework and cycle time do not move until May. The second strong conclusion is that supplier Z disruption caused a real rework and performance shock from May through roughly September. The normalised DFG distance increases sharply in May, and the relation `Assess -> Request Info` doubles in the same month that supplier Z’s share more than doubles.

The staffing effect is plausible but less sharply identified. Cycle time and p90 continue to worsen after June, while Assess service time changes only modestly. This points to queueing rather than slower work, but the supplier backlog and the July priority rule overlap with the staffing period. Therefore I cannot cleanly assign the full June-to-August deterioration to capacity loss. The priority-rule effect is even harder to establish from the monthly aggregate: the expected signature is a redistribution of waiting time from high-complexity to standard cases, which would preserve or only modestly change the median. We cannot observe that signature in the supplied monthly summaries.

Seasonality is well supported for October-November, where volume and high-complexity share jump and Senior Assessment use rises sharply. The December recovery is ambiguous because the natural seasonal decline and the December interventions occur together. I would only attribute part of the December improvement to the overtime and document-quality campaign.

The clearest causal attribution is for logging drift; the strongest mediation problem is supplier Z’s non-random complexity. Because supplier Z cases were already more complex, part of the May rework increase may be a composition effect. I would revise the account if event-level segmentation showed that non-supplier-Z cases had the same rework increase, or if high-complexity cases outside supplier Z showed the same queue-time growth after July.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw fitness drops more than 0.10 while mapped fitness stays stable and duplicate/technical labels appear, THEN treat the event as logging/schema drift and audit event provenance, BECAUSE the migration changed labels without changing business behavior.

2. IF mapped fitness declines and `Assess -> Request Info` increases in a segment served by a disrupted supplier, THEN investigate document-bundle completeness and supplier-specific rework, BECAUSE incomplete incoming documents trigger a legitimate control-flow loop rather than a random process change.

3. IF cycle time rises mainly through reconstructed queue waiting before `Assess` while recorded Assess service time stays roughly stable, THEN model assessor capacity and backlog rather than retraining assessors, BECAUSE the bottleneck is waiting, not service duration.

4. IF aggregate median cycle time rises but high-complexity waiting decreases and standard waiting increases after a priority rule, THEN retain the rule only after evaluating the fairness/efficiency trade-off, BECAUSE priority queues reallocate delay rather than remove it.

5. IF volume and high-complexity share rise in October and Senior Assessment rate jumps, THEN separate seasonal population shift from adverse process drift before changing resources, BECAUSE annual renewal season mechanically increases complexity and workload.

6. IF resource missingness is concentrated in migrated activities and time period, THEN exclude or down-weight resource attributes from conformance and organizational mining, BECAUSE the missingness is a provenance artifact and not proof of absent staff.

7. IF rework and cycle time remain above baseline after the supplier declares recovery, THEN inspect backlog and persistent behavior rather than assuming recovery, BECAUSE one mechanism may recover while downstream queues and staffing shortages remain unresolved.
