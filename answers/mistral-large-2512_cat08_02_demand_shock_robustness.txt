# Diagnostic-Lab Process Redesign for Robustness Under Stress

## 1. Stress Scenarios

**Scenario A: Winter Infection Wave**
- Demand mix: 35% Urgent, 25% Complex-panel, 30% Routine, 10% Repeat-after-failure
- Volume: 2.2× normal (4,000 cases/week)
- Disruptions: Courier arrival compression (all samples arrive 10:00-12:00), 20% increase in recollection requests due to poor sample quality
- Key stress: Pre-analytical check and medical validation bottlenecks, assay machine fragmentation

**Scenario B: Assay Machine M2 Outage**
- Demand mix: 25% Urgent, 20% Complex-panel, 45% Routine, 10% Repeat-after-failure
- Volume: 1.8× normal (3,300 cases/week)
- Disruptions: M2 completely unavailable for 48 hours, M1 operating at 120% capacity with 30% higher failure rate
- Key stress: Assay run capacity, repeat assay loops, technical validation backlog

**Scenario C: Specialist Absence Cascade**
- Demand mix: 30% Urgent, 22% Complex-panel, 38% Routine, 10% Repeat-after-failure
- Volume: 1.5× normal (2,750 cases/week)
- Disruptions: Primary specialist reviewer out for 5 days, secondary specialist working at 50% capacity, medical validators working overtime
- Key stress: Hold for specialist review queue, medical validation bottleneck, technical validation backlog

**Scenario D: Courier Delay & Registration Surge**
- Demand mix: 28% Urgent, 18% Complex-panel, 44% Routine, 10% Repeat-after-failure
- Volume: 2.0× normal (3,650 cases/week)
- Disruptions: Major courier delay (all samples arrive 6-8 hours late), digital registration system outage (all orders require manual entry)
- Key stress: Registration clerks, pre-analytical check, intake capacity

## 2. Bottleneck-Migration Analysis

**Scenario A: Winter Infection Wave**
1. Initial bottleneck: Courier intake  Registration clerks (queue jumps from 0.7h to 8h+)
2. Secondary bottleneck: Pre-analytical check (queue from 1.8h to 15h) due to:
   - Increased volume
   - Higher recollection requests (20% more loops)
   - Urgent cases bypassing batching, fragmenting preparation
3. Tertiary bottleneck: Medical validation (queue from 2h to 30h) because:
   - Complex-panel cases (25%) require medical validation
   - Urgent cases (35%) also require medical validation
   - Specialist review cases accumulate
4. Final bottleneck: Assay machines (queue from 4h to 25h) due to:
   - Fragmented schedules from priority rules
   - Increased repeats from rushed processing

**Scenario B: Assay Machine M2 Outage**
1. Immediate bottleneck: Assay run (queue from 4h to 48h) due to:
   - M2 unavailable
   - M1 operating at 120% with higher failure rate
2. Secondary bottleneck: Technical validation (queue from 3.5h to 24h) because:
   - Increased volume from M1
   - Higher repeat assay rate (30% more)
3. Tertiary bottleneck: Medical validation (queue from 2h to 18h) due to:
   - Complex-panel cases waiting for results
   - Specialist review cases accumulating
4. Final bottleneck: Pre-analytical check (queue from 1.8h to 12h) as:
   - Recollection loops increase
   - Intake capacity reduced by registration backlog

**Scenario C: Specialist Absence Cascade**
1. Initial bottleneck: Hold for specialist review (queue from 5h to 72h)
2. Secondary bottleneck: Medical validation (queue from 2h to 36h) because:
   - Specialist review cases accumulate
   - Medical validators working overtime but still insufficient
3. Tertiary bottleneck: Technical validation (queue from 3.5h to 20h) as:
   - Cases wait for medical validation
   - Repeat assays increase due to rushed processing
4. Final bottleneck: Assay machines (queue from 4h to 16h) due to:
   - Fragmented schedules from priority rules
   - Increased repeats from M2 usage

**Scenario D: Courier Delay & Registration Surge**
1. Immediate bottleneck: Registration clerks (queue from 0.7h to 12h) due to:
   - All samples arriving late
   - Manual registration required
2. Secondary bottleneck: Pre-analytical check (queue from 1.8h to 20h) because:
   - Registration backlog delays intake
   - Higher recollection requests from rushed processing
3. Tertiary bottleneck: Assay machines (queue from 4h to 18h) as:
   - Batching disrupted by late arrivals
   - Priority rules fragment schedules
4. Final bottleneck: Medical validation (queue from 2h to 14h) due to:
   - Complex-panel cases waiting for results
   - Specialist review cases accumulating

## 3. Robust Redesign Package

**Core Interventions:**
1. **Dynamic Priority Triage System (I1 + modifications)**
   - Implement Urgent-first dispatch at preparation and assay, but with:
     - Time-based escalation: Urgent cases get priority for first 12 hours, then Complex-panel cases get priority
     - WIP limits: No more than 30% of assay capacity can be dedicated to Urgent cases at any time
     - Batch preservation: Routine cases maintain minimum batch size of 8 to prevent complete fragmentation
   - *Robustness*: Prevents starvation of Complex-panel cases while still meeting Urgent targets. Adapts to different demand mixes.
   - *Assumptions*: Accurate case typing at registration, real-time WIP monitoring.
   - *Failure mode*: If Urgent volume exceeds 40%, Complex-panel cases may still be starved.

2. **Pre-Analytical Defect Triage (I6 + enhancements)**
   - Implement early rejection (within 2 hours) of poor samples, but with:
     - Automated sample quality scoring using image analysis
     - Physician notification portal for rejected samples
     - Dedicated recollection coordinator to handle physician callbacks
   - *Robustness*: Reduces recollection loops that clog intake capacity across all scenarios.
   - *Assumptions*: Sample quality can be assessed within 2 hours, physicians will respond to notifications.
   - *Failure mode*: If rejection rate exceeds 15%, physician pushback may reduce compliance.

3. **Cross-Training Pool (I4 + I5 + modifications)**
   - Create a flexible resource pool with:
     - 2 technical validators cross-trained for accession correction
     - 1 medical validator cross-trained for technical validation
     - Temporary evening shift for medical validation during peaks (I5)
     - Dynamic allocation based on queue lengths
   - *Robustness*: Addresses multiple bottlenecks (accession correction, technical validation, medical validation) without permanent capacity addition.
   - *Assumptions*: Cross-training can be completed in 4 weeks, staff can handle context switching.
   - *Failure mode*: If all specialists are out, the pool may be insufficient.

4. **Courier & Registration Smoothing (I7 + I8)**
   - Implement:
     - Staggered courier pickup slots (I7) with financial incentives for compliance
     - Automated registration for digital orders (I8) with fallback to manual
     - Registration queue monitoring with automatic overtime authorization
   - *Robustness*: Prevents intake bottlenecks that cascade through the system.
   - *Assumptions*: Couriers can adjust routes, digital metadata is complete for 60%+ of orders.
   - *Failure mode*: If digital system fails, registration bottleneck may return.

**Supporting Interventions:**
5. **Assay Machine Load Balancing (I3 + modifications)**
   - Dedicate M1 to Urgent and Complex-panel cases, but:
     - Route only 70% of Routine cases to M2
     - Implement predictive maintenance alerts for M2
     - Create overflow protocol to M1 when M2 queue exceeds 24 hours
   - *Robustness*: Protects critical cases while preventing M2 failure cascades.
   - *Assumptions*: M1 can handle overflow without increased failure rate.

6. **Complex-Panel Overflow Protocol (I10 + modifications)**
   - Outsource 30% of Complex-panel cases during peaks, but:
     - Only when specialist review queue exceeds 48 hours
     - Maintain traceability through digital chain of custody
     - Prioritize cases with longest expected validation time
   - *Robustness*: Provides relief valve for specialist review bottleneck.
   - *Assumptions*: Partner lab can maintain quality standards.

## 6. Residual Vulnerabilities

1. **Medical Validation Bottleneck Persistence**
   - Even with cross-training and overflow protocols, medical validation remains the ultimate constraint during extreme peaks (Urgent >40% + Complex-panel >25%).
   - *Redesign-induced*: The dynamic priority system may push too many cases to medical validation simultaneously.

2. **Assay Machine Reliability**
   - M2 remains a single point of failure. The load balancing intervention increases its utilization, potentially accelerating wear.
   - *Redesign-induced*: Routing more cases to M2 during normal periods may increase failure rates.

3. **Specialist Review Concentration**
   - The overflow protocol reduces pressure but creates a new dependency on the partner lab's specialist review quality.
   - *Redesign-induced*: May incentivize over-reliance on outsourcing.

4. **Cross-Training Fatigue**
   - Staff may experience burnout from frequent context switching between roles.
   - *Redesign-induced*: The flexible resource pool requires constant adaptation.

5. **Digital Order Dependency**
   - The registration smoothing intervention assumes high digital order volume, which may not be reliable during system outages.
   - *Redesign-induced*: Creates vulnerability if digital system fails.

## 7. Minimum Monitoring Dashboard

**Leading Indicators (Daily Monitoring):**
1. **Intake Health**
   - Registration queue length (threshold: >4h)
   - Pre-analytical queue length (threshold: >8h)
   - Recollection request rate (threshold: >12% of daily volume)
   - *Warning band*: Yellow 4-8h (registration), 8-12h (pre-analytical), 10-15% (recollection); Red >8h, >12h, >15%

2. **Process Flow**
   - Urgent case WIP as % of assay capacity (threshold: >30%)
   - Complex-panel case WIP as % of medical validation capacity (threshold: >40%)
   - Batch wait time (threshold: >12h)
   - *Warning band*: Yellow 25-35% (Urgent), 30-40% (Complex-panel), 8-12h (batch); Red >35%, >40%, >12h

3. **Resource Utilization**
   - M2 utilization (threshold: >95% for 4h)
   - Medical validator utilization (threshold: >90% for 8h)
   - Specialist review queue (threshold: >24h)
   - *Warning band*: Yellow 90-95% (M2), 85-90% (medical), 12-24h (specialist); Red >95%, >90%, >24h

4. **Quality Signals**
   - Repeat assay rate (threshold: >10%)
   - Recollection request rate (threshold: >12%)
   - Manual accession correction rate (threshold: >8%)
   - *Warning band*: Yellow 8-10% (repeat), 10-12% (recollection), 6-8% (correction); Red >10%, >12%, >8%

**Lagging Metrics (Weekly Review):**
- p90 cycle time by case type
- Percentage of cases with queue >12h
- Overall rework rate
- Contractual penalty events (Urgent cases >18h)

**Scenario-Specific Triggers:**
- Winter Infection Wave: Urgent cases >30% + medical validation queue >24h for 2 days
- Assay Machine Outage: M2 utilization >110% for 4h + repeat assay rate >15%
- Specialist Absence: Specialist review queue >36h for 12h
- Courier Delay: Registration queue >8h for 6h

**Qualitative Signals:**
- Physician complaints about recollection requests
- Staff reports of context-switching fatigue
- Courier compliance with staggered slots
- Partner lab turnaround time for outsourced cases
