# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

Process mining models serve distinct operational and analytical functions. Evaluating a model without defining its intended deployment context leads to flawed governance decisions. We distinguish four primary use cases:

1. **Exploration and Process Discovery**: Used by analysts to discover informal variants, identify unmapped execution paths, and assess raw operational friction.
   * *Metric Priority*: High recall/fitness ($>0.95$). High permissiveness is acceptable to capture unexpected real-world behavior. Precision is secondary ($0.40–0.60$).
2. **Conformance Enforcement and Automated Auditing**: Used in regulatory compliance engines, automated workflow gateways, and continuous controls monitoring.
   * *Metric Priority*: Strict precision ($>0.90$) and zero-tolerance for non-compliant sequences (e.g., skipping mandatory checks or unauthorized approvals). Fitness must be high on compliant traces, but non-compliant traces must fail alignment or yield clear diagnostics.
3. **Simulation and Predictive Process Monitoring**: Used for capacity planning, discrete-event queue modeling, and remaining-time predictions.
   * *Metric Priority*: Balanced fitness and precision, strict mathematical soundness (no deadlocks or livelocks), realistic branching probabilities, and absence of artificial state-space explosions.
4. **Stakeholder Communication and IT Architecture**: Used as standard operating procedure (SOP) documentation and technical specification for software development.
   * *Metric Priority*: High simplicity ($>0.80$), low cognitive load, structural block composition, zero label duplication, and direct mapping to organizational roles.

### Acceptable Fitness/Precision Trade-offs by Use Case

```
Use Case                      Fitness Target   Precision Target   Primary Risk of Failure
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Exploration                   > 0.95           > 0.40             Ignoring real execution paths
Conformance Enforcement       > 0.90 (valid)   > 0.90             Allowing non-compliant breaches
Simulation / Prediction       > 0.85           > 0.85             State explosion / biased queues
Stakeholder Communication     > 0.80           > 0.70             Misinterpretation / unmaintainability
```

Model S was optimized purely for exploration, resulting in severe compliance gaps. Model C was overfitted to training variants, causing catastrophic holdout degradation. Neither is acceptable for production deployment in its current form.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

### Strongest Case For Model S
Model S presents a clean, block-structured architecture with zero duplicate labels, high visual clarity (comprehension score 4.4), low computational overhead (0.018s median alignment runtime), and robust holdout fitness (0.981). It provides a lightweight framework for high-level process visualization and discovery.

### Strongest Case Against Model S
Model S is fundamentally non-compliant and unsafe for business enforcement. By wrapping every verification check (`Credit Check`, `Technical Review`, `Supplier Review`, `Expedite Review`) inside an exclusive choice with a silent transition (`tau`) under a parallel block `+`, Model S allows every verification step to be skipped entirely or executed in arbitrary, illegal orders. Furthermore, Model S completely omits `Correct Dossier`, rendering it incapable of representing operational re-checks.

### Five Undesirable or Policy-Ambiguous Admitted Behaviors

1. **Complete Skip of Mandatory Checks (Trace Violation P2)**:
   * *Trace*: `['Register Dossier', 'Manager Approval', 'Release Order']`
   * *Mechanism*: All four branches in the parallel block choose the silent transition `tau`.

2. **Premature Expedite Review (Trace Violation P4)**:
   * *Trace*: `['Register Dossier', 'Expedite Review', 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review', 'Manager Approval', 'Release Order']`
   * *Mechanism*: Parallel execution allows `Expedite Review` to fire prior to mandatory verification tasks.

3. **Partial Verification Bypass (Trace Violation P2)**:
   * *Trace*: `['Register Dossier', 'Technical Review', 'Auto Approval', 'Release Order']`
   * *Mechanism*: `Credit Check` selects `tau`, allowing automated approval on an unverified credit profile.

4. **Arbitrary Unstructured Interleaving**:
   * *Trace*: `['Register Dossier', 'Expedite Review', 'Supplier Review', 'Auto Approval', 'Release Order']`
   * *Mechanism*: Expedite and Supplier reviews execute without either mandatory check firing.

5. **Rejection of Legitimate Re-check Loops (Trace Violation P6)**:
   * *Trace*: `['Register Dossier', 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review', 'Correct Dossier', 'Technical Review', 'Manager Approval', 'Release Order']`
   * *Mechanism*: Model S lacks the `Correct Dossier` label in its alphabet, forcing alignment cost penalties on valid operational correction loops.

```
Model S Structural Flaw:
+--- (Parallel) -----------------------------------------------+
|  ---> X('Credit Check', tau)                                 |
|  ---> X('Technical Review', tau)                             |  <-- Every check is optional
|  ---> X('Supplier Review', tau)                              |      and can interleave arbitrarily!
|  ---> X('Expedite Review', tau)                              |
+--------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Noise Tolerance vs. Accidental Semantic Flaws
The permissiveness of Model S is not a designed noise-tolerance policy; it is an accidental semantic flaw resulting from naive process-tree inductive mining parameters. By allowing `tau` in every parallel branch, the miner relaxed control-flow constraints entirely, creating an over-permissive model.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

### Strongest Case For Model C
Model C offers high training precision (0.941). On known, historically frequent training traces, it enforces strict sequence order, ensuring that no observed training case bypasses mandatory checks or executes expedited steps out of sequence.

### Strongest Case Against Model C
Model C is a brute-force branch enumeration. It models process flows by constructing eight explicit parallel sequences inside a top-level `XOR` choice. This architectural anti-pattern creates 33 duplicate activity labels, leading to severe operational and analytical failure modes:
1. **Catastrophic Generalization Collapse**: Holdout fitness falls to 0.744, and generalization drops to 0.412. Unseen valid combinations of known process steps are rejected.
2. **State-Space Explosion and Performance Penalty**: Alignment search requires 0.440s per trace (24× slower than Model S) due to nondeterministic state transitions created by label duplication.
3. **Rigid Correction Handling**: Variant 7 explicitly demands that *both* `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` repeat after a `Correct Dossier`, failing cases where only a single check requires re-evaluation.

```
Model C Architectural Anti-Pattern (Branch Enumeration):
X (
  -> Trace Variant 1 (5 activities),
  -> Trace Variant 2 (5 activities),
  ...
  -> Trace Variant 8 (5 activities)
)
--> 33 Duplicate Labels | 0.281 Simplicity | Infinite Maintenance Overhead
```

### Precision as an Artifact of Memorization
Model C's high training precision (0.941) is a false positive generated by standard precision algorithms (e.g., ETConformance or alignment-based precision). Because the model explicitly enumerates observed training paths, its allowed language closely mirrors the observed training log. However, because it fails to capture the underlying process grammar (such as true concurrency and modular loops), it treats legitimate, compliant variant combinations as non-compliant, mistaking structural rigidity for audit readiness.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

Ranked by decision impact on model selection and deployment governance:

1. **Positive-Log-Only Precision Inflation (Impact: High)**
   * *Pathology*: Precision metrics assess how much extra behavior a model allows beyond the observed training log. They do not evaluate business compliance rules.
   * *Consequence*: Model C scores 0.941 precision simply by memorizing the training set, despite failing valid business traces.

2. **Fitness Optimization via Unconstrained Silent Transitions (Impact: High)**
   * *Pathology*: Alignment algorithms maximize trace fitting by leveraging `tau` steps.
   * *Consequence*: Model S achieves 0.992 training fitness by allowing mandatory steps to be skipped via silent transitions without cost penalty.

3. **Generalization Rewards Over-Permissiveness (Impact: High)**
   * *Pathology*: Token-based generalization measures state transition frequencies across tokens. Highly permissive nets distribute tokens broadly, inflating scores.
   * *Consequence*: Model S receives a false high score (0.907), while Model C is penalized (0.412) for isolated execution paths.

4. **Duplicate Label Alignment Aliasing (Impact: Medium-High)**
   * *Pathology*: Petri nets with duplicate transition labels introduce non-determinism during A* alignment search.
   * *Consequence*: Model C's 33 duplicate labels cause search space explosion, leading to high runtimes (0.440s) and unpredictable alignment paths.

5. **Train/Holdout Split Variance without Stratification (Impact: Medium-High)**
   * *Pathology*: Random 80/20 splits without feature stratification (e.g., external supplier flags, auto-approvals) create domain shift between splits.
   * *Consequence*: Model C's holdout fitness drops to 0.744 because rare valid combinations were assigned exclusively to the holdout set.

6. **Alignment-Cost Weighting Sensitivity (Impact: Medium)**
   * *Pathology*: Standard alignment algorithms assign uniform cost (1.0) to model moves and log moves.
   * *Consequence*: Skipping a mandatory credit check incurs the same alignment penalty as skipping an optional expedited review.

7. **Missing-Attribute Bias in Discovery (Impact: Medium)**
   * *Pathology*: Process discovery algorithms operating on flat control-flow logs cannot observe conditional context (e.g., `is_external` or `affected_check`).
   * *Consequence*: Miners either over-generalize (Model S) or over-fit (Model C) to represent conditional routing.

8. **Ungrounded Stakeholder Scoring (Impact: Low-Medium)**
   * *Pathology*: Informal 1–5 visual scores measure visual aesthetics rather than semantic correctness.
   * *Consequence*: Model S scores 4.4 due to visual simplicity, concealing underlying compliance defects.

# 7. Third Alternative

To address the limitations of Model S and Model C, we specify `model_third` using a **hybrid procedural backbone with declarative guards**. The procedural backbone enforces sound sequence boundaries, true concurrency for mandatory checks, structured execution for optional steps, and a formal re-check loop.

### Formal Semantics of `model_third`

$$\begin{aligned}
\text{ModelThird} = \rightarrow(& \text{'Register Dossier'}, \\
& \text{MandatoryPhase}, \\
& \text{OptionalPhase}, \\
& \text{CorrectionLoop}, \\
& \text{ApprovalPhase}, \\
& \text{'Release Order'} )
\end{aligned}$$

Where:
* $\text{MandatoryPhase} = +(\text{'Credit Check'}, \text{'Technical Review'})$
* $\text{OptionalPhase} = +(\text{X}(\text{'Supplier Review'}, \tau), \text{X}(\text{'Expedite Review'}, \tau))$
* $\text{CorrectionLoop} = *\left(\tau, \rightarrow\left(\text{'Correct Dossier'}, +(\text{X}(\text{'Credit Check'}, \tau), \text{X}(\text{'Technical Review'}, \tau))\right)\right)$
* $\text{ApprovalPhase} = \text{X}(\text{'Manager Approval'}, \text{'Auto Approval'})$

```
                                  model_third Architecture
                                  
 [Register Dossier]
         |
         v
 +--- Mandatory Checks (AND) -------+
 |  --> Credit Check                |   <-- BOTH mandatory; complete in any order
 |  --> Technical Review            |
 +----------------------------------+
         |
         v
 +--- Optional Reviews (AND) -------+
 |  --> X('Supplier Review', tau)   |   <-- Optional; MUST follow mandatory checks;
 |  --> X('Expedite Review', tau)   |       can occur in any order relative to each other
 +----------------------------------+
         |
         v
 +--- Correction Loop (LOOP) ---------------------------------------------------+
 |  tau (Exit Loop)                                                             |
 |  OR                                                                          |
 |  -> 'Correct Dossier' -> + ( X('Credit Check', tau), X('Technical Review', tau) ) <-- Selective re-check
 +------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
         |
         v
  X('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval')  <-- Exactly one approval
         |
         v
  [Release Order]
```

### PM4Py Python Implementation

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import Transition, SilentTransition, StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL

def build_model_third():
    """
    Constructs model_third as a block-structured ProcessTree that strictly enforces:
    1. Mandatory Credit Check & Technical Review in parallel (cannot be skipped).
    2. Optional Supplier & Expedite Reviews occurring AFTER mandatory checks.
    3. Formal loop for Correct Dossier allowing selective re-checks.
    4. Mutually exclusive Manager or Auto Approval.
    """
    # Define Activity Nodes
    reg = ProcessTree(label='Register Dossier')
    cc = ProcessTree(label='Credit Check')
    tr = ProcessTree(label='Technical Review')
    sr = ProcessTree(label='Supplier Review')
    er = ProcessTree(label='Expedite Review')
    cd = ProcessTree(label='Correct Dossier')
    ma = ProcessTree(label='Manager Approval')
    aa = ProcessTree(label='Auto Approval')
    rel = ProcessTree(label='Release Order')
    
    tau1 = ProcessTree(label=None)
    tau2 = ProcessTree(label=None)
    tau3 = ProcessTree(label=None)
    tau4 = ProcessTree(label=None)
    tau5 = ProcessTree(label=None)

    # 1. Mandatory Phase: Both Credit Check AND Technical Review required in parallel
    mandatory_phase = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.PARALLEL, children=[cc, tr])
    
    # 2. Optional Phase: Supplier & Expedite Reviews (optional, concurrent, after mandatory)
    opt_sr = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[sr, tau1])
    opt_er = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[er, tau2])
    optional_phase = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.PARALLEL, children=[opt_sr, opt_er])
    
    # 3. Selective Re-check after Correct Dossier
    re_cc = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[cc, tau3])
    re_tr = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[tr, tau4])
    recheck_block = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.PARALLEL, children=[re_cc, re_tr])
    
    correction_body = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[cd, recheck_block])
    
    # Loop Structure: * ( tau, correction_body )
    # Loop executes tau (exit), or correction_body then loops back
    correction_loop = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[tau5, correction_body])
    
    # 4. Approval Choice: Exactly one of Manager or Auto Approval
    approval_phase = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[ma, aa])
    
    # Main Process Backbone Sequence
    model_third = ProcessTree(
        operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
        children=[
            reg,
            mandatory_phase,
            optional_phase,
            correction_loop,
            approval_phase,
            rel
        ]
    )
    return model_third

# Instantiate
model_third = build_model_third()

# Verification & Soundness
net, initial_marking, final_marking = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)
is_sound = pm4py.check_soundness(net, initial_marking, final_marking)
print(f"[MODEL_THIRD] Petri net soundness check: {is_sound}")
```

### Declarative Rules & Python Policy Checkers

Because `is_external` and `affected_check` attributes are absent from the discovery log, pure control-flow miners cannot restrict `Supplier Review` or constrain post-correction re-checks. We define post-hoc declarative python rules to enforce these constraints:

```python
def validate_policy_rules(trace_events, attributes=None):
    """
    Post-hoc declarative validation layer for domain rules unobservable in basic control-flow.
    """
    events = [e['concept:name'] for e in trace_events]
    
    # P1 Rule: First and Last
    if events[0] != 'Register Dossier' or events[-1] != 'Release Order':
        return False, "Violation P1: Register Dossier must be first, Release Order must be last."

    # P2 Rule: Mandatory Checks before Approval
    app_idx = min([i for i, e in enumerate(events) if e in ('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval')], default=999)
    if 'Credit Check' not in events[:app_idx] or 'Technical Review' not in events[:app_idx]:
        return False, "Violation P2: Credit Check and Technical Review are mandatory before approval."

    # P4 Rule: Expedite Review must follow mandatory checks and precede approval
    if 'Expedite Review' in events:
        exp_idx = events.index('Expedite Review')
        cc_idx = events.index('Credit Check') if 'Credit Check' in events else 999
        tr_idx = events.index('Technical Review') if 'Technical Review' in events else 999
        if exp_idx < cc_idx or exp_idx < tr_idx or exp_idx > app_idx:
            return False, "Violation P4: Expedite Review must follow mandatory checks and precede approval."

    # P5 Rule: Exactly one approval
    approvals = [e for e in events if e in ('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval')]
    if len(approvals) != 1:
        return False, "Violation P5: Exactly one approval activity is permitted."

    # P7 Rule: Unprovoked repeated checks
    for check in ['Credit Check', 'Technical Review']:
        indices = [i for i, e in enumerate(events) if e == check]
        if len(indices) > 1:
            for idx in indices[1:]:
                # Check if a 'Correct Dossier' preceded this specific re-check
                if 'Correct Dossier' not in events[:idx]:
                    return False, f"Violation P7: Unprovoked repeated check '{check}' without preceding Correct Dossier."

    # Contextual Attribute Rule (if log contains extended data)
    if attributes:
        if attributes.get('is_external') is False and 'Supplier Review' in events:
            return False, "Violation P3: Supplier Review present on internal order."
            
    return True, "Compliant"
```

### Attribute Limitations Analysis
1. **Missing `is_external`**: Forces the control-flow model to treat `Supplier Review` as optional (`X(Supplier Review, tau)`) for all traces. This allows internal orders to run `Supplier Review` without triggering control-flow fitness penalties, delegating strict filtering to the declarative policy wrapper.
2. **Missing `affected_check`**: Forces the loop block after `Correct Dossier` to allow either `Credit Check`, `Technical Review`, or both. This prevents rejecting legitimate single-check corrections (resolving Model C's defect in B6).

# 9. Validation Experiments

To replace reliance on unstratified metrics, we specify a formal evaluation framework:

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                           VALIDATION SUITE ARCHITECTURE                           |
|                                                                                   |
|  [ Event Log ] ---> Stratified Split (Seed: 42) --+--> Training Set (80%)         |
|                                                   +--> Holdout Set (20%)          |
|                                                                                   |
|  [ Evaluation Test Benches ]                                                      |
|    Bench A: Curated Negative Trace Suite (Policy Violations)                   |
|    Bench B: Perturbation Suite (Missing/Duplicate Event Injection)             |
|    Bench C: Alignment-Cost Sensitivity Matrix                                  |
|    Bench D: Standard Metrics (Disaggregated Fitness, Precision, Simplicity)    |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Experimental Setup and Reproducibility Protocol

1. **Stratified Split**: Train/Holdout splits must use a fixed random seed (`seed=42`) and stratify across explicit trace features (`is_external`, `approval_type`, `has_correction`).
2. **Negative Trace Benchmark**: Evaluate models against synthetic non-compliant traces (e.g., B3, B4, B8) to compute true Negative Rejection Rate (NRR).
3. **Perturbation Test Suite**: Inject random event drops (10%) and duplicate events (10%) into holdout traces to evaluate model stability under log noise.
4. **Alignment-Cost Sensitivity**: Compute alignment fitness across varying model-move penalty matrices ($C_{model} \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$).
5. **Human Comprehension Protocol**: Measure domain-expert comprehension using timed tasks (e.g., path identification, rule-checking) administered to $N=10$ process engineers.

### Executable PM4Py Test Bench Skeleton

```python
import pm4py
import numpy as np

def run_reproducible_eval_suite(log, model_tree, seed=42):
    """
    Executes a stratified holdout validation benchmark for process tree models.
    """
    # 1. Soundness Check
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_tree)
    sound = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm)
    if not sound:
        raise ValueError("Model failed soundness evaluation! Deployment halted.")

    # 2. Convert Log and Perform Split
    # Stratification logic wrapper (simulated over log traces)
    np.random.seed(seed)
    traces = list(log)
    indices = np.random.permutation(len(traces))
    split_idx = int(0.8 * len(traces))
    
    train_traces = [traces[i] for i in indices[:split_idx]]
    holdout_traces = [traces[i] for i in indices[split_idx:]]

    # 3. Disaggregated Metric Computations
    fit_train = pm4py.fitness_alignments(train_traces, net, im, fm)['log_fitness']
    fit_holdout = pm4py.fitness_alignments(holdout_traces, net, im, fm)['log_fitness']
    prec_train = pm4py.precision_alignments(train_traces, net, im, fm)
    gen_tbr = pm4py.generalization_tbr(train_traces, net, im, fm)
    simp_arc = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm, variant="arc_degree")

    results = {
        "is_sound": sound,
        "train_fitness": fit_train,
        "holdout_fitness": fit_holdout,
        "train_precision": prec_train,
        "generalization": gen_tbr,
        "simplicity": simp_arc
    }
    
    return results

def run_negative_trace_benchmark(net, im, fm, negative_traces):
    """
    Evaluates model's ability to reject non-compliant traces (True Negative Rate).
    """
    rejected = 0
    for trace in negative_traces:
        # Align negative trace; fitness < 1.0 indicates rejection/alignment penalty
        fit = pm4py.fitness_alignments([trace], net, im, fm)['log_fitness']
        if fit < 0.95:  # Strict threshold for non-conformance rejection
            rejected += 1
    
    tnr = rejected / len(negative_traces)
    return tnr

# 11. Dissenting View

### Rebuttal to Governance Recommendation

An auditor or risk control manager could argue that deploying `model_third` introduces unacceptable compliance risks. Because the control-flow log lacks explicit attributes for `is_external` and `affected_check`, `model_third` relies on optional branching (`tau`) for `Supplier Review` and post-correction re-checks.

In a strict regulatory environment, any model that permits a trace through automated mechanisms without hard control-flow blocks creates exposure. An auditor may argue that **Model C is the only compliant option** for formal verification: even if Model C rejects unseen valid variants, rejection forces a manual audit review, ensuring zero unvetted traces pass through automatically. From this perspective, operational friction is preferred over automated non-compliance, making Model C's low holdout fitness an acceptable cost of strict enforcement.

```
                      Risk vs. Flexibility Tension
                      
   Model S                   model_third                   Model C
   (Permissive)              (Balanced)                    (Strict/Rigid)

 High Compliance Risk                       High Operational Friction
 (Allows Violations)                         (Rejects Unseen Valid Traces)

### Evidentiary Conditions Required to Shift Decision

To change our decision and adopt a different model architecture, the following evidence would be required:

1. **Log Enrichment**: Re-extracting the event logs to include `is_external` and `affected_check` data fields. With these attributes present, `model_third` could be replaced by a fully constrained, attribute-conditioned model family, eliminating the need for optional `tau` branches.
2. **Empirical Negative Log Data**: Providing an audited historical set of confirmed fraudulent traces. If `model_third` fails to achieve $\ge 0.99$ rejection on these empirical traces, its control-flow boundaries would need to be tightened further.
3. **Operational Cost Metrics**: Demonstrating that the financial cost of manual audit reviews caused by Model C's rejection of unseen valid variants is lower than the risk-adjusted cost of automated processing under `model_third`.
