The shared mechanism is queueing-theoretic and site-independent: if two tasks with no data dependency truly execute in overlapping intervals, the verification phase's critical path shrinks from the sum toward the maximum of the two service times, reducing waiting before Assessment and hence cycle time. That arrow chain — treatment to true overlap to waiting to cycle time — is the only part I would transport with confidence.

Site-specific mechanisms sit on the arrows into `true_overlap` and out of `waiting_before_assessment`. At A, digital documents, cross-trained reviewers, a cooperative case system, and low API latency make the treatment translate almost fully into true overlap. At B, selection nodes fire on nearly every enabler: originals requirements block early Document Check for 62% of cases, the case lock blocks simultaneous ownership, and specialization prevents capacity from flowing between queues. Downstream, B adds legal capacity and the supplement loop, mechanisms absent at A.

The effect modifiers are `originals_required`, `digital_documents`, `case_complexity`, `IT_case_lock`, `role_specialization`, and the four capacity nodes: they do not merely shift baselines, they change the treatment effect itself, from near-A magnitude (digital, non-legal cases) to zero or negative (originals-plus-legal cases hitting a saturated assessor pool).

A similar control-flow diagram guarantees nothing because control flow encodes only precedence. The effect is produced by resources, data prerequisites, IT mechanics, and queue policy — none of which appear in the process tree, and all of which differ across these sites.

## 5. Quantitative transport scenarios

Reasoning that maps the evidence to the ranges. At B, sequential verification costs roughly identity (3.4h) + document (8.1h) = 11.5h of service on the critical path; perfect overlap reduces that to max = 8.1h, a ceiling of ~3.4h median saving per fully eligible case, before any queueing effects. That ceiling applies cleanly only to the ~38% digital cases; for the 62% originals cases the DFG shows identity often already runs before originals arrive (Register→Identity 2,050 vs. Register→Originals 1,860; Identity→Originals 1,020), so the incremental gain there is perhaps 0.5–1.5h from formalizing the remaining cases. A's observed 9h median drop is not the treatment effect: after subtracting mix shift (~2–4h plausible), +11% FTE, API latency, and huddles, a defensible A treatment effect on the median is perhaps 2–4h — consistent with the min-service-time logic. Downstream, 3 assessors already sustain a 20h wait; utilization is high, so arrival smoothing matters and earlier completions can lengthen that queue, hitting the tail hardest (the bottleneck-shift scenario). Legal (2 FTE, 40% of cases, 26% supplement rate) similarly caps tail gains and creates the congestion scenario.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

**Do not copy Site A.** The modified intervention, "Segmented Early-Start Verification":

- **Eligibility rules.** Wave 1: cases that are digital-at-registration AND not legal-track (~25–38% of volume). Wave 2 (conditional on wave-1 success): originals-required, non-legal cases, with the originals-aware variant below. Legal-track cases enter only in wave 3 with the checkpoint variant.
- **Concurrent task creation/execution.** For eligible digital cases: create Identity Check and Document Check at Register; they may execute concurrently by different reviewers. For originals-required cases: create Identity Check at Register; create Document Check only upon Originals Receipt (system event), so Identity Check runs in parallel with the originals wait and, once originals arrive, with any remaining identity work.
- **Statutory prerequisites that remain mandatory.** Originals Receipt strictly precedes Document Check when originals are legally required; Assessment strictly requires completion of both checks; Legal Review remains mandatory for public-sector/high-value cases; supplement handling follows the statutory loop. No statutory ordering is relaxed.
- **Queue and WIP controls.** Per-reviewer cap of 5 open verification tasks (adapting A's rule of 6 to B's specialization); a stratum-level admission valve: if median waiting-before-Assessment exceeds 24h for 5 consecutive working days, pause new parallel releases. Daily queue huddles adopted explicitly as a named co-intervention from day one in both pilot and control groups (so they do not confound).
- **Resource responsibilities.** Identity pool (4 FTE) owns Identity Check; document pool (7 FTE) owns Document Check; simultaneous ownership of one case by one reviewer from each pool is enabled by the IT change below. Assessors and legal reviewers unchanged.
- **Legal-review and supplement loops.** For legal-track cases (wave 3): Document Check produces a versioned checkpoint; if Request Supplement occurs, only the delta is re-verified, and the re-check event is logged as `Document Check (supplement)` to keep loop iterations distinguishable.
- **Logging requirements.** IT precondition: dual active ownership per case with system-generated start/complete events; manual timestamp back-entry disabled for pilot cases; every event carries a source flag (system vs. manual); Originals Receipt logged as its own event.

Partial-order relation set (per-case dependencies; `<` = must precede):

```text
Nodes: Register, Identity Check, [Originals Receipt], Document Check,
       Assessment, [Legal Review, {Request Supplement, Document Check', Assessment'}*],
       X(Approve, Reject), Notify

Register < Identity Check
Register < Originals Receipt                       (originals-required cases)
Originals Receipt < Document Check                 (originals-required cases)
Register < Document Check                          (digital cases; no other predecessor)
Identity Check < Assessment
Document Check < Assessment
Assessment < Legal Review                          (legal-track cases)
Legal Review < Request Supplement                  (0..n loop iterations)
Request Supplement < Document Check' < Assessment' < Legal Review   (loop body)
{Assessment | last Legal Review} < X(Approve, Reject) < Notify

Unordered (concurrent) pairs:
Identity Check || Document Check                   (digital cases)
Identity Check || Originals Receipt                (originals cases)
Identity Check || Document Check                   (originals cases, after receipt, if identity still open)
```

## 7. Pilot for transport learning

| design element | specification | causal purpose | operational safeguard | process-mining measurement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| unit of assignment | case, randomized at Register within eligible strata | avoids team-level confounding; cases share pools anyway, so team randomization would not prevent interference | routing rule embedded in registration system, not left to clerks | assignment flag as case attribute in the event log |
| randomization / phasing | wave 1: 50/50 randomization within digital non-legal stratum; waves 2–3 phased (stepped-wedge) into originals and legal strata | wave 1 identifies the causal effect where the mechanism is cleanest; stepped wedge identifies stratum-specific effects with time controls | waves gated on wave-1 stop rules | per-wave, per-stratum cohorts comparable in the log |
| eligible strata | (digital × non-legal), (originals × non-legal), (legal-track), each split by complexity | strata are the effect modifiers from Section 4; effects are expected to differ by stratum | complex legal cases enter last | stratum recorded as case attributes; variant analysis per stratum |
| treatment versions | V1 full parallel at Register (digital); V2 identity-early + document-on-receipt (originals); V3 checkpointed verification (legal) | distinguishes mechanism variants so failure of one version does not condemn the others | each version has its own task-creation rule in the system | distinct task-creation event types per version |
| primary outcomes | median case cycle time; median waiting-before-Assessment; per stratum | the transported mechanism should move exactly these | none needed | timestamp differences from system-generated events |
| secondary outcomes | p90 cycle time, rework %, supplement-loop iterations, appeal % (90-day), throughput per FTE | detect tail harm, quality harm, and bottleneck shift | guardrail thresholds pre-registered | loop counting via repeated-activity detection; queue sojourns from enablement-to-start times |
| interference controls | shared identity/document/assessor pools monitored: utilization and queue length by group; capacity ring-fencing if control-group waits rise | treated cases can slow controls through shared queues (SUTVA violation); must be measured, not assumed away | if control-group waiting-before-Assessment rises >15% vs. its own baseline, pause enrollment | group-tagged queue metrics from the log |
| power / minimum support | wave-1 stratum ≈ 350–500 cases/quarter; ~200 per arm detects a 2h median shift against ~10h IQR at 80% power (rank-based test); minimum 150 cases/arm before any interim look | prevents declaring success/failure on noise | no interim decisions before minimum support | case counts per variant monitored continuously |
| duration | 2 quarters wave 1; 1 quarter each subsequent wave; appeal outcome tracked 90 days beyond | covers seasonality and the appeal window | calendar pre-committed | log window aligned to enrollment dates |
| stop rules | stop stratum if: rework +3pp over baseline, or p90 +10h, or legal queue sojourn +25%, or timestamp-audit failure (>10% back-entered events) | converts Section 4 failure modes into tripwires | automatic weekly guardrail report | audit of event source flags; conformance checking of treated cases against the intended partial order |
| transportability analysis | estimate stratum-specific effects at B; reweight by A's mix to check whether A's result is recoverable; compare measured true overlap vs. intended overlap | separates "mechanism works at B" from "A's number was mix and co-interventions" | — | overlap % computed from interval intersection of system-generated events only |

The pilot separates mechanism failure from implementation failure by measuring the mechanism's intermediate variable — true overlap of verification intervals from system-generated timestamps — independently of the outcome. If overlap does not materialize (lock workarounds, task-creation rules ignored, back-entered events flagged by the audit), that is implementation failure: the treatment was never delivered, and the fix is IT and management practice, not abandonment. Conformance checking of treated cases against the intended partial order quantifies this fidelity per case. If overlap does materialize but cycle time does not improve, that is mechanism failure at B — and the queue metrics say why: rising waiting-before-Assessment indicates bottleneck shift to the 3-assessor pool; rising supplement-loop iterations indicate the early-check/rework interaction; flat everything indicates the parallelizable window was already being exploited informally (as the baseline DFG suggests). Randomization within strata protects against the confounding that invalidated Site A's before/after evidence: mix shifts, capacity additions, and the huddle co-intervention are either held equal across arms or deliberately applied to both. Finally, the stratified stepped-wedge design turns effect-modifier hypotheses into estimable contrasts, so a failure in the legal stratum with success in the digital stratum yields a segmentation decision rather than a global verdict.

## 8. PM4Py model-construction code

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 1) Site A process trees (before / after) via parse_process_tree
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
site_a_before = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "-> ( 'Register', 'Identity Check', 'Document Check', 'Assessment', "
    "X ( 'Approve', 'Reject' ), 'Notify' )"
)
site_a_after = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "-> ( 'Register', + ( 'Identity Check', 'Document Check' ), 'Assessment', "
    "X ( 'Approve', 'Reject' ), 'Notify' )"
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 2) Convert both Site A trees to accepting Petri nets
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
net_a_before, im_a_before, fm_a_before = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(site_a_before)
net_a_after,  im_a_after,  fm_a_after  = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(site_a_after)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 3) Site B candidate as a POWL model
#    Structure: strict partial order over Register, Identity Check,
#    optional Originals Receipt, Document Check, Assessment,
#    optional Legal Review with a supplement loop, decision, Notify.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
register   = Transition(label="Register")
identity   = Transition(label="Identity Check")
originals  = Transition(label="Originals Receipt")
doc_check  = Transition(label="Document Check")
assessment = Transition(label="Assessment")
approve    = Transition(label="Approve")
reject     = Transition(label="Reject")
notify     = Transition(label="Notify")

# Originals Receipt is required only for ~62% of cases (data-dependent);
# control flow can only express it as an exclusive choice with a skip.
originals_opt = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                             children=[originals, SilentTransition()])

# Supplement loop body: Request Supplement, then repeated Document Check
# and Assessment (distinct labels keep loop iterations visible in traces).
req_supp   = Transition(label="Request Supplement")
doc_redo   = Transition(label="Document Check (supplement)")
asmt_redo  = Transition(label="Assessment (supplement)")
loop_body  = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[req_supp, doc_redo, asmt_redo])
loop_body.order.add_edge(req_supp, doc_redo)
loop_body.order.add_edge(doc_redo, asmt_redo)

# LOOP: do = Legal Review, redo = supplement body (0..n iterations)
legal      = Transition(label="Legal Review")
legal_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[legal, loop_body])

# Legal review applies only to public-sector / high-value cases (~40%):
# again expressible only as XOR with a silent skip.
legal_opt  = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                          children=[legal_loop, SilentTransition()])

decision   = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[approve, reject])

powl_b = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, identity, originals_opt, doc_check,
                                   assessment, legal_opt, decision, notify])

# 4) Explicit order edges. Identity Check and Document Check are
#    UNORDERED w.r.t. each other (concurrency), but Assessment has
#    incoming edges from BOTH, so it waits for both verifications.
powl_b.order.add_edge(register, identity)
powl_b.order.add_edge(register, originals_opt)
powl_b.order.add_edge(originals_opt, doc_check)   # statutory: originals before doc check
powl_b.order.add_edge(identity, assessment)       # Assessment waits for identity...
powl_b.order.add_edge(doc_check, assessment)      # ...AND for document verification
powl_b.order.add_edge(assessment, legal_opt)
powl_b.order.add_edge(legal_opt, decision)
powl_b.order.add_edge(decision, notify)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 5) Convert POWL to an accepting Petri net
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
net_b, im_b, fm_b = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(powl_b)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 6) Workflow-net / soundness checks
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
for name, (net, im, fm) in {
    "Site A before": (net_a_before, im_a_before, fm_a_before),
    "Site A after":  (net_a_after,  im_a_after,  fm_a_after),
    "Site B POWL":   (net_b,        im_b,        fm_b),
}.items():
    is_wfnet = pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net)
    soundness = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm)
    print(f"{name}: workflow net = {is_wfnet}, sound = {soundness[0]}")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 7) Optional playout: sample traces from the Site B model
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
sim_log = pm4py.play_out(net_b, im_b, fm_b)
for i, trace in enumerate(sim_log[:5]):
    print(f"trace {i}:", [event["concept:name"] for event in trace])

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 8) What this model CANNOT encode:
# - Data-dependent routing: whether originals are legally required (62%)
#   and whether legal review applies (40%) depend on case attributes;
#   the XOR-with-skip makes these choices nondeterministic, so playout
#   frequencies will not match reality and conformance checking cannot
#   penalize a digital case that "skips" Originals Receipt lawfully vs.
#   an originals case that skips it unlawfully.
```

## 9. Recommendation

`conditional go`

**What should be transferred.** The structural idea only: remove the artificial sequential dependency between identity and document verification wherever no statutory or data prerequisite forbids it, combined with Site A's two genuinely portable operational practices — a per-reviewer WIP cap and daily queue huddles. The huddles and cap should be adopted explicitly and separately, because at Site A they were co-interventions whose effect is currently laundered into the parallelization headline.

**What should not be copied.** The blanket rule "create both tasks at Register for every case." For 62% of Site B's cases, Document Check legally cannot start before Originals Receipt, so the copied rule would create idle tasks and WIP noise; for the 40% legal-track cases, early document checks risk being invalidated by supplements and re-loaded onto a 7-FTE document pool; and under the legacy case lock the intervention is not even executable. Site A's 9-hour median improvement must not be used as the business case: it bundles a mix shift toward simpler claims, an 11% capacity increase, an API upgrade, and huddles.

**Which subgroup enters first.** Digital-at-registration, non-legal cases — roughly a quarter to a third of volume — under case-level randomization, after the IT dual-ownership fix and logging discipline are verified.

**Expected benefits and risks.** A realistic median gain of 2–3.5 hours within the first stratum (about 1 hour on the overall median), with negligible tail improvement, since Site B's p90 is governed by originals delay, legal capacity, and supplement loops. Principal risks: bottleneck shift into the 3-assessor pool (median improves, p90 worsens), rework creep above the 17% baseline, and fake overlap from back-entered timestamps.

**Triggers.** Expand to originals-required cases if the first stratum shows a ≥2h median gain with rework within +1.5pp and stable assessment waiting. Modify (capacity or checkpointing) if true overlap is achieved but waiting-before-Assessment or legal sojourn absorbs the gain. Abandon if, with verified implementation fidelity, cycle time is flat and rework rises — evidence that the mechanism itself does not transport.

## 10. Assumption register

1. Identity Check and Document Check at Site B use operationally independent data and can be validly executed concurrently — why needed: concurrency is only lawful and meaningful if neither check consumes the other's output; how to test: sample 50 cases with domain experts and trace data inputs of each check; consequence if false: parallelization produces wrong-order verifications, rework, and possible legal defects, and the intervention must be dropped.
2. The legacy case-lock can be modified to allow two simultaneous active task owners within the pilot budget and timeline — why needed: without it the treatment cannot be delivered at all; how to test: IT feasibility spike delivering dual ownership in a test environment before wave 1; consequence if false: the pilot becomes the IT-lock failure scenario (placebo treatment, corrupted logs) and must not start.
3. System-generated timestamps in the pilot scope reflect true task start/complete within minutes, with back-entry disabled — why needed: true overlap is the manipulation check separating mechanism from implementation failure; how to test: audit event source flags and compare a sample against observed work; consequence if false: overlap and duration metrics are unusable and no causal conclusion can be drawn.
4. The assessor pool (3 FTE) has enough slack, or an agreed overflow mechanism, to absorb earlier case arrivals — why needed: otherwise verification gains relocate into the assessment queue; how to test: measure assessor utilization and queue sojourn at baseline and weekly during the pilot; consequence if false: bottleneck-shift scenario — median flat or slightly better, p90 worse — requiring capacity action or scope reduction.
5. Site A's median improvement is substantially attributable to co-interventions and mix shift, so Site B should expect at most 2–4h from the mechanism itself — why needed: sets honest success thresholds and power calculations; how to test: reweight Site A's after-period by its before-period complexity mix and decompose the change; consequence if false (true effect larger): the pilot is conservatively powered and will still detect it — a benign error.
6. Digital, non-legal cases at Site B are identifiable at Register with high accuracy — why needed: eligibility routing and stratified randomization depend on classifying cases at intake; how to test: retrospective classification of one month of closed cases against ground truth; consequence if false: strata are contaminated, effects are diluted, and legally constrained cases may be parallelized unlawfully.
7. Early document checking in legal-track cases does not increase supplement-triggered full re-verification beyond checkpointing's ability to contain it — why needed: wave 3's viability rests on delta re-verification working; how to test: measure re-check scope per supplement in a small wave-3 sample before scaling; consequence if false: legal/rework congestion scenario; legal-track cases stay permanently on the sequential path.
8. Treated and control cases sharing the same reviewer pools do not materially interfere (control outcomes are not degraded by treated-case WIP) — why needed: randomized-comparison validity (SUTVA); how to test: track control-group queue waits against their own pre-pilot baseline; consequence if false: the pilot overstates the treatment effect, and design must switch to time-sliced or pool-separated assignment.
