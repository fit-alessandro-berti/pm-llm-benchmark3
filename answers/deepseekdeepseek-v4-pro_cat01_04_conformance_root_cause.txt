The remaining nonconformance (missing `Compliance Check`, `Manual Override`) indicates either a logging gap or genuine behavioural deviation.

From August, **model obsolescence** adds another layer: the policydriven `Supervisor Signoff` is absent from the normative tree, so even after artefact cleaning, fitness drops to 66%. Adding an optional signoff lifts it to 86%, confirming that the model no longer reflects the mandated process, not that the process has gone out of control.

Business outcomes stayed stable because the underlying operational workflow changed little; the artefacts distorted the recorded trace without altering the real approval/review logic. However, **stable KPIs do not prove the data are perfect nor do they rule out smallscale noncompliance** – the unresolved missing compliance events (4–5% of enhanced reviews) and `Manual Override` could mask genuine bypasses, especially if compliance detection is incomplete. Multiple mechanisms thus coproduce the observed pattern: artefacts dominate the measured fitness collapse, while a modest real concern (possibly noncompliance) hides in the residual noise, obscured by the very artefacts we removed.

## 7. Provisional conclusion

**Primary explanation for the measured conformance collapse.**  
The sudden drop in fitting traces from 93% (AprilMay) to 32% (JuneJuly) is overwhelmingly an artefact of three concurrent logging and datapipeline changes, not a massive operational breakdown. First, the UI label change from `Classify` to `Risk Screen` created logonly moves that the model could not match. Second, the injection of the technical event `Queue Routed` inserted a step that had no counterpart in the normative tree. Third, the ETL’s switch to warehouseingestion timestamps reversed the order of `Decision` and `Notify` in a subset of traces. Progressive normalisation—label mapping, removing the technical event, and ordering by source sequence—restored fitness to 89%, very close to the baseline. The later AugustSeptember decline (to 66% after the same cleaning) is explained by model obsolescence: the introduction of a mandatory `Supervisor Signoff` for highvalue claims made the normative process tree incomplete. Adding an optional signoff raised fitness to 86%, confirming that the model no longer reflects the mandated process. I assess **high confidence (~85%)** that these artefacts and the model gap account for over 90% of the conformance loss.

**Separate assessment of a possible real compliance problem.**  
Even after all normalisation, 4–5% of enhancedreview traces still lack a recorded `Compliance Check` and a small number contain `Manual Override`. Whether these reflect genuine bypasses or missing events cannot be determined without a targeted audit. Business outcomes (appeals, approvals, cycle time) remained stable, which suggests that if noncompliance exists, it is small and has not yet affected overall quality. However, the compliance department’s own warning of incomplete detection means we cannot rule out a modest but real compliance gap. My confidence that an actual compliance problem exists is **moderate (around 40%)**—the signals are weak and could equally be a logging completeness issue.

**Immediate actions.**
- **IT**: formalise the eventsemantic contract; rerun conformance using sourcesequence ordering and a clean event dictionary; remove `Queue Routed` from logs if confirmed purely technical.
- **Process owners**: accelerate the investigation of the 55–60 enhancedreview cases without a recorded check by manually tracing the decision in source systems; decide whether `Manual Override` should be a modelled step or a compliance incident.
- **Compliance**: expand incident detection to cover controlflow deviations beyond appeal rate; commission a reviewerlevel analysis to see if deviations cluster.
- **What must not be claimed yet**: that the conformance drop proves a major compliance failure, or conversely that employees are fully compliant simply because appeals are unchanged. The data tell us the measurement system broke; they do not yet fully exonerate the process.

## 8. Model-governance recommendation

1. **Version every process model and tie it to a release calendar.** When a new operational policy or system version is deployed, the corresponding process model must be updated and versioned before conformance dashboards go live.
2. **Define an explicit eventsemantic contract.** Each event type must carry a stable semantic identifier (e.g., a business activity code) that persists across UI label changes, so conformance checks use canonical names, not screen labels.
3. **Prohibit technical events from the conformancerelevant log.** Gatewaygenerated events such as `Queue Routed` must be flagged and excluded by default; their inclusion must require a documented justification that they represent a real process step.
4. **Anchor timestamp provenance to sourcesystem sequence numbers.** Conformance pipelines must use the earliest immutable order stamp (e.g., source sequence number or original transaction timestamp) for ordering, and store warehousetime only as metadata.
5. **Incorporate dataaware constraints in the conformance model.** When a rule depends on a case attribute (e.g., signoff only for highvalue claims), the conformance model should include that guard or be supplemented with attributebased postprocessing; a pure controlflow model that merely makes the step optional is overpermissive and masks violations.
6. **Automate postrelease validation.** After every UI, ETL, or policy change, run a predefined conformance smoketest against a regression suite of known traces; an alert must fire if fitness falls below a threshold, triggering a modelevent reconciliation before the change contaminates operational reporting.
