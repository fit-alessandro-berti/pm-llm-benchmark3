## 5. Risks and compensating controls

**Auditability gaps**  
- Risk: If automation logs are not integrated with core banking audit trails, compliance cannot verify decision chains.  
- Compensating control: Mandatory audit trails for every model suggestion, human override, and final decision, timestamped with user, rationale, and activity ID. Random sampling and monthend compliance reports enforced by the foureyes governance board.

**False positives/negatives**  
- Risk (A3): KYC hit preclassifier may miss hits (false negatives) or flag too many (false positives), increasing exception load and customer friction.  
- Compensating control: All flagged cases require mandatory human escalation; dualreview gate before any process step proceeds; periodic review of falsenegative/positive rates with threshold adjustments.

**Model drift**  
- Risk (A2, A6, A9): Classification recommender, financialstatement normalization, and contractrule library may degrade over time as loan products, customer profiles, or regulations change.  
- Compensating control: Scheduled model retraining quarterly with new data; manual spotchecks monthly; rulelibrary version control with compliance signoff for any changes; regression testing in a sandbox before production deployment.

**Exception handling overload**  
- Risk (A4, A5, A11): Entitymismatch assistant has limited coverage (38% rework rate on 1750 cases); customer nonresponse dominates missingdoc loops (52% repeated); loan activation bot cannot operate if controls incomplete, causing pipeline stalls.  
- Compensating control: Defined SLAs for exception resolution (e.g., entity mismatch 24h, missing documents 48h customer response) with escalation to senior specialists (compliance 3 FTE). Fallback manual paths embedded in all automation; SLA breach triggers automatic case rerouting to human queue.

**Foureyes compliance**  
- Risk: Automation may inadvertently complete credit analysis and committee packaging by the same person for Highexposure cases during monthend rush, violating the 5.8% violation rate.  
- Compensating control: Systemenforced role separation at activitylevel permissions; audit logs of who performed which step; monthend automated reconciliation report; any override requires senior compliance signoff and is logged for audit.

**External valuation dependency**  
- Risk (A7): Triage cannot control valuer quality or timing; may create disputes or SLA breaches, extending the 58h wait.  
- Compensating control: Parallel submission to multiple valuers where possible; SLA tracking dashboard with valuer performance metrics; alternative valuation methods (desktop analysis) for lowrisk cases; escalation to senior collateral team if SLA breached beyond threshold.

**Customer friction**  
- Risk (A5, A10): Portal nudging and esignature adoption may increase customer complaints or dropoff if instructions are rigid or identity issues arise; RM resistance to changes that increase customer effort.  
- Compensating control: Flexible instruction design with multiple channels (email, portal, RM verbal); customer feedback loop integrated into case notes; RM oversight dashboard to monitor adoption rates and complaint trends; A10 adoption limited to cases with completed KYC and contract to mitigate identity risk.

## 7. Final recommendation

**Recommended portfolio: Portfolio 2 – Bottleneck/Synchronization (A4, A6, A7, A8)**

**Defensible tradeoff argument why the highestfrequency activity should not be automated first**

The bank’s initial instinct to automate the highestfrequency activity (*Receive application  Register application*, 12000 cases) is understandable but processmining evidence shows that **frequency alone is misleading** for endtoend performance. The processmining artifacts reveal that total cycle time is dominated by synchronization delays and rework in lowerfrequency steps:

- **Waiting times:** Median waiting time before KYC check is 4h, before Financial document review is 18h, before External valuation is 58h, and before Committee packaging is 26h. These four steps alone account for a combined median wait of ~106h per case, far exceeding the 3minute service time of registration.
- **Criticalpath blockers:** *Resolve entity mismatch* (1750 freq, 31h wait, 38% rework) is on the critical path in 82% of cases when present and blocks KYC and signing. *Wait for external valuation* (2350 freq, 58h wait) is required for 71% of Equipment finance and 18% of Workingcapital cases, creating a synchronization point that dictates committeepackaging timing.
- **Rework loops:** Financial document review has a 24% exception rate; *Request missing documents* has a 52% repeated rework rate; *Committee packaging* waits for KYC status, normalized financial statements, collateral valuation, and creditanalysis notes, creating conditional delays.
- **Control findings:** Foureyes violations (5.8% Highexposure monthend), KYCclearancebeforesigning provisional signing (6.5%), and contracttermmatch remediation loops (8.5% overall, 14% Creditline renewal) are all linked to these synchronization points, not to registration.

Automating registration (A1) would save labour on 12000 cases but would **accelerate case inflow into alreadyconstrained downstream queues** (KYC ops, credit analysts 9 FTE, compliance 3 FTE, external valuers) without reducing total cycle time. Processmining data shows back office utilization currently falls below 65% after 15:00, but increased WIP from faster registration could shift utilization patterns and exacerbate analyst overload (resource note warns that automation increasing clean inflow to analysts may overload them unless normalization is improved). Moreover, misclassification from a hasty A2 implementation could route cases to wrong control paths, increasing foureyes violations.

In contrast, the **Bottleneck/Synchronization portfolio directly targets the activities and DFG relations that dominate endtoend performance**:  
- A4 unblocks the 31h entitymismatch wait and 38% rework, preventing KYC and signing blockages.  
- A6 speeds the 20h financialnormalization wait before credit analysis, enabling faster clean inflow without overloading the 9 FTE analysts when paired with capacity planning.  
- A7 triages the 58h externalvaluation wait, reducing idle time and parallelizing valuer engagement for the 2350 cases that traverse it.  
- A8 autoassembles committee packaging (26h wait) when artifacts are clean, reducing garbagein/garbageout rework.

This portfolio also advances the compliancefirst mandate: A4 and A6 improve entity and data quality that underpin the foureyes principle and contractterm matching; A8’s autoassembly must enforce role separation and audit logs to curb the 8.5% remediation loops. The quickwin portfolio (A1, A2, A10, A5) can follow in Phase 12 of the rollout **after** bottleneck alignment, ensuring downstream capacity is aligned and control risks are mitigated.

**Final recommendation:** Proceed with the Bottleneck/Synchronization portfolio (A4, A6, A7, A8) as the primary focus for the first year, supported by the phased rollout plan in Section 6. This choice maximizes endtoend cycletime reduction, directly addresses the synchronization delays and control risks that dominate performance, and preserves compliance integrity—without the unintended consequences of automating highfrequency steps before upstream bottlenecks and controls are stabilized.
