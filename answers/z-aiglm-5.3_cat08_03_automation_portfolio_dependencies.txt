## 3. Portfolio alternatives

**P1 — Quick-win (A1, A2, A5, A10).** Optimizes visible labor savings, intake speed, the doc loop, and signing reminders with high-feasibility modules. **P4 — Flow-and-controls (A5, A6, A9, A10)** *(recommended candidate; rationale in §7).* Optimizes the highest-volume rework loops and back-half waits while enforcing the two controls automation must not erode. Sacrifices: the entity-mismatch critical path (31h, ~15% of cases) and Equipment-finance external wait (58h) wait for wave 2; classification error risk persists until A2; four-eyes enforcement is deferred (pressure reduced indirectly).

## 7. Final recommendation

**Recommend P4: A5 + A6 + A9 + A10.** It is the only four-module set that simultaneously (a) removes the two highest-volume rework loops (52% doc loop, 8.5% contract remediation), (b) attacks the largest aggregate waiting segments (normalization 20h, contract 22h, signing 44h × 7,300), (c) satisfies compliance's mandate with genuine control-strengthening modules (A9's decision-match rules and KYC gate; A10's signing gate), and (d) respects the two-deep-integration limit.

**On the highest-frequency activity:** registering applications (11,880 cases) should *not* be the first automation. The process-mining evidence is unambiguous: this process is **waiting-dominated, not touch-dominated** — signing has 0 minutes of service and 44h of waiting; external valuation 0 minutes and 58h; document review 45 minutes and 18h. Registration's 8 minutes and 1.5h wait are trivial in comparison, and the back-office "queue" is an arrival-pattern artifact — utilization already falls below 65% after 15:00, so the fix is rescheduling, not a bot. Worse, automating registration first would accelerate inflow into the two genuinely scarce pools — the 3-FTE compliance specialists (hub-dependent on two seniors) and the 9-FTE analysts — which the social-network analysis explicitly identifies as the overload risk. A1 retains real value (duplicate detection is control-positive; the labor saving is real) and enters wave 2 once flow is stabilized.

The same logic defers A11: automating the final control checkpoint while 6.5% of signings are provisional and 8.5% of contracts need remediation would automate the consumption of unreliable control signals. A9 and A10 are deployed as *enforcing* gates — they block non-compliant flow rather than assume it — which is precisely what makes later automation (A11, and eventually A1's full auto-registration) safe.

Honest trade-offs: Equipment-finance cycle time stays capped by the 58h external valuation (A7, wave 2, enabled by A2's early collateral trigger); ~15% of cases still face the 31h entity-mismatch critical path (A3+A4 is the wave-2 headliner, protecting the 3-FTE specialist pool); and four-eyes enforcement waits for A8/A12 — mitigated in year 1 because smoother, less month-end-concentrated flow removes the rush conditions in which the 5.8% violations occur. Frequency tells you where the work is; the DFG, waiting times, loops, and resource networks tell you where the *performance* is — and the portfolio should follow the latter.
