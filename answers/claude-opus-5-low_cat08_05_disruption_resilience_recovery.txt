# Disruption, Resilience and Recovery Analysis — Pharmaceutical Wholesaler Cyber Incident

## 1. Disruption timeline

**T0 — Day -30 to Day -1 (baseline, 42,000 cases).**
Operational state: stable, system-mediated order-to-delivery. The DFG is essentially a single dominant variant (START → Receive order → Validate authorization → Inventory reservation → Pick → Pack → Quality/release check → Dispatch → Proof of delivery → Invoice close), carrying ~36,800 of 42,000 cases to invoice close. The only structural side-paths are controlled, modelled exceptions: Compliance hold (700, of which 520 return to validation), Backorder wait (900 → 600 pick / 300 cancel) and Repack/recheck (450 → 400 back to quality check). Risk posture: low and *measured* — traceability exceptions 0.8%, cold-chain documentation gaps 0.4%, controlled-substance dual-control gaps 0.2%, rework 3.5%, cancellations 2.1%. Conformance fitness 0.96 with 91% fully fitting traces confirms that the normative model is a real description of behaviour, not an aspiration. This is the reference against which every later number must be read.

**T1 — Day 0 morning (WMS unavailable).**
Discontinuity point. The system of record for inventory, scanning and release disappears. No separate metrics are given for this window, which is itself a finding: the transition from "system-mediated" to "improvised" was not instrumented, so the moment at which the organisation *decided* to run degraded is not visible in the log. Risk posture: undefined, because controls were neither formally suspended nor formally maintained.

**T2 — Day 0 afternoon to Day 2 (manual workaround, 5,600 cases).**
Operational state: a genuinely different process, not a degraded version of the old one. Receive order no longer flows to Validate authorization at all; it flows to Emergency triage (5,100) or Defer noncritical order (500). Three dominant variants emerge:
- *Compliance-preserving manual variant*: Emergency triage → Manual authorization check (2,500) → Manual inventory snapshot check → Manual pick list.
- *Control-bypass variant*: Emergency triage → Skip system authorization check (1,600 = 31% of triaged orders) → Manual inventory snapshot check.
- *Hospital fast lane*: Emergency triage → Hospital-critical fast lane (1,000 → 920 to snapshot check).

Downstream, the traceability collapse is concentrated in three edges: of 3,600 manual packs, only 1,650 (46%) passed a Temporary release control, while 1,800 went directly to Dispatch without scan; combined with the 1,450 controlled dispatches, 3,250 shipments left the site with no system scan. Only 2,600 produced a Phone proof of delivery — roughly 650 dispatched cases have *no* delivery evidence of any kind — and only 2,100 reached END via Spreadsheet invoice hold. Adding 430 cancellations, roughly 2,500 of 5,600 cases closed inside T2; the remainder were carried forward as open, evidence-incomplete work. That carried-forward mass is the seed of the T4 audit tail.

Risk posture: service protected, control destroyed. Hospital-critical median cycle time held at 10h vs 8h baseline (p90 21h vs 15h) and fill rate held at 91% — a strong outcome. But Pharmacy-standard collapsed (median 54h vs 23h, p90 118h vs 49h, fill rate 61%) and Wholesale-bulk effectively stopped (p90 180h vs 72h). Compliance metrics moved by an order of magnitude: traceability exceptions 9.5% (~12×, ≈532 cases), cold-chain documentation missing 4.8% (12×, ≈269 cases), controlled-substance dual-control missing 2.9% (14.5×, ≈162 cases). Fitness fell to 0.71 with only 44% fitting traces, with move-on-log deviations (Skip system authorization check, Dispatch without scan, Phone proof of delivery, Spreadsheet invoice hold) and move-on-model deviations (missing Quality/release check, missing scan, missing Proof of delivery, missing temperature record). The move-on-model list is the more serious of the two: it says controls were *absent*, not merely performed differently.

**T3 — Day 3 to Day 6 (partial recovery, 11,400 cases).**
Order intake returns but inventory sync and scanning are unreliable — the most dangerous phase in terms of *false confidence*, because the system now looks authoritative while its inventory state is not. Metrics are roughly halfway back: traceability exceptions 5.4%, cold-chain 2.3%, dual-control 1.4%, rework 9.0%, cancellations 5.2%, fitness 0.82 / 59% fitting. Hospital-critical is already near-normal (median 9h, p90 18h) while Pharmacy-standard (median 38h, p90 90h) and Wholesale-bulk (median 84h, p90 160h) still carry the deferral debt from T2. Note the asymmetry: **service recovery for the protected segment ran ahead of control recovery for everyone.** Volume of 11,400 cases over 4 days (~2,850/day) against a baseline of 2,000/day shows backlog burn-down was already competing with fresh intake.

**T4 — Day 7 to Day 21 (stabilization, 35,000 cases).**
Cycle time is, on the surface, recovered: Hospital-critical is at baseline (8h / 16h); Pharmacy-standard median 28h vs 23h; Wholesale-bulk median 58h vs 36h and p90 120h vs 72h — bulk is the last to normalise, consistent with it having absorbed the deferral policy. But the DFG shows the process is structurally *not* the baseline process. Four new recovery constructs carry material volume: Backlog prioritization (1,500, of which 450 cancel — a 30% cancellation rate inside the backlog vs 3.0% overall), Reconcile manual reservation (1,400, of which 300 cancel), Proof-of-delivery remediation (1,200 → 800 to invoice close), and Audit remediation (2,300 after invoice close, of which only 2,100 reach END). Compliance metrics remain 2–3× baseline (traceability 2.2%, cold-chain 0.9%, dual-control 0.5%, rework 5.8%) and fitness is 0.90 / 76% fitting — still short of the 0.96 / 91% baseline.

Risk posture: **operationally recovered, evidentially not.** The Invoice close → Audit remediation edge is the signature finding of the whole case — compliance work is happening *after* the financial and operational close of the case, which means the metric that management watches (cycle time to invoice close) went green while the obligation was still open. The residual 200-case gap between Audit remediation entries and Audit remediation → END exits, plus the persistent queue noted in the summary, indicate the tail extends beyond Day 21.

## 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

The two look identical on a throughput chart and are opposite in kind. **Resilience is the substitution of a control under degraded conditions: the control objective is still met, by a different mechanism, and the fact that the mechanism changed is visible in the record.** **Concealed noncompliance is the deletion of a control while the process continues to emit signals that imply the control was performed** — the case closes, the invoice issues, the cycle-time metric looks normal, and nothing in the routine reporting distinguishes the case from a compliant one.

The operational test that separates them in this log is: *did the adaptation produce evidence at the time it was performed?* Manual authorization check (B) produced a compliance-staff decision. Temporary release control (F) produced a pharmacist judgment. Phone proof of delivery (H) produced a customer confirmation. These are substitutions — lower-fidelity, but real and contemporaneous. Skip system authorization check (C) produced *nothing*: there is no artefact, because no decision was made. Dispatch without scan (G) produced nothing at the moment custody transferred. Manual pick list (E) produced a physical action whose evidence was promised for later. That is why C and G appear as move-on-log deviations *and* generate move-on-model deviations (missing scan, missing POD, missing temperature record): the log records an activity where the model expects a control, and the control never happened anywhere.

A second test is *whether the metric that management watches still measures the obligation.* In T2 and T4 it did not. Cycle time to Invoice close returned to baseline in T4 for Hospital-critical (8h median, 16h p90) while 2,300 cases carried an open Audit remediation obligation *after* that close. The Invoice close → Audit remediation edge is the concealment mechanism made explicit in the DFG: the process was redesigned, informally and under pressure, so that closure precedes compliance. That is the case's central lesson, and it is why fitness at 0.90 with 76% fitting traces in T4 should be read as "one case in four still does not conform" rather than "nearly recovered".

**True resilience in this case: B, F (as designed for controlled substances), I (as an act of triage), J (as an act of sequencing), K, and L as a capability.** These preserved either the control objective or the integrity of the record, and each is visible in the log as a deliberate, staffed activity. B is the strongest: it held a real authorization control for half of all triaged orders at the height of the disruption. I and J deserve particular credit — deliberately shedding and re-sequencing demand is textbook graceful degradation, and the 91% hospital fill rate against a 61% pharmacy fill rate is the measurable proof that the sacrifice was made consciously rather than suffered randomly.

**Risky shortcuts — throughput without control: C and G, with E as a near-miss.** C shipped against assumed authorization; G broke chain of custody at the one point where custody actually changes hands. Both are irrecoverable: no amount of T4 remediation can retroactively establish that a customer was authorised on Day 1, or that consignment X contained lot Y at handoff. E is less severe only because the physical act genuinely occurred and some evidence (paper labels) existed, but "scans were later reconstructed" is an evidentiary fiction if reconstruction was from memory rather than from a contemporaneous artefact. The 12× jump in traceability exceptions is the joint fingerprint of C, E and G.

**Ambiguous, depending on risk appetite and on facts the log does not contain: A, D, H, I, J, and L-as-executed.**
- **A** is resilience if the fast lane substituted an offline dual-check for the electronic one, and noncompliance if it simply skipped it. The DFG shows 920 of 1,000 fast-lane cases going straight to the inventory snapshot with no authorization node in between, which leans toward the latter — a serious finding given these are hospital orders.
- **D** is resilience on Day 0 and noncompliance by Day 2; the same activity changes character with elapsed time. The 21% cancellation rate on reconciliation is the retrospective price.
- **H** is adequate evidence for ambient and inadequate for controlled substances — the same activity, two different verdicts by product class.
- **I and J** are resilient if the prioritisation rules were explicit, pre-agreed and communicated; they are unfair rationing if they were improvised at the desk. The 30% cancellation rate inside backlog prioritisation is the number an auditor or a disappointed pharmacy customer will ask about, and the log offers no criterion to answer with.
- **L** is a genuine remediation capability positioned in the wrong place; the capability is resilience, the placement is concealment.

The honest summary: **this organisation's people performed well and its control architecture did not.** Service to the segment that mattered most was protected at real cost to others, which is a defensible choice. But the controls that disappeared were not chosen — they disappeared wherever the system that enforced them disappeared, because there was no designed degraded mode. Resilience was an achievement of individuals; noncompliance was an emergent property of the design.

## 4. Recovery process model

Notation: BPMN-style bullets with an explicit control-gate vocabulary. `[GATE]` = a hard blocking condition that cannot be waived by operations; `[X-OR]` = exclusive decision point; `→` = sequence flow; `‖` = parallel; **DEGRADED-n** = the declared degradation level. Every activity below emits a case-level event with actor ID, timestamp and mode flag (`NORMAL` / `DEGRADED-n`), so that post-incident conformance checking can partition the log by mode rather than having to infer it.

### 4.0 Design principle
Do not attempt to run the normal process badly. Declare a **named degraded mode** with its own model, its own minimum controls and its own metrics. Then every T2-style deviation becomes conformant behaviour against a mode-specific model, and the only real deviations are the ones that matter. The failure at T1 was not that the WMS died; it was that nobody switched models, so 5,600 cases were executed against a normative model that had become physically impossible.

### 4.1 Trigger conditions (entry and exit)
- **Entry, automatic:** WMS unavailable > 30 minutes; **or** scan-event rate < 40% of trailing 7-day hourly median for 2 consecutive hours; **or** inventory-sync lag > 4 hours; **or** declared cyber incident at severity ≥ 2. Any one trigger auto-opens a `Disruption` case and pages the Incident Commander.
- **Declaration:** Incident Commander declares **DEGRADED-1** (systems partially available, scanning unreliable — the T3 state) or **DEGRADED-2** (system of record unavailable — the T2 state) within 60 minutes. Declaration is a logged event with a named accountable individual. *This event did not exist in T1 and is the highest-value single addition in this design.*
- **Exit, staged and separately gated:**
  - *Operational exit:* systems available AND inventory reconciled AND scan rate ≥ 95% of baseline for 24h.
  - *Evidence exit:* open remediation items = 0 for controlled-substance, cold-chain and recall-sensitive cases; ≤ baseline rate for ambient.
  - **The incident is not closed until both gates pass.** In this case the organisation crossed the operational gate around Day 7–10 and had still not crossed the evidence gate at Day 21.

### 4.2 Triage rules
```
Receive order
  → [X-OR] Triage (mandatory, ≤15 min SLA, rules published in advance)
      Priority = f(customer criticality, clinical urgency, product risk, stock cover at customer)
      P1  Hospital-critical, or any order flagged clinically urgent          → Critical lane
      P2  Cold-chain / controlled-substance / recall-sensitive, any customer → Controlled lane
      P3  Pharmacy-standard ambient                                          → Standard lane
      P4  Wholesale-bulk, replenishment, non-urgent                          → Deferral candidate
```
- P4 deferral is permitted **only** with a logged `Defer` event carrying reason code, decision-maker, revised commitment date, and an automatic customer notification. Deferral is never silent. (This fixes I: the shedding was right, the silence was not.)
- Deferral capacity is capped: no more than X% of a segment's volume may be deferred per day without escalation to the Incident Commander, preventing a segment from being quietly starved as Pharmacy-standard was at 61% fill.
- **Critical lane accelerates sequencing, never controls.** A P1 order jumps the queue; it does not skip authorization. This is the explicit correction to A.

### 4.3 Minimum controls by product/customer risk
These are floor conditions. They apply in every mode; only the *mechanism* degrades.

| Product class | Authorization | Release / QA | Custody evidence | Delivery evidence | Waivable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Controlled substance** | Two-person offline check against cached authorization register, both IDs logged | 100% pharmacist release, dual control, no sampling | Signed manifest + photo of label/lot/expiry at handoff | Recipient signature (wet or electronic); **phone POD not acceptable** | **No — [GATE], stop shipment** |
| **Cold-chain** | Cached-register check, single trained checker | 100% release incl. temperature-logger read at pack and at handoff | Signed manifest + temperature record | Signature or phone POD **plus** temperature record | **No — [GATE], stop shipment** |
| **Recall-sensitive** | Cached-register check **plus** recall-status check against a register refreshed ≤ 4h | 100% release, lot verified | Lot/batch recorded at pick, photo evidence | Signature or phone POD | **No — [GATE]** |
| **Ambient, P1/P2 customer** | Cached-register check | Risk-based sampling ≥ 20% | Manifest + photo | Phone POD acceptable | Sampling rate waivable by IC, logged |
| **Ambient, P3/P4** | Cached-register check | Risk-based sampling ≥ 10% | Manifest | Phone POD acceptable | Yes, with logged IC approval |

Two enabling capabilities make this feasible and are the main investments:
1. **Offline cached authorization register** — a nightly-refreshed, read-only, cryptographically signed export of customer/product eligibility, licences, recall flags and standing orders, held on standalone devices. This removes the *reason* C existed: teams skipped authorization because there was nothing to check against, not because they wanted to.
2. **Offline scan buffer** — handheld scanners (or a phone app) that capture scan, lot, expiry, actor and timestamp locally and sync when systems return. This removes the *reason* G existed. Handoff stays as fast as an unscanned handoff; the evidence is created contemporaneously rather than reconstructed.

### 4.4 Manual evidence capture
Principle: **evidence is created at the moment of the act, never reconstructed afterwards.** Every degraded-mode activity must emit a minimum evidence packet: what (SKU, lot, expiry, quantity), who (named actor ID), when (device timestamp), under what authority (mode flag + approver for any waiver).
- Pick: offline scan or, as last resort, photo of label with lot/expiry + picker ID.
- Pack: manifest number, pack station, packer ID; temperature-logger serial for cold-chain.
- Release: releaser ID, sampling decision recorded even when the sample was *not* taken (so coverage is measurable — F's 46% coverage was invisible at the time).
- Dispatch: courier ID, signed manifest, timestamp — offline-buffered, never omitted.
- POD: signature capture where required; phone POD permitted only for ambient, with caller ID, recipient name and time, and an automatic flag if the Dispatch→POD delay exceeds 24h (the T2 profile showed this delay was highly variable and therefore untrustworthy).
- Every degraded case carries a `degraded_evidence = true` attribute that follows it to close and drives the reconciliation queue automatically — no manual identification of affected cases.

### 4.5 Backlog prioritization (recovery phase)
```
Systems return
  → Freeze intake priority rules (published, versioned, timestamped)
  → Score backlog: clinical urgency > product risk > age-in-queue > commercial value
  → [X-OR] Backlog disposition
       → Reinstate  (default)
       → Re-confirm with customer  (age > 48h, or stock position changed)
       → Cancel  [GATE: requires customer contact + reason code + supervisor approval]
  → Merge with live intake under a published capacity split (e.g. 70% backlog / 30% new
    until backlog age p90 < 24h), so fresh orders cannot permanently starve deferred ones
```
The T4 log shows 450 of 1,500 backlog cases cancelled with no visible criterion. Under this design, cancellation is an approved, customer-contacted, reason-coded event, and the cancellation rate per segment is monitored explicitly for fairness.

### 4.6 Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow
The structural change: **remediation moves from after Invoice close to before it, for anything risk-bearing.**
```
Case in degraded mode
  → ‖ (a) Inventory reconciliation: manual reservation vs actual on-hand, lot-level
    ‖ (b) Evidence completeness check: authorization, scan, release, temperature, POD
  → [X-OR] Evidence status
       Complete
         → Invoice close → END
       Incomplete, ambient only
         → Invoice close (permitted) ‖ Remediation queue  [case stays OPEN in reporting]
       Incomplete, controlled / cold-chain / recall-sensitive
         → [GATE] Invoice close BLOCKED
         → Remediation → [X-OR] Recoverable?
              Yes → evidence restored → Invoice close → END
              No  → Escalate: QP / Responsible Person decision
                    → Deviation record + regulatory notification assessment
                    → Product disposition (recall, quarantine, customer notification)
                    → END with permanent deviation flag
```
Three non-negotiables encoded here:
- **A case is not "complete" while remediation is open.** Reporting must show open-obligation count alongside cycle time, so the T4 illusion (cycle time green, 2,300 obligations open) cannot recur.
- **Unrecoverable evidence is escalated, not closed.** Some T2 gaps — authorization never performed, custody never recorded — *cannot* be remediated. Pretending otherwise is the deeper compliance failure. These must reach a Responsible Person for a documented deviation decision, not be absorbed into a data-cleanup queue.
- **Remediation is resourced from a standing budget**, not from the same staff running current operations, or the tail simply extends (as it did past Day 21).

## 5. Resilience indicators

### Leading indicators (during disruption — visible within hours, drive action)
| Domain | Indicator | Trigger / target |
|---|---|---|
| Throughput | Degraded-mode completions per hour vs baseline hourly rate | < 50% for 4h → escalate capacity |
| Service protection | P1 (Hospital-critical) fill rate and median cycle time, hourly | Fill < 95% or median > 1.5× baseline (i.e. > 12h) → escalate |
| Service equity | Fill rate **by segment**, hourly — P3/P4 tracked as prominently as P1 | Any segment < 75% for 12h → IC decision on rationing, customer comms mandatory |
| Traceability | % dispatches with contemporaneous scan/manifest evidence | < 98% → stop-and-fix; T2 ran at ~0% and nobody was alerted |
| Control completeness | % controlled-substance orders with dual control; % cold-chain with temperature record; % orders with any authorization check | Any < 100% for gated classes → **stop shipment for that class** |
| Control coverage | Release-check coverage rate (F ran at 46% and this was not visible at the time) | Below the mode's minimum sampling floor → escalate |
| Waivers | Count of IC-approved control waivers, by class, per shift | Any waiver on a gated class → automatic escalation |
| Data staleness | Age of inventory snapshot; age of authorization register | Snapshot > 8h or register > 24h → block picking of recall-sensitive and controlled items |
| Backlog | Deferred-order count and age p50/p90, by segment | Age p90 > 48h → capacity or comms action |

### Lagging indicators (recovery quality — weekly, until the evidence gate closes)
| Domain | Indicator | Baseline reference |
|---|---|---|
| Traceability | Traceability exception rate | 0.8%; T2 9.5%, T3 5.4%, T4 2.2% — target return to ≤ 1.0% |
| Control completeness | Cold-chain documentation missing / controlled dual-control missing | 0.4% / 0.2%; T4 still 0.9% / 0.5% |
| Rework | Rework/correction case rate | 3.5%; T4 5.8% |
| Cancellation | Cancellation rate, **split by segment and by cause** (deferral, stock-out, reconciliation) | 2.1% overall; T4 3.0% overall but 30% within backlog prioritisation |
| Hidden workload | **Open remediation obligations after operational close** — count, age p90, and days-to-zero | Should be 0 at incident close; was still non-zero at Day 21 (2,300 raised, 2,100 closed) |
| Evidence irrecoverability | Count of cases where evidence *cannot* be reconstructed, by product class | The number that determines regulatory exposure; currently not measured at all |
| Conformance | Fitness and % fitting traces, **against the mode-appropriate model** | Baseline 0.96/91%; T4 0.90/76% — recovery is incomplete until both return |
| Fairness | Cycle-time and fill-rate ratio between best- and worst-served segment, over time | T2 ratio was extreme (91% vs 61% fill); measure how long inequity persisted, not just its peak |
| Recovery lag | Days from operational-exit gate to evidence-exit gate | The gap between "looks recovered" and "is recovered" — the single number that captures this incident's core failure |

Two composite measures worth reporting to the board:
- **Control-integrity index** = % of cases in the period meeting all mandatory controls for their product class. This would read ~0.96 at baseline, and far lower in T2 — and, unlike cycle time, it would not have gone green in T4.
- **Deferred-service debt** = cumulative case-days of delay imposed on deliberately deferred customers, by segment. It makes the cost of protecting P1 explicit and payable rather than invisible.

## 6. Recommendations for future disruptions

**Priority 1 — Define and rehearse a declared degraded operating mode (DEGRADED-1 / DEGRADED-2) with its own process model and minimum controls.**
*Effort:* Medium (6–10 weeks: SOP design, QA/regulatory sign-off, training). *Benefit:* High — converts improvisation into designed behaviour, makes conformance checking meaningful during the incident, and removes the ambiguity that let C and G happen. *Residual risk:* A pre-written mode may not fit the actual failure shape; mitigate with a generic "control substitution" principle plus IC discretion, bounded by the non-waivable gates.

**Priority 2 — Deploy the offline cached authorization register and offline scan buffer.**
*Effort:* Medium-high (technical build, standalone devices, nightly signed export, quarterly restore test). *Benefit:* Very high — these two capabilities directly remove the *cause* of the two irrecoverable deviation classes (C: 1,600 cases; G: 3,250 cases) rather than remediating their effects. *Residual risk:* Cached data is stale by construction (up to 24h), so a same-day licence suspension or recall is missed; mitigate with a 4-hourly recall-flag refresh over an independent channel and by treating recall-sensitive stock as gated.

**Priority 3 — Make hard gates non-waivable for controlled substances, cold-chain and recall-sensitive product, including invoice-close blocking.**
*Effort:* Low-medium (policy + system rule). *Benefit:* High — bounds the worst-case regulatory and patient-safety exposure and, critically, stops the "close first, comply later" pattern shown by the Invoice close → Audit remediation edge. *Residual risk:* Some critical shipments will be genuinely blocked and hospitals will be short. This is a deliberate and correct trade: it converts an invisible compliance risk into a visible, escalated service decision made by a named accountable person.

**Priority 4 — Publish, version and pre-agree the triage and deferral rules with customers, and make every deferral and backlog cancellation a logged, communicated, reason-coded event.**
*Effort:* Low internally; medium commercially (customer negotiation). *Benefit:* High — resolves the J ambiguity permanently, defends against fairness and contractual challenge, and converts the 61% pharmacy fill rate from a silent failure into a managed expectation. *Residual risk:* Customers may object to their assigned tier, or attempt to escalate their own classification; manage contractually with clinical-urgency criteria rather than commercial size.

**Priority 5 — Restructure remediation: move the evidence gate before invoice close for gated classes, staff remediation from a standing reserve, and report open obligations alongside cycle time.**
*Effort:* Medium (process and reporting change, plus resourcing). *Benefit:* High — eliminates the concealment mechanism and the post-recovery hidden workload; makes the "days from operational exit to evidence exit" gap visible to management. *Residual risk:* Cash collection slows for blocked cases; quantify and accept as the price of evidence integrity, and confine blocking to gated product classes so the working-capital impact stays bounded.

**Priority 6 — Impose time-to-live on degraded data artefacts (inventory snapshot ≤ 8h, authorization register ≤ 24h) with mandatory refresh and automatic blocking on expiry.**
*Effort:* Low. *Benefit:* Medium-high — directly addresses D's staleness, which drove the 12.5% rework rate, the 7.8% cancellation rate, and the 21% cancellation rate within T4 reconciliation. *Residual risk:* Refresh may be impossible in a total outage; define an explicit fallback (physical stock count for gated SKUs only).

**Priority 7 — Correct the release-control coverage rule: 100% for controlled substances *and* cold-chain, risk-based sampling for ambient only, with coverage measured live.**
*Effort:* Low (policy); medium (pharmacist capacity in-incident). *Benefit:* Medium-high — closes the specific gap where F protected controlled substances (2.9% gap) but left cold-chain uncovered (4.8% gap). *Residual risk:* Pharmacist capacity becomes the binding constraint; mitigate with a trained-deputy pool and mutual-aid arrangements.

**Priority 8 — Build surge compliance capacity (cross-trained staff, mutual aid, retained third party) and pre-agree courier POD-substitution protocols.**
*Effort:* Medium (training, contracts). *Benefit:* Medium-high — B was the best-performing adaptation and its only limitation was capacity; expanding it would have absorbed most of the 1,600 cases that went to C. Pre-agreed courier protocols make signature capture feasible when the WMS is down, removing the need for H on gated product. *Residual risk:* Cross-trained staff are less proficient; require periodic competency checks and log checker identity so quality is auditable.

**Priority 9 — Rehearse annually with a full simulated WMS outage, and run post-incident conformance analysis partitioned by declared mode.**
*Effort:* Medium recurring. *Benefit:* Medium — surfaces exactly the coverage blind spots (F at 46%, POD at 80%) that were invisible in real time, and validates that the offline capabilities actually work under load. *Residual risk:* Exercises are optimistic relative to real incidents; include unannounced elements and measure control-integrity, not just throughput.

## 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should **accept** that in a severe disruption the organisation will deliberately protect hospital-critical supply at the explicit expense of pharmacy and bulk service — the 91% versus 61% fill-rate split in T2 was the right call and should be pre-authorised, published and rehearsed rather than improvised. It should also accept slower cycle times, higher cancellation rates, deliberate demand shedding, and lower-fidelity but *contemporaneous* evidence (manual authorization checks, photo-and-manifest custody records, phone POD for ambient product): degraded evidence honestly captured and clearly labelled is a legitimate operating state, and the T2 staff who improvised these substitutions performed well. What management should **not** accept is the deletion of a control while the process keeps emitting the signals of a completed, compliant case — specifically shipping on assumed authorization (C), courier handoff with no custody record (G), release checks silently covering only 46% of packs (F as executed), a fast lane that buys speed for the highest-risk customers by removing their verification (A as executed), and above all the Invoice close → Audit remediation sequence, which let cycle-time metrics return to baseline in T4 while 2,300 compliance obligations remained open and 200 were still unresolved at Day 21. After-the-fact remediation is not compliance; for authorization decisions never made and custody never recorded, it is not even possible, and the correct response to those cases is a documented deviation and a Responsible Person decision, not a data-cleanup ticket. Concretely: fund the offline authorization register and offline scan buffer, make the gates for controlled, cold-chain and recall-sensitive product non-waivable including invoice-close blocking, publish the triage rules, and report open remediation obligations on the same dashboard as cycle time. The evidence that would change this recommendation is threefold: if a review of the fast-lane and skipped-authorization cases shows they were in fact confined to long-standing customers with verified standing authorizations and no recall exposure, then C's severity drops and cached-register investment can be scaled back; if the T2 pick-list reconstructions were built from contemporaneous paper artefacts with lot and expiry rather than from recollection, then E is resilience and the scan-buffer case rests on G alone; and if the offline capabilities prove infeasible or unaffordable at the required reliability, then the correct fallback is not to reopen the shortcuts but to lower the declared degraded-mode throughput ceiling and shed more demand earlier — accepting a smaller, transparently rationed service rather than a larger one whose compliance status cannot be demonstrated.
