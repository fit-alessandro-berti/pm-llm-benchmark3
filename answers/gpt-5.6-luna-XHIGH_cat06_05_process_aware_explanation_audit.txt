## 1. Leakage and temporal-consistency findings

| Item | Finding type | Severity (critical/high/medium/low) | Why | Required remediation |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| total_case_duration_hours | Direct temporal and target-proximate leakage | critical | It is calculated after closure, whereas the score is produced at t0. It also nearly encodes the settlement-time target itself and can include the consequences of all later work. | Remove it from the training and explanation feature set. If elapsed time is useful, construct a prefix-only clock or remaining-slack feature using timestamps available at t0, then retrain. |
| appeal_seen_anywhere | Complete-trace leakage | critical | An appeal can occur after t0 and is only possible after a Partial or Deny decision. A complete-trace indicator reveals a future branch and often a future decision outcome. | Remove it. Use an as-of-t0 indicator or count for an appeal already observed, with the strict rule that future appeals are absent; at this prefix it would normally be zero. |
| final_handler_team | Closure-populated future field | critical | The warehouse populates it at closure. The fact that it equals the t0 team in about 65% of cases is not evidence that the closure value was available at t0. | Exclude the closure field, or reconstruct the historical assignment/team snapshot as of t0 with an availability timestamp. |
| num_document_requests_total | Complete-trace aggregation | critical | It counts requests from the whole case, including requests made after t0. It can therefore reveal how much future rework occurred. | Replace it with a strictly prefix-bounded count, such as requests observed by t0, and record the feature’s source and availability timestamps. |

Describe it as an association with expected workload, not as a proven cause. |

| full_trace_variant_id | Complete-trace path leakage | critical | The identifier is computed from the full trace and can encode downstream requests, decisions, appeals, closure, and the realized route. It is unavailable at t0. | Remove it from the early model and explainer. Use a prefix variant or state representation derived only from the observed prefix. |
| Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed | Process-inconsistent ordering plus future-trace use | critical | The normative process requires damage assessment before the final decision and closure. A close-to-damage edge is therefore impossible under reliable ordering; the same timestamp and missing sequence index can have inverted source order or reflect a data defect. It is also necessarily beyond the stated prefix. | Recover a reliable sequence index or represent tied events as a partial order, then align against the process model. Do not use the edge unless the event semantics are repaired; otherwise exclude and investigate the source. |
| Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded | Future but process-valid pattern | critical | An appeal followed by another settlement decision is explicitly allowed after a Partial or Deny decision. The pattern is not intrinsically impossible, but it is future information at t0 and may be confused if repeated coarse decision labels are collapsed. | Keep it only for post-appeal analyses or after the appeal has actually been observed. For early prediction, use occurrence-indexed, prefix-only events and retrain without the future pattern. |
| Documents Requested -> Documents Received | Future, process-feasible sequence pattern | critical | The transition is compatible with an evidence cycle, but the sequence explainer appears to derive it from the complete trace. At the stated prefix neither event has been observed. | Recompute sequence contributions on the observed prefix. If a count is needed, use requests/receipts with event and availability time no later than t0. |
| Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened | Future-possible concurrent activity | critical | Fraud investigation may be opened after a trigger and may run in parallel with evidence collection or damage assessment, so this is not automatically impossible. It is nevertheless a later event for a prediction immediately after coverage check, and adjacency in a serialized trace may be an artifact of concurrency. | Use only a fraud event observed by t0. Model concurrency explicitly or use a process-state indicator; validate the trigger and event order rather than treating this DFG edge as a rule. |
| Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded | Future, process-feasible sequence pattern | critical | The order is compatible with the normative process, but both activities are after the stated prefix. The negative contribution is an association in later traces, not evidence that damage assessment reduces breach risk. | Exclude from the t0 explanation and recompute from prefix events. Use it only in a later prediction problem whose observation time makes it available. |
| claimant_channel | Availability-undocumented proxy candidate | medium | A channel such as Broker is plausibly known at registration, but the supplied lineage does not establish its source availability time. It may also encode socioeconomic or access differences. | Verify point-in-time availability, then subject it to proxy, fairness, and robustness review. If retained, describe the channel association and not a causal effect of the claimant’s choice. |
| final_decision | Future outcome leakage | critical | A decision is downstream of damage assessment and may be followed by an appeal and another decision. Partial is not observable immediately after coverage check and can be close to the target or its downstream consequences. | Remove from the t0 model and explanations. Use a decision-as-of-t0 field only for a later prediction time. |
| initial_reserve | Lineage-unknown, potentially mutable severity feature | high | The name suggests an initial value, but no timestamp or freeze guarantee is supplied. It cannot be assumed to have been available before t0; reserve amounts are often updated as information arrives. | Reconstruct the first reserve version with an availability timestamp no later than t0, or exclude it. Once repaired, assess its severity/proxy role and recalibrate. |
| risk_score_current | Mutable feature without historical versioning | high | The current score may have been recalculated after t0, and no feature timestamp distinguishes the t0 value from a later value. | last_resource_role | Complete-trace/future resource field | critical | “Last” is defined over the complete case, so the Special Investigation Unit role may be assigned long after t0. It is not a t0 resource assignment. | Remove it from the early explanation. Replace it with the resource or role assigned as of t0 only after historical assignment timestamps are available. |

Even if its global importance is small, the value may reflect later employment changes and is not a valid historical feature. | Repair the as-of join or exclude it from all prefix models and explanations; audit any derived team/role attributes similarly. |

## 2. Process-model consistency checks

The validation should operate on an explicit observation record for every claim: prediction time t0, the event prefix visible at t0, feature values, source event times, source availability or ingestion times, and sequence indices. A feature passes the temporal test only when its value and the information needed to compute it were available at or before t0. For mutable values, the relevant version must be the version valid at t0, not merely a row extracted later. All joins should be backward as-of joins, with a recorded match time and a failure state for stale or future matches. Boundary tests should include events at exactly t0, events later on the same day, late-arriving events, and claims whose extraction date differs from t0.

Use a process state machine or a normative Petri-net/state-transition model to derive the set of possible states after each prefix. Prefix conformance checks should verify that the observed activities can be aligned to the model and should report missing, inserted, repeated, and reordered events. At the prefix immediately after Coverage Check Completed, a fast-track damage assessment may be enabled; a document request may be enabled for the evidence branch; fraud investigation is future-possible if its trigger is satisfied and may be concurrent; settlement, appeal, and closure require later prerequisites. Documents Received requires a preceding request, an appeal requires the appropriate first decision, and closure requires the final decision or withdrawal. “Future-possible” is different from “enabled now”: an activity can be legal later while still being unavailable as a t0 explanation. An activity that cannot be aligned without an impossible prerequisite should be reported as a conformance or data-quality defect, not silently treated as a predictive signal.

Use a directly specified normative model for these checks. A DFG or footprint discovered from historical data is useful for finding frequent transitions and candidate anomalies, but frequency does not establish a rule: a rare valid transition can be legitimate, and a frequent transition can be caused by a systematic logging or serialization error. Alignment cost, missing-event rates, and unknown-event mappings should be reported because a low-quality alignment can make an invalid trace appear valid.

Same-timestamp events need a reliable sequence index or a declared partial-order semantics. If the index is missing, the validator should not infer an order from file order or an arbitrary sort. It should either retain the tie as unordered, test all process-consistent linearizations, or fail the sequence explanation when the possible orders have different meanings. The Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed pattern is especially suspect because its only apparent support is a tied timestamp and it conflicts with the normative order.

Feature lineage tests should classify every feature as event-derived, snapshot-derived, mutable, complete-trace aggregate, or label-derived. A static lineage rule should reject any aggregate whose source event or source version has an effective or availability time after t0, even when the aggregate is named “initial” or “current.” A complete-trace scan should also inspect sequence-explainer inputs, not just the tabular feature store.

Repeated Settlement Decision Recorded events must be represented with an occurrence number or decision identifier. The first decision may be Approve, Partial, Deny, or Withdrawn; an appeal is allowed only after Partial or Deny and can lead to a second decision. The validator should check that relationship and that Claim Closed follows the final decision or withdrawal. Two decisions without a valid appeal, or a closure between the first and second decision, should be flagged rather than collapsed. This preserves a legitimate appeal-to-reconsideration pattern while preventing a coarse activity label from being mistaken for a single one-time event.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

- **Defensible predictive signal.** Initial damage complexity and elapsed_since_registration_at_t0 are admissible after their stated timestamp guarantees are tested. A repaired prior_claim_count_before_t0, an initial reserve whose historical first version is verified, and a verified registration channel may also be predictive. These features may support a statement such as “the model associates this observed claim state or history with higher or lower breach risk.” They do not establish that complexity, prior claims, a reserve, or a claimant action causes delay. Operational actionability requires a separate intervention analysis.

- **Process-state or congestion signal.** queue_length_at_t0 is a useful process-state measure when the snapshot is genuinely available at t0. Prefix-derived state, such as the observed branch, age/slack, or counts of document requests already seen, can be used in the same way if it is bounded by the prefix. Such signals can identify workload or triage conditions and may be operationally actionable, but a SHAP contribution does not show that reducing the queue would reduce the probability for a particular claim. That claim needs causal or experimental evidence.

- **Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review.** claimant_region, claimant_channel, initial_reserve, prior-claim history, and possibly employee or resource attributes can encode network access, language, socioeconomic status, severity, or institutional allocation. Temporal availability is necessary but not sufficient for acceptance. These features need subgroup performance, calibration, false-positive/false-negative, missingness, and sensitivity analyses, together with a governance decision about legitimate use. They should not automatically be deleted merely because they are correlated, nor should their correlation be presented as a causal explanation or as a reason to treat a claimant differently without policy justification.

- **Drift/calendar proxy.** calendar_week is known at t0, but its contribution may represent a holiday period, staffing pattern, or policy regime. It may be useful for forecasting under a stable operating calendar, but “week 52 causes delay” is unsupported. Use temporal validation, explicit known-calendar and policy variables where appropriate, and drift monitoring; consider restricting or regularizing the feature if it fails to transport across regimes.

- **Unusable until lineage is repaired.** total_case_duration_hours, appeal_seen_anywhere, final_handler_team, num_document_requests_total, full_trace_variant_id, final_decision, last_resource_role, the complete-trace sequence patterns, the future-inclusive document snapshot, the unversioned current risk score, and the unversioned employee-experience join cannot support a t0 explanation. The current prior-claim SQL and initial-reserve lineage also belong here until repaired. They may be useful in retrospective analysis or a later prediction task, but they must be removed from this early model and its explanations until point-in-time recomputation proves admissibility.

Across all classes, predictive validity, causal interpretation, operational actionability, and fairness acceptability are separate standards. A feature can pass one and fail another: region can be available and predictive but a problematic proxy; queue length can be actionable as a candidate intervention but not causally validated; and a future appeal can be highly predictive while being unusable at t0.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Repair the feature store first.** Store event time, valid-from and updated-at times, ingestion time, source version, reliable sequence index, and feature computation time for every value. Provide a point-in-time feature API that performs backward-only as-of joins and records whether a match was future, stale, late-arriving, or missing. Keep complete-trace aggregates in a separately governed namespace that cannot be selected by an early-prediction job.

2. **Rebuild the t0 dataset and lineage.** Materialize every feature from the observed prefix and historical snapshots, with strict t0 inclusion rules. Replace total counts with prefix counts, closure teams with historical assignments, the nightly document snapshot with a t0 snapshot, and the current risk score with a versioned score. Fix the prior-claim inequality and its availability semantics. Exclude any field whose lineage cannot be demonstrated.

3. **Add automated process-conformance gates.** Run state-machine or Petri-net alignments, prefix checks, declarative prerequisite checks, and concurrency-aware sequence tests before training and before explaining predictions. Use DFGs and footprints for diagnostics, not as normative rules. Flag impossible edges, missing prerequisites, unexplained loops, and excessive alignment cost. Explicitly test the valid appeal-to-second-decision path using occurrence identifiers.

4. **Retrain, recalibrate, and re-evaluate after removal.** Refit the model on the repaired prefix-only data and use temporal holdouts that include policy and holiday regime changes. Recheck discrimination, calibration, calibration by time and process branch, and the distribution of scores. Do not reuse the old probabilities or old SHAP values after the feature set changes.

5. **Validate explanations for stability and provenance.** Generate local and global explanations only from the same point-in-time feature record used for the prediction. Check stability under bootstrap samples, nearby observation times, feature ablation, and alternate valid event linearizations for ties. Report uncertainty and contribution sensitivity, and attach each explanation to feature timestamps and lineage checks.

6. **Run proxy and fairness review without conflating it with leakage review.** Evaluate region, channel, reserve, prior history, and employee/resource attributes for subgroup calibration, error rates, missingness, and robustness to removal or perturbation. Document legitimate operational purposes and human-review safeguards. A feature should not be retained merely because it predicts, and should not be removed solely because it is correlated; the decision should account for fairness, governance, usefulness, and transportability.

7. **Monitor both schema and process drift.** Alert on new or missing fields, changed timestamps, future as-of matches, late-arriving data, sequence-index loss, mutable fields without versions, impossible activity edges, new process variants, queue-snapshot staleness, subgroup calibration changes, and holiday or policy regime shifts. Re-run the conformance and explanation audit whenever the workflow, warehouse schema, target definition, or feature computation changes.

8. **Separate prediction from intervention claims.** Use the repaired explanations to describe what the model uses at t0. If the organization wants to claim that reallocating staff, reducing queues, changing routing, or altering a process will prevent breaches, test those interventions with a suitable causal design or controlled operational experiment rather than inferring effects from feature importance.
