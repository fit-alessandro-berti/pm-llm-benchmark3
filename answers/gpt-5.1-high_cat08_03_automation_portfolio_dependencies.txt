## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

### Upstream data quality dependencies

- **A1, A2, A5** sit at or near the intake and directly shape data quality for the rest of the process.  
  - Poor configuration in **A1/A2** propagates wrong product/exposure flags, causing misrouted KYC paths, missing collateral valuations, and incorrect committee requirements.  
  - **A5** determines whether Financial document review receives complete, well-instructed files or keeps cycling through the Financial document review → Request missing documents → Financial document review loop.
- **A6** relies on both the **existence** and **structure** of financial statements; without A5’s checklists and portal nudging, A6 will be forced to handle many low-quality PDFs, undermining extraction quality and increasing exception handling.
- **A8** (Committee pack auto-assembly) is highly sensitive to upstream consistency: KYC status (KYC check and Resolve entity mismatch), normalized statements (A6), collateral valuations (A7), and Credit analysis notes. Garbage-in/garbage-out here can cause last-minute pack fixes and committee delays.
- **A9** needs clean, authoritative data from:  
  - **Committee decision** (terms, conditions, limits),  
  - KYC status, and  
  - Product/exposure classification (A2) to pick the right template and control rules.  
  Any weakness upstream turns contract automation into a fast way of replicating errors at scale.
- **A10** depends on the validity of contracts from **A9** (or existing manual contracts) and the correct KYC status; otherwise, it can accelerate incorrectly constructed or prematurely signed deals.
- **A11** is at the tail but critically depends on *all* upstream controls being correctly represented in core systems; it must never “paper over” missing data or unresolved remediation.
- **A12** relies on instrumentation across the process:  
  - detection of KYC-before-signing violations,  
  - Contract preparation → Remediate control issue → Contract preparation loops, and  
  - four-eyes violations in High-exposure SME cases.

### Legal/compliance controls

- **KYC and sanctions controls** primarily involve KYC check, Resolve entity mismatch, and downstream legal steps.  
  - **A3** influences which cases are fast-tracked versus escalated to Compliance specialists. It must enforce conservative thresholds, especially because the handover network shows high dependency on two senior specialists.  
  - **A4** supports resolving mismatches, which are both a major source of delay and a control point to ensure the correct legal entity signs the contract.
- **Contract and committee consistency** controls span Committee decision, Contract preparation, Customer signing, and Loan activation.  
  - **A9** is the main automation candidate to enforce that contract terms match Committee decision and exposure parameters, addressing the high rate of contract-remediation loops, especially in Credit-line renewals.  
  - **A10** must enforce “no signing without KYC clearance” to stop the observed pattern where Customer signing occurs provisionally and is remediated later.
- **Four-eyes principle** in High-exposure SME cases requires a separation between Credit analysis and Committee packaging.  
  - **A8** can help enforce that separation by routing packaging to compliant staff and logging who contributed to which part of the pack.  
  - **A12** can detect and monitor four-eyes violations as part of its remediation dashboard.
- **Auditability** cuts across A2, A3, A6, A8, A9, and A11. Each must store: model input, suggestion, human override, and final decision in a tamper-evident way to meet the “auditable trail” requirement and support internal/external reviews.

### Human escalation capacity

- **Compliance specialists (3 FTE)** are a thin resource.  
  - **A3** must reduce trivial workload and prioritize truly risky hits, not simply push more borderline cases into their queue. Metrics like “specialist time per escalated case” and “age of highest-risk open cases” need to be monitored.  
  - **A4** should be framed as a decision-support tool that reduces time spent per mismatch while keeping final decisions with specialists, especially for High-exposure SME and complex group structures.
- **Credit analysts (9 FTE)** are central in the working-together network and are at risk of overload if upstream automation only increases clean inflow.  
  - **A5** and **A1/A2** may increase the share of applications that arrive quickly with complete data. Without **A6**, analysts might face higher throughput expectations without relief on manual normalization.  
  - **A6** must therefore be paired with inflow-boosting automations so that analyst time per case falls at least as fast as clean inflow rises.
- **Legal ops (4 FTE)** are underutilized when upstream KYC is slow, but overloaded at month-end.  
  - **A9** can reduce their drafting work, allowing them to focus on exceptions and policy/rule maintenance.  
  - **A12** will initially increase their workload by surfacing more contract and control issues; capacity planning and phased rollout are necessary.
- **Relationship managers (RMs)** drive many missing-document loops and handle customer communication.  
  - **A5** and **A10** must be designed to assist RMs (e.g., pre-built instructions, status tracking) rather than bypass them; otherwise, resistance will be high, and work will shift from RMs to back-office support or customers.

### External valuation dependency

- **Collateral valuation** is an external bottleneck, with Wait for external valuation showing a 58h median wait.  
  - **A7** can triage cases (e.g., low vs. high exposure) and route them to valuers with appropriate capacity and specialization, and automate reminders.  
  - The value of **A7** depends on early and accurate identification of collateral requirements (via Classify product and exposure and potentially A2), and on external partners honoring SLAs.
- **Committee packaging (A8)** and **Committee decision** both wait on collateral valuations when required. Hence, automating **A7** without improving KYC/docs/normalization may only marginally reduce total cycle time; the full benefit comes when A5/A6 are already improving the readiness of non-collateral inputs.

### Core-banking integration constraints

- The “deep integration” limit (two modules in the first six months) mainly affects modules that must read/write core records or change legal/financial state: **A1, A2, A6, A7, A8, A9, A10, A11, A12** to varying degrees.
- The most critical deep integrations in the first wave are:  
  - **A9** – because it directly manipulates contracts and control rules tied to exposures and Committee decisions.  
  - **A10** – because it must ensure signed contracts and KYC status are correctly reflected before Loan activation and because it touches customer-facing flows.  
  Other modules (e.g., **A5** as a portal layer, or pilot versions of **A6**) can initially be implemented with lighter integration to avoid exceeding IT’s early capacity.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

### 3.1 Quick-win portfolio

**Proposed modules:** A1 (Auto-registration), A5 (Missing-document checklist and nudging), A9 (Contract generation with control checks), A10 (E-signature and reminders).

- **What it optimizes**
  - **Political and visible wins**:  
    - A1 and A10 provide easily communicated improvements (“faster onboarding, digital signing”) on very visible steps: Receive application → Register application and Contract preparation → Customer signing.
  - **End-to-end customer experience**:  
    - A5 reduces frustrating document back-and-forth; A9 and A10 make the final stages (contract and signing) smoother and more predictable.
  - **Controls on contracts**:  
    - A9 directly addresses contract-remediation loops and ensures better alignment between Committee decision and contract terms.
- **What it sacrifices**
  - **Limited focus on KYC bottlenecks**:  
    - KYC check, Resolve entity mismatch, and KYC-before-signing violations are only indirectly addressed; A3/A4/A12 are not yet implemented.
  - **Deferred treatment of analyst bottlenecks**:  
    - No A6, so improved completeness from A5 and faster intake from A1 may increase pressure on Credit analysts, who already are central and at risk of overload.
  - **Partial treatment of synchronization waits**:  
    - External valuation and Committee packaging remain as-is; the Receive application → Register application speed-up may actually make queues more visible downstream without resolving them.

### 3.2 Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio

**Proposed modules:** A4 (Entity-mismatch assistant), A5 (Missing-document checklist and nudging), A6 (Financial-statement extraction and normalization), A8 (Committee pack auto-assembly).

- **What it optimizes**
  - **Major internal waiting points and rework loops**:  
    - **A4** addresses long waits around Resolve entity mismatch, which block both KYC and Contract preparation on the critical path.  
    - **A5** reduces the Financial document review → Request missing documents cycles and 8h+ waits.  
    - **A6** cuts the 20h median wait for Normalize financial statements and shortens the path to Credit analysis.  
    - **A8** shortens the 26h wait for Committee packaging once prerequisites are ready.
  - **Synchronization of parallel tracks**:  
    - Because KYC and Financial document review run partly in parallel, A4/A5/A6/A8 align their completion times so Committee packaging can start promptly, reducing idle time near the Committee decision step.
- **What it sacrifices**
  - **Customer-facing “wow” factor**:  
    - No A1 or A10, so the improvements are mostly internal; customers still experience paper-heavy or manual signing and a slow-feeling start.
  - **Direct control reinforcement**:  
    - Although KYC delays and mismatches are reduced, explicit control-enforcing modules like A3, A9, and A12 are missing; control improvements are more indirect.
  - **Tail-end optimization**:  
    - Contract preparation, Customer signing, and Loan activation are left largely manual, so some late-stage delays persist even as upstream improves.

### 3.3 Compliance-first portfolio

**Proposed modules:** A3 (KYC hit pre-classifier), A4 (Entity-mismatch assistant), A9 (Contract generation with control-rule checks), A12 (Control remediation workbench).

- **What it optimizes**
  - **Regulatory and control risk reduction**:  
    - **A3** speeds low-risk KYC while sharpening focus on true hits, reducing risk exposure in the KYC check → Resolve entity mismatch area.  
    - **A4** reduces both delay and residual risk in entity resolution, especially important for High-exposure SME and cross-border entities.  
    - **A9** enforces alignment between Committee decision and Contract preparation, directly addressing observed contract-remediation loops and Credit-line renewal inconsistencies.  
    - **A12** increases visibility of control violations (e.g., KYC-before-signing, four-eyes breaches, contract mismatches) and organizes remediation.
  - **Auditability and oversight**:  
    - This combination builds strong audit trails and dashboards across KYC, contract, and remediation activities.
- **What it sacrifices**
  - **Cycle time and customer experience**:  
    - No A5, A6, A7, A8, or A10; large waiting times at missing-document loops, committee packaging, external valuation, and signing remain largely unchanged.  
    - The process becomes safer before it becomes substantially faster.
  - **Analyst workload reduction**:  
    - No A6; Credit analysts may remain a bottleneck even as compliance work is better triaged.

### 3.4 Balanced “control and bottleneck” portfolio (basis for recommendation)

**Proposed modules:** A5 (Missing-document checklist and nudging), A6 (Financial-statement extraction and normalization), A9 (Contract generation with control-rule checks), A10 (E-signature and reminders).

- **What it optimizes**
  - **Data quality and analyst productivity**:  
    - A5 reduces document-related rework and waiting; A6 reduces analyst effort per case and speeds availability of normalized data for Credit analysis and Committee packaging.
  - **End-of-process delays and contract controls**:  
    - A9 and A10 collectively reduce Contract preparation and Customer signing waits and cut remediation loops while strengthening contract controls and KYC-before-signing enforcement.
  - **Customer-visible improvements**:  
    - A10 produces a clear experience upgrade (digital, reminder-driven signing) without forcing customers into excessive rigidity if designed thoughtfully.
- **What it sacrifices**
  - **Early intake automation**:  
    - A1 and A2 are deferred; manual registration and classification remain but are not primary bottlenecks compared to KYC, documents, and signing.
  - **Deep KYC automation**:  
    - A3 and A4 are not in the first-year budget, so KYC improvements rely on better documents and clearer processes rather than ML-driven hit triage or entity-resolution assistants.
  - **Explicit remediation tooling**:  
    - A12 is postponed; remediation still happens but without a dedicated workbench, though some remediation volume will fall due to better contracts (A9).

## 4. Expected process-level effects

### 4.1 Quick-win portfolio (A1, A5, A9, A10)

- **Cycle time**
  - Faster Receive application → Register application via A1 and fewer loops at Financial document review → Request missing documents (A5) shorten early and mid-process steps.  
  - A9 and A10 reduce Contract preparation and Customer signing durations and waits, so the tail end (Contract preparation → Customer signing → Loan activation) becomes significantly shorter.
- **Waiting time**
  - Median waiting time before Register application (1.5h) is reduced, but this is a relatively small share of total cycle time.  
  - More impactful reductions come from:  
    - Document loops (A5) and  
    - Long waits before Customer signing (44h) through A10’s reminders and digital signing.  
  - External valuation waits and Committee packaging waits remain mostly unchanged.
- **Rework**
  - A5 reduces document rework loops; A9 addresses Contract preparation → Remediate control issue → Contract preparation loops by enforcing rule checks.  
  - Misclassification issues (Classify product and exposure) and KYC exception loops (KYC check → Resolve entity mismatch → KYC check) are not directly addressed.
- **Handovers**
  - A1 may reduce back-and-forth between Portal/clerks and Back office at intake.  
  - A10 reduces RM/customer handovers for signing and reminders by automating contacts, but KYC and valuation handovers are unaffected.
- **Resource demand**
  - Back office data-entry demand drops due to A1; some of that capacity can be redeployed to managing document completeness and exceptions.  
  - Legal ops drafting work drops due to A9; they can reallocate time to higher-risk cases and rule maintenance.  
  - Analysts and Compliance specialists see little direct relief and may face more smooth, possibly higher-volume inflow.
- **Control violations**
  - Contract mismatch violations reduce due to A9.  
  - If A10 is configured to hard-block signing until KYC is complete, KYC-before-signing violations should fall; otherwise, they may persist or even become easier to perform quickly.  
  - Four-eyes principle violations remain unaffected.

### 4.2 Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio (A4, A5, A6, A8)

- **Cycle time**
  - A4 reduces long delays on Resolve entity mismatch, improving cases where mismatches occur and often lie on the critical path.  
  - A5 and A6 significantly reduce time before Credit analysis by cutting document loops and normalization time.  
  - A8 reduces time between Credit analysis/valuation completion and Committee decision by enabling earlier, automated Committee packaging.
- **Waiting time**
  - KYC-related waits (4h median before KYC check and 31h before Resolve entity mismatch) shrink due to faster mismatch resolution (A4) and smoother KYC progress.  
  - The 18–20h waits in Financial document review and Normalize financial statements are reduced by A5 and A6.  
  - The 26h median wait for Committee packaging is cut as A8 assembles packs whenever prerequisites are met.
- **Rework**
  - A5 reduces frequent document rework loops (Financial document review → Request missing documents → Financial document review).  
  - A6 standardizes inputs, reducing rework in Credit analysis and Committee packaging caused by inconsistent formats.  
  - Committee packaging rework is reduced when A8 pulls consistent documents, assuming upstream quality is strong.
- **Handovers**
  - Fewer loops between Back office, RMs, and customers around missing documents; fewer back-and-forths between analysts and RMs to “fix” packs before committee.  
  - However, committee and valuation handover patterns remain mostly unchanged; only the timeliness of packaging improves.
- **Resource demand**
  - Compliance specialists’ demand for mismatch-related work becomes more focused (A4) but may not drop dramatically in volume; time per mismatch decreases.  
  - Credit analysts benefit substantially from A6, reducing their per-case effort and freeing capacity for judgment-intensive work.  
  - RMs and analysts do less manual collation for Committee packaging due to A8.
- **Control violations**
  - Entity-resolution errors may fall due to decision-support from A4, but strong human review is still needed.  
  - KYC-before-signing violations and contract/committee mismatches remain largely unaffected because signing and contract generation are not part of this portfolio.  
  - Four-eyes violations might be reduced if A8 is configured to enforce role separation, but that would require explicit design.

### 4.3 Compliance-first portfolio (A3, A4, A9, A12)

- **Cycle time**
  - A3 and A4 speed KYC-related steps for many cases while ensuring more effective prioritization of high-risk ones; KYC check and Resolve entity mismatch waiting times decrease.  
  - A9 shortens Contract preparation, particularly for straightforward deals.  
  - A12 mainly reduces the time to remediate detected control issues, trimming parts of the Remediate control issue loop.
- **Waiting time**
  - Waits before KYC check and before Resolve entity mismatch shrink, but document and valuation waits remain high.  
  - Waits before Customer signing and external valuation are largely unchanged, so total end-to-end times may still be dominated by those.
- **Rework**
  - KYC rework due to mis-hits and mismatches is reduced via A3 and A4, though more accurate detection might temporarily increase discovered exceptions.  
  - Contract remediation rework is significantly lowered by A9.  
  - A12 allows more systematic remediation with clearer ownership and tracking, reducing recurrence in the medium term.
- **Handovers**
  - Internal KYC escalations from Compliance ops to specialists become more structured, with clearer triage and escalation from A3 and A4.  
  - There may be more visible remediation handovers coordinated through A12, but each is more efficient and auditable.
- **Resource demand**
  - Compliance specialists’ time is focused on meaningful escalations, but initial tuning of A3 may increase escalated volume until thresholds stabilize.  
  - Legal ops benefit from A9’s automation but may be drawn into remediation coordination via A12.  
  - Analysts and RMs see little direct workload change.
- **Control violations**
  - Expected significant reduction in:  
    - Undetected KYC issues,  
    - Entity mismatch problems, and  
    - Contract terms not matching Committee decision.  
  - A12 helps identify and quantify four-eyes violations and KYC-before-signing breaches, but actual reduction of those violations depends on how strongly the bank enforces remediation outcomes and adjusts frontline behavior.

### 4.4 Balanced “control and bottleneck” portfolio (A5, A6, A9, A10)

- **Cycle time**
  - A5 and A6 together reduce cycle time through the KYC/docs/analysis/committee segment by minimizing missing-document loops and speeding Normalize financial statements → Credit analysis.  
  - A9 and A10 cut the tail from Committee decision → Contract preparation → Customer signing → Loan activation by reducing drafting and signing delays.
- **Waiting time**
  - Median waits before Financial document review and Normalize financial statements fall as documents arrive more complete (A5) and are processed faster (A6).  
  - The 44h median waiting time at Customer signing is substantially reduced via A10’s digital workflow and reminders.  
  - KYC-specific waits and external valuation waits remain but become relatively larger shares of total lead time, highlighting them for subsequent waves.
- **Rework**
  - Document-related rework cycles shrink with A5; poorly formatted statements are less likely to cause back-and-forth with analysts because A6 standardizes inputs.  
  - Contract-related rework loops are reduced via A9’s rule checks, with fewer Contract preparation → Remediate control issue → Contract preparation iterations.  
  - E-sign reminders (A10) reduce manual reminder loops on Customer signing.
- **Handovers**
  - Fewer document and contract loops mean fewer handovers between Back office, RMs, customers, analysts, and Legal ops.  
  - Handovers become more “single-pass”: from normalized data to Credit analysis, then clean contracts to signing, then to Loan activation.
- **Resource demand**
  - Back office workload on document chasing falls; RMs spend less time clarifying document requirements and chasing signatures.  
  - Credit analysts gain capacity due to A6’s normalization; they can handle increased volumes or dedicate more time to judgment-heavy High-exposure SME cases without burnout.  
  - Legal ops shift from drafting to oversight and rule maintenance with A9; they also see reduced month-end overload due to fewer last-minute contract fixes.  
  - Operations (Loan activation) may see more stable inflow of ready-to-activate loans but not an overwhelming increase given the existing 8h wait and 16min service time.
- **Control violations**
  - Contract/committee mismatches decrease thanks to A9.  
  - If configured correctly, A10 will enforce KYC status before enabling signing, reducing KYC-before-signing violations.  
  - Four-eyes principle violations are not directly addressed but become easier to monitor if contract data and workflows are more structured; this can be combined with manual governance in the short term.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

- **Auditability and decision transparency**
  - All model-based or rule-based modules (A2, A3, A6, A7, A8, A9, A11) must log: inputs, model version, suggested action, human override, and final outcome.  
  - Compensating controls: centralized logging service, immutable audit records, and regular internal audits that sample decisions at KYC, credit, collateral, and contract stages.
- **False positives and false negatives**
  - **A3 (KYC pre-classifier)** and **A7 (valuation triage)** pose the greatest risk of dangerous false negatives; **A2** and **A6** can create subtle mis-routings or mis-measurements.  
  - Compensating controls:  
    - Conservative thresholds and always-on human review for high-exposure or unusual patterns.  
    - Shadow mode deployments comparing model output with current manual decisions for an initial period.  
    - Key risk indicators (KRIs) such as “confirmed KYC violations after automated green flags” tracked at senior level.
- **Model drift and policy changes**
  - Over time, customer mix, product features, and regulatory requirements change, making static models and rule sets obsolete.  
  - Compensating controls:  
    - Formal model-governance process with periodic recalibration and back-testing for A2, A3, A6, A7.  
    - Versioned rule libraries for A9 and A11 with structured change management, approvals, and regression testing.
- **Exception handling and human workload shifts**
  - Automation can inadvertently route more difficult, ambiguous cases to human experts, concentrating cognitive load on a small set of Compliance specialists, senior analysts, and Legal staff.  
  - Compensating controls:  
    - Explicit capacity planning and staffing scenarios using process-mining data (e.g., volume of escalations after A3).  
    - Clear exception workflows (including SLAs and ownership) in A3, A4, A6, A8, A9, A12 so that exceptions do not get stuck.  
    - Training and change management so RMs and back-office staff understand new workflows and do not bypass them with workarounds.
- **Four-eyes compliance and segregation of duties**
  - Automating parts of Credit analysis, Committee packaging, or Contract preparation can blur role boundaries if not carefully designed.  
  - Compensating controls:  
    - In systems touched by A6, A8, and A9, enforce that no single user can both perform Credit analysis and complete Committee packaging for High-exposure SME.  
    - Use A12’s remediation/dashboard capabilities (in later phases) to monitor all detected four-eyes violations and enforce corrective action.
- **Customer identity and consent**
  - A10 (e-sign) introduces risks around verifying customer identity and obtaining proper consent, especially when combined with remote channels.  
  - Compensating controls:  
    - Strong authentication mechanisms linked to KYC records.  
    - Explicit consent flows, including clear display of final terms derived from Committee decision via A9.  
    - Strict KYC-complete precondition before any signing invitation is sent.
- **Over-automation of downstream steps without robust upstream controls**
  - A11 (Loan activation bot) is especially risky if implemented before KYC, contract, and committee controls are fully automated and reliable.  
  - Compensating controls:  
    - Explicit rule that A11 is only considered after A9 and related control tooling are stable with low error rates.  
    - Hard technical gating in activation workflows that blocks activation if any KYC, collateral, or contract flags remain open.

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

Given the budget limit of four modules in the first year and the constraint of only two deep core-banking integrations in the first six months, the recommended rollout centers on the **balanced portfolio** (A5, A6, A9, A10) with staged depth of integration.

### Phase 0 (Months 0–3): Design, governance, and instrumentation

- Establish **model and rule governance**: define owners for KYC, credit, contract, and activation rules; agree on audit-log requirements for all future modules.  
- Enhance **process-mining dashboards** to track baseline metrics: end-to-end lead time by segment/product, waiting times at KYC, documents, valuation, committee, contract, and signing; rework loops; control violations (KYC-before-signing, four-eyes breaches, contract mismatches).  
- Prepare **data standards** for documents and contracts so that A5, A6, and A9 can operate on consistent structures.

### Phase 1 (Months 3–6): First go-lives within deep-integration limit

Use the two deep integrations for the **control-critical and customer-visible tail** while starting light-weight data-quality improvements upstream.

- **Deep integration 1 – A9: Contract generation with control-rule checks**
  - Integrate with Committee decision data, product/exposure classification, and KYC status.  
  - Start with lower-risk segments (e.g., Micro and standard Working-capital products) and gradually expand coverage.  
  - **Key metrics and gates:**  
    - ≥50% reduction in contract-remediation loops, especially for Credit-line renewals.  
    - Near-elimination of “contract terms do not match Committee decision” issues for automated contracts.  
    - No material legal incidents attributable to automation during pilot.
- **Deep integration 2 – A10: E-signature adoption and reminder automation**
  - Integrate with the contract repository and KYC status so that invitations are only sent for KYC-cleared cases.  
  - Launch for segments with high digital adoption first, then expand.  
  - **Key metrics and gates:**  
    - Reduction of median Customer signing wait from 44h by at least 30–40% in pilot segments.  
    - Reduction of signing-related reminder loops by at least 50%.  
    - Zero instances of signing before KYC clearance in the automated flow.
- **Light integration – A5 (initial version)**
  - Implement as checklists and improved instructions in portals/CRM without heavy core-banking changes.  
  - Focus on the highest-frequency missing documents identified from the Financial document review → Request missing documents loop.  
  - **Key metrics:** decrease in requests for missing documents per case and improved first-pass completeness.

### Phase 2 (Months 6–12): Expand upstream data quality and analyst productivity

- **Scale and enrich A5**
  - Extend checklists and nudging rules to more products (Equipment finance and Credit-line renewal), and tailor by exposure band.  
  - Introduce RM dashboards highlighting cases blocked by missing documents to reduce unnecessary customer friction.
- **Introduce A6: Financial-statement extraction and normalization**
  - Now that document quality is improved, integrate A6 with the document store and credit tools, starting with Micro and simpler SME cases.  
  - Require analyst verification for key figures, especially in High-exposure SME cases.  
  - **Key metrics and gates:**  
    - Reduction in Normalize financial statements waiting time and service time per case.  
    - No significant increase in post-approval credit-quality issues attributable to extraction errors.  
    - Stable or reduced time spent by analysts per application.
- **Stabilization and tuning**
  - Iterate on A9’s rule library based on observed exceptions and regulatory updates.  
  - Tune A10 reminder frequency and channel mix to balance faster signing with minimal customer friction (monitor opt-out and complaint rates).  
  - Use process-mining to ensure that end-to-end cycle time improvements do not rely on increased policy exceptions.

### Monitoring metrics and gate criteria (across phases)

- **Cycle and waiting times**
  - Median and 90th percentile end-to-end lead times by segment/product.  
  - Waiting times at KYC, Financial document review, Normalize financial statements, Wait for external valuation, Committee packaging, Contract preparation, and Customer signing.
- **Rework and loops**
  - Frequency of Financial document review → Request missing documents → Financial document review loops.  
  - Frequency of Contract preparation → Remediate control issue → Contract preparation loops.
- **Control indicators**
  - Rate of KYC-before-signing violations.  
  - Rate of contract/committee mismatches.  
  - Four-eyes violations on High-exposure SME cases.
- **Resource and workload metrics**
  - Utilization and overtime for Compliance specialists, Credit analysts, Legal ops, and Back office.  
  - Volume and age of exceptions in each queue (KYC escalations, document exceptions, contract exceptions).
- **Gate criteria**
  - Only expand coverage of each module (to more segments/products) when:  
    - Process KPIs (cycle time, rework) improve or remain stable.  
    - Control KPIs (violations) do not worsen and ideally improve.  
    - No critical incidents related to automation are recorded in the prior review period.

## 7. Final recommendation

I recommend implementing the **balanced “control and bottleneck” portfolio** in the first year: **A5 (Missing-document checklist and nudging), A6 (Financial-statement extraction and normalization), A9 (Contract generation with control-rule checks), and A10 (E-signature and reminders)**, following the phased rollout above.

- This portfolio **does not automate the highest-frequency step first** (Register application via A1) because process-mining evidence shows that end-to-end performance is dominated by deeper synchronization points and rework loops: KYC/entity mismatches, missing documents, financial-statement normalization, contract remediation, and slow signing. Automating A1 alone would simply push more applications into existing bottlenecks, risking overload of Compliance ops and Credit analysts without materially reducing total cycle time.  
- Instead, the recommended modules **improve data quality and analyst productivity upstream (A5, A6) and enforce strong controls while shortening the legal/signing tail (A9, A10)**, directly addressing high waiting times and control findings around contract consistency and KYC-before-signing.  
- This approach satisfies the requirement that at least one first-wave module strengthens controls (A9, and potentially A10 when configured to enforce KYC clearance), respects the two-deep-integration limit in the first six months, and prepares the ground for future waves (e.g., KYC pre-classification, entity-mismatch assistants, activation bot) to safely build on a more reliable and better-instrumented process.  
- In summary, **frequency is treated as one factor among many**; by prioritizing modules where delays, rework, and control risk intersect, the bank achieves a more defensible, resilient, and customer-friendly automation journey than it would by simply automating the highest-frequency activity first.
