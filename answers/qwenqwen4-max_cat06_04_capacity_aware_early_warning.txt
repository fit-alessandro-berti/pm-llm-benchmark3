The rule therefore scores **expected causal net value under uncertainty**, not breach probability, and it separates *eligibility* (hard gates) from *priority* (soft scoring) from *timing* (option value).

# 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Alert identity.** An alert is keyed by `(case_id, mechanism_hypothesis, evidence_hash)`. Refreshing the model does **not** create a new alert.

- **Suppression:** same evidence_hash and |p_adj| < 0.05  collapse into the existing alert; increment a counter; no re-scoring for capacity; surface once per shift in a digest.
- **Cool-down:** after a swarm, **90 min hard cool-down**; a second swarm requires a materially new event **and** a *different* mechanism hypothesis **and** senior sign-off. Lifetime cap: 2 swarms/case unless a duty-manager override is logged.
- **Materially new event (whitelist):** new customer log/telemetry; failed fix attempt; scope/complexity change; dependency or customer-side status change; **stage stall** (no stage transition for > 60 min while the window narrows); calibration-relevant data repair (e.g., failed feature restored).
- **Re-entry:** new event  recompute p_adj, ARR_adj, break-even, tier. Note that **p sign is not the trigger**: actionability, not probability direction, governs.
- **Escalation ladder:** (1) suppressed alert with rising calibrated risk over 3 refreshes  mandatory human review (protects against a policy that is systematically blind); (2) window closing with positive net  immediate duty-senior page; (3) any case declined 3× with p_adj > 0.6  senior review + documented non-swarm plan.
- **Temporal allocation:** re-optimise every 30 min, but **commit only at swarm start**; unstarted allocations are options, not promises. Reserve checkpoints are time-boxed against latest-feasible-start. Unused slots never create pressure to lower the bar (§6 trigger).

| | I-05 | I-07 |
|---|---|---|

| Evidence rule | **Fails**  suppressed, no slot | **Passes**  legitimately re-scored despite p falling 0.750.72 |

# 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**1. Value-based prioritisation starving low-value cases.** With V spanning €5k–€100k, RANEV is effectively a contract-value ranking; I-04 and I-08 dominate purely on V. Safeguards: (a) **floor quota** — 25% of weekly slots to cases below the median contract value, with the break-even bar relaxed to zero-net for that quota; (b) publish a **shadow ranking with V winsorised at the 75th percentile** each day and log every rank flip (a standing audit of how much money is driving allocation); (c) **minimum service standard**: every eligible high-risk case receives a non-slot mitigation (senior consult, info-chasing, customer comms) within 30 min, so "no swarm"  "no service"; (d) monitor **avoidable-breach rate** by segment, not breach rate.

**2. NNLS uncertainty.** Sparse validation must not become a hidden penalty. Safeguards: hierarchical/partial-pooling estimates (shrink toward global, never toward zero); **uncertainty is charged symmetrically** — wide intervals raise both upside and downside, and the case is not dropped for being unmeasured; explicit **learning quota** (1 slot in 8; I-09 today) with interpreter present and structured effect logging; a target of n  150 treated + matched control NNLS cases before any segment-specific effect claim; quarterly report on NNLS sample accrual.

**3. Differential calibration.** Uncorrected, SME is under-served (under-prediction) and Strategic over-served. But the corrections themselves are risky: the Strategic offset follows a *recent redesign* and may be transient. Safeguards: time-decayed offsets with weekly re-estimation and CIs; offsets expire unless re-validated; never apply an offset without n  200; report decisions under both raw and corrected p for one shift per week; alarm if a segment's selection rate moves >10 pp purely from an offset change.

**4. Selective intervention corrupting future labels.** Swarmed cases that don't breach teach the model "cases like this are safe"  future under-alerting on exactly the cases that most need help (performativity/treatment leakage). Safeguards: log treatment flag, propensity, policy version, mechanism; **exclude treatment indicators from the deployed risk model's features** and train the risk model on the *untreated* stream (target = "breach under no swarm"); keep a **5–10% randomised holdout inside the near-break-even band** (obligation and clearly-robust cases excluded — randomise only where we are genuinely indifferent); use intent-to-treat labels for effect estimation; maintain separate risk and effect models; monitor treatment-rate drift as a first-class drift signal.

**5. Spillover harms concentrated on the untreated.** The six exposed cases are, by construction, the ones we *didn't* pick — plausibly lower-value SME/Public Sector. The Base row shows euros positive while net breach count  0: **we are transferring risk, not only removing it.** Safeguards: an **externality ledger** attributing measured interrupt minutes and modelled breach to named cases; a per-case cap (no case absorbs > 0.05 added breach risk per day from others' swarms; breach of cap  that case jumps the queue for the next slot or receives a compensating consult); **congestion throttle** (diagnosis-queue utilisation > 85%  max 2 slots/day and spillover multiplier ×1.5); report spillover concentration by segment (share of imposed delay, Gini) alongside slot allocation by segment; prefer swarm timing in low-congestion windows.

**6. Burden asymmetry.** €5,000 strategic disruption vs €50 SME means the *cost* term protects high-touch accounts from disruption while low-value customers absorb repeated interventions. Treat disruption as a **constraint** (max N interventions per customer per quarter), not only a price.

# 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Predictive.** Daily: alert volume, precision@k, imputation rate per feature (I-06's queue field is a named watch item), feature PSI. Weekly by segment: reliability curves, calibration intercept/slope, ECE, Brier, with CIs; segment n reported next to every metric. **Trigger:** |intercept| > 0.03 with n  200, or ECE > 0.05  recompute offsets; Strategic re-estimated weekly until two consecutive stable weeks.

**Causal effect (the weakest link — today's ranges are priors).** Instrument every decision: score, eligibility gate outcomes, propensity, mechanism hypothesis, abort events, swarm start/stop minutes. Outcomes: breach, **time-to-diagnosis-complete** (fast surrogate), time-to-resolution, and queue delay for untreated cases. Identification strategy, in order of strength: (1) randomised marginal-band holdout; (2) **capacity/specialist-absence shocks as instruments** for treatment; (3) regression discontinuity at the break-even ARR threshold; (4) matched difference-in-differences with pre-trend checks. Hierarchical partial pooling across segments; replace elicited ARR ranges with empirical posteriors as n accrues. **Power realism:** ~3–4 slots/day  ~80/month; detecting a 0.10 ARR with confidence needs several hundred per arm — so expect 6–12 months for segment-level claims, use surrogates and pooling in the interim, and **never present interim point estimates as identified effects**.

**Spillover.** Measure interrupt minutes directly from specialist time logs; estimate breach on untreated cases via congestion-instrumented matched comparisons; re-estimate the 0.01–0.03 coefficient and the "six exposed cases" count monthly. **Trigger:** measured per-case p > 0.03 or delay > 20 min/slot  raise , cut daily slots, re-price the reserve.

**Capacity.** Slot utilisation, unused-slot count (an *intended* KPI, not waste), reserve-release outcomes, regulatory-arrival time distribution (learn it and set the reserve checkpoint by option value rather than by habit), swarm overrun rate, abort rate, specialist interruption minutes, ordinary-diagnosis throughput.

**Fairness.** Selection rate by segment **conditional on eligibility and score band**; among cases that would have breached untreated, fraction served (equal-opportunity gap); avoidable-breach rate; suppressed-alert counts by segment (I-05-type suppression must not concentrate on SME); externality concentration; NNLS sample accrual; repeat-decline counts. **Trigger:** conditional selection gap > 10 pp for two consecutive weeks  activate/expand the floor quota and review V-winsorised ranking.

**Policy-induced drift and rule changes.** Pin model + policy versions; run a **challenger policy in shadow mode**; monthly off-policy evaluation (clipped IPS/DR on logged propensities) plus counterfactual replay of rejected cases. Pre-registered change rules: rolling-30-slot net impact < €1,000/slot  raise the break-even bar or cut slots; two consecutive weeks of negative realised net  **kill switch**, revert to consult-only mode pending review; a segment/stage whose empirical ARR upper CI falls below break-even  stop swarming that stratum (I-03's stage rule is the template); reserve checkpoint moved when the arrival distribution shifts. Governance: daily 12:00/16:00 ops huddle (decisions + overrides), weekly calibration board, monthly causal-and-fairness review, quarterly policy re-authorisation with a documented override register.

**Bottom line under uncertainty:** the 3-slot portfolio {I-09, I-02, I-04} is the choice that is clearly positive in Optimistic, positive in euros but roughly breach-neutral in Base, and bounded-but-negative in Adverse — with the abort rule, the congestion throttle, the reserved fourth slot, and the externality ledger as the instruments that keep the Adverse tail from becoming the realised outcome.
