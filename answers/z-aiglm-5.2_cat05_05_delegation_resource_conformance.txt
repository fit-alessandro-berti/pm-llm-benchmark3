For each case, the normalized human identity (after alias collapse) who executes `Draft_Change_Request` must differ from the normalized human who executes `Approve_Change`. Service accounts are excluded from this comparison unless individually attributed.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

### 4.1 Repeated Drafter–Approver Pair (Clara & Ben)

**Indicator:** Clara drafted and Ben approved 18 of 23 HIGH-risk production changes in 60 days. Same production cell. No rotation policy.

| Benign explanation | Adverse explanation |
|---|---|
| Small production cell with limited qualified QM delegates; Ben was the primary D-QM-01 delegate during Asha's absence (08-10–08-14); natural concentration. | Mutual convenience approval: Clara and Ben developed an informal "approve my drafts" arrangement, bypassing independent review. The 78% concentration suggests gatekeeping. |

**Discriminating evidence:** Review the substantive content of Ben's approvals — did he document independent technical review, or were approvals perfunctory? Check if other QM delegates were available and bypassed. Examine communication records for quid-pro-quo language. Analyze approval latency — if Ben approved within minutes of drafting, independent review is questionable.

### 4.2 Reciprocal LOW-Risk Approvals (Ben drafts, Clara approves)

**Indicator:** Ben drafted 4 LOW-risk training changes that Clara approved. No financial benefit known.

| Benign explanation | Adverse explanation |
|---|---|
| Cross-functional flexibility; Ben occasionally drafts training changes within his expertise; Clara approves as Process Engineer with document authority. No financial link. | Reciprocal back-scratching: Ben approves Clara's HIGH-risk drafts, Clara approves Ben's LOW-risk drafts. Even without financial benefit, the pattern normalizes mutual approval and erodes independent review. |

**Discriminating evidence:** Check whether Clara has formal authority to approve LOW-risk training changes (role master). Examine timing correlation — did Clara's approvals of Ben's drafts cluster around Ben's approvals of Clara's drafts? Review whether the four LOW-risk changes required substantive review or were routine.

### 4.3 Shared Workstation / Service-Account Attribution (CHG-503)

**Indicator:** `svc_qms` verification ran from Diego's workstation. Diego implemented the same change. Badge log shows Diego entered the room. No re-auth. CCTV unavailable.

| Benign explanation | Adverse explanation |
|---|---|
| Shared terminal in the production area; another engineer (e.g., Eva) used the same terminal after Diego, without re-authenticating individually. Service account was configured for automated verification. | Diego performed both implementation and verification, using the service account to mask the SoD breach. The shared terminal and absent CCTV facilitate this. |

**Discriminating evidence:** Badge exit time for Diego; badge entries for other personnel between 12:00 and 12:30; terminal session logs showing user switching; whether the system supports concurrent sessions; the service account's configured permissions.

### 4.4 Absence Conflict — Asha on Leave with VPN (CHG-504)

**Indicator:** Asha on annual leave but VPN session coincides with approval. Credential sharing cannot be excluded.

| Benign explanation | Adverse explanation |
|---|---|
| Asha genuinely handled one urgent HIGH-risk case from home during leave, consistent with her statement. VPN timing (09:15–09:40) brackets the approval (09:22). | Another person (possibly Ben or Clara) used Asha's credentials via VPN to approve a change that Ben's delegation would have covered anyway — suggesting urgency to bypass delegate or preference for principal signature. |

**Discriminating evidence:** VPN endpoint IP geolocation (home vs. office); device fingerprint (Asha's personal laptop vs. office machine); 2FA/biometric challenge logs; Asha's written, dated statement; whether other approvals occurred during the VPN window.

### 4.5 Absence Conflict — Ben on Sick Leave (CHG-506)

**Indicator:** Ben on certified sick leave from 12:00; account used at 13:00 from office IP. Ben denies performing the action.

| Benign explanation | Adverse explanation |
|---|---|
| Ben left a session open before going on sick leave; a colleague used the active session to approve an urgent change, assuming Ben's authority carried over. Ben genuinely did not perform it. | Someone intentionally used Ben's credentials (shared or known) to maintain the Clara-Ben approval pipeline, exploiting Ben's absence to approve changes under his name. The office IP (not Ben's home) suggests physical presence by another person. |

**Discriminating evidence:** Ben's badge exit time vs. session start; CCTV at the office workstation; login timestamps showing whether the session was continuous or newly initiated at 13:00; whether Ben's password was recently changed; IT logs for concurrent sessions.

### 4.6 Invalid Subdelegation Chain (CHG-510)

**Indicator:** Farid (valid IC delegate) attempted to subdelegate to Clara via email-only D-IC-04, despite D-IC-03's explicit prohibition. Clara then approved her own draft.

| Benign explanation | Adverse explanation |
|---|---|
| Emergency pressure during CI-44; Farid misunderstood subdelegation rules and informally asked Clara to handle it; Clara believed the email constituted valid authority. | Farid and Clara colluded to create a paper trail granting Clara approval authority she never held, enabling self-approval of her own draft under the guise of emergency response. The email-only evidence and the 10-minute gap between draft and approval suggest pre-arrangement. |

**Discriminating evidence:** Farid's statement on D-IC-04; whether the system validated D-IC-03's no-subdelegation clause; Clara's knowledge of delegation rules; the substantive urgency of CHG-510 vs. CHG-505 (both emergency, same incident, but CHG-505 used Farid properly); communication logs between Farid and Clara.

### 4.7 Summary of Suspicion vs. Proof

No single indicator proves collusion. The combination of (a) concentrated Clara-Ben approval pipeline, (b) reciprocal LOW-risk approvals, (c) Ben's account used during sick leave from an office IP, and (d) the invalid subdelegation enabling Clara's self-approval creates a **cumulative pattern warranting formal investigation**. However, each indicator has plausible benign explanations, and the absence of financial evidence weakens the collusion hypothesis. The strongest adverse indicator is CHG-506 (Ben's denial + office IP during sick leave), which points to potential credential sharing rather than collusion per se.

def check_approval_authority(row, delegations, standing_roles, asha_absence):
    """Check whether the approver had QM or valid IC authority at event time."""
    case = row['case:concept:name']
    ts = row['time:timestamp']
    resource = row['resource_normalized']
    risk = row['risk']
    scope = row['scope']
    emergency = row['emergency']

    result = {
        'case': case, 'timestamp': ts, 'approver_resource': resource,
        'risk': risk, 'scope': scope, 'emergency': emergency,
        'authority_source': None, 'delegation_id': None,
        'authority_valid': False, 'reason': []
    }

    # Standing QM check
    qm_roles = standing_roles[
        (standing_roles['role'] == 'QM') &
        (standing_roles['user_id'] == resource) &
        (standing_roles['role_start'] <= ts) &
        (standing_roles['role_end'] > ts)
    ]
    if len(qm_roles) > 0:
        result['authority_source'] = 'standing QM'
        result['authority_valid'] = True
        return result

    # Standing IC check (for emergency)
    if emergency:
        ic_roles = standing_roles[
            (standing_roles['role'].str.contains('IC')) &
            (standing_roles['user_id'] == resource) &
            (standing_roles['role_start'] <= ts) &
            (standing_roles['role_end'] > ts)
        ]
        if len(ic_roles) > 0:
            result['authority_source'] = 'standing IC'
            result['authority_valid'] = True
            return result

    # Delegation check
    matching_delegations = delegations[
        (delegations['delegate'] == resource) &
        (delegations['start'] <= ts) &
        (delegations['end'] > ts) &
        (delegations['scope'] == scope)
    ]
    for _, d in matching_delegations.iterrows():
        if not d['valid']:
            result['reason'].append(f"{d['delegation_id']} is invalid (subdelegation)")
            continue
        # Check principal absence if required
        if d['principal_absent_required'] and d['principal'] == 'u_as':
            if ts not in asha_absence:
                # Check if weekend (08-15, 08-16)
                if ts.weekday() < 5:
                    result['reason'].append(
                        f"{d['delegation_id']}: principal not confirmed absent"
                    )
                    continue
        result['authority_source'] = f"delegation {d['delegation_id']}"
        result['delegation_id'] = d['delegation_id']
        result['authority_valid'] = True
        return result

    if not result['authority_valid']:
        result['reason'].append(
            f"No standing QM/IC role or valid delegation for "
            f"{resource} at {ts} (scope={scope}, emergency={emergency})"
        )
    return result

from datetime import timedelta

def business_days_after(start_date):
    """Yield business days after start_date (not counting start_date itself)."""
    d = start_date + timedelta(days=1)
    while True:
        if d.weekday() < 5:  # Mon-Fri
            yield d
        d += timedelta(days=1)

def is_business_day(dt):
    return dt.weekday() < 5

def add_business_days(start, n):
    """Add n business days to start, not counting start itself."""
    count = 0
    d = start + timedelta(days=1)
    while count < n:
        if d.weekday() < 5:
            count += 1
        d += timedelta(days=1)
    return d - timedelta(days=1)  # last valid business day

                d['role'] == 'QM' and d['scope'] == impl_scope and

## 6. Control Improvements

### Delegation Creation
- Require delegation records to be entered in the QMS database **before** the effective interval begins. A signed PDF is necessary but not sufficient; the system should reject approvals by delegates whose delegation is not in the live database at event time.
- Add a mandatory scope-matching step: the system must validate that the case's `scope` field matches the delegation's `scope` before accepting the approval. This would have blocked Ben's retrospective review for CHG-505 (cooling_system) under D-QM-01 (production_equipment).
- Prohibit retroactive delegation database creation. If a migration creates records late, require a formal data-integrity audit and retroactive validation against signed source documents.

### Effective-Time Recording
- Store delegation intervals as UTC timestamps with millisecond precision and a mandatory `created_at` audit field.
- Implement a real-time validity check: at the moment of `Approve_Change` or `Retrospective_QM_Review`, the system queries active delegations and returns only those whose `start  event_time < end`.

### Principal Availability
- Integrate HR leave systems with the QMS so that principal-absence status is automatically available for delegation validation.
- Trigger security alerts when a user on formal leave establishes a VPN session or performs QMS actions. Require a documented justification (e.g., "urgent case handled personally") logged at the time, not retroactively.
- Consider whether a principal acting during leave should invalidate outstanding delegations (to avoid both principal and delegate acting simultaneously on different cases).

### Alias Management
- Enforce a single canonical user ID per person. Phase out legacy aliases like `b.koch` or migrate them to a formal alias table with system-level normalization.
- Display the canonical identity name on all approval screens so users cannot unknowingly act under a different alias.
- Run a periodic alias audit: detect cases where two user IDs map to the same human and flag any SoD violation that was masked by alias divergence.

### Individual Authentication
- Require individual re-authentication (username + password or FIDO token) for every `Approve_Change`, `Verify_Implementation`, and `Retrospective_QM_Review` action, regardless of terminal session state.
- On shared terminals, implement session lock with mandatory re-auth when switching between users.
- Deploy FIDO2 hardware tokens for all users authorized to approve HIGH-risk changes. The CHG-509 evidence (FIDO token + badge) demonstrates strong attribution; extend this standard to all cases.
- Retain CCTV coverage for all workstations used for change-control actions. The absence of CCTV for CHG-503 created an evidentiary gap.

### Emergency Review
- Implement an automated timer upon `Implement_Change` for emergency cases that counts business days and alerts the QM (and delegate) 4 hours before the 2-business-day deadline.
- Scope-check the retrospective reviewer's authority against the case scope, not just the role title. Ben's cooling_system review would have been blocked if the system validated D-QM-01's scope.
- Require the retrospective reviewer to document why the principal QM was unavailable and why the delegate was chosen.

### Pair Rotation
- Introduce a formal pair-rotation policy: no drafter–approver pair may exceed 50% of HIGH-risk approvals in any 30-day window. The 78% Clara-Ben concentration would have triggered review.
- Log and alert on reciprocal approvals: when person A approves person B's drafts and person B approves person A's drafts within the same period, flag for compliance review.
- Maintain a diversity metric: the percentage of HIGH-risk cases approved by different QM-authority holders. Low diversity should trigger workforce planning or delegation expansion.

### Network Monitoring
- Deploy periodic (weekly) `discover_handover_of_work_network` and `discover_working_together_network` analysis on the event log. Alert when a single handover edge exceeds a threshold (e.g., >60% of HIGH-risk cases).
- Monitor for unusual approval latency: if approval occurs within X minutes of drafting, flag for substantive-review-quality audit.
- Track service-account usage patterns: alert when a service account session originates from a workstation normally assigned to the implementer of the same case.

### Evidential Retention
- Retain CCTV, badge logs, VPN session logs, terminal session records, and authentication logs for a minimum of 7 years aligned with pharmaceutical GMP retention requirements.
- Link evidential records to change-control case IDs at event time, not retroactively. Each `Approve_Change` should create an evidence bundle (who, what terminal, what badge, what authentication method).
- For shared terminals, log the physical workstation ID, room, and badge-in/badge-out times as metadata on every change-control event.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

### Systemic Judgment

Of the ten cases examined, the apparent resource deviations break down as follows:

**Genuine authority failures (3 cases):** CHG-502 (self-approval, LOW risk), CHG-507 (non-QM approval of HIGH-risk change by Eva when she was Independent Verifier), and CHG-510 (Clara self-approved relying on an invalid subdelegation, with Ben performing an out-of-scope retrospective review). These represent real control breakdowns where the system permitted actions by resources lacking the required authority.

**Data/identity problems (2 cases):** CHG-503 (service-account verification unattributable due to shared terminal and absent re-authentication) and CHG-508 (alias usage masking a self-approval violation). These stem from identity management weaknesses rather than intentional misconduct.

**Suspicious but indeterminate (2 cases):** CHG-506 (Ben's account used during certified sick leave from an office IP, Ben denies action) and CHG-504 (Asha's VPN session during leave with credential-sharing risk). These require forensic investigation (badge logs, CCTV, device fingerprints) before classification.

**Authorized with caveats (3 cases):** CHG-501 (clean delegation), CHG-505 (valid emergency approval but late/out-of-scope retrospective review — the approval itself was authorized; the failure is in the retrospective step), and CHG-509 (valid delegation with strong service-account attribution).

### Organizational-Pattern Risk

The 78% concentration of Clara-Ben drafter–approver pairs, the reciprocal LOW-risk approvals, and the repeated use of Ben as the sole QM delegate during Asha's absence constitute a **significant organizational-pattern risk**. While no single indicator proves collusion (RR6), the cumulative pattern suggests that the four-eyes principle has been functionally eroded: Ben and Clara operate as an approval pipeline rather than as independent reviewers. The absence of a formal pair-rotation policy allowed this concentration to develop unchecked.

### Uncertainty

- **CHG-506** carries the highest individual-case uncertainty: whether Ben performed the approval or someone used his credentials cannot be resolved without badge-exit times, CCTV, and session-continuity logs.
- **CHG-504** is likely Asha's genuine action (VPN timing is consistent), but credential sharing cannot be excluded without device-level forensics.
- The Clara-Ben collusion hypothesis remains unproven; the evidence supports "erosion of independent review through routine collaboration" more strongly than "deliberate collusion."

### Three Highest-Priority Investigations

1. **CHG-506 credential compromise investigation:** Determine who used Ben Koch's account at 13:00 on 2026-08-13 from an office workstation while he was on certified sick leave. Obtain badge exit logs, CCTV footage, session-continuity records, and login IP details. If an impostor is confirmed, escalate to security and audit all approvals made from that workstation during the sick-leave period.

2. **Clara-Ben approval pipeline audit:** Review the substantive content of all 18 HIGH-risk production changes that Clara drafted and Ben approved in the preceding 60 days. Assess whether Ben performed genuine technical review or perfunctory approval. Examine approval latency, review documentation quality, and whether other qualified QM delegates were available but bypassed. Implement an immediate pair-rotation policy pending findings.

3. **CHG-510 invalid subdelegation and emergency-approval pathway review:** Investigate how Clara obtained and relied on D-IC-04 (the prohibited email-only subdelegation from Farid). Determine whether the system validated the no-subdelegation clause in D-IC-03, why it accepted Clara's approval, and whether Farid understood the prohibition. Review all emergency changes during CI-44 to identify other potential subdelegation attempts. Close the system gap that allowed self-approval under an invalid delegation.
