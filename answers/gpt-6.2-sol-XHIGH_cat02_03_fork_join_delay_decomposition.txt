# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

For case $c$ and branch $i\in\{A,B,C\}$, let $a_c$ be arrival, $r_c$ fork release, $s_{ci}$ and $e_{ci}$ branch start and completion, and $s_c^F$ and $e_c^F$ final start and completion. All differences are in minutes. Treat the logged start-to-completion intervals as service. Interpreting these intervals as continuously active work requires the additional assumption that work did not pause.

| Quantity | Definition |
|---|---|
| Upstream elapsed time | $U_c=r_c-a_c$ |
| Branch queue delay | $Q_{ci}=s_{ci}-r_c$ |
| Branch service | $S_{ci}=e_{ci}-s_{ci}$ |
| Release-to-completion time for a branch | $L_{ci}=Q_{ci}+S_{ci}=e_{ci}-r_c$ |
| Join readiness under the stated AND-join rule | $J_c=\max_i e_{ci}$ |
| Branch synchronization wait | $W_{ci}=J_c-e_{ci}$ |
| Fork-join elapsed time | $T_c=J_c-r_c=\max_i L_{ci}$ |
| Critical branch set | $K_c=\{i:L_{ci}=T_c\}$ |
| Downstream gap | $D_c=s_c^F-J_c$ |
| Final service | $P_c=e_c^F-s_c^F$ |
| Total case flow time | $Y_c=e_c^F-a_c=U_c+T_c+D_c+P_c$ |

$Q_{ci}$ and $D_c$ measure elapsed time after the earliest modeled eligibility point. Their interpretation as resource queues requires that the work is dispatchable at that point and waiting for a resource. The upstream interval may also mix work and waiting.

For each branch, $Q_{ci}+S_{ci}+W_{ci}=T_c$. Adding these identities across branches gives $3T_c$ because the branches share the same clock. In particular, $\sum_i W_{ci}$ counts each minute once for every completed branch still waiting. In F07, A and C wait simultaneously for 25 minutes, and C also waits alone for 27 minutes before A finishes. The sum is 77 branch-minutes in a 52-minute union of waiting intervals, all within the 75-minute fork-join stage. For an additive case decomposition, use $T_c$ once, or substitute $Q_{ck}+S_{ck}$ for any $k\in K_c$.

# 3. Aggregate critical-path analysis

The observed mean flow time is 115.25 minutes, comprising 19.58 upstream, 61.00 in the fork-join stage, 14.67 after join readiness, and 20.00 in final service. To quantify how much each branch controls readiness, define $H_{ci}=\max(0,L_{ci}-\max_{j\ne i}L_{cj})$. This is the most the join can advance by accelerating branch $i$ alone in case $c$ with the other branches fixed. It is zero if another branch already finishes at or after $i$. Means below include all 12 cases.

C controls F04 and F08, where its queues are 35 and 25 minutes, respectively. Its high mean synchronization wait reflects how early it finishes in most other cases.

All twelve cases have a unique critical branch at the recorded precision. The smallest leads over the runner-up are five minutes in F01, F08, and F10. Where a modeled change creates a tie, all last-finishing branches are co-critical and shortening any one of them further yields zero additional join gain on its own.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

For an isolated reduction of $x_{ci}$ minutes in one branch's queue or service, hold fork release, the other branch timings, and final service fixed. The earlier readiness is $\Delta J_c=\min(x_{ci},H_{ci})$. This calculation tests the effect of attaining the prescribed branch timing. Attaining it may require changing how branch resources are scheduled.

Consider three ways to map readiness into final completion. With each case's observed downstream gap held fixed, the earlier readiness passes through minute for minute. With fixed final appointments, all the modeled branch gains yield zero flow-time savings and become extra post-join wait. For a capacity-sensitive comparison, replay the final station under first-come, first-served (FCFS) by inferred readiness, with case ID breaking ties. The replay assumes initially idle, interchangeable servers, one server per case, immediate dispatch from a common queue, the supplied cases as the entire workload, and unchanged final service. Each acceleration is compared against a replay with the same two servers and original readiness times. Those assumptions make the model a sensitivity analysis for pooling and availability.

**A service reduced by 20%.** A's service becomes 40 minutes for every case. Readiness advances five minutes in F01 and ten minutes in F11, where B becomes co-critical with A. The other ten cases get zero join benefit because B or C already determines readiness. At a fixed downstream gap this is 15 case-minutes saved, or 1.25 per case. That arithmetic would start F11 at 11:40 while both stations have other work in the observed schedule, so delivering the full gain would require a schedule change. In the two-server replay, F01 saves five minutes and F11 saves zero. For F11 the earlier 11:20 readiness ties F10, F10 takes the free slot by case ID, and F11 still starts at 11:35. The nominal A service workload falls by 120 interval-minutes across the 12 cases if the redesign is feasible. That could free assay capacity and shift which branch is critical even where case flow time barely changes.

**B queue reduced by 50%.** Eight cases get earlier readiness. F01, F04, F08, and F11 get zero join benefit because A or C sets the join. At fixed downstream gaps the saving is 92.5 case-minutes, or 7.71 per case. For example, F07's B queue falls from 30 to 15 and the B path falls from 75 to 60, advancing readiness to 10:40. In F10 the ten-minute queue reduction buys only five minutes at the join because A then controls readiness. A also becomes solely critical in F05 and ties B in F02 and F06. In the two-server replay the eight B-critical cases retain their direct gains, and F11 finishes five minutes earlier because rescheduled cases free a server sooner. Total modeled flow savings rise to 97.5 case-minutes, or 8.125 per case. B's active workload stays fixed in the timing model, while earlier starts may change contention at B and arrival patterns at F. The fractional minute for F09 comes from scaling its observed queue, with no added measurement precision.

**Final dispatch and capacity.** Replay the original readiness times using immediate pooled dispatch on two servers, then add one server under the same policy. The two-server schedule saves 171 case-minutes relative to observed completion times, or 14.25 per case, with a positive saving in each case. This observed-to-replay comparison changes dispatch and resource eligibility assumptions. The incremental third server saves five more case-minutes, all in F11, or 0.42 per case. The other eleven cases can already start on readiness in the two-server replay and get zero benefit from the third server. Readiness and branch synchronization stay fixed in these replays. The change reduces modeled waiting at F while the third server spreads the same service workload across more capacity.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

1. **Reduce B's pre-start delay through a scheduling pilot.** Log when each test becomes dispatchable, which machine or operator can take it, and why it waits. Pilot earlier assignment of already eligible jobs and measure the queue distribution, join readiness, end-to-end flow-time tail, completions per staffed hour, and any change in test quality. The replay suggests the largest tail benefit comes from moving the B-critical cases earlier. If the same test work simply shifts earlier, expect lower waiting and similar total busy minutes. Throughput can rise if the pilot recovers time lost to avoidable dispatch or setup gaps on a resource that limits output. Check peak concurrency and machine and operator utilization, since advancing jobs can move contention to another time window or resource. A and C wait less for the join in cases where B finishes earlier and still determines readiness. When A comes to determine readiness, early B completions may themselves wait, and C remains the constraint in F04- and F08-like cases. Track whether earlier joins create bursts at F.

2. **Pilot readiness-based dispatch across the two final resources where cross-assignment is feasible.** Instrument the actual eligibility timestamp, any mandatory hold, staffing and assignment constraints, and reasons eligible work waits while a server is available. Compare current dispatch with a common ready queue under the same staffing, preserving required release checks and priorities. Measure flow-time tail, completed cases per staffed hour under load, and busy, staffed, and eligible-idle time by resource. Earlier dispatch reduces post-join waiting and can improve throughput when eligible jobs are held during usable capacity. The logged demand is 120 service-minutes per resource over the 220-minute first-ready-to-last-completion window, about 55% per resource within this sample and window. Pooling reschedules that work and may balance peaks. A third server would dilute utilization at this volume, with the replay showing limited incremental benefit. Pilot pooling first and measure whether simultaneous busy time still explains a material share of the tail. With the branches unchanged, their pre-join synchronization waits stay fixed. As post-join delay falls, B's queue and the occasional C delay become more visible determinants of end-to-end flow.

# 6. Reproducible Python

```python
from heapq import heapify, heappop, heappush
from io import StringIO

import pandas as pd
import pm4py

raw = """case arrival fork_release A_start A_end B_start B_end C_start C_end final_start final_end final_resource
F01 07:55 08:10 08:10 09:00 08:15 08:55 08:20 08:38 09:08 09:28 F1
F02 08:05 08:25 08:25 09:15 08:45 09:25 08:30 08:50 09:40 10:00 F2
F03 08:20 08:40 08:40 09:30 09:00 09:45 08:45 09:03 10:00 10:20 F1
F04 08:35 08:55 08:55 09:45 09:05 09:35 09:30 09:52 10:10 10:30 F2
F05 08:50 09:10 09:10 10:00 09:35 10:10 09:15 09:35 10:20 10:40 F1
F06 09:05 09:25 09:25 10:15 09:55 10:30 09:40 10:00 10:45 11:05 F2
F07 09:20 09:40 09:40 10:30 10:10 10:55 09:45 10:03 11:10 11:30 F1
F08 09:35 09:55 09:55 10:45 10:00 10:30 10:20 10:50 11:05 11:25 F2
F09 09:50 10:10 10:10 11:00 10:35 11:15 10:15 10:35 11:30 11:50 F1
F10 10:05 10:25 10:25 11:15 10:45 11:20 10:30 10:50 11:40 12:00 F2
F11 10:20 10:40 10:40 11:30 10:50 11:20 10:45 11:03 11:50 12:10 F1
F12 10:35 10:55 10:55 11:45 11:25 12:10 11:00 11:18 12:20 12:40 F2
"""
data = pd.read_csv(StringIO(raw), sep=r"\s+", index_col="case")
for col in data.columns.drop("final_resource"):
    data[col] = pd.to_datetime("2026-05-19 " + data[col], format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M")

def minutes(delta):
    return delta / pd.Timedelta(minutes=1)

# Custom pandas measurements. Directly-follows performance orders events
# and cannot recover the fork release, the AND-join, or synchronization waits.
branches = ["A", "B", "C"]
starts = data[[f"{i}_start" for i in branches]].set_axis(branches, axis=1)
ends = data[[f"{i}_end" for i in branches]].set_axis(branches, axis=1)
queue = minutes(starts.sub(data["fork_release"], axis=0))
service = minutes(ends - starts)
path = queue + service
join_ready = ends.max(axis=1)
fork_join = minutes(join_ready - data["fork_release"])
sync = path.rsub(fork_join, axis=0)
critical = path.eq(fork_join, axis=0)
# Retain all co-critical branches, always in A, B, C order.
critical_labels = critical.apply(lambda row: "/".join(row.index[row]), axis=1)
lead = pd.DataFrame({
    i: (path[i] - path.drop(columns=i).max(axis=1)).clip(lower=0)
    for i in branches
})
upstream = minutes(data["fork_release"] - data["arrival"])
downstream = minutes(data["final_start"] - join_ready)
final_duration = data["final_end"] - data["final_start"]
final_service = minutes(final_duration)
flow = minutes(data["final_end"] - data["arrival"])
assert (upstream + fork_join + downstream + final_service).eq(flow).all()
assert all((x >= 0).to_numpy().all() for x in (upstream, queue, service, sync, downstream, final_service))

cases = pd.DataFrame({"upstream_elapsed": upstream})
for i in branches:
    cases[f"{i}_queue"] = queue[i]
    cases[f"{i}_service"] = service[i]
    cases[f"{i}_sync"] = sync[i]
cases["join_ready"] = join_ready.dt.strftime("%H:%M")
cases["critical_branch"] = critical_labels
cases["fork_join_elapsed"] = fork_join
cases["downstream_queue"] = downstream
cases["final_service"] = final_service
cases["total_flow_time"] = flow

comparison = pd.DataFrame({
    "service_mean": service.mean(), "service_min": service.min(), "service_max": service.max(),
    "queue_mean": queue.mean(), "queue_min": queue.min(), "queue_max": queue.max(),
    "path_mean": path.mean(), "path_max": path.max(), "critical_count": critical.sum(),
    "sync_mean": sync.mean(), "lead_mean": lead.mean(), "lead_max": lead.max(),
})

path_a = path.copy()
path_a["A"] = queue["A"] + 0.8 * service["A"]
path_b = path.copy()
path_b["B"] = 0.5 * queue["B"] + service["B"]
gain_a = fork_join - path_a.max(axis=1)
gain_b = fork_join - path_b.max(axis=1)
ready_a = join_ready - pd.to_timedelta(gain_a, unit="min")
ready_b = join_ready - pd.to_timedelta(gain_b, unit="min")

def replay(ready, servers):
    # Identical, initially idle servers. All ready cases are dispatchable.
    # Sort by readiness, then case ID. Use the earliest free server.
    available = [(ready.min(), server) for server in range(servers)]
    heapify(available)
    jobs = ready.rename("ready").reset_index().sort_values(["ready", "case"])
    scheduled = {}
    for case, ready_at in jobs.itertuples(index=False, name=None):
        free_at, server = heappop(available)
        start = max(free_at, ready_at)
        scheduled[case] = start
        heappush(available, (start + final_duration[case], server))
    return pd.Series(scheduled).reindex(data.index)

two = replay(join_ready, 2)
three = replay(join_ready, 3)
a_two = replay(ready_a, 2)
b_two = replay(ready_b, 2)
savings = pd.DataFrame({
    "A_fixed_gap": gain_a,
    "B_fixed_gap": gain_b,
    "A_vs_two": minutes(two - a_two),
    "B_vs_two": minutes(two - b_two),
    "obs_to_two": minutes(data["final_start"] - two),
    "two_to_three": minutes(two - three),
})
savings["obs_to_three"] = savings["obs_to_two"] + savings["two_to_three"]
savings_summary = pd.DataFrame({
    "total_saved": savings.sum(), "mean_saved": savings.mean(),
    "positive_cases": savings.gt(0).sum(), "zero_cases": savings.eq(0).sum(),
    "negative_cases": savings.lt(0).sum(),
})
flows = pd.DataFrame({
    "observed": flow,
    "A_fixed_gap": flow - gain_a,
    "B_fixed_gap": flow - gain_b,
    "FCFS_two": flow - savings["obs_to_two"],
    "FCFS_A_two": minutes(a_two + final_duration - data["arrival"]),
    "FCFS_B_two": minutes(b_two + final_duration - data["arrival"]),
    "FCFS_three": flow - savings["obs_to_three"],
})
flow_summary = pd.DataFrame({
    "mean": flows.mean(), "p90_linear": flows.quantile(0.9), "max": flows.max(),
})

# Count only simultaneous activity visible in the supplied F intervals.
timeline = pd.date_range(join_ready.min(), data["final_end"].max(), freq="min")
busy = sum(
    ((timeline >= row.final_start) & (timeline < row.final_end)).astype(int)
    for row in data.itertuples()
)
both_busy_wait = pd.Series({
    c: ((timeline >= join_ready[c]) & (timeline < data.at[c, "final_start"]) & (busy == 2)).sum()
    for c in data.index
})

intervals = pd.concat([
    data.reset_index()[["case", start_col, end_col]]
    .rename(columns={"case": "case:concept:name", start_col: "start_timestamp",
                     end_col: "time:timestamp"})
    .assign(**{"concept:name": activity})
    for activity, start_col, end_col in (
        [(i, f"{i}_start", f"{i}_end") for i in branches]
        + [("F", "final_start", "final_end")]
    )
], ignore_index=True)
events = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    intervals, case_id="case:concept:name", activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp", start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
)
# This PM4Py measure checks start-to-completion interval lengths only.
service_seconds = pm4py.get_service_time(
    events, aggregation_measure="mean", start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
)
service_check = pd.Series(service_seconds).reindex(branches + ["F"]) / 60
expected_service = pd.concat([service.mean(), pd.Series({"F": final_service.mean()})])
assert len(events) == 4 * len(data)
assert (service_check - expected_service).abs().lt(1e-9).all()

print(cases.round(2).to_string())
print("\nBranch comparison\n" + comparison.round(2).to_string())
print("\nPer-case savings\n" + savings.round(2).to_string())
print("\nAggregate savings\n" + savings_summary.round(2).to_string())
print("\nFlow times\n" + flow_summary.round(2).to_string())
print("\nBoth busy during observed gaps\n" + both_busy_wait.to_string())
print("\nPM4Py mean service in minutes\n" + service_check.round(2).to_string())
```

# 7. Limits of inference

- The cases are from one day. Critical frequencies, tail statistics, and replay gains describe this workload. Estimating causal effects and steady-state capacity would require further evidence. A real intervention could change setups, staffing, case mix, and the order in which other jobs run.
- Branch resource assignments, rosters, occupancy histories, and competing workloads are unobserved. Overlapping work intervals indicate concurrency in the log. Inferring capacity from those overlaps would require knowing how many resources each activity occupies and whether the occupancy lasts throughout the interval. Similarly, a branch's elapsed pre-start time can include priority rules, availability windows, or unlogged prerequisites.
- The intervals cannot reveal pauses, preemption, or work performed intermittently between start and completion. The calculated service measures elapsed duration. The synchronization measures record a completed branch's wait for the other branches, with no direct information on resource retention.
- The maximum branch completion is readiness under the stated AND-join. Actual authority to dispatch F could depend on another approval, scheduled release, eligibility by resource, priorities, downtime, or cases outside this extract. The post-join gap therefore measures time available for investigation, and attributing it to capacity requires independent evidence of who was eligible and what the resources were doing. Earlier join readiness can yield earlier release, extra waiting, or rescheduling effects depending on how F dispatches.
