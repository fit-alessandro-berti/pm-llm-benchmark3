# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

1. **Data quality**: The absence of interruption reasons might lead to misinterpretation of the dispatch decisions.
2. **Data noise**: The estimates may be inaccurately predicted, leading to wrong decisions.
3. **Policy changes**: The dispatch policy might change under certain conditions such as SLA risks.
4. **Human error**: The dispatch decisions could be influenced by human error or lack of expertise.

# 7. Final synthesis

The analysis suggests that:
- QA is most identifiable, with appointment scheduling as the primary discipline, but the role of skill-based routing is not fully confirmed.
- QB is less identifiable, with preemptive priority as the primary discipline and appointment scheduling as a secondary component.
- QC is identifiable, with SPT as the primary discipline, but it is affected by deadline constraints.
- QD is the least identifiable, with skill-based routing as the primary discipline, but appointment scheduling also plays a significant role.

The claimed policy of FIFO should be approached with caution as it is not consistently observed across all queues. The riskiest inference is that of FIFO, due to the contradictory evidence from QB and QC.
