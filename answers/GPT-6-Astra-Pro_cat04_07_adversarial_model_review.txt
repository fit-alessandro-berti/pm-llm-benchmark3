# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

**Neither original model is suitable as a stand-alone compliance authority.** Model S accepts explicit policy violations; Model C rejects behavior that the supplied policy probes already identify as valid. The appropriate response is not to average their scores, but to separate descriptive usefulness, policy correctness, and uncertainty about the event record.

The governing evidence is the supplied policy, model expressions, reported measurements, feedback, and probes. The numerical results remain **unverified reports**: the original event log, split membership, preprocessing, alignment costs, and executable evaluation pipeline were not supplied.  

| Intended use              | Acceptable trade-off                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Exploration               | Some over-approximation can reveal combinations worth investigating. It must not be presented as authorization to execute those combinations. Missing corrections remain a material descriptive limitation.                                           |
| Conformance enforcement   | Mandatory controls are hard requirements. Good average fitness cannot compensate for admitting approval without required checks. Unknown attributes should produce an unresolved assessment, not automatic acceptance or an accusation of misconduct. |
| Simulation                | Control-flow admissibility is necessary but insufficient. Routing probabilities, correction frequencies, durations, resources, and logging-error mechanisms would need separate evidence.                                                             |
| Stakeholder communication | Comprehensibility matters, but a diagram that communicates the wrong obligations is not an acceptable simplification. The displayed model must explain its accompanying data rules.                                                                   |

I interpret `->` as sequence, `+` as parallel composition whose visible events may interleave, `X` as exclusive choice, and `tau` as an invisible transition. Acceptance means **exact membership of a complete visible trace**, not a favorable alignment score. Completion-order flexibility does not, by itself, establish actual temporal overlap or resource independence.

For compact traces, I use:

`R` = Register Dossier; `C` = Credit Check; `T` = Technical Review; `S` = Supplier Review; `E` = Expedite Review; `D` = Correct Dossier; `M` = Manager Approval; `A` = Auto Approval; `L` = Release Order.

Two interpretive boundaries matter. First, “Supplier Review is required only for externally sourced orders” establishes an external-order obligation, but does not unambiguously prohibit an internal order from receiving an unnecessary supplier review. Second, the supplied policy does not settle every consequence of correcting a dossier after other reviews. The third alternative makes those choices explicit rather than presenting them as discovered facts.

The reported training-to-holdout fitness differences are **0.011 for S** and **0.229 for C**. These are differences on the reported fitness scale—not percentages of cases incorrectly classified.

The documented token-based generalization implementation penalizes transitions with low replay frequency, including a penalty for transitions not visited. This explains why repeatedly visited shared transitions can score better than duplicated copies spread across variants. It does **not** make the measure a direct test of future-language correctness. ([PM4Py Documentation][1])

Likewise, the documented arc-degree simplicity measure depends on average node degree and a baseline parameter, rather than simply counting activities or branches. Consequently, an executable model with explicit synchronization can score worse than a superficially simpler but semantically weaker representation. ([PM4Py Documentation][2])

I would use **hard policy gates followed by disaggregated trade-offs**, not a composite score.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

**Strongest case for S.** It is compact, exposes both approval alternatives, avoids duplicated visible labels, and accommodates many event-order combinations. For exploratory work on an imperfect event record, that flexibility can reduce distracting mismatch reports. Its reported speed and comprehension results are consistent with a lightweight exploratory role, although neither result has been reproduced.

**Strongest case against S.** Its permissiveness is not confined to an observation-error mechanism. It is encoded as executable behavior: all four reviews are independently optional and mutually unordered before approval. 

Concrete admitted behavior includes:

| Trace admitted by S                                              | Problem                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `R M L`                                                          | Both mandatory checks are absent.                                                                         |
| `R C A L`                                                        | Technical Review is absent.                                                                               |
| `R T M L`                                                        | Credit Check is absent.                                                                                   |
| `R E C T M L`                                                    | Expedite Review precedes both mandatory checks.                                                           |
| `R C E T M L`                                                    | Expedite Review occurs while Technical Review remains incomplete.                                         |
| `R C T M L`, with **external = true** and complete event capture | Required Supplier Review is absent. The same visible trace can be valid for a different sourcing context. |

The first five counterexamples follow directly from the optional parallel branches and contradict P2 or P4. The sixth exposes the missing data condition in P3. 

The distinction between **noise tolerance and semantic error** is important:

* Optionalizing a mandatory check might intentionally accommodate a missing log event, but that should mean “execution evidence is incomplete,” not “the check may legitimately be omitted.”
* Allowing Expedite Review before the mandatory checks is an ordering relaxation. It is not a necessary consequence of tolerating a missing Supplier Review event.
* S does not model `Correct Dossier` at all and allows each review at most once. It therefore fails to represent legitimate correction behavior even while admitting serious omissions.

Its visible language can be characterized exactly: choose any subset of `{C,T,S,E}`, execute that subset in any order, then choose one approval. Therefore,

$$
|\mathcal L(S)|=2\sum_{k=0}^{4}\binom{4}{k}k!=130.
$$

This is a structural count, not an estimate of real-world behavior frequency. It demonstrates that S’s flexibility is broad but **not** correction-aware.

The exploration defense is legitimate only with clear labeling: **“exploratory over-approximation; not a policy specification.”**

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

**Strongest case for C.** Every branch requires registration, both mandatory checks, exactly one approval, and release. Its Expedite Review occurrences follow both mandatory checks. It also contains an explicit correction example rather than deleting correction from the activity vocabulary.

A finite allowlist can be defensible when every listed variant has been independently authorized and novel cases are routed to review. That is different from claiming that frequent historical variants automatically constitute policy.

**Strongest case against C.** It encodes eight complete sequences rather than factoring the process’s independent choices. Consequently, freedoms already supported individually are not generally composable. For example, the baseline alternate check order and Auto Approval are each represented, but their combination in B2 is excluded. Supplier Review plus Auto Approval is excluded entirely. 

There are several distinct problems.

**Branch enumeration.** Adding an approved combination requires another branch or coordinated changes to existing branches. Common registration, approval, and release logic is replicated.

**Correction handling.** C permits exactly one correction in exactly one context, followed by both mandatory checks in the fixed order `C,T`. It cannot transparently express Credit-only correction, Technical-only correction, repeated corrections, or alternate recheck order. Conversely, its correction branch can over-require rework when only one check was affected.

**Unseen combinations.** The holdout feedback specifically identifies combinations involving Supplier Review, Auto Approval, and alternate check order. That is evidence consistent with a compositional coverage failure, although the frequency and contribution to the fitness decline cannot be quantified from the supplied information. 

**Possible training-variant memorization.** Eight enumerated sequences, high training precision, and a large holdout fitness decline are consistent with overfitting. They do not prove it: policy drift, preprocessing differences, or capture errors remain alternative explanations.

**Duplicate-count inconsistency.** Counting the visible leaves in the supplied expression gives:

| Counting definition                      | Value for the literal C tree |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------: |
| Visible leaf occurrences                 |                           48 |
| Distinct visible labels                  |                            9 |
| Excess occurrences beyond one per label  |                       **39** |
| Labels occurring more than once          |                            7 |
| Occurrences belonging to repeated labels |                           46 |

Thus, **33 does not match these common definitions for the literal tree**. A different converted or reduced artifact might explain it, but that artifact and counting function must be supplied. This is a clarification requirement, not evidence of deliberate misreporting. 

Finally, C is not simply a subset of S: seven C traces are admitted by S, but C’s correction trace is not. The models have different deficiencies, rather than lying on one uncomplicated permissiveness scale.

# 7. Third Alternative

## Design: a validity-state Petri net with explicit data-policy checks

I propose **`model_third`**, a compact state-based procedural model rather than an enumeration of complete variants.

Its central concept is: **a mandatory check is either due or currently valid**. A correction invalidates only the affected check states. Approval requires both states to be valid.

### Explicit policy interpretation

Supplier Review is required for known external orders. For internal orders, the model permits zero or one Supplier Review; prohibiting it would require an additional policy decision. Expedite Review is optional, occurs at most once, and requires both mandatory checks to be currently valid when it occurs. Supplier Review may occur before or after it.

A correction has a nonempty scope in `{{C}, {T}, {C,T}}`. Corrections may repeat, including while a check is already due. A successful check on the latest dossier version satisfies the outstanding obligation for that check. An unaffected, already-valid check cannot repeat.

This interpretation does **not** require every correction to be followed immediately by a completed recheck before another correction. Nor does it prohibit a correction before the first round of checks is complete.

Two choices need owner ratification: correction scope is restricted to the two mandatory checks, and correction does not invalidate a previously completed Supplier or Expedite Review. Those choices are compatible with the supplied correction examples, but are not fully settled by the policy text.  

### Compact formal description

During dossier work, the marking contains one token in each pair:

$$
C_{\mathrm{due}}+C_{\mathrm{ok}}=1,\qquad
T_{\mathrm{due}}+T_{\mathrm{ok}}=1,
$$

and analogous pending/done pairs for Supplier and Expedite Review.

| Transition                  | Token effect                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Register                    | Creates the work token, both mandatory-check due tokens, and both optional-review pending tokens.                    |
| Credit / Technical check    | Moves its own token from `due` to `ok`.                                                                              |
| Correction with scope \(A\) | Requires the work token; sets each affected check in \(A\) to `due`, leaving unaffected checks unchanged.            |
| Supplier Review             | Moves supplier pending to done. A silent skip exists only in the unconditioned/internal-compatible model.            |
| Expedite Review             | Moves expedite pending to done while requiring and preserving both mandatory-check `ok` tokens.                      |
| Skip Expedite               | Silently moves expedite pending to done.                                                                             |
| Manager or Auto Approval    | Consumes the work token, both mandatory-check `ok` tokens, and both review done tokens; produces one approval token. |
| Release                     | Consumes the approval token and produces the sole final token.                                                       |

A correction needs eight internal transition variants: two current-state patterns for each singleton scope and four for the both-check scope. They all have the visible label `Correct Dossier`.

That duplication is deliberate and localized. It represents correction semantics, not memorized complete journeys. **Zero duplicate labels is not the design objective.**

### Self-contained PM4Py construction

The following builds an ordinary place/transition net. A place included on both sides of a transition is an ordinary self-loop used to preserve a required token.

```python
from itertools import product
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import PetriNet, Marking
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.utils import petri_utils

ACT = dict(zip("RCTSEDMAL", [
    "Register Dossier", "Credit Check", "Technical Review",
    "Supplier Review", "Expedite Review", "Correct Dossier",
    "Manager Approval", "Auto Approval", "Release Order",
]))
CHECKS = frozenset({"C", "T"})
SCOPES = (frozenset({"C"}), frozenset({"T"}), CHECKS)

def build_third(external=None):
    """external=True removes the Supplier Review skip."""
    if external is not None and not isinstance(external, bool):
        raise TypeError("external must be bool or None")

    net = PetriNet("dossier_validity_state")
    names = [
        "source", "work", "C_due", "C_ok", "T_due", "T_ok",
        "S_pending", "S_done", "E_pending", "E_done",
        "approved", "sink",
    ]
    places = {name: PetriNet.Place(name) for name in names}
    net.places.update(places.values())

    def add(name, code, pre, post, scope=None):
        label = None if code is None else ACT[code]
        transition = PetriNet.Transition(name, label)
        if scope is not None:
            transition.properties["affected_checks"] = tuple(
                ACT[c] for c in sorted(scope)
            )
        net.transitions.add(transition)
        for place in pre:
            petri_utils.add_arc_from_to(places[place], transition, net)
        for place in post:
            petri_utils.add_arc_from_to(transition, places[place], net)

    add("register", "R", ["source"],
        ["work", "C_due", "T_due", "S_pending", "E_pending"])

    for check in ("C", "T"):
        add("check_" + check, check,
            [check + "_due"], [check + "_ok"])

    add("supplier", "S", ["S_pending"], ["S_done"])
    if external is not True:
        add("skip_supplier", None, ["S_pending"], ["S_done"])

    add("expedite", "E",
        ["E_pending", "C_ok", "T_ok"],
        ["E_done", "C_ok", "T_ok"])
    add("skip_expedite", None, ["E_pending"], ["E_done"])

    for scope in SCOPES:
        ordered = sorted(scope)
        for statuses in product(("due", "ok"), repeat=len(ordered)):
            name = "correct_" + "_".join(ordered + list(statuses))
            pre = ["work"] + [
                c + "_" + state
                for c, state in zip(ordered, statuses)
            ]
            post = ["work"] + [c + "_due" for c in ordered]
            add(name, "D", pre, post, scope)

    for approval in ("M", "A"):
        add("approve_" + approval, approval,
            ["work", "C_ok", "T_ok", "S_done", "E_done"],
            ["approved"])

    add("release", "L", ["approved"], ["sink"])
    return (
        net,
        Marking({places["source"]: 1}),
        Marking({places["sink"]: 1}),
    )

model_third, im_third, fm_third = build_third(external=None)
```

**The transition property is metadata, not an automatically enforced PM4Py data guard.** Ordinary label-based alignment can choose a correction variant without knowing whether that scope actually applied. The following policy check is therefore an essential part of the alternative.

### Required external policy check

Here `affected[i]` contains the recorded scope of correction event `i`, using zero-based positions and codes such as `{"T"}`. Production integration should use stable event identifiers.

The checker retains possible validity states when scope is missing, but does not convert a compatible interpretation into verified evidence.

```python
def policy_check(codes, external=None, affected=None):
    """
    Complete-record assessment, not a live-prefix API.

    MATCH: the record satisfies the stated interpretation.
    MISMATCH: no permitted interpretation fits the supplied record/data.
    UNKNOWN: a fitting interpretation exists, but required data is missing.

    Incomplete event capture requires separate evidence adjudication.
    """
    codes = tuple(codes)
    affected = {} if affected is None else dict(affected)

    if external is not None and not isinstance(external, bool):
        raise TypeError("external must be bool or None")

    for i, raw in affected.items():
        if (not isinstance(i, int) or not 0 <= i < len(codes)
                or codes[i] != "D"):
            raise ValueError("Scope keys must index correction events")
        if isinstance(raw, str) or frozenset(raw) not in SCOPES:
            raise ValueError("Scope must be a nonempty subset of C and T")
        affected[i] = frozenset(raw)

    def result(status, reason):
        return {"status": status, "reason": reason}

    if (len(codes) < 3
            or codes[0] != "R"
            or codes[-1] != "L"
            or codes[-2] not in {"M", "A"}
            or any(x not in {"C", "T", "S", "E", "D"}
                   for x in codes[1:-2])):
        return result("MISMATCH", "Registration/work/approval/release order")

    if codes.count("S") > 1 or codes.count("E") > 1:
        return result("MISMATCH", "Supplier/Expedite occurs more than once")

    states = {frozenset()}       # Currently valid mandatory checks
    unknown = []

    for i, activity in enumerate(codes[1:-2], start=1):
        if activity in CHECKS:
            states = {
                valid | {activity}
                for valid in states if activity not in valid
            }

        elif activity == "D":
            scopes = (affected[i],) if i in affected else SCOPES
            if i not in affected:
                unknown.append(f"Missing correction scope at event {i}")
            states = {
                valid - scope
                for valid in states for scope in scopes
            }

        elif activity == "E":
            states = {valid for valid in states if CHECKS <= valid}

        if not states:
            return result("MISMATCH", f"No valid check state at event {i}")

    if CHECKS not in states:
        return result("MISMATCH", "Approval lacks current valid C and T")

    if "S" not in codes:
        if external is True:
            return result("MISMATCH", "Required Supplier Review absent")
        if external is None:
            unknown.append("Source type missing and Supplier Review absent")

    if unknown:
        return result("UNKNOWN", "; ".join(unknown))
    return result("MATCH", "Record satisfies the stated interpretation")
```

Examples:

```python
# B6: valid only with the supported Technical-only scope.
assert policy_check(
    "RCTDTML", external=False, affected={3: {"T"}}
)["status"] == "MATCH"

assert policy_check(
    "RCTDTML", external=False, affected={3: {"C", "T"}}
)["status"] == "MISMATCH"

assert policy_check(
    "RCTDTML", external=False
)["status"] == "UNKNOWN"

# The baseline trace cannot certify an external order without Supplier Review.
assert policy_check("RCTML", external=True)["status"] == "MISMATCH"
```

A recorded mismatch is **not automatically a proven execution violation** when capture is incomplete. Conversely, a missing event must not be silently inserted and then treated as evidence that its control occurred.

# 9. Validation Experiments

The third alternative is a candidate specification, not a claimed empirical winner. Its validation must test both the Petri net and the policy checker.

import pm4py
from collections import Counter

def evaluate_metrics(train_log, holdout_log, net, im, fm):
    sound, diagnostics = pm4py.check_soundness(
        net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=False
    )
    if not sound:
        return {
            "soundness": False,
            "diagnostics": diagnostics,
            "rejected": "Resolve unsoundness before metric evaluation",
        }

    labels = Counter(
        t.label for t in net.transitions if t.label is not None
    )
    return {
        "pm4py_version": pm4py.__version__,
        "soundness": sound,
        "train_fitness": pm4py.fitness_alignments(

        "holdout_fitness": pm4py.fitness_alignments(

        ),
        "train_precision": pm4py.precision_alignments(
            train_log, net, im, fm

            t.label is None for t in net.transitions

### Explicit alignment-cost experiment

```python
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import Event, Trace
from pm4py.algo.conformance.alignments.petri_net import (
    algorithm as align_alg,
)

def align_with_costs(labels, net, im, fm,
                     log_costs=None, model_costs=None, tau_cost=1):
    log_costs = log_costs or {}
    model_costs = model_costs or {}
    trace = Trace([Event({"concept:name": x}) for x in labels])
    P = align_alg.Parameters

    parameters = {
        P.PARAM_TRACE_COST_FUNCTION: [
            log_costs.get(x, 10000) for x in labels
        ],
        P.PARAM_MODEL_COST_FUNCTION: {
            t: tau_cost if t.label is None
            else model_costs.get(t.label, 10000)
            for t in net.transitions
        },
        P.PARAM_SYNC_COST_FUNCTION: {
            t: 0 for t in net.transitions
        },
        P.PARAM_MAX_ALIGN_TIME_TRACE: 60,
    }
    return align_alg.apply_trace(
        trace, net, im, fm,
        parameters=parameters,
        variant=align_alg.Variants.VERSION_DIJKSTRA_NO_HEURISTICS,
    )
```

The separate cost mappings are supported by the alignment interface. A timeout or missing result must remain a failed/unknown evaluation, not be converted into a favorable score. ([PM4Py Documentation][3])

Exact probe membership should be checked independently of normalized alignment fitness. The supplied implementation includes visible-transition replay with silent closure, so it does not assume that `fitness == 1` or raw alignment cost zero is a universal membership test.

### What was actually verified here

I executed an independent, ordinary-Python enumeration of the third model’s transition rules. It found **19 reachable markings**; every marking was 1-safe and could reach the final marking; every transition was reachable; and no final marking retained other tokens. The probe and adversarial outcomes shown above were checked against those rules.

The construction also has a direct invariant argument: each active mandatory-check pair contains exactly one token, approval consumes all active control tokens, and release produces only the final token. Repeated corrections can produce arbitrarily long traces without producing unbounded token counts. Soundness here means completion remains possible, not that every infinite correction sequence must terminate.

**PM4Py was unavailable in the execution environment, so its construction, conversion, soundness, alignment, and metric calls were not executed here.** Those checks remain part of the pinned-environment validation program. No new training/holdout metric values are claimed.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

**Model S may remain an exploratory baseline, but not an enforcement model.** Its interface should display the mandatory-check and Expedite-order limitations prominently. It should not be used to conclude that omitted events represent permitted work, and its inability to represent correction must remain visible.

**Model C may remain a historical variant catalog and regression baseline.** It can identify “not represented by this catalog,” but that finding must not be labeled “policy violation.” Its audit-ready claim should be withdrawn unless the branches have independently approved policy meaning and the missing data conditions are enforced elsewhere. The existing rejection of B2 already prevents treating this particular catalog as a complete expression of the supplied policy.

**Retire both originals as stand-alone normative authorities, not as retained analytical artifacts.**

Advance Third to shadow-mode evaluation, conditional on four deployment requirements:

1. The process owner ratifies sourcing semantics, current-check validity, correction scope, treatment of successive corrections, and whether Supplier or Expedite Review can be invalidated.
2. Source type, correction scope, dossier version, and event-capture provenance are recorded reliably enough for the intended decisions.
3. The net and policy checker are deployed and versioned together, with no path that authorizes release on control-flow fitness alone.
4. Blind behavioral, empirical, soundness, comprehension, maintenance, and operational tests meet predeclared acceptance criteria.

For autonomous authorization, an unresolved required fact should lead to **evidence collection or authorized review**, not silent approval. For retrospective audit, the same uncertainty should not automatically become an allegation that the underlying work was omitted.

Third’s main advantage is not a predicted improvement in every metric. It is the explicit separation of **what is mandatory, what may vary, what a correction invalidates, and what cannot be established from the available evidence**.

# 11. Dissenting View

**Rebuttal.**

The recommendation may underestimate the operational risk of replacing a familiar allowlist with a custom validity-state model and a separate policy interpreter. The third alternative is compact in source code, but its correction variants, hidden routing, and uncertainty states could be harder for auditors to inspect than eight explicit sequences. Its data guard is also outside ordinary label-based alignment, creating a new failure mode: one component can be upgraded or bypassed while the other continues reporting apparently satisfactory conformance.

The proposed correction semantics deserve particular skepticism. Treating one later check as satisfying several outstanding corrections, allowing correction before both initial checks finish, and retaining an earlier Expedite Review after correction are reasonable interpretations, not agreed requirements. Introducing them during a model-quality review risks turning the reviewer into the policy author.

A safer interim strategy could be to repair C only with expressly ratified variants, retain S for discovery, and route novel combinations to a documented approval process. That may impose additional review work, but an organization with low volumes or high consequences could rationally prefer visible conservatism to inferred flexibility. Until reliable attributes exist and the complete monitoring system has demonstrated lower total error and review burden, the third alternative should not receive a presumption of superiority.

**Evidence that would change my decision.** I would favor a corrected, guarded allowlist if the owner explicitly adopted that policy, measured novelty and review volumes were manageable, and it outperformed Third on documented comprehension and operational tests without misclassifying approved behavior. I would revise Third if correction records showed that Supplier or Expedite Review must also be invalidated, or that separate checks are required for successive revisions. Conversely, reliable attribute capture, blind unseen-combination success, integrated guard testing, and lower measured review burden would strengthen the case for Third.
