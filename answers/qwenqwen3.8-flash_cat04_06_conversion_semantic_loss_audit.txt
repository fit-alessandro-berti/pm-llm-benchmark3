```
def bounded_visible_traces(net, im, fm, max_visible=12, max_steps=80, max_silent_run=10, max_correct_defects=2):
    traces = set()
    if net is None or im is None or fm is None: return traces
    start = normalize_marking(im)
    final_key = marking_key(fm)
    stack = [(start, (), 0, 0, 0, 0, ())]  # marking, visible, steps, visible_len, correct_defects, silent_run, silent_path_keys
    while stack:
        marking, visible, steps, vlen, cds, silent_run, silent_path = stack.pop()
        if marking_key(marking) == final_key:
            traces.add(visible)
            continue
        if steps >= max_steps or vlen >= max_visible or cds > max_correct_defects:
            continue
        try:
            enabled = pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, marking)
        except Exception as e:
            continue

            is_visible = label is not None
            if is_visible and vlen >= max_visible:
                continue
            new_marking = fire_transition(net, marking, t)
```

# 4. Preservation and Loss Audit

| Source invariant | POWLPetri | PetriBPMN | PetriTree | BPMN round trip | Evidence or uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Register first | expected preserved | expected preserved | conversion failed | expected preserved | Sequence start is usually robust, but BPMN layout may still visually obscure it. |
| Assess/Prepare unordered | expected preserved | ambiguous | conversion failed | ambiguous | Net may fork tokens and imply true concurrency; BPMN may serialize or use parallel gateways that overstate concurrency. |
| Legal optional silent | expected preserved | approximated | conversion failed | ambiguous | Silent XOR may become an empty path, omitted node, or misleading exclusive gateway. |
| Verification join after Assess+Prepare | expected preserved | ambiguous | conversion failed | ambiguous | Token join is clear in the net, but BPMN may collapse silent branches or show a single path. |
| Legal/verification overlap | expected preserved | ambiguous | conversion failed | ambiguous | Lack of edge is a partial-order fact; BPMN layout and gateway choice may impose artificial order. |
| Configure before Inspect | expected preserved | expected preserved | conversion failed | expected preserved | Local sequence is usually preserved, but loop rendering may make it look optional. |
| At least one iteration | expected preserved | ambiguous | conversion failed | ambiguous | BPMN loop semantics may allow zero iterations unless the converter encodes the do-part correctly. |
| Correct Defect binary loop | expected preserved | ambiguous | conversion failed | ambiguous | The net must prevent exit after redo; BPMN back-edge visualization may not make this explicit. |
| Release join | expected preserved | ambiguous | conversion failed | ambiguous | Silent legal completion makes the join partly invisible in traces; net-level token checks are needed. |
| Archive finality | expected preserved | expected preserved | conversion failed | expected preserved | Final event is usually preserved, but layout may suggest post-final paths if silent routing remains. |
| Activity-label uniqueness | expected preserved | ambiguous | conversion failed | ambiguous | Labels can be preserved, but BPMN may add empty or synthetic labels for gateways/events. |
| Initial/final markings | expected preserved | ambiguous | conversion failed | ambiguous | BPMN start/end events do not directly expose markings; round-trip nets must be checked for exact final marking. |
| Gateway/routing-node semantics | expected preserved | ambiguous | conversion failed | ambiguous | BPMN gateways may not map one-to-one to net routing; parallel versus exclusive choice is a major visual risk. |

**True concurrency versus arbitrary interleaving.** The POWL source states absence of order between certain nodes. A Petri net conversion may represent that by token duplication, which is stronger than mere interleaving and can imply true concurrency. If the intended business semantics is “either order is allowed but not necessarily simultaneous,” the net may be overpermissive at the model level even if visible traces look similar. BPMN parallel gateways can make this overpermissiveness visually explicit.

**Lack of an edge between Optional Legal Review and verification.** The source does not require `Legal Review` to finish before verification begins, nor does it require verification to finish before legal review. A faithful net should allow legal review to interleave with verification events. A process tree or BPMN converter may force one branch to complete before the other, losing this invariant.

**Silent optional behavior.** The XOR with `SilentTransition()` means the legal branch may complete without any visible event. After hiding tau, optionality is observable only as the presence or absence of `Legal Review`. BPMN may render the silent branch as an empty path or omit it, making the join before `Release Change` visually misleading.

**Binary-loop semantics.** `LOOP(trial_body, correct_defect)` is not a generic BPMN loop with an arbitrary condition. The intended semantics is at least one `Configure Trial` / `Inspect Trial` iteration, with `Correct Defect` only as a separator before another iteration. BPMN loop visualization may suggest zero iterations or may allow `Correct Defect` to be followed directly by release unless the converter preserves the do/redo discipline.

**Join before Release Change.** `Release Change` consumes completion from both the optional-legal branch and the verification loop. Because the legal branch may be silent, trace-level observation alone cannot always prove the join. Net-level final-marking checks and token-flow inspection are required.

**Activity-label uniqueness.** The source uses distinct visible labels. Conversion can preserve them, but BPMN round-trip may introduce empty labels, synthetic routing labels, or duplicate labels for internal transitions. Label uniqueness matters because trace equivalence is defined over visible labels.

**Initial and final markings.** The accepting Petri net should have a well-defined initial marking and final marking. BPMN conversion does not expose markings directly. Round-trip validation must check that completed visible traces end in the exact final marking, not merely in “some” end state.

**Gateway or routing-node ambiguity in BPMN visualization.** BPMN gateways may be generated from net routing structure, but their visual placement and type may not faithfully communicate whether a branch is optional, concurrent, silent, or synchronized. A gateway that looks exclusive may represent a net choice caused by silent routing; a parallel gateway may imply mandatory concurrency where the source only required unordered execution.

```python
import pm4py

# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

RUN_REACHABILITY = True

# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Marking and transition firing helpers.
# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

def normalize_marking(m):
    try:
        return {p: int(t) for p, t in m.items() if int(t) > 0}
    except Exception:
        return m


def make_marking_like(im, m):
    try:
        return type(im)(m)
    except Exception:
        return m


def marking_key(m):
    m = normalize_marking(m)
    try:
        return tuple(sorted((id(p), int(t)) for p, t in m.items()))
    except Exception:
        return str(m)


def same_marking(m1, m2):
    return marking_key(m1) == marking_key(m2)


def visible_label(t):
    label = getattr(t, "label", None)
    if label is None:
        return None
    s = str(label)
    return None if s == "" else s


def fire_transition(net, marking, transition):
    pre = getattr(transition, "pre", None)
    post = getattr(transition, "post", None)

    if pre is None:
        pre = []
    if post is None:
        post = []

    new = normalize_marking(marking)

    try:
        for p in pre:
            if new.get(p, 0) <= 0:
                return None
            new[p] -= 1
            if new[p] == 0:
                del new[p]

        for p in post:
            new[p] = new.get(p, 0) + 1
    except Exception:
        return None

    return new


# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Bounded visible-trace exploration.
# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

def bounded_visible_traces(
    net,
    im,
    fm,
    max_visible=12,
    max_steps=120,
    max_silent_run=20,
    max_correct_defects=2,
):
    """
    Explores visible traces up to explicit bounds.

    - Hides transitions whose label is None or empty string.
    - Requires exact final-marking equality for a completed trace.
    - Avoids infinite silent cycling with a silent-run bound and per-silent-run
      visited marking keys.
    - Enforces a visible-length bound and a loop-iteration separator bound.
    """
    traces = set()

    if net is None or im is None or fm is None:
        return traces

    start = normalize_marking(im)
    final_key = marking_key(fm)

    # State:
    # marking, visible_tuple, steps, visible_len, correct_defects,
    # silent_run, silent_path_keys
    stack = [(start, (), 0, 0, 0, 0, ())]

    while stack:
        marking, visible, steps, vlen, cds, silent_run, silent_path = stack.pop()

        if marking_key(marking) == final_key:
            traces.add(visible)
            continue

        if steps >= max_steps or vlen >= max_visible or cds > max_correct_defects:
            continue

        try:
            enabled = pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(
                net, make_marking_like(im, marking)
            )
        except Exception:
            continue

        if enabled is None:
            continue

        for t in enabled:
            label = visible_label(t)
            is_visible = label is not None

            if is_visible and vlen >= max_visible:
                continue

            new_marking = fire_transition(net, marking, t)
            if new_marking is None:
                continue

            if is_visible:
                new_visible = visible + (label,)
                new_vlen = vlen + 1
                new_cds = cds + (1 if label == "Correct Defect" else 0)

                if new_cds > max_correct_defects:
                    continue

                stack.append(
                    (
                        new_marking,
                        new_visible,
                        steps + 1,
                        new_vlen,
                        new_cds,
                        0,
                        (),
                    )
                )
            else:
                if silent_run >= max_silent_run:
                    continue

                new_key = marking_key(new_marking)
                if new_key in silent_path:
                    continue

                stack.append(
                    (
                        new_marking,
                        visible,
                        steps + 1,
                        vlen,
                        cds,
                        silent_run + 1,
                        silent_path + (new_key,),
                    )
                )

    return traces


# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Direct visible-trace replay, including silent transitions.
# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

def replay_visible_trace(net, im, fm, trace, max_steps=250, max_silent_run=30):
    """
    Returns True iff there exists a transition sequence whose visible labels
    exactly equal `trace` and whose final marking exactly equals `fm`.
    """
    if net is None or im is None or fm is None:
        return False

    trace = tuple(str(x) for x in trace)
    start = normalize_marking(im)
    final_key = marking_key(fm)

    # State: marking, position_in_trace, steps, silent_run, silent_path_keys
    stack = [(start, 0, 0, 0, ())]

    while stack:
        marking, pos, steps, silent_run, silent_path = stack.pop()

        if pos == len(trace) and marking_key(marking) == final_key:
            return True

        if steps >= max_steps or silent_run > max_silent_run:
            continue

        try:
            enabled = pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(
                net, make_marking_like(im, marking)
            )
        except Exception:
            continue

        if enabled is None:
            continue

        for t in enabled:
            label = visible_label(t)
            new_marking = fire_transition(net, marking, t)

            if new_marking is None:
                continue

            if label is None:
                if silent_run >= max_silent_run:
                    continue

                new_key = marking_key(new_marking)
                if new_key in silent_path:
                    continue

                stack.append(
                    (
                        new_marking,
                        pos,
                        steps + 1,
                        silent_run + 1,
                        silent_path + (new_key,),
                    )
                )
            else:
                if pos < len(trace) and label == trace[pos]:
                    stack.append((new_marking, pos + 1, steps + 1, 0, ()))

    return False

def rg_size(rg):
    if rg is None:
        return None

    for attr in ("nodes", "states", "vertices"):
        if hasattr(rg, attr):
            try:
                return len(getattr(rg, attr))
            except Exception:
                pass

    try:
        return len(rg)
    except Exception:
        return None
```

# 6. Visual-Semantics Review

A BPMN diagram can be behaviorally acceptable at the underlying net level and still be misleading to stakeholders.

**Parallel gateways.** If the converter creates a parallel gateway after `Register Change`, stakeholders may read it as “`Assess Risk` and `Prepare Evidence` must execute simultaneously.” The source only requires that they are unordered after registration. True concurrency may be a model-level artifact of token duplication. If the business intent is interleaving under a single resource or single token, the BPMN should be annotated: “Parallel gateway represents permitted interleaving; simultaneous execution is not asserted.”

**Exclusive gateways.** The optional legal branch may appear as an XOR gateway with one visible path and one empty or silent path. If the silent path is omitted, stakeholders may think `Legal Review` is mandatory. If the empty path is drawn, they may misread it as a normal sequence flow. Annotation should state: “XOR with silent skip: `Legal Review` may occur or the branch may complete silently.”

**Loop visualization.** BPMN loops often show a back edge or an exclusive gateway after `Inspect Trial`. This can suggest that `Correct Defect` is an optional final action or that zero iterations are possible. The source semantics are stricter: at least one `Configure Trial` / `Inspect Trial` iteration must occur, and `Correct Defect` is only a separator before another iteration. Annotation should state: “Loop do-part is mandatory. `Correct Defect` cannot be followed directly by `Release Change`; it must be followed by another `Configure Trial` / `Inspect Trial` iteration.”

**Silent routing nodes.** Silent transitions may be rendered as small circles, empty tasks, or omitted routing artifacts. These nodes can carry important synchronization semantics, especially for the join before `Release Change`. If omitted, the diagram may appear to allow release after verification even when the optional legal branch has not completed. Documentation should map each silent routing node to an invariant ID, for example: “Silent router satisfies optional-legal branch completion for INV-09.”

**Apparent ordering of graph layout.** BPMN layout engines place nodes left-to-right or top-to-bottom for readability, not semantics. A layout may draw `Legal Review` after `Prepare Evidence`, suggesting a required order that the source does not impose. Stakeholder documentation should include: “Horizontal and vertical placement are layout artifacts; only gateway types, sequence flows, and annotations define semantics.”

**Recommended annotations.**
- Add BPMN documentation attributes to gateways: “parallel = permitted interleaving,” “XOR = optional silent legal review,” “loop = at least one iteration.”
- Attach invariant IDs to tasks and gateways: `INV-01` through `INV-10`.
- Provide a sidecar JSON file mapping BPMN element IDs to source POWL nodes and invariant IDs.
- Include a short “Reading this diagram” note warning that layout order is not semantic order.
- For stakeholder-only views, consider hiding silent routing nodes but preserving an explicit annotation that silent completion is possible.

# 7. Rejection Criteria

A conversion result should be rejected or quarantined if any of the following occurs.

1. **Primary compilation fails.** `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(source_powl)` raises, returns `None`, or returns a net with null initial or final marking.

2. **Workflow-net check fails.** `pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net)` returns `False` or raises for the source net or a BPMN round-trip net.

3. **Soundness check fails.** `pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm)` returns `False`, raises, or reports dead transitions, unreachable final marking, or improper termination.

4. **Required positive interleaving is lost.** A positive witness such as `Legal Review` occurring before `Prepare Evidence`, or `Legal Review` occurring between `Configure Trial` and `Inspect Trial`, cannot be replayed on the converted or round-tripped model.

5. **Forbidden negative witness is admitted.** The model accepts a trace such as release before verification, inspect before configure, zero verification iterations, `Correct Defect` followed directly by `Release Change`, or `Archive Change` before `Release Change`.

6. **Exact final-marking test fails.** A completed visible trace does not end in the exact final marking required by the accepting Petri net.

7. **Optional behavior collapses.** The model always requires `Legal Review`, never allows `Legal Review`, or otherwise loses the XOR-with-silent optionality.

8. **Loop semantics are weakened.** The model allows zero iterations, allows `Correct Defect` as a final exit, or allows release after `Correct Defect` without another full trial iteration.

9. **Join before release is lost.** The model allows `Release Change` without completion of both the optional-legal branch and the verification loop, as detected by net-level token flow or reachability analysis.

10. **BPMN round-trip introduces extra behavior.** The round-tripped net admits visible traces not present in the source net within the same exploration bound, especially traces that violate INV-01 through INV-10.

11. **Process-tree intermediate is used despite mismatch.** If `pm4py.convert_to_process_tree` succeeds but the resulting BPMN fails positive/negative witness tests, the tree-derived BPMN must be quarantined.

12. **Activity labels are lost or duplicated.** Visible transitions no longer carry the source labels, or multiple distinct source activities are mapped to the same visible label, making trace equivalence unreliable.

# 8. Canonical Representation Recommendation

**Authoritative representation.** The source POWL model should remain authoritative. It directly expresses the intended partial order, optional XOR with silent behavior, loop do/redo structure, and synchronization before release. Converted models are not authoritative unless they have been validated against the POWL invariants.

**Canonical model for storage.** Store the source POWL as the canonical normative model. If downstream execution requires a lower-level formalism, store the accepting Petri net produced by `POWL -> accepting Petri net` as a compiled derivative, not as the source of truth. The compiled net is useful because it supports soundness checking, reachability analysis, and bounded trace exploration.

**Stakeholder visualization model.** Use BPMN generated from the accepting Petri net as the stakeholder visualization. Do not use a process tree as the default visualization unless the source is known to be block-structured and the tree conversion passes validation.

**Derivative artifacts.** The following are derivative and must be regenerable from the authoritative POWL:
- `net_from_powl`
- `im_from_powl`
- `fm_from_powl`
- `bpmn_from_net`
- `tree_from_net`, if it exists
- `bpmn_from_tree`, if it exists
- all round-tripped nets
- all PNG/SVG visualizations

**Required validation evidence for derivatives.** Each derivative stored alongside the POWL should include:
- conversion success status;
- workflow-net boolean;
- soundness result;
- bounded visible-trace comparison against the source net;
- positive witness replay results;
- negative witness rejection results;
- footprint summary, if available;
- reachability graph size or failure status, if available;
- source POWL hash and generated artifact hash;
- timestamp and PM4Py version.

**Process-tree intermediate policy.** A process-tree intermediate should not be allowed as a canonical or default artifact. It may be allowed only as an optional, explicitly labeled visual simplification if:
- `pm4py.convert_to_process_tree` succeeds;
- the resulting BPMN round-trips to a sound accepting net;
- all positive witnesses replay;
- all negative witnesses are rejected;
- bounded trace comparison does not reveal missing required interleavings.

If any condition fails, the process-tree path should be rejected rather than used to produce stakeholder BPMN.

**Avoiding round-trip drift.** Future edits should follow this rule:
1. Edit the authoritative POWL model.
2. Recompile to accepting Petri net.
3. Generate BPMN from the newly compiled net.
4. Run the invariant and round-trip validation suite.
5. Store the new POWL, new net, new BPMN, and validation report together.

Do not edit BPMN and convert it back to POWL or Petri net as a source of truth. BPMN is a visualization and stakeholder artifact; it may lose silent routing, loop discipline, or concurrency semantics. If a BPMN change is requested, translate the request back into a POWL invariant change, then regenerate all downstream artifacts.

# 9. Residual Uncertainty

Even if every proposed test passes, the following claims cannot be fully established.

1. **Full behavioral equivalence for unbounded loop repetitions.** The bounded exploration only tests traces up to a fixed visible length and a fixed number of `Correct Defect` separators. It cannot prove equivalence for arbitrary numbers of verification iterations.

2. **True concurrency versus interleaving intent.** Petri net and BPMN traces are linearized visible sequences. They cannot by themselves prove whether the original business intent allowed simultaneous execution or only either order. The net may overgenerate concurrency even when all visible interleavings match.

3. **Silent internal state fidelity.** After hiding tau, two nets may produce the same visible traces while differing in internal silent states, token distribution, or join behavior. Silent routing before `Release Change` may be semantically important but invisible in traces.

4. **Resource, data, and policy constraints.** The model validates control flow only. It cannot establish whether the right person performs `Legal Review`, whether evidence is sufficient, whether risk thresholds are met, or whether trial configuration data is valid.

5. **BPMN visual semantics.** A sound underlying net does not guarantee that the rendered BPMN communicates the same meaning to stakeholders. Gateway placement, layout order, omitted silent nodes, and loop drawing can still mislead even when formal validation passes.

6. **Process-tree conversion success as proof of faithfulness.** If `convert_to_process_tree` succeeds, that only proves a block-structured representation exists. It does not prove the representation preserves the source’s non-block-structured concurrency, silent optionality, or loop discipline unless the full witness suite also passes.
