## 1. What is observed

`automation=1` is a branch-month availability flag: Alpha and Beta switch on 202604, Gamma switches on 202607, Delta and Epsilon never switch on in the observed window. Availability is not usage — actual treatment intensity, proxied by `automated_fast_path_pct`, sits at a flat ~4–6% baseline in all branches before/without adoption and then climbs gradually and unevenly after go-live (Alpha 25%48%, Beta 27%50%, Gamma 25%32%), consistent with gradual staff adoption rather than an on/off switch. Branch selection into the first wave was explicit and non-random: Alpha and Beta were chosen for high volume and weak historical cycle times, so their baseline levels and trajectories are not comparable by design to the untreated branches. Several unrelated operational changes coincide with rollout: a centralized fraud desk from March shifts escalation for every branch; a routing campaign raises arrivals ~10% at Alpha/Beta from April and ~5% at Gamma from July; FTE counts move by one or two staff in different branches in different months, sometimes the same month automation intensifies; and a May product change raises `high_complexity_pct` in several branches regardless of automation status. Outcome measurement has independent limits: `csat_1to5` is a survey mean with response rates fluctuating 20–45% and unknown counts, so its precision and non-response bias cannot be assessed; cycle-time and rework/escalation figures are branch-month aggregates with no case-level or queue-state detail underneath them.

**Interference/treatment-version notes:** the routing campaign reallocates arrivals across branches, so SUTVA (no interference) is questionable — a "control" branch's arrival volume is not independent of treated branches' status. "Automation" is also not one treatment: the automated fast path and the automated-escalated path are different treatment versions with different mechanisms, and the fraud desk is a shared, non-branch-specific channel that both treated and control branches pass through.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary: staggered-adoption group-time ATT / event study (Callaway–Sant'Anna / Sun–Abraham style)** | cohort dummies (April, July) × event-time indicators | not-yet-treated branches at each calendar period (Gamma pre-July, Delta/Epsilon throughout) | arrivals, fte, high_complexity_pct, calendar-month FE | E1, E3, E4, E6 aggregated to overall and event-time ATT | parallel trends conditional on covariates absent treatment; no anticipation; no cross-unit interference | pre-treatment event-study coefficients 0; placebo test using only never-treated pairs; covariate balance pre-period | selection of Alpha/Beta on poor prior performance biases parallel trends (mean reversion); routing campaign breaks no-interference |
| Alt 1: synthetic control per treated branch | single adoption date per branch | weighted combination of Delta, Epsilon, pre-period Gamma | pre-period trajectory of each outcome, arrivals growth, complexity | branch-specific ATT trajectory | good pre-period fit; donor pool untreated and unaffected by same idiosyncratic shocks | pre-period RMSPE; placebo-in-space by "treating" Delta/Epsilon | only 2–3 usable donors  unstable/overfit weights, unreliable placebo inference |
| Alt 2: comparative interrupted time series (segmented regression, control-adjusted) | level/slope break at each branch's go-live date | Delta/Epsilon calendar-time trend used to net out shared shocks | month FE, complexity, arrivals, fte as covariates | level/slope change at adoption | shared shocks (fraud desk, product change) hit treated and control equally | check for coincident slope breaks in controls; sensitivity to window length | routing-campaign and FTE shocks are *not* shared with controls, so ITS residual mixes automation with these |
| Alt 3: dose-response with instrumented intensity | continuous `automated_fast_path_pct`, instrumented by months-since-go-live | within-branch variation over time | branch & month FE, complexity, arrivals | E2 | instrument affects outcomes only through usage intensity (exclusion) | first-stage strength; sensitivity to alternative instruments | exclusion likely violated — time-since-go-live also proxies learning, complexity drift, and FTE changes |

The staggered event-study design is preferred here because timing is genuinely staggered (April/July/never) and treatment effects plausibly evolve over calendar time (the Alpha/Beta implementation dip followed by partial recovery, versus Gamma's smoother post-period). A naïve two-way fixed-effects regression pooling all periods would implicitly use already-treated Alpha/Beta as comparison units for Gamma's later effect, and would average an early "worse" period with a later "better" period into a single, potentially sign-flipped coefficient (a Goodman-Bacon problem) — exactly the pattern visible in the raw numbers. The event-study/group-time approach instead uses genuinely not-yet-treated branches at each calendar date and reports effects by event-time, which is the only way to represent the implementation-dip pattern honestly. Synthetic control is attractive in spirit but starved of donors (only two never-treated branches plus a short pre-period Gamma), making weight estimation unstable. Comparative ITS is useful as a robustness check but cannot separate automation from the branch-specific routing/FTE shocks that hit only the treated units. None of these designs resolve the core selection problem (Alpha/Beta chosen for poor prior performance), so all should be read as conditional, not proof of causality.

**3. Treatment-intensity (dose-response) view — Alpha, post-adoption months**

fast-path%: 25,32,39,46,48; p90: 97.3,112.6,119.9,109.6,101.2. Approximate Pearson r  0.20 — a weak, almost uninformative *linear* association, because the true relationship is hump-shaped in time (congestion rises, then eases as adoption matures and FTE/complexity stabilize). A naive linear dose-response regression would badly understate this dynamic.

**4. Pre-trends and regression-to-mean**

Only 3 pre-period points exist per branch (Jan–Mar) for Alpha/Beta, which is too little to reliably distinguish a real differential pre-trend from noise. Critically, Alpha and Beta were chosen *because* they were historically weak performers — the textbook setup for regression to the mean, which would produce an apparent "improvement" in relative terms even with zero causal effect. The DiD estimates above cannot rule this out; they can only be read as "automation-branches moved better than expected relative to control-branch trend," not as a clean causal effect.

**5. Sensitivity / bounded conclusions**

- *median_cycle_h*: bounded between "no true effect" (0%) and "moderate relative improvement" (~10% vs. counterfactual trend); absolute cycle time has **not** clearly fallen for Alpha/Beta.
- *escalation_pct*: bounded between "no automation-specific effect beyond the fraud desk" (0) and "+1.5–2pp incremental increase" (~+10–15% relative), because the fraud-desk shock (shared) and automation-specific triage conservatism (branch-specific) cannot be fully separated with these data.

## 6. Threats, tests, and decision consequences

| threat | why it matters here | direction of likely bias | test or additional evidence | consequence if threat is severe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Selection into adoption | Alpha/Beta chosen for poor prior performance | overstates benefit (mean reversion) | longer pre-period event study; compare baseline levels | apparent gains are statistical artifact |
| Nonparallel trends | only 3 pre-periods per branch, no formal pre-trend test possible | unknown sign | placebo tests on never-treated pairs | DiD/event-study estimates invalid |

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**expand with an embedded evaluation**

Next branch: **Delta**, not Delta and Epsilon simultaneously. Delta's baseline profile (arrivals ~630–770/month, complexity 18–23%, escalation ~9%) is closer to Gamma's low-volume profile than to Alpha/Beta's, giving a cleaner test of whether the pattern generalizes beyond the two high-volume, historically-weak branches that were deliberately targeted first. Keep Epsilon as an active **not-yet-treated control** for at least the following two quarters so the same "not-yet-treated comparison" logic used for Alpha/Beta/Gamma remains available.

Phase-in mechanism: use a **stepped-wedge** design — stagger Delta's go-live from Epsilon's by at least 3 months rather than switching both at once, preserving a clean control arm at every stage. If speed is essential, consider a **within-branch partial rollout** in Delta (automate a randomly chosen subset of case types or intake channels) to create a genuinely randomized internal contrast — the strongest design feasible given only five branches total.

Minimum measurement: at least 6 months of stable pre-treatment data (current pre-periods of 3–8 months are thin for trend estimation) and at least 6 months post go-live before drawing conclusions, since Alpha showed a 3–5 month implementation dip before improvement appeared.

Data to collect: case-level event logs (timestamps per activity) rather than only monthly aggregates, enabling real bottleneck/variant analysis; fraud-desk queue and wait times by branch; case-level path taken (fast vs. escalated, automated vs. manual) paired with complexity flags; FTE/shift-level staffing logs; and survey response counts alongside csat means.

Spillover monitoring: track fraud-desk aggregate queue times and routing-campaign allocation percentages monthly as explicit covariates, not implicit background conditions.

Guardrails: pause further automated-routing changes if branch p90 cycle time or escalation_pct rises more than a pre-set threshold (e.g., +20% over a rolling 3-month baseline) without offsetting FTE capacity; maintain a manual fallback path.

Decision rule for scale-up: expand fully only if Delta's event-study/synthetic-control estimates replicate the direction and rough magnitude seen in Alpha/Beta/Gamma (median and rework non-worsening relative to control, escalation increase within guardrail bounds) over the full post-period, using cluster- or randomization-based inference rather than point estimates alone.
