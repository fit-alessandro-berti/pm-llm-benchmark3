## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Six observations that would weaken the top-ranked batch-synchronization explanation:**
1. OVEN-1 has a fixed thermal-start schedule at 12:05 regardless of job readiness; batch timing is planned, not synchronized.
2. J1’s “ready at 08:10” is paperwork readiness; C1 from L8 is not physically available until 11:55, so the 235 min is component delay, not batch wait.
3. RACK-7 cleaning is a post-curing sanitize step that can run in parallel with B2 loading; it is not a hard gate.
4. The 12:05 B1 start is a supervisor’s efficiency choice; B1 could have started at 08:10 with only J2 (if J1/J3 were not needed), meaning synchronization is elective.
5. J3’s FIX-A hold is confounded by CNC-2 setup; the idle gap is machine setup, not fixture wait.
6. B1 and B2 are actually continuous-flow batches in reality, and the “start” timestamp is a reporting artifact.

**Six additional fields / event types to discriminate hypotheses:**
1. Explicit `WaitForBatch`, `WaitForTool`, `WaitForLot`, `WaitForShipment` events with timestamps.
2. ComponentLot quality-inspection results linked to specific Job IDs and component revisions.
3. Machine state-change events with reason codes (`idle_reason`: batch_wait, tool_wait, maintenance, lot_release).
4. Tool calibration/fixture-assignment logs with `Calibrate`/`Assign`/`Release` events for FIX-A, CAL-3, RACK-7.
5. Shipment dispatch-window constraints and courier-booking times to separate planned vs. forced consolidation.
6. Batch-release policy rules (minimum load %, maximum wait time) as event attributes or process parameters.

These would allow direct measurement of synchronization delay vs. capacity delay vs. data artifact, rather than inference from gap lengths.
