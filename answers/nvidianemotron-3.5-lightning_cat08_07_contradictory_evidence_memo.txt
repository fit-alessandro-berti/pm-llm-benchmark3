**1. Executive Memo**

**To:** Senior Management, Fiber Deployment Division  
**From:** Process Improvement Office  
**Date:** December 2, 2024  
**Subject:** Decision on Redesign R – Scale, Modify, or Invest Further?

**Situation**  
A 6-week pilot of Redesign R (9,200 cases across four regions) introduced coordinator ownership, merged address validation/scheduling, a predictive rule to skip field surveys for mediumrisk addresses, and moved activation checks after billing start. Processmining evidence shows a clear tradeoff: median cycle time improved by 2.4 days, handovers dropped 65%, and visible cost per installation fell EUR 23. However, the tail of duration worsened sharply (p90 +7.5 days, p95 +17 days), firsttimeright installation fell 6 percentage points, complaints rose 61%, manual activations doubled, and SLA breaches increased overall. Segment analysis reveals that simple, lowrisk houses benefit substantially, while apartments, older buildings, and business customers experience notable deterioration, especially in tail duration, rework, and activation compliance.

**Tradeoff Summary**  
Redesign R delivers speed and handover efficiency for a majoritysimplehouse segment but introduces compliance gaps, resilience risk, and downstream rework for complex cases. The pilot model discovered high fitness (0.94) but low precision (0.51), meaning the observed cases fit the new path extremely well, but the model permits many invalid combinations (e.g., billing start before activation test, repeated visits without survey, missing permit checks). Coordinator utilization reached 93% in two regions, and when coordinators were absent, cases stalled due to missing context. Downstream cost data excludes complainthandling after week 4, so the true cost impact is likely worse than the EUR 161 vs. 184 visiblecost comparison suggests.

**Recommendation**  
Do not nationalscale Redesign R in its current form. Do not roll back entirely, as simplehouse gains are real and handover reduction improves accountability. Instead, adopt a **hybrid, measurementdriven rollout (Alternative E)** with the following immediate steps:
- **Pause** expansion of the surveyskipping rule and postbilling activation checks beyond simple, lowrisk houses.
- **Scale** coordinator ownership only for simple, lowbuildingrisk addresses (the 34% pilot segment that shows –6.0 day median improvement and +3 pp firsttimeright gain).
- **Invest** in the eight measurement priorities (detailed in Section 5) before any further expansion.
- **Reevaluate** after 8 weeks of controlled deployment with segmentspecific controls and the toppriority data collection.

This approach captures the proven benefits for the beneficiary segment while systematically addressing the tailrisk, compliance, and resilience issues that currently limit the redesign’s viability.

**3. Explanation of Metric Conflicts**

- **High fitness but low precision:** The pilot discovered model fits 94% of observed cases because the coordinator path dominates simplehouse trajectories. However, the model permits many legally possible sequences (e.g., billing start before activation test, repeated visits without survey), resulting in precision of only 0.51. This is not a modeling error; it reflects that the redesign intentionally deviated from the baseline path for efficiency, and the event log captures all deviations.

- **Improved median but worse tail duration:** The merge of address validation/scheduling and coordinator ownership significantly accelerated the typical case (median 2.4 days). However, the skiprule for mediumrisk addresses and the postbilling activation check systematically lengthen the tails for complex cases: apartments encounter incorrect skip classifications, older buildings miss permit checks, and manual activation adds uncertain steps. The p90/p95 metrics are driven by these segments, which constitute 47% of the pilot sample.

- **Fewer handovers but lower resilience:** Coordinator ownership reduced 3+handover cases from 34% to 12%, improving accountability. Yet utilization hit 93% in two regions, and when coordinators were absent, cases stalled because contextual knowledge was not shared. The “workingtogether network” became more centralized, reducing distributed expertise and increasing dependency on individual coordinators.

- **Lower visible cost but higher rework/downstream cost:** Mean cost before complaint handling improved (EUR 161 vs. 184), but estimated total cost including complaints/revisits rose from EUR 198 to 207. The pilot’s cost data explicitly excluded downstream complaint handling after week 4. Manual activations (15% vs. 7%), second visits (18% vs. 11%), and complaint escalation after billing start (8% vs. 3%) all shift cost downstream, eroding the apparent visiblecost benefit.

- **Improvement for simple houses but deterioration for apartments/old buildings:** Segmentlevel evidence is unambiguous: simple, lowrisk houses enjoy median reductions of 6 days, better firsttimeright, and fewer complaints. Apartments (29% of pilot) suffer p90 increases of +12 days, 8point drops in firsttimeright, and a 5point complaint rise. Oldbuilding, permitsensitive cases show +1 day median, +25 day p90, –11 pp firsttimeright, and +7 pp complaints. The redesign’s efficiencies are segmentcontingent, not universal.

**6. Final Recommendation**

**Decision:** Adopt **Alternative E** – a hybrid, measurementdriven rollout of Redesign R – with immediate actions, targeted pauses, and a defined evidence threshold before any broader expansion.

- **Immediate action:**  
  - Deploy Redesign R **only for simple, lowbuildingrisk houses** (the 34% pilot segment showing –6.0 day median improvement, +3 pp firsttimeright, and –1 pp complaint rate).  
  - Keep coordinator ownership for this segment, but **cap utilization at 80%** by adding backup coordinators or rotating shifts in the two pilot regions at 93% utilization.  
  - **Pause** any expansion of the surveyskipping rule and postbilling activation checks beyond this segment.

- **What to pause:**  
  - Nationwide scaling of Redesign R.  
  - Further rollout of the mediumrisk surveyskip rule and activationafterbilling check to apartments, old buildings, or business customers.  
  - Any increase in coordinator workload beyond 80% utilization without concurrent resilience measures.

- **What to scale:**  
  - Coordinatorownership model **restricted to simple, lowrisk addresses**.  
  - Merged addressvalidation/scheduling task for this segment (proven time saver).  
  - Processmining–based conformance monitoring (M7) as an ongoing governance tool.

- **What to measure next (immediate priority):**  
  1. **M2** – Instrument start timestamps for Installation visit, Activation test, and Manual activation (enables accurate cycletime splits and tail diagnosis).  
  2. **M6** – Validate the buildingrisk predictive rule against actual fieldsurvey outcomes (determines if skiprule can be safely reapplied).  
  3. **M7** – Run weekly conformance monitoring for “Billing start before Activation test” and “Missing permit check” across the deployed segment (ensures compliance does not reemerge).  
  4. **M4** – Capture full downstream cost (60day postbilling) for the deployed cohort (closes the visiblevstotal cost gap).

- **Threshold / evidence for broader rollout:**  
  - **M6** shows ruleaccuracy 85% for the nextrisk stratum, or a corrected rule that eliminates >90% of the observed p90/p95 worsening in apartments/old buildings.  
  - **M2** confirms that tail duration for the pilot segment converges to within 2 days of baseline p90 after accounting for measurement artifact.  
  - **M7** conformance violations for “Billing start before Activation” and “Missing permit check” remain **5%** in the deployed segment over 8 weeks.  
  - **M4** total cost (including 60day downstream) for the deployed segment is **EUR 175** (i.e., at least as good as baseline visible cost and not worsened by rework).  

If all four thresholds are met within an 8week measurement window, management may **gradually expand** Redesign R to include mediumrisk houses with the corrected skip rule and activation protocol, accompanied by continued conformance monitoring and utilization caps. If any threshold fails, the redesign should be **reengineered** (e.g., alternate skip logic, prebilling activation, or sharedteam ownership) before further scale.

This approach respects the segmentspecific benefits uncovered by the pilot, avoids imposing known tail and compliance risks on complex cases, and builds an evidence loop that makes any future national scaling defensible and datadriven.
