## 1. What is observed

The dataset tracks five service branches across eight monthly periods (2026-01 to 2026-08). Binary **automation availability** (`automation=1`) was enabled for high-volume, high-cycle-time branches Alpha and Beta in April 2026, followed by Gamma in July 2026, while Delta and Epsilon remained untreated controls. However, availability does not equal full adoption: **actual treatment intensity** (`automated_fast_path_pct`) scaled gradually from 4–6% baseline noise to 48% (Alpha), 50% (Beta), and 32% (Gamma), reflecting operational ramp-up. 

**Branch selection** into the rollout was non-random and endogenous: early adopters possessed worse baseline median cycle times (~45–50 hours) and larger workloads than controls (~33–41 hours). **Contemporaneous operational changes** co-occurred throughout the rollout period:
1. A centralized fraud desk opened in March for all branches, increasing baseline escalation rates.
2. A workload routing campaign in April directed +10% case arrivals to Alpha and Beta, and +5% to Gamma in July.
3. Product complexity surged organization-wide in May, raising `high_complexity_pct` by 5 percentage points across all branches.
4. Productive FTE staffing fluctuated independently (Alpha lost 2 FTEs; Beta gained 1 FTE; Gamma lost 1 FTE; Delta gained 1 FTE).

Finally, **outcome measurement limitations** constrain inference: CSAT survey response rates ranged from 20% to 45% without reported denominator counts, and branch-month aggregations blur individual case-level routing paths and unmeasured external queue wait times at the central fraud desk.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary: Staggered DiD with Not-Yet-Treated Controls (Callaway-Sant'Anna / Sun-Abraham)** | Binary cohort timing flags ($g \in \{Apr, Jul\}$) across relative event time $e$ | Not-yet-treated (Gamma pre-July) and never-treated (Delta, Epsilon) | Time-varying workload per FTE (`arrivals/fte`), `high_complexity_pct`, fraud desk flag | Group-Time Average Treatment Effects $ATT(g,t)$ aggregated to ITT | Parallel trends in counterfactual outcomes conditional on workload ratio and complexity; no unobserved cross-branch spillovers. | Event-study pre-trend testing ($e < 0$ coefficients equal to zero); placebo treatment dates. | Unobserved time-varying branch-specific shocks (e.g., local manager process changes, fraud desk queue congestion). |
| **Alternative 1: Synthetic Control / Augmented SCM** | Fixed rollout dates per treated branch (Alpha: Apr, Beta: Apr, Gamma: Jul) | Weighted convex combination of control branches (Delta, Epsilon) matching pre-rollout dynamics | Pre-treatment trajectory of outcomes, baseline FTE, baseline complexity | Individual branch synthetic counterfactual ITT ($ATT_i$) | Convex combination of controls reproduces counterfactual trajectory of treated branch in the absence of treatment. | In-space placebo tests (assigning pseudo-treatment to Delta/Epsilon); leave-one-out sensitivity. | Overfitting pre-period noise due to small donor pool ($N_{donor}=2$) and short pre-period ($T_{pre}=3$). |
| **Alternative 2: Two-Stage Least Squares (2SLS) / IV** | Continuous treatment intensity (`automated_fast_path_pct`) | Lower-intensity branch-months | `arrivals/fte`, `high_complexity_pct`, branch and month fixed effects | LATE per percentage point of fast-path adoption | Instrumental Variable (Rollout policy $\times$ Time) affects cycle time *only* through fast-path adoption (exclusion restriction). | First-stage $F$-statistic; tests of overidentifying restrictions. | Exclusion restriction violation: automation rollout directly altered specialist queue priorities and local work practices. |

### Justification of Primary Strategy

The primary strategy—a staggered Difference-in-Differences design utilizing non-treated and not-yet-treated units with covariate adjustment for workload intensity (`arrivals / fte`) and case complexity (`high_complexity_pct`)—is superior to standard two-way fixed-effects (TWFE) OLS regressions. Naïve TWFE models generate biased treatment estimates when treatment timing is staggered and treatment effects evolve dynamically over time (e.g., learning curves), because previously treated units act as controls for newly treated units, creating negative weight distortions.

By isolating cohort-specific average treatment effects $ATT(g,t)$, this strategy explicitly models the progressive adoption intensity of automation. Furthermore, controlling for workload ratios (`arrivals/fte`) directly addresses the contemporaneous confounding induced by the April routing campaign (+10% volume to Alpha/Beta) and May staffing losses in Alpha (-2 FTEs). While limitations persist due to the small cluster size ($N=5$), this design transparently leverages the late adoption of Gamma (July) as an intermediate control, enabling robust pre-trend validation prior to treatment contrast.

### 2. Comparison Against Never-Treated Controls (Delta & Epsilon)
Between Jan–Mar and Apr–Aug, never-treated control branches experienced substantial performance degradation due to external operational shocks (May product complexity surge):
- **Delta (Control):** `median_cycle_h` increased by **+5.87 h** (+17.0%); `p90_cycle_h` increased by **+17.31 h** (+24.1%).
- **Epsilon (Control):** `median_cycle_h` increased by **+4.44 h** (+11.0%); `p90_cycle_h` increased by **+13.58 h** (+16.4%).

**Difference-in-Differences Arithmetic (Unadjusted):**
- **Alpha vs Controls DiD (Median Cycle Time):** $-0.40 - (+5.16) = \mathbf{-5.56\text{ hours}}$.
- **Alpha vs Controls DiD (P90 Cycle Time):** $+9.65 - (+15.45) = \mathbf{-5.80\text{ hours}}$.
- **Beta vs Controls DiD (Median Cycle Time):** $+1.86 - (+5.16) = \mathbf{-3.30\text{ hours}}$.
- **Beta vs Controls DiD (P90 Cycle Time):** $+9.81 - (+15.45) = \mathbf{-5.64\text{ hours}}$.

*Arithmetic Interpretation:* Raw cycle times rose in Alpha and Beta post-April, but they rose *substantially less* than in control branches that lacked automation during the May workload/complexity surge.

### 3. Treatment Intensity Analysis
Regressing outcomes on continuous `automated_fast_path_pct` across all branch-months:
- **Median Cycle Time:** A 10 percentage point increase in fast-path usage is associated with a **-0.42 hour** reduction in median cycle time ($p = 0.38$, unadjusted OLS panel model).
- **P90 Cycle Time:** A 10 percentage point increase in fast-path usage is associated with a **+1.85 hour** increase in p90 cycle time ($p = 0.12$), capturing queue congestion during initial implementation months before recovering in August.

### 4. Pre-Trends and Regression to the Mean
From Jan to Mar, treated branches were stable (Alpha median: 50.4h $\rightarrow$ 49.4h; Beta median: 45.4h $\rightarrow$ 46.4h). Control branches were also stable (Delta: 34.4h $\rightarrow$ 36.2h; Epsilon: 41.4h $\rightarrow$ 40.2h). Pre-trends were parallel. However, because Alpha and Beta were selected specifically for high baseline cycle times, apparent post-treatment stability relative to control deterioration could partially reflect regression to the mean if baseline cycle time peaks were transient.

### 5. Sensitivity and Bounded Effect Intervals
- **Median Cycle Time Net Effect:** Bounded between **-6.0 hours** (full DiD model adjusting for routing volume) and **+1.8 hours** (raw pre/post comparison without controls).
- **P90 Cycle Time Net Effect:** Bounded between **-5.8 hours** (relative to non-treated branches experiencing extreme congestion) and **+9.8 hours** (unadjusted pre/post expansion).

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**Chosen Policy Option:** `expand with an embedded evaluation`

### Implementation Framework

#### 1. Target Branches & Phase-in Mechanism
Expansion should immediately incorporate **Delta** and **Epsilon** using a **stepped-wedge randomized cluster trial design**. Delta will be randomly assigned to receive automation in Month 9 (September 2026), while Epsilon will serve as a pure randomized control until Month 11 (November 2026), at which point it will adopt the system.

#### 2. Measurement Horizons & Queue-State Data
- **Pre-period baseline:** Maintain mandatory 2-month pre-expansion tracking of individual case logs.
- **Post-period evaluation:** Require 3 months of steady-state post-enablement tracking per cohort.
- **Granular Data Collection:** Transition immediately from aggregate branch-month summaries to case-level event logs. Collect exact activity timestamps (`Register`, `Triage`, `Specialist_Queue_Entry`, `Specialist_Review_Start`, `Resolve`, `Close`), worker IDs, risk scores, and queue wait times at both the local branch and central fraud desk levels.

#### 3. Spillover Monitoring & Guardrails
- **Fraud Desk Tracking:** Track central fraud desk backlog and incoming referral rates per branch daily. If central fraud desk wait time exceeds 24 hours, automated triage escalation thresholds must auto-adjust to reduce referral volume.
- **Customer Harm Guardrails:** 
  - **P90 Cap:** If a branch’s `p90_cycle_h` increases by $>15\%$ over baseline for two consecutive months, manual triage overrides will be reintroduced.
  - **CSAT Floor:** Trigger an immediate operational audit if branch CSAT falls below $3.75$ or if tail-case CSAT diverges from fast-path CSAT by $>0.5$ points.

#### 4. Scale-up Decision Rule
Full organizational scale-up across all future branches will proceed if and only if the stepped-wedge evaluation demonstrates:
1. A statistically significant reduction in `median_cycle_h` of $\ge 4.0$ hours relative to randomized controls ($p < 0.05$ via Wild Cluster Bootstrap);
2. Non-inferiority in `p90_cycle_h` (defined as $< +5\%$ relative increase post-ramp-up);
3. Non-inferiority in CSAT ($\ge -0.05$ point difference against control).

## 10. Final claim ladder

- **Observed:** Following the enablements in April and July, adoption intensity (`automated_fast_path_pct`) scaled gradually to ~50% in Alpha and Beta and ~32% in Gamma, while specialist escalation rates increased structural baseline levels by 2 to 3 percentage points across all adopting branches.
- **Suggestive:** Automated triage mitigated cycle time degradation during the May workload and product complexity surge, enabling adopting branches to achieve net median cycle time improvements of 3 to 5 hours relative to non-treated control branches by August.
- **Conditionally causal:** Assuming parallel counterfactual trends conditional on workload per FTE and case complexity, enabling automated triage reduces median branch cycle time by approximately 2.0 to 5.5 hours, but does not prevent severe specialist queue tail bottlenecks (`p90_cycle_h`) during operational surges.
- **Not supported:** The claim that "automation reduces cycle time by X%" as a single overall aggregate statement is unsupported by the data, as is any claim that automated triage improves customer satisfaction (CSAT) or reduces 90th percentile tail delays.
