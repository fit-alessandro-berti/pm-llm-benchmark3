## 2. Counterfactual continuations
1. D: alternate screen runs now. If it clears the fuzzy match and a refreshed score falls below 70, the escalation condition may disappear; otherwise an independent second review remains mandatory. If S4 is free by about 35 minutes, S4 reviews; if not, switch to S2. After that review: approve -> execute; unresolved concern -> hold and possibly file a SAR; clearly adverse findings -> reject. Main uncertainty: S4 timing, whether the alternate screen materially changes the match assessment, and whether the stale 72 score still remains at least 70 when refreshed.
2. C: S2 reviews immediately after the post-evidence analyst state is recorded. Branches are approve -> execute within the remaining window if the review is quick enough; unresolved concern -> hold/SAR; adverse findings -> reject. Main uncertainty: review duration and whether the current evidence is sufficient for approval versus hold.
3. E: more evidence is requested from the customer. The case returns hours later to evidence received/validated, then analyst review, and likely second-officer review if either the risk score stays at least 70 or the sanctions match remains unresolved. Likely outcomes are eventual approve, reject, or extended hold. Main uncertainty: customer responsiveness and whether new evidence actually resolves the match.
4. F: if the internal rejection threshold is already satisfied, reject now with a documented rationale and stop execution risk. If the threshold is not actually satisfied, the action should not stand and the case must return to the normal review path. Main uncertainty: whether current facts are enough for a policy-valid rejection rather than only a hold/escalation.
5. G: attempt to reach the Compliance Director and create a justified incident ID. If either prerequisite fails, there is still no valid override and normal processing resumes with less time left. If both succeed, an exception route becomes available, but execution is still only valid if the override is properly recorded. Main uncertainty: reachability, justification strength, and time consumption.

## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning
The key dominance point is that A and B trade at most about €20k-€35k of deadline value for plausibly €100k-€500k+ of unlawful-execution exposure plus non-monetary consequences. That asymmetry dominates any speed advantage, so a favorable forecast cannot rescue them.

Among conformant options, the real tradeoff is between speed, decision quality, and spillovers. C is the fastest valid path and best protects P-884’s deadline. D is slightly slower because it spends 15-30 minutes on more information, but that information is valuable on a €480k payment to a sanctioned-border region with an unresolved 0.46 match and a stale high risk score; D also may avoid harming P-901 if S4 arrives near the short end of its ETA. E and F control execution risk but are overconservative as first moves because the predicted ultimate approval rate after conformant review is still 0.55-0.75, so both carry a meaningful chance of avoidable customer harm in the €15k-€80k range.

I treat the SLA and approval predictions as directional, not precise interventional utilities, because they are observational and staffing changes, alternate screening, or exception handling can shift them.

## 4. Conformance constraints
A maps to rules 6 and 9 and fails both: with risk still at least 70 on the current record and an unresolved fuzzy match, execution without second-officer review is not a valid approval path.

B maps to rules 6, 7, and 9 and fails them: the mandatory independent second review is missing, and R17 cannot satisfy the four-eyes principle by self-certifying.

C is conformant under rules 6-9 if S2 performs a real independent review and execution occurs only after approval. Rule 5 also means the post-evidence analyst state should be properly recorded before final approval is entered.

D is conformant under rules 5-9 if the alternate screening result is treated as additional evidence and does not substitute for the required second review while the present escalation conditions remain.

E is conformant under rule 5 because it continues the evidence loop. After new evidence, the case returns to analyst review and, if the risk or match trigger still exists, to second-officer review before approval/execution.

F is only valid if a documented internal policy threshold for rejection is met on the current facts. What is needed is audit-ready evidence that the unresolved match, sanctioned-border context, amount, and validated record together satisfy the bank’s rejection standard, not merely suspicion or inconvenience.

G is governed by rule 10 and is not enabled now. It becomes valid only if both prerequisites are actually satisfied: Compliance Director authorization and a valid, justified incident identifier recorded in the case.

Rules 8 and 11 still apply across the conformant branches: a hold or SAR filing does not authorize execution, and any SAR must not be disclosed to the customer.

No favorable numerical forecast can make A or B recommendable, because the missing approval path and broken four-eyes control are hard gating conditions, not soft penalties in a utility function.

## 5. Resource spillovers
S2 is the fastest conformant route for P-884, but it directly harms P-901 by increasing that case’s breach probability by about 0.12-0.28. That spillover is acceptable only when waiting for S4 would materially worsen P-884’s outcome.

S4 avoids that spillover. If S4 is available in roughly 35 minutes, D becomes especially attractive because the alternate screen can finish before or near S4’s availability, giving the second officer a better record without consuming P-901’s buffer.

The alternate vendor has only three expedited calls left. Using one on this case is proportionate because the payment is large, geographically sensitive, and currently ambiguous, but the call should run in parallel and should not be allowed to create an open-ended wait.

G has the worst time-consumption profile: 20-60+ minutes can disappear before learning that no valid override exists, leaving too little time for the ordinary second-review path.

If S4 is confirmed at 35 minutes, D strengthens its lead over C because it avoids P-901 spillover at tolerable delay. If S4 is confirmed at 65 minutes, C should move above D immediately; at that point the ranking becomes C, D, E, F, G, A, B because waiting any longer for S4 costs too much deadline value.

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence
Primary recommendation: take D as an adaptive conformant plan. Launch the alternate sanctions screen now, have the case state updated for the post-evidence analyst step immediately, and set a hard cutoff at about 35 minutes for S4. If S4 is available by then, use S4 for the independent second review; if not, stop waiting and switch at once to C with S2.
