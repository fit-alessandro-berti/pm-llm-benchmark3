# 2. Competing role hypotheses  

## Hypothesis 1: Formal **Coordinator / Exception Desk**  
**Mechanism**: After Bot_type automated decisions (e.g., Fraud Screen), Mira receives flagged cases, investigates missing information, and assigns them to the appropriate specialist (medical, legal, adjuster). She also reopens cases for clarification, acting as the central “queue cleaner.”  
**Supporting evidence**:  
- High handover outdegree & betweenness (0.82, 0.77).  
- 64% of reopened claims and 71% of complaints involve her.  
- Absence leads to queue buildup for exceptionflagged cases.  
**Limiting evidence**:  
- Low workingtogether degree suggests limited joint work rather than formal coordination within teams.  
- No explicit role label in the log; she could be a temporary role.  
**Predictions**:  
- Further logs will show a rising trend in exceptionflagged cases if her capacity remains unchanged.  
- Introducing a **backup exceptiondesk** (e.g., a temporary second coordinator) would flatten the 90thpercentile cycle time for exception cases.  

## Hypothesis 2: **Hidden Bottleneck / KeyPerson Dependency**  
**Mechanism**: Because many critical decision loops (e.g., Mira  Omar  Mira, Mira  Priya  Mira) loop back to her within a short span, she becomes a **single point of failure**. Any slowdown (absence, overload) ripples through the process.  
**Supporting evidence**:  
- 6day absence caused 90thpercentile cycle time for exception cases to increase from 8.4 to 13.9 days.  
- High subcontracting betweenness (0.69) indicates loops that rely on her.  
- Compliance reviewer’s concern about “uncontrolled discretion.”  
**Limiting evidence**:  
- Servicetime data for Mira is not provided; delays could stem from external specialist response times rather than her processing speed.  
**Predictions**:  
- Monitoring **Mira’s handling rate vs. specialist leadtime** will reveal if specialist delays are the primary cause.  
- Adding **parallel exception handlers** should reduce queue accumulation without affecting overall throughput.  

## Hypothesis 3: **Organizational Boundary Spanner / Triage Broker**  
**Mechanism**: Mira sits at the intersection of **automated/bot** decisions and **specialist** interventions. She translates bot outputs into specialist tasks and viceversa, effectively **spanning boundaries** of the claimhandling ecosystem.  
**Supporting evidence**:  
- High handover from Bot_Rules to her, then to specialists (Omar, Priya, adjusters).  
- Role discovery shows a distinct activity profile not shared by bots or specialists.  
- “Queue cleaner” description aligns with a **triage** function.  
**Limiting evidence**:  
- Peripheral workingtogether degree may indicate that she does **not** regularly cooperate within specialist teams beyond brief handoffs.  
- Data may reflect that many of her events are **routing** rather than **valueadding** judgment.  
**Predictions**:  
- Process redesign moving more triage logic into Bot_Rules could reduce Mira’s centrality, testing whether specialty leads still accept the filtered cases.  
- Evaluating **exceptionrate per bottohuman handover** will show if boundary spanning is essential or duplicative.
