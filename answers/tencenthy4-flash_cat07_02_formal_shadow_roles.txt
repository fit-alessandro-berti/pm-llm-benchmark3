  Giorgio is not merely a front-office agent with odd extra activities. His pricing and negotiation work during Fatima’s absence, combined with pricing certification and medium-high case mix, supports a **commercial shadow** interpretation.  
  **Benefits:** continuity during absence, practical backup for A7/A8, useful knowledge of complex front-office-commercial cases.  
  **Risks:** pricing duration nearly doubled during substitution; incomplete authority boundaries around A9; possible drift between “shadowing” and actually owning commercial decisions. This is not improper — it is informal resilience — but should be controlled with checklists, delegation rules, and post-event review.

- **R13_Mateo — cross-functional escalation broker**  
  Mateo appears wherever cases cross boundaries: reopen, coordination, technical/commercial/security handoffs, preliminary approval, and escalation filtering.  
  **Benefits:** reduces fragmentation, routes complex cases, may reduce false escalations.  
  **Risks:** the security-filtering episode shows the danger of a broker becoming a gatekeeper. Two critical cases waiting over two days indicates that routing efficiency must not override specialist review. His preliminary A9 work also needs clear separation from Hana’s final authority.

- **R05_Erik — rare but critical specialist**  
  Erik is a low-frequency, high-consequence role. Standard clustering would undervalue him because A6 is rare compared with A1/A2/A4/A12.  
  **Benefits:** deep security review for high-risk cases, critical intervention capacity.  
  **Risks:** single-point-of-failure; when his flow is reduced or mediated by Mateo, critical cases can wait. This role needs capacity protection rather than elimination based on low counts.

- **R14_Nora — automation boundary role**  
  Nora is a bridging boundary between automated intake and human work.  
  **Benefits:** scale and consistency in A1/A2.  
  **Risks:** if A2 precheck quality is assumed sufficient for all cases, downstream rework can increase. Automation should route exceptions explicitly.

- **R11_Kai and R12_Lina — temporary-workforce shadow layer**  
  They are not shadow roles in the sense of hidden authority; they are a shadow capacity layer for low-complexity work.  
  **Benefits:** seasonal load absorption.  
  **Risks:** incomplete A2 notes led to reopens, so their apparent efficiency may transfer work downstream.

- **R09_Ivan and R10_Julia — quality gatekeeping bridge**  
  They bridge technical implementation and closure through A11 and A14. Their role is not “secondary technical analyst” but control plus organizational learning.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# -----------------------------
# 1. Load / format dataframe
# -----------------------------
# df_raw should contain:
# case:concept:name, concept:name, time:timestamp, org:resource,
# case_complexity, customer_segment, product_line,
# temporary_substitution, escalation_level

df = df_raw.copy()

df_fmt = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
)

df_fmt["case:concept:name"] = df_fmt["case:concept:name"].astype(str)
df_fmt["concept:name"] = df_fmt["concept:name"].astype(str)
df_fmt["org:resource"] = df_fmt["org:resource"].astype(str)
df_fmt["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df_fmt["time:timestamp"])

df_fmt["case_complexity"] = pd.to_numeric(df_fmt["case_complexity"], errors="coerce")
df_fmt["escalation_level"] = pd.to_numeric(df_fmt["escalation_level"], errors="coerce")

def to_bool(s):
    return s.astype(str).str.strip().str.lower().isin(["1", "true", "yes", "y", "t"])

df_fmt["temp_event_flag"] = to_bool(df_fmt["temporary_substitution"])

# Case-level temporary substitution indicator
df_fmt["temp_case_flag"] = (
    df_fmt
    .groupby("case:concept:name")["temp_event_flag"]
    .transform("max")
)

df_fmt = df_fmt.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"]).reset_index(drop=True)

log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df_fmt)

# -----------------------------
# 2. Discover organizational roles
# -----------------------------
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(
    log,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
    role_key="org:role"
)

# Normalize PM4Py role output to a resource-level table.
# Exact structure can vary by PM4Py release, so this is defensive.
def roles_to_table(roles_obj):
    rows = []

    for role_name, payload in roles_obj.items():
        if isinstance(payload, dict):
            resources = payload.get("resources", payload.get("resource", []))
            activities = payload.get("activities", payload.get("activity", []))
        elif isinstance(payload, (list, tuple, set)):
            resources = list(payload)
            activities = []
        else:
            resources = [payload]
            activities = []

        if isinstance(resources, str):
            resources = [resources]
        if isinstance(activities, str):
            activities = [activities]

        for r in resources:
            rows.append({
                "discovered_role": role_name,
                "org:resource": str(r),
                "discovered_role_activities": ", ".join(map(str, activities))
            })

    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

role_tbl = roles_to_table(roles)

# -----------------------------
# 3. Activity-based resource similarity
# -----------------------------
resource_similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
    log,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource"
)

# If returned as a DataFrame, inspect selected pairs.
if isinstance(resource_similarity, pd.DataFrame):
    selected = [
        r for r in [
            "R03_Chen", "R04_Dina",
            "R09_Ivan", "R10_Julia",
            "R11_Kai", "R12_Lina",
            "R06_Fatima", "R07_Giorgio",
            "R08_Hana", "R13_Mateo",
            "R05_Erik", "R13_Mateo"
        ] if r in resource_similarity.index
    ]
    print(resource_similarity.loc[selected, selected])
else:
    print(resource_similarity)

# -----------------------------
# 4. Handover / working-together / subcontracting networks
# -----------------------------
handover_net = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
    log,
    resource_key="org:resource"
)

working_together_net = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(
    log,
    resource_key="org:resource"
)

subcontracting_net = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(
    log,
    resource_key="org:resource"
)

def preview_network(net, n=20):
    if isinstance(net, dict):
        return list(net.items())[:n]
    return list(net)[:n]

print("Handover sample:", preview_network(handover_net))
print("Working-together sample:", preview_network(working_together_net))
print("Subcontracting sample:", preview_network(subcontracting_net))

# -----------------------------
# 5. Join role output with complexity and substitution indicators
# -----------------------------
case_stats = (
    df_fmt
    .groupby("case:concept:name")
    .agg(
        case_complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
        max_escalation_level=("escalation_level", "max"),
        temp_case_flag=("temp_case_flag", "max"),
        product_line=("product_line", "first"),
        customer_segment=("customer_segment", "first")
    )
    .reset_index()
)

resource_case_map = (
    df_fmt[["case:concept:name", "org:resource"]]
    .drop_duplicates()
    .merge(case_stats, on="case:concept:name", how="left")
)

resource_case_stats = (
    resource_case_map
    .groupby("org:resource")
    .agg(
        cases_touched=("case:concept:name", "nunique"),
        median_case_complexity=("case_complexity", "median"),
        high_critical_case_share=("case_complexity", lambda s: (s >= 0.70).mean()),
        temp_substitution_case_share=("temp_case_flag", "mean"),
        max_escalation_seen=("max_escalation_level", "max")
    )
    .reset_index()
)

resource_event_freq = (
    pd.crosstab(df_fmt["org:resource"], df_fmt["concept:name"], normalize="index")
    .add_prefix("freq_")
    .reset_index()
)

# Optional HR metadata table: formal title, contract, certifications, team, etc.
# role_enriched should be interpreted with this metadata, not instead of it.
resource_meta = pd.DataFrame([
    # Example rows only; populate from the org chart / HR system.
    {"org:resource": "R08_Hana", "formal_title": "Supervisor", "certifications": "approval"},
    {"org:resource": "R06_Fatima", "formal_title": "Commercial Specialist", "certifications": "pricing"},
    {"org:resource": "R07_Giorgio", "formal_title": "Senior Service Agent", "certifications": "entitlement,pricing"},
    {"org:resource": "R05_Erik", "formal_title": "Security Specialist", "certifications": "security"},
])

role_enriched = (
    role_tbl
    .merge(resource_case_stats, on="org:resource", how="left")
    .merge(resource_event_freq, on="org:resource", how="left")
    .merge(resource_meta, on="org:resource", how="left")
)

# -----------------------------
# 6. Guard against frequency-only over-interpretation
# -----------------------------
# A. Look at rare/high-risk resources, not just high-volume ones.
print(
    role_enriched
    .sort_values(
        ["high_critical_case_share", "median_case_complexity"],
        ascending=False
    )[
        [
            "discovered_role",
            "org:resource",
            "cases_touched",
            "median_case_complexity",
            "high_critical_case_share",
            "temp_substitution_case_share",
            "formal_title",
            "certifications"
        ]
    ]
    .head(20)
)

# B. Explicitly flag approval-like activity by non-supervisors.
approval_cols = [
    c for c in resource_event_freq.columns
    if "A9" in c or "Approval" in c or "Supervisor" in c
]

if approval_cols:
    approval_review = role_enriched[
        role_enriched[approval_cols].fillna(0).sum(axis=1) > 0
    ][
        [
            "org:resource",
            "discovered_role",
            "formal_title",
            "certifications",
            "median_case_complexity",
            "high_critical_case_share"
        ]
    ]
    print("Resources touching approval-like activities:", approval_review)

# C. Treat substitutions as temporal evidence, not competence labels.
print(
    role_enriched[role_enriched["temp_substitution_case_share"] > 0][
        [
            "org:resource",
            "discovered_role",
            "temp_substitution_case_share",
            "median_case_complexity",
            "high_critical_case_share"
        ]
    ]
)

# Interpretation rule:
# - PM4Py role discovery and similarity are descriptive signals.
# - Final role labels must combine frequency + sequence + complexity + authority +
#   substitution windows + rework/outcomes + HR metadata.
# - Do not infer formal authority from A9 frequency alone.
# - Do not infer competence from temporary substitution alone.
# - Do not infer low importance from low event count.
```

# 6. Operational interpretation

Management should use the mined structure as a **decision-support map**, not as an org chart by itself.

- **Staffing:**  
  Preserve a stable front-line core for A1/A2/A4/A12, but do not cut experienced agents just because bots and temps perform many of the same activities. Low-complexity load can be absorbed by Nora, Kai, and Lina, but medium/high cases still need Amira, Ben, Giorgio, and the specialists. Technical work should be tiered: Chen should not be treated as interchangeable with Dina simply because their activity profiles are similar.

- **Training:**  
  Temps need targeted training on A2 note quality and escalation triggers, because incomplete entitlement notes caused reopens. Giorgio should receive controlled commercial cross-training, with pricing checklists and post-substitution review. Mateo needs training on escalation boundaries, especially when he filters security referrals or prepares approvals. Quality analysts should be used as a feedback loop into KB updates and rework prevention.

- **Risk control:**  
  Keep final A9 authority with Hana. Treat Mateo’s A9 work as preliminary unless policy says otherwise. Protect Erik’s A6 capacity for high-risk cases; routing changes should not reduce critical reviews without risk sign-off. Monitor Fatima absence scenarios because pricing duration and quality degrade when Giorgio shadows. Add review controls when temps close simple requests with later reopens.

- **Resilience:**  
  The organization has useful informal resilience: Giorgio can partially cover commercial work, Mateo can route cross-functional incidents, and quality analysts capture knowledge. The goal should be to formalize these capabilities through delegation rules, checklists, and escalation thresholds rather than eliminate them.

- **Caution 1 — mined roles are descriptive, not normative:**  
  A role-discovery algorithm does not know who is allowed to approve, reject, or filter critical work. Hana’s role is authority-heavy even if her event count is modest. Erik’s role is risk-heavy even if A6 is rare.

- **Caution 2 — substitutions are not proof of permanent competence:**  
  Giorgio covering Fatima and Mateo preparing approvals show adaptability under absence, but slower pricing reviews and Hana’s rechecks show limits. Temporary work should be logged as a resilience signal and a training signal, not as proof of equivalence.

- **Caution 3 — similarity is not authority or risk equivalence:**  
  Chen and Dina, Hana and Mateo, and Fatima and Giorgio have overlapping activity signatures but different complexity levels, decision rights, and failure costs. Staffing changes based only on similarity would erode control.

- **Caution 4 — event logs omit important context:**  
  Case notes, informal approvals, customer severity, and organizational policy may not be fully represented in the dataframe. Any staffing decision should be validated with process owners, HR metadata, and outcome quality.
