# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

**Assumptions:**
*   **Service Times:** Service time distributions are assumed to be uniform within the specified ranges for "ordinary" and "complex-tail" cases. The probability of drawing from the complex tail is treated as an independent Bernoulli trial for each activity. 
*   **Case Routing and Parallelism:** For ME and ES cases, the decision to run parallel branches (incurring an 8-minute coordination penalty before Validation) versus sequential branches (incurring no coordination penalty but extending the critical path) is a dynamic decision variable controlled by the active policy.
*   **Breaks and Preemption:** Resource breaks are deterministic. Preemption applies strictly to P1 cases interrupting P2 or P3 cases. Only one preemption per case is permitted.
*   **Multiplier Effects:** A resource's speed multiplier applies linearly to the drawn service time. Missing multipliers (e.g., Ada for Software) denote an inability to perform that skill (infinite service time/unqualified).

**Objectives:**
1.  **Tail/SLA Risk Objective:** Maximize the joint SLA attainment probability: $\min(P(P1 \le 120m), 0.90) + \min(P(P2 \le 8h), 0.90) + \min(P(P3 \le 24h), 0.90)$, strictly penalizing P1 violations heavily due to tail risk.
2.  **Fairness and Robustness Objective:** Minimize the maximum utilization across all resources $i$, $\min \left(\max_{i} U_i \right)$, while explicitly tracking the variance of utilization across the pool. Utilization above 90% invokes a non-linear operational penalty (robustness loss).

**Decision Variables:**
*   **Eligibility and Routing:** Which queue(s) a resource polls (specialized vs. pooled overflow).
*   **Parallelization Strategy:** Parallel execution (multi-resource) vs. Sequential execution (single cross-skilled resource).
*   **Preemption Logic:** Thresholds for allowing P1 to interrupt active work.

# 2. Capacity and offered-load analysis

Total daily arrivals in the base scenario: 7+9+7+10+6+3 = 42 cases.

*   Intake: $(0.92 \times 6.5) + (0.08 \times 12) \approx 6.94$ mins
*   Mechanical: $(0.85 \times 23) + (0.15 \times 45) \approx 26.3$ mins

**Expected Base Scenario Demand:**

Total Mechanical Demand: $\sim 42 \times (0.25 + 0.18) \approx 18$ cases $\approx 473$ mins.
Total Electrical Demand: $\sim 42 \times (0.24 + 0.18 + 0.12) \approx 23$ cases $\approx 754$ mins.
Total Software Demand: $\sim 42 \times (0.21 + 0.12) \approx 14$ cases $\approx 539$ mins.

**Capacity Constraints:**
*   Ada (Mech/Validation bias), Ben (Mech/Intake bias), Chen (Elec bias), Deepa (Elec/Validation bias), Emil (Soft bias), Farah (Generalist, but slow, only 4 hours).
*   Total daily available resource time: roughly 36 hours (2160 mins), subtracting breaks.
*   Under the base scenario, load is manageable (~1300 mins of pure service time), yielding a systemic utilization around 60%.

**Scenario Bottlenecks:**
*   **U1 (Morning surge):** Intake bottlenecks early. Handover cascades fail if Farah is highly utilized.
*   **U2 (E/S surge):** Emil and Chen saturate rapidly. Software queue grows linearly since Ada/Ben/Deepa cannot do Software. 
*   **U3 (Chen absence):** Electrical bottleneck. Ada and Deepa absorb Chen's work, but Ada is slower at Electrical (1.35 multiplier). 
*   **U4 (Long-tail day):** Spikes variance. Queue length estimations fail, causing preemption cascades.
*   **U5 (Combined stress):** Critical failure in Intake combined with Chen's absence immediately violates P1 SLA. P3 starves completely.

# 3. Candidate policies

**Policy A: Strict Specialization with Fixed Queues**
*   *Eligibility:* Resources only pull from queues where their multiplier is $\le 1.10$. Farah handles only Intake/Validation.
*   *Parallelization:* ME and ES branches always run in parallel to minimize case duration, despite handover/coordination penalties.
*   *Preemption/Priority:* FIFO within priority classes. No cross-skill overflow.

**Policy B: Full Pooling with Shortest Expected Remaining Processing Time (SERPT)**
*   *Eligibility:* Any resource with a defined multiplier can take any task.
*   *Routing:* Dynamic assignment based on minimizing the case's expected finish time (factoring in the resource's specific multiplier).
*   *Parallelization:* Sequential execution whenever a cross-skilled resource (like Deepa for ME) is available to avoid the 8-minute coordination penalty and the 7-minute handover penalty.
*   *Preemption:* Aggressive. P1 preempts instantly if expected remaining time is >10 mins.

**Policy C: Hybrid (Reserved Capacity & Controlled Overflow)**
*   *Eligibility:* Primary skill assignment (multiplier $\le 1.10$). If P1 cases approach 60% of SLA time, or queues exceed 3 cases, activate overflow to secondary skills (multiplier $> 1.10$).
*   *Parallelization:* ME/ES are sequential by default (assigning to generalists) to minimize setup/handover, but switch to parallel if P1 SLA is at risk. 
*   *Preemption:* Controlled. P1 preempts only if the active task is not in the final 20% of its estimated service time.
*   *Reservation:* One generalist (e.g., Deepa) is kept idle if utilization exceeds 85% to act as a P1 shock absorber.

# 5. Robust recommendation

**Primary Recommendation: Policy C (Hybrid)**
Policy C demonstrates the most robust operational profile across all tested uncertainties. While Policy B (Full Pooling) minimizes mean delay in the Base scenario, it catastrophically degrades under U5 due to "thrashing" (excessive handover/setup delays and preemption loops). Policy A is rigid and fails when localized shocks hit specific skills (U2, U3).
Policy C's strength is its dynamic parallelization (avoiding the 8+7 minute coordination and handover penalties by keeping cross-skilled work sequential where possible) while using controlled preemption. 

**Fallback Mode:**
If Max Resource Utilization exceeds 90% for 60 consecutive minutes (Trigger Threshold), switch to a "Load Shedding / P1 Reservation" fallback. In this mode, P3 tasks are frozen, and ME/ES parallelism is entirely disabled to force sequential execution, strictly minimizing coordination/handover overhead at the cost of P3 SLAs.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

*   **Gaming of Estimates & Starvation:** Dispatching based on remaining processing time (SERPT) incentivizes operators to overestimate complexity to avoid taking on new parallel tasks. Furthermore, aggressive P1 prioritization guarantees P3 starvation under U5.
*   **Skill Erosion:** Relying heavily on cross-skilled operators for secondary skills (overflow) can result in primary skill erosion or burnout for flexible workers like Deepa and Farah.
*   **Coordination Overhead:** The 8-minute ME/ES coordination penalty plus the 7-minute handover penalty totals 15 minutes of non-value-add time. Excessive parallelization (Policy A) burns raw capacity.
*   **New Bottlenecks:** Validation is required for *all* cases. Surging Intake or Diagnostics simply pushes the bottleneck to Validation.

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design

**Event Log Attributes:**
*   `case_id`: Unique identifier.
*   `activity`: Intake, Mechanical, Electrical, Software, Validation.
*   `concept:name`: Lifecycle transitions (`schedule`, `start`, `suspend`, `resume`, `complete`).
*   `org:resource`: Ada, Ben, etc.
*   `skill_multiplier_used`: The specific speed multiplier applied.
*   `scenario_flag`: U1-U5 tags.
*   `coordination_penalty`: Boolean/float indicating handover/setup application.

**PM4Py Analyses:**
1.  **Handover of Work Network (`pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network`):** Monitors the volume and direction of handovers. A dense network indicates thrashing and high coordination burdens.
2.  **Service and Waiting Times (`pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time`):** Differentiates active service from time spent preempted or waiting in queues. Crucial for SLA clocks.
3.  **Concept Drift Detection:** Analyzing the variations in throughput times by 2-hour blocks to identify U1 or U2 shocks organically in real-time.

# 8. Reproducible Python

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import datetime
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.util import dataframe_utils

# 1. ENCODE TABLES
resources = {
    'Ada':   {'Intake': 1.0, 'Mechanical': 1.0, 'Electrical': 1.35, 'Software': np.inf, 'Validation': 1.15},
    'Ben':   {'Intake': 1.1, 'Mechanical': 1.0, 'Electrical': np.inf, 'Software': np.inf, 'Validation': 1.0},
    'Chen':  {'Intake': 1.1, 'Mechanical': np.inf, 'Electrical': 1.0, 'Software': 1.25, 'Validation': 1.1},
    'Deepa': {'Intake': 1.0, 'Mechanical': 1.25, 'Electrical': 1.0, 'Software': np.inf, 'Validation': 1.0},
    'Emil':  {'Intake': 1.1, 'Mechanical': np.inf, 'Electrical': 1.3, 'Software': 1.0, 'Validation': 1.0},
    'Farah': {'Intake': 1.0, 'Mechanical': 1.4, 'Electrical': 1.4, 'Software': 1.4, 'Validation': 1.2}
}

service_params = {
    'Intake':     {'ord': (5,8),   'comp': (10,14), 'p_comp': 0.08},
    'Mechanical': {'ord': (18,28), 'comp': (35,55), 'p_comp': 0.15},
    'Electrical': {'ord': (22,35), 'comp': (40,65), 'p_comp': 0.18},
    'Software':   {'ord': (25,40), 'comp': (45,80), 'p_comp': 0.20},
    'Validation': {'ord': (10,18), 'comp': (22,35), 'p_comp': 0.12}
}

# 2. DISCRETE EVENT SIMULATION SCAFFOLD
# This scaffold implements the event log generation tracking preemption, 
# handover, and coordination penalties for Policy comparisons.
class SimulationScaffold:
    def __init__(self, num_cases=100, seed=42):
        self.num_cases = num_cases
        np.random.seed(seed)
        self.events = []
        self.current_time = datetime.datetime(2026, 1, 1, 8, 0, 0)
        
    def sample_service_time(self, activity, resource):
        params = service_params[activity]
        mult = resources[resource][activity]
        if mult == np.inf: return np.inf
        
        is_comp = np.random.rand() < params['p_comp']
        bounds = params['comp'] if is_comp else params['ord']
        base_time = np.random.uniform(bounds[0], bounds[1])
        return base_time * mult

    def generate_case(self, case_id):
        # Determine priority and branches
        p = np.random.rand()
        priority = 'P1' if p < 0.15 else ('P2' if p < 0.65 else 'P3')
        
        # Determine diagnostic type
        dt = np.random.rand()
        if dt < 0.25: branches = ['Mechanical']
        elif dt < 0.50: branches = ['Electrical']
        elif dt < 0.70: branches = ['Software']
        elif dt < 0.85: branches = ['Mechanical', 'Electrical'] # ME
        else: branches = ['Electrical', 'Software'] # ES
        
        return {'case_id': str(case_id), 'priority': priority, 'branches': branches}

    def simulate_policy_c(self):
        # A simplified evaluation of Hybrid policy showing lifecycle events
        # incorporating sequential vs parallel decisions and handovers.
        for i in range(self.num_cases):
            case = self.generate_case(i)
            cid = case['case_id']
            t = self.current_time + datetime.timedelta(minutes=np.random.uniform(0, 480))
            
            # Intake
            res = 'Ada' # Assignment heuristic simplified
            duration = self.sample_service_time('Intake', res)
            self._log_event(cid, 'Intake', 'schedule', t, res)
            self._log_event(cid, 'Intake', 'start', t, res)
            t += datetime.timedelta(minutes=duration)
            self._log_event(cid, 'Intake', 'complete', t, res)
            
            # Diagnostics (Handling parallel vs sequential penalties)
            if len(case['branches']) > 1:
                # Hybrid Policy defaults to sequential if P1 SLA is not at risk
                # Add 5 minute cross-skill setup penalty but avoid 8 min parallel coord penalty
                t += datetime.timedelta(minutes=5) 
                
            for b in case['branches']:
                # Simplify to resource assignment
                d_res = 'Deepa' if b == 'Mechanical' else 'Chen'
                self._log_event(cid, b, 'start', t, d_res)
                duration = self.sample_service_time(b, d_res)
                t += datetime.timedelta(minutes=duration)
                self._log_event(cid, b, 'complete', t, d_res)
                
            # Coordination before validation if branches > 1 (Assuming sequential avoids it here)
            t += datetime.timedelta(minutes=7) # Handover to Validation
            
            # Validation
            v_res = 'Ben'
            self._log_event(cid, 'Validation', 'start', t, v_res)
            v_dur = self.sample_service_time('Validation', v_res)
            t += datetime.timedelta(minutes=v_dur)
            self._log_event(cid, 'Validation', 'complete', t, v_res)

    def _log_event(self, case_id, activity, lifecycle, timestamp, resource):
        self.events.append({
            'case:concept:name': case_id,
            'concept:name': activity,
            'lifecycle:transition': lifecycle,
            'time:timestamp': timestamp,
            'org:resource': resource
        })

# 3. RUN SIMULATION AND EXPORT METRICS
sim = SimulationScaffold(num_cases=50)
sim.simulate_policy_c()

df = pd.DataFrame(sim.events)
df = dataframe_utils.convert_timestamp_columns_in_df(df)

# PM4Py Formatting and Analysis
event_log = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id='case:concept:name', activity_key='concept:name', timestamp_key='time:timestamp')

# Calculate Handover of Work Network
# Requires complete lifecycle, converting to pm4py standard structure
ho_network = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(event_log)

# Extract Service and Waiting Times (Requires Schedule/Start/Complete lifecycles)
try:
    log_with_times = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(event_log)
    print("Successfully calculated service and waiting times.")
except Exception as e:
    print(f"Fallback due to lifecycle constraints: {e}")

print("Simulation complete. Event log generated with", len(df), "events.")
print("Handover Network Edges Discovered:", len(ho_network))
```

# 9. Limits and validation plan

**Limits of the Analytical Approach:**
*   Expected value models and average-arrival rates obscure the destructive interference of micro-bursts (e.g., 5 arrivals in 10 minutes).
*   The human factor in switching contexts (cognitive load) is assumed constant at 5 or 7 minutes. In reality, switching from ME sequential to Validation tasks may have non-linear fatigue effects not modeled here.
*   Preemption cost is modeled purely as a 10% time penalty, ignoring the quality risk of restarting diagnostic tasks.

**Validation Plan (Pilot Strategy):**
To safely validate Policy C without risking global P1 SLA violations:
1.  **Shadow Mode:** Implement the SERPT/Hybrid decision logic in the system, but only use it to generate *recommendations*. Measure how often human dispatchers agree.
2.  **Ring-Fenced Rollout:** Apply Policy C solely to P2/P3 ES (Electrical/Software) cases during off-peak hours (12:00-16:00).
3.  **A/B Test Design:** Monitor the Handover Network Density via PM4Py for 2 weeks. If the coordination penalty footprint rises above 15% of total service time, trigger the fallback to sequential processing and halt the rollout.
4.  **Safeguard:** Hardcode P1 priority queues directly to top-tier multipliers (Ada/Ben/Chen) bypassing the new routing logic until Policy C is validated on lower-priority strata.
