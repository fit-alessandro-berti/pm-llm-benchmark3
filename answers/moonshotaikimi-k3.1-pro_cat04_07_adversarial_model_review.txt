# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

The central mistake would be to treat this as a single-model leaderboard. At least four uses require different evidence:

| Use case | Appropriate emphasis | Fitness/precision trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Exploration and hypothesis generation** | Coverage of observed behavior, robustness to incomplete logging, speed, and an intelligible activity map | Low precision can be acceptable if the model is explicitly descriptive and over-acceptance is visible. High fitness still does not make a trace policy-compliant. |
| **Descriptive conformance and audit investigation** | Reliable deviation diagnostics, traceable data preprocessing, and separation of observed behavior from normative policy | Neither very low precision nor very low holdout fitness is decisive. Investigators need to know whether a deviation reflects missing events, an unapproved combination, or a model omission. |
| **Preventive compliance enforcement** | Exact mandatory behavior, false acceptance of forbidden traces, soundness, and explicit treatment of unknown data | A hard semantic failure is a veto. Model S’s acceptance of skipped checks cannot be offset by speed or fitness. A closed whitelist such as Model C is acceptable only if the business formally adopts that closed-world policy and provides a workable approval route for new variants. |
| **Simulation** | Calibrated arrival, duration, branching, resource, rework, and data models | The reported structural metrics establish almost none of this. Model S would simulate impossible executions; Model C would underrepresent legal combinations. |
| **Stakeholder communication and change impact analysis** | Correct performance on comprehension and modification tasks, modular ownership, and stable terminology | A small picture is not necessarily a faithful explanation. Conversely, enumerating variants is not a maintainable communication strategy. |

I therefore distinguish a **descriptive model of what was logged**, a **normative model of what is permitted**, and an **observation/missingness model of what may have occurred without being logged**. Model S largely sacrifices the second to accommodate the third. Model C largely treats frequent observations as policy.

For trace reasoning below, I assume standard PM4Py process-tree semantics:

- `->` is sequence;
- `X` is exclusive choice;
- `+` is parallel execution of its children;
- `tau` emits no visible event.

Consequently, each `X(activity, tau)` in Model S independently executes that activity at most once or skips it, and the four parallel branches can be interleaved. Model C chooses exactly one complete sequence branch. “Accepts” means that a complete model execution produces exactly the probe’s visible events and reaches completion; it does **not** mean that an alignment can repair the trace with deviations.

Trace shorthand is:

- `R` Register Dossier
- `C` Credit Check
- `T` Technical Review
- `Su` Supplier Review
- `Ex` Expedite Review
- `D` Correct Dossier
- `M` Manager Approval
- `A` Auto Approval
- `Z` Release Order

Finally, absence of the external/internal and affected-check attributes makes parts of P3 and P6 **unidentifiable from the discovery log alone**. I will not infer externality from the presence of Supplier Review or infer which checks were affected from which checks happen to repeat.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

**Strongest case for Model S.** Model S is compact, fast, apparently stable across the reported split, and easy to approach. It preserves several useful properties: Register Dossier is first, Release Order is last, exactly one approval occurs, checks can occur in either order, and each modeled review occurs at most once. Its parallel composition also avoids Model C’s arbitrary Cartesian-product omissions. For exploratory work with noisy event logs, a broad model can be preferable to a brittle variant catalogue, particularly when the purpose is to inventory activities and generate questions rather than issue compliance decisions. Its rejection of repeated checks also means it does not accept B8.

That is a real use case, not merely an excuse for low precision. The problem is that the discovery team has labelled observational tolerance as process semantics.

**Concrete undesirable or policy-ambiguous admissions include:**

1. **Both mandatory checks skipped:**  
   `R M Z` — B3, explicitly forbidden.

2. **Technical Review omitted:**  
   `R C M Z` — Credit Check alone is treated as sufficient for approval.

3. **Credit Check omitted, with automatic approval:**  
   `R T A Z` — a particularly consequential false acceptance if used for automated control.

4. **Expedite Review before either mandatory check:**  
   `R Ex C T M Z` — B4, explicitly forbidden.

5. **Expedite Review after only one mandatory check:**  
   `R C Ex T M Z` — violates the requirement that Expedite Review follow **both** checks.

6. **Only nonmandatory reviews performed:**  
   `R Su Ex A Z` — skips both checks and also permits Expedite Review without its prerequisites.

7. **Supplier Review before the checks:**  
   `R Su C T M Z` — S accepts this. The current P1–P7 do not clearly settle Supplier Review’s earliest position, so this is policy-ambiguous rather than automatically forbidden. It nevertheless demonstrates that S supplies no meaningful review-phase ordering.

8. **External order without Supplier Review:**  
   `R C T M Z`, with an independent case attribute `externally_sourced=true` — visibly identical to a valid internal case, but allegedly missing a required review. S accepts both and cannot distinguish them.

9. **Supplier Review on an internal order:**  
   `R C T Su M Z`, with `externally_sourced=false` — policy-ambiguous if “only for externally sourced orders” is intended to forbid, rather than merely not require, internal Supplier Review.

These are not all consequences of deliberate noise tolerance. It is useful to separate the design choices:

| Design feature | Possibly intentional and useful | Semantic consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Parallel check branches | Avoids choosing between the two legal check orders | Reasonable in isolation |
| Optional Supplier Review | Avoids asserting a conditional obligation without the external attribute | Reasonable as a structural approximation, but insufficient for policy decisions |
| Broad review ordering | Accommodates incompletely observed variants | Also allows Supplier Review in unresolved positions |
| `tau` on Credit Check and Technical Review | May have been intended to tolerate missing events | Converts a mandatory execution obligation into genuinely optional behavior |
| Unconstrained Expedite Review branch | May have been intended to support flexible ordering | Fails its core prerequisite; this is not necessary for missing-event tolerance |
| No Correct Dossier or recheck structure | Keeps the model small | Omits an entire agreed policy mechanism rather than merely abstracting it |

Most importantly, a silent path meaning “Credit Check did not occur in this execution” is not an observation model for “Credit Check occurred but its event was not logged.” Robustness to missing events should be represented through explicit data-quality states, alignment diagnostics, or probabilistic observation assumptions. It should not silently turn every mandatory activity into an optional one.

F7 is therefore only partly persuasive: **low precision can be acceptable for exploration, but these particular semantic omissions must be prominently displayed, and the model must not be reused as an enforcement artifact merely because it is available in PM4Py.**

# 7. Third Alternative

I recommend a **modular procedural backbone plus explicit data-dependent policy checks and an uncertainty state**. This is preferable to forcing all uncertainty into `tau` branches or enumerating complete traces.

Let the visible structural language be:

\[
R\;(CT\mid TC)\;
\bigl(D(C\mid T\mid CT\mid TC)\bigr)^*
\;(\epsilon\mid Su\mid Ex\mid SuEx\mid ExSu)\;
(M\mid A)\;Z .
\]

This has the following semantics:

- Both initial mandatory checks occur exactly once, in either order.
- Zero or more correction cycles occur in a defined pre-review correction window.
- Each cycle contains Correct Dossier followed by Credit-only, Technical-only, or both rechecks in either order.
- Supplier Review and Expedite Review each occur at most once. Either can be omitted; if both occur, either order is allowed.
- Expedite Review is after the initial checks and before approval.
- Exactly one approval occurs.
- A repeated mandatory check cannot occur outside a correction cycle.
- The structural model does **not** decide which recheck mode was justified or whether Supplier Review was externally required. Those are separate policy checks.

The correction window before Supplier/Expedite Review, the at-most-once treatment of those reviews, and the nonempty recheck mode are **explicit design assumptions requiring stakeholder confirmation**, not silent deductions from the log.

The following self-contained PM4Py code constructs that state-machine backbone. It uses named states and transitions rather than one branch per complete variant.

```python
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import PetriNet, Marking
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.utils import petri_utils

R  = "Register Dossier"
C  = "Credit Check"
T  = "Technical Review"
Su = "Supplier Review"
Ex = "Expedite Review"
D  = "Correct Dossier"
M  = "Manager Approval"
A  = "Auto Approval"
Z  = "Release Order"

net = PetriNet("model_third")
P = {}

def add_place(name):
    P[name] = PetriNet.Place(name)
    net.places.add(P[name])

for name in (
    "start", "need_both", "need_T", "need_C", "checks_ok",
    "after_D", "after_recheck_C", "after_recheck_T",
    "review_none", "review_Su", "review_Ex", "review_SuEx",
    "approval_point", "approved", "end"
):
    add_place(name)

def add_transition(name, label, source, target):
    # label=None denotes a silent routing transition.
    t = PetriNet.Transition(name, label)
    net.transitions.add(t)
    petri_utils.add_arc_from_to(P[source], t, net)
    petri_utils.add_arc_from_to(t, P[target], net)
    return t

# Register and the two mandatory initial checks, in either order.
add_transition("register", R, "start", "need_both")
add_transition("initial_C_first", C, "need_both", "need_T")
add_transition("initial_T_after_C", T, "need_T", "checks_ok")
add_transition("initial_T_first", T, "need_both", "need_C")
add_transition("initial_C_after_T", C, "need_C", "checks_ok")

# Either leave the correction region or perform another correction cycle.
add_transition("finish_corrections", None, "checks_ok", "review_none")
add_transition("correct_dossier", D, "checks_ok", "after_D")

# After D: C only, T only, CT, or TC. No zero-recheck cycle is provided.
add_transition("recheck_C_start", C, "after_D", "after_recheck_C")
add_transition("finish_C_only", None,
               "after_recheck_C", "checks_ok")
add_transition("recheck_T_after_C", T,
               "after_recheck_C", "checks_ok")

add_transition("recheck_T_start", T, "after_D", "after_recheck_T")
add_transition("finish_T_only", None,
               "after_recheck_T", "checks_ok")
add_transition("recheck_C_after_T", C,
               "after_recheck_T", "checks_ok")

# Optional Supplier/Expedite region: none, either alone, or both in either order.
add_transition("supplier_first", Su, "review_none", "review_Su")
add_transition("expedite_first", Ex, "review_none", "review_Ex")
add_transition("supplier_after_expedite", Su, "review_Ex", "review_SuEx")
add_transition("expedite_after_supplier", Ex, "review_Su", "review_SuEx")

add_transition("exit_without_reviews", None,
               "review_none", "approval_point")
add_transition("exit_after_supplier", None,
               "review_Su", "approval_point")
add_transition("exit_after_expedite", None,
               "review_Ex", "approval_point")
add_transition("exit_after_both_reviews", None,
               "review_SuEx", "approval_point")

# Exactly one approval, then release.
add_transition("manager_approval", M, "approval_point", "approved")
add_transition("auto_approval", A, "approval_point", "approved")
add_transition("release_order", Z, "approved", "end")

model_third = net
model_third_initial = Marking({P["start"]: 1})
model_third_final = Marking({P["end"]: 1})

# model_third, model_third_initial, model_third_final
```

A post-structural decision function should distinguish `PASS`, `FAIL_OR_INVESTIGATE`, and `PROVISIONAL_REVIEW`. The following skeleton shows the required data logic. `structural_ok` must come from exact replay/reachability, not from a fitness threshold. `correction_cycles` is produced by an independently tested parser and contains one entry per `D`, with the repeated checks observed in that cycle and the independently sourced affected-check metadata.

```python
def assess_case(trace, case_attributes, correction_cycles, structural_ok):
    if not structural_ok:
        # Could be a real deviation or an incomplete log; it is never a pass.
        return "FAIL_OR_INVESTIGATE"

    violations = []
    unknowns = []

    external = case_attributes.get("externally_sourced")
    supplier_count = sum(activity == Su for activity in trace)

    if external is None:
        unknowns.append("P3 externality is not identifiable")
    elif external is True and supplier_count != 1:
        violations.append(
            "external order lacks exactly one visible Supplier Review; "
            "investigate event completeness as well as execution"
        )
    elif external is False and supplier_count:
        unknowns.append(
            "policy must clarify whether Supplier Review is forbidden internally"
        )

    for cycle in correction_cycles:
        affected = cycle.get("affected")
        repeated = frozenset(cycle.get("repeated", ()))

        if affected is None:
            unknowns.append("correction justification is not identifiable")
        else:
            affected = frozenset(affected)
            if (
                affected not in THIRD_POLICY["allowed_recheck_sets"]
                or affected != repeated
            ):
                violations.append(
                    "recheck set does not equal the affected-check set"
                )

    if violations:
        return "FAIL_OR_INVESTIGATE"
    if unknowns:
        return "PROVISIONAL_REVIEW"
    return "PASS"
```

This design still has limitations:

1. **Externality is unavailable in the discovery log.** A structurally absent Supplier Review cannot be classified as valid internal behavior, external noncompliance, or an external activity with a missing event. Deployment therefore requires a reliable source attribute or an explicit manual-review route.
2. **Affected checks are unavailable.** B6 can be structurally accepted but remains provisional. If the correction affected Technical Review alone, it is valid; otherwise, it is not.
3. **Unknown metadata never becomes a silent pass.** In particular, the model does not make Credit Check or Technical Review optional to accommodate missing events.
4. **The correction window may be too restrictive.** A correction following Supplier Review or Expedite Review is not represented. Stakeholders must either approve that restriction or extend the state machine while preserving review-state information.
5. **Auto Approval eligibility is unspecified.** The backbone implements P5 only; it does not invent financial, risk, or resource rules for automatic approval.
6. **It is not a calibrated simulation model**, and it is not immune to duplicate-label, silent-transition, or alignment-metric pathologies. Its advantage is clearer semantics and modular change, not automatic metric superiority.

# 9. Validation Experiments

**1. Freeze the policy oracle before further modelling.**

Create a versioned, stakeholder-signed suite containing valid, forbidden, and unknown cases. It must be independent of any candidate model. Include:

- B1–B8 and X1–X4;
- omission of Credit Check and Technical Review separately;
- Expedite after only one check and after approval;
- zero, two, or misplaced approvals and release without approval;
- repeated C/T without `D`;
- correction cycles whose repeated set disagrees with known affected checks;
- external cases without Supplier Review;
- all pairwise and selected higher-order combinations of check order, approval type, Supplier presence, Expedite presence/order, and correction mode.

“Unknown” cases must not be relabelled negative merely to make a conservative model look precise.

**2. Reproduce and improve the case split.**

- Preserve an immutable raw-log snapshot and publish preprocessing code, activity-mapping tables, timestamp/tie-breaking rules, PM4Py version, dependencies, and alignment settings.
- Split by case, not by event. If several cases belong to one dossier or business entity, evaluate grouped splitting to prevent entity leakage.
- Publish exact train/validation/test case-ID hashes. Use a fixed primary seed and repeated sensitivity splits; also evaluate a later temporal split.
- Perform variant filtering and model tuning only on training/validation data.
- Because F4 and the existing holdout have already influenced this review, obtain a new locked confirmation holdout.
- Keep the same evaluation cases for all models and report support per stratum.

A minimal split skeleton is:

```python
import random
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import EventLog

PRIMARY_SEED = 20250308  # An evaluation setting, not a reported result.

def case_id(case):
    return case.attributes["concept:name"]

def split_cases(log, seed=PRIMARY_SEED, train_fraction=0.8):
    ids = sorted({case_id(case) for case in log})
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    rng.shuffle(ids)

    cut = int(train_fraction * len(ids))
    train_ids = set(ids[:cut])
    test_ids = set(ids[cut:])

    train = EventLog([c for c in log if case_id(c) in train_ids])
    test = EventLog([c for c in log if case_id(c) in test_ids])

    manifest = {
        "seed": seed,
        "train_case_ids": sorted(train_ids),
        "test_case_ids": sorted(test_ids),
        # Add raw-data hash, preprocessing version, grouping decisions, etc.
    }
    return train, test, manifest
```

**3. Use attribute-stratified holdouts.**

Source externality and affected-check metadata independently; do not infer them from review events. Report results separately for:

- internal, external, and unknown externality;
- Supplier Review present, genuinely absent, and potentially unlogged;
- `C,T` versus `T,C`;
- Manager versus Auto Approval;
- no Expedite, Expedite-only, `Su,Ex`, and `Ex,Su`;
- no correction, Credit-only, Technical-only, both, multiple cycles, and unknown affected checks;
- event-completeness strata and case-length bands.

Small strata should be shown with their support rather than hidden inside an aggregate.

**4. Run unseen-combination experiments.**

Use leave-one-combination-out tests such as:

- train without Auto × Supplier and test it;
- train without technical-first × Expedite;
- train without `Ex,Su` while retaining `Su,Ex`;
- train without correction × Auto Approval;
- test selective and multiple correction cycles separately.

Generate expected labels from the signed policy, not from `model_third`, to avoid a self-confirming evaluation.

**5. Perturb missing and duplicate events.**

Starting from policy-labelled traces, inject controlled perturbations with published seeds and rates:

- remove C, T, Supplier Review, Correct Dossier, or approval events separately;
- simulate both “activity occurred but was unlogged” and “activity truly did not occur”;
- vary missingness by externality and correction status, not only uniformly;
- inject exact duplicate events, duplicated C/T without `D`, legitimate rechecks after `D`, duplicate Supplier/Expedite events, and duplicate approvals;
- test whether preprocessing silently deduplicates events and thereby hides P7.

The evaluation must report both alignment behavior and the final governance status. A missing mandatory event may produce a data-quality investigation, but must not become a compliant execution merely because the model can skip it.

**6. Perform alignment-cost sensitivity analysis.**

Evaluate at least the documented default and several preregistered cost schemes, varying visible log/model move costs and, separately, policy-weighted diagnostic costs for missing mandatory checks. Record invisible-move treatment and inspect whether optimal alignments change. Do not mix custom-cost and default-cost numbers in one comparison table. Policy-weighted alignment remains a diagnostic; it does not replace exact acceptance tests.

**7. Check soundness and exact reachability.**

For every converted net:

- run soundness diagnostics with the intended initial and final markings;
- check for dead transitions, deadlock, improper completion, and tokens left at completion;
- check reachability of every business activity and every intended correction mode;
- enumerate the third model’s correction-free language and bounded correction depths, such as zero, one, and two cycles, against an independent regular-language reference;
- compute probe acceptance by state-space search over `(marking, next_trace_event)`, including silent-transition closure. Alignment fitness is not an acceptance oracle.

`pm4py.play_out` can provide additional sanity samples, but generated examples do not prove reachability properties or policy correctness.

**8. Report metrics separately and retain diagnostics.**

**9. Measure stakeholder comprehension with tasks.**

Use a counterbalanced protocol across compliance, operations, IT, and process-owner participants. Give comparable training time and ask participants to:

- classify B3, B4, B5, and B6;
- identify whether an unseen combination is permitted;
- locate where Credit-only recheck logic should change;
- add Auto Approval to a Supplier/Expedite combination;
- explain what happens when externality or affected-check metadata is unknown.

Report participant count, role mix, task accuracy, completion time, modification errors, confidence, and a predefined 1–5 rubric with dispersion. A preference score alone is insufficient.

**10. Apply hard rejection rules before comparing aggregate quality.**

For enforcement, reject a candidate regardless of excellent aggregate metrics if it:

- is unsound or cannot reliably reach proper completion;
- exactly accepts an approved high-severity negative, such as skipped mandatory checks or early Expedite Review, without flagging it;
- converts missing externality or affected-check metadata into `PASS`;
- fails reproducibility requirements;
- rejects core valid behavior while presenting itself as a general policy model, unless an explicitly approved whitelist/manual-review process applies;
- cannot meet operational latency or maintainability requirements after controlled testing.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

1. **Do not approve either original for preventive compliance enforcement.** Model S has direct high-severity false acceptances. Model C is an incomplete, manually enumerated policy sample whose holdout behavior and maintenance profile are inconsistent with the agreed composition rules.
2. **Model S may remain available for read-only exploration**, with a persistent warning that it omits mandatory controls and that its alignments are not compliance decisions. It should not be used for automated release, simulation of compliant operations, or audit sign-off. An independent mandatory-control checklist must accompany any operational use of its exploratory output.
3. **Model C should not automatically classify unseen traces as noncompliant.** Its branches are useful as a source of observed-variant regression cases. It could serve only as a temporary whitelist if the process owner formally adopts that closed-world policy and every rejection receives timely human review. Otherwise, retire it from operational modelling and archive it for reproducibility.
4. **Retire both originals from the normative-model shortlist**, rather than selecting a winner by aggregate score. This does not require deleting useful exploratory or historical artifacts.
5. **Advance `model_third` only to shadow validation at this stage.** Before detective or preventive deployment it must:
   - pass soundness, exact reachability, and independent reference-language tests;
   - resolve X3, the correction-window assumption, review cardinality, internal Supplier Review semantics, and Auto Approval eligibility;
   - obtain reliable externality and affected-check data, or demonstrate a safe and operationally viable manual-review path;
   - show zero unflagged high-severity false acceptances in the curated suite and acceptable false rejection across valid feature combinations;
   - pass missing/duplicate-event, unseen-combination, and alignment-cost sensitivity tests on a new locked holdout;
   - meet documented runtime, comprehension, modification, ownership, and monitoring requirements;
   - version the Petri net, policy dictionary, reference parser, and validation suite together.

Deployment should proceed from exploration to shadow mode, then to detective conformance with human review. Preventive enforcement should be considered only after those gates. Simulation requires a separate calibration and validation effort.

# 11. Dissenting View

**Dissenting rebuttal.** The recommendation may confuse a better policy specification with a better validated model. Model S never claimed to be an enforcement artifact; paired with a small mandatory-control checklist, it could remain the cheapest, fastest exploratory view, while the proposed state machine duplicates control logic in Petri-net routing and Python and introduces new failure modes. Model C’s holdout collapse is also not intrinsically bad: an audit organization may deliberately approve a closed set of variants and require every recombination, however plausible, to undergo change control. On that reading, high precision and rejection of B5 are appropriate until the owner signs a new policy. More importantly, `model_third` is only a design. It has no reported fitness, precision, performance, or user results, and it adds assumptions stakeholders have not accepted: corrections precede Supplier/Expedite Review, those reviews occur at most once, and every correction selects a nonempty recheck mode. Without externality and affected-check data, its provisional path may create a large manual queue while giving users false confidence. The prudent decision could therefore be to freeze both originals for enforcement, retain S for exploration and C as a temporary whitelist, and postpone selecting any replacement.

**Evidence that would change my decision.** I would give greater weight to a C-based whitelist if the policy owner formally adopted closed-world semantics, independent review showed that the disputed holdout deviations were genuinely unapproved, and C could be generated from a modular specification rather than manually maintained. I would prefer S plus an independent control service over my third model if prospective head-to-head testing showed equal or better control outcomes, lower total operating cost, and materially better user performance. Conversely, poor third-model comprehension, excessive provisional queues, unacceptable correction-window false rejections, or persistent inability to source the two missing attributes would justify redesign rather than deployment.
