3. Decision quality degrades sharply when mandatory review is removed, increasing compliance failures.
4. Timestamps reflect clock changes or batch updates, making waiting times artificial.
5. Positivity fails: no unflagged cases with risk_score >0.85.
6. Reviewers change behavior under flag visibility, e.g., rushing flagged cases, reversing service-time patterns.

## 9. Final synthesis
Directly observed: flagged cases have ~14 min arrival-to-triage-start versus 148 min for unflagged, shorter triage service, but much longer triage-to-manual start, higher manual and escalation rates, and ~991 min total cycle versus ~490 min. Plausible mechanism: flag bundles priority triage with mandatory manual review and escalation, shifting work downstream and congesting shared review and senior queues, especially for high complexity and high load arrivals. Unidentified: the causal share of priority versus review mandate, the extent of queue spillover, and the role of unmeasured underlying risk driving both flag and processing intensity. Natural direct/indirect effects are not identified due to exposure-induced mediator confounding and interference. The most useful next evidence comes from a limited pilot that separates priority from mandatory review and protects review capacity for flagged cases, with queue-aware randomization and falsification checks on negative control stages.
