# 1. Data audit and estimands

## 1.1 Formal Queueing Definitions, Units, and Operating Conventions

The system under analysis is a single-queue service center observed across 28 consecutive days ($d \in \{1, \dots, 28\}$, from 2026-04-06 to 2026-05-03). The operational boundaries and aggregated metrics are defined as follows:

*   **Operating Window & Boundary Conventions:** The facility operates under a 12-hour daily window from 07:00:00 to 19:00:00 (720 operating minutes / $T = 12.0\text{ hours}$ per day). The facility is closed overnight from 19:00:00 to 07:00:00 (12 non-operating hours). No service processing occurs during closed hours; unprocessed cases remain stored in the queue (frozen overnight carry-over backlog).
*   **Arrival Count ($A_d$, `arrivals`):** The total integer count of new cases arriving at the queue within the 12-hour open window on day $d$. The daily average arrival intensity is $\bar{\lambda}_d = A_d / 12.0\text{ cases/hour} = A_d / 720\text{ cases/minute}$.
*   **Completion Count ($C_d$, `completed`):** The total integer count of cases fully serviced and discharged during the 12-hour open window on day $d$. The daily average completion intensity (effective throughput) is $\bar{\mu}_d = C_d / 12.0\text{ cases/hour} = C_d / 720\text{ cases/minute}$.
*   **Daily Net Flow & Cumulative Backlog ($\Delta N_d$, $B_d$):** The daily net change in system inventory during operating hours is $\Delta N_d = A_d - C_d$. The cumulative net drift relative to the start of the observation period is $B_d = \sum_{i=1}^d \Delta N_i$.
*   **Time-Averaged Work-in-Process ($L_d$, `mean_wip`):** The time-weighted average number of active cases residing within system boundaries (cases in service plus cases waiting in buffer, $L = L_s + L_q$), estimated via discrete 15-minute periodic state sampling ($K = 48$ snapshots per 12-hour operating day):
    $$\bar{L}_d = \frac{1}{48} \sum_{k=1}^{48} N(t_{d,k})$$
    where $N(t_{d,k})$ is the instantaneous case count at snapshot $k$ on day $d$.
*   **Active Service Duration Quantiles ($S_{50,d}$, $S_{90,d}$, `median_service_min`, `p90_service_min`):** The empirical 50th and 90th percentiles of hands-on touch time (active processing time) measured exclusively for cases that completed service on day $d$. This explicitly excludes waiting/queuing duration ($W_q$).
*   **Concurrently Available Capacity ($c_d$, `staff_fte`):** The mean full-time equivalent staffing concurrently available to process cases during operating hours. Total available labor-minutes on day $d$ is $c_d \times (720 - O_d)$, where $O_d$ is the outage duration.
*   **Complex Process Variant Proportion ($\gamma_d$, `complex_pct`):** The percentage of daily arriving cases routed into a multi-step complex execution path ($\gamma_d \in [0, 100]$).
*   **Outage Duration ($O_d$, `outage_min`):** Unscheduled operational downtime during open hours where service capacity is completely halted ($c_d = 0$ for duration $O_d$). On 2026-04-15, $O_d = 180\text{ min}$ (11:00 to 14:00).
*   **Morning Peak Ratio ($P_{\text{peak},d}$, `peak_08_10_pct`):** The proportion of daily arrivals entering the queue between 08:00:00 and 09:59:59 ($P_{\text{peak},d} = A_{d,[08:00, 10:00)} / A_d$).
*   **Hourly Arrival & Completion Vectors ($a_{d,h}$, $c_{d,h}$):** Hourly bin counts for $h \in \{0, 1, \dots, 11\}$, spanning $[07:00, 08:00), \dots, [18:00, 19:00)$.

---

## 1.2 Limitations of the Supplied Aggregated Data

1.  **Absence of Micro-Event Logs:** The dataset provides pre-aggregated daily summaries rather than raw event traces ($\langle \text{case\_id}, \text{activity}, \text{start\_timestamp}, \text{complete\_timestamp}, \text{resource} \rangle$). Individual case trajectories, routing variants, and true sojourn times cannot be directly computed.
2.  **Unobserved Boundary Conditions ($L(0)$):** Initial backlog at 07:00:00 on Day 1 ($L_0$) is unrecorded. Daily starting inventory can only be inferred through cumulative net flow balance.
3.  **Right-Censored Service Times:** Service time quantiles ($S_{50}, S_{90}$) are reported exclusively on *completed* cases. Cases trapped in queue or in service across the 19:00 boundary are omitted, creating selective right-censoring during congested regimes.
4.  **Discretization & Aliasing of Snapshot WIP:** 15-minute snapshot averages smooth out high-frequency intraday queue spikes.
5.  **Coarse Capacity Metric:** `staff_fte` is a constant daily average that obscures lunch breaks, staggered shifts, fatigue, and intraday resource allocation.

---

## 1.3 Six Threats to Naive Inference

1.  **Conflating Active Service Time with Total Flow Time (Sojourn Time):**
    *Threat:* Naive analysts frequently treat `median_service_min` (24–36 min) as customer turnaround time.
    *Reality:* Total flow time $W$ consists of waiting time plus service time ($W = W_q + S$). In heavy-traffic or non-stationary regimes, waiting time dominates active service by an order of magnitude (implied flow times range from 2.4 hours to over 6 hours).
2.  **Survivorship and Censoring Bias in Congested Service Quantiles:**
    *Threat:* Estimating system processing requirements solely from completed cases during backlogged days.
    *Reality:* When a queue builds up (e.g., Apr 15 outage, Apr 20–28 overload), servers often complete shorter, simpler cases first (or FIFO queues discharge older, simpler arrivals), while long multi-step cases linger in progress. Completed-case statistics systematically underestimate the actual work content of arriving demand during congested periods.
3.  **Invalidity of Little's Law Under Non-Stationary Net Flow:**
    *Threat:* Applying $L = \lambda W$ across pooled datasets or transient shock windows.
    *Reality:* Little's Law strictly requires asymptotic stochastic stationarity and flow conservation ($\lambda = \mu$). On days with acute outages ($\Delta N = +47$) or chronic drift ($\sum \Delta N = +94$ in Week 3), $L(t)$ is non-stationary, rendering direct division $L/\lambda$ mathematically unrepresentative of actual case experience.
4.  **Aggregation Masking and Simpson's Paradox Across Operating Modes:**
    *Threat:* Using 28-day pooled averages ($\bar{A} = 89.96$, $\bar{c} = 5.14\text{ FTE}$, $\bar{L} = 29.86$) to dimension staffing or evaluate performance.
    *Reality:* The center exhibits two radically distinct structural modes: Weekdays ($\sim 108\text{ arr/day}$, $6.0\text{ FTE}$) and Weekends ($\sim 45\text{ arr/day}$, $2.5\text{ FTE}$). Pooling generates a synthetic "average day" that never physically occurs and inflates variance indicators (pooled Fano factor $\approx 10.0$ vs weekday arrival Fano factor $\approx 0.66$).
5.  **Attribution Ambiguity in Case-Mix vs. Process/Coding Changes:**
    *Threat:* Assuming the jump in `complex_pct` on April 20 (from 22% to 44%) is purely exogenous demand.
    *Reality:* A simultaneous increase in complex percentage, median service time (+40%), and tail duration (+75%) can be caused by (a) true customer mix change, (b) a reclassification/coding shift in process-mining metadata, (c) upstream routing modifications, or (d) worker slowdown/multitasking under congestion. Without activity-level event logs, these causes remain conflated.
6.  **Reporting Discrepancies in Peak Window Definitions:**
    *Threat:* Directly matching hourly vector bins to aggregate KPI columns.
    *Reality:* On 2026-04-07, `peak_08_10_pct` is reported as 31%, but summing hourly vector bins $[08:00, 09:00)$ (5) and $[09:00, 10:00)$ (12) yields $17/101 = 16.8\%$. Summing bins $[09:00, 10:00)$ (12) and $[10:00, 11:00)$ (20) yields $32/101 = 31.7\%$. This indicates a 1-hour bin offset or distinct definition between event-logging bins and summary reporting.

## 2.2 Analytical Justification of Primary Regimes

1.  **Treatment of Outage (R3) and Outage Recovery (R4):**
    R3 cannot be modeled as normal queueing; it is a structural interruption where effective capacity drops to zero for $25\%$ of the working day. Treating R4 (completions = 129) as a standard high-productivity regime is an error: R4 represents non-stationary backlog drawdown enabled by transient emergency staffing (7.0 FTE) and an inflated upstream queue inventory ($L = 52$).
2.  **The Supercritical Overload Regime (R5):**
    Prior to April 20, mean service duration was $\approx 28\text{ min}$, yielding a daily service capacity of $(6.0 \times 720) / 28 \approx 154\text{ cases/day}$ (well above the 100 arrivals/day, $\rho \approx 0.66$). When the complex mix jumped to $44.6\%$, mean service time dilated to $\approx 44\text{ min}$ (estimated via lognormal quantile mapping: $\sigma = (\ln 83 - \ln 35)/1.282 = 0.673 \implies \mathbb{E}[S] = 35 \exp(0.673^2/2) \approx 43.9\text{ min}$).
    The daily processing capacity of 6.0 FTE fell to:
    $$\mu_{\text{max}} = \frac{6.0 \times 720\text{ min}}{43.9\text{ min}} \approx 98.4\text{ cases/day}$$
    Because arrival intensity averaged $114.0\text{ cases/day}$, traffic intensity became:
    $$\rho = \frac{114.0 \times 43.9}{6.0 \times 720} \approx 1.16 > 1.0$$
    The queue transitioned into an unstable integrator accumulating $\approx 13.4$ unserved cases per weekday, driving snapshot WIP from 37 to 50.
3.  **The Staffing Intervention (R7 & R8):**
    On 2026-04-29, management raised capacity to 7.0 FTE (5040 labor-min/day). Processing capacity rose to $5040 / 40.0 \approx 126\text{ cases/day}$. This shifted traffic intensity back below unity ($\rho \approx 0.91$), enabling completions (121–126/day) to outpace arrivals (116–120/day) and steadily drain accumulated WIP.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

To avoid forcing a single taxonomy, two alternative operational segmentations are proposed.

```
Alternative Segmentation Schemas:

[ Schema A: Queue-Balance State Model ]
  State I: Equilibrium (Apr 06-14, 18-19)  --> ΔN ≈ 0, WIP ∈ [12, 23], ρ < 0.80
  State II: Exogenous Shock (Apr 15)       --> Outage = 180m, ΔN = +47, WIP = 47
  State III: Capacity Deficit (Apr 20-28)  --> ΔN > 0 sustained, WIP -> 50, ρ > 1.15
  State IV: Backlog Drain (Apr 16-17, 29-01)--> ΔN < 0 sustained, WIP declining

[ Schema B: Intraday Arrival Profile Model ]
  Profile 1: Unimodal Smooth (Apr 06-19 Wkdays) --> Peak 08-10 ~ 30%, Hourly Fano = 3.2
  Profile 2: Hyper-Bursty Peak (Apr 20-03 Wkdays)--> Peak 08-10 ~ 45%, Hourly Fano = 10.1
  Profile 3: Flat Weekend (All Weekends)         --> Peak 08-10 ~ 19%, Hourly Fano = 1.3
```

---

## 3.1 Alternative A: Queue-Balance and Stability-State Model (4 Regimes)

This segmentation categorizes the system strictly by the sign of the macroscopic drift $\frac{dL}{dt} \approx \Delta N = A - C$:

1.  **State I: Stationary Equilibrium ($\Delta N \approx 0$, $N=11\text{ days}$):** Covers Week 1 and pre-outage/post-outage Week 2 (Apr 06–14, Apr 18–19, excluding Apr 15–17). Flow conservation holds perfectly ($\sum \Delta N = +5$ over 11 days). System is stable, queue delay is bounded, and Little's Law is valid.
2.  **State II: Exogenous Capacity Shock ($\Delta N \gg 0$, $N=1\text{ day}$):** April 15 outage. Acute physical system downtime ($O_d = 180\text{ min}$). WIP spikes from 23 to 47.
3.  **State III: Supercritical Deficit Drift ($\Delta N > 0$ sustained, $N=9\text{ days}$):** April 20 to April 28. Chronic structural instability where $\rho > 1$. Cumulative backlog increases by $+106\text{ cases}$ across 9 days. Queue operates as an accumulating buffer.
4.  **State IV: Subcritical Backlog Draining ($\Delta N < 0$ sustained, $N=5\text{ days}$):** April 16–17 (outage recovery) and April 29 – May 01 (staffing intervention weekdays). Total net flow is $-48\text{ cases}$. Capacity is elevated above demand to clear prior accumulation.

*Operational Story:* This segmentation serves executive SLA and risk management. It categorizes operating periods into in-control steady states, acute interruptions, unsustainable operational decay, and active remediation phases.

---

## 3.2 Alternative B: Intraday Arrival Dispersion Profile (3 Regimes)

This segmentation groups operating days by their intraday arrival distribution and burstiness metrics:

1.  **Profile 1: Broad Unimodal Weekday Profile (Baseline Period, Apr 06–19 Weekdays):**
    $P_{\text{peak}} \approx 28\text{--}33\%$; hourly arrival variance $\sigma^2 \approx 27.2$, Fano factor $I_D \approx 3.23$, $CV \approx 0.62$. Arrivals build moderately between 09:00 and 12:00.
2.  **Profile 2: Hyper-Bursty Bimodal Weekday Profile (Post-Mix Shift, Apr 20–May 01 Weekdays):**
    $P_{\text{peak}} \approx 40\text{--}50\%$; hourly arrival variance $\sigma^2 \approx 97.0$, Fano factor $I_D \approx 10.12$, $CV \approx 1.03$. Massive arrival spikes occur at 10:00 (34 cases/hr) and 13:00 (24 cases/hr), causing intraday transient queue explosion regardless of daily mean staffing.
3.  **Profile 3: Flat Low-Intensity Weekend Profile (All Weekends):**
    $P_{\text{peak}} \approx 17\text{--}22\%$; hourly arrivals remain between 1 and 8 cases/hr; Fano factor $I_D \approx 1.29$, $CV \approx 0.55$.

*Operational Story:* This segmentation addresses workforce scheduling. It highlights that even if daily capacity matches daily arrivals, failure to align hourly staffing to intraday arrival spikes creates severe intraday queue buildup.

## 4.1 Three Critical Distortions Created by Pooled Analysis

### 1. Artificial Bimodal Averaging (Weekday vs. Weekend Masking)
*   **The Masked Behavior:** The pooled mean arrival rate ($\bar{A} = 89.96$) and pooled staffing level ($\bar{c} = 5.14\text{ FTE}$) represent a fictitious operating regime. Weekdays operate at $108.0\text{ arrivals}$ with $6.2\text{ FTE}$; weekends operate at $45.4\text{ arrivals}$ with $2.6\text{ FTE}$.
*   **Mathematical Consequence:** Pooling generates an artificial arrival variance of $\sigma^2 = 902.2$ and a pooled Fano factor of $I_D = 10.03$. However, within weekdays, arrival variance is only $\sigma^2 = 71.5$, yielding a sub-Poisson Fano factor of $I_D = 0.66$ ($CV = 0.08$). The apparent overdispersion in the 28-day dataset is almost entirely an artifact of calendar pooling.

### 2. Concealment of the April 20 Supercritical Transition
*   **The Masked Behavior:** The 28-day pooled traffic intensity ($\bar{\rho} \approx 0.85$) suggests a well-utilized but stable queue.
*   **Mathematical Consequence:** This completely obscures the fact that on April 20, the complex case proportion doubled ($21.8\% \to 44.6\%$), increasing the mean service time from $28.1\text{ min}$ to $43.9\text{ min}$. Because staffing was kept at 6.0 FTE, the true traffic intensity during R5 jumped to $\rho \approx 1.16 > 1.0$.
*   **Operational Consequence:** The system was in a state of structural queue collapse, accumulating $+94\text{ cases}$ of unserved backlog across 7 weekdays. A pooled average suggests that performance was stable throughout April.

### 3. Misinterpreting Backlog Drawdown as "High Worker Productivity"
*   **The Masked Behavior:** On April 16 ($C=129$), April 17 ($C=112$), April 29 ($C=121$), and April 30 ($C=126$), daily completed counts were exceptionally high compared to the pooled average ($\bar{C} = 86.04$).
*   **Mathematical Consequence:** In a pooled regression, these days appear as high-throughput "high productivity" outliers.
*   **Reality:** These high completion counts were achieved because (a) large backlogs had accumulated ($L = 52$ on Apr 16, $L = 50$ on Apr 28), ensuring servers suffered zero starvation, and (b) management deployed emergency staffing (7.0 FTE). Treating backlog draining as ordinary high-capacity performance leads to unrealistic baseline throughput targets.

## 5.2 Dispersion Indices and Queueing Implications

*   **Fano Factor ($I_D = \sigma^2 / \mu$):** For a stationary Poisson point process, $I_D = 1.0$.
    *   On 2026-04-07 (Baseline), $I_D = 3.23$ reflects moderate non-homogeneous diurnal variation.
    *   On 2026-04-21 (Mix-Shift Weekday), $I_D = 10.12$ and $c_v = 1.03$ reveal hyper-bursty batch arrivals (34 arrivals in a single hour from 10:00 to 11:00).
*   **Queueing Penalty of Burstiness:** In general $G/G/c$ queueing approximations (e.g., Kingman's heavy-traffic formula / Allen-Cunneen approximation), expected waiting time scales directly with the squared coefficient of variation:
    $$\mathbb{E}[W_q] \approx \left(\frac{c_a^2 + c_s^2}{2}\right) \left(\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\right) \mathbb{E}[S]$$
    When intraday arrivals shift from $c_a^2 \approx 0.38$ (Apr 07) to $c_a^2 \approx 1.06$ (Apr 21), expected queue delay triples independently of any change in total daily volume.

## 5.4 Why Four Representative Days Cannot Establish a Full Stochastic Model

1.  **High Epistemic Uncertainty & Small Sample Size ($N=4$):** Four curated days represent only $14.3\%$ of the timeline. They cannot capture inter-day variance in arrival timing, lunch dips, or weather-induced surges.

3.  **Unobserved Joint Arrival-Service Dependencies:** Cross-correlations between arrival bursts and server slowdown (e.g., worker fatigue or multitasking during spikes) cannot be estimated without continuous intraday event logs across all 28 days.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little's Law states that the long-term time-averaged inventory equals the long-term average throughput multiplied by the average flow time ($L = \lambda W$, or $W = L / \lambda$).

*   **Units & Scaling:**
    *   $L$: Mean cases in system from snapshots (cases).
    *   $\lambda$ or $\mu$: Throughput proxy in cases per 12-hour day ($T = 12.0\text{ hours} = 720\text{ minutes}$).
    *   Implied Flow Time $W$:
        $$W_{\text{hours}} = \frac{L}{\lambda_{\text{daily}}} \times 12.0\text{ hours} = \frac{L}{\lambda_{\text{hourly}}}$$
        $$W_{\text{minutes}} = \frac{L}{\lambda_{\text{daily}}} \times 720\text{ minutes}$$

# 7. Operational implications

## 7.1 Workforce Dimensioning and Dynamic Shift Scheduling

1.  **Permanent Baseline Capacity Adjustment for Case-Mix Shift:**
    Under the post-April 20 regime ($\sim 44\%$ complex cases, mean service time $\approx 44\text{ min}$), the previous baseline staffing of 6.0 FTE ($4320\text{ staff-min/day}$) provides a maximum capacity of only $98\text{ cases/day}$, guaranteeing queue instability under weekday demand of $114\text{ arrivals/day}$.
    *Recommendation:* Permanently maintain baseline weekday staffing at **no less than 7.5 FTE** ($5400\text{ staff-min/day}$). This establishes a nominal processing capacity of $\approx 123\text{ cases/day}$, securing an operating traffic intensity of:
    $$\rho = \frac{114 \times 43.9}{7.5 \times 720} \approx 0.92$$
    Weekend staffing must be permanently set to **3.0 FTE** (not 2.5 FTE) to prevent weekend backlog accumulation.
2.  **Dynamic Intraday Shift Alignment (Matching the 08:00–11:00 Surge):**
    Flat 12-hour FTE coverage is unsuited to the observed arrival point process (e.g., April 21, where $47\%$ of arrivals occur in the morning and a single hour sees 34 arrivals).
    *Recommendation:* Implement staggered 8-hour overlapping shifts:
    *   *Early Shift (07:00–15:30, 4 FTE):* Covers opening and early morning intake.
    *   *Peak Mid-Day Shift (08:30–17:00, 4 FTE):* Deployed specifically to absorb the 09:00–12:00 burst (concentrating 8.0 concurrent FTE during peak hours).
    *   *Closing Shift (10:30–19:00, 2 FTE):* Handles afternoon intake and clears daily queue before closing.

---

## 7.2 Automated Backlog and Outage Recovery Protocols

1.  **Threshold-Triggered Outage Surge Protocol:**
    As proven on April 15, an unscheduled 3-hour outage injects $+47\text{ cases}$ of carry-over backlog.
    *Recommendation:* Establish an automated operational rule: whenever unscheduled downtime exceeds 60 minutes, trigger an emergency **Outage Recovery Surge** authorizing $+1.0\text{ to } +1.5\text{ FTE}$ of overtime/on-call staffing for the subsequent two working days (replicating the successful recovery seen in R4).
2.  **Controlled Queue-Draining Trajectories:**
    Attempting to clear backlog without surge staffing forces newly arriving cases to experience extreme queue delays for multiple weeks. Draining should be planned at a rate of 5–10 net cases per day.

---

## 7.3 Peak Smoothing and Queue Routing Triage

1.  **Staggered Arrival Appointments & Intake Throttling:**
    Implement customer appointment scheduling or batch intake smoothing to cap maximum hourly arrivals at 15 cases/hour, eliminating the catastrophic 34-case spikes.
2.  **Variant-Based Fast-Track Routing ($c\mu$-Scheduling):**
    Currently, standard simple cases (median service 24 min) are trapped behind complex multi-step cases (p90 service 85 min) in a single FIFO queue.
    *Recommendation:* Establish a dedicated Fast-Track lane (2 FTE assigned exclusively to single-step variants) while routing complex cases to specialized senior staff. By the $c\mu$ rule of queueing theory, prioritizing high-completion-rate jobs dramatically reduces overall mean WIP and average waiting time across the entire population.

---

## 7.4 Enhanced Process-Mining Telemetry & Data Collection

1.  **Event-Level Logging:** Transition from daily tabular aggregations to transactional XES / Parquet event logs recording exact ISO-8601 timestamps for `case_id`, `activity_name`, `lifecycle:start`, `lifecycle:complete`, `resource_id`, and `variant_path`.
2.  **Boundary Queue Snapshots:** Explicitly log initial queue inventory at 07:00:00 ($L_0$) and closing inventory at 19:00:00 ($L_{\text{close}}$) to track exact overnight carry-over.
3.  **Outage Event Logging:** System logs must record precise downtime start/end timestamps and track whether cases in progress were aborted, paused, or lost.

---

## 7.5 Risky Policy Identified Under Pooled Averages: The "Pooled Capacity Fallacy"

*   **The Flawed Policy:** A management consultant looking only at the 28-day pooled summary observes an average of $89.96\text{ arrivals/day}$ and a pooled median service time of $30.96\text{ min}$. The consultant calculates total daily workload as:
    $$\text{Workload} = 89.96\text{ cases} \times 30.96\text{ min} = 2785\text{ labor-minutes} = 46.4\text{ staff-hours/day}$$
    Across a 12-hour day, $46.4 / 12 = 3.87\text{ FTE}$. Factoring in a target $80\%$ utilization, the consultant recommends downsizing staffing to **5.0 FTE across all days**, projecting substantial labor cost savings with "adequate capacity."
*   **The Catastrophic Consequence:**
    Under the actual operating conditions of Weeks 3 and 4:
    *   Weekday arrivals average $114\text{ cases/day}$, and true mean service time is $43.9\text{ min}$.
    *   Required daily workload is $114 \times 43.9 = 5005\text{ labor-minutes} = 83.4\text{ staff-hours/day}$.
    *   A 5.0 FTE team provides only $5.0 \times 12 \times 60 = 3600\text{ labor-minutes/day}$.
    *   Traffic intensity under this policy explodes to:
        $$\rho = \frac{5005}{3600} \approx 1.39 \gg 1.0$$
    *   The queue accumulates an unserved deficit of $\approx 32\text{ cases every single day}$ ($+160\text{ cases/week}$). Within two weeks, customer waiting times would exceed several days, causing SLA collapse, severe customer abandonment, and operational failure.

# 4. LITTLE'S LAW EVALUATION FOR EXPLICITLY CHOSEN WINDOWS
# ==============================================================================
# System is open 12 hours (07:00-19:00 = 720 min/day).
# Implied flow time W (hours) = (mean_wip / daily_rate) * 12.0 hours

littles_rows = []
for reg, grp in df_daily.groupby('primary_regime'):
  l_bar = grp['mean_wip'].mean()
  lambda_arr = grp['arrivals'].mean()
  mu_comp = grp['completed'].mean()
  net = grp['net_flow'].sum()

  w_arr_hrs = (l_bar / lambda_arr) * 12.0
  w_comp_hrs = (l_bar / mu_comp) * 12.0

  if reg in ['R1_Baseline_Wkday', 'R2_Baseline_Wkend']:
    verdict = 'applicable'
  elif reg in [
      'R7_Staff_Intervention_Wkday',
      'R8_Staff_Intervention_Wkend',
      'R5_MixShift_Overload_Wkday',
      'R6_MixShift_Overload_Wkend',
  ]:
    verdict = 'approximate only'
  else:
    verdict = 'not defensible'

  littles_rows.append({
      'Regime': reg,
      'WIP_L': round(l_bar, 2),
      'Arr_Lambda': round(lambda_arr, 2),
      'Comp_Mu': round(mu_comp, 2),
      'NetFlow_Sum': net,
      'W_Arr_hrs': round(w_arr_hrs, 2),
      'W_Comp_hrs': round(w_comp_hrs, 2),
      'Verdict': verdict,
  })
