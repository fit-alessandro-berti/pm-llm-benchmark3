## 1. Leakage and temporal-consistency findings

Admissibility requires both that a fact belongs to the prefix ending immediately after the first Coverage Check Completed event and that its particular value was available by t0. An old event timestamp does not establish when a value became known. A process-feasible event can still leak because it occurs later; a record available early can still violate the process model. These are separate findings.

Severity reflects the threat to an honest early prediction or explanation. Critical findings directly reveal outcomes or encode substantial future trajectories; high findings require exclusion or repair before use; medium findings principally concern interpretation, robustness, or fairness; low findings indicate no identified defect under the stated lineage. Mean absolute SHAP-like importance establishes neither effect direction nor causation, and the contaminated model's rankings cannot be treated as rankings for a clean model.

## 2. Process-model consistency checks

Build two linked validation layers: point-in-time feature reconstruction and normative process conformance. Passing one does not imply passing the other.

1. **Establish the exact prediction boundary.** For each case, identify the first Coverage Check Completed event by authoritative occurrence order and record the scoring cutoff. Use an event identifier and reliable sequence index to distinguish events at the same timestamp. Require every feature dependency to have an eligible occurrence/effective time and to have been available by t0. Backdated corrections and late-arriving records must not enter an earlier reconstruction. Preserve the actual historical value rather than substituting today's corrected version.

2. **Replay availability and joins.** Build features from the eligible prefix and historical dimension versions. Enforce one-sided queue joins; reject even a one-minute-future snapshot. Check initial values against their creation/version histories. For prior claims, test earlier versus later creation, late arrival, current-case exclusion, and entity linkage. For documents, test uploads and refreshes on both sides of the cutoff on the same date. For employee experience and risk scores, verify the dependency chain as well as the field timestamp. Lack of history means “unverified/exclude,” not “assume unchanged.”

3. **Test dependence on complete traces.** Review SQL and feature code for final/last aggregations, case-wide counts, unrestricted windows, closure joins, and variants generated before prefix truncation. Use suffix-invariance tests: hold the prefix fixed while adding document cycles, changing later assignments/decisions, or appending an appeal. Every t0 feature and its resulting score must remain unchanged. Re-extract the same prefix at later dates; changes require an explained versioning issue and must not silently rewrite historical availability. Inspect indirect dependencies, including upstream scores and the features hidden in local aggregates.

4. **Align against the process, treating an unfinished prefix as unfinished.** Use a branching/concurrent state model, such as a Petri-net alignment, to represent the fast track, repeated request-receive-validate cycles, assessment, optional triggered investigation, decision, conditional appeal, and closure. Do not require a valid prefix to have completed the entire lifecycle. Declarative checks can enforce request-before-receipt, receipt-before-validation, eligible-decision-before-appeal, assessment-before-decision, and final-decision/withdrawal-before-closure. Represent evidence loops explicitly. Do not infer from the numbered description that investigation must be serial with assessment, or that the fast-track assessment must be followed by an obligatory duplicate assessment event.

5. **Resolve order ambiguity without fabricating order.** Specify whether each explainer arrow means direct succession or an ordered subsequence. Inspect the source's ordering guarantees and distinguish a domain sequence index from ingestion order, which may differ from execution order. For ties with no trustworthy ordering, retain a partial order or enumerate permitted linearizations. A directional pattern that changes across unresolved orderings is not established. In Z, an assessment-before-closure ordering may remove the apparent reversed pair, but the rest of the trace must still establish the required decision. If no allowed ordering aligns, flag a conformance defect; if only some align, flag uncertainty. Neither outcome makes those events available at t0.

6. **Check the state to which an explanation refers.** With only registration and coverage observed, the evidence branch's first document request or the eligible fast-track assessment can be a next enabled activity. A fraud investigation is conditionally enabled only if a qualifying trigger and applicable rule are established. Receipt/validation require their preceding evidence events; settlement decision requires the applicable assessment progression; appeal requires an earlier Partial/Deny decision; closure requires a final decision or withdrawal. These later activities can be future-possible without being enabled now. Claiming an appeal or closure has already occurred contradicts the supplied prefix. Strict closure-before-assessment is process-inconsistent under this lifecycle, subject to the tie uncertainty. Explanations must distinguish observed facts, enabled actions, and uncertain future paths.

7. **Validate repeated decisions semantically.** Preserve distinct decision event identifiers, outcomes, and their relation to an appeal. A sequence with an initial Partial or Deny decision, an appeal, and a reconsidered decision is legitimate. An appeal before any decision, or one supported only by an Approve decision, violates the stated appeal rule. Two decision labels alone neither establish a valid reconsideration nor justify deduplication: investigate actual duplicates, missing appeal events, or other documented exceptions. The pattern Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded is therefore different from a proven closure-before-assessment ordering.

8. **Use discovery diagnostics as evidence, not authority.** DFGs reveal common adjacency and suspicious reversed edges; footprints summarize observed ordering/concurrency. Both lose context and can reflect timestamp artifacts, label coarsening, and noncompliant behavior. Frequency does not turn an edge into a normative rule, and absence does not prohibit a rare legitimate path. Alignments are only as sound as the model, event mapping, and recorded order; declarative rules need adequate case attributes and cannot recover missing facts. Review discrepancies against the applicable process version instead of forcing every trace into a possibly outdated model.

A minimum regression set should include a valid fast track, multiple evidence cycles, triggered fraud parallel with assessment, a legitimate appeal/redecision, an appeal without an eligible decision, strict closure-before-assessment, unresolved tied events, and several post-t0 mutations that must leave prefix features unchanged. Keep temporal failures and conformance failures as separate test results. A genuinely pre-t0 anomalous record should be investigated as a data-quality issue or real exception, rather than mislabeled as leakage solely because it violates the normative model.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

The classes below can overlap. Temporal admissibility is a prerequisite; predictive usefulness, causal identification, operational actionability, and fairness then require different evidence.

| Class | Surviving or repairable signals | What may be claimed | What may not be claimed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defensible predictive signal | Frozen `initial_damage_complexity`; correctly reconstructed prior-claim history; an initial reserve if its timing is verified | A clean model may find an association with breach risk conditional on the observed prefix. Complexity is a credible initial workload indicator; prior history and reserve may add information, subject to validation. | That these features necessarily cause delay; that more prior claims necessarily mean greater complexity; or that their old importance/direction survives removal of leakage. |
| Process-state or congestion signal | `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`; an eligible queue snapshot; current assignment and experience after repair; observed branch progress and pending work at appropriate checkpoints | Prefix age describes elapsed target time. A timestamp-valid queue measures load and may predict waiting. Prefix events describe completed or pending work. Such signals can support triage if a separate evaluation shows benefit. | That reducing the measured queue by a given amount will reduce breach risk by its SHAP contribution; that changing teams has an identified causal effect; or that later evidence rework is already present at this checkpoint. |
| Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review | `claimant_region`; channel if verified; potentially reserve, prior history, and assignment | A proxy can contain useful predictive information and can be retained if performance, fairness, and use-case evidence support it. Region's signal may reflect service-network or communication differences. | That a region or channel has an inherent propensity to delay, that correlated socioeconomic traits explain an individual claim, or that deleting a single proxy automatically produces a fair system. |
| Drift/calendar proxy | `calendar_week`; explicit policy/holiday/planned-capacity features known at t0 | Week may identify a historical operating environment. Direct, available policy and calendar variables can make the association more interpretable and testable. | That week itself causes breaches, that the fitted association recurs every year, or that holiday and policy effects can be separately identified when they strongly coincide. |
| Unusable until lineage is repaired | Current queue values with unresolved join direction; prior-claim count; nightly document count; final/current team or role; current risk score; employee experience; unverified channel/reserve | These are candidates for historical reconstruction or replacement by explicitly defined t0 counterparts. Their informational meaning must be reassessed after repair. | That a plausible name, frequent agreement with historical values, or an apparently sensible attribution establishes availability. Complete-trace outcomes remain excluded; a prefix counterpart is a new feature, not a license to retain the original value. |

**Predictive validity** requires as-of availability, consistent train/serve definitions, and useful performance on independently held-out future cohorts. Correlation is sufficient for a predictive relationship when these conditions hold; it is not a reason for automatic removal. However, a temporally available constant prefix feature may provide no discrimination: document-request counts are zero and activity prefixes match in the supplied cases.

**Causal interpretation** requires an identified intervention, plausible causal assumptions, and evidence beyond attribution, such as a randomized evaluation or a defensible quasi-experimental design. Complexity can influence both routing and delay; late routing can respond to already-emerging delay; policy and holidays can confound calendar associations. A SHAP background distribution and its handling of correlated features can change how predictive credit is allocated without identifying any of these mechanisms.

**Operational actionability** concerns a feasible decision made at t0 and its demonstrated consequences. Congestion may motivate a capacity or routing investigation, but observational importance does not quantify the benefit of intervening. An immutable characteristic can help identify service needs without being an intervention target. Changing a field's encoded value is not equivalent to changing the underlying process.

**Fairness review** must examine who receives resources or adverse treatment, group and relevant intersectional calibration, error rates, service outcomes, and proxy redundancy. Compare models with and without suspect proxies and with admissible operational substitutes. Interpret performance and fairness tradeoffs in the deployment context; neither automatic retention nor automatic deletion is sufficient. Feature perturbation alone does not establish causal fairness.

For the case revisions below, no individual queue observation/publication timestamps were supplied. Consequently, the congestion feature is preserved as a valid candidate, but X's and Y's particular queue values are withheld until the as-of check establishes their eligibility. Channel and reserve are also withheld pending provenance. The retained directions are qualitative candidates suggested by the reported associations, not new SHAP values or confirmed effects in a refitted model. The revised explanations deliberately provide no replacement probability.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Immediately withdraw confidence in the affected early scores and explanations.** Remove known future fields and full-trace patterns from the candidate input contract, and quarantine uncertain fields until their availability can be demonstrated. Audit the full dependency graph, including low-ranked features, upstream risk models, encoders, and the local “other features” aggregate. Do not obtain replacement probabilities by subtracting leaked contributions, zeroing fields, or renormalizing the surviving importance scores: refitting changes the learned relationships, baseline, interactions, and attributions.

2. **Create a historical feature-store contract.** For each feature, document entity/granularity, definition, event/effective time, first availability time, computation/publication time, source version, transformation version, and permitted staleness. Store immutable historical versions or bitemporal records for mutable scores, reserves, assignments, and employee attributes. Distinguish a value that existed in the world from one the prediction service could access. Enforce backward as-of joins and explicit missingness when no eligible version exists. A current warehouse overwrite must never silently serve as historical truth.

3. **Rebuild training and evaluation data from exact prefixes.** Materialize each example immediately after the first coverage completion using only then-available versions. Repair historical counts and document/queue joins, replace complete-case features with precisely defined prefix counterparts, and ensure training/serving parity. Keep future outcomes exclusively in labels. Confirm the target's clock, settlement endpoint, handling of reconsideration/withdrawal, and adequate follow-up for cases near the extraction boundary. Do not label insufficiently observed cases as non-breaches. Use time-based splits by prediction date, case-level separation, and appropriate entity checks; fit encoders and preprocessing within training data only.

4. **Make process and availability validation release gates.** Automate the replay, suffix-invariance, tie-ambiguity, and process tests described above. Version the normative model with policy changes and document real exceptions. Attach provenance and state checks to explanation generation so an explanation cannot cite an unobserved future activity as an observed reason. Distinguish confirmed violations, unresolved order, and incomplete but valid prefixes in both logs and review outputs.

5. **Refit, evaluate, and calibrate the clean model.** Evaluate discrimination, precision/recall at operational thresholds, Brier score or log loss, and calibration curves on chronologically later data. Check performance by policy regime, branch-relevant early characteristics, and service groups. Fit any calibrator on separate eligible validation data and evaluate it on held-out data; recalibration alone cannot rescue a model trained with unavailable features. Compare against simple admissible baselines and quantify uncertainty. A performance drop after removing leakage is evidence about the original evaluation, not a reason to restore future inputs.

6. **Validate the explanations themselves.** Document the SHAP-like method, output scale/link, background population, feature dependence assumptions, and treatment of interactions. Check reconstruction of the actual explained output in the appropriate units, but recognize that arithmetic agreement cannot establish temporal validity. Regenerate explanations from the clean model and assess stability across temporal folds, retraining seeds, reasonable backgrounds, queue staleness, and admissible perturbations. Examine correlated-feature groups as well as individual rankings; report uncertainty or unstable direction rather than forcing a single confident story. Separate model association from any independently tested causal or operational claim.

7. **Complete proxy, fairness, and actionability review before operational use.** Evaluate region, channel, reserve, history, and assignment jointly with relevant service variables, including the consequences of their inclusion and removal. Measure group calibration, errors, resource allocation, and service outcomes at actual decision thresholds. Check whether supposedly more operational substitutes merely reproduce the same proxy. Validate capacity, routing, or assistance interventions through an appropriate prospective or causal evaluation; do not translate attribution magnitudes into promised reductions in delay.

8. **Monitor the deployed definitions and process continuously.** Track feature availability failures, future joins, staleness, missing historical versions, unexpected overwrites, duplicate events, timestamp ties, and schema or label-semantic changes. Monitor conformance by process version, feature and attribution distributions, calendar/policy drift, subgroup errors, and calibration once outcomes mature. Alert on new activity variants or changed decision/appeal semantics and trigger investigation or revalidation. Keep an auditable link from every displayed explanation to its model version, feature versions, cutoff, and admissibility checks.
