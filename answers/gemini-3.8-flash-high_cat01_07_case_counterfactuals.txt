## 1. Feasibility model

```text
Baseline and Variant Partial Orders:

1. Mandatory Global Sequence:
   Register < Triage
   Decision < Notify

2. Verification Prerequisite Paths:
   Path A (Originals_Required == False):
     Triage < Identity_Check
     Triage < Document_Check_1
     Identity_Check || Document_Check_1  (concurrent execution permitted)
   
   Path B (Originals_Required == True):
     Triage < Identity_Check
     Triage < Originals_Receipt < Document_Check_1
     Identity_Check || Originals_Receipt  (identity check may proceed prior to originals receipt)

3. Assessment Join:
   Identity_Check < Assessment_1
   Document_Check_1 < Assessment_1
   (Assessment_1 requires completion of both Identity_Check and Document_Check_1)

4. Escalation and Compliance Concurrency (Advisory Branches):
   Preliminary Escalation: Triage < Escalation_Advice_Prelim < Compliance_Prelim
   Escalation_Advice_Prelim || {Identity_Check, Document_Check, Assessment}
   Reactive Escalation: Assessment_k < Escalation_Advice_Reactive < Compliance_Review

5. Rework Loops (k >= 1):
   {Assessment_k, Compliance_Review} < Request_More_Info_k
   Request_More_Info_k < Document_Check_{k+1} < Assessment_{k+1}

6. Terminal Gating to Decision:
   Standard Risk/Value (High_Risk == False AND High_Value == False):
     Assessment_final < Decision
   High Risk or High Value (High_Risk == True OR High_Value == True):
     Assessment_final < Compliance_Review_final < Decision
```

```text
Conditional, Declarative, and Resource Constraints:

1. Control-Flow & Routing Rules:
   - Initial & Terminal: Start = Register; Terminal = Notify; exactly one Decision per case.
   - Verification Concurrency: Concurrent(Identity_Check, Document_Check) <=> (Originals_Required == False) OR (Originals_Receipt.completed == True).
   - Mandatory Compliance Gate: (High_Risk == True OR High_Value == True) => (Compliance_Review in Path AND Assessment_final < Compliance_Review < Decision).
   - Compliance Non-Substitution: Compliance_Prelim.completed == True DOES NOT SATISFY the requirement for Compliance_Review_final.
   - Rework Obligation: (Request_More_Info_k in Path) => (Document_Check_{k+1} in Path AND Assessment_{k+1} in Path).
   - High-Risk Rework Re-affirmation: (Request_More_Info_k in Path AND (High_Risk == True OR High_Value == True)) => Compliance_Review_final must follow Assessment_{k+1}.
   - Truncated Rework Policy: If Rework_Prohibited == True, Request_More_Info cannot be emitted; Decision must evaluate existing record (Approve_with_Conditions OR Reject).

2. Separation of Duties (Four-Eyes Principle):
   - For any case c: Resource(Assessment_final) != Resource(Compliance_Review_final).
   - Resource(Decision) = M1.

3. Resource Allocation & Mutual Exclusion:
   - Resource Pools: Triage in {T1, T2}; Identity in {I1, I2}; Documents in {D1, D2}; Assessment in {A1, A2}; Compliance in {C1}; Escalation in {S1}; Decision/Notify in {M1}.
   - Non-Preemption: For any resource r, Interval(r, task_i) INTERSECT Interval(r, task_j) = EMPTY for all i != j.
   - C1 Inelasticity: |Compliance Pool| = 1 (C1 only); any advance reservation locks C1 capacity against incoming queue.

4. Temporal & Queue Dynamics:
   - Start_Time(task) >= max(max_{p in Pred(task)} Completion_Time(p), Arrival_Time(task) + Queue_Wait(Resource(task), Arrival_Time)).
   - Queue forecasts are stochastic expectations subject to arrival volatility and priority reordering.
```

### Classification of Specified Interventions

- **K-411 alters timing only:** The intervention removes an artificial software-lock dependency between `Identity Check` and `Document Check`. It preserves the baseline task set, activity definitions, resource assignments, and decision criteria. By establishing true task independence (`Identity Check || Document Check`), it compresses the verification span to $\max(\text{Duration}(\text{Identity}), \text{Duration}(\text{Document}))$ and advances the case's arrival timestamp at the Assessment queue without altering process routing or policy.
- **K-219 alters routing and timing (with latent control-flow loop risk):** The intervention switches resource binding for `Document Check` from D1 to D2 upon originals arrival. This changes task routing across resource pools, exchanging a longer queue (D1) for a slower service rate (D2). If D2 struggles with handwritten documents, it conditionally triggers a downstream control-flow loop (`Request More Info -> Document Check -> Assessment`), altering the execution graph.
- **K-104 alters routing and timing:** The intervention introduces an early concurrent branch at Triage, dispatching preliminary dossiers to S1 and C1 in parallel with verification (`Escalation_Advice || {Identity, Document}`). It also alters resource scheduling by pre-booking a provisional C1 slot, shifting downstream compliance start times and reducing final compliance duration. The structural terminal requirements remain intact.
- **K-337 alters the allowed decision policy and control-flow structure:** The intervention actively prohibits the second `Request More Info` loop after `Assessment 2`, truncating the cyclical control-flow graph. It changes the operational decision policy from an iterative evidentiary standard ("rework until discrepancies are resolved") to a bounded-evidence decision policy ("render a conditional approval with risk covenants or an outright rejection on the existing record").

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

- `Factual mechanism:` K-104 involved complex corporate documentation and high-risk/high-value attributes. At Triage (Mon 08:20–08:50), T1 detected a sanctions ambiguity but failed to escalate it. The case experienced two major queuing bottlenecks: 13 hours in D1's queue (starting Mon 21:40, completing Tue 05:40) and 9.5 hours in A1's queue (starting Tue 15:10). At Tue 19:10, A1 rediscovered the sanctions ambiguity, halting the case for reactive Escalation Advice from S1 (Tue 19:20–21:20). S1 advised a formal compliance review. The case entered C1's queue at Tue 21:20, sat overnight during a 16-hour queue backlog, and required C1 to read all complex corporate evidence de novo (4.5 h duration, Wed 13:10–17:40) before M1 approved with conditions on Wed 18:40.
- `Minimal intervention:` At Triage completion (Mon 08:50), T1 immediately dispatches a preliminary escalation dossier to S1 and registers a provisional reservation in C1's scheduling queue. S1 conducts early advisory analysis and C1 performs a preliminary compliance screening concurrently while I1 and D1 execute verification. Final compliance review remains mandatory and strictly sequenced after A1's final Assessment.
- `Temporal propagation:` S1 receives the dossier Mon 08:50. With minimal baseline escalation backlog, S1 conducts advisory analysis Mon 09:30–11:30, drafting sanctions guidance and alerting C1. C1 performs a 1.5-hour preliminary entity check on Monday afternoon. Concurrently, I1 (Mon 10:40–12:50) and D1 (Mon 21:40–Tue 05:40) proceed on their observed schedules. When A1 begins Assessment at Tue 15:10, S1's advisory note is already appended to the file. A1 assesses continuously through Tue 19:10 without pausing for an escalation referral. Because C1 holds preliminary analysis notes and a provisional booking, C1 takes the case at Wed 08:30 (avoiding the factual wait until Wed 13:10). Furthermore, C1's review duration shrinks from 4.5 h to 2.5 h because the ownership structure was pre-analyzed. Final Compliance concludes Wed 11:00; M1 signs Decision by Wed 11:40.
- `Process-feasibility check:` The counterfactual path strictly preserves the normative control-flow: Register $\to$ Triage $\to$ [Identity $\parallel$ Document] $\to$ Assessment $\to$ Compliance Review $\to$ Decision $\to$ Notify. ### System-Wide 100-Case Weekly Rollout Analysis

Rolling out these interventions across 100 weekly cases exposes severe systemic friction and shifts operational bottlenecks. First, reserving scarce compliance capacity (C1) creates an immediate capacity failure. With C1 as the sole certified reviewer facing an existing 16–20 hour backlog, pre-booking slots for dozens of cases functions as hard blocking. Because upstream document checks and assessments exhibit variable completions, reserved C1 slots either sit idle during upstream delays or displace unreserved urgent cases, driving C1 queue wait times nonlinearly toward infinity under near-unity utilization.

Second, substituting D1 with D2 for handwritten documents severely distorts resource balance. D2 requires 6–8 hours for handwritten files compared to D1's 4 hours. Shifting 100 handwritten cases consumes an extra 200–400 labor hours, overwhelming D2 and ballooning D2's queue from 2 hours to over 20 hours. This strangles standard digital cases that D2 could otherwise clear rapidly.

Third, rework dynamics diverge sharply: prohibiting secondary rework loops (K-337) permanently eliminates hundreds of hours of repetitive verification, whereas relying on D2 for complex handwriting increases clarification requests, triggering cascading rework loops that compound backlogs across both verification and assessment.

Fourth, parallelizing verification (K-411) does not consume extra labor, but its lead-time compression pushes waves of completed verifications into the assessment queue (A1/A2) simultaneously. This causes acute mid-day queue spikes, worsening assessment delays when assessors are in training.

Fifth, weekend and external-response boundaries create extreme temporal cliffs: external suppliers take 8–36 hours to respond, meaning rework requests issued after Thursday noon incur 70+ hour weekend stalls. Eliminating late-week rework loops avoids these massive dead-time traps.

Finally, under high workload, apparent concurrency often degrades into batch logging. Workers facing system locks enter synthetic timestamps after sequential processing rather than achieving true parallel execution. Genuine speedup requires verified real-time task independence rather than administrative record decoupling.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is parallelizing verification via controlled lock release (K-411). It respects all normative prerequisites, preserves decision quality, requires no incremental resource hours, and relies entirely on eliminating artificial software-enforced idle time. Because it does not alter risk policies or worker assignments, its cycle-time benefits carry minimal operational downside, provided database transactional integrity is maintained.

Conversely, the most dangerous intervention is truncating rework loops to force bounded-evidence decisions (K-337). While it achieves dramatic cycle-time reductions—cutting out over 100 hours by bypassing weekend supplier dead-time—it transforms the evidentiary standard. For high-risk, high-value cases, suppressing verification of unresolved discrepancies exposes the organization to catastrophic financial loss, regulatory sanctions, and costly legal appeals. Speed is purchased by shifting operational risk into balance-sheet and legal liability.

The local counterfactual that is least transportable to a system-wide policy is substituting D1 with D2 for difficult documents (K-219), closely followed by reserving C1 capacity (K-104). In a single case, K-219 exploits an idle or shorter D2 queue without noticeable spillover. However, across 100 cases, routing difficult handwriting to a generalist creates a severe allocative distortion: D2 takes up to twice as long, saturating the D2 pool, stranding standard digital cases, and elevating rework rates that multiply total system load. Similarly, single-case C1 reservations appear frictionless but induce severe blocking and starvation when scaled across a solitary certified compliance reviewer.

To evaluate these interventions rigorously, the most valuable additional data are: (1) high-resolution micro-level queue entry and resource idle timestamps to distinguish true queuing latency from batch-logging artifacts; (2) task-level rework conditional probabilities segmented by document type and worker; and (3) real-time C1 queue abandonment and preemption logs to quantify the hidden collateral cost of capacity reservations on urgent unreserved cases.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural`: The normative control-flow prerequisites—specifically that `Register` precedes `Triage`, both `Identity Check` and `Document Check` precede `Assessment`, and `Decision` precedes `Notify`—remain invariant across all counterfactual executions, and parallelizing verification preserves the complete information set required for downstream assessment.
2. `structural`: The Four-Eyes principle ($\text{Resource}(\text{Final Assessment}) \ne \text{Resource}(\text{Final Compliance Review})$) is an absolute compliance constraint that cannot be relaxed, waived, or satisfied by preliminary advisory screening.
3. `temporal`: Queue snapshot estimates reflect prevailing Poisson arrival and service patterns, such that a case arriving at a queue experiences wait times proportional to the snapshot backlog, holding other case arrivals and service completions approximately constant under single-case counterfactuals.
4. `resource`: The compliance review resource pool is perfectly inelastic during the observation window, consisting exclusively of a single certified reviewer (C1), such that any capacity allocated or provisionally reserved for a target case directly subtracts from C1's available operating hours.
5. `quality`: Resource D2 has a substantially higher error and clarification-request probability on difficult handwritten documents (estimated at 20–30%) compared to D1 (estimated at <5%), whereas D1's processing time is lower due to domain specialization on complex documentation.
6. `interference`: Under single-case counterfactuals, system-wide interference is negligible and other cases proceed along their observed schedules; however, under scaled multi-case policy rollouts, local resource reallocations create queue spillover, displacing non-target cases and causing nonlinear queue expansion at bottleneck resources.
7. `behavioral`: Task timestamps in system logs reflect genuine service initiation and completion times rather than retrospective batch entry or administrative manipulation, ensuring that observed task durations represent true worker processing intervals.
8. `quality`: External response latency to `Request More Info` is exogenously determined by external party operating hours, exhibiting severe right-skewed delays across weekends (e.g., Friday-to-Monday gaps) that cannot be expedited by internal process interventions.
