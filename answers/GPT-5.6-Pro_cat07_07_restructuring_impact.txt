# 1. Mechanism map

The management narrative distinguishes **formal organizational handovers** from **recorded resource-to-resource transitions**. The proposed structure may reduce ad hoc cross-team transfers, yet the supplied routing estimates do not predict fewer recorded handovers overall.

A useful diagnostic is:

[
\text{handover-wait exposure index}
===================================

\sum_e
\left(
\text{frequency of edge }e
\times
\text{median wait after }e
\right)
]

This is not an estimate of total case delay: medians are not additive, cases may traverse several edges, and some work may overlap. It is nevertheless a useful directional stress indicator.

| Handover family               | Current frequency per 100 | Proposed frequency per 100 | Current exposure index | Proposed exposure index | Direction                            |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------: | -------------------------: | ---------------------: | ----------------------: | ------------------------------------ |
| Intake chain                  |                       180 |                        175 |                  124.4 |                   110.6 | Slight improvement                   |
| Technical referral and return |                        67 |                         77 |                  164.8 |                   243.2 | **+47.6%**                           |
| Approval referral and return  |                        81 |                         92 |                  159.3 |                   224.4 | **+40.9%**                           |
| Legal/Risk referral           |                         9 |                         12 |                   86.4 |                   126.0 | **+45.8%**                           |
| Cross-team transfer           |                         6 |                          3 |                   53.4 |                    18.0 | Strong improvement                   |
| **Total listed transitions**  |                   **343** |                    **359** |              **588.3** |               **722.2** | **+4.7% handovers; +22.8% exposure** |

Thus, the likely benefit is **fewer irregular cross-team handovers**, not necessarily fewer handovers in total. A shared queue can also make handovers appear to disappear if queue-entry, work-start, advice-ready, and advice-pulled events are not logged.

Under the simplifying assumption that N_Tech, S_Tech, and E_Tech are comparable one-FTE positions, their current mean utilization is approximately **73.7%**. A “15% increase” must be defined:

* A 15% relative increase gives about **84.7%**.
* A 15-percentage-point increase gives about **88.7%**.

The second interpretation leaves little operational slack. Neither interpretation justifies adding broad legal/risk and difficult-QC demand without a capacity analysis, particularly while Legal and Risk are already above 90%.

# 2. Scenario forecasts

The numerical ranges below are **planning hypotheses**, not fitted statistical forecasts. Current volume is 2,100 cases per month. The case-volume-weighted current rework and SLA miss rates are approximately 9.31% and 7.57%. A weighted average of team medians is about 7.46 days and of team p90 values about 18.64 days, but these are not true pooled quantiles.

| Scenario   | Indicative overall median | Indicative p90 | Rework rate | SLA miss rate |
| ---------- | ------------------------: | -------------: | ----------: | ------------: |
| Optimistic |                  6.9–7.3d |     16.5–18.5d |        8–9% |        6–7.5% |
| Central    |                  7.2–7.9d |         19–22d |     9.5–11% |      7.5–9.5% |
| Adverse    |                  8.2–9.5d |        24–30+d |      12–16% |        11–15% |

## Optimistic scenario

This scenario requires effective pooling, protected specialty capacity, explicit local ownership, accurate routing, and continued access to local technical consultation.

**Handover pattern.** Ad hoc cross-team transfers fall to approximately 2–3 per 100 cases. Technical and approval referrals become more standardized, but the total recorded handover count remains around 340–355 per 100 rather than falling dramatically. Unnecessary referral-return loops are prevented by good triage, and most advice returns to the original caseworker.

**Queue and waiting-time pattern.** Pooling smooths East’s overload without saturating the common pool. Technical Shared Service median waiting settles around 2.5–3.5 hours rather than the management estimate of 4.0 hours. Approval median waiting remains near 2.0–2.6 hours. Legal/Risk waiting remains around or below its current level because specialist triage is separated from substantive legal/risk work. Queue p90 values remain stable and backlog clears after ordinary peaks.

**Cycle-time distribution.** The organization-wide median falls modestly, but the more important result is a stable or lower p90. East improves most: its median could fall from 8.6 days toward 7.3–8.0 days and its p90 from 22.8 days toward approximately 18.5–21 days. North and South remain within roughly 0.3 days of their current medians rather than funding East’s improvement through deterioration.

**Rework and quality pattern.** Standard advice templates and broader specialist exposure reduce inconsistent decisions. Overall rework falls toward 8–9%, with no increase among complex or locally sensitive cases. Increased Legal/Risk referrals represent earlier detection rather than over-referral, evidenced by a high rate of materially useful advice and no subsequent quality penalty.

**Role clarity and accountability.** Each case retains a named local owner from intake to close. Shared specialists own only the advice task, not the entire case. Advice is returned to the same owner whenever possible, and the shared approval queue identifies a named approver and reason for decision.

**Fairness implications.** East gains without measurable harm to North or South. Conditional waiting and SLA gaps narrow after controlling for complexity, priority, and specialty. Customer segments requiring regional or language accommodation receive either local co-working or a designated specialist rather than an additional unowned handover.

**Early warning signs.** Optimism should be questioned if technical referrals exceed approximately 41 per 100 without falling rework, if p90 waiting rises while the median remains stable, if completed advice waits unclaimed, if North/South p90 cycle time rises, or if local technical exposure declines sharply.

## Central scenario

This is the most plausible scenario if the management routing table is approximately realized, pooling helps East, but local informal advice weakens and the shared queues operate close to capacity.

**Handover pattern.** The listed handovers rise to approximately 359 per 100 cases. Cross-team handovers fall, but technical round-trip transitions rise from 67 to 77 and approval-related transitions from 81 to 92. The handover network becomes visibly hub-and-spoke. At resource level, subcontracting loops may appear to decline when advice returns to a different caseworker, even though team-level referral-return loops increase.

**Queue and waiting-time pattern.** The proposed medians—4.0 hours into Technical Shared Service, 2.2 hours back to the local team, 3.0 hours into approval, and 10.5 hours into Legal/Risk—are approximately realized. The main deterioration appears above the median: p90 and p95 waiting increase during demand peaks, absences, and weeks with many complex cases.

**Cycle-time distribution.** Simple and highly automated cases improve slightly, so the mean or median may appear stable. High-complexity cases acquire additional waiting. A plausible organization-wide median is 7.2–7.9 days with p90 around 19–22 days. East improves to roughly 7.8–8.5 days, while North moves toward 7.0–7.6 and South toward 7.5–8.2. Aggregate convergence may therefore partly represent deterioration in stronger teams.

**Rework and quality pattern.** Standardization reduces some inconsistent approvals, but loss of informal technical advice and regional context creates more returned recommendations and difficult-QC failures. Overall rework rises modestly to 9.5–11%. Complex cases may rise by 2–4 percentage points even where the all-case rate changes little.

**Role clarity and accountability.** Formal roles look simpler in an organizational chart, and role discovery may identify fewer broad roles. Operational ownership is less clear: the local worker waits, the shared specialist advises, another local worker pulls the advice, and a shared approver completes the decision. `reassignment_count` and unique resources per case rise.

**Fairness implications.** East gains, but North and South lose some local advantage. High-complexity, lower-priority, and regionally sensitive segments wait longer. Priority routing may protect urgent cases while repeatedly deferring ordinary but difficult cases. Team differences narrow without necessarily representing Pareto improvement.

**Early warning signs.** The strongest signals are sustained shared-service utilization above approximately 85%, a rising p90-to-median waiting ratio, positive backlog slope, more advice returned to a different local worker, increasing `reassignment_count`, more generic handover reasons, referral growth without quality improvement, and falling local-expertise usage.

## Adverse scenario

This scenario occurs if the shared queue becomes a common bottleneck, the supervisor reduction is premature, and technical, legal/risk, and difficult-QC work compete without protected capacity.

**Handover pattern.** Formal cross-team transfers remain low, but hidden rerouting, queue bouncing, and reassignments increase total handovers to roughly 380–420 per 100 cases. The most common variants contain repeated specialist, approval, or Quality Check activities. The central service dominates handover-network centrality.

**Queue and waiting-time pattern.** Technical median waiting exceeds 6 hours and its p90 exceeds a normal working day. Approval median waiting exceeds 5 hours. Legal/Risk median waiting moves above approximately 14 hours, with a much longer tail. Arrivals repeatedly exceed completions, the oldest open item grows older each week, and priority work displaces lower-priority work rather than eliminating it.

**Cycle-time distribution.** Overall median cycle time rises to approximately 8.2–9.5 days, p90 reaches 24–30 days or more, and SLA misses rise toward 11–15%. East’s expected benefit disappears because it now shares a congested queue with North and South. North/South deterioration is especially visible in their p90 and p95 values.

**Rework and quality pattern.** Shared specialists provide shorter, less contextualized advice under load. Local workers misunderstand or incompletely implement it, Quality Check returns more cases, and supervisors send cases back for clarification. Rework reaches approximately 12–16%, with particularly high rates after legal/risk and complex technical handovers.

**Role clarity and accountability.** The queue becomes the de facto owner. Cases can sit in “advice complete” or “awaiting pull” states without a clearly accountable person. Approvers receive partially understood cases and local workers treat the shared service as responsible for downstream outcomes.

**Fairness implications.** High-priority cases remain relatively protected, while low-priority complex cases accumulate. Customer segments needing language or regional accommodation experience more handovers and repeated contact. Workers in North and South lose development opportunities, while shared specialists receive a disproportionate load of difficult and emotionally demanding work.

**Early warning signs.** These include daily backlog growth, increasing age of the oldest work item, utilization above 90%, repeated emergency reprioritization, a sharp rise in handover-reason categories such as “queue balancing” or “wrong queue,” more than one reassignment per affected case, rising incomplete-case inventory, and simultaneous p90 deterioration across all three teams.

# 3. Leading and lagging indicators

The existing schema has three important measurement gaps:

1. Completion timestamps alone do not separate queue waiting from active service. Add `Queue Entered`, `Work Started`, `Work Completed`, and `Advice Pulled`, or start/complete lifecycle timestamps.
2. There is no explicit case-owner field. Resource continuity and `reassignment_count` are only proxies for accountability.
3. There is no SLA deadline or SLA target field. Exact SLA misses require a case-level deadline or a documented mapping from priority/segment to target.

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1. **Tail latency.** A small improvement for ordinary cases can offset a large deterioration among the slowest 10% or 5%. The p90, p95, longest open-queue age, and conditional tails should be primary rollout measures.

2. **High-complexity case deterioration.** Automation may accelerate simple eligibility cases while every high-complexity case receives an additional central-queue wait. The overall average can improve even though the cases most dependent on expertise become slower.

3. **Completed-case and right-censoring bias.** Early post-change reports usually include cases that were easy enough to finish quickly. Congested central-queue cases remain open and are absent from completed-case averages. Intake-cohort survival curves and open-case age must accompany completed-case statistics.

4. **East gains masking North/South losses.** East starts with the highest median, p90, rework, SLA miss rate, and technical utilization. A substantial East improvement could numerically cancel moderate deterioration in North and South. This would be an aggregate improvement but not an unambiguously successful restructuring.

5. **Local knowledge loss.** Regional customer conventions may prevent misunderstanding before it becomes logged rework. Their loss may first appear as additional customer contacts, specialist clarification, or poorer satisfaction rather than immediate cycle-time deterioration.

6. **Fairness across customer segments.** Customers requiring language or regional accommodation may need more explanation and more transfers. A priority discipline may also repeatedly defer low-priority complex cases. Neither effect is visible in an unsegmented mean.

7. **Learning debt.** Routing hard work away from local teams can improve near-term throughput while reducing future technical capability. The cost appears months later as higher referral rates, lower resilience, and difficulty filling specialist roles internally.

8. **Accountability gaps.** The shared specialist can finish advice quickly while the case waits for someone to pull and interpret it. Specialist-task duration looks good, but end-to-end ownership deteriorates. Without a case-owner field, this can remain largely invisible.

9. **Exception-handling failure.** Standard cases may follow fewer and faster variants while unusual cases bounce between Technical Shared Service, Legal/Risk, approval, and Quality Check. Rare variants can be operationally costly despite having little effect on the mean.

10. **Common-mode resilience risk.** Centralization can reduce vulnerability to one local specialist’s absence while increasing vulnerability to a shared-service outage, simultaneous sickness, or a common demand surge. Ordinary-period averages do not measure disruption recovery.

11. **Misleading role simplification.** Role discovery may show fewer roles after centralization. That can mean standardization, but it can also mean a single broad shared role is performing technical, legal/risk triage, quality, and escalation work without clear boundaries.

12. **Utilization as a false success measure.** Higher utilization can coexist with lower system responsiveness. A specialist pool at 88–92% utilization may produce more completed specialist tasks while generating much longer queue tails and less capacity for urgent cases.

# 5. Pilot design

## Pilot scope and sequencing

The restructuring should be decomposed rather than launching technical centralization, difficult Quality Check, expanded Legal/Risk routing, supervisor removal, and shared approval simultaneously.

| Phase                                    | Scope                                                                                                                                                                                              | Purpose                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Instrumentation and shadow routing       | Add queue lifecycle, routing-rule, case-owner and capacity fields. Run proposed routing rules without changing actual assignment.                                                                  | Estimate demand by specialty, expected queue mix, misrouting and capacity requirements before operational exposure. |
| Phase 1: Technical Shared Service        | Pool technical specialists for a stratified subset of eligible high-complexity cases. Preserve local consultation sessions. Keep approval, difficult QC and Legal/Risk operating models unchanged. | Test the pooling-versus-local-expertise mechanism in isolation.                                                     |
| Phase 2: Shared approval                 | Introduce shared approval for a controlled subset while retaining current supervisor headcount during the test.                                                                                    | Test standardization and queue capacity before removing a position.                                                 |
| Phase 3: Quality and specialty expansion | Add difficult Quality Check and broader Legal/Risk triage only after capacity and waiting guardrails pass. Prefer skill-based virtual queues rather than one FIFO queue.                           | Avoid combining heterogeneous work before its demand and service-time distributions are understood.                 |
| Rollout gate                             | Expand by team or case stratum only after stable leading and mature lagging results.                                                                                                               | Prevent an attractive early average from triggering premature rollout.                                              |

## Comparison group

An East-only before/after pilot would be biased toward a positive result because East is currently the most capacity-constrained team. The comparison should include all three teams.

Where operationally permissible, eligible cases should be assigned using a stable case-ID hash within strata defined by:

* origin team;
* complexity;
* required specialty;
* priority;
* customer segment;
* local-expertise flag.

Approximately half of eligible cases can use the pilot model and half the current model. East may receive a larger pilot share because of its overload, but it should retain a contemporaneous comparison group.

If case-level randomization is not acceptable, use a **stepped-wedge design** in which teams or case strata enter the pilot in a predetermined order. Estimate effects using matched difference-in-differences rather than a simple pre/post comparison.

## Observation windows

Use:

* at least 12 weeks of pre-change data;
* a 2–4 week shadow or stabilization period;
* at least 12 weeks of active pilot data;
* a further 4–6 weeks for late cases, rework, and SLA outcomes to mature.

Leading queue indicators should be reviewed daily and weekly. Lagging metrics should be reported by intake cohort rather than by the calendar date on which cases happen to close.

## Required segmentation

Every principal result should be shown for:

* North, South and East separately;
* low, medium and high complexity;
* technical, legal, risk, security and no-specialty cases;
* customer segment;
* priority;
* location;
* `local_expertise_flag`;
* selected interactions, especially team×complexity, team×specialty, and customer-segment×complexity.

Any language or accommodation requirement should be logged explicitly rather than inferred only from customer segment or location.

## Initial guardrail metrics

The thresholds below are proposed operational gates and should be recalibrated using baseline confidence intervals.

| Guardrail                        | Initial trigger                                                                                                | Response                                                            |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Technical Shared Service waiting | Median above 4.0h, or p90 more than 20% above matched baseline for two consecutive weeks                       | Reduce routing scope, split queue, or add capacity                  |
| Backlog stability                | Arrivals exceed completions for 10 working days, or oldest-item age continuously increases                     | Pause expansion and clear backlog                                   |
| Specialist utilization           | Productive utilization above 88% for two weeks or above 92% for three working days                             | Add protected capacity; do not treat the level as a success         |
| Approval waiting                 | Median above 3.0h or p90 more than 25% above local baseline                                                    | Retain supervisor capacity or introduce value/complexity lanes      |
| Legal/Risk load                  | Median exceeds 10.5h, backlog grows, or utilization remains above 90%                                          | Do not broaden referrals without extra capacity or demand reduction |
| Total handovers                  | More than 10% above the current 343 per 100 cases                                                              | Investigate bounce, reassignment and pull behavior                  |
| Rework                           | Increase greater than 1 percentage point overall or 2 points in a major stratum                                | Review advice quality and restore local co-working                  |
| SLA misses                       | Increase greater than 1 point overall or 2 points for any major team/segment                                   | Pause rollout for the affected stratum                              |
| Tail cycle time                  | Team or high-complexity p90 increases more than 10%                                                            | Narrow routing or reserve expedited capacity                        |
| Fairness                         | Adjusted p90-wait gap rises more than 20%, or adjusted SLA gap rises more than 2 points                        | Modify priority/routing rules and add accommodation support         |
| Ownership                        | Named-owner coverage below 98%, same-owner return falls more than 5 points, or mean reassignment rises by 0.25 | Reinforce ownership rules and pull responsibilities                 |
| Local learning                   | Local difficult-case exposure falls more than 20% over a quarter without compensating training                 | Introduce rotations, co-working and mandatory feedback sessions     |

A severe breach—such as a rapidly growing mandatory Legal/Risk backlog or a twofold increase in a major segment’s p90—should permit immediate routing restriction rather than waiting for multiple reporting cycles.

## Staff feedback collection

Process metrics should be paired with structured operational evidence:

* a weekly five-question pulse for caseworkers, specialists and approvers covering role clarity, queue transparency, access to local advice, workload fairness and learning;
* weekly review of a sample of delayed, reworked, misrouted and accommodation-sensitive cases, linked to case IDs;
* short annotations asking whether local informal advice would have prevented the referral;
* monthly review of specialist workload mix, interruptions and emotionally difficult cases;
* customer-contact review for repeated explanations, language issues and inconsistent advice.

Feedback should be compared with logged behavior. For example, reported ownership ambiguity should be checked against advice-pull delay, resource changes, and reassignment counts.

## Rollback and adjustment triggers

Rollback need not mean abandoning pooling. Available responses include:

* restoring embedded specialist days or local office hours;
* splitting technical, legal/risk and difficult-QC work into skill-based virtual queues;
* reserving capacity for East or for accommodation-sensitive cases;
* capping new referrals temporarily;
* restoring the removed supervisor position;
* requiring advice to return to the named case owner;
* changing priority-aging rules so low-priority complex cases cannot starve;
* adding capacity before expanding Legal/Risk detection.

A sensible rule is to pause expansion after one severe guardrail breach or two moderate breaches persisting for two reporting weeks.

# 6. Recommendation

**Modify the proposal and pilot it first. Do not proceed directly to full rollout.**

There is a credible benefit: pooling can reduce East’s dependence on E_Tech, improve absence cover, reduce poor ad hoc cross-team transfers, and standardize some decisions. The current organization is not uniformly healthy—East and Legal/Risk already show capacity pressure.

However, the proposed routing estimates themselves do not support a broad claim of fewer handovers or less waiting:

* listed handovers rise from 343 to 359 per 100 cases;
* the handover-wait exposure index rises by approximately 23%;
* technical, approval and Legal/Risk referral exposure all increase;
* approval volume rises while a supervisor position is removed;
* Legal and Risk are already at 93% and 91% utilization.

The proposal should therefore be modified in five ways:

1. **Use skill-based virtual queues rather than one undifferentiated shared FIFO.** Technical assessment, substantive Legal/Risk work, and difficult Quality Check have different skills, priorities and service-time distributions. They can share routing infrastructure without sharing unrestricted capacity.

2. **Retain supervisor headcount during the approval pilot.** Capacity should be removed only after observed arrival rates, service times and tail waiting demonstrate that the shared queue has sufficient slack.

3. **Preserve a named local case owner.** The shared service should own an advice task, not the whole case. Advice should normally return to the same owner, with an explicit fallback if that person is absent.

4. **Preserve local expertise through rotations and co-working.** Local specialist office hours, joint review of complex cases, feedback on referrals, and temporary rotations into the shared service should be designed into the model rather than added after deskilling appears.

5. **Treat utilization as a constrained outcome, not the primary objective.** A 15% relative increase from the simple current technical average would put utilization around 84.7%; a 15-point increase would put it around 88.7%. Either must be accepted only if backlog, tail latency, quality, fairness and resilience remain within guardrails.

Full rollout should require stable leading indicators and mature outcome cohorts showing all of the following:

* East’s p90 and SLA performance improve materially;
* North and South meet non-deterioration guardrails;
* high-complexity and accommodation-sensitive cases do not suffer;
* rework and quality remain stable or improve;
* central queue backlog is non-growing;
* role clarity and same-owner return remain high;
* local skill exposure is preserved;
* absence and disruption tests show faster, not slower, recovery.

Management should **not** infer from an early improvement in average cycle time that the restructure has succeeded. Early averages can be dominated by simple cases, exclude still-open congested cases, hide p90 deterioration, mask North/South harm with East gains, and say nothing about local learning or resilience. Likewise, fewer formal cross-team handovers do not establish fewer total handovers, fewer discovered roles do not establish clearer accountability, and higher utilization does not establish higher end-to-end throughput.

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

PM4Py’s organizational-mining interfaces support resource handover, working-together, subcontracting, resource-similarity and role analyses. Handover-of-work is resource-oriented directly-follows behavior, working-together connects resources collaborating on cases, and activity-based similarity compares resource activity profiles. Its rework helper identifies cases in which an activity repeats. ([PM4Py][1])

The following is a PM4Py-style analysis sketch. Pin and test the PM4Py version used in production because simplified API signatures can change.

The supplied schema does not contain an SLA deadline, true activity start timestamps, explicit queue events, or a case-owner field. The sketch therefore:

* computes inter-event elapsed time but does not label it pure queue waiting;
* uses PM4Py service/waiting enrichment only when start timestamps are available;
* leaves SLA misses undefined until a deadline table is joined;
* uses resource continuity and reassignment counts as ownership proxies.

```python
from __future__ import annotations

from typing import Any, Iterable
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py


CASE = "case:concept:name"
ACTIVITY = "concept:name"
TIMESTAMP = "time:timestamp"
RESOURCE = "org:resource"
START_TIMESTAMP = "time:start_timestamp"

LOCAL_TEAMS = {"North", "South", "East"}

REQUIRED_COLUMNS = {
    CASE,
    ACTIVITY,
    TIMESTAMP,
    RESOURCE,
    "team",
    "location",
    "required_specialty",
    "priority",
    "case_complexity",
    "customer_segment",
    "handover_reason",
    "reassignment_count",
    "local_expertise_flag",
    "outcome",
    "rework_flag",
}

CASE_SEGMENTS = [
    "origin_team",
    "case_complexity",
    "required_specialty",
    "customer_segment",
    "priority",
    "location",
    "local_expertise_flag",
]


def first_non_null(series: pd.Series) -> Any:
    values = series.dropna()
    return values.iloc[0] if not values.empty else pd.NA


def last_non_null(series: pd.Series) -> Any:
    values = series.dropna()
    return values.iloc[-1] if not values.empty else pd.NA


def normalize_boolean(series: pd.Series) -> pd.Series:
    if pd.api.types.is_bool_dtype(series):
        return series.fillna(False)

    return (
        series.fillna(False)
        .astype(str)
        .str.strip()
        .str.lower()
        .isin({"true", "1", "yes", "y"})
    )


def load_log(path: str, period: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = pd.read_csv(path)

    missing = REQUIRED_COLUMNS.difference(df.columns)
    if missing:
        raise ValueError(f"{path} is missing required columns: {sorted(missing)}")

    df[TIMESTAMP] = pd.to_datetime(df[TIMESTAMP], utc=True, errors="raise")

    if START_TIMESTAMP in df.columns:
        df[START_TIMESTAMP] = pd.to_datetime(
            df[START_TIMESTAMP], utc=True, errors="raise"
        )

    if "sla_deadline" in df.columns:
        df["sla_deadline"] = pd.to_datetime(
            df["sla_deadline"], utc=True, errors="coerce"
        )

    df["period"] = period
    df["_source_order"] = np.arange(len(df))

    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id=CASE,
        activity_key=ACTIVITY,
        timestamp_key=TIMESTAMP,
    )

    df = df.sort_values(
        [CASE, TIMESTAMP, "_source_order"],
        kind="mergesort",
    ).reset_index(drop=True)

    # Origin team must be derived from the local part of the case.
    # Filtering directly on event-level "team" after centralization would
    # classify every shared-service case as belonging to the shared unit.
    local_events = df[df["team"].isin(LOCAL_TEAMS)]
    origin_team = (
        local_events.sort_values([CASE, TIMESTAMP])
        .groupby(CASE)["team"]
        .first()
    )
    df["origin_team"] = df[CASE].map(origin_team)

    # An explicit organizational-unit column is preferable.
    # This fallback retains bots/shared resources where team is missing.
    df["org_unit"] = df["team"].where(df["team"].notna(), df[RESOURCE])

    return df


before = load_log("before.csv", "before")
after = load_log("after.csv", "after")


def filter_cases(
    log: pd.DataFrame,
    filters: dict[str, Iterable[Any]],
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    PM4Py case-level event-attribute filtering.

    Case-level filtering is appropriate only where the attribute is
    semantically stable for the case. Use origin_team, not event-level team,
    for team-performance comparisons after centralization.
    """
    result = log

    for column, allowed in filters.items():
        allowed_set = set(allowed)
        result = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            result,
            attribute_key=column,
            values=allowed_set,
            level="case",
            retain=True,
            case_id_key=CASE,
        )

    return result


def case_metrics(log: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    x = log.sort_values([CASE, TIMESTAMP, "_source_order"]).copy()
    grouped = x.groupby(CASE, sort=False)

    result = pd.DataFrame(index=grouped.size().index)
    result.index.name = CASE

    result["start_time"] = grouped[TIMESTAMP].min()
    result["last_observed_time"] = grouped[TIMESTAMP].max()

    close_times = (
        x[x[ACTIVITY].eq("Close")]
        .groupby(CASE)[TIMESTAMP]
        .max()
    )
    result["close_time"] = close_times.reindex(result.index)
    result["is_closed"] = result["close_time"].notna()

    # Use closed cases for completed-cycle distributions.
    result["cycle_days"] = (
        result["close_time"] - result["start_time"]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 86400.0

    for column in [
        "origin_team",
        "case_complexity",
        "required_specialty",
        "customer_segment",
        "priority",
        "location",
        "local_expertise_flag",
    ]:
        result[column] = grouped[column].agg(first_non_null)

    result["outcome"] = grouped["outcome"].agg(last_non_null)
    result["reassignment_count"] = (
        grouped["reassignment_count"].max().fillna(0)
    )
    result["unique_resources"] = grouped[RESOURCE].nunique()

    explicit_rework = grouped["rework_flag"].apply(
        lambda s: normalize_boolean(s).any()
    )
    rework_activity = grouped[ACTIVITY].apply(
        lambda s: s.eq("Rework").any()
    )
    result["rework_case"] = explicit_rework | rework_activity

    # Direct resource changes.
    x["previous_resource"] = grouped[RESOURCE].shift(1)
    x["resource_change"] = (
        x["previous_resource"].notna()
        & x[RESOURCE].notna()
        & x[RESOURCE].ne(x["previous_resource"])
    )
    result["direct_handover_count"] = (
        x.groupby(CASE)["resource_change"]
        .sum()
        .reindex(result.index)
        .fillna(0)
    )

    # Exact SLA misses require a joined case-level deadline.
    if "sla_deadline" in x.columns:
        deadlines = grouped["sla_deadline"].agg(last_non_null)
        result["sla_deadline"] = pd.to_datetime(
            deadlines, utc=True, errors="coerce"
        )
        result["sla_miss"] = (
            result["is_closed"]
            & result["sla_deadline"].notna()
            & result["close_time"].gt(result["sla_deadline"])
        )
    else:
        result["sla_miss"] = pd.Series(
            pd.NA, index=result.index, dtype="boolean"
        )

    return result.reset_index()


def direct_handover_edges(log: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Resource-to-resource transitions.

    inter_event_hours is time from the source event timestamp to the next
    event timestamp. With completion-only timestamps, it combines queue
    waiting and the next activity's service time.
    """
    x = log.sort_values([CASE, TIMESTAMP, "_source_order"]).copy()
    grouped = x.groupby(CASE, sort=False)

    x["to_resource"] = grouped[RESOURCE].shift(-1)
    x["to_activity"] = grouped[ACTIVITY].shift(-1)
    x["to_time"] = grouped[TIMESTAMP].shift(-1)
    x["to_team"] = grouped["team"].shift(-1)
    x["to_unit"] = grouped["org_unit"].shift(-1)

    handovers = x[
        x["to_resource"].notna()
        & x[RESOURCE].notna()
        & x[RESOURCE].ne(x["to_resource"])
    ].copy()

    handovers["inter_event_hours"] = (
        handovers["to_time"] - handovers[TIMESTAMP]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0

    handovers = handovers.rename(
        columns={
            RESOURCE: "from_resource",
            ACTIVITY: "from_activity",
            "team": "from_team",
            "org_unit": "from_unit",
        }
    )

    case_rework = case_metrics(log)[[CASE, "rework_case"]]
    handovers = handovers.merge(case_rework, on=CASE, how="left")

    n_cases = max(log[CASE].nunique(), 1)

    # First aggregate within edge-case so that rework rates are case-weighted.
    edge_cases = (
        handovers.groupby(
            ["from_resource", "to_resource", CASE],
            dropna=False,
            as_index=False,
        )
        .agg(
            transition_count=(CASE, "size"),
            median_inter_event_hours=("inter_event_hours", "median"),
            rework_case=("rework_case", "max"),
        )
    )

    edge_summary = (
        edge_cases.groupby(
            ["from_resource", "to_resource"],
            dropna=False,
            as_index=False,
        )
        .agg(
            events=("transition_count", "sum"),
            cases=(CASE, "nunique"),
            median_inter_event_hours=(
                "median_inter_event_hours", "median"
            ),
            p90_inter_event_hours=(
                "median_inter_event_hours",
                lambda s: s.quantile(0.90),
            ),
            rework_rate_after_edge=("rework_case", "mean"),
        )
    )

    edge_summary["events_per_100_cases"] = (
        100.0 * edge_summary["events"] / n_cases
    )
    return edge_summary


def activity_edge_performance(log: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Quantiles for directly-following activity pairs. This complements
    discover_performance_dfg, whose aggregation may be version-dependent.
    """
    x = log.sort_values([CASE, TIMESTAMP, "_source_order"]).copy()
    grouped = x.groupby(CASE, sort=False)

    x["next_activity"] = grouped[ACTIVITY].shift(-1)
    x["next_time"] = grouped[TIMESTAMP].shift(-1)
    x["elapsed_hours"] = (
        x["next_time"] - x[TIMESTAMP]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0

    edges = x[x["next_activity"].notna()]

    return (
        edges.groupby([ACTIVITY, "next_activity"], as_index=False)
        .agg(
            frequency=(CASE, "size"),
            cases=(CASE, "nunique"),
            mean_elapsed_hours=("elapsed_hours", "mean"),
            median_elapsed_hours=("elapsed_hours", "median"),
            p90_elapsed_hours=(
                "elapsed_hours",
                lambda s: s.quantile(0.90),
            ),
            p95_elapsed_hours=(
                "elapsed_hours",
                lambda s: s.quantile(0.95),
            ),
        )
        .sort_values("frequency", ascending=False)
    )


def role_entropy(log: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Activity entropy is a role-breadth indicator, not a complete role-clarity
    measure. High entropy may mean flexibility or uncontrolled role expansion.
    """
    counts = pd.crosstab(log[RESOURCE], log[ACTIVITY])
    probabilities = counts.div(counts.sum(axis=1), axis=0)
    entropy = -(probabilities * np.log2(probabilities.where(
        probabilities > 0
    ))).sum(axis=1)

    return pd.DataFrame(
        {
            RESOURCE: entropy.index,
            "activity_entropy": entropy.values,
            "activity_count": (counts > 0).sum(axis=1).values,
            "event_count": counts.sum(axis=1).values,
        }
    )


def variant_count(value: Any) -> int:
    # PM4Py can return counts or collections depending on log representation.
    if isinstance(value, (int, np.integer)):
        return int(value)
    return len(value)

    unit_handover_network = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(

    top_variant_log = pm4py.filter_variants(

    return {

def summarize_slice(
    log: pd.DataFrame,
    period: str,
    slice_name: str,
) -> dict[str, Any]:
    cases = case_metrics(log)
    closed = cases[cases["is_closed"]].copy()

    sla_observed = cases["sla_miss"].notna()

    return {
        "period": period,
        "slice": slice_name,
        "cases": len(cases),
        "closed_cases": len(closed),
        "cycle_mean_days": closed["cycle_days"].mean(),
        "cycle_p50_days": closed["cycle_days"].quantile(0.50),
        "cycle_p90_days": closed["cycle_days"].quantile(0.90),
        "cycle_p95_days": closed["cycle_days"].quantile(0.95),
        "rework_rate": cases["rework_case"].mean(),
        "sla_miss_rate": (
            cases.loc[sla_observed, "sla_miss"].astype(float).mean()
            if sla_observed.any()
            else np.nan
        ),
        "mean_direct_handovers": cases["direct_handover_count"].mean(),
        "p90_direct_handovers": cases[
            "direct_handover_count"
        ].quantile(0.90),
        "mean_unique_resources": cases["unique_resources"].mean(),
        "mean_reassignments": cases["reassignment_count"].mean(),
    }


def one_way_slice_specs(log: pd.DataFrame):
    yield "all", {}

    for column in CASE_SEGMENTS:
        values = log[column].dropna().unique()
        for value in values:
            yield f"{column}={value}", {column: {value}}

    # Selected interaction slices should be specified rather than producing
    # every possible sparse combination.
    for team in sorted(LOCAL_TEAMS):
        for complexity in log["case_complexity"].dropna().unique():
            yield (
                f"team={team}|complexity={complexity}",
                {
                    "origin_team": {team},
                    "case_complexity": {complexity},
                },
            )


comparison_rows: list[dict[str, Any]] = []

for period, log in [("before", before), ("after", after)]:
    for slice_name, specification in one_way_slice_specs(log):
        sliced = filter_cases(log, specification) if specification else log

        if sliced[CASE].nunique() == 0:
            continue

        comparison_rows.append(
            summarize_slice(sliced, period, slice_name)
        )

comparison = pd.DataFrame(comparison_rows)

# Side-by-side before/after outcome comparison.
comparison_wide = comparison.pivot(
    index="slice",

        "mean_unique_resources",
        "mean_reassignments",
    ],
)

# Resource-edge comparison.

after_edges = direct_handover_edges(after).add_suffix("_after")

edge_comparison = before_edges.merge(
    after_edges,

for segment in after["customer_segment"].dropna().unique():

    customer_segment_reports[segment] = {
```

Without these fields, a process-mining analysis can detect slower transitions and more resource changes, but it cannot reliably distinguish active work from queue waiting, identify unowned queue states, calculate exact utilization, or determine true SLA misses.
