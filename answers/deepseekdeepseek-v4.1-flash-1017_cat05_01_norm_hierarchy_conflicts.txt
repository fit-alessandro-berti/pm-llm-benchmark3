## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts treated as given.** GPIP-4 is a board-approved global policy effective before all relevant acts. Aachen SOP DE-AAC-PROC-17 Rev. 6 is a controlled local procedure. ECD-2 is a joint COO/Incident Commander directive effective 2026-07-14 09:00 through 2026-07-18 18:00 with a stated scope of critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the contamination event. The CFO email is not a controlled policy document. The CRO did not issue or ratify an A7 exception. The ERP outage and possible clock errors affect evidential weight, not the underlying normative rules.

**Interpretive assumptions and method.**
1. **Authority.** GPIP-4 controls Northbridge-wide substantive integrity, compliance, segregation, and threshold approvals. Local SOPs may be stricter or operationalize evidence, but cannot silently weaken GPIP-4. ECD-2 can act as an incident overlay for local operational workflow because B7 contemplates corporate incident directives superseding “ordinary local workflow steps,” but ECD-2 is not, on its face, a GPIP-4 A7 CRO exception. The CFO email can interpret Finance-control mechanics within the CFO’s authority, but it is low-grade evidence and cannot waive Compliance controls or GPIP-4 segregation of duties.
2. **Specificity and issue dimension.** I do not apply a single total hierarchy. For GPIP-4 integrity/compliance/segregation, GPIP-4 and a valid A7 exception dominate. For local PO timing and approved-supplier onboarding within ECD-2 scope/time, ECD-2 can override SOP B1/B3 through B7/C4. For Finance approvals, CFO D1 may satisfy or clarify Finance mechanics only within its stated ECD window and EUR 150,000 cap, subject to GPIP-4 A1/A3 limits.
3. **Later-in-time is not automatically higher.** ECD-2 is later than SOP but does not defeat GPIP-4 merely by being newer. CFO D1 is later than both but is uncontrolled email.
4. **Emergency necessity.** Emergency necessity can excuse only the specific controls covered by a valid emergency norm. It does not create a general waiver of Finance, Compliance, new-supplier, or segregation controls.
5. **Scope and time.** ECD-2 applies only to acts within its window and stated scope. “Critical” is objective enough to require a documented link to containment of the current contamination event; an internal label alone is not conclusive. Backup contingency stock expected to last three days is presumptively outside ECD-2.
6. **Retrospective evidence.** A late ERP timestamp is not conclusive when contemporaneous evidence is stronger. However, later approval does not automatically cure a missing pre-commitment approval; it mitigates.
7. **Burden of proof.** The party relying on an exception bears the burden to show valid authority, scope, timing, and evidence. Where a mandatory pre-commitment GPIP-4 control is plainly missing, I classify as noncompliant. Where the decisive fact is genuinely unprovable from the record, I note the evidence gap and state what would reverse the assessment.
8. **Severity scale used.** High = missing mandatory GPIP-4 pre-commit control, high amount, new/unapproved supplier, or scope abuse. Medium = local approval/order/authorization breach or ECD extension defect. Low = documentation/logging or otherwise justified timing deviation.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Incident Overlay and Exception Clause.**

1. **Authority.** Only the Chief Risk Officer may suspend GPIP-4 controls, by a written, scoped, time-limited exception expressly citing the affected GPIP-4 clauses. A joint COO/Incident Commander directive may modify ordinary local workflow timing and supplier-onboarding steps, but it does not waive GPIP-4 segregation of duties, Compliance clearance, Finance Controller approval, or new-supplier controls unless the CRO co-signs and expressly identifies each waived clause.

2. **Scope.** Every incident directive must define the covered products, cost centers, suppliers, time window, and objective “critical” criteria, such as “required within 24 hours to prevent a confirmed sterilization-line stoppage or imminent safety harm.” Backup or contingency stock not needed within the stated horizon is outside scope. Facilities, cafeteria, and non-production purchases are outside incident procurement authority absent a separate valid emergency.

3. **Thresholds and segregation.** The requester or committer may never be the final business approver. For purchases EUR50,000, an independent budget owner and Finance Controller must approve before supplier commitment. For purchases >EUR100,000 or any new supplier, Compliance must clear before supplier commitment. Incident authorization by two IC members may substitute for operational approvals only where the CFO has adopted a controlled written delegation and only up to EUR150,000. It never substitutes for Compliance clearance or segregation. Above EUR150,000, Legal clearance is additional and does not waive Finance, Compliance, or segregation controls.

4. **Records and evidence.** Verbal emergency authorization is allowed only to prevent imminent harm or line stoppage. It must be recorded within four hours in a controlled incident log or signed electronic record. A queued signed PDF, telephone recording, supplier acknowledgment, or access log may rebut an ERP timestamp during a documented outage or clock error. A purchase order must be created immediately after the commitment and no later than the next business day or the end of the outage, whichever is sooner.

5. **Expiry and extension.** Incident directives expire automatically at the stated time. Extensions must be written, signed by the original authorities, and—if any GPIP-4 control is affected—co-signed by the CRO. Verbal extensions are invalid. Post-expiry acts default to normal GPIP-4 and local SOP requirements; retrospective approval may mitigate but does not cure a missing pre-commitment control.

6. **Anti-normalization safeguard.** Incident procurement shall be monitored by amount, count, supplier status, and repeated reliance on the same exception. A daily CRO/Compliance/Finance review is required for all emergency purchases; cumulative emergency spend above a defined threshold or repeated use for the same category automatically triggers a full audit and expiry/review of the incident overlay. Emergency status cannot become an alternative standard operating model.
