## 1. Modeling assumptions
- **Object types and their capitalization**: Order, Item, Package, Delivery, Invoice, Payment. All object types are capitalized to maintain clean categorical consistency.
- **E2O qualifier vocabulary**: We use active verbs when the event operates directly on the object ('creates', 'adds', 'reserves', 'packs', 'dispatches', 'issues', 'records', 'confirms', 'closes') and 'relates_to' when an object provides necessary contextual linkage (e.g., packing a package operationally 'relates_to' the corresponding Order). 
- **O2O direction conventions**: The relations move hierarchically from the container/aggregate to the constituents. For example, Order -> Item ('contains'), Package -> Item ('contains'), Delivery -> Package ('carries'), Invoice -> Order ('bills'), and Payment -> Invoice ('partially_settles' or 'completes_settlement').
- **Logistics and financial event relations**: We relate logistics and financial events directly only to their immediate physical or financial documents (e.g., 'Confirm Delivery' strictly to the Delivery, 'Record Payment' strictly to Payment and Invoice). We avoid propagating these back to Orders in the E2O tables, relying instead on structural O2O mapping to deduce analytical connections.
- **Prevention of analytical relation propagation**: By maintaining a strict "source-minimal" E2O policy downstream, we avoid injecting synthetic business facts into the event log. If 'Record Payment' was directly mapped to an Item, it would falsely imply the source system records payments at the line-item level.
- **Version-dependent PM4Py assumption**: PM4Py handles `OCEL` instantiation differently across major versions (v2.7+ vs v3.0+). We assume either a constructor passing dataframes as kwargs is available, or we gracefully fallback to instantiating an empty `OCEL` object and directly assigning the dataframe attributes.

## 3. Data-model audit
- **Object Types & Objects**: 
  - `Order`: O100, O200
  - `Item`: I1, I2, I3
  - `Package`: P1, P2
  - `Delivery`: D1, D2
  - `Invoice`: V1
  - `Payment`: M1, M2
- **O2O Qualifiers & Cardinality**: 
  - `contains`: 6 (Orders to Items, Packages to Items)
  - `carries`: 2 (Deliveries to Packages)
  - `bills`: 2 (Invoice to Orders)
  - `partially_settles`: 1 (Payment to Invoice)
  - `completes_settlement`: 1 (Payment to Invoice)
- **Activity Touches**:
  - `Create Order`: Order
  - `Add Item`: Order, Item
  - `Reserve Stock`: Order, Item
  - `Pack Package`: Package, Item, Order
  - `Dispatch Package`: Package, Delivery
  - `Issue Invoice`: Invoice, Order
  - `Record Payment`: Payment, Invoice
  - `Confirm Delivery`: Delivery
  - `Close Order`: Order
- **Consequential Events**: E7 (`Pack Package` for P1) and E6 (`Reserve Stock`) are highly consequential. By binding Items, Packages, and two independent Orders (O100, O200) simultaneously, these events act as powerful convergence points. They force the OC-DFG and Petri Net to merge independent order lifecycles into a synchronized physical logistics thread before they branch again.

## 4. Expected flattened behavior
- **Order Flattening**:
  - **Trace shape**: 2 traces (O100, O200) with lengths of ~8 and ~5 events respectively.
  - **Copied shared events**: `Reserve Stock` and `Issue Invoice` will be copied into both traces.
  - **Omitted lifecycle aspects**: `Confirm Delivery` and `Record Payment` are entirely omitted because they lack direct relation to `Order`.
  - **Likely DFG edges**: Create Order -> Add Item -> Reserve Stock -> Pack Package -> Issue Invoice -> Close Order.
  - **Misleading interpretations**: The duplication of `Issue Invoice` in flattened logs heavily implies two invoices were issued, doubling the perceived billing activity and potentially inflating cost analysis.
- **Package Flattening**:
  - **Trace shape**: 2 traces (P1, P2), lengths of 2.
  - **Copied shared events**: None.
  - **Omitted lifecycle aspects**: Picking/Item addition, invoicing, payments, and ultimate delivery confirmations.
  - **Likely DFG edges**: Pack Package -> Dispatch Package.
  - **Misleading interpretations**: Hides the complex, multi-origin order logic inside P1 (combining different orders), looking like a trivial linear flow.
- **Delivery Flattening**:
  - **Trace shape**: 2 traces (D1, D2), lengths of 2.
  - **Copied shared events**: None.
  - **Omitted lifecycle aspects**: All upstream fulfillment and downstream financial flows.
  - **Likely DFG edges**: Dispatch Package -> Confirm Delivery.
  - **Misleading interpretations**: Looks like an isolated, siloed process completely divorced from customer demand.
- **Invoice Flattening**:
  - **Trace shape**: 1 trace (V1).
  - **Copied shared events**: None.
  - **Omitted lifecycle aspects**: The entire physical fulfillment and dispatching.
  - **Likely DFG edges**: Issue Invoice -> Record Payment -> Record Payment.
  - **Misleading interpretations**: Completely blind to the operational execution delays that likely triggered the invoice.
- **Payment Flattening Weakness**: Flattening on Payment yields two traces (M1, M2) each of length 1 (`Record Payment`). A single-event trace generates zero edges in a DFG. It fails to capture any process sequence or lifecycle, proving that Payments are mere state transitions on an Invoice, not a standalone lifecycle.

## 5. Expected object-centric structures
- **OC-DFG**: Shows clear path divergencies and convergencies, colored by object type. You will see green (Order) edges converging alongside red (Item) edges into the `Reserve Stock` and `Pack Package` nodes, then branching out. `Issue Invoice` acts as another massive sync node before handing off entirely to blue (Invoice) edges toward `Record Payment`.
- **OTG (Object Type Graph)**: Visualizes the static schema directly reflecting the O2O mapping: Order connects to Item, Package connects to Item, Delivery connects to Package, Invoice connects to Order, and Payment connects to Invoice.
- **ET–OT**: A bipartite graph effectively summarizing the touchpoints modeled in the E2O relations (e.g., `Pack Package` drawing edges to `Package`, `Item`, and `Order`).
- **Object-centric Petri net**: Discovers variable arcs and synchronized transitions. Tokens belonging to O100 and O200 will be consumed simultaneously by the `Reserve Stock` transition and subsequently the `Pack Package` transition for P1. M1 and M2 act as sequential, single-token consumptions updating V1's downstream marking.
- **Object-interactions summary**: Will expose how frequently O100 and O200 cross paths. It will surface high coupling among O100, O200, P1, and V1, demonstrating that the objects do not run in isolated tracks but are deeply interdependent.

## 6. Modeling alternatives
1. **Source-minimal relations (Strict isolation)**: Events only link to the most immediate physical/data object they touch (e.g., `Issue Invoice` relates ONLY to `Invoice`, not `Order`).
   - *Impact on discovery*: Produces highly disconnected OC-DFG components (a finance island, a logistics island). Control flow across systems can only be reconstructed by heavily relying on the static O2O graph. 
2. **Full operational involvement (Maximal E2O)**: Every downstream event is forcibly mapped to every upstream object (e.g., `Confirm Delivery` gets an explicit relation to the Delivery, the Packages, the Items, and the Orders).
   - *Impact on discovery*: Massive edge inflation in the OC-DFG. All object lifecycles appear perfectly synchronized until the end, generating a highly tangled, spaghetti-like visual model. However, flattened event logs are extremely rich.
3. **O2O-propagated analytical relations as Events**: Treat static O2O facts as instantaneous events (e.g., introducing a synthetic `Link Invoice to Order` activity).
   - *Impact on discovery*: Inflates the event count, but strictly temporalizes hierarchical structures. It makes the exact moment an object joins another discoverable in a standard DFG, but risks conflating data-entry operations with true business execution.

## 7. Failure modes
1. **Relation inflation**: Over-associating every event with all downstream/upstream objects creates visual spaghetti in the OC-DFG, making it unreadable.
2. **Missing direct E2O links**: Failing to link `Pack Package P1` explicitly to both O100 and O200 breaks the shared synchronization requirement, hiding the fact that two orders merged here.
3. **Treating O2O propagation as observation**: Treating structural hierarchies (like a Delivery containing a Package) as temporal events misrepresents static supply-chain architecture as instantaneous state changes.
4. **Duplicate shared events after flattening**: As highlighted, `Issue Invoice` will appear in both O100 and O200 traces, which naive analytics might sum, falsely doubling expected revenue.
5. **Ambiguous payment allocation**: Payments M1 and M2 settle V1, which bills O100 and O200. Without line-item splits on the payments, mapping the exact revenue strictly to O100 vs O200 is analytically ambiguous.
6. **Overly short Payment traces**: Treating structural payment objects as process lifecycles breaks sequential miners since they yield traces of length 1.
7. **Constructor/API version mismatch**: Hardcoding `OCEL(events=..., ...)` kwargs crashes on newer PM4Py versions that expect different initialization structures, disrupting the pipeline.
8. **False total order for same-time events**: If events were batched on identical timestamps (e.g., 00:00:00), flattening might arbitrarily order them, misleading miners into deducing causal dependencies where none exist.

## 8. Final semantic checks
1. **Event Count Check**: The `events` DataFrame must contain exactly 17 rows, ensuring no duplicate timestamps or missed actions.
2. **Synchronized Reservation**: The `relations` DataFrame for `E6` (`Reserve Stock`) must contain exactly four rows mapping to I1, I3, O100, and O200.
3. **Cross-Order Packing**: The `relations` DataFrame for `E7` (`Pack Package` P1) must contain five rows to capture P1, I1, I3, O100, and O200.
4. **O2O Edge Integrity**: The `o2o` DataFrame must contain exactly 12 relations, spanning Items to Orders/Packages, Packages to Deliveries, and Invoices/Payments.
5. **No Synthetic Propagation**: The `Confirm Delivery` events (`E13`, `E14`) must only relate to `Delivery` objects in the `relations` DataFrame, verifying adherence to the downstream source-minimal policy.
6. **Payment Amounts Preservation**: The `events` DataFrame must contain non-null `amount` values (100.0, 150.0) precisely at `E12` and `E15`, verifying that financial facts weren't abstracted away into purely structural `object` tables.
