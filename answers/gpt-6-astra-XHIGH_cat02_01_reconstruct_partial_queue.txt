# 1. Assumptions and notation

The evidence supports **early queueing pressure at S1, followed by an increasingly important S2 constraint**. Missing starts change the allocation of time between waiting and service; they do not change station residence times or total WIP. The reconstruction below separates nominal rounded-time calculations from timestamp uncertainty and future, unobserved time.

- **A1 — Coverage.** Treat the 22 supplied cases as the complete relevant cohort, with no additional work already present at the beginning of the window. Nominally, both stations are empty immediately before 08:00. Without this coverage assumption, the reconstructed quantities describe the supplied cases only; facility-wide WIP and workload could be larger. P01's actual arrival could be slightly before 08:00 because of rounding; exposure calculations clip to the observation window.
- **A2 — Clock and station boundaries.** Assume the external-arrival clock and operational clock are synchronized apart from the stated rounding. S1 entry is external arrival; S2 entry is S1 completion, as specified. An unknown offset between systems would introduce additional uncertainty into S1 waits and total case sojourns. No extra transport duration is inserted.
- **A3 — Resource execution.** Use strict, nonpreemptive, single-task execution on each named resource, with no undocumented assistance or migration. Treat a start-to-completion interval as resource-occupied service. This is an estimate of active service under the assumption that no unrecorded pauses release the resource. Actual hands-on effort cannot be distinguished from occupied interval time using these data alone.
- **A4 — Rounding and calendars.** Each recorded event time has a bounded error of approximately ±2.5 minutes. Nominal calculations use the reported times. Uncertainty calculations preserve case precedence, resource nonoverlap and availability; errors are not assumed independent or uniformly distributed. The 14:00 status observation is authoritative. The main availability denominator treats the supplied C2 maintenance calendar, [10:55, 11:10), as exact; Section 6 also considers rounding of that calendar.
- **A5 — Event ordering.** Use half-open queue and service intervals. At a shared reported timestamp, process completions/resource releases, then arrivals or transfers, then starts; report the state after the entire timestamp group. Thus a completion and next start at 11:00 can use the same resource without nominal overlap. Do not count artificial peaks between tied events. Rate bins are [left, right); their conservation identity uses states immediately before the bin boundaries. Snapshot tables instead show states after all events at their displayed time.
- **A6 — Missing starts and discipline.** Bound each missing start using its queue entry, preceding completion on the same resource, own completion, subsequent work and maintenance. Allow zero service as a mathematical limiting bound because no positive minimum is supplied; it is not a claim that an activity took no time. Do not impose FIFO. The observed S2 order includes overtaking.
- **A7 — Imputation scenarios.** Use earliest-feasible, interval-midpoint and latest-feasible missing starts. The midpoint is a reproducible central sensitivity calculation, not a fitted expectation. Choices may vary independently across missing activities. These scenarios and rounding envelopes are not confidence intervals, and no missing-at-random assumption is made.
- **A8 — Censoring.** Keep OPEN and waiting cases in all exposure and occupancy calculations. For OPEN work, report elapsed service separately from unknown final service. For waiting cases, report elapsed waiting separately from unknown final waiting. No future completion, start, positive minimum remaining duration or finite upper duration is assumed. A case still at S1 has not yet entered S2.

Unless otherwise stated, durations are minutes, rates are cases/hour, and busy time is resource-minutes. “Central” means A7's midpoint calculation with nominal timestamps.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

For case i at station s, let e be station entry, b service start and c service completion. For completed, started work:

- Queue waiting: w = b − e.
- Active-service estimate: v = c − b, under A3.
- Station sojourn: r = c − e = w + v.
- Completed-case total sojourn: S2 completion − external arrival = w₁ + v₁ + w₂ + v₂.

A missing start therefore makes waiting and service uncertain in opposite directions, with their sum fixed at the recorded station sojourn. Their separate extrema must not be added as though independent.

For an OPEN activity, replace c by 14:00 **only when measuring observed exposure**, giving elapsed service 14:00 − b and elapsed station sojourn 14:00 − e. The true final durations exceed these elapsed quantities. For an entered but unstarted activity, observed waiting is 14:00 − e and observed service is zero; eventual service remains unknown.

A nonnegative duration computed from two rounded endpoints has an approximate envelope [max(0, d − 5), d + 5]. An elapsed duration measured to the exact cutoff has an envelope [max(0, d − 2.5), d + 2.5]. These are marginal bounds; shared endpoints and resource constraints couple them.

**Missing-start constraints.** The following bounds use the fixed recorded resource assignments and A3. “Next start” checks compatibility of the known completion with following work; it does not force immediate dispatch.

| Activity | Queue entry | Preceding same-resource completion | Next same-resource start | Nominal feasible start | Marginal start envelope with rounding |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P05, S2, C1 | 09:25 | P03, 09:45 | P07, 10:20 | 09:45–10:15 | 09:42:30–10:17:30 |
| P07, S1, I1 | 08:45 | P05, 09:25 | P09, 10:05 | 09:25–10:00 | 09:22:30–10:02:30 |
| P11, S2, C2 | 11:05 | P08, 12:00 | P13, 12:30 | 12:00–12:25 | 11:57:30–12:27:30 |
| P15, S1, I1 | 10:10 | P13, 11:40 | P17, 12:20 | 11:40–12:15 | 11:37:30–12:17:30 |

In particular, P11's S2 start cannot simply be placed at its 11:05 queue entry or the 11:10 maintenance end: C2 subsequently executes P08 through 12:00, before P11's known completion. A nonpreemptive P11 interval ending at 12:25 must follow P08.

For any time t:

- Qₛ(t) counts entered cases whose service has not started.
- Bₛ(t) counts started cases whose service has not completed.
- Lₛ(t) = Qₛ(t) + Bₛ(t) is station WIP, including service.
- Total system WIP is L₁(t) + L₂(t), equivalently external arrivals minus S2 completions under A1. An S1-to-S2 transfer does not change total system WIP.

Build these step functions from entry, start and completion events. Time averages use integrals over the 360-minute window, not averages of event-time snapshots. Queue area is the sum of clipped queue-interval lengths; busy area is the sum of clipped occupied intervals; WIP area is their sum. Missing starts redistribute queue and busy area without changing WIP area.

For resource j, busy time is the length of the **union** of its occupied intervals inside the window. Utilization is busy time divided by available resource time. Available times are 360 minutes for I1, I2 and C1, and 345 for C2. Station denominators are therefore 720 for S1 and 705 for S2. Maintenance is unavailable capacity, not busy service.

For [u, v), count station entries Aₛ and completed services Dₛ. Arrival rate is Aₛ/(v − u), and departure/throughput rate is Dₛ/(v − u), with time expressed in hours. Service starts are a separate flow: they remove cases from the queue but not station WIP.

Little's Law, L = λW, requires a matched population, boundary and time basis, with suitable stability or boundary treatment. No subperiod here convincingly supports a stationary queue approximation. Section 4 instead checks the exact finite-window occupancy identity, explicitly retaining censored residence time.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

**Observed early pressure is strongest at S1.** In 08:00–10:00, external arrivals are 6.5/hour while S1 completions are 3/hour. S1 waiting grows from 5 minutes for P01/P02 to 50–70 minutes for several subsequent cases. Its central mean queue is about 3.35 during this block, versus 0.71 at S2. This is evidence of an arrival burst exceeding S1's delivered output, not proof that S1 is the permanent limiting station.

**S2 becomes increasingly constraining as work moves downstream.** During 10:00–12:00, central utilization is about 80% at S1 and 89% of available capacity at S2. S2 receives seven cases and completes five. During 12:00–14:00, S1 receives only one new case and completes seven, draining its upstream backlog. S2 receives seven and completes six; its central mean queue rises to about 3.73, compared with 3.29 at S1. S2 ends with four cases waiting and two active.

Recorded S2 services are generally longer than S1 services. Using recorded closed-interval means solely as an illustrative service-capacity calculation, two resources could provide about 2 × 60/28.1 = 4.3 services/hour at S1 and 2 × 60/40 = 3.0 at S2, before dispatch gaps, maintenance and case-mix effects. This supports S2 as a plausible sustained capacity constraint after the initial surge, but these figures are not measured attainable capacities. The finite window does not identify a unique permanent bottleneck.

The best-supported interpretation is therefore **time-varying queueing pressure: an early S1 accumulation, overlapping constraints around late morning, and a persistent downstream S2 backlog as S1 drains**. A larger upstream queue and a slower downstream station can coexist. Full-window utilization alone is insufficient to decide the bottleneck because startup, missing starts, censoring and lost availability affect that comparison.

**Maintenance matters, but does not explain all S2 delay.** The stated outage removes 15 resource-minutes, about 2.1% of S2's nominal 720-minute capacity, and halves instantaneous capacity for 15 minutes. Immediately after 10:55, P08, P09 and P10 are waiting at S2 while P07 occupies C1. P09 starts on C1 at 11:00; P11 joins the queue at 11:05. C2 resumes recorded service with P08 at 11:15.

C2's nominal gap from P06's 10:50 completion to P08's 11:15 start is 25 minutes: 15 of maintenance and 10 outside maintenance. The 10 outside minutes may reflect handling, dispatch or logging; their cause is not observed. Under a counterfactual of immediate dispatch and no outage, P08 could have started at 10:50, but assigning the entire 25-minute improvement to maintenance would also remove those other gaps. The outage represents about 0.38 of a 40-minute service in workload terms, not an identified number of lost completions or a fixed delay to subtract from every case.

**Dispatch is not global FIFO.** P09 starts S2 before P08 despite entering later. P17 starts while the earlier-entering P16 remains waiting at 14:00. These order differences survive ±2.5-minute rounding. Priorities, resource eligibility, case readiness and logging omissions are possible explanations, not supplied facts. The data do not justify imposing FIFO to manufacture missing starts or inferring that every available resource could process every waiting case immediately.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

**Three nominal scenarios.** Earliest starts are optimistic for waiting and imply the most service; latest starts are pessimistic for waiting and imply the least service. Neither is uniformly optimistic for system performance.

| Scenario | Missing-start rule | S1 busy / queue area | S2 busy / queue area | Mean S1 wait among starters | Mean S2 wait among starters |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| Early | All four starts at their lower bounds | 605 / 1,480 | 570 / 770 | 65.2 | 36.6 |
| Central | All four interval midpoints | 570 / 1,515 | 542.5 / 797.5 | 66.9 | 38.3 |
| Late | All four starts at their upper bounds | 535 / 1,550 | 515 / 825 | 68.6 | 40.0 |

Busy and queue areas are resource-minutes and case-minutes, respectively. Each row preserves S1 WIP area 2,085 and S2 WIP area 1,340. Nominal peak queues remain 7 and 5 across the full missing-start bounds, not just the midpoint.

The midpoints imply missing S2 services of 15 and 12.5 minutes, shorter than most recorded S2 services. A hypothesis that missing cases resemble recorded work would favor earlier starts, but cannot be imposed as fact: P05's nominal S2 service cannot exceed 30 minutes and P11's cannot exceed 25 under the resource constraints. Blindly inserting the observed 40-minute mean would create resource overlaps. Likewise, midpoint S1 services of 17.5 minutes need not represent typical inspection effort.

The three rows align choices for presentation; the four missing starts can vary independently. For example, late S1 starts and early S2 starts yield utilization of 74.3% at S1 and 80.9% at S2, reversing their central full-window ranking. Thus the precise utilization ranking is not a stable bottleneck indicator.

**Rounding sensitivity.** Including rounded endpoint uncertainty gives these marginal missing-duration envelopes:

| Missing activity | Waiting envelope | Service envelope |
|---|---:|---:|
| P05, S2 | 15–55 | 0–35 |
| P07, S1 | 35–80 | 0–40 |
| P11, S2 | 50–85 | 0–30 |

Under A3–A4, broad feasible occupied-time envelopes are approximately **442.5–697.5 minutes at S1 (61.5–96.9%)** and **450–630 at S2 (63.8–89.4%)**. They allow coordinated extreme rounding errors and limiting zero durations for missing services; they are mathematical bounds, not likely utilization intervals. The narrower nominal scenarios are more informative about missingness alone.

The upper bounds use the union of each resource's possible occupied intervals, preventing overlaps at shared uncertain endpoints from being counted twice. Upper occupied times by resource are I1 357.5, I2 340, C1 332.5 and C2 297.5 minutes. These bounds can be realized here by choosing compatible boundaries within the rounding bands. Simply giving every recorded duration its independent +5-minute maximum would overcount some same-resource handoffs.

Rounding also gives marginal mean-WIP envelopes of approximately 5.50–6.08 at S1 and 3.49–3.96 at S2, assuming the supplied roster is complete. They must not be combined as independent system bounds because S1 completions are shared transfer timestamps. Pointwise queue peaks and counts at exact five-minute boundaries are more sensitive than integrated occupancy. The nominal peaks and snapshots in Section 4 are not claims of exact unrounded states.

If maintenance endpoints themselves were rounded operational timestamps, the outage could last approximately 10–20 minutes and the S2 availability denominator would be 700–710 resource-minutes. The midpoint busy estimate would then imply about 76.4–77.5% utilization. Neither that alternative nor feasible event rounding removes the observed late S2 backlog. P01's rounded arrival at the opening boundary also means 21 or 22 arrivals may fall strictly within the actual six-hour window; all 22 still belong to the analyzed cohort.

**Censoring sensitivity.** At 14:00, P15 and P17 contribute 30 and 5 elapsed S2 service minutes; P21 contributes 30 elapsed S1 minutes. Omitting them would understate resource use. Conversely, treating those elapsed values as completed service durations would bias service-time summaries. Final service durations have no finite upper bound from this extract. The same applies to the final waits of P16, P18, P19, P20 and P22. None is a zero-wait observation.

Completed-case averages are especially unrepresentative of the day's entire workload because later entrants face a different queue state and many remain unfinished. This reconstruction makes no extrapolation from the completed subset to the eventual cohort mean.

**Stable conclusions:** the stated final state has eight unfinished cases; S1 experiences strong early accumulation; S2 retains substantial downstream work as external arrivals taper; missing starts cannot change recorded station sojourns, completed-case totals or nominal WIP; and non-FIFO S2 ordering is observed. **Unstable conclusions:** exact active-service means, exact utilization ranking, precise handoff-time queue peaks, the causal delay attributable to maintenance, and final durations for unfinished cases.

A useful operational follow-up would capture start events reliably, record dispatch/eligibility reasons for overtaking, and distinguish resource-occupied time from pauses. Those observations would resolve more of the bottleneck ambiguity than imposing a single missing-duration average.

    # Retain distinct status columns even though all non-times parse to NaT.
    parsed[col + '_status'] = raw[col].map(
        lambda x: 'BLANK' if x == '' else x if x in ('MISSING', 'OPEN') else 'RECORDED')
    parsed[col] = pd.to_datetime(raw[col].map(
        lambda x: date + x if x not in ('', 'MISSING', 'OPEN') else None))

# A normalized station table includes only cases that have entered that station.
rows = []
for r in parsed.to_dict('records'):
    for st in ['s1', 's2']:
        entry = r['arrival_s1'] if st == 's1' else r['s1_end']
        if pd.isna(entry):
            continue  # Not yet in this station, rather than a zero wait.
        rows.append(dict(case=r['case'], station=st.upper(), entry=entry,
                         start=r[st + '_start'], end=r[st + '_end'],
                         resource=r[st + '_resource'],
                         start_status=r[st + '_start_status'],
                         end_status=r[st + '_end_status']))
base = pd.DataFrame(rows)

# Strict single-resource nonpreemption; end order equals service order.
# These data have no overlapping maintenance/service interval.
missing_bounds = []
for ix, r in base[base.start_status.eq('MISSING')].iterrows():
    prior = base[(base.station == r.station) & (base.resource == r.resource)
                 & base.end.notna() & (base.end <= r.end) & (base.case != r.case)]
    lo = max(r.entry, prior.end.max()) if len(prior) else r.entry
    if r.resource == 'C2' and lo < OUT1 and r.end > OUT0:
        lo = max(lo, OUT1)
    hi = r.end  # A zero-duration boundary is included, not asserted as realistic.
    following = base[(base.station == r.station) & (base.resource == r.resource)
                     & base.start.notna() & (base.start >= r.end)]
    assert following.empty or r.end <= following.start.min()
    assert lo <= hi
    base.loc[ix, 'lo'] = lo
    base.loc[ix, 'hi'] = hi
    missing_bounds.append([r.case, r.station, lo, hi])


def scenario(alpha):
    d = base.copy()
    m = d.start_status.eq('MISSING')
    d.loc[m, 'start'] = d.loc[m, 'lo'] + alpha * (d.loc[m, 'hi'] - d.loc[m, 'lo'])
    # Endpoint is an exposure cutoff, never an imputed completion.
    d['wait_stop'] = d.start.fillna(T1)
    d['service_stop'] = d.end.fillna(T1)
    d['wait_obs_min'] = (d.wait_stop - d.entry) / MIN
    d['service_obs_min'] = np.where(d.start.notna(), (d.service_stop - d.start) / MIN, 0.0)
    d['sojourn_obs_min'] = (d.service_stop - d.entry) / MIN
    assert np.allclose(d.wait_obs_min + d.service_obs_min, d.sojourn_obs_min)
    for _, g in d[d.start.notna()].groupby(['station', 'resource']):
        g = g.sort_values(['end', 'start'], na_position='last')
        last_end = None
        for r in g.itertuples():
            assert r.entry <= r.start <= r.service_stop
            assert last_end is None or last_end <= r.start
            if r.resource == 'C2':
                assert r.service_stop <= OUT0 or r.start >= OUT1
            last_end = r.service_stop
    return d


def exposure(begin, end, left=T0, right=T1):
    lo = begin.clip(lower=left)
    hi = end.clip(upper=right)
    return ((hi - lo) / MIN).clip(lower=0).fillna(0)


def snapshot(d, t):
    entered = d.entry <= t
    active = entered & d.start.notna() & (d.start <= t) & (d.end.isna() | (d.end > t))
    waiting = entered & (d.start.isna() | (d.start > t))
    return int(waiting.sum()), int(active.sum()), int((waiting | active).sum())


def summarize(d, name):
    result = []
    for st, g in d.groupby('station'):
        qa = exposure(g.entry, g.wait_stop).sum()
        ba = exposure(g.start, g.service_stop).sum()
        wa = exposure(g.entry, g.service_stop).sum()
        times = sorted(set([T0, T1] + g.entry.dropna().tolist()
                           + g.start.dropna().tolist() + g.end.dropna().tolist()))
        states = [snapshot(g, t) for t in times if T0 <= t <= T1]
        completed = g.end.notna()
        started = g.start.notna()
        result.append(dict(scenario=name, station=st, entrants=len(g),
                           completed=int(completed.sum()), started=int(started.sum()),
                           wait_started=g.loc[started, 'wait_obs_min'].mean(),
                           service_completed=g.loc[completed, 'service_obs_min'].mean(),
                           sojourn_completed=g.loc[completed, 'sojourn_obs_min'].mean(),
                           queue_area=qa, busy_area=ba, wip_area=wa,
                           mean_Q=qa / 360, peak_Q=max(x[0] for x in states),
                           mean_WIP=wa / 360, peak_WIP=max(x[2] for x in states),
                           utilization=ba / (720 if st == 'S1' else 705)))
    return result

scenarios = {name: scenario(a) for name, a in [('early', 0.0), ('midpoint', .5), ('late', 1.0)]}
summary = pd.DataFrame([r for name, d in scenarios.items() for r in summarize(d, name)])
print('MISSING-START NOMINAL BOUNDS')
print(pd.DataFrame(missing_bounds, columns=['case', 'station', 'earliest', 'latest']).to_string(index=False))
print('\nSTATION SCENARIOS (minutes; utilization is available-time fraction)')
print(summary.round(3).to_string(index=False))
central = scenarios['midpoint']
print('\nCASE METRICS: observed-to-cutoff for censored rows, not final durations')
print(central[['case', 'station', 'wait_obs_min', 'service_obs_min', 'sojourn_obs_min',
               'start_status', 'end_status']].to_string(index=False))
print('\nCOMPLETED CASE SOJOURN FROM EXTERNAL ARRIVAL (minutes)')
complete = parsed.s2_end.notna()
case_sojourn = ((parsed.loc[complete, 's2_end'] - parsed.loc[complete, 'arrival_s1']) / MIN)
print(pd.DataFrame({'case': parsed.loc[complete, 'case'], 'total_sojourn_min': case_sojourn}).to_string(index=False))
print('Completed-case mean:', round(case_sojourn.mean(), 2))

print('\nPOST-EVENT SNAPSHOTS: Q / active / WIP')
ss = []
for t in pd.date_range(T0, T1, freq='1h'):
    item = {'time': t.strftime('%H:%M')}
    for st in ['S1', 'S2']:
        vals = [snapshot(d[d.station.eq(st)], t) for d in scenarios.values()]
        item[st] = f'Q={min(v[0] for v in vals)}..{max(v[0] for v in vals)}, active={min(v[1] for v in vals)}..{max(v[1] for v in vals)}, WIP={vals[0][2]}'
    item['total_WIP'] = sum(snapshot(central[central.station.eq(st)], t)[2] for st in ['S1', 'S2'])
    ss.append(item)
print(pd.DataFrame(ss).to_string(index=False))

print('\nTWO-HOUR RATES: [left,right), counts and rates per hour')
rates = []
for left in pd.date_range(T0, T1 - pd.Timedelta(hours=2), freq='2h'):
    right = left + pd.Timedelta(hours=2)
    for st, g in central.groupby('station'):
        a = int(((g.entry >= left) & (g.entry < right)).sum())
        c = int(((g.end >= left) & (g.end < right)).sum())
        rates.append(dict(interval=f'{left:%H:%M}-{right:%H:%M}', station=st,
                          arrivals=a, completions=c, arrivals_h=a/2, completions_h=c/2))
print(pd.DataFrame(rates).to_string(index=False))

print('\nBUSY MINUTES AND AVAILABLE-TIME UTILIZATION BY RESOURCE')
res = []
for name, d in scenarios.items():
    for resource, g in d[d.start.notna()].groupby('resource'):
        busy = exposure(g.start, g.service_stop).sum()
        available = 345 if resource == 'C2' else 360
        res.append(dict(scenario=name, resource=resource, busy_min=busy, util=busy/available))
print(pd.DataFrame(res).round(3).to_string(index=False))

print('\nSUBPERIOD BUSY MINUTES: midpoint (S2 outage removed from denominator)')
sub = []
for left in pd.date_range(T0, T1 - pd.Timedelta(hours=2), freq='2h'):
    right = left + pd.Timedelta(hours=2)
    for st, g in central.groupby('station'):
        busy = exposure(g.start, g.service_stop, left, right).sum()
        available = 240 - (15 if st == 'S2' and left <= OUT0 < right else 0)
        sub.append(dict(interval=f'{left:%H:%M}-{right:%H:%M}', station=st, busy=busy,
                        util=busy/available, mean_Q=exposure(g.entry,g.wait_stop,left,right).sum()/120,
                        mean_WIP=exposure(g.entry,g.service_stop,left,right).sum()/120))
print(pd.DataFrame(sub).round(3).to_string(index=False))

# Deterministic endpoint envelopes. Merge possible busy intervals per resource
# rather than counting overlaps twice. Their union is an upper bound; in these
# data it is attainable by splitting shared boundaries inside the rounding bands.
E = pd.Timedelta(minutes=2.5)
def union_minutes(intervals):
    merged = []
    for a, b in sorted(intervals):
        a, b = max(a, T0), min(b, T1)
        if b <= a:
            continue
        if merged and a <= merged[-1][1]:
            merged[-1] = (merged[-1][0], max(b, merged[-1][1]))
        else:
            merged.append((a, b))
    return sum((b - a) / MIN for a, b in merged)

round_rows = []
for st, g in base.groupby('station'):
    known = g.start.notna() & g.end.notna()
    dur = (g.loc[known, 'end'] - g.loc[known, 'start']) / MIN
    open_ = g.start.notna() & g.end_status.eq('OPEN')
    elapsed = (T1 - g.loc[open_, 'start']) / MIN
    lower = (dur - 5).clip(lower=0).sum() + (elapsed - 2.5).clip(lower=0).sum()
    upper = 0.0
    for resource, h in g[g.resource.ne('')].groupby('resource'):
        intervals = []
        for r in h.itertuples():
            begin = (r.lo if r.start_status == 'MISSING' else r.start) - E
            end = T1 if r.end_status == 'OPEN' else r.end + E
            assert resource != 'C2' or end <= OUT0 or begin >= OUT1
            intervals.append((begin, end))
        upper += union_minutes(intervals)
    available = 720 if st == 'S1' else 705
    wip_low = exposure(g.entry + E, (g.end - E).fillna(T1)).sum()/360
    wip_high = exposure(g.entry - E, (g.end + E).fillna(T1)).sum()/360
    round_rows.append(dict(station=st, busy_low=lower, busy_high=upper,
                           util_low=lower/available, util_high=upper/available,
                           mean_WIP_low=wip_low, mean_WIP_high=wip_high))
print('\nBOUNDED ROUNDING ENVELOPES (not confidence intervals)')
print(pd.DataFrame(round_rows).round(3).to_string(index=False))

print('\nFINITE-WINDOW OCCUPANCY IDENTITIES (not a steady-state Little law claim)')
for st, g in central.groupby('station'):
    a = exposure(g.entry, g.service_stop).sum()
    closed_area = g.loc[g.end.notna(), 'sojourn_obs_min'].sum()
    censored_area = g.loc[g.end.isna(), 'sojourn_obs_min'].sum()
    assert np.isclose(a, closed_area + censored_area)
    print(st, 'area=', a, 'mean_WIP=', round(a/360,3),
          'completed contribution=', round(closed_area/360,3),
          'censored contribution=', round(censored_area/360,3),
          'arrival rate x mean clipped residence=', round(len(g)/360*(a/len(g)),3))

# PM4Py-compatible interval events: keep only observed, closed intervals first.
# OPEN is never relabeled as a completion at 14:00; MISSING is not set to zero.
def as_events(d):
    e = d.rename(columns={'case':'case:concept:name', 'station':'concept:name',
                           'start':'start_timestamp', 'end':'time:timestamp',
                           'resource':'org:resource'})
    return e[['case:concept:name', 'concept:name', 'start_timestamp',
              'time:timestamp', 'org:resource']].copy()

usable = base[base.start.notna() & base.end.notna()]
