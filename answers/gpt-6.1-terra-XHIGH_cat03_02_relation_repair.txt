## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

- **Hard invariant:** Added relations reference existing events and objects; O2O source and target have the intended types and direction. Preserve the observed records, and do not add duplicate relation tuples or invent events or objects. An empty E2O qualifier below means unspecified, not a claim about a business role.
- **Strong default:** An ordinary pack has one Item and one Package, a dispatch has one Package and one Delivery, and an invoice has one Invoice and at least one Order. A payment has exactly one Payment and normally one Invoice. These are defeasible business expectations, not database constraints. A bulk dispatch, an item-only outage record, and unapplied cash are explicitly possible.
- **Strong default:** Respect lifecycle order: adding an item precedes packing, packing precedes dispatch, and dispatch normally precedes confirmation. An event need not directly mention every object reachable through O2O; absence of such an E2O relation is not itself a defect.
- **Weak heuristic:** Compare identifiers at their actual specificity. `PK8-2` strongly suggests PK-8, while `N-421?` favors DEL-Y's `N-421` over DEL-X's `N-420`, but the question mark matters. `N-42*` is compatible with both deliveries. Same-time dispatches and a common NORTH route provide context, not identification.
- **Weak heuristic:** C17 and 130 + 100 = 230 support a consolidated invoice; the two payment amounts summing to 230 support installment settlement. Fees, discounts, other payments and unapplied cash remain possible. The bank reference independently identifies PAY-2 at ev14, not its invoice.
- **Evidence against a repair:** An existing different object of a normally single-valued type, an inconsistent exact identifier, impossible chronology, or an unsupported cross-object propagation weighs against an edge. The existing `DEL-X carries PK-7` and ev09's N-420 make DEL-X a weaker candidate for ev10 than DEL-Y; they do not rule out bulk operations.

I combine clues qualitatively, giving direct specific identifiers and observed co-occurrence more weight than arithmetic, shared customer, route, or timing. Existing O2O structure can support a hypothesis but does not automatically propagate relations to events. Confidences are subjective plausibility scores, not calibrated probabilities; competing scores need not sum to one because hypotheses can overlap or the truth can be outside the candidates. Bundle A uses only its explicitly selected proposals, including provisional ones; the status of a proposal does not make it an observed fact.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A — conservative/minimum-change repair.** Add P1–P4: ev08–PK-8 and PK-8 `packs` IT-B1; ev10–DEL-Y and DEL-Y `carries` PK-8. These four additions follow the two most specific incomplete references and their natural O2O interpretations. P2–P4 remain provisional; an even stricter repair could add P1 alone. Leave ev11 attached only to ORD-A: it may acknowledge X, Y, both, or an order without a carrier identifier. Do not add ORD-B to ev12 or INV-44, or INV-44 to ev14 or PAY-2. This gives PK-8 two pack events and one dispatch, and DEL-Y one dispatch, without inventing a delivery confirmation or asserting settlement.

**Bundle B — combined acknowledgment and consolidated billing.** Add P1–P4, P7–P8, and P9–P12. The portal acknowledges DEL-X and DEL-Y together at ev11. Both deliveries have dispatch followed by confirmation; the shared PK-8 carries items from both orders. INV-44 bills both orders, and PAY-1 and PAY-2 are two payments against it. This makes an internally coherent, but evidence-dependent, story: the same customer and totals are consistent with consolidation, and the ambiguous confirmation permits a combined acknowledgment. It does not require ev11 to mention ORD-B or either payment event to mention an order: O2O reachability is not direct event participation. There may have been charges, other allocations, or an order-only acknowledgment instead.

A third coherent interpretation is A's packing and DEL-Y dispatch, but ev11 confirms DEL-X alone because ORD-A's IT-A1 is in PK-7; the alternative DEL-Y alone is also plausible because PK-8 contains ORD-A's IT-A2. The wildcard and shared package make either unique assignment uncertain; A deliberately selects neither. For ev10, DEL-X handling both packages is possible but weaker than DEL-Y because the uncertain reference resembles N-421, not N-420. It should be investigated, not silently combined with P3 as a second delivery at an ordinary dispatch.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

- **Order:** ORD-A has creation, two add-item events, confirmation and invoicing; ORD-B has creation and one add-item event. ORD-B's missing delivery or invoice events are incomplete observation, not an orphan object. Both orders contain items in O2O, and their add-item E2O co-occurrences independently support those existing associations. A does not infer package, dispatch, or payment events directly onto either order.
- **Item:** Each item appears at an add event and a later pack event. Existing order `contains` relations agree with observed add events. IT-A1 and IT-A2 also have observed pack co-occurrence and matching existing `packs` edges. IT-B1's new package edge rests on the repaired ev08, not an independent source. PK-8 packing two items from different orders is plausible consolidation, not evidence that either order was fully delivered.
- **Package:** PK-7 has pack then dispatch. PK-8 has ev07 and ev08 packs followed by ev10 dispatch; two items per package are allowed, even though each pack event normally has one item and package. Its shared contents connect different orders at the object level; they need not merge the orders into a single case in every extraction method.
- **Delivery:** DEL-X participates in ev09; DEL-Y participates in ev10 only after repair. Neither has an observed confirmation E2O in A. DEL-Y was an event-orphan before repair but is not thereafter; the object is retained even if the ev10 hypothesis is withdrawn. DEL-X `carries` PK-7 agrees with observed dispatch co-occurrence. New DEL-Y `carries` PK-8 is derived from the proposed ev10 relation; it is not independent corroboration.
- **Invoice:** INV-44 has issue and PAY-1's record-payment events; it bills ORD-A and is settled by PAY-1. These O2O edges align with observed event co-occurrences, though co-occurrence does not by itself establish the qualifier. There is no chosen relation to ORD-B or PAY-2. B's one invoice for two orders and two payments is a plausible many-to-many pattern in the wider model, not an automatic cardinality violation.
- **Payment:** PAY-1 and PAY-2 each participate in their own payment event. PAY-1 is linked to INV-44 both at ev13 and by an observed O2O edge. PAY-2 has no selected invoice relation and may be unapplied. No event is wholly orphaned in A; every listed event has at least one observed object. A deliberately retains incomplete lifecycles.

The critical circularity is P1 → P2 and P3 → P4: after inferring an event link, the proposed O2O link cannot be counted as a second independent confirmation of that event link. The same warning applies to the paired invoice and payment proposals in B. Existing O2O and E2O records can be separately observed yet still arise from the same underlying source system, so even their agreement is not necessarily statistically independent.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

These effects assume the indicated graphs and traces are built from direct E2O participation, with no automatic O2O-to-E2O propagation. Their exact counts and ordering depend on the implementation, qualifiers, filtering, and tie handling; the two dispatch events have identical timestamps. The OTG's chosen discovery variant and execution-extraction definition also matter.

- **OC-DFG:** In the unrepaired log PK-8 has `Pack Item → Dispatch Package`. A adds ev08 to its lifecycle, producing `Pack Item → Pack Item → Dispatch Package`: the old direct edge from ev07 to ev10 disappears for this object, although the same activity-level edge remains via ev08 and possibly other objects. DEL-Y gains a dispatch but no directly-follows edge from a single event. In B, DEL-X and DEL-Y each acquire `Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery`; INV-44 gains a second payment, yielding `Issue Invoice → Record Payment → Record Payment` instead of its prior direct issue-to-first-payment fragment alone. ORD-B gains `Add Item → Issue Invoice`. Object-level contributions change even when an aggregated activity edge already exists.
- **OTG:** In a co-event interaction OTG, A introduces interaction between IT-B1 and PK-8 and between PK-8 and DEL-Y. B adds interactions between the two deliveries and ORD-A at ev11, between ORD-B and INV-44/ORD-A at ev12, and between PAY-2 and INV-44 at ev14. Lifecycle-based descendant, cobirth or codeath edges can also change when first or last events change. An OTG built specifically from O2O edges instead changes by P2 and P4 in A and the additional billing and settlement edges in B; it must not be assumed that a particular PM4Py OTG variant uses O2O qualifiers as its source.
- **ET–OT graph:** A increases the `Pack Item–Package` and `Dispatch Package–Delivery` relation counts; those activity/type pairs already exist. B introduces `Confirm Delivery–Delivery`, with two delivery relations at one event, and increases `Issue Invoice–Order` and `Record Payment–Invoice` counts. The number of events for an activity does not necessarily equal its number of E2O relations.
- **Flattenings:** Order flattening in A is unchanged: ORD-B ends at `Add Item` and ORD-A includes confirmation and invoice. B adds ev12 to ORD-B's trace, duplicating that one event across the two Order cases. Package flattening adds ev08 to PK-8 in A and B. Delivery flattening in A gives DEL-Y ev10; B additionally gives both deliveries ev11, duplicating the single confirmation across cases. Invoice flattening is unchanged in A; B adds ev14 to INV-44. Payment flattening is unchanged in both because PAY-2 already participates in ev14. O2O-only additions do not change a direct-E2O flattening.
- **Frequency measures:** The log still has 14 event records in all bundles: three pack events, two dispatches, one confirmation, one invoice issue and two payment records. For pack events, distinct Package objects rise from two to two in A, but Package E2O occurrences rise from two to three. For confirmations, B changes Delivery occurrences from zero to two and distinct Delivery objects from zero to two, while the event count stays one. If “total objects” counts all event–object occurrences, these are incidence counts, not distinct-object counts; under another tool's aggregation the numbers may differ.
- **Equivalent-execution clustering around Order:** A's new co-event links connect ORD-B's IT-B1 through PK-8 to the ORD-A side in an interaction-graph extraction, potentially changing components, order-rooted neighborhoods and their variants. B also directly co-occurs ORD-A and ORD-B at ev12 and connects both deliveries at ev11. An extraction that produces one neighborhood per leading Order may instead duplicate shared events or objects in both executions. Consequently clusters and variant frequencies can merge, split, or gain shared structure depending on the execution and equivalence definition; no particular clustering result follows from the relation table alone.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

This outline uses canonical OCEL column names and reconstructs the denormalized activity, timestamp and object type on E2O rows from the authoritative event and object tables. It checks tuple duplicates, including qualifiers, and preserves the input DataFrames. It makes no claim that inferred O2O relations have independent evidential support.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.ocel.obj import OCEL

# Canonical columns assumed in inputs:
# events: ocel:eid, ocel:activity, ocel:timestamp
# objects: ocel:oid, ocel:type
# relations: ocel:eid, ocel:oid, optionally ocel:qualifier
# o2o: ocel:oid, ocel:oid_2, ocel:qualifier
original_events = events.copy(deep=True)
original_objects = objects.copy(deep=True)
original_relations = relations.copy(deep=True)
original_o2o = o2o.copy(deep=True)

ev = original_events.copy(deep=True)
ob = original_objects.copy(deep=True)
rel = original_relations.copy(deep=True)
links = original_o2o.copy(deep=True)

assert ev["ocel:eid"].notna().all() and ev["ocel:eid"].is_unique
assert ob["ocel:oid"].notna().all() and ob["ocel:oid"].is_unique
assert ev[["ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp"]].notna().all().all()
assert ob["ocel:type"].notna().all()
ev["ocel:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(ev["ocel:timestamp"], utc=True)

if "ocel:qualifier" not in rel.columns:
    rel["ocel:qualifier"] = ""
rel["ocel:qualifier"] = rel["ocel:qualifier"].fillna("")
if links.empty:
    links = links.reindex(columns=["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"])
links["ocel:qualifier"] = links["ocel:qualifier"].fillna("")

e2o_new = pd.DataFrame([
    {"ocel:eid": "ev08", "ocel:oid": "PK-8", "ocel:qualifier": ""},
    {"ocel:eid": "ev10", "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:qualifier": ""},
])
o2o_new = pd.DataFrame([
    {"ocel:oid": "PK-8", "ocel:oid_2": "IT-B1", "ocel:qualifier": "packs"},
    {"ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:oid_2": "PK-8", "ocel:qualifier": "carries"},
])
rel = pd.concat([rel, e2o_new], ignore_index=True, sort=False)
links = pd.concat([links, o2o_new], ignore_index=True, sort=False)

# Refresh denormalized relation columns; retain other relation attributes.
rel = rel.drop(columns=["ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp", "ocel:type"],
               errors="ignore")
rel = rel.merge(ev[["ocel:eid", "ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp"]],
                on="ocel:eid", how="left", validate="many_to_one")
rel = rel.merge(ob[["ocel:oid", "ocel:type"]],
                on="ocel:oid", how="left", validate="many_to_one")

assert rel[["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid"]].notna().all().all()
assert rel["ocel:eid"].isin(ev["ocel:eid"]).all()
assert rel["ocel:oid"].isin(ob["ocel:oid"]).all()
assert links[["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2"]].notna().all().all()
assert links["ocel:oid"].isin(ob["ocel:oid"]).all()
assert links["ocel:oid_2"].isin(ob["ocel:oid"]).all()
assert not rel.duplicated(["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"]).any()
assert not links.duplicated(["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"]).any()

# Version-dependent: OCEL constructor import and keyword arguments can vary.
ocel = OCEL(events=ev, objects=ob, relations=rel, o2o=links)
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)
order_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Order")
delivery_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Delivery")
# Inspect results and compare against the original log using the same variant.
# Do not treat automatically enriched O2O associations as proof of qualifiers.
```

Integrity checks establish structural validity, not whether the proposed relations occurred. In production, also validate qualifier/type semantics and review duplicate findings rather than silently dropping them. Some PM4Py releases differ in APIs and how graph variants use O2O.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. Treating ev11's wildcard as DEL-X alone would create a specific dispatch-to-confirmation path even though DEL-Y or a combined acknowledgment is possible.
2. Adding both deliveries to ev11 merely to complete their traces would fabricate a two-delivery synchronization if the portal recorded only ORD-A.
3. Assigning ev10 to DEL-X as well as DEL-Y would make an ordinary dispatch appear to involve multiple deliveries, and could merge independent routes or executions.
4. Turning the invoice's 230 into certain evidence for both orders can create a false consolidated invoice and duplicate ev12 in Order flattening; taxes or fees may explain the value.
5. Assigning PAY-2 to INV-44 because the amounts sum to 230 can create a false second payment and settlement path; the cash could be unapplied.
6. Propagating package contents to dispatch events would create Item dispatch activities absent from the observed E2O records, changing Item traces and DFG frequencies.
7. Propagating an invoice's orders to every payment event would invent Order payment activities and misleading case durations, even if the invoice genuinely bills both orders.
8. Reversing `DEL-Y carries PK-8` into `PK-8 carries DEL-Y`, or adding both directions without a distinct inverse qualifier, would misrepresent the O2O semantics and distort directed relationship analysis.
