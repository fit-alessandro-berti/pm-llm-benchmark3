## 1. Candidate matrix

**Evidence base used for the ratings.** All figures come from the activity table and DFG. The observation window is not stated, so the absolute hours matter less than the ratios between them.

- **Workload by pool (frequency × mean service time, rework excluded):**
  - Back office: about 4,800 h (Register 1,584 h, Classify 2,364 h, Request missing documents 825 h) across 12 FTE, so roughly **400 h per FTE**. Utilization also drops below 65% after 15:00.
  - Credit analysts: about 29,700 h (Financial document review 6,900 h, Normalize 6,500 h, Credit analysis 16,300 h), plus their share of Committee packaging (3,250 h shared with relationship managers). That comes to roughly **3,300–3,500 h per FTE** across 9 FTE. This is about eight times the back-office load per head, so analysts are the capacity constraint.
  - Compliance specialists: about 1,600 h for Resolve entity mismatch across 3 FTE. The volume is modest, but the handover network sends it to **two senior specialists**. The 31 h wait is therefore a key-person queue, not a raw-capacity queue.
  - Legal ops: about 4,400 h plus remediation work across 4 FTE, with month-end peaks.
- **Waiting burden (frequency × median wait; indicative only, because these are medians and some branches run concurrently):**

  | Activity | Waiting burden (case-hours) |
  |---|---|
  | Customer signing | ≈321k |
  | Credit analysis | ≈249k |
  | Contract preparation | ≈167k |
  | Financial document review | ≈166k |
  | External valuation | ≈136k |
  | Normalize | ≈104k |
  | Committee packaging | ≈101k |
  | Resolve entity mismatch | ≈54k, concentrated on about 1,435 critical-path cases (82% of 1,750) |
  | **Register application** | **≈18k, about 1% of the total** |

- **Loops:**
  - Request missing documents → Financial document review happens 2,800 times, so about 30% of review events are re-reviews.
  - Resolve entity mismatch → KYC check happens 1,160 times.
  - Contract preparation → Remediate control issue happens 650 times. Only 510 return, so 140 exit without returning.
  - 21% of signings go through the reminder loop.
- **Typical case:** a simple case with no collateral, no committee and no loops accumulates about 145 h of median waiting. A committee case with collateral and loops exceeds 300 h. Register's 1.5 h wait and 8 min of service are therefore about 0.5–1% of case cycle time.

**Why the ranking diverges from frequency.**
- A1 and A11 rank last despite high frequency and feasibility. They sit where waiting is small, labour is not scarce, and the controls they depend on are unreliable. Each would also consume one of the two deep-integration slots.
- A4 ranks high despite 1,750 cases. It sits on the critical path, blocks both KYC and signing, and relieves a two-person dependency.
- A9 ranks first because it combines real labour saving in a peaking pool with the only preventive control over two of the three control findings.

### Upstream data-quality dependencies
- **Classification feeds everything downstream.** The 8% error rate at Classify product and exposure determines:
  - which checklist A5 sends;
  - whether A7 predicts a valuation;
  - whether the case takes the High-exposure path (committee and four-eyes);
  - which template and rule set A9 applies.

  Before any ML recommender (A2), deterministic exposure rules should be introduced, using the requested amount plus existing exposure from core banking. A9 should also re-check classification consistency before contract release.
- **Document quality drives A6.** A6 fails on low-quality PDFs. A5 is its natural precursor because it can request native digital statements, accounting-system exports or bank-statement feeds instead of scans. A6 should be rolled out by document-quality tier.
- **A8 is garbage in, garbage out.** Committee packaging waits for KYC status, normalized statements, valuation and credit notes. The DFG also shows an inconsistency: incoming relations to Committee packaging (Credit analysis → packaging 2,700 plus valuation → packaging 1,980, total 4,680) exceed its 3,900 events. This points to repeated packaging (15% rework) or logging duplication. It must be reconciled before A8 is specified.
- **A9 needs a structured decision.** An 8.5% contract-remediation rate, 14% for renewals, suggests committee terms are re-keyed from free-text minutes. A structured decision term sheet is a Phase-0 prerequisite.
- **The event log itself needs repair.** KYC check and Financial document review run concurrently but are recorded sequentially:
  - Classify → KYC check: 7,250
  - Classify → Financial document review: 3,940
  - KYC check → Credit analysis: 4,200

  Without start and complete timestamps, critical-path estimates will credit KYC improvements to cases where document review is actually the longer branch. Four-eyes monitoring also needs reliable resource IDs.

### Legal and compliance controls
- **KYC before signing.**
  - The gate must exist at contract release (A9) before signing is accelerated (A10).
  - 380 cases go Resolve entity mismatch → Contract preparation without re-entering KYC check. Either "resolution" is an undocumented clearance or it is a bypass. An explicit "KYC cleared" event must be defined and required.
  - Provisional signing (6.5% of cases, 1.1% remediated) probably concentrates in the mismatch variant, where KYC is blocked for 31 h and relationship managers push ahead. This hypothesis should be verified by filtering the log.
- **Terms must match the decision.** A9 prevents mismatches, A12 handles residual cases, and A11 must require both to pass.
- **Four-eyes.**
  - Separation of duties (SoD) must be enforced by identity in the workflow: whoever completes Credit analysis cannot complete Committee packaging for High-exposure SME cases.
  - An automation account never counts as one of the "eyes".
  - A8 must not auto-complete packaging under the analyst's identity.
  - The 5.8% of violations occur in the month-end rush, which links them to analyst overload. A6 therefore helps indirectly.
- **Auditability.** Every model-based module (A2, A3, A4, A6, A7, A8) must log the suggestion, confidence, model version, inputs, human override with a reason code, and the final decision. Rule-based modules (A9, A11) must log rule-library versions.

### Human escalation capacity
Every module creates exception work. The pool that absorbs it must have room.

| Exception source | Receiving pool | Current signal | Implication |

| A4 advisory cases | Specialists, plus KYC ops for simple cases | Handover dependency on 2 seniors | A4 must widen who can resolve, not just speed up the seniors |

### External valuation dependency
- Wait for external valuation is outside direct control: 58 h median, 19% late. Of 2,350 waits, only 1,980 flow directly to packaging, so about 370 detour into re-valuation, disputes or other paths.
- The internal levers are **when** valuation is requested and **from whom**:
  - Today it is requested after Credit analysis (Credit analysis → Collateral valuation request: 2,400).
  - Triggering at classification for Equipment finance (71% need) moves the 12 h + 58 h behind the document review, normalization and credit-analysis queues.
  - For Working-capital (18% need), early triggering wastes valuation fees, so prediction (A7) is needed there.
- For High-exposure cases, a late valuation that misses the weekly committee cut-off costs a full week. A8's synchronization benefit is therefore capped by A7 or by valuer SLAs.
- A7 depends on partner APIs and contracts, not core banking. That makes it a light-integration candidate once slots free up.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

### P1: Quick-win portfolio (A1, A2, A5, A10)
- **Optimizes:** high feasibility, fast delivery, visible labour reduction on high-frequency activities, and a customer-facing speed-up at signing. It is the politically attractive option and gives management the "highest-frequency activity automated" headline.
- **Sacrifices:**
  - Leaves the analyst constraint, the specialist critical path and valuation synchronization untouched.
  - Its back-office savings are not realizable because the pool already has afternoon slack.
  - Without a KYC gate, A10 accelerates provisional signing.
  - Meets the compliance condition only weakly (A2 reduces misrouting but adds its own misclassification risk).
  - Uses both deep slots (A1 and A10) on modest value.

### P2: Bottleneck and synchronization portfolio (A4, A5, A6, A7)
- **Optimizes:** waiting time and tail (P90) cycle time at the loops and synchronization points: the mismatch blocking variant, the missing-document loop, analyst capacity for normalization, the external valuation wait, and alignment of inputs at Committee packaging. It needs only one deep integration (A6).
- **Sacrifices:**
  - No module primarily strengthens controls, so it fails the compliance condition as specified.
  - Contract remediation (8.5%) and provisional signing (6.5%) remain.
  - Faster upstream flow lands on legal ops at month end without A9.
  - Visible labour savings are small, which is hard to defend politically.

### P3: Compliance-first portfolio (A2, A3, A9, A12)
- **Optimizes:** control-path integrity from classification (A2) through screening (A3) and contract terms (A9) to remediation (A12), with a full audit trail. It directly targets terms mismatch and KYC-before-signing.
- **Sacrifices:**
  - Little effect on the largest waits: analyst queues, valuation, the missing-document loop and the mismatch queue.
  - A3 adds false-negative exposure and escalation pressure on 3 specialists.
  - Two deep-ish integrations (A3, A9) are consumed.
  - Labour relief goes mainly to legal ops, not to the constrained analyst pool.

### P4: Balanced flow-and-control portfolio (A9, A5, A6, A4), recommended
- **Optimizes:** it combines four targets:
  - the dominant capacity constraint (analysts, via A6 and A5);
  - the dominant loop (missing documents, via A5);
  - the critical-path blocking variant and key-person risk (mismatch, via A4);
  - the main control failures, KYC-before-signing and terms mismatch (A9).

  It includes one high-labour-saving control module (A9) and one visible customer-facing win (A5). It uses exactly two deep integrations (A9, A6) and two light ones (A5, A4).
- **Sacrifices:**
  - No valuation module; the early-trigger rule and valuer SLAs stand in for it.
  - No e-signature module; an adoption campaign using A9-generated e-sign-ready contracts stands in for it.
  - No "registration automated" headline.
  - Four-eyes relies on workflow SoD configuration and an A9 detective check rather than A8.
  - A6 carries delivery risk.

| Portfolio | Modules | Deep slots used | Control-strengthening module | Primary target | Main sacrifice |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 Quick-win | A1, A2, A5, A10 | A1, A10 | Weak (A2) | Labour and visibility | Bottlenecks and controls |
| P2 Bottleneck | A4, A5, A6, A7 | A6 | None | Waiting and synchronization | Compliance condition |
| P3 Compliance-first | A2, A3, A9, A12 | A3, A9 | A9, A12, A3 | Control violations | Cycle time; specialist overload |
| P4 Flow-and-control | A9, A5, A6, A4 | A9, A6 | A9 (plus A4) | Constraint, loops, controls | Valuation and e-sign deferred |

## 4. Expected process-level effects

### P1: Quick-win
- **Cycle time:** small change. Removing most of the Register (1.5 h) and Classify (2.0 h) waits saves about 3 h of a 145–300+ h case. A5 shortens the Request missing documents → review loop. A10 gives the one real gain, at the 44 h signing wait. Because throughput is set by the analysts, end-to-end time barely changes except at signing.
- **Waiting time:** queues move rather than disappear. Back office today acts as an unintended buffer that meters inflow. Auto-registration releases portal bursts straight into KYC ops (4 h), document review (18 h) and the analyst queues (20 h, 28 h), which lengthen.
- **Rework:** Register rework (4%) and duplicates fall. Classification errors (8%) fall if A2 performs. Missing-document loops (2,800) fall. The Resolve → KYC loop (1,160) and Contract → Remediate loop (650) are unchanged.
- **Handovers:** fewer back-office touches. The KYC ops → senior specialist dependency and the RM + analyst packaging handovers are unchanged.
- **Resource demand:** back office is freed, but it already had slack, so the cash saving is illusory unless staff are redeployed. Analysts receive more, cleaner and faster inflow with no A6 relief, so the constraint tightens. Specialists and legal ops are unchanged.
- **Control violations:** A2 may reduce wrong-control-path routing, but auto-accepted under-classification is a new risk. Ungated A10 is likely to **increase** provisional signing above 6.5%. Four-eyes and terms mismatch are unchanged.

### P2: Bottleneck and synchronization
- **Cycle time:** the largest reduction in P90 and in two variants:
  - **Mismatch variant:** the 31 h wait shrinks, and the 1,160 Resolve → KYC loop passes run faster and with less rework.
  - **Equipment-finance variant:** valuation is moved earlier, taking the Credit analysis → Collateral valuation request edge (2,400) off the critical path and hiding the 58 h wait behind analyst work.

  Committee packaging stops waiting on the last-arriving artifact as often.
- **Waiting time:**
  - Waits in the analyst pool (document review 18 h, Normalize 20 h, Credit analysis 28 h) fall because A6 frees capacity; queueing delay falls non-linearly when utilization drops from near saturation.
  - Customer-response delay in the missing-document loop falls.
  - The 12 h wait before Collateral valuation request falls with A7 routing.
- **Rework:** repeated requests (52%) fall. Mismatch rework (38%) falls with structured evidence. Normalization rework (16%) falls on good documents but may rise on low-quality PDFs. Contract remediation (8.5%) is untouched.
- **Handovers:** fewer RM → back office → customer → analyst cycles. KYC ops escalate less exclusively to the two seniors. Fewer re-requests to external valuers.
- **Resource demand:**
  - Analysts get net relief (less normalization, about 30% fewer re-reviews), partly consumed by higher throughput.
  - More cases reach Committee packaging and the weekly committee sooner, so the committee becomes the next constraint for High-exposure cases.
  - Legal ops receive more cases, faster, with no A9. The month-end peak worsens and the 22 h Contract preparation wait may grow.
- **Control violations:** no preventive control. Four-eyes violations may fall indirectly through less month-end analyst pressure. Provisional signing may fall because fewer cases are stuck in mismatch, but it is not blocked. Terms mismatch is unchanged.

### P3: Compliance-first
- **Cycle time:** modest. Fewer Contract → Remediate loops save a remediation cycle for 8.5% of contracts (14% of renewals). A3 speeds clean, no-hit KYC, but that branch is usually shorter than document review, so there is little end-to-end effect.
- **Waiting time:** the 22 h Contract preparation wait and month-end backlog fall. The 31 h Resolve entity mismatch wait may **rise** if A3 escalates conservatively.
- **Rework:** Contract remediation falls, classification errors fall, and KYC exceptions (11%) are triaged more consistently. Missing-document and mismatch rework are unchanged.
- **Handovers:** legal ops ↔ remediation handovers fall. KYC ops → specialist handovers may increase.
- **Resource demand:** legal ops relieved. Specialists more loaded. Analysts remain the constraint. New demand appears for rule-library maintenance and model validation.
- **Control violations:** strongest effect.
  - Provisional signing is blocked at contract release.
  - Terms mismatch is caught before signing.
  - Classification errors that skip High-exposure controls fall.
  - The audit trail is complete.
  - Four-eyes improves only if A9's check and workflow SoD are included.

### P4: Balanced flow-and-control (recommended)
- **Cycle time:** the base path shortens through the analyst pool (A6 plus A5). The mismatch variant shortens (A4). Contract preparation and remediation shorten (A9). Two non-module changes add to this: the Equipment-finance early valuation trigger, and the e-signature adoption push using A9-generated e-sign-ready contracts, which targets part of the 44 h signing wait.
- **Waiting time:**
  - Waits fall at document review, Normalize, Credit analysis, Resolve entity mismatch and Contract preparation.
  - A4 smooths arrivals to legal ops, which today sit idle while KYC is slow and then face a surge. That lowers the month-end peak.
  - Watch Committee packaging and Committee decision, which will receive more cases sooner.
- **Rework:**
  - Request → document review loops (2,800) fall.
  - Resolve entity mismatch rework (38%) falls, and passes through the Resolve → KYC loop are faster.
  - Contract → Remediate (650) falls, especially for Credit-line renewal once its rules are clarified.
  - The 380 Resolve → Contract preparation cases without re-clearance fall to zero by design.
- **Handovers:** RM → customer → back office → analyst cycles fall. Specialist work moves from two seniors to a wider group, with the assistant providing structured evidence. Legal ops ↔ remediation handovers fall.
- **Resource demand:**
  - Analysts: net relief. A6 offsets the higher clean inflow from A5, which is exactly the overload risk flagged in the resource observations.
  - Back office: redeployed from repeated requests to proactive chasing in the afternoon slack.
  - Specialists: relieved and less key-person-dependent.
  - Legal ops: absorb higher arrivals through A9.
  - New roles: legal ownership of the rule library, an A6 exception queue in the analyst pool, model-risk validation, audit-trail sampling.
  - Committee capacity becomes the next constraint to manage, for example by reviewing whether the weekly cadence needs an extra month-end sitting.
- **Control violations:**
  - **Provisional signing** is blocked preventively by A9's KYC-cleared check. Its main driver, cases stuck in mismatch, shrinks through A4.
  - **Terms mismatch** is checked before release.
  - **Four-eyes** gets preventive workflow SoD (Phase 0), a detective A9 check before contract release, and lower month-end analyst pressure.
  - **Auditability** is built into every module.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Risk | Where | Compensating control | Monitoring signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Loss of auditability** (who decided what, on which suggestion) | A4, A6, A9 (and A2, A3, A7, A8 later) | Immutable log of input snapshot, model or rule version, suggestion, confidence, human action, override reason code, final decision and timestamp; model and rule registry; retention aligned with KYC and credit-file rules | Audit-trail completeness = 100% on a sample; share of override reason codes left blank |
| **False negatives** (a risky case passes) | A4 (wrong entity match accepted), A6 (misread debt or revenue), A9 (missed term mismatch), A3 (year 2) | A4 advisory only, with a named human decision and dual review for High-exposure; A6 accounting-identity checks, key-field verification by the analyst, and confidence routing; A9 parallel run against legal review before go-live; 100% QA in pilot, then risk-based sampling | Post-hoc QA defect rate; downstream catches (remediation after signing, credit-grade revisions) |
| **False positives** (good cases blocked, creating new queues) | A9 false blocks, A6 low-confidence floods, A5 over-rigid checklists | Tuned thresholds with owner sign-off; exception SLAs; legal override path with a reason code; checklist allows "not applicable, reason" | False-block rate; exception queue age; customer complaints; abandonment after Request missing documents (currently ~500 do not return) |
| **Model and rule drift** | A6 (new statement formats, accounting standards), A4 (registry changes), A9 (policy and product changes), A5 (product mix) | Drift monitoring on input distributions and confidence; periodic revalidation by model risk; effective-dated rule library with a change-control board (legal, compliance, product); regression suite of reference contracts | Rising override rate, falling confidence, rising correction time; rule-change backlog |
| **Automation bias** (humans rubber-stamp suggestions) | A4, A6 | Override-rate floor alerts (too few overrides is also a warning); blind re-review samples; training that the human remains accountable | Override rate trend by user; QA defects on accepted suggestions |
| **Exception handling overload** | Analysts (A6 exceptions), specialists (A4), legal ops (A9 failures) | Capacity budget for exception queues before go-live; manual fallback and kill switch per module; back-office afternoon slack for A5 chasing; stagger A9 rollout away from month end | WIP and age per exception queue; utilization by pool and hour; month-end legal backlog |
| **Four-eyes compliance** | Credit analysis vs. Committee packaging for High-exposure cases; later A8 | Preventive SoD in the workflow (identity-based); automation accounts are never an "eye"; A9 detective check before contract release; month-end surge plan so analysts are not forced to self-package | Violation rate (5.8% baseline, target 0); attempted-violation blocks at month end |
| **KYC-before-signing bypass** | Resolve → Contract preparation (380), provisional signing (6.5%) | Explicit "KYC cleared" event required by A9 before contract release; any provisional path allowed only by documented exception approval (compliance), logged and remediated via A12 later | Provisional signing rate; contracts released without a clearance event (target 0) |
| **Outdated or ambiguous legal rules** | A9, especially Credit-line renewal (14% remediation) | Legal owns the rule library; the renewal policy is clarified before renewal rules go live; unresolved ambiguities route to a human, not to a default | Remediation rate by product; rule-ambiguity tickets |
| **Customer friction and RM resistance** | A5 (and A10 later) | Co-design checklists with RMs; one complete list replaces repeated requests (the current 52% repeat rate is itself friction); pre-filled instructions reduce RM effort | Customer response time, complaints, RM adoption rate |
| **Bottleneck migration** | Committee (weekly cadence), legal ops, activation | Monthly process-mining review of queue shifts; committee slot planning; pre-agreed capacity responses | Waiting time at Committee packaging and decision; legal ops backlog |
| **Key-person dependency persists** | Two senior specialists | Use A4 to codify their heuristics; KYC ops handle simple mismatches with assistant support; seniors approve only complex cases | Handover network centralization; share resolved by non-seniors |

Accountability does not move to the machine. Every model suggestion is advisory unless a validated deterministic rule applies. The decision of record always has a named human owner, and compliance and legal remain responsible for the rules the modules execute. Model risk management (second line) validates the models, and internal audit tests the trail.

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

### Phase 0: Foundations (months 0–2), no module go-live
- **Repair the event log:** start and complete timestamps, KYC and document-review concurrency, reliable resource IDs. Then baseline P50/P90 cycle time by segment and product, the waits per activity, and the loop frequencies from the DFG.
- **Configure controls that need no module:**
  - Identity-based SoD for High-exposure SME packaging.
  - A block on releasing a contract for signing without a "KYC cleared" event.
  - Deterministic exposure-class rules.
- **Create a structured committee decision term sheet**, which A9 requires.
- **Process fixes that stand in for deferred modules:**
  - Early valuation trigger for Equipment finance at classification.
  - Renegotiate valuer SLAs.
  - Redeploy back-office afternoon slack to missing-document follow-up.
  - Start an e-signature adoption push with RMs.
- **Verify key hypotheses in the log:** does provisional signing concentrate in the mismatch variant? What drives the 380 Resolve → Contract preparation cases?

### Phase 1: First wave (months 2–6), two deep slots used by A9 and A6
1. **A5** (light: portal, CRM). Pilot on Equipment finance and Working-capital at month 3, then scale. This is the visible quick win.
2. **A4** (light: KYC case tool, registries, read-only). Shadow mode in months 3–4; assisted mode from month 5, with 100% QA by seniors.
3. **A9** (deep slot 1). Build in months 2–5 and run in parallel with legal ops review in months 5–6. Go live at month 6 for Equipment finance and Working-capital. The go-live avoids month-end.
4. **A6** (deep slot 2). Build from month 3; shadow run from month 5 on native-digital statements (tier 1).

A5 and A4 must not write to core banking in this phase. Any such need is deferred to after month 6.

### Phase 2: Scale and harden (months 6–9)
- A9 extends to Credit-line renewal once the renewal policy is clarified.
- A6 goes live for tier-1 documents, then pilots tier 2 (scans).
- A4 expands to KYC ops for simple mismatch types.
- A monthly bottleneck-migration review covers Committee packaging and decision, legal ops month-end backlog, and analyst WIP.

### Phase 3: Stabilize and prepare year 2 (months 9–12)
- Realize the benefits: redeploy freed capacity to the next constraint (committee packaging support, exception queues).
- Year-2 business cases, in order:
  1. **A10** (now safe behind the A9 KYC gate)
  2. **A8** (inputs now reconciled via A6 and A4)
  3. **A7**
  4. **A12**
  5. **A2**
  6. **A3**
  7. **A1** and **A11**, last.

### Monitoring metrics
- **Process:** P50/P90 cycle time by segment and product; median wait at each affected activity. Loop rates:
  - Request missing documents → document review per 100 reviews
  - Resolve → KYC passes per mismatch case
  - Contract → Remediate per 100 contracts
  - Signing reminder loop
- **Resources:** utilization by pool and hour; analyst WIP; specialist queue age; share of mismatches resolved by non-seniors; legal ops month-end backlog; committee agenda load.
- **Controls:** provisional signing rate; four-eyes violations; contracts released without KYC clearance; terms-mismatch rate; audit-trail completeness.
- **Models and rules:** precision and recall against QA, confidence distribution, override rate, correction time, drift indicators, false-block rate.

### Gate criteria
Thresholds are illustrative and to be agreed with the process owner, compliance and legal.

| Module | Go / scale gate | Stop / rollback trigger |
|---|---|---|
| A5 | Repeated-request share falls from 52% toward ≤35% in the pilot; Request → review loops per 100 reviews down ≥25%; no rise in abandonment or complaints; RM feedback neutral or better | Abandonment or complaint increase; RMs bypassing the checklist |
| A4 | Shadow agreement with senior decisions at the agreed level; zero incorrect clearances in 100% QA; median wait before Resolve entity mismatch trending from 31 h toward ≤15 h; Resolve → Contract preparation without a recorded clearance = 0 | Any confirmed wrong clearance: revert to shadow and review |
| A9 | Parallel run with zero missed term mismatches against legal review; false-block rate within tolerance (e.g. ≤5%); 0 contracts released without KYC clearance; remediation trending from 8.5% toward ≤4% (renewals from 14% toward ≤7%); rule library signed off by legal | Any signed contract with wrong terms caused by a rule defect: halt auto-release, return to manual drafting with checks |
| A6 | Key-field accuracy (revenue, EBITDA, debt, equity) meets the threshold on tier 1; accounting identities pass; analyst verification time well below manual normalization time; no unexplained shift in credit-grade distribution; normalization rework not above 16% | Accuracy or drift below threshold: route that tier to manual |
| Portfolio | P90 cycle time down in the targeted variants; four-eyes violations → 0; provisional signing → near 0; no new queue exceeding its baseline without a capacity response | Bottleneck migration unmanaged for two consecutive reviews |

**Contingencies.**
- If A6 fails its tier-1 gate by month 7, swap in A7, which is light integration and can go live in months 8–12.
- If A9 slips, A12 becomes the first-wave control module, so the compliance condition is still met.
- If Phase-0 analysis shows provisional signing is unrelated to the mismatch variant, and specialist staffing can fix the 31 h queue, consider A10 (behind the A9 gate) instead of A4.

## 7. Final recommendation

**Recommend P4, the balanced flow-and-control portfolio: A9, A5, A6, A4.** It is the only portfolio that addresses all four things at once:
- the true capacity constraint, credit analysts (A6, with A5 cutting 2,800 re-review loops);
- the critical-path blocking variant and key-person risk (A4 on the 31 h mismatch queue, critical in 82% of cases);
- the two most consequential control findings (A9 gating KYC-before-signing and terms = decision);
- the two-deep-integration limit, by spending both slots on high-leverage modules (A9, A6) and keeping A5 and A4 light.

It includes a control-strengthening module in the first wave, as compliance requires. It gives management a visible customer-facing win (A5). A6 absorbs the extra analyst demand that faster upstream flow creates, which the resource observations explicitly warn about. The main sacrifices are the valuation module and e-signature. Both are partly covered by non-module measures in year 1, and both are sequenced for year 2, when they will be safer and more valuable: A10 behind the KYC gate, A8 on reconciled inputs.

**Should the highest-frequency activity (Register application) be automated first? No.**
1. **Low process value.** Register adds about 1.5 h of waiting and 8 min of service to a case that accumulates 145–300+ h. It carries about 1% of aggregate waiting. Throughput is set by the analyst pool, not by registration.
2. **No realizable saving.** Back office is the most visible queue, but it has the lowest load per FTE (about 400 h against about 3,300+ h for analysts) and falls below 65% utilization after 15:00. The labour "saving" would be idle capacity, not cost reduction.
3. **Faster registration makes the constraint worse.** Back office currently meters inflow. Auto-registration would release bursts into KYC ops, document review and the analyst queues. That lengthens the 18–28 h waits and raises month-end pressure, which is exactly when four-eyes violations (5.8%) occur.
4. **Scarce integration capacity.** A1 needs deep core-banking integration, so it would use one of only two first-half slots.
5. **No control benefit.** A1 improves no control finding. Compliance's condition and the three documented violations point elsewhere.

A1 becomes sensible in year 2: after the analyst and specialist constraints are relieved, if volume grows or the 28% non-digital intake needs handling, and when duplicate detection can use a stable customer master. Management's appetite for a high-frequency win is better met by A5. It touches every document-reviewed case, is customer-visible, is fast to deliver, and removes a loop relationship managers themselves create. Management should also get a process-level dashboard showing end-to-end cycle time and control-violation trends, not activity-level labour minutes.

Automation in this portfolio moves work and responsibility rather than removing them. Analysts become validators of extracted figures. Specialists become approvers of assisted resolutions. Legal ops become owners of a rule library. Compliance keeps full accountability for the controls the modules enforce.
