## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Stipulated facts (established by the record itself, independent of interpretation):**
- M0 is the current, controlled, formally approved normative model, and CS-9 is a controlled standard effective 2025-01-01 onward. Their authority status is not in dispute.
- PD-3 is a steering-committee decision recorded only in minutes/email; it was never incorporated into CS-9 or M0. Its effective window is 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31.
- FD-7 is an emergency directive with an explicit hard window: 2026-07-10 00:00 through 2026-07-12 23:59, scoped to critical relief suppliers, spend-capped at EUR 20,000, requiring sanctions-before-activation plus daily monitoring and quality review within two business days.
- From 2026-07-01, Notify_Requester moved to CRM; notification missingness rose from 2% to 29%; older CRM links are mutable free-text.
- The literal traces of every case are as printed; the *order* is observed, not inferred.
- Aggregates: 312 Auto_Approve cases (289 apparently eligible, 14 clearly ineligible, 9 missing attributes); 47 Request_More_Info cases, none activating before evidence arrived; 6 post-pilot Auto_Approve cases in early August.

**Disputed propositions (turn on authority, scope, identity, or logging):**
- Whether PD-3 and FD-7 can legitimately relax CS-9 at all, given neither amended the controlled standard (authority).
- Whether rejected leads (SUP-709) and reactivations (SUP-710) are in scope of M0 at all (scope; the scope document is admittedly ambiguous).
- Whether same-human / related-human approvals (SUP-712, SUP-715) are violations, given M0 does not encode the resource rule but CS-9 (and COI policy) do (identity/authority).
- Whether missing notification events (SUP-706) reflect misconduct or CRM logging loss (logging).
- Whether QA_Clearance = Quality_Review (SUP-713) and duplicate Register (SUP-711) are semantic-equivalence/data-quality issues rather than control-flow violations.

**Burden of proof.**
- *To declare noncompliance:* the reviewer bears the burden of showing, on a balance of probabilities, that (a) a controlled rule applied to the case, (b) the case fell within that rule's scope and time, and (c) the behavior violated it, with the deviation being a genuine business-behavior deviation and not solely a logging or labeling artifact. Where the only evidence of a violation is absence of an event that migrated to a lossy channel, the burden is not met absent corroboration.
- *To accept an exception:* the party invoking the exception (Operations) bears the burden of showing that a *duly authorized* overlay existed, that the case fell within its explicit scope, time, and quantitative limits, and that all compensating controls were satisfied. Desirability, normalization, or good outcomes do not discharge this burden. An exception that has expired or was never authorized to weaken CS-9 fails as a matter of law regardless of merit.

This asymmetry is deliberate: control weakening requires positive authority; control conformance is the default.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The operation is materially noncompliant, and M0/CS-9 should be enforced.

**Authority and expiry are dispositive, not technicalities.** PD-3 was never incorporated into CS-9 or M0, and the Compliance observer expressly stated she had not approved weakening CS-9. A steering committee cannot, by minute and email, override a controlled standard. Even if PD-3 were valid, it expired 2026-07-31; FD-7 expired 2026-07-12 23:59. SUP-714 (Auto_Approve on 2026-08-02) and the six early-August Auto_Approve cases are post-expiry uses of a control relaxation that no longer existed and never had standing to weaken CS-9. SUP-705 (2026-07-13) is a post-expiry emergency use; "the flood still factually existed" is precisely the argument the rule of law forecloses — directives expire on their terms, not on operational sentiment. Normalization is an aggravating factor, not a defense: Operations "normalized the pilot flow and believed permanent approval was imminent," which shows a control-drift culture rather than a justified exception.

**Deviations hidden by M0 are still deviations.** M0 does not encode the CS-9 resource rule (requester ≠ approver), so a trace can "fit M0" and still violate CS-9. SUP-712 (same human via two aliases) and SUP-715 (approver and quality reviewer are spouses in the same reporting line, no disclosure recorded) are real control failures: separation-of-duties and conflict-of-interest are the substance CS-9 protects, and the model's silence conceals them. These must be surfaced, not excused because the box-and-arrow flow matched.

**High-risk cases are severe.** SUP-703 is the clearest violation: a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier auto-approved despite ineligibility, with a first purchase of EUR 180,000 — far above the EUR 20,000 emergency cap and outside PD-3's LOW-risk/approved-group scope. SUP-708 activated a HIGH-risk supplier 40 minutes *before* Sanctions_Check with no emergency or pilot applying — a direct breach of the one invariant every source preserves (sanctions before activation). That no transaction occurred before clearance mitigates loss but not the control breach. Internal Audit's flagging of SUP-703 and SUP-708 is well founded.

**Aggregate control drift.** 14 clearly ineligible Auto_Approve cases plus 9 with missing eligibility attributes plus 6 post-pilot August cases demonstrate systematic weakening of manual approval. The 29% notification missingness after CRM migration is itself a control/evidence failure that Operations owns.

**Desirable ≠ authorized.** Request_More_Info (SUP-707; 47 cases) may be excellent practice, but "desirable" behavior does not retroactively amend a controlled model. The remedy is to amend policy, not to bless unlogged, un-modeled loops after the fact.

The prosecution concedes genuine model/data defects (see §3) but insists they do not convert unauthorized, expired, or high-risk control weakening into compliance.

## 3. Defense Brief

M0 is obsolete, incomplete, incorrectly scoped, and in several cases applied to inadequate data.

**Concurrency is mis-modeled as forcing an ordering illusion.** Within `checks`, Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review are concurrent, which is correct; but M0 forces `checks` entirely before `decision` and `decision` before activation, so any legitimate provisional-activation or reordered-check pattern is scored as a violation even when the business invariant (sanctions before activation) holds. SUP-704 satisfies FD-7 exactly (sanctions before activation, EUR 18,000 < cap, daily monitoring, quality/approval next business day). Its "deviation" is entirely an artifact of M0 lacking an emergency overlay — this is a MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT, not misconduct.

**Missing loop for Request_More_Info.** M0 has no loop and no activity for Request_More_Info, yet 47 cases use it and none activated before evidence arrived (SUP-707). This is Compliance-endorsed, risk-reducing behavior the model literally cannot express. Scoring it as nonconformance is a model incompleteness, not a business defect.

**Pilot/emergency overlays are real governance instruments.** SUP-702 is fully PD-3-eligible (LOW-risk DE, approved group, 2026-06-15, in window), sanctions before activation, quality next business day, stable notification_id at +2h. Within a properly layered architecture this is a JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION; M0's inability to represent the auto-approval route is the defect.

**CRM migration corrupts the evidence base.** SUP-706 has no notification event in the onboarding log, but CRM shows a message at +3h — the "missingness" is a known 29% data-loss artifact of the 2026-07-01 migration, and the mutable text link is a data-integrity weakness, not proof of a skipped notification. Declaring noncompliance here would punish a logging change. This is DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE.

**Scope errors.** SUP-709 (rejected on sanctions pre-screen) may not be an M0 case at all — the model owner says M0 begins only after pre-screen acceptance, and the scope document is ambiguous. SUP-710 (reactivation with still-valid certification) has no explicit CS-9 procedure; its lack of Register_Supplier/Quality_Review reflects that it is a different process, not a defective run of this one. Judging either against M0 is a scoping error.

**Label equivalence and data quality.** SUP-713's QA_Clearance is an approved rename of Quality_Review with audit-verified equivalent content; treating it as a missing activity is a mapping/label-catalog gap. SUP-711's duplicated Register_Supplier shares one source transaction ID — a migration duplication (data quality), not a real second registration.

**Trace deviation ≠ business misconduct.** Operations is right that a high raw deviation rate largely reflects obsolescence and incomplete scope. The defense nonetheless concedes genuine risk: SUP-703 and SUP-708 are real, severe breaches; post-expiry Auto_Approve (SUP-705, SUP-714, the six August cases) exceeded even the exceptions' own limits and cannot be defended; and informal practice, however desirable, must be formalized before it is relied upon.

**2. Severity/confidence pattern.** The high-severity, high-confidence findings cluster tightly: SUP-703, SUP-708 (control-flow/high-risk), and SUP-712, SUP-715 (resource/COI). Expiry cases (SUP-705, SUP-714) are moderate-severity, high-confidence noncompliance. Defects and data-quality items are low severity.

**3. System-level judgment.**
- *Operational compliance:* Partially noncompliant with a genuine control-drift problem. The evidence establishes (a) two severe control-flow breaches, (b) systematic post-expiry and ineligible Auto_Approve use (14 ineligible + 6 August + SUP-705/714), and (c) hidden resource/COI failures that the model conceals. This is more than obsolescence.
- *Model validity:* M0 is materially incomplete and mis-scoped. It lacks auto-approval, emergency/provisional, and Request_More_Info routes; it does not encode resource/COI, amount, risk, date, eligibility, or reactivation/reject scope; and it interacts badly with the CRM evidence change. A large fraction of the raw "deviation rate" is model error, so the raw rate cannot be used as the compliance metric.
- *Reconciliation:* Operations is right that the model is broken and right that most deviations are not misconduct — but wrong that this excuses expired, ineligible, or SoD/COI breaches. Both the operation and the model require remediation.

**4. Where I reject each side.**
- *Reject the prosecution* on SUP-704 (valid FD-7), SUP-702 (valid PD-3), SUP-706 (data loss, not skipped step), SUP-707 (endorsed loop the model can't express), SUP-711/713 (data-quality/label), and SUP-709/710 (scope). Enforcing M0 literally here would penalize model defects and logging changes, not misconduct.
- *Reject the defense* on SUP-703 and SUP-708 (severe, indefensible), on SUP-705/714 and the August cohort (expiry and lack of amendment are dispositive; "factual emergency" and "imminent approval" are not authority), and on SUP-712/715 (model silence on resource/COI rules does not repeal the CS-9/COI rules themselves). Desirability and normalization never discharge the burden to show authorized, in-scope relaxation.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

A single POWL cannot carry authority, amounts, dates, identity, and eligibility. Use a layered architecture; each concern lives at the layer that can actually enforce it.

**Layer A — Control-flow (POWL).** A revised M1 with an XOR over four approval routes after concurrent checks: standard manual approval; low-risk auto-approval; emergency/provisional (activation before quality/approval); and rejection. Concurrency of Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review is preserved for the standard route; the emergency route models Activate before Quality/Approval with Sanctions still preceding activation. A Request_More_Info self-loop is approximated by a redo/loop construct before the decision. Notify_Requester precedes Close_Case on every route. Belongs in POWL because it is pure control-flow ordering and choice.

**Layer B — Data-aware eligibility/amount/time rules (pandas/data checks + DECLARE-style constraints).** Route selection is *gated* by data the POWL cannot see: auto-approval requires LOW risk AND approved-group membership AND an active pilot authority on the event date; emergency route requires an active FD directive on the event date, spend ≤ cap, daily-monitoring evidence, and quality-review-within-2-business-days. These are precondition predicates evaluated over an eligibility snapshot and effective-dated rule versions. Belongs in pandas/data checks (quantitative, temporal) with DECLARE/log-skeleton constraints for ordering-response rules (e.g., "Activate response-precedes Sanctions_Check", "Auto_Approve requires eligibility flag").

**Layer C — Resource and conflict-of-interest rules (organizational controls + data checks).** requester ≠ approver (resolved through an alias/identity table); quality-reviewer and approver must not share a conflict relationship (same person, spouse, same reporting line) absent a recorded disclosure and independent re-review. POWL cannot express resource identity; enforce via data checks over resolved actor identities plus an org-relationship graph, backed by an HR-verified control.

**Layer D — Policy-overlay governance for pilots/emergencies (organizational controls + versioned rule registry).** Every relaxation (PD-3, FD-7) must exist as an effective-dated, authority-signed rule version with explicit scope, quantitative caps, compensating controls, and hard expiry. Conformance is checked against the rule version *in force on the immutable event timestamp*, not the record timestamp. No expiry extension without a new signed version. This directly resolves SUP-705/714 and the August cohort.

**Layer E — Explicit scope rules (scope catalog + organizational controls).** Define, authoritatively: rejected/pre-screen leads (are they M0 cases? — resolves SUP-709), reactivations (a distinct sub-process with cert-validity rules — resolves SUP-710), and withdrawals. Also a label/version catalog mapping QA_Clearance↔Quality_Review with effective dates (SUP-713), and migration dedup keyed on source transaction ID (SUP-711).

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# --- Activities ---
register    = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions   = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality     = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
rmi         = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
skip_rmi    = SilentTransition()

manual_appr = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
auto_appr   = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
activate    = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
reject      = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
notify      = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close       = Transition(label="Close_Case")

# --- Request_More_Info: repeatable mechanism ---
# LOOP(body=Request_More_Info, redo=silent): 0..n info requests before the decision.
# Operator.LOOP children = [do, redo]; this executes 'do' then optionally repeats.
rmi_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[rmi, skip_rmi])

# --- Standard route: concurrent sanctions+quality, then manual approval, then activate ---
std_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])  # concurrent, no edge
std_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[std_checks, manual_appr, activate])
std_branch.order.add_edge(std_checks, manual_appr)
std_branch.order.add_edge(manual_appr, activate)

# --- Low-risk auto-approval route: sanctions before activate; auto-approve replaces manual ---
# Quality_Review may follow activation (per pilot); modeled concurrent-after-sanctions.
auto_pre = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, auto_appr])
auto_pre.order.add_edge(sanctions, auto_appr)
auto_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[auto_pre, activate, quality])
auto_branch.order.add_edge(auto_pre, activate)
# quality left unordered vs activate => may occur before OR after activation

# --- Emergency/provisional route: sanctions -> activate; quality & approval AFTER ---
emg_pre = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, activate])
emg_pre.order.add_edge(sanctions, activate)
emg_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[emg_pre, quality, manual_appr])
emg_branch.order.add_edge(emg_pre, quality)
emg_branch.order.add_edge(emg_pre, manual_appr)

# --- Rejection route ---
reject_branch = reject

# --- Decision: exclusive choice over the four routes ---
decision = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                        children=[std_branch, auto_branch, emg_branch, reject_branch])

# --- Full model: register -> (optional info loop) -> decision -> notify -> close ---
M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, rmi_loop, decision, notify, close])
M1.order.add_edge(register, rmi_loop)
M1.order.add_edge(rmi_loop, decision)
M1.order.add_edge(decision, notify)
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)
```

**Stated limitations / defensible approximations.**
- POWL cannot gate which XOR branch is *permitted*: auto/emergency routes here are *reachable* for any case, so the model over-approximates. The eligibility, amount, risk, date, and authority conditions that separate SUP-702 (valid) from SUP-703/714 (invalid) MUST be enforced in the overlay layers below, not in POWL.
- `Request_More_Info` is modeled as a LOOP whose redo child is silent, approximating "0..n info requests then proceed." True guard "no activation before requested evidence arrives" is a data constraint, not control-flow.
- The auto route leaves Quality_Review unordered relative to Activate_Supplier to permit the pilot's "quality up to 2 business days after activation"; the 2-business-day bound itself is a data-time check.
- Resource/COI identity cannot be expressed in POWL at all and is delegated entirely to Layer C.

**Normalized policy-overlay table (evaluated per case against the immutable event timestamp).**

| rule_id | version | effective_start | effective_end | route_gated | scope_predicate | quantitative_limit | compensating_controls | authority_signoff |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CS-9 | v1 | 2025-01-01 | (open) | standard | all in-scope onboarding | none | requester≠approver; COI disclosure | Controlled/approved |
| PD-3 | v1 | 2026-06-01 | 2026-07-31 | auto | risk=LOW AND group=approved AND country=DE | none | sanctions+MDM pre-activation; quality ≤2 bd | Steering cmte (NOT a CS-9 amendment) |
| FD-7 | v1 | 2026-07-10T00:00 | 2026-07-12T23:59 | emergency | supplier=critical_relief | spend ≤ EUR 20,000 | sanctions pre-activation; daily monitoring; quality+approval ≤2 bd | Emergency directive |
| RMI | proposed | TBD | (open) | any (pre-decision) | requires missing evidence | none | activation blocked until evidence arrives | Pending Compliance approval |
| REACT | proposed | TBD | (open) | reactivation sub-process | prior cert still valid | none | re-verify sanctions | Pending policy |

Route validity = (branch executed) AND (a rule_id gating that branch is in force on event_ts) AND (scope_predicate true on eligibility_snapshot) AND (quantitative_limit satisfied) AND (compensating_controls evidenced). Any failure => NONCOMPLIANT; missing snapshot/evidence => DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE.

**Event/data-collection proposal (to make the overlays enforceable and auditable):**
- **Stable notification IDs:** persist `notification_id` as an immutable foreign key in the onboarding log at emission time (fixes the SUP-706 / 29%-loss problem); deprecate mutable text links.
- **Rule/version IDs:** stamp each case with the `rule_id`+`version` claimed for its route, plus the resolved `authority_signoff`.
- **Eligibility snapshots:** capture risk level, corporate-group membership, and country as an immutable snapshot at decision time (not re-derived later), enabling SUP-702 vs SUP-703 discrimination.
- **Actor aliases:** an identity table resolving aliases to canonical persons (SUP-712) plus an org-relationship graph for COI (SUP-715).
- **Delegation/authority evidence:** signed record for any approval, including emergency retrospective approvals (SUP-704/705).
- **Emergency/pilot authority:** link each exception case to a specific overlay `rule_id`+`version`; block route selection when no active authority exists on `event_ts`.
- **Immutable effective/record timestamps:** store both the business `event_ts` and system `record_ts`; conformance always evaluates against `event_ts` so post-dated links and late edits cannot manufacture eligibility (resolves SUP-705/714 and the mutable-link risk).
- **Label/version catalog:** effective-dated activity-name mappings (QA_Clearance↔Quality_Review) so approved renames are not scored as missing activities (SUP-713).
- **Migration dedup:** deduplicate on `source_transaction_id` before conformance (SUP-711).
