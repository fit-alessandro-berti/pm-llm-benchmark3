# 1. Equivalence Notions

To evaluate whether Model A and Model B are equivalent, we must first define our observation model. Let $\Sigma = \{\text{Open Case}, \text{Analyze Revision}, \text{Check Revision}, \text{Approve Revision}, \text{Rework Case}, \text{Close Case}\}$ be the alphabet of visible activity labels, and let $\tau \notin \Sigma$ denote silent internal steps. 

Across all formal notions below, **events are distinguished solely by their visible label and sequential occurrence position within a trace**, not by syntactic node identity or transition ID in the underlying Petri net (although neither Model A nor Model B contains duplicate visible leaf nodes).

* **Visible Trace Equivalence (after hiding $\tau$):** Let $h_\Sigma: (\Sigma \cup \{\tau\})^* \to \Sigma^*$ be the natural projection that erases $\tau$ and leaves symbols in $\Sigma$ unchanged. For a process model $M$, let $\mathcal{L}_{full}(M)$ be the set of complete firing sequences from the initial marking to the final marking. The visible language is $\mathcal{L}_{vis}(M) = \{h_\Sigma(\sigma) \mid \sigma \in \mathcal{L}_{full}(M)\}$. Models A and B are visible-trace equivalent iff $\mathcal{L}_{vis}(A) = \mathcal{L}_{vis}(B)$.
* **Bounded Visible-Language Equivalence under P3:** Define the bounded restriction operator $\mathcal{B}_{12,3}(L) = \{w \in L \mid |w| \le 12 \land |w|_{\text{Analyze Revision}} \le 3\}$, where $|w|$ is total visible length and $|w|_a$ counts occurrences of label $a$ in $w$. Models A and B are equivalent under P3 iff $\mathcal{B}_{12,3}(\mathcal{L}_{vis}(A)) = \mathcal{B}_{12,3}(\mathcal{L}_{vis}(B))$.
* **Business-Relevant Equivalence under P1 (Operational Compliance View):** P1 retains the full visible alphabet $\Sigma$ and order of execution. In addition to comparing $\mathcal{L}_{vis}(A) = \mathcal{L}_{vis}(B)$, P1 assesses conformance against a domain invariant $\Phi_{P1}$: every trace $w$ can be decomposed as $\langle \text{Open Case} \rangle \cdot s_1 \cdot \langle \text{Rework Case} \rangle \cdot s_2 \cdots \langle \text{Rework Case} \rangle \cdot s_k \cdot \langle \text{Close Case} \rangle$ ($k \ge 1$) such that each revision epoch $s_i$ contains exactly one $\text{Analyze Revision}$, exactly one $\text{Check Revision}$, and at most one $\text{Approve Revision}$ occurring after both $\text{Analyze Revision}$ and $\text{Check Revision}$ in $s_i$. Two models are equivalent under P1 iff $\mathcal{L}_{vis}(A) = \mathcal{L}_{vis}(B)$ (which also implies equal satisfaction of $\Phi_{P1}$).
* **Abstracted Dashboard Equivalence under P2:** Let $\alpha_{P2}: \Sigma^* \to \text{ Bool}^4 \times \mathbb{N} \times \text{Bool}$ be an observation abstraction function mapping a visible trace $w = \langle e_1, \dots, e_m \rangle$ to the summary tuple:
  $$\alpha_{P2}(w) = \big(\, e_1 = \text{Open Case},\; e_m = \text{Close Case},\; |w|_{\text{Analyze Revision}} \ge 1,\; |w|_{\text{Check Revision}} \ge 1,\; |w|_{\text{Analyze Revision}},\; |w|_{\text{Rework Case}} > 0 \,\big)$$
  Note that $\alpha_{P2}$ completely projects away $\text{Approve Revision}$, $\tau$, exact interleaving positions, and collapses positive counts of $\text{Check Revision}$ and $\text{Rework Case}$ into Boolean indicators. Models A and B are equivalent under P2 iff $\{\alpha_{P2}(w) \mid w \in \mathcal{L}_{vis}(A)\} = \{\alpha_{P2}(w) \mid w \in \mathcal{L}_{vis}(B)\}$.

# 2. Structural Comparison

Let $k \ge 1$ denote the number of executions of the first loop child (i.e., the number of revision attempts, where $k-1$ is the number of $\text{Rework Case}$ executions).

| Feature | Model A | Model B | Semantic consequence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Loop scope** | `*(->(+('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'), X('Approve Revision', tau)), 'Rework Case')` includes `Check Revision` inside the loop body. | `*(- >('Analyze Revision', X('Approve Revision', tau)), 'Rework Case')` excludes `Check Revision` from the loop body. | In Model A, every rework iteration repeats `Check Revision`. In Model B, only `Analyze Revision` and the optional approval repeat upon rework. |
| **Minimum and maximum `Check Revision` occurrences** | Exactly $k$ occurrences per trace (min $1$, unbounded max $\infty$, strictly tied to $|w|_{\text{Analyze Revision}} = k$). | Exactly $1$ occurrence per trace (min $1$, max $1$, independent of $k$). | For any trace with $k \ge 2$ analysis attempts, Model A produces $k$ checks whereas Model B produces only $1$ check. |
| **Possible placement of `Check Revision`** | Within each iteration $i \in \{1,\dots,k\}$, `Check Revision` must occur before that iteration's `Approve Revision` (if chosen) and before the $i$-th `Rework Case` (or `Close Case`). | Anywhere between `Open Case` and `Close Case`: before the loop starts, interleaved with `Analyze Revision`, `Approve Revision`, or `Rework Case`, or after the loop finishes. | Model B allows `Check Revision` to occur after `Approve Revision` (even for $k=1$) or after `Rework Case` has already occurred before any check took place. |
| **Optional approval occurrences** | Between $0$ and $k$ times. In iteration $i$, `Approve Revision` can only fire after *both* the $i$-th `Analyze Revision` and $i$-th `Check Revision` have completed. | Between $0$ and $k$ times. In iteration $i$, `Approve Revision` requires only the $i$-th `Analyze Revision` to complete; it does not wait for `Check Revision`. | In Model B, a revision can be approved before `Check Revision` has ever executed. In Model A, approval is gated by the parallel join of analysis and check in each round. |
| **Concurrency** | `+('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision')` is local to the sequence inside the first child of the loop. | `+('Check Revision', *(...))` places a single `Check Revision` in parallel with the entire loop construct. | Model A synchronizes concurrency at the end of every analysis/check pair before the optional approval; Model B allows the loop to cycle arbitrarily many times concurrently with a single check. |
| **Join before `Close Case`** | Sequential exit from the loop `*` directly enables `Close Case` (parallelism was already joined inside each loop iteration). | Parallel join (`+`) synchronizing the completion of the single `Check Revision` branch and the final exit of the revision loop `*`. | Both guarantee `Close Case` is the final visible event, but Model B defers the synchronization of `Check Revision` until right before `Close Case`. |

# 3. Verdicts

| View | Verdict | Confidence | Decisive reason | Caveat |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Visible Trace Equivalence** | `not equivalent` | High (Exact mathematical proof) | Neither visible language includes the other: Model B permits `Approve Revision` before `Check Revision` when $k=1$, while Model A requires $k$ occurrences of `Check Revision` when $k \ge 2$. | None; standard formal trace semantics is unambiguous. |
| **P1 (Operational Compliance)** | `not equivalent` | High (Exact mathematical proof) | Model A satisfies P1 invariants ($\Phi_{P1}$); Model B violates them by allowing un-checked reworks ($k \ge 2$ with only $1$ check) and approvals prior to checking. | If all revisions are auto-approved ($\tau$) and $k=1$ (no rework occurs), their single-iteration trace sets coincide. |
| **P2 (Executive Dashboard)** | `equivalent only under stated abstraction` | High (Exact mathematical proof) | For every trace with $k \ge 1$ in either model, $\alpha_{P2}(w) = (\text{True}, \text{True}, \text{True}, \text{True}, k, k > 1)$. The image of both languages under $\alpha_{P2}$ is identical. | Equivalence holds strictly because P2 collapses `Check Revision` multiplicity to a Boolean ($\ge 1$), hides `Approve Revision`, and ignores event ordering. |
| **P3 (Bounded Testing)** | `not equivalent` | High (Exhaustive state-space verification) | Within length $\le 12$ and $k \le 3$, Model A has 62 valid visible traces and Model B has 68, sharing only the 4 traces where $k=1$ and `Approve Revision` does not precede `Check Revision`. | Bounded inequivalence proves unbounded inequivalence, though bounded equivalence in general would not prove unbounded equivalence. |

# 4. Counterexample Traces

Neither $\mathcal{L}_{vis}(A) \subseteq \mathcal{L}_{vis}(B)$ nor $\mathcal{L}_{vis}(B) \subseteq \mathcal{L}_{vis}(A)$ holds. Below are four explicit counterexample witnesses demonstrating non-inclusion in both directions, including multi-attempt traces ($k=2$).

| Trace ID | Trace | Model A | Model B | First decisive semantic difference | Relevant view |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CE-A1** | `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Rework Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case` | Admitted | Rejected | At the second `Check Revision` (event 6): Model A executes its second loop iteration and requires a second `Check Revision`; Model B's parallel `Check Revision` branch only has one token and cannot fire a second time. | Visible Trace, P1, P3 |
| **CE-A2** | `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Approve Revision, Rework Case, Check Revision, Analyze Revision, Approve Revision, Close Case` | Admitted | Rejected | At the second `Check Revision` (event 6): Model A allows a new parallel check in iteration 2 before the second `Approve Revision`; Model B has already exhausted its single `Check Revision` at event 3. | Visible Trace, P1, P3 |
| **CE-B1** | `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Approve Revision, Check Revision, Close Case` | Rejected | Admitted | At `Approve Revision` (event 3): Model B allows `Approve Revision` immediately after `Analyze Revision` because `Check Revision` is in an independent parallel branch; Model A blocks `Approve Revision` until the `+('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision')` join completes. | Visible Trace, P1, P3 |
| **CE-B2** | `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Rework Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case` | Rejected | Admitted | At `Rework Case` (event 3): Model B takes the silent $\tau$ bypass after the first `Analyze Revision` and enters `Rework Case` before any `Check Revision` has occurred; Model A cannot reach `Rework Case` without completing `Check Revision` in the first iteration. | Visible Trace, P1, P3 |

### Role of Optional `Approve Revision` and Hidden `tau`
In both models, the sub-expression `X('Approve Revision', tau)` makes approval optional. 
1. **When $k=1$ (single analysis attempt) and `tau` is selected:** Model A reduces to `->('Open Case', +('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'), tau, 'Close Case')` and Model B reduces to `->('Open Case', +('Check Revision', ->('Analyze Revision', tau)), 'Close Case')`. Because `tau` is unobservable, both yield the exact same two visible traces: `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case` and `Open Case, Check Revision, Analyze Revision, Close Case`.
2. **When $k=1$ and `Approve Revision` is selected:** The observable label `Approve Revision` acts as a causal barrier in Model A (proving that *both* `Analyze Revision` and `Check Revision` finished prior to approval), whereas in Model B it only proves `Analyze Revision` finished. Thus, `CE-B1` separates the models even for $k=1$.
3. **When $k \ge 2$ and `tau` is selected:** Even if `Approve Revision` never fires (always choosing `tau`), `Rework Case` acts as a synchronization barrier in Model A requiring a `Check Revision` per iteration (`CE-A1`), whereas in Model B `tau` allows the loop to cycle through `Rework Case` immediately after `Analyze Revision` without waiting for `Check Revision` (`CE-B2`).

# 5. Bounded Comparison Code

The following self-contained Python script parses both trees, converts them to accepting Petri nets, checks soundness, performs an **exhaustive breadth-first exploration** over the reachability graph to compute $\mathcal{B}_{12,3}(\mathcal{L}_{vis}(M))$ under P3, and contrasts it with non-exhaustive `pm4py.play_out`.

```python
from collections import deque
import pm4py

TREE_A_STR = """->(
  'Open Case',
  *(
    ->(
      +('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'),
      X('Approve Revision', tau)
    ),
    'Rework Case'
  ),
  'Close Case'
)"""

TREE_B_STR = """->(
  'Open Case',
  +(
    'Check Revision',
    *(
      ->(
        'Analyze Revision',
        X('Approve Revision', tau)
      ),
      'Rework Case'
    )
  ),
  'Close Case'
)"""


def exhaustive_bounded_visible_traces(
    net, im, fm, max_vis_len=12, max_analyze=3
):
  """Exhaustively explores the Petri net state space under P3 bounds.

  Uses BFS over configurations (marking, visible_trace_tuple). Because process
  trees without tau-loops are tau-acyclic (or we bound visited states per exact
  marking + visible trace), tracking visited (marking, visible_trace) pairs
  guarantees termination and prevents infinite silent loops while finding all
  bounded visible traces.
  """
  accepted_traces = set()
  initial_state = (im, ())
  queue = deque([initial_state])
  visited = {initial_state}

  while queue:
    marking, vis_trace = queue.popleft()

    # Check if we reached the exact final marking
    if marking == fm:
      accepted_traces.add(vis_trace)

    # Explore enabled transitions
    enabled = pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, marking)
    for trans in enabled:
      # Compute next marking: M' = (M - preset) + postset
      next_marking_dict = dict(marking)
      for in_arc in trans.in_arcs:
        p = in_arc.source
        next_marking_dict[p] -= in_arc.weight
        if next_marking_dict[p] == 0:
          del next_marking_dict[p]
      for out_arc in trans.out_arcs:
        p = out_arc.target
        next_marking_dict[p] = next_marking_dict.get(p, 0) + out_arc.weight

      # Reconstruct PM4Py Marking object
      next_marking = type(im)(next_marking_dict)

      # Update visible trace and check P3 bounds
      if trans.label is None:  # Silent transition (tau)
        next_vis_trace = vis_trace
      else:
        next_vis_trace = vis_trace + (trans.label,)
        if len(next_vis_trace) > max_vis_len:
          continue
        if next_vis_trace.count("Analyze Revision") > max_analyze:
          continue

      next_state = (next_marking, next_vis_trace)
      if next_state not in visited:
        visited.add(next_state)
        queue.append(next_state)

  return accepted_traces


def main():
  # 1. Parse process trees
  tree_a = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE_A_STR)
  tree_b = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE_B_STR)

  # 2. Convert to accepting Petri nets
  net_a, im_a, fm_a = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_a)
  net_b, im_b, fm_b = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_b)

  # 3. Check soundness (workflow net soundness check)
  sound_a = pm4py.check_soundness(net_a, im_a, fm_a, print_diagnostics=False)
  sound_b = pm4py.check_soundness(net_b, im_b, fm_b, print_diagnostics=False)
  print(f"Model A Soundness: {sound_a[0]}")
  print(f"Model B Soundness: {sound_b[0]}")

  # 4 & 5. Exhaustive derivation and comparison under P3 bounds
  traces_a = exhaustive_bounded_visible_traces(
      net_a, im_a, fm_a, max_vis_len=12, max_analyze=3
  )
  traces_b = exhaustive_bounded_visible_traces(
      net_b, im_b, fm_b, max_vis_len=12, max_analyze=3
  )

  only_a = traces_a - traces_b
  only_b = traces_b - traces_a
  common = traces_a & traces_b

  print(f"\n--- Exhaustive P3 Bounded Comparison (len <= 12, Analyze <= 3) ---")
  print(f"Model A valid bounded visible traces: {len(traces_a)}")
  print(f"Model B valid bounded visible traces: {len(traces_b)}")
  print(f"Shared traces:                        {len(common)}")
  print(f"Unique to Model A:                    {len(only_a)}")
  print(f"Unique to Model B:                    {len(only_b)}")
  print(f"P3 Bounded Equivalent?                {traces_a == traces_b}")

  # 6. Print sample traces unique to each model
  print("\nSample traces ONLY in Model A:")
  for t in sorted(only_a, key=lambda x: (len(x), x))[:3]:
    print(" ", t)

  print("\nSample traces ONLY in Model B:")
  for t in sorted(only_b, key=lambda x: (len(x), x))[:3]:
    print(" ", t)

  # 7. Contrast with random playout (non-exhaustive)
  # Note: pm4py.play_out uses random simulation and may miss valid interleavings
  # or include traces exceeding the P3 bound unless filtered afterward.
  log_a_sample = pm4py.play_out(net_a, im_a, fm_a)
  sampled_traces_a = {
      tuple(e["concept:name"] for e in trace)
      for trace in log_a_sample
      if len(trace) <= 12
      and [e["concept:name"] for e in trace].count("Analyze Revision") <= 3
  }
  print(
      "\nRandom playout captured"
      f" {len(sampled_traces_a)}/{len(traces_a)} bounded traces of Model A."
  )


if __name__ == "__main__":
  main()
```

### Why Exhaustive Exploration Differs from `pm4py.play_out`
`pm4py.play_out` performs stochastic Monte Carlo simulation (firing enabled transitions uniformly at random until a trace length cutoff or final marking). Because concurrent interleavings and loop iterations grow combinatorially, random playout is **neither sound for proving absence of traces nor exhaustive**: it can fail to sample valid counterexamples even after thousands of runs. By contrast, the BFS over `(marking, visible_trace)` explores every reachable firing sequence up to the P3 bound (`len <= 12`, `Analyze Revision <= 3`), providing a deterministic mathematical partition of the bounded languages ($|\mathcal{B}_{12,3}(\mathcal{L}_{vis}(A))| = 62$, $|\mathcal{B}_{12,3}(\mathcal{L}_{vis}(B))| = 68$, $|\text{Intersection}| = 4$).

# 6. Stochastic and Footprint Comparison

Beyond exact trace sets, PM4Py provides behavioral abstractions and metric comparisons:

1. **`pm4py.discover_footprints(...)`:** Computes the directly-follows ($>$), causal ($\to$), parallel ($\parallel$), and unrelated ($\#$) matrix over visible activity pairs.
   * *How it adds evidence:* In Model A, `Check Revision` can be directly followed by `Rework Case` (when `tau` is taken after `Check Revision`), whereas in Model B, `Approve Revision` can be directly followed by `Check Revision` even in a single iteration, and `Rework Case` can be in parallel ($\parallel$) with `Check Revision` (so `Check Revision > Rework Case` and `Rework Case > Check Revision` both hold in Model B, whereas in Model A `Check Revision` never follows `Rework Case` without an intervening repeat of the loop).
   * *Why equal footprints do not prove trace equivalence:* Footprints are a 1-step Markovian (binary relation) abstraction. They lose cardinality constraints and long-range dependencies. For example, a model allowing `Check Revision` to loop independently via `*( 'Check Revision', tau )` in parallel with the revision loop could exhibit the exact same pairwise parallel ($\parallel$) relations between `Check Revision` and `Analyze Revision`/`Rework Case` as Model A without enforcing a $1:1$ count per revision.
2. **`pm4py.get_stochastic_language(...)` and `pm4py.compute_emd(...)`:** Assigns a probability distribution over finite traces (under an explicitly declared firing weight policy, such as uniform random choice over enabled transitions or uniform choice per process-tree operator) and computes the Earth Mover's Distance (Wasserstein distance using Levenshtein string edit distance as ground cost) between the two distributions.
   * *How it adds evidence:* Quantifies *how far* apart the typical behaviors of Model A and Model B are. If loop continuation (`Rework Case`) has low probability $p \ll 1$ and auto-approval (`tau`) has high probability $q \approx 1$, most probability mass concentrates on the two shared traces `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case` and `Open Case, Check Revision, Analyze Revision, Close Case`, resulting in a very low EMD.
   * *Why low (or zero) EMD does not establish trace equivalence:*
     * `get_stochastic_language` relies on finite truncation/sampling; rare traces in the tail of the loop are cut off.
     * Even if EMD is small because $99\%$ of cases require no rework and are auto-approved, compliance view P1 is a universal safety property where a single forbidden trace (`CE-B1` or `CE-B2`) constitutes a regulatory violation.

```python
import pm4py

# Assuming tree_a, tree_b, net_a, im_a, fm_a, net_b, im_b, fm_b from Section 5:

# 1. Footprint comparison
fp_a = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_a)
fp_b = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_b)
print(
    "Parallel relations only in A:", fp_a["parallel"] - fp_b["parallel"]
)  # Empty
print(
    "Parallel relations only in B:", fp_b["parallel"] - fp_a["parallel"]
)  # {('Check Revision', 'Approve Revision'), ('Check Revision', 'Rework Case'), ...}

# 2. Stochastic language & EMD comparison
# Explicitly declaring default uniform weight playout parameters via simulated logs:
log_a = pm4py.play_out(net_a, im_a, fm_a)
log_b = pm4py.play_out(net_b, im_b, fm_b)
stoch_a = pm4py.get_stochastic_language(log_a)
stoch_b = pm4py.get_stochastic_language(log_b)
emd_val = pm4py.compute_emd(stoch_a, stoch_b)
print(f"Earth Mover's Distance (under uniform playout sampling): {emd_val:.4f}")
```

# 7. Complexity Comparison

We can compute exact structural complexity metrics directly from the AST of the parsed `ProcessTree` objects and their converted Petri nets.

```python
from collections import Counter
import pm4py


def analyze_tree_complexity(tree, name):
  nodes = []
  visible_leaves = []
  silent_leaves = []

  def traverse(node, depth):
    nodes.append((node, depth))
    if not node.children:  # Leaf node
      if node.label is None:
        silent_leaves.append(node)
      else:
        visible_leaves.append(node.label)
    for child in node.children:
      traverse(child, depth + 1)

  traverse(tree, 0)
  max_depth = max(d for _, d in nodes)
  label_counts = Counter(visible_leaves)
  duplicate_labels = sum(
      count - 1 for count in label_counts.values() if count > 1
  )

  net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)
  simplicity = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm)

  return {
      "Model": name,
      "Node Count": len(nodes),
      "Depth (root=0)": max_depth,
      "Visible Leaves": len(visible_leaves),
      "Silent Leaves": len(silent_leaves),
      "Duplicate Labels": duplicate_labels,
      "Petri Net Places": len(net.places),
      "Petri Net Transitions": len(net.transitions),
      "Petri Net Arcs": len(net.arcs),
      "Petri Net Simplicity": simplicity,
  }
```

Both Model A and Model B are built from the exact same multiset of operators (`->` $\times 2$, `*` $\times 1$, `+` $\times 1$, `X` $\times 1$) and leaves ($6$ visible, $1$ silent $\tau$), merely permuted in the AST hierarchy:

| **Silent Leaves (`tau`)** | `1` | `1` | Inside `X('Approve Revision', tau)`. |
| **Duplicate-Label Count** | `0` | `0` | Every visible activity label appears exactly once in the process tree syntax. |
| **Petri Net Simplicity (`pm4py.simplicity_petri_net`)** | Identical inverse arc-degree formula | Identical inverse arc-degree formula | `pm4py.simplicity_petri_net` computes $\frac{1}{1 + \max(\bar{d} - 2, 0)}$ where $\bar{d}$ is the mean degree of places/transitions. Because both trees use the exact same operator types and arities, standard Petri net conversion yields identical or near-identical arc-degree simplicity ($\approx 0.67\text{--}0.73$ depending on PM4Py's internal silent-transition reduction during `convert_to_petri_net`). |

# 8. Simplified Model

### Target Business Purpose Selection
A single simplification **cannot simultaneously preserve P1 and reduce the structure to P2's minimal abstraction**, because P1 requires tracking `Approve Revision` and enforcing per-revision `Check Revision`, whereas P2 explicitly discards `Approve Revision`, hides silent steps, ignores exact interleaving of `Analyze Revision` and `Check Revision`, and collapses `Check Revision` multiplicity to a single Boolean indicator ("at least one check occurred").

* **For P1:** Model A is already minimal! It has $0$ duplicate labels, and every operator in Model A directly encodes an irreducible requirement of P1 (start/end sequence, revision loop with `Rework Case`, concurrent `Analyze Revision` and `Check Revision` per revision, and optional `Approve Revision`). Removing any node from Model A violates P1.
* **Target Chosen — P2 (Executive Dashboard View):** If the target semantics is P2, both Model A and Model B are over-specified. Under P2:
  1. `Approve Revision` and its optional `X('Approve Revision', tau)` choice are completely unobservable and can be eliminated.
  2. Repeated occurrences of `Check Revision` are collapsed to a single Boolean indicator, and the relative order of `Analyze Revision` and `Check Revision` is ignored. Thus, placing a single `Check Revision` in sequence (or parallel) outside the `*( 'Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case' )` loop preserves the exact P2 dashboard abstraction $\alpha_{P2}$ while eliminating concurrency (`+`) and silent choice (`X(..., tau)`).

### 1. Compact Process-Tree Expression (`simplified_model`)

```text
->(
  'Open Case',
  *('Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case'),
  'Check Revision',
  'Close Case'
)
```

### Structural Comparison Against Originals

# 9. Final Recommendation

1. **Suitability for P1 (Operational Compliance View):** **Use Model A exclusively.** Model A is the only model that enforces the compliance invariant that every analysis attempt is paired with its own `Check Revision` prior to optional approval (`Approve Revision`) and prior to `Rework Case` or `Close Case`. Model B is defective for P1 because it permits un-checked reworks (`CE-B2`) and premature approvals before checking (`CE-B1`). Furthermore, **Model A cannot be structurally simplified without breaking P1**.
2. **Suitability for P2 (Executive Dashboard View):** **Both Model A and Model B are suitable, and `simplified_model` can safely replace either one** for dashboard KPI aggregation or high-level conformance checking under $\alpha_{P2}$. Because P2 hides `Approve Revision`, ignores exact interleaving between analysis and checking, and reduces `Check Revision` counts to a Boolean existence flag, `simplified_model` (`->('Open Case', *('Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case'), 'Check Revision', 'Close Case')`) preserves $100\%$ of P2's observable semantics while reducing tree size from $12$ nodes to $7$, depth from $4\text{--}5$ to $2$, and eliminating concurrency and silent transitions. However, `simplified_model` **must not** replace Model A in the operational workflow engine where P1 compliance is enforced.
3. **Warning on the Limits of Bounded Comparison (P3):** While our P3 bounded exploration ($\text{length} \le 12$, $\text{Analyze Revision} \le 3$) definitively **refuted** equivalence between Models A and B (because any single counterexample within the bound disproves equality of the full languages), **bounded equivalence is never a formal proof of unbounded equivalence**. In models containing loops (`*`), two nets can agree on all traces up to length $N$ and diverge only on the $(N+1)$-th iteration (for instance, if a secondary resource place holds $4$ tokens, divergence only appears when $\text{Analyze Revision} \ge 5$). Unbounded equivalence requires structural bisimulation, state-space equivalence on the full finite reachability graph (for $k$-bounded nets where visible trace multiplicity is tracked symbolically), or automata-theoretic language inclusion proofs.
