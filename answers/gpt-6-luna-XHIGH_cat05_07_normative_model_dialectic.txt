## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Established.** M0 requires registration, then Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review in either order or concurrently, followed by an exclusive choice between (a) Manual_Procurement_Approval then Activate_Supplier and (b) Reject_Supplier, then notification and closure. It does not express the requester/approver identity rule, risk or spend eligibility, provisional activation, repeated information requests, rejected-lead intake, reactivation, conflicts of interest, or event provenance. CS-9 is formally approved and effective for all cases here. It requires both checks before activation, manual approval before activation, and different humans for requester and manual approver. It allows abbreviated pre-screening for rejected leads but leaves their M0 scope unresolved.

PD-3 describes a bounded pilot, not an amendment incorporated into CS-9 or M0: June 1–July 31, 2026; eligible low-risk German suppliers in an approved corporate group; sanctions and automated master-data validation before activation; Auto_Approve instead of manual approval; quality review no later than two business days after provisional activation. The record establishes a steering-committee decision and the observer's “monitored exception” statement, but not the committee's delegated authority to waive the formal standard. The observer later said she had not approved weakening CS-9. FD-7 is a more explicit, narrowly scoped emergency directive, effective July 10–12 only. It permits provisional activation before quality review and manual approval for critical flood-relief suppliers, subject to pre-activation sanctions, a EUR 20,000 cap until retrospective approval, daily monitoring, and quality review within two business days.

Notification absence in the onboarding log is weak evidence of non-occurrence after the CRM migration: only about 70% of notifications are mirrored. A stable `notification_id` is stronger evidence than a mutable text link. Event order is stipulated as observed, though reliable chronology still depends on source timestamps. SUP-711 has a duplicate source transaction ID; SUP-713 has audit evidence of equivalent renamed quality activity; SUP-706's CRM message is associated only through mutable text entered later.

**Disputed or unproved.** The record does not resolve whether PD-3 had authority to create an exception to CS-9; whether all reported eligibility fields and event times are reliable; whether rejected leads and reactivations fall inside M0's intended population; whether SUP-706's notification belongs to that case; or whether SUP-715's relationship triggered the COI disclosure rule. Operational desirability, low risk, or a trace mismatch alone resolves none of these questions.

**Burden of proof.** To declare noncompliance, the auditor should establish with reliable evidence that the case is in scope, identify the applicable rule/version, and show a violation of its control-flow, resource, or data requirements. Severity should reflect exposure and control purpose, not mismatch count. The operator relying on an exception should produce its authority, scope, contemporaneous eligibility facts, and required safeguards. An unproved exception does not silently displace CS-9. Conversely, uncertain scope or logging should be classified as a model/data defect or indeterminate evidence, not as proven misconduct.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest prosecution case is that CS-9 remains the formal default unless an authorized, applicable exception is demonstrated. M0 is incomplete, but its central safeguards—both checks before activation and manual approval before activation—reflect CS-9. The PD-3 minutes/email do not amend CS-9, and the compliance observer expressly disclaimed approval to weaken it. PD-3 may evidence a controlled operational exception if the committee had delegated authority; that authority is not established. Familiarity with the pilot flow cannot make it permanent.

Several cases demonstrate substantive failure rather than model obsolescence. SUP-708 activated a high-risk supplier 40 minutes before sanctions clearance, with no pilot or emergency. The absence of transactions before clearance mitigates loss, not the breach. SUP-703 was a high-risk new foreign supplier, plainly outside PD-3 eligibility, yet an operator selected “pilot”; its EUR 180,000 first purchase shows material exposure. SUP-705 used the FD-7 pattern after automatic expiry. The ongoing flood explains pressure, but does not extend a directive; the EUR 12,000 spend being under the cap does not supply authority. SUP-712 conforms to M0's visible sequence but violates CS-9's separate-human requirement because two aliases identify one requester/approver. SUP-714 used auto-approval after the pilot ended, without a permanent amendment. Expected future approval is not present authority.

The aggregate record shows a governance problem as well as individual deviations. Of 312 auto-approval cases, 14 appear clearly ineligible and 9 lack eligibility attributes; the 289 that appear eligible are not thereby proven authorized under CS-9. Six auto-approvals in early August show normalization after the pilot window. Forty-seven Request_More_Info cases reveal an omitted practice but do not excuse bypassing required checks or approval. Notification missingness rose to 29%, making that metric unreliable, but it does not weaken independent sanctions, approval, or identity evidence in the serious cases.

The prosecution must not count every M0 mismatch as a violation. SUP-702 appears to satisfy the pilot's operational criteria; SUP-704 appears to satisfy FD-7. SUP-707 is desirable and safe on the stated facts. SUP-709 and SUP-710 raise scope gaps; SUP-711 is a duplicate record; SUP-713 is supported label equivalence; SUP-706 is unresolved. The defensible prosecution position is to enforce the formal baseline, recognize only documented exceptions with proven authority, and revise M0 and collection practices rather than inflate violations with known artifacts.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense is that M0 is not a complete operational specification or a reliable stand-alone conformance oracle. Its two checks are unordered, and the model omits the pilot and emergency routes, repeated information requests, rejected-lead intake, reactivation, label mapping, resource separation, and data provenance. Applying one raw replay score to all cases conflates violations with valid overlays, scope disputes, and logging artifacts.

SUP-702 is a strong case-level fit to PD-3: complete eligibility fields; eligible low-risk DE group supplier during the effective window; sanctions before activation; Auto_Approve replacing manual approval; quality review the next business day; and a stable CRM notification ID. The steering decision and observer's contemporaneous description support a sanctioned, monitored pilot, although authority to derogate from CS-9 still needs confirmation. SUP-704 falls inside FD-7's dates and stated purpose; sanctions came first, spend was EUR 18,000, monitoring existed, and quality and approval followed the next business day. These should not be called ordinary violations solely because they differ from M0.

Other apparent deviations have credible non-misconduct explanations. CS-9 allows abbreviated screening for rejected leads, so SUP-709 may properly omit quality; whether it is in M0's population is unsettled. SUP-710 is a reactivation, not ordinary onboarding, and reportedly had a valid prior quality certification; CS-9 provides no explicit reactivation procedure. SUP-711's repeated registration rows share a migration transaction ID. SUP-713's QA_Clearance is supported by audit evidence as equivalent to Quality_Review, so a late mapping entry does not prove the check was absent. SUP-706's CRM message may be genuine, and the migration explains missing log events, but the mutable link is insufficient to prove case association. SUP-707 collected missing certificates before the decision; stakeholders and Compliance regard the practice as desirable. Punishing it as a breach solely because M0 lacks a loop would confuse model fit with control quality.

The defense cannot turn obsolescence into blanket immunity. PD-3 had an end date; FD-7 expired automatically. Informal expectations of a permanent change do not create authority. SUP-703's ineligibility, high-risk context, and large first purchase defeat the pilot defense. SUP-708's chronology has no exception. SUP-712 violates an explicit resource rule notwithstanding control-flow conformance. SUP-714 is outside the pilot window. Teams must document authority, eligibility, safeguards, and extensions when relying on exceptions.

**System judgment.** The operation is materially noncompliant in a bounded but important set of cases: SUP-703 and SUP-708 are severe substantive failures; SUP-705, SUP-712, and SUP-714 are additional violations. The 14 apparently ineligible and 9 incomplete-attribute auto-approval cases require review; they do not show that all 312 cases were violations. Nor do the 289 apparently eligible cases prove waiver authority. Six early-August auto-approvals show governance failure after expiry, even if individual risk was low.

M0 remains a useful baseline for ordinary onboarding, but it is not a complete operational specification or sufficient compliance oracle. It omits supported overlays and distinct scopes, information loops and resource rules, while event capture has material gaps. I reject the prosecution's overreach if it treats each trace deviation as a violation or assumes M0 defines every case. I reject the defense's overreach if it treats desirability, emergency continuity, pilot normalization, or likely eligibility as self-authorizing. Retain CS-9 as the default; decide exceptions case by case from authority, scope, date, eligibility, and safeguards.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

Use a layered specification with an explicit case type and versioned rule selection. POWL describes control-flow possibilities; it does not grant permission to take an exceptional route. An overlay must be active and case attributes must satisfy it.

| Layer | Proposed rule | Representation |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary onboarding | Register; optional repeated information gathering; concurrent sanctions and quality checks; then manual approval before activation or rejection; notify before closure. | POWL for sequence, XOR, concurrency, and loop. |
| Pilot | Only eligible low-risk DE group cases during June 1–July 31; sanctions and automated master validation before provisional activation; Auto_Approve instead of manual approval; quality within two business days; notification before closure. | Separate POWL route and data-aware gate; route-conditioned DECLARE/log-skeleton constraints. |
| Emergency | Only critical flood-relief cases during FD-7's exact interval; sanctions before provisional activation; daily monitoring; quality within two business days; retrospective approval before spend exceeds EUR 20,000. Automatic expiry. | Separate POWL route; dates, scope, cap, and authority in data checks; directive and extension in organizational controls. |
| Rejected intake | Define whether pre-screen leads are inside the onboarding population. If included, allow abbreviated sanctions pre-screen and rejection, with no activation. | Explicit POWL route and case-registry scope rule. |
| Reactivation | Own case type. Validate prior certification scope/expiry, require current sanctions and manual approval before activation; refresh quality when certification is expired, inadequate, or out of scope. | POWL branch for valid-cert and refresh paths; data checks and approved reactivation policy. |
| Withdrawal | Distinguish pre-activation withdrawal from post-activation termination; notify and record closure/termination reason. | POWL branches and activation-state data rule. |
| Resource and COI | Requester and manual approver are different canonical people; capture delegation. Require disclosure and prescribed mitigation for relevant reviewer/approver relationships. | DECLARE/data checks for identity conditions; assignment, identity, disclosure, and mitigation controls organizationally. |
| Event quality | Canonical activity IDs and effective-dated label mapping; source-backed deduplication; immutable notification joins and history. | Event schema, pandas reconciliation, and source-system controls. |

Use route-conditioned DECLARE/log-skeleton rules for sanctions before any activation, manual approval before standard activation, quality before standard activation, notification before closure, no activation on rejection, and one canonical registration after proven deduplication. Do not apply a global quality-before-all-activation constraint because PD-3 and FD-7 expressly allow delayed review. Use pandas/data checks for dates and business-day deadlines, cumulative spend/currency, risk/country/group eligibility, actor identity, COI disclosures, duplicate source IDs, and notification missingness. Organizational controls must approve and publish rule versions, establish delegated authority, monitor exceptions, stop use at expiry, and prevent informal practice from being represented as policy.

Define case categories and boundaries explicitly: `new_onboarding`, `rejected_intake`, `reactivation`, `withdrawal_pre_activation`, and `termination_post_activation`. If a category is excluded from M0, keep it in a governed process and report the exclusion rather than silently removing it from the compliance denominator.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

This POWL is a control-flow envelope. Pilot/emergency branches are gated by policy and data outside the model. The optional repeatable information stage uses an XOR between silent skip and a three-child loop: request is the body, silent redo allows another request, and silent exit ends the loop. Provisional status, authority, eligibility, deadlines, and spend are event attributes or data rules. Each occurrence uses its own transition object. The code reads no external files.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def po(nodes, edges=()):
    """Build a partial order; only listed edges impose precedence."""
    result = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for left, right in edges:
        result.order.add_edge(left, right)
    return result


def seq(*nodes):
    return po(nodes, zip(nodes, nodes[1:]))


def xor(*children):
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=list(children))


# Optional zero-or-more Request_More_Info events before the route.
info_loop = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[
        Transition(label="Request_More_Info"),  # do
        SilentTransition(),                     # redo: request again
        SilentTransition(),                     # exit
    ],
)
info_stage = xor(SilentTransition(), info_loop)

# Standard onboarding: sanctions and quality are concurrent. After both,
# choose either manual approval then activation, or rejection. Both notify
# before closure.
standard_checks = po([
    Transition(label="Sanctions_Check"),
    Transition(label="Quality_Review"),
])
approved_branch = seq(
    Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval"),
    Transition(label="Activate_Supplier"),
)
standard_decision = xor(
    approved_branch,
    Transition(label="Reject_Supplier"),
)
standard_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
standard_close = Transition(label="Close_Case")
standard_route = po(
    [standard_checks, standard_decision, standard_notify, standard_close],
    [
        (standard_checks, standard_decision),
        (standard_decision, standard_notify),
        (standard_notify, standard_close),
    ],
)

# PD-3 pilot route. Eligibility, authority, dates, and review deadline are
# external guards. Post-activation quality and notification may be unordered,
# but both must finish before closure.
pilot_prechecks = po([
    Transition(label="Sanctions_Check"),
    Transition(label="Automated_Master_Data_Validation"),
])
pilot_auto = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
pilot_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
pilot_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
pilot_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
pilot_close = Transition(label="Close_Case")
pilot_route = po(
    [pilot_prechecks, pilot_auto, pilot_activate, pilot_quality,
     pilot_notify, pilot_close],
    [
        (pilot_prechecks, pilot_auto),
        (pilot_auto, pilot_activate),
        (pilot_activate, pilot_quality),
        (pilot_activate, pilot_notify),
        (pilot_quality, pilot_close),
        (pilot_notify, pilot_close),
    ],
)

# FD-7 emergency route. Mark Activate_Supplier as provisional in event data.
# Monitoring, review, retrospective approval, and notification all follow
# activation and all precede closure.
em_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
em_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
em_monitor = Transition(label="Daily_Transaction_Monitoring")
em_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
em_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
em_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
em_close = Transition(label="Close_Case")
emergency_route = po(
    [em_sanctions, em_activate, em_monitor, em_quality, em_approval,
     em_notify, em_close],
    [
        (em_sanctions, em_activate),
        (em_activate, em_monitor),
        (em_activate, em_quality),
        (em_activate, em_approval),
        (em_activate, em_notify),
        (em_monitor, em_close),
        (em_quality, em_close),
        (em_approval, em_close),
        (em_notify, em_close),
    ],
)

# Explicit abbreviated pre-screen rejection route.
pre_screen_reject = seq(
    Transition(label="Sanctions_Check"),
    Transition(label="Reject_Supplier"),
    Transition(label="Notify_Requester"),
    Transition(label="Close_Case"),
)

# New onboarding: registration and information stage precede one guarded route.
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
route_choice = xor(
    standard_route,
    pilot_route,
    emergency_route,
    pre_screen_reject,
)
new_onboarding = po(
    [register, info_stage, route_choice],
    [(register, info_stage), (info_stage, route_choice)],
)

# Reactivation has a separate entry and certificate validation. Data rules
# decide whether the valid-certificate or quality-refresh path is admissible.
valid_cert_path = seq(
    Transition(label="Sanctions_Check"),
    Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval"),
    Transition(label="Activate_Supplier"),
)
refresh_checks = po([
    Transition(label="Sanctions_Check"),
    Transition(label="Quality_Review"),
])
refresh_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
refresh_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
refresh_cert_path = po(
    [refresh_checks, refresh_approval, refresh_activate],
    [(refresh_checks, refresh_approval), (refresh_approval, refresh_activate)],
)
reactivation_start = Transition(label="Reactivation_Request")
cert_validation = Transition(label="Validate_Prior_Quality_Certification")
reactivation_choice = xor(valid_cert_path, refresh_cert_path)
reactivation_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
reactivation_close = Transition(label="Close_Case")
reactivation_route = seq(
    reactivation_start,
    cert_validation,
    reactivation_choice,
    reactivation_notify,
    reactivation_close,
)

withdrawal_route = seq(
    Transition(label="Withdrawal_Recorded"),
    Transition(label="Notify_Requester"),
    Transition(label="Close_Case"),
)

# The case registry and overlay gate this scope-level choice.
M1 = xor(new_onboarding, reactivation_route, withdrawal_route)
```

**Normalized policy-overlay table.** Store one immutable versioned record for each rule or exception and associate every route decision with the selected version. `authority_status` must be explicit; a route label cannot imply authority.

| rule_version_id | Scope and effective interval | Authority/status | Gate and obligations |
|---|---|---|---|
| CS-9:v1 | In-scope onboarding; 2025-01-01 onward | Formally approved baseline | Sanctions and quality before activation; manual approval before activation; requester and manual approver are different humans. |
| PD-3:v1 | Eligible low-risk DE supplier in approved corporate group; 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31 | Steering decision; delegated waiver authority needs verification; not incorporated into CS-9 | Eligibility snapshot; sanctions and automated master validation before provisional activation; Auto_Approve replaces manual; quality within two business days; record monitoring/exception. |
| FD-7:v1 | Critical relief suppliers needed to prevent flood-related shutdown; 2026-07-10 00:00 to 2026-07-12 23:59 | Explicit emergency directive; automatic expiry | Sanctions before provisional activation; daily monitoring; EUR 20,000 cap until retrospective approval; quality within two business days. No post-expiry use without new authority. |
| INTAKE:v1 | Leads rejected at pre-screen | Scope owner must declare inclusion/exclusion from M0 and KPIs | If included, use abbreviated route and prohibit activation; retain lead and scope decision. |
| REACT:v1 (proposed) | Reactivations | Requires approval before adoption | Validate prior certificate scope/expiry; current sanctions; refresh quality when required; manual approval before activation. |
| WITHDRAW:v1 (proposed) | Withdrawals and terminations | Requires approval before adoption | Distinguish pre-activation withdrawal from post-activation termination; record reason, notification, and terminal event. |

**Event and collection proposal.** Use append-only event records with `case_id`, unique `event_id`, `source_transaction_id`, canonical `activity_id`, displayed label, effective-dated label-map version, event time, immutable recorded time, source system, lifecycle/sequence metadata, and correction/supersession links. Do not overwrite prior labels or timestamps; append corrections with actor, time, and reason. Deduplicate SUP-711-like rows only when source transaction identity and migration audit support it.

Persist `case_type`, `route_id`, `rule_version_id`, `overlay_id`, exception/decision ID, authority instrument ID, authorizer and delegation evidence, effective interval, and reason for each route selection. Store an eligibility snapshot as of the decision: risk rating/source, country, group membership, master-data validation, sanctions result/time, and evaluating actor/system. For FD-7 also store critical-supplier justification, provisional flag/time, currency, initial and cumulative spend, cap remaining, monitoring evidence, retrospective approval, and quality deadline/outcome. Calculate business-day deadlines with a versioned calendar and timezone.

Record actors with stable privacy-preserving person IDs, effective-dated aliases, roles, and delegation chains. Capture requester, approver, quality reviewer, authorizer, identity-resolution provenance/confidence, COI disclosures, and mitigations. Require immutable `notification_id` as the CRM-to-case foreign key; retain sent/delivered status and event/recorded times. Replace mutable text joins with stable keys and preserve CRM audit history. Reconcile CRM and onboarding events by source and period; missing mirrors are not proof that no message was sent.

At evaluation, resolve case type and policy version first, validate event identity/order second, apply route-specific policy and data guards third, then report conformance separately from evidence confidence. This avoids collapsing expired exceptions, scope defects, and weak event linkage into one replay deviation score.
