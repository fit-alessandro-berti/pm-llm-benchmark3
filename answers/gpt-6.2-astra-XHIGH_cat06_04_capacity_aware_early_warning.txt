## 1. Decision rule

At each process event, 30-minute refresh or slot release, consider open cases before Diagnosis Complete. The case owner must identify a diagnostic question the seniors can still resolve and confirm that they can start in time to affect diagnosis. Time to the SLA deadline alone does not establish that window. Check the swarm ledger for prior treatment. A second swarm requires a materially new event, a new actionable question and approval from the duty lead.

Estimate the risk under ordinary care, p₀, separately from the absolute reduction attributable to a timely swarm, d. As provisional calibration sensitivities, add 0.04–0.09 to SME predictions and subtract 0.05–0.12 from Strategic Enterprise predictions, clipping to [0,1]. Retain the Public Sector point estimate with sampling uncertainty. For Non-Native-Language Support, use a pooled calibration estimate with a wide uncertainty band and obtain language-accessible case review. These adjustments describe groups. Historical predictions may also reflect past treatment, so they are only proxies for p₀. Cap any assumed positive reduction at the corresponding p₀. A correction to baseline risk does not establish a corresponding change in swarm effectiveness.

For today's ranking, let L and M be the lower bound and midpoint of the supplied effect range. Use 0.25L + 0.75M as the planning effect, giving some weight to downside risk. Score the direct gain as G = planning effect × avoided-breach value − €450 − false-alarm burden. Charging the full burden on every swarm gives a conservative budget. If a false alarm means the case would have met its SLA under ordinary care, the expected charge would be (1 − p₀) × burden. The supplied effect ranges are hypotheses, and a swarm can deliver zero benefit.

For each feasible next slot subtract the **marginal loss to the exposed queue**, H = sum of each untreated case's incremental breach probability times its breach value. Recompute H as staffing, case mix and congestion change. For the noon decision, a working value of €20,000 per exposed breach and a 0.02 increment give H ≈ 6 × 0.02 × €20,000 = €2,400 per slot. The six values are unknown. At dispatch, check the actual exposed cases, their breach values and whether incoming work leaves ordinary diagnosis understaffed. Choose the feasible portfolio with the highest positive incremental gain after queue losses, subject to the reserve and any documented contractual, safety or service-access obligations. Check imminent losses of the opportunity to treat when scheduling. Escalate any conflict with an obligation to the duty lead and record the decision. One verified, actionable alert can qualify immediately. Keep a pending alert open across refreshes and require changed evidence to page again.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

Maintain one alert record per case and diagnostic episode, with its owner, evidence, last decision, treatment time and next review. Recompute silently on refresh. After a page, apply a 60-minute notification cool-down. Page again for a newly actionable log or test, corrected data, a changed diagnostic hypothesis, or a verified change in urgency that alters dispatch. A baseline risk movement of at least 0.05 tied to a real input is a review trigger. A consequential new fact can trigger review at any probability change. Override the cool-down immediately for a closing treatment window or mandatory escalation. A slot release can promote a waiting case without another model alert.

I-05 stays on the waiting list under its first alert. Expiry of the cool-down alone does not page the owner again. I-07's uploaded log triggers immediate review even though the prediction fell from 0.75 to 0.72. If it identifies a fix the seniors can expedite, update the estimated benefit and reschedule while time remains. After a swarm, suppress further swarm requests throughout the episode unless the duty lead verifies a materially new, treatable issue. Diagnosis Complete or closure ends swarm eligibility and cancels any booking.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

- **Access despite lower contract value.** Give every eligible case an owner and a review before its diagnostic window closes. Among close net scores, favor imminent loss of opportunity, larger absolute breach reduction and longer waiting time. Track missed windows by value band. Persistent starvation triggers a trial of a capacity floor for high-need cases with positive overall benefit.
- **Calibration and language access.** Audit SME underprediction and Strategic Enterprise overprediction by stage and redesign period. Obtain accessible diagnostic review for language-support cases, record missing or translated evidence, and report uncertainty intervals. Sparse data should prompt review and better sampling. It should never automatically disqualify I-09 or justify an indefinite delay.
- **Treatment changes labels.** A timely close after a swarm could be a prevented breach. Retain the pre-dispatch forecast, treatment decision and outcome separately. Counting every treated success as a false alarm would teach the next model to deprioritize cases that respond well.
- **Distribution of queue harm.** For each dispatch, log which untreated cases lose senior time, their stage, segment and SLA slack. Compare delays and breaches by group and shift, including cases never alerted. If one group repeatedly absorbs the delay, protect ordinary diagnostic coverage or reduce swarm frequency on those shifts. Track whether the change improves both access and untreated outcomes.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**During each shift**, record prefix and event times, model and policy versions, raw and adjusted risk, evidence quality, effect assumptions, queue exposure, decision reason, overrides and reserve use. Track slot occupancy, actual senior minutes, time from alert to start, starts before Diagnosis Complete, duplicate pages, ordinary diagnosis delay and customer disruption. Recompute H before each start. A failed queue feed calls for manual verification. An estimated H above the next candidate's G suspends that dispatch pending review.

**Weekly, after deadlines mature**, inspect reliability plots, calibration error and Brier scores by stage, segment, workflow version and missing-data status, with intervals and counts. Estimate ordinary-care calibration in comparable untreated cases while accounting for treatment selection and queue exposure. Compare eligible-to-reviewed, reviewed-to-treated and missed-window rates, breach reductions and spillover losses across value, language and contract groups at similar urgency. Report both raw and need-adjusted differences. Track changes in arrivals, feature missingness, treatment rates and queue occupancy alongside model drift.

**Estimate causal effects prospectively.** When capacity binds and owners judge cases comparably appropriate, use a recorded random tie-break among eligible cases. Preserve mandatory escalations and analyze assignment as well as actual start. Randomize permitted swarm intensity across comparable queue shifts where feasible to estimate harm to untreated neighbors and total portfolio outcomes. Allow for carryover between shifts. Log assignment probabilities and pre-action covariates. Estimate effects by stage and start delay, with partial pooling for small groups. Where randomization is infeasible, compare contemporaneous eligible cases at the same decision time with overlap checks and propensity-adjusted estimates. Report remaining confounding and wide subgroup intervals.

**Govern changes.** Review outcomes monthly and after a workflow or staffing change. Investigate sustained calibration bias across two mature reporting windows, concentrated missed opportunities, or ordinary-queue delays persistently beyond the base 20 minutes per slot. Recalibrate baseline risk for predictive bias. Revise effect weights, dispatch cutoffs, reserve timing or queue limits when measured treatment and spillover outcomes warrant it. Replay proposed changes on time-held-out decision logs with credible counterfactual comparisons, then pilot by shift. Expand only if net outcomes improve within agreed service and fairness limits. Restore the prior rule if untreated harm or loss of access exceeds those limits.
