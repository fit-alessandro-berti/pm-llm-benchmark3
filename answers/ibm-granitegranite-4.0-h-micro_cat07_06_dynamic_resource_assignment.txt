# 1. Objectives and constraints

## Optimization objectives
1. **Throughput**: Maximize the number of cases processed within their SLA deadlines.
2. **Skill development**: Ensure that assignments provide opportunities for training and knowledge application.
3. **Workload fairness**: Distribute workload evenly among resources, avoiding overloading any single resource.
4. **Continuity of case ownership**: Maintain the same owner for a case when possible, especially for complex cases.
5. **SLA risk**: Minimize risk of missing SLA deadlines.
6. **Resilience**: Design the assignment to be robust against resource absence or sudden spikes in workload.

## Constraints
### Safety/Compliance
1. **Skill matching**: Assign cases only to resources with a skill level equal to or higher than the required skill, unless supervised.
2. **Specialist routing**: Security Review must be performed by skill level 3, or by skill level 1–2 only under explicit supervision.
3. **SLA compliance**: Ensure that all cases meet their SLA deadlines or are flagged for risk.

### Preference trade-offs
1. **Workload fairness vs. continuity**: Prefer continuity over strict workload balance when continuity is critical.
2. **Training vs. SLA risk**: Prioritize training opportunities when SLA risk is acceptable and manageable.
3. **Overtime risk**: Avoid assigning high-complexity cases to resources with high overtime risk unless no safer alternative exists.

# 2. Assignment policy

## Algorithm/Pseudocode

```python
def assign_cases(event_log):
    # Initialize resource and case data structures
    resources = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(event_log)
    cases = pm4py.filter_case_performance(event_log)

    # Define priority and SLA scoring
    priority_scores = {p: (4 - p) for p in sorted(cases['case_priority'].unique(), reverse=True)}
    sla_scores = {c['sla_deadline']: (48 - c['sla_deadline']) for c in cases}

    # Initialize assignment list
    assignments = []

    # Iterate through cases
    for case in cases:
        required_skill = case['required_skill']
        priority = priority_scores[case['case_priority']]
        sla_time = sla_scores[case['sla_deadline']]

        # Filter eligible resources
        eligible_resources = [r for r in resources if r['skill'] >= required_skill]

        # Calculate skill-match score
        skill_match_score = max(r['skill'] for r in eligible_resources)

        # Check for supervised assignments
        if priority >= 3 and required_skill == 3:
            supervisor = None
            for r in eligible_resources:
                if r['team'] == 'Risk' or r['training_priority'] == 'high':
                    supervisor = r['org:resource']
                    break

            if supervisor:
                assignments.append((case['case'], supervisor, 'Supervised Training'))
            else:
                assignments.append((case['case'], 'Fallback to next best', 'No Supervision'))
        else:
            # Assign based on skill-match and SLA urgency
            best_resource = max(eligible_resources, key=lambda r: (r['skill'], sla_time))
            assignments.append((case['case'], best_resource['org:resource'], 'Skill Match'))

        # Record assignment reasons and trade-offs
        assignments[-1] += (', Trade-off: ' + assignments[-1][2])

    return assignments
```

# 4. Comparison with baseline policies

## First-available routing
- **Throughput**: Moderate, as it does not consider skill matching or SLA urgency.
- **SLA risk**: Higher due to potential mismatch of skill and urgency.
- **Error/rework risk**: Increased due to inadequate skill matches.
- **Fairness**: Fair in workload distribution but may lead to skill mismatches.
- **Skill development**: Limited, as trainees are often assigned tasks below their competence.
- **Continuity**: Poor, as cases are frequently reassigned.
- **Resilience**: Lower, as it does not consider resource availability or workload.

## Specialist-only routing
- **Throughput**: Lower, as it may create bottlenecks for specialists.
- **SLA risk**: Lower for high-complexity cases, but overall SLA risk may increase due to specialist scarcity.
- **Error/rework risk**: Lower for high-complexity cases due to specialist involvement.
- **Fairness**: Uneven, as non-specialists may be overloaded.
- **Skill development**: High, as specialists are frequently utilized.
- **Continuity**: Poor, as specialists may be overburdened and unable to maintain continuity.
- **Resilience**: Lower, due to reliance on specialist availability.

## Proposed explainable dynamic policy
- **Throughput**: Balanced, as it considers SLA urgency and skill match while avoiding overload.
- **SLA risk**: Optimized by prioritizing SLA urgent cases and matching skills where possible.
- **Error/rework risk**: Reduced by assigning cases to appropriately skilled resources and supporting training.
- **Fairness**: Improved through workload distribution and limiting reassignments.
- **Skill development**: Enhanced by providing opportunities for trainees under supervised conditions.
- **Continuity**: Maintained by assigning cases to current owners when continuity is critical.
- **Resilience**: High, due to flexibility in handling resource availability and workload spikes.

# 5. Explanation to workers

The new assignment policy uses a combination of case urgency, resource skill, and continuity considerations to ensure efficient and fair workload distribution. Cases with high SLA urgency or high complexity are prioritized and assigned to the most qualified resources, often with supervision for training opportunities. Routine tasks are handled by available resources to maintain balance. The policy aims to minimize rework and SLA risks while supporting skill development through supervised assignments. Workers should focus on understanding the rationale behind each assignment, particularly for training opportunities and SLA critical cases, to ensure smooth operation and continuous improvement.

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators

To audit the deployed policy, the following indicators can be extracted from the event logs using PM4Py:

1. **Handover frequency**: Count the number of times cases are reassigned.
2. **Reassignment count**: Track the total number of reassignments per case.
3. **Skill mismatch**: Measure the difference between required skill and assigned skill.
4. **Workload distribution**: Analyze the number of cases per resource over time.
5. **Overtime risk exposure**: Identify resources with high overtime risk and the cases assigned to them.
6. **SLA misses**: Count the number of cases that missed their SLA deadlines.
7. **Rework/appeal after assignment**: Track the number of reworks or appeals following case assignment.
8. **Training assignment share**: Calculate the proportion of assignments that support training priorities.
9. **Specialist bottleneck indicators**: Monitor the workload and SLA performance of specialist resources.
10. **Deviations from assignment policy**: Identify any cases that do not adhere to the policy rules and investigate reasons.

# 7. PM4Py implementation sketch

```python
import pm4py

# Load event log
event_log = pm4py.load_csv('path_to_log.csv')

# Define the assignment policy function
def dynamic_assignment(event_log):
    # Convert to DataFrame
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(event_log)

    # Apply the assignment algorithm
    assignments = assign_cases(df)

    # Save the assignments for audit
    pm4py.save_vis_sna(assignments, 'assignment_audit')

    return assignments

# Run the assignment
assignments = dynamic_assignment(event_log)

# Audit indicators extraction
```
