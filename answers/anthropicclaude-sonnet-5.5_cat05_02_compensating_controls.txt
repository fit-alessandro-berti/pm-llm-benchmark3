## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

**Dimensions**

| Dimension | Full | Partial | Fail or weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | Before release, or before settlement with a verified hold capability and enough time to review. | After release but before settlement, hold unverified, or the window is short. | After settlement or after the recall window. |
| **Independence** | The approver is verified independent of actor(s) and unconflicted, and the credential is verified. | Independence assumed but undocumented. | The approver sets the actor's rating or is the actor, or the credential is shared. |
| **Coverage** | The controls together address T1–T3 and T6 for the case's risk profile. | Some threats are covered and others left open. | AV-2 alone for a high-risk payment, or no control at the decision point. |
| **Overrideability** | No override, or an override by an independent manager with a recorded rationale. | Overrideable but done by the right person. | The actor can override their own block. |
| **Recoverability** | Hold before settlement, or audit inside the recall window. | Audit after 24h but with a legal recovery route. | Nothing can be recovered. |
| **Evidence quality** | Artefacts reviewed and logged (invoice, bank details, receipt or service report). | The fact of approval is logged but the depth is unknown. | Unsigned checklist, rubber stamp, unverifiable token. |
| **Collusion resistance** | The approver is independent of all actors, with rotation and pair analytics. | A fixed pair with no analytics. | A pair with shared workstation and heavy co-occurrence and no independent check. |

**Tiers**

- **Hard-fail gates** (any one makes the package ineffective for the payment):
  - the actor controls an override of their own block;
  - the sole approver is conflicted;
  - no functioning control exists at the decision point for a high-inherent-risk payment.
- **Fully equivalent.** All dimensions are full, or one is partial with low case risk. An independent human, or an equally strong control, had authority to stop the payment before irrevocability, with substantive review, and timely audit follows. Equivalence is measured against a well-functioning four-eyes pair, not against perfection.
- **Partially equivalent.** There are no hard fails, but one or more soft gates are unverified or degraded (a late token, unknown review depth, unverified pair independence).
- **Merely mitigating.** The package is mainly detective or post-settlement, or covers only a narrow automated scope. It shortens detection or reduces impact but does not stop the threat.
- **Ineffective.** A hard fail is present, or the controls were absent, bypassed, or defeated at the decision time.

**Risk scaling.** The required tier rises with inherent risk (amount, new vendor, bank change, missing receipt, emergency context). A partial package can be tolerable for EUR 1,800 to a six-year vendor and unacceptable for EUR 480,000 to a new vendor.

**Mapping to classifications.** Full maps to OBJECTIVE_MET, partial to OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET, and mitigating or ineffective to OBJECTIVE_FAILED. A stopped payment is LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION. Unresolved key facts give INDETERMINATE.

**Why "same expected loss" is not the only standard:**
1. **Tail risk.** Equal expectations can hide rare, unrecoverable EUR 900k losses, and risk appetite is not linear.
2. **Adversarial behaviour.** Fraudsters shift to the weakest documented path, so probabilities are not stationary.
3. **Deterrence and accountability.** A second named person changes behaviour, which loss estimates ignore.
4. **Recoverability asymmetry.** A loss caught pre-settlement is different from one caught after the recall window.
5. **Estimation uncertainty.** Fraud probabilities and their dependence on collusion cannot be measured reliably.
6. **Normalization of deviance.** Tolerating convenience shortcuts erodes the control culture (AP-201).
7. **Regulatory and audit expectations.** Segregation of duties is a stated objective, not only a loss lever.
8. **Hindsight bias.** AP-208's payment was legitimate, but the ex-ante control posture was not.

**Semantics of a manager action between release and settlement.** I treat it as **hybrid**:
- It is **preventive with respect to loss** because settlement, not release, is the irrevocable event. A hold-capable manager can still stop the loss.
- It is **detective with respect to the authorization objective**, because the release decision itself was made without independent approval.

I count it as preventive-equivalent only when four conditions hold:
1. A hold or cancel capability is verified.
2. The window is long enough for a real review.
3. The manager is independent and unconflicted.
4. There is evidence of substantive review.

AP-207 meets these (hold still assumed). AP-201 meets most but its review depth is unknown. AP-208's ratification after settlement is purely detective.

## 5. Counterfactual and Residual-Risk Analysis

Ranges are qualitative.

- **AP-202.** The loss mechanism is an insider-linked new vendor, a bank change, and a self-overridden warning. The control that should have broken the chain was AV-2 or an independent manager's block, and the override defeated it. Everything remains exposed. A truly independent second releaser who does not control the credential would likely have stopped it (a large reduction). The residual is collusion or credential sharing with m09, which a second releaser would not fix. The override credential itself must be made non-shareable.
- **AP-204.** The mechanism is a fabricated service receipt, possibly with collusion or an outside actor. AV-2 cannot break it. An independent receipt confirmation by the budget owner, or MG-4 at this value, might. The exposure is the full amount after the recall window. A truly independent releaser would give a low-to-moderate reduction. It does not help if the receipt is convincing or the pair colludes.
- **AP-208.** The mechanism is diversion through a recently changed bank account at a young vendor, with AV-2 absent. A pre-release bank callback, or waiting for AV-2 to be restored, would break the chain, and nothing did. The full EUR 900k was exposed. A second releaser with a mandatory hold until validation would give a substantial reduction. A second releaser who simply clicks would give little.
- **AP-209.** The hypothetical mechanism is a collusive or fictitious payment through an entrenched pair. AV-2 covers only limited checks. The timely audit partly breaks the chain (recall window open). Exposure is modest. A rotating, independent second releaser would give a moderate reduction in collusion opportunity, and the current evidence is inconclusive.
- **AP-206.** The mechanism is a backdated token to legitimize single-operator action. The independent pre-release approval and AV-2 break the chain. The remaining exposure is token integrity and the m11 review depth. An independent second releaser adds only a small improvement.
- **AP-207.** The mechanism is single-operator error or fraud in an emergency. Ratification by m15 before settlement breaks the chain, leaving a 35-minute window. A second releaser would give a small improvement and might delay a safety repair.
- **AP-201.** The mechanism is a low-value error or a minor fictitious item. AV-2 and m04's review break it. A second releaser would make almost no difference, but routine same-user use without ED-1 is a governance concern.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

**Evidence to collect.** Capture these in the event log, which currently lacks them:
- Emergency_Token_Created (issuer, reason, time).
- Bank_Settlement and Settlement_Hold events for every payment.
- Audit start and completion.
- Credential identity separate from the acting user.
- Workstation and session identifiers.
- Manager review artefacts (invoice opened, bank details viewed, receipt viewed).
- Receipt provenance (who uploaded it).
- Vendor and bank-change history.
- Control outage events.

**Monitoring rules.** Thresholds are illustrative and should be tuned to staffing and volumes.

| Metric | Rule | What an alert means | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Same-user rate** | Per user and team. Track the unjustified rate (no valid ED-1) separately from the raw rate. | Shortcut use, training or staffing gaps. | Fraud. |
| **Override rate** | Per user and manager. **Hard alert** when the override actor differs from the credential owner, or when the preparer overrides. | Possible credential sharing or self-approval. | Intent. A small team may share practices that IT did not anticipate. |
| **Late manager approval** | After release (hybrid), after settlement, or more than 24h. | The preventive value was lost or weak. | That the payment was wrong. |
| **Audit before recall window** | Emergency same-user cases audited within 24h and one business day. Track queue ageing. | Whether RA-7 can be corrective. | A timely audit is not a clean audit. |
| **Repeated resource pairs** | Pair concentration relative to the rota. Flag when a pair handles most of each other's payments. | Weak independence or collusion opportunity. | That the pair colludes. A dedicated cell, rota, or small team can explain it. |
| **Shared workstations** | Same workstation or session for preparer and releaser. | Possible loss of independence. | Misconduct. Shared desks and VDI exist. |
| **Bank-change proximity** | Hours between bank change and payment. Alert for changes within 7 days and also 8–30 days. | Diversion risk. | That the change was illegitimate. |
| **Control outages** | Payments released during an AV-2 outage. Mandatory halt or manual fallback. Duration and count. | Unmonitored risk windows. | That any payment was wrong. |
| **Emergency patterns** | Token created after prepare. Amounts just under EUR 100k. Vendor age near 180 days. Emergency frequency per user. | Token abuse or threshold hugging. | That the emergency was not real. |

**Alert versus proof.** An alert is a hypothesis generator that routes cases to human review. The review should:
- check benign explanations first (a documented two-person cell, a genuine outage);
- corroborate with independent evidence;
- follow due process (HR, legal) before drawing conclusions about individuals.

df["audit_h_after_release"] = hrs(df["audit_ts"], df["release_ts"])

def bd_deadline(ts):  # end of the next business day (UTC); one reading of "within one business day"
    if pd.isna(ts):
        return pd.NaT
    return ts.normalize() + pd.offsets.BDay(1) + pd.Timedelta(days=1) - pd.Timedelta(seconds=1)
df["audit_deadline_1bd"] = pd.to_datetime(df["release_ts"].apply(bd_deadline), utc=True)

# 6. Evidence flags
df["ed1_c1_token_before_prepare"] = (df["token_present"] & df["token_ts"].notna()
                                     & (df["token_ts"] < df["prepare_ts"]))
df["ed1_c2_amount_lt_100k"] = df["amount_eur"] < 100_000
df["ed1_c3_vendor_ge_180d"] = df["vendor_age_days"] >= 180
df["ed1_c4_no_bank_change_7d"] = df["bank_change_7d"].eq(False)
df["ed1_c5_av_full_no_override"] = df["av_log"].eq("full_pass")
df["ed1_c6_indep_mgr_before_settle"] = df["mgr_independent"].eq(True) & (df["mgr_ts"] < df["settle_ts"])
df["ed1_c7_audit_within_1bd"] = df["audit_ts"] <= df["audit_deadline_1bd"]
ED1 = [c for c in df.columns if c.startswith("ed1_c")]

def ed1_status(r):
    if not (r["completed"] and r["nominal_violation"]):
        return "n/a"
    failed = [c[4:] for c in ED1 if not bool(r[c])]
    return "all 7 evidenced" if not failed else "NOT evidenced: " + ", ".join(failed)
df["ed1_evidence"] = df.apply(ed1_status, axis=1)

AV_MAP = {"full_pass": "full_pass (AV-2 scope only)", "override": "warning_overridden",
          "unavailable": "unavailable_at_release", "blocked": "blocked", "warning": "warning_no_override",
          "none": "none"}
df["av_coverage"] = df["av_log"].map(AV_MAP)
df["mgr_before_av"] = df["mgr_ts"].notna() & df["av_ts"].notna() & (df["mgr_ts"] < df["av_ts"])
df["override_by_preparer"] = df["override_res"].notna() & (df["override_res"] == df["preparer"])
df["override_actor_ne_credential_owner"] = (df["override_res"].notna()
        & df["override_credential_owner"].notna() & (df["override_res"] != df["override_credential_owner"]))
df["bank_change_flag"] = df["bank_change_7d"].eq(True)

def mgr_timing(r):
    if pd.isna(r["mgr_ts"]): return "none_recorded"
    if pd.notna(r["release_ts"]) and r["mgr_ts"] < r["release_ts"]: return "before_release"
    if pd.notna(r["settle_ts"]):
        return "after_release_before_settlement" if r["mgr_ts"] < r["settle_ts"] else "after_settlement"
    return "after_release_settlement_unknown"
df["mgr_timing"] = df.apply(mgr_timing, axis=1)

def audit_timing(r):
    if pd.isna(r["audit_ts"]): return "none_recorded"
    if r["audit_h_after_release"] <= 24: return "inside_24h_recall_window"
    if r["audit_ts"] <= r["audit_deadline_1bd"]: return "within_1bd_but_after_24h"
    return "late"
df["audit_timing"] = df.apply(audit_timing, axis=1)

distinct = df["completed"] & (df["preparer"] != df["releaser"])
df["pair"] = df["preparer"].where(distinct) + ">" + df["releaser"].where(distinct)
df["pair_count_in_sample"] = df["pair"].map(df["pair"].value_counts())
df["repeated_pair_flag"] = (df["pair_count_in_sample"] >= 2) | (df["joint_payments_30d"] >= PAIR_THRESHOLD)
df["pair_workstation_flag"] = df["repeated_pair_flag"] & df["shared_workstation"].eq(True)

def prompts(r):
    p = []
    if r["blocked_attempt"]: p.append("blocked attempt: check resubmission pattern")
    if r["nominal_violation"] and r["ed1_evidence"].startswith("NOT"): p.append("same-actor release without evidenced ED-1")
    if r["override_by_preparer"]: p.append("preparer overrode AV-2 warning")
    if r["override_actor_ne_credential_owner"]: p.append("override actor != credential owner")
    if r["completed"] and r["av_log"] in ("unavailable", "override", "warning"): p.append("released without clean AV-2 pass")
    if r["mgr_timing"] in ("after_settlement", "after_release_settlement_unknown"): p.append("manager review not shown before settlement")
    if r["mgr_independent"] is False: p.append("manager not independent")
    if r["completed"] and r["bank_change_flag"]: p.append("bank change in prior 7 days")
    if r["completed"] and r["audit_timing"] in ("within_1bd_but_after_24h", "late", "none_recorded"):
        p.append("audit outside 24h recall window")
    if r["repeated_pair_flag"]: p.append("repeated pair (alert, not proof)")
    if r["pair_workstation_flag"]: p.append("shared workstation with repeated pair")
    if r["receipt_evidence"] in ("missing", "fabricated"): p.append("receipt evidence defective")
    return "; ".join(p) if p else "none"
df["review_prompts"] = df.apply(prompts, axis=1)

# 7. Output
pd.set_option("display.width", 250)
pd.set_option("display.max_colwidth", 90)
print(BANNER)
print("\nNominal FEP-1 violators among completed payments:", sorted(nominal_violations), "| source:", src)
print("Blocked or attempted-but-not-completed:", sorted(blocked_attempts))
print("\n--- Timing and coverage ---")
print(df[["amount_eur", "nominal_violation", "av_coverage", "mgr_action", "mgr_timing",
          "mgr_h_after_release", "audit_h_after_release", "audit_timing"]].to_string())
print("\n--- ED-1 evidence (same-actor completed payments only) ---")
print(df[["ed1_evidence"]].to_string())
print("\n--- Triage prompts ---")
print(df[["pair", "repeated_pair_flag", "bank_change_flag", "review_prompts"]].to_string())
print("\n" + BANNER)

**PM4Py behaviour I cannot guarantee across versions:**
- The wrapper `filter_four_eyes_principle` has been documented inconsistently, so it may return the violating cases or the conforming ones. Some versions also accept extra keyword arguments (such as `keep_violations`) that I do not rely on. The code therefore reconciles the returned set against an independent pandas computation and falls back to pandas if neither reading matches.
- Cases lacking one of the two activities (such as AP-205) are expected to be excluded from both sets, but this depends on the implementation.
- `format_dataframe` may add helper columns, re-sort, or emit deprecation warnings. I re-coerce the timestamps to UTC afterwards.
- Business-day arithmetic (`BDay`) with tz-aware timestamps, and the interpretation of "within one business day", are my assumptions. The 24h recall flag is separate.
- pandas `groupby.apply` warnings are avoided by applying to a single column.
