### Why both directions in a DFG do **not** by themselves prove concurrency  

The DFG contains bidirectional edges, e.g., (`Credit Check`, `Engineering Review`) with count 17 and (`Engineering Review`, `Credit Check`) with count 11. These counts aggregate **across** all 30 traces and simply record that *some* traces observed CE and *some* observed EC. Within any single trace, the two activities could be sequential (C then E **or** E then C), overlapping, or even interleaved with other activities. The presence of both directions therefore proves **variation** in the log, not **concurrency** inside a single run. Attributing concurrency solely from DFG edge counts would be a logical leap; the model must treat the two checks as *possibly* ordered or partially ordered, not as definitively concurrent.

# 2. Shared Assumptions (common to all three models)

1. **Eventcompletion semantics** – Each activity is atomic and has a completion timestamp. If two activities share the same timestamp, the ingestion index decides their relative order (S7).  
2. **Missing Supplier Confirmation** – Approximately 510% of Supplier Confirmation events may be absent because of an integration problem (S5). The models therefore treat **S** as *optional* and may be omitted entirely.  
3. **Absent external/stocked attribute** – The log does not export whether a component is “external” or “stocked”. Consequently, all three models must allow **S** to be absent for *any* order, reflecting the possibility that a given order is stocked (S4).  
4. **Optionality representation** – Optionality (Expedite Review, Supplier Confirmation) is expressed through controlflow alternatives (XOR/OR in ProcessTree, or partialorder edges in POWL) together with explicit “may be omitted” semantics.  
5. **Start / end constraints** – Register Order is always the first activity; Archive Case is always the last (D1). Release Production always follows Approve Configuration (D2).

# 3. Conservative Candidate  

## 3.1 Inductivebias statement  
The conservative model adopts a **strict inductive bias**: only behaviours directly supported by at least two observed variants **or** by highconfidence domain rules (D1D2, D6) are admitted. It avoids any generalization that would permit combinations not present in the log, thereby minimising overfitting but risking underfitting of legitimate business variations (e.g., stocked components skipping S, or truly concurrent C/E). The model encodes a linear sequence that matches the most frequent observed variant and uses an XOR to choose between the two observed orders of Credit Check and Engineering Review, while treating Supplier Confirmation and Expedite Review as optional *only* when they appear in the data.  

## 3.2 Compact formal expression  
A ProcessTree that linearises the dominant trace and branches on the C/E order, with optional S and Expedite attached after the C/E block but before Approve Configuration, and with S and Expedite allowed **only** if they are present in the observed variants.

* New business rules that introduce Expedite Review *before* one of the checks would be rejected, even if later justified.

# 4. Balanced Candidate  

## 4.1 Inductivebias statement  
The balanced model seeks the **most plausible generalization** while **explicitly managing uncertainty**. It encodes the observed orderings as a **partial order**: both Credit Check and Engineering Review must precede Approve Configuration, but their relative order is left unspecified. Supplier Confirmation may occur after either check and must precede Approve Configuration, but may be omitted (reflecting S5 and S4). Expedite Review is modelled as an optional transition that requires both checks to have occurred and must precede Approve Configuration; its order relative to Supplier Confirmation is also left open (partialorder). The result is a compact POWL partialorder model that captures the majority of observed variants while allowing a controlled set of unobserved but plausible interleavings.  

## 4.2 Decision between sequence, concurrency, and partial order  

| Activity | Modelling choice | Rationale |
|----------|------------------|-----------|
| **Credit Check  Engineering Review** | **Partial order** (both must happen before A, order free) | Both CE and EC are observed (V1, V2). A fixed sequence would either over or underconstrain. |
| **Supplier Confirmation** | **Optional with precedence from {C,E}** | V3V5 show S after one check; V5 shows S between checks. D5 allows S after either check. A partialorder edge from each check to S captures “afterCorafterE”. |
| **Expedite Review** | **Optional, after {C,E}, before A; order relative to S free** | D6 mandates Ex after both checks and before A. Observed variants place Ex before or after S (V6, V7). A partialorder edge C/EExA plus separate optional edges allow ExS. |
| **All other edges** | **Mandatory** (D1D2, D3D4 as above) | Highconfidence rules are encoded as fixed edges. |

# 5. Permissive Candidate  

## 5.1 Inductivebias statement  
The permissive model adopts a **broad inductive bias**: any behaviour **consistent with the broad domain rules (D1D7, S1S7)** is admitted, even if never observed. The only hard constraints are the highconfidence rules (start/end, ARl, C/EA). All other activities (S, Ex) are **completely optional** and may appear in **any order** before Approve Configuration, including the possibility of appearing **after** other activities that the more restrictive models would forbid. The model therefore maximises generalisability at the expense of precision.  

## 5.2 Boundary between “reasonable generalization” and forbidden behaviour  

| Forbidden (even in permissive model) | Allowed (reasonable generalisation) |
|--------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Approve Configuration **before** both Credit Check and Engineering Review (violates D3) | Any order of C/E as long as both precede A. |
| Supplier Confirmation **after** Approve Configuration (violates D4) | S may appear anywhere **before** A, or be omitted entirely. |
| Expedite Review **without** both checks completed (violates D6) | Ex may be omitted, or may appear anywhere **before** A provided CE have occurred. |
| Expedite Review **after** Approve Configuration (violates D6) | Ex must precede A. |
| Register Order not first or Archive Case not last (violates D1) | Start/end are fixed. |
| Release Production without Approve Configuration (violates D2) | ARl is mandatory. |

All other interleavings of S and Ex (including S before C, Ex before S, both omitted, etc.) are **admitted** as plausible generalisations.

net_con, im_con, fm_con = convert_to_petri_net(model_conservative)

# Balanced – POWL
net_bal, im_bal, fm_bal = convert_to_petri_net(model_balanced)

# Permissive – POWL
net_perm, im_perm, fm_perm = convert_to_petri_net(model_permissive)

# -------------------------------------------------
# 3. Soundness checks
# -------------------------------------------------
def check_soundness(net, im, fm, name):
    result = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)
    print(f"\n--- Soundness ({name}) ---")
    print(result)

check_soundness(net_con, im_con, fm_con, "Conservative")
check_soundness(net_bal, im_bal, fm_bal, "Balanced")
check_soundness(net_perm, im_perm, fm_perm, "Permissive")

# -------------------------------------------------
# 4. Fitness, precision, generalization, simplicity
# -------------------------------------------------
def compute_metrics(net, im, fm, label):
    # Fitness (alignment-based)
    fit = pm4py.fitness_alignments(log, net, im, fm)
    print(f"\n--- Fitness ({label}) ---")
    print(f"  Fitness (proportion of trace covered): {fit['fitness']:.3f}")

    # Precision (alignmentbased)
    prec = pm4py.precision_alignments(log, net, im, fm)
    print(f"  Precision (properly allowed moves): {prec['precision']:.3f}")

    # Generalization (TBreplay)
    gen = pm4py.generalization_tbr(log, net, im, fm)
    print(f"  Generalization (TBreplay): {gen['generalization']:.3f}")

    # Simplicity (Petrinet)
    simp = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm)
    print(f"  Simplicity (places/transitions): {simp['simplicity']:.3f}")

compute_metrics(net_con, im_con, fm_con, "Conservative")
compute_metrics(net_bal, im_bal, fm_bal, "Balanced")
compute_metrics(net_perm, im_perm, fm_perm, "Permissive")

# -------------------------------------------------
# 5. Bounded playout (sampled) – NOT a proof of language inclusion
# -------------------------------------------------
print("\n--- Sampled playout (1000 traces per model) ---")
for name, net, _ in [("Conservative", net_con, None),
                     ("Balanced", net_bal, None),
                     ("Permissive", net_perm, None)]:
    played = pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm, number_of_traces=1000, unique=True)
    print(f"{name}: {len(played)} unique traces generated")
    # quick check: how many of the played traces are already in the original log?
    played_set = set(tuple(t) for t in played)
    original_set = set(tuple(t) for t in pm4py.get_event_log_as_list(log))
    overlap = played_set & original_set
    print(f"  Overlap with original 30case log: {len(overlap)}")

# -------------------------------------------------
positive_traces = [
    # All valid under at least one model
    "Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",

    "Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",   # no S
    "Register Order, Engineering Review, Credit Check, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case", # no S

negative_traces = [
    # Violate highconfidence rules
    "Register Order, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",                # A before checks
    "Register Order, Credit Check, Release Production, Archive Case",                         # missing E before A

    "Register Order, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case", # Ex before checks

print("\n--- Positive/negative trace validation ---")
for name, net, im, fm in [("Conservative", net_con, im_con, fm_con),
                           ("Balanced", net_bal, im_bal, fm_bal),
                           ("Permissive", net_perm, im_perm, fm_perm)]:
    # Positive
    pos_log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(positive_traces)
    pos_fit = pm4py.fitness_alignments(pos_log, net, im, fm)
    print(f"{name} – positive traces accepted: {pos_fit['fitness']:.3f}")

    # Negative
    neg_log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(negative_traces)
    neg_fit = pm4py.fitness_alignments(neg_log, net, im, fm)
    print(f"{name} – negative traces rejected: {1 - neg_fit['fitness']:.3f}")

# -------------------------------------------------
# 7. Limitations statement
# -------------------------------------------------
print("\n--- Limitations ---")
print("- Sampled playout (1000 traces) provides a *snapshot* of the language; it does **not** prove language inclusion or exclusion.")
print("- Precision and generalization metrics are computed via alignments on the *finite* log; they cannot capture behaviours outside the observed 30 cases.")
print("- The models’ POWL conversion assumes a flat set of transitions; silent transitions for start/end may be inferred but are not explicitly modelled here.")

# 9. Recommendation and DataCollection Plan  

## 9.1 Recommendation  

| Goal | Recommended candidate | Rationale |
|------|----------------------|-----------|
| **Immediate production use (complianceoriented)** | **Balanced** | Enforces the core highconfidence rules (D1D2, D3D6) while explicitly modelling the main sources of uncertainty (C/E order, optional S, optional Ex). It offers a good tradeoff: fitness remains high on the existing 30 cases, precision risk is moderate, and the model can be explained to stakeholders as “checks must both finish before approval, supplier confirmation may be omitted, expedite may appear anywhere before approval but only after both checks”. |
| **Sensitivity / “whatif” analysis** | **Permissive** | Provides the broadest space of plausible behaviours, enabling the team to explore edge cases (e.g., S omitted, Ex before S, S before checks) and to assess how often such patterns occur in future data. Useful for scenario planning and for identifying where data gaps exist. |

## 9.2 Datacollection plan to resolve ambiguities  

| Ambiguity | Attribute / timestamp to collect | How it helps distinguish hypotheses |
|-----------|----------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| **Order of Credit Check vs Engineering Review** | *Start timestamp* for each check (e.g., `CC_start`, `EC_start`) and *completion timestamp* (`CC_end`, `EC_end`). | If `CC_end`<`EC_start` in most cases  sequential CE; if `EC_end`<`CC_start`  sequential EC; if overlap (neither ends before the other starts)  concurrent/parallel. |
| **Optionality of Supplier Confirmation** | *External/Stocked flag* (binary) and *reason code* for missing S (integration error vs stocked component). | A flag that is “stocked” = 1 would explain Somission without integration issues; “integration error” = 1 would support S5’s missingS hypothesis. |
| **Placement of Expedite Review relative to S** | *Timestamp* for `Expedite Review` completion (`Ex_end`) alongside `Supplier Confirmation` (`S_end`). | Comparing `Ex_end` vs `S_end` tells whether Ex typically precedes or follows S, allowing us to test D6’s “may occur before or after S” clause. |
| **Timestamps of Release Production & Approve Configuration** | Already logged, but ensure they are *completion* timestamps, not just start. | Guarantees D2 (ARl) is enforced in the data and reduces reliance on logorder heuristics. |
| **Ingestion index for tied timestamps** | Record the order in which events were ingested when timestamps are equal. | Provides a deterministic breaktie mechanism, matching S7 and reducing ambiguity in DFG edge directions. |

**Suggested future trace set (maximally informative):**  

1. A trace where **C** and **E** are truly concurrent (same start/end window).  
2. A trace with a **stocked** component that **skips** S entirely.  
3. A trace where **Expedite Review** occurs **before** Supplier Confirmation but **after** both checks.  
4. A trace where **Supplier Confirmation** occurs **before** Credit Check (to test D5’s “after either check” rule).  

Collecting these four scenario logs (each a few dozens of cases) would allow us to:  

* Verify whether C/E are ever concurrent.  
* Confirm that Somission is driven by the stocked flag rather than integration errors.  
* Quantify how often Ex precedes vs follows S in practice.  
* Validate or refute the “S may begin after either C or E” hypothesis (D5).

# 10. Uncertainty Statement  

| Modelling decision | Calibrated confidence (subjective probability) | Alternative interpretation that would materially change the recommendation |
|--------------------|----------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| **C/E ordering** (must both happen before A, order free) | **0.45** that the true order is **CE** in the majority of cases, **0.45** that it is **EC**, **0.10** that they are **concurrent** (overlap) in some runs. | If new data reveal a strong systematic bias (e.g., 90% CE), the balanced model’s partialorder would become overly permissive; the conservative model (forcing a fixed XOR) or a revised model that adds a deterministic sequence would be preferable. |
| **Supplier Confirmation optionality** (may be omitted, may appear after C or E) | **0.80** that S is **optional** (consistent with S4/S5), **0.15** that S is **mandatory** for all external components (per S3), **0.05** that missing S is always due to integration bugs and the flag is unavailable. | If a subsequent audit uncovers that *every* component is externally sourced and the integration issue never occurs, the balanced/permissive models would be **overpermissive** regarding Somission; a conservative model that reintroduces S as mandatory would be favoured. |
| **Expedite Review placement** (must be after both checks, before A; order relative to S free) | **0.70** that Ex **always** respects the “after CE, before A” rule, **0.20** that Ex can appear **before S** in some cases, **0.10** that Ex is sometimes **omitted** entirely (beyond the 2 observed traces). | If future logs show Ex occurring *without* both checks (e.g., after only C), the D6 constraint would be violated, forcing a redesign that either tightens the model or documents a new business rule. |
| **Partialorder vs totalorder representation** (balanced model uses PO) | **0.75** that a partialorder adequately captures the observed variation, **0.20** that a total order (e.g., always CE) is more realistic, **0.05** that full concurrency is the norm. | Discovering that the checks are *always* run in parallel (timestamps overlap) would shift confidence toward a **concurrency** model (perhaps a ProcessTree with ANDparallel nodes), altering the balanced recommendation. |

**Overall calibrated confidence:**  

- The **balanced** model retains **0.75** overall confidence given the current evidence distribution.  
- The **conservative** model’s confidence is lower (**0.55**) because it risks underfitting legitimate variations (Somission, C/E concurrency).  
- The **permissive** model’s confidence is **0.65** for exploratory purposes but suffers from high precision risk.

**Key alternative interpretation:**  
If future processmining results demonstrate that **Credit Check and Engineering Review are *always* executed concurrently** (identical start/end timestamps), the balanced partialorder would no longer be the most parsimonious representation; a **ProcessTree with an ANDparallel node** for the two checks would be more appropriate, and the recommendation would shift toward a **conservative** or a newly defined **concurrencyaware** model. Conversely, if data reveal that **Supplier Confirmation is *never* omitted** (the 510% missingS rate is solely due to a bug that is now fixed), the balanced model would become **overpermissive**, and the **conservative** model (forcing S) would be preferred for compliance. Monitoring these two dimensions will be the most impactful way to refine the model choice.
