# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

Treating raw `[start, complete]` intervals as literal single-server physical execution distorts queueing, capacity, throughput, and conformance metrics in predictable directions depending on the generating mechanism.

### Cluster-by-Cluster Bias Direction and Mechanism

* **G1 (`Apply label`, `RBT-7` — Genuine simultaneous batch on 4-item fixture)**
  * *Waiting time*: **[Obs]** Mean raw wait is `4.25 min` (`2`–`6 min`). **Unbiased** as actual physical pre-process wait, though `2`–`4 min` of that wait for `L01`–`L03` is *batch-formation (Synchronization) delay* waiting for `L04` (`08:58`) and cycle start (`09:00`), not capacity overload.
  * *Service time*: **[Obs]** `3.0 min` per item (`09:00`–`09:03`). **Unbiased per item** and **unbiased per batch** (`3.0 min`), *unless* an analyst divides batch time by 4 (`0.75 min`), which would misstate unit residence time in the fixture.
  * *Resource utilization*: **Severely inflated ($\times 4$, +300%)** if computed naively as $\sum (\text{complete}_i - \text{start}_i) / T_{\text{window}} = 12\text{ min} / 3\text{ min} = 400\%$ assuming $C=1$, instead of $12 / (4 \times 3) = 100\%$ during the active cycle.
  * *Bottleneck detection, throughput & conformance*: Naive sum-of-durations flags `RBT-7` as a false utilization bottleneck; batch throughput is $4\text{ items} / 3\text{ min} = 80\text{ items/hr}$ during active run; conformance against a 1-piece-flow Petri net may falsely penalize simultaneous token transitions unless batch semantics are modeled.

* **G2 (`Surface inspection`, `T-B` — Start batching over sequential execution)**
  * *Waiting time*: **Biased downward (underestimated)**. **[Obs]** Raw mean wait is `12.25 min` (`18, 14, 11, 6 min`). **[Est]** Under sequential execution (**[Assump-A3]**: `S01` `10:00`–`10:08`, `S02` `10:08`–`10:14`, `S03` `10:14`–`10:21`, `S04` `10:21`–`10:27`), true waits are `18, 22, 25, 27 min` (mean **`23.0 min`**, understated by **`-10.75 min`** or **`-46.7%`**). Pressing "begin group" at `10:00` hides in-batch queue waiting inside apparent service.
  * *Service time*: **Biased upward (inflated)**. **[Obs]** Raw mean service is `17.50 min` (`8, 14, 21, 27 min`). **[Est]** Reconstructed sequential service times are `8, 6, 7, 6 min` (mean **`6.75 min`**, inflated by **`+10.75 min`** or **`+159%`**).
  * *Resource utilization*: **Biased upward**. Summed case durations equal `70 min` over a `27 min` active span (`259%` apparent active utilization; `116.7%` over a 60-min hour vs `45.0%` true active share `27/60`).
  * *Bottleneck detection, throughput & conformance*: False service-time bottleneck (looks like a slow `17.5 min` inspection with high variance rather than a crisp `6.75 min` task); interval-level throughput over `10:00`–`10:27` (`8.89 items/hr`) is unaffected, but Little's Law ($L = \lambda W$) breaks if applied to service vs queue sub-states; SLA rules on max inspection duration falsely trigger on later items in every group (`S03`, `S04`).

* **G3 (`Release review`, `T-C` — End batching / folder submission)**
  * *Waiting time*: **Unbiased upstream, biased upward downstream**. **[Obs]** Raw upstream wait (`start - queue_arrival`) is `22, 18, 19, 19 min` (mean `19.50 min`), which accurately reflects pre-review queue wait because `start` was logged progressively. However, *downstream* steps waiting on `E01`–`E03` experience up to `20 min` of extra folder-holding delay until `11:30`.
  * *Service time*: **Biased upward (inflated)**. **[Obs]** Raw mean service is `16.75 min` (`28, 20, 13, 6 min`). **[Est]** Inter-start sequential durations are `8, 7, 7, 6 min` (mean **`7.00 min`**, inflated by **`+9.75 min`** or **`+139%`**) because post-review folder wait is absorbed into `complete - start`.
  * *Resource utilization*: **Biased upward**. Summed durations = `67 min` across `28 min` active span (`239%` apparent active utilization vs `100%` true active utilization during `11:02`–`11:30`).
  * *Bottleneck detection, throughput & conformance*: Falsely diagnoses `Release review` as a slow, high-effort activity (`16.75 min` labor cost) rather than a `7 min` review plus a batching transfer policy; creates bursty downstream arrivals (`4` items released at `11:30`), distorting downstream queue variance; may trigger false "concurrent sign-off" segregation-of-duties or workload alerts.

* **G4 (`Pack item`, `T-D` — Sequential batching / burst drain)**
  * *Waiting time*: **Unbiased (subject to `±1 min` `:00`-second truncation)**. **[Obs]** Raw waits grow linearly (`7, 10, 13, 16, 19 min`; mean `13.0 min`) because arrival rate during `11:58`–`12:02` (`1.0 item/min`) exceeds service rate (`0.25 item/min`).
  * *Service time & Utilization*: **Unbiased** (`4.0 min/item`, `20 min` active over `12:05`–`12:25` = `100%` active utilization during run), with `±1 min` individual uncertainty if seconds were truncated.
  * *Bottleneck detection, throughput & conformance*: Genuine local transient congestion (`15 items/hr` packing capacity vs `60 items/hr` burst arrival); however, `P01`'s initial `7 min` wait (`11:58`–`12:05`) could reflect deliberate batch-threshold waiting ("wait until 5 items accumulate") vs finishing an unlogged task—conflating policy wait with capacity saturation if unverified.

* **G5 (`Root-cause analysis`, `analyst_pool` — Shared account + multitasking)**
  * *Waiting time*: **Unbiased at case level** (`15, 15, 16, 15 min`; mean `15.25 min`, up to `1 min` precision), **misattributed at queue-model level** if modeled as $M/M/1$ instead of $M/M/c$ ($c \le 3$).
  * *Service time*: **Biased upward relative to uninterrupted touch time** where multitasking occurs. **[Obs]** `A01` (`25 min`) and `A03` (`20 min`) span intervals where 4 cases are simultaneously open across $\le 3$ analysts (`13:12`–`13:20`), meaning elapsed `[start, complete]` includes cognitive context-switching or paused time.
  * *Resource utilization*: **Severely biased upward per "resource"** if `analyst_pool` is treated as $C=1$: summed time is `25 + 15 + 20 + 10 = 70 min` over a `30 min` window (`233%` utilization). Divided across $C=3$ analysts, pool utilization during `13:00`–`13:30` is $70 / 90 = 77.8\%$ (still slightly overstated by paused multitasking time).
  * *Bottleneck detection, throughput & conformance*: Treating `analyst_pool` as one hyper-overloaded worker (`233%`) triggers false burnout/overload alarms while hiding per-analyst performance dispersion; non-FIFO completions (`A02` and `A04` overtake `A01`) look like priority-jumping conformance violations if audited as a single FIFO server.

* **G6 (`Close ticket`, `T-F` — Deferred end-of-shift bulk data entry)**
  * *Waiting time*: **Massively biased upward** (`35` to `175 min`, mean **`103.43 min`**).
  * *Service time*: **Collapsed to zero** (`0.0 min` for all 7 tickets).
  * *Resource utilization*: **Biased toward `0%`** (`0 min` logged work across the entire afternoon!).
  * *Bottleneck detection, throughput & conformance*: Creates a paradoxical **"phantom queue bottleneck with an idle worker"** (detailed below), distorts hourly throughput into a fake end-of-shift spike (`7` completions at `17:00`, `0` during `14:00`–`16:59`), and causes false SLA waiting-time breaches or retroactive timestamp conformance flags.

* **G7 (`Manual measure`, `T-G` — 15-min timestamp coarsening)**
  * *Waiting time*: **Biased downward and physically nonsensical**. **[Obs]** Raw waits are `+2, -1, -4, -7, -11 min` (mean **`-4.20 min`**). Flooring `start` to `14:00` subtracts up to `14:59` of true start time, creating negative queue waits that crash queueing formulas or get clipped to `0`, hiding real wait.
  * *Service time*: **Biased upward**. **[Obs]** Logged service is `15.0 min` per item (`75 item-min` total). **[Est]** Staggered `queue_arrival` (`13:58`–`14:11`) and 1-min staggered `recorded_at` (`14:16`–`14:20`) indicate sequential measurements of roughly **`1`–`4 min` per item**, inflated to `15 min` (`+275%` to `+1400%`) by snapping `[start, complete]` to `[14:00, 14:15]`.
  * *Resource utilization*: **Biased upward ($\times 5$ within slot)**. Summed durations = `75 min` inside a `15 min` window (`500%` utilization).
  * *Bottleneck detection, throughput & conformance*: Falsely flags `T-G` as both a slow `15 min` bottleneck (`500%` loaded) and a **conformance violator** (starting `M02`–`M05` *before* upstream `queue_arrival`, producing apparent out-of-order causal inversions in process discovery / alignment checking!).

* **G8 (`Repair triage`, `T-H` — Queue accumulation after capacity loss)**
  * *Waiting time*: **Accurate as total customer wait (`38`–`58 min`, mean `48.20 min`), but severely biased upward as congestion wait** if downtime (`15:00`–`15:35` safety check) is omitted. Roughly `30`–`38 min` per case is *unavailability blockage*, while only `0, 5, 10, 16, 20 min` (mean ~`10.2 min`) is serial queue drain after `15:38`.
  * *Service time & Utilization*: **Service time unbiased** (`7.0 min/item`). **Utilization biased downward** if denominator is total clock hours without subtracting scheduled safety unavailability (`35 min` active / `73 min` span = `47.9%` vs `100%` of *available* capacity during `15:38`–`16:13`).
  * *Bottleneck detection, throughput & conformance*: Naive analysis sees long `48.2 min` waits paired with moderate `48%` utilization—misleading managers into blaming operator sluggishness or adding triage headcount instead of rescheduling/staggering the `35 min` safety check! Cross-system clock drift (`15:35` safety end vs `15:38` triage start) can also create a `3 min` unexplained idle gap or apparent overlap if drifted the other way.

### Why G6-Like Data Simultaneously Creates Inflated Waiting and Near-Zero Service
G6 illustrates a fundamental **sojourn-time conservation trap** under deferred bulk logging. For any case $i$ arriving at $a_i$ whose true physical execution occurs over $[s_i^*, c_i^*]$ ($a_i \le s_i^* < c_i^* \le t_{\text{bulk}}$), true waiting time is $W_i^* = s_i^* - a_i$ and true service time is $S_i^* = c_i^* - s_i^*$. When an operator instead uses a shift-end bulk closure form at $t_{\text{bulk}} = \text{17:00}$ that stamps $s_i = c_i = t_{\text{bulk}}$, the logged metrics become:
$$S_i^{\text{logged}} = t_{\text{bulk}} - t_{\text{bulk}} = 0$$
$$W_i^{\text{logged}} = t_{\text{bulk}} - a_i = \underbrace{(s_i^* - a_i)}_{W_i^*\text{ (true wait)}} + \underbrace{(c_i^* - s_i^*)}_{S_i^*\text{ (true service)}} + \underbrace{(t_{\text{bulk}} - c_i^*)}_{\Delta_i^{\text{defer}}\text{ (reporting lag)}}$$
Two destructive distortions happen at once:
1. **Service time collapses to zero** ($S_i^{\text{logged}} = 0$, or $< 1\text{ min}$ under minute censoring), making `T-F` appear `0%` utilized and effortlessly fast.
2. **All displaced service time ($S_i^*$) plus hours of post-work reporting delay ($\Delta_i^{\text{defer}}$) are swept directly into logged waiting time ($W_i^{\text{logged}}$)**, inflating mean wait to `103.4 min`.

In bottleneck diagnostics, this produces the classic **administrative mirage**: `Close ticket` lights up red as the worst waiting-time bottleneck in the plant (`103.4 min` wait) while simultaneously showing `0%` resource utilization—tempting analysts to conclude that `T-F` is ignoring a massive queue all afternoon, when in reality work may have been completed promptly and merely batch-logged at `17:00`.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Independent pandas diagnostics per resource / cluster
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def compute_resource_overlap(sub: pd.DataFrame) -> tuple[float, int]:
    """
    Compute unordered pair overlap rate and peak concurrency for a resource.
    Positive-length intervals overlap if max(s_i, s_j) < min(e_i, e_j);
    zero-duration point events overlap if s_i == s_j == e_i == e_j.
    """
    starts = sub["start_dt"].tolist()
    ends = sub["complete_dt"].tolist()
    m = len(starts)
    total_pairs = m * (m - 1) // 2
    if total_pairs == 0:
        return 0.0, m

    overlap_pairs = 0
    for i in range(m):
        for j in range(i + 1, m):
            if pd.isna(starts[i]) or pd.isna(starts[j]) or pd.isna(ends[i]) or pd.isna(ends[j]):
                continue
            if starts[i] == ends[i] and starts[j] == ends[j]:
                if starts[i] == starts[j]:
                    overlap_pairs += 1
            else:
                if max(starts[i], starts[j]) < min(ends[i], ends[j]):
                    overlap_pairs += 1

    # Sweep-line peak concurrency (+1 at start, -1 at end; for point events +1 before -1 at t)
    events = []
    for s, e in zip(starts, ends):
        if pd.isna(s) or pd.isna(e):
            continue
        if s == e:
            events.append((s, 0, 1))   # point start processed before point end
            events.append((e, 2, -1))
        else:
            events.append((s, 1, 1))   # interval end (-1, rank 0.5) processed before interval start (+1, rank 1)
            events.append((e, 0, -1))  # abutting [12:05,12:09] & [12:09,12:13] => peak concurrency 1
    events.sort(key=lambda x: (x[0], x[1]))

    cur, peak = 0, 0
    for _, _, delta in events:
        cur += delta
        peak = max(peak, cur)

    return overlap_pairs / total_pairs, peak


# Resource-level overlap (identical to cluster-level here since 1 resource per cluster)
res_overlap = {}
for res, grp in df.groupby("resource", sort=False):
    res_overlap[res] = compute_resource_overlap(grp)

diag_rows = []
for cluster_id, g in df.groupby("cluster", sort=False):
    g_seq = g.sort_values("sequence_no")
    n = len(g)
    res = g["resource"].iloc[0]
    ov_rate, peak_conc = res_overlap[res]

    wait_min = (g["start_dt"] - g["queue_arrival_dt"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
    serv_min = (g["complete_dt"] - g["start_dt"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
    rec_lag_sec = (g["recorded_at_dt"] - g["complete_dt"]).dt.total_seconds()

    # Adjacency count among consecutive sequence_no rows: complete_{i} == start_{i+1}
    adj_count = int((g_seq["complete_dt"].iloc[:-1].values == g_seq["start_dt"].iloc[1:].values).sum())

    # Quarter-hour boundary share across start and complete
    lifecycle_ts = pd.concat([g["start_dt"], g["complete_dt"]]).dropna()
    qtr_hr_share = float(
        ((lifecycle_ts.dt.minute % 15 == 0) & (lifecycle_ts.dt.second == 0)).mean()
    )

    # Check if sequence_no order contradicts recorded_at monotonicity
    seq_rec_inversion = not g_seq["recorded_at_dt"].is_monotonic_increasing

    # Queue build-up at earliest start in cluster
    first_start = g["start_dt"].min()
    queued_at_first_start = int((g["queue_arrival_dt"] <= first_start).sum())

    diag_rows.append({
        "cluster": cluster_id,
        "res": res,
        "n": n,
        "prec_doc": g["precision"].iloc[0],
        "ov_rate": round(ov_rate, 2),
        "peak_conc": peak_conc,
        "max_eq_start": int(g.groupby("start_dt").size().max()),
        "max_eq_end": int(g.groupby("complete_dt").size().max()),
        "adj_pairs": f"{adj_count}/{n - 1}",
        "wait_m_mean": round(wait_min.mean(), 1),
        "serv_m_mean": round(serv_min.mean(), 1),
        "rec_lag_s_med": round(rec_lag_sec.median(), 1),
        "rec_lag_s_span": round(rec_lag_sec.max() - rec_lag_sec.min(), 1),
        "qtr_hr_pct": int(round(100 * qtr_hr_share)),
        "q_at_s0": f"{queued_at_first_start}/{n}",
        "flags": ",".join(filter(None, [
            "ZERO_SERV" if g["flag_zero_service"].any() else "",
            f"NEG_WAIT({int(g['flag_negative_wait'].sum())})" if g["flag_negative_wait"].any() else "",
            "SEQ_REC_INV" if seq_rec_inversion else "",
            "NO_SEC_LIFECYCLE" if g["flag_sec_omitted_vs_prec"].any() else "",
        ])) or "OK",
    })

diag_df = pd.DataFrame(diag_rows).set_index("cluster")
combined = diag_df.join(pm4py_df)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

print(diag_df.to_string())

### 2. Exact Timestamps vs. Interval-Censored by Stated Precision

**[Assump-A4: Censoring Rules]** Under stated precision $\Delta$, a minute-level timestamp $t$ (`1 min` precision) represents an unknown true instant $t^* \in [t, t + 60\text{s})$ under floor truncation (or $[t - 30\text{s}, t + 30\text{s})$ under nearest-minute rounding), constrained causally by $a_i^* \le s_i^* \le c_i^* \le r_i^*$ when clocks are aligned (**[Assump-A1]**). For `15 min` precision in G7, the rule is explicitly undocumented ("down/up"), meaning an unanchored timestamp $t$ could span up to $(t - 15\text{m}, t + 15\text{m})$.

* **G1, G4, G8 (`1 sec` stated precision vs. whole-minute lifecycle strings)**:
  * *If exact (`:00` sec literal)*: G1 service is `180s`; G4 service is `240s`; G8 service is `420s`.
  * *If `1 sec` applies only to DB/PLC internals while lifecycle export truncated seconds (`[t, t+59s]`)*:
    * **G1**: **[Bnd]** Because `recorded_at` is `09:03:02`–`09:03:03`, causal ordering $c_i^* \le r_i^*$ pins `complete` tightly to **`[09:03:00, 09:03:03]`**!
