# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

The implemented model uses an accepting Petri net for the stable workflow, DECLARE for selected ordering and exclusion rules, a Log Skeleton for defensible count and order invariants, and separate monitors for contextual, resource, and authorization rules. Temporal deviations produce warnings. A case must satisfy every applicable hard constraint; passing one layer does not override failure in another.

Material assumptions:

- The test traces are complete cases. `Close Onboarding` ends the case, and no later event belongs to that onboarding instance. An unresolved eventual obligation is a violation at completion; in an ongoing case it is normally pending.
- Events represent completed activities. Trace order is the authoritative execution order. Timestamps measure elapsed wall-clock time, with timezones normalized. Equal timestamps need a trustworthy event sequence; otherwise strict ordering can be indeterminate.
- The business outcomes in R3 are `Activate Vendor` and `Reject Vendor`. Clarifications occur after receipt and before either outcome, possibly interleaved with checks, classification, diligence, and approvals. None may remain outstanding at either outcome.
- Clarifications are paired sequentially: one response per request, no unsolicited responses, and no overlapping requests. This pairing convention strengthens the literal eventual-response requirement and makes the intended cycles explicit.
- Each initial check occurs once. Further `Verify Bank Account` events are allowed after activation. Procedural state distinguishes initial verification from subsequent verification because the visible label alone does not.
- Classification chooses one of the three specified branches. The activity model verifies branch structure, not the substantive correctness of a risk assessment. That would require a risk attribute and decision policy; no additional risk-scoring rule is invented for the tests.
- The rejection route is the direct branch after classification. Rejection after diligence or approval is outside the stated backbone. Supporting it would require a coordinated revision of the model and R2 interpretation.
- Escalations may repeat, but occur after screening and before the business outcome. One opinion can satisfy multiple earlier unresolved escalations. An opinion before an escalation does not satisfy it. An opinion may follow rejection, provided all obligations are discharged before closure; optional additional opinions are also allowed.
- R9 compares every verification, including later re-verification, with the unique applicable approval in an activated case. A rejected case without approval has no applicable approval-resource comparison. Resource identifiers must resolve to stable person identities.
- Bank changes and suspensions are allowed after activation, including after first payment, until closure. Every change requires a later suspension. Re-verification is required after a change only before a later first payment. One later suspension or verification may satisfy multiple preceding changes.
- R11 does not say suspension must precede payment or introduce a reinstatement activity. Neither restriction is added. A change after the only first payment requires suspension but does not independently require another verification.
- Under the emergency alternative, the 24-hour deadline starts at activation and includes the deadline boundary. Authorization must already be effective at activation. Event order establishes that the opinion is subsequent; coarse timestamps can report zero elapsed time.

Two coherent resolutions of R5/R6 are:

1. **Strict legal policy, implemented here.** R5 remains unconditional. Every escalation preceding activation must have a later opinion before activation. R6's permission for early activation is rejected as an incompatible proposed exception. Even externally authorized early activation fails this policy version. This preserves the legal gate but can reject emergencies Operations considers justified and delay onboarding; the governance choice must be explicit.
2. **Formally amended emergency policy.** Replace the unconditional wording of R5 with an explicit exception. Normal activation retains the legal gate. Early activation requires independently verified, case-bound joint authorization from Legal and Operations, effective before activation, followed by an opinion within 24 hours of activation. This deliberately permits a period of activation without an opinion. Risks include authorization misuse and exposure during that period. Missing authorization evidence leaves the exception unproven; an early activation followed by a prompt opinion is insufficient evidence.

The second resolution is a policy amendment, not simultaneous literal enforcement of R5 and R6. Record the policy version with each assessment. T6 fails the implemented strict policy. Under the amended policy, its observed timing is acceptable, but the log cannot establish its emergency condition or authorization.

# 2. Requirement-to-Formalism Mapping

“Authoritative” identifies the implementation that decides the stated rule. Other layers provide diagnostic redundancy, not votes.

| Requirement | Primary formalism | Exact encoding | Why this formalism | Redundancy or limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | DECLARE, authoritative | `noncoexistence(Reject Vendor, Activate Vendor)` | Symmetric exclusion applies to the whole case regardless of order. | Also enforced by the procedural choice and skeleton `never_together`. One directed `nonsuccession` would be weaker. |
| R2 | Accepting Petri net, authoritative | The single classification token chooses rejection, manager approval, or diligence followed by compliance approval. Only the two approval branches enable activation. Accepted approval-count sum is one; rejected approval-count sum is zero. | Expresses the conditional choice and obligatory approval. | DECLARE and skeleton exclusions alone do not require an approval. Unary `exactly_one` on both approval labels would be wrong. |
| R3 | DECLARE, authoritative | `altresponse(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)`, `altprecedence(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)`, and `nonsuccession(F, C)` for each outcome F and each clarification activity C. | Permits repeated pairs and intervening work while preventing overlapping requests and responses after outcomes. | Skeleton count equality and later-response requirements are weaker. The net permits side events without pairing them. |
| R4 | DECLARE, authoritative | `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` | Every escalation requires a later opinion. | Also skeleton `always_after`. It does not alone require the opinion before activation. |
| R5 | Contextual sequence monitor, authoritative | At activation position j, for every escalation position i < j, require an opinion position k with i < k < j. | Expresses the conditional three-event relation without requiring opinions in non-escalated cases. | Activity order suffices, but the selected binary DECLARE templates do not directly encode this exact conditional without strengthening it. An automaton or Petri-net monitor could also enforce it. |
| R6 | Versioned policy and external authorization/time monitor, authoritative | Strict version: no emergency override. Amended version: unresolved escalation at activation requires verified prior joint authorization and a subsequent opinion no more than 24 hours after activation. | Authorization, role authority, emergency status, and deadlines need additional evidence. | The log cannot recover absent authorization. This hard deadline is separate from R12. |
| R7 | DECLARE, authoritative | `precedence(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` | Each escalation has a screening prerequisite. | Skeleton uses `(Sanctions Escalation, Run Sanctions Screening)` in `always_before`. The net's side-event gate is deliberately weaker. |
| R8 | DECLARE, authoritative | `noncoexistence(Enhanced Due Diligence, Manager Approval)` and `noncoexistence(Compliance Approval, Manager Approval)` | Both rules exclude coexistence in either order. | Also enforced by route choice and skeleton exclusions. |
| R9 | Resource-aware monitor, authoritative | Every applicable verification/approval resource pair differs; every capture/activation resource pair differs. | Activity-only models cannot compare people. | Missing identities produce an indeterminate comparison, not proof of separation. |
| R10 | Log Skeleton, authoritative for these count rules | `{0, 1}` in `activ_freq` for `Activate Vendor`, `Reject Vendor`, and `Release First Payment`. | Expresses optional occurrence with a maximum of one. | The net also prevents repetition. At-most-once does not impose existence. |
| R11 | Contextual sequence monitor, authoritative | A post-activation change sets `suspension_due` and `bank_dirty`. A later suspension clears the former; later verification clears the latter. Payment with `bank_dirty` fails; completion with `suspension_due` fails. | Requires lifecycle context and occurrence-sensitive obligations. | The net permits these events. Unconditional change-to-verification response would overconstrain changes with no later payment. |
| R12 | Matched temporal-profile monitor, authoritative for warnings | Store the three specified means and standard deviations in seconds; warn when `abs(elapsed - mean) > 2.0 * standard_deviation`. | Keeps expected timing separate from hard ordering rules. | Missing endpoints are not zero-duration observations. Raw pair enumeration needs adaptation for repeated clarification cycles. |

# 3. Procedural Backbone

The compact core is:

```text
Receive Onboarding Request
  → AND(Capture Master Data, Run Sanctions Screening, Verify Bank Account)
  → Classify Risk
  → XOR(
       Reject Vendor → Close Onboarding,
       Manager Approval → Activate Vendor → Release First Payment → Close Onboarding,
       Enhanced Due Diligence → Compliance Approval
         → Activate Vendor → Release First Payment → Close Onboarding
     )
```

The net also permits repeated `Request Clarification`, `Receive Clarification`, and `Sanctions Escalation` before the business outcome; `Obtain Legal Opinion` while the case is live; and `Change Bank Details`, `Suspend Vendor`, and `Verify Bank Account` after activation, before closure. These are permissive side-event loops. The other layers impose their matching, ordering, and consequential obligations.

The following code is self-contained for a PM4Py installation and assigns the accepting-net triple to `procedural_model`.

```python
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import PetriNet, Marking
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.utils.petri_utils import add_arc_from_to

net = PetriNet("controlled_vendor_onboarding")
place_names = [
    "start", "capture_todo", "screen_todo", "bank_todo",
    "capture_done", "screen_done", "bank_done", "classified",
    "diligence_done", "approved", "rejected", "active_unpaid",
    "active_paid", "before_outcome", "live", "end",
]
places = {name: PetriNet.Place(name) for name in place_names}
net.places.update(places.values())

def add_transition(name, label, inputs, outputs):
    transition = PetriNet.Transition(name, label)
    net.transitions.add(transition)
    for source in inputs:
        add_arc_from_to(places[source], transition, net)
    for target in outputs:
        add_arc_from_to(transition, places[target], net)
    return transition

add_transition(
    "receive_request", "Receive Onboarding Request", ["start"],
    ["capture_todo", "screen_todo", "bank_todo", "before_outcome", "live"],
)
add_transition("capture", "Capture Master Data",
               ["capture_todo"], ["capture_done"])
add_transition("screen", "Run Sanctions Screening",
               ["screen_todo"], ["screen_done"])
add_transition("initial_bank_verification", "Verify Bank Account",
               ["bank_todo"], ["bank_done"])
add_transition("classify", "Classify Risk",
               ["capture_done", "screen_done", "bank_done"], ["classified"])

add_transition("reject", "Reject Vendor",
               ["classified", "before_outcome"], ["rejected"])
add_transition("manager_approval", "Manager Approval",
               ["classified"], ["approved"])
add_transition("diligence", "Enhanced Due Diligence",
               ["classified"], ["diligence_done"])
add_transition("compliance_approval", "Compliance Approval",
               ["diligence_done"], ["approved"])
add_transition("activate", "Activate Vendor",
               ["approved", "before_outcome"], ["active_unpaid"])
add_transition("first_payment", "Release First Payment",
               ["active_unpaid"], ["active_paid"])
add_transition("close_rejected", "Close Onboarding",
               ["rejected", "live"], ["end"])
add_transition("close_accepted", "Close Onboarding",
               ["active_paid", "live"], ["end"])

for name, label in [
    ("clarification_request", "Request Clarification"),
    ("clarification_response", "Receive Clarification"),
    ("sanctions_escalation", "Sanctions Escalation"),
]:
    add_transition(name, label, ["before_outcome"], ["before_outcome"])

add_transition("legal_opinion", "Obtain Legal Opinion", ["live"], ["live"])

for phase in ["active_unpaid", "active_paid"]:
    for name, label in [
        ("change_bank", "Change Bank Details"),
        ("suspend", "Suspend Vendor"),
        ("reverify_bank", "Verify Bank Account"),
    ]:
        add_transition(name + "_" + phase, label, [phase], [phase])

initial_marking = Marking({places["start"]: 1})
final_marking = Marking({places["end"]: 1})
procedural_model = (net, initial_marking, final_marking)
```

Receipt produces three independent check tokens. Classification consumes all three completed-check tokens, implementing concurrency followed by synchronization. Its single output token enforces exclusive route choice. Diligence enables only compliance approval; the manager branch bypasses diligence. Both approval branches lead to activation. Rejection and activation consume the same `before_outcome` token, preventing both outcomes in one execution.

First payment consumes the unpaid state and therefore cannot repeat. Each closure consumes the appropriate terminal-route state and the live token, leaving exactly the final marking. The repeated bank-verification and closure labels have distinct internal transition names. All arcs are ordinary Petri-net arcs, including consume-and-return self-loops.

Clarification requests and responses can loop and interleave with other work, but pairing is authoritative in DECLARE. The net alone intentionally accepts an unanswered request, as in T5. Similarly, it permits an opinion after activation, allowing the policy layer to distinguish strict and amended R5/R6 behavior. Escalation's screening prerequisite is checked declaratively.

This is an exact representation of the permissive procedural component, with no process-tree approximation. Full compliance is the intersection with the other hard layers. Post-activation changes and repeat verification are included; the obligations they trigger are handled separately. Rejection after diligence, repeated payments, reopening, and a full suspension/reinstatement lifecycle are deliberately excluded pending explicit policy changes.

Here, “earlier” and “later” mean strictly earlier/later event positions.

| Encoded rule or explicitly listed group | Direction and semantics |
|---|---|
| `init(Receive Onboarding Request)` | The first activity is `Receive Onboarding Request`. |
| `exactly_one(Receive Onboarding Request)`; `exactly_one(Close Onboarding)` | Each named activity occurs once. The net, rather than this template, makes closure the last event. |
| `noncoexistence(Reject Vendor, Activate Vendor)` | Both outcomes cannot occur, in either order. |
| `noncoexistence(Reject Vendor, Manager Approval)`; `noncoexistence(Reject Vendor, Compliance Approval)` | Each listed approval is incompatible with rejection, in either order. |
| `noncoexistence(Enhanced Due Diligence, Manager Approval)` | Diligence and manager approval cannot both occur. |
| `noncoexistence(Compliance Approval, Manager Approval)` | The two approval types cannot both occur. |
| `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` | Every escalation requires a later opinion. Other activities, including activation, can intervene under this rule alone. |
| `response(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` | Every activation requires a later first payment. |
| `response(Release First Payment, Close Onboarding)` | Every first payment requires a later closure. |
| `response(Reject Vendor, Close Onboarding)` | Every rejection requires a later closure. |
| `precedence(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` | Each escalation requires earlier screening; screening does not require escalation. |
| `precedence(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` | Each first payment requires earlier activation. Payment is the constrained occurrence. Together with response, this supplies both directions of the dependency. |
| `altresponse(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` | Every request needs a later response before the next request, if any. Other activities may intervene. |
| `altprecedence(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` | Every response needs an earlier request since the preceding response, if any. This excludes unsolicited responses and repeated responses without a new request. |
| `nonsuccession(Activate Vendor, Request Clarification)`; `nonsuccession(Activate Vendor, Receive Clarification)` | Neither clarification activity may follow activation; both may occur before it. |
| `nonsuccession(Reject Vendor, Request Clarification)`; `nonsuccession(Reject Vendor, Receive Clarification)` | Neither clarification activity may follow rejection; both may occur before it. |

`response(A, B)` creates a future obligation when A occurs. `precedence(A, B)` checks an earlier prerequisite when B occurs. Neither implies the other. `altresponse` permits intervening work but prohibits another A before the required B. `chainresponse` would require B immediately after A and is deliberately not used.

`noncoexistence` is symmetric exclusion. `nonsuccession(A, B)` prohibits only the order A before B and permits B before A. That distinction lets clarifications occur before an outcome while excluding contradictory outcomes in either order.

The clarification constraints jointly force completion before an outcome: an outstanding request cannot be repaired afterward because its required response would violate `nonsuccession`. R5 is not encoded as unconditional `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)`, which would incorrectly require opinions without escalation. Nor do we prohibit all post-activation opinions, which would reject harmless additional opinions after a timely required one.

# 5. Log-Skeleton Model

This partial specification uses equal counts, focal-activity order requirements, symmetric exclusions, and selected bounded frequencies. Both orientations of symmetric relations are made explicit.

```python
def symmetric(pairs):
    return set(pairs) | {(b, a) for a, b in pairs}

log_skeleton = {
    "equivalence": symmetric({
        ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"),
        ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"),
    }),
    "always_before": {
        ("Classify Risk", "Capture Master Data"),
        ("Classify Risk", "Run Sanctions Screening"),
        ("Classify Risk", "Verify Bank Account"),
        ("Manager Approval", "Classify Risk"),
        ("Compliance Approval", "Enhanced Due Diligence"),
        ("Activate Vendor", "Classify Risk"),
        ("Release First Payment", "Activate Vendor"),
        ("Sanctions Escalation", "Run Sanctions Screening"),
    },
    "always_after": {
        ("Receive Onboarding Request", "Close Onboarding"),
        ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"),
        ("Release First Payment", "Close Onboarding"),
        ("Reject Vendor", "Close Onboarding"),
        ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"),
        ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"),
    },
    "never_together": symmetric({
        ("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor"),
        ("Reject Vendor", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Reject Vendor", "Compliance Approval"),
        ("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Compliance Approval", "Manager Approval"),
    }),
    "activ_freq": {
        "Receive Onboarding Request": {1},
        "Capture Master Data": {1},
        "Run Sanctions Screening": {1},
        "Classify Risk": {1},
        "Enhanced Due Diligence": {0, 1},
        "Manager Approval": {0, 1},
        "Compliance Approval": {0, 1},
        "Reject Vendor": {0, 1},
        "Activate Vendor": {0, 1},
        "Release First Payment": {0, 1},
        "Close Onboarding": {1},
    },
    "directly_follows": set(),
}
```

The orientation and scope of every pair are as follows:

- **`equivalence (A, B)`** means equal occurrence counts, including zero. Activation and first payment have equal counts; clarification requests and responses have equal counts. Reverse entries express the same equalities.
- **`always_before (A, B)`** uses A as the focal activity: every A requires an earlier B. Thus classification requires each check; manager approval requires classification; compliance approval requires diligence; activation requires classification; payment requires activation; and escalation requires screening. The prerequisite is the **second** element, unlike DECLARE `precedence(A, B)`.
- **`always_after (A, B)`** uses A as the focal activity: every A requires a later B. Receipt requires later closure; activation requires later payment; payment and rejection require later closure; requests require later responses; escalations require later opinions. The successor is the second element.
- **`never_together (A, B)`** excludes both labels from one case in either order. Its five unordered exclusions match the five DECLARE exclusions. Reverse entries add no distinct business rule.
- **`activ_freq`** restricts the listed activities only. `{0, 1}` permits absence, whereas `{1}` requires one occurrence. No finite maximum is asserted for clarification, escalation, opinions, bank changes, suspensions, or repeated verification.
- **`directly_follows`** is empty. Mandatory adjacency is unjustified with concurrent checks and interleaved side activities, including between approval and activation or payment and closure.

The frequency map is intentionally partial, not a discovered complete activity catalogue. Its contract is `count(activity) in allowed_counts` for each listed entry. A PM4Py deployment adapter must preserve this meaning rather than treating omitted activities as forbidden; if necessary, run the explicit partial frequency check separately from the relation checks. Do not impose an arbitrary finite count cap to approximate unbounded loops.

The order constraints do not order the three checks among themselves. `(Classify Risk, Verify Bank Account)` only requires a prior verification for classification; it permits later re-verification. We omit equivalence between verification and a single-occurrence activity, and omit `always_after(Verify Bank Account, Classify Risk)`, because they would conflict with valid post-activation verification.

The skeleton overlaps the procedural and DECLARE layers but cannot express conditional branch selection or full alternate clarification pairing. Two requests followed by two responses can satisfy count equality and later-response requirements while violating R3. No disagreement with the selected policy is intentional; discrepancies require inspection of rule versions, orientation, or adapter semantics.

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"):
        (48 * 3600, 24 * 3600),
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"):
        (12 * 3600, 6 * 3600),
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"):
        (72 * 3600, 36 * 3600),
}
zeta = 2.0
```

For each valid matched pair, compute elapsed seconds and warn exactly when `abs(elapsed_seconds - mean_seconds) > zeta * standard_deviation_seconds`. Equality with a boundary is within tolerance.

| Pair | Mean seconds | Standard deviation seconds | Inclusive non-warning interval |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Request Clarification → Receive Clarification | 172800 | 86400 | 0–96 hours |
| Sanctions Escalation → Obtain Legal Opinion | 43200 | 21600 | 0–24 hours |
| Activate Vendor → Release First Payment | 259200 | 129600 | 0–144 hours |

These expectations produce warnings, not automatic hard failures. A missing required endpoint is handled by a response or backbone rule; no duration is invented. A case with no usable pair is `not evaluated` by this layer. Missing or unreliable timestamps also prevent the affected measurement.

Occurrence matching is explicit:

```text
Clarification:
    Keep the single outstanding request.
    Match its response to it, then clear it.
    If pairing is invalid, report R3 rather than fabricate a duration.

Sanctions:
    Keep every unresolved escalation occurrence.
    On an opinion, match each pending escalation to this first later opinion.
    Clear those pending escalations.

First payment:
    Match the unique activation to the unique later first payment.
    If uniqueness or order is invalid, report the hard constraint and
    do not arbitrarily select a pair for normative timing.

For each valid match:
    Calculate elapsed time and apply the profile with zeta = 2.0.
```

`pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(log, temporal_profile, zeta=2.0)` supplies pair-based diagnostics. Raw eventually-follows pairing can compare an early clarification request with responses from later cycles. Those cross-cycle durations are not the authoritative measurements here. Use the matching monitor above or an adapter that evaluates matched pair instances. This distinction does not change T1–T8 because their relevant occurrences do not form multiple cycles.

The amended R6 deadline is separate: it is hard when the emergency exception is used, and starts at activation. The soft R12 escalation/opinion clock starts at escalation. Passing one does not prove the other.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

Normalize events and external evidence to the following contract:

| Check | Required attributes/evidence | Missing-data treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Case identity and event order | `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, authoritative order, and `event_id` for stable evidence references | Missing case/activity identity prevents affected evaluation. Unresolved order ties make order-sensitive checks indeterminate. |
| Verification versus approval | `org:resource` on every verification and the applicable approval; stable person mapping | Unknown identity makes that comparison `not evaluated`. A known same-person pair still proves failure. |
| Capture versus activation | `org:resource` on both activities and stable person mapping | Same treatment. Missing values are not real people and cannot prove inequality. |
| Strict legal gate and bank-change obligations | Activity identity and trustworthy within-case order | No resource or risk attribute is needed. Unreliable relevant ordering prevents the affected check. |
| Emergency exception | External emergency assertion; case/activation-bound authorization evidence; both authorizers' verified identities and roles; authorization effective time; activation/opinion timestamps | Missing proof gives `not evaluated`; verified denial or invalid evidence gives `fail`. Unproven authorization does not operationally enable early activation. |
| Temporal and emergency clocks | Timezone-aware `time:timestamp`; trusted observation time for running-case deadlines | Do not impute times. Mark the affected check `not evaluated` unless another known fact proves failure. |
| Substantive risk-route audit, if added | Trusted `risk_class`, decision-policy version, and permitted route mapping | Without these, substantive risk-decision correctness is `not evaluated`. This additional audit is outside the R1–R12 test verdicts, which assess observed branch structure. |

The following precise pseudocode runs per case. Retain requirement identifiers and event references. Each comparison has known satisfaction, known failure, or unknown status; missing data never erases an independently established failure.

```text
SEGREGATION OF DUTIES (R9)

approvals := events labelled Manager Approval or Compliance Approval
activations := all Activate Vendor events
verifications := all Verify Bank Account events
captures := all Capture Master Data events

if activations is nonempty:
    if approvals contains exactly one event:
        approval := that event
        for each verification:
            if either person's identity is unavailable:
                record UNKNOWN for this comparison
            else if person(verification) = person(approval):
                record FAIL R9 with both event references
    else:
        record UNKNOWN for approval applicability
        # Missing or duplicate approval is independently a control-flow issue.

    for each capture:
        for each activation:
            if either person's identity is unavailable:
                record UNKNOWN for this comparison
            else if person(capture) = person(activation):
                record FAIL R9 with both event references

# A properly rejected case has no applicable approval comparison.
# All verification occurrences, including later ones, are compared.
```

Retrospectively, the full case supplies the comparison set. Online, compare past verifications when approval occurs, and compare each later verification with the stored approval. A four-eyes filtering call alone is not a complete certificate: branch applicability, both resource rules, repeated verification, and missing identity handling must still be explicit.

```text
LEGAL GATE AND EMERGENCY POLICY (R4/R5/R6)

pending_escalations := empty list
emergency_deadlines := empty list

for each event e in authoritative order:
    if e is Sanctions Escalation:
        append e to pending_escalations

    if e is Activate Vendor and pending_escalations is nonempty:
        if policy_version = STRICT:
            record FAIL R5 at e
            # A later opinion cannot repair a missed activation gate.

        else if policy_version = AMENDED_EMERGENCY:
            evidence := resolve_external_authorization(case_id, e.event_id)
            if necessary evidence is missing:
                record UNKNOWN for emergency authorization
            else:
                require all of:
                    explicit authorization of emergency activation
                    evidence bound to this case and activation
                    authorization by both Legal and Operations
                    externally verified identities and role authority
                    authorization effective no later than activation
                    authorization valid and not revoked at activation
                if a requirement is known false:
                    record FAIL R6 at e
                else if a necessary comparison cannot be established:
                    record UNKNOWN for emergency authorization

            if activation timestamp is known:
                append (e, activation_time + 24 hours) to emergency_deadlines
            else:
                record UNKNOWN for emergency timing

    if e is Obtain Legal Opinion:
        clear pending_escalations
        for each still-open emergency deadline:
            # The opinion is subsequent by event order.
            if required timestamps are missing or inconsistent:
                record UNKNOWN for timing
            else if opinion_time > deadline:
                record FAIL R6 for late opinion
            else:
                record PASS for this deadline
            close that deadline

at case completion:
    if pending_escalations is nonempty:
        record FAIL R4
    for each still-open emergency deadline:
        record FAIL R6 for missing subsequent opinion

for an ongoing case:
    unresolved R4 obligations remain pending
    if trusted current time exceeds an open emergency deadline:
        record FAIL R6 for deadline expiry
    otherwise retain the deadline as pending
```

The R4 portion duplicates DECLARE diagnostically, providing escalation/opinion witnesses. An opinion only clears escalations already encountered, so a previous opinion does not excuse a subsequent escalation. Under the amended policy, timing is assessed even when authorization is unknown: T6 demonstrably meets the ten-hour activation-to-opinion interval, without demonstrating authorization. A known late-opinion failure remains a failure despite missing authorization.

Strict R5 needs no emergency attribute: activation with a pending escalation fails. The amended version requires an external evidence resolver to establish authorization validity. An unverified note or copied boolean does not establish joint authorization.

```text
POST-ACTIVATION BANK CHANGES (R11)

activated := False
bank_dirty := False
suspension_due := False

for each event e in authoritative order:
    if e is Activate Vendor:
        activated := True

    else if e is Change Bank Details and activated:
        bank_dirty := True
        suspension_due := True
        retain e as a pending-change witness

    else if e is Verify Bank Account and activated:
        bank_dirty := False
        # Suspension obligation is unaffected.

    else if e is Suspend Vendor and activated:
        suspension_due := False
        # Verification obligation is unaffected.

    else if e is Release First Payment and activated:
        if bank_dirty:
            record FAIL R11 at this payment
        # Later verification cannot repair the payment's violation.

    if suspension_due:
        record FAIL R11 for an unanswered post-activation change
    # bank_dirty alone is not a failure: verification was conditional
    # on a later first payment, which may never follow the change.
```

A suspension or verification before a change does not satisfy that change. A second change resets both flags. Payment after re-verification can occur while suspension is still pending because R11 makes suspension eventual, rather than a payment prerequisite. If that is unacceptable, add an explicit policy rule. To prove that verification examined the newly changed account, add bank-account/version identifiers; activity order alone cannot establish that substantive fact.

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

**R5/R6 cannot be silently combined.** R5 prohibits exactly the early-activation behavior R6 seeks to permit. Adding a 24-hour obligation does not override the prohibition. The implemented version disables the exception; the alternative amends R5 and requires authorization evidence. Different verdicts under these versions are policy differences, not conformance-engine errors.

**Duplicates need ownership and deduplication.** The net, DECLARE, and skeleton all enforce several outcome and approval exclusions. Response and `always_after` overlap, and R10 also follows from the net. These checks can expose implementation discrepancies, but they are not independent business incidents and must not be used for majority voting. Report one R1 violation with several supporting layer findings. T4 and T6 show why multiple activity-only passes cannot overrule a resource or contextual failure.

**Loops invalidate tempting invariants.** Global verification frequency `{1}` is incompatible with post-activation re-verification. Equating verification count to capture, classification, activation, or approval is also wrong. Requiring classification after every verification would reject later verification. Escalation and opinion counts need not match under the stated many-to-one response interpretation. Clarification count equality depends on the pairing assumption and still cannot establish correct order. Mandatory adjacency is fragile when side events may intervene.

**Conditional obligations must remain conditional.** Unconditional legal-opinion precedence would wrongly reject T1 and T2. Requiring verification after every bank change would overconstrain changes after the only first payment. Treating suspension as permanently disabling payment would add an unstated rule and, without a reinstatement activity, could make a changed-bank route impossible. Suspension and verification discharge different obligations.

**Vacuity is not completion.** A response with no source passes; precedence with no constrained target passes; noncoexistence passes when both labels are absent. These are correct semantics but do not establish a completed onboarding. The backbone and explicit counts supply receipt, classification, route completion, and closure. At-most-once does not imply existence. Likewise, no temporal observations is not evidence of normal timing.

**Missing case and event data remain missing evidence.** Activity sequences cannot establish emergency status, joint authorization, the substantive risk classification, the account version examined, or a resource's natural-person identity. Known violation, unknown assessment, and verified satisfaction must remain distinct. Missing resources cannot prove separation, and missing authorization cannot silently qualify a case for an exception.

**Timestamp-only evidence is limited.** Completion timestamps do not prove start times, effort, causal ordering at equal times, or actual overlap of parallel work. A trace may be a valid interleaving without proving that activities overlapped physically. Logging delay and clock skew can distort temporal and emergency measurements. These profiles use elapsed calendar hours rather than business hours. Repeated activities require explicit matching.

**Support/confidence metadata is not normative truth.** The values of `1.0` specify selected rules here; they do not assert empirical support from an observed sample. Mined statistics may reflect missing data, selection bias, or vacuity. A threshold cannot resolve R5/R6, grant authorization, or turn a statistical regularity into a legal obligation. Policy owners determine hard constraints and warning severity.

**The eight examples leave coverage gaps.** They do not exercise bank changes, multiple clarification cycles, repeated escalation, missing resources, or tied timestamps. Additional distinguishing witnesses, each embedded in an otherwise valid complete backbone, should include:

- `Request Clarification`, `Request Clarification`, `Receive Clarification`, `Receive Clarification`: fails R3 despite balanced counts.
- `Activate Vendor`, `Change Bank Details`, `Release First Payment`, `Verify Bank Account`, `Suspend Vendor`: fails R11 at payment.
- `Activate Vendor`, `Change Bank Details`, `Verify Bank Account`, `Release First Payment`, `Suspend Vendor`: can satisfy R11.
- `Activate Vendor`, `Release First Payment`, `Change Bank Details`, `Suspend Vendor`: can satisfy R11 without further verification.

These examples distinguish the intended policy from accidental frequency, adjacency, timing-matching, or suspension overconstraints.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

Keep the accepting Petri net with its initial and final markings as the canonical procedural representation. It directly represents concurrency, exclusive routes, repeated visible verification, and lifecycle-scoped side activities. Check whole-trace accepting conformance rather than projecting away exceptional events and presenting backbone fitness as full compliance.

Use a versioned policy registry as the authoritative source of interpretation. Record each requirement's owning implementation, the selected R5/R6 resolution, pairing/completion assumptions, and generated dictionaries and monitors. The full hard specification is their conjunction. Temporal warnings accompany the decision without removing otherwise compliant traces from the control-flow language.

Run checks in this order:

1. Select the policy version and validate case boundaries, activity identities, event order, timestamps, and required evidence. Preserve missing-data findings.
2. Check accepting-net conformance for synchronization, routes, lifecycle, and closure.
3. Run DECLARE and Log Skeleton checks with explicit pair orientations and partial frequency semantics.
4. Run the resource, legal-gate/emergency, and bank-change monitors on original events and external evidence. Continue even if another layer has failed.
5. Match valid temporal occurrences and calculate warnings. Do not invent durations for missing endpoints or use ambiguous cross-cycle matches as normative evidence.
6. Aggregate by requirement: any known hard failure yields `fail`; required unknown evidence prevents certified passage; otherwise a temporal deviation yields `warning`, and all applicable hard checks passing without warnings yields `pass`.

Before deployment, verify the PM4Py adapters against this specification, particularly DECLARE precedence direction, Log Skeleton focal-pair orientation and omitted frequencies, and repeated-occurrence temporal matching. T1–T8 and the distinguishing loop examples provide acceptance criteria.

Show the overall verdict, policy version, earliest decisive event, requirement identifier, and supporting evidence, with later violations and temporal warnings separately available. Surface contradictory findings with their scopes rather than averaging scores. A resource-only failure, deliberate procedural permissiveness, missing authorization, and a contradictory policy definition require different explanations.
