# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

**Principle:** core = high frequency *+* consistent complexity *+* formal certification/authority *+* stable across weeks. Peripheral = infrequent, or frequent only during substitution windows, or low-complexity slices of an activity, or work that requires later recheck.

**R-AUTO:** Core A1/A2 are entirely low-complexity prechecks (median 0.11). No peripheral work. Evidence: zero events beyond A2. Authority: no discretion — Fragment 1 always hands to human for A4/Close. Do not count its 1370 events as capacity equivalent to humans.

**R-FRONT (Amira/Ben):** Core A2/A4/A12 are routine, high-volume, low-medium complexity. Frequency proves specialization. Peripheral A3/A8/A7 are low-complexity triage or data gathering before handover to Technical/Commercial, not independent resolution. Amira’s peripheral is more substantive (42x A8, 18x A7, median 0.34) than Ben’s (25x A8, 5x A7, median 0.29), indicating senior-generalist vs pure routine. Substitution test: neither covers A6/A9/A11.

**R-TEMP (Kai/Lina):** Core is a *subset* of R-FRONT core (A1/A2/A4/A12) restricted to low complexity (median ~0.18). No genuine peripheral — 8-12x A3 and 3-4x A8 are likely miscodes or assisted tasks. Evidence: 0 critical cases, 0 A6/A7/A9/A11/A14, and 17 reopens from incomplete Validate notes. Their Close (190/180) looks productive by count but is fragile.

**R-TECH (Chen/Dina):** Core A3/A5/A10 are high frequency *and* high complexity. This is true specialization. Peripheral A6/A11/A14 differentiate seniority: Chen 24x A6 vs Dina 6x A6; Chen 42 critical vs 9; Chen median 0.71 vs 0.48. Dina’s core is same activities but lower-complexity slice — similar mix, different risk. Both do occasional A2/A4/A12, but only as rework/diagnostics context, not front-office duty.

**R-COMM (Fatima):** Core A7/A8 are medium-high complexity with pricing cert. Frequency (122/90) aligns with authority. Peripheral A9 (16x) is *not* core approval — Fragment 4 shows Hana approves after negotiation. Fatima’s A9 is likely recommendation/attendance. Occasional A2/A12 are case context. Substitution evidence is key: when Fatima absent, duration doubled (3874 min), proving core requires tacit pricing knowledge that frequency alone hides.

**R-HYBRID-SENIOR (Giorgio):** Has two cores by context: routine front-office core (A2/A4/A12) + shadow commercial core (A7/A8). The latter is peripheral by formal job (Senior Service Agent, Front Office) but core by capability (pricing cert, 7.4y) and by criticality (120 high, 36 critical, median 0.58 — far above Ben/Kai). # 3. Shadow and bridging roles

Shadow/bridging roles are not misbehavior; they are informal resilience that carries both benefit (continuity, speed, load balancing) and risk (slower quality, hidden single points of failure, authority ambiguity). They must be named, not forced into formal clusters.

**1. R07_Giorgio as commercial shadow:**
Formal title Senior Service Agent, Front Office. Informally holds pricing cert + 7.4y tenure and performs 72x A7 and 88x A8 — ~60% of Fatima’s pricing volume — plus 20x A9 attendance. Evidence: similarity 0.71 to Fatima (vs 0.84 to Amira) shows hybrid; Fragment 4 allows Giorgio *or* Fatima to negotiate; Weeks 3-4 substitution sustained flow during 6-day absence.
Benefit: prevented pricing queue collapse; knows front-office context for complex cases (median 0.58).
Risk: median pricing duration 3874 min, indicating lower efficiency/possibly higher concession risk; if management codes Giorgio as “second Fatima,” they hide pricing single-point-of-failure and under-invest in formal backup/training. Needs paired review, not autonomous pricing authority.

**2. R13_Mateo as cross-functional escalation broker:**
Incident Coordinator who appears in all fragments (3,5) as Coordinate step: Diagnose  Mateo  Implement; Reopen  Mateo  specialist  Hana. Handover-of-work centrality highest; working-together ties to Technical, Security, Commercial, Management. Performs 16x A6, 10x A7, 44x A9, 42x A13 — not as specialist but as triage/router.
Benefit: Weeks 7-8 filtering of low-risk security referrals reduced false escalations and protected Erik for true criticals; Week 5 coverage of Hana prevented approval stall.
Risk: 13/21 preliminary approvals rechecked; 2 critical security waits >2 days when filtering too aggressive. Brokerage looks like approval/security/commercial work by count but lacks final authority and deep expertise. Formalizing Mateo as approver or security gate without certification would transfer risk without competence.

**3. R05_Erik as rare but critical specialist:**
Security Specialist with only 170 total events vs 800-1300 for front-office/technical, but median 0.86 and 55 critical cases. A6 (96x) gates Enterprise Security path.
Benefit: deep expertise for high-impact tail; cross-functionalFragments show he is invoked selectively after Diagnose.
Risk of naïve view: low count = dispensable or part-time. In fact, absence creates tail risk and bottleneck; overloading him with routine referrals (pre-filter failure) wastes capacity. Needs protected capacity + explicit escalation criteria, not utilization maximization. His occasional A3/A5/A10 should not be read as Technical membership — they are security-scoped implementation.

**Additional bridging/boundary roles:**

*Automation boundary (R14_Nora):* Bot does 760x A1 + 610x A2 at median 0.11. Benefit: absorbs seasonal load, standardizes precheck. Risk: incomplete automation boundary — temps + R01/R02 re-validate; if bot validation trusted blindly, downstream rework/reopen rises. Role is infrastructure, not staff.

*Temporary-workforce buffer (R11_Kai/R12_Lina):* Benefit: absorb simple Register-Validate-Respond-Close volume (Fragment 1). Risk: 0 critical experience + 17 reopens from Validate notes show training debt. High Close counts mask quality cost shifted to R01/R02/R13 and Quality.

*Quality gatekeeping (R09_Ivan/R10_Julia):* Bridge between Technical/Implement and Close via A11 + A14. Benefit: late/day coverage, KB codification (96/104 updates) builds organizational memory. Risk: if pressured to “help” Implement load because they do 35x A10, gate independence erodes.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# --- 1. load / format dataframe (PM4Py structure) ---
df = pd.read_csv("order_to_resolution_10w.csv", parse_dates=["time:timestamp"])
# expected cols: case:concept:name, concept:name, time:timestamp, org:resource,
#                case_complexity, customer_segment, product_line,
#                temporary_substitution, escalation_level

df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    timest_format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"
)
log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)  # or keep dataframe API

# --- 2. naïve organizational roles (activity-frequency clustering) ---
# uses activity-profile vectors; DO NOT interpret directly as formal roles
roles_naive = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(
    df,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
    # e.g. hierarchical / k-means under the hood; tune eps/min_samples
    # to show instability:
)
print(roles_naive)
# Expected pitfall demo: Chen/Dina together, Amira/Ben/Kai/Lina together,
# Giorgio with Front, Erik as noise, Mateo with Hana or as noise.

# --- 3. activity-based resource similarity (cosine over normalized counts) ---
sim_matrix = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
    df,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource"
)
# inspect selected pairs from scenario table
for a,b in [("R03_Chen","R04_Dina"),("R01_Amira","R02_Ben"),
            ("R06_Fatima","R07_Giorgio"),("R08_Hana","R13_Mateo")]:
    print(a,b, sim_matrix.loc[a,b])

# --- 4. social networks: handover, working-together, subcontracting ---
how_net = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(df)       # Fragment 1-5 flow
wt_net  = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(df)       # co-presence per case
sub_net = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(df)         # A -> B -> A patterns

# e.g. is Mateo a broker? high betweenness in how_net but low self-loop closure
# e.g. does Dina subcontract high-complexity to Chen?

# --- 5. join role output with complexity + substitution + authority ---
# aggregate per resource-activity-case
feat = df.groupby(["org:resource","concept:name"]).agg(
    n_events=("concept:name","size"),
    median_complexity=("case_complexity","median"),
    share_critical=("case_complexity", lambda s: (s=="critical").mean()),
    share_substitution=("temporary_substitution","mean"),
    median_service_min=("@@service_time", "median")  # after get_service_time
)

# service / waiting time to test shadow competence (Fatima vs Giorgio on A7)
df["@@service_time"] = pm4py.get_service_time(df)  # or service-time calc
df["@@wait"] = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df)  # waiting before activity
print(df.query("`concept:name`=='Pricing Exception Review'")  # A7
        .groupby("org:resource")["@@service_time"].median())

# case-level complexity by resource (Table 2 reproduction)
case_res = df.drop_duplicates(["case:concept:name","org:resource"]).merge(
    df[["case:concept:name","case_complexity"]].drop_duplicates(),
    on="case:concept:name"
)
print(pd.crosstab(case_res["org:resource"], case_res["case_complexity"]))

# isolate substitution windows (do NOT treat as permanent competence)
subst = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(df, "temporary_substitution", [True, 1, "True"])
print(subst.groupby(["org:resource","concept:name"]).size())
# weeks 3-4 Giorgio A7/A8; week 5 Mateo A9; weeks 7-8 Erik/Mateo security triage

variants = pm4py.get_variants(df)
# check Fragment 4: Validate -> Pricing -> Negotiate -> Approval resource sequence
# check Fragment 6: temp Close -> Reopen -> rework

# --- 6. guardrail: report frequency cluster AND complexity/authority gap ---
# For any pair with similarity >0.85, require:
#  |median_complexity diff| + |critical share diff| + recheck rate + cert check
# before merging. Example assertion:
# Chen-Dina sim 0.94 BUT median 0.71 vs 0.48 -> keep stratified, not merged.
# Mateo-Hana A9 overlap BUT Mateo recheck 13/21 -> broker, not approver.
```

Interpretation rule coded above: never promote a high-similarity or high-frequency edge to formal authority without `case_complexity`, `escalation_level`, `temporary_substitution`, `product_line`, and handover recheck evidence.

# 6. Operational interpretation

**How to use the structure:**

*Staffing:* Protect scarce gates (Erik security, Hana approval, Fatima pricing) with explicit backup, not headcount by volume. Keep bot + temps for low-complexity absorption, but cap temp autonomy to Validate/Standard Response with checklist and mandatory senior review before Close. Roster Chen on late shift for complex diagnostics; pair Dina with Chen for mentoring, not independent critical ownership. Formalize Giorgio as designated commercial backup with paired sign-off, and Mateo as triage coordinator with clear escalation thresholds (e.g., auto-escalate Enterprise Security high/critical to Erik, all A9 to Hana).

*Training:* Temps need Validate Entitlement note quality (root of 17 reopens); Technical need security-adjacent training to reduce false referrals; Giorgio needs pricing refresher to close 3874 min gap; Mateo needs triage criteria to avoid >2-day critical waits; Quality KB updates (Ivan/Julia ~200 total) should be fed back into front-office scripts to prevent repeat reopens.

*Risk control:* Make authority explicit in system: only Hana can finally close escalation_level=high/critical A9; Mateo’s A9 = “preliminary”; Erik must see unfiltered critical security queue even when Mateo filters low-risk. Monitor service time per resource-activity (A7 by Fatima vs Giorgio) and recheck rate (MateoHana) as early warnings. Track reopen rate by closer (temp vs permanent) and Validate completeness.

*Resilience:* Cross-train Amira/Giorgio/Mateo as bridges, rotate Dina through high-complexity shadowing, document pricing/security playbooks so absence (Fatima 6 days, Hana workshop, Enterprise Security surge) does not rely on tacit shadow knowledge alone. Maintain Quality gate independence even under load — do not reassign Ivan/Julia to Implement throughput.

**Two (actually three) cautions against staffing only on mined roles:**

1. **Do not replace Fatima with Giorgio or Hana with Mateo because substitution frequency looks sufficient.** Substitution volumes (31x A7, 21x A9) were temporary and lower quality (74 min vs 38 min; 62% rechecked). Permanent replacement would institutionalize slower pricing and pseudo-approvals without accountability, increasing commercial and compliance risk.

2. **Do not merge Chen/Dina or Amira/Ben or Front/Temp based on >0.9 similarity, and do not downsize Erik for low volume.** Similar profiles hide complexity/risk stratification (Chen 42 critical vs Dina 9; Amira 58 high vs Ben 31; temps 0 critical). Cutting “duplicate” headcount or Erik’s “low utilization” would remove the only cover for high/critical tails and create bottleneck/failure demand (reopens, waits, false escalations).

3. **Do not reward high Close counts (temps 180-190, Ben 280) as productivity without reopen/waiting-time adjustment.** Frequency clusters reward closure speed while externalizing cost to rework (Fragment 6), Quality, and coordinators. Staffing incentives must weight complete, first-time-right resolution by complexity, not raw event counts.
