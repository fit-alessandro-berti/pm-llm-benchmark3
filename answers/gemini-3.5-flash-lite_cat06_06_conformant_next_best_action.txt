## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

The decision space exhibits severe asymmetry:
1. **Operational/Deadline Loss (€20,000–€35,000):** Linear monetary loss resulting from SLA breach or missed execution value.
2. **Customer Harm (€15,000–€80,000):** Arises from false rejections, unnecessary holds, or delayed liquidity to an established supplier in a sanctioned-border region.
3. **Regulatory / Compliance Harm (€100,000–€500,000+ plus non-monetary consequences):** Arises from unlawful execution (breaching Rules 6 and 9). This includes statutory fines, potential facilitation of sanctions evasion, loss of banking license, and personal liability for officers.

**Dominance Argument:** Regulatory and compliance harm utterly dominates operational and customer harm. An expected regulatory penalty of €200,000 with a 10% probability yields an expected risk value of €20,000, which completely eclipses the entire operational gain of executing P-884 (€35,000). Therefore, **any action that bypasses mandatory controls (Rules 6 or 9) is strictly dominated**, regardless of how favorable the numerical deadline forecast appears.

* **Rule 7 (Four-Eyes Principle):** Analyst R17 performed the first review. Therefore, R17 is strictly barred from performing the second review. **Action B is nonconformant.**
* **Rule 9 (Execution Path):** Payment Execution is conformant *only* after a valid approval path. Executing without Second Officer Review violates Rule 9. **Action A is nonconformant.**
* **Why Actions A and B cannot be justified by numerical forecasts:** Predictive process monitoring outputs (e.g., SLA breach probability 0.48–0.64) describe operational risk, not legal or regulatory risk. Conformance checking is a hard-constraint verification layer; predictive utility optimization cannot override explicit regulatory mandates (Goodhart's Law / compliance override fallacy).
* **Conditions for Action F (Rejection):** Requires documented evidence satisfying the institution's threshold for risk-based refusal under anti-money laundering (AML) policies. Absent this, rejection is arbitrary and legally vulnerable.
* **Conditions for Action G (Emergency Override):** Requires *both* (1) authorization from a Compliance Director *and* (2) a valid, recorded incident identifier. Since neither is currently satisfied, Action G is an unenabled exception path.
