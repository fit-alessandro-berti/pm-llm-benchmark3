## 1. Interaction-graph description

The system is best described as a typed, qualified interaction graph rather than six independent job traces.

Important node types: Job (J1–J6), Component (C1–C6), ComponentLot (L7, L8), Machine (CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1), Tool (FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3), Batch (B1, B2), Shipment (S1–S3).

Important edge types and qualifiers:
- `requires` (Job→Component): bill-of-material edge; directly declared in O2O.
- `contains` (ComponentLot→Component): quality coupling; a lot event propagates along it to several jobs at once. Directly declared.
- `batches` (Batch→Job): synchronization barrier; the batch cannot start before its latest member. Directly declared.
- `consolidates` (Shipment→Job): downstream barrier; dispatch waits for the latest packed member. Directly declared.
- `uses` (Job/Batch→Machine or Tool): derived from shared events (the 12:05 `Batch Cure` relates B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1–J3, C1–C3; J4's `Machine Process` relates J4, C4, CNC-2, FIX-A). Direct observations.
- `blocks` (Tool→Job, Lot→Job): inferred. There is no `Wait for Tool` activity, so FIX-A blocking J4 from 09:50 to 11:45 is reconstructed from acquire/release timestamps and CNC-2 idleness. RACK-7 blocking B2 is inferred from its "cleaning/quarantine" state, and L7's effect on J2/J5 is only partially linked because the extraction dropped lot events from the Job view.

Compact edge list (D = directly observed, I = inferred):

```
J1 -requires-> C1 (D)      L8 -contains-> C1,C3 (D)
J2 -requires-> C2 (D)      L7 -contains-> C2,C5 (D)
J3 -requires-> C3 (D)      B1 -batches-> J1,J2,J3 (D)
J4 -requires-> C4 (D)      B2 -batches-> J4,J5 (D)
J5 -requires-> C5 (D)      S1 -consolidates-> J1,J4 (D)
J6 -requires-> C6 (D)      S2 -consolidates-> J2,J3 (D)
B1,B2 -cured_on-> OVEN-1 (D)   S3 -consolidates-> J5,J6 (D)
B1,B2 -use-> RACK-7 (D)        J3,J4 -use-> FIX-A (D)
J4 -machined_on-> CNC-2 (D)    J5,J6 -tested_on-> TEST-1 (D)
FinalTest -requires-> CAL-3 (D)
FIX-A -blocks-> J4 (I)         RACK-7 -blocks-> B2/J4 (I)
CAL-3 -blocks-> J6 (I)         L7 -blocks-> J5 (D), L7 -/-> J2 (override, D)
J4-lateness -delays-> S1 -delays-> J1 (I, composition of D edges)
```

Three higher-order motifs (more than two object types):
1. Batch–Tool–Machine–Job barrier: {J1,J2,J3} → B1 → (OVEN-1 ∧ RACK-7). The batch needs a machine and a shared rack in conjunction; B2 shows the rack, not the oven, can bind. Any member's lateness or the rack's unavailability delays all members.
2. Lot–Component–Job–Batch quality fan-out: L7 → {C2,C5} → {J2,J5} → {B1,B2}. One quarantine event fans out to two jobs in two different batches, coupling B1 and B2 through a quality object neither batch "contains."
3. Job–Tool–Machine–Shipment cascade: J3 holds FIX-A → J4's machining on CNC-2 slips → J4 packs late → S1 holds → J1 dispatches late. J3 and J1 share no machine, batch, lot, or shipment, yet J3's tool hold plausibly delays J1's dispatch.

A fourth: tool-as-serializer at TEST-1 — every `Final Test` needs CAL-3, so CAL-3's recalibration cycle, not TEST-1 capacity, sets test throughput.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

All figures are counterfactual gaps, not causal proof; each depends on the stated assumption.

1. B1 synchronization, J1: ready 08:10, `Batch Cure` starts 12:05 → 3 h 55 min waiting. Counterfactual: J1 could have cured individually or earlier, and readiness timestamps are real. If oven economics require full batches, part of this is a deliberate trade-off, not waste.
2. B1 synchronization, J2: ready 08:25, start 12:05 → 3 h 40 min. Same counterfactual, with a caveat: C2 belongs to L7, quarantined 10:20–13:50. If the quarantine should have applied, J2's legitimate ready time was 13:50 and J2 was not waiting at all — it was processed early via supervisor override. (J3: 11:55→12:05 = 10 min; J3 sets the batch start.)
3. FIX-A contention, J4: J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50; `Machine Process` starts 11:45 → 1 h 55 min queue. FIX-A is released by J3 at 11:40 and J4 starts 5 min later, so up to 1 h 50 min is attributable to fixture unavailability. Counterfactual: a second FIX-A (or resequencing J3) and no other constraint (operator, program) binds. Supporting, not proving: CNC-2 idles 10:05–10:47 (42 min) while J4 waits, so machine capacity was not the constraint.
4. RACK-7 for B2/J4: J4 ready 13:10, B2 starts 14:50 → 1 h 40 min. OVEN-1 idle from 12:55, but RACK-7 in cleaning/quarantine until 14:35. Decomposition: 13:10–14:35 (1 h 25 min) to RACK-7; 5 min to J5's readiness (14:40); ~10 min loading. Counterfactual: a second rack, or curing J4 without J5.
5. S1 consolidation, J1: packed 13:30, S1 dispatches 19:25 → 5 h 55 min. Against the job-DFG Pack→Dispatch median of 1.1 h, J1 could have left ~14:36, so ~4 h 49 min is consolidation-induced. Proximate cause: J4's 19:10 pack (S1 leaves 15 min later, consistent with S2's 25 min and S3's 35 min windows). Counterfactual: J1 ships alone or re-paired; extra transport cost ignored.
6. CAL-3 for J6: ready 16:05, test starts 17:25 → 1 h 20 min. Of this, 30 min is TEST-1 legitimately busy with J5 (16:10–16:35); 50 min (16:35–17:25) is CAL-3 recalibration plus 5 min, while TEST-1 idles. Counterfactual: between-test recalibration is skippable, or a second calibrated unit exists → J6 saves ~50 min.
7. L7 quarantine, J5: 10:20 to 13:50 = 3 h 30 min lot hold. J5 is batch-ready 14:40, 50 min after release, consistent with the hold binding. Counterfactual: earlier lot disposition; assumes no other blocker.

Cascade cross-check: J4's ~1 h 50 min (FIX-A) plus ~1 h 25 min (RACK-7) roughly explains why a job with normal processing times packs at 19:10, which generates most of J1's 5 h 55 min shipment wait. Delays compound across object types without any machine being busy.

Ranking rationale: batch synchronization produced the largest directly measured, mechanism-attributed waits (up to 3 h 55 min, with an explicit shared event). Tool contention ranks second: three independent episodes with the tell-tale idle-machine signature, though every instance is inferred. Shipment consolidation produced the single biggest number (5 h 55 min) but ranks third because it mostly amplifies upstream lateness. Ranking tools first is defensible if one weights root causes over proximate waits, since FIX-A and RACK-7 caused the J4 lateness that drove S1's hold.

## 4. What flattening misses

Job — Visible: per-job control flow, rework loops, edge medians. Hidden: inter-job coupling; lot events (dropped by the extraction); which resource a gap was spent waiting for. Misleading conclusion: "no job exceeds 1.8 SD of the mean, so nothing is wrong" — with n=6 and every job inflated by shared waits, the mean absorbs the systemic delay; the 1.1 h Pack→Dispatch median conceals J1's 5 h 55 min.

Machine — Visible: utilization, activity sequences, event gaps. Hidden: jobs queueing while the machine idles for a tool, batch partner, or lot release. Misleading conclusion: "OVEN-1 at 44% with a 73-min max gap has spare capacity, so no bottleneck" — while B1 members waited up to 3 h 55 min and OVEN-1 idled 12:55–14:50 for RACK-7.

Batch — Visible: Load→Cure→Unload cycle, sizes, cure durations. Hidden: member readiness dispersion (08:10 vs 11:55), i.e., who paid for synchronization. Misleading conclusion: "batches run promptly once loaded, so batching is efficient."

Tool — Visible: acquire/release chains (FIX-A held 09:00–11:40, CAL-3 recalibration, RACK-7 cleaning). Hidden: which jobs waited (no `Wait for Tool` events), so demand is invisible. Misleading conclusion: "tools are used serially without conflict" because the queue is unlogged.

ComponentLot — Visible: Quarantine 10:20 → Release 13:50 on L7. Hidden: downstream impact on J2/J5/B1/B2 given inconsistent Job linkage; the J2 override in a separate system. Misleading conclusion: "one 3.5 h quarantine, contained" when it straddled two batches and provoked a policy violation.

Shipment — Visible: Create→Load→Dispatch, 0.8 h + 0.3 h medians. Hidden: by construction, the interval between a job's Pack and its shipment's Create — J1's 5 h 55 min is unrepresentable. Misleading conclusion: "dispatch takes ~1.1 h, logistics is fine."

Why low utilization coexists with long waiting: jobs queue for a conjunction of resources (machine ∧ tool ∧ batch partners ∧ lot clearance ∧ shipment partners), and utilization measures one conjunct. When the binding constraint is FIX-A, RACK-7's state, CAL-3's recalibration, or a not-yet-ready partner, the machine idles while the job waits — waiting accumulates in exactly the periods that make utilization look comfortable. Synchronization converts one object's lateness into many objects' waiting without consuming machine time.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

Narrative A — J3 → FIX-A → CNC-2 → J4 → B2/OVEN-1 → S1 → J1 (Job, Tool, Machine, Batch, Shipment). Temporal precedence: J3 holds FIX-A 09:00–11:40; J4 arrives 09:50; CNC-2 idle 10:05–10:47; J4 processes from 11:45; batch-ready 13:10; B2 cures 14:50; J4 packs 19:10; S1 dispatches 19:25; J1 packed since 13:30. Resource feasibility: CNC-2 was free but FIX-A was not; later OVEN-1 was free but RACK-7 was not — twice the tool, not the machine, was infeasible. Policy constraints: pairing J1 with J4 in S1 is a consolidation policy; dispatch 15 min after last pack matches S2/S3. Causal speculation: that J4 would have started ~09:50 with a free fixture, and J1 would have shipped ~5 h earlier if unpaired — plausible, unproven (operators, truck schedules unobserved).

Narrative B — L7 → C2/C5 → J2/J5 → B1/B2 → S2/S3 (ComponentLot, Component, Job, Batch, Shipment). Temporal precedence: quarantine 10:20; B1 cures 12:05 with J2 inside; release 13:50; J5 ready 14:40; B2 cures 14:50; J5 tests 16:10. Resource feasibility: nothing physical blocked J2 — the block was informational; J5 respected it and lost ~3.5 h. Policy constraints: the hold was enforced for J5 and overridden for J2 (recorded only in a separate system) — identical lot membership, opposite outcomes. Causal speculation: J5's late test slot pushed the J5→CAL-3→J6 sequence into the recalibration window — speculative, since the recalibration trigger is unknown. Risk note: if the quarantine was substantive, B1's output containing C2 carries latent quality risk into S2.

Narrative C — J5 → TEST-1/CAL-3 → J6 → S3 (Job, Machine, Tool, Shipment). Temporal precedence: J5/J6 ready 16:00/16:05; J5 tests 16:10–16:35; CAL-3 recalibrates 16:35–17:20; J6 tests 17:25–17:50; S3 leaves 35 min after its last pack. Resource feasibility: TEST-1 idle 16:35–17:25 — machine feasible, calibration infeasible; CAL-3 serializes an apparently parallel-capable station. Policy constraints: "every Final Test requires CAL-3" is a certification rule; between-job recalibration may itself be mandated. Causal speculation: J6's ~50 min is attributable to CAL-3 only if recalibration was skippable or duplicable; if mandated, it is structural cost, not waste. Since J6 plausibly packs last in S3, the 50 min propagates into S3's dispatch and hence into J5's lead time — a feedback loop through the shipment object.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

1. Ready-window batching on OVEN-1 (reduces synchronization). Target: batch synchronization. Rule: start a cure once accumulated member waiting exceeds a threshold, or admit only jobs ready within a 60–90 min window. Benefit: J1/J2-type waits shrink to the window size. Helped: early-ready jobs (J1, J2). Harmed: late jobs (J3, J5) lose their free ride; energy per unit rises with smaller batches; higher OVEN-1 utilization could later create a real oven bottleneck. Perverse metric: "oven fill rate %" — maximizing it recreates wait-for-slowest. Evaluation: distribution of (batch-start − member-ready) per Job–Batch pair, before/after, plus energy per cured unit.

2. Duplicate or de-conflict FIX-A; schedule RACK-7 cleaning off-peak (addresses tools). Target: shared-tool contention. Benefit: J4 recovers up to ~1 h 50 min (FIX-A) + ~1 h 25 min (RACK-7), propagating ~5 h to S1/J1. Helped: J4, J1, B2. Harmed: capital cost; shifted cleaning may squeeze maintenance; a second fixture can mask a schedulable sequencing problem (J3 before J4 was a choice). Perverse metric: "tool utilization" — a duplicated, half-idle fixture looks wasteful and invites removal. Evaluation: acquire-latency (request→acquire) per Job–Tool pair — requires intervention 5 — plus CNC-2 idle-while-job-waiting time.

3. Overlapped calibration for CAL-3. Target: tool-as-serializer at TEST-1. A second calibration unit, or recalibrate during the next job's load. Benefit: ~50 min for J6 and every back-to-back test pair. Harmed: calibration-lab workload; risk of testing against a drifted standard — costlier than 50 min. Perverse metric: "tests per shift" pressures staff to stretch recalibration intervals. Evaluation: TEST-1 idle intervals overlapping CAL-3 maintenance events, per test pair.

4. Dynamic shipment re-pairing with a wait cap (addresses shipment policy). Target: consolidation holds. Rule: if a packed job's partner is projected >2 h late, re-assign it to the next compatible shipment or ship solo. Benefit: J1 ships ~14:30 instead of 19:25. Helped: early finishers. Harmed: freight cost rises; the late partner (J4) sees no gain and may lose a consolidation discount. Perverse metric: "trucks dispatched full %" punishes the re-pairing that protects lead time. Evaluation: (dispatch − pack) per Job–Shipment pair jointly with cost per shipment.

5. Logging upgrade (improves logging, not operations). Target: data-integration artifacts. Add explicit `Request Tool`/`Wait for Tool` events; tool state changes (RACK-7 cleaning start/end) as first-class events; consistent E2O linkage of lot events to Jobs (the gap that hid Quarantine/Release from J2/J5's traces); imported supervisor overrides; `Ready for Batch`/`Ready for Test` milestones. Benefit: converts tool and lot inference into measurement; enables every evaluation above. Harmed: nobody operationally, though exposed waiting may embarrass tool/batch owners. Perverse metric: "delay events logged per team" invites under-reporting. Evaluation: fraction of Job–Tool interactions with explicit request events; equal lot-event linkage counts across pipelines.

6. Lot-disposition fast lane and override governance. Target: component-lot coupling. Prioritize disposition of lots feeding imminent batches (L7's hold straddled both); make overrides like J2's a logged, rule-bound event with automatic flagging of downstream B1/S2. Benefit: J5-type holds shrink; traceability improves. Harmed: quality lab loses triage autonomy; a fast lane can starve routine lots. Perverse metric: "average disposition time" invites rubber-stamped releases. Evaluation: join of lot events to job readiness times; count of overrides and their downstream shipment exposure.

## 7. PM4Py analysis outline

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd

# ---- 1. Object-centric structure: who interacts with whom, and how often ----
inter = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)   # pairwise object interactions via shared events
obj_graph = pm4py.discover_objects_graph(ocel, graph_type="object_interaction")
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)          # object-type graph: (type, type) -> co-occurrence frequency
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)        # event-type -> object-type involvement (finds Batch Cure touching 9 objects)

# ---- 2. Object-centric DFG with TWO frequency metrics compared ----
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
ev_couples = ocdfg["edges"]["event_couples"]     # metric A: frequency as #event pairs
uniq_objects = ocdfg["edges"]["unique_objects"]  # metric B: frequency as #distinct objects
rows = []
for ot in ev_couples:
    for edge in ev_couples[ot]:
        rows.append({"otype": ot, "edge": edge,
                     "event_couples": len(ev_couples[ot][edge]),
                     "unique_objects": len(uniq_objects.get(ot, {}).get(edge, []))})
edge_cmp = pd.DataFrame(rows)
# Divergence flags convergence/divergence: e.g. one Batch Cure event but 3 Jobs on the same edge
edge_cmp["fanout"] = edge_cmp["unique_objects"] / edge_cmp["event_couples"].clip(lower=1)
print(edge_cmp.sort_values("fanout", ascending=False).head(15))

# ---- 3. Flatten per object type and contrast performance DFGs ----
perf = {}
for ot in ["Job", "Machine", "Batch", "Tool", "ComponentLot", "Shipment"]:
    flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, ot)
    dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(flat)
    perf[ot] = dfg
# The SAME physical interval shows up differently per view: compare, e.g.,
# Job view 'Stage Component -> Batch Cure' vs Batch view 'Load Batch -> Batch Cure'.

# ---- 4. Waiting must come from job-side readiness, NOT machine-event gaps ----
# Join each Job's readiness milestone to the shared batch/test event via E2O relations.
rel = ocel.relations  # columns: ocel:eid, ocel:activity, ocel:timestamp, ocel:oid, ocel:type
job_ev = rel[rel["ocel:type"] == "Job"]
ready = (job_ev[job_ev["ocel:activity"] == "Ready for Batch"]
         .groupby("ocel:oid")["ocel:timestamp"].min().rename("ready_at"))
cure = (job_ev[job_ev["ocel:activity"] == "Batch Cure"]
        .groupby("ocel:oid")["ocel:timestamp"].min().rename("cure_at"))
sync_wait = pd.concat([ready, cure], axis=1).dropna()
sync_wait["batch_sync_wait_h"] = (sync_wait["cure_at"] - sync_wait["ready_at"]).dt.total_seconds()/3600
print(sync_wait)   # expect ~3.9h (J1), ~3.7h (J2), ~0.17h (J3)
# Analogous joins: Pack -> shipment Dispatch (consolidation wait);
# tool Release (FIX-A by J3) -> next Machine Process using same tool (contention gap).

# ---- 5. Per-job features to correlate coupling with lead time ----
feat_data, feat_names = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel, "Job")
features = pd.DataFrame(feat_data, columns=feat_names)
# e.g. correlate related-object counts (Batch, Tool, Lot degree) with total lead time.

# ---- 6. Optional: networkx for the cross-instance cascade paths ----
G = pm4py.convert_ocel_to_networkx(ocel)
# Search paths J3 -> FIX-A -> J4 -> B2 -> S1 -> J1 to make the coupling explicit.
```

## 8. Falsification and additional data

Observations that would weaken the top-ranked explanation (batch synchronization):
1. "Ready for batch" timestamps record staging paperwork, not physical readiness — J1's 08:10 is fictitious and the 3 h 55 min vanishes.
2. OVEN-1 requires a minimum thermal load of three jobs, or a fixed 12:05 energy-tariff window — the start was forced, not synchronization waste.
3. J1/J2 underwent other recorded work between 08:10 and 12:05 — they were not idle.
4. RACK-7 was also unavailable before 12:05 — the B1 wait re-attributes to tools.
5. Over a longer horizon, member readiness within batches is tightly clustered and B1 is a one-off — low recurrence kills rank 1.
6. Curing jobs individually is technically impossible (cure recipe, fixture geometry), voiding the counterfactual.
7. Per-cure energy cost dwarfs waiting cost — batching is optimal despite the waits.

Additional fields/event types to discriminate among hypotheses:
1. Explicit `Request Tool`/`Grant Tool` events → measures true tool queues; separates FIX-A contention from operator or program delays for J4.
2. Machine idle-reason codes (awaiting-tool / awaiting-batch / awaiting-material / no-demand) → separates machine capacity from tool and sync hypotheses.
3. Batch-formation policy attributes (min/max size, planned start, tariff window) → tests whether 12:05 was forced or chosen.
4. Shipment booking data (carrier cut-offs, cost per dispatch, consolidation contracts) → tests whether S1's hold was policy or necessity.
5. CAL-3 calibration policy (per-test mandatory vs drift-triggered) → determines whether J6's 50 min is structural compliance cost.
6. Quality-lab disposition records for L7 and the override authorization for J2 → tests whether lot coupling is a real risk channel and whether B1/S2 carry latent exposure.
7. Operator/shift roster linked to events → alternative explanation for the 09:50–11:45 CNC-2 gap.
8. A unified extraction linking lot and override events to Jobs in both pipelines → quantifies how much of the picture is a data artifact.
