## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

To systematically repair the incomplete OCEL 2.0 dataset without artificially manufacturing deterministic certainties, we establish a rule-based evidence model. Each rule is classified by its reliability status under real-world operational conditions:

*   **Hard invariant:**
    *   *Referential Integrity:* Every E2O edge must link an existing event ID (`ocel:eid`) to an existing object ID (`ocel:oid`); every O2O edge must link valid source and target object IDs.
    *   *Directionality:* O2O qualifier semantics are strictly directional (e.g., source `DEL-X` `carries` target `PK-7` is distinct from the inverse).
*   **Strong default:**
    *   *Cardinality constraints:* `Create Order` (1 Order), `Add Item` (1 Order, 1 Item), `Pack Item` (1 Item, 1 Package), `Dispatch Package` (1 Package, 1 Delivery), `Record Payment` (1 Payment, 1 Invoice).
    *   *Lifecycle sequencing:* Order creation must precede item additions; packaging must precede dispatch; dispatch must precede delivery confirmation; invoicing must precede payment settlement.
*   **Weak heuristic:**
    *   *Identifier and Prefix Matching:* Matches between prefixes (e.g., `PK-8` scanned in `ev07`, `scanner_ref=PK8-1` in `ev07`, and `PK8-2` in `ev08`) strongly suggest functional associations.
    *   *Temporal Proximity & Routing:* Events occurring within minutes of each other carrying matching attributes (e.g., `route=NORTH`, `customer=C17`) indicate process alignment.
    *   *Amount Arithmetic:* Sum matching (e.g., `ORD-A` declared value 130 + `ORD-B` declared value 100 = `INV-44` total 230) provides circumstantial evidence for billing aggregations, but tax, fee, and split-payment dynamics mean it remains defeasible.
*   **Evidence against a repair:**
    *   Direct domain contradictions (e.g., linking a payment for amount 130 (`PAY-2`) to an invoice or order exclusively intended for 100 without a clear balance split or multi-payment structure).
    *   Card-violating duplicates that breach the 1-to-1 or 1-to-many expected bounds without an explicit exception log (e.g., a single `Add Item` event mapping to multiple distinct orders simultaneously).

**Combining Evidence Types:** 
We synthesize these categories by computing a confidence score. Temporal proximity combined with prefix matches and structural alignment (e.g., matching `route_hint` to `route`) yields higher confidence ($>0.8$). Pure arithmetic coincidences without structural metadata support remain provisional ($0.4–0.6$). If evidence is conflicting or missing, the relation is left unresolved to prevent downstream bias in process discovery.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

### Bundle A: Conservative / Minimum-Change Repair
*   **Philosophy:** Adhere strictly to explicit observational bounds. Do not invent financial cross-links or multi-order invoices without direct metadata proof.
*   **ev08 and PK-8:** Connect `ev08` to `PK-8` via E2O and add O2O edge `PK-8 packs IT-B1` based on the strong scanner reference suffix (`PK8-2`).
*   **ev10 and DEL-X / DEL-Y:** Link `ev10` to `DEL-Y` acknowledging the prefix `N-421?` matching `DEL-Y` (`N-421`), and add `DEL-Y carries PK-8`.
*   **ev11 and delivery objects:** Map `ev11` exclusively to `DEL-X` using the `N-420` alignment and customer route context, leaving `DEL-Y` unlinked to confirmation.
*   **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Reject any bills relation between `INV-44` and `ORD-B`. `INV-44` exclusively bills `ORD-A` (total value 130 does not match 230 on its own, implying `INV-44` might be partial or incomplete, but we do not force structural links).
*   **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Leave `PAY-2` unlinked to `INV-44` as unapplied cash, reflecting the absence of an explicit invoice reference in the base log.

### Bundle B: Plausible Alternative Interpretation (Aggregating Financials & Deliveries)
*   **Philosophy:** Exploit holistic arithmetic and customer-level consolidation (Customer C17). Assume multi-order invoices and bulk payment settlements.
*   **ev08 and PK-8:** Identical to Bundle A (scanner prefix is robust).
*   **ev10 and DEL-X / DEL-Y:** Treat `ev10` as a bulk dispatch event covering both `PK-7` and `PK-8` dispatched via `DEL-X`, or link `PK-8` to `DEL-Y`. Let us route `PK-8` via `DEL-Y`.
*   **ev11 and delivery objects:** Map `ev11` to *both* `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y` under the assumption that `N-42*` represents a batch delivery confirmation for all NORTH route packages for customer C17 on that day.
*   **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Accept that `INV-44` is a consolidated invoice billing both `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` because $130 + $100 = $230$, matching `INV-44` total. Add O2O `INV-44 bills ORD-B`.
*   **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Link `PAY-2` (amount 130) to `INV-44` as a second settling payment, matching the remaining balance of the consolidated invoice ($230 - 100 = 130$). Add O2O `PAY-2 settles INV-44`.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

*   **Order (`ORD-A`, `ORD-B`):** 
    *   *Plausibility:* Both orders exhibit clean lifecycles (`Create Order` $\rightarrow$ `Add Item`). `ORD-A` proceeds through packaging, delivery confirmation, and invoicing. `ORD-B` contains an item packed in `PK-8`, but stops short of explicit delivery or invoicing in Bundle A.
    *   *Orphans:* `ORD-B` lacks downstream delivery confirmation and invoice linkage in Bundle A, representing an open/unbilled order state.
*   **Item (`IT-A1`, `IT-A2`, `IT-B1`):** 
    *   *Plausibility:* All items are created via `Add Item` and packed into packages (`PK-7`, `PK-8`). 
    *   *Orphans / Cardinality:* No orphan items. 1-to-1 mapping between items and packages is preserved.
*   **Package (`PK-7`, `PK-8`):** 
    *   *Plausibility:* Both packages are packed and dispatched (`DEL-X`, `DEL-Y`). 
    *   *Circular Reasoning Check:* In Bundle A, the O2O edge `PK-8 packs IT-B1` is inferred directly from the repair of `ev08`'s E2O link. We must acknowledge that if `ev08`'s E2O addition was speculative, treating the resulting O2O edge as independent validation constitutes circular reasoning. We mitigate this by tying confidence strictly to the scanner prefix metadata rather than downstream graph density.
*   **Delivery (`DEL-X`, `DEL-Y`):** 
    *   *Plausibility:* `DEL-X` is linked to `PK-7` and confirmed via `ev11`. `DEL-Y` carries `PK-8` via `ev10`. 
    *   *Orphans:* `DEL-Y` lacks an explicit delivery confirmation event in Bundle A, modeling a transit-in-progress or unconfirmed delivery state.
*   **Invoice & Payment (`INV-44`, `PAY-1`, `PAY-2`):** 
    *   *Plausibility:* `INV-44` bills `ORD-A` and is settled by `PAY-1` (100). `PAY-2` (130) remains an unapplied payment object in Bundle A. 
    *   *Suspicious Cardinality:* Bundle B's multi-order invoice linkage creates a many-to-one cardinality on `INV-44` (`bills` multiple orders), which is plausible for consolidated billing but obscures individual order settlement tracking.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

*   **OC-DFG (Object-Centric Directly-Follows Graph):**
    *   *Unrepaired log:* Missing edges around `ev08` and `ev10` fragment the DFG, causing `Pack Item` to appear disconnected from `PK-8`, and `Dispatch Package` to exhibit sink nodes.
    *   *Bundle A:* Restores clean local object paths. `Pack Item` $\rightarrow$ `Dispatch Package` edges become fully connected for both packages.
    *   *Bundle B:* Introduces cross-order synchronization loops where `Issue Invoice` connects concurrently to `ORD-A` and `ORD-B`, causing artificially inflated concurrency in financial traces.
*   **OTG (Object Transition Graph):**
    *   *Bundle A* maintains strict object boundaries. Packages transition cleanly from `packed` to `dispatched`.
    *   *Bundle B* forces joint object lifecycles where items from `ORD-B` are entangled with invoices belonging nominally to `ORD-A`.
*   **ET–OT Graph:** Edges between events and object instances become denser. In Bundle A, event nodes have precise single- or dual-object degrees. In Bundle B, `Issue Invoice` and `Record Payment` exhibit high degree cardinality, connecting multiple orders and invoices simultaneously.
*   **Flattenings:**
    *   *Order flattening:* In Bundle A, `ORD-B` traces terminate prematurely at packaging. In Bundle B, `ORD-B` traces extend all the way to invoicing and payment settlement.
    *   *Delivery flattening:* Flattening on `Delivery` yields two distinct traces in Bundle A (`DEL-X` confirmed, `DEL-Y` dispatched-only), whereas Bundle B couples them via batch confirmations.
*   **Activity Frequencies & Equivalent-Execution Clustering:** Flattening distorts case variant counts. Bundle B artificially groups disparate customer orders into single-case execution clusters based on invoice totals, masking underlying operational bottlenecks in individual order processing times.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1.  **False Financial Aggregation (Invoice Merging):** Forcing `INV-44` to bill both `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` solely because their values sum to 230 creates a false multi-order invoice structure, destroying per-order financial auditing accuracy.
2.  **Spurious Cash Allocation (Payment Misattribution):** Linking `PAY-2` to `INV-44` without explicit bank reference cross-validation manufactures a false payment settlement link, resulting in incorrect accounts receivable aging models.
3.  **Premature Lifecycle Closure:** Forcing `ORD-B` to inherit a delivery confirmation and invoice status when observational data stops at packaging creates phantom operational closures that hide actual fulfillment delays.
4.  **Conflated Delivery Routing:** Assigning ambiguous delivery confirmations (`N-42*`) across multiple competing delivery objects (`DEL-X`, `DEL-Y`) simulates concurrent deliveries that violate physical single-carrier routing constraints.
5.  **Circular Graph Validation:** Treating inferred E2O events as independent verification for O2O edges (or vice versa) creates an echo chamber of self-validating heuristics, artificially inflating process confidence metrics in downstream discovery algorithms.
