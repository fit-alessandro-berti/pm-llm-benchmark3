**Alias normalization.** Every raw `org:resource` value is mapped to a human key before any segregation-of-duties (SoD), authority or network computation: `u_bk`/`b.koch` → Ben, `u_as` → Asha, and so on. SoD rules compare humans, not accounts. A check on raw IDs misses CHG-508 (drafted by `b.koch`, approved by `u_bk`). An identity that cannot be mapped becomes `UNKNOWN[...]` and is escalated; it is never treated as compliant.

**Service-account attribution.** Service accounts are resolved case by case through an evidence-graded attribution table:
- **STRONG**: an individual authenticator such as FIDO, a smart card, or an e-signature re-authentication, plus corroborating station and badge data. The action is attributed to that human and can support conformance.
- **WEAK**: circumstantial evidence only, such as workstation ownership or room entry. For conformance, the action stays `UNATTRIBUTED[svc@case]`, a unique placeholder that equals no human. SoD is therefore reported as "not demonstrated" rather than "passed". A separate adverse scenario applies the weak candidate, to show what the case would look like if the candidate did act.

## 2. Rule Interpretation

Notation: H(e) is the normalized human of event e (or UNATTRIBUTED). Roles(h, t) is the set of standing roles of h valid at time t. Absent(p, t) means p is on recorded leave or formal unavailability at t and has no contradicting active session.

**Valid delegation.** Valid(δ, e) holds if and only if all of the following are true:
- δ.delegate = H(e), and e is attributed (not a service account or weak attribution);
- δ.start ≤ ts(e) ≤ δ.end;
- scope(case) ∈ δ.scope, and act(e) is within δ's authorized acts. Delegations are read narrowly: an unlisted scope or act is not covered.
- if δ requires it, Absent(δ.principal, ts(e));
- δ is not a subdelegation, or its parent allows subdelegation;
- the grant existed before ts(e), proven by contemporaneous, integrity-protected evidence.

**RR1.** For each case: for every Draft event d and every Approve event a, H(d) ≠ H(a). If either side is UNATTRIBUTED, the result is *not demonstrated*, which counts as nonconformance under RR5 but not as proof of a breach.

**RR2.** The same test applies between Implement_Change and Verify_Implementation.

**RR3.** If risk = HIGH and the case is not an emergency, every Approve event a needs QM ∈ Roles(H(a), ts(a)), or some delegation δ with role QM and Valid(δ, a). Being on leave does not remove a standing role. The role is evaluated from interval tables, never from the current master.

**RR4.** For HIGH-risk emergencies, the approval must satisfy (i) or (ii):
- (i) QM authority as in RR3 (the normal route remains allowed);
- (ii) IC ∈ Roles(H(a), ts(a)), or a valid IC delegation, **and** a Retrospective_QM_Review r with QM authority in which ts(r) ≤ Deadline(ts(Implement_Change)), **and** a credible emergency: the flag is true, an incident record is linked, and a safety rationale is documented.
- Approval authority is judged at approval time. The exception is *justified* only after a timely, authorized retrospective review. A failed review does not retroactively make a valid IC approval "unauthorized", but the case as a whole is nonconformant.
- A retrospective review never cures an approval that was invalid at its own timestamp.

**RR4 business-day counting.**
- Business days are Monday to Friday, excluding site holidays; there were none in the period, and 08-15/08-16 are weekend days.
- The clock starts at Implement_Change, when the unreviewed change takes effect. Starting at approval gives identical results here.
- Primary rule: the deadline is 23:59:59 on the second business day after the implementation day.
- Sensitivity rule (stricter): the implementation time plus 2 business days, to the same clock time.
- The site timezone is not given. For CHG-505 (late) and CHG-510 (on time), the verdict holds for any offset from UTC−12 to UTC+14.
- **CHG-505**: implemented Tue 08-11 13:10; deadline Thu 08-13 23:59 (strict rule: Thu 13:10). The review on Fri 08-14 16:00 is **late**.
- **CHG-510**: implemented Tue 08-11 14:30; reviewed Wed 08-12 10:00, which is **on time**.

**RR5.** A service account is never a human. Its action counts toward conformance only with STRONG session attribution. Aliases are normalized before RR1/RR2. Unmapped or weakly mapped actions mean conformance is not demonstrated.

**RR6.** Repeated pairs, reciprocity, shared workstations and concentrated handovers can at most produce SUSPICIOUS_PATTERN. A misconduct or collusion finding needs independent corroboration, such as attributed credential misuse, deception, falsified records, substantive review failures, or benefit.

**A principal acting while on leave.**
- Leave is an HR status, not a revocation of authority. Unless the SOP suspends signatory rights during leave, a QM approval by the principal on leave is technically authorized, provided the identity is reliably attributed.
- Because leave makes impersonation more plausible, a higher attribution standard applies: MFA device, source location, and e-signature re-authentication.
- While the principal is demonstrably active, a delegation conditioned on "principal absent" is suspended for that window. Here no delegate act falls in Asha's 09:15–09:40 session, so the point is moot.

**Can a later database entry prove an earlier delegation?**
- No, not on its own. A record created 08-12 09:15 proves registration at 08-12 09:15, nothing earlier.
- The existence of an earlier grant can be proven by a contemporaneous, tamper-evident artifact: a qualified e-signature with a trusted timestamp, a controlled document number, or server-side email or DMS logs.
- For D-QM-01, the signed PDF dated 08-09 18:00 carries the proof if its signature and timestamp validate. If the "timestamp" is only file metadata, the delegation is unproven for acts before 08-12 09:15 (only CHG-501 is affected), and CHG-501 falls to INDETERMINATE.
- Registration after use is itself a data-integrity and control defect.

## 5. PM4Py and pandas Checking Code

How the code represents service-account attribution, and what stays manual:
- **Attribution representation.** `SVC_ATTRIBUTION` is a manually maintained, evidence-graded table.

- **Manual judgments.** The following remain manual:
  - the attribution grades themselves;
  - authenticity of the D-QM-01 PDF;
  - whether `cooling_system` counts as production equipment, and whether retrospective review counts as "approval";
  - whether Asha counts as "absent" on a weekend;
  - emergency credibility and the CI-44 linkage;
  - whether Ben really acted during sick leave;
  - any conclusion about collusion.

    return "NOT_FORMALLY_ABSENT_WEEKEND" if ts.dayofweek >= 5 else "PRESENT"


def delegation_checks(human, required_role, ts, scope, activity):
    rows = []
    for _, d in DELEGATIONS[(DELEGATIONS["delegate"] == human) &
                            (DELEGATIONS["role"] == required_role)].iterrows():
        parent_ok = True
        if pd.notna(d["subdelegation_of"]):
            parent = DELEGATIONS.set_index("deleg_id").loc[d["subdelegation_of"]]
            parent_ok = bool(parent["subdelegation_allowed"])
        pa = principal_absent(d["principal"], ts) if d["requires_principal_absent"] else "N/A"
        evidence_before = min([x for x in [d["signed_artifact_ts"], d["system_record_ts"]]
                               if pd.notna(x)], default=pd.NaT)
        rows.append(dict(
            deleg_id=d["deleg_id"],
            in_interval=bool(d["start"] <= ts <= d["end"]),
            in_scope=scope in d["scopes"],
            act_covered=activity in d["acts"],
            principal_condition=pa,
            principal_ok=pa in ("ABSENT", "N/A"),
            no_prohibited_subdelegation=parent_ok,
            evidence_predates_event=bool(pd.notna(evidence_before) and evidence_before <= ts),
            system_record_predates_event=bool(pd.notna(d["system_record_ts"]) and d["system_record_ts"] <= ts),
        ))
    out = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    if not out.empty:
        out["valid"] = out[["in_interval", "in_scope", "act_covered", "principal_ok",
                            "no_prohibited_subdelegation", "evidence_predates_event"]].all(axis=1)
    return out

                          "delegation_detail": None, "authority": "NOT_REQUIRED_BY_RR"})

                          "delegation_detail": None, "authority": "STANDING_ROLE"})
    dc = delegation_checks(human, required, ts, row["scope"],
                           "Approve_Change" if act == "Approve_Change" else act)
    if act == "Approve_Change" and row["emergency"] and "QM" in roles:
        return pd.Series({"required_role": required, "standing": sorted(roles),
                          "delegation_detail": None, "authority": "STANDING_ROLE(QM)"})

        verdict = "NO_AUTHORITY"
    elif dc["valid"].any():
        v = dc[dc["valid"]].iloc[0]
        verdict = "VALID_DELEGATION" if v["system_record_predates_event"] else \

        verdict = "INVALID_DELEGATION"
    return pd.Series({"required_role": required, "standing": sorted(roles),
                      "delegation_detail": dc.to_dict("records") if not dc.empty else None,
                      "authority": verdict})

## 6. Control Improvements

1. **Delegation creation.**
   - Create delegations only in the validated QMS, never as email or ad-hoc PDF.
   - Each delegation needs structured fields: principal, delegate, role, scope taxonomy codes, permitted activities (explicitly listing "Approve_Change" and/or "Retrospective_QM_Review"), incident ID for IC delegations, principal-absence condition, and a subdelegation flag.
   - The system should technically block subdelegation when the parent prohibits it; D-IC-04 should have been impossible to act on.
   - Delegations should require an independent acknowledgement (for example by QA or the site head).
2. **Effective-time recording.**
   - Keep three separate timestamps: signed-at (with a trusted timestamp), registered-at, and effective-from/to.
   - Approvals must be blocked unless a registered delegation exists at the event time, so registration after use (D-QM-01 during migration) cannot happen.
   - Migrations must carry original grant evidence and produce reconciliation reports.
3. **Principal availability.**
   - Maintain a machine-readable availability calendar (leave, sick leave, weekends, on-call) linked to delegation conditions.
   - Define "absent" in the SOP, including weekends. Record principal overrides during leave with a reason code and a step-up authentication.
   - Certified sick leave should automatically suspend a delegate's signing rights unless the person is explicitly re-enabled.
4. **Alias management.** Enforce one human, one identity: a golden-record mapping from HR to identity and access management (IAM) to QMS. Retire legacy accounts such as `b.koch`, and run a nightly job that flags multiple active accounts per human. SoD checks run on the human key.
5. **Individual authentication.**
   - Remove shared service-account sessions for GxP signatures. Every approval, verification and review requires 21 CFR Part 11-style two-component e-signature or FIDO re-authentication.
   - Shared terminals such as WS-DM-04 should use tap-in/tap-out badges with auto-lock.
   - Record MFA device, source IP and geolocation for every signature.
6. **Emergency review.**
   - Emergency changes must reference a registered incident. A retrospective QM review task should be generated automatically, due by the end of the second business day, with escalation at 50% and 100% of the time.
   - The system should reject a retrospective reviewer lacking an in-scope QM delegation.
   - Require a documented safety rationale, and hold a periodic QA trend review of emergency flags to prevent overuse.
7. **Pair rotation.**
   - Introduce approver rotation, or a second approver, for HIGH-risk changes once a drafter–approver dyad exceeds a threshold (for example 50% over a rolling 60 days).
   - Formalize LOW-risk approval rights so that no drafter self-approves (CHG-502, CHG-508). Separate approver and verifier where possible (CHG-509).
8. **Network monitoring.** Run monthly process mining of handover, working-together and drafter–approver matrices on normalized identities, compared against a roster-based expected network. Alerts should trigger a *review of evidence*, not a finding, and include delegation-coverage lookbacks such as Ben's 18 approvals.
9. **Evidential retention.** Retain signed delegation artifacts with trusted timestamps, QMS audit trails (client and server time), badge, VPN, MFA and CCTV logs for at least the GMP record retention period, and incident records linked to change IDs. Put a legal hold on all evidence for 08-09 to 08-16 now.
