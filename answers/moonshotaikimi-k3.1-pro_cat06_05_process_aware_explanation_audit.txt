## 2. Process-model consistency checks

A concrete validation suite should run in CI on historical training construction, online/serving features, and generated explanations:

1. **Construct and replay the exact prediction prefix.**
   - Identify `t0` from the **first** `Coverage Check Completed`, using event time and a reliable sequence index where available.
   - Replay only events and feature versions that both occurred and were available by `t0`. Event time alone is insufficient for nightly snapshots, mutable scores, and late-arriving records.
   - Assert prefix-time contracts for every feature: source timestamps, snapshot availability, version history, and join keys. Separately test queue tolerance, current-claim exclusion, late data, and time zones.

2. **Detect complete-trace dependence with lineage and suffix-invariance tests.**
   - statically flag lineage containing closure fields, extraction-date joins, unbounded case aggregates, and names such as `total`, `final`, `anywhere`, `last`, `current`, or `full_trace` for manual review;
   - dynamically compute each purported `t0` feature on the prefix, then append different valid suffixes while holding the prefix fixed. A genuine `t0` feature must not change. The same test can be applied to the complete feature vector and prediction.
   - This distinguishes a legitimately prefix-derived document count or variant from a similarly named full-trace field.

3. **Check activity-order feasibility with a prefix-aware normative model.**
   - Encode the fast-track branch, repeated evidence cycles, damage assessment, conditional and parallel fraud investigation, decision outcomes, appeal, reconsideration, withdrawal, and terminal closure.
   - Use prefix alignment or prefix-aware declarative rules so an incomplete prefix is not penalized merely for lacking future activities. Data-aware rules are needed for conditions such as the decision outcome required before appeal and the trigger required for fraud investigation.
   - Separately test an available but process-inconsistent record—for example, an appeal asserted before any eligible decision. Temporal availability does not make such a state valid.

4. **Treat equal timestamps as partial order, not as a known sequence.**
   - Build directly-follows edges only where timestamp plus reliable sequence information supports them.
   - Without a sequence index, enumerate or reason over feasible orders. If at least one order conforms, classify the trace as ambiguous rather than definitely nonconformant; if every order violates the model, report a process violation.
   - For Z, test both orders in the context of the whole trace. Reversing the two labels is not itself a conformance repair, and neither resolution makes the pattern available at `t0`.

5. **Classify every activity referenced by an explanation at `t0`.**
   - **Observed:** Claim Registered and Coverage Check Completed.
   - **Potentially enabled next:** Documents Requested, or Damage Assessment Completed on the fast-track branch; Fraud Investigation Opened only if its trigger and concurrency conditions permit it.
   - **Not currently enabled but possibly future:** subsequent evidence activities, settlement decision, appeal under its outcome condition, and closure. Enablement does not mean the activity has occurred or can be used as an observed feature.
   - **Infeasible if genuinely ordered:** closure followed by further damage assessment, or an appeal before an eligible decision.
   - Thus `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is feasible later but still leaked at `t0`, whereas the close/damage pattern has an additional ordering concern.

6. **Handle repeated settlement decisions semantically.**
   - Do not impose a generic “one decision per case” rule on the coarse activity label.
   - A second decision is legitimate when linked to an intervening appeal after Partial or Deny. A repeated decision without that context may instead be duplication or a deviation requiring investigation.
   - Claim Closed should follow a final decision or withdrawal, with no ordinary subsequent activities.

Alignments can localize deviations but may hide problems through low-cost “repairs” and depend on model completeness and cost choices. Footprints and declarative constraints complement one another but may miss data conditions or concurrency. A DFG is descriptive: a frequent edge is not automatically normative, a rare edge is not automatically impossible, and sorting, coarse labels, and parallelism can distort it. None of these checks establishes causation.

Some items are **not merely awaiting repair for this prediction task**: total case duration, appeal-anywhere, final decision, full-trace variant, last-role information, and post-`t0` sequence occurrences must be excluded at `t0`. Newly defined prefix analogues, where meaningful, are different features. Using the original events at a later prediction time is also a different task.

Four standards should remain separate:

- **Predictive validity:** point-in-time availability, process-consistent data, out-of-time performance and calibration, and adequate robustness. Correlated features can share or exchange SHAP credit; mean absolute importance supplies neither a universal direction nor causation.
- **Causal interpretation:** requires a causal question and credible identification of confounding, selection, timing, and intervention effects. Temporal ordering and SHAP importance alone are insufficient.
- **Operational actionability:** requires a feasible intervention whose benefits, costs, and harms have been evaluated. Queue length might inform a staffing experiment, and complexity might inform a tested triage policy, but neither explanation is itself an intervention recommendation.
- **Fairness and appropriate use:** a feature can be predictive yet still produce unacceptable proxy or allocation harms. Conversely, correlation or sensitivity does not prove that a feature has no legitimate use. Review the complete model and its decisions, not just the presence or absence of region.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **P0 — Stop leakage from being operationalized.**
   - Quarantine the critical/high-risk fields above and suspend use of the current `t0` explanations and probabilities for operational decisions pending refitting.
   - Separate post hoc outcome/trajectory analytics from the early-prediction feature view. Do not attempt to rescue performance by deleting features only from the explanation while leaving them in the model.

2. **P0/P1 — Build a point-in-time feature store and complete lineage inventory.**
   - Store source event time, availability/ingestion time, feature valid-from/to times, version, snapshot time, and extraction lineage. Use immutable registration values or historically versioned dimensions for team, role, reserve, score, and employee experience.
   - Enforce as-of joins and an allowlist for the first-coverage `t0` view. Keep labels and post-outcome fields physically and logically separate from predictors.
   - Add automated checks for extraction-date joins, unbounded case aggregates, closure fields, nightly snapshots, missing sequence indices, and suffix dependence. Include lower-ranked features such as employee experience.

3. **P1 — Recompute every training and serving feature from prefixes.**
   - Replay historical prefixes and compare them with warehouse values. Where history is unavailable, drop the feature rather than reconstructing it from current or final values.
   - Apply the suffix-invariance, availability, same-timestamp, and process-conformance tests from Section 2. Include synthetic tests for fast track, multiple evidence cycles, parallel fraud investigation, legitimate appeal/reconsideration, withdrawal, terminal closure, and ambiguous timestamps.

4. **P1 — Refit, validate, and recalibrate honestly.**
   - Train a clean prefix-only model using fully matured labels and case-level, out-of-time validation/test splits. Compare against a simple clean baseline and expect performance to fall when leaked information is removed.
   - After feature removal, reassess discrimination, Brier/log loss, calibration intercept and slope, reliability plots, and performance at the intended alert threshold. Recalibrate on appropriate validation data and confirm on untouched later data; recalibration alone cannot repair a still-leaked model.
   - Regenerate global and local explanations on the clean model, documenting their output scale. Never derive revised probabilities by subtracting old contributions.

5. **P2 — Test explanation stability and correlated-feature behavior.**
   - Measure rank, sign, and local-narrative stability across reasonable background datasets, model seeds/refits, time windows, and relevant subgroups. Examine credit sharing among region, channel, reserve, history, and operational variables.
   - Use grouped or complementary analyses where helpful, but do not interpret stability as causality. Suppress overly precise narratives when contributions are small or unstable.

6. **P2 — Conduct proxy, fairness, robustness, and actionability reviews.**
   - Evaluate performance and calibration by region and other relevant groups, including intersections, missingness, holiday/policy periods, and repair-network conditions. Compare models with and without proxies and with more direct operational variables; assess deployment and allocation harms, not just feature importance.
   - Review queue, calendar, reserve, and history signals for temporal transportability. Evaluate any proposed staffing or triage intervention separately, ideally with experimental or credible quasi-experimental evidence and human oversight.

7. **P3 — Monitor schema, process, feature, and explanation drift.**
   - Alert on lineage or SQL changes, new/renamed activities, altered decision outcomes, missing sequence indices, snapshot-schedule changes, rising unavailable-at-`t0` values, suffix dependence, and unexpected process-state combinations.
   - Monitor process variants, appeal/reconsideration patterns, concurrency, queue distributions, policy/holiday changes, feature missingness, calibration, and explanation stability. Treat DFG changes as drift signals requiring investigation, not automatic normative violations.
   - Version the feature contracts, process model, policy context, training data, and calibrated model together; define review, rollback, and periodic revalidation gates.
