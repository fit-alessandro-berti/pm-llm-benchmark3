## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

I treat the flag as a **policy bundle** (priority lane + mandatory referral + downstream consequences), not as a magically manipulable cause. "Set flag = 0" (P1) changes what staff see; "change what happens after the flag" (P2–P4) changes the routing rules. These are different interventions with different estimands.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | P0 vs P1: full flag bundle on vs off, downstream rules unchanged | case; all arrivals in a fixed window | total cycle time (arrivalclose) | total effect (policy contrast / cluster-ITT analogue) | Yes as a policy contrast; No as an "effect of the flag" | flag is deterministic in `risk_score` (no overlap: flagged 0.64–0.93, unflagged 0.28–0.61); shared queues  interference; case mix drifts over calendar time |
| E2 | flag on vs off, downstream policy held fixed | case | arrivaltriage_done (triage completion) | total effect on an intermediate outcome | Yes as policy contrast | priority lane displaces unflagged cases (interference at triage); triage resource is perfectly co-assigned with flag (T1/T2 vs T3/T4) |
| E3 | flag on vs off, decomposed by whether manual review occurs | case | cycle time | natural direct/indirect effects (NDE/NIE) — considered and **rejected**; interventional analogue (E3i) preferred | No, as natural effects | cross-world independence fails: review findings, reviewer load, and queue state at entry are affected by the flag and confound mediatoroutcome; mediator is deterministic (10/10) among flagged |
| E3i | referral occurrence randomized at observed flag-specific rates (conceptual evaluation of P2) | case | cycle time | interventional indirect effect via manual-review **occurrence** | Yes in principle | requires randomizing referrals (ethics/power); referral changes the downstream information set |
| E4 | all cases reviewed; review-queue priority for flagged randomized (P3-style contrast) | case | cycle time | interventional (separable) indirect effect via manual-review **waiting** | Yes as a policy | fixed capacity makes waits zero-sum (interference); queue state at entry must be measured |
| E5 | review fixed; escalation criteria/capacity varied (P4-style contrast) | case | cycle time; decision outcome | interventional indirect effect via escalation occurrence and `escalation_queue_wait` | Partially | escalation is decided on post-review information (exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounding); senior capacity is scarce and shared |
| E6 | flag on, manual review set to *absent* for everyone (P2 as a controlled intervention) | case | total cycle time | controlled direct effect analogue (policy-realizable) | Yes as policy; not a pure "direct effect" | fixing review still alters the information available for escalation and the decision, so other channels shift |
| E7 | P0 vs P2 (or P4) | case | correct-decision indicator from blind audit; reject rate | total effect on decision quality | Partially | no ground-truth labels in the log; only 4 rejections; escalation selects hard cases |
| E8 | shift-level share of flagged cases  | unflagged case within a shift | triage wait; review wait | spillover / interference effect (exposure mapping) | Partially | SUTVA violation; exposure is cluster-level; only one day of data here |

**Framework choice.** I use (a) **total effects as policy contrasts** (cluster-ITT style), (b) **interventional (randomized-mediator) indirect effects and separable effects** for pathway attribution, and (c) **controlled-direct-effect analogues** where the policy can actually set the mediator (P2/P3/P4). I do **not** use natural direct/indirect effects as primary quantities because their cross-world independence assumption (no mediator–outcome confounder affected by exposure) is visibly violated here: review findings, queue state, and reviewer assignment are all consequences of the flag/review and causes of later outcomes. Where I mention NDE/NIE it is to explain why they fail, not to claim them.

Interference: the triage lane, review pool, and senior team are shared, so one case's flag changes other cases' waits — mandatory referrals lengthen unflagged review waits, and lane-jumping lengthens unflagged triage waits. SUTVA fails; exposure is partly a cluster quantity (flag share per shift). Exposure-induced confounding: conditioning on `manual_review` or `escalation` to isolate later stages conditions on variables the flag moved; review findings (`U_review_finding`), reviewer assignment, and queue state at entry are affected by the flag and confound reviewescalation and escalationoutcome. Treatment–confounder feedback: the priority lane shifts `triage_done`, which sets queue-entry order into review; queue state then affects review waiting and escalation propensity — a time-varying confounder affected by prior treatment, so static adjustment (e.g., regressing on `arrival_load` alone) is insufficient; g-methods or sequential stratification are needed. Complexity nearly determines escalation, so escalation contrasts largely compare complex vs non-complex cases.

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 keep current system | flagged fast (0.2h), unflagged slow (2.5h) | review pool congested by mandatory referrals (6.5h flagged waits); escalation wait ~7h | flagged ~16.5h; unflagged ~8.2h | baseline; all rejects currently caught at escalation | unflagged triage displaced; review pool crowded | observational only, confounded | keep as the control arm; do not scale on this evidence |
| P1 suppress display, same information | flagged lose lane  triage wait rises toward ~2.5h | referrals become judgment-only  review/escalation queues shrink | flagged cycle likely falls (review queue removed) despite slower triage; net ambiguous | **high**: systematic review of high scores lost; misses plausible | positive for unflagged (triage + review) | weak (pure counterfactual) | only as a research arm with decision audit |
| P2 priority triage, no mandatory review | unchanged (lane kept) | review queue relieved for everyone | flagged 4 to 8h (removes ~6.5h wait + 2.7h service for cases a reviewer wouldn't have pulled anyway) | **high**: rejects currently surface in review/escalation | unflagged review waits improve | moderate on timing, unknown on quality | pilot only with blinded decision-quality monitoring |
| P3 current routing + protected review capacity | unchanged | flagged review wait 6.5h  ~2–3h if capacity is net-added | flagged 3 to 5h | low (review still universal) | minimal if capacity added; negative if reallocated from unflagged | **strongest mechanism evidence** (queue congestion directly observed) | **recommended pilot** |
| P4 keep review, change escalation criteria/capacity | unchanged | escalation wait ~7h falls with capacity or tighter criteria | escalated cases 3 to 6h (40–60% of flagged) | **moderate–high**: 50% of escalated cases are rejected; criteria changes shift the reject mix | spillover to unflagged escalators | moderate | second-wave pilot after P3, with audit |

**Pilot: P3 — protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases (6 weeks, one site).**
Eligibility: all cases with valid logs; flagged cases form the intervention arm; unflagged cases are observed for spillover, not excluded. Assignment: cluster-randomized by 4-hour shift block, stratified day/night — protected reviewer-hours on/off. Clustering limits interference contamination and prevents self-selection. Queue-capacity guardrail: if unflagged review-wait P90 exceeds baseline +25% or review queue depth crosses a pre-set threshold, a float reviewer is automatically released; breaches are logged as spillover events, never silenced. Primary outcome: flagged-case time from `triage_done` to close, with `manual_queue_wait` as the mechanistic mediator. Secondary outcomes: unflagged review wait (spillover), escalation rate and escalation wait, triage wait, and blinded decision-audit concordance on a re-reviewed sample. Minimum process data: start/stop timestamps for every stage, queue-length snapshot at each stage entry, resource IDs, `risk_score`, `complexity`, `arrival_load`, final outcome. Stopping conditions: unflagged wait harm persisting despite the guardrail; audit disagreement above threshold; no flagged review-wait reduction after two weeks; staffing limits breached. Analysis is intention-to-treat at the shift level, with queue-state-adjusted secondary models.

# ---------- 1. Parse timestamps ('-' -> NaT = stage absent, NOT zero-length) ----------

TS = ["arrival","triage_start","triage_done","manual_start","manual_done",
      "escalation_start","escalation_done","close"]
for c in TS:
    cases[c] = pd.to_datetime(cases[c], errors="coerce")

# ---------- 2. Wide case table -> event-level log (PM4Py-compatible) ----------
rows = []
def emit(r, activity, start, complete, resource):
    # absent stages (NaT) emit NO event: nothing is fabricated
    if pd.notna(start) and pd.notna(complete):
        rows.append({"case:concept:name": r["case"], "concept:name": activity,
                     "start_timestamp": start, "time:timestamp": complete,
                     "org:resource": resource, "flag": r["flag"],
                     "complexity": r["complexity"], "risk_score": r["risk_score"],
                     "arrival_load": r["arrival_load"], "outcome": r["outcome"]})

for _, r in cases.iterrows():
    emit(r, "Register",      r["arrival"],          r["arrival"],         "SYSTEM")   # instantaneous boundary event
    emit(r, "Triage",        r["triage_start"],     r["triage_done"],     r["triage_resource"])
    emit(r, "Manual Review", r["manual_start"],     r["manual_done"],     r["review_resource"] if pd.notna(r["manual_start"]) else "NA")
    emit(r, "Escalation",    r["escalation_start"], r["escalation_done"], "SENIOR")
    emit(r, "Close",         r["close"],            r["close"],           "SYSTEM")   # instantaneous boundary event

events = pd.DataFrame(rows).sort_values(["case:concept:name","time:timestamp"]).reset_index(drop=True)

# ---------- 3. Format for PM4Py (supplying the start-timestamp key) ----------
import pm4py

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk.** If unobserved severity (poorly captured by `risk_score`) drives both flagging and slow downstream handling, the "flag causes delay" reading reverses to "severity causes delay" — e.g., if gray-zone cases flagged for administrative reasons show the same long review waits as score-flagged cases.
2. **Queue interference.** If P3's protected capacity does not shorten flagged review waits, or unflagged review waits deteriorate by more than the flagged gain, the congestion mechanism — and the policy recommendation — reverses.
3. **Decision quality.** If blinded audits show that removing mandatory review (P2) or tightening escalation (P4) increases missed-risk errors, cycle-time gains reverse into net harm and the ranking of policies flips.
4. **Timestamp semantics.** If `manual_start` records scheduling/assignment rather than work start, or team clocks skew overnight, the 6.5h "review queue wait" may be a batching or clocking artifact, and the entire stage decomposition must be reinterpreted.
5. **Positivity / overlap.** The flag is deterministic in `risk_score` (gap between 0.61 and 0.64). If no gray-zone cases ever exist, within-score and threshold-based identification is impossible, and any claim of a score-adjusted flag effect must be withdrawn.
6. **Reviewer behavior change.** If reviewers respond to protected capacity by expediting flagged reviews but lowering scrutiny (faster, shallower sign-offs) or by gaming escalation criteria, service-time improvements reverse into quality losses.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed:** flagged cases reach triage in ~0.2h versus ~2.5h and complete triage faster (0.60 vs 0.95h); every flagged case is reviewed, waiting ~6.5h versus ~1.9h; 60% versus 20% escalate; escalated cases absorb ~9 extra hours (queue ~7h plus senior service ~2h); mean cycle 16.5h versus 8.2h; all four rejections occur after escalation. Flag is deterministic in `risk_score`; flag perfectly determines triage resources; complexity nearly determines escalation.

**Plausible mechanism:** the flag is a policy bundle — lane acceleration plus mandatory referral. The delay is manufactured downstream: universal referral floods a shared review pool (flagged waits 3–4× unflagged, worse under high load), and review feeds a slow escalation path constrained by scarce senior capacity. Congestion, not the flag's information content, is the leading explanation for the gap.

**Unidentified:** whether hidden severity or complexity drives both routing and duration; how reviewers would behave under different rules; decision quality (no ground truth); natural direct/indirect effects are not identified — only interventional, policy-level analogues, and spillovers contaminate any case-level contrast.

**Most useful next evidence:** a cluster-randomized P3 pilot — protected review capacity for flagged cases — because it directly manipulates the dominant identified bottleneck, measures spillover onto unflagged cases via queue-state snapshots, preserves the review function so decision-quality audits stay interpretable, and, paired with prospective gray-zone sampling, begins restoring the overlap this observational data lacks.
