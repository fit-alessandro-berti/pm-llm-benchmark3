## 1. Feasibility model

\`\`\`text
Register ≺ Triage
Triage ≺ Identity Check
Triage ≺ Document Check task creation
Identity Check ≺ Assessment
Document Check ≺ Assessment
Assessment ≺ Decision ≺ Notify

If originals are required:
  Originals Receipt ≺ Document Check
  Identity Check may overlap Originals Receipt and the wait for Document Check

If no originals are required:
  Identity Check || Document Check is permitted after Triage, subject to resource queues

For a high-risk or high-value case:
  latest relevant final Assessment ≺ final Compliance Review ≺ Decision
  a preliminary compliance screen may occur before the final Assessment but is not the final review

For each Request More Info_i:
  Request More Info_i ≺ Document Check_{i+1} ≺ Assessment_{i+1}
  if high-risk or high-value, Assessment_{i+1} ≺ new or explicitly reaffirmed Compliance Review

For final Assessment and final Compliance Review:
  resource(final Assessment) != resource(final Compliance Review)

All service intervals on one resource are non-overlapping.
\`\`\`

\`\`\`text
Conditional constraints
- IF originals are required, no Document Check may start before Originals Receipt.
- IF originals are not required, the Identity Check and Document Check may be concurrent.
- IF high-risk OR high-value, a final Compliance Review is mandatory after the latest relevant Assessment and before Decision.
- IF Request More Info occurs, the next Document Check and Assessment are mandatory; a prior check or assessment cannot be silently reused.
- IF the next review is a high-risk/high-value final review, compliance must be new or explicitly reaffirmed after the new Assessment.
- IF an intervention reserves Compliance capacity early, the reservation does not authorize final compliance before the final Assessment.
- IF the second rework loop in K-337 is suppressed, the unresolved discrepancy must be recorded and reflected in a conditional approval or rejection.
- IF K-411 uses parallel task ownership, each task must have a real-time creation, start, completion, and responsible-resource record.

Declarative constraints
- Decision must precede Notify.
- The same person cannot perform both the final Assessment and final Compliance Review.
- A resource cannot perform two service intervals at the same time.
- Queue estimates are uncertain forecasts, not deadlines or precedence overrides.
- A faster route is not allowed to remove a required prerequisite or compliance control.
\`\`\`

K-104 changes the timing and scheduling of escalation and compliance preparation, while retaining the same final route. K-219 changes routing by substituting D2 for D1, with possible rework. K-411 changes task orchestration and routing from serialized creation to controlled parallel work, but not the decision policy. K-337 changes timing and, explicitly, the allowed decision policy: it removes a permitted information-gathering loop and forces a decision on unresolved evidence. None of the other interventions removes a mandatory final review or the four-eyes rule.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism: The case was registered and triaged promptly, but the sanctions ambiguity was only operationally escalated after A1 completed on Tuesday evening. The long D1 queue and the later C1 queue therefore sat between readiness and final notification; C1 also had to reconstruct the evidence rather than use prepared context.

Minimal intervention: At Monday Triage, S1 receives a preliminary packet and C1 receives a provisional reservation. The final route is otherwise unchanged. The packet is preparation, not a compliance decision, and the reserved slot cannot be used until the final Assessment is complete.

Temporal propagation: S1 can identify missing evidence or likely questions while I1 and D1 proceed. If the packet is useful, C1 spends less time orienting and the reservation reduces the post-Assessment wait. The completion still depends on D1, A1, C1, and M1, so earlier readiness does not guarantee earlier notification. If the early packet displaces an urgent case, the system-level queue effect can offset this case’s gain.

Process-feasibility check: The path satisfies Triage before both checks, both checks before A1, final Assessment before final C1, and C1 before Decision. S1’s advisory work may overlap with checks. C1 remains the only final compliance resource, and the intervention cannot turn preliminary work into a final review.

Best/base/worst case: Best is a useful packet plus a protected C1 slot, producing a same-day final review after A1 and an approval with conditions substantially earlier. Base is a 0–12-hour reduction with the same decision but better prepared reasoning. Worst is duplicate or misleading preparation plus a displaced urgent case, yielding little gain or a delay and possibly a more restrictive outcome.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Actual C1 start-time distributions after reservations, the fraction of preliminary packets that reduce final review effort, S1 packet-completeness rates, and the number and severity of non-target cases delayed by held C1 capacity would determine whether the reservation is beneficial.

### K-219

Factual mechanism: Originals arrived at 17:30, but the D1 route waited until Tuesday 09:30 and completed at 13:30. That route was accurate, yet A2 did not begin until 20:00, so the document resource and the assessment queue both contributed to elapsed time.

Minimal intervention: Keep registration, triage, identity, originals receipt, assessment, decision, and notification unchanged; only assign the post-receipt Document Check to D2. If D2 finds an ambiguity, create a genuine clarification and repeat the required document and assessment steps.

Temporal propagation: D2’s earlier queue position can make the document result available before D1 would have started or completed. The case then joins the A2 queue earlier, which may convert document savings into assessment waiting rather than final savings. If handwriting produces a clarification, the external response interval dominates and adds a new D2/A2 cycle; the intervention then reverses from a speed gain to a delay while possibly preventing a wrong approval.

Process-feasibility check: Originals Receipt precedes D2, Identity Check and Document Check both precede Assessment, and Decision precedes Notify. There is no high-risk/high-value compliance requirement. D2 cannot be treated as complete merely because it starts earlier; a clarification is a new rework path.

Best/base/worst case: Best is a clean, legible file, with D2 finishing early enough for A2 to move forward and notification later Tuesday. Base is modest or no end-to-end gain because A2 absorbs the earlier readiness. Worst is a clarification plus slow external response, producing a later decision but potentially higher document reliability.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: D2’s handwriting-specific error and clarification rates, actual start queues at the Monday receipt time, rework duration, and whether D1 or D2’s findings correlate with downstream corrections would determine the trade-off.

### K-337

Factual mechanism: Assessment 2 was already a substantive review and left one discrepancy unresolved. The factual policy allowed a second Request More Info, which incurred a weekend-spanning supplier wait, then required Document Check 3 and Assessment 3 before C1 could review the case.

Minimal intervention: Stop after Assessment 2’s explicit finding. Record the discrepancy, send no second external request, and proceed to C1 followed by M1. M1 must reject or approve with conditions that address the unresolved evidence; the process cannot represent the evidence as complete.

Temporal propagation: The intervention removes an entire external-response interval and two downstream service intervals, so the case becomes compliance-ready on Friday rather than Monday or Tuesday. The gain is partly exposed to C1’s queue and M1’s availability. The decision may also create downstream condition monitoring, appeal handling, or later remediation, so elapsed case time falls while lifecycle effort or exposure can rise.

Process-feasibility check: The first rework loop remains intact. After Assessment 2, final C1 still occurs after the latest relevant Assessment and before Decision. A1 is not C1, satisfying four-eyes. The unresolved discrepancy is visible in the compliance and decision records, and a rejection or conditional approval is an actual decision rather than silent reuse of stale evidence.

Best/base/worst case: Best is conservative conditional approval with a short C1 wait and a 2–3-day reduction. Base is a faster conditional approval or rejection with a 1–3-day reduction and higher monitoring or appeal risk. Worst is rejection or conditional approval that later proves under-supported, producing loss, remediation, or a compliance challenge despite the initial speed.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: The materiality of the remaining discrepancy, historical outcomes of similar conditional approvals, C1 and M1 queue times on Fridays, appeal/loss rates, and the effectiveness of post-decision controls would determine whether forced closure is acceptable.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: Although identity and documents could legally run concurrently, the legacy case lock created only the identity task. D2 therefore waited for I2 to complete, after which the document work reached the already growing A2 queue.

Minimal intervention: Create both verification tasks at Triage and grant controlled independent ownership. Capture task creation, start, completion, and responsible resource in real time; do not backfill an earlier start after the fact.

Temporal propagation: D2 can enter its queue while I2 is running, so both prerequisites may complete earlier. The case is then released to A2 earlier, but A2 may be the binding constraint. If A2 begins at the same time as the factual path, the cycle time is unchanged even though readiness improves; if many such cases arrive together, earlier release creates an assessment wave and can lengthen other cases.

Process-feasibility check: There are no originals, so parallel Identity Check and Document Check are allowed. Both still complete before Assessment, and Decision still precedes Notify. Resource intervals remain non-overlapping. Real-time logs preserve the distinction between genuine concurrency and a later batch entry that falsely suggests it.

Best/base/worst case: Best is an earlier D2 completion that fits an A2 gap, shortening notification by several hours. Base is little end-to-end change because A2 absorbs the gain. Worst is a poorly controlled workaround or synchronized release burst that creates audit exceptions and delays non-target assessments.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Task-creation logs, time from both-check completion to A2 start, A2 queue displacement, resource-overlap exceptions, and audit findings from a small controlled rollout would show whether parallelization produces true throughput.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 early escalation and C1 reservation | More early S1 activity; C1 service may be better prepared, but a scarce C1 slot is held before readiness | Target’s post-Assessment C1 wait; possibly C1 orientation time | C1 queue for urgent non-target cases if reserved capacity is unused or displaced; S1 advisory queue | Urgent high-risk/high-value cases awaiting C1 | Mixed, potentially negative at peak load | A reservation has opportunity cost and is valuable only if the case reaches C1 near the slot |
| K-219 route to D2 | D2 demand rises; D1 demand falls; expected clarification rework may add D2/A2 work | D1 queue and cases waiting for D1 | D2 queue; A2 queue if earlier documents arrive in bursts | Cases routed to D2, especially handwritten or complex files; A2 cases after rework | Mixed and variance-increasing | The faster start is offset discontinuously when one ambiguous file triggers an external wait |
| K-337 suppress second rework | Removes one external response, one D1 check, one A1 assessment, and their downstream handoffs | External-response, D1, A1, and possibly C1 queues | Later conditional-monitoring, remediation, or appeal workload rather than necessarily an immediate service queue | Other D1/A1/C1 cases may benefit from released capacity; affected parties bear more decision risk | Faster operational flow but potentially negative quality/risk effect | Avoided rework is not avoided total work if unresolved evidence returns after decision |
| K-411 controlled parallel verification | Same verification service volume, earlier D2 arrival, and more simultaneous open tasks | Case-lock/document-release wait | A2 assessment queue when many cases become ready together | Non-target cases behind the A2 burst; audit staff if logging exceptions rise | Small positive locally, neutral or negative at saturation | Moving readiness earlier crosses an A2 capacity threshold and creates a wave |

For 100 similar cases, the main issue is that local savings do not add linearly. K-104’s provisional C1 reservations consume the only fully certified compliance resource’s scheduling flexibility. If packets arrive incomplete, C1 may have idle reserved gaps; if they mature together, the reservations displace other urgent cases and lengthen the C1 queue. Preliminary work can reduce per-case review effort, but it cannot create certified review hours.

K-219 transfers load from D1 to D2. That may be attractive when D1 is the local bottleneck, but D2’s queue can become the new bottleneck. A small clarification probability creates a large tail because external responses range from 8 to 36 hours and may cross a weekend. Rework also consumes Assessment capacity, so the average may improve while the tail and quality variance worsen.

K-337 removes 100 second loops, freeing external-response slots, D1, A1, and handoff capacity. It also creates 100 decisions with known unresolved discrepancies. Conditional monitoring, appeals, remediation, or losses can reintroduce work outside the original process and may exceed the saved service time. Weekend effects are avoided operationally but not necessarily eliminated from later supplier or appeal handling.

K-411 creates earlier simultaneous releases into Assessment. With 100 cases, A2 is likely to become the binding queue even if D2 and I2 have spare capacity; non-target cases are displaced. Real-time timestamps can establish true concurrency, whereas batch entry can make apparent throughput look better without changing work. Thus the rollout needs queue-aware throttling, not unconditional parallel release.

Final synthesis\n\nK-411 appears most robust. Its counterfactual is structurally valid for this non-original, standard, non-high-risk case: it restores an allowed parallel relationship that the legacy lock accidentally removed. It does not weaken compliance or decision rules, and it is easy to turn off. Its gain may disappear into the A2 queue, but that is measurable and operationally reversible.\n\nK-337 is the most dangerous despite its attractive speed. Suppressing the second information loop removes a weekend delay and substantial service work, but it forces a decision with a known discrepancy. A conditional approval or rejection can be defensible only when the residual uncertainty is controlled; otherwise the saved cycle time is purchased with exposure that may surface later as loss, appeal, remediation, or regulatory criticism.\n\nThe least transportable local result is K-104’s apparent benefit from reserving a C1 slot. One case can profit from a protected slot, while 100 reservations compete for one certified reviewer and displace urgent cases. K-411 also illustrates a second transportability limit: earlier readiness is not earlier completion once a shared Assessment queue saturates. K-219’s clean-path gain likewise cannot be generalized without its handwriting clarification tail.\n\nThe most valuable additional data are event-level task creation, queue entry, service start, completion, and handoff timestamps, joined to resource calendars. For K-104, packet completeness, C1 preparation time, and displaced-case priority are critical. For K-219, handwriting difficulty, clarification reason, rework, and downstream error outcomes matter. For K-337, discrepancy materiality, conditional-control performance, appeals, and realized losses matter. For K-411, true overlap and A2 queue displacement distinguish throughput from timestamp illusion.\n\n## 8. Assumption audit\n\n1. **structural** — Register and Triage are completed before any verification task is serviceable.\n2. **structural** — In no-originals cases, Identity Check and Document Check are permitted to overlap once both tasks are created.\n3. **temporal** — The snapshot queue estimates are used as approximate waits and can vary with arrivals, priorities, breaks, and training.\n4. **resource** — C1 remains the only fully certified final compliance reviewer during the relevant window, and M1 remains the decision signer.\n5. **behavioral** — An early K-104 packet can be read by S1/C1 while verification proceeds, but preliminary work does not itself authorize a final compliance decision.\n6. **quality** — D2 has a higher clarification or interpretation risk on handwritten documents than D1, while a clean D2 read is otherwise acceptable.\n7. **quality** — In K-337, the unresolved discrepancy is material enough that it must be surfaced in the decision rather than silently treated as resolved.\n8. **interference** — At 100 cases, earlier readiness, reserved capacity, rework, and batch timing can migrate bottlenecks and displace non-target cases.
