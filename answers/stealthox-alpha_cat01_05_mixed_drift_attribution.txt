## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The raw–mapped fitness gap measures how much of the apparent March failure is bookkeeping. In January–February the two series coincide (~0.92–0.93). From March, raw fitness loses ~0.37–0.44 while mapped fitness never falls below 0.86, so label mapping plus deduplication recovers roughly 93–96% of the raw loss in March–April. The gap therefore implies that the normative model no longer matches the emitted labels — `Validate Eligibility` was renamed `Eligibility Screen` and a technical `Open Case` was duplicated — and by itself says nothing about whether the work actually changed.

It also does not prove the process was unchanged. A mapping is an assumption of semantic equivalence: if the migrated screen performed fewer checks, mapped fitness would still fall. It does fall later (0.88 by May, 0.86 by July), which is genuine behavioral drift that no label repair removes.

Duplicates and missing resources damage different analyses. Duplicated `Open Case` events inflate frequency-weighted artifacts — DFG edge counts, token-based fitness, events per case — while barely affecting variant structure or event ordering. Missing resources leave control-flow conformance untouched but invalidate handover matrices, per-team and per-resource performance, and workload views; because missingness concentrates in migrated activities it is non-random, so complete-case resource comparisons are biased, not merely noisier.

Post-May patterns cannot be logging alone: distinct labels stay fixed at 11 after March; duplicate and missingness rates move by single percentage points while rework doubles and median cycle time rises ~45%; the rework loop appears in mapped, deduplicated DFG rates; and service times come from operational tables unaffected by renaming. Logging drift explains the March step; it cannot generate the May–July rework hump, the June queueing inflection, or the October senior-assessment surge.

**4. Candidate change points per drift type.**
- Logging drift: (a) **2026-03-01** — step in labels (911), raw fitness, duplicates (04%), missing resources (14.8%); (b) **2026-06/07** — missing-resource peak (6.26.5%) suggests a second, weaker logging regime, confounded with staffing redistribution of work.
- Behavioral drift: (a) **2026-05-05** — rework loop doubles (1224/100); (b) **2026-07-01** — priority scheduling policy (queue discipline is system behavior); a third gradual candidate is senior-routing creep (59/100 between March and July, before the seasonal mix shift).
- Performance drift: (a) **2026-06-15** — median slope steepens sharply (MayJun +4.3h vs AprMay +0.8h) with flat service time; (b) **2026-10-01** — second shock (p90 96116h, volume +13%); July 1 is an additional tail-specific candidate (p90 grows faster than median in Jul–Aug).

**5. One global change point or many?** The data support **multiple mechanism-specific change points**, not one global point. The artifacts peak at different times: rework peaks in July, Senior Assessment in October, missing resources in July, mapped-fitness dips at May and October, cycle time in November. A single global change point (e.g., March) cannot generate this staggered pattern, and March is demonstrably semantic. Confirmation requires per-metric CUSUM/PELT on case-level data with seasonal covariates; on aggregates this remains an inference, not a test result.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 — full mapped log | all cases (labels mapped, duplicates removed) | Reference population, headline conformance and KPIs | all cases | Raw vs normalized; month over month | Aggregation masks heterogeneity (Simpson effects) |
| L1a — month × supplier status | Z vs non-Z | Localize supplier rework vs supplier mix | 30 cases/cell; else collapse to quarter | Within-supplier DiD on rework | Supplier tied to region/complexity |
| L1b — month × complexity class | high vs standard | Separate mix from routing/cycle behavior | 30 cases/cell | SA routing per HC case; class cycle times | Complexity flag misclassification |
| L1c — month × priority class | priority class | Detect redistribution of delay | 30 cases/cell | Class waiting pre/post Jul 1 | Priority correlates with complexity; do not condition on post-assessment attributes |
| L2 — activity × team × month | resource, activity | Resource completeness, handover, service time | 200 events/cell | Missingness by activity×team; workload shifts | MNAR missingness biases complete-case views |
| L3 — seasonal strata | calendar month, prior-year windows | Recurring-vs-new discrimination | 1 prior year (3 preferred); else seasonal indices from legacy logs | Same-month YoY drift tests | Year-specific shocks contaminate the seasonal baseline |

Fallback rule: any cell below support is merged into its parent stratum and flagged, so no statistic is computed on tiny cells.

**Primary monitoring population:** the mapped, deduplicated full log stratified by supplier-Z status × complexity class, with the **non-Z / standard-complexity stratum as the primary sensitive control population**. It is large (~55–65% of ~700 cases/month  400+ cases), unexposed to the supplier shock and less exposed to the seasonal complexity swing, so drift there signals organization-wide causes quickly; the exposed strata (Z, HC) are monitored in parallel to localize supplier and seasonal mechanisms. This balances sensitivity and specificity while avoiding both overaggregation and tiny cells.

No universal threshold is used: each monitor's threshold comes from its own historical null distribution (quantiles, CUSUM limits, or EWMA control limits) computed on a stable reference window. **Seasonality** is handled by seasonal reference windows (same calendar month in prior years, STL residuals on weekly series) and by requiring sustained deviation (2–3 consecutive windows) before paging. **Multiple testing** is handled by a two-tier design — population-level gates (DFG distance, conformance, p90 waiting, rework CUSUM) fire first, and drill-down monitors are only evaluated when a gate triggers — plus Benjamini–Hochberg FDR control at 5% across simultaneously active monitors, with alert history used to estimate the empirical false-discovery rate.

    return base


def l1(a, b): return sum(abs(x - y) for x, y in zip(a, b))
def l2(a, b): return math.sqrt(sum((x - y) ** 2 for x, y in zip(a, b)))
def cosine(a, b):
    num = sum(x * y for x, y in zip(a, b))
    den = math.sqrt(sum(x * x for x in a)) * math.sqrt(sum(y * y for y in b))
    return num / den if den else float("nan")


def dfg_distance_analysis():

    for m, vec in DFG_RATES.items():

        top = sorted(deltas, key=lambda t: -abs(t[1]))[:3]

        print(f"{m:9s}{l1(vec, base):7.1f}{l2(vec, base):7.1f}"

def fitness_recovery():

def flag_change_points(series, k=3.5):
    """(A5) Explainable rule: flag months whose first difference deviates
    from the median difference by more than k * MAD (robust step detector)."""
    diffs = [series[i] - series[i - 1] for i in range(1, len(series))]
    med = stats.median(diffs)
    mad = stats.median([abs(d - med) for d in diffs]) or 1e-9
    return [i + 1 for i, d in enumerate(diffs) if abs(d - med) > k * mad]


def change_point_scan():
    months = [r[COL["month"]] for r in MONTHLY_SUMMARY]
    print("\ncandidate change points (robust MAD rule on month-to-month diffs)")
    for k in ("rework", "median", "p90", "supplier", "missing", "dup",
              "mapped", "hc"):
        vals = [r[COL[k]] for r in MONTHLY_SUMMARY]
        idx = flag_change_points(vals)
        hits = ", ".join(f"{months[i]} (delta {vals[i] - vals[i - 1]:+.2f})"
                         for i in idx) or "none"
        print(f"  {k:9s}: {hits}")
    print("  note: step detector only; gradual onsets (e.g., the June queueing"
          " ramp) require CUSUM/PELT on event-level data.")

def normalized_copy(log_df, mapping=LABEL_MAP, dedup_open_case=True,
                    dedup_window="10min"):
    """(B7a) Return a normalized COPY; the original log is never overwritten.
    Relabels migrated activities and drops near-duplicate consecutive
    'Open Case' technical events within the same case and time window."""
    out = log_df.copy()
    out[ACT] = out[ACT].replace(mapping)
    if dedup_open_case:
        out = out.sort_values([CASE, TS]).reset_index(drop=True)
        ts = pd.to_datetime(out[TS])

               & out[CASE].eq(out[CASE].shift())
               & out[ACT].eq(out[ACT].shift())
               & ((ts - ts.shift()).abs() <= pd.Timedelta(dedup_window)))

## 9. Provisional attribution

**Strongest conclusions.** (1) The March fitness collapse is logging/semantic drift (confidence ~90%): the label rename, duplicated `Open Case`, and resource-field loss fully account for it — mapping recovers 93–96% of the raw loss and the mapped DFG distance versus baseline is ~3 per 100, i.e., behavior was essentially intact in March. (2) The May rework doubling is real behavioral drift triggered by Supplier Z's disruption (~75%): the timing, the doubled Request-Info loop (1224/100), the mapped-fitness dip, and the partial reversal after September 10 all align. (3) The June–August cycle deterioration is queueing, not slower work (high confidence): Assess service moved only 4.85.4h while the median rose ~14h; the staffing cut and rising load are the natural drivers.

**Only plausible.** The October–November pattern as seasonal case-mix drift is strongly supported circumstantially (historical pattern, magnitudes, December recede), but the senior-assessment rate per high-complexity case also rose (~19~54 per 100 HC cases), implying a behavioral or recording component; the historically inconsistent complexity flag keeps a semantic alternative alive. Persistent behavioral adaptation (elevated September–December rework and senior routing versus baseline) is plausible but confounded by residual backlog and mix. The priority rule's redistribution effect is plausible and directionally supported (tail growing faster than the median after July 1) but unverified in aggregates.

**Where mediation/interaction blocks clean attribution.** Supplier share mediates aggregate rework (population and behavior entangled); rework consumes assessor capacity, mediating the staffingqueueing path; the priority rule interacts with the rising HC share so its benefit erodes exactly when seasonality strikes, and monthly aggregates hide the harmed standard-class cohort (Simpson); December improvement is fully confounded between seasonal receding, overtime, and the quality campaign — no clean credit can be assigned.

**What would make me revise.** Flat within-segment rework in non-Z cases would reclassify May as population drift only; class-level waiting showing no post-July divergence would drop the redistribution claim; prior-year Oct–Dec logs showing similar senior-assessment surges would reclassify October as purely recurring; random missingness by team would rehabilitate resource metrics; a formal changepoint dating the queueing inflection to May rather than June would shift primacy from staffing to supplier backlog.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. `IF` raw fitness falls >0.15 within one month while mapped fitness stays within 0.03 of its trailing baseline and distinct activity labels increase, `THEN` classify the event as logging/semantic drift, apply the label map and deduplication, and re-baseline all raw-series dashboards, `BECAUSE` label substitution and duplicated technical events break conformance checking without any change in actual behavior.
2. `IF` the AssessRequest InfoCollect Documents loop rate exceeds 1.5× its seasonal baseline for two consecutive weeks, `THEN` segment the loop rate by supplier and open a supplier data-quality ticket with a bundle-completeness audit, `BECAUSE` incomplete supplier bundles mechanically generate re-request loops, and within-segment rates separate supplier behavior from supplier mix.
3. `IF` median queue waiting before Assess rises >20% while Assess service time stays within 10% of baseline, `THEN` declare queueing drift and trigger a capacity review (overtime, reallocation) rather than assessor-performance actions, `BECAUSE` stable service times with rising waiting indicate a utilization (capacity/demand) problem, not slower processing.
4. `IF` high-complexity waiting falls while standard-complexity waiting rises after a queue-priority change, `THEN` report the change as redistribution rather than improvement and add a standing standard-class p90 alert, `BECAUSE` priority reordering shifts delay between classes and monthly aggregates hide the harmed cohort (Simpson's paradox).
5. `IF` high-complexity share, senior-assessment routing, or volume deviates >2 from the same-calendar-month seasonal expectation, `THEN` classify it as population/seasonal drift, re-baseline cycle-time and conformance targets, and compare against prior-year months before declaring new drift, `BECAUSE` case mix changes both control flow and durations, and recurring seasonality mimics structural change.
6. `IF` missing-resource rates rise only in migrated activities while label and event counts remain stable, `THEN` quarantine resource-based analyses (handover matrices, per-team KPIs), run complete-case sensitivity checks, and file a data-quality incident, `BECAUSE` non-random missingness biases resource analyses but does not by itself indicate process change.
7. `IF` any metric improves inside a known intervention window (e.g., December), `THEN` require confirmation against the seasonal baseline and the intervention log before crediting the intervention, and keep the monitor armed for relapse next season, `BECAUSE` seasonal receding and active interventions are confounded and the improvement may reflect timing of recurrence, not treatment effect.
