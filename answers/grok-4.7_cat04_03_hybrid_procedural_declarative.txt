# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

Assumptions used by the implemented model:

- A case is one completed vendor onboarding. Every test trace is complete. `Close Onboarding` occurs exactly once. In-flight cases are outside the hard rules that require a successor before close; they need prefix checking, not this model unchanged.
- The backbone is an exclusive choice after `Classify Risk`: reject, low-risk acceptance, or high-risk acceptance. An accepted case is one that executes `Activate Vendor`. A rejected case is one that executes `Reject Vendor`.
- `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, and `Verify Bank Account` are all mandatory. Capture and screening occur once. Verify occurs once on the backbone and may occur a second time only under R11.
- "Final outcome" in R3 means the first of `Reject Vendor` and `Activate Vendor`. `Close Onboarding` is administrative completion after that decision.
- R2 and R8 use "should", while R12 explicitly says flag-but-do-not-reject. R2 and R8 are treated as hard constraints because they restate the exclusive route. R12 stays soft.
- R9 compares exact `org:resource` identity. The applicable approval is `Manager Approval` on the low-risk route and `Compliance Approval` on the high-risk route. If both approvals occur, segregation is checked against each of them, and the case has already broken R8.
- Each of `Sanctions Escalation` and `Obtain Legal Opinion` is assumed to occur at most once, so "the opinion precedes activation" is a single timestamp comparison. The written requirements do not state that cap.
- Elapsed time is continuous wall-clock time. No business calendar is given. One hour is 3600 seconds. Equal timestamps do not satisfy "precedes".
- `support` and `confidence` on the declarative rules are normative modeler assignments, not frequencies mined from these eight traces.
- Clarification cycles are unbounded in the business rule. The procedural model admits them only in the pre-classification window used by the conforming examples. A finite log-skeleton frequency set is a checker accommodation, not a business cap.
- Post-activation `Change Bank Details` / `Suspend Vendor` / a second `Verify Bank Account` are real behavior under R11 and are intentionally outside the backbone language.

R5 and R6 cannot be true of the same total function over these logs. R5 requires `Obtain Legal Opinion` before `Activate Vendor` whenever `Sanctions Escalation` occurred. R6 allows the opposite order, but only with joint authorization that is not an event-log attribute, and then requires the opinion within 24 hours. The log cannot show that authorization. A model that deletes the order rule in order to "implement" R6 accepts every premature activation, authorized or not.

Resolution A, legal-strict, is the implemented policy. If escalation and activation both occur, `Obtain Legal Opinion` must exist and its timestamp must be strictly earlier than `Activate Vendor`. There is no log-internal emergency exemption. The 24-hour figure is not read as authorization.

Resolution B, operations exemption with a fail-closed join. Activation may precede the opinion only when an external authorization record for that case says the activation was jointly authorized, and the opinion timestamp is at most 24 hours after `Activate Vendor`. A missing record is a violation. Being inside the 24-hour window is not evidence of authorization. On T6 the opinion is 10 hours after activation and 17 hours after escalation, so both clocks would be satisfied, and the case would still fail closed unless the authorization row is actually joined.

A third presentation option is quarantine: the case is held for legal review and is not straight-through processed. That is a workflow reaction to Resolution A, not a third semantics, and it does not itself append `Reject Vendor`.

Compliance risk of choosing A. A genuinely authorized emergency is dispositioned as a violation because the proof is outside the log. Operators may answer by backdating an opinion or by activating outside the system. Those failures are at least visible. Choosing B without a reliable join would hide unauthorized early activation, including T6 as it stands. The implemented stack therefore keeps A, records B as an explicit alternative checker, and never treats a timestamp as a proxy for the missing authorization attribute.

# 2. Requirement-to-Formalism Mapping

The authoritative layer is the one that decides the case when layers disagree. Other encodings are monitors and must be revised together with the authority.

| Requirement | Primary formalism | Exact encoding | Why this formalism | Redundancy or limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | DECLARE `noncoexistence` | `("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor")` | The rule is co-occurrence, independent of order. `nonsuccession` would still allow the reverse order. | The process-tree XOR and log-skeleton `never_together` repeat it. |
| R2 | Process tree | XOR of reject (neither approval), low-risk (`Manager Approval` then activate), and high-risk (`Enhanced Due Diligence` then `Compliance Approval` then activate). | The tree makes the route and "exactly one approval on the accept path" generative. | DECLARE `noncoexistence` covers only mutual exclusion and exclusion with reject. It does not say an accepted case has at least one of the two approvals. An external counter duplicates the count for auditors. |
| R3 | DECLARE `altresponse` and `altprecedence`, plus an external matcher | `("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification")` in both alternate templates. The matcher requires each response before the next request and before the final outcome. | Alternation is a declarative correlation. "Before the final outcome" refers to a third activity, which alternate response does not mention. | The tree allows only contiguous pairs in the pre-classification window, so it is stricter than the text and can reject a legally paired clarification that sits elsewhere. `chainresponse` is intentionally unused: the response need not be the next event. Log-skeleton equivalence repeats the count only. |
| R4 | DECLARE `response` | `("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion")` | Escalation obliges a later opinion. `responded_existence` would accept an opinion that only occurred before the escalation. | Log-skeleton `always_after` repeats response-strength, not the activation deadline. Equivalence of the two activities was rejected because an extra opinion would be a different rule. |
| R5 | External conditional check, under Resolution A | If escalation and activation both occur, an opinion timestamp must be strictly earlier than activation. | No standard DECLARE template scopes "opinion before activate" to "only if escalation occurred". | Unconditional `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` would reject every ordinary acceptance. `nonsuccession(Activate Vendor, Obtain Legal Opinion)` was rejected as the authority: it also condemns a post-activation opinion when no escalation occurred, and it cannot see an authorization attribute. The tree embeds A inside the acceptance branches only. |
| R6 | External check, not enabled as a waiver | Join an authorization record that is absent from the log; require the opinion within 24 hours after activation; missing record fails closed. | The deciding attribute is not in the event log, so no activity-only formalism can apply the exemption safely. | The temporal profile's upper band for escalation to opinion is also 24 hours, but it is measured from escalation, it is two-sided in form, and R12 makes it a warning. It is not an implementation of R6. |
| R7 | DECLARE `precedence` | `("Run Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation")` | This is exactly "escalation only after screening", and it is vacuous when there is no escalation. | The tree allows escalation only after classification on an acceptance branch, which is narrower than R7. A pre-classification escalation can satisfy DECLARE and still fail the tree. |
| R8 | DECLARE `noncoexistence` | `("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Manager Approval")` and `("Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval")` | Both sentences are co-occurrence bans. | The XOR repeats them. Log-skeleton `never_together` repeats them. |
| R9 | Resource layer | Custom segregation check. `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` is the library form for one concrete pair. | Resources are not activities. | Vacuous when the approval or the activation does not occur. Fail closed when the activity occurs and `org:resource` is missing. |
| R10 | Log skeleton `activ_freq` | `Activate Vendor`, `Reject Vendor`, and `Release First Payment` each map to `{0, 1}`. | DECLARE `absence` means zero occurrences. `exactly_one` forbids the reject path or the accept path. Frequency sets are the cardinality tool. | The tree also generates each of them at most once. There is no separate at-most-one DECLARE template in the set used here. |
| R11 | External trace walk | After activation, each `Change Bank Details` must be followed by `Suspend Vendor`. Any later `Release First Payment` must have a `Verify Bank Account` strictly between that change and the payment. | The obligation is conditional on position relative to activation. A first verification already precedes payment on every backbone accept path, so global precedence of verify before payment does not force the second verification. | Unconditional DECLARE `response(Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor)` was rejected: it would also oblige a suspension for a pre-activation change. The backbone whitelist does not contain these events; a matching R11 pattern is an explicit override, not a silent pass. |
| R12 | Temporal profile | The three pairs below, in seconds, with `zeta = 2.0`. | The requirement gives mean, standard deviation, and a zeta test, and it says to flag rather than reject. | A control-flow encoding would turn T7 into a hard failure. The profile does not see resources, authorization, or unmatched requests. |

Backbone steps 1 through 5 are authoritative in the process tree of section 3. DECLARE precedence from each of the three checks to `Classify Risk` is a redundant monitor for step 3.

# 3. Procedural Backbone

The implemented language is block-structured, so the canonical object is a process tree. Sequence is `→`, parallel is `∧`, exclusive choice is `×`, loop is `⟳`, and `τ` is a silent step.

```text
→(
  Receive Onboarding Request,
  ∧(Capture Master Data, Run Sanctions Screening, Verify Bank Account),
  ⟳(τ, →(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)),
  Classify Risk,
  ×(
    →(Reject Vendor, Close Onboarding),
    →(
      ∧(Manager Approval, ×(τ, →(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion))),
      Activate Vendor,
      Release First Payment,
      Close Onboarding
    ),
    →(
      ∧(
        →(Enhanced Due Diligence, Compliance Approval),
        ×(τ, →(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion))
      ),
      Activate Vendor,
      Release First Payment,
      Close Onboarding
    )
  )
)
```

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator, ProcessTree

def leaf(label):
    return ProcessTree(label=label)

def silent():
    return ProcessTree()

def op(operator, children):
    node = ProcessTree(operator=operator)
    node.children = list(children)
    for child in node.children:
        child.parent = node
    return node

def seq(*children):
    return op(Operator.SEQUENCE, children)

def par(*children):
    return op(Operator.PARALLEL, children)

def xor(*children):
    return op(Operator.XOR, children)

def loop(do, redo):
    return op(Operator.LOOP, [do, redo])

def optional_escalation():
    # New nodes on every call: a process-tree node has one parent.
    return xor(
        silent(),
        seq(leaf("Sanctions Escalation"), leaf("Obtain Legal Opinion")),
    )

procedural_model = seq(
    leaf("Receive Onboarding Request"),
    par(
        leaf("Capture Master Data"),
        leaf("Run Sanctions Screening"),
        leaf("Verify Bank Account"),
    ),
    loop(
        silent(),
        seq(leaf("Request Clarification"), leaf("Receive Clarification")),
    ),
    leaf("Classify Risk"),
    xor(
        seq(leaf("Reject Vendor"), leaf("Close Onboarding")),
        seq(
            par(leaf("Manager Approval"), optional_escalation()),
            leaf("Activate Vendor"),
            leaf("Release First Payment"),
            leaf("Close Onboarding"),
        ),
        seq(
            par(
                seq(
                    leaf("Enhanced Due Diligence"),
                    leaf("Compliance Approval"),
                ),
                optional_escalation(),
            ),
            leaf("Activate Vendor"),
            leaf("Release First Payment"),
            leaf("Close Onboarding"),
        ),
    ),
)
```

Concurrency. After receipt, the three checks are one parallel block. All six orders are in the language. The block joins before any clarification and before `Classify Risk`.

Choices. Classification is followed by exactly one of the three routes. The low-risk route has `Manager Approval` and not `Compliance Approval`. The high-risk route has `Enhanced Due Diligence` then `Compliance Approval` and not `Manager Approval`. The reject route has neither approval, no activation, and no first payment. Acceptance always continues `Activate Vendor` → `Release First Payment` → `Close Onboarding`. Both accept routes and the reject route end in one close. The two `Activate Vendor` leaves, and the duplicated payment and close leaves, are distinct nodes with the same label because a tree node cannot have two parents. Alignment is on labels.

Clarification loops. The loop's do-child is silent and its redo-child is `Request Clarification` then `Receive Clarification`. That yields zero or more pairs. Inside this tree the two events of a pair are contiguous, and every pair sits after the three checks have all completed and before `Classify Risk`. That is stronger than R3, which allows intervening events and any window that still finishes before the final outcome. The tree is therefore a supported-window approximation. Alternation and the deadline are authoritative in DECLARE plus the external matcher. T2 is inside the window. T5 is inside the window and still fails, because the redo-child cannot stop between the request and the response.

Terminal outcomes. Rejection and activation are in different XOR branches, so the tree itself excludes R1. It also excludes a second activation, a second rejection, and a second first payment.

Escalation. On either acceptance branch, `×(τ, →(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion))` runs concurrently with the approval work and the whole parallel joins before `Activate Vendor`. Any interleaving of that pair with `Manager Approval`, or with `Enhanced Due Diligence` → `Compliance Approval`, is allowed, including T7's order `Sanctions Escalation`, `Enhanced Due Diligence`, `Obtain Legal Opinion`, `Compliance Approval`, `Activate Vendor`. T6's order is not in the language: the opinion cannot be executed after activation. This is the Resolution A embedding. It is narrower than the literal cross-cutting rules: escalation cannot appear on the reject branch, cannot appear between screening and classification, and the opinion is chained to the escalation inside the optional block. Those scope limits are the approximation. They are sound for every trace the tree accepts. They are not complete for every trace that satisfies only R4 and R7.

Post-activation behavior. `Change Bank Details`, `Suspend Vendor`, and a second `Verify Bank Account` are deliberately absent. R11 is not block-structured against the single backbone verification, and no supplied trace exercises it. Inserting those events is a procedural deviation unless the external R11 checker confirms the pattern and the deployment override in section 10 is applied.

# 4. DECLARE Model

Template names are the PM4Py keys. Unary rules use an activity key. Binary rules use `(source, target)`. Every constraint is normative, so `support` and `confidence` are both `1.0`. A discovery threshold must not be applied to this dictionary.

Semantics used below:

- `init`: the activity is the first event.
- `exactly_one`: the activity occurs once.
- `precedence(A, B)`: if B occurs, A occurs strictly earlier. Absent B is vacuous. This does not require B.
- `response(A, B)`: if A occurs, B occurs strictly later. Absent A is vacuous. This does not require A. It is stronger than `responded_existence`, which would allow B anywhere in the case.
- `succession(A, B)` would be the conjunction of `precedence(A, B)` and `response(A, B)`. The dictionary keeps the two directions as separate keys so a trace can fail one and satisfy the other.
- `altprecedence(A, B)`: every B is preceded by its own A, with no other B in between.
- `altresponse(A, B)`: every A is followed by its own B before the next A.
- `chainresponse` would require B to be the immediate next event. R3 does not say that, so it is not used. The process tree happens to be contiguous in its supported window; that strictness is a tree approximation, not a DECLARE rule.
- `noncoexistence(A, B)`: A and B never both occur, in either order. The template is symmetric; one tuple is stored.
- `nonsuccession(A, B)` would forbid A followed later by B and would still allow B followed by A, or either activity alone. That is the wrong operator for R1 and R8, and it was rejected as the encoding of R5.

| Rule | Direction | Semantics |
|---|---|---|
| `init(Receive Onboarding Request)` | Unary, position | Every case starts with receipt. Cardinality is separate. |

| `precedence(Classify Risk, Manager/EDD/Compliance/Reject)` | Classification before the route | A route activity implies an earlier classification. It does not force the route. |
| `precedence` and `response` on `(Enhanced Due Diligence, Compliance Approval)` | Both directions | High-risk pair is a succession. Either direction can fail alone. |
| `precedence` and `response` on `(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` | Both directions | Payment implies earlier activation, and activation on a completed case implies a later payment. T8 breaks the response direction only. |
| `response(Manager Approval, Activate Vendor)` and `response(Compliance Approval, Activate Vendor)` | Approval commits to activation | Vacuous if that approval is absent. Does not require an approval before every activation, so high-risk traces still satisfy the manager rule. |
| `response(Reject Vendor, Close Onboarding)` and `response(Release First Payment, Close Onboarding)` | Outcome is closed | Vacuous on the other route. |
| `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` | Escalation before a later opinion | Authoritative R4. Satisfied by T6 even though the opinion follows activation. |
| `altprecedence` and `altresponse` on clarification | Each response matches one open request, in order | Authoritative pairing for R3. Weaker `response` is not also stored. `chainresponse` is not used. |
| Five `noncoexistence` pairs | Symmetric ban | R1, the reject half of R2, and both R8 bans. Absence of either activity satisfies the rule. This is not `nonsuccession`. |

What DECLARE is not asked to decide: conditional R5, the R6 waiver, both segregation rules, the second-verification half of R11, and "the clarification response arrives before the final outcome". Those need timestamps, resources, or a third activity.

# 5. Log-Skeleton Model

Intended checker semantics, which deployment must confirm against the installed PM4Py version:

- `(a, b)` in `always_before` means every occurrence of `b` has a strictly earlier `a`. If `b` is absent, the pair is vacuous. This is precedence-shaped. It is not "a and b always co-occur".
- `(a, b)` in `always_after` means every occurrence of `a` has a strictly later `b`. If `a` is absent, the pair is vacuous. This is response-shaped, not alternate response.
- The same ordered pair may appear in one of those relations or in both. Both means succession. Example: `(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` is in both. `(Reject Vendor, Close Onboarding)` is only in `always_after`, because accept-path closures have no rejection. `(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` is only in `always_before`, because screening does not imply escalation.
- `(a, b)` in `never_together` means the two counts are not both positive. Both orientations are stored because some checkers look up the tuple as written. The relation is symmetric and corresponds to `noncoexistence`, not to `nonsuccession`.
- `(a, b)` in `equivalence` means `count(a) = count(b)`, with absent activities counted as 0. Both orientations are stored. Equal counts say nothing about order.
- `directly_follows` is a whitelist of permitted adjacent pairs, not a list of mandatory chains. A trace adjacency outside the set is a deviation. The empty set must not be used: a whitelist checker would then reject every adjacent pair.
- `activ_freq` maps an activity to the set of allowed counts. Activities omitted from the key are unconstrained only if the wrapper treats omission that way. This dictionary lists every known label so that omission is not accidentally read as `{0}`.

```python
        ("Obtain Legal Opinion", "Enhanced Due Diligence"),
        ("Obtain Legal Opinion", "Compliance Approval"),
        ("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Compliance Approval"),
        ("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Sanctions Escalation"),
        ("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Obtain Legal Opinion"),
        ("Compliance Approval", "Sanctions Escalation"),
        ("Compliance Approval", "Obtain Legal Opinion"),
        ("Compliance Approval", "Activate Vendor"),
        ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"),
        ("Release First Payment", "Close Onboarding"),
    },
}
```

Frequency classes. The normative keys are the `{1}` anchors and the R10 keys `{0, 1}` for rejection, activation, and first payment. `Verify Bank Account: {1, 2}` reserves the R11 re-verification without making a third check legal. `{0, 1}` on escalation, opinion, due diligence, and both approvals matches the tree's optional single use; the written rules state that cap only indirectly. The ranges on clarification, bank change, and suspension are accommodations so a whitelist-style frequency check does not treat those labels as forbidden. They are not business maxima. Clarification equivalence remains well-defined at count 0, which is how T1 and T3 satisfy `Request Clarification ~ Receive Clarification`.

Overlap and disagreement.

- `always_before(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` matches DECLARE R7 and is vacuous on T1–T5 and T8. The direct-follows whitelist is stricter: screening is not an allowed predecessor of escalation, because the tree places escalation after classification. If those two disagree, R7's authority is DECLARE. The direct-follows failure means "outside the supported window".
- `always_after(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` matches DECLARE response. It does not say the opinion precedes activation. T6 satisfies it.
- `equivalence(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` is intentionally absent. T6 would also satisfy it (counts 1 and 1) while still violating Resolution A, so the count relation would be a poor signal.
- `equivalence(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` catches T5's unmatched request as a count defect. It would accept one request and one response in the wrong order. Order belongs to DECLARE `altprecedence`.
- `never_together` repeats R1, R2's reject exclusions, and R8. The reject/due-diligence and reject/payment pairs are extra backbone invariants. They should be dropped if the XOR is ever widened. They are not a second definition of R2.
- Direct-follows is the adjacency projection of the process tree, including the six check orders, the clarification window, and the Resolution A shuffles of the optional escalation block with the approval work. It duplicates the tree on ordering. Its T6 violations are the adjacencies `Activate Vendor → Obtain Legal Opinion` and `Obtain Legal Opinion → Release First Payment`. Those are the policy-A ban showing up a second time, not a finding that the opinion is missing.
- Direct-follows has no edges for `Change Bank Details` or `Suspend Vendor`. A trace can satisfy the frequency accommodation and still fail the whitelist. The external R11 checker is the authority for that pattern.

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (172800, 86400),
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): (43200, 21600),
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"): (259200, 129600),
}
zeta = 2.0
```

The values are the R12 hour figures converted to seconds: 48h and 24h, 12h and 6h, 72h and 36h. `pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(log, temporal_profile, zeta=2.0)` expects timestamp deltas in seconds. Hour offsets used as if they were seconds will not implement this profile.

A pair is scored when a source occurrence has a later target. The intended pairing is each source with the next unmatched target. The deviation is `z = |Δ - mean| / sd`, and the case is flagged when `z > 2.0`. For every published pair, `mean - 2·sd = 0` and the upper bounds are 96 hours, 24 hours, and 144 hours. With a strict `>` test, the whole interval from a zero duration through the upper bound is inside the band. The practical effect is to flag excessive delay. A zero duration sits exactly on `z = 2.0` and is not flagged.

These flags are warnings. R12 says the expectations are normative enough to flag and not normative enough to reject the case automatically. The profile also has nothing to say about an unmatched source: T5's open clarification is a control-flow defect, and the missing response produces no delta. A late opinion that is still in the legal order (T7, 34 hours, `z = 22/6 ≈ 3.67`) remains a backbone-conformant case with a warning. A fast first payment at 2 hours has `z ≈ 1.94`, which is inside the band.

The escalation–opinion upper bound of 24 hours numerically resembles R6. It is a different measurement. The profile starts at escalation, is a warning, and does not read an authorization attribute. R6, if adopted, would start at emergency activation and would be a hard external condition.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

Required event attributes are `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `time:timestamp`, and `org:resource`. The authorization table is not an event attribute. It is an external row keyed by case id, with `joint_authorization` (boolean), `authorized_by`, and `authorized_at`.

Missing-attribute policy:

- Missing case id or activity label: data-quality failure. Do not score the event as compliant.
- Missing timestamp on an activity that a check must order: that check fails closed. Do not treat the order as satisfied.
- Activity absent: the obligation that is conditional on that activity is vacuous and passes. Control-flow layers still handle the missing backbone activity.
- Activity present and `org:resource` missing: segregation fails closed. A blank resource is not "different".
- Authorization row missing while Resolution B is being asked to waive an order violation: fail closed. Do not infer the waiver from the 24-hour gap.

`pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, activity1, activity2)` keeps traces in which the two activities are performed by different resources. Use it only on the subset of cases that contain both activities. If a library version drops cases that lack one of the two activities, that drop is a false failure and the explicit walk below is the specification. The library call cannot express "whichever approval this route used", so the low-risk pair and the high-risk pair are selected in code.

```python
import pandas as pd

CASE = "case:concept:name"
ACT = "concept:name"
TS = "time:timestamp"
RES = "org:resource"

FINAL_OUTCOMES = ("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor")
APPROVALS = ("Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval")

def _events(df, case_id):
    part = df[df[CASE] == case_id].sort_values(TS, kind="mergesort")
    return list(part.itertuples(index=False))

def _times(events, label):
    return [getattr(e, TS) for e in events if getattr(e, ACT) == label]

def _resources(events, label):
    found = []
    for e in events:
        if getattr(e, ACT) != label:
            continue
        resource = getattr(e, RES, None)
        if resource is None or (isinstance(resource, float) and pd.isna(resource)) or resource == "":
            return None  # present but unknown: caller fails closed
        found.append(resource)
    return found

def check_r9(events):
    """Segregation. Vacuous if the restricted activity does not occur."""
    failures = []
    verifiers = _resources(events, "Verify Bank Account")
    if verifiers is None:
        failures.append("R9 missing resource on Verify Bank Account")
    else:
        for approval in APPROVALS:
            approvers = _resources(events, approval)
            if approvers is None:
                failures.append(f"R9 missing resource on {approval}")
            elif verifiers and approvers and set(verifiers) & set(approvers):
                failures.append(f"R9 same resource on Verify Bank Account and {approval}")
    capturers = _resources(events, "Capture Master Data")
    activators = _resources(events, "Activate Vendor")
    if capturers is None:
        failures.append("R9 missing resource on Capture Master Data")
    if activators is None:
        failures.append("R9 missing resource on Activate Vendor")
    if capturers and activators and set(capturers) & set(activators):
        failures.append("R9 same resource on Capture Master Data and Activate Vendor")
    return failures

def check_r3_deadline(events):
    """Alternate matching, and every response before the final outcome."""
    failures = []
    open_requests = []
    outcome_at = next(
        (getattr(e, TS) for e in events if getattr(e, ACT) in FINAL_OUTCOMES),
        None,
    )
    for e in events:
        label = getattr(e, ACT)
        stamp = getattr(e, TS)
        if label == "Request Clarification":
            if outcome_at is not None and stamp >= outcome_at:
                failures.append("R3 request at or after final outcome")
            open_requests.append(stamp)
        elif label == "Receive Clarification":
            if not open_requests:
                failures.append("R3 response without an open request")
            else:
                open_requests.pop(0)
                if outcome_at is not None and stamp >= outcome_at:
                    failures.append("R3 response at or after final outcome")
    if open_requests:
        failures.append("R3 request left unanswered")
    return failures

def check_r2_counts(events):
    """Auditor duplicate of the route counts. The tree remains authoritative."""
    n = lambda label: sum(getattr(e, ACT) == label for e in events)
    rejected = n("Reject Vendor")
    activated = n("Activate Vendor")
    approvals = n("Manager Approval") + n("Compliance Approval")
    failures = []
    if rejected and activated:
        failures.append("R1/R2 both terminal outcomes")
    if rejected and approvals:
        failures.append("R2 approval inside a rejected case")
    if activated and approvals != 1:
        failures.append("R2 accepted case lacks exactly one approval")
    return failures

def check_r5_r6(events, policy, authorization):
    """
    policy "legal_strict": Resolution A.
    policy "operations_emergency": Resolution B, fail closed without authorization.
    """
    esc = _times(events, "Sanctions Escalation")
    opinion = _times(events, "Obtain Legal Opinion")
    activate = _times(events, "Activate Vendor")
    if not esc:
        return []
    if not opinion:
        return ["R4/R5 escalation without a legal opinion"]
    if not activate:
        return []  # R5's activation deadline is vacuous; R4 still applies elsewhere
    # At-most-once assumption: compare the recorded pair.
    if opinion[0] < activate[0]:
        return []
    if policy == "legal_strict":
        return ["R5 opinion does not precede activation after escalation"]
    if policy != "operations_emergency":
        return ["unknown R5/R6 policy"]
    if authorization is None or not authorization.get("joint_authorization", False):
        return ["R6 fail-closed: joint authorization record missing"]
    if activate[0] < opinion[0] and (opinion[0] - activate[0]) <= pd.Timedelta(hours=24):
        return []
    return ["R6 opinion later than 24 hours after emergency activation"]

def check_r11(events):
    activations = _times(events, "Activate Vendor")
    if not activations:
        changes = _times(events, "Change Bank Details")
        if changes:
            return ["R11 bank change with no activation; pattern does not apply as written"]
        return []
    activation_at = activations[0]
    failures = []
    changes = [
        e for e in events
        if getattr(e, ACT) == "Change Bank Details" and getattr(e, TS) > activation_at
    ]
    suspends = _times(events, "Suspend Vendor")
    verifies = _times(events, "Verify Bank Account")
    payments = _times(events, "Release First Payment")
    for change in changes:
        changed_at = getattr(change, TS)
        if not any(s > changed_at for s in suspends):
            failures.append("R11 post-activation bank change without a later suspension")
        for paid_at in payments:
            if paid_at <= changed_at:
                continue
            if not any(changed_at < v < paid_at for v in verifies):
                failures.append("R11 no re-verification between bank change and later payment")
    return failures

def resource_data_report(df, authorizations=None, policy="legal_strict"):
    authorizations = authorizations or {}
    report = {}
    for case_id, part in df.groupby(CASE, sort=False):
        events = _events(df, case_id)
        findings = []
        findings += check_r9(events)
        findings += check_r3_deadline(events)
        findings += check_r2_counts(events)
        findings += check_r5_r6(events, policy, authorizations.get(case_id))
        findings += check_r11(events)
        report[case_id] = findings
    return report
```

Library form for the single low-risk pair, after the log has real timestamps and resources:

```python
compliant_low_risk = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
    log, "Verify Bank Account", "Manager Approval"
)
compliant_activation = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
    log, "Capture Master Data", "Activate Vendor"
)
```

Cases removed by those calls are the pairwise violations only when both activities occur. The high-risk approval pair is `("Verify Bank Account", "Compliance Approval")`. Rejected cases are vacuous for both segregation rules and must not be reported as four-eyes failures merely because activation is absent.

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

Duplicated enforcement. R1, R8, the reject half of R2, R4, and R7 are each monitored in more than one layer. That is useful only if the report names the authoritative layer. T6 is the case that shows a careless AND of layer booleans is wrong: DECLARE passes, the tree fails, and both results are informative. T8 fails the same business ban in the tree, in `noncoexistence`, and in `never_together`; those three are one defect. Direct-follows duplicates the tree almost event-for-event, including the Resolution A shuffles, so a direct-follows failure should be grouped with the alignment rather than counted as independent evidence.

Loops and post-activation changes. `exactly_one` is safe only on activities the backbone does not repeat. Putting it on `Request Clarification` would make R3's "several cycles" unsatisfiable. The clarification frequency range `{0..10}` will falsely fail an eleventh legal cycle; DECLARE alternation will not. `equivalence` of request and response survives loops only as a count. A pattern of two requests followed by two responses can keep equal counts and satisfy ordinary response while breaking `altresponse`. After activation, `response(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` remains true if the single payment happens after a suspension, and it becomes false for a completed activation that never pays. R11's second verification makes `Verify Bank Account ~ Capture Master Data` false, which is why verify is not in that equivalence class. The direct-follows whitelist has no legal slot for the bank-change pattern, so a trace can pass the external R11 walk and still fail the tree. That disagreement is expected. The override is explicit and limited to a matching R11 pattern. An unconditional "extra activities are fine" override would also swallow T6.

Conditional rules that standard templates distort. `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` looks like R5 and rejects T1. `nonsuccession(Activate Vendor, Obtain Legal Opinion)` accepts T7 and rejects T6, but it also rejects a post-activation opinion in a case that never escalated, and it cannot implement R6. Global precedence of `Verify Bank Account` before `Release First Payment` is already true on the backbone because of the first verification, so it does not enforce R11. These encodings are documented omissions, not silent weakenings.

Vacuity. `precedence`, `response`, `altresponse`, `always_before`, and `always_after` are all satisfied when their triggering activity is absent. T1 does not demonstrate sanctions control, clarification control, or rejection control. A dashboard that counts vacuous constraints as passed controls will show a healthy compliance score on ordinary low-risk cases while the escalation rule has never fired. Reports should label each constraint `active pass`, `vacuous`, or `violated`. Equivalence does not have this problem for the clarification pair: count 0 equals count 0, and that equality is an active observation about both sides.

Missing case attributes. There is no risk class, no route code, no emergency flag, and no joint-authorization flag. The route is inferred from which approval occurred. That inference collapses on T8, which has a manager approval and a rejection. There is no attribute to decide whether `Obtain Legal Opinion` was the R4 consequence or an unrelated opinion. R6's deciding fact cannot be reconstructed from activity names or from the 10-hour gap on T6.

Timestamp-only limitations. The profile sees delays, not order obligations that lack a completed pair. It does not know calendars, so a 34-hour weekend delay and a 34-hour weekday delay are the same warning. With `zeta = 2` the lower bound of each published pair is zero, so the test does not flag suspiciously fast work unless a later policy changes zeta or the means. Equal timestamps fail closed for "precedes" and are invisible to a model that only stores the activity sequence. Hour offsets must be converted to real durations before the seconds-based profile is applied.

Support and confidence. The values `1.0` and `1.0` mean "this is a rule we intend to enforce", not "this happened in every historical case". Escalation is rare. A miner run on a year of T1-like cases would assign `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` a tiny support and a thresholded discovery would delete R4, which is the rule compliance most needs. Confidence must not be reused as a runtime on/off switch. If a mined model is later merged with this one, normative rules keep confidence 1.0 and keep their authority label even when the observed support is low.

Checker-semantic blind spots. If the installed log-skeleton conformance treats an absent activity as "pair not present in the trace skeleton" rather than count 0, then vacuous `always_before` pairs and zero-count equivalences will false-fail T1 and T3. If direct-follows is interpreted as mandatory adjacencies rather than a whitelist, the set in section 5 is the wrong object and should be disabled. Those two checks need a fixture run on T1 before the dictionary is trusted.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

The canonical procedural representation is `procedural_model`, the process tree in section 3. It is the generative picture of receipt, the concurrent checks, the supported clarification window, the three routes, and the Resolution A placement of escalation. It is not the whole policy.

Authority stays split:

- Process tree: backbone route, supported windows, and the in-language embedding of Resolution A.
- DECLARE: R1, R3's alternation, R4, R7, R8, and the redundant route monitors. `init` is authoritative for the start event.
- Log skeleton: R10 and the other numeric frequencies; direct-follows is a duplicate of the tree, not an independent policy.
- Temporal profile: R12 warnings only.
- Resource/data layer: R9, the textual deadline half of R3, the R2 count duplicate, authoritative conditional R5, the disabled-by-default R6 waiver, and authoritative R11.

Run every layer on every completed case. Do not short-circuit after the first failure. Short-circuiting on the tree would hide T4's segregation defect behind a pass, and short-circuiting the other way would hide that T6 still satisfied R4.

Practical order inside the case report:

1. Attribute completeness, so a vacuous pass is not confused with a missing column.
2. Resource and conditional checks, which name R9, R5, R6, R11, and the clarification deadline directly.
3. DECLARE, with each constraint marked active, vacuous, or violated.
4. Log skeleton frequencies, equivalences, and never-together. Show direct-follows deviations under the procedural finding.
5. Process-tree alignment, as the explanation of backbone insertions, deletions, and illegal moves.
6. Temporal profile last, rendered as warnings with the observed duration and `z`, never as an automatic reject.

Disposition. If any authoritative hard check fails, the case is not straight-through processed. It opens a review task. The checker does not append `Reject Vendor` or invent an opinion. If the only findings are temporal, the disposition is warning and the case remains eligible to complete. If a post-activation bank-change pattern passes `check_r11` and the only tree or direct-follows deviations are the inserted `Change Bank Details`, `Suspend Vendor`, and the extra `Verify Bank Account` in the required order, report reason `post_activation_bank_change` and do not treat those insertions as a second policy. Any other insertion stays a backbone deviation.

Contradictions are shown side by side. The case page lists the authoritative result and the redundant result, especially when one passes and the other fails. T6's template is the required wording: R4 is satisfied because the opinion eventually occurs; Resolution A is violated because that opinion is after activation; R6 is unavailable because `joint_authorization` is absent. A single red "conformance failed" mark is not sufficient. Policy-dependent cases carry a banner that names Resolution A as the active rule and Resolution B as a what-if that becomes available only when the authorization source is connected. Until that source exists, B cannot override A.
