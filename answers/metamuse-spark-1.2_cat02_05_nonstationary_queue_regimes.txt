# 1. Data audit and estimands

## Queueing quantities, units and convention

- **System:** Single pooled queue (unknown network structure, one aggregate WIP). Observed only during **opening hours 07:00-19:00 = 12 h = 720 min** per day. No service while closed, but backlog can carry over overnight. Daily `arrivals` and `completed` are **counts per 12h opening window**, not 24h. Intensities `` and `` expressed as **per opening-hour** and per minute where needed.
- **(t) – arrival intensity:** `arrivals / 12` per hour. Proxy for offered load. Assumes uniform counting inside 07-19; intraday pattern shown only for 4 representative days.
- **(t) – completion intensity / throughput X:** `completed / 12` per hour. Equals throughput only if flow conserved over window + boundary inventory change small.
- **S – active handling time (service time):** `median_service_min` and `p90_service_min` are **service component only, excluding waiting**. Not case flow time (sojourn). No mean provided; median < mean for right-skew. Cannot be substituted for `W`.
- **L – WIP:** `mean_wip` is **time-average number in system** from 15-min snapshots during opening hours. Includes waiting + in-service. Not virtual backlog at instant 07:00 or 19:00.
- **c(t) – capacity:** `staff_fte` mean concurrently available FTE during opening hours. Pressure proxies: `L / c`, ` * E[S] / c` (requires mean S), `completed / c`.
- **Case mix:** `complex_pct` % arrivals of multi-step variant. Affects `E[S]` and tail.
- **Intraday shape:** `peak_08_10_pct` % daily arrivals in 2h window 08:00-09:59 vs 12h uniform 16.7%. Representative hourly vectors give 12 bins 07-08 ... 18-19 for 4 days.

**Estimands:** regime-specific ``, `X`, distribution of `S` (central and tail), `L`, pressure, plus burstiness/overdispersion and stationarity. Any Little's Law `W = L / _eff` is **implied flow time (waiting+service) in opening-hour time**, not median service.

## Limitations of aggregates

- No per-case event log, no 07:00 backlog time series, no departure-time vs arrival-time linkage.
- Daily summaries suppress intraday queue build-up and closing-time carry-over.
- `mean_wip` snapshots cannot be decomposed into queue vs service.
- `staff_fte` is mean, hides schedule and break effects.

## Threats to naive inference (5)

1. **Non-stationarity by regime mixing:** Demand, `S`, burstiness and capacity all shift together. Pooled `,,L` average over incompatible operating points  Simpson's reversal.
2. **Carry-over and boundary non-conservation:** `completed  arrivals` over short windows due to backlog motion and overnight closure. Using single-day `arrivals` as throughput proxy violates Little's Law stationarity/conservation condition. Outage day deficit 47 cases becomes next-days surplus.
3. **Service vs flow-time confusion:** `median_service_min` is not `E[W]`. Heavy tail (p90  1.8-2.4× median) means mean > median; queuing adds further gap. `L/`  140-280 min vs median 24-36 min.
4. **Case-mix confounding / measurement change:** `complex_pct` jumps 21%44% around 2026-04-20 coincident with median +10 min, p90 +35 min, peak share +15pp. Could be true mix, coding re-label, or backlog-induced priority change. Cannot identify without case-level label audit.
5. **Overdispersion & intraday burstiness suppressed:** Daily means hide variance within day. Representative day 2026-04-21 shows 2h concentration 42% of daily arrivals (08:00-09:59 normally ~30%). Variance/mean >>1. Pooling inflates variance and creates serial dependence.
6. **Capacity and intervention timing ambiguity:** `staff_fte` change on 2026-04-29 not logged intra-day; weekend baseline staffing differs structurally (2.5 vs 6.0 FTE). Ignoring calendar structure biases utilization estimate.
7. **Serial dependence and common cause:** Daily points share backlog state; errors not independent. Outage  recovery autocorrelation, and mix shock persists. Standard CI/`p`-values invalid without accounting.

*Assumptions labeled when used:* (A1) 12h window for rate denominators; (A2) `mean_wip` comparable across days despite snapshot sampling error; (A3) representative hourly vectors are illustrative not population of regime; (A4) mean service > median by factor 1.1-1.3 estimated from p90 spread, not observed.

- **Recommendation:** Staff to **regime-specific load**, not pooled mean. For R4 mix, need +1.5-2.0 FTE vs baseline (67.5) to return 0.8 with burst buffer. Intervention +1 FTE (to 7.0) only partially closes gap (0.73-0.88). Consider **skill-mix**: complex cases need longer handling and possibly senior FTE; adding 1 generic FTE helps throughput (X rose 8.310.1) but median only fell 3532 – mix still drives tail.
- **Weekend staffing:** WIP/c on weekends late period 20/2.5=8 vs early 12/2.5=4.8 – carry-over from weekdays stresses weekends. Maintain 3.0 FTE upgrade seen 05-02/03 if weekday backlog >30.

## Backlog recovery

- Outage deficit 47 Cases needs 1.5× surplus days at R1 surplus capacity (~1/h ×12h=12/day). R3 delivered 44 in 2 days with surge 7 FTE – adequate. **Model recovery as transient, not high throughput benchmark:** Do not set 10.75 completions/h (16th) as target; it required draining queue + extra staffing.
- R4 accumulated +79 over 7 days; R5 drained only 17 in 3 days at +0.6/h surplus. At that rate, **11 more days** needed to clear R4 backlog (62 net remaining after R5) even if mix stays. If mix reverts, faster. Plan explicit backlog burn-down window with temporary overtime, not just +1 FTE.
- **Policy:** After any outage or mix shift, track **net  = cum arrivals - cum completions** and **WIP trend**, not daily completions alone. Trigger surge when  > 2× baseline L (40).

## Peak smoothing / burstiness

- `peak_08_10_pct` rose 30%47%, and vector 04-21 shows two spikes (10-11h 34, 13-14h 24). Poisson would expect 8-10/h. Burst adds queue that persists for hours because completions smooth (Fano <1). **Intraday capacity** should flex to morning/afternoon peaks, not flat 6 FTE. Options: stagger shifts to cover 08-11 and 13-14, appointment slots, or upstream arrival shaping.
- **Data collection:** Log true arrival timestamps, not just hourly bins, to estimate sub-hourly clustering and autocorrelation.

## Data-collection recommendations

1. **Event log with case ID, arrival ts, start-service ts, completion ts, variant code:** Enables true `W`, waiting, `E[S]`, and PM4Py discovery; current median/p90 insufficient.
2. **Backlog snapshots at 07:00 and 19:00 daily** to enforce Little's boundary accounting and measure carry-over calendar wait.
3. **Staff roster per 15-min**, not daily mean, to compute time-varying `c(t)` and `(t)`.
4. **Case-mix audit:** Validate whether 04-20 jump is coding change vs true shift; dual-code sample of 100 cases before/after.
5. **Intraday hourly counts continuously** (not 4 samples) to model Fano and lag dependence properly.

## Pooled-average policy that looks beneficial but is risky segmented

**Policy:** *"Average throughput 102/day matches average arrivals 108/day with current 6.3 FTE; maintain staffing and use outage-recovery productivity (10-11/h) as evidence capacity is sufficient."*

**Why pooled it looks fine:** Pooled `X` 8.5/h vs `` 9.0/h gap only 0.5/h; recovery days prove team can do 10.75/h; pooled utilisation 0.80.

**Why segmented it fails:** Recovery throughput is **backlog-assisted** with empty queue after outage and surge staffing – not sustainable. In R4, sustainable `X` at 6 FTE is only 8.3/h vs `` 9.3/h – deficit 1.0/h. Maintaining 6 FTE guarantees linear backlog growth (+11/day), WIP doubling (2044) and `W` 2.4h5.3h, with p90 breaching SLA by 40 min. Morning burst 34/h cannot be served at 8/h without queue spill into afternoon. Continuing pooled policy would accumulate ~60-70 case backlog per week, forcing weekend overtime or quality failure, while management believes system is “near balanced”.
