# 2. Scenario forecasts

## Optimistic scenario: Effective pooling with controlled queues
*Assumes case-note standardization succeeds prior to go-live, automation absorbs simple intake/eligibility volume, tech triage genuinely offloads routine work from Legal/Risk, and approval standards reduce per-case approval service time enough for 2 supervisors to cope.*

* **Handover pattern:** Ad hoc cross-team handovers drop from 6 to $\le 3$ per 100 cases. Handover-of-work graphs show clean star-shaped delegation to shared queues with minimal repeat bounce-backs (`reassignment_count` median stays near baseline).
* **Queue / waiting-time pattern:** Tech wait times settle near management’s 3.5–4.0h estimate; East’s tech waits drop noticeably relative to its pre-change bottleneck. Legal/Risk wait stabilizes around 9–10.5h because tech triage filters out false-positive escalations. Shared approval wait holds at ~3.0h.
* **Cycle-time distribution:** Overall median improves modestly (~7.4d $\to$ 6.8–7.1d). **East** sees substantial relief (median 8.6d $\to$ ~7.4d; P90 22.8d $\to$ ~19.0d). **North and South** remain roughly flat on median (6.8–7.4d) with a minor P90 bump (+0.5–1.0d) due to pull-queue latency.
* **Rework and quality pattern:** Standardized decision criteria and better notes keep overall rework flat or slightly lower (~8.5–9.0%), with East’s rework dropping from 11.5% toward ~9.5%. Difficult QC catches defects before `Close`.
* **Role clarity and accountability:** Clear SLAs on pull queues keep ownership legible; `discover_organizational_roles` identifies clean, distinct clusters (Local Intake/Casework, Tech Shared Service, Legal/Risk, Shared Approval).
* **Fairness implications:** Inter-team equity improves (East customer satisfaction rises from 3.8 toward 4.1) without materially harming North/South. Regional-accommodation cases are flagged early and routed with affinity back to former local specialists.
* **Early warning signs (that the optimistic path is holding vs. slipping):** Even here, watch for rising medium-complexity escalations after week 6 and any drift in Legal/Risk queue depth above 2 days of WIP.

## Central scenario: Divergent team effects and localized bottlenecks (most likely)
*Assumes restructuring proceeds largely as proposed without extra Legal/Risk capacity or full case-note overhaul. Pooling helps East’s simple/medium tech work, but supervisor headcount reduction, +33% Legal/Risk referrals, and loss of local context create hidden friction.*

* **Handover pattern:** Nominal handovers *rise* net overall. `discover_subcontracting_network` shows increased ping-pong loops (`local caseworker  shared queue  local caseworker`) driven by incomplete case notes (`handover_reason = clarification`). `discover_working_together_network` shows sharp decay in cross-role ties within North and South.
* **Queue / waiting-time pattern:** Simple cases stay fast (Bot_Intake + local flow). However, **two bottlenecks emerge**: (1) the 2-person Shared Approval queue stretches from 2.4h to 5–8h median wait (and >24h at P90) under ~75% higher per-supervisor load; (2) Legal/Risk wait jumps from 9.6h to 14–20h as utilization pushes past 95%. Tech Shared Service runs at ~84–88% utilization, creating 5–7h waits during mid-week peaks.
* **Cycle-time distribution:** **Bimodal divergence.** Overall average/median cycle time looks deceptively stable (~7.3–7.7d) because East improves slightly (median 8.6d $\to$ ~7.9d) and simple cases move quickly. However, **P90 cycle time worsens across North (16.4d $\to$ ~19–21d) and South (18.2d $\to$ ~21–23d)**, while high-complexity cases across all teams slow by 20–35%.
* **Rework and quality pattern:** Rework falls slightly on routine standardized decisions in East, but **rises in North/South (from 8.2–9.0% to 10.5–12.5%)** on complex and `local_expertise_flag == True` cases due to missing regional nuance and fewer informal pre-checks.
* **Role clarity and accountability:** Ambiguity develops around "difficult QC" vs. "Specialist Review" and who chases stalled shared-queue items. Pull latency (time between specialist advice completion and local caseworker pick-up) widens to 3–6h. `reassignment_count` rises by 25–40%.
* **Fairness implications:** **Geographic leveling-down:** East narrows the gap partly because North/South degrade. Customers requiring regional/language accommodation suffer measurably higher rework (+4–6 percentage points) and SLA misses than standard segments.
* **Early warning signs:** Pull-queue dwell time >4h; approval queue WIP growing week-over-week; `reassignment_count >= 2` exceeding 15% of complex cases; North/South caseworkers routing borderline medium-complexity cases to Tech Shared Service.

## Adverse scenario: Central queue collapse, defensive routing, and capability erosion
*Assumes Legal/Risk hits >100% effective arrival load, Tech Shared Service absorbs unplanned Legal/Risk spillover plus difficult QC and rework loops, 1 of the 2 remaining supervisors takes leave, and local staff defensively escalate cases.*

* **Handover pattern:** Severe hub congestion and rework loops. Cases bounce repeatedly across `Local Caseworker  Tech Shared Service  Legal/Risk  Quality Check`. Variant count explodes (`get_variants` shows long-tail exception paths growing >50%).
* **Queue / waiting-time pattern:** Non-linear queueing breakdown (Kingman effect). Legal/Risk waits blow past 24–48h; Tech Shared Service utilization exceeds 92% and waits exceed 12–18h; a single supervisor absence causes Approval waits to spike >24h. Waiting time accounts for >85% of total case sojourn time.
* **Cycle-time distribution:** Even median cycle time deteriorates across all teams (8.5–10.5d), while **P90 cycle times blow out** to 24–28d in North/South and 28–35d in East. SLA miss rates exceed 15–20% overall and >30% for high-complexity/regulated cases.
* **Rework and quality pattern:** Compounding rework spiral (14–18% overall): rushed specialists under queue pressure issue generic advice; local staff misapply it without regional adaptation; difficult QC bounces cases back into the same saturated specialist queue, creating self-reinforcing load.
* **Role clarity and accountability:** Breakdown of end-to-end ownership ("queue abandonment"). High cherry-picking in shared pull queues (easy items pulled first, hard/ambiguous items age indefinitely). Staff morale drops and informal knowledge-sharing collapses.
* **Fairness implications:** Acute structural inequity. Standard, low-complexity cases in digitally savvy segments still clear reasonably well, while complex, regulated, and regional/language-accommodation customers face severe delays, repeated contacts, and elevated adverse `outcome` rates.
* **Early warning signs:** Growing unfinished WIP (arrival rate > departure rate) in Legal/Risk or Approval for >5 consecutive business days; cherry-picking index (age of oldest 10% unpulled queue items rising rapidly); >20% jump in `handover_reason` coded as "missing info" or "wrong queue"; spike in voluntary escalation of low/medium-complexity cases.

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

Relying on overall average (or even median) cycle time is analytically dangerous here because ~60% of cases do not require technical specialists and ~90% do not require Legal/Risk. A 0.5-day speed-up on high-volume simple cases (via automation and East relief) can easily mask severe deterioration elsewhere.

1. **Tail latency explosion (P90/P95 distortion):** Queueing delays near 90%+ utilization are heavily right-skewed. Overall median cycle time could remain near 7.4 days while P90 stretches from 16–18 days to 22–26 days, breaching SLAs and causing customer escalations that averages completely smooth over.
2. **High-complexity case degradation (mix masking):** High-complexity and multi-specialty cases (`required_specialty in {legal, risk, security}`) traverse *all three* centralized queues sequentially (Tech $\to$ Legal/Risk $\to$ Shared Approval $\to$ Difficult QC). Their end-to-end wait times can double even while low-complexity cases move faster, hiding systemic failure inside a stable pooled mean.
3. **Simpson’s paradox across teams and local knowledge loss:** East (520 cases/month) starts far worse (8.6d median, 22.8d P90) than North (820 cases, 6.8d) and South (760 cases, 7.4d). Early improvements in East can offset gradual degradation in North and South—especially on cases with `local_expertise_flag == True`—making enterprise averages look flat while institutional regional knowledge quietly erodes.
4. **Fairness disparities across customer segments:** Customers needing regional or language accommodation represent a minority of total volume. Even if their rework doubles or their SLA miss rate triples due to loss of local specialist familiarity and extra queue handovers, the aggregate average barely moves.
5. **Delayed learning and skill atrophy:** Routing all hard cases away from local teams can temporarily *improve* local throughput (fewer interruptions, simpler caseloads). The cost—local caseworkers losing diagnostic competence, becoming unable to screen out unnecessary referrals, and losing promotion pipelines into specialist roles—only manifests 6–12 months later as rising escalation rates and fragility.
6. **Accountability erosion and hidden pull-queue aging:** Average cycle time of *closed* cases suffers from right-censoring: orphaned or cherry-picked cases sitting unpulled in shared queues do not enter completed-case averages until weeks later. Ambiguous ownership between shared approvers, specialists, and local caseworkers creates silent aging backlogs invisible in closed-case averages.
7. **Exception-handling and absence fragility:** Standard-path averages do not capture what happens under non-normal conditions—such as a rare regulatory exception, a surge in `priority == 'high'` cases, or one of the two remaining supervisors taking sudden leave. A streamlined average process can simultaneously become far less resilient to shocks.

# 5. Pilot design

A full big-bang rollout carries unacceptable queueing risk (especially cutting the 3rd supervisor and increasing Legal/Risk referrals by 33% simultaneously). A **phased, quasi-experimental pilot** isolates pooling benefits from bottleneck risks.

### Pilot scope (modified for safety)
* **Pre-pilot prerequisite (Weeks -4 to 0):** Standardize case-note templates and mandatory handover fields (`handover_reason`, structured summary, `local_expertise_flag`) across all teams to address the current 18% cross-team rework root cause.
* **Pilot intervention (Teams South and East):**
  * Form a **2-person Virtual Technical Shared Service** pooling `S_Tech` (68% baseline util) and `E_Tech` (82% baseline util). Retain 20% protected daily "local office hours" for informal caseworker consultation to test whether informal advice prevents defensive escalations.
  * Introduce **priority affinity routing** (cases with `local_expertise_flag == True` prefer the specialist from their home region unless wait exceeds 4h).
  * Pilot a **shared approval queue** across South and East *without* eliminating a supervisor yet (simulate 2-supervisor capacity analytically using service-time logs before making irreversible headcount cuts).
  * Pilot **structured Legal/Risk triage** only on a capped subset (e.g., 50% of newly detected cases) until net offload on Legal/Risk is verified.

### Comparison group
* **Team North serves as the contemporaneous control group** (retaining embedded `N_Tech` and local `N_Supervisor`, while receiving the same standardized case-note template to isolate structural pooling effects from documentation improvements).
* Use **Difference-in-Differences (DiD)** comparing pre/post changes in South (healthy baseline) and East (overloaded baseline) against North, matched on `case_complexity`, `required_specialty`, `priority`, and `customer_segment` to control for case-mix shifts and seasonality.

### Pre/post observation window
* **Pre-pilot baseline:** 12 weeks of historical event logs (ensuring complete observation of P90 tails of 16–23 days and capturing monthly/seasonal variance).
* **Ramp / burn-in period:** 2 weeks (excluded from primary outcome comparison, monitored daily via leading indicators).
* **Active pilot window:** 10–12 weeks (at least 3–4× the P90 cycle time of ~23 days so long-running high-complexity cases close without severe right-censoring).

### Segmentation plan
All metrics are evaluated both overall and stratified across four mandatory slices:
1. **Team:** East (expected beneficiary) vs. South (test of local-expertise erosion) vs. North (control).
2. **Complexity:** `low`, `medium`, `high` (`case_complexity`).
3. **Specialty:** None, Technical, and `{legal, risk, security}` (`required_specialty`).
4. **Customer & local context:** `customer_segment` crossed with `local_expertise_flag` (explicitly isolating language/regional accommodation cases).

### Guardrail metrics (pilot bounds)
* **Legal/Risk queue wait:** P90 wait must not exceed pre-pilot baseline by >15% for two consecutive weeks; utilization capped below 95%.
* **South & East P90 cycle time:** South P90 cycle time must not worsen by >10% (+1.8 days) relative to North DiD trend.
* **Fairness guardrail:** SLA miss gap and rework rate for `local_expertise_flag == True` cases must not worsen by >2.5 percentage points relative to standard cases.
* **Rework & ping-pong guardrail:** Share of cases with `reassignment_count >= 2` must not increase by >25% over baseline; `rework_flag` must not rise >2 percentage points in South.
* **WIP stability:** Open aging cases (>15 days) in shared queues must not grow for >3 consecutive weeks.

### Staff feedback collection
* **Weekly 3-minute pulse survey** for pilot intake, caseworkers, specialists, and supervisors covering: ease of getting informal advice, clarity of case ownership, perceived workload sustainability, and note quality.
* **Structured friction log** embedded in the workflow (tagging cases where context was lost, advice was unclear, or ownership stalled).
* **Biweekly qualitative retrospectives** separating local caseworkers from shared-service specialists to surface unspoken accountability tensions and skill-development concerns.

### Rollback and adjustment triggers
* **Amber triggers (Adjust without halting):** Pull-queue dwell time >4h; medium-complexity escalation rate up >15%; South rework up 1.5–2.0 pp; pulse ownership-clarity score <3.5/5.
  * *Actions:* Expand local office hours, tighten intake checklist, enforce affinity routing for regional cases, add queue-aging alerts.
* **Red triggers (Immediate pause / partial rollback):** Legal/Risk or Approval WIP growing continuously for 10 business days with P90 waits >2× baseline; SLA miss rate up >4 pp for any `customer_segment` or `local_expertise_flag == True`; overall P90 cycle time worsening >20% vs. North control.
  * *Actions:* Suspend new Legal/Risk routing triggers, revert South high-complexity regional cases to local affinity, and freeze any supervisor headcount reduction or North onboarding until root causes are resolved.

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: Modify the design and pilot first.** Do **not** proceed to full rollout as currently specified, and do **not** reject restructuring outright.

### Rationale
The status quo is genuinely unsustainable in two places: East is overloaded (`E_Tech` at 82%, 22.8d P90, 11.5% rework, 9.8% SLA miss) and Legal/Risk specialists are dangerously saturated (91–93%). Pooling technical capacity and standardizing cross-team documentation directly address real operational weaknesses. However, the proposal as written pairs sensible pooling with **two high-risk capacity errors**:
1. Cutting 1 of 3 supervisors while raising approval volume by +16.7% (~75% more approvals per remaining supervisor).
2. Expanding Legal/Risk routing by +33% into a 91–93% utilized queue without proven net workload relief.

### Required modifications prior to piloting
1. **Decouple supervisor headcount reduction** from the initial shared-approval pilot; only reduce supervisor capacity if automation or simplified approval thresholds demonstrably cut per-case approval service time by >40%.
2. **Protect Legal/Risk capacity** before widening detection rules (either add part-time capacity or prove via pilot that tech triage cuts Legal/Risk service time per case by >25%).
3. **Standardize case notes first** and adopt a **hybrid virtual shared-service model** (preserving protected local advisory hours and regional affinity routing for `local_expertise_flag == True` cases) to retain local knowledge and caseworker learning.

### Conditional go / no-go decision criteria
Proceed from the South/East pilot to full rollout (including North) **only if**, after the 10–12 week pilot window (adjusted against North via DiD):
* East P90 cycle time and SLA miss rate improve meaningfully (e.g., $\ge 10\%$ reduction in P90, $\ge 1.5$ pp drop in SLA misses) **without** degrading South’s P90 cycle time by >5% or rework by >1 pp.
* Legal/Risk and Approval queue waiting times remain stable (no upward WIP drift; P90 waits within guardrails).
* No statistically or operationally meaningful widening of SLA/rework disparities for `local_expertise_flag == True` or minority `customer_segment` groups.
* Role clarity and learning/advisory pulse scores remain $\ge 3.8/5$, and `discover_subcontracting_network` bounce-backs do not trend upward.

### What management must NOT infer from early averages
* **Do not declare victory from Month 1 average cycle time:** Early closed cases are systematically biased toward short, simple cases (**right-censoring**); stalled high-complexity and Legal/Risk cases have not yet closed.
* **Do not mistake East’s relief for universal success:** Improved aggregate averages can hide deterioration in North/South or regional-accommodation segments (**Simpson’s paradox**).
* **Do not equate higher specialist utilization with higher value:** Pushing utilization from 74% to 88–92% looks efficient on a utilization dashboard while quietly priming the process for exponential queue delays, burnout, and rework.
* **Do not infer sustainable local capability from short-term local throughput:** Removing hard cases makes local staff look faster initially, masking gradual skill atrophy and defensive escalation that surface months later.

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

The following Python analysis sketch illustrates how to compare `pre_df` (baseline log) and `post_df` (pilot/post-change log) across social networks, performance DFGs, service/waiting times, organizational roles, stratified slices, rework, and SLA misses using PM4Py.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load, format, and enrich pre/post event logs
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
def prepare_log(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = df.copy()
    df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"], utc=True)
    formatted = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    )
    # Insert case-level service and waiting times (adds @@case_service_time, @@case_waiting_time)
    enriched = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(formatted)
    return enriched


def compute_case_summary(df: pd.DataFrame, sla_days: float = 14.0) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Aggregate event log to case level for distribution, SLA, rework, and fairness checks."""
    case_df = (
        df.sort_values("time:timestamp")
        .groupby("case:concept:name", as_index=False)
        .agg(
            start_time=("time:timestamp", "min"),
            end_time=("time:timestamp", "max"),
            team=("team", "first"),
            location=("location", "first"),
            required_specialty=("required_specialty", "first"),
            priority=("priority", "first"),
            case_complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
            customer_segment=("customer_segment", "first"),
            local_expertise_flag=("local_expertise_flag", "max"),
            rework_flag=("rework_flag", "max"),
            reassignment_count=("reassignment_count", "max"),
            outcome=("outcome", "last"),
            service_time_sec=("@@case_service_time", "first"),
            waiting_time_sec=("@@case_waiting_time", "first"),
        )
    )
    case_df["cycle_time_days"] = (
        case_df["end_time"] - case_df["start_time"]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 86400.0
    case_df["waiting_share"] = case_df["waiting_time_sec"] / (
        case_df["service_time_sec"] + case_df["waiting_time_sec"] + 1e-9
    )
    case_df["sla_miss"] = case_df["cycle_time_days"] > sla_days
    return case_df


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Organizational networks, roles, and resource similarity
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
def analyze_organization(df: pd.DataFrame) -> dict:
    # Social network analyses (SNA objects expose `.connections` dict {(r1, r2): weight})
    hw_net = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(df)
    wt_net = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(df)
    sub_net = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(df)  # A -> B -> A ping-pong loops

    # Activity-based resource similarity & role discovery (tracks role clarity & skill narrowing)
    res_sim = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(df)
    roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(df)

    # Example summary statistics from SNA connections
    def top_edges(sna_obj, n=10):
        return sorted(sna_obj.connections.items(), key=lambda x: x[1], reverse=True)[:n]

    return {
        "handover_top_edges": top_edges(hw_net),
        "handover_total_weight": sum(hw_net.connections.values()),
        "working_together_top_edges": top_edges(wt_net),
        "subcontracting_top_loops": top_edges(sub_net),
        "subcontracting_total_weight": sum(sub_net.connections.values()),
        "resource_similarity_pairs": top_edges(res_sim),
        "discovered_roles": [
            {"activities": list(r.activities), "originators": r.originator_importance}
            for r in roles
        ],
    }


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Control-flow variants, performance DFG, service time, rework, temporal drift
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
def analyze_process_performance(df: pd.DataFrame) -> dict:
    # Frequency and performance DFGs (performance DFG measures arc waiting/sojourn times)
    dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(df)
    perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(df)

    # Variants (check proliferation of exception paths vs. standardization)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(df)
    top_10_variants = sorted(variants.items(), key=lambda x: x[1], reverse=True)[:10]
    top_variant_keys = [v[0] for v in top_10_variants]
    top_variants_log = pm4py.filter_variants(df, top_variant_keys)
    top_10_coverage = (
        top_variants_log["case:concept:name"].nunique()
        / df["case:concept:name"].nunique()
    )

    # Service times per activity & rework cases per activity
    service_times = pm4py.get_service_time(df)
    rework_per_activity = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(df)

    # Temporal features (useful for detecting weekday queue spikes or absence shocks)
    temporal_features = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(df)

    return {
        "variant_count": len(variants),
        "top_10_variant_coverage": top_10_coverage,
        "dfg_top_edges": sorted(dfg.items(), key=lambda x: x[1], reverse=True)[:15],
        "perf_dfg_slowest_edges": sorted(
            perf_dfg.items(), key=lambda x: x[1], reverse=True
        )[:15],
        "service_time_by_activity": service_times,
        "rework_cases_per_activity": rework_per_activity,
        "temporal_features_sample": temporal_features.head(),
    }


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Stratified comparison (team, complexity, specialty, customer segment)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
def compare_segmented_metrics(
    pre_df: pd.DataFrame, post_df: pd.DataFrame, segment_col: str
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    pre_cases = compute_case_summary(pre_df)
    post_cases = compute_case_summary(post_df)

    def summarize(cdf: pd.DataFrame, label: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
        return (
            cdf.groupby(segment_col)
            .agg(
                cases=("case:concept:name", "count"),
                median_cycle_d=("cycle_time_days", "median"),
                p90_cycle_d=("cycle_time_days", lambda s: np.percentile(s, 90)),
                p95_cycle_d=("cycle_time_days", lambda s: np.percentile(s, 95)),
                median_wait_share=("waiting_share", "median"),
                rework_rate=("rework_flag", "mean"),
                sla_miss_rate=("sla_miss", "mean"),
                pct_reassigned_2plus=("reassignment_count", lambda s: (s >= 2).mean()),
            )
            .add_suffix(f"_{label}")
        )

    pre_s = summarize(pre_cases, "pre")
    post_s = summarize(post_cases, "post")
    comp = pre_s.join(post_s, how="outer")
    comp["delta_p90_cycle_d"] = comp["p90_cycle_d_post"] - comp["p90_cycle_d_pre"]
    comp["delta_rework_pp"] = (comp["rework_rate_post"] - comp["rework_rate_pre"]) * 100
    comp["delta_sla_miss_pp"] = (
        comp["sla_miss_rate_post"] - comp["sla_miss_rate_pre"]
    ) * 100
    return comp


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Targeted deep dive using pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
def run_full_pre_post_comparison(pre_raw: pd.DataFrame, post_raw: pd.DataFrame):
    pre_df = prepare_log(pre_raw)
    post_df = prepare_log(post_raw)

    # A. Organization & process-level diffs
    org_pre, org_post = analyze_organization(pre_df), analyze_organization(post_df)
    perf_pre = analyze_process_performance(pre_df)
    perf_post = analyze_process_performance(post_df)

    # B. Mandatory fairness, complexity, specialty, and team stratifications
    segment_reports = {
        col: compare_segmented_metrics(pre_df, post_df, col)
        for col in [
            "team",
            "case_complexity",
            "required_specialty",
            "customer_segment",
            "local_expertise_flag",
        ]
    }

    # C. Focused slice: high-complexity + legal/risk cases (highest bottleneck risk)
    def slice_high_risk(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
        sub = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            df, attribute_key="case_complexity", values=["high"], level="case"
        )
        sub = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            sub,
            attribute_key="required_specialty",
            values=["legal", "risk", "security"],
            level="case",
        )
        return sub

    high_risk_pre = slice_high_risk(pre_df)
    high_risk_post = slice_high_risk(post_df)
    high_risk_perf_diff = {
        "pre_perf_dfg": pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(high_risk_pre)[0],
        "post_perf_dfg": pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(high_risk_post)[0],
        "pre_subcontracting": pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(high_risk_pre).connections,
        "post_subcontracting": pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(high_risk_post).connections,
        "pre_rework": pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(high_risk_pre),
        "post_rework": pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(high_risk_post),
    }

    # D. Focused slice: North/South cases requiring local expertise (loss-of-expertise test)
    def slice_local_expertise_ns(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
        sub = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            df, attribute_key="team", values=["North", "South"], level="case"
        )
        sub = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            sub, attribute_key="local_expertise_flag", values=[True], level="case"
        )
        return sub

    local_ns_pre = slice_local_expertise_ns(pre_df)
    local_ns_post = slice_local_expertise_ns(post_df)
    local_expertise_report = compare_segmented_metrics(
        local_ns_pre, local_ns_post, segment_col="team"
    )

    return {
        "org_pre": org_pre,
        "org_post": org_post,
        "perf_pre": perf_pre,
        "perf_post": perf_post,
        "segment_reports": segment_reports,
        "high_risk_perf_diff": high_risk_perf_diff,
        "local_expertise_ns_report": local_expertise_report,
    }
```
