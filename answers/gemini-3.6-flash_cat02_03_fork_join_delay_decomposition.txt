# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

To rigorously analyze flow-time bottlenecks in an AND-fork / AND-join parallel process without double counting time, we decompose each case $c$ into sequential, non-overlapping stages.

### Mathematical Definitions

Let $c$ denote a case index. Define the milestone timestamps as:
*   $T_{\text{arr}}(c)$: Arrival timestamp.
*   $T_{\text{fork}}(c)$: Fork release timestamp (earliest time parallel branches are permitted to start).
*   $T_{\text{start}, i}(c)$ and $T_{\text{end}, i}(c)$: Start and completion timestamps for branch $i \in \{A, B, C\}$.
*   $T_{\text{f\_start}}(c)$ and $T_{\text{f\_end}}(c)$: Start and completion timestamps for the final Release Decision $F$.

#### Sequential Delay and Service Components:
1.  **Upstream Elapsed Time ($D_{\text{up}}$):**
    $$D_{\text{up}}(c) = T_{\text{fork}}(c) - T_{\text{arr}}(c)$$
2.  **Branch-Specific Queue Delay ($Q_i$):**
    $$Q_i(c) = T_{\text{start}, i}(c) - T_{\text{fork}}(c) \quad \text{for } i \in \{A, B, C\}$$
3.  **Branch Active Service Time ($S_i$):**
    $$S_i(c) = T_{\text{end}, i}(c) - T_{\text{start}, i}(c) \quad \text{for } i \in \{A, B, C\}$$
4.  **Branch Total Flow Time ($D_i$):**
    $$D_i(c) = Q_i(c) + S_i(c) = T_{\text{end}, i}(c) - T_{\text{fork}}(c)$$
5.  **Join-Ready Milestone ($T_{\text{join}}$):**
    $$T_{\text{join}}(c) = \max_{i \in \{A, B, C\}} T_{\text{end}, i}(c)$$
6.  **Critical Branch ($i^*$):**
    $$i^*(c) = \arg\max_{i \in \{A, B, C\}} T_{\text{end}, i}(c) \quad \text{(with deterministic tie-breaking: } A > B > C \text{)}$$
7.  **Elapsed Fork-Join Stage Time ($D_{\text{FJ}}$):**
    $$D_{\text{FJ}}(c) = T_{\text{join}}(c) - T_{\text{fork}}(c) = \max_{i \in \{A, B, C\}} D_i(c) = Q_{i^*}(c) + S_{i^*}(c)$$
8.  **Branch Synchronization Wait ($W_{\text{sync}, i}$):**
    $$W_{\text{sync}, i}(c) = T_{\text{join}}(c) - T_{\text{end}, i}(c) = D_{\text{FJ}}(c) - D_i(c)$$
9.  **Downstream Queue Delay ($Q_{\text{final}}$):**
    $$Q_{\text{final}}(c) = T_{\text{f\_start}}(c) - T_{\text{join}}(c)$$
10. **Final Service Time ($S_{\text{final}}$):**
    $$S_{\text{final}}(c) = T_{\text{f\_end}}(c) - T_{\text{f\_start}}(c)$$
11. **Total Case Flow Time ($T_{\text{flow}}$):**
    $$T_{\text{flow}}(c) = T_{\text{f\_end}}(c) - T_{\text{arr}}(c) = D_{\text{up}}(c) + D_{\text{FJ}}(c) + Q_{\text{final}}(c) + S_{\text{final}}(c)$$

---

### Proof: Why Summing Branch Synchronization Waits Double Counts Time

In an AND-join split, branches run concurrently. The physical elapsed time of the fork-join block is governed strictly by the maximum completion duration across all branches:
$$D_{\text{FJ}}(c) = \max_{i} (Q_i(c) + S_i(c))$$

For any given branch $i$, its branch flow duration plus its synchronization wait equals the total elapsed fork-join time:
$$D_i(c) + W_{\text{sync}, i}(c) = (T_{\text{end}, i}(c) - T_{\text{fork}}(c)) + (T_{\text{join}}(c) - T_{\text{end}, i}(c)) = T_{\text{join}}(c) - T_{\text{fork}}(c) = D_{\text{FJ}}(c)$$

If an analyst attempts to sum $Q_i(c) + S_i(c) + W_{\text{sync}, i}(c)$ across all $N$ branches ($N=3$), the resulting sum is:
$$\sum_{i=1}^{N} \left( Q_i(c) + S_i(c) + W_{\text{sync}, i}(c) \right) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} D_{\text{FJ}}(c) = N \times D_{\text{FJ}}(c)$$

For $N=3$, summing across branches produces three times the actual elapsed calendar duration of the stage. To preserve additive decomposition without double counting, the stage duration must be represented by the critical branch path $Q_{i^*}(c) + S_{i^*}(c)$, while $W_{\text{sync}, i}(c)$ represents passive, concurrent slack time experienced by non-critical branches.

### Detailed Counterfactual Evaluations

#### Counterfactual 1: Reducing Branch A Service Time by 20% (50 min $\rightarrow$ 40 min)
*   **Direct Benefits at Fork-Join Block:**
    *   `F01`: Original ends $(A=09:00, B=08:55, C=08:38) \rightarrow$ New ends $(A=08:50, B=08:55, C=08:38)$. New `join_ready` = $08:55$. **Savings: 5 min** (Branch B becomes new critical path).
    *   `F11`: Original ends $(A=11:30, B=11:20, C=11:03) \rightarrow$ New ends $(A=11:20, B=11:20, C=11:03)$. New `join_ready` = $11:20$. **Savings: 10 min** (Tied critical path between A and B).
*   **Zero-Benefit Cases (10 out of 12 cases / 83.3%):**
    *   In cases `F02`–`F10` and `F12`, Branch B or C completed later than 40 minutes post-release. Accelerating A by 10 minutes provides **0 minutes of flow-time reduction** because the process remains gated by Branch B or C.
*   **Aggregate Stage Impact:** Total join time saved across 12 cases = **15.0 min** (Mean: $1.25\text{ min/case}$).

#### Counterfactual 2: Reducing Branch B Queue Delay by 50%
*   **Direct Benefits at Fork-Join Block:**
    *   Benefits 8 out of 12 cases directly (`F02`, `F03`, `F05`, `F06`, `F07`, `F09`, `F10`, `F12`).
    *   Per-case savings at `join_ready`: `F02` (10 min), `F03` (10 min), `F05` (10 min), `F06` (15 min), `F07` (15 min), `F09` (12.5 min), `F10` (5 min), `F12` (15 min).
*   **Zero-Benefit Cases (4 out of 12 cases / 33.3%):**
    *   `F01` and `F11` (Branch A critical); `F04` and `F08` (Branch C critical).
*   **Aggregate Stage Impact:** Total join time saved across 12 cases = **92.5 min** (Mean: $7.71\text{ min/case}$).
*   **Path Shift Dynamic:** In cases `F02`, `F05`, and `F10`, cutting B's queue delay shifts the critical path onto Branch A once B's completion time drops below A's fixed 50-minute threshold.

#### Counterfactual 3: Adding Final-Station Capacity / Eliminating Downstream Queueing
*   **Direct Benefits:**
    *   Every single case ($100\%$ of cases) experiences a downstream queue delay ranging from 8 to 20 minutes (Mean: $13.83\text{ min}$).
    *   Completely eliminating downstream queueing reduces total case flow time for all 12 cases unconditionally.
*   **Aggregate Impact:** Total case flow time saved = **166.0 min** (Mean: $13.83\text{ min/case}$).

---

### Downstream Masking and Secondary Effects

A critical process-mining insight is the interaction between upstream branch improvements and downstream station congestion:

1.  **Downstream Masking:** If Station F (resources F1/F2) operates with a fixed dispatch schedule or constrained capacity, achieving an earlier `join_ready` timestamp at the fork-join block does **not** guarantee an earlier final completion time. Instead, the case simply arrives earlier at the downstream queue, increasing $Q_{\text{final}}$ minute-for-minute and masking upstream improvements.
2.  **Combined Intervention Necessity:** To realize the 92.5 minutes of savings from Counterfactual 2 in end-to-end flow time, downstream capacity at Station F must either be expanded or dynamically re-scheduled to process synchronized cases as soon as `join_ready` is achieved.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

### Recommendation 1: Dynamic Capacity Allocation & Queue Reduction for Machine Stress Test (Branch B)

*   **Mechanism:** Implement cross-training so that administrative staff (Branch C) or lab technicians (Branch A) can support Branch B machine setups during morning peak arrival windows (08:30–11:00). Alternatively, introduce a dynamic dispatch rule prioritizing cases whose Branch A assay is already underway.
*   **Impact on Metrics:**
    *   Cuts Branch B mean queue delay from $19.17\text{ min}$ to $<5\text{ min}$.
    *   Reduces average fork-join stage elapsed time by $\sim 7.71\text{ min/case}$.
    *   Substantially compresses tail flow-time variance (e.g., reduces `F07` flow time from 130 min).
*   **Risks & Secondary Bottlenecks:**
    *   *New Bottleneck Floor:* Once Branch B queueing is eliminated, Branch A's fixed $50\text{ min}$ service time becomes the hard non-reducible floor for $91.7\%$ of cases.
    *   *Resource Over-utilization:* Cross-training administrative staff may cause minor queue spillovers in Branch C if Doc Review volume surges.

### Recommendation 2: Downstream First-In-First-Out (FIFO) Fast-Track & Capacity Realignment at Release Station F

*   **Mechanism:** Transition Station F from a passive batch-arrival model to a pull-based notification system. Configure automated alerts when a case reaches $T_{\text{join}}$ so that F1/F2 resources prepare the workbench 5 minutes prior to synchronization.
*   **Impact on Metrics:**
    *   Directly eliminates $13.83\text{ min}$ of non-value-added downstream queueing per case.
    *   Unlocks 100% of the time savings achieved by upstream branch interventions.
    *   Reduces mean case flow time from $115.25\text{ min}$ to $101.42\text{ min}$.
*   **Risks & Resource Utilization:**
    *   *Labor Costs:* May require adjusting F1/F2 shift start times slightly earlier (08:30 instead of 09:08) to handle early-morning arrivals (`F01`).
    *   *Starvation vs. Queueing:* Staggered arrivals may lead to brief periods of resource idle time at Station F between case completions.

---

### Implementation & Measurement Strategy

1.  **Pilot Phase:** Implement Recommendation 2 (Station F dynamic dispatch) first. Because downstream queueing is an unconstrained waste component affecting all cases, eliminating it requires no upstream operational changes and immediately yields a 13.83 min/case flow-time reduction.
2.  **Phase 2 Implementation:** Roll out Recommendation 1 (Branch B cross-training).
3.  **Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) to Monitor:**
    *   Branch B Queue Time ($Q_B$).
    *   Synchronization Wait Ratio ($W_{\text{sync}, C} / D_{\text{FJ}}$).
    *   Downstream Queue Delay ($Q_{\text{final}}$).
    *   Resource Utilization rates for F1, F2, and Tech resources.

# 7. Limits of inference

While event logs provide accurate temporal measurements, process-mining inferences from timestamps alone are subject to fundamental limits:

1.  **Causality vs. Correlation in Queue Delays:**
    *   Timestamps record *when* work started (`B_start`), but cannot explain *why* queueing occurred. A 30-minute queue delay on Branch B could stem from physical machine setup constraints, operator unavailability, batching policies, or upstream sample preparation delays.
2.  **Resource Capacity and External Workloads:**
    *   The dataset tracks 12 cases over a single day. It does not record resource staffing levels, shift schedules, or whether resources (e.g., Techs on Branch B) were concurrently handling external tasks or non-monitored cases outside this batch.
3.  **Work Preemption and Unobserved Interruptions:**
    *   Active service time is calculated as $T_{\text{end}} - T_{\text{start}}$. This implicitly assumes continuous, uninterrupted labor. If a technician starts Branch A at 08:10, pauses for 15 minutes to recalibrate equipment, and finishes at 09:00, the true active hands-on labor is 35 minutes, not 50 minutes.
4.  **True Nature of Downstream Waiting ($Q_{\text{final}}$):**
    *   The delay $T_{\text{final\_start}} - T_{\text{join}}$ is modeled as downstream resource queueing. However, timestamps cannot prove whether this delay is caused by resource starvation at F1/F2, a physical transport/runner delay between stations, or an intentional batch-release policy (e.g., releasing completed cases to release review only at fixed 20-minute time blocks).
5.  **Upstream Operational Mechanics:**
    *   `fork_release - arrival` is labeled upstream elapsed time ($15\text{--}20\text{ min}$). Without sub-activity logs during this window, we cannot determine whether this represents active preparation work, buffer queueing, or administrative intake processing.
