    def _seq(*children):
        return ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, children=list(children))

        return ProcessTree(label=label)

def _fmt_move(move):
    lm, mm = (move if isinstance(move, (tuple, list)) and len(move) == 2

    lm, mm = _clean(lm), _clean(mm)
    if lm and mm:
        return f"S({mm})"
    if lm:

    if mm:

## 1. Materiality Framework

Two orthogonal axes: severity (consequence) and confidence (evidential certainty). Never merged.

Severity dimensions (weighted lexicographically-ish):
1. Control objective at stake. Hierarchy: payment integrity (approval before payment, P3) > mandatory fraud screening on triggered claims (P2) > auto-approval eligibility guardrails (P1) > evidence/audit-trail completeness (P4 + documentation duties) > analytics/model quality. Rationale: higher objectives protect irreversible outflows of money; lower ones protect information and decisions.
2. Amount/exposure: direct loss ceiling and regulatory materiality proxy. Amount alone never decides: €4,500 (CL-610) with a fresh bank change can outrank €18,000 (CL-609) without fraud indicators.
3. Reversibility: a payment to a fraudulent account is often unrecoverable within hours; notification and evidence defects are correctable. Irreversibility upgrades severity.
4. Timing: sequence violations (pay before approval) are categorically worse than post-hoc documentation gaps; the 24h notification window (P4) is measured in hours.
5. Customer and regulatory harm: diverted claimant funds, vulnerable customers, conduct findings, SAR/AML obligations, audit findings.
6. Recurrence/systemicity: per-case risk × frequency. A low-severity pattern recurring 74 times/month or a guard absent across the auto-approval population can outrank a single larger case for remediation priority.
7. Evidence reliability: system-of-record timestamps (bank acceptance) strong; signed external documents strong only if stably linked; mutable text links and verbal statements weak. Severity is assessed on the evidenced facts; weak evidence lowers confidence, not automatically severity — but it can cap how far we act until verified.
8. Model scope (P6): deviations caused by the model omitting legitimate variants (deny/withdraw) are analytics/governance issues, not operational failures.

Severity anchors:
- 5 — Breach of payment-integrity controls (payment before applicable approval; mandatory screen absent on a triggered claim) with material exposure or fraud indicators; irreversible; potentially reportable.
- 4 — Explicit P1/P2 trigger violated creating a plausible fraud vector or material exposure that requires urgent verification (e.g., payment to a freshly changed account without screening).
- 3 — Control gap on moderate exposure (P1 breach without fraud indicators) or a required control on a high-value case supported only by weak evidence; prompt investigation.
- 2 — Audit-trail/evidence-quality deficiency where the substantive control appears performed; correctable; escalate if verification fails.
- 1 — No business deviation: technical noise, duplicates, label mismatch, silent moves, model-scope artifacts.

Confidence scale:
- High — corroborated by system-of-record data or unambiguous attributes (timestamps, amounts, risk codes); no plausible innocent alternative.
- Medium — single-source or mutable evidence; innocent alternatives remain open (late recording, integration gap, linkage error).
- Low — contested interpretation or missing linkage.

Principles: (i) severity = what is at stake if our reading is right; confidence = how sure we are of the reading. (ii) A missing log event is not proof an activity did not occur — evidence may live in another channel (P4) or an unintegrated system; but where policy demands documented evidence (P2 screening), the absence of documentation is itself the control failure, so confidence in the failure can be high even when occurrence is unknowable. (iii) Act on severity, hedge with confidence: severity-5/medium-confidence still triggers immediate investigation; severity-2/medium triggers evidence verification. (iv) Confidence in the diagnosis  probability of realized loss: CL-602's control failure is certain; whether fraud occurred is a separate question. (v) Severity carries explicit escalation triggers when verification fails (e.g., CL-608 24 if portal delivery is disproved).

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Definition: raw_cost = min over alignments  c(move) for a fixed triple (model, log, cost function). It measures representational disagreement between two artifacts. Business risk is a property of the world: what was at stake, what left the firm, what can be recovered, what a regulator will find. The mapping between the two is many-to-many in both directions; in this portfolio it is close to uninformative.

(a) Cost-function design. Costs are uniform 1 per visible move, no business weighting (stated). CL-601 costs 3 for three telemetry log-moves on a fully compliant €1,200 claim; CL-602 costs 1 for a €480,000 HIGH-risk payment with a fresh bank change and no documented fraud screen. The most dangerous case in the portfolio is at the cheapest meaningful cost tier; a harmless case is three times more expensive. With unit costs, an alignment is a similarity metric, not a risk metric.

(b) Irreversibility and repairability. The aligner "repairs" CL-604's pay-before-approval by re-ordering moves for a total of 3 — the same price as CL-601's autosave noise. But alignment repair is fictional: the bank accepted €90,000 at 10:03, and no edit sequence reverses that. P3 exists precisely because cheap repair  cheap consequence. Cost treats deviations as spelling errors; business treats some as wire transfers.

(c) Data conditions are invisible. CL-609 (€18,000, MEDIUM, auto-approved) and CL-610 (bank change one hour before payment, auto-approved) both cost exactly 0 — perfect fitness — because the process tree's Auto Approve branch is unconditional. P1/P2 live in attributes (amount, risk, bank-change recency) that a control-flow alignment never reads. Four such zero-cost failures exist. Fitness  compliance: the model encodes only necessary sequencing, not sufficiency conditions.

(d) Hidden moves. CL-605 contains T(tau,c=0): the silent routing transition is an artifact of the treePetri-net conversion of the XOR. It is fitting by construction and correctly priced 0. Two lessons: silent moves must be excluded from any deviation count, and — conversely — a cost of 0 is not evidence of correctness (see (c)). Zero cost can mean "perfect" or "perfectly wrong."

(e) Activity granularity and labels. CL-611 costs 2 because the core system says "Assess Damage" and the procedure says "Assess Loss" for the same act; the case is otherwise flawlessly executed (screen, manual approval, pay, notify). CL-601 costs 3 partly because UI keystrokes are logged as events (granularity mismatch). Vocabulary and granularity inflate cost without changing what happened.

(f) Duplicated telemetry. CL-603's second Notify event is a connector retry with the same message ID — one customer-facing action, two log events, cost 1. At-least-once delivery semantics price themselves into conformance. (74 cases/month: a systematic cost bias with no systematic risk.)

(g) Missing-but-external evidence. CL-606 and CL-608 each cost 1 for a model move that means "the core log lacks this event." In CL-606 a signed adjuster report at 10:05 suggests the assessment happened elsewhere; in CL-608 a portal message at +3h suggests notification happened in another channel (P4 explicitly permits this). ## 1. Materiality Framework

**Two orthogonal axes.** *Severity* answers: "if our reading of the evidence is right, how much is at stake?" *Confidence* answers: "how sure are we of the reading?" They are recorded separately, never averaged, and never merged with technical alignment cost, which is a property of the (model, log, cost function) triple, not of the business.

**Severity dimensions (in rough priority order):**

1. **Control objective at stake.** Hierarchy: payment integrity (approval before payment, P3) > mandatory fraud screening on triggered claims (P2) > auto-approval eligibility guardrails (P1) > evidence/audit-trail completeness (P4 and documentation duties) > analytics/model quality. Higher objectives protect irreversible outflows of money; lower ones protect information.
2. **Amount / exposure.** Direct loss ceiling and a proxy for regulatory materiality. Amount alone never decides: €4,500 with a one-hour-old bank change (CL-610) outranks €18,000 without fraud indicators (CL-609).
3. **Reversibility.** A payment to a diverted account is often unrecoverable within hours; notification and evidence defects are correctable. Irreversibility upgrades severity.
4. **Timing.** Sequence violations (pay before approval) are categorically worse than post-hoc documentation gaps; the P4 window is 24 hours.
5. **Customer / regulatory harm.** Diverted claimant funds, conduct and records-retention findings, SAR/AML obligations.
6. **Recurrence / systemicity.** Per-case risk × frequency: a benign-looking defect recurring 74 times/month, or a guard absent across the whole auto-approval population, can outrank a single larger case in remediation priority.
7. **Evidence reliability.** System-of-record timestamps (bank acceptance) = strong; signed external documents = strong only if stably linked to the case; mutable text links and verbal statements = weak. Weak evidence lowers confidence; it caps how far we act before verification, but does not by itself lower what is at stake.
8. **Model scope (P6).** Deviations caused by the model omitting legitimate variants (denial, withdrawal) are analytics/governance issues, not operational failures.

**Severity anchors:**

- **5** — Breach of payment-integrity controls (payment before applicable approval; mandatory screen absent on a triggered claim) with material exposure or fraud indicators; irreversible; potentially reportable.
- **4** — Explicit P1/P2 trigger violated, creating a plausible fraud vector or material exposure requiring urgent verification.
- **3** — Control gap on moderate exposure (e.g., P1 breach without fraud indicators), or a required control on a high-value case supported only by weak evidence; prompt investigation.
- **2** — Audit-trail / evidence-quality deficiency where the substantive control appears to have been performed; correctable; escalate if verification fails.
- **1** — No business deviation: technical noise, duplicates, label mismatch, silent moves, model-scope artifacts.

**Confidence scale:** *High* = corroborated by system-of-record data or unambiguous attributes; no plausible innocent alternative. *Medium* = single-source or mutable evidence; innocent alternatives remain open (late recording, integration gap, linkage error). *Low* = contested interpretation or missing linkage.

**How confidence differs from severity — operating principles:**
- Severity is the consequence; confidence is the evidential probability. A severity-5/medium-confidence finding still triggers immediate investigation (asymmetric loss); a severity-2/medium finding triggers evidence verification.
- A missing log event is **not** proof an activity did not occur — evidence may live in another channel (P4) or an unintegrated system. But where policy demands *documented* evidence (P2 screening), the absence of documentation is itself the control failure; confidence in the failure can be high even when the underlying occurrence is unknowable (CL-602).
- Confidence in the diagnosis is not the probability of realized loss: CL-602's control failure is certain; whether fraud actually occurred is a separate question for investigation.
- Severity carries explicit escalation triggers: e.g., CL-608 moves 24 if portal delivery is disproved; CL-606 moves 24 if the adjuster report cannot be linked to the case.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

**Principle applied throughout:** a missing log event is *not* proof that an activity did not occur — it may be logged elsewhere, mislinked, or performed in an unintegrated system. Discriminating evidence is what separates the hypotheses.

**CL-602 — missing Fraud Screen.**
- *H1 (process bypass):* screening never performed; verbal check substituted.
- *H2 (integration gap):* screening performed in a dedicated fraud tool that does not write to the core log.
- *H3 (correlation error):* screen event written to another case ID.
- *Discriminating evidence:* query the fraud-screening system by claimant/case number and time window; check whether other HIGH cases in the same period carry fraud events (if they do, integration works and H1 gains weight for this case); the approver's system-access logs (did she open the fraud tool?); timestamps of the bank change vs. approval.
- *Note:* under every hypothesis, P2's documented-evidence requirement failed, so the control failure stands; only the response differs (fraud investigation vs. integration fix vs. data repair).

**CL-604 — payment before recorded approval.**
- *H1 (genuine sequencing breach):* payment released before any decision.
- *H2 (late recording):* decision made earlier (verbal/email) and recorded at 10:20.
- *H3 (batch artifact):* payment file prepared at a prior cut-off; the 10:03 bank acceptance reflects batch execution, making timestamps misleading.
- *Discriminating evidence:* payment batch/file logs and execution vs. value dates; approver email/calendar and workflow audit trail; whether the approval record has an effective-time field; prevalence across cases (a batch artifact would recur at the same cut-off time across many cases).
- *Note:* even under H2/H3, approval-before-payment was not demonstrable *at execution time* — a control weakness remains; severity holds pending facts.

**CL-610 — auto approve despite bank change (Pattern B).**
- *H1 (missing guard):* the rule never checks bank changes.
- *H2 (TOCTOU race):* the guard ran at decision time; the bank change landed after the decision but before payment.
- *H3 (visibility gap):* the bank-change event lives in a system the decision service does not consult.
- *Discriminating evidence:* rule configuration/version at case time; decision timestamp vs. bank-change ingestion timestamp vs. payment timestamp; whether the other three synchronous auto-approve cases show the same signature (immediately-preceding bank change  race; scattered conditions  missing guard); replay the rule with corrected ordering.
- *Note:* H2 implies the fix is a re-check at payment execution, not merely a decision-time guard.

**CL-606 — M(Assess Loss) with an external adjuster report (Pattern D).**
- *H1 (integration gap):* assessment performed externally, not logged.
- *H2 (linkage error):* the report belongs to another case and the assessment is genuinely missing.
- *H3 (rubber-stamping):* the report was signed without substantive assessment.
- *Discriminating evidence:* whether this case is one of the 2 with unstable document-to-case IDs; the adjuster's assignment records; consistency between report contents (damage description, amounts) and the claim data; adjuster-system timestamps.
- *Note:* a signed report is evidence of activity, not of quality; and the missing core event is not proof the activity did not occur.

**CL-608 — M(Notify Claimant) with a CRM portal message (Pattern G).**
- *H1 (channel fragmentation):* the portal message was genuinely sent at +3h; the core is simply not correlated.
- *H2 (post-hoc link):* the mutable text link was added or edited afterward; the message may belong to another case or may never have been delivered.
- *H3 (draft/scheduled):* the message was drafted but not delivered.
- *Discriminating evidence:* immutable portal delivery logs / SMTP-queue records / message IDs in append-only storage; hash comparison of the current link text against stored values; claimant read receipts or complaints; whether other cases show the same mutable-link pattern.
- *Note:* until verified, treat P4 as "probably met, not demonstrable" — which is itself a records-control finding.

**CL-611 — "Assess Damage" vs. "Assess Loss" (Pattern E).**
- *H1 (mapping gap):* one activity, two labels — supported by the signed procedure and the post-case mapping update.
- *H2 (two activities):* damage assessment and loss assessment are genuinely distinct and conflated.
- *Discriminating evidence:* do the two labels ever co-occur within one case (if yes  two activities; if never  one activity, two labels); work instructions and role descriptions; user interviews; mapping-table version history.
- *Note:* if H2 held, the model needs a second activity, not a mapping fix.

**CL-603 + 74-case connector pattern — duplicate notification.**
- *H1 (transport retry):* at-least-once connector re-emitted the same message — strongly supported by identical message IDs.
- *H2 (business re-send):* a user re-sent — would usually create a new message ID.
- *H3 (producer double-write):* the core emitted the event twice.
- *Discriminating evidence:* connector retry logs vs. event timestamps; message-ID equality within each pair; whether claimants report receiving two visible messages; producer logs.
- *Note:* identical message IDs across the pair strongly favor transport-level duplication.

**CL-607 / CL-612 — scope dispute (Pattern F).**
- *H1 (artifact):* the model scope excludes denied/withdrawn variants; costs are artifacts (the model-owner reading).
- *H2 (in-scope):* these variants belong in scope because customer-communication duties apply (the process-owner reading).
- *Discriminating evidence:* governance minutes and policy documents on intake scope; regulatory expectations for denial communication; measured volumes and notification timeliness of denied/withdrawn cases once instrumented.
- *Note:* this is a governance decision, not an evidence question; both readings agree nothing operationally wrong happened in these two cases.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

`raw_cost` is the minimum, over all alignments, of the sum of move costs for a fixed triple: (model, log, cost function). It measures representational disagreement between two artifacts. Business risk is a property of the world: what was at stake, what left the firm, what can be recovered, what a regulator will find. In this portfolio the mapping between the two is close to uninformative:

1. **Cost-function design.** Costs are uniform 1 per visible move with no business weighting (stated). CL-601 costs 3 for three telemetry log-moves on a fully compliant €1,200 claim; CL-602 costs 1 for a €480,000 HIGH-risk payment to a one-day-old bank account with no documented fraud screen. The most dangerous case sits at the cheapest meaningful tier; a harmless case is three times more expensive. Under unit costs, an alignment is a similarity metric, not a risk metric.

2. **Irreversibility and the fiction of repair.** The aligner "repairs" CL-604's pay-before-approval by reordering moves for a total of 3 — the same price as CL-601's autosave noise. Worse, the single real payment is charged twice (L(Pay) once, M(Pay) once), so two of the three cost units are artifacts of the repair rather than counts of events. No edit sequence reverses a bank acceptance at 10:03. P3 exists precisely because cheap repair  cheap consequence.

3. **Data conditions are invisible.** CL-609 (€18,000, MEDIUM, auto-approved) and CL-610 (bank change one hour before payment, auto-approved) both cost exactly 0 — perfect fitness — because the process tree's Auto Approve branch is unconditional. P1 and P2 live in attributes (amount, risk, bank-change recency) that a control-flow alignment never reads; four such zero-cost failures exist. Fitness is not compliance: the model encodes necessary sequencing, not sufficiency conditions.

4. **Hidden moves.** CL-605 contains T(tau, c=0): a silent routing transition produced by the treePetri-net conversion of the XOR. It is fitting by construction and correctly priced 0. Two lessons: silent moves must be excluded from any deviation count — and, conversely, a cost of 0 is not evidence of correctness (see point 3). Zero cost can mean "perfect" or "perfectly wrong."

5. **Activity granularity and labels.** CL-611 costs 2 because the core system says "Assess Damage" and the procedure says "Assess Loss" for the same act; the case is otherwise flawlessly executed. CL-601's cost is inflated by keystroke-level UI events. Vocabulary and granularity add cost without changing what happened.

6. **Duplicated telemetry.** CL-603's second Notify event is a connector retry with the same message ID — one customer-facing action, two log events, cost 1, and 74 cases/month of the same. At-least-once delivery semantics price themselves into conformance statistics.

7. **Missing-but-external evidence.** CL-606 and CL-608 each cost 1 for a model move meaning "the core log lacks this event." In CL-606 a signed adjuster report at 10:05 suggests the assessment happened in another system; in CL-608 a portal message at +3h suggests notification happened in another channel — which P4 explicitly permits. A model move can denote "not logged," "logged elsewhere," or "not done"; edit distance cannot tell which. CL-602's single M(Fraud Screen) shows the same move type at severity 5: identical syntax, opposite semantics.

8. **Model scope.** CL-607 and CL-612 cost 4 — the top tier — because the model has no denial or withdrawal branch and must "imagine" Assess/Approve/Pay/Notify that were *correctly never performed*. The cost is literally a charge for not doing things that should not have been done. Most raw-cost  4 cases are such artifacts; ranking by cost would send investigators to the cases where nothing went wrong, while CL-609/CL-610 at cost 0 would never be examined.

9. **Non-uniqueness of the alignment itself.** For CL-603, skipping either duplicate notification is optimal; alternative optimal alignments exist elsewhere. The cost is well-defined; the story is not.

**Cost vs. severity across the portfolio:** (601: 3/1), (602: 1/5), (603: 1/1), (604: 3/5), (605: 0/1), (606: 1/2), (607: 4/1), (608: 1/2), (609: 0/3), (610: 0/4), (611: 2/1), (612: 4/1). The Spearman correlation is weak (computed in Section 7): the two severity-5 cases cost 1 and 3, while the two cost-4 cases are severity 1. Raw cost is usable only as a triage *search key* after filtering scope artifacts, collapsing label/telemetry noise, and joining data-aware policy checks — and if cost-based ranking is retained at all, the cost function must be re-weighted (e.g., P3-relevant moves weighted ×100). Business prioritization must rest on the materiality framework, not on the edit distance.

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# (1) Parse the supplied process-tree string.
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
PT_STRING = ("-> ( 'Register Claim', 'Validate Coverage', 'Assess Loss', "
             "X ( -> ( 'Fraud Screen', 'Manual Approve' ), 'Auto Approve' ), "
             "'Pay Claim', 'Notify Claimant' )")

tree = None
if hasattr(pm4py, "parse_process_tree"):
    for candidate in (PT_STRING, PT_STRING.replace(" ", "")):
        try:
            tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(candidate)
            print("[1] Process tree parsed from the supplied string.")
            break
        except Exception as err:
            print(f"[1] parse_process_tree attempt failed ({err}); next form...")

if tree is None:
    # Fallback: build the semantically identical tree programmatically
    # (guards against grammar/format differences across PM4Py versions).
    from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator, ProcessTree

    def _seq(*children):
        return ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, children=list(children))

    def _xor(*children):
        return ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR, children=list(children))

    def _leaf(label):
        return ProcessTree(label=label)

    tree = _seq(
        _leaf("Register Claim"),
        _leaf("Validate Coverage"),
        _leaf("Assess Loss"),
        _xor(_seq(_leaf("Fraud Screen"), _leaf("Manual Approve")),
             _leaf("Auto Approve")),
        _leaf("Pay Claim"),
        _leaf("Notify Claimant"),
    )
    print("[1] Used programmatic fallback (identical semantics).")

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# (4) Conformance diagnostics via alignments.
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
def _fmt_move(move):
    """Render a PM4Py alignment move in the report's S/L/M/T notation.
    Handles both None-style and '>>'-style skip representations."""
    try:
        lm, mm = move
    except Exception:
        return str(move)

    def _clean(x):
        return None if x is None or str(x) in ("None", ">>", "") else str(x)

    lm, mm = _clean(lm), _clean(mm)
    if lm and mm:
        return f"S({mm})"
    if lm:
        return f"L({lm})"
    if mm:
        return f"M({mm})"
    return "T(tau)"

pm4py_costs, pm4py_moves = {}, {}
try:
    results = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(event_log, net, im, fm)
    for trace, res in zip(event_log, results):
        case_id = trace.attributes.get("concept:name")
        pm4py_costs[case_id] = res.get("cost")
        pm4py_moves[case_id] = [_fmt_move(m) for m in res.get("alignment", [])]
    print("[4] Alignments computed. NOTE: PM4Py may return an alternative "
          "optimal alignment, or different move/cost formatting, than the "
          "supplied authoritative notation; treat any difference as an "
          "artifact, not a correction.")
except Exception as err:
    print(f"[4] Alignment computation failed: {err}")

              f"moves={' '.join(pm4py_moves.get(case_id, []))}")
