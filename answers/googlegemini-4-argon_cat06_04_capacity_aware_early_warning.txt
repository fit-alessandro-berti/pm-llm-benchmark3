## 1. Decision rule

A deployable intervention policy must allocate scarce capacity by maximizing **Expected Net Portfolio Value (ENPV)**—balancing direct causal risk reduction against operational costs, customer disruption, queue externalities, and option value—rather than ranking raw observational probabilities ($p_{\text{now}}$).

### Step 1: Hard Eligibility Gates (Process & State Filter)
A case $i$ is eligible for a Senior Diagnostic Swarm at time $t$ if and only if it passes four structural gates:
1. **Stage Gate:** Current stage $\in \{\text{Triage}, \text{Diagnosis Started}\}$. Once `Diagnosis Complete` is reached, the causal effect approaches zero; the case is routed to resolution-expediting workflows instead.
2. **Treatment History Gate:** The case has not previously received a swarm, unless a **Materially New Event (MNE)**—such as a diagnostic failure, new log upload, or architectural discovery—has been logged since the prior swarm.
3. **Alert Persistence & Novelty Gate:** If the alert is a repeat alert ($k > 1$) without an intervening swarm, it is eligible only if:
   - An MNE has occurred since alert $k-1$, **OR**
   - The alert represents a structural escalation across a decision tier due to genuine time decay rather than a static 30-minute refresh cycle.
4. **Data Integrity & Feasible Horizon Gate:** 
   - All Tier-1 features (specifically live queue and stage timers) must be non-imputed. If imputed, the case is placed on a 15-minute **Data Verification Hold** unless time-to-SLA ($T_{\text{SLA}}$) $< 2.0\text{ hours}$.
   - $T_{\text{SLA}}$ must exceed minimum swarm execution + minimum viable resolution time ($0.75\text{ h} + 0.75\text{ h} = 1.5\text{ h}$). Cases at exactly $1.5\text{ h}$ require an immediate pre-flight feasibility check by the Triage Lead.

### Step 2: Segment-Specific Calibration Adjustment
Because observational predictions suffer from known structural drift, we map raw $p_{\text{now}}$ to a calibrated baseline probability $\tilde{p}_i = \text{clip}(p_{\text{now}, i} + \delta_{\text{seg}}, 0.01, 0.99)$:
- **SME ($\delta_{\text{SME}} = +0.065$):** Adjusts upward for the $0.04\text{–}0.09$ systematic underprediction.
- **Strategic Enterprise ($\delta_{\text{Strat}} = -0.085$):** Adjusts downward for the $0.05\text{–}0.12$ overprediction following the workflow redesign.
- **Public Sector ($\delta_{\text{Pub}} = 0.00$):** Retains baseline $p_{\text{now}}$.
- **Non-Native-Language Support ($\delta_{\text{NNLS}} = 0.00$, widened interval):** Retains point estimate but applies an epistemic uncertainty penalty to the lower bound during robust optimization.

### Step 3: Causal Effect Discounting and False-Alarm Weighting
Let $[\Delta p_{i,\min}, \Delta p_{i,\max}]$ be the supplied absolute risk-reduction range, with midpoint $\overline{\Delta p}_i$.
1. **Effective Risk Reduction ($\Delta p_i^{\text{eff}}$):** In the base policy, we use $\overline{\Delta p}_i$, capped at $\tilde{p}_i$. However, if $\tilde{p}_i < 0.45$, we apply a **Low-Risk Realizability Discount** ($\gamma = 0.75$) because low-risk cases in `Triage` frequently resolve organically without needing senior diagnostic redirection.
2. **Expected False-Alarm Burden ($B_i^{\text{FA}}$):** Disruption occurs primarily when we interrupt the customer and case workflow for a case that would not have breached anyway, or when the intervention fails to help. We weight the stated burden $F_i$ by the probability of a non-breach under no intervention, plus a baseline disruption factor:
   $$\text{Expected Burden}_i = F_i \cdot \left(0.5 + 0.5(1 - \tilde{p}_i)\right)$$
   *(Note: For direct auditable comparisons in Section 2 and 3, we also evaluate the conservative specification where 100% of $F_i$ is incurred upon swarming).*

### Step 4: Marginal Portfolio Value & Spillover Hurdle
For each eligible case $i$, calculate the **Direct Expected Net Benefit ($\text{DENB}_i$)** under full false-alarm burden accounting:
$$\text{DENB}_i(\text{Base}) = \left(\Delta p_i^{\text{eff}} \times V_i\right) - C_{\text{swarm}} - F_i$$
where $V_i$ is the value of avoiding breach and $C_{\text{swarm}} = €450$.

To account for epistemic uncertainty in segments with sparse validation (e.g., Non-Native-Language Support) or wide effect ranges, we compute a **Robust Priority Score ($S_i$)** using a Hurwicz criterion ($\lambda = 0.65$ weight on the Base midpoint, $1-\lambda = 0.35$ weight on the conservative lower bound $\Delta p_{i,\min}$):
$$S_i = 0.65 \cdot \text{DENB}_i(\text{Base}) + 0.35 \cdot \text{DENB}_i(\text{Lower})$$

**The Spillover & Option-Value Hurdle:**
Using a slot removes two senior specialists for 45 minutes, imposing an aggregate delay of 8–20 minutes and increasing breach risk by $\delta_{\text{spill}} \in [0.01, 0.03]$ across $N_{\text{untreated}} = 6$ diagnosis-stage cases. If the average breach value of an untreated diagnosis-stage case in the general queue is $\bar{V}_{\text{queue}} \approx €15,000$, the expected marginal spillover cost per slot is:
$$\text{Spillover Cost} = 6 \times \bar{\delta}_{\text{spill}} \times \bar{V}_{\text{queue}} = 6 \times 0.02 \times €15,000 = €1,800 \quad (\text{Range: } €900\text{–}€2,700)$$

**Allocation Rule:**
1. Rank all eligible cases by $S_i$.
2. Allocate slots sequentially only to cases where $S_i > \text{Spillover Hurdle} (€1,800)$ **AND** $\text{DENB}_i(\text{Lower}) > €0$ (unless protected by an SLA-equity floor, defined in Section 5).
3. **Reserve Slot Policy:** Hold the 4th slot in reserve for the anticipated before-15:00 regulatory incident unless a live case has an immediate urgency horizon ($T_{\text{SLA}} \le 4.0\text{ h}$) **and** its $S_i$ exceeds the expected option value of the regulatory reserve ($V_{\text{reg}}^{\text{opt}} \approx €4,500$ net of spillover).

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

Because the predictive model refreshes every 30 minutes on prefix state features, static or slowly evolving cases generate redundant alerts. Treating every 30-minute refresh as an independent decision leads to alert fatigue, oscillatory prioritization, and wasted capacity.

### Temporal Governance Architecture
1. **State-Hash Suppression (Alert De-duplication):**
   - Each alert $k$ generates a state vector $E_k = (\text{Stage}, \text{AssignedQueue}, N_{\text{logs}}, N_{\text{comms}}, \text{DiagCode}, \text{DataQualityFlag})$.
   - If $E_k == E_{k-1}$ and $|\Delta p_{\text{now}}| = |p_{\text{now}, k} - p_{\text{prev}, k-1}| \le 0.05$ (pure elapsed-time decay), the alert is classified as a **Static Refresh**. It is suppressed from triggering an active swarm allocation review and logged silently.
2. **Cool-Down Window:**
   - **Wait / Deferred Decision Cool-Down:** Once a case is evaluated and placed in `Wait` (e.g., I-04, I-05, I-08, I-10), it enters a **90-minute cool-down** (3 refresh cycles) during which it cannot re-alert the Swarm Coordinator unless an override trigger occurs.
   - **Post-Swarm Lockout:** Once a case receives a swarm, it enters a **Permanent Stage Lockout**. A second swarm is strictly forbidden unless an explicit **Materially New Event (MNE)** occurs *and* the Swarm Lead certifies that the initial 45-minute swarm was blocked by missing external data that has now arrived.
3. **Escalation & Re-Entry Triggers (Breaking Cool-Down):**
   A suppressed or cooled-down case immediately re-enters the active candidate pool if any of four conditions holds:
   - **Event Trigger (MNE):** Arrival of new diagnostic telemetry, customer log upload, failed sub-test, or dependency failure ($E_k \neq E_{k-1}$).
   - **Probability Jump Trigger:** $\Delta p_{\text{now}} \ge +0.10$ over a single 30-minute window, verified as **non-imputed** (preventing data dropouts like I-06 from triggering automatic escalation without verification).
   - **Stage Transition Trigger:** The case transitions from `Triage` $\to$ `Diagnosis Started` while $\tilde{p}_i \ge 0.50$ (e.g., when I-04 enters active diagnosis).
   - **SLA Stagnation Watchdog (Anti-Starvation Escalation):** A case remains in `Triage` with zero event updates for $\ge 2.0\text{ hours}$ while $T_{\text{SLA}} \le 4.0\text{ hours}$. Instead of consuming a $€450$ Senior Swarm, this triggers a **5-minute Triage Manager Ping** to verify the ticket is not orphaned.

### Explicit Comparison: Case I-05 vs. Case I-07

| Dimension | Case I-05 (`Triage`, 5.0 h left, $p: 0.55 \to 0.57$) | Case I-07 (`Triage`, 1.5 h left, $p: 0.75 \to 0.72$) |
|---|---|---|
| **Alert History & Mechanics** | **3rd alert; no new event.** The $+0.02$ shift ($0.55 \to 0.57$) is purely deterministic clock-decay from the 30-minute model refresh cycle. | **3rd alert; new customer log uploaded.** The $-0.03$ shift ($0.75 \to 0.72$) reflects the model ingesting a new event feature ($E_3 \neq E_2$). |

However, 60+ minutes in `Triage` with no event suggests possible *queue stagnation*. | **High informational novelty.** Even though $p_{\text{now}}$ ticked slightly down ($0.72$, or $\tilde{p} \approx 0.785$ after SME adjustment), the newly uploaded customer log provides concrete diagnostic material that did not exist at Alert 1 or 2. |

However, with `1.5 h` left and $V = €5,000$, its net benefit ($€800$) fails the $€1,800$ spillover hurdle, and a 45-min swarm leaves insufficient resolution time. |

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

Optimizing strictly for expected pecuniary value ($\Delta p_i \times V_i$) under imperfect calibration and shared queues creates five structural distortions that will degrade both equity and long-term model validity if left unchecked.

### 1. Value-Based Starvation of SME and Lower-Value Cases
- **Risk:** Strategic Enterprise contracts ($€25\text{k}\text{–}€100\text{k}$) are $5\times\text{ to }20\times$ larger than SME contracts ($€5\text{k}\text{–}€11\text{k}$). A pure expected-Euro rule systematically starves SME cases (I-05, I-07, I-10) even when SME treatment effects are substantially higher ($0.20\text{–}0.40$ vs. $0.05\text{–}0.22$). Over time, chronic SLA breaches in the SME segment cause reputational churn and violate contractual good-faith obligations.
- **Concrete Safeguard (Hybrid Normalized Utility & SME Quota Floor):**
  1. Replace raw contract value $V_i$ in the scoring formula with a **Concave Blended Value** $V_i^* = 0.6 V_i + 0.4 \bar{V}_{\text{portfolio}} \cdot \left(\frac{V_i}{\bar{V}_{\text{seg}}}\right)$, which weights relative within-segment importance alongside raw Euro value.
  2. Enforce a **Rolling Weekly Segment Floor**: At least **20% of swarm slots** over any rolling 5-day window must be allocated to SME/Public Sector cases whenever eligible cases exist with $\Delta p_i \ge 0.25$ and $\tilde{p}_i \ge 0.60$.

### 2. Epistemic Uncertainty in Non-Native-Language Support (I-09)
- **Risk:** Small validation samples mean both baseline risk and causal effect estimates are noisy. Risk-averse algorithms that heavily penalize variance will systematically under-allocate swarms to Non-Native-Language Support cases, compounding linguistic barriers with algorithmic neglect. Conversely, deploying a swarm of specialists who lack language tools wastes the slot.
- **Concrete Safeguard (Bounded Uncertainty Penalty + Tooling Verification):**
  1. Cap the epistemic uncertainty discount on sparse segments at a maximum of **15% of the lower bound** (preventing variance penalties from zeroing out priority scores, thereby keeping I-09 in Tier 1).
  2. Require **Language-Fit Routing**: When swarming an NNLS case like I-09, pair the Senior Specialist with a language-aligned liaison or real-time translation workflow so the 45-minute intervention is not lost to communication friction.

### 3. Differential Calibration Across Segments
- **Risk:** The model underpredicts SME risk by $0.04\text{–}0.09$ and overpredicts Strategic Enterprise risk by $0.05\text{–}0.12$. Without explicit adjustment, Strategic cases appear artificially urgent while SME cases are filtered out below thresholds—doubling down on the pecuniary bias.
- **Concrete Safeguard (Group-Specific Conformal / Post-Hoc Recalibration):**
  1. Apply immediate additive segment offsets ($\delta_{\text{SME}} = +0.065$, $\delta_{\text{Strat}} = -0.085$) in the live decision engine.
  2. Replace global Platt scaling with **Mondrian (segment-stratified) isotonic calibration** updated bi-weekly on untreated observations.

### 4. Performative Prediction & Selective Label Censorship (Feedback Loops)
- **Risk:** When we intervene on high-risk cases (e.g., I-09, I-02, I-01) and prevent breaches, their logged outcome is `Breach = 0`. If future model retraining naively treats intervened cases as standard observational data, the model will learn that cases with features matching I-09, I-02, and I-01 have *low* breach risk. On the next retraining cycle, the model will underpredict risk for precisely the cases that need intervention most.
- **Concrete Safeguard (Causal Treatment Logging & IPW Retraining):**
  1. Log a mandatory treatment indicator $W_i \in \{0, 1\}$, propensity score $\pi_i = P(W_i = 1 \mid X_i, \text{QueueState})$, and exact timestamp for every candidate evaluation.
  2. **Prohibit retraining the baseline risk model ($P(Y^0 = 1 \mid X)$) on unadjusted $W_i = 1$ outcomes.** Retrain the baseline prognostic model using Inverse Probability of Censoring Weighting (IPCW) on untreated cases ($W_i = 0$) plus pseudo-outcomes from a Doubly Robust (AIPW) estimator.

### 5. Asymmetric Spillover Harms Falling on Untreated Groups
- **Risk:** Senior specialists are pulled from the shared diagnosis queue to swarm high-value cases. The resulting 8–20 minute delay per slot ($\delta_{\text{spill}} = 0.01\text{–}0.03$) falls directly on untreated cases in `Diagnosis Started`. Because lower-value SME and Public Sector cases are less likely to receive a swarm, they disproportionately bear the queue delay caused by swarming Strategic Enterprise cases—effectively transferring breach risk from rich contracts to small contracts.
- **Concrete Safeguard (Queue-Congestion Circuit Breaker & Priority Compensation):**
  1. **Congestion Circuit Breaker:** If live diagnosis queue wait time exceeds **25 minutes** or active untreated `Diagnosis Started` cases with $T_{\text{SLA}} < 3.0\text{ h}$ exceed 8, freeze new Triage-stage swarms and restrict swarms to maximum 1 active pair at a time.
  2. **Spillover Compensation Rule:** Any untreated case in `Diagnosis Started` that experiences $>15\text{ minutes}$ of queue delay due to concurrent swarms is automatically bumped to the head of the standard diagnosis dispatch queue (`Priority-1 Dispatch`) as soon as the next regular engineer frees up.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

To maintain decision-theoretic validity over time, monitoring is structured across five distinct pillars with explicit statistical triggers for policy revision.

### 1. Operational & Data-Quality Monitoring (Real-Time / Daily)
- **Metrics:**
  - *Feature Integrity Rate:* Percentage of live predictions with imputed Tier-1 features (e.g., live queue field failures as seen in I-06).
  - *Alert Hygiene Ratio:* Ratio of Static Refresh alerts (suppressed) to Actionable Novel Alerts, and Swarm Override frequency.
  - *Stage Execution Compliance:* Percentage of swarms initiated and completed prior to `Diagnosis Complete`, and post-swarm resolution buffer remaining.
- **Action Trigger:** If any Tier-1 feature imputation rate exceeds **3% over a 2-hour window**, automatically switch the decision engine to a **Fallback Conservative Model** (excluding the failing feature) and require manual Triage Lead sign-off before slot allocation.

### 2. Predictive Calibration Monitoring (Weekly / Bi-Weekly)
- **Metrics:**
  - Segment-stratified **Expected Calibration Error (ECE)**, **Brier Score**, and **Calibration-in-the-Large (O/E ratio)** evaluated strictly on *untreated* cases ($W_i = 0$, weighted by inverse propensity of treatment $1/(1 - \pi_i)$).
- **Action Trigger:** If segment-specific $|O/E - 1.0| > 0.05$ or segment ECE $> 0.04$ over a rolling 100-case window per segment, update the segment offset $\delta_{\text{seg}}$ within 24 hours and trigger a stratified isotonic recalibration refit.

### 3. Causal-Effect ($\hat{\tau}$) Estimation Plan (Monthly)
Because supplied effect ranges ($[0.05, 0.40]$) are uncertain priors, we must identify true Conditional Average Treatment Effects ($\text{CATE}(x) = E[Y^0 - Y^1 \mid X=x]$) without unethical wholesale randomization of critical incidents:
- **Identification Strategy:**
  1. **Regression Discontinuity / Boundary Randomization:** For borderline Tier-2/Tier-3 cases where $S_i$ falls within $\pm €400$ of the Spillover Hurdle ($€1,400\text{–}€2,200$), randomize swarm allocation with probability $p_{\text{treat}} = 0.50$ whenever capacity permits.
  2. **Natural Queue-Capacity Instrumental Variable (IV):** Exploit exogenous variation in specialist availability (e.g., shift overlaps, external regulatory arrivals consuming the reserve slot) as an instrument for treatment among eligible Tier-1/Tier-2 cases.
  3. **Proximal Intermediate Outcomes:** Track immediate post-swarm mediators—**Time-to-Diagnosis-Complete ($\Delta T_{\text{diag}}$)** and **Re-assignment / Redirection Rate**—which have higher signal-to-noise ratios than binary SLA breach.
- **Action Trigger:** Update case-type effect priors $[\Delta p_{\min}, \Delta p_{\max}]$ monthly using Empirical Bayes shrinkage combining prior bounds with Doubly Robust CATE estimates. If a segment's realized $\hat{\tau}$ falls below $0.08$ over 50 treated cases, downgrade its priority multiplier by 50%.

### 4. Capacity & Spillover Externality Monitoring (Weekly)
- **Metrics:**
  - *Empirical Queue Delay Multiplier:* Regress realized wait-time expansion in `Diagnosis Started` ($\Delta W_{\text{queue}}$) and untreated breach rates against the number of concurrent swarms executed per shift ($N_{\text{swarms}} \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$), controlling for total arrival volume.
  - *Reserve Slot Utilization Rate:* Proportion of days the reserved 4th slot is used by a genuine high-priority afternoon arrival vs. expiring unused at 15:00.
- **Action Trigger:**
  - If empirical delay exceeds **22 minutes per slot** or spillover breach increase exceeds **0.03 per untreated case**, raise the Spillover Hurdle from $€1,800$ to $€2,700$ and cap maximum daily swarms at **3 slots**.
  - If the Reserve Slot expires unused on **$>60\%$ of days** over a 3-week period, move the reserve release deadline earlier from 15:00 to **13:30** so deferred cases like I-04 or verified I-06 can capture the capacity in time.

### 5. Fairness & Disparity Auditing (Bi-Weekly)
- **Metrics:**
  - *Treatment Allocation Parity:* Ratio of swarm allocation rate to breach-eligible case volume across SME, Public Sector, Strategic Enterprise, and Non-Native-Language Support.
  - *Net Externality Incidence:* Share of total swarm-induced delay minutes absorbed by each customer segment (measuring whether SME/Public Sector cases absorb $>70\%$ of queue delays while receiving $<20\%$ of swarms).
  - *Realized SLA Breach Rate by Segment:* Tracking whether policy deployment narrows or widens the gap in contractual breach rates across segments.
- **Action Trigger:** If any segment absorbs a **Net Negative Portfolio Impact** (Spillover breaches suffered $>$ Direct breaches prevented) over two consecutive weeks, automatically activate the **Spillover Compensation Rule** (Priority-1 Dispatch for delayed untreated tickets in that segment) and increase the segment’s weight in the Concave Blended Value function $V_i^*$ by $+0.15$.
