## 1. Interaction-graph description

The six jobs never share a component, and each job-flattened case follows the same activity skeleton. They nonetheless form one connected component once object-to-object relations and event co-membership are projected. The binding structure is a set of misaligned cliques: batch `{J1,J2,J3}` and `{J4,J5}` (J6 unbatched), shipment `{J1,J4}`, `{J2,J3}`, `{J5,J6}`, and lot `{J1,J3}` via L8 and `{J2,J5}` via L7. A delay can cross a clique boundary because these partitions do not coincide.

Node types that carry the coupling are Job, Component, ComponentLot, Batch, Machine, Tool, and Shipment. Component nodes are the local requirements (Ji requires Ci). They do not themselves connect jobs. Connection appears only through lots, batches, tools, machines, and shipments.

Directly observed edges come from the O2O table:

- `requires`: J1→C1, J2→C2, J3→C3, J4→C4, J5→C5, J6→C6
- `contains`: L7→C2, L7→C5, L8→C1, L8→C3
- `batches`: B1→J1, B1→J2, B1→J3, B2→J4, B2→J5
- `consolidates`: S1→J1, S1→J4, S2→J2, S2→J3, S3→J5, S3→J6

Directly observed event hyperedges (one event, many objects), distinct from master-data edges:

- `Batch Cure` at 12:05 co-relates `{B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1, J2, J3, C1, C2, C3}`
- `Machine Process` at 11:45 co-relates `{J4, C4, CNC-2, FIX-A}`
- `Quarantine Lot` at 10:20 and `Release Lot` at 13:50 co-relate `{L7, C2, C5, J2, J5}` in the object-centric evidence
- J3 holds FIX-A from 09:00 to 11:40 (acquire/release)
- RACK-7 is in cleaning/quarantine until 14:35 (tool state)
- J5’s final test 16:10–16:35 and J6’s final test 17:25–17:50 each require CAL-3 on TEST-1; CAL-3 is recalibrated 16:35–17:20

Inferred edges, not logged as relations: J4 is blocked by J3’s hold on FIX-A for at least the CNC-2 idle window 10:05–10:47; J1 and J2 are held to J3 by an AND-join on B1; J6 is held by CAL-3’s recalibration while TEST-1 is idle; J1’s packed unit waits for J4 inside S1; B1 and B2 are sequential users of the same rack and oven. The supervisor override that let J2 enter B1 during the L7 quarantine lives in another system, so the J2–L7 violation is external evidence. L8’s coupling of J1 and J3 is structural only: no lot event is recorded. There is no `Wait for Tool` activity, so every tool wait is reconstructed from state and idle time.

Job–job projection (mechanism in parentheses): J1–J2 (B1), J1–J3 (B1 and L8), J2–J3 (B1 and S2), J2–J5 (L7), J4–J5 (B2), J1–J4 (S1), J5–J6 (S3 and CAL-3), J3–J4 (FIX-A, inferred), and {J1,J2,J3}–{J4,J5} (shared OVEN-1 and RACK-7). One path from J6 to J1 is J6–S3–J5–B2–J4–S1–J1. J6 has neither a lot nor a batch and is still coupled to the morning jobs.

Three higher-order motifs:

1. **Cure clique.** One `Batch Cure` binds three jobs, three components, a batch, a machine, and a tool. The oven cycle exists only when the job clique, RACK-7, and OVEN-1 are jointly available. Frequency of this hyperedge is 1; member slack is measured in hours.
2. **Lot gate with a one-sided override.** L7 contains C2 and C5 and therefore sits on J2 and J5. The same quarantine interval is honored for J5 and bypassed for J2, who is already inside B1 at 12:05, before `Release Lot` at 13:50. The motif spans ComponentLot, Component, Job, and Batch, and the two siblings land in different batches.
3. **Cross-partition bridge plus false machine idle.** S1 consolidates J1 from B1 with J4 from B2, so shipment membership stitches two oven cliques. Separately, FIX-A held by J3 leaves CNC-2 idle while J4 and C4 are already at the machine, and CAL-3’s recalibration leaves TEST-1 idle while J6 is ready. Tool state, not machine load, fills those idle windows.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

These figures are accounting identities under an explicit counterfactual. They are not causal estimates. Several large segments sit upstream of a later AND-join and can be absorbed before departure; absorption is noted where the timestamps show it. Segments are sequential for a single job and must not be summed across jobs as if they were independent losses.

**J1 in B1.** Ready 08:10, J3 ready 11:55, cure 12:05. Total slack \(12{:}05-08{:}10 = 3\,\text{h}\,55\,\text{min}\). Portion until the last partner is ready: \(11{:}55-08{:}10 = 3\,\text{h}\,45\,\text{min}\). Start lag after the last partner: \(10\,\text{min}\). Counterfactual: B1 starts at J1’s readiness if the policy is a single-job cure and OVEN-1 and RACK-7 are free from 08:10. That resource premise is not in the log. The same arithmetic with an AND-join counterfactual attributes \(3\,\text{h}\,45\,\text{min}\) to waiting for J3 and \(10\,\text{min}\) to start-up. J1 then packs at 13:30, \(1\,\text{h}\,25\,\text{min}\) after cure, and S1 does not leave until 19:25, so this morning slack is absorbed by shipment consolidation under the observed J4 pack time.

**J2 in B1.** Slack \(12{:}05-08{:}25 = 3\,\text{h}\,40\,\text{min}\), of which \(11{:}55-08{:}25 = 3\,\text{h}\,30\,\text{min}\) ends at J3’s readiness and \(10\,\text{min}\) is start lag. Counterfactual: same AND-join, with readiness trusted. A second counterfactual conflicts with it. L7 is quarantined from 10:20 to 13:50 (\(3\,\text{h}\,30\,\text{min}\)). Under a strict lot gate J2 could not enter at 12:05. The override is what makes the 12:05 cure feasible for C2. From 10:20 to 12:05 J2 is inside both a lot hold and a batch hold; adding those two numbers double-counts one interval.

**FIX-A and J4.** J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50; `Machine Process` starts at 11:45 (\(1\,\text{h}\,55\,\text{min}\) later) on `{J4, C4, CNC-2, FIX-A}`. CNC-2 is idle 10:05–10:47 (\(42\,\text{min}\)) while J3 still holds FIX-A (released 11:40). Start is \(5\,\text{min}\) after that release. Conservative counterfactual: the binding missing object during the attested idle is FIX-A, so \(42\,\text{min}\) is the defensible fixture-associated wait. Extended counterfactual: J4 would have started at 10:05 had FIX-A been free, giving \(11{:}45-10{:}05 = 1\,\text{h}\,40\,\text{min}\). The extended figure assumes CNC-2 stayed available from 10:47 to 11:40, which is not attested. Relative to B2, even the extended delay is absorbed: J4 is batch-ready at 13:10, and B2 cannot start before J5 at 14:40 and RACK-7 at 14:35. Moving the 11:45 start earlier does not move the 14:50 cure under those later gates.

**J1 and S1.** J1 packed 13:30, J4 packed 19:10, S1 dispatched 19:25. Post-pack sojourn \(19{:}25-13{:}30 = 5\,\text{h}\,55\,\text{min}\). Trailing lag after the last partner \(19{:}25-19{:}10 = 15\,\text{min}\). Partner wait \(19{:}10-13{:}30 = 5\,\text{h}\,40\,\text{min}\). S2 and S3 dispatch 25 and 35 minutes after their own last pack. Counterfactual: S1 dispatches J1 alone at 13:30 plus S2’s 25-minute lag (13:55), so the consolidation premium is \(19{:}25-13{:}55 = 5\,\text{h}\,30\,\text{min}\). This assumes the 19:25 departure is produced by waiting for J4 rather than by an independent cutoff, vehicle, or customer window.

**CAL-3 and J6.** Both are test-ready at 16:00 and 16:05. J5 occupies TEST-1 and CAL-3 from 16:10 to 16:35 (\(25\,\text{min}\)). CAL-3 is recalibrated 16:35–17:20 (\(45\,\text{min}\)) while TEST-1 is idle until 17:25 (\(50\,\text{min}\)). J6 runs 17:25–17:50. Ready-to-start for J6 is \(17{:}25-16{:}05 = 1\,\text{h}\,20\,\text{min}\). Counterfactual splitting the confound: during 16:10–16:35 machine and tool are jointly busy, so that \(25\,\text{min}\) is serialization, not a pure tool effect. During 16:35–17:25 the machine is free. If J6 started at 16:35 plus the same \(5\,\text{min}\) lag later observed after recalibration (16:40), the recalibration-associated shift is \(45\,\text{min}\). Attributing the whole \(1\,\text{h}\,20\,\text{min}\) to CAL-3 would ignore TEST-1’s occupancy by J5.

**B2 near-tie (additional).** OVEN-1 is idle 12:55–14:50 (\(1\,\text{h}\,55\,\text{min}\)). J4 is ready 13:10, RACK-7 is free 14:35, J5 is ready 14:40, B2 starts 14:50. J4’s nominal sync slack is \(1\,\text{h}\,40\,\text{min}\). After J4 is ready, the rack is unavailable for \(1\,\text{h}\,25\,\text{min}\), but J5 is also unready in that window, so the interval is joint. The unique extra partner gap after the rack frees is \(14{:}40-14{:}35 = 5\,\text{min}\), then \(10\,\text{min}\) of start lag. Counterfactual: if J5 had been ready by 13:10, the start would still wait for RACK-7 until 14:35, plus about \(10\,\text{min}\), near 14:45 rather than 14:50. L7’s \(3\,\text{h}\,30\,\text{min}\) quarantine shifts J5’s own history, and J5 is ready only \(50\,\text{min}\) after the 13:50 release, but on this sample it misses the B2 start by about \(5\,\text{min}\) because rack cleaning ends at 14:35. The two gates are a near-tie. The machine-flattened “longest OVEN-1 gap” of \(73\,\text{min}\) does not equal this \(115\,\text{min}\) idle.

It is not a claim that one object caused every delay, and it is not an additive decomposition. For S1’s 19:25 departure, J4’s pack at 19:10 is the last gate; J1’s \(3\,\text{h}\,55\,\text{min}\) batch slack is real WIP and is absorbed before that departure. ## 4. What flattening misses

A flattening keeps one object type as the case notion and turns every other object into an attribute, a duplicate event, or nothing.

**Job.** Visible: a shared path from Release Job through Stage Component, Machine Process, Inspect, Pack, and Dispatch, plus a rework loop on two cases, and the fact that no total duration exceeds 1.8 SD above the six-job mean. Hidden: L7’s quarantine, because those events were not “directly related to the Job” in this extractor; the identity of batch and shipment partners; FIX-A’s hold while J4 has no tool event. Duplicated: B1’s single cure can appear once per member, so one oven cycle becomes three independent Machine Process cases. Misleading conclusion: the median Pack→Dispatch of 1.1 h is a healthy handoff. J1’s pack-to-dispatch is 5 h 55 min; the median conceals that tail, and “no outlier job” is compatible with a high mean caused by waits that several jobs share.

**Machine.** Visible: CNC-2, OVEN-1, and TEST-1 sequences and utilizations of 54%, 44%, and 58%, with reported longest gaps of 48, 73, and 39 minutes. Hidden: the object that was unavailable during the idle (FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3, or a missing partner). The 12:55–14:50 oven idle is 1 h 55 min, longer than the reported 73-minute gap, so the gap statistic is already a different construction than object-centric idle time. Misleading conclusion: spare machine capacity means the system is unconstrained and can take more load. Extra load would increase contention for FIX-A, RACK-7, and CAL-3.

**Batch.** Visible: membership, the single B1 cure, and sync slack when ready times are joined to that event. Hidden: J6 entirely, rack cleaning between B1 and B2, the external override, and S1’s later charge of B2 lateness to J1. Misleading conclusion: B2 is efficient because it starts 10 minutes after J5, or batching is minor because only two batch–machine interactions occur. J4 had already waited 1 h 40 min, and the oven had been idle since 12:55.

**Tool.** Visible: J3’s acquire/release of FIX-A, CAL-3’s recalibration interval, RACK-7’s state until 14:35, and the rack’s presence on both cures. Hidden: the waiter. J4 has no FIX-A event until 11:45; J6 has no CAL-3 event during 16:35–17:20. Misleading conclusion: tools have no queue, therefore no contention. The queue was never an activity.

**ComponentLot.** Visible: L7’s 10:20 quarantine and 13:50 release, and C2/C5 with J2/J5 when relations are kept. Hidden: L8, if the view is event-driven, since no L8 event exists; J4 and J6; the override stored elsewhere, which makes J2 look as blocked as J5. Misleading conclusion: a lot hold delays every contained job equally. On these clocks it delayed J5 and was waived for J2, who was cured while C2’s lot was still quarantined.

**Shipment.** Visible: three cases of Create Shipment → Load → Dispatch, medians 0.8 h and 0.3 h. Hidden: who is inside the shipment, when each job was packed, and the 5 h 40 min J1 spends waiting for J4. Misleading conclusion: shipping adds about 0.3 h and is not a bottleneck. The largest sojourn in the evidence sits outside that DFG by construction.

Low machine utilization coexists with long job waiting because a cure, a CNC start, or a final test fires only when every required object is feasible at once: partner jobs, tool state, lot release, and the machine. Utilization measures only the machine literal. When FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3, J3, or J5 is the false literal, the machine idles and the job waits. Batch fill rules create that idle on purpose. Each flattening conditions on one type and treats the other literals as outside the case, so every individual view can look normal while the conjunction is slow. Managers then experience cascades that no single DFG contains: the binding object flips whenever a near-tie such as RACK-7 at 14:35 and J5 at 14:40 changes order.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**Narrative A — B1 cure clique and a later shipment absorb.** Temporal precedence: J1 ready 08:10, J2 ready 08:25, J3 acquires FIX-A at 09:00 and releases it at 11:40, J3 batch-ready 11:55, one cure at 12:05 on B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1, J2, J3, C1, C2, C3, J1 packed 13:30, J4 packed 19:10, S1 dispatched 19:25. Resource feasibility: the cure event shows the oven, the rack, and all three components were jointly assigned at 12:05; it does not show they were free at 08:10. Policy: an AND-join on B1 is the reading that matches a start 10 minutes after the last member; S1’s rule keeps a packed J1 until J4. Speculation: J3’s fixture occupancy is why J3 was last, and shortening it would have started B1 earlier. The 15 minutes between 11:40 and 11:55 is consistent with that story and does not establish it. J3’s work before 09:00 is unknown. Even a much earlier cure would leave S1’s 19:25 departure unchanged if J4 still packs at 19:10, unless an earlier B1 also pulls RACK-7’s cleaning earlier and that cleaning is what holds B2.

**Narrative B — fixture hold, false CNC idle, absorbed before B2.** Temporal precedence: J3 holds FIX-A from 09:00 to 11:40; J4 arrives at CNC-2 at 09:50; CNC-2 idles 10:05–10:47; J4’s process at 11:45 names J4, C4, CNC-2, and FIX-A; J4 is batch-ready at 13:10; B2 starts at 14:50. Resource feasibility: during the 42-minute idle the machine is free, the job is present, and the fixture is held by another job, so a start at 10:05 was infeasible for lack of FIX-A. Feasibility after 10:47 is unknown. Policy: nothing in the log records a priority rule; the missing `Wait for Tool` activity is an observability gap, not evidence that J4 was free to choose the machine. Speculation: prioritizing J4 over J3 on FIX-A would move J4’s 13:10 readiness earlier and move J3’s 11:55 readiness later, which would lengthen J1’s and J2’s B1 slack. On the observed numbers, J4’s CNC shift does not move B2, because 13:10 is already earlier than both 14:35 and 14:40.

**Narrative C — one lot, two batches, an override, and a near-tie with the rack.** Temporal precedence: L7, containing C2 (J2) and C5 (J5), is quarantined at 10:20 and released at 13:50. B1’s cure at 12:05 already includes J2 and C2. J5 is batch-ready at 14:40. RACK-7 is blocked until 14:35. OVEN-1 has been idle since 12:55. B2 starts at 14:50 on J4 and J5. Resource feasibility: until 13:50 a strict lot rule makes J5’s downstream activities infeasible; the same rule would have made J2’s presence in the 12:05 cure infeasible. The override, recorded outside this log, is what relaxed feasibility for C2 only. Policy: sibling components in one lot were treated by two different release rules, and they sit in two different batches, so the waiver and the enforcement land on different oven cycles. Speculation: releasing L7 at 10:20 would have made J5 ready about 50 minutes later, near 11:10, and B2 would then have been rack-constrained until 14:35. That is a small move of the cure. It is a large change in J5’s own waiting and in whether C2 was cured while quarantined. Clock order alone does not say whether the quarantine was a quality hold, a lab delay, or a data hold.

A fourth, shorter chain is enough to show J6 is not independent. J5 and J6 are ready at 16:00 and 16:05; J5 tests to 16:35; CAL-3’s recalibration and TEST-1’s idle overlap until J6 starts at 17:25. S3 consolidates J5 with J6 and dispatches 35 minutes after its last pack. Precedence and the exclusive CAL-3 rule support a tool insertion on J6. Whether that insertion moves S3 depends on pack times, which are not given.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

**1. Relax B1’s AND-join (synchronization).** Target: rank-1 batch synchronization. Expected benefit: up to about 3 h 45 min of WIP for J1 and 3 h 30 min for J2 on this pattern, if partial curing is technically valid. Helped: J1, J2, and floor congestion at the oven. Potentially harmed: J3, if a partial rack changes the cure recipe; energy and RACK-7, if more cycles mean more cleaning; B2, if a use-triggered clean of an earlier B1 collides with J4, or helped if that clean finishes before 14:35 and moves the near-tie. The sign of the B2 spillover depends on whether cleaning is triggered by B1’s unload or ends at a fixed clock. New cost: lower fill, possible metallurgical deviation, more setup. Perverse metric: oven utilization or jobs per batch, both of which reward holding J1 and J2 until J3. Object-centric evaluation: primary endpoints are member slack (cure timestamp minus each member’s ready timestamp) and WIP sojourn of J1 and J2; co-primary endpoints are RACK-7 state relative to B2’s start, J4’s pack time, S1’s dispatch, and any quality hold on C1–C3. Compare the object graph before and after; do not stop at OVEN-1 utilization.

**2. Pool the single tools (tools).** Target: FIX-A exclusivity, CAL-3’s on-path recalibration, and RACK-7’s single-rack cleaning. Expected benefit: remove the 42-minute attested CNC idle for J4, the 45-minute recalibration shift for J6, and the rack’s claim on the 12:55–14:50 oven idle. Helped: J4 at CNC, J6 at test, and whichever of J4/J5 is rack-blocked at B2. Potentially harmed: J3 if a second fixture is funded by taking FIX-A off J3 mid-operation; test validity if recalibration is skipped rather than moved off-path; B1 if a second rack is staffed by delaying B1’s load. New cost: capital, calibration traceability, cleaning labor. Perverse metric: tool utilization. Keeping FIX-A and CAL-3 busy looks efficient and recreates the queue. Object-centric evaluation: fraction of machine-idle intervals that overlap a named tool-down state, sojourn from “job present at machine” to start, and downstream pack time of S1 and S3 members. A second FIX-A that does not move 19:10 is a local win only; the design should publish that distinction.

**3. Time-box or split S1 (shipment policy).** Target: consolidation of an early B1 job with a late B2 job. Expected benefit: on this day, about 5 h 30 min to 5 h 40 min of packed waiting for J1, the segment that actually determines J1’s departure. Helped: J1 and any customer waiting on J1. Potentially harmed: J4, if the split removes a shared freight booking; the dock, if partial shipments multiply; S2 and S3, if the same rule forces them to leave before a partner who was intentionally consolidated. New cost: freight, receiving overhead, possible contractual fill rules. Perverse metric: shipment-level on-time rate or cube fill. Both improve if Create Shipment is delayed until J4 is packed, which is exactly how the current DFG hides J1. Object-centric evaluation: packed-to-dispatch per job object, stratified by whether the job was the first or last member of its shipment, with freight cost and partner lateness as co-primary endpoints. A win that only moves the shipment case’s start timestamp fails the design.

**4. Put lot events, overrides, and waits in one OCEL (logging, not operations).** Target: rank-5 observability. Propagate `Quarantine Lot` and `Release Lot` to J2 and J5 under a documented relation rule; ingest the supervisor override into the same log; record tool-state intervals with a start time, a reason, and the jobs that requested the tool; record blocker object ids for batch and shipment holds. Expected benefit: an analyst can see L7, FIX-A, and S1 without a side channel. Physical delays do not fall because of this. Helped: the next investigation of J2 versus J5 and of J4’s CNC idle. Potentially harmed: operators, if wait events become a blame ledger; analysts, if indiscriminate linking manufactures false motifs such as a spurious L8 delay. New cost: instrumentation and a one-time jump in reported cycle time when waits that were always present become stored. Perverse metric: count of wait events, or median job duration in the week the extractor changes. The first motivates under-logging; the second makes the logging project look like a performance regression. Object-centric evaluation: replay the known incidents (J4/FIX-A, L7/J5, S1/J1) and score whether each flattening and the object graph surfaces the blocking object. Success is recovery of the blocker, not a shorter DFG.

**5. Treat co-lot siblings by one release rule.** Target: the asymmetric L7 override. Expected benefit: J2 and J5 stop diverging silently; quality status of C2 matches C5. Helped: J5’s predictability, and whoever must defend the 12:05 cure. Potentially harmed: J1 and J3, if the consistent rule is “hold the whole lot,” because B1 would then have waited at least until 13:50 instead of 12:05; customers, if the consistent rule is “release early,” and C2/C5 later fail. New cost: either longer B1 slack or a weaker quarantine. Perverse metric: override count. Driving it to zero by keeping overrides in the side system improves the metric and preserves the blind spot. Object-centric evaluation: for each lot, the distribution of member start times relative to Release Lot, plus quality outcomes of overridden versus held components, with B1 and B2 slack as spillover endpoints.

**6. Realign membership only where the customer constraint allows.** Target: S1 bridging B1 and B2, and L7 bridging those same batches. Expected benefit: a long J4 path stops charging itself to J1; a lot hold stops touching both oven cycles. Helped: the early member of each current clique. Potentially harmed: the customer or route that caused J1 and J4 to share S1; heat-treat quality if batch composition is changed to chase the shipment graph. New cost: more shipments or worse cure mixes. Perverse metric: number of shipment objects, which falls when consolidation is aggressive and rises when coupling falls. Object-centric evaluation: length of the shortest job–job path that uses a shipment edge plus a batch or lot edge, together with packed-to-dispatch of the early member. If customer-of-record forbids the realignment, record that constraint as data rather than forcing the clique change.

Spillovers to watch as a set: freeing FIX-A for J4 can delay J3 and therefore B1 and J1’s WIP, without moving S1 if J4 is still not the cure’s critical member. Starting B1 earlier helps J1’s WIP immediately and helps S1 only if RACK-7 cleaning is use-triggered and currently on B2’s critical path. Enforcing L7 on J2 protects the quarantine story and lengthens the cure that J1 and J3 are waiting for. Splitting S1 helps J1’s departure and can raise J4’s freight. Logging waits makes every flattened duration look worse in the first extract that includes them. Machine-utilization targets push in the opposite direction from all of the above.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

Observations that would weaken the top-ranked explanation (B1’s AND-join as the main account of the large, hidden, cross-job waits):

1. OVEN-1 or RACK-7 was down, occupied, or not at temperature from 08:10 until about 12:00, so J1 and J2 were resource-blocked rather than partner-blocked.
2. The 08:10 and 08:25 “ready” marks mean only that a component was staged, and a further unrecorded prerequisite (recipe, labor, inspection) completed near 12:05 for every member at once.
3. J3’s 11:55 timestamp is a late write-back, and the physical job was ready much earlier.
4. The batch rule already allows a partial load, and 12:05 coincides with a shift change, a power window, or an operator decision unrelated to J3.
5. Across a longer history, multi-job batches usually start within minutes of the first member’s readiness, so this B1 slack is a one-off.
6. A certificate, customer hold, or material review on C1 or C2, independent of J3, was released just before 12:05.
7. J2 is not actually in the 12:05 cure’s material list, and the co-relation of J2 with that event is an integration error. That would also weaken the override story.
8. J1’s total sojourn excluding the 08:10–12:05 interval is already the outlier the job summary denies, which would mean the morning slack is ordinary processing mis-labeled as readiness.

Fields and event types that would discriminate the hypotheses:

1. Tool-state intervals with start and end, reason (busy, cleaning, quarantine, calibration), and the requesting job, especially the start of RACK-7’s cleaning and whether B1’s unload triggered it.
2. Explicit wait events whose blocker is an object id: wait-for-tool, wait-for-batch-partner, wait-for-lot, wait-for-shipment-partner.
3. Machine occupancy intervals, not just event timestamps, so the 73-minute gap and the 1 h 55 min oven idle can be reconciled.
4. A single relation rule that either always links lot events to J2 and J5 or always does not, plus the override as an event in this OCEL with actor, time, and waived constraint.
5. A readiness checklist (component, program, labor, tool, quality) so “ready for batch” has one meaning.
6. Shipment policy attributes: customer, route, cutoff, minimum fill, and a hold-for-partner flag, to test the 19:25 departure against an independent window.
7. Shift, operator, and planned calendar, to separate breaks and changeovers from object contention on the 10:05 and 16:35 idles.
8. Whether CAL-3’s 16:35–17:20 recalibration was a periodic rule, a failure, or a manual choice, and the queue of jobs that requested TEST-1.
9. Quality result for C2 after the in-quarantine cure, which separates a timing coupling from a defect coupling.
10. Reservation timestamps for FIX-A, so J4’s claim at 09:50 is either a logged request or only an inference from a later acquire.
