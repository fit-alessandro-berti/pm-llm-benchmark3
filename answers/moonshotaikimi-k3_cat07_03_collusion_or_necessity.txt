def segment_durations(df, a1, a2):

### 4.3 Emergency vs. non-emergency comparison

```python
emg  = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(log, "emergency_flag", [True],  level="case", retain=True)
nemg = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(log, "emergency_flag", [True],  level="case", retain=False)

def segment_minutes(frame, a1, a2):
    t1 = frame[frame["concept:name"] == a1].groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min()
    t2 = frame[frame["concept:name"] == a2].groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min()
    return (t2 - t1).dropna().dt.total_seconds() / 60

emg_ids  = set(emg["case:concept:name"].unique())
d_emg  = segment_minutes(df[df["case:concept:name"].isin(emg_ids)],  "Create Purchase Order", "Approve Purchase Order")
d_nemg = segment_minutes(df[~df["case:concept:name"].isin(emg_ids)], "Create Purchase Order", "Approve Purchase Order")

from scipy.stats import mannwhitneyu
mannwhitneyu(d_emg, d_nemg)   # is the speed gap explained by emergency_flag alone?
```

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

**Would increase concern:**
- `entered_by`/`approved_by` showing the *same individual* behind the shared account on both sides of a four-eyes pair.
- Vendor-master history showing new V-77x bank accounts that do not match vendor-confirmed details (recently opened, out-of-jurisdiction, or differing across cases).
- Absence of change tickets for the V-77x migration window; missing callback/verification records.
- In the 11 loops: payment released to an interim account, then bank details reverted.
- Payments released before Goods Receipt/Invoice Match; amounts clustered just below approval thresholds (`amount_band` boundary).
- Access logs showing one workstation/IP for both Lena and Kim events; out-of-hours `manual_upload` batches without tickets.
- Emergency overrides by the same pair on non-emergency spend, or a rising quarter-over-quarter trend.

**Would reduce concern:**
- Change-management ticket IDs linking each V-77x bank change to the platform-migration project, plus vendor confirmation letters.
- A corrected legacy_erp mapping table that eliminates apparent same-resource Create/Approve violations.
- The `quarter_close_upload` spreadsheet showing original justifications by a *different* person within 3 working days.
- Distinct `entered_by` assistants under the shared account, consistent with the delegation matrix.
- Emergency requisitions, rush-channel pre-approval, and on-call rosters matching case timestamps for Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo.
- Maker/checker dual-control records in the vendor-master system for the bank changes; goods receipt and invoice match present and timely.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Our review of the quarter's procure-to-pay event log identified control exceptions that warrant targeted follow-up but do not, by themselves, establish misconduct. Most high-frequency buyer–approver pairings are consistent with normal workload allocation and informal specialization, and the accelerated approvals involving Buyer_Carla/Approver_Gita and Buyer_Dan/Manager_Hugo occur predominantly in emergency-flagged medical-supply and facilities-repair cases, where a designated rush channel and delegated urgent approval provide a plausible explanation. These patterns should nonetheless be verified against emergency documentation.

The most concerning subgroup is the cluster of vendor bank-detail changes followed within minutes by payment approval for vendor family V-77x, concentrated in manual uploads and associated with VendorMaster_Lena and AP_Kim, including nine cases in which one resource performed both steps and eleven tight correction loops. These are control exceptions, not findings of fraud: a legitimate banking-platform migration for V-77x, combined with missing ticket linkage in the log, may explain much of the pattern, and that hypothesis should be tested first.

Data-quality limitations materially constrain attribution. A shared approval account, legacy-ERP resource-mapping errors, batch-migrated retrospective justifications, and absent links between maintenance tickets and procurement cases mean some apparent violations may be artifacts and some individuals may be misidentified.

We recommend prioritized review of the roughly 44 bank-change/payment cases, beginning with the nine segregation-of-duties exceptions and any post-payment bank changes; reconciliation of resource identities using entered_by, approved_by, and access logs; verification of emergency documentation for a risk-based sample of fast approvals; and remediation of mapping, shared-account, and case-linkage gaps, with quarterly monitoring thereafter. No conclusion regarding collusion or fraud is expressed or implied.
