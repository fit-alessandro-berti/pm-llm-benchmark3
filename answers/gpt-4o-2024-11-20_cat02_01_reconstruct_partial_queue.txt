# 1. Assumptions and notation

## Assumptions

- **A1 (Event-order convention):** Events (queue entry, service start, service end) occur in sequence for each case.
- **A2 (Timestamp rounding):** Each timestamp represents an interval of ±2.5 min. Simultaneous events are handled by assuming queue-to-service transitions happen as early as logically possible.
- **A3 (Queue entry):** For a blank S1 start, the case is waiting for S1 at 14:00. For a case with completed S1 but a blank S2 start, entry into the S2 queue occurred at S1 completion.
- **A4 (Service duration boundaries):** MISSING starts for S1 or S2 are bounded by the resource availability and preceding/following activity on the same resource. A midpoint estimate is computed as part of sensitivity analysis.
- **A5 (Resource exclusivity):** Each resource exclusively handles one case at a time.
- **A6 (Availability adjustment):** C2 was unavailable from 10:55 to 11:10. Cases assigned to C2 during this window are delayed.
- **A7 (Capacity):** Both S1 and S2 typically have two resources available simultaneously. Capacity drops temporarily for S2 during maintenance.
- **A8 (OPEN cases):** OPEN cases have their elapsed service time treated as lower bounds in the calculation. Service start is not backdated or assumed completed.

## Notation

- `arrival_s1`: External timestamp when a case appears at S1.
- `s1_start/s1_end`: Service start and end times at S1.
- `s2_start/s2_end`: Service start and end times at S2.
- `service time`: Active service duration (e.g., from `s1_start` to `s1_end`).
- `waiting time`: Time spent in a queue prior to service.
- `sojourn time`: Total time in a station (waiting + service).
- `censoring_or_missingness_flag`: Explicit marking of missing timestamps or ongoing processes.
- `WIP`: Work in progress (cases under service or waiting in queues).
- `utilization`: Fraction of resource capacity actively servicing cases.

---

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

## Queue and service times

- **S1 waiting:** `S1_wait = max(0, S1_start - arrival_s1)`
- **S1 service:** `S1_service = max(0, S1_end - S1_start)`  
- **S2 waiting:** `S2_wait = max(0, S2_start - S1_end)`
- **S2 service:** `S2_service = max(0, S2_end - S2_start)`  
- **Total sojourn time per station:** `Sojourn_station = Queue time + Service time`

## Station metrics

- **Arrivals:** Count of cases entering the station during a time period.
- **Departures:** Count of cases completing service during a time period.
- **WIP:** Cases actively in service or in queue at a given time.
- **Busy time:** Aggregated time resources spend servicing cases.
- **Utilization:** `Utilization = Busy time / (Total resource time × Resources)`

## Little's Law check
Little's Law assumes a stable, steady-state system where `WIP = Arrival rate × Mean sojourn time`. This will only be applied to time intervals where arrivals, departures, and station states suggest equilibrium.

## Treatment of MISSING and OPEN cases
- MISSING starts (e.g., P05, P07, P11) are imputed using constraints from observed events:
  - Latest permissible start = S1 or S2 end (minus minimum plausible service duration).
  - Midpoint imputation is used in sensitivity analysis.
- OPEN cases are treated cautiously:
  - Active service time = elapsed time from the recorded start until 14:00.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

## Bottleneck Observation

1. **S1 Bottleneck Early (08:00–10:00):**
   - Higher S1 queue lengths observed relative to S2.
   - S1 resources are highly utilized, close to capacity limits.

2. **S2 Bottleneck Late (10:00–14:00):**
   - Delayed S2 services during the C2 maintenance interval (10:55–11:10).
   - S2 queues grow afterward as cases accumulate.

3. **Switch in Bottleneck:** Consistent with task variations and maintenance impact, the bottleneck appears to shift from S1 to S2 mid-morning.

## Temporal Dynamics
- **C2 unavailability disrupted flow:** Clear delay propagation in S2 once C2 goes down for maintenance.
- **Timestamp rounding:** Queue wait estimates are subject to ±2.5 min variations due to rounding.

---

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

## Scenarios
1. **Optimistic:** Assumes minimal wait times and earliest possible starts for MISSING events.
2. **Central (Default):** Midpoint imputation for missing starts, treating OPEN service times as lower bounds.
3. **Pessimistic:** Assumes maximum plausible S1/S2 wait times and interprets OPEN activities as indicative of heavy queuing.

## Stable Conclusions
- Bottleneck shifts mid-morning from S1 to S2.
- C2 maintenance has a clear queue-propagation effect at S2.
- Busy-time utilization estimates remain high across different scenarios.

## Unstable Conclusions
- Precise queue wait durations and WIP estimates vary with rounding choices and missing data assumptions.
