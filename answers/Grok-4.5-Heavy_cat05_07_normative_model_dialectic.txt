## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Stipulated facts** (established by the materials without reliance on contested authority or logging assumptions):
- CS-9 is the controlled, formally approved compliance standard effective 2025-01-01. It requires Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review before every activation, Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier, and distinct humans for requester and manual approver.
- M0 is the PM4Py-style StrictPartialOrder that encodes the control-flow subset of those rules: Register_Supplier precedes concurrent Sanctions_Check ∥ Quality_Review, which precedes an XOR between the approved branch (Manual_Procurement_Approval → Activate_Supplier) and Reject_Supplier; Notify_Requester and Close_Case follow.
- PD-3 existed as a steering-committee decision (minutes + email) with an explicit effective window 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31, limited to LOW-risk DE suppliers already in an approved corporate group, allowing Auto_Approve in place of Manual_Procurement_Approval and Quality_Review up to two business days after provisional activation, while still requiring Sanctions_Check before activation. It was never incorporated into CS-9 or M0. The Compliance observer initially allowed a “monitored exception” but later stated she had not approved weakening CS-9.
- FD-7 existed as a time-bounded emergency directive effective 2026-07-10 00:00 to 2026-07-12 23:59 for critical relief suppliers, permitting provisional activation before Quality_Review and Manual_Procurement_Approval provided Sanctions_Check preceded activation, spend stayed ≤ EUR 20 000 until retrospective approval, and daily monitoring plus Quality_Review occurred within two business days. It expired automatically.
- From 2026-07-01 Notify_Requester migrated to CRM; approximately 70 % of notifications are mirrored into the onboarding log; older links are mutable text fields.
- Aggregate counts: 312 Auto_Approve cases in June–July (289 PD-3-eligible, 14 clearly ineligible, 9 missing attributes); 47 cases with Request_More_Info (none activated before evidence arrived); notification missingness rose from 2 % to 29 %; six Auto_Approve cases in the first week of August; Internal Audit flagged SUP-703 and SUP-708 as potentially severe.
- The fifteen traces and their accompanying context statements are as given in the case table.
- No permanent policy amendment for auto-approval or Request_More_Info exists. CS-9 is silent on an explicit reactivation procedure and ambiguous on whether rejected pre-screen leads are governed by the full M0 path.

**Disputed propositions** (depend on authority hierarchy, scope interpretation, identity resolution, or logging assumptions):
- Whether an unincorporated pilot or emergency directive can create a valid exception to CS-9 while it is active.
- Whether post-expiry continuation of the same pattern is justified by residual factual emergency or by an “imminent” permanent change.
- Whether rejected leads and reactivations fall inside the scope of M0.
- Whether a missing notification event after the CRM migration constitutes missing required activity or a known data-quality artifact.
- Whether alias resolution to the same human or an undisclosed spousal relationship in the same reporting line constitutes a resource/COI violation enforceable against M0-conforming traces.
- Whether a post-hoc approved label rename (QA_Clearance) is equivalence or deviation.
- Whether desirable practice (Request_More_Info loops) or operational normalization can cure or mitigate control-flow nonconformance.

**Burden-of-proof approach**:
- To declare NONCOMPLIANT: clear and convincing evidence of a violation of a then-applicable controlled rule (primarily CS-9 sequencing or authority) after (a) stripping known data artifacts (CRM mutability, migration duplicates, post-approved label mappings) and (b) removing cases that fall inside a contemporaneous written overlay whose stated eligibility, temporal, and amount conditions are met. Informal belief, post-hoc rationalization, or “factual need” without written authority does not discharge the burden.
- To accept a JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION: contemporaneous written authority (PD-3 or FD-7 text) whose eligibility predicates, effective window, and quantitative limits are demonstrably satisfied by the case attributes and evidence. Model incompleteness or desirable outcomes shift the classification toward MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT or DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE rather than operational fault, but do not create authority where none was issued.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The operation is materially noncompliant with the only controlled normative standard, CS-9, and its implementing model M0. Temporary overlays and desirable practices cannot override or retroactively amend a controlled policy.

CS-9 is “Controlled and formally approved.” It mandates Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review before activation, Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier, and distinct humans. M0 encodes the control-flow portion of those rules. PD-3 is merely steering-committee minutes and email; it was never incorporated into CS-9 or M0. The Compliance observer’s later statement that she “had not approved weakening CS-9” confirms the hierarchical limit. FD-7 is a three-day emergency directive that expired by its own terms. Neither instrument possesses authority to permanently alter the controlled standard. Therefore any activation that omits prior Quality_Review or substitutes Auto_Approve outside the precise temporal and eligibility windows of the temporary instruments violates CS-9.

Temporal limits are strict. PD-3 ended 31 July 2026; FD-7 ended 12 July 2026 23:59. SUP-705 (13 July) and SUP-714 (2 August) occurred after the respective authorities expired. Aggregate evidence shows six further Auto_Approve cases in the first week of August. Operations’ claims of “ongoing flood emergency” or “permanent approval imminent” are informal and unsupported by written extension or policy amendment. Continuing an expired exception is classic normalization of deviance.

High-risk and clearly unauthorized deviations are decisive:
- SUP-703 applied Auto_Approve to a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier on 20 June, with a first purchase of EUR 180 000. This is willful misapplication of a low-risk DE-group rule, producing material exposure.
- SUP-708 activated a HIGH-risk supplier 40 minutes before Sanctions_Check with no emergency or pilot in force. CS-9’s sanctions-before-activation rule is absolute; absence of subsequent transactions does not cure the temporal violation of a core compliance control.
- SUP-705 continued the provisional pattern after FD-7 expired without written authority.
- SUP-714 continued Auto_Approve after PD-3 expired on the belief that permanence was imminent—an argument that would render every pilot self-perpetuating.

Systemic patterns reinforce the point. Of 312 Auto_Approve cases, 14 are clearly ineligible and 9 lack eligibility attributes; these are selection errors, not logging artifacts. Internal Audit correctly flagged SUP-703 and SUP-708 as potentially severe. Notification missingness rose to 29 % after the CRM migration, but that is a data-quality problem; it does not excuse the underlying control-flow and authority breaches.

Desirable outcomes do not amend policy. Request_More_Info cycles (47 cases, illustrated by SUP-707) and permanent low-risk auto-approval are regarded as desirable by Operations and even by Compliance. That desirability is irrelevant to present conformance. Policy is amended by controlled change, not by accumulated practice or stakeholder preference. M0’s inability to express loops or auto-routes is a model defect that must be corrected prospectively; it does not license current nonconformance with the rules M0 does express.

Prosecution acknowledges genuine model and data defects: M0 lacks any representation of Request_More_Info, of the resource/SoD rule, of reactivation, or of rejected-lead scope; the CRM migration produced incomplete and mutable notification evidence (SUP-706); label changes (SUP-713) and duplicate events (SUP-711) create mapping noise; concurrent checks in M0 do not prohibit the pilot/emergency orderings when those overlays were valid. These defects inflate apparent deviation rates and must be remediated. They do not, however, extinguish the clear, high-severity violations of CS-9’s core sequencing and authority rules that remain visible once temporary windows and data artefacts are stripped away. Enforcement of the controlled standard, accompanied by urgent formal amendment where practice has outrun the model, is required to restore integrity.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest process-aware case is that M0 is obsolete relative to authorized temporary overlays, incomplete as a normative description of required behavior, incorrectly scoped for several case classes, and applied against incomplete or mutated event data. Many apparent deviations are therefore artifacts of model-data mismatch rather than material operational noncompliance.

M0 contains no LOOP or equivalent for Request_More_Info. Source N5 and aggregate evidence establish that Operations routinely inserts one or more such cycles to obtain missing certificates; Compliance regards the practice as desirable; none of the 47 observed instances activated before evidence arrived. StrictPartialOrder + XOR cannot encode this without either silent transitions or an explicit OperatorPOWL(LOOP). The model therefore systematically flags desirable, risk-reducing behavior as nonconforming. Similarly, resource and separation-of-duties constraints required by CS-9 (requester ≠ manual approver) and conflict-of-interest disclosure are absent from M0; control-flow conformance of SUP-712 and SUP-715 is therefore silent on the actual policy questions those cases raise.

PD-3 created a monitored exception for LOW-risk DE suppliers already belonging to an approved corporate group. Auto_Approve replaces Manual_Procurement_Approval; Sanctions_Check and automated master-data validation remain mandatory before activation; Quality_Review may lag provisional activation by up to two business days. SUP-702 satisfies every stated condition inside the effective window, with complete eligibility fields and CRM notification evidence. FD-7 authorized provisional activation before Quality_Review and Manual_Procurement_Approval for critical relief suppliers during a three-day flood window, provided Sanctions_Check preceded activation, spend remained ≤ EUR 20 000 until retrospective approval, and daily monitoring plus Quality_Review occurred within two business days. SUP-704 meets these conditions exactly. Both overlays were formally issued instruments; the Compliance observer explicitly permitted the pilot to proceed “as a monitored exception.” M0, which predates both, cannot be treated as the sole normative reference while the overlays were in force.

CS-9 states that rejected leads “may use an abbreviated pre-screen process” but does not decide whether such leads constitute cases governed by M0. Process owner and model owner disagree (SUP-709). CS-9 likewise contains no explicit reactivation procedure. SUP-710 is a reactivation of a previously closed supplier whose quality certification remained valid; the absence of Register_Supplier and Quality_Review is therefore expected under any reasonable scope interpretation, not a control-flow violation of an onboarding model that assumes a green-field supplier.

From 2026-07-01 Notify_Requester moved to a CRM service; only ~70 % of notifications are mirrored into the onboarding log, and older links are mutable text fields. SUP-706’s missing notification event is therefore an expected data-quality artifact; CRM contains a message with a later-entered mutable link. SUP-711’s duplicated Register_Supplier shares a single source transaction ID and is a migration artifact. SUP-713’s QA_Clearance is a UI/procedure rename whose mapping was later approved; audit documents confirm equivalent content. Treating these traces as process noncompliance confuses logging incompleteness with operational misconduct.

M0 already models Sanctions_Check ∥ Quality_Review. Observed interleavings, and the deliberate post-activation placement of Quality_Review under PD-3 and FD-7, are consistent with the then-applicable rules rather than violations of them. The high aggregate deviation rate claimed by Operations is largely explained by the combination of an incomplete base model, temporary overlays, and post-migration notification loss.

The defense does not claim that every deviation is justified. SUP-703 applied Auto_Approve to a high-risk ineligible foreign supplier with EUR 180 000 first purchase; SUP-708 activated a high-risk supplier before Sanctions_Check with no overlay in force; post-window continuation of Auto_Approve (SUP-714 and six aggregate cases) and of the emergency pattern (SUP-705) occurred without written extension or permanent amendment. Informal normalization cannot permanently displace CS-9. Material risk therefore remains for ineligible high-risk and post-authority cases. Nevertheless, the proper remedy is model and data revision plus explicit governance of overlays, not retrospective enforcement of an incomplete and superseded control-flow skeleton against traces that were produced under different, formally issued rules or under known logging defects.

## 5. Final Judgment

**Primary classifications** (dominant reason stated; MIXED used only where control-flow and non-control-flow rules genuinely diverge and both are material):

- SUP-701: COMPLIANT (severity n/a, confidence high). Perfect control-flow fit + distinct humans.
- SUP-702: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION (severity low, confidence high). Exact match to active PD-3 eligibility, window and allowances; CRM evidence present.
- SUP-703: NONCOMPLIANT (severity high, confidence high). Ineligible high-risk foreign supplier + EUR 180 k spend under misapplied pilot.
- SUP-704: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION (severity low-medium, confidence high). Exact match to active FD-7 conditions, spend under cap, monitoring present.
- SUP-705: NONCOMPLIANT (severity medium, confidence medium-high). Post-expiry of FD-7 with no written extension; factual need does not create authority.
- SUP-706: DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE (severity low, confidence medium). CRM migration + mutable link; other controls sound.
- SUP-707: MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT (severity low, confidence high). M0 cannot express the desirable, risk-reducing Request_More_Info loop.
- SUP-708: NONCOMPLIANT (severity high, confidence high). Sanctions after activation on high-risk supplier with no authorizing overlay.
- SUP-709: MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT (severity low, confidence medium). CS-9/M0 scope for rejected leads is ambiguous; abbreviated path is plausible.
- SUP-710: MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT (severity low-medium, confidence medium). No reactivation procedure defined; prior certification assumed valid.
- SUP-711: DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE (severity none, confidence high). Pure migration duplicate with identical source transaction ID.
- SUP-712: MIXED (severity medium, confidence medium-high). Control-flow fits M0; dominant noncompliance is the CS-9 distinct-human resource rule (aliases resolve to same person).
- SUP-713: MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT (severity low, confidence high). Post-approved label equivalence with audit support for content identity.
- SUP-714: NONCOMPLIANT (severity medium, confidence high). Auto_Approve after PD-3 expiry; belief in imminent permanence creates no authority.
- SUP-715: MIXED (severity medium, confidence medium). Control-flow fits M0; dominant issue is missing COI disclosure for spouses in the same reporting line under the applicable disclosure policy.

**System-level judgment**. Operational compliance is partial-to-noncompliant. Clear, material residual risk exists on high-risk misuse (SUP-703, SUP-708) and on post-authority normalization (SUP-705, SUP-714 + six aggregate August cases). A substantial fraction of the apparent deviation rate, however, is explained by model incompleteness (missing Request_More_Info loop, missing resource/COI encoding, missing reactivation and rejected-lead scope), by authorized but unincorporated temporary overlays, and by known data-quality defects after the CRM migration. M0 is therefore incomplete and partially obsolete as a normative control-flow description; it must be revised. CS-9 remains the binding controlled standard until formally amended. Temporary overlays were valid while active but created an expectation of permanence that was never governed; informal practice cannot rewrite controlled standards.

**Rejection of each side’s over-reach**. Pure prosecution is rejected because it treats valid, contemporaneous written overlays (PD-3, FD-7) and known model/data defects as if they were invisible, thereby inflating the noncompliance rate and ignoring the distinction between trace deviation and business misconduct. Pure defense is rejected because it underplays the authority hierarchy (CS-9 remains controlling), the hard expiry of temporary instruments, and the genuine high-severity cases; desirable outcomes and residual factual need do not create authority where none was issued in writing.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

A layered architecture is required because pure control-flow cannot express data-dependent eligibility, temporal authority, amount caps, resource identity, or conflict-of-interest rules.

**Layer 1 – Revised control-flow (POWL)**. Over-approximate all currently observed and desirable routes so that legitimate traces under any authorized regime are accepted by the model; enforce the selection of the correct route via the data and overlay layers.
- Concurrent Sanctions_Check ∥ Quality_Review on the standard path.
- Explicit low-risk auto-approval route (Auto_Approve → Activate_Supplier, with Quality_Review allowed after).
- Explicit emergency/provisional route (Sanctions_Check → Activate_Supplier, followed by concurrent or sequential Quality_Review and Manual_Procurement_Approval).
- Repeatable Request_More_Info before the decision point (LOOP approximation).
- Rejection path and mandatory Notify_Requester → Close_Case.
- Register_Supplier as the normal start (reactivation handled by scope rule).

**Layer 2 – Data-aware eligibility / amount / time rules** (pandas or equivalent post-filters, or DECLARE/log-skeleton constraints). Snapshot at case start: risk_level, country, corporate_group_id, approved_group_flag, estimated_spend, actual_first_spend, effective_policy_version, authority_ref (CS-9 | PD-3 | FD-7 | None). Enforce:
- Auto route only if risk = LOW ∧ country = DE ∧ approved_group = true ∧ date ∈ PD-3 window (or successor permanent rule).
- Emergency route only if authority_ref = FD-7 ∧ date ∈ window ∧ first_spend ≤ 20 000 ∧ critical_relief flag.
- Quality_Review lag ≤ 2 business days when provisional.
- Sanctions_Check always precedes Activate_Supplier under every regime.

**Layer 3 – Resource and conflict-of-interest rules** (organizational / identity service + log attributes). Resolved actor_ids (canonical person identifiers, not aliases), requester ≠ manual_approver, quality_reviewer and manual_approver not in the same reporting line without recorded disclosure and mitigation, delegation evidence when dual roles are authorized.

**Layer 4 – Policy-overlay governance**. Every pilot or emergency must be issued as a controlled instrument with explicit effective_from / effective_to, eligibility predicates, quantitative limits, required monitoring, and automatic expiry. A monitored-exception register records the authority_ref on every case that uses a non-standard route. No informal continuation after expiry.

**Layer 5 – Explicit scope rules**.
- Rejected leads that fail pre-screen sanctions may terminate after Sanctions_Check + Reject_Supplier + Notify + Close; they are not required to execute Quality_Review or Manual approval.
- Reactivation of a previously closed supplier whose quality certification remains valid may omit Register_Supplier and Quality_Review; a reactivation flag and prior-cert validity check are mandatory.
- Withdrawals / cancellations before activation are out of scope of the activation model.

**Placement**. Control-flow shapes → POWL. Cardinality, eventual presence, and simple order constraints that survive data variation → DECLARE or log-skeleton. Eligibility, amounts, dates, actor identity, COI disclosure → pandas/data-quality checks or external guards on case attributes. Authority issuance, expiry, and scope definitions → organizational policy controls and the monitored-exception register.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# Core activities
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
rmi = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
auto = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")
skip = SilentTransition()  # for optional paths and loop exit

# Request_More_Info: 0..n repetitions before the decision point
# LOOP(body, exit) – body is RMI, exit is silent; this approximates the desirable cycle
rmi_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[rmi, skip])

# --- Route 1: Standard manual path (CS-9 baseline) ---
# concurrent sanctions || quality, then manual -> activate
std_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])
std_approve = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[manual, activate])
std_approve.order.add_edge(manual, activate)
std_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[std_checks, std_approve])
std_route.order.add_edge(std_checks, std_approve)

# --- Route 2: Low-risk auto-approval (PD-3 style, quality may follow activate) ---
auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, auto, activate, quality])
auto_route.order.add_edge(sanctions, auto)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto, activate)
auto_route.order.add_edge(activate, quality)  # deferred quality allowed

# --- Route 3: Emergency / provisional (FD-7 style) ---
# sanctions -> activate, then quality and manual (concurrent or sequential)
emerg_post = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[quality, manual])
emerg_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, activate, emerg_post])
emerg_route.order.add_edge(sanctions, activate)
emerg_route.order.add_edge(activate, emerg_post)

# --- Route 4: Rejection (may occur after sanctions / partial checks) ---
reject_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, reject])
reject_route.order.add_edge(sanctions, reject)

# Top-level exclusive choice among the four routes
decision = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[std_route, auto_route, emerg_route, reject_route]
)

# Common suffix: notification then close
suffix = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[notify, close])
suffix.order.add_edge(notify, close)

# Overall model: Register -> optional RMI loop -> decision -> suffix
# (RMI may also be inserted inside routes if needed; this placement covers the common pre-decision pattern)
M_revised = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, rmi_loop, decision, suffix])
M_revised.order.add_edge(register, rmi_loop)
M_revised.order.add_edge(rmi_loop, decision)
M_revised.order.add_edge(decision, suffix)
```

**Limitation statement**. Pure POWL cannot enforce data-dependent route selection (risk level, country, group membership, date window, spend cap, actor identity). The model therefore over-approximates by making all four routes available; the correct route for any concrete case must be validated by the policy-overlay and data layers. The LOOP construction permits zero or more Request_More_Info cycles; it does not by itself guarantee that activation is blocked until evidence arrives—that guard belongs in the data layer. Reactivation and abbreviated rejection are handled by scope rules outside the POWL (they may omit Register or Quality). SilentTransition is used only for loop exit and optional skips; it is not used to hide mandatory controls.

**Normalized policy-overlay table** (conceptual; to be maintained as controlled data):

| rule_id | authority_source | effective_from | effective_to | eligibility_predicates | required_order_constraints | amount_cap | monitoring |
|---------|------------------|----------------|--------------|------------------------|----------------------------|------------|------------|
| CS9-BASE | CS-9 | 2025-01-01 | open | any | sanctions & quality before activate; manual before activate | none | standard |
| PD3-AUTO | PD-3 | 2026-06-01 | 2026-07-31 | risk=LOW ∧ country=DE ∧ approved_group=true | sanctions before activate; quality ≤2 BD after | none | monitored exception |
| FD7-EMERG | FD-7 | 2026-07-10 | 2026-07-12 23:59 | critical_relief=true | sanctions before activate; quality & manual within 2 BD | 20000 EUR until retrospective | daily transaction |
| (future permanent auto) | (to be issued) | … | … | … | … | … | … |

**Event / data-collection proposal** (mandatory attributes on every case / event):
- case_id, event_id, activity, timestamp_immutable (system-generated, non-editable).
- notification_id (UUID, immutable, generated by CRM or onboarding system at send time; required for Notify_Requester).
- authority_ref (CS-9 | PD-3 | FD-7 | REACTIVATION | REJECT_PRESCREEN | None) and policy_version.
- eligibility snapshot at Register or first decision: risk_level, country, corporate_group_id, approved_group_flag, estimated_spend.
- actual_first_spend, spend_currency (for amount-cap checks).
- actor_id_resolved (canonical person identifier after alias resolution) for requester, sanctions_officer, quality_reviewer, manual_approver, activator.
- delegation_evidence (if dual-role or substitute).
- coi_disclosure_flag and coi_mitigation_ref (for quality/approver pairs).
- reactivation_flag and prior_cert_valid_until (for reactivation scope).
- critical_relief_flag (for emergency route).
- source_transaction_id (to detect migration duplicates).

These attributes enable the pandas/data-layer checks, the monitored-exception register, and reliable conformance diagnostics that separate genuine control-flow or authority violations from model incompleteness and logging artefacts.
