## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

A useful reference is the average January–March breach rate by variant: approximately **15.7% for V1, 10.7% for V2, and 31.0% for V3**. Holding these rates fixed and changing only the monthly variant proportions gives an approximate composition-only counterfactual. It is not an accounting identity because the headline and variant tables have different maturity conditions, but it helps determine whether path mix is sufficient.

**January.** This is part of the stable baseline. Labels are complete, data-quality defects are negligible, mean risk is close to prevalence, AUROC is 0.81, and calibration error is low. V3 is already the highest-risk path, but its 20% share and 0.32 breach rate are stable business characteristics rather than drift.

**February.** Metrics and process composition remain effectively unchanged. The small AUROC increase to 0.82 and breach increase to 0.19 are compatible with ordinary variation. There is no evidence of drift requiring action.

**March.** This completes the reliable reference period. Data quality, route distribution, discrimination, and calibration remain stable. January–March should therefore be the primary pre-change comparison window.

**April.** April is primarily a **schema and feature-pipeline incident**. The migration changed the diagnosis activity name, so some scores were generated at the wrong prefix and others were absent. Unknown activities, missing queue features, and unmapped variants all spike at the same time that mean predicted risk rises from 0.18 to 0.28 and AUROC falls from 0.81 to 0.67. The observed breach rate remains near baseline at 0.19. That combination—large input-quality discontinuity, inflated scores, poor ranking, but stable outcome prevalence—is much more consistent with malformed features than with sudden business concept drift. The 18% “other” variants should not be interpreted as genuine process innovation until the log is normalized and deduplicated.

**May.** The 6 May patch produces a substantial recovery: unknown activities fall to 2%, queue-feature missingness to 3%, AUROC rises to 0.78, and ECE falls to 0.05. It does not completely restore the baseline. Plausible explanations include the first five days still using the defective path, source-version inconsistencies, or residual feature logic. The known unrecomputed cache specifically contaminates April backfills, so it should not automatically be blamed for every May case. A day-by-day and source-by-source replay is needed. May is a transition month, not evidence of a new concept regime.

**June.** June is a genuine **routing and process-mix change**, but not strong evidence of concept drift. V2 rises to 52%, its breach rate is only 0.09, and diagnosis-to-remote-fix time remains stable. Using the January–March variant rates with June’s mix gives an expected breach rate of about **15.8%**, close to the observed 15%. Thus, most of the overall prevalence change can be explained by putting more cases on a lower-risk route. The model’s mean risk of 0.20 is too high for the new mix, creating moderate calibration deterioration, while AUROC remains fairly strong at 0.79. A mixture-aware recalibration or intended-route feature is more proportionate than immediate full retraining.

**July.** July is the first credible sign of the supplier-driven regime change. The parts edge rises from 42 to 65 hours, and V3 breach increases to 0.46. The supplier problem began on 10 July, so a month-level average mixes pre- and post-shock cases. Label maturity is 98%, making this signal substantially more trustworthy than October. The composition-only estimate is about **16.5%**, compared with 19% observed. Aggregate mean risk and prevalence remain close, and ECE is only 0.042, but this can hide segment cancellation: V3 may already be underpredicted while lower-risk cases are overpredicted.

**August.** August is no longer explainable by process mix alone. The parts edge doubles to 130 hours, V3 expands to 35%, and its breach rate reaches 0.64. A composition-only calculation using baseline within-variant rates gives about **19.0%**, far below the observed 28%. Mean predicted risk remains 0.19, AUROC falls to 0.68, and ECE rises to 0.12. This is simultaneous **performance-process drift** and **model-relative concept drift**: the path has become slower, and diagnosis-time cases that previously had a certain risk now breach much more often because the external supplier state is missing from the model. Label maturity of 93% introduces some uncertainty but is not remotely sufficient to explain the whole change.

**September.** Several mechanisms overlap. The parts edge remains extremely slow at 148 hours; V3 reaches 42%; and Partner Z contributes 24% of cases, with 58% routed through V3. The aggregate breach rate is 0.31 against mean predicted risk of 0.21, AUROC is 0.66, and ECE is 0.14. This combines continued supplier-driven process and concept drift with a new population and route mix. Descriptively, the reported source rates imply
(0.24 \times 0.45 + 0.76 \times 0.27 \approx 0.313), close to the headline 0.31. Relative to a hypothetical all-non-Z cohort at 0.27, the source mix contributes roughly four percentage points. That is not a causal Partner Z effect: the difference includes V3 routing, rurality, device age, and unequal label maturity. Moreover, the non-Partner-Z rate of 0.27 is itself far above the January–March baseline, so Partner Z cannot explain the broader deterioration. With only 82% label maturity, all September source comparisons remain provisional.

**October.** October must not be interpreted as recovery merely because the currently observed breach rate falls to 0.16. Only 55% of labels are mature, median delay has risen to 52 days, and the missing labels disproportionately come from longer resolution paths. Meanwhile, the parts edge worsens to 155 hours, V3 remains at 41%, and Partner Z rises to 27%. The apparent direction of calibration even reverses—mean risk 0.23 versus observed breach 0.16—but this is exactly what selective omission of long, breach-prone cases can produce. AUROC, Brier, and ECE are also calculated on the selected labeled subset and are therefore not directly comparable with mature months. October is primarily a **measurement and censoring problem layered on continued process and population pressure**, not evidence that the supplier or model problem disappeared.

The periods therefore correspond to different statistical changes:

* April changes the mapping from raw events to measured features.
* June changes the distribution of routes and therefore the distribution of cases seen by the model.
* August–September change route performance and the conditional risk of breach, with September adding a new population.
* October changes which outcomes are observable by the reporting cutoff.

Treating all four as “model drift” would lead to the wrong remedy in at least three of the four periods.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

1. **Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting.**
   For every case-start month, variant, source, and score band, estimate the probability that a label is available by case age or by days after the promised resolution date. Recompute breach rate, calibration intercept and slope, Brier score, ECE, AUROC, and operational decision metrics at common vintages such as 45, 60, and 90 days. Treat unlabeled cases as censored; add inverse-probability-of-censoring weighting or survival-based sensitivity analysis.

   Under the **label-delay hypothesis**, October’s breach rate and calibration will move materially as labels arrive, with the largest movement among V3, Partner Z, high-risk cases, and long paths. October evaluated at a fixed 45-day vintage should look more comparable to earlier cohorts at the same vintage than the current mixed-vintage dashboard suggests. Under a **true October recovery or concept reversal**, final and fixed-vintage metrics should remain close to the current values, and label availability should be nearly independent of path, source, and score after controlling for case age. Stability after full maturity would weaken censoring as the main explanation.

2. **Full replay after activity normalization and duplicate cleaning.**
   Starting from raw event records, map both diagnosis labels to the canonical `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, apply the same source-aware duplicate rule to old and new sources, sort using stable event identifiers, reconstruct the prefix at the first valid canonical diagnosis event, and recompute every derived feature and frozen-model score. Compare April before and after repair, split at 1 April, 20 April, and 6 May.

   Under the **pipeline-failure hypothesis**, April’s diagnosis-anchor coverage should rise, unknown and unmapped shares should collapse, the cached event-count discrepancy should disappear, the score distribution should move toward January–March, and AUROC/Brier/ECE should improve substantially. Under a **real business-drift hypothesis**, repaired features will be internally consistent but predictive deterioration will remain, and the normalized DFG will still show meaningful route or edge-performance changes. Little or no metric recovery after a correct replay would falsify the pipeline defect as April’s principal cause.

3. **Calibration and discrimination stratified by month, variant, and source.**
   On mature or fixed-vintage labels, calculate AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, adaptive-bin reliability curves, and calibration intercept/slope from
   [
   \operatorname{logit} P(Y=1)=\alpha+\beta\operatorname{logit}(p).
   ]
   Report clustered bootstrap intervals and minimum segment sample sizes. Variant is a retrospective diagnostic stratum unless intended route was already known at prediction time.

   Under **composition-only covariate drift**, V1, V2, and V3 performance should remain stable within strata, while aggregate calibration changes because their weights change. Reweighting the baseline strata to the current mix should reproduce much of the aggregate movement. Under **concept drift**, V3’s calibration intercept, slope, or discrimination will deteriorate even within comparable cases, particularly after 10 July. If only the intercept changes while AUROC and slope remain stable, recalibration is sufficient; if ranking and slope deteriorate, retraining is indicated.

4. **Process-variant, DFG, and performance-DFG comparison.**
   Maintain two cohort definitions. An event-calendar view measures activities and workload occurring during each month. A case-start view follows complete observed traces of cases entering that month and is appropriate for eventual variant proportions and cross-month edge durations. Compare normalized V1–V3 shares, unmapped prefixes, DFG edge frequencies, and median/p90 edge times. Report the proportion of `Order Part` events still lacking a subsequent `Part Received`, since completed-edge medians alone can be right-censored.

   Under **genuine process drift**, June’s V2 frequency shift and July–September’s parts-edge deterioration should persist after normalization and across source systems; the remote edge should remain stable. Under a **logging artifact**, April’s new label, unknown nodes, duplicate loops, and apparent variants should disappear after cleaning, with no corresponding change in business edge times. If the parts-edge increase vanishes after timestamp and duplicate repair, the supplier-process explanation would be weakened.

5. **Test whether the outcome relationship changed conditional on the same path.**
   Within V3, compare January–June with July–September using matching, weighting, or flexible regression on prediction-time score and diagnosis-time covariates. Include month/supplier-regime interactions. Rurality, device age, source, and intended route should be included when available. Realized `Order Part -> Part Received` duration may be added only as a retrospective mediator analysis, not as a deployable feature.

   Under **concept drift**, an August–September month or supplier-regime effect will remain after balancing prediction-time covariates, and cases with the same pre-diagnosis score will breach more often. Under **covariate or population shift only**, the month effect should vanish after balancing the richer feature set. If adding realized parts wait removes the month effect, that identifies the supplier delay as the mechanism, but it does not make realized wait a valid prediction-time feature. Conditioning on eventual variant must also be treated cautiously because routing can occur after the prediction point.

6. **Partner Z transport and population-standardization checks.**
   Estimate the probability of belonging to Partner Z using overlapping prediction-time variables, including rurality, device age, geography, intended route, and case characteristics. Perform two analyses: transport legacy/non-Z cases to the Partner Z covariate distribution, and standardize September back to the pre-Z source and route distribution. Check overlap and effective sample size rather than extrapolating outside support.

   Under a **pure population-shift explanation**, the apparent source-level breach and calibration gap should become small after transport, while within-overlap model residuals remain similar. Under a **Partner-Z-specific operational or concept effect**, a material residual source effect will remain. Under **differential label delay**, the estimated source difference will change as vintages mature or after censoring weighting. Poor overlap would not prove a source effect; it would show that a global model is extrapolating and that additional data or a partially pooled segment model is needed.

7. **Feature missingness, prediction-anchor, and lineage audit.**
   For every day, source, schema version, and model version, report: raw row counts; canonical diagnosis events; eligible cases; predictions produced; predictions produced at the correct anchor; duplicate counts; unknown-label counts; queue-feature missingness; cached versus independently recomputed event counts; timestamp ordering violations; and feature checksums. Separately recompute breach labels from promised date and final resolution data for mature cases to verify the outcome API’s semantic consistency.

   Under **pipeline failure**, discontinuities should align with 1 April, 20 April, or 6 May and be concentrated in the migrated source or cached features. Under **business drift**, lineage and missingness should remain stable while path frequencies, feature values, or edge times change. If independently reconstructed labels disagree with API labels by month, an outcome-definition or outcome-pipeline problem is present despite the absence of a documented definition change.

8. **Frozen-score replay and recalibration-versus-retraining decomposition.**
   Score all repaired cohorts with the original frozen model. Compare four evaluations: original raw features, repaired features, repaired features with baseline composition weights, and repaired features with an intercept/slope recalibrator. Add candidate diagnosis-time supplier, route, rurality, and device-age features only in a separate retraining experiment.

   Under a **calibration-only problem**, repaired within-segment AUROC remains stable and the recalibrator materially improves Brier/ECE without changing ranking. Under a **representation or concept problem**, AUROC and calibration slope remain poor after repair and simple recalibration, while the new variables improve out-of-time ranking and segment calibration. If repair alone restores April but not August–September, that directly separates the pipeline episode from the later business drift.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Immediate containment**

Freeze automated retraining and automatic calibrator updates. Mark October predictive metrics as provisional and do not lower risk thresholds based on its observed 0.16 breach rate. Complete the canonical activity mapping, extend duplicate cleaning consistently across both sources, and require exactly one correctly anchored prediction per eligible case. Recompute all April derived features and frozen-model scores rather than merely changing the displayed activity label.

At the operational level, expose the parts-supplier condition as a transparent rule alongside the model. A V3 or high probability-of-V3 case during a severe parts backlog should receive an explicit risk uplift or manual review. This is containment of a known omitted condition, not a claim that the repaired model is already retrained. Source membership alone should not trigger an adverse action.

**Short-term recalibration**

Recalibrate only with mature, pipeline-clean data and only after stratified tests:

* For V1 and V2, a calibration update is reasonable if AUROC and calibration slope remain stable. June may justify a routing-aware intercept adjustment because the aggregate prevalence fell mainly through increased V2 usage.
* For V3, a temporary supplier-regime or route-specific intercept is reasonable only if within-V3 ranking remains adequate. The August–September AUROC decline means a global intercept alone is unlikely to be sufficient.
* Do not fit a calibrator to October at the current cutoff. It would learn from a selected subset that underrepresents long paths.
* Do not fit one global May–September calibrator without interactions. June needs a downward aggregate correction, whereas August–September need an upward V3 correction; one intercept would average incompatible regimes.

The decision rule should be explicit: stable within-segment AUROC and slope plus an intercept shift implies recalibration; deterioration in within-segment ranking or slope implies feature repair and retraining.

**Feature and pipeline repair**

The production feature set should include, when available at the diagnosis prediction time:

* intended route or probabilities of V1/V2/V3;
* current supplier, parts backlog, recent delivery reliability, and estimated part availability;
* Partner/source identifier with appropriate regularization;
* rural travel distance and device age;
* source-system and schema version;
* explicit missingness indicators;
* independently recomputed prefix length and event-count features.

No realized `Part Received` timestamp, final variant, or post-diagnosis repair information may enter the training prefix unless it was genuinely known at score time. If the routing decision occurs after scoring, use an intended-route signal or route-probability model rather than the realized final variant.

**Full retraining**

Full retraining is justified after three gates pass:

1. feature lineage and prefix reconstruction are correct;
2. target cohorts have sufficiently mature, non-selectively observed labels or defensible censoring weights;
3. the new features improve out-of-time discrimination or calibration beyond what segment recalibration achieves.

Use time-based and regime-based validation rather than random cross-validation. At minimum, hold out the supplier-shock period and Partner Z cases in separate tests. Compare a pooled model, a pooled model with interactions, and a partially pooled mixture-of-experts approach. The supplier shock may be temporary, so retain an explicit supplier-state feature rather than teaching the model that every future V3 case permanently has August-level risk.

| Month             | Training status at the stated cutoff                         | Rationale                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **January–March** | **Safe baseline**                                            | Mature labels, stable pipeline, and stable process.                                                                                                                                         |
| **April**         | **Unsafe as currently stored**                               | Labels are mature, but prediction anchors, duplicates, cached features, and activity mappings are corrupted. It becomes usable only after complete raw-event reconstruction and validation. |

| **August**        | **Unsafe for ordinary complete-case training at the cutoff** | Only 93% mature and the missingness may be path-related. Once mature, it is essential current-regime data for V3 adaptation.                                                                |

A **variant-specific architecture is justified in principle**, because V2 remains stable and low-risk while V3 is governed by parts availability. The deployable segmentation must nevertheless use information available at diagnosis. A pooled base model with route-specific calibration or probabilistic gating is safer than three wholly separate models unless each route has adequate mature volume.

A **Partner-Z-only model is not yet justified**. There are only two Partner Z months, both affected by incomplete labels, and source differences are strongly entangled with rurality, device age, and V3 routing. Prefer a pooled model with source interactions or hierarchical shrinkage. Reconsider a separate component after mature cohorts demonstrate persistent residual differences and sufficient feature overlap.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

| **Intervention and deployment**  | Model, calibrator, feature, and policy versions; score threshold; alert/action rate; capacity denial; intervention timing; treated versus untreated cases; manual overrides; and policy changes.                                                                       | A changed intervention policy can change observed outcomes and create apparent concept drift. Performance must be stratified or causally adjusted for intervention exposure before updating the model. |

**Alert dependencies**

1. **Data-quality red takes precedence.** Classify the episode as a pipeline incident, freeze learning updates, and replay data. Do not “fix” calibration around malformed features.
2. **Label-maturity red makes predictive alerts provisional.** Continue data-quality and process monitoring, but require fixed-vintage confirmation before recalibration or retraining.
3. **Process or population red with stable within-stratum predictive metrics** calls for operational action, reweighting, route-aware thresholds, or recalibration—not necessarily retraining.
4. **Mature labels plus clean lineage plus within-stratum calibration-slope or AUROC deterioration** constitutes the strongest retraining trigger.
5. **Deployment-policy changes are another gate.** If interventions or capacity allocation changed, separate policy effects from model drift before interpreting the observed outcome relationship.
6. A severe parts-edge alarm may immediately escalate to supplier management even when October labels are immature; the associated model update waits for mature evidence.

([GitHub][1])

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE, ACT, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
DIAG = "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
ACTIVITY_MAP = {"Diagnostic Assessment Completed": DIAG}

KNOWN_VARIANTS = {
    ("Case Created", "Triage", DIAG, "Dispatch Technician",
     "Repair Completed", "Case Closed"): "V1",
    ("Case Created", "Triage", DIAG, "Remote Fix", "Case Closed"): "V2",
    ("Case Created", "Triage", DIAG, "Order Part", "Part Received",
     "Dispatch Technician", "Repair Completed", "Case Closed"): "V3",
}


def monthly_process_evidence(
    path: str = "field_service_events.csv",
    queue_col: str = "queue_feature",
) -> dict:
    df = pd.read_csv(path)
    required = {CASE, ACT, TS}
    if missing := required - set(df.columns):
        raise ValueError(f"Missing mandatory columns: {sorted(missing)}")
    rows_read = len(df)

    # Normalize before prefix, variant, or temporal-feature extraction.
    df["activity_raw"] = df[ACT]
    df[ACT] = df[ACT].astype("string").replace(ACTIVITY_MAP)
    df[TS] = pd.to_datetime(df[TS], utc=True, errors="coerce")
    invalid = df[[CASE, ACT, TS]].isna().any(axis=1)
    df = df.loc[~invalid].copy()
    df[CASE] = df[CASE].astype("string")
    df["_event_month"] = df[TS].dt.strftime("%Y-%m")

    # Prefer stable source event IDs. The fallback removes only exact
    # same-time payload duplicates, not all repeated activity occurrences.
    event_id = next(
        (c for c in ("source_event_id", "event_id", "event:id")
         if c in df.columns),
        None,
    )
    if event_id:
        dedup_key = [
            c for c in ("source_system", event_id) if c in df.columns
        ]
        duplicate = (
            df[event_id].notna()
            & df.duplicated(dedup_key, keep="first")
        )
    else:
        dedup_key = [CASE, ACT, TS] + [
            c for c in
            ("source_system", "org:resource", "lifecycle:transition")
            if c in df.columns
        ]
        duplicate = df.duplicated(dedup_key, keep="first")

    duplicate_audit = (
        df.assign(duplicate_removed=duplicate)
        .groupby("_event_month", as_index=False)
        .agg(
            rows_in=(CASE, "size"),
            duplicates_removed=("duplicate_removed", "sum"),
        )
    )
    duplicate_audit["duplicate_share"] = (
        duplicate_audit["duplicates_removed"]
        / duplicate_audit["rows_in"]
    )

    clean = df.loc[~duplicate].copy()
    clean["org:resource"] = (
        clean["org:resource"].fillna("MISSING_RESOURCE")
        if "org:resource" in clean.columns
        else "MISSING_RESOURCE"
    )
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        clean,
        case_id=CASE,
        activity_key=ACT,
        timestamp_key=TS,
    )

    def edge_hours(performance_dfg: dict, edge: tuple) -> object:
        value = performance_dfg.get(edge)
        if isinstance(value, dict):
            value = value.get("median")
        return pd.NA if value is None else float(value) / 3600.0

    event_rows, case_rows, variant_rows = [], [], []

    for month in pd.period_range("2026-01", "2026-10", freq="M"):
        start = month.start_time.tz_localize("UTC")
        end = (
            (month + 1).start_time.tz_localize("UTC")
            - pd.Timedelta(microseconds=1)
        )
        start, end = start.isoformat(), end.isoformat()

        # Event-calendar cohort: work executed in this month.
        # Traces are clipped, so edge counts mean both events occurred in-month.
        event_cohort = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            log, start, end, mode="events"
        )
        if len(event_cohort):
            dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(event_cohort)
            event_rows.append({
                "month": str(month),
                "events": len(event_cohort),
                "active_cases": event_cohort[CASE].nunique(),
                "unknown_activity_share":
                    event_cohort[ACT].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean(),
                "part_edges_with_both_events_in_month":
                    dfg.get(("Order Part", "Part Received"), 0),
            })

        # Case-start cohort: whole currently observed traces of cases whose
        # first event is in this month. Results stabilize as cases complete.
        case_cohort = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            log, start, end, mode="traces_starting_in"
        )
        if not len(case_cohort):
            continue

        n_cases = int(case_cohort[CASE].nunique())
        diagnosis_rows = case_cohort[case_cohort[ACT].eq(DIAG)]
        performance_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
            case_cohort,
            perf_aggregation_key="median",
        )

        case_rows.append({
            "month": str(month),
            "started_cases": n_cases,
            "diagnosis_anchor_coverage":
                diagnosis_rows[CASE].nunique() / n_cases,
            "closed_case_share":
                case_cohort.loc[
                    case_cohort[ACT].eq("Case Closed"), CASE
                ].nunique() / n_cases,
            "queue_missing_at_diagnosis":
                diagnosis_rows[queue_col].isna().mean()
                if queue_col in diagnosis_rows.columns
                and len(diagnosis_rows)
                else pd.NA,
            "part_edge_median_hours":
                edge_hours(
                    performance_dfg,
                    ("Order Part", "Part Received"),
                ),
            "remote_edge_median_hours":
                edge_hours(
                    performance_dfg,
                    (DIAG, "Remote Fix"),
                ),
        })

        counts = {"V1": 0, "V2": 0, "V3": 0, "OTHER": 0}
        for trace, count in pm4py.get_variants(case_cohort).items():
            name = KNOWN_VARIANTS.get(tuple(trace), "OTHER")
            counts[name] += int(count)

        variant_rows.extend({
            "month": str(month),
            "variant": name,
            "cases": count,
            "share_of_started_cases": count / n_cases,
        } for name, count in counts.items())

    temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
        log,
        grouper_freq="M",
        activity_key=ACT,
        timestamp_key=TS,
        case_id_key=CASE,
        start_timestamp_key=TS,
        resource_key="org:resource",
    )

    return {
        "event_calendar_monthly": pd.DataFrame(event_rows),
        "case_start_monthly": pd.DataFrame(case_rows),
        "variant_monthly": pd.DataFrame(variant_rows),
        "temporal_features_monthly": temporal,
        "duplicate_audit_monthly": duplicate_audit,
        "lineage": {
            "rows_read": rows_read,
            "invalid_core_rows_removed": int(invalid.sum()),
            "duplicates_removed": int(duplicate.sum()),
            "dedup_key": dedup_key,
            "activity_mapping": ACTIVITY_MAP,
        },
    }
```

The event-calendar summary answers “what work occurred during this month?” and may contain partial traces. The case-start summary answers “what eventually happened to cases entering this month?” and includes later events, so its variants and edge durations are retrospective and must be accompanied by `closed_case_share`. Neither view should silently substitute for the other.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

| Wrong diagnosis or action                                                                             | Likely failure                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Mitigation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Retraining on October’s currently labeled cases**                                                   | The training sample underrepresents long, unresolved, and likely breach-prone paths. A new model may learn that long-path profiles are safer than they are, lower scores precisely where risk is highest, and encode outcome-API behavior rather than business risk.                    | Exclude October from ordinary supervised training, preserve unresolved labels as censored, use fixed-vintage evaluation, and require maturity or validated censoring weights before inclusion.                                                 |
| **Recalibrating around April’s pipeline defect**                                                      | A calibrator could compensate numerically for wrong prefixes and malformed event-count features while leaving individual rankings and operational explanations invalid. Once the pipeline is repaired, that compensating calibration would itself become wrong.                         | Repair normalization, duplicate handling, and prefix reconstruction first. Replay the frozen model on recomputed features and recalibrate only after lineage reconciliation.                                                                   |
| **Treating the August–September deterioration as variant mix only**                                   | Baseline mix predicts only about 19–20% breach, well below the observed 28–31%. Ignoring the within-V3 jump would continue severe underprediction of parts-dependent cases and misallocate intervention capacity.                                                                       | Monitor V3 calibration and supplier-edge performance separately, add supplier/backlog features, use a temporary transparent V3 regime adjustment, and retrain after mature labels confirm the conditional shift.                               |
| **Treating June’s routing change as a failed model requiring full retraining**                        | Unnecessary retraining could discard a still-useful ranking model and overfit a short policy period. The main issue is that more cases moved to the stable, low-risk V2 path.                                                                                                           | Perform composition standardization and within-variant validation. Use intended-route features or route-aware calibration when within-route discrimination remains stable.                                                                     |
| **Concluding that Partner Z is intrinsically poor or unfairly scored from aggregate September rates** | Partner Z’s rate is confounded by V3 routing, rural distance, device age, supplier conditions, and slower label maturity. A blanket source penalty could unfairly disadvantage rural customers and conceal the real parts-process problem.                                              | Wait for mature labels, stratify by route and operational characteristics, perform transport/overlap analysis, and evaluate residual calibration. Fairness conclusions require relevant protected-group information that is not supplied here. |
| **Building a variant-specific model with the realized final variant**                                 | If the final path is determined after diagnosis, using it during training creates future-information leakage. Offline performance will be inflated and unreproducible at deployment.                                                                                                    | Use only intended route known at scoring time or a diagnosis-time route-probability model. Enforce timestamp-aware feature lineage tests.                                                                                                      |
| **Using one global recalibrator across June and August–September**                                    | June predominantly needs a lower aggregate intercept because of more V2 cases, while August–September require higher V3 risk. A global adjustment may appear acceptable overall while worsening both important segments.                                                                | Use stratified intercept/slope tests and, where deployable, route- or supplier-regime-specific calibration with hierarchical shrinkage.                                                                                                        |
| **Assuming duplicate removal is harmless and applying an aggressive rule**                            | Deduplicating every repeated activity within a case could remove legitimate rework or repeated status events, distort prefix length, and erase real loops from the DFG.                                                                                                                 | Prefer stable source event IDs. Otherwise deduplicate only exact same-time payload matches, preserve raw rows and duplicate reasons, and compare variants and edge counts before and after cleaning.                                           |
| **Interpreting process-edge alarms as automatic evidence for model retraining**                       | The supplier edge can deteriorate before enough outcomes mature. Immediate retraining on incomplete targets would combine a correct operational signal with an invalid learning sample.                                                                                                 | Let the performance-DFG alarm trigger supplier and capacity action immediately, but gate model updates on clean features, mature labels, and demonstrated within-stratum predictive deterioration.                                             |
| **Ignoring changes in intervention or alert policy**                                                  | If high-risk cases receive more help, observed post-intervention outcomes can make the model appear better calibrated or can reverse apparent risk relationships. Retraining without accounting for treatment selection may suppress scores for cases that benefited from intervention. | Log eligibility, score, action, capacity denial, timing, and policy version. Evaluate untreated or policy-comparable cohorts and use causal adjustment where interventions materially affect outcomes.                                         |
