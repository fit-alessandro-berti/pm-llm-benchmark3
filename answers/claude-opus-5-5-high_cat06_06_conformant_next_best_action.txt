## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

**Ordering principle (lexicographic, not a single weighted sum).**
1. First apply feasibility and conformance. Mandatory controls define the set of permitted actions and are not cost terms to be traded away. A and B are removed, and G is removed until it is enabled.
2. Among the remaining options, give priority to irreversible and asymmetric harms (unlawful execution, SAR tipping-off, falsified records).
3. Then compare the monetary ranges, using dominance and regret rather than point estimates.

**Monetary comparison among conformant plans (ranges, not point estimates).**
- *Deadline value.* This is €20k–35k and is realised only if the payment is approved and executed by T+90.
  - P1: 0.30–0.55 × €20k–35k ≈ €6k–19k.
  - P2: 0.03–0.15, i.e. about €1k–5k.
  - The P1-over-P2 gain in on-time execution probability is about 0.2–0.45, worth roughly €4k–16k.
- *P-901 spillover (P1 only).* The breach probability rises by 0.12–0.28 for 45 min, and up to about 0.35 if the review runs 75 min. P-901's value is not given. If it is similar to P-884 (€20k–35k), the expected spillover is about €2.4k–12k. On these assumptions, **P1's net advantage over P2 is between about −€8k and +€13k, with a modestly positive centre.** This is effectively a close call, which is why P1 contains an explicit gate rather than an unconditional instruction.
- *False rejection or prolonged hold (€15k–80k).*
  - F turns this into an expected ≈ €8k–60k, because the case would have been approved 55–75% of the time.
  - E incurs a large share of it through a 6–24 h hold, plus the lost deadline value.
  - P1 and P2 incur only a short overrun in the branches where the deadline is missed. A 30–60 min overrun, possibly absorbed by an agreed extension, is far less than a "prolonged hold".
- *Dominance arguments.*
  - P1 weakly dominates C alone. The screening finishes (T+15–30) before S2's review can finish (≥T+45), so it adds information at no time cost. Its only cost is one of three API calls.
  - P1 and P2 dominate E standalone, because E remains available as a branch if the second officer asks for more evidence.
  - P1 and P2 dominate F: a conformant rejection is still available within 45–85 min on better evidence, and an earlier rejection has almost no deadline value.
  - G is dominated by P1 on time (G's valid output, if any, arrives no earlier than P1's decision) and on certainty of conformance.

**Regulatory and non-monetary asymmetry.** Unlawful execution is irreversible (funds cannot be recalled), carries a €100k–500k tail, and brings non-monetary consequences (supervisory action, licence risk, personal liability, reputational damage). Deadline harm is bounded, partly recoverable through an extension or compensation, and borne commercially. Even if we ignored the constraint and monetised, A's *incremental* benefit over P1 is at most about €10k–25k. A breaks even only if P(execution is unlawful) × €100k–500k is below that, i.e. P below about 2–25%. With a 0.46 fuzzy match whose calibration is unknown, a sanctioned-border destination and a score of 72, nobody can credibly certify a probability that low. More importantly, the control breach itself is *certain*, whatever the counterparty's true status. So even a monetary view does not favour A, and minimax regret strongly favours conformant plans.

**Uncertainty about intervention effects.**
- The forecasts are observational:
  - "SLA breach 0.48–0.64 under normal processing" was presumably learned when second officers were routinely available.
  - The "approval 0.55–0.75" figure comes from conformant reviews, not from expedited reviews with an extra screening step.
- My case-specific timing arithmetic for the S4 route gives a much higher miss probability (≈0.75–0.97) than the 0.48–0.64 forecast. I weight the explicit resource state more heavily, because the current situation (one interruptible officer, one late officer) is probably under-represented in the training history. The gap is itself evidence that the forecast does not transport.
- Expedited reviews under deadline pressure may be shorter (selection: simple cases were historically expedited) or of lower quality (automation and deadline bias). An alternate screening may change reviewer behaviour in either direction.
- None of these uncertainties is large enough to change the exclusion of A and B. They do affect the P1 vs P2 order, which is why that order is gated.

**Why A and B cannot become recommended through a favourable forecast.**
1. A forecast estimates *what will happen*. A mandatory control defines *what is permitted*. Predictive monitoring ranks actions *within* the permitted set and cannot enlarge that set.
2. Rules 6, 7 and 9 have no clause allowing a trade-off for expected value. The only exception mechanism is rule 10, and its conditions are unmet.
3. The favourable numbers do not even point the way A needs. "0.55–0.75 approval after a conformant review" means that 25–45% of such cases are *not* approved on review. A executes all of them. The approval forecast is conditional on the review taking place and says nothing about the safety of skipping it.
4. The forecasts are observational and do not transport to an intervention (removing a control) that never appears in the training data.
5. For B, a four-eyes control exists precisely to prevent one person's judgement or interest from deciding alone. No accuracy statistic about R17 can satisfy it, and self-certification also creates a false record.

**What would make F valid.**
- R17's post-evidence Analyst Review is recorded.
- There is a documented, policy-defined rejection criterion that is demonstrably met. Examples:
  - the fuzzy match is corroborated by secondary identifiers (name plus date of birth or incorporation, address, registration or ID numbers, or ownership and control links to a listed party);
  - the validated evidence is inconsistent with the stated supplier relationship;
  - the refreshed risk score exceeds a rejection threshold;
  - the alternate screening confirms the hit.
- If the organisation reads rule 8 as requiring the rule-6 review before any final decision, an independent second officer (S2 or S4) signs off.
- Any SAR decision is taken and recorded separately, and the rejection notice to the customer contains no SAR or suspicion disclosure.

A 0.46 confidence score by itself, for an established supplier, is unlikely to meet such a threshold.

**What would make G valid.**
- A Compliance Director explicitly authorises it, with verified identity, a timestamp and a stated scope (which control is waived and why).
- A genuine incident identifier is recorded, created *before* execution and justified by an actual incident, such as a screening-system outage that prevents normal review. Commercial deadline pressure is not an incident, and a retroactive or manufactured incident ID is invalid.
- Even a valid internal override cannot make a payment to a sanctioned party lawful. Realistically, G could cover procedural exceptions, not sanctions clearance.

## 5. Resource spillovers

**S2 versus S4.**
- *S2 route.* The second review can start at about T+10 and finish at T+45–85, giving an on-time probability of about 0.5–0.75 conditional on approval. The cost is P-901, which has the *earlier* deadline (T+70). The 0.12–0.28 breach increase is quoted for a 45-min interruption, but S2's review may take 35–75 min, so the realistic range is about +0.10–0.35.
- *S4 route.* The earliest start is T+35–65, finishing at T+75–155, so the P-884 deadline is usually missed. There is no spillover, and the review is less rushed.
- *Deciding information.* The deciding unknown is P-901's stake. The operations lead should get it in the first 10 minutes, together with:
  - whether S2 can pause P-901 at a safe point (not mid-way through a control step);
  - whether S4, on arrival, can take over P-901 (S4 must be independent of P-901's first analyst);
  - whether S2 is close to finishing P-901 anyway. In that case, "S2 right after P-901" beats both interruption and waiting for S4.
- If P-901's breach cost clearly exceeds P-884's, choose S2 only if a handover to S4 can protect P-901. Otherwise choose P2.
- Fairness and quality point: interrupting one high-value compliance review to rush another raises the risk of degraded review quality on *both* cases. S2 must be explicitly authorised to take the time the evidence needs, and the deadline must not become a reason to approve.

**Alternate-screening API quota (3 left).**
- Using one call on P-884 is justified: €480k, an unresolved match, a sanctioned-border region and a decision needed within 90 minutes.
- It runs concurrently, so it costs no time. It raises the value of whichever second review follows (faster, better calibrated, fewer evidence-request holds).
- Two calls remain for the rest of the day. Check first whether P-901 or other queued cases have unresolved matches that need a call more urgently.
- Do not spend a second call on P-884 to re-roll an inconclusive result.

**Time spent waiting for an exception.**
- G's lead time (20–60 min or more, reachability uncertain), plus creating and justifying an incident, would use most or all of the 90-minute window. The expected output is "no valid override", because no genuine incident exists.
- If G replaces P1, the P-884 deadline is lost and nothing is gained.
- If G runs alongside P1, it adds pressure on the reviewers and creates an audit trail suggesting an attempt to get around controls.
- Recommendation: do not request an override. An *informational* escalation to the Compliance Director is fine: "high-value, two rule-6 triggers, competing for S2 with P-901, requesting a prioritisation decision." It is a legitimate resourcing question and may settle the S2 allocation faster.

**S4 available at T+35 versus T+65.**
- **S4 at T+35.** P2 finishes at T+75–125, giving an on-time probability of about 0.10–0.26 (conditional on approval ≈ 0.2–0.35). Its gap to P1 shrinks to about 0.1–0.3 in on-time probability, roughly equal to P1's P-901 cost. On the tie-breakers (no interruption, no rushed review, no queue-jumping, P-901's earlier deadline), **P2 moves to rank 1 and P1 drops to rank 2.**
- **S4 at T+65.** P2 finishes at T+105–155, so the on-time probability is ≈ 0. P2 then only avoids spillover and gives up the P-884 deadline for certain. **P1 is clearly rank 1** unless P-901's breach cost is more than about 1.5–2 times P-884's.
  - S4 at T+65 also cannot rescue P-901 before its T+70 deadline, so P-901 mitigation depends on S2 returning.
  - P2 stays second, and E moves closer to P2 because both miss the deadline, though P2 still resolves hours sooner.
- **S4 at around T+50.** Use P1 if P-901 can be paused safely; otherwise use P2.
- In every scenario the ordering of C alone, E, F, G, A and B relative to each other is unchanged.

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence

**Primary recommendation: Plan P1.** Start the parallel conformant preparation now:
- R17 records the post-evidence Analyst Review and prepares a dossier for the second officer;
- refresh the risk score;
- run one alternate sanctions screening;
- the relationship manager makes a neutral deadline-extension enquiry without disclosing any suspicion or SAR;
- the operations lead confirms S4's ETA and P-901's pause point and stake.

At a gate no later than T+10, S2 pauses P-901 and performs an independent Second Officer Review. The screening result is added to S2's review when it arrives.
- If S2 approves, record Approve and execute.
- If S2 rejects, record the reasons.
- If suspicion remains, hold and file a SAR without disclosure.
- If the evidence is still insufficient, request more evidence (E).

When S4 arrives, S4 takes over P-901 if eligible, to limit the spillover.

**Fallback: Plan P2.** Use the same parallel preparation and screening, keep S2 on P-901, and have S4 perform the Second Officer Review on arrival. Switch to P2 at the gate if any of the following holds:
- S4 is confirmed free by about T+40;
- P-901 cannot be paused safely;
- P-901's stake clearly exceeds P-884's and no handover to S4 is possible.

In either route, F is taken only if the documented rejection threshold is shown to be met. G, A and B are not pursued.

**Confidence.**
- High (≈ 0.9) that the correct top tier is "parallel alternate screening plus an independent second-officer review".
- Very high (> 0.95) that A, B and G belong at the bottom.
- Medium (≈ 0.55–0.65) that P1 rather than P2 is the better implementation, because the comparison depends on P-901's stake and S4's ETA, neither of which is known yet.

**Events that should trigger immediate reranking.**
- S4's confirmed ETA (≤ T+40 favours P2; ≥ T+50 favours P1).
- P-901's status: its stake, its pause point, or S2 being close to finishing it.
- The alternate-screening result. A strong hit moves the case to Reject or Hold + SAR, and S2 is released after recording the decision. A documented false match speeds up approval.
- The refreshed risk score. If it is below 70 *and* the match is formally resolved, check whether rule 6 still applies and, if not, release S2.
- The customer agreeing or refusing a deadline extension. An extension makes P2 dominant.
- S2's review running past about T+60 without being close to a decision. The deadline is then likely lost, so reassess whether to return S2 to P-901.
- Any genuine incident together with Director authorization (only then is G re-evaluated).
- Any new information that supports suspicion, which triggers the SAR and hold branch.
