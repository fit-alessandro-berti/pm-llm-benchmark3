## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

### Scenario A — Smooth path: normal chemistry, normal microbiology, no QA exception

**Chemistry waiting and service**

- At 15:30 Friday, the two analysts have approximately 0.4–2.0 hours of current work remaining.
- Under risk-priority handling, B-417 could start approximately 16:00–17:00, but its 1.5–4.5 hour service time means Friday completion is only possible in the favorable part of that range.
- Under FIFO-like handling, the urgent plus three ordinary samples ahead make Friday completion unlikely; B-417 either starts late Friday and pauses or does not start until Monday.
- A practical smooth-path chemistry result range is therefore:
  - **best case:** Friday roughly 17:30–18:00;
  - **more typical if it spills:** Monday roughly 09:30–13:00.
- This is queue waiting plus service time, not just the 1.5–4.5 hour analytical duration.

**Microbiology incubation and readout**

- Incubation started Friday 14:00 and becomes technically ready Sunday approximately 12:00–20:00.
- If on-call weekend readout is authorized, the result can be available Sunday roughly 13:00–23:00.
- If no authorization is granted, technical readiness over the weekend waits for Monday staffing; readout begins from 08:00 Monday and usually finishes about 08:30–09:30.

**Parallel join time**

- The join is:

\[
T_{\text{join}}=\max(T_{\text{chemistry usable}}, T_{\text{microbiology usable}})
\]

- It is **not** chemistry time plus microbiology time.
- In the ordinary no-weekend-readout case, chemistry often completes by Monday morning/noon, while microbiology is usable around Monday 08:30–09:30. Thus the join is commonly Monday around 09:30–13:00, depending on chemistry spillover.
- If weekend readout occurs, QA is closed Sunday, so the effective operational benefit is limited: QA still starts Monday 08:00, unless chemistry is itself later.

**QA waiting and service**

- At a Monday join, two reviewers are available from 08:00.
- The five currently known batches may have been partly processed over Friday/Saturday, but Monday arrivals and overdispersion create a broad queue.
- A reasonable routine estimate is:
  - QA wait: about 0.5–4 working hours;
  - QA service: about 0.8–3.5 working hours.
- For a Monday morning join, QA acceptance is therefore plausibly Monday around 11:00–17:30.

**Optional rework/investigation**

- None on this scenario. This is conditional on no Documentation Query and no investigation.

**Final release**

- Electronic Release takes 0.25–0.75 hours continuously after QA acceptance.
- Smooth terminal event: **Electronic Release Monday approximately 11:30–18:15**, with later Monday more representative than the earliest edge.

### Scenario B — Chemistry rerun or substantial chemistry Monday spillover

**Chemistry waiting and service**

- The initial chemistry result is obtained after the same uncertain Friday/Monday queue process.
- A borderline result triggers a rerun with approximately 0.10–0.20 probability in ordinary conditions, and more if the excursion is confirmed.
- The rerun receives priority and needs 1.0–3.0 hours of lab service.
- If the initial result arrives late Friday, there is insufficient time to finish the rerun before 18:00; the rerun is then paused or starts Monday at 08:00.
- Representative chemistry usable-result windows:
  - favorable rerun: Monday roughly 09:00–11:00;
  - more typical rerun after late initial completion: Monday roughly 11:00–15:00;
  - adverse queue/service case: Monday late afternoon.

**Microbiology incubation and readout**

- If microbiology is normal, it is usable Sunday evening with on-call or Monday roughly 08:30–09:30 without on-call.
- It may therefore be ready before chemistry on this scenario.

**Parallel join time**

- The maximum now commonly becomes chemistry:

\[
T_{\text{join}}=\max(T_{\text{chemistry with rerun}},T_{\text{microbiology}})
\approx T_{\text{chemistry with rerun}}
\]

- Typical join: Monday around 11:00–15:00, potentially later.

**QA waiting and service**

- A later Monday join can meet a still-variable QA queue.
- If the join occurs around midday or afternoon, QA may complete same day only if queue and service are favorable.
- Allow about 0.5–4 hours of waiting plus 0.8–3.5 hours of review, with some work pushed to Tuesday if acceptance cannot be reached by 20:00.

**Optional rework/investigation**

- The rerun is an adverse signal for governance even if the final result is acceptable.
- Therefore investigation probability should use the elevated 0.30–0.55 conditional regime rather than the 0.04–0.10 routine regime.
- If no investigation is opened, release can still occur Monday or Tuesday.
- If a documentation-only investigation opens, add 8–20 working hours; if laboratory investigation opens, add 3–7 calendar days.

**Final release or rejection**

- Without QA exception: Electronic Release is plausibly Monday late afternoon through Tuesday midday.
- With an investigation: Wednesday through the following week is more realistic.
- Rejection remains possible only if evidence establishes a critical defect; rerun alone is not rejection.

### Scenario C — Delayed microbiology readout or contamination confirmation

**Chemistry waiting and service**

- Chemistry may finish Friday in the favorable priority case or Monday morning/afternoon under FIFO/spillover.
- In this scenario, chemistry is often complete before the final microbiology result and is therefore not the controlling branch.

**Microbiology incubation and readout**

There are two materially different subcases.

1. **Normal microbiology, but no weekend on-call authorization**
   - Technical readiness: Sunday 12:00–20:00.
   - Result waits for Monday staffing.
   - Readout/recording: Monday about 08:30–09:30.
   - This is a calendar-induced delay, not additional incubation.

2. **Contamination indication**
   - Primary incubation/readout produces an indication around Sunday with on-call, or Monday morning without it.
   - Confirmation requires an additional 20–30 hours of continuous incubation.
   - If the indication is read Monday morning, confirmation becomes technically ready Tuesday roughly 04:30–15:30, then requires 0.5–1.5 hours of staffed readout.
   - In the practical central case, usable confirmed microbiology is **Tuesday morning through Tuesday afternoon**.
   - If an earlier weekend indication is read under on-call, confirmation may technically mature Monday, but its readout still depends on staffing and QA’s later availability.

**Parallel join time**

- For no-weekend readout but normal result:

\[
T_{\text{join}}\approx \max(T_{\text{chemistry}},\text{Monday 08:30--09:30})
\]

- For contamination confirmation:

\[
T_{\text{join}}\approx T_{\text{microbiology confirmation}}
\]

because it is generally later than chemistry.

**QA waiting and service**

- A normal Monday readout can still permit Monday QA completion, subject to the queue.
- A Tuesday confirmation join faces two-reviewer weekday capacity, but queue conditions remain uncertain.
- Estimate roughly 0.5–4 hours QA wait plus 0.8–3.5 hours review in a routine case; longer under Monday/Tuesday congestion.

**Optional rework/investigation**

- A contamination indication, even if not ultimately disqualifying, places the batch in the elevated investigation-probability regime: approximately 0.30–0.55.
- A confirmed contamination or critical defect can produce rejection rather than release.
- A documentation-only investigation adds 8–20 working hours; a laboratory investigation adds 3–7 calendar days.

**Final release or rejection**

- Normal delayed-readout path: Electronic Release Monday afternoon to Tuesday.
- Contamination-confirmation path without major investigation: likely Tuesday late or Wednesday.
- With investigation or critical defect: Wednesday through the following week; terminal event may be Electronic Release or Batch Rejected.

### Scenario D — QA Documentation Query or investigation tail

**Chemistry waiting and service**

- Assume both branches have usable results, typically by Monday morning/afternoon in the no-confirmation case.
- Chemistry is no longer the active clock once QA has opened review, unless additional laboratory investigation is requested.

**Microbiology incubation and readout**

- Likewise, the microbiology branch is complete at the join.
- The relevant effect is that rerun, contamination indication, or confirmed excursion changes QA’s likelihood of investigation.

**Parallel join time**

- The join remains the maximum of the two branch completion times.
- A representative normal-result join is Monday around 09:00–13:00; an adverse microbiology confirmation join is Tuesday.

**QA waiting and service**

- Initial QA wait/service occurs first.
- Suppose the reviewer starts Monday but finds a documentation gap:
  - Documentation Query probability: 0.15–0.30.
  - Query resolution adds 4–12 working hours before QA can resume.
  - Because QA has limited Saturday hours and no Sunday work, a query raised late Friday/Saturday or late Monday may cross a calendar boundary.
- For a Monday query, the short end can return Tuesday morning; the upper end can extend into Wednesday.

**Optional rework/investigation**

- If both tests are normal and no excursion is confirmed, investigation remains relatively uncommon, about 0.04–0.10.
- If rerun, contamination indication, or excursion exists, investigation probability rises to about 0.30–0.55.
- Documentation-only investigation:
  - 8–20 working hours;
  - usually pushes a Monday join to Wednesday–Friday.
- Laboratory investigation:
  - 3–7 calendar days;
  - pushes the terminal event into late week or the following week.
- Query and investigation can overlap in the sense that the same case may need both; their durations should not be treated as independent or automatically additive if the same evidence-gathering resolves both. For a conservative tail, however, sequential handoffs can make much of both delay real.

**Final release or rejection**

- After QA accepts, Electronic Release is only 0.25–0.75 hours and is not the principal delay.
- If the investigation establishes a critical defect, rejection recording is 0.5–1.0 hours after that decision.
- Thus the tail is governed by quality-system rework and decision-making, not release-system execution.
