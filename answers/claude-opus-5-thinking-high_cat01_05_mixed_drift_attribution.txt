# Drift diagnosis: document-eligibility process, 2026-01 to 2026-12

Before the required sections, three arithmetic consistency checks that condition everything below (they were computed from the supplied tables, not assumed):

- **The normalized DFG table is internally consistent with the raw snippets.** 2026-03: `Register->Eligibility Screen` (500) + `Register->Validate Eligibility` (140) = 640 = all cases, i.e. exactly 100 per 100 cases and a **partial (78%) label rollout**. `Open Case -> Register` = 26 ≈ 4% x 640 = 25.6, matching `duplicate_open_case_pct`. 2026-05: 163/680 = 24.0 and 2026-07: 207/715 = 28.9 reproduce the `Assess->RequestInfo` rates of 24 and 29. 2026-10: `Assess -> Senior Assessment` 167/760 = 22.0. This raises trust in the mapped series and lets me treat the two tables as one object.
- **Rework did not deepen, it spread.** In every month `Assess->RequestInfo` per 100 cases equals `rework_variant_pct` almost exactly (12/12, 24/24, 29/29, 26/26, 21/21). So the number of loops per reworked case stayed at ~1; only the *share of cases entering a loop* rose. This rules out "loop escalation / repeated info requests" as a mechanism and favours a one-shot document-deficiency mechanism.
- **`RequestInfo->CollectDocs` is consistently ~1 per 100 cases below `Assess->RequestInfo`**, so a small stream of cases stalls after `Request Info` and never returns documents. Small, but it is a real leak and belongs in monitoring.

## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The March gap (raw 0.55, mapped 0.90) closes about 93% of the conformance deficit - (0.90-0.55)/(0.925-0.55) - and the recovery ratio stays between 0.85 and 0.96 in every later month. That implies the *dominant* driver of the raw collapse is vocabulary and provenance, not behaviour: a renamed activity plus an injected technical event make an unchanged trace unalignable against the old normative model. It does not imply the process was unchanged. A label mapping is an assertion of semantic identity; recovering fitness only shows the mapping is *sufficient* to realign traces, and any behavioural change occurring on 2026-03-01 would be absorbed silently into the same step. Mapping can also hide a real split - `Eligibility Screen` may be a different check with different rules, and the 22% of March cases still carrying the old label may be a genuinely different pathway rather than a rollout remnant.

The two artifacts damage different analyses asymmetrically. Duplicated `Open Case` events break control-flow analyses (DFG edges, variant counts, alignments, log skeletons) but barely touch durations, since they are near-instantaneous technical events; missing resources do the opposite - they leave control flow intact but destroy resource/handover analysis, workload attribution and any staffing-drift test, and because missingness is concentrated in migrated activities it is not ignorable.

Post-May patterns cannot be logging drift. Mapped fitness itself degrades (0.925 -> 0.86-0.88) *after* mapping; label count stays at 11 with no new activities; the mapped `Assess->RequestInfo` rate doubles from 12 to 29 per 100 cases; and cycle time and p90 rise steeply. Renaming events changes none of those.

### 3.2 Normalized DFG-distance analysis

**Measure.** Two complementary distances over the 7 supplied mapped relation rates r_e (occurrences per 100 cases), reference = 2026-01:

- d_L1(m) = sum_e |r_e(m) - r_e(ref)| - interpretable directly as "excess or missing edge executions per 100 cases".
- TV(m) = 0.5 * sum_e |p_e(m) - p_e(ref)| where p_e = r_e / sum_e r_e - a *shape* distance, insensitive to the overall number of edges per case.

**Assumptions.** (i) Omitted edges (`Decision->Close`, `Open Case` edges, start/end, self-loops) are treated as unchanged, so both numbers are **lower bounds** on true DFG distance. (ii) Rates are per-case frequencies, not a probability distribution, which is why raw L1 is reported alongside, not instead of, TV. (iii) Rates are aggregated to whole months, so within-month onsets (05-05, 06-15, 07-01, 09-10, 12-05) are diluted. (iv) Directly-follows counts are order-only and blind to duration, so this distance cannot see performance drift at all - by construction.

| edge | 03 | 05 | 07 | 09 | 10 | 12 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Register->Eligibility | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Eligibility->CollectDocs | 0 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 1 |
| CollectDocs->Assess | 1 | 4 | 6 | 3 | 5 | 2 |
| Assess->RequestInfo | 0 | 12 | 17 | 8 | 14 | 9 |
| RequestInfo->CollectDocs | 0 | 12 | 17 | 8 | 14 | 9 |
| Assess->SeniorAssessment | 1 | 3 | 5 | 3 | 18 | 10 |
| Assess/Senior->Decision | 1 | 7 | 11 | 4 | 10 | 5 |
| **d_L1 (per 100 cases)** | **3** | **40** | **59** | **28** | **64** | **36** |
| d_L1 / sum(ref) | 0.7% | 9.9% | 14.6% | 6.9% | 15.9% | 8.9% |
| **TV (shape)** | **0.004** | **0.063** | **0.089** | **0.044** | **0.102** | **0.063** |
| rework edges' share of d_L1 | - | 60% | 58% | 57% | 44% | 50% |
| senior edge's share of d_L1 | - | 8% | 8% | 11% | 28% | 28% |

Four results matter:

1. **March is DFG-invisible after mapping** (d_L1 = 3, TV = 0.004, within any plausible sampling noise for ~640 cases). Combined with raw fitness 0.55, this is the cleanest possible separation of semantic from behavioural drift.
2. **May and July distance is rework-dominated** (~58-60% of d_L1 on two edges that are the same loop counted twice).
3. **October is the largest distance but has a different composition**: the senior-assessment edge alone contributes 28%, an edge that was noise-level (4-5 per 100) all year.
4. **December's shape distance (0.063) is as large as May's (0.063)** even though rework has largely subsided - because the senior-assessment edge stays at 14 per 100 and contributes 28% of December's L1. The composition of the drift changed while its magnitude did not, and the process did not return to its January shape.

One caution that also weakens my own numbers: `Assess/Senior->Decision` falls from 83 to 72-73 per 100 exactly when congestion peaks. Part of that "drift" is almost certainly **right-censoring** - cases registered but not yet decided inside the extraction window - not a behavioural refusal to decide. The decision-edge contribution to d_L1 (7-11 points) should therefore be discounted, and I would recompute all distances on completed-case cohorts only.

### 3.3 Cycle-time decomposition

Target quantities: median +44.5% by August, +57.3% by November; p90 +61.8% and +77.9%. I decompose approximately additively on the log scale, ln(median_t/median_0), and report **ranges**, because monthly medians of a mixture are not linear in the mixture weights and I have no per-case data.

Anchors I can actually compute:

- **Service**: `Assess` median service 4.8 -> 5.4 h (+0.6 h, +12.5%). With ~1.29 assessments per case in July-August, direct service contribution is ~+0.8 h against a 31-45 h cycle: **~2-3 percentage points of the 44.5**, i.e. **4-8% of the deterioration**. Explicitly small; the supplied observation says so and the arithmetic agrees.
- **Assessment work demand** = cases x (1 + rework share) x service time: January 620 x 1.12 x 4.8 = ~3.33 k h; July 715 x 1.29 x 5.4 = ~4.98 k h (**+49%**); November 790 x 1.29 x 5.4 = ~5.50 k h (**+65%**, before the extra senior-assessment work of ~170-200 sessions/month). Capacity moved the other way from 2026-06-15. If the assessor pool was ~25-30 and 4 left, capacity fell ~13-16%, so the demand/capacity ratio rose by a factor ~1.49/0.86 ≈ **1.7**. In any G/G/c queue this is the region where waiting time is convex and explosive - moving utilization from ~0.75 to ~0.90+ multiplies expected wait several-fold while service time barely moves. That is precisely the observed pattern (service +12.5%, waiting "much more", p90/median 2.18 -> 2.47).
- **Case mix**: high-complexity share +8.5 pp (August) and +24.5 pp (November). If high-complexity cases are 1.3-1.6x longer, the direct mix effect on a mean is 8.5% x (0.3-0.6) ≈ 2.6-5.1% in August and 7-15% in November; the effect on the *median* is smaller still while high-complexity remains a minority.
- **Control-flow/rework composition**: rework share 11.5% -> 27-29%. Under the extreme assumption that every reworked case is slower than every non-reworked case, the median moves from the 56.5th to the ~70th percentile of the non-rework distribution; fitting a lognormal to (median 31.15, p90 68) gives sigma ≈ 0.61 and a pure-composition uplift of ~+26%. Realistic overlap between the two distributions cuts this substantially, so I treat ~10-20 percentage points as the credible composition band.

| component | August (+44.5% median) | November (+57.3% median) | basis / caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| case mix (complexity) | 5-12% of the deterioration | 15-25% | mix shift x assumed 1.3-1.6x duration ratio; ratio is assumed, not observed |
| control-flow / rework | 25-40% | 20-30% | +15.5 to +17.5 pp rework prevalence; loops per case unchanged, so this is prevalence-driven |
| service time | 4-8% | 3-6% | directly measured (4.8 -> 5.4 h) x ~1.3 executions; the only well-identified term |
| queueing / backlog | 40-60% | 40-55% | residual + convex utilization argument + rising p90/median + explicit statement that reconstructed waiting rose much more than service |
| interaction / unallocated (incl. priority redistribution, censoring) | 5-15% | 5-15% | priority rule shifts wait between classes with ~zero aggregate signature; censoring biases late months |

Ranges deliberately do not sum to exactly 100%: the components are **not orthogonal**. Supplier-driven rework adds ~200 extra assessments/month, which *raises utilization*, so part of the "queueing" share is a **mediated supplier effect**, and part of the "case mix" share operates *through* both rework and service time. Total supplier-attributable share of the May-August deterioration is therefore larger than its direct control-flow term - I would put it at 40-60% for that window, and near zero for October-November, where mix and volume take over with supplier Z back to 26-27%.

With only 12 monthly points, I would not trust any single test; I would run PELT/binary segmentation with a BIC-type penalty and CUSUM on **weekly** series per metric, and treat the above as pre-registered hypotheses rather than discoveries, to avoid searching 12 points x 12 metrics for the best-looking break.

### 3.5 One global change point or several?

**Several, mechanism-specific.** The evidence is that the metrics change at *different times with different shapes*:

- raw fitness: one step in March, flat afterwards (range 0.48-0.58) - a step function;
- mapped fitness and rework: no March movement, a step in May, a trough in July, partial recovery in September, second dip in October-November - correlated with each other, not with raw fitness;
- median and p90 cycle time: no May step of the same size, then monotone growth June-August with p90 outrunning the median - a ramp, not a step;
- case mix and volume: nothing until October, then a large jump - a seasonal pulse;
- senior-assessment rate: nothing until October, then a level shift that persists into December.

A single global change point would have to be placed somewhere in May-July and would (i) mis-date the logging break by 2-4 months, (ii) attribute the March fitness collapse to behaviour, (iii) attribute the October mix jump to the supplier, and (iv) hide the fact that recovery is *partial and dimension-specific*. Formally, a multiple-change-point model with metric-specific breakpoints will beat any shared-breakpoint model here, and the correct summary is: **one logging change point (March, plus a recording change in October), one behavioural change point (May, modified in July and again in October), one performance regime change (May onset, June-July steepening, October-November seasonal peak), and one population change point (October)** - with only the performance dimension showing a clear December reversal.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 - global, weekly | time (ISO week) | Change-point detection with enough temporal resolution to date mid-month triggers (05-05, 06-15, 07-01, 09-10, 12-05) | >= 100 completed cases/week; otherwise roll to biweekly | Onset dating, ramp vs step discrimination | Aggregation masks all mechanisms; usable only for timing, never for attribution |
| L1 - regime x logging convention | period (pre-migration, transition Mar-Apr, post-migration), label version (raw, mapped) | Isolate semantic drift and quarantine the mixed-convention window | Transition period analysed as two sub-logs; require >= 100 cases per convention per month | Raw vs mapped conformance; validity test of the mapping itself | Treating the transition as "post" imports old-convention cases and dilutes the step |
| L2 - supplier x complexity (monthly) | supplier (Z, other, unknown/missing), complexity (high, standard, unrecorded) | Core attribution stratum: separates supplier-driven rework from mix-driven rework | >= 150 cases/cell/month; pool to quarter if below; keep `unknown` as an explicit cell, never drop it | Z-conditional vs non-Z rework, within-stratum cycle time, reweighting decomposition | Complexity is itself drift-affected (October recording change), so conditioning on it can absorb real drift - use an independent complexity proxy as a robustness check |
| L3 - + priority class (post-2026-07 only) | priority class assigned by the rule | Test priority-induced redistribution of waiting | >= 75 cases/class/month; report waiting quantiles, not means | High vs standard waiting distributions, crossing test | Priority is assigned *because* of complexity, so it is a collider-adjacent variable; conditioning on it while also conditioning on complexity can induce spurious associations |
| L4 - + team / resource availability | team, assessor tenure cohort, resource-present vs resource-missing | Attribute queueing to capacity and test behavioural adaptation at the individual level | >= 30 cases per team-month for control-flow only; do not report per-resource timing below 30 events; use hierarchical shrinkage toward the team mean | Per-assessor behaviour over time, workload vs waiting, handover changes | 5-6% non-random resource missingness makes this the most bias-prone level; results are directional only |
| L5 - region / customer cohort | region, customer segment | Check whether the "supplier effect" is really a region effect | >= 100 cases/region/month; collapse small regions into an explicit "other" bucket | Supplier vs region deconfounding | Supplier Z is non-randomly assigned to regions, so region and supplier are partly non-identifiable; report the collinearity rather than a point estimate |

**Primary monitoring population: standard-complexity, non-Supplier-Z cases with a complete trace, evaluated weekly on mapped labels.** Rationale: this stratum is the closest thing to a control group in the data. It is minimally exposed to the two large exogenous shocks (supplier bundle quality, seasonal complexity mix), so movements in it cannot be explained away as "the mix changed" - which is exactly the objection that neutralizes every aggregate alarm in this case. Its volume is large and reasonably stable (roughly 55-70% of monthly cases even in the worst months), so weekly monitoring is statistically viable. And it is the stratum that *should* be protected: it is where priority-induced delay redistribution lands, and where a genuine internal process degradation would first become visible without a convenient external excuse.

Two mandatory companions, so this choice does not become blindness: (i) **Supplier Z and high-complexity strata are monitored as separate watch populations** with their own baselines, never pooled into the primary; (ii) **the full log is monitored for composition itself** (stratum weights), because if the primary stratum shrinks the process can degrade globally while the primary looks healthy.

## 7. Monitoring architecture

No universal threshold: thresholds are per-monitor, expressed relative to a monitor-appropriate reference window, and split into "investigate" and "escalate" bands.

| monitor | statistic/artifact | reference window | alert logic | diagnostic follow-up | likely false-positive source | owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Seasonality.** Volume, complexity mix and anything driven by them (cycle time, senior-assessment rate) are compared against a **seasonally matched reference** (same ISO weeks, prior years) or against a fitted seasonal baseline (STL decomposition, or a regression with month/week-of-year terms), and the alert is on the *residual*. Where no history exists - as here - the honest move is to widen the band for those specific monitors, label the alert "seasonality unverified", and start accumulating a seasonal baseline now. Label, schema, ordering and conformance monitors are *not* seasonally adjusted, since there is no legitimate reason for vocabulary to change with the calendar. Critically, monitors must distinguish **recurrence from new drift**: an October alarm that matches the prior-year seasonal profile in shape and amplitude is recurrence (act on capacity, not on diagnosis); one that exceeds the seasonal envelope, or whose per-edge/per-stratum composition differs from prior seasons, is new drift.

**Multiple testing.** With ~13 monitors x multiple strata x weekly cadence, uncorrected testing guarantees weekly false alarms. Controls: (i) a fixed, pre-registered monitor set - no fishing across strata; (ii) two-of-two consecutive-period persistence for all statistical monitors (this alone cuts the false-alarm rate roughly quadratically) plus a minimum effect size, so tiny-but-significant moves in high-volume strata do not page anyone; (iii) Benjamini-Hochberg FDR at 10% within each monitor family (labels, control flow, performance, population, resources, outcomes) rather than one global correction, so a family with a genuine break does not spend the whole budget; (iv) a hierarchy - global monitors gate stratified drill-downs, and stratified alerts are reported as diagnostics of a triggered global alert rather than as independent alarms; (v) monitor L0-L2 levels weekly, L3-L5 monthly, matching cadence to cell size.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

def validate(monthly, dfg, verbose=True):
    """Structural checks plus cross-validation against the raw DFG snippets."""
    problems, notes = [], []

    pct_cols = [
        "high_complexity_pct", "supplier_z_pct", "fast_variant_pct",
        "rework_variant_pct", "missing_resource_pct", "duplicate_open_case_pct",
    ]
    for col in pct_cols:
        bad = monthly.loc[~monthly[col].between(0, 100), "month"].tolist()
        if bad:
            problems.append(f"{col} out of [0,100] in {bad}")

    for col in ("raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness"):
        bad = monthly.loc[~monthly[col].between(0, 1), "month"].tolist()
        if bad:
            problems.append(f"{col} out of [0,1] in {bad}")

    bad = monthly.loc[monthly["mapped_model_fitness"] < monthly["raw_model_fitness"], "month"]
    if len(bad):
        problems.append(f"mapped < raw fitness in {bad.tolist()} (mapping should not hurt)")

    over = monthly.loc[monthly["fast_variant_pct"] + monthly["rework_variant_pct"] > 100, "month"]
    if len(over):
        problems.append(f"fast+rework variant share exceeds 100% in {over.tolist()}")

    if monthly["period"].diff().dropna().nunique() > 1:
        problems.append("months are not contiguous")

    bad = monthly.loc[monthly["p90_cycle_h"] <= monthly["median_cycle_h"], "month"]
    if len(bad):
        problems.append(f"p90 <= median in {bad.tolist()}")

    if not set(dfg["month"]).issubset(set(monthly["month"])):
        problems.append("DFG months not present in the monthly summary")

    # Cross-validation: normalized rates vs raw counts / cases.
    cases = monthly.set_index("month")["cases"]
    checks = [
        ("2026-01", "Assess->RequestInfo", 75),
        ("2026-05", "Assess->RequestInfo", 163),
        ("2026-07", "Assess->RequestInfo", 207),
        ("2026-10", "Assess->SeniorAssessment", 167),
    ]
    d = dfg.set_index("month")
    for month, edge, raw_count in checks:
        if month in d.index:
            implied = 100.0 * raw_count / cases[month]
            stated = float(d.loc[month, edge])
            ok = abs(implied - stated) <= 1.0
            notes.append(
                f"{month} {edge}: raw {raw_count}/{cases[month]} = {implied:.1f} per 100 "
                f"vs stated {stated:.0f} -> {'consistent' if ok else 'INCONSISTENT'}"
            )

    # March label rollout completeness (raw snippet).
    march_cases = cases["2026-03"]
    notes.append(
        f"2026-03 Register edges: 500 new label + 140 old label = {500 + 140} vs "
        f"{march_cases} cases -> rollout {100 * 500 / march_cases:.0f}% migrated"
    )
    notes.append(
        f"2026-03 Open Case->Register = 26 vs duplicate_open_case_pct 4% of "
        f"{march_cases} = {0.04 * march_cases:.0f} -> consistent"
    )

    # Loops per reworked case: rework prevalence vs Assess->RequestInfo rate.
    merged = dfg.merge(monthly[["month", "rework_variant_pct"]], on="month")
    merged["loops_per_reworked_case"] = (
        merged["Assess->RequestInfo"] / merged["rework_variant_pct"]
    )
    notes.append(
        "loops per reworked case (Assess->RequestInfo / rework share): "
        + ", ".join(
            f"{m}={v:.2f}" for m, v in
            zip(merged["month"], merged["loops_per_reworked_case"])
        )
        + "  -> ~1.0 everywhere: prevalence rose, loop depth did not"
    )
    notes.append(
        "RequestInfo->CollectDocs is ~1 per 100 cases below Assess->RequestInfo in "
        "every month -> a small stream stalls after Request Info"
    )

    if verbose:
        print("=" * 72)
        print("A1. VALIDATION")
        print("=" * 72)
        print("problems:", problems if problems else "none")
        for n in notes:
            print("  -", n)
    return problems, notes

RELATIVE_METRICS = ["cases", "median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h"]
ABSOLUTE_METRICS = [
    "high_complexity_pct", "supplier_z_pct", "fast_variant_pct",
    "rework_variant_pct", "missing_resource_pct", "duplicate_open_case_pct",
    "raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness",
]


def baseline_relative(monthly, baseline_months=BASELINE_MONTHS):
    base = monthly[monthly["month"].isin(baseline_months)]
    out = pd.DataFrame({"month": monthly["month"]})
    for col in RELATIVE_METRICS:
        b = base[col].mean()
        out[f"{col}_pct_vs_base"] = (100.0 * (monthly[col] - b) / b).round(1)
    for col in ABSOLUTE_METRICS:
        b = base[col].mean()
        out[f"{col}_delta_vs_base"] = (monthly[col] - b).round(3)
    out["p90_over_median"] = (monthly["p90_cycle_h"] / monthly["median_cycle_h"]).round(2)
    out["mom_median_pct"] = (100.0 * monthly["median_cycle_h"].pct_change()).round(1)
    out["mom_rework_pp"] = monthly["rework_variant_pct"].diff().round(1)
    out["mom_supplier_z_pp"] = monthly["supplier_z_pct"].diff().round(1)
    out["mom_high_cplx_pp"] = monthly["high_complexity_pct"].diff().round(1)
    # Assessment work demand proxy: cases x (1 + rework share) x Assess service time.
    service_h = np.where(monthly["period"] <= pd.Period("2026-04", "M"), 4.8, 5.4)
    demand = monthly["cases"] * (1 + monthly["rework_variant_pct"] / 100.0) * service_h
    out["assess_demand_h"] = demand.round(0)
    out["assess_demand_pct_vs_base"] = (
        100.0 * (demand - demand[monthly["month"].isin(baseline_months)].mean())
        / demand[monthly["month"].isin(baseline_months)].mean()
    ).round(1)
    return out

# A3. Transparent DFG distances
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

def dfg_distances(dfg, reference_month="2026-01", edges=EDGES):
    """Two explainable distances on the supplied per-100-cases relation rates.

    d_l1  : sum |rate_t - rate_ref|, in 'edge executions per 100 cases'.
    tv    : total variation distance after normalizing each month's edge vector
            to sum 1; a SHAPE distance, blind to edges-per-case volume.
    Both are LOWER BOUNDS because omitted edges are assumed unchanged.
    """
    ref = dfg.loc[dfg["month"] == reference_month, edges].iloc[0].astype(float)
    ref_p = ref / ref.sum()
    rows = []
    for _, r in dfg.iterrows():
        v = r[edges].astype(float)
        l1 = float(np.abs(v - ref).sum())
        p = v / v.sum()
        tv = float(0.5 * np.abs(p - ref_p).sum())
        contrib = {f"contrib_{e}": float(abs(v[e] - ref[e])) for e in edges}
        rework_share = (
            (contrib["contrib_Assess->RequestInfo"] + contrib["contrib_RequestInfo->CollectDocs"]) / l1
            if l1 > 0 else np.nan
        )
        senior_share = (
            contrib["contrib_Assess->SeniorAssessment"] / l1 if l1 > 0 else np.nan
        )
        rows.append(dict(
            month=r["month"], edge_mass=float(v.sum()),
            d_l1=round(l1, 1), d_l1_rel=round(l1 / float(ref.sum()), 4),
            tv=round(tv, 4),
            rework_share_of_l1=None if np.isnan(rework_share) else round(rework_share, 2),
            senior_share_of_l1=None if np.isnan(senior_share) else round(senior_share, 2),
            **{k: round(x, 1) for k, x in contrib.items()},
        ))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)


def pairwise_dfg_distance_matrix(dfg, edges=EDGES):
    months = dfg["month"].tolist()
    M = pd.DataFrame(0.0, index=months, columns=months)
    vals = {m: dfg.loc[dfg["month"] == m, edges].iloc[0].astype(float) for m in months}
    for a, b in itertools.combinations(months, 2):
        d = float(np.abs(vals[a] - vals[b]).sum())
        M.loc[a, b] = M.loc[b, a] = round(d, 1)
    return M

# A4. Raw-to-mapped fitness recovery
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

def fitness_recovery(monthly, baseline_months=BASELINE_MONTHS):
    base = monthly[monthly["month"].isin(baseline_months)]["mapped_model_fitness"].mean()
    raw, mapped = monthly["raw_model_fitness"], monthly["mapped_model_fitness"]
    out = pd.DataFrame({"month": monthly["month"]})
    out["raw"] = raw
    out["mapped"] = mapped
    out["gap_mapped_minus_raw"] = (mapped - raw).round(3)
    denom = (base - raw).replace(0, np.nan)
    # Share of the raw deficit (relative to the baseline mapped level) that the
    # label mapping + technical-event removal recovers. ~1.0 => pure logging drift.
    out["recovery_ratio"] = ((mapped - raw) / denom).round(3)
    # What the mapping CANNOT explain: residual mapped-fitness deficit.
    out["residual_mapped_deficit"] = (mapped - base).round(3)
    return out

# A5. Simple, explainable change-point flags
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

def flag_change_points(values, months, k=3.0, min_abs=0.0, min_history=3,
                       persistence=2, direction="both"):
    """Robust expanding-window rule: flag month t when |x_t - median(history)|
    exceeds max(k * 1.4826 * MAD(history), min_abs), for `persistence`
    consecutive months. Deliberately crude: 12 monthly points cannot support
    a model-based change-point test, and this rule is auditable by hand."""
    x = np.asarray(values, dtype=float)
    raw_flags = []
    for t in range(len(x)):
        if t < min_history:
            raw_flags.append(False)
            continue
        hist = x[:t]
        med = np.median(hist)
        mad = np.median(np.abs(hist - med))
        sigma = 1.4826 * mad
        thr = max(k * sigma, min_abs)
        dev = x[t] - med
        if direction == "up":
            raw_flags.append(dev > thr)
        elif direction == "down":
            raw_flags.append(-dev > thr)
        else:
            raw_flags.append(abs(dev) > thr)
    flags = []
    for t in range(len(raw_flags)):
        window = raw_flags[t:t + persistence]
        flags.append(bool(raw_flags[t] and len(window) == persistence and all(window))
                     or bool(raw_flags[t] and t + persistence > len(raw_flags)))
    return pd.DataFrame({"month": list(months), "value": x,
                         "flag_raw": raw_flags, "flag_persistent": flags})


CHANGE_POINT_SPEC = {
    # metric: (minimum effect size worth flagging, drift dimension)
    "raw_model_fitness":    (0.05, "logging/semantic"),
    "mapped_model_fitness": (0.02, "behavioral (post-mapping residual)"),
    "distinct_activity_labels": (1.0, "logging/schema"),
    "missing_resource_pct": (1.0, "measurement/missingness"),
    "duplicate_open_case_pct": (1.0, "logging/technical events"),
    "rework_variant_pct":   (3.0, "behavioral/control-flow"),
    "median_cycle_h":       (3.0, "performance"),
    "p90_cycle_h":          (8.0, "performance (tail/queueing)"),
    "high_complexity_pct":  (4.0, "population/case-mix"),
    "supplier_z_pct":       (5.0, "population (or supplier-field semantics)"),
    "cases":                (50.0, "load/volume"),
}


def all_change_points(monthly, spec=CHANGE_POINT_SPEC):
    rows = []
    for metric, (min_abs, dimension) in spec.items():
        res = flag_change_points(monthly[metric], monthly["month"], min_abs=min_abs)
        for _, r in res.iterrows():
            if r["flag_raw"]:
                rows.append(dict(
                    metric=metric, dimension=dimension, month=r["month"],
                    value=r["value"], persistent=bool(r["flag_persistent"]),
                    coincides_with=KNOWN_EVENTS.get(r["month"], "-"),
                ))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows).sort_values(["month", "metric"]).reset_index(drop=True)

CASE_ID, ACTIVITY, TIMESTAMP, RESOURCE = "case_id", "activity", "timestamp", "resource"

LABEL_MAPPING = {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}
TECHNICAL_ACTIVITIES = {"Open Case"}


def b1_format_log(df, case_id=CASE_ID, activity=ACTIVITY, timestamp=TIMESTAMP,
                  resource=RESOURCE):
    """Standardize an event-level dataframe for PM4Py without mutating the input."""
    import pm4py
    work = df.copy()
    work[timestamp] = pd.to_datetime(work[timestamp], utc=True, errors="coerce")
    if work[timestamp].isna().any():
        raise ValueError("unparseable timestamps: fix extraction before mining")
    work = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        work, case_id=case_id, activity_key=activity, timestamp_key=timestamp
    )
    if resource in work.columns:
        work["org:resource"] = work[resource]
    # Stable intra-case order; ties are a known migration artifact (monitor M3).
    work = work.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"], kind="mergesort")
    return work.reset_index(drop=True)


def b7_normalized_view(log, mapping=None, drop_activities=None):
    """Return a NEW log with the eligibility label mapped and technical events
    removed. The original log object is never overwritten, so raw and normalized
    conformance can be compared side by side (see b8_raw_vs_normalized)."""
    mapping = LABEL_MAPPING if mapping is None else mapping
    drop_activities = TECHNICAL_ACTIVITIES if drop_activities is None else drop_activities
    out = log.copy()
    out["concept:name:raw"] = out["concept:name"]
    out["concept:name"] = out["concept:name"].replace(mapping)
    out = out[~out["concept:name"].isin(drop_activities)].copy()
    # Also collapse exact consecutive duplicates within a case (technical repeats).
    same_case = out["case:concept:name"].eq(out["case:concept:name"].shift())
    same_act = out["concept:name"].eq(out["concept:name"].shift())
    zero_gap = (
        out["time:timestamp"] - out["time:timestamp"].shift()
    ).dt.total_seconds().fillna(1e9).abs() < 1.0
    return out[~(same_case & same_act & zero_gap)].reset_index(drop=True)


def b2_slice_window(log, start, end, mode="traces_contained"):
    """mode='traces_contained' avoids left/right truncation bias; use
    'traces_intersecting' to study in-flight backlog, and 'events' only for
    event-level counts, never for cycle times."""
    import pm4py
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(log, str(start), str(end), mode=mode)


def month_windows(log, freq="MS"):
    periods = pd.period_range(
        log["time:timestamp"].min(), log["time:timestamp"].max(), freq="M"
    )
    return [(p.start_time, p.end_time) for p in periods]


def b3_monthly_dfgs(log, mode="traces_contained"):
    """{month: (dfg, start_activities, end_activities)} plus per-100-case rates."""
    import pm4py
    result = {}
    for start, end in month_windows(log):
        window = b2_slice_window(log, start, end, mode=mode)
        if window.empty:
            continue
        n_cases = window["case:concept:name"].nunique()
        dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_dfg(window)
        rates = {edge: 100.0 * cnt / n_cases for edge, cnt in dfg.items()}
        result[str(start.to_period("M"))] = dict(
            dfg=dfg, start_activities=sa, end_activities=ea,
            n_cases=n_cases, rates_per_100_cases=rates,
        )
    return result


def dfg_rate_distance(rates_a, rates_b):
    """Same L1 / TV pair as Part A, but over the FULL edge set, so it is no
    longer a lower bound. Missing edges count as rate 0."""
    keys = set(rates_a) | set(rates_b)
    a = np.array([rates_a.get(k, 0.0) for k in keys], dtype=float)
    b = np.array([rates_b.get(k, 0.0) for k in keys], dtype=float)
    l1 = float(np.abs(a - b).sum())
    pa = a / a.sum() if a.sum() else a
    pb = b / b.sum() if b.sum() else b
    per_edge = sorted(
        ((k, abs(x - y)) for k, x, y in zip(keys, a, b)),
        key=lambda t: -t[1],
    )
    return dict(d_l1=l1, tv=float(0.5 * np.abs(pa - pb).sum()),
                top_contributors=per_edge[:15])


def b4_monthly_variants(log, top_n=20):
    import pm4py
    out = {}
    for start, end in month_windows(log):
        window = b2_slice_window(log, start, end)
        if window.empty:
            continue
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(window)
        sizes = {k: (len(v) if hasattr(v, "__len__") else int(v))
                 for k, v in variants.items()}
        total = sum(sizes.values()) or 1
        ranked = sorted(sizes.items(), key=lambda kv: -kv[1])
        out[str(start.to_period("M"))] = dict(
            n_variants=len(sizes),
            top=[(k, v, round(100.0 * v / total, 2)) for k, v in ranked[:top_n]],
            coverage_top_n=round(100.0 * sum(v for _, v in ranked[:top_n]) / total, 2),
            entropy=round(float(-sum((v / total) * np.log(v / total)
                                     for _, v in ranked if v > 0)), 3),
        )
    return out


def b5_temporal_profile(reference_log, test_logs, zeta=6.0):
    """Learn the profile ONLY on a validated reference window, then score later
    windows. Re-estimating per month would normalize drift away (monitor M6)."""
    import pm4py
    profile = pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(reference_log)
    results = {}
    for name, lg in test_logs.items():
        diagnostics = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_temporal_profile(
            lg, profile, zeta=zeta
        )
        n = len(diagnostics) if diagnostics is not None else 0
        deviating = sum(1 for d in diagnostics if d) if n else 0
        results[name] = dict(
            n_cases=n, deviating_cases=deviating,
            deviating_share=round(deviating / n, 4) if n else None,
        )
    return profile, results


def b6_declarative_models(log, noise_threshold=0.05, with_declare=True):
    """Log skeleton / DECLARE constraint sets. Comparing constraint SETS across
    periods is often a cleaner behavioural-drift signal than DFG counts, because
    it is insensitive to frequency changes driven purely by case mix."""
    import pm4py
    models = {"log_skeleton": pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(
        log, noise_threshold=noise_threshold)}
    if with_declare:
        try:
            models["declare"] = pm4py.discover_declare(log)
        except Exception as exc:  # older PM4Py versions
            models["declare"] = f"unavailable: {exc}"
    return models


def b8_raw_vs_normalized(log, reference_log_normalized=None):
    """Dual-track conformance: same cases, two label conventions, one model.
    recovery_ratio near 1 => logging/semantic drift; a residual drop in the
    normalized track => behaviour the mapping cannot explain."""
    import pm4py
    raw = log                       # untouched
    norm = b7_normalized_view(log)  # new object
    base = reference_log_normalized if reference_log_normalized is not None else norm
    net, im, fm = pm4py.discover_petri_net_inductive(base, noise_threshold=0.2)
    fit_raw = pm4py.fitness_alignments(raw, net, im, fm)
    fit_norm = pm4py.fitness_alignments(norm, net, im, fm)
    prec_norm = pm4py.precision_alignments(norm, net, im, fm)
    r, m = fit_raw["log_fitness"], fit_norm["log_fitness"]
    return dict(
        raw_fitness=r, normalized_fitness=m, normalized_precision=prec_norm,
        gap=m - r,
        recovery_ratio=(m - r) / (1 - r) if r < 1 else None,
        raw_labels=sorted(raw["concept:name"].unique().tolist()),
        normalized_labels=sorted(norm["concept:name"].unique().tolist()),
    )


def b9_waiting_vs_service(log, activity="Assess", start_suffix=None):
    """Split the pre-Assess interval into queue waiting and service. With only
    completion timestamps, 'waiting' is the gap from the previous event, which
    over-attributes idle time to the queue - state this whenever quoting it."""
    lg = log.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])
    lg = lg.assign(prev_ts=lg.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].shift())
    target = lg[lg["concept:name"] == activity].copy()
    target["gap_h"] = (
        target["time:timestamp"] - target["prev_ts"]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0
    target["month"] = target["time:timestamp"].dt.to_period("M").astype(str)
    return target.groupby("month")["gap_h"].describe(
        percentiles=[0.5, 0.9]
    ).round(2)


def b10_stratified_drift(log, strata=("supplier", "complexity", "priority_class")):
    """Per-stratum monthly DFG rates + variant mix, so the reweighting test can
    be run: if within-stratum rates are stable and only weights move, the drift
    is POPULATION drift, not behavioural drift."""
    out = {}
    for var in strata:
        if var not in log.columns:
            out[var] = "attribute absent from log"
            continue
        out[var] = {}
        for value, sub in log.groupby(var, dropna=False):
            if sub["case:concept:name"].nunique() < 150:
                out[var][str(value)] = "below minimum support (150 cases)"
                continue
            out[var][str(value)] = b3_monthly_dfgs(sub)
    return out

## 9. Provisional attribution

**Strongest conclusions.** The March conformance collapse is a semantic and provenance event, not a process change: the mapping recovers 93-96% of the deficit, the mapped DFG distance is 3 per 100 cases, the label count rises by exactly the two documented artifacts, and cycle time and rework are flat (~95% confidence). Rework prevalence genuinely doubled from May and is tightly linked to Supplier Z: onset within a month of 2026-05-05, co-movement, and recession as the Z share recedes (~85-90%; but the *tripling of the Z share itself* is unexplained by the timeline and may be a field-definition change, ~50%). The performance deterioration is dominated by queueing, not work content: service time rose 12.5% while assessment demand rose ~49% against reduced capacity, and p90/median climbed 2.18 to 2.47 (~85%). October-November is population-driven: complexity nearly doubled and volume rose 24-29%, on a system that never recovered capacity.

**Plausible only.** That the priority rule redistributed delay from high-complexity to standard cases (~45%; documented policy, unmeasured effect, and its signature is aggregate invisibility). That the October senior-assessment surge is partly a recording change (~60%, on the senior/complexity ratio jumping 0.27 to 0.54). That behavioural adaptation persisted after supplier recovery (~55%, on 2-3 pp residuals). That December's improvement was caused by the interventions rather than seasonal relief and censoring (~40%).

**Where attribution cannot be clean.** Supplier-driven rework *causes* extra assessments, so it acts partly through the queue: "supplier" and "staffing" shares overlap by construction, and no aggregate decomposition separates a mediator from a co-cause. Complexity, supplier and region are mutually confounded, since Z serves a non-random population that was already more complex. The complexity flag is simultaneously a stratifier and a drift-affected variable. December confounds three things at once.

**What would revise this.** Per-stratum rates showing the non-Z rework rate rising in May would move me from supplier-specific to general behavioural drift. Priority-class waiting distributions that fail to cross would kill hypothesis 4. Finding the decision-rate decline persists on completion cohorts would upgrade a censoring artifact into real behaviour. Prior-year October data diverging in shape would turn recurrence into new drift. Evidence that `supplier_z` is a derived flag whose population rule changed on 2026-05-05 would undercut the strongest quantitative link in this account.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules
