## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The raw-versus-mapped fitness gap is strong evidence of a label/provenance event, not proof of a real process collapse. In March, raw fitness falls from about 0.92 to 0.55, but after mapping `Eligibility Screen` to `Validate Eligibility` and removing duplicated `Open Case` events, mapped fitness is about 0.90. Most of the raw non-conformance was therefore an artifact of renaming and a technical duplicate event. This does not imply the business process was unchanged. Mapping reconstructs only syntactic equivalence. If the new eligibility screen performs different checks than the old `Validate Eligibility` step, the mapped model will hide a genuine semantic change. Similarly, removing duplicate `Open Case` events is justified only when they are confirmed technical duplicates; if some represent true case reopenings, deleting them erases real behavior.

Duplicated technical events distort control-flow and variant counts, but they do not fully explain cycle-time changes. Missing resource identifiers do not change event order or durations, but they bias queue/workload analyses and resource-based alignments. The post-May changes cannot be dismissed as logging drift: `Assess -> Request Info` rates double, rework variants double, cycle time increases, and supplier Z share jumps. None of these are produced by the March label rename. Even after mapping and deduplication, these patterns remain, and they align with supplier disruption, staffing cuts, the priority rule, and seasonality. Thus the raw gap identifies a provenance problem, while mapped and other metrics identify subsequent real operational drift.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 All mapped cases | mapped labels, deduplicated events, month | overall operational health | All cases | time, intervention effects | mixes all mechanisms |
| L1 Time window | week/month, season, intervention flag | detect sudden, gradual, and seasonal change | 30 cases per week; otherwise aggregate to month | before/after events, seasonal baseline | multiple testing and small weekly cells |
| L2 Migration domain | pre/post migration, label mapping, duplicate technical events | separate semantic/provenance drift from real process drift | 30 cases per stratum | raw vs mapped fitness, missingness, duplicate rates | stratum assignment depends on label mapping |
| L3 Supplier | supplier Z vs other, region/product | isolate supplier-driven document quality | 50 cases per group per month; else aggregate to quarter | rework, cycle time, `Request Info` by supplier | supplier Z is nonrandom and correlated with complexity |
| L4 Complexity | independent complexity score from intake, not `Senior Assessment` outcome | isolate case-mix drift | 50 cases per complexity stratum | high vs standard behavior, Senior Assessment rate | historical `Senior Assessment` labels are inconsistent |
| L5 Priority class | priority class, complexity, queue entry time | detect priority-induced delay redistribution | 50 cases per class per week; else use two-week rolling window | high vs standard wait, p90 | priority assignment is endogenous to complexity |
| L6 Resource/team | assessor/team, workday, missing resource flag | detect staffing and workload effects | 20 cases per team per month; pool small teams | service time, workload, queue length | missing resource identifiers are nonrandom |
| L7 Sentinel | standard complexity, non-supplier-Z, complete resource, no duplicate events | detect core process drift with fewer confounders | 100 cases per month; if less, relax to non-supplier-Z only | core behavior over time | filtering on completeness may bias if missingness is caused by process failure |

The primary monitoring population should be the sentinel segment: standard-complexity, non-supplier-Z, resource-complete cases. It removes the two largest known population confounders, supplier disruption and seasonal high-complexity mix, allowing the monitoring system to detect whether the core business process itself is changing. All-cases summaries remain necessary for operational oversight, but they should not be the primary drift-detection population.

## 7. Monitoring architecture

| monitor | statistic/artifact | reference window | alert logic | diagnostic follow-up | likely false-positive source | owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Label/schema monitor | distinct activity labels, label mapping mismatch rate, `Eligibility Screen` proportion | baseline mapping table and pre-migration months | alert if any new unmapped label appears or raw-mapped fitness gap exceeds 0.15 | inspect schema change; update semantic mapping if justified | rare labels; system version rollouts | Data Engineering |
| Event-order and technical duplicates | duplicate `Open Case` rate, log skeleton violations, alignment cost | Jan-Feb baseline plus 13-week moving baseline | alert if duplicate rate exceeds Poisson upper bound for two consecutive weeks | determine whether duplicates are technical or real reopenings; adjust event generation | true reopenings misclassified as duplicates | Data Engineering |
| DFG distance | Euclidean distance between normalized monthly DFG fragments | 13-week moving median after seasonal adjustment | alert if distance exceeds 95th percentile of in-control history | decompose contributing edge changes; inspect new variants | seasonal mix; omitted edges | Process Mining |
| Variant distribution | top-5 variant share, Gini coefficient, number of variants per 100 cases | rolling annual baseline | alert if top-5 share shifts more than 5 points or Gini exceeds 2 | review new variants and their causes | transient user behavior; small sample | Process Excellence |
| Rework monitor | Request Info loops per case, rework variant pct, loop duration | same month prior year plus 13-week robust baseline | alert if 4-week moving average exceeds expected seasonal level by 20% | identify document types and suppliers driving loops | annual renewal; coding changes | Quality/Operations |
| Temporal profile | median and 90th percentile of activity-pair durations, especially before `Assess` | 13-week robust baseline | alert if median duration deviates by more than 2 or 20% | locate queueing vs service-time drivers | holidays; weekend effects | Operations |
| Queue-wait monitor | reconstructed waiting time before `Assess` by priority class | pre-priority Jan-Jun baseline; rolling seasonal baseline | alert if standard-class wait rises more than 25% or high-class wait falls sharply | review priority weights and staff allocation | class assignment errors; case-mix changes | Operations |
| Service-time monitor | median service time for `Assess` and `Senior Assessment` by resource/team | 90-day rolling baseline | alert if median service time shifts more than 2 or 20% | check assessor workload, system slowness, training | small team samples; complexity mix | Team Leads |
| Case-mix monitor | high-complexity pct, supplier Z pct, region/product share | rolling annual proportions | alert if any proportion shifts more than 5 points or 2 | reweight analyses; update sampling plans | coding changes in complexity fields | Business Analytics |
| Resource-completeness monitor | missing_resource_pct by activity/team, duplicate Open Case pct | baseline plus acceptable missing threshold | alert if missingness increases more than 1.5 points for two months | trace logging pipeline; perform sensitivity analysis | incomplete exports; archival cases | Data Engineering |
| Conformance monitor | mapped model fitness, alignments, raw-mapped gap | baseline mapped fitness 0.90 | alert if mapped fitness falls below 0.85 or raw-mapped gap exceeds 0.15 | validate label mapping; if real drift exists, assess process redesign | seasonal variants not in normative model | Process Excellence |
| Outcome monitor | approve/reject rates, final decision rate, appeals/complaints | rolling 12-month baseline | alert if outcome proportion shifts more than 2 or 5 points | quality audit; compare to manual review | seasonal policy changes | Quality |

No single universal threshold should be used. Each monitor should use its own in-control reference distribution, seasonal adjustment, and control limits. Seasonality can be handled with same-month prior-year baselines, deseasonalized series, or seasonal factors. For multiple testing, apply a false-discovery-rate adjustment or require that at least two monitors from independent families exceed their control limits before raising a formal alarm.

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest conclusion is that the March raw-fitness collapse is primarily logging/semantic drift. The raw-mapped gap is about 0.35 in March, while mapped fitness remains about 0.90, and the median cycle time is essentially unchanged. I assign this 85–90% confidence. The label mapping and duplicate-event removal recover most, but not all, conformance. This does not rule out a real semantic change in the eligibility step; it means the observed non-conformance is mostly an artifact.

Supplier-driven rework is highly plausible but not fully proven. I assign roughly 70–75% confidence. May 5 aligns exactly with the supplier Z share tripling, rework doubling, and `Assess -> RequestInfo` doubling. September recovery aligns with lower supplier share, lower rework, and shorter cycle times. However, supplier Z cases were already more complex and are nonrandom, so part of the effect is case-mix mediation.

Staffing-driven queueing and priority-induced delay redistribution are plausible but confounded. I assign about 55–65% confidence. Service time remained nearly flat while reconstructed queue waiting increased, pointing to queueing. But the July priority rule begins only three weeks after the staffing cuts, and monthly aggregates cannot separate the two. It is likely that both contributed, with standard cases bearing more delay.

The October-November seasonal population drift is plausible at about 70% confidence. Volume, high-complexity share, Senior Assessment rate, and p90 all rose together. This is expected during renewal season. It is not a new mechanism, but it worsens an already strained system.

Persistent behavioral adaptation is the weakest claim, about 35–45% confidence. September remains above baseline even after supplier recovery, but the staffing deficit, backlog, and seasonal load are still present. There is no direct observation of assessor behavior changes.

Interactions prevent clean attribution. A global change point is not supported by the data. I would revise the account if event-level data became available. If supplier Z cases do not show higher rework rates after controlling for complexity, the supplier hypothesis would be weakened. If mapped fitness also collapses after a validated label mapping, then the migration included a true process change. If standard-case waiting does not increase after the priority rule, the priority redistribution is less harmful. If missingness is correlated with performance, the segmentation must be adjusted. The December improvements are encouraging but cannot yet be separated from lower seasonal load.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw-model fitness drops by more than 0.10 while mapped-model fitness remains within 0.03 of baseline and cycle time is stable, THEN audit label mapping and duplicate-event handling before treating the process as nonconforming, BECAUSE semantic/logging drift creates apparent control-flow violations.

2. IF mapped-model fitness also declines while rework loops and variant counts increase, THEN execute event-level conformance and variant analysis, BECAUSE label mapping alone cannot explain added loops or new variants.

3. IF supplier Z share and rework rates both rise and later fall after supplier recovery, THEN prioritize supplier document quality and recheck incoming bundles, BECAUSE supplier disruption is a plausible generator of the rework loop.

4. IF reconstructed queue wait before `Assess` increases while `Assess` service time barely changes after a staffing reduction, THEN adjust staffing and queue capacity rather than blaming assessor speed, BECAUSE a capacity shortage shows as queueing, not longer service.

5. IF high-complexity cases experience shorter waits and standard cases longer waits after a priority rule, THEN keep the priority rule only with standard-case SLA guardrails, BECAUSE priority redistributes delay rather than adding or reducing total delay.

6. IF October-November volume and high-complexity share rise and the Senior Assessment rate jumps, THEN compare to prior-year seasonal baseline and avoid fixed thresholds from pre-renewal months, BECAUSE seasonal case mix explains periodic changes.

7. IF missing resource identifiers and duplicated technical events are concentrated in migrated activities, THEN preprocess/annotate the log and re-run all segmentations with and without affected cases, BECAUSE nonrandom missingness biases resource and queue attributions.
