

# 1. Disruption timeline

**T0 (Baseline, Day -30 to -1):**  
Operational state was stable with high conformity (fitness 0.96). Cycle times were optimized (Hospital-critical median 8h) and compliance exceptions were minimal (traceability 0.8%, cold-chain 0.4%, controlled-substance dual-control 0.2%). The process followed the normative flow with few deviations.

**T1-T2 (Incident & Manual Workaround, Day 0 to 2):**  
WMS became unavailable. Teams adopted high-variability variants (fitness 0.71; 44% traces fit baseline). Throughput was preserved for Hospital-critical orders (91% fill rate) by triaging them early (Adaptation A). However, this phase introduced significant risk: traceability exceptions spiked to 9.5%, and cold-chain/controlled-substance documentation gaps grew (4.8% and 2.9% respectively). Cycle times doubled for non-critical segments (Pharmacy-standard median 54h).

**T3 (Partial Recovery, Day 3 to 6):**  
Order intake returned but synchronization was unstable. Fitness improved (0.82), yet traceability exceptions remained high at 5.4%. Rework rates were 9.0%, indicating downstream correction was required. The system was partially online, leading to hybrid manual/automated flows.

**T4 (Stabilization, Day 7 to 21):**  
Systems mostly returned, but operational recovery masked compliance debt. While cycle times nearly normalized, an audit-remediation queue persisted (2,300 cases). Fitness reached 0.90, but the "after-the-fact" compliance tail (Invoice close -> Audit remediation) created hidden workload not present in baseline metrics.

# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

True resilience preserves the mission (hospital supply) while maintaining or substituting critical controls. Patterns **A**, **B**, **F**, **I**, and **J** qualify. For example, **A** protected hospitals despite skipping standard authorization, but was paired with **B** (manual check) to validate intent. **I** sacrificed pharmacy volume to protect hospital capacity, aligning with risk appetite.

Concealed noncompliance preserves throughput or speed by dropping controls without immediate documentation, creating hidden risk. Patterns **C** and **G** are clear examples. **C** (skip authorization) assumed standing approval without verification, creating a legal gap. **G** (dispatch without scan) removed the handoff audit trail entirely, relying on later reconstruction which had high variance (move-on-log deviations).

Ambiguous adaptations depend on execution. **D** (snapshot check) was resilient if snapshots were fresh, but risky as time passed. **L** (audit remediation) appears corrective but conceals noncompliance by moving it to a post-operation queue (T4), violating the principle that compliance evidence should exist at the time of dispatch. The baseline had 0.8% traceability exceptions; T2 had 9.5%. While some gaps were fixed in T4, the 2,300 cases in the audit queue show that compliance was *deferred*, not *maintained*.

# 4. Recovery process model

We propose a structured **Emergency Response Process (ERP)** that activates automatically during disruptions. This model prioritizes traceability over speed for high-risk categories.

**Trigger Conditions**
*   WMS unavailable > 15 minutes.
*   Critical temperature alarm (cold-chain) active.
*   Controlled-substance dual-control system failure.

**Triage Rules (Pre-Pick)**
1.  **Classify:** Hospital-critical (Life-saving) vs. Pharmacy-standard vs. Wholesale-bulk.
2.  **Authorization:** Offline authorization lists (Pattern B) required for Hospital/Controlled. Skip (Pattern C) is prohibited.
3.  **Inventory:** Use cached inventory view refreshed every 30 minutes (modified Pattern D).

**Minimum Controls by Risk**
*   **Controlled-substance:** Manual dual-signature log must be completed physically. Scan event recorded on offline device.
*   **Cold-chain:** Temperature logger must be physically attached and photographed. Manual time-stamp entry required.
*   **Ambient:** Offline pick sheet with handwritten timestamps.

**Manual Evidence Capture**
*   Offline scanner app or paper log with unique case ID.
*   All scans/events must be synchronized immediately when connectivity returns (K).
*   Proof-of-delivery: Digital signature preferred; phone (H) only for low-risk, non-controlled orders.

**Backlog Prioritization (Post-Recovery)**
*   Queue items by Customer Criticality  Product Risk  Age of Backlog (Pattern J).
*   Prioritize Hospital-critical and Controlled-substance backlogs first.

**Reconciliation and Audit Flow**
*   **Automated Sync:** Upon system return, auto-match offline logs to digital orders.
*   **Exception Queue:** Any offline case without match goes to Audit Remediation (L) *before* invoice close.
*   **Audit Closure:** No order moves to "Invoice Close" without evidence of traceability restoration.

# 5. Resilience indicators

**Leading Indicators (During Incident)**
*   **Offline Control Adherence:** % of controlled/cold-chain orders with manual dual-control/temp logs completed.
*   **Snapshot Age:** Average time since last inventory refresh (target < 30 mins).
*   **Capacity Utilization:** % of physical throughput dedicated to Hospital-critical orders (target > 80% of capacity).
*   **Deviation Rate:** % of orders requiring manual overrides (track against baseline).

**Lagging Indicators (Post-Incident)**
*   **Traceability Exception Rate:** % of orders with missing scans/logs at case closure (target < 1% of manual flow).
*   **Compliance Debt:** Number of cases in Audit Remediation queue post-stabilization (target < 50 cases per week).
*   **Fairness Ratio:** Fill rate (Hospital) / Fill rate (Pharmacy) (target  1.5).
*   **Rework Rate:** % of manual orders needing correction post-synchronization (target < 5%).

# 6. Recommendations for future disruptions

1.  **Implement Offline "Shadow WMS":** Deploy a lightweight, offline-capable interface on tablets for triage and logging (High Effort, High Benefit, Low Risk). This eliminates Pattern E & G deviations by capturing structured data immediately.
2.  **Automate Authorization Lists:** Pre-validate hospital customers on static offline lists that sync daily (Medium Effort, High Benefit, Medium Risk). This supports Pattern A/B safely.
3.  **Establish "Hard Stop" Controls:** Enforce system constraints that block dispatch for Controlled/Cold-chain orders without evidence capture (Low Effort, High Benefit, Low Risk). Prevents Pattern C/G entirely.
4.  **Audit Debt Budget:** Allocate dedicated staff during T4 to clear audit queues within 48 hours (Low Effort, Medium Benefit, Low Risk). Reduces hidden workload from Pattern L.
5.  **Communicate Triage Policy:** Formalize and communicate deferment rules for non-critical orders (Low Effort, Medium Benefit, Medium Risk). Mitigates dissatisfaction from Pattern I.

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should accept slower cycle times and higher operational costs during disruptions, as seen in T2 where Pharmacy fill rates dropped to 61% to protect Hospital supply. However, management must not accept loss of traceability for Controlled-substances and Cold-chain products, even if it reduces throughput. The evidence shows that deferred audit remediation (T4) is not equivalent to real-time compliance; the 2,300 remediation cases represent debt that could result in regulatory penalties. If regulators permit alternative verification for specific non-life-saving items, the policy can evolve; otherwise, manual controls must remain rigorous to avoid concealed noncompliance.
