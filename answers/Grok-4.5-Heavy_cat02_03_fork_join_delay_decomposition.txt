# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

Let \(c\) index cases and \(i \in \{A,B,C\}\).

- \(\mathrm{upstream\_elapsed}(c) = \mathrm{fork\_release}(c) - \mathrm{arrival}(c)\)
- \(\mathrm{queue}_i(c) = \mathrm{start}_i(c) - \mathrm{fork\_release}(c)\)
- \(\mathrm{service}_i(c) = \mathrm{end}_i(c) - \mathrm{start}_i(c)\)
- \(\mathrm{join\_ready}(c) = \max_i \mathrm{end}_i(c)\)
- \(\mathrm{sync\_wait}_i(c) = \mathrm{join\_ready}(c) - \mathrm{end}_i(c)\)
- \(\mathrm{fork\_join\_elapsed}(c) = \mathrm{join\_ready}(c) - \mathrm{fork\_release}(c) = \max_i\bigl(\mathrm{queue}_i(c)+\mathrm{service}_i(c)\bigr)\)
- \(\mathrm{downstream\_queue}(c) = \mathrm{final\_start}(c) - \mathrm{join\_ready}(c)\)
- \(\mathrm{final\_service}(c) = \mathrm{final\_end}(c) - \mathrm{final\_start}(c)\)
- \(\mathrm{total\_flow\_time}(c) = \mathrm{final\_end}(c) - \mathrm{arrival}(c)\)  
  \(= \mathrm{upstream\_elapsed}(c) + \mathrm{fork\_join\_elapsed}(c) + \mathrm{downstream\_queue}(c) + \mathrm{final\_service}(c)\)

Critical branch(s) of \(c\): \(\arg\max_i \mathrm{end}_i(c)\). Ties (none observed) are reported as the alphabetically sorted list of all maximizers.

**Why summing synchronization waits double-counts.**  
\[
\sum_i\mathrm{sync\_wait}_i(c) = 3\cdot\mathrm{join\_ready}(c) - \sum_i\mathrm{end}_i(c).
\]
The calendar interval from the earliest branch completion to \(\mathrm{join\_ready}\) is counted once for every non-critical branch. That interval is concurrent, not sequential; only the critical-path length \(\max_i(\mathrm{queue}_i+\mathrm{service}_i)\) advances case elapsed time. Summing therefore invents phantom time that never appears on the case clock.

**Assumption (labelled).** Timestamps are complete-event and accurate to one minute; \(\mathrm{join\_ready}\) is inferred solely from the stated rule that \(F\) may start only after all three branches finish.

**Influence ranking (reasoned).**  
B is unambiguously the most influential branch: highest critical-path frequency, highest mean and when-critical (q+s), and sole driver of the observed tails (75 min fork-join on F07/F12). A ranks second: its fixed 50 min service is consistently large, yet it is critical only when both B and C finish earlier; the two A-critical cases still yield modest savings under acceleration. C ranks third: lowest mean (q+s), high average synchronization wait (usually finishes first), and only occasional queue spikes that make it critical. Frequency of A and C is identical, but the consistent magnitude of A’s contribution places it above C. No numerical ties appear in the ranking.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

**Assumption (labelled).** Branch counterfactuals recompute only \(\mathrm{join\_ready}\) and keep observed downstream_queue fixed. This is optimistic: several final-resource 0-gaps exist (F1: F03→F05, F07→F09→F11; F2: F06→F08), so an earlier join may be partially masked by residual occupation of F1/F2. Secondary congestion relief is therefore not quantified.

**CF1 – reduce A service by 20 % (A_s = 40).**  
New join = max(40, B_qs, C_qs).  
- Direct benefit only on original A-critical cases: F01 saves 5 (B takes over), F11 saves 10.  
- Zero benefit on the other 10 cases (B or C remains critical).  
- Aggregate mean join (hence total-flow) saving = 1.25 min.  
- Secondary: A becomes less likely to be critical; any residual A-queue pressure disappears, but B’s dominance is unchanged.

**CF2 – reduce B queue by 50 %.**  
New B_qs' = 0.5·B_q + B_s; new join = max(A_qs=50, B_qs', C_qs).  
- Benefits all 8 original B-critical cases: savings 5–15 min (F02/F03/F05:10; F06/F07/F12:15; F09:12.5; F10:5 after switch to A).  
- Zero benefit on the 4 A/C-critical cases.  
- Aggregate mean saving = 7.71 min (system-wide sum 92.5).  
- Secondary: A becomes critical more often (F02,F05,F10); tails shrink; earlier arrivals at F may tighten downstream queues (unmodelled).

**CF3 – reduce downstream queue by 50 % (proxy for added final-station capacity).**  
Join unchanged; every case saves exactly half its observed downstream_queue.  
- Direct benefit on all 12 cases; no zeros.  
- Mean saving = 7.33 min.  
- Bound (ideal zero downstream): mean 14.67.  
- Secondary: lower F occupancy reduces masking of future branch improvements; possible idle capacity if over-provisioned.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

**Redesign 1 – Target B queue (additional stress-test capacity or improved scheduling).**  
Expected throughput gain via shorter critical-path cases; flow-time tail reduction (especially the 75 min fork-join instances); B resource utilization expected to fall while A utilization may rise.  
New bottleneck risk: A (now more frequently critical) or residual F congestion if more cases reach join earlier. Synchronization effect: mean B-sync rises slightly, C-sync falls.  
Pilot first: instrument B resource count, utilization and queue-cause logs; measure post-change critical-branch frequencies and tail quantiles of total flow.

**Redesign 2 – Increase final-station capacity (third resource or dynamic F1/F2 load balancing).**  
Expected mean flow-time cut ≈7 min (or up to 15 under ideal), uniform across cases, reduced masking of branch improvements, higher sustainable throughput.  
Risk: under-utilization if arrival rate to F does not justify the extra resource; possible new idle-time policy effects. Synchronization unchanged.  
Pilot first: quantify current F1/F2 occupancy (already several 0-gaps on F1) and the distribution of inter-arrival times at join_ready; measure residual downstream_queue after capacity addition before combining with Redesign 1.

```python
import pandas as pd
from datetime import datetime
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.util import dataframe_utils

# 1. Construct supplied table in memory
raw = [
    ["F01","07:55","08:10","08:10","09:00","08:15","08:55","08:20","08:38","09:08","09:28","F1"],
    ["F02","08:05","08:25","08:25","09:15","08:45","09:25","08:30","08:50","09:40","10:00","F2"],
    ["F03","08:20","08:40","08:40","09:30","09:00","09:45","08:45","09:03","10:00","10:20","F1"],
    ["F04","08:35","08:55","08:55","09:45","09:05","09:35","09:30","09:52","10:10","10:30","F2"],
    ["F05","08:50","09:10","09:10","10:00","09:35","10:10","09:15","09:35","10:20","10:40","F1"],
    ["F06","09:05","09:25","09:25","10:15","09:55","10:30","09:40","10:00","10:45","11:05","F2"],
    ["F07","09:20","09:40","09:40","10:30","10:10","10:55","09:45","10:03","11:10","11:30","F1"],
    ["F08","09:35","09:55","09:55","10:45","10:00","10:30","10:20","10:50","11:05","11:25","F2"],
    ["F09","09:50","10:10","10:10","11:00","10:35","11:15","10:15","10:35","11:30","11:50","F1"],
    ["F10","10:05","10:25","10:25","11:15","10:45","11:20","10:30","10:50","11:40","12:00","F2"],
    ["F11","10:20","10:40","10:40","11:30","10:50","11:20","10:45","11:03","11:50","12:10","F1"],
    ["F12","10:35","10:55","10:55","11:45","11:25","12:10","11:00","11:18","12:20","12:40","F2"],
]
cols = ["case","arrival","fork_release","A_start","A_end","B_start","B_end",
        "C_start","C_end","final_start","final_end","final_resource"]
df = pd.DataFrame(raw, columns=cols)

def parse(t):
    return datetime.strptime("2026-05-19 " + t, "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M")
for c in cols[1:-1]:
    df[c] = df[c].map(parse)

# 2. Parse timestamps and calculate every decomposition component
df["upstream"] = (df.fork_release - df.arrival).dt.total_seconds() / 60
for br in "ABC":
    df[f"{br}_q"] = (df[f"{br}_start"] - df.fork_release).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    df[f"{br}_s"] = (df[f"{br}_end"] - df[f"{br}_start"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    df[f"{br}_qs"] = df[f"{br}_q"] + df[f"{br}_s"]
df["join_ready"] = df[["A_end","B_end","C_end"]].max(axis=1)
df["forkjoin"] = (df.join_ready - df.fork_release).dt.total_seconds() / 60
for br in "ABC":
    df[f"{br}_sync"] = (df.join_ready - df[f"{br}_end"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60
df["down_q"] = (df.final_start - df.join_ready).dt.total_seconds() / 60
df["final_s"] = (df.final_end - df.final_start).dt.total_seconds() / 60
df["total"] = (df.final_end - df.arrival).dt.total_seconds() / 60

# 3. Identify critical branches (deterministic ties: sorted alphabetical join)
def crit(row):
    m = row["join_ready"]
    return "/".join(sorted(b for b in "ABC" if row[f"{b}_end"] == m))
df["critical"] = df.apply(crit, axis=1)

print("=== Case-level decomposition (minutes) ===")
print(df[["case","upstream","A_q","A_s","B_q","B_s","C_q","C_s",
          "forkjoin","critical","down_q","final_s","total"]].to_string(index=False))

# 4. Three counterfactuals
# CF1: A service -20%
df["A_qs1"] = 40.0
df["join1"] = df[["A_qs1","B_qs","C_qs"]].max(axis=1)
df["save1"] = df.forkjoin - df.join1
# CF2: B queue -50%
df["B_qs2"] = 0.5 * df.B_q + df.B_s
df["join2"] = df[["A_qs","B_qs2","C_qs"]].max(axis=1)
df["save2"] = df.forkjoin - df.join2
# CF3: downstream -50%
df["save3"] = 0.5 * df.down_q

print("\n=== Counterfactual join/total savings (fixed down_q) ===")
print(df[["case","save1","save2","save3"]].to_string(index=False))
print("Mean savings CF1/CF2/CF3:", df.save1.mean(), df.save2.mean(), df.save3.mean())

# 5-6. PM4Py interval-event DataFrame
rows = []
for _, r in df.iterrows():
    for act, st, en in [("A", r.A_start, r.A_end),
                        ("B", r.B_start, r.B_end),
                        ("C", r.C_start, r.C_end),
                        ("F", r.final_start, r.final_end)]:
        rows.append({"case:concept:name": r.case,
                     "concept:name": act,
                     "start_timestamp": st,
                     "time:timestamp": en})
event_df = pd.DataFrame(rows)
event_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    event_df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp"
)

# recover service times (matches our A_s/B_s/C_s/final_s)
svc = pm4py.get_service_time(event_df)
print("\n=== PM4Py service times (seconds) ===")
print(svc)

# performance DFG (avg durations on directly-follows arcs)
dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(event_df)
print("\n=== PM4Py performance DFG (sample arcs) ===")
for k, v in list(dfg.items())[:6]:
    print(k, v)

# 7. Comments on custom calculations
# Standard DFG / service-time methods recover only active service durations
# and sequential directly-follows performance. They do NOT recover:
#   - branch queue after fork_release (no logged 'fork' activity that
#     every branch starts from),
#   - synchronization waiting (requires custom max(end_i) logic),
#   - critical-branch identification,
#   - fork-join elapsed = max(q_i + s_i).
# All of those quantities are therefore computed with pure pandas above.
```

# 7. Limits of inference

From the supplied timestamps alone the following cannot be identified:

- **Causality of queues.** Whether a branch queue is caused by limited resources, priority rules, batching, or upstream variability is invisible; only the realized waiting interval is observed.
- **Branch resource capacity.** No resource identifiers exist for A/B/C, so utilization, multi-case interference, and whether a queue would shrink under added capacity cannot be quantified.
- **Work preemption / interruptions.** Complete-event data give net service times; any preemption, multi-tasking or idle gaps inside a service interval are unobserved.
- **Nature of downstream waiting.** \(\mathrm{final\_start}-\mathrm{join\_ready}\) may be pure resource queue, a deliberate release policy, batching, or a combination; the two observed resources and their 0-gaps are consistent with a queue but do not prove it.
- **Inter-case cascade effects.** Counterfactual earlier join times may be masked by residual occupation of F1/F2 (several 0-gaps already exist); full queueing dynamics across cases are not recoverable from the single-day snapshot.
- **Arrival process and long-run behaviour.** Only twelve cases on one day are given; steady-state throughput, variability of upstream preparation, and seasonal effects remain unknown.
