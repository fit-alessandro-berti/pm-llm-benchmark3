## 1. Executive causal assessment

Verdict: **mainly confounded** — confidence **60/100**.

The strongest evidence against a harmful Orion effect is the reversal between crude and complexity-stratified comparisons. Overall, Orion averages 21.96h versus Nova's 15.24h (+6.72h), but Orion is faster within every complexity stratum: High 31.57h versus 35.00h, Medium 17.17h versus 19.38h, and Low 7.50h versus 9.64h. This is consistent with Orion's adverse case mix: 42.9% High-complexity versus 12.5% for Nova, 64.3% compliance-required versus 25.0%, and 92.9% night work versus 25.0%. Review service times are also very similar within complexity, suggesting queueing and case mix, not slower reviewing, drive the crude gap.

Evidence against a definitive “Orion is better” verdict is substantial. Cells are tiny — Nova-High n=2 and Orion-Low n=2 — and complexity is only a coarse proxy for difficulty. Routing reportedly selects Orion for difficult cases, so unobserved within-stratum case risk could bias results in either direction. Shift, arrival load, and resource group are also nearly collinear, and only completed cases are observed.

The 50% reassignment may therefore fail or backfire: moving difficult, night, compliance-heavy cases to Nova could transfer queues rather than remove them, overload less-certified reviewers, increase rework or appeals, and worsen cycle time. Any benefit would likely come from load balancing, not from removing an intrinsically harmful “Orion effect.” A small, stratified pilot is needed before scale-up.

**Final recommendation (120–200 words):** Do not implement the blind 50% reassignment. The crude Orion penalty reverses within every complexity stratum, so the proposal rests on a compositional comparison and risks moving difficult, night, compliance-heavy work to a group with fewer certified reviewers and less night coverage. That could relocate queues, increase rework, and harm quality without removing any bottleneck. Instead, first instrument timestamps, queue state, reviewer identity, and incomplete cases; then relieve night/compliance queue capacity while holding labels constant; and run a small stratified randomized routing pilot for cases genuinely eligible for either group. Scale only if the pilot shows a pre-specified cycle-time benefit without worse rework, completion, or 30-day outcomes. Treat any scale-up as a routing-policy redesign with monitoring and rollback, not as punishment or reward for a group label.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. Unobserved within-complexity case risk driving routing — most valuable addition: a pre-assignment risk/difficulty score (or routing-engine score and reason codes) recorded before group assignment.
2. Near-collinearity of group, shift, and load with empty cells (e.g., Orion-Day n=1) — most valuable design change: a stratified randomized routing pilot creating overlap across complexity × shift × load.
3. Queueing versus compliance versus service time cannot be separated from `wait_before_review_h` — most valuable field: full event timestamps with resource and queue-entry/exit times for Classify, Compliance Check, and each Review.
4. Selection into completed cases (abandoned/still-open invisible) — most valuable field: a complete case registry including open/abandoned cases with censoring time and reason.
5. Group is a bundle of skill, capacity, and roster, and 30-day quality is coarse/post-process — most valuable field: reviewer ID with certification and per-episode service records, linked to a validated quality outcome.
