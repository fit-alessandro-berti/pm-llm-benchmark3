# Incident-Response Temporal Conformance Under Timestamp Uncertainty

## 1. Normalization Assumptions

**A1 — Interval construction, two stages in a fixed order.** Every recorded value is expanded
into a *clock-time* window determined by the display/rounding semantics of its source, and only
then corrected for clock error. Formally, with a displayed value `d`, resolution window
`[r_lo, r_hi]` and clock-offset bound `[a, b]`:

```
clock_window = [d + r_lo, d + r_hi]
true_window  = [d + r_lo - b, d + r_hi - a]
```

This follows the stated direction `clock_offset = recorded_clock - true_clock`, hence
`true = recorded - offset`, so the *upper* offset bound `b` shifts the *lower* true bound and vice
versa. Reversing this is the single most common error in this kind of analysis; I flag it because it
changes several classifications by minutes.

**A2 — Resolution windows per source.**

| Source | Window in clock terms | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| HUB | `[-0.5 s, +0.5 s]` | exact to displayed second plus stated ±0.5 s measurement uncertainty |
| OPS-BER, VENDOR-NY, LEGACY-BER | `[0 s, +60 s)` | floor to minute: displayed `mm` means clock in `[mm:00, mm+1:00)` |
| SENSOR | `[-5 s, +5 s]` | round to nearest 10 s |
| MOBILE-LON | `[-30 s, +30 s)` | round to nearest minute |

Floor and round-to-minute windows have an **open** upper bound. I write `:59.999` throughout as
shorthand for that open bound; the millisecond is notation, not a claim of millisecond precision.
No classification in this analysis turns on that millisecond.

**A3 — Total uncertainty half-widths (worth stating plainly).** After both stages the true-time
windows are: HUB ±1.5 s; SENSOR ±10 s; OPS-BER 4 min 30 s wide; LEGACY-BER 3 min wide;
VENDOR-NY 7 min wide; MOBILE-LON 11 min wide (16 min under A8 primary). HUB is effectively a
point at SLA scale; every genuinely uncertain finding below traces to a non-HUB source or a
missing event.

**A4 — Time-zone conversion.** Local wall-clock values are converted with the IANA database
(`zoneinfo`), not fixed offsets: `Europe/Berlin` CEST = UTC+2 and CET = UTC+1, `Europe/London`
BST = UTC+1, `America/New_York` EDT = UTC−4. Conversion is applied to the displayed local value;
the resolution window is then applied in UTC. This is equivalent to applying it locally except for
a window straddling a transition instant, which does not occur here.

**A5 — DST fold (T-405).** On 2025-10-26 a Berlin local time of `02:mm` is genuinely ambiguous:
it maps to `00:mm UTC` (pre-transition, CEST) **or** `01:mm UTC` (post-transition, CET). Because the
fold was not logged, each T-405 event starts as a **union of two disjoint intervals one hour apart**.
I do not pick a fold by convention. I prune the union using the recorded ingestion order
(A6), which is a genuine piece of evidence, and I report which combinations survive.

**A6 — Causal and ingestion-order constraints.** Where the problem asserts an order, I treat it as
a hard constraint and use it in two ways: (i) fold/alternative combinations with no consistent
non-decreasing assignment are eliminated; (ii) surviving single intervals are tightened by arc
consistency (`lo_i := max(lo_i, lo_{i-1})`, `hi_i := min(hi_i, hi_{i+1})`). This tightening is
*sound* — it never discards a true time that satisfies both the source bounds and the asserted
order — and it is what makes T-407 and T-409 testable at all. When a duration interval computed by
naive subtraction admits negative values, I clamp the lower bound to 0 rather than reporting a
negative duration, and I say so.

**A7 — Inferred Investigation_Start (T-404).** `Investigation_Start = Investigation_Complete −
duration`, with duration in `[1080 s, 1500 s]` from the signed work record. Both uncertainties are
retained: the inferred interval is
`[IC_lo − 1500 s, IC_hi − 1080 s]`, i.e. 7 min 3 s wide. I do **not** collapse the work record to
a nominal value. Assumption: the work record's "investigation duration" is measured from
Investigation_Start to Investigation_Complete, and the two are the endpoints of one continuous
episode. If investigation was suspended and resumed, the inference is invalid.

**A8 — Mobile entry delay (T-406), the substantive assumption.** The phrase "may have entered the
displayed time up to five minutes late" is ambiguous between two readings, and they give different
answers, so I report both and designate a primary:

- **Primary (occurrence-widening).** The *displayed value itself* may be up to 5 min later than the
  occurrence. Entry delay therefore shifts my estimate of **occurrence time**, extending the lower
  bound by 300 s: `Investigation_Start ∈ [12:03:30Z, 12:19:29.999Z]`.
- **Alternative (record-only).** The user typed late but typed the *correct* occurrence time. Delay
  then affects only the record timestamp, not occurrence: `[12:08:30Z, 12:19:29.999Z]`.

I keep the entry delay algebraically separate from the clock bound rather than merging them into one
±number, because they are different error mechanisms with different remedies (A8 is a UI/lifecycle
problem; the clock bound is a synchronization problem). **This assumption alone flips T-406 S2
between POSSIBLE_VIOLATION and CERTAIN_PASS**, and it is the clearest example in this dataset of a
conclusion resting on semantics rather than data.

**A9 — Alert_Sent is not Alert_Received (T-407).** I do not equate them. I construct a *derived*
receipt interval `Alert_Received ∈ [Alert_Sent_lo, Alert_Sent_hi + 1200 s]` from the
`[0, 20] min` delivery-latency bound, then apply the plausible-but-unlogged causal assumption
"receipt not after investigation start" (A6), which tightens the upper bound to
`Investigation_Start_hi`. Because no receipt acknowledgment exists, every T-407 S1 result is
reported **twice**: under derived-receipt semantics, and as INDETERMINATE under strict semantics
where an unlogged event cannot anchor a legal deadline. I regard the strict reading as the correct
one for *enforcement* and the derived reading as the correct one for *triage*.

**A10 — SENSOR completion semantics (T-401).** SENSOR device events are completion events, which
matches `Containment_Complete` as an activity whose SLA-relevant instant is its completion. No
start/complete split is available, so I treat the SENSOR value as the completion instant with no
extra duration allowance. If SENSOR's "completion" actually reports the completion of an internal
detection cycle that lags the containment action, T-401 S2 and S3 would need re-derivation.

**A11 — Weak offset evidence (T-409).** For the T-409 hour, NTP diagnostics are absent and only a
`[−12 min, +12 min]` engineering estimate exists. I use it, because a bounded weak estimate still
yields a bounded interval, but I mark evidence strength **Weak** and confidence **Low**, and I
report the strict alternative: if an unvalidated engineering estimate is not accepted as a bound,
the offset is effectively unbounded and every T-409 pair involving OPS-BER becomes INDETERMINATE
rather than POSSIBLE_VIOLATION. Note the estimate is 8× looser than OPS-BER's normal bound, so
T-409's start time is the *least* trustworthy timestamp in the dataset apart from the missing ones.

**A12 — Independence and no distributions.** Feasible sets are combined as intervals, not random
variables. `duration ∈ [b_lo − a_hi, b_hi − a_lo]` treats the two event times as independently
uncertain within their bounds. This is conservative (widest defensible), and it is the right default
here because clock error is systematic, not independent noise: two events from the *same* source in
the *same* hour share most of their offset error, so same-source durations are in reality tighter
than I report. This matters only for T-405 (all three events LEGACY-BER); I note it there and do not
exploit it, since the offset bound is not documented as time-correlated. I attach **no probability**
to any point inside an interval. Where I quote a "violating share" it is a fraction of interval
*width*, an exposure indicator only — not a probability of violation.

**A13 — Rule semantics.** S1–S3 are one-sided deadlines: violation iff `duration > limit`.
Temporal-profile bands are two-sided: `[μ − ζσ, μ + ζσ]`, with wholly-below and wholly-above both
counting as certain deviations. Union handling: I classify each disjoint component and combine —
unanimous PASS is CERTAIN_PASS, unanimous VIOLATION is CERTAIN_VIOLATION, mixed is
POSSIBLE_VIOLATION. I never merge disjoint components into one hull before classifying.

**A14 — Testability.** A rule is testable only if both endpoint events exist or are defensibly
inferable. `Close_Incident` exists only for T-401 and T-408, so S3 is INDETERMINATE by absence for
the other seven cases. This is missing-data indeterminacy, not a pass.

## 3. Certain/Possible/Indeterminate Findings

**Severity scale.** Critical = certain breach of a hard SLA with a large margin; High = certain
breach with a small margin, or a possible breach whose violating region dominates the feasible set;
Medium = possible breach with a substantial violating region; Low = possible breach only in a narrow
sliver; None = certain pass. Percentages are shares of feasible interval **width** (exposure
indicator, not probability — see A12).

**Confidence** rates the strength of the *evidence* behind the classification, independent of the
verdict: High = all binding endpoints from HUB/SENSOR; Medium = a floor-to-minute source or a
documented inference is binding; Low = weak/unsynchronized source, user-entered value, or an
unlogged semantic assumption is binding.

than per-phase conformance, and phase-level SLAs are what the current instrumentation cannot

## 5. PM4Py Temporal-Conformance Code

The script below was executed end to end against pm4py 2.7.23.4, pandas, and the standard-library
`zoneinfo`. It reads no external files.

```python
# =========================================================================
# Interval-based temporal conformance for incident cases T-401 .. T-409.
# Self-contained: no external files are read.
#
# Semantics implemented (see Section 1 of the analysis):
#   1. resolution/display window is applied FIRST, in source-clock terms;
#   2. then clock correction, using clock_offset = recorded_clock - true_clock,
#      so true in [clock_lo - offset_hi, clock_hi - offset_lo];
#   3. DST-ambiguous local times become TWO alternative intervals (a union);
#   4. asserted causal/ingestion order prunes fold combinations and tightens
#      intervals by arc consistency;
#   5. durations are computed by interval subtraction over surviving unions.
# =========================================================================
import itertools
from datetime import datetime, timedelta
from zoneinfo import ZoneInfo

import pandas as pd
import pm4py

UTC = ZoneInfo("UTC")
OPEN_EPS = timedelta(milliseconds=1)   # stands in for an open upper bound

# ----------------------------------------------------------------- metadata
SOURCES = {
    "HUB":        {"tz": "UTC",              "res": "sec_pm_half",  "off": (-1.0, 1.0)},
    "OPS-BER":    {"tz": "Europe/Berlin",    "res": "floor_minute", "off": (-90.0, 120.0)},
    "SENSOR":     {"tz": "UTC",              "res": "round_10s",    "off": (-5.0, 5.0)},
    "VENDOR-NY":  {"tz": "America/New_York", "res": "floor_minute", "off": (-180.0, 180.0)},
    "LEGACY-BER": {"tz": "Europe/Berlin",    "res": "floor_minute", "off": (-60.0, 60.0)},
    "MOBILE-LON": {"tz": "Europe/London",    "res": "round_minute", "off": (-300.0, 300.0)},
}
# resolution window in seconds, relative to the displayed value, in clock terms
RES_WINDOW = {
    "sec_pm_half":  (-0.5, 0.5),    # exact to the second +/- 0.5 s measurement
    "floor_minute": (0.0, 60.0),    # displayed mm:00 means [mm:00, mm+1:00)
    "round_10s":    (-5.0, 5.0),
    "round_minute": (-30.0, 30.0),  # [-30 s, +30 s)
}
OPEN_UPPER = {"floor_minute", "round_minute"}

CHAIN = ["Alert_Sent", "Alert_Received", "Investigation_Start",
         "Investigation_Complete", "Containment_Complete", "Close_Incident"]

       "S2": ("Investigation_Start", "Containment_Complete", 45 * 60),
       "S3": ("Containment_Complete", "Close_Incident", 36 * 3600)}

    ("Alert_Received", "Investigation_Start"):        (600.0, 180.0),
    ("Investigation_Start", "Containment_Complete"): (2100.0, 600.0),
    ("Alert_Received", "Containment_Complete"):      (2700.0, 720.0),
    ("Containment_Complete", "Close_Incident"):     (86400.0, 21600.0),

# ------------------------------------------------------- interval machinery
def utc_candidates(local_str, tzname):
    """Displayed local wall clock -> candidate UTC instants.

    Returns two instants for a time inside a DST fold (ambiguous local time),
    one otherwise. The fold was not logged, so both remain alternatives.
    """
    naive = datetime.strptime(local_str, "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S")
    tz = ZoneInfo(tzname)
    out = []
    for fold in (0, 1):
        inst = naive.replace(tzinfo=tz, fold=fold).astimezone(UTC)
        if inst not in out:
            out.append(inst)
    return out


def event_intervals(local_str, system, off=None, extra=(0.0, 0.0)):
    """Feasible true-time interval(s) in UTC for one recorded value.

    `off` overrides the source clock-offset bound (T-409 weak estimate).
    `extra` widens the result in seconds (lo_shift, hi_shift), used only for
    the mobile entry-delay assumption, and kept separate on purpose.
    """
    meta = SOURCES[system]
    r_lo, r_hi = RES_WINDOW[meta["res"]]
    o_lo, o_hi = off if off is not None else meta["off"]
    out = []
    for base in utc_candidates(local_str, meta["tz"]):
        lo = base + timedelta(seconds=r_lo - o_hi + extra[0])
        hi = base + timedelta(seconds=r_hi - o_lo + extra[1])
        if meta["res"] in OPEN_UPPER:
            hi -= OPEN_EPS
        out.append((lo, hi))
    return out


def prune_folds(events):
    """Keep only fold alternatives compatible with the asserted event order.

    A combination is feasible iff a non-decreasing selection exists, which the
    earliest-feasible greedy sweep decides exactly for a total-order chain.
    """
    chain = [a for a in CHAIN if a in events]
    keep = {a: set() for a in chain}
    for combo in itertools.product(*[range(len(events[a]["iv"])) for a in chain]):
        t, ok = None, True
        for k, a in enumerate(chain):
            lo, hi = events[a]["iv"][combo[k]]
            pick = lo if t is None else max(lo, t)
            if pick > hi:
                ok = False
                break
            t = pick
        if ok:
            for k, a in enumerate(chain):
                keep[a].add(combo[k])
    if not any(keep[a] for a in chain):
        return events, False
    for a in chain:
        events[a]["iv"] = [events[a]["iv"][i] for i in sorted(keep[a])]
    return events, True


def propagate(events):
    """Arc-consistency tightening of single-interval events under the order.

    Sound (never removes a feasible true time) and it is what makes the
    T-407 inferred receipt and the T-409 weak offset testable at all.
    """
    chain = [a for a in CHAIN if a in events and len(events[a]["iv"]) == 1]
    bounds = {a: list(events[a]["iv"][0]) for a in chain}
    for i in range(1, len(chain)):
        bounds[chain[i]][0] = max(bounds[chain[i]][0], bounds[chain[i - 1]][0])
    for i in range(len(chain) - 2, -1, -1):
        bounds[chain[i]][1] = min(bounds[chain[i]][1], bounds[chain[i + 1]][1])
    for a in chain:
        events[a]["iv"] = [tuple(bounds[a])]
    return events


def duration_union(events, a, b, ordered=True):
    """Feasible duration interval(s) in seconds, preserving disjoint unions."""
    if a not in events or b not in events:
        return []
    out = []
    for ia in events[a]["iv"]:
        for ib in events[b]["iv"]:
            lo = (ib[0] - ia[1]).total_seconds()
            hi = (ib[1] - ia[0]).total_seconds()
            if ordered:
                if hi < 0:
                    continue                 # contradicts the asserted order
                lo = max(lo, 0.0)
            out.append((lo, hi))
    return sorted(out)


# ------------------------------------------------------------ classification
def classify_deadline(dur, limit):
    lo, hi = dur
    if hi <= limit:
        return "CERTAIN_PASS"
    if lo > limit:
        return "CERTAIN_VIOLATION"
    return "POSSIBLE_VIOLATION"


def classify_band(dur, lo_b, hi_b):
    lo, hi = dur
    if lo >= lo_b and hi <= hi_b:
        return "CERTAIN_PASS"
    if hi < lo_b or lo > hi_b:
        return "CERTAIN_VIOLATION"
    return "POSSIBLE_VIOLATION"


def combine(labels):
    """Union of disjoint feasible intervals: unanimity or POSSIBLE."""
    if not labels:
        return "INDETERMINATE"
    uniq = set(labels)
    return labels[0] if len(uniq) == 1 else "POSSIBLE_VIOLATION"

            "iv": event_intervals(row.recorded_local, row.system, **opts),

        "iv": [(ic[0] - timedelta(minutes=25), ic[1] - timedelta(minutes=18))],
        "system": "HUB+work-record", "kind": "inferred"}

        cases[c], feasible = prune_folds(cases[c])
```

### 5.1 Version-isolated assumption

The only version-fragile part is `run_pm4py`. On pm4py 2.7.23.4 the signature is
`conformance_temporal_profile(log, temporal_profile, zeta=1.0, activity_key=..., timestamp_key=...,
case_id_key=..., return_diagnostics_dataframe=False)`, returning a list — one entry per case, in
case order — of deviation records. I rely on only three positional facts: fields 0 and 1 are the
activity pair, and the **last** field is the deviation in standard deviations. Older builds expose
the same algorithm as `pm4py.algo.conformance.temporal_profile.algorithm.apply(log, profile,
parameters={"zeta": z})`, which the `TypeError` branch covers. If a build returns a diagnostics
DataFrame instead of tuples (via `return_diagnostics_dataframe=True`), only `run_pm4py` and the
unpacking loop need changing; the interval analysis is entirely independent of pm4py and would be
unaffected. Case alignment assumes the returned list follows sorted case order, which holds for the
sorted DataFrame passed in; a build that reorders cases would need an explicit case-key join.

### 5.2 Observed midpoint results and what they prove

pm4py flagged, on the **midpoint** log: at ζ=1, deviations in T-401, T-402, T-403, T-404, T-405 and
T-408; at ζ=2, only T-402 (AR→IS, 2.75 sd) and T-408 (CC→Close, 2.40 sd); at ζ=3, nothing at all.

Reconciled against the interval analysis, **26 of 87 case-pair-ζ cells disagree**, in both
directions:

- **Over-calling (6 cells)** — midpoint asserts a deviation where the interval analysis can only say
  POSSIBLE: T-401 AR→IS (ζ=1), T-402 AR→IS (ζ=2), T-403 AR→IS (ζ=1), T-403 AR→CC (ζ=1), T-404 AR→IS
  (ζ=1), T-405 AR→IS (ζ=1). Escalating these would mean sanctioning a team on the strength of an
  arbitrary midpoint choice.
- **Masking (20 cells)** — midpoint reports no deviation where a possible violation genuinely
  exists. The worst are T-406, T-407 and T-409 AR→IS, masked at **every** ζ, because each midpoint
  lands near 510–690 s, comfortably inside even the ζ=1 band, while the feasible intervals span
  roughly 200–1170 s, 0–1023 s and 0–1021 s. A midpoint reviewer would close these three cases as
  clean; interval analysis shows they are unresolved at every ζ tested.
- Most damning, at ζ=3 the midpoint log is **completely silent**, including for T-408 CC→Close —
  a case whose 36-hour SLA breach is certain with a ≥ 2.4 hour margin.

The mechanism is straightforward: a midpoint is one arbitrarily chosen member of the feasible set,
so it inherits none of the set's structure. It cannot express "wholly above the band" versus "merely
overlapping it," which is exactly the certain/possible distinction the task requires. pm4py's
temporal profile is a correct implementation of a point-timestamp method; the limitation is in the
input representation, not the library. **pm4py does not natively resolve timestamp uncertainty** —
it has no concept of clock offset, display resolution, DST fold, or missing-event inference, and it
cannot be made to produce certain/possible labels without the interval layer above it. Its output
here is a cross-check on the arithmetic and a demonstration of what point-based conformance loses,
not a source of findings.

A final caution on the midpoint log: T-405's midpoints are chronologically sensible **only because
folds were pruned first**. Feeding the raw local timestamps to pm4py would have produced
`Investigation_Start` 45 minutes *before* `Alert_Received`, a negative duration and a nonsensical
eventually-follows relation. The one case that most needs interval reasoning is also the one where
the point-based tool fails first.

## 7. Governance Recommendation

**G1 — UTC storage with the original zone retained.** Persist every event as an instant in UTC
*and* keep the originating IANA zone identifier plus the UTC offset in effect. Never store bare
local wall-clock strings, and never store fixed numeric offsets in place of a zone ID: `+02:00` is
not `Europe/Berlin` and loses the information needed to reason about future transitions. Reject
three-letter abbreviations (`CEST`, `EDT`) as authoritative; they are display artefacts and are
ambiguous across regions.

**G2 — Raw timestamp retention, immutable.** Retain the exact bytes as received alongside the
normalized instant: `raw_value`, `raw_timezone`, `source_system`, `ingestion_time`. Normalization
must be a derived, recomputable column, never a destructive overwrite. Every case in this analysis
depended on reading the raw display form: had T-401's `10:14` already been "helpfully" stored as
`10:14:00.000`, the 60-second floor window would have been invisible and S1 would have been reported
as a false CERTAIN_PASS. Store the analysis engine version with each normalization so conclusions
can be replayed when bounds are revised.

**G3 — Source clock-offset telemetry as a first-class, per-event fact.** Move from daily aggregate
NTP diagnostics to a continuously recorded offset series per source, and attach to each event the
offset bound **in force at that instant**, with the measurement's own provenance and validity
window. Two specific requirements follow from T-409: (i) a missing offset measurement must render
events *explicitly* unbounded rather than silently inheriting yesterday's bound or a default; and
(ii) engineering estimates must be tagged as a distinct, lower evidence class from measured
synchronization, never merged into the same field. Alert when a source's offset bound exceeds a
configured fraction of the tightest SLA it feeds — MOBILE-LON's ±300 s against a 900 s deadline
should have been flagged as unfit for purpose before any conformance question was asked.

**G4 — Resolution and precision metadata.** Every timestamp column carries a declared
`resolution` (`second`, `minute`, `10s`) and `rounding_mode` (`floor`, `nearest`, `ceiling`), plus a
`measurement_uncertainty` where known. Without `rounding_mode`, `10:14` is unresolvably ambiguous
between a 60 s window starting at 10:14:00 and a 60 s window centred on it — a 30 s systematic
shift, enough to flip T-401 S1 and T-405 S1. Prefer capture at second or better precision at the
source: minute-floored displays were the binding constraint in five of the nine cases here, and no
downstream analysis can recover what the display discarded.

**G5 — Explicit start/complete lifecycle semantics.** Adopt an explicit lifecycle attribute
(`start`, `complete`, `suspend`, `resume`) on every event, per the XES convention, and record both
endpoints for any activity with material duration. Two concrete gaps here: SENSOR's completion-only
semantics (A10) left no way to check whether the containment action itself fit the window, and
T-404's missing `Investigation_Start` forced a derived interval whose validity depends on the
investigation never having been suspended — a condition that suspend/resume events would settle
outright. Where a start must be inferred, persist it as a first-class interval-valued attribute
flagged `inferred`, with the inference rule recorded, so no consumer mistakes it for an observation.

**G6 — DST fold must be logged, not reconstructed.** Record the fold indicator (or, equivalently,
store the UTC instant at capture) for every event in an ambiguous local hour. T-405 was recoverable
only because ingestion order happened to eliminate seven of eight fold combinations; that was luck,
not design, and with a different arrival pattern the case would have been left with a one-hour
irreducible ambiguity. Best practice removes the problem at source: capture in UTC and render local
time only for display. As a compensating control, run an automated ambiguity sweep over the two
ambiguous hours each autumn and quarantine affected events for review.

**G7 — Causal identifiers instead of inferred causality.** Propagate a correlation identifier
end-to-end — `incident_id`, `alert_id`, and a `caused_by` pointer linking receipt to send,
investigation to receipt, containment to investigation. Log a receipt acknowledgment for every
dispatched alert. T-407 exists as a problem only because the send/receive pair had no
acknowledgment: the analysis had to substitute an unlogged causal assumption for a fact, and the
resulting verdict flips between POSSIBLE_VIOLATION and CERTAIN_VIOLATION depending on which event
the SLA names. Order asserted by ingestion sequence is a weak substitute and should be recorded with
its own provenance (assigned at receipt, monotonic per source) whenever it is relied upon, as in
T-405.

**G8 — External-source contracts.** Require event-level synchronization evidence from vendors as a
contractual term, not a best effort: second precision, UTC, stated rounding, and a per-event or
per-interval offset attestation. VENDOR-NY's ±180 s with no event-level synchronization made T-403's
S1 unresolvable at ζ=1, 2 and 3 — no internal improvement can compensate. Until such terms exist,
SLAs anchored on vendor-supplied events should be measured with an explicit, published allowance
equal to the vendor's uncertainty, so that responders are not held to a deadline the instrumentation
cannot adjudicate.

**G9 — Enforcement policy: interval overlap is NOT sufficient.** This is the central governance
decision, and my recommendation is a tiered rule:

| Classification | Enforcement action |
|---|---|
| CERTAIN_VIOLATION | Enforceable. Sanctions, formal breach reporting, remediation. Here: T-408 S2, T-408 S3 (all-HUB, robust to every tolerance); T-402 S1 (robust at 0 and, in part, at higher tolerances) |
| POSSIBLE_VIOLATION | **Not enforceable.** Route to a *data-quality* queue, not a performance queue. Record the violating share for prioritization, and require the responsible source owner — not the responder — to supply the evidence in Section 6 |
| INDETERMINATE | Not enforceable and not a pass. Must remain visibly open; a missing `Close_Incident` must never age out into implied compliance |
| CERTAIN_PASS | Closed |

Enforcing on overlap would mean sanctioning teams for the organization's own instrumentation
defects: eight of the eleven unresolved SLA findings here are driven by rounding, unsynchronized
clocks, user entry, or missing events rather than by any disputed responder action. Conversely,
treating POSSIBLE as a pass would have quietly cleared T-403, where 57% of the feasible interval
violates. Publish the tolerance and ζ parameters as governed configuration with change control and
an effective-date history; as Section 4 shows, a 5-minute tolerance silently eliminates every S1
finding except one 31.5-second sliver, and ζ=3 silently eliminates every profile deviation including
T-408's certain 38-hour closure breach. Parameters that can erase findings are policy, not tuning.

**G10 — Separate the two instruments, and report certainty on the face of every metric.** Keep the
legal/operational SLA and the statistical temporal profile in distinct reports with distinct owners:
T-409 IS→CC deviates *below* the profile band while passing S2 certainly, and T-408 IS→CC breaches
S2 certainly at every tolerance while passing the profile at ζ=3. Neither is evidence about the
