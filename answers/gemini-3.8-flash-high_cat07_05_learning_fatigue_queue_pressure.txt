# 2. Integrated explanation

The observed changes in service time, error rates, and rework over the work period cannot be explained by any single mechanism. Instead, they reflect a dynamic interplay between structural case-mix selection, queue-dependent pacing, coping workarounds, and cognitive fatigue.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                  THE SHIFT PERFORMANCE LIFECYCLE                                  |
+------------------------------------+--------------------------------+-----------------------------+
| Phase 1: Shift Opening (Events 1-15)| Phase 2: Equilibrium (16-50)   | Phase 3: Pressure (51-76+)  |
| - High Complexity Backlog (0.72)   | - Normalized Complexity (0.50) | - Low Complexity (0.43)     |
| - Senior Carryover Triage          | - Balanced Queue (31-33)       | - High Queue / WIP (>70)    |
| - Thorough Deliberation (18.8 min) | - Flow State (12.9-13.6 min)   | - Rushed Processing (10.8m) |
| - Errors Contained (3.4%)          | - Lowest Errors (2.5-2.7%)     | - Error Surge (7.9%-10.9%)  |
+------------------------------------+--------------------------------+-----------------------------+
```

### Phase 1: Shift Opening and the "Warm-up" Illusion (Sequence Positions 1–15, Minutes 0–90)
At shift start, service time is at its maximum (mean 18.8 min in bins 1–5), while error rates (3.4%) and rework (6.1%) remain moderate. Although a minor component of this duration reflects cognitive orientation (workers re-establishing context, opening software tools, and reviewing shift handover notes), the primary driver is **case-mix selection bias**. 

Shifts inherit unresolved carryover from prior periods (e.g., Night shift or weekend backlogs). These carryover cases are disproportionately complex (mean complexity score 0.72 vs. 0.43 late in shift) and time-critical (41% high-priority). Furthermore, work allocation at shift opening is not random: senior personnel (Senior_A, Senior_B, Senior_C) preferentially claim or are assigned these complex cases (e.g., Seniors log 15.9 min in their first 20 events). Because workers take the necessary time to deliberate on these difficult claims, errors are contained relative to case difficulty. As this initial backlog is cleared, mean complexity rapidly declines (0.61 in bins 6–15, 0.54 in bins 16–30), mechanically reducing average service times.

### Phase 2: Steady-State Flow and True Learning (Sequence Positions 16–50, Minutes 90–270)
Between sequence positions 16 and 50, the operational environment reaches equilibrium. Queues stabilize at manageable levels (mean queue 31–33), and case complexity normalizes to 0.50–0.54. Here, genuine cognitive warming and task fluency are visible: service time stabilizes at 12.9–13.6 min, and error rates reach their lowest levels across the shift (2.5%–2.7%), with rework dropping to 4.8%–5.0%. 

In this regime, workers operate within their cognitive capacity: case arrival rates roughly match processing capacity, allowing thorough evaluation without panic. Workers across all seniority levels exhibit consistent quality under these low-WIP conditions (errors remain between 2.2% for Seniors and 4.4% for Juniors).

### Phase 3: Queue Inundation, Speed-Accuracy Trade-off, and Workarounds (Sequence Positions 51–76+, Minutes 270–480+)
In the latter half of the shift, the system bifurcates depending on queue pressure. In shifts where queue accumulation is severe—most notably the Day shift, where customer demand peaks late and queues balloon from 38 to 75—workers encounter severe queue pressure (mean queue length 62 in bins 51–75, rising to 88 in bins 76+).

Under mounting queue pressure and visible backlog, workers do not slow down; they **accelerate**. Mean service time drops to 11.4 min (bins 51–75) and further to 10.8 min (bins 76+). This late-shift acceleration is **not learning**; it represents an induced **speed-accuracy trade-off**. Workers adopt coping mechanisms to survive the backlog:
1. **Heuristic satisficing and rationing:** Workers truncate verification protocols, skim supporting documentation, and make hasty eligibility and triage decisions. For low-complexity cases under high WIP (>70), service time drops from 12.7 to 9.4 min, but errors nearly triple from 2.1% to 5.8%. For high-complexity cases, service time is compressed from 22.4 to 16.8 min, causing errors to reach 10.9%.
2. **Batching workarounds:** Under queue pressure, junior staff (Junior_H, Junior_I) resort to batching Document Review (processing 9–18 cases consecutively in rapid succession), artificially driving down recorded service times while failing to conduct individualized scrutiny.
3. **Delayed Quality Checks:** Rather than conducting continuous quality checks, workers defer Quality Check activities until the final 45 minutes of the shift. This creates a severe end-of-shift bottleneck, resulting in superficial checks that fail to catch critical errors, which subsequently surface as 7-day rework (13.7% in bins 76+).
4. **Automation friction:** High queue pressure impairs human judgment when interacting with Bot_Risk. Pressured workers either override Bot_Risk classifications impulsively or rubber-stamp edge cases, increasing downstream corrections.

### The Moderating Roles of Fatigue, Experience, and Circadian Context
Fatigue acts as a **vulnerability multiplier** rather than an isolated cause. Under low WIP (<30), even after hours on duty, workers maintain low error rates (Seniors 2.2%, Mid 2.9%, Juniors 4.4%, Night 3.7%). When unpressured, fatigued workers compensate by maintaining careful pacing. However, when high queue pressure is superimposed on late-shift fatigue, cognitive control collapses. 

This collapse is sharply moderated by experience:
- **Senior staff** possess robust, compiled cognitive schemas and task management skills. When high WIP hits, their error rate rises modestly to 4.9%.
- **Mid-level staff** degrade moderately, reaching a 6.8% error rate.
- **Junior staff**, lacking automated schemas, rely on working memory. Under simultaneous late-shift fatigue and high WIP, their cognitive capacity is overwhelmed, causing error rates to spike to 12.4%.

Finally, the **Night shift** demonstrates that circadian fatigue does not automatically produce high error rates. Night workers face both circadian lows and high case complexity (56% high-complexity cases in the first hour), but because their queue remains bounded (ending queue 55 vs. Day's 75), they do not rush (maintaining a 15.2 min service time in their final two hours). Consequently, their end-of-shift error rate is 5.1%—substantially lower than Day shift workers (7.5%), who are rushed by an unmanaged queue.

# 3. Proposed analyses

To rigorously disentangle these competing mechanisms, we propose a unified statistical and process-mining analytical framework.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    ANALYTICAL PIPELINE ARCHITECTURE                                |
+-----------------------------------+--------------------------------+-------------------------------+
| 1. PM4Py Temporal & Event Prep    | 2. Workaround & DFG Discovery  | 3. Econometric Disentanglement|
| - Service / Waiting times         | - Batch discovery              | - Panel Fixed Effects (Worker)|
| - Arrival / Finish rates          | - Rework loops per activity    | - Piecewise Splines (WIP/Pos) |
| - WIP & Queue feature extraction  | - Performance DFG bottlenecks  | - Complexity Matching (CEM)   |
+-----------------------------------+--------------------------------+-------------------------------+
```

### 1. Statistical and Econometric Models
1. **Within-Resource Panel Fixed-Effects Regressions:**
   To isolate within-worker variations from between-worker selection bias (e.g., seniors taking hard cases), we fit fixed-effects models:
   $$\ln(\text{ServiceTime}_{ijk}) = \alpha_i + \beta_1 \text{SeqPos}_{ijk} + \beta_2 \text{WIP}_{ijk} + \beta_3 \text{Complexity}_{jk} + \mathbf{X}_{ijk}\boldsymbol{\gamma} + \epsilon_{ijk}$$
   $$\text{logit}(P(\text{Error}_{ijk}=1)) = \eta_i + \delta_1 \text{SeqPos}_{ijk} + \delta_2 \text{WIP}_{ijk} + \delta_3 \text{Complexity}_{jk} + \delta_4 (\text{WIP}_{ijk} \times \text{Experience}_i) + \mathbf{X}_{ijk}\boldsymbol{\theta}$$
   where $\alpha_i, \eta_i$ are worker fixed effects, $i$ indexes resource, $j$ indexes shift, and $k$ indexes event sequence.

2. **Nonlinear Shift-Position Splines vs. Circadian Harmonics:**
   Decompose temporal effects into:
   - Within-shift sequence: Restricted cubic splines on `minutes_since_shift_start` (capturing fatigue/warm-up).
   - Circadian effects: First- and second-order Fourier terms $\sin(2\pi t / 24), \cos(2\pi t / 24)$ on clock hour $t$.
   - Queue pressure: Piecewise linear splines on `queue_length_at_start` with knots at 30 and 70.

3. **Stratified Complexity Matching:**
   Implement Coarsened Exact Matching (CEM) or Propensity Score Weighting on `case_complexity_score`, `priority`, and `activity_family` to compare identical claims processed at positions 1–5 vs. 16–30 vs. 76+.

4. **Experience and Queue Interaction:**
   Model the interaction $\text{WIP} \times \text{resource\_experience\_months}$ to quantify how organizational tenure moderates resilience to backlog surges.

### 2. Process-Mining Analyses
- **Batch Detection:** Use `pm4py.discover_batches` to identify sequential and concurrent batching by resource and activity family, quantifying artificial service time compression.
- **Rework and Feedback Loops:** Compute activity-level rework matrices using `pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity` and evaluate 7-day downstream rework loops using Directly-Follows Graphs (DFG).
- **Trace Clustering:** Cluster process traces via `pm4py.cluster_log` to detect variant pathways associated with late-shift quality check deferrals and Bot_Risk override loops.

### 3. PM4Py and Python Implementation Code

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
import statsmodels.api as sm
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Step 1: Format Log and Enrich with Temporal and Performance Metrics
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
def prepare_and_enrich_event_log(df_raw: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Format dataframe for PM4Py and compute queue, service, and waiting times."""
    # Ensure correct PM4Py column mappings
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df_raw,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp"
    )
    
    # Calculate service time and waiting time per event
    df = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
        df,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp"
    )
    
    # Calculate arrival and finish rates across sliding operational windows
    df = pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate(
        df,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )
    
    # Extract temporal features (hour of day, day of week, elapsed seconds)
    df = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
        df,
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )
    
    # Compute circadian harmonic regressors from hour of day
    df["clock_hour"] = df["time:timestamp"].dt.hour + df["time:timestamp"].dt.minute / 60.0
    df["circadian_sin"] = np.sin(2 * np.pi * df["clock_hour"] / 24.0)
    df["circadian_cos"] = np.cos(2 * np.pi * df["clock_hour"] / 24.0)
    
    # Clean service time in minutes
    if "service_time" in df.columns:
        df["service_time_min"] = df["service_time"] / 60.0
    else:
        df["service_time_min"] = (df["time:timestamp"] - df["start_timestamp"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
        
    df["log_service_time"] = np.log(df["service_time_min"].clip(lower=0.1))
    return df

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Step 2: Batching Detection via PM4Py
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
def detect_batching_behavior(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """Detect sequential and concurrent batching by resource and activity."""
    # Discover activity batches with max inter-arrival of 120 seconds
    batches = pm4py.discover_batches(
        df,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
        resource_key="org:resource",
        max_inter_arrival_time=120
    )
    
    # Flag batched events in dataframe
    batched_event_indices = set()
    for batch in batches:
        # Check for sequential batching threshold (>5 events in cluster)
        if len(batch.events) >= 5:
            for ev in batch.events:
                batched_event_indices.add(ev)
                
    df["is_batched"] = df.index.isin(batched_event_indices).astype(int)
    return df, batches

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Step 3: Econometric Modeling - Disentangling Competing Drivers
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
def run_econometric_disentanglement(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """Fit fixed-effects regressions controlling for case-mix, sequence, and WIP."""
    # Model 1: Log Service Time (OLS with Resource Fixed Effects)
    formula_service = (
        "log_service_time ~ sequence_position_within_shift + minutes_since_shift_start "
        "+ wip_at_start + queue_length_at_start + case_complexity_score + priority "
        "+ is_batched + circadian_sin + circadian_cos "
        "+ C(activity_family) + C(shift_id) + C(org_resource)"
    )
    model_service = smf.ols(formula=formula_service, data=df).fit(cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": df["shift_id"]})
    
    # Model 2: Error Probability (Logistic Regression with Resource Experience Interactions)
    formula_error = (
        "error_flag ~ sequence_position_within_shift + minutes_since_shift_start "
        "+ wip_at_start + case_complexity_score + is_batched "
        "+ queue_length_at_start * resource_experience_months "
        "+ circadian_sin + circadian_cos + C(shift_id) + C(activity_family)"
    )
    model_error = smf.logit(formula=formula_error, data=df).fit(disp=False)
    
    # Model 3: Downstream 7-day Rework Probability
    formula_rework = (
        "rework_flag ~ error_flag + wip_at_start + case_complexity_score "
        "+ sequence_position_within_shift + is_batched + C(org_resource)"
    )
    model_rework = smf.logit(formula=formula_rework, data=df).fit(disp=False)
    
    return model_service, model_error, model_rework

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Step 4: Process Mining - DFG, Rework Matrices, and Log Clustering
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
def run_process_mining_diagnostics(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """Discover performance DFGs, activity rework patterns, and trace clusters."""
    # 1. Activity-level rework counts
    rework_cases = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(
        df,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )
    
    # 2. Performance DFG under Low WIP vs High WIP
    df_low_wip = df[df["wip_at_start"] <= 30]
    df_high_wip = df[df["wip_at_start"] > 70]
    
    perf_dfg_low, start_low, end_low = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
        df_low_wip,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )
    
    perf_dfg_high, start_high, end_high = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
        df_high_wip,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )
    
    # 3. Trace clustering to isolate deviant rework pathways
    clustered_logs = pm4py.cluster_log(
        df,
        no_clusters=3,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )
    
    return rework_cases, (perf_dfg_low, perf_dfg_high), clustered_logs
```

### 1. Strictly cap shift length for all workers
- **Expected Benefit:** Caps physical and cognitive exhaustion; eliminates the extreme fatigue tail where judgment degrades; reduces probability of prolonged exposure to sleep deprivation during night and extended day shifts.
- **Operational Risk:** If shift length is capped without expanding hourly processing capacity, pending cases are forcibly truncated, causing sudden shift-boundary handovers. Handover friction leads to context loss, increased case start-up latency on subsequent shifts, and higher carryover backlog.
- **Monitoring Indicators:**
  - `shift_duration_minutes` distribution.
  - Count of `case_handover_events` per shift boundary.
  - Mean service time and error rate in the final 30 minutes before capped departure.
  - Volume of unassigned carryover WIP transferred to the subsequent shift log.

### 2. Move complex cases away from the first hour of each shift
- **Expected Benefit:** Conceptually provides workers a "warm-up" period on standardized, low-cognitive-load claims to establish operational flow.
- **Operational Risk:** **Strongly counter-productive.** High early service times reflect senior workers methodically resolving overnight complex carryover. Shifting complex cases away from Hour 1 forces them into later hours (Hours 5–8). During the Day shift, this coincides with peak incoming demand (WIP > 70). As empirical data proves, high complexity combined with high WIP produces an unacceptable 10.9% error rate. Deferring complex cases guarantees quality disasters during peak load.
- **Monitoring Indicators:**
  - Hour-of-shift complexity distribution (`mean(case_complexity_score)` by hour).
  - SLA breach rate for high-priority claims (`time_to_first_action` for `priority == 'High'`).
  - Error rate on high-complexity claims processed during hours 5–8.

### 3. Add a queue-pressure alert when WIP > 70
- **Expected Benefit:** Provides situational awareness to team leads; triggers automated operational protocols (e.g., dynamic claim throttling, supervisor case-routing, selective overtime activation).
- **Operational Risk:** If displayed directly to frontline workers without offering capacity relief or load-shedding, the alert induces acute psychological stress and panic. Workers accelerate their satisficing heuristics, rush decisions, and engage in batching workarounds, worsening error rates.
- **Monitoring Indicators:**
  - Frequency and duration of `WIP_alert_active` flags.
  - Step-change in `mean(service_time)` immediately following alert activation (monitoring for panic rushing).
  - Differential error rate and rework rate between `WIP > 70` periods with and without alert broadcasts.

### 4. Force random assignment of cases to reduce selection bias
- **Expected Benefit:** Balances case difficulty evenly across the workforce; prevents cherry-picking of easy claims; eliminates confounding selection bias in observational performance evaluations.
- **Operational Risk:** **Operationally damaging.** Random assignment eliminates skill-based routing. Assigning high-complexity claims (score > 0.70) to junior personnel results in massive error spikes (Juniors show 12.4% error rates under stress), frequent referral loops (`Specialist Referral`), and severe customer friction. Conversely, assigning trivial tasks to senior personnel wastes high-cost expertise.
- **Monitoring Indicators:**
  - Standard deviation of `case_complexity_score` across resources (target = 0 under perfect randomization).
  - Referral loop frequency (`Specialist Referral` count per case).
  - Junior resource rework and error rates stratified by case complexity score.

### 5. Introduce mandatory micro-breaks in the final two hours
- **Expected Benefit:** Restores attentional capacity and executive function; interrupts the compounding cycle of late-shift cognitive fatigue; prevents perceptual tunneling.
- **Operational Risk:** Removing workers from production during the final two hours temporarily halts processing capacity, causing pending queue length to spike even higher unless arrival flow is throttled. Workers returning from micro-breaks may rush even faster to clear the resulting backlog before shift end.
- **Monitoring Indicators:**
  - Compliance rate logged via activity timestamp gaps (`micro_break_duration`).
  - Pre-break vs. post-break error rate comparison in sequence positions 50+.
  - Slope of queue growth during scheduled break windows (`delta(queue_length) / delta(time)`).

### 6. Defer low-priority quality checks to the next shift
- **Expected Benefit:** Relieves operational congestion during the final 45 minutes of the shift; stops workers from conducting superficial, rushed end-of-shift quality checks.
- **Operational Risk:** **Violates core process integrity.** Quality checks are the primary defense against error escapes. Deferring quality checks delays error detection, allows flawed decisions to proceed to `Customer Callback` or `Close Case`, and converts easily correctable errors into expensive 7-day downstream rework loops. Furthermore, it inflates carryover queues for incoming shifts.
- **Monitoring Indicators:**
  - `Quality Check` latency (`timestamp(Quality Check) - timestamp(Eligibility Decision)`).
  - Shift-over-shift carryover volume of pending QC events.
  - Downstream 7-day rework rate (`rework_flag == True`) and error escape rate to customers.

### 7. Add an extra senior reviewer during day-shift demand peaks
- **Expected Benefit:** **Highly effective operational intervention.** Directly injects high-tier processing capacity precisely when arrival demand outstrips supply (14:00–17:00). Prevents queue length from crossing the critical threshold of 70, protects junior workers from queue-induced panic, and provides senior oversight for complex claims and Bot_Risk overrides.
- **Operational Risk:** Marginal increase in labor cost; requires dynamic scheduling and accurate demand forecasting to ensure the senior resource is deployed during actual peak influx.
- **Monitoring Indicators:**
  - Day shift ending queue length (`mean(queue_length)` at 17:00; target < 45).
  - Junior error rate during peak hours (14:00–17:00).
  - Proportion of cases where `wip_at_start > 70` during the Day shift.
  - Net reduction in organization-wide 7-day rework volume.

### 8. Penalize workers with higher late-shift error rates
- **Expected Benefit:** None. A punitive managerial approach based on a fundamentally flawed understanding of system dynamics.
- **Operational Risk:** **Catastrophic organizational and ethical failure.** Violates Goodhart’s and Campbell’s Laws. Penalizing workers for errors caused by systemic queue pressure and staffing imbalances leads to:
  - Active concealment of errors and suppression of `Rework Correction` logging.
  - Extreme risk aversion: workers refuse or stall complex cases, transferring them to peers.
  - Artificial slowdowns or fraudulent batch-logging to manipulate sequence positions.
  - Demoralization, burnout, and acute attrition, particularly among junior and mid-level staff.
- **Monitoring Indicators:**
  - Unrecorded rework detected through post-close customer complaints.
  - Rate of contested case reassignments and rejected handovers.
  - Voluntary employee turnover and absenteeism metrics.

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

Evaluating human workers using raw event-log performance metrics without understanding systemic confounders poses severe ethical, legal, and operational risks.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    ETHICAL & METHODOLOGICAL PITFALLS                               |
+--------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Selection Bias           | Seniors take difficult carryovers; Juniors get simple batched cases.    |
| Measurement Distortion   | Batching and delayed logging compress recorded time, masking real work. |
| Management Failure       | Under-staffing creates high WIP; workers are blamed for queue pressure. |
| Automation Trap          | Bot_Risk failures under load force flawed human overrides.              |
| Punitive Perversion      | Punishing error rates induces gaming, concealment, and toxic turnover.  |
+--------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. Selection Bias and Confounding Case-Mix
Attributing long service times or early-shift errors to worker incompetence commits the **ecological fallacy**. Senior workers deliberately take on the most difficult cases (mean complexity 0.72) and high-priority carryovers at shift start to shield the team and resolve critical customer issues. If management evaluates raw speed, these senior workers appear less efficient than junior workers who later process low-complexity claims (0.43). Without adjusting for `case_complexity_score`, `priority`, and `activity_family`, performance rankings reflect case assignment patterns rather than individual capability.

### 2. Measurement Error and Logging Artifacts
Event logs capture transactional timestamps, not true human cognitive effort:
- **Batching Artifacts:** Juniors H and I process 9–18 Document Reviews in rapid sequence. Their logged service time appears extraordinarily fast (e.g., seconds per case). In reality, the worker reviewed the physical/digital documents beforehand and simply clicked through the UI in batch mode, or rubber-stamped the queue without review. Measuring service time from start-to-complete timestamps creates an illusion of high productivity while masking substandard work.
- **End-of-Shift Logging Clumping:** Quality checks deferred to the end of the shift result in bursts of logged activity in the final 45 minutes. These timestamps reflect data entry flurries rather than real-time case progression.

### 3. Queue Pressure as an Organizational and Management Failure
The dramatic surge in late-shift error rates (up to 12.4% for Juniors and 10.9% for high-complexity claims under high WIP) is an **induced systemic failure**, not an individual moral hazard. Management is responsible for scheduling capacity to match arrival patterns. When Day shift staffing is insufficient to handle late afternoon demand peaks, queue length doubles (38 to 75). Workers face an impossible operational dilemma: either slow down to maintain quality (causing queues to explode, breaching SLAs, and leaving unmanageable carryover), or accelerate to keep the queue moving (inevitably causing errors). Blaming frontline workers for errors created by structural understaffing is ethically indefensible.

### 4. Flawed Automation and Shared Responsibility
Bot_Risk performs automated Risk Classification. Under high queue pressure (>70), human corrections of Bot_Risk spike. This indicates either that Bot_Risk classification quality degrades under atypical edge-case loads, or that rushed humans make erroneous overrides. In either case, the error cannot be attributed solely to the human worker. Holding human workers liable for errors arising from flawed human-AI interaction protocols creates an unjust operational environment.

### 5. Punitive Policies, Gaming, and System Degradation
Instituting punitive measures based on raw late-shift error rates triggers predictable dysfunctional behaviors:
- **Metric Gaming:** Workers optimize their logged behavior to protect themselves rather than serve the customer. They may artificially fragment cases, delay clicking "complete" until the next shift, or cherry-pick easy cases from the shared queue.
- **Error Concealment:** If an error is penalized, workers will avoid logging `Rework Correction` or `Quality Check` defects, allowing flawed claims to escape to customers.
- **Psychological Unsafety:** Blaming workers destroys trust, exacerbates stress-induced cognitive fatigue, and drives out capable staff, increasing turnover and worsening understaffing.

# 6. Monitoring dashboard specification

To support balanced, ethical, and operationally rigorous decision-making, we define a continuous process-mining monitoring dashboard.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                 ENTERPRISE PM MONITORING DASHBOARD                                |
+----------------------------------+--------------------------------+-------------------------------+
| PANEL 1: WORKLOAD & CONGESTION   | PANEL 2: SPEED & PRODUCTIVITY  | PANEL 3: QUALITY & INTEGRITY  |
| - Dynamic WIP & Buffer Index     | - Complexity-Adjusted Service  | - Risk-Adjusted Error Rate    |
| - Bottleneck Waiting Times       | - Batching Workaround Monitor  | - 7-Day Downstream Recidivism |
+----------------------------------+--------------------------------+-------------------------------+
| PANEL 4: FAIRNESS & HANDOVER     | PANEL 5: AUTOMATION INTEGRITY  | PANEL 6: REAL-TIME ALERTS     |
| - Cognitive Load Gini Index      | - Bot_Risk Override & Accuracy | - Dynamic Congestion Throttles|
| - Handover Aging & Carryover WIP | - Human-AI Confusion Matrix    | - Fatigue / Micro-Break Guard |
+----------------------------------+--------------------------------+-------------------------------+
```

### Indicator 1: Complexity-Adjusted Service Time (CAST)
- **Definition & Purpose:** Normalized duration of active case processing, adjusted for intrinsic case complexity, priority weight, and activity family. Prevents penalizing staff for handling difficult cases.
- **Data Source & Event Log Fields:** `start_timestamp`, `time:timestamp`, `case_complexity_score`, `priority`, `concept:name`, `org:resource`.
- **PM4Py / Algorithmic Logic:**
  $$\text{ServiceTime} = \text{timestamp} - \text{start\_timestamp}$$
  Fit baseline GLM: $\ln(\text{ServiceTime}) = f(\text{Complexity}, \text{Priority}, \text{Activity})$.
  $$\text{CAST}_{ijk} = \frac{\text{Observed Service Time}_{ijk}}{\text{Expected Baseline Service Time}_{jk}}$$
- **Visualization Type:** Dual-axis control chart showing raw vs. complexity-adjusted service times over shift sequence position, disaggregated by resource seniority.
- **Thresholds & Operational Triggers:** Alert when CAST < 0.60 (flagging potential rushed processing or superficial review) or CAST > 1.80 across 3 consecutive events (flagging stuck cases or missing documentation).

### Indicator 2: Stage-to-Stage Queue Waiting Time & Bottleneck Latency
- **Definition & Purpose:** Passive idle time spent by cases residing in unassigned queues between activities (e.g., between `Initial Triage` and `Document Review`).
- **Data Source & Event Log Fields:** `time:timestamp` of event $n$, `start_timestamp` of event $n+1$, `case:concept:name`, `queue_length_at_start`.
- **PM4Py / Algorithmic Logic:** Calculated via `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time`. Aggregate mean and 95th percentile waiting time per transition pair across sliding 60-minute windows.
- **Visualization Type:** Performance Directly-Follows Graph (DFG) overlay where edge widths represent queue volume and color shading represents mean waiting time.
- **Thresholds & Operational Triggers:** Warning at Waiting Time > 45 min for standard cases; Critical alert at > 15 min for high-priority cases.

### Indicator 3: Dynamic Work-in-Progress (WIP) and Buffer Saturation Ratio
- **Definition & Purpose:** Real-time count of active and queued cases within the facility relative to standard processing capacity.
- **Data Source & Event Log Fields:** `time:timestamp`, `shift_id`, `wip_at_start`, `queue_length_at_start`.
- **PM4Py / Algorithmic Logic:** Derived using `pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` and active trace concurrency tracking.
  $$\text{Buffer Saturation Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current WIP}}{\text{Active Staff Count} \times \text{Target Concurrency per Worker (4)}}$$
- **Visualization Type:** Area chart of facility WIP across time, overlaid with capacity thresholds (Green: < 30, Amber: 30–70, Red: > 70).
- **Thresholds & Operational Triggers:** Amber Alert at WIP = 50 (supervisors monitor queue); Red Alert at WIP > 70 (triggers senior floating capacity and secondary routing).

### Indicator 4: Risk-Adjusted Error Rate (RAER)
- **Definition & Purpose:** Proportion of processed events tagged with `error_flag == True`, normalized by expected baseline error probability for the given case complexity and activity family.
- **Data Source & Event Log Fields:** `error_flag`, `case_complexity_score`, `activity_family`, `org:resource`, `shift_id`, `minutes_since_shift_start`.
- **PM4Py / Algorithmic Logic:** Compute expected errors via logistic regression baseline.
  $$\text{RAER} = \frac{\sum \text{Observed Errors}}{\sum P(\text{Error} \mid \text{Complexity}, \text{Activity})}$$
- **Visualization Type:** Heatmap of RAER indexed by shift hour (x-axis) and resource group / seniority tier (y-axis).
- **Thresholds & Operational Triggers:** Statistical Process Control (SPC) rule: alert if RAER exceeds upper control limit ($+3\sigma$ above historical mean) for 2 consecutive hours.

### Indicator 5: Downstream 7-Day Recidivism (Rework Loop Rate)
- **Definition & Purpose:** Frequency with which closed or triaged cases return for rework, correction, or appeal within 7 calendar days.
- **Data Source & Event Log Fields:** `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `rework_flag`, `time:timestamp`.
- **PM4Py / Algorithmic Logic:** `pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity` mapped to trace variant graphs. Calculate the proportion of cases containing repetitive execution of `Document Review`, `Eligibility Decision`, or `Rework Correction`.
- **Visualization Type:** Sankey diagram showing initial decision routing flowing into either `Close Case` or `Rework Correction` loops, stratified by processing shift.
- **Thresholds & Operational Triggers:** Target < 5.0%; Warning at 7.5%; Critical escalation at > 10.0% 7-day rework rate.

### Indicator 6: Case-Mix Complexity & Priority Drift Index
- **Definition & Purpose:** Continuous monitoring of incoming and in-process claim difficulty to detect shifts in operational burden across shifts and sequence positions.
- **Data Source & Event Log Fields:** `case_complexity_score`, `priority`, `shift_id`, `sequence_position_within_shift`.
- **PM4Py / Algorithmic Logic:** Rolling mean and interquartile range of `case_complexity_score` and percentage share of `priority == 'High'`, computed in bins of 10 sequence positions.
- **Visualization Type:** Multi-line trend chart tracking average complexity across sequence positions for Early, Day, Late, and Night shifts.
- **Thresholds & Operational Triggers:** Alert when first-hour complexity > 0.65 (indicates heavy carryover; prompts supervisor to balance workload).

### Indicator 7: Batching Workaround & Concurrency Intensity Metric
- **Definition & Purpose:** Detects sequential and simultaneous batch processing where workers bundle cases to bypass individual review.
- **Data Source & Event Log Fields:** `start_timestamp`, `time:timestamp`, `org:resource`, `concept:name`.
- **PM4Py / Algorithmic Logic:** Integrated execution of `pm4py.discover_batches(..., max_inter_arrival_time=120)`. Calculate the Batching Intensity Ratio:
  $$\text{BIR} = \frac{\text{Count of Events in Discovered Batches} \ge 5}{\text{Total Events Processed by Resource}}$$
- **Visualization Type:** Scatter plot of Inter-Event Arrival Time vs. Service Time per worker, highlighting clustered batch signatures in red.
- **Thresholds & Operational Triggers:** Flag any resource where BIR > 0.15 on non-automated activities, or where $\ge 8$ consecutive claims show identical timestamps.

### Indicator 8: Handover Carryover Volume and Queue Aging
- **Definition & Purpose:** Measures the volume, age, and complexity of unresolved cases transferred between shifts at operational boundaries.
- **Data Source & Event Log Fields:** `case:concept:name`, `time:timestamp`, `shift_id`, `shift_start_time`, `case_complexity_score`.
- **PM4Py / Algorithmic Logic:** Identify traces with active events spanning shift boundaries where no completion activity (`Close Case`, `Eligibility Decision`) occurred before the shift end time.
- **Visualization Type:** Stacked bar chart showing shift carryover volume by complexity tier (Low, Medium, High) at each shift boundary (06:00, 09:00, 14:00, 22:00).
- **Thresholds & Operational Triggers:** Warning when carryover at shift boundary > 40 cases; Critical alert when high-complexity carryover > 20 cases.

### Indicator 9: Worker-Load Fairness and Cognitive Equity Index (Gini Allocation)
- **Definition & Purpose:** Monitors equitable distribution of complex, high-stress claims across on-duty personnel to prevent burnout and unequal exposure to difficult work.
- **Data Source & Event Log Fields:** `org:resource`, `case_complexity_score`, `shift_id`, `resource_experience_months`.
- **PM4Py / Algorithmic Logic:** Compute the Gini coefficient of cumulative case complexity scores assigned to resources within each shift:
  $$G = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} |c_i - c_j|}{2n^2 \bar{c}}$$
  where $c_i$ is the sum of complexity scores handled by worker $i$.
- **Visualization Type:** Lorenz curve of complexity allocation per shift, accompanied by a fairness gauge (0.0 = perfect equity, 1.0 = total concentration).
- **Thresholds & Operational Triggers:** Target Gini $\le 0.25$; Warning at Gini > 0.40 (triggers automated re-balancing in case dispatching engine).
