## 2. Rule Interpretation

**RR1 – Drafter/Approver Separation**  
- Enforced at event timestamp: No human may perform both `Draft_Change_Request` and `Approve_Change` for the same change case.

**RR2 – Implementer/Verifier Separation**  
- Enforced at event timestamp: No human may perform both `Implement_Change` and `Verify_Implementation` for the same case.

**RR3 – HighRisk Approval Authority**  
- For `risk = HIGH` and `scope` affecting critical assets (e.g., production equipment, cooling system), `Approve_Change` must be executed by the QM (`Asha`) or a **valid** QM delegate.  
- **Validity criteria**:  
  1. **Effective interval** must enclose the approval timestamp.  
  2. **Scope coverage** must include the case’s scope.  
  3. **Principal absence** is required (if the principal is on leave, the delegate may act).  
  4. **No prohibited subdelegation** – a delegate may not act on behalf of another delegate unless expressly allowed.  
- A later record (e.g., delegation entry in QMS DB) retroactively proves delegation only if the interval truly covers the event.  

**RR4 – Emergency Substitution**  
- Applies when `emergency = true`.  
  - **Implementer** may be the Incident Commander (IC) or a valid IC delegate.  
  - **Retrospective QM Review** must occur **within two business days** (MonFri, excluding site holidays). Business days are counted from the `Implement_Change` timestamp.  
  - The emergency flag must stem from a credible safety rationale (not merely a policy shortcut).  

**RR5 – Identity Evidence**  
- Service accounts are nonhuman. Conformance is allowed **only** if a **reliable session attribution** ties the action to an authorized human (badge log, FIDO token, VPN correlation with that human).  
- All aliases for a human are **normalized** (e.g., `b.koch`  `u_bk`) before segregationofduties checks.  

**RR6 – Suspicion versus Proof**  
- Indicators such as repeated pairs, reciprocal approvals, shared workstations, or concentrated handovers are **suspicious patterns** but **not proof** of collusion. Supporting evidence (e.g., session logs, absence records) is required to elevate suspicion to proven misconduct.  

**BusinessDay Calculation for RR4**  
- Count consecutive calendar days starting the next **business day** after `Implement_Change`. Skip weekends and sitespecific holidays (none reported for 11–14Aug).  

**Later Delegation Proof**  
- A delegation record created **after** an event cannot retroactively validate that event unless the record is timestamped **earlier** (e.g., via preevent database entry) or the effective interval explicitly includes the past event. In practice, the delegation must exist **prior** to the approval timestamp (e.g., DQM01 present before 11Aug).

## 4. Collusion and AlternativeExplanation Analysis

**Repeated Pair: Clara  Ben (Highrisk production changes)**  
- **Ben’s sickleave anomaly (CHG506)**: Ben approved a change on 13Aug while officially on sick leave.  
  - *Ben’s benign explanation*: The approval could have been performed by a trusted colleague using Ben’s credentials as an emergency proxy, not a sign of systematic collusion.  
  - *Ben’s adverse explanation*: Ben used his leave period to surreptitiously approve changes to maintain workflow continuity, indicating collusion.  
  - **Discriminating evidence**: Presence of a VPN log showing Ben’s workstation IP in an office network (consistent with onsite shift) vs evidence of credential sharing (absent).  
- **Serviceaccount ambiguity (CHG503 vs CHG509)**  
  - *Benign*: Shared workstation WSDM04 allowed Diego’s session to be captured by svc_qms without reauthentication, leading to ambiguous attribution.  
  - *Adverse*: Possible intentional redirection of verification to a nonhuman account to hide a human’s involvement, implicating collusion.  
  - **Discriminating evidence**: Badge/FIDO log for CHG509 (strong) versus lack thereof for CHG503 (weak).  

**Reflexive Approvals (Clara drafts, Ben approves; Ben drafts, Clara approves)**  
- *Ben’s selfapproval in CHG508* (lowrisk training) may be a procedural shortcut authorized offrecord.  
- *Adverse scenario*: Ben may be exploiting a known loophole to approve his own work, indicating collusion with himself (selfdamage).  

**Shared Workstations / Credential Sharing**  
- Multiple cases involve workstation WSDM04 (shared by Diego and others).  
  - *Benign*: Standard practice for emergency response where multiple personnel may use the same console.  
  - *Adverse*: Persistent sharing suggests a lack of individual authentication, enabling unauthorized actors to masquerade.  

**Absence Conflicts**  
- Asha’s leave overlaps with several highrisk approvals (CHG504). Ben’s valid delegation covers this, but the VPN session on 13Aug hints at possible credential sharing.  
- *Benign*: VPN session could be legitimate if Asha shared credentials temporarily for urgent cases.  
- *Adverse*: If the session was unauthorized, it constitutes collusion with a delegate (Ben acting on behalf of a principal who did not consent).  

**Alternative Explanations**  
- **Technical Glitch**: Delegation timestamps entered incorrectly (e.g., DIC03 interval mistyped), causing apparent violations that are actually data errors.  
- **Policy Ambiguity**: Lowrisk training changes may be exempt from drafter/approver separation, justifying CHG508.  
- **Emergency Overreach**: CHG505’s emergency flag might have been entered erroneously, leading to improper approval and review timing.  

**Evidence to Discriminate**  
1. **Session logs** linking every `svc_qms` action to a human (already available for CHG509).  
2. **VPN/Audit logs** showing who logged into each workstation at the relevant timestamps.  
3. **Delegation database timestamps** confirming they predate the events they cover.  
4. **Emergency justification document** (if any) attached to CHG505.

    # Simple filter: check if the same resource appears in consecutive Draft/Approve events for a case
    key = lambda row: (row['case:concept:name'], row['concept:name'])
    filtered = []
    seen = {}
    for idx, row in activity_log.iterrows():
        key_val = key(row)
        if key_val not in seen:
            seen[key_val] = row['org:resource']
            filtered.append(row)
        else:
            if seen[key_val] != row['org:resource']:
                filtered.append(row)
            else:
                # same actor for both steps  violation
                pass
    return pd.DataFrame(filtered)

## 6. Control Improvements

1. **Delegation Creation & Timing**  
   - Require delegation records to be **precreated** with explicit **effective start and end timestamps** before any associated case activity.  
   - Automate a check that the delegation interval **covers** the approval/implement timestamps; otherwise autoreject the activity and trigger an alert.  

2. **PrincipalAvailability Integration**  
   - Integrate leave/sickstatus APIs with the changecontrol system so that when a principal is on leave, the system **automatically** presents only valid delegates for approval fields.  
   - Flag any attempt by a principal to perform an action while on leave (except for documented emergency overrides).  

3. **Alias Management**  
   - Enforce a **single canonical alias** per human; implement a governance process to retire obsolete aliases and update all logs accordingly.  
   - Store aliastocanonical mappings in a central registry with version control.  

4. **ServiceAccount Attribution**  
   - Require a **sessionlinking artifact** (badge/FIDO token log, VPN login timestamp) for any action performed by a service account.  
   - Store these artifacts alongside the change event in an immutable audit log.  

5. **Emergency Review Timing**
