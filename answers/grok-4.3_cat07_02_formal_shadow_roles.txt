# 2. Core versus peripheral activities
Routine Frontline Handlers treat A1/A2/A4/A12 as core (high volume, low complexity); A8 and A13 appear only under load or error. Complex Technical Resolvers own A3/A5/A10 as core; A6 is peripheral and limited to the senior member. Security & Escalation Specialist performs A6 almost exclusively; other activities are rare and high-stakes. Commercial Exception Handler centers on A7/A8; substitution data shows these require pricing certification not held by substitutes. Hybrid Senior Agent maintains a deliberately broad core set that overlaps multiple domains. Approval & Governance Gate keeps A9 as the sole core activity; everything else is oversight or rework. Quality & Knowledge Gate centers on A11/A14; earlier activities are diagnostic only. Cross-functional Coordinator has no single core activity but owns coordination and preliminary approvals on escalated paths. Automation Boundary is restricted to A1/A2; any downstream work would violate its system scope.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles
R07_Giorgio functions as a commercial shadow: he executes pricing and negotiation work during Fatima’s absence and on complex front-office cases, providing resilience but risking longer cycle times and inconsistent decisions because he lacks formal pricing certification. R13_Mateo acts as cross-functional escalation broker, filtering low-risk security referrals for Erik and performing preliminary approvals later rechecked by Hana; this reduces false escalations yet creates two-day delays on critical cases and blurs formal authority lines. R05_Erik remains a rare but critical specialist whose low volume masks high organizational risk exposure; his interventions are essential yet create single-point bottlenecks when load spikes. Additional shadow elements include the temporary agents (R11/R12) who silently absorb volume yet generate downstream rework, and the quality analysts who gatekeep knowledge yet have no formal authority over earlier technical decisions.

# 4. Ambiguous assignments and alternative interpretations
R07_Giorgio could be viewed either as an extended Frontline Handler with commercial privileges or as a permanent Commercial Exception Handler embedded in Front Office; substitution data plus the 0.71 similarity to Fatima versus 0.84 similarity to Amira would decide. R13_Mateo could be classified as a second Approval Gate or as a pure Coordinator; the fact that 13 of his A9 events were later rechecked by Hana and that he filters security referrals supports the broker interpretation over formal approver. R01_Amira could be treated as a senior Routine Frontline Handler or as an informal backup Commercial Handler; her higher medium/high-case count and A7/A8 activity relative to Ben would distinguish the two.
