## 1. Analytical assumptions

**Case notion:** In this OCEL 2.0 snapshot, a *case* is identified with the primary participant of an activity – e.g., an **Order** (O1O4) that is the source of creation, invoicing, payment, etc. However, the snapshot also contains *shared* elements (packages, deliveries, invoices, payments) that service multiple orders. Thus a single case notion may either be ordercentric or objectcentric, but both perspectives coexist and must be examined without privileging one a priori.

**Convergence:** Convergence occurs when multiple activities on different object types that logically belong to the same business process collapse into a single, coherent trajectory when observed through a particular object lens (e.g., Ordercentric flattening). It is achieved when the O2O relations are faithfully propagated or when the chosen object naturally aggregates the relevant events.

**Divergence:** Divergence arises when flattening on a less central object splits a single business event into several disjoint traces, or when the same logical event is duplicated across multiple objecttype lenses, leading to multiple, partially overlapping process views. This can inflate event frequencies, truncate lifecycles, or obscure crossorder relationships.

**Treatment of copied shared events:** The problem statement specifies that a shared event is *copied* into every relevant case when flattening on an object type to which that event is directly related. For our analysis we treat these copies as **evidence of aggregation** (helpful for visibility) but also as a source of **duplication** that may bias frequency counts and DFG traversal. Both aspects must be explicitly flagged during interpretation.

**Allowed analytical transformations:** We permit:
- **Relation propagation** through O2O links *only as an optional transformation* (e.g., automatically adding “packs” or “carries” edges to the flattened log) to highlight crossorder dependencies. This is not the default but can be toggled to test convergence risk.
- **Event deduplication** where copies are identified by identical timestamps and identical participant sets across different object lenses.

## 2. Object-type comparison matrix

| Object type | Natural business question | What is preserved | What is lost | Convergence risk | Divergence risk | Expected event duplication | Expected DFG/Performance distortion | Overall suitability |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Order** | “What is the endtoend fulfilment for each order?” | All creation, invoicing, payment, and closure activities that explicitly involve the order (e.g., e01e31 for O1O4). | Implicit crosspackage and crossdelivery relationships that are not directly attached to a single order (e.g., shared package P3 involves O2 and O3). | **Low** – most ordercentric events are directly attached, giving a coherent sequence per order. | **Medium** – because shared packages and deliveries generate duplicated subpaths; also, partial payments cause split payment events that diverge per order. | **High** – events like dispatch and delivery that involve multiple orders (e.g., e16) will appear in each order’s trace, inflating count. | **Moderate distortion** – DFG will contain redundant branches for shared packages; performance (trace length) is inflated, but business logic remains clear. | **High** – best for orderlevel KPIs (lead time, FSMR) despite duplication. |
| **Item** | “How does each item move from order to delivery?” | Finegrained packing and movement events per item (e.g., I11  P1  D1). | Highlevel orderlevel decisions, invoicing, and payment events that are not itemspecific. | **Medium** – itemcentric flattening will converge only on packing/dispatch/confusion steps; invoicing/payment steps are missing. | **High** – a single item (e.g., I11) appears in multiple orders only via indirect O2O links; without propagation, divergence is severe. | **Low** – item appears only in its explicit events (e.g., e05e15). | **Low distortion** for itemlevel logistics but **high loss** of macroprocess context. | **Medium** – useful for inventory flow analysis but insufficient for orderlevel performance. |
| **Package** | “Which orders share packages and how are they delivered?” | All packing and dispatch actions per package (P1P4). | Orderspecific creation and invoicing events that are not linked to packages (e.g., creation of O1 without package context). | **High** – packages naturally aggregate orders, providing good convergence for fulfillment steps. | **Medium** – because packages may be overrepresented in dispatch events (shared deliveries) leading to multiple parallel traces for the same delivery (e.g., D2 carries P2 and P3). | **Medium** – shared packages cause duplication of dispatch events across order traces if flattened per package without deduplication. | **Moderate distortion** – DFG will merge across orders, but without order view, measuring ordercentric KPIs is difficult. | **High** – excellent for logistics and consolidation analysis (crossorder packages, consolidated delivery). |
| **Delivery** | “When are deliveries dispatched and confirmed?” | All dispatch and confirmation events tied to a delivery (D1D3). | Creation, itempacking, and payment events that precede or follow delivery without direct attachment. | **Medium** – deliverycentric view aligns well with final steps (dispatch, confirmation) but diverges early (order creation). | **High** – a single delivery may involve multiple orders (e.g., D2 for P2 and P3) causing underrepresentation of orderlevel divergences. | **Medium** – duplicate dispatch actions when orders share packages are reflected in multiple delivery traces. | **Moderate distortion** – timeline of ordercentric phases is compressed; useful for carrier performance but not for orderspecific metrics. | **Medium** – good for carrierlevel metrics, less for orderlevel. |
| **Invoice** | “Which orders are billed, and how are payments structured?” | All invoicing and payment events linked to invoices (V1V3). | Order creation, item addition, and package dispatch events that are not invoicerelated. | **Low** – invoicing naturally aggregates orders (O1, O2, O3 for V2, etc.). | **Low** – because invoices already consolidate multiple orders, divergence is minimal. | **Low** – payment events are duplicated where applicable (V2 paid in two instalments). | **Low distortion** – DFG will show consolidated billing/payment paths, preserving highlevel flow. | **High** – optimal for financial KPIs (billing cycles, payment latency) but loses logistics granularity. |
| **Payment** | “How are payments recorded and settled per order?” | All settlement events per payment (M1M4). | Nonfinancial steps (order creation, packing) that are not paymentrelated. | **Low** – payment logs directly reflect settlement steps per order. | **Low** – payment events rarely diverge because they are tied to specific invoices. | **Low** – partial payments (M2) and completion (M3) cause multiple events for same invoice but still ordered. | **Low distortion** – clear timeline of financial closure; ideal for payment analytics. | **High** – best for financial reconciliation, but does not show fulfillment chain. |

**Key takeaways from the matrix**

- **Order** and **Invoice**/ **Payment** are the most *businessquestionaligned* for endtoend analysis, preserving highlevel decision points while offering some preservation of crossorder information (via shared packages/invoices).  
- **Package** excels at showing *crossorder consolidation* but will require extra steps (relation propagation) to keep orderlevel KPIs intact.  
- **Delivery** provides a *finalstage convergence* but loses early process context.  
- **Item** offers the finest granularity but suffers from severe divergence unless O2O relations are propagated.  

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

1. **Crossorder package duplication (P3)**  
   - **Flattening:** Packagecentric.  
   - **Events involved:** e13 (pack package for I21 from O2 and I31 from O3).  
   - **DFG edge:** P3  Dispatch (D2). Because P3 is linked to two orders (O2, O3), flattening per package will produce two separate trace branches for the same dispatch event (one for O2, one for O3).  
   - **Effect:** Frequency inflation of the dispatch activity; lifecycle appears split, potentially misestimating order lead time.  
   - **Nature:** Harmful for orderlevel performance metrics but informative for logistics consolidation analysis.

2. **Shared delivery (D2) consolidation**  
   - **Flattening:** Deliverycentric.  
   - **Events involved:** e16 (dispatch involving P2 and P3, thus D2).  
   - **DFG edge:** D2  Confirmation (e24). When flattened per delivery, D2’s activities will be duplicated across the orders it serves (O1, O2, O3), leading to multiple parallel confirmation traces.  
   - **Effect:** Overcounting of confirmations, potential bias in throughput calculations.  
   - **Nature:** Harmful for deliverylevel performance, but the duplication highlights the presence of consolidation, useful for carrier planning.

3. **Consolidated invoicing (V2)**  
   - **Flattening:** Invoicecentric.  
   - **Events involved:** e19 (invoice V2 for O2 and O3) and payment events e22 (partial) and e25 (completion).  
   - **DFG edge:** V2  Record Payment (M2, M3). Flattening per invoice aggregates the two orders, but the payment events are still tied to the invoice; no extra duplication occurs, thus convergence is high.  
   - **Effect:** Minimal distortion – provides a clean view of payment phase for the combined orders.  
   - **Nature:** Informative; preserves financial convergence.

4. **Partial payments (M2 & M3)**  
   - **Flattening:** Paymentcentric.  
   - **Events involved:** e22 (partial settlement of V2) and e25 (completion of V2).  
   - **DFG edge:** M2  M3. The presence of two sequential payment events within the same invoice leads to a split lifecycle: the first payment does not close the invoice, so the DFG shows a “partial  complete” transition.  
   - **Effect:** Lifetime of the invoice case is artificially elongated, potentially misleading latency calculations.  
   - **Nature:** Harmful for paymentcycle KPIs but necessary to capture the business reality of instalment payments.

5. **Nearsingleton lifecycle (Order O4)**  
   - **Flattening:** Ordercentric.  
   - **Events involved:** O4 appears in e04, e10e31 without any shared packages, deliveries, or invoices beyond its own.  
   - **DFG edge:** O4  Create Order  Add Item  Pack Package  Dispatch  Issue Invoice  Record Payment  Close Order.  
   - **Effect:** The trace is virtually uninterrupted, so any downstream bias (e.g., duplication from shared objects) does not affect O4. However, if one were to aggregate across all orders, the singleton would dominate frequency statistics, potentially skewing overall process metrics.  
   - **Nature:** Contextdependent – useful for outlier detection but risky if aggregated without weighting.

6. **Duplication of shared events due to copying rule**  
   - **Flattening:** Any object type (e.g., Order).  
   - **Events involved:** Dispatch events e15, e16, e17, which involve shared packages (P1P4) and thus are copied to each order participating.  
   - **DFG edge:** Order  Dispatch  Delivery. Each order’s trace contains multiple dispatch nodes for the same physical dispatch activity.  
   - **Effect:** Inflated trace length and activity frequency, distorting “average” leadtime calculations.  
   - **Nature:** Harmful for performance metrics but beneficial for visibility of shared resources.

7. **Loss of earlystage decision context (Itemcentric flattening without propagation)**  
   - **Flattening:** Itemcentric.  
   - **Events involved:** Item addition events e05e10 are present, but ordercreation events e01e04 are not directly linked.  
   - **DFG edge:** I11  Pack  Dispatch. The missing upstream creation step breaks the causal chain, causing divergence in perceived order initiation time.  
   - **Effect:** Process timeline appears to start at item addition rather than order creation, leading to misestimated initiation latency.  
   - **Nature:** Harmful for highlevel initiation analysis unless the analyst explicitly adds the missing context via relation propagation.

8. **Performance bias from “createorder” only traces (Ordercentric with no later events)**  
   - **Flattening:** Ordercentric, filtering out events after closure (e.g., ignoring e28e31 if closed orders are excluded).  
   - **Events involved:** O1’s creation (e01) without subsequent fulfilment steps.  
   - **DFG edge:** O1  (no continuation).  
   - **Effect:** Underrepresentation of fulfilment phases, artificially improving ordercreation efficiency metrics.  
   - **Nature:** Harmful for endtoend efficiency assessments; corrective only if the analysis scope is limited to initiation.

## 4. Traditional versus objectcentric analysis portfolio

### Recommended flattened logs
- **Ordercentric log** (retain all orderrelated events). Use for orderlevel KPIs (lead time, firsttimefulfilment rate, resource allocation).  
- **Packagecentric log** (propagate “packs” and “carries” edges). Use for logistics consolidation, sharedresource utilisation, and crossorder bottleneck detection.  
- **Invoicecentric log** (propagate “bills” and payment relations). Use for financial reconciliation, payment latency, and creditdecision analytics.  
- **Paymentcentric log** (retain settlement events). Use for cashflow tracking, instalmentpayment patterns, and fraud detection.

### Questions each flattened log can answer safely
| Flattened log | Safe business questions |
|---|---|
| Ordercentric | What is the average time from order creation to dispatch? Which orders experience partial payments? How many orders are closed per day? |
| Packagecentric | Which packages serve multiple orders? What is the average dwell time per package between packing and dispatch? Which packages are bottlenecks in delivery consolidation? |
| Invoicecentric | How many orders are billed together? What is the median payment latency per invoice? How many invoices receive multiple payment instalments? |
| Paymentcentric | What proportion of invoices are settled in full vs. in instalments? What is the distribution of payment amounts? How often are payment completions delayed beyond the invoice due date? |

### Questions requiring objectinteraction summaries or OCDFG/OTG/ETOT graphs
- **Crossorder fulfilment pattern analysis** (e.g., which orders share the same package and delivery, and how that affects lead time).  
- **Resource utilisation across objects** (e.g., how many dispatch events are concurrent due to shared deliveries).  
- **Temporal overlap of financial vs. logistics phases** (e.g., payment completion vs. delivery confirmation).  

### Reconciliation of contradictory findings
1. **Map overlapping traces** – align ordercentric traces with packagecentric traces using the shared event timestamps (e.g., matching e16’s dispatch to D2’s carry).  
2. **Weighted aggregation** – apply frequency weights based on the proportion of shared vs. exclusive events (e.g., discount duplicated dispatch events by the number of orders they serve).  
3. **Layered visualization** – produce a layered process map where the outer layer shows orderlevel flow and inner layers highlight shared logistics and financial subflows, allowing analysts to contextsensitive interpret each layer.  

## 5. Leadobject decision

**Decision:** **B. Use a primary lead object plus explicitly secondary lifecycles.**  

### Rationale
- **Primary lead object:** **Order** – it naturally aggregates the most critical business activities (creation, invoicing, payment, closure) and aligns with managerial KPIs. The matrix shows that Ordercentric flattening preserves highlevel decision points while maintaining low divergence for financial events (Invoice/Payment).  
- **Secondary lifecycles:** **Package** and **Invoice** – both provide essential crossorder and financial aggregation that cannot be captured solely by Order traces. By explicitly incorporating these secondary lifecycles, we avoid the “singleton” distortion of Orderonly analysis (especially for O4) and mitigate the duplication risks identified in the concrete distortion analysis.  

### Supporting evidence
- **Convergence risk:** Packagecentric analysis (high convergence for logistics) complements Order’s low convergence risk, while Invoicecentric analysis (low divergence) reinforces Order’s financial pathway, reducing overall divergence.  
- **Practical utility:** Orders define the business contracts; packages illustrate how those contracts are physically realised; invoices record the financial settlement. Together they give a *holistic* endtoend view without forcing a single object to carry all semantic weight.  

### Counterargument
- **Complexity:** Introducing two secondary lifecycles adds analytical overhead and may confuse stakeholders accustomed to a single, monolithic case definition.  

**Residual risk:** Overreliance on Ordercentric metrics could still obscure finegrained logistics inefficiencies if Package analysis is not thoroughly integrated. This risk can be mitigated by maintaining separate logs and performing crossvalidation (as outlined in the reconciliation step).  

### Conditions for change
- If future process changes eliminate shared packages (i.e., each order receives a dedicated package), the primary lead object could safely shift to **Item** or **Delivery** for greater granularity.  
- If financial consolidation moves to a single invoice per order, **Invoice** could become the sole secondary lifecycle, simplifying the model to a **PrimaryOrder / SecondaryInvoice** configuration.  
- If managerial focus shifts entirely to logistics (e.g., a carrierownership scenario), the decision could pivot to **PrimaryPackage / SecondaryOrder**, but this would require rebalancing KPIs accordingly.

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

1. **Removing or downweighting copied shared events**  
   - **Procedure:** Identify shared events (e.g., dispatch events that appear in multiple order traces) and remove duplicate instances based on identical timestamps and participant sets. Rerun DFG discovery and compute KPIs (average lead time, throughput).  
   - **Metric comparison:** Compare weighted vs. unweighted trace lengths and activity frequencies; assess impact on orderlevel latency (  5%).  

2. **Comparing direct E2O relations with an explicitly propagatedrelation variant**  
   - **Procedure:** Generate two versions of the ordercentric log: (a) direct flattening (no propagation) and (b) flattened log with “packs” and “carries” edges propagated using `apply_relation_propagation`.  
   - **Metric comparison:** Count of packagetodelivery connections, convergence scores (e.g., Jaccard similarity of DFGs), and identification of hidden bottlenecks (e.g., packages without clear dispatch path).  

3. **Changing the lead object**  
   - **Procedure:** Run the entire pipeline with **Package** as the primary lead object instead of **Order**, keeping secondary logs unchanged.  
   - **Metric comparison:** Evaluate how orderlevel KPIs (e.g., order completion time) shift; check for consistency between Packagecentric DFG and original Ordercentric DFG; use crossvalidation to decide if performance metrics diverge beyond an acceptable tolerance (e.g., ±10%).  

4. **Comparing frequency metrics based on events, unique objects, and total objects in the OCDFG**  
   - **Procedure:** Compute three frequency profiles for the OCDFG: (i) raw event counts, (ii) unique object participation per activity, (iii) total object count across all activities.  
   - **Metric comparison:** Analyse correlation (Pearson r) between these profiles; identify activities where event frequency heavily skews due to duplicated shared objects (e.g., dispatch). Use these insights to adjust weighting in downstream KPI calculations.  

**Validation steps**

- **Groundtruth alignment:** Use the explicit EVENTOBJECT RELATIONS table to manually verify that discovered DFG edges (e.g., P3  D2) match the source data.  
- **Statistical testing:** Perform paired ttests on KPI estimates (average lead time, payment latency) between original and sensitivityadjusted logs to confirm robustness.  
- **Stakeholder review:** Present the four sensitivity outcomes to domain experts (logistics, finance) to validate that identified distortions align with observed business realities (e.g., known crossorder consolidations).  

These checks collectively ensure that the chosen leadobject strategy (Order + Package/Invoice) is not an artefact of specific data representations and that any distortions are quantified and mitigated.
