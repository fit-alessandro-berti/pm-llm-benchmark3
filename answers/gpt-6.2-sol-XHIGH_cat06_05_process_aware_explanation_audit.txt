## 1. Leakage and temporal-consistency findings

The prediction cutoff is immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed` event. A feature must describe information the scorer could have retrieved by that cutoff. An activity used as an observed process signal must also be consistent with the ordered prefix. Severity reflects the risk to the forecast and its explanation.

| Item | Finding type | Severity (critical/high/medium/low) | Why | Required remediation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `total_case_duration_hours` | Direct future leakage | critical | Computed at closure. It encodes elapsed time beyond the cutoff and closely reflects the delay being predicted. | Exclude from training and scoring. Use registration-to-cutoff elapsed time for the amount of time already spent. |
| `appeal_seen_anywhere` (X) | Complete-trace leakage | critical | Both presence and absence across the case depend on the future. The decision required to enable an appeal has yet to occur in the cutoff prefix. | Drop the flag at this checkpoint. An observed appeal flag is usable only for a later prediction whose prefix includes a valid appeal. |
| `final_handler_team` | Closure-populated field | critical | The warehouse value is assigned at closure. Its 65% agreement with the cutoff team provides no historical availability guarantee. | Recover assignment history with effective and recorded times. Join the team visible at the cutoff or omit the feature. |
| `num_document_requests_total` | Complete-trace leakage | critical | The eventual count includes evidence cycles after coverage. A request count at this checkpoint is zero in the stated process. | Use cutoff-bounded counts at checkpoints where requests can already have occurred. Drop the total from the early model. |
| `queue_length_at_t0` (X, Y) | Congestion signal with join risk | high | A symmetric ±5 minute match may choose a future snapshot, and an earlier snapshot may have been published late. | Select the latest snapshot measured and available by the cutoff, at most five minutes old. Verify the snapshot's own input window and handle stale or missing readings explicitly. |
| `initial_damage_complexity` (X) | Admissible intake signal | low | Entered at registration and frozen before the cutoff. It is an intake assessment of case complexity. | Preserve the frozen value. Validate its predictive contribution across time and claim mix. |

| `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened` | Feasible conditional transition and future leakage | critical | The first event is observed. A fraud opening requires a trigger, and the prefix records no opening. Parallel work can affect adjacency. | Exclude the pair at this cutoff. A trigger independently recorded and visible by the cutoff can be evaluated as a separate predictor. |
| `Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded` | Feasible later pattern and future leakage | critical | Assessment can precede the initial decision. Both occur beyond the observed coverage prefix. | Exclude from early scoring. For a later prefix, check the ordering and which decision the pattern refers to. |

The global ranking describes attribution magnitude in the supplied model. It cannot identify early risk drivers until the model and explanations are rebuilt on eligible inputs. Removing a displayed term from a case explanation cannot repair the corresponding probability.

## 2. Process-model consistency checks

1. **Replay the prediction boundary.** Identify the first coverage completion using case IDs, event IDs, and reliable sequencing. Capture the transaction or stream position immediately after that event. Require each input record's effective time and actual first availability to the scorer to be within the cutoff. Replay the version visible then, including for fields later corrected or backfilled. Test late-arriving events with old business timestamps, a queue snapshot on each side of the cutoff, and events tied with the trigger. A later tied event can be outside the prefix even when its timestamp equals the cutoff timestamp.
2. **Trace data dependencies and test suffix invariance.** Inspect SQL, batch windows, aggregates, joins, encoders, and inputs to upstream scores. Replay claims with identical cutoff-visible inputs, including shared queue and history snapshots, while varying subsequent requests, decisions, appeals, and closure. Features and scores for a fixed model should remain identical. Repeat with different extraction dates and a late backfill. The label may change with the suffix and should flow through a separate training and evaluation pipeline.
3. **Check conformance against the normative process.** Use a stateful model or guarded alignment that permits the fast track, repeated request–receipt–validation cycles, and triggered fraud work interleaved with evidence or assessment. Verify that the observed prefix can extend to a valid case. Test feasible interleavings, a receipt without a matching request, closure before assessment, and activity after terminal closure. Report prefix violations independently of timestamp violations. For example, an appeal record already visible to the scorer without an eligible prior decision passes an availability check and fails the process guard.
4. **Check the state asserted by each explanation.** For observed patterns, require both endpoints and their relevant order inside the cutoff prefix. Specify whether an arrow denotes direct succession or eventual succession. At coverage, the evidence route can start with a request and assessment can follow on the fast track. Fraud opening is enabled only if its trigger and stage conditions are met, and otherwise may become possible later. An appeal or reconsideration is future-possible on the right branch and cannot occur next in the stated prefix. A closure followed by assessment within the same case is infeasible. Test these statuses independently of the explainer's contribution signs.
5. **Treat ties as partial orders.** Prefer a sequence index only after validating that it preserves business order. Otherwise use trusted transaction evidence or examine plausible orders consistent with the available metadata. Flag features that vary across unresolved orders, especially across the prediction boundary. For Z, the tied timestamps do not establish the reported edge, and reversing a pair does not establish that the required decision occurred. A model alignment can suggest a feasible ordering for diagnosis. It cannot supply missing event provenance.
6. **Check the semantics of decision repetitions.** Use event IDs, recorded outcome, and appeal linkage to distinguish duplicates from a later decision. Accept an initial Partial or Deny followed by a valid appeal and a linked reconsideration decision, even if both decisions share a coarse label or an outcome. Test and flag appeals after Approve or Withdrawn and repeated decisions with no supporting appeal under the stated model.

A directly-follows graph or footprint can flag suspicious adjacencies and suggest test cases. Its frequencies reflect observed behavior, including logging artifacts, and cannot establish the rules for triggers, loops, outcome guards, or parallel work.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

Predictive validity requires cutoff availability and evidence of generalization and calibration. A causal interpretation requires a design that identifies the effect of a specified change. Operational actionability also requires a feasible intervention and evidence that acting on the signal improves outcomes under real resource constraints. Fairness review examines which groups experience systematic errors or differential treatment, including downstream effects when scores guide work assignment.

| Class | Signals | Defensible interpretation and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Defensible predictive signal | Frozen initial damage complexity. Intake reserve and genuinely prior claim count after lineage repair. | These may capture differences in claim mix or history that predict breach out of time. Their contributions alone do not show that setting a different reserve or changing a complexity label would change settlement time. Prior history can also warrant proxy review. |
| Process-state or congestion signal | Registration-to-cutoff elapsed time, verified cutoff queue length, and the observed coverage milestone. | Age measures time already spent and can measure remaining slack once the target clock is confirmed. Queue length measures observed backlog and can help contextualize risk. The shared milestone identifies the scoring state and supplies no between-case variation in activity sequence here. The effect of redistributing work requires a separate evaluation. |
| Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review | Region and, once verified, channel or cutoff-visible team. | A proxy can improve prediction while reflecting differences in service access or case mix. Check incremental value, subgroup calibration and errors, and the effects of routing decisions before approving its use. Region attribution provides no evidence that geography itself causes delay. |
| Drift/calendar proxy | Calendar week, with cutoff-known holiday or operating-policy information where available. | Week may index historical regimes. Validate across periods and regime changes before relying on the association. A week attribution cannot disentangle the holiday and policy effects. |
| Unusable until lineage is repaired | Current risk score, same-day document count, prior claim count as implemented, initial reserve and channel as supplied, employee experience, and any queue reading without a valid as-of match. | Hold these values out until historical replay or source records establish their exact version and availability. For closure and complete-trace fields, define a distinct prefix feature where one exists. Historical provenance for an eventual outcome cannot make that outcome available early. |

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Invalidate the affected early forecasts and fix the input contract.** Remove complete-trace fields from the scoring and training paths and hold unverifiable fields out of the cutoff model. Record business time, ingestion or publication time, effective versions, and source ordering in the feature store. Persist the exact feature vector served at prediction time. Require point-in-time joins and compare historical replay against logged serving values.
2. **Rebuild the training data and gate it with conformance tests.** Recompute features from historical prefixes at the first coverage event using the same logic as serving. Run boundary, suffix-invariance, and process-conformance tests in CI. Define the settlement label separately using the agreed clock, endpoint, treatment of appeals and withdrawals, and outcome maturity for unresolved cases. Keep outcome information on the label side of the pipeline.
3. **Retrain, evaluate, and calibrate after removal.** Split cases chronologically by prediction time, with time held out for calibration and a later untouched test period. Compare with simple cutoff-eligible baselines. Check discrimination, calibration curves and Brier score, plus calibration by policy era, holiday period, and relevant subgroups where sample sizes support it. Select any operational thresholds using the rebuilt scores and the costs of missed breaches and unnecessary escalations.
4. **Regenerate and test explanations.** Use a background population drawn from eligible coverage prefixes. Verify that local contributions reconstruct the rebuilt score on the stated output scale. Test sign and rank stability across resamples, reference populations, and time periods. Examine how attribution shifts among correlated predictors and report a group contribution when individual credit is unstable. Restrict sequence explanations and perturbations to cutoff-available, process-feasible inputs.
5. **Review proxies and downstream allocation.** Measure incremental value and subgroup calibration, false-positive and false-negative rates, and intervention rates at proposed thresholds. Check intersections where sample sizes permit and compare variants with and without geographic, channel, and resource proxies. Review whether prioritization improves timeliness and whether it shifts delay or access burdens to other claimants.
6. **Monitor schema, process, and model drift.** Alert on missing or stale timestamps, join failures, tied-event rates, unrecognized activity or field values, conformance deviations, and changes in policy or routing. Track feature distributions, explanation stability, and calibration as outcomes mature. Revalidate the feature contract and process rules after material changes.
