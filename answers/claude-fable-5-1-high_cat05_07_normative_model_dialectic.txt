## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### 1.1 What the evidence establishes (stipulated)

Normative baseline
- CS-9 is the only controlled, formally approved norm. It has been in force since 2025-01-01 and has not been amended. It requires (a) Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review before activation, (b) Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier, and (c) requester ≠ manual approver.
- M0 encodes only part of CS-9. Its language is exactly: Register_Supplier, then Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review in either order, then either (Manual_Procurement_Approval, Activate_Supplier) or Reject_Supplier, then Notify_Requester, then Close_Case. M0 contains no loop, no Request_More_Info, no Auto_Approve, no provisional activation, no reactivation entry point, no timing, no amounts, and no resource rule. Consequently "fits M0" is a necessary but not sufficient condition for CS-9 compliance, and "does not fit M0" is not sufficient for a CS-9 violation.
- PD-3 exists as a steering-committee decision recorded in minutes and email, with an explicit window 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31. It was never incorporated into CS-9 or M0. It carries conditions: LOW risk, DE, approved corporate group, Sanctions_Check and automated master-data validation before activation, Quality_Review within two business days of provisional activation.
- FD-7 was in force 2026-07-10 00:00 to 2026-07-12 23:59 and expired automatically. It carries conditions: critical relief suppliers, Sanctions_Check before activation, EUR 20,000 spend cap until retrospective approval, daily monitoring, Quality_Review within two business days.
- From 2026-07-01, Notify_Requester is executed in a CRM system and only ~70% of notifications are mirrored to the onboarding log; missingness rose from 2% to 29%. Older CRM links are mutable text.
- Aggregate: 312 Auto_Approve cases in June/July; 289 appear eligible, 14 clearly ineligible, 9 undetermined. 47 cases contain Request_More_Info and none activated before the evidence arrived. Six further Auto_Approve cases in the first week of August (post-PD-3).
- Case-level trace orders, dates and amounts are as recorded in the table (subject to the specific data challenges noted below for SUP-706, SUP-711, SUP-712, SUP-713).

### 1.3 Burden-of-proof approach

For declaring a case NONCOMPLIANT, the reviewer bears the burden and must show all four:
1. an applicable norm that was in force at the time of the relevant event (CS-9 always; PD-3/FD-7 only within their windows and conditions);
2. that the case is in scope of that norm (rejected leads and reactivations are not presumed in scope of M0 while the scope document is ambiguous, but they are in scope of CS-9's substantive requirements wherever an activation occurs);
3. that the deviation is real, i.e., it survives the data-quality challenge: it cannot be fully explained by a documented logging or migration artifact (duplicate transaction ID, known CRM mirroring gap, approved label mapping);
4. that no valid authority covered the deviation at event time.
Standard: preponderance of evidence for control-flow findings backed by system-generated events; a higher "clear and convincing" bar before labelling a case severe or attributing misconduct to a person.

For accepting a deviation as a JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, the burden shifts to the operation and must show all four:
1. written authority in force at the timestamp of the deviating event (not before, not after, not "in substance");
2. case-level eligibility under that authority, evidenced by attributes captured at or before the deviating event (not reconstructed afterwards);
3. the authority's compensating controls were performed (post-activation Quality_Review within window, spend cap respected, monitoring present);
4. the authority itself was issued by a body competent to derogate, or, where that competence is doubtful (PD-3), the case handler acted within the instructions actually given to them and the competence defect is booked as a system-level governance finding rather than a case-level violation.
Absence of exception documentation is resolved against the exception. Absence of an event that is explained by a known systemic collection defect (CRM notifications after 2026-07-01) is resolved as indeterminate for that event only, not as a violation and not as compliance.

Rule for the deviation-vs-model-defect boundary: if the observed behavior is (a) not prohibited by CS-9's substantive requirements, (b) accepted by Compliance as desirable or required by a scope gap, and (c) merely inexpressible in M0, the finding is MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT. If the behavior violates a substantive CS-9 requirement (sanctions before activation, approval before activation, requester ≠ approver), the finding is NONCOMPLIANT regardless of what M0 can or cannot express.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The prosecution's thesis is not that every trace that misfits M0 is misconduct. It is that the operation has substituted informal authority, personal judgment and good intentions for controlled process, and that the model's defects have been used as cover for that substitution.

Formal authority. CS-9 is the only approved standard. PD-3 is a set of minutes and a follow-up email. It was never incorporated into CS-9 or M0. The Compliance observer's note "pilot may proceed as a monitored exception" is, on its face, a monitoring arrangement, not an approval to remove Manual_Procurement_Approval from "every activation"; the observer herself later said she had not approved weakening CS-9. Under any reasonable governance hierarchy, a controlled standard is amended through its own change procedure, and a body that wanted to run a pilot should have issued a time-boxed, signed derogation attached to CS-9. It did not. Therefore all 312 Auto_Approve activations in June and July lack an approval step that CS-9 requires for "every activation." At best 289 of them were performed under an instruction whose authority is unproven; 14 were performed in cases the instruction itself excluded; 9 cannot even be evaluated because the eligibility fields the instruction relied on were not captured.

Expiry. Both overlays were explicitly time-boxed and both were exceeded. FD-7 ended at 2026-07-12 23:59 and "expired automatically." SUP-705 on 2026-07-13 followed the emergency pattern with no written extension. Operations' argument that "the flood still factually existed" is precisely the argument the directive's automatic expiry was designed to foreclose: the drafters chose a date, not a factual test, so that continuation would require a fresh, accountable decision. PD-3 ended 2026-07-31; SUP-714 on 2026-08-02 and six August cases used Auto_Approve because Operations "believed permanent approval was imminent." Belief in an imminent policy is not a policy. The existence of these cases shows that the operation had no hard stop tied to the effective window: neither the UI nor the team behaviour changed when authority lapsed.

Normalization of exceptions. The sequence is textbook normalization of deviance: an emergency directive (FD-7) is followed by an emergency-pattern case after expiry (SUP-705); a pilot (PD-3) is followed by pilot-pattern cases after expiry (SUP-714, six more); and Operations now asks for a permanent auto-approval branch on the basis that it is already how they work (N5). SUP-703 shows the eligibility control was operator-selectable: an operator ticked "pilot" for a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier and the system activated it. 23 of 312 auto-approvals (7.4%) are either clearly ineligible or unverifiable. A control that fails silently at 7% under a pilot is not a control that should be made permanent.

High-risk cases. SUP-703: HIGH-risk, new, foreign, Auto_Approve, first purchase EUR 180,000, no Manual_Procurement_Approval ever. This is a substantive CS-9 breach (approval before activation) with real exposure and with no overlay that covers it, because PD-3 excluded exactly this supplier profile. SUP-708: Activate_Supplier 40 minutes before Sanctions_Check, HIGH-risk, no emergency, no pilot. Sanctions before activation is the one requirement every source (CS-9, PD-3, FD-7) preserves without exception. "No transactions occurred before clearance" is fortunate, not compliant; the control did not prevent the exposure, chance did.

Deviations hidden by M0. Conformance to M0 has been giving false assurance. SUP-712 fits M0 perfectly, yet requester and manual approver resolve to the same human through two aliases, breaching an explicit CS-9 rule. SUP-715 fits M0 perfectly, yet the quality reviewer and the approver are spouses in the same reporting line and the disclosure required by the conflict-of-interest policy was never recorded. The CRM migration has meanwhile made one M0 activity unobservable in 29% of cases; the operation cannot currently demonstrate that requesters are notified, and the compensating evidence it points to (SUP-706) is a mutable text link written two days after the fact. A conformance rate computed over these conditions overstates compliance in both directions, and Operations should not be allowed to cite M0's deviation rate as "mostly obsolescence" while at the same time benefiting from M0's blindness to identity and timing.

Desirable outcomes do not amend policy. SUP-707's Request_More_Info cycles produced a better decision; the 47 such cases never activated early. Good. But desirability is an argument for changing CS-9 and M0 through the change process, which nobody did. SUP-705 stayed under the (lapsed) cap and performed the (lapsed) compensating controls; that makes it lower-severity, not authorized. SUP-714 was eligible under a pilot that no longer existed. If outcomes could retroactively authorize the behavior that produced them, no time-box, cap or eligibility rule would ever bind.

Concessions. The prosecution accepts that M0 is defective and that some misfits are not misconduct: SUP-711 is a migration duplicate with the same source transaction ID; SUP-713 is a label rename with documented equivalent content; SUP-707 is inexpressible in M0 but harmless; SUP-709 and SUP-710 fall into genuine scope gaps (rejected leads, reactivation); SUP-706's missing notification is more likely a CRM mirroring gap than an omission. The prosecution also accepts that SUP-704 met every condition of a directive that was in force. None of these concessions helps the operation on SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-705, SUP-714, SUP-712 or the August cases, and the fact that the model defects were known and never routed through change control is itself part of the pattern.

System-level claim. The operation is materially noncompliant: two severe substantive breaches (SUP-703, SUP-708) in a sample of fifteen, a demonstrated pattern of continuing exception behavior after authority lapsed (SUP-705, SUP-714, six August cases), an operator-selectable eligibility control with a 7.4% failure/unknown rate, and identity/COI breaches invisible to the conformance tooling. Enforcement of CS-9 should be reasserted immediately, and any future auto-approval or emergency route must be adopted through CS-9's own amendment process with system-enforced eligibility and hard expiry.

## 3. Defense Brief

The defense's thesis is not that anything goes. It is that M0 is an outdated, partial rendering of CS-9 that was applied to a log with known collection defects and an undefined scope, and that most of what the prosecution counts as "deviation" is either behavior the norm itself allows, behavior a competent authority tolerated under conditions that were met, or artefacts of modelling and data.

Concurrency and the semantics of M0. M0 already declares Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review concurrent, so their relative order is never a deviation; the prosecution correctly does not claim it. But M0's concurrency is also its rigidity: `checks` must complete (both activities) before `decision`. That makes it impossible to reject a lead on sanctions failure without first performing a quality review that has become pointless (SUP-709). The model owner's own reading ("M0 begins only after pre-screen acceptance") concedes that M0 was never designed for that case. A model that either excludes the case from scope or forces a useless activity is defective for that case in both readings.

Missing loops. Forty-seven cases contain Request_More_Info; none activated before the requested evidence arrived; Compliance agrees the behavior is desirable. M0 has no loop and no activity for it, so every one of those cases counts as a deviation containing two or more "log moves." This is a pure model artefact: the behavior is additive (it inserts activities before the decision without removing or reordering any CS-9 requirement). SUP-707 is a stronger case than SUP-701, not a weaker one: the approver had the certificates.

Pilot overlay. PD-3 was decided by the steering committee, the body that governs the process, with Compliance present and on record that the pilot "may proceed as a monitored exception." A monitored exception is exactly what SUP-702 is: LOW-risk, DE, approved corporate group, Sanctions_Check before activation, Quality_Review the next business day, notification at +2 hours with a stable notification_id, eligibility fields complete. 289 of 312 auto-approvals (92.6%) appear eligible. If the committee's competence to authorize a pilot is doubted, that is a defect in the organization's governance design, which is not a fault attributable to case handlers who followed a documented decision. The correct response is to fix the norm and the model, not to book 289 correctly handled cases as violations.

Emergency overlay. SUP-704 met every FD-7 condition: critical pump supplier, activation on 2026-07-11 inside the window, Sanctions_Check before activation, EUR 18,000 under the cap, daily monitoring, Quality_Review and Manual_Procurement_Approval the next business day. It is a justified exception, and the prosecution concedes it. SUP-705 on 2026-07-13 is identical in every substantive control, including a lower spend (EUR 12,000); the only difference is that the directive's clock had run out at midnight while the flood had not. The defense does not claim the directive was still in force; it claims that the substantive risk was controlled identically and that the severity should reflect that. The defect exposed is that FD-7 had no extension procedure and nobody in authority was reachable or responsible for extending it.

CRM migration. From 2026-07-01, Notify_Requester moved to a CRM that mirrors only ~70% of notifications. Notification missingness rose from 2% to 29% at exactly that point. SUP-706 has a CRM message at +3 hours. When one activity's collection is known to be broken, conformance checking of that activity measures the data pipeline, not the process. The defense asks that all post-2026-07-01 "missing Notify_Requester" findings be classified as indeterminate until the notification_id link is stabilized, and that this is booked as a data-engineering defect.

Reactivation. SUP-710 reactivates a supplier closed six months earlier. CS-9 has no reactivation procedure. Requiring Register_Supplier for an already registered supplier is meaningless; the prior quality certification remained valid; Sanctions_Check and Manual_Procurement_Approval were both performed before Activate_Supplier. Nothing CS-9 substantively requires was skipped except a quality review whose object (a valid certification) already existed. This is a scope gap.

Rejected leads. SUP-709 is an intake case in the process owner's reading and out of scope in the model owner's reading. In neither reading is anything wrong: the lead failed sanctions and was rejected, notified and closed. The only "deviation" is M0's insistence on Quality_Review before any decision.

Label equivalence and data artefacts. SUP-713's QA_Clearance is Quality_Review under a renamed procedure and UI; audit documents show equivalent content; the mapping was approved. SUP-711 has two Register_Supplier rows with the same source transaction ID, a migration duplicate. Both are trivially reconciled by an activity mapping table and a deduplication rule; treating them as deviations inflates the deviation rate for no compliance benefit.

Trace deviation versus business misconduct. The substantive controls CS-9 cares about, sanctions before activation and approval before spend, held in fourteen of fifteen cases for sanctions and in every case except SUP-703 for approval-before-first-spend (SUP-704 and SUP-705 had caps and retrospective approvals). Even SUP-708, a genuine breach, produced no transaction before clearance and was cleared 40 minutes later. Deviation counts do not measure risk; the prosecution's headline numbers conflate a broken model, a broken log, an ambiguous scope and a handful of real problems.

Concessions. The defense accepts genuine risk and genuine limits. SUP-703 is serious: an ineligible HIGH-risk supplier was auto-approved with a EUR 180,000 first purchase, and the fact that an operator could select "pilot" for it shows that eligibility was not system-enforced. SUP-708 is a real breach of the only universal rule; "no harm" mitigates severity, not classification. SUP-712, if the alias resolution holds, is a real segregation-of-duties breach. SUP-714 and the six August cases show that Operations continued a lapsed pilot on expectation rather than authority; the defense does not argue that practice can extend a mandate, only that practice is evidence that the norm needs amendment. Informal practice can inform revision of CS-9; it cannot replace it.

System-level claim. M0 is invalid as a compliance yardstick: it is obsolete (no Request_More_Info, no pilot route), under-scoped (rejected leads, reactivations), incomplete relative to CS-9 (no resource rule), and applied to a log that has lost 29% of one of its activities. The correct system-level response is to revise the norm and the model, stabilize data collection, and then re-measure, rather than to enforce a model that the organization's own governance body has been working around for two months.

### 5.2 System-level judgment

Operational compliance. The operation is materially noncompliant, but for narrower reasons than the prosecution's headline. It is not noncompliant because 312 cases used Auto_Approve or because 47 cases used Request_More_Info; the former were overwhelmingly performed under a documented (if imperfectly authorized) time-boxed decision, and the latter are harmless behavior the model cannot express. It is noncompliant because:
1. two of fifteen sampled cases (SUP-703, SUP-708) are substantive breaches of the rules every source preserves (approval before activation; sanctions before activation), one with EUR 180,000 exposure;
2. exception behavior demonstrably continued after authority lapsed (SUP-705; SUP-714 and six August cases), which is direct evidence that time-boxes were not operationalized and that exceptions were being normalized;
3. eligibility for the pilot was operator-selectable rather than system-enforced, with 14 clearly ineligible and 9 unverifiable auto-approvals (7.4%);
4. identity and conflict-of-interest controls (SUP-712, SUP-715) failed in cases that M0 conformance reports as clean.

Model validity. M0 is invalid as the sole normative yardstick. It is obsolete (no Request_More_Info, no auto-approval route, no provisional route), incomplete relative to its own source standard (no resource rule), under-scoped (rejected leads and reactivations undefined), and it has been applied to a log that lost 29% of one activity after 2026-07-01 and that contains migration duplicates and an unmapped label rename. Its deviation rate therefore measures a mixture of model debt, data debt, scope debt and real noncompliance, and cannot be cited by either side without decomposition. Operations' claim that the rate is "mostly obsolescence" is unproved because no denominator was provided; on the fifteen sampled cases, six are model/scope/data findings, two are justified exceptions, three are fully compliant and five are noncompliant, i.e., the model-and-data share is large but the noncompliant share is not negligible and includes the two most severe cases.

### 5.3 Where each side's argument is rejected

Rejected from the prosecution:
- That all 312 Auto_Approve cases are violations because PD-3 was informal. PD-3 was a documented decision of the governing committee with Compliance present; case handlers who met its conditions inside its window acted within their instructions. The competence defect belongs to governance and is booked there (Section 6), not against SUP-702 or the 289 eligible cases.
- That SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-711, SUP-713 evidence noncompliance. They evidence model and data debt.
- That SUP-706 is a failure to notify. The known 29% collection gap makes the missing event indeterminate; the prosecution has not met its burden under 1.3(3).
- That the "high deviation rate" is itself evidence of misconduct. Without decomposition it is evidence of nothing.

Rejected from the defense:
- That a factual emergency extended FD-7 (SUP-705). The directive's automatic expiry is a design feature; the remedy for an ongoing emergency is a fresh written decision, which was available and not sought.
- That expectation of a permanent amendment covers SUP-714 and the August cases. It does not; it is the definition of normalization.
- That "no harm occurred" in SUP-708 bears on classification. It bears on severity only.
- That M0 fit means anything for SUP-712 and SUP-715. The resource and COI rules are binding whether or not M0 encodes them.
- That the deviation rate is "mostly obsolescence." Unproved for lack of a denominator, and the sampled severe cases are not obsolescence.
- That SUP-710's skipped Quality_Review is fully justified. It is a scope gap, and the skip rests on an unwritten rule; it is classified as a model/scope defect with low severity, not as compliant.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The revision separates concerns into layers so that each rule lives where it can actually be evaluated, and so that "fits the model" can never again be read as "compliant."

Layer 0: Scope and case definition (organizational, enforced by pandas case-construction rules)
- A case is created at Register_Supplier (new supplier) or Reactivate_Supplier (supplier previously closed) and ends at Close_Case.
- Rejected leads are in scope from Register_Supplier; pre-screen rejection on sanctions failure is a modelled route (no Quality_Review required). This resolves SUP-709 in the process owner's favour while making the model owner's concern (no pointless QR) explicit.
- Reactivations are in scope; Quality_Review may be replaced by Confirm_Valid_Certification only when a certification with a recorded validity end date after the activation date exists (data rule R-REACT-1).
- Withdrawals (requester withdraws before decision) are a modelled outcome.
- Case attributes are snapshotted at registration and at each decision: risk_class, country, corporate_group_id, group_approval_status, supplier_type (standard/critical_relief), requester_actor_id.

Layer 1: Control flow (POWL model M1, Section 7)
- Five routes joined by an exclusive choice: standard, pre-screen rejection, low-risk auto-approval, emergency/provisional, reactivation. Notify_Requester and Close_Case follow every route.
- Standard route: Sanctions_Check ∥ Quality_Review ∥ zero-or-more Request_More_Info, then XOR(Manual_Procurement_Approval → Activate_Supplier, Reject_Supplier, Withdraw_Request).
- Auto route: Sanctions_Check ∥ Master_Data_Validation, then XOR(Auto_Approve → Activate_Supplier → Quality_Review, Escalate_To_Manual → Quality_Review → Manual_Procurement_Approval → Activate_Supplier, Reject_Supplier). Escalation is the modelled answer to SUP-703: an ineligible case leaves the auto route instead of being forced through it.
- Emergency route: Emergency_Authorization ∥ Sanctions_Check, then Provisional_Activation, then Quality_Review ∥ Manual_Procurement_Approval ∥ one-or-more Daily_Transaction_Monitoring, then XOR(Confirm_Activation, Revoke_Provisional_Activation). Provisional_Activation is a distinct label from Activate_Supplier so that provisional status is visible in the trace and not only in an attribute.
- What POWL is used for: ordering, concurrency, choice and repetition. What it is not used for: any condition on who, how much, when, or under which authority.

Layer 2: Cross-route invariants (DECLARE / log-skeleton constraints evaluated on the same log)
These hold regardless of route and are evaluated independently of M1 so that a route mis-selection cannot hide a breach:
- C1 precedence(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier) and precedence(Sanctions_Check, Provisional_Activation): every activation of any kind is preceded by a completed sanctions check with result = clear. (Catches SUP-708 in any route.)
- C2 precedence({Manual_Procurement_Approval, Auto_Approve}, Activate_Supplier): non-provisional activation requires an approval event.
- C3 response(Provisional_Activation, Manual_Procurement_Approval) and response(Provisional_Activation, Quality_Review): every provisional activation is followed by both (time bound in Layer 3).
- C4 response(Activate_Supplier ∪ Provisional_Activation ∪ Reject_Supplier ∪ Withdraw_Request, Notify_Requester) and precedence(Notify_Requester, Close_Case).
- C5 exactly-once(Register_Supplier ∪ Reactivate_Supplier) per case; at-most-once(Activate_Supplier), at-most-once(Provisional_Activation).
- C6 not-coexistence(Auto_Approve, Manual_Procurement_Approval) unless Escalate_To_Manual is present (a plain not-coexistence is too strict; the log-skeleton "directly follows after escalation" form is used).
- C7 response(Request_More_Info, Info_Received) and precedence(Info_Received, {Manual_Procurement_Approval, Auto_Approve}) once Info_Received is logged (currently the aggregate shows none activated early, but the constraint should be checkable, not inferred).

Layer 3: Data-aware eligibility, amount and time rules (pandas checks over the flat event table with the attributes proposed in Section 7)
- R-AUTO-1: Auto_Approve is valid only if an overlay of type PILOT or PERMANENT with rule_id in the auto-approval family is active at the Auto_Approve timestamp (effective_from ≤ ts ≤ effective_to, status = APPROVED, signed by the required roles).
- R-AUTO-2: eligibility snapshot at Auto_Approve: risk_class = LOW, country = DE, group_approval_status = APPROVED, all fields non-null. Null → escalate, never approve (fixes the 9 unknowns).
- R-AUTO-3: Quality_Review completes within two business days of Activate_Supplier on the auto route.
- R-EMER-1: Provisional_Activation is valid only if Emergency_Authorization references a directive_id active at the Provisional_Activation timestamp and the supplier_type snapshot = critical_relief.
- R-EMER-2: cumulative spend before Manual_Procurement_Approval ≤ cap stated in the directive (EUR 20,000 for FD-7).
- R-EMER-3: Quality_Review and Manual_Procurement_Approval within two business days of Provisional_Activation; at least one Daily_Transaction_Monitoring event per calendar day until Confirm_Activation.
- R-REACT-1: Confirm_Valid_Certification requires certification_valid_to ≥ activation date and certification_scope covering the supplier category.
- R-TIME-1: all rule evaluation uses the immutable record timestamp (system clock at write) and the business effective timestamp; overlays are evaluated against the effective timestamp; disputes resolved against the record timestamp.
- R-LABEL-1: activity mapping table (QA_Clearance → Quality_Review) with mapping_effective_from; a mapping approved later than the event is applied but flagged as "retroactive mapping" for audit.
- R-DEDUP-1: events with identical (case_id, activity, source_transaction_id) are collapsed and the duplicate is logged as an ETL defect.

Layer 4: Resource and conflict-of-interest rules (pandas over actor tables; organizational for disclosure)
- S1: canonical_actor_id(requester) ≠ canonical_actor_id(Manual_Procurement_Approval performer). Alias table maintained by IAM, not free text (fixes SUP-712).
- S2: canonical_actor_id(Quality_Review performer) ≠ canonical_actor_id(Manual_Procurement_Approval performer).
- S3: declared-relationship check: pairs (reviewer, approver) and (requester, approver) are screened against a relationship register (household, reporting line); a hit requires a recorded disclosure_id and an assignment approval before the second event (fixes SUP-715).
- S4: delegation: any approval performed by a delegate must carry delegation_id with delegator, delegate, scope and validity; the delegator counts as the approver for S1–S3.
- S5: Auto_Approve is a system actor; the rule engine version (rule_version_id) is the "approver" and is recorded on the event.

Layer 5: Policy-overlay governance for pilots and emergencies (organizational, backed by the overlay registry table)
- Every derogation from CS-9 is an overlay record with: overlay_id, type (PILOT / EMERGENCY / PERMANENT_AMENDMENT), authority body, named approvers with signatures, effective_from, effective_to, maximum duration, eligibility predicate, compensating controls, spend cap, monitoring requirement, extension procedure (who may extend, by when, in writing), status (DRAFT / APPROVED / EXPIRED / RATIFIED / REVOKED), and a link to the CS-9 change request.
- The onboarding system reads the registry: routes that depend on an overlay are hidden or blocked in the UI when no overlay is active (hard stop, fixing SUP-705/714 and the August cases).
- Compliance sign-off is a required signature for any overlay that removes or reorders a CS-9 check; a "monitored exception" note without signature does not create an overlay.
- Post-expiry ratification is possible but is a distinct record; cases performed between expiry and ratification remain NONCOMPLIANT with severity reduced by ratification, so that ratification cannot rewrite history.
- Pilot promotion to permanent requires: eligibility failure rate (ineligible + unknown) below a threshold (proposed 1%), zero operator-selectable eligibility, and a CS-9 amendment.

Layer 6: Organizational controls
- Eligibility is computed by the system from snapshotted attributes; operators cannot select a route that the eligibility engine did not offer (root cause of SUP-703).
- Activation requires a sanctions result object, not a checkbox (root cause of SUP-708).
- Change control for M1 and for the activity mapping table; model owner and process owner co-sign scope decisions.
- Quarterly reconciliation of CRM notifications against onboarding cases by notification_id; missingness above 2% blocks the conformance report for that activity.
- Internal Audit samples justified exceptions, not only deviations.

Where each part belongs
- POWL (Layer 1): routes, concurrency (Sanctions ∥ Quality; Sanctions ∥ Master_Data_Validation; Emergency_Authorization ∥ Sanctions), the Request_More_Info repetition, the choices (approve/reject/withdraw; confirm/revoke), and the notification-before-closure tail.
- DECLARE / log skeleton (Layer 2): invariants that must hold across all routes and that a route choice could otherwise mask (sanctions before any activation; approval before non-provisional activation; response obligations after provisional activation; cardinalities).
- pandas / data checks (Layers 0, 3, 4): scope construction, eligibility snapshots, amounts, business-day windows, overlay-in-force checks, alias resolution, relationship screening, deduplication, label mapping.
- Organizational controls (Layers 5, 6): authority, signatures, hard stops in the UI, change control, reconciliation, audit sampling, disclosure.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

Design notes and stated limitations before the code:
- POWL cannot express data-dependent choice, so route selection (standard vs auto vs emergency vs reactivation) is an unconditional XOR in the model; the conditions that make a route legitimate live in the overlay table and data rules below. Alignment against M1 answers "is this an admissible shape"; Layers 2–5 answer "was it allowed for this case at this time."
- Each route carries its own intake transition (Register_Supplier or Reactivate_Supplier) so that the intake is coupled to the route. Repeated labels across routes are legitimate in POWL (distinct Transition objects, same label).
- Request_More_Info is a genuine loop: LOOP(do = silent, redo = Request_More_Info) yields zero or more repetitions, placed concurrently with the checks and before the decision. This is the semantics SUP-707 requires and not an approximation.
- Daily_Transaction_Monitoring is LOOP(do = Daily_Transaction_Monitoring, redo = silent), i.e., one or more. The "one per calendar day" cardinality is a data rule (R-EMER-3).
- Emergency_Authorization and Provisional_Activation are new required labels. Historical FD-7 cases (SUP-704) will not fit M1 until their authorization is backfilled from the FD-7 record and Activate_Supplier is relabelled Provisional_Activation via the mapping table; this is deliberate, so that provisional activations cannot be logged as ordinary activations.
- Master_Data_Validation is a new required event on the auto route, because PD-3 required it and the current log does not show it. Historical pilot cases (SUP-702) therefore do not fit M1 as logged; the gap is a data-collection finding, not a reason to weaken the model.
- Ordering edges in a StrictPartialOrder between a transition and a nested operator are respected by PM4Py's POWL-to-Petri-net conversion; the self-check at the end of the code demonstrates the expected fitting and non-fitting traces without reading any external file.

```python
"""
Revised normative POWL model M1 for supplier onboarding (PM4Py).
Self-contained: builds the model, converts it to a Petri net, and aligns a few
inline example traces to demonstrate intended semantics. No external files.
"""
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL,
                                    Transition, SilentTransition)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def T(label):
    return Transition(label=label)


def seq(*nodes):
    """Strict partial order that totally orders the given nodes."""
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for a, b in zip(nodes, nodes[1:]):
        po.order.add_edge(a, b)
    return po


# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# ROUTE 1 - STANDARD ROUTE (CS-9 default)
# Register -> { Sanctions || Quality || (Request_More_Info)* } -> decision
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
reg_s = T("Register_Supplier")
sanc_s = T("Sanctions_Check")
qual_s = T("Quality_Review")                # QA_Clearance mapped to this label at data layer
rmi_s = T("Request_More_Info")
# zero-or-more Request_More_Info: LOOP(do = tau, redo = RMI)
rmi_loop_s = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP,
                          children=[SilentTransition(), rmi_s])

mpa_s = T("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
act_s = T("Activate_Supplier")
approve_s = seq(mpa_s, act_s)
reject_s = T("Reject_Supplier")
withdraw_s = T("Withdraw_Request")
decision_s = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                          children=[approve_s, reject_s, withdraw_s])

assess_s = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanc_s, qual_s, rmi_loop_s, decision_s])
assess_s.order.add_edge(sanc_s, decision_s)
assess_s.order.add_edge(qual_s, decision_s)
assess_s.order.add_edge(rmi_loop_s, decision_s)

standard_route = seq(reg_s, assess_s)

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# ROUTE 2 - PRE-SCREEN REJECTION (rejected leads; scope rule Layer 0)
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
reg_p = T("Register_Supplier")
sanc_p = T("Sanctions_Check")
rej_p = T("Reject_Supplier")
prescreen_reject_route = seq(reg_p, sanc_p, rej_p)

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# ROUTE 3 - LOW-RISK AUTO-APPROVAL ROUTE (valid only under an active overlay)
# Register -> { Sanctions || Master_Data_Validation } ->
#   XOR( Auto_Approve -> Activate -> Quality_Review,
#        Escalate_To_Manual -> Quality_Review -> MPA -> Activate,
#        Reject )
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
reg_a = T("Register_Supplier")
sanc_a = T("Sanctions_Check")
mdv_a = T("Master_Data_Validation")
auto_a = T("Auto_Approve")
act_a = T("Activate_Supplier")
qual_a = T("Quality_Review")                # post-activation; <= 2 business days is a data rule
esc_a = T("Escalate_To_Manual")             # eligibility not met -> leave the auto path
qual_a2 = T("Quality_Review")
mpa_a = T("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
act_a2 = T("Activate_Supplier")
rej_a = T("Reject_Supplier")

auto_path = seq(auto_a, act_a, qual_a)
escalated_path = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[esc_a, qual_a2, mpa_a, act_a2])
escalated_path.order.add_edge(esc_a, qual_a2)
escalated_path.order.add_edge(esc_a, mpa_a)
escalated_path.order.add_edge(qual_a2, mpa_a)
escalated_path.order.add_edge(mpa_a, act_a2)
auto_decision = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                             children=[auto_path, escalated_path, rej_a])

pre_auto = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanc_a, mdv_a])        # concurrent gates
auto_route = seq(reg_a, pre_auto, auto_decision)

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# ROUTE 4 - EMERGENCY / PROVISIONAL ROUTE (valid only under an active directive)
# Register -> { Emergency_Authorization || Sanctions } -> Provisional_Activation ->
#   { Quality_Review || MPA (retrospective) || Daily_Monitoring+ } ->
#   XOR( Confirm_Activation, Revoke_Provisional_Activation )
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
reg_e = T("Register_Supplier")
auth_e = T("Emergency_Authorization")       # carries directive_id (authority evidence)
sanc_e = T("Sanctions_Check")
pact_e = T("Provisional_Activation")        # deliberately distinct from Activate_Supplier
qual_e = T("Quality_Review")
mpa_e = T("Manual_Procurement_Approval")    # retrospective approval
mon_e = T("Daily_Transaction_Monitoring")
mon_loop_e = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP,
                          children=[mon_e, SilentTransition()])   # one or more
retro_e = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[qual_e, mpa_e, mon_loop_e])  # concurrent
conf_e = T("Confirm_Activation")
revoke_e = T("Revoke_Provisional_Activation")
outcome_e = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[conf_e, revoke_e])

gates_e = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[auth_e, sanc_e])        # both before provisional activation
emergency_route = seq(reg_e, gates_e, pact_e, retro_e, outcome_e)

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# ROUTE 5 - REACTIVATION ROUTE (closed supplier re-opened)
# Reactivate -> { Sanctions || XOR(Quality_Review, Confirm_Valid_Certification) } ->
#   XOR( MPA -> Activate, Reject )
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
react_r = T("Reactivate_Supplier")
sanc_r = T("Sanctions_Check")
qual_r = T("Quality_Review")
cert_r = T("Confirm_Valid_Certification")   # evidence event; validity is a data rule
qual_or_cert_r = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[qual_r, cert_r])
mpa_r = T("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
act_r = T("Activate_Supplier")
rej_r = T("Reject_Supplier")
dec_r = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[seq(mpa_r, act_r), rej_r])
checks_r = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanc_r, qual_or_cert_r])
reactivation_route = seq(react_r, checks_r, dec_r)

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# TOP LEVEL: route choice, then notification, then closure
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
route = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                     children=[standard_route, prescreen_reject_route, auto_route,
                               emergency_route, reactivation_route])
notify = T("Notify_Requester")
close = T("Close_Case")
M1 = seq(route, notify, close)

print("M1 =", M1)

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# Self-check on inline traces (no external files). Demonstrates that the
# model admits the intended shapes and rejects the substantive breaches.
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
try:
    import pandas as pd
    import pm4py

    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(M1)

    examples = {
        # expected FIT
        "SUP-701 standard": ["Register_Supplier", "Sanctions_Check", "Quality_Review",
                             "Manual_Procurement_Approval", "Activate_Supplier",
                             "Notify_Requester", "Close_Case"],
        "SUP-707 with RMI loop": ["Register_Supplier", "Sanctions_Check", "Request_More_Info",
                                  "Request_More_Info", "Quality_Review",
                                  "Manual_Procurement_Approval", "Activate_Supplier",
                                  "Notify_Requester", "Close_Case"],
        "SUP-709 pre-screen reject": ["Register_Supplier", "Sanctions_Check", "Reject_Supplier",
                                      "Notify_Requester", "Close_Case"],
        "SUP-702 auto (with MDV + notify)": ["Register_Supplier", "Sanctions_Check",
                                             "Master_Data_Validation", "Auto_Approve",
                                             "Activate_Supplier", "Quality_Review",
                                             "Notify_Requester", "Close_Case"],
        "SUP-704 emergency (relabelled)": ["Register_Supplier", "Emergency_Authorization",
                                           "Sanctions_Check", "Provisional_Activation",
                                           "Daily_Transaction_Monitoring", "Quality_Review",
                                           "Manual_Procurement_Approval", "Confirm_Activation",
                                           "Notify_Requester", "Close_Case"],
        "SUP-710 reactivation": ["Reactivate_Supplier", "Sanctions_Check",
                                 "Confirm_Valid_Certification", "Manual_Procurement_Approval",
                                 "Activate_Supplier", "Notify_Requester", "Close_Case"],
        "SUP-703 escalated (what should have happened)": [
            "Register_Supplier", "Sanctions_Check", "Master_Data_Validation",
            "Escalate_To_Manual", "Quality_Review", "Manual_Procurement_Approval",
            "Activate_Supplier", "Notify_Requester", "Close_Case"],
        # expected NON-FIT
        "SUP-708 activation before sanctions": ["Register_Supplier", "Quality_Review",
                                                "Activate_Supplier", "Sanctions_Check",
                                                "Manual_Procurement_Approval",
                                                "Notify_Requester", "Close_Case"],
        "SUP-706 no notification": ["Register_Supplier", "Sanctions_Check", "Quality_Review",

    for k, (cid, acts) in enumerate(examples.items()):
```

### 7.2 Event and data-collection proposal

Event-level attributes (every event; immutable once written)
- case_id; event_id (UUID); activity (canonical label); activity_raw (as emitted by the source system); mapping_id (to the activity mapping table; null if no mapping applied); mapping_effective_from.
- effective_timestamp (business time of the action, source-system clock, tz-aware); record_timestamp (append-only write time of the log store); source_system; source_transaction_id (used by R-DEDUP-1).
- actor_alias (as seen in the source); actor_canonical_id (resolved via IAM alias table, with alias_resolution_version); actor_role; on_behalf_of_canonical_id and delegation_id (null unless delegated; delegation record carries delegator, delegate, scope, valid_from, valid_to, approver).
- rule_id and rule_version_id for any automated decision (Auto_Approve, Master_Data_Validation, Sanctions_Check result); rule_engine_run_id.
- overlay_id and overlay_version for any event on the auto or emergency route (Auto_Approve, Emergency_Authorization, Provisional_Activation); directive_id for emergency events.
- result fields: sanctions_result (CLEAR / HIT / PENDING) with screening_provider_reference; quality_result; approval_decision; amount and currency on purchase-order events fed from the ERP for cap checks.

Case-level eligibility snapshots (written at Register/Reactivate and re-snapshotted at each decision event; never overwritten, only appended with snapshot_timestamp)
- risk_class; country; corporate_group_id; group_approval_status; supplier_type (standard / critical_relief); requester_canonical_id; certification_id, certification_valid_to, certification_scope (for reactivations); incident_id (for emergencies).
- Auto-approval eligibility is evaluated from the snapshot current at Auto_Approve; the evaluation result and the field values used are stored with the Auto_Approve event so that eligibility can be re-audited without re-deriving it.

Notification evidence
- Notify_Requester is emitted by the CRM with notification_id (immutable, system-generated), channel, recipient_canonical_id, sent_timestamp, delivery_status, and case_id as a foreign key (not free text). The onboarding log store subscribes to the CRM event stream; mirroring is verified daily by count reconciliation and any gap above 2% raises a data-quality incident and suppresses the notification constraint from the conformance report until reconciled.
- Legacy CRM links entered as mutable text are migrated once into notification_id where a unique match exists; unmatched links are marked provenance = MANUAL_LATE and never count as evidence of compliance (they can still count against a claim of non-notification).

Authority evidence
- Emergency_Authorization is a logged event carrying directive_id, issuer_canonical_id, signature_reference, effective_from, effective_to; it is emitted from the overlay registry when a case is attached to a directive, not typed by the operator.
- Auto_Approve carries overlay_id; if the registry shows no APPROVED overlay in force at effective_timestamp, the system does not offer the auto route (hard stop) and any Auto_Approve that nonetheless appears is a system incident as well as a compliance finding.

Relationship and COI evidence
- relationship_register: (canonical_id_a, canonical_id_b, relationship_type, declared_on, disclosure_id, assignment_approval_id). Rule S3 joins reviewer/approver and requester/approver pairs to this register; a hit without disclosure_id and assignment_approval_id before the second event is a finding.

Activity mapping table
