# 1. Objectives and constraints

Complete cases accurately and before their deadlines. Among assignments with acceptable SLA risk, favor timely completions, preserve valuable case knowledge, distribute work according to available hours, give people supervised practice, and keep qualified cover for the next arrival. Count a case that returns for correction as rework when evaluating throughput.

**Safety and decision limits**

- Skill 0 excludes a worker from performing the activity, including as a supervised trainee. For high and urgent work, the performer needs skill 2 or higher unless explicitly supervised. This policy also pairs level-1 trainees on normal and low work with a competent mentor.
- Security requires a level-3 performer or a level-1/2 performer explicitly supervised by R7_Gita or R8_Hugo. C001 specifically requires a specialist to perform the review. Final Approval requires a level-2/3 finalizer. A level-1 worker may prepare it with a named finalizer.
- Limit each person to four distinct case assignments in the batch. Count retained assignments and both members of a pair conservatively. Reserve time for review, handover and sign-off as well as execution. Use a high-overtime-risk worker only for an urgent case after verifying that no safer qualified schedule can meet the need.
- Batch Quality Checks only for normal/low priority with **more than 24 hours** remaining at the batching decision. Release work early enough to meet the earlier deadline.

**Preferences and conditional targets**

Prefer the current owner on high-complexity, high-handover-cost work. Distinguish the case owner, who maintains the customer relationship, from the person qualified to perform a particular step. Consider a supervised plan before moving a weakly skilled owner, subject to the activity rules. Aim for documented development on at least 20% of normal/low decisions whenever safe schedules permit it. For this batch that means **two of nine**. A training assignment requires substantive work on the learner’s recorded priority, a booked mentor and feedback. Observation alone earns no credit.

# 2. Assignment policy

1. **Build candidates at each decision.** On a ready event, completion, absence or material deadline change, update the outstanding queue, calendars, commitments, qualifications, training priorities and owners. Generate solo assignments and named performer–mentor or preparer–finalizer pairs. Specify who owns the case afterward. Apply the skill, supervision, batching, overtime and four-case rules above. Reject plans that double-book people or depend on unconfirmed cover.
2. **Score urgency against a feasible completion time.** For case `c` and candidate plan `a`, estimate the distribution of time to the SLA milestone, including queueing, handover, service, supervision, business hours and any remaining downstream steps. Let `p(c,a)` be its predicted miss probability and let *slack* be time until deadline minus the 90th percentile of that duration. For a proposed one-hour deferral, score urgency as `priority weight × (miss probability if deferred − miss probability if started now)`, using initial weights of 4, 3, 2 and 1 for urgent, high, normal and low. Escalate already-negative slack immediately, even if further delay barely changes miss probability. Use earliest deadline for otherwise equal urgency. Recheck the risks to other cases that share the same worker. A free worker and a 2-hour deadline alone do not establish feasibility.
3. **Show what each assignment gains and costs.** Use the following components to rank feasible *batch schedules*. Keep the component values in the decision record.

   | Component | How to score it |
   |---|---|
   | Skill and throughput | Score skill fit by the estimated probability of first-pass success for this activity, skill level, complexity and loop history. Estimate effort separately and reward expected first-pass completions in the planning horizon. For a pair, include both people’s time and the required review. |
   | Continuity | Charge a transfer penalty that increases with handover band, complexity and prior reassignments. Treat complexity of 0.75 or above as high for this snapshot. Use the historical 2.7-hour median extra wait on complex transfers and 3.3-hour extra service for a weak retained owner as starting estimates when applicable. Do not add them again if already captured in the time model. Give a third transfer extra scrutiny given the observed 1.8× rework and 1.4× appeal rates above two reassignments. Validate these group-level associations on comparable cases. |
   | Workload and overtime | Compare projected committed effort with staffed hours for each decision horizon. Include existing commitments, mentoring and the proposed batch. Penalize high peak utilization, imbalance in utilization, and loss of shift-end buffer. Open-case count helps break ties when effort estimates are uncertain. |
   | Development | Credit one normal/low case decision when a qualified learner performs an agreed task on their documented priority. Protect enough mentor time to check it. Count each case decision once even if two people participate. |
   | Resilience | Penalize plans that leave little qualified capacity for expected arrivals, especially in Security and Approval. Assess cover if one specialist becomes unavailable. A competent finalizer can preserve specialist time for an urgent case. |

4. **Select and explain the schedule.** First protect safety and keep priority-weighted misses within locally agreed risk limits. Within that envelope, meet the training target if feasible. Among remaining schedules, maximize first-pass completions and skill fit, less transfer, workload, overtime and lost-cover penalties. Scale components to comparable ranges and agree the weights with workers and service leads before deployment. Break close ties by retaining the owner, then greater staffed headroom, then a recorded rotation among equals.
5. **Handle exceptions.** If the queue cannot meet its deadlines, surface the endangered cases immediately. Seek qualified cover or reprioritize with the duty lead and record the forecast delay. If mentoring creates the conflict, move the training slot or release the case to its qualified lead and record the shortfall. Never silently relax a qualification or sign-off rule. Log the selected plan, closest feasible alternative, decisive reason, policy version, forecast and any human override.

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators

Use one decision ID per ready activity instance, with its participants and roles. Keep the decision timestamp, policy version, frozen candidate and workload snapshots, skill and training-priority versions, planned slots, mentor reservation, chosen owner, reason and overrides. Link actual performers, reviews, final sign-offs and outcomes to it. Preserve case and activity-instance IDs across retries. Compare cohorts by activity, priority, complexity, team and shift. Show denominators and distinguish planned from delivered work.

| Indicator | Definition and audit use |
|---|---|
| Handover frequency | Within each ordered case trace, divide adjacent executed-step pairs with different performers by all adjacent pairs with known performers. Inspect directed edges by activity and team. Keep mentor reviews identifiable so they do not masquerade as owner transfers. |
| Reassignment count | Count recorded changes from one named case owner to another, excluding first assignment. Reconcile with the logged counter, accounting for its value at the start of the window. Report the fraction of cases above two and high-cost transfers separately. |
| Skill mismatch | Compare skill as of execution with required skill, priority, specialist notes and activity rules. Report zero-skill actions, missing required supervision and unauthorized finalizers per audited activity. Track valid supervised work separately and flag missing evidence. |
| Workload distribution | Count distinct decisions per participant per batch. Compare assigned and actual hours, including mentor time, divided by staffed hours. Report median, upper tail, maximum and cap breaches by team and shift. |
| Overtime risk exposure | Share of assignments and booked hours going to people marked high risk at decision time. Verify urgency and exhausted alternatives for each exception. Compare with actual out-of-shift hours from calendars or time records. |
| SLA misses | For cases whose deadline has passed, count those completed late or still open past deadline, divided by all cases due in that cohort. Report lateness, priority and forecast calibration. Use milestone completion when the SLA applies to a step. |
| Rework and appeals | For a fixed follow-up window, measure the share of assigned cases with a subsequent correction, repeated diagnosis or appeal. Link the subsequent event to the relevant decision when possible. Compare like case mix by transfer count, band and skill. Flag incomplete follow-up. |
| Training assignment share | Distinct normal/low decisions with documented priority-matched, hands-on supervised work divided by all normal/low decisions. Compare booked and delivered shares with 20%. Record cancellations, feedback and later demonstrated proficiency. |
| Specialist bottlenecks | Measure ready queue length, ready-to-start p90 wait, busy/staffed hours, and share handled by the busiest specialist for Security and Approval. Track qualified cover by shift, including who can legally finalize, and observe delays during absences. |
| Policy deviations | Replay each decision with its historical inputs and policy version. Count forbidden assignments, absent sign-offs, fifth bookings, ineligible batches and unexplained departures from the ranking. Report approved exceptions, missing evidence and unapproved breaches separately. |

Review individual exceptions with the workers involved. Network centrality or long case duration can reflect scarce qualifications, missing documents or time spent teaching. Check those explanations before changing routing weights or drawing conclusions about a worker.

# 7. PM4Py implementation sketch

The execution log supports sequence and network analysis. Decision-time eligibility, queue waits and protected mentor time additionally require the decision ledger, effective-dated roster, calendars and ready/start/complete timestamps. The following Python sketch uses a completed business-activity view with one row per execution, a ledger with one row per assignment decision, and a participation table linking decisions to workers. The ledger preserves unsuccessful proposals, open cases and policy exceptions too.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

case, activity = "case:concept:name", "concept:name"
end, resource = "time:timestamp", "org:resource"
x = completed_activities.copy()
x[end] = pd.to_datetime(x[end], utc=True)
order = [case, end]
if "source_sequence" in x:
    order.append("source_sequence")
x = x.sort_values(order, kind="stable")
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(x, case_id=case, activity_key=activity,
                             timestamp_key=end)

handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log)
together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)
similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log)
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log)
pm4py.save_vis_sna(handover, "handover.png")

costly = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log, "handover_cost_band", ["high"], level="case", retain=True
)
paths = pm4py.get_variants(costly)
urgent_ids = assignments.loc[assignments.case_priority.eq("urgent"), case].unique()
urgent_traces = log.loc[log[case].isin(urgent_ids)]
long_cases = pm4py.filter_case_performance(log, 24 * 3600, float("inf"))
dfg, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)

if "start_timestamp" in log and log["start_timestamp"].notna().all():
    log["start_timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(log["start_timestamp"], utc=True)
    service = pm4py.get_service_time(log, start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")
    case_times = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
        log, start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp"
    )
    quality = log.loc[log[activity].eq("Quality Check")].copy()
    batches = pm4py.discover_batches(quality, start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")

normal_low = assignments.loc[assignments.case_priority.isin(["normal", "low"])]
training_evidence = normal_low["delivered_priority_matched_training"]
training_share = training_evidence.fillna(False).mean()
missing_training_evidence = training_evidence.isna().sum()
batch_load = participations.groupby(["batch_id", "participant"]).decision_id.nunique()
cap_breaches = batch_load[batch_load > 4]
```

The urgent cohort contains whole traces of cases that were urgent at a decision. The 24-hour duration filter is useful for investigating slow traces. Compute SLA compliance separately using each case’s deadline. Align each detected quality batch with priority and remaining SLA **at batch formation**. Validate directed handovers against adjacent actual performers and validate ownership changes against the ledger. Working-together links indicate shared cases, and activity similarity and discovered roles indicate patterns of practice. Verify supervision and permission against recorded reviews and the roster.

A completion-only performance DFG shows elapsed transitions, which can mix waiting, service and other delays. Measure queue wait directly from ready to start and service from start to completion, with calendar and overlap conventions documented. If recorded order for tied timestamps is unavailable, flag ambiguous transitions. Use earlier periods to estimate durations and rework risk, test forecasts on later periods, and recalibrate with worker feedback when staffing or case mix changes.
