## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

**Hard invariants:** preserve the supplied events, objects, and observed relations; add no new entity. Every relation must reference existing IDs. Event and object IDs must be unique within their tables, and identical qualified relation tuples must not be duplicated. Preserve O2O direction: a delivery carries a package, not the reverse. These structural requirements do not make the domain defaults infallible.

**Strong defaults:** use the stated activity cardinalities, allowing their explicit exceptions. Prefer creation/addition before packing, packing before dispatch, and dispatch before confirmation along the same object's history. Multiple items may share a package, an order may span packages, and an invoice may cover multiple orders. Payment recording usually involves an invoice, but unapplied cash remains possible. No rule requires every object to reach every lifecycle stage.

**Weak heuristics:** scanner and carrier identifier similarity, temporal proximity, matching customer and route, and compatible amounts increase support. `PK8-2`, following `PK8-1`, is particularly persuasive for ev08. `N-421?` favors DEL-Y but contains uncertainty; `N-42*` distinguishes neither delivery. NORTH and C17 are shared context, not unique keys. The equations 130 + 100 = 230 and 100 + 130 = 230 support consolidation and settlement without proving either. Gross weights cannot identify contents without item weights.

**Evidence against a repair:** conflicting identifiers, multiple packages on an ordinary item-packing event, reversed qualifiers, unsupported direct participation inferred through long relationship chains, or timing incompatible with the proposed history. Missing confirmation is not itself evidence that dispatch was impossible.

Combine these signals qualitatively, avoiding double counting: an event amount and its linked payment object's amount describe the same transaction. Existing O2O structure constrains plausible assignments, but missing O2O is not negative proof. Newly inferred E2O and O2O edges are dependent hypotheses. Untimed O2O records also cannot establish when a relationship became valid.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A: conservative/minimum-change repair.** Add P01–P02 only. ev08 gains PK-8, and that package contains items from both orders. Leave ev10's delivery unresolved, favoring DEL-Y without materializing it. Leave ev11 order-only; neither delivery is thereby known to be confirmed. Keep INV-44's observed ORD-A association and leave ORD-B's possible billing unresolved. Keep PAY-2's invoice unresolved, allowing unapplied cash.

This is minimal conditional on accepting the strongest packing inference and representing it in both relation tables. It is not a mathematical claim that repair requires additions. ORD-B now participates in a shared package; PK-8 has a plausible packing-to-dispatch history whose delivery is unknown. DEL-Y remains unconnected, and missing financial associations remain explicit. The invoice discrepancy is unexplained; a fee or missing billed order is possible, not established.

**Bundle B: consolidated invoice and joint delivery acknowledgment.** Add P01–P04 and P07–P12. Thus ev08 uses PK-8; ev10 dispatches it through DEL-Y; ev11 acknowledges both DEL-X and DEL-Y. INV-44 bills both orders, and PAY-2 joins PAY-1 as an invoice payment. Do not add P05–P06 or P13–P16.

The complete interpretation is coherent: ORD-A splits across two deliveries; ORD-B shares its PK-8 shipment; both dispatches precede the joint acknowledgment and invoice. Two payments total 230. Joint acknowledgment is allowed by the domain, but the wildcard does not prove its scope. ev11 retains only ORD-A as a directly observed order participant; confirming a shared delivery does not automatically add ORD-B. This bundle makes several assumptions together and should not inherit the confidence of its strongest edge.

**Bundle C: one delivery carries both packages.** Add P01–P02 and P05–P06. ev08 still uses PK-8; ev10 uses DEL-X, treating `N-421?` as unreliable. Keep ev11 order-only, ORD-B billing unresolved, and PAY-2 unallocated to a known invoice. DEL-X has two simultaneous package-dispatch events, which is plausible for one delivery carrying two packages. DEL-Y remains orphaned. This interpretation is weaker than DEL-Y dispatch because it discounts the more specific carrier evidence, but remains possible.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

For selected Bundle A:

- **Order:** both have creation and item addition. ORD-A additionally has confirmation and invoicing; ORD-B lacks these direct events. Neither is orphaned. Order-to-items is legitimately one-to-many. Add Item E2O and observed `contains` O2O records separately support the recorded memberships, although their extraction provenance might be shared.
- **Item:** every item is added before packing. IT-B1 gains a package without acquiring a second packing event. Existing IT-A1/IT-A2 package memberships have both observed packing E2O and O2O support; IT-B1's two repaired representations share one inference.
- **Package:** PK-7 has one packing event before dispatch. PK-8 has two packing events before dispatch, with one package per Pack Item event. Its cross-order contents are plausible for customer C17. PK-8's delivery remains missing in both representations; no order-exclusive package constraint was supplied.
- **Delivery:** DEL-X has a dispatch and observed `carries` support. DEL-Y has neither E2O nor O2O links and remains an orphan object. DEL-X lacks explicit confirmation. These are incomplete histories, not grounds for manufactured events.
- **Invoice:** INV-44 is issued before PAY-1 is recorded against it. Its single observed billed order has both E2O and O2O support. Total 230 versus declared value 130 is a discrepancy requiring investigation, not an invariant violation.
- **Payment:** each payment has exactly one recording event. PAY-1 has observed invoice participation and `settles` support. PAY-2 is an isolated payment/event component, not an eventless object; its missing invoice is compatible with unapplied cash.

Every event already references an object, so there are no objectless orphan events. The derived Order–Package association is many-to-many: ORD-A spans two packages, while PK-8 contains items from both orders. This is permitted, not a direct O2O relation to invent. Treating P02 as independent corroboration of P01 would be circular. The same warning applies to P03/P04, P09/P10, and P11/P12 in Bundle B; two stored representations are not two independent witnesses.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**OC-DFG.** In the unrepaired log, PK-8 has ev07 → ev10. A inserts ev08 between them, producing a Package-specific `Pack Item → Pack Item` self-loop. `Pack Item → Dispatch Package` remains, but its PK-8 event pair changes from ev07/ev10 to ev08/ev10. PK-7's pair is unchanged.

B additionally creates Delivery-specific `Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery`, supported by DEL-X's ev09/ev11 and DEL-Y's ev10/ev11. Invoice gains `Record Payment → Record Payment` through ev13/ev14. ORD-B gains `Add Item → Issue Invoice` through ev05/ev12. These are direct-follow relations within object histories, not evidence that intermediate business work never occurred. C instead creates an ambiguous, same-timestamp ordering between two DEL-X dispatches; a linearizing implementation may report `Dispatch Package → Dispatch Package`.

**OTG.** Keep explicit qualified O2O structure separate from event-based co-participation. A adds a Package–Item instance link, joining the orders' neighborhoods through PK-8. B adds Delivery–Package, Invoice–Order, and Payment–Invoice instance links. All these type-level O2O patterns already exist, so type adjacency can remain unchanged while multiplicities change. For an OTG derived from E2O co-participation, B also introduces Order–Delivery interaction at ev11; same-type Delivery–Delivery and Order–Order interactions arise if that implementation retains self-type edges. Check the installed routine's projection before interpreting its output as qualified O2O semantics.

**ET–OT graph.** A increases support for the existing `Pack Item—Package` association. B introduces `Confirm Delivery—Delivery`; it increases participation in existing `Dispatch Package—Delivery`, `Issue Invoice—Order`, and `Record Payment—Invoice` associations. O2O additions alone do not create activity participation.

**Flattenings.** Direct E2O flattening yields:

- **Order:** A preserves ORD-A's Create → Add → Add → Confirm → Issue and ORD-B's Create → Add. B appends Issue to ORD-B, duplicating ev12 across two order cases.
- **Package:** A and B give PK-8 Pack → Pack → Dispatch, replacing Pack → Dispatch. Neither gains confirmation without a direct package E2O link.
- **Delivery:** originally and in A, DEL-X has Dispatch only; DEL-Y has no event case. B gives both Dispatch → Confirm, duplicating ev11 across their cases.
- **Invoice:** original/A is Issue → Record; B is Issue → Record → Record.
- **Payment:** both singleton Record traces remain unchanged in A and B; linking an invoice does not propagate its issuance into payment traces.

**Frequencies.** Distinct source-event counts remain unchanged. Below, U counts distinct participating object IDs per activity across types; T counts distinct event–object incidences, ignoring multiple qualifiers for the same pair.

| Activity | Events, all bundles | Unrepaired U/T | A U/T | B U/T |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Pack Item | 3 | 5/5 | 5/6 | 5/6 |
| Dispatch Package | 2 | 3/3 | 3/3 | 4/4 |
| Confirm Delivery | 1 | 1/1 | 1/1 | 3/3 |
| Issue Invoice | 1 | 2/2 | 2/2 | 3/3 |
| Record Payment | 2 | 3/3 | 3/3 | 3/4 |

For type-specific statistics, restrict those definitions to that type: Package-linked Pack events rise from two to three in A, while distinct packages remain two. Event frequencies within an object type can therefore change even though global activity-event frequencies do not. Flattened row counts also increase through event duplication.

**Equivalent-execution clustering around Order.** A leaves direct order traces unchanged, but neighborhood-based extraction can now overlap or join ORD-A and ORD-B executions through PK-8. B adds a shared invoice and joint acknowledgment, expands neighborhoods, and changes ORD-B's direct trace. Shared events may occur in both extracted executions. Neither bundle proves the orders equivalent: item counts, packing structure, and directly related confirmation differ. Cluster results depend on extraction radius, type/attribute abstraction, and shared-event handling.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

The outline assumes canonical OCEL column names. It checks the supplied tables before adding Bundle A and reconstructs denormalized relation metadata from authoritative event/object tables. It does not infer extra relations through enrichment. API availability and the marked constructor call require an installed-version check; this code is an outline, not a claimed execution result.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.ocel.obj import OCEL

# Preserve every original table before normalization or repair.
original = {
    "events": events.copy(deep=True),
    "objects": objects.copy(deep=True),
    "relations": relations.copy(deep=True),
    "o2o": o2o.copy(deep=True),
}
e, ob, r, oo = [original[k].copy(deep=True)
                for k in ("events", "objects", "relations", "o2o")]
EID, OID, OID2 = "ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2"
ACT, TS, TYPE, Q = (
    "ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp", "ocel:type", "ocel:qualifier"
)
assert {EID, ACT, TS}.issubset(e.columns)
assert {OID, TYPE}.issubset(ob.columns)
assert {EID, OID}.issubset(r.columns)
assert {OID, OID2}.issubset(oo.columns)
for frame in (r, oo):
    if Q not in frame.columns:
        frame[Q] = ""
    frame[Q] = frame[Q].fillna("")

def check_integrity(ev, obj, rel, objrel):
    assert ev[EID].notna().all() and ev[EID].is_unique
    assert obj[OID].notna().all() and obj[OID].is_unique
    assert ev[[ACT, TS]].notna().all().all()
    assert obj[TYPE].notna().all()
    assert rel[[EID, OID, Q]].notna().all().all()
    assert objrel[[OID, OID2, Q]].notna().all().all()
    assert rel[EID].isin(ev[EID]).all()
    assert rel[OID].isin(obj[OID]).all()
    assert objrel[OID].isin(obj[OID]).all()
    assert objrel[OID2].isin(obj[OID]).all()
    assert not rel.duplicated([EID, OID, Q]).any()
    assert not objrel.duplicated([OID, OID2, Q]).any()

    # Direction/type contracts for the supplied business qualifiers.
    types = obj.set_index(OID)[TYPE]
    contracts = {
        "contains": ("Order", "Item"),
        "packs": ("Package", "Item"),
        "carries": ("Delivery", "Package"),
        "bills": ("Invoice", "Order"),
        "settles": ("Payment", "Invoice"),
    }
    for qualifier, (source_type, target_type) in contracts.items():
        rows = objrel.loc[objrel[Q].eq(qualifier)]
        assert rows[OID].map(types).eq(source_type).all()
        assert rows[OID2].map(types).eq(target_type).all()

check_integrity(e, ob, r, oo)
before_r = set(r[[EID, OID, Q]].itertuples(index=False, name=None))
before_oo = set(oo[[OID, OID2, Q]].itertuples(index=False, name=None))

# Keep confidence/justification separately as provenance, as in the JSON.
e2o_add = pd.DataFrame([{EID: "ev08", OID: "PK-8", Q: ""}])
o2o_add = pd.DataFrame([{OID: "PK-8", OID2: "IT-B1", Q: "packs"}])
r = pd.concat([r, e2o_add], ignore_index=True, sort=False)
oo = pd.concat([oo, o2o_add], ignore_index=True, sort=False)
check_integrity(e, ob, r, oo)  # Also detects a previously applied addition.
assert before_r.issubset(set(r[[EID, OID, Q]].itertuples(index=False, name=None)))
assert before_oo.issubset(set(oo[[OID, OID2, Q]].itertuples(index=False, name=None)))

# Preserve source clock values; UTC here is a consistent analysis convention
# for the supplied timezone-naive timestamps, not a recovered source timezone.
e[TS] = pd.to_datetime(e[TS], errors="raise", utc=True)
r = r.drop(columns=[ACT, TS, TYPE], errors="ignore")
r = r.merge(e[[EID, ACT, TS]], on=EID, how="left", validate="many_to_one")
r = r.merge(ob[[OID, TYPE]], on=OID, how="left", validate="many_to_one")
check_integrity(e, ob, r, oo)

# Count objects rather than qualified rows. Defaults are audit findings,
# not universal assertions: ev10/ev14 intentionally remain incomplete.
cardinality = (r.groupby([EID, TYPE])[OID].nunique()
               .unstack(fill_value=0))
assert cardinality.loc["ev08", "Item"] == 1
assert cardinality.loc["ev08", "Package"] == 1
assert set(r.loc[r[EID].eq("ev08"), OID]) == {"IT-B1", "PK-8"}
assert e.set_index(EID).loc["ev07", TS] < e.set_index(EID).loc["ev08", TS]
assert e.set_index(EID).loc["ev08", TS] < e.set_index(EID).loc["ev10", TS]

# VERSION-DEPENDENT OCEL CONSTRUCTOR: verify the installed version's
# signature, canonical-column parameters, and support for the o2o keyword.
repaired_ocel = OCEL(events=e, objects=ob, relations=r, o2o=oo)

required_apis = (
    "discover_ocdfg", "discover_otg", "discover_etot", "ocel_flattening"
)
missing = [name for name in required_apis if not hasattr(pm4py, name)]
if missing:
    raise RuntimeError(f"Installed PM4Py lacks required APIs: {missing}")

ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(repaired_ocel)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(repaired_ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(repaired_ocel)
order_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(repaired_ocel, "Order")
delivery_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(repaired_ocel, "Delivery")

# Inspect graph semantics/return formats for the installed version.
# Do not interpret generic O2O enrichment as proof of packs/carries/bills.
# Repeat on fresh original copies with Bundle B's specified additions
# to compare event-object support, traces, and graph edges without leakage.
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. Assigning ev10 to both deliveries to cover uncertainty creates simultaneous double delivery membership; encode competing hypotheses separately.
2. Adding PK-8 to ev09 because dispatch timestamps match creates another package dispatch and possibly a spurious same-time self-loop.
3. Forcing ev11 onto either delivery, or both, fabricates completion and delivery-duration measurements from a wildcard acknowledgment.
4. Propagating ev11 through package contents to ORD-B creates an unsupported Confirm → Issue fragment in Bundle B's order flattening.
5. Using 230 = 130 + 100 as proof of consolidated billing invents ORD-B's invoice participation and shared execution boundaries.
6. Allocating PAY-2 by amount alone can fabricate full invoice settlement; additionally attaching it directly to ORD-A invents an order payment step.
7. Assuming packages cannot mix orders could wrongly move IT-B1 away from PK-8 or discard a legitimate shared shipment.
8. Expanding every O2O neighbor into every event duplicates activities across cases, inflates object-based counts, and makes inferred edges appear self-confirming.
