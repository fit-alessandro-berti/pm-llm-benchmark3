# 1. Mechanism map

The restructuring changes the organization from **three local “clustered” teams with embedded specialists** to a **hub-and-spoke shared-service model**. In process-mining terms, that primarily alters (i) the **handover-of-work / subcontracting networks**, (ii) **waiting-time edges** around specialist and approval activities in a **performance DFG**, and (iii) **role structure and role stability** inferred from resource–activity patterns.

| Restructuring action | Mechanism (why it changes the process) | Observable event-log signals (what should move) | Process-mining measures to track |
|---|---|---|---|
| Merge `N_Tech/S_Tech/E_Tech` into **Technical Shared Service** | Pooling increases utilization and standardization, but introduces a central queue and context boundary | Resources for technical work become a shared pool; more cases route to specialist; more “return-to-caseworker” advice steps | Handover-of-work: edges to/from TechSS; subcontracting edges (caseworker→TechSS→caseworker); waiting after handover into TechSS; role discovery shows a distinct “TechSS” role |
| Centralize **Specialist Review + Legal/Risk + difficult Quality Check** into one shared-service queue | Multi-class queue interference: QC/triage can consume specialist capacity; high utilization can explode queueing delays | Longer waits before specialist/QC events; more ping‑pong when inputs are incomplete; higher reassignment_count for escalations | Performance DFG: time on edges into Specialist Review/Legal/Risk/QC; handover network shows shared queue as a hub; temporal features show bursts/backlogs |
| Create **shared approval queue** (removing one local supervisor) | Standardization may reduce local variance, but can blur accountability and create an approval bottleneck | Higher frequency of caseworker→approval; more “ownership” handovers; possible loops back to caseworker | Handover network: approval as central node; waiting time before approval; variants containing approval rework loops; reassignment_count distribution |
| Routing rules: “all high complexity → TechSS”; specialty detection expands {legal, risk, security} | Better routing can reduce rework; mis-routing increases back-and-forth and queue load | Share of high-complexity cases hitting TechSS rises; share of legal/risk/security cases rises; handover_reason changes (e.g., “misrouted”, “missing info”) | Segment-level performance: by `case_complexity`, `required_specialty`, `priority`; drift in variants and performance DFG edges |
| “Pull completed specialist advice from a shared queue” | Reduces ad hoc cross-team cover, but can create “advice waiting” idle time and coordination overhead | Longer gaps between specialist completion and local follow-up; more cases with multiple resources but unclear owner | Waiting time between specialist event and next local event; number of resources per case; temporal features on post-specialist idle time |

Key effect pathways (mechanism map → measurable effects):

- **Nominal handover reduction**: removing embedded specialists can reduce *within-team* handover loops (caseworker↔embedded tech), but may be offset by more “queue-mediated” handovers (caseworker→TechSS→caseworker, plus approval).  
  - Measures: handovers per case; unique resources per case; frequency of “back-and-forth” subsequences (variants) involving specialist and approval steps.

- **Central queue formation**: shared services create hub nodes; under load, waiting times become highly non-linear and tails fatten.  
  - Measures: handover network hub-ness (share of edges touching TechSS/approval/legal/risk); median and P90/P95 waiting time after those handovers; performance DFG edge times into shared-service activities.

- **Specialist utilization**: pooling can raise utilization (management target +15%), but higher utilization can increase queueing delay disproportionately near capacity.  
  - Measures: per-resource and per-role service-time totals; active time vs calendar time proxies; queue waiting times as leading indicator of “utilization too high”.

- **Rework**: standardization can reduce inconsistent decisions, but context loss + weaker informal local advice can increase rework for complex or region-specific cases.  
  - Measures: `rework_flag`; rework cases per activity (especially QC, Specialist Review, Implementation); repeated activities within a case; rework rate conditional on `local_expertise_flag`.

- **Local expertise loss**: routing “hard cases away” reduces local learning and informal prevention of referrals; effects may show first as more referrals and later as quality drift.  
  - Measures: trend in `local_expertise_flag`; decreasing share of technical assessment done locally; increasing referrals per complexity band; rising reassignments for “clarification”.

- **Role clarity**: shared approval and queue-mediated work can blur “who owns the case” even if tasks are standardized.  
  - Measures: rising `reassignment_count`; more handovers with reasons like “ownership/clarification”; increased number of resources per case; organizational roles become less stable over time (role drift).

- **Resilience to absence**: pooling can improve resilience to a single specialist’s absence, but increases **single-point-of-failure** risk if the central queue is small or coupled to QC/legal/risk.  
  - Measures: sensitivity of waiting times to absences/peaks (temporal features); backlog propagation across all teams when shared resources are constrained; variance of cycle time over time.

- **Fairness across teams/customer segments**: central queues can equalize service, or amplify disparities via priority rules, regional accommodation needs, and information-quality differences.  
  - Measures: group gaps (North vs South vs East; customer_segment; location; language/regional accommodation proxies) in median and tail cycle times, waiting times, SLA misses; disparity indices (e.g., ratio of P90s) controlling for complexity/priority.

# 2. Scenario forecasts

All scenarios assume Intake and simple Eligibility stay local; differences come from **capacity, queue discipline, routing accuracy, knowledge capture (case notes), and how tightly QC/legal/risk are coupled to the same shared queue**.

## Optimistic scenario (well-staffed shared services + strong knowledge capture)

- **Handover pattern**
  - Cross-team handovers drop materially (ad hoc cover largely replaced by predictable TechSS/approval handovers).
  - Caseworker→TechSS increases mainly for genuinely high-complexity cases; “bounce-back” loops stay low (few repeated referrals).
  - Approval handovers rise but remain mostly single-pass (few approval→caseworker→approval loops).

- **Queue/waiting-time pattern**
  - Median waits near the management estimate (TechSS ~4h; approval ~3h; legal/risk ~10–11h) with controlled tails (P90 doesn’t blow up).
  - Waiting time after specialist completion (specialist→next local action) remains short due to clear pull/ownership rules.

- **Cycle-time distribution**
  - Medians improve slightly overall; **East improves the most** (pooling relieves its previously overloaded technical specialist).
  - Tail (P90/P95) is stable or improves for East and does not worsen for North/South because shared services absorb variability effectively.

- **Rework and quality pattern**
  - Rework stable or slightly down, especially for specialty cases due to more consistent decisions and better routing.
  - QC rework does not increase because difficult QC capacity is protected from being crowded out by legal/risk triage.

- **Role clarity and accountability**
  - “Case owner” remains the local caseworker; shared-service work is clearly subcontracted with SLAs and structured handover_reason codes.
  - `reassignment_count` stays flat; organizational roles become clearer (a distinct TechSS role, clear approval role).

- **Fairness implications**
  - Team-level disparities narrow (East catches up); customer-segment gaps do not widen because routing and advice quality are consistent.

- **Early warning signs (if this scenario is failing)**
  - Rising P90 waiting times into TechSS/approval despite stable medians.
  - Increasing specialist→caseworker→specialist short-cycle loops (signals misrouting or missing information).
  - Segment-level tail gaps emerging (specific customer_segment/location repeatedly in the slow tail).

## Central scenario (capacity adequate but tight; mixed note quality; modest coupling effects)

- **Handover pattern**
  - Nominal cross-team handovers fall, but queue-mediated handovers rise as expected; some increase in repeated specialist touchpoints for complex cases.
  - North/South lose some informal local tech “pre-check”, causing slightly more referrals than planned for medium complexity.

- **Queue/waiting-time pattern**
  - TechSS and approval medians modestly worse than estimate (e.g., TechSS 5–7h; approval 3–5h), with noticeable tail growth.
  - Legal/risk waits increase more than planned because these specialists were already near capacity; variability increases during peaks.

- **Cycle-time distribution**
  - Average/median cycle time may look flat or slightly improved (fewer ad hoc transfers and more automation), but **P90/P95 worsen**, especially for high-complexity and specialty cases.
  - East median improves; North/South may be flat or slightly worse; the main change is a heavier tail.

- **Rework and quality pattern**
  - Rework increases slightly for high-complexity cases due to context loss and more back-and-forth clarification.
  - QC-related rework rises in specific weeks (when shared queue is congested) rather than continuously.

- **Role clarity and accountability**
  - `reassignment_count` increases modestly, concentrated around shared approval and specialist interfaces.
  - Organizational roles remain identifiable but show more overlap (approval staff doing triage; specialists doing QC triage).

- **Fairness implications**
  - Fairness risk appears as **tail unfairness**: certain teams/segments disproportionately occupy the slowest decile (e.g., accommodation-heavy segments or teams with weaker note standardization).
  - East benefits on average; some segments in North/South show rising waits due to loss of local expertise.

- **Early warning signs**
  - Growing dispersion of waiting times by team/segment while overall median is stable.
  - Rising frequency of “handover_reason=clarification/missing info/misrouted”.
  - Legal/risk queue time increasing week-over-week (a sign of approaching unstable utilization).

## Adverse scenario (central bottleneck + strong coupling + degraded local expertise)

- **Handover pattern**
  - Central hubs dominate: large jump in caseworker→TechSS, caseworker→legal/risk, and repeated referrals; cross-team handovers can rebound (workarounds/escapes).
  - Cases show multi-pass patterns (caseworker→TechSS→caseworker→approval→caseworker→TechSS…), increasing handovers per case even if some local handovers disappeared.

- **Queue/waiting-time pattern**
  - TechSS and approval become unstable queues: median waits climb (e.g., TechSS >8h) and tails explode (P90 measured in days).
  - Legal/risk congestion spills into QC and vice versa because they share a queue; “difficult QC” starves or blocks specialist throughput.
  - Post-specialist idle time grows (advice done but not picked up), revealing ownership ambiguity.

- **Cycle-time distribution**
  - **Average cycle time may remain deceptively stable** if low-complexity cases are fast, but the distribution bifurcates: a long tail of complex/specialty cases dominates SLA misses.
  - East may improve slightly in median (pooling helps), but still sees severe tail issues because the bottleneck is now shared across all teams.

- **Rework and quality pattern**
  - Rework rises sharply for complex/specialty cases (more rework_flag; more repeated QC and Specialist Review).
  - Outcome quality drifts (more adverse outcomes, more inconsistent approvals), lagging behind the immediate queue symptoms.

- **Role clarity and accountability**
  - Ownership becomes unclear: reassignment_count rises across all teams; handover_reason shifts toward “ownership”, “escalation”, “missing info”.
  - Role discovery shows blurred boundaries (resources across many activities) and role instability over time.

- **Fairness implications**
  - Fairness deteriorates: specific customer segments and locations systematically experience higher tail delays and higher SLA-miss probability even after controlling for complexity/priority.
  - Priority rules can create “fairness debt” (low-priority groups wait extremely long), increasing perceived unfairness and complaints.

- **Early warning signs**
  - Rapid week-over-week increase in waiting-time quantiles for central queue entries.
  - Rising share of cases with multiple specialist touches and high reassignment_count.
  - Tail gaps across teams/segments widen quickly (e.g., P90 East improves while P90 North/South worsens, or vice versa).

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

Stable or improved average cycle time can hide important failure modes. At minimum, watch the following (each has a concrete log signature):

- **Tail latency inflation**: median stable but P90/P95 grows, especially around TechSS/legal/risk/approval waits.  
  - Log signal: rising upper quantiles of waiting time after central handovers; performance DFG edges into specialist/approval lengthen.

- **High-complexity case harm**: averages dominated by low/medium complexity; high-complexity cases become the “sacrifice cohort.”  
  - Log signal: segmented quantiles by `case_complexity=high` worsen; more specialist bounce-backs; higher rework_flag in high complexity.

- **Bottleneck shift (local → central) without visible mean change**: local work speeds up while shared queues slow down; mean can net out.  
  - Log signal: local activity durations shrink; central queue waiting grows; hub nodes dominate handover network.

- **Local knowledge loss and learning erosion**: initial standardization may look good; degradation appears later as local staff stop practicing technical work and referral volume creeps up.  
  - Log signal: `local_expertise_flag` trend down; rising specialist referral rate at the same complexity; role drift over months.

- **Fairness degradation (distributional)**: one segment/team repeatedly lands in the slow tail even when global averages are OK.  
  - Log signal: growing team/segment gaps in P90/P95 cycle time and shared-service waiting; elevated SLA misses for specific segments after controlling for complexity/priority.

- **Accountability/role clarity erosion**: shared approval and queue pulling can diffuse ownership; work is “done” but not progressed.  
  - Log signal: rising reassignment_count; longer gaps after approvals/advice; more handover_reason tags indicating ownership/clarification.

- **Exception handling and workaround growth**: teams may bypass queues (calls, emails) or create unlogged steps; the system log shows strange gaps or sudden jumps.  
  - Log signal: increased variance; longer “silent” periods between events; new variants with irregular loops; cross-team handovers reappear as workarounds.

- **Resilience fragility**: pooling helps ordinary variability but can create a single point of failure; averages look fine until a shock.  
  - Log signal: strong correlation between shared-service absences/peaks and system-wide waiting spikes; temporal features show synchronized slowdowns across all teams.

# 5. Pilot design

A full rollout is high-risk because it couples previously semi-independent queues. Use a pilot that is **phased, segmented, and guarded by tail/fairness metrics**.

- **Pilot scope**
  - Phase 0 (2–4 weeks): standardize case notes/handovers across locations (address the known issue that cross-team cover fails due to inconsistent notes).
  - Phase 1 (6–10 weeks): pilot **Technical Shared Service only**, but start with **East** as the first adopting team (expected to benefit due to current overload).
  - Phase 2 (6–10 weeks): expand TechSS to one of North/South; introduce shared approval only for a narrow subset (e.g., medium/high-value) with explicit ownership rules.
  - Phase 3: only if guardrails hold, test coupling of difficult QC with specialist queues (or keep QC separate if coupling proves harmful).

- **Comparison group**
  - Use a **stepped-wedge** rollout: teams not yet migrated serve as contemporaneous controls (e.g., East treated first, North/South controls; then one of North/South treated while the other controls).
  - Within a team, optionally keep a small band of cases on “legacy local tech consult” to form an internal control for learning/quality (careful to avoid cherry-picking by complexity).

- **Pre/post observation window**
  - Pre: at least 8 weeks (captures weekly seasonality); Post: at least 8 weeks after a 1–2 week stabilization/ramp.
  - Analyze in weekly slices to detect emerging instability and fairness drift early.

- **Segmentation (required)**
  - By **team** (North/South/East), **case_complexity** (low/med/high), **required_specialty** (none vs legal/risk/security vs technical), **customer_segment**, plus **priority** and **location**.
  - Always report both **central tendency** (median) and **tails** (P90/P95) per segment.

- **Guardrail metrics (stop-the-line thresholds)**
  - Shared-service waiting time: P90 wait into TechSS/approval/legal-risk must not exceed a predefined threshold (e.g., +30% vs baseline) for high complexity or specialty cases.
  - Tail cycle time: P95 cycle time for high complexity must not increase beyond a preset margin, even if median improves.
  - Rework: rework_flag rate and QC/implementation rework cases per activity must not exceed baseline + tolerance.
  - Fairness: max segment gap (e.g., ratio of P90 cycle times across customer segments) must not widen beyond tolerance after controlling for complexity/priority.
  - Role clarity: reassignment_count and “ownership/clarification” handover_reason share must not trend upward week-over-week.
  - Utilization safety: keep legal/risk and TechSS utilization below a safe operating band; rising utilization with rising waits is a trigger to add capacity or decouple queues.

- **Staff feedback collection**
  - Weekly short pulse survey for caseworkers and shared-service staff: perceived clarity of ownership, time lost to coordination, perceived fairness, learning opportunities.
  - Structured tagging in `handover_reason` (add/standardize categories for “misrouted”, “missing info”, “ownership”, “regional accommodation”, “absence cover”) to connect qualitative signals to log data.
  - Targeted interviews for East vs North/South to capture heterogeneous effects (East likely benefits earlier; North/South may feel expertise loss).

- **Rollback or adjustment triggers**
  - Immediate rollback/decouple if: (i) P90 waiting into shared services accelerates for 2+ consecutive weeks, (ii) fairness gaps expand sharply, (iii) legal/risk queue time rises persistently (already near capacity), or (iv) reassignment_count and ownership-related handovers spike.
  - Adjustment options before rollback: add capacity, split queues (QC separate from legal/risk), improve intake/routing accuracy, introduce local tech “office hours” for informal prevention of unnecessary referrals.

# 6. Recommendation

**Pilot first, with modifications**, and make full rollout conditional on tail, fairness, and role-clarity indicators—not on early averages.

- Proceed to a **phased pilot** because the proposal has plausible benefits (pooling can relieve East; standardization can reduce inconsistent specialist decisions) but also credible systemic risks (central bottlenecks, coupling of legal/risk/QC, learning loss, fairness).
- Modify the design before piloting:
  - **Avoid a single undifferentiated shared-service queue** for Tech + legal/risk + difficult QC unless capacity and discipline are proven; start with **separate queues or protected capacity** for legal/risk and QC.
  - Preserve **local expertise and learning** via local technical “liaisons/office hours” or rotating local staff through TechSS, and by requiring shared-service advice to include structured knowledge for local reuse.
  - Define **case ownership** explicitly: local caseworker remains accountable; shared services operate as subcontractors with SLAs and clear handover_reason taxonomy.

What management should **not infer from early averages**:

- A stable or improved mean cycle time can coexist with **worsened tails** (especially for high-complexity and specialty cases) and **unfairness** across segments.
- Early improvements can reflect **selection effects** (e.g., smoother cases completing faster) while complex cases queue up, showing harm later.
- Higher measured utilization is not “free”: when utilization rises near capacity, **queueing delays can grow non-linearly**, and coupling QC/legal/risk can create hidden bottlenecks that only appear under peaks/absences.

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

# --- Load + format ---
df = pd.read_csv("event_log.csv")
df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"], utc=True)

df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)

# If required by your PM4Py version:
log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)

change_ts = pd.Timestamp("2026-09-01", tz="UTC")  # set to pilot/rollout start
log_pre = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(log, "time:timestamp", [])  # placeholder if you keep as log
df_pre = df[df["time:timestamp"] < change_ts].copy()
df_post = df[df["time:timestamp"] >= change_ts].copy()
log_pre = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df_pre)
log_post = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df_post)

# --- Helpers ---
def case_cycle_hours(df0: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.Series:
    g = df0.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"]
    return (g.max() - g.min()).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0

def q(x):
    x = np.asarray(x)
    return {"p50": float(np.quantile(x, 0.50)),
            "p90": float(np.quantile(x, 0.90)),
            "p95": float(np.quantile(x, 0.95))}

def filt(df0, **attr_to_values):
    out = df0
    for attr, vals in attr_to_values.items():
        out = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(out, attr, list(vals))
    return out

# --- 1) Handover-of-work network (structure + key edges) ---
hon_pre = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log_pre, resource_key="org:resource")
hon_post = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log_post, resource_key="org:resource")
# Extract edge weights / focus on hub nodes (TechSS, shared approval, legal/risk resources)

# --- 2) Working-together and subcontracting networks ---
wt_pre = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log_pre, resource_key="org:resource")
wt_post = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log_post, resource_key="org:resource")

sub_pre = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(log_pre, resource_key="org:resource")
sub_post = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(log_post, resource_key="org:resource")
# Subcontracting is especially useful for caseworker→shared service→caseworker patterns.

# --- 3) Variants and performance DFG (path + waiting hotspots) ---
variants_pre = pm4py.get_variants(log_pre)
variants_post = pm4py.get_variants(log_post)

dfg_pre = pm4py.discover_dfg(log_pre)
dfg_post = pm4py.discover_dfg(log_post)

perf_dfg_pre = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log_pre)
perf_dfg_post = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log_post)
# Look for growth in performance on edges into Specialist Review / Legal / Risk / Approval / Quality Check.

# --- 4) Service and waiting times ---
log_pre_sw = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log_pre)
log_post_sw = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log_post)

service_pre = pm4py.get_service_time(log_pre)   # aggregate/inspect by activity/resource/role
service_post = pm4py.get_service_time(log_post)

# --- 5) Resource similarity + organizational roles (role clarity / drift) ---
sim_pre = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log_pre, activity_key="concept:name", resource_key="org:resource")
sim_post = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log_post, activity_key="concept:name", resource_key="org:resource")

roles_pre = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log_pre, activity_key="concept:name", resource_key="org:resource")
roles_post = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log_post, activity_key="concept:name", resource_key="org:resource")
# Track role count, role-to-activity entropy, and stability over weekly windows.

# --- 6) Filtering by team / complexity / specialty / customer segment (heterogeneity + fairness) ---
segments = [
    ("East_high", dict(team=["East"], case_complexity=["high"])),
    ("North_high", dict(team=["North"], case_complexity=["high"])),
    ("South_high", dict(team=["South"], case_complexity=["high"])),
    ("LegalRisk", dict(required_specialty=["legal", "risk", "security"])),
]

for name, flt in segments:
    pre_s = filt(df_pre, **flt)
    post_s = filt(df_post, **flt)
    ct_pre = case_cycle_hours(pre_s).dropna()
    ct_post = case_cycle_hours(post_s).dropna()
    print(name, "cycle_hours pre", q(ct_pre), "post", q(ct_post))

# Fairness: compare tails across customer segments within the same complexity/priority band
for seg in sorted(df["customer_segment"].dropna().unique()):
    pre_seg = filt(df_pre, customer_segment=[seg], case_complexity=["high"])
    post_seg = filt(df_post, customer_segment=[seg], case_complexity=["high"])
    if pre_seg["case:concept:name"].nunique() < 30 or post_seg["case:concept:name"].nunique() < 30:
        continue
    print("segment", seg, "high-complex pre", q(case_cycle_hours(pre_seg)), "post", q(case_cycle_hours(post_seg)))

# --- 7) Rework and SLA misses ---
rework_pre = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(log_pre)
rework_post = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(log_post)

# SLA miss example (define SLA thresholds by priority externally):
SLA_HOURS = {"low": 240, "medium": 168, "high": 72}  # example only
def sla_miss_rate(df0):
    ct = case_cycle_hours(df0)
    # join priority per case (take first non-null per case)
    pr = df0.groupby("case:concept:name")["priority"].first()
    miss = ct > pr.map(SLA_HOURS)
    return float(miss.mean())

print("SLA miss pre", sla_miss_rate(df_pre), "post", sla_miss_rate(df_post))
print("SLA miss East pre", sla_miss_rate(filt(df_pre, team=["East"])),
      "post", sla_miss_rate(filt(df_post, team=["East"])))

# --- 8) Resilience / temporal effects (peaks, absences, synchronized slowdowns) ---
temp_pre = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(df_pre)
temp_post = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(df_post)
# Use temporal features to spot weekly spikes, synchronized slowdowns across teams, and sensitivity to shared-service constraints.
```
