# 1. Queue-by-queue diagnosis

Assumptions used throughout:

- `A1`: An appointment is a target/protected window, not an absolute guarantee of exact start. This is needed because `A06` starts after 09:15 while `D06` is held to exactly 11:30.
- `A2`: Multi-skill requirements such as `firmware+electrical` require one simultaneously qualified resource, not two sequential handoffs. This is needed to interpret `D05` and `D07`.
- `A3`: A job is usually not started if it is expected to overlap a known unavailability unless an interruption is explicitly logged. This is needed for `D-F` idling before the 12:00 meeting.
- `A4`: For interrupted work in `QB`, comparisons to shortest-job logic use estimated remaining time = `est_min` minus minutes already served. This is needed to evaluate `B01` at 09:31 and 10:14.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

**QA**

- Supporting cases: `A02`, `A05`, and `A07` support FIFO fallback. Each is started ahead of either a higher-priority or shorter alternative when no appointment is clearly due.
- Supporting cases: `A03` and `A06` support the secondary appointment component. `A03` is honored exactly at 08:20, while `A06` is not started early but is taken once it is overdue.
- Challenging case: `A03` is a direct violation of pure FIFO. This is well explained by the known appointment field and does not require an undocumented exception.
- Challenging case: `A04` and `A08` show that visible priority is not the main rule. That does not challenge the FIFO-plus-appointment diagnosis; it challenges a pure priority explanation.

**QB**

- Supporting cases: `B02` supports a preemptive-urgency component because it interrupts `B01` mid-service.
- Supporting cases: `B01` supports a resume/FIFO component because it is resumed at 09:31 and again preferred at 10:14 over higher-priority or shorter waiting jobs.
- Supporting case: `B03` at 09:48 supports FIFO fallback because it is the oldest waiting job and is chosen over both a shorter (`B04`) and a higher-priority (`B05`) alternative.
- Challenging case: `B05` at 10:00 challenges any simple FIFO-or-resume story. It can be absorbed into the hybrid diagnosis as a manual priority intervention, but that intervention is not predictable from visible fields alone.
- Challenging case: `B07` challenges any appointment explanation because its 10:30 appointment is not honored either at 10:28 or 10:36. This needs no undocumented exception if appointments in `QB` are purely informational; otherwise it implies a hidden override.

**QC**

- Supporting cases: `C02`, `C03`, and `C06` support SPT. In all three decisions, a relatively short job is chosen while FIFO, priority, and deadline-based alternatives point elsewhere.
- Supporting case: `C01` being executed last among the initial backlog is exactly the kind of starvation pattern SPT can create.
- Challenging case: `C05` at 10:54 violates pure SPT because `C06` is shorter. This can be explained by a documented-or-undocumented SLA rescue mode keyed to lateness risk and priority, so it does not necessarily falsify SPT as the baseline.
- Challenging case: `C01` at 11:40 over `C07` violates pure SPT and pure deadline logic. The cleanest explanation is anti-starvation aging; without that, an undocumented exception is required.
- Challenging case: `C04` at 10:32 is the hardest to explain. It conflicts with pure FIFO, pure priority, pure SPT, and pure deadline rescue. This is the one episode most likely to depend on missing readiness/state information or an undocumented batching rule.

**QD**

- Supporting cases: `D05` and `D10` strongly support skill-based routing. `D05` waits for `D-F`, and `D10` is taken by `D-X` while `D-F` is still unavailable.
- Supporting cases: `D04` then `D08` on `D-M`, and `D07` over `D10` on `D-X`, support local FIFO within resource-feasible subsets rather than global priority or SPT.
- Supporting case: `D06` supports the appointment component because `D-X` appears to be held for its 11:30 slot.
- Challenging case: the 11:20-11:30 idle period on `D-X` challenges pure skill-based routing. It is plausibly explained by appointment reservation for `D06`; otherwise a hidden blocked state is needed.
- Challenging case: the 11:36-12:20 idle period on `D-F` challenges any pure work-conserving rule. Assumption `A3` explains it reasonably, but without a "cannot start across meeting" flag this remains an inferred exception rather than a directly observed one.

Second, several competing explanations make the same immediate prediction but imply different earlier choices; `QC` is the clearest example, where a true priority/deadline rule would have pulled `C05` forward well before 10:54.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

| rank | exception or alternative explanation | plausibility | how it would alter the diagnosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unlogged reservation or blocked-state fields on resources (`reserved for appointment`, `not ready to start long job before break`, setup/cleanup) | very high | Would make `QD` look less like an undocumented hybrid and more like a clean skill-based queue with explicit reservations; it would also make `QA`'s calibration gap and `QB`'s interventions easier to interpret |
| 2 | A hidden urgency/SLA flag distinct from visible numeric priority | high | Would reconcile `QB`'s dramatic preemption of `B01` by `B02` and `QC`'s eventual rescue of `C05` without claiming that numeric priority is the whole dispatch rule |
| 3 | Per-resource subqueues or early assignment ownership inside one logical queue | high-moderate | Would strengthen the "local FIFO after routing" reading in `QD` and reduce the apparent contradictions caused by comparing all waiting jobs globally |
| 4 | Arrival scan time differs from true queue-entry or ready-for-dispatch time | moderate | Could blur a few close FIFO calls, especially around appointments, but it is too small to overturn the major patterns such as `D05` waiting for `D-F` or `B02` preempting `B01` |
| 5 | Nonpreemptibility/minimum uninterrupted block and explicit avoidance of jobs that would cross a known interruption | moderate | Would directly explain `D-F` idling from 11:36 to 12:20 and would support the idea that `QB`'s interruptions are exceptional rather than ordinary dispatch points |
| 6 | Noisy or strategically biased `est_min` values | moderate-low | Would weaken the confidence in `QC` as SPT, because the apparent shortest-job order could be an artifact of estimate quality rather than the actual rule |

The most important practical point is that several "violations" do not force a different queue discipline if one missing operational field is added. `QD` is the clearest case: a reservation flag would explain most of what otherwise looks hybrid.

# 7. Final synthesis

Across the four queues, the most plausible reading is: `QA` is mainly FIFO with appointment overrides; `QB` is not stationary and looks like a hybrid in which urgent arrivals can preempt but ordinary flow often falls back to resume/FIFO; `QC` looks SPT-like until deadline/aging exceptions appear; `QD` is dominated by skill-based routing with local FIFO behavior and explicit appointment/resource reservations layered on top.

`QD` is the most identifiable queue because hard skill constraints and resource availability sharply limit the feasible decision set. `QB` is the least identifiable because the logged interruption reasons imply manual or hidden-state interventions, so a single clean queue discipline is not consistently visible in the trace.

The most dangerous policy to infer from these observations alone is that visible numeric priority governs dispatch. In `QA` it is repeatedly ignored, in `QB` one dramatic preemption would tempt overgeneralization, and in `QD` skill feasibility dominates priority. A close second is pure SPT in `QC`: the early sequence looks textbook-shortest-first, but later rescue and anti-starvation behavior show why start order alone is not enough.
