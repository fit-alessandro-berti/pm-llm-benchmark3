# 1. System boundary and assumptions

Measure the impact attributable to an order from receipt and availability checking through picking or production, packing, every shipment leg, delivery or cancellation, and downstream returns, repairs, and replacements. Include warehouse and production energy where measured, including setups, idle time, CNC, heat treatment, inspection, and rework. Count raw material consumed and scrapped, packaging used and discarded, and inventory made or held under the new rules that later becomes obsolete. Count split deliveries, reverse freight, and an attributable share of empty vehicle movements. Track electricity in kWh, transport in kgCO₂e, and materials in kg. Where reliable factors exist, estimate the upstream emissions of electricity and changed material or packaging consumption and disclose the factors and coverage. Exclude capital equipment, infrastructure, unchanged upstream manufacture of purchased stock, and customer use and end-of-life of installed parts. Track operational downtime as a service outcome.

Use all received orders for the primary cohort, including cancellations, and report absolute totals alongside impact per received order and per fulfilled line. Compare like product and service segments. The flattened order log can obscure which line waited, which shipment carried it, and which order shared a batch. Link order, line, batch, shipment, and return records. Allocate a shared batch or trip once using a documented driver such as machine time, load, or space used, and report sensitivity to the allocation. Key uncertainties include carrier load and empty mileage, electricity and material factors, missing outcomes, and the lag between production, returns, and write-offs. The supplied path averages are observational and reflect different mixes of urgency, parts, and routes.

The segment mix puts roughly 87% of reported order energy and 85% of reported scrap in Made-to-order. Critical-downtime contributes roughly 41% of reported transport emissions on 11% of orders. These figures guide where to start and use only the impacts captured in the supplied averages.

# 3. Intervention options

1. **Priority pick and ground routing (S1, S4).** Start with Standard-stock Service-contract orders, where SP1 violations affect 22% of cases. Give eligible orders priority picking and book ground when the end-to-end probability of keeping the promise exceeds 85% and the routing decision meets the contract's service requirements. Include inventory readiness, pick and carrier cutoffs, destination reliability, and disruption status in the estimate. Keep air available for genuine short slack or carrier disruption. Record the options, predicted arrival probabilities, policy decision, pick-to-dispatch time, actual delivery, and lateness for both prioritized and displaced orders.
2. **Twice-daily truck consolidation (S5).** Offer scheduled departures for non-urgent stock orders with enough slack for the 12–24-hour hold and a credible delivery buffer. Pool consignments on lanes with sufficient volume and release to parcel ground if a truck cutoff or route disruption threatens the promise. Monitor Package standard → Consolidated truck, actual dispatch wait, utilization, route emissions, rebookings, and the tail of delivery time.
3. **Line-aware availability and partial-shipment rules (S11).** The DFG records 1,010 Pick from stock → Partial shipment transitions. The selected ground-plus-backorder variant averages 7.2 kgCO₂e and is 24% late. Check available-to-promise stock and credible replenishment dates for every line before releasing freight. Send one shipment when all lines can meet the commitment together, and send an early subset when a contract or a customer's operational need warrants it. Track inventory accuracy, first and final delivery, cancellations, contacts, shipment legs, and whether prevented splits create whole-order holds or extra emergency releases.
4. **Capped dynamic CNC and heat-treatment batches (S2).** Group compatible, confirmed Made-to-order work and release when the load is efficient or the earliest order reaches its segment cap or latest safe start, whichever comes first. Compute the latest safe start from the promise and remaining production, inspection, packing, and transit time. Coordinate both batch queues so they do not consume the same slack twice. CNC_A drops from 2.8 to 1.3 kWh per item across the observed size bands while median batch wait rises from 4 to 38 hours. Heat-treatment batch waits are associated with 1.7× late-delivery risk. Track both queues, per-item energy, cap releases, urgent capacity, downstream expedites, and aged or written-off output.
5. **Selective CNC_B-to-CNC_A routing (S3).** Target compatible Made-to-order parts that can finish on CNC_A with adequate slack. Observed rework is 18% on CNC_B and 9% on CNC_A, and the selected CNC_B rework variant averages 32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap, and 32% lateness. Reducing repeat machining can save energy and material. Retain CNC_B for tight schedules, capacity pressure, or outage cover when the quality plan supports its use. Compare like parts and specifications, and track machine queues, first-pass yield, repeat energy and scrap, late deliveries, and air bookings after machining.
6. **Risk-based inspection with protected capacity (S7).** Use a targeted gate for high-rework machine and part combinations and staff it to avoid rushed checks. The DFG shows 470 Inspection → Rework production transitions, and the log links rushed inspection to later defects. Earlier detection can avoid packing, shipping, and replacing a defective part. Track the inspection queue, time and stage of detection, repeat machining, false rejects, customer escapes, and returns. Recorded in-process rework may rise initially as more defects are caught before shipment.
7. **Packaging compatibility and Q1 enforcement (S6).** Check actual route, part geometry and weight, and inspection certification before packing and again after an incompatible route change. Cover both Package standard and Package reinforced → Rework packaging. Enforce reinforced packaging for Made-to-order parts over 8 kg unless inspection certifies low damage risk. Reinforcement doubles packaging material in the supplied comparison and reduces damage-related returns by 40% for heavy Made-to-order parts. Review total packaging and avoided replacements together. Track weight, pack type and mass, route changes, certified exceptions, repacking, and damage returns.
8. **Critical-downtime triage (S9).** At availability check, distinguish verified production stoppage from precautionary urgency using stock availability, the customer's outage status, and the time by which the part is needed. Give uncertain or confirmed downtime rapid escalation. Offer reliable stock and ground to precautionary orders where the promise can be met. The observed Critical stock/ground exception is 9% late versus 3% on the emergency-production dedicated-van variant, so test only eligible cases. Log the triage decision, later reclassification, promised and actual receipt, and downtime consequences.
9. **Shared emergency courier lanes (S8).** Pool compatible Critical-downtime pickups and destinations where the courier can guarantee the required departure and delivery. Dispatch a dedicated van when pooling would endanger the commitment or when shared capacity fails, with explicit SP2 justification. Verify reductions in actual trips and empty mileage, including repositioning, and monitor pickup and delivery tails, lane capacity, fallback activations, and disruption performance.
10. **Carbon-aware promise negotiation (S10).** Offer Regular Made-to-order customers a realistic later date when the extra time would enable an efficient batch or ground shipment. Preserve the original requested date, the quoted options, and the accepted commitment. Respect a customer's need for the earlier date and release the order promptly once committed. Track acceptance, order abandonment and repeat contacts, actual dates against both requested and agreed dates, batch waits, freight mode, and impact per fulfilled line.
11. **Decision and exception logging (S12).** At routing and packaging decisions, record feasible alternatives, reason codes, approvals where required, and eventual outcomes. Use the record to address repeat avoidable expedites, repacking, and policy gaps. The 31% of dedicated-van cases missing SP2 justification identifies a documentation gap to investigate. Use a quick capture at dispatch for urgent orders and review it afterward. Track completeness, time to authorize, repeated overrides, and service outcomes.

# 5. Rebound and displacement risks

- **Batching and expedited freight.** Savings at CNC_A or heat treatment can be offset when long holds lead to air shipments, cancellations, or emergency runs. Apply one end-to-end slack budget to both queues and count downstream expedites on the affected orders.
- **Transport and displaced capacity.** Priority picking can make other stock orders late and generate air freight elsewhere. Underfilled truck departures or courier repositioning can erase transport savings. Measure emissions and service across all orders sharing the pick shift or carrier lane, including empty legs.
- **Packaging and reverse logistics.** Lighter packaging can increase damage, returns, and replacement production. Blanket reinforcement can increase waste on low-risk parts. Apply Q1 and the compatibility matrix, then measure first shipment, return, and replacement as one outcome.
- **Rework and bottlenecks.** Migration to CNC_A can overload the machine or push downstream inspections into rush work. A broadly applied gate can also create inspection queues and late freight. Track queues, defect escapes, rework hours, and actual scrap across both machines and the cell.
- **Obsolescence and inventory.** Producing extra items to fill a batch or adding stock to prevent splits can move impact into unsold inventory. The 6% unshipped after 14 days when forecasts are wrong is an early signal. Tie releases to demand, track aging by batch, and follow the units through final disposition.
- **Customer behavior.** Longer quoted dates or unexpected complete-order holds can prompt earlier or duplicate orders, cancellations, more urgent requests, or outside purchases. Track cancellations and linked reorders, collect feedback, and compare service against the customer's originally requested date as well as the accepted promise.
- **Resilience and concentration.** Consolidating onto a single route or relying on CNC_A may look efficient in normal operations and fail during a carrier outage or machine breakdown. Check the independence and usable capacity of fallback routes, machines, and inspection resources under surge demand.

# 6. Balanced recommendation

Start with priority-pick ground for eligible Standard-stock Service-contract orders, line-aware availability, and packaging compatibility checks. These changes address documented avoidable air, split freight, and packaging loops while preserving expedited service and early shipment of needed lines. Pilot twice-daily consolidation on non-urgent lanes with reliable demand and parcel-ground backup. Use end-to-end delivery probability and the applicable contract requirements at every routing decision.

For Made-to-order, implement capped CNC and heat-treatment batching alongside selective CNC_A assignment and a staffed inspection gate. The package addresses energy at setup and scrap from repeat machining while controlling both queues. Keep qualified CNC_B capacity for overflow and disruption, and reserve urgent slots. Offer flexible promises to Regular customers whose schedules genuinely accommodate the production or ground-service window.

For Critical-downtime, use quick triage and test shared lanes where pickup, delivery, and fallback capacity are verified. Keep air, dedicated vans, and emergency production available where the promise or downtime exposure warrants them. For each Service-contract route, verify an alternate with usable capacity during a disruption. Choose lower-impact paths among options that meet the delivery commitment, packaging rules, and fallback requirements. Use the exception log across all three segments to review decisions and refine eligibility as evidence accumulates.

# 7. Validation and governance plan

- **Establish a traceable baseline.** Link line readiness, batches, inspections, shipments, cancellations, returns, and write-offs back to the received order. Reconcile the 12,000 starts with the 11,730 reported terminal transitions and distinguish open orders from missing or incomplete records. Validate energy units, batch duplication, and freight allocation. Use timestamped decisions and the information available at decision time. A DFG edge is a transition count. Check combined emergency and partial flows using case and line traces and data-aware rules.
- **Pilot with credible comparisons.** Run routing and batching recommendations in shadow mode to check predicted delivery and release decisions against real outcomes. Then compare concurrent controls or use a staggered rollout across lanes, shifts, or machine groups that share capacity. Match or adjust for part mix, weight and geometry, destination, available slack, backlog, demand, and disruption. Start transport pilots with eligible stock and precautionary orders. Test shared couriers under surge and carrier failure before relying on them for true downtime. Wait for delivery, return, and inventory cohorts to mature, and report sample sizes and uncertainty, especially for Critical-downtime.
- **Monitor service and environmental outcomes together.** Set acceptable limits with operations and contract owners before rollout for late delivery, p90 complete lead time, cancellation, quality escapes, and downtime performance by segment. Also monitor rework, damage, absolute and per-order environmental impacts, batch-item write-offs, and disruption recovery. Track on-time complete fulfillment for all accepted orders and show cancellations separately so cohort exclusions cannot improve the apparent service result. Pause or roll back affected policies when guardrails are breached or fallback capacity fails.
- **Audit conformance at the decision point.** For SP1, check if ground or consolidation had a predicted probability above 85% at booking, verify calibration by lane and segment, and review eligible cases sent by a higher-emission mode. For SP2, require documented Critical-downtime justification at dedicated-van dispatch and audit it against the alternatives available then. For Q1, check Made-to-order parts over 8 kg against actual pack type and a valid inspection certificate when lighter packaging is used. For R1, verify live Service-contract alternatives and run failover drills.
- **Govern exceptions and expansion.** Record the order and lines affected, timestamp, reason, alternatives and predicted on-time performance, decision owner, approval or certification where required, and actual outcome. Give dispatch ownership of SP1 and SP2 decisions, quality ownership of Q1 certification, and the service owner responsibility for R1. Review exceptions by lane, segment, machine, and reason to correct recurring causes. Expand changes when the observed environmental benefit persists through returns and inventory disposition and segment service, quality, and resilience stay within the agreed limits.
