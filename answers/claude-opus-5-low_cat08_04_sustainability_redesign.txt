# Sustainable Redesign of the Spare-Parts Fulfilment Process

## 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Scope chosen: gate-to-customer-door operational footprint of the order fulfilment process, plus the material consequences of process failure.**

Inside the boundary:
- **Production energy** at `machine_group` level (CNC_A, CNC_B, heat_treat, inspection_cell) as recorded in `energy_kwh` per event, including the energy of rework loops (`Inspection -> Rework production -> CNC production`) and of re-heat-treatment where it occurs.
- **Outbound transport emissions** in `transport_kgco2e` for all four `route_mode` values, including *second* shipments generated by `Partial shipment -> Backorder wait -> Pick from stock` and by damage returns.
- **Material flows** captured by `material_scrap_kg` (machining and rework scrap) and `package_material_kg` (standard vs reinforced packaging, plus packaging discarded in `Rework packaging` loops).
- **Obsolescence** of batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days (6% of batch output) — treated as embodied energy + material written off, i.e. an environmental loss even though no "waste" event is logged.
- **Cancellation waste**: the 410 + 60 `Cancel due to delay` cases; any work already performed (emergency production, picking, packaging) is sunk impact with zero service value.

Outside the boundary (acknowledged but not optimised here):
- Cradle-to-gate embodied impact of raw stock and purchased parts; supplier-side inbound logistics; facility HVAC/lighting; IT and process-mining infrastructure; end-of-life of the spare part at the customer; the customer's own downtime emissions (a diesel genset or an idled line can dwarf our transport delta — this is why Critical-downtime cannot simply be de-expedited).
- Empty-return leg of dedicated vans is *inside* the boundary conceptually (the evidence says vans "often return empty") but we have no attribute for it; we assume the logged `transport_kgco2e` of 52.0 for the critical/van path already reflects a poorly-utilised movement and treat it as the best available proxy.

Assumptions and residual uncertainty:
1. The log is **flattened by order**, so multi-line orders attribute all energy/scrap/packaging to one case. Per-item intensities (e.g. 1.3 vs 2.8 kWh at CNC_A) are therefore not directly comparable with per-order averages. Any batching benefit must be recomputed on a per-line-item basis before it is trusted.
2. `transport_kgco2e` is assumed to be a carrier-supplied or modelled figure, not measured; relative differences (air ≈ 4–6× ground; consolidated truck −35…−55%) are more reliable than absolute levels.
3. `energy_kwh` is assumed to be machine-metered and does not include idle/standby energy of machines waiting for a batch. Long `Batch wait` may therefore hide standby energy that the current KPI rewards.
4. Grid carbon intensity is not in the log; energy is treated in kWh, not CO2e, so energy and transport cannot be summed into a single number without a stated conversion factor. We keep them as separate criteria rather than fabricating one.
5. **Low model precision** (emergency paths and partial shipments recombine during disruption) means any before/after comparison must be done on variant-stable segments or with disruption periods explicitly flagged, otherwise improvement will be confounded with disruption frequency.
6. No damage/return event type exists in the DFG; returns are inferred from `rework_flag`, `Rework packaging`, and Q1 conformance findings. Adding an explicit return event is a prerequisite for closing the material loop.

## 2. Sustainability-related process indicators

Indicators are defined so each can be computed directly from the event log, at segment (`case_type` × `customer_type`) and variant level.

**Energy**
- E1 `kWh per produced line item` by `machine_group` and `batch_size` band — the batching curve, recomputed per item not per order.
- E2 `Rework energy share` = energy in events after the first `Inspection` on cases with `rework_flag` ÷ total production energy. Directly prices the CNC_B rework problem.
- E3 `Standby-exposed wait` = median `Batch wait` duration × machines held. Proxy for the energy the current metric hides.

**Transport**
- T1 `kgCO2e per delivered order` by segment and `route_mode`.
- T2 `Air share where slack existed` = share of orders with `route_mode = parcel_air` whose `promised_lead_time_hours` minus achievable ground time > 0. This is the SP1 violation measured in tonnes, not in percent of cases.
- T3 `Repeat-shipment factor` = shipment events per order (partial shipment + backorder + damage return re-ship). A value > 1.0 is pure avoidable transport.
- T4 `Consolidation fill rate` = orders per `Consolidated truck` departure; and `dedicated van justification rate` (SP2 event present).

**Materials**
- M1 `Scrap kg per produced item` by machine_group, split first-pass vs rework-generated.
- M2 `Packaging kg per order` by packaging type, plus M2b `packaging discarded in Rework packaging loops` (the 260+360 entries into `Rework packaging`).
- M3 `Obsolescence rate` = batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days (currently 6%) and their embodied kWh + kg.

**Rework / quality**
- R1 `First-pass yield` = 1 − (`Inspection -> Rework production` ÷ inspections), by machine_group and part family. Currently 470/(1710+1780) ≈ 13% overall, 18% CNC_B vs 9% CNC_A for MTO.
- R2 `Packaging-route incompatibility rate` = `Rework packaging` transitions ÷ packaging events (currently ~620 / ~10 000).
- R3 `Q1 conformance` = MTO parts > 8 kg shipped reinforced or with an inspection low-damage certification event.
- R4 `Inspection dwell vs defect escape` — correlation of inspection queue time with downstream rework, to detect rushed inspections.

**Batching**
- B1 `Realised batch size distribution` and B2 `batch wait p50/p90 by segment`.
- B3 `Batch-induced lateness` = late-delivery rate of batched vs immediate CNC cases (evidence: 1.7× for heat-treat waits).
- B4 `Slack consumed by batching` = batch wait ÷ promised_lead_time_hours.

**Service**
- S1 Median and p90 cycle time by segment; S2 `Late delivery rate` vs `promised_lead_time_hours`; S3 `Cancellation-due-to-delay rate` (currently ~4% of cases reach a cancel transition); S4 `Critical-downtime restoration time` (the metric that must not degrade).

**Resilience**
- X1 `Route redundancy index` = number of distinct feasible `route_mode` values with recent successful deliveries per Service-contract lane (R1 compliance).
- X2 `Machine redundancy` = share of MTO demand producible on both CNC_A and CNC_B without due-date breach — protects against the S3 risk of over-concentrating on CNC_A.
- X3 `Disruption recovery time` = time to restore normal variant mix after an emergency/partial-shipment spike.
- X4 `Exception-log completeness` = share of SP1/SP2/Q1 deviations carrying a justification event.

## 3. Intervention options

### S1. Green shipping recommender (lead-time-, reliability- and segment-aware)
- **Target**: all `Package standard/reinforced -> Ship air | Ship ground | Consolidated truck | Dedicated van` decisions; highest value on the 5% "Stock → standard pack → air" variant and the 800 reinforced→air transitions.
- **Mechanism**: chooses the lowest-emission mode whose predicted on-time probability exceeds the SP1 threshold (85%), using observed lane-level transit distributions rather than nominal carrier promises. Converts habitual air into ground/consolidated where slack genuinely exists.
- **Service/quality/resilience**: service neutral by construction *if* the reliability model is calibrated; risk is model over-confidence on thin lanes. Resilience improves only if the recommender is required to keep a fallback lane per R1 — otherwise it can monocrop onto one cheap carrier.
- **Evidence to monitor**: T1, T2, S2 by segment, X1; and calibration curves (predicted vs realised on-time) per lane, refreshed monthly.

### S2. Dynamic batching with segment-specific waiting caps
- **Target**: `Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC production` (3 020 cases) and heat-treatment batching.
- **Mechanism**: keeps the energy benefit of the 12–20 batch band (2.8 → 1.3 kWh/item at CNC_A) but caps waiting: e.g. Critical-downtime never batches, Service-contract MTO caps at a wait consuming ≤ 25% of promised lead time, Regular MTO up to a longer cap. Batches close on *slack expiry or size*, whichever first.
- **Service/quality**: cuts the 118h median and 21% late rate of the dominant MTO batch variant; small energy give-back because some batches close under-full. Reduces obsolescence because batches are demand-triggered rather than forecast-filled.
- **Evidence**: B1–B4, E1 recomputed per item, M3, S2 for MTO segments; monitor whether under-full batches erase the energy gain.

### S3. Route MTO work from CNC_B to CNC_A where queue and slack allow
- **Target**: the "MTO → batch wait → CNC_B → inspection → rework → ground" variant (5% of orders, 32.0 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap, 32% late).
- **Mechanism**: CNC_B's 18% rework rate (vs 9% on CNC_A) means roughly double the loops back through `Rework production -> CNC production`, each consuming energy and scrapping material. Shifting eligible parts removes the *cause* of the worst-impact variant rather than trimming its symptoms.
- **Service/quality/resilience**: quality clearly up, late-delivery down (rework is the biggest lateness driver in that variant). Risk: CNC_A becomes a bottleneck and its queue lengthens, pushing other cases into express transport — a displacement risk. Resilience *falls* if CNC_B is allowed to atrophy; keep a minimum qualification volume on CNC_B (X2).
- **Evidence**: R1 by machine, E2, M1 split first-pass/rework, CNC_A queue time, X2, and whether expedite_flag rises on non-shifted cases.

### S4. Priority pick + ground instead of air for Standard-stock Service-contract
- **Target**: the 22% SP1 violations in Standard-stock Service-contract (16% of volume, currently 7.8 kgCO2e/order vs 4.2 for Regular).
- **Mechanism**: the segment's 18h median against its promised lead time shows air is buying time that is not needed; buy the time inside the warehouse (priority picking, cut-off-aware wave planning) instead of in the air. Air is retained where computed slack is insufficient — this is deliberately not a blanket ban.
- **Service/quality**: cycle time rises modestly but stays inside promise; the 5% late rate must be held. Quality unaffected. Resilience improves slightly (less dependence on a single air feeder).
- **Evidence**: T2, S1/S2 for this segment specifically, pick-to-pack time, and complaint/expedite-request volume as a behavioural early warning.

### S5. Twice-daily consolidated truck departures for non-urgent stock orders
- **Target**: the 42% "Stock → standard pack → ground" variant and the existing 850 consolidated-truck flows.
- **Mechanism**: 35–55% transport CO2e reduction per order by raising fill rate; two departures rather than one keeps the consolidation wait near the low end of the 12–24h range.
- **Service/quality**: adds a bounded wait; acceptable for Regular stock orders with 62h p90 against generous promises, not acceptable for Critical-downtime. Resilience improves if the truck lane is a genuine alternative to parcel for Service-contract customers (R1), but worsens if a single truck departure becomes a single point of failure — hence two departures, not one.
- **Evidence**: T4 fill rate, T1, S2 for stock segments, and the share of orders that miss a departure and then get expedited (rebound signal).

### S6. Packaging–route compatibility check before shipment
- **Target**: the `Rework packaging` loops (260 from standard, 360 from reinforced, 550 returning).
- **Mechanism**: a pre-pack rule using part geometry/weight and the *already chosen* route_mode eliminates double-packaging: packaging material consumed twice, labour, and delay. Pure waste elimination with essentially no service trade-off.
- **Service/quality**: cycle time improves slightly; damage risk unchanged or better. No resilience downside.
- **Evidence**: R2, M2/M2b, and Q1 conformance (many rework-packaging events likely coincide with Q1 violations).

### S7. Inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations
- **Target**: `Inspection -> Rework production` (470) and the inspection_cell queue.
- **Mechanism**: the evidence says rushed inspections miss defects that surface later as packaging rework or customer returns. A gate that allocates full inspection time to CNC_B parts, heat-treated parts and >8 kg MTO parts converts a *late, expensive* failure (rework loop: full re-machining energy + scrap + a second shipment) into an *early, cheap* one. Also generates the Q1 low-damage certification that legitimises standard packaging on some parts.
- **Service/quality**: adds inspection dwell but removes rework loops that cost far more time; net cycle time likely improves for the affected variants. Quality clearly up.
- **Evidence**: R1, R4 (dwell vs escape), M1 rework scrap, T3 repeat shipments, R3.

### S8. Shared emergency courier lanes replacing some dedicated vans
- **Target**: "Critical → emergency production → reinforced pack → dedicated van" (8% of orders, 52.0 kgCO2e — by far the highest transport intensity), and the 31% of van cases lacking SP2 justification.
- **Mechanism**: pooled/scheduled emergency lanes (shared with other urgent shipments, or a milk-run over an industrial cluster) avoid the empty return leg while keeping same-day delivery windows.
- **Service/quality/resilience**: must be applied only where the pooled lane's guaranteed window still meets the downtime need; true downtime cases keep the dedicated van. Resilience is *improved* by having two urgent modes rather than one, provided the shared lane's capacity is contracted, not spot-bought.
- **Evidence**: S4 restoration time for critical cases, T1 for the critical segment, SP2 justification completeness, X1.

### S9. Split Critical-downtime into true downtime vs precautionary urgent
- **Target**: the 11% Critical-downtime Service-contract segment; enables S8 to be applied safely.
- **Mechanism**: today one label triggers the whole emergency machinery (emergency production 760 cases, dedicated van, reinforced pack). Precautionary-urgent orders — where the customer holds a spare or the machine is redundant — can take the "Critical → stock/ground exception" style path (1.2 kWh, 4.8 kgCO2e) that already exists at 3% share and performs acceptably.
- **Service/quality/resilience**: the highest-leverage intervention *and* the highest-risk one: mis-classification directly causes customer downtime. Requires customer-confirmed criteria at order intake, an auditable classification event, and a default-to-critical rule under ambiguity.
- **Evidence**: classification distribution and drift over time, late-delivery and restoration time for the reclassified group, escalation/complaint rate, and any rise in `expedite_flag` set after the fact (a sign of mis-classification).

### S10. Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular made-to-order
- **Target**: MTO Regular (22% of orders, 94h median, 18% late, 18.5 kWh).
- **Mechanism**: offering the customer an explicit slower/greener date at order entry legitimises batching and ground/consolidated transport, and — critically — *reduces the 18% late rate* by promising dates the process can actually meet. Emissions fall because slack is created up front instead of being consumed by expediting later.
- **Service/quality**: measured late-delivery falls; perceived speed falls. Cancellation risk if the offered date is uncompetitive — cap the extension and make it opt-in.
- **Evidence**: promise-vs-actual distribution, S2, S3 cancellation rate, acceptance rate of green dates, and expedite_flag applied after a green date was accepted (rebound).

### S11. Reduce partial shipments by changing availability-check rules
- **Target**: `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment` (1 010) and the "partial + backorder" variant (7% share, 79h median, **24% late**, 1.4 kWh, 7.2 kgCO2e — roughly double the transport of the clean stock path).
- **Mechanism**: a stricter availability check (with a short, bounded hold when the missing line arrives within the promise) prevents splitting an order into two shipments and two packaging events. Environmental gain comes from T3 falling toward 1.0.
- **Service/quality**: mixed — some customers *want* the available lines immediately. Rule must be segment-aware: Critical-downtime always ships partial; Regular stock orders default to hold-and-consolidate; Service-contract by contract terms. Note 60 backorder cases end in cancellation — the current partial path is not service-friendly either.
- **Evidence**: T3, share of Partial shipment transitions, backorder wait durations, S3 cancellations, and customer-initiated split requests.

### S12. Sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides
- **Target**: governance across all paths; directly addresses the 31% missing SP2 justifications and the 22% SP1 violations.
- **Mechanism**: no environmental benefit by itself; it makes the other eleven interventions *auditable and durable*. Forces a structured reason code as an event, turning silent deviation into measurable, reviewable data — and improves model precision, which is currently low.
- **Service/quality/resilience**: none directly; slight intake friction. Resilience improves because deviations during disruption become legible instead of noise.
- **Evidence**: X4 completeness, reason-code Pareto, and repeat-override concentration by customer/lane (indicates a rule that is wrong, not a user who is non-compliant).

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching rebound (energy → waiting → expedite).** S2/S5 create waiting. Waiting consumes promise slack; when slack runs out, cases exit via `expedite_flag`, `Ship air`, or `Emergency production`. A 1.5 kWh/item energy saving at CNC_A is trivially erased by one air shipment at ~4–6× ground CO2e. *Control:* B4 slack-consumption cap and a hard rule that a case which entered a batch may not later be air-expedited without an SP1 exception record.

2. **Obsolescence displacement.** Larger batches lower kWh/item but 6% of batch output is unshipped at 14 days. Unshipped items carry 100% of their energy and scrap with 0% service value — an energy "saving" per item that never becomes a delivered item is an absolute increase. *Control:* M3 tracked as a first-class indicator; batches formed from firm demand, not forecast fill.

3. **Machine-concentration displacement (S3).** Moving work off CNC_B lengthens the CNC_A queue, pushing marginal cases into longer batch waits or emergency routing — and eroding X2 machine redundancy. The rework saving on CNC_B could reappear as lateness and expediting elsewhere. *Control:* shift only within measured CNC_A slack; keep a qualification floor on CNC_B and fix its root cause (tooling/inspection) in parallel.

4. **Packaging trade-off in both directions.** Cutting reinforced packaging halves `package_material_kg` but reinforced packaging reduces damage returns by 40% on heavy MTO parts; a return is a full second shipment plus possible re-production — far worse than the packaging saved. Conversely, over-applying reinforced packaging inflates material *and* triggers `Rework packaging` when it is incompatible with the route. *Control:* Q1-compliant, inspection-certified, route-aware packaging decisions (S6+S7), with M2b and return rate watched together.

5. **Rework and inspection displacement.** An inspection gate (S7) that adds dwell without adding capacity simply moves the queue upstream: `Inspection_cell` congestion is already named as the cause of rushed inspections and rework loops, so a stricter gate applied to a saturated cell can *increase* both lateness and defect escape. *Control:* gate must be paired with capacity or triage, and validated via R4 rather than assumed.

6. **Customer behavioural rebound.** Carbon-aware promise dates (S10) and de-expedited Service-contract shipping (S4) invite customers to compensate: pre-emptively flagging orders as urgent, inflating Critical-downtime classification (undoing S9), ordering earlier and larger "just in case" (raising customer-side stock and obsolescence), or escalating to account managers who override the recommender. The measured green gain then evaporates while the log still shows compliance. *Control:* monitor segment-mix drift, expedite_flag rates, and post-hoc reclassification; make overrides cost something visible (S12).

7. **Consolidation single-point-of-failure (resilience displacement).** Concentrating ground volume onto two truck departures improves fill rate but reduces X1 route redundancy for Service-contract customers, breaching R1 during carrier disruption — and disruption is exactly when partial shipments and emergency paths explode (the source of the model's low precision). *Control:* maintain a contracted parcel fallback and test it periodically with real volume, not on paper.

8. **Metric displacement / measurement rebound.** Optimising `energy_kwh` per order while standby energy during `Batch wait` is unmetered makes batching look better than it is; optimising `transport_kgco2e` per *order* rewards partial shipments being counted as two cheap orders rather than one. *Control:* per-line-item normalisation, T3 repeat-shipment factor, and E3 standby exposure.

## 6. Balanced recommendation

The coherent package is **"buy time upstream so you don't buy it in the air, and stop paying twice for the same part."** It is one design with three mutually-supporting layers, not twelve independent green ideas.

**Layer 1 — Eliminate pure waste first (no service trade-off, funds the rest): S6, S7, S3, S11.**
These target work that is redone: packaging rework loops (~620 events), inspection escapes and CNC_B's doubled rework rate, and split shipments that double transport and packaging while delivering 24% late. Every one of these reduces energy, scrap, material *and* cycle time simultaneously — there is no trade-off to balance, so they go first and they de-risk everything after. S3 and S7 are deliberately coupled: shifting work off CNC_B without strengthening inspection just relocates the defects, and S3 must respect a CNC_B qualification floor so machine redundancy (X2) survives.

**Layer 2 — Create legitimate slack, then spend it on low-carbon modes: S10, S2, S5, S1, S4.**
The core insight from the data is that the MTO segments are *already* late (18% / 13%) and the Standard-stock Service-contract segment is *already* fast (18h median) yet still flies. So: negotiate realistic, carbon-aware dates with Regular MTO customers (S10) — this improves the late-delivery KPI while creating the slack that makes S2's capped batching and S5's consolidated departures safe rather than reckless. Batching caps are set per segment from that slack, never from an energy target. The shipping recommender (S1) and the priority-pick substitution (S4) then convert slack into mode choice, with SP1's 85% reliability threshold as the binding constraint — air remains available wherever slack is genuinely insufficient, which is precisely why this is not a blanket air ban.

**Layer 3 — Protect the critical segment while removing its worst emissions: S9 then S8.**
Critical-downtime is 11% of volume but carries 38 kgCO2e/order, and the emergency-van variant 52. It is also the segment where an environmental error becomes a customer's stopped production line. So the sequence matters: first classify honestly (S9, with customer-confirmed criteria and default-to-critical under ambiguity), then re-route only the precautionary-urgent subset onto shared lanes (S8). True downtime keeps dedicated vans, keeps emergency production, keeps its 3% late rate — this is the explicit application of the constraint that the lowest-emission option is not chosen where it breaks service or resilience. Shared lanes must be contracted capacity, which also *adds* a second urgent mode and strengthens R1.

**Layer 0, running underneath everything — S12.** The exception log is what makes the package durable. Today 22% SP1 and 31% SP2 deviations are invisible; a redesign layered on top of invisible deviation will decay within two quarters. Reason-coded overrides also raise model precision, which is currently the main obstacle to measuring anything during disruption.

**Why this is balanced.** Environmental gain is concentrated where the log says impact actually sits — rework variants (32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap), avoidable air on a segment with slack, the 52 kgCO2e van path, and double shipments — rather than spread thinly as generic "ship slower." Service is protected by construction: the largest cycle-time increases fall on Regular customers who have explicitly agreed to them, while Service-contract and Critical-downtime service levels are held as hard constraints and the two segments with the *worst* current lateness (MTO) get *faster*, not slower. Quality improves through inspection and packaging fixes rather than being traded away. Resilience is treated as a constraint with its own indicators (X1, X2) precisely because the three biggest emission levers — machine concentration, transport consolidation, and van substitution — all erode redundancy if applied naively.

**Sequencing:** Layer 1 immediately (weeks 1–8, low risk, self-funding). Layer 2 as a piloted rollout by segment (weeks 6–20), S10 before S2/S5 so batching has slack to consume. Layer 3 last (weeks 16+), S9 shadow-classified for a full cycle before any van is removed.

## 7. Validation and governance plan

**Design of the evaluation.** Validate on the event log, not on a spreadsheet model. Use segment-stratified before/after comparison with the variant mix held constant, plus staged rollout so a control group exists: pilot S4 and S5 on a subset of lanes/customers and compare against matched non-pilot lanes over the same weeks. Because model precision is low and disruption weeks recombine emergency and partial-shipment paths, tag disruption periods explicitly (carrier incidents, machine downtime) and report results both including and excluding them. Never compare a pilot month to a pre-pilot month without this control — the improvement will otherwise be indistinguishable from a quiet quarter.

**Conformance monitoring.**
- *SP1* — weekly conformance check on ground/consolidated selection where predicted on-time probability > 85%. Baseline: 22% violation in Standard-stock Service-contract. Target: violations fall *and* each remaining one carries a reason code. Track T2 in absolute kgCO2e so the improvement is expressed as avoided emissions, not just a compliance percentage.
- *SP2* — every `Dedicated van` case must have a preceding justification event. Baseline: 31% missing. This is measured as an event-existence check in the log; a van dispatch without justification should be blockable at execution, not merely reported. After S9, additionally check that the justification cites *true* downtime.
- *Q1* — for MTO parts > 8 kg, check for either reinforced packaging or an inspection low-damage certification event. Correlate violations with `Rework packaging` transitions and returns; the current finding that Q1 violations correlate with rework loops gives a ready-made validation hypothesis: if S6/S7 work, that correlation weakens and R2 falls.
- *R1* — monthly redundancy audit: for each Service-contract lane, at least one alternative route_mode with a recent successful delivery. S5 and S1 must be gated on this check passing.

**Segment-level service monitoring (guardrails with stop conditions).** Publish a weekly dashboard of median and p90 cycle time, late-delivery rate, and cancellation rate for all five segments. Pre-agreed thresholds halt or roll back the relevant intervention:
- Critical-downtime late delivery > 4% (current baseline) or restoration time worsening → suspend S8/S9 immediately.
- Standard-stock Service-contract late delivery > 5% → revert S4 on affected lanes.
- MTO late delivery not improving within one quarter of S2/S10 → batching caps are too loose.
- `Cancel due to delay` rate rising in any segment → S11 or batching is over-holding.
Guardrails are asymmetric on purpose: environmental targets are optimised, service floors are constraints.

**Rebound surveillance.** Explicitly instrument the risks in §5: T3 repeat-shipment factor, M3 obsolescence, expedite_flag rate *after* a green date or batch assignment was accepted, Critical-downtime classification drift (share of orders labelled critical over time), CNC_A queue length and X2, and consolidation fill rate vs missed-departure expedites. A green intervention whose rebound indicator moves adversely is reported as a failure even if its headline metric improved.

**Exception governance.** All SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides are recorded as first-class events with actor, reason code, and segment (S12). A monthly review board (operations, quality, sustainability, key-account) reads the reason-code Pareto and does one of three things with each recurring pattern: fix the process, change the rule, or accept it as a documented standing exception with an owner and review date. Concentration of overrides in one customer, lane, or planner is treated as evidence that the *rule* is mis-specified, not that the person is non-compliant. Rules themselves are versioned in the log so that any before/after analysis knows which policy version was in force.

**Data quality prerequisites** — without these the validation is not credible: per-line-item energy and scrap allocation (the log is currently flattened by order), an explicit damage-return event type, a batch open/close event pair so B4 slack consumption is computable, a recorded justification event schema for SP2, and a stated kWh→CO2e conversion factor if energy and transport are ever to be reported as one figure. Re-run the conformance and indicator suite quarterly, and re-discover the process model annually to check whether precision has improved — rising precision is itself evidence that exception handling has become disciplined.
