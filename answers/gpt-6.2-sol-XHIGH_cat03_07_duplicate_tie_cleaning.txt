## 1. Duplicate-handling decision tree

Generate candidates from source keys, message links and same-activity/time buckets with object overlap. Compare source meanings, object roles, identifiers, payloads and qualified event-to-object (E2O) relations.

1. **Exact technical replay.** If the source guarantees key uniqueness at this granularity and the same source key, version, business payload and qualified relations recur with only transport metadata differences, drop the extra analytical row. Archive every delivery. A changed business payload under the same key goes to version or conflict review.
2. **Same physical or business occurrence.** If reconciliation, a stable occurrence identifier, or equivalent evidence establishes identity, merge attributes and compatible relations into one canonical event. Resolve attributes by field-level authority and preserve every source value.
3. **Derived summary.** If a downstream record reports a detail event or a set of events, retain both with a refined label or qualifier and record the derivation. At a declared coarser granularity, merge only a verified one-to-one representation with compatible roles. An aggregate over multiple events stays separate at either granularity used here.
4. **Harmonization collision.** If the source activities denote distinct transitions, retain both with refined labels or qualifiers. Keep the harmonized and source-native meanings in provenance.
5. **Concurrent same-label events on different objects.** If identifiers or roles establish distinct occurrences, retain both unchanged. Shared higher-level objects such as an order or invoice are expected.
6. **Unresolved candidate.** Quarantine the proposed merge for review and retain both unchanged in the analytical view while adjudication is pending. Record the evidence, ambiguity, and candidate IDs.

## 3. Timestamp-tie ordering rules

Order by observed UTC business timestamp at its recorded precision. Within each timestamp, build a precedence graph before sorting. Keep typed reasons for edges, and map source IDs through the canonical crosswalk. For example, SCANNER’s e04-before-e05 sequence still orders canonical e03 before e05. Drop an internal edge after contraction only when the scenario has explicitly approved treating its two endpoints as one transition. Flag cycles and trusted precedences that conflict with observed timestamps for review. A contraction that creates a cycle needs different merge decisions or better evidence.

- **Domain and lifecycle constraints** carry semantic precedence when verified for the relevant occurrence and object role. Carrier acceptance precedes handover. M1 authorization precedes its capture. A package’s picks can precede its actual packing when the links identify that same packing episode. Shared V1 alone establishes no invoice-before-authorization rule.
- **Source-specific semantics** can support a causal edge when an originating message is tied to a downstream update. A source meaning also distinguishes warehouse release from carrier acceptance. Impose release-before-acceptance only if the workflow confirms what acceptance means for this delivery.
- **Within-source sequence** orders tied records in a comparable producer stream. Treat it as evidence of business precedence only where the source guarantees transaction order. Otherwise it supplies a local deterministic convention. The PSP sequence orders e13 before e14 in the chosen view, with no inferred lifecycle dependency across M1 and M2. Compare no sequence numbers across systems.
- **Ingestion time** is diagnostic metadata. For remaining incomparable events, use a topological sort that always takes the smallest stable canonical ID among ready nodes. The resulting linearization is deterministic. Record the precedence graph or edge audit alongside that rank so consumers can distinguish unresolved order from established precedence.

Use e03, e05, e06, e07 at **10:00**, assuming both picks feed this pack and e07 reports it. Without the I2 lifecycle constraint, e03, e06, e05, e07 is also admissible. The evidence-based and mechanical views omit e07. At **10:15**, use e08, e09, e10. If only acceptance-before-handover is established, e09, e08, e10 is another admissible order. At **12:00**, use e11, e12, e13, e14, e15 after folding e16 into e11. ERP orders e11 before e15. PSP orders e12, e13, e14. With no cross-source invoice/payment precedence, e12, e13, e14, e11, e15 is also admissible. The mechanical view omits e15. If an upstream message is linked to e15, add its verified precedence. In particular, an e14-before-e15 edge would make the proposed e13/e15 contraction incompatible with the e13-before-e14 sequence.

Assigning microseconds before choosing the order can turn file or arrival order into apparent cause and effect, such as a capture preceding authorization. Prefer a separate tie rank. Use an adjusted timestamp only in a tool-specific copy, after ordering, with checks for timestamp-bucket crossings and with durations calculated from the originals.

## 4. Conservative cleaning pipeline

Snapshot the raw OCEL and pre-check candidate groups. Remove confirmed technical replays if present. Here the source keys in the table are distinct, so this step removes zero rows. Contract e04 into e03 and e16 into e11 using the reconciliation facts, a field-authority map, and qualified E2O validation. Keep e06 and e07 and retain the invoice-level e15. Refine e08 to `Release Package from Warehouse` and e09 to `Accept Package at Carrier`. Use the status and balance labels from the decision table. Retain original timestamps, harmonized labels, available native meanings, original IDs, source keys, and per-source sequences. Order using the canonical crosswalk and retain tied business timestamps and the precedence evidence.

The conservative view has **15 events and 39 E2O relations**, assuming one listed relation per event and object and compatible qualifiers on the duplicate pairs. The scan contraction removes three redundant relations and the invoice contraction removes two. All eight objects remain.

## 5. Aggressive alternatives

- **Evidence-based semantic merging** uses physical packing as its unit and assumes the ERP notification is verified to come one-to-one from e06’s message. Contract e07 into e06 on top of the conservative verified merges. That yields **14 events and 37 E2O relations**. Keep e15 since the summarized payments have no verified one-to-one link to it. The projected trace loses the ERP status observation and any analysis of the lag from packing to that update. If the message link cannot be verified, publish the conservative count and treat this as a conditional sensitivity run.
- **Mechanical PM4Py merge** starts with the verified pairs resolved and uses original harmonized labels on two isolated, explicitly selected pairs, e06/e07 and e13/e15. It yields **13 events and 35 E2O relations** given one output event per pair and the union of their object endpoints. Preserve e14 and the release/acceptance distinction. The extra contraction falsely associates an invoice-level update with M1 and makes M1 look special solely because of the chosen pair. The same heuristic could select M2 instead.

With `have_common_object=True`, e13 and e14 can still qualify for merging in a whole same-label/time bucket. They share O1 and V1, while their Payment links identify M1 and M2. The flag tests overlap and cannot establish agreement on the event-defining object and role. The mechanical pair selection and protected IDs are explicit experimental controls.

## 6. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation

Use the listed IDs and compatible qualifiers. Populate the field-authority map from the source contract. The example archives source rows and assumes the e06/e07 lineage condition in the evidence view.

```python
from collections import defaultdict
from copy import deepcopy
from heapq import heappop, heappush
from itertools import combinations

import pandas as pd
import pm4py

raw = deepcopy(ocel_raw)
E, A, T = raw.event_id, raw.event_activity, raw.event_timestamp
O, OT = raw.object_id, raw.object_type
Q = getattr(raw, "qualifier", "ocel:qualifier")
raw.events["original_timestamp"] = raw.events[T]
raw.events["original_activity"] = raw.events[A]
raw.events[T] = pd.to_datetime(raw.events[T], utc=True)
raw.relations[T] = pd.to_datetime(raw.relations[T], utc=True)
if Q not in raw.relations:
    raw.relations[Q] = None

def validate(log):
    assert log.events[E].notna().all() and log.events[E].is_unique
    assert log.objects[O].notna().all() and log.objects[O].is_unique
    assert log.events[T].notna().all()
    events, objects = log.events.set_index(E), log.objects.set_index(O)
    for endpoint, table in [(E, events), (O, objects)]:
        assert log.relations[endpoint].isin(table.index).all()
    for column, endpoint, table in [(A, E, events), (T, E, events),
                                    (OT, O, objects)]:
        assert log.relations[column].eq(log.relations[endpoint].map(table[column])).all()
    for name, endpoint, table in [("e2e", E, events), ("o2o", O, objects)]:
        links = getattr(log, name, None)
        if links is not None and len(links):
            assert links[endpoint].isin(table.index).all()
            assert links[endpoint + "_2"].isin(table.index).all()
    changes = getattr(log, "object_changes", None)
    if changes is not None and len(changes):
        assert changes[O].isin(objects.index).all()

validate(raw)
ledger = raw.events.set_index(E, drop=False)
objects_by_event = raw.relations.groupby(E)[O].agg(frozenset)
precheck = raw.events.assign(related_objects=raw.events[E].map(objects_by_event))
same_label_time = [
    group for _, group in precheck.groupby([A, T], sort=False) if len(group) > 1
]
repeated_source_keys = [
    group for _, group in precheck.groupby(["source", "source_event_key"], sort=False)
    if len(group) > 1
]
approved_replays = []  # (keep, replay), approved against the source's key/version contract
authority = {}        # (canonical eid, field name) -> authoritative source
protected = {"e05", "e14"}

def pair_relations(log, ids):
    return log.relations.loc[log.relations[E].isin(ids)].copy()

def qualified_links(relations):
    return {(oid, None if pd.isna(role) else role)
            for oid, role in relations[[O, Q]].itertuples(index=False, name=None)}

def contract(log, alias, keep, remove, rule):
    assert keep != remove and remove not in protected
    ids = {keep, remove}
    members = log.events.loc[log.events[E].isin(ids)]
    assert len(members) == 2
    e2e = getattr(log, "e2e", None)
    if e2e is not None and len(e2e):
        if e2e[[E, E + "_2"]].isin(ids).any().any():
            raise ValueError("Resolve incident E2E links before contraction")

    relations = pair_relations(log, ids)
    keep_links = qualified_links(relations.loc[relations[E].eq(keep)])
    remove_links = qualified_links(relations.loc[relations[E].eq(remove)])
    shared = {oid for oid, _ in keep_links} & {oid for oid, _ in remove_links}
    if {link for link in keep_links if link[0] in shared} != {
        link for link in remove_links if link[0] in shared
    }:
        raise ValueError("Review the two interpretations of the shared object roles")

    row = members.loc[members[E].eq(keep)].iloc[0]
    combined = relations.assign(**{E: keep, A: row[A], T: row[T]}).drop_duplicates()
    if combined.duplicated([E, O, Q]).any():
        raise ValueError("Resolve conflicting relation attributes before contraction")
    keep_mask = log.events[E].eq(keep)
    metadata = {E, A, T, "original_timestamp", "original_activity", "source",
                "source_event_key", "source_seq", "ingested_at", "payload_hash"}
    for (canonical, field), source in authority.items():
        if canonical == keep:
            assert field not in metadata
            values = members.loc[members["source"].eq(source), field].dropna()
            if len(values) == 1:
                log.events.loc[keep_mask, field] = values.iloc[0]

    log.events = log.events.loc[~log.events[E].eq(remove)].copy()
    log.relations = pd.concat(
        [log.relations.loc[~log.relations[E].isin(ids)], combined], ignore_index=True
    )
    for source_id, canonical in alias.items():
        if canonical == remove:
            alias[source_id] = keep
    validate(log)
    return {"canonical": keep, "removed_from_view": remove, "rule": rule}

def library_contraction(log, alias, keep, remove, *, technical):
    ids = {keep, remove}
    scope = deepcopy(log)
    scope.events = scope.events.loc[scope.events[E].isin(ids)].reset_index(drop=True)
    scope.relations = pair_relations(scope, ids).reset_index(drop=True)
    scope.objects = scope.objects.loc[scope.objects[O].isin(scope.relations[O])].copy()
    for name in ("e2e", "o2o", "object_changes"):
        table = getattr(scope, name, None)
        if table is not None:
            setattr(scope, name, table.iloc[:0].copy())
    assert len(scope.events) == 2
    assert len(scope.events[[A, T]].drop_duplicates()) == 1
    expected_objects = set(scope.relations[O])
    if technical:
        left, right = ledger.loc[keep], ledger.loc[remove]
        assert pd.notna(left["source_event_key"]) and pd.notna(left["payload_hash"])
        assert left.drop([E, "ingested_at"]).equals(right.drop([E, "ingested_at"]))
        left_links = scope.relations.loc[scope.relations[E].eq(keep)].drop(columns=E)
        right_links = scope.relations.loc[scope.relations[E].eq(remove)].drop(columns=E)
        common = pd.concat([left_links, right_links]).drop_duplicates()
        assert len(common) == len(left_links.drop_duplicates()) == len(right_links.drop_duplicates())
        result = pm4py.ocel_drop_duplicates(scope)
        rule = "verified technical replay"
    else:
        # WARNING: this experimental heuristic infers identity from label, time and overlap.
        result = pm4py.ocel_merge_duplicates(scope, have_common_object=True)
        rule = "scoped mechanical PM4Py merge"
    if len(result.events) != 1 or set(result.relations[O]) != expected_objects:
        raise ValueError("Library contraction did not produce the expected pair and endpoints")
    # Apply the accepted contraction with a stable ID and governed attributes and roles.
    return contract(log, alias, keep, remove, rule)

def ordered_view(log, alias):
    # e06 -> e07 assumes the status reports this packing episode.
    # e03/e05 -> e06 models the two picks as inputs to this same pack.
    edges = {
        ("e03", "e06"): {"same packing episode"},
        ("e05", "e06"): {"same packing episode"},
        ("e06", "e07"): {"modeled WMS message lineage"},
        ("e09", "e10"): {"confirmed carrier precedence"},
        ("e12", "e13"): {"M1 lifecycle"},
    }
    for (_, source), stream in ledger.groupby([T, "source"], sort=False):
        sequence = pd.to_numeric(stream["source_seq"]).dropna().sort_values()
        for (before, first), (after, second) in combinations(sequence.items(), 2):
            if first < second:
                edges.setdefault((before, after), set()).add(f"{source} sequence")

    times = log.events.set_index(E)[T]
    successors = defaultdict(set)
    indegree = dict.fromkeys(times.index, 0)
    precedence = []
    for (before, after), reasons in sorted(edges.items()):
        u, v = alias[before], alias[after]
        precedence.append({"original_edge": (before, after), "canonical_edge": (u, v),
                           "reasons": sorted(reasons), "internal_to_merge": u == v})
        if u == v:
            continue
        if times[u] > times[v]:
            raise ValueError(f"Precedence contradicts business timestamps: {u}, {v}")
        if times[u] == times[v] and v not in successors[u]:
            successors[u].add(v)
            indegree[v] += 1

    ordered_ids, ranks = [], {}
    for _, group in log.events.groupby(T, sort=True):
        ready = []
        for eid in group[E]:
            if indegree[eid] == 0:
                heappush(ready, eid)
        emitted = 0
        while ready:
            eid = heappop(ready)
            ranks[eid] = emitted
            ordered_ids.append(eid)
            emitted += 1
            for successor in sorted(successors[eid]):
                indegree[successor] -= 1
                if indegree[successor] == 0:
                    heappush(ready, successor)
        if emitted != len(group):
            raise ValueError("Cycle in tied events after canonicalization")
    log.events["tie_rank"] = log.events[E].map(ranks)
    log.relations[A] = log.relations[E].map(log.events.set_index(E)[A])
    try:
        log = pm4py.ocel_sort_by_additional_column(deepcopy(log), "tie_rank")
    except KeyError as error:
        if error.args != ("tie_rank",):
            raise
        # Support implementations which expect the additional column on E2O rows.
        log.relations["tie_rank"] = log.relations[E].map(ranks)
        log = pm4py.ocel_sort_by_additional_column(log, "tie_rank")
    assert log.events[E].tolist() == ordered_ids
    log.events = log.events.reset_index(drop=True)
    synchronize(log)
    validate(log)
    return log, precedence

def synchronize(log):
    indexed = log.events.set_index(E)
    for column in (A, T, "tie_rank"):
        log.relations[column] = log.relations[E].map(indexed[column])
    position = dict(zip(log.events[E], range(len(log.events))))
    log.relations = (
        log.relations.assign(event_order=log.relations[E].map(position))
        .sort_values("event_order", kind="stable").reset_index(drop=True)
    )

def timestamp_adapter(log):
    timed = pm4py.ocel_add_index_based_timedelta(deepcopy(log))
    ids = log.events[E].tolist()
    original = log.events.set_index(E)[T]
    adjusted = timed.events.set_index(E).loc[ids, T]
    distinct = original.drop_duplicates().tolist()
    upper = original.map(dict(zip(distinct, distinct[1:])))
    if (not adjusted.is_monotonic_increasing or not adjusted.is_unique
            or not adjusted.ge(original).all()
            or not (upper.isna() | adjusted.lt(upper)).all()):
        return None  # Keep rank-based analysis when the synthetic timestamps fail checks.
    timed.events = timed.events.set_index(E).loc[ids].reset_index()
    timed.events["adjusted_timestamp"] = timed.events[T]
    synchronize(timed)
    validate(timed)
    return timed

labels = {
    "e07": "Update Packing Status",
    "e08": "Release Package from Warehouse",
    "e09": "Accept Package at Carrier",
    "e15": "Update Invoice Balance",
}
views, adapters, audit = {}, {}, {}
for scenario in ("raw", "conservative", "evidence", "mechanical"):
    log = deepcopy(raw)
    aliases = {eid: eid for eid in ledger.index}
    decisions = []
    if scenario != "raw":
        for keep, replay in approved_replays:
            decisions.append(library_contraction(log, aliases, keep, replay, technical=True))
        decisions.append(contract(log, aliases, "e03", "e04", "scan reconciliation"))
        decisions.append(contract(log, aliases, "e11", "e16", "invoice reconciliation"))
        if scenario == "evidence":
            decisions.append(contract(log, aliases, "e06", "e07", "verified WMS message"))
        if scenario == "mechanical":
            for keep, remove in [("e06", "e07"), ("e13", "e15")]:
                decisions.append(library_contraction(log, aliases, keep, remove, technical=False))
        log.events[A] = log.events[E].map(labels).fillna(log.events[A])
    view, precedence = ordered_view(log, aliases)
    assert protected <= set(view.events[E])
    views[scenario] = view
    adapters[scenario] = timestamp_adapter(view)
    audit[scenario] = {"source_to_canonical": aliases, "decisions": decisions,
                       "precedence": precedence}
```

Use the same tie policy, object populations and edge denominators across views. The raw baseline uses original labels and the same precedence evidence, with event ID as the final fallback. Flatten by object instance, with one occurrence per event and object. ET–OT weights below count E2O links per event type and object type. For the OTG, distinguish a co-occurrence edge supported by at least one event from its event support count and from explicit O2O relations.

For the **OTG**, explicit O2O rows stay as supplied. The co-occurrence topology of object pairs and the resulting object-type connections are unchanged because each contraction uses equal or subset E2O endpoint sets. O1–P1 co-occurrence support goes from 9 to 8 to 7 to 7 events across the four views. O1–V1 goes from 6 to 5 to 5 to 4. For example, the last ranked event for V1 changes from e16 to e15 to e14 in the mechanical view, within the same recorded second.

For **rework and performance**, attribute self-edges to the contributing IDs before interpreting them. The raw I1 repeat is a duplicate observation. The remaining Order pick and capture repeats represent different lower-level objects. The ERP packing repeat represents a status observation. Compare a second admissible linearization to identify unstable edges, especially among invoice issuance and PSP events and between release and acceptance. Report transition support over valid linearizations or bounds when practical. Measure elapsed time from original timestamps. Tied transitions have zero *recorded* elapsed time at second resolution, even for the known acceptance-to-handover sequence. The e10-to-e17 observed interval remains 22 hours 45 minutes in all views. Keep original harmonized activity, native activity or source meaning where available, refined activity, original timestamp and precision, tie rank, adjusted timestamp where generated, and the versioned precedence evidence. ## 9. Failure modes and safeguards

1. **Collapse distinct partial payments.** Check Payment IDs and roles, protect e14, and compare per-payment capture counts.
2. **Count abstraction-level updates as rework.** Refine status and summary labels and retain derivation evidence.
3. **Order by ingestion time.** Use verified precedence and a stable fallback, with ingestion reserved for diagnostics.
