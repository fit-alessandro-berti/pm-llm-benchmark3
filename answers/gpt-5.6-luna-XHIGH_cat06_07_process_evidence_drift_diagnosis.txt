## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

January through March are the clean baseline: labels are fully mature, the path distribution is stable, unknown activity is at most 0.3%, missing queue is 1%, and AUROC, Brier, ECE, predicted risk, and observed breach rate are all stable. These months are suitable as a reference regime, but they are not automatically representative of the later routing and supplier regimes.

April should be treated primarily as a schema and feature-pipeline incident. The activity rename occurred on 1 April, but the feature pipeline still recognized only the old diagnosis label. Consequently, the prediction prefix could be absent or anchored at the wrong event. Duplicate handling was also asymmetric, and an 18% unmapped/other path share is consistent with broken event interpretation. The high mean score, high ECE, and large missing queue share are exactly what a feature-construction failure can produce. Because April labels are mature and no outcome-definition change was recorded, the evidence does not support calling this an April concept drift. The May patch improved the data, but incomplete backfill of the cached event-count feature means May is a recovery/validation month rather than a perfectly clean continuation of the baseline.

June is a different event. The new remote-routing policy changed the population entering each process variant: V2 increased to 52% and V1 fell to 30%. Remote diagnosis-to-fix time did not change, and V2's matured breach rate actually fell to 0.09. The overall observed breach rate fell to 0.15 while the mean prediction remained 0.20. This can create aggregate calibration and ranking changes through mixture shift even if the conditional relationship within each path is unchanged. It is therefore evidence of process and covariate/population shift, not by itself evidence of concept drift. June is also the first post-policy regime and should be evaluated separately from January–May.

July is the first month with strong evidence of an operational deterioration in parts-dependent work. The parts edge lengthened to 65 hours and V3's matured breach rate rose to 0.46 from 0.35 in June. The 98% maturity is nearly complete, so the signal is more credible than October's, although the small residual censoring should still be handled. The supplier issue began on 10 July, so a month-level statistic blends pre- and post-change cases; a date-split analysis is preferable.

August combines process drift and a likely changed target relationship with a new mix. Supplier delay becomes severe: the V3 share is 35%, the parts edge reaches 130 hours, and matured V3 breach rate reaches 0.64. The model remains around 0.19 on average while the observed breach rate is 0.28, with AUROC 0.68 and ECE 0.120. This supports underprediction in V3 and/or for cases whose risk is no longer represented by the original features. However, 93% maturity and the larger V3 share mean the aggregate result should still be decomposed by variant, age, and supplier exposure rather than assigned to one mechanism.

September adds a population shift on top of the parts problem. Partner Z accounts for 24% of cases, has a 58% V3 share versus 36% for other sources, and serves rural customers with older devices. The V3 parts edge remains very long and its matured breach rate is 0.61. Partner Z's currently observed breach rate is 0.45 versus 0.27 elsewhere, but the source comparison is confounded by slower labels and the different path/covariate mix. Thus September is a joint process, population, covariate, and possible concept-drift month, not a clean test of model fairness or model failure.

October is heavily confounded by outcome maturity. Only 55% of labels are mature, median label delay is 52 days, and the backlog selectively affects longer paths. The 0.16 observed breach rate is likely a partial ascertainment statistic, not a stable estimate of the true October rate. V3 remains 41% and Partner Z 27%, so the process and population changes continue, but AUROC 0.64, Brier 0.205, ECE 0.130, and the mean-versus-observed comparison cannot be treated as final performance evidence. October should be used for input, process, deployment, and maturity monitoring, not ordinary target-based retraining.

The resulting timeline has at least four different episodes: a representation failure in April, a routing-policy mix change in June, supplier-driven process and risk deterioration in July–September with a Partner Z population shift in September, and an observation/censoring problem in October. A single drift label would hide these distinctions and would lead to the wrong intervention.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

1. **Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting.** For each start month, variant, source, and path length, plot the fraction of cases with a final outcome as a function of case age and the cumulative breach rate as labels mature. Recompute AUROC, Brier, ECE, and breach rates on fixed-vintage cohorts, such as cases at least 60 or 90 days old, and use inverse-probability-of-observation weights if appropriate. Under label delay/censoring, October's breach rate should rise as the backlog resolves and its metrics should move toward the values of mature long-path cohorts; under real concept/process deterioration, the mature October or late-August/September cohorts will retain a conditional excess risk after age is controlled. The curve should also reveal whether missingness depends on V3 or source rather than being random.

2. **Replay after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning.** Rebuild every diagnosis prefix from raw events after mapping both diagnosis labels to one canonical activity, preserving an audit table of original and normalized values. Remove only exact duplicate events automatically; flag non-identical same-key or conflicting event IDs for review, then recompute cached features for all affected April and May cases. Under a pipeline-failure hypothesis, April's unknown/missing shares and metric degradation should largely disappear and predictions should agree with a clean backfill; under true drift, data-quality counts will improve but the later within-variant breach and residual calibration problem will remain. Compare old and replayed features case by case, not just in monthly aggregates.

3. **Stratified calibration and discrimination.** Produce reliability diagrams, calibration intercept/slope, Brier decomposition, ECE/reliability bins, AUROC/PR-AUC, and top-risk capture by month, V1/V2/V3, source/partner, supplier exposure, and label age. Use mature labels and report both raw and mix-standardized results. If aggregate deterioration is mostly covariate or population mix shift, within-variant/source discrimination and calibration should be close to baseline after reweighting; if concept drift is real, a calibration gap or ranking loss will persist within comparable strata. If only Partner Z is affected, the source-stratified result will show that a pooled metric is hiding the localization.

4. **Process-variant and DFG/performance-DFG comparison.** Discover variants and compare transition counts, conformance, and edge-duration distributions for calendar cohorts and case-start cohorts. Inspect Order Part -> Part Received by supplier, date, and V3; use diagnosis-to-Remote Fix as a negative control. Under process drift, the V2/V3 frequencies and parts-edge quantiles will change at the policy/supplier dates and explain a substantial part of the breach increase; under concept drift without process change, edge distributions and path mix will be stable while outcomes change; under a pipeline problem, anomalous transitions and unknown activities will shrink after replay. The DFG should be supplemented by performance-DFG durations because a count-only graph cannot expose supplier delay.

5. **Conditional outcome-relationship test.** On sufficiently mature cases, compare the outcome relationship after conditioning on diagnosis-time features, variant, source, calendar regime, and known-at-score-time route/part indicators. Use interactions or a survival/logistic model only as a diagnostic, with time-blocked validation; post-diagnosis actual delivery time may be used for explanation but must not be used as a prediction feature unless it is known at scoring time. Under concept drift, the residual relationship or score calibration will differ by period even within the same path and matched feature strata. Under process drift or an omitted process covariate, adding an allowed, pre-score supplier/route/SLA feature or stratifying by exposure should absorb much of the change. Under a pipeline failure, the difference should concentrate in records with bad prefixes or stale lineage and disappear after replay.

6. **Partner Z population reweighting/transport check.** Match or reweight non-Partner-Z cases to Partner Z on V1/V2/V3, rural distance, device age, geography, diagnosis-time features, and any known supplier/route variables. Evaluate only mature outcomes or use a maturity model for the weights. Under population shift with a stable conditional relationship, the weighted non-Z risk should approximate Partner Z risk and within-stratum calibration should be similar; under a source-specific concept/process effect, a residual Partner Z excess remains after matching. If the gap disappears only after adding rural distance or device age, those missing features are likely important for transportability rather than evidence of source-specific unfairness.

7. **Feature missingness and lineage audit.** For every model feature, record source field, transformation version, event anchor, computation timestamp, null reason, and coverage by month/source/variant. Reconcile event counts, diagnosis timestamps, duplicate flags, and queue features from raw data to the feature store; verify that no feature uses events after Initial Diagnosis Completed. Under pipeline failure, missingness and feature-value discontinuities will align with the April rename, source, or patch version and replay will repair them. Under substantive drift, lineage and coverage will remain sound while the target relationship or process-time distributions change. A schema/lineage failure should block metric interpretation even when labels are mature.

8. **Date and intervention discontinuity checks.** Split around 1 April, 6 May, 1 June, 10 July, 1 September, and the October API backlog, and compare cases immediately before and after each boundary with seasonality and case-age adjustment. A discontinuity at a deployment or policy boundary supports pipeline/process drift; a gradual change in V3 performance tied to supplier delay supports operational deterioration; a metric change only when label age is short supports censoring. This test helps avoid attributing all of a month to an event that occurred partway through it.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

- **Immediate containment.** Freeze automatic retraining and production threshold changes. Quarantine April scores/features and any May cases whose diagnosis anchor or cached feature was not replayed. Keep scoring available only after a schema and lineage check; route high-risk V3 cases through a conservative operational review or supplier escalation while the evidence is resolved. Label October metrics as provisional. Preserve model/version, policy, source, and feature-store identifiers with every score.

- **Short-term recalibration.** After normalization, replay, and a mature-label gate, test an intercept/slope recalibration on a recent clean cohort. A temporary variant-specific intercept or threshold can be justified if rankings remain useful but base rates have moved, particularly for V3. Recalibration is not an adequate response to April's malformed prefixes, and it is unlikely to fix a material AUROC loss or a supplier-specific ranking failure. Do not fit a calibration map to October's 55%-mature labels.

- **Feature repair and process enrichment.** Make the diagnosis-label mapping canonical, use deterministic event identity and duplicate rules, invalidate and recompute all dependent cached features, and add data-quality gates for unknown activities and missing queue values. Add features that are available at diagnosis and are permitted operationally, such as route/partner, known part requirement, supplier/SLA state known at score time, rural distance, and device age. Do not add the realized Order Part -> Part Received duration as a feature when that duration occurs after the score; it is a diagnostic or target-process variable unless an equivalent forecast is available at score time.

- **Full retraining.** Retrain only after the pipeline replay passes audit, an outcome-maturity rule is met, and enough cases from the new process regimes have final labels. Use time-based validation with a holdout after the supplier and Partner Z changes, report variant/source performance, and consider censoring weights. A candidate model should be compared with the repaired incumbent, not only with the broken April feature set.

For training eligibility, January–March are safe for a clean historical baseline. June is label-mature and can be used for a post-routing-policy evaluation or training slice, but it must be marked as a different regime. July is nearly mature and can be used after case-age/date splitting and supplier-exposure checks. April is unsafe until all affected features are replayed; May is conditionally usable only for verified clean cases; August and September are not safe for ordinary unweighted training until their label-age and source/path selection are handled; October is unsafe for ordinary supervised training while only 55% of labels are mature. Matured, explicitly selected August–September cases can be used in a weighted, time-aware analysis before the full cohort matures.

A single shared model with variant indicators/interactions and separate calibration is the default. V2 has a stable short remote edge and a different risk regime, while V3 has materially different exposure and an abrupt supplier response, so variant-specific calibration or a hierarchical/partial-pooling model is justified. Fully separate V2/V3 models should be adopted only if each has sufficient mature volume, stable monitoring, and distinct operational decisions; otherwise they increase variance and make source/population shifts harder to diagnose. Partner Z should not receive a separate model merely because its aggregate rate is higher; first perform the transport and within-stratum tests.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

The monitoring system should retain raw events, normalized events, feature snapshots, scores, model/policy versions, and eventual labels so that every alert can be reconstructed. Each monitor should report by calendar month and by case-start month; the former describes operational activity occurring in a month, while the latter attributes cases to their starting regime and is more suitable for outcome analysis.

- **Predictive-performance monitors:** once labels mature, track AUROC, PR-AUC, Brier, top-decile capture, score quantiles, score volume, and observed/predicted breach rate. Always break these out by variant, source/partner, supplier exposure, and label age. Keep an unlabeled score-distribution monitor active in real time, but do not treat score movement as proof of target drift.

- **Calibration monitors:** track calibration intercept and slope, ECE/reliability bins, Brier components, and calibration curves for V1/V2/V3 and each important source. Compare raw and mix-standardized calibration. A calibration alarm should first ask whether the target cohort is mature and whether the feature lineage is healthy.

- **Label-maturity monitors:** track the completion curve by case age, median and tail label delay, fraction mature at each vintage, outcome-API backlog, and maturity by path/source. Maintain fixed-vintage reports and censoring weights. A low-maturity alarm changes the interpretation and suppresses target-based retraining; it does not itself say that the model has drifted.

- **Data-quality and lineage monitors:** validate schema and activity mappings, unknown-activity share, duplicate/conflicting-event rate, missingness and null reasons, event ordering, timestamp validity, diagnosis-anchor coverage, feature freshness, score availability, and transformation/version lineage. April-like failures should be release-blocking and should invalidate affected metrics until replayed.

- **Process-variant monitors:** track variant frequencies, unmapped/other share, transition counts, conformance deviations, route-policy changes, and the number of cases in each variant. Alert on changes such as the June V2 increase or September V3/Partner Z concentration, but send them to stratification and process review rather than directly to retraining.

- **Performance-DFG monitors:** monitor edge-level median, P90/P95, variance, and missingness, especially Order Part -> Part Received, segmented by supplier, partner, variant, and start date. Track edge changes against a DFG count baseline and use the stable diagnosis-to-Remote Fix edge as a control. Performance-DFG alerts should trigger operational/SLA investigation and a conditional risk test.

- **Population monitors:** monitor source/partner share, V1/V2/V3 composition, rural distance, device age, geography, and other model inputs using distribution distances or quantile shifts. Run domain-shift and transport reports for Partner Z, and monitor whether representation differs across groups before making fairness claims.

- **Intervention and deployment monitors:** log model, feature-pipeline, mapping, routing-policy, supplier, and threshold changes; monitor action rates, queue load, review capacity, response times, overrides, and outcomes of interventions. A threshold change can alter the process population, so post-deployment metrics need an intervention flag and a contemporaneous control or stepped rollout where feasible.

Alerts should be gated in dependency order:

1. A data-quality/lineage red alert blocks interpretation of affected predictive metrics and blocks recalibration or retraining.
2. If label maturity is below the configured vintage threshold, raw breach, Brier, ECE, and AUROC alerts are marked provisional. The system uses maturity curves and fixed-vintage views instead; it does not automatically retrain.
3. If data and labels pass, a process-variant, performance-DFG, or population alert requires stratified/reweighted analysis before declaring concept drift.
4. Only a persistent conditional calibration or discrimination failure on clean, mature, representative data can open a human-reviewed concept-drift/retraining decision. A retraining gate should require a repaired-pipeline replay, an approved data window, a time-based holdout, and a rollback plan.

~~~python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE, ACT, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"

def _edges(dfg):
    return [{"from": a, "to": b, "value": v} for (a, b), v in dfg.items()]

def monthly_process_evidence(path="field_service_events.csv"):
    raw = pd.read_csv(path)
    aliases = {
        "case_id": "case_id", "case:concept:name": "case_id",
        "activity": "activity", "concept:name": "activity",
        "timestamp": "timestamp", "time:timestamp": "timestamp",
    }
    raw = raw.rename(columns={c: aliases[c] for c in raw.columns if c in aliases})
    required = {"case_id", "activity", "timestamp"}
    missing = required - set(raw.columns)
    if missing:
        raise ValueError(f"missing columns: {sorted(missing)}")
    if "source" not in raw:
        raw["source"] = "unknown"
    raw["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["timestamp"], utc=True)
    raw["activity"] = raw["activity"].astype("string").str.strip()
    # Normalize before any prefix, variant, or temporal feature is computed.
    raw["activity"] = raw["activity"].replace({
        "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
    }).fillna("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY")

    audit = {"input_rows": len(raw)}
    exact = raw.duplicated(keep="first")
    audit["exact_duplicates_removed"] = int(exact.sum())
    raw = raw.loc[~exact].copy()
    # Do not silently delete non-identical collisions: retain and report them.
    key = ["case_id", "activity", "timestamp", "source"]
    audit["ambiguous_event_keys_flagged"] = int(raw.duplicated(key, keep=False).sum())

    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        raw, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp"
    ).sort_values([CASE, TS])
    tz = log[TS].dt.tz
    months = sorted(log[TS].dt.strftime("%Y-%m").dropna().unique())
    first_seen = log.groupby(CASE)[TS].min()
    monthly = []

    for month in months:
        start = pd.Timestamp(f"{month}-01")
        end = start + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)
        if tz is not None:
            start, end = start.tz_localize(tz), end.tz_localize(tz)

        # Event-calendar cohort: events occurring in the month; traces may be partial.
        calendar = pm4py.filter_time_range(log, start, end, mode="events")
        # Case-start cohort: all currently available events for cases first seen in month.
        ids = first_seen[(first_seen >= start) & (first_seen < end)].index
        started = log[log[CASE].isin(ids)].copy()
        dfg = pm4py.discover_dfg(started, case_id_key=CASE, activity_key=ACT,
                                 timestamp_key=TS)
        pdfg = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
            started, case_id_key=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS
        )
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(started, activity_key=ACT)
        temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
            started, case_id_key=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS
        )
        nums = temporal.select_dtypes("number")
        monthly.append({
            "month": month,
            "event_calendar": {
                "events": len(calendar), "cases_touched": calendar[CASE].nunique(),
                "unknown_share": float((calendar[ACT] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean())
                if len(calendar) else 0.0,
            },
            "case_start": {
                "cases": len(ids), "events_available": len(started),
                "variant_counts": {str(k): len(v) for k, v in variants.items()},
                "dfg": _edges(dfg), "performance_dfg": _edges(pdfg),
                "temporal_feature_summary": nums.agg(["count", "mean", "median"])
                    .round(3).to_dict(),
            },
            "feature_missingness": log.isna().mean().round(4).to_dict(),
        })
    audit["output_months"] = months
    return {"audit": audit, "monthly": monthly}
~~~

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

- **Retraining on immature labels.** The model can learn that unresolved October cases are non-breaches or otherwise fit a selected subset of easy-to-label cases. This biases base rates, calibration, and possibly variant effects. Mitigate with a fixed-age maturity gate, maturity curves by path/source, censoring-aware estimation, and a time-based mature holdout.

- **Calibrating around the April pipeline bug.** A calibration layer fitted to inflated April predictions would encode malformed diagnosis prefixes and stale cached features. It might make the broken representation look numerically better while damaging clean data. Mitigate by blocking April, replaying normalized/deduplicated features, comparing case-level lineage, and calibrating only on an independently verified clean cohort.

- **Ignoring genuine supplier-driven process/concept deterioration.** A pooled recalibration could hide severe V3 underprediction while the Order Part -> Part Received edge continues to expand. Missed breaches and late interventions would persist. Mitigate with supplier- and variant-level performance-DFG alerts, temporary V3 operational controls, known-at-score-time supplier/SLA features, and a retraining decision based on mature conditional residuals.

- **Calling Partner Z unfair or unsafe from an unstratified rate gap.** The 0.45 versus 0.27 comparison confounds source, V3 mix, rural distance, device age, supplier exposure, and label maturity. A premature source-specific model or threshold could deny service or allocate resources unfairly. Mitigate with mature-label source-stratified calibration, matched/reweighted transport checks, explicit fairness metrics within relevant strata, and collection of missing legitimate covariates.

- **Treating June's route-mix change as concept drift.** Retraining on the changed V2/V3 mixture may make the model chase a policy decision rather than learn a changed outcome relationship, and could erase useful baseline information. Mitigate with variant-stratified metrics, mix standardization, policy-date annotations, and a shared model with interactions or hierarchical calibration.

- **Using post-diagnosis process outcomes as predictive features.** Adding actual parts receipt time would make retrospective metrics look strong but would leak future information at the scoring point. Mitigate with feature-availability timestamps and lineage checks, using post-diagnosis durations only for diagnosis/monitoring unless a forecast of them is available before scoring.

- **Applying one global threshold during composition and capacity changes.** The June routing policy and Partner Z onboarding can change the number and type of cases sent to intervention even if the score is unchanged, creating operational overload or missed high-risk cases. Mitigate with volume/action-rate monitors, variant-aware threshold simulation, capacity limits, and a logged, reversible rollout.
