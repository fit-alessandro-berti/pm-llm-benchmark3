## 1. Interaction-graph description

**Node types:** Job, Component, ComponentLot, Machine, Tool, Batch, Shipment.

**Edge types and qualifiers observed:**
- `requires` (JobComponent) — direct, structural, static.
- `contains` (ComponentLotComponent) — direct, static.
- `batches` (BatchJob) — direct, static membership.
- `consolidates` (ShipmentJob) — direct, static membership.
- `Batch Cure` — a single event relating {B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1, J2, J3, C1, C2, C3} — direct multi-object event.
- `Quarantine Lot` / `Release Lot` — events relating {L7, C2, C5, J2, J5} — direct, but *inconsistently propagated* to Job objects depending on extraction pipeline (flagged as such).
- `acquire`/`release` Tool — inferred hold intervals from paired events (FIX-A for J3, J4).
- `Calibrate`/`Final Test` — direct event linking {TEST-1, CAL-3, Job}.
- `Pack``Dispatch` timing relative to Shipment membership — direct events, but *waiting* between Pack and Dispatch is not itself an observed edge; it is derived by subtracting timestamps across two object types (Job and Shipment).

**Higher-order motifs (3 object types):**

1. **Batch-Tool-Machine-Job barrier motif:** {B1, RACK-7, OVEN-1, J1, J2, J3} — a batch event forces synchronization of three jobs on one tool/machine pair; readiness times differ by hours (J1 08:10, J2 08:25, J3 11:55) but all share one `Batch Cure` timestamp (12:05).
2. **ComponentLot-Job-Job coupling motif:** {L7, C2, C5, J2, J5} — two unrelated jobs (different batches, different shipments) are coupled only through shared lot quarantine/release, invisible in the Job-only flattening.
3. **Tool-Machine-Job contention without wait-event motif:** {FIX-A, CNC-2, J3, J4} — J3 holds FIX-A 09:00–11:40; CNC-2 is idle 10:05–10:47 while J4 (present at 09:50) cannot start Machine Process until 11:45. No `Wait for Tool` event exists, so the causal link is inferred from co-temporal tool custody, not observed directly.
4. **Tool-Machine-Job-Job recalibration motif:** {CAL-3, TEST-1, J5, J6} — J6 (ready 16:05) is delayed past J5’s test completion (16:35) by a mandatory recalibration event (16:35–17:20) attached to the *tool*, not to either job.
5. **Job-Shipment-Job motif:** {S1, J1, J4} — J1 finishes Pack at 13:30 but S1 does not dispatch until 19:25, driven entirely by J4’s pack time (19:10), a job in a different batch (B2) and different component lot lineage.

**Adjacency / edge-list (abbreviated, direct vs inferred marked):**

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

All calculations assume readiness timestamps are trustworthy proxies for "could have started immediately if unconstrained" — a conjectural counterfactual, since readiness itself may be softened by upstream unlogged waits.

1. **J1 synchronization wait in B1:** 12:05  08:10 = **3h 55min**. Counterfactual: J1 could have entered cure immediately upon readiness, independent of J2/J3. This is an upper bound, not a proven avoidable delay, since OVEN-1/RACK-7 availability before 12:05 is not confirmed.

2. **J2 synchronization wait in B1:** 12:05  08:25 = **3h 40min**. Same counterfactual. Note J2 also entered B1 *before* its lot (L7) was released (Release Lot at 13:50), via supervisor override — meaning the observed batch start (12:05) actually *precedes* nominal lot clearance, so the “wait” here is not lot-caused; it is attributable to co-batching with slower-ready J3, not to L7.

3. **J3 marginal wait in B1:** 12:05  11:55 = **10 min** — negligible; J3 is effectively the pacing job for B1's start, consistent with batch-sync hypothesis (the batch waited for the last-ready member).

4. **FIX-A-associated delay for J4:** CNC-2 idle window 10:05–10:47 (42 min) plus additional wait until Machine Process actually starts at 11:45. Naive gap: 11:45  09:50 (J4 arrival) = **1h 55min**. If we attribute delay only to the portion overlapping FIX-A custody (09:00–11:40 held by J3), the overlapping-with-J4-waiting portion is 09:50–11:40 = **1h 50min**. Counterfactual: had FIX-A been free at 09:50, J4 might have started near the 10:05–10:47 idle window. This is conjecture — no `Wait for Tool` event confirms FIX-A as the blocking resource; CNC-2 idleness could equally reflect operator scheduling.

5. **S1 consolidation delay for J1:** Packed 13:30, dispatched 19:25  **5h 55min** held after being pack-complete, attributable to waiting for J4 (packed 19:10). Counterfactual: if J1 shipped alone or via a different consolidation policy, dispatch could have occurred ~13:30–14:30 (allowing load time). This is a direct arithmetic fact about elapsed time, but the *causal* claim that J4 "caused" the wait is an inference from co-membership in S1, not a logged causal event.

6. **CAL-3-associated delay for J6:** J6 ready at 16:05; TEST-1 idle 16:35–17:25 for recalibration; J6 actually tests 17:25–17:50. Naive wait = 17:25  16:05 = **1h 20min**. Of this, the recalibration window itself (16:35–17:20, 45 min) is a hard policy-driven block during which no job could be tested. Counterfactual: had recalibration been scheduled off-peak or CAL-3 duplicated, J6 could plausibly have tested immediately after J5 finished (16:35), saving up to ~50 minutes. This is a plausible mechanism, not a proven one — J6's own readiness definition may already embed slack.

Batch synchronization ranks first because it has the largest, most directly quantifiable waiting (3h40–3h55, corroborated in both B1 and B2), a clear mechanism (last-ready job paces the group), and partial visibility once batch-object timestamps are joined — unlike tool contention, which remains an inference. Tool contention ranks second because though plausible and quantitatively similar in scale, it depends on an absent event type. Shipment consolidation is ranked third: it is *arithmetically* the most certain (no inference needed, just subtraction) but affects fewer job pairs currently evidenced, and is a policy artifact rather than a resource constraint. Lower-ranked mechanisms have weaker or more circumstantial support.

## 4. What flattening misses

**Job flattening:** Makes visible per-job stage progression and a seemingly uniform six-job timing distribution (no job >1.8 SD from mean). Hides all cross-job coupling: batch barriers, lot quarantines, shipment consolidation. Misleading conclusion: "no individual job is anomalous, so there's no systemic issue" — false, because the anomaly lives in *pairs and groups*, not individuals.

**Machine flattening:** Makes visible utilization and typical activity sequences per machine. Hides *why* gaps occur (tool custody, batch composition) and duplicates the appearance of idle capacity as "spare," when it is structurally blocked. Misleading conclusion: "CNC-2 at 54% utilization has slack" — but the 42-min idle window (10:05–10:47) coexists with a job (J4) unable to proceed due to FIX-A custody, not lack of demand.

**Batch flattening (implicit, via Batch-Machine/Batch-Tool pairs):** Makes visible the existence of synchronization barriers (Batch–Machine frequency = 2, flagged explicitly as low-frequency-but-high-impact). Hides the internal heterogeneity of readiness times within a batch. Misleading conclusion: two Batch-Machine interactions look statistically negligible, masking that each event gates three jobs' worth of downstream schedule.

**Tool flattening:** Makes visible acquire/release intervals (when logged). Hides tool-induced waiting entirely when no `Wait for Tool` event exists (FIX-A/J4 case) and hides that RACK-7 status ("cleaning/quarantine" until 14:35) throttles B2 independent of OVEN-1's own idle state (12:55–14:50 idle, yet blocked by tool state, not machine state). Misleading conclusion: OVEN-1 "idle" implies no constraint, when the constraint is the rack, a different object entirely.

**ComponentLot flattening:** Makes visible quarantine/release windows and which components share lineage. Hides which *specific jobs* are actually behaviorally coupled, because Job–ComponentLot linkage is admitted to be inconsistent ("direction depends on graph convention", "inconsistently linked"). Misleading conclusion: L7's quarantine appears to affect "C2 and C5" as components, obscuring that it differentially affected J2 (proceeded via override) versus J5 (waited), i.e., policy overrides can decouple nominally identical constraints.

**Shipment flattening:** Makes visible CreateLoadDispatch timing, uniformly ~1.1h total, looking efficient. Hides the pre-load waiting of already-packed jobs (J1's 5h55min post-pack wait folded into nothing, since the metric starts at Create Shipment). Misleading conclusion: "no shipment bottleneck" — technically true for the measured segment, false for the job's total experience.

**Why low machine utilization coexists with long job waiting:** Utilization is computed over machine-observed events (SetupProcessRelease, or LoadCureUnload), which only count time the machine is *actively engaged*. Waiting that occurs because a *tool*, *batch partner*, or *lot* is unavailable does not register as machine activity or machine gap-with-known-cause; it registers as an unlabeled idle interval attributed generically to "gap," which utilization statistics simply exclude from the denominator's numerator. A job can wait for hours while its machine looks underused, because the constraining resource is a different object type entirely.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**Narrative A — Batch/Tool/Machine/Job (B1, RACK-7, OVEN-1, J1/J2/J3):**
Temporal precedence: J1 ready 08:10, J2 ready 08:25, J3 ready 11:55; single Batch Cure event 12:05 links all three plus C1, C2, C3, RACK-7, OVEN-1. Resource feasibility: OVEN-1 and RACK-7 must simultaneously be free and loaded with all batch members before cure starts — a hard co-occurrence requirement evidenced by the single shared event. Policy constraint: batching policy evidently requires full membership readiness before cure (implied by the 12:05 start aligning closely with J3's 11:55 readiness, not J1/J2's earlier readiness). Causal speculation: it is *plausible but unproven* that J3's lateness specifically forced the delay for J1/J2, since we lack direct evidence that OVEN-1/RACK-7 were otherwise ready at, say, 08:30.

**Narrative B — Tool/Machine/Job/Job (FIX-A, CNC-2, J3, J4):**
Temporal precedence: J3 acquires FIX-A 09:00, releases 11:40; J4 reaches CNC-2 09:50; J4's Machine Process starts 11:45, five minutes after FIX-A release. Resource feasibility: Machine Process for J4 explicitly relates to FIX-A as well as CNC-2, meaning FIX-A is a necessary co-resource. Policy constraint: none stated — this appears to be a resource-availability issue, not a rule. Causal speculation: the five-minute gap between FIX-A release (11:40) and J4 start (11:45) is suggestive but not proof of direct causation, since intervening setup/logging lag could equally explain it; the *absence* of a Wait-for-Tool event means we are inferring blockage from temporal adjacency, which the instructions caution against treating as proof.

**Narrative C — ComponentLot/Job/Job/Batch-Shipment (L7, J2, J5, B1/B2, S2/S3):**
Temporal precedence: Quarantine Lot event 10:20 relates L7 (containing C2, C5), J2, J5; Release Lot at 13:50. Resource feasibility: nominally, C2 (for J2) and C5 (for J5) should both be unavailable until 13:50. Policy constraint: a supervisor override (recorded in a separate system) allowed J2 into B1 before release — an explicit policy exception, not a resource fact. Causal speculation: J5's actual delay until after 13:50 is consistent with lot-release gating, but because J2 shows the *same* nominal constraint yet proceeded early, we cannot assert lot release is a uniformly enforced mechanism — its effect is conditional on administrative discretion, which is a policy/organizational variable absent from the object graph itself except as a footnote.

**Narrative D — Job/Machine/Tool/Shipment (J5, J6, CAL-3, TEST-1, S3):**
Temporal precedence: J5 tests 16:10–16:35; CAL-3 recalibrates 16:35–17:20; J6 tests 17:25–17:50; S3 (consolidating J5, J6) dispatches 35 minutes after last job packed. Resource feasibility: Final Test requires CAL-3 for both jobs, and recalibration is presumably triggered by usage (policy) rather than by a fault (data does not distinguish). Causal speculation: it is tempting to say "J6 was delayed by CAL-3 recalibration triggered by J5's test," but the *triggering rule* (usage-based vs. scheduled) is not evidenced; we only observe temporal adjacency.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

1. **Target: batch synchronization.** *Intervention:* allow partial/rolling batch starts (cure sub-batches when 2 of 3 members ready, or stagger cure racks). *Benefit:* reduces J1/J2-type multi-hour waits. *Helped:* J1, J2, future fast-ready batch members. *Harmed:* may reduce oven thermal efficiency, could delay slow-ready jobs like J3 further if rack capacity is split. *New risk:* more frequent OVEN-1 cycling, higher energy/tooling wear. *Perverse incentive:* a "batch fill rate" KPI could be gamed by delaying ready jobs to wait for full batches, worsening the very problem. *Evaluation:* object-centric before/after comparison of JobBatch waiting time distributions conditioned on batch composition heterogeneity (variance of readiness within a batch).

2. **Target: shared-tool contention.** *Intervention:* acquire a second FIX-A unit or instrument explicit `Wait for Tool` events for observability first. *Benefit:* removes inferred 1h50 delay for J4-type cases. *Helped:* J4 and any job queued behind a long-holding job like J3. *Harmed:* capital cost; if only logging is added (no duplicate tool), no throughput benefit yet. *New risk:* false confidence if a second fixture is purchased before confirming the mechanism is real (see falsification section). *Perverse incentive:* tool utilization % as a KPI could discourage acquiring a redundant tool even though low utilization can still cause queueing delay (cf. Section 4). *Evaluation:* compare Job–Tool interaction logs pre/post for changes in machine-start latency conditioned on tool release events.

3. **Target: shipment consolidation policy.** *Intervention:* allow partial shipment dispatch or a maximum consolidation-wait SLA (e.g., dispatch after 2h regardless of partner readiness). *Benefit:* J1 would save up to ~5h in S1 case. *Helped:* early-packed jobs like J1. *Harmed:* may raise per-shipment logistics cost (more, smaller shipments), affecting jobs like J4 that would have shared cost. *New risk:* increased shipment count budgets. *Perverse incentive:* "average dispatch delay after pack" as a KPI might be improved by simply changing shipment groupings without addressing root pack-time variance, hiding the real driver (why J4 packs so late relative to J1). *Evaluation:* object-centric comparison of Job–Shipment wait time (PackDispatch) segmented by whether shipment partners share a Batch/Lot upstream, to check if consolidation delay correlates with upstream coupling rather than shipment policy alone.

4. **Target: component-lot release coupling and overrides.** *Intervention:* formalize the override mechanism into the primary system (rather than "a separate system") so overrides are logged as first-class O2O events. *Benefit:* removes ambiguity about why J2 proceeded early while J5 waited. *Helped:* future analysts and schedulers; potentially J5-like jobs if overrides can be applied more consistently. *Harmed:* none directly, though formalizing overrides could reduce supervisor flexibility. *New risk:* administrative overhead. *Perverse incentive:* an "override usage rate" KPI could suppress legitimate overrides needed for urgent jobs, or conversely encourage overuse to hit speed targets, weakening quarantine's purpose. *Evaluation:* extract Job–ComponentLot relations with a revised pipeline (per the note that "an alternative integration pipeline would relate these events to J2 and J5") and re-run object-centric performance analysis to see whether lot-coupling delay estimates change materially.

5. **Target: logging/data-integration improvement (not operations).** *Intervention:* instrument explicit `Wait for Tool`, `Wait for Batch`, and `Wait for Lot Release` events, and standardize Job–ComponentLot linkage direction/consistency. *Benefit:* converts multiple "plausible/conjectural" mechanisms into directly observable ones, enabling correct ranking. *Helped:* all analysis stakeholders; indirectly all jobs via better-targeted future interventions. *Harmed:* none operationally, though logging overhead and change-management cost exist. *New risk:* temporary data schema instability during transition. *Perverse incentive:* a "data completeness" KPI could incentivize logging volume over logging *quality* (e.g., trivial events created just to hit a count), diluting signal. *Evaluation:* compare hypothesis confidence (as in Section 3's ranking) before and after instrumentation — specifically check whether inferred FIX-A and CAL-3 delays match newly logged explicit wait durations.

6. **Target: CAL-3 recalibration scheduling.** *Intervention:* schedule recalibration during known idle windows (e.g., overnight) rather than reactively after use. *Benefit:* removes the 45-min block between J5 and J6 tests. *Helped:* J6 and future back-to-back test pairs. *Harmed:* may increase recalibration frequency/cost if usage-based triggering is actually necessary for accuracy (unverified). *New risk:* test quality risk if recalibration cadence is loosened without evidence it's safe. *Perverse incentive:* "TEST-1 uptime" KPI might discourage recalibration entirely. *Evaluation:* object-centric comparison of Tool (CAL-3) idle/active windows against Job wait times pre/post schedule change.

## 7. PM4Py analysis outline

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Assume `ocel` is a pre-loaded OCEL log (pm4py OCEL object)

# 1. Global object interaction summary (counts, not just waiting inference)
interactions = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)
print(interactions.sort_values(by=interactions.columns[-1], ascending=False).head(20))

# 2. Object graph (co-occurrence across events) to find higher-order motifs
obj_graph = pm4py.discover_objects_graph(ocel, graph_type="object_interaction")

# 3. Object-type transition graph (OTG) to see type-level coupling frequencies
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)

# 4. Event-to-object-type matrix, to check which event types touch which object types
#    (helps confirm e.g. Batch Cure touches Job+Machine+Tool+Component simultaneously)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# 5. Object-centric DFG, comparing per-object-type frequency AND performance,
#    not collapsing to a single flattened job view
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
pm4py.view_ocdfg(ocdfg)  # visual comparison of edge frequency across object types

# 6. Flatten separately per object type for comparison of frequency metrics
job_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Job")
batch_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Batch")
tool_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Tool")

# 7. Performance DFGs per flattening -- compare frequency vs performance metric,
#    not just machine-gap-derived waiting
perf_dfg_job, sa_job, ea_job = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(job_flat)
perf_dfg_batch, sa_b, ea_b = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(batch_flat)

# Cross-check: frequency-based DFG discrepancy between Job-level and Batch-level views
freq_dfg_job, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(job_flat)
freq_dfg_batch, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(batch_flat)

# 8. Extract OCEL features per object (for downstream ML / statistical comparison
#    of waiting attributable to co-membership in Batch/Lot/Shipment, not machine gaps)
feature_table, feature_names = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(
    ocel,
    obj_type="Job",
    include_obj_types=["Batch", "Tool", "ComponentLot", "Shipment", "Machine"]
)
features_df = pd.DataFrame(feature_table, columns=feature_names)

# 9. Optional: convert to networkx to directly query multi-hop motifs
#    e.g. paths Job -> Batch -> Tool -> Machine -> Job (shared-batch, shared-tool motifs)
G = pm4py.convert_ocel_to_networkx(ocel)

# Example motif query: find jobs connected only via ComponentLot (not via Batch/Shipment)
lot_only_pairs = []
for j1 in [n for n in G.nodes if str(n).startswith("J")]:
    for j2 in [n for n in G.nodes if str(n).startswith("J")]:
        if j1 < j2:
            try:
                common = set(G.neighbors(j1)) & set(G.neighbors(j2))
                lot_common = {c for c in common if str(c).startswith("L")}
                batch_common = {c for c in common if str(c).startswith("B")}
                if lot_common and not batch_common:
                    lot_only_pairs.append((j1, j2, lot_common))
            except Exception:
                pass

print("Jobs coupled only via ComponentLot (not Batch):", lot_only_pairs)
```

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations that would weaken the top-ranked (batch synchronization) explanation:**
1. Discovery that OVEN-1/RACK-7 were *also* unavailable (e.g., under maintenance) before 12:05, independent of J3's readiness — meaning the batch wasn't actually waiting on J3.
2. A pattern across many more batches showing cure start time is *fixed by shift schedule* (e.g., always starts at 12:05/14:50 regardless of readiness), implying a scheduling policy, not synchronization-to-last-member.
3. Evidence that J3's own 11:55 readiness was itself artificially delayed by an unrelated upstream cause (e.g., FIX-A), making the "batch waited for J3" framing circular.
4. Batches with homogeneous readiness times still showing multi-hour start delays (would point to machine/tool causes instead).
5. RACK-7 or OVEN-1 logs showing genuine unavailability windows overlapping the presumed "waiting" period.
6. Confirmation that "readiness" timestamps themselves include buffer/slack added by planners, making raw subtraction (Section 2, calc 1–2) an overestimate of true avoidable delay.

**Additional fields/event types to discriminate among hypotheses:**
1. Explicit `Wait for Tool` / `Wait for Batch` / `Wait for Lot` events with start/end timestamps.
2. Machine maintenance/availability calendars (to separate true capacity constraints from tool/batch-induced idling).
3. A consistent, bidirectional Job–ComponentLot linkage (resolving the "direction depends on graph convention" ambiguity).
4. Logged reason codes for RACK-7's "cleaning/quarantine" status (duration drivers, whether triggered by policy or fault).
5. CAL-3 recalibration trigger metadata (usage-count-based vs. fixed-schedule-based).
6. A full audit log of supervisor overrides (currently "in a separate system") merged into the OCEL, including who authorized J2's early entry into B1 and under what criteria.
7. Shipment routing/cost data, to evaluate whether consolidation delay (S1) is driven by logistics cost minimization policy or by simple default batching rules.
8. Historical batch-composition data across many cycles, to test whether "last-ready-job paces batch" holds statistically rather than as a single-instance coincidence.
