## 1. Materiality Framework

**Severity Scale (1–5)**
- **1 – Noise / Model Mismatch:** Harmless technical artifacts, telemetry, retries, or events outside the model's intended scope. No control, financial, or customer impact.
- **2 – Documentation / Evidence Gap:** Operational compliance achieved, but audit trail, system logging, or evidence linkage is weak or fragmented. Moderate audit/regulatory exposure.
- **3 – Process Deviation (Medium):** Activity performed out of sequence or via workaround, but mitigated by downstream controls or low exposure. Reversible or low financial impact.
- **4 – Policy Violation / Control Bypass:** Mandatory controls skipped, data-aware thresholds ignored, or high-risk paths executed without required gates. Significant financial, fraud, or compliance exposure.
- **5 – Critical / Irreversible Failure:** Payment executed before approval, control failure on high-value/high-risk claim, or irreversible harm to customer/regulatory standing. Immediate containment required.

**Confidence vs. Severity**
- **Severity** reflects the magnitude of business risk if the deviation is real (exposure, irreversibility, control objective impact).
- **Confidence** reflects the certainty that the deviation represents an actual operational issue rather than a logging artifact, model scope gap, or data mapping lag. High confidence + high severity = urgent action. Low confidence + high severity = requires evidence validation before escalation.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment cost measures edit distance under a uniform technical cost function; it does not capture control objectives, data conditions, irreversibility, or evidence quality. Five cases illustrate this rigorously:

1. **CL-602 (Cost=1, Severity=4):** A single missing model move (`M(Fraud Screen)`) yields low technical cost, but represents a bypass of a mandatory fraud control on a EUR 480k HIGH-risk claim with a recent bank change. The cost function cannot weight the financial exposure or regulatory breach, making cost=1 dangerously optimistic.
2. **CL-604 (Cost=3, Severity=5):** The alignment shows a log move on Pay, a missing Fraud Screen, synchronous Manual Approve, and a missing Pay. Cost=3 reflects three edit operations, but the business reality is a pay-before-approve timing violation (P3). Irreversibility and control failure severity are invisible to the cost function.
3. **CL-607 & CL-612 (Cost=4, Severity=1):** High costs arise because the model expects a payable path, but the claim was denied or withdrawn. The cost function penalizes scope mismatch as if it were operational non-compliance. P6 explicitly notes scope dispute; business risk is negligible.
4. **CL-609 & CL-610 (Cost=0, Severity=4):** Fully synchronous alignments yield zero cost, yet both violate P1/P2 data-aware policies (auto-approve on EUR 18k/MEDIUM risk and recent bank change). The model lacks data-condition encoding, so the alignment engine sees perfect conformance while business policy is breached. Zero cost masks critical fraud exposure.
5. **CL-601 & CL-603 (Cost=3/1, Severity=1):** Costs are inflated by UI telemetry and transport retries. The uniform cost function treats `L(Autosave)` and `L(Notify Claimant)` duplicates identically to missing controls, despite P5 explicitly excluding them from business activities. Cost here is purely technical noise.

**Conclusion:** Alignment cost is a structural fitness metric, not a risk metric. It ignores policy overlays, data attributes, irreversibility, evidence fragility, and model scope. Business severity must be derived from control objectives, exposure, and policy compliance, not edit distance.
