# 2. Shared Assumptions

- Log events are completion events only. “Before” means “completed before”, not “started before”. Duration and overlap are unknown.
- Activities with identical timestamps are ordered by the ingestion index (S7); this can create arbitrary B/C orders.
- D optionality is an abstraction. Because the external/stocked attribute is absent, a trace without D cannot distinguish:
  1. supplier confirmation not required,
  2. supplier confirmation required but skipped by mistake,
  3. supplier confirmation occurred but the event was lost by the integration problem described in S5.
- E optionality is modeled as `X(E, tau)`, meaning E either occurs once or is skipped. E also occurs at most once.
- D is modeled as `X(D, tau)` for the same reason: D either appears once or is absent.
- Start and end rules D1 are enforced in all models: every completed trace starts with Register Order and ends with Archive Case.
- D2 is enforced in all models: Release Production cannot occur before Approve Configuration; in fact the observed traces place them adjacently, and we model that as FG.
- None of the models claims to have discovered the true process. They are three competing hypotheses.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

**Inductive-bias statement.**  
The conservative model maximizes fidelity to the observed 30 cases. Its bias is “do not admit any complete trace unless it was directly observed.” It is an XOR enumeration of the seven observed variants. This is deliberately overfitted and is useful as an audit baseline, not as a prediction model. The duplicate labels across XOR branches are allowed in PM4Py, but they make alignment diagnostics and visualizations more expensive; exact enumeration also scales poorly to logs with hundreds of distinct variants.

**Compact formal expression.**

Let `A = Register Order`, `B = Credit Check`, `C = Engineering Review`, `D = Supplier Confirmation`, `E = Expedite Review`, `F = Approve Configuration`, `G = Release Production`, `H = Archive Case`.

M_{conservative} = XOR(
\rightarrow(A,B,C,D,F,G,H),
\rightarrow(A,C,B,D,F,G,H),
\rightarrow(A,B,C,F,G,H),
\rightarrow(A,C,B,F,G,H),
\rightarrow(A,B,D,C,F,G,H),
\rightarrow(A,B,C,D,E,F,G,H),
\rightarrow(A,C,B,E,D,F,G,H)
)
\]

# 4. Balanced Candidate

**Inductive-bias statement.**  
The balanced model adds only the most defensible generalizations: the mirror of the observed B-D-C interleaving, and the two plausible placements of E relative to D. It does not allow arbitrary interleavings. It treats B/C order as an XOR of the two observed completion orders rather than as a true parallel block, because completion timestamps do not prove concurrency and because placing constraints such as “E after both checks” is cleaner with two ordered templates.

**Compact formal expression.**

\[
M_{balanced} = \rightarrow(A, XOR(P_1,P_2,P_3,P_4), F, G, H)
\]

\[
P_1 = \rightarrow(B, C, +(X(D,\tau), X(E,\tau)))
\]

\[
P_2 = \rightarrow(C, B, +(X(D,\tau), X(E,\tau)))
\]

\[
P_3 = \rightarrow(B, D, C, X(E,\tau))
\]

\[
P_4 = \rightarrow(C, D, B, X(E,\tau))
\]

Here `+` is parallel composition, `X` is exclusive choice, and `` is a silent skip.

**Decision rationale: sequence, concurrency, and partial order.**

- **B/C order:** The DFG and V1/V2/V4/V7 show both BC and CB. S1 would force B before C, but S2 and D7 make concurrency plausible. I do not use a single `AND(B,C)` block because D can occur between B and C (V5), and E must wait for both checks. Instead, I use two sequence templates P1 and P2 for “B first” and “C first”. This is equivalent to the observed completion-order uncertainty and avoids claiming true time overlap.
- **D position:** D is optional. In P1/P2, D occurs after both checks and can be interleaved with E. In P3/P4, D occurs after the first check and before the second, matching V5 and its plausible mirror. This honors D4 and the low-medium rule D5.
- **E position:** E is optional and always after B and C. In P1/P2, D and E are placed in a small parallel composition, so E may occur before or after D. In P3/P4, D occurs before the second check, and E after both checks, so E naturally occurs after D.
- **No loops:** every selected activity occurs at most once because D and E are protected by `X(..., tau)`.

**Code.**

```python
balanced_str = (
    "->( 'Register Order', X( "
    "->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', +( X( 'Supplier Confirmation', tau ), X( 'Expedite Review', tau ) ) ), "
    "->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', +( X( 'Supplier Confirmation', tau ), X( 'Expedite Review', tau ) ) ), "
    "->( 'Credit Check', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Engineering Review', X( 'Expedite Review', tau ) ), "
    "->( 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Credit Check', X( 'Expedite Review', tau ) ) "
    "), 'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case' )"
)

model_balanced = pm4py.parse_process_tree(balanced_str)
```

**Behavior deliberately excluded.**

- D before any check, e.g. `A,D,B,C,F,G,H`.
- E before both B and C, e.g. `A,B,E,C,F,G,H`.
- Two D or two E events in one trace.
- D after F, or E after F.
- Arbitrary interleavings such as `A,D,B,E,C,F,G,H`.

**Known underfitting risks.**

- It still does not represent true concurrency; it only represents two completion-order templates.
- It does not allow D to occur after C but before B except through the mirrored P4 branch. That is intentional, but it may underfit if the real process is a genuine partial order.
- It cannot represent missing D events in positions other than the four templates.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

**Inductive-bias statement.**  
The permissive model uses a strict partial order to admit all interleavings that do not violate the high/medium-high constraints. It deliberately relaxes the low-medium rule D5: Supplier Confirmation is allowed to float before either check completes. This is useful for sensitivity analysis: if future traces show D before B/C, then D5 is weaker than assumed.

**Compact formal expression.**

Let `OpD = X(D, )` and `OpE = X(E, )`.

\[
M_{permissive} = StrictPartialOrder(
A < B,\ A < C,\ A < OpD,\ A < OpE,
\]
\[
B < OpE,\ C < OpE,
\]
\[
B < F,\ C < F,\ OpD < F,\ OpE < F,
\]
\[
F < G,\ G < H
)
\]

There is no order between B, C, and OpD, so D may appear before, between, or after B and C. This is the key relaxation.

**Boundary between reasonable generalization and forbidden behavior.**

- Allowed: D at any position before F; E after B and C and before F; B/C in any order.
- Still forbidden: wrong start/end, G before F, F before B/C/D/E, more than one D, more than one E, and E before both B and C.

**Code.**

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

def optional(label):
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[Transition(label), SilentTransition()]
    )

A = Transition("Register Order")
B = Transition("Credit Check")
C = Transition("Engineering Review")
D = optional("Supplier Confirmation")
E = optional("Expedite Review")
F = Transition("Approve Configuration")
G = Transition("Release Production")
H = Transition("Archive Case")

model_permissive = StrictPartialOrder()

for node in [A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H]:
    model_permissive.order.add_node(node)

model_permissive.order.add_edge(A, B)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(A, C)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(A, D)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(A, E)

model_permissive.order.add_edge(B, E)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(C, E)

model_permissive.order.add_edge(B, F)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(C, F)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(D, F)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(E, F)

model_permissive.order.add_edge(F, G)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(G, H)
```

**Behavior deliberately excluded.**

- Duplicate D or duplicate E.
- E before both checks.
- F before any required or selected prior activity.
- G before F, H before G, or A not first.
- Any trace containing an unknown activity label.

# 7. Comparative Model Matrix

| Criterion | Conservative | Balanced | Permissive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expected fitness on 30 observed cases | Very high; exact log replay. | Very high; all observed variants are accepted by construction. | Very high; all observed variants are accepted by construction. |
| Expected precision risk | Low risk; only observed traces are allowed. | Medium risk; a few plausible mirror traces are admitted. | High risk; many unobserved interleavings are admitted. |
| Generalization potential | Low; exact XOR enumeration overfits. | Medium; selected mirror and E/D alternatives. | High; broad partial order covers many unseen orders. |
| Structural simplicity | Simple as a list, but repetitive and non-scalable. | Moderate; four compact templates. | Compact partial-order representation. |
| Explainability | High; every trace can be pointed to in the log. | Medium-high; templates are understandable. | Lower; D can float, which is harder to explain to compliance. |
| Robustness to missing Supplier Confirmation events | Low; only the observed no-D positions are allowed. | Medium; D is optional in several structural positions. | High; D is optional and structurally unconstrained. |
| Handling of unseen interleavings | None. | Selected mirror and D/E order variants only. | All interleavings consistent with the partial-order skeleton. |
| Suitability for compliance versus exploration | Compliance/audit baseline. | Compliance with cautious exploration. | Exploration and sensitivity analysis. |

    for trace in observed_variants

model_conservative = pm4py.parse_process_tree("X(" + ", ".join(branches) + ")")

# Balanced
balanced_str = (

    "->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', +( X( 'Supplier Confirmation', tau ), X( 'Expedite Review', tau ) ) ), "
    "->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', +( X( 'Supplier Confirmation', tau ), X( 'Expedite Review', tau ) ) ), "
    "->( 'Credit Check', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Engineering Review', X( 'Expedite Review', tau ) ), "
    "->( 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Credit Check', X( 'Expedite Review', tau ) ) "
    "), 'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case' )"
)
model_balanced = pm4py.parse_process_tree(balanced_str)

# Permissive
def optional(label):
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[Transition(label), SilentTransition()]
    )

A = Transition("Register Order")
B = Transition("Credit Check")
C = Transition("Engineering Review")
D = optional("Supplier Confirmation")
E = optional("Expedite Review")

model_permissive = StrictPartialOrder()

model_permissive.order.add_edge(A, B)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(A, C)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(A, D)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(A, E)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(B, E)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(C, E)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(B, F)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(C, F)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(D, F)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(E, F)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(F, G)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(G, H)

models = {

    "balanced": model_balanced,
    "permissive": model_permissive,
}

negative_traces = [

    "Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",

    # Release Production before Approve Configuration.
    "Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Release Production, "
    "Approve Configuration, Archive Case",

    # Duplicate Supplier Confirmation.
    "Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Supplier Confirmation, "

def build_checker(model):

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

**Immediate use recommendation:** `model_balanced`. It fits all 30 observed traces, enforces the high/medium-high rules, and admits only a small set of plausible unobserved variants. It is explainable enough for compliance conversations and more useful than the exact conservative enumeration.

**Sensitivity analysis recommendation:** `model_permissive`. Run it in parallel on new data. Pay particular attention to whether any new trace begins with `Supplier Confirmation` before `Credit Check` or `Engineering Review`. If that appears, D5 is weaker than assumed and the balanced model is too strict. If it never appears after sufficient data, the permissive model can be discarded.

**Concrete data-collection plan.**

1. Collect lifecycle timestamps: for every activity, record `start` and `complete` events. This is the only way to resolve whether B and C are truly concurrent, overlapping, or sequential.
2. Export the external/stocked component attribute for each order, or at minimum a `supplier_confirmation_required` boolean. This directly separates S3/S4/S5 and tells us whether no-D is a true skip or a data loss.
3. Add a stable event/trace ID and an explicit ingestion timestamp. S7’s ingestion-order ambiguity can then be checked.
4. Record whether `Supplier Confirmation` was sent to an external supplier; if possible, record the supplier response event separately.
5. Future traces that would be maximally informative:
   - `Register Order, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check, Engineering Review, ...` — would support the permissive model and weaken D5.
   - `Register Order, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check, ...` — would support the balanced P4 mirror and show that D can occur after C before B.
   - `Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Expedite Review, Approve Configuration, ...` — would show E can occur without D.
   - `Register Order, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Engineering Review, ...` — would contradict D6 and require revision.
   - External-component cases with no Supplier Confirmation — would confirm S5 missingness, not true optionality.
6. Use holdout evaluation: collect the next 50–100 traces, then compare balanced and permissive by held-out fitness, precision, and simplicity.

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

Calibrated confidence in the major decisions:

- **D1/D2 (start/end, F before G): high.** More than 90% confidence. No plausible process interpretation explains away these constraints.
- **D3 (B/C before F): medium-high.** Around 75–90% confidence. All observed traces satisfy it, but there is no lifecycle evidence to prove that F never occurs before one of the checks.
- **D4 (D before F): medium-high.** Around 75–90% confidence. Always observed before F, but missing D events blur the strength of this rule.
- **D5 (D may start after either B or C): low-medium.** Around 40–60% confidence. The observed B-D-C trace supports it, but the Mirrored C-D-B trace is absent and the missing-data problem weakens the evidence.
- **D6 (E after B and C, before F): medium.** Around 60–75% confidence. Compliance statement S6 is credible, but only two E traces were observed.
- **B/C concurrency: medium.** Around 50–70% confidence. S2 says parallel, S1 says sequential, and D7 says the log cannot resolve it.
- **D optionality due to S4/S5: high as an abstraction, but not as a causal process fact.** Around 90% confidence that no-D can mean either skipped or missing; low confidence about which applies to any individual trace.

A plausible alternative interpretation that would materially change the recommendation is that Finance’s S1 is true and B always finishes before C, while the observed CB traces are caused by same-timestamp ingestion order or timestamp rounding. In that case, balanced and permissive models are too permissive, and a BC sequence model would be preferable.

A second material alternative is that Supplier Confirmation is truly mandatory for every external component and every no-D trace is due to the integration loss described in S5. Then the optional `X(D, tau)` in the balanced and permissive models is an artifact of missing data, and the recommendation changes to a model that treats D as mandatory but potentially unobserved.
