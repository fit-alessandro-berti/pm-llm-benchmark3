## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict:** `not identifiable from the supplied evidence`  
**Confidence:** 80 / 100

The claim that assigning cases to Orion causes longer completion times is unsupported, yet Orion's true causal effect—beneficial, neutral, or harmful—cannot be identified from these 30 completed cases.

**Strongest evidence for non-identifiability:** Crude and complexity-stratified patterns reverse sign (Simpson's reversal). Overall, Orion averages 21.96 h versus Nova's 15.24 h (+6.71 h crude), because Orion handles far more High-complexity (42.9% vs. 12.5%), compliance-required (64.3% vs. 25.0%), and night-shift (92.9% vs. 25.0%) cases. Within every complexity tier, Orion is faster—Low (-2.14 h), Medium (-2.21 h), and High (-3.43 h)—yielding a complexity-standardized contrast of -2.51 h. However, stratification does not establish causality. Overlap collapses across shift and load (zero Orion cases in Medium-Day or High-Day; only one Nova case each in Medium-Night and High-Night). Coarse complexity tiers leave room for unobserved case risk in routing exceptions, and observing only completed cases introduces right-censoring bias if Orion's hardest claims remain open or abandoned. Moreover, 75%–96% of Orion's within-tier edge lies in `wait_before_review_h` rather than `review_service_h`, indicating that `resource_group` reflects a bundle of routing, shift staffing, and queueing dynamics rather than an isolated reviewer-speed treatment.

**Strongest evidence against total non-identifiability:** The consistent sign reversal across all three complexity tiers and small service-time differences (-0.13 to -0.39 h) strongly indicate that Orion's crude +6.71 h disadvantage is mainly driven by case-mix confounding rather than slow reviewing.

**Why moving 50% of Orion's work to Nova is risky:** Reassignment would succeed only if hidden right-censoring masks genuine Orion slowness that Nova can absorb. It will fail if groups perform comparably under equal case mix, and it risks severe backfire: shifting complex, compliance-heavy, night-arriving cases to a less-certified, day-heavy pool can overload Nova's queues, lengthen service times, and increase rework or appeals.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Phase 1: Instrumentation & Baseline Audit (Weeks 1–3)** | Deploy full-lifecycle logging (no routing changes): capture open/abandoned claims, activity start/end timestamps, continuous risk score at `Classify`, active reviewer headcount/WIP, and de-identified reviewer ID | Eliminates selection bias (`case_completed`) and separates queueing from compliance and review service | All incoming claims (observational) | Zero operational disruption; read-only telemetry and privacy-preserving reviewer IDs | Complete survival/cycle-time curves (including censored WIP); decomposed waits (`Classify->Compliance`, `Compliance->Review`, `Review->Request Info`); DFG conformance and queue utilization | Directly tests H3 (right-censoring), H4 (compliance waiting), and baseline positivity/WIP overlap (H1) before touching routing | Halt downstream phases if logging completeness < 98% or timestamp drift prevents separating queue wait from service time |
| **Phase 2: Queueing & Compliance Capacity Optimization (Weeks 4–7)** | Target `wait_before_review_h` directly—without relabeling groups—by adding dedicated cross-shift compliance check capacity, separating compliance queues from review queues, and implementing WIP-aware dispatch priority | Addresses the dominant delay component (75%–96% of within-stratum gap) and compliance-step queueing without degrading certification match | Queue/shift capacity windows (pre/post + stepped-wedge across shifts or claim lines) | Preserve Orion certification rules for restricted High claims; cap WIP shift transfers to avoid starving Day or Night queues | Pre- vs. post-intervention `wait_before_review_h` split into compliance wait vs. review wait; queue length/WIP; `review_service_h`; rework rate; open-case backlog | Tests H1 and H4 by isolating how much cycle time improves purely through queue/compliance capacity rather than changing `resource_group` | Roll back queue-priority or compliance-slot changes if p90 `wait_before_review_h` rises >15% for any complexity tier or open backlog grows >10% for 3 consecutive days |
| **Phase 3: Stratified Randomized Routing Pilot (Weeks 8–13)** | Randomly assign **10%–15%** of overlap-eligible Medium and non-restricted High claims arriving during overlapping/staffed shift windows 50/50 between Orion and Nova (matched on risk tier and shift) | Identifies true causal effect of `resource_group` (total, service-time, rework, and quality effects) while holding case mix, shift, and censoring constant | Individual eligible claim at `Classify` (stratified by complexity, compliance requirement, and shift) | Exclude claims legally requiring Orion certification; cap Nova inflow at +10% of baseline capacity to prevent queue blowup; blind post-close quality audit on 20% of pilot cases | Intention-to-treat and per-protocol survival/cycle times (including open cases); decomposed wait vs. `review_service_h`; `Request Info` rate; blind quality-audit error rate; pre-routing negative-control duration | Decisively discriminates H2 (Orion beneficial) vs. H5 (Orion harmful) vs. H1 (pure queueing/shift) with valid positivity and zero censoring bias | Immediately pause pilot if either group's p90 cycle time exceeds baseline by >20%, rework rises >8 pp, or audited decision-error rate increases >3 pp |
| **Phase 4: Evidence-Gated Policy Redesign (Week 14+)** | Scale targeted routing or staffing changes only in complexity/shift strata showing statistically and operationally verified net benefit; consider cross-training/certifying Nova staff if reviewer skill drives gains | Converts causal findings into sustainable capacity allocation while respecting queueing equilibrium (SUTVA) | Complexity × shift routing policy rules | Gradual ramp (10% increments every 2 weeks) with continuous queue-utilization monitoring and ongoing audit sampling | Ongoing stratum-level cycle time, queue utilization, rework, and quality audit trends; periodic DFG drift checks | Validates external validity under higher volume (detects equilibrium queueing interference) | Freeze ramp and revert last increment if marginal queue wait offsets service-time savings or quality audit breaches SLA |

**Final recommendation:** Reject the proposal to blindly reassign 50% of Orion's future cases to Nova. The committee's rationale rests on a crude +6.71 h cycle-time difference that reverses to -2.51 h once adjusted for complexity, while 75%–96% of within-stratum differences stem from pre-review queueing rather than review speed. Shifting half of Orion's largely complex, compliance-heavy, night-shift workload into Nova risks congesting Nova's queues, lengthening service times among less-certified reviewers, and causing unmeasured quality failures.

Instead, execute the phased learning plan above. First, instrument the complete case lifecycle—including open and abandoned claims, activity timestamps, and queue WIP—to remove censoring bias and isolate compliance versus review bottlenecks. Second, target `wait_before_review_h` directly via dedicated compliance capacity and WIP-aware queue management, addressing the largest delay component without breaking certification alignment. Third, conduct a small, bounded, stratified randomized routing pilot (10%–15% of overlap-eligible claims) with blind quality audits and strict rollback rules. Expand routing changes gradually only in strata where causal evidence proves net cycle-time improvement without harming quality.

COMPLEXITY_ORDER = ["Low", "Medium", "High"]
SHIFT_ORDER = ["Day", "Night"]
GROUP_ORDER = ["Orion", "Nova"]
MIN_CELL_N = 3


def format_variant(variant_key) -> str:
    """Format PM4Py variant keys consistently across PM4Py 2.x versions."""
    if isinstance(variant_key, (tuple, list)):
        return ">".join(map(str, variant_key))
    return str(variant_key).replace(",", ">")


def main() -> None:
    print("NOTE: Diagnostic summary only; observational contrasts do not prove causality.\n")

    # 1. Load data
    df = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(CSV_DATA))
    df["complexity"] = pd.Categorical(df["complexity"], categories=COMPLEXITY_ORDER, ordered=True)
    df["shift"] = pd.Categorical(df["shift"], categories=SHIFT_ORDER, ordered=True)
    df["has_rework"] = (df["rework_count"] > 0).astype(int)
    df["has_appeal"] = (df["outcome_30d"] == "Appeal").astype(int)

        df.groupby("group", observed=False)
        .agg(
            cases=("case_id", "count"),

            median_cycle_h=("cycle_time_h", "median"),

            night_shift_pct=("is_night", lambda s: 100 * s.mean()),
            compliance_pct=("is_compliance", lambda s: 100 * s.mean()),
            rework_pct=("has_rework", lambda s: 100 * s.mean()),
            appeal_pct=("has_appeal", lambda s: 100 * s.mean()),

        .reindex(GROUP_ORDER)
    )
    print("=== 1. CRUDE GROUP COMPARISON ===")
    print(crude.round(2).to_string())
    crude_diff = crude.loc["Orion", "mean_cycle_h"] - crude.loc["Nova", "mean_cycle_h"]
    print(f"\nCrude cycle-time contrast (Orion - Nova): {crude_diff:+.2f} h\n")

    # 2b. Complexity-stratified comparison
    strat = (
        df.groupby(["complexity", "group"], observed=False)
        .agg(
            cases=("case_id", "count"),
            mean_cycle_h=("cycle_time_h", "mean"),
            median_cycle_h=("cycle_time_h", "median"),
            mean_wait_h=("wait_before_review_h", "mean"),
            mean_service_h=("review_service_h", "mean"),
            rework_pct=("has_rework", lambda s: 100 * s.mean()),
        )
    )
    print("=== 2. COMPLEXITY-STRATIFIED COMPARISON ===")
    print(strat.round(2).to_string())

    print("\nWithin-complexity contrasts (Orion - Nova):")
    for comp in COMPLEXITY_ORDER:
        n_o = int(strat.loc[(comp, "Orion"), "cases"])
        n_n = int(strat.loc[(comp, "Nova"), "cases"])
        d_ct = strat.loc[(comp, "Orion"), "mean_cycle_h"] - strat.loc[(comp, "Nova"), "mean_cycle_h"]
        d_wait = strat.loc[(comp, "Orion"), "mean_wait_h"] - strat.loc[(comp, "Nova"), "mean_wait_h"]
        d_serv = strat.loc[(comp, "Orion"), "mean_service_h"] - strat.loc[(comp, "Nova"), "mean_service_h"]

        print(
            f"  {comp:6s}: cycle={d_ct:+.2f} h | wait={d_wait:+.2f} h ({wait_share:.1f}% of cycle gap) "

    print("\nPositivity / sparse-cell warnings:")
    for (comp, sh), row in overlap.iterrows():
        n_o, n_n = int(row["Orion"]), int(row["Nova"])
        if n_o == 0 or n_n == 0:
            print(
                f"  [WARNING] ZERO OVERLAP in ({comp}, {sh}): Orion={n_o}, Nova={n_n}. "
                "Nonparametric group contrast is not supported in this cell."
            )
        elif n_o < MIN_CELL_N or n_n < MIN_CELL_N:
            print(
                f"  [WARNING] POOR SUPPORT (<{MIN_CELL_N} cases) in ({comp}, {sh}): "
                f"Orion={n_o}, Nova={n_n}."
            )

    # Also inspect shift x arrival_load collinearity
    print("\nShift x Arrival Load counts (all cases):")
    print(pd.crosstab(df["shift"], df["arrival_load"]).to_string())
    if (df.loc[df["shift"] == "Night", "arrival_load"] == "High").all():
        print("  [WARNING] Perfect collinearity: 100% of Night cases have arrival_load='High'.")

    # 5. Process-mining diagnostics with PM4Py (control-flow only; NO fabricated timestamps for performance)
    print("\n=== 5. PM4PY CONTROL-FLOW DIAGNOSTICS (VARIANTS & DFGs) ===")
    print("NOTE: pm4py.parse_event_log_string is used strictly for control-flow ordering,")
    print("      NOT for timestamp-based performance or duration calculations.\n")

    group_variants = {}
    group_dfgs = {}
    group_n = {}

    for grp in GROUP_ORDER:
        sub = df[df["group"] == grp]
        group_n[grp] = len(sub)
        traces = sub["trace"].tolist()
        # Parse trace strings directly with '>' delimiter
        event_log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(traces, sep=">")
        dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(event_log)
        raw_variants = pm4py.get_variants(event_log)

        group_variants[grp] = {format_variant(k): int(v) for k, v in raw_variants.items()}
        group_dfgs[grp] = {edge: int(cnt) for edge, cnt in dfg.items()}
        print(f"{grp} (n={len(sub)}): start={dict(start_acts)}, end={dict(end_acts)}")

    # Compare trace variants side by side
    all_variants = sorted(set(group_variants["Orion"]) | set(group_variants["Nova"]))
    var_rows = []
    for var in all_variants:
        c_o = group_variants["Orion"].get(var, 0)
        c_n = group_variants["Nova"].get(var, 0)
        var_rows.append(
            {
                "variant": var,
                "Orion_n": c_o,
                "Orion_pct": round(100.0 * c_o / group_n["Orion"], 1),
                "Nova_n": c_n,
                "Nova_pct": round(100.0 * c_n / group_n["Nova"], 1),
            }
        )
    var_df = pd.DataFrame(var_rows)
    print("\nTrace variant comparison:")
    print(var_df.to_string(index=False))
    for _, r in var_df.iterrows():
        if r["Orion_n"] < MIN_CELL_N or r["Nova_n"] < MIN_CELL_N:
            print(
                f"  [WARNING] Sparse variant support (<{MIN_CELL_N} cases): "
                f"{r['variant']} (Orion={r['Orion_n']}, Nova={r['Nova_n']})."
            )

    # Compare directly-follows relations side by side
    all_edges = sorted(set(group_dfgs["Orion"]) | set(group_dfgs["Nova"]))
    dfg_rows = []
    for src, dst in all_edges:
        c_o = group_dfgs["Orion"].get((src, dst), 0)
        c_n = group_dfgs["Nova"].get((src, dst), 0)
        dfg_rows.append(
            {
                "edge": f"{src} -> {dst}",
                "Orion_count": c_o,
                "Orion_per_case": round(c_o / group_n["Orion"], 2),
                "Nova_count": c_n,
                "Nova_per_case": round(c_n / group_n["Nova"], 2),
            }
        )
    dfg_df = pd.DataFrame(dfg_rows)
    print("\nDirectly-follows graph (DFG) edge comparison:")
    print(dfg_df.to_string(index=False))


if __name__ == "__main__":

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Right-censoring and survivorship bias from observing only completed cases:** Still-open or abandoned claims may be concentrated in Orion's High-complexity queue, potentially biasing Orion's observed cycle time downward.  
   *Single most valuable addition:* A full longitudinal cohort extract containing **all started claims (completed, still-open, and abandoned) with case start, censor, and closure timestamps**.

2. **Non-identifiability between resource group, shift, and arrival load (positivity failure):** Because Orion handles almost exclusively Night/High-load work and has zero Medium-Day or High-Day cases, observational adjustment cannot separate group effects from shift/load regimes.  
   *Single most valuable design change:* A **stratified randomized routing pilot** assigning a bounded fraction of overlap-eligible cases across Orion and Nova during common shift windows.

3. **Unobserved within-tier case risk and routing-engine overrides:** Coarse 3-tier complexity labels cannot reveal whether harder cases within each tier—or anomalous overflow exceptions—drive assignment to Orion or Nova.  
   *Single most valuable addition:* The **exact pre-routing risk score and routing-engine feature log recorded at `Classify`** (before group assignment).

4. **Queueing congestion, compliance-wait entanglement, and SUTVA interference:** Because 75%–96% of Orion's within-tier advantage lies in `wait_before_review_h`, we cannot predict how Nova's queues would react under higher load or how much delay comes from compliance checks versus review queues.  
   *Single most valuable addition:* **Activity-level start and complete timestamps paired with real-time queue WIP and active reviewer counts** per resource pool.

5. **Unmeasured decision-quality and reviewer-certification trade-offs:** With only 2 noisy 30-day appeals and no reviewer identifiers, we cannot tell whether faster completion sacrifices accuracy or whether any service-time edge reflects individual certification rather than group workflow.  
   *Single most valuable addition:* A **blind post-close quality-audit error score** conducted on a stratified random sample of closed claims across both groups.
