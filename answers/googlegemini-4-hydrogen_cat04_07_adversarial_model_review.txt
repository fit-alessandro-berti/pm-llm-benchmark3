# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

This review evaluates **Model S** and **Model C** for a custom-order dossier process across four distinct operational use cases, because no single fitness/precision pair is appropriate for all purposes:

1. **Exploratory process discovery and bottleneck diagnosis:** Here the primary risk is *blindness*—discarding rare or emerging valid paths before analysts see them. Moderate precision is tolerable if deviations are easy to inspect, provided the model does not hallucinate impossible structural dependencies or erase mandatory controls from view.
2. **Conformance enforcement and compliance auditing:** Here the primary risk is *false negatives* (approving policy-violating cases such as skipped mandatory checks or unexcused rework). High precision against a **curated negative-behavior standard** is mandatory; high training fitness achieved by making mandatory controls optional is disqualifying.
3. **Simulation, capacity planning, and what-if analysis:** Requires structurally valid routing probabilities, explicit rework cycles, and attribute-conditioned branching (e.g., external-order rate driving `Supplier Review`). Over-permissive concurrency or flat variant enumeration produces invalid queueing and resource-utilization forecasts.
4. **Stakeholder communication and process governance:** Requires semantic transparency—business, compliance, and IT owners must read the model and correctly infer *what is forbidden* as well as *what is typical*. Visual neatness that misleads readers about mandatory gates is worse than overt complexity.

**Scope assumptions and evidentiary gaps:**
- Neither team provided random seeds, case-ID split lists, log-filtering scripts, or alignment-cost vectors; therefore, all reported numbers are treated as unverified claims rather than ground truth.
- Per **P3** and **P6**, two decision-relevant case/event attributes—`external/internal` sourcing status and `affected_check` after `Correct Dossier`—are absent from the discovery log. Any purely control-flow model is fundamentally under-determined on those two policies.
- Per **F4**, `Supplier Review` events may be partially unlogged in some historical cases, confounding missing-event noise with legitimately internal orders.

### Strongest Case Against Model S
Model S purchases its high fitness and simplicity by **turning mandatory compliance controls into optional parallel branches** and **deleting rework (`Correct Dossier`) entirely**. In Model S, all four middle activities sit inside `+(X(act, tau), ...)`. Consequently, the model treats mandatory checks (`Credit Check`, `Technical Review`), conditional checks (`Supplier Review`), and gated escalations (`Expedite Review`) as semantically indistinguishable optional side-tasks. Its low training precision (0.584) is not benign exploratory tolerance—it reflects acceptance of blatantly illegal executions, while simultaneously failing to represent legitimate rework (P6/F6).

   `['Register Dossier', 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review', 'Supplier Review', 'Manager Approval', 'Release Order']`
   *(Without the external/internal attribute, Model S treats unnecessary supplier review as identically valid to a required external review.)*
6. **Omission bias (fails P6/F6):** Model S does not include `'Correct Dossier'` or any loop, so valid correction traces like `['Register Dossier', 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review', 'Correct Dossier', 'Technical Review', 'Manager Approval', 'Release Order']` cannot be generated at all and only pass alignment via log-move penalties.

### Intentional Noise Tolerance vs. Accidental Semantic Flaws
- **Intentional / defensible tolerance:** Making `Supplier Review` optional (`X('Supplier Review', tau)`) is a defensible structural accommodation for missing `external/internal` metadata (P3) and partially missing `Supplier Review` logs (F4), and placing `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` in parallel is required by P2.
- **Accidental semantic flaws:** Wrapping `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` in `X(..., tau)` has nothing to do with missing attributes—they are unconditionally mandatory (P2). Placing `Expedite Review` in parallel with them rather than sequentially after them violates explicit ordering policy (P4). Omitting `Correct Dossier` entirely is an incomplete discovery artifact (likely from noise filtering during discovery).

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

### Strongest Case for Model C
Model C enforces several critical control-flow invariants that Model S violates: every single enumerated branch starts with `Register Dossier` (P1), executes both `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` before any approval (P2), restricts `Expedite Review` to occur strictly after both mandatory checks (P4), enforces a single approval before `Release Order` (P5), and refuses to allow repeated checks unless preceded by `Correct Dossier` (P7). In an enforcement setting where *only* historically vetted variants were permitted until manually promoted, Model C exhibits low false-acceptance risk on known invalid patterns (B3, B4, B8) and achieves 0.941 training alignment precision.

### Strongest Case Against Model C
Model C is **not a process model in any generalizing sense—it is a brittle lookup table of 8 frequent training variants** wrapped in a top-level `X(...)`.
- **Branch enumeration and combinatorial fragility:** With 2 check orders × 2 supplier options × 2 expedite options × 2 approval options, there are **16** basic valid non-rework combinations alone (before even considering `ER` before vs. after `SR`, which adds 4 more = **20** non-rework valid traces). Model C enumerates only 7 non-rework paths plus 1 rigid rework path. Any legitimate unseen combination—such as `Technical Review -> Credit Check -> Auto Approval` (B2) or `Technical Review -> Credit Check -> Supplier Review -> Expedite Review -> Auto Approval` (B5)—is rejected, explaining the catastrophic holdout fitness drop from **0.973 to 0.744** and generalization collapse to **0.412**.
- **Duplicate labels and architectural opacity (F3):** Model C's tree contains 48 activity occurrences across 9 unique labels (7 labels duplicated, 39 excess copies; reported as 33 under an undocumented metric). Changing a single business rule around `Credit Check` or `Auto Approval` requires editing up to 9 separate places across branches, destroying ownership boundaries and driving alignment runtime up 24× (0.440 s/case) and simplicity down to 0.281.
- **Defective correction handling (P6, F6):** Model C encodes only one correction variant (`CC -> TR -> CD -> CC -> TR -> MA`), forcing *both* checks to repeat in fixed order, disallowing multiple `Correct Dossier` cycles, and forbidding single-check reworks (B6), `Auto Approval`, `Supplier Review`, or `Expedite Review` on corrected dossiers.
- **Illusory precision:** Model C's 0.941 training precision is an artifact of **memorizing training variants** rather than modeling the underlying partial-order and rework rules. When evaluated on holdout traces combining already-known valid elements (F4), it fails precisely because memorization does not generalize.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

Ranked from **highest to lowest decision impact** on the governance board's choice:

1. **Fitness rewarded by optionalizing mandatory behavior (Rank 1 — Critical):**
   By wrapping `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` in `X(..., tau)`, Model S eliminates model-move penalties whenever a case skips mandatory checks or logs events out of order. A compliance violation is literally *rewarded* with near-1.0 fitness (0.992 train / 0.981 holdout), tricking decision-makers into equating high fitness with policy conformance.
2. **Positive-log-only precision (Rank 2 — Critical):**
   As noted in F5, alignment precision only measures escaping edges relative to *observed positive training traces*. It never tests whether a model blocks specific known-illegal traces (B3, B4, B8). Consequently, Model C's 0.941 precision conflates variant memorization with compliance validity, while Model S's 0.584 understates how dangerous its specific escaping edges are (skipping `CC`/`TR` vs. harmless `SR`/`ER` permutations).
3. **Train/holdout split sensitivity and undocumented preprocessing (Rank 3 — High):**
   Without seeds, split code, or exact case IDs, a random 80/20 split can distort results: if Model S filtered out rare `Correct Dossier` traces during preprocessing while Model C retained them (or vice versa), training/holdout fitness are not even measured on the same event distribution. Temporal drift (e.g., `Auto Approval` rolled out late in the log) would also punish Model C much worse under a chronological split than a random split.
4. **Missing-event and missing-attribute bias (Rank 4 — High):**
   F4 states `Supplier Review` events may be missing in some cases, and P3/P6 state `external/internal` and `affected_check` are absent from the log. Model S gets a "free" fitness boost from missing `Supplier Review` events because `SR` is optional; Model C suffers holdout penalties whenever an external order drops `SR` or shifts `AA`. Meanwhile, *no* metric can verify P3 or P6 compliance from control flow alone.
5. **Generalization rewarded by excessive permissiveness (Rank 5 — High):**
   Token-based generalization (S: 0.907 vs. C: 0.412) rewards models whose transitions and places are heavily exercised per unit size. Because Model S collapses 9 activities into a tiny parallel-XOR block, every trace exercises the same few elements, yielding 0.907 even though the model accepts blatantly illegal traces. Conversely, Model C splits identical behavior across 8 parallel branches, starving each duplicate transition of visits.
6. **Stakeholder-score methodology flaws (Rank 6 — Medium-High):**
   An informal 1–5 comprehension rating (S: 4.4, C: 1.9) measures *aesthetic simplicity*, not *semantic comprehension*. Business users frequently misread `+(X(CC, tau), X(TR, tau))` as "both checks happen in parallel (maybe sometimes noisy)" rather than "both checks can be skipped legally by the model." Without quiz questions on probes like B3 and B4, the 4.4 score induces false confidence.
7. **Duplicated-label effects (Rank 7 — Medium):**
   Model C's undocumented "33" duplicate labels (out of 48 leaf instances) bloat arc-degree simplicity (0.281), fragment token replay counts (hurting generalization), and can cause alignment/automaton precision algorithms to misestimate escaping behavior depending on whether state exploration merges equivalent visible labels or keeps transition identities distinct.
8. **Silent-transition effects (Rank 8 — Medium):**
   Model S has 4 parallel `tau` branches, creating $2^4 = 16$ invisible routing combinations. Silent transitions complicate escaping-edge precision calculations and allow invisible bypasses that mask structural non-conformance.
9. **Alignment-cost sensitivity (Rank 9 — Low-Medium):**
   Standard unit alignment costs (`log_move = 1`, `model_move = 1`) treat skipping `Credit Check` (a major compliance breach) as numerically identical to missing an optional `Supplier Review` or adding a duplicate check. On a 6-event trace, 1 skipped check still yields fitness $\approx 0.83$–$0.86$, hiding severe risk in aggregate averages.
10. **Runtime confounding by model size (Rank 10 — Low-Medium):**
    Model C's 0.440 s/case alignment cost vs. Model S's 0.018 s/case reflects synchronous-product explosion over 48 duplicated transitions in an 8-way XOR, not an inherent cost of enforcing rules P1–P7. A properly factored partial-order model enforces stronger rules at a fraction of Model C's runtime.

# 7. Third Alternative

We propose a **hybrid POWL + Companion Declarative/Data-Aware Policy Model (`model_third`)**. Pure control flow cannot enforce attribute-conditioned rules (P3, P6) without missing-attribute fabrication; therefore, `model_third` enforces every **unconditional** control-flow invariant structurally via a compact **POWL** model, and pairs it with **explicit post-hoc policy checks** that verify attribute-dependent rules when metadata is present and flag unverified cases rather than silently passing them.

### 1. Compact Formal Description
Let `model_third` be the strict partial order (POWL) over blocks:
- `reg = 'Register Dossier'`
- `cc_init = 'Credit Check'`, `tr_init = 'Technical Review'` (mandatory initial checks)
- `sr_opt = X('Supplier Review', tau)` (control-flow optional; gated by companion `is_external` rule)
- `er_opt = X('Expedite Review', tau)`
- `rework_loop = *(tau, ->('Correct Dossier', X('Credit Check', 'Technical Review', +('Credit Check', 'Technical Review'))))`
- `approval = X('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval')`
- `rel = 'Release Order'`

With partial-order precedence edges:
- `reg -> {cc_init, tr_init, sr_opt}`
- `{cc_init, tr_init} -> er_opt`
- `{cc_init, tr_init} -> rework_loop`
- `{sr_opt, er_opt, rework_loop} -> approval -> rel`

### 2. Self-Contained PM4Py Implementation (`model_third`)

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
    OperatorPOWL,
    StrictPartialOrder,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def build_model_third():
    # Mandatory boundary and initial check nodes
    reg = Transition(label="Register Dossier")
    cc_init = Transition(label="Credit Check")
    tr_init = Transition(label="Technical Review")

    # Optional in control flow due to missing external/internal & Expedite conditions
    sr_opt = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [Transition(label="Supplier Review"), SilentTransition()],
    )
    er_opt = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [Transition(label="Expedite Review"), SilentTransition()],
    )

    # Rework loop: 0 or more cycles of Correct Dossier -> {CC | TR | (CC || TR)}
    cd = Transition(label="Correct Dossier")
    cc_recheck = Transition(label="Credit Check")
    tr_recheck = Transition(label="Technical Review")
    both_recheck = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[
            Transition(label="Credit Check"),
            Transition(label="Technical Review"),
        ]
    )  # unordered => parallel (either order)

    recheck_choice = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [cc_recheck, tr_recheck, both_recheck],
    )
    redo_seq = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[cd, recheck_choice])
    redo_seq.order.add_edge(cd, recheck_choice)

    rework_loop = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.LOOP,
        [SilentTransition(), redo_seq],
    )

    # Exactly one approval before release
    approval = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [Transition(label="Manager Approval"), Transition(label="Auto Approval")],
    )
    rel = Transition(label="Release Order")

    # Top-level strict partial order
    po = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[reg, cc_init, tr_init, sr_opt, er_opt, rework_loop, approval, rel]
    )
    po.order.add_edge(reg, cc_init)
    po.order.add_edge(reg, tr_init)
    po.order.add_edge(reg, sr_opt)

    po.order.add_edge(cc_init, er_opt)
    po.order.add_edge(tr_init, er_opt)

    po.order.add_edge(cc_init, rework_loop)
    po.order.add_edge(tr_init, rework_loop)

    po.order.add_edge(sr_opt, approval)
    po.order.add_edge(er_opt, approval)
    po.order.add_edge(rework_loop, approval)
    po.order.add_edge(approval, rel)

    return po


model_third = build_model_third()
net_third, im_third, fm_third = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)
is_sound, diagnostics = pm4py.check_soundness(net_third, im_third, fm_third, print_diagnostics=True)
```

### 3. Companion Declarative Rules and Post-Hoc Policy Checks

```python
DECLARATIVE_POLICY = {
    "P1_boundaries": {
        "init": "Register Dossier",
        "end": "Release Order",
    },
    "P2_mandatory_checks": ["Credit Check", "Technical Review"],
    "P3_supplier_review_gate": {
        "activity": "Supplier Review",
        "required_when": {"is_external": True},
        "forbidden_when": {"is_external": False},
        "on_missing_attribute": "UNVERIFIED_EXTERNAL_STATUS",
    },
    "P4_expedite_precedence": {
        "activity": "Expedite Review",
        "must_follow_all": ["Credit Check", "Technical Review"],
        "must_precede_any": ["Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"],
    },
    "P5_exclusive_approval": ["Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"],
    "P6_correction_recheck": {
        "trigger": "Correct Dossier",
        "allowed_rechecks": {"Credit Check", "Technical Review"},
        "on_missing_affected_checks": "POLICY_DEPENDENT_UNVERIFIED",
    },
    "P7_unexcused_repeat": {
        "monitored": ["Credit Check", "Technical Review"],
        "requires_intervening": "Correct Dossier",
    },
}


def evaluate_posthoc_policy(trace_activities, case_attrs=None, correction_events_attrs=None):
    """
    Companion data/declarative evaluator complementing structural alignment on model_third.
    """
    case_attrs = case_attrs or {}
    correction_events_attrs = correction_events_attrs or []
    findings = []

    # P3: Supplier Review vs external/internal attribute
    has_sr = "Supplier Review" in trace_activities
    is_ext = case_attrs.get("is_external", None)
    if is_ext is True and not has_sr:
        findings.append(("VIOLATION_P3", "External order missing Supplier Review."))
    elif is_ext is False and has_sr:
        findings.append(("VIOLATION_P3", "Internal order executed Supplier Review."))
    elif is_ext is None:
        findings.append(("UNVERIFIED_P3", "Cannot verify Supplier Review necessity; is_external missing."))

    # P6 & P7: Segment trace by Correct Dossier occurrences
    cd_indices = [i for i, a in enumerate(trace_activities) if a == "Correct Dossier"]
    if cd_indices:
        if not correction_events_attrs or len(correction_events_attrs) != len(cd_indices):
            findings.append(
                ("POLICY_DEPENDENT_P6", "Correct Dossier present without affected_check metadata.")
            )
        else:
            # Verify each correction cycle repeated exactly the affected checks
            boundaries = cd_indices + [len(trace_activities)]
            for idx, start in enumerate(cd_indices):
                end = boundaries[idx + 1]
                segment = [
                    a for a in trace_activities[start + 1 : end]
                    if a in {"Credit Check", "Technical Review"}
                ]
                expected = set(correction_events_attrs[idx].get("affected_checks", []))
                if set(segment) != expected or len(segment) != len(expected):
                    findings.append(
                        ("VIOLATION_P6", f"Correction #{idx+1} rechecked {segment}, expected {sorted(expected)}.")
                    )

    # P7 check: repeated mandatory check without intervening Correct Dossier
    seen_since_cd = set()
    for act in trace_activities:
        if act == "Correct Dossier":
            seen_since_cd.clear()
        elif act in {"Credit Check", "Technical Review"}:
            if act in seen_since_cd:
                findings.append(("VIOLATION_P7", f"Repeated {act} without preceding Correct Dossier."))
            seen_since_cd.add(act)

    return findings
```

### 4. Explicit Semantics of `model_third`
- **Mandatory checks (P2):** Both `cc_init` and `tr_init` are non-optional nodes preceding `er_opt`, `rework_loop`, and `approval`. Neither can be skipped, and because there is no edge between them, they may complete in either order or concurrently.
- **Optional `Supplier Review` (P3):** `sr_opt` is `X('Supplier Review', tau)` ordered only between `reg` and `approval`. Thus `Supplier Review` can run before, concurrently with, or after the mandatory checks and before or after `Expedite Review`, without falsely rejecting valid interleavings. Crucially, missing `is_external` data is **not** silently rubber-stamped: `evaluate_posthoc_policy` explicitly flags `UNVERIFIED_P3` until `is_external` is logged.
- **`Expedite Review` ordering (P4):** `er_opt` is optional (`X('Expedite Review', tau)`), strictly follows *both* `cc_init` and `tr_init`, strictly precedes `approval`, and is unordered relative to `sr_opt` (allowing `ER` before or after `SR`).
- **Exactly-one approval (P5):** `approval = X('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval')` strictly precedes `rel` and follows all review/rework nodes.
- **Correction and recheck behavior (P6, P7):** `rework_loop` uses `Operator.LOOP(tau, ->('Correct Dossier', X('Credit Check', 'Technical Review', +('Credit Check', 'Technical Review'))))`. It allows zero or more corrections; every `Correct Dossier` forces at least one recheck (`CC` alone, `TR` alone, or both in either order); and because the only secondary `CC`/`TR` transitions sit inside `redo_seq` after `Correct Dossier`, any repeated mandatory check without `Correct Dossier` (B8) is structurally rejected. Notice `model_third` uses only **13 visible transitions** (only 4 duplicate occurrences vs. Model C's ~33–39), preserving clean modularity.

### 5. Limitations from Missing Attributes and Modeling Boundaries
- Because `is_external` is missing from the log, control flow alone cannot distinguish a missing `Supplier Review` event on an external case (F4) from a legitimate internal case (P3); `evaluate_posthoc_policy` must hold those cases as `UNVERIFIED_P3` until CRM/ERP sourcing flags are joined.
- Because `affected_check` is missing, `model_third` accepts any non-empty subset `{CC, TR}` after `Correct Dossier`, meaning a trace that repeats the *wrong* check (e.g., `TR` instead of `CC`) cannot be caught without logging `affected_check`.
- Two deliberate control-flow boundary choices should be validated with domain owners: (i) `rework_loop` sits after *both* `cc_init` and `tr_init` (so an early mid-flight `Correct Dossier` occurring before `Technical Review` ever starts would incur an alignment penalty), and (ii) `Supplier Review` is not included inside `rework_loop` (so if a dossier correction can also force repeating `Supplier Review`, `recheck_choice` would need to include `Supplier Review`).

# 9. Validation Experiments

Before any model is approved, run the following reproducible validation suite (without fabricating metrics in advance):

1. **Deterministic splits and published preprocessing:** Archive raw log hash, event-filtering scripts, and split indices using both a seeded random case split (`seed=42`) and a **chronological 80/20 split** (to detect policy drift such as `Auto Approval` rollout).
2. **Attribute-stratified holdout tests:** On an audit-annotated sample (joining `is_external` and `affected_checks` from ERP/CRM or manual dossier audit), stratify holdout metrics across `internal vs. external`, `expedited vs. standard`, `no-rework vs. reworked`, and `Manager vs. Auto Approval`.
3. **Curated negative trace suite:** Construct $\ge 30$ policy-forbidden traces covering skipped `CC`/`TR` (B3, A2), premature `ER` (B4), unexcused repeats (B8), missing/double approvals, wrong boundaries, and wrong-check rework (`affected_checks={CC}` but `TR` repeated). Measure **Negative Rejection Rate (NRR)** via both strict fitness (`< 1.0`) and policy-monitor flags.
4. **Missing-event and duplicate-event perturbation tests:** Randomly drop `Supplier Review` (10%, 30%) and `Credit Check` (5%) from valid holdout traces, and inject duplicate `Credit Check` events without `Correct Dossier`, verifying that missing mandatory checks drop fitness sharply while missing `Supplier Review` triggers `UNVERIFIED_P3` rather than silent pass.
5. **Unseen-combination (leave-variant-group-out) tests:** Hold out valid combinations absent from training (e.g., all `{TR->CC, Auto Approval}` or `{ER before SR, SR}` variants) and measure holdout fitness separately on these unseen valid combinations vs. known combinations.
6. **Alignment-cost sensitivity:** Re-run conformance diagnostics under (a) unit costs `(1, 1)` and (b) **risk-weighted costs** (e.g., model move on mandatory `CC`/`TR` = 10, `ER` ordering violation = 8, `SR` = 1) to verify whether high aggregate fitness collapses when compliance skips are appropriately weighted.
7. **Soundness, reachability, and play-out audit:** Run `pm4py.check_soundness`, verify zero deadlocks/livelocks, and sample `pm4py.play_out` traces to confirm no forbidden traces (e.g., B3, B4, B8) are generated.
8. **Disaggregated reporting:** Report training fitness, holdout fitness (random & temporal), alignment precision, token generalization, arc-degree simplicity, runtime, and NRR separately.
9. **Blinded stakeholder comprehension protocol:** Administer a standardized 10-question trace-judgement test (including B2, B3, B4, B6, B8, A1) to compliance, operations, and IT reviewers viewing each diagram *without* narrative coaching, measuring **factual interpretation accuracy** alongside perceived clarity.
10. **Hard veto rejection rule:** A candidate model is **automatically rejected regardless of aggregate fitness/precision/simplicity** if any of the following fail: (i) Petri net is unsound; (ii) Negative Rejection Rate on critical compliance traps (B3, B4, B8, A2) $< 100\%$; (iii) unseen-valid-combination holdout fitness $< 0.95$; or (iv) stakeholder accuracy on mandatory-gate questions $< 85\%$.

```python
import random
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import EventLog


def deterministic_case_split(log, train_ratio=0.8, seed=42):
    case_ids = sorted({trace.attributes["concept:name"] for trace in log})
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    rng.shuffle(case_ids)
    cut = int(len(case_ids) * train_ratio)
    train_ids, test_ids = set(case_ids[:cut]), set(case_ids[cut:])
    train_log = EventLog([t for t in log if t.attributes["concept:name"] in train_ids])
    test_log = EventLog([t for t in log if t.attributes["concept:name"] in test_ids])
    return train_log, test_log


def evaluate_model(model, train_log, holdout_log, negative_log, custom_cost_fn=None):
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    sound, diag = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=False)

    # Disaggregated standard conformance & quality metrics
    train_fit = pm4py.fitness_alignments(train_log, net, im, fm)
    holdout_fit = pm4py.fitness_alignments(holdout_log, net, im, fm)
    train_prec = pm4py.precision_alignments(train_log, net, im, fm)
    gen = pm4py.generalization_tbr(train_log, net, im, fm)
    simp = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm, variant="arc_degree")

    # Negative-trace suite: strict rejection requires alignment fitness < 1.0
    neg_diag = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(negative_log, net, im, fm)
    neg_rejected = sum(1 for d in neg_diag if d["fitness"] < 1.0)
    nrr = neg_rejected / len(neg_diag) if neg_diag else 0.0

    # Play-out sample for manual/automated policy screening
    simulated_log = pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm, parameters={"no_traces": 500})

    # Optional risk-weighted alignment check
    weighted_diag = None
    if custom_cost_fn is not None:
        weighted_diag = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(
            holdout_log, net, im, fm, model_cost_function=custom_cost_fn
        )

    return {
        "sound": sound,
        "soundness_diagnostics": diag,
        "train_fitness": train_fit,
        "holdout_fitness": holdout_fit,
        "train_precision": train_prec,
        "generalization_tbr": gen,
        "simplicity_arc_degree": simp,
        "negative_rejection_rate": nrr,
        "simulated_traces": len(simulated_log),
        "weighted_diagnostics": weighted_diag,
    }
```

# 11. Dissenting View

**Rebuttal (197 words):**
Deploying `model_third` prematurely introduces a dangerous governance illusion: *normative design masquerading as empirical discovery*. Both Model S and Model C were derived from the actual event log, yet `model_third` was hand-crafted largely from stakeholder wishes (P1–P7). In real operations, written policy often lags behind legitimate workarounds—for example, urgent dossiers where `Correct Dossier` occurs before `Technical Review` finishes, or where a correction invalidates `Supplier Review`. Because `model_third` hard-codes rework only *after* both initial checks and excludes `Supplier Review` from rework, it may silently misclassify valid operational exceptions as noncompliant just like Model C. Meanwhile, Model S's advocate (F7) is partly right: in an environment with missing `Supplier Review` logs (F4) and missing attributes (P3, P6), an honest high-level map paired with simple SQL/rule queries for mandatory-check presence (`count(CC)>=1`, `count(TR)>=1`, `ER` timestamps after `CC`/`TR`) could achieve identical compliance enforcement *without* building a custom POWL model containing 4 duplicated check transitions and an unproven UX footprint. Before retiring Model S, governance should prove that `model_third`'s structural loop actually outperforms a simpler exploration model plus lightweight log-level compliance assertions.

**Evidence that would change our decision:**
1. If running `model_third` against the real log shows substantial fitness loss ($> 5\%$) caused specifically by legitimate mid-flight corrections before `Technical Review` finishes or `Supplier Review` rework cycles, we would revise `model_third`'s rework placement before deployment rather than treating those cases as noncompliant.
2. If blinded stakeholder testing shows users score $< 85\%$ reading the POWL `LOOP`/rework notation, while scoring $\ge 90\%$ on a simpler milestone model paired with explicit tabular compliance rules, we would keep the simpler visual diagram for communication and enforce P1–P7 programmatically in the audit layer.
3. If an audited sample proves that many apparent `B3`/`B4` traces in historical logs are actually missing-timestamp / batch-logging artifacts rather than real control bypasses, enforcement should wait for upstream logging fixes rather than flagging false compliance breaches.
