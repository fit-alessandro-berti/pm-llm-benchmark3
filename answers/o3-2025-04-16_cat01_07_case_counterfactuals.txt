## 1. Feasibility model  

```
Register  <  Triage
Triage    <  (Identity Check , Document Check , Originals Receipt?)
(Identity Check & Document Check)  <  Assessment
Originals Receipt  <  Document Check   (only if originals required)
Assessment  <  Decision
Decision   <  Notify
Assessment  <  Compliance Review       (high-risk or high-value only)
Compliance Review  <  Decision
Request-More-Info_i  <  Document Check_(i+1)  <  Assessment_(i+1)
Escalation Advice  <  (Compliance Review | Decision)   (if invoked)
```

```
Declarative / conditional constraints
1. Identity Check and Document Check may overlap when originals not required.
2. High-risk OR high-value  final Compliance Review required and must follow last Assessment.
3. Four-eyes rule: the person who performs the last Assessment may not perform the last Compliance Review.
4. After any Request-More-Info, the loop (Document Check  Assessment) must repeat; Compliance Review must be repeated or explicitly reaffirmed.
5. Resources are single-threaded; service intervals may not overlap for the same resource.
6. Queue time is stochastic; forecasts in the snapshot are means, not guarantees.
```

Timing vs. routing vs. decision policy  
• K-104 and K-411 change only timing (earlier escalation or parallel task creation).  
• K-219 changes routing (different document checker).  
• K-337 changes the admissible decision policy (suppresses an information-request loop).

## 3. Counterfactual derivations  

### K-104  
Factual mechanism:  
Compliance is triggered late; C1 receives a cold file and waits behind an 8-hour queue.

Minimal intervention:  
At 08:55 Mon Triage emails a slim escalation packet to S1 and books C1 for Wed 09:00-12:00.

Temporal propagation:  
• S1 reads 12:00-14:00 Mon while identity is still pending – no queue impact.  
• C1 skims 08:00-10:00 Tue; marks pages for fast return. This does not satisfy the “after Assessment” rule, so the final slot is still needed.  
• Document Check ends 05:40 Tue as before; Assessment pulls the flagged sanctions items and finishes 18:30 (40 min gain).  
• Reserved slot lets C1 start 09:00 Wed instead of waiting until 13:10; pre-reading cuts service from 4.5 h to 2.5 h.  
• Decision moves forward to 12:15 Wed; Notify 12:25.  

Process-feasibility check:  
All precedence rules kept; four-eyes OK (A1 vs C1). C1 and S1 do not overlap other booked work per snapshot.

Best/base/worst case:  
Best 9 h (C1 only 2 h), base 6 h, worst 3 h (if C1 must re-read).  

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:  
1. C1 emergency on Tue consumes the reserved slot.  
2. S1 unavailable Monday – no prep benefit.  

### K-219  
Factual mechanism:  
Routing to specialist D1 avoids handwriting risk but creates a 16-hour wait.

Minimal intervention:  
Change router rule: “If D2 backlog 6 h and document type = handwritten standard, assign D2.”

Temporal propagation:  
• Originals land 17:30 Mon. D2 backlog 5 h, so start 22:00 Mon.  
• D2 service 6 h  completes 04:00 Tue, 9.5 h earlier than factual.  
• Assessment queue snapshot 09:00 Tue has A2 backlog 6 h; the earlier readiness moves the case ahead by about 4 h, so Assessment now 16:00-18:30 Tue.  
• Manager is still available 08:30 Wed, saving 0-3 h more.

Process-feasibility check:  
All constraints obeyed. Originals present, four-eyes irrelevant.

Best/base/worst case:  
Best 5 h if no rework and A2 available. Worst +20 h if D2 requests clarification and supplier takes 24 h.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:  
Observed rework frequency >40 % or D2 backlog >8 h.

### K-337  
Factual mechanism:  
Second RMI crosses a weekend; 54 h of calendar delay, 12 h of staff effort.

Minimal intervention:  
Governance rule: “High-risk second RMI requires risk-committee approval—default proceed with conditions.”

Temporal propagation:  
• Assessment 2 ends 10:30 Fri.  
• No RMI-2; file sent to C1 queue (5 h). C1 starts 14:00 Fri, ends 18:00.  
• Manager M1 unavailable after 18:00 Fri; signs 08:30-09:00 Mon.  
• Notify 09:00-09:10 Mon.

Process-feasibility check:  
Still complies with rules because at least one compliance review follows the last assessment. Four-eyes preserved (A1 vs C1). Decision uses incomplete data but notes conditions.

Best/base/worst case:  
Best 56 h (if M1 signs Fri 18:30); base 44 h; worst 36 h (Mon morning sign-off).

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:  
Regulator later deems conditions inadequate, or loss event materialises.

### K-411  
Factual mechanism:  
Legacy lock prevents parallel verification; Document task not created until Identity ends.

Minimal intervention:  
Triage macro creates both tasks; controlled override field `lock_override=Y`, audit log captures start-end in real time.

Temporal propagation:  
• Identity assigned 09:15, starts 11:30 as before.  
• D2 picks document task at 10:00 Tue (backlog 4 h), works 10:00-14:00.  
• Both verifications complete by 14:00-Tue.  
• Assessment queue peak yields slot Wed 08:00-10:30 (2 h earlier than factual).  
• Decision 13:00-13:20 Wed, Notify 13:20-13:30.

Process-feasibility check:  
Concurrency allowed (no originals). Audit log contains true timestamps.

Best/base/worst case:  
Best 7 h (if A2 slot 07:00); base 4 h; worst 3 h (if earlier completion only lengthens wait in assessment queue).

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:  
Inaccurate logging causes audit failure; D2 overload pushes start after 13:00.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis  

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early escalation + C1 booking (K-104) | +2 h S1, +2-3 h early C1, 1 h late C1 | Compliance (selected slot) | Compliance (other slots) | other high-risk files | ambiguous | reservation creates blocking when C1 is single-server |
| D2 substitution (K-219) | +4-7 h D2, 4-6 h D1 | D1 backlog | D2 backlog | standard digital cases | likely neutral | rework probability is convex in handwriting difficulty |
| Suppress 2nd RMI (K-337) | 12 h D1, 8 h A1, 8 h C1 future | D1 & A1 | Appeal/legal follow-up queue | downstream enforcement team | uncertain | risk costs are lumpy (rare but high) |
| Parallel verification (K-411) | +0 h work, but higher concurrency at I2 & D2 | none systemic | Assessment | medium-risk routine cases | slightly negative | queue spill-back when both verifications feed same bottleneck |

100-case weekly rollout (summary)  
Early escalation would reserve 300 h of C1 capacity up-front. Because C1 has only 40 h/week available, the reservation queue would explode, forcing renegotiation and actually **lengthening** average compliance wait unless a release-when-ready algorithm is added.  
Shifting 100 handwritten jobs from D1 to D2 would move 500-600 h of load. D2 becomes the new bottleneck and its longer service times add 50-150 h of net work. D1 under-utilisation partially compensates only if routing stays flexible.  
Suppressing second rework loops removes 1 000 h of document and assessment effort, but throws 10-15 marginal cases into conditional approvals or rejections. The legal/enforcement function absorbs 200-300 h and weekend effects disappear for those files, freeing Monday queues.  
Forced concurrency creates 100 additional overlapping identity/document service windows but does not increase total effort. Assessment inflow timing shifts left by 0.5 day, adding 50 h to the Tuesday-Wednesday assessment peak while Thursday slack appears. Without capacity smoothing, A-queue length oscillates.  
The non-linear pieces are: (1) single-server C1 saturation, (2) rework feedback loops which may either vanish or explode, (3) assessment “accordion” effect when many cases arrive a few hours earlier than before, and (4) weekend calendar gaps whose removal yields disproportional gains.

## 7. Final synthesis  
Parallel verification (K-411) emerges as the most robust improvement: it shaves a few hours without consuming scarce specialist capacity and is easily rolled back if audit-logging problems arise. Early escalation with slot reservation (K-104) is promising but must be throttled; a pilot can reveal whether pre-reading truly speeds final review or merely displaces the queue. By contrast, suppressing the second rework loop (K-337) is the most dangerous: while it delivers the biggest time saving, it deliberately lowers evidential completeness, shifting risk to appeals and potential losses—costs that may surface months later and dwarf the saved effort. The D2 substitution (K-219) shows that a seemingly benign routing tweak can backfire; in system-wide use it would flip the document bottleneck from D1 to D2 and may even lengthen average times if rework is common.  

The local counterfactual for K-104 is the least transportable: what works when only one file reserves a C1 slot will collapse under a hundred similar reservations, saturating the single compliance reviewer. The most valuable additional data would be: (1) per-case C1 touch-time decomposed into duplicate reading vs. new analysis; (2) real error-induced rework rates by document checker and document type; (3) minute-level queue length traces to quantify how early task creation affects subsequent bottlenecks; and (4) appeal incidence linked to evidence gaps. Such data would allow calibrated simulations before broad rollout.

## 8. Assumption audit  

1. structural – A single certified reviewer (C1) is the only resource allowed to perform final compliance review.  
2. temporal – Resource calendars follow a 08:00-20:00 window with no overtime unless shown in factual log.  
3. resource – The snapshot queue forecasts are mean values; we assume coefficient of variation 0.6 for wait time.  
4. behavioral – D2 has a 25 % probability of requesting clarification on handwritten documents.  
5. quality – Early compliance pre-reading reduces final review time by 1-3 h without increasing error probability.  
6. interference – Reserving C1 time blocks other cases rather than triggering dynamic rescheduling.  
7. temporal – Weekend delays affect only external suppliers and managerial sign-off, not internal reviewers.  
8. structural – Lock-override macro correctly records true start/finish timestamps and does not silently back-date events.
