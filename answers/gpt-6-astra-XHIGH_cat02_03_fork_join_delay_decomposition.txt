# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

All durations below are in minutes. For case \(c\), let \(a_c\) be arrival, \(r_c\) fork release, \(s_{ci}\) and \(e_{ci}\) branch \(i\)'s start and end, and \(f_c\) and \(g_c\) final start and end, where \(i\in\{A,B,C\}\).

**Interpretive assumptions.** The supplied rule is an AND-join: all three branches are mandatory, independently eligible from \(r_c\), and completed once. No additional prerequisite is required between their completion and F. Calling start-to-end time “active service” assumes uninterrupted work; the directly observed quantity is an interval duration. Calling the other intervals “queueing” assumes they do not contain an unlogged prerequisite, hold, or release policy. The arithmetic remains valid if these operational interpretations do not.

| Component | Definition | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Upstream elapsed | \(U_c=r_c-a_c\) | Preparation plus any upstream waiting; its internal split is unknown. |
| Branch queue interval | \(Q_{ci}=s_{ci}-r_c\) | Time from eligibility to branch start. |
| Branch service interval | \(P_{ci}=e_{ci}-s_{ci}\) | Observed start-to-end duration. |
| Branch completion offset | \(X_{ci}=Q_{ci}+P_{ci}=e_{ci}-r_c\) | Queue plus service, measured from the common fork. |
| Join ready | \(J_c=\max_i e_{ci}\) | Derived readiness milestone. |
| Fork-join elapsed | \(T_c=J_c-r_c=\max_i X_{ci}\) | One elapsed interval, despite three branches. |
| Branch synchronization wait | \(W_{ci}=J_c-e_{ci}=T_c-X_{ci}\) | Completed branch waiting for the remaining branches. |
| Downstream interval | \(D_c=f_c-J_c\) | Queueing before F under the stated interpretation. |
| Final service | \(P_{cF}=g_c-f_c\) | Observed F duration. |
| Total flow time | \(L_c=g_c-a_c=U_c+T_c+D_c+P_{cF}\) | Elapsed time for the case. |

The critical-branch set is \(\mathcal K_c=\{i:e_{ci}=J_c\}\). Keep all tied branches. If a single label is needed, use the first in the fixed order A, B, C; this convention does not imply sole causal responsibility.

For **each** branch,

\[
Q_{ci}+P_{ci}+W_{ci}=T_c.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\sum_i(Q_{ci}+P_{ci}+W_{ci})=3T_c,
\qquad
\sum_i W_{ci}=3T_c-\sum_i X_{ci}.
\]

The branch timelines overlap. For example, in F02, A waits from 09:15 to 09:25 while C also waits throughout those same ten minutes. Summing their waits counts that elapsed interval twice. Synchronization waits can legitimately be reported as **branch-minutes of completed work awaiting the join**, but cannot be added to the already complete fork-join elapsed time. They also do not establish that branch resources remain occupied.

An additive critical-path representation is obtained by selecting \(i_c^*\in\mathcal K_c\):

\[
L_c=U_c+Q_{ci_c^*}+P_{ci_c^*}+D_c+P_{cF}.
\]

There is no extra synchronization term on that path. All branch-specific queues, services, and waits should remain available as diagnostics alongside this elapsed-time decomposition.

# 3. Aggregate critical-path analysis

**B most strongly controls observed join readiness and is the strongest branch improvement target.** A has the longest service interval but frequently finishes while B is still queued or working. Whether earlier readiness becomes earlier total completion depends on F's scheduling response.

In the table, summaries separated by slashes are **mean / median / P90 / maximum**, except completion offsets, which show **mean / P90 / maximum**. P90 uses linear interpolation over these twelve observations; it is a descriptive sample statistic, not a population guarantee.

| Branch | Service summary | Queue summary | Fork-to-completion offset | Last-completing frequency | Mean synchronization | Mean isolated removable join delay | Influence assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| A | 50 / 50 / 50 / 50 | 0 / 0 / 0 / 0 | 50 / 50 / 50 | 2/12 = 16.7% | 11.00 | 1.25 | Second by isolated mean gain; close to C. Sets a universal 50-minute stage floor while unchanged. |
| B | 37.50 / 37.50 / 45 / 45 | 19.17 / 20 / 30 / 30 | 56.67 / 74 / 75 | 8/12 = 66.7% | 4.33 | 10.00 | First by frequency, mean removable delay, and observed tail. |
| C | 20.17 / 20 / 21.8 / 30 | 10.42 / 5 / 24 / 35 | 30.58 / 53 / 57 | 2/12 = 16.7% | 30.42 | 1.00 | Tied with A in frequency; episodic administrative delays matter. |

There are no observed critical-branch ties. A and C have the same critical frequency, and their isolated mean opportunities differ by only 0.25 minutes per case. Ranking A definitively above C on investment value would require intervention costs and broader evidence.

To make “contribution” precise, define a branch's exclusive completion margin:

\[
M_{ci}=\left[e_{ci}-\max_{j\ne i}e_{cj}\right]_+.
\]

Holding the other branch ends fixed, advancing branch \(i\)'s end by \(\delta_{ci}\ge0\) advances readiness by

\[
\Delta J_{ci}=\min(\delta_{ci},M_{ci}).
\]

A noncritical or tied branch has zero isolated margin. The mean-margin column is the maximum mean readiness gain from accelerating that branch alone sufficiently; it is not an additive allocation of all elapsed time. Margins are:

- **A:** 5 minutes in F01 and 10 in F11; total 15, mean 1.25, maximum 10.
- **B:** 10, 15, 10, 15, 25, 15, 5, and 25 in F02, F03, F05, F06, F07, F09, F10, and F12; total 120, mean 10, maximum 25.
- **C:** 7 in F04 and 5 in F08; total 12, mean 1, maximum 7.

The maximum stage duration is 75 minutes, reached in F07 and F12. B accounts for the final 25 minutes beyond the runner-up in both. These are also the two largest total flow times, 130 and 125. Stage P90 is 74 and total-flow P90 is 124.5. B's queue-plus-service exposure thus matters in both the mean and the observed tail. C controls F04 and F08 because its completion offsets are 57 and 55, exceeding A's 50-minute floor.

A mean service ranking would be A > B > C; the completion-influence ranking is **B > A approximately C**, with a small A advantage under the margin metric. Even mean completion offsets do not replace case-level maxima: \(\operatorname{mean}(T)=61\), whereas \(\max_i\operatorname{mean}(X_i)=56.67\).

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

**Assumptions for isolated branch calculations.** Arrival, fork release, other branch intervals, and final-service duration stay fixed. A's acceleration reduces its service interval from 50 to 40 minutes at the same start. B's acceleration halves its queue interval, moving its start and end earlier without changing its service. These are interventions on recorded intervals, not demonstrated feasible resource plans. Half-minute results are model outputs, not newly observed timestamp precision.

First calculate readiness gains exactly under those assumptions. For a simple total-flow projection, also retain each case's observed downstream lag: \(f'_c=J'_c+D_c\). Then \(\Delta L_c=\Delta J_c\). The third counterfactual sets \(D'_c=0\), keeping the original joins and final service. It is an ideal downstream intervention, not an assertion that buying one resource removes every hold.

| F01 | 5 | 0 | 8 |
| F02 | 0 | 10 | 15 |

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| Downstream interval eliminated | 100.58 | 114.0 | 115 | 12 |

**1. A service −20%.** This saves 120 branch-service minutes across the cohort, assuming the intervals represent active service, but advances the join by only 15 case-minutes. F01 gains five minutes before B becomes critical at 08:55; F11 gains ten before A and B tie at 11:20. All ten other cases have zero readiness gain because B or C still finishes last. The observed flow-time tail does not improve under unchanged downstream lags. Secondary benefits could include released assay capacity for other cases, but A has no observed queue here. A generally waits longer for synchronization when it finishes earlier, and reduced service effort need not translate into reduced case elapsed time.

**2. B queue −50%.** This removes 115 branch-queue minutes and advances joins by 92.5 case-minutes. F01, F04, F08, and F11 have zero direct gain because A or C remains critical. Gains are capped by the next branch: F05 saves ten stage minutes despite a 12.5-minute B acceleration, and F10 saves five despite a ten-minute acceleration. F02 and F06 end with A–B ties. F07 and F12 each gain 15 minutes and still have B critical. F09's modeled join becomes 11:02:30.

After this intervention, A is uniquely critical in four cases and shares two ties; B is uniquely critical in four and shares those same two ties; C remains critical in two. Thus the next improvement target changes. Faster B completion reduces synchronization waiting for A and C where readiness moves earlier. It can increase B's own synchronization waiting once B overtakes another branch. Eliminating B's queue entirely would gain at most 120 join case-minutes with A and C fixed, so the half-queue intervention already captures 92.5/120 = 77.1% of that isolated opportunity. Its practical feasibility depends on why B waits and whether changing priorities merely transfers delay to other work.

**3. Reduce downstream waiting; evaluate dispatch before extra capacity.** Eliminating the recorded downstream interval directly saves 8–20 minutes in every case. No case has zero direct gain under this ideal intervention, since every recorded downstream interval is positive. Branch criticality and branch synchronization remain unchanged.

The data does not demonstrate that all 176 downstream minutes were caused by two busy final resources. For example, F03 is ready at 09:45; the supplied F1 schedule is empty from 09:28 to 10:00, yet F03 starts at 10:00. This could reflect other work, resource unavailability, eligibility, or a release rule.

A scheduling sensitivity analysis assumes **only these twelve jobs, two identical pooled resources available throughout, 20-minute nonpreemptive final service, no setups, and work-conserving FCFS dispatch**. Jobs are ordered by join time, with case ID breaking ties; the earliest available resource is chosen, with resource ID breaking ties. Resource assignments may change. The replay starts every case at its join except F11, which waits five minutes until 11:35. Consequently:

- Better dispatch with the same two resources saves **171 case-minutes**, mean **14.25**, relative to the observed final completions. Mean flow becomes **101.00**, P90 **114**, and maximum **115**.
- Three resources under the same policy produce zero waiting. The third resource's **incremental** benefit over the two-resource replay is only **five minutes in F11**, or **0.4167 per case**. The other eleven cases gain zero from that marginal capacity addition.
- If each resource is always available and each job always takes twenty minutes, nominal F capacity is six jobs/hour with two resources and nine with three. Those are capacity calculations, not observed throughput forecasts. Fork releases in this cohort are fifteen minutes apart.

The first-to-last cohort window from earliest join, 09:00, to last observed final completion, 12:40, contains 440 available resource-minutes under an always-available two-resource assumption. The log records 240 busy minutes, or **54.5% of that window**. This is an observed-work fraction under a chosen denominator, not established real utilization: missing work and calendars could change it substantially.

**How F can mask or amplify branch gains.** Keeping all observed final starts fixed is another feasible timing response to earlier joins. Under that response, both branch interventions deliver **zero total-flow gain**: earlier readiness becomes additional downstream waiting. Conversely, retaining downstream lag carries the join gain through, but does not enforce capacity. In the A projection, F11 would start at 11:40 while the unchanged F09 and F10 intervals already occupy both final resources. Its full ten-minute gain is therefore not a feasible two-resource schedule with those other starts fixed.

For A20, F11 becomes ready ten minutes earlier but still starts at 11:35: its replay queue grows from five to fifteen minutes, absorbing the whole readiness gain. For B50, earlier preceding completions remove F11's five-minute replay queue even though F11's own join does not advance. This secondary benefit makes B's total replay gain exceed its direct readiness gain.

These are explicit alternative models, not identified causal effects or universal bounds. Resequencing, priorities, shared branch resources, or hidden work could redistribute benefits. In particular, do not add savings calculated against different baselines. The code also replays the joint B50-plus-dispatch scenario directly: under the stated closed-cohort assumptions it saves 268.5 case-minutes relative to the observed schedule, yielding mean flow 92.875 and maximum 100.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

**Redesign 1: reduce B's eligibility-to-start delay through measured dispatch and availability improvements.** Begin with the cause of B's 20–30-minute waits: machine availability, operator coverage, setups, release rules, and competing work. Pilot a policy that reduces avoidable delay without shortening required stress-test service. This is the strongest branch intervention supported by the observed completion margins.

- **Throughput:** Earlier starts can improve throughput if usable B capacity is currently idle or disrupted. Reprioritizing work alone may only redistribute delay. Halving queue time does not itself increase physical test capacity or prove a sustainable throughput increase.
- **Flow-time tail:** The isolated projection reduces maximum flow from 130 to 115 and P90 from 124.5 to 114.5, conditional on downstream response. The largest direct gains include F07 and F12.
- **Utilization and new bottlenecks:** B service demand remains 450 minutes across these cases. Utilization rises only if the redesign fills idle capacity or enables additional output; it cannot be inferred from shorter queues alone. As B improves, A's 50-minute service becomes more influential, while C's delayed reviews and F's availability can become limiting.
- **Synchronization:** A and C generally spend less time waiting for B in cases where the join advances. B may instead finish early and wait for A or C; reducing every synchronization wait is neither possible nor the optimization objective.
- **Pilot and measurement:** Record B's resource IDs, availability and setup intervals, actual eligibility, queue-entry reasons, priorities, and all competing cases. Measure join times, final completion times, queue distributions, service quality, and any displaced waiting. Compare comparable workloads across multiple days; estimate throughput and tail effects at the system level.

**Redesign 2: dispatch ready cases promptly across F1/F2, adding capacity only if measured availability requires it.** Expose an explicit all-branches-complete readiness signal and pool eligible work across final resources where operational rules allow. Reconcile apparent idle periods with calendars and unlogged work before purchasing another station.

- **Throughput:** Work-conserving dispatch can use existing capacity better. The nominal two-resource capacity already exceeds the observed fork-release cadence under the simple service assumptions, so a third resource is not justified by this cohort alone. Actual demand bursts, other workload, downtime, and eligibility may change the decision.
- **Flow-time tail:** The two-resource replay projects a maximum of 115 and P90 of 114, with a 14.25-minute mean reduction versus observed. These results depend on the simplified workload and calendar assumptions and should be checked with operational replay or a controlled pilot.
- **Utilization and new bottlenecks:** Better dispatch should reduce avoidable idle time while ready work waits. Extra capacity without more demand can lower utilization; holding the same 220-minute window and observed workload fixed, three resources would have only 36.4% recorded occupancy. Prompt F service may expose B, A, or C as the remaining constraint, and a successful B redesign may change F's arrival bursts.
- **Synchronization:** This intervention does not change branch-to-join synchronization when branch timings stay fixed. It targets waiting after readiness. Keep these measures separate so a downstream improvement is not incorrectly credited as reduced branch synchronization.
- **Pilot and measurement:** Log join-ready events, dispatch decisions, eligibility restrictions, staffing calendars, setups, other jobs, and reasons for holds. Test whether a ready case could actually use an apparently idle resource. Compare dispatch-only and additional-capacity schedules on the full workload; monitor quality, priority fairness, overtime, waiting tails, and completed cases per staffed hour.

A blanket A speedup is a lower-priority flow-time intervention for this sample, although it could be valuable for assay capacity or cost. The F04/F08 administrative delays also warrant targeted investigation: their isolated join margins are seven and five minutes, so a small inexpensive C improvement could be worthwhile despite its lower aggregate influence.

# 6. Reproducible Python

The following self-contained script requires pandas and PM4Py. It embeds the supplied data, checks the elapsed-time identities and replay feasibility, retains all critical ties, prints the counterfactuals with their comparison baselines, and creates 48 interval-event rows. PM4Py's interval-based mean service calculation reproduces A = 50, B = 37.5, C = 20.1667, and F = 20 minutes; fork-join synchronization is calculated separately in pandas.

```python
from io import StringIO
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# All input is embedded; no files, network access, or process discovery needed.
RAW = '''case arrival fork_release A_start A_end B_start B_end C_start C_end final_start final_end final_resource
F01 07:55 08:10 08:10 09:00 08:15 08:55 08:20 08:38 09:08 09:28 F1
F02 08:05 08:25 08:25 09:15 08:45 09:25 08:30 08:50 09:40 10:00 F2
F03 08:20 08:40 08:40 09:30 09:00 09:45 08:45 09:03 10:00 10:20 F1
F04 08:35 08:55 08:55 09:45 09:05 09:35 09:30 09:52 10:10 10:30 F2
F05 08:50 09:10 09:10 10:00 09:35 10:10 09:15 09:35 10:20 10:40 F1
F06 09:05 09:25 09:25 10:15 09:55 10:30 09:40 10:00 10:45 11:05 F2
F07 09:20 09:40 09:40 10:30 10:10 10:55 09:45 10:03 11:10 11:30 F1
F08 09:35 09:55 09:55 10:45 10:00 10:30 10:20 10:50 11:05 11:25 F2
F09 09:50 10:10 10:10 11:00 10:35 11:15 10:15 10:35 11:30 11:50 F1
F10 10:05 10:25 10:25 11:15 10:45 11:20 10:30 10:50 11:40 12:00 F2
F11 10:20 10:40 10:40 11:30 10:50 11:20 10:45 11:03 11:50 12:10 F1
F12 10:35 10:55 10:55 11:45 11:25 12:10 11:00 11:18 12:20 12:40 F2
'''

branches = ("A", "B", "C")  # Stable order for all critical-branch ties.
df = pd.read_csv(StringIO(RAW), sep=r"\s+").set_index("case")
time_cols = ["arrival", "fork_release", "final_start", "final_end"]
time_cols += [f"{b}_{x}" for b in branches for x in ("start", "end")]
for col in time_cols:
    df[col] = pd.to_datetime("2026-05-19 " + df[col],
                             format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M")
minute = pd.Timedelta(minutes=1)

def minutes(x):
    return x / minute

def critical_sets(finish):
    # Retain every maximizer. The first member is only a reporting convention.
    return finish.apply(
        lambda row: tuple(b for b in branches if row[b] == row.max()), axis=1
    )

assert (df.fork_release >= df.arrival).all()
for b in branches:
    assert (df[f"{b}_start"] >= df.fork_release).all()
    assert (df[f"{b}_end"] >= df[f"{b}_start"]).all()
    df[f"{b}_queue"] = minutes(df[f"{b}_start"] - df.fork_release)
    # Observed interval duration; equals active service only without interruptions.
    df[f"{b}_service"] = minutes(df[f"{b}_end"] - df[f"{b}_start"])
    df[f"{b}_elapsed"] = minutes(df[f"{b}_end"] - df.fork_release)

finish = pd.DataFrame({b: df[f"{b}_end"] for b in branches})
# Fork release, max-based join, critical sets and synchronization waits are
# custom pandas calculations. A completion-ordered directly-follows graph
# can put overlapping parallel branches in a false sequential order. Its edge
# performance does not itself recover fork-join synchronization waiting.
df["join_ready"] = finish.max(axis=1)
critical = critical_sets(finish)
df["critical_branch"] = critical.map(lambda xs: "+".join(xs))
df["critical_primary"] = critical.map(lambda xs: xs[0])
df["upstream_elapsed"] = minutes(df.fork_release - df.arrival)
df["fork_join_elapsed"] = minutes(df.join_ready - df.fork_release)
df["downstream_queue"] = minutes(df.final_start - df.join_ready)
df["final_service"] = minutes(df.final_end - df.final_start)
df["total_flow_time"] = minutes(df.final_end - df.arrival)
assert (df.downstream_queue >= 0).all()
assert (df.final_service >= 0).all()
for b in branches:
    df[f"{b}_sync"] = minutes(df.join_ready - finish[b])
    other_end = finish.drop(columns=b).max(axis=1)
    df[f"{b}_margin"] = minutes(finish[b] - other_end).clip(lower=0)
    residual = (df[f"{b}_queue"] + df[f"{b}_service"]
                + df[f"{b}_sync"] - df.fork_join_elapsed)
    assert residual.abs().max() < 1e-9
components = ["upstream_elapsed", "fork_join_elapsed",
              "downstream_queue", "final_service"]
assert (df[components].sum(axis=1) - df.total_flow_time).abs().max() < 1e-9
all_branch_cols = [f"{b}_{x}" for b in branches
                   for x in ("queue", "service", "sync")]
assert (df[all_branch_cols].sum(axis=1)
        - 3 * df.fork_join_elapsed).abs().max() < 1e-9

branch_rows = []
for b in branches:
    row = {"branch": b}
    for metric in ("service", "queue", "elapsed"):
        s = df[f"{b}_{metric}"]
        row.update({f"{metric}_mean": s.mean(),
                    f"{metric}_median": s.median(),
                    f"{metric}_p90": s.quantile(.9),
                    f"{metric}_max": s.max()})
    row["critical_n"] = critical.map(lambda xs: b in xs).sum()
    # Inclusion frequencies can sum above n when there are ties.
    row["critical_fractional_n"] = critical.map(
        lambda xs: (1 / len(xs)) if b in xs else 0
    ).sum()
    row["sync_mean"] = df[f"{b}_sync"].mean()
    row["margin_mean"] = df[f"{b}_margin"].mean()
    row["margin_max"] = df[f"{b}_margin"].max()
    branch_rows.append(row)
branch_summary = pd.DataFrame(branch_rows).set_index("branch")

# Isolated interventions: all other branch starts and durations stay fixed.
finish_a = finish.copy()

finish_b = finish.copy()

    .5 * df.B_queue + df.B_service, unit="min"

         "D0": df.join_ready.copy()}
zero = pd.Series(0.0, index=df.index)

cf_rows = []
for name, ready in joins.items():
    # A20/B50 carry join gains through using unchanged observed downstream lag.
    # These are arithmetic projections, not resource-feasible schedules.
    # D0 is an ideal no-downstream-wait scenario, not a guaranteed capacity effect.
    lag = zero if name == "D0" else df.downstream_queue
    flow = (df.upstream_elapsed + minutes(ready - df.fork_release)
            + lag + df.final_service)
    saving = df.total_flow_time - flow
    per_case[f"{name}_join_gain"] = minutes(df.join_ready - ready)
    per_case[f"{name}_flow_gain"] = saving
    per_case[f"{name}_flow"] = flow
    cf_rows.append({"scenario": name, "total_gain": saving.sum(),
                    "mean_gain": saving.mean(),
                    "positive_cases": int((saving > 0).sum()),
                    "zero_cases": int((saving == 0).sum()),
                    "mean_flow": flow.mean(), "p90_flow": flow.quantile(.9),
                    "max_flow": flow.max()})
cf_summary = pd.DataFrame(cf_rows).set_index("scenario")

# Closed-cohort FCFS replay: pooled identical resources, initially available,
# no other jobs, setups, calendars, eligibility rules, or release restrictions.
# Intervals are half-open; a resource is reusable at its completion timestamp.
def replay(ready, resource_count=2):
    available = [df.fork_release.min()] * resource_count
    jobs = pd.DataFrame({"ready": ready, "duration": df.final_service})
    jobs["_case_order"] = jobs.index
    jobs = jobs.sort_values(["ready", "_case_order"], kind="stable")
    rows = []
    for case, job in jobs.iterrows():
        k = min(range(resource_count), key=lambda x: (available[x], x))
        start = max(job["ready"], available[k])
        end = start + pd.Timedelta(minutes=float(job["duration"]))
        available[k] = end
        rows.append({"case": case, "start": start, "end": end,
                     "resource": f"F{k + 1}"})
    result = pd.DataFrame(rows).set_index("case").reindex(df.index)
    assert (result.start >= ready).all()
    for _, group in result.groupby("resource"):
        group = group.sort_values("start")
        assert (group.start.iloc[1:].reset_index(drop=True)
                >= group.end.iloc[:-1].reset_index(drop=True)).all()
    return result

base2 = replay(df.join_ready, 2)
base3 = replay(df.join_ready, 3)
replays = {"F2_dispatch": base2, "F3_dispatch": base3,
           "A20_plus_F2_dispatch": replay(joins["A20"], 2),
           "B50_plus_F2_dispatch": replay(joins["B50"], 2)}

replay_gains["F3_vs_observed"] = minutes(df.final_end - base3.end)

replay_gains["B50_vs_same_F2_policy"] = minutes(

for name, schedule in replays.items():
    flow = minutes(schedule.end - df.arrival)

    replay_rows.append({"scenario": name, "gain_vs_observed": gains.sum(),
                        "mean_gain_vs_observed": gains.mean(),
                        "mean_flow": flow.mean(), "p90_flow": flow.quantile(.9),
                        "max_flow": flow.max()})
```

# 7. Limits of inference

- **Timing identities versus causality.** Join readiness follows from the stated AND-join rule, but the log does not prove what causes branch starts or final starts. Criticality identifies which observed end sets readiness; it does not identify a capacity bottleneck, intervention cost, or causal return on investment. Queue reductions were assumed, not estimated from a staffing or machine model.
- **Meaning of waiting.** Arrival-to-fork time includes upstream preparation and any waiting. Fork-to-branch-start time may include eligibility constraints beyond simple queueing. Join-to-final-start time could represent a queue, batch release, authorization, transport, scheduled appointment, or unavailable resource. The decomposition cannot distinguish these mechanisms without additional events and rules.
- **Branch resources and capacity.** Branch resource identities, staffing, machine counts, calendars, and other workloads are absent. Overlapping intervals do not reveal true capacity without knowing whether work is exclusive, automated, batched, or shared. A's zero queue in these twelve cases does not prove unlimited capacity; B's positive queue does not prove machine saturation.
- **Active work and preemption.** Start/end pairs measure elapsed service intervals. They do not identify pauses, preemption, unattended time, interruptions, or internal handoffs. Consequently, service-minute savings are active-labor or machine-time savings only under additional assumptions. A completed branch's synchronization wait also need not occupy its resource.
- **Final-station model.** F1/F2 assignments and recorded service intervals permit overlap checks but do not reveal all work or availability. Apparent idle periods are not proof of waste. The pooled FCFS replay is a sensitivity model; it changes dispatch and possibly assignments, and is not a reconstruction of the actual policy.
- **Counterfactual interactions.** Accelerating one branch may alter resource queues for other cases, final arrival order, priority decisions, and quality or rework. The isolated interval edits omit those effects; the final replay includes only the stated downstream interactions. Direct join savings and final-flow savings need not coincide, and independently calculated gains need not be additive.
- **Small sample, resolution, and selection.** Twelve cases on one day cannot establish stable critical frequencies, population percentiles, seasonality, or sustainable throughput. Minute-level timestamps limit precision; modeled half-minute changes should not be interpreted as measurements. There are no observed critical ties, but the interventions create ties, which must be retained rather than silently attributed to one branch. It is also unknown whether this is a complete workload, whether unfinished cases are missing, or whether boundary conditions bias apparent waiting and utilization.
