## 1. Conformance semantics

Conformance concerns the rule’s meaning, scope, and supporting evidence, not simply graph-set membership. I distinguish a deviation in the recorded representation from a verified business-process violation.

- **Forbidden behavior:** a positive observation matching an explicit prohibition, such as N3. The graph establishes a prohibited recorded pattern; provenance determines whether the business behavior occurred.
- **Missing mandatory behavior:** an applicable requirement lacks its required witness. Establish scope, observation completeness, event coverage, and temporal meaning before concluding that the business process failed.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior:** an observed relation is unspecified or outside coverage. Record it descriptively; novelty alone establishes no violation.
- **Typical-pattern absence:** an expected tendency is absent. This can warrant investigation but is not mandatory noncompliance, regardless of rarity.
- **Likely data-quality error:** identifiers, joins, timestamps, or missing associations plausibly explain a deviation. This describes its cause and can coexist with a definite violation in the recorded data.
- **Abstraction mismatch:** the graph’s relation does not faithfully express the business obligation, especially direct succession versus eventual occurrence.
- **Insufficient evidence:** summaries cannot resolve applicability, identity, ordering, completeness, or exceptions. Keep the finding provisional.

Closed-world reasoning applies to explicit prohibitions and mandatory requirements within scope. It does not turn every absent normative edge into a prohibition, or every missing typical edge into an obligation. No minimum frequency or statistical tolerance is supplied.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An OC-DFG establishes observed succession after projecting events onto objects of a particular type. Counts describe participating objects or transitions according to the counting convention. It cannot establish complete histories, business deadlines, or correct correlations by itself.

N1 explicitly requires **eventual validation**, with adjacency only an approximation. A trace Create → Review → Validate satisfies that obligation despite lacking Create → Validate adjacency. Conversely, the 98 observed adjacencies do not establish complete compliance for the cohort. Determine whether the other 2 Orders are standard and whether adequate follow-up exists. The 3 reviewed Orders cannot automatically be identified with those 2.

A review omitted from the summary but present in the trace can break adjacency. A review absent from the underlying log cannot itself break logged adjacency; clarify what “unrecorded” means.

N4 covers all 200 externally shipped Packages. Its 2 missing adjacencies remain candidates until packing, ordering, and adjacency semantics are checked; they are not evidence of two unshipped Packages. N3 expresses a broader prohibition than direct succession: also search for dispatch after cancellation with intervening events.

The 72 invoicing and 83 closing counts may overlap. No validated-cohort denominator permits calculating missing closure from 83. N5’s 92/110 payment coverage, 4 write-offs, and 14 no-successor observations require identity reconciliation and follow-up, not an invented compliance threshold.

### OTG

An OTG establishes aggregate relations between object types. Without witnesses and weight definitions, it cannot establish event-by-event participation, cardinality, or genuine business births.

T1’s 1,450 and T2’s 200 interactions are consistent with expected connectivity. They do not resolve Add Item role gaps or the 2 Dispatch events without Delivery. Existing associations, other events, and different counting units can preserve an aggregate edge despite local defects.

T6’s single cobirth is prohibited if it represents actual shared creation. First appearance in a truncated log is weaker evidence. T4, T5, and the unregulated 7 codeaths impose no additional prohibition.

### ET–OT

ET–OT identifies event-type/object-type associations, but aggregate weights do not necessarily count distinct events. E2 and E3 must both hold for each Add Item event; subtracting weights cannot locate failures.

The supplied context directly establishes 2 E6 gaps and 1 E8 gap. Carrier references support recovery, but are not Delivery relations. T3’s typical status permits flexibility at aggregate interaction level; it does not cancel E8’s mandatory Invoice role for events without an explicit exception type. Thus an acceptable OTG pattern can coexist with an ET–OT violation. E10 establishes a prohibited recorded association, with strong grounds to investigate data provenance before attributing business behavior.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

**Mandatory**

- N1: 98/100 adjacency coverage; classify the other 2 as business violations only if standard scope, missing eventual validation, and adequate observation are established.
- N4: 198/200 coverage in established shipment scope; distinguish missing packing from adjacency artifacts.
- E6: 2 missing Delivery roles; E8: 1 missing Invoice role. E8’s exception changes applicability only with verified explicit typing.
- E2–E3: 1,448/1,450 links require event-level checking. T1–T2 and E1, E3–E5, E7 provide positive evidence, not universal certification.

**Forbidden**

- N3: 2 post-cancellation dispatch patterns; T6: 1 cobirth; E10: 1 sensor–Invoice link. Verified bad provenance changes causal attribution, while the recorded prohibited pattern remains a finding until corrected.

**Typical**

- N2: 83 direct closures; intervening invoicing for 72 is compatible.
- N5: 92/110 payment successions, 4 write-offs, and 14 no-successor cases; T3: 92 interactions. Absence alone is not mandatory failure.

**Unregulated**

- Review pathways and RiskCase relations: 3; Review–Customer: 3; Order–Customer: 100; codeath: 7; reopen–close: 1.
- These remain unregulated here unless another applicable rule is established. A review path still must satisfy N1 when in scope, and reopening does not remove N3.

## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch

The sketch preserves diagnostics separately, checks event roles directly, and labels graph-summary evidence explicitly. The signature adapter fails on unfamiliar APIs. Scope, exception, and temporal judgments remain external to graph comparison.

```python
import inspect
import pm4py

# Inputs supplied by the caller:
# ocel, norm_ocdfg (dict), norm_otg (tuple), norm_etot (tuple).
observed = {
    "OC-DFG": pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel),
    "OTG": pm4py.discover_otg(ocel),
    "ET-OT": pm4py.discover_etot(ocel),
}

def observed_argument(fn, graph):
    # Check the installed release's signature. Never silently substitute
    # a graph for an OCEL, or mistake a normative argument for observed data.
    params = [p for p in inspect.signature(fn).parameters.values()
              if p.kind in (p.POSITIONAL_ONLY, p.POSITIONAL_OR_KEYWORD)]
    if len(params) < 2:
        raise TypeError(f"Inspect unsupported conformance API: {fn.__name__}")
    name = params[0].name.lower()
    if "norm" in name or "reference" in name:
        raise TypeError(f"Check argument order for {fn.__name__}")
    if "ocel" in name or name in {"log", "event_log"}:
        return ocel
    if any(s in name for s in ("dfg", "otg", "etot", "graph", "model")):
        return graph
    raise TypeError(f"Explicit API adapter needed for {fn.__name__}")

# Preserve library outputs verbatim; their missing/extra-edge labels are
# structural diagnostics, not complete business interpretations.
raw_diagnostics = {
    "OC-DFG": pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(
        observed_argument(pm4py.conformance_ocdfg, observed["OC-DFG"]),
        norm_ocdfg),
    "OTG": pm4py.conformance_otg(
        observed_argument(pm4py.conformance_otg, observed["OTG"]), norm_otg),
    "ET-OT": pm4py.conformance_etot(
        observed_argument(pm4py.conformance_etot, observed["ET-OT"]), norm_etot),
}

# MUST be maintained outside bare graph structures: status, scope,
# exceptions, time semantics, and the distinction between birth and first
# observation. A forbidden edge must not become an expected positive edge.
# Fields: status, family, typed relation, applicability/meaning.
META = {
    "N1": ("mandatory", "OC-DFG", ("Order", "Create Order", "Validate Order"),
           "New standard Orders; eventual validation, not necessarily adjacency"),
    "N2": ("typical", "OC-DFG", ("Order", "Validate Order", "Close Order"),
           "Intermediate events allowed"),
    "N3": ("forbidden", "OC-DFG", ("Order", "Cancel Order", "Dispatch Package"),
           "Same Order; any later dispatch, including nonadjacent events"),
    "N4": ("mandatory", "OC-DFG", ("Package", "Pack Package", "Dispatch Package"),
           "External shipment; exact adjacency semantics need clarification"),
    "N5": ("typical", "OC-DFG", ("Invoice", "Issue Invoice", "Record Payment"),
           "Credit notes, write-offs, open credit terms allowed"),
    "N6": ("unspecified", "OC-DFG", ("Order", "Reopen Order", "Close Order"), "Outside coverage"),
    "T1": ("mandatory", "OTG", ("Order", "object_interaction", "Item"), "Each Add Item interaction"),
    "T2": ("mandatory", "OTG", ("Package", "object_interaction", "Delivery"), "Each Dispatch Package interaction"),
    "T3": ("typical", "OTG", ("Invoice", "object_interaction", "Payment"), "Unapplied cash possible"),
    "T4": ("unspecified", "OTG", ("Order", "object_interaction", "Customer"), "Other policy"),
    "T5": ("unspecified", "OTG", ("Order", "object_interaction", "RiskCase"), "Review allowed"),
    "T6": ("forbidden", "OTG", ("Order", "object_cobirth", "Payment"), "Actual shared business birth"),
    "E1": ("mandatory", "ET-OT", ("Create Order", "Order"), "Every event"),
    "E2": ("mandatory", "ET-OT", ("Add Item", "Order"), "Every event"),
    "E3": ("mandatory", "ET-OT", ("Add Item", "Item"), "Every event"),
    "E4": ("mandatory", "ET-OT", ("Pack Package", "Package"), "Every event"),
    "E5": ("mandatory", "ET-OT", ("Dispatch Package", "Package"), "Every event"),
    "E6": ("mandatory", "ET-OT", ("Dispatch Package", "Delivery"), "Every event"),
    "E7": ("mandatory", "ET-OT", ("Record Payment", "Payment"), "Every event"),
    "E8": ("mandatory", "ET-OT", ("Record Payment", "Invoice"),
           "Except explicitly typed unapplied cash; no such typing in snapshot"),
    "E9": ("unspecified", "ET-OT", ("Manual Compliance Review", "RiskCase"), "Allowed rare interaction"),
    "E10": ("forbidden", "ET-OT", ("Sensor Heartbeat", "Invoice"), "Every sensor event"),
}
INDEX = {(family, edge): rid
         for rid, (status, family, edge, scope) in META.items()}

def classify(family, edge, evidence, count, source,
             scope_confirmed=False, cause="unresolved"):
    rid = INDEX.get((family, edge))
    status = META[rid][0] if rid else "unspecified"  # Open-world default.
    if status == "forbidden" and evidence == "present":
        label = "prohibited recorded relation; verify business provenance"
    elif status == "mandatory" and evidence == "adjacency_gap":
        label = "mandatory candidate; verify temporal semantics and complete trace"
    elif status == "mandatory" and evidence == "missing":
        label = ("mandatory recorded-role gap" if scope_confirmed else
                 "mandatory candidate; scope or coverage unresolved")
    elif status == "typical" and evidence != "present":
        label = "typical-pattern absence; no mandatory violation"
    elif status == "unspecified":
        label = "unregulated; no violation established"
    else:
        label = "compatible observed evidence; not universal certification"
    return dict(rule=rid, family=family, edge=edge, status=status,
                evidence=evidence, count=count, source=source,
                classification=label, cause=cause)

semantic_findings = []

# Snapshot evidence is entered explicitly, NOT inferred from unknown raw
# diagnostic schemas. Production adapters should attach object/event IDs.
for rid, evidence, count in [
    ("N1", "adjacency_gap", 2), ("N4", "adjacency_gap", 2),
    ("N3", "present", 2), ("T6", "present", 1),
    ("N5", "missing", 18), ("N6", "present", 1),
]:
    _, family, edge, _ = META[rid]
    semantic_findings.append(classify(
        family, edge, evidence, count, "supplied graph summary"))

for family, edge, count in [
    ("OC-DFG", ("Order", "Validate Order", "Issue Invoice"), 72),
    ("OC-DFG", ("Invoice", "Issue Invoice", "Write Off"), 4),
    ("ET-OT", ("Manual Compliance Review", "Customer"), 3),
    ("OTG", ("Package", "object_codeath", "Delivery"), 7),
]:
    semantic_findings.append(classify(
        family, edge, "present", count, "supplied graph summary"))

# Per-event incidence avoids subtracting aggregate relation weights.
# Standard OCEL relations contain the object-type column.
eid = ocel.event_id_column
activity = ocel.event_activity
otype = ocel.object_type_column
roles = (ocel.relations[[eid, otype]].dropna().drop_duplicates()
         .groupby(eid)[otype].agg(set).to_dict())

for rid, (status, family, edge, scope) in META.items():
    if family != "ET-OT" or status not in {"mandatory", "forbidden"}:
        continue
    event_type, required_type = edge
    event_ids = ocel.events.loc[
        ocel.events[activity].eq(event_type), eid].drop_duplicates().tolist()
    # E8: do not infer unapplied-cash exemption from a missing Invoice.
    # No event is explicitly exempt in this snapshot. Other snapshots need
    # a validated exception predicate maintained alongside META.
    bad_ids = [event_id for event_id in event_ids
               if ((required_type not in roles.get(event_id, set()))
                   if status == "mandatory" else
                   (required_type in roles.get(event_id, set())))]
    if bad_ids:
        finding = classify(
            family, edge, "missing" if status == "mandatory" else "present",
            len(bad_ids), "ocel event incidence", scope_confirmed=True,
            cause="suspected reused-ID join" if rid == "E10" else "unresolved")
        finding["event_ids"] = bad_ids
        semantic_findings.append(finding)

# Separate trace investigations must test N1 eventuality, N3 all-later
# dispatch, N4 packing semantics, and T6 true births. Aggregate subtraction
# cannot implement these. Check T1/T2 using same-event role witnesses too.
assessment = {
    "observed_models": observed,
    "raw_diagnostics": raw_diagnostics,
    "semantic_findings": semantic_findings,
    "rule_metadata": META,
}
```

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 scope and eventuality.** Undefined “standard” and deadlines risk false positives or indefinitely pending validation. Clarify eligible Order classes, accepted intermediate events, and completion deadlines; classify as **mandatory**.
2. **N4 adjacency and packing.** Unclear sequencing risks accepting unpacked shipments or flagging legitimate inspections. Specify packing-before-dispatch, acceptable intervening events, and repacking treatment for external shipments; classify as **mandatory**.
3. **N3 cancellation semantics.** Ambiguous effective timestamps and Order–Package mapping can permit prohibited dispatch or accuse the wrong Order. Define cancellation effectiveness, correlation, and tie handling while retaining post-cancellation dispatch as **forbidden**.
4. **T6 lifecycle provenance.** Equating first appearance with birth creates false violations. Require source-backed business-birth semantics and retain actual shared Order–Payment creation as **forbidden**.
5. **Delivery representation.** Attribute-only carrier references create inconsistent E6 enforcement. Define the Delivery identifier, allowed resolution procedure, and event association deadline; explicit resolved linkage should be **mandatory**.
6. **Unapplied cash.** T3 flexibility and E8’s typing exception invite inconsistent treatment. Define explicit exception evidence, typing, and correction procedures; Invoice linkage should remain **mandatory** outside that exception. Do not retrospectively exempt the event by assumption.
7. **Typical completion expectations.** Missing timing and exception guidance makes open invoices or intermediate closure paths look defective. Define N2/N5 follow-up cohorts and documented exceptions as **typical**, unless a separate mandatory deadline is deliberately adopted.
8. **Unregulated lifecycle and review coverage.** Unclear policy ownership encourages inconsistent enforcement. Keep reopening, Customer/RiskCase review participation, and codeath **explicitly unspecified** here, with links to owning policies where applicable.
9. **Evidence and counting conventions.** Undefined weights and extraction horizons invite invalid subtraction and coverage claims. Define event counts, unique-object counts, multiplicity, censoring, and provenance as a **mandatory** evidence contract. This creates no process-frequency threshold.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

- **Immediate containment:** prioritize the 2 N3 cases and the 1 T6 case. Check shipment state and block further affected dispatch while cancellation remains effective. Inspect the cobirth source event promptly; contain its creation path if a live prohibited mechanism is confirmed. Quarantine the sensor–Invoice association pending provenance review.
- **Data repair:** replace the reused-ID join with verified typed identifiers; resolve the 2 carrier references; correct the payment’s Invoice relation or substantiated exception typing. Preserve source records and repair history, then rediscover affected models.
- **Process investigation:** reconstruct N3 ordering and T6 true creation first. Then examine the 2 N4 histories, 2 N1 candidates, Add Item incidence, and 14 open/censored invoices. Separate missing capture from omitted business actions.
- **Norm revision:** agree the clarifications above with policy owners, version them, and record applicability dates. Preserve the distinction between interpretation, data correction, and changed obligations.
- **Monitoring:** check prohibited relations and mandatory event roles individually; track unresolved eventual obligations with observation horizons. Monitor typical-pattern rates descriptively, stratified by scope. Set no invented tolerance bands and do not alert solely on rare unregulated behavior.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

Robust conclusions are the explicit prohibited recorded patterns, the 2 E6 and 1 E8 recorded-role gaps, and the absence of grounds to condemn unregulated relations merely for novelty. Business conclusions for N1/N4 and typical absences depend on scope, timing, and model completeness. E10’s cause is strongly suspected data error; T6 and other findings also require provenance checks. Graph summaries alone cannot establish true births, complete histories, exact defective-event counts from unequal weights, legitimate exception typing, or verified operational harm.
