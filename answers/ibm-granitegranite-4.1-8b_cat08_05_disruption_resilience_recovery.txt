# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

**True Resilience** (designated *Keep* or *Modify* with concrete safeguards):

- **A (Hospitalcritical fast lane)** – Preserves lifesaving supply; risk mitigated by lightweight manual verification.
- **F (Temporary release control)** – Maintains safety for controlled substances despite system loss.
- **J (Backlog prioritization)** – Ensures critical orders are serviced first when capacity is scarce.
- **K (Reconcile manual reservation)** – Restores data integrity and reduces downstream rework.
- **L (Audit remediation)** – Closes compliance gaps, protecting regulatory standing.

**Risky Shortcuts** (designated *Retire* or *Modify* with high risk):

- **C (Skip system authorization check)** – Directly violates authorization controls; no safeguard acceptable.
- **G (Dispatch without scan)** – Severely compromises traceability; unacceptable for regulated goods.
- **I (Defer noncritical order)** – Causes hidden cancellation risk and fairness issues; should be refined.

**Ambiguous / ContextDependent** (designated *Modify* with riskappetite tuning):

- **B (Manual authorization check)** – Qualitypreserving but capacityconsuming; may be kept for highrisk segments only.
- **D (Manual inventory snapshot check)** – Useful for early hours but degrades as freshness wanes; needs stricter refresh cadence.
- **E (Manual pick list)** – Essential for flow continuity but requires downstream traceability capture.
- **H (Phone proof of delivery)** – Acceptable for speed but must be fortified for controlled products.

**Key distinction**: *Resilience* is achieved when an adaptation **maintains essential service continuity** while **introducing controllable, mitigated risks** that align with mission priorities (e.g., hospital supply). *Concealed noncompliance* occurs when an adaptation **preserves throughput** at the expense of **core regulatory or traceability controls**, creating hidden liabilities that surface only in audits or postincident reviews.

# 4. Recovery process model

Below is a **BPMNstyle process tree** (described in structured text) that institutionalizes a robust, triggerdriven recovery workflow for future cyberincident disruptions.

```text
[RecoveryProcess]
  |
  |-- [TriggerDetection]
  |     |-- If WMS unavailable (no heartbeat) OR cyberincident alert  Activate
  |     |-- Log incident timestamp (T1) and severity level
  |
  |-- [IncidentTriage]
        |-- Evaluate Impact Matrix (by Customer Risk, Product Type)
        |    |-- Hospitalcritical / Controlled  HighPriority
        |    |-- Pharmacystandard  MediumPriority
        |    |-- Wholesalebulk  LowPriority
        |-- Assign Incident Commander (IC) and Recovery Workgroup
  |
  |-- [ResilienceFallbackPath]
        |-- For HighPriority customers:
        |     |-- Enable **HospitalCritical Fast Lane** (A)
        |     |-- Initiate **Temporary Release Control** (F) for Controlledsubstance
        |     |-- Deploy **Manual Authorization Check** (B) only where no standing auth
        |     |-- Create **Manual Inventory Snapshot** (D) refreshed every hour
        |
        |-- For MediumPriority (Pharmacystandard):
        |     |-- Use **Manual Pick List** (E) with dualcapture IDs
        |     |-- Allow **Manual Authorization Check** (B) on a limitedscope whitelist
        |
        |-- For LowPriority (Wholesalebulk):
        |     |-- Apply **Defer Noncritical Order** (I) to preserve capacity
        |     |-- Maintain **Backlog Prioritization** (J) queue for later processing
  |
  |-- [EvidenceCapture]
        |-- All manual steps generate **digital evidence**:
        |     |-- Snapshot  PDF timestamped
        |     |-- Authorization  Barcodeless token recorded in ERP
        |     |-- Pick list  Linked to order ID via QR code
        |     |-- POD  Phone signature captured with timestamp & device ID
        |     |-- Release control  Signed log uploaded to secure vault
  |
  |-- [BacklogReconciliation]
        |-- Once primary systems return:
        |     |-- Run **Reconcile Manual Reservation** (K) to align inventory
        |     |-- Flag any **Traceability Exceptions** (L) for audit remediation
  |
  |-- [AuditRemediationLoop]
        |-- Continuous **AuditRemediation** (L) with:
        |     |-- Realtime alerts if missing scan, POD, temperature, or auth
        |     |-- Automated workflow to correct or flag for manual closure
  |
  |-- [PostIncident Review]
        |-- Conduct **LessonsLearned Workshop** within 5 business days
        |-- Update **Impact Matrix**, **Trigger Conditions**, and **Standard Operating Procedures**
```

**Explanation of notation**  
- Nodes are denoted by capital letters (`[Node]`).  
- Horizontal lines (`|`) indicate hierarchical flow.  
- Subpaths under a node are indented to show parallel or conditional branches.  
- The model explicitly maps *trigger detection*  *triage*  *productriskbased resilience fallback*  *evidence capture*  *reconciliation*  *continuous audit remediation*  *review*.  

This process ensures that **core controls (authorization, traceability, temperature documentation) never disappear**, while **flexibility** is built in for highrisk customers, preserving missioncritical supply lines.

# 5. Resilience indicators

| Indicator | Leading (preincident) | Lagging (postincident) | Target / Acceptable Range |
|-----------|------------------------|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| **Throughput protection** | % of hospitalcritical orders processed within 8h under simulated WMS outage (runbook test monthly). | Hospitalcritical fill rate after real incident (Day27). | 90% during outage; baseline 96%. |
| **Service protection** | Capacity buffer (hours) reserved for highpriority queues (e.g., 20% of peak). | Cancellation rate across customer segments. | 2% for hospital, 5% for pharmacy, 10% for bulk. |
| **Traceability completeness** | Proportion of orders with both picklist ID and QC check before dispatch (tested in drill). | Traceability exceptions after recovery (L). | 1% across all segments. |
| **Control completeness** | Ratio of controlledsubstance orders with dualcontrol logged (preincident audit). | Missing dualcontrol rate during workaround (C, G). | 0.5% (baseline 0.2%). |
| **Backlog age** | Average days backlog items sit before triage (measured during normal ops). | Max backlog age after incident (days). | 2days for highpriority, 5days for medium, 10days for low. |
| **Rework rate** | Percent of orders flagged for correction during simulated failure (dryrun). | Rework / correction case rate after incident (E, K). | 4% for hospital, 6% for pharmacy, 8% for bulk. |
| **Fairness across customers** | Survey/feedback score for perceived service equity (quarterly). | Cancellation or deferment dissatisfaction scores (I). | 4/5 overall, no segment <3.5/5. |

These indicators give **realtime visibility** (leading) and **postmortem validation** (lagging), enabling proactive adjustments and confirming that resilience actions truly protect missioncritical operations.

# 7. Management tradeoff statement

Management must **accept** the necessity of **temporary, lightweight shortcuts** (e.g., fastlane triage for hospitals) that **preserve critical patient supply** while **accepting a disciplined mitigation framework** (lightweight manual verification, dualcapture evidence). **What should not be accepted** are **uncontrolled skips** of authorization or dispatch without any traceability mechanism, as these create hidden regulatory and compliance liabilities that can lead to fines, product recalls, or loss of hospital trust.

Evidence that would **change the recommendation** includes:

- **Demonstrated system restoration time** consistently under two hours, allowing full automated checks to resume quickly – which would reduce need for manual shortcuts.
- **Validation of a reliable standingauthorization system** showing zero falsepositives over a sixmonth pilot, eliminating the need for skipcheck adaptations.
- **Realtime auditremediation alerts** achieving <0.2% falsepositive rate, making afterthefact remediation unnecessary.

Until such evidence exists, the current set of **resiliencefocused adaptations with mitigating controls** remains the prudent path forward.
