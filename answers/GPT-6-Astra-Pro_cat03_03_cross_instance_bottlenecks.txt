## 1. Interaction-graph description

**The six jobs form one connected dependency network.** My leading explanation is synchronization amplified by tool and release-policy constraints—not continuously overloaded machines.

Use nodes typed **Job, Component, ComponentLot, Machine, Tool, Batch, Shipment**, plus event nodes preserving joint participation. Keep three edge layers: qualified business O2O relations; event–object incidence; and time-dependent states such as holding, unavailable, or awaiting partners.

```text
Supplied business O2O relations:
Ji --requires--> Ci                         i = 1,...,6
L7 --contains--> {C2,C5};  L8 --contains--> {C1,C3}
B1 --batches--> {J1,J2,J3}; B2 --batches--> {J4,J5}
S1 --consolidates--> {J1,J4}
S2 --consolidates--> {J2,J3}
S3 --consolidates--> {J5,J6}

Event/state relationships:
Batch Cure@12:05 --participants-->
    {B1,OVEN-1,RACK-7,J1,J2,J3,C1,C2,C3}
B2 --uses--> {OVEN-1,RACK-7}
J3 --holds [09:00,11:40)--> FIX-A
Machine Process@11:45 --participants--> {J4,C4,CNC-2,FIX-A}
Final Test(J5/J6) --requires--> {TEST-1,CAL-3}
Quarantine/Release(L7) --affects-via-components--> {J2,J5}
```

The business edges are supplied observations. Machine/tool-use links reflect supplied event participation. FIX-A’s continuous hold is reconstructed from acquisition/release, assuming no missing release or sharing. “J4 waits because of FIX-A” remains an inference. Lot-to-Job participation is integration-dependent: preserve source-direct versus derived provenance rather than silently treating enriched links as source observations.

Four higher-order motifs matter: **Job–Component–Batch–Machine–Tool** is a joint processing barrier; **Job–Tool–Machine–Job** links competing work; **ComponentLot–Component–Job–Batch** propagates release restrictions across batches; **Batch–Job–Shipment–Job–Batch** reconnects jobs after processing. S3 extends this network to J6. L8 links J1/J3 structurally, but no L8 disruption is supplied.

A co-event pair graph must not replace the original hyperedges: one B1 cure is not three independent cures. PM4Py’s interaction summary emits ordered object pairs; Job–ComponentLot and ComponentLot–Job counts may describe the same interactions, not independent evidence. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][1])

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

All calculations assume a common operating day. **Elapsed exposure is observed; preventability depends on the stated counterfactual.**

**B1.** J1 waits 12:05 − 08:10 = **235 minutes**; J2 waits 12:05 − 08:25 = **220 minutes**. Relative to J3’s 11:55 readiness, these decompose into **225 + 10** and **210 + 10** minutes. Counterfactual: each could receive a feasible run ten minutes after its own readiness, with no competing resource or policy restriction. Hypothetical start-time savings are then 225/210 minutes—not a demonstrated jointly feasible schedule. J2’s quarantine at 10:20 makes its early readiness potentially revocable; continuous eligibility requires the override’s effective time and scope.

**FIX-A/J4.** Arrival-to-start is 11:45 − 09:50 = **115 minutes**. Of this, **110 minutes** overlap J3’s recorded hold, followed by five minutes after release. Only **42 minutes**, 10:05–10:47, overlap explicitly documented CNC idleness. Counterfactual: with J4 otherwise eligible at 10:05, an available fixture, five-minute setup, and no competing reservation, machining could start at 10:10: **95 minutes earlier**. Neither 110 nor 42 minutes is a proven causal saving.

**B2.** J4 waits 14:50 − 13:10 = **100 minutes**, comprising 90 minutes until J5 becomes ready and ten thereafter. Oven idleness is **115 minutes**; rack unavailability overlaps **100** of those. Under an AND-gate counterfactual holding other readiness fixed, earliest feasibility is:

$$
\max(12{:}55_{\text{oven}},14{:}35_{\text{rack}},
13{:}10_{\text{J4}},14{:}40_{\text{J5}})=14{:}40.
$$

With the observed ten-minute launch lag, adding a rack alone saves **zero**. Improving both rack availability and J5 readiness could help; their exposures cannot be added.

**L7.** Quarantine lasts 13:50 − 10:20 = **210 minutes**. J2 enters B1 **105 minutes before release**; J5 becomes batch-ready **50 minutes after release**. Counterfactual: earlier authorized release helps only while lot clearance is binding. Neither 210 nor 50 minutes identifies recoverable J5 delay; other prerequisites and processing are unobserved.

**CAL-3/J6.** Readiness-to-test is 17:25 − 16:05 = **80 minutes**: 30 until J5 finishes, 45 recalibration, five after recalibration. Counterfactual: preserve J5-first sequencing and five-minute handover, but provide qualified calibration availability at 16:35. J6 starts at 16:40, saving **45 minutes**, not 80.

**S1/J1.** Packed-to-dispatch is 19:25 − 13:30 = **355 minutes**: **340** awaiting J4’s packing plus **15** afterward. Counterfactual: permit independent shipment with an available carrier and unchanged 15-minute handling. J1 departs at 13:45, **340 minutes earlier**. Without those conditions, this is partner-wait exposure rather than guaranteed savings.

## 4. What flattening misses

PM4Py flattening includes events related to the selected object type; it does not automatically traverse every business O2O relationship. Thus coverage depends on integration choices. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][2])

| Viewpoint    | Visible                                                        | Hidden or duplicated; misleading conclusion                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Job          | Own milestones and recorded elapsed intervals.                 | Shared B1 cure appears in three traces; source extraction omits L7 events. Median Pack→Dispatch = 1.1 h does not describe J1’s 5 h 55 min dwell.                                           |
| Machine      | Processing sequences and machine-associated events.            | Eligibility, unavailable tools and waiting partners can be absent. A normal Setup→Process→Release path does not establish prompt job service.                                              |
| Batch        | One joint cure and batch-associated loading.                   | Member readiness may be absent; enriched rack maintenance can appear in multiple batch cases. Short Load→Cure time does not imply little pre-batch waiting.                                |
| Tool         | Acquisition/release, cleaning and calibration, where recorded. | Jobs unable to acquire the tool may never enter its trace. A normal Recalibrate→Test sequence can coexist with J6’s long wait.                                                             |
| ComponentLot | Quarantine/release lifecycle.                                  | Different job exceptions are hidden; propagating one hold into multiple jobs multiplies exposure records, not physical quarantine events. One lot path does not imply identical treatment. |
| Shipment     | Create→Load→Dispatch execution.                                | Early packing outside the shipment lifecycle is omitted; dispatch is duplicated across job traces. Fast loading-to-dispatch does not establish fast fulfillment.                           |

Low machine utilization is compatible with long waiting because production requires **simultaneous** machine, tool, material, policy and partner eligibility. Spare machine time alone cannot satisfy that conjunction. Six jobs without a 1.8-standard-deviation outlier also establish neither acceptable absolute lead times nor absence of common delays.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**FIX-A → J4 → B2 → S1.** **Temporal precedence:** J3 releases FIX-A at 11:40; J4 machines at 11:45, becomes batch-ready at 13:10, joins B2 at 14:50 and packs at 19:10. **Resource feasibility:** machining requires CNC-2 plus FIX-A; curing uses OVEN-1 plus RACK-7. **Policy:** B2 couples J4/J5, and S1 couples J4/J1. **Causal speculation:** fixture delay might propagate to shipment, but J5’s later readiness can absorb a J4 improvement. Advancing J4 alone need not advance B2 or S1.

**L7 → C2/C5 → J2/J5 → B1/B2 → OVEN-1/RACK-7.** **Temporal precedence:** quarantine begins 10:20; J2 cures at 12:05 under an external override; release is 13:50; J5 is ready 14:40. **Resource feasibility:** B2 also needs rack release at 14:35. **Policy:** quarantine eligibility differs because of the override. **Causal speculation:** L7 plausibly delays J5, thereby J4, but removing the lot restriction alone may leave the rack binding. No evidence establishes that B1 caused the rack quarantine.

**C5 → J5 → CAL-3/TEST-1 → J6 → S3.** **Temporal precedence:** J5’s test ends 16:35, calibration ends 17:20, and J6 starts 17:25. **Resource feasibility:** TEST-1 without qualified CAL-3 cannot test. **Policy:** S3 consolidates J5/J6. **Causal speculation:** delayed J6 testing may postpone S3 and hold J5, but packing times and transport availability are missing. Recalibration follows J5; the evidence does not establish that J5 triggered it.

## 7. PM4Py analysis outline

The outline preserves qualified O2O relations separately from derived interaction graphs. OTG summarizes derived object relationships; ETOT exposes activity–object-type coverage. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][3])

OC-DFG comparisons distinguish event pairs, unique objects and event-pair/object occurrences. For B1’s cure, one physical event has three Job incidences—not three oven operations. ([PM4Py Documentation][4])

Flattened performance DFGs report elapsed relationships, not causal queue time. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][3]) Object features provide retrospective lifecycle/work-in-progress diagnostics; they do not establish causal effects. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][5])

The timing calculations were checked; PM4Py calls were not executed here. [Download the Python outline](sandbox:/mnt/data/ocel_analysis_outline.py).

```python
from copy import deepcopy
import warnings
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Input: an OCEL named `ocel`; timestamps already use a common, correct timezone.
# Original qualified/provenance-bearing tables are never overwritten.
E, O = ocel.event_id_column, ocel.object_id_column
A, T, K = ocel.event_activity, ocel.event_timestamp, ocel.object_type_column
O2 = f"{O}_2"
Q = getattr(ocel, "qualifier", "ocel:qualifier")

needed = ("ocel_objects_interactions_summary", "discover_objects_graph",
          "discover_otg", "discover_etot", "discover_ocdfg", "ocel_flattening",
          "discover_performance_dfg", "extract_ocel_features")
missing_api = [name for name in needed if not hasattr(pm4py, name)]
if missing_api:
    raise RuntimeError(f"PM4Py {pm4py.__version__} lacks: {missing_api}")

for frame, columns in ((ocel.events, [E, A, T]),
                       (ocel.objects, [O, K]), (ocel.relations, [E, O])):
    if not set(columns).issubset(frame.columns):
        raise ValueError(
            f"Missing OCEL columns: {set(columns) - set(frame.columns)}"
        )
    if frame[columns].isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError("Null identifiers, types, activities, or timestamps")
if ocel.events[E].duplicated().any() or ocel.objects[O].duplicated().any():
    raise ValueError("Event and object identifiers must be unique")
if not (ocel.relations[E].isin(ocel.events[E]).all()
        and ocel.relations[O].isin(ocel.objects[O]).all()):
    raise ValueError("Dangling E2O references")

# Incidence-only analysis view: several qualifiers must not multiply one E2O pair.
view = deepcopy(ocel)
view.events = view.events.sort_values(T, kind="stable")
view.relations = view.relations.drop_duplicates([E, O]).copy()
for col in (A, T):
    view.relations[col] = view.relations[E].map(view.events.set_index(E)[col])
view.relations[K] = view.relations[O].map(view.objects.set_index(O)[K])
view.relations = view.relations.sort_values(T, kind="stable")
r = view.relations

# Keep co-event interaction graphs separate from explicitly qualified O2O edges.
interactions = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(view)
# The API emits both orientations; retain each unordered object pair once/event.
pairs = interactions.loc[
    interactions[O] < interactions[O2]
].drop_duplicates([E, O, O2]).copy()
pairs["type_pair"] = [
    tuple(sorted((a, b)))
    for a, b in zip(pairs[K], pairs[f"{K}_2"])
]
interaction_counts = pairs.groupby("type_pair").agg(
    distinct_events=(E, "nunique"),
    event_pair_occurrences=(E, "size")
)
object_graph = pm4py.discover_objects_graph(
    view, graph_type="object_interaction"
)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(view)       # Derived relationships, not causality.
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(view)     # Activity/object-type coverage.
business_edges = ocel.o2o.copy()    # Preserve qualified business relationships.

ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(view)
frequency_rows = []
for typ, edges in ocdfg["edges"]["event_couples"].items():
    for edge, couples in edges.items():
        frequency_rows.append({
            "object_type": typ,
            "source": edge[0],
            "target": edge[1],
            "event_pairs": len(couples),
            "unique_objects": len(
                ocdfg["edges"]["unique_objects"][typ][edge]
            ),
            "event_pair_object_occurrences": len(
                ocdfg["edges"]["total_objects"][typ][edge]
            )
        })
edge_frequencies = pd.DataFrame(frequency_rows)

# Additional comparison: one event versus its multiple object incidences.
activity_frequencies = r.groupby([A, K]).agg(
    distinct_events=(E, "nunique"),
    unique_objects=(O, "nunique"),
    event_object_incidences=(O, "size")
)


def flattened_performance(log, object_type):
    flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(log, object_type)
    if flat.empty:
        return flat, None
    if flat.duplicated(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"]).any():
        warnings.warn(
            f"{object_type}: tied timestamps need ordering sensitivity checks"
        )
    flat = flat.sort_values(
        ["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"], kind="stable"
    )
    # Elapsed directly-follows times, NOT inferred queue/service/causal delay.
    return flat, pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
        flat, perf_aggregation_key="all"
    )


flattenings = {
    typ: flattened_performance(view, typ)
    for typ in sorted(view.objects[K].unique())
}

# Targeted lot-link audit and candidate enrichment, not unrestricted O2O closure.
lot_link_audit = pd.DataFrame()
candidate_job_view = None
if not business_edges.empty:
    if not {O, O2, Q}.issubset(business_edges.columns):
        raise ValueError("Qualified O2O columns required for the lot-link audit")
    if not (business_edges[O].isin(view.objects[O]).all()
            and business_edges[O2].isin(view.objects[O]).all()):
        raise ValueError("Dangling O2O references")

    type_of = view.objects.set_index(O)[K]
    b = business_edges.assign(
        source_type=business_edges[O].map(type_of),
        target_type=business_edges[O2].map(type_of)
    )
    contains = b.loc[
        (b[Q] == "contains") & (b.source_type == "ComponentLot")
        & (b.target_type == "Component"), [O, O2]
    ].rename(columns={O: "lot", O2: "component"})
    requires = b.loc[
        (b[Q] == "requires") & (b.source_type == "Job")
        & (b.target_type == "Component"), [O, O2]
    ].rename(columns={O: "job", O2: "component"})
    lot_jobs = contains.merge(requires, on="component")

    lot_events = r.loc[
        (r[K] == "ComponentLot")
        & r[A].isin(["Quarantine Lot", "Release Lot"]), [E, O]
    ]
    expected = (
        lot_events.rename(columns={O: "lot"})
        .merge(lot_jobs, on="lot")[[E, "job"]].drop_duplicates()
    )
    actual = r.loc[r[K] == "Job", [E, O]].rename(columns={O: "job"})
    lot_link_audit = expected.merge(
        actual, on=[E, "job"], how="left",
        indicator=True, validate="one_to_one"
    )

    # Presence does NOT establish a direct link in the original source system.
    missing = lot_link_audit.loc[
        lot_link_audit["_merge"] == "left_only", [E, "job"]
    ]
    extra = (
        missing.rename(columns={"job": O})
        .merge(view.events[[E, A, T]], on=E, validate="many_to_one")
        .merge(view.objects[[O, K]], on=O, validate="many_to_one")
    )
    extra[Q] = "affected_via_lot"
    extra["analysis:relation_origin"] = "derived_candidate_requires_contains"

    candidate = deepcopy(view)
    candidate.relations = pd.concat(
        [r, extra], ignore_index=True
    ).sort_values(T)
    candidate_job_view = flattened_performance(candidate, "Job")
else:
    warnings.warn("No O2O relations: lot-to-job linkage cannot be audited")

# Eventless objects have no observed lifecycle; retain them in the business graph,
# but exclude them from lifecycle feature computation.
feature_view = deepcopy(view)
active_ids = set(r[O])
feature_view.objects = feature_view.objects.loc[
    feature_view.objects[O].isin(active_ids)
]
if not feature_view.o2o.empty:
    feature_view.o2o = feature_view.o2o.loc[
        feature_view.o2o[O].isin(active_ids)
        & feature_view.o2o[O2].isin(active_ids)
    ]

# Retrospective diagnostics only; six jobs do not support a predictive/causal model.
job_features = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(
    feature_view, obj_type="Job", include_obj_id=True,
    enable_object_lifecycle_paths=True,
    enable_object_work_in_progress=True
)

# Auxiliary evidence supplied in the question; not invented OCEL event labels.
# Assumption: these clock times belong to the same operating day.
def clock(value: str) -> pd.Timedelta:
    return pd.to_timedelta(value + ":00")


batch_waits = pd.DataFrame([
    ("J1", "B1", "08:10", "12:05"),
    ("J2", "B1", "08:25", "12:05"),
    ("J3", "B1", "11:55", "12:05"),
    ("J4", "B2", "13:10", "14:50"),
    ("J5", "B2", "14:40", "14:50")
], columns=["job", "batch", "ready", "start"])

for col in ("ready", "start"):
    batch_waits[col] = batch_waits[col].map(clock)
latest_ready = batch_waits.groupby("batch")["ready"].transform("max")
minute = pd.Timedelta(minutes=1)
batch_waits["nominal_wait_min"] = (
    batch_waits.start - batch_waits.ready
) / minute
batch_waits["peer_readiness_gap_min"] = (
    latest_ready - batch_waits.ready
) / minute
batch_waits["launch_lag_min"] = (
    batch_waits.start - latest_ready
) / minute
batch_waits["readiness_caveat"] = batch_waits.job.map({
    "J2": "Quarantine at 10:20; reconcile override validity"
}).fillna("")


def overlap_minutes(a: str, b: str, c: str, d: str) -> float:
    """Overlap of two explicit intervals; never a causal-delay estimator."""
    if clock(b) < clock(a) or clock(d) < clock(c):
        raise ValueError("Reversed interval or unhandled overnight interval")
    return max(
        pd.Timedelta(0),
        min(clock(b), clock(d)) - max(clock(a), clock(c))
    ) / minute


exposure_minutes = {
    "J4_wait_overlaps_FIX_hold":
        overlap_minutes("09:50", "11:45", "09:00", "11:40"),
    "CNC_idle_overlaps_FIX_hold":
        overlap_minutes("10:05", "10:47", "09:00", "11:40"),
    "J6_wait_overlaps_CAL_recalibration":
        overlap_minutes("16:05", "17:25", "16:35", "17:20")
}

results = dict(
    interactions=interaction_counts,
    object_graph=object_graph,
    qualified_o2o=business_edges,
    otg=otg, etot=etot, ocdfg=ocdfg,
    edge_frequencies=edge_frequencies,
    activity_frequencies=activity_frequencies,
    flattenings=flattenings,
    lot_link_audit=lot_link_audit,
    candidate_job_view=candidate_job_view,
    job_features=job_features,
    batch_waits=batch_waits,
    interval_exposures=exposure_minutes
)
print(activity_frequencies)
print(edge_frequencies)
print(lot_link_audit)
print(batch_waits)
print(exposure_minutes)
```

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations weakening rank 1** would include: (1) corrected readiness timestamps close to actual batch starts; (2) batch membership being assigned only after an independently fixed start, rather than governing it; (3) verified oven unavailability throughout the apparent early-ready interval; (4) independent tool/operator restrictions preventing earlier curing even with all partners ready; (5) comparable ready jobs starting without their nominated partners, contradicting a mandatory synchronization barrier; (6) controlled batch-policy trials showing no reduction in readiness-to-start waiting when other prerequisites are demonstrably satisfied. Some weaken attribution to policy rather than the existence of measured waiting.

**Discriminating fields and events** should include: (1) readiness assertions, revocations and physical prerequisites; (2) timestamped tool requests/acquisitions/releases and qualified substitutes; (3) machine busy/idle/down/reserved intervals plus operator calendars; (4) versioned batch membership, compatibility and minimum-load rules; (5) lot disposition, component membership and authorized override validity; (6) cleaning/quarantine reasons and calibration triggers; (7) actual packing, shipment membership changes and carrier cutoffs; (8) source event IDs, relation provenance, timestamp semantics and extraction windows.
