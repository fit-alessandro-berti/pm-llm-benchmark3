## 1. Process-structural comparison

Site A supports a plausible mechanism, not an identified transferable effect. Its median fell by nine hours, but parallelization arrived with several other changes. Site B should test a legally constrained partial order, with eligibility and capacity controls.

The DFG is evidence about recorded adjacency, not a precedence specification. Different successors can arise from variants, concurrency serialized into a log, repeated activities, or recording artifacts. In particular, the two reported Register-successor counts sum to 3,910, exceeding 3,400 cases. That is incompatible with an ordinary directly-follows occurrence count if every case contains exactly one Register event. Audit the counting convention, repeated registration, duplicates, and extraction before fitting a model. The 2,108 Originals Receipt → Document Check occurrences equal 62% of cases numerically, but do not independently establish case-level compliance. Likewise, 360 Legal Review → Request Supplement occurrences do not identify 360 distinct reworked cases, and the 345 supplement-to-document occurrences do not prove 15 violations: this is a selected, potentially incomplete DFG.

The shared mechanism is a partial-order change: when verification inputs are independent and ready, replacing unnecessary precedence with concurrency can shorten the time until both checks finish. Assessment still needs an AND-join. Capacities, queue discipline, and repeat work then determine whether earlier readiness becomes earlier completion. These mechanisms plausibly exist at both sites, although their parameters need not match.

Site-specific mechanisms include the statutory originals gate, mandatory legal review, specialization of resource pools, and the case lock. Original-receipt variability can dominate the critical path; legal supplements create repeated work absent from A's supplied tree. Timestamp quality also differs, changing measured overlap without necessarily changing execution. Selection nodes therefore point to both operational and measurement mechanisms, rather than merely identifying a different population.

Effect modifiers include document readiness, originals requirements and delay, complexity, legal eligibility, role specialization, IT capability, each resource pool's capacity, arrival load, API latency, and WIP policy. Some are eligibility constraints; others determine the magnitude or even direction of benefit. True overlap and waiting are mediators, so adjusting them away would not estimate the total intervention effect.

At A, calendar period jointly predicts treatment and co-interventions, preventing its before/after contrast from isolating parallelization. Similar control-flow diagrams do not establish common legal permissions, service distributions, resource contention, or measurement validity. Transport requires comparable treatment versions, supported target strata, and evidence that the relevant response mechanisms persist. Reweighting A's case mix alone cannot repair absent support for B's originals, lock, and legal-review configurations.

## 5. Quantitative transport scenarios

Define change as intervention minus concurrent baseline: negative hours indicate improvement. B's reference median is 44 h and p90 is 110 h. The following are conditional planning scenarios, not estimated effects, confidence intervals, or exhaustive bounds. Confidence scores describe the present evidential support for each numerical scenario, not probabilities summing to 100.

Three calculations anchor the scenarios:

1. With fixed, queue-free service times, ready-document sequencing takes \(I+D\), whereas concurrency takes \(\max(I,D)\); the saving is \(\min(I,D)\). Substituting A's reported medians only as representative durations gives a structural saving of 3.5 h before, or 3.1 h using post-period services. This does not explain or identify the observed nine-hour median reduction. B's analogous representative calculation is \(3.4+8.1-8.1=3.4\) h. Actual savings depend on complete distributions and queues.
2. For required originals, a fully serial representative path is \(I+R+D=29.5\) h. Releasing identity and receipt together gives \(\max(I,R+D)=26.1\) h, saving 3.4 h. But if receipt already starts at registration, the baseline may be \(\max(I,R)+D\), also 26.1 h for these values: additional concurrency then saves zero. The relevant comparison must be measured, not inferred from DFG order. Long receipt delays are not removed by either treatment.
3. Let \(e\) be the observed share with ready non-original documents, no mandatory legal review, stable independent inputs, and the initial complexity eligibility. Only \(0\leq e\leq0.38\) is established. Even before readiness and complexity filters, the non-original/non-legal intersection can lie anywhere from 0% to 38%. Assuming independence and treating all non-original cases as ready would give \(0.38\times0.60=22.8\%\), solely an illustrative planning assumption.

For S1, representative direct savings of roughly 0–3.4 h require an additional assumed 1–5 h reduction in internal queues to support a 4–8 h central improvement. The favorable tail range further assumes 8–18 h of removable internal waiting among slow cases; A's observed −18 h makes this a useful stress-test endpoint, not transferable proof. If B's tail is predominantly external receipt time, S1's p90 range fails.

S2 allows some eligible cases to realize most of the 3.4 h representative branch saving, with a small queue benefit or coordination cost. Its overall ranges additionally assume modest spillovers, a smooth distribution around the median, and little eligible mass at the overall p90. An eligibility percentage alone cannot determine an overall quantile change. Under no interference and unchanged untreated outcomes, the appropriate calculation is the mixture \(F_1(t)=eF_{E,1}(t)+(1-e)F_{U,0}(t)\), followed by inversion of that distribution. Multiplying a subgroup median effect by e is invalid.

S3 assumes direct savings survive for ordinary cases while affected slow cases gain 5–15 h of downstream queue time. A synthetic population demonstrates average improvement with tail harm while matching B's two baseline quantiles: 60% take 44 h, 25% take 70 h, and 15% take 110 h. If their changes are respectively −3, −1 and +8 h, the mean changes by \(0.60(-3)+0.25(-1)+0.15(8)=-0.85\) h, from 60.4 to 59.55 h; the median falls from 44 to 41 h, while p90 rises from 110 to 118 h. This is an illustration, not reconstructed case data.

S4 assumes almost no direct saving, central coordination costs up to about three hours, and larger delays for conflicted cases. S5 assumes 1–3 h of potential upstream savings are offset by several hours of added routine queuing, with 10–30 h of extra queue/repetition time among slow cases. These queue increments and quality changes are explicit sensitivity inputs to replace with pilot measurements.

A resource simulation must use arrival rates, available productive calendars, touch-time distributions, external delays, routing probabilities, repeated visits, and supported task ownership. For role r, a useful load diagnostic is \(\rho_r=\lambda E[V_r]E[S_r]/c_r\), when service times and available capacity use compatible units and the factorization is appropriate; otherwise estimate total role work per arriving case directly. The reported service medians cannot be substituted for mean labor demand. Doing so naively would imply 2,890 identity hours per reviewer and about 3,934 document hours per reviewer over six months, even before repeats. This is a warning to resolve elapsed-service versus hands-on-work definitions and calendars, not an estimate of actual utilization. With only aggregates, neither a calibrated queueing model nor a unique transport effect is identified.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

Adopt a staged, readiness-gated release policy. Creating a blocked work item is administrative preparation; it is not permission to execute its activity.

- **Eligibility:** Initially include cases that need no originals and no mandatory Legal Review, have complete usable digital documents at registration, have stable independent verification inputs, and meet prespecified low/moderate-complexity criteria. Confirm supported concurrent ownership and audit logging before enrollment. Route cases with uncertain statutory classification or input dependence through the existing compliant path. Later stages may include originals and legal cases under distinct treatment versions.
- **Concurrent execution:** After Register, release Identity Check and a document-readiness branch. For eligible ready cases, release Document Check alongside Identity Check. For originals cases admitted in a later stage, initiate Originals Receipt and Identity Check independently after Register; release Document Check only after authenticated receipt. Identity and Document Check may overlap if identity is still running when documents become ready. There is no requirement to force overlap when it offers no useful gain.
- **Mandatory gates:** Assessment requires valid completion of both checks against the current relevant inputs. Required originals must precede document execution. Public-sector or high-value classification makes legal review mandatory. Final decision requires the applicable assessment and legal clearance; Notify follows the decision.
- **Queue and WIP controls:** Use oldest-ready-first within declared priority classes, with an aging rule against starvation. Each reviewer may perform only one active hands-on task at a time. Distinguish ready, active, awaiting external input, and completed states. Set assigned-ready workload and pool release limits from a measured shift of productive capacity during run-in, then hold limits fixed during the comparison. Pause additional releases when assessment/legal ready work exceeds its agreed workload ceiling. Dormant originals requests must not occupy an active reviewer slot or hold the whole-case lock. Do not import A's six-task rule without calibration.
- **Responsibilities:** Identity's four reviewers own identity results; seven document reviewers own receipt validation and document checks; three assessors own assessment joins; two legal reviewers own required legal clearance and supplement decisions. Receipt intake is an administrative event unless local measurement shows a separate resource need. Assign one case coordinator for escalation, with multiple supported role-specific work owners. Keep staffing and queue practices comparable between pilot arms.
- **Legal and supplement handling:** Route mandatory cases to Legal Review after Assessment. A supplement request opens a new iteration, waits for the supplement to arrive in the legally required form, then repeats Document Check and Assessment before returning to Legal Review. The routine candidate deliberately repeats both after a legal supplement; any later selective reuse needs explicit validation. Previously valid identity results may be reused only when relevant identity inputs are unchanged. An identity-changing supplement blocks the routine path and triggers a controlled exception with identity re-verification before reassessment. Preserve an audit trail through repeated requests; escalation may manage prolonged cases but cannot waive legal requirements.
- **Logging:** Emit immutable case ID, activity-instance ID, iteration, document/input version, assignment arm/version, baseline eligibility, role/resource, task-created, ready, actual-start, suspend/resume, complete, and recorded-at timestamps. Record receipt evidence, lock conflicts, queue entry/exit, clearance, blocked-start attempts, corrections, outcome and appeal follow-up. Preserve actual event time separately from back-entry time; do not silently rewrite history. Validate clocks and distinguish overlapping elapsed task intervals from simultaneous productive work.

Compact partial-order specification, where `||` means no imposed order, `;` means sequence, and `X_guarded` denotes a choice made by verified case data:

```text
Register ; (Identity Check || DocumentPath) ; Assessment ; LegalPath
         ; X(Approve, Reject) ; Notify

DocumentPath = X_guarded(
    originals required: Originals Receipt ; Document Check,
    no originals and inputs ready: Document Check
)

LegalPath = X_guarded(
    legal review not required: tau,
    legal review required:
        LOOP(
            do   = Legal Review,
            redo = Request Supplement ; Supplement Receipt
                   ; Document Check [new iteration]
                   ; Assessment [new iteration]
        )
)

Mandatory relations:
Register < Identity Check
Register < Originals Receipt                  [when required]
Originals Receipt < Document Check            [when required]
Register < Document Check                    [ready, no-original path]
Identity Check < Assessment
Document Check < Assessment
Assessment < Legal Review                    [when required]
Legal clearance < final decision             [when required]
final decision < Notify
```

The loop executes Legal Review at least once and returns to it after every redo. No order is imposed between identity and the receipt/document branch beyond their common registration prerequisite. The data guards and exception handling are operational requirements; the abstract control-flow model below does not enforce them by itself.

Implementation failure means the assigned policy did not produce its intended executable partial order: tasks remained locked, documents were not actually ready, owners bypassed the system, or recorded overlap reflected back-entry. Audit those first-stage outcomes independently of cycle time. Mechanism failure means supported concurrency genuinely increased, but the joint verification gate did not advance, or earlier readiness failed to improve completion. The first pattern suggests little removable precedence or an originals-dominated critical path. The second suggests assessment/legal congestion, repeated work, or another downstream constraint. Earlier gate completion accompanied by longer downstream waits and rising shared backlog is a bottleneck shift.

Keep common staffing, API, huddles and recording practices stable to make these distinctions interpretable. Compare task release, validated execution overlap, gate readiness, downstream waiting, repeat work and final outcomes in sequence. These are diagnostic mediator analyses, not automatically identified causal decompositions. Combine them with randomized assignment, baseline strata and saturation evidence before expanding. Report whether the tested IT improvement was shared by both arms, since that defines the treatment effect being estimated.

## 8. PM4Py model-construction code

The candidate below models routine, stable-identity cases, including the later originals/legal extension. Its unguarded XOR alternatives form a structural envelope of permitted variants; operational case-data guards must choose the lawful variant. Every activity instance and every silent transition is a fresh object. The partial-order helper explicitly supplies the transitive closure and rejects cycles.

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
    OperatorPOWL,
    StrictPartialOrder,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def strict_order(nodes, precedence_edges):
    """Build a strict partial order; edge endpoints are these exact children."""
    nodes = list(nodes)
    index = {id(node): i for i, node in enumerate(nodes)}
    if len(index) != len(nodes):
        raise ValueError("A node cannot occur twice in the same child list")

    reach = set()
    for source, target in precedence_edges:
        if id(source) not in index or id(target) not in index:
            raise ValueError("Precedence endpoints must be direct child nodes")
        reach.add((index[id(source)], index[id(target)]))

    # Explicit transitive closure: do not depend on a converter adding it.
    for k in range(len(nodes)):
        for i in range(len(nodes)):
            if (i, k) in reach:
                for j in range(len(nodes)):
                    if (k, j) in reach:
                        reach.add((i, j))

    if any(i == j for i, j in reach):
        raise ValueError("Precedence relations contain a cycle")

    model = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=nodes)
    for i, j in sorted(reach):
        model.order.add_edge(nodes[i], nodes[j])
    return model


def xor(*children):
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=list(children))


# Site A: these supplied process trees omit empirical rework and resources.
a_before = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->('Register', 'Identity Check', 'Document Check', 'Assessment', "
    "X('Approve', 'Reject'), 'Notify')"
)
a_after = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->('Register', +('Identity Check', 'Document Check'), 'Assessment', "
    "X('Approve', 'Reject'), 'Notify')"
)

a_before_net, a_before_im, a_before_fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(a_before)
a_after_net, a_after_im, a_after_fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(a_after)


# Site B: initial verification and mandatory AND-join.
register = Transition(label="Register")
identity = Transition(label="Identity Check")
originals = Transition(label="Originals Receipt")

# External guard: skip only when originals are not required and inputs are ready.
# Plain POWL XOR does not inspect the case's statutory/data attributes.
originals_choice = xor(SilentTransition(), originals)
document = Transition(label="Document Check")
assessment = Transition(label="Assessment")

# Legal redo branch. These are fresh instances, not references to initial work.
request_supplement = Transition(label="Request Supplement")
supplement_receipt = Transition(label="Supplement Receipt")
document_repeat = Transition(label="Document Check (supplement)")
assessment_repeat = Transition(label="Assessment (supplement)")

# Supplement Receipt includes validation of originals whenever legally required.
# This routine loop assumes identity inputs remain valid; identity-changing
# supplements require the controlled exception workflow described in the text.
redo = strict_order(
    [request_supplement, supplement_receipt, document_repeat, assessment_repeat],
    [
        (request_supplement, supplement_receipt),
        (supplement_receipt, document_repeat),
        (document_repeat, assessment_repeat),
    ],
)

legal_review = Transition(label="Legal Review")
# A POWL LOOP has two children: do, redo. Its language is do (redo do)*.
# Consequently every supplement round returns to Legal Review before exit.
legal_loop = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[legal_review, redo],
)

# External guard: public-sector OR high-value => choose legal_loop.
# Operational exit from the loop additionally requires legal clearance.
legal_choice = xor(SilentTransition(), legal_loop)
decision = xor(Transition(label="Approve"), Transition(label="Reject"))
notify = Transition(label="Notify")

b_candidate = strict_order(
    [
        register,
        identity,
        originals_choice,
        document,
        assessment,
        legal_choice,
        decision,
        notify,
    ],
    [
        (register, identity),
        (register, originals_choice),
        (originals_choice, document),
        # Both explicit edges are necessary: Assessment waits for BOTH checks.
        (identity, assessment),
        (document, assessment),
        (assessment, legal_choice),
        (legal_choice, decision),
        (decision, notify),
        # No identity -> document or identity -> originals_choice edge:
        # the identity and receipt/document branches may execute concurrently.
    ],
)

b_net, b_im, b_fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(b_candidate)

accepting_nets = {
    "A before": (a_before_net, a_before_im, a_before_fm),
    "A after": (a_after_net, a_after_im, a_after_fm),
    "B candidate": (b_net, b_im, b_fm),
}

print("PM4Py version:", getattr(pm4py, "__version__", "unknown"))
for name, (net, initial_marking, final_marking) in accepting_nets.items():
    is_workflow_net = pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net)
    soundness_result = pm4py.check_soundness(
        net, initial_marking, final_marking
    )
    # PM4Py versions returning diagnostics use (is_sound, diagnostics).
    is_sound = (
        bool(soundness_result[0])
        if isinstance(soundness_result, tuple)
        else bool(soundness_result)
    )
    print(name, "workflow_net=", is_workflow_net, "sound=", is_sound)
    if not is_workflow_net or not is_sound:
        raise AssertionError((name, is_workflow_net, soundness_result))

# Optional untimed structural playout; disabled to keep checks inexpensive.
RUN_PLAYOUT = False
if RUN_PLAYOUT:
    from itertools import islice
    log = pm4py.play_out(b_net, b_im, b_fm)
    for trace in islice(log, 3):
        print([event["concept:name"] for event in trace])

# LIMITS:
# - Plain XOR does not enforce originals/legal eligibility or clearance guards.
# - The net does not encode the 4/7/3/2 resource capacities, scheduling, WIP,
#   task ownership/case locks, service durations, receipt delays, or appeals.
# - It permits concurrency but cannot guarantee actual overlap in execution.
# - It does not enforce input versions or model the identity-change exception.
# - Soundness checks completion/deadlock properties of this structural net;
#   it does not prove statutory compliance, quality, or timely termination.
# - Looping can continue indefinitely even when an exit remains reachable.
# - Playout produces interleavings, not validated concurrent timestamps or
#   calibrated performance predictions. Add guarded execution and a resource/
#   time-aware simulation for those purposes, using audited B measurements.
```

Executed locally with PM4Py 2.7.23.8: all three converted nets passed both workflow-net and soundness checks. This validates the abstract control flow, including concurrent verification and the legal redo path; it does not validate the external guards or predict performance.

## 9. Recommendation

**conditional go**

Transfer the principle of removing unnecessary precedence while retaining required joins. Begin with ready digital claims that require neither originals nor mandatory legal review, have stable independent verification inputs, and meet a defined low/moderate-complexity rule. This subgroup offers the clearest test of whether real concurrency advances verification completion. Its size must be measured: the 38% without an originals requirement is an upper bound, not a confirmed digital-ready population.

Do not copy universal immediate document execution, A's six-task cap, or its nine-hour median gain as a business-case estimate. Original receipt remains a prerequisite where required; mandatory legal review and supplement reassessment remain intact. A's outcome combines possible concurrency gains with an API upgrade, more document capacity, easier cases, and queue huddles. Its slightly higher rework and appeal rates also preclude assuming that speed came without quality risk.

Proceed only after supported simultaneous task ownership, reliable event capture, and input/version gates work in run-in. Keep these foundations and non-treatment practices common across randomized arms. For the first eligible subgroup, a planning range of two to five hours shorter median cycle time is plausible but unproven; the whole-site benefit could be small. Originals delays, three assessors, two legal reviewers, and repeat work make tail congestion a material risk even when the average improves.

Expand only when randomized evidence shows meaningful eligible-case improvement, verified execution fidelity, acceptable tail uncertainty, adequate quality evidence, and no displacement of untreated cases. Test originals cases through concurrent identity/receipt initiation and gated document release; test legal cases as a separate extension. Require evidence at higher treatment saturation before extrapolating to full rollout.

Modify release limits or queue capacity when verification improves but downstream waiting grows. Repair implementation when locks or recording prevent genuine concurrency, then retest. Abandon the proposed release policy in a subgroup if faithful execution yields no useful net benefit, persistent tail harm, or unacceptable repeat work. A statutory breach or serious ownership/version error warrants immediate suspension. Inconclusive quality results justify continued limited observation, not automatic expansion.

## 10. Assumption register

1. **The initial subgroup's checks can use stable, independent inputs** — why needed: concurrent execution must produce valid results without avoidable duplication — how to test: inspect input dependencies and audit identity/document changes in run-in and randomized cases — consequence if false: retain the required precedence for that subgroup or redesign validation before testing concurrency.

2. **Statutory originals and legal-review eligibility can be classified correctly before relevant task release** — why needed: the candidate relies on guarded routing and mandatory prerequisites — how to test: compare classification and receipt/clearance evidence against expert-reviewed case samples, including borderline cases — consequence if false: block uncertain releases, correct rules, and pause affected enrollment.

3. **A measurable initial eligible population exists and later target strata have sufficient support** — why needed: the joint ready-digital/non-original/non-legal/complexity share is unknown, and transport requires overlap in relevant characteristics — how to test: count joint baseline strata in arrival cohorts and examine severity distributions — consequence if false: extend recruitment, narrow claims, or declare the intended subgroup effect unlearned rather than extrapolating from A.

4. **Supported concurrent task ownership can preserve atomic joins and document versions** — why needed: nominal task creation must translate into safe executable overlap — how to test: exercise simultaneous ownership, failure/retry and stale-result cases in run-in and audit production task events — consequence if false: withhold concurrent execution until the platform is repaired; do not rely on back-entry workarounds.

5. **Recorded activity timing can distinguish true execution, readiness, external waiting and data entry** — why needed: mechanism fidelity and queue measurements otherwise become ambiguous — how to test: reconcile immutable timestamps with independent task observations and audit DFG extraction and registration counts — consequence if false: repair measurement before interpreting overlap or duration effects.

6. **Resource demand and repeated visits can be measured well enough to control release and characterize spillovers** — why needed: service medians do not identify workload, utilization or downstream capacity — how to test: collect role calendars, touch time, queue states, arrivals and versioned repeat visits; validate a workload model on held-out periods — consequence if false: retain conservative release limits and defer numerical capacity or whole-site performance claims.

7. **Randomization, common co-interventions and recorded resource sharing preserve an interpretable treatment contrast** — why needed: uncontrolled staffing, API, queue or spillover differences could recreate A's confounding — how to test: audit assignment, baseline balance, concurrent changes, staffing quotas, borrowing and saturation — consequence if false: report the compromised estimand, repair implementation or use a sufficiently supported cluster/saturation experiment before expansion.

8. **Completion, rework and appeals can be followed consistently across arms and target strata** — why needed: apparent speed gains may otherwise reflect selective completion, immature appeals or different outcome definitions — how to test: reconcile all assigned arrivals with open/closed inventories, censoring, audited defects and mature 90-day follow-up — consequence if false: extend follow-up and repair linkage; withhold quality-equivalence and definitive transport claims.
