## 1. Decision rule

The rule is built around one principle: a slot is spent only when the expected causal benefit of the swarm on this case, discounted for the model's known biases, pays for the swarm's direct cost, the customer burden and the harm it does to the untreated queue. It must also beat the option value of keeping the slot. A high `p_now` on its own never qualifies a case.

Working assumptions (these are parameters and should be replaced with measured values as they become available):
- A swarm uses both senior specialists for 45 minutes, so swarms run one after another: 12:00, 12:45, 13:30, 14:15 and so on.
- L_post is the minimum realistic time from `Diagnosis Complete` to `Closed` for a critical incident. I use 90 minutes as a placeholder; it should be the historical median for the incident class.
- Latest feasible start: LFS = deadline − 45 min − L_post.
- Untreated diagnosis-stage cases are worth about €15k on average (range €10–20k). This is a little below the €17.5k median of the listed cases, because alerting cases probably skew toward higher value.
- Base spillover per used slot: S = 6 cases × δ × V_untreated. With δ = 0.02 (range 0.01–0.03) this gives about €1,800 (range €900–2,700). In live operation, S is computed from the actual six most exposed untreated diagnosis-stage cases and a congestion-dependent δ. For example, δ = 0.01 when the diagnosis queue is below its normal level, 0.02 at normal load, and 0.03–0.045 when it is congested.

**Step A – Eligibility gates (all must pass)**
- G1 Stage: the case must be before `Diagnosis Complete` (Opened, Triage or Diagnosis Started), and it must be unlikely to reach `Diagnosis Complete` before the swarm can start. Cases at `Diagnosis Complete` or later are sent to the resolution-stage playbook, never to the swarm queue.
- G2 Time feasibility: the current time must be at or before LFS. If a case fails this gate, its supplied effect range is treated as unrealizable unless a case-specific estimate explicitly accounts for the short horizon.
- G3 Repeat swarm: the case has had no prior swarm. Alternatively, a materially new event has occurred since the last swarm (see Section 4); in that case a duty-manager sign-off and a 50% higher net-benefit hurdle are required.
- G4 Data validity: no key live feature may be imputed. If one is, the case goes into a VERIFY state (a manual check of at most 30 minutes). Until it is verified, it is scored on the more pessimistic of the imputed score and the last valid score.
- G5 Alert validity: the alert must be (a) triggered by a new process event, (b) persistent across at least 2 consecutive refreshes, or (c) urgent, meaning LFS falls within the next 60 minutes. Refreshes without a material new event are merged into the existing alert episode and add no priority.

**Step B – Robustified probabilities.** Each case gets an interval, not a point estimate:

| Segment | Adjustment to p_now | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| SME | +0.04 to +0.09 | backtest underprediction |
| Strategic Enterprise | −0.12 to −0.05 | overprediction after workflow redesign |
| Public Sector | ±0.03 (uncertainty band only) | calibrated, but moderate sample |
| Non-Native-Language Support (NNLS) | ±0.15 (symmetric widening, no directional shift) | small sample; direction of error unknown |
| Imputed key feature | interval spans [p_prev, p_now] until verified | possible artefact |

These adjusted probabilities are used for urgency, false-alarm likelihood and capping effects. They are observational: they are never treated as the probability under intervention, and they are never used as the effect itself.

**Step C – Effect intervals.** The supplied absolute-risk-reduction (ARR) ranges are treated as priors, not as known causal effects. They are adjusted as follows:
- (i) Segments with a known bias are scaled proportionally by p_adj_mid / p_now. This means ×0.85 for Strategic (range 0.75–0.92) and ×1.10 for SME (range 1.07–1.16). The scaling is symmetric, so neither group is treated preferentially.
- (ii) For sparse or uncertain segments (NNLS, unverified imputation), the lower bound is halved.
- (iii) The ARR is capped at 0.8 × p_adj_mid, because a swarm cannot remove more risk than exists.

**Step D – Value.** For each eligible case i:
- Base net benefit: NB_i = ARR_mid × V_i − €450 − B_i − S
- Robust direct benefit: RB_i = ARR_low × V_i − €450 − B_i. This must be at least 0, so that the swarm pays its own direct cost even at a pessimistic effect.

Here V_i is the value of avoiding breach and B_i is the false-alarm burden. The full false-alarm burden is subtracted, which is conservative. Weighting it by (1 − p_adj) would roughly halve it but would not change the ranking.

Non-monetary obligations are handled in two ways. Regulatory incidents have a claim on the reserve slot (Step F). Management can also set an optional per-breach non-monetary weight W to prevent value-starvation (Section 5). I recommend €2–3k, but it is a governance decision, so today's allocation uses W = 0 and I state where it would change the result.

**Step E – Selection and scheduling.**
- Intervene if and only if gates G1–G5 pass, NB_i > 0, RB_i ≥ 0, and the case ranks within the non-reserved slots when ranked by NB_i.
- Near-ties (NB within about €500) are broken in favour of the earlier LFS, because the less urgent case can still compete for later or released slots. Once the learning programme is running, near-ties are broken by a logged lottery instead (Section 6).
- Selected swarms are executed in LFS order, not score order.
- Pre-commit check at swarm start: confirm the case is still before `Diagnosis Complete` and that the latest refresh has not changed its tier. If either check fails, cancel and give the slot to the next case.

**Step F – Reserve.**
- Keep R = 1 slot until 15:00 whenever q × NB_reg exceeds the NB of the marginal candidate. Here q is the probability that the regulatory incident arrives and NB_reg is the value of a swarm on it, including regulatory obligations.
- Even at q ≈ 0.1–0.3 and NB_reg ≈ €10k, the reserve is worth about €1–3k, which exceeds today's marginal candidates (€150–825).
- Reserve override: a live case with NB ≥ €3,000 and RB ≥ 0 may take the reserve.
- If the reserve is unused at 15:00, it is released to the best re-scored eligible case. Several current cases have LFS well after 15:00, so the reserve's opportunity cost is small.

**Step G – Non-swarm tier.** Cases that fail on economics or feasibility but carry real risk get actions that do not use senior capacity: queue-lead review, fast-tracking new evidence to the assigned diagnostician, pre-booking the customer validation window, pre-staging resolution engineers, and proactive customer communication.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Alert episodes.** The first alert on a case opens an episode. Model refreshes every 30 minutes update the episode's risk trend but do not create new alerts, notifications or priority. The number of repeat alerts is never a priority input; this prevents refresh frequency from turning into urgency.

**Material new event.** Any of the following counts; elapsed-time features alone never do:
- a process transition (for example Triage → Diagnosis Started);
- reassignment or a change of resolver group;
- a customer upload, log or new information;
- a severity or priority change, or a new linked or duplicate incident;
- a reopen, or a customer escalation;
- a data correction to a key feature (such as the queue field for I-06);
- a change in adjusted risk of at least 0.10 driven by features other than elapsed time.

**Cool-down.** After a reviewed "wait" decision, re-notification is suppressed for the longer of 60 minutes and 2 refreshes, capped at 25% of the remaining time to deadline. The case stays in the candidate pool with its latest score. It can still receive a released or cancelled slot; suppression affects notification, not eligibility.

**Suppression.**
- (a) Refreshes within an episode where the change in adjusted risk is below 0.05 and there is no material new event are silent.
- (b) Cases at or past `Diagnosis Complete` are removed from the swarm queue and sent to the resolution-stage playbook.
- (c) Cases whose LFS has passed are removed from the swarm queue and sent to non-swarm actions.
- (d) After 3 suppressed refreshes, the case gets one human queue-lead review. This catches stalls and stuck models rather than re-alerting.

**Escalation (breaks the cool-down).**
- A material new event.
- An increase in adjusted risk of at least 0.10 that is not explained by elapsed time.
- A "last call": the case's LFS falls within the next 60 minutes. This gives one final evaluation per episode, so that waiting cases are not silently timed out.
- A regulatory or contractual flag.

**Repeat swarms.** These are blocked by default. They are allowed only if a material new event has occurred since the swarm, the case is still before `Diagnosis Complete`, the re-scored NB clears a hurdle 50% higher, and the duty manager signs off. The case's own post-swarm outcome data are then flagged for effect estimation.

**Re-entry.** A suppressed or waiting case is re-scored from scratch and re-enters the candidate pool on any of the following:
- a material new event;
- a slot release (the 15:00 reserve release, or a cancellation because a planned case reached `Diagnosis Complete`);
- a data fix;
- a new capacity window (for example the next day).

Prior alert count is not carried over as priority. Hysteresis applies: to exit the alert state, adjusted risk must fall at least 0.05 below the trigger level, which prevents flapping.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-based prioritization starving lower-value cases.** Ranking by ARR × V systematically favours large contracts. Today the three highest breach-avoidance-per-slot cases (I-05 ≈ 0.36, I-07 ≈ 0.29, I-01 ≈ 0.27) all go unswarmed, while I-04 is swarmed at about 0.16. That is defensible for one day but corrosive if it happens every day. Safeguards:
- Add a per-breach non-monetary weight W (recommended €2–3k, decided by governance) to every case's value. It reflects contractual, reputational and reporting obligations that are not in V. With W = €3k, I-05 would overtake I-02 today.
- Weekly floor: at least 20% of used slots go to the case with the highest *breaches avoided per slot* among eligible cases with value below the median, provided that case still has RB ≥ 0.
- Track each value tier's share of slots against its share of calibrated, avoidable breach risk. If the ratio falls below 0.6 for any tier over 4 weeks, review W or the floor.

**Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support.**
- Uncertainty that comes from our small validation sample must not become a penalty on the customer. NNLS intervals are widened symmetrically; there is no downward shift and no "exclude if uncertain" rule. That is why I-09 is selected despite the widening.
- Every NNLS swarm includes language support. Otherwise the intervention can fail for reasons unrelated to the case, and the effect estimate would be biased downward, a self-fulfilling "swarms don't help NNLS".
- Suppressed or declined NNLS alerts are double-checked by a human for the first 3 months.
- Collect data deliberately: NNLS cases in the near-tie band are prioritized for the randomized learning programme (Section 6) until the segment has at least 150 labelled untreated cases and at least 40 swarmed cases.

**Differential calibration across segments.**
- Uncorrected Strategic overprediction would double-favour Strategic cases: higher apparent risk combined with higher value. Without correction, I-02, I-04 and I-08 would all look stronger, and I-03 would even top the list.
- Uncorrected SME underprediction does the opposite to SMEs.
- Safeguards:
  - Recalibrate per segment (beta or isotonic calibration with hierarchical shrinkage toward the global curve), carrying intervals rather than point shifts.
  - Apply calibration factors symmetrically to effects, so that corrections do not favour either side.
  - Update segment bias intervals monthly using untreated cases only.
  - Block segment-specific corrections built on fewer than 100 outcome labels; use the widened interval instead.

**Selective intervention changing future training labels.** Swarmed cases breach less *because they were swarmed*. If they are fed back as ordinary labels, the model learns that high-risk patterns are low-risk, and future alerts are suppressed exactly where swarms work. The same cases then relapse, a performative feedback loop. Safeguards:
- Log treatment status, time, stage at treatment and the propensity (the probability of selection under the rule) for every alert.
- Train the breach-risk model on untreated cases and pre-intervention prefixes, with inverse-propensity weighting. Alternatively, model treatment explicitly as an intervention variable and predict the *untreated* potential outcome.
- Keep a frozen shadow model to detect drift caused by the policy itself.
- Maintain a randomized near-tie band, which gives unconfounded comparisons.

**Spillover harms systematically falling on untreated groups.** The swarm's cost is carried by whoever is waiting in the diagnosis queue while seniors are away. If swarms go mostly to Strategic cases, the delay may fall disproportionately on SME, Public Sector or NNLS cases. Today, I-06 (Public Sector) or I-02 is itself one of the exposed untreated cases. Safeguards:
- Log each untreated case's *spillover exposure* (minutes in the diagnosis queue while a swarm is running).
- Cap exposure at 90 senior-absence minutes per untreated case per day. Once a case hits the cap, it gets priority from the backfill diagnostician.
- Always backfill during swarms, and do not start non-urgent swarms under congestion.
- Monthly, compare breach rates of exposed-untreated against matched unexposed cases by segment. If excess breach concentrates in one segment beyond 1.5 times the average excess, increase that segment's weight in S.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Decision logging (prerequisite).** For every alert and decision, record:
- p_now, p_adj interval, ARR prior, NB, RB, S and δ;
- the gate outcomes and reason codes;
- the decision and propensity;
- swarm start and end, and stage at swarm;
- a diagnosis-queue snapshot;
- per-case spillover exposure;
- the final outcome, time to breach, and censoring status.

**Operational monitoring (daily).**
- Share of swarms started before `Diagnosis Complete` (target 100%).
- Swarms cancelled at the pre-commit check.
- Adherence to the 45-minute cap.
- Alert-to-swarm latency against LFS.
- Reserve use rate and regulatory arrival rate.
- Time from swarm to `Diagnosis Complete`.
- Customer complaints attributable to swarms (to validate the false-alarm burden figures).
- Imputation and missingness rates of live features. The queue field must alert its owner within 30 minutes of failing.

**Predictive monitoring (weekly, on untreated cases).**
- Segment calibration-in-the-large, reliability curves and expected calibration error; Brier score.
- Alert precision.
- Episodes per case and repeat-alert noise.
- Input drift (population stability index on key features).
- A shadow-model comparison, to separate genuine drift from drift caused by the policy.
- Trigger: segment calibration error beyond ±0.05 on at least 200 untreated cases in two consecutive 4-week windows leads to recalibration of that segment and an update of its bias interval.

**Causal-effect monitoring.**
- **Estimation.**
  - Near-tie lottery: when the top candidates for a slot have NB within ±€500 (as I-02, I-05 and I-06 are today), a logged randomization decides. This is ethically clean, because expected values are indistinguishable.
  - Outside the band, use doubly robust estimation with the logged propensities.
  - Update ARR priors in a hierarchical Bayesian model by segment and stage, so that NNLS borrows strength from other segments while its own data accumulate.
- **Power.** Detecting a 0.20 ARR at a base risk of 0.6 needs about 95 cases per arm. At 3–4 swarms per day that is weeks overall but months per segment, which is why pooling is needed.
- **Triggers.**
  - If the 80% posterior interval for a segment or stage excludes the supplied midpoint, replace the prior.
  - If the effect at Triage differs from Diagnosis Started by more than 0.08, split eligibility by stage.
  - If the adverse "model wrong" rate exceeds 25% of swarms (the swarm found no actionable issue), tighten G5.

**Capacity and spillover monitoring.**
- Estimate δ by comparing diagnosis waiting times and breach rates of untreated diagnosis-stage cases during swarm windows against non-swarm windows, adjusted for queue length. Randomize the swarm start time within each case's feasible window to get clean contrasts.
- Track specialist utilization, the diagnosis queue length during and after swarms, and the slot shadow price (the NB of the best case left unserved each day).
- Triggers:
  - If estimated δ exceeds 0.03 per slot, raise S, cap daily swarms at 3, or make backfill mandatory.
  - If the reserve is used on fewer than 10% of days over 8 weeks, move the release time to 13:30 or reserve only on days with regulatory signals.
  - If the shadow price is regularly above €3k, make the case for additional senior capacity.

**Fairness monitoring (monthly).** By segment and value tier, track:
- slot share against calibrated avoidable-risk share;
- breach rate of untreated cases;
- spillover exposure minutes and excess breaches among exposed cases;
- suppression and override rates (especially NNLS);
- calibration gaps.

Thresholds are the ones in Section 5: a slot-share ratio below 0.6, or a spillover excess above 1.5 times the average.

**Detecting drift caused by the policy.**
- Watch for a falling observed breach rate among *treated* high scorers alongside stable calibration among *untreated* cases. That is the expected pattern.
- A falling observed rate among untreated high scorers, or a shift in the model's feature importance toward swarm-correlated features, signals label contamination. The response is to retrain on untreated data with propensity weighting and compare against the frozen shadow model.

**Changing the threshold or allocation rule.**
- Parameters (δ, W, segment bias intervals, ARR priors, L_post, reserve release time) change only through a monthly governance review, with versioning and a before/after backtest on logged decisions (off-policy evaluation).
- A change is adopted only if the estimated net benefit improves under all three scenarios, or improves in base without worsening adverse by more than 10%, and no fairness threshold is breached.
- Emergency changes (a data-feed failure, or a spillover spike) can suspend non-urgent swarms immediately, pending review.
