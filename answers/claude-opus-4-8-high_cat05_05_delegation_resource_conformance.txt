## 2. Rule Interpretation

**RR1 (Drafter ≠ Approver).** For a case, `human(Draft_Change_Request) ≠ human(Approve_Change)` after alias normalization. Same human on both = violation regardless of risk.

**RR2 (Implementer ≠ Verifier).** `human(Implement_Change) ≠ human(Verify_Implementation)` after alias/service-account resolution. If the verifier is a service account, the violation depends on the human it attributes to.

**RR3 (HIGH-risk approval authority).** For HIGH-risk cases, `Approve_Change` must be by the QM or a *valid* QM delegate. A delegation is valid only if **all** hold at the event: (a) effective interval covers the event timestamp; (b) scope covers the change (risk + object, e.g. production-equipment vs cooling_system vs training-document); (c) the principal is absent/formally unavailable when the delegation requires it; (d) no prohibited subdelegation was used. Failing any prong voids the delegated authority for that event.

**RR4 (Emergency substitution).** A HIGH-risk *emergency* change may be implemented after approval by the IC or a valid IC delegate, but a QM or valid QM delegate must perform `Retrospective_QM_Review` **within two business days**, and the emergency flag + safety rationale must be credible. **Business-day counting:** start counting from the day after the implementation event; Saturdays, Sundays, and site-calendar holidays are excluded; the deadline is the end of the second business day. The period 08-15/08-16 are weekend days and the site had no holiday, so those days do not count.

**Action by a principal while on leave.** Leave is an HR/availability status, not a revocation of role. A standing QM who acts personally while on leave is *technically authorized by role* (RR3 is satisfied on the authority prong), but leave weakens identity assurance: a login during leave is a red flag that must be corroborated by session evidence (VPN window, MFA, IP). I therefore classify such events as authorized-on-role but flag the identity question separately, rather than assuming misconduct (per the "do not infer misconduct from patterns" instruction).

**Can a later database entry prove an earlier delegation?** No. A delegation must exist and be effective *at the event time*. A QMS DB row created on 08-12 during migration cannot, by itself, establish authority for an 08-11 event. However, an independent contemporaneous artifact (the signed PDF timestamped 08-09 18:00, before the effective interval opened) *can* evidence the delegation, provided the PDF's integrity is trusted. So the delegation stands on the PDF, not on the migrated row; the row is corroborating metadata only and its late creation is a data-quality issue, not proof of forgery.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

Per RR6, repeated pairs and shared terminals are *indicators*, not proof. For each indicator I give a benign and an adverse reading plus a discriminating test.

- **Clara-drafts / Ben-approves (18/23 HIGH prod changes).**
  - Benign: they share a production cell; proximity and topic expertise naturally concentrate work; no formal rotation policy exists to spread it.
  - Adverse: a fixed drafter–approver dyad defeats independent review and could rubber-stamp changes.
  - Discriminator: audit approval latency and rejection rate (do any Ben approvals ever reject Clara drafts?), plus whether outcomes differ from changes reviewed by others.
- **Ben-drafts / Clara-approves (4 LOW training) — reciprocal.**
  - Benign: normal LOW-risk task swapping; no financial benefit known.
  - Adverse: reciprocal approvals ("you sign mine, I sign yours") erode independence.
  - Discriminator: check whether reciprocity ever appears on HIGH-risk cases; review content quality of those approvals.
- **Shared workstation WS-DM-04 (CHG-503 svc_qms).**
  - Benign: a legitimate operator used a shared verify terminal under the service account without re-auth (poor hygiene, not fraud).
  - Adverse: Diego (implementer) verified his own work through the service account → RR2 breach hidden behind svc_qms.
  - Discriminator: session/re-auth logs, badge-out time vs event time, keystroke logs; CCTV (currently unavailable).
- **Asha VPN during leave (CHG-504).**
  - Benign: Asha genuinely handled one urgent case remotely (she says so), within a tight VPN window matching the approval time.
  - Adverse: credential sharing—someone used her account; security cannot exclude it.
  - Discriminator: device fingerprint/MFA method, geolocation of VPN, whether other actions occurred in the same session.
- **Ben account during certified sick leave (CHG-506).**
  - Benign: Ben briefly logged in from the office despite illness.
  - Adverse: impersonation / shared credential (office IP, not home; Ben denies).
  - Discriminator: badge entry to that office workstation, MFA prompts answered, comparison of typing/behavioral biometrics.
- **Concentrated handovers (Clara→Ben; Diego implementations).** Benign: small team; adverse: bottleneck enabling collusion. Discriminator: handover-network centrality vs peer sites and vs a rotation baseline.

No indicator here meets the bar for *proof* of collusion; the strongest concrete integrity problems are the out-of-scope emergency QM reviews (CHG-505, CHG-510), the void subdelegation (CHG-510), and the sick-leave account use (CHG-506).

```python
        and scope in d["scopes"] and ((d["needs_absent"] is None) or asha_absent(rt))
        for d in DELEGATIONS)
    print(c, "reviewer", reviewer, "business_days<=2:", bdays <= 2,
          "valid_QM_reviewer:", valid_qm, "(scope", scope + ")")

# --- Networks (evidence only; NO automatic collusion verdict) -------------
ho = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log_df)
wt = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log_df)
print("\nHandover-of-work edges:", ho)
print("Working-together edges:", wt)

# --- Optional: cases where an approval-like activity is done by diff. resources
try:
    diff_res = pm4py.filter_activity_done_different_resources(log_df, "Approve_Change")
    print("\nApprove_Change done by different resources (cross-case view):",
          sorted(diff_res["case:concept:name"].unique()))
except Exception as e:
    print("filter_activity_done_different_resources not applied:", e)
```

**What the code decides vs. what stays manual.** The code deterministically flags RR1/RR2 SoD via `filter_four_eyes_principle`, evaluates RR3/RR4 authority and delegation validity via explicit interval/scope/absence/subdelegation prongs, and computes RR4 timeliness with business-day logic. Service-account attribution is *explicit data* (`SVC_ATTRIBUTION` with a strength label): only "strong" evidence substitutes a human for automatic SoD; "weak" attributions remain `svc_qms` so they surface for manual review. The handover and working-together networks are emitted as **evidence only** — the script never labels anything "collusion." Judgments left to humans: credibility of the emergency flag and safety rationale, whether Asha was genuinely absent/unavailable on 08-15, whether the 08-13 VPN and office-IP sessions were really the named humans, and the integrity of the signed delegation PDF.

## 6. Control Improvements

- **Delegation creation & effective-time recording.** Require delegations to be registered in the authoritative QMS *before* the effective interval opens, with an immutable, signed, hash-chained record; forbid back-dated or migration-created rows from establishing authority. Store scope as structured fields (risk level + change object) so scope checks are machine-verifiable (this would have caught the cooling-vs-production mismatch in CHG-505/510).
- **Principal availability.** Integrate HR leave/on-call status into the approval workflow so a delegation that requires "principal absent" is auto-validated, and so any login by a principal on leave triggers step-up authentication and an audit note (CHG-504).
- **Alias management.** Maintain a single identity graph mapping all aliases (`u_bk`/`b.koch`) to one human; run SoD checks only on the resolved identity to prevent alias-based self-approval (CHG-508).
- **Individual authentication / service accounts.** Prohibit service accounts from performing GxP-signable steps; require per-event human re-authentication (FIDO) at shared terminals so events like CHG-503 cannot hide the actual human.
- **Emergency review.** Enforce a system-computed 2-business-day clock for `Retrospective_QM_Review`, block closure until a *scope-valid* QM/QM-delegate signs, and require a structured emergency justification with safety rationale.
- **Subdelegation.** Technically enforce "no subdelegation" flags so an attempted onward pass (D-IC-04) cannot be exercised (CHG-510).
- **Pair rotation & network monitoring.** Introduce a drafter–approver rotation policy and monitor handover/working-together centrality and reciprocal-approval ratios as *risk indicators* feeding investigation, not automatic sanctions.
- **Evidential retention.** Retain VPN/MFA/device fingerprints, badge logs, and CCTV for a defined period; the CHG-503 CCTV gap and weak session logs are exactly what block resolution.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

Most of the *apparent* nonconformance splits into three buckets. **Genuine authority failures (highest confidence):** CHG-507 (Eva approved a HIGH change with no QM role and no delegation), CHG-502 and CHG-508 (single-person draft-and-approve, the latter masked by an alias), and CHG-510 (approval on a void subdelegation, plus self-draft/approve). The two emergency retrospective reviews (CHG-505, CHG-510) are also authority failures because Ben's QM delegation is scoped to production equipment and cannot cover cooling-system reviews. **Identity/data-quality problems:** CHG-503 (weakly attributed service-account verify at a shared terminal) and CHG-506 (Ben's account used one hour into certified sick leave, from an office IP, with Ben denying it) — these are not yet proof of misconduct but cannot be cleared without session evidence. **Organizational-pattern risk:** the concentrated Clara→Ben approval dyad and the reciprocal LOW-risk approvals are indicators only and, per RR6, do not by themselves establish collusion.

Net judgment: a meaningful share of the flags are *real control breaks* driven by scope-blind delegation and weak identity binding, layered on top of *fixable data-quality gaps* (late/migrated delegation records, service-account signing). Uncertainty is highest wherever human attribution rests on shared accounts or leave-period logins.

**Three highest-priority investigations:** (1) CHG-506 — determine who actually operated Ben's account during his sick leave (badge/MFA/device forensics); (2) CHG-505 and CHG-510 — resolve the out-of-scope emergency QM reviews and the void D-IC-04 subdelegation, and obtain a valid QM retrospective review or ratification; (3) CHG-503 — establish the human behind the `svc_qms` verification to confirm or clear a potential RR2 (implementer-verifies-own-work) breach.
