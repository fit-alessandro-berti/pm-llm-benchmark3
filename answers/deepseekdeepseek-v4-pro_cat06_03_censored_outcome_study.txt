## 1. Target definition

**Primary target:** Time to 90-day default after account activation, with right censoring, estimated for the population of credit applications that are eventually activated under current policy.

- **Prediction time:** After the third event or 7 calendar days from application start (whichever occurs first), ensuring minimum process visibility.
- **Horizon:** 90 days after `Account Activated` event.
- **Unit of analysis:** Credit application case.
- **Population:** All applications started during the observation window that become activated (the "activated subpopulation").
- **Estimand:** The conditional hazard function of default within 90 days of activation, given process prefix information available at prediction time, among cases that would be activated under the observed credit policy.

**Rationale:** This formulation respects the fundamental reality that default can only be observed for activated accounts. It does not impute counterfactual outcomes to rejected/withdrawn cases and does not treat them as "non-default" for convenience. The survival framework naturally handles right censoring from immature labels and administrative end-of-study. By conditioning on eventual activation, we make the population well-defined without needing untestable assumptions about what would happen to rejected applicants if they were approved.

**Alternative target:** Multi-state outcome for all 48,000 applications with states {Activated & Non-default, Activated & Default, Rejected, Withdrawn, Transferred, Censored/Open}, predicted at the same prefix point.

This answers "what happens to applications" rather than "will activated accounts default." It requires handling competing risks (e.g., rejection precludes default observation) and is useful for operational resource allocation, but it conflates lender decisions with borrower behavior. I choose the primary target because it isolates borrower credit risk from underwriting policy—a cleaner risk construct even though it conditions on policy-mediated activation.

## 2. Cohort construction

**Inclusion rules:**
- Application started between 1 January 2024 and 31 December 2025.
- At least 3 events or 7 elapsed days from start (prefix completeness requirement).
- Case start date allows full 90-day outcome window plus 35-day feed latency before extraction date of 15 February 2026.

**Exclusion rules:**
- Cases starting after 15 November 2025 (cannot mature: 90-day outcome horizon + 35-day feed latency would extend beyond 15 Feb 2026 extraction).
- Cases with no events beyond application start (empty prefix).
- Transferred cases without linkage information (60% linkage coverage means 40% are effectively lost to follow-up).

**Cohort flow table (all 48,000 applications):**

| Cohort segment | Count | Label status | Analysis disposition |
|----------------|-------|--------------|---------------------|
| Activated: default within 90d (mature) | 2,700 | Event observed | Uncensored event |
| Activated: non-default, mature | 18,900 | Censored at 90d | Right-censored (administrative) |
| Activated: outcome immature | 3,600 | Delayed/missing label | Right-censored at extraction |
| Rejected | 9,100 | No default observable | Excluded from primary; used in sensitivity |
| Withdrawn | 5,700 | No default observable | Excluded; competing outcome in sensitivity |
| Transferred (linked, outcome available) | 1,740 | Possibly observable | Excluded from primary; sensitivity analysis |
| Transferred (unlinked, lost) | 1,160 | Permanently missing | Excluded |
| Still open (no decision) | 5,100 | Immature | Excluded; can be used to study selection |

**Primary analysis cohort:** 25,200 cases (2,700 defaults + 18,900 mature non-defaults + 3,600 immature activated cases, with the last group contributing censored observations).

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

**Types of censoring and missingness:**

1. **Administrative right censoring:** Non-default cases reach 90 days without event. Standard survival censoring; non-informative conditional on covariates.
2. **Delayed labels (immature outcomes):** 3,600 activated accounts whose 90-day window plus feed latency extends beyond extraction date. These are right-censored at min(extraction_date - activation_date, 90) days. This assumes label availability is independent of default risk conditional on activation date and observables.
3. **Selection through activation:** Only ~52% of started applications reach activation. The primary analysis conditions on activation, so estimates apply to the activated subpopulation. This is not missingness but a domain restriction that must be transparent.
4. **Transfer as competing event or loss to follow-up:** Transferred cases (2,900) leave the originating system. For the ~60% with linkage, the outcome might be observable; for the remaining ~40%, it is permanently missing. I exclude all transfers from the primary analysis and treat unlinked transfers as potentially informative censoring in sensitivity analysis.
5. **Permanently missing outcome feeds:** Partner-originated cases have elevated risk of missing outcomes even after activation. If partner channel is recorded, I stratify sensitivity analyses by channel and treat partner-originated cases as a separate subgroup.

**Primary analysis strategy:**
Survival analysis (Cox proportional hazards or discrete-time hazard model) from activation, with right censoring for non-defaults at 90 days, for immature cases at current follow-up time, and excluding transfers and non-activated cases. This assumes:
- Censoring is non-informative (testable: compare covariate distributions of early vs. late activations).
- The activation process, while selective, produces a well-defined subpopulation of interest.

**Sensitivity analysis:**
- Inverse probability of censoring weighting (IPCW) for immature outcomes, modeling censoring probability given prefix features and activation date.
- Include linked transfers (where outcome is available) and compare hazard ratios with/without them.
- Bounded estimand approach: assume rejected cases would have default rates between 0.5× and 3× the observed activated rate, and compute bounds on population-level default risk under activation-for-all counterfactual.

**Assumptions assessment:**
- *Testable:* Proportional hazards, covariate balance across early/late activation periods, similar default patterns across channels.
- *Partially testable:* Whether linked transfers have similar default patterns to non-transferred activated cases (test on the 60% linked subset).
- *Untestable:* What would happen to rejected applicants if approved (fundamental causal limitation); whether permanently missing feeds are missing at random.

## 4. Prefix extraction

**Prefix specification:**

I extract prefixes at the earlier of: (a) the 3rd event timestamp, or (b) 7 calendar days after `Application Started`. This captures enough process signal while being early enough for operational value.

**Extraction rules:**

- **Fixed event-count method:** Via `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(log, length=3)`. Cases with fewer than 3 events are excluded unless they reach the 7-day elapsed-time threshold.
- **Elapsed-time method:** Filter events to those with timestamp  (case_start + 7 days). For cases exceeding 3 events within 7 days, the event-count prefix is used to ensure consistent prefix structure.
- **Short cases:** Applications that are rejected, withdrawn, or transferred in fewer than 3 events and less than 7 days are excluded from the primary analysis. They are documented as "early-terminated" and counted in cohort flow.
- **Document loops:** Multiple `Request Documents  Credit Review` cycles are treated as distinct events. The prefix includes all events in the cycle up to the cutoff. Rework count (number of document requests before cutoff) is captured as a feature.
- **Activity renaming (post-15 Oct 2025):** A mapping table standardizes activity names to pre-migration conventions using the earliest available name for each semantic activity. Duplicate events from migration (same timestamp, same activity within 1 second) are deduplicated by keeping the first occurrence.
- **Future events (strict exclusion):** Any event with timestamp after the prefix cutoff is dropped from the feature construction step. This is enforced by a timestamp filter BEFORE any aggregation or feature calculation.
- **Prediction time anchor:** The prefix end time becomes `prediction_time`. The survival clock starts at `activation_time` (which may be after prediction time). This time gap is captured as a feature.

## 5. Process-aware features

**Features available at prediction time (prefix end):**

**Control-flow features:**
- Activity sequence in prefix (encoded as n-gram presence or using learned embeddings from training data only).
- Number of distinct activities seen.
- Whether the prefix includes `Identity/KYC Check`, `Credit Review`, `Request Documents`.
- Variant ID of the prefix trace (using training-set variant mapping).
- Rework indicator: whether `Credit Review` appears more than once in the prefix.
- Last activity in the prefix.

**Timing features:**
- Elapsed time from application start to prediction time.
- Time between application start and first `Credit Review`.
- Waiting time between `Request Documents` and next `Credit Review` (if applicable).
- Day-of-week and hour-of-day of application start.
- Whether the case has exceeded median processing time for its variant up to the prefix endpoint.

**Resource and queue features:**
- Number of distinct resources handling the case.
- Resource identity of the last event (anonymized/encoded).
- Whether the case has ever been assigned to a junior resource (experience proxy at extraction time, adjusted by subtracting elapsed time since event—acknowledged approximation).

**Rework features:**
- Number of `Request Documents` events in the prefix.
- Binary flag: case looped through `Credit Review` more than once.
- Time spent in document-request loops.

**Static case attributes:**
- `case:channel` (origination channel).
- `case:region`.
- `case:requested_amount`.
- `risk_score` value at prediction time (most recent update before or at prefix end).
- `final_limit` — EXCLUDED (populated after final decision; see leakage audit).
- `documents_total` — EXCLUDED if snapshot timestamp is after prediction time (see leakage audit).

**Calendar and cohort features:**
- Application start month and quarter (captures seasonality and policy regime).
- Indicator for pre vs. post 1 July 2025 policy change.
- Indicator for pre vs. post 15 October 2025 system migration (to adjust for potential data quality effects).
- Days since last policy change.

**Features requiring historical reference data:**
- Case's rank ordering by processing time relative to all training cases of the same variant (computed using training-set quantiles only).
- Rolling 30-day approval rate and default rate by channel/region (from training data only, lagged to avoid look-ahead).

## 6. Leakage audit

| Feature or data element | Leakage or bias risk | Valid availability rule | Keep/repair/drop | Rationale |
|-------------------------|---------------------|-------------------------|-----------------|-----------|
| `final_limit` | HIGH: populated after final credit decision, post-acceptance | Must be NULL at prefix end for non-decided cases; only populated after Accept | DROP | Contains future decision information; using it would leak the credit decision outcome into the prefix. Only use `requested_amount` and `risk_score`. |
| `decision_reason` | HIGH: populated at or after Accept/Reject, which post-dates prefix | Only available after terminal decision event | DROP | Directly reveals or strongly predicts the outcome of the credit review process, which is future information at prediction time. |
| `collection_status` | CRITICAL: updated after activation, may explicitly mention default | Never available at prefix end (collections occur post-activation) | DROP with audit check | This field directly leaks the target. Presence in the feature table would be a fatal leakage error. Code must verify it is absent from all prefix-extracted features. |
| Future events (Contract Signed, Account Activated, Collections Contact, Default Recorded) | CRITICAL: these events occur after prediction time | Timestamp > prediction_time | DROP | Core principle of prefix extraction: no event with timestamp exceeding prefix cutoff may enter any feature. Enforced by `events_df['time:timestamp'] <= prefix_end` filter. |
| `documents_total` | MODERATE: nightly snapshot may include same-day documents uploaded after prediction time | Only safe if snapshot timestamp is before or at prediction time | REPAIR: use only if `documents_total` timestamp  prediction_time | Same-day timing creates ambiguity. Repair by comparing document snapshot timestamp to prefix end; if snapshot is later, use previous day's value or set to missing. Document this as a conservative adjustment. |
| `risk_score` (updated versions) | MODERATE: may be updated at events after prediction time | Use most recent `risk_score` value where update timestamp  prefix end | KEEP (versioned): use last value before or at prefix cutoff | Risk scores evolve over the case. Using the version valid at prediction time is appropriate. Explicitly filter `risk_score` updates to those before prefix end. |
| Resource experience | MODERATE: stored as experience at extraction time, not at event time | Subtract elapsed time between event timestamp and extraction date from experience value | REPAIR: approximate historical experience as `experience_at_extraction - (extraction_date - event_date) / 365` | Imperfect repair (doesn't account for part-time, leaves, etc.) but substantially better than using future experience. Note assumption of continuous tenure. |
| `label_available_at` | HIGH: if used as feature, leaks when outcome will be observed | Not available at prediction time (indicates when label feed arrived) | DROP as feature; KEEP only for censoring logic | This timestamp is needed to determine label maturity for survival analysis, but must never enter the model as a predictor. |
| Case duration (total) | CRITICAL: computed from start to terminal event | Cannot be known at prefix end | DROP if used as feature | Only elapsed time from start to prefix end is valid. Total case duration leaks future processing time. |
| Variant frequencies from full dataset | MODERATE: frequencies computed on all data include future cases | Compute variant frequencies using only training data from earlier time periods | REPAIR: use temporally-constrained reference data | Variant prevalence changes over time; computing on full dataset leaks future distribution. Use only training-period cases started before the validation case. |

**Additional audit procedure:** At feature construction time, programmatically verify that for every case, all events used in feature computation have `timestamp  prefix_end_time`. Any violation triggers an error, not a warning.

## 7. Validation design

**Temporal split structure (not random):**

- **Training set:** Cases started 1 January 2024 – 31 March 2025, with labels maturing by 15 February 2026. This provides 15 months of training data with complete outcome windows.
- **Validation set:** Cases started 1 April 2025 – 30 June 2025. Contains pre-policy-change cases with mature labels. Used for hyperparameter tuning and early stopping.
- **Test set 1 (pre-policy-change):** Cases started 1 July 2024 – 30 September 2024. Same policy regime as training, mature labels. Tests generalization within regime.
- **Test set 2 (post-policy-change):** Cases started 1 July 2025 – 15 November 2025. Different policy regime. Tests robustness to policy shift.
- **Backtesting windows:** Rolling 3-month prediction windows with 1-month step size, refitting on expanding window of all prior labeled data.

**Why random split is misleading:**
A random split mixes pre- and post-policy-change cases, obscures temporal drift, risks leaking future policy effects into training (since cases from post-July 2025 share the training set with pre-July cases), and fails to answer "does this model work on tomorrow's applications?" A lender deploying on 1 January 2026 needs to know performance on cases similar to those arriving then—temporal validation directly addresses this.

**Label maturity in validation:**
For test cases with immature labels (started after 15 November 2025), apply right censoring at `min(extraction_date - activation_date, 90)`. Exclude cases from evaluation metrics that require fully observed outcomes unless censoring is properly handled by the metric.

**Evaluation metrics:**
- **Time-dependent AUC (C-index):** Measures discriminative ability across survival times, handles censoring.
- **Brier score at 90 days:** Proper scoring rule for survival predictions, assesses calibration and discrimination jointly.
- **Calibration plots:** Predicted vs. observed default rates at 90 days, by decile of predicted risk.
- **Subgroup performance:** Separate metrics by `case:channel`, `case:region`, policy regime (pre/post July 2025), and risk score terciles.
- **Expected calibration error (ECE):** Quantifies systematic over/under-confidence.
- **Decision-curve analysis:** Net benefit across risk thresholds to assess operational utility.

**Distribution shift monitoring:**
- Track KL divergence between training and test prefix feature distributions.
- Monitor population stability index (PSI) for key features (risk_score, requested_amount, processing times).
- Report performance degradation from Test Set 1 to Test Set 2 as an estimate of policy-change impact.

**System migration handling:**
- Flag cases that span the 15 October 2025 migration date.
- Test whether activity-renamed cases have systematically different predictions or feature distributions.
- If significant, either exclude migration-contaminated cases from evaluation or include an indicator feature for migration-period cases.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

**What the study can claim:**
- Given a credit application that follows the current underwriting process and reaches its third event or seventh day, the model estimates the probability that, if activated, it will default within 90 days of activation.
- The model captures statistical associations between observable prefix characteristics and default outcomes within the historical policy regime.
- Relative risk rankings (case A higher risk than case B) are valid within the activated subpopulation.

**What the study cannot claim:**
- It does NOT predict "the probability that any application will default" because default is undefined for rejected and withdrawn cases.
- It does NOT estimate counterfactual default risk under a different credit policy (e.g., "what if we accepted all applicants?"). Such estimates would require untestable assumptions about rejected applicants' true default propensity.
- It is NOT a causal model. The observed associations may reflect lender selection effects (e.g., loans granted to apparently low-risk applicants who nonetheless default due to unobservables) rather than inherent borrower risk.
- It does NOT generalize to transferred applications that leave the system, especially unlinked transfers with permanently missing outcomes.

**Selection effects vs. predictive association:**
The model learns `P(default | activation, prefix)`, not `P(default | prefix)`. The activation step creates a selected sample: approvees are systematically lower-risk on observables than the full applicant pool. This is a feature of the credit-granting process, not a flaw—but it means the model's risk scores are conditional on passing underwriting. Using these scores to change underwriting (e.g., accepting more marginal applications) changes the selection mechanism, potentially invalidating the model's calibration.

**Three ways deployment could change the data-generating process:**

1. **Selective-acceptance feedback loop:** If the model is used to accept previously-rejected applicants, the composition of the activated population changes. The model was never trained on these cases, and their outcomes may differ systematically from assumptions. Observed default rates in the activated pool may rise, causing model performance to degrade (calibration drift) even if the model's ranking ability remains stable.

2. **Behavioral adaptation:** If loan officers learn that certain prefix patterns trigger high-risk scores, they may change their documentation or review behavior to influence the model's input (e.g., adding more events, requesting documents earlier). This changes the relationship between prefixes and outcomes.

3. **Policy-change confounding:** If the model is deployed alongside the 1 July 2025 policy change, it is impossible to distinguish model impact from policy impact. Performance monitoring must account for contemporaneous policy shifts.

**Decision-use boundary:**
- The model is suitable for EARLY WARNING triage (flagging high-risk activated cases for closer monitoring) but NOT for automated credit decisions on new applications.
- For underwriting decisions, a separate model would need to be developed on a population that includes rejected applicants with some form of outcome imputation or experimentation (e.g., randomized accept/reject pilot).
- Model outputs should be presented as risk categories with confidence intervals, not point predictions, given substantial uncertainty from activation selection and label latency.
- Deployment must include real-time monitoring of prediction distributions, with triggers for retraining if evidence of drift exceeds thresholds.

## 9. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation skeleton

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.util import dataframe_utils
from pm4py.algo.filtering.log.timestamp import timestamp_filter
from datetime import datetime, timedelta
import warnings
warnings.filterwarnings('ignore')

# =============================================================================
# CONFIGURATION
# =============================================================================
DATA_EXTRACTION_DATE = pd.Timestamp('2026-02-15')
OBSERVATION_START = pd.Timestamp('2024-01-01')
POLICY_CHANGE_DATE = pd.Timestamp('2025-07-01')
MIGRATION_DATE = pd.Timestamp('2025-10-15')
TRAIN_END = pd.Timestamp('2025-03-31')
VAL_START, VAL_END = pd.Timestamp('2025-04-01'), pd.Timestamp('2025-06-30')
TEST1_START, TEST1_END = pd.Timestamp('2024-07-01'), pd.Timestamp('2024-09-30')
TEST2_START, TEST2_END = pd.Timestamp('2025-07-01'), pd.Timestamp('2025-11-15')
PREFIX_N_EVENTS = 3  # event-count threshold
PREFIX_DAYS = 7       # elapsed-time threshold
OUTCOME_HORIZON_DAYS = 90
FEED_LATENCY_MAX = 35

# =============================================================================
# 1. LOAD AND PREPARE EVENT DATA
# =============================================================================
# read the CSV; parse timestamps on load
events_df = pd.read_csv('applications_events.csv', parse_dates=['time:timestamp'])
events_df = events_df.sort_values(['case:concept:name', 'time:timestamp'])

# Standardise to PM4Py-expected column names
events_df = dataframe_utils.convert_timestamp_columns_in_df(events_df)
events_df.rename(columns={'case:concept:name': 'case:concept:name'}, inplace=True)

# format for PM4Py: set case, activity, timestamp columns
log_params = {
    'case_id_key': 'case:concept:name',
    'activity_key': 'concept:name',
    'timestamp_key': 'time:timestamp'
}
events_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    events_df,
    case_id=log_params['case_id_key'],
    activity_key=log_params['activity_key'],
    timestamp_key=log_params['timestamp_key']
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. ACTIVITY RENAMING (post-migration standardisation)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# map migration-renamed activities back to pre-migration names
# (assumes a manually curated mapping; here we illustrate the pattern)
ACTIVITY_MAPPING = {
    'KYC_Check_V2': 'Identity/KYC Check',
    'Credit_Review_New': 'Credit Review',
    'Doc_Request_New': 'Request Documents',
    'Contract_Signed_V2': 'Contract Signed',
    'Account_Activated_New': 'Account Activated'
}
events_df['concept:name'] = events_df['concept:name'].replace(ACTIVITY_MAPPING)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. DEDUPLICATE MIGRATION DUPLICATES
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# flag events within 1 second of another event in same case + activity
events_df['time_diff'] = events_df.groupby(
    ['case:concept:name', 'concept:name']
)['time:timestamp'].diff().dt.total_seconds().abs()
duplicate_mask = (events_df['time_diff'] < 1) & (events_df['time_diff'].notna())
events_df = events_df[~duplicate_mask].drop(columns=['time_diff'])
print(f"Removed {duplicate_mask.sum()} duplicate migration events")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. BUILD CASE-LEVEL SUMMARY TABLE
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
case_summary = events_df.groupby('case:concept:name').agg(
    start_time=('time:timestamp', 'min'),
    end_event_time=('time:timestamp', 'max'),
    n_events=('concept:name', 'count'),
    has_activated=('concept:name', lambda x: ('Account Activated' in x.values)),
    has_rejected=('concept:name', lambda x: ('Rejected' in x.values)),
    has_withdrawn=('concept:name', lambda x: ('Withdrawn' in x.values)),
    has_transferred=('concept:name', lambda x: ('Transferred' in x.values)),
    channel=('case:channel', 'first'),
    region=('case:region', 'first'),
    requested_amount=('case:requested_amount', 'first'),
    default_90d=('default_90d', 'max'),  # max aggregates binary flag safely
    label_available_at=('label_available_at', 'first')
).reset_index()

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. DETERMINE PREFIX CUTOFF FOR EACH CASE
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def get_prefix_cutoff(case_events):
    """Return prefix cutoff timestamp: earlier of Nth event or elapsed days."""
    start = case_events['time:timestamp'].min()
    if len(case_events) >= PREFIX_N_EVENTS:
        nth_event_time = case_events['time:timestamp'].iloc[PREFIX_N_EVENTS - 1]
    else:
        nth_event_time = pd.Timestamp.max
    elapsed_cutoff = start + pd.Timedelta(days=PREFIX_DAYS)
    return min(nth_event_time, elapsed_cutoff)

case_cutoffs = events_df.groupby('case:concept:name').apply(get_prefix_cutoff).reset_index()
case_cutoffs.columns = ['case:concept:name', 'prefix_end_time']
case_summary = case_summary.merge(case_cutoffs, on='case:concept:name')

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. COHORT SEGMENTATION
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Determine outcome maturity
case_summary['activation_time'] = np.where(
    case_summary['has_activated'],
    events_df[events_df['concept:name'] == 'Account Activated'].groupby('case:concept:name')['time:timestamp'].min(),
    pd.NaT
)
case_summary['outcome_window_end'] = case_summary['activation_time'] + pd.Timedelta(days=OUTCOME_HORIZON_DAYS)
case_summary['label_mature_date'] = case_summary['outcome_window_end'] + pd.Timedelta(days=FEED_LATENCY_MAX)
case_summary['label_is_mature'] = case_summary['label_mature_date'] <= DATA_EXTRACTION_DATE

# Categorise cases
conditions = [
    case_summary['has_activated'] & (case_summary['default_90d'] == 1) & case_summary['label_is_mature'],
    case_summary['has_activated'] & (case_summary['default_90d'] == 0) & case_summary['label_is_mature'],
    case_summary['has_activated'] & ~case_summary['label_is_mature'],
    case_summary['has_rejected'],
    case_summary['has_withdrawn'],
    case_summary['has_transferred'],
    ~case_summary['has_activated'] & ~case_summary['has_rejected'] & ~case_summary['has_withdrawn'] & ~case_summary['has_transferred']
]
choices = [
    'activated_default_mature',
    'activated_nondefault_mature',
    'activated_immature',
    'rejected',
    'withdrawn',
    'transferred',
    'open_no_decision'
]
case_summary['cohort'] = np.select(conditions, choices, default='unknown')

print("===== COHORT FLOW TABLE =====")
print(case_summary['cohort'].value_counts())
print(f"Total cases: {len(case_summary)}")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. DEFINE PRIMARY ANALYSIS COHORT
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
primary_cohort_mask = case_summary['cohort'].isin([
    'activated_default_mature',
    'activated_nondefault_mature',
    'activated_immature'
])
primary_cases = case_summary[primary_cohort_mask]['case:concept:name'].tolist()
print(f"Primary analysis cohort: {len(primary_cases)} cases")

# Filter events to primary cohort only
primary_events = events_df[events_df['case:concept:name'].isin(primary_cases)].copy()

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 8. TEMPORAL TRAIN/VALIDATION/TEST SPLIT
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def assign_split_group(start_date):
    if start_date <= TRAIN_END:
        return 'train'
    elif VAL_START <= start_date <= VAL_END:
        return 'val'
    elif TEST1_START <= start_date <= TEST1_END:
        return 'test1'
    elif TEST2_START <= start_date <= TEST2_END:
        return 'test2'
    else:
        return 'exclude'

case_summary['split_group'] = case_summary['start_time'].apply(assign_split_group)

# For reference: random split baseline (NOT USED, only for comparison discussion)
# train_idx, test_idx = pm4py.split_train_test(
#     log, train_percentage=0.7
# )  # NOTE: This would ignore temporal structure; shown only as anti-pattern

print("\n===== TEMPORAL SPLIT COUNTS =====")
print(case_summary['split_group'].value_counts())

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 9. PREFIX EXTRACTION (no future leakage)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# For each case, keep only events with timestamp <= prefix_end_time
prefix_events_list = []
for case_id, case_data in primary_events.groupby('case:concept:name'):
    cutoff = case_summary.loc[case_summary['case:concept:name'] == case_id, 'prefix_end_time'].values[0]
    prefix = case_data[case_data['time:timestamp'] <= cutoff].copy()
    prefix_events_list.append(prefix)

prefix_events = pd.concat(prefix_events_list, ignore_index=True)
print(f"\nTotal events in prefixes: {len(prefix_events)}")

# LEAKAGE AUDIT: confirm no future events slipped in
events_with_cutoff = prefix_events.merge(
    case_summary[['case:concept:name', 'prefix_end_time']],
    on='case:concept:name'
)
future_violations = events_with_cutoff[
    events_with_cutoff['time:timestamp'] > events_with_cutoff['prefix_end_time']
]
assert len(future_violations) == 0, f"CRITICAL: {len(future_violations)} future events in prefixes!"

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 10. PM4Py PREFIX EXTRACTION (fixed event-count method)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# convert prefix events to event log
prefix_log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(prefix_events)

# extract prefixes of length PREFIX_N_EVENTS
prefixes_3 = pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(prefix_log, length=PREFIX_N_EVENTS)
print(f"Number of 3-event prefixes extracted: {len(prefixes_3)}")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 11. PROCESS-AWARE FEATURES (selection, demonstrating approach)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# DFG from training-period cases only (no future information leakage)
train_cases = case_summary[case_summary['split_group'] == 'train']['case:concept:name'].tolist()
train_prefix_events = prefix_events[prefix_events['case:concept:name'].isin(train_cases)]
train_prefix_log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(train_prefix_events)

# discover DFG on training data only
dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(train_prefix_log)
print(f"\nDFG discovered: {len(dfg)} edges from training data only")

# variant analysis on training prefixes
variants = pm4py.get_variants(train_prefix_log)
print(f"Number of prefix variants in training: {len(variants)}")

# temporal features (justified: based on prefix timestamps only)
temp_features = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
    prefix_events[prefix_events['case:concept:name'].isin(train_cases)],
    case_id_key='case:concept:name',
```
