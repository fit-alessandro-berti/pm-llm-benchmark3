## 1. Feasibility model

```text
X ≺ Y means X completes by the time Y starts. Subscripts identify evidence rounds.

Register ≺ Triage
Triage ≺ Identity Check
Triage ≺ Document Check₁
Identity Check ≺ Assessment₁
Document Check₁ ≺ Assessment₁
Originals Receipt ≺ Document Check₁                         [originals required]
Assessmentᵢ ≺ Compliance Reviewᵢ                             [if performed in round i]
Assessmentᵢ or Compliance Reviewᵢ issues Request More Infoᵢ  [if evidence is reopened]
Request More Infoᵢ issued ≺ response received ≺ Document Checkᵢ₊₁ ≺ Assessmentᵢ₊₁
Assessment_final ≺ Decision ≺ Notify
Assessment_final ≺ final Compliance Review or reaffirmation ≺ Decision  [high risk or high value]
Triage packet sent ≺ S1 advice                               [K-104]
Packet available ≺ preliminary C1 screen                     [if a screen is performed]
```

```text
Both initial checks must close before the first assessment. Digital checks may overlap.
When originals are required, identity work may proceed before receipt. Document work must wait for the originals it uses.
Each information request reopens the evidence. The next assessment must use the rechecked documents.
Only Assessment or Compliance Review may issue a formal information request. A document checker may flag a defect.
For high risk or high value, C1 must review or explicitly reaffirm the latest assessed evidence. Preliminary work earns no final sign-off.
The final assessor and final compliance reviewer must be different people. M1 signs before notification.
Service intervals on each named resource must be disjoint. Release times, forecasts and provisional reservations do not establish actual service starts.
K-337 forbids a second information request. The recorded decision must dispose of the outstanding discrepancy.
K-411 requires independently owned tasks, real-time service records and a join on the same evidence version.
```

K-104 moves the existing escalation earlier and adds preparation and provisional scheduling. It changes the timing of review and may change the work C1 must do. K-411 changes task release and locking to permit overlap. Both preserve the substantive assessment and decision rules. K-219 changes document routing from D1 to D2, which can change the evidence path. K-337 changes the allowed decision policy by removing an evidence-gathering option.

The single-case comparisons hold other arrivals and commitments approximately fixed, allowing displacement where an intervention requires it. There is a conflict in the supplied logs. D1 serves K-219 on Tuesday 09:30–13:30 inside K-337's Tuesday 08:30–14:30 interval. Each history can anchor its own comparison. A joint replay would need corrected resource or interval data. All times below are illustrative calendar times in the April 6 week unless marked “next week.” Cycle time runs from registration start to notification completion.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism: Triage identified the sanctions issue on Monday. The complex D1 check finished Tuesday 05:40. A1 assessed Tuesday 15:10–19:10, then waited for S1's advice. C1 began Wednesday 13:10. Moving advice earlier can eliminate the post-assessment consultation, but most of the interval to C1 was a wait for service.

Minimal intervention: Keep registration, triage, I1, D1 and A1 at their factual times. At Monday 08:50 send S1 the flagged material and seek a provisional final C1 slot. In one execution S1 advises Monday 09:10–11:10, overlapping I1. C1 uses an available Tuesday 04:00–05:30 interval during D1's check to read the packet and mark findings that require the completed file.

Temporal propagation: Assume Wednesday 13:10 is the final slot retained after reprioritization. A1's Tuesday 19:10 completion releases the assessed file. If 1½ h of the earlier reading carries forward, C1 works Wednesday 13:10–16:10 instead of until 17:40. M1 could sign 16:30–17:00 and notify by 17:10. An unchanged 18:00 signing appointment instead absorbs the entire gain. The proposed pre-read needs a real opening in a heavily booked calendar, especially given Monday's two urgent additions.

Process-feasibility check: The early screen is explicitly preliminary. C1 must reconcile it with D1's completed evidence and A1's assessment and record the final sanctions disposition. A1 and C1 satisfy four-eyes. The screen and final review occupy separate C1 intervals. The reservation either consumes an available opening or displaces another file.

Best/base/worst case: A released Tuesday 19:30–22:30 final slot and a 22:40 M1 start yield notification by 23:20, 19 h 20 min earlier. The base case saves 1 h 30 min and retains approval with conditions. If the packet proves stale, C1 may need all 4½ h again. Losing the hold to an urgent case and signing Thursday 08:30 could put notification at 09:10, 14 h 30 min later. A changed sanctions finding requires a fresh disposition.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: C1 calendar and preemption records could rule out the pre-read or Tuesday final slot. Versioned annotations showing that the final reviewer redid all preliminary reading, or an M1 calendar with only the factual appointment, would remove the base time saving.

### K-219

Factual mechanism: I2 finished Monday 16:20. Originals arrived 17:30, but D1 started Tuesday 09:30 and finished 13:30. A2 assessed Tuesday 20:00–22:30. M1 notified Wednesday 09:00. Two separate waits stand between an earlier document start and an earlier notification.

Minimal intervention: Keep T2, I2 and the external receipt unchanged. Assign D2 at Monday 17:30 and let D2 finish prior commitments. The Monday 16:00 forecast of about 5 h for D2 supports a possible 21:00 start. It does not establish that D2 is free at receipt.

Temporal propagation: At 6 h of service, D2 finishes Tuesday 03:00. The 12½ h earlier start buys 10½ h earlier readiness after allowing for D2's two extra service hours. A2 might take the queued file Tuesday 12:30–15:00 and M1 sign at 15:30, giving notification at 16:00 and a 17 h saving. Earlier eligibility lets the case enter the assessment queue overnight. If the next M1 opening is Wednesday 08:30, the factual notification time still holds.

Process-feasibility check: D2 reads the originals after receipt. A2 assesses only after identity and document checks close. If D2 flags an illegible entry, A2 can issue a formal request. The response must pass a new document check and assessment before M1 decides. This standard, low-risk case has no mandatory C1 stage.

Best/base/worst case: A D2 opening at receipt, a clean 4 h read and early Tuesday assessment and signature could deliver notice by Tuesday 10:00, saving 23 h. The base schedule saves 17 h with the same approval. Alternatively, a Tuesday 15:00 request answered Thursday 03:00, D2 recheck Thursday 10:00–16:00 and A2 reassessment Friday 08:00–10:30 could push notification to Friday 11:30, 50½ h later. An undetected wrong amount could be more consequential than the delay.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Matched service records for handwritten originals could show that difficult scripts erase the queue advantage through repeats or misreads. D2's actual release times, fixed signing rounds and independent rereads of approved files could each overturn the clean-read benefit.

### K-337

Factual mechanism: Keep the history through Friday 10:30, including the first supplier response Wednesday 11:30–Thursday 08:00 and D1's Thursday recheck. A1 still found a discrepancy in Assessment 2. The second response took 70½ calendar hours, Friday 10:30 to next Monday 09:00. D1 and A1 then performed 3½ h and 2½ h of additional service before C1 reviewed.

Minimal intervention: At the close of Assessment 2, record the inconsistent totals, the supported amount and the implications for disposition. Suppress only the second request and its dependent third document check and assessment. Route the assessed record, including the unresolved item, to C1.

Temporal propagation: C1 eligibility moves to Friday 10:30. As an example, if C1 first has room next Monday 10:00–15:00, M1 can sign 15:30–16:00 and notify by 16:10. This saves 41 h relative to next Wednesday 09:10. The avoided supplier wait includes a weekend that C1 and M1 may also be unavailable to work. It cannot simply be deducted from the factual finish.

Process-feasibility check: Assessment 2 by A1 is now final. C1 must review that round after Friday 10:30, document whether exposure can be bounded, and pass its findings to M1. M1 might approve only the independently supported amount if authorized, or reject with reasons if the discrepancy prevents approval. Neither disposition assumes the missing confirmation arrives later as a prerequisite to this decision.

Best/base/worst case: C1 Friday 11:30–15:30 and M1 at 16:00 yield notice at 16:40, 112 h 30 min earlier. The Monday base case might end in a reasoned rejection where confirmation previously secured approval. If only the original next-Tuesday 15:30 C1 slot and next-Wednesday signing round are available, notice gains nothing. Complex review or an appeal can add delay, and an inadequately restricted approval can create loss.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Friday-to-Monday service calendars could eliminate the fast paths. The disputed amount, binding conditions M1 can actually enforce, and blinded adjudication against the subsequently received confirmation would test whether earlier closure changes the correct disposition. Frequent successful appeals would undermine a benefit measured only to first notice.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: Triage released only identity work. I2 finished Tuesday 13:20, after which D2 could take ownership and work 15:00–19:30. The completed file then waited 14 h for A2 to start Wednesday 09:30. A2 finished at 12:00 and M1 notified at 14:30.

Minimal intervention: At Tuesday 09:35 issue two child tasks against one versioned digital file. I2 and D2 each own and sign a distinct result. Record actual work starts and completions as they happen. Keep both service durations and the assess-and-sign policy unchanged.

Temporal propagation: Keep I2 at Tuesday 11:30–13:20. A D2 opening at 12:30, consistent with the 08:00 queue forecast of roughly 4 h for a new arrival, allows service until 17:00. The checks overlap for 50 min. The join advances 2½ h because D2 also avoids waiting for task creation and its later dispatch. If the earlier queue entry earns A2 Wednesday 08:30–11:00 and M1 at 13:00, notification is at 13:30, just 1 h earlier.

Process-feasibility check: Both results must commit against the same evidence version before A2 can start. A version conflict requires reconciliation. Use separate task leases and result records so one worker cannot overwrite the other's work. Distinct I2 and D2 intervals allow genuine overlap without double-booking a worker. M1 signs after A2, with no mandatory C1 review.

Best/base/worst case: A cancellation that gives A2 Tuesday 17:15–19:45 and M1 at 20:00 yields notice at 20:30, 18 h earlier. The base case saves 1 h with the same approval. If A2 and M1 keep the factual appointments, the 2½ h readiness gain produces zero notification gain. A failed version check requiring a fresh read can instead cost a slot.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Server-side work events and task leases might show that one check still waited for the lock despite overlapping entered timestamps. An unchanged distribution of A2 and M1 starts after genuinely earlier joins would reverse the claim of faster notification while preserving the evidence of faster verification.

Consider four separate rollout scenarios, each offering its policy to 100 similar cases during one week. Follow those arrivals and their queue neighbors to completion, including work carried into the next week. Multiplying one case's service hours estimates offered work. Multiplying its hours saved cannot estimate throughput.

With K-104's illustrative 1½ h screen, 100 packets offer C1 150 h of preliminary reading alone. Saving 1½ h on each final review would pay back that reading, yet C1 could still lose usable capacity to expired holds. An urgent arrival can displace a reservation or wait behind it. A single reviewer has at most 168 calendar hours that week before shifts and other duties are considered.

At K-219's base durations, 100 clean switches remove 400 h of offered D1 work and send 600 h to D2. Even a continuously available D2 could not clear that cohort in a week. D1's complex cases could benefit as standard digital cases lose their early openings on D2. Clarification adds another read and assessment. External responses can bunch after a weekend, producing a second arrival peak.

Under K-337, count avoided third passes only for files that would have entered that loop. The freed senior-assessment capacity benefits other files. Earlier referrals can flood C1 before Friday close, transferring weekend waiting from suppliers to the internal queue. Count appeals, later corrections and realized loss alongside first decisions.

K-411 moves existing D2 work forward. A wave of completed pairs can turn A2 into the bottleneck and fill the next M1 signing round. Compare server-timed, version-matched service overlap with independently recorded work and ingestion times. Batch entry alone cannot free capacity. Across all four rollouts, compare backlog, tail waits and completions by priority for treated and neighboring cases, including unfinished work at week end.

## 7. Final synthesis

K-411 is the strongest first intervention. The lock imposes a delay even when two qualified people could examine the same version independently. Releasing both tasks can recover that time without lowering the evidence standard. In the example, verification finishes 2½ hours earlier, yet notification moves only one hour. The operational question is how often an earlier join actually earns an earlier assessment and signature. K-219 is also testable because an independent reader can compare D2's interpretation with the original handwriting.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite its potential speed. The missing supplier response was evidence used to approve the factual case. A rejection on Friday can be procedurally valid and still deny an applicant who would have established eligibility on Monday. A restricted approval is defensible only if the supported exposure can be identified and the restriction enforced. Appeals and losses belong in the comparison even when they arise after notification.

K-104's reserved slot is the least transportable local advantage. One file can advance by taking an opening ahead of others. Giving that privilege to an entire cohort creates no additional certified reviewer time. The decision hinges on reusable reading minutes, expired holds and which urgent cases had to wait.

The most valuable data would link case and evidence-version IDs to queue entry, priority changes, actual service intervals and signing calendars. C1 reservation, release and preemption histories would identify displacement. Task-level work records would verify concurrency. Linking legibility, disputed amounts and later confirmations to corrected decisions, appeals and losses would expose quality costs that a faster first notice can conceal.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural` The stated prerequisites govern all paths. A response reopens document checking and assessment, and any required final C1 review uses the latest assessed evidence.
2. `temporal` The unchanged prefix retains its factual timestamps. Proposed service openings after divergence are scenarios conditional on calendars and priority, including weekend availability.
3. `resource` The named workers stay within their roles and each performs one service at a time. The conflicting Tuesday D1 traces support separate case histories pending reconciliation.
4. `behavioral` S1 and C1 can identify reusable portions of a preliminary packet. C1 revisits them when the completed evidence differs.
5. `quality` Some handwritten originals admit a clean D2 read. Difficult script can trigger clarification or an undetected error whose rate is unknown.
6. `structural` On K-337's available evidence, M1 can issue a reasoned rejection or an enforceable restricted approval after C1 review. The unresolved item remains visible in the record.
7. `resource` K-411's system can isolate task ownership and commit two independently timed results against one version. Conflicts require reconciliation before assessment.
8. `interference` Single-case changes face approximately factual background demand. At rollout, staff capacity is shared, and waits, priorities and rework arrivals respond to the policy.
