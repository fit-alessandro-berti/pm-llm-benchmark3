# 1. Cluster diagnoses

Conventions used throughout: **[O]** observation taken from the supplied rows, **[E]** estimate derived from them, **[B]** bound, **[H]** hypothesis, **[A]** assumption, **[R]** recommendation.

Assumptions (each is needed for the calculations that follow):

- **A1** – Waiting = `start − queue_arrival`, service = `complete − start`, recording lag = `recorded_at − complete`. Needed to turn the fields into measures; it is exactly this definition that fails when `start` or `complete` is a logging time.
- **A2** – A value printed as `HH:MM` is read as `HH:MM:00`. Needed to compute lags in seconds. [O] None of the 14 distinct start/complete instants from the "1 sec" sources (PLC, scanner) carries a non-zero second, whereas their `recorded_at` values do; [H] the extract truncates lifecycle fields to the minute, so their effective precision may be 1 min even where 1 s is documented.
- **A3** – The upstream clock behind `queue_arrival` is approximately aligned with the local clocks. Needed for any waiting time; it is doubtful for G7 and explicitly unverified for the G8 safety event.
- **A4** – Capacity is 4 for RBT-7 and at most 3 for `analyst_pool` (both from the context); every other resource is one person doing one item at a time. Needed for utilization and for the "concurrency above capacity" test; this is precisely what the shared-account scenario in section 5 relaxes.
- **A5** – Under interval censoring the true instant lies within one precision unit of the logged value, rounding direction unknown. Needed for bounds.
- **A6** – Each fragment shows all work of that resource in its window. Needed for utilization; if false, the utilization figures are lower bounds.

**Why G6-like data produces inflated waiting and near-zero service at once.** Only one instant per case is observed, the bulk submission at 17:00, and the form writes it into both lifecycle fields. With A1 the computation gives service = 17:00 − 17:00 = 0 and waiting = 17:00 − arrival, which is the entire sojourn. In reality sojourn = true wait + true service + hold until entry, and only that sum is identified. The same unobserved quantity (service plus hold) is therefore subtracted from service and added to waiting: the two errors are equal in size and opposite in sign, so they do not cancel in any per-component statistic. [B] Each case has wait and service somewhere in [0, 17:00 − arrival]; if T-F worked the seven tickets serially after 14:05, total service is at most 175 min, a mean of at most 25 min. Knock-on effects: utilization of T-F reads 0%, the queue reads up to seven tickets, throughput reads zero for three hours and then seven per minute, and deadline checks report lateness that may be only a recording delay. PM4Py assigns G6 the same category as the physical batch G1.

# 4. PM4Py and pandas analysis

`pm4py.discover_batches` groups events by (activity, resource), merges intervals that overlap or whose gap is at most `merge_distance` seconds, keeps groups of at least `min_batch_size` events, and names the group by exact equality tests in a fixed order: Simultaneous, Batching on Start, Batching on End, Sequential batching, and otherwise Concurrent batching. The last category is a residual and does not require overlap. Queue arrival, recording time, precision, capacity and identity quality play no part, which is why the script adds them in pandas.

def to_ts(s):
    """HH:MM or HH:MM:SS on 2026-05-26; blank -> NaT (blank means 'unavailable', never 'zero')."""

def pm4py_batches(frame, merge_distance, min_batch_size, use_start=True):
    """Returns {cluster: 'PM4Py category(size)'}; a category is a temporal pattern, not a physical claim."""
    ev = pd.DataFrame({
        "case:concept:name": frame["case"].values,
        "concept:name": frame["activity"].values,
        "org:resource": frame["resource"].values,
        "time:timestamp": frame["pm_complete"].values,
        "cluster": frame["cluster"].values,
    })
    if use_start:
        ev["start_timestamp"] = frame["pm_start"].values
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(ev, case_id="case:concept:name", activity_key="concept:name",
                                 timestamp_key="time:timestamp", start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")
    assert len(log) == len(frame), "format_dataframe dropped rows"
    if use_start:  # the simplified interface reads the start column from the dataframe properties
        assert log.attrs.get("pm4py:param:start_timestamp_key") == "start_timestamp"
    res = pm4py.discover_batches(log, merge_distance=merge_distance, min_batch_size=min_batch_size,
                                 activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
                                 case_id_key="case:concept:name", resource_key="org:resource")
    out = {}
    for (_act, _res), _n, by_type in res:
        for btype, batches in by_type.items():
            for _b_start, _b_end, events in batches:
                clusters = sorted({case2cluster[e[2]] for e in events})
                for cl in clusters:
                    out.setdefault(cl, []).append(f"{btype}({len(events)})")
    return {cl: " + ".join(sorted(v)) for cl, v in out.items()}

def n_groups(series, tol_s):
    """Number of equality groups when timestamps closer than tol_s are chained together."""
    v = series.dropna().sort_values()
    return int((v.diff().dt.total_seconds() > tol_s).sum() + 1) if len(v) else 0


def interval_stats(g):
    """Overlap, adjacency, peak concurrency and busy ratio for the intervals of one resource/cluster."""
    iv = g.dropna(subset=["start", "complete"]).sort_values(["start", "complete", "sequence_no"])
    s, e = iv["start"].tolist(), iv["complete"].tolist()
    pairs = list(itertools.combinations(range(len(s)), 2))
    overlap = [min(e[i], e[j]) > max(s[i], s[j]) for i, j in pairs]
    adjacent = [s[i + 1] == e[i] for i in range(len(s) - 1)]
    points = sorted([(t, 1) for t in s] + [(t, -1) for t in e], key=lambda x: (x[0], x[1]))
    cur = peak = 0
    for _, d in points:
        cur += d
        peak = max(peak, cur)
    span_min = (max(e) - min(s)).total_seconds() / 60 if s else np.nan
    busy = iv["service_min"].sum() / span_min if span_min and span_min > 0 else np.nan
    if len(iv) and iv["zero_duration"].all():
        # Zero-length "intervals" carry no overlap/adjacency information: report coincident points only.
        return pd.Series({"overlap_rate": np.nan, "adjacent_rate": np.nan,
                          "peak_conc": int(iv.groupby("start").size().max()), "busy_ratio": np.nan})
    return pd.Series({
        "overlap_rate": np.mean(overlap) if pairs else np.nan,
        "adjacent_rate": np.mean(adjacent) if adjacent else np.nan,
        "peak_conc": peak,
        "busy_ratio": busy,
    })


def queue_stats(g):
    """Queue length = arrived but not yet started (recorded start); evaluated at every recorded start."""
    a, s = g["queue_arrival"], g["start"].dropna()
    qlen = [int(((a <= t) & ~(g["start"] < t)).sum()) for t in s.unique()]
    span = (a.max() - a.min()).total_seconds() / 60
    return pd.Series({
        "q_at_first_start": int((a <= s.min()).sum()) if len(s) else np.nan,
        "q_max": max(qlen) if qlen else np.nan,
        "interarrival_min": span / (len(a) - 1) if len(a) > 1 else np.nan,
        "neg_waits": int(g["start_before_arrival"].sum()),
    })


def cluster_row(g):
    res = g["resource"].iloc[0]
    cap = CAPACITY.get(res, 1)
    seq_sorted = g.sort_values("sequence_no")
    ist, qst = interval_stats(g), queue_stats(g)
    prec_min = g["precision_s"].iloc[0] / 60
    row = {
        "n": len(g),
        "resource": res,
        "start_grp_0/60/900s": "/".join(str(n_groups(g["start"], t)) for t in (0, 60, 900)),
        "end_grp_0/60/900s": "/".join(str(n_groups(g["complete"], t)) for t in (0, 60, 900)),
        "overlap_rate": round(ist["overlap_rate"], 2),
        "adjacent_rate": round(ist["adjacent_rate"], 2),
        "peak_conc": int(ist["peak_conc"]),
        "capacity": cap,
        "busy_ratio": round(ist["busy_ratio"], 2),
        "lag_s_min-max": f"{g['lag_s'].min():.0f}-{g['lag_s'].max():.0f}",
        "bulk_insert": g["recorded_at"].nunique() == 1 and len(g) > 1,
        "rec_monotone_in_seq": bool(seq_sorted["recorded_at"].is_monotonic_increasing),
        "precision_s": int(g["precision_s"].iloc[0]),
        "prec/median_service": round(prec_min / g["service_min"].median(), 2) if g["service_min"].median() > 0 else np.inf,
        "quarter_grid": bool(g["on_quarter_grid"].all()),
        "subminute_used": bool(g["subminute_used"].any()),
        "zero_dur": int(g["zero_duration"].sum()),
        "lifecycle_missing": int(g["lifecycle_imputed"].sum()),
        "mean_wait": round(g["wait_min"].mean(), 1),
        "mean_service": round(g["service_min"].mean(), 1),
        "q_at_first_start": qst["q_at_first_start"],
        "q_max": qst["q_max"],
        "interarrival_min": round(qst["interarrival_min"], 1),
        "neg_waits": int(qst["neg_waits"]),
        "shared_account": res in SHARED_ACCOUNTS,
        "arrivals_in_outage": np.nan,
        "restart_gap_min": np.nan,
    }
    if res in DOWNTIME:
        d0, d1 = (to_ts(x) for x in DOWNTIME[res])
        row["arrivals_in_outage"] = round(g["queue_arrival"].between(d0, d1).mean(), 2)
        row["restart_gap_min"] = (g["start"].min() - d1).total_seconds() / 60
    # Rule cascade: data-quality explanations are tested BEFORE operational ones.
    same_start, same_end = g["start"].nunique() == 1, g["complete"].nunique() == 1
    if row["lifecycle_missing"] == len(g):
        reading = "lifecycle unavailable: keep, exclude from service/waiting"
    elif row["zero_dur"] == len(g) and row["bulk_insert"]:
        reading = "bulk submission / logging batch (no service information)"
    elif row["prec/median_service"] >= 1 or row["neg_waits"] > 0:
        reading = "coarsened timestamps (interval-censored)"
    elif same_start and same_end:
        reading = ("simultaneous, within capacity (physical batch plausible)" if row["peak_conc"] <= cap
                   else "simultaneous but above capacity (artifact suspected)")
    elif same_start:
        reading = "common start + staggered ends (group-start logging vs parallel work)"
    elif same_end:
        reading = "staggered starts + common end (bulk submit vs common release)"
    elif row["overlap_rate"] == 0 and row["adjacent_rate"] == 1:
        if row["arrivals_in_outage"] == 1:
            reading = "serial drain after recorded outage (other clock: offset unverified)"
        elif row["q_at_first_start"] == len(g):
            reading = "serial back-to-back, queue pre-accumulated (burst or unlogged unavailability)"
        else:
            reading = "serial back-to-back"
    elif row["overlap_rate"] > 0:
        reading = ("overlap on shared account (identity unresolved)" if row["shared_account"]
                   else "overlap on one identity (multitasking or logging artifact)")
    else:
        reading = "no batch-like pattern"
    if row["peak_conc"] > cap and "capacity" not in reading:
        reading += " [peak concurrency > capacity]"
    row["pandas_reading"] = reading
    return pd.Series(row)


diag = pd.DataFrame({cl: cluster_row(g) for cl, g in df.groupby("cluster")}).T.reindex(clusters)

# Interval-censored bounds (ASSUMPTION: true instant lies within +/- one precision unit of the logged value).

Reading of the comparison:

- **Parameter sensitivity.** On the recorded data `merge_distance` (0, 60, 900 s) changes nothing, because no cluster contains a gap: intervals overlap or touch, and the type test uses exact equality that the parameter does not relax. `min_batch_size=5` removes G1, G2, G3 and G5 only because they have four events; that is a size filter, not evidence about batching.
- **Fragility of equality.** Shifting the k-th row by k seconds, only in sources with documented precision of 1 min or coarser, turns G2, G3, G6 and G7 into "Concurrent batching", and at `merge_distance=0` G6 is no longer a batch at all. A label that flips under a perturbation far below the stated precision is not a physical finding. G1, G4 and G8 keep their labels because their sources are documented at 1 s and were not shifted.
- **Dependence on lifecycle fields.** Without the start column, G3 becomes "Simultaneous" and G4 and G8 become "Concurrent batching" although they have no overlap. The category describes the fields supplied, not the work.
- **Where the independent diagnostics add information.** G1, G6 and G7 share one PM4Py category but separate cleanly on zero durations, one-shot insertion, precision relative to service time, negative waits and concurrency against capacity. G4 and G8 share a category and all timestamp diagnostics; only the external outage record separates them. G5's label is correct as a pattern but silent on the fact that four concurrent cases exceed three analysts. The mean censored bounds printed by the script are wide exactly where expected: G6 wait and service in [0, 104.4] min, G7 in [0, 13.8] min, against ±1 min or less elsewhere.

**Resource IDs trusted versus treated as shared accounts**

**Same temporal pattern, several physical explanations**

1. **Identical start and identical end (G1, G6, G7).** A fixture batch, a bulk form submission and quarter-hour rounding produce the same pattern and the same PM4Py category. They are separated by precision relative to duration, zero durations, one-shot insertion, negative waits and station capacity, not by the timestamps themselves.
2. **Back-to-back serial intervals behind a pre-accumulated queue (G4, G8).** Ordinary FIFO work after an arrival burst, a deliberate serial batch, a drain after an outage, and a scanner that derives each start from the previous completion are indistinguishable in the lifecycle fields. G8 leans toward the outage only because of an external record on another clock.
3. **Common start with staggered ends (G2).** A group-start button followed by serial inspection, genuinely parallel inspection with individual sign-off, and several inspectors on one tablet all fit.
4. **Staggered starts with a common end (G3).** Bulk submission of finished work, a real folder-level release decision, and reviews kept open concurrently all fit. Which one holds decides whether 11:30 is a logging time or the operational handover.
5. **Irregular overlap on one identifier (G5).** Parallel work by different people, multitasking by one person, and cases left open while idle all fit; the count of four against three analysts shows that parallel people alone is not sufficient.
6. **Waits that are constant or negative (G5, G7).** A fixed dispatch delay, a clock offset between upstream and local systems, and rounding can each produce them.

# 6. Data-collection redesign

Ordered by how much analytical information each item recovers in this data.

1. **[R] Execution timestamps per item.** Capture start and complete when the work happens, per item, from the device or a single tap, not from a form filled in afterwards. Replace the T-B group-start with a group event plus per-item starts; forbid the bulk form from writing its submission time into lifecycle fields; have the scanner emit an explicit start scan and document any derived field. This removes the main defect in G2, G3, G6 and the derived-start doubt in G4 and G8.
2. **[R] Recording timestamps kept separate and typed.** Store `recorded_at` as insertion time only, add `updated_at`, an entry-mode flag (real-time, deferred, bulk) and a submission ID shared by all rows of one bulk entry. This makes logging batches self-identifying and resolves the G5 inconsistency between `sequence_no` and `recorded_at`.
3. **[R] Group/batch identifiers.** Add a physical batch ID with fixture or carrier, slot and batch size (G1), and a separate logical group ID for folders or UI groups (G2, G3). Operational batches and logging batches must never share a field.
4. **[R] Resource and badge identity.** Remove shared logins or add a badge or operator field per event; record whether an identifier denotes a person, a team or a machine (G5, and the open question for T-B).
5. **[R] Source clocks.** Synchronize all systems (NTP or equivalent), store UTC with offset, record the clock source per event and log measured drift. Calibrate the upstream queue system, the legacy MES and the safety system against each other; this is the blocking issue for G8 and a competing explanation in G7.
6. **[R] Timestamp precision metadata.** Record per field the resolution, the rounding rule (floor, ceiling, nearest) and the estimated accuracy, and keep the raw value alongside any rounded one. Document the MES rule or stop rounding. Export seconds where the source has them (A2).
7. **[R] Workstation capacity and availability.** Maintain master data on parallel capacity per station or pool and its changes over time, plus an availability calendar containing safety checks, breaks, shifts and breakdowns on the same clock as the event log. Needed for every utilization figure and for the G4/G8 distinction.
8. **[R] Pause/resume events.** Add suspend and resume transitions with reason codes so that effort can be separated from elapsed time under multitasking (G5), holds before submission (G3) and interruptions.

Until this exists, each extract should carry a per-source reliability note for `start`, `complete` and resource, so that analyses can select measures by field quality.

# 7. Final adjudication

**Interpretable with high confidence**

- **G1**: a physical batch of four on the fixture (80). Usable with batch-aware measures: cycle time counted once, waits read as batch-formation time.
- **G4**: serial one-piece work with credible waits and service (80), subject to confirming how `start` is produced. Whether it is an intended batch or an ordinary queue is undetermined and matters little for performance figures.
- **G6**: a logging batch (90). High confidence in the diagnosis, which means low usability: the lifecycle fields are not execution times.
- **G7**: coarsened timestamps (85). Again a confident diagnosis of unusable resolution.

**Exclude or interval-censor in queue analysis**

- **G6**: exclude from service-time, utilization and queue-length analysis; keep arrival-to-close as an upper bound and waiting as the interval [0, sojourn].
- **G7**: interval-censor start and complete; drop the negative waits from point estimates instead of clipping them; use only at 15 min aggregation or coarser.
- **G2**: keep sojourn and completion times; treat item start, and therefore waiting and service, as censored in [10:00, own completion], with the serial reconstruction as a labelled estimate.
- **G3**: keep waiting; treat work end as censored in [start, 11:30] and report the hold until submission separately.
- **G5**: keep waiting and case-level elapsed time; report utilization only for the pool and never per identifier.

**Operational validation required before any performance conclusion**

- **G8**: measure the offset between the safety clock and the repair queue clock and confirm that the check blocked T-H. Without it, the 48 min mean wait cannot be attributed to availability or to load.
- **G5**: establish how many people used the login in that half hour and whether cases are held open while idle; this decides between pooled parallel work and multitasking.
- **G2**: observe whether T-B inspects grouped items one by one or in parallel.
- **G3**: confirm whether release takes effect at submission and how often folders are submitted in bulk.
- **G4 and G1, lower priority**: confirm the scanner's start logic and the reason for the idle 11:58–12:05; confirm whether the PLC stamps items or fixture cycles.

Across all eight clusters, identical or adjacent timestamps establish a common cause, not common execution. In this data the common cause is physical in one cluster (G1), plausibly operational in two (G4, G8), a property of the recording interface in three (G2, G3, G6), a property of the clock in one (G7) and a property of the identity model in one (G5).
