```text
        v

Shared-queue layer:
flag/manual_review -> U_shared_manual_load -> manual_queue_wait
escalation -> U_senior_load -> escalation_queue_wait
arrival_load -> U_shared_manual_load / U_senior_load
```

Interference arises because manual and escalation queues are shared: a flagged case’s entry changes queue length and therefore other cases’ `manual_queue_wait` and `escalation_queue_wait`; the effect of `P0` is population-level, not individual-level. Exposure-induced confounding occurs when flag-induced manual review creates findings that influence escalation and close, while queue wait after triage is both affected by the flag and affects later review/escalation. Treatment-confounder feedback appears over time: the flag increases review demand, which raises future manual queue wait, which may lead reviewers or managers to adjust routing. `risk_score` and `complexity` are pre-flag confounders; `risk_score` is imperfect, so unobserved underlying risk can drive both the flag and later scrutiny/outcome. These features break simple natural direct/indirect decompositions.

## 3. Stage decomposition

Durations are in minutes and summarized by medians. Optional-stage cells show `median [number of cases in the subgroup with that observed stage]`. Absent stages are not imputed as zero. If no case in the subgroup has the stage, the cell is `NA`. `residual-to-close` is measured from the last completed stage: `escalation_done` if present, otherwise `manual_done`, otherwise `triage_done`.

Flagged cases start triage much sooner (median 12 vs 150 minutes) and have shorter triage service (36 vs 57 minutes), consistent with a priority lane. However, all flagged cases enter manual review and have long triage-done-to-manual-start waits (median 405 minutes); escalated flagged cases have median total cycle times above 1300 minutes. Unflagged cases are not comparable: only selected unflagged cases receive review/escalation, and high-complexity unflagged cases also have long cycles. Thus the observed pattern is consistent with acceleration at triage but delay downstream through routing and queues. These contrasts are not automatically causal because the flag depends on risk score and complexity, manual review and escalation are post-treatment selectors, shared queues create interference, and timestamps record waiting/resource availability as well as processing effort.

## 4. Direct and mediated pathway analysis

| pathway | candidate interpretation | observable evidence | required assumptions | competing explanation | intervention targeting this pathway | expected downstream side effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `flag -> priority_lane -> triage_wait/triage_service` | flag display puts cases into a priority triage lane, reducing time to start and complete triage | flagged median arrival-to-triage-start 12 vs 150; triage service 36 vs 57 | priority assignment is the operative change; triage resources and queue state comparable; no hidden case-mix difference | flagged cases may arrive when triage capacity is available; specific triage resources may be faster | `P1` suppress flag/priority, or keep priority while changing downstream review | removing priority may slow flagged triage; keeping priority may continue to crowd triage resources |
| `flag -> manual_review occurrence` | flag triggers near-automatic manual review, adding a downstream stage | 10/10 flagged cases reviewed vs 4/10 unflagged | flag changes review probability independent of underlying risk and triage findings | high underlying risk/complexity would lead to review even without flag | `P2` remove automatic mandatory review for selected flagged cases | fewer reviews and shorter cycles, but possible loss of detection/quality |
| `flag -> manual_queue_wait` | flagged cases accumulate in shared manual-review queue, causing long wait after triage | flagged triage-done-to-manual-start median 405 vs 105 among reviewed unflagged | queue state, time of day, and reviewer capacity comparable; wait not due to batch scheduling | manual review may be scheduled in batches; high-load periods may drive waits; reviewed unflagged are selected | `P3` protected manual-review capacity, queue segmentation, or service-level target | flagged waits fall, but unflagged waits may rise if capacity is fixed |
| `flag -> manual_service duration` | flagged reviews are more thorough or require more work | manual service medians are similar: 165 flagged vs 180 unflagged; not the largest delay | service duration reflects case work, not queue stoppage or resource idling | unflagged reviewed cases may be harder; complexity confounds service time | checklists, automation, reviewer guidance, or scope reduction | shorter service may reduce thoroughness; scope changes may affect escalation |
| `flag -> escalation occurrence/wait` | flag-induced review or risk perception pushes cases into scarce senior escalation | 6/10 flagged escalated vs 2/10 unflagged; flagged escalated total median 1314 vs 528 non-escalated | escalation criteria are stable; senior capacity independent of flag except through review findings | high underlying risk/complexity causes escalation; senior scarcity creates delays regardless of flag | `P4` change escalation criteria or add senior capacity | fewer or faster escalations reduce cycle time but may harm decision quality |
| `complexity/risk_score -> flag and downstream processing` | flag is correlated with case mix; high complexity/risk cases take longer anyway | High complexity total median 1167 vs Low 264; flagged cases are mostly Medium/High | `risk_score` and `complexity` capture relevant risk; no important unobserved risk | omitted underlying risk drives both flag and long processing | risk-calibrated routing and staffing rather than blunt flag suppression | better targeting may reduce delays, but threshold errors may misclassify risky cases |
| shared-queue interference | flagged cases increase load in review/escalation queues, delaying themselves and others | flagged high-load total median 1086 vs flagged normal-load 735; shared reviewers such as M2 appear in long paths | queue dynamics are stable; observed load proxies capture congestion | time-of-day arrivals, resource shifts, or batch processing create apparent load effects | capacity guardrails, admission control, separate queues, or dynamic prioritization | gains for flagged cases may be offset by losses for unflagged cases |
| reviewer/resource assignment | specific triage or review resources have different speeds, skills, or thresholds | resources vary by path, e.g. T1/T2 for triage, M1/M2/M3 for review; escalated high-complexity cases often use M2 | resource assignment is ignorable conditional on case attributes | resource skill or availability is correlated with case difficulty | cross-training, load balancing, or resource-specific service targets | training and reassignment may reduce short-term throughput |
| decision-quality tradeoff | extra review/escalation may be justified if it changes or improves decisions | reject rate is similar in these small data: 2/10 flagged and 2/10 unflagged; high-risk cases include rejects | observed approve/reject proxies true quality; no unobserved adverse outcomes | underlying risk, not flag policy, drives rejects; small sample hides quality differences | policy changes paired with quality monitoring and adverse-event follow-up | faster processing may increase incorrect approvals or missed risks |

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Does the flag itself cause downstream delay/quality changes near the scoring cutoff? | regression-discontinuity or local-threshold design around the observed flag cutoff, approximately between `risk_score` 0.61 and 0.64 | running variable `risk_score - cutoff`; small bandwidth; adjust for `complexity`, `arrival_load`, hour, triage resource | cases just above/below threshold are comparable; no manipulation of score; stable threshold | covariate balance near cutoff; density test for sorting; placebo cutoffs | discontinuity in complexity, hidden risk, or resources at cutoff would weaken causal interpretation |
| How much delay is due to queue congestion rather than flag routing? | queue-state-aware duration model or discrete-event simulation using derived queue lengths at each stage transition | arrival load, hour, number of open manual reviews, number waiting for senior team, resource shift | queue state is measured well enough; no unmeasured congestion shocks | compare models with and without queue state; residual plots; stratify by high/normal load | if flag effect disappears after conditioning on queue state, delay is mostly congestion-mediated |
| What is the causal effect of the flag policy bundle or its components? | policy-change rollout, shift-level randomized encouragement, or randomized waiver of mandatory review | assignment indicator, baseline risk score, complexity, arrival load, time block | encouragement affects treatment only through policy; compliance measurable; spillovers limited or clustered | compliance rates; balance; spillover tests on unflagged queue waits | large spillovers or noncompliance would require interference-aware estimands |
| Can the effect be decomposed into review occurrence, review waiting, and escalation? | parametric g-formula, sequential g-estimation, or interventional mediation using stochastic mediator distributions | baseline risk score/complexity/arrival load; time-varying queue state; prior stage durations | no unmeasured mediator-outcome confounding conditional on history; consistency; interference modeled or absent | sensitivity analysis for mediator-outcome confounding; placebo mediator; compare simulated stage distributions to observed | strong exposure-induced confounding or unmeasured review findings would invalidate simple decomposition |
| Are observed associations contaminated by hidden bias? | negative-control outcome/exposure tests | same covariates as main model | negative control should be unaffected by the tested pathway | for manual-review capacity changes, arrival-to-triage-start should be null; pre-flag period or far-below-threshold cases as negative exposure | effect on a negative control would indicate confounding or specification error |
| Could unobserved underlying risk explain the flag-cycle-time association? | latent-factor sensitivity analysis or E-value style bounding using outcome, complexity, and risk score as proxies | risk score, complexity, outcome, arrival load | sensitivity model plausibly bounds hidden risk | compare flagged/unflagged cases with similar risk score; assess reject/adverse outcome patterns | a small amount of unmeasured risk sufficient to explain the association would reverse policy confidence |
| What are direct and spillover effects in shared queues? | cluster-randomized or time-block interference design; estimate direct and indirect effects under partial interference | cluster/shift, flagged proportion, queue capacity, resource mix | interference mainly within defined queue/shift clusters; stable queue dynamics | vary cluster size; test whether unflagged waits change with flagged proportion | if unflagged delays exceed flagged gains, system-level recommendation reverses |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0: keep current system | flagged cases receive faster triage | flagged cases enter manual review and escalation queues, increasing downstream load | flagged cycle time remains long; system effect depends on quality benefit | lower immediate miss risk due to scrutiny, but unverified quality benefit | high: flagged cases can crowd unflagged review/senior capacity | strong descriptive evidence, weak causal evidence | keep only as baseline until pilot evidence is obtained |
| P1: suppress flag display, keep risk score visible | flagged cases lose priority triage and slow down at triage | fewer automatic reviews/escalations, but humans may still act on risk score | ambiguous: slower triage but less mandatory downstream processing | high if risk score alone misses important risk; compliance concerns | frees review/senior capacity but burdens triage | weak; not directly observed | not recommended as blanket policy; useful only as diagnostic contrast |
| P2: retain priority triage, remove automatic mandatory manual review | flagged triage remains fast | manual-review queue pressure falls for eligible flagged cases | likely shorter flagged cycle time, especially for Medium/low-risk cases | moderate to high if waived cases contain hidden risk | unflagged review waits may improve if capacity freed | moderate; supported by stage decomposition but not causally identified | preferred limited pilot for carefully selected flagged cases |
| P3: retain routing, add protected manual-review capacity for flagged cases | unchanged; priority triage remains | flagged manual queue wait falls, but protected capacity may reduce capacity for others | flagged cycle time falls if manual wait is bottleneck; system effect uncertain | low if review quality is maintained | negative spillover possible for unflagged cases unless new capacity is added | moderate; queue-wait evidence strong but capacity effect untested | use if manual-review wait is confirmed as main bottleneck and capacity can be added |
| P4: retain manual review, change escalation criteria or capacity | unchanged | senior escalation queue pressure changes; fewer or faster escalations | escalated flagged cases may shorten substantially; non-escalated cases unchanged | high if criteria loosened; possible benefit if senior review improves decisions | senior-team capacity tradeoffs with other high-risk work | low; escalation paths are few and selected | target only after review/escalation criteria are audited |

Selected pilot: `P2`, because it separates priority triage from mandatory manual review.

Eligibility: flagged cases with `risk_score` 0.60–0.79, Medium complexity, no prior rework, and no triage-nurse concern; exclude High complexity or score 0.85. Randomization: after priority triage completion, assign 1:1, stratified by `arrival_load` and hour, to waiver (manual review only if requested) or standard mandatory review. Queue-capacity guardrail: pause waivers if manual-review queue length exceeds its 90th percentile or unflagged median review wait rises >20%; retain protected reviewer capacity for controls and high-risk unflagged cases. Primary outcome: total cycle time. Secondary outcomes: manual-review rate, escalation rate, time to escalation, reopen/adverse return within 7 days, approve/reject distribution, unflagged waiting time, reviewer workload. Minimum process data: all stage start/end timestamps, queue length at triage_done, reviewer/senior IDs, waiver override reason, `risk_score`, `complexity`, `arrival_load`, outcome, and adverse follow-up. Stopping conditions: safety stop for higher adverse/reopen rate in waiver arm; operational stop for guardrail breaches; futility if cycle-time reduction is <30 minutes after 200 cases. This pilot estimates the controlled direct effect of removing mandatory review while preserving priority triage.

```python
            print(f"  {a} -> {b}: {count}")

        print("Slowest performance edges:")
        for (a, b), val in sorted(perf_dfg.items(), key=lambda kv: kv[1], reverse=True)[:10]:
            print(f"  {a} -> {b}: {fmt_perf(val)}")

    warnings.warn(
        "DFG performance differences are descriptive only. They are not causal "
        "effects because flag assignment is confounded, stages are post-treatment, "
        "and shared queues create interference.",
        UserWarning,
    )

except ImportError:
    warnings.warn(
        "pm4py is not installed; the event log and stage summaries were computed, "
        "but DFG discovery was skipped.",
        UserWarning,
    )
```

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. Hidden risk: if cases just above the flag threshold have substantially higher unmeasured risk not captured by `risk_score` or `complexity`, and that risk drives manual review, escalation, and adverse outcomes, then removing mandatory review would be unsafe and the observed delay may be justified.

2. Queue interference: if protected capacity or review waivers increase total system cycle time because unflagged or high-risk cases experience larger queue delays than the gains for flagged cases, then queue-friendly policy recommendations reverse.

3. Decision quality: if the waiver or flag-suppression arms show more incorrect approvals, reopens, complaints, or adverse follow-up events, then speed-focused policies should be reversed in favor of review or escalation safeguards.

4. Timestamp semantics: if `manual_start` means queue entry rather than work start, if local clock shifts occur, or if stage timestamps are batch-entered retrospectively, then the wait/service decomposition and bottleneck interpretation are invalid.

5. Positivity or lack of overlap: if there are no comparable unflagged cases near the flag threshold or within important risk/complexity strata, then local causal contrasts and mediation estimates are not identifiable.

6. Behavior change by reviewers: if reviewers change triage effort, escalation thresholds, or documentation behavior when the flag is suppressed or review is waived, then the assumed policy contrast no longer represents the intended intervention.

## 9. Final synthesis

Directly observed: flagged cases receive faster triage, with median arrival-to-triage-start of 12 minutes versus 150 minutes for unflagged cases, and shorter triage service. Despite this, flagged cases have much longer total cycle times, median 987 versus 345 minutes. All flagged cases enter manual review, and flagged escalated cases have the longest paths. The dominant observed delays are after triage: triage-done-to-manual-start waiting and escalation waiting, not the close residual.

Plausible mechanism: the flag triggers a policy bundle—priority triage, near-automatic manual review, and more frequent escalation into scarce shared queues. Faster triage therefore does not cause longer total time directly; it moves cases into downstream queues where mandatory review and escalation create delay, amplified by case mix and congestion.

Unidentified: the separate causal effects of flag display, priority triage, mandatory review occurrence, review waiting, and escalation are not identified from these observational timestamps alone. Underlying risk, review findings, queue length, and reviewer behavior are partly unobserved, and interference violates simple natural mediation assumptions.

Most useful next evidence: a limited `P2` pilot that retains priority triage but randomly waives automatic manual review for carefully selected medium-risk flagged cases, with queue guardrails and quality monitoring. This directly tests whether manual-review occurrence is the main delay mediator while revealing quality and spillover consequences.
