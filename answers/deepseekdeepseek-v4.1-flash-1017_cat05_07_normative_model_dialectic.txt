## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Stipulated / supported facts.** CS-9 is the formally approved, controlled normative standard from 2025-01-01. SUP-705 followed the FD-7 provisional pattern after FD-7 expired. SUP-714 used Auto_Approve after PD-3 expired, supported only by normalization and belief that permanent approval was imminent. Aggregate evidence reinforces the pattern: 14 cases are clearly PD-3-ineligible, 9 have missing eligibility attributes, and six post-pilot August cases used Auto_Approve.

The prosecution must acknowledge genuine model defects. M0 cannot express Request_More_Info, Auto_Approve, provisional activation, reactivation, rejected-lead pre-screen, label synonyms, or the requester/approver rule. Data collection is also defective: notification mirroring fell to 70%, old CRM links are mutable, migration duplicated SUP-711, and SUP-713’s label differs but appears audit-equivalent. These defects explain some trace deviations and prevent treating every deviation as misconduct. But they do not excuse SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, or the post-expiry continuation in SUP-705. Desirable outcomes, such as SUP-707 obtaining missing certificates before approval or SUP-708 avoiding pre-clearance transactions, do not retroactively amend policy. Normalization of the pilot flow does not create permanent authority. The correct response is to enforce CS-9 while fixing M0 and the data pipeline, not to treat operational convenience as normative.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense case is that M0 is an obsolete and incorrectly scoped artifact, and the operation is being judged against a model that cannot represent key approved or beneficial behavior. M0’s `checks` block permits sanctions and quality concurrency, but it forces both before the decision and cannot express Request_More_Info loops. SUP-707 is not misconduct: missing certificates were obtained before approval, stakeholders regard the behavior as desirable, and Compliance agrees information requests are desirable. M0 simply has no loop. SUP-713 is a label problem: QA_Clearance was an approved synonym for Quality_Review, supported by audit documents showing equivalent content, even if the mapping approval came one week later. SUP-711 is a migration duplicate: the same source transaction ID appears twice, so the duplicate is a data-cleaning defect, not two registrations. SUP-706 is a logging-evidence problem: CRM contains a notification at +3 hours, and the onboarding log’s missing notification is consistent with the known CRM migration gap; the mutable link weakens proof, but the business event is plausible.

The defense also has strong overlay arguments for some cases. SUP-702 is an eligible LOW-risk German supplier in an approved corporate group during PD-3, with sanctions before activation and quality review the next business day; CRM also contains a stable `notification_id`. Assuming PD-3’s monitored-exception authority was valid, this is a justified pilot exception, not misconduct. SUP-704 is within FD-7: critical pump supplier, 2026-07-11, sanctions before activation, EUR 18,000 under the EUR 20,000 cap, daily monitoring exists, and quality plus manual approval occurred the next business day. SUP-709 is a rejected lead that failed sanctions pre-screen and never activated; CS-9 allows an abbreviated pre-screen for rejected leads and does not clearly place them in M0. SUP-710 is a reactivation with prior valid quality certification; CS-9 has no explicit reactivation rule and M0 has no reactivation route, so missing Register and Quality may be a scope defect.

The defense must, however, acknowledge real limits. PD-3 does not justify SUP-703 because the supplier was high-risk, new, foreign, and ineligible. FD-7 does not justify SUP-705 because no written extension exists. SUP-708 is not excused by the absence of transactions; the activation-before-sanctions sequence is exactly what CS-9 forbids. SUP-714 has no valid post-pilot authority. SUP-712 is a genuine segregation-of-duties issue if alias resolution is reliable. SUP-715 raises a real conflict-of-interest control concern even if no explicit relationship prohibition exists, because disclosure was required and no record exists. Informal normalization cannot create permanent policy. The defense therefore supports revision and data repair, but not blanket absolution.

**System-level judgment.** The operation is not uniformly noncompliant, but it is materially noncompliant in specific high-risk and authority-sensitive areas. SUP-703 and SUP-708 are serious breaches. SUP-705, SUP-712, SUP-714, and the post-pilot aggregate show authority drift and normalization of exceptions. At the same time, M0 is invalid as a complete conformance model: it omits RMI, Auto_Approve, emergency/provisional handling, resource rules, rejected leads, reactivations, and label synonyms. The data pipeline also has material defects due to CRM mirroring and mutable links. The correct system judgment is: CS-9 remains normative unless validly amended; PD-3 and FD-7 can justify only their exact, time-boxed, scoped cases; M0 must be replaced by a layered model with policy overlays and data-aware checks.

**Where I reject the prosecution.** I reject the claim that every deviation from M0 is noncompliance. SUP-707 is a model defect, SUP-713 is label equivalence, SUP-711 is a duplicate-data defect, and SUP-706 is not proved noncompliant. SUP-702 and SUP-704 are justified if their overlays are honored. **Where I reject the defense.** I reject the claim that frequency, normalization, operational belief, or desirable outcomes amend CS-9. SUP-703 is ineligible high-risk auto-approval, SUP-708 is prohibited activation-before-sanctions, SUP-705 lacks an emergency extension, SUP-714 is post-pilot, and SUP-712 violates an explicit resource rule if identity is reliable. ## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

**Layer 1 — Scope and case-type rules.** Define separate case types: new supplier onboarding, rejected pre-screen lead, reactivation, withdrawal, pilot, and emergency. CS-9 should state explicitly whether rejected leads are inside or outside M0. Preferred rule: maintain a separate pre-screen intake model; M0-like controls begin after pre-screen acceptance. Reactivations should have a dedicated model requiring sanctions, identity/register check, validity check of prior quality certification, and manual approval if material attributes changed or certification expired. Withdrawals should have an explicit `Withdraw_Application` path with notification/closure rules.

**Layer 2 — POWL control-flow.** The revised POWL should represent Register_Supplier, an optional repeatable Request_More_Info phase, an exclusive choice among standard, low-risk auto, emergency/provisional, and reject routes, followed by Notify_Requester and Close_Case. The standard route requires concurrent sanctions and quality checks before manual approval and activation. The auto route requires sanctions and automated master-data validation before Auto_Approve/activation, with quality before closure or within an authorized due period. The emergency route permits sanctions before provisional activation, then quality and retrospective manual approval before notification/closure. The code in Section 7 implements this approximation.

**Layer 3 — DECLARE / log-skeleton constraints.** Use declarative constraints for data-aware ordering and repeated behavior that POWL cannot naturally express: sanctions must precede activation on standard and emergency routes; quality must precede activation on standard; manual approval must precede activation on standard; notification must precede closure; RMI cycles must be allowed and must be followed by resolution before approval; quality must occur within two business days after provisional activation where overlays allow it; Auto_Approve must be prohibited outside a matching policy overlay; post-expiry overlays must be inactive; no activation may occur before sanctions except on a valid emergency overlay. DECLARE can also encode absence/response constraints for required monitoring and retrospective approval.

**Layer 4 — Pandas/data checks.** Enforce eligibility, amounts, dates, identity, aliases, notification IDs, and policy matching in data checks, not by hiding them in control-flow. Required checks include risk snapshot, country, corporate group, amount cap, cumulative spend, business-day due dates, effective/expiry timestamps, immutable record timestamps, actor alias resolution, delegation evidence, conflict-of-interest disclosure, duplicate source transaction IDs, and CRM notification reconciliation. Policy matching must use the event time and the recorded authority snapshot, not retroactively inferred status.

**Layer 5 — Organizational controls.** Authority to create pilot/emergency overlays must be explicit and documented. CS-9 amendments require the controlled change process. Pilot decisions must specify scope, conditions, monitoring, compliance acknowledgement, sunset date, and conversion rule. Emergencies require a written instrument with start/end, scope, cap, monitoring, retrospective approval, and automatic expiry. Operations cannot extend an expired emergency by asserting facts. Conflict-of-interest disclosure and recusal must be managed organizationally, with identity resolution and delegation evidence retained.

    if len(nodes) == 1:
        return nodes[0]
    spo = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for a, b in zip(nodes, nodes[1:]):
        spo.order.add_edge(a, b)
    return spo
