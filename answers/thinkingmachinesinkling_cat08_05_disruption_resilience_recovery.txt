3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

Resilience is the capacity to maintain mission-critical service **while adapting controls proportionally**, with transparency, recoverability, and a clear path back to conformance. Concealed noncompliance is the appearance of recovery—throughput preserved, orders shipped, cycle-time metrics eventually normalized—while fundamental controls disappear in ways that are invisible to operational dashboards and only partially recoverable after the fact.

In this case, **true resilience** is visible in patterns **A, B, F, I, and J**:
- **A** (Hospital-critical fast lane) protected a 91% fill rate for hospitals, but only becomes true resilience when paired with **B** (manual authorization check). Without B, A degrades into concealed noncompliance.
- **B** maintained eligibility verification using offline resources; it was transparent (staff knew they were in manual mode) and recoverable (lists could be audited).
- **F** applied 100% manual release for controlled substances; this was a deliberate, visible control substitution, not a hidden omission.
- **I** (deferral) and **J** (prioritization) were adaptive capacity-management decisions that were explicit and reversible.

**Risky shortcuts / concealed noncompliance** dominate **C, G, and partially D/E/H**:
- **C** (skip authorization) preserved throughput for ambient pharmacy/wholesale orders but eliminated a non-waivable control. The operational metrics (fill rate, cycle time) did not reveal the gap; only the conformance data did (fitness 0.71, model deviation for missing authorization).
- **G** (dispatch without scan) created the largest traceability gap. The DFG shows “Dispatch without scan  Phone proof of delivery” as a high-volume path (2,600 cases), and the temporal profile shows highly variable delay. Operational metrics showed orders “dispatched” and eventually “delivered,” but the missing scan meant chain-of-custody was broken. After-the-fact audit (L) could not fully restore the real-time traceability state.
- **D** (stale snapshot) was reasonable at T2 onset but became concealed risk as inventory aged; without physical verification, reservations could be false.
- **E** and **H** maintained flow but relied on reconstruction (scans rebuilt later, phone entry delayed). The 9.5% traceability exception rate and the long audit tail (2,300 cases entering remediation in T4) prove that reconstruction is not real-time compliance.

The **ambiguous zone** includes **A without B, E without parallel capture, and K/L themselves**:
- **A** is resilient if authorization is verified manually; it is concealed noncompliance if authorization is skipped entirely.
- **E** is resilient if pickers fill parallel lot-capture sheets; it is concealed risk if they rely solely on post-hoc spreadsheet reconstruction.
- **K and L** are necessary recovery mechanisms, but if management treats “reconciled” or “audit-closed” as equivalent to “never failed,” they become sources of hidden workload and false confidence. The stabilization metrics (fitness 0.90, 76% fitting; rework 5.8%; cancellation 3.0%) are better than T2 but still not baseline, and the audit queue proves that operational “stabilization” masked an ongoing compliance deficit.

The critical abductive interpretation is that **throughput preservation in T2 is not sufficient evidence of resilience**. The hospital fill rate (91%) reflects resilience only because the system still attempted control via B and F; the pharmacy and wholesale throughput that relied on C, G, and unverified D represents concealed noncompliance that manifested later as the 2.2% traceability exception rate, 0.9% missing cold-chain documentation, and the persistent audit queue in T4.

4. Recovery process model

The redesigned model is presented in a structured process-tree notation with explicit conditions. Each node represents a decision or action; branches are labeled by customer criticality (C) and product risk (P).

**Notation explanation:**
- `[Condition]`  branch
- ``  flow
- `||`  parallel evidence capture (must occur simultaneously with main flow, not after)
- `{Artifact}`  mandatory manual record produced
- ``  time-bound reconciliation or scan-back requirement

**Trigger conditions (Declare Incident Level):**
- WMS unavailable >30 minutes **OR** inventory synchronization failure >15 minutes **OR** confirmed cybersecurity event affecting logistics systems.
- Declare **L2 (Manual Mode)** if scanning, reservation, and authorization systems are unavailable; declare **L1 (Partial)** if only scanning/reservation is degraded; declare **L3 (Critical-Only)** if capacity <30% of baseline.

**Triage rules (Receive order  [Triage]):**
```
Receive order
  |
  [Customer Criticality + Product Risk]
  |
   Hospital-critical (C=H)  Emergency Triage
        P  {Controlled-substance, Cold-chain, Recall-sensitive}  Proceed with B (manual auth) + F (100% manual release) + parallel capture
        P = Ambient  Proceed with B (manual auth or pre-authorized emergency list); do NOT use C
  
   Pharmacy-standard (C=P)  Standard / Defer
        Capacity >70% and P = Ambient  Proceed with B
        Capacity <70% or P  {Controlled, Cold-chain, Recall}  Proceed with B + F; if capacity <40%, defer with notification (I)
        P = Ambient, capacity collapsed  Defer to backlog queue (I) with 2h notification
  
   Wholesale-bulk (C=W)  Defer (I)
         Only process if contract-critical or after P1–P4 backlog cleared; never bypass B
```

**Minimum controls by product/customer risk (during L2/L3):**
- **Controlled-substance (all C levels):** 100% manual dual-control (F) documented on signed form; 100% manual authorization (B); manual lot/serial capture on paper (E || {Lot-sheet}); dispatch only with manual courier manifest signed by courier and pharmacist (never “without scan”; replace G with manual manifest); POD requires physical signature or digital courier capture within 4h (replace H with structured capture); temperature/security check documented.
- **Cold-chain:** 100% temperature documentation at pick, pack, and handoff using manual log (F modified); inventory snapshot (D) permitted only if snapshot <12h old and physical bin temperature verified; never dispatch without temperature log entry.
- **Recall-sensitive:** Manual recall-list verification (offline updated list) before pick; if recall match found, immediate hold; lot captured in parallel.
- **Ambient / Pharmacy:** Manual authorization (B) required; snapshot inventory (D) permitted with 24h limit; manual pick list (E) with reconstructed scan permitted only if parallel lot sheet is not required (ambient non-traceable).

**Manual evidence capture (|| parallel):**
Every manual-phase order must generate artifacts before or at the physical step, not reconstructed after:
- `{Auth-form}`: Signed/offline-verified authorization (B).
- `{Inventory-check}`: Timestamped snapshot reference + physical verification signature (D modified).
- `{Pick-list}`: Paper/spreadsheet with handwritten lot/serial for traceable items (E || {Lot-sheet}).
- `{Release-form}`: Pharmacist signature and check result for CS; temperature log entry for cold-chain (F).
- `{Dispatch-manifest}`: Courier signature, order ID, timestamp, temperature (if cold-chain) (replaces G).
- `{POD-capture}`: Structured form with recipient name, time, order ID; for CS, require scanned signature or courier digital capture within 4h (replaces H).

**Backlog prioritization (J formalized):**
After system return (T3/T4), backlog is processed by priority class, not age alone:
```
Priority 1: C=H + P  {Controlled, Cold-chain, Recall-sensitive} (any age)
Priority 2: C=H + P=Ambient
Priority 3: C=P + P  {Controlled, Cold-chain, Recall-sensitive}
Priority 4: C=P + P=Ambient (oldest first within class)
Priority 5: C=W (process only after P1–P4 cleared; notify customers)
```
Deferral notifications (I) must be sent within 2h with expected processing window.

**Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow:**
- **Reconciliation (K):** Automated job runs every 4h in T3/T4, comparing manual reservation artifacts, pick lists, dispatch manifests, and POD captures against system records. Unreconciled cases escalate at 12h. Target: 100% reconciliation within 72h of system return.
- **Audit remediation (L):** Separated from operational metrics. Cases with missing `{Auth-form}`, `{Dispatch-manifest}` scan, `{POD-capture}`, or temperature log enter the audit queue (not the operational backlog). Critical missing evidence (CS auth, cold-chain temperature, recall verification) escalates to Quality/Regulatory within 24h; do not “close” by assumption. Non-critical gaps must be remediated within 14 days. The audit queue size is reported separately from cycle-time metrics to prevent false recovery signals.

5. Resilience indicators

**Throughput and service protection (leading / lagging):**
- **Leading:** Triage activation time (minutes from incident declaration to first emergency order released); Hospital-critical fill rate (real-time hourly, not end-of-day average); Capacity utilization % by segment; Deferral notification rate (% deferred customers notified within 2h).
- **Lagging:** Median/p90 cycle time by customer segment; Cancellation rate by segment; Fill-rate differential (Hospital vs Pharmacy vs Wholesale).

**Traceability (leading / lagging):**
- **Leading:** % manual orders with parallel evidence captured (not reconstructed); Scan-back rate within 4h of system return; Temperature log completeness for cold-chain manual orders; Dispatch manifest completeness.
- **Lagging:** Traceability exception rate (% missing lot/serial/scan/POD); Audit-remediation queue size (absolute count and % of manual-phase cases); % cases with permanently unrecoverable evidence.

**Control completeness (leading / lagging):**
- **Leading:** Manual authorization execution rate (B); Dual-control execution rate for CS orders (F); Temperature documentation rate for cold-chain; Recall verification rate.
- **Lagging:** Model conformance fitness; Percentage of traces fitting normative model; Move-on-model deviation rate (missing release, scan, proof, temperature); Rework rate by root cause (missing control vs physical error).

**Backlog age and reconciliation (leading / lagging):**
- **Leading:** Age of oldest unreconciled manual reservation; Daily reconciliation throughput (cases corrected/day).
- **Lagging:** % manual-phase orders still unreconciled at Day +7; % in audit queue at Day +14; Time from system return to full record closure.

**Fairness across customer groups (leading / lagging):**
- **Leading:** Fill-rate gap between Hospital-critical and Pharmacy-standard; Backlog priority class distribution (are bulk orders being unfairly delayed?).
- **Lagging:** Customer complaint rate by segment; Contract breach rate; Cancellation rate differential (Pharmacy vs Wholesale).

These indicators must be reported on **separate dashboards**: one for operational recovery (cycle time, fill rate, backlog age) and one for compliance recovery (fitness, exception rates, audit queue size, reconciliation completeness). Declaring recovery complete requires both dashboards to return within baseline ranges, not just the operational one.

7. Management trade-off statement

Management **should accept** temporary degradation of pharmacy and wholesale cycle times, the use of manual processes with parallel evidence capture, and the deferral of non-critical orders with explicit customer notification—provided these actions are governed by a visible risk matrix and do not eliminate authorization or traceability for controlled, cold-chain, or recall-sensitive products. Management **should not accept** any adaptation that removes authorization verification entirely (C), that dispatches traceability-critical orders without an equivalent manual chain-of-custody record (G), or that treats reconstructed scans, phone confirmations, or after-the-fact audit closure as equivalent to real-time compliance (E, H, L). Management **must also not accept** declaring operational recovery complete—e.g., “systems are back, backlog is clearing”—while the audit-remediation queue remains elevated, conformance fitness is below 0.95, or cold-chain/controlled-substance exception rates remain materially above baseline (0.4% and 0.2%, respectively). The recommendation to maintain strict manual controls for high-risk products and to separate audit metrics from operational KPIs would change only if there were **independent regulatory confirmation** that temporary relaxation of dual-control or authorization is permissible, or if courier/digital capture technologies could provide legally equivalent real-time traceability during system outages—evidence that is not present in this event log. Until such evidence exists, the recovery design should prioritize transparent control substitution over throughput preservation for high-risk segments.
