# 1. Fairness dimensions

Assessing procedural fairness in a public-benefits administration system requires looking beyond raw outcome parity. While equal approval rates across demographic groups might seem desirable on the surface, differences in underlying eligibility, case complexity, and risk profiles mean that raw outcome equality is neither necessary nor sufficient for fairness. Evaluating the administrative justice and equity of the process requires analyzing multiple distinct dimensions:

1. **Outcome Parity (Raw Outcome Comparison)**: Measures the unadjusted distribution of final decisions across groups (e.g., Group A approval rate of 78% vs. Group D of 58% and Group B of 63%). Raw outcome parity provides an initial signal of differential impact, but fails to distinguish between legitimate differences in program eligibility and systematic administrative bias or structural disadvantage.
2. **Adjusted Outcome Disparity (Conditional Parity)**: Assesses whether applicants with identical observable characteristics and complexity profiles (risk band, completeness, household size, channel, region) achieve the same approval probability. The preliminary adjusted model reveals statistically meaningful negative disparities for Group B (-5.5 percentage points), Group C (-2.2 pp), and Group D (-3.1 pp) relative to Group A, demonstrating that raw case-mix differences do not fully explain final decision gaps.
3. **Procedural Burden (Administrative Frictional Cost)**: Evaluates the number of procedural hurdles, extra evidence collection steps, handovers, and compliance requests imposed on applicants. Group B experiences an adjusted +9.5 pp higher rate of "Request Missing Documents" and Group C/B experience higher supervisor review rates (+5.5 pp and +8.0 pp adjusted), showing that marginalized groups face higher administrative friction and scrutiny before reaching a decision.
4. **Timeliness and Latency Distribution**: Examines cycle times, queue delays, and extreme tail latencies (90th percentile durations). While Group A has a median cycle time of 8.1 days (90th percentile: 21.4 days), Group B faces 13.7 days (90th percentile: 34.2 days) and Group C faces 15.9 days (90th percentile: 38.8 days). Even after covariate adjustment, Groups B and C experience substantial time penalties (+2.6 days and +4.1 days), reflecting systematic structural latency in accessing public entitlements.
5. **Consistency of Routing**: Analyzes whether applicants presenting comparable risk and documentation profiles follow equivalent process pathways. Inconsistency arises when subjective caseworker discretion causes minority language or in-person applicants to be routed to supervisor review or fraud checks more frequently than identical majority-language online applicants.
6. **Quality and Accessibility of Rights-Preserving Support**: Evaluates whether statutory accommodations (language assistance and disability accommodations) facilitate equal access without penalizing applicants. Under the current process, seeking rights-preserving support acts as a structural bottleneck: Language Support Contact introduces a median 3.8-day queue wait, and Accommodation Review introduces a 4.7-day queue wait, creating a "support penalty."
7. **Appeal and Rework Burden**: Assesses the frequency with which applicants must resubmit documentation, undergo rework, or enter formal appeal processes to correct erroneous initial decisions. Group D (16% raw, +5.7 pp adjusted appeal probability) and Group B (13% raw, +4.4 pp adjusted appeal probability) bear disproportionate post-decision burdens, suggesting higher rates of initial administrative error or communication failure.
8. **Resource-Allocation and Capacity Fairness**: Examines the distribution of administrative capacity and caseworker workload across demographic groups. Group A cases are distributed across 52 general caseworkers, whereas Group B and C cases are funneled through severe resource constraints (7 bilingual staff and 5 accommodation specialists), directly generating queueing delays and fragmented multi-party handovers.

# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy

To rigorously isolate procedural discrimination and structural bottlenecks from legitimate complexity-driven routing, the evaluation strategy must account for confounding covariates, non-linear routing dependencies, and unobserved biases.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                  ADJUSTED COMPARISON PIPELINE                                     |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  1. Stratification & Exact Matching                                                               |
|     (Strata: risk_score_band x documentation_completeness x channel x region x prior_cases)       |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                                  |
                                                  v
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  2. Path & Variant Decomposition (PM4Py)                                                          |
|     - Compare variant distributions within strata (e.g., V1 vs V2 vs V4 vs V5)                    |
|     - Test for conditional routing divergence (P(Supervisor Review | Strata, Group))              |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                                  |
                                                  v
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  3. Waiting-Time & Service-Time Decomposition                                                     |
|     - Total Cycle Time = Active Service Time + Stage Queue Waits + Handover Queue Waits           |
|     - Isolate support-step queue latency from actual case evaluation time                         |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                                  |
                                                  v
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  4. Resource-Assignment & Network Analysis                                                        |
|     - Handover of Work Network: measure handover density, cross-role transfers, rework loops      |
|     - Workload / Queue Utilization: calculate case-to-resource ratios per pool                    |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                                  |
                                                  v
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  5. Covariate Sensitivity & Bias Auditing                                                         |
|     - Test endogeneity of risk_score_band and documentation_completeness                          |
|     - Subgroup checks: In-person vs. Online channels; Region-level backlog stratification         |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 2.1. Matching and Stratification Strategy
- **Coarsened Exact Matching (CEM) / Propensity Stratification**: Construct balanced strata on objective baseline attributes:
  - `risk_score_band` (Low, Medium, High)
  - `documentation_completeness` (Complete, Partially Complete, Incomplete)
  - `channel` (Online, In-person, Phone/Paper)
  - `household_size_band` (Single, Small, Large)
  - `prior_case_count` (0, 1-2, 3+)
  - `region` (Regions 1 to N)
  - `submission_cohort_month` (to control for seasonal backlog spikes)
- **Within-Strata Estimators**: Estimate Average Treatment Effects on the Treated (ATT) across groups for approval probability, total cycle time, probability of secondary document requests, and supervisor review escalation.

### 2.2. Path and Variant Comparison
- Measure whether identical strata (e.g., High Completeness + Low Risk + Online) exhibit divergent variant frequencies across groups.
- Compare the probability of achieving the "happy path" (Variant V1: Fast-track approval) versus entering document-request loops (V2) or supervisor review pathways.

### 2.3. Waiting-Time Decomposition
- Decompose total case cycle time \( T_{\text{total}} \) into:
  \[
  T_{\text{total}} = \sum T_{\text{service}}(a_i) + \sum T_{\text{queue}}(a_i \rightarrow a_{i+1}) + \sum T_{\text{handover}}(r_j \rightarrow r_k)
  \]
- Isolate queue waiting time caused by resource starvation in specialized queues (`Completeness Check` \(\rightarrow\) `Request Missing Documents` and `Language Support Contact` \(\rightarrow\) `Eligibility Assessment`) from active case evaluation time.

### 2.4. Resource-Assignment Comparison
- Construct bipartite assignment graphs connecting applicant groups to caseworker pools.
- Measure caseworker congestion, case-to-staff ratios, and the number of distinct organizational handovers per case.

### 2.5. Sensitivity Analysis for Potentially Biased Covariates
- **Endogeneity Bounds**: Because `risk_score_band` and `documentation_completeness` may themselves reflect systemic bias (e.g., minority applicants flagged more frequently due to algorithmic bias or unclear forms), conduct sensitivity analyses:
  1. *Unadjusted baseline vs. Fully adjusted model*.
  2. *Model excluding `risk_score_band`* to check if risk scoring mediates unfair denials.
  3. *Model instrumenting `documentation_completeness`* with language preference and channel to isolate true applicant omissions from language-barrier artifacts.
  4. *Rosenbaum bounds for hidden confounding* to assess how strong an unobserved confounder must be to explain away adjusted disparities.

### 2.6. Subgroup Checks by Region and Channel
- Perform full subgroup analyses stratified by `region` (to verify whether disparities are localized to specific under-resourced regional offices) and `channel` (in-person vs. online), testing for cross-channel friction.

# 3. Path and resource disparities

### 3.1. Justified Disparities (Driven by Legitimate Complexity)
1. **Routing to Fraud Review (Group D)**: 28% of Group D cases follow Variant V3 (Risk Screening \(\rightarrow\) Fraud Review \(\rightarrow\) Supervisor Review), compared to 8–11% for other groups. This is largely explained by Group D’s legitimate case-mix indicators: 51% high risk band and 49% high prior-case count. In the absence of evidence showing fraud reviews are applied arbitrarily within risk bands, routing higher-risk cases to enhanced scrutiny is procedurally standard.
2. **Routing to Accommodation Review (Group C)**: 31% of Group C cases follow Variant V5, directly reflecting that 62% of Group C applicants request disability accommodations. Tailoring procedural steps to applicant-requested accommodations is rights-preserving and legally mandated.
3. **Routing to Language Support (Group B)**: 24% of Group B cases follow Variant V4, aligning with the 57% share needing language support.

### 3.2. Concerning Disparities (Procedural Inequities and Structural Penalties)
1. **The "Support Penalty" (Capacity Starvation in Rights-Preserving Steps)**:
   - Requesting language support or accommodation imposes severe, uncompensated queue delays: Group B waits **3.8 median days** between `Language Support Contact` and `Eligibility Assessment`; Group C waits **4.7 median days** between `Accommodation Review` and `Eligibility Assessment`.
   - These delays occur because 1,250 Group B cases rely on only **7 bilingual caseworkers** (and 3 regional officers), while 820 Group C cases rely on only **5 accommodation specialists**. Meanwhile, 3,600 Group A cases are distributed across **52 caseworkers**.
   - This translates into substantial adjusted cycle time penalties (+2.6 days for B, +4.1 days for C), penalizing applicants purely for exercising their statutory rights to support.
2. **Disproportionate Document Requests (Adjusted Evidence Burden)**:
   - Group B exhibits an adjusted **+9.5 percentage point** higher probability of receiving a `Request Missing Documents` action, even after controlling for baseline `documentation_completeness`, channel, and risk band.
   - This indicates that caseworkers either fail to understand non-standard documentation from minority applicants or default to formal document requests rather than resolving minor ambiguities through language support.
3. **Elevated Supervisor Review Escalation**:
   - Adjusted probabilities of `Supervisor Review` are **+8.0 pp higher for Group B** and **+5.5 pp higher for Group C**. In two regions, accommodation specialists also serve as supervisor reviewers, creating duplicate touchpoints, role conflicts, and extended wait times (1.6 to 1.9 days).
4. **Fragmented Handover Topologies**:
   - Group B and C cases experience ping-pong routing: *General Caseworker \(\rightarrow\) Bilingual/Accommodation Specialist \(\rightarrow\) General Caseworker \(\rightarrow\) Supervisor Reviewer*. Every handover adds queue latency, risk of context loss, and applicant confusion.
5. **Post-Decision Harm (Appeals and Rework)**:
   - Group D has an adjusted +5.7 pp higher appeal rate (16% raw) and Group B has +4.4 pp (13% raw). Group B also has a 22% rework rate (vs. 11% for Group A). This indicates that initial determinations for these groups are frequently flawed, poorly communicated, or lacking necessary administrative assistance, forcing applicants to use the costly appeal mechanism.

# 4. Plausible mechanisms

The observed disparities stem from four primary mechanisms spanning organizational capacity, administrative friction, legitimate policy routing, and systemic feedback loops:

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                      PLAUSIBLE MECHANISMS                                         |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  [Mechanism 1: Structural Resource Bottleneck]                                                    |
|  Rights-preserving steps -> Funneled to 7 bilingual / 5 specialist staff -> Large queue latency   |
|                                                                                                   |
|  [Mechanism 2: Language & Channel Administrative Friction]                                        |
|  Language/in-person barriers -> Premature "Request Docs" (+9.5pp) & Supervisor escalation (+8.0pp) |
|                                                                                                   |
|  [Mechanism 3: Legitimate Complexity Routing]                                                     |
|  High risk score / disability -> Mandatory Fraud Review (V3) & Accommodation Review (V5)          |
|                                                                                                   |
|  [Mechanism 4: Feedback Loops & Upstream Measurement Bias]                                        |
|  Historical surveillance in Risk Band -> Defensive caseworker scrutiny -> Denials -> Appeals     |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Mechanism 1: Structural Resource Bottleneck ("Rights-Preserving Capacity Deficit")
- *Type*: Concerning structural mechanism.
- *Description*: The agency established statutory rights to language assistance and disability accommodation in policy, but failed to allocate proportional staffing capacity. Funneling over 700 language-support cases to 7 caseworkers and over 500 accommodation cases to 5 specialists creates massive queue backlogs. Because overall agency performance targets do not set dedicated SLAs for support steps, these queues accumulate unmonitored delays.

### Mechanism 2: Administrative Friction and Defensive Processing
- *Type*: Concerning behavioral/procedural mechanism.
- *Description*: General caseworkers encountering non-majority language documentation or in-person channel submissions experience communication friction. Instead of directly resolving minor ambiguities, caseworkers engage in "defensive processing"—either issuing formal `Request Missing Documents` notices (+9.5 pp adjusted) or escalating cases to `Supervisor Review` (+8.0 pp adjusted) to shift decision responsibility. This multiplies handovers, extends cycle times, and frustrates applicants.

### Mechanism 3: Legitimate Complexity-Driven Policy Routing
- *Type*: Legitimate policy mechanism.
- *Description*: Program integrity guidelines mandate specialized workflows for objective complexity indicators: high risk scores require fraud screening; requested accommodations require specialist feasibility assessment. Group D's high concentration of high-risk scores (51%) and prior cases (49%) naturally channels 28% of its volume through Fraud Review (Variant V3), inherently extending processing time relative to unencumbered fast-track applications (Variant V1).

### Mechanism 4: Feedback Loops and Algorithmic/Measurement Bias
- *Type*: Concerning systemic/analytical mechanism.
- *Description*: `risk_score_band` and `prior_case_count` may encode historical administrative scrutiny rather than true fraud propensity (e.g., communities subjected to more frequent audits historically accumulate higher prior case records and risk scores). When caseworkers see elevated risk bands, confirmation bias leads to stricter evidentiary standards and higher denial rates, which subsequently generates higher appeal rates (+5.7 pp adjusted for Group D) when initial rejections fail to survive appeal scrutiny.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

Relying solely on the current event log attributes introduces significant analytical limitations that prevent definitive causal attribution of discrimination versus unobserved complexity:

```
+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| Attribute / Dimension             | Specific Limitation & Bias Mechanism                           |
+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| risk_score_band                   | Encodes historical enforcement bias, socioeconomic proxies,    |
|                                   | and systemic surveillance rather than objective fraud.         |
+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| documentation_completeness        | Conflates applicant non-compliance with system inaccessibility |
|                                   | (e.g., forms unavailable in primary language).                |
+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| channel & language_preference     | Coarse categories; misses dialect, digital literacy, assistive |
|                                   | tech barriers, and in-person office waiting conditions.        |
+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| org:resource Workload             | Daily granularity obscures intra-day surges, queue priority,   |
|                                   | caseworker experience, and dual-role conflicts.               |
+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
| case_outcome & appeal_flag        | Binary flags omit decision rationale, explanation quality,     |
|                                   | appeal overturn rates, and dignity of treatment.               |
+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------+
```

1. **Measurement Bias in Risk Scoring (`risk_score_band`)**:
   - Risk scoring algorithms frequently rely on proxies (neighborhood, credit history, prior administrative contact) that correlate with protected demographics.
   - Treating `risk_score_band` as an objective control variable may inadvertently "control away" real systemic discrimination embedded in the risk scoring tool itself.
2. **Endogeneity of `documentation_completeness`**:
   - Completeness is treated as an applicant-level failure, but it is heavily endogenous to the agency's communication quality. If application forms, document checklists, and online portals are poorly translated or inaccessible to screen readers, incomplete submissions reflect administrative failure rather than applicant unresponsiveness.
3. **Unobserved Applicant Needs and Interaction Quality**:
   - The log captures coarse binary flags (`disability_accommodation_requested`, `language_preference`) but lacks clinical or operational detail regarding specific accommodation needs (e.g., ASL interpreter vs. physical accessibility vs. cognitive assistance).
   - Crucially, the log records no data on the dignity of treatment, procedural clarity, or whether caseworkers provided proactive guidance.
4. **Unobserved Resource Workload and Caseworker Discretion**:
   - Caseworker activity is recorded only at daily granularity. The dataset does not track concurrent caseload volume, individual worker experience levels, implicit bias, or the exact queue prioritization rules used by individual staff.
5. **Lack of Appeal Substantive Outcomes**:
   - The dataset records `appeal_flag` and `case_outcome`, but omits whether appeals resulted in **reversals/overturns** or upholdings. A high appeal rate coupled with a high overturn rate is conclusive proof of erroneous initial denials; without overturn data, the administrative error rate cannot be definitively proven.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects

To eliminate procedural disparities without compromising program integrity or removing vital support pathways, the agency should implement targeted administrative and structural reforms:

```
+-------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
| Mitigation Strategy                 | Core Operational Mechanism            | Potential Side Effects & Trade-offs   |
+-------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
| 1. Expand Support Staff Capacity    | Hire/cross-train bilingual and        | Budgetary costs; training overhead;   |
|                                     | disability specialist caseworkers.    | dilution of specialist expertise.     |
+-------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
| 2. Support-Step Specific SLAs       | Establish dedicated queue-wait caps   | Risk of rushed, superficial reviews   |
|                                     | for language and accommodation steps. | to avoid breaching time limits.       |
+-------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
| 3. Integrated Pod Routing           | End-to-end case handling by multi-    | Reduced flexibility in general pool;  |
|    (Eliminate Handover Ping-Pong)   | disciplinary pods (no handovers).     | uneven workload spikes across pods.   |
+-------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
| 4. Plain-Language & Multilingual    | Clear, actionable notices with        | Upfront drafting/IT costs; longer     |
|    Explanation Notices              | proactive pre-denial outreach.        | initial drafting time per notice.     |
+-------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
| 5. Continuous Disparity Dashboard   | Real-time PM4Py tracking of adjusted  | Metric fixation / gaming; alert       |
|                                     | cycle times, handovers, and appeals.  | fatigue among managers.               |
+-------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
| 6. Algorithmic Audit of Risk & Form | Retrain risk models; redesign forms   | Short-term operational disruption;    |
|    Completeness Standards           | for readability and accessibility.    | potential temporary fraud risk.       |
+-------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
```

### 6.1. Add Capacity for Language Support and Accommodation Review
- **Action**: Increase the staffing of bilingual caseworkers from 7 to 25 and accommodation specialists from 5 to 18, reflecting true demographic demand. Cross-train general caseworkers to handle basic language routing and standardized accommodations.
- **Trade-offs & Side Effects**: Incurs significant budgetary expenditure and recruitment lead times. Rapid cross-training risks diluting deep domain expertise on complex disability accommodation standards.

### 6.2. Implement Dedicated Service-Level Agreements (SLAs) for Support Steps
- **Action**: Establish binding internal timeliness thresholds (e.g., max 24 hours queue wait for `Language Support Contact` and `Accommodation Review`). Decouple support-step wait times from overall caseworker efficiency evaluations so workers are not disincentivized from taking support cases.
- **Trade-offs & Side Effects**: Strict time caps may cause caseworkers to rush evaluations, perform superficial accommodation reviews, or prematurely mark support steps as "completed" without fully resolving applicant needs to avoid SLA breaches.

### 6.3. Redesign Routing to Eliminate Multi-Party Handovers (Integrated Pod Model)
- **Action**: Replace fragmented handovers (*General \(\rightarrow\) Specialist \(\rightarrow\) General \(\rightarrow\) Supervisor*) with dedicated multidisciplinary pods. A bilingual or accommodation-certified caseworker owns the case end-to-end from intake to decision.
- **Trade-offs & Side Effects**: Reduces dynamic load-balancing flexibility across the general caseworker pool; specialized pods may experience uneven workload volatility during localized surge periods.

### 6.4. Enhance Decision Explanations and Pre-Denial Opportunity to Cure
- **Action**: Replace generic rejection notices with detailed, plain-language, multilingual explanations specifying exact missing verification items. Implement an informal 5-day "cure period" via direct phone/messaging before formal denial.
- **Trade-offs & Side Effects**: Slightly extends active service time at the `Decision Draft` stage and requires administrative resources for outreach, though this is heavily offset by reduced formal appeals.

### 6.5. Real-Time Process Mining and Disparity Monitoring
- **Action**: Deploy automated PM4Py-based monitoring pipelines that track adjusted cycle times, handover counts, document request rates, and appeal rates across applicant groups on a monthly basis.
- **Trade-offs & Side Effects**: Danger of "Goodhart’s Law"—managers may pressure caseworkers to hit statistical parity targets artificially (e.g., approving borderline cases to balance numbers) rather than ensuring consistent procedural justice.

### 6.6. Comprehensive Audit of `risk_score_band` and Documentation Checklists
- **Action**: Conduct an independent statistical audit of the risk-scoring algorithm to eliminate racially or socioeconomically biased predictor variables. Re-evaluate documentation checklists to ensure in-person and minority-language applicants are not asked for redundant proofs.
- **Trade-offs & Side Effects**: Recalibrating risk models may temporarily lower fraud detection sensitivity or require policy renegotiation with regulatory bodies.

# 7. PM4Py analysis plan

The following production-ready Python script demonstrates how to utilize PM4Py to extract process variants, compute performance DFGs, analyze handover networks, insert waiting times, and verify declarative procedural constraints across applicant groups.

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

def run_procedural_fairness_analysis(log_csv_path: str):
    """
    Executes an end-to-end procedural fairness and process mining assessment
    using PM4Py on public benefits event log data.
    """
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    # 1. Load and Format Event Log
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    df = pd.read_csv(log_csv_path)
    df['time:timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(df['time:timestamp'])
    
    # Format dataframe to PM4Py standard conventions
    event_log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id='case:concept:name',
        activity_key='concept:name',
        timestamp_key='time:timestamp'
    )
    
    # Convert to EventLog object for advanced structural and declarative algorithms
    log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(event_log)
    
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    # 2. Stratification & Group Sub-Log Extraction
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    groups = ['Group A', 'Group B', 'Group C', 'Group D']
    group_logs = {}
    
    for g in groups:
        sub_df = event_log[event_log['applicant_group'] == g]
        group_logs[g] = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(sub_df)
        
    print("=== 1. VARIANT DISTRIBUTION ACROSS APPLICANT GROUPS ===")
    for g in groups:
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(group_logs[g])
        total_cases = len(group_logs[g])
        print(f"\n--- {g} (Total Cases: {total_cases}) ---")
        sorted_variants = sorted(variants.items(), key=lambda x: len(x[1]), reverse=True)
        for idx, (var, cases) in enumerate(sorted_variants[:5], 1):
            share = (len(cases) / total_cases) * 100
            print(f"  Top {idx}: Share={share:.1f}% ({len(cases)} cases) | Path: {var}")

    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    # 3. Waiting-Time and Service-Time Insertion
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    print("\n=== 2. SERVICE TIME VS. WAITING TIME DECOMPOSITION ===")
    # Insert waiting and service time metrics directly into event log
    log_with_times = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
        log,
        activity_key='concept:name',
        timestamp_key='time:timestamp',
        resource_key='org:resource'
    )
    
    for g in groups:
        g_sub = event_log[event_log['applicant_group'] == g]
        durations = pm4py.get_all_case_durations(pm4py.convert_to_event_log(g_sub))
        durations_days = np.array(durations) / 86400.0  # Convert seconds to days
        print(f"{g}: Median Duration = {np.median(durations_days):.2f}d, "
              f"90th Percentile = {np.percentile(durations_days, 90):.2f}d")

    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    # 4. Performance DFG & Bottleneck Discovery
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    print("\n=== 3. PERFORMANCE DFG: EDGE QUEUE TIMES (DAYS) ===")
    target_edges = [
        ('Completeness Check', 'Request Missing Documents'),
        ('Language Support Contact', 'Eligibility Assessment'),
        ('Accommodation Review', 'Eligibility Assessment'),
        ('Risk Screening', 'Fraud Review'),
        ('Supervisor Review', 'Decision Draft')
    ]
    
    for g in groups:
        perf_dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(group_logs[g])
        print(f"\n--- {g} Queue Delays ---")
        for edge in target_edges:
            if edge in perf_dfg:
                mean_wait_sec = perf_dfg[edge].get('mean', 0)
                median_wait_sec = perf_dfg[edge].get('median', 0)
                print(f"  {edge[0]} -> {edge[1]}: "
                      f"Median = {median_wait_sec/86400.0:.2f}d, "
                      f"Mean = {mean_wait_sec/86400.0:.2f}d")
            else:
                print(f"  {edge[0]} -> {edge[1]}: Not traversed")

    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    # 5. Handover of Work Network (Organizational Friction)
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    print("\n=== 4. ORGANIZATIONAL HANDOVER & ROLE ANALYSIS ===")
    for g in ['Group A', 'Group B', 'Group C']:
        handover_net = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
            group_logs[g],
            resource_key='org:resource',
            timestamp_key='time:timestamp'
        )
        roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(group_logs[g])
        print(f"{g}: Handover Network Nodes = {len(handover_net.nodes)}, "
              f"Edges (Transfers) = {len(handover_net.edges)}, "
              f"Discovered Roles = {len(roles)}")

    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    # 6. Declarative & Log Skeleton Conformance Checking
    # ---------------------------------------------------------
    print("\n=== 5. PROCEDURAL CONSTRAINT & FAIRNESS AUDIT ===")
    # Define procedural fairness constraints:
    # Rule 1: If Language Support is requested, Language Support Contact MUST precede Eligibility Assessment
    # Rule 2: If Accommodation is requested, Accommodation Review MUST occur before Decision Draft
    # Rule 3: Fast-track cases (Low Risk + Complete) should NEVER undergo Supervisor Review
    
    skeleton = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(
        group_logs['Group A'],
        noise_threshold=0.05
    )
    
    # Check conformance of Group B and Group C against baseline skeleton
    for g in ['Group B', 'Group C', 'Group D']:
        conf_results = pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton(group_logs[g], skeleton)
        violations = sum(1 for c in conf_results if not c['is_conformant'])
        print(f"{g}: Skeleton Conformance Violations = {violations} / {len(conf_results)} cases "
              f"({(violations/len(conf_results))*100:.1f}%)")

if __name__ == '__main__':
    print("PM4Py Procedural Fairness Analysis Framework Initialized.")
```

# 8. Bottom-line assessment

Based on the synthesis of process mining, adjusted regression modeling, queueing analysis, and organizational data, the public-benefits administration process is **procedurally unfair in specific, demonstrable operational dimensions**, while remaining **indeterminate regarding intentional individual discrimination or raw outcome parity bias**.

### 1. Where Procedural Unfairness is Conclusively Demonstrated
- **The "Rights-Preserving Support Penalty"**: The evidence demonstrates systematic structural unfairness against **Group B (minority language)** and **Group C (disability accommodation)**. Applicants who exercise statutory rights to language support and disability accommodation face severe queueing bottlenecks (median 3.8 days and 4.7 days wait, respectively) caused directly by an institutional failure to provision adequate specialist capacity (only 7 bilingual staff for 1,250 Group B cases; only 5 accommodation specialists for 820 Group C cases). This results in statistically significant adjusted cycle time penalties (+2.6 days for B, +4.1 days for C) that cannot be attributed to applicant complexity.
- **Disproportionate Administrative Friction and Scrutiny**: Group B applicants face an adjusted +9.5 percentage point higher likelihood of document re-requests and +8.0 percentage points higher supervisor review escalation after controlling for objective documentation completeness, risk tier, channel, household size, region, and cohort. This demonstrates unequal procedural hurdles and defensive administrative handling.
- **Fragmented Handover Topologies**: Groups B and C are subjected to high-friction, multi-party handover loops (*General \(\rightarrow\) Specialist \(\rightarrow\) General \(\rightarrow\) Supervisor*) that introduce administrative delay, risk of context loss, and elevated rework (22–24%).

### 2. Where the Evidence is Legitimate or Indeterminate
- **Legitimate Routing Divergence**: The higher routing of Group D to Fraud Review (28% in Variant V3) is legitimately explained by underlying objective risk indicators (51% high risk band, 49% prior case history).
- **Indeterminate Upstream Bias**: Whether Group D’s lower adjusted approval rate (-3.1 pp) and higher appeal rate (+5.7 pp) represent administrative bias remains indeterminate because `risk_score_band` and `prior_case_count` may themselves encode historical surveillance disparities rather than true fraud risk. Furthermore, because appeal overturn rates are unobserved in the log, we cannot definitively separate unjustified initial denials from justified fraud rejections.

### Conclusion
The process fails the standard of procedural justice: it structurally penalizes applicants who require statutory language and accommodation assistance, imposes higher evidentiary friction on minority-language applicants, and funnels vulnerable groups through uncoordinated, resource-starved queues. Addressing these disparities requires immediate organizational restructuring—expanding bilingual and specialist capacity, setting dedicated support SLAs, adopting integrated pod routing, and auditing upstream risk algorithms—rather than focusing narrowly on superficial outcome parity.
