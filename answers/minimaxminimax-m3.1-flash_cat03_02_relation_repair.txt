## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

The repairs below are hypotheses, not reconstructions of a unique ground truth. Confidence values are calibrated qualitative support scores, not statistical probabilities.

- **Hard invariant**
  - Every E2O source must be an existing event and every target an existing object; every O2O endpoint must be an existing object. No events or objects may be invented.
  - E2O edges point from event to object; O2O edges are directional. Thus `DEL-X carries PK-7` does not imply `PK-7 carries DEL-X`.
  - Adding an already present event–object or object–object pair is not a repair.
  - Existing observed relations should not be removed merely because they look unusual.

- **Strong default**
  - `Create Order` normally has one Order; `Add Item` one Order and one Item; `Pack Item` one Item and one Package; `Dispatch Package` one Package and one Delivery.
  - `Record Payment` normally links one Payment and one Invoice, although unapplied cash is allowed.
  - The normal lifecycle order is create  add  pack  dispatch  confirm, followed by invoice and payment. These are defaults rather than infallible axioms.
  - A package may contain several items, and an invoice may bill several orders.

- **Weak heuristic**
  - Identifier similarity is informative. Here `PK8-2` closely follows the observed `PK8-1  PK-8` mapping, while `N-421?` resembles `DEL-Y.carrier_ref=N-421`.
  - Time supports lifecycle compatibility but does not discriminate `DEL-X` from `DEL-Y`: both dispatch events occur at 10:00.
  - Amount matching is circumstantial: `130 + 100 = 230`, and `100 + 130 = 230`, but fees, discounts, taxes, or other cash can produce the same figures.
  - Customer `C17` fails to distinguish `ORD-A` from `ORD-B`; both have that customer. Route `NORTH` also fails to distinguish the deliveries.
  - Existing O2O structure supports the *shape* of a repair—for example, a Delivery carrying a Package—but is not independent confirmation of a newly inferred event edge.

- **Evidence against a repair**
  - `PK8-2` and the item-only `ev08` are compatible with the documented scanner-outage case.
  - The question mark in `N-421?` and wildcard in `N-42*` materially weaken exact carrier matching.
  - `ORD-A` is the only observed order at `ev11`; adding `ORD-B` would rely on transitive and customer-level reasoning.
  - `PAY-2` could be unapplied cash or payment of another obligation not represented in this fragment.
  - Arithmetic consistency must not be counted twice merely because an E2O edge and a derived O2O edge use the same amount.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

### Bundle A: conservative/minimum-change repair

- **ev08 and PK-8:** Add A-01 and A-02 provisionally. The scanner token is unusually specific, and the existing `PK8-1  PK-8` mapping makes reconciliation plausible. Nevertheless, both edges remain provisional because an unreconciled scanner outage is explicitly allowed.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Add A-03 and A-04 provisionally. `N-421?` is much closer to `DEL-Y` than to `DEL-X`; equal timestamps and identical routes do not create a competing preference. Do not add `DEL-X carries PK-8`.
- **ev11 and the delivery objects:** Retain the observed `ev11  ORD-A` relation and make no new delivery edge. `N-42*` is compatible with either delivery, both deliveries together, or a portal fallback that retained only the order.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Add A-05 and A-06 provisionally. The exact two-order sum and shared customer make this more plausible than an unexplained 100 charge, but the status remains provisional because arithmetic alone cannot establish billing.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Leave U-03 and U-04 unresolved. `PAY-2` remains a legitimate unapplied-cash event. This leaves `INV-44` only partly settled but preserves the allowed lifecycle.

This bundle creates plausible lifecycles for all objects but deliberately leaves two semantic gaps: no delivery confirmation object and no application of `PAY-2`.

### Bundle B: batch-confirmation and completed-settlement interpretation

Bundle B inherits A-01 through A-06 and adds B-01 through B-04.

- **ev08 and PK-8:** Interpret `PK8-2` as a reconciled scan of a consolidated package containing both `IT-A2` and `IT-B1`.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Interpret `N-421?` as the delivery reference for `DEL-Y`, with `DEL-Y` carrying `PK-8`.
- **ev11 and the delivery objects:** Retain `ORD-A` and interpret `N-42*` as a bulk acknowledgement of both `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y`. This is allowed by the “several deliveries acknowledged together” rule, but it is less certain than a single delivery.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Continue to interpret the invoice as covering both same-customer orders.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Interpret the two payments as jointly settling the 230 invoice.

Bundle B yields more complete order, package, delivery, invoice, and payment lifecycles, but at the cost of asserting a batch acknowledgement and a payment application that are not uniquely established.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

- **Order:** `ORD-A` has a coherent create/add/pack/invoice lifecycle. `ORD-B` has create/add and, provisionally, pack/dispatch through `PK-8`; A-05/A-06 also connect it to the invoice. Sharing `PK-8` between `IT-A2` and `IT-B1` is unusual but legitimate as consolidated packing. It must not be treated as evidence that the orders themselves are merged.
- **Item:** All three items have observed order links. `IT-B1` acquires a package under A-01/A-02. If that repair is wrong, `IT-B1` becomes an unpacked item, which is permissible during an outage.
- **Package:** `PK-7` has a complete item–dispatch–delivery path. `PK-8` has `IT-A2`, provisionally `IT-B1`, and a provisional dispatch through `DEL-Y`. A package containing two items from two orders creates cross-order paths but no suspicious many-to-many relation.
- **Delivery:** `DEL-X` has `PK-7` and `ev09`. `DEL-Y` gains `PK-8` and `ev10` under Bundle A, but neither delivery receives a direct confirmation edge. That is an acceptable semantic gap under the order-only confirmation rule.
- **Invoice:** `INV-44` bills `ORD-A` and provisionally `ORD-B`. One invoice billing two orders is plausible. It does not prove that both order totals are separately itemized.
- **Payment:** `PAY-1` remains linked to `INV-44`. `PAY-2` has its Record Payment event but no application in Bundle A. It is not a strict orphan event or object, only an unresolved settlement relation. The amount 100 does not justify reallocating `PAY-1` to `ORD-B`, because its observed invoice relation is stronger.
- **Event/object orphans:** Every event already has at least one observed E2O relation, and every object is connected to at least one event. Thus there are no strict graph orphans. `PAY-2` and the delivery confirmations are semantic or lifecycle gaps.
- **Cardinality:** The notable one-to-many structures are Package  Items, Invoice  Orders, and, in Bundle B, Payment  Invoice for one invoice. These are normal business cardinalities. No item is placed in two packages and no delivery carries two packages under Bundle A.
- **Existing E2O plus O2O support:** The pre-existing pairs for `ORD-A/IT-A1`, `ORD-A/IT-A2`, `ORD-B/IT-B1`, `PK-7/IT-A1`, `PK-8/IT-A2`, `DEL-X/PK-7`, `INV-44/ORD-A`, and `PAY-1/INV-44` have both forms of representation. Their provenance is unknown, so they are record-level corroboration rather than guaranteed statistically independent evidence.
- **Circularity:** A-01 and A-02, A-03 and A-04, and A-05 and A-06 are paired hypotheses derived from the same underlying event evidence. Treating each pair as two independent confirmations would be circular. No new O2O edge is used to claim that its corresponding E2O edge is certain.

# PM4Py releases differ in accepted argument names/positional order.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Assigning `ev11` uniquely to `DEL-X` or `DEL-Y`** would manufacture one confirmation path and discard the equally plausible batch or order-only interpretations.
2. **Adding both deliveries and `ORD-B` to `ev11`** would turn a wildcard acknowledgement into a specific three-order batch confirmation, distorting order clustering.
3. **Assigning `ev10` to `DEL-X` because both dispatches occur at 10:00** ignores the stronger `N-421?` versus `N-421` identifier evidence.
4. **Assigning `IT-B1` to `PK-7`** would create a second item in package 7, break the scanner-token pattern, and connect `ORD-B` to the wrong delivery.
5. **Treating `PK-8` as carrying two items as a many-to-many error** would be too strong: packages legitimately consolidate items, including items from different orders.
6. **Treating the 230 invoice total as proof that both orders are billed** could invent an order link when the excess amount represents fees, tax, or another obligation.
7. **Treating the 130 payment as proof that it settles `INV-44`** could convert unapplied cash into a false settlement and create an apparently fully paid invoice.
8. **Adding inverse O2O edges** such as `PK-8 carries DEL-Y` violates qualifier direction and can create spurious object-type transitions.
9. **Counting each added E2O/O2O pair as two independent confirmations** would inflate confidence and make the repaired graph appear more certain than its evidence permits.
10. **Forcing every object into a complete lifecycle** would erase legitimate uncertainty around scanner outages, unapplied cash, and portal loss of carrier identifiers.
