## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

### Upstream data-quality dependencies

- **A6 depends on A5 and registration/classification quality.**  
  Financial-statement normalization cannot fix missing or wrong documents. The DFG shows `Financial document review -> Request missing documents` 3,300 times and `Request missing documents -> Financial document review` 2,800 times, with 52% repeated. A5 improves the completeness of the input to A6.

- **A8 depends on multiple upstream artifacts.**  
  Committee packaging waits for KYC status, normalized financial statements, collateral valuation if required, and credit-analysis notes. Therefore A8 should not precede A4/A3, A6, and ideally A7.

- **A9 depends on clean KYC and Committee decision data.**  
  Contract preparation depends on clean KYC and decision data. The DFG shows `Committee decision -> Contract preparation` 3,650 times and `Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue` 650 times. A9 is high value but can propagate errors if KYC or decision data are unstable.

- **A11 depends on all controls being complete.**  
  Loan activation is downstream of KYC clearance, contract execution, and signing. The table explicitly says activation cannot proceed if controls are incomplete, so A11 is not a first-year candidate unless A4/A9/A10 and control gates are mature.

### Legal and compliance controls

- **KYC clearance must precede Customer signing.**  
  Provisional signing occurred in 6.5% of cases, and 1.1% required remediation. Therefore A10 and A11 must be gated by A4/A3/A9 controls.

- **Contract terms must match Committee decision.**  
  Contract-remediation loops are 8.5% overall and 14% for Credit-line renewal. A9 should include rule-based checks and a human override trail, not just document generation.

- **Four-eyes principle applies to High-exposure SME cases.**  
  Credit analysis and Committee packaging should not be completed by the same person. Violations occur in 5.8% of High-exposure cases during month-end rush. A8 and A9 must enforce role separation; automation must not blur maker/checker boundaries.

- **Automation must preserve an auditable trail.**  
  Every model suggestion, human override, and final decision must be logged. This is especially critical for A3, A4, A6, A8, and A9.

### Human escalation capacity

- **Compliance specialists are a hard constraint.**  
  There are only 3 compliance specialists, and the handover network shows high dependency from KYC ops to two senior specialists. A4 and A3 must reduce, not relocate, specialist load. A4 should include triage, evidence assembly, and maker-checker, while A3 should escalate high-risk hits rather than auto-clear them.

- **Credit analysts are central and can be overloaded by A6.**  
  Credit analysts are 9 FTE and central in the working-together network. A6 can reduce analyst time on normalization, but it may increase clean inflow into Credit analysis. Without capacity rebalancing, Credit analysis waiting time (28h) could worsen.

- **Back office is visible but not always the binding constraint.**  
  Back office has 12 FTE and the most visible queue, but utilization falls below 65% after 15:00. A1 may reduce visible registration work, but it may simply accelerate cases into Classify, KYC, and Financial document review queues.

- **Legal ops is underutilized when upstream KYC is slow but overloaded at month-end.**  
  A9 can reduce routine Contract preparation work, but it may shift legal effort to rule maintenance, exception review, and month-end backlog management.

### External valuation dependency

- **A7 is partner-dependent but process-critical.**  
  `Wait for external valuation` has 58h median waiting time and 19% late arrivals. Collateral valuation is required in 71% of Equipment finance and 18% of Working-capital cases. A7 can triage and parallelize valuer work, but it cannot fully control external valuers.

- **A7 feeds A8.**  
  Committee packaging waits for collateral valuation if required. If A7 reduces external delay, A8 will see more complete packs earlier, but the weekly Committee decision cadence may become the next constraint.

### Core-banking integration constraints

- **Deep integration should be reserved for modules that change core case state.**  
  A4, A6, A9, and A11 are the most likely to require deep integration because they touch KYC status, financial data, contract/decision data, or activation.

- **First six months allow only two deep integrations.**  
  A practical sequence is to use the first two deep integrations for **A4 and A6**, because they address critical path and upstream data quality. **A9** can be integrated after upstream gates are proven.

- **A5, A7, A10, and A12 can often be implemented with lighter integration.**  
  A5 can live in the customer portal; A7 can use partner routing; A10 can use e-signature APIs; A12 can use a workflow workbench. This preserves core-integration capacity for A4/A6/A9.

### Concurrency dependency

- **KYC check and Financial document review are often concurrent, though the system records them sequentially.**  
  Automation must not force artificial serialization. A4 and A5/A6 should be designed to operate on the same case in parallel where the real process allows it. The DFG shows `Classify -> KYC check` 7,250 and `Classify -> Financial document review` 3,940, indicating split paths that should be treated as concurrent where observed.

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

Recommended first-year portfolio: **Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4, A5, A6, A9**.

This respects the constraint of **at most four modules**, satisfies compliance by including **A4 and A9 as control-strengthening modules**, and respects the **two-deep-integration limit in the first six months** by deep-integrating **A4 and A6** first, then adding **A9** after upstream gates.

### Integration constraint handling

- **First six months:** deep integrations are limited to **A4 and A6**.  
- **A5** is portal/workflow-based and does not consume a deep core-banking integration slot.  
- **A9** is introduced after month six, once KYC, document, and financial-data quality are demonstrably improved.  
- **A11** is not included because it is downstream and cannot operate safely unless controls are complete.

### Monitoring focus

The rollout should track not only activity-level savings but also:

- Whether `Resolve entity mismatch` remains on the critical path.  
- Whether `Financial document review -> Request missing documents -> Financial document review` loops shrink.  
- Whether `Normalize financial statements -> Credit analysis` flow becomes cleaner without overloading credit analysts.  
- Whether `Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue` loops decline.  
- Whether provisional signing, four-eyes violations, and contract-terms mismatches decline.  
- Whether back-office, compliance, credit-analyst, and legal workloads shift in a controlled way.

## 7. Final recommendation

**Recommend the Bottleneck/Synchronization portfolio: A4, A5, A6, A9.**

The highest-frequency activity, **Register application**, should not be automated first as the main strategic move. It is high frequency, high feasibility, and politically attractive, but the process-mining evidence shows it is not the dominant end-to-end constraint. Register application appears in position 2 for 98% of cases, has only 1.5h median waiting time, and has relatively low rework. Automating it first would mainly accelerate cases into downstream queues: `Classify product and exposure -> KYC check` and `Classify -> Financial document review`, where the real synchronization problems appear.

The more damaging constraints are lower-frequency or loop-heavy activities:

- **Resolve entity mismatch** has only 1,750 occurrences but 31h median wait, 38% rework, and is on the critical path in 82% of cases when present. A4 directly reduces this KYC critical-path delay and dependence on scarce compliance specialists.  
- **Request missing documents** creates repeated loops: 3,300 requests, 2,800 returns, and 52% repeated. A5 reduces customer-response delay and improves input quality for A6.  
- **Normalize financial statements** has 75 min mean service time, 20h wait, and 16% rework. A6 improves the `Financial document review -> Normalize -> Credit analysis` chain and reduces analyst manual effort.  
- **Contract preparation and remediation** create legal risk and month-end overload. A9 strengthens controls by checking KYC clearance, decision alignment, and contract terms, but it must be sequenced after upstream data-quality improvements.

This portfolio balances **cycle time, waiting time, rework, handovers, resource demand, and control risk**. It satisfies compliance because A4 improves KYC clearance integrity and A9 enforces contract and control rules. It respects IT constraints because A4 and A6 can be the two deep integrations in the first six months, while A9 is introduced after upstream gates. It also accounts for changed human workload: compliance specialists may be relieved by A4, back office by A5, credit analysts by A6 if capacity is rebalanced, and legal ops by A9 if exception ownership is clear.

A1 remains a valid future automation, but it is best treated as a **later low-risk efficiency module**, not the first process-level move. A11 should not be pursued in year one because Loan activation cannot safely operate unless upstream controls are complete. If Equipment finance volume or external valuation delay proves to dominate cycle time in the first half-year, **A7** should be the first candidate to swap into the portfolio, likely replacing A5 only if missing-document loops have already been reduced through process changes.
