## 2. Process-model consistency checks
To ensure robust monitoring, a concrete validation suite must be implemented:
- **Prefix-time availability:** Introduce data masking validation during training. Ensure features rely only on records with `timestamp <= t0` to simulate strict prediction-time states.
- **Activity-order feasibility:** Utilize declarative process checks rather than relying purely on frequency. For example, `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` must be flagged as an invalid backward edge because a declarative rule defines `Claim Closed` as terminal.
- **Same-timestamp ambiguity:** Enforce a monotonic ingestion sequence index (or system ID) to resolve concurrent logs. This prevents false topological sorts and artificial parallelisms when timestamp collisions occur.
- **Complete-trace information:** Implement automated SQL/aggregation scans to ensure no variables sum, count, or identify paths over an entire `case_id` history, filtering strictly by `event_time <= t0`.
- **Enabled, impossible, or future-possible activities:** Apply prefix alignment checks. At `t0`, the trace is simply `Claim Registered -> Coverage Check`. Therefore, `Settlement Decision Recorded` is merely future-possible, not yet enabled or executed, making any sequence explanations citing it invalid at prefix-time.
- **Repeated Settlement Decision legitimacy:** Use declarative models rather than basic Directly-Follows Graphs (DFGs) or simplistic footprint matrices. While a standard DFG might treat repeated `Settlement Decision Recorded` events as rare anomalies or unpermitted loops, declarative models understand the semantic context and explicitly legitimize these loops when caused by the normative `Appeal` step. A frequent DFG edge is not automatically a normative rule, nor is a rare edge necessarily a violation.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations
Features that survive or can be repaired should be classified as follows:
- **Defensible predictive signal:** (`initial_damage_complexity`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, `initial_reserve`)
  *What may be claimed:* These are valid predictors of processing time based on intrinsic claim difficulty or time buffers.
  *What may not be claimed:* Direct causal mechanisms cannot be established without structural models (e.g., delays may stem from triage policies rather than the complexity itself).
- **Process-state or congestion signal:** (`queue_length_at_t0`)
  *What may be claimed:* Highlights resource contention and operational load at prediction time. This is actionable for organizational load balancing.
  *What may not be claimed:* This is an environmental factor, not an intrinsic attribute of the claimant's case, and implies no fault on the claimant.
- **Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review:** (`claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`)
  *What may be claimed:* These features are predictive via their correlations with resources, repair network density, or demographics.
  *What may not be claimed:* These are not direct causal drivers of delay. They should never be used to penalize claimants, justify systemic service degradation, or ignore structural inequalities.
- **Drift/calendar proxy:** (`calendar_week`)
  *What may be claimed:* Operationally predictive due to known operational policies and holiday periods.
  *What may not be claimed:* This is highly vulnerable to temporal distribution shift and cannot be claimed as a stable, generalizable attribute of claims over time.
- **Unusable until lineage is repaired:** (`prior_claim_count_before_t0`, `risk_score_current`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `Employee experience`)
  *What may be claimed:* Currently, nothing. Once restored to strict `t0` point-in-time states through infrastructure fixes, they may become defensible or congestion signals.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation
A comprehensive validation and remediation plan should be executed in the following priority order:
1. **Feature-store design (Time-Travel):** Transition to a point-in-time architecture. Ensure all mutable database fields (like `risk_score_current` and `Employee experience`) track historical `updated_at` timestamps to prevent extraction-date leakage.
2. **Prefix-only recomputation:** Purge all complete-trace features (e.g., `total_case_duration_hours`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_handler_team`) from the pipeline. Retrain the model strictly on prefix-truncated sequences bounded strictly by `t0`.
3. **Process-conformance tests:** Ingest events with a reliable sequence index to resolve same-timestamp collisions. Enforce declarative process models to automatically filter and reject impossible or future sequence patterns from the sequence explainer.
4. **Calibration after feature removal:** The removal of dominant target-leaking features will drastically shift model probabilities and reduce overall AUC. Mandatory recalibration of risk scores against the new legitimate baseline must be performed.
5. **Proxy/fairness analysis:** Conduct a formal disparate impact analysis on `claimant_region` and `claimant_channel` before deployment to ensure systemic operational biases are not being amplified.
6. **Explanation stability:** Run perturbation tests to ensure local SHAP/sequence values remain stable under minor temporal changes (like queue length fluctuations) across similar prefix traces.
7. **Monitoring for schema or process drift:** Establish ongoing statistical monitoring for highly sensitive operational features like `calendar_week` and queue length distributions to quickly detect changes in operational policies or holiday impacts.
