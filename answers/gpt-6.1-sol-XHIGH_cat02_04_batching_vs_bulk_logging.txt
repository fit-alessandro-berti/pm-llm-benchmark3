In PM4Py, `merge_distance` controls temporal grouping by proximity; it is not a measurement-error correction or an instruction to infer physical concurrency. Lowering it cannot recover distinct times already collapsed into the same source timestamp.

# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

Define nominal queue waiting as `start − queue_arrival`, nominal service envelope as `complete − start`, and apparent recording lag as `recorded_at − complete`. The following numerical observations assume **A1:** displayed timestamps are used literally, with omitted seconds interpreted as zero, and clocks are aligned for subtraction. This assumption permits descriptive calculations; it is not evidence that the clocks actually agree. No assumption makes a group-open field an individual execution start.

For utilization, distinguish the sum of case envelopes from the union of resource-occupied intervals. Neither identifies active labor without lifecycle semantics. Actual utilization also needs a defined observation window, available capacity, and coverage of other work; these fragments cannot establish shift utilization. Activity-completion throughput needs a fixed wall-clock window and unique case counts, not the sum of durations or the number of discovered batches.

- **G1:** Nominal waits are 6, 5, 4 and 2 minutes, averaging **4.25**; every service envelope is **3 minutes**. The cell's interval union is 3 minutes, whereas summing cases gives 12 item-minutes. If one fixture cycle processes all four, 12 item-minutes is a valid item-occupancy total but an invalid single-cell busy-time total. Counting four completions in a three-minute operating cycle gives 1.33 items/minute for that cycle, conditional on the lifecycle interpretation. If labeling is internally serial, group start can underestimate individual waiting and overestimate individual active service. Any wait-for-batch-formation component belongs in waiting, not necessarily a capacity bottleneck. Counting one discovered batch as one item would understate item throughput by fourfold.

- **G2:** Nominal mean waiting is **12.25 minutes**, mean service envelope **17.5 minutes**, and summed envelopes **70 minutes** within a **27-minute** union. A group-open start used as individual execution start moves time from waiting into service: individual waiting is underestimated, service overestimated, and case-duration summation suggests 259% of one worker's capacity over that span. Under the explicitly hypothetical assumption **A2** that inspections run continuously in completion order from 10:00, durations would be 8, 6, 7 and 6 minutes, and actual waits 18, 22, 25 and 27 minutes: means **6.75** and **23**, respectively. This illustrates the bias, not a reconstructed truth. Genuine common setup could justify shared elapsed time but still requires allocation rules for labor. Completion timestamps may support throughput timing if their semantics are valid; the misleading starts instead distort admission and service bottleneck diagnosis.

- **G3:** Nominal mean waiting is **19.5 minutes** and mean service **16.75 minutes**; summed service is **67 minutes** inside a **28-minute** union. If starts are genuine but completions are assigned at folder submission, service and work-in-process duration are inflated, while start-based waiting is unaffected by that particular error. Under hypothetical **A3**, each review finishes when the next one starts and the last finishes at 11:30: durations become 8, 7, 7 and 6 minutes, totaling 28. This is one feasible serial interpretation, not a correction. A real common final release makes the longer case elapsed times meaningful for release lead time, but not necessarily for active review effort. Deferred completions concentrate apparent throughput at 11:30 and can falsely locate the bottleneck in review rather than release/reporting. A one-minute recorded lag does not exclude much earlier physical completion.

- **G4:** Nominal waits are 7, 10, 13, 16 and 19 minutes, averaging **13**; each service is **4 minutes**. Summed and union service both equal **20 minutes**, with no positive overlap. These observations support serial activity throughput of five items per 20-minute processing span, conditional on accurate execution timestamps. Treating this as a simultaneous batch would falsely replace individual starts with 12:05, reducing mean waiting to 5 minutes, and could attribute concurrent capacity to one worker. Treating the cluster as one item would also suppress throughput. The growing waits reflect ordered queue drainage; the seven-minute initial delay might reflect group formation, scheduling, setup, other work or capacity loss. Its cause cannot be identified from these records.

- **G5:** Nominal mean waiting is **15.25 minutes**, mean envelope **17.5 minutes**, summed envelopes **70 minutes**, and union **30 minutes**. Trusting the login as one worker produces 233% apparent load over that span. Treating the entire 30-minute union as labor can instead undercount effort contributed by several people. With exactly three continuously available analysts, 70/90 = **77.8%** would be a provisional envelope-to-capacity ratio, not measured utilization. More decisively, four intervals coexist for eight minutes, so they cannot all represent uninterrupted exclusive service even with three analysts. Switching, suspended tasks, missing participants, or lifecycle errors must also be considered. If starts mean genuine first engagement, they can measure first-response waiting but conceal later pauses; if they mean task opening, waiting is underestimated and active service overestimated. Completion counts remain observable, but team capacity and individual bottlenecks cannot be inferred. In source sequence order, `recorded_at` moves backward twice; source insertion and database insertion may be different stages, so neither order should be used as a physical execution order without validation.

- **G6:** Nominal waits are **175, 148, 122, 110, 76, 58 and 35 minutes**, averaging **103.43**, while every nominal service duration is **zero**. If work occurred earlier and both lifecycle fields were replaced by the 17:00 entry time, actual execution and reporting delay are assigned entirely to “waiting,” and actual service disappears. Thus inflated waiting and near-zero service arise from the same field substitution. Fixed-window utilization from these intervals becomes zero; rate calculations using zero service become undefined, and seven reported completions appear as an artificial spike. This can suggest both a severe upstream queue bottleneck and an exceptionally fast close activity. If 17:00 is a real administrative transition, waiting to that transition may be meaningful, but preparatory service remains unobserved. Under **A4**, aligned clocks and true execution between arrival and a genuine 17:00 closure, each true initial wait lies between zero and its nominal wait; this is only a conditional bound. It does not separate waiting from service. The 17:01 insertion adds one minute to recorded lead time; it does not establish when substantive work happened.

- **G7:** Nominal waits are **2, −1, −4, −7 and −11 minutes**, averaging **−4.2**, and all nominal services are **15 minutes**. Four negative waits refute a literal aligned-clock, arrival-before-start interpretation. Quarter-hour rounding can make starts too early and finishes too late, thereby understating waiting and overstating service; different rounding directions can reverse those biases. Summed service of **75 minutes** versus a **15-minute** union falsely suggests five-way concurrency under a single-worker interpretation. Clipping negative waits would hide the contradiction and still yield biased waiting. Throughput appears concentrated at 14:15; its actual timing and the corresponding bottleneck remain unidentified. Use censoring after validating the quantizer, or exclude these fields from point-estimate queue/service analysis.

- **G8:** Nominal waits are **38, 43, 48, 54 and 58 minutes**, averaging **48.2**; each service is **7 minutes**, with a **35-minute** serial union. All five cases have arrived before the first start. Long waits coupled with regular service are consistent with unavailable capacity followed by recovery; they do not show slow triage service. If the safety check consumed the same worker's capacity, omitting it understates total worker utilization and can misidentify safety work as repair idleness. A queue/scanner clock offset shifts waiting; a safety/scanner offset changes whether the apparent three-minute gap is real. Scanner-only service durations are less sensitive to a constant offset of that scanner clock. The active recovery span yields five completions per 35 minutes; the first-arrival-to-last-completion span is 73 minutes. These answer different throughput questions and establish no steady-state rate.

Conformance analysis must preserve these distinctions. G1 can be lawful multi-item capacity use; G2/G3 can create false resource-overlap violations; G5 cannot support individual staffing or authorization checks; G6 can create artificial service-SLA compliance and waiting-SLA failure; G7 can create a false arrival-before-start violation; and G8 cannot establish safety-before-repair compliance until clock error is bounded. G4/G8 adjacency across different cases is not a within-case routing edge. Each case here contains only one activity fragment, so ordinary end-to-end control-flow conformance cannot be established from this dataset. Equal timestamps and source insertion order must not manufacture causal precedence.

Missing or reversed intervals, if present, would remain in the master diagnostics with explicit exclusion counts; only the interval detector requires usable bounds. No lifecycle repair or guessed worker allocation occurs. The code uses a common displayed-time convention for arithmetic and PM4Py representation; it does not synchronize source clocks.

df = pd.read_csv(StringIO(DATA), sep="|", keep_default_na=False)
for col in ["queue_arrival", "start", "complete", "recorded_at"]:
    text = df[col].replace("", pd.NA)
    df[col] = pd.to_datetime("2026-05-26 " + text, format="mixed",
                             errors="coerce").astype("datetime64[ns]")
df["precision_s"] = df["precision"].map({"1 sec": 1, "1 min": 60, "15 min": 900})
df["valid_interval"] = (df["start"].notna() & df["complete"].notna()
                         & df["complete"].ge(df["start"]))
df["wait_min"] = (df["start"] - df["queue_arrival"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60
df["service_min"] = (df["complete"] - df["start"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60
df["lag_s"] = (df["recorded_at"] - df["complete"]).dt.total_seconds()
# These flags remain attached to every row; they do not repair timestamps.
FLAGS = {
    "G1": "cycle-level timestamps possible",
    "G2": "group-open start may precede individual work",
    "G3": "folder submission may replace individual completion",
    "G4": "serial; deliberate batching unproved",
    "G5": "shared login; active attention unknown",
    "G6": "zero intervals; execution lifecycle suspect",
    "G7": "undocumented rounding; four starts before arrival",
    "G8": "capacity-loss hypothesis; safety clock unaligned",
}
df["quality_flag"] = df["cluster"].map(FLAGS)


def overlap_stats(g):
    intervals = list(g.loc[g["valid_interval"], ["start", "complete"]]
                     .itertuples(index=False, name=None))
    pairs = list(combinations(intervals, 2))
    overlap = sum(max(a[0], b[0]) < min(a[1], b[1]) for a, b in pairs)
    positive = g[g["valid_interval"] & g["complete"].gt(g["start"])]
    if positive.empty:
        return (overlap / len(pairs) if pairs else float("nan")), 0.0, 0
    changes = pd.concat([
        pd.DataFrame({"t": positive["start"], "delta": 1}),
        pd.DataFrame({"t": positive["complete"], "delta": -1}),
    ])
    active = changes.groupby("t")["delta"].sum().sort_index().cumsum()
    times = active.index.to_series()
    minutes = (times.shift(-1) - times).dt.total_seconds().fillna(0) / 60
    return (overlap / len(pairs) if pairs else float("nan")), float(minutes[active > 0].sum()), int(active.max())

    resource_rows.append({"resource": resource, "overlap_rate": rate,
                          "union_min": union, "max_active": peak})
resource_diagnostics = pd.DataFrame(resource_rows).set_index("resource")

diagnostics = []

    starts = g["start"].dropna().value_counts()
    ends = g["complete"].dropna().value_counts()
    # Queue estimates require arrivals and starts on aligned clocks and causal order.
    queue_usable = (g["queue_arrival"].notna().all() and g["start"].notna().all()
                    and g["wait_min"].ge(0).all())
    if queue_usable:
        changes = pd.concat([
            pd.DataFrame({"t": g["queue_arrival"], "delta": 1}),
            pd.DataFrame({"t": g["start"], "delta": -1}),
        ])
        qpeak = int(changes.groupby("t")["delta"].sum().sort_index().cumsum().max())
    else:
        qpeak = float("nan")  # retain the cluster and display its invalidity
    endpoints = pd.concat([g["start"], g["complete"]]).dropna()
    grid = endpoints.dt.second.eq(0) & endpoints.dt.microsecond.eq(0)
    quarter_grid = grid & endpoints.dt.minute.mod(15).eq(0)
    reversals = (g.sort_values(["source", "sequence_no"])
                 .groupby("source")["recorded_at"].diff().lt(pd.Timedelta(0)).sum())
    first_start = g["start"].min()
    diagnostics.append({
        "cluster": cluster, "n": len(g), "resource": g["resource"].iloc[0],
        "start_eq_max": int(starts.max()) if len(starts) else 0,
        "end_eq_max": int(ends.max()) if len(ends) else 0,
        "start_eq_groups": int(starts.ge(2).sum()),
        "end_eq_groups": int(ends.ge(2).sum()),
        "overlap_rate": rate, "max_active": peak, "union_min": union,
        "wait_mean_min": g["wait_min"].mean(),
        "service_mean_min": g["service_min"].mean(),
        "service_sum_min": g["service_min"].sum(min_count=1),
        "lag_mean_s": g["lag_s"].mean(), "lag_min_s": g["lag_s"].min(),
        "lag_max_s": g["lag_s"].max(), "precision_s": g["precision_s"].iloc[0],
        "minute_grid_pct": 100 * grid.mean(),
        "quarter_grid_pct": 100 * quarter_grid.mean(),
        "queue_peak_nominal": qpeak,
        "arrived_before_first_start": int(g["queue_arrival"].lt(first_start).sum()),
        "arrival_span_min": (g["queue_arrival"].max() - g["queue_arrival"].min()).total_seconds() / 60,
        "first_start_delay_min": (first_start - g["queue_arrival"].min()).total_seconds() / 60,
        "negative_wait_rows": int(g["wait_min"].lt(0).sum()),
        "zero_duration_rows": int(g["service_min"].eq(0).sum()),
        "pm_excluded_rows": int((~g["valid_interval"]).sum()),
        "record_order_reversals": int(reversals), "quality_flag": FLAGS[cluster],
    })
diag = pd.DataFrame(diagnostics).set_index("cluster")

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

**Resource identity.** If the human IDs are trusted as individual workers, G4/G8 fit exclusive serial work, while G2/G3 overlapping case envelopes require shared setup, awaiting release, multitasking, or misleading lifecycle fields. Trusting `analyst_pool` as one person would contradict the explicit shared-login context and create artificial overload. RBT-7 is a machine with a four-place fixture, so a single resource ID does not imply one-item capacity.

If all human IDs are treated as potentially shared, overlap could represent independent workers rather than multitasking, and apparent per-person utilization and resource conformance become unidentified. This does not alter the timestamps themselves or establish that extra workers existed. G4/G8 remain nonoverlapping among the observed cases, but pooled identities cannot reveal unobserved parallel work. For G5, the known maximum of three analysts still rules out four uninterrupted exclusive services over 13:12–13:20. Minute-scale censoring cannot plausibly eliminate an eight-minute common overlap under ordinary one-bin rounding assumptions. Shared identity is therefore a strong explanation, but not a complete explanation.

**Exact timestamps versus censored timestamps.** Nominal calculations use A1. Under exact, aligned and semantically genuine lifecycles, G1/G2/G3/G5/G7 show substantial overlap, G4/G8 show adjacency, and G6 has zero-duration transitions. Applying the source precision creates uncertainty about those equalities and boundaries. Precision bounds quantization only under an explicit rounding model; it does not bound clock offset, recording delay, or semantic substitution.

For example, under labeled assumption **A5**, nearest-bin rounding with width `p` gives each true timestamp a range roughly `reported ± p/2`. Floor rounding gives `[reported, reported + p)`, and ceiling rounding gives `(reported − p, reported]`. When floor versus ceiling is unknown, a conservative one-bin envelope `reported ± p` can be used as a sensitivity assumption, but it is not a documented accuracy guarantee. Apply these models to local lifecycle fields; upstream arrivals and database insertion times require their own precision and clock metadata.

Given feasible start range `[sL, sU]`, completion range `[cL, cU]`, and an aligned exact arrival `q`, waiting lies in `[sL − q, sU − q]`, intersected with nonnegative waiting under an arrival-before-service assumption. Service has a conservative range `[max(0, cL − sU), cU − sL]`, subject to feasible ordered endpoints; a negative upper bound means the assumptions are inconsistent. Uncertain arrival times widen the waiting range. For two positive-duration intervals, overlap is definite when the latest feasible starts precede the earliest feasible completions; otherwise overlap may be possible or impossible. Capacity constraints and correlated batch timestamps should further restrict feasible schedules rather than treating all endpoint errors as independent.

- **G1:** One-second quantization weakens exact equality slightly but cannot explain away three-minute shared cycle occupancy. It cannot distinguish simultaneous labeling from serial actions inside one shared fixture cycle.
- **G2/G3:** Minute censoring can perturb short gaps, but does not resolve whether the fields represent group admission or folder submission. Lifecycle semantics dominate the error budget.
- **G4/G8:** Exact touching can become short gaps or short overlaps under one-second censoring. The broad serial pattern remains, conditional on faithful execution fields; long parallel execution is not supported. The initial queue delays still depend on clock alignment.
- **G5:** Fine time intervals do not recover worker identity or active attention. Treat intervals as task envelopes until assignment and pause data are available.
- **G6:** Same-minute start and finish could conceal sub-minute elapsed actions under a known quantizer, but cannot recover earlier substantive work if the timestamps were overwritten. Censoring cannot repair an unknown lifecycle meaning.
- **G7:** Under hypothetical **A6**, start rounded down and completion rounded up, an observed 14:00–14:15 pair brackets execution within that quarter-hour. Serial measurements can fit: for example, constructed intervals 14:00–14:01, 14:01–14:04, 14:04–14:07, 14:07–14:11 and 14:11–14:14 obey the supplied arrivals and round to the supplied lifecycle pairs. A constructed simultaneous interval 14:11–14:14 for every case fits too. These are counterexamples proving non-identifiability, not recovered observations. If completion was rounded down instead, actual finishes could extend toward 14:30. A6 must therefore not become an unqualified correction.

At least four observational ambiguities remain: G1's identical endpoints support either a physical batch or a replicated cycle envelope; G2's common starts support either shared setup with serial work or genuine overlapping work; G3's common ends support either a true joint release or delayed submission; and G6/G7 show that identical timestamps can arise from administrative entry or quantization. G4/G8 further demonstrate that serial adjacency alone cannot distinguish deliberate sequential batching from ordinary busy-server queue drainage.

Do not add random jitter or impose `sequence_no` as execution order to resolve these ambiguities. Preserve ties and alternative feasible schedules. A source sequence can order insertion within that source, but neither establishes physical order nor synchronizes different systems. Investigate G5's two backward database-time jumps before treating source and database order as equivalent.

# 6. Data-collection redesign

1. **Separate execution from administration.** Record per-item actual start and physical finish, group/setup start and finish, final release, form submission, and database insertion as distinct fields or events. Mark every timestamp as instrument-observed, operator-entered, inferred or retrospectively assigned. Preserve raw values and unavailable execution fields rather than copying submission time into them. Prioritize G6, G2 and G3 because semantic substitution can overwhelm timestamp precision.

2. **Align clocks and document quantization.** Retain source event time, source time zone/offset, clock synchronization state, measured drift/error bounds, event creation time and central ingestion time. Document rounding direction, truncation, granularity, display/export precision and manual backdating rules separately from accuracy. Preserve unrounded raw ticks where possible. Prioritize the G7 quantizer and G8 queue/scanner/safety alignment; keep queue-arrival precision separate from local precision.

3. **Recover identity and capacity.** Retain badge/session identity alongside the shared account, machine/workstation ID, operator-to-workstation assignment and changing team size. Record whether work requires exclusive attention. Capture workstation capacity and mode: G1 needs fixture-slot and labeling-head semantics, not just nominal capacity four; G5 needs actual analyst attendance and assignment, not “up to three.”

4. **Represent groups explicitly.** Add operational batch, fixture/cycle and folder IDs, item membership, group creation/release times and the reason for grouping. Use a separate submission-group ID for database entry so an operational batch is not inferred from a common insert. Multiple cases and one cycle can be linked through an object relationship or membership table while preserving case-level events.

5. **Capture resource state and queue admission.** Record available/unavailable periods with reasons, safety/setup/other-duty assignments, dispatch, individual service admission, pause/resume, task switching and active-work segments. Link the safety event to the actual resource and clock. Distinguish first response from cumulative active service and from time waiting while a task is open.

6. **Validate at the source and retain auditability.** Alert on impossible ordering after accounting for clock/precision bounds, unexplained zero durations, capacity violations, shared identities without assignments and source/database order reversals. Preserve original records, event IDs, source sequence and correction provenance. Validate one complete observed cycle or work session in each ambiguous area before retrospectively changing timestamps.

# 7. Final adjudication

- **High-confidence temporal conclusions:** G4 and G8 show serial activity intervals; G2 shows common group-open starts; G3 shows common reported completion; G5's identity is pooled; G6's execution lifecycle is suspect; and G7's timing is coarsened and unsuitable for literal waiting calculations. G1 strongly supports a shared machine-cycle envelope and plausibly a four-item operational batch, with lower confidence in simultaneous individual labeling. G8's safety-driven capacity-loss explanation remains provisional.

- **Queue-analysis treatment:** Exclude G6 from point estimates of execution waiting and service until substantive execution times are recovered, while retaining its administrative closure data. Exclude G7 from exact queue analysis or interval-censor it under a validated rounding/clock model. For G2, report queue-to-group-admission separately and withhold individual execution-wait estimates. G3's start-based waits can be used conditionally if starts and clock alignment are validated; its individual completion/service estimates require validation or bounds. G5 can support descriptive queue-to-recorded-start values conditionally, but not individual active service or utilization. G1/G4/G8 provide the strongest candidates for conditional waiting estimates after lifecycle and queue-clock validation.

- **Operational validation before performance claims:** Verify G1's internal cycle and capacity semantics, G2's setup and individual starts, G3's actual finish/release distinction, G5's badge assignments and active attention, G6's overwritten fields, G7's rounding, and G8's resource availability and clock offset. G4 needs group-policy evidence before its sequential signature is called deliberate batching. No cluster supplies enough coverage or capacity history to establish shift utilization, a steady-state throughput rate, or an end-to-end process bottleneck.

PM4Py's labels are useful reproducible descriptions of recorded timing. Performance conclusions depend on whether those timestamps and identities measure the execution quantities being analyzed.
