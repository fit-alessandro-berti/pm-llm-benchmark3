# Digital Twin for Building-Permit Process Simulation

## 1. Model Scope and State Variables

### Digital Twin Boundary
- **Scope**: End-to-end building-permit process from application submission to permit issuance/rejection, including rework loops and cross-case dependencies.
- **Level of Detail**: Activity-level simulation with resource contention, calendar effects, and case attributes (type, district, architect, value, risk flags).
- **Time Horizon**: 18-month simulation with non-stationary demand patterns and policy interventions.

### Entities and State Variables
| Entity | State Variables |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Case** | `case_id`, `case_type`, `district`, `architect_id`, `application_value`, `requires_fire`, `requires_environment`, `requires_public_notice`, `status`, `arrival_time`, `due_date`, `priority`, `related_cases` (list), `objection_flag` (boolean) |
| **Resource** | `resource_id`, `role` (clerk/planner/fire/environmental/inspector/legal), `capacity`, `calendar` (shift patterns, holidays), `current_load`, `specialization` (e.g., Simple renovation planners) |
| **Queue** | `queue_id`, `activity`, `cases`, `max_size` (unlimited for most queues), `prioritization_rule` (FIFO, SLA-based, architect bundling) |
| **Calendar** | `calendar_id`, `type` (resource/holiday/council meeting), `start_time`, `end_time`, `recurrence` (e.g., weekly for inspectors) |

### Resources and Calendars
- **Human Resources**:
  - **Clerks**: 5 FTE, Monday-Friday 08:00-16:00 (Monday dedicated to portal support).
  - **Planners**: 8 FTE, Monday-Friday 09:00-17:00 (2 specialized for Simple renovation).
  - **Fire Reviewers**: 2 FTE equivalent, Tuesday-Thursday only.
  - **Environmental Reviewers**: 3 FTE, Monday-Friday (40% reserved for non-permit duties).
  - **Inspectors**: 4 FTE, field visits Tuesday-Friday (travel time varies by district).
  - **Legal Officers**: 2 FTE, no work during council meeting weeks (except urgent cases).
- **System Resources**: Unlimited capacity (e.g., portal submissions, permit issuance).
- **Applicant/Third-Party**: Modeled as external actors with response-time distributions.

### Cross-Case Dependencies
1. **Architect Bundling**: Cases from the same architect in the same district can be batched for inspection scheduling, reducing travel time but increasing wait time for the first case.
2. **Geographic Bundling**: Inspections in the same district can be grouped to reduce travel time.
3. **Objection Propagation**: Public-notice duration extends if objections are detected (modeled via proxy: longer public-notice periods).
4. **Related Applications**: Multiple applications from the same architect may share rework loops or dependencies (e.g., if one is rejected, others may be affected).

### Queues and Prioritization
- **Queues**: Each activity (e.g., Planning review) has a dedicated queue with FIFO prioritization unless overridden by:
  - **SLA-based**: Cases approaching statutory deadlines (e.g., 60-day SLA for Simple renovation).
  - **Architect Bundling**: Cases from the same architect are prioritized to form inspection batches.
  - **Aging Cases**: Cases older than a threshold (e.g., 30 days) are prioritized for Planner reserve.

## 3. Assumptions

### Arrivals
1. **Non-Stationary Demand**: Arrival rates follow a seasonal pattern with peaks in March-May and September-November (1.7x baseline).
2. **Weekly Patterns**: Monday submissions are 2.2x higher than Wednesday submissions.
3. **Architect Bursts**: Top 20 architects submit 31% of cases, often in bursts (modeled as Poisson processes with higher intensity for these architects).
4. **Case Mix**: Case-type proportions (Simple renovation 45%, New residential 28%, Commercial 17%, Complex variance 10%) are stable unless interventions alter them.

### Service Times
1. **Activity Durations**: Service times are log-normally distributed with parameters estimated from the log (e.g., Planning review: median 3.5h, p90 14h).
2. **Travel Time**: Inspection travel time depends on district (D3/D5 have longer travel times).
3. **Legal Check**: Affected by council meeting weeks (no work during these weeks).
4. **Public-Notice Period**: Fixed minimum 14 days; extended to 60 days with objections (modeled probabilistically).

### Routing
1. **Conditional Reviews**: Fire-safety review is required if `requires_fire=yes` or building area exceeds a threshold (inferred from case type).
2. **Public-Notice**: Required for Complex variance and some New residential cases (modeled via `requires_public_notice` flag).
3. **Decision Drafting**: Waits for all required reviews (Planning, Fire, Environmental, Public-notice, Inspection).
4. **Rework**: Probability of rework after Planning review, Inspection, Legal check, or appeal is estimated from the log.

### Resources/Calendars
1. **Part-Time Resources**: Fire reviewers work only Tuesday-Thursday; inspectors are field-based Tuesday-Friday.
2. **Council Meetings**: Legal officers do not work during council meeting weeks (except urgent cases).
3. **Specialization**: 2 planners handle only Simple renovation cases.
4. **Capacity**: Human resources have fixed FTE capacities; system tasks have unlimited capacity.

### Rework
1. **Rework Triggers**: Estimated from the log (e.g., 19% of cases require missing documents after Completeness check).
2. **Rework Loops**: Modeled as probabilistic loops (e.g., 10% chance of rework after Inspection).
3. **Appeals**: Complex variance cases may enter appeal loops (modeled as additional rework stages).

### Applicant/Third-Party Response
1. **Applicant Delays**: Response to "Request missing documents" is heavy-tailed (median 5 days, p90 15 days).
2. **Third-Party Delays**: Objection processing is modeled via extended public-notice periods.
3. **Batching**: Applicants may bundle related applications (e.g., same architect) to reduce inspection travel time.

### Cross-Case Dependencies
1. **Architect Bundling**: Cases from the same architect in the same district can be batched for inspection scheduling.
2. **Geographic Bundling**: Inspections in the same district can be grouped to reduce travel time.
3. **Objection Propagation**: Public-notice duration extends if objections are detected (modeled probabilistically).

### Non-Stationary Demand

### Missing Data

## 5. Warm-up, Replications, and Uncertainty Treatment

### Warm-up and Run Length
- **Warm-up Period**: 3 months (to stabilize queues and resource utilization).
- **Simulation Length**: 18 months (aligned with event-log horizon).
- **Replications**: 30 replications per scenario (to achieve 95% confidence intervals with ±5% margin for key metrics).

### Random Seeds and Confidence Intervals
- **Seeds**: Use a fixed seed per replication (e.g., `seed = 42 + replication_id`) for reproducibility.
- **Confidence Intervals**: Report 95% CI for:
  - Median/mean duration by case type.
  - SLA compliance (e.g., % cases completed within 60 days).
  - Resource utilization (e.g., planner utilization).
  - Backlog size at end of simulation.

### Sensitivity Analysis
1. **Heavy-Tailed Durations**: Test robustness to log-normal vs. Pareto distributions for service times.
2. **Objection Probability**: Vary objection extension probability (e.g., 10% vs. 30% increase in public-notice duration).
3. **Resource Capacity**: Test ±20% variation in resource FTE.
4. **Arrival Seasonality**: Test ±10% variation in peak/off-peak arrival rates.

### Handling Heavy Tails
- Use **log-normal** distributions for service times (e.g., Environmental review).
- For applicant delays (e.g., "Receive missing documents"), use **Pareto** distributions with shape parameter estimated from the log.
- Truncate distributions at p99 to avoid extreme outliers.

## 6. Validation Strategy

### Face Validation
- **Stakeholder Review**: Present simulation outputs to municipal process owners for feedback on realism (e.g., queue lengths, durations).
- **Process Walkthrough**: Simulate a known case (e.g., P001) and verify step-by-step against the event log.

### Event-Log Replay Validation
- **Replay Metrics**: Compare simulated vs. real log metrics:
  - Case mix by type.
  - Variant frequencies.
  - Median/mean duration by activity.
  - Resource utilization.
- **Statistical Tests**: Use Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests for duration distributions.

### Distributional Validation
- **Service/Waiting Times**: Compare empirical vs. simulated distributions (e.g., Q-Q plots for Planning review durations).
- **Arrival Process**: Compare simulated vs. real arrival patterns (e.g., monthly seasonality).

### Resource-Utilization Validation
- **Queue Lengths**: Compare simulated vs. real queue lengths at key activities (e.g., Planning review).
- **Resource Idle Time**: Validate against HR data for planner/fire reviewer calendars.

### Bottleneck Validation
- **Identify Bottlenecks**: Compare simulated bottlenecks (e.g., Legal check during council meeting weeks) with real-world observations.
- **Policy Impact**: Validate that interventions (e.g., D4 temporary inspector) reduce bottlenecks in peak months.

### Out-of-Sample Validation
- **Holdout Data**: Reserve 20% of the log for validation (not used in calibration).
- **Predictive Metrics**: Compare simulated vs. real outcomes for holdout cases (e.g., duration, rework).
