# 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

The logical dependency map differs from a literal reading of the directly-follows graph (DFG):

```text
Validated intake and case identity [A1 optional]
    -> confirmed product/exposure and required control path [A2 optional]
         -> KYC work [A3 optional; A4 supports mismatch resolution]
         -> document completeness [A5] -> normalization [A6]
                                         -> human credit analysis
         -> authorized collateral request and valuation, if required [A7 optional]

Committee readiness = cleared KYC
                    AND required normalized financials
                    AND accepted valuation, if required
                    AND reconciled credit-analysis notes
    -> pack assembly [A8 optional] + independent completion where required
    -> authorized committee decision
    -> matching contract [A9 optional]
    -> signing only after verified KYC clearance [A10 optional]
    -> activation only after all required controls [A11 optional]

A12 supports control evidence, unresolved-issue ownership, escalation,
and release status across the relevant stages.
```

Credit analysis and collateral work may overlap where policy permits. The observed `Credit analysis -> Collateral valuation request` edge, with 2,400 transitions, describes current sequencing; it does not prove that a completed credit analysis is always necessary to initiate valuation. Conversely, earlier ordering must not be assumed permissible without validating authorization, eligibility, and avoidable valuation costs.

**Upstream data quality.** A2 supplies decisions on required documents, collateral, and exposure controls, but the A2 module itself is not mandatory: verified human classification can satisfy the prerequisite. A5 improves document availability; A6 still needs validity and numerical checks. A6 then supplies accepted financial data to credit analysis and committee readiness. A8 cannot repair contradictory source artifacts, and A9 cannot infer missing approval terms. Every downstream step needs the current version of the underlying entity, documents, and approval; material changes invalidate affected readiness checks.

**Concurrency and joins.** KYC and financial review often run concurrently despite sequential system records. The 7,250 `Classify -> KYC` and 3,940 `Classify -> Financial document review` transitions do not establish mutually exclusive routes. Use lifecycle timestamps and business validation to recover overlap. At an AND-join, readiness is governed by the last required artifact, not the sum of branch durations. Making financials ready earlier may have little immediate cycle-time effect if valuation remains last; clearing a mismatch has substantial effect when that mismatch determines readiness. Measure which prerequisite actually holds each case.

**Legal and compliance dependencies.** KYC clearance must precede actual signing across every channel. For High-exposure SME cases, a different accountable person must complete committee packaging from the person who completed credit analysis; an automation account is not an independent reviewer. Contract terms must match the approved decision. Committee processing applies to the relevant variants, not every application: the 3,900 committee decisions and 7,600 contract preparations indicate different paths. Other paths still need a policy-authorized decision and applicable controls.

A12 records evidence and remediation; it does not itself confer KYC clearance or interpret unresolved policy. Its scoped core integration must connect control disposition to enforceable release status. Where a channel cannot consume that status, an authorized human must verify authoritative evidence before release, and that channel remains outside unattended processing. An unresolved or stale control status blocks release. A9, A10, and A11 therefore depend on proven control capabilities, but not necessarily on purchasing A12 specifically.

The 380 `Resolve entity mismatch -> Contract preparation` transitions warrant investigation. They may represent a valid status update, incomplete logging, or a bypass; they are not proof of clearance. Similarly, absence of a remediation event is not proof that all controls passed.

**Human escalation capacity.** A3 and A4 depend on a staffed compliance exception service, with accountable case owners and backup reviewers. Three specialists, with referrals concentrated on two seniors, cannot absorb unlimited uncertain outputs. A6 depends on analyst validation capacity, especially for poor scans and complex accounts. A8 requires independent reviewers for High-exposure cases even if assembly is automated. A12 needs compliance and legal owners able to resolve issues rather than merely record them. Human responsibility remains explicit throughout.

**External valuation.** A7 depends on partner responsiveness and quality. The 58h wait and 19% late rate cannot be eliminated by internal routing alone. Prioritize authorized requests early enough to overlap other work, use qualified backup valuers, and escalate overdue work. Rejected or disputed valuations remain incomplete artifacts. Equipment finance, with 71% requiring collateral, merits separate analysis from Working-capital, at 18%; aggregate averages would hide this difference.

**Integration constraint.** In the recommended portfolio, reserve the first six months' two deep core-banking integrations for A12 and A6. Implement A5 through the existing customer portal and an approved document/status feed, with human reconciliation where needed. This is a planning assumption to validate, not a claim that portal work is free. If A5 requires another deep core integration, keep its initial scope standalone and defer that connection until month seven. A4's core integration also starts no earlier than month seven. No additional automated classifier, pack generator, contract generator, or bot is hidden inside these four modules.

# 3. Portfolio alternatives

Each portfolio contains four modules. Each includes a control-strengthening module in its first wave, and all require valid manual controls for functions they do not automate.

| Portfolio | Modules | What it optimizes | What it sacrifices | Feasible first-six-month integration allocation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Quick-win** | **A1, A2, A9, A10** | Visible administrative savings, cleaner initial routing, quicker preparation and signing of already-ready cases | Direct relief of mismatch specialists, document loops, normalization load, and external valuation waits | A9 and A1 receive the two deep integrations. A9 launches in the first wave for a validated product subset with control checks. A2 starts as a human-confirmed recommender; A10 follows after month six and after signing gates pass |
| **Bottleneck/synchronization — recommended** | **A12, A5, A6, A4** | Reliable readiness of the KYC and financial branches; fewer repeated requests; relief of scarce analyst and specialist capacity | Broad intake labor savings, automated valuation routing, committee assembly, contract generation, and signing adoption | A12 and A6 receive the two deep integrations. First wave is A12 plus A5 using existing portal capabilities. A4 integrates after month six |
| **Compliance-first** | **A12, A2, A3, A9** | Correct control paths, supervised KYC triage, traceable remediation, and decision-to-contract consistency | Direct normalization relief, entity-resolution assistance, customer document improvements, and valuation/signing delays | A12 and A3 receive the two deep integrations. First wave includes A12 and human-confirmed A2. A9 integrates after month six |

The quick-win portfolio is viable only if A9 can verify required inputs and enforce its control checks in the first wave. If that prerequisite fails, releasing A1 alone would not satisfy the control-module requirement. The compliance-first portfolio keeps A3 advisory: it does not authorize automatic clearance of low-scored hits.

The recommended portfolio addresses synchronization primarily between KYC and financial readiness. It deliberately leaves the external valuation branch without A7. This is the largest limitation for Equipment finance, and makes A7 the leading reserve candidate if product-level evidence shows valuation is the dominant remaining constraint. Funding A7 would require replacing an uncommitted module or a later-year budget, not adding a fifth module.

# 4. Expected process-level effects

These are directional expectations to test on comparable cohorts. Benefits must not be added across overlapping branches or counted again when one module improves another's input. A shorter activity queue is useful only if safe completion improves or resource demand falls without moving equivalent work elsewhere.

## Quick-win portfolio: A1, A2, A9, A10

- **Cycle time:** A1 shortens the reliable `Receive -> Register` path, observed 11,880 times, and A2 improves the 11,680 `Register -> Classify` transitions. However, registration's 1.5h median wait is small beside mismatch resolution, financial work, valuation, and signing waits. A9 and A10 could materially improve the final stages for already-cleared cases, particularly straightforward variants that avoid committee and collateral dependencies. Overall improvement remains limited when upstream prerequisites arrive late.
- **Waiting time:** Back-office waiting should fall, and clean cases may see less of the 22h contract-preparation and 44h signing waits. The 31h mismatch queue, 20h normalization queue, 28h credit-analysis queue, and 58h external wait remain. Automating reminders does not guarantee quicker customer response. High-exposure cases still face the weekly committee cadence.
- **Rework:** A2 should prevent some wrong-path errors. A9 directly targets `Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue` (650) and the 510 returns to preparation. Credit-line renewal is a valuable test segment because its contract-remediation rate is 14%, versus 8.5% overall. The financial-review/document-request loop remains largely untreated. Erroneous duplicate detection or classification can introduce new rework.
- **Handovers:** Fewer routine clerk transfers, contract drafting exchanges, and RM signing chases are plausible. Staff instead handle duplicate disputes, classification uncertainty, identity problems, and failed e-sign attempts. A9's saved drafting effort does not remove approval or legal accountability.
- **Resource demand:** Back-office effort decreases, but the observed utilization below 65% after 15:00 suggests scheduling and redeployment opportunities before staffing reductions. Cleaner intake may increase demand on nine analysts and three compliance specialists. Four legal staff may receive sharper batches after upstream clearance; A9 can reduce drafting effort but legal exceptions persist. A10 can shift work to customer support and activation operations.
- **Control violations:** Confirmed classification and A9 checks should reduce wrong control paths and mismatched contracts. A10 must block signing without verified KYC and consent. These modules do not inherently solve the 5.8% High-exposure four-eyes violations during month-end rush: independent completion must be enforced through existing workflow and reviewer allocation. Faster movement without those controls could amplify violations.

## Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A12, A5, A6, A4

- **Cycle time:** A4 targets the clearest case-level critical-path evidence: mismatch resolution lies on the critical path in 82% of affected cases. Its benefit applies to that subset, not 82% of all applications. A5 and A6 improve the concurrent financial branch, making their combination with A4 more valuable at shared readiness points. High-exposure cases may reach a committee cutoff earlier, potentially avoiding an extra weekly cycle, but committee cadence itself remains unchanged.
- **Waiting time:** A4 aims to reduce the 31h wait for mismatch specialists. A5 targets repeated customer requests around financial review's 18h queue, while A6 addresses normalization's 20h queue and capacity feeding credit analysis's 28h queue. Measure customer-response time separately: reminders cannot remove unavailable documents. Equipment finance may still wait predominantly on valuation; the 1,980 `Wait for external valuation -> Committee packaging` transitions show why internal improvements will not eliminate that dependency.
- **Rework:** A5 targets `Financial document review -> Request missing documents` (3,300) and the 2,800 returns, together with the 52% repeated-request finding. A6 addresses the 16% normalization exception rate, provided reconciliation catches extraction errors. A4 seeks fewer repeated `Resolve entity mismatch -> KYC check` transitions (1,160), not the removal of necessary KYC rechecks. A12 supports correct resolution of contract-control loops, but does not deliver A9's automatic contract matching or generation.
- **Handovers:** Complete, consolidated requests reduce RM/back-office/analyst exchanges. Evidence prepared by A4 should reduce repeated referrals from KYC operations to the same senior specialists. A12 gives control exceptions one owner and an explicit next action. Required specialist approval and High-exposure independent review remain; success means fewer unproductive transfers, not indiscriminately fewer reviewers.
- **Resource demand:** A6 releases analyst capacity before A4 increases KYC-cleared inflow. Initially, analyst validation and compliance review can increase. Deploy back-office staff with suitable training to document assistance and evidence preparation, using afternoon availability where demonstrated. Keep clearance and credit judgment with authorized specialists. Plan legal and operations capacity for newly released cases and month-end peaks; otherwise a reduced KYC queue simply becomes a contract queue.
- **Control violations:** A12 provides the first-wave control benefit through traceable remediation, verified readiness, and enforced release conditions. It must prevent unresolved KYC from reaching signing and prevent same-person completion in High-exposure cases. Contract matching remains an evidenced human check. Report both the 6.5% provisional-signing finding and the 1.1% requiring remediation; the smaller figure does not make the other premature signings acceptable. Detected issues may initially rise as visibility improves, while new release violations should fall.

## Compliance-first portfolio: A12, A2, A3, A9

- **Cycle time:** Better classification and earlier control resolution should reduce avoidable restarts. A9 improves downstream processing for cases whose evidence is already clean. Initially, stopping unsafe provisional signing and investigating previously hidden issues can lengthen reported processing time; that is preferable to treating an invalid shortcut as improvement.
- **Waiting time:** A3 may reduce routine KYC triage effort, but difficult mismatches remain in the 31h queue without A4. False positives could increase waits at the two heavily relied-upon senior specialists. A12 and A9 can reduce contract holds and repeated preparation; financial normalization, external valuation, customer-response time, and committee cadence remain largely unchanged.
- **Rework:** A2 targets the 8% classification exception rate, while A9 targets decision/contract mismatches, especially renewals. A12 makes remediation evidence reusable and ownership clear. Human review of A3 output must catch false negatives before clearance. Document-request repetition and poor financial normalization remain major sources of rework.
- **Handovers:** Structured KYC referrals and linked control evidence reduce clarification exchanges. Conversely, conservative escalation and independent approvals can increase necessary handovers. Assess whether transfers deliver a completed decision or simply redistribute unresolved tasks.
- **Resource demand:** Compliance takes on model validation, quality sampling, overrides, and policy adjudication. This is demanding for a three-person specialist pool. A9 saves drafting effort for legal ops, while policy and rule maintenance consume some of that capacity. Faster compliant inflow still reaches nine analysts without A6's relief; back-office labor savings are limited because A1 is absent.
- **Control violations:** This portfolio offers the broadest direct coverage of classification, supervised KYC triage, remediation, and contract consistency. A12 must enforce four-eyes completion and pre-signing KYC; A9 alone does not satisfy either across all channels. It improves control reliability only if audit trails, independent review, and rule ownership work in practice, including month end.

# 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Risk introduced or amplified | Compensating controls and accountable handling |
|---|---|
| **Opaque suggestions, overrides, or automated actions** | Maintain a tamper-evident case history linking input/source versions, model and rule versions, suggested output, confidence where relevant, human acceptance or override and reason, responsible identity, final decision, and timestamps. Preserve evidence sufficient to reconstruct the decision. Compliance owns control accountability; technology operates the tooling |
| **False negatives in KYC or entity matching** | Validate on adjudicated examples and difficult segments. Keep A3 advisory and require authorized human disposition; A4 must present evidence without autonomously declaring clearance or merging entities. Sample apparently straightforward accepted cases as well as escalations. Any critical miss triggers containment and investigation before expansion |
| **False positives and exception overload** | Track referral rates, unnecessary escalations, handling time, and queue age by reason and segment. Staff the review queue before increasing coverage. Adjust thresholds only through approved validation, not simply to make the backlog disappear. Provide escalation to backup specialists |
| **Wrong product/exposure path or duplicate suppression** | Require confirmed classification for control selection; route uncertainty to an authorized reviewer and withhold release pending resolution. Review exposure-boundary cases and conflicting identifiers. Do not silently reject or consolidate a possible duplicate; preserve recovery and reconciliation paths |
| **A6 produces credible but incorrect financial figures** | Link extracted fields to source pages; validate accounting identities, reporting periods, currency, units, signs, and totals. Reject unsupported or poor-quality inputs into a staffed manual route. Require analyst acceptance of financials and retain human credit judgment, especially for High-exposure cases |
| **Model drift or changing document/customer mix** | Version models and mappings; monitor accuracy, confidence distributions, overrides, and exception rates by product, exposure, source channel, and PDF quality. Investigate changes against adjudicated samples. Revalidate before rollout of new versions and support rollback |
| **A5 increases customer friction** | Co-design instructions with RMs; issue a consolidated, product-appropriate checklist, explain requests, show accepted documents, and stop nudges when the condition is satisfied. Cap reminders and provide human help and accessible alternatives. Distinguish missing from invalid documents; renewals need current required evidence without indiscriminately requesting everything again |
| **Four-eyes control becomes a superficial bot approval** | Record who completed credit analysis and who independently completed packaging. Block identical-person completion for High-exposure cases. Attribute accepted automated work to the accountable humans; a service account cannot supply the second pair of eyes. Reserve independent reviewer cover at month end and test the control under peak workload |
| **Premature signing or activation through stale status or another channel** | Verify authoritative KYC clearance for the correct entity before signing; revalidate after material changes. Block unresolved or stale states. Cover portal, RM-assisted, and manual channels. Activation additionally requires the valid executed contract, matching approval, and all required control evidence. No outage fallback may bypass these conditions |
| **Outdated legal rules or unresolved policy ambiguity** | Legal/compliance owners approve and version rules, effective dates, templates, and exception policy. Compare contract terms to the authoritative decision and amended approvals. Prioritize renewal scenarios in validation. A12 escalates ambiguity to named policy owners; it does not invent a resolution |
| **Duplicate writes, integration failure, or inconsistent status** | Use case identifiers, idempotent actions, reconciliation, access controls, and monitored failures. Link downstream actions to the exact evidence version checked. Define a manual fallback preserving the same controls; stop unattended processing if authoritative status cannot be established |
| **External routing sacrifices valuation quality** | Use approved, suitably qualified valuers; measure timeliness alongside rejected valuations, disputes, and rework. Confirm authorization before ordering early and avoid duplicate requests. Escalate missed commitments and use qualified alternatives where available. Do not treat an unaccepted valuation as ready |
| **Automation shifts work into invisible human queues** | Give every exception an owner, reason, service target, aging alert, backup, and terminal disposition. Include research, output verification, customer support, and downstream remediation in workload measures. Reduce automated release volume or coverage when review capacity is exceeded; do not close exceptions merely to meet a metric |

# 6. Recommended phased rollout

Deploy **A12 → A5 → A6 → A4**, with A12 and A5 forming the first production wave. The plan uses four modules in the year. Dates are conditional on readiness gates; a failed gate delays expansion rather than justifying a control bypass.

| Phase | Modules and work | Integration use | Release gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Month 1: establish evidence and controls** | Baseline complete case cohorts and open work; validate actual KYC/document concurrency and artifact readiness. Confirm owners for KYC, contract matching, four-eyes review, model validation, and exceptions. Correct unsafe operating procedures and arrange reviewer coverage immediately | Validate A12/A6 designs and whether A5 can use existing portal connections; no third deep integration is committed | Reliable case linkage and status definitions; agreed pilot cohorts and acceptance criteria; all signing channels identified; staffed fallback and escalation; explicit legal/compliance policy for unresolved cases |
| **Months 2–3: first production wave** | **A12 first**, with control evidence, ownership, overdue escalation, and validated release status. Then **A5** for selected product/exposure combinations, using consolidated RM-approved instructions and portal feedback | **Deep integration 1: A12.** A5 uses existing portal/document interfaces or a limited sidecar with human reconciliation | Demonstrate blocked signing for uncleared KYC, blocked same-person High-exposure completion, and evidenced contract/decision checking. A12 closure must require valid disposition. A5 must reduce avoidable repeat requests without worsening abandonment, complaints, or analyst backlog |
| **Months 4–6: create analyst capacity** | **A6**, initially on supported, good-quality statements; begin in shadow mode, then analyst-approved production. Coordinate expansion with A5's cleaner inflow | **Deep integration 2: A6.** No other module receives a new deep core integration before month seven | Independent validation meets risk-owner-approved field and reconciliation tolerances. Poor-quality documents reliably fall back to humans. Total analyst effort per comparable case, including verification and rework, improves; credit-analysis queue tails do not materially worsen |
| **Months 7–9: address the specialist blocker** | **A4**, starting with well-understood mismatch types and evidence gathering. Retain human clearance. Train qualified backups, standardize case evidence, and expand only as review capacity permits | A4's deep integration may now proceed. Any deferred A5 connection is separately scheduled within IT capacity | No critical incorrect entity linkage or unauthorized clearance in validation; difficult cases reliably escalate. Mismatch queue age and repeated referrals improve, while compliance, analyst, and legal queues remain within agreed capacity limits |
| **Months 10–12: scale and verify** | Expand the same four modules across validated variants and document sources. Evaluate through a month-end peak and several committee cycles. Adjust staffing and schedules; retain manual handling for unsupported cases | Stabilize the four modules; no A7, A8, A9, A10, or A11 module is added | Sustained improvement in safe end-to-end completion or net constrained-resource demand, no control deterioration, functioning audit reconstruction and fallback, and no hidden growth in exceptions, withdrawals, or downstream tails |

**Capacity actions are part of rollout.** Back office's afternoon utilization finding supports checking whether suitable staff can provide document assistance and prepare evidence; it does not establish full-day excess capacity or qualify them to clear KYC. Compliance should standardize evidence requirements and develop authorized backup coverage to reduce dependence on two seniors. Analysts need protected validation time before A6's savings materialize. Legal ops needs a staffing plan for increased releases and month-end peaks. Maintain separate accountability for High-exposure analysis and packaging even under deadline pressure.

For collateral cases, use existing staff to review when authorized valuation requests can start and follow up on late partners. This is an operating change, not an unbudgeted A7 deployment, and its benefits should be measured separately. Likewise, align preparation with existing committee cutoffs without assuming automation changes the committee's weekly schedule.

**Monitoring and decision criteria:**

- **End-to-end outcomes:** Measure application-to-activation median and tail times, safe completions per period, and age of open work. Segment by Micro/SME/High-exposure, product, collateral requirement, mismatch presence, document quality, and automation eligibility. Track withdrawals, rejections, and customer abandonment separately. The difference between 12,000 receipts and 7,100 activations is not evidence that all remaining cases failed; cases may be open, rejected, withdrawn, or outside the same cohort.
- **Waiting and synchronization:** Track elapsed and active handling time separately, using consistent business/calendar definitions. Measure mismatch queue time, customer-response time, normalization and credit-analysis queues, time waiting for each committee prerequisite, missed committee cutoffs, legal queue peaks, and signing waits. Reconstruct branch overlap from reliable timestamps; do not add the supplied medians or assume DFG edges prove serial execution.
- **Rework and handovers:** Monitor repeated document requests per case, first-time-complete submissions, financial reconciliation failures, mismatch-to-KYC returns, repeated specialist referrals, and contract-remediation loops. Distinguish necessary independent reviews from avoidable exchanges. Reconcile the activity table's 12% contract exceptions with the 8.5% control-remediation measure rather than assuming their definitions are identical.
- **Resource demand:** Measure work arrival rates, work in progress, handling time including verification, exception complexity, overtime, and available qualified reviewers by time of day. Track referral concentration on the two senior compliance specialists and analyst workload across review, normalization, analysis, and packaging. A lower automated task time is insufficient if total human effort or downstream queue age rises.
- **Control quality:** Track pre-signing KYC violations, High-exposure same-person completion, mismatched contract terms, unsupported overrides, false positives/negatives, and audit completeness. Use the stated 6.5%, 1.1%, 5.8%, and 14% findings as separate baselines with verified denominators and periods. Distinguish newly detected historical defects from new violations created after deployment.
- **Customer and adoption outcomes:** Track checklist completion, repeated/contradictory requests, time to usable documents, complaints, abandonment, RM overrides, and use of human assistance. Assess whether eligible users actually adopt the module, not only whether the feature is available.

Before each pilot, process and risk owners should approve acceptance thresholds, observation periods, and sample coverage using measured volumes and case mix. Use staged rollout or matched comparison cohorts, accounting for month-end conditions and committee schedules. Do not claim causality from an unadjusted before/after average.

Confirmed premature signing, an unauthorized clearance, or a four-eyes bypass requires immediate containment of the affected route, case review, and correction before expansion. Zero observed incidents in a small pilot is insufficient assurance: include deliberate tests of missing evidence, stale status, changed approvals, duplicate actions, low-quality inputs, and system unavailability. Persistently rising exception tails or downstream work in progress is a capacity gate, even when an individual module meets its accuracy target.

# 7. Final recommendation

Choose the **bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A12, A5, A6, and A4**. It combines a first-wave control improvement with document-loop prevention, analyst capacity relief, and targeted assistance at a demonstrated KYC critical-path blocker. A5 provides an early visible benefit for staff and customers; A6 prepares the analyst pool before A4 releases more stalled cases. A12 makes unresolved controls and responsible human decisions part of the release process throughout.

Do **not** automate registration first in this portfolio. A1 is highly feasible and attractive for labor savings, but its eight-minute service activity and 1.5h wait do not dominate the documented synchronization delays. The back-office pool also has time-of-day capacity that deserves operational attention. Accelerating registration before improving financial readiness and exception capacity risks making the downstream queues larger.

The trade-off is deliberate: this portfolio leaves substantial valuation and signing waits, manual contract preparation, and committee cadence in place. A7 is the next candidate to reassess for collateral-heavy business, while A9 is a strong subsequent option for renewal-related contract rework. A1 becomes more compelling when downstream capacity can absorb its faster inflow and its savings have a credible redeployment plan. Judge the first year by faster, auditable, compliant completions and sustainable human workload—not by the number of activities automated.
