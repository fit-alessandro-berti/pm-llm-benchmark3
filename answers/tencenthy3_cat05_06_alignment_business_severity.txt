## 1. Materiality Framework

A severity rating must reflect business consequences, not the mechanical edit distance of an alignment. The following dimensions are combined into a 5point scale:

* **Control objective** – Does the case undermine a mandatory gate (fraud screen, manual approval, payment hold, notification)?
* **Amount / exposure** – Euro amounts above EUR 50k or any bankchange flag elevate potential loss.
* **Reversibility** – An executed payment is hard to reverse; a missing log entry is cheap to remediate.
* **Timing** – Paybeforeapproval (P3) is worse than a postpayment documentation gap.
* **Customer / regulatory harm** – Missing notification or deniedclaim mishandling creates compliance risk even when money is correct.
* **Recurrence** – Patterns repeated across many cases (e.g., retries, autoapprove violations) raise priority.
* **Evidence reliability** – External signed documents are stronger than mutable text links or absent logs.
* **Model scope** – Denied / withdrawn leads (P6) are out of scope for the payable model; forcing them produces artificial cost.

**Severity definitions (1–5)**  
5 = Critical: unauthorized or irreversibly wrong payment, missing mandatory fraud/approval control on high exposure.  
4 = Major: dataaware policy breach or weak control on nontrivial exposure.  
3 = Moderate: missing or weak evidence of a required step, but external proof or low harm.  
2 = Minor: label/scope mismatch with no financial impact.  
1 = Negligible: fully compliant, silent moves, or harmless telemetry/retry noise.

**Confidence** is the evidential certainty that the alignment semantics correspond to the real business state. It is independent of severity. A case can be severity 5 but medium confidence (e.g., `M(Fraud Screen)` may mean “not logged” rather than “not performed”); a case can be severity 1 with high confidence (UI telemetry). ## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

The supplied `raw_cost` uses a uniform penalty of 1 for every visible log/model move and 0 for hidden moves. This design decouples cost from business materiality in several ways:

1. **Costfunction design ignores data conditions.** CL609 and CL610 are fully synchronous (`raw_cost = 0`) because the process tree permits the AutoApprove branch for any claim. Yet both violate P1/P2 – CL610 even skips a mandatory fraud screen triggered by a sameday bank change. Zero cost masks a major control failure.
2. **Scope mismatch inflates cost without exposure.** CL607 and CL612 carry `raw_cost = 4` because the payable model forces missing `M(Assess Loss/Auto Approve/Pay Claim)` moves that are *correctly not performed* for denied/withdrawn claims. High cost here indicates modelscope error, not financial risk.
3. **Irreversibility is not weighted.** CL604 shows `raw_cost = 3` for a EUR 90k payment released before manual approval (P3 violation). The edit distance is modest, but the business consequence is potentially unrecoverable mispayment.
4. **Missingbutexternal evidence is underestimated.** CL606 has `raw_cost = 1` (`M(Assess Loss)`) while an external adjuster report exists; the single move cost hides an audittrail gap on a EUR 75k HIGHrisk claim. Conversely CL608’s `M(Notify Claimant)` costs 1, but weak mutable CRM evidence on a EUR 250k claim creates real compliance risk.
5. **Duplicated telemetry and retries add cost without meaning.** CL601 costs 3 due to `Open Workbench`, `Autosave`, `Close Workbench`; CL603 costs 1 for a retry duplicate. These are harmless technical noise (P5) yet contribute equally to raw cost with true control failures.
6. **Hidden moves are costfree but not riskfree in general.** CL605’s `T(tau,c=0)` is legitimate routing; however, a silent transition could in other settings conceal an unlogged decision. The zero cost is informative only because context confirms compliance.
7. **Activity granularity and label mismatch.** CL611 costs 2 because “Assess Damage” vs “Assess Loss” appear as log/model mismatches; the underlying control (assessment occurred, fraud screen done) is intact, showing cost reflects syntactic granularity, not exposure.

Thus raw cost is a *technical diagnostic*, not a risk measure. Business severity must be assigned by overlaying P1–P6, amounts, timing, and evidence reliability as done above.

traces = {
