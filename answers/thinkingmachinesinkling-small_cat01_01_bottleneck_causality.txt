## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Small randomized routing pilot**: randomly assign 10% of eligible low-to-medium cases (day and night) to Orion vs Nova, maintaining capacity buffers and existing routing rules elsewhere | Direct group effect isolated from selection / case-mix; tests whether label causes delay | Individual case; stratified by complexity and shift | Cap at ~3 cases/day; maintain certified coverage; no forced reassignment of high-complexity without consent; real-time wait monitoring | Compare DFG variants per arm; trace conformance; wait/service/rework decomposition; median and 90th percentile cycle | Unbiased ATE for subpopulation under randomization; reveals whether group effect exists when mix is forced | Stop if pilot branch mean cycle >20% above control or quality appeal rate >2×; rollback if staff overtime exceeds 15% |
| 2 | **Queue and compliance capacity**, not group label: add cross-trained certified reviewer hours during high-load night windows; streamline compliance check handoff; reduce queue accumulation | Queue_wait mechanism; tests whether delay is capacity/shift-constrained rather than group-inherent | Shift / queue state (not case label); interrupted time-series design | Do not change routing rules; protect existing case assignments; measure before/after over 4 weeks | Queue-state reconstruction from timestamps; conformance checking on compliance step; directly-follows analysis; wait_before_review trend | Identifies whether reducing queue reduces cycle independent of group; separates capacity from skill | Rollback if wait does not decrease after 2 weeks or if service errors increase |
| 3 | **Retrospective matching + mechanism study** + negative control: propensity-score match on complexity, shift, load, compliance; analyze mediation via compliance/rework paths; compare registration/registration-to-classify times as negative controls | Mechanism decomposition; detects unobserved confounding; validates measurement | Retrospective cases; matched pairs and sensitivity analyses | No operational disruption; test overlap; require >3 per cell | Group-specific DFG comparison; mediation path lengths; variant frequency; overlap diagnostics | Bounds direct vs indirect effects; sensitivity to unobserved_case_risk; detects selection if match fails | Stop analysis if overlap <20% common support, if n<3 in >30% of strata, or if negative control diverges (indicating measurement bias) |

**Final recommendation (120–200 words):**

Reject the 50% reassignment proposal as currently framed. The crude Orion–Nova gap is almost certainly confounded by complexity, shift, arrival load, and compliance requirements rather than caused by the resource-group label. Stratified comparisons show Orion is faster within comparable complexity bands, and standardized weighting reverses the aggregate sign. Because Orion hosts 93% of night work, holds more certified reviewers, and is routed the hardest cases, blind movement risks overloading Nova’s day-oriented, lower-certified capacity, increasing queue_wait rather than reducing it, and degrading quality—particularly if compliance and rework pathways are not scaled. Instead, launch Phase 1 randomized pilot for low/medium cases to estimate an unbiased subgroup effect, run Phase 2 capacity/comppliance interventions to attack the dominant wait mechanism, and use Phase 3 matching with process-mining DFG comparison to decompose direct versus mediated paths. Monitor process semantics—wait versus service, trace variants, conformance—not just cycle-time means. The safest path is to learn whether group assignment causes delay under controlled conditions before disrupting operational routing, and to treat queue and compliance capacity as the more actionable levers.
