### 1.4 Burden-of-proof approach

**Three levels must be kept apart.**
1. A *trace deviation* is a distance from M0. It proves nothing about compliance on its own.
2. A *norm violation* requires a norm in force, in scope, and unexcused.
3. *Misconduct* requires intent, concealment, or disregard of a known gate.

Only level 2 supports a compliance finding. Level 3 needs separate evidence that this analysis does not have for any case.

**Declaring noncompliance.** The reviewer bears the burden of showing all of the following:
- (a) an identifiable norm in force at event time;
- (b) that the case is within its scope;
- (c) reliable evidence of the act, meaning its order, time and actor identity;
- (d) that no valid, evidenced exception covers it.

The standard is preponderance for a control finding and clear-and-convincing evidence for a "severe" label. Model misfit alone never suffices.

**Accepting an exception.** The proponent bears the burden of showing all of the following:
- (a) written authority from a body competent to vary that norm;
- (b) that the authority was in force at event time;
- (c) that scope conditions were met, shown by contemporaneous data rather than after-the-fact reconstruction;
- (d) that the mandatory safeguards are evidenced.

Exceptions are construed strictly. Later ratification, belief that a change is coming, and factual need mitigate severity but do not convert a breach into compliance.

**Evidence gaps.**
- A missing record does not support a finding of noncompliance.
- It also does not support a finding of compliance.
- If the record was itself a mandated control artefact and stays unresolved after remediation, it becomes a recordkeeping finding.

**Dual attribution.** I attribute separately to the operator, who acted on what the system and published decisions allowed, and to governance, which designed, authorised or failed to sunset the regime. Operators who followed a published steering decision in good faith are not noncompliant merely because governance cut corners. Governance still carries the defect.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

**Thesis.** The operation has allowed exceptions to become practice, without formal authority, beyond their expiry, and in some cases against high-risk suppliers. M0's misfit metric understates the problem, because the worst control failures are sometimes invisible to it.

### 2.1 Formal authority
- CS-9 is controlled and formally approved. PD-3 is a meeting minute and an email, "not incorporated into CS-9 or M0". A steering committee can prioritise a pilot, but a standard that says *every* activation requires Manual_Procurement_Approval is not varied by minutes.
- The Compliance observer's own words defeat the authority claim. "Monitored exception" was followed by a denial that she approved weakening CS-9. On the strongest reading, all 312 pilot Auto_Approve cases lack a valid waiver, as do any deferred Quality_Reviews.
- PD-3 also defers Quality_Review past activation. CS-9 says Quality_Review comes *before* activation. That is a second derogation, equally unauthorised on this reading.

### 2.2 Expiry and normalization
- PD-3 ran 2026-06-01 to 07-31 and FD-7 ran 07-10 to 07-12, both with hard end dates.
- SUP-714 (08-02) and six further August cases used Auto_Approve after the window closed. The operators' belief that "approval was imminent" is reliance on a proposal, not a rule.
- SUP-705 (07-13) copied FD-7 behaviour the day after the directive "expired automatically". An automatic sunset was a deliberate design choice. Replacing it with "it was still a flood" makes every directive renewable by the people it constrains.
- Normalization is the mechanism. A temporary exception that nobody shuts off becomes the default.
- SUP-704's follow-up steps took place after FD-7 expired. FD-7 says nothing about completion after expiry.

### 2.3 Deviations M0 hides or fails to catch
- **Fit is not compliance.**
  - SUP-712 fits M0 perfectly, yet requester and approver are the same human under two aliases. That breaches the CS-9 segregation-of-duties rule, which M0 does not encode.
  - SUP-715 also fits, yet the quality reviewer and the approver are spouses in the same reporting line with no disclosure recorded.
  - Two of the three fitting cases in the sample have a non-control-flow problem.
- **Logging is now a control weakness.**
  - Moving Notify_Requester to a CRM without a reliable case link reduced audit visibility from 2% to 29% missing.
  - SUP-706 is unverifiable because the link is mutable text entered two days later. A notification that cannot be tied to a case by an immutable key cannot be relied on.
  - Shifting the evidence burden to the audited party's ability to reconstruct it afterwards is a control failure.

### 2.4 High-risk cases
- **SUP-703.** The pilot gate is a dropdown ("Operator selected 'pilot' despite ineligibility"). A HIGH-risk new foreign supplier bypassed manual approval and received a EUR 180,000 first purchase before Quality_Review.
  - At 14 clearly ineligible and 9 unknown out of 312, the pilot's gating control demonstrably failed.
  - This is exactly why CS-9 requires a human approver.
- **SUP-708.** Activation 40 minutes before sanctions clearance on a HIGH-risk supplier, with no pilot or emergency. No regime, including PD-3 and FD-7, tolerates this. Manual approval also came after activation.
  - "No transactions occurred" is luck and timing, not a control. The breach is that the supplier was live.
  - Internal Audit flagged it as potentially severe.

### 2.5 Why desirable outcomes do not retroactively amend policy
- SUP-707 and the 47 Request_More_Info cases may be good practice. Stakeholder approval is a reason to *amend* CS-9, not evidence that CS-9 was never binding.
- Operations' wish for a permanent auto-approval branch is an open proposal. No amendment exists.
- Retroactive label mapping (SUP-713) and "we were going to approve it" (SUP-714) have the same structure: governance catching up with practice after the fact.

### 2.6 What the prosecution must concede (genuine model and data defects)
- M0 has no loop, so SUP-707 and 47 cases are flagged for behaviour CS-9 does not prohibit. Request_More_Info is not noncompliance.
- M0's reject branch requires Quality_Review, which CS-9 does not. SUP-709 is a model defect at least in part.
- SUP-711 is an ETL duplicate. SUP-713 is a relabel. Both are artifacts.
- SUP-710's absence of Register and Quality_Review is unaddressed by CS-9, and a fresh sanctions check plus manual approval was done.
- SUP-702 and SUP-704 look like textbook uses of their overlays. The prosecution's case against them is about authority and evidence, not about the operators.
- Some of the notification gap is a real migration artifact.

## 3. Defense Brief

**Thesis.** M0 is a 2025 artefact that predates the pilot, the emergency directive, the CRM migration, and several normal routes. Most of its "deviations" are either permitted, out of scope, or measurement artefacts. The real risks are few and specific, and they should be isolated rather than hidden in a high overall deviation rate.

### 3.1 Concurrency, what M0 actually means
- M0 makes Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review concurrent, so the model itself says only that both precede the decision. Order between them is irrelevant.
- The real CS-9 invariant is narrower than M0. It says both are required *before activation*, not before any decision.
- M0 therefore over-constrains the reject path, which is why SUP-709 (rejected after sanctions failed) does not fit.
- A sanctioned or sanctions-flagged lead forced through Quality_Review would be wasteful and arguably undesirable.

### 3.2 Missing loops
- Request_More_Info occurs in 47 cases, none of which activated before the evidence arrived.
- SUP-707 gathered missing certificates through two requests before approval. That is stronger assurance than M0's single pass, and compliance agrees.
- M0 flags this as a deviation only because POWL has no loop in the model.
- Penalising it would push operators to hide rework or approve incomplete files.

### 3.3 Pilot and emergency overlays
- **SUP-702** matches PD-3 as written:
  - LOW-risk DE supplier in an approved group;
  - inside the pilot window;
  - Sanctions_Check before activation;
  - Quality_Review the next business day;
  - complete eligibility fields.
  - 289 of 312 (92.6%) appear eligible, so the pilot worked as designed in the great majority of cases.
- **SUP-704** matches FD-7 as written:
  - inside the window;
  - critical supplier;
  - sanctions first;
  - EUR 18,000 against a 20,000 cap;
  - daily monitoring;
  - quality and approval the next business day.
- Judging these against M0 is judging them against the wrong norm. The right object is M0 plus the overlay in force at the time.
- PD-3 was a documented, time-boxed, scoped decision that the Compliance observer knew about and described as a monitored exception. Operators who followed it were acting on a published organisational decision.

### 3.4 Post-expiry cases are governance lapses, not misconduct
- SUP-714 is LOW-risk and eligible, and the same safeguards were in place. The breach is that the pilot's end date was not enforced in the system and the permanent rule was late. That is a failure of sunset design, not of judgment.
- SUP-705 shows the same safeguards as SUP-704 (sanctions first, EUR 12,000 spend) in a flood that factually continued. The directive expiring automatically while recovery continued is a governance gap.
- Neither case involved concealment, and neither involved a high-risk supplier.

### 3.5 CRM migration and logging
- From 2026-07-01 Notify_Requester moved to a CRM, and about 70% of notifications mirror into the onboarding log. The remainder exist in the CRM.
- The 2% to 29% jump tracks the migration date. Measuring a service that moved out of the log as if the activity were skipped conflates observability with behaviour.
- SUP-702 has a CRM notification with a stable ID at +2 hours, and SUP-706 has a CRM message at +3 hours. The notifications very probably happened.
- A mutable text link should be treated as weak evidence, not as proof of absence.

### 3.6 Reactivation, rejected leads, label equivalence
- **SUP-710.** CS-9 has no reactivation procedure. The case has a fresh sanctions check and manual approval, and the prior quality certification was still valid. The case log starts without Register_Supplier because the supplier already exists.
- **SUP-709.** CS-9 itself acknowledges an abbreviated pre-screen for rejected leads and does not say M0 governs them. No activation occurred, so the Quality_Review obligation never arose.
- **SUP-713.** The UI and procedure renamed the activity, and audit documents show equivalent content. The mapping was approved a week late, which is model maintenance lag.
- **SUP-711.** The duplicate carries the same source transaction ID, so it is one event, not two.

### 3.7 Trace deviation is not business misconduct
- Conformance checking measures distance from a model. It does not measure harm, authority or intent.
- Six of the twelve non-fitting sample cases are model, scope or data artefacts (§5.3). Reporting all twelve as "noncompliance" would bury SUP-703 and SUP-708 in noise.

### 3.8 What the defense must concede (genuine risk and limits on informal practice)
- **SUP-703 and SUP-708 are real failures.** One is a HIGH-risk ineligible supplier through an auto-approval gate, with EUR 180,000 of exposure. The other is activation before sanctions. Informal practice cannot excuse either.
- **Pilot authority is weak.** PD-3 was never incorporated into CS-9, and the Compliance observer declined to approve weakening it. "Operations normalized it" is not authority.
- **Post-expiry use is still unauthorised use.** Unenforced end dates are a reason to fix the system, not a licence to keep going. Six August Auto_Approve cases suggest drift.
- **SUP-712 is a real segregation-of-duties breach** if alias resolution holds.
- **SUP-715 needs follow-up.** The spouse relationship with no recorded disclosure is unresolved.
- **The 14 ineligible and 9 unknown cases** show the pilot gate failed at a rate that matters.
- **Missing logs cannot be treated as proof of compliance.** The 2% baseline shows some real omissions, and 29% is too many to explain away without evidence.

### 5.2 System-level judgment

**Operational compliance.** The operation is not systemically disregarding CS-9, but it has localised, material control failures and a real governance weakness.
- **Confirmed substantive failures.**
  - The pilot gate failed on at least 14 of 312 cases (4.5%), and up to 23 (7.4%) if the nine unknown cases are also ineligible.

  - Eligibility was operator-selected.
  - CRM migration reduced evidence quality without a controlled change.
- **Not established.** Intentional concealment or misconduct is not established for any case. The sample is illustrative and not random, so its proportions should not be extrapolated.

**Model validity.** M0 is partially obsolete, incomplete and mis-scoped, but its core invariants remain valid.
- It cannot express Request_More_Info, rejection without Quality_Review, reactivation, label changes, or the pilot and emergency overlays. It does not encode segregation of duties, conflict of interest, amounts, risk or dates.

- **Half are model, scope or data artefacts (6):** 706, 707, 709, 710, 711 and 713.

- **A third are genuine noncompliance (4):** 703, 705, 708 and 714.
- **Of the three cases that fit, at least one has a hidden breach (712), and one is unresolved (715).**

This partly supports Operations ("mostly obsolescence") for the misfit count, but it does not cover the four genuine failures. It also shows that fitness misleads in both directions: it over-flags benign cases and under-flags resource-rule breaches.

### 5.4 Where I reject each side's argument

**Prosecution, rejected:**
- *"All 312 pilot cases are noncompliant."* Authority is unproved, not disproved. I would not attribute that failure to operators who followed a documented committee decision, and 289 cases appear to meet its stated conditions.
- *"M0 misfit is noncompliance."* SUP-707, 709, 710, 711 and 713 show it is not.
- *"Missing notification is a failure."* For SUP-706 the evidence is indeterminate, not adverse.
- *"Desirable outcomes show the rule was ignored."* Request_More_Info is not prohibited by CS-9.
- *"FD-7 follow-ups after expiry invalidate SUP-704."* I read obligations attached to in-window activations as surviving expiry, though FD-7 should say so explicitly.

**Prosecution, accepted:**
- Authority defects are real.
- Post-expiry use is unauthorised.
- SUP-703, SUP-708 and SUP-712 are genuine failures.
- Fit is not compliance.

**Defense, rejected:**
- *"A factual emergency extends FD-7"* (SUP-705). Facts do not grant authority, and an automatic sunset exists to force an explicit renewal decision.
- *"Imminent approval"* (SUP-714). A proposal is not a rule.
- *"No transactions occurred"* (SUP-708). The control is about activation, not about whether it was exploited.
- *"High deviation rate is mostly obsolescence"* as a blanket claim. It is about half true for misfits and false for the severe cases.
- *"Logging gaps are migration artefacts"* as a blanket claim. The 2% baseline shows some real omissions, and ungoverned migration is itself a control failure.
- *"Pilot practice is authority."* Operators' familiarity with PD-3 does not cure its authority gap.

**Defense, accepted:**
- M0 over-constrains rejection.
- M0 has no loop.
- Overlays should be modelled, not ignored.
- SUP-702 and SUP-704 are justified on the stated conditions.
- Scope for leads and reactivations needs explicit rules.
- SUP-711 and SUP-713 are artefacts.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

### 6.1 Layers

**Layer 0: Authority register (organisational control).**
- Every norm, pilot or emergency has an ID, version, issuing body, compliance sign-off, and effective from/to. PD-3 and FD-7 are recorded retroactively as overlays with "authority status" fields, including "unconfirmed" for PD-3.
- Only a CS-9 amendment with a model version bump makes something permanent.

**Layer 1: Control-flow (POWL).**
- The model structure: a standard route, optional overlay routes, a repeatable Request_More_Info, rejection and withdrawal routes, notification before close.
- Overlay routes are included only for the overlays in force at the event date. Conformance uses the composition effective at event time, so the pilot cannot be retroactively built into the permanent model.

**Layer 2: Declarative constraints (DECLARE or log-skeleton style).** These cover what POWL cannot correlate across branches:
- `Precedence(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier)`, an invariant for every overlay.
- `Precedence(Manual_Procurement_Approval, Activate_Supplier)` on the standard route.
- `Response(Activate_Supplier, Quality_Review)` within two business days where an overlay permits deferral.
- `Response(Activate_Supplier, Manual_Procurement_Approval)` (retrospective) under FD-7.
- `NotCoExistence(Auto_Approve, Manual_Procurement_Approval)` outside FD-7.
- `NotCoExistence(Reject_Supplier, Activate_Supplier)`.
- `Precedence(evidence_received, Activate_Supplier)` for each Request_More_Info request.
- `Precedence(Notify_Requester, Close_Case)`.

**Layer 3: Data-aware rules (pandas or rule-engine checks).**
- Pilot eligibility from a snapshot (risk, country, group membership).
- Spend caps with cumulative spend.
- Validity windows by event time.
- Business-day deadlines with a holiday calendar.
- Master-data-validation presence.
- Certification validity for reactivation.

**Layer 4: Resource and conflict-of-interest rules.**
- Canonical person ID behind all aliases, with recorded method and confidence.
- `requester  approver` on canonical IDs, with delegation evidence.
- Reviewer and approver relationship check against an HR and disclosure registry, which can require disclosure or recusal.
- Auto-approval is treated as an approver identity (rule ID and version), not an absence.

**Layer 5: Overlay governance.**
- Lifecycle: Proposed  Compliance-approved (named owner, risk assessment)  Registered with machine-readable scope  Enabled by feature flag  Monitored weekly (eligibility, usage, incidents)  Automatically closed to new use at `valid_to`.
- The system computes eligibility and offers the overlay route only when eligible. Operators do not select "pilot".
- In-flight obligations survive expiry, and this is stated in the overlay.
- Extensions require a signed amendment *before* expiry. Emergencies need a pre-authorised renewal mechanism by a named role.
- Retroactive ratification is recorded as a separate event. It mitigates severity but does not convert the case to compliant.
- A suspension trigger applies if ineligible use exceeds a stated threshold.

**Layer 6: Scope rules.**
- **Lead pre-screen.** A record with no Register_Supplier event is a LEAD_PRESCREEN case under an abbreviated model (Sanctions_Check  accept or reject), linked to an ONBOARDING case by `lead_id` on acceptance. If a Register_Supplier event exists, it is an ONBOARDING case, which is my default given SUP-709. Rejection never requires Quality_Review, since CS-9 ties it to activation.
- **Reactivation.** A REACTIVATION case requires a fresh Sanctions_Check, manual approval and requesterapprover. Quality_Review is required unless a recorded certification is valid, risk profile is unchanged, and the certification is within its policy validity window. Compliance must define that window.
- **Withdrawal.** Before activation it is a terminal outcome, with no skipped checks counted as noncompliance. After activation it is suspension or offboarding, a separate case type. Any activation still needs Sanctions_Check first.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

### 7.1 Model and checks

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import Transition, SilentTransition, StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator
import pandas as pd


# ---------- small helpers ----------
def T(label):
    """Fresh node per occurrence: POWL nodes must not be shared between parents."""
    return Transition(label=label)

def PO(nodes, edges=()):
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=nodes)
    for a, b in edges:
        po.order.add_edge(a, b)
    return po

def XOR(*children):
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=list(children))

def optional(node):
    # silent transitions only appear inside choices/loops, never directly in a partial order
    return XOR(node, SilentTransition())

def repeatable(label):
    # zero-or-more repetitions: do = tau, redo = activity  ->  tau (A tau)*
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[SilentTransition(), T(label)])


# ---------- routes ----------
def standard_route():
    sanc, qual, rmi = T("Sanctions_Check"), T("Quality_Review"), repeatable("Request_More_Info")
    man, act, notify = T("Manual_Procurement_Approval"), T("Activate_Supplier"), T("Notify_Requester")
    return PO([sanc, qual, rmi, man, act, notify],
              [(sanc, man), (qual, man), (rmi, man), (man, act), (act, notify)])

def auto_route(require_mdv_event=True):
    """PD-3 overlay: sanctions + automated master-data validation before Auto_Approve/activation;
    Quality_Review deferred (deadline is a data rule). Eligibility is NOT enforceable here."""
    sanc, auto = T("Sanctions_Check"), T("Auto_Approve")
    mdv = T("Automated_Master_Data_Validation")
    mdv = mdv if require_mdv_event else optional(mdv)      # legacy logs lack this event
    act, qual, notify = T("Activate_Supplier"), T("Quality_Review"), T("Notify_Requester")
    # qual is deliberately unordered w.r.t. act/notify: notification may precede deferred QR (cf. SUP-702)
    return PO([sanc, mdv, auto, act, qual, notify],
              [(sanc, auto), (mdv, auto), (auto, act), (act, notify)])

def emergency_route():
    """FD-7 overlay: sanctions before provisional activation; QR and (retrospective) approval
    may follow; monitoring is an optional repeating event. Spend cap / window = data rules."""
    sanc, rmi, act = T("Sanctions_Check"), repeatable("Request_More_Info"), T("Activate_Supplier")
    qual, man = T("Quality_Review"), T("Manual_Procurement_Approval")   # attribute approval_mode=RETROSPECTIVE
    mon = repeatable("Daily_Transaction_Monitoring")
    notify = T("Notify_Requester")
    return PO([sanc, rmi, act, qual, man, mon, notify],
              [(sanc, act), (rmi, act), (act, notify)])

def terminal_route(label):
    """Reject / withdraw: checks optional (CS-9 ties Quality_Review to activation only)."""
    sanc, qual = optional(T("Sanctions_Check")), optional(T("Quality_Review"))
    rmi, end, notify = repeatable("Request_More_Info"), T(label), T("Notify_Requester")
    return PO([sanc, qual, rmi, end, notify],
              [(sanc, end), (qual, end), (rmi, end), (end, notify)])


# ---------- composed models ----------
def build_revised_powl(overlays=("PD3", "FD7"), require_mdv_event=True):
    """overlays = overlays in force at event time. Pass () for the permanent core model."""
    routes = [standard_route()]
    if "PD3" in overlays:
        routes.append(auto_route(require_mdv_event))
    if "FD7" in overlays:
        routes.append(emergency_route())
    routes += [terminal_route("Reject_Supplier"), terminal_route("Withdraw_Request")]
    decision = XOR(*routes)
    register, close = T("Register_Supplier"), T("Close_Case")
    return PO([register, decision, close], [(register, decision), (decision, close)])

def build_reactivation_powl():
    """REACTIVATION case type. Quality_Review may be skipped only if certification is valid (data rule)."""
    sanc, qual = T("Sanctions_Check"), optional(T("Quality_Review"))
    approve = PO([T("Manual_Procurement_Approval"), T("Activate_Supplier"), T("Notify_Requester")])
    m, a, n = approve.children if hasattr(approve, "children") else (None, None, None)
    # (re)build edges explicitly to avoid depending on attribute layout
    man, act, notify = T("Manual_Procurement_Approval"), T("Activate_Supplier"), T("Notify_Requester")
    approve = PO([man, act, notify], [(man, act), (act, notify)])
    reject = PO([T("Reject_Supplier"), T("Notify_Requester")])
    r0, r1 = reject.children if hasattr(reject, "children") else (None, None)
    rej, rnot = T("Reject_Supplier"), T("Notify_Requester")
    reject = PO([rej, rnot], [(rej, rnot)])
    decision, close = XOR(approve, reject), T("Close_Case")
    return PO([sanc, qual, decision, close],
              [(sanc, decision), (qual, decision), (decision, close)])


M_CORE = build_revised_powl(overlays=())                      # permanent rules only
M1 = build_revised_powl(overlays=("PD3", "FD7"))               # composed model for events inside both windows
M_REACT = build_reactivation_powl()
print(M1)

try:                                                          # version-dependent; POWL -> Petri net
    import pm4py
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(M1)
    print("Petri net places/transitions:", len(net.places), len(net.transitions))
except Exception as exc:
    print("POWL->Petri conversion unavailable in this PM4Py version:", exc)


# ---------- data-aware overlay checks (synthetic demo, no external files) ----------
overlay_register = pd.DataFrame([
    dict(overlay_id="PD3", version="1.0", kind="PILOT",
         valid_from=pd.Timestamp("2026-06-01 00:00"), valid_to=pd.Timestamp("2026-07-31 23:59:59"),
         authority_status="UNCONFIRMED_NOT_IN_CS9"),
    dict(overlay_id="FD7", version="1.0", kind="EMERGENCY",
         valid_from=pd.Timestamp("2026-07-10 00:00"), valid_to=pd.Timestamp("2026-07-12 23:59:59"),
         authority_status="ISSUER_TO_BE_EVIDENCED"),
]).set_index("overlay_id")

events = pd.DataFrame([
    dict(case_id="SUP-703", overlay_id="PD3", event_time=pd.Timestamp("2026-06-20 10:00"),
         risk="HIGH", country="FR", in_group=False, cum_spend=180000),
    dict(case_id="SUP-714", overlay_id="PD3", event_time=pd.Timestamp("2026-08-02 09:00"),
         risk="LOW", country="DE", in_group=True, cum_spend=0),
    dict(case_id="SUP-704", overlay_id="FD7", event_time=pd.Timestamp("2026-07-11 11:00"),
         risk="HIGH", country="DE", in_group=False, cum_spend=18000),
    dict(case_id="SUP-705", overlay_id="FD7", event_time=pd.Timestamp("2026-07-13 11:00"),
         risk="HIGH", country="DE", in_group=False, cum_spend=12000),
])

def overlay_finding(row):
    o = overlay_register.loc[row["overlay_id"]]
    if not (o["valid_from"] <= row["event_time"] <= o["valid_to"]):
        return "OUTSIDE_WINDOW"
    if row["overlay_id"] == "PD3" and not (row["risk"] == "LOW" and row["country"] == "DE" and row["in_group"]):
        return "INELIGIBLE"
    if row["overlay_id"] == "FD7" and row["cum_spend"] > 20000:

events["finding"] = events.apply(overlay_finding, axis=1)
print(events[["case_id", "overlay_id", "finding"]])

# segregation of duties on canonical identities (alias map comes from an identity-resolution service)
alias_map = {"j.smith": "P-17", "john.smith@corp": "P-17", "a.lee": "P-42"}
def sod_violation(requester_alias, approver_alias):
    r, a = alias_map.get(requester_alias), alias_map.get(approver_alias)

print("SUP-712-style SoD check:", sod_violation("j.smith", "john.smith@corp"))

# de-duplicate migration artefacts using the source transaction id (SUP-711-style)
raw = pd.DataFrame([dict(case_id="SUP-711", activity="Register_Supplier", source_txn_id="T-1"),
                    dict(case_id="SUP-711", activity="Register_Supplier", source_txn_id="T-1")])
```

### 7.2 Known limitations of the model (not hidden)

- **Eligibility, amounts, risk, dates, identity and authority cannot be expressed in POWL.** The auto route is structurally present whenever PD3 is in the overlay set. It is the data layer and the overlay register that say whether a given case was entitled to it. `build_revised_powl` composes by event time so the pilot does not become permanent by construction.
- **Routes are independent XOR branches.** POWL cannot correlate a choice on one branch with a required obligation elsewhere. To keep the deferred obligations tied to their route, I placed Notify_Requester and the deferred Quality_Review or retroactive approval *inside* each route and let Close_Case follow the whole route. The cost is that Notify_Requester is duplicated across routes.
- **Request_More_Info is a zero-or-more loop placed before approval and activation.** That approximates "before a decision". It cannot express that activation must wait for the evidence that was requested. That is the `Precedence(evidence_received, Activate_Supplier)` data check.
- **Mid-process withdrawal is approximated as a terminal route.** Withdrawal after activation belongs to a separate offboarding case type.
- **Deadlines** (QR within two business days, FD-7 cap until retrospective approval) are data rules, since POWL has no time.
- **`build_reactivation_powl` contains redundant scaffolding lines** (unused first-pass nodes) that can be deleted. Only the final `man/act/notify` and `rej/rnot` constructions feed the returned model. I left them visible rather than silently rewriting.
- **MDV event.** Current logs have no Automated_Master_Data_Validation event. Until it is collected, replay the PD-3 period with `require_mdv_event=False`, and treat missing MDV as a data gap, not as a violation.

### 7.4 Event and data-collection proposal

**Identity and ordering**
- `event_id` (immutable UUID) and `source_txn_id` for deduplication.
- `event_time` (business time) and `record_time` (system-written, immutable, never editable).
- `source_system`, `activity_raw`, `activity_std`, `mapping_version_id`.

**Notification**
- `notification_id` generated by the onboarding service when notification is requested, and passed to the CRM as an idempotency key.
- CRM returns `crm_message_id` and delivery status.
- An append-only link ledger records `notification_id`, `case_id`, `link_method` (IDEMPOTENCY_KEY or LEGACY_MANUAL_TEXT), `link_author`, `link_recorded_at`. Legacy mutable links carry a "weak evidence" weight.
- A daily reconciliation job lists unlinked CRM messages and cases with no notification, with an alert threshold near the 2% baseline.

**Rule and overlay**
- `rule_id`, `rule_version` and `overlay_id` on every event that relies on an overlay.
- `overlay_authority_ref` (document ID, approver IDs, compliance sign-off) and `overlay_status_at_event`.
- An `approval_mode` attribute (STANDARD, AUTO, RETROSPECTIVE) on approval events.

**Eligibility and amounts**
- An `eligibility_snapshot` taken at decision time, with risk level, country, group ID, group approval ID, snapshot time and hash. It is never recomputed afterwards.
- A master-data-validation event with result.
- `cumulative_spend_to_date`, `spend_cap` and `retro_approval_time` until retrospective approval.
- A `critical_designation_id` for FD-7 cases.
- `qr_due_at`, computed from a named business calendar.

**Actors**
- `actor_id` (canonical), `actor_alias`, and `identity_resolution_id` with method and confidence.
- `role`, `delegation_id` and delegation evidence.
- Reviewer-approver relationship check result and `coi_disclosure_id`.

**Request_More_Info**
- `request_id`, requested-item list, `response_received_time`, and a link to the activation it gates.

**Case type**
- `case_type` (ONBOARDING, LEAD_PRESCREEN, REACTIVATION, OFFBOARDING), `lead_id`, and prior-case reference for reactivations.

**Change control**
- Any migration of an activity to another system needs a controlled change with an evidence plan, including the linking key, before cut-over. The CRM move lacked this, which is why SUP-706 is indeterminate.
