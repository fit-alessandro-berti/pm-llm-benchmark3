t_cd = add_trans("Correct Dossier", net, ["p3"], ["p3"]) # Self-loop on opt block

- Expedite Review: Optional, placed after mandatory checks via `p3` merge point.
- Approval: Exclusive choice ensures exactly one occurs.
- Correction: Loop from `Correct Dossier` returns to `p3`, allowing partial or full rechecks, but the net’s structure prevents repeats without the correction trigger.

**Limitations**: The missing `external` and `affected_check` attributes mean the model cannot auto-route or auto-select partial repeats. It relies on post-hoc DECLARE alerts and manual validation for conditional logic. This is unavoidable without data enrichment.

# 9. Validation Experiments

**Evaluation Program**:
1. **Reproducible splits**: Use `train_test_split(log, test_size=0.2, random_state=42)`. Publish preprocessing pipeline and seeds.
2. **Attribute-stratified holdout**: Filter log by simulated `external` flag. Test models separately on internal vs. external subsets.
3. **Curated negative suite**: Compile 50 forbidden traces (B3, B4, B8, plus crafted violations like skipped checks, expedite-before-checks, repeats without correction).
4. **Perturbation tests**: Inject missing Supplier Review events, duplicate mandatory checks, and reorder checks to test robustness.
5. **Unseen-combination tests**: Generate 100 valid permutations not in training (e.g., Supplier + Auto + alternate order) and measure fitness drop.
6. **Alignment-cost sensitivity**: Test `default`, `event_cost={'skip': 1, 'insert': 1}`, `transition_cost={'silent': 0}`.
7. **Soundness & reachability**: `pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm)` and `pm4py.check_reachability(net, im, fm)`.
8. **Metrics reporting**: Report fitness, precision, generalization, simplicity separately. Do not aggregate.
9. **Stakeholder comprehension**: Structured survey (n=30) with 5 traces; ask to identify violations. Score 1–5. Publish protocol.
10. **Decision rule**: Reject if holdout fitness < 0.85 OR negative-suite precision < 0.95 OR soundness fails OR comprehension < 3.5.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

- **Exploration**: Model S may be retained for initial pattern discovery and dashboard prototyping, provided it is explicitly labeled as non-compliant for enforcement use.
- **Compliance**: Neither original model is fit for production enforcement. Model C’s overfitting and rigidity will cause excessive false rejections. Model S’s permissiveness will allow violations.
- **Retirement**: Model C should be retired from compliance workflows. Model S should be deprecated for any conformance-checking use.
- **Third Alternative Deployment Conditions**: Must achieve holdout fitness  0.88, negative-suite precision  0.95, pass soundness/reachability checks, and achieve stakeholder comprehension  3.5. The DECLARE alerting mechanism for missing attributes must be integrated into the monitoring dashboard.

# 11. Dissenting View

The third alternative’s complexity risks operational friction. Business users value speed and simplicity; introducing DECLARE constraints, external flag alerts, and partial-correction loops may overwhelm non-technical stakeholders and slow down case handling. Model S’s permissiveness is not a flaw but a pragmatic feature for agile environments where policy evolves faster than process mining can capture. Model C’s memorization might be acceptable if the process is stable and variants are genuinely limited; rejecting unseen combinations forces human review, which could be more efficient than automated blocking. I would reconsider my recommendation if: (1) stakeholder surveys show the third model’s comprehension drops below 3.0 due to cognitive overload, (2) holdout fitness for the third model falls below 0.85 because over-constraining blocks legitimate edge cases, or (3) the business explicitly adopts a “trust but verify” monitoring strategy where alignment deviations trigger human escalation rather than model-based blocking, making S’s high fitness and low runtime more valuable than C’s precision.
