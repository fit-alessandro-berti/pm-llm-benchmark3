## 1. Decision rule

To move beyond naive breach-probability ranking, our portfolio-level intervention policy combines **Stage Eligibility**, **Calibration-Adjusted Net Expected Value (CANEV)**, **Information Gain (for alert persistence)**, and **Queue Capacity Constraints**. 

### Stage Eligibility (Hard Filter)
An incident is eligible for a Senior Diagnostic Swarm if and only if its current stage is strictly before `Diagnosis Complete`. 
- **Exclusion:** Cases at or past `Diagnosis Complete` (e.g., I-03) are strictly ineligible; swarms yield negligible risk reduction here, wasting €450 and senior time.

### Calibration Adjustments
Before computing decision metrics, we correct raw observational probabilities ($p_{now}$) using segment-level adjustment factors derived from backtests and workflow audits:
- **Strategic Enterprise:** Apply a downward shift of $\Delta p = -0.08$ (correcting for the recent workflow redesign overprediction of $0.05\text{--}0.12$).
- **SME:** Apply an upward shift of $\Delta p = +0.06$ (correcting for underprediction of $0.04\text{--}0.09$).
- **Public Sector:** Retain $p_{now}$ ($\Delta p = 0.00$), noting moderate sample size.
- **Non-Native-Language Support:** Retain $p_{now}$ but flag uncertainty bounds as wider by $\pm 50\%$.
- **Imputed Data (e.g., I-06):** Apply a data-quality penalty (confidence discount of $20\%$ on the estimated effect size) due to missing live queue features.

### Alert Persistence & Minimum Evidence Change
To prevent alert fatigue and redundant interventions:
- **Rule of No New Evidence:** Repeated alerts without a material event (defined as a newly uploaded customer log, explicit system state change, or sudden SLA compression) are **suppressed**. If $\Delta p$ changes solely due to time decay or model smoothing without new features (e.g., I-05), the case is down-ranked or blocked from intervention.
- **Rule of Material Evidence:** Repeated alerts accompanied by new operational or customer data (e.g., I-07) bypass suppression and are evaluated on their updated information value.

### Prioritization (CANEV Metric)
For eligible cases passing persistence checks, we rank using the Calibration-Adjusted Net Expected Value:

$$\text{CANEV} = \mathbb{E}[\text{Effect}] \times V_{\text{breach}} - C_{\text{swarm}} - B_{\text{false}}$$

Where:
- $\mathbb{E}[\text{Effect}]$ = Midpoint of the estimated absolute risk-reduction range, discounted for data quality and calibration uncertainty.
- $V_{\text{breach}}$ = Customer value of avoiding breach.
- $C_{\text{swarm}}$ = Fixed intervention cost (€450).
- $B_{\text{false}}$ = Case-specific false-alarm burden.

We further adjust the final ranking by subtracting a **Queue Externality Penalty** (€2,000 per slot used, reflecting the aggregate delay and spillover breach risk imposed on the 6 diagnosis-stage cases sharing the queue).

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

To manage dynamic workflows without thrashing senior specialists, we enforce a strict temporal lifecycle for alerts:

### Cool-Down & Suppression Logic
- **Cool-Down Period:** Once an incident triggers an alert (whether acted upon or suppressed), it enters a mandatory **60-minute cool-down window** during which subsequent model updates cannot re-trigger an active intervention flag unless a material event occurs.
- **Suppression (e.g., I-05):** I-05 has generated three alerts driven entirely by 30-minute model re-scoring cycles with **no new event**. Under our policy, I-05 is placed on **active suppression**. Zero senior capacity is allocated; the alert is logged as a model smoothing artifact.

### Escalation & Re-Entry Logic
- **Re-Entry with Evidence (e.g., I-07):** I-07 has generated a repeated alert, but it is accompanied by a **new customer diagnostic log**. This breaks suppression. The new file introduces genuine information asymmetry reduction. We evaluate I-07 for intervention (and successfully allocate Slot 3).
- **Escalation Trigger:** If an untreated case experiences SLA compression exceeding $50\%$ of its remaining time window *and* receives a material data update, it skips standard queue ranking and escalates to Tier 1 priority.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

Deploying predictive interventions on live enterprise workflows creates systemic risks that require explicit, structural safeguards:

### 1. Value-Based Prioritization Starving Lower-Value Cases
*Risk:* Prioritizing high-value Strategic Enterprise cases ($€100\text{k}$) like I-08 starves lower-value SME or Public Sector cases (e.g., I-01, €9,000), creating a two-tiered service quality divide.
*Safeguard:* **Segment Quota / Floor.** Reserve at least 25% of monthly swarm capacity for cases with $V_{\text{breach}} < €15,000$, and apply a progressive adjustment weight that boosts lower-value cases if their risk-reduction efficiency $(\text{Effect} / C_{\text{swarm}})$ is exceptionally high.

### 2. Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support
*Risk:* Case I-09 resides in a sparse calibration segment. Under- or over-estimating its risk leads to misallocated capacity or uncalibrated interventions.
*Safeguard:* **Uncertainty Penalty & Human Audit.** Impose a $20\%$ confidence penalty on the expected effect size for sparse segments, requiring a 5-minute human senior triage review before a swarm is locked in for non-native language cases.

### 3. Differential Calibration Across Segments
*Risk:* Uncorrected model bias (e.g., SME underprediction vs. Strategic Enterprise overprediction) systematically skews resource allocation toward systematically overpredicted segments.
*Safeguard:* Enforce the segment-specific calibration shifts ($\Delta p$) defined in Section 1, and conduct bi-weekly calibration audits (Hosmer-Lemeshow tests per segment).

### 4. Selective Intervention Changing Future Training Labels
*Risk:* Intervening on high-risk cases prevents breaches on those cases. If future machine learning models train on this data without accounting for intervention, the model learns that swarms "failed" or that high-risk cases never breach, corrupting future predictions (the autologistic feedback loop).
*Safeguard:* **Propensity Score Logging & Label Censoring.** Every intervened case is flagged in the feature store with its intervention propensity score. Future model training runs must either exclude intervened cases or use inverse probability weighting (IPW) / causal forest architectures.

### 5. Spillover Harms Falling on Untreated Groups
*Risk:* Pulling two senior specialists into swarms delays the general diagnosis queue, disproportionately harming untreated, lower-tier cases that lack the political weight of Strategic Enterprise clients.
*Safeguard:* Monitor queue wait times by segment. If queue lag for SME/Public Sector exceeds 20 minutes, automatically trigger a freeze on new Strategic Enterprise swarms.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

To maintain policy integrity under uncertainty, we establish a five-pillar monitoring framework:

### 1. Operational Monitoring (Real-Time)
- **Metrics:** Swarm slot utilization, senior specialist idle/interruption time, and diagnosis queue length.
- **Trigger:** If queue length exceeds 8 cases or aggregate delay exceeds 25 minutes per slot, automatically suspend Tier 3 interventions and activate queue-clearing protocols.

### 2. Predictive Monitoring (Daily)
- **Metrics:** False-positive rate per segment, alert volume trends, and suppression rates (tracking frequency of cases like I-05).
- **Trigger:** If suppression rate exceeds $40\%$ of total alerts, retrain the underlying scoring model's feature weights to reduce sensitivity to temporal smoothing.

### 3. Causal-Effect Monitoring (Monthly)
- **Metrics:** Realized breach reduction attributable to swarms versus control baselines.
- **Method:** Implement an opportunistic **A/B rollout or randomized encouragement design** (holding out $10\%$ of borderline eligible cases from swarms) to measure true causal lift rather than observational correlation.

### 4. Capacity & Spillover Auditing (Bi-Weekly)
- **Metrics:** Breach rates of untreated cases sharing queues with intervened cases.
- **Trigger:** If untreated cases sharing a queue with swarms exhibit a statistically significant surge in breach rates exceeding the predicted 0.01–0.03 delta, reduce the base slot count from 4 to 3 to protect baseline queue health.

### 5. Threshold Recalibration Governance
- **Review Cadence:** Monthly review board comprising Operations, Data Science, and Account Management.
- **Decision Rule for Threshold Adjustment:** If the net-impact economic return turns negative for two consecutive weeks (mirroring the Adverse Scenario), tighten the CANEV intervention hurdle rate by $15\%$ and increase the false-alarm burden penalty ($B_{\text{false}}$) across all segments.
