## 2. Process-model consistency checks

To ensure models operate strictly within valid process boundaries, the validation suite must execute the following automated checks before feature ingestion and model scoring:

1. **Prefix-Time Availability Check**: Verifies that every feature value is derived exclusively from events where $\text{timestamp}(e) \le t_0$. Any feature referencing data past the first `Coverage Check Completed` timestamp is flagged and rejected.
2. **Activity-Order Feasibility (Conformance) Check**: Evaluates event traces against the normative process model using a Petri net or Directly-Follows Graph (DFG) conformance checker. Traces violating mandatory precedence constraints (e.g., `Claim Closed` before `Damage Assessment Completed`) are isolated as data-corruption anomalies.
3. **Same-Timestamp Ambiguity Resolution**: Detects events sharing identical timestamps. Where sequence indexes are missing, the validation suite rejects deterministic assumptions about intra-millisecond event ordering and drops sequence-based patterns relying on ambiguous pairs.
4. **Complete-Trace Feature Audit**: Scans feature extraction pipelines for retrospective keywords (e.g., `_total`, `_final`, `_seen_anywhere`, `_current`) and automatically blocks features that fail point-in-time isolation tests.
5. **Enabling and Reachability Verification**: Ensures that local explanations do not cite activities that are merely *future-possible* or *normatively disabled* at $t_0$. At $t_0$ (immediately after `Coverage Check Completed`), the only enabled next steps are fast-track damage assessment, document requests, or fraud investigation triggers. Downstream states like `Settlement Decision Recorded` or `Claim Closed` are structurally unreachable.
6. **Appeal Semantics Validation**: Validates that repeated `Settlement Decision Recorded` events strictly follow an `Appeal` event succeeding an initial `Partial` or `Deny` decision, preventing misinterpretation of data loops as standard linear paths.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

| Feature Class | Permissible Claim (What May Be Claimed) | Prohibited Claim (What May NOT Be Claimed) |
|---|---|---|
| **Defensible Predictive Signal** (`initial_damage_complexity`, `initial_reserve`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0`) | These features reflect baseline case complexity and historical engagement known at $t_0$, correlating with inherent processing time. | They *do not* represent operational bottlenecks or prove that initial complexity directly causes a 10-day breach. |
| **Process-State or Congestion Signal** (`queue_length_at_t0`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`) | High queue congestion and elapsed pre-check time reduce operational slack, increasing breach probability due to resource constraints. | They *do not* imply that the claimant or case specifics caused the delay; the bottleneck is systemic. |
| **Proxy Requiring Fairness/Robustness Review** (`claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`) | These variables stratify risk across demographic, geographic, or intake channels for operational capacity planning. | They *must not* be interpreted as inherent risk drivers of the claimant, nor used for discriminatory routing without disparate impact justification. |
| **Drift / Calendar Proxy** (`calendar_week`) | Captures periodic operational capacity drops (e.g., staffing shortages during holidays or policy rollouts). | It *does not* reflect intrinsic claim characteristics; it measures external temporal load fluctuations. |
| **Unusable Until Lineage is Repaired** (`final_handler_team`, `risk_score_current`, `num_document_requests_total`) | Currently unusable due to severe leakage and missing historical timestamps. | Must *not* be used in predictions until feature-store lineage is strictly bounded to point-in-time snapshots at $t_0$. |

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

To ensure ongoing model validity, compliance, and operational utility, the following prioritized roadmap must be implemented:

1. **Feature-Store Redesign & Point-in-Time Correctness**: Re-architect the feature store to maintain immutable, versioned feature values with explicit validity timestamps. Prohibit any feature extraction queries that lack an upper bound of `<= t_0`.
2. **Historical Feature Timestamping**: Audit all warehouse tables (`final_handler_team`, `risk_score_current`, etc.) to ensure historical change-logs are captured. If historical availability cannot be guaranteed, deprecate the features.
3. **Prefix-Only Recomputation Pipeline**: Establish an automated validation gate that replays historical event logs up to arbitrary prefix timestamps ($t_0$) to verify that models score using strictly past information without accessing future trace attributes.
4. **Automated Process-Conformance Testing**: Integrate process-mining conformance checkers into the ML pipeline to continuously validate that generated sequence patterns and feature lineages comply with the normative process model (e.g., preventing backward-in-time transitions or impossible activity sequences).
5. **Model Recalibration After Feature Removal**: Retrain the predictive model after stripping leaked features (`total_case_duration_hours`, `full_trace_variant_id`, post-decision variables). Re-evaluate calibration curves and performance metrics (AUC-ROC, Brier score) to ensure robust performance without leakage.
6. **Explanation Stability Monitoring**: Monitor SHAP/explanation stability across similar case prefixes to detect sensitivity to minor feature variations or data artifacts.
7. **Proxy and Fairness Analysis**: Conduct regular disparate impact and fairness audits on sensitive attributes like `claimant_region` and `claimant_channel` to ensure predictive stratification does not result in systemic bias against specific claimant groups.
8. **Drift and Schema Monitoring**: Implement real-time monitoring for concept drift, process drift (e.g., changing cycle times or routing policies), and data schema changes to alert model operations teams when operational environments diverge from training baselines.
