## 1. Target definition

**Primary formulation: an observed-policy, multi-state early-warning target for all applications.** A scalar “90-day default probability for every application” is not directly observable because default is defined only after activation. The primary model should therefore predict the application’s possible observed paths rather than silently assigning zero default risk to paths that never create a credit exposure.

Let (t_p) be an early prediction landmark and (\mathcal H(t_p)) the information available through that time. Let (A,R,W,T) denote activation, rejection, withdrawal, and transfer times. The principal reported risk is

[
P!\left(
A \le t_p+180\text{ days},;
A<\min(R,W,T),;
D_{90}=1
\mid
\mathcal H(t_p),\ \pi_{\text{observed}}
\right),
]

where (D_{90}) means default within 90 days after Account Activated and (\pi_{\text{observed}}) is the historical lending and routing policy.

This joint activation-and-default risk must be reported alongside the corresponding probabilities of:

* rejection;
* withdrawal;
* transfer;
* activation followed by no default through day 90;
* remaining in process at the application horizon.

The 180-day application horizon is a **design assumption** because no application-resolution SLA is supplied. It should be replaced by the contractual horizon and accompanied by 30-, 90-, and 270-day sensitivity results. The post-activation horizon remains exactly 90 days.

**Prediction time.** The study is a dynamic landmark study:

* Every application receives an initial score immediately after `Application Started`.
* Scores may be updated after the second and third canonical, deduplicated events.
* The primary performance table is reported after the third event, normally the first `Credit Review`.
* An application contributes only while it is still in process. No prediction is generated after rejection, withdrawal, transfer, or activation.

This design covers every valid application at the first landmark without pretending that all applications remain eligible at later landmarks.

**Unit of analysis.** The unit is an application at a prespecified landmark. The main (k=3) analysis has at most one row per application. Analyses combining (k=1,2,3) use application-level clustering so that repeated prefixes are not treated as independent cases.

**Population.** The cohort ledger contains all 48,000 applications started between 1 January 2024 and 31 December 2025. The risk set at a later landmark contains applications that are still in process at that landmark. Landmark attrition is reported, not hidden.

**Why this is defensible.** Rejection, withdrawal, and transfer are represented as competing process outcomes rather than “non-default.” The estimand is defined under the observed policy, can be estimated from observed paths with explicit censoring assumptions, and produces operationally meaningful probabilities for triage.

A credible **secondary target** is

[
P(D_{90}=1\mid \text{eventually activated},\mathcal H(t_p)).
]

This answers, “Among accounts selected for activation under the historical policy, which were riskier?” It is useful for booked-portfolio forecasting, but it conditions on a future, policy-driven selection event. It is therefore not a default-risk estimate for rejected, withdrawn, transferred, or still-open applications and should not be deployed as such.

A stronger alternative is the counterfactual target

[
P(D_{90}^{,\text{activate under policy }\pi}=1\mid\mathcal H(t_p)),
]

meaning default if an application were activated under specified terms and limits. That target requires consistency, positivity, adequate measurement of approval determinants, and no unmeasured confounding—or preferably randomized or quasi-experimental policy variation. The supplied export does not identify it.

A time-to-default model beginning at activation is another useful alternative, but it answers post-booking monitoring rather than early application triage. It also requires a reliable default date; if only `default_90d` is trustworthy, the stage should remain a censored fixed-horizon outcome rather than inventing an exact failure time.

## 2. Cohort construction

The cohort should be assembled before selecting labeled cases.

**As-of rules**

1. Include applications with a valid `Application Started` timestamp in the observation window. Missing case identifiers, conflicting starts, impossible temporal orderings, and simultaneous conflicting terminal outcomes are quarantined and counted.
2. Process features use events through the prediction time and never after 31 December 2025.
3. The analysis vintage is 15 February 2026. A label is usable only when:

   * the application was activated;
   * `default_90d` is non-missing and internally consistent; and
   * `label_available_at <= 15 February 2026`.
4. `Accepted` and `Contract Signed` do not establish credit exposure. The 90-day clock starts only at `Account Activated`.
5. A non-default is an explicit matured label of zero. Absence of `Default Recorded`, absence of collections activity, or a missing label is not a non-default.
6. For an unlabeled activated account:

   * `activation_time + 90 days > cutoff` means the outcome horizon is right-censored;
   * the 90-day horizon has ended but `activation_time + 125 days > cutoff` means the feed may still be within the stated 35-day delay;
   * `activation_time + 125 days <= cutoff` with no label is classified as missing after the normal grace period, not as non-default.
7. Conservatively, activation on or before **13 October 2025** is required for the full 90-day horizon plus the maximum stated 35-day feed delay to have elapsed by 15 February 2026. Exact `label_available_at` dates can permit earlier use of individual labels.
8. Linked transfer identifiers should be assigned a common application-lineage identifier before splitting data. The original and partner-platform records must not enter different folds.
9. The provided 5,100 “still open” cases should be reconciled against a case-status snapshot. If the event file truly stops on 31 December 2025, the implementation cannot reconstruct January–February process transitions merely because extraction occurred later.

| ------------------------------------- | ---------: | ---------: | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Activated, explicit non-default label |     18,900 |      39.4% | Observed `activated → no default through day 90`                                                |
| Activated, explicit default label     |      2,700 |       5.6% | Observed `activated → default within day 90`                                                    |
| Activated, label not available        |      3,600 |       7.5% | Subdivide into 90-day censoring, feed-delay pending, and missing-after-grace; never assign zero |
| Rejected                              |      9,100 |      19.0% | Competing outcome; default under activation is undefined under the observed path                |
| Withdrawn                             |      5,700 |      11.9% | Competing outcome; no observed credit exposure                                                  |
| Transferred                           |      2,900 |       6.0% | Competing outcome in the source-platform estimand; linked continuation in sensitivity analysis  |

Approximately 1,740 of the 2,900 transfers may be linkable and approximately 1,160 may remain unlinked. These are subdivisions of the 2,900 transfers, not additional applications.

The 21,600 labeled activated accounts have a default proportion of (2{,}700/21{,}600=12.5%), but that is a selected, activated population. Conversely, (2{,}700/48{,}000=5.625%) is merely the number of defaults observed by this snapshot divided by all starts; it is not a matured all-application default probability.

For the fixed (k=3) landmark, cases with fewer than three valid events are not deleted from the overall study. They remain represented in the (k=1), (k=2), and elapsed-time analyses. Cases that terminate before the third event are counted as early competing outcomes and are not scored retrospectively after their outcome.

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

| Mechanism                                          | Interpretation                                                                      | Primary treatment                                                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Still open when process observation ends           | Ordinary administrative right censoring of the application transition               | Censor at the last reliable process-observation cutoff; do not create a terminal class unless the full application horizon was observed |
| Activated but fewer than 90 days observed          | Ordinary right censoring of post-activation default                                 | Censor post-activation follow-up at the analysis cutoff                                                                                 |
| Ninety days completed but feed not yet received    | Delayed outcome ascertainment, not evidence of non-default                          | Use `label_available_at`; withhold from binary evaluation until observed or treat through an observation-delay model                    |
| Rejected or withdrawn                              | Default is structurally undefined under the observed path                           | Treat as competing process outcomes, not censored activated accounts and not zeros                                                      |
| Activated only after policy selection              | Selective outcome observation through activation                                    | Preserve activation as an explicit transition; describe conditional default as policy-selected                                          |
| Transferred                                        | Competing route plus possible loss of downstream follow-up                          | Treat as absorbing transfer in the primary source-platform estimand; continue linked lineages in sensitivity analysis                   |
| Outcome feed missing after the normal grace period | Potentially informative missing outcome, especially for partner-originated accounts | Model label observation and perform missing-not-at-random sensitivity analyses                                                          |

**Primary analysis strategy**

The primary statistical analysis has two connected stages.

First, estimate transitions from “in application processing” to activation, rejection, withdrawal, and transfer, with still-open cases right-censored. This can be implemented as a multi-state or cause-specific competing-risk model.

Second, for activated accounts, estimate default by day 90 while retaining activated accounts with incomplete follow-up as censored. Because the stored target is fixed-horizon, this stage may be implemented with discrete-time survival, censoring-aware binary regression, or a multi-state transition to `default` versus an administrative `survived to day 90` state. No exact default timestamp should be inferred solely from a binary flag.

The joint early-warning probability is obtained from the two stages rather than by training only on the activated complete cases. Label observation should be modeled separately among activated accounts. Stabilized inverse-probability-of-observation or censoring weights can use variables known before the feed should arrive, such as source system, partner origin, activation date, policy era, channel, region, amount, and prefix information. Weight distributions and truncation sensitivity must be reported.

**Sensitivity analyses**

1. **Activated complete cases versus observation-weighted analysis.** Compare the naïve matured-activated estimate with an estimate weighted for label observation. A material difference is evidence that complete-case evaluation is not representative even of activated accounts.
2. **Missing-feed tipping points.** Recalculate calibration and event rates assuming progressively higher default prevalence among missing-after-grace accounts. The broad endpoints—every missing feed is non-default versus every missing feed is default—provide transparent outer bounds.
3. **Transfer handling.** Compare transfer-as-competing-event, linked-continuation analysis, and inverse-probability-of-linkage weighting among the approximately 60% linked subset.
4. **Label-delay windows.** Repeat maturity classification using the exact observed delay distribution and conservative 7-, 21-, and 35-day grace assumptions.
5. **Conditional activated target.** Report the activated-only model as a secondary, explicitly selected-population analysis rather than presenting it as the all-application result.

**Assumption status**

Directly testable elements include cohort arithmetic, timestamp consistency, duplicate rates, mapping coverage, conflicting labels, observed label-delay distributions, and observed-covariate differences between linked and unlinked transfers or observed and missing feeds.

Conditional independent censoring, missing-at-random label observation, missing-at-random transfer linkage, and stability within a policy era are only partially testable. Covariate-balance and residual diagnostics can challenge these assumptions but cannot prove them.

The default outcomes that rejected or withdrawn applicants would have experienced under activation, the outcome distribution among permanently missing feeds, and the absence of unmeasured approval confounding are fundamentally untestable from this export alone.

## 4. Prefix extraction

**Canonicalization precedes prefixing.** Preserve the raw activity label, then map source- and date-specific labels to governed canonical names. Unmapped post-migration labels are quarantined or represented as an explicit `UNMAPPED_ACTIVITY`; they are not silently merged with a guessed activity.

Only exact technical duplicates should be automatically removed—for example, identical case, canonical activity, timestamp, resource, and source-system rows. Near duplicates are flagged for review because repeated `Documents Requested`, `Documents Received`, and `Credit Review` events may be genuine loops.

For tied timestamps, use a stable source sequence when available. If no reliable ordering exists, sequence-sensitive features should treat tied events as an unordered set or be stress-tested under alternative tie orderings.

**Fixed event-count prefixes.** Produce prefixes at (k=1,2,3), with (k=3) as the primary comparison and optional (k=5) as a later-warning analysis. Prefix extraction occurs only after removing forbidden columns. PM4Py’s fixed-prefix operation truncates longer cases and leaves shorter cases as shorter prefixes; consequently, prefix length must remain an explicit feature rather than being mistaken for three observed events. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][1])

For each fixed prefix:

* prediction time is the timestamp of its last event;
* all events must satisfy `event timestamp <= prediction time`;
* the application must not already have activated, rejected, withdrawn, or transferred;
* an anomalous third event that is not the expected first `Credit Review` is reported as a separate coverage/process-quality stratum.

**Elapsed-time prefixes.** Independently construct landmarks such as 24, 48, and 72 hours after `Application Started`, with 48 hours as the main elapsed-time analysis. Include only events whose timestamps are no later than the exact landmark. Do not round to the calendar day. An application that terminates before the 48-hour landmark receives no post-outcome 48-hour prediction; its earlier event-based prediction remains evaluable.

**Short cases.** An open application with only one or two events receives a shorter prefix with `n_events`, `short_prefix`, and missing-landmark indicators. A case that already reached a competing outcome is not padded or scored after that outcome.

**Document loops.** Preserve all genuinely repeated document and review events. Derive numbers of document cycles, review revisits, elapsed time inside each cycle, and repeated-resource or handover patterns.

The nightly `documents_total` snapshot cannot be used to generate a prefix unless every contributing upload has a trustworthy timestamp no later than (t_p). Otherwise it is replaced by a count of timestamped upload events or dropped.

Finally, `time:timestamp` is being used as a proxy for live availability. Because the schema lacks a separate event-ingestion timestamp, back-entered events could appear retrospectively available. That assumption should be audited or stress-tested with an ingestion-lag exclusion window.

## 5. Process-aware features

| Feature source                             | Candidate features                                                                                                                                                           | Required construction                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Static case attributes**                 | Channel, region, requested amount, initial source system, application-start hour/day/month                                                                                   | Take the first value known by the landmark; flag inconsistent supposedly static values                           |
| **Prefix-derived control flow**            | Prefix length, last activity, ordered activity sequence, activity counts, number of credit reviews, document cycles, KYC repetition, backtracks, training-variant identifier | Use only canonical, deduplicated events through (t_p); preserve loop counts                                      |
| **Prefix-derived timing**                  | Time since start, time since previous event, KYC-to-review duration, current loop duration, maximum and mean inter-event gap, weekday/hour                                   | Interpret as elapsed inter-event time, not necessarily service or waiting time                                   |
| **Prefix-derived risk trajectory**         | First and latest `risk_score`, change, slope, range, missingness, number of updates                                                                                          | Use only versions timestamped at or before (t_p); never backward-fill a later score                              |
| **Prefix-derived organizational features** | Current resource, number of unique resources, handovers, returns to an earlier resource, source-system changes                                                               | Use resource information appearing in the prefix; avoid extraction-time employee attributes                      |
| **Historical reference features**          | Resource’s prior case count, prior activity mix, rolling queue workload, recent arrival rate, historical transition rarity, prefix-variant frequency                         | Calculate strictly from records preceding (t_p); freeze or roll forward within each temporal fold                |
| **Calendar and regime features**           | Old/new credit-policy era, pre/post-migration era, days since each change, calendar month                                                                                    | Known at prediction time; also use for stratification and recalibration                                          |
| **Observation-process features**           | Source system, partner origin, transfer-linkage availability, activation vintage                                                                                             | Primarily used for missing-label and linkage models; report risk models with and without unstable source proxies |

Queue load at (t_p) should count applications that had started but had not experienced a known terminal transition by (t_p). It must not use their eventual closing times as if those were already known.

Resource experience should be reconstructed as prior completed cases or prior active days before (t_p). The warehouse’s extraction-time experience measure is not historically valid.

Training-only DFG edge frequencies and variant frequencies can be used to characterize whether a prefix is common or unusual, but those reference artifacts must be fitted only on the past training period. Test-period DFGs are useful for drift diagnosis, not feature construction.

Because the schema supplies one event timestamp rather than reliable start and completion timestamps for each activity, inter-event durations cannot be cleanly decomposed into service and waiting time. Calling a service/waiting-time enrichment function under those conditions would attach stronger semantics than the data support.

## 7. Validation design

The primary split is by application start time and application lineage, not by event row and not randomly.

| Panel            | Application-start period | Purpose                                                                       |
| ---------------- | ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Development      | 1 Jan–31 Dec 2024        | Model fitting and historical-reference feature construction                   |
| Validation       | 1 Jan–30 Jun 2025        | Hyperparameter selection and calibration under the old policy and old system  |
| Out-of-time test | 1 Jul–31 Dec 2025        | Prospective-style evaluation under policy change and, later, system migration |

The out-of-time test should be reported in separate strata:

* 1 July–30 September 2025: new credit policy, pre-migration system;
* 1–14 October 2025: short transition period;
* 15 October–31 December 2025: new policy and migrated system.

Each model-building origin must use only labels whose `label_available_at` is no later than that origin. Evaluation may occur at a later adjudication date, but labels that arrived later must not be presented as if they had been available during model fitting.

A rolling-origin analysis should supplement the single split: fit through month or quarter (q), predict (q+1), update the risk set and censoring at that fold’s cutoff, and repeat. Historical event availability cannot be perfectly reconstructed if `data_extract_time` is only the final snapshot time rather than an event-ingestion timestamp; this limitation must be recorded.

A crucial maturity constraint is that activation must be no later than **13 October 2025** for the full 90-day horizon plus 35-day feed grace to have elapsed by 15 February 2026. Because migration occurred on 15 October 2025, there is no conservatively complete post-migration default cohort at this extraction. Some post-migration labels may already be present, but conventional complete-case binary performance for that era would be prematurely optimistic or selective. Post-migration results should therefore emphasize:

* process and feature drift;
* mapping and duplicate quality;
* observed Stage-A transitions;
* censoring-aware preliminary default estimates;
* label-availability and feed-missingness rates.

Final post-migration calibration should be deferred until those outcomes mature—not replaced by treating current missing labels as negatives.

A random case split is misleading because it mixes both sides of the policy change, both activity-label regimes, different label maturities, and different queue conditions. It can also place linked source and partner identifiers in different folds and allow globally calculated resource, DFG, or target-encoding features to use future cases.

**Metrics for the primary multi-state target**

* Cumulative-incidence calibration for activation, rejection, withdrawal, transfer, and activation-followed-by-default at 30, 90, and 180 days.
* IPCW Brier score at the prespecified horizons and integrated Brier score over time.
* Time-dependent discrimination for the activation-default path.
* Calibration intercept and slope, not merely a reliability plot.
* Proper state-probability log score where censoring adjustment is valid.
* Recall, positive predictive value, and expected alert volume at the lender’s actual review capacity.

For the secondary activated-only target, report weighted Brier score, log loss, calibration slope/intercept, PR-AUC, ROC-AUC, and recall/precision at fixed capacity. PR-AUC is particularly informative given the 12.5% default proportion in the currently labeled activated export, but it does not correct the selected cohort by itself.

Confidence intervals should be generated by bootstrap at the application-lineage level, keeping all landmarks from one lineage together. Weighted analyses should report effective sample size and sensitivity to weight truncation.

Subgroup reporting should include channel, region, requested-amount bands, source system, policy era, migration era, transferred versus non-transferred, linked versus unlinked transfer, partner origin, and short-prefix status. Report calibration and coverage as well as discrimination. The absence of protected-class attributes means the study cannot claim comprehensive fairness.

Distribution-shift monitoring should compare activity frequencies, DFG edges, variant frequencies, prefix lengths, document-loop rates, duplicate rates, unmapped labels, `risk_score` distributions, queue proxies, missingness, label-delay distributions, and feed-observation rates. Recalibration or era-specific models should be considered when calibration changes even if ranking metrics remain stable.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

| The study can support                                                                                   | The study cannot support                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Out-of-time predictive associations between early histories and observed-policy process paths           | The default outcome a rejected or withdrawn applicant would have experienced if activated            |
| Conditional 90-day default risk among accounts selected for activation, with explicit selection caveats | A causal effect of approving, rejecting, requesting documents, changing a limit, or sending an alert |
| Censoring-aware estimates for similar policy, source-system, and observation regimes                    | A policy-invariant “intrinsic default risk” for every application                                    |
| Diagnosis of process, policy, migration, and label-observation shifts                                   | Final post-migration default calibration from the currently immature snapshot                        |
| Operational ranking for additional review, subject to capacity and monitoring                           | Automatic denial decisions justified solely by the predictive score                                  |

Predictive association must not be confused with counterfactual default risk. Activation is a policy-controlled selection event; transfer changes both the process route and observability; permanently missing partner feeds can make even activated complete cases selective.

Deployment can change the data-generating process in several ways:

1. Alerts may change acceptance, rejection, limits, or routing, thereby changing who becomes exposed to credit and invalidating the original observed-policy estimand.
2. Additional reviews or document requests may lengthen cases and create the very timing and rework features the model uses.
3. Review capacity diverted to alerted cases may increase waiting for non-alerted cases, producing queue interference and spillovers.
4. Employees or applicants may adapt to the model, alter risk-score overrides, or change document-submission behavior.
5. More partner transfers can alter both the activated population and the probability of receiving an outcome feed.
6. Selective intervention can make later labels depend on earlier model predictions, creating a feedback loop in retraining data.

The initial deployment boundary should therefore be decision support for review prioritization, with abstention for unmapped activities, missing critical prefix fields, or out-of-distribution regimes. It should not be used to assert that an unactivated applicant “would not default.” A counterfactual underwriting model requires a separately designed causal study with specified activation terms and adequate policy variation.

## 9. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation skeleton

The official PM4Py API documents the top-level dataframe-formatting, fixed-prefix, DFG-discovery, and variant-retrieval operations used below. The statistical survival and multi-state models are intentionally left to an appropriate statistical package rather than represented by a fabricated PM4Py function. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][2])

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE, ACT, TS, RES = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp", "org:resource"
EVENT_START = pd.Timestamp("2024-01-01", tz="UTC")
EVENT_CUTOFF = pd.Timestamp("2025-12-31 23:59:59", tz="UTC")
EXTRACT_CUTOFF = pd.Timestamp("2026-02-15 23:59:59", tz="UTC")
POLICY_CHANGE = pd.Timestamp("2025-07-01", tz="UTC")
MIGRATION = pd.Timestamp("2025-10-15", tz="UTC")
H90, FEED_GRACE = pd.Timedelta(days=90), pd.Timedelta(days=35)
FIXED_K, ELAPSED_HOURS = 3, 48

# Read one analysis vintage. label_available_at, not eventual warehouse state, gates labels.
date_cols = [TS, "label_available_at", "data_extract_time"]
raw = pd.read_csv("applications_events.csv", parse_dates=date_cols)
for c in date_cols:
    raw[c] = pd.to_datetime(raw[c], errors="coerce", utc=True)
raw["_row_order"] = np.arange(len(raw))
raw["activity_raw"] = raw[ACT]
# Fill from a governed source/date-specific mapping; empty is runnable but must be audited.
ACTIVITY_MAP: dict[tuple[str, str], str] = {}
raw[ACT] = [ACTIVITY_MAP.get((str(s), str(a)), str(a))
            for s, a in zip(raw["source_system"], raw["activity_raw"])]
canonical = {
    "Application Started", "Identity/KYC Check", "Credit Review",
    "Documents Requested", "Documents Received", "Accepted", "Contract Signed",
    "Account Activated", "Rejected", "Withdrawn", "Transferred",
    "Collections Contact", "Default Recorded",
}
raw["unmapped_post_migration"] = raw[TS].ge(MIGRATION) & ~raw[ACT].isin(canonical)
# Event features stop at the event window; extraction timestamp is only an availability audit.
events = raw.loc[raw[TS].ge(EVENT_START) & raw[TS].le(EVENT_CUTOFF) &
                 (raw["data_extract_time"].isna() |
                  raw["data_extract_time"].le(EXTRACT_CUTOFF))].copy()
pre_dedup_n = len(events)
dup_key = [CASE, ACT, TS, RES, "source_system"]
events["exact_duplicate"] = events.duplicated(dup_key, keep=False)
events = (events.sort_values([CASE, TS, "_row_order"])
                .drop_duplicates(dup_key, keep="first"))
duplicates_removed = pre_dedup_n - len(events)
events = pm4py.format_dataframe(events, case_id=CASE,
                                 activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS)

# Build one row per application and the first observed transition out of processing.
case = pd.DataFrame(index=pd.Index(events[CASE].unique(), name=CASE))
case["case_start"] = events.groupby(CASE)[TS].min()
case["last_event_time"] = events.groupby(CASE)[TS].max()
for activity, col in [("Account Activated", "activation_time"),
                      ("Rejected", "rejected_time"),
                      ("Withdrawn", "withdrawn_time"),
                      ("Transferred", "transferred_time")]:
    case[col] = events.loc[events[ACT].eq(activity)].groupby(CASE)[TS].min()
transition_names = {"activation_time": "activated", "rejected_time": "rejected",
                    "withdrawn_time": "withdrawn", "transferred_time": "transferred"}
long = (case[list(transition_names)].rename(columns=transition_names).stack()
        .rename("transition_time").reset_index()
        .rename(columns={"level_1": "first_transition"})
        .sort_values([CASE, "transition_time", "first_transition"]))
first = long.groupby(CASE, as_index=False).first().set_index(CASE)
case = case.join(first[["first_transition", "transition_time"]])
case["state_asof"] = case["first_transition"].fillna("still_open")
case["stage_a_censor_time"] = case["transition_time"].fillna(EVENT_CUTOFF)

# Collapse repeated labels only after enforcing the historical availability date.
known = events.loc[events["default_90d"].notna() &
                   events["label_available_at"].notna() &
                   events["label_available_at"].le(EXTRACT_CUTOFF)]
label_conflict = known.groupby(CASE)["default_90d"].nunique().gt(1)
latest = (known.sort_values([CASE, "label_available_at", TS])
               .groupby(CASE).tail(1).set_index(CASE))
case["default_90d"] = latest["default_90d"]
case["label_available_at"] = latest["label_available_at"]
case["horizon_end"] = case["activation_time"] + H90
case["latest_expected_feed"] = case["horizon_end"] + FEED_GRACE
case["label_observed"] = case["default_90d"].notna()
case["cohort_status"] = np.select(
    [case["state_asof"].eq("rejected"), case["state_asof"].eq("withdrawn"),
     case["state_asof"].eq("transferred"), case["state_asof"].eq("still_open"),
     case["label_observed"] & case["default_90d"].eq(1),
     case["label_observed"] & case["default_90d"].eq(0),
     case["horizon_end"].gt(EXTRACT_CUTOFF),
     case["latest_expected_feed"].gt(EXTRACT_CUTOFF)],
    ["rejected_competing", "withdrawn_competing", "transferred_competing",
     "open_right_censored", "activated_default_labeled",
     "activated_nondefault_labeled", "activated_90d_not_mature",
     "activated_feed_delay_pending"],
    default="activated_feed_missing_after_grace")

# Primary split is temporal. A transfer-linkage table should first assign one lineage_id
# so an original case and its partner continuation cannot cross folds.
case["split"] = np.select(
    [case["case_start"].lt("2025-01-01"), case["case_start"].lt(POLICY_CHANGE)],
    ["train", "validation"], default="test")
case["policy_era"] = np.where(case["case_start"].lt(POLICY_CHANGE), "old", "new")
case["migration_era"] = np.where(case["case_start"].lt(MIGRATION), "pre", "post")

# Whitelist event-time inputs before prefixing. No target, decision, collections, or snapshot fields.
safe_cols = [CASE, ACT, TS, RES, "case:channel", "case:region",
             "case:requested_amount", "risk_score", "source_system"]
safe_events = events[safe_cols].sort_values([CASE, TS]).copy()
fixed_prefix = pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(safe_events, length=FIXED_K,
                                            case_id_key=CASE)
fixed_prefix = fixed_prefix.sort_values([CASE, TS])
pred_time = fixed_prefix.groupby(CASE)[TS].max().rename("prediction_time")
pt = pred_time.reindex(case.index)
eligible = case["transition_time"].isna() | case["transition_time"].gt(pt)
fixed_prefix = fixed_prefix[fixed_prefix[CASE].isin(case.index[eligible])]
pred_time = fixed_prefix.groupby(CASE)[TS].max().rename("prediction_time")

# Separate 48-hour landmark; cases already terminal at 48h are not scored after outcome.
landmark = case["case_start"] + pd.Timedelta(hours=ELAPSED_HOURS)
elapsed_ok = case["transition_time"].isna() | case["transition_time"].gt(landmark)
elapsed_prefix = safe_events.join(case["case_start"], on=CASE)
elapsed_prefix = elapsed_prefix.loc[
    elapsed_prefix[CASE].isin(case.index[elapsed_ok]) &
    elapsed_prefix[TS].le(elapsed_prefix["case_start"] +
                           pd.Timedelta(hours=ELAPSED_HOURS))].drop(columns="case_start")

# Prefix-derived features; genuine review/document loops remain visible as counts.
g = fixed_prefix.groupby(CASE, sort=False)
features = g.agg(n_events=(ACT, "size"), prefix_start=(TS, "min"),
                 prediction_time=(TS, "max"), last_activity=(ACT, "last"),
                 unique_resources=(RES, "nunique"), risk_first=("risk_score", "first"),
                 risk_current=("risk_score", "last"), channel=("case:channel", "first"),
                 region=("case:region", "first"), amount=("case:requested_amount", "first"))
features["elapsed_hours"] = (features["prediction_time"] -
                             features["prefix_start"]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
features["document_requests"] = fixed_prefix[ACT].eq("Documents Requested").groupby(fixed_prefix[CASE]).sum()
features["credit_reviews"] = fixed_prefix[ACT].eq("Credit Review").groupby(fixed_prefix[CASE]).sum()
features["handover_count"] = fixed_prefix.groupby(CASE)[RES].apply(
    lambda s: max(int(s.ne(s.shift()).sum()) - 1, 0))
features["risk_delta"] = features["risk_current"] - features["risk_first"]
features = features.join(case[["split", "policy_era", "migration_era", "cohort_status"]])

# Process diagnostics are estimated on training prefixes only, never on test/future traces.
train_ids = case.index[case["split"].eq("train")]
train_prefix = fixed_prefix[fixed_prefix[CASE].isin(train_ids)]
dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(train_prefix)
variants = pm4py.get_variants(train_prefix)

forbidden = {"final_limit", "decision_reason", "collection_status", "default_90d",
             "label_available_at", "data_extract_time", "documents_total"}
assert not (forbidden & set(fixed_prefix.columns))
assert (fixed_prefix[TS] <= fixed_prefix[CASE].map(pred_time)).all()
assert not label_conflict.any(), "Conflicting repeated labels require adjudication."

counts = case["cohort_status"].value_counts()
counts.loc["activated_unlabeled_total"] = counts.reindex([
    "activated_90d_not_mature", "activated_feed_delay_pending",
    "activated_feed_missing_after_grace"]).fillna(0).sum()
print(counts.sort_index())
if len(case) != 48000: print(f"WARNING: expected 48,000 applications, found {len(case):,}")
print({"cases": len(case), "duplicates_removed": duplicates_removed,
       "unmapped_post_migration_rows": int(events["unmapped_post_migration"].sum()),
       "training_variants": len(variants), "training_dfg_edges": len(dfg)})
```
