# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

## **Handover-of-work network**
**What it implies:**
- Mira_Coordinator is the **primary exception-handling gateway**—she receives claims from Bot_Rules (920/4750 = ~20% of triage claims) and redistributes them to specialists (Omar, Priya) or adjusters (Carla, Diego, Elise, Farid).
- She acts as a **filter**: only 22% of claims pass through her, but she handles 64% of reopened claims and 71% of complaints, suggesting she focuses on high-complexity cases.
- **High betweenness (0.77)** confirms she is a critical bridge between automated routing (Bot_Rules) and specialist work.
- **Asymmetric role**: She initiates many handovers (out-degree 0.82) but also receives them (in-degree 0.51), indicating she both **routes inbound exceptions** and **escalates internally**.

**What it does not imply:**
- She is not a **collaborative specialist** (e.g., co-working with adjusters on the same claim for extended periods).
- Her role is not **stable team leadership** (e.g., like Alice_TeamLead or Bruno_TeamLead, who have persistent working relationships).
- The network does not show her **direct involvement in claim resolution** (e.g., performing medical/legal reviews herself).

---

## **Working-together network**
**What it implies:**
- **Low co-occurrence (weighted degree 0.16)** means Mira_Coordinator rarely appears alongside other resources on the **same claim for multiple consecutive events**.
- This aligns with her **triage/routing function**: she may touch a case briefly (e.g., assign a specialist) and then leave, while the specialist works independently.
- **Peripheral to team leads (Alice/Bruno)**: She does not collaborate with them on the same claims, suggesting she operates **outside their direct oversight**.
- **Lowest co-occurrence with specialists (Omar/Priya)**: This may reflect that once she assigns a case, the specialist works autonomously.

**What it does not imply:**
- She is **not a bottleneck**—low co-occurrence can also mean she **avoids parallel work** (e.g., no "shadowing" of adjusters).
- Her absence does not **directly** cause collaboration breakdowns (since her role is more about **routing than hands-on work**).
- The network does not capture **asynchronous coordination** (e.g., emails, tickets, or system notes not logged in the event log).

---

## **Subcontracting network**
**What it implies:**
- **High betweenness (0.69)** confirms she is a **central mediator** in specialist workflows:
  - She routes cases to Omar/Priya (medical/legal) and then **receives their responses**, often reopening or assigning further actions.
  - The pattern *"Request Missing Info  Specialist Review  Reopen/Assign"* shows she **orchestrates iterative work**.
- **High frequency of "return trips"** (e.g., Omar  Mira  next action) suggests she **manages feedback loops** in complex cases.
- **Not a passive router**: She often **re-engages** with the same case after specialists complete work, indicating **active monitoring**.

**What it does not imply:**
- She is not a **specialist herself** (her events are mostly routing/assignment, not review activities).
- The network does not show **direct conflict resolution** (e.g., mediating between adjusters and specialists).
- Her role is not **fully automated** (unlike Bot_Rules, which has a closed-loop routing pattern).

## **Performance/time statistics**
**What it implies:**
- **High variance in case cycle time contribution**: Mira_Coordinator’s presence correlates with **longer paths** in exception cases (median 4.2 min per event, but high exception rates).
- **Exception flag correlation (0.48)**: She is **overrepresented in problematic cases**, suggesting she **identifies (but may not always resolve) issues**.
- **Low median service time (4.2 min)**: Her events are **quick decisions**, but the **delayed impact** (e.g., specialist bottlenecks) is visible in later stages.
- **Absence impact**: When she was out, **exception cases queued up**, proving she is a **critical filter** for high-risk claims.

**What it does not imply:**
- She is **not the slowest resource** (e.g., Omar/Priya have longer median times per event).
- Her **high betweenness does not mean she is overworked**—her event count (960) is lower than adjusters (1000+).
- The **high exception rate is not solely her fault**—it may reflect **inherent complexity** in cases she handles.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira_Coordinator plays a **critical but ambiguous role** in the claims process: she acts as the **primary exception-handling gateway**, routing high-risk cases to specialists while managing rework loops. Her **high centrality in handover and subcontracting networks** means she is indispensable for complex claims, but her **low working-together co-occurrence** suggests she operates as a **triage broker rather than a collaborative leader**.

**Key risks**:
- **Key-person dependency**: Her absence causes **exception case delays**, risking compliance.
- **Uncontrolled discretion**: Without clear escalation rules, **case handling may lack consistency**.
- **Specialist overload**: She routes cases to Omar/Priya, who already handle **high-exception volumes**.

**Recommended actions**:
1. **Formalize her escalation criteria** to reduce discretionary risk.
2. **Cross-train adjusters** in basic exception handling to mitigate key-person risk.
3. **Monitor specialist workloads** and exception case cycle times post-intervention.

**Do not remove her role** without first **testing alternative coordination mechanisms**. Instead, **gradually distribute her functions** while ensuring **quality and efficiency are maintained**.
