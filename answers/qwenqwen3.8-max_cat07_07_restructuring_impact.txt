Therefore, the benefit case must rest on reduced rework, better specialist effectiveness, fewer informal interruptions, and improved East-team load — not simply on a lower nominal handover count.

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

| Risk | Why average cycle time can hide it | Process-mining signal |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Tail latency deterioration | Average can improve while p90/p99 worsen, especially if simple cases flow quickly and complex cases queue | Cycle-time quantiles by `case_complexity`, `required_specialty`, `team`, and `customer_segment` |
| 2. High-complexity case penalty | High-complexity cases may be routed to the shared queue and wait longer, while low-complexity cases improve the average | Filter `case_complexity = high`; compare waiting, rework, SLA misses, and p90 cycle time |

| 5. Learning and skill atrophy | If hard cases are routed away, local staff stop developing technical judgment; this appears later as weaker triage and more rework | Activity participation by local resources, activity-based resource similarity, organizational role changes, long-term rework trends |
| 6. Accountability diffusion | Shared approval can make ownership unclear without changing average cycle time much | More distinct approvers per case, higher `reassignment_count`, ambiguous `handover_reason`, role-discovery instability |

# 5. Pilot design

## Pilot scope

Use a **limited, instrumented pilot** rather than a full rollout.

Recommended pilot scope:

- Pilot only the **Technical Shared Service** first.
- Do not immediately implement the full shared approval queue.
- Keep Intake and simple Eligibility Review local.
- Keep Legal/Risk shared, but ring-fence capacity so the pilot does not overload already scarce specialists.
- Include high-complexity technical cases and cases requiring technical/security review.
- Retain at least one local technical liaison or embedded specialist capacity in each team during the pilot.

Preferred routing design:

- For eligible high-complexity cases, route a controlled percentage, for example 50%, to the Technical Shared Service.
- Keep the remaining eligible cases with local embedded specialists as a concurrent control.
- If case-level randomization is operationally impossible, use a phased rollout: start with East, then add South, then North, using matched comparison cases.

## Comparison group

The pilot needs a credible comparison group.

Options:

1. **Within-team control lane**
   - Treatment: high-complexity cases routed to Technical Shared Service.
   - Control: matched high-complexity cases remaining with local embedded specialists.
   - Match on `team`, `case_complexity`, `required_specialty`, `customer_segment`, `priority`, and `location`.

2. **Stepped-wedge team comparison**
   - East pilots first because E_Tech is overloaded.
   - North and South act as comparison teams.
   - Use matched cases because East has a different baseline.

3. **Difference-in-differences**
   - Compare pre/post changes in pilot cases against pre/post changes in control cases.
   - This helps separate general time trends from restructuring effects.

## Pre/post observation window

Recommended timeline:

- **Baseline period:** 8–12 weeks before the pilot.
- **Stabilization period:** 2–4 weeks after go-live, excluded from strict evaluation because of learning effects.
- **Pilot evaluation period:** 12 weeks minimum.
- **Extended outcome window:** 6 months for learning, fairness, and customer-satisfaction effects.

Weekly reviews should track leading indicators. Monthly reviews should track lagging indicators.

## Segmentation

Every metric should be segmented by:

- `team`: North, South, East.
- `location`.
- `case_complexity`: low, medium, high.
- `required_specialty`: technical, legal, risk, security, none.
- `customer_segment`: for example enterprise, SMB, public sector, language-sensitive or region-sensitive segments.
- `priority`.
- `handover_reason`.
- `local_expertise_flag`.
- `reassignment_count`.

Do not evaluate the pilot only at the global level. East may improve while North/South worsen, and simple cases may improve while complex cases worsen.

## Guardrail metrics

The pilot should have explicit guardrails.

Examples:

| Guardrail | Threshold for concern |
|---|---|
| Median wait after `local caseworker  Technical Shared Service` | Sustained increase above 4–6 hours |
| p90 wait after technical handover | Sustained increase above 8 hours |
| High-complexity p90 cycle time | More than 10% worse than matched control |
| Rework rate after shared technical advice | More than 1.5 percentage points above baseline/control |
| SLA miss rate | More than 1.5 percentage points above baseline/control |
| Queue WIP | Rising for 2 consecutive weeks without demand increase |
| Specialist utilization | Sustained above 90–92% without additional capacity |
| Reassignment_count | More than 10–15% above baseline/control |
| Fairness gap | p90 cycle-time gap between teams or customer segments widening materially |
| Local expertise usage | Sharp decline in `local_expertise_flag` without compensating controls |
| Staff role clarity | Survey score decline or repeated complaints about ownership |

## Staff feedback collection

Process mining alone is insufficient because some effects are off-log or qualitative.

Collect:

- Weekly pulse survey for local caseworkers, specialists, approvers, and implementation staff.
- Structured interviews after weeks 2, 6, and 12.
- Queue comments or handover notes categorized by issue type.
- Local liaison escalation log.
- Feedback from customer-facing staff about language, regional conventions, and repeated customer explanations.
- Qualitative review of cases with high reassignment_count or repeated handoffs.

## Rollback or adjustment triggers

Rollback or adjustment should be triggered if, for two consecutive weeks or one critical monthly review:

- Technical Shared Service waiting exceeds guardrails.
- High-complexity p90 cycle time or SLA misses worsen materially.
- Rework after shared advice rises materially.
- Legal/Risk waiting worsens because technical triage is delayed.
- Fairness gaps widen across teams or customer segments.
- Staff report unclear ownership or shared approval ambiguity.
- Queue WIP grows faster than arrivals.
- Local technical skill usage collapses without a mitigation plan.

Possible adjustments before full rollback:

- Add queue triage capacity.
- Restore local technical liaison hours.
- Limit new routing to only the highest-complexity cases.
- Add priority rules with fairness monitoring.
- Improve handover templates and case notes.
- Delay shared approval rollout until technical queue is stable.

# 6. Recommendation

Recommendation: **modify and pilot first; do not proceed with full rollout immediately.**

The restructuring has plausible benefits:

- East may benefit because E_Tech is already overloaded.
- Shared specialists may improve consistency.
- Standardized approvals may reduce local variation.
- Pooled capacity can improve coverage for absences.
- Specialist utilization may increase.

However, the risks are substantial:

- Legal/Risk specialists are already near capacity.
- Technical Shared Service may become a hidden bottleneck.
- Local expertise loss may increase referrals and rework.
- Shared approval may reduce accountability.
- High-complexity cases may suffer tail delays.
- North/South may lose local responsiveness even if East improves.
- Learning effects may weaken future resilience.

The proposal should be modified before piloting:

1. Do not treat “15% higher specialist utilization” as a pure benefit. If utilization approaches saturation, waiting time can increase nonlinearly.
2. Add explicit queue ownership and accountable approvers.
3. Retain local technical liaisons or local escalation paths.
4. Add triage capacity for Technical Shared Service and Legal/Risk.
5. Log queue entry, queue pickup, handover_reason, reassignment_count, and local_expertise_flag consistently.
6. Monitor p90/p99 cycle time and waiting time, not only averages.
7. Monitor fairness by team, location, customer_segment, priority, and required_specialty.
8. Delay full shared approval rollout until the technical shared queue is stable.

Management should not infer success from early average cycle time alone. Early averages can improve because simple cases flow faster, while high-complexity cases, legal/risk cases, and region-sensitive cases deteriorate at the tail. Early averages can also hide rework that appears later, skill decay that appears over months, and fairness shifts across teams or customer segments.

Proceed to broader rollout only if the pilot shows:

- Stable or improved p90 cycle time for high-complexity cases.
- No material increase in rework after shared specialist advice.
- Stable or improved SLA performance.
- No widening fairness gaps.
- Clear accountability in shared approval.
- Queue WIP under control.
- Staff feedback indicating acceptable role clarity.

def case_summary(log):
    d = pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(log)

    summary = d.groupby(CASE).agg(
        start_time=(TS, "min"),
        end_time=(TS, "max"),
        team=("team", "first"),
        location=("location", "first"),
        case_complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
        required_specialty=("required_specialty", "first"),
        customer_segment=("customer_segment", "first"),
        priority=("priority", "first"),
        reassignment_count=("reassignment_count", "max"),
        local_expertise_flag=("local_expertise_flag", "max"),
        outcome=("outcome", "last"),
        rework_flag=("rework_flag", "max"),
    ).reset_index()

    summary["cycle_days"] = (
        summary["end_time"] - summary["start_time"]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 86400

    # If SLA miss is explicitly logged in outcome:
    summary["sla_miss"] = (
        summary["outcome"]
        .str.contains("SLA miss", case=False, na=False)
    )

    # Optional threshold-based SLA miss.
    # Replace 10 with the actual SLA threshold in days.
    summary["sla_miss_threshold"] = summary["cycle_days"] > 10

    return summary

before_summary = case_summary(before_log)
after_summary = case_summary(after_log)

        "p99_cycle_days": summary_df["cycle_days"].quantile(0.99),
        "sla_miss_rate": summary_df["sla_miss"].mean(),
        "sla_miss_threshold_rate": summary_df["sla_miss_threshold"].mean(),
        "rework_rate": summary_df["rework_flag"].mean(),
        "avg_reassignment_count": summary_df["reassignment_count"].mean(),
    }
