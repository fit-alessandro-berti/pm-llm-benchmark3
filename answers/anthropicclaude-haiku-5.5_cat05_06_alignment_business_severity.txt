## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity is the business consequence if the deviation is real. Confidence is how well the evidence supports that the deviation actually occurred and was correctly characterized. The two are scored separately. A severe case with low confidence (CL-602) needs investigation first, not dismissal or immediate closure.

**Severity dimensions (scored 1–5 by analyst judgment, not by formula):**

| Dimension | What raises severity |
|---|---|
| Control objective | Breaches a preventive gate (fraud screen, approval, bank-change check) rather than a documentation or label step |
| Amount / exposure | Payment value and whether the claim is high-value relative to the authority threshold |
| Reversibility | Paid and settled (hard to recover) vs. pending, held, or recallable |
| Timing | Failure before payment vs. after; a notification breach vs. an on-time notification |
| Customer / regulatory harm | Wrong payee, unnotified claimant, duplicate communication, incorrect denial |
| Recurrence | Systematic vs. one-off; frequency across the month |
| Evidence reliability | Whether the control evidence (logs, documents, links) can be trusted or audited |
| Model scope | Whether the deviation is inside the model's stated scope (payable claims) or disputed |

**Anchors:**
- **1:** Telemetry, compliant or cosmetic, no customer or financial effect.
- **2:** Labeling, duplicate communication, or out-of-scope outcome with no payment; governance follow-up needed.
- **3:** Evidence gap for a control that may have operated, or a notification-evidence weakness, with meaningful value at stake.
- **4:** Clear policy violation on a payment that did not necessarily involve fraud, or a bank-change signal without confirmed fraud.
- **5:** Payment made without a required gate, or a payment-diversion pattern on a high-value claim, where recovery is uncertain.

**Confidence** is assessed on three questions: Does the log record the event? Is there independent corroboration (bank records, documents, timestamps)? Is the business metadata (risk rating, amount, bank-change date) reliable at decision time? Absence of a log event is not proof that the activity did not occur, and absence of a logged activity is not proof that it did.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

Missing events are not proof that activities did not occur. Each hypothesis below must be tested against independent evidence.

**CL-602 (fraud screen missing on EUR 480k bank-change payment)**
- H1: Fraud screen was performed through an unlogged channel (e.g., a separate fraud platform or a verbal check recorded nowhere). Discriminating evidence: query the fraud platform by claim, claimant, and new account; obtain timestamps and notes for the verbal check; check whether a bank-change verification call was recorded.
- H2: Fraud screen was not performed; the approver accepted an informal check. Discriminating evidence: absence of any screen record across systems; approver's interview record; whether the approver's authority covers screening; comparable cases where the approver's approvals lacked screen records.
- H3: The fraud screen event exists under a different claim ID (correlation failure). Discriminating evidence: search by bank-account hash, claimant identity, and time window; check for a duplicate or re-keyed claim.

**CL-604 (EUR 90k paid before approval)**
- H1: Payment instruction was released before approval (control bypass or system gating failure). Discriminating evidence: payment-system audit logs showing who or what released the instruction; approval workflow state at 10:03; whether an approval token was required.
- H2: The logged Pay Claim is a bank-acceptance timestamp for an instruction queued earlier, while approval was pending; the instruction was then executed after approval. Discriminating evidence: bank settlement record (value date, settlement timestamp); the number of payment instructions for the claim.
- H3: Timestamp error (approval time mis-recorded). Discriminating evidence: approval system clock vs. core log clock; approver's workstation logs.

**CL-606 (external assessment not in core)**
- H1: Loss assessment was performed externally and is valid, but integration did not write the event. Discriminating evidence: adjuster's firm records, licensing check, report content matching the claim's loss amount, document metadata creation time.
- H2: The report belongs to a different claim or was reused. Discriminating evidence: claim ID and claimant identity on the report; document hash versus other claims' documents.
- H3: No assessment was performed; the report was post-dated. Discriminating evidence: document creation metadata vs. signed date; adjuster's own system record.

**CL-608 (notification evidence for EUR 250k claim)**
- H1: The portal message was sent and delivered at +3h, and the CRM link is correct. Discriminating evidence: portal delivery receipt with immutable message ID; claimant access logs.
- H2: The CRM link points to another claim or a template. Discriminating evidence: message content and claim identifier inside the message; CRM audit trail for edits to the link field.
- H3: No notification was sent; the CRM entry is a draft. Discriminating evidence: portal outbound records; claimant-side confirmation.

**CL-609 (EUR 18k MEDIUM-risk auto-approval)**
- H1: The risk rating or amount at decision time was different (stale data), so the auto-approval appeared compliant when made. Discriminating evidence: decision-time snapshot of amount and risk; risk-scoring history for the claim.
- H2: The guard is not implemented in the workflow; auto-approval is unconditional. Discriminating evidence: workflow rule configuration; the set of all auto-approvals with MEDIUM risk or amount > EUR 5,000 across the month.
- H3: A user override or role misconfiguration allowed auto-approval. Discriminating evidence: user role logs; override records.

**CL-610 (EUR 4.5k auto-approved one hour after bank change)**
- H1: Bank change was legitimate (customer-initiated, verified). Discriminating evidence: change-request channel and verification record; callback log to a known number; claimant contact history.
- H2: Bank change was fraudulent (account takeover or payment redirection). Discriminating evidence: new account's ownership and prior claims; device or IP metadata on the change request; whether funds arrived at the new account.
- H3: The bank-change timestamp is incorrect (recorded after payment or with a timezone error). Discriminating evidence: source-system timestamps for the change and payment; system clock audit.

**CL-611 ("Assess Damage" vs. "Assess Loss")**
- H1: Labels are synonyms; no control gap. Discriminating evidence: signed procedure definitions; mapping table version active at the event time; other cases using each label.
- H2: "Assess Damage" is a distinct step and the loss assessment was skipped. Discriminating evidence: assessment artifacts (documents, valuation records) for this claim; the procedure's definition of each step.
- H3: The mapping table change created a mismatch for this claim only. Discriminating evidence: whether cases before and after the mapping update show consistent labels for the same activity.

**CL-603 (duplicate notification)**
- H1: Single notification with a retry copy in logs only. Discriminating evidence: message ID and transport logs; claimant-side delivery records.
- H2: Two genuine notifications sent. Discriminating evidence: distinct content or send times; the connector's retry configuration.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

**Cost-function design.** The supplied cost function assigns cost 1 to every visible log or model move. That treats a missing telemetry event (CL-601, cost 3) the same as a missing fraud screen (CL-602, cost 1 for a single move), but the business consequences are not comparable. The uniform cost cannot distinguish a harmless workbench click from the one event that controls whether EUR 480k is paid. CL-602 scores 1; CL-601 scores 3. The ordering is inverted relative to business risk.

**Scope.** CL-607 and CL-612 score 4 each because the model does not cover denied and withdrawn outcomes. Their cost measures the model's limits. No payment occurred in either case. Conversely, if denied claims were in scope, these alignments would represent a different kind of issue. The cost is correct about the model fit and uninformative about risk.

**Hidden moves and zero-cost policy failures.** CL-605 contains a silent transition and scores 0; this is correct, because its path is compliant and the silent move is a routing construct. CL-609 and CL-610 also score 0 but are serious policy failures. The model permits Auto Approve unconditionally, so the alignment is perfect while the data-aware policy is violated. A cost of 0 is not evidence of compliance when the relevant guard depends on data attributes the model does not encode.

**Data conditions.** The same model path has different risk depending on attributes: CL-610 (EUR 4,500, bank change one hour before) and CL-605 (EUR 800, no bank change) have identical structure and cost 0 each. CL-610's risk is in the data. Alignment alone cannot see it.

**Activity granularity.** CL-611's "Assess Damage" vs. "Assess Loss" is a single move pair with cost 2. Whether it is a naming issue or a missing assessment depends on the procedure definition, not on the move count. Similarly, CL-604's fix (reorder moves) costs very little, but the business fact (payment before approval) is severe. The cheap repair describes the model, not what happened to the money.

**Duplicated telemetry.** CL-603 and the 74-case pattern show retry duplicates that inflate event counts. Those duplicates cost 1 each, and the total cost across many cases could look like a large operational issue when the underlying problem is a single connector.

**Missing-but-external evidence.** CL-606's core log lacks Assess Loss, costing 1. The assessment may exist in an external adjuster system and be fully valid. A missing core event is a correlation gap, not an operational failure, and it should not be scored as an absence of work.

**Irreversibility.** CL-604's payment was accepted by the bank at 10:03. Once settled, it may be unrecoverable, making a single alignment move with cost 3 a severe and irreversible loss. The alignment has no concept of reversibility. A cost-1 alignment on a pending payment and a cost-1 alignment on a settled payment are very different situations.

**Conclusion.** Alignment cost is a useful diagnostic of model fit and log consistency. It is not a severity measure. Business prioritization must combine the alignment with policy overlays, amounts, reversibility, evidence quality, and customer impact.

MOVE_RE = re.compile(r"^([SLMT])\((.*?)(?:,c=(\d+))?\)$")

def parse_alignment(s):
    moves = []
    for tok in (t.strip() for t in s.split(ARROW)):
        m = MOVE_RE.match(tok)
        if m is None:
            raise ValueError(f"Unparsed move token: {tok}")
        moves.append((m.group(1), m.group(2), int(m.group(3) or 0)))
    return moves

summary_rows = []
for case, s in SUPPLIED.items():
    moves = parse_alignment(s)

    acts = LOG_TRACES[case]
    pay_idx = acts.index("Pay Claim") if "Pay Claim" in acts else None

def p1_check(case, row):
    if not row["auto_approve_logged"]:
        return "n/a"
    m = META[case]
    if m["amount_eur"] is None or m["risk"] is None or m["bank_change_7d"] is None:
        return "UNKNOWN"
    ok = m["amount_eur"] <= 5000 and m["risk"] == "LOW" and not m["bank_change_7d"]
    return "OK" if ok else "VIOLATION"

def p2_required(case):
    m = META[case]
    if m["amount_eur"] is None:
        return "n/a"
    req = m["amount_eur"] > 50000 or m["risk"] == "HIGH" or m["bank_change_7d"] is True
    return "required" if req else "not required"

**Notes on the code:**
- PM4Py's alignment costs and optimal alignments may differ from the supplied notation (e.g., weighting of log and model moves, choice among equally optimal alignments). Treat `pm4py_cost` as a diagnostic cross-check on the supplied `supplied_raw_cost`, not a replacement for it.
- `payment_before_approval` and `p2_fraud_or_manual_gap` are evidence flags derived from the supplied alignments; they are not severity judgments.
- `analyst_severity_1_5` and `analyst_confidence` are entered from the Section 2 narrative and kept separate from technical cost by design. Any change to them should be made by a reviewer with the case evidence, not by recalculating from move counts.
- `raw_cost_rank_for_contrast` is shown only to make the ranking mismatch visible in the output.
