# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

The stable backbone is mandatory for completed onboarding cases. I interpret an accepted case as one containing `Activate Vendor`; a rejected case contains `Reject Vendor`. `Activate Vendor` and `Reject Vendor` are the final outcome events, and `Close Onboarding` is the last event of a completed case. The high- and low-risk routes are inferred from their activities; an actual risk classification value is not supplied, so the model cannot establish that the chosen route matches the assessed risk. The three initial checks and classification occur once in the backbone; additional `Verify Bank Account` events are permitted and checked separately. Activities and resources are case-sensitive identities, and event order is a reliable total order, including a tie-breaker for equal timestamps.

Clarification requests may occur at multiple points before the outcome, but each must be answered before the next request and before either outcome. Other events may intervene. I do not prohibit unsolicited clarification responses. Sanctions escalations may occur more than once, after screening and before the outcome; each needs an opinion after it. One later opinion may discharge several escalations. An opinion after rejection is permitted, provided it occurs before closure. A change after activation need not be followed by a payment, but still requires a subsequent suspension by closure. For each later payment there must be a verification after every preceding post-activation change. A verification may satisfy several changes. The first payment is at most once, as stated.

Two coherent policy choices are:

1. **Legal priority, selected here:** if an escalation precedes activation, an opinion after that escalation must precede activation. No emergency exception is recognized. R4 still applies to rejected cases. This is conservative with respect to Legal's statement, but can block an Operations-approved urgent activation and may be operationally unacceptable.
2. **Authorized emergency exception:** normally require the same ordering, but allow activation first when an independently verifiable joint authorization exists. Each still-pending opinion must arrive within 24 hours after activation; it must also follow the relevant escalation. This implements R6 but conflicts with Legal's stated unconditional rule and exposes the organization to a legal-policy breach unless Legal approves the exception. Missing or unverified authorization is not evidence of permission.

Both policies cannot be simultaneously normative for an activation after an escalation and before an opinion. The chosen policy is a modeling decision, not an assertion that either department has authority to override the other. Escalations after an outcome are disallowed by this model assumption; a later operational escalation would need a separate lifecycle policy.

# 2. Requirement-to-Formalism Mapping

The authoritative layer means the layer whose result governs that rule; duplicates are diagnostics. All checks apply to completed cases unless explicitly described as pending.

| Requirement | Primary formalism | Exact encoding | Why this formalism | Redundancy or limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Procedural | XOR between rejection and either accepted route | Stable exclusive outcome | DECLARE `noncoexistence` and skeleton `never_together` duplicate it. |
| R2 | Procedural | Rejection has neither approval; low route has exactly one manager approval; high route exactly one compliance approval | Route cardinality and outcome are naturally procedural | DECLARE exclusion rules and skeleton frequencies are partial duplicates; the procedural check uses a projection. |
| R3 | DECLARE plus external guard, jointly authoritative | `altresponse(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)`; external pending-request state rejects an outcome before the response | Alternate response handles repeat cycles with unrelated intervening events | Alternate response alone permits a response after the outcome. No chain response is imposed. |
| R4 | DECLARE | `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` | Each escalation has a later opinion | One opinion can satisfy multiple escalations; an open case has a pending obligation. |
| R5 | Data-aware policy layer | For every escalation before activation, there is an opinion strictly between them | Conditional three-event ordering | `precedence(Opinion, Activation)` would wrongly require an opinion in cases with no escalation. |
| R6 | Data-aware policy layer | Alternative policy only: for each pending escalation at activation, authenticated joint authorization and an opinion after activation within 24 hours | Requires authorization evidence and a deadline | Not active in the selected strict policy; absent authorization is indeterminate, not a valid exception. |
| R7 | DECLARE | `precedence(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` | Each escalation requires an earlier screening | Procedural projection removes escalations, so it cannot enforce this. |
| R8 | DECLARE | `noncoexistence(Enhanced Due Diligence, Manager Approval)` and `noncoexistence(Compliance Approval, Manager Approval)` | Case-wide exclusion | Also follows from backbone XOR and is partially duplicated by the skeleton. |
| R9 | Resource-aware layer | Compare all verification resources against applicable approval resources and all master-data resources against activation resources; require inequality | Needs event-level resource identity | Missing identity produces an indeterminate check, not a pass. |
| R10 | Procedural | At most one activation, rejection, and first payment | Cardinality is fixed by the backbone | Skeleton frequency sets independently check counts; DECLARE `absence` means zero, not at most one. |
| R11 | Data-aware state validator | After each post-activation change require a later suspension; any later payment requires a verification after that change and before payment | The condition depends on position relative to activation and the latest changes | Generic response from every change to suspension would overconstrain pre-activation changes. Additional verification events are projected out of the backbone. |
| R12 | Temporal profile | Three specified pairs, seconds, `zeta = 2.0`; flag `abs(elapsed - mean) > 2 * standard deviation` | Statistical performance expectation | Warning only; pair matching and missing timestamps require explicit handling. |

# 3. Procedural Backbone

Compactly, with `;` for sequence, `||` for parallel interleaving and `XOR` for exclusive choice:

```text
Receive Onboarding Request;
(Capture Master Data || Run Sanctions Screening || Verify Bank Account);
Classify Risk;
XOR(
    Reject Vendor,
    Manager Approval; Activate Vendor; Release First Payment,
    Enhanced Due Diligence; Compliance Approval; Activate Vendor; Release First Payment
);
Close Onboarding
```

The following builds a self-contained PM4Py process tree. Each occurrence of a visible label is a separate tree leaf; both activation leaves have the same visible label.

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

def leaf(label):
    return ProcessTree(label=label)

def block(operator, *children):
    node = ProcessTree(operator=operator)
    for child in children:
        child.parent = node
        node.children.append(child)
    return node

checks = block(
    Operator.PARALLEL,
    leaf("Capture Master Data"),
    leaf("Run Sanctions Screening"),
    leaf("Verify Bank Account"),
)
low = block(
    Operator.SEQUENCE,
    leaf("Manager Approval"),
    leaf("Activate Vendor"),
    leaf("Release First Payment"),
)
high = block(
    Operator.SEQUENCE,
    leaf("Enhanced Due Diligence"),
    leaf("Compliance Approval"),
    leaf("Activate Vendor"),
    leaf("Release First Payment"),
)
procedural_model = block(
    Operator.SEQUENCE,
    leaf("Receive Onboarding Request"),
    checks,
    leaf("Classify Risk"),
    block(Operator.XOR, leaf("Reject Vendor"), low, high),
    leaf("Close Onboarding"),
)
```

The tree accepts all six orderings of the three checks, then requires classification and exactly one route. `Close Onboarding` follows rejection or payment. It does not directly model clarification loops, escalations, opinions, bank changes, suspensions, or repeat bank verification. To check the backbone, project the full trace to its tree labels and retain only the first `Verify Bank Account`; check all other events and every repeat verification on the unprojected trace. This is an intentional approximation of cross-cutting behavior, not permission to ignore those events. Reject unknown labels before projection. Full-trace closure, clarification, escalation, and post-activation constraints are checked separately. The projection alone can accept incorrectly placed or incomplete auxiliary activity. No atomic completion times or concurrency can be established from a list of single timestamps; parallelism here means allowed interleavings.

# 4. DECLARE Model

```python
rule = lambda: {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0}
declare_model = {
    "init": {
        "Receive Onboarding Request": rule(),
    },
    "altresponse": {
        ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): rule(),
    },
    "response": {
        ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): rule(),
    },
    "precedence": {
        ("Run Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation"): rule(),
    },
    "noncoexistence": {
        ("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor"): rule(),
        ("Reject Vendor", "Manager Approval"): rule(),
        ("Reject Vendor", "Compliance Approval"): rule(),
        ("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Manager Approval"): rule(),
        ("Compliance Approval", "Manager Approval"): rule(),
    },
}
```

| Template and key | Direction and semantics |
|---|---|
| `init(Receive Onboarding Request)` | The first event is the request. |
| `altresponse(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` | Every request has a later response before the next request, if any; other events may intervene. |
| `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` | Every escalation has some later opinion, possibly shared by multiple escalations. |
| `precedence(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` | Every escalation has an earlier screening; it does not require an escalation after each screening. |
| `noncoexistence(Reject Vendor, Activate Vendor)` | Neither can occur in a case containing the other, in either order. |
| `noncoexistence(Reject Vendor, Manager Approval)` | Rejection and manager approval cannot both occur. |
| `noncoexistence(Reject Vendor, Compliance Approval)` | Rejection and compliance approval cannot both occur. |
| `noncoexistence(Enhanced Due Diligence, Manager Approval)` | Due diligence and manager approval cannot both occur. |
| `noncoexistence(Compliance Approval, Manager Approval)` | The two approvals cannot both occur. |

Response is triggered by the source; precedence constrains the target. Alternate response requires a response before another source, whereas chain response would require the response immediately next and is too restrictive. Noncoexistence forbids both orders; nonsuccession is directional and cannot express R1. Values of `1.0` are explicit author-supplied metadata, not empirical measurements or additional constraints.

# 5. Log-Skeleton Model

Apply this skeleton to a projection containing **only** the six labels present in `activ_freq`. This matters for PM4Py versions that treat an activity omitted from `activ_freq` as a deviation, rather than as unconstrained. The projection must be made without reordering events. Empty `directly_follows` imposes no adjacency constraint.

```python
log_skeleton = {
    "equivalence": set(),
    "always_before": {
        ("Release First Payment", "Activate Vendor"),
    },
    "always_after": {
        ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"),
    },
    "never_together": {
        ("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor"),
        ("Reject Vendor", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Reject Vendor", "Compliance Approval"),
        ("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Compliance Approval", "Manager Approval"),
    },
    "activ_freq": {
        "Activate Vendor": {0, 1},
        "Reject Vendor": {0, 1},
        "Release First Payment": {0, 1},
        "Manager Approval": {0, 1},
        "Compliance Approval": {0, 1},
        "Enhanced Due Diligence": {0, 1},
    },
    "directly_follows": set(),
}
```

For PM4Py skeletons, `always_before(X, Y)` means an occurrence of `Y` is earlier than `X`; thus payment requires an earlier activation. `always_after(X, Y)` means an occurrence of `Y` is later than `X`; thus activation requires a later payment in a completed accepted case. `never_together` pairs are symmetric in meaning: the five pairs above forbid their two members from co-occurring. Frequency sets allow zero or one occurrence of each listed activity. These checks overlap with the backbone and DECLARE; they are intentionally weaker than the full procedural route rules. The given before/after pairs concern singleton activities, avoiding ambiguity about how a library handles multiple occurrences. `equivalence` is empty because frequency equality would be unsafe for repeatable activities.

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (48 * 3600, 24 * 3600),
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): (12 * 3600, 6 * 3600),
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"): (72 * 3600, 36 * 3600),
}
zeta = 2.0
```

Flag absolute standardized deviation strictly greater than `2.0`; equality is within the profile. Negative durations or missing timestamps are data-quality problems, not valid measurements. For repeat clarification cycles, match each request to its first response before the next request; for escalation, evaluate each escalation against its first later opinion; for payment, use the unique activation and payment. Only matched pairs are timed. Missing responses are control-flow or pending-obligation findings, not a zero-duration measurement. PM4Py versions can evaluate more than one later pair when activities repeat, so use explicit pairing if needed to obtain these intended per-obligation semantics. The profile is a statistical warning, including for unusually short durations; it does not override hard ordering or deadlines.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

The following precise pseudocode uses the full, ordered case. Each event has `activity`, `resource`, and `timestamp`; case metadata contains a stable case ID and, for the exception policy, independently verified joint authorization linked to this case and activation. `BEFORE` means strict event order. A missing required resource, timestamp, event-order tie-breaker, or authorization proof yields `not evaluated` for the affected check and an explicit data-quality finding. Known violations remain failures even when another check is indeterminate.

```text
validate(case, policy):
    require a case ID, complete event list, activity labels, and deterministic event order
    if any is missing: return not evaluated with a data-quality finding
    if unknown activity labels exist: report fail
    if Close Onboarding is not the final event of a completed case: report fail

    # R3: altresponse is also checked by DECLARE; this adds the outcome boundary.
    pending_request = false
    outcome_seen = false
    for event in case.events:
        if event.activity == Request Clarification:
            if pending_request or outcome_seen: report fail
            pending_request = true
        if event.activity == Receive Clarification and pending_request:
            pending_request = false
        if event.activity in {Activate Vendor, Reject Vendor}:
            if pending_request: report fail
            outcome_seen = true
        if event.activity == Sanctions Escalation and outcome_seen:
            report fail  # explicit pre-outcome escalation assumption
    if pending_request at completed-case end: report fail

    # R9: use every relevant event, including repeat verifications.
    approvals = all Manager Approval and Compliance Approval events
    for v in all Verify Bank Account events:
        for a in approvals:
            compare_different_resources(v, a)
    for m in all Capture Master Data events:
        for a in all Activate Vendor events:
            compare_different_resources(m, a)
    # compare_different_resources reports fail if equal, not evaluated if either
    # identity is missing, otherwise pass. If a route has no applicable approval,
    # the procedural layer reports the missing approval; do not invent a resource.

    # R4 and R5/R6. Event indices are strict ordering; closure marks completeness.
    for each Sanctions Escalation e:
        opinions = all Obtain Legal Opinion events strictly after e
        if opinions is empty: report fail for R4 in a completed case
        for each Activate Vendor a strictly after e:
            if an opinion o exists with e BEFORE o BEFORE a:
                continue
            if policy == LEGAL_PRIORITY:
                report fail for R5 at a
            else if policy == AUTHORIZED_EMERGENCY:
                auth = verified_joint_authorization(case.id, a.id)
                if auth is missing or unverifiable:
                    report not evaluated for authorization; do not accept exception
                else if auth is false or does not cover this activation:
                    report fail
                else:
                    o = first opinion strictly after a
                    if o is absent: report fail in a completed case
                    else if either timestamp is missing: report not evaluated
                    else if o.timestamp - a.timestamp < 0: report data-quality failure
                    else if o.timestamp - a.timestamp > 24 hours: report fail
                    # o is after e because e BEFORE a BEFORE o

    # R11: process each change independently.
    for each Change Bank Details c with an Activate Vendor before c:
        if no Suspend Vendor occurs strictly after c by closure: report fail
        for each Release First Payment p strictly after c:
            if no Verify Bank Account v satisfies c BEFORE v BEFORE p:
                report fail at p

    aggregate failures, indeterminate checks, and passes separately
```

For an open case, future obligations such as an unanswered request, an opinion, or a suspension are pending rather than failed until their relevant deadline, outcome boundary, or case completion. Resource checks require stable, comparable person IDs; missing or ambiguous IDs are indeterminate. For emergency authorization, a free-text assertion is insufficient: the external evidence must identify both authorizers, their authority, the case and activation, and its validity at activation. Without these attributes T6 cannot be established as an authorized exception. In the strict selected policy, authorization is irrelevant to the R5 result.

A production implementation should also validate that the risk value recorded at `Classify Risk` matches the selected route. The risk value is absent from the supplied cases, so that additional check is not evaluated here. Similarly, timestamps and sequence numbers must be trustworthy; a timestamp alone cannot establish who approved an exception or resolve tied events.

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

The procedural XOR, DECLARE noncoexistence, and skeleton never-together all enforce parts of the same outcome and route exclusions. The overlap gives independent diagnostics, not three votes on policy. Skeleton frequency and procedural structure also repeat R10. A disagreement must be investigated for projection, version, or rule differences rather than resolved by majority.

A skeleton frequency equivalence between requests and responses would wrongly forbid unsolicited responses and could conceal wrong ordering; one response per request is not the stated rule. An unconditional `response(Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor)` would incorrectly cover changes before activation. Restricting bank verification to exactly one occurrence in the full trace would conflict with R11. An unconditional opinion-before-activation precedence would require an opinion even when there was no escalation. Do not add these attractive but incompatible shortcuts.

Response, precedence, and noncoexistence are often vacuously satisfied when their triggering activities are absent. Their satisfaction alone does not establish that an outcome, an approval, or a payment exists; the procedural model supplies those obligations. Completed-case checks must be distinguished from monitoring a live case with pending obligations.

The log has no risk classification value, emergency flag, proof of joint authorization, or verification linkage to particular bank details. Event labels alone cannot prove that a new verification covered the changed account. Reliable case IDs, resource identities, timestamps, and event ordering are also necessary. Equal timestamps without a tie-breaker cannot establish strict precedence, and recorded event timestamps do not prove physical concurrency, authorization, or completion of unlogged actions.

`support` and `confidence` are metadata here, supplied as `1.0` for the requested dictionary format. They are not evidence that a requirement is legally valid or that every historical case complies; discovered values can reflect incomplete, biased, or nonconforming logs. Policy ownership and versioning must be explicit.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

Keep the process tree as the canonical representation of the stable route structure, and retain the full, unprojected event stream as the source of evidence. Version an authoritative policy specification that includes the selected Legal-priority decision, the DECLARE rules, resource and state checks, and the temporal warning thresholds. The process tree is authoritative for route execution; the policy layer is authoritative for cross-cutting obligations and attributes.

First validate event identity, case completeness, ordering, timestamps, and required attributes. Then run procedural conformance on the documented projection, DECLARE on the full case, and skeleton conformance on its six-label projection. Run the full-trace state, resource, and selected policy checks, followed by the temporal profile on explicitly matched pairs. Report hard failures, pending obligations, data-quality indeterminacy, and performance warnings separately; do not convert an unavailable check into a pass.

Show users the violated rule, triggering events and times, evidence and missing attributes, layer, and policy version. Surface the R5/R6 conflict as a policy decision with both alternative outcomes where relevant, rather than silently merging them or suppressing a contradictory finding. Route policy disagreements and missing authorization evidence to the responsible owners for resolution.
