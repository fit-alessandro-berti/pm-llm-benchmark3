# 1. Mechanism map

The most plausible gain is better access to technical capacity in East. Its specialist is 82% utilized and its 90th-percentile cycle time is 22.8 days, compared with 71% and 16.4 days in North and 68% and 18.2 days in South. The main risk is that a local consultation becomes a formal referral followed by two waits, one for advice and one for someone to pick it up. The post-change figures are management estimates. All edge frequencies below are transfers per 100 cases, so a case can contribute several transfers.

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

- **Tail delay and unfinished cases.** Faster simple closures can lower the mean among closed files while the oldest open files continue to age. Plot p90/p95, the open-age distribution and the fraction still unresolved at each case age.
- **Compounding waits for high complexity.** One case can need technical advice, a licensed legal/risk decision, shared approval and a difficult quality check. Inspect total queue time and repeat queue visits on that complete route, including waits at its last step.
- **Loss of local knowledge.** A standard decision can be fast and still overlook a regional convention. Check quality failures, customer callbacks and audited accuracy by expertise flag and region, particularly in North and South.
- **Unequal access.** An East-wide gain can mask deterioration for a small language group within East. Compare matched or standardized wait, reassignment, accuracy and SLA rates as well as raw gaps. Customer segment alone may not identify accommodation need.
- **Learning loss.** Central experts can resolve today's cases quickly while local staff get less practice. Track co-review exposure now and independent decision accuracy over subsequent rotations.
- **Accountability gaps.** A review marked complete can leave advice unclaimed for days. Match each completed review to an acknowledgment, an owner and a next action, and age unmatched items.
- **Exception handling.** Security work without a qualified assignee, ambiguous routing or a disputed approval may be rare enough to have little effect on the mean. Inspect exception variants, time to escalation and eventual resolution.
- **Fragile capacity.** Good performance with the full roster can disappear when one reviewer is absent. Measure the impact across all three teams and how long the backlog takes to return to its pre-absence level.

# 5. Pilot design

1. **Establish a baseline.** Use 12 weeks of pre-change arrivals for all three teams. Verify case boundaries, routing definitions, SLA clocks and comparable activity labels. Record queue entry, service start/finish, advice ready/accepted, named owner, decision signer, rostered qualified hours and absences. Capture the actual value band for approval and accommodation need where appropriate. These details cannot all be recovered from the supplied columns.
2. **Run a concurrent comparison.** Keep simple intake and eligibility local. Within each originating team, randomly assign eligible arrivals to pooled or existing local routing before the routing decision, balancing assignments on complexity, specialty and priority. Follow the assigned case through advice, approval, quality check and closure, even when someone overrides its route. Include high-complexity and specialty cases, and retain simple local cases as a reference for spillover. Keep the existing supervisor available for rescue while testing the proposed reduced approval roster. Record each rescue.
3. **Protect comparison capacity.** Roster qualified hours for each arm in proportion to expected workload, and log cross-cover and shared legal/risk demand. Define precedence and ownership for cases meeting both technical and specialty rules. Start with a limited share in each team, then increase it in predeclared waves if guardrails hold. A split-roster test may understate full pooling benefits or hub congestion. Test a higher-load wave and an absence-cover exercise before extrapolating.
4. **Observe and compare.** Run the pilot for 12 weeks, then follow each intake cohort through its applicable SLA and a common reopening window, for example 30 days after closure. Review open ages throughout. Compare assigned arms within team and report changes from each team's baseline. Stratify by team, complexity, specialty and customer segment, adjust for priority, calendar and case mix, and report uncertainty. Extend observation for sparse specialties and segments. Check whether queue staffing, automation or demand changed at the same time.
5. **Listen to the people doing the work.** Each week ask local caseworkers, technical staff and approvers who owned their last stalled case, what informal advice was lost, and whether they received feedback. Walk a sample of slow, returned and apparently successful files with their owners. Pair customer feedback on repeated explanations and accommodations with a blinded audit of decisions in both arms.
6. **Precommit to action.** Illustrative pilot guardrails, to calibrate against baseline variation before launch, are a greater than 20% rise in high-complexity p90 cycle time, a greater than 2 percentage-point rise in SLA misses or verified rework, or a comparable widening of a team or segment SLA gap versus control. Pause expansion for persistent breaches across two adequately sized reviews. Act sooner if arrivals exceed completions and oldest queue age grows for two staffed weeks. Restore a local duty specialist or approval cover according to where the delay occurs. Immediately reroute a case with a critical decision error or no qualified reviewer, and suspend the affected route until reviewed. For sparse groups, review overdue cases individually and gather more evidence before expansion.

# 6. Recommendation

**Pilot a modified design before deciding on rollout.** Keep a named local case owner through closure, require acknowledgment of returned advice, reserve qualified capacity for legal/risk/security and difficult quality checks, and preserve local coaching through co-review. Test reduction of supervisor capacity reversibly. East has the clearest opportunity for relief. North and South provide a direct test of what is lost when embedded advice moves away.

Expand in waves if access improves where constrained, tail times and quality meet the pilot guardrails in each team, ownership works during exceptions, and accommodation groups and local skill development hold up under representative load. If East benefits and North or South repeatedly breaches a guardrail, retain local coverage there and test the adjusted model. An early lower mean, fewer cross-team edges or higher occupancy cannot establish fewer true handovers, sufficient spare capacity, equal treatment or sustained learning. Those judgments require mature outcomes, open-case ages, subgroup results and evidence from absence cover.

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

The following Python-style pseudocode uses one row per performed activity for network and variant discovery. Queue milestones are kept separately for waiting-time analysis. The named helpers describe local data preparation or calculations, and the pm4py calls identify the discovery steps.

~~~python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

C, A, T, R = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp", "org:resource"
dims = ["origin_team", "case_complexity", "required_specialty", "customer_segment"]

# Use comparable time zones and actual event sequence to resolve equal timestamps.
# Flag ties of unknown order and test their effect on directional edges.
raw = ordered_events(events, case=C, timestamp=T, tie_breaker=source_sequence_if_known)
work = one_row_per_performed_activity(raw)  # keep true performer, exclude queue IDs as actors
work["original_activity"] = work[A]
work[A] = map_to_common_activity(work[A])  # e.g. local/shared approval -> Approval
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(work, case_id=C, activity_key=A, timestamp_key=T)
# Keep original_activity, team and routing fields for detailed queue/route analysis.

# One row per arriving case, indexed by C. Take origin from the first local
# intake/owning team. Freeze strata as known at intake and retain unknowns.
# Join pilot assignment, contractual due time (including pauses) and final
# closure/outcome as observed by the extract date. Retain open arrivals.
cases = case_registry(raw, intake_assignments, sla_rules, as_of)
cases["cohort"] = label_by_intake_date_and_assignment(cases, windows)
# Use pre, pilot_control and pilot_shared. Flag transition or mixed-policy
# cases separately. Never assign period from the date of the last event.

def compare_slice(period, filters, horizon):
    k = cases.loc[cases["cohort"].eq(period)].copy()
    for field, value in filters.items():
        k = k.loc[k[field].eq(value)]          # origin/attributes fixed at case level
    x = log.loc[log[C].isin(k.index)].copy()  # preserve other teams' events on each case
    if k.empty:
        return {"n": 0}

    # f has complete intake-to-close traces; exclude left-truncated cases.
    # For process comparisons use the same follow-up horizon in each arm.
    # Report completion coverage; keep unresolved cases in the outcome analysis.
    f = complete_traces_within_followup(x, k, horizon)
    out = {"n": len(k), "n_complete_traces": f[C].nunique()}
    if not f.empty:
        out["how"] = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(f)
        out["together"] = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(f)
        out["subcontract"] = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(f)
        out["similarity"] = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(f)
        out["roles"] = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(f)
        out["variants"] = pm4py.get_variants(f)
        out["dfg"] = pm4py.discover_dfg(f)
        out["performance_dfg"] = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(f)
        out["repeat_activity_cases"] = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(f)

        # Count adjacent changes on ordered events inside each case.
        g = f.groupby(C, sort=False)
        edges = f.assign(src=g[R].shift(), src_team=g["team"].shift())
        changed = edges.loc[edges["src"].notna() & edges[R].notna()
                            & edges["src"].ne(edges[R])]
        out["edges_per_100_complete"] = (
            100 * changed.groupby(["src", R]).size() / f[C].nunique()
        )
        out["case_share_per_edge"] = changed.groupby(["src", R])[C].nunique() / f[C].nunique()
        # Repeat with stable team/role mappings and mark bot edges. Match referral
        # and advice IDs to distinguish same-owner returns from team-only returns.
        out["handover_detail"] = classify_transfers(changed, referrals, owner_history)
        out["edge_p50_p90"] = adjacent_elapsed_quantiles(f, by_case=C)

    # All arrivals, including incomplete cases. Compare intake cohorts at a
    # common age; censor time-to-close at the last observation if still open.
    out["cycle_survival_p50_p90_p95"] = time_to_close_with_censoring(k, as_of, horizon)
    due = k.loc[k["due"].notna() & k["due"].le(as_of)]
    missed = due["close"].isna() | due["close"].gt(due["due"])
    out["sla"] = (missed.sum(), len(due))      # divide only for nonempty denominators
    out["open_age"] = as_of - k.loc[k["close"].isna(), "intake"]
    m = cases_with_equal_rework_followup(k, as_of, horizon)
    y = x.loc[x[C].isin(m.index)]
    flags = y.loc[is_true(y["rework_flag"]) | y[A].eq("Rework"), C]
    out["verified_rework"] = (flags.nunique(), len(m))  # validate flag completeness
    out["outcomes"] = audited_outcome_rates(m, y)
    return out

slices = [{}] + observed_slices(cases, dims, include_intersections=True)
for s in slices:
    for period in ("pre", "pilot_control", "pilot_shared"):
        report(s, period, compare_slice(period, s, common_followup))

# Use original timestamps and matched work-item visits for time decomposition.
# A second referral in the same case needs its own visit ID.
if valid_service_intervals_available:
    timed = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(interval_log, start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")
    by_activity = pm4py.get_service_time(interval_log, start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")
    compare_case_and_activity_times(timed, by_activity, cases)
if queue_milestones_available:
    visits = match_queue_visits(queue_milestones, cases, as_of)
    visits["wait"] = visits["start"] - visits["entered"]
    visits["pickup"] = visits["acknowledged"] - visits["advice_ready"]
    visits["open_wait_age"] = (as_of - visits["entered"]).where(visits["start"].isna())
    report_queue_distributions(visits, by=["cohort", "queue", "priority"] + dims)
~~~

Read the networks together. Handover of work counts sequential transfers, working together measures resources appearing on the same case, and subcontracting highlights return patterns such as caseworker → reviewer → same caseworker. Compare frequencies per case and shares under consistent network settings. Inspect variants to distinguish a planned advice return from an extra clarification loop. Resource similarity and discovered roles describe observed activity mixes. Pair them with qualification records, owner histories and staff reports before interpreting changes as skill gain, loss or role clarity.

With only the supplied timestamp, a performance DFG can measure elapsed time between logged activities. That interval may include queueing, service, off-shift time and unlogged work. Report it as elapsed time until queue milestones or validated service intervals are available. For parallel work, account for overlapping intervals when estimating case service and qualified busy hours. Count planned repeated activities separately from verified rework, and use actual SLA rules and deadlines to classify misses.
