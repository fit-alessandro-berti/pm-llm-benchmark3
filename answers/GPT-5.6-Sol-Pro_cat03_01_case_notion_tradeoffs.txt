## 1. Analytical assumptions

A **case notion** is a projection rule: choose an object type, make each object of that type a case, retain only events directly E2O-related to that object, and order them by timestamp. It is a viewpoint, not a claim that the selected object is the unique process instance. The baseline analysis does not infer E2O links from O2O relations.

I use **convergence** for a shared event or downstream object joining several objects of the selected case type. It can cause copy-convergence, such as e16 being copied into O1, O2, and O3, or aggregation-convergence, such as D2 becoming one delivery case that silently contains three orders. **Divergence** is the converse fan-out: one selected case object branches into several secondary lifecycles whose events are interleaved into one linear trace, as O1 branches to P1 and P2.

Copied events are **both evidence and duplication**. They correctly show that several objects were affected by one operation, but they are duplicate observations for physical workload, event volume, and independence assumptions. I therefore retain event identifiers and distinguish physical events, unique affected objects, and event-object associations.

Permitted transformations are explicit, reversible sensitivity variants: one-hop, qualifier-aware O2O propagation with a provenance flag; inverse-cardinality weights of `1/k` for an event copied to `k` cases; and contextual enrichment that does not relabel propagated events as native lifecycle events. I would not apply unrestricted transitive propagation or overwrite the direct-E2O baseline.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

1. **Order flattening—O1’s split becomes serial repetition.** O1 contains e11/e12, e15/e16, and e23/e24, producing `Pack Package→Pack Package`, `Dispatch→Dispatch`, and `Confirm Delivery→Confirm Delivery`. These are not necessarily rework; they are P1/D1 and P2/D2 branches forced into timestamp order. This is harmful for a single-thread control-flow interpretation but informative about order-level multiplicity.

2. **Order flattening—cross-order P3 creates context-dependent edges.** The same physical pack event e13 is copied to O2 and O3. In O2 it is followed by e16, contributing `Pack Package→Dispatch`; in O3 it is followed by e14, contributing `Pack Package→Pack Package`. One event therefore acquires different local successors in different copied cases. That is harmful for work-frequency attribution but valuable evidence that P3 couples O2 and O3.

3. **Order and Item flattening—cross-order P4 looks like independent executions.** Events e14, e17, and e27 are copied across O3/O4 and across I32/I41. The Item log counts two identical Add→Pack→Dispatch→Confirm variants even though both use the same P4, D3, and event IDs. Treating those traces as independent samples is harmful; counting two affected items or orders is legitimate.

4. **Package flattening—D2 inflates a physical logistics edge.** P2 and P3 both receive e16 and e24, so `Dispatch→Confirm Delivery` occurs four times across package cases although only D1, D2, and D3 execute that pair. D2’s 4 h 55 m transit is entered twice. This is harmful for carrier workload and event-level averages, but appropriate for package-level service exposure.

5. **Order flattening—D2 is tripled.** Dispatch e16 and confirmation e24 are copied into O1, O2, and O3. A row-count dashboard reports three dispatches and three confirmations for one delivery operation, and a performance analysis repeats the same D2 interval three times. This is harmful for physical volume and statistical independence, but informative when the denominator is orders affected.

6. **Order flattening—consolidated V2 doubles finance activity.** e19, e22, and e25 are each copied to O2 and O3. The Order log consequently contains four Issue Invoice occurrences for three invoice events and six Record Payment occurrences for four payment events. V2’s issue-to-final-settlement duration is also counted twice. This is harmful for accounting workload, but valid for identifying two orders exposed to V2’s settlement timing.

7. **Invoice flattening—partial settlement resembles payment rework.** V2 has `Issue Invoice→Record Payment→Record Payment`, yielding one `Record Payment→Record Payment` edge. Without the O2O qualifiers, the edge cannot distinguish M2 `partially_settles` from M3 `completes_settlement`; it may be mislabeled as a duplicate or correction. The repetition is informative only when those qualifiers remain available.

8. **Order flattening—parallel finance and logistics becomes a loop-like sequence.** O2 and O3 contain e22 → e24 → e25, hence `Record Payment→Confirm Delivery→Record Payment`. This does not show that payment processing returned to delivery; it shows a first V2 instalment before D2 confirmation and completion afterward. It is harmful as a monolithic normative control flow, but informative for cross-lifecycle timing.

9. **Payment flattening—near-singleton lifecycles create vacuous regularity.** M1, M2, M3, and M4 each contain only one Record Payment event. The DFG is empty, all durations are zero, and every case has the same one-activity variant. This is harmful for discovery or conformance claims; it is useful only for counting and timestamping transactions.

10. **Item and Delivery flattening—variant uniformity hides structural and temporal heterogeneity.** All six Item traces share one variant, and all three Delivery traces share Dispatch→Confirm. Yet I11 travels via P1/D1, I12/I21/I31 via consolidated D2, and I32/I41 via P4/D3; delivery duration ranges from 3 h to 22 h 45 m. A strict log skeleton may describe the sequence correctly while concealing the materially different coupling and performance contexts.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

I would create Order, Item, Package, Delivery, and Invoice projections, always retaining event IDs and shared-event cardinality. Order safely answers order-impact questions and Create-to-Close timing; it does not safely answer physical activity volume. Item answers unit-level fulfilment time. Package answers pack-to-confirm service per package. Delivery answers transit time per shipment. Invoice answers time to first and final payment and instalment incidence. Payment should be retained as a transaction table, not treated as a meaningful discovered process.

The object-centric portfolio carries the structural questions. An **OC-DFG** should compare each object type without losing the shared event identities. The **OTG** should expose Order–Item–Package–Delivery and Order–Invoice–Payment coupling. The **ET–OT graph** should verify which activities are directly attached to which types rather than assuming propagation. The **object interaction summary** should surface high-degree events such as e16/e24, each related to nine objects. Order-centered **equivalent-execution clustering** can contrast O1’s split, O2’s P3/V2 sharing, O3’s two cross-order packages, and O4’s P4 path, while recognizing that their neighborhoods overlap.

Contradictions are reconciled by naming the denominator. Dispatch has three physical events, three Delivery objects, four Package objects, six Item objects, four unique Order objects, and six Order-event associations. These are different valid quantities, not competing estimates of one quantity. Every result should therefore be reported as physical-event count, unique-object impact, and total object-event associations; performance should similarly separate delivery-level duration from package-, item-, and order-impact-weighted duration.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Position B — Use a primary lead object plus explicitly secondary lifecycles.**

**Decision criteria.** I prioritize endpoint coverage, customer/accountability meaning, direct rather than propagated event coverage, exposure to convergence/divergence, and whether shared work can be interpreted without pretending it is independently owned by each case.

**Strongest supporting evidence.** Order is the only type with explicit start and end events and direct coverage of every event e01–e31. It therefore supports customer-facing Create-to-Close outcomes and keeps fulfilment, delivery, invoicing, payment, and closure in one accountability view. None of Item, Package, Delivery, Invoice, or Payment spans that scope.

**Strongest counterargument.** Order is also the most distorted projection: 42 rows represent 31 event IDs. e16/e24 connect O1–O3, while e14/e17/e27 connect O3–O4, so all four orders belong to one interaction network rather than four independent executions. O1 and O3 also contain genuine branch interleavings. This makes a pure Order model especially unsafe for workload, bottleneck, and causal-performance claims.

**Residual risk.** Analysts may silently count shared D2 or V2 activity several times, interpret branch repetitions as rework, allocate full shared delay or cost to every order, and overfit four distinct order variants. Secondary Package, Delivery, Invoice, and Payment lifecycles must therefore remain first-class, not decorative drill-downs.

**Change conditions.** I would move to position A only if governance is exclusively order-SLA based and shared operations have defensible allocation keys. I would move to position C if cross-order packaging, delivery, or billing becomes pervasive enough that order neighborhoods routinely overlap at scale, or if the main decision concerns physical capacity, carrier performance, or cash application rather than customer-order outcomes.

## 6. PM4Py analysis outline

The block uses PM4Py’s current top-level aliases: flattening returns a DataFrame, `discover_dfg` returns DFG/start/end dictionaries, and the OC-DFG, OTG, ET–OT, interaction-summary, and equivalent-execution functions are exported at top level. ([GitHub][1])

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

OBJECT_TYPES = ["Order", "Item", "Package", "Delivery", "Invoice", "Payment"]

# Baseline OCEL inspection: no O2O-to-E2O propagation is performed here.
print(ocel)
print("PM4Py version:", pm4py.__version__)

activity_frequency = ocel.events[ocel.event_activity].value_counts(dropna=False)
object_inventory_frequency = ocel.objects[ocel.object_type_column].value_counts(
    dropna=False
)
e2o_association_frequency = ocel.relations[
    ocel.object_type_column
].value_counts(dropna=False)
object_type_activities = pm4py.ocel_object_type_activities(ocel)

print("\nActivity frequency (physical events):\n", activity_frequency)
print("\nObject inventory frequency:\n", object_inventory_frequency)
print("\nE2O association frequency by object type:\n", e2o_association_frequency)
print("\nActivities directly related to each object type:\n", object_type_activities)

available_types = set(pm4py.ocel_get_object_types(ocel))
missing_types = set(OBJECT_TYPES) - available_types
if missing_types:
    raise ValueError(
        f"OCEL is missing expected object types: {sorted(missing_types)}"
    )

flattened_logs = {}
frequency_dfgs = {}
start_activities = {}
end_activities = {}
log_skeletons = {}
flattening_diagnostics_rows = []

for object_type in OBJECT_TYPES:
    flat_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type)
    flattened_logs[object_type] = flat_log

    dfg, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(flat_log)
    frequency_dfgs[object_type] = dfg
    start_activities[object_type] = starts
    end_activities[object_type] = ends

    log_skeletons[object_type] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(
        flat_log,
        noise_threshold=0.0,
    )

    event_case_cardinality = flat_log.groupby(ocel.event_id_column)[
        "case:concept:name"
    ].nunique()

    relation_rows = len(flat_log)
    unique_event_ids = flat_log[ocel.event_id_column].nunique()

    flattening_diagnostics_rows.append(
        {
            "object_type": object_type,
            "cases": flat_log["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
            "case_event_rows": relation_rows,
            "unique_event_ids": unique_event_ids,
            "extra_copied_rows": relation_rows - unique_event_ids,
            "expansion_factor": relation_rows / unique_event_ids,
            "shared_event_ids": event_case_cardinality[
                event_case_cardinality > 1
            ].to_dict(),
        }
    )

flattening_diagnostics = pd.DataFrame(
    flattening_diagnostics_rows
).set_index("object_type")

print("\nFlattening diagnostics:\n", flattening_diagnostics)
print("\nOrder DFG:\n", frequency_dfgs["Order"])
print(
    "\nPayment DFG (expected to be empty for singleton traces):\n",
    frequency_dfgs["Payment"],
)

# Object-centric structures retain event identities and multiple denominators.
object_centric_dfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
object_type_graph = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
event_type_object_type_graph = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)
object_interactions_summary = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)

ocdfg_activity_frequency_global = {
    metric: {
        activity: len(evidence)
        for activity, evidence in object_centric_dfg[
            "activities_indep"
        ][metric].items()
    }
    for metric in ("events", "unique_objects", "total_objects")
}

ocdfg_activity_frequency_by_type = {
    metric: {
        object_type: {
            activity: len(evidence)
            for activity, evidence in activity_map.items()
        }
        for object_type, activity_map in object_centric_dfg[
            "activities_ot"
        ][metric].items()
    }
    for metric in ("events", "unique_objects", "total_objects")
}

ocdfg_edge_frequency = {
    metric: {
        object_type: {
            edge: len(evidence)
            for edge, evidence in edge_map.items()
        }
        for object_type, edge_map in object_centric_dfg[
            "edges"
        ][metric].items()
    }
    for metric in ("event_couples", "unique_objects", "total_objects")
}

interaction_type_frequency = (
    object_interactions_summary
    .groupby(
        [
            ocel.object_type_column,
            f"{ocel.object_type_column}_2",
        ]
    )
    .size()
    .sort_values(ascending=False)
)

event_object_cardinality = (
    ocel.relations
    .groupby(ocel.event_id_column)[ocel.object_id_column]
    .nunique()
    .rename("related_object_count")
    .to_frame()
    .join(
        ocel.events.set_index(ocel.event_id_column)[
            [ocel.event_activity]
        ],
        how="left",
    )
    .sort_values("related_object_count", ascending=False)
)

# Justified by Position B: cluster only around the primary Order viewpoint.
order_equivalent_execution_clusters = pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(
    ocel,
    object_type="Order",
)

order_cluster_sizes = {
    repr(signature): len(executions)
    for signature, executions
    in order_equivalent_execution_clusters.items()
}

analysis_bundle = {
    "flattened_logs": flattened_logs,
    "frequency_dfgs": frequency_dfgs,
    "start_activities": start_activities,
    "end_activities": end_activities,
    "log_skeletons": log_skeletons,
    "flattening_diagnostics": flattening_diagnostics,
    "ocdfg": object_centric_dfg,
    "ocdfg_activity_frequency_global": ocdfg_activity_frequency_global,
    "ocdfg_activity_frequency_by_type": ocdfg_activity_frequency_by_type,
    "ocdfg_edge_frequency": ocdfg_edge_frequency,
    "otg": object_type_graph,
    "etot": event_type_object_type_graph,
    "object_interactions_summary": object_interactions_summary,
    "interaction_type_frequency": interaction_type_frequency,
    "event_object_cardinality": event_object_cardinality,
    "order_equivalent_execution_clusters": (
        order_equivalent_execution_clusters
    ),
    "order_cluster_sizes": order_cluster_sizes,
}

# These structures support denominator-aware semantic comparison.
# Visual inspection alone does not determine the right case notion.
print("\nHighest-degree events:\n", event_object_cardinality.head(10))
print(
    "\nOrder equivalent-execution cluster sizes:\n",
    order_cluster_sizes,
)
```

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

1. **Copied-event treatment.** Recompute counts under three denominators: raw case-event associations; one physical count per event ID; and inverse-cardinality weighting. In the Order view, e16/e24 receive weight 1/3, while e13/e14/e17/e19/e22/e25/e27 receive 1/2. For path analysis, use OC-DFG event-couple evidence rather than arbitrarily assigning a shared event to one case. A conclusion that exists only under raw row counts is duplication-sensitive.

2. **Direct versus propagated relations.** Keep the supplied direct-E2O OCEL as baseline, then build a separate provenance-labelled, one-hop variant. Examples are propagating Add Item to its Package through `packs`, Pack Package to its Delivery through `carries`, and Issue Invoice to M1–M4 through the settlement relations. Compare trace lengths, DFGs, and duplication factors. Propagated events are contextual evidence, not automatically native lifecycle events.

3. **Lead-object switch.** Repeat outcome rankings with Order, Item, Package, Delivery, and Invoice. Order durations are almost identical at about 26 hours, whereas Delivery durations are 3 h, 4 h 55 m, and 22 h 45 m. If a claimed bottleneck or variability result disappears when the denominator changes, report it as viewpoint-dependent rather than globally true.

4. **OC-DFG frequency semantics.** Compare `events`, `unique_objects`, and `total_objects` for both activities and edges. For Dispatch, the snapshot has three event IDs, four unique Orders, and six Order-event associations; Record Payment has four event IDs, four unique Orders, and six Order-event associations. Large gaps identify consolidation or repeated participation, not data inconsistency.

5. **Consolidation leave-out and performance weighting.** Recompute models after excluding D2’s e16/e24, then after excluding V2’s e19/e22/e25. Also compare delivery-level durations with package-, item-, and order-impact-weighted durations. This reveals whether a few high-degree shared objects dominate the DFG, average performance, or cluster structure. With only four orders, treat all such comparisons as descriptive robustness checks rather than population-level inference.
