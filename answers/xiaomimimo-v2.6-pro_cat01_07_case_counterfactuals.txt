**Factual mechanism:** Rule 7 turns each unresolved discrepancy into a full loop: `RMI  DC  AS`. The second loop consumed 70.5 h of external waiting (Fr 10:30  Mo 09:00, spanning a weekend), 3.5 h of D1, 2.5 h of A1 and one more compliance-relevant milestone — to confirm one inconsistency that ultimately proved benign.

**Minimal intervention:** A **path/policy** intervention: after `AS2` (Fr 10:30) the action `RMI` is removed from the admissible action set, and the decision must be `approve-with-conditions` or `reject` with the discrepancy recorded. Everything up to Fr 10:30 is bit-identical to the factual run (minimal change preserved). Mandatory constraints are preserved where they still apply: `CR` must follow the **latest** `AS` (= `AS2`) and precede `DEC`; four-eyes holds (A1 assessed, C1 reviewed); no `DC3/AS3` exists to be preceded.

**Temporal propagation:** The case becomes *decision-eligible* (subject to CR) at Fr 10:30 instead of Tu 10:30 the following week — a 4-day readiness shift created purely by deleting an external wait. `CR` then runs on evidence with one open item, which is harder than the factual review: 3.5-5.5 h vs 4 h. Whether the saving is 1 day or 5.6 days depends on two calendar gates: whether C1 has a Friday block (backlog has been clearing since We) and whether M1 signs before ~19:00. Friday CR 12:00-17:00  M1 Fr 17:30-18:00  saving  135 h. Monday CR 08:30-13:00  M1 Mo 14:00  saving  43 h. A missed Monday block  Tu 08:30 decision  saving  24 h. Note the *logged* saving overstates the *effective* saving whenever the conditional decision is later amended or appealed (5-15%), which silently re-imports a rework loop outside this trace.

**Process-feasibility check:** Under the amended policy the path `AS2  CR  DEC  NOT` satisfies rules 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 9. Rule 7 is not triggered (no `RMI` occurs). The one genuine compliance argument is rule 5's intent: the review must be defensible, and a documented open item with a binding condition is defensible but weaker than the factual "evidence accepted". Feasible, but this is a decision-policy change, not a schedule change.

**Best/base/worst case:** *Best* 130 to 135 h (Friday CR and Friday signature, approve-with-conditions). *Base* 43 to 60 h (Monday signature, approve-with-conditions). *Worst* 15 to 25 h (C1 unavailable Monday, review needs an S1 consult and a 5.5 h read, Tuesday signature) — or effectively 0 if the decision is contested and reopened.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** (i) if the third confirmation had been *material* (i.e., would have changed the decision), the "benign discrepancy" premise is refuted and expected loss dominates the time saving; (ii) if amendment/appeal rates after conditional approvals exceed ~20%, the logged cycle-time saving is an artefact; (iii) if C1 refuses to sign with an open item in most cases, the counterfactual path is infeasible and the case must loop anyway.

### K-411

**Factual mechanism:** Purely technical serialisation. Rule 3 permits `IC || DC` (no originals), but the legacy case lock creates only the identity task at Triage; the document task exists only at Tu 13:20. This costs 1.7 h of extra D2 queue wait (start 15:00 vs ~13:20) and delays the *verification join* to 19:30, after which the case waits for the assessment and manager windows.

**Minimal intervention:** Instantiate both tasks at Triage (Tu 09:35) with a controlled ownership workaround and event-time timestamps. No activity is added or removed, no resource is substituted, and the partial order is unchanged — only the *instantiation time* of `DC` and the logging semantics change.

**Temporal propagation:** `IC` is unchanged (I2's queue gates it: 11:30-13:20). `DC` now queues from 09:35 rather than 13:20; against D2's 4 h forecast queue it starts  12:00-12:30 and, being digital/standard, runs 4-4.5 h  ends 16:15-17:00 instead of 19:30 (2.5 to 3.2 h). The join therefore occurs  16:30-17:00. That is the **only certain gain**. Transmission downstream is partial: the case sits 2-3 h higher in A2's ordering, so Assessment may start Tu 18:00-20:00 or only We 08:00-09:00 (vs We 09:30 factually). The residual gain is then quantised by M1's window: an Assessment ending before ~18:30 gets a same-day signature (large jump, up to 18 h), otherwise the case waits for the We morning slot (small gain). This is exactly why "earlier readiness" is not "earlier completion".

**Process-feasibility check:** Rule 3 explicitly sanctions the concurrency; rules 1, 2, 8 preserved; rule 11 preserved (I2  D2); no CR needed (low risk/value) so rule 5/9 are vacuous. The only compliance-relevant risk is evidential: timestamps must be event-time, otherwise the log would show `IC || DC` in cases where a single person actually worked serially — a rule-11 audit defect.

**Best/base/worst case:** *Best* 15 to 18 h (Assessment Tu 17:30-20:00, M1 signs within its window or first thing We 08:00  Notify  We 08:45). *Base* 3 to 6 h (Assessment We 08:00-10:30  M1 11:00  Notify 11:10). *Worst*  0 h, or +2 to +8 h if timestamp defects force task re-entry.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** (i) if assessment scheduling is by fixed daily appointment rather than queue order, the readiness gain is not transmitted at all (  0) and only the logging risk remains; (ii) if the workaround yields batch-entered or unverifiable timestamps in > 5% of tasks, the intervention fails an auditability test; (iii) if the legacy merge of two concurrently owned tasks causes conflicts in > 10% of cases, reprocessing offsets the saving.

**100-case weekly rollout.** At 100 cases per week the interventions stop being independent, because three of them push demand toward single-server pools. *Reserving scarce compliance capacity* (K-104) is the clearest failure mode: C1 has one working day of capacity and 3-6 h per review, so 100 reservations cannot be honoured. Reservations are placed against forecasts (rule 12) and expire unused, creating holes no other case may take, while non-reserving arrivals — precisely the urgent Monday cases in the snapshot — queue behind parked capacity. The compliance bottleneck does not migrate; it congests. *D1/D2 substitution* (K-219) changes the mix rather than capacity: D2 needs 30-70% more hours per file, so D2 becomes the document bottleneck while D1 (the only resource efficient on complex corporate files) either idles or absorbs all the hard work. *Additional versus avoided rework* dominates the tails: a 15-30% clarification rate yields 15-30 extra rule-7 loops per week, each adding a document check, an assessment and an 8-36 h external wait that crosses weekends — meanwhile K-337's suppressed loop *removes* roughly 3.5 h of D1, 2.5 h of A1 and 3.5 days of waiting per avoided loop, so its document and assessment queues genuinely shrink. *Assessment queues* are the residual bottleneck in every variant: K-411 and K-219 both deliver cases to A1/A2 earlier, sharpening the peak rather than adding hours. Finally, *timestamp behaviour versus true concurrency* matters for measurement: batch entry will manufacture apparent `IC || DC` parallelism in K-411, inflate measured throughput, break rule-11 overlap auditing and corrupt the very queue data needed to evaluate the rollout.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is **K-411's parallelized verification**. It relaxes no rule and touches no decision: it only removes a serialization manufactured by the legacy case lock, one that rule 3 already permits. Its gain is modest (typically 2-6 h, up to 18 h when it crosses a manager signing window) but it has one sign, essentially no quality downside, and it is trivially reversible. It is also the only intervention whose pilot produces infrastructure (event-time logging, queue-elasticity estimates) that every other study needs.

The most dangerous is **K-337's suppressed rework loop**, despite being the fastest by 1-5.5 days. It is fast precisely because it deletes the only activity that could resolve the discrepancy, converting a data problem into a decision problem: a conditional approval with an open item, weaker compliance defensibility and an appeal tail that never appears in the process log. The speed is borrowed from quality and the debt is discovered later.

**K-104 is the least transportable** from one case to a system policy. For a single case, reserving a C1 block is nearly free. For 100 cases it is rationing of a single-server resource: reservations are booked against unreliable forecasts, expire unused, and displace exactly the urgent cases the system cares about most. The same asymmetry weakly affects K-219 (D2 becomes the document bottleneck and rework tails multiply) and K-411 (the assessment queue absorbs the earlier arrivals).

The most valuable additional data are: true per-task creation/start/stop event times (to separate real from false concurrency); queue position and arrival time at each pool (to estimate elasticity rather than assume it); M1's signing-window calendar; clarification and rework rates by document type and resource; and post-decision outcomes (amendments, appeals, losses) so suppressed loops can be priced rather than hidden.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural` — The generic partial order (rules 1-8) and the four-eyes rule (rule 9) hold in every counterfactual except K-337, where the amendment is explicit and limited to the second `Request More Info`.
2. `temporal` — Snapshot queue figures are work-content forecasts (rule 12) with roughly ±30-50% error; overnight work exists (D1 Mo 21:40-Tu 05:40) but is not continuous, and external supplier responses pause over weekends (K-337's 70.5 h wait).
3. `resource` — M1 signs in discrete weekday windows (approximately 08:00-19:00, lag 0.5-2 h) and not at night or weekends; this window structure creates the ±12-18 h quantisation seen in K-219 and K-411.
4. `resource` — C1 is a single fully certified server with no cross-cover (so a reservation is a withdrawal from the shared queue), while S1 is advisory only and may legitimately overlap other checks.
5. `behavioral` — Preliminary compliance/escalation work on a packet that lacks document-check and assessment output yields only partial reuse (0.5-2 h of the claimed 1-3 h) and carries an anchoring risk for the final reviewer.
6. `quality` — In K-337 the disputed item ultimately proved benign (factual DC3 accepted the confirmation), so the modal counterfactual decision is approve-with-conditions rather than reject; defensibility is weaker but not absent if the open item is recorded.
7. `interference` — Reserved C1 blocks are honoured first-come and displace non-reserving arrivals (including the two urgent cases added Mo 16:00), and expired reservations are not instantly reusable by others at 100-case scale.
8. `behavioral` — D2 raises a clarification on difficult handwritten material in roughly 15-30% of comparable cases, and any such clarification triggers the full rule-7 loop (new Document Check + new Assessment) rather than a light verbal check.
