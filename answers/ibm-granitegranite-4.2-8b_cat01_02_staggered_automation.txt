**Why primary strategy is preferable:** The staggered event study with relative time directly addresses the core challenge of staggered adoption (different rollout months) and uses the only credible comparison group (never-treated Delta/Epsilon), avoiding the need for matching (which is fragile with only 2 never-treated branches) and avoiding reliance on a single trend estimate (ITS). It explicitly tests the parallel trends assumption via placebo plots and slope comparison, which is diagnostic for the main failure mode. It incorporates observed time-varying confounders (`fte`, routing flag, product-change) as covariates at the branch-month level, respecting that confounders change over time. It avoids naive two-way FE regression (which would ignore staggered timing and could confound pre-trend differences). Given the small number of branches (5) and only 8 months of data, the event study approach is transparent, requires minimal distributional assumptions, and clearly separates identification assumptions (parallel trends for never-treated) from estimation. ITS is less suitable because FTE changes (e.g., Alpha losing FTE in May) occur within the post-rollout period, risking confounding the "intervention" signal; matching is limited by only two never-treated branches and pre-rollout similarity may not hold perfectly. Thus, the primary strategy is the most defensible given data constraints and causal logic.

## 8. Rollout recommendation
`expand with an embedded evaluation`

**Rationale and concrete plan:**  
Delta (next target) is the most comparable never-treated branch: stable `median_cycle_h` (~38–42h), stable `p90_cycle_h` (~79–94h), stable `csat` (~4.0–4.1), and no recent FTE loss (fte ~22–23). Delta received consistent routing (no extra ~10% increase like Alpha/Beta), and its `high_complexity_pct` rose moderately (1823%) in May, matching the product change pattern. Pausing to redesign is unnecessary (automation logic clear; no major process flaw seen); expanding immediately risks deploying without monitoring early tail pressure (seen in Alpha/Beta May). Thus, embed a lightweight, causal-aware evaluation in Delta’s rollout.

- **Treatment assignment/phase-in:** Roll out automation to Delta starting in September 2026 (first full month without concurrent routing surge; routing campaign targets Alpha/Beta/Gamma, not Delta). Use full availability (`automation=1` from Sep 1), but monitor `automated_fast_path_pct` weekly. No phased intensity; let managers use naturally (track `automated_fast_path_pct`).
- **Minimum pre/post measurements:** Collect branch-month summaries for `median_cycle_h`, `p90_cycle_h`, `automated_fast_path_pct`, `escalation_pct`, `rework_pct`, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct`, and csat (monthly survey, record response rate if possible). Pre-period: Aug 2026 (1 month); post-period: Sep–Nov 2026 (3 months). Also record simple routing indicator (e.g., % of cases from central campaign if applicable to Delta; if none, note "no extra routing").
- **Process-mining outcomes & queue-state data to collect:** Add two low-effort metrics: (1) `specialist_review_wait_days` estimated as average time from `Automated Triage` to `Specialist Review` start (if staff logs available; otherwise, ask triage lead for typical wait <24h vs >48h; record as binary "within 24h" or ">24h" for rough signal), and (2) `borderline_cases_flag` (yes/no per case, recorded by triage staff at registration if borderline per automation rule; e.g., "needs specialist check"). *No PM4Py or event logs needed; just staff-reported flag at registration.* Collect these alongside existing aggregates. *Do not require fraud-desk wait times (unknown), but flag borderline cases helps infer local specialist load trend.*
- **Spillover monitoring:** Track Delta’s `escalation_pct` and `p90_cycle_h` monthly. Compare Delta’s `p90` trend to Delta’s own pre-rollout trend (Aug baseline). If Delta’s `p90` in Sep >10% above its Aug `p90` (e.g., Aug p90 ~88h  Sep p90 >98h), trigger a 1-week review: check `specialist_wait_days` (if >24h), `automated_fast_path_pct` usage, and staff feedback. Also compare Delta’s `automated_fast_path_pct` rise pattern to Alpha/Beta’s (slow vs fast); if Delta’s fast-path pct <20% by end of Sep (suggesting slower adoption), note but do not stop rollout (learning phase normal).
- **Guardrails for customer harm:** If csat in Delta drops >0.2 points from Aug average (e.g., Aug avg 4.05  Sep avg <3.85) *and* response rate 30%, pause automation for one month to investigate (e.g., check frontline feedback); otherwise continue. Guardrail is only for csat + sufficient response; avoids over-reacting to noise.
- **Decision rule for scale-up:** After 3 months (Sep–Nov), evaluate using the event-study pattern (no regression to claim):
  - If Delta’s median `cycle_h` in Oct–Nov avg  its Aug median (i.e., no net median increase), and Delta’s `p90` in Oct–Nov avg  its Aug p90 (i.e., tail not persistently worse), and no csat drop >0.1 point (with response 30%), then expand automation to Epsilon in December 2026 with same embedded evaluation (repeat measurements).
  - If Delta’s `p90` in Sep >10% above Aug p90 *and* `specialist_wait_days` >24h in Sep, pause rollout to Epsilon; instead, run a 2-week process tweak (e.g., adjust borderline flag rule with triage lead) before re-rollout.
  - If Delta’s median improves but `p90` worsens early (like Alpha May), continue rollout but add monthly `p90` check (spillover monitor); no scale-up to Epsilon until Delta’s `p90` recovers to Aug level by Nov.
  *This rule uses only observed branch-level summaries, no precise effect size; it is operational, causal-aware (checks pattern), and respects uncertainty.*

> *Why not "expand immediately"?* Delta has no recent FTE loss (fte stable ~22–23), but routing may increase slightly if campaign extends; without monitoring early tail (seen in Alpha/Beta May), risk repeating temporary p90 rise. Without evaluation, cannot confirm if benefit holds after learning.  
> *Why not "pause and redesign"?* Automation logic works (fast path used >25% in Alpha by Apr); no process flaw evident; Delta is ready (no pilot needed); pausing wastes opportunity to learn with minimal cost. Redesign (e.g., change rule) would need testing too, but embedded evaluation is lighter.  
> *Why not "do not expand"?* Evidence shows automation can reduce median cycle time later post (Jul–Aug for adopters), and Delta’s profile matches adopters post-complexity rise; no strong evidence of universal harm. Safe to test with evaluation.
