# 1. Assumptions and notation

- **A1 — Rounded timestamps.** All reported operational and arrival times are treated as nominal timestamps rounded to five minutes. A reported event is approximately within ±2.5 minutes of its actual time. A difference between two rounded event times, such as a completed service or waiting time, is therefore nominally reported below and can vary by about ±5 minutes. An elapsed time to the fixed 14:00 cutoff varies by about ±2.5 minutes because only its start is rounded.
- **A2 — Event ordering at ties.** For nominal queue reconstruction, simultaneous reported-time events are ordered as: finish/release service; transfer completed S1 cases into the S2 queue; register external S1 arrivals; then start eligible service. Thus a resource can finish one activity and start another at the same reported time. Actual order inside a rounded five-minute bin is uncertain; interval counts near 10:00 and 12:00 can move by one case.
- **A3 — Capacity and sequence.** S1 has I1 and I2 and S2 has C1 and C2, each single-task. Cases follow S1 then S2, and no case bypasses a station. Logged activities are treated as resource work, subject to the stated rounding and missingness. The 10:55–11:10 C2 maintenance removes 15 resource-minutes from S2 availability.
- **A4 — Missing-start bounds.** For the four completed activities marked MISSING, the nominal starts are constrained by queue entry, their known completion, and the prior activity on the same resource: P07/S1 is in [09:25, 10:00), P15/S1 in [11:40, 12:15), P05/S2 in [09:45, 10:15), and P11/S2 in [12:00, 12:25). These are feasible reported-time bounds; strict positivity makes the upper endpoint exclusive. The bounds use the previous same-resource release, which is later than queue entry in each case. The central estimates are interval midpoints: 09:42:30, 11:57:30, 10:00, and 12:12:30 respectively. These are estimates, not recovered observations.
- **A5 — Right censoring.** OPEN service is counted only through 14:00; its eventual duration is at least that elapsed time and has no observed upper bound. A case still waiting at 14:00 has an elapsed queue age that is a lower bound on its eventual wait. No final duration is imputed for either type of censoring.
- **A6 — WIP population.** WIP counts below cover the 22 supplied cases. For the integrated finite-window calculations, I assume there are no unlisted cases already in process at 08:00. If there were, the reported WIP would omit them; the prompt supplies no opening inventory with which to add them.
- **A7 — Imputation scenarios.** “Early/long” places each missing start at the earliest resource-feasible bound; “midpoint” uses A4; “late/short” places it one minute before the known completion so its service remains positive. These scenarios use nominal timestamps; rounding may widen all bounds.

Let (a_i) be external arrival to S1, (e_{1i}) S1 completion / S2 queue entry, (s_{1i}) and (s_{2i}) station starts, and (e_{2i}) S2 completion. Times and differences in the case table are in minutes unless shown as clock times.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

For station (k), with queue-entry time (q_{ki}), start (s_{ki}), and completion (e_{ki}):

- Waiting time is (W_{ki}=s_{ki}-q_{ki}). For S1, (q_{1i}=a_i); for S2, (q_{2i}=e_{1i}). For an unstarted case at 14:00, report (14{:}00-q_{ki}) as an elapsed, right-censored wait, not its eventual wait.
- Active service is (B_{ki}=e_{ki}-s_{ki}). For an OPEN activity, only (14{:}00-s_{ki}) is observed; this is elapsed service, a lower bound on final service. A missing start makes service an interval, not a point.
- Station sojourn is (T_{ki}=W_{ki}+B_{ki}=e_{ki}-q_{ki}) for completed work. It is anchored by observed queue entry and completion even if an internal start is missing. For OPEN/waiting cases it is right-censored at 14:00.
- Case total sojourn for a completed case is (T_i=e_{2i}-a_i), including both queues and both services. The 14 completed cases have nominal total sojourns listed in section 3.
- Queue length (Q_k(t)) counts entered cases not yet started. Active count (A_k(t)) counts started cases not yet completed; OPEN work remains active through the cutoff. Station WIP is (L_k(t)=Q_k(t)+A_k(t)). Total WIP is (L_1(t)+L_2(t)), equivalently listed arrivals not yet departed S2.
- Period arrival/departure rate is its event count divided by the period length. S1 arrivals are external arrivals; S2 arrivals are S1 completions. S1 departures are S1 completions; S2 departures are completed S2 cases. Periods below use nominal half-open intervals [08,10), [10,12), [12,14:00], with the stated tie convention.
- Busy time sums observed resource occupancy, including elapsed time for OPEN work and scenario service time for missing starts. S1 utilization divides by 2×360 resource-minutes. S2 utilization is shown against both 2×360 nominal minutes and 705 available resource-minutes after the C2 outage.

For a known completed duration (d), lower/central/upper interpretations are approximately (d-5,d,d+5) minutes (clipped at zero). For a MISSING start, the station-order feasibility interval gives the lower/upper scenario bounds and its midpoint the central scenario. For OPEN activity, elapsed service is reported separately and is never substituted for final service.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

**Observed evidence.** In 08:00–10:00, external S1 arrivals are about 6.5/h versus six observed S1 completions in two hours (3/h). S1 waiting for the P01–P13 arrival cohort averages roughly 45–48 minutes, and its station WIP reaches about seven. In the same period, S2 waiting for its first six entries averages about 13–18 minutes and S2 WIP peaks near four. This is strongest evidence for an early S1 intake/resource constraint, with queue buildup ahead of inspection.

In 10:00–12:00, S1 still receives 4/h and completes about 3.5/h, while the P14–P21 arrival cohort's S1 wait averages around 98–103 minutes. S2 receives seven cases and completes five in the period; its queue-entry cohort waits about 43–46 minutes. Both stations are under sustained load, with S1's older queue still large and S2's downstream queue becoming material.

In 12:00–14:00, only one new S1 arrival is recorded, while seven S1 cases complete. S1's queue is largely worked down. S2 nevertheless receives seven cases, completes six, and ends with four waiting plus two active. The three cases that have started S2 in this late-arrival cohort wait an average of about 68 minutes, and four more are right-censored in the queue. The late evidence points more strongly to S2 as the immediate constraint at the cutoff.

**Interpretation and uncertainty.** The defensible conclusion is a time-varying combination: S1 is the clearest early constraint under high incoming load; both stations contribute through the middle; by the end, accumulated S2 work and long S2 waits make S2 the downstream constraint. Nominal service durations are around 25–30 minutes at S1 and often 40–50 minutes at S2. With two resources each, those spans imply S1's nominal service capacity is generally higher than S2's, though neither utilization estimate alone proves a bottleneck.

C2 was unavailable from 10:55 to 11:10. Its P06 work ended at 10:50 and its next logged start, P08, is 11:15, so the outage overlaps a period when C2 could not start work. It removes 15 resource-minutes and plausibly delays a queued case's opportunity to start. The evidence does not identify exactly how much P08's 55-minute wait, or later waits, is attributable to maintenance: queue discipline, intended assignment times, and whether another resource could have served the work are not observed. The outage is a contributing capacity loss, not a complete explanation for the downstream queue.

            ev.extend([(a, 1), (b, -1)])
    # Group same-time events, equivalent to half-open occupancy intervals.
    e = pd.DataFrame(ev, columns=["time", "delta"]).groupby("time").delta.sum()
    n = best = 0
    for delta in e.sort_index():
        n += int(delta)
        best = max(best, n)
    return best

def snapshot_counts(stage, station, t):
    x = stage[(stage.station == station) & (stage.entry <= t) &
              (stage.occupancy_end >= t)]
    q = int(((x.start.isna()) | (x.start > t)).sum())
    active = int((x.start.notna() & (x.start <= t) &
                  ((x.occupancy_end > t) | (x.open & (x.occupancy_end == cutoff)))).sum())
    return q, active
