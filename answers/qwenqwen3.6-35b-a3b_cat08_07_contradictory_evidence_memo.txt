### 1. Executive Memo

**To:** Executive Leadership Team  
**From:** Process Excellence & Data Strategy  
**Subject:** Decision on Fiber-Installation Redesign R: Hybrid Deployment Recommended  

**Recommendation:**  
We recommend **Option E: Hybrid Deployment**. Scale coordinator ownership for simple, low-risk cases; immediately pause the predictive survey-skip rule and post-billing activation; and run targeted measurement to validate risk models and downstream costs.

**Rationale & Trade-offs:**  
Redesign R delivers significant efficiency for the 34% of cases involving simple houses, reducing median cycle time by 6 days and visible costs. However, the redesign degrades performance for the remaining 66% (apartments, old buildings, business customers). The predictive rule for skipping surveys misclassifies medium-risk addresses, leading to missed permits, rework, and a 25-day increase in p90 duration for old buildings. Post-billing activation, intended to accelerate billing, is causing billing disputes and shifting complaints from "waiting" to "service not working."

The process mining artifacts confirm these tensions. High fitness (0.94) with low precision (0.51) indicates the model captures the new coordinator path but allows excessive, uncontrolled variations (e.g., billing before activation, repeated visits). Coordinator ownership reduced handovers (34%  12%) but created a single point of failure; 93% utilization and centralized context sharing mean coordinator absence stalls cases, reducing organizational resilience.

**Decision Trade-off:**  
Scaling R nationally risks systemic tail-latency and brand damage from complaints, outweighing speed gains for simple cases. Rolling back forfeits proven efficiency for simple installs. A hybrid approach captures value where the model works, contains risk where it fails, and uses measurement to fix the predictive rule and cost visibility before wider adoption.

**Next Steps:**  
Deploy hybrid routing immediately. Pause survey skipping and post-billing activation. Initiate M6 (rule validation) and M4 (full cost measurement). If p90 duration and complaint rates return to baseline for apartments within 6 weeks, consider controlled expansion of the survey skip for validated medium-risk cases.

### 3. Explanation of Metric Conflicts

**High Fitness vs. Low Precision:**  
Fitness (0.94) indicates most pilot cases follow the new coordinator path, which is simpler for simple cases. Precision (0.51) is low because the redesign introduced flexible exceptions (skip survey, manual activation, post-billing) that the discovered model captures as permissible transitions. The model is overly permissive; frequent *move-on-log* events (e.g., Billing start before Activation test) show the model allows sequences that deviate from standard compliance or best practice, indicating the process is becoming less standardized despite higher fit to observed data.

**Improved Median vs. Worse Tail:**  
The median improvement is driven by the 34% of simple houses where merging validation/scheduling and skipping surveys drastically cuts time. The tail deterioration (p90/p95) is driven by complex cases. For apartments and old buildings, the survey skip rule often fails, leading to missed permits and second visits. Additionally, high coordinator utilization creates bottlenecks for these complex cases, increasing delays. The distribution has shifted left for the bulk but right for the tail.

**Fewer Handovers vs. Lower Resilience:**  
Handovers dropped because a single coordinator owns the case end-to-end. However, resilience dropped due to centralization. With 93% utilization, the coordinator is a bottleneck. The "working-together" network is centralized, meaning context is not shared across teams. When a coordinator is absent, cases stall because no one else has the context, unlike the baseline where handovers distributed knowledge and load.

**Lower Visible Cost vs. Higher Rework/Downstream Cost:**  
Visible cost dropped (EUR 184  161) because fewer field visits and surveys were performed for simple cases. However, rework indicators (second visits, manual activation) increased. The cost savings are front-loaded, while costs related to failed installations and complaints are pushed downstream. Since complaint handling data is incomplete, the true cost (EUR 207 estimate) is likely higher, and the "savings" may be illusory for complex cases.

**Simple vs. Complex Segment Divergence:**  
The redesign assumes a uniform benefit from simplification. Simple houses benefit from reduced steps. Apartments and old buildings suffer because the predictive rule fails (medium risk), permit checks are skipped for older buildings, and activation tests are delayed. The "one-size-fits-all" coordinator model cannot handle the variability of complex builds efficiently, leading to deterioration in quality and speed for these segments.

### 4. Decision Alternatives

**A. Scale Redesign R nationally**  
*Advantages:* Maximizes speed and cost savings for simple cases; reduces handovers nationally.  
*Risks:* Widespread tail latency, high complaint rates, billing disputes, coordinator burnout, compliance risks from skipped checks.  
*Conditions:* Only reasonable if the predictive rule is fixed, post-billing activation is removed, and coordinator capacity is increased to <85% utilization.

**B. Roll back to baseline**  
*Advantages:* Restores stability, quality, and compliance; eliminates tail latency and complaints.  
*Risks:* Loses EUR 23 visible cost savings per simple case; misses speed gains; ignores segment-specific benefits.  
*Conditions:* Justifiable if complaint-driven churn exceeds cost savings or if regulatory risk from skipped permits is unacceptable.

**C. Scale coordinator ownership, suspend survey skipping/post-billing**  
*Advantages:* Retains accountability and handover reduction; removes the two most damaging rules (skip rule, post-billing).  
*Risks:* Loses speed gains from merged tasks for simple cases; coordinator load may remain high without survey skip; manual activation may still spike.  
*Conditions:* Good middle ground if merged tasks can be optimized separately; requires monitoring manual activation rates.

**D. Run controlled regional experiment**  
*Advantages:* Low risk; generates better data on segment effects and cost.  
*Risks:* Delays benefits for simple cases; slow learning cycle; may not capture systemic coordination issues.  
*Conditions:* Appropriate if management prioritizes data certainty over immediate value capture.

**E. Hybrid: Deploy R for simple cases, pause risky rules, measure**  
*Advantages:* Captures value where it works; mitigates risk where it fails; enables targeted improvement of risk prediction; manages coordinator load by routing complexity elsewhere.  
*Risks:* Increased routing complexity; requires robust triage logic; needs active measurement.  
*Conditions:* Optimal balance of value and risk; allows scaling based on evidence.
