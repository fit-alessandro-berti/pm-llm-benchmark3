# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

G1–G2 inflate apparent concurrency, under-estimating service time and over-estimating utilization.  
G3 masks true service times by forcing identical completes, biasing conformance toward “batch” rules and under-estimating waiting time.  
G4 produces realistic per-case service times but risks double-counting resource occupancy if treated as concurrent.  
G5 shared-account artifact merges multiple workers into one resource trace, under-estimating utilization and hiding parallelism.  
G6 creates both inflated waiting (queue_arrival to artificial 17:00 start) and near-zero service (start = complete) because bulk submission collapses lifecycle; bottleneck detection spuriously flags the 17:00 boundary.  
G7 15 min rounding censors all durations, producing quantized service times and unreliable waiting-time distributions.  
G8 sequential pattern yields correct service times once clock offset is removed; otherwise service times appear artificially lengthened.

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

Treating resource IDs as reliable versus shared changes G5 from shared-account to concurrent work and weakens G2/G3 batch claims. Treating timestamps as exact versus interval-censored dissolves G7 and weakens G3/G6. Same pattern supports multiple explanations in G2 (start batch vs simultaneous), G3 (end batch vs coarsening), and G6 (bulk submission vs queue accumulation).

# 6. Data-collection redesign

Capture badge-level resource identity on every event; record both start and complete at 1 s granularity; synchronize all source clocks to a common NTP source; add explicit batch/group identifiers at the workstation; log workstation capacity and pause/resume events; store original unrounded timestamps alongside rounded ones.

# 7. Final adjudication

High-confidence: G1, G7. Exclude or interval-censor: G3, G6, G7. Require operational validation: G2, G5, G8 before any performance conclusion.
