## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The raw-to-mapped fitness gap (collapse from ≈0.925 to 0.55 in March while mapped fitness recovers to ≈0.90) shows that the dominant March deviation is a semantic/labeling and technical-event artifact of the system migration, not a wholesale restructuring of the intended control flow. Mapping “Eligibility Screen” back to “Validate Eligibility” and removing duplicated Open Case events restores conformance to the normative model, indicating that the core sequence Register → Validate → Collect Documents → Assess → Decision is still largely observed. Label mapping, however, recovers conformance statistics without proving the business process was unchanged: it only normalizes vocabulary and strips obvious technical duplicates. Concurrent subtle routing shifts or performance changes remain invisible to fitness alone.

Duplicated technical Open Case events can inflate case counts, insert spurious early paths or loops into DFGs, and bias variant discovery or alignment-based fitness if left unhandled. Missing resource identifiers (jump from ≈1 % to 5 %+ and persistently elevated) mainly impair resource-centric analyses (workload balancing, social networks, assessor-level performance) and any mining that conditions on resources; they do not themselves generate the observed control-flow or cycle-time signals.

 Crucially, the post-May patterns—roughly doubled Assess→RequestInfo rates, elevated rework_variant_pct, rising median/p90 cycle times, and later increases in Senior Assessment—survive the identical mapping and deduplication. These therefore cannot be dismissed as residual logging drift. They constitute genuine control-flow and performance drift that coincides with the supplier disruption and the subsequent staffing and seasonal pressures. Mapped fitness itself shows only modest further erosion, confirming that the process remains recognizable while its quantitative behavior has shifted.

The data support multiple mechanism-specific change points rather than one global break. Mapped fitness remains relatively stable after March while rework rates, cycle times and case-mix shift at distinct dates that match the external events. A single global change-point detector would therefore mis-attribute later real process drift to the earlier logging event (or vice versa).

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest conclusion is that the March fitness collapse is almost entirely a logging/semantic artifact of the system migration. The sudden appearance of the “Eligibility Screen” label, the rise in distinct activity labels, the appearance of duplicated Open Case events, and the jump in missing resource identifiers coincide exactly with the documented migration date; mapping the new label and removing the technical duplicates restores fitness from 0.55 to ≈0.90. Residual missingness continues but does not drive later control-flow or cycle-time signals.

The May jump in rework (Assess→RequestInfo rate from 12 to 24 per 100 cases, rework_variant_pct from 13 to 24) is most plausibly generated by Supplier Z’s incomplete document bundles. The supplier_z_pct spike is abrupt, matches the disruption date, and Supplier Z serves a non-random subset of customers; partial recovery of both the supplier share and the rework rate in September further supports the link. Because these rates survive label mapping, the change is genuine control-flow drift, not residual logging.

From mid-June the dominant additional mechanism is staffing-driven queueing. Four assessors left, capacity was not fully replaced, Assess service time rose only ≈0.6 h, yet median and especially p90 cycle times continued to climb. The residual after accounting for rework loops and modest case-mix change is therefore queue waiting—resource/organizational drift that interacts with the still-elevated supplier load.

The July priority rule is a plausible but weaker contributor to delay redistribution: aggregates cannot reveal whether high-complexity waiting fell while standard-case waiting rose. Its evidence strength is therefore limited to the rule’s existence and the later rise in Senior Assessment visibility.

October–November is dominated by recurring seasonal population/case-mix drift: high_complexity_pct roughly doubles, volume rises, and Senior Assessment becomes far more frequent, exactly on the historical renewal date. This seasonal surge amplifies the already elevated queue and residual rework.

December improvements reflect the joint action of lower seasonal load plus the explicit overtime and document-quality interventions; clean separation of the two is impossible with the given aggregates.

Mediation and interaction prevent clean single-cause attribution for the peak cycle times: supplier incompleteness increases rework loops, which increase load on a reduced assessor pool, which is further stressed by the seasonal complexity surge, while the priority rule redistributes waiting inside that pool. The strongest causal claims are therefore the March logging event and the May supplier-driven rework; the staffing-queue and seasonal-mix attributions are highly plausible but quantitatively entangled; the priority effect remains only plausible until stratified waiting times are examined.

The account would be revised if event-level analysis showed (a) no increase in reconstructed queue waiting after June, (b) rework rates inside the non-Z population rising as much as inside Z, (c) multi-year seasonal profiles that do not match the 2026 October spike, or (d) Assess service times that actually account for a larger share of the cycle-time growth.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw model fitness falls >0.25 while mapped fitness remains >0.85 and new activity labels or duplicate technical events appear, THEN treat as logging/semantic drift and verify the label-mapping table and technical-event filter, BECAUSE a recoverable fitness gap is the signature of vocabulary or provenance change rather than control-flow redesign.

2. IF Assess→RequestInfo rate or rework_variant_pct rises >8 percentage points and the rise is concentrated inside the Supplier Z segment, THEN escalate to supplier document-quality review and temporarily increase sampling of Z bundles, BECAUSE the May 2026 pattern showed supplier incompleteness as the dominant generator of extra rework loops.

3. IF median or p90 cycle time rises >15–20 % while Assess service time stays within 15 % of baseline, THEN diagnose queueing/capacity shortfall and review staffing levels and priority-rule impact by complexity class, BECAUSE the June–August 2026 deterioration was driven by waiting, not by longer service times.

4. IF high_complexity_pct exceeds 35 % outside the historical October–November window, THEN treat as unexpected population drift and compare against renewal forecasts, BECAUSE the documented seasonal surge is the only period in which such a large mix shift is expected.

5. IF L1 distance on the monitored DFG fragment exceeds 25 relative to the stable reference and the dominant edges are RequestInfo or Senior Assessment, THEN recompute full variants and alignments stratified by supplier and complexity, BECAUSE those two edges were the primary carriers of behavioral change after March.

6. IF missing_resource_pct remains >3 % after the migration window or becomes correlated with cycle time, THEN quarantine resource-centric analyses and investigate capture completeness by activity, BECAUSE non-random missingness biases any downstream workload or social-network mining.

7. IF mapped fitness declines below 0.85 while label vocabulary is stable, THEN investigate residual control-flow deviations (new paths, broken constraints) rather than re-mapping, BECAUSE once the migration artifact is removed a further fitness drop signals genuine process change that must be diagnosed with alignments or DECLARE constraints.
