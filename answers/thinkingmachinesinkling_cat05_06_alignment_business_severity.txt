## 1. Materiality Framework

**Severity (1–5)** integrates: (a) control objective breached (approval, fraud screening, notification, audit trail); (b) financial exposure (amount); (c) reversibility (payment made vs. blocked); (d) timing (pre-approval pay is irreversible in practice); (e) customer/regulatory harm (undisclosed high-risk payment, missing mandatory notification proof); (f) recurrence (pattern across cases); (g) evidence reliability (immutable core log vs. mutable CRM text vs. verbal claim); and (h) model scope (whether the deviation is real operational failure or an artifact of a payable-only model).

- **1 – Negligible**: Harmless technical noise, correct operational outcome, no policy breach, fully reversible.
- **2 – Low**: Model-scope or label mismatch with correct behavior; weak audit trail but likely compliant; low exposure.
- **3 – Moderate**: Real audit/data gap or policy breach with medium exposure; requires correction but not emergency reversal.
- **4 – High**: Mandatory control bypass (e.g., fraud/manual approval skipped for a risk-trigger) with significant exposure or regulatory exposure.
- **5 – Critical**: Severe control failure with high exposure and low reversibility—especially payment before approval, high-value/high-risk fraud-screen absence with unreliable substitute evidence, or systemic bypass.

**Confidence** is separate from severity. *Severity* answers: “If this deviation is real and unmitigated, how bad is the harm?” *Confidence* answers: “How certain are we that the deviation is real, not an alignment artifact, missing integration, or scope error?” Confidence is reduced when: the only evidence is a model move with external documentation present (CL-606), a mutable link (CL-608), a verbal statement without system trace (CL-602), or an out-of-scope denial/withdrawal (CL-607, CL-612).

**2. Zero-cost alignments can hide critical policy failures (CL-605, CL-609, CL-610).** CL-605 has cost 0 because the hidden transition `T(tau)` is technically fitting; the claim is compliant. But CL-609 (EUR 18,000 MEDIUM risk, no bank change) and CL-610 (EUR 4,500 LOW risk but bank changed one hour prior) also have cost 0 because the tree structurally permits `Auto Approve`. The model encodes no data-aware conditions. Thus, a severe P2 bypass (bank-change fraud indicator ignored) and a P1 breach (medium-risk auto-approval) both score 0—identical to a perfect claim. Alignment cost is therefore blind to data-aware policy overlays.

**3. Scope errors inflate cost without indicating harm (CL-607, CL-612).** CL-607 (denied claim) and CL-612 (withdrawn claim) score 4—the highest in the set—because the payable-only model expects `Assess Loss`, `Auto Approve`, `Pay Claim`, and `Notify Claimant` that correctly never happened. The cost reflects model mismatch, not operational failure. If an organization used `raw_cost` to prioritize remediation, it would investigate correct denials and withdrawals before investigating CL-602’s fraud-gap or CL-604’s pay-before-approval.

**4. Hidden silent moves (`T`) are fitting but do not prove policy compliance (CL-605).** The `tau` transition has cost 0 and is technically correct, but a zero-cost alignment does not certify that P1 conditions were checked; it only certifies structural conformance to the tree. Analysts must explicitly verify data conditions (amount, risk, bank-change) separately.

**5. Label mismatches are double-penalized while activity is present (CL-611).** `L(Assess Damage)` and `M(Assess Loss)` generate cost 2, yet only one assessment occurred. The cost treats a taxonomy error as two missing/excess activities, overstating deviation magnitude while understating that the control objective (assessment performed) was actually met.

**6. External evidence is ignored (CL-606, CL-608).** CL-606 has cost 1 for missing `Assess Loss`, but a signed external report exists. CL-608 has cost 1 for missing `Notify Claimant`, but a portal message exists within 3 hours. The cost function has no mechanism to reduce penalty when external, linked evidence exists, nor to weight it differently when the link is mutable (CL-608) versus stable.

**7. Duplicated telemetry is weighted equally with missing approvals (CL-601, CL-603).** A retry duplicate (`L(Notify Claimant)`) costs 1—the same as missing a fraud screen. The cost function cannot distinguish between noise that should be pre-filtered (P5) and a control-relevant gap.

**8. Irreversibility and timing are unrepresented (CL-604).** In CL-604, the payment at 10:03 is irreversible in practice, yet the alignment distributes cost across three moves (`L(Pay)`, `M(Fraud Screen)`, `M(Pay)` after approval) without any special weight for the temporal inversion. A cost function that treated pre-approval payment as an infinite-cost or high-weight event would rank CL-604 correctly; the uniform cost does not.

**Conclusion:** The `raw_cost` is useful as a diagnostic input for identifying structural misalignments, but it must be kept in a separate column from business severity. Remediation priorities must be driven by control-objective breach (P1–P6), financial exposure, reversibility, evidence reliability, and recurrence—not by edit distance.
