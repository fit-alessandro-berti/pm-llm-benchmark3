    """Try to fire the transition with the given name if enabled; return new marking."""

# 7. Repair Trade-Offs and Residual Risks

- **Distinguishability of waiver vs. standard review:**  
  In Repair A the waiver token still passes through the distinct place `p_waived` before entering `t_join_waived`. The two paths are clearly separated until the decision place `p_decision`. In Repair C the waiver token moves through `p_waived` and then a silent transition merges it into `p_review_done`; the fact a waiver was used is still visible because `p_waived` holds a token transiently, but after the silent move the immediate cause of the review token disappears. Both preserve the ability to distinguish the two review types at the business level.

- **Silent transitions and confusion:**  
  Repair A contains no additional silent transitions. Repair C introduces one silent transition (`t_waived_to_review`). Because it is the sole outgoing arc of `p_waived` and the incoming arc to `p_review_done` is not shared with other behaviours, it does not create confusion or duplicate behaviour.

- **Soundness vs. business alignment:**  
  Soundness guarantees that the net can always reach the final marking with no leftover tokens and no deadlocks. It does **not** by itself prove that the mandatoryanalysis rule is enforced for every path; a sound net could still have analysis skipped if the net were designed to do so intentionally. In our repairs, the mandatoryanalysis property is enforced by the synchronisation of `p_analysis_done` in every path to authorization.

- **Regression tests:**  
  *Positive traces* to test:
  - Normal path: `t_register, t_split, t_analyze, t_standard_review, t_join_normal, t_authorize`. Should end in `[sink]`.
  - Waiver path: `t_register, t_split, t_analyze, t_waive_review, t_join_waived, t_authorize`. Should end in `[sink]`.
  - Interleaved orderings (analysis first, waiver first, simultaneous) should all be valid.
  *Negative traces* to verify that mandatory analysis holds:
   - Try to fire `t_authorize` before `t_analyze` has completed on the waiver path – must be impossible.
   - Try to reach a marking with a token in `sink` and any other place – must not exist.

- **Detecting an accidental bypass of mandatory analysis:**  
  One can examine the reachability graph for any state where `sink` is marked together with another place, or where `t_authorize` is enabled while a token still resides in the analysis branch (`p_analysis` or `p_analysis_done`). Model checking queries such as “is there a reachable marking where `sink  1` and `p_analysis_done  1`?” would instantly flag a bypass. In source code, a static check that every transition consuming a token from a place connected to the sink also consumes all mandatory completion tokens serves as a lintlike guard.

# 8. Final Recommendation

**Deploy Repair A (add the synchronisation arc).**  
It is the smallest possible change (one arc), fully respects the original business intent, needs no new places or transitions, and does not rely on silent movements that could complicate logging or monitoring. The waiver and standard review remain cleanly separated until the synchronisation point, making it straightforward to audit which path was taken.

**Keep Repair C as a fallback.**  
If, for architectural reasons, the team prefers having a single “decision” join (e.g., to simplify downstream reporting that expects exactly one synchronisation point), Repair C unifies the two outcomes without sacrificing mandatory analysis. The cost is higher and it introduces a silent transition, but it is still sound and preserves the distinction via the transient `p_waived` token. It would be considered when future maintenance benefits from having fewer join transitions.
