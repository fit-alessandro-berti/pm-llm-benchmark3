# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

**Framing:** The apparent contradiction (central in handover/subcontracting, peripheral in working-together) is largely a difference in what each metric *counts*, not a paradox. Handover and subcontracting networks measure *relay and delegation structure*; working-together networks measure *shared case-level work content*. A resource whose events are short, single-touch, and concentrated on an exception tail will score high on the first two and low on the third. That is the classic signature of a **broker/dispatcher**, not an anomaly — *provided* two artifact explanations (Section 2, H4) are excluded. Centrality itself is neither good nor bad; it marks *dependency*, and dependency can be designed (an exception desk) or accidental (a single point of failure).

### Handover-of-work network
- **What it implies:** Mira has the highest out-degree (0.82) and betweenness (0.77). She is the primary relay between the automated triage layer (Bot_Rules — itself a substantial broker with betweenness 0.41), the temp workforce, and the specialist/adjuster pools. Her in-degree (0.51) is also high: specialists return answers to her (OmarMira 160, PriyaMira 140) and temps escalate to her — she is a **two-way hub** (dispatch *and* reabsorb).
- **What it does *not* imply:** effort, authority, decision quality, or that she "does" the work. Her edges are cheap (median event 4.2 min). Note also that raw frequencies look internally inconsistent with her event count (sum of her outgoing edges  1,600+ vs. 960 events), so either the edge weights use a within-case (not strictly consecutive) definition or there is an extraction/aggregation issue — this must be reconciled before trusting *absolute* weights (relative hub status is corroborated independently by subcontracting and the absence experiment).
- **Bots/vendors:** Bot_IngestBot_Rules (4,040) dominates raw counts and must be normalized away; Vendor_Pay is a terminal sink (in-degree 0.26, out-degree 0.00) fed by the team leads; **Mira has no direct vendor edges** — payment release and document verification bypass her entirely.

### Working-together network
- **What it implies:** her co-case rates with adjusters (6–8%) and leads (4–5%) are far below intra-team pairs (19–27%). She is not embedded in any team's core work, and notably she barely co-works *with the formal leads* — she operates as a **parallel channel**, not under their coordination.
- **What it does *not* imply:** irrelevance. Three mechanisms explain the low score: (a) **thin-touch profile** — 960 events over 905 cases  1.06 events/case; routing/assignment/reopen events create handovers, not co-work; (b) **portfolio skew** — she concentrates on the exception/reopen/complaint tail (48% exception share), which overlaps only partially with adjusters' mainstream portfolios, diluting any co-case denominator; (c) the co-case metric's **denominator is unspecified** (per all cases? per resource's cases?), though the *ordering* versus team pairs is robust to that ambiguity.
- **Structural reading:** brokerage (handover betweenness 0.77) and cohesion (working-together 0.16) are different roles. A structural-hole broker is *supposed* to score low on cohesion. The combination is expected for a dispatcher and only becomes suspicious if artifact explanations hold.

### Subcontracting network
- **What it implies:** the loops MiraOmarMira (126), MiraPriyaMira (98), and Bot_RulesMiraBot_Rules (62) show she **initiates specialist delegation, reabsorbs results, and closes feedback loops with the rules engine**. Her subcontracting betweenness (0.69) is the highest in the log: hub-and-spoke delegation with retained case ownership.
- **What it does *not* imply:** she performs specialist work; loops are also few relative to her handover volume — most specialist contacts are single-pass with results returning via ordinary handover.
- **Contrast:** Temp_1Vendor_Verify (68) is an external subcontracting loop that **bypasses her**; Vendor_Pay loops are terminal (no return). External-vendor coordination is therefore only partially under her control.

### Performance/time statistics
- Her 4.2-minute median service time says her impact is **not touch-time but allocation**: her "high variance" cycle-time contribution operates through waiting, queueing, and downstream assignment. The system's heavy touch times sit elsewhere (Omar 37 min, Priya 51 min) and heavy *waiting* components with the vendors (2.1h / 1.3h) — Mira plausibly acts as the **valve in front of these scarce capacities**, which is why operations fears specialist overload without her.
- The **absence natural experiment** is the strongest causal evidence available: ordinary claims unaffected; exception-path P90 rose 8.413.9 days. This rules out a *pure* logging artifact. But it does not yet isolate the mechanism: the delay could be unsent *Request Missing Information* events (downstream starvation) rather than slow routing decisions, and a general staffing dip that week is not excluded without a control window.

### Role-discovery / activity-profile similarity
- Her activity set (Request Missing Information, Reopen, Escalate Complaint, Assign Specialist) is **unique in the log**. In activity-similarity space she should form a singleton or bridging cluster — distinct from leads (Triage/Approve profile) and temps (Validate/Close profile). Role discovery would likely isolate her as a distinct organizational role rather than merge her with any team.
- Caveats: role discovery is sensitive to activity granularity and ignores sequence and frequency; and **"Assign Specialist" is absent from the declared activity dictionary** — a data-consistency flag (see Risks).

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

No hypothesis below is confirmed; they differ in falsifiable predictions, and the evidence deliberately underdetermines them.

### H1 — Formal coordinator / exception desk
- **Mechanism:** a designed routing rule sends exception-flagged cases from Bot_Rules to a staffed exception desk; she triages, requests information, assigns specialists, reopens, and escalates per SOP.
- **Supporting:** 48% exception share in her cases; Bot_RulesMira is the largest human inbound edge (920); her activity profile matches desk functions exactly; absence degrades *only* the exception path — precisely the desk's scope; ~22% case touch share is consistent with a ~20–30% exception rate.
- **Contradicting/limiting:** the process owner's "queue cleaner" remark suggests weak formal mandate; near-zero co-work with leads (4–5%) is odd for a formally integrated role; no SOP or rationale is visible in the log.
- **Predictions:** Bot_RulesMira volume tracks exception_flag incidence with a short lag; her routing targets correlate with case attributes (region/team alignment); during any documented fallback, Bot_Ruleslead edges appear in the log; exception P90 degrades during absence and recovers immediately on return.

### H2 — Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency
- **Mechanism:** all exception routing funnels through one person; queues form during absence; the routing criteria are tacit and non-transferable.
- **Supporting:** betweenness 0.77; absence P90 +65% with flat normal-flow median; 64% of reopens and 71% of complaints; both temps and specialists route through her.
- **Contradicting/limiting:** her total touch time is tiny (960 × 4.2 min  ~1 hour/workday) — she is not capacity-constrained in processing terms; the queue may reflect missing *authority or knowledge* in backups rather than her personal throughput; the P90 shift may partly be downstream starvation (unsent information requests), not her decision latency.
- **Predictions:** waiting time *immediately before* her events grows with exception inflow (queueing signature); her timestamps show batching (bursts after idle gaps); a trained backup halves the absence effect; exception volume growth produces super-linear growth in pre-Mira waiting.

### H3 — Organizational boundary spanner / triage broker
- **Mechanism:** she spans otherwise weakly connected clusters — automation layer, temp pool, two adjuster teams, two specialist functions — and her value is bridging, not membership.
- **Supporting:** the only resource with substantial edges into every cluster; the canonical broker signature (high betweenness 0.77, low cohesion 0.16); adjusters' description ("knows where hard cases should go") is brokerage knowledge; she produces cross-team patterns (e.g., rework to juniors in either team) that leads do not.
- **Contradicting/limiting:** team leads also bridge (to their adjusters and Vendor_Pay); her near-zero co-work with leads suggests she may *bypass* rather than integrate the formal hierarchy; betweenness is partly inflated by the star topology of any exception desk — a different person in the same seat would score similarly.
- **Predictions:** in her absence the exception subgraph fragments (temps and Bot_Rules connect to leads instead); new direct edges Bot_RulesOmar/Priya appear (formal escalation); her betweenness persists after volume normalization; her choices correlate with attributes leads do not use (e.g., specialist backlog).

### H4 — Data artifact / automated attribution (with "automation monitor" as sub-case)
- **Mechanism:** routing/assignment steps are logged under her ID by the workflow engine (shared exception-desk account, default queue owner, or system-generated "Assign Specialist" events), inflating centrality without human action.
- **Supporting:** 1.06 events/case; very short median service time; "appears after Bot_Rules then disappears for several events" is consistent with queue-state logging; "Assign Specialist" is missing from the declared activity dictionary; the Bot_RulesMiraBot_Rules loop looks machine-mediated; low working-together fits "no human collaboration underlying the events."
- **Contradicting/limiting:** the absence experiment produced *real operational degradation* — a pure artifact would not change case outcomes; adjusters' testimony indicates genuine human decisions; her events span multiple activity types.
- **Predictions:** timestamps at exact engine intervals or outside working hours; near-zero variance service times; lifecycle:transition always "complete" with no "start"; event volume uncorrelated with the working calendar; re-attributing events to the queue/engine collapses her centrality *and* the absence effect disappears (it would not — so test jointly).

### H5 — Informal team lead / quality gatekeeper
- **Mechanism:** without a formal lead title she has accumulated de facto control over the hard tail through cross-team visibility; she gates access to scarce specialist attention.
- **Supporting:** 71% of complaints and 64% of reopens; adjusters attribute routing expertise to her; the compliance reviewer's "uncontrolled discretion" worry presupposes real decision power; juniors' elevated exception shares (0.35–0.36 vs. seniors' 0.29–0.33) could reflect her channeling rework.
- **Contradicting/limiting:** the process owner's dismissal suggests the organization does not recognize (or resents) this authority; low co-work with leads could mean parallel silos rather than gatekeeping; no outcome data yet showing her routing improves quality; if exception_flag is set upstream by Bot_Rules, her "gate" is selection only, not detection.
- **Predictions:** stable specialist preferences (disproportionate complex-case allocation to particular individuals after case-mix controls); cases she routes show lower re-reopen rates than fallback-routed ones; complaint outcomes improve under her intake; documented rationale would reduce deviation from attribute-based rules.

### H6 — Specialist scheduler / capacity rationer
- **Mechanism:** Omar and Priya are scarce (330/260 events; 37/51-min touch times); she rations and sequences access, and feeds outcomes back into Bot_Rules' screening.
- **Supporting:** MiraOmar (210) and MiraPriya (180) dominate specialist inflow; specialists return to her; subcontracting loops show initiate-return structure; Bot_RulesMiraBot_Rules (62) shows feedback into automated fraud screening.
- **Contradicting/limiting:** loop counts are small relative to handover counts; the log shows no prioritization criteria (could be FIFO); her automation-feedback loops are rare.
- **Predictions:** her assignment order correlates with priority/amount_band beyond arrival order; specialist idle time is lower when she routes than under fallback; fraud re-flag rates fall after her updates.

# 3. Risk assessment

- **Bottleneck risk — moderate-to-high, scope-limited.** The absence experiment shows a real dependency, but *only* on the exception path. The arithmetic of her touch time argues against a raw-capacity bottleneck; the likely mechanism is decision/authority concentration. Distinguish by measuring queueing *before* her events.
- **Coordination risk — moderate.** A single point of coordination failure with tacit rules; fallback behavior has been observed only once and informally. Her near-zero co-work with the formal leads also raises a **duplication/conflict risk**: two routing authorities (leads for routine, Mira for exceptions) with weak mutual visibility.
- **Key-person risk — high.** Bus factor 1 for exception routing knowledge; 71% of complaints and 64% of reopens depend on her; no documented criteria; knowledge leaves with her (turnover, absence, resignation).
- **Compliance/control risk — moderate, and structural rather than behavioral.** Detection, assignment, reopening, escalation, *and complaint intake* are concentrated in one actor — a segregation-of-duties concern. Complaints about handling could be routed to the person whose routing is being complained about: a structural conflict of interest. Assignment rationale appears unlogged, weakening auditability. If exception_flag is set by her own actions, part of the "48% exception" statistic is circular. **Calibration:** centrality is not evidence of misconduct; no log evidence of favoritism or suppression exists. The issue is control design, not demonstrated wrongdoing.
- **Workload fairness risk — uncertain, testable.** Specialists' high exception shares are inherent to their function. Juniors (Elise/Farid) show *higher* exception shares than seniors — this may be skill-appropriate delegation or systematic rework channeling; it cannot be judged without outcome-adjusted analysis. Her discretion could create inequities by region/channel/amount_band even without intent. Her own workload is light; the fairness issue is distributional, not her overload. Temps' low exception shares fit routine work.
- **Data-quality risk — non-trivial and decision-relevant.** (1) Handover edge frequencies exceed source-resource event counts under the standard consecutive-event definition — the metric basis must be reconciled; (2) "Assign Specialist" is missing from the activity dictionary; (3) possible shared/service-account attribution; (4) co-case-rate denominator unspecified; (5) exception_flag provenance unknown; (6) lifecycle semantics behind "service time" are unclear (4–7-second bot times suggest complete-only logging); (7) vendor "service times" are likely elapsed, not effort. These determine which hypotheses survive.

# 4. Further process-mining analyses

- **Handover-of-work network:** run `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` on (a) all cases, (b) exception-only cases, (c) the absence window vs. a matched control window (via `pm4py.filter_time_range`). Compare Mira's betweenness across slices. Add a **node-removal simulation**: delete the Mira node and recompute connectivity/average shortest path in the exception subgraph to quantify fragmentation. Separately, recompute edges as *strictly consecutive* resource changes to resolve the frequency/event-count inconsistency.
- **Working-together network:** recompute `pm4py.discover_working_together_network` under alternative definitions (1 shared case vs. 2 shared activities vs. same-activity co-occurrence); compute Jaccard overlap of Mira's and each adjuster's case sets **restricted to exception cases**. If her cohesion rises sharply within the exception subset, the "portfolio dilution" explanation is confirmed; if not, she genuinely co-works little even where she is involved.
- **Subcontracting network:** run `pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network` with n=2 and n=3; compare cycle time and re-reopen rates for cases with Mira-mediated loops vs. lead-mediated or direct Bot_Rulesspecialist paths; test whether loop presence predicts subsequent reopens.
- **Activity-based similarity / role discovery:** apply `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` (cluster dendrogram — does Mira form a singleton?) and `pm4py.discover_organizational_roles` (does a distinct "exception-controller" role emerge?); cross-check with `pm4py.discover_network_analysis` for normalized centrality. Run sensitivity checks on activity aggregation level.
- **Filtering-based cohort analysis:** use `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` (exception_flag=True), `pm4py.filter_variants` (variants containing Reopen Claim / Escalate Complaint), and `pm4py.filter_time_range` (absence window vs. matched baseline). Build a "Mira vs. no-Mira" cohort matched on amount_band, priority, and region, and estimate her effect on cycle time and reopens (difference-in-differences around the absence). Also re-run all networks on filtered logs to test robustness of her centrality.
- **Service/waiting-time analysis:** use `pm4py.get_service_time` per (resource, activity) to confirm her thin-touch profile, and `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` to decompose case duration. Measure waiting time on the event *immediately preceding* her events (pre-assignment queue proxy) and *following* them (pickup delay). Analyze her inter-event time distribution for batching. During the absence window, identify **where** exception cases dwell (which activity states accumulate) to localize the true delay point — routing decision vs. unsent information requests.
- **Extras:** a routing-choice model predicting her specialist assignment from case attributes (the residual = a measurable discretion index); conformance checking on the position of Escalate Complaint; specialist-utilization Gini under her routing vs. fallback.

# 5. Recommendation

**Change immediately (low-regret, reversible):**
- Document Mira's routing criteria and add a structured `assignment_rationale` attribute logged with every Assign Specialist / Reopen / Escalate event; fix the activity dictionary; verify resource accounts (rule out shared IDs) and lifecycle-transition logging; establish the provenance of exception_flag.
- Designate and train a **backup coordinator** with real authority to assign specialists (shadowing, not a paper backup).
- Introduce a minimal segregation control for complaints: mirror every Escalate Complaint to an independent queue owner, even if Mira continues to work them.

**Monitor (KPIs over 8–12 weeks):**
- Exception-path P90 and queue length immediately before Mira's events; share of exception cases requiring her; specialist utilization distribution and allocation Gini across adjusters; rework/reopen rates by adjuster seniority; fallback performance during the next absence (treat it as a natural experiment); Bot_RulesMira loop frequency; complaint cycle times; batching patterns in her event timestamps.

**Do not change yet:**
- Do **not** remove, reassign, or marginalize Mira; do **not** hard-automate specialist assignment; do **not** restructure lead authority; do **not** treat her centrality as evidence of misconduct. Any reduction of her role must be preceded by an operating substitute: documented rules + a trained backup + Bot_Rules-generated pre-triage suggestions with human override. Reassess after the decision-log data matures and after one controlled pilot in which a second coordinator handles a randomized subset of exception cases.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira_Coordinator is the hub of our exception handling. She touches only about one claim in five, yet nearly two-thirds of reopened claims and seven in ten complaints pass through her. The network evidence shows she relays work between automated triage, temporary staff, the adjuster teams, and the medical and legal specialists, then takes the results back. She is not a heavy doer of case work — her actions are short — which is why she looks peripheral in collaboration statistics while being the most connected person in work handovers. During her six-day absence, routine claims were unaffected, but exception cases queued, and the slowest tenth took over five days longer. This confirms a real dependency on one person. The key risks are single-point-of-failure, undocumented routing know-how, and weak separation between detecting problems and choosing who handles them. We recommend documenting her criteria, training a backup, improving logging, and monitoring — not removing her.
