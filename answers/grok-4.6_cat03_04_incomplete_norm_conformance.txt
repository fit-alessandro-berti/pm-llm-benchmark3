## 1. Conformance semantics

This snapshot is judged against a **deliberately incomplete** object-centric norm. Classification therefore separates *what the norm decides* from *what the graphs merely show*.

**Forbidden behavior** is a closed-world class. A finding is forbidden only when an observed relation matches a relation explicitly marked forbidden (N3, T6, E10) and the stated scope applies to the same object identities. Residual doubt is allowed on identity and extraction quality, not on the legal status of the relation itself.

**Missing mandatory behavior** is closed-world only inside the stated scope. A mandatory gap exists when the scope applies (standard newly created Orders; packages intended for external shipment; every event of a named type; Add Item / Dispatch Package interactions) and the required relation is not observed at the abstraction the rule actually uses. “Eventually” rules are not automatically “directly-follows” rules. A missing DF edge is not, by itself, a missing mandatory outcome.

**Unexpected but unregulated behavior** is open-world. Relations absent from the normative edge set, or marked unspecified (N6, T4, T5, E9), are not violations merely because they appear. Examples include `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, `Manual Compliance Review`–Customer, Package–Delivery `object_codeath`, and `Reopen Order -> Close Order`.

**Typical-pattern absence** is a desirability gap, not noncompliance. Typical relations (N2, N5, T3) describe a common happy path. Write-offs, open invoices, credit terms, unapplied cash, and intermediate fulfilment steps are named as legitimate exceptions. Frequency shortfalls without a tolerance band are therefore not breaches.

**Likely data-quality error** is preferred when the additional context gives a concrete extraction mechanism (reused identifier join, missing type label, attribute used instead of a qualifier) that would produce the same graph edge or hole without a real business step.

**Abstraction mismatch** applies when the business rule is existential or eventual, but the summary is a DF, type-graph, or ET–OT projection. The same case can be conforming at one family and suspicious at another.

**Insufficient evidence** applies when denominators, object identity, event typing, or lifecycle completeness are not recoverable from counts alone (for example, whether the two Orders lacking `Create Order -> Validate Order` were ever validated later).

Open-world is the default: silence is not prohibition. Closed-world is reserved for forbidden relations and for mandatory columns *within scope*. PM4Py diagnostics report graph differences; they do not assign these semantic classes.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An OC-DFG establishes *observed directly-follows frequencies per object type*. It can show that 98 of 100 Orders have `Create Order` directly followed by `Validate Order` (N1’s approximation), that `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` occurs twice (N3), that `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` holds for 198 of 200 externally shipped Packages (N4), and that invoice successors split into payment (92), write-off (4), and none (14) (N5).

It cannot establish eventual occurrence, parallelism, or object identity across types. It cannot prove that the two Orders without Create→Validate never validated, nor that the three Create→Manual Compliance Review Orders are a disjoint failure set (98 + 3 > 100, so the outgoing relations are not a partition of first successors). It cannot see whether cancel and dispatch share the same Order id without the underlying OCEL.

Counts without tolerance bands are descriptive. N1’s scope is “every newly created *standard* Order should *eventually* enter validation.” Treating that as a DF rule is the central abstraction error: an unrecorded or out-of-model review between create and validate breaks DF while preserving the business obligation. N2 is typical and explicitly allows invoice, review, or fulfilment between validate and close; the 72 `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` paths and the 83 `Validate Order -> Close Order` paths can coexist without either being a violation. N6’s single reopen→close path is unspecified. N4’s two gaps are inside the external-shipment scope, so they are in-scope mandatory questions—but still DF questions. A pack→inspect→dispatch sequence would look like a N4 hole.

### OTG

An OTG establishes that object types co-occur under a relationship *somewhere* in the log: interaction, cobirth, codeath. T1 (1450) and T2 (200) look satisfied at this abstraction. T3 (92) is typical. T4 (100) and T5 (3) are unspecified. T6 (1 cobirth) is a true forbidden structural signal. The seven Package–Delivery codeath edges are unregulated; the context (shared confirmation) is a coherent lifecycle ending, not a silent prohibition.

OTG cannot say *which event* created the edge. That is why T2 = 200 can hold while E6 = 198: two Packages may interact with Delivery on a confirmation event rather than on `Dispatch Package`. Conforming OTG, suspicious ET–OT. Conversely, a forbidden cobirth is stronger than a DF anomaly because cobirth asserts a single birth event, which is closer to T6’s wording.

### ET–OT

ET–OT establishes that events of a type are linked to objects of a type. E1, E3, E4, E5, and E7 match the snapshot (100, 1450, 200, 200, 93). E2 is short by two versus E3. E6 is short by two versus E5. E8 is short by one versus E7, and the snapshot contains no “unapplied cash” type, so a closed reading of E8 flags that one event. E9 is unspecified. E10 is forbidden and coincides with a suspected join.

ET–OT cannot see object-to-object cardinality or lifecycle order. An Add Item bound to an Item but not an Order is an E2 gap even if T1 later shows that Item on an Order. A Dispatch bound to a Package and a carrier *attribute* but not a Delivery object is an E6 gap even if T2 is complete. A relation can therefore be conforming in OTG and missing in ET–OT, or forbidden in ET–OT and still a DQ artefact.

**Eventually versus directly-follows.** N1 is the textbook case. “Should eventually enter validation” is a liveness/scope rule. The summary “approximates this with a directly-follows expectation.” If investigation shows the two Orders were validated after a review, N1 holds and the DF hole is model incompleteness. If they were never validated, N1 fails. The graph summary cannot decide. Applying the same collapse to N4 or N2 would manufacture breaches from ordinary intermediates.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

**Mandatory (present or gap).** Present: E1 Create Order–Order 100; E3 Add Item–Item 1450; E4 Pack–Package 200; E5 Dispatch–Package 200; E7 Record Payment–Payment 93; T1 Order–Item 1450; T2 Package–Delivery 200 at OTG. Gaps, conditional on scope/abstraction: N1 Create→Validate 98/100 (*gap only if those Orders never validate; DF hole alone is not enough*); N4 Pack→Dispatch 198/200 for externally shipped packages (*gap if no legitimate intermediate; abstraction mismatch if one exists*); E2 Add Item–Order 1448/1450; E6 Dispatch–Delivery 198/200 (*OTG may already satisfy the interaction*); E8 Record Payment–Invoice 92/93 (*mandatory if unlabeled; exception if the event is unapplied cash in substance*).

**Forbidden.** N3 Cancel→Dispatch, 2 Orders (process). T6 Order–Payment cobirth, 1 (structural; reclassify toward DQ if extract merge is proven). E10 Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice, 1 (formal forbid; reclassify to DQ if the reused-id join is proven).

**Typical.** N2 Validate→Close 83: presence of a typical path, not a requirement that all validated Orders close that way. N5 Issue→Record Payment 92/110, Issue→Write Off 4, 14 open invoices: shortfalls are named exceptions, not breaches. T3 Invoice–Payment 92: desirable coupling, unapplied cash allowed.

**Unregulated / unspecified.** `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72); `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review` (3); N6 Reopen→Close (1); T4 Order–Customer (100); T5 Order–RiskCase (3); E9 Manual Compliance Review–RiskCase (3); Manual Compliance Review–Customer (3); Package–Delivery codeath (7). Condition that would reclassify: a *future* mandatory/forbidden extension of the norm, or proof that one of these edges is only an identifier collision (then DQ, still not a process violation under the current text).

## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch

```python
import pm4py

# Bare graphs carry topology/weights only.
# Maintain mandatory | forbidden | typical | unspecified *outside* these structures
# (norm_ocdfg / norm_otg / norm_etot metadata, or a parallel dict keyed by relation id).
STATUS = {
    "N1": "mandatory", "N2": "typical", "N3": "forbidden",
    "N4": "mandatory", "N5": "typical", "N6": "unspecified",
    "T1": "mandatory", "T2": "mandatory", "T3": "typical",
    "T4": "unspecified", "T5": "unspecified", "T6": "forbidden",
    "E1": "mandatory", "E2": "mandatory", "E3": "mandatory",
    "E4": "mandatory", "E5": "mandatory", "E6": "mandatory",
    "E7": "mandatory", "E8": "mandatory",  # exception: typed unapplied cash (none in snapshot)
    "E9": "unspecified", "E10": "forbidden",
}

obs_ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
obs_otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
obs_etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# Preserve raw diagnostics; do not overwrite with business labels.
raw_ocdfg = pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(obs_ocdfg, norm_ocdfg)
raw_otg = pm4py.conformance_otg(obs_otg, norm_otg)
raw_etot = pm4py.conformance_etot(obs_etot, norm_etot)

raw = {"ocdfg": raw_ocdfg, "otg": raw_otg, "etot": raw_etot}

def classify(diag, family, status_by_id, context):
    """Semantic layer: graph difference != legal interpretation."""
    rows = []
    for item in diag:
        nid = item.get("norm_id")
        status = status_by_id.get(nid, "unspecified")
        missing = item.get("missing", False)
        unexpected = item.get("unexpected", False)
        if status == "forbidden" and unexpected:
            sem = "forbidden_behavior"
        elif status == "mandatory" and missing:
            if context.get("abstraction_mismatch", {}).get(nid):
                sem = "abstraction_mismatch"
            elif context.get("data_quality", {}).get(nid):
                sem = "likely_data_quality_error"
            else:
                sem = "missing_mandatory_behavior"
        elif status == "typical" and missing:
            sem = "typical_pattern_absence"
        elif status in {"unspecified", "typical"} and unexpected:
            sem = "unexpected_but_unregulated"
        elif context.get("data_quality", {}).get(nid):
            sem = "likely_data_quality_error"
        else:
            sem = "insufficient_evidence" if item.get("partial") else "unregulated_or_conforming"
        rows.append({**item, "family": family, "norm_status": status, "semantic_class": sem})
    return rows

# Context must be curated; it is not in the OC-DFG/OTG/ET-OT graphs.
context = {
    "abstraction_mismatch": {
        "N1": "eventual validation approximated as DF",
        "N4": "external shipment scope; intermediates possible",
        "E6": "carrier attribute vs Delivery object; T2 weight 200",
    },
    "data_quality": {
        "E10": "suspected join on reused numeric id",
        "E8": "unapplied cash possible but unlabeled",
        "T6": "possible flattened birth event",
    },
}

semantic = (
    classify(raw_ocdfg, "ocdfg", STATUS, context)
    + classify(raw_otg, "otg", STATUS, context)
    + classify(raw_etot, "etot", STATUS, context)
)

# raw remains the system-of-record diagnostic; semantic is an overlay.
result = {"raw_pm4py": raw, "semantic_classification": semantic}
```

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 is written as eventual validation but measured as DF.** Risk: false breaches whenever any legitimate event sits between create and validate, including the observed Manual Compliance Review. Clarification: require validation *before fulfilment/cancel* as mandatory, and state that DF is only a diagnostic heuristic. Keep the obligation **mandatory**; mark the DF form **unspecified** or drop it.

2. **“Standard Order” is undefined.** Risk: rush, internal, or already-validated conversions are scored as N1 failures. Clarification: define inclusion/exclusion types. Classification: **mandatory** inside the defined class; **explicitly unspecified** outside it.

3. **N4 intermediates are unnamed.** Risk: pack→inspect→dispatch looks like a mandatory hole. Clarification: list allowed intervening event types or restate as “pack before dispatch on the same Package” (eventually / precedence). Status: **mandatory** precedence; DF adjacency **typical** or **unspecified**.

4. **E8’s unapplied-cash exception requires a type that this snapshot never uses.** Risk: every unlabeled unapplied payment becomes a formal E8 breach while T3 and finance practice allow it. Clarification: accept a Payment-only `Record Payment` as **typical** when application status ∈ {unapplied, on-account}, and **mandatory** Invoice binding otherwise; require the label.

5. **T2 vs E6 disagree on where Delivery must bind.** Risk: double-counting one operational issue as two control failures, or missing dispatches that only gain Delivery at confirmation. Clarification: **mandatory** that every *external* `Dispatch Package` qualify a Delivery *or* a typed carrier reference; OTG interaction may remain **mandatory** as an eventual object graph constraint.

6. **Codeath, cobirth (except T6), and dual qualifiers on Manual Compliance Review are silent.** Risk: analysts will treat seven Package–Delivery codeaths or Customer-on-review as violations by closed-world habit; conversely T6’s single cobirth may be a flatten artefact with no extract rule. Clarification: mark same-event Package–Delivery end as **typical** or **explicitly unspecified**; state whether review may bind Customer (**typical** or **unspecified**); add an extract rule that split births cannot emit T6.

7. **No identity, timing, or tolerance rules.** Risk: N3 cannot be confirmed as same-Order, same-lifecycle; 98/100 and 198/200 have no materiality band, inviting both over- and under-reaction. Clarification: N3 is **forbidden** only for the same Order object in one lifecycle (reopen/recreate defined); statistical bands stay **unspecified** here, with absolute review for any forbidden count ≥ 1.

8. **Sensor–finance binding has no extract prohibition to match E10.** Risk: reused ids will keep manufacturing E10 edges. Clarification: keep the business rule **forbidden**; add a technical constraint (disjoint id spaces) as a data-quality **mandatory** on the pipeline, not as a shop-floor process rule.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Immediate containment.** Prioritize N3: identify the two Orders, block further logistics, recover or reverse the dispatches if goods moved, and check credit/invoicing. Quarantine the single T6 coborn Order–Payment pair from automated posting. Retract the E10 Sensor–Invoice link from any finance or audit mart so it cannot be consumed as a real posting. Do not freeze the 198 clean packs/dispatches, the 98 Create→Validate Orders, or the 14 open invoices.

**Data repair.** Reproduce the Sensor–Invoice join on the reused numeric id and drop or namespace-correct it. For the two `Dispatch Package` events, promote the carrier attribute to a Delivery object or a typed qualifier so E6 and T2 describe the same fact. Label or split the unlabeled `Record Payment` as unapplied cash if finance confirms. Re-extract the two Add Item events missing an Order qualifier and compare them to the 1450 Order–Item OTG edges. Re-parse the T6 birth event to see whether Order and Payment were falsely merged.

**Process investigation.** Walk the two cancel-then-dispatch cases with warehouse and order-desk: cancel after pick, system allowing dispatch on cancelled state, or wrong object id. Only if N1’s two Orders never validated, investigate skipped validation—not because DF is missing, but because the eventual rule would then fail. For N4’s two shipped packages, look for an intervening check, a missing pack event, or a non-external path mis-flagged as external. Leave write-offs (4), open invoices (14), reopen (1), compliance reviews (3), customer links (100), and codeath (7) out of escalation; rarity is not severity.

**Norm revision.** Rewrite N1 as eventual/precedence; define standard Order; recast N4 as precedence plus allowed intermediates; align E6/T2 and E8/T3; declare codeath and review–Customer unspecified or typical; keep forbidden counts at zero-tolerance. Record that PM4Py missing/unexpected edges are inputs to this overlay, not verdicts.

**Monitoring.** Ongoing zero-count alerts for N3, T6, and E10 (with an E10 DQ suppressor when the join key pattern matches). Sample-based review of E2/E6/E8 singleton gaps. Aging dashboard for N5’s open invoices, not a conformance red flag. Do not alert on N2 < 100%, on N6, or on T4/T5/E9.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

**Robust:** N3’s two cancel→dispatch paths are forbidden if object identity holds; E1/E3/E4/E5/E7 hold; T1 is present; N5’s write-offs and open invoices are in-policy typical exceptions; N6, T4, T5, E9, Validate→Issue Invoice, review–Customer, and Package–Delivery codeath are not violations under this norm; PM4Py graph diffs are not a complete legal reading.

**Depend on incompleteness:** whether N1’s 2/100 and N4’s 2/200 are breaches or DF over-approximations; whether E8’s single Invoice-less payment is mandatory failure or an untyped allowed exception; whether T2’s 200 already discharges the dispatch–delivery obligation that E6 scores as 198; any judgment that treats unlisted edges as illicit.

**Depend on suspected data quality:** E10 (reused-id join); possibly T6 (flattened birth); possibly E2’s two unbound Add Items; possibly E6 if Delivery exists only as a carrier attribute or later confirmation.

**Cannot be decided from graph summaries alone:** same-object identity for N3 and T6; whether the two non-DF Orders were ever validated; whether 98+3 Create successors overlap; the validated-Orders denominator behind N2’s 83; the business meaning of seven codeaths beyond the given confirmation story; and any statistical “tolerance” the norm explicitly refuses to set.
