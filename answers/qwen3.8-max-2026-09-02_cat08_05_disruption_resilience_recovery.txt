# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

In this case, resilience should not be equated with strict conformance to the baseline process. Some deviations were necessary and valuable. The key distinction is whether the adaptation was **temporary, risk-aware, governed, evidence-preserving, and recoverable**, or whether it **removed a required control while making throughput look normal**.

## True resilience

True resilience protected patient-critical supply while preserving or reconstructing enough control evidence.

Examples:

- **Manual authorization check (B)** preserved the authorization control when the system was unavailable.
- **Temporary release control (F)** was resilient where it added pharmacist review for controlled-substance orders, although it needed stronger coverage for cold-chain evidence.
- **Hospital-critical fast lane (A)** was resilient when used as explicit triage, not as a blanket bypass of authorization.
- **Defer noncritical order (I)** was resilient if it prevented collapse of critical capacity, but only if fairness and customer communication were managed.
- **Backlog prioritization (J)** and **reconcile manual reservation (K)** were resilient recovery mechanisms when explicit, governed, and auditable.
- **Audit remediation (L)** is part of resilience only when it is a planned, resourced recovery activity. It is not a substitute for real-time control.

## Risky shortcuts and concealed noncompliance

Risky shortcuts preserved movement but removed or weakened controls that are essential for pharmaceutical wholesale compliance.

Key examples:

- **Skip system authorization check (C)** created an authorization gap. It may have been operationally convenient for known ambient customers, but it becomes concealed noncompliance if not documented, validated, and retrospectively verified.
- **Dispatch without scan (G)** was the most dangerous shortcut. It preserved dispatch throughput but removed chain-of-custody evidence. For controlled-substance, cold-chain, and recall-sensitive products, this is not a minor deviation; it undermines recall readiness, regulatory traceability, and customer safety.
- **Phone-only proof of delivery (H)** is weak evidence for regulated products, especially where signature, temperature, or custody transfer is required.
- **Missing quality/release check**, missing scan, missing proof of delivery, and missing temperature-control record were move-on-model deviations during T2. These are not harmless process variants; they are missing required controls.
- **Spreadsheet invoice hold** ending cases outside the normal invoice-close path created financial and compliance ambiguity.

## Ambiguous adaptations depending on risk appetite

Several adaptations are neither clearly resilient nor clearly noncompliant. Their classification depends on governance, product risk, documentation, and timing.

- **Hospital-critical fast lane (A)** is acceptable if it includes minimum manual controls for regulated products. It is unacceptable if it silently bypasses authorization for controlled or cold-chain items.
- **Manual inventory snapshot check (D)** is reasonable early in the incident but becomes risky as the snapshot ages.
- **Manual pick list (E)** is acceptable if paired with offline capture, lot verification, and reconstruction. It becomes risky if it creates untraceable downstream records.
- **Phone proof of delivery (H)** may be acceptable for low-risk ambient deliveries if followed by stronger confirmation. It is inadequate for controlled or cold-chain products.
- **Defer noncritical order (I)** is acceptable if transparent and fair. It becomes a customer-relationship and fairness risk if Pharmacy-standard and Wholesale-bulk customers are indefinitely deprioritized.
- **Audit remediation (L)** is necessary, but it should not be treated as equivalent to contemporaneous compliance. Reconstructing evidence after dispatch may reduce uncertainty, but it cannot recreate the assurance that a control operated at the time of release.

The conformance findings support this distinction. Baseline fitness was high at **0.96** with **91% fitting traces**. Manual-workaround fitness fell to **0.71** with only **44% fitting traces**, showing that T2 was not merely a different process but a materially less controlled one. Stabilization improved to **0.90** fitness and **76% fitting traces**, but the persistence of audit remediation shows that operational recovery did not fully restore compliance assurance.

# 4. Recovery process model

The redesigned recovery model should be a **risk-tiered degraded-operations and recovery process**, not an improvised return to normal.

Notation used below:

- `[Trigger]` = condition that activates a process state.
- `{Gate}` = mandatory control or decision point.
- `=>` = transition to next state.
- Indentation shows sequence or branching.

## Trigger conditions and operating states

1. **Degraded Manual Mode**
   - Trigger: Warehouse management system unavailable for more than 30 minutes during active shipping hours, or cyber containment isolates warehouse operations.
   - Action: Activate incident command, triage rules, and manual evidence capture.

2. **Manual Custody Mode**
   - Trigger: Scanning unavailable for more than 2 hours, or dispatch scan failure rate exceeds an agreed threshold.
   - Action: Use approved manual chain-of-custody kit: prenumbered manifest, carrier ID, driver signature, customer signature, photo evidence, and GPS or delivery note where available.

3. **Controlled-Substance Manual Register Mode**
   - Trigger: Controlled-substance ledger, electronic authorization, or dual-control system unavailable.
   - Action: Pharmacist-controlled manual register, dual signatures, positive customer authorization, lot/serial capture, and no phone-only proof of delivery.

4. **Cold-chain Evidence Mode**
   - Trigger: Temperature monitoring integration unavailable or carrier temperature evidence cannot be captured.
   - Action: Manual temperature readings at release and handover, qualified carrier confirmation, and quarantine if temperature evidence is missing.

5. **Hybrid Recovery Mode**
   - Trigger: Order intake restored, but inventory synchronization or scanning remains unreliable.
   - Action: Continue risk-based manual controls, open exception queues, and prevent automatic closure of regulated orders with missing evidence.

6. **Stabilization and Compliance Closure Mode**
   - Trigger: Systems restored, but reconciliation, audit remediation, or exception queues remain open.
   - Action: Separate operational closure from compliance closure. Continue audit remediation with SLAs and management reporting.

7. **Return to Normal**
   - Criteria: System integrity verified, reconciliation complete or risk-accepted, control testing passed, traceability exceptions near baseline, missing controlled/cold-chain evidence resolved, and audit-remediation queue closed or explicitly risk-accepted.

## Triage rules

| Priority | Segment/product combination | Routing rule |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** | Hospital-critical orders containing cold-chain, controlled-substance, recall-sensitive, or life-critical items | Fulfill first. Do not defer. Full minimum controls required. |
| **P2** | Hospital-critical ambient orders; Pharmacy-standard cold-chain, controlled-substance, or recall-sensitive orders | Fulfill with manual or hybrid controls. Authorization and product-risk controls required. |
| **P3** | Pharmacy-standard ambient orders; Wholesale-bulk urgent clinical or contract-critical orders | Fulfill with simplified controls where allowed. May be delayed, but subject to fairness caps and customer communication. |
| **P4** | Wholesale-bulk noncritical orders, nonurgent replenishment, low-risk stock transfers | Defer or hold. Communicate revised ETA. Do not allow indefinite silent deferral. |

Triage governance rules:

- Priorities must be explicit and documented before execution.
- Overrides require named approver and reason code.
- Pharmacy-standard and Wholesale-bulk segments must have minimum service floors to avoid unfair sacrifice.
- Deferred orders must receive customer notification and revised ETA.
- Oldest P2 and P3 orders should be escalated periodically to prevent hidden backlog aging.

## Minimum controls by product/customer risk

| Product/customer risk | Minimum controls in degraded mode | Prohibited shortcuts |
|---|---|---|
| **Controlled-substance, any customer** | Positive authorization, dual control, pharmacist release, lot/serial capture, manual controlled register, chain-of-custody evidence, recipient signature. | Skip authorization, dispatch without custody evidence, phone-only proof of delivery, unremediated missing dual-control. |
| **Cold-chain, any customer** | Temperature reading at release and handover, qualified carrier, temperature monitor or manual log, proof of delivery with temperature evidence where required. | Release without temperature evidence, unqualified carrier, silent dispatch with missing temperature documentation. |
| **Recall-sensitive items** | Lot capture, customer lot confirmation, traceability record complete before dispatch, recall contact details verified. | Dispatch without lot traceability or customer linkage. |
| **Hospital-critical ambient** | Preapproved or manual authorization, inventory check, pick verification, release sampling or pharmacist/compliance approval, proof of delivery. | Silent skip of authorization, dispatch without delivery evidence. |
| **Pharmacy-standard and Wholesale-bulk ambient** | Authorization, inventory check, pick/pack verification, proof of delivery. May be deferred under transparent rules. | Indefinite deferral without communication, unmanaged cancellation, hidden deprioritization. |

## Manual evidence capture

Manual evidence must be contemporaneous, attributable, and clearly marked as manual. It must not be backdated.

Required manual evidence:

- Unique manual case or form ID.
- Incident period flag, for example T2 manual workaround.
- Operator ID and approver ID.
- Timestamp of manual action.
- Customer, product, quantity, lot/serial where applicable.
- Authorization basis: preapproved matrix, phone confirmation, pharmacist approval.
- Inventory source: snapshot time, manual count, reservation ID.
- Release decision: pharmacist or quality approver, sampling rationale.
- Dispatch evidence: carrier name, driver ID, manifest number, signature, photo.
- Proof of delivery: customer name, signature or confirmation method, time, temperature reading if cold-chain.
- Exception note: what was missing, why, who approved, remediation owner.

Upload and reconciliation rules:

- P1 evidence should be uploaded within 12 hours where possible.
- P2/P3 evidence should be uploaded within 24 hours.
- Regulated product cases should not be considered compliance-closed until required evidence is captured or a formal risk assessment is completed.
- Manual records should be linked to the order before invoice close where feasible; otherwise, invoice close should trigger an audit-remediation task.

## Backlog prioritization

When systems partially recover, backlog should be prioritized using an explicit risk score, not informal triage.

Suggested risk score:

- Product risk: controlled-substance, cold-chain, recall-sensitive = high.
- Customer criticality: Hospital-critical = high, Pharmacy-standard = medium, Wholesale-bulk = lower.
- Clinical urgency: life-critical or patient-impacting = high.
- Order age: older orders receive increasing priority.
- Contractual or regulatory obligation: add priority where required.

Process:

1. Create backlog risk score for all open and deferred orders.
2. Sequence P1 first, then oldest P2, then P3 with fairness caps.
3. Publish daily backlog position by segment.
4. Require governance approval for bulk cancellations.
5. Log all priority overrides.
6. Communicate ETAs to affected customers.

## Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow

Reconciliation and audit remediation should be treated as a formal workstream, not as leftover cleanup.

Process tree:

```text
Systems restored
 Step 1: Identify all manual-workaround cases
    Flag cases from T2 and T3 by adaptation type
 Step 2: Three-way match
    Order record
    Inventory reservation/lot record
    Financial/invoice record
 Step 3: Control-evidence match
    Authorization evidence
    Release/quality evidence
    Dispatch scan or manual custody evidence
    Proof of delivery
    Temperature evidence
    Controlled-substance dual-control evidence
 Step 4: Exception classification
    Missing authorization
    Missing scan/custody
    Missing temperature record
    Missing dual-control
    Missing proof of delivery
    Inventory mismatch
 Step 5: Containment and remediation
    Obtain missing evidence where possible
    Confirm with customer/carrier
    Quarantine product if safety cannot be established
    Notify customer or regulator where required
    Record actual event times; do not backdate
 Step 6: Compliance closure
    Separate compliance closure from operational closure
 Step 7: Root cause and playbook update
     Why control was missed
     Which adaptation failed
     What trigger/control should change
```

Important principle: **operational recovery is not compliance recovery**. A case can be delivered and invoiced while still having unresolved traceability, temperature, authorization, or custody gaps.

# 5. Resilience indicators

## Leading indicators

| Indicator | What it tells management | Target or alert threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **Hospital-critical acceptance rate** | Whether critical orders are being protected during disruption. | At least 95% of Hospital-critical orders accepted into fulfillment within agreed triage window; alert below 90%. |
| **Triage lane adherence** | Whether orders are being routed according to explicit risk rules. | At least 98% of orders routed to correct priority lane. |
| **Manual authorization completion before release** | Whether authorization control is preserved in degraded mode. | 100% for P1/P2 regulated or high-risk orders; any missing triggers stop/release review. |
| **Cold-chain temperature capture before dispatch** | Whether cold-chain product safety evidence is being created contemporaneously. | 100% for cold-chain orders; missing temperature evidence triggers quarantine or release hold. |
| **Controlled-substance dual-control capture** | Whether controlled-substance custody controls are intact. | 100%; any missing dual-control should stop dispatch or trigger secure quarantine. |
| **Dispatch custody completeness** | Whether dispatch has scan or approved manual chain-of-custody. | 100% for controlled, cold-chain, recall-sensitive; at least 98% overall. |
| **Manual evidence upload latency** | Whether manual records are being captured quickly enough to be useful. | At least 95% of P1 manual evidence uploaded within 12 hours; P2/P3 within 24 hours. |
| **Backlog age by segment** | Whether deferred work is accumulating invisibly. | Alert if Hospital-critical backlog age exceeds 12h, Pharmacy-standard exceeds 72h, Wholesale-bulk exceeds 120h, or any segment exceeds twice baseline p90. |
| **Deferred-order communication rate** | Whether deferral is transparent to customers. | 100% of deferred orders have customer notification and revised ETA. |
| **Exception queue burn-down** | Whether recovery is reducing control debt or merely postponing it. | Alert if new exceptions exceed resolved exceptions for two consecutive days. |

## Lagging indicators

| Indicator | What it tells management | Target or alert threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **Throughput and cycle time by segment** | Whether service levels are returning to baseline. | Alert if p90 cycle time remains more than 1.5 times baseline for more than three business days. |
| **Fill rate by segment** | Whether critical and noncritical customers are being served fairly. | Hospital-critical fill should remain near 90% or higher; Pharmacy-standard and Wholesale-bulk should have agreed service floors and recover toward baseline. |
| **Cancellation rate by segment** | Whether disruption is being hidden through silent or forced cancellations. | Stabilization cancellation rate should approach baseline 2.1%; alert if above 3.0% without documented customer request or safety reason. |
| **Traceability exception rate** | Whether end-to-end order traceability is restored. | Baseline 0.8%; stabilization target near or below 1.0%, with zero unresolved exceptions for regulated high-risk cases. |
| **Cold-chain temperature documentation missing** | Whether cold-chain product safety evidence is complete. | Target 0%; any missing record requires quarantine, customer notification, or quality risk assessment. |
| **Controlled-substance dual-control missing** | Whether controlled-substance legal custody controls are complete. | Target 0%; any missing record requires immediate investigation and possible regulatory assessment. |
| **Rework/correction rate** | Whether process instability is creating downstream corrections. | Baseline 3.5%; stabilization target below 4.0%, alert if above 6.0%. |
| **Conformance fitness and fitting traces** | Whether actual process behavior matches the normative controlled process. | Fitness at least 0.95 and fitting traces at least 90% before declaring normal recovery. |
| **Proof-of-delivery completeness and timeliness** | Whether delivery evidence is complete, especially for regulated products. | At least 98% complete within 24 hours; phone-only proof of delivery should be 0% for controlled-substance orders. |
| **Audit-remediation queue volume and age** | Whether hidden compliance workload remains after operational recovery. | Remediation cases should close within agreed SLA, for example 10 business days; alert for cases open beyond 30 days. |
| **Inventory accuracy after reconciliation** | Whether manual inventory workarounds caused stock or lot errors. | Inventory accuracy near pre-incident level; unexplained adjustments and lot mismatches require investigation. |
| **Fairness index across customer groups** | whether resilience for hospitals came at disproportionate cost to pharmacies or wholesalers. | Compare fill rate and p90 cycle time across segments. Alert if Pharmacy-standard or Wholesale-bulk performance remains materially worse without documented risk rationale. |

# 6. Recommendations for future disruptions

| Priority | Recommendation | Implementation effort | Expected benefit | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P0** | Implement product-risk stop-ship rules: no controlled-substance, cold-chain, or recall-sensitive dispatch unless minimum evidence is captured. | Low | Prevents patient-safety and regulatory escapes during disruption. | Reduced throughput for regulated products during severe outages; possible short-term stockouts. |
| **P0** | Replace silent skip-authorization with a preapproved emergency authorization matrix, documented approver, and retrospective verification. | Medium | Preserves speed for known customers while closing the authorization gap seen in adaptation C. | Matrix may become stale; standing authorizations need periodic review. |
| **P0** | Deploy a manual chain-of-custody kit for dispatch without scan: prenumbered manifests, carrier ID, driver signature, customer signature, photo evidence, and quarantine rules for missing evidence. | Medium | Directly addresses the largest traceability gap, adaptation G. | Manual evidence can be entered late or incorrectly; requires discipline and audit. |
| **P0** | Strengthen cold-chain and controlled-substance degraded-mode evidence capture: manual temperature logs, qualified carrier confirmation, pharmacist dual release, manual controlled register. | Medium | Protects product efficacy, legal custody, and regulatory defensibility. | Slower fulfillment and possible product loss when temperature evidence cannot be established. |
| **P1** | Create a triage and service-tier playbook with fairness guardrails for Pharmacy-standard and Wholesale-bulk customers. | Medium | Protects Hospital-critical supply while preventing silent sacrifice of other segments. | Capacity constraints may still require difficult trade-offs. |
| **P1** | Deploy offline/mobile evidence capture and barcode scanning for manual pick lists, lot/serial capture, dual signatures, and later scan reconstruction. | High | Reduces traceability exceptions, rework, and audit tail. | Device, network, and training failures still possible; needs fallback paper process. |
| **P1** | Establish a reconciliation control tower with separate audit-remediation queue, SLAs, owners, and compliance closure status. | Medium | Makes hidden post-recovery workload visible and prevents premature declaration of recovery. | Data matching may remain difficult if manual records are incomplete. |
| **P1** | Implement an inventory-confidence program: timestamped snapshots, snapshot age limits, manual reservation expiry, targeted cycle counts, and recall-lot verification. | Medium to High | Reduces risk from stale inventory snapshots and incorrect lot allocation. | Additional holds or counts may slow fulfillment during early disruption. |
| **P2** | Build real-time process mining/conformance dashboards tracking fitness, fitting traces, move-on-log deviations, move-on-model deviations, and temporal delays. | High | Gives early warning of concealed noncompliance and objective recovery evidence. | Model tuning and false positives require operational ownership. |
| **P2** | Invest in cyber-resilient architecture: segmented warehouse operations, read-only offline inventory snapshots, immutable audit logs, and alternate scanning/network path. | High | Reduces likelihood and severity of future WMS outages. | Cost and implementation complexity; residual cyber risk remains. |
| **P2** | Conduct role-specific training and tabletop exercises for manual authorization, controlled-substance degraded mode, cold-chain evidence capture, and triage governance. | Medium | Improves speed and compliance of human response during the first 24–72 hours. | Human error and staff turnover require recurring training. |

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should accept temporary, documented, risk-tiered manual workarounds that protect Hospital-critical supply, provided they preserve minimum evidence for authorization, custody, temperature, and controlled-substance controls, and are followed by rapid reconciliation. It should not accept silent skipping of authorization, dispatch without chain-of-custody evidence, missing cold-chain or controlled-substance records, informal deprioritization of non-hospital customers, or treating audit remediation as equivalent to real-time compliance while declaring operational recovery. The recommendation should change if evidence shows that manual controls provide equivalent assurance—such as 100% dual signatures, complete temperature logs, complete proof of delivery, no quality escapes, regulator acceptance of alternative evidence, and inventory reconciliation within tolerance. Conversely, if missing evidence, product-quality events, regulatory breaches, or unresolved audit queues persist, management should pause shipments of regulated high-risk products until controls are restored.
