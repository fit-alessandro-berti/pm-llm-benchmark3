## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts (taken as given):** the texts of GPIP-4 (A), SOP DE-AAC-PROC-17 (B), ECD-2 (C), the CFO email (D), the outage note ON-44, the case table timestamps and corroborating evidence, and the organizational facts (CRO unavailable and never ratified ECD-2; Incident Command members are not designated budget owners; the CFO owns the Finance control framework).

**Interpretations and assumptions (mine, stated as such):**

1. **Source authority.** A board-approved global policy (A) sits at the top for *integrity controls* (segregation of duties A1, pre-commitment approvals A2, Compliance clearance A3), because A7 reserves formal exceptions to the Chief Risk Officer and the CRO neither issued nor ratified ECD-2. I treat ECD-2 as a *valid temporary operational overlay* — not an A7 exception — because: (i) B7 expressly contemplates corporate incident directives superseding "ordinary local workflow steps"; (ii) the organization has previously treated signed incident directives as temporary policy overlays; (iii) the COO can issue operational directives. Consequence: ECD-2 can displace *operational/timing/onboarding* rules (B1 PO-before-commit, B3 supplier register) within its scope and window, but cannot displace A1 (SoD) or A3 (Compliance clearance), which it does not even mention (C5). Silence is not override.
2. **Specificity.** A more specific, expressly addressed issue in a narrower instrument prevails on that issue within its scope and time (lex specialis), provided the issuer has authority over that issue.
3. **Later-in-time.** Later-in-time only counts within the same authority level. The CFO email (D) postdates everything but is an uncontrolled email; it binds only where the CFO has delegated authority — the Finance control framework (D3 concedes it cannot waive Compliance or SoD). I treat D1/D2 as a valid *interpretation of Finance controls* (substituting joint Incident Command authorization for Finance_Approve up to EUR 150,000, plus Legal above that), effective **prospectively from 2026-07-15 07:20** and only inside the ECD-2 window it references. Assumption flagged: whether D1 also substitutes for the "independent budget owner" leg of A2 is genuinely uncertain, since IC members are not budget owners; I treat D1 as covering the Finance-side approvals it plausibly owns, and record the budget-owner gap as residual uncertainty rather than an automatic breach where every integrity control was otherwise met.
4. **Emergency necessity.** An emergency justifies only deviations that are *necessary and proportionate at the time of the act*. A genuine emergency does not excuse a deviation that could have been avoided (e.g., bypassing a reachable Plant Manager), and labeling something "emergency" does not make it one (B4 defines it: imminent safety harm or stoppage within six hours).
5. **Timing of assessment.** Rules are applied at the time of the relevant act (usually Commit_to_Supplier), not at case completion. Per A6, later approval mitigates consequences but does not cure a missing pre-commitment control.
6. **Retrospective evidence.** Per ON-44, a late or drifted ERP timestamp is not conclusive against stronger contemporaneous evidence (signed PDFs, telephone recordings, supplier acknowledgments, access logs). Late *recording* of a timely act is a logging issue, not a substantive breach — provided the norm itself only requires recording within a window (C3, B6).
7. **Burden of proof.** Whoever invokes an exception bears the burden of proving its existence, scope, and timeliness. An unevidenced verbal extension of an expired written directive fails that burden. Conversely, the assessor must not classify as NONCOMPLIANT solely on absent records where the norm violated is evidential rather than substantive; that yields INDETERMINATE or a logging finding.

**Decision method.** For each case: (1) fix the time and scope of each act; (2) determine which instruments were in force and applicable to that act; (3) resolve conflicts issue-by-issue using the hierarchy in Section 2; (4) test claimed exceptions against authority, scope, window, and necessity; (5) weigh evidence per point 6; (6) classify, exposing residual uncertainty in the confidence column rather than hiding it.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-502 → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** Premise that must change: read C4's precedence over "supplier-onboarding requirements" as encompassing A3's new-supplier Compliance clearance (clearance-as-onboarding), and treat C3's joint authorization as the incident-time functional equivalent of A2's dual approval. Credibility: moderate. It is textually available and the deviations were cured within hours under real urgency. It is weakened by C5 (ECD-2 conspicuously does not mention Compliance), by A5's ban on silently weakening GPIP-4, and by A7 reserving exceptions to the CRO. On balance I find it credible enough to lower severity, not to flip the classification.

**P-503 → INDETERMINATE.** Premise that must change: adopt Operations' scope reading (backup-line components are "critical sterilization-line lots"), treat the IC "critical contingency" label as a binding scope determination, and read C3 as displacing A1 for incident purchases. Credibility: weak. Even granting the scope point, no source purports to waive segregation of duties, and PROC-3 authorizing PROC-3's own commitment defeats the purpose of C3 itself (two-person control). The 3-day stock buffer also removes temporal necessity. This is the case least sensitive to interpretation.

**P-505 → NONCOMPLIANT.** Premise that must change: treat the ERP timestamp (18:06) as controlling, reject the 9-minute clock-error claim, and treat the supplier acknowledgment 17:59:31 as evidence of negotiation rather than of a completed commitment. Then the commitment post-dates expiry and no instrument excuses the post-hoc Finance approval or PO absence. Credibility: weak-to-moderate. It contradicts ON-44's instruction to prefer contemporaneous non-ERP evidence, and an inbound supplier *acknowledgment* at 17:59:31 presupposes a commitment already communicated. A clock audit would settle it either way — this is the case most exposed to a single new piece of evidence.

**P-506 → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** Premise that must change: accept that a verbal IC extension of ECD-2 is effective, or that contamination-recovery purchases enjoy a continuing implied necessity after expiry. Credibility: weak. ECD-2 derived its legitimacy precisely from being written, jointly signed, scoped, and time-limited; an unrecorded verbal extension has none of those features, and C3's own recording discipline (4 hours) shows the directive regime rejects unevidenced authority. If a contemporaneous recording of the extension surfaced, I would move to INDETERMINATE pending authority analysis, not directly to justified.

**P-508 → NONCOMPLIANT.** Premise that must change: read D1's "up to EUR 150,000" as a hard ceiling on the substitution, so that above 150k the full A2 regime (independent budget owner + Finance Controller, pre-commitment) revives, with D2's Legal clearance merely additive to it. Credibility: moderate — it is the more literal reading of D1. I discount it because "obtain Legal clearance *as well*" only makes sense as an increment to an otherwise sufficient IC-authorization path, and the CFO owns the control being substituted. This is the interpretive coin-flip in the set; hence the secondary INDETERMINATE.

**P-501 → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** Premise that must change: either the ERP commit timestamp of 08:50 is wrong in the *other* direction (the outage began 08:30; if the true act were after 09:00 and within a broad ECD scope, C-rules could apply), or "reachable" is contested and an implied standing delegation to shift leads existed. Credibility: weak on the first (ECD scope would still be strained for a routine valve under the 24h-vs-backup dispute — though this valve was for the affected line, the commit preceded the window on all evidence) and untested on the second (no delegation record exists; B4 demands *active* delegation).

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

**Four distinct phenomena in this data set — they must not be conflated:**

- **Policy breach (substantive):** the act itself violated a rule in force — P-503 (SoD, Compliance, Finance), P-504 (supplier, Compliance, pretext), P-506 (expired directive, PO/Plant sequence), P-501 (unauthorized committer), P-502 (pre-commit Compliance/Finance missing at act time).
- **Logging defect (act timely, record poor):** P-505's missing Create_PO event and possible ERP clock skew; P-507's 14:30 ERP entry for an authorization actually recorded in a signed PDF at 12:40; ON-44-window timestamps generally.
- **Late evidence of a timely act:** P-505's commitment — proven timely by the 17:59:31 supplier acknowledgment despite the 18:06 ERP stamp; P-507's authorization — timely per the 12:40 PDF. These are compliant acts that a naive timestamp-only audit would misclassify.
- **Inability to prove compliance:** P-506's alleged verbal extension — possibly true, but the invoking party cannot discharge the burden; classified against the invoker, with the classification explicitly reversible if a contemporaneous record surfaces.

**Immediate case-level remediation:**
1. P-503: escalate to Compliance and the CRO; obtain retrospective Compliance review of the 140k purchase and the supplier; remove PROC-3 from self-affecting authorization roles pending review; document that any ratification mitigates but does not cure (A6).
2. P-502: complete a post-hoc file demonstrating the new supplier would have cleared Compliance; record the act-time rationale; no disciplinary tone — the operational deviations were validly excused.
3. P-504: recover the emergency form, correct the record, counsel SHIFT-2; run new-supplier compliance screening retroactively; flag as the exemplar of emergency-label misuse in training.
4. P-505: audit the ERP clock offset for 07-18; create the missing PO retroactively with a cross-reference note; preserve the supplier acknowledgment email as the controlling time evidence.
5. P-506: obtain statements from the named IC members about the alleged extension within a fixed deadline; until then treat as a breach; complete Plant approval documentation.
6. P-507/P-508: preserve the signed PDFs and clearance sequence as the positive template; obtain a written CFO confirmation of the intended >150k regime (closes the P-508 ambiguity prospectively and clarifies it retrospectively).

**Monitoring actions:** (i) conformance checking with two timestamps per event (system time and best-evidence time) during any declared outage; (ii) an automated SoD check that joins requester/committer/authorizer identities per purchase; (iii) a directive registry with hard expiry alerts and a rule that no purchase may cite a directive after its recorded end; (iv) quarterly review of all emergency-form purchases against the B4 definition to detect normalization drift; (v) reconciliation of C3's 4-hour recording obligation against actual recording latency.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Proposed clause GPIP-4 §A8 (replacing the A7 ambiguity and the B7/C4 vagueness):**

1. **Exception authority.** Only the Chief Risk Officer, or a pre-named deputy on a published escalation list, may suspend an integrity control (segregation of duties, Compliance clearance, dual pre-commitment approval). An incident directive signed by the COO and Incident Commander is a valid **operational overlay**: it may modify PO timing, supplier-register use, approval routing, and evidence channels — never integrity controls. If the CRO and all deputies are unavailable, integrity controls remain in force and purchases requiring them must either wait or proceed as documented noncompliance subject to mandatory post-incident review.
2. **Scope and definitions.** Every directive must define its covered items by named lines/lots or an objective test (e.g., "components whose absence stops a covered line within 24 hours"), its monetary ceiling, and its expiry to the minute. Undefined scope terms are construed narrowly against the party invoking the directive.
3. **Thresholds and roles.** During a directive: joint authorization by two Incident Command members — **neither of whom is the requester, committer, or beneficiary** — substitutes for routing-level approvals up to a ceiling stated in the directive; Finance Controller substitution requires prior written CFO delegation filed in the controlled repository; Compliance clearance for new suppliers may be **expedited (target: 2 hours)** but never skipped.
4. **Retrospective records.** Verbal authorizations must be recorded within 4 hours in a designated system; during outages, a signed and hash-stamped PDF or recorded call is equivalent evidence. A timely act evidenced by contemporaneous third-party records is compliant notwithstanding later system timestamps.
5. **Expiry and extension.** Directives end automatically at expiry; extension requires the same signatures and form as issuance. Verbal extensions are void.
6. **Anti-normalization safeguard.** Each entity may invoke the emergency track at most a set number of times per quarter without triggering an automatic Internal Audit review; every emergency purchase is reviewed post-incident against the directive's objective scope test, and any purchase found outside scope is reported to the Audit Committee regardless of amount. Uncontrolled communications (email, chat) can interpret but never create or extend an exception.
