# 1. Cluster diagnoses

**How to read this section.** "Observation" means something read directly from the rows. "Hypothesis" means an interpretation of how the work was physically carried out. Confidence is my credence in the top explanation relative to the listed alternatives. It is not a probability that the timestamps are correct.

**Working assumptions (labelled, used throughout):**

- **A1, clock alignment.** `queue_arrival` (upstream clock) and the local lifecycle fields are assumed to be roughly synchronised. This is needed before any waiting time can be computed. G7 (negative waits) and G8 (different clocks, stated) put pressure on this assumption, and G5's constant 15-minute wait is at least consistent with an offset.
- **A2, insertion after the fact.** `recorded_at` is assumed to be at or after the true completion. Insertion should not precede the fact it records. This lets `recorded_at` serve as an upper bound on true completion.
- **A3, seconds in 1-second sources.** For the 1-second sources (PLC, scanner), the start/complete values are shown as HH:MM. I read them as HH:MM:00, but they may be truncated on extraction. The seconds in `recorded_at` suggest the real values carry seconds that we are not shown.
- **A4, capacity.** A named human ID (T-B, T-C, T-D, T-F, T-G, T-H) is assumed to be one worker with capacity 1 unless there is evidence otherwise. RBT-7 has capacity 4 (fixture), and analyst_pool has at most 3 people.
- **A5, no hidden work.** The fragments are assumed to contain all of each resource's work in the window shown. Without this, utilisation statements are lower bounds.

**Diagnostic contrasts that matter most:**

- **Start or end equality alone cannot tell a physical batch from a logging batch.** Capacity separates them (G1: 4 ≤ 4). So does apparent concurrency growing or falling with rank (G2 rises 1→4, G3 falls 4→1). So does a shared `recorded_at` (G3, G6).
- **Exact adjacency with 1 s precision is itself suspicious.** Genuinely separate human scans rarely line up to the second. G4 and G8 need a check that the starts are not derived.
- **Coarsening is recognisable** by values lying on grid multiples, and by contradictions with a finer clock (G7: start before arrival; `recorded_at` spaced 1 min apart).

# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

Definitions: waiting = start − queue_arrival; service = complete − start; utilisation = Σ service / (capacity × window); bottleneck detection means ranking activities or resources by waiting, service, or utilisation. The minutes below are computed from the rows.

**G1, genuine fixture batch.**
- **Waiting:** 6, 5, 4 and 2 min. This time is real but consists of wait-to-fill (a batching policy), not congestion. A queue model that assumes item-by-item service will attribute it to lack of capacity, overstating the need for capacity.
- **Service:** 3 min per item is the cycle time. Effort per item is 0.75 fixture-minutes. If the batch goes unrecognised, per-item cost is overstated four times.
- **Utilisation:** read as a capacity-1 resource, this is 12 item-min in 3 min, i.e. 400%, which is impossible. With capacity 4 it is 100% of the fixture during the cycle.
- **Throughput:** 4 items per 3-minute cycle, arriving in bursts downstream. Downstream arrivals will look batched too.
- **Conformance:** no issue. Four simultaneous executions are legitimate.
- **Bottleneck:** there is a false-positive risk from utilisation >100% under capacity 1. The real lever is the fill policy (release partial batches?), not speed.

**G2, logging start batch.**
- **Waiting:** underestimated for S02–S04. Recorded waits are 18, 14, 11 and 6 min. Under the serial hypothesis they are 18, 22, 25 and 27 min. The bias grows with position in the group.
- **Service:** overestimated. Recorded 8, 14, 21, 27 (mean 17.5). Serial reconstruction gives 8, 6, 7, 6 (mean 6.75). Queueing inside the group is booked as work.
- **Utilisation:** 70 recorded service-min in 27 min gives 2.59 "concurrent inspections" for one inspector, an overstatement. The reconstructed value is 1.0.
- **Throughput:** unbiased. The completions are real.
- **Conformance:** temporal constraints such as "inspection ≤ 15 min" are falsely violated by S03 and S04.
- **Bottleneck:** inspection looks like a slow-service problem, when the evidence points to a queue before 10:00 (up to 4 waiting) plus normal ~7-minute service. The wrong fix would follow (speeding up inspection instead of starting groups earlier).

**G3, bulk submission.**
- **Waiting (arrival → start):** 22, 18, 19 and 19 min. Approximately unbiased, provided the starts are genuine.
- **Service:** overestimated for early items. Recorded 28, 20, 13, 6. Serial reconstruction gives 8, 7, 7, 6.
- **Hold time:** the 11:30 stamp mixes two things, the end of work and the hand-over. If 11:30 is the real release, then E01's 20 minutes between the end of its review (≈11:10) and release is batching delay. It belongs to waiting (hold) and not to service. If downstream starts are measured from 11:30, the downstream waiting is correct. If the analyst "corrects" completion to 11:10, downstream waiting gets inflated. So the correction depends on which question is being asked: effort (use the inferred done time) or flow (use the release time).
- **Utilisation:** busy/span = 2.39 for one reviewer, overstated.
- **Throughput:** 4 per 30 min is correct in total, but it looks like a pulse at 11:30.
- **Conformance:** any rule that E-cases may be released only after review is satisfied trivially. SLA checks on review duration are falsely violated.
- **Bottleneck:** Release review looks like a long-service capacity problem. The underlying issue is batching of the release.

**G4, serial single server under a burst.**
- **Waiting:** 7, 10, 13, 16 and 19 min. This is a genuine queue caused by the arrival rate (≈1/min) exceeding the service rate (1/4 min) during the burst. It is unbiased if A1 holds. The initial 7 minutes has an unknown cause.
- **Service:** 4 min each, unbiased if the starts are scanned. If the start is derived from the previous completion, any idle or setup time between items is booked as service. Service would then be slightly overstated and idle time hidden.
- **Utilisation:** 100% during 12:05–12:25, which is real for this window. It must not be extrapolated from a 20-minute burst.
- **Throughput:** 5 items in 20 min, capped by the server.
- **Conformance:** fine.
- **Bottleneck:** a genuine transient bottleneck. Calling it "sequential batching" (the PM4Py label) might push towards batch-size interventions, which are irrelevant here. The levers are arrival smoothing or temporary extra capacity.

**G5, shared account over parallel analysts.**
- **Waiting:** 15, 15, 16 and 15 min. Plausible. The suspicious constancy suggests a pickup or polling rule or a clock offset, which should be validated.
- **Service:** 10–25 min of elapsed case-open time. This overstates effort if analysts multitask (which is required, because 4 cases are open at once with at most 3 analysts).
- **Utilisation:** read as one resource, 70/30 = 233%, which is impossible. With 3 analysts it is 78%. With 2 analysts it is 117%, so multitasking is still required. Per-person utilisation cannot be identified.
- **Throughput:** correct at pool level.
- **Conformance:** four-eyes and segregation-of-duty checks, handover networks and working-together metrics are all unverifiable. Two different cases could have the same or different people behind them.
- **Bottleneck:** misranks. Either "one analyst doing 2.3× work" (hero or overload narrative) or, once treated as a pool, an unknown c. Without c, M/M/c-style reasoning is impossible.

**G6, bulk entry at 17:00.**
- **Waiting:** inflated. Recorded waits are 175, 148, 122, 110, 76, 58 and 35 min (mean ≈ 103.4).
- **Service:** 0 for all seven.
- **Why both at once:** the form writes the entry moment into both lifecycle fields. Waiting = start − arrival therefore absorbs everything that happened between arrival and data entry: the true waiting, the real closing work, and any post-work idle time before the form was filled. Service = complete − start = 17:00 − 17:00 = 0. Sojourn (arrival → 17:00) is preserved as an upper bound, but its split is fabricated. Only if the 17:00 sweep is real is the zero service roughly true (seconds per closure) and the long waits genuine. In that case the process has a real end-of-day batching policy, which is itself a finding.
- **Utilisation:** T-F appears 0% busy while a queue of up to 7 builds. The pattern "idle resource with a long queue" is diagnostic of missing execution data, not of laziness.
- **Throughput:** 0 closures per hour from 14:05 to 17:00, then 7 in one minute. Intraday throughput and WIP curves are fabricated. The daily count is fine.
- **Conformance:** events of the same case that follow the true closure (e.g., notifications) may appear before "Close ticket", producing false ordering violations. SLA-on-closure breaches are inflated.
- **Bottleneck:** Close ticket becomes the top waiting-time bottleneck with zero service, an artefact either way.

**G7, 15-minute coarsening.**
- **Waiting:** M01 +2, M02 −1, M03 −4, M04 −7 and M05 −11 min. These are negative or garbled. If averaged, they cancel against genuine waits elsewhere and bias mean waiting downward.
- **Service:** uniformly "15 min". Under a floor(start)/ceil(complete) rule, true service can be anything in (0, 15]. For M05, which arrived at 14:11, it is ≤ 4 min if complete ≤ 14:15. Under floor/floor with A2, completion ∈ [14:15, recorded_at], so service is ≤ ~16–20 min.
- **Utilisation:** 75 recorded min in a 15-minute window gives 500%, which is impossible. Once interval-censored, it is not identifiable.
- **Throughput:** 5 per quarter, correct only at quarter-hour granularity.
- **Conformance:** "start before arrival" produces false violations. Ordering against other activities within a quarter is undetermined.
- **Bottleneck:** unidentifiable. It is spuriously high if the 15 minutes are read as service.

**G8, backlog after a safety check.**
- **Waiting:** 38, 43, 48, 54 and 58 min. The dominant component (≈35–38 min) is unavailability. A steady-state queueing view attributes it to insufficient T-H capacity and overstates waiting caused by congestion.
- **Service:** 7 min each, unbiased (subject to the derived-start caveat).
- **Utilisation:** if the safety check is not in the log, T-H is 35/73 min ≈ 48% busy during 15:00–16:13 while five items wait, which is contradictory. Including the check (≈35 min), T-H is ≈ 96–100% occupied.
- **Throughput:** zero, then 5 in 35 min. The recovery rate is visible and useful.
- **Conformance:** if the safety check is a precondition, the clock mismatch could make the first triage appear to start before the check ended. At present there is a 3-minute margin (15:35 vs 15:38), which could vanish under a small offset.
- **Bottleneck:** there is a false structural-bottleneck risk. The fix is availability planning (schedule safety checks off-peak, or cover them with another person), not permanent capacity.

**Cross-cutting direction of bias:**
- **Logging start batches (G2):** waiting is too low and service too high.
- **Logging end batches (G3, G6):** service is too high (G3), or waiting too high with service at zero (G6, where the start is a placeholder).
- **Coarsening (G7):** the sign is undetermined, and some results are impossible.
- **Shared accounts (G5):** utilisation per resource is too high, and the social or conformance analysis is invalid.
- **Capacity loss (G8):** waiting is misattributed to capacity.

def max_concurrency(g, pad_s=0):
    ev = []
    for s, c in zip(g["start"], g["complete"]):
        s2, c2 = s + pd.Timedelta(seconds=pad_s), c - pd.Timedelta(seconds=pad_s)
        if c2 > s2:
            ev += [(s2, 1), (c2, -1)]
    ev.sort(key=lambda x: (x[0], x[1]))
    cur = best = 0
    for _, d in ev:
        cur += d
        best = max(best, cur)
    return best

def pair_stats(g):
    p = int(g["precision_s"].iloc[0])
    rows = list(g[["start", "complete"]].itertuples(index=False))
    n_pairs = certain = possible = 0
    for (s1, c1), (s2, c2) in itertools.combinations(rows, 2):
        n_pairs += 1
        lo, hi = max(s1, s2), min(c1, c2)
        if hi - lo > pd.Timedelta(seconds=2 * p):
            certain += 1            # overlap survives +-precision censoring
        if hi - lo > pd.Timedelta(seconds=-2 * p):
            possible += 1           # overlap cannot be excluded under censoring
    return n_pairs, certain, possible

def cluster_diag(g):
    g = g.sort_values(["start", "complete", "sequence_no"])

    wait = (g["start"] - g["queue_arrival"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60

    first_start = g["start"].min()

    return pd.Series({
        "n": len(g),
        "resource": g["resource"].iloc[0],
        "prec_s": p,
        "start_eq_grp": int(g["start"].value_counts().max()),

        "sec_visible": bool((all_ts.dt.second != 0).any()),

**What this shows:**

- **PM4Py assigns the same label, "Simultaneous", to a physical fixture batch (G1), a bulk-entry placeholder (G6) and a coarsening artefact (G7).** It calls both a burst queue (G4) and a post-outage backlog (G8) "Sequential batching". Its categories come from equality and adjacency tests on (start, complete) pairs for each activity–resource. They carry no information about capacity, source, precision or identity.
- **`merge_distance` has no effect here.** Each activity–resource pair has a single contiguous group, and PM4Py merges an interval if the gap is ≤ merge_distance, so the exact adjacency in G4 and G8 merges even at 0 s. `min_batch_size` only filters by count (min=5 removes the four-case clusters), which says nothing about semantics.
- **Replacing completion with `recorded_at` changes the labels** (G1 → start batching, G4/G8 → concurrent) because insertion seconds break exact equalities. PM4Py's category tests are exact-equality tests, so they are fragile at the level of individual seconds.
- **Flooring to 15 minutes turns G5 into "Batching on Start".** Coarsening fabricates batches.
- **Suspect rows are kept and flagged, never dropped.** The flags are the G6 zero-duration placeholders, the G7 start-before-arrival and coarse-precision rows, and the G5 shared-account rows. A missing start is filled with the completion for PM4Py only, under an explicit flag.

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

**A. Resource IDs trusted versus treated as possibly shared.**

| cluster | If IDs are trusted (one person or one machine) | If IDs may be shared accounts or proxy entry | Net effect on conclusions |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | One 4-slot machine, a simultaneous batch | Not applicable (a PLC cell) | Unchanged |
| G2 | One inspector with 4 overlapping inspections, so hidden serial starts are implied | If two inspectors share the tablet, staggered completions could be two parallel streams after a joint 10:00 start. Service would then be closer to the recorded values (e.g., S01 ‖ S02 …), and the serial reconstruction could understate service. | The top diagnosis drops to about 55 and physical concurrency becomes plausible |
| G3 | One reviewer; end batching is the only coherent reading | Several reviewers submitting through one T-C login could genuinely have reviewed in parallel, and the recorded service (28/20/13/6) could be real elapsed time | The bulk-submission reading weakens but remains likely, given the self-report |
| G4, G8 | One packer or triager in a single-server busy period | A shared scanner used alternately would still produce serial scans. Perfect adjacency is a single-server signature, so sharing is unlikely. | Robust |
| G5 | One person with 4 concurrent cases (233% busy), so the data is implausible unless it is heavy multitasking or open sessions | A pool of ≤3 analysts at 78% (c = 3) or 117% (c = 2). Parallel servers explain the non-FIFO completion. Multitasking is still required for the 4-way overlap. | Reading resource as identity is not defensible; only pool-level metrics are valid |
| G6 | T-F did the closing work, and the timing is unknown | T-F may be the clerk entering closures done by others (proxy entry), so even the performer is uncertain | Performer and timing are both unreliable, so it drops out of resource analysis |
| G7 | T-G measured all five | Any mix of measurers is possible | Unchanged; already unidentifiable |

**B. Timestamps taken as exact versus interval-censored by their stated precision.**

The censoring rules I use (assumption A6): a value t at precision p is taken to lie in [t − p, t + p], because the rounding direction is undocumented for the legacy MES and unknown elsewhere. The additional constraints are true start ≥ queue_arrival, true completion ≤ `recorded_at` (A2), and start ≤ complete.

- **G1 (p = 1 s).** The equalities hold to within ±1 s. The simultaneous-batch reading is unaffected.
- **G2 (p = 1 min).**
  - Start equality survives as "within about 2 minutes".
  - Completion gaps of 6–8 min are far larger than 2 min, so the stagger is real.
  - The censored bounds for the starts of S02–S04 without the serial assumption are [10:00 − 1, complete_i). S02's waiting ∈ [13, 28) min and its service ∈ (0, 15] min. The serial imputation is one point inside these bounds and not the only one.
- **G3 (p = 1 min).** Submission equality holds within 1 min (all `recorded_at` = 11:31). E01's inferred review-done time lies in (11:02, 11:30]. The serial hypothesis narrows it to ≈11:10. The waits (18–22 min) are robust to ±1 min.
- **G4 and G8 (p = 1 s).**
  - Adjacency is exact only if we trust that the values are HH:MM:00 (A3).
  - The `recorded_at` seconds (e.g., P01 at 12:09:04 vs P02 start 12:09) show that at 1-second resolution, "adjacency" could hide either a small overlap or a small gap. PM4Py then relabels them "Concurrent" (see Section 4).
  - The queueing conclusions (waits growing with position; 38–58 min in G8) are robust at any plausible precision.
- **G5 (p = 1 min).** 6/6 pairwise overlaps and a 4-way overlap (13:13–13:19) survive ±1-minute censoring. So "exceeds 3 analysts" is a robust finding, not a precision artefact.
- **G6 (p = 1 min, but semantically much worse).** Taken as exact, service is 0 and waiting 35–175 min. Censored by semantics rather than precision, completion_i ∈ [arrival_i, 17:00] and start_i ∈ [arrival_i, completion_i]. Waiting ∈ [0, 17:00 − arrival_i] and service ∈ [0, 17:00 − arrival_i]. The only hard constraint is that the total work of T-F on these seven is ≤ 175 min if T-F is a single serial worker. Nothing about the split survives.
- **G7 (p = 15 min).** Taken as exact: service is 15 min for all five, the batch is simultaneous, and waits are negative. Under censoring every one of these disappears.
  - Floor/ceil rule: start_i ∈ [max(arrival_i, 14:00), 14:15) and complete_i ∈ (start_i, 14:15].
  - Floor/floor rule: complete_i ∈ [14:15, recorded_at_i].
  - A nearest-quarter rule is inconsistent with M05 (arrival 14:11 > 14:07:30), unless the clocks are skewed. This is weak evidence that the start is floored.
  - The only order information left is `sequence_no`/`recorded_at` (1 min apart), which is consistent with serial entry and possibly serial execution.

**C. The same temporal pattern supports several physical explanations (at least three cases):**

1. **Identical start and identical end (G1, G6, G7; PM4Py "Simultaneous").** This fits a physical fixture batch (G1, capacity 4), a bulk-entry placeholder (G6, zero duration, one `recorded_at`), and grid coarsening (G7, 15-min values). Only metadata separates them: capacity, source type, precision, and lag. The time stamps alone cannot.
2. **Exact back-to-back adjacency with a growing queue (G4, G8; "Sequential batching").** This fits a single server clearing a random burst (G4), a backlog after an outage (G8), an operational wave or tote batch released as a group, or starts derived from the previous completion scan. Telling them apart needs availability records, group IDs and independent start scans.
3. **Common start with staggered ends (G2).** This fits a group-button logging artefact over serial work, a real common setup followed by serial work, parallel inspection with sequential sign-off, or two inspectors sharing one tablet ID.
4. **Staggered starts with a common end (G3).** This fits bulk submission after serial work, a genuine folder-level release (a real hold), or interleaved multitasking with a single close.
5. **Heavy irregular overlap (G5).** This fits several people behind one login, one person multitasking, or sessions left open across pauses. The data force at least some of the latter two (4 > 3).
6. **A long idle wait before the first start while items accumulate** (G1 6 min, G2 18 min, G4 7 min, G5 ~15 min, G8 38 min). This fits wait-to-fill batching policy, resource unavailability (G8's safety check), a clock offset between the upstream and local systems, or unlogged other work by the same resource (violating A5).

# 6. Data-collection redesign

In priority order, ranked by how much analytic bias each gap causes in this sample and how cheap the fix is:

| Priority | Item | Recommendation | Fixes / validates |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Execution vs. recording timestamps | Capture item-level start and complete at the moment of execution, generated by the device (scan, button, PLC). Store `recorded_at` separately, and never copy it into lifecycle fields. Add an `entry_mode` attribute (real-time / backfilled / bulk / imputed) and `entered_by`. Bulk forms must require (or at least allow) the time each item was actually done; otherwise leave the lifecycle fields NULL rather than filling placeholders. | G6 (placeholder start = complete), G3 (submission vs. work end), G2 |
| 2 | Resource / badge identity | Add badge-level identity to every action in shared VDI or pool accounts (SSO step-up or badge tap). Separate `performer` from `recorder`. Log pool staffing per shift (number of analysts logged in). | G5, G6 proxy entry, SoD/four-eyes conformance |
| 3 | Group / batch identifiers | Emit an explicit `batch_id` (fixture cycle, inspection group, folder, tote) together with batch-level events (group opened, batch started, batch released) and item-level events within the batch. Record the release policy (full, timeout). | G1 (wait-to-fill vs. congestion), G2, G3, G4 (wave vs. burst) |
| 4 | Source clocks | Synchronise all sources via NTP. Record `source_clock_id` and the measured offset per day. Put the safety system, the upstream queue system and the local sources on a common time base, or log offsets for correction. | G8 (safety-check attribution), G7 negative waits, G5's constant 15-min wait |
| 5 | Pause/resume and availability | Log suspend/resume per item, plus resource-availability events (breaks, safety checks, downtime, meetings, other work). This allows waiting to be split into "resource busy", "resource unavailable" and "batch formation". | G8, G5 multitasking, G2/G3 interleaving, utilisation denominators (A5) |
| 6 | Timestamp precision metadata | For each field and source, document the precision, the rounding rule (floor / ceil / nearest) and the estimated true accuracy. Export full resolution (seconds are currently missing for the 1-second sources). Replace or wrap the legacy MES so that it keeps raw times, with the quarter-hour value as a derived field. | G7, G4/G8 adjacency doubts (A3), G1 |
| 7 | Workstation capacity | Keep a master-data table of capacity per resource or station (fixture slots, staffed servers, allowed parallel cases per person), versioned over time. | G1 (4-slot), G5 (c ≤ 3), the utilisation denominator everywhere |
| 8 | Validation | Run a short observation study (e.g., one shift of time-and-motion per work area) to check the imputations and hypotheses above before scaling analysis. | G2, G3, G6, G8 |

# 7. Final adjudication

**High confidence, interpretable now:**
- **G1** is a genuine simultaneous fixture batch. Analyse it with batch-aware metrics: capacity 4, per-cycle utilisation, and waiting split into wait-to-fill and congestion.
- **G4** is a serial single server under an arrival burst. Waiting and service are usable, with the caveat that the start may be derived and the 7-minute initial delay is unexplained.
- **G8 (service only).** The 7-minute triage service times and the queue-clearing rate are usable.

**Exclude from, or interval-censor in, queue analysis:**
- **G6.** Exclude from service-time and utilisation. For waiting and sojourn, use only the interval completion ∈ [arrival, 17:00] and the sojourn upper bound. Never use zero service or the 35–175 min "waits" as point values.
- **G7.** Interval-censor everything. Exclude it from ordering, waiting and service point estimates, and do not report the negative waits. It can contribute at most to counts per quarter hour.
- **G2 and G3.** Keep them, but interval-censor the hidden lifecycle field: G2 starts ∈ [10:00, own complete), and G3 work completion ∈ (start, 11:30]. Report the serial reconstructions (service ≈ 6–8 min) as labelled scenarios, not as facts. Do not use recorded service times (up to 27–28 min) as effort.

**Require operational validation before any performance conclusion:**
- **G2:** does "begin group" precede serial inspection, or is there real common setup or parallel inspection?
