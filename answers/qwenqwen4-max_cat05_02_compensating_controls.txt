## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 is a *means*. Its end is: **no single human can cause an erroneous or fraudulent transfer to become irrevocable without an independent, competent, evidenced check.** Below, that end is decomposed into ten objectives/threat scenarios, with an honest mapping of what each compensating control can and cannot do.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A package is assessed against eight gates. Gates are **conjunctive on the case's dominant threat**, not additive: ten weak activities do not sum to one independent pre-irrevocability gate.

**G0 — Applicability.** Was there a *completed* payment (`Release_Payment`)? An attempt that was blocked (AP-205) is outside FEP-1's scope; it is a preventive success plus a behavioural signal, not a four-eyes violation.

**G1 — Authorization legitimacy.** Either FEP-1 satisfied by distinct humans, or *all seven* ED-1 conditions evidenced. A deviation that is substantively safe but unauthorized is still a governance breach (AP-201).

**G2 — Independence, substantively.** Distinct user IDs are necessary, not sufficient. Test: shared credentials; credential-assignment mismatch; unresolved conflict of interest (rating-setting supervisor); shared workstation/session; stable pair concentration; whether the second actor can be overruled or economically pressured by the first.

**G3 — Timing relative to the point of irrevocability.** Ordered strength: before `Release_Payment` > after release but before settlement *with verified hold/stop capability* > after settlement inside recall window > after recall window. The decisive boundary is the loss event, not the process step.

**G4 — Coverage of the dominant threat.** Identify the one or two mechanisms most likely to cause loss *in this case* (new vendor + bank change  O3; consulting/service payment  O5; outage  O10) and ask whether any control in the package actually addresses them. A clean AV-2 pass is strong evidence *only* for O2–O4/O7.

**G5 — Overrideability and override integrity.** Who could override, who did, whose credential, was the override authority independent of the beneficiary? A self-override collapses prevention and authorization into one actor.

**G6 — Evidence quality.** Documented review of invoice + bank details + supporting receipt; signed checklists; retrievable artefacts. "Manager_Ratify occurred"  "a competent review occurred."

**G7 — Recoverability.** Audit start inside the recall window, recall capability actually available, funds traceable, escalation path defined. Otherwise corrective capacity  0.

**G8 — Collusion resistance.** Rotation, dual custody, out-of-band vendor confirmation, independent sampling of the pair's population, device/session separation.

**Verdict rubric**

| Verdict | Test result |
|---|---|
| **Fully equivalent  OBJECTIVE_MET** | G0 completed; G1 satisfied (distinct humans, or all ED-1 conditions evidenced); G2 independence holds substantively; G3 independent human gate before release *or* before irrevocability with hold capability; G4 dominant threat covered; G5 no interested-party override; G6 documented; G7 audit in-window |
| **Partially equivalent  OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET** | A real independent gate before irrevocability with adequate coverage, but a bounded gap in G1 (authorization/necessity), G3 (post-release), G6 (review depth) or G7; residual risk concentrated in named uncovered threats; loss exposure low or recoverable |
| **Merely mitigating** | Probability/detectability improved, but no independent pre-irrevocability gate, or dominant threat uncovered. Maps to PARTIALLY_MET at best; to FAILED when the uncovered threat is also unrecoverable |
| **Ineffective  OBJECTIVE_FAILED** | Independence absent or defeated at the decisive moment **and** dominant threat uncovered **and** no recoverability; **or** a confirmed defect reached irrevocability. Benign outcome does not rescue it |
| **LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION** | No completed payment; a preventive control held; FEP-1 not applicable, residual behavioural inquiry remains |
| **INDETERMINATE** | A *decisive* fact has two plausible readings that change the verdict. Must be published with a decision rule, an interim conservative treatment, and the evidence that resolves it |

**Why "same expected loss" is not the only standard.** (a) *Verifiability*: expected loss cannot be computed where the evidence itself may be fabricated (AP-204) or missing (AP-208). (b) *Correlated failure*: expected-loss arithmetic assumes independent layers; when the preparer also releases and overrides, failures are perfectly correlated and the model is wrong, not merely optimistic. (c) *Authorization is deontic*: ED-1's preconditions define who may deviate; a lucky unauthorized deviation is still a breach of delegated authority. (d) *Tail non-fungibility*: sanctions/AML breaches, criminal fraud and reputational harm are not interchangeable with small expected euro amounts. (e) *Precedent and moral hazard*: tolerated convenience deviations (AP-201) and retro-fitted tokens (AP-206) change future behaviour and scale across the portfolio. (f) *Assurance and auditability*: external auditors and regulators need evidenced separation, not an internal loss estimate. (g) *Outcome bias*: AP-208 was legitimate and AP-204 was not — treating the first as acceptable because it happened to work inverts the logic of control assessment.

**Chosen semantics for "manager action after Release_Payment but before Bank_Settlement."** I evaluate controls against the **point of irrevocability**, not against the process-step boundary. Therefore such an action is a **hybrid: detective with respect to the process step, conditionally preventive with respect to the loss event.** It counts as preventive *only* when three things are evidenced: (i) the payment was still holdable/stoppable at that moment, (ii) the manager had authority to stop it, and (iii) the manager reviewed substance (invoice, bank details, receipt) rather than ratifying a completed fact. Absent (i)–(iii) it degrades to detective. This is why AP-207 (documented review, ED-1 explicitly permits pre-settlement approval) is MET *conditional on hold evidence*, AP-201 is only PARTIALLY_MET (independent and pre-settlement, but unauthorized and with a late audit), and AP-208's post-settlement ratification contributes nothing preventive at all. Reporting both boundaries separately — step-conformance and loss-event conformance — avoids the two symmetric errors of calling every post-release approval "too late" and calling every pre-settlement ratification "preventive."

## 5. Counterfactual and Residual-Risk Analysis

Likelihood bands are **ordinal judgements** (negligible / low / moderate / high / very high), not calibrated probabilities.

**AP-202 — diversion to a related-party account.** *Mechanism:* new vendor onboarded, bank details changed 2h before payment, no receipt; preparer suppresses the two warnings that encode exactly this threat using a credential belonging to his own rating-setting supervisor. *Chain-breaker that should have fired:* AV-2's block-or-independent-override path — the bank-change warning should have been non-overridable by the preparer, and the override credential should have been unusable by its non-owner. m09's ratification 2 days later broke nothing. *Still exposed:* the full EUR 480,000, plus the unresolved question of whether the supervisor was complicit or merely careless — which determines whether *any* control in this branch can be trusted. *Truly independent second releaser:* would very likely have refused a new-vendor payment with a 2-hour-old bank change and no receipt; likelihood of loss drops from **very high to low**, and even a compromised second releaser would have needed to defeat an out-of-band callback. Note the counterfactual is about *independence and coverage*, not headcount: a second releaser inside the same capture relationship would not have helped.

**AP-204 — fabricated service receipt inside a conforming pair.** *Mechanism:* consulting payment with no real deliverable; AV-2 matches PO data happily; the "second pair of eyes" shares a workstation and has approved 37 payments with the same partner in 30 days. *Chain-breaker:* RA-7 **did** break the concealment chain (it found the fabrication) but could not break the *loss* chain — detection came ~4 days after release. The control that would have broken the loss chain is an independent receipt-provenance check (vendor-side confirmation / three-way match with an independent receiver), which no control in the package performs. *Still exposed:* EUR 220,000 plus the unvalidated remainder of the 37-payment population — the correlated exposure is the real number. *Truly independent second releaser:* only helps if "independent" means outside the cell and empowered to challenge the receipt's existence; a rotating third human with the same documentation would likely still have passed it. Loss likelihood moves from **high to low-moderate**, and the residual is dominated by document authenticity, not by who clicks release.

**AP-206 — retro-fitted emergency authorization.** *Mechanism:* not payee fraud but **authorization laundering** — a real outage at 12:40, prepare at 13:00, token minted at 13:02. If tokens can be created after the fact, ED-1's ex-ante boundary becomes ex-post justification and every future convenience deviation can be dressed as an emergency. *Chain-breaker:* m11's independent pre-release approval genuinely covers the payment-integrity threat; nothing in the package covers the token-provenance threat except a system constraint that tokens cannot be backdated. *Still exposed:* EUR 95,000 payment risk is low; the governance exposure is the entire emergency population. *Truly independent second releaser:* would make the payment safe but would **not** fix the token issue — it would merely make it invisible, because the case would then conform nominally and never be reviewed. This is the clearest illustration that nominal conformance can *hide* a control-design defect.

**AP-207 — hold-window dependence.** *Mechanism:* single-operator release at 14:05; if the bank had executed immediately rather than settling at 16:00, m15's 14:40 review would have been purely detective and the payment unrecoverable before anyone independent looked at it. *Chain-breaker:* the settlement lag plus a documented, substantive m15 review; secondarily the in-window audit. *Still exposed:* the ~115-minute window is an *operational accident*, not a designed control; anchoring bias makes post-release rejection rarer than pre-release rejection. *Truly independent second releaser:* removes the dependence on the lag entirely — likelihood of an unrecoverable defective transfer falls from **low to negligible**. Recommendation: keep pre-settlement approval as a permitted fallback, but instrument the hold capability rather than assuming it.

**AP-208 — degraded-mode high-value release.** *Mechanism:* bank details changed 3 days earlier; AV-2 down; unsigned manual checklist; EUR 900,000 released by one operator; ratification only after settlement. The payment was legitimate — that is luck, not control. *Chain-breaker that should have fired:* a hard stop — ED-1 is unavailable (amount  100k, recent bank change, AV not operational), so the correct behaviour was to hold the payment or route it to two independent humans with an out-of-band bank confirmation. *Still exposed:* the whole EUR 900,000 was at risk with **zero** ownership evidence and zero recoverability; and every other payment during the same outage window is presumptively equally unprotected. *Truly independent second releaser:* necessary but **not sufficient** here — two humans with no ownership evidence and an unsigned checklist is shared guessing. What changes the outcome is evidence (callback to a pre-existing vendor contact) plus a hold rule; independence alone moves likelihood from **very high to moderate**, independence + out-of-band verification to **low**.

**AP-201 — convenience deviation, benign this time.** *Mechanism:* no second actor available or sought; a small office-supply payment happens to be correct; the independent review lands after release, and the audit lands 25h after release — one hour outside the recall window. *Chain-breaker:* m04's pre-settlement review is the only real gate; AV-2 covers the data-integrity threats. *Still exposed:* EUR 1,800 is trivial, but the *pattern* is not: an unauthorized same-user path with a habitually late audit is a template that scales to AP-202-sized amounts. *Truly independent second releaser:* removes both the loss risk and the precedent; likelihood of an unrecoverable defective payment goes from **low to negligible**, and — more importantly — the same-user rate stops normalizing.

**AP-209 — clean payment, unverified independence.** *Mechanism:* if the u12/u13 cell is not substantively independent, this payment had *one* effective decision-maker despite two signatures, and the absence of a detected defect is weak evidence (the same audit method missed nothing here but only caught AP-204 four days late). *Chain-breaker:* timely audit plus AV-2 cover data-integrity threats; nothing in the package tests the pair's independence. *Still exposed:* EUR 42,000 direct, plus correlated exposure across the cell's population. *Truly independent second releaser:* if drawn from outside the cell with separate session/device and receipt-challenge authority, likelihood of an undetected defective payment falls from **indeterminate (possibly moderate-high) to low**. Until the organizational design is produced, the honest position is that we cannot state which of the two worlds we are in.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

**Evidence to collect (making controls auditable, not merely present).**
1. **Review artefacts**, not events: for every MG-4, store the invoice, bank-detail screen, supporting receipt, reviewer level (L3+), and a structured checklist with an explicit reject option; record rejections — a manager who never rejects anything is a monitoring signal.
2. **Authentication and session attribution**: per-action user, device/workstation fingerprint, session ID, credential owner vs. credential user. This is the single artefact that would resolve AP-202 (credential sharing) and AP-204/AP-209 (shared workstation).
3. **Token provenance**: issuance time from the token service's own clock, issuer identity, linked incident ticket, clock-skew measurements, and an immutability rule forbidding backdating. Resolves AP-206.
4. **Irrevocability timeline**: bank submission, ack, settlement, hold/stop availability and authority, recall attempts and outcomes. Without this, "pre-settlement approval" cannot be classified as preventive (AP-201, AP-207).
5. **Outage records**: AV-2 availability intervals, affected releases, fallback authorizations, signed manual checklists (AP-208).
6. **Vendor-side evidence**: bank-change verification via a pre-existing contact, beneficial-ownership/related-party screening, receipt confirmation from an independent receiver.
7. **Organizational design**: mandate, rotation and reporting lines for payment cells; COI register including rating-setting relationships.

**Monitoring rules (alert  proof).**

| Rule | Suggested trigger | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Same-user rate | Any `Prepare`=`Release` without a valid pre-prepare token  per-case alert; monthly rate > ~1% of completed payments  trend alert | Unauthorized deviation is a *fact*; fraud is not implied (AP-201) |
| Override rate & self-override | Any override by the preparer/releaser, or credential owner  credential user  **critical alert**; override rate > ~2% of validations  review | AP-202 pattern; establishes a control-design breach, not the intent behind it |
| Late manager approval | Bucket approvals: pre-release / post-release-pre-settlement / post-settlement. Post-settlement share > 0 for same-user cases  alert | Timing fact; needs hold-capability evidence before judging sufficiency |
| Audit-before-recall-window | `audit_start  release + 24h` (and  next business day for ED-1); flag any miss; report "% same-user payments audited in-window" as a KPI | AP-201 missed by 1h; corrective capacity is a clock property, not an activity count |
| Repeated resource pairs | Pair share of a user's releases > ~50% over 30 days, or pair count  ~20/month, or pair Herfindahl above peer baseline  alert | AP-204/AP-209: 37/30 days. A dedicated cell is a legitimate explanation; the alert demands the design document, not a sanction |
| Shared workstation / session | Same device fingerprint or overlapping sessions across preparer and releaser  **high alert** | Strong independence-defeating indicator; still compatible with shift handover on a shared terminal |
| Bank-change proximity | Change  7 days  hard block / non-overridable by preparer;  30 days  enhanced verification; combine with vendor age < 180 days  escalate | AP-202 (2h), AP-208 (3d). Proximity is a risk factor, not evidence of wrongdoing |
| Control outage | Any release while AV-2 unavailable  **critical**; require dual independent authorization + signed checklist + hold option | AP-208. Also alert on unsigned checklists and on releases clustered in outage windows |
| ED-1 condition scoring | Compute all seven conditions per case; publish "conditions met / 7" and never a boolean "emergency: yes" | AP-208 fails 3; AP-206 fails 1 on the record |
| Attempted-but-blocked releases | Count `Attempt_Release`/blocks separately from FEP violations; alert on repeat attempts by the same user or same invoice across cases | AP-205 is a preventive success plus an inquiry, not a violation |
| Rubber-stamp detection | Approval latency < ~30s, zero rejection rate, approvals outside working hours, bulk approvals  review | Weak-evidence indicator for MG-4 |
| Related-party / employee-linkage screening | Vendor bank/account/address/phone matches to HR data  alert | AP-202's relative linkage; requires investigation before any conclusion |

**Alert vs. proof — explicit discipline.** Every rule above produces a *hypothesis with a named alternative explanation*: shared workstation  shift terminal; repeated pair  designed cell; late token  clock skew; bank change  legitimate vendor treasury update; override  documented business exception. An alert shifts the burden of evidence; it does not establish misconduct. Conversely, a **confirmed defect that reached irrevocability** (AP-204) or a **defeated independence mechanism** (AP-202) is a control finding regardless of whether intent is ever proven. Case conclusions should carry both a finding and a confidence level, and INDETERMINATE cases must carry a decision rule and an owner, not silence.

**PM4Py behaviour I cannot guarantee across versions.** (1) **Filter polarity**: `filter_four_eyes_principle` has been documented both as "keep cases satisfying the four-eyes principle" and as a compliance filter returning violators; the code therefore computes an independent pandas ground truth and *empirically reconciles* the returned set, complementing it if it matches the conforming set, and warns if it matches neither. (2) **Signature/parameters**: `stream_filter_key` and the accepted log type (`EventLog` vs. `DataFrame`) vary; `format_dataframe` may lack `resource_key` (handled via `inspect.signature`) and the historical `timest_format` spelling/parsing differs, so timestamps are pre-normalised to `+00:00` ISO strings. (3) **Resource semantics**: whether the filter uses `org:resource`, `org:group`, or a stream-specific attribute, and how it treats missing resources, is version-dependent — blanks are explicitly filled with `SYSTEM` here, and shared service accounts must be added to `SHARED_SERVICE_ACCOUNTS` because the filter cannot know they are not independent humans. (4) **Scope**: the filter has no notion of "completed payment", so `Attempt_Release` cases (AP-205) are excluded by intersecting with the completed-payment set rather than trusting the filter. (5) `convert_to_event_log` / `dataframe_utils.to_event_log` naming and case-attribute keys (`concept:name`) may differ; failures degrade gracefully to pandas with an explicit note. (6) The business-day rule uses `pd.tseries.offsets.BDay(1)` at the same clock time, which ignores holidays and cut-off times — it is an approximation of "one business day", and the 24-hour recall proxy is a separate, deliberately distinct column.
