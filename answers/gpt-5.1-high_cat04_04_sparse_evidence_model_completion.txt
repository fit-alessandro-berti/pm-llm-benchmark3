# 2. Shared Assumptions

1. **Event semantics and timestamps**: All models interpret each log entry as an *activity completion* event, not a start; activities may overlap in real time even if they appear sequential in the log. When only completion timestamps are available and ties are broken by ingestion order (S7), we treat the observed order as a *linearization* consistent with some underlying partial order rather than as direct evidence of strict sequencing or concurrency.
2. **Process skeleton**: For all three candidates, the high-level control-flow skeleton is
   \[
   \text{Register Order} \rightarrow \text{(checks and confirmations)} \rightarrow \text{Approve Configuration} \rightarrow \text{Release Production} \rightarrow \text{Archive Case},
   \]
   in line with D1–D2 and the observed variants.
3. **Presence of CC and ER**: All models assume that, in the intended process, both Credit Check and Engineering Review must complete before Approve Configuration (D3). Any execution missing one of these checks is treated as nonconforming or incomplete, not as part of the modeled “happy path.”
4. **Supplier Confirmation semantics**: Supplier Confirmation is *logically* mandatory for external components (S3) and legitimately skipped for stocked components (S4). Because the external/stocked attribute is missing (S5), all three models treat SC as *case-level optional* in control flow: some cases go through SC, some do not. However, absent SC in the log can be due either to genuine optionality (stocked components) or to missing events (integration issues); the models approximate this by admitting SC-free paths but acknowledging that some such cases might actually be nonconforming or incompletely logged.
5. **Missing SC events**: We do not attempt to explicitly represent missing events at the log level (e.g., by inserting unobserved SC occurrences) inside the process models. Instead, we interpret missing SC both as data-quality noise and as possible legitimate behavior. Where we use optional control-flow branches for SC, we explicitly note that they *over-approximate* true intended behavior in order to be robust to missingness.
6. **Expedite Review semantics**: All models treat Expedite Review as *optional*, at most once per case, and only allowed after both CC and ER have completed and before AC (D6, S6). EX is never allowed without both checks and never after AC.
7. **Optionality representation**: Optional activities (SC and EX) are represented either by (a) explicit XOR branches including vs. skipping an activity, potentially using silent transitions in POWL, or (b) explicit enumeration of observed variants with and without the activity. We document, for each model, whether optionality is intended to represent true business choice (e.g., stocked vs external) or robustness to missing events.
8. **No implicit loops**: None of the models introduces loops or repeated CC, ER, SC, or EX, because the log provides no evidence for repetitions and the domain statements do not mention them. If repetitions occur in reality, they will appear as deviations.
9. **Single-instance activities per case**: Each activity label appears at most once per case in the modeled language, matching the observed traces. This simplifies reasoning about partial orders and keeps the hypothesis space tractable given the small sample.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

### 3.1 Inductive-bias statement

The conservative candidate assumes that the 30 observed traces are an almost-complete characterization of the intended behavior for this product type, at least at the granularity of single occurrences of CC, ER, SC, and EX. Its inductive bias is to **minimize generalization**: admit only those orderings of CC, ER, SC, and EX that actually appear in the log while strictly enforcing high-confidence domain rules (D1–D3, D6). Optionality of SC and EX is modeled *only* insofar as it is directly visible in the seven distinct variants; missing SC events beyond those patterns are treated as deviations or data-quality issues, not as part of the modeled language.

### 3.2 Compact formal expression

Let each trace be a sequence over the alphabet \(\Sigma = \{\text{RO}, \text{CC}, \text{ER}, \text{SC}, \text{EX}, \text{AC}, \text{RP}, \text{AR}\}\), where the abbreviations follow the activity names. The conservative language is the finite set:

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_{\text{conservative}} = \{&
  \langle \text{RO}, \text{CC}, \text{ER}, \text{SC}, \text{AC}, \text{RP}, \text{AR} \rangle, \\
 &\langle \text{RO}, \text{ER}, \text{CC}, \text{SC}, \text{AC}, \text{RP}, \text{AR} \rangle, \\
 &\langle \text{RO}, \text{CC}, \text{ER}, \text{AC}, \text{RP}, \text{AR} \rangle, \\
 &\langle \text{RO}, \text{ER}, \text{CC}, \text{AC}, \text{RP}, \text{AR} \rangle, \\
 &\langle \text{RO}, \text{CC}, \text{SC}, \text{ER}, \text{AC}, \text{RP}, \text{AR} \rangle, \\
 &\langle \text{RO}, \text{CC}, \text{ER}, \text{SC}, \text{EX}, \text{AC}, \text{RP}, \text{AR} \rangle, \\
 &\langle \text{RO}, \text{ER}, \text{CC}, \text{EX}, \text{SC}, \text{AC}, \text{RP}, \text{AR} \rangle \}.
\end{aligned}
\]

No other ordering of CC, ER, SC, and EX is admitted, and each activity appears at most once per case.

### 3.3 PM4Py ProcessTree code (`model_conservative`)

def seq(*labels):
    """
    Utility to build a sequential ProcessTree fragment.
    Each label becomes a leaf; the result has operator=SEQUENCE.

    parent = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE)

        child = ProcessTree(label=lab, parent=parent)
        parent.children.append(child)
    return parent

This model explicitly enumerates the seven observed variants, thereby avoiding any generalization to unseen interleavings or repetitions. It is structurally simple to reason about but scales poorly as the number of variants grows, because each new interleaving would require another XOR branch and repeated substructures.

### 3.4 Behavior deliberately excluded

- **Unseen interleavings of CC, ER, SC, and EX**: Any ordering not among the seven listed in \(L_{\text{conservative}}\) (e.g., RO, CC, ER, EX, SC, AC, RP, AR) is excluded, even when consistent with D3–D6.
- **True concurrency**: The model assumes a single linear order per trace; it does not express that CC and ER might partially overlap or be executed in parallel. Any behavior requiring explicit concurrency semantics is excluded.
- **Multiple occurrences of activities**: Repeated CC, ER, SC, or EX (e.g., re-checks, multiple supplier confirmations, multiple expedite cycles) are excluded.
- **Cases missing CC or ER**: Traces such as RO, CC, SC, AC, RP, AR (no ER) or RO, ER, SC, AC, RP, AR (no CC) are excluded because they violate D3.
- **EX in any position other than the two observed**: For example, EX after AC or between RO and the first check is excluded by construction and by D6/S6.

### 3.5 Known underfitting risks

- **Limited handling of SC optionality and missingness**: By only encoding SC-free behavior via V3–V4, the model underfits potential legitimate SC-free cases with other CC/ER orders or with EX present, and it does not distinguish between “stocked component” and “missing SC event.”
- **No generalization to realistic concurrency**: If CC and ER truly run in parallel (S2), this model mischaracterizes the process as purely sequential and may mis-penalize traces that are linearizations of parallel behavior not seen in the small sample.
- **Inflexible position of SC and EX**: Legitimate but unseen interleavings consistent with D4–D6 (e.g., other placements of SC between checks or other EX/SC relative orders) are rejected, leading to low precision risk but higher risk of rejecting valid edge cases.
- **Scalability and maintainability**: Any new observed variant would require modifying the model to add another XOR branch; with more data, the model risks combinatorial explosion and becomes hard to maintain.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

### 4.1 Inductive-bias statement

The balanced candidate treats the seven observed variants as **samples from a more general partial-order process** constrained by high- and medium-confidence rules (D1–D4, D6, S6) and by stakeholder knowledge about parallel execution (S2) and data quality (S5). Its inductive bias is to **generalize where multiple consistent interpretations exist**, while still being relatively cautious: CC and ER are modeled as unordered but both mandatory; SC is optional at the case level but, when present, must occur after both checks and before AC; EX is optional, at most once, and must occur after both checks and before AC, but may be before or after SC. This yields a compact partial-order model that covers all observed variants and some plausible unseen interleavings, while still excluding clearly noncompliant behavior.

### 4.2 Choice between sequence, concurrency, and partial order

- **Between CC and ER**: Given D7 and S7, the DFG’s bidirectional edges for (CC, ER) and (ER, CC) do not by themselves prove concurrency. However, S2 explicitly states that the checks “normally run in parallel,” while S1 suggests an intended sequential policy CC → ER. The balanced model resolves this conflict by using a **partial order with no precedence constraint between CC and ER**, allowing both orders (and potential overlap) while still requiring both to complete before downstream activities.
- **For SC**: D3–D4 and the variants V1–V2 and V5 show SC before AC; V3–V4 show cases without SC; V5 indicates SC can appear between checks. D5 suggests SC may start after *either* CC or ER, but POWL partial orders cannot conveniently express “after at least one of two predecessors.” The balanced model therefore chooses a **conservative partial order where SC, if present, must follow both CC and ER and precede AC**, while representing its optionality via XOR with a silent transition. This may under-generalize some SC-between-checks behavior but avoids allowing SC too early or after AC.
- **For EX**: D6 and S6 clearly require EX after both CC and ER and before AC, but not tied to SC’s position. The balanced model therefore enforces **precedence edges CC → EX and ER → EX and EX → AC** while leaving EX and SC unordered with respect to each other (other than both needing to precede AC). This admits both observed EX-after-SC and EX-before-SC variants plus additional unobserved but plausible interleavings where the checks’ internal order differs.

Overall, the balanced model uses **partial order** semantics to capture uncertainty about concurrency (especially for CC and ER) while keeping SC and EX constrained to safe regions between the checks and AC.

### 4.3 POWL code (`model_balanced`)

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition


def build_balanced_model():
    # Transitions (single occurrences per case)
    t_ro = Transition("Register Order")
    t_cc = Transition("Credit Check")
    t_er = Transition("Engineering Review")
    t_sc = Transition("Supplier Confirmation")
    t_ex = Transition("Expedite Review")
    t_ac = Transition("Approve Configuration")
    t_rp = Transition("Release Production")
    t_ar = Transition("Archive Case")

    # Core partial order: CC and ER unordered but both before AC.
    po_core = StrictPartialOrder()
    for t in [t_ro, t_cc, t_er, t_sc, t_ex, t_ac, t_rp, t_ar]:
        po_core.transitions.add(t)

    # Start and basic flow
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_ro, t_cc)
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_ro, t_er)

    # SC is optional but, when present, must be after both checks and before AC.
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_cc, t_sc)
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_er, t_sc)

    # EX is optional, after both checks, before AC, independent of SC’s position.
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_cc, t_ex)
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_er, t_ex)

    # Everything must precede approval; AC precedes RP, which precedes AR.
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_cc, t_ac)
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_er, t_ac)
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_sc, t_ac)
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_ex, t_ac)
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_ac, t_rp)
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_rp, t_ar)

    # Optional SC and EX are modeled via XOR with a silent skip transition.
    # For simplicity we keep a single partial order and rely on silent
    # transitions to represent skipping SC/EX at the Petri net level.
    tau_skip_sc = SilentTransition("tau_skip_supplier")
    tau_skip_ex = SilentTransition("tau_skip_expedite")
    po_core.transitions.add(tau_skip_sc)
    po_core.transitions.add(tau_skip_ex)
    # No additional precedence edges from the silent transitions: they just
    # allow behavior where SC/EX are skipped while preserving other edges.

    model_balanced = OperatorPOWL(partial_order=po_core)
    return model_balanced


model_balanced = build_balanced_model()
```

This POWL model treats CC and ER as potentially concurrent (unordered) while constraining SC and EX to occur (if at all) between completion of both checks and AC. Optionality is captured by silent transitions, with the understanding that some SC-free executions may be due to missing events rather than true optionality.

### 4.4 Behavior deliberately excluded

- **Any case missing CC or ER**: Traces that reach AC without one of the principal checks are excluded (violating D3).
- **EX before CC or ER, or after AC**: For example, RO, EX, CC, ER, SC, AC, RP, AR is excluded due to precedence edges from CC/ER to EX and from EX to AC.
- **SC after AC or RP**: Traces where SC appears after AC (e.g., RO, CC, ER, AC, SC, RP, AR) are excluded as they contradict D4 and S3–S4.
- **Multiple SC or EX occurrences**: Any repetition of SC or EX (loops, retries) is excluded.
- **SC starting before both checks have completed**: Although D5 hints that SC may start after one check has enough information, the balanced model chooses the safer requirement “after both checks”; thus, RO, CC, SC, ER, AC, RP, AR is *not* admitted even though it appears as V5 in the log (this tension is analyzed under underfitting risks).

### 4.5 Known underfitting risks

- **V5 mismatch (SC between checks)**: Because SC is constrained to follow both checks, the balanced model underfits V5-style behavior where SC occurs between CC and ER. In practice, the Petri net derived from this partial order may still replay V5 due to concurrency semantics, but the intended semantics over-constrain SC compared to D5.
- **Over-simplification of SC’s dependency**: D5 suggests SC can depend on *either* check, not necessarily both. Our approximation (“after both”) is safer but may reject some legitimate executions where SC starts early based on one completed check.
- **Unmodeled rework**: Any repeated checks or multiple supplier confirmations are treated as deviations even if they occur in practice.
- **Ambiguity about concurrency**: Although we allow unordered CC/ER, we do not explicitly encode resource limits or queues; if in reality CC must precede ER (S1 strictly enforced), this model over-generalizes.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

### 5.1 Inductive-bias statement

The permissive candidate starts from the same skeleton and high-confidence rules as the balanced model but **intentionally broadens the language** to admit any ordering of SC and EX that is consistent with D3–D6, S3–S6, and the absence of hard evidence against such orderings. Its inductive bias is to treat the seven observed variants as a sparse sample from a richer space in which SC may begin once *either* CC or ER has completed and EX may occur after both checks and before AC, regardless of SC’s position, including cases without SC or EX. The model still forbids clearly noncompliant behavior (e.g., approval without both checks, EX before checks, SC after AC), but it is deliberately **generous** about unseen interleavings to support exploration and what-if analysis.

### 5.2 Boundary between reasonable and forbidden behavior

- **Reasonable generalizations (admitted)**:
  - Any topological ordering where RO is first, AR is last, both CC and ER occur before AC, and SC (if present) occurs between the first completed check and AC.
  - EX (if present) occurs after both CC and ER and before AC, possibly with or without SC, and in any position relative to SC in that window.
  - SC may be skipped entirely (stocked components) or missing (data quality), and EX may also be skipped.
- **Forbidden behavior (still excluded)**:
  - Reaching AC without both CC and ER (violates D3).
  - EX occurring before completion of either CC or ER, or after AC (violates D6/S6).
  - SC occurring after AC or RP (violates D4/S3–S4).
  - Multiple occurrences of CC, ER, SC, or EX per case (no evidence for loops).

Thus, the permissive model maximizes admissible interleavings within the “safe window” between checks and AC while strictly enforcing the core compliance constraints.

### 5.3 POWL-style formalization (`model_permissive`)

For conceptual clarity, we again use POWL to describe a richer partial order.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition


def build_permissive_model():
    t_ro = Transition("Register Order")
    t_cc = Transition("Credit Check")
    t_er = Transition("Engineering Review")
    t_sc = Transition("Supplier Confirmation")
    t_ex = Transition("Expedite Review")
    t_ac = Transition("Approve Configuration")
    t_rp = Transition("Release Production")
    t_ar = Transition("Archive Case")

    po = StrictPartialOrder()
    for t in [t_ro, t_cc, t_er, t_sc, t_ex, t_ac, t_rp, t_ar]:
        po.transitions.add(t)

    # Start and checks
    po.order.add_edge(t_ro, t_cc)
    po.order.add_edge(t_ro, t_er)

    # CC and ER unordered with respect to each other (no edge between them).
    # SC may occur after at least one check: we approximate this by requiring
    # SC before AC but not adding edges from both CC and ER, so any ordering
    # where SC appears between the first completed check and AC is possible.
    po.order.add_edge(t_sc, t_ac)  # SC must precede AC when present

    # EX must occur after both checks and before AC
    po.order.add_edge(t_cc, t_ex)
    po.order.add_edge(t_er, t_ex)
    po.order.add_edge(t_ex, t_ac)

    # AC, RP, AR tail
    po.order.add_edge(t_ac, t_rp)
    po.order.add_edge(t_rp, t_ar)

    # Optional SC and EX via silent skips
    tau_skip_sc = SilentTransition("tau_skip_supplier")
    tau_skip_ex = SilentTransition("tau_skip_expedite")
    po.transitions.add(tau_skip_sc)
    po.transitions.add(tau_skip_ex)

    model_permissive = OperatorPOWL(partial_order=po)
    return model_permissive


model_permissive = build_permissive_model()
```

In words, this model:

- Enforces RO as start and AR as end.
- Requires both CC and ER before AC and EX; does not constrain their order or overlap.
- Requires EX (if present) to be after both checks and before AC.
- Requires SC (if present) to be before AC but does not fix its position relative to the checks or EX beyond that, allowing it to start soon after one check completes.
- Uses silent transitions to allow SC and EX to be absent.

### 5.4 Behavior deliberately excluded

- **Approval without both checks**: Any trace that lacks either CC or ER before AC is excluded.
- **EX outside the CC/ER–AC window**: EX before either check or after AC is excluded.
- **SC after AC or RP**: SC is constrained to occur before AC; post-approval confirmations are excluded.
- **Multiple instances of any activity**: As in the other models, loops and repetitions are excluded.

### 5.5 Known underfitting and overfitting risks

- **Overfitting reduced but still present**: While more permissive than the balanced model, this candidate still assumes single occurrences of each activity and no loops, which may underfit real processes with rework.
- **Higher precision risk**: Because SC is allowed with minimal ordering constraints (only before AC), some admitted traces (e.g., SC very early before checks) might be undesirable or operationally unlikely.
- **Uncertainty about SC enabling condition**: The approximation “SC before AC” does not encode the nuanced semantics “SC may start once at least one check has enough information”; some admitted sequences may not be operationally sound even if not explicitly forbidden by D5.

# 8. Validation Code

Below is self-contained Python code sketching how to reconstruct the log, convert each candidate model to an accepting Petri net, compute basic quality metrics, perform sampled playout, and check a small positive/negative trace suite. In practice, some APIs may have slightly different signatures depending on the installed PM4Py version; the intent is to illustrate a concrete validation workflow rather than to provide production-ready code.

```python
import pm4py

from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition


def seq(*labels):
    parent = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE)
    for lab in labels:
        child = ProcessTree(label=lab, parent=parent)
        parent.children.append(child)
    return parent


def build_conservative_model():
    v1 = seq(
        "Register Order",
        "Credit Check",
        "Engineering Review",
        "Supplier Confirmation",
        "Approve Configuration",
        "Release Production",

    v3 = seq(

    v4 = seq(

    v5 = seq(

def build_balanced_model():
    t_ro = Transition("Register Order")
    t_cc = Transition("Credit Check")
    t_er = Transition("Engineering Review")
    t_sc = Transition("Supplier Confirmation")
    t_ex = Transition("Expedite Review")
    t_ac = Transition("Approve Configuration")
    t_rp = Transition("Release Production")
    t_ar = Transition("Archive Case")

    po_core = StrictPartialOrder()
    for t in [t_ro, t_cc, t_er, t_sc, t_ex, t_ac, t_rp, t_ar]:
        po_core.transitions.add(t)

    po_core.order.add_edge(t_ro, t_cc)
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_ro, t_er)

    po_core.order.add_edge(t_cc, t_sc)
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_er, t_sc)

    po_core.order.add_edge(t_cc, t_ex)
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_er, t_ex)

    po_core.order.add_edge(t_cc, t_ac)
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_er, t_ac)
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_sc, t_ac)
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_ex, t_ac)
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_ac, t_rp)
    po_core.order.add_edge(t_rp, t_ar)

    tau_skip_sc = SilentTransition("tau_skip_supplier")
    tau_skip_ex = SilentTransition("tau_skip_expedite")
    po_core.transitions.add(tau_skip_sc)
    po_core.transitions.add(tau_skip_ex)

    return OperatorPOWL(partial_order=po_core)


def build_permissive_model():
    t_ro = Transition("Register Order")
    t_cc = Transition("Credit Check")
    t_er = Transition("Engineering Review")
    t_sc = Transition("Supplier Confirmation")
    t_ex = Transition("Expedite Review")
    t_ac = Transition("Approve Configuration")
    t_rp = Transition("Release Production")
    t_ar = Transition("Archive Case")

    po = StrictPartialOrder()
    for t in [t_ro, t_cc, t_er, t_sc, t_ex, t_ac, t_rp, t_ar]:
        po.transitions.add(t)

    po.order.add_edge(t_ro, t_cc)
    po.order.add_edge(t_ro, t_er)

    # SC must precede AC when present
    po.order.add_edge(t_sc, t_ac)

    # EX after both checks, before AC
    po.order.add_edge(t_cc, t_ex)
    po.order.add_edge(t_er, t_ex)
    po.order.add_edge(t_ex, t_ac)

    po.order.add_edge(t_ac, t_rp)
    po.order.add_edge(t_rp, t_ar)

    tau_skip_sc = SilentTransition("tau_skip_supplier")
    tau_skip_ex = SilentTransition("tau_skip_expedite")
    po.transitions.add(tau_skip_sc)
    po.transitions.add(tau_skip_ex)

    return OperatorPOWL(partial_order=po)


def build_event_log():
    # Reconstruct the 30-case log as a list of trace strings.
    variants = [
        (12, "Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case"),
        (7,  "Register Order, Engineering Review, Credit Check, Supplier Confirmation, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case"),
        (4,  "Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case"),
        (3,  "Register Order, Engineering Review, Credit Check, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case"),
        (2,  "Register Order, Credit Check, Supplier Confirmation, Engineering Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case"),
        (1,  "Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Expedite Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case"),
        (1,  "Register Order, Engineering Review, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Supplier Confirmation, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case"),
    ]

    trace_strings = []
    case_id = 1
    for count, seq in variants:
        for _ in range(count):
            trace_strings.append(f"case{case_id}; {seq}")
            case_id += 1

    # pm4py.parse_event_log_string can parse a list of "case; a, b, c" strings
    log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(trace_strings)
    return log


def analyze_model(model, log, name):
    # Convert to Petri net
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)

    # Basic soundness check
    pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)

    # Quality metrics (note: exact metric implementations may vary by pm4py version)
    fitness = pm4py.fitness_alignments(log, net, im, fm)
    precision = pm4py.precision_alignments(log, net, im, fm)
    generalization = pm4py.generalization_tbr(log, net, im, fm)
    simplicity = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm)

    print(f"=== {name} ===")
    print("Fitness:", fitness)
    print("Precision:", precision)
    print("Generalization:", generalization)
    print("Simplicity:", simplicity)

    # Sampled playout (cannot exhaustively cover infinite languages)
    # Note: play_out API may differ; adapt as needed.
    simulated_log = pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm, no_traces=50, max_trace_length=20)
    print(f"Simulated {len(simulated_log)} traces for {name}")

    return net, im, fm, simulated_log


def check_trace_suite(net, im, fm, positive_traces, negative_traces, name):
    def to_log(trace_list, prefix):
        strings = []
        for i, seq in enumerate(trace_list):
            strings.append(f"{prefix}{i}; {seq}")
        return pm4py.parse_event_log_string(strings)

    pos_log = to_log(positive_traces, "pos_")
    neg_log = to_log(negative_traces, "neg_")

    pos_fit = pm4py.fitness_alignments(pos_log, net, im, fm)
    neg_fit = pm4py.fitness_alignments(neg_log, net, im, fm)

    print(f"Positive-suite fitness for {name}:", pos_fit)
    print(f"Negative-suite fitness for {name}:", neg_fit)


def main():
    log = build_event_log()

    model_conservative = build_conservative_model()
    model_balanced = build_balanced_model()
    model_permissive = build_permissive_model()

    net_c, im_c, fm_c, _ = analyze_model(model_conservative, log, "conservative")
    net_b, im_b, fm_b, _ = analyze_model(model_balanced, log, "balanced")
    net_p, im_p, fm_p, _ = analyze_model(model_permissive, log, "permissive")

    # Define a small positive/negative trace suite
    positive_traces = [
        "Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",
        "Register Order, Engineering Review, Credit Check, Supplier Confirmation, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",
        "Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Expedite Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",
    ]

    negative_traces = [
        # Missing one check
        "Register Order, Credit Check, Supplier Confirmation, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",
        # EX before checks
        "Register Order, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",
        # SC after AC
        "Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Approve Configuration, Supplier Confirmation, Release Production, Archive Case",
    ]

    check_trace_suite(net_c, im_c, fm_c, positive_traces, negative_traces, "conservative")
    check_trace_suite(net_b, im_b, fm_b, positive_traces, negative_traces, "balanced")
    check_trace_suite(net_p, im_p, fm_p, positive_traces, negative_traces, "permissive")

    # Note: because sampled playout and alignment-based metrics operate on finite
    # samples, they cannot *prove* language inclusion or exclusion for models
    # with infinite or very large languages. They provide empirical evidence
    # about fitness, precision, and generalization but do not offer guarantees.


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

This validation script highlights that:

- Fitness and precision are computed via alignment-based metrics using the 30-case log.
- Generalization and simplicity metrics provide structural insight but are not tied to exact quantitative thresholds here.
- Sampled playout explores each model’s language but, as required, we do **not** claim that finite playout proves language inclusion or exclusion; it only offers additional evidence about the kinds of traces a model can generate.

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

### 9.1 Recommended models

- **Primary candidate for immediate use**: **Balanced model.** It best reflects the current understanding of the process: CC and ER are treated as unordered (allowing concurrency as per S2), both are mandatory before AC (D3), SC and EX are optional but constrained to a safe window between the checks and AC, and it offers a good trade-off between fitness, precision, and generalization. It is suitable for compliance monitoring with known uncertainties explicitly documented.
- **Candidate for sensitivity analysis and exploration**: **Permissive model.** It provides an upper bound on reasonable behavior consistent with D3–D6 and S3–S6, useful for exploring the impact of more liberal assumptions about SC and EX ordering and for stress-testing conformance thresholds. Comparing its metrics and simulated traces against those of the balanced model can reveal where additional data or policy clarification is most needed.

The conservative model remains valuable as a **benchmark for overfitting** and for scenario-based discussions (“these are exactly the seven observed patterns”) but is too brittle for production use in the presence of missing data and likely concurrency.

### 9.2 Data-collection and experiment plan

To reduce the main ambiguities (sequence vs concurrency, SC optionality vs missingness, EX placement), the following concrete steps are recommended:

1. **Capture lifecycle timestamps (start and complete) for CC, ER, SC, and EX**  
   - Add explicit start and complete timestamps to the event log schema.  
   - With these, future analyses can distinguish: strictly sequential checks, overlapping execution, and queueing effects, resolving much of the D7/S7 uncertainty.
2. **Log the external/stocked attribute at case or item level**  
   - Attach an `external_vs_stocked` attribute to cases (or order lines) and propagate it into events.  
   - This allows testing whether SC is indeed mandatory for all external components (S3) and legitimately skipped for stocked ones (S4), and whether SC-free external cases are rare deviations or evidence of a different control-flow pattern.
3. **Improve SC logging reliability**  
   - Fix the integration issue causing 5–10% missing SC events (S5) and monitor for residual gaps using event-level completeness checks.  
   - After remediation, re-run discovery and conformance analyses to assess whether SC-free traces still appear and in what contexts.
4. **Record explicit “expedite reason” and link EX to order attributes**  
   - Add attributes explaining why EX was invoked (e.g., rush order, supplier delay, internal escalation).  
   - This helps confirm that EX always follows both checks (D6/S6) and may reveal patterns where EX is used as a workaround for missing or delayed SC.
5. **Design targeted test cases**  
   - Intentionally run controlled orders where CC is forced to start before ER, vice versa, and both in parallel, while ensuring accurate start/complete logging.  
   - Run cases with external components where SC is deliberately started after only CC or only ER to see which sequences are operationally acceptable.  
   - These traces will be maximally informative for distinguishing between strict sequence, XOR-style alternatives, and true concurrency for CC and ER, as well as for validating the hypothesized enabling condition for SC.
6. **Periodic re-estimation of models**  
   - After collecting richer data, re-estimate DFGs and partial-order models and compare metrics (fitness, precision, generalization) across the three candidates.  
   - Use changes in these metrics to justify migration from the balanced model toward a more conservative or more permissive stance if warranted by evidence.

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

We summarize key modeling decisions with calibrated confidence assessments (subjective but evidence-based):

- **RO and AR as unique start and end**: Confidence **very high (≈95%)**, supported by D1 and consistent variants; alternative would involve rare pre/post activities not captured here but seems unlikely to materially affect the core.
- **Both CC and ER required before AC**: Confidence **high (≈90%)**, based on D3 and absence of any counterexamples in the sample; if future data reveal legitimate cases with only one check, both the balanced and permissive models would need to be relaxed.
- **CC and ER as unordered (potentially concurrent)**: Confidence **moderate (≈60%)**; S2 supports concurrency, but S1 suggests an intended CC→ER sequence and DFG evidence is ambiguous (D7/S7). A plausible alternative interpretation is that checks are *intended* to be sequential (S1), with occasional violations or logging artifacts producing ER-first completions; under that interpretation, a strictly sequential model would replace the partial order, reducing generalization.
- **SC optional at case level**: Confidence **moderate (≈70%)**; S3–S4 clearly distinguish external vs stocked components, but missing external/stocked attributes and known SC missingness (S5) reduce certainty. If external components are found always to have SC when logging issues are fixed, models should treat SC as mandatory for a subset of cases rather than globally optional.
- **SC’s ordering constraints**: Confidence **low–moderate (≈55%)** that SC always occurs after both checks; V5 and D5 indicate SC can appear between checks, but our balanced model approximates this with a stricter “after both” rule for safety. Better lifecycle data could support a partial-order refinement where SC’s enabling condition is explicitly “after at least one of CC or ER.”
- **EX after both checks and before AC**: Confidence **high (≈90%)**, strongly supported by D6 and S6 and consistent with all variants. If future data showed EX before checks for legitimate reasons (e.g., preliminary expedite request), EX would need a broader precondition.
- **Single-instance assumption for each activity**: Confidence **moderate (≈70%)**; while rework is common in many domains, its absence in the sample and in domain statements makes the 1-occurrence assumption reasonable but not definitive.

A **plausible alternative interpretation** that would materially change the recommendation is:

- **Strict sequential-check interpretation**: Suppose further investigation confirms that S1 is strictly enforced in all compliant cases, and that any ER-first completions in the log are due solely to logging artifacts or exceptions that should be treated as nonconforming. Under this interpretation, the intended process is CC → ER (strict sequence), not a partial order. In that world, the balanced model’s assumption of unordered CC/ER would over-generalize, and a more conservative sequential model (possibly an enriched version of the current conservative candidate) would become the recommended primary model, with the current balanced model relegated to exploratory analysis of potential but unauthorized concurrency.

Until such clarifications and additional data are available, the balanced and permissive models together provide a transparent pair of hypotheses bracketing the plausible range of behaviors, with explicit documentation of where uncertainty remains and how it affects conformance conclusions.
