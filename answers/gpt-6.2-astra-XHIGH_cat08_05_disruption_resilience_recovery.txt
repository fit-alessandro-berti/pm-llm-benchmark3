# 1. Disruption timeline

Hospital supply was the strongest service outcome. During manual operations, hospitals retained a 91% fill rate and their median cycle time increased by two hours. Pharmacy fill fell to 61%, and the wholesale median rose by 60 hours. By stabilization, the hospital median had recovered, while wholesale delays and the workload of proving what had shipped persisted.

# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

A substitute is resilient when it protects the service patients need and leaves evidence that the required decision was made by an authorized person in time to act. B meets that test when its sources are current. J and K are resilience mechanisms for allocating capacity and restoring trustworthy stock. L is a resilience mechanism for detection, containment and learning. Its success is an honest disposition of each gap. An unperformed pre-dispatch check remains a breach even after the investigation closes.

C and an undocumented G are shortcuts. Their speed masks uncertainty about entitlement and custody. A, D, E, F, H and I are conditional. A recent stock count, an authenticated receipt or a short deferral can be reasonable, depending on product risk, evidence quality and how long the workaround lasts. Management has discretion over queue order and acceptable service delay. Mandatory eligibility, release, temperature and custody requirements still apply in every lane.

The T2 conformance alignment found extra events such as phone proof and spreadsheet invoice hold, alongside missing release, scan, proof and temperature events. An invoice hold can prevent premature billing. A missing electronic event could reflect either a valid offline check or a control failure. Test both explanations by matching the order and lot to dated originals, pharmacist signatures, logger readings and courier receipts. Record separately a timely verified check, an unresolved evidence gap and a confirmed omission. The hospital results support the triage explanation, but order mix, cancellations and still-open cases can also change reported cycle times. Case-level linkage is needed to attribute effects to a specific lane.

# 4. Recovery process model

1. **Declare degraded operation and establish a cutover.** Activate when the WMS is unavailable or when authorization, stock, scan or custody data fail an integrity check. The incident lead and quality lead record affected functions, sites, time and last trusted data versions. Enumerate in-flight orders and their last verified state. Check physical staging before reissuing a pick or dispatch instruction. Give each order one incident ID and one authoritative write path.
2. **Triage by need and product risk.** Confirm the delivery deadline, patient impact, stockout horizon and alternatives. Give time-critical orders the next qualified verifier, picker and suitable courier. A pharmacy serving an imminent stockout can qualify. Within equal urgency, prioritize the earliest safe deadline, then oldest order. Record the decision, override owner and next review. If a site lacks qualified staff or equipment, route to a verified capable site or hold the shipment.
3. **Apply cumulative release gates.** All orders, including ambient and hospital fast-lane orders, need current customer/product authorization, verified available quantity, product/lot/expiry matched to order, documented quality release and identified recipient and courier. Cold-chain orders also need approved packaging, a functioning temperature monitor linked to the parcel, dispatch readings and review of transit evidence at receipt. Controlled substances need the applicable authorization, two identified authorized people independently signing required checks, secure transport and signed custody transfers through receipt. Recall-sensitive orders need a fresh recall-status check and a lot-to-quantity-to-recipient record usable for immediate notification. Apply every relevant gate to mixed orders. Hold affected lines if evidence is insufficient. Separately release eligible lines only if their identity, authorization and records remain intact.
4. **Capture evidence as work occurs.** Use numbered offline forms or a controlled offline register. Link the order, reservation, physical pick, release, parcel, handoff and receipt. Include actual event time, actors, lot, quantity, evidence reference and exception reason. Record snapshot age and every stock movement against one running lot balance. Recount when the agreed freshness limit expires or balances disagree. At delivery, verify the named recipient and record time and quantity. Flag overdue proof for follow-up and hold invoice close where required.
5. **Control the backlog and hybrid restart.** Assign each waiting order a promised review time. Reassess deadlines each shift, notify customers of delays and reserve capacity for aging orders within clinical constraints. Restore each function only after integrity checks and end-to-end test orders pass. During partial recovery, intake can issue IDs while the offline ledger still owns reservations. Switch writers at a recorded cutoff after totals match to avoid duplicate allocations, dispatches or invoices.
6. **Reconcile and close in two stages.** Match every incident ID to electronic records, physical stock by lot, release evidence, carrier receipt, delivery proof and invoice. Import once, retaining originals, actual event time and separate entry time. Send discrepancies to a named owner with a deadline. Quality staff investigate high-risk gaps first, quarantine remaining implicated stock and assess recipient notification or recall when warranted. Keep the commercial outcome and quality disposition separate. Resume normal routing after stock, scanning and handoffs are validated. Close incident oversight only when every manual order is accounted for, critical exceptions have a quality disposition and residual corrective actions have owners and dates. Track audit labor and elapsed time until those actions close.

# 5. Resilience indicators

| Indicator | Leading signal and action during a shift | Lagging outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Throughput | Compare new orders/hour with **control-complete** dispatches/hour and available qualified release capacity. Add capacity if the queue grows. | Unique released and delivered orders per staffed hour by segment/product, plus deferred and cancelled volumes. |
| Critical service | Count orders approaching a clinical deadline or local stockout without verified stock, a verifier or a courier. Escalate to triage. | Critical quantity filled / critical quantity requested, on-time delivery against need-by time, and median/p90 cycle time by segment. |
| Traceability | Count parcels awaiting a linked lot, courier signature or due receipt. An unlinked handoff triggers immediate investigation. | Orders with a traceability exception / dispatched orders, time to obtain proof and time to identify recipients of a selected lot. |
| Control completeness | Count held orders by missing gate and block release while a mandatory gate is open. Review cold-chain receipt evidence when due. | By eligible product and stage, share with timely authorization, release, temperature and dual signatures. Report verified late entry, missing evidence and confirmed misses separately. |
| Backlog age and fairness | Track oldest and p90 wait, overdue reviews, remaining time to stockout and priority overrides by customer group and urgency. Escalate any order nearing its safe deadline. | Compare fill, lateness, deferral and cancellation across groups both overall and within comparable urgency/product bands. Investigate persistent gaps. |
| Rework and recovery debt | Track new discrepancies versus resolutions per shift, oldest open high-risk item and staff hours still required. Assign capacity when arrivals exceed resolutions. | Corrected orders / affected orders, remediation staff hours, p90 time from invoice close to quality disposition, and days until the audit queue clears. |

Use unique-order cohorts by intake date and incident exposure. Carry open orders across phase boundaries and show their age alongside completed-case percentiles. Report denominators, product eligibility and hours observed. Retain T0 as a service comparator. Any dispatch missing a mandatory control warrants review even if the aggregate rate looks better than baseline.

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Accept slower bulk fulfillment and time-limited, reviewed deferrals to protect imminent patient need. Give urgent orders first access to qualified controls. Reject shipment based on customer history alone, an undocumented custody transfer or a promise to reconstruct a required check later. Expand an offline lane when drills and incident records show timely controls, successful lot-to-recipient tracing and a backlog that staff can clear. If equal-urgency pharmacies miss clinical deadlines more often, change the allocation rule. If a mandatory release or custody check fails, suspend the affected lane until its control works.
