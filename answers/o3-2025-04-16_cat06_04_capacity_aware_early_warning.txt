## 1. Decision rule  
1. Eligibility  
    a. Case must still be before “Diagnosis Complete”.  
    b. No swarm performed on the case since the last materially new event.  

2. Probability-and-value scoring  
    Adjusted breach probability =  
    • Public Sector: p_now  
    • SME: p_now + 0.06 (mean recent under-prediction)  
    • Strategic Enterprise: p_now – 0.08 (mean recent over-prediction)  
    • Non-Native-Language: p_now (flag as high-uncertainty)  

    Expected direct €-gain per case =  
    (mid-point of case-specific risk-reduction range) × (value of breach avoided)  
      – €450 swarm labour  
      – false-alarm burden.  

    For segments with small samples or data-quality warnings (Non-Native-Language, imputed records) multiply the mid-point effect by 0.7 to reflect extra uncertainty.  

3. Queue-externality charge  
    For portfolio planning, subtract €1 800 per slot (0.02 spill-over risk × 6 cases × €15 000 average loss) from every candidate’s expected gain.  

4. Prioritisation  
    Rank by “adjusted expected net gain”.  
    Accept cases while:  
    • rank-ordered gain > 0;  
    • capacity left after reserving one slot for unforeseeable incidents;  
    • no fairness block (see §5).  

5. Alert persistence filter  
    A fresh alert is actionable only if, since the last alert, either  
    • process stage advanced, or  
    • any predictive feature changed by 10 % relative weight, or  
    • customer has added artefacts.  
    Otherwise the alert is suppressed for a 2-hour cool-down.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy  
Cool-down: 2 h (4 model refreshes).  
Suppression: if no qualifying feature change, do not page analysts.  
Escalation: after 2 suppressed alerts (4 h) a human reviewer must check for hidden changes.  
Re-entry: once any qualifying change occurs or 8 h elapse, the case re-enters normal alerting.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks  
1.  High-value bias: rule could systematically favour Strategic Enterprise. Safeguard: enforce 20 %–40 % capacity budget for SME/Public Sector unless their expected net gain <0.  
2.  Non-Native-Language uncertainty: down-weight effect (0.7) and log all such decisions for post-hoc audit; commission targeted causal study.  
3.  Differential calibration: maintain segment-specific Platt recalibration weekly; reject global thresholding.  
4.  Label shift: swarmed cases are more likely to meet SLA, thus future training labels are biased. Add “intervention applied” flag to training set and train uplift/causal models.  
5.  Spill-overs: untreated Diagnosis-stage cases disproportionately belong to SME. Monitor queue-delay metrics by segment; cap daily spill-over risk score per segment.  

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan  
Operational  
• Real-time dashboard of slot use, reserve balance, and queue length.  

Predictive  
• Weekly calibration plots by segment; Brier score trend.  
• Drift detector on key features (e.g., live queue length).  

Causal-effect  
• Run a batched, randomised hold-out of 10 % eligible cases (A/B) to re-estimate average treatment effect and heterogeneity quarterly.  

Capacity & congestion  
• Track average diagnostic delay added per swarm; if 3-week moving average >20 min, increase externality charge parameter.  

Fairness  
• Monthly report: share of capacity spent per segment, average spill-over harm sustained, and relative SLA-breach rates.  

Policy update  
• If net benefit in last 100 interventions <0 or fairness metric outside control limits, convene steering group to revise thresholds, calibration offsets, or reservation rule.
