## 1. Materiality Framework

**Severity (1–5)** measures the business consequence if my reading of the evidence is right. It is not the alignment cost. I score each case on eight dimensions and then make a judgment, not a sum.

| Dimension | What I ask |
|---|---|
| Control objective | Does the deviation touch a preventive money-out control (P1/P2/P3), a customer-communication obligation (P4), or only bookkeeping? |
| Amount/exposure | How much money is at stake, and is it capped? |
| Reversibility | Is the payment irreversible or recallable? Is the evidence gap fixable by retrieving documents? |
| Timing | Did payment precede approval? Is there a timestamp conflict, as in CL-604 (10:03 vs 10:20)? |
| Customer/regulatory harm | Is there a risk of fraud loss, a missed notification, or an unfair denial? |
| Recurrence | Is it a one-off or a systemic path (74 connector duplicates, 4 policy-violating auto-approvals, 9 adjuster cases)? |
| Evidence reliability | Is the evidence system-generated and immutable, or a mutable text field or verbal claim? |
| Model scope | Does the model legitimately cover this case (P6), or is the "deviation" an artifact of scope? |

**Severity scale**
- **5 – Critical control failure:** money released without the required approval or fraud control, or required control evidence is absent on a high-exposure or bank-change claim. Needs same-day containment.
- **4 – Serious policy breach:** a preventive rule was bypassed and paid, with moderate exposure or a systemic path.
- **3 – Material evidence gap:** a required control may have happened, but its proof is unreliable on a high-value case.
- **2 – Minor or conditionally acceptable deviation:** the control objective appears met, but the evidence or scope needs tidying.
- **1 – Noise or model artifact:** telemetry, retries, label mismatch, silent moves, or out-of-scope cases with no indication of payment.

**Confidence** is separate. It measures how well the evidence supports my characterization (system timestamps, corroborating sources, immutability, identifier stability, scope agreement). A case can be severity 5 with medium confidence, as in CL-602, where the approver claims a verbal check I cannot verify. The right response is to investigate urgently, not to downgrade. A case can also be severity 1 with high confidence, as in CL-603. Confidence never lowers severity. It determines whether the action is "contain and investigate" or "log and close".

Where severity is provisional, I state what would escalate or de-escalate it. A missing event is an evidence gap, not proof that the activity did not happen.

I can't check the P4 24-hour notification deadline or most P2 timing conditions for lack of timestamps, except where stated. Those checks are flagged as untested.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

**Cost-function design.** Every visible move costs 1, with no business weighting. CL-604 (cost 3) is an irreversible payment-before-approval failure on EUR 90k, while CL-601 (cost 3) is a compliant EUR 1,200 claim with UI noise. Identical cost, opposite meaning. CL-602 (cost 1) carries a potential EUR 480k fraud exposure.

**Scope.** CL-607 and CL-612 have cost 4 because the model has no denial or withdrawal branch. The cost measures model incompleteness, not wrongdoing. The alignment also invents a payment path, and it chooses Auto Approve simply because that branch needs one model move where Fraud Screen plus Manual Approve needs two. A reader who takes the alignment literally would conclude that an auto-approval happened.

**Hidden moves.** CL-605's tau move costs 0 and is technically fitting. It means the model takes a routing step and means nothing in business terms. Zero cost there is correct. Zero cost is also the trap in the next point.

**Data conditions.** CL-609 (EUR 18k, MEDIUM) and CL-610 (bank change one hour before payment) are fully synchronous at cost 0, yet both violate P1, and 610 also violates P2. The tree has no data guards, so it accepts any trace with an Auto Approve branch. A cost-ranked list would put these at the bottom, although they belong in the top tier.

**Activity granularity.** CL-611 counts one rename as two moves (a log move plus a model move), doubling the cost for a label mismatch that changes no business outcome.

**Duplicated telemetry.** CL-603 and the 74 connector duplicates add cost for events that are not business activities (P5). CL-601 adds 3 for UI events.

**Missing-but-external evidence.** CL-606 (external adjuster) and CL-608 (CRM notification) look like missing activities, but the work occurred outside the core log. The real question is evidence quality: a stable document-to-case link for the adjuster report, and an immutable link for the CRM message. Cost does not capture that.

**Irreversibility.** CL-604 is a repairable alignment but not a repairable business event. The model "repairs" it by inserting a payment after approval, yet the money has already gone. P3 states exactly this: a cheap alignment repair does not undo a severe control failure.

**Optimality caveat.** For CL-604, the log sequence Pay Claim  Manual Approve could also align at cost 2 with an Auto Approve branch: M(Auto Approve), S(Pay Claim), L(Manual Approve). That would be cheaper, but semantically worse, because it implies auto-approval on a EUR 90k claim. I follow the supplied alignment as authoritative, but this is a further reason to read the moves semantically.

**When cost is informative.** It flags structural deviation for triage, for example CL-604's extra moves. It works as a detector of scope problems (most cost  4 cases are denials and withdrawals) and of systematic logging defects (74 duplicates). It should never stand in for materiality.
