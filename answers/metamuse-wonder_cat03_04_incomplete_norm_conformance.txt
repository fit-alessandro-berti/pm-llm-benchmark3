## 1. Conformance semantics

The norm is not a complete description of permissible business reality. It is a partial specification containing a small number of enforceable obligations and prohibitions, several expectations that tolerate exceptions, and explicit areas of silence. Correct interpretation therefore requires separating the *surface pattern* in a discovered model from the *semantic status* assigned to that pattern by the norm.

**Forbidden behavior** is the only category where a closed-world reading is justified. If a relation is explicitly marked forbidden, and the observation truly concerns the same objects, event types, relationship type, ordering, and scope as the prohibition, then its occurrence is a conformance violation regardless of rarity. Examples are N3 `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` for the same Order, T6 `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)`, and E10 `Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice`. Rarity does not mitigate a forbidden relation; even one verified instance violates the rule. However, a forbidden-looking edge in a summary is not automatically a verified violation: identifier joins, wrong object linkage, or abstraction artifacts must still be excluded.

**Missing mandatory behavior** concerns an expected relation that does not appear where its scope applies. Closed-world reasoning is permitted only for the mandatory columns and scopes stated in the norm. N1, N4, T1, T2, and E1–E8 fall into this family. Yet the summary usually reports aggregate presence or absence, not object-level proof. A count such as 98 of 100 for N1 establishes an exception set of at most two Orders, but does not by itself prove that those Orders never validated, that they were standard Orders, or that no intervening event distorted the directly-follows abstraction. Mandatory gaps therefore require scope checking and object-level trace inspection before being called definite noncompliance.

**Unexpected but unregulated behavior** is behavior about which the norm is silent or explicitly unspecified. Under open-world semantics, absence from the normative edge set is not evidence of prohibition. N6 `Reopen Order -> Close Order`, T4 Order–Customer, T5 Order–RiskCase, E9 Manual Compliance Review–RiskCase, `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer`, and Package–Delivery `object_codeath` belong here unless another policy outside this norm governs them. Such behavior may be interesting, rare, or worthy of norm clarification, but it is not a violation of *this* norm.

**Typical-pattern absence** is weaker than missing mandatory behavior. N2, N5, and T3 describe common desirable patterns with named legitimate exceptions. A lower-than-complete frequency, such as 92 of 110 Invoices reaching `Record Payment` under N5, is a prompt for explanation, not a defect count. The 18 remaining Invoices cannot be labeled nonconforming without distinguishing write-offs, credit notes, open terms, timing censoring, and genuinely overdue unpaid invoices.

**Likely data-quality error** is a separate axis from business conformance. An edge may be present in the log because of identifier reuse, incorrect joins, missing relationship records, or mis-typed events. The E10 `Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice` edge is explicitly suspected to result from a reused numeric identifier. If that suspicion is verified, the correct conclusion is “bad linkage in the log,” not “the business linked a sensor stream to an Invoice.” Conversely, data-quality explanations must not be used as a convenient excuse for real violations; the two N3 Cancel–Dispatch cases require evidence before reclassification.

**Abstraction mismatch** arises when the normative business statement and the observed model relation are not logically equivalent. N1 states that a newly created standard Order should *eventually* enter validation, but the OC-DFG approximates this with a *directly-follows* edge. An unrecorded review between creation and validation can break the direct edge while preserving eventual validation. Similarly, N4 depends on scope — packages intended for external shipment — which the OC-DFG count (198 of 200) partially encodes but cannot independently verify.

**Insufficient evidence** applies when the summary cannot decide among violation, legitimate exception, censoring, and data error. Aggregate counts hide object identity, timestamps, lifecycle completion, event attributes, and whether the observation window ended before the expected successor could occur. Under this heading fall most small gaps of one or two cases until object-level inspection is performed.

In short: forbidden edges justify closed-world violation logic; mandatory edges justify scope-limited closed-world expectation logic; typical edges justify exception-seeking logic; unspecified edges justify open-world neutrality. Conflating these four statuses is the central interpretive error to avoid.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An OC-DFG can establish, per object type, which event-type pairs occur in directly-follows order for individual objects, and with what frequency. It is good at surfacing candidate forbidden sequences (N3: 2 Orders) and at quantifying how often an expected direct successor appears (N1: 98/100; N4: 198/200; N2-related closure: 83 Orders; N5: 92/110).

It cannot establish eventual reachability, causality, elapsed time, or whether an intermediate event legitimately occurred. Its counts are also not mutually exclusive paths: the 72 Orders with `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` and the 83 with `Validate Order -> Close Order` overlap in unknown ways, so neither number measures “skipped invoicing” or “skipped closure.” The OC-DFG also cannot verify scope predicates such as “standard Order” (N1) or “intended for external shipment” (N4); those come from attributes outside the graph.

The central danger is reading “eventually expected” as “directly follows.” N1’s business meaning is eventual validation. If an unrecorded review sits between creation and validation for the 2 exception Orders, the direct edge disappears while the business rule holds. Treating the OC-DFG edge as the rule itself manufactures two false violations; ignoring the gap entirely would be equally wrong, because genuinely unvalidated Orders produce the same summary. The correct move is an object-level eventual-follows check before classification. Similarly, N2 is only typical and explicitly contemplates intermediate invoice, review, or fulfilment events, so `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72) is a plausible intermediate, not an intrusion, and direct `Validate -> Close` absence for some Orders is not countable noncompliance.

### OTG

The Object Type Graph can establish which object-type relationships exist and their aggregate weights: Order–Item interaction 1450 (T1, conforming), Package–Delivery interaction 200 (T2, present at type level), Invoice–Payment interaction 92 (T3, consistent with the ET–OT count), plus unspecified Order–Customer 100 (T4) and Order–RiskCase 3 (T5). It can also surface a forbidden structural pattern: Order–Payment `object_cobirth` weight 1 (T6).

It cannot establish per-event or per-object completeness. A type-level edge with weight 200 does not prove every Dispatch event linked a Delivery — indeed ET–OT shows only 198 such links, so OTG and ET–OT must be read together. Weights also conflate repeated interactions with distinct objects, and the graph says nothing about ordering or timing. `object_codeath` (Package–Delivery, 7) illustrates abstraction asymmetry: the norm governs interaction and cobirth but is silent on codeath, so a relation can be structurally prominent yet normatively neutral. The same edge could become suspicious only under a future norm or another model family — e.g., if codeath coincided with missing delivery confirmation in ET–OT evidence — but that suspicion would come from outside the OTG itself.

### ET–OT

ET–OT analysis can establish whether events of a type involve objects of a type, and it gives the sharpest mandatory-gap signals because E1–E8 are stated per event: `Create Order–Order` 100/100 conforms; `Pack Package–Package` 200 and `Dispatch Package–Package` 200 conform; the gaps are precise small sets — 2 Add Item events (E2), 2 Dispatch events (E6), 1 Record Payment event (E8) — plus the forbidden `Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice` 1 (E10).

It cannot establish whether a missing link reflects a real process omission or a recording omission, nor can it see business justification stored in attributes. E6 is the clearest case: both Delivery-less Dispatch events carry a carrier reference, so the event may be operationally complete while the object graph is incomplete. E8 shows the opposite tension: T3 tolerates unapplied cash at type level, but E8 permits the exception only for events explicitly typed as unapplied cash, and none are so typed — so the single event is formally a mandatory-gap candidate whose business substance may still be legitimate. ET–OT also demonstrates cross-abstraction divergence: Manual Compliance Review–RiskCase (3) is unspecified-but-legitimate under E9, while the co-occurring Customer link (3) has no norm entry at all; neither rarity nor co-occurrence converts them into violations.

Across all three families, counts affect interpretation through denominators and exception-set size: a gap of 2 in 100 (N1) or 200 (N4, E6) localizes investigation to named objects, while 14 successor-less Invoices (N5) is too large and too exception-prone to individualize without ageing data. Small absolute counts cut both ways — they make forbidden findings (N3: 2; T6: 1) cheap to verify exhaustively, and they make statistical “tolerance” arguments unnecessary and inappropriate.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

**Mandatory bucket**
- N1 `Create Order -> Validate Order` (98/100): mandatory, but moves to abstraction-mismatch/insufficient-evidence if the 2 Orders eventually validated via an unrecorded intermediate.
- N4 `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` (198/200): mandatory for externally shipped Packages; leaves the bucket if a Package’s external-shipment scope flag is wrong.
- T1 Order–Item interaction (1450) and E3 Add Item–Item (1450): mandatory and observed conforming.
- E1 (100), E4 (200), E5 (200), E7 (93): mandatory and observed conforming.
- E2 Add Item–Order (1448/1450), E6 Dispatch–Delivery (198/200), E8 Record Payment–Invoice (92/93): mandatory-gap candidates; E6 softens if carrier-reference substitution is ratified, E8 softens only if the event is re-typed as unapplied cash per E8’s exception clause.

**Forbidden bucket**
- N3 `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` (2 Orders): forbidden; leaves the bucket only if object linkage or event ordering in the log is proven wrong.
- T6 Order–Payment `object_cobirth` (1): forbidden; leaves the bucket only if the shared birth is a proven import artifact.
- E10 `Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice` (1): forbidden on its face; expected to move to data-quality error once the reused-identifier join is confirmed.

**Typical bucket**
- N2 `Validate Order -> Close Order` (83): typical; presence of `Validate -> Issue Invoice` (72) reflects allowed intermediates.
- N5 `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` (92/110), with 4 `Write Off` and 14 open: typical; individual Invoices enter concern only via ageing/due-date evidence, not the ratio.
- T3 Invoice–Payment interaction (92): typical and consistent; unapplied cash is legitimate at this level.

**Unregulated bucket**
- N6 `Reopen Order -> Close Order` (1); T4 Order–Customer (100); T5 Order–RiskCase (3); E9 Manual Compliance Review–RiskCase (3); `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review` (3); `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer` (3); `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72) as an OC-DFG edge; Package–Delivery `object_codeath` (7). None is a violation under this norm; each becomes classifiable only if a future norm version assigns it a status.

## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch

```python
import pm4py

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# Discovery: bare graph structures ONLY.
# IMPORTANT: pm4py's discovered/normative graphs carry edges and
# frequencies, but NOT the mandatory / typical / forbidden /
# unspecified semantics, scopes, or exception clauses (N1-N6,
# T1-T6, E1-E10). That metadata must be maintained OUTSIDE these
# structures, e.g. in NORM_METADATA below, versioned with the norm.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
obs_ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
obs_otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
obs_etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# Raw PM4Py diagnostics: preserved untouched as evidence layer.
raw_diag_ocdfg = pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(ocel, norm_ocdfg)
raw_diag_otg = pm4py.conformance_otg(ocel, norm_otg)
raw_diag_etot = pm4py.conformance_etot(ocel, norm_etot)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# External semantic metadata (NOT derivable from the graphs):
# keyed by norm ID, holding status, scope predicate, exceptions.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
NORM_METADATA = {
    "N1": {"status": "mandatory", "scope": "standard Order",
           "reading": "eventual, approximated as directly-follows"},
    "N3": {"status": "forbidden", "scope": "same Order"},
    "N5": {"status": "typical",
           "exceptions": ["credit note", "write-off", "open terms"]},
    "N6": {"status": "unspecified"},
    "T6": {"status": "forbidden", "scope": "same business event"},
    "E8": {"status": "mandatory",
           "exceptions": ["event typed as unapplied cash"]},
    "E10": {"status": "forbidden"},
    # ... one entry per norm ID N1-N6, T1-T6, E1-E10 ...
}

def semantic_classify(family, raw_diagnostics, metadata):
    """Separate interpretation layer.

    Consumes PM4Py's raw diagnostics WITHOUT overwriting them and
    attaches norm-semantic classes. Anything absent from the norm
    defaults to 'unregulated' (open-world), never to 'violation'.
    """
    classified = []
    for diag in raw_diagnostics:
        norm_id = diag.get("norm_id")  # mapping supplied by analyst
        meta = metadata.get(norm_id)
        if meta is None:
            semantic = "unregulated / insufficient evidence"
        elif meta["status"] == "forbidden":
            semantic = "forbidden - verify DQ before final verdict"
        elif meta["status"] == "mandatory":
            semantic = "missing mandatory - check scope & abstraction"
        elif meta["status"] == "typical":
            semantic = "typical-pattern absence - seek exceptions"
        else:  # unspecified
            semantic = "unregulated behavior"
        classified.append({
            "family": family,
            "pm4py_diagnostic_raw": diag,   # preserved verbatim
            "norm_id": norm_id,
            "semantic_class": semantic,
            "norm_status": None if meta is None else meta["status"],
        })
    return classified

findings = (
    semantic_classify("OC-DFG", raw_diag_ocdfg, NORM_METADATA)
    + semantic_classify("OTG", raw_diag_otg, NORM_METADATA)
    + semantic_classify("ET-OT", raw_diag_etot, NORM_METADATA)
)

# findings is an interpretation overlay; raw_diag_* remain the
# untouched PM4Py evidence and must be reported alongside it.
```

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **Eventual vs. directly-follows semantics (N1).** *Risk:* analysts alternately over-report skipped validations or dismiss real ones, depending on local habit. *Clarification:* state N1 as an eventual-follows rule with a maximum validation latency, and require OC-DFG direct-follows gaps to trigger an eventual-follows check. *Status:* mandatory (eventual form); the direct-follows version should be explicitly only an approximation.

2. **Scope predicates are not operationalized (N1 “standard Order”, N4 “external shipment”).** *Risk:* the 2 N4 exception Packages and the 2 N1 exception Orders cannot be finally classified from graphs, inviting inconsistent manual judgments. *Clarification:* define the exact attributes/flags that instantiate scope and require them in the log. *Status:* mandatory (as scoping definitions for the existing mandatory rules).

3. **Unapplied-cash exception mismatch (E8 vs. T3).** *Risk:* T3 tolerates unapplied cash while E8 tolerates it only if explicitly typed; the 1 untyped Record Payment event will be classified differently by different teams. *Clarification:* either introduce the explicit event subtype and require its use, or amend E8 to accept Finance-attested unapplied cash. *Status:* mandatory for the typing/labelling duty; typical for the Invoice–Payment association itself.

4. **Delivery substitution by carrier reference (E6/T2).** *Risk:* 2 Dispatch events are graph-incomplete but operationally documented; recurring disputes over whether Delivery objects must exist. *Clarification:* either require Delivery object creation at dispatch without exception, or formally accept a carrier reference as an alternative fulfilment. *Status:* mandatory if Delivery objects are required downstream; otherwise explicitly unspecified with the attribute route documented.

5. **Intermediate steps between validation and closure (N2).** *Risk:* `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72) and similar steps get misread as deviations from a two-step norm. *Clarification:* enumerate permitted intermediates or restate N2 as eventual closure after validation. *Status:* typical (preferred path), with intermediates explicitly unspecified.

6. **Unspecified relations likely to be re-litigated case by case (N6, T4, T5, E9, codeath, Manual Compliance Review–Customer).** *Risk:* rare-but-legitimate behavior (e.g., 3 RiskCase reviews, 7 codeath edges) attracts repeated false escalations, while genuinely novel misuse hides in the same bucket. *Clarification:* record a deliberate decision per relation — govern it or permanently park it. *Status:* explicitly unspecified for codeath and Review–Customer; typical for Order–RiskCase where risk policy expects it; N6 reopening deserves a business decision, proposed typical pending that decision.

7. **No freshness, censoring, or tolerance rules.** *Risk:* the 14 successor-less Invoices (N5) and other end-of-window gaps are misread as absences, and teams invent ad-hoc percentage thresholds. *Clarification:* define observation-window handling, invoice ageing thresholds for escalation, and state that forbidden relations have zero tolerance while mandatory gaps are assessed per-object, not by invented bands. *Status:* mandatory as an interpretation rule for N5 escalation timing; no statistical tolerance should be attached to N3/T6/E10.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Immediate containment (highest-severity first)**
- Finding 1 (N3, 2 Orders): identify the dispatched-after-cancel Packages, confirm physical shipment status with the carrier, and attempt interception/recall; suspend auto-dispatch for cancelled Orders until linkage is verified.
- Finding 2 (T6, 1 cobirth): inspect the single originating event, confirm whether a real Payment was created with the Order, and if so route to Finance for reversal/regularization; block the originating transaction pattern if it is a system defect. Do not escalate the benign rare items (3 RiskCase reviews, 1 Reopen–Close, 7 codeath) — rarity alone is not a containment trigger.

**Data repair**
- Confirm and fix the reused-numeric-identifier join behind E10; invalidate and regenerate the affected `Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice` link.
- For E2 (2 events), E6 (2 events), and E8 (1 event): backfill missing object relations where source records prove them, or annotate events where the relation genuinely never existed; correct external-shipment/standard-Order scope flags if misapplied (N1, N4).
- Add explicit “unapplied cash” event typing if Finance confirms the E8 case, rather than silently editing history.

**Process investigation**
- Object-level eventual-follows audit of the 2 N1 Orders and packing-record audit of the 2 N4 Packages; root-cause whether events were skipped or merely unrecorded.
- Review cancellation-to-dispatch timing and system guards for N3, and the order/payment creation transaction for T6; interview Finance on the 14 open Invoices using ageing, not the 92/110 ratio.

**Norm revision**
- Adopt the clarifications in Section 6: eventual-form N1, operational scope predicates, E8/T3 harmonization, E6 carrier-reference decision, enumerated N2 intermediates, and explicit per-relation decisions for currently unspecified edges.

**Monitoring**
- Standing alerts with zero tolerance for N3, T6, and E10 patterns (after E10’s join is fixed, so alerts measure business reality, not join noise); per-object exception queues for N1, N4, E2, E6, E8 gaps; ageing-based dashboards for N5; and a periodic review of unregulated-bucket frequencies purely to inform future norm versions — never as automatic violations.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

**Robust conclusions:** N3 forbids dispatch after cancellation for the same Order, so the 2 observed cases are the highest-priority candidate violations; T6 forbids Order–Payment cobirth, making the single case a genuine candidate violation; unspecified relations — N6 Reopen–Close (1), T4 Order–Customer (100), T5/E9 RiskCase reviews (3), Manual Compliance Review–Customer (3), `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72), and Package–Delivery codeath (7) — are not violations under this norm, however rare or frequent.

**Conclusions dependent on norm incompleteness:** all typical-pattern readings (N2, N5’s 92/110, T3) depend on exception information the norm gestures at but does not operationalize; the E8 case depends on the unresolved tension between E8’s explicit-typing clause and T3’s tolerance of unapplied cash; and the E6 cases depend on whether a carrier reference is an accepted substitute for a Delivery object.

**Conclusions dependent on suspected data-quality issues:** the E10 Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge is expected to dissolve into a join error; the mandatory gaps at N1 (2), N4 (2), E2 (2), and E8 (1) could each wholly or partly reflect missing records, wrong scope flags, or unrecorded intermediates rather than process omissions.

**What cannot be decided from graph summaries alone:** whether any individual Order eventually validated, whether the cancelled Orders’ Packages physically shipped, the overlap between the 72 Invoice-step and 83 Close-step Orders, whether the 14 open Invoices are overdue or merely young, and the true business legitimacy of any single-object exception. Those determinations require object-level event sequences, timestamps, attributes, and source-system records — the summaries justify investigation, not final legal or business verdicts.
