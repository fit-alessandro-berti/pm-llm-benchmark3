## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation
The raw fitness collapse in March (0.93 to 0.55) followed by recovery to 0.90 after mapping `Eligibility Screen` to `Validate Eligibility` and deduplicating `Open Case` implies that most of the apparent conformance loss was semantic and provenance-related, not a sudden violation of the intended ordering. The persistent raw–mapped gap of 0.32–0.40 thereafter, however, shows the mapping was never complete for raw-label analyses, and mapped fitness still fell from 0.925 to 0.86–0.88 in May–October. A label mapping can recover conformance by construction if the renamed activity occupies the same position in traces; it cannot prove assessment criteria, document quality, eligibility checking, or decision thresholds were unchanged. Likewise, removing duplicate technical `Open Case` events restores the business trace but erases evidence about system reliability and workload generation.

Duplicated events inflate counts, create spurious loops and variant diversity, and distort DFG frequencies if not removed, while missing resource identifiers—rising from about 1% to 5–6.5%—bias resource, team, handover, and queueing analyses because missingness is concentrated in migrated activities and is not missing at random. They barely affect activity-order conformance after cleaning.

Post-May patterns cannot be dismissed as logging drift alone because mapped DFGs show AssessRequest Info rising from 12 to 24–29 per 100 cases, rework variants doubling, decision transitions falling, and cycle times rising 41–44% above baseline, with partial reversal after Supplier Z recovery. Those are changes in conditional frequencies and durations on normalized traces, requiring behavioral, population, and resource explanations in addition to logging effects.

**3.2 Normalized DFG-distance analysis**

I use the mean absolute difference, equivalently the L1 distance divided by the number of included edges, across six varying normalized edges per 100 cases: Eligibility to Collect Documents, Collect Documents to Assess, Assess to Request Info, Request Info to Collect Documents, Assess to Senior Assessment, and Assessment to Decision. Register to Eligibility is excluded because it is 100 in every supplied month and would only dilute the average. Calendar months not supplied are not interpolated.

|---|---:|---:|---:|

| 2026-03 | 3 | 0.5 | 3 vs Jan |
| 2026-05 | 40 | 6.7 | 37 vs Mar |
| 2026-07 | 59 | 9.8 | 19 vs May |
| 2026-09 | 28 | 4.7 | 31 vs Jul |
| 2026-10 | 64 | 10.7 | 36 vs Sep |

Euclidean distance gives the same ordering. The fragment distance is near zero in March after mapping, supporting semantic equivalence for the eligibility edge, then rises sharply by May, partially recovers in September, and rises again in October mainly because Assess to Senior Assessment increases from 7 to 22 per 100. Assumptions are that omitted edges are unchanged, rates are incidence per 100 cases rather than conditional transition probabilities, and the combined Assess or Senior Assessment to Decision edge approximates two distinct origins. Therefore this is a fragment distance, not a complete DFG, variant, or alignment distance.

These components interact: rework creates more Assess demand, case mix creates more service and senior demand, staffing loss converts demand into queueing, and the priority rule reallocates queueing between classes. They should not be interpreted as independent causal shares.

**3.4 Candidate change points**

Logging drift: first, 2026-03-01, where raw fitness, label inventory, duplicate Open Case, and missing resource all change while median cycle does not. Second, 2026-10-01 is a candidate change point for Senior Assessment visibility, but unlike March it is confounded by the seasonal increase in high complexity and the July priority rule.

Behavioral drift: first, 2026-05-05, where Assess to Request Info doubles and the rework variant jumps. Second, 2026-07-01, when the priority rule begins. A third candidate is October, when the senior pathway expands sharply.

Performance drift: first, May 2026, with median cycle +4.5h month-to-month and p90 +13h. Second, the June 15 staffing loss followed by the July-August queue peak. September and October are additional performance change points, the former a partial reversal and the latter a seasonal step.

**3.5 One global change point or several?**

The data support multiple mechanism-specific change points rather than one global change point. A single change point fitted to raw fitness would select March and miss real post-May drift; a single change point fitted to median cycle would select around May or July and miss the March semantic break and October population step. September and December partial reversals also contradict a single permanent regime shift. The more defensible model is staggered and overlapping: March logging, May supplier-driven behavior, June-July resource and queueing, September partial supplier recovery, October seasonal population and senior-pathway change, and December mixed easing.

## 6. Segmentation plan
| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 Overall cohort | Registration month, normalized activity labels, case identifier | Primary denominator for all process, fitness, variant, and performance measures | At least 100 cases per reported month; otherwise pool months and suppress unstable rates | Overall drift, seasonality, and recovery | Lowest selection bias if duplicate technical events are removed but all business cases remain |
| L1 Period regime | Baseline, migration-only, supplier onset, staffing and priority period, supplier recovery, renewal season, December intervention | Separates known event windows without treating the whole year as one regime | At least 100 cases and at least 20 occurrences of the focal edge per regime; pool adjacent regimes if not met | Mechanism-specific change-point comparisons | Defining periods by known events can create confirmation bias, so event dates should be pre-specified and sensitivity windows reported |
| L2 Supplier by complexity | Supplier Z versus non-Z crossed with high versus standard complexity | Separates supplier-driven rework from complexity-driven senior and duration effects | At least 100 cases per cell per quarter and at least 30 cases for a median; pool quarters or collapse cells below the threshold | Within-stratum behavioral DFGs and standardized population effects | Complexity may be recorded after assessment or influenced by senior review, creating post-treatment conditioning if it is not measured at registration |
| L3 Priority and queue class | Priority class at queue entry, complexity at registration, waiting band | Detects redistribution of delay after July 1 | At least 100 cases per class per quarter and at least 30 observed queue episodes | High versus standard waiting, service, and cycle comparisons | Conditioning on realized senior status or actual waiting can introduce collider bias; priority should be defined before Assess |
| L4 Resource and team | Assessor, team, and missing-resource category | Detects staffing, handover, and provenance effects | At least 30 cases per resource or team per quarter; report missing as its own category rather than dropping it | Resource DFGs, service variation, and missingness sensitivity | Complete-case restriction selects migrated and potentially different cases; small teams can produce unstable comparisons |
| L5 Pathway and variant | Fast, rework, senior, and decision pathways | Diagnostic decomposition of where drift occurs | At least 100 cases per pathway per quarter for DFGs; use counts rather than rates for rare pathways | Locates loops, senior use, and decision changes | Variant is an outcome of the process, so conditioning on it can block or distort the causal effect being estimated; use it diagnostically, not as the primary denominator |

The primary monitoring population should be all business cases in the normalized event log, grouped by registration month, including all suppliers, complexity classes, teams, and resource-missing records. Duplicate technical Open Case events should be removed from the business trace but retained in a separate provenance count. This avoids conditioning on complete resources, fast pathways, non-rework outcomes, or post-assessment complexity. A supplier-by-complexity standardized series should be reported alongside the overall series so that population change can be distinguished from within-stratum behavioral change.

Seasonality should be handled with month-of-year expectations once at least two prior years are available. Until then, October-November should be treated as a provisional seasonal window: volume and complexity changes inside that window should be compared with a seasonal allowance, but changes that exceed the allowance, persist into December, or appear in within-stratum DFGs should still alert. Behavioral and performance rates should also be standardized to the baseline supplier-by-complexity mix.

Multiple testing should be controlled hierarchically. Screening alerts can use Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery control across the monitor family, followed by corroboration from a second artifact or a second consecutive window before operational action. Thresholds should be metric-specific, based on baseline variability and minimum operationally important effect sizes, rather than one universal percentage or p-value.

def _require_pm4py():

def format_event_log(df, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity",
                     timestamp_key="timestamp", resource_key="resource"):
    """Format an event-level dataframe as a PM4Py event log without modifying df."""
    _require_pm4py()
    work = df.copy()
    required = [case_id, activity_key, timestamp_key]
    missing = [c for c in required if c not in work.columns]
    if missing:
        raise KeyError(f"Missing required columns: {missing}")
    work[timestamp_key] = pd.to_datetime(work[timestamp_key])
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(
        work,
        case_id=case_id,
        activity_key=activity_key,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
        resource_key=resource_key,
    )

def slice_time_window(log, start, end):
    """Slice an event log to [start, end] using PM4Py time filtering."""
    _require_pm4py()
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(log, pd.to_datetime(start), pd.to_datetime(end))

def monthly_dfg(log, start, end):
    """Discover a DFG for one time window."""
    _require_pm4py()
    return pm4py.discover_dfg(slice_time_window(log, start, end))

    """Compute variant counts and shares for one time window."""

    """Compute the temporal profile for one time window."""
    _require_pm4py()

def discover_structure_models(log):
    """Optionally discover a log skeleton and, if available in the PM4Py version, DECLARE."""
    _require_pm4py()
    results = {}
    try:
        results["log_skeleton"] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(log)
    except Exception as exc:
        results["log_skeleton_error"] = str(exc)
    if hasattr(pm4py, "discover_declare_skeleton"):
        try:
            results["declare_model"] = pm4py.discover_declare_skeleton(log)

            results["declare_model_error"] = str(exc)

        try:
            results["declare_model"] = pm4py.discover_declare_model(log)
        except Exception as exc:
            results["declare_model_error"] = str(exc)

def normalize_event_log(log, mapping=None, remove_duplicate_open_case=False):
    """Return a new normalized EventLog; the original log is not overwritten."""
    _require_pm4py()
    mapping = dict(LABEL_MAPPING if mapping is None else mapping)
    normalized_log = pm4py.EventLog()
    for trace in log:
        new_trace = pm4py.Trace(attributes=dict(trace.attributes))
        seen_open_case = False
        for event in trace:
            new_event = pm4py.Event(attributes=dict(event.attributes))
            activity = new_event.attributes.get("concept:name")
            if remove_duplicate_open_case and activity == "Open Case":
                if seen_open_case:
                    continue
                seen_open_case = True
            if activity in mapping:
                new_event.attributes["concept:name"] = mapping[activity]
            new_trace.append(new_event)
        normalized_log.append(new_trace)
    return normalized_log

def compare_raw_and_normalized(log, mapping=None, remove_duplicate_open_case=True):
    """Compare raw and normalized variants and DFGs without overwriting the original log."""
    _require_pm4py()
    normalized_log = normalize_event_log(
        log,
        mapping=mapping,
        remove_duplicate_open_case=remove_duplicate_open_case,
    )
    return {
        "raw_variants": pm4py.get_variants(log),
        "normalized_variants": pm4py.get_variants(normalized_log),
        "raw_dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(log),
        "normalized_dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(normalized_log),
        "normalized_log": normalized_log,
    }

## 9. Provisional attribution
Strongest conclusions are: (1) March represents primarily logging/semantic and provenance drift, strength about 95/100, because raw fitness fell 0.38 while mapped fitness fell only 0.025, labels changed in the expected position, and duplicates and missing resources appeared simultaneously; (2) May onward represents genuine behavioral and performance drift, not logging alone, because normalized Assess->Request Info doubled to 24-29 per 100, rework variants rose from 11.5% to 27-29%, and mapped fitness and cycle time deteriorated; (3) Supplier Z incompleteness is the leading explanation for the May rework step, strength about 80, supported by its share rising 18% to 43% in April-May and partial rework reversal after September recovery; and (4) queueing, rather than Assess service time, dominated the July-August cycle peak, because service rose only about 0.6 hours while median cycle rose 13.9 hours above baseline.

Only plausible, not established, are staffing as amplifier of queueing (about 65), priority redistribution by complexity (about 45), a genuine October expansion of the senior pathway versus seasonal recording (about 60), and December improvement from overtime/document campaign versus seasonal easing (about 40). High complexity and Supplier Z were already associated before May, volume was already rising, and rework itself creates additional Assess work, so supplier, staffing, complexity, and priority effects mediate and confound each other. The July priority rule can shorten high-complexity waiting while lengthening standard waiting, invisible in monthly medians; similarly, senior assessment in October may reflect more high-complexity cases, threshold change, recording change, or all three.

I would revise this account if supplier-stratified DFGs showed rework rising equally for non-Z cases, if queue timestamps showed waiting before Assess did not rise after June staffing loss, if priority-stratified temporal profiles showed no redistribution, or if event-level alignments showed the mapped March traces genuinely violated ordering. Conversely, confirmation would require supplier-by-complexity DFGs, queue and service decomposition by priority class, and resource-complete sensitivity analyses.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules
1. IF raw fitness collapses while mapped fitness and normalized DFG distance remain stable, THEN freeze behavioral conclusions, audit labels and schema provenance, and report raw and mapped results separately, BECAUSE the pattern indicates logging or semantic drift rather than a proven change in the business process.

2. IF Assess to Request Info and rework variants rise sharply within Supplier Z cases after a supplier event and fall after supplier recovery, THEN trigger supplier document-quality review and incomplete-bundle checks, BECAUSE the pattern indicates supplier-driven rework rather than a general assessor change.

3. IF queue waiting before Assess rises sharply while Assess service time remains stable, THEN review assessor capacity, backlog, and arriving rework demand before changing clinical or eligibility criteria, BECAUSE the pattern indicates resource and queueing drift rather than slower assessment work.

4. IF high-priority waiting falls or rises less while standard-case waiting rises after a priority rule, THEN review queue allocation and equity of delay by priority class, BECAUSE the pattern indicates redistribution of delay rather than an overall capacity improvement.

5. IF volume and high-complexity share rise in the renewal season while within-stratum DFGs, rework, and service rates remain stable, THEN classify the change as seasonal population drift and monitor against seasonal expectations, BECAUSE the pattern indicates case-mix change rather than new behavior within comparable cases.

6. IF missing resource exceeds the alert level or becomes concentrated in migrated activities, THEN suspend resource, team, and handover comparisons until provenance is repaired and repeat them with missing as an explicit category, BECAUSE non-random missingness can manufacture apparent resource drift.

7. IF mapped fitness falls, DFG distance rises, and decision or senior-pathway rates change within supplier and complexity strata for two consecutive windows, THEN open a behavioral drift investigation using alignments, variants, DECLARE constraints, and temporal profiles, BECAUSE persistent within-stratum structural change is not explained by logging or case mix alone.
