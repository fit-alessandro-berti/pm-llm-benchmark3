## 1. Materiality Framework

Raw alignment cost measures syntactic edit distance between an event log trace and a control-flow Petri net under a chosen cost function; it is agnostic to financial exposure, policy rules, evidence quality, and system architecture. To prioritize deviations rigorously, materiality must be evaluated across eight business and diagnostic dimensions:

1. **Control Objective**: Does the deviation bypass a preventive fraud, credit, or authorization gate (e.g., `Fraud Screen`, `Manual Approve`), or is it merely a logging/routing artifact?
2. **Amount / Financial Exposure**: Potential monetary loss if a control fails (e.g., EUR 480,000 vs. EUR 800, or EUR 0 for a denied pre-screen).
3. **Reversibility**: Whether funds have already settled externally (`Pay Claim` completed) versus pre-payment exceptions where exposure can still be contained.
4. **Timing & Sequencing**: Whether controls occurred *before* irreversible disbursement (P3) and within regulatory SLAs (e.g., 24-hour notification under P4).
5. **Customer & Regulatory Harm**: Delayed/missing claimant notices, duplicate communications, wrongful payments, or inability to demonstrate statutory AML/fraud due diligence to regulators.
6. **Recurrence**: Isolated anomaly versus systemic connector retry (74 cases/month), recurring policy-rule gap (4 auto-approve violations/month), or chronic integration gap (9 external-adjuster cases/month).
7. **Evidence Reliability**: Whether non-repudiable, immutable audit evidence exists (e.g., signed adjuster report vs. unrecorded "verbal check" vs. mutable CRM text link).
8. **Model Scope**: Whether the trace belongs to the model's intended domain (payable claims) or represents an out-of-scope lifecycle path (denied or withdrawn claims under P6).

### Severity Scale (1–5)
* **Severity 5 (Critical)**: Confirmed bypass of core financial/fraud controls on high-exposure or irreversible payouts, or payment before approval (P2/P3 failures). Immediate financial recovery/containment and executive escalation required.
* **Severity 4 (High)**: Systemic data-aware policy failures allowing automated disbursement despite violated risk/bank-change rules (P1/P2), creating acute fraud or compliance exposure even on synchronous model paths.
* **Severity 3 (Moderate)**: Substantive controls appear executed, but cross-system audit evidence is fragile or mutable on high-value/high-risk claims (e.g., P4 evidentiary weakness), creating regulatory or dispute risk.
* **Severity 2 (Low–Moderate)**: Substantive activity occurred in the right sequence via external/secondary systems with intact external artifacts, leaving only a core-system log gap or minor correlation weakness.
* **Severity 1 (Informational / No Business Risk)**: Harmless UI telemetry, idempotent transport retries, silent routing transitions (`tau`), historical ETL label mismatches, or valid out-of-scope business outcomes (denials/withdrawals).

### Severity vs. Confidence
**Severity** measures the *business consequence* if the indicated state of affairs is real (exposure, regulatory breach, fraud loss). **Confidence** (High / Moderate / Low) measures *epistemic certainty* about what actually occurred in reality versus what appears in the log.
* A case can have **High Severity and High Confidence** (CL-604: bank settlement timestamp 10:03 vs. manual approval at 10:20 proves out-of-sequence payment).
* A case can have **High Severity and Moderate Confidence** (or uncertain root cause) if a control might have occurred verbally/offline (CL-602), though the *compliance failure* (lack of audit evidence) remains High Confidence.
* A case can have **Moderate Severity and Moderate Confidence** when external records exist but linkage is weak (CL-608's mutable CRM field).

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Raw alignment cost is strictly a **combinatorial graph-edit metric** over symbolic activity labels; equating it to business risk creates severe false positives and dangerous false negatives. Seven mechanisms demonstrated in the supplied cases explain why:

1. **Uniform Cost-Function Design (`c = 1`)**: Every visible log or model move was assigned an arbitrary unit cost of `1`. Consequently, skipping a mandatory `Fraud Screen` on a EUR 480,000 HIGH-risk claim after a bank change (**CL-602**, `raw_cost=1`, Severity 5) scores the exact same technical penalty as a harmless messaging retry (**CL-603**, `raw_cost=1`, Severity 1), and *three times less* than harmless UI clicks (**CL-601**, `raw_cost=3`, Severity 1).
2. **Model-Scope Mismatch (P6)**: When out-of-scope outcomes such as coverage denials (**CL-607**) or valid claimant withdrawals (**CL-612**) are aligned against a payable-claims-only tree, the aligner must fabricate model moves (`Auto Approve`, `Pay Claim`, `Notify Claimant`) to reach the final marking. This inflates `raw_cost` to `4`—the highest in the log—despite zero operational or financial error.
3. **Data Conditions & Policy Blindness**: Standard control-flow Petri nets cannot evaluate case attributes. Both **CL-609** (EUR 18,000 MEDIUM-risk auto-approved) and **CL-610** (EUR 4,500 auto-approved 1 hour after a beneficiary bank change) follow a valid XOR branch in the process tree and therefore achieve `raw_cost = 0`, yet both violate P1/P2 and represent severe fraud and compliance failures (Severity 4).
4. **Hidden / Silent Routing Moves**: In **CL-605**, `T(tau,c=0)` appears in the alignment solely because converting the process tree's `X(...)` choice construct into an accepting Petri net introduces structural silent transitions. Silent moves incur zero cost and represent *fitting* model routing, not omitted business work.
5. **Activity Granularity & Label Drift**: Fine-grained UI telemetry (**CL-601**: `Open Workbench`, `Autosave`, `Close Workbench`) adds `L` moves at `c=1` each without changing business semantics (P5). Similarly, a pure naming mismatch (`Assess Damage` vs. `Assess Loss` in **CL-611**) triggers *both* a log move and a model move (`raw_cost = 2`) for a single compliant activity.
6. **Duplicated Telemetry / Retries**: Transient connector retries (**CL-603**, and 74 monthly cases sharing the same `message_id`) append redundant `L(Notify Claimant)` events (`raw_cost = 1`), reflecting IT transport behavior rather than a missed control or customer harm.
7. **Missing-but-External Evidence vs. True Irreversibility**: A single model move `M(Assess Loss, c=1)` in **CL-606** reflects an unlogged event whose substantive work *did* occur on time via a licensed external adjuster (Severity 2), and `M(Notify Claimant, c=1)` in **CL-608** reflects a CRM portal message whose linkage is weak (Severity 3). By contrast, **CL-604** (`raw_cost=3`) involves an *irreversible* EUR 90,000 bank settlement occurring 17 minutes before manual approval (P3)—where an alignment can "repair" the sequence cheaply on paper, while the enterprise has already lost preventive control over cash disbursement.
