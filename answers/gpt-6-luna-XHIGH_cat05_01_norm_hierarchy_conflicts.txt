## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts supplied.** GPIP-4 is board-approved, global, and effective throughout these events. The Aachen SOP is controlled, local, and in force. ECD-2 has a defined written window, subject-matter scope, and express rules for PO timing, supplier-register status, and dual Incident Command authorization. It is signed by the COO and Incident Commander, but not by the Chief Risk Officer. The CFO email is an ordinary email; the CFO is expressly authorized to interpret Finance controls, but no supplied document grants the CFO power to waive GPIP-4 controls. ERP times can be unreliable for events entered during the stated outage; P-505 separately says the ERP clock may have been fast. The scenario also says signed incident directives have previously been treated as temporary policy overlays, but not for a new-supplier Compliance clearance case.

**Assumptions for the primary classifications.**

- GPIP-4 is the baseline for company-wide integrity and financial controls. Local and incident rules may add detail and stricter controls. They displace a corporate control only where an authorized exception or sufficiently clear, scoped overlay is established. The supplied facts do not establish that the COO and Incident Commander can issue a formal A7 exception, or that ECD-2 was one; prior practice supports an overlay as to local workflow, but does not prove it waived GPIP-4.
- I read ECD-2's express precedence over “ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding requirements” to permit C1 and C2 within its time and subject scope, and to establish C3 as the incident authorization requirement. I do not read this wording to waive GPIP-4 segregation, the independent budget-owner and Finance Controller requirements, or Compliance clearance. C4 and SOP B7 leave room for ECD-2 to supersede other ordinary local steps, but do not identify them or eliminate every local control.
- I treat purchase approvals as intended to be obtained before commitment when they function as authorizations to commit. B2 does not say “before commitment” as expressly as A2, so conclusions resting only on B2 timing have lower confidence. B4 expressly changes PO sequence in a qualifying emergency; it does not expressly authorize commitment without other approvals. A6 means later approval may mitigate consequences but is not automatically a cure for a required pre-commit approval.
- The CFO email can plausibly interpret or streamline Finance-framework operational approvals during ECD-2 up to EUR 150,000. I do not treat “sufficient for all operational approvals” as an express amendment of A2's two independent roles or as an A7 exception. The email's “essential integrity controls” sentence supports retaining those protections. Its effect on an actual Finance Controller approval remains contestable; no facts show that the Incident Command members were budget owners or Finance Controllers.
- “Critical sterilization-line lots” is ambiguous. Direct affected-line parts needed within hours are strong inclusions; backup-line components with three days of stock are doubtful. A purchase outside ECD-2 still has to satisfy the SOP and GPIP-4.
- A commitment occurs when the company becomes bound, not necessarily at an ERP posting. A supplier call's start is evidence of contact, but acceptance and commitment should be established from the recording, acknowledgment, and terms. For P-505, contemporaneous external evidence and the stated clock offset deserve more weight than ERP time alone.
- For an exception, the proponent should provide evidence of authority, scope, timing, and conditions. Missing evidence is not proof that an act did not occur, but does not establish compliance. I distinguish a sequence defect, a documentation defect, late evidence of a timely act, and inability to prove a control.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

- **P-501 — COMPLIANT.** Read B2 literally: it requires Plant_Approve and Finance_Approve for a purchase over EUR 25,000 but does not expressly say they must precede commitment. Both occurred later, and B4 expressly permits commitment before PO in a qualifying emergency. This is credible because A2 expressly says “before” commitment. It is weaker under the primary reading because Plant and Finance approvals authorize commitment, and B4 does not waive them. A local definition allowing post-commit B2 approval would reverse the conclusion.
- **P-503 — JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION if several premises change.** A broad reading of C6/C4 could treat backup-line components needed for contingency during the incident as “critical,” regardless of three days of stock. If PROC-3 was a distinct authorized IC member, C3 could be met. The more difficult premise is that CFO D1 validly satisfied A2's Finance Controller and independent budget-owner roles, or that a formal exception amended them. Neither is established; missing A3 clearance is independently decisive unless a valid exception also covered Compliance or ECD-2 is held to be such an exception.
- **P-504 — INDETERMINATE.** A narrow B scope could exclude cafeteria facilities because the SOP covers maintenance and production-support purchases in a production context. With no production effect and no supplied global rule requiring an approved supplier or pre-PO sequence for this amount, the prompt would not establish which rule SHIFT-2 breached. This is credible given the scope wording. It is weaker if routine cafeteria equipment repair is “maintenance” at the Aachen site; then B1/B3 apply.
- **P-505 — JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION or COMPLIANT if role and timing premises change.** If ERP was nine minutes fast, 18:06 corresponds to 17:57, and the acknowledgment at 17:59:31 may establish pre-expiry commitment. A2 remains. If CFO's authorized interpretation makes joint IC approval a valid Finance Controller approval and one authorizer was an independent budget owner, applicable pre-commit controls may be satisfied. D1 makes this credible; missing role evidence and D4 make it weak. If binding commitment occurred after 18:00, this alternative fails on scope.
- **P-507 — JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION if D1 fully supplies both finance roles.** D1 could be read as the CFO's authorized determination that joint IC authorization suffices for all operational approvals under EUR 150,000, including A2 approvals. The IC pair was distinct, PDF evidence can show timely record, and Compliance was timely. This alternative is credible because D1 is explicit and CFO has Finance interpretive authority. It is weaker because IC members are not shown to be an independent budget owner and Finance Controller; treating one operational decision as both distinct global roles risks removing A2's independence.
- **P-508 — JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION if a separate valid Finance interpretation/exception exists.** A broader CFO interpretation might make IC authorization sufficient for required financial controls, while BUY-8 was separated from two final authorizers under A1. This is credible as to A1 and emergency scope. It is weak as to A2 because the amount exceeds D1's EUR 150,000 cap and no Finance event or CRO exception is shown.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

Four outcomes must be distinguished:

1. **Substantive policy breach:** P-502's late Compliance clearance; P-503's missing Compliance clearance; and absent or post-commit global approvals in P-502, P-503, P-505, P-506, P-507, and P-508 are substantive on the primary interpretation. P-501 likely has a local pre-commit approval breach. P-504 likely has local supplier and sequence breaches if the SOP covers cafeteria maintenance.
2. **Logging defect:** P-507's ERP authorization entry at 14:30 is not conclusive evidence of a late C3 record. A signed PDF created at 12:40 is within four hours if authentic, signed by both members, and preserved/queued as a record. Reconcile the records without treating ERP entry time as act time.
3. **Late evidence of a timely act:** P-502's verbal authorization was allowed and its 12:55 record is within four hours. For P-505, supplier acknowledgment and possible clock offset may establish a pre-expiry commitment more reliably than ERP. ON-44 similarly calls for contemporaneous evidence to correct recovery times; it cannot excuse a substantive control failure.
4. **Inability to prove compliance:** P-503's IC membership/independence and actual need, P-505's binding acceptance point, P-506's alleged extension and emergency facts, and budget-owner roles across cases remain unproved. Missing proof warrants targeted reconstruction; it should not be converted into an assumption that the requirement was met.

Immediate case-level actions:

- **P-501:** Preserve outage-window call/supplier records and approval audit trail; record the B2 timing interpretation and later approvals as mitigation, not automatic cure. Confirm emergency prediction and delegation evidence.
- **P-502:** Record A3 and likely A2 sequence failures; verify approver identities and any budget-owner record; review why Compliance clearance followed commitment and determine supplier/lot disposition under site quality controls.
- **P-503:** Pause any uncommitted balance; establish whether PROC-3 was an IC member; retrieve demand, stock, and containment records; obtain independent Compliance/Finance review. Do not backdate approvals. Escalate continued use or payment through authorized channels.
- **P-504:** Establish whether cafeteria repair falls within SOP; document supplier exception and obtain normal retrospective review for remediation only. Verify whether food-safety risk existed; “site continuity” is not the defined emergency test.
- **P-505:** Preserve call recording, acknowledgment headers, acceptance terms, ERP clock configuration, and approval logs. Determine binding time and whether any independent budget-owner/Finance approval existed before it. Record expiry determination; do not treat later Finance approval as an automatic cure.
- **P-506:** Do not rely on undocumented extension; determine whether competent authority issued a valid written extension and whether B4 conditions existed. Note late Plant approval and missing global budget-owner approval; use ordinary process for further purchases.
- **P-507:** Preserve PDF, signatures, creation metadata, queue and outage logs, and ERP audit trail. If authenticated, correct the record as timely C3 evidence without changing underlying timestamps. Separately investigate missing A2 roles; the PDF is not a financial approval unless it contains one.
- **P-508:** Verify whether IC authorizers held budget-owner or Finance Controller authority; if not, document A2 breach and route remaining obligations through normal Finance governance. Preserve Legal clearance as satisfying D2, not replacing Finance approval.

For monitoring, create an incident procurement register capturing request, authorization, commitment, PO, approval, clearance, and record times separately; applicable policy and exception; and actors and roles. Reconcile outage-affected records against calls, supplier acknowledgments, signed PDFs, and access logs. Review emergency purchases soon after the event for missing controls, owners, corrective action, and repeat patterns. Track post-commit approvals separately from timely approvals and sample supplier-register exceptions and repeated “critical” labels.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Proposed hierarchy and exception clause**

> **Hierarchy and emergencies.** This Policy applies to every Northbridge purchase. Local procedures may add controls and specify evidence. A temporary incident directive may vary a local workflow requirement only if it identifies the affected rule, entities/site, purchase category, effective start and expiry, decision owner, and required records. It does not vary this Policy unless the Chief Risk Officer, or a formally named delegate acting under written CRO delegation, issues or expressly co-signs a written exception identifying each Policy clause varied, permitted transactions and maximum amount, substitute controls, and expiry. COO or Incident Command signatures alone do not vary Policy controls.
>
> **Controls that remain unless the CRO exception says otherwise.** Emergency status never by itself waives segregation of duties, independent budget-owner and Finance Controller approvals, Compliance clearance, or an expressly stated pre-commit requirement. A requester or supplier committer may not be final business approver. An exception must name independent substitute approvers; one incident authorization cannot satisfy multiple independent roles unless the exception expressly permits it and preserves equivalent independence. New-supplier and amount thresholds remain unless the exception names them.
>
> **Emergency execution and records.** The directive must define qualifying emergency, covered goods/services, evidence of necessity, and whether PO, supplier onboarding, Plant, Finance, and Legal steps may be sequenced differently. Any permitted verbal authorization must identify two distinct authorized people, be logged with actual action time and reason as soon as systems permit and no later than four hours, and be reconciled to signed evidence. A later log entry may prove a timely act if authenticated; it does not move the act time or cure an approval that was actually late.
>
> **Expiry and safeguards.** The directive expires automatically at its stated time. Extension requires a new written instrument approved by the same or higher authority before expiry; oral claims or continuing operational need do not extend it. Each emergency purchase must be entered in a central register and independently reviewed within two business days. Repeat use of the emergency route for the same category, supplier, or recurring condition requires a corrective-action review and ordinary sourcing plan; emergency status may not be renewed for routine replenishment.
