## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### Established facts

- **M0 semantics:** after `Register_Supplier`, both `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` must complete, in either order or concurrently, before an XOR decision. Approval requires `Manual_Procurement_Approval` before `Activate_Supplier`; rejection excludes activation. `Notify_Requester` then precedes `Close_Case`.
- **CS-9 is the baseline controlled standard** from 2025-01-01. It requires:
  - sanctions and quality checks before activation;
  - manual approval before every activation;
  - requester and approver to be different humans.
- **PD-3** temporarily authorized an auto-approval pilot for eligible cases from 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31, but was not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. Its compliance authority is contested.
- **FD-7** expressly authorized provisional activation for qualifying emergency suppliers from 2026-07-10 through 2026-07-12, subject to sanctions clearance, EUR 20,000 cap, monitoring, and retrospective controls.
- `Notify_Requester` logging became incomplete after the CRM migration: observed missingness increased from 2% to 29%, while only about 70% of notifications were mirrored.
- More-information cycles are operationally used and considered desirable, but M0 cannot represent them.
- Of 312 auto-approved June/July cases, 289 appear eligible, 14 are clearly ineligible, and 9 lack sufficient eligibility data.
- Six auto-approvals occurred after PD-3 expired.
- SUP-703 and SUP-708 contain serious substantive control failures, not merely cosmetic trace differences.
- SUP-712 fits M0 but violates CS-9’s human-identity separation rule.

### Disputed or unproved propositions

- Whether PD-3 was an adequately authorized exception to CS-9, rather than merely operational permission to conduct a monitored but technically nonconforming pilot.
- Whether failed pre-screen leads such as SUP-709 are within M0’s case population.
- Whether a reactivation such as SUP-710 is a fresh onboarding governed by every CS-9 requirement.
- Whether SUP-706’s mutable CRM linkage proves notification for this case.
- Whether retrospective approval of the `QA_Clearance` mapping confirms that the label was equivalent at the event time; the audit documents strongly support substantive equivalence, but governance was late.
- Whether SUP-715 violated a conflict rule. The disclosed facts establish missing disclosure, but not a categorical prohibition on the relationship.
- Whether “the emergency still existed” after FD-7 expired. The factual condition may have continued, but legal or policy authority did not.
- Whether lack of transactions before sanctions clearance mitigates SUP-708. It mitigates realized harm, not the breach of the pre-activation control.
- Whether all 289 apparently eligible pilot cases met every timing and validation requirement. Aggregate evidence does not establish case-level compliance.

### Burden-of-proof approach

A finding of **NONCOMPLIANT** should require, on the balance of reliable evidence:

1. the case was within the rule’s subject-matter and temporal scope;
2. the obligation was valid and applicable;
3. trustworthy event or business evidence shows a breach; and
4. no valid exception applied.

For high-severity allegations, such as activation before sanctions clearance or unauthorized high-risk auto-approval, identity, timestamps, and scope should be corroborated rather than inferred solely from a lossy event log.

An asserted exception carries a stricter production burden: the operator should establish contemporaneous authority, effective dates, eligibility, required compensating controls, and immutable evidence. Informal expectation, retrospective preference, or continuation of emergency conditions does not establish authorization.

Where evidence is absent because collection is demonstrably incomplete, the conclusion should be **DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE**, not automatic innocence or automatic breach. Where applicability itself is unresolved, **MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT** is more accurate than noncompliance.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest prosecution case is that M0 is imperfect, but those defects do not explain the operation’s most important failures.

### Formal requirements remained controlling

CS-9 is controlled and formally approved. PD-3 was recorded in minutes and email, never incorporated into CS-9 or M0, and the Compliance observer expressly denied approving a weakening of CS-9. The most favorable reading of PD-3 is a narrow, monitored, temporary exception—not a general amendment. It cannot protect:

- ineligible suppliers, as in SUP-703;
- cases after 2026-07-31, as in SUP-714 and six aggregate August cases;
- missing sanctions or validation controls;
- cases with unproved eligibility among the nine incomplete records.

Likewise, FD-7 had explicit timestamps and expired automatically. SUP-704 fits its scope and controls, but SUP-705 occurred on July 13. The continued factual flood does not extend delegated authority. Allowing operators to infer extensions would defeat the purpose of bounded emergency governance.

### Exceptions were normalized beyond their limits

The evidence shows exception drift:

- 14 pilot cases were clearly ineligible.
- Nine lacked attributes needed to establish eligibility.
- Six more used auto-approval immediately after expiration.
- SUP-714 expressly reflects normalization based on anticipated permanent approval rather than actual approval.
- SUP-703 involved a high-risk foreign supplier, a false pilot selection, and EUR 180,000 first spend.

This is not just M0 obsolescence. It is evidence that operations converted a constrained trial into a de facto rule without completing policy change.

### Severe cases breached substantive controls

SUP-703 is a material failure under any plausible model. It was high-risk, not eligible for PD-3, lacked manual approval, deferred quality review, and involved EUR 180,000. No defensible pilot or emergency overlay applies.

SUP-708 is likewise materially noncompliant. Sanctions clearance occurred 40 minutes after activation, with no exception. The fact that no transaction occurred before clearance reduced realized exposure but does not satisfy the preventive requirement. Activation may itself confer system access, ordering capability, or reputational risk.

SUP-712 demonstrates why fitness against M0 is not equivalent to compliance. The requester and approver were the same human behind aliases, directly violating CS-9. A control framework cannot accept a syntactically fitting trace while ignoring resource identity.

SUP-705 and SUP-714 show conduct after explicit expiry. SUP-715 at least breached the disclosure expectation in the conflict-of-interest policy, although the record is insufficient to prove prohibited approval.

### Desirable conduct does not amend policy

Requesting missing certificates in SUP-707 was sensible and reduced risk. The prosecution should concede that M0 falsely marks this desirable behavior as deviant. But stakeholder approval of the behavior does not authorize unrelated auto-approval, post-activation review, or expired emergency practice.

Similarly, audit evidence can support `QA_Clearance` as equivalent to `Quality_Review` in SUP-713, but retrospective mapping approval reveals weak change control. Good substantive outcomes do not retroactively rewrite controlled standards.

### Data defects are real but bounded

The CRM migration makes missing notification events unreliable evidence of non-notification. SUP-702 has strong CRM evidence; SUP-706 has weaker, mutable linkage. The duplicate event in SUP-711 is plainly a migration defect. Those concessions do not rebut reliable evidence of premature or unauthorized activation.

The central prosecution proposition is therefore supported: the high deviation rate is partly inflated by model and data defects, but the operation also contains repeated, material, and sometimes deliberate violations of valid control boundaries.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense is not that all conduct was acceptable. It is that raw M0 conformance substantially overstates misconduct because M0 is an incomplete, temporally flat model applied to heterogeneous cases and imperfect logs.

### M0 is materially behind actual authorized process variants

M0 cannot represent:

- PD-3 auto-approval;
- FD-7 provisional activation;
- post-activation quality review under authorized overlays;
- repeated `Request_More_Info`;
- reactivation;
- abbreviated rejected-lead pre-screening;
- activity-label evolution;
- CRM-external notification evidence;
- resource separation or conflict rules.

Therefore, a low token-replay score against M0 is not a reliable compliance rate. It conflates policy breach, authorized exception, label mismatch, duplicate ingestion, missing collection, and scope ambiguity.

SUP-702 illustrates the problem. It was eligible, occurred within PD-3, had sanctions before activation, quality the next business day, and a stable CRM notification ID. Calling it misconduct solely because M0 lacks `Auto_Approve` and requires quality beforehand would ignore the most case-specific authority and evidence.

SUP-704 is even clearer. FD-7 expressly authorized the observed sequence, and all stated compensating controls were met. M0’s rejection of it is a model-version failure.

### POWL concurrency must be interpreted correctly

Within `checks`, M0 imposes no order between sanctions and quality. Their sequence in a trace is not itself a deviation. SUP-701 and SUP-712 conform in control-flow terms whether sanctions or quality was recorded first, provided both precede the decision.

Conversely, M0’s block edge from `checks` to `decision` means both checks must complete before either approval/rejection branch. That is stricter than CS-9’s wording for rejection and possibly inconsistent with abbreviated pre-screening.

### Extra activities and loops are not misconduct

SUP-707 obtained missing certificates before approval through two information cycles. No activation occurred before evidence arrived. The unmodeled loop is a model omission, not an operational control failure.

The same aggregate pattern appears in 47 cases, none of which activated before requested evidence arrived. This is systematic evidence that M0 is incomplete in a safety-enhancing direction.

### Logging problems explain some deviations

SUP-702’s missing onboarding notification is cured by stable CRM evidence. SUP-706’s case is less certain because the text link is mutable, but the post-migration jump from 2% to 29% missingness strongly suggests collection failure rather than a 27-point collapse in actual notification performance.

SUP-711’s duplicate registration shares one source transaction ID and is a classic ingestion duplicate. It should be deduplicated, not prosecuted as repeated business registration.

SUP-713’s `QA_Clearance` had substantively equivalent content documented in audit materials. The late mapping approval is a governance defect but does not turn completed quality work into absent quality work.

### Scope is unresolved for rejection and reactivation

SUP-709 may be an intake lead failing a pre-screen before M0 begins. If so, requiring a quality review is wasteful and contrary to CS-9’s allowance for abbreviated rejected-lead handling. The model owner and process owner disagree, and the scope document is ambiguous. Noncompliance cannot fairly be declared until the case-start rule is resolved.

SUP-710 is a reactivation with still-valid prior quality certification. CS-9 does not expressly define whether reactivation is a fresh onboarding. Missing `Register_Supplier` may reflect the wrong case type, and absent quality review may be permissible certificate reuse. This is a governance and scope gap, not a proved breach.

### Trace deviation is not equivalent to business misconduct

A process-mining trace is evidence, not the source of authority. The following must be distinguished:

- authorized variant: SUP-704;
- plausibly authorized but governance-contested pilot: SUP-702;
- model omission: SUP-707;
- data defect: SUP-711;
- scope ambiguity: SUP-709 and SUP-710;
- label equivalence: SUP-713;
- genuine breach: SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714.

The defense must nevertheless concede that informal practice cannot outrank controlled policy. Anticipated permanent approval does not save SUP-714, and ineligibility destroys the pilot defense for SUP-703.

For SUP-715, “MIXED” is not based on uncertainty alone: two distinct propositions have different outcomes. The onboarding sequence is compliant; the failure to record a required conflict disclosure is adverse, while disqualifying conflict or biased approval remains unproved.

### System-level judgment

**Operational compliance:** materially deficient, but not accurately measurable by raw M0 conformance.

The record establishes genuine material noncompliance:

- 14 clearly ineligible pilot uses;
- nine additional unproved eligibility cases;
- six post-expiry auto-approvals;
- severe examples SUP-703 and SUP-708;
- demonstrated SoD failure in SUP-712;
- unauthorized continuation in SUP-705.

These are not explained away by model obsolescence. Exception boundaries were not reliably enforced, and temporary practices were normalized.

At the same time, the evidence does **not** establish that all 312 auto-approved cases were noncompliant. The 289 apparently eligible cases should be separately assessed under PD-3, with attention to its authority defect. Nor should missing notifications, information loops, duplicate ingestion, label changes, rejected leads, and reactivations be counted uniformly as business violations.

**Model validity:** M0 remains a useful representation of the standard happy path, but it is not fit as the sole normative or audit model. It is incomplete in control flow, blind to time, amount, risk, identity, authority, and data provenance, and ambiguous in population scope.

### Where each side is rejected

I reject the prosecution’s implication that formal M0 fitness is a sufficient compliance test. SUP-704, SUP-707, SUP-711, and likely SUP-713 show that it is not. I also reject treating missing CRM-mirrored events as proof of omitted business action.

I reject the defense’s broader claim that obsolescence accounts for the material failures. M0’s defects do not excuse SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, or the 14 clearly ineligible pilot cases. Informal normalization, favorable outcomes, unchanged flood conditions, or expected amendments cannot substitute for valid authority.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

A defensible architecture should separate **structural process behavior** from **case predicates, temporal authority, resource constraints, and evidence quality**.

### 1. Revised control-flow layer: POWL

The POWL model should represent:

- a standard route with concurrent sanctions and quality checks;
- manual approval before activation;
- an eligible pilot/auto route;
- an emergency provisional route;
- repeatable information requests;
- rejection;
- notification before closure.

The model should describe the route shapes, not pretend to enforce dates, country, risk, amount, or delegated authority. Different route labels should be explicit so an overlay can validate why a route was selected.

### 2. Behavioral invariants: DECLARE or log-skeleton constraints

Use declarative constraints for cross-cutting obligations that are awkward to encode in one structured POWL:

- `Activate_Supplier` implies prior `Sanctions_Check` in every route.
- Standard activation implies prior quality and manual approval.
- Auto activation implies prior sanctions, master-data validation, and `Auto_Approve`.
- Provisional activation implies later quality and retrospective approval within deadlines.
- Every terminal decision implies notification before closure.
- Rejection excludes activation unless a documented new case/version starts.
- Requested evidence must be received or waived before approval/activation.
- No post-closure substantive activity except controlled correction or case reopening.

These constraints should be versioned by effective dates.

### 3. Data-aware controls: pandas or rule-engine checks

Use tabular checks for:

- risk class, jurisdiction, approved-group membership;
- pilot eligibility snapshot as of the decision time;
- effective date and event time;
- emergency classification and directive window;
- EUR 20,000 cumulative spend cap until retrospective approval;
- two-business-day quality deadline;
- master-data validation completion;
- certification validity for reactivation;
- immutable mapping from business activity labels to canonical labels;
- notification reconciliation between CRM and onboarding logs.

The checks must operate on event-time snapshots, not current mutable master data.

### 4. Resource and conflict controls

Use identity-resolution and organizational controls for:

- requester and manual approver must resolve to different canonical human IDs;
- delegation must be valid at approval time;
- service accounts must resolve to accountable principals where required;
- relationships or reporting-line conflicts must trigger disclosure and, where policy requires, recusal or independent review;
- aliases must not evade segregation-of-duties checks.

A categorical spouse prohibition should not be invented. The present rule should require disclosure and route the case according to the actual conflict policy.

### 5. Overlay governance

Every pilot or emergency overlay should have:

- unique `rule_version_id`;
- controlling authority and signed approval artifact;
- legal/policy precedence statement;
- exact effective start and end timestamps;
- eligible population predicate;
- route changes;
- compensating controls;
- owner and monitoring plan;
- automated expiry;
- explicit disposition of in-flight cases;
- immutable amendment and revocation history.

Systems should reject an overlay route if no active rule version matches the case snapshot. Extensions must be approved before or at the relevant event time; retrospective ratification should be recorded as remediation, not rewritten as contemporaneous authority.

### 6. Explicit scope rules

Define separate case types:

- **Pre-screen lead:** may be rejected after sanctions/intake pre-screen without full quality review.
- **Accepted onboarding:** full standard or authorized overlay routes apply.
- **Reactivation:** requires a reactivation start event, fresh sanctions, validity checks for reusable quality evidence, and approval rules defined by risk and dormancy duration.
- **Withdrawal:** requester withdrawal before decision should terminate through `Withdraw_Case`, notification where required, and closure.
- **Reapplication:** begins a new case linked to the prior case rather than reopening history invisibly.

These definitions belong in controlled policy and event schemas, with corresponding model variants.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The following model is deliberately permissive about route selection. POWL expresses allowed structural variants; external rules must determine whether a case was entitled to select the auto or emergency branch.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def sequence(*nodes):
    """Construct a strict sequence while retaining nested POWL blocks."""
    block = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for left, right in zip(nodes, nodes[1:]):
        block.order.add_edge(left, right)
    return block


# Canonical activities
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
request_more_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
master_data = Transition(label="Automated_Master_Data_Validation")
manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
provisional_activate = Transition(label="Provisional_Activate_Supplier")
monitor = Transition(label="Daily_Transaction_Monitoring")
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

# Repeatable information-gathering approximation:
# LOOP(do, redo) permits one or more Request_More_Info occurrences under
# process-tree/POWL loop semantics. XOR with tau additionally permits zero.
tau_no_info = SilentTransition()
info_repeat = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[request_more_info, SilentTransition()]
)
optional_info_cycle = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[tau_no_info, info_repeat]
)

# Standard route:
# sanctions and quality are concurrent/unordered, but both must finish
# before manual approval, activation, notification, and closure.
standard_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])
standard_route = sequence(
    standard_checks,
    manual_approval,
    activate
)

# Low-risk pilot/permanent-auto route:
# sanctions and master-data validation are concurrent prerequisites.
# Quality is represented after activation because the authorized pilot
# permits completion within two business days. External rules enforce
# eligibility, effective dates, and deadline.
auto_prerequisites = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, master_data])
auto_route = sequence(
    auto_prerequisites,
    auto_approve,
    activate,
    quality
)

# Emergency route:
# sanctions must precede provisional activation. Monitoring is modeled as
# a required evidence event, followed by retrospective quality and manual
# approval. Amount caps, daily cardinality, criticality, and deadlines are
# external data/declarative constraints.
emergency_route = sequence(
    sanctions,
    provisional_activate,
    monitor,
    quality,
    manual_approval
)

# Rejection is a separate decision outcome. Whether quality is required
# before rejection depends on case scope (pre-screen vs accepted onboarding)
# and is checked outside this deliberately broad route model.
decision = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[standard_route, auto_route, emergency_route, reject]
)

# Notification is structurally mandatory before closure for every outcome.
M1 = sequence(
    register,
    optional_info_cycle,
    decision,
    notify,
    close
)

print(M1)
```

### POWL limitations and approximation

- `Operator.LOOP` support and rendering can vary by PM4Py version. The model uses an XOR with a silent transition to allow zero information requests and a loop branch for repetition. If the installed version gives unsuitable loop semantics, use a bounded unrolling for discovery only, while enforcing true repeatability declaratively.
- The model places the information cycle before the route decision. Real cases may request more information at several stages. A broad “before terminal decision” rule is better enforced in DECLARE or as a lifecycle submodel than by duplicating loops throughout POWL.
- Emergency monitoring is represented by one activity token; POWL cannot verify “daily” cardinality against elapsed calendar days.
- The emergency route uses `Provisional_Activate_Supplier` rather than overloading `Activate_Supplier`. This distinction should be emitted by the source system. A later `Confirm_Activation` event could be added once retrospective controls are complete.
- The broad rejection route intentionally supports pre-screen rejection. Accepted-onboarding rejection requirements must be checked using `case_type` and policy predicates.
- A production architecture may use separate route-specific POWL models plus a governed router. That is safer than treating this union model as proof that every structurally available route is authorized.

### Event and case-data proposal

Each event should contain:

- `event_id`: globally unique and immutable;
- `case_id`, `case_type`, and linked prior case ID;
- canonical `activity_code` plus source label;
- `activity_mapping_version_id`;
- `occurred_at_utc` and immutable `recorded_at_utc`;
- source-system ID and source transaction ID;
- correction/supersession link rather than destructive update;
- actor account ID and canonical human ID;
- actor role, delegation ID, and delegation evidence hash;
- `rule_version_id` and selected route;
- authority artifact ID;
- reason code for exception selection;
- notification ID, channel, send timestamp, delivery status, and immutable case linkage.

At each consequential decision, persist an **eligibility snapshot** containing:

- risk level and risk-model version;
- country and approved-group status;
- critical-supplier designation;
- master-data validation result;
- quality certificate ID, validity period, and reuse decision;
- cumulative spend at activation and until final approval;
- requester and approver canonical human IDs;
- conflict disclosure status and review outcome;
- policy version and business-calendar version used for deadlines.

### Collection and validation controls

1. Emit notification events directly from CRM to an append-only event bus; do not rely on optional mirroring.
2. Use stable foreign keys, not mutable free text, for case linkage.
3. Deduplicate on source transaction ID while preserving raw rows and recording the deduplication decision.
4. Maintain an effective-dated actor-alias table so SoD checks resolve identities as they were known at event time.
5. Preserve both event time and record time to expose late entry and retrospective mapping.
6. Prevent selection of auto or emergency routes unless an active overlay and complete eligibility snapshot exist.
7. Freeze expiry automatically; continuation requires a new signed rule version.
8. Reconcile onboarding, CRM, procurement, spend, monitoring, and identity data, with unresolved joins reported as evidence-quality exceptions rather than silently treated as conforming or nonconforming.
