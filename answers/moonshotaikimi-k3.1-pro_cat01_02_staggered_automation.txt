**Not-yet/never-treated comparisons.** Define the descriptive difference-in-differences as adopter change minus control change, using equal branch weights. Negative cycle-time values mean a more favorable relative change, not necessarily an absolute reduction.

| descriptive contrast | median DiD, h | p90 DiD, h | rework DiD | escalation DiD | CSAT DiD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Alpha/Beta, Mar  Apr; controls Gamma/Delta/Epsilon | 1.57 | 2.85 | 2.20 pp | +0.90 pp | +0.058 |
| Alpha/Beta, Jan–Mar  Jul–Aug; controls Delta/Epsilon | 4.87 | 6.52 | 1.85 pp | +0.90 pp | +0.092 |
| Gamma, May–Jun  Jul–Aug; controls Delta/Epsilon | 4.18 | 7.65 | 2.10 pp | +0.925 pp | +0.103 |

The mature p90 row illustrates why interpretation matters: Alpha/Beta p90 **increased by 8.2 h on average**, but Delta/Epsilon increased by 14.72 h. Thus 6.52 h is “less growth than controls,” not an observed tail reduction. In the shortest Gamma transition, JuneJuly versus Delta/Epsilon, median and p90 DiDs are 3.65 h and 6.70 h.

**Treatment-intensity check.** June to August holds complexity and FTE constant within each branch and has nearly identical arrival declines, while adopters gain another 9 pp of fast-path intensity.

| branch | fast-path intensity change | arrivals change | median change | p90 change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Alpha | +9 pp | 9.7% | 19.1% | 15.6% |
| Beta | +9 pp | 9.7% | 8.2% | 7.0% |
| Delta | 0 pp | 9.6% | 8.5% | 7.1% |
| Epsilon | 0 pp | 9.7% | 17.4% | 15.0% |

A naïve within-adopter intensity slope would credit rising use with improvement. Yet Beta/Delta and Alpha/Epsilon have remarkably similar changes despite very different intensity changes. This does **not** establish no dose effect; it shows that calendar demand, branch composition, and learning prevent a credible dose-response estimate from these aggregates.

**Pre-trends, regression to the mean, and sensitivity.** Alpha/Beta provide only three pre-months: Alpha is nonmonotone and Beta drifts slightly upward, so there is no reliable trend or seasonality estimate. Selection on historically poor performance can nevertheless produce regression to the mean. Gamma deteriorates in May–June and then improves, while untreated branches also improve in July–August, making seasonal reversion a serious alternative explanation. Regression to the mean cannot explain every raw pattern—Alpha/Beta p90 rises—but it can inflate selected median improvements.

Window sensitivity is substantial. Using all post months instead gives Alpha median/p90 changes of 0.4/+9.7 h and Beta +1.9/+9.8 h; using all six Gamma pre-months gives 3.4/0.9 h. For Gamma’s JuneJuly contrast, using Delta or Epsilon alone gives median DiDs of 4.4 or 2.9 h and p90 DiDs of 8.1 or 5.3 h. Workload also changes: Alpha arrivals/FTE rises from about 31.2 to 40.1 across its table windows, and Beta’s from 31.2 to 36.5; Gamma’s immediate window is approximately 35.9 to 36.8. These ranged results are sensitivity scenarios, not confidence intervals. CSAT cannot be similarly bounded usefully because response counts and nonrespondent outcomes are unavailable.

## 6. Threats, tests, and decision consequences

| threat | why it matters here | direction of likely bias | test or additional evidence | consequence if threat is severe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Selection into adoption | Alpha/Beta were chosen for high volume and poor historical times; Gamma followed a readiness pilot | Regression to the mean favors pre/post benefit; persistent branch weakness could instead conceal benefit | Obtain longer historical data; model the selection rule; match on levels, trends, volume, and complexity | Adopter ATT does not transport to Delta/Epsilon; raw improvements may be spurious |
| Nonparallel trends and seasonality | Untreated branches also improve in July–August, and baseline levels differ | Naïve pre/post comparisons generally overstate benefit; relative DiD direction can vary by control | Longer pre-period, leads, placebo dates, leave-one-control-out analysis | Neither level nor percentage DiD is causally interpretable |
| Time-varying confounding | Routing, demand, product changes, and local practices coincide with rollout | Extra demand may bias against automation; unobserved local improvements may bias toward it | Daily arrivals by source and risk; documented practice changes; adjusted and unadjusted estimands | Reported effect may represent the implementation bundle rather than automation |
| Treatment intensity and version | Availability masks gradual uptake, learning, and possible rule changes | Binary estimates can attenuate mature benefit or average early harm with later benefit; dose slopes can be favorably selected | Monthly proficiency, overrides, system version, eligibility, and case-level assignment | No single “automation effect” or durable steady-state estimate |
| Fraud-desk spillovers and interference | Automated triage refers more borderline cases to a shared desk; waits are unrecorded | Congested controls can make relative DiD look favorable while total network harm is understated | Branch-specific referral, queue-entry, wait, capacity, and service-level data by adoption saturation | Control outcomes are not untreated counterfactuals; network cost is unknown |
| Staffing changes | Alpha loses two FTE, Beta gains one, and other branches also change | Alpha’s loss likely biases against benefit; gains elsewhere can bias toward it | Productive hours by activity, overtime, absence, backlog, and arrivals/FTE | Apparent cycle changes may be labor-capacity changes; adjustment may also block endogenous staffing responses |
| Case mix and composition | May complexity increases; fast-path selection changes the composition of remaining paths | Higher complexity biases raw post comparisons against benefit; unobserved selection can bias either way | Case-level preregistration risk, channel, and variant-stratified distributions | Median and p90 changes may be compositional rather than within-case improvements |
| Anticipation and readiness | Gamma’s pilot and manager preparation may begin before recorded availability | Pre-period contamination can attenuate a level shift or create a false trend | Pilot logs, training dates, pre-adoption system access, and anticipation leads | Event time zero is misdated and pre-trends are invalid |
| Survey nonresponse | Response varies around 20–45%, with no counts or respondent linkage | Direction unknown; improvements or harms can be exaggerated by changing respondent composition | Invitation census, response counts, channel and risk weights, linked case outcomes, nonresponse follow-up | No defensible customer-satisfaction effect or survey uncertainty interval |
| Measurement aggregation | Branch-month medians, p90s, and percentages conceal mixtures and within-branch variance | Direction unknown; Simpson-type reversals and misleading average-of-medians interpretations are possible | Case-level event data and variant/risk-specific distributions | Cannot infer case effects, decompose queues, or pool medians validly |
| Very few branches | There are only five clusters and as few as two credible controls | Not a signed bias, but cluster-robust significance can be severely anticonservative | Randomization-based inference prospectively; leave-one-branch-out analysis; avoid asymptotic claims | Apparently precise estimates may be noise or one-branch artifacts |
| Dynamic effects and unequal follow-up | Alpha/Beta have five post-months, Gamma two, and learning continues | Pooling can hide early harm or mature benefit and create misleading cohort weights | Prespecified event-time effects and longer follow-up at stable intensity | A pooled coefficient is not a useful rollout estimand |
| Treatment measurement and contamination | Fast-path percentage is 4–6% even when availability is zero | Usually attenuates an availability contrast, but direction is uncertain if versions differ | Audit denominators, background automation, pilot access, and case-completion rules | Availability and intensity are misclassified |
| Rework or harm displacement | Recorded rework can fall while work moves off-branch or after Close | Likely favors automation if only recorded in-branch loops are counted | Cross-variant loop counts, customer contacts, post-close reopening, fraud-desk activity | Apparent efficiency may be process substitution rather than reduced total work |
| Shared routing and transportability | Routing can shift both volume and case types between branches; Delta/Epsilon differ from adopters | Direction unknown; controls may be affected and expansion effects may be smaller or larger | End-to-end routing flows and prospective evaluation in Delta/Epsilon | Even a valid adopter ATT would not answer the expansion question |

## 7. Effect assessment

Confidence scores reflect confidence in the directional assessment, not the precision of a causal estimate. Numeric intervals below are scenario ranges from descriptive comparisons, not statistical confidence intervals.

| outcome | qualitative effect assessment | plausible range or scenario interval | confidence 0-100 | strongest supporting evidence | strongest counterevidence | claim leadership may safely make |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| `median_cycle_h` | Suggestive short-run relative improvement, but heterogeneous and not causally identified | Least-disrupted transition DiDs about **1.6 to 3.7 h**, roughly 3–8% of differing baselines; an alternative mature contrast is 4.9 h, while raw full-post Alpha/Beta changes include worsening. Zero or adverse causal effects remain possible. | 40 | Several availability comparisons are favorable relative to concurrent controls, especially Gamma | Endogenous selection, short pre-trends, workload changes, and raw branch heterogeneity | “Some comparisons associate availability with lower median cycle time; a universal percentage reduction is not established.” |
| `p90_cycle_h` | Tail effect unresolved; favorable relative comparisons coexist with absolute tail worsening | Transition DiDs about **2.9 to 6.7 h**, but mature Alpha/Beta p90 is **7–10% above own baseline**. | `rework_pct` | Most consistent suggestive signal: less recorded rework around implementation, with durability uncertain | Initial raw reductions **2.0–2.4 pp**; descriptive adjusted contrasts approximately **1.85 to 2.2 pp**. Later raw Alpha rework is 1.3 pp above its early baseline. | 55 | Similar initial pattern at both adoption dates while concurrent controls are approximately flat | May complexity shock, possible work displacement, and only two calendar adoption transitions | “Initial implementation coincided with about 2 pp less recorded rework; durable net work reduction is unproven.” |
| `escalation_pct` | Suggestive increase in specialist referral after availability; queue-time consequence unknown | Roughly **+0.8 to +1.0 pp** in immediate transition comparisons; longer raw own-baseline increases range from about **+1 to +3.1 pp**, including secular changes. | 60 | Repeated initial increase, including Gamma’s comparatively stable-complexity window | Shared fraud-desk changes, routing, and case mix can alter referrals independently | “Automation has not reduced escalation; plan and measure specialist capacity for a possible increase.” |
| `csat_1to5` | Customer-satisfaction effect not identified | Adjusted respondent-mean contrasts are approximately **+0.06 to +0.10**, but no defensible all-customer causal interval exists; nonresponse permits materially different effects. | 15 | No obvious sustained decline in reported means and some small favorable contrasts | Response rates of 20–45%, missing counts, changing case mix, and small operational signals | “No reliable CSAT benefit or harm can be claimed from these data.” |

Accordingly, the single statement **“automation reduces cycle time by X%” is not defensible**. Median and p90 require separate claims, and neither actual-use effects nor expansion effects are identified.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

expand with an embedded evaluation

Make Delta and Epsilon the next evaluation sites, but do not switch either branch on uniformly. After readiness screening, match operationally separable teams within each branch on baseline arrivals, complexity, p90, and staffing, then randomize teams to early or delayed waves. Delayed teams should remain untreated for a prespecified 12-week comparison window. Give Epsilon’s high-backlog teams priority for implementation support because of its greater complexity and tail burden, not priority in treatment assignment. If team queues cannot be separated, randomize eligible-case offers within both branches and treat branch-level system effects as a later question. Within early waves, randomly encourage eligible cases toward automation, log manual overrides, and retain human review; completed fast-path status must not define assignment.

Extract at least six months of case-level pre-period data before each wave, preferably 12 months to assess seasonality. Follow every wave for at least four months after availability, with additional follow-up until intensity stabilizes. Report monthly outcomes but collect daily queue states.

Collect case identifiers, activity timestamps, variant, registration risk and channel, automated offer and actual use, overrides, system version, anonymized resources, handoffs, active versus waiting time, rework loops, specialist and fraud-desk referrals, backlog, arrivals, productive hours, overtime, and post-close contacts. Report median, p90, and p99 by variant and risk stratum. Monitor completed cases per productive FTE alongside quality, so fast-path substitution is not mistaken for capacity creation.

For spillovers, record fraud-desk queue length, wait, capacity, and referring branch; also monitor shared-specialist queues and interbranch routing. Guardrails should include immediate review of severe customer harm and suspension of a wave if p90, backlog, or rework breaches prespecified noninferiority margins—for example, a 10% p90 margin—for two consecutive weekly reviews. CSAT alone cannot clear or stop a wave.

Scale only if randomization-based uncertainty excludes the p90 noninferiority breach, demonstrates a prespecified operationally material median or capacity gain, and shows no sustained escalation, rework, complaint, or customer harm after shared-queue costs. Otherwise revise triage rules or capacity and repeat the randomized evaluation.

def fit_descriptive(label, treatment_columns):
    regressors = treatment_columns + [
        "log_arrivals", "log_fte", "high_complexity_pct"
    ]
    X = pd.concat([df[regressors], branch_fe, month_fe], axis=1)
    X = sm.add_constant(X.astype(float), has_constant="add")
    y = df["log_median"].astype(float)

    # Branch-clustered covariance is requested, but five clusters are inadequate
    # for reliable cluster-asymptotic standard errors or p-values.
    fit = sm.OLS(y, X).fit(
        cov_type="cluster",
        cov_kwds={"groups": df["branch"]}
    )
    print(f"\n{label}")
    print(pd.DataFrame({
        "coefficient_log_scale": fit.params[treatment_columns],
        "branch_cluster_se": fit.bse[treatment_columns]
    }).round(4).to_string())
    for term in treatment_columns:
        pct_association = 100 * np.expm1(fit.params[term])
        print(f"{term}: {pct_association:.2f}% descriptive conditional "
              "association; NOT a causal effect.")
    return fit

## 10. Final claim ladder

- Observed: Fast-path intensity rose gradually; initial adoption windows showed roughly 2 percentage points less recorded rework and about 1 point more escalation relative to controls, while median and p90 had no single stable raw pattern.
- Suggestive: Some availability comparisons are consistent with a few hours lower median cycle time, but absolute p90 often remained elevated and the CSAT evidence is survey-limited.
- Conditionally causal: Only with conditional parallel trends, no anticipation or shared-desk interference, and adequate handling of demand, staffing, and case mix could selected availability contrasts be interpreted as short-run adopter ATTs; actual-use and Delta/Epsilon effects remain unidentified.
- Not supported: A universal claim that “automation reduces cycle time by X%,” creates capacity, improves p90 or CSAT, or justifies unrestricted immediate expansion.
