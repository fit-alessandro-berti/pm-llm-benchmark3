## 2. Redesign A: Decoupled two-axis triage — risk-gated lanes with a new "reviewed approval" route

**Redesigned process flow.**

1. `Intake claim`  **segment-aware decision engine** (replaces the outdated, non-segment-aware auto screen) classifies on three independent axes: claim value (€400 threshold), customer segment (Premium/Standard), and risk attributes (defect type, evidence completeness, fraud signals) — plus a fulfilment-mode tag (replace vs repair).
2. **Lane GREEN — expedited compliant.** Value  €400, low-risk accessory/low-risk device, evidence complete: automated evidence attestation  dispatch  close. 4. **Lane RED — reviewed approval (new edge).** Value > €400, fraud signal, or high-value device: mandatory `Manual eligibility review` or `Fraud/risk review`  **new decision node "Reviewed approval"**  pre-dispatch part-verification gate  dispatch  close. Where the defect type requires repair, the claim diverts to the repair path instead. **Review no longer implies repair.**
5. **Repair path** keeps its structure but gains part-price pre-fetch (removing the named cause of `Manual -> Repair quote` mean 22.0 h / p90 60.0 h).
6. `Quality check` becomes **mandatory for high-value repairs** (12.5% rework vs 4.0%) and uses currently idle inspector capacity.
7. **Reopen control:** every `Customer complaint/reopen` passes a mandatory eligibility checkpoint (R3) before escalation, closure or denial; the escalation board is reserved for genuine exceptions.

**Which segments/paths/activities it targets.** The 2,550 `Auto -> Fast approval` flows (R1/R2 exposure); the 35% high-value population (22%/18% fast-path reopens, €410 wrong-replacement cost); the structural `Manual -> Repair quote` coupling; and the 9.2% reopen rate. It deliberately does **not** touch the accessory fast lane.

**Mechanism of improvement.** Two mechanisms, both structural. First, it *restores a policy-sanctioned edge the log does not contain*: R1 explicitly allows review-then-approval, yet the DFG shows zero `Manual eligibility review -> Fast approval` transitions, so today every reviewed claim is forced through `Repair quote` (22 h)  `Customer approval` (30 h)  `Repair device` (20 h)  `Quality check` (9 h)  **81 h of mean chain before any queue time**, to resolve an *eligibility* question. Decoupling eligibility risk from fulfilment mode lets a €500 claim with clean evidence and a replaceable defect be reviewed and dispatched in hours instead of days. Second, it moves the compliance decision from a global rule to a **lane assignment made once at intake**, so R1/R2 become structurally impossible to violate in GREEN rather than being policed after the fact.

**What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved.** (i) **Review capacity.** Moving even 30–40% of the 2,550 fast approvals into a review lane adds ~765–1,020 reviews — **+29% to +39% over the observed 2,600 manual inflows**, at or above the top of the stated 15–35% warning band, and close enough to the 82% cliff that the fix could make high-value claims *slower* than today. (ii) The evidence loop, portal abandonment and part-price waits are untouched — that is Redesign B's job. (iii) The escalation board's 45-minute daily slot and the fraud specialists' Monday peaks are untouched. (iv) Residual accessory reopens (6–8%) remain, accepted as economically rational at €95 expected wrong-replacement cost. (v) **Conformance optics temporarily worsen**: the new edge will appear as a move-on-log until the normative Petri net is updated, so measured fitness may fall from 0.91 before it improves — leadership must be pre-warned or the pilot will be read as a regression.

## 3. Redesign B: Interlocked single-path hardening — one-shot evidence, a hard value interlock, part-price pre-fetch, and a selective pre-dispatch gate

**Redesigned process flow.** The topology is *kept*; the content of each activity and the interlocks between them are changed.

1. `Intake`  **updated segment-aware auto screen** (two-week deployment) carrying a **hard value interlock**: if value > €400 and no review is recorded, `Fast approval` is blocked and the claim is routed to review. Clean claims go to `Manual eligibility review` (7 reviewers); fraud signals go to `Fraud/risk review` (2 specialists) — routing on signal, not value alone, so the scarce specialists are not consumed.
2. **One-shot evidence contract:** a single evidence request with a 24-hour clock via the portal plus an assisted channel; a second request auto-escalates to a senior reviewer with an 8-hour SLA instead of re-entering the loop.
3. **Evidence attestation before dispatch** for every non-exempt case (R2), enforced at the dispatch node and also at `Escalation board -> Dispatch replacement` (65 cases) — the exit today's interlock thinking would miss.
4. **Part-price pre-fetch** on all repair quotes, attacking the stated cause of the delay in `Manual -> Repair quote` (mean 22.0 h, p90 60.0 h) and `Repair quote -> Customer approval` (mean 30.0 h, p90 80.0 h).
5. **Pre-dispatch quality gate, triggered by risk attributes** (defect type + part-complexity + value), applied to high-value replacements only — 6–18 hours added, used to attack the wrong-part driver behind `Dispatch -> complaint` (mean 96.0 h, p90 180.0 h) and the 12.5% high-value rework rate.
6. **R3 checkpoint** inserted before any reopen exit reaches close, denial or dispatch, converting the current 120–200 bypassing cases into reviewed ones.
7. `Quality check` mandatory for high-value repairs; escalation board repurposed to exceptions only.

**Which segments/paths/activities it targets.** Primarily the **"unclear defect / missing evidence" segment (22%, 140 h median, highest abandonment)** — 1,430 evidence entries, ~370 double-loop cases, and **320 of 390 total abandonments (82%) originating at the evidence step**; secondarily the high-value wrong-part rework; thirdly the R1/R2/R3 deviation set.

**Mechanism of improvement.** B removes *causes* rather than *routes*: evidence incompleteness (the loop with mean 34.0 h and 22.4% abandonment), part-price latency (a data problem), and part-selection error (a verification problem). Because it keeps a single auditable skeleton and prunes the unruly low-frequency activity combinations that today drag model precision down, **conformance becomes measurable rather than merely reported** — precision should rise, which is itself a diagnostic benefit. On economics it makes an explicit, defensible bet consistent with the 31%9% finding: for claims above €400, a slower reviewed path with 5–6% reopens is cheaper than a fast path with 18–22% reopens and €410 replacement errors.

**What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved.** (i) **It accepts a speed regression on high-value claims.** Claims that are today (wrongly) fast-approved in 20–28 h will move toward the 96–120 h reviewed band. B does not try to make review fast — that is A's contribution; it tries to make the slow path *less slow and less costly*. This must be stated openly to the sponsor rather than buried. (ii) **The evidence portal can raise abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers**, and that cohort correlates with the already-worst 22% segment — without a staffed assisted fallback, B can make the process worse. (iii) The rules update may push manual inflow up 15–35%, straight into the 82% queue cliff, so the two-week deploy must be staged behind an inflow guardrail. (iv) **The `Manual -> Repair quote` structural coupling is untouched**, so reviewed-claim median duration stays long even as its tail improves. (v) The pre-dispatch gate adds 6–18 h to high-value dispatches and consumes currently scarce inspector capacity. (vi) It needs IT/catalog dependencies for part-price pre-fetch; if the catalog has gaps, the loop simply *relocates from evidence to part price*.

## 4. Redesign C: Pull-based flow control with detective post-dispatch interception — WIP caps, pooled capacity, and proactive recovery

**Redesigned process flow.**

1. `Intake`  **customer choice of fulfilment mode at intake** (replacement or repair offered up front), which removes the `Repair quote -> Customer approval` mean 30.0 h **and** the `Customer approval -> Repair device` mean 20.0 h — roughly 50 h of customer-facing wait on the repair chain.
2. Auto screen runs as a **thin flag, not a gate**: it marks value/risk/evidence status and populates an **exception register** where policy permits deferred review; where policy does not permit it (R1's human-review requirement), review is **batched into a daily async review window** instead of sitting mid-process as a serial queue step.
3. **WIP caps / pull discipline:** in-progress manual reviews capped at 82% utilization (207 productive hours of the 252 available each week); fraud queue capped; campaign Mondays pre-allocated capacity. Work-in-progress is held *at intake with full visibility* rather than mid-process.
4. **Capacity pooling:** manual reviewers cross-trained to L1 fraud screening, reserving the 2 senior specialists for genuine fraud; **one underused quality-check inspector reassigned to post-dispatch verification and proactive contact**.
5. **Escalation board  30-minute daily exception triage with async decision rights**, removing the 54 h mean / 130 h p90 escalation wait and adding the R3 checklist.
6. **Post-dispatch proactive verification:** within 6–24 hours of dispatch, an automated part-match and eligibility check runs; where a mismatch is predicted, the customer is contacted **before the complaint window opens** (mean 96.0 h, p90 180.0 h) and the part is corrected as service recovery rather than a reopen.
7. **Sampled post-close audit (10–20%)** for R1/R2 evidence, feeding the exception register; automated monitoring of reopen signals.
8. **Proactive status and expectation setting** for Standard segments, whose 72-hour tolerance is explicitly *conditional on status visibility*.

**Which segments/paths/activities it targets.** The 46% Standard population (25% Standard accessory + 21% Standard high-value) where tolerance exists and compliance exceptions are most negotiable; the high-value wrong-part rework (12.5%); the queue-driven p90 tail (`Manual -> Repair quote` p90 60.0 h, `Fraud -> Repair quote` p90 50.0 h, `Escalation` p90 130.0 h); and the Monday/campaign fraud peak.

**Mechanism of improvement.** Three mechanisms, none of which is a routing change. First, C **exploits the latency of failure as an intervention window**: the very long tail that looks like a defect — mean 96 h / p90 180 h from dispatch to reopen — is in fact *slack*, long enough to detect and correct a wrong part days before the customer complains. Second, it **attacks variance rather than the average**: removing the 82% queue cliff and the Monday fraud peak compresses p90 while leaving p50 roughly flat — the correct objective given that the mandate cannot be met by average cycle time alone. Third, it converts scarce batch capacity (a 45-minute daily escalation slot) into an async flow, so the strong CSAT effect of escalation stops being a bottleneck.

**What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved.** (i) **Ex-ante compliance is not achieved.** A post-hoc audit cannot cure an R1 violation that already occurred at approval, and "complaint after close" is already a named deviation. C is only defensible where rules permit automated attestation or where the business accepts a documented, regulator- or legal-approved **exception register with remediation**. For R1's human-review requirement, C needs at least a thin interlock — at which point it partially overlaps B. This is a genuine organizational/compliance decision, not a modelling choice, and it must be escalated before piloting. (ii) **New labour is not in the resource plan**: intercepting even 10–20% of ~2,300 dispatches means 230–460 proactive contacts per log span. (iii) **WIP caps move waiting to the front**: customer-perceived "submitted  in progress" time can lengthen, and Standard tolerance for 72 hours is explicitly conditional on visibility — if visibility is not excellent, complaints rise despite an improving p90. (iv) Proactive contact can be perceived as an admission of fault and *trigger* complaints. (v) Detection will initially **raise measured defect counts** (an honest-looking regression), and C does nothing for the 140 h unclear/evidence segment or portal abandonment.

## 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A**
- **Failure 1 — the review queue cliff.** Moving ~765–1,020 fast approvals into review (+29–39% over observed manual inflow) pushes the 7-reviewer pool past 82% utilization (207 of 252 h/week). The queue then grows non-linearly and the redesign makes the high-value path *slower* than the 96–120 h baseline it was meant to improve — the classic outcome of "fixing" speed with a step that has no capacity.
- **Failure 2 — lane misclassification.** Value and defect-type attributes are not shown to be reliable, and the segment table has **no mid-risk bucket** at all. A high-value claim landing in GREEN is a fresh R1/R2 breach hidden behind a confident-looking automation; a Premium accessory landing in RED crosses the 24-hour threshold and triggers service credits — 2–5 pp of complaint benefit bought at credit cost.
- **Failure 3 — reviewers default to repair quote.** `Repair quote` is the only existing human exit from manual review besides evidence/fraud. Without new decision rights and a normative-net amendment, reviewers will keep routing to it, the decoupling never materialises, and the ~81 h chain persists while the organisation has paid for a redesign.
- **Bottleneck migration:** Manual eligibility queue  then `Fraud/risk review` (2 specialists) if red-lane routing leans on it  then the pre-dispatch gate and `Quality check`.

**Redesign B**
- **Failure 1 — portal-driven abandonment.** The prompt warns the portal may raise abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers, and that cohort maps onto the 22% "unclear/missing evidence" segment that already has the highest abandonment. Without a genuinely staffed assisted fallback, abandonment rises from a 7.8% base and valid claims are lost — B repairs R2 compliance while worsening the customer outcome it was meant to protect.
- **Failure 2 — interlock shock.** The rules update raises manual inflow 15–35%; combined with the value interlock, the queue crosses 82% and `Manual -> Repair quote` (already p90 60.0 h) stretches further. The pilot then reports a cycle-time *regression* that is really a mix shift — compliance improved while the headline metric worsened.
- **Failure 3 — incomplete interlock coverage.** Blocking `Fast approval` alone misses `Escalation board -> Dispatch replacement` (65 cases) and reopen exits, so ~28% of reopen exits still bypass R3 while the organisation reports R1/R2 success — the most dangerous failure mode because it produces false confidence.
- **Bottleneck migration:** `Quality check` — if the gate is triggered by value alone rather than risk attributes, ~1,200–1,400 high-value dispatches at an assumed 0.5–1.5 inspector-hours each = 600–1,800 h against roughly 648 h of quarterly capacity for 2 inspectors (assumption-dependent, stated for sensitivity) — **then** the part-price catalog/IT dependency, **then** assisted-channel staff.

**Redesign C**
- **Failure 1 — the compliance posture is rejected.** R1 requires a human review *before* Fast approval; an after-the-fact audit cannot cure it. Either legal/regulator blocks C, or exceptions accumulate silently and token-replay fitness falls below 0.91 while reported quality improves. This is C's existential risk, not a detail.
- **Failure 2 — interception never actually happens.** Proactive contact for 230–460 dispatches is unfunded; if unstaffed, C degenerates into "the current fast path with better reporting" — identical reopens, higher audit cost.
- **Failure 3 — WIP caps and fault-admission optics.** Front-loaded waiting lengthens "submitted  in progress" against a 72-hour tolerance that is explicitly conditional on visibility; and proactive contact may be read as an admission of fault, triggering complaints. Measured rework also rises because detection improved.
- **Bottleneck migration:** the new post-dispatch verification/proactive-contact pod; then **intake** (waiting relocated to the front of the process, invisible in activity-level metrics); then escalation, which can rise once visibility improves and customers engage more.

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**Phase 0 — measurement and data integrity (2 weeks, mandatory before any pilot).**
- Reconcile the DFG's broken node balances (`Manual` 2,600 in / 1,400 out; `Fraud` 980 / 760; `Dispatch` 2,365 / 2,580; `complaint` 510 / 430; only 3,625 of 5,000 cases reach END) and re-extract including the `close -> reopen` edge that the alignment names but the DFG omits.
- **Capture activity handle time.** Every duration in the prompt is an edge/waiting time; without handle time, no capacity, cost or utilization claim — including everything above — remains more than conditional.
- Capture the **claim-value distribution against the €400 threshold** (bounds R1 exposure, currently 0–2,550) and add the missing mid-risk bucket.
- Define the economic unit as **cost per stable close** — a close not followed by a reopen within 30 days — including complaint handling, reshipment, credits and reopen labour, so the 31%9% gap can be decomposed.

**Pilot 1 (weeks 3–6): Redesign B, lowest-regret slice — no routing change.**
One-shot evidence contract + portal with a staffed assisted fallback + part-price pre-fetch + automated evidence attestation before dispatch for non-exempt cases. Scope: **Standard low-risk accessory (25%, 72-hour tolerance, visibility-friendly)** with the unclear/missing-evidence segment as a secondary cell.
*Metrics:* % cases with 2 evidence requests (baseline 26%); `evidence -> next step` p50/p90 (baseline mean 34.0 h / p90 88.0 h); abandonment (baseline 7.8% overall, 22.4% of evidence entries); R2 attestation coverage on non-exempt dispatches; assisted-channel volume; manual utilization vs 82%; cost per stable close.
*Go:* repeat requests  25%; abandonment  1–2 pp overall and 2–3 pp in the low-literacy cohort; attestation 95%; manual utilization <82%.
*Stop:* abandonment  >3 pp overall or >5 pp in the low-literacy cohort; assisted channel exceeds staffed capacity; repeat requests  <10%.

**Pilot 2 (weeks 7–14): Redesign A, narrow slice — the new reviewed-approval edge.**
Scope: claims in a defined value band (e.g., €400–€1,500) with defect type "replace" and complete evidence  `Manual`/`Fraud` review  reviewed approval  pre-dispatch gate  dispatch, with a contemporaneous control cell on the current path.
*Metrics:* route median against the chain it replaces (`Repair quote` 22 h + `Customer approval` 30 h + `Repair device` 20 h + `Quality check` 9 h  81 h mean plus queues); R1 coverage; reopen rate against **both** comparators (fast 14.1%, repair 3.3%) — not against an overall average; manual and fraud utilization vs the 82% cliff and the Monday peak; **% of new-route cases still ending in `Repair quote`** (the direct test of whether decoupling is real); cost per stable close.
*Go:* reviewed-approval median 24–36 h; reopen 8–10%; utilization <82% at +20% inflow; <50% fall-through to repair quote.
*Stop:* sustained utilization >85%; reopen >12%; fall-through to repair >70%; p90 of review wait  versus baseline.

**Pilot 3 (weeks 9–16, overlapping): attribute-triggered pre-dispatch gate + R3 reopen checkpoint.**
Scope: high-value replacements carrying defect-type/part-complexity risk signals (not value alone — value-only saturation is the failure mode), plus a mandatory eligibility checkpoint on every reopen exit.
*Metrics:* high-value rework (baseline 12.5%)  target <8%; `Dispatch -> complaint` mean 96.0 h / p90 180.0 h tail compression; gate elapsed addition (allow 6–18 h; flag if p90 >18 h); `Quality check` utilization; % of reopen exits passing manual review (baseline bypass 24–39%)  target 95%.
*Go:* high-value rework  3 pp with gate addition 12 h p90 and inspector utilization <85%; R3 compliance 95%.
*Stop:* gate addition >18 h p90 persistently; inspector utilization >85%; high-value complaints unchanged.

**Pilot 4 (weeks 15–24): Redesign C — only after Pilots 1–3 have cleaned rework.**
Scope: Standard high-value (21%) and Standard accessory (25%) cells; **Premium excluded initially** (strict expectations plus high compliance risk). Introduce WIP caps at 82%, pooled/cross-trained capacity, escalation as async triage, and post-dispatch proactive interception.
*Metrics:* p50 **and** p90 — both customer-perceived and per-activity queue time; queue length and utilization at *every* activity (to detect migration); proactive contacts per 100 dispatches; interception rate (contacted-then-not-reopened vs control); reopen-lag-adjusted reopen rate; exception-register size; CSAT/visibility score.
*Go:* p90  15–20% with p50 not  >10%; interception 15% of would-be reopens; exception register not growing; CSAT stable or .
*Stop:* p50  >10% for two consecutive weeks; exception register growing (compliance debt accruing); reopens unchanged; CSAT  (contact perceived as fault admission).

**Sequencing logic.**
1. **Data first** — the DFG does not conserve flow and handle times are absent; every capacity and cost claim built on it is conditional.
2. **Loop elimination before re-routing** — evidence and part-price waiting contaminate the measurement of any structural change; this slice also carries the largest abandonment source (82% of abandonments) and improves R2 compliance with no routing risk.
3. **Structural change (A) once headroom is known** — the new edge needs a measured queue budget, not an assumed one.
4. **Detective/pull design (C) last** — it needs the cleanest data, the largest organizational change, and a compliance stance that only makes sense after preventive options have been built and measured.
5. **Observation window:** reopens arrive with mean 72–96 h and p90 150–180 h after approval, so **no pilot may be declared successful inside one week**; minimum window is 8–10 days, 30 days preferred.

**How to learn whether improvements are real rather than shifted downstream.**
- **Measure per stable close, never at `Quality/compliance close`.** The current process already shows the trap: 31% cheaper before rework, 9% after. Every pilot must report cost and cycle time including complaints, reshipments, credits and reopen labour.
- **Include censored cases.** Only 3,625 of 5,000 cases reach END; medians computed on completed cases systematically flatter the slow process because its unfinished cases are excluded. Use survival-style accounting or fixed-horizon cohorts.
- **Fixed-cohort comparison by segment × value × defect type, never overall averages.** Re-routing changes the mix: after A, the residual fast path will *look* better and the review path will *look* worse even with zero real improvement — a pure composition effect that will be misread as a result.
- **Maintain hold-out control cells for 4–6 weeks per pilot.**
- **Watch all activities, not just the piloted one** — manual at 82%, fraud Mondays, `Repair quote` part-price wait, `Quality check`, escalation's 45-minute slot. The redesign has worked only if *total* queue time across all activities falls.
- **Keep a total rework ledger** (evidence loops, reopens, reshipments, credits, rework repairs, escalation minutes) and compare like-for-like cohorts, explicitly testing whether the 31%-to-9% pattern is repeating in a new form: fewer reopens but more assisted-channel contacts; fewer repeat requests but more abandonment; fewer wrong parts but longer gate queues.
- **Re-run token replay and alignments after each pilot**, and distinguish "the model needs updating" (a legitimate new edge) from "a new deviation" — if fitness moves off 0.91 / 68% fitting, classify the change before judging it.
