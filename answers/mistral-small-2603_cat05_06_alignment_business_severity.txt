## 1. Materiality Framework

**Severity Framework (1–5 scale)**
| Dimension               | 1 (Negligible)                          | 2 (Low)                                 | 3 (Moderate)                            | 4 (High)                                | 5 (Critical)                            |
|-------------------------|-----------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| **Control Objective**   | Minor logging gap; no policy impact     | Non-critical policy deviation           | Policy deviation with compensating controls | Key control bypassed                    | Irreversible control failure            |
| **Amount/Exposure**     | < EUR 5,000                             | EUR 5,000–50,000                        | EUR 50,000–250,000                      | EUR 250,000–1M                          | > EUR 1M                                |
| **Reversibility**       | Easily reversible                        | Reversible with effort                  | Reversible with significant effort      | Difficult to reverse                    | Irreversible                            |
| **Timing**              | Within SLA                              | Minor SLA breach                        | Major SLA breach                        | Critical delay                          | No evidence of completion               |
| **Customer/Regulatory Harm** | No harm                              | Minor inconvenience                     | Moderate reputational risk              | Regulatory breach                       | Regulatory sanction or fraud            |
| **Recurrence**          | Isolated                                | Low frequency                           | Medium frequency                        | High frequency                          | Systemic                                |
| **Evidence Reliability**| Strong, verifiable                      | Mostly verifiable                       | Some gaps; plausible                    | Significant gaps; weak                  | No credible evidence                    |
| **Model Scope**         | Out of scope (e.g., telemetry)          | Minor scope mismatch                    | Policy overlay not encoded              | Core process deviation                  | Entire process bypassed                 |

**Confidence vs. Severity**
- **Confidence** reflects the reliability of the evidence and the clarity of the deviation’s impact on business objectives. It is not a direct proxy for severity.
  - *High confidence*: Direct evidence (e.g., missing approval for a EUR 90,000 claim) with no plausible alternative explanation.
  - *Low confidence*: Ambiguous evidence (e.g., hidden model move with no business activity trace) or conflicting data (e.g., external adjuster report vs. missing log event).
- **Severity** is an assessment of the *business impact* of the deviation, regardless of the confidence in its detection. A low-confidence but high-severity case (e.g., potential fraud) may warrant urgent investigation.

## 3. Repeated Deviation Patterns

### Pattern 1: Retry Duplicates and Telemetry Noise
- **Cases**: CL-601, CL-603
- **Frequency**: 74 cases/month with duplicate `Notify Claimant` retries; UI telemetry noise in all cases.
- **Business Risk**: Negligible (P5 violations). No customer/regulatory harm.
- **Likely Cause**: Poor event-logging design (UI telemetry and retry connectors treated as activities).
- **Confidence**: 5 (clear pattern; no ambiguity).
- **Owner**: IT/Logging Team
- **Recommended Action**:
  - Exclude UI telemetry and retry events from activity logs.
  - Update process tree to explicitly exclude non-business activities.

### Pattern 2: External-Adjuster Evidence Gaps
- **Cases**: CL-606, CL-602 (partial)
- **Frequency**: 9 high-value cases with adjuster reports; 2 lack stable document-to-case links.
- **Business Risk**: Moderate to high (undocumented approvals, missing evidence).
- **Likely Cause**: Poor integration between external adjuster systems and core claims system.
- **Confidence**: 3 (evidence is external; may be incomplete).
- **Owner**: Claims Operations + IT Integration
- **Recommended Action**:
  - Implement automated document-to-case linking with immutable identifiers.
  - Require adjuster reports to be logged as `Assess Loss` events before approval.

### Pattern 3: Data-Aware Policy Failures (Auto Approve)
- **Cases**: CL-610, CL-609 (false negative), CL-607/CL-612 (scope issues)
- **Frequency**: 4 cases with synchronous `Auto Approve` despite policy violations.
- **Business Risk**: High (undocumented approvals, scope mismatches).
- **Likely Cause**: Data integration gaps (bank changes, withdrawal events) and model scope ambiguity (P6).
- **Confidence**: 4 (clear policy violations but data gaps).
- **Owner**: Data Engineering + Model Owners
- **Recommended Action**:
  - Integrate bank-change data into approval logic (P1).
  - Clarify model scope (P6) and add withdrawal/denial branches.

### Pattern 4: Missing Evidence (Notification and Assess Loss)
- **Cases**: CL-608, CL-606
- **Frequency**: High-value claims with missing core log events but external evidence.
- **Business Risk**: High (unreliable evidence, potential regulatory breach).
- **Likely Cause**: Poor logging of critical activities; reliance on mutable CRM fields.
- **Confidence**: 3 (external evidence may be incomplete).
- **Owner**: Claims Operations + IT
- **Recommended Action**:
  - Enforce immutable logging for `Notify Claimant` and `Assess Loss`.
  - Audit CRM links for reliability.

### Pattern 5: Label Mismatches
- **Cases**: CL-611
- **Frequency**: Isolated but recurring (data mapping table updates post-go-live).
- **Business Risk**: Low (no policy impact; activity occurred).
- **Likely Cause**: Inconsistent activity naming conventions; delayed mapping updates.
- **Confidence**: 4 (clear labeling issue).
- **Owner**: Business Analysts + IT
- **Recommended Action**:
  - Standardize activity names across systems.
  - Enforce mapping updates before process changes go-live.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

1. **Cost-Function Design**:
   - The cost function assigns uniform penalties (c=1) to all visible/log/model moves, ignoring business significance. For example:
     - **CL-601**: Raw cost=3 for UI telemetry noise (severity=1).
     - **CL-604**: Raw cost=3 for timing deviation (severity=5 due to irreversible payment).
   - Business risk depends on *irreversibility* (e.g., payment before approval) and *evidence reliability* (e.g., undocumented fraud checks), not move counts.

2. **Scope Mismatch**:
   - The model scope (P6) excludes denied/withdrawn claims, but the alignment penalizes model moves for these cases (e.g., **CL-607**, **CL-612**). Raw cost reflects model misalignment, not policy violation.

3. **Hidden Moves**:
   - Silent moves (`T(tau)`) have cost=0 but may obscure policy violations (e.g., **CL-605**). The cost function misses these.

4. **Data Conditions**:
   - Policy P1 depends on bank-change data, which may not be in the log. The cost function penalizes missing `Fraud Screen` (e.g., **CL-610**) without considering whether the data was available.

5. **Activity Granularity**:
   - Label mismatches (e.g., `Assess Damage` vs. `Assess Loss` in **CL-611**) incur cost=2, but the business activity occurred. The cost function conflates labeling noise with actual deviations.

6. **Duplicated Telemetry**:
   - Retry events and UI telemetry (e.g., **CL-603**) inflate raw cost without business impact. The cost function treats noise as deviations.

7. **Missing-but-External Evidence**:
   - Cases like **CL-606** (external adjuster report) and **CL-608** (CRM link) have low raw cost for missing events but high severity due to unreliable evidence. The cost function ignores external data.

8. **Irreversibility**:
   - **CL-604** (payment before approval) has raw cost=3 but severity=5 because the payment cannot be reversed. The cost function does not account for reversibility.
