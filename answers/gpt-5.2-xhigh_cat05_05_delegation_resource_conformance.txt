## 2. Rule Interpretation

### RR1 — Drafter/approver separation (four-eyes)
- **Formalization**: For each case \(c\), let \(d\) be the `Draft_Change_Request` event and \(a\) the `Approve_Change` event. After alias normalization and (only if justified) service-account attribution, require:
  - \(Human(d) \neq Human(a)\)
  - \(Human(d)\) and \(Human(a)\) must be resolvable to humans; if either is a service account without strong attribution, mark RR1 as **not reliably evaluable** for the control objective (even if a tool-level four-eyes filter “passes”).

### RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation (four-eyes)
- **Formalization**: For each case \(c\), let \(i\) be `Implement_Change` and \(v\) be `Verify_Implementation`. Require \(Human(i) \neq Human(v)\) with the same identity-evidence caveat as RR1.

### RR3 — HIGH-risk approval authority (non-emergency)
- **Formalization**: For HIGH-risk and non-emergency cases, `Approve_Change` must be performed by:
  - **QM principal** at event time (standing role QM), or
  - **Valid QM delegate** at event time, where validity requires all of:
    - **Interval**: approval timestamp is within the delegation effective interval (inclusive).
    - **Scope**: case `scope` is covered by the delegation scope (treated here as exact match to the case-level `scope` value).
    - **Principal-absence condition**: satisfied at event time when required (evaluated from formal leave/unavailability records; mere “it was a weekend” is not assumed to equal principal absence).
    - **No prohibited subdelegation**: actor is the named delegate, and delegation does not rely on a prohibited onward delegation.

### RR4 — Emergency substitution (HIGH-risk + emergency)
- **Emergency approval authority**: For HIGH-risk emergency cases, implementation is permitted after approval by the **IC principal** or a **valid IC delegate** (interval + scope + no prohibited subdelegation; D-IC-03 does not require principal absence).
- **Retrospective QM review**: A `Retrospective_QM_Review` must occur **within two business days** and must be performed by QM or valid QM delegate (same validity logic as RR3).
- **Business-day counting**:
  - Business days are **Mon–Fri**, excluding holidays; no holidays are present in the provided calendar evidence.
  - “Within two business days” is interpreted as **by 23:59:59 UTC of the second business day after the trigger** (trigger = `Implement_Change`; if missing, use `Approve_Change`).
  - Example: trigger on Tue 2026-08-11 ⇒ day1 Wed 2026-08-12, day2 Thu 2026-08-13 ⇒ deadline Thu 23:59:59.
- **Emergency credibility**: only the emergency flag is provided. Absence of a safety rationale means the exception is treated as **plausible but not fully evidenced**.

### RR5 — Identity evidence
- **Service account**: cannot satisfy human-role constraints unless session evidence maps it to a human at the event time.
- **Aliases**: must be normalized before SoD checks. Here, only `b.koch` ≡ `u_bk` is applied.
- **Event-time roles**: role intervals are evaluated at the event timestamp (e.g., Eva is not QM on 2026-08-09 and is not QM on 2026-08-15 either, even though her standing role changed to Quality Engineer on 2026-08-12).

### RR6 — Suspicion versus proof
- Repeated pairs, reciprocity, concentrated handovers, shared workstations, and absence conflicts are treated as **risk indicators** that motivate additional investigation and sampling. They are **not proof** of collusion without supporting evidence.

### Principal action while on leave and “late” delegation records
- **Principal acts while on leave**: If the principal has the standing role (QM), the action remains **authorized** for RR3, but it is flagged as an **availability/credential-risk anomaly**.
- **Delegation registered after the event**: a post-hoc database entry alone does not prove event-time authority. A contemporaneous signed delegation artifact (e.g., a signed PDF timestamped before the event) increases confidence that authority existed at the event time.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

### Indicator: repeated Clara (draft) → Ben (approve) pairing in HIGH-risk production changes
- **What it suggests**: concentration of authority pathways and potential rubber-stamping risk; may reduce independence even without explicit RR1/RR2 violations.
- **Benign explanations**:
  - Stable team assignment and specialization in one production cell.
  - High throughput with limited QM coverage during Asha’s leave, making Ben the operational delegate.
- **Adverse explanations**:
  - Coordinated “fast-pass” approvals with inadequate scrutiny.
  - Social pressure or implicit quid pro quo leading to weak challenge function.
- **Discriminating evidence**:
  - Approval latency distributions, rejection/return rates, and comment quality for Ben vs Asha.
  - Audit sampling of the 18/23 cases for substantive review artifacts (risk assessment, attachments, deviation justification).
  - Formal workload/coverage plan showing why Ben was delegated and whether second-level review existed.

### Indicator: reciprocal pattern (Ben drafts LOW-risk training changes that Clara approves)
- **Benign explanations**:
  - Cross-coverage for low-risk administrative work; informal division of labor.
  - Clara is the documentation steward for training materials.
- **Adverse explanations**:
  - Reciprocity used to normalize weak governance and exchange approvals.
  - Training-document updates used as a “testing ground” for bypass practices.
- **Discriminating evidence**:
  - Presence/absence of independent QA checks on training changes.
  - Content-level diffs and risk assessments for those training changes.
  - Rotation policies or peer review requirements for training-document updates.

### Indicator: service account (`svc_qms`) used for verification steps
- **Benign explanations**:
  - System integration design forces verification through a service identity while a human performs the review elsewhere.
  - Temporary account provisioning issue required a workaround.
- **Adverse explanations**:
  - Intentional masking of the true actor (including self-verification by implementers).
  - Shared credentials enabling non-authorized staff to “complete” regulated steps.
- **Discriminating evidence**:
  - Per-session or per-action mapping from `svc_qms` to an authenticated human (step-up auth, token logs).
  - Terminal/session logs and application audit trails proving who executed the verification action.
  - Policy stating when service accounts are allowed for regulated steps and compensating controls (dual attestation).

### Indicator: CHG-503 `svc_qms` session from Diego-associated workstation (WS-DM-04)
- **Benign explanations**:
  - Verifier used a nearby shared terminal in the maintenance area due to time pressure.
  - Workstation assignment list is stale; WS-DM-04 used by multiple roles.
- **Adverse explanations**:
  - Implementer performed verification, defeating RR2’s control objective.
  - Shared workstation enables “hand-off theater” without real independence.
- **Discriminating evidence**:
  - Individual re-authentication at the workstation, badge proximity logs, and application-level “effective user” fields.
  - Configuration management records for workstation assignment and access control lists.

### Indicator: approvals/reviews recorded during formal absence (Asha on leave; Ben on sick leave)
- **Benign explanations**:
  - Asha handled a single urgent case while on leave (supported by VPN timing overlap).
  - Sick leave start time was administrative; Ben remained onsite briefly.
- **Adverse explanations**:
  - Credential compromise or delegation of credentials to colleagues.
  - Intentional use of absent persons’ identities to pass authority checks.
- **Discriminating evidence**:
  - MFA/FIDO token events, device fingerprints, badge logs, workstation logins at the exact timestamps.
  - HR/incident records documenting any authorized “work while on leave” exceptions.

### Indicator: attempted IC subdelegation (D-IC-04) used by Clara in CHG-510
- **Benign explanations**:
  - Misunderstanding of delegation constraints during an incident; email used in lieu of proper registration.
- **Adverse explanations**:
  - Attempt to bypass “no subdelegation” constraints to approve self-drafted emergency changes.
- **Discriminating evidence**:
  - Incident command logs showing who held authority at the time.
  - Training/awareness records about delegation rules.
  - System enforcement evidence indicating whether the QMS would have blocked subdelegation if properly used.

## 6. Control Improvements

- **Delegation creation and enforcement**
  - Create delegations in the QMS **before** first use; require immutable timestamps and signer identity.
  - Make delegation scope machine-enforceable (controlled vocabulary aligned to `scope`), including explicit incident identifiers for IC delegations.
  - Enforce “no subdelegation” technically (block onward delegations, prevent approvals when a subdelegation chain is detected).
- **Effective-time and migration integrity**
  - Preserve original creation times and signatures during migrations; flag any delegation records created after their first referenced event.
  - Bind each approval to the specific delegation artifact (ID + hash of signed PDF) used to justify it.
- **Principal availability and absence conditions**
  - Integrate leave and on-call schedules so “principal absent/unavailable” is auditable at event time.
  - Require explicit “principal unavailable” attestation for delegations whose validity depends on it (especially on weekends).
  - Flag and review any principal approvals performed during recorded leave.
- **Alias and identity management**
  - Maintain a canonical identity map and prevent parallel usernames for the same person in regulated steps.
  - Detect and alert on mixed-alias usage within a single case (e.g., `b.koch` vs `u_bk`) before closure.
- **Authentication and service accounts**
  - Prohibit service accounts for human approval/verification steps, or require step-up per-user re-auth and store the effective user in the audit trail.
  - Eliminate shared workstations for regulated actions or require individual re-authentication on every regulated event.
  - Implement anomaly detection for actions during sick leave/leave and for office-IP actions claimed not performed by the user.
- **Emergency governance (RR4)**
  - Enforce retrospective QM review deadlines with automatic escalation and case blocking if overdue.
  - Require a structured emergency rationale and incident linkage (e.g., CI-44) in the case record.
- **Segregation-of-duties strengthening**
  - Add compensating controls for small-team contexts: second-level review, sampling, or rotation for repeated drafter/approver pairs.
  - Monitor “self-approval” and “verification via service account” as high-priority exceptions.
- **Network monitoring and evidential retention**
  - Periodically compute working-together and handover networks to detect concentration and sudden shifts, then drive targeted audit sampling.
  - Retain badge logs, MFA/FIDO logs, workstation session logs, and incident-command logs for the full compliance retention period.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

The log shows a mix of (a) **genuine authority/SoD failures** and (b) **identity/evidence weaknesses** that prevent reliable conformance conclusions. Clear control failures include self-approvals (CHG-502, CHG-508, CHG-510), a HIGH-risk approval without QM authority (CHG-507), and an emergency process with an overdue or weakly authorized retrospective QM review (CHG-505). Identity/evidence issues materially affect assurance in HIGH-risk cases where approvals or verifications are recorded under `svc_qms` (CHG-503, CHG-509) or under a delegate’s account during sick leave (CHG-506), which can conceal either benign workarounds or unauthorized actions.

Overall, a reasonable systemic read is that **roughly half** of the apparent deviations are likely **true governance breakdowns** (especially RR1/RR3/RR4 violations), **roughly a third** reflect **identity/data quality deficiencies** (service accounts, absence conflicts, aliasing), and the remainder indicates **organizational-pattern risk** (high concentration of Clara→Ben handoffs) that increases the chance of weak challenge even when formal rules are met. Uncertainty remains high where service-account attribution or absence-condition evidence is missing.

Highest-priority investigations are:
