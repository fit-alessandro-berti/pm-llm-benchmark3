# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

For case \(c\) and branch \(i\in\{A,B,C\}\), let:

- \(a_c\): arrival
- \(f_c\): fork release
- \(s_{ic}, e_{ic}\): branch start and completion
- \(s^F_c, e^F_c\): final-activity start and completion

All durations below are in minutes.

### Components

\[
U_c=f_c-a_c
\]

is upstream elapsed time. It cannot be split further into upstream queue and service from the supplied timestamps.

For each branch:

\[
Q_{ic}=s_{ic}-f_c
\]

is the observed post-release launch delay. Calling it a physical queue assumes that every delay after release is caused by branch resource availability; it could also include a branch-specific release policy.

\[
S_{ic}=e_{ic}-s_{ic}
\]

is elapsed branch service, and

\[
R_{ic}=Q_{ic}+S_{ic}=e_{ic}-f_c
\]

is branch release-to-completion time.

Under the stated AND-join rule:

\[
J_c=\max_i e_{ic}
\]

is `join_ready`, and

\[
K_c=\{i:e_{ic}=J_c\}
\]

is the set of critical branches. If a single label is required for a tie, the deterministic order \(A<B<C\) is used while retaining the full tie set separately.

The elapsed fork-join stage is:

\[
R_c=J_c-f_c=\max_i R_{ic}
\]

Branch-specific synchronization waiting is:

\[
W_{ic}=J_c-e_{ic}=R_c-R_{ic}
\]

The downstream delay and final service are:

\[
D_c=s^F_c-J_c
\]

\[
S^F_c=e^F_c-s^F_c
\]

Finally:

\[
T_c=e^F_c-a_c=U_c+R_c+D_c+S^F_c
\]

### Why branch synchronization waits are not additive case elapsed time

For every branch separately:

\[
Q_{ic}+S_{ic}+W_{ic}=R_c
\]

Thus, with three branches:

\[
\sum_i(Q_{ic}+S_{ic}+W_{ic})=3R_c
\]

Using that sum in a case flow-time equation would count the parallel fork-join interval three times:

\[
U_c+\sum_i(Q_{ic}+S_{ic}+W_{ic})+D_c+S^F_c
=T_c+2R_c
\]

Similarly, adding \(\sum_i W_{ic}\) on top of \(R_c\) overstates elapsed time because the synchronization waits overlap one another and overlap work still occurring on later branches. They are valid branch-token waiting measures, but not additive case elapsed time. For example, F07 has \(25+0+52=77\) branch-minutes of synchronization waiting inside a fork-join interval that lasts only 75 minutes.

A useful single-branch influence measure is the critical lead:

\[
L_{ic}=\max\left(0,R_{ic}-\max_{j\ne i}R_{jc}\right)
\]

It is the maximum case-level fork-join saving available by accelerating only branch \(i\), before another branch takes over. A tied branch has zero immediate critical lead.

### Interpretation

1. **B is the strongest controller of completion.**  
   It is critical in eight cases, including F07 and F12 where the fork-join stage reaches its maximum of 75 minutes. Its total critical lead is \(12\times10=120\) minutes, versus 15 for A and 12 for C.

2. **A has the longest mean service but is not the dominant branch.**  
   A always takes 50 minutes and never queues, while B’s mean queue of 19.2 minutes pushes its mean release-to-completion time to 56.7 minutes. This is why service time alone gives the wrong ranking.

3. **B also controls the tail.**  
   The fork-join P90 is 74 minutes and the maximum is 75; B’s own offset has the same P90 of 74 and maximum of 75.

4. **A and C are a defensible near-tie for second place.**  
   A is a stable 50-minute baseline and has a slightly larger observed total critical lead. C has a much smaller average offset but a more volatile queue: its P90 offset is 53 and its maximum is 57, enough to overtake A in F04 and F08. In redesign terms, C’s administrative queue spikes deserve attention even though its mean service is short.

5. **Synchronization profiles confirm the ranking.**  
   B has only 4.3 minutes of mean synchronization waiting because it is commonly last. C waits 30.4 minutes on average after completion because it usually finishes early. Reducing C service further would generally create more C synchronization rather than reduce case flow.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

## Counterfactual assumptions

**Assumption CF-1:** Branch changes do not alter arrival, fork release, other branch durations, or final service.

**Assumption CF-2:** Percentage reductions are calculated as continuous minutes. Thus a five-minute queue can become 2.5 minutes, despite the original one-minute timestamp resolution.

**Assumption CF-3:** The branch-counterfactual savings below are direct fork-join savings and potential total-flow savings if the downstream wait duration remains unchanged and the final start can move earlier. If final activities are fixed appointments, the final starts may remain unchanged and these branch savings would instead be absorbed by larger downstream waits.

**Assumption CF-4:** The immediate-final scenario assumes all relevant final work is represented, F1/F2 and an added third resource are interchangeable, and dispatch is work-conserving. These assumptions are not established by the log.

## 1. Reduce A service by 20%

A service falls from 50 to 40 minutes in every case.

- **Direct workload reduction:** \(12\times10=120\) branch-service minutes.
- **Fork-join and potential flow saving:** only 15 minutes, or 1.25 per case.
- **Propagation ratio:** \(15/120=12.5\%\) of the removed A elapsed service reaches case flow under CF-3.

Only F01 and F11 benefit:

- F01 saves 5 minutes; B becomes critical at 45 minutes.
- F11 saves 10 minutes; A and B tie at 40 minutes.
- The other ten cases receive zero benefit because B or C remains critical.

The observed P90 and maximum total flow time remain 124.5 and 130 minutes. Thus this redesign could improve A’s resource capacity, but it does little for the case-flow tail in this sample. It also causes A to finish earlier and wait longer at the join in many cases.

## 2. Reduce B queue delay by 50%

The total observed B queue is 230 minutes, so the direct branch waiting reduction is 115 minutes.

- **Fork-join and potential flow saving:** 92.5 minutes, or 7.71 per case.
- **Positive cases:** all eight cases where B was originally critical.
- **Zero cases:** F01 and F11 remain A-controlled; F04 and F08 remain C-controlled.
- **Masking inside the join:** F05 saves 10 rather than its 12.5-minute B queue reduction because A takes over at 50; F10 saves 5 rather than 10 for the same reason.

Under full downstream propagation, sample total-flow P90 falls from 124.5 to 114.5 minutes, and the maximum falls from 130 to 115. This is materially better than accelerating A.

Likely secondary effects are:

- A becomes critical or tied in more cases.
- C remains the limiting branch in its two queue-spike cases.
- B synchronization waiting increases once B finishes before A or C.
- Earlier join readiness may send cases to F sooner and increase final-station contention.
- If the queue reduction is achieved only by reprioritization, delay may be redistributed between cases rather than removed. A system-wide reduction may require capacity, variability, setup, or release-control changes, none of which can be distinguished here.

## 3. Add final capacity or otherwise remove downstream waiting

An optimistic immediate-start scenario removes the 176 observed downstream minutes:

- Mean total flow falls from 115.25 to 100.58 minutes.
- P90 falls from 124.5 to 114.0.
- Maximum falls from 130 to 115.
- Every case benefits because every observed downstream delay is positive.

However, this should not automatically be interpreted as a 176-minute benefit from purchasing a third station. Under the closed-log and interchangeable-resource assumptions, a work-conserving two-server FCFS schedule would create only one capacity wait: five minutes for F11. The other observed waits occur when such a simplified two-server model would have capacity available. Under that model:

- immediate dispatch with the existing two resources removes 171 of the 176 observed minutes;
- a third resource removes only the remaining five-minute F11 conflict.

Therefore, the data are at least as consistent with release rules, appointments, qualifications, omitted work, or non-work-conserving dispatch as with insufficient final capacity. A third station is an upper-bound scenario, not a causal estimate.

### Downstream masking of branch improvements

If observed final starts are fixed, none of the 15 minutes from the A counterfactual or 92.5 minutes from the B counterfactual changes final completion. The earlier joins simply enlarge `downstream_queue`. If F is dynamically dispatched and has capacity, some or all of the fork-join savings can propagate. The supplied timestamps cannot determine where between these two scheduling responses the real process lies.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

## Recommendation 1: Pilot a B queue-reduction intervention

B is the best branch target because it is critical in 8 of 12 cases, has a mean queue of 19.2 minutes, and controls the fork-join tail. A reasonable pilot could test additional flexible capacity, setup reduction, release smoothing, or a revised dispatch rule, but the log does not identify which mechanism is appropriate.

- **Throughput:** If B queueing reflects insufficient capacity, reducing it may increase effective fork-join throughput. If it is only reprioritization, aggregate throughput may not change.
- **Flow-time tail:** The modeled 50% queue reduction cuts potential total-flow P90 by about 10 minutes and maximum flow by 15 minutes.
- **Utilization:** Additional B capacity would probably reduce utilization per B resource; scheduling or variability improvements could reduce queues without the same utilization penalty. B resource identities and availability are not logged, so this trade-off is not measurable yet.
- **New bottlenecks:** A’s fixed 50-minute duration becomes the main floor, while C remains critical during administrative queue spikes. F may also receive work earlier.
- **Synchronization:** B will finish earlier and incur more synchronization waiting. That is acceptable if case flow improves, but it means B’s local “idle after completion” should not be misclassified as a new inefficiency.
- **Measure first:** Record B queue entry, resource assignment, resource availability, setups, WIP, dispatch priority, interruptions, and reasons for delayed start. Pilot on a limited release cohort and compare fork-join P90, critical-branch shares, quality, and queue displacement to other cases.

## Recommendation 2: Make final dispatch readiness-driven before buying permanent capacity

The first final-station redesign should be to validate and, where allowed, shorten the delay between `join_ready` and final start. A work-conserving pull rule using the existing two resources should be piloted before adding a permanent third resource.

- **Throughput:** Immediate dispatch does not change the 240 observed minutes of final service workload, but it can improve effective throughput if current delays reflect avoidable idle or release loss.
- **Flow-time tail:** Eliminating observed downstream delay has the largest modeled mean benefit and reduces maximum flow from 130 to 115 minutes.
- **Utilization:** F1 and F2 each perform six 20-minute activities, or 120 observed service minutes, but utilization cannot be calculated without working-time and omitted-work denominators. A third resource may be poorly utilized; the idealized two-server simulation suggests only one five-minute overlap that needs it.
- **New bottlenecks:** Once final delay is reduced, B becomes more visibly dominant, followed by A and C queue spikes.
- **Synchronization:** Final dispatch does not change branch synchronization directly, but it prevents downstream waiting from masking branch improvements.
- **Risks and pilot:** Automatic pull may violate hidden approvals, appointment rules, resource qualifications, or priority constraints. First log explicit `join_ready`, eligibility time, dispatch-decision time, reason codes, resource availability, calendars, and all competing final work. Pilot immediate dispatch with F1/F2; add temporary flex capacity only during measured overlap periods.

import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Construct the supplied table in memory and parse timestamps
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
csv_text = """case,arrival,fork_release,A_start,A_end,B_start,B_end,C_start,C_end,final_start,final_end,final_resource

F12,10:35,10:55,10:55,11:45,11:25,12:10,11:00,11:18,12:20,12:40,F2
"""

df = pd.read_csv(StringIO(csv_text), dtype=str)
time_cols = [
    "arrival", "fork_release",
    "A_start", "A_end", "B_start", "B_end", "C_start", "C_end",
    "final_start", "final_end"
]
for col in time_cols:
    df[col] = pd.to_datetime(
        "2026-05-19 " + df[col],
        format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M"
    )

BRANCHES = ["A", "B", "C"]  # also defines deterministic tie priority
to_minutes = lambda td: td.dt.total_seconds() / 60.0

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Custom pandas fork-join decomposition
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
df["upstream_elapsed"] = to_minutes(df["fork_release"] - df["arrival"])

for b in BRANCHES:
    df[f"{b}_queue"] = to_minutes(df[f"{b}_start"] - df["fork_release"])
    df[f"{b}_service"] = to_minutes(df[f"{b}_end"] - df[f"{b}_start"])
    df[f"{b}_response"] = to_minutes(df[f"{b}_end"] - df["fork_release"])

df["join_ready"] = df[[f"{b}_end" for b in BRANCHES]].max(axis=1)
df["fork_join_elapsed"] = to_minutes(df["join_ready"] - df["fork_release"])

for b in BRANCHES:
    df[f"{b}_sync"] = to_minutes(df["join_ready"] - df[f"{b}_end"])

df["downstream_queue"] = to_minutes(df["final_start"] - df["join_ready"])
df["final_service"] = to_minutes(df["final_end"] - df["final_start"])
df["total_flow_time"] = to_minutes(df["final_end"] - df["arrival"])

# Preserve every tied branch, and also choose A then B then C deterministically
# when a downstream consumer requires one critical_branch value.
def datetime_critical(row):
    maximum = max(row[f"{b}_end"] for b in BRANCHES)
    ties = [b for b in BRANCHES if row[f"{b}_end"] == maximum]
    return pd.Series({
        "critical_branches": "|".join(ties),
        "critical_branch": ties[0]
    })

df[["critical_branches", "critical_branch"]] = df.apply(
    datetime_critical, axis=1
)

# Validate the non-double-counting identities.
assert np.allclose(
    df["total_flow_time"],
    df["upstream_elapsed"] + df["fork_join_elapsed"]
    + df["downstream_queue"] + df["final_service"]
)
for b in BRANCHES:
    assert np.allclose(
        df["fork_join_elapsed"],
        df[f"{b}_queue"] + df[f"{b}_service"] + df[f"{b}_sync"]
    )

case_cols = [
    "case", "upstream_elapsed",
    "A_queue", "A_service", "A_sync",
    "B_queue", "B_service", "B_sync",
    "C_queue", "C_service", "C_sync",
    "join_ready", "critical_branch", "fork_join_elapsed",
    "downstream_queue", "final_service", "total_flow_time"
]
case_output = df[case_cols].copy()
case_output["join_ready"] = case_output["join_ready"].dt.strftime("%H:%M")
print("\nCASE DECOMPOSITION")
print(case_output.to_string(index=False))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Aggregate branch comparison, including critical lead
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
def p90(series):
    return series.quantile(0.90)

branch_rows = []
for b in BRANCHES:
    tied_set = df["critical_branches"].apply(lambda x: b in x.split("|"))
    other = [x for x in BRANCHES if x != b]
    other_max = df[[f"{x}_response" for x in other]].max(axis=1)
    critical_lead = (df[f"{b}_response"] - other_max).clip(lower=0)

    branch_rows.append({
        "branch": b,
        "service_mean": df[f"{b}_service"].mean(),
        "service_p90": p90(df[f"{b}_service"]),
        "service_max": df[f"{b}_service"].max(),
        "queue_mean": df[f"{b}_queue"].mean(),
        "queue_p90": p90(df[f"{b}_queue"]),
        "queue_max": df[f"{b}_queue"].max(),
        "response_mean": df[f"{b}_response"].mean(),
        "response_p90": p90(df[f"{b}_response"]),
        "response_max": df[f"{b}_response"].max(),
        "critical_count": int(tied_set.sum()),
        "critical_share": tied_set.mean(),
        "critical_join_mean": df.loc[tied_set, "fork_join_elapsed"].mean(),
        "critical_join_max": df.loc[tied_set, "fork_join_elapsed"].max(),
        "mean_sync": df[f"{b}_sync"].mean(),
        "critical_lead_mean_all_cases": critical_lead.mean(),
        "critical_lead_max": critical_lead.max()
    })

branch_summary = pd.DataFrame(branch_rows).round(2)
print("\nBRANCH SUMMARY")
print(branch_summary.to_string(index=False))

print("\nMEAN ADDITIVE CASE DECOMPOSITION")
print(
    df[[
        "upstream_elapsed", "fork_join_elapsed",
        "downstream_queue", "final_service", "total_flow_time"
    ]].mean().round(2).to_string()
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Counterfactuals
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
base_offsets = pd.DataFrame(
    {b: df[f"{b}_response"] for b in BRANCHES},
    index=df.index
)

def numeric_critical_labels(offset_table):
    all_labels, chosen = [], []
    for _, row in offset_table.iterrows():
        maximum = row.max()
        ties = [b for b in BRANCHES if np.isclose(row[b], maximum)]
        all_labels.append("|".join(ties))
        chosen.append(ties[0])  # deterministic A, then B, then C
    return pd.DataFrame(
        {"all": all_labels, "chosen": chosen},
        index=offset_table.index
    )

# CF1: A service falls by 20%; A queue remains unchanged.
cf_a_offsets = base_offsets.copy()
cf_a_offsets["A"] = df["A_queue"] + 0.8 * df["A_service"]
cf_a_stage = cf_a_offsets.max(axis=1)
cf_a_labels = numeric_critical_labels(cf_a_offsets)
df["cf_A20_join_saving"] = df["fork_join_elapsed"] - cf_a_stage
df["cf_A20_new_critical"] = cf_a_labels["all"]
# Potential flow assumes the observed downstream wait duration is retained.
df["cf_A20_new_total"] = df["total_flow_time"] - df["cf_A20_join_saving"]

# CF2: B queue falls by 50%; service remains unchanged.
cf_b_offsets = base_offsets.copy()
cf_b_offsets["B"] = 0.5 * df["B_queue"] + df["B_service"]
cf_b_stage = cf_b_offsets.max(axis=1)
cf_b_labels = numeric_critical_labels(cf_b_offsets)
df["cf_Bq50_join_saving"] = df["fork_join_elapsed"] - cf_b_stage
df["cf_Bq50_new_critical"] = cf_b_labels["all"]
df["cf_Bq50_new_total"] = df["total_flow_time"] - df["cf_Bq50_join_saving"]

# CF3: work-conserving FCFS final scheduling with an added third resource.
# All final resources are assumed interchangeable and available by the first
# join-ready timestamp. This is an optimistic redesign assumption, not an
# inference that the observed downstream waits were caused by capacity.
def fcfs_wait_minutes(frame, servers):
    available = [frame["join_ready"].min()] * servers
    waits = pd.Series(index=frame.index, dtype=float)
    order = sorted(
        frame.index,
        key=lambda idx: (frame.at[idx, "join_ready"], frame.at[idx, "case"])
    )
    for idx in order:
        server = min(range(servers), key=lambda k: (available[k], k))
        ready = frame.at[idx, "join_ready"]
        start = max(ready, available[server])
        waits.at[idx] = (start - ready).total_seconds() / 60.0
        available[server] = start + pd.Timedelta(
            minutes=float(frame.at[idx, "final_service"])
        )
    return waits

df["cf_final_wait_2server_fcfs"] = fcfs_wait_minutes(df, servers=2)
df["cf_final_wait_3server_fcfs"] = fcfs_wait_minutes(df, servers=3)
df["cf_final_capacity_saving"] = (
    df["downstream_queue"] - df["cf_final_wait_3server_fcfs"]
)
df["cf_final_capacity_new_total"] = (
    df["upstream_elapsed"] + df["fork_join_elapsed"]
    + df["cf_final_wait_3server_fcfs"] + df["final_service"]
)

per_case_cf = df[[

# 7. Limits of inference

- **Causality is not identified.** The data show where delay occurs, not why. In particular, they do not establish that reducing a measured queue by a specified percentage is operationally feasible.
- **Branch resource capacity is unknown.** No A, B, or C resource identities, calendars, utilization denominators, setups, WIP, or competing workload are supplied.
- **Queue attribution is conditional.** `branch_start - fork_release` is an observed launch delay. It is a physical resource queue only if there are no hidden branch release rules, appointments, batching, or prerequisites.
- **Service may include preemption or inactivity.** Start/end timestamps identify elapsed intervals, not hands-on work. Interruptions, pauses, rework inside an interval, and multitasking cannot be detected.
- **The join rule is assumed rather than directly observed.** `join_ready` is reconstructed as the maximum branch completion under the stated AND-join rule. A hidden approval or data-transfer step would invalidate that interpretation.
- **Final waiting may not be a resource queue.** The observed waits are not well explained by a simple closed, work-conserving two-server schedule. They could reflect release policy, qualifications, appointments, omitted cases, breaks, or unlogged tasks.
- **Final-resource interchangeability is unknown.** F1 and F2 may not be eligible for every case, and an added third resource may not be interchangeable.
- **The FCFS capacity diagnostic assumes a closed workload.** If other final-station work is omitted, its conclusion that two resources are usually sufficient does not hold.
- **Counterfactual queue interactions are not identified.** Earlier joins can change queue order and contention at F. Holding downstream wait constant and holding final start fixed are alternative models, not observed facts.
- **Throughput and utilization cannot be estimated reliably.** Twelve cases from one date provide flow-time observations, but no observation window, shift calendar, lost demand, or complete capacity denominator.
- **Minute resolution limits tie interpretation.** No ties appear in the recorded data, but branch completions within the same underlying minute could be practically simultaneous.
- **Generalization is uncertain.** The ranking B \(>\) A approximately C is strong for these 12 cases, but no sampling model or evidence across days, shifts, products, or demand regimes is available.
