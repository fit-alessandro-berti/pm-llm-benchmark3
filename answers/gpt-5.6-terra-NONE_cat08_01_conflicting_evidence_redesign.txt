# 1. Current-process diagnosis

The process has a genuine speed–quality–compliance trade-off, not a single “best” path.

**The attractive fast path is also the main source of downstream risk.** The dominant flow is:

`Intake  Auto eligibility screen  Fast approval  Dispatch replacement  Quality/compliance close`

It is high-volume: 3,600 cases enter auto screening, 2,550 go directly to fast approval, and 2,300 then go to dispatch. It is operationally appealing because auto screening costs only **EUR 0.20 per case**, has effectively unlimited capacity, and the `Auto eligibility screen  Fast approval` transition has a **0.4-hour median**. Fast approval cases are initially **31% cheaper** than reviewed cases.

However, the apparent saving is substantially eroded downstream:

- **14.1%** of Fast approval cases complain or reopen, versus **3.3%** for the repair-quote/customer-approval/repair route.
- The `Fast approval  Customer complaint/reopen` edge has a **72-hour mean** and **150-hour p90**; after dispatch, the comparable mean is **96 hours** and p90 is **180 hours**. These are not immediate corrections; they are delayed failures that damage customer experience and generate reshipments, service credits, and escalations.
- After complaints, 310 cases return to manual eligibility review and 120 go to the escalation board, demonstrating that the process is often deferring review rather than eliminating it.
- Once complaints, reshipments, and service credits are counted, Fast approval is only **9% cheaper**, not 31% cheaper.

**Compliance deviations are concentrated around the fast path.** Token-based replay gives reasonably high overall fitness (**0.91**) but only **68% fitting traces**. That combination means the broad model can replay much of the log, while a substantial minority of individual cases violate the normative process. The alignment diagnostics identify three recurring deviations:

1. **Fast approval before evidence completeness**;
2. **Customer complaint/reopen after close**;
3. **Dispatch without risk review for high-value devices**.

These are especially consequential because:

- **R1** requires manual or fraud/risk review before Fast approval for claims over EUR 400.
- **R2** prohibits dispatch before evidence completeness except for Premium low-risk accessories.
- **R3** requires reopened claims to pass Manual eligibility review before final close or denial.

Low precision in the discovered model reinforces the concern: observed low-frequency combinations of auto approval, risk review, and repair are not necessarily legitimate alternative operating models; some are policy-incompatible behavior that the redesign should constrain rather than preserve.

**Evidence completion is the largest visible avoidable delay loop.** The DFG shows 1,430 cases entering `Request missing evidence` from auto and manual review, with 890 returning to manual review and 220 routed onward to fraud/risk review. The performance graph identifies the expensive wait: `Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility review` has a **34-hour mean**, **26-hour median**, and **88-hour p90**. Further, **26%** of cases entering missing-evidence handling receive at least two requests. This is consistent with fragmented, unclear, or poorly targeted evidence collection rather than simply “customers being slow.” The unclear-defect/missing-evidence segment, 22% of volume, has a **140-hour median cycle time**, highest abandonment, and repeated requests.

**The reviewed path has quality benefits but is not uniformly desirable.** It should not be treated as the universal answer:

- For Premium low-risk accessories, manual-path median cycle time is **46 hours**, versus **8 hours** on the fast path, and service-credit exposure rises sharply after 24 hours. The complaint reduction is real but modest: **6% fast versus 3% manual**.
- For Premium high-value devices, the trade-off reverses: fast path median is **20 hours**, but complaints/reopens are **22%**, versus **5%** on the reviewed/repair path. R1/R2 compliance exposure is also high.
- Standard high-value devices show the same pattern: **18% fast-path complaints versus 6% reviewed/repair**, with high cost for a wrong replacement.
- The reviewed repair route has its own customer delay: `Manual eligibility review  Repair quote` has a **22-hour mean** and **60-hour p90**, with the temporal profile specifically showing this is often too slow for Premium cases.

**Replacement accuracy and repair quality are distinct defects requiring different controls.**

- The long-tail `Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen` pattern is linked to **incorrect replacement-part selection**, not merely eligibility delay.
- A wrong replacement has expected cost of **EUR 95** for low-risk accessories and **EUR 410** for high-value devices.
- On repair cases, rework is concentrated among high-value devices: **12.5%** rework versus **4.0%** for low-risk devices. The DFG confirms a repair loop: 45 quality checks lead to rework repair, and 42 return to repair.
- Quality inspection capacity is underused except after repair campaigns, while Manual eligibility review becomes unstable above **82% utilization**. This suggests the process is over-relying on scarce review capacity for some controls while underusing quality capability for others.

In short, the current process is not simply “too slow.” It is **too indiscriminate**: it lets some risky claims move quickly with insufficient evidence or risk control, while forcing low-risk claims and evidence-poor claims through slow, repeated, and often poorly designed handoffs.

# 2. Redesign A: Risk-tiered straight-through processing with mandatory control gates

## Redesigned process flow

Replace the current largely generic auto-screen logic with a segment- and risk-aware routing model:

1. **Intake claim**
2. **Enriched auto eligibility/risk classification**, using customer tier, product value/risk class, claim value, defect type, evidence completeness, and replacement-part confidence.
3. Route into one of three lanes:
   - **Express lane:** Premium low-risk accessories with complete evidence and high-confidence replacement selection. Auto approval and dispatch remain permissible under the R2 exception.
   - **Controlled fast lane:** Standard low-risk accessories and low-risk devices with complete evidence. Auto approval is allowed, but dispatch requires an automated evidence-completeness confirmation and lightweight replacement-selection check.
   - **Reviewed lane:** high-value devices, claims above EUR 400, unclear defects, incomplete evidence, fraud/risk flags, or low-confidence replacement matches. These must receive Manual eligibility review and/or Fraud/risk review before Fast approval, repair quote, or dispatch.
4. For high-value replacements, introduce a **pre-dispatch quality gate** focused on part compatibility, product serial number, entitlement, and evidence/risk-control completion.
5. Close only after the required control record is present. Any reopen follows R3: Manual eligibility review before final closure or denial.

The auto-screen update is a policy/routing redesign, not merely a faster version of current automation. It encodes R1 and R2 as decision gates and prevents unsupported pathway combinations that contributed to low model precision.

## Segments, paths, or activities targeted

- **Primary target:** Premium and Standard high-value devices, especially fast-approved cases currently exposed to 22% and 18% complaint/reopen rates.
- **Secondary target:** low-risk cases that can safely retain speed, especially Premium low-risk accessories, which require completion inside 24 hours to avoid service-credit exposure.
- **Activities targeted:** Auto eligibility screen, Fast approval, Fraud/risk review, Dispatch replacement, Quality/compliance close.
- **Deviations targeted:** Fast approval before evidence completeness; dispatch without appropriate risk review for high-value devices; closure that does not reliably enforce required review after reopening.

## Mechanism of improvement

This redesign moves controls **upstream and selectively**, instead of waiting for an incorrect dispatch to generate a complaint 72–96 hours later.

- It protects the economically and reputationally costly high-value population from inappropriate straight-through approval.
- It preserves fast processing where the evidence supports it and policy permits it, rather than sending all low-risk claims into Manual eligibility review.
- The pre-dispatch gate attacks the specific causal pattern identified in the temporal diagnostics: long-tail post-dispatch reopens linked to incorrect part selection.
- It uses the fact that Fast approval is only 9% cheaper after downstream failure costs; avoiding even a portion of high-value wrong replacements can justify more selective upstream review.
- It should improve conformance by making R1/R2 violations technically difficult rather than dependent on individual judgment.

## What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved

- It accepts some additional elapsed time for high-value and ambiguous claims. A 6–18-hour pre-dispatch gate is an intentional trade-off where the wrong-replacement exposure is EUR 410 and complaint rates are high.
- It may increase Manual eligibility inflow by the stated **15–35%** associated with revised auto rules, risking reviewer queues above the 82% utilization threshold.
- It does not, by itself, solve the repeated-evidence loop or the 140-hour cycle time of the unclear-defect segment.
- It may not materially improve the repair quote delay caused by part-price confirmation.

# 3. Redesign B: Evidence-first digital resolution and assisted exception service

## Redesigned process flow

Redesign the missing-evidence portion of the process as a dedicated evidence-resolution service rather than a repeated handoff back to Manual eligibility review.

1. **Intake claim**
2. **Evidence triage at intake**:
   - Complete and coherent evidence proceeds to eligibility/risk routing.
   - Incomplete or unclear evidence enters an evidence-resolution queue.
3. In the evidence-resolution queue:
   - A guided portal requests only claim-specific evidence, showing examples, serial-number capture, defect photos/video prompts, and real-time completeness status.
   - The system performs immediate validation of mandatory fields and document quality.
   - Customers predicted to struggle digitally—based on failed upload attempts, accessibility needs, channel choice, or repeated requests—are offered assisted capture through call-center support, retailer support, or a callback.
   - A case owner or rules engine determines whether a single missing item can be waived, substituted, or escalated based on product risk and R2 requirements.
4. Once evidence is complete:
   - Low-risk cases re-enter the appropriate automated or controlled-fast lane.
   - High-value/unclear cases proceed to Manual eligibility or Fraud/risk review with a structured evidence packet, not another generic request.
5. For cases unlikely to proceed, use explicit customer status, deadlines, and an easy “withdraw/continue” decision rather than passive abandonment.

This creates a separate, measurable sub-process for evidence completion instead of allowing `Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility review  Request missing evidence` cycling.

## Segments, paths, or activities targeted

- **Primary target:** the **22% unclear defect/missing evidence** segment, with its 140-hour median cycle time, repeated requests, and high abandonment.
- **Secondary target:** Manual eligibility reviewers, who currently receive 890 cases from missing-evidence requests and spend effort rediscovering what is missing.
- **Activities targeted:** Request missing evidence, Manual eligibility review, intake data capture, eligibility assessment.
- **Specific DFG pattern targeted:** 650 auto-screen cases and 780 manual-review cases move into missing evidence, while 26% of entrants receive at least two requests.

## Mechanism of improvement

The key mechanism is reducing **avoidable iterations**, not simply accelerating reviewers.

- A portal that reduces repeated evidence requests by **30–45%** addresses a known recurrence pattern directly.
- Immediate validation avoids the 34-hour average wait from a request to another manual review only to discover evidence is still inadequate.
- Structured evidence packets allow Manual eligibility and Fraud/risk reviewers to make a decision at first substantive review, reducing rework and avoidable transfers.
- The assisted path addresses the stated downside of a portal: greater abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers. Without this alternative, a portal could improve internal time while worsening equity and customer outcomes.
- Better status visibility is particularly suitable for Standard low-risk customers, who can tolerate up to **72 hours** if visibility is good. It also reduces uncertainty for Premium customers while they await a necessary review.

## What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved

- This design does not solve the wrong-part-selection problem after dispatch; a claim can have complete evidence and still receive an incorrect replacement.
- It can increase visible handling effort at intake and require new portal, integration, accessibility, and support capabilities.
- It may deliberately slow some cases at the front by refusing to issue a fast approval before sufficient evidence is established. That is preferable to violating R2 except in the permitted Premium low-risk accessory exception.
- It cannot make every unclear-defect case fast; some genuinely require expert diagnosis, fraud/risk assessment, or repair evaluation.

# 4. Redesign C: Premium service orchestration with repair-or-replace decisioning

## Redesigned process flow

Create a differentiated service model for Premium and high-value claims, built around early case ownership and an explicit repair-versus-replacement decision rather than defaulting to a generic fast replacement.

1. **Intake claim**
2. **Premium/high-value identification and priority assignment**
3. A **case orchestrator** or digital decision service immediately determines:
   - Eligibility/evidence state;
   - product value and replacement cost;
   - defect type and repairability;
   - replacement-part confidence;
   - whether R1/R2 require review;
   - service promise, including Premium 24-hour milestones.
4. Route accordingly:
   - **Premium low-risk accessory with complete evidence:** retain expedited replacement, possibly under the R2 exception, with priority dispatch.
   - **Premium high-value device:** rapid reviewed decision, with a time-boxed Manual eligibility/Fraud review and an early technical choice between repair quote/repair and replacement.
   - **High-value replacement with uncertain fit:** pre-dispatch compatibility confirmation or technician validation.
   - **Repairable high-value devices:** prioritized repair quote and repair slot; quality check is mandatory, with risk-based enhanced inspection for high-value products.
5. If the claim is at risk of missing the Premium service commitment, trigger a proactive communication and recovery action, such as temporary replacement, clear ETA, or service credit authorization according to policy.
6. Complaints/reopens are assigned to the same owner or a dedicated recovery queue. The required Manual eligibility review under R3 remains mandatory, but the case is not left to wait for the once-daily escalation board unless it truly requires exception authority.

This is a service-model redesign: it changes ownership, prioritization, and the repair-or-replace decision, rather than simply changing evidence capture or eligibility rules.

## Segments, paths, or activities targeted

- **Primary target:** Premium high-value devices, 14% of cases, where fast-path complaints are **22%** versus **5%** on reviewed/repair paths and compliance risk is high.
- **Secondary target:** Premium low-risk accessories, 18% of cases, where speed is critical because service-credit exposure is high beyond 24 hours.
- **Activities targeted:** Manual eligibility review, Fraud/risk review, Repair quote, Customer approval, Repair device, Quality check, Escalation board, customer communications.
- **Temporal issue targeted:** `Manual eligibility review  Repair quote` is often too slow for Premium cases, with a 22-hour mean and 60-hour p90.

## Mechanism of improvement

The redesign recognizes that Premium service expectations differ by risk class:

- For Premium low-risk accessories, unnecessary manual delay is more damaging than the modest difference between 6% and 3% reopen rates. The design preserves a controlled express service.
- For Premium high-value devices, the 22% fast-path reopen rate is too high to justify “fast replacement” as the default. Early technical decisioning avoids a costly wrong replacement and makes the 5% reviewed/repair outcome more attainable.
- A named owner and time-boxed queue reduce the customer experience harm of a necessary review. The objective is not merely to shorten average review time but to ensure that Premium cases do not sit behind lower-priority work or await a daily escalation-board slot.
- Mandatory enhanced quality checks for high-value repairs address the **12.5%** repair rework rate, while the currently underused quality-check resource can absorb some work outside campaign peaks.
- Proactive recovery reduces the impact of unavoidable delays, which is important because customer experience depends on predictability and visibility as well as completion time.

## What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved

- This is intentionally more expensive and operationally complex than a universal process. It reserves differentiated capacity for Premium/high-value claims and may be inappropriate for Standard low-risk accessories.
- It does not eliminate the need for skilled Manual eligibility, Fraud/risk, and technical capacity; it may expose their limits more clearly.
- If service credits or temporary replacements are used too freely, it can improve customer satisfaction while increasing total cost.
- It does not resolve the general missing-evidence population as comprehensively as Redesign B.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

## Redesign A: Risk-tiered straight-through processing with mandatory control gates

**How it could fail**

1. **Manual-review overload.** Updating auto rules may increase Manual eligibility inflow by 15–35%. With seven reviewers and sharp queue growth above 82% utilization, high-value claims could become compliant but slow, particularly after demand peaks.
2. **False confidence in poor input data.** If product value, claim value, defect type, evidence completeness, or replacement compatibility are inaccurate at intake, the routing model can still place risky claims in the controlled fast lane.
3. **Gate becomes a rubber stamp.** A lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate may add 6–18 hours without catching wrong part selections if it lacks reliable compatibility data, clear accountability, or sampling feedback.
4. **Policy rigidity harms legitimate exceptions.** Premium low-risk accessory claims could lose their permitted R2 speed advantage if the gating logic applies controls indiscriminately.

**Likely bottleneck migration**

The bottleneck is likely to move from downstream complaints/escalations to **Manual eligibility review, Fraud/risk review, or the pre-dispatch gate**. Fraud/risk is especially vulnerable because only two specialists are available and demand is already concentrated on Mondays and after marketing campaigns.

## Redesign B: Evidence-first digital resolution and assisted exception service

**How it could fail**

1. **Digital abandonment rises.** The portal may reduce repeat requests while increasing abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers, producing an apparently faster internal process but worse access and inequitable outcomes.
2. **Portal prompts are still generic.** If requests are not defect- and product-specific, customers may upload more documents without supplying decision-useful evidence, preserving the loop in another form.
3. **Evidence validation creates false negatives.** Aggressive automated checks can reject valid evidence, adding friction and creating more contacts.
4. **“Complete” evidence is mistaken for “low risk.”** Completeness should not bypass R1, Fraud/risk review, or high-value replacement controls; otherwise the redesign may reduce one deviation while preserving another.

**Likely bottleneck migration**

The queue may move from Manual eligibility review to **assisted evidence support, intake validation, or exception handling**. If the portal succeeds, the remaining cases reaching reviewers may be disproportionately complex, increasing average review effort even as volume falls.

## Redesign C: Premium service orchestration with repair-or-replace decisioning

**How it could fail**

1. **Priority capacity crowds out Standard customers.** Reserving reviewer, technician, or quality capacity for Premium cases can lengthen Standard queues and create a hidden deterioration in overall customer outcomes.
2. **Premium becomes synonymous with automatic replacement.** If commercial pressure overrides risk-based decisioning, high-value Premium claims may continue to receive wrong replacements, preserving the 22% fast-path reopen problem.
3. **Case ownership adds coordination without authority.** A case orchestrator who cannot obtain technical decisions, override queues, or arrange customer recovery may add another handoff rather than reduce waiting.
4. **Escalation and recovery costs drift upward.** Temporary replacements, service credits, and proactive interventions can become routine rather than exception-based.

**Likely bottleneck migration**

Pressure is likely to concentrate in the **priority Manual eligibility/Fraud review queue, repair quote/part-price confirmation, and high-value quality inspection**. The escalation board may be relieved, but technical decision capacity may become the new constraint.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

## First pilot

Begin with **Redesign A in a narrow, controlled high-value cohort**:

- Include Premium and Standard high-value device claims with sufficient volume to observe outcomes.
- Do not apply the redesign to Premium low-risk accessories initially; their 24-hour service-credit exposure makes an overcautious pilot risky.
- Enforce R1/R2 routing logic and add the pre-dispatch compatibility gate only for high-value replacements.
- Compare against a contemporaneous matched control group using the current process, stratified by Premium/Standard status, defect type, claim value, and evidence completeness.

This is the first pilot because the high-value fast-path outcome gap is strongest: 22% versus 5% reopen for Premium high-value and 18% versus 6% for Standard high-value. It also directly addresses documented conformance deviations and the high EUR 410 expected cost of a wrong replacement.

## Metrics to monitor

Monitor **leading, lagging, and distributional** measures, not just average cycle time.

**Conformance and control metrics**

- R1 compliance for claims above EUR 400.
- R2 compliance, including evidence-completeness confirmation before dispatch.
- R3 compliance for reopened claims.
- Fitting-trace percentage and alignment deviation rates, especially:
  - Fast approval before evidence completeness;
  - dispatch without risk review for high-value devices;
  - closure/denial after reopen without manual review.
- Rate of policy-disallowed pathway combinations, given the current low model precision.

**Customer and quality metrics**

- Complaint/reopen rate by segment, value class, and route.
- Wrong replacement/part mismatch rate and reshipment rate.
- Repair rework rate for high-value devices.
- Premium service-credit exposure and percentage meeting the relevant service milestones.
- Customer contact volume, status-related contacts, satisfaction, and complaint severity.

**Time metrics**

- Median and p90 end-to-end cycle time, not only mean.
- Median and p90 waiting time for Manual eligibility, Fraud/risk review, pre-dispatch gate, repair quote, and quality check.
- Time from dispatch to complaint/reopen, because a reduction in short-cycle cases is not meaningful if delayed failures rise.
- Time to meaningful customer update for cases that cannot be resolved immediately.

**Cost and capacity metrics**

- Review hours per case, Fraud/risk hours per case, inspection workload, reshipments, service credits, and replacement error costs.
- Manual-review utilization and queue age; treat approaching the 82% threshold as an early warning.
- Fraud/risk queue behavior on Mondays and after campaigns.
- Net cost per completed claim inclusive of complaint recovery, not only front-end handling cost.

## Stop/go thresholds

Use pre-agreed qualitative or range-based thresholds rather than claiming a precise ROI from the current data.

**Stop or redesign the pilot if:**

- R1/R2 violations do not materially decline, or any systematic new compliance bypass appears.
- High-value complaint/reopen rates do not show a meaningful reduction from the current fast-path range of roughly 18–22%, particularly if cycle time increases materially.
- Manual eligibility utilization repeatedly exceeds approximately 82%, with rising p90 queue delay or spillover to non-pilot cases.
- The pre-dispatch gate adds time but shows little evidence of catching compatibility/selection defects.
- Premium service performance deteriorates enough to create materially greater service-credit exposure.

**Continue and expand if:**

- High-value complaint/reopen and wrong-replacement rates decline materially while high-value p90 completion time remains operationally acceptable.
- R1/R2/R3 adherence improves demonstrably, including fewer alignment deviations.
- Additional review/gate work is offset by lower reshipment, escalation, and service-recovery demand.
- Queue impact is manageable through staffing, triage, or capacity rebalancing rather than simply accumulating work.

A practical initial target is not “zero complaints” or “fastest possible cycle time.” It is a clear movement away from the current high-value fast-path complaint range toward the reviewed-path outcome range, without converting all claims into 96–120-hour experiences.

## Sequencing logic

1. **Pilot Redesign A for high-value replacements first.**  
   It directly addresses the highest-risk contradiction: fast handling produces unacceptable downstream failure and compliance risk in high-value segments.

2. **Add Redesign B for the unclear/missing-evidence segment after baseline evidence instrumentation is in place.**  
   Before launching broadly, classify whether repeated evidence requests are caused by missing customer documents, unclear prompts, poor intake data, internal review inconsistency, or technical validation failures. Pilot the portal with an assisted alternative from day one. Expand only if repeat-request reduction does not come at the cost of materially higher abandonment or worse outcomes for digitally vulnerable customers.

3. **Introduce Redesign C after control and evidence signals are reliable.**  
   Premium orchestration should use the routing and evidence signals created by A and B. Otherwise it risks becoming an expensive expedited queue that accelerates poorly evidenced or noncompliant decisions.

4. **Rebalance capacity before broad rollout.**  
   If A increases reviewed inflow, protect Manual eligibility capacity and examine whether underused quality inspectors can operate the pre-dispatch compatibility gate. If B reduces repetitive review work, redirect released reviewer capacity to high-value and Premium cases rather than reducing service capability prematurely.

## How to verify that improvements are real rather than shifted downstream

Use case-level, end-to-end measurement with delayed outcome windows.

- Track each claim from intake through closure and for a sufficiently long post-dispatch/post-close period to capture the current 72–96-hour mean complaint transitions and their long tails.
- Include reshipments, service credits, escalations, reopen review, and repair rework in the outcome definition. A faster closure is not an improvement if it produces later customer complaint/reopen events.
- Compare performance separately by Premium/Standard, low-risk/high-value, defect type, evidence state, and channel. Aggregate averages can conceal a harmful shift from high-value to low-risk performance or from digital to assisted customers.
- Monitor non-pilot queues and outcomes to detect displacement: for example, a successful high-value pilot should not be declared successful if Standard cases suffer because reviewer capacity was reallocated.
- Re-run conformance checking and temporal-profile analysis after each stage. Improvement should appear not only as reduced average duration, but as fewer R1/R2/R3 deviations, fewer illegal pathway combinations, lower post-dispatch reopen tails, and reduced repeated-evidence loops.
- Use operational review of sampled cases at key decision points. Process mining can show that a gate was traversed; sampling is needed to establish whether the evidence-completeness and replacement-selection decisions were substantively correct.

The recommended direction is therefore a **segmented portfolio**, not one universal “optimized” flow: controlled speed for genuinely low-risk claims, evidence-focused resolution for ambiguous claims, and deliberate review plus differentiated service for high-value and Premium situations.
