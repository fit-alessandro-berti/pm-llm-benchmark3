## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-501  JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION / COMPLIANT.** *Premise that must change:* (i) that B4's delegation sentence is a **condition** on emergency substitution rather than a restriction only where the manager is absent, and (ii) that oral contemporaneous concurrence by the Plant Manager or Incident Command can be established (the ERP was down 08:30–13:00, so the missing authorisation is plausibly a recording gap). *Credibility:* moderate-high — the substantive need (four-hour predicted stoppage) is documented by engineering, the ordering relief is expressly available under B4, and Plant/Finance approvals followed the same afternoon. *Weakness:* the brief states the Plant Manager was reachable and did not formally delegate; "actively delegated" is a documentary requirement, and the 08:50 commitment sits ten minutes before ECD-2's window, so no directive can regularise it. I keep NONCOMPLIANT, but only at Medium confidence, and treat telephony/access logs as the decisive evidence.

**P-502  JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION (whole case).** *Premise that must change:* (i) ECD-2's C4 precedence over "supplier-onboarding requirements" must be read to include the A3 new-supplier compliance gate (not merely the B3 register), on the argument that bypassing the register necessarily entails bypassing the register-based compliance check; and (ii) A4's "other controls" must be read to refer only to controls ECD-2 itself addresses, with the two-member IC authorisation (C3) as the proportionate ex-ante substitute for Finance approval and the same-day Compliance clearance (18:30) as no more than late confirmation. *Credibility:* moderate — ECD-2 is co-signed at executive level, the emergency was real, and the authorisation was two-member, pre-commitment, and contemporaneously corroborated by a supplier recording. *Weakness:* A4 and C5 are express; the CRO channel was never exercised and never ratified; the organisation itself has never treated a signed directive as overriding new-supplier compliance clearance; and D1 (which does speak to sufficiency) postdates the act. A narrower reading of C4 is textually superior.

**P-503  (scope/severity only) NONCOMPLIANT remains, but severity may drop/raise.** *Premise that must change:* (i) that a written Incident Command determination defines "critical sterilization-line lots" to include contingency spares for the backup line despite three days' stock cover (Operations' reading of C6), and/or (ii) that "final business approver" in A1 denotes only the budget/Finance sign-off, so that PROC-3's participation in a joint *operational* emergency authorisation is not "final business approval" but merely corroboration. On (ii) I adopt A-10 (broad reading), because A1 is expressly non-derogable and C3 is a novel approval architecture whose segregation consequences must be assumed to be within GPIP-4's purpose. *Credibility:* (i) moderate — that is precisely the internal-audit dispute, and the constraining body (COO/IC) has attempted a determination ("critical contingency"); (ii) moderate in isolation, but it cannot rescue the case because A3 (>100k, no clearance) still fails and D3 forbids the CFO from touching compliance. *Consequence:* no plausible alternative converts P-503 into a compliant purchase; at most scope and severity shift.

**P-505  COMPLIANT / JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** *Premise that must change:* (i) the commitment time must be placed at or before 18:00 — which requires accepting that a 9-minute-fast ERP clock plus a 17:59:31 acknowledgment email and a 17:57 supplier call are reliable, and that ERP's 18:06 reflects the recovery-lag/clock error (ON-44 supports discounting ERP; the acknowledgment is the better evidence, exactly as the prompt requires); and (ii) D1's sufficiency must be accepted as satisfying A2/B2 for an 88k in-window purchase, or the separate Finance/Plant events must be found in a non-ERP system. *Credibility:* moderate-high for (i); (ii) I already accept this construction in P-507, so consistency favours it here too. *Weakness:* if the ERP clock attestation contradicts the 9-minute skew, commitment is at 18:06 and the case becomes NONCOMPLIANT on A2/B2 (plus B1 if the PO post-dates). This is why I hold INDETERMINATE: the outcome-determinative fact is a timestamp, and the best evidence for it is contemporaneous documents that are only *probative* of commitment.

**P-506  JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION / INDETERMINATE.** *Premise that must change:* that a written instrument extending ECD-2 overnight exists (or that the COO/IC had a pre-existing, delegated authority to extend a co-signed directive orally), and that the subject matter "critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the current contamination event" reaches cleaning chemicals consumed in the recovery operation. *Credibility:* low-to-moderate — the buyer's oral extension is not implausible as a practice, and post-expiry continuity of an incident response is not unusual; but the extension must be produced in writing to be relied on. *Weakness:* A7's writing requirement, B7's silence, and the two-limb subject matter (cleaning chemicals are consumables, not "lots") make the asserted cover thin; and the burden sits on the organisation. Absent the instrument, the deviation stands.

**P-507  NONCOMPLIANT.** *Premise that must change:* (i) that the CFO's D1 cannot constitute a valid Finance-framework determination reaching GPIP-4 A2 — i.e., that A4's reservation to the CRO means no Finance-side substitution is possible without a CRO exception (or that IC members are not qualified approvers at all, since the IC charter does not designate them budget owners); and (ii) that the 12:40 signed PDF does not evidence an 08:55 authorisation or satisfy C3's four-hour recording requirement, and/or that the sealed seals/8-hour need fall outside ECD-2's scope. *Credibility:* moderate for (i) (it is the corrective, hard-line reading, and C5's silence on Finance is the counter-argument); low for (ii) (the PDF's creation timestamp falls inside the recording window, and the ERP entry at 14:30 is a textbook late entry). I keep JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION but with Medium confidence, and I flag the A2-channel conclusion as the reversible element.

**P-508  COMPLIANT / JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** *Premise that must change:* that D1 and D2 form a tiered scheme — "IC joint authorisation is sufficient for operational approvals" generally, and above EUR 150,000 Legal clearance is required *as well* — so that D1's sufficiency survives the 150,000 figure, which then functions only as the Legal-clearance trigger. *Credibility:* moderate — arguably high, since D2's word "as well" presupposes that the underlying sufficiency continues; the CFO owns the Finance framework, and other controls (A1, A3, Legal) were all satisfied here. *Weakness:* D1 is an uncontrolled email with an internal qualifier (D4) and no repository record; D3 limits it to Finance controls; and the express cap is the natural reading of "up to EUR 150,000". ## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Proposed replacement of GPIP-4 A4/A7 (with consequential amendments to SOP B4–B7) — "Emergency Procurement Directive (EPD)":**

> **EPD-1 (Authority).** Only the Chief Risk Officer (CRO) may issue a written exception to A1, A2 or A3. The COO and the Incident Commander may jointly issue an **Emergency Procurement Directive**, which constitutes an exception **only** to the local workflow steps expressly listed in it. A directive signed by the COO and Incident Commander is **not** an exception to A1, A2 or A3; any such exception requires a CRO instrument. An EPD must be entered in the controlled policy repository within 2 hours of issue and published with a machine-readable identifier.
>
> **EPD-2 (Scope, definition and thresholds).** Each EPD must state (i) a closed list of qualifying asset/category identifiers — e.g., "sterilisation line items required for continuation of Lot L-x within 24 hours", with **no** definitional discretion to the requester; (ii) a maximum monetary cap and an aggregate cap per incident (proposed: EUR 100,000 aggregate per EPD, EUR 250,000 per incident), above which a CRO exception is mandatory; and (iii) the precise GPIP-4 clauses suspended — and by default **none** of A1, A3 or the record-integrity provisions. Items whose absence is merely economic (spoilage, convenience, warranty-driven replacement) are not emergency-qualifying.
>
> **EPD-3 (Thresholds and new suppliers).** Unregistered suppliers may be used only where Compliance_Clearance has been obtained **before** commitment, and only with a written supplier-qualification plan within 5 working days. Compliance clearance is not suspendable under any emergency instrument.
>
> **EPD-4 (Segregation of duties).** The segregation rule in A1 is absolute in emergencies: no requester, buyer or committer may be an authoriser, and the joint authorisers must be selected from a standing roster of persons who have attested financial authority. If a member of the authorising body lacks documented spending authority, the authorisation is void and the purchase may not proceed on that basis.
>
> **EPD-5 (Temporal operation and expiry).** An EPD has a maximum duration of 72 hours and a hard expiry timestamp; expiry is enforced by the ERP, which must refuse commitments referencing an expired instrument. Extension requires a **written** instrument signed by both original signatories (an oral extension has no effect) and a CRO notification within 2 hours. Acts committed before an EPD's effective time are governed by the rules then in force and are not regularised retrospectively.
>
> **EPD-6 (Records and retroactivity).** Joint authorisation may be verbal, but must be captured in the EPD log within 4 hours by a person other than the authorisers, recording the date, time, participants, decision and the person who made the commitment; a later database entry is never the sole evidence of a timely act. Late approvals added after a commitment may mitigate consequence but never cure a missing pre-commitment control, and any such approval must be labelled "retrospective — mitigation only" in the record.
>
> **EPD-7 (Scope of pre-commitment approvals).** A CFO determination may qualify how a Finance-side approval is to be given, including that a specified approval body may exercise it; it may not waive A2, and any such determination must be in writing, time-limited, cap-limited and consistent with A4. Determinations above the cap require CRO endorsement.
>
> **EPD-8 (Safeguards against emergency normalisation).** (a) Every EPD and emergency record is analysed monthly against objective necessity criteria, with escalation to the Audit Committee for any recurring instrument, category or requester; (b) three or more EPDs in a rolling quarter, or aggregate emergency spend above 20% of the site's quarterly procurement, mandates a policy review of A4/A7 and a suspension of EPD eligibility for that category; (c) each EPD carries an expiry ledger and a "post-incident review" obligation within 30 days, with findings reported to the board risk committee; (d) emergency use of any form, log or instrument for purposes outside the qualifying categories voids the record and triggers a disciplinary and systemic review; (e) the emergency register is reconciled monthly against the ERP commitment log, and any commitment without a live, in-scope instrument is reported as a deviation by definition.
>
> **EPD-9 (Evidence hierarchy).** Where a system record conflicts with contemporaneous attributable documentation (signed instruments, recorded calls, access logs, supplier acknowledgements), the contemporaneous document prevails; system time-availability and clock-sync records must be retained for 24 months so that the reliability of any timestamp can be tested after the fact.

*Drafting note:* EPD-2 (closed scope definition and aggregate caps), EPD-5 (written extension), EPD-6 (four-hour recording with an independent recorder) and EPD-8 are the four provisions that, had they existed, would have converted P-503, P-504, P-506 and P-505 respectively from disputed or non-compliant outcomes into determinable ones; the ERP interlocks in §6 are the detectability layer that makes EPD-9 operative.
