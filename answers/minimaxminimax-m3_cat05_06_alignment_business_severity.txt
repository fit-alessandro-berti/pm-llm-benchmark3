## 1. Materiality Framework

Business severity in claim-payment conformance is not the sum of edit-distance penalties. It is a function of:

- **Control objective breached.** P1 (auto-approve eligibility), P2 (high-risk / large / bank-change triggers manual), P3 (no pay before approval), P4 (notification timeliness and integrity). Breaches differ in degree: a Pay-before-Approve on a EUR 480k claim is not in the same category as a label mismatch.
- **Amount and exposure.** Absolute euros at risk, fraud-attractiveness (HIGH-risk and recent bank change are classic invoice-fraud indicators), and counterparty (new vs established claimant).
- **Reversibility.** Pre-payment issues are recoverable by recall, clawback, or corrected re-run; post-payment issues on closed accounts are far less recoverable. Pay Claim is the irreversibility inflection point.
- **Timing.** Whether the breach occurred before, during, or after the irreversibility window, and whether downstream controls could still intercept.
- **Customer and regulatory harm.** Customer includes the claimant (denial without due cause, delayed payout, mishandled withdrawal) and the insurer (regulator, audit, clawback cost).
- **Recurrence.** A one-off is a case; a pattern (e.g., 74 retry-duplicate notifications, four auto-approval bypasses) is a control gap.
- **Evidence reliability.** A mutable CRM text field pointing to a portal message is weaker than a core-system event with a stable message ID and signed audit trail. Missing log events are weaker than missing artifacts.
- **Model scope.** The normative tree is for payable claims. Denied pre-screens and withdrawals may legitimately fall outside its footprint.

**Confidence is distinct from severity.** A Pay-before-Approve at EUR 90k with a bank-acceptance timestamp 17 minutes before manual approval is *high severity, high confidence*. A missing core-log `Assess Loss` at EUR 75k when an external adjuster document exists is *high severity, lower confidence* — the activity may have occurred and just not landed in the core log. Severity is how bad if true; confidence is how sure we are it is true. The LLM judge should be able to read both columns without conflating them.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Raw alignment cost under uniform-cost (1 per visible move) edit distance is an arithmetic convenience, not a risk measure. The following five-case walk demonstrates why.

1. **CL-601 (raw_cost 3, severity 1).** Three UI telemetry moves inflate cost by three but represent no business activity. A naive reader would treat CL-601 as more risky than CL-609 (raw_cost 0). The opposite is true.

2. **CL-609 and CL-610 (raw_cost 0, severity 3–5).** Two of the most consequential control failures in the set have perfect alignment. The structural model permits Auto Approve; the data predicates that should block it live outside the tree. Cost function 0 means "no edit needed," but P1 and P2 are violated silently. This is the canonical case where lower cost means higher risk.

3. **CL-602 (raw_cost 1, severity 5).** A EUR 480k HIGH-risk claim with a one-day-old bank change and an unverified verbal fraud check has only one missing event in the alignment. The single M(Fraud Screen) edit hides a control failure that may have cost the insurer hundreds of thousands of euros. Cost 1, risk extreme.

4. **CL-605 (raw_cost 0, severity 1).** A hidden tau move is a model-routing artifact. Cost 0 here is correct — there is no risk — but it is correct because the move is *silent and fitting*, not because the case is benign in general. The same cost number is reached in CL-609/CL-610 for completely different reasons.

5. **CL-607 and CL-612 (raw_cost 4 each, severity 2).** Both cases have cost dominated by scope mismatch (denial/withdrawal branches missing from the model). The cost reflects model design limitations, not payment-control failures. No money moved in either case.

The deeper reasons cost fails as a risk proxy:

- **Cost-function design.** Uniform cost treats every L, M, and synchronous move identically. P3 (Pay before approval) and a synonym mismatch are penalized the same. The cost function does not encode business weight.
- **Model scope.** M0 covers payable claims. Non-payable terminals (deny, withdraw) inflate cost without indicating risk.
- **Hidden moves.** Tau transitions are silent and zero-cost, and they can absorb real routing decisions (CL-605). A cost function that treats silence as fitting cannot distinguish "no event occurred" from "no log evidence of an event."
- **Data conditions.** Amount, risk level, and bank-change windows are predicates on attributes, not activities. They never appear as moves and cannot be encoded in edit distance.
- **Activity granularity.** UI telemetry (CL-601) and retry duplicates (CL-603) are at the same granularity as business activities in the log, so they cost the same despite vastly different risk.
- **Duplicated telemetry.** 74 retry cases share one root cause; raw cost treats them as 74 independent one-cost events rather than as one systemic issue.
- **Missing-but-external evidence.** CL-606 and CL-608 have missing core events but real artifacts elsewhere; the cost function sees a missing event, not the compensating evidence.
- **Irreversibility.** Edit-distance repair is independent of whether money has moved. CL-604's pay-before-approve is cheaply repaired by the alignment algorithm but not by the bank.

This is why every case in Section 2 carries a separate Confidence column, and why Section 5 ranks remediation by business risk reduction rather than by alignment cost reduction.

## 7. PM4Py Reproduction and Aggregation Code

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Parse the normative process tree
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
tree_str = ("-> ( 'Register Claim', 'Validate Coverage', 'Assess Loss', "
            "X ( -> ( 'Fraud Screen', 'Manual Approve' ), 'Auto Approve' ), "
            "'Pay Claim', 'Notify Claimant' )")
tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_str)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Convert to an accepting Petri net
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Construct a small event log from the case traces.
#    Each case's trace mirrors what was observed in the supplied
#    authoritative alignments (including log-only events such as
#    UI telemetry, retry duplicates, and label synonyms).
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
case_traces = {
    'CL-601': ['Register Claim','Validate Coverage','Open Workbench','Assess Loss',
               'Autosave','Auto Approve','Pay Claim','Notify Claimant','Close Workbench'],
    'CL-602': ['Register Claim','Validate Coverage','Assess Loss',
               'Manual Approve','Pay Claim','Notify Claimant'],  # Fraud Screen missing in log
    'CL-603': ['Register Claim','Validate Coverage','Assess Loss','Auto Approve',
               'Pay Claim','Notify Claimant','Notify Claimant'],   # retry duplicate
    'CL-604': ['Register Claim','Validate Coverage','Assess Loss','Pay Claim',
               'Manual Approve','Notify Claimant'],                 # out-of-order
    'CL-605': ['Register Claim','Validate Coverage','Assess Loss',
               'Auto Approve','Pay Claim','Notify Claimant'],
    'CL-606': ['Register Claim','Validate Coverage',
               'Fraud Screen','Manual Approve','Pay Claim','Notify Claimant'],  # Assess Loss missing
    'CL-607': ['Register Claim','Validate Coverage','Deny Claim','Notify Claimant'],
    'CL-608': ['Register Claim','Validate Coverage','Assess Loss','Fraud Screen',
               'Manual Approve','Pay Claim'],                         # Notify Claimant missing
    'CL-609': ['Register Claim','Validate Coverage','Assess Loss',
               'Auto Approve','Pay Claim','Notify Claimant'],
    'CL-610': ['Register Claim','Validate Coverage','Assess Loss',
               'Auto Approve','Pay Claim','Notify Claimant'],
    'CL-611': ['Register Claim','Validate Coverage','Assess Damage','Fraud Screen',
               'Manual Approve','Pay Claim','Notify Claimant'],       # label mismatch
    'CL-612': ['Register Claim','Validate Coverage','Assess Loss','Withdraw Claim'],
}

rows = []
ts_base = pd.Timestamp('2024-01-01 09:00:00')
for i, (case_id, trace) in enumerate(case_traces.items()):
    for j, act in enumerate(trace):
        rows.append({
            'case:concept:name': case_id,
            'concept:name': act,
            'time:timestamp': ts_base + pd.Timedelta(minutes=i*60 + j*5),
        })
log_df = pd.DataFrame(rows)
log_df['time:timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(log_df['time:timestamp'])

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Run conformance diagnostics (alignments).
#    Note: PM4Py may produce a different optimal alignment than the
#    supplied authoritative notation, particularly for cases with
#    out-of-order execution (CL-604), scope-mismatch terminals
#    (CL-607, CL-612), and label synonyms (CL-611). The supplied
#    alignments are treated as authoritative for business judgment.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
alignments = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(log_df, net, im, fm)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Build a move-summary dataframe aligned with the supplied notation.
#    For each case we record: log-only moves (L), model-only moves (M),
#    synchronous moves (S), and any hidden tau moves (T). Raw cost is
#    the sum under uniform cost 1 per visible move.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
supplied_alignments = {
    'CL-601': [('S','Register Claim'),('S','Validate Coverage'),
               ('L','Open Workbench',1),('S','Assess Loss'),
               ('L','Autosave',1),('S','Auto Approve'),
               ('S','Pay Claim'),('S','Notify Claimant'),
               ('L','Close Workbench',1)],
    'CL-602': [('S','Register Claim'),('S','Validate Coverage'),
               ('S','Assess Loss'),('M','Fraud Screen',1),
               ('S','Manual Approve'),('S','Pay Claim'),('S','Notify Claimant')],
    'CL-603': [('S','Register Claim'),('S','Validate Coverage'),
               ('S','Assess Loss'),('S','Auto Approve'),
               ('S','Pay Claim'),('S','Notify Claimant'),
               ('L','Notify Claimant',1)],
    'CL-604': [('S','Register Claim'),('S','Validate Coverage'),
               ('S','Assess Loss'),('L','Pay Claim',1),
               ('M','Fraud Screen',1),('S','Manual Approve'),
               ('M','Pay Claim',1),('S','Notify Claimant')],
    'CL-605': [('S','Register Claim'),('S','Validate Coverage'),
               ('S','Assess Loss'),('T','tau',0),
               ('S','Auto Approve'),('S','Pay Claim'),('S','Notify Claimant')],
    'CL-606': [('S','Register Claim'),('S','Validate Coverage'),
               ('M','Assess Loss',1),('S','Fraud Screen'),
               ('S','Manual Approve'),('S','Pay Claim'),('S','Notify Claimant')],
    'CL-607': [('S','Register Claim'),('S','Validate Coverage'),
               ('L','Deny Claim',1),('M','Assess Loss',1),
               ('M','Auto Approve',1),('M','Pay Claim',1),('S','Notify Claimant')],
    'CL-608': [('S','Register Claim'),('S','Validate Coverage'),
               ('S','Assess Loss'),('S','Fraud Screen'),
               ('S','Manual Approve'),('S','Pay Claim'),
               ('M','Notify Claimant',1)],
    'CL-609': [('S','Register Claim'),('S','Validate Coverage'),
               ('S','Assess Loss'),('S','Auto Approve'),
               ('S','Pay Claim'),('S','Notify Claimant')],
    'CL-610': [('S','Register Claim'),('S','Validate Coverage'),
               ('S','Assess Loss'),('S','Auto Approve'),
               ('S','Pay Claim'),('S','Notify Claimant')],
    'CL-611': [('S','Register Claim'),('S','Validate Coverage'),
               ('L','Assess Damage',1),('M','Assess Loss',1),
               ('S','Fraud Screen'),('S','Manual Approve'),
               ('S','Pay Claim'),('S','Notify Claimant')],
    'CL-612': [('S','Register Claim'),('S','Validate Coverage'),
               ('S','Assess Loss'),('L','Withdraw Claim',1),
               ('M','Auto Approve',1),('M','Pay Claim',1),
               ('M','Notify Claimant',1)],
}

move_rows = []
for case_id, moves in supplied_alignments.items():
    raw_cost = sum(cost for _, _, cost in moves)
    for kind, act, cost in moves:
        move_rows.append({
            'case': case_id,
            'move_type': kind,
            'activity': act,
            'move_cost': cost,
        })

move_df = pd.DataFrame(move_rows)

# Per-case raw cost summary
raw_cost_df = (move_df.groupby('case')['move_cost']
               .sum()
               .reset_index()
               .rename(columns={'move_cost': 'raw_cost'}))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Aggregate move patterns, policy-overlay flags, and risk inputs.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
amounts = {
    'CL-601': 1200, 'CL-602': 480000, 'CL-603': 3200, 'CL-604': 90000,
    'CL-605': 800,  'CL-606': 75000,  'CL-607': None, 'CL-608': 250000,
    'CL-609': 18000,'CL-610': 4500,   'CL-611': 62000,'CL-612': 2000,
}
risk_level = {
    'CL-601':'LOW','CL-602':'HIGH','CL-603':'LOW','CL-604':'MEDIUM',
    'CL-605':'LOW','CL-606':'HIGH','CL-607':None,'CL-608':'HIGH',
    'CL-609':'MEDIUM','CL-610':'LOW','CL-611':'HIGH','CL-612':'LOW',
}
bank_change_recent = {
    'CL-601': False, 'CL-602': True, 'CL-603': False, 'CL-604': False,
    'CL-605': False, 'CL-606': False, 'CL-607': None, 'CL-608': False,
    'CL-609': False, 'CL-610': True, 'CL-611': False, 'CL-612': False,
}

def policy_flags(row):
    amt = amounts[row['case']]
    rk = risk_level[row['case']]
    bc = bank_change_recent[row['case']]
    if amt is None:
        return pd.Series({'P1_violated': False, 'P2_violated': False,
                          'P3_pay_before_approve': False, 'p1_or_p2_violated': False})
    p1 = (amt > 5000) or (rk != 'LOW') or (bc is True)
    p2 = (amt > 50000) or (rk == 'HIGH') or (bc is True)
    p3 = False
    for kind, act, cost in supplied_alignments[row['case']]:
        if kind == 'L' and act == 'Pay Claim':
            p3 = True
    return pd.Series({
        'P1_violated': bool(p1),
        'P2_violated': bool(p2),
        'P3_pay_before_approve': bool(p3),
        'p1_or_p2_violated': bool(p1 or p2),
    })

policy_df = raw_cost_df.apply(policy_flags, axis=1)
summary_df = pd.concat([raw_cost_df, policy_df], axis=1)

# Counts of move types per case
move_pivot = (move_df.groupby(['case','move_type']).size()
              .unstack(fill_value=0)
              .reset_index())

# Final evidence table: raw cost + move counts + policy flags.
# Note: business severity (1–5) and confidence are *not* derivable from
# these columns; they remain narrative judgment from Section 2.
evidence_df = (summary_df
               .merge(move_pivot, on='case', how='left')
               .fillna(0))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. Risk-prioritization evidence table (no formula replacement).
#    The table surfaces candidates that combine policy-overlay
#    violations with low raw cost (the dangerous zero-cost failures)
#    or with high raw cost (the scope-inflated cases). The final
#    severity rank is set by narrative judgment, not by a score.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
evidence_df['risk_signal'] = (
    evidence_df['P3_pay_before_approve'].astype(int) * 3
  + evidence_df['P2_violated'].astype(int) * 2
  + evidence_df['P1_violated'].astype(int)
)
risk_priority_table = evidence_df.sort_values(
    by=['risk_signal','raw_cost'], ascending=[False, True]
).reset_index(drop=True)

print("=== Per-case evidence table ===")
print(evidence_df.to_string(index=False))
print("\n=== Risk-priority candidates (sorted) ===")
print(risk_priority_table[['case','raw_cost','P1_violated',
                           'P2_violated','P3_pay_before_approve',
                           'risk_signal']].to_string(index=False))
print("\n=== Notes ===")
print("- CL-609 and CL-610: raw_cost 0 but P1 violated; classic")
print("  zero-cost policy failure. CL-604: Pay-before-Approve via")
print("  L(Pay Claim) move. CL-602: M(Fraud Screen) at EUR 480k HIGH.")
print("- CL-605: T(tau) silent move; tau is fitting per supplied notation.")
print("- CL-607 and CL-612: raw cost driven by scope mismatch, not control.")
print("- CL-601 and CL-603: raw cost driven by UI / retry noise, not control.")
```

The reproduction is intentionally a faithful replay of the supplied alignments rather than a recomputation: PM4Py's `conformance_diagnostics_alignments` may produce different optimal alignments for cases with reorderings (CL-604), label synonyms (CL-611), or scope mismatches (CL-607, CL-612). For those cases, the supplied alignments are treated as authoritative. The evidence table deliberately keeps `raw_cost` and the narrative `Severity 1–5` in separate universes — the dataframe surfaces candidates that combine policy-overlay flags with low raw cost (the dangerous zero-cost failures), but the severity ranking is set by Section 2's judgment, not by a computed score.
