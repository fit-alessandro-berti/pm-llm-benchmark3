# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

**Resilience** in this context means adapting the process to preserve mission-critical value (hospital supply, core throughput) while maintaining *minimum viable controls* that satisfy regulatory and safety requirements, even if the execution path deviates from the baseline. **Concealed noncompliance** occurs when adaptations prioritize throughput or convenience over real-time control, deferring compliance to post-hoc remediation, thereby creating audit debt, traceability gaps, and regulatory exposure that metrics alone mask.

In this incident, **true resilience** is demonstrated by A (hospital fast lane), B (manual auth check), I (defer noncritical), and J (backlog prioritization). These adaptations reallocated scarce capacity to protect life-critical supply while preserving or substituting controls. They align with the DFG shift to `Emergency triage` and `Backlog prioritization`, which maintained service where it mattered most without abandoning compliance fundamentals.

**Risky shortcuts** are C (skip authorization), G (dispatch without scan), and H (phone POD for controlled/cold-chain). These directly caused the 9.5% traceability exception rate, 4.8% cold-chain documentation gap, and 2.9% dual-control gap in T2. The DFG shows `Skip system authorization check` (1,600) and `Dispatch without scan` (1,800) as major flow branches that bypassed mandatory GxP/DEA controls. Throughput was preserved, but chain-of-custody was broken, creating unacceptable regulatory and patient-safety risk.

**Ambiguous/context-dependent** adaptations are D, E, F, K, and L. D and E are resilient early in T2 but become noncompliant as data stale and scan gaps accumulate. F is resilient for controlled substances but fails for cold-chain. K and L are necessary recovery mechanisms but become concealed noncompliance when treated as substitutes for real-time control. The T4 audit-remediation tail (2,300 cases) proves that post-hoc fixes cannot replicate real-time traceability; they only delay risk realization. Resilience requires controls *at the point of execution*, not reconstruction after the fact.

# 4. Recovery process model

**Trigger Conditions**  
- WMS downtime > 2 hours OR inventory sync failure > 10% OR scanning failure rate > 5% for > 1 hour.  
- Automatic activation of Fallback Mode; notify compliance, ops, and customer success leads.

**Triage Rules**  
1. Hospital-critical (life-saving/emergency)  Priority 1  
2. Cold-chain & Controlled-substance  Priority 2 (strict control enforcement)  
3. Pharmacy-standard  Priority 3  
4. Wholesale-bulk  Priority 4 (defer if capacity < 70%)  
- Triage decision logged with timestamp and operator ID; no implicit assumptions.

**Minimum Controls by Product/Customer Risk**  
- *All orders*: Manual authorization check against offline master list; dual-signature required.  
- *Cold-chain*: Physical temp log at dispatch + photo of gauge; no shipment if log missing.  
- *Controlled-substance*: Dual-control verification (two authorized staff); sealed tamper-evident packaging; manual chain-of-custody log.  
- *Recall-sensitive*: Batch/lot manual verification; quarantine if mismatch.  
- *Ambient/Standard*: Standard manual checklist; no authorization bypass allowed.

**Manual Evidence Capture**  
- Standardized paper/digital fallback forms with: order ID, customer, product, batch/lot, temp (if applicable), dual signatures, timestamp, courier ID.  
- POD: Signed physical receipt or timestamped photo; phone confirmation only for ambient/standard, logged with call recording/reference ID.  
- All forms scanned/uploaded to secure offline queue within 4 hours of dispatch.

**Backlog Prioritization**  
- Rule-based queue: Priority 1  2  3  4.  
- Time-boxed processing: Max 48h backlog age for Priority 1/2; auto-escalate to management if breached.  
- Transparent communication: Automated status updates to customers with expected fulfillment windows.

**Reconciliation and Audit-Remediation Flow**  
- Parallel track: Dedicated reconciliation team processes manual logs daily; max 500 cases/day to prevent bottleneck.  
- Daily closure target: 95% of fallback cases reconciled within 24h; unresolved cases flagged for compliance review.  
- Audit remediation capped at 5% of daily volume; if exceeded, trigger root-cause analysis and pause non-critical processing until controls stabilize.  
- Post-incident: 72h compliance audit of fallback period; lessons integrated into updated SOPs and drill scenarios.

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should accept strategic triage (hospital fast lane, deferral of non-critical bulk) and temporary manual workflows, provided they enforce minimum real-time controls for authorization, cold-chain temperature, and controlled-substance dual verification. It should not accept bypassing authorization checks, dispatching without traceability logs, or relying on post-hoc audit remediation as a compliance substitute, as these create unacceptable regulatory, safety, and recall-traceability risk. The recommendation shifts only if an offline-certified WMS fallback is deployed, or if regulatory guidance explicitly permits temporary control exemptions with documented, real-time mitigations and capped remediation windows. Until then, resilience must be measured by control completeness, not throughput alone.
