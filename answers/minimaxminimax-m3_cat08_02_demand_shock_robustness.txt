# Robust Process Redesign: Regional Diagnostic-Lab Sample Workflow

## 1. Stress scenarios

Four scenarios are defined that vary by **case-mix** and **disruption type**, not just by raw volume. A fifth "compound" scenario is added because in practice these rarely arrive alone.

| ID | Scenario name | Trigger | Why it differs from baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S1** | Winter respiratory wave (mix shift) | Infection season; rising Urgent + Complex-panel share | Volume +0% but mix rotates toward Urgent (1829%) and Complex-panel (1320%); per-case resource intensity rises |
| **S2** | Assay-machine degradation / partial outage | M1 or M2 downtime, calibration drift, or reagent shortage | M2 already has higher repeat rate; outage forces asymmetric routing; repeat-after-failure cases cluster on M2 after night shifts |
| **S3** | Validator / specialist absence | Friday specialist gap plus 1 MV sick leave / vacation | Specialist review queue rises 5h36h between normal and peak; one absence doubles the effective load on the remaining resource |
| **S4** | Courier compression / delay | Weather, traffic, or hub outage | Arrival spikes at 10:00 and 17:00 push intake bursts into pre-analytical and assay windows; sample integrity degrades with delays |
| **S5** | Compound: S1 + S3 + S4 | A bad winter Friday | Multiple bottlenecks simultaneously active; tests whether policies are orthogonal or conflict |

These were chosen because they stress **different combinations of stations** (S1 stresses assay+MV+specialist; S2 stresses assay+routing; S3 stresses MV+specialist; S4 stresses intake+pre-analytical). A redesign that only addresses S1 will fail S2–S4.

## 2. Bottleneck-migration analysis

For each scenario I trace (a) where overload begins, (b) where it migrates after a candidate intervention, and (c) what the *next* bottleneck becomes. The baseline peak utilization is the anchor:

> **Pre-analytical 96% · Assay 98% · TV 101% · MV 93% · Specialist 87% · Prep 89% · Accession 91%**

### S1 – Winter infection wave (demand-mix shift)
- **Initial overload:** Assay machines (98%) and Medical validation (93%). Complex-panel rises 1320% of volume but consumes ~3× the assay cycles and ~3× the MV time per case. The Technical-validation queue (3.5h16h) is already past the inflection point.
- **Migration after naïve I1 (strict Urgent-first):** Routine and Repeat-after-failure cases starve at Preparation  Batch-wait. Batch-wait queue rises from 11h to ~20h, the **Repeat rate climbs** (because starved cases are re-routed to M2 with higher failure rate), and 72h breaches grow. *Bottleneck migrates upstream from assay to prep/batch-wait and downstream to specialist review (now 87%).*
- **Migration after I5 (evening MV shift):** Helps the 93% MV queue, but TV is already at 101% peak. Without new TV capacity, evening MV sits idle or pulls cases into a TV bottleneck at end-of-day. Specialist review stays at 87% with 36h queue; the 48h HoldMedical edge is the worst-degraded edge in the data.
- **Migration after I3 (M1 to Urgent/Complex, M2 to Routine):** Frees M1 for Complex panels. M2 takes all Routine volume, and **Repeat-after-failure rises** because M2's repeat rate is already elevated. The RepeatAssay edge was already the worst performance edge in peak (26h). This pushes the bottleneck back into assay, plus Repeat-after-failure cycle time worsens.

### S2 – Assay-machine degradation / outage

If M1 fails, Urgent/Complex hits M2 and incurs higher repeat rates. The 11401260 Repeat-edge during peak already shows the system is close to a repeat-driven collapse.

## 3. Robust redesign package

The package below is designed to remain stable across S1–S5. It avoids three failure modes called out in the question: (a) optimising only the average, (b) shifting bottleneck without shrinking it, (c) starving one case type to feed another.

### R1. WIP-cap policy at every station (not just at assay)
- **What:** Hard cap on cases at each station equal to ~1.5× its service rate × service time. Above cap, the upstream station is signalled to pause release of that case type.
- **Why robust:** Bottleneck migration in every scenario above was driven by uncontrolled WIP. A WIP cap is the only lever that *prevents* migration rather than reacting to it.
- **Depends on:** Accurate service-time measurement (which the log provides), and acceptance that some cases will wait rather than be processed.
- **Where it may fail:** If caps are set per-station without per-case-type differentiation, Complex-panel will be starved (because it consumes multiple units of capacity per case). **Therefore cap by case type, not just by count.**

### R2. Early pre-analytical defect triage within 2h of arrival (I6, refined)
- **What:** Visual + automated check at intake; reject visibly haemolysed/insufficient/clotted samples within 2h rather than at full pre-analytical check.
- **Why robust:** Reduces the *Request recollection* loop (760/18120 = 4.2% normal, 780/9730 = 8.0% peak). Recollection loops consume intake capacity (clerks spend time on physician clarification — see notes) and amplify the S4 courier compression effect because re-routed samples return in the *next* spike. Also reduces the load on M2 night-shift repeat clustering by intercepting problem samples before they hit the machine.
- **Depends on:** Trained staff at intake; this is also a stress test on registration clerks who are already at 85% peak — must be paired with I8.
- **Where it may fail:** If pre-analytical staff is the same pool as defect triage, it adds load to the 96% peak station. Therefore: **dedicated triage role, not borrowed from main pre-analytical.**

### R3. Auto-registration for digitally-complete orders (I8) — but only as a *release* of capacity, not a capacity cut
- **What:** Auto-register orders with complete digital metadata; redeploy saved clerk time to recollection-loop management and courier slot negotiation.
- **Why robust:** Registration was at 68% normal / 85% peak — moderate, not extreme. Auto-registration's main value is **freeing clerk time to manage upstream loops** (Request-recollection clarification) rather than reducing headcount. Recollection loops grow disproportionately during peaks (760  780) and during S4.
- **Depends on:** Metadata quality from ordering systems (cannot assume it is uniformly good).
- **Where it may fail:** If auto-registered cases have higher downstream defect rates, the benefit disappears. Must monitor defect rate by registration mode.

### R4. Courier slot smoothing (I7) with **bounded smoothing**
- **What:** Stagger courier arrival slots so 10:00 and 17:00 spikes spread to a flatter profile; keep a small buffer window for true Urgent transport.
- **Why robust:** Acts on the *upstream cause* of pre-analytical bursts and night-shift repeat clustering rather than reacting downstream. Helps S4 directly and S1/S3 indirectly.
- **Depends on:** Couriers willing to shift; hospital/clinic cooperation. Cannot fully eliminate spikes, only dampen them.
- **Where it may fail:** Acute collections (e.g., sepsis workups) cannot be delayed; smoothing must be opt-out for true stat.

### R5. Adaptive batching — not larger batches (I2)
- **What:** Keep batch size at ~12, but make batching *per case-type*: small (6) batches for Urgent and any Complex-panel sub-component; medium (12) for Routine.
- **Why robust:** I2 as proposed (1224) improves prep efficiency but, during S1 (Urgent share rises to 29% and Complex to 20%), would push Urgent into longer waits because the bypass rate for Urgent drops from 74% to 58% during peak. Adaptive batching preserves prep efficiency for Routine *without* starving Urgent.
- **Depends on:** Discipline at the prep bench to keep small batches moving; clear visual signals.
- **Where it may fail:** Without priority rules (I1-style), prep staff may merge small Urgent batches into medium ones for convenience. The monitoring signal must catch this.

### R6. Pre-emptive cross-training of accession-correction specialists (I4) — but **only at baseline, not peak**
- **What:** Two accession specialists cross-train into technical validation *only when* TV utilization is below 80%. During peak (TV at 101%) they remain on accession.
- **Why robust:** I4 as proposed relieves accession (91% peak) but creates a shortage at TV (101% peak). Inverting the trigger — train in the off-peak, deploy at peak — addresses both without trading one bottleneck for another.
- **Depends on:** Training capacity for the cross-skill curriculum; the log says it takes 4 weeks, so the pool must be built before peak, not during.
- **Where it may fail:** If a cross-trained resource is needed for both stations simultaneously (rare but possible during S5), the policy must have a clear escalation owner.

## 4. Scenario-specific policies

These policies should be **armed** only when a specific trigger fires. Each has explicit off-switches.

### P-S1: Urgent-first dispatch at prep + assay (I1, *narrowed*)
- **Trigger:** Activate when Urgent share of incoming volume > 22% (current peak is 29%) **AND** Urgent p90 > 24h.
- **Action:** Strict priority at prep and assay *for Urgent only*; Routine and Repeat-after-failure wait. Complex-panel uses its own protected lane (see P-S1b).
- **Off-trigger:** Urgent share falls back below 18% **OR** Urgent p90 < 18h for 3 consecutive days. Also off if WIP cap (R1) trips at prep bench for >2h, indicating starvation of the Routine stream.
- **Why narrow:** I1 as proposed starves Routine and Complex-panel. The narrowing — *Urgent-only priority, Complex-panel in protected lane* — avoids starvation of the highest-stress case type.

### P-S1b: Complex-panel protected lane
- **Trigger:** Always on when Complex-panel share > 15%; reinforced when specialist review queue > 24h.
- **Action:** Pre-allocate one M1 capacity window per shift to Complex-panel assays; pre-allocate one MV slot per day. Specialist review gets a daily protected 90-min slot.
- **Off-trigger:** Complex-panel share falls below 12% for 2 weeks.

### P-S2: Machine-failure routing protocol
- **Trigger:** Activate when any single machine utilization > 95% for >4h **or** when a machine is in unplanned downtime.
- **Action:** Switch from dedication (R/I3) to *load-balanced* mode: M1 takes Urgent + first-come first-served; M2 takes a mix with explicit M2-repeat-cap (if M2 repeat rate > baseline +50% in a 4-hour window, divert M2 volume to M1).
- **Off-trigger:** Both machines < 85% utilization for 8h.

### P-S3: Outsource Complex-panel overflow (I10, *conditional*)
- **Trigger:** Activate when (a) local specialist review queue > 24h **AND** (b) partner-lab quoted turnaround < local p90 Complex-panel time.
- **Action:** Outsource only the *excess* above a local threshold — keep a minimum Complex-panel volume locally to maintain competency and traceability.
- **Off-trigger:** Either (a) or (b) fails. Critically: do not activate I10 during a courier disruption (S4) — partner-lab transport adds 12h on top of an already-degraded intake.
- **Why conditional:** I10's 12h transport delay is acceptable when local specialist queue is >24h, but worse than the local queue when S4 is not present. Static I10 policy is a known failure mode.

### P-S3b: Evening medical-validation shift (I5, *gated*)
- **Trigger:** Activate when MV queue > 12h **AND** TV queue < 6h (TV must be unblocked enough to feed MV).
- **Action:** Bring in evening MV shift; close at TV-queue trigger of >10h.
- **Off-trigger:** Either trigger false for 2 consecutive days.

### P-S4: Surge pre-analytical capacity + intake re-routing
- **Trigger:** Activate when 10:00-arrival batch exceeds normal 10:00 batch by >30% **OR** when courier reports >60-min delay.
- **Action:** Shift 2 registration clerks to intake-triage for the spike window; recall on-call pre-analytical staff for the 11:00–14:00 window.
- **Off-trigger:** Two consecutive normal-pattern days.

### P-S5: Compound-scenario policy
- **Trigger:** 2 of {S1, S3, S4} active simultaneously.
- **Action:** Escalate to ops director; freeze non-essential changes; apply all active policies simultaneously; daily review of the dashboard in §7.
- **Off-trigger:** All sub-triggers false for 3 days.

## 6. Residual vulnerabilities

After this redesign the following remain fragile, including one **created by the redesign itself**:

1. **Vulnerability introduced by the redesign — partner-lab traceability (P-S3):** Outsourcing Complex-panel cases creates a chain-of-custody seam. Any defect at the partner lab loops back into the *Manual accession correction* edge (currently 420/8530 = 4.9% peak) and re-enters our rework rate, which is not visible in our internal KPIs. This risk grows during S3+S5 compound scenarios.
2. **Vulnerability introduced by the redesign — auto-registration metadata drift (R3):** If a partner EMR changes a field, auto-registered cases may pass metadata errors downstream that human clerks would have caught. The defect rate by registration mode is the leading indicator; without it, the redesign silently degrades the next station.
3. **Friday specialist absence:** P-S1b partially mitigates this with a protected 90-min slot, but a specialist absence on a *peak Friday* (S3) still exceeds that slot for Complex-panel volume. The redesign does not eliminate this dependency.
4. **M2 elevated repeat rate:** Not addressed. R2 reduces the *input* to that pattern; P-S2 adds a repeat-cap. The machine itself remains a structural source of rework. If M2 fails during S2, the system still has a single point of failure for ~50% of assay capacity.
5. **Courier 10:00/17:00 spikes cannot be fully eliminated:** R4 dampens them but the structural cause (route economics for couriers) is outside the lab's control. A severe weather event (S4 extreme) will still produce a burst.
6. **WIP caps may surface latent capacity problems:** If caps are set too tight, the system will appear "stable" but reject work — a silent degradation in service to clients, not visible in any individual station's metric.
7. **Cross-trained resources (R6) are a workforce risk:** Cross-trained staff are a finite pool; if they are overused during peak, they burn out or accuracy drops — exactly the failure mode described for registration clerks working >9h shifts.

## 7. Minimum monitoring dashboard

A small set of leading indicators to track **daily during peak periods** (S1–S5). Thresholds are *warning bands*, not hard cutoffs.

| Indicator | Type | Threshold (warning band) | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **WIP at assay bench** vs cap | Leading | > 80% of cap | Tighten upstream release; check batching discipline |
| **Pre-analytical queue depth** | Leading | > 6h median during 10:00–14:00 | Activate P-S4 surge |
| **Urgent p90** | Leading | > 22h | Activate P-S1; check bypass rate at prep |
| **Bypass rate for Urgent at prep** | Leading | < 60% (current peak = 58%) | Investigate batching discipline — likely R5 failure |
| **Repeat-after-failure rate on M2** | Leading | > baseline + 25% over 4h | Activate M2-cap in P-S2; consider shifting to M1 |
| **Hold-for-specialist queue** | Leading | > 12h median | Activate P-S3b (MV) or P-S3 (outsource) per trigger |
| **TV queue** | Leading | > 8h | *Deactivate* P-S3b if active (gating); consider releasing cross-trained TV back |
| **Defect rate by registration mode** | Leading | Auto-registered defect > manual defect + 1pp | Suspend R3 auto-registration; investigate metadata source |
| **Request-recollection loop count** | Leading | > 8% of intake | R2 failure; review defect-triage staffing |
| **Courier arrival spike deviation** | Leading | 10:00 batch > normal 10:00 + 30% | P-S4 trigger |
| **Courier >60-min delay incidents** | Leading |  2/day | P-S4 trigger |
| **Urgent 18h SLA breach rate** | Lagging | > 2% (penalty) | Already failed; root-cause required |
| **Routine 72h SLA breach rate** | Lagging | > 5% | Already failed; check starvation under P-S1 |
| **Overall rework %** | Lagging | > 11% (peak baseline) | Check P-S3 partner-lab contribution; check M2 repeat rate |
| **Complex-panel p90** | Lagging | > 180h (peak = 210h) | P-S3 (outsource) evaluation; specialist-review coverage |

**Leading vs lagging distinction:** The first 10 rows are *predictive* — they fire *before* a SLA breach. The last 4 are *outcome* metrics that confirm or refute the redesign's effect. Optimising only the lagging metrics reproduces the historical-average failure mode called out in the problem statement.

**Why this dashboard is minimal:** Each row maps to a specific scenario trigger (P-S1…P-S5) and a specific bottleneck in §2. Indicators are chosen so that one is leading for every plausible failure path. Adding more metrics would dilute attention and push the team back toward average-based thinking.
