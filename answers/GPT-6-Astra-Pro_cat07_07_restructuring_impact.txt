# 1. Mechanism map

**The most defensible forecast is a redistribution of work and delay—not an assured reduction in handovers or cycle time.** East has a plausible opportunity to benefit from pooled technical capacity. North and South have more local expertise and stable working relationships to lose. Legal/Risk and shared approval are the principal capacity concerns.

All baseline figures and management estimates below come from the supplied scenario. Future effects are conditional hypotheses; there are no post-change observations from which to estimate causal effects or calibrated probabilities.

## What management’s own estimates imply

Assuming the listed handover categories are mutually exclusive and measured consistently:

| Quantity                                      | Current | Management estimate | Implication                                                       |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------: | ------------------: | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Listed handovers per 100 cases                |     343 |                 359 | **Increase of 4.7%**, not a reduction                             |
| Technical outward and return handovers        |      67 |                  77 | Increase of 14.9%                                                 |
| Approval-related outward and onward handovers |      81 |                  92 | Increase of 13.6%                                                 |
| Legal/Risk referrals                          |       9 |                  12 | Increase of 33.3%                                                 |
| Cross-team handovers                          |       6 |                   3 | Genuine projected reduction, but a small part of the listed total |

These are partial edge counts, not a complete routing model. They cannot establish the number of complete loops, and they should not be treated as transition probabilities.

A useful diagnostic is the frequency-weighted median-wait index:

$$
I=\sum_h \frac{\text{handover frequency}_h}{100}
                 \times \text{median wait}_h.
$$

It increases from **5.883 to 7.222 hours per case-equivalent**, approximately **22.8%**. This is **not expected total waiting time**: conditional medians are not additive expectations, the table is incomplete, and some work may overlap. Nevertheless, the estimate does not support a claim that coordination delays will automatically fall.

PM4Py’s handover network represents successive recorded resource participation; its working-together network represents participation in the same case. ## Capacity checks before treating utilization as a benefit

**Technical capacity.** Under an explicit equal-available-hours assumption, current technical utilization averages:

$$
(71+68+82)/3=73.7\%.
$$

A **15% relative increase** would mean approximately **84.7% utilization**; a **15-percentage-point increase** would mean **88.7%**. Management must distinguish these interpretations. Neither means 15% greater productivity: specialists may simply spend more time on triage, clarification or rework.

**Legal/Risk capacity.** With unchanged specialty mix, service time and staffing, increasing referrals from 9 to 12 multiplies offered workload by \(4/3\). Applied to the current utilizations, that produces illustrative demand-to-capacity ratios of approximately **121–124%**. That is not achievable utilization; it is an overload stress test indicating accumulating unfinished work. Improved detection might be valuable, but it needs corresponding capacity or reduced work per referral.

**Approval capacity.** If three supervisors currently provide equal approval capacity and removing one leaves two, unchanged effort per approval would imply:

$$
\frac{49}{42}\times\frac{3}{2}=1.75
$$

times the approval workload per unit of remaining capacity. Actual supervisor duties and availability are unknown, so this is a sensitivity calculation—not a forecast.

A common queue should therefore be modeled as **multiple skill-constrained workloads**, not one interchangeable workforce. In particular, no named security specialist is supplied. Security-routing feasibility must be established before that rule goes live.

“Leading” means available before enough cases have completed to assess final outcomes. It does not mean an indicator proves future harm.

Two measurement distinctions are essential.

First, with only one timestamp per event, the log cannot establish actual service duration or pure queue waiting. PM4Py exposes separate start- and completion-timestamp parameters for service-time analysis; using the completion timestamp as both does not supply the missing duration. Add queue entry, task start, pauses/resumptions where relevant, and completion. Report elapsed time and agreed working-calendar time separately. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][2])

Second, `get_rework_cases_per_activity` detects activities repeated within a case. Repetition can be legitimate; one occurrence of an explicitly labeled `Rework` activity can already represent a quality failure. Keep repetition, explicit rework and independently validated defects as separate measures. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][2])

For fairness, publish both unadjusted disparities and comparisons within legitimate need/priority strata. Do not “adjust away” extra handovers or rework caused by the restructuring, because those may be mechanisms of harm.

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

**Tail latency and unfinished-case exclusion.** Fast cases may dominate early completions while difficult work accumulates. A falling completed-case average can coexist with rising p95, overdue work and oldest-case age. Track intake cohorts, unfinished work and fixed-horizon completion probabilities.

**Disproportionate harm to high-complexity cases.** Local automation may accelerate numerous simple cases enough to offset much longer waits for a smaller complex-case population. Examine complexity-specific distributions and the time spent at each shared-service dependency.

**Loss of local knowledge.** A standardized answer may arrive quickly but be unsuitable for a regional convention or customer’s circumstances. Consequences may appear later as another customer contact, failed implementation, complaint or correction—not in the original case’s measured cycle time.

**Fairness across locations and customer segments.** East may improve while North/South deteriorate; a large customer segment may improve while a smaller accommodation-sensitive segment suffers. Equal raw waiting times are not necessarily fair when legitimate urgency and accommodation needs differ. Assess appropriate access, avoidable delay and outcome quality together.

**Loss of learning and future capability.** Removing hard work from local teams can improve current throughput while weakening future technical judgment. The effects may emerge only after experienced specialists leave or absences require local cover. Fewer local technical events are an exposure warning, not sufficient evidence of deskilling.

**Accountability gaps.** A case can move quickly through formal review while nobody clearly owns advice implementation, exception resolution or customer communication. Measure advice acknowledgement, owner changes, unowned intervals and the identity of the final accountable decision-maker.

**Exception-handling failure.** A routing rule may work for ordinary cases but fail for combined technical/legal needs, security cases, unusual regional requirements or disputed approvals. Rare variants should be reviewed rather than discarded as noise. No reduction in variant count proves that legitimate exceptions are now handled well.

**Reduced resilience and hidden workload transfer.** Normal-week performance may remain stable because specialists work overtime or local staff perform unrecorded clarifications. The same design can fail during absence, a central-system outage or a demand surge. Assess capacity under stress and include informal support and mentoring in workload accounting.

# 5. Pilot design

## Scope and sequencing

**Pilot a modified technical shared service first—not the entire restructuring simultaneously.** Retain local case ownership, protected regional advice time and all supervisor positions during the initial test. Keep Legal/Risk access skill-specific, and do not add difficult Quality Check to the technical pool without an explicit capacity allocation.

Begin with a proposed **25% of eligible new technical cases from each of North, South and East**. Include a planned representation of high-complexity and accommodation-sensitive cases; a pilot limited to easy cases would not test the main risks. Preserve an exception route for work that cannot safely follow the pilot pathway.

Before launch, standardize case-note requirements and establish who may perform security work. Also add case value: the proposed medium/high-value approval rule cannot be audited from the listed columns.

## Comparison group and spillovers

Randomize eligible cases within team and relevant intake strata to pilot versus existing routing, retaining assignment for the case’s lifetime. Compare contemporaneous arms primarily; use pre-change performance to understand trends and case mix.

Protect and record the specialist capacity allocated to each route. Shared personnel, informal advice and Legal/Risk capacity can contaminate the comparison. Record this exposure and monitor **all affected cases**, not only pilot cases. A pilot is not successful if its advantage is purchased by delaying the comparison group.

The pilot estimates performance at its tested routing share. It does not establish what happens when 100% of eligible demand enters the shared service. Increase the share in stages only after workload and tail-delay checks.

## Observation window and analysis

Use a proposed **12-week baseline**, a separately identified **two-week transition period**, and a **12-week pilot intake window**, followed by at least **eight weeks of outcome follow-up**. Extend follow-up where deadlines or outcomes remain immature. These are design choices, not evidence that every relevant outcome matures within eight weeks.

Keep transition cases visible in operational reporting but separate from clean policy-cohort comparisons. Do not split a case into “before” and “after” event fragments and then compare the fragments as complete traces.

Segment by **origin team, intake complexity, specialty need, customer segment and priority**. Report important intersections, especially team × complexity and segment × accommodation need. Freeze baseline attributes at the routing decision; keep later reclassifications as outcomes or time-varying process evidence.

Estimate uncertainty at the case level, accounting for shared workers and time blocks. Show denominators and censoring. Sparse specialty/segment cells require case review and cautious conclusions, not an automatic declaration of parity.

## Guardrails and response rules

The following are proposed operating thresholds to agree before launch—not universal statistical or queueing constants.

| Guardrail                             | Proposed trigger                                                                                                                              | Response                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Capacity and backlog                  | New technical/approval workload exceeds 90% of allocated capacity while backlog age grows for two consecutive weekly reviews                  | Freeze expansion, rebalance duties or add qualified capacity. Existing Legal/Risk load must not be worsened without capacity provision. |
| Tail performance                      | Adjusted p90 queue wait or mature-cohort p90 cycle time deteriorates by more than 20% against the comparison route in two informative reviews | Diagnose the affected queue/segment and reduce or revise pilot routing                                                                  |
| Quality and SLA                       | Mature-cohort validated rework or SLA miss rate worsens by more than 2 percentage points in a material team or segment                        | Hold expansion and review causes; use uncertainty intervals, but do not wait for significance to rescue visibly harmed cases            |
| Critical control or ownership failure | Missing required qualified review, unauthorized approval, or a critical case with no accountable owner                                        | Immediate case rescue and pause of the affected routing rule                                                                            |
| Learning and accommodation            | Repeated loss of planned mentoring or substantiated accommodation failures                                                                    | Restore protected local capability and revise staffing/routing before further expansion                                                 |

Collect a brief weekly staff pulse on ownership clarity, advice usability, context loss, interruption load and learning access. Combine anonymous feedback with structured reviews of delayed, reworked and successful cases from every team. Do not turn resource-network metrics into individual productivity rankings.

After technical routing demonstrates stable benefits, test specialist/Quality Check integration and shared approval **as separate changes**. Consider removing supervisor capacity only after approval performance has been tested under realistic demand and absence conditions.

# 6. Recommendation

**Modify the proposal and pilot first.** A hybrid model is preferable as the initial test: pooled specialist access with skill-specific dispatch, named local ownership, protected regional expertise, mentoring capacity and explicit responsibility for advice acknowledgement.

The rationale is balanced. East may genuinely benefit from access to North/South technical capacity, and standardization may improve decision consistency. Conversely, management’s own estimates predict more listed handovers and higher waits on most central-review edges. Legal/Risk referral growth and the proposed supervisor reduction require capacity evidence that is currently absent.

Expansion should require a joint result: meaningful access or performance benefits, stable upper-tail delays, acceptable quality, no material adverse team/segment effects, and no reliance on overtime or erosion of local capability. Benefits in East must not automatically justify avoidable deterioration elsewhere.

Management should **not infer from early averages** that central queues are stable, complex cases benefit, fewer organizational nodes mean fewer real handovers, higher utilization means higher productivity, or standardized activity profiles mean clear accountability. Nor should a successful low-volume pilot be extrapolated directly to full demand.

The decision criterion should be **better quality-adjusted service across the portfolio with sustainable capacity**, not simply fewer local loops or higher specialist utilization.

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

The sketch below expects actual event rows and an audited case registry. It does not create synthetic post-change observations.

The registry contains one row per case with frozen `origin_team`, `intake_complexity`, `intake_specialty`, `intake_segment`, `intake_priority`, `intake_time`, `period` and randomized `arm`. An optional `sla_deadline` must come from the contractual rules. The example assumes `Close` is terminal and is the contractual endpoint; change that explicitly where the SLA concerns another milestone.

Before analysis, validate timestamp meanings, timezone offsets, source event identifiers, Boolean flags and terminal status. Deduplicate by source identity—not merely by identical activity names. Resolve timestamp ties from trustworthy source ordering; do not manufacture a causal ordering for concurrent events.

**Filtering must retain whole cases.** Filtering only events performed by Team East, for example, would remove shared-service events from East-origin cases and create misleading paths. PM4Py supports case-level attribute filtering for this purpose. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][3])

```python
from typing import Any
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

C, A, T, R = (
    "case:concept:name", "concept:name",
    "time:timestamp", "org:resource"
)
BOTS = {"Bot_Intake", "Bot_Eligibility"}


def whole_cases(df: pd.DataFrame, **conditions) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Conditions refer to frozen case attributes replicated on every event."""
    result = df
    for attribute, values in conditions.items():
        result = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            result, attribute, list(values),
            level="case", retain=True, case_id_key=C
        )
    return result.copy()


def mine(df: pd.DataFrame) -> dict[str, Any]:
    if df.empty:
        return {"n_cases": 0}

    x = df.sort_values([C, T], kind="stable").copy()
    n = x[C].nunique()

    # Preserve bots in the sequence: removing their events first can invent
    # human-to-human adjacency. Separate bot edges when interpreting results.
    result = {
        "n_cases": n,
        "handover": pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(x, beta=0),
        "team_handover": pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
            x, beta=0, resource_key="team"
        ),
        "working_together": pm4py.discover_working_together_network(x),
        "subcontracting": pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(x, n=2),
    }

    # Human activity profiles; no sequence-based handover inference here.
    humans = x.loc[~x[R].isin(BOTS)]
    if not humans.empty:
        result["resource_similarity"] = (
            pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(humans)
        )
        result["roles"] = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(humans)

    dfg, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(x)
    performance_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
        x, perf_aggregation_key="all"
    )
    result["dfg_per_100_cases"] = {
        edge: 100 * count / n for edge, count in dfg.items()
    }
    result["performance_dfg"] = performance_dfg
    result["start_activities"], result["end_activities"] = starts, ends

    variants = pm4py.get_variants(x)  # DataFrame input: variant -> count
    result["variant_shares"] = {
        variant: count / n for variant, count in variants.items()
    }
    repeat_counts = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(x)
    result["repeated_activity_cases_per_100"] = {
        activity: 100 * count / n
        for activity, count in repeat_counts.items()
    }

    # Raw counts and edge quantiles have explicit, comparable denominators.
    x["previous_resource"] = x.groupby(C)[R].shift()
    x["previous_time"] = x.groupby(C)[T].shift()
    x["gap_hours"] = (
        x[T] - x["previous_time"]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 3600

    changed = x["previous_resource"].notna() & (
        x[R] != x["previous_resource"]
    )
    edges = x.loc[changed]
    result["resource_changes_per_100_cases"] = 100 * len(edges) / n
    result["edge_gap_quantiles"] = (
        edges.groupby(["previous_resource", R])["gap_hours"]
        .quantile([0.50, 0.90, 0.95]).unstack()
    )
    # These completion-to-completion gaps are NOT pure queue waiting.

    if "start_timestamp" in x and x["start_timestamp"].notna().all():
        if (x["start_timestamp"] > x[T]).any():
            raise ValueError("Task start occurs after completion.")

        result["activity_service_seconds"] = pm4py.get_service_time(
            x, aggregation_measure="median",
            start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp"
        )

        # Use additive case decomposition only for non-overlapping,
        # complete, non-preemptive task intervals.
        ordered = x.sort_values([C, "start_timestamp", T])
        no_overlap = all(
            not g["start_timestamp"].lt(g[T].cummax().shift()).any()
            for _, g in ordered.groupby(C)
        )
        if no_overlap:
            timed = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
                x.copy(), start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp"
            )
            result["case_time_decomposition"] = timed[
                [C, "@@service_time", "@@waiting_time", "@@sojourn_time"]
            ].drop_duplicates(C)
        else:
            result["case_time_decomposition"] = (
                "Overlapping intervals: calculate interval-union busy time "
                "and direct work-item queue waits separately."
            )

    return result


def compare(
    raw: pd.DataFrame,
    registry: pd.DataFrame,
    *,
    as_of: str,
    followup_days: int = 56
) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """
    Compare intake cohorts at a common follow-up horizon.
    Raw timestamps must already have explicit timezone offsets.
    rework_flag must be audited Boolean data.
    """
    required = {C, A, T, R, "team", "rework_flag", "outcome"}
    if required - set(raw.columns):
        raise ValueError(f"Missing event columns: {required - set(raw.columns)}")
    if registry[C].duplicated().any():
        raise ValueError("Registry must contain one row per case.")
    if raw[[C, A, T, R]].isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError("Missing core event attributes require investigation.")

    cutoff = pd.to_datetime(as_of, utc=True)
    events = raw.copy()
    events[T] = pd.to_datetime(events[T], utc=True, errors="raise")
    if "start_timestamp" in events:
        events["start_timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(
            events["start_timestamp"], utc=True, errors="raise"
        )

    # Conservative guard: use an explicit tie-aware analysis when needed.
    if events.duplicated([C, T]).any():
        raise ValueError("Resolve timestamp ties before sequence analysis.")

    live = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        events.loc[events[T] <= cutoff].copy(),
        case_id=C, activity_key=A, timestamp_key=T
    )

    meta = registry.copy()
    meta["intake_time"] = pd.to_datetime(meta["intake_time"], utc=True)
    meta["horizon_end"] = (
        meta["intake_time"] + pd.Timedelta(days=followup_days)
    )
    # Include all cases with sufficient follow-up, not merely closed cases.
    meta = meta.loc[meta["horizon_end"] <= cutoff].copy()

    x = live.merge(meta, on=C, how="inner", validate="many_to_one")
    x = x.loc[x[T] <= x["horizon_end"]].copy()

    cases = meta.set_index(C)
    closing = (
        x.loc[x[A].eq("Close")]
        .sort_values(T).drop_duplicates(C, keep="last").set_index(C)
    )
    cases["closed_at"] = closing[T]
    cases["outcome_at_close"] = closing["outcome"]
    cases["closed_by_horizon"] = cases["closed_at"].notna()
    endpoint = cases["closed_at"].fillna(cases["horizon_end"])
    cases["observed_days"] = (
        endpoint - cases["intake_time"]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 86400

    explicit_rework = (
        x.assign(rework_seen=x["rework_flag"].eq(True) | x[A].eq("Rework"))
        .groupby(C)["rework_seen"].any()
    )
    cases["rework_seen_by_horizon"] = explicit_rework.reindex(cases.index)

    # Nullable labels: open/not-yet-due is NOT an on-time outcome.
    cases["sla_miss"] = pd.Series(pd.NA, index=cases.index, dtype="boolean")
    if "sla_deadline" in cases:
        deadline = pd.to_datetime(cases["sla_deadline"], utc=True)
        known = deadline.notna() & (
            cases["closed_by_horizon"] | (deadline < cases["horizon_end"])
        )
        cases.loc[known, "sla_miss"] = endpoint[known] > deadline[known]

    reports = {}
    segment_attributes = [
        "origin_team", "intake_complexity",
        "intake_specialty", "intake_segment"
    ]

    for (period, arm), group in x.groupby(["period", "arm"]):
        reports[(period, arm, "all")] = mine(group)

        for attribute in segment_attributes:
            for value in group[attribute].dropna().unique():
                selected = whole_cases(group, **{attribute: [value]})
                reports[(period, arm, attribute, value)] = mine(selected)

        reports[(period, arm, "East_high")] = mine(
            whole_cases(
                group, origin_team=["East"], intake_complexity=["high"]
            )
        )

        # Same reference variants in both periods; keep rare variants in
        # the main analysis rather than filtering them out of the scorecard.
        review_variants = [
            variant for variant in pm4py.get_variants(group)
            if "Rework" in variant
        ]
        if review_variants:
            reports[(period, arm, "explicit_rework_variants")] = mine(
                pm4py.filter_variants(group, review_variants)
            )

    return {
        "reports": reports,
        "case_outcomes": cases,
        "live_log": live,  # Retained for uncapped operational WIP analysis.
    }
```

The performance DFG provides edge-level timing aggregates; the separate edge quantiles above make upper-tail monitoring explicit. With completion-only events, interpret both as elapsed inter-event timing—not service duration or causal queue delay. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][4])
