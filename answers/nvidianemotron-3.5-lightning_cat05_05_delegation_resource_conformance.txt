## 2. Rule Interpretation

**RR1 – Drafter/approver separation:**  
At event timestamp, if the same normalized human identity performs `Draft_Change_Request` and `Approve_Change` for the same case, a violation occurs. Counted via aliasnormalized identity (`u_bk`/`b.koch`  Ben Koch).

**RR2 – Implementer/verifier separation:**  
At event timestamp, if the same human performs `Implement_Change` and `Verify_Implementation` for the same case, a violation occurs.

**RR3 – Highrisk approval authority:**  
For HIGHrisk cases, `Approve_Change` must be performed by the Quality Manager (Asha Rao, from 20260101) or a valid QM delegate. A delegation D is valid at event time iff all hold:  
(a) D’s effective interval covers the event timestamp;  
(b) D’s scope covers the change type (e.g., `production_equipment`, `cooling_system`);  
(c) the principal (Asha) is absent or formally unavailable when the delegation’s condition requires it (per absence evidence);  
(d) no prohibited subdelegation occurred.  
*Later database entries* (e.g., QMS migration records created after the fact) do **not** by themselves prove delegation validity at event time; signed/physical evidence predating the event is the primary proof. (Example: DQM01 signed PDF 20260809 18:00 is primary; DB record created 20260812 09:15 is a migration artifact.)

**RR4 – Emergency substitution:**  
A HIGHrisk emergency change may be implemented after approval by the Incident Commander (Diego from 20260701) or a valid IC delegate (Farid under DIC03 for CI44 only). A QM or valid QM delegate must perform `Retrospective_QM_Review` within **two business days** of the change event. Business days = calendar days excluding weekends and site holidays (20260815 and 16 are weekend, no holiday in period). The exception is justified **only** when the emergency flag and a safety rationale are credible. If the review is performed by a QM delegate, the delegation’s effective interval and principalabsence condition must also hold at review time.

**RR5 – Identity evidence:**  
A service account is not a human identity. It can support conformance **only** if reliable session evidence (workstation assignment, badge logs, FIDOtoken proximity, CCTV where available) maps the action to an authorized human. Before any SoD check, all human aliases belonging to the same person must be normalized to a single identity key. Failure to normalize can produce false SoD violations or mask real ones.

**RR6 – Suspicion versus proof:**  
Repeated resource pairs, unusual reciprocal approvals, shared workstations, and concentrated handovers are **indicators** only. They are not proof of collusion without supporting evidence (e.g., credential logs, timed access records, independent corroboration). Evaluation must distinguish between organizationalpattern risk and actual rule violation.

**Principal on leave treatment:**  
If a principal performs a duty while on leave, the action is evaluated against the same rules as if present, but the leave status is a factual element for delegation validity (RR3 condition “principal absent when required”) and for assessing whether the action constitutes an unauthorized bypass of the delegation mechanism. If the principal acts while absent without a valid delegation or emergency exception, it is treated as an unauthorized act or a data/identity problem if credential sharing is uncertain. Eventtime evaluation must use the absence evidence provided, **not** the current user master.

**Later DB entry proving earlier delegation:**  
As with RR3, a QMS database record created during migration cannot by itself serve as primary evidence that a delegation was in effect at an earlier event. The signed PDF timestamp predating the event is the reliable eventtime evidence; the later DB entry is secondary context only.

## 6. Control Improvements

- **Delegation creation:** Standardize a template requiring effectiveinterval start/end, explicit scope description, principalabsence condition documentation, and a “no subdelegation” checkbox. Link creation to HR leave/absence system so that delegations automatically expire or trigger review when the principal’s status changes.
- **Effectivetime recording:** Enforce timestamped, immutable records for all delegations (e.g., signed PDF with validation timestamp). Prohibit backdating; any postevent DB entries must be annotated as migration artifacts and not used for eventtime conformance.
- **Principal availability:** Integrate calendar/leavemanagement system directly with the changecontrol engine. Autoflag any `Approve_Change` or `Implement_Change` by a role holder whose master record shows active leave, prompting a delegationvalidity check before acceptance.
- **Alias management:** Enforce a centralized alias registry in the IAM system. Before any segregationofduties or delegation check, run a normalization routine that maps all known aliases (`u_bk`/`b.koch`, etc.) to a single human key. Prevent creation of orphan or duplicate aliases.
- **Individual authentication:** Require multifactor authentication (MFA) for all changecontrol actions, especially `Approve_Change` and `Verify_Implementation`. Disable sharedworkstation use for HIGHrisk cases; if shared terminals are unavoidable, mandate reauthentication every 2 hours or on privilege escalation, with session logs sent to SIEM.
- **Emergency review (RR4):** Implement an automated workflow trigger: when `emergency = true`, route `Approve_Change` to the IC (or valid IC delegate), generate a `Retrospective_QM_Review` task with a duedate calculated as “two business days” from the change timestamp (excluding weekends/holidays), and require a safetyrationale attachment. Track SLA breaches and escalate to management.
- **Pair rotation:** Formalize a rotatingpair policy for HIGHrisk production changes. Document rotating drafter/approver pairs, required delegation status at handover, and a maximum consecutivedays limit for any single pair. Audit pair compliance quarterly.
- **Network monitoring:** Deploy workstationlevel authentication logs, CCTV where feasible, and FIDOtoken audit trails for all serviceaccount actions. Integrate with SIEM for anomaly detection on sharedworkstation use (e.g., login outside assigned shift, multiple users from one terminal).
- **Evidential retention:** Mandate digital signing with timestamps for all delegations. Retain session logs, badge records, and VPN metadata for a minimum of 12 months. Archive leave/absence records synchronized with the changecontrol system. Implement an audit trail for aliasnormalization events, showing who normalized what and when.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

The apparent nonconformance across the ten examined changecontrol cases is best understood as a **mix of three distinct risk dimensions**:

- **Genuine authority failure** (40% of cases): Clear rule violations where no valid delegation or exception applies. Highlights include CHG502 (drafter/approver selfapproval), CHG507 (nonQM approving HIGHrisk), CHG510 (Clara using an invalid subdelegation to approve an emergency change), and CHG506 (approval after sickleave start with credential compromise evidence). Key examples are CHG503 (svc_qms verify with weak human attribution), CHG504 (QM approving while on leave with VPNsession ambiguity), and CHG509 (DQM02 principalabsence condition conflict). In these cases, the control objective is undermined by missing or ambiguous evidence rather than deliberate misconduct.
- **Organizationalpattern risk** (30% of cases): Repeated collaboration, shared workstations, and absent formal pairrotation policies that create systemic vulnerability, even if individual events are currently explicable. The ClaraBen pair, reciprocal trainingchange approvals, and sharedterminal use (CHG503, CHG509) fall here. While not proof of collusion, they indicate control weaknesses that could be exploited.

**Uncertainty:** The largest source of uncertainty resides in the identityattribution gaps (serviceaccount sessions, VPNactor verification, sickleave credential use). Without definitive logging of individual authentication, the line between “authorized substitution” and “unauthorized act” remains subjective.
