# Warranty Process Redesign Advisory

## 1. Current-process diagnosis

**The dominant path is genuinely fast — and that is precisely the problem.** The frequency DFG shows 2,300 of 5,000 cases (46%) run Intake  Auto screen  Fast approval  Dispatch replacement  Quality/compliance close. Chaining the performance DFG medians (0.5 + 0.4 + 1.8 + 3.2) gives roughly a **6-hour median end-to-end**, at near-zero marginal decision cost (EUR 0.20 auto screen). No reviewed path comes close: the segment table shows 46–120h medians for manual routes.

**But the fast path's cost advantage mostly evaporates under its own failure tail.** The rework statistics show Fast approval is **31% cheaper before rework but only 9% cheaper after** complaints, reshipments, and service credits — i.e., roughly three-quarters of the nominal advantage is consumed by rework. The complaint mechanism is visible in the performance DFG: Fast approval  Customer complaint/reopen (mean 72h, p90 150h) and Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen (**mean 96h, p90 180h**), with the temporal profile attributing the latter to incorrect replacement part selection. These complaints surface *weeks* after the case was counted "closed," so average cycle-time reporting systematically flatters the fast path.

**The contradiction resolves by segment, not by choosing a winner.** Fast-path complaint rates are 14.1% versus 3.3% for the repair path overall — but the segment outcomes show the damage is concentrated: **22% (Premium high-value) and 18% (Standard high-value)** on the fast path versus 5–6% reviewed, while Premium low-risk accessories show only 6% fast-path complaints and face *service-credit exposure above 24h* — meaning the slow "safer" path (46h median) would actually be worse for them. With wrong-replacement expected costs of EUR 95 (accessories) versus EUR 410 (high-value), the fast path is economically sound for low-risk accessories and economically irrational for high-value devices. Today's auto-screen rules are outdated and not segment-aware, so they impose one trade-off curve on all segments.

**Compliance is breached at scale, and the measurement caveats matter.** Token-based replay gives fitness 0.91 but only **68% fitting traces** (~1,600 nonconforming cases). Alignment diagnostics name the exact deviations: Fast approval before evidence completeness, Dispatch without risk review for high-value devices (R1/R2 exposure), and complaint/reopen after close (R3 exposure). Note also that the discovered model's **low precision** means rare-path noise is high — compliance conclusions should rest on alignments against the normative net, not on the discovered model's exotic paths.

**The evidence loop is the biggest tail driver.** Request missing evidence fires 1,430 times (650 auto + 780 manual), returns 890 cases to manual review at **34h mean / 88h p90 per iteration**, repeats 2× in 26% of cases entering it, and produces 320 of the 390 abandonments. The unclear-defect/missing-evidence segment (22% of volume) sits at a **140h median** — it is the process's center of gravity for slowness, and none of the fast-path debate touches it.

**Resource structure explains why the queues behave as they do.** Manual review has 252 productive hours/week (7 × 36h) with a sharp queue inflection above 82% utilization; fraud/risk has only 2 specialists with Monday/campaign spikes; repair-quote delay is explicitly attributed to waiting for part-price confirmation (30h mean to customer approval); quality check is *underused*; the escalation board is one 45-minute daily slot.

**One honest caveat:** only 3,625 cases reach a terminal node (3,015 closed + 220 denied + 390 abandoned) against 5,000 starts. Right-censoring means slow paths look somewhat worse than they are and fast-path complaint tails are undercounted. Cross-path comparisons should be read as directional, not exact — and no precise ROI or Pareto dominance can be computed from this data alone.

## 2. Redesign A: Risk-Tiered Authorization — Segment-Aware Fast Lanes

**Redesigned flow.** Keep the existing topology but rewrite the auto-screen rule engine to authorize the fast lane *by segment × value band*: (a) Premium and Standard low-risk accessories  fast lane retained, with a hard 24h SLA for Premium; (b) all high-value devices  fast approval disabled; routed to a *tiered* review (automated document/risk checklist first, human fraud/risk review only on flags) before any dispatch; (c) unclear-defect claims  excluded from auto-approval entirely pending evidence. R2's existing exception (Premium low-risk accessory may dispatch pre-completeness) is retained; everywhere else, evidence completeness becomes a hard precondition encoded in the rules.

**Targets.** The 2,550 auto-screen fast approvals and the alignment deviation "dispatch without risk review for high-value devices"; the 35% of volume that is high-value; the 24h Premium accessory threshold.

**Mechanism.** This reallocates the fast lane to where its economics survive (accessories: 6–8% complaints, EUR 95 error cost, service-credit cliff at 24h) and withdraws it where it fails (high-value: 18–22% complaints, EUR 410 error cost, R1/R2 exposure). Because the rule update is deployable in two weeks, this is the fastest lever available. It directly attacks the 31%9% cost-erosion pattern by removing the cases that generate the erosion.

**Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved.** The prompt warns stricter auto rules may push **+15–35% inflow to manual review** — against a pool already near its 82% utilization cliff, this is the design's central bet and its central risk. It does nothing for the 140h unclear-defect cohort, does not shorten the repair path, and leaves the part-price confirmation wait untouched. Some high-value cases will get slower by design.

## 3. Redesign B: Evidence-Complete-at-Intake — Single-Pass Evidence and Decision Postponement

**Redesigned flow.** Restructure *when decisions happen* rather than who authorizes them: at intake, a defect-type/segment-driven evidence checklist is generated and presented through a new evidence portal as **one consolidated request** (no iterative top-ups). Eligibility decisions — auto or manual — are made only after completeness is confirmed, eliminating the approve-then-request pattern the alignments flag. Assisted intake (phone/callback option, guided upload) protects low-digital-literacy customers; reminders and a visible status tracker replace silent waiting.

**Targets.** The Request missing evidence loop (1,430 occurrences, 890 backflows at 34h mean, 26% repeat rate), the 320 abandonments, and the unclear-defect segment (22% of volume, 140h median, highest abandonment).

**Mechanism.** The portal's expected 30–45% reduction in repeated requests compresses the worst loop in the process; single-pass requests remove the 34h-mean round trips; deciding *after* completeness makes R2 conformant by construction rather than by inspection. This converts the process's largest tail (p90 88h per evidence iteration) into a bounded front-end step.

**Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved.** Clean cases (the ~78% that never need extra evidence) must not be forced through evidence-first — applying it universally would breach the Premium 24h threshold, so this design accepts added intake friction only for the evidence-prone population. It does not fix wrong-part dispatch, does not touch authorization rules, carries real abandonment risk if digital-only, and is a longer IT lead-time than Redesign A. Bottlenecks will migrate to customer response time — external waiting that internal dashboards won't see unless instrumented.

---

## 4. Redesign C: Right-First-Time Fulfillment — Pre-Dispatch Gate and Empowered Recovery

**Redesigned flow.** Leave routing largely as-is; change the *execution and recovery* layer: (a) a lightweight **pre-dispatch quality gate** (part-number/device-match verification) applied only to high-value dispatches, staffed by the currently underused quality inspectors to hold added latency near the 6h lower bound of the stated 6–18h range; (b) an automated part-price lookup to eliminate the part-price-confirmation wait that drives the 30h Repair quote  Customer approval leg; (c) a redesigned complaint/reopen lane: empowered agents with an authority matrix (instant goodwill replacement for low-risk segments below a threshold; priority rework slots for high-value), with a templated rapid re-review that satisfies R3 before any post-reopen closure, and the escalation board reserved for genuine high-value/Premium disputes.

**Targets.** The Dispatch  Complaint long tail (96h mean, p90 180h, attributed to wrong part selection), the 12.5% rework-repair rate on high-value devices, the 30h quote-wait inside the 96–120h reviewed paths, and the 40–54h reopen-handling legs (310 to manual review, 120 to escalation).

**Mechanism.** This design buys first-time-right at the point of physical fulfillment, where the EUR 410 error cost lives, using idle capacity (2 inspectors, EUR 38/h) rather than new headcount. Shortening the quote wait attacks the largest *controllable* chunk of the expensive-but-good repair path, making the quality path competitive rather than mandatory. Formalizing the reopen lane turns a conformance deviation (reopen after close) into a governed, R3-compliant recovery process.

**Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved.** High-value dispatches get 6–18h slower *on purpose* — a defensible trade only if the gate actually catches errors. It does not fix upstream over-use of fast approval for high-value (that's Redesign A's job), leaves the evidence loop intact, and the escalation board's scalability ceiling remains.

## 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A**
- *Review-capacity collapse:* rerouting high-value cases floods 7 reviewers (82% cliff) and 2 fraud specialists; a +15–35% inflow scenario could erase the speed win and breach Premium 24h SLAs collateral-style. **Migration:** bottleneck moves from dispatch/complaint handling to fraud/risk review, spiking Mondays and post-campaign.
- *Over-correction by rules:* conservative auto rules shunt borderline accessory claims into manual review, degrading the segments the fast lane serves best. **Migration:** complaint types shift from fulfillment errors to SLA-breach service credits.
- *Silent workaround:* under load, staff dispatch high-value without review; deviations persist but hide if monitoring tracks averages instead of alignments.

**Redesign B**
- *Digital exclusion:* the portal cuts repeat requests but raises abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers — the 320 abandonments could grow even as loop metrics improve, a net loss disguised as efficiency.
- *Front-end friction creep:* evidence-first applied too broadly delays clean cases and breaches the Premium 24h threshold. **Migration:** bottleneck moves from evidence ping-pong to (a) burst arrivals into manual review as completed evidence lands simultaneously, and (b) invisible external customer wait time.

**Redesign C**
- *Quality theater:* under campaign pressure the pre-dispatch gate degenerates into a rubber stamp — near-100% pass rates with no complaint reduction, while 6–18h is added for nothing. **Migration:** bottleneck moves to the 2 quality inspectors during repair campaigns.
- *Recovery-lane moral hazard / R3 breach:* overly generous instant replacements invite opportunistic claims; fast-lane closures that skip the templated re-review violate R3. **Migration:** dispute volume migrates to the escalation board, whose 45-minute daily slot cannot scale; part-price automation with stale data creates a new wrong-quote complaint type.

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**Stage 0 (Weeks 1–2) — Instrumentation.** Fix event-log capture of evidence-completeness timestamps; define cohorts as segment × entry-path; set baselines for complaint rate at *t+60 days post-close*, fully loaded cost per case (labor + replacement + service credits + reshipment), and weekly alignment-deviation counts. No design changes yet.

**Stage 1 (Weeks 2–6) — Pilot Redesign A scoped to accessories only.** Deploy segment-aware rules for Premium/Standard low-risk accessories; leave high-value rules unchanged initially (this caps the manual-inflow shock). *Monitor:* Premium accessory median (must stay 24h), accessory complaint rates (guardrail: no worse than 6–8% baseline), manual-review utilization (hard ceiling 82%, alarm 85%), R1/R2 deviations in high-value (must not rise). *Go:* SLAs held, utilization <82%, complaints flat or down  extend strictness to high-value with temporary reviewer capacity. *Stop:* sustained utilization >85–88% or accessory complaints +2pp  pause, add capacity, re-tune thresholds.

**Stage 2 (Weeks 6–14) — Pilot Redesign C on Standard high-value dispatches.** Pre-dispatch gate staffed by idle inspectors; automated part-price lookup live. *Monitor:* DispatchComplaint occurrences (target 30% relative reduction), high-value rework repair (12.5%  9%), added latency (10h p90), gate pass rate (a >97–98% pass rate signals rubber-stamping). *Go/stop:* complaints down with latency 10h  extend to Premium high-value; complaints unchanged after 6–8 weeks  dismantle the gate rather than let it become ritual.

**Stage 3 (Months 4–6) — Pilot Redesign B on the unclear-defect cohort only.** Portal + consolidated single request + assisted-channel fallback. *Monitor:* repeat-request rate (expect 30–45% from the 26% base), abandonment (guardrail: no more than ~10% relative increase vs. the 320 baseline), unclear-defect median (140h  target <100h), channel-mix integrity. *Stop:* abandonment rises or assisted-channel uptake collapses  redesign intake assistance before scaling.

**Stage 4 (Month 6+) — Integrated rollout.** Full rule set, permanent capacity rebalance (cross-trained inspectors, leveled fraud scheduling), conformance dashboard with weekly alignment counts (targets: fitness 0.95, fitting traces 80–85%).

**Sequencing logic.** Cheapest/fastest deployable first (rule update: 2 weeks), lowest-regret segments first (accessories, where fast-path economics demonstrably hold), then execution fixes where error cost is highest (high-value), then the structural IT change last (longest lead, behavioral risk). Each stage ships with a counterbalancing guardrail so no stage optimizes one metric blindly.

**How to tell real improvement from displaced failure.** (1) Judge everything on a 60-day post-close window and fully loaded cost — the current process's core deception is that its failures arrive after the case is counted closed. (2) Run cohort holdouts (roll out by region or claim-type slices) for difference-in-differences rather than before/after averages, which censoring contaminates. (3) Watch the *next* queue at every stage: manual-review utilization, fraud Monday loads, inspector backlog — a cycle-time win that saturates a downstream resource is displacement, not improvement. (4) Pair every speed metric with a quality guardrail (complaint rate, rework rate, alignment deviations) so metric gaming is visible within one reporting cycle.
