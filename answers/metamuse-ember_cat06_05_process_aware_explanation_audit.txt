## 2. Process-model consistency checks

**A. Point-in-time availability audit.** For every feature, record source event or field, event time, ingestion time, valid-from and valid-to, supersession rules, and snapshot time. Require feature time to be less than or equal to `t0` for every training and scoring example. Simulate the feature store as it existed at `t0` using an as-of join. Any feature whose value changes when recomputed at `t0` fails, except for a documented queue-snapshot tolerance that is itself verified against `t0`.

**B. Prefix truncation and future-perturbation tests.** Truncate each trace immediately after the first Coverage Check Completed, using the source sequence index rather than timestamp alone. Recompute all features from that prefix and compare them to production values. Then delete, shuffle or alter all post-`t0` events and require features, predictions and local explanations to remain unchanged. A change proves complete-trace dependence. This test should remove or quarantine `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, `risk_score_current`, `last_resource_role`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, and the defective prior-claim SQL.

**C. Activity-order feasibility with conformance methods.** Encode the normative process as a state machine or Petri-net-like model with the fast-track alternative, evidence loop, parallel fraud branch, settlement, appeal loop and terminal closure. Use **alignment** to find the closest model path for an observed prefix and quantify deviations. Use **footprint** relations derived from the normative model to test precedence such as Damage Assessment eventually before Closure. Use **declarative constraints** for rules such as Appeal only after Partial or Deny, Documents Received only after Requested, and Closure only as the final activity. Use a **discovered DFG** only diagnostically: a frequent edge is not automatically normative, because artifacts and common variants can be frequent, while legitimate parallel fraud or fast-track paths can be rare. Conversely, a DFG edge that contradicts the normative model, such as Closure to Damage Assessment, is a data-quality alarm, not proof of a new process rule.

**D. Enabled, future-possible and impossible at `t0`.** Replay the prefix to its `t0` state. Enabled activities are Documents Requested, Damage Assessment Completed on the fast track, and conditionally Fraud Investigation Opened after a trigger. Future-possible activities include Documents Received after a request and a first Settlement Decision after Damage Assessment. Impossible activities at `t0` include Appeal, a second Settlement Decision and Claim Closed. An explainer must distinguish an observed prefix pattern from a merely possible future pattern. Patterns using future-possible or impossible activities cannot be presented as observed early warnings.

**E. Same-timestamp ambiguity.** Identify groups of events sharing a timestamp and measure how often the sequence index is missing. Enumerate orderings consistent with the tied timestamps and test whether each pattern and feature is invariant across those orderings. Only invariant patterns may pass. Quarantine non-invariant cases such as Case Z and fix ingestion ordering at source. Timestamp order alone is not a reliable sequence.

**F. Repeated Settlement Decision semantics.** Count occurrences of Settlement Decision Recorded per case. A second occurrence is legitimate only if the first decision was Partial or Deny and an Appeal intervenes. Features and explanations must split first and second decisions by occurrence index and decision type. Without that split, Appeal to Settlement Decision is semantically legitimate in a complete trace but still unavailable and ambiguous at `t0`.

**Acceptance gates.** Zero critical leakage features, 100% point-in-time feature availability, zero impossible prefix activities, documented handling of all timestamp ties, and full prefix-truncation invariance are required before any explanation is released.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

**Defensible predictive signal.** Surviving members include `initial_damage_complexity`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, provisionally `initial_reserve`, and a repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0`. These may be claimed to be associated with breach risk in historical data assembled at the same prediction time and may support ranking or triage if calibrated. They may not be claimed to cause breach, nor that changing the feature would change the outcome. Predictive validity requires point-in-time correctness, time-based validation and calibration; it does not establish causality or actionability.

**Process-state or congestion signal.** Members include `queue_length_at_t0`, elapsed time and remaining slack, and the observed prefix state that coverage is complete while documents, damage assessment and fraud investigation have not yet been observed. These may be described as current workload and slack context. They may not be claimed to prove that reducing queue length by a specified amount will prevent an individual breach, because staffing, arrival surges and case mix are common causes. Operational use is limited to monitoring, prioritisation and further investigation.

**Proxy requiring fairness and robustness review.** Members include `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, `initial_reserve` and, after repair, `prior_claim_count_before_t0`. These may show predictive association in the audited historical population. They may not be described as delay drivers or used for differential treatment without fairness review. The audit must examine group calibration and error rates, test whether effects are mediated by operational factors such as repair-network coverage, language support, channel submission quality or documented complexity, and prefer those operational mediators when they explain the association. Accuracy alone does not settle fairness or causal interpretation.

**Drift/calendar proxy.** The principal member is `calendar_week`, together with any policy-period effects it absorbs. It may flag a historical period association and prompt drift investigation. It may not be claimed that a particular week is inherently operationally worse or that the effect will recur. Holiday calendars, policy versions and staffing periods must be modelled explicitly, and validation must use future time periods.

**Unusable until lineage is repaired.** Repairable candidates include `final_handler_team` replaced by `team_at_t0`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot` replaced by documents received at or before `t0`, `risk_score_current` replaced by `risk_score_at_t0`, `last_resource_role` replaced by `resource_role_at_t0`, and extraction-date `employee_experience` replaced by experience at `t0`. Current values may not be claimed as early signals. Some features are irreparable at this prediction time because their concept is inherently future-looking, including total duration, appeal anywhere, total document requests, full trace variant, final decision and future sequence patterns. Repaired versions are new features requiring fresh backfill, training and validation, not a renaming exercise.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

**P0 - Contain the current model.** Freeze the leaked model and its explanations for operational use. Mark the reported probabilities and rankings for Cases X, Y and Z as not credible at `t0`, notify downstream users, and retain the original artefacts only for audit.

**P1 - Rebuild the feature store around prediction time.** Store bitemporal event time and ingestion time, mandatory sequence index, validity intervals for team, role, score and reserve fields, immutable registration-time attributes, and snapshot timestamps for queue data. Every feature definition must state its prediction time and an enforceable rule that feature time is less than or equal to `t0`. Implement as-of joins and reject nightly or extraction-date aggregates that cross `t0`.

**P2 - Recompute prefix-only data and enforce process conformance.** Truncate traces at the first Coverage Check Completed, regenerate features, remove irreparable future features, and introduce repaired features only after point-in-time backfill. Add automated alignment, footprint and declarative checks; enabledness classification at `t0`; same-timestamp permutation tests; repeated-decision occurrence tests; and future-perturbation invariance tests as release gates.

**P3 - Recalibrate after feature removal.** Expect performance to fall once leakage is removed and treat that fall as the honest early-prediction performance. Use time-based training, validation and test splits, recalibrate with Platt or isotonic methods on held-out later data, and report calibration overall and by queue level, complexity, week, region and channel. Reassess decision thresholds and uncertainty rather than preserving the original probabilities.

**P4 - Validate explanation stability.** Recompute global and local explanations only on the prefix-only model. Bootstrap cases and time periods to quantify SHAP-rank and local-contribution stability, test sensitivity to the queue snapshot tolerance, and verify that perturbing post-`t0` events never changes an explanation. Publish explanation cards stating the prediction time, admissible prefix, feature lineage and causal boundary.

**P5 - Complete proxy and fairness analysis.** For region, channel, reserve and prior history, measure calibration, false-positive and false-negative rates by group; investigate mediation through network coverage, language support, submission quality and complexity; and replace proxies with operational variables where feasible. Document whether each retained proxy is used for triage only, and prohibit adverse or causal use without legal and ethical review.

**P6 - Monitor schema and process drift.** Track activity frequencies, trace variants, evidence and appeal loop rates, timestamp-tie rates, sequence-index completeness, feature and queue-snapshot age distributions, policy changes and holiday calendars. Monitor calibration and discrimination over time, alert on schema changes or new process paths, and repeat point-in-time and conformance audits before retraining or releasing updated explanations.
