# 1. Current-process diagnosis

The current warranty process suffers from a severe misalignment between routing logic, compliance requirements, and segment-specific risks. The central contradiction is that the most frequent path—Fast Approval (2,550 cases, median 0.4h from auto-screen)—appears highly efficient and initially 31% cheaper, but creates disproportionate downstream chaos. It produces a 14.1% complaint/reopen rate (compared to 3.3% for repair paths), and resolving these complaints takes an excessive amount of time (Customer complaint -> Manual eligibility review has a mean of 40h and p90 of 100h). Consequently, the actual cost savings of the fast path shrink to merely 9% post-rework, while severely damaging the customer experience and violating compliance policies.

This dynamic is evidenced by at least five specific process-mining artifacts:
1. **Conformance/Alignment Diagnostics:** There are frequent move-on-log deviations showing "Fast approval before Evidence completeness" and "Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices," directly violating Normative Rules R1 and R2.
2. **Customer Segment Outcomes:** Premium high-value devices pushed through the fast path experience a massive 22% complaint/reopen rate, alongside high compliance risk.
3. **Rework Statistics:** The fast path is 31% cheaper before rework but only 9% cheaper after accounting for the costs of complaints, reshipments (EUR 410 error cost for high-value), and service credits.
4. **Performance DFG:** The "Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen" edge has an extreme resolution time (mean 96.0h, p90 180.0h), indicating that when the fast path fails, it fails catastrophically.
5. **Rework/Loop Data:** The "Unclear defect / missing evidence" segment (22% of cases) gets trapped in repetitive loops, with "Request missing evidence" occurring at least twice in 26% of cases, ballooning the median cycle time to 140 hours.

# 2. Redesign A: Segment-Aware Automated Triage

- **Redesigned process flow:** Intake claim -> Segment-Aware Auto Screen -> Route immediately to Fast Approval (Premium Low-Risk), OR Route to Manual/Risk Review (High-Value / EUR >400), OR Route to standard processing.
- **Targets:** The "Auto eligibility screen" activities, specifically focusing on Premium low-risk accessories (18% share) and High-value devices (Premium 14%, Standard 21%).
- **Mechanism of improvement:** By updating the outdated auto-screen rules (a two-week deployment), the system becomes segment-aware. It explicitly guarantees R1 compliance by forcing claims >EUR 400 out of the fast path, while exploiting the R2 exception to accelerate Premium low-risk accessories, ensuring they beat the 24-hour service-credit exposure window (currently 8h median on fast path). 
- **Sacrifices:** This redesign deliberately sacrifices resource utilization and standard case speed. Updating the rules will increase manual-review inflow by 15-35%. Given there are only 7 reviewers and queues grow sharply at >82% utilization, Standard customers will likely experience degraded cycle times in the manual queue to protect Premium SLAs and compliance.

# 3. Redesign B: Pre-Dispatch Quality Gating

- **Redesigned process flow:** Intake claim -> Auto Screen -> Fast Approval -> Pre-Dispatch Quality Gate -> Dispatch replacement.
- **Targets:** The "Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint" loop, specifically targeting high-value devices (where wrong replacements cost EUR 410) and the long-tail temporal deviation of incorrect replacement part selection.
- **Mechanism of improvement:** Inserts a lightweight, 6-18 hour quality gate using the currently underutilized quality inspectors (2 inspectors) before dispatch. By catching incorrect replacement selections proactively, this prevents the downstream 96h mean delay of post-dispatch complaints and avoids the EUR 410 physical error cost, drastically improving the true cost and first-time-right quality.
- **Sacrifices:** Deliberately sacrifices the initial speed illusion of the Fast Approval path. Adding 6-18 hours means the median cycle time for the fast path (currently 1.8h to dispatch) will increase to ~8-20h. It trades upfront speed for backend quality and cost containment.

# 4. Redesign C: Upstream Digital Evidence Portal

- **Redesigned process flow:** Customer Portal (Forced evidence/diagnostics) -> Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen -> (Reduced) Request missing evidence loops -> Technical decision.
- **Targets:** The "Unclear defect / missing evidence" segment (22% share) and the highly repetitive "Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review" cycle (median 26h, mean 34h per loop).
- **Mechanism of improvement:** Deploys a new evidence portal that shifts the burden of data completeness to the customer *before* the claim enters the core workflow. By reducing repeated evidence requests by 30-45%, it dramatically cuts the 140-hour median cycle time for this segment and relieves pressure on the manual eligibility reviewers, who currently waste time chasing customers.
- **Sacrifices:** Deliberately accepts a higher abandonment rate for customers with low digital literacy (currently "Claim abandoned" accounts for 390 endpoints). It sacrifices volume and accessibility for standard customers to protect operational capacity and reduce the sheer length of the evidence-gathering loops.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A (Segment Triage):**
- *Failure mode 1:* The 15-35% increase in manual volume pushes the 7 reviewers permanently over the 82% utilization threshold, causing a queue collapse.
- *Failure mode 2:* Hardcoded rules struggle with edge cases, accidentally sending complex but cheap claims to expensive manual review.
- *Displaced bottleneck:* Moves from downstream rework (Customer complaint) to the upstream Manual Eligibility Review queue.

**Redesign B (Quality Gating):**
- *Failure mode 1:* The 6-18h gate delay accidentally triggers SLA service-credit penalties for Premium customers who expected a next-day replacement.
- *Failure mode 2:* The 2 quality inspectors become overwhelmed during post-marketing campaigns, delaying *all* fast approvals.
- *Displaced bottleneck:* Moves from downstream Escalation Board/Rework to the pre-dispatch Quality Check station.

**Redesign C (Evidence Portal):**
- *Failure mode 1:* The portal UI is too complex, causing massive customer churn and generating negative brand sentiment/escalations.
- *Failure mode 2:* Customers upload low-quality evidence (e.g., blurry photos) to bypass portal gates, meaning the 30-45% reduction in repeat requests never materializes.
- *Displaced bottleneck:* Moves from internal Manual Review to the Customer Intake phase (external bottleneck).

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**First pilot:** 
Roll out **Redesign B (Quality Gating)** restricted solely to *Standard High-value devices*. This avoids jeopardizing Premium SLAs while targeting the segment with the highest financial penalty for wrong dispatches (EUR 410) and a high fast-path reopen rate (18%).

**Metrics to monitor:**
- Fast path complaint/reopen rate (target: reduction from 18% to <5%).
- Quality gate cycle time (target: <12 hours).
- Total reshipment/rework cost per case.

**Stop/go thresholds:**
- *Stop:* If the quality gate adds >18 hours consistently OR if the complaint rate does not drop by at least 50%.
- *Go:* If replacement error costs drop by >60% and cycle time addition remains <12 hours.

**Sequencing logic:**
1. **Stabilize (Redesign B):** Stop the hemorrhaging of EUR 410 errors and 96h rework loops first. By proving the value of doing things right the first time, you buy operational breathing room.
2. **Re-route (Redesign A):** Once downstream quality is stable, update the auto-screen rules to fix compliance (R1) and accelerate Premium Low-Risk (R2). Doing this second prevents the new manual volume from colliding with high rework volume.
3. **Prevent (Redesign C):** Finally, implement the evidence portal to solve the unstructured intake problem, reducing the total volume of work entering the now-stabilized system.

**Learning whether improvements are real (not shifted):**
To ensure bottlenecks aren't just displaced, monitor the End-to-End Cycle Time alongside the Token-based replay fitness. Specifically, watch the "Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close" edge. If this edge remains stable while the "Customer complaint/reopen" edge frequency drops, the improvement is real. If the manual eligibility queue median time spikes above 15 hours, costs have simply shifted from rework to upfront waiting.
