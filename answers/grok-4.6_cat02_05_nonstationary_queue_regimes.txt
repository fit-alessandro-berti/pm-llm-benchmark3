# 1. Data audit and estimands

The center is treated as open **07:00–19:00** (12.0 h = 720 min). No service occurs while closed. Daily `arrivals` and `completed` are **opening-hours counts**, not 24 h counts and not same-case paired flows. Overnight carry-over is possible; a completion need not have arrived that day.

**Queueing quantities and units**

| Symbol / label | Definition used here | Units |
|---|---|---|
| \(\lambda\) | Opening-hours arrival intensity \(=\text{arrivals}/12\) | cases / open-hour |
| \(\mu_{\text{out}}\) | Opening-hours completion intensity \(=\text{completed}/12\) | cases / open-hour |
| \(L\) | `mean_wip`: time-average occupancy from 15-minute snapshots | cases |
| \(S_{\text{med}}, S_{p90}\) | Active handling time **excluding wait** | minutes |
| \(c\) | `staff_fte`: mean concurrent FTE while open | FTE |
| \(\rho_{\text{med}}\) | Offered-load **index** \(=\text{arrivals}\times S_{\text{med}}/(c\times 720)\) | dimensionless |
| \(L/c\) | Occupancy per available FTE | cases / FTE |
| Net flow | \(\text{completed}-\text{arrivals}\) | cases / day |
| Implied \(W_{\text{open}}\) | \(L/\lambda\) or \(L/\mu_{\text{out}}\) **only** in windows judged below | open-hours in system |

\(\rho_{\text{med}}\) is **not** utilization. If service is right-skewed, mean service \(\ge\) median, so \(\rho_{\text{med}}\) understates offered load. Completions per FTE is a throughput-per-capacity descriptor, not a quality metric.

**Assumption A1.** Intensities are opening-hour rates; arrivals and service overnight are taken as zero.

**Assumption A2.** `mean_wip` is not labeled as opening-hours-only versus 24 h. All Little’s-Law arithmetic below is an **opening-hours scenario**: \(L\) is treated as mean occupancy while open, and \(W_{\text{open}}\) is time in system **while the center is open**. Calendar sojourn would add closed nights. If snapshots include the night, \(L\) mixes frozen overnight stock with daytime queues and the same ratio is not an opening-hours sojourn. This ambiguity is a hard limit on the aggregates.

**Assumption A3.** The 07:00 stock is not supplied. The running sum of net flow is a **change** in unfinished work, not the stock. Over 28 days, \(\sum(\text{completed}-\text{arrivals})=-110\); that is a lower bound on how much unfinished work grew if 06 Apr opened empty, and a change of \(-110\) regardless of the unknown opening stock.

**Assumption A4.** Daily medians/p90s are **not** mean flow times and are never substituted for \(W\).

**Assumption A5.** The intervention is dated 29 Apr but not timestamped. That day’s `staff_fte=7.0` is a daily mean, so 29 Apr is a contaminated mixture of pre- and post-implementation hours.

**Assumption A6.** `complex_pct` is an observed label. A real mix shift, a coding change, or both can produce the same column.

**Assumption A7.** Hourly vectors are used as printed. 07 Apr, 15 Apr, and 25 Apr match the daily totals. 21 Apr totals also match (\(115\) arrivals, \(101\) completions), but the 08:00–09:59 share in the vector is not \(47\%\) (see §5). The conflict is treated as a data-quality finding, not forced into agreement.

**At least five threats to naive inference**

1. **Non-stationarity from four different mechanisms**: weekly seasonality, a 180-minute outage plus recovery, a step change in complex mix and morning peak share around 20 Apr, and a staffing intervention from 29 Apr. Pooling them estimates a day that never occurs.
2. **Unobserved boundaries and carry-over.** 07:00 backlog is missing; completions are not same-day arrivals; WIP can sit frozen 12 h overnight. Daily balance equations are incomplete.
3. **Wrong time quantities.** Median/p90 service exclude waiting. Mean flow time is not in the table. Using \(S_{\text{med}}\) as \(W\) understates sojourn by a factor of several in every plausible window.
4. **Snapshot and sampling design.** Fifteen-minute means can miss intra-bin spikes. Whether nights are included is unknown. `staff_fte` is a daily mean and does not have to be present at the 08–10 peak.
5. **Days are not i.i.d.** Adjacent weekdays move together; WIP and net flow run for a week at a time; weekends are a different level. Standard errors that assume 28 independent days are too small.
6. **Selection in the WIP.** `complex_pct` is a share of **arrivals**. If complex cases linger, they are over-represented in \(L\) relative to arrivals (a stock–flow mismatch).
7. **Recovery is not capacity.** Days with `completed ≫ arrivals` after the outage are backlog liquidation, not a new stationary throughput.
8. **Label uncertainty.** The mix jump may be coding. If it is only a label change, service-time and WIP increases still happened; the causal story changes, the operational deterioration does not.

**Outage and recovery treatment.** R3 is a **freeze of servers, not of demand**. About \(17+11+9=37\) arrivals land in the three zero-completion hours. R4 then completes \(30\) more cases than it arrives over two days, while \(L\) falls \(52\to 35\). That does not close the \(-47\) hole (and weekend 18–19 Apr is only about balanced). Treating 16–17 Apr as “the system can do \(120+\) completions per day” confuses a drain-down of stored work with a sustainable arrival-matching rate. Extra FTE in R4 is a **shock-recovery control**, then it is withdrawn (20 Apr is back to \(6.0\)) just as the mix step arrives — a plausible contributor to the following week’s instability, not proof that \(6\) FTE is enough.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

A serious alternative is a **three-epoch calendar** that keeps weekly seasonality inside each epoch instead of crossing day-type with epoch:

| Epoch | Dates | Operational story |
|---|---|---|
| **E1 Pre-shock system** | 06–14 Apr (weekdays + weekends) | A functioning weekly cycle: ~100 weekday arrivals at 6 FTE and ~40 weekend arrivals at 2.5 FTE; locally balanced; short service; moderate WIP |
| **E2 Uncontrolled shock + new mix** | 15–28 Apr | One causal soup: outage, recovery staffing, then a persistent mix/peak-share shift with the old weekday roster. Backlog accumulates on weekdays and is not cleared on weekends |
| **E3 Staffed response under the new mix** | 29 Apr–03 May | +~1 weekday FTE and +0.5 weekend FTE; outflow exceeds inflow on weekdays; WIP slopes down but remains above E1 |

This is the executive narrative: “stable → broken → being repaired.” It is better if the decision is “which fortnight are we in?” and worse if the decision is “how many heads on a Wednesday versus a Saturday, and was the outage the same problem as the mix?”

**Compared with the primary cut**

- **Strengths of E1–E3.** Parsimony; matches how a manager dates an incident and an intervention; does not pretend 25–26 Apr is a precise standalone regime (\(n=2\)).
- **Weaknesses.** E2 **confounds three mechanisms** (capacity shock, mix/peak change, leftover inventory). Pooled E2 rates are almost uninterpretable: 16 Apr’s \(129\) completions sit next to 15 Apr’s \(58\) and 22 Apr’s \(103\). Weekends inside E2 pull \(\lambda\) down and hide that weekday outflow never rose with weekday inflow. E3 is only five days and still includes the untimed intervention start.
- **Strengths of the primary cut.** Separates demand (weekend vs weekday, burst vs ordinary), mix, capacity shock, and control action. Shows the central fact that **R5 outflow \(\approx\) R1 outflow** while offered work and \(L\) jump.
- **Weaknesses of the primary cut.** Eight labels on 28 serially dependent days; R6/R8 are thin; 18–19 Apr could be argued into a “post-outage residual” bin; 13–14 Apr already show slight creep (WIP \(21\)–\(23\), complex \(22\)–\(24\%\)).

A second alternative, not tabulated at length, is **flow-balance state** rather than calendar: *balanced* (\(|\text{net}|\) small and \(L\) flat), *accumulating* (R3, R5, R6), *recovering* (R4, R7). That is the right frame for control policy, but it **lags** the mix change (the mix jumps on 20 Apr; the worst inventory is 27–28 Apr) and would mis-classify a well-staffed high-WIP drain as “healthy high throughput.”

The primary segmentation is preferred for diagnosis; E1–E3 is preferred for a board timeline; flow-balance is preferred for daily control. None is ground truth.

# 4. Pooled versus segmented statistics

All 28 days, equal-day weights: mean arrivals \(90.0\), mean completions \(86.0\), mean \(L=29.9\), mean \(c=5.14\), mean of daily \(S_{\text{med}}=31.0\), arrival-weighted complex share \(\approx 33.8\%\), net \(-110\) cases. Opening-hours \(\lambda_{\text{pool}}=7.50\) / h. The ratio \(29.9/7.50=4.0\) h is a **descriptive mix of incompatible windows**, not a sojourn (see §6).

**Way 1 — Weekday/weekend composition**

Weekends are \(8/28=28.6\%\) of days but only \(359/2519=14.3\%\) of arrivals. Equal-day pooling describes a fictional \(90\)-arrival day.

| Slice | Days | Mean arrivals | Mean completions | Mean \(L\) | Mean \(c\) | \(\rho_{\text{med}}\) (from slice means) | \(L/c\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Pooled, all days | 28 | 90.0 | 86.0 | 29.9 | 5.14 | 0.75 | 5.8 |
| All weekdays | 20 | 108.0 | 103.2 | 34.8 | 6.20 | 0.74 | 5.6 |
| All weekends | 8 | 44.9 | 43.3 | 17.4 | 2.62 | 0.78 | 6.6 |
| R1 only | 7 | 100.1 | 99.6 | 20.3 | 5.93 | 0.59 | 3.4 |
| R5 only | 7 | 114.0 | 100.6 | 44.3 | 5.93 | 0.93 | 7.5 |

The all-weekday row still looks “fine” (\(\rho_{\text{med}}\approx 0.74\)) because it **averages R1, the outage, recovery, R5, and R7**. That is a second composition bias on top of the weekend mix. A model fitted to \(90\) arrivals and \(5.1\) FTE has the wrong \(\lambda\), the wrong \(c\), and the wrong stability conclusion for the days that carry \(86\%\) of volume.

Weekend \(L/c\) is **higher** than baseline weekday \(L/c\) even before the mix shift. Pooling “low weekend volume = slack” is the wrong sign for pressure per head.

**Way 2 — Complex-case mix (and the service-time step)**

Arrival-weighted complex share is \(\approx 21\%\) in R1–R2, \(25\)–\(27\%\) through outage/recovery, then \(\approx 44\%\) from 20 Apr onward. The pooled \(34\%\) is a number that appears on **no** day after 06 Apr (observed daily values sit in \(18\)–\(27\) then \(39\)–\(47\)).

| Window | Complex (arr.-wtd.) | Daily \(S_{\text{med}}\) | Daily \(S_{p90}\) |
|---|---:|---|---|
| R1 | \(\approx 20.9\%\) | \(24\)–\(26\) | \(44\)–\(50\) |
| R5 | \(\approx 44.6\%\) | \(34\)–\(36\) | \(78\)–\(88\) |
| Pooled | \(\approx 33.8\%\) | daily values \(24\)–\(36\); mean \(31\) | daily values \(44\)–\(88\); mean \(\approx 67\) |

A single service distribution centered near \(31\) / \(67\) minutes is a **mixture of two regimes**, not the process a case actually draws from. If the label jump is only a coding change (A6), the service and WIP step still forbids pooling; only the causal attribution changes.

**Way 3 — Outage/recovery and the staffing intervention**

Pooled completion intensity (\(86\) / day) sits between a collapsed day (\(58\)) and recovery days (\(129\), \(112\)) and later intervention days (\(121\), \(126\), \(116\)). The pool cannot see that:

- R1 and R5 have almost the **same** weekday completions (\(\approx 100\)) and the same completions/FTE (\(\approx 16.8\) vs \(17.0\)).
- The deterioration is therefore **not** “staff got slower at completing work” in the throughput-per-FTE sense; it is more arrivals, longer handling, and a fatter tail against an unchanged outflow cap.
- R4’s high completions coincide with the **highest** \(L\) in the file (\(52\)). High \(\mu_{\text{out}}\) here is inventory liquidation after a freeze.
- R7’s benefit is a **slope**: \(L\) \(43\to 32\) and net \(+6\) / day, not a comparison of R7’s mean \(L=37\) with the pooled \(L=29.9\). Against the pool, the intervention still looks “worse than average.” Against R5, it is the first weekday drain in a fortnight.

**Simpson-like reversals the pool creates**

1. **Volume vs delay.** R2 has far fewer arrivals than R1 but longer implied opening-hours sojourn and higher \(L/c\). A pooled “higher \(\lambda\) \(\Rightarrow\) higher wait” story has the wrong sign across day-type.
2. **Productivity vs congestion.** Completions per FTE are flat R1→R5 while \(L\) more than doubles. A pooled productivity dashboard looks stable; the queue is not.
3. **Recovery vs capability.** The best completion counts are R4 (and then R7). Using them as proof of spare capacity justifies removing the extra FTE on 20 Apr — after which R5 accumulates \(\approx 13\) cases/day.
4. **Intervention vs the average.** R7 mean \(L=37 > 29.9\) pooled, so a before/after against the grand mean says the intervention failed. A within-mix before/after says it reversed the weekday random walk.
5. **Dispersion.** Within R1 or R5, daily arrival Fano factors are \(\approx 0.23\)–\(0.25\) (under-dispersed). Pooled daily arrivals have Fano \(\approx 9.7\). Almost all “overdispersion” is **mixture**, not a wild Poisson process (see §5).

Robust takeaway: use **ranges and regime medians**, not a 28-day mean, for any parameter that enters a queueing model.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

**Hourly vectors (bins 07–08, …, 18–19)**

Population Fano \(=\mathrm{Var}_n/\mathrm{mean}\) and \(\mathrm{CV}=\mathrm{sd}_n/\mathrm{mean}\) on the 12 hourly counts:

| Day | Role | Arrivals mean | Arr. Fano | Arr. CV | Completions Fano | Completions CV |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 07 Apr | Ordinary weekday | 8.42 | 2.96 | 0.59 | 1.56 | 0.43 |
| 15 Apr | Outage (arrivals still ordinary-shaped) | 8.75 | 3.85 | 0.66 | 1.54* | 0.56* |
| 21 Apr | Bursty mix-shift weekday | 9.58 | 9.28 | 0.98 | 0.90 | 0.31 |
| 25 Apr | Weekend | 4.25 | 1.18 | 0.53 | 0.70 | 0.41 |

\*15 Apr completion moments are not a stochastic model: three structural zeros.

Observations:

- R1-like demand is already **over-dispersed relative to Poisson** (Fano \(\approx 3\)), with a unimodal morning peak (12+20 in 09–11 on 07 Apr; \(32/101\approx 32\%\), consistent with `peak_08_10_pct=31`).
- 21 Apr is a different **intraday law**: spikes \(34\), \(24\), \(14\) in non-adjacent hours. Fano \(\approx 9\). Completions stay smooth (Fano \(<1\)) — a capacity cap acting as a low-pass filter. Hourly net flow on 21 Apr has a \(+21\) hour and a pathwise peak of \(+27\) from that day’s flow alone (plus whatever opened at 07:00). Six FTE cannot absorb a \(34\)-arrival hour when handling times are ~half an hour (a hard bound on completions is on the order of \(10\)–\(12\) / h even if fully busy).
- 25 Apr is near-Poisson in the hourly arrival Fano, unimodal, and low. Weekend smoothness plus weekday burstiness is another reason not to pool.
- 15 Apr **arrivals** look like an ordinary weekday; the failure is completion zeros. A demand-only burstiness model misses the outage entirely.

**Inconsistency (observation, not a fix).** `peak_08_10_pct=47` on 21 Apr implies \(\approx 54\) arrivals in 08:00–09:59. The printed vector’s 08–09 and 09–10 bins are \(3+8=11\). Totals match the daily table; the 08–10 share does not. Possible readings: the vector is a shape cartoon, the peak column uses another clock, or there is a recording error. Burstiness conclusions use the vector’s actual spikes; the \(47\%\) column is still evidence that morning concentration rose from ~\(30\%\) (R1) to ~\(40\)–\(50\%\) (R5).

**Daily sequence**

- **Within-regime daily arrivals are under-dispersed** (R1 Fano \(\approx 0.23\); R5 Fano \(\approx 0.25\)). Day-to-day volume is not the random driver. **Between-regime mixture** produces pooled Fano \(\approx 9.7\).
- **Weekly seasonality** (weekday ~100–120, weekend ~36–53) induces strong lag-1 autocorrelation if weekends are left in the series.
- **Trend and runs.** Weekday arrivals climb across the four weeks. After 20 Apr there are **seven consecutive weekday deficits** of \(-10\) to \(-16\), then three surpluses under R7. WIP on weekdays is locally persistent (\(18\)–\(23\), then a spike \(47\)–\(52\), then a climb \(37\to 50\), then a descent \(43\to 32\)). That is serial dependence from backlog state, not white noise.
- **Change points (heuristic, A9, not ground truth).** A two-mean split that maximizes the two-piece between-group sum of squares on `complex_pct` lands at **20 Apr**. The same split on `median_service_min` agrees. `outage_min` isolates **15 Apr**. A sign change in weekday net flow plus a step in `staff_fte` isolates **29 Apr**. `mean_wip` jumps twice (15 Apr and 20 Apr) and should not be used as a single-break statistic. Four weeks cannot separate a one-time Monday policy/coding change from the start of a seasonal month.

**Why four hourly days cannot specify a stochastic model**

They are one ordinary weekday, one outage, one bursty mix-shift weekday, and one weekend — i.e., **one draw from each of four non-exchangeable conditions**, not a sample from a single arrival process. There is no information on day-to-day variation of the hourly profile, on the distribution of spike timing, on the 07:00 stock process, or on residual service after close. Fitting \(M/M/c\), \(M/G/c\), or a single hyperexponential arrival law to the pooled \(90\) / day would encode the wrong Fano, the wrong peak, the wrong \(c(t)\), and the wrong service tail.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little’s Law relates **time-average occupancy**, an **arrival (or throughput) rate on the same clock**, and **mean sojourn on that clock**, for a window whose boundary stocks are comparable and whose internal process is close enough to stationary that the ratio is not dominated by transients. Median service is never \(W\). Completions are the better throughput proxy only when the window is about balanced **and** one wants the sojourn of **leaving** work; arrivals are the proxy for sojourn of **entering** work. When stocks trend, the two ratios diverge and neither is a mean sojourn.

**Assumption A2 repeated.** Calculations use opening-hours \(\lambda=\text{arrivals}/12\) or \(\mu_{\text{out}}=\text{completed}/12\) and the supplied daily \(L\). If snapshots include nights, every \(W_{\text{open}}\) below is mis-specified.

| R4 16–17 Apr recovery | \(L\) \(52\to 35\); large boundary mismatch | Completions count old cases; \(\mu_{\text{out}}>\lambda\) | Daily means are not the same system | The point of the window is carry-over burn-down | Transient control action | Not computed as a sojourn. \(L/\lambda\approx 5.0\) h and \(L/\mu_{\text{out}}\approx 4.3\) h are **descriptive ratios only** | **Not defensible** |
| R5 mix-shift weekdays | \(L\) \(37\to 50\); net \(-94\) in 7 days | Arrivals and completions **disagree** by \(\approx 1.1\) / h | Rising level: a single \(L\) is a path average, not an equilibrium | High and growing; complex cases likely selected into the stock | Clear non-stationarity | Not computed as a sojourn. For magnitude only: \(L/\lambda\approx 4.7\) h vs \(L/\mu_{\text{out}}\approx 5.3\) h | **Not defensible** as \(W\); the two ratios are a **bound sketch** that delay is several hours, not \(35\) min |
| R6, R8 (2-day weekends) | Friday spillover; \(n=2\) | Fragile | Thin | High | Insufficient | Not computed | **Not defensible** |
| R7 intervention weekdays | \(L\) \(43\to 32\); intervention mid-window on 29 Apr | \(\mu_{\text{out}}>\lambda\) by design | Falling level | Drain of R5 stock | Transient; contaminated first day | Not computed as a sojourn | **Not defensible** |
| Pooled 28 days | Mixes every row above; net \(-110\) | Neither proxy represents a single process | \(L\) is a mixture (outage spike + two levels + weekends) | Dominant | Multiple change points | \(29.9/7.50=4.0\) h is **not** a sojourn | **Not defensible** |
| Single day 07 Apr (hourly counts, no hourly \(L\)) | Intraday inventory builds then drains (hour-end path from that day’s flow: about \(-3\) to \(+6\) if start were 0) | Hourly arr \(\neq\) hourly comp | No hourly WIP supplied; daily \(L=20\) is not hour-specific | Unknown 07:00 stock | Intraday non-stationary | Cannot form \(L(t)/\lambda(t)\) | **Not defensible** |

**What can be said without pretending to have \(W\).** In every window where \(L/\lambda\) is even roughly meaningful, it is **several times** \(S_{\text{med}}\) (R1: \(\approx 140\) min vs \(25\) min median service). That gap is waiting and overnight-open-hour exposure, not “the median is the mean.” Substituting \(S_{\text{med}}\) for flow time is not a conservative approximation; it is a different estimand.

# 7. Operational implications

**Staffing.** Roster to **R1 vs R2 vs R5/R7**, not to the pooled \(90\) arrivals and \(5.1\) FTE. R1 was approximately balanced at \(\approx 6\) weekday FTE under the old mix. R5 keeps that roster while \(\lambda\) rises \(\approx 14\%\) and median handling rises \(\approx 40\%\); \(\rho_{\text{med}}\) moves \(0.59\to 0.93\) and weekday inventory grows \(\approx 13\) / day. Completions per FTE do not diagnose this. R7 shows that \(\approx +1\) weekday FTE (to \(\sim 7\)) plus a slightly shorter tail is enough to **flip the sign** of net flow, not enough in three days to return \(L\) to \(20\). Weekend pressure per FTE was already higher than R1; the move \(2.5\to 3.0\) in R8 is directionally right. Do not staff the 08–10 bin to the daily-mean hourly rate: on a 21 Apr-like day the peak hour is a large multiple of the 12 h mean.

**Backlog recovery.** After R3, two days of raised FTE (R4) burned about \(30\) of the day’s \(-47\) hole and left \(L\) at \(35\) — still far above R1 — then extra FTE was removed the morning the mix step appears. That sequencing is a plausible amplifier. From the end of R5 the file is about \(110\) cases behind a zero-start counterfactual, and R7 drains \(\approx 6\) weekday cases/day. **Hypothesis (not observed):** if R7’s surplus persists and arrivals stay in the \(115\) band, several further weekdays of the higher roster are required; weekends at \(3.0\) FTE only hold the line. Recovery staffing should be **time-limited and WIP-triggered**, and should not be judged on completion counts alone.

**Peak smoothing.** The mix epoch is not only “more work” but **more simultaneous work**. `peak_08_10_pct` moves from \(\sim 30\%\) to \(\sim 40\)–\(50\%\), and the 21 Apr vector adds secondary spikes. With \(S_{\text{med}}\approx 35\) min, a \(30+\) arrival hour is an inventory event no 6 FTE pool can clear inside the hour. Operationally: appointment or callback windows, stagger of complex work, a short overflow roster 08–11, and a rule that complex variants are not all released at 09:00. Smoothing **daily** totals (pushing volume to Saturday) does not fix an 09:00 spike.

**Data collection.** The aggregates are insufficient for a queueing-law claim in every window except a cautious R1/R2 opening-hours scenario. Collect: (i) 07:00 and 19:00 census WIP every day; (ii) an event log with case id, start/wait/end timestamps, variant, and resource; (iii) the intervention clock time and who was added; (iv) a note on whether `complex_pct` is a coding change; (v) mean as well as median/p90 **service** and a true mean **sojourn**; (vi) hourly WIP, not only hourly counts; (vii) whether snapshots run overnight. Until then, do not publish a single \(W\).

**Policy that looks beneficial on pooled averages and risky when segmented.**  
*Hold weekday FTE at \(6.0\) (or staff to the pooled \(90\) arrivals / \(5.1\) FTE) because pooled \(\rho_{\text{med}}\approx 0.75\), pooled completions per FTE \(\approx 17\) are stable, and 16 Apr demonstrated \(129\) completions with \(7\) FTE so the system “has headroom.”*  

Under the primary segmentation that policy is the R5 failure mode: outflow is already capped near \(100\) / weekday, the new mix and the morning burst make \(\rho_{\text{med}}\) approach \(1\) on a **median** (optimistic) load index, and inventory follows a weekday random walk of about \(-13\) / day. The 16 Apr headroom is leftover work after a freeze, not a sustainable R5 capacity. The same pooled view also makes R7 look unnecessary because R7’s mean \(L\) still exceeds the grand mean. The segmented view says the extra FTE is the first control that stops the walk.

A related risk is an SLA written at the pooled \(4\) h implied sojourn: R1 volume would look as if it failed a tight SLA or passed a loose one for the wrong reason, while R5’s several-hour delay — where most arrivals sit after 20 Apr — is averaged away.

def lag1_acf(x):
    x = np.asarray(x, dtype=float)
    x = x - x.mean()
    if np.dot(x, x) == 0:
        return np.nan
    return float(np.dot(x[:-1], x[1:]) / np.dot(x, x))

# Transparent heuristic: maximize two-piece between-SS on a series
def best_mean_split(values, dates):
    x = np.asarray(values, dtype=float)
    n = len(x)
    best = None
    for i in range(2, n - 1):  # split so first i days | last n-i
        left, right = x[:i], x[i:]
        score = i * (n - i) / n * (left.mean() - right.mean()) ** 2
        rec = (score, dates.iloc[i], i, left.mean(), right.mean())
        if best is None or score > best[0]:
            best = rec
    return best
