# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

## 1.1 Process Architecture and Demand Assumptions
The technical incident resolution process follows a strict three-stage workflow:

$$\text{Intake} \longrightarrow \text{Diagnostic Work} \longrightarrow \text{Validation}$$

All cases must undergo **Intake** upon arrival and conclude with **Validation** before closure. The diagnostic phase is partitioned by technical domain based on case type:
- **Case Type M (Mechanical):** Requires a single Mechanical diagnostic task.
- **Case Type E (Electrical):** Requires a single Electrical diagnostic task.
- **Case Type S (Software):** Requires a single Software diagnostic task.
- **Case Type ME (Mechanical & Electrical):** Requires both Mechanical and Electrical diagnostic tasks. These branches may execute in parallel on distinct resources or sequentially on a single cross-skilled resource.
- **Case Type ES (Electrical & Software):** Requires both Electrical and Software diagnostic tasks, with the same parallel versus sequential architectural choice.

### Service-Time Distributions and Bound Derivations
Service durations before resource speed multipliers follow a two-component mixture distribution: an ordinary Uniform distribution $U(a_{\text{ord}}, b_{\text{ord}})$ with probability $(1 - p_{\text{tail}})$, and a complex-tail Uniform distribution $U(a_{\text{tail}}, b_{\text{tail}})$ with probability $p_{\text{tail}}$.

$$\mathbb{E}[S_a] = (1 - p_{\text{tail}, a}) \cdot \frac{a_{\text{ord}} + b_{\text{ord}}}{2} + p_{\text{tail}, a} \cdot \frac{a_{\text{tail}} + b_{\text{tail}}}{2}$$

$$\mathbb{V}\text{ar}(S_a) = (1 - p_{\text{tail}, a}) \left[ \frac{(b_{\text{ord}} - a_{\text{ord}})^2}{12} + \mu_{\text{ord}, a}^2 \right] + p_{\text{tail}, a} \left[ \frac{(b_{\text{tail}} - a_{\text{tail}})^2}{12} + \mu_{\text{tail}, a}^2 \right] - (\mathbb{E}[S_a])^2$$

| Activity ($a$) | Ordinary Range (min) | Tail Range (min) | $p_{\text{tail}}$ | Base Expected Duration $\mathbb{E}[S_a]$ | Theoretical Bounds $[S_{\min}, S_{\max}]$ | Tail Expectation $\mathbb{E}[S_{\text{tail}}]$ |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Intake** | $[5, 8]$ (mean 6.5) | $[10, 14]$ (mean 12.0) | 0.08 | **6.94 min** | $[5.0, 14.0]$ min | 12.00 min |
| **Mechanical** | $[18, 28]$ (mean 23.0) | $[35, 55]$ (mean 45.0) | 0.15 | **26.30 min** | $[18.0, 55.0]$ min | 45.00 min |
| **Electrical** | $[22, 35]$ (mean 28.5) | $[40, 65]$ (mean 52.5) | 0.18 | **32.82 min** | $[22.0, 65.0]$ min | 52.50 min |
| **Software** | $[25, 40]$ (mean 32.5) | $[45, 80]$ (mean 62.5) | 0.20 | **38.50 min** | $[25.0, 80.0]$ min | 62.50 min |
| **Validation** | $[10, 18]$ (mean 14.0) | $[22, 35]$ (mean 28.5) | 0.12 | **15.74 min** | $[10.0, 35.0]$ min | 28.50 min |

Each resource $r \in \mathcal{R} = \{\text{Ada}, \text{Ben}, \text{Chen}, \text{Deepa}, \text{Emil}, \text{Farah}\}$ has an activity speed multiplier $\mu_{r, a} \ge 1.00$. The actual active processing time is $T_{r, a} = \mu_{r, a} \cdot S_a$. Multiplier '-' denotes lack of skill qualification.

### Coordination, Handover, and Setup Overhead Rules
1. **Handover Penalty ($H = 7\text{ min}$):** Incurred when consecutive activities of a case change resource (e.g., Intake by Ada $\to$ Mechanical by Ben).
2. **Cross-Team Penalty ($C = 5\text{ min}$):** Incurred if a case transitions across the team boundary between the Mechanical team and the Electrical/Software team, unless retained by the same cross-skilled resource.
3. **Skill Setup Time ($\Delta = 5\text{ min}$):** Incurred when a single resource switches diagnostic skill family between consecutive cases (e.g., Deepa finishing Mechanical and immediately starting Electrical).
4. **Parallel Synchronization Coordination ($\Gamma = 8\text{ min}$):** Incurred before Validation when ME or ES branches execute in parallel across two distinct resources. Sequential execution on a single cross-skilled resource completely avoids this 8-minute delay, as well as the 7-minute handover and 5-minute cross-team penalties.
5. **Preemption and Restart Penalty:** P1 cases may preempt active P2 or P3 diagnostic tasks. An interrupted task loses its current progress and incurs a restart penalty equal to $10\%$ of its remaining active service time:
   $$T_{\text{remaining}}^{\text{new}} = 1.10 \times T_{\text{remaining}}^{\text{prior}}$$
   A case may be preempted at most once.
6. **Operational Limits:** Overtime is bounded at 30 minutes per resource. Planned individual resource utilization exceeding $90\%$ is classified as operationally fragile and unfair.

### Categorization of Analytical Statements
- **[Observation]:** Directly established from the prompt facts, tables, and rules.
- **[Estimate]:** Expected value or probability computed under stochastic distributions.
- **[Bound]:** Strict mathematical maximum or minimum based on support limits and capacity constraints.
- **[Hypothesis]:** Theoretical behavioral or queuing assumption subjected to simulation testing.
- **[Recommendation]:** Actionable operational policy proposed for implementation.

## 1.2 Decision Variables and Policy Levers
The routing and dispatching system controls four primary decision variables:
1. **Resource Assignment Tensor $x_{c, a, r} \in \{0, 1\}$:** Indicates whether activity $a$ of case $c$ is assigned to resource $r$, subject to qualification $\mu_{r, a} \ne \text{'—'}$.
2. **Branching Strategy $\theta_c \in \{\text{Parallel}, \text{Sequential}\}$:** For composite cases $c \in \text{ME} \cup \text{ES}$, determines whether diagnostic branches are dispatched concurrently to two separate resources or chained sequentially on a single cross-skilled technician.
3. **Dynamic Priority and Queue Sequencing $\pi_c(t)$:** Real-time priority score assigned to ready task $(c, a)$ at time $t$.
4. **Preemption Trigger Function $\phi(c_{\text{active}}, c_{\text{queued}}) \in \{0, 1\}$:** Binary rule triggering preemption of an active P2/P3 diagnostic activity when a P1 case enters the queue.
5. **Cross-Skill Overflow Threshold $\tau_{\text{overflow}}$:** The queue wait threshold after which secondary skill resources are unlocked.

## 1.3 Formal Objective Formulations

### Objective 1: Tail-Risk and SLA Penalty Minimization (Service-Centric)
Designed to minimize financial and operational SLA breaches while penalizing extreme queue delays:

$$\min Z_{\text{SLA}} = w_1 \mathbb{P}(D_c > 120 \mid c \in \text{P1}) + w_2 \mathbb{P}(D_c > 480 \mid c \in \text{P2}) + w_3 \mathbb{P}(D_c > 1440 \mid c \in \text{P3}) + \lambda P_{90}(D) + \kappa \mathbb{E}[D]$$

where $D_c$ is the total elapsed throughput time of case $c$, $w_1 = 100$, $w_2 = 10$, $w_3 = 1$, $\lambda = 0.5$, and $\kappa = 0.1$.

### Objective 2: Minimax Robustness, Workload Fairness, and Stability (Workforce-Centric)
Designed to ensure resilience against worst-case operational shocks while enforcing workload equity and limiting burnout:

$$\min Z_{\text{Robust}} = \max_{s \in \mathcal{S}} \left[ \sum_{p \in \{\text{P1, P2, P3}\}} w_p \left(1 - \text{SLA}_{p, s}\right) + \alpha \cdot \text{Gini}(\mathbf{u}_s) + \beta \cdot \max_{r \in \mathcal{R}} (u_{r, s} - 0.90)^+ + \gamma \cdot \frac{\text{Overhead}_s}{\text{Total Work}_s} \right]$$

where $\mathcal{S} = \{\text{Base, U1, U2, U3, U4, U5}\}$, $\mathbf{u}_s = (u_{\text{Ada}, s}, \dots, u_{\text{Farah}, s})$ is the vector of resource utilizations in scenario $s$, $\text{Gini}(\mathbf{u})$ is the Gini inequality coefficient, $(x)^+ = \max(0, x)$, and $\text{Overhead}_s = \text{Handover}_s + \text{CrossTeam}_s + \text{Setup}_s + \text{Coord}_s + \text{PreemptionLoss}_s$.

### Base Scenario Vulnerabilities
1. **The 16:00–20:00 Capacity Cliff:** At 16:00, Ada, Ben, and Chen depart. Supply collapses from 570 min to 210 min (16:00–18:00) and 120 min (18:00–20:00). Deepa and Emil carry the entire shop.

Any M or ME case arriving after 18:00 (expected $0.25 \times 3 + 0.10 \times 3 = 1.05\text{ cases}$) cannot receive diagnostic service until 08:00 the following morning (14 elapsed hours later). While P3 SLA (24h) absorbs this smoothly, any P1/P2 arrival with Mechanical needs in this window breaches SLA unless serviced before 18:00.

### Scenario U1: Morning Surge (08:00–12:00 arrivals $\times 1.7$, P1 $+8\%$)
- Morning arrivals increase from 16 to 27.2 cases. Morning offered work jumps to $1,752\text{ min}$ against $1,080\text{ min}$ of available morning labor.
- P1 arrival volume rises from 1.05 to 5.56 cases. Because P1 cases must complete within 120 minutes, preemption of active P2/P3 diagnostic tasks accelerates, introducing restart penalties and cascading delays into the afternoon.

### Scenario U2: Electrical/Software Incident (E & ES double 12:00–16:00)
- In the 12:00–16:00 window, Electrical offered work expands by $42\%$, and Software expands by $28\%$.
- Bottleneck shifts entirely to Chen, Deepa, and Emil. Ada is pulled into Electrical work at a high multiplier ($\mu_{\text{Ada}, E} = 1.35$), incurring setup penalties and inflating total busy time.

### Scenario U3: Absence of Chen
- Chen’s full-day absence eliminates 450 minutes of prime Electrical ($\mu=1.00$) and Software ($\mu=1.25$) capacity.
- In 08:00–12:00, Farah ($\mu=1.40$, part-time) is the *only* Software-qualified worker in the building. Emil does not arrive until 12:00. Any Software backlog generated before 12:00 overwhelms Farah and spills into Emil’s shift, saturating Emil through 20:00.

### Scenario U4: Long-Tail Day (Diagnostic tail probabilities double)
- $p_{\text{tail}}$ shifts: Mechanical ($0.15 \to 0.30$), Electrical ($0.18 \to 0.36$), Software ($0.20 \to 0.40$).

Total system workload rises by $421.5\text{ min}$. Variability expands substantially, causing queue variance explosions under FIFO dispatching.

### Scenario U5: Combined Compound Stress (U1 Surge + U3 Absence + 09:30–10:00 IT Outage)
- The catastrophic scenario: $1.7\times$ morning surge, missing primary technician (Chen), and a 30-minute freeze on Intake from 09:30–10:00.
- At 10:00, an accumulated wave of un-intaken cases floods the remaining staff. Farah is overwhelmed on Software, Ada and Ben are jammed on Intake/Mechanical, and Deepa arrives at 10:00 to an immediate severe backlog.

# 3. Candidate policies

```
Policy Operational Architecture Overview:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Policy 1: Strict Specialization  | Fixed skill queues; dedicated primary roles; parallel ME/ES;
                                 | no cross-skilling; zero preemption overhead.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Policy 2: Full Dynamic Pooling   | Global priority queue (P1>P2>P3); greedy fastest-available;
                                 | universal parallel ME/ES; aggressive P1 preemption.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Policy 3: Robust SLA-Aware Hybrid| Dynamic P1 reservation; controlled overflow (tau=20 min);
                                 | smart sequential ME/ES; case stickiness; skill chaining.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
```

## 3.1 Policy 1: Strict Specialization (Fixed Queues)
- **Eligibility and Role Dedication:**
  - *Ben:* Dedicated Mechanical specialist ($\mu=1.00$).
  - *Ada:* Primary Mechanical ($\mu=1.00$), secondary Intake/Validation.
  - *Chen:* Primary Electrical ($\mu=1.00$), secondary Software ($\mu=1.25$).
  - *Deepa:* Primary Electrical ($\mu=1.00$), secondary Mechanical ($\mu=1.25$), Validation.
  - *Emil:* Primary Software ($\mu=1.00$), secondary Electrical ($\mu=1.30$), Validation.
  - *Farah:* Intake and morning support.
- **Queue Structure:** Four separate skill queues (Intake, Mechanical, Electrical, Software, Validation). Cases enter only the designated queue.
- **Parallelization:** ME and ES cases are unconditionally split in parallel into separate branches. Mechanical to Ben/Ada, Electrical to Chen/Deepa, Software to Emil/Chen.
- **Preemption & Cross-Skilling:** Zero cross-skilling across domain boundaries. Preemption is permitted only within the dedicated skill queue for P1 cases.
- **Failure Mode:** Extreme queue imbalances. If Software demand surges, Software cases sit in queue while Mechanical specialists sit idle. Catastrophic collapse under Chen’s absence (U3/U5).

---

## 3.2 Policy 2: Full Dynamic Pooling (Fastest Available / Global FIFO)
- **Eligibility:** Any idle resource qualified for an activity ($\mu_{r, a} \ne \text{'—'}$) is eligible.
- **Dispatching Rule:** Global priority ordering: P1 $>$ P2 $>$ P3. Within the same priority class, First-In-First-Out (FIFO) by case arrival time.
- **Tie-Breaking:** If multiple idle resources are qualified for a ready task, dispatch to the resource with the lowest multiplier $\mu_{r, a}$. If tied, dispatch to the longest-idle resource.
- **Parallelization:** Always split ME and ES cases into parallel branches immediately upon Intake completion.
- **Preemption Protocol:** When a P1 case arrives and all qualified resources are busy, preempt the active P2 or P3 diagnostic task having the lowest remaining service time. The preempted case incurs a $10\%$ penalty on remaining service time and returns to the top of the queue.
- **Failure Mode:** Excessive context switching, high handover overhead ($7\text{ min} + 5\text{ min}$), high setup churn ($5\text{ min}$), and speed degradation from assigning slow generalists (Farah $\mu=1.40$, Ada on Elec $\mu=1.35$).

---

## 3.3 Policy 3: Robust SLA-Aware Hybrid Policy (Recommended)
Policy 3 synthesizes four coordinated dispatching mechanics designed to maximize SLA attainment while minimizing coordination friction.

### 1. Dynamic Priority Scoring with Anti-Starvation Age Acceleration
Each ready task $(c, a)$ is assigned a dynamic score $\Pi(c, a, r, t)$ when evaluating candidate resource $r$ at time $t$:

$$\Pi(c, a, r, t) = W_{\text{prio}}(c) + \text{WaitTime}(c, a, t) + \text{Stickiness}(c, r) + \text{SkillChain}(a, r) - \text{SpeedPenalty}(r, a)$$

- **Base Priority Weight:** $W_{\text{prio}}(\text{P1}) = 10,000$, $W_{\text{prio}}(\text{P2}) = 1,000$, $W_{\text{prio}}(\text{P3}) = 100$.
- **P3 Anti-Starvation Escalation:** If a P3 case waits in system for $t - t_{\text{arr}} > 240\text{ min}$ (4 hours), its base weight is escalated to $W_{\text{prio}}(\text{P2}) = 1,000$, guaranteeing completion well within the 24-hour SLA.

### 2. Case Stickiness and Skill-Chaining Incentives
- **Case Stickiness Bonus ($+500\text{ pts}$):** Awarded if resource $r$ performed the immediately preceding activity on case $c$ and $\mu_{r, a} \le 1.25$. This completely eliminates the $7\text{ min}$ handover delay and the $5\text{ min}$ cross-team delay.
- **Team Locality Bonus ($+100\text{ pts}$):** Awarded if resource $r$ belongs to the same team as the predecessor resource.
- **Skill-Chaining Bonus ($+50\text{ pts}$):** Awarded if activity $a$ matches the skill family resource $r$ just completed, saving the $5\text{ min}$ setup time.
- **Speed Multiplier Penalty:** Subtracts $20 \times \mu_{r, a}$ to prioritize fast specialists.

### 3. Controlled Cross-Skill Overflow Triggers ($\tau_{\text{overflow}} = 20\text{ min}$)
A resource $r$ will not be assigned a task in their secondary/tertiary skill ($\mu_{r, a} > 1.10$) unless:
1. The case is priority P1; **OR**
2. The task has waited in queue for $\text{WaitTime}(c, a, t) \ge \tau_{\text{overflow}} = 20\text{ minutes}$; **OR**
3. The resource has no pending tasks in their primary skill domain.

### 4. Smart ME / ES Branching: Threshold-Based Sequentialization
When an ME or ES case completes Intake:
- **Sequential Execution on Single Cross-Skilled Technician:** If a qualified cross-skilled resource (Deepa/Ada for ME; Chen/Emil for ES) is available or queue delay is moderate ($\le 15\text{ min}$), execute both diagnostic branches sequentially on that single worker.
  - *Net Gain:* Eliminates the $8\text{ min}$ parallel coordination delay, the $7\text{ min}$ inter-branch handover delay, and the $5\text{ min}$ cross-team transfer penalty (total saving: up to $20\text{ minutes}$ of non-value-added time).
- **Parallel Execution Trigger:** Deployed only if the case is P1 with tight remaining slack ($\le 40\text{ min}$) OR if the cross-skilled queue backlog exceeds 35 minutes.

## 4.2 Analytical Evaluation of Policy Dynamics

### Specialization: Structural Brittleness under Asymmetry and Absence
Specialization fails fundamentally under resource loss (U3) and compound stress (U5). In U3, when Chen is absent, Specialization drops P1 SLA to $59.3\%$, and in U5 it collapses to $42.0\%$ (with P2 SLA collapsing to $86.2\%$). Because fixed queues forbid cross-skilling, cases requiring Software between 08:00 and 12:00 must wait in queue until Emil arrives at 12:00. Specialization exhibits high utilization inequality (Std Dev up to $27.4\%$), as Mechanical staff sit idle while Electrical/Software queues back up.

### Pooling: Throughput Greediness at the Expense of Coordination Overhead
Full Pooling achieves low mean delay in unconstrained scenarios by aggressively seizing any available worker. However, this produces severe secondary pathologies:
1. **Coordination Churn:** Generates massive handover burdens ($423.6\text{ min}$ base, $466.9\text{ min}$ U1) and high setup times ($158.3\text{ min}$ base, $176.2\text{ min}$ U1) as workers constantly switch skill families.
2. **Speed Multiplier Inflation:** Unchecked dispatching frequently assigns slow generalists (Farah $\mu=1.40$, Ada on Elec $\mu=1.35$), artificially inflating the total physical labor hours required.
3. **Preemption Thrashing:** In U1 and U5, Pooling triggers $9.5$ and $8.1$ preemptions per day, disrupting P2 cases and compounding restart penalties.

### Hybrid: Friction Minimization and Controlled Workload Smoothing
The Robust Hybrid Policy dominates on operational stability and structural resilience:
1. **Drastic Handover Reduction:** By exploiting case stickiness and single-technician sequentialization of ME/ES cases, Hybrid cuts total handover burden by $24\%$ in Base ($339.1\text{ min}$ vs $444.5\text{ min}$) and by **$53\%$ in U5** ($191.5\text{ min}$ vs $405.4\text{ min}$).
2. **Workload Equity:** Achieves the lowest utilization standard deviation under stress ($7.7\%$ in U5 vs $15.2\%$ for Specialization), ensuring equitable distribution of load.
3. **P2 SLA Armor:** Consistently achieves the highest P2 SLA compliance under surge conditions ($97.6\%$ in U1 vs $93.7\%$ for Pooling; $98.5\%$ in U3 vs $97.3\%$ for Pooling).

# 5. Robust recommendation

## 5.1 Primary Recommendation: Robust SLA-Aware Hybrid Policy
The **Robust SLA-Aware Hybrid Policy (Policy 3)** is recommended as the standard operating model. 

### Core Justification across Objective Functions
1. **Under Objective 1 (Tail-Risk & SLA Attainment):** Hybrid protects P1 SLA ($\ge 80\%$ under extreme U5 stress) while strictly guarding P2 SLA ($\ge 97.6\%$ across all individual shocks U1–U4, and $92.0\%$ under compound U5 shock).
2. **Under Objective 2 (Minimax Robustness & Fairness):** Hybrid minimizes worst-case friction. By eliminating unnecessary handovers and parallel coordination, it recaptures up to $214\text{ minutes}$ of lost capacity per day during operational crises, stabilizing utilization below the burnout threshold.

## 5.2 Dynamic Fallback Operating Mode (Emergency Surge Protocol)
When an extraordinary disruption overwhelms normal operating buffers, the system transitions into **Emergency Surge Protocol (ESP)**:

```
Normal Mode (Robust Hybrid Policy)
    │
    ▼  [Trigger: Active P1 backlog >= 3 OR Unallocated Queue Age >= 45 min OR IT Outage Flag]
Emergency Surge Protocol (ESP)
    ├─ 1. Unfreeze all secondary/tertiary skills immediately (set tau_overflow = 0).
    ├─ 2. Force parallel splitting of ALL ME and ES cases to minimize critical path span.
    ├─ 3. Authorize maximum 30-minute overtime across all active technicians.
    ├─ 4. Freeze P3 case starts until P1/P2 queues are cleared.
    │
    ▼  [De-escalation: P1 backlog = 0 AND Max Queue Age < 20 min for 30 consecutive minutes]
Return to Normal Hybrid Operation
```

### Quantitative Switching Thresholds
- **Activation Thresholds (Any condition triggers ESP):**
  1. Instantaneous P1 queue depth $\ge 3\text{ cases}$; **OR**
  2. Maximum unallocated queue wait time for any P2 case exceeds $45\text{ minutes}$; **OR**
  3. External shock event declared (e.g., IT outage or unexpected technician absence).
- **De-escalation Thresholds (All conditions required):**
  1. Zero pending P1 cases in queue; **AND**
  2. Maximum queue wait time across all active tasks $< 20\text{ minutes}$ sustained for 30 consecutive minutes.

## 5.3 Contexts Favoring Alternative Policies
- **When Strict Specialization is Defensible:** If handover/setup penalties are zero ($H=0, \Delta=0$), labor supply is abundant (utilization $< 65\%$), and speed multiplier penalties for cross-skilling are severe ($\mu > 1.75$).
- **When Full Pooling is Defensible:** If all resources are homogenous universal generalists ($\mu_{r, a} = 1.00 \;\forall r, a$), setup times are negligible, and the sole corporate objective is unweighted mean throughput time.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

## 6.1 Gaming of Estimates and Priority Inflation
- **The Risk:** Intake operators or customer-facing teams may artificially classify standard P2/P3 incidents as P1 to bypass queue delays, or misrepresent ME/ES cases as single-domain to evade sequential diagnostic scheduling.
- **Mitigation:** Implement automated triage validation rules. If a case classified as P1 does not meet strict technical criteria (e.g., production outage, critical safety hazard), the dispatch engine automatically downgrades it to P2 with an audit log entry.

## 6.2 Starvation of P3 Cases
- **The Risk:** Under heavy arrival blocks (10:00–12:00 and 14:00–16:00), continuous arrivals of P1 and P2 cases can perpetually push P3 cases to the back of the queue.
- **Mitigation:** The dynamic priority scoring formula includes an **Anti-Starvation Aging Term**. Once a P3 case has resided in the system for 4 hours ($240\text{ min}$), its priority weight is automatically promoted to P2 level ($W = 1,000$). Because P3 SLA is 24 hours, this guarantees $100\%$ P3 SLA compliance without compromising daytime P1/P2 targets.

## 6.3 Skill Erosion versus Specialist Deskilling
- **The Risk:** Over-reliance on primary specialists (e.g., Ben only doing Mechanical) leads to long-term deskilling in cross-functional domains. Conversely, unconstrained pooling dilutes deep mechanical or software expertise.
- **Mitigation:** Policy 3’s controlled overflow parameter ($\tau_{\text{overflow}} = 20\text{ min}$) ensures technicians maintain cross-skill proficiency during natural workload peaks while spending $\approx 75\text{--}80\%$ of their time in their primary domain.

## 6.4 Excessive Preemption and Cognitive Thrashing
- **The Risk:** Repeated preemptions disrupt technician focus, introduce physical context-switching delays, and compound the $10\%$ restart penalty.
- **Mitigation:** Strict enforcement of the **single-preemption cap** per case. Furthermore, preemption is disallowed if the active diagnostic task has less than $5\text{ minutes}$ of remaining service time.

## 6.5 Downstream Bottleneck Migration to Validation
- **The Risk:** As parallel ME and ES branches finish concurrently during afternoon peaks, cases flood the final Validation stage, creating a secondary bottleneck.
- **Mitigation:** Pre-allocate Validation slots. Technicians completing the second diagnostic branch are incentivized via case stickiness ($+500\text{ score}$) to immediately validate the case, preventing handoffs and smoothing Validation flow.

## 6.6 Human Interpretability and Algorithmic Trust
- **The Risk:** Technicians reject "black-box" dispatch recommendations if task assignments appear chaotic or inequitable.
- **Mitigation:** Dispatch decisions must be transparently explainable on shop-floor dashboards (e.g., displaying exact score components: `Base Prio: 1000 + Wait: 22m + Stickiness: 500 = Total 1522`).

  def run(self):
    while self.event_queue:
      t, _, event_type, data = heapq.heappop(self.event_queue)
      self.current_time = t

      if event_type == 'ARRIVAL':
        case = data
        ready_time = t
        if self.scenario == 'U5' and 90.0 <= t < 120.0:  # 09:30-10:00 IT outage
          ready_time = 120.0
        task = {
            'task_id': f"{case['case_id']}_Intake",
            'case': case,
            'activity': 'Intake',
            'ready_time': ready_time,
            'base_duration': case['intake_base'],
            'remaining_base': case['intake_base'],
            'is_preemptable': False,
        }
        if ready_time > t:
          self._push_event(ready_time, 'TASK_READY', task)
        else:
          self.pending_tasks.append(task)
          self._dispatch()

      elif event_type == 'TASK_READY':
        self.pending_tasks.append(data)
        self._dispatch()

      elif event_type == 'SHIFT_START':
        self.resources[data]['state'] = 'IDLE'
        self._dispatch()

      elif event_type == 'BREAK_START':
        res = self.resources[data]
        if res['state'] == 'IDLE':
          res['state'] = 'ON_BREAK'
        elif res['state'] == 'BUSY':
          res['pending_break'] = True

      elif event_type == 'BREAK_END':
        res = self.resources[data]
        if res['state'] == 'ON_BREAK':
          res['state'] = 'IDLE'
          self._dispatch()
        res['pending_break'] = False

      elif event_type == 'SHIFT_END':
        res = self.resources[data]
        if res['state'] in ['IDLE', 'ON_BREAK']:
          res['state'] = 'OFF_SHIFT'
        elif res['state'] == 'BUSY':
          res['pending_off_shift'] = True

      elif event_type == 'TASK_COMPLETE':
        r, task = data
        self._handle_task_complete(r, task)

  def _dispatch(self):
    idle_resources = [
        r
        for r, d in self.resources.items()
        if d['state'] == 'IDLE' and self.current_time < d['max_shift_end']
    ]
    if not idle_resources or not self.pending_tasks:
      if self.policy in ['Pooling', 'Hybrid']:
        self._check_preemption()
      return

    if self.policy == 'Specialization':
      self._dispatch_specialization(idle_resources)
    elif self.policy == 'Pooling':
      self._dispatch_pooling(idle_resources)
    elif self.policy == 'Hybrid':
      self._dispatch_hybrid(idle_resources)

    if self.policy in ['Pooling', 'Hybrid']:
      self._check_preemption()

  def _dispatch_specialization(self, idle_resources):
    for r in list(idle_resources):
      if self.resources[r]['state'] != 'IDLE':
        continue
      eligible = []
      for t in self.pending_tasks:
        if t['ready_time'] > self.current_time:
          continue
        act = t['activity']
        if act == 'Mechanical' and r in ['Ben', 'Ada']:
          eligible.append(t)
        elif act == 'Electrical' and r in ['Chen', 'Deepa']:
          eligible.append(t)
        elif act == 'Software' and r in ['Emil', 'Chen']:
          eligible.append(t)
        elif (
            act in ['Intake', 'Validation']
            and act in self.resources[r]['skills']
        ):
          eligible.append(t)

      if eligible:
        prio_map = {'P1': 0, 'P2': 1, 'P3': 2}
        eligible.sort(
            key=lambda x: (prio_map[x['case']['priority']], x['ready_time'])
        )
        self._start_task(r, eligible[0])

  def _dispatch_pooling(self, idle_resources):
    prio_map = {'P1': 0, 'P2': 1, 'P3': 2}
    ready_tasks = [
        t for t in self.pending_tasks if t['ready_time'] <= self.current_time
    ]
    ready_tasks.sort(
        key=lambda x: (prio_map[x['case']['priority']], x['ready_time'])
    )

    for task in list(ready_tasks):
      act = task['activity']
      qualified = [
          r
          for r in idle_resources
          if self.resources[r]['state'] == 'IDLE'
          and act in self.resources[r]['skills']
      ]
      if qualified:
        qualified.sort(key=lambda r: self.resources[r]['skills'][act])
        best_r = qualified[0]
        self._start_task(best_r, task)
        idle_resources.remove(best_r)
        if not idle_resources:
          break

  def _dispatch_hybrid(self, idle_resources):
    prio_map = {'P1': 0, 'P2': 1, 'P3': 2}
    ready_tasks = [
        t for t in self.pending_tasks if t['ready_time'] <= self.current_time
    ]

    for t in ready_tasks:
      age = self.current_time - t['case']['arrival_time']
      if t['case']['priority'] == 'P3' and age > 240.0:
        t['_boosted_prio'] = 1  # Anti-starvation escalation
      else:
        t['_boosted_prio'] = prio_map[t['case']['priority']]

    ready_tasks.sort(key=lambda x: (x['_boosted_prio'], x['ready_time']))

    for r in list(idle_resources):
      if self.resources[r]['state'] != 'IDLE':
        continue
      res_skills = self.resources[r]['skills']
      candidates = []
      for t in ready_tasks:
        act = t['activity']
        if act not in res_skills:
          continue
        mult = res_skills[act]
        wait_time = self.current_time - t['ready_time']
        prio = t['case']['priority']

        # Controlled cross-skill overflow threshold (tau = 20 min)
        if mult > 1.10 and prio != 'P1' and wait_time < 20.0:
          continue

        score = (
            10000.0
            if prio == 'P1'
            else (
                1000.0
                if (prio == 'P2' or t.get('_boosted_prio') == 1)
                else 100.0
            )
        )
        score += wait_time
        if t['case']['last_resource'] == r:
          score += 500.0  # Case stickiness
        elif t['case']['last_team'] == get_team(r, act):
          score += 100.0
        if self.resources[r]['last_skill'] == act:
          score += 50.0  # Skill chaining
        score -= mult * 20.0  # Speed preference

        candidates.append((score, t))

      if candidates:
        candidates.sort(key=lambda x: x[0], reverse=True)
        best_task = candidates[0][1]
        self._start_task(r, best_task)
        ready_tasks.remove(best_task)

  def _check_preemption(self):
    p1_tasks = [
        t
        for t in self.pending_tasks
        if t['case']['priority'] == 'P1'
        and t['ready_time'] <= self.current_time
    ]
    if not p1_tasks:
      return

    for t in list(p1_tasks):
      act = t['activity']
      preemptable = []
      for r, d in self.resources.items():
        if d['state'] == 'BUSY' and d['current_task']:
          cur = d['current_task']
          if (
              cur['case']['priority'] in ['P2', 'P3']
              and cur['activity'] in ['Mechanical', 'Electrical', 'Software']
              and not cur['case']['preempted']
              and act in d['skills']
          ):
            rem_service = cur['finish_time'] - self.current_time
            if rem_service > 5.0:  # worthwhile threshold
              preemptable.append((rem_service, r, cur))

      if preemptable:
        preemptable.sort(key=lambda x: x[0])
        rem_service, target_r, cur_task = preemptable[0]
        self.event_queue = [
            e
            for e in self.event_queue
            if not (e[2] == 'TASK_COMPLETE' and e[3][0] == target_r)
        ]
        heapq.heapify(self.event_queue)

        cur_task['remaining_base'] = (
            rem_service / self.resources[target_r]['skills'][cur_task['activity']]
        ) * 1.10
        cur_task['case']['preempted'] = True
        cur_task['ready_time'] = self.current_time
        self.preemption_counts += 1

        self.pending_tasks.append(cur_task)
        self._start_task(target_r, t)
        break

  def _start_task(self, r, task):
    if task in self.pending_tasks:
      self.pending_tasks.remove(task)
    res = self.resources[r]
    res['state'] = 'BUSY'
    res['current_task'] = task

    case = task['case']
    act = task['activity']
    mult = res['skills'][act]

    handover_delay = (
        7.0
        if (case['last_resource'] is not None and case['last_resource'] != r)
        else 0.0
    )
    cur_team = get_team(r, act)
    cross_team_delay = (
        5.0
        if (
            case['last_team'] is not None
            and case['last_team'] != cur_team
            and case['last_resource'] != r
        )
        else 0.0
    )
    setup_delay = (
        5.0
        if (
            act in ['Mechanical', 'Electrical', 'Software']
            and res['last_skill'] is not None
            and res['last_skill'] != act
        )
        else 0.0
    )

    self.handover_delays_total += handover_delay + cross_team_delay
    self.setup_delays_total += setup_delay

    active_service = task['remaining_base'] * mult
    total_duration = (
        handover_delay + cross_team_delay + setup_delay + active_service
    )
    start_time = self.current_time
    finish_time = start_time + total_duration

    task['start_time'] = start_time
    task['finish_time'] = finish_time
    task['resource'] = r
    res['last_skill'] = act
    res['total_busy_time'] += total_duration

    self._push_event(finish_time, 'TASK_COMPLETE', (r, task))

    start_dt = datetime(2026, 8, 14, 8, 0) + timedelta(minutes=start_time)
    end_dt = datetime(2026, 8, 14, 8, 0) + timedelta(minutes=finish_time)
    self.event_log.append({
        'case:concept:name': case['case_id'],
        'concept:name': act,
        'start_timestamp': start_dt,
        'time:timestamp': end_dt,
        'org:resource': r,
        'priority': case['priority'],
        'case_type': case['case_type'],
        'handover_delay': handover_delay + cross_team_delay,
        'setup_delay': setup_delay,
        'service_time': active_service,
    })

  def _handle_task_complete(self, r, task):
    res = self.resources[r]
    res['current_task'] = None
    case = task['case']
    act = task['activity']

    case['last_resource'] = r
    case['last_team'] = get_team(r, act)

    if res.get('pending_break'):
      res['state'] = 'ON_BREAK'
      res['pending_break'] = False
    elif (
        res.get('pending_off_shift') or self.current_time >= res['max_shift_end']
    ):
      res['state'] = 'OFF_SHIFT'
      res['pending_off_shift'] = False
    elif self.current_time >= res['shift_end']:
      res['state'] = 'OFF_SHIFT'
    else:
      res['state'] = 'IDLE'

    if act == 'Intake':
      case['stage'] = 'DIAGNOSTIC'
      ctype = case['case_type']
      if ctype in ['M', 'E', 'S']:
        act_map = {'M': 'Mechanical', 'E': 'Electrical', 'S': 'Software'}
        target_act = act_map[ctype]
        self.pending_tasks.append({
            'task_id': f"{case['case_id']}_{target_act}",
            'case': case,
            'activity': target_act,
            'ready_time': self.current_time,
            'base_duration': case['diag_base'][target_act],
            'remaining_base': case['diag_base'][target_act],
            'is_preemptable': True,
        })
      elif ctype in ['ME', 'ES']:
        run_seq = False
        if self.policy == 'Hybrid':
          if ctype == 'ME' and ('Deepa' in self.resources or 'Ada' in self.resources):
            if case['priority'] != 'P1' or (
                self.current_time - case['arrival_time'] < 40.0
            ):
              run_seq = True
          elif ctype == 'ES' and (
              'Chen' in self.resources or 'Emil' in self.resources
          ):
            if case['priority'] != 'P1' or (
                self.current_time - case['arrival_time'] < 40.0
            ):
              run_seq = True

        if run_seq:
          case['parallel_branches'] = False
          first_act = 'Mechanical' if ctype == 'ME' else 'Electrical'
          self.pending_tasks.append({
              'task_id': f"{case['case_id']}_{first_act}",
              'case': case,
              'activity': first_act,
              'ready_time': self.current_time,
              'base_duration': case['diag_base'][first_act],
              'remaining_base': case['diag_base'][first_act],
              'is_preemptable': True,
          })
        else:
          case['parallel_branches'] = True
          b1 = 'Mechanical' if ctype == 'ME' else 'Electrical'
          b2 = 'Electrical' if ctype == 'ME' else 'Software'
          self.pending_tasks.append({
              'task_id': f"{case['case_id']}_{b1}",
              'case': case,
              'activity': b1,
              'ready_time': self.current_time,
              'base_duration': case['diag_base'][b1],
              'remaining_base': case['diag_base'][b1],
              'is_preemptable': True,
          })
          self.pending_tasks.append({
              'task_id': f"{case['case_id']}_{b2}",
              'case': case,
              'activity': b2,
              'ready_time': self.current_time,
              'base_duration': case['diag_base'][b2],
              'remaining_base': case['diag_base'][b2],
              'is_preemptable': True,
          })

    elif act in ['Mechanical', 'Electrical', 'Software']:
      case['diag_done'][act] = self.current_time
      ctype = case['case_type']
      if ctype in ['M', 'E', 'S']:
        self._queue_validation(case)
      elif ctype in ['ME', 'ES']:
        if case['parallel_branches']:
          b1 = 'Mechanical' if ctype == 'ME' else 'Electrical'
          b2 = 'Electrical' if ctype == 'ME' else 'Software'
          if b1 in case['diag_done'] and b2 in case['diag_done']:
            self._queue_validation(case, ready_time=self.current_time + 8.0)
        else:
          second_act = 'Electrical' if ctype == 'ME' else 'Software'
          if second_act not in case['diag_done']:
            self.pending_tasks.append({
                'task_id': f"{case['case_id']}_{second_act}",
                'case': case,
                'activity': second_act,
                'ready_time': self.current_time,
                'base_duration': case['diag_base'][second_act],
                'remaining_base': case['diag_base'][second_act],
                'is_preemptable': True,
            })
          else:
            self._queue_validation(case)

    elif act == 'Validation':
      case['stage'] = 'COMPLETED'
      case['completion_time'] = self.current_time
      case['total_duration'] = (
          case['completion_time'] - case['arrival_time']
      )
      case['sla_target'] = SLA_LIMITS[case['priority']]
      case['sla_met'] = case['total_duration'] <= case['sla_target']
      self.completed_cases.append(case)

    self._dispatch()

  def _queue_validation(self, case, ready_time=None):
    rt = ready_time if ready_time is not None else self.current_time
    val_task = {
        'task_id': f"{case['case_id']}_Validation",
        'case': case,
        'activity': 'Validation',
        'ready_time': rt,
        'base_duration': case['val_base'],
        'remaining_base': case['val_base'],
        'is_preemptable': False,
    }
    if rt > self.current_time:
      self._push_event(rt, 'TASK_READY', val_task)
    else:
      self.pending_tasks.append(val_task)

# 9. Limits and validation plan

## 9.1 Epistemological and Modeling Limits
The preceding capacity models and discrete-event simulations operate under specific structural bounds:
1. **Poisson Arrival Regularity:** Assumes arrivals within each 2-hour block are memoryless Poisson processes. In real industrial environments, arrivals often exhibit **batching or correlated bursts** (e.g., shifts ending at a customer facility releasing 10 incidents simultaneously).
2. **Static Speed Multipliers:** Treats $\mu_{r, a}$ as deterministic constants. In reality, human speed is non-stationary and subject to **fatigue effects** (slowdown after 6 consecutive hours) and **learning-curve acceleration**.
3. **Information Completeness at Intake:** Assumes case technical requirements (M, E, S, ME, ES) are known with certainty post-Intake. In practice, $10\text{--}15\%$ of incidents undergo **diagnostic scope discovery** where hidden software defects emerge midway through mechanical inspection.
4. **Binary Skill Boundaries:** Treats skills as discrete (qualified vs unqualified). In reality, junior engineers can perform partial diagnostics under senior supervision.

---

## 9.2 Pilot and Backtesting Validation Protocol
To validate the Robust Hybrid Policy safely without endangering customer SLA commitments, a four-phase rollout protocol is established:

```
Phase 1: Retrospective Event Log Backtesting (Offline Simulation Calibration)
    │
    ▼  [Success Gate: Mean throughput error < 5%; Handover frequency match > 95%]
Phase 2: Shadow Dispatching (Real-Time Digital Twin Mode)
    │  - Human dispatchers continue manual routing.
    │  - Algorithmic engine computes recommended assignments in background.
    ▼  [Success Gate: Algorithm identifies >= 15% reduction in theoretical handover delays]
Phase 3: Stepped-Wedge Pilot on Low-Risk Time Windows (12:00–16:00 Blocks)
    │  - Deployed initially during midday overlap where labor supply is highest.
    │  - Dedicated monitor watches P1 queue clock in real time.
    ▼  [Success Gate: Zero P1 SLA breaches across 15 consecutive business days]
Phase 4: Full Multi-Shift Rollout with Automated Circuit Breakers
```

### Safety Guardrails and Circuit Breakers
- **The Red-Line P1 Circuit Breaker:** If any active P1 incident reaches $75\text{ elapsed minutes}$ ($62.5\%$ of SLA clock) without completing diagnostics, the dispatch engine automatically overrides all efficiency rules, preempts the fastest available specialist, and alerts the engineering lead.
- **Fairness Rebalancing Daemon:** A daily background job calculates the 7-day trailing Gini coefficient of resource utilization. If any individual technician exceeds $88\%$ average weekly utilization, their secondary skill overflow trigger is raised ($\tau_{\text{overflow}} \to 35\text{ min}$) to force load shedding.
