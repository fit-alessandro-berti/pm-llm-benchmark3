# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

A process model cannot be judged in the abstract; its fitness-versus-precision trade-off is governed by the operational decision it supports. For governance review, we distinguish four intended use cases:

1. **Exploratory Discovery and Bottleneck Diagnosis**: Aimed at surfacing dominant pathways, rework loops, and waiting-time hotspots from noisy logs. High recall (fitness) and structural readability take priority over tight precision, because dropping messy traces hides operational friction. However, exploration still requires **vocabulary completeness**—a model that omits rework activities altogether cannot diagnose rework bottlenecks.
2. **Conformance Enforcement and Compliance Auditing**: Aimed at detecting policy breaches (e.g., releasing an order without mandatory credit/technical checks). Here, **false negatives for non-compliance** (admitting forbidden traces because the model is over-permissive) are catastrophic, whereas **false positives for non-compliance** (flagging valid unseen combinations as violations) create alert fatigue and erode auditor trust. Non-negotiable policy invariants act as hard constraints, not soft metric weights.
3. **Discrete-Event Simulation and Capacity Planning**: Aimed at generating synthetic caseloads (`play_out`) to forecast staffing for credit analysts, engineers, suppliers, and managers. Both extreme over-generalization (generating zero-check orders or premature expedites) and extreme overfitting (replaying only eight historical sequences) invalidate throughput and queue estimates.
4. **Stakeholder Communication and Process Documentation**: Aimed at establishing a shared contract across Compliance, Operations, and IT. Apparent visual simplicity is dangerous if stakeholders misread permissive constructs (such as silent skips in parallel blocks) as mandatory steps.

**Review Assumptions**:
- Both submitted models are evaluated under standard PM4Py `ProcessTree` semantics (`->` sequence, `X` exclusive choice, `+` parallel/interleaved execution, `tau` silent skip).
- Because neither team supplied random seeds, filtering scripts, alignment cost tables, or holdout case IDs, reported quantitative differences are treated as **unverified observational evidence** subject to split, cost, and data-quality confounding.
- Policies P1–P7 represent agreed business intent, while missing log attributes (`sourcing_type` for P3 and `affected_checks` for P6) represent **epistemic observability limits** in the event log—not permission to treat conditional obligations as unconstrained choices.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

### Strongest Case For Model S
Model S captures the macro-lifecycle framing (`Register Dossier` $\rightarrow$ reviews $\rightarrow$ single approval $\rightarrow$ `Release Order`) in a compact, duplicate-free structure that aligns in 18 ms per case. It naturally accommodates arbitrary completion order between `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` (P2), allows `Supplier Review` and `Expedite Review` to interleave in either order (P4), and enforces mutual exclusion between `Manager Approval` and `Auto Approval` (P5). Where upstream logging drops `Supplier Review` events (F4), its `X('Supplier Review', tau)` branch prevents entire traces from failing alignment due to telemetry gaps.

### Strongest Case Against Model S
Model S achieves high fitness by **liquidating core compliance controls**. Wrapping mandatory checks in `X(..., tau)` inside a single parallel (`+`) block conflates concurrency with unconstrained optionality and erases ordering dependencies before `Expedite Review`. Equally damning—and unnoticed by superficial comprehension ratings—Model S **completely omits `Correct Dossier` from its alphabet** and contains no loop operator. Thus, it is paradoxically *over-permissive* on initial compliance gates while *over-restrictive* (blind) toward legitimate rework cycles (P6).

### Concrete Undesirable and Policy-Ambiguous Admitted Behaviors
1. **Zero-check rubber-stamping (violates P2)**:
   `Register Dossier, Auto Approval, Release Order` (as well as B3 with `Manager Approval`). Every branch of the `+` operator selects `tau`, releasing an unvetted custom order.
2. **Single-check partial bypass (violates P2)**:
   `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Manager Approval, Release Order`. `Technical Review` silently takes `tau`; an order with unreviewed engineering specs is approved.
3. **Expediting before *any* mandatory check begins (violates P4)**:
   `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` (Probe B4). Because `Expedite Review` sits inside the same `+` operator as `Credit Check` and `Technical Review`, no causal arc constrains its start.
4. **Expediting mid-check before *both* mandatory checks complete (violates P4)**:
   `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order`. P4 requires `Expedite Review` to follow *both* mandatory checks; parallel interleaving admits execution after just one.
5. **Expediting and supplier sign-off with zero internal checks (violates P2 & P4)**:
   `Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Expedite Review, Auto Approval, Release Order`. External and urgent flags fire without either `Credit Check` or `Technical Review`.
6. **Policy-ambiguous external bypass / internal waste (violates P3)**:
   `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` on an externally sourced order (admitted without warning), or `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Supplier Review, Auto Approval, Release Order` on an internally sourced order.

### Intentional Noise Tolerance vs. Accidental Semantic Flaws
Making `Supplier Review` skippable (`X('Supplier Review', tau)`) can be defended as **intentional tolerance** for an unlogged sourcing attribute (P3) and occasional missing supplier events (F4). By contrast, three defects are **accidental semantic flaws**:
- Optionalizing `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` via `tau` when no stakeholder reported missing events for mandatory internal checks;
- Using flat parallelism (`+`) instead of a partial order, destroying the `Credit/Tech` $\prec$ `Expedite` precedence;
- Dropping `Correct Dossier` entirely (relying on alignment log-move penalties to paper over the ~0.008 fitness gap rather than modeling P6/P7).

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

### Strongest Case For Model C
Model C acts as a strict whitelist of eight verified historical pathways. Every accepted trace is guaranteed to start with `Register Dossier`, complete **both** `Credit Check` and `Technical Review` prior to any `Expedite Review` or approval, execute `Expedite Review` only after mandatory checks, pass through exactly one approval gate (`Manager Approval` or `Auto Approval`), and end with `Release Order`. It cannot admit zero-check releases (B3), premature expedites (B4), or uncorrected duplicate mandatory checks (B8).

### Strongest Case Against Model C
Model C confuses **memorizing frequent training traces** with **modeling process policy**:

- **Combinatorial Branch Enumeration**: Even *without* rework loops, the policy space spans 2 mandatory check orders $\times$ (no supplier / supplier) $\times$ (no expedite / expedite, with valid interleavings) $\times$ 2 approval modes—over 16 valid loop-free trace variants. Model C hard-codes just 7 loop-free branches and 1 rework branch, rejecting every valid combination that happened not to pass a frequency cutoff in the 80% training split.
- **Label Duplication and Architectural Fragility**: Across its 8 sequence branches, Model C instantiates 48 leaf activity nodes for only 9 distinct business activities (reported as 33 duplicates under the team's undocumented net-conversion count). For IT architecture (F3), there is no single locus of ownership for `Credit Check` (9 copies) or `Manager Approval` (7 copies); adding a pre-release audit step requires synchronized edits across every branch.
- **Unseen Combinations and Holdout Collapse (0.973 $\rightarrow$ 0.744)**: Holdout fitness plunges by 22.9 percentage points because legitimate orthogonal features combine in unseen ways (F4): e.g., `Technical Review` $\rightarrow$ `Credit Check` paired with `Auto Approval` (B2), any external order routed through `Auto Approval` (B5), or `Expedite Review` preceding `Supplier Review` (explicitly allowed by P4, absent from Branch 6). Treating B2 and B5 as "noncompliant until approved" (F8) contradicts already-approved stakeholder rules P2, P3, P4, and P5.
- **Rigid, Non-Transparent Correction Handling**: Branch 7 (`Reg -> Cred -> Tech -> Corr -> Cred -> Tech -> Mgr -> Rel`) hard-codes one rigid rework pattern. It rejects dossiers that initially ran `Tech -> Cred` before correction, dossiers requiring $\ge 2$ corrections (P6), corrected dossiers routed to `Auto Approval` or `Supplier Review`, and targeted rechecks of *only* `Credit Check` or *only* `Technical Review` (F6, B6).
- **Precision as a Memorization Artifact**: Model C's 0.941 training precision is not evidence of well-discovered invariants; any prefix-disjoint union of observed traces achieves near-1.0 escaping-edges precision on the training set because each branch offers only a single deterministic successor after branch selection.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

Ranked from highest (1) to lowest (10) risk of misleading the governance board:

1. **Fitness Rewarded by Optionalizing Mandatory Behavior**: Wrapping mandatory checks in `X(..., tau)` lets Model S align incomplete, noisy, and blatantly non-compliant cases at zero model-move cost. Fitness (0.992 / 0.981) rewards recall on observed positives while remaining completely blind to critical compliance gates being bypassed.
2. **Positive-Log-Only Precision**: Escaping-edges precision (`precision_alignments`) evaluates state permissiveness solely along prefixes visited by observed positive training traces (F5). It never probes whether catastrophic traces like B3 or B4 are admitted, while rewarding Model C (0.941) for memorizing training trajectories.
3. **Missing-Event Bias and Unlogged Decision Attributes**: Omitting `sourcing_type` (P3) and `affected_checks` (P6)—compounded by missing `Supplier Review` events (F4)—forces purely control-flow miners into a false dilemma: either make conditional steps globally optional (Model S, inflating escape edges) or enumerate observed variants (Model C, collapsing on holdout).
4. **Generalization Rewarded by Excessive Permissiveness**: Token-based replay generalization (`generalization_tbr`) is a function of $\sum (1 / \sqrt{\text{visits}})$ per transition. Model S scores 0.907 because every trace funnels through the same 4 parallel branches (via execution or `tau`), whereas a flower model would score even higher. Conversely, Model C scores 0.412 because visits are diluted across 48 duplicated leaf transitions.
5. **Alignment-Cost Sensitivity**: Under default uniform costs (cost 1 per log/model move, cost 0 per `tau`), omitting `Correct Dossier` entirely from Model S incurs only a tiny per-trace penalty (yielding 0.992 fitness), and skipping `Credit Check` is penalized no more severely than a missing `Supplier Review` telemetry event.
6. **Train/Holdout Split Sensitivity and Potential Leakage**: Without random seeds, temporal boundaries, or case-stratification code, an unstratified 80/20 split easily strands multi-factor combinations (`Supplier Review` + `Auto Approval` + `Tech -> Cred`, per F4) disproportionately in the holdout partition, exaggerating or distorting variance across runs.
7. **Duplicated-Label Effects**: Model C's 33+ duplicate labels fracture the state space, artificially boosting training precision (narrowing enabled transitions per state), tanking TBR generalization, degrading simplicity (0.281), and multiplying A* search states during alignment.
8. **Silent-Transition (`tau`) Effects**: Free `tau` transitions in Model S allow zero-cost bypasses during alignment and create combinatorial silent state transitions in the underlying Petri net, inflating enabled-action frontiers in escaping-edges precision (0.584) without visibly appearing in simplified business diagrams.
9. **Stakeholder-Score Methodology**: An informal 1–5 visual preference poll (4.4 vs. 1.9) confounds *cognitive ease of viewing* with *accuracy of semantic interpretation*. Business raters almost certainly read Model S's central block as "perform the needed reviews" without realizing it admits zero-check approvals and premature expediting.
10. **Runtime Confounding by Model Size and Non-Fitting Search**: Model C's 0.440 s median runtime reflects A* alignment backtracking across 8 duplicate-labeled branches when holdout traces fail to match—not the intrinsic computational cost of enforcing policies P1–P7, which a compact partial-order net aligns in milliseconds.

# 7. Third Alternative

### Architectural Strategy
We synthesize a **Hybrid Balanced POWL Backbone + Explicit Policy/Attribute Audit Layer**:
1. **`model_third` (POWL Control-Flow Model)**: Uses `StrictPartialOrder` and `OperatorPOWL` to enforce every pure control-flow invariant (`P1`, `P2`, `P4`, `P5`, `P7`, and targeted rework structure for `P6`) with **minimal, purposeful label duplication** (only 4 extra nodes total across the entire model, separating mandatory first-pass checks from post-correction rechecks).
2. **External Declarative & Attribute Policy Check (`DECLARATIVE_POLICY_SPEC` + `audit_case_against_policy`)**: Evaluates data-conditioned policies (`P3`, `P6`) and suspicious patterns (`P7`) using three-valued governance logic (`VALID`, `VIOLATION`, `INDETERMINATE_MISSING_DATA`) rather than silently treating missing attributes as free passes.

### 1. Compact Formal Description
Let the top-level process be a strict partial order $\mathcal{P} = (V, \prec)$ over nodes:
- $v_{\text{reg}} = \texttt{Register Dossier}$, $\quad v_{\text{rel}} = \texttt{Release Order}$
- $v_{\text{cred}} = \texttt{Credit Check}$, $\quad v_{\text{tech}} = \texttt{Technical Review}$ (mandatory initial checks, unordered)
- $v_{\text{supp}} = \text{XOR}(\texttt{Supplier Review}, \tau)$
- $v_{\text{rework}} = \text{LOOP}\big(\tau,\; \texttt{Correct Dossier} \prec \text{XOR}(\texttt{Credit Check},\; \texttt{Technical Review},\; \texttt{Credit Check} \parallel \texttt{Technical Review})\big)$
- $v_{\text{exp}} = \text{XOR}(\texttt{Expedite Review}, \tau)$
- $v_{\text{app}} = \text{XOR}(\texttt{Manager Approval}, \texttt{Auto Approval})$

With minimal precedence relation $\prec$ generated by:
$$v_{\text{reg}} \prec \{v_{\text{cred}}, v_{\text{tech}}, v_{\text{supp}}\}, \quad \{v_{\text{cred}}, v_{\text{tech}}\} \prec v_{\text{rework}} \prec v_{\text{exp}} \prec v_{\text{app}} \prec v_{\text{rel}}, \quad v_{\text{supp}} \prec v_{\text{app}}$$

Supplemented by data-aware predicates over case attributes $\alpha$:
- **D1 (P3 Sourcing Guard)**: $(\alpha.\text{sourcing} = \texttt{external}) \iff (\texttt{Supplier Review} \in \sigma)$
- **D2 (P6 Targeted Recheck Guard)**: For each $i$-th occurrence of $\texttt{Correct Dossier}$ with post-correction check multiset $R_i$ before the next correction or approval, $R_i = \alpha.\text{affected\_checks}[i] \subseteq \{\texttt{Credit Check}, \texttt{Technical Review}\}$ with $R_i \neq \emptyset$.

### 2. Self-Contained PM4Py Code Constructing `model_third`

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def build_model_third() -> StrictPartialOrder:
    # Entry and exit activities (P1)
    register = Transition(label="Register Dossier")
    release = Transition(label="Release Order")

    # Mandatory initial checks in either order (P2)
    credit_init = Transition(label="Credit Check")
    tech_init = Transition(label="Technical Review")

    # Conditionally required Supplier Review (P3; enforced with attribute layer)
    supplier_opt = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [Transition(label="Supplier Review"), SilentTransition()],
    )

    # Targeted correction & recheck cycle: 0 or more iterations (P6, P7, F6)
    # Each Correct Dossier must be followed by Credit only, Tech only, or both.
    correct = Transition(label="Correct Dossier")
    recheck_cred_only = Transition(label="Credit Check")
    recheck_tech_only = Transition(label="Technical Review")
    recheck_both = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[
            Transition(label="Credit Check"),
            Transition(label="Technical Review"),
        ]
    )  # No edges => concurrent execution in either order

    recheck_target = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [recheck_cred_only, recheck_tech_only, recheck_both],
    )

    rework_step = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[correct, recheck_target])
    rework_step.order.add_edge(correct, recheck_target)

    # LOOP(tau, rework_step) executes rework_step zero or more times
    rework_loop = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.LOOP,
        [SilentTransition(), rework_step],
    )

    # Optional Expedite Review after mandatory checks & rework, before approval (P4)
    expedite_opt = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [Transition(label="Expedite Review"), SilentTransition()],
    )

    # Exactly one approval before release (P5)
    approval_xor = OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [Transition(label="Manager Approval"), Transition(label="Auto Approval")],
    )

    # Assemble top-level StrictPartialOrder
    po = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[
            register,
            credit_init,
            tech_init,
            supplier_opt,
            rework_loop,
            expedite_opt,
            approval_xor,
            release,
        ]
    )

    # Minimal Hasse precedence edges
    po.order.add_edge(register, credit_init)
    po.order.add_edge(register, tech_init)
    po.order.add_edge(register, supplier_opt)

    po.order.add_edge(credit_init, rework_loop)
    po.order.add_edge(tech_init, rework_loop)

    po.order.add_edge(rework_loop, expedite_opt)
    po.order.add_edge(expedite_opt, approval_xor)
    po.order.add_edge(supplier_opt, approval_xor)
    po.order.add_edge(approval_xor, release)

    return po


model_third = build_model_third()
net_third, im_third, fm_third = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)
```

### 3. Declarative Rule Specification and Post-Hoc Policy Checker

```python
from typing import Any, Dict, List, Optional

DECLARATIVE_POLICY_SPEC: Dict[str, Dict[str, Any]] = {
    "P3_EXTERNAL_SUPPLIER_IFF": {
        "type": "attribute_biconditional",
        "attribute": "sourcing_type",
        "expected_value": "external",
        "target_activity": "Supplier Review",
        "missing_attribute_status": "INDETERMINATE_MISSING_DATA",
    },
    "P6_TARGETED_RECHECK_MATCH": {
        "type": "rework_scope_match",
        "trigger_activity": "Correct Dossier",
        "attribute": "affected_checks",  # List[Set[str]] per correction event
        "allowed_targets": {"Credit Check", "Technical Review"},
        "missing_attribute_status": "INDETERMINATE_MISSING_DATA",
    },
    "P7_NO_UNCORRECTED_REPEAT_MANDATORY": {
        "type": "max_count_per_epoch",
        "delimiter_activity": "Correct Dossier",
        "guarded_activities": {"Credit Check", "Technical Review"},
        "max_per_epoch": 1,
        "violation_status": "VIOLATION",
    },
}


def audit_case_against_policy(
    activities: List[str],
    sourcing_type: Optional[str] = None,
    affected_checks: Optional[List[set]] = None,
) -> Dict[str, Any]:
    """
    Post-hoc policy & data-attribute audit complementing `model_third` alignments.
    Returns overall status in {'VALID', 'INDETERMINATE_MISSING_DATA', 'VIOLATION'}.
    """
    findings: List[Dict[str, str]] = []

    # 1. P7 check: no repeated mandatory check within the same correction epoch
    epochs: List[List[str]] = [[]]
    for act in activities:
        if act == "Correct Dossier":
            epochs.append([])
        else:
            epochs[-1].append(act)

    for idx, epoch in enumerate(epochs):
        for mandatory in ("Credit Check", "Technical Review"):
            if epoch.count(mandatory) > 1:
                findings.append({
                    "rule": "P7",
                    "status": "VIOLATION",
                    "detail": f"'{mandatory}' repeated in epoch {idx} without preceding 'Correct Dossier'.",
                })

    # 2. P6 check: post-correction rechecks match affected_checks
    rework_epochs = epochs[1:]
    if rework_epochs:
        for r_idx, r_epoch in enumerate(rework_epochs, start=1):
            executed = [a for a in r_epoch if a in {"Credit Check", "Technical Review"}]
            if not executed:
                findings.append({
                    "rule": "P6",
                    "status": "VIOLATION",
                    "detail": f"Correction #{r_idx} followed by zero mandatory rechecks.",
                })
            elif affected_checks is None or len(affected_checks) < r_idx:
                findings.append({
                    "rule": "P6",
                    "status": "INDETERMINATE_MISSING_DATA",
                    "detail": f"Correction #{r_idx} executed {set(executed)}, but 'affected_checks' is unlogged.",
                })
            elif set(executed) != set(affected_checks[r_idx - 1]):
                findings.append({
                    "rule": "P6",
                    "status": "VIOLATION",
                    "detail": f"Correction #{r_idx} executed {set(executed)} != expected {affected_checks[r_idx - 1]}.",
                })

    # 3. P3 check: Supplier Review iff external order
    has_supplier = "Supplier Review" in activities
    if sourcing_type is None:
        findings.append({
            "rule": "P3",
            "status": "INDETERMINATE_MISSING_DATA",
            "detail": f"Supplier Review present={has_supplier}, but 'sourcing_type' is unlogged.",
        })
    elif sourcing_type == "external" and not has_supplier:
        findings.append({
            "rule": "P3",
            "status": "VIOLATION",
            "detail": "External order missing mandatory 'Supplier Review' (or missing telemetry event).",
        })
    elif sourcing_type == "internal" and has_supplier:
        findings.append({
            "rule": "P3",
            "status": "VIOLATION",
            "detail": "Internal order executed unnecessary 'Supplier Review'.",
        })

    statuses = {f["status"] for f in findings}
    if "VIOLATION" in statuses:
        overall = "VIOLATION"
    elif "INDETERMINATE_MISSING_DATA" in statuses:
        overall = "INDETERMINATE_MISSING_DATA"
    else:
        overall = "VALID"

    return {"overall_status": overall, "findings": findings}
```

### 4. Explicit Behavioral Semantics
- **Mandatory Checks (P2)**: `credit_init` and `tech_init` have no `tau` bypass and no ordering edge between them; every valid trace must complete both in either order after `Register Dossier`.
- **Optional `Supplier Review` (P3)**: Structurally concurrent with `credit_init`, `tech_init`, `rework_loop`, and `expedite_opt` (bounded between `Register Dossier` and `approval_xor`). Control-flow permits presence or absence; `audit_case_against_policy` enforces biconditional matching once `sourcing_type` is joined.
- **`Expedite Review` Ordering (P4)**: Placed after `{credit_init, tech_init} -> rework_loop` and before `approval_xor`, while completely unordered relative to `supplier_opt`. Thus `Expedite Review` strictly follows both mandatory checks (and any rework re-verification), strictly precedes approval, and freely occurs before or after `Supplier Review`. *(Design note: placing `rework_loop -> expedite_opt` prevents expediting an actively broken dossier mid-correction; if business policy later permits expediting prior to a late correction, replacing `(rework_loop, expedite_opt)` with direct edges `(credit_init, expedite_opt)`, `(tech_init, expedite_opt)`, and `(rework_loop, approval_xor)` relaxes that gate cleanly.)*
- **Exactly-One Approval (P5)**: `approval_xor` enforces a strict XOR between `Manager Approval` and `Auto Approval` immediately preceding `Release Order`.
- **Correction and Recheck Behavior (P6, P7, F6)**: `LOOP(tau, Correct Dossier -> XOR(Cred, Tech, Cred || Tech))` allows zero or multiple corrections. Without `Correct Dossier`, neither mandatory check can execute twice (blocking P7/B8). After `Correct Dossier`, *at least one* mandatory check must repeat—supporting Credit-only, Tech-only (B6), or concurrent both-check re-verification in either order.

### 5. Limitations Caused by Missing Log Attributes
- **Unlogged `sourcing_type` and Missing `Supplier Review` Telemetry (P3, F4)**: On the raw control-flow log alone, `model_third` cannot distinguish (a) a compliant internal order, (b) an external order that illegally skipped `Supplier Review`, and (c) an external order whose `Supplier Review` event was dropped by logging pipelines. The post-hoc layer explicitly surfaces `INDETERMINATE_MISSING_DATA` rather than reporting false certainty.
- **Unlogged `affected_checks` Scope (P6, F6)**: When `Correct Dossier` is followed by `Technical Review` alone (B6), `model_third` confirms structural validity ($\ge 1$ recheck occurred after correction), but cannot verify without `affected_checks` whether `Credit Check` was also affected and improperly skipped. Additionally, if an external correction solely required repeating `Supplier Review` without re-running `Credit Check` or `Technical Review`, `recheck_target` would need an additional `Supplier Review` branch gated by `affected_checks`.

# 9. Validation Experiments

### Evaluation Protocol Design
1. **Reproducible Splits and Published Preprocessing**: Commit deterministic extraction and preprocessing code with SHA-256 log hashes. Evaluate across both a **5-fold seeded case-stratified split** (`seed in [42, 101, 202, 303, 404]`) and an **out-of-time temporal split** (first 80% of cases by `Register Dossier` timestamp vs. final 20%) to detect concept drift.
2. **Attribute-Stratified Holdout Tests**: Join order-header metadata (`sourcing_type`, `affected_checks` from ERP/ticketing tables) on an audited validation sample. Report conformance separately across strata: internal vs. external, straight-through vs. reworked ($1$ vs. $\ge 2$ corrections), and `Manager` vs. `Auto Approval`.
3. **Curated Negative Trace Suite**: Construct a version-controlled suite of $\ge 25$ policy-violating synthetic traces covering every rule P1–P7 (including B3, B4, B8, A2, A4, missing `Release Order`, dual approvals, and internal orders with `Supplier Review`). Measure **Negative-Suite Rejection Rate (Specificity)**—exact zero-cost alignment must reject 100% of hard control-flow violations.
4. **Missing-Event and Duplicate-Event Perturbation Tests**: Starting from clean compliant holdout traces, synthetically drop `Supplier Review` at $5\%, 10\%, 20\%$ rates (simulating F4) and inject unlogged duplicate `Credit Check` events at $5\%, 10\%$ rates (simulating P7). Track fitness sensitivity and diagnostic precision of alignment log/model moves.
5. **Compositional Unseen-Combination Holdout**: Construct a targeted split where individual building blocks (`Tech -> Cred`, `Supplier Review`, `Expedite Review`, `Correct Dossier`, `Auto Approval`) appear in training, but specific valid 3-way combinations (e.g., B5, A1, A3) appear *only* in holdout.
6. **Alignment-Cost Sensitivity Analysis**: Compare uniform unit move costs (`1` for all moves) against a **compliance-weighted cost schedule** (`cost = 10` for model/log moves on mandatory gates `Register`, `Credit Check`, `Technical Review`, `Manager/Auto Approval`, `Release`; `cost = 2` for `Correct Dossier` and `Expedite Review`; `cost = 1` for telemetry-prone `Supplier Review`).
7. **Formal Soundness and Reachability Verification**: Run `pm4py.check_soundness` to verify boundedness, proper completion, option to complete, and absence of dead transitions before any empirical scoring.
8. **Disaggregated Multi-Criteria Reporting**: Report training fitness, holdout fitness, compositional-holdout fitness, training/holdout alignment precision, negative-probe specificity, TBR generalization, arc-degree simplicity, duplicate-label count (explicitly defined as `len([t for t in net.transitions if t.label]) - len({t.label for t in net.transitions if t.label})`), and p50/p95 runtime **separately** without scalar averaging.
9. **Task-Based Stakeholder Comprehension Protocol**: Replace informal 1–5 beauty polls with a blinded, counterbalanced comprehension test ($n \ge 12$ participants across Compliance, Operations, and IT). Participants inspect a standardized diagram of each model and classify 8 test traces (4 valid, 4 invalid) within 10 minutes; score **objective classification accuracy** and time-to-answer alongside NASA-TLX cognitive load.
10. **Non-Compensatory Governance Rejection Rule**: Automatically **reject** any compliance candidate—regardless of fitness, simplicity, or generalization—if it: (a) fails `check_soundness`, (b) accepts *any* critical negative control-flow probe (Specificity $< 1.00$ on P1/P2/P4/P5/P7 hard violations), (c) exhibits compositional-holdout fitness $< 0.90$ on verified valid traces, or (d) achieves $< 80\%$ stakeholder trace-classification accuracy.

### PM4Py Validation Code Skeleton

```python
import time
import random
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import EventLog, Trace, Event
from pm4py.algo.conformance.alignments.petri_net import algorithm as alignments_algo


def traces_to_event_log(trace_dict: dict[str, list[str]]) -> EventLog:
    log = EventLog()
    for case_id, acts in trace_dict.items():
        trace = Trace(attributes={"concept:name": case_id})
        for idx, act in enumerate(acts):
            trace.append(Event({"concept:name": act, "order_idx": idx}))
        log.append(trace)
    return log


def reproducible_case_split(
    log: EventLog, train_ratio: float = 0.8, seed: int = 42
) -> tuple[EventLog, EventLog]:
    cases = list(log)
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    rng.shuffle(cases)
    cut = int(len(cases) * train_ratio)
    return EventLog(cases[:cut]), EventLog(cases[cut:])


def count_duplicate_labels(net) -> int:
    labels = [t.label for t in net.transitions if t.label is not None]
    return len(labels) - len(set(labels))


def build_compliance_cost_params(net) -> dict:
    high_criticality = {
        "Register Dossier",
        "Credit Check",
        "Technical Review",
        "Manager Approval",
        "Auto Approval",
        "Release Order",
    }
    model_cost = {}
    for t in net.transitions:
        if t.label is None:
            model_cost[t] = 0
        elif t.label in high_criticality:
            model_cost[t] = 10000
        elif t.label == "Supplier Review":
            model_cost[t] = 1000
        else:
            model_cost[t] = 2000

    # Log-move costs keyed by activity label
    log_cost = {
        act: (10000 if act in high_criticality else 1000 if act == "Supplier Review" else 2000)
        for act in {
            "Register Dossier", "Credit Check", "Technical Review",
            "Supplier Review", "Expedite Review", "Correct Dossier",
            "Manager Approval", "Auto Approval", "Release Order",
        }
    }
    return {
        alignments_algo.Variants.VERSION_STATE_EQUATION_A_STAR.value.Parameters.PARAM_MODEL_COST_FUNCTION: model_cost,
        alignments_algo.Variants.VERSION_STATE_EQUATION_A_STAR.value.Parameters.PARAM_TRACE_COST_FUNCTION: log_cost,
    }


def evaluate_candidate(model, train_log: EventLog, holdout_log: EventLog, negative_log: EventLog) -> dict:
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    is_sound = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)

    t0 = time.perf_counter()
    holdout_diags = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(holdout_log, net, im, fm)
    elapsed_per_case = (time.perf_counter() - t0) / max(len(holdout_log), 1)

    # Exact zero-deviation check on curated negative suite
    neg_diags = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(negative_log, net, im, fm)
    rejected_negatives = sum(1 for d in neg_diags if d["fitness"] < 1.0)
    neg_specificity = rejected_negatives / max(len(negative_log), 1)

    # Compliance-weighted alignment check
    weighted_params = build_compliance_cost_params(net)
    weighted_diags = alignments_algo.apply_log(holdout_log, net, im, fm, parameters=weighted_params)

    # Generative play-out sanity check for simulation readiness
    simulated_log = pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm)

    return {
        "sound": is_sound,
        "duplicate_labels": count_duplicate_labels(net),
        "train_fitness": pm4py.fitness_alignments(train_log, net, im, fm)["average_trace_fitness"],
        "holdout_fitness": pm4py.fitness_alignments(holdout_log, net, im, fm)["average_trace_fitness"],
        "train_precision": pm4py.precision_alignments(train_log, net, im, fm),
        "generalization_tbr": pm4py.generalization_tbr(train_log, net, im, fm),
        "simplicity_arc_degree": pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm, variant="arc_degree"),
        "negative_suite_specificity": neg_specificity,
        "mean_alignment_sec_per_case": elapsed_per_case,
        "weighted_holdout_alignments": weighted_diags,
        "sample_playout_cases": len(simulated_log),
    }
```

# 11. Dissenting View

### Rebuttal to the Recommendation
Rejecting Model C in favor of a compositional hybrid POWL architecture mistakes regulatory conservatism for technical overfitting. In custom-order fulfillment involving credit exposure and external suppliers, combinatorial generalization is not inherently desirable: just because `Auto Approval`, `Expedite Review`, `Supplier Review`, and repeated `Correct Dossier` cycles are individually valid does not mean their simultaneous combination is safe. Under an allowlist governance philosophy, unseen combinations—such as auto-approving a twice-corrected, expedited external dossier—represent unvetted compound risk that *should* trigger conformance exceptions until compliance formally signs off on the variant. Model C enforces this strict allowlist using a single, self-contained formalism where every permitted end-to-end path can be read line by line by an auditor without reasoning about partial-order concurrency. By contrast, the proposed third alternative splits process semantics across a POWL net and an external Python rule layer dependent on ERP attributes (`sourcing_type`, `affected_checks`) that do not currently exist in the event log. Deploying a dual-engine architecture today adds integration overhead while downgrading unverified data-dependent cases to `INDETERMINATE`, creating manual triage backlogs. Retaining Model C for strict exception gating—while periodically appending approved variants—offers stronger immediate audit defensibility with zero upstream data-engineering prerequisites.

### What Evidence Would Change This Decision
I would reverse my recommendation and endorse a managed allowlist (an expanded, version-controlled successor to Model C) if **three pieces of evidence** emerged:
1. **Interaction Risk Evidence**: Historical credit-default or fulfillment-failure audits show significantly higher loss rates on compound variants (e.g., `Auto Approval` combined with `Expedite Review` or `Correct Dossier`), proving that P2–P6 are *not* risk-orthogonal and that unseen combinations genuinely require manual compliance gating (validating F8).
2. **Low True Variant Entropy**: Stratified full-year log analysis shows that $>99\%$ of legitimate order volume falls into $\le 15$ stable end-to-end trace variants, making explicit allowlist governance operationally cheap without alert fatigue.
3. **Intractable Upstream Data Gaps**: IT architecture confirms that `sourcing_type` and `affected_checks` cannot be extracted or joined within a 6–12 month horizon, leaving the hybrid model's `INDETERMINATE` bucket too large for practical audit triage.
