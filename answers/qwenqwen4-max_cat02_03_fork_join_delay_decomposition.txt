```python
def shapley(e_matrix):  # rows cases, cols branches
    import itertools
    n = 3
    v = lambda S: np.max(e_matrix[:, list(S)], axis=1).mean() if S else 0.0
    phi = {i:0 for i in range(n)}
    for perm in itertools.permutations(range(n)):
        S=set(); prev=0
        for i in perm:
            S.add(i); cur=v(S); phi[i]+= (cur-prev)/math.factorial(n); prev=cur
    return phi
```

# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

**Notation.** Case index `c  {F01…F12}`, branch index `i  {A, B, C}`. All quantities in minutes; timestamps are complete-event (interval) data on 2026-05-19.

| symbol | meaning |
|---|---|
| `arr_c` | arrival |
| `fork_c` | fork_release |
| `s_{c,i}`, `e_{c,i}` | branch start / end |
| `fs_c`, `fe_c` | final_start / final_end |

**Definitions (all exact, no estimation):**

1. Upstream elapsed: `u_c = fork_c  arr_c`
2. Branch queue: `q_{c,i} = s_{c,i}  fork_c` ( 0 by the stated rule)
3. Branch service: `v_{c,i} = e_{c,i}  s_{c,i}` (elapsed interval, *not* pure effort — see §7)
4. Branch path latency: `p_{c,i} = q_{c,i} + v_{c,i} = e_{c,i}  fork_c`
5. Join readiness (AND-join, unlogged, derived): `J_c = max_i e_{c,i}`
6. Fork–join stage elapsed: `FJ_c = J_c  fork_c = max_i p_{c,i}`
7. Branch synchronization wait: `w_{c,i} = J_c  e_{c,i} = FJ_c  p_{c,i}  0`
8. Critical set: `K_c = argmax_i e_{c,i}`; label `k_c = min(K_c)` under fixed priority A  B  C (deterministic tie rule; ties also reported as a set and split 1/|K_c| for frequency counting)
9. Downstream queue: `d_c = fs_c  J_c`
10. Final service: `f_c = fe_c  fs_c`
11. Total flow: `T_c = fe_c  arr_c`

**Exact ledger identity (no double counting):**

`T_c = u_c + FJ_c + d_c + f_c`  and, equivalently, the **critical-path form**
`T_c = u_c + q_{c,k_c} + v_{c,k_c} + d_c + f_c`.

A third valid form anchors on *any* branch `i` (useful for attribution):
`T_c = u_c + p_{c,i} + w_{c,i} + d_c + f_c`. With `i = A`: `FJ_c = 50 + w_{c,A}` in this log.

**Why summing all synchronization waits double counts.** Every `w_{c,i}` is the interval `[e_{c,i}, J_c]`. All three intervals *end at the same instant* and therefore overlap; they are concurrent idle spans, not sequential ones. Algebraically:

`_i w_{c,i} = 3·FJ_c  _i p_{c,i}`

so the naive "add everything" ledger gives

`u_c + _i (q+v)_{c,i} + _i w_{c,i} + d_c + f_c = u_c + 3·FJ_c + d_c + f_c = T_c + 2·FJ_c`.

The overstatement is exactly `2·FJ_c` (mean **122 min**, max **150 min** here). Concrete proof from F07: `w_A = 25`, `w_C = 52`, sum **77 > FJ = 75** — the "waits" alone exceed the whole stage. Only the critical branch has `w = 0`; a noncritical branch's wait is *contained inside* the critical branch's remaining service.

`_i w_{c,i}` is still a meaningful metric — **completed-branch holding minutes** (finished samples/documents parked before release) — but it belongs to an inventory/holding-cost ledger, never to the elapsed-time ledger.

**Assumptions used (labelled):**
- **A1 (AND-join):** `J_c = max` branch end; no partial release, no rework, each activity occurs once. Required because `join_ready` is unlogged.
- **A2 (permission semantics):** `fork_release` is a true earliest-permitted-start; verified `min q_{c,i} = 0`, no negative queues.
- **A3 (resolution):** treat minute timestamps as exact; differences  1 min are not interpreted.
- **A4 (counterfactual locality):** accelerating one branch does not change other branches' service/queue, and does not change upstream `u_c`.
- **A5 (downstream model, two variants):** (i *fixed-lag*) `d_c` stays at its observed value; (ii *congestion-aware*) `fs_c = simulate(J_c, 2 servers) + r_c`, where `r_c` is a case-specific non-capacity lag calibrated from the baseline (§4). Both are reported; they bracket the truth.

- Both B queue (30, tied max) and B service (45, max) are at their observed extremes — the tail is a **B phenomenon**, not an A phenomenon.

**F11** (arrival 10:20, fork 10:40)
- `u = 20`; A `q=0, v=50, p=50`; B `q = 10:5010:40 = 10`, `v = 11:2010:50 = 30`, `p = 40`; C `q = 5`, `v = 18`, `p = 23`.
- `J = 11:30 = A_end`, `FJ = 50`, `K = {A}`; `w_A = 0, w_B = 10, w_C = 27`.
- `d = 11:50  11:30 = 20` (joint max); `f = 20`; `T = 12:10  10:20 = 110 = 20 + 50 + 20 + 20` .
- Interpretation: **no B or C improvement can help F11 at all.** A 20%  `p_A = 40` ties B at 40  `J = 11:20`, saving 10 under fixed-lag; but under the congestion-aware model F11 then waits for F1 until 11:35 and starts at 11:50 anyway  **saving 0** (see §4). Its biggest lever is the 20-min post-join lag.

## Branch comparison

¹ *Influence budget* = total minutes removable across the 12 cases if that branch's completion were made instantaneous, all else fixed (an upper bound, `_c [FJ_c  max_{ji} p_{c,j}]`).

## Why the ranking is B  A  C (and why mean service is the wrong criterion)

- **A has the largest mean service (50 min) but ranks second.** Its variance is *zero*: `p_A  50` in 12/12 cases. A variable that never varies cannot explain flow-time variability; it can only set a **floor**. Pearson r between A service and total flow is undefined (zero variance).
- **B controls the join most often and hardest.** 8/12 sole-critical; mean `p_B = 56.7 > 50`; smallest mean slack (`w_B = 4.3`). r(B_queue, total flow)  **+0.78**.
- **Tail ownership is total.** The five longest cases (F07 130, F12 125, F03/F06/F09 120) are *all* B-critical; every case with `FJ  65` is B-critical. `FJ` p90 = 74.0 and flow p90 = 124.5 are B-driven. Mean `FJ` conditional on criticality: B **65.0**, C 56.0, A 50.0.
- **Queue + service, not service.** On the critical path, mean queue = **21.7 min/case** (35.5% of `FJ`, 18.8% of total flow) versus critical service 39.3 min. B contributes 190 of the 260 critical-queue minutes.
- **C is episodic, not systematic.** C is critical only in F04 and F08, and both are queue spikes (35, 25), not service overruns. r(C_queue, total flow)  **0.17** — C's spikes land in otherwise-fast cases and are usually absorbed by slack (`w_C` mean 30.4).
- **Near-ties / fragility (honest reporting).** No exact ties, but margins  7 min in four cases: F01 (A over B by 5), F10 (B over A by 5), F08 (C over A by 5), F04 (C over A by 7). With ±1-min resolution these labels are safe; with ±3-min process jitter F01/F10/F08 could flip. Criticality *shares* should therefore be read as  B 2/3, A ~1/6, C ~1/6, not as precise probabilities (n = 12).

## Two defensible attribution methods (same ranking, different magnitudes)

| attribution of mean `FJ` = 61.0 min | A | B | C |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Critical-path minutes (winner-takes-all) | 8.33 (13.7%) | 43.33 (71.0%) | 9.33 (15.3%) |
| Shapley value of the max-game `v(S)=E[max_{iS} p_i]` | 21.04 (34.5%) | 28.75 (47.1%) | 11.21 (18.4%) |

Subset values: `v(A)=50, v(B)=56.67, v(C)=30.58, v(AB)=60, v(AC)=51, v(BC)=59.75, v(ABC)=61`. The Shapley view credits A for the **floor it imposes on every case**, even when it is not last. Both methods rank B first; they disagree about *how much* A matters, which is exactly the distinction between "drives variability/tail" (B) and "limits the achievable minimum" (A).

**Structural bound worth stating:** since `p_A  50`, `FJ_c  50` for every case. No B/C improvement, however large, can push the fork–join stage below 50 min or total flow below `u_c + 50 + d_c + 20`.

**Aggregate flow shares (mean 115.25):** upstream 19.58 (17.0%), fork–join 61.0 (52.9%), post-join 14.67 (12.7%), final service 20.0 (17.4%).

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

## Calibrating the downstream station first (needed for CF3 and for masking)

Observation: `final_resource` **alternates perfectly** F1, F2, F1, F2 …  routing looks like round-robin, not discretionary. A work-conserving simulation of the final station (2 servers, 20-min service, FCFS on `J_c`; and, separately, pinned round-robin routing — both give identical results) yields:

- simulated capacity queue = **0 for 11 cases, 5 min for F11** (total 5 min, mean 0.42);
- residual `r_c = observed final_start  simulated start` = 8, 15, 15, 18, 10, 15, 15, 15, 15, 20, 20, 10  ** 171 min, mean 14.25**, all  0.

**Hypothesis H1 (well supported, not proven):** the post-join delay is *mostly not* capacity queueing. Offered load is 12 cases per 190 min of join readiness against a 2×20-min capacity (  0.63–0.67); 11 of 12 `final_start` values sit on a 5-minute grid, and `fork_release` is spaced at exactly 15 min all day. This looks like a **slot-/handover-/release-policy lag**, not congestion. Consequently, "adding F capacity" and "removing the lag" are very different interventions and must be modelled separately.

### CF1 — A service 20% (50  40)
- **Direct benefit:** only where A was critical: F01 (5) and F11 (10). Total 15 min, **1.25 min/case (1.1%)**.
- **Zero-benefit cases: 10/12.** In eight of them B remains critical, in two (F04, F08) C remains critical. A's *entire* influence budget is 15 min, so this intervention already **exhausts** A's leverage — making A even faster is worth exactly 0 additional minutes.
- **Criticality migration:** A drops to 0 sole-critical; F11 becomes an **A/B tie at 40** (handled deterministically: label A, set {A,B}, ½ credit each). B becomes sole-critical in 9, tied in 1.
- **Masking (important):** under the congestion-aware model, F11's join moves 11:30  11:20 but F1 is busy until 11:35, so its simulated queue grows 5  15 and **the whole 10-min gain is absorbed**. Total benefit collapses to 5 min (0.42/case). So CF1's honest range is **5–15 min total**, i.e. 0.4–1.3 min/case.
- **Uncertainty:** per-case savings sd  3.11, SE  0.90  approximate 95% interval **[0.7, +3.2] min/case**, i.e. **statistically indistinguishable from zero** at n = 12 (and cases are cadence-dependent, so this interval is optimistic).
- **Secondary effects:** A finishes earlier  A's holding wait rises in B-critical cases; if A's 20% speed-up comes from shorter assays, quality/rework risk is unmodelled; A resource utilization falls (freeing capacity, see §5).

### CF2 — B queue 50%
- **Direct benefit: 92.5 min total, 7.71 min/case (6.7%)**; 8/12 cases benefit; the tail improves most (max 130  115, p90 124.5  114.5).
- **Zero-benefit cases: F01, F04, F08, F11** — A (F01, F11) or C (F04, F08) already dominates.
- **Efficiency:** 92.5 of B's 120-min influence budget = **77% capture**; per minute of branch-path removed, B queue converts at **0.80 min/min** versus A service at **0.125 min/min** ( 6.4× more leverage).
- **Criticality migration / diminishing returns:** afterwards A is sole-critical in 4 cases, tied in 2, B sole-critical in 4, C in 2; **6 cases sit exactly on the 50-min A floor**. The next minute of B improvement is worth much less than the first.
- **Secondary effect (favourable):** compressing joins also relieves the one congestion event — F11's 5-min capacity queue disappears even though its join time is unchanged, giving **97.5 min** under the congestion-aware model. So B improvements are **not masked**; they are mildly amplified. Re-simulating the new join sequence yields **zero** capacity queue in all 12 cases.
- **Modelling caveat:** halving odd queues produces 2.5-min values below the log's 1-min resolution; rounding each to floor/ceiling shifts the total by at most ±6 min (±0.5/case). Fractional values are retained and flagged.
- **Uncertainty:** per-case sd  6.35, SE  1.83  approx. 95% interval **[3.7, 11.7] min/case**; clearly positive, but wide.

### CF3 — final-station capacity vs. release lag
- **CF3a (add F3, keep the policy lag):** removes only the simulated capacity queue  **5 min total (0.42/case), 11/12 cases unaffected**, tail unchanged. Under H1 this is the *entire* achievable benefit of extra capacity. It would drop F utilization from   0.67 to  0.44 — paying for idle capacity.
- **CF3b (event-driven release, lag  0, keep 2 servers):** **171 min total, 14.25 min/case (12.4%)**, every case benefits, max 130  115. Approx. 95% interval **[11.8, 16.7] min/case**.
- **CF3c (both):** 176 min total = *all* observed post-join delay; the extra 5 min over CF3b confirms capacity is nearly irrelevant here.
- **Bounds rather than a point estimate:** the true capacity-driven share of `d_c` lies between 5 min (work-conserving 2-server model, both routing variants) and 176 min (if F1/F2 also serve unlogged work, or if starts are batched by policy). The data cannot resolve this; §7 specifies the measurement that would.

### Interaction effects (non-additivity)
- CF1 + CF2 jointly = **117.5 min**, versus 15 + 92.5 = 107.5 separately  **+10 min super-additivity** (F01 +2.5, F05 +2.5, F10 +5): when two branches are near-tied, fixing only one leaves the other binding.
- CF2 + CF3c = **268.5 min (22.4/case, 19.4%)**, mean flow 92.9, max 100 — the two levers act on *different, non-overlapping* segments of the ledger, so they are essentially additive.
- **Standardized 10-min sensitivity** (equal effort per branch): B 75, A 15, C 12  the ranking is robust to intervention size, not an artifact of the 20%/50% choices.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

Context for both: `fork_release` occurs **exactly every 15 minutes** (08:10 … 10:55)  the plant is **release-paced at 4 cases/h**, while final-station capacity is 6 cases/h. Neither redesign below raises throughput by itself; they cut flow time and WIP. By Little's law, WIP  ·W: baseline 4/h × 1.921 h  **7.7 cases in system**.

## Redesign 1 — Reserve B's machine slot at fork release + pre-fork kitting (attack `q_B`)

*Target:* the 19.2-min mean B queue, which supplies 190 of 260 critical-path queue minutes and owns 100% of the flow-time tail.

- **Throughput:** unchanged (4/h, set upstream). Do not sell this as a capacity project.
- **Flow-time tail:** the main prize — mean 7.7 to 8.1 min, **p90 10, max 15**; the five worst cases all improve by 10–15 min.
- **Resource utilization:** B *service* is unchanged, so B server-minutes are unchanged; only start times move earlier. Observed B concurrency already peaks at **4** (10:51), so reservation may raise the peak to 5 unless slots are levelled. Estimated _B  37.5/(4×15) = 0.63.
- **New bottleneck:** A. Post-CF2, six cases sit on the **50-min A floor**; A's estimated utilization is **50/(4×15) = 0.83**, the highest in the plant (A concurrency  4 observed at 08:59). Further B gains are worth  27.5 more minutes in total.
- **Synchronization effects:** completed-branch holding falls (mean `w_C` 30.4  22.7; `w_A` 11.0  3.3) — less finished-work inventory and less sample ageing, a genuine side benefit not visible in flow time.
- **Risks:** (i) the queue may be **technician- or setup-bound, not machine-bound** — reservation would then do nothing; (ii) pulling B earlier may steal capacity from unlogged case types; (iii) benefit estimates rest on 12 cases from one day at one cadence; (iv) 2.5-min model resolution.
- **Pilot / measure first:** add `org:resource` and a queue-reason code to B events; log *machine-free*, *operator-free*, *setup-done* separately for 2 weeks; then pilot slot reservation on alternate cases. Success metrics: `q_B` distribution (mean and p90), critical-branch mix, count of cases hitting the 50-min A floor, and `d_c` (to detect masking).

## Redesign 2 — Event-driven release handover instead of the post-join lag (attack `r_c  14.25 min`), *not* a third F resource

*Target:* the 12.7% of flow time between `join_ready` and `final_start`, of which only ~0.4 pp is explained by capacity.

- **Throughput:** unchanged; WIP falls by ·W  4/h × 0.2375 h  **0.95 case** ( 1.5 cases combined with Redesign 1).
- **Flow-time tail:** mean 14.25 min (12.4%), max 15, p90 10.5; **every** case benefits, including F11/F01 where branch work cannot help. Combined with Redesign 1: mean **92.9 (19.4%)**, max **100 (23%)**, p90  99.7.
- **Resource utilization:** F stays at 2 servers,   0.67 at the current cadence. **Explicitly reject CF3a**: a third resource buys  5 min total and drops  to 0.44. If throughput is later raised, F binds at a 10-min cadence (  1.0) and A binds at 12.5 min with 4 slots — so the capacity order is **A first, then F**.
- **New bottleneck / synchronization:** removing the buffer exposes F directly to join variability. Re-simulation of the accelerated (CF2) join sequence produces zero queue with 2 servers, but headroom is thin (peaks of exactly 2 concurrent finals, several back-to-back handoffs at 10:00, 10:20, 11:05, 11:30, 11:50). Pooling F1/F2 into one queue instead of strict round-robin is a free, low-risk hardening step.
- **Risks:** the residual may be a **mandated review/cooling/compliance window or a scheduled committee** (11/12 final starts on a 5-min grid; `d` mode = 15) and simply not removable; or F1/F2 may serve unlogged work, in which case our simulation understates congestion and the capacity conclusion reverses. Also, C producing a continuous release dossier to enable event-driven F is a process/quality change, not just a scheduling change.
- **Pilot / measure first:** log an explicit `release_ready` event and a reason code for every minute between `join_ready` and `final_start`; log F1/F2 occupancy **including external work**; run event-driven start on a pilot lane for 2 weeks; track `d_c`, its 5-min-grid signature, and F queue length. Only if `d_c` proves to be discretionary lag should the change be scaled.

*(Cheap third option, not recommended as a headline: a backup documentation reviewer to kill C's spikes in F04/F08 is worth at most 12 min in total across the day — do it only if it is free.)*

# 6. Reproducible Python

```python
"""
Fork-join delay decomposition, criticality analysis and counterfactuals.
Self-contained: the prompt's table is embedded below.

Custom pandas vs PM4Py:
  * join_ready = max(branch ends), fork-join elapsed, per-branch synchronization
    waiting, critical-branch identity and the max-based counterfactuals are ALL
    custom pandas/numpy calculations. A directly-follows graph (performance DFG)
    flattens parallel branches into one observed interleaving and measures gaps
    between *consecutive* events; it cannot represent an AND-join, so it cannot
    recover join_ready, synchronization waiting, or the critical branch.
  * PM4Py is used only where its semantics fit: per-activity service time from
    interval events (start_timestamp -> time:timestamp), plus a performance DFG
    shown for contrast/inspection.
"""
import io, math, itertools
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

RAW = """case arrival fork_release A_start A_end B_start B_end C_start C_end final_start final_end final_resource
F01 07:55 08:10 08:10 09:00 08:15 08:55 08:20 08:38 09:08 09:28 F1
F02 08:05 08:25 08:25 09:15 08:45 09:25 08:30 08:50 09:40 10:00 F2
F03 08:20 08:40 08:40 09:30 09:00 09:45 08:45 09:03 10:00 10:20 F1
F04 08:35 08:55 08:55 09:45 09:05 09:35 09:30 09:52 10:10 10:30 F2
F05 08:50 09:10 09:10 10:00 09:35 10:10 09:15 09:35 10:20 10:40 F1
F06 09:05 09:25 09:25 10:15 09:55 10:30 09:40 10:00 10:45 11:05 F2
F07 09:20 09:40 09:40 10:30 10:10 10:55 09:45 10:03 11:10 11:30 F1
F08 09:35 09:55 09:55 10:45 10:00 10:30 10:20 10:50 11:05 11:25 F2
F09 09:50 10:10 10:10 11:00 10:35 11:15 10:15 10:35 11:30 11:50 F1
F10 10:05 10:25 10:25 11:15 10:45 11:20 10:30 10:50 11:40 12:00 F2
F11 10:20 10:40 10:40 11:30 10:50 11:20 10:45 11:03 11:50 12:10 F1
F12 10:35 10:55 10:55 11:45 11:25 12:10 11:00 11:18 12:20 12:40 F2"""

BR = ["A", "B", "C"]                      # deterministic tie priority: A > B > C
DAY = pd.Timestamp("2026-05-19")
df = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(RAW), sep=r"\s+", engine="python")
TCOLS = [c for c in df.columns if c not in ("case", "final_resource")]
for c in TCOLS:
    df[c] = pd.to_datetime("2026-05-19 " + df[c])
    df[c + "_m"] = (df[c] - DAY).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0   # minutes since midnight

# ---------- 1. decomposition (custom pandas: AND-join semantics) ----------
df["upstream_elapsed"] = df.fork_release_m - df.arrival_m
for b in BR:
    df[f"{b}_queue"]   = df[f"{b}_start_m"] - df.fork_release_m
    df[f"{b}_service"] = df[f"{b}_end_m"]   - df[f"{b}_start_m"]
    df[f"{b}_path"]    = df[f"{b}_queue"] + df[f"{b}_service"]
ends = df[[f"{b}_end_m" for b in BR]]
df["join_ready_m"] = ends.max(axis=1)                       # J_c = max_i e_ci
df["FJ"] = df.join_ready_m - df.fork_release_m              # = max_i p_ci
for b in BR:
    df[f"{b}_sync"] = df.join_ready_m - df[f"{b}_end_m"]    # overlapping idle spans
df["downstream_queue"] = df.final_start_m - df.join_ready_m
df["final_service"]    = df.final_end_m - df.final_start_m
df["total_flow_time"]  = df.final_end_m - df.arrival_m

def critical(ends_df):
    """Return (label, set, tie count). Ties: fixed priority A>B>C for the label,
    full set retained, 1/k credit for frequency counting."""
    labels, sets, ks = [], [], []
    for _, r in ends_df.iterrows():
        m = max(r[f"{b}_end_m"] for b in BR)
        win = [b for b in BR if abs(r[f"{b}_end_m"] - m) < 1e-9]
        labels.append(win[0]); sets.append("+".join(win)); ks.append(len(win))
    return labels, sets, ks

df["critical_branch"], df["critical_set"], df["n_crit"] = critical(ends)
df["crit_credit"] = 1.0 / df.n_crit
df["sum_sync"] = df[[f"{b}_sync" for b in BR]].sum(axis=1)
df["naive_ledger"] = (df.upstream_elapsed + df[[f"{b}_path" for b in BR]].sum(axis=1)
                      + df.sum_sync + df.downstream_queue + df.final_service)
assert np.allclose(df.naive_ledger, df.total_flow_time + 2 * df.FJ)   # double-count proof
assert np.allclose(df.upstream_elapsed + df.FJ + df.downstream_queue
                   + df.final_service, df.total_flow_time)           # exact ledger
assert (df[[f"{b}_queue" for b in BR]].to_numpy() >= 0).all()

hhmm = lambda m: f"{int(m)//60:02d}:{int(round(m))%60:02d}"
show = df[["case", "upstream_elapsed"] + [f"{b}_{k}" for b in BR for k in ("queue", "service")]
          + ["FJ", "critical_branch", "downstream_queue", "final_service", "total_flow_time"]].copy()
show.insert(8, "join_ready", df.join_ready_m.map(hhmm))
print("== Case-level decomposition (minutes) =="); print(show.round(2).to_string(index=False))
print("\n== Synchronization / double counting ==")
print(df[["case"] + [f"{b}_sync" for b in BR] + ["sum_sync", "FJ", "naive_ledger",
      "total_flow_time"]].round(2).to_string(index=False))

# ---------- 2. final-station simulation (capacity vs policy lag) ----------
def simulate(ready, service, n_servers=2, pinned=None):
    """Work-conserving FCFS; 'pinned' = list of resource labels (round-robin lanes)."""
    ready = np.asarray(ready, float); service = np.asarray(service, float)
    order = sorted(range(len(ready)), key=lambda i: (ready[i], i))
    starts = np.zeros(len(ready))
    if pinned is None:
        free = [-1e9] * n_servers
        for i in order:
            k = min(range(n_servers), key=lambda k: (free[k], k))
            starts[i] = max(ready[i], free[k]); free[k] = starts[i] + service[i]
    else:
        free = {}
        for i in order:
            k = pinned[i]; f = free.get(k, -1e9)
            starts[i] = max(ready[i], f); free[k] = starts[i] + service[i]
    return starts

svc = df.final_service.to_numpy(); J0 = df.join_ready_m.to_numpy()
sim2 = simulate(J0, svc, 2)
sim2p = simulate(J0, svc, pinned=df.final_resource.tolist())
assert np.allclose(sim2, sim2p)          # pooled FCFS == observed round-robin here
df["sim_queue"] = sim2 - J0
df["policy_lag"] = df.final_start_m.to_numpy() - sim2      # residual r_c >= 0
assert (df.policy_lag >= -1e-9).all()
print("\n== Final station: observed vs work-conserving 2-server simulation ==")
print(df[["case", "downstream_queue", "sim_queue", "policy_lag"]].round(2).to_string(index=False))
print("totals: observed %.1f | capacity-explained %.1f | residual %.1f"
      % (df.downstream_queue.sum(), df.sim_queue.sum(), df.policy_lag.sum()))

# ---------- 3. counterfactual engine ----------
def rebuild(ends_df, mode, n_servers=2):
    """mode: fixed_lag | policy_lag (capacity re-simulated + r_c) | capacity_only."""
    j = ends_df.max(axis=1).to_numpy()
    if mode == "fixed_lag":
        st = j + df.downstream_queue.to_numpy()
    elif mode == "policy_lag":
        st = simulate(j, svc, n_servers) + df.policy_lag.to_numpy()
    else:
        st = simulate(j, svc, n_servers)
```

# 7. Limits of inference

**Causality.** Everything here is observational decomposition plus *model-based* counterfactuals. The identity `T = u + FJ + d + f` is arithmetic, not causal; the counterfactual savings are conditional on A4–A5 (locality, no behavioural response, no quality/rework feedback). r(B_queue, flow)  0.78 is consistent with B causing delay but also with a common cause (a shared technician pool, a load wave, a case-mix shift) driving both B and C queues. No intervention, no control group, no case attributes exist in the log.

**Branch resource capacity is not logged.** Only `final_resource` exists. A/B/C capacities can only be **lower-bounded from observed concurrency, assuming non-preemption and no resource sharing**: A  4 (08:59), B  4 (10:51), C  3 (09:47 and 10:32), F = 2 given. Utilization figures in §5 (_A  0.83, _B  0.63, _C  0.45, _F  0.67) are *estimates conditional on those bounds and on the 15-min cadence*; if actual capacity is larger, utilization is lower and the "A is the next bottleneck" claim weakens.

**Queue composition is unidentified.** `q_{c,i}` is a single residual: it may contain waiting for a machine, for an operator, for setup/kitting, for a batch to fill, or a scheduled appointment. This matters decisively — Redesign 1 only works if B's queue is machine-slot-bound. Similarly, C's spikes (35, 25 min) are unexplained; a shared clerk with F, or with B, is a *hypothesis*, not an observation.

**Preemption and effort are invisible.** With only start/end intervals, `v_{c,i}` is *elapsed* time, not processing effort; interruptions, multitasking, and overnight-style pauses are indistinguishable from work. Resource-hour and cost estimates derived from these intervals are upper bounds on effort.

**Zero-variance fields are suspicious.** `A_queue  0`, `A_service  50`, `final_service  20`, `u = 20` in 11/12 cases. This is consistent with dedicated deterministic capacity and fixed protocols — but equally consistent with **imputed/derived timestamps** (e.g., `A_start` written as `fork_release`, or durations rounded to a standard). If `A_start` is imputed, A's queue is not merely zero, it is *unmeasured*, and A's influence budget of 15 min is an artifact. This cannot be resolved from the data given.

**Is the post-join delay a queue?** Not identifiable. Evidence against pure congestion: a work-conserving 2-server model (both pooled FCFS and the observed round-robin lanes) reproduces every final start except F11 (5 min), leaving 171 of 176 min unexplained; 11/12 final starts lie on a 5-minute grid; the release cadence is exactly 15 min. Plausible alternatives — a scheduled release committee, a mandatory review/cooling window, transport/handover, batching, or F1/F2 serving unlogged external work — are all consistent with these timestamps. The honest statement is a **bound**: capacity-driven queueing accounts for between 5 and 176 minutes of the 176-minute total, and CF3's benefit is correspondingly between 171 and 5 minutes. Logging `release_ready`, reason codes, and full F1/F2 occupancy would collapse this interval.

**Upstream is a black box.** `u_c` cannot be split into upstream queue vs upstream service; the near-constant 20 min (and 15 for F01, the day's first case) suggests a fixed slot or protocol, not a measured process.

**Statistical and window limits.** n = 12, one day, one cadence, one shift; no replication, no case mix. Approximate intervals quoted in §4 assume i.i.d. cases, which the deterministic 15-min release violates (probably optimistic). The window is truncated (arrivals 07:55–10:35, last completion 12:40): no right-censored cases are visible, later arrivals and any afternoon regime change are absent, and the observed 12 cases / 285 min  2.5 cases/h is a ramp-up/drain average, **not** the steady-state 4 cases/h implied by the release cadence. Finally, minute resolution means any modelled saving below ~1 min (e.g., CF3a's 0.42 min/case) should be read as "approximately zero", and criticality labels with 5-min margins (F01, F10, F08) are the least robust conclusions in the analysis.
