## 2. Rule Interpretation

### Formalization of RR1–RR6
- **RR1 (Drafter/Approver Separation)**: For every case $c$ (regardless of risk level), let $H(\text{act})$ be the resolved human identity executing $\text{act}$. Conformance requires $H(\text{Draft\_Change\_Request}_c) \neq H(\text{Approve\_Change}_c)$.
- **RR2 (Implementer/Verifier Separation)**: For every case $c$, conformance requires $H(\text{Implement\_Change}_c) \neq H(\text{Verify\_Implementation}_c)$, where both identities are reliably resolved humans.
- **RR3 (High-Risk Approval Authority)**: If $\text{risk}_c = \text{HIGH}$ and $\text{emergency}_c = \text{false}$, then at approval timestamp $t_a$, $H(\text{Approve\_Change}_c)$ must either (a) hold an active standing QM role at $t_a$, or (b) hold a valid QM delegation $D$ such that:
  1. $t_a \in [D_{\text{start}}, D_{\text{end}}]$;
  2. $\text{scope}_c \subseteq D_{\text{scope}}$;
  3. if $D$ requires principal absence, the principal is absent or formally unavailable at $t_a$; and
  4. $D$ is a direct authorized delegation with no prohibited subdelegation.
- **RR4 (Emergency Substitution)**: If $\text{risk}_c = \text{HIGH}$ and $\text{emergency}_c = \text{true}$, `Approve_Change` at $t_a$ may be executed by a standing IC or valid IC delegate (satisfying interval, scope, and no-subdelegation constraints), **provided that**:
  1. the emergency flag and safety rationale are credible; and
  2. a standing QM or valid QM delegate executes `Retrospective_QM_Review` at $t_r$ within **two business days** of the emergency approval/implementation.
- **RR5 (Identity Evidence)**: Non-human accounts (`svc_qms`) provide zero authority or SoD conformance by themselves; conformance requires reliable session evidence (e.g., MFA/FIDO + workstation/badge logs) mapping the event to an authorized human. Known human aliases must be normalized prior to RR1/RR2 checks.
- **RR6 (Suspicion vs. Proof)**: Statistical or network anomalies (repeated pairs, reciprocal low-risk approvals, shared terminals, concentrated handovers) establish investigative hypotheses, not proof of collusion. Finding misconduct requires corroborating evidence (e.g., intent, falsification, credential sharing, or control bypass).

### Specific Interpretive Rules Applied
1. **Counting business days for RR4**: Business days are Monday–Friday excluding site holidays. Because `2026-08-15` (Sat) and `2026-08-16` (Sun) were weekend days and no holidays occurred in the period, `2026-08-11` was Tuesday, `2026-08-12` Wednesday (+1 business day), `2026-08-13` Thursday (+2 business days), and `2026-08-14` Friday (+3 business days). Thus, for an emergency approved/implemented on `2026-08-11`, the two-business-day deadline expired on `2026-08-13` (end of business day 2, or 48 business hours by `2026-08-13 13:00–13:10`). `CHG-505`'s review on `2026-08-14 16:00` (+3 business days) fails RR4.
2. **Action by a principal while on leave**: Standing authority derives from role tenure, not physical presence, so an action proven to be executed personally by the standing QM while on leave does not automatically fail RR3 on formal authority alone. **However**, it triggers two critical consequences:
   - *Identity/compromise risk*: Action during recorded leave creates a red flag for credential sharing; where security cannot exclude credential sharing (`CHG-504`), identity reliability is degraded to **indeterminate**.
   - *Delegation revocation/availability conflict*: Because `D-QM-01` is conditioned on *"while Asha is absent"*, Asha's active VPN session at `09:15–09:40` on `2026-08-13` undermines the premise that she was unavailable for urgent approvals that day (`CHG-506`), unless her intervention is treated as an isolated emergency interruption rather than a return to duty.
3. **Can a later database entry prove an earlier delegation?**: **No—not by itself.** In a GxP/pharmaceutical QMS, an audit-trailed database record created *after* the event (`2026-08-12 09:15` during migration) cannot independently prove pre-event authorization, because retroactive data creation is indistinguishable from backdating. Here, however, `D-QM-01` is corroborated by a **signed PDF timestamped `2026-08-09 18:00`** (prior to the effective interval). Provided the PDF's digital signature and timestamp integrity are verified, the PDF proves the delegation existed before `CHG-501` (`2026-08-11`), while the `2026-08-12` DB record is treated as a delayed migration artifact rather than primary proof.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

Under **RR6**, structural concentration and anomalous logs are diagnostic indicators rather than proof of collusion. Each indicator requires weighing benign operational explanations against adverse explanations and collecting discriminating evidence.

### 1. Repeated Pair Concentration (Clara drafting and Ben approving 18/23 HIGH-risk production changes)
- **Benign explanation**: Clara (Process Engineer) and Ben (Quality Engineer) are assigned to the same production cell, and no pair-rotation policy exists. During Asha's leave (`08-10`–`08-14`) and routine cell specialization, Clara naturally submits the cell's drafts and Ben is the natural local reviewer/delegate.
- **Adverse explanation**: Cozy reciprocity or rubber-stamping—Ben approves Clara's changes with minimal technical scrutiny to clear cell backlogs or conceal inadequate process justifications.
- **Discriminating evidence**: Compare review turnaround times and rejection/rework rates for Clara–Ben pairs versus Asha's 3 approvals; inspect technical review comments, attached test evidence, and deviation/CAPA rates on those 18 changes.

### 2. Reciprocal Pattern (Ben drafting 4 LOW-risk training changes approved by Clara)
- **Benign explanation**: Cross-functional workflow convenience on low-risk training documents: when Ben authors a quality/training SOP update, a Process Engineer (Clara) who executes the process on the floor is a logical operational reviewer, with zero financial gain involved.
- **Adverse explanation**: Quid-pro-quo ("you approve mine, I'll approve yours") circumvention of independent review, trading lenient LOW-risk approvals for lenient HIGH-risk production approvals.
- **Discriminating evidence**: Audit timestamps and comment depth across the 4 training changes; check whether Clara's approvals occurred shortly after Ben approved disputed HIGH-risk changes, and verify whether Process Engineers normally hold approval authority for training documents under SOPs.

### 3. Shared Workstations and Service Account Use (`svc_qms` on `WS-DM-04` in `CHG-503`; `WS-IV-02` in `CHG-509`)
- **Benign explanation**: Shop-floor/lab legacy setup where terminal `svc_qms` stays logged in for efficiency (e.g., `WS-IV-02` where Eva badged and used her FIDO token 2 minutes before verification; or another verifier walked into Diego's area in `CHG-503` without logging Diego out).
- **Adverse explanation**: Deliberate obfuscation of self-verification (**RR2 breach**) by Diego in `CHG-503` using the anonymous `svc_qms` session on his own terminal, or sharing active sessions to bypass independent verification steps.
- **Discriminating evidence**: Door badge-out logs, CCTV, physical test records signed by the verifier, IP/MAC/Active Directory screensaver unlock logs, and FIDO-token assertions at exact verification timestamps (`12:30` for `CHG-503`, `12:00` for `CHG-509`).

### 4. Absence Conflicts (Asha's VPN session on `2026-08-13` in `CHG-504`; `u_bk` sick-leave activity on `08-13`/`08-14` in `CHG-506` & `CHG-505`)
- **Benign explanation**: Conscientious (though poorly controlled) crisis management—Asha logged in briefly from home on annual leave to unblock an urgent operational issue (`CHG-504`); or in `CHG-506`, an IT/admin session or mistaken HR sick-leave start time (`12:00` vs afternoon) caused a time-zone/HR recording mismatch.
- **Adverse explanation**: Credential sharing or unauthorized proxy use—Asha shared her credentials while away, or an on-site coworker (such as Clara or Diego) used Ben's unlocked office workstation/password (`CHG-506`, office IP, Ben denies it) to forge QM approval while Ben was sick.
- **Discriminating evidence**: Office workstation hostname, physical badge swipe logs for Ben/Clara/Diego at `2026-08-13 13:00` and `2026-08-14 16:00`, VPN source IP/device certificate and MFA challenge logs for `u_as` on `08-13 09:15`, and forensic browser/Windows Event ID 4624 logs.

### 5. Credential Compromise and Alias Exploitation (`b.koch` / `u_bk` in `CHG-508`; office IP in `CHG-506`)
- **Benign explanation**: Uncleaned identity migration (`b.koch` legacy SSO vs `u_bk` QMS account) allowed Ben to accidentally bypass QMS SoD validation on a routine training document (`CHG-508`), while an auto-locked/shared kiosk caused an erroneous login attribution in `CHG-506`.
- **Adverse explanation**: Intentional exploitation of dual aliases (`b.koch` + `u_bk`) to self-approve without triggering automated RR1 blocks (`CHG-508`), and deliberate password sharing/insider misuse of `u_bk` from an office desk (`CHG-506`).
- **Discriminating evidence**: IAM/Active Directory login correlation (did `b.koch` and `u_bk` originate from the exact same machine/MAC within 30 minutes on `08-12`?), password-reset/MFA logs for `u_bk`, and interviews regarding how `b.koch` remained active.

        return f"NO_STANDING_OR_DELEGATED_{req_role}", "None"

    d = dl.iloc[0]
    issues = []
    if not d["valid_instrument"]:
        issues.append("PROHIBITED_SUBDELEGATION")
    if d["scope"] != scope:
        issues.append(f"SCOPE_MISMATCH({d['scope']}!={scope})")
    if d["requires_absence"] and not is_on_leave(d["principal"], ts):
        issues.append("PRINCIPAL_NOT_FORMALLY_ON_LEAVE")
    if actor_leave:
        issues.append(f"ACTOR_ON_{actor_leave[0].upper()}")

    if not issues:
        return f"VALID_DELEGATION({d['del_id']})", d["del_id"]
    return "DELEGATION_FAILED:" + "|".join(issues), d["del_id"]

auth_events = df[df["concept:name"].isin(["Approve_Change", "Retrospective_QM_Review"])].copy()
auth_results = auth_events.apply(evaluate_authority, axis=1, result_type="expand")
auth_events["authority_flag"] = auth_results[0]
auth_events["delegation_id"] = auth_results[1]

# RR4 business-day lag check for Retrospective_QM_Review

retro["bus_days"] = [
    int(np.busday_count(a.date(), r.date())) for a, r in zip(retro["approve_ts"], retro["time:timestamp"])
]

print("=== RR1 (Draft vs Approve) Violations (Normalized Aliases) ===")

## 6. Control Improvements

1. **Delegation Creation**: Prohibit email-only or informal delegations (`D-IC-04`). Enforce electronic QMS workflow creation with mandatory principal sign-off, explicit `no_subdelegation` technical locks, and automated validation against parent delegation clauses.
2. **Effective-Time Recording**: Separate `valid_from` / `valid_to` from `record_created_at`, and disallow backdated delegations in production QMS. Any emergency/migration backfill (`D-QM-01`) must require a linked deviation/change ticket and immutable hash/signature verification of the pre-existing offline artifact.
3. **Principal Availability & Leave Integration**: Integrate HR leave/sick-leave feeds directly with IAM/QMS so that: (a) an account on certified sick leave (`u_bk`, `08-13 12:00`+) is suspended from electronic signature rights; and (b) if a principal logs in while on leave (`u_as` in `CHG-504`), the system either requires documented leave-cancellation/emergency override or automatically suspends leave-contingent delegations (`D-QM-01`). Also clarify whether weekend days satisfy *"principal must be absent"* (`D-QM-02`).
4. **Alias Management**: Conduct immediate IAM reconciliation to merge/retire legacy aliases (`b.koch` $\rightarrow$ `u_bk`), enforce a single immutable `employee_uuid` behind all QMS logins, and bind SoD rules (`RR1`, `RR2`) to `employee_uuid` rather than string usernames.
5. **Individual Authentication**: Eliminate shared service accounts (`svc_qms`) for human GxP approvals/verifications. Require per-action hardware FIDO/MFA re-authentication (21 CFR Part 11 / EU Annex 11 compliant electronic signatures) on shared terminals (`WS-DM-04`, `WS-IV-02`) with short idle auto-lockouts.
6. **Emergency Review Enforcement**: Make incident ID (`CI-44`) and safety rationale mandatory structured fields for `emergency=true` changes, enforce exact scope matching (`cooling_system` vs `production_equipment`), and configure automated 24-hour and 48-business-hour escalation alerts to the principal QM and Site Quality Head for `Retrospective_QM_Review` (`RR4`).
7. **Pair Rotation**: Introduce a risk-based reviewer rotation/cooling-off guideline for HIGH-risk changes (e.g., capping consecutive `Draft`–`Approve` pairs within the same production cell at $\le 50\%$ or requiring periodic cross-cell QA review) without implying guilt.
8. **Network Monitoring**: Run scheduled PM4Py organizational mining (handover-of-work, working-together, and after-hours/leave correlation) as a continuous compliance QA telemetry stream, routing anomalies to Quality Compliance for contextual review rather than automated disciplinary flags.
9. **Evidential Retention**: Retain synchronized workstation login/lock logs, VPN MFA/device telemetry, badge entry/exit logs, and shop-floor CCTV for at least the change-control audit retention window so shared-terminal incidents (`CHG-503`, `CHG-506`) do not remain indeterminate.
