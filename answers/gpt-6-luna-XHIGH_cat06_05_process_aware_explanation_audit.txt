## 2. Process-model consistency checks

1. **Define and enforce the prediction cut.** For every case, establish t0 as immediately after the first Coverage Check Completed event, using a reliable event sequence index where timestamps tie. For each feature dependency, check both event/effective time and the time the value became available to the scoring system. Require all source records to be available by t0. Recompute features from a prefix-only view and compare them with production values. A timestamp alone is insufficient if data was ingested later or backfilled.

2. **Check activity order against the normative model.** Build an explicit state model or declarative constraints for registration, coverage, the fast track/evidence cycles, damage assessment, optional parallel investigation, decisions, appeal eligibility, reconsidered decisions, and closure. Align traces and prefixes to this model and report missing, repeated, or out-of-order activities. At the given t0 prefix, the claim is at coverage completion: Damage Assessment or a Documents Requested evidence cycle are feasible next branches; investigation is future-possible after a trigger and can overlap; decisions, appeal, and closure are not yet enabled by the observed prefix. They may be reachable later, but reachability is not present-time evidence. A prefix checker should label activities as observed, currently enabled, future-reachable, or inconsistent rather than treating all events in a completed trace as current state.

3. **Treat sequence summaries as suffix-sensitive.** Generate sequence explanations only from the observed prefix, or clearly define a later landmark at which each pattern is available. Check each pattern occurrence's start and end event against t0. For example, the documents cycle and damage-to-decision edge are feasible process transitions but unavailable here; the appeal-to-decision edge is also unavailable here even though it is semantically legitimate after an eligible decision. No complete-trace DFG or variant can be presented as an early explanation.

4. **Resolve tied timestamps before asserting order.** If events share a timestamp, use a source sequence index or another validated ordering key. Without one, represent their relation as ambiguous/unordered and test plausible orderings against the normative model. Do not infer `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` from storage order alone. Preserve raw timestamps, ingestion timestamps, and sequence metadata so this can be audited.

5. **Track repeated decision occurrences and semantics.** Do not collapse all `Settlement Decision Recorded` events into one Boolean or assume the label is unique. Identify each occurrence, its outcome (Approve, Partial, Deny, Withdrawn), whether it is initial or reconsidered, and its relation to an appeal. Check the constraint that an appeal can follow only Partial or Deny, while permitting a later decision after a valid appeal. This avoids incorrectly flagging a legitimate reconsideration as a duplicate or incorrectly treating a future decision as available at t0.

6. **Use process discovery summaries as diagnostics, not law.** Prefix alignments expose deviations from the normative model; directly-follows graphs and footprints summarize observed ordering and concurrency; declarative checks test explicit conditions such as appeal eligibility. A frequent DFG edge is descriptive, not automatically a required transition, and a rare or absent edge is not automatically impossible. Validate discovered patterns against the supplied process semantics, source-system behavior, and known exceptions. These checks establish consistency and lineage, not causal effects.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

- **Defensible predictive signal.** Registration-frozen `initial_damage_complexity` and prefix-derived `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` are available at t0. A properly repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0` or historically timestamped `initial_reserve` may also be candidates. They can be described as recorded characteristics associated with model risk, conditional on the training data and current model. Their importance does not show that changing the characteristic would change settlement time. Complexity may help stratification but is not necessarily actionable after registration; prior history also deserves proxy review.

- **Process-state or congestion signal.** An as-of `queue_length_at_t0` is a legitimate snapshot of workload, and a prefix-only number of document requests or evidence-cycle state can be a legitimate process signal at a landmark where those events have occurred. These may support operational triage if the definitions are stable and the predictions are useful. Say that the model associates observed congestion or observed rework with risk. Do not say that queue length or rework caused a breach, or that reducing a queue will reduce breaches, without a causal evaluation. At this t0 there is no observed document cycle in the supplied prefix.

- **Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review.** `claimant_region` is known at registration; `claimant_channel` may be, once its availability is confirmed; and repaired prior-claim history may also proxy differences in access, channel, language, or socioeconomic circumstances. Predictive validity can be tested without asserting these variables are causes. Review subgroup calibration and errors, proxy dependence, robustness to distribution changes, intended use, and the relevant fairness constraints. A feature should not be removed solely because it is correlated, nor treated as a legitimate operational lever solely because it predicts.

- **Drift/calendar proxy.** `calendar_week` is known at t0, but its association is entangled with policy and holidays. It can be useful for forecasting under the same operating regime; it is not evidence that a particular week causes delay. Evaluate with temporal holdouts, inspect policy/holiday periods separately, and monitor whether a changed regime invalidates the association. If policy or holiday status is explicitly modeled, document its definition and purpose.

- **Unusable until lineage is repaired.** Complete-case duration, appeal flag, total request count, and full-trace variant are not t0 features. Closure-populated final team, extraction-date employee experience, current mutable risk score, complete-case last role, the nightly same-day document snapshot, and the current prior-claim SQL likewise cannot be used as stated. The `initial_reserve` and `claimant_channel` values need proven as-of lineage. Complete-trace sequence patterns are not prefix explanations; the close-before-assessment pattern also fails the normative order check unless reliable source ordering resolves the apparent tie. These fields may be reconsidered only after historical as-of reconstruction and prefix recomputation.

Predictive validity asks whether a feature improves forecasts on appropriate held-out data. Causal interpretation asks what would happen under an intervention and needs a causal design. Operational actionability asks whether a team can safely change or respond to the signal and whether doing so helps. Fairness review asks who bears errors or downstream effects and whether the use is justified. Passing one standard does not imply passing the others.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Make as-of lineage part of the feature store.** Persist event time, effective time, ingestion/availability time, source version, and transformation for each value. Provide immutable point-in-time joins keyed to each case's t0. Reject future snapshots even when they fall within a clock tolerance, and keep historical team, employee, reserve, and risk-score versions.

2. **Rebuild at the prediction landmark.** Generate train, validation, and inference features from the same prefix-only implementation. For each example, truncate at the first Coverage Check Completed event and include only values available by that cut. Replace complete-case counts, variants, roles, durations, and flags with prefix-derived counterparts only where the meaning is useful and the value is observable. Record the cut and feature lineage with every prediction.

3. **Automate process-conformance tests.** Test state reachability and ordering against the normative process, including evidence loops, optional parallel investigation, appeal eligibility, repeated decision occurrences, and closure. Add unit checks that identify impossible transitions and label future-reachable versus currently enabled activity. Treat timestamp ties without a reliable sequence key as ambiguous. Use alignments, footprints, DFGs, or declarative constraints as diagnostics; review discovered edges against process knowledge rather than converting frequent edges into rules.

4. **Re-estimate and recalibrate after removal.** Retrain after excluding leaked or unresolved features; do not subtract their SHAP values from old scores. Evaluate discrimination and calibration by relevant time periods and process states, and select thresholds for the intended triage use. Report uncertainty and abstain from communicating a probability when required as-of inputs cannot be reconstructed.

5. **Validate explanation stability and fidelity.** On held-out cases, compare prefix-only explanations across reasonable model seeds, nearby landmarks, and small input perturbations. Check that explanation values reconcile with the recomputed prediction, that top drivers are not dominated by unstable correlated substitutes, and that local narratives do not assert a future activity as observed. Version the model, feature definitions, explanation method, and process model together.

6. **Review proxies and operational use.** Measure subgroup calibration, false-positive/false-negative rates, and error severity for region, channel, repaired prior history, and other plausible proxies. Test sensitivity to removing or grouping proxies, without treating correlation alone as proof of invalidity. Decide whether a signal has a justified purpose and whether staff can act on it fairly. Test proposed congestion interventions separately; model attribution is not causal evidence.

7. **Monitor schema and process drift.** Alert on changed event labels, missing sequence indexes, shifts in timestamp ties, feature freshness, as-of join failures, new or broken transitions, changed policy/calendar regimes, and input or calibration drift. Re-run lineage, prefix, process-conformance, subgroup, and explanation-stability checks after schema, process, warehouse, or policy changes.
