## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

January through March are the clean baseline. Metrics are stable, label maturity is 100%, variant mix is steady around 45% V1, 35% V2, 20% V3, and there is no sign of a material process or data-quality break. These months define the pre-incident reference.

April is primarily a schema and pipeline incident, not a sudden business-process collapse. Observed evidence is the renamed diagnosis activity, 17% unknown activity, 14% missing queue feature, 18% unmapped/other variants, and the audit finding that prefixes were built at the wrong event or not built at all. The sharp drop in AUROC and calibration is therefore best explained by corrupted feature generation and wrong prediction timing. A real April operational deterioration is not impossible, but it is not required by the evidence and should be falsified by rebuilding from normalized raw logs.

May is a partial recovery month, not a return to a perfectly clean steady state. Unknown activity falls to 2% and AUROC rebounds to 0.78, which supports the pipeline-incident explanation. But the cached “number of events before diagnosis” feature was not fully recomputed for April-derived records, and duplicate handling was asymmetrical across sources after 20 April. May should therefore be treated as mostly repaired but not pristine until lineage and feature recomputation are verified.

June is a real routing and case-mix change. V2 remote resolution jumps to 52%, overall observed breach rate falls to 0.15, and the remote-fix edge remains operationally stable. This looks like process drift plus covariate drift: the model is now seeing more low-risk remote cases than in the training regime. The evidence does not require concept drift in June because within-variant breach rates remain close to prior levels.

July is the start of a genuine operational deterioration concentrated in parts-dependent work. The supplier issue begins on 10 July, the `Order Part -> Part Received` edge jumps to 65 h, and V3 breach rises to 0.46. Overall metrics worsen only moderately because the shock starts mid-month and label maturity is still 98%. July is therefore the transition from “mix shift” to “real changed risk inside V3.”

August is not just more of June. It combines severe process deterioration and likely concept drift. The parts edge reaches 130 h, V3 share rises to 35%, V3 breach reaches 0.64, AUROC falls to 0.68, and ECE to 0.120 while mean predicted risk stays near 0.19. That pattern is inconsistent with a pure prevalence shift: the same broad path has become much riskier.

September adds a second overlapping shift rather than merely extending August. Supplier-driven V3 risk is still high, but now Partner Z enters with different population characteristics and a heavier V3 mix. September’s 0.31 observed breach rate and 0.66 AUROC are therefore a blend of process deterioration, probable concept drift, population shift, and growing label-maturity bias. It should not be summarized as a single “model drift month.”

October is qualitatively different again. The backlog in the outcome API leaves only 55% label maturity and disproportionately delays the longer, breach-prone paths. The apparent drop in observed breach to 0.16 is therefore not comparable to mature months and should not be read as a true performance recovery. October is primarily an evaluation and labeling problem layered on top of continuing adverse process conditions.

The key reason April, June, August-September, and October should not be treated as one homogeneous failure is that the mechanisms differ. April is feature corruption at the prediction point. June is real process mix change without strong evidence of changed within-path risk. August-September are overlapping operational and population changes with likely concept drift inside V3. October is heavily censored, so the headline metrics are not decision-grade.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

| Test | What to run | Expected under one hypothesis | Expected under competing hypothesis |

Recompute breach rate, AUROC, Brier, and ECE only on cohorts observed at a fixed vintage such as 60 days. | If label delay/censoring is driving October, October’s apparent 0.16 breach rate should rise materially as labels mature, especially for V3 and Partner Z. Performance metrics should change substantially across vintages. | If October genuinely improved, fixed-vintage metrics should stay similar as labels mature and the long-path segments should not fill in disproportionately late. |

| Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month | Compute AUROC, Brier, ECE, and reliability plots for V1, V2, V3, Partner Z, and non-Partner-Z separately for each month. | If June is mostly mix/covariate drift, within-variant performance should stay near baseline and only pooled calibration should move because the prevalence mix changed. | If concept drift is present, within-V3 calibration and possibly AUROC should degrade sharply in July-September even after stratification. |

Stable remote-fix timing with worsening parts-edge timing supports a localized operational problem. | If the issue is mostly a pipeline bug, control-flow anomalies should center on unknown/unmapped labels without corresponding deterioration in true business-time edges after log repair. |
| Conditional outcome-relationship tests on the same path | Within V3 or a tighter path definition, compare breach rates and score calibration across months after adjusting for observed covariates. A logistic model with month indicators and month-by-path interactions is sufficient. | If concept drift exists, July-September month effects should remain large even after conditioning on path, source, and observed covariates. | If only process/covariate mix changed, the month effect should shrink substantially once the comparison is limited to the same path and segment. |
| Population reweighting or transport-style checks for Partner Z | In September, reweight non-Partner-Z cases to match Partner Z’s observed covariate distribution and compare risk, calibration, and residual error. | Feature missingness and lineage audits | Audit monthly rates of unknown activities, missing queue feature, prefix-build success at the diagnosis event, duplicate counts, and the cached “events before diagnosis” feature distribution by source and deployment window. | If data-pipeline failure is central in April-May, these lineage indicators should show abrupt breaks aligned to 1 April and 6 May and then normalize after repair. | If the later deterioration is operational rather than technical, lineage metrics should remain stable from June onward while process and label-maturity monitors worsen. |

These tests also falsify explanations. April’s pipeline story is weakened if rebuilt April remains bad after lineage repair. June’s “just a mix shift” story is weakened if V2 and V1 also show large within-segment degradation. The Partner Z population-shift story is weakened if mature, reweighted within-variant comparisons still show unexplained source-specific risk changes. The October censoring story is weakened only if fixed-vintage evaluation remains stable as labels mature.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

Immediate containment should separate evaluation integrity from model changes. Do not retrain on October. Do not use raw April for training or performance judgment. Freeze any automated retraining trigger until both data-lineage checks and label-maturity gates pass. For live risk management, apply a temporary conservative treatment to likely V3 or parts-dependent cases because the strongest current underprediction risk is concentrated there.

Short-term recalibration is justified only for the cleanly diagnosed parts of the problem. June looks like a recalibration problem more than a full model failure: the routing policy increased low-risk V2 volume, so pooled risk is overstated while discrimination remains near baseline. A small intercept or segment-wise recalibration on mature, repaired data can address that. August-September are different: a single global recalibration is too weak because the conditional relationship inside V3 has likely changed. If an interim fix is needed before full retraining, use segment-specific calibration or score floors for V3 and possibly for Partner Z once maturity-adjusted evidence supports it.

Feature repair must come before any serious model refresh. Recompute April-May derived features from normalized logs, unify deduplication across old and new sources, and add features that speak to the new regime: source/partner, rural distance or a travel proxy, device age, and features that proxy parts-delay exposure or supplier stress. Without those repairs, a new model would simply relearn data defects and miss the main post-July risk drivers.

Full retraining should wait for mature labels from the post-supplier, post-Partner-Z regime and for repaired feature lineage. Safe months now as clean pre-incident reference are January-March. May is usable only after full recomputation verifies that cached and missing features are repaired. June is usable after repair and is useful for learning the remote-routing mix change. July is borderline: it is useful only if split or tagged as an onset month because the supplier issue starts mid-month. August and September are unsafe until label maturity is close to complete and source/path maturation bias is quantified. October is unsafe for both evaluation and training until the backlog clears and maturity normalizes.

A variant-specific or segment-specific approach is justified, but not blindly. V2 remains stable and operationally distinct, while V3 is the locus of supplier-sensitive risk. If likely route or part-dependence can be inferred at prediction time, a shared backbone with variant-aware features plus per-variant calibration is the minimum. Separate models are justified only if stable early segmentation is available and mature training volume is sufficient. For Partner Z, start with explicit source and geography features plus source-level calibration checks; a fully separate model is premature until the labels mature and the residual gap after reweighting is known.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

Use a gated monitoring stack rather than a single drift alarm.

Predictive monitors should track AUROC, PR-AUC if class balance changes materially, Brier, and score-distribution drift by case-start month. Calibration monitors should track ECE, calibration slope/intercept, and reliability plots overall and by V1/V2/V3, Partner Z, and non-Partner-Z.

Label-maturity monitors should track fraction matured by cohort age, median label delay, pending-label counts by month, and maturity gaps by variant and source. These monitors are upstream gates: if maturity falls below threshold or long-path segments are disproportionately pending, downstream predictive alerts should be marked “evaluation not decision-grade,” not converted automatically into retraining actions.

Data-quality monitors should track unknown activity share, duplicate-event rate, prefix-build success at the diagnosis event, missing queue feature, cached pre-diagnosis event-count distribution, and versioned lineage of mapping patches. A data-quality alarm should route to pipeline remediation first. It should quarantine affected months from training and suppress “model drift” conclusions until recomputation is done.

Process-variant monitors should track monthly shares of V1/V2/V3/unmapped variants and first-order DFG shifts. Performance-DFG monitors should track medians and upper tails for `Order Part -> Part Received`, diagnosis to `Remote Fix`, and other operationally critical edges. If these move while lineage is clean, the alert should go jointly to operations and model owners; if only the model metrics move while process metrics are stable, then concept drift or feature insufficiency becomes more likely.

Population monitors should track source mix, Partner Z share, rural-distance distribution, device-age distribution, and a simple source-classifier AUC or population-stability score. These should feed fairness and transport checks, but they should not by themselves trigger a model rollback.

Intervention and deployment monitors should record workflow migrations, mapping patches, supplier incidents, routing policy changes, partner onboarding, model versions, recalibration versions, and feature-pipeline versions. This change log is necessary to align breakpoints with likely causes.

Alert dependencies should be explicit:
1. If label-maturity alarm fires, freeze performance interpretation on affected cohorts and switch to fixed-vintage dashboards.
2. If data-lineage alarm fires, route to feature/pipeline repair and block retraining on affected months.
3. Only when label maturity is acceptable and lineage is clean should persistent within-segment calibration/discrimination degradation escalate to retraining.
4. If process/performance-DFG alarms fire with stable lineage, start operational mitigation immediately even before retraining.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py


def monthly_process_evidence(path="field_service_events.csv"):
    # Adjust these source column names to match the local extract.
    raw = pd.read_csv(path, parse_dates=["event_time"])
    raw = raw.rename(
        columns={
            "case_id": "case:concept:name",
            "activity": "concept:name",
            "event_time": "time:timestamp",
            "resource": "org:resource",
        }
    )

    # Normalize the migrated diagnosis label before any feature or prefix logic.
    raw["concept:name"] = raw["concept:name"].replace(
        {"Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"}
    )

    # Auditable duplicate strategy: collapse exact duplicates on key event fields.
    raw = (
        raw.sort_values(
            ["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "concept:name", "org:resource"]
        )
        .drop_duplicates(
            subset=[
                "case:concept:name",
                "concept:name",
                "time:timestamp",
                "org:resource",
            ],
            keep="first",
        )
    )

    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        raw,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    ).sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])

    case_start = (
        df.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min().rename("case_start")
    )
    df = df.merge(case_start, on="case:concept:name", how="left")

    months = pd.period_range(
        df["time:timestamp"].min().to_period("M"),
        df["time:timestamp"].max().to_period("M"),
        freq="M",
    )

    summaries = []
    for month in months:
        start = month.start_time
        end = (month + 1).start_time

        # Event-calendar cohort: events that happened during the month.
        event_slice = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            df, start.isoformat(), end.isoformat(), mode="events"
        )

        # Case-start cohort: cases that started during the month.
        month_cases = df.loc[
            (df["case_start"] >= start) & (df["case_start"] < end),
            "case:concept:name",
        ].unique()
        case_slice = df[df["case:concept:name"].isin(month_cases)]

        dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(case_slice)
        perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(case_slice)
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(case_slice)
        temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
            case_slice, grouper_freq="M", case_id_key="case:concept:name"
        )

        summaries.append(
            {
                "month": str(month),
                "event_calendar_events": int(len(event_slice)),
                "case_start_cases": int(len(month_cases)),
                "unknown_activity_share": float(
                    (event_slice["concept:name"] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean()
                )
                if len(event_slice)
                else 0.0,
                "variant_count": int(len(variants)),
                "top_variants": sorted(
                    variants.items(), key=lambda kv: kv[1], reverse=True
                )[:5],
                "order_part_to_received_perf": perf_dfg.get(
                    ("Order Part", "Part Received")
                ),
                "diag_to_remote_fix_perf": perf_dfg.get(
                    ("Initial Diagnosis Completed", "Remote Fix")
                ),
                "temporal_feature_rows": int(len(temporal)),
            }
        )

    return pd.DataFrame(summaries)
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

One failure mode is retraining on immature August-October labels. That would teach the model that long, breach-prone cases are safer than they really are, especially in V3 and possibly Partner Z. The mitigation is to impose a training gate based on cohort maturity, not reporting month, and require maturity parity across critical segments.

Another failure mode is recalibrating around the April-May pipeline bug. If the scores were wrong because the prefix was built at the wrong activity and cached features were stale, then recalibration would normalize a broken feature space instead of fixing it. The mitigation is to forbid recalibration or retraining on those months until lineage repair and raw-log recomputation are complete.

A third failure mode is ignoring the genuine supplier-driven concept drift and treating August as only a covariate shift. That would leave the model systematically underestimating breach risk for parts-dependent cases and delay operational escalation. The mitigation is immediate V3-focused monitoring and interim policy overrides, followed by retraining with supply-sensitive features once labels mature.

A fourth failure mode is over-attributing September’s Partner Z gap to unfair model behavior without stratified evidence. Because Partner Z has more V3 cases, different unmodelled covariates, and slower label maturation, a raw error gap is not yet a clean fairness finding. The mitigation is mature, within-variant, reweighted evaluation before any source-specific threshold or model split.

A fifth failure mode is misreading June as a model-collapse month and launching unnecessary full retraining. June looks more like a routing-policy mix change with modest pooled miscalibration than a breakdown of within-variant ranking. The mitigation is to check stratified calibration first and prefer targeted recalibration over wholesale retraining when within-segment performance is stable.

The general rule is to diagnose in layers: first label readiness, then data lineage, then process and population change, and only then the residual model error. Reversing that order creates expensive and avoidable mistakes.
