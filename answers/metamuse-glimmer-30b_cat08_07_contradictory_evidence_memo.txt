## 1. Executive memo

Redesign R delivers real benefits for simple, low-risk installs and harms for apartments, old buildings and business customers. National scale is not justified. Full rollback would discard proven gains in median speed and handovers.

The pilot shows a classic process simplification trade-off: centralising ownership and merging validation/scheduling cut handovers from 34% to 12% and improved median cycle time from 14.2 to 10.8 days. It also degraded control. First-time-right fell 82% to 76%, complaints within 30 days rose 6.1% to 9.8%, manual activation doubled 7% to 15%, second visits rose 11% to 18%, and p90/p95 cycle time worsened 31.0 to 38.5 days and 45.0 to 62.0 days. Visible cost fell to EUR 161 but estimated full cost including complaints/revisits rose to EUR 207 on incomplete data.

Segment analysis explains the conflict. Simple house, low risk 34% of pilot volume gained -6.0 days median, -8.0 days p90, +3pp first-time-right and -1pp complaints. Apartment medium risk 29% of volume lost -8pp first-time-right and +5pp complaints with p90 +12.0 days. Old building permit-sensitive 18% of volume saw p90 +25.0 days and first-time-right -11pp. Process mining confirms loss of control: pilot model fitness 0.94 but precision 0.51, frequent move-on-log Billing start before Activation test, Installation visit repeated without Field survey, missing Permit check for older apartments, and Billing start -> Complaint escalation becoming a new long-tail relation. Coordinator utilization hit 93% in two regions and second-visit team utilization rose 66% to 92%, indicating bottleneck and reduced resilience.

Decision: do not scale R nationally and do not roll back. Keep the accountability gains, suspend the harmful relaxations, and segment the rollout with measurement.

## 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

High fitness but low precision: Fitness 0.94 means observed pilot traces are well explained by the discovered model. Precision 0.51 means the model allows many behaviour combinations not actually intended, e.g., skipped survey, manual activation, second visit and billing start in many orders. The redesign made the happy path easier to follow, but removed constraints so the model now permits harmful variants.

Improved median but worse tail: Merging validation/scheduling and coordinator ownership accelerates the majority of simple cases. Apartments, old buildings and permit-sensitive cases experience missed surveys, missing permits, failed visits and delayed second visits, generating a long tail of Billing start -> Complaint escalation.

Fewer handovers but lower resilience: Handovers fell because one coordinator owns a case. The working-together network became centralised. When a coordinator is absent cases stall because context is not shared, and coordinator utilization at 93% creates queueing.

Lower visible cost but higher rework/downstream cost: Cost reporting excludes downstream complaint handling after week 4. Visible process cost falls due to fewer surveys and handovers. Rework rises via second visits +11% to +18%, manual activation +7% to +15% and complaints +6.1% to +9.8%, raising full cost.

Improvement for simple houses but deterioration for apartments/old buildings: The skip-survey rule and post-billing activation help low-complexity cases where risk is low. For medium-risk apartments and permit-sensitive old buildings the rule omits necessary checks, leading to failed installs, manual activation and billing disputes.

## 4. Decision alternatives

**A. Scale Redesign R nationally**
Advantages: captures median speed and handover gains at scale. Risks: amplifies tail duration, first-time-right loss, complaints, billing-before-activation compliance risk and coordinator overload. Reasonable only if skip rule and activation timing are fixed and risk stratification is reliable.

**B. Roll back to baseline**
Advantages: restores control, precision and first-time-right. Risks: loses proven median improvement and handover reduction for simple cases; forgoes accountability benefits. Reasonable only if safety/compliance risk from billing-before-activation is unacceptable.

**C. Scale only coordinator-ownership, suspend survey skipping and post-billing activation**
Advantages: keeps accountability and handover reduction while removing main rework drivers. Risks: loses some speed for medium-risk cases; requires change management to reintroduce surveys. Reasonable as a conservative step if measurement is limited.

**D. Run controlled regional experiment with better segment/risk measurement**
Advantages: generates causal evidence with proper instrumentation and reason codes. Risks: delays benefit capture. Reasonable when uncertainty about building-risk rule and cost is high.

**E. Hybrid: deploy R only for simple low-risk houses, add controls for apartments/old buildings, measure before wider scale**
Advantages: preserves gains where they are strong, contains harm where it is large, and enables learning. Risks: operational complexity of segment routing. Reasonable as balanced path given current evidence.

## 6. Final recommendation

**Recommended decision: Hybrid segment-limited rollout with controls, not national scale and not full rollback.**

Immediate action:
* Pause survey skipping and post-billing activation for all segments.
* Keep coordinator ownership and merged validation/scheduling for simple low-risk houses where benefits are proven.
* Reinstate mandatory Field survey and Permit check for apartment medium risk and old building permit-sensitive segments.
* Cap coordinator utilization at 85% by adding backup coordinators and re-enabling limited handover for absence coverage.

What to pause: Survey skip rule and activation after billing start nationally.

What to scale: Coordinator ownership and merged validation/scheduling for Simple house, low building risk cases only. Broader rollout of those elements pending evidence.

What to measure next: M6, M3, M4, M2, and M7 within 8 weeks. Run a controlled regional experiment for survey skipping stratified by building risk with randomization.

Threshold for broader rollout: First-time-right  80% and complaints within 30 days  7% for a segment, p90 cycle time not worse than baseline for that segment, manual activation  10%, and conformance rate for Billing start after successful Activation test  95%, sustained for 6 weeks with complete downstream cost data.
