# 6. Additional event data needed

Prioritized data collection plan:

1. **Dispatch-decision log.** Timestamp of each decision, the eligible job set, the selected job, and the ranked candidate list. This is the highest value because most ambiguities are about what was plausibly waiting.

2. **Queue-entry and scan timestamps.** Separate physical arrival, scan time, and queue-admission time. This is needed for QB B05, QC C06, and any boundary case affected by the two-minute scan delay.

3. **Resource state and availability changes.** Exact available/unavailable intervals, including meeting prep, calibration, breaks, and setup. This is critical for QD D-F 11:36–12:00 and QA A1 calibration.

4. **Appointment policy and tolerance per queue or job.** Exact time versus window, early/late tolerance, and whether appointments can preempt FIFO or only reserve capacity. Needed for QA A03/A06, QB B07, and QD D06.

5. **Preemption and resumption policy.** Whether interrupted jobs resume with priority, whether resumed jobs restart, and whether preemption is allowed only for priority 1. Needed for QB B01/B02/B03.

6. **Current estimated remaining time at each decision.** `est_min` at arrival may differ from updated remaining-time estimates used internally. Needed to test SPT in QA, QB, and QC.

7. **Deadline/SLA escalation rules.** Thresholds for “at risk” or “endangered,” and whether late jobs automatically preempt. Needed for QC C05 and C07.

8. **Skill-to-queue routing matrix and resource assignment rules.** Which resources may work which jobs, and whether some jobs are pre-assigned to specific queues despite overlapping skills. Needed for QD.

9. **Policy version or SOP changes.** Any change during the observed interval could explain QC’s mixed SPT/FIFO/deadline behavior.

10. **Override and reassignment reason codes.** Every manual dispatch override should be logged with an owner/reason. This would clarify QB B03 and QC exceptions.

# 7. Final synthesis

The most identifiable queue is **QA**. Its behavior is strongly FIFO, with A03’s 08:20 appointment as the only major deviation, and that deviation is explicitly appointment-tagged.

The least identifiable queue is **QC**. It looks SPT-dominated at first, then appears to escalate for the late priority-1 job C05, then makes an SPT-like choice for C06, then makes a FIFO-like choice for C01 before C07. No single rule explains all decisions, and the two-minute scan delay cannot explain the main SPT violation at 10:32.

The most dangerous claimed policy to infer from these observations alone would be **strict priority scheduling**. A few episodes, especially B02 preempting B01, suggest priority behavior. But across the dataset, many priority-1 jobs—A04, A08, D08, D10—wait behind priority-2 or priority-3 jobs. Priority appears heavily overridden by appointments, FIFO, skill constraints, availability, and possibly undocumented dispatcher interventions. Inferring strict priority from isolated preemptions would produce a misleading process model.
