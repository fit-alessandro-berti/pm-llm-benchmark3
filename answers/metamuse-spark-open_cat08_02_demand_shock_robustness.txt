## 2. Bottleneck-migration analysis
Bottleneck migration logic: utilization >85-90% + median queue >10-12h = unstable queue (Little’s law: WIP explodes). Relieving upstream without downstream capacity moves queue, not removes it. Performance DFG medians show where waiting is injected.

**S1 – Winter Wave migration:**
Primary: Courier intake (7.5h) + Registration (2.8h, 85% util, overtime error risk)  ReceivePre-analytical 29h.
Secondary: Pre-analytical check 96% util, 9.5h queue; Accession correction 91% util, 14.0h; Pre-analyticalTest prep 13h. Only ~88% of checks go directly to prep vs 93% normal; recollection + manual correction double.
Tertiary: Preparation benches 89% util, 11.0h queue; Batch wait cases +55% (7601180) and BatchAssay 22h.
Quaternary: Assay machines 98% util, 18h queue, repeats increase assay load by ~7-8%.
Quinary: Technical validators 101% util (unsustainable, queue infinite in theory), 16h queue; TechnicalMedical 24h.
Final: Medical validators 22h queue; Specialist review 36h; MedicalRelease only 5h – so release is not the constraint.

Migration trap: **I8 automate registration** relieves 6885% registration bottleneck, but directly exposes Pre-analytical 96% and Accession 91%. DFG evidence: RegisterReceive 181209730 flows unchanged, but ReceivePre-analytical queue already 9h. Automating without throttling increases arrival variability into a near-saturated check station  recollection loops increase  clerks then spend time on recollections, negating registration gain. Similarly **I1 strict Urgent-first at prep/assay** accelerates Urgent (currently Urgent bypasses batch wait only 58% in peak vs 74% normal) but fragments schedules and starves Routine/Complex-panel. Routine tolerates batching, but priority starvation already pushes Routine p90 to 120h vs 72h target; stricter priority increases WIP of Routine, which later floods assay when Urgent lulls, creating oscillation. Next bottleneck after I1: Technical validation (101% util cannot absorb prioritized surge) and Medical validation (Urgent/Complex both need it).

**S2 – Assay Degradation migration:**
Primary: Assay M1/M2 98% util, 18h queue. Batch wait becomes holding buffer: Test prepBatch wait 614h, BatchAssay 922h.
Secondary backward: Prep benches block (cannot release, queue 11h), then Pre-analytical 9.5h, then Courier intake 7.5h – upstream appears idle but actually blocked downstream.
Secondary forward: Repeat assay loop 38h + RepeatAssay 1026h doubles effective assay demand per Repeat-after-failure case. If **I3 dedicate M1 to Urgent/Complex, Routine to M2** is applied, M2’s higher failure rate concentrates Routine volume onto the less reliable machine. Evidence: repeats already clustered after M2 usage. This relieves M1 for critical cases (good for Urgent p90) but overloads next bottleneck: Repeat assay queue and Technical validation (repeat cases often go RepeatMedical directly but still consume validation). During high Routine load, M1 would idle while M2 queues >24h – pure capacity stranding.

Forward starvation then surge: While assay queued, Technical validators (101% util) starve, then are flooded with lump batch releases  TechnicalMedical 24h spike  Medical 22h  Specialist 36h. Adding capacity only at assay without WIP limits simply moves the 18h queue to 16h+22h downstream.

**S3 – Specialist Drought migration:**
Primary: Medical validators 93% util 22h queue + Specialist review 87% 36h + HoldMedical 48h. TechnicalMedical share jumps 22.6%42.6%, Hold share 1.7%7.6%. Even though assay queue 18h, the binding constraint for Urgent/Complex is validation.
If **I5 evening medical shift** drains Medical queue, next bottleneck is Specialist review (single Friday absence creates 36h median, 610 cases peak vs 290 normal) and then upstream Technical validation 101% – evening shift can pull more cases into Technical, increasing TechnicalHold 612h. Without Hold relief, Medical drain is temporary.
If **I4 cross-train 2 technical validators to accession correction** is used to relieve Accession 14h queue, it directly creates shortage in Technical validation already at 101% – TechnicalMedical 24h worsens, Medical queue then appears relieved but only because Technical cannot feed it (false improvement). Downstream overload reversed: fixing upstream accession without protecting technical validation moves bottleneck one station downstream, increasing total cycle time because Technical is on the critical path for all types.

**S4 – Pre-analytical Cascade migration:**
Primary: Pre-analytical check 96% 9.5h + Accession 91% 14h + Request recollection 8% share, RecollectionReceive 4260h loop. This is a re-entrant loop: each defect consumes intake, registration, and courier capacity twice.
If **I2 increase batch size 1224 for Routine** is applied in isolation to improve prep efficiency (prep util 6989%), it improves setup but adds Batch wait 14h and lumps assay arrivals. Urgent bypass drops 74%58% already; larger batches without hard exceptions push Urgent into batch delays (Batch wait 614h normal)  Urgent p90 33h worsens  pressure to apply I1 strict priority  starves Routine again  WIP before batch grows  Prep queue 11h becomes 15h+.
If **I6 early defect triage within 2h** is applied, it relieves prep/assay of bad samples (saves 413h Pre-analyticalTest prep + 515h AssayTechnical waste), but next bottleneck is Courier intake + Registration explanation time. Evidence: variant notes recollection loops reduce intake capacity because clerks explain errors. Early triage increases early rejections (short-term Request recollectionCancel surge 290370 already), overloading registration clerks who are at 85% and error-prone after 9h shifts. Without smoothing (I7) and throttling, triage alone moves queue from assay to intake.

You cannot prioritize all case types simultaneously – capacity is conserved. Urgent-first, Complex-panel dedication, and Routine batch efficiency are mutually competing for the same prep/assay/validation pools.

## 3. Robust redesign package
Robust = helps in 3 scenarios, reduces tail (p90) and queue>12h% more than median, reversible, does not assume stable mix. All include explicit downstream protection.

**R1 – Early pre-analytical defect triage + feedback loop (I6, modified) – ROBUST**
What: 2-hour rule: samples with missing/inconsistent metadata, hemolysis, volume, cold-chain breach flagged at ReceivePre-analytical edge (currently 29h) by dedicated triage nurse + vision/checklist, not by validator. Reject/flag within 2h, auto-notify ordering physician via template (reduces clerk explanation time). Pair with I7 smoothing to avoid intake surge.
Why robust: Cuts wasted load across all scenarios. Saves downstream 13h Pre-analyticalPrep, 15h AssayTechnical, and 26h repeat loop for bad samples. Reduces rework 11.8~8% and recollection loop 60h penalty. Evidence: Pre-analyticalRequest recollection 7h peak vs 3h normal – earlier decision avoids 9.5h queue + prep queue.
Downstream overload risk: Increases immediate Request recollectionReceive volume and clerk explanation load (Register 85% util). Mitigation: template automation + cap recollections per clerk per shift; monitor Cancel rate (already 6.3% peak) to avoid defensive cancellations.
Assumptions: ~30% of recollections are detectable early (conservative); physician acceptance.
Fails when: defect is analytical (M2 failure) not pre-analytical – then no benefit; or couriers deliver batched defects (cold-chain) – then triage itself queues.

**R2 – Courier leveling + admission throttling (I7 + I8 throttled) – ROBUST**
What: Renegotiate pickups from 2 spikes (10:00,17:00) to 4 leveled slots (08:30,11:30,14:30,17:30) + cap automated registration: auto-register only when Pre-analytical queue <8h; otherwise hold in digital buffer and release pull-based. Keep manual check for incomplete metadata.
Why robust: Addresses S1 and S4 primary bottlenecks without adding headcount. Intake queue 1.07.5h and Register 0.72.8h are arrival-variability driven. Leveling reduces burst that drives Pre-analytical 96%  sustained overload. Throttling prevents I8’s failure mode (automating into saturated check).
Downstream overload: Leveled evening slot may push registration into overtime >9h error zone  accession errors  Manual correction 14h. Mitigation: short overlap shift 14:00-18:00 for registration, not full overtime; monitor error rate via Manual accession correction share (2.7%4.3%).
Assumptions: Courier contractor can shift; 70% of sites comply.
Fails when: courier delays (weather) compress again – then need scenario policy for delay (see §4).

**R3 – Controlled batching with hard Urgent bypass (I2 limited + I1 soft) – ROBUST**
What: Keep batch size 12, allow 24 ONLY for Routine when ALL conditions hold: Prep queue >8h AND Urgent bypass compliance >70% AND Assay queue <15h. Enforce physical lane separation: Urgent/Complex-panel never batched; Routine batched. Replace strict Urgent-first with **weighted WIP caps**: Urgent : Routine : Complex = 40% : 35% : 25% of WIP before prep/assay, reviewed every 4h. This respects Routine tolerates batching but is delayed by priority rules (variant).
Why robust: Improves prep efficiency (6989% util) without starving Routine to p90 120h or fragmenting assay schedules. Prevents I2’s downside (large batches delay Urgent) and I1’s downside (starve Routine, increase WIP). Evidence: Urgent bypass already degraded 7458%; hard bypass protection restores it.
Downstream overload: Larger Routine batches lump assay arrivals  Assay 18h queue spike  Technical 16h. Mitigation: batch release pacing – max 2 Routine batches/hour into assay, interleaved with Urgent singles; monitor BatchAssay 922h edge.
Assumptions: Bench discipline to enforce lanes; assay scheduler can interleave.
Fails when: Routine proportion drops <25% (S1) – then batching irrelevant; or M2 degraded – lumps amplify repeats.

**R4 – One-person cross-skill buffer, not two (I4-lite) – ROBUST with guardrail**
What: Cross-train ONE technical validator (not two) to accession correction, max 30% time, only when Accession queue >12h AND Technical validator queue <10h for 2 consecutive readings. Rotate weekly, 4-week training already planned. Maintain Technical validation floor capacity 3 validators always.
Why robust: Relieves accession bottleneck (only 2 specialists, 91% util, 14h queue, ManualTest prep 16h) which unblocks many cases, but protects Technical 101% bottleneck. DFG shows accession path only 500420 cases but each blocks downstream test prep for 16h. One-person buffer gives elasticity without collapsing technical validation.
Downstream overload: Drains accession, feeds prep+assay faster  assay 98% overload if not paced. Mitigation: couple with R3 WIP caps; accession release throttled to 10 cases/hour max.
Assumptions: Cross-training does not degrade validation quality; accession work is learnable.
Fails when: technical validator sick leave – buffer must be suspended (rule).

**R5 – Conditional evening medical validation hours (I5-capped) – ROBUST if capped**
What: On-call evening shift 17:00-20:00 for Medical validation, max 3 evenings/week, triggered only (see §4), plus next-morning late start to avoid fatigue. Focus on Urgent+Complex-panel queue (Complex 3.1× need Medical). Do not extend technical validation evening unless medical queue cleared.
Why robust: Targets final bottleneck where queue exploded 222h and HoldMedical 1848h, directly reduces Urgent p90 33h breach. Small capacity addition where utilization 93% unsustainable. Not “capacity everywhere” – single pool, limited hours.
Downstream overload: Drains medical, exposes Release/reporting; increased throughput may increase rework detection later. Also overtime fatigue may increase validation errors  repeats. Mitigation: cap hours, monitor rework rate and Repeat assay 1260 peak; stop if repeat rate rises >13%.
Assumptions: overtime budget, validator willingness.
Fails when: specialist-review absence persists – medical validators wait for specialist input (Hold 36h), so evening work idle.

No package optimizes historical median 38h Routine /11h Urgent alone; all target p90, queue>12h% (54%30% goal), and SLA breach rate. Total WIP caps prevent optimizing one station at expense of next.

## 4. Scenario-specific policies
Each policy has ON trigger (with hysteresis) and OFF trigger, and is disabled otherwise to avoid permanent distortion.

**P-A – Strict Urgent-first dispatch at prep & assay (I1) – CONDITIONAL**
ON when: rolling 24h Urgent p90 >17h (approaching 18h penalty) for 2 consecutive days AND Routine WIP before prep <120 cases AND Technical validator queue <12h.
OFF when: Urgent p90 <14h for 2 days OR Routine p90 >90h OR Routine queue>12h% >40%.
Rationale: Helps S1/S3 for Urgent but starves Routine/Complex (conflict hint). Only when downstream validation can absorb. Otherwise use R3 weighted caps, not strict priority.

**P-B – Large batch 24 for Routine (I2-full) – CONDITIONAL**
ON when: Prep queue >10h AND Assay queue <12h AND Urgent bypass compliance >70% AND mix Routine >45%.
OFF when: Urgent mix >30% OR Assay queue >15h OR BatchAssay median >20h.
Rationale: Only efficient when Routine-heavy and assay not saturated. In S1 (Urgent/Complex surge) keep 12; in S2 (assay degraded) large batches worsen lumps and repeats.

**P-C – Machine dedication M1Urgent/Complex, M2Routine (I3) – CONDITIONAL**
ON when: M2 repeat rate >12% (vs normal ~6.5%) AND M1 availability 100% AND Complex-panel queue >15h median AND Routine proportion <45% for 3 days.
OFF when: M2 repeat rate >18% (then dedicating Routine to failing M2 amplifies repeats) OR M1 degraded OR Routine backlog >150 cases.
Rationale: Relieves M1 for critical cases in S2/S1, but increases repeats if M2 is the degraded machine – evidence higher failure rate. Must monitor Repeat assayAssay 1026h edge. Alternative is load-balance, not dedicate.

**P-D – Outsource Complex-panel overflow (I10) – CONDITIONAL**
ON when: Specialist-review queue >30h median AND Medical validator queue >20h for 3 days AND Complex-panel share >22% AND Urgent SLA breach >15%.
Cap: max 25-30% of Complex-panel volume, only low-risk panels; partner 12h transport vs local HoldMedical 48h + 36h queue = 84h – outsourcing reduces tail despite transport. Require digital chain-of-custody.
OFF when: Specialist queue <15h for 2 days OR transport delay >18h (weather) OR traceability errors >2%.
Rationale: Helps S3 Specialist Drought; 12h delay better than 210h p90 for Complex (peak 210h). Not robust baseline because traceability complexity + Repeat-after-failure risk if outsourced results need revalidation locally.

**P-E – Evening medical validation surge (I5) – CONDITIONAL**
ON when: Medical WIP >80 cases OR Medical queue >15h OR TechnicalMedical median >20h for 2 days.
OFF when: Medical queue <8h for 2 days OR rework rate >13% (fatigue signal) OR next-day validator absence.
Max 3 nights/week, with WIP cap downstream to release.

**P-F – Courier delay response (inverse of R2) – CONDITIONAL for S4-delay**
ON when: Courier arrival variance >3h (delay)  switch from leveling to **holding assay slots**: keep 20% assay capacity idle for 4h window to absorb late surge, delay Routine batch releases (R3) to prevent overtime error zone >9h.
OFF when: arrivals back within 1h of schedule for 2 days.

**Never-on baseline:** **I9 Hold Routine releases until batch report** – not recommended in any scenario. It reduces release work (25h edge) but adds artificial waiting to Routine already at p90 120h >72h target, increases WIP before release, and hides assay/validation bottlenecks. Only consider if Release work becomes >20% of medical validator time (not evidenced).

All policies require explicit trade-off review before activation: which downstream station will be overloaded.

## 6. Residual vulnerabilities
1. **Single-point specialist fragility persists.** Even with R5 evening hours, one specialist unavailable every second Friday + no backup for Hold for specialist review (610 peak cases, 36h median, HoldMedical 48h). R-package cannot eliminate this without hiring; P-D outsourcing only partial. Friday remains predictable breach day for Complex-panel (p90 210h).

2. **M1 single-machine dependency.** R3 pacing assumes at least one reliable assay machine. If both M1/M2 degraded (S2 severe), assay queue 18h  no downstream policy helps; WIP caps will idle prep but not reduce cycle time. M2’s higher failure rate means any policy routing Routine to M2 amplifies Repeat-after-failure 2× assay consumption and night-shift clustering remains unaddressed.

3. **Night-shift quality loop.** Variant shows Repeat-after-failure clustered after night shifts and M2 usage. No robust intervention directly addresses night staffing/competency; triage and batching do not prevent assay repeats. Residual tail risk: 11.8% rework peak understates night-specific repeat rate which may be >20%.

4. **Regulatory/SLA trap: 18h Urgent penalty vs 72h Routine target trade-off is zero-sum at 95%+ utilization.** Weighted WIP caps (R3) mitigate but do not eliminate choice: you cannot prioritize all types simultaneously. During S1+S3 combined, any improvement in Urgent p90 3322h still pushes Routine p90 120~100h, still above 72h target. Historical averages (Routine 38h median normal) are misleading; contracts will still breach in peaks without explicit SLA renegotiation or volume caps.

5. **Vulnerability caused by redesign itself: Throttled automation + early triage creates a “digital buffer” and early rejection surge.** R1+R2 intentionally hold cases in digital buffer when Pre-analytical >8h and increase early rejections. This reduces assay waste but increases visible Cancel rate (already 3.16.3%) and physician friction (recollection explanations). If physicians resubmit without correction, loop re-enters as new case, inflating Register volume (RegisterReceive 9730 peak) and masking true demand. Automation also risks propagating metadata errors downstream faster – bad digital metadata auto-registered then fails at accession 14h queue, now with less human check. Monitoring Cancel% and Manual correction share becomes critical; redesign trades downstream load for upstream administrative load.

6. **Courier leveling fragility to external delay.** R2 assumes couriers can shift slots; winter weather or traffic collapses leveling back to 10:00/17:00 spikes, instantly recreating 7.5h intake queue and 9.5h pre-analytical queue. No buffer stock exists for samples (cold-chain).

7. **Cross-skill buffer burnout.** R4’s one-person 30% cap depends on individual availability; during peak 3-week period technical validation at 101% leaves no slack – activator condition (Technical queue <10h) will rarely be met, so accession 14h queue will persist despite training. Training investment may be idle when most needed.
