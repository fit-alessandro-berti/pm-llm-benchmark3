# 2. Expected temporal signatures

### Tolerance convention

For the matrix, “smaller tolerance” means testing equality with less allowed time mismatch—for example, exact equality versus a one-second tolerance:

- At **exact equality**, equal stored values remain equal under any smaller tolerance.
- A smaller tolerance rejects merely near-adjacent or near-equal timestamps.
- With **interval-censored timestamps**, a smaller tolerance can also reveal distinctions hidden inside a shared coarse bin.

The signatures are diagnostic expectations, not physical proofs. In particular, `queue_arrival` is an upstream event and should not automatically be treated as the time an item became physically ready for the named worker.

| Hypothesis | start equality | end equality | adjacency | overlap | recording lag | source precision | resource identity reliability | queue-arrival pattern | behavior under a smaller time tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Genuine simultaneous batch | Usually equal starts | Usually equal ends if the whole batch completes together | No meaningful adjacency | Strong positive overlap | Usually short, but may be bulk-written | Can occur at 1-second or coarser precision | Must normally identify the relevant cell, fixture, or worker | Arrivals often accumulate shortly before the batch | Equal literal timestamps remain equal. At finer source resolution, true within-batch start order may still emerge. |

# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

### General measurement hazards

The relevant nominal quantities are:

- **Observed waiting:** `start  queue_arrival`.
- **Observed service:** `complete  start`.
- **Naive resource utilization:** the sum of case durations divided by resource count and observation-window length.
- **Union busy time:** the time during which at least one case is active.

When intervals on one resource overlap, summed duration can exceed elapsed time. That can indicate parallel work, incorrect resource identity, grouped timestamps, or a resource-capacity error. It is not automatically a utilization error.

### G1 — Apply label

- **Waiting time:** The observed waits are 2–6 minutes, averaging 4.25 minutes. If 09:00 was a real group release, these waits are genuine time in an upstream or pre-cell queue. If four records were stamped together after processing had already begun, waiting is overstated.
- **Service time:** Every case appears to take three minutes. If the cell labels four items together, this is a meaningful batch service interval. If it labels them individually in a tight sequence, three minutes is the batch-cycle duration, not four independent case services.
- **Utilization:** Summing the four three-minute intervals gives 12 item-minutes in a three-minute window. Treating RBT-7 as capacity one produces an impossible 400%. Using the stated four-item fixture capacity gives `12 / (4 × 3) = 100%` slot utilization.
- **Throughput:** A genuine parallel batch yields four items in three minutes, or 80 items/hour. Four serial three-minute operations would yield only 20 items/hour.
- **Conformance and bottleneck:** The equal timestamps produce apparently perfect three-minute traces, but they do not prove four independent executions. The cell should not be declared a four-worker bottleneck merely because four case intervals overlap; it may be one four-position fixture.

### G2 — Surface inspection

- **Waiting time:** Observed waits are 18, 14, 11, and 6 minutes. These are accurately measured **time to the group start** if the group action really initiated the work. If each case had previously been worked under another representation, they are not valid individual execution waits.
- **Service time:** The apparent durations are 8, 14, 21, and 27 minutes, totaling 70 minutes in a 27-minute first-start-to-last-complete window. If one operator actually inspected cases serially, these intervals include time waiting for preceding inspections and overstate individual service.
- **Utilization:** The summed-duration calculation gives `70 / 27  259%` for one T-B. That is evidence that the timestamps cannot all be exclusive occupancy of one operator, not evidence of superior utilization.
- **Throughput:** The apparent parallel interpretation yields four inspections in 27 minutes, about 8.9/hour. Serial execution of the recorded durations would require 70 minutes, about 3.4/hour.
- **Conformance and bottleneck:** The grouped start creates false concurrent traces for a likely single-operator activity. Parallel-looking traces can conceal T-B as the actual bottleneck. Group start is a legitimate event, but it should not be substituted for case-level execution in a conformance or resource-capacity model.

### G3 — Release review

If 11:02 was also a folder-level administrative event, the waits are overstated; the supplied context supports a common end much more strongly than a common start.
- **Service time:** Observed durations are 28, 20, 13, and 6 minutes, totaling 67 minutes in a 28-minute window. A **hypothesis**, not an observation, is that four approximately seven-minute reviews ran consecutively from 11:02 to 11:30. The data do not identify the true durations.

# 4. PM4Py and pandas analysis

- The tight `merge_distance` exposes exact-boundary or near-boundary patterns.
- The wider distance demonstrates how temporal grouping becomes more permissive.
- `min_batch_size` values of 2 and 5 expose sensitivity to batch-size thresholds.
- PM4Py’s category names are printed as returned by the installed release; they are not translated into physical conclusions.
- All rows are retained, including G6’s zero-duration administrative records and G7’s coarse timestamps. No service duration is imputed.

def diagnose_group(g):
    g = g.sort_values("start_timestamp").reset_index(drop=True)

    starts = g["start_timestamp"]
    ends = g["time:timestamp"]

    total_pairs = len(g) * (len(g) - 1) // 2
    positive_overlap_pairs = 0
    inclusive_contact_pairs = 0

    for i in range(len(g)):
        for j in range(i + 1, len(g)):
            latest_start = max(starts.iloc[i], starts.iloc[j])
            earliest_end = min(ends.iloc[i], ends.iloc[j])

            # Strict overlap: shared elapsed time, not merely endpoint contact.
            if latest_start < earliest_end:
                positive_overlap_pairs += 1

            # Inclusive contact: endpoint touching and point coincidences count.
            if latest_start <= earliest_end:
                inclusive_contact_pairs += 1

    # Sweep for maximum positive-duration concurrency.
    # At equal timestamps, ends (-1) are processed before starts (+1).
    sweep = []
    for start, end in zip(starts, ends):
        sweep.append((start, 1))
        sweep.append((end, -1))
    sweep.sort(key=lambda item: (item[0], item[1]))

    active = 0
    max_positive_concurrency = 0
    for _, delta in sweep:
        active += delta
        max_positive_concurrency = max(max_positive_concurrency, active)

    # Adjacency is meaningful only when the preceding case has positive service.
    adjacent_pairs = 0
    for i in range(len(g) - 1):
        duration_i = (ends.iloc[i] - starts.iloc[i]).total_seconds()
        if duration_i > 0 and ends.iloc[i] == starts.iloc[i + 1]:
            adjacent_pairs += 1

    start_counts = starts.value_counts(dropna=False)
    end_counts = ends.value_counts(dropna=False)

    wait_minutes = (starts - g["queue_arrival"]).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
    service_minutes = (ends - starts).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0
    recording_lag_seconds = (
        g["recorded_at"] - g["time:timestamp"]
    ).dt.total_seconds()

    first_start = starts.min()
    last_arrival = g["queue_arrival"].max()

    return {
        "n_cases": len(g),
        "resource": g["resource"].iloc[0],
        "source": g["source"].iloc[0],
        "documented_precision": g["precision"].iloc[0],
        "precision_seconds": int(g["precision_seconds"].iloc[0]),
        "lifecycle_semantics": "; ".join(sorted(g["lifecycle_semantics"].unique())),
        "start_eq_max": int(start_counts.max()),
        "end_eq_max": int(end_counts.max()),
        "start_eq_groups_ge2": int((start_counts >= 2).sum()),
        "end_eq_groups_ge2": int((end_counts >= 2).sum()),
        "adjacent_pairs": adjacent_pairs,
        "overlap_rate": (
            positive_overlap_pairs / total_pairs if total_pairs else float("nan")
        ),
        "contact_rate_inclusive": (
            inclusive_contact_pairs / total_pairs if total_pairs else float("nan")
        ),
        "max_positive_concurrency": max_positive_concurrency,
        "lag_median_s": float(recording_lag_seconds.median()),
        "lag_min_s": float(recording_lag_seconds.min()),
        "lag_max_s": float(recording_lag_seconds.max()),
        "mean_wait_min": float(wait_minutes.mean()),
        "max_wait_min": float(wait_minutes.max()),
        "mean_service_min": float(service_minutes.mean()),
        "total_service_min": float(service_minutes.sum()),
        "observed_window_min": float(
            (ends.max() - starts.min()).total_seconds() / 60.0
        ),
        "arrival_span_min": float(
            (g["queue_arrival"].max() - g["queue_arrival"].min()).total_seconds()
            / 60.0
        ),
        "arrivals_before_first_start": int(
            (g["queue_arrival"] <= first_start).sum()
        ),
        "first_start_minus_last_arrival_min": float(
            (first_start - last_arrival).total_seconds() / 60.0
        ),
    }

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

## 5.1 Trusted versus shared resource IDs

### If resource IDs are trusted as exclusive single workers

- **G1** requires a capacity exception: one RBT-7 resource has four simultaneous case intervals. The supplied four-item fixture makes this defensible, but the analysis must use four fixture slots rather than one worker slot.
- **G2** becomes implausible as four exclusive T-B occupancies. The grouped-start/serial-completion interpretation becomes more attractive.
- **G3** similarly cannot be four exclusive T-C reviews. Serial review followed by folder submission is the most coherent reconciliation.
- **G5** is impossible as one exclusive worker: 70 work-minutes are recorded in a 30-minute union, with four overlapping cases. Treating `analyst_pool` as one person would create an impossible utilization of approximately 233%.
- **G7** cannot be five exclusive T-G measurements. Coarsening is necessary regardless of how resource identity is trusted.
- **G4** and **G8** remain serial and are little affected by this assumption.
- **G6** remains uninterpretable because zero-duration administrative fields do not become meaningful through a trusted resource identity.

Trusting an ID as the same worker also does not itself establish exclusivity. Multitasking, session spans, and group-transaction fields can still create overlaps.

### If shared accounts are modeled explicitly

For G5, a three-analyst denominator gives an estimated utilization of `70 / 90  78%` during 13:00–13:30. This is a useful bound under the assumptions that:

1. each recorded interval represents one analyst’s work,
2. an analyst is counted at most once in the denominator,
3. the recorded durations are not inflated by queue or session time, and
4. overlapping tasks can be assigned to different analysts.

The four-way overlap means at least one analyst must multitask, or some intervals must include something other than exclusive work. Badge-level data are therefore necessary, not merely a denominator improvement.

## 5.2 Exact timestamps versus interval censoring

### Exact stored timestamps

The exact-value diagnoses are clear:

- G1: equal starts and ends.
- G2: equal starts, distinct ends.
- G3: distinct starts, equal ends.
- G4: serial adjacency.
- G5: overlapping intervals under an unreliable pooled identity.
- G6: equal zero-duration stamps.
- G7: equal quarter-hour outputs.
- G8: serial adjacency after an arrival buildup.

### Interval-censored interpretation

Documented precision is not documented accuracy. Treating a timestamp as an interval weakens literal equality:

- For 1-second sources, equal labels still imply events in the same one-second bin, not necessarily the same physical instant.
- For minute sources, common starts or ends remain common **bins**, but ordering within the bin is unknown.
- Under a simple truncation model, G6’s same-minute start and complete fields imply less than one minute of elapsed time, not zero. Under constant bulk-field semantics, however, the log gives no bound on the underlying work. The source name and all-zero pattern favor the latter interpretation, but do not prove it.
- For G4 and G8, exact adjacency labels at a one-second boundary could conceal a small gap or overlap within that second. This does not materially alter the strong seven-minute or four-minute serial pattern.
- For G7, the floor-start/ceiling-complete interpretation gives a conditional start interval of `[13:45, 14:00)` and completion interval of `[14:00, 14:15]`. It implies 15–45 minutes of possible service and can make waits negative. Because the rule is undocumented, these are conditional bounds, not established facts.
- Minute-level endpoint uncertainty also makes G2 and G3 waits less precise. Their grouped-start and grouped-end diagnoses remain plausible because they are supported by the interface and business context, not by equality alone.

A valid censoring analysis would retain the original labels, attach documented interval bounds, and use interval-censored or partial-order methods where execution order and duration bounds matter. It should not silently replace every event by a point estimate.

## 5.3 Temporal patterns supporting multiple physical explanations

1. **G1 and G2 — equal starts:**  
   The same stored pattern can represent genuinely parallel work, a common setup/group action followed by serial work, or a system writing several cases together. G1’s fixture context raises the plausibility of parallelism; G2’s group button raises the plausibility of a common group action.

2. **G3 — equal ends:**  
   This can represent a real simultaneous group completion, serial reviews closed together, or a folder-level form submission. The explicit submission behavior makes the last interpretation strongest.

3. **G5 — overlapping intervals:**  
   These can represent multiple analysts working concurrently, one analyst multitasking, a shared session remaining open, or a pooled login causing different people’s events to be attributed to one resource.

4. **G6 — equal starts and equal ends:**  
   This can represent a bulk administrative update, several genuinely short actions stamped at one scheduled time, or work completed earlier but backfilled at 17:00. The `manual_bulk_form` source and identical recording minute favor the first explanation.

5. **G7 — equal coarse boundaries:**  
   This can represent a genuinely simultaneous batch, several items processed serially within a 15-minute bucket, or a common group action combined with coarse completion recording.

The temporal detector cannot resolve these alternatives without the operational semantics of the fields.

# 6. Data-collection redesign

The following priorities preserve the existing data while making the necessary distinctions measurable.

## Priority 0 — Establish an execution-event contract

For each source and field, document:

- What physical or administrative event causes the timestamp.
- Whether `queue_arrival` means upstream arrival, local arrival, work readiness, or merely record creation.
- Whether `start` means the user pressed a button or work physically began.
- Whether `complete` means physical completion, review sign-off, form submission, or database status update.
- Whether a lifecycle value may be copied from a group, folder, fixture, or bulk operation to individual cases.

This is the highest-priority intervention for G2, G3, G6, and G7.

## Priority 0 — Capture per-case execution timestamps and pauses

Record, for each individual case:

1. local work-ready or local enqueue time;
2. actual execution start;
3. each pause and resume;
4. actual execution completion;
5. any blocking reason and outage interval.

Do not replace the upstream `queue_arrival`; retain it and add the local readiness event. This allows waiting to be separated into upstream delay, local queueing, assignment delay, and execution delay.

## Priority 0 — Preserve recording time separately from event time

Keep:

- the raw event time supplied by the source;
- the database insertion time;
- an ingestion transaction or message identifier;
- source-system event creation time where available.

This distinguishes delayed recording from late execution and is essential for G2, G3, and G6.

## Priority 0 — Synchronize and characterize source clocks

Use a common time standard and measure the offset between:

- the repair queue and safety system in G8;
- local source clocks and central ingestion;
- shared VDI sessions and badge readers in G5.

Retain the measured offset and uncertainty rather than silently shifting one clock. G8 cannot support precise outage attribution until this is done.

## Priority 0 — Recover individual and physical resource identity

For humans, retain badge identity plus the shared login and session mapping. Do not discard one in favor of the other.

Distinguish:

- employee or analyst;
- login account;
- workstation;
- equipment cell;
- fixture slot;
- physical item.

This is needed to resolve G5 and to prevent G1’s four-item fixture from being treated as four workers.

## Priority 1 — Add explicit group and batch identifiers

Assign separate identifiers for:

- an operational batch of items processed together;
- a group-start UI transaction;
- a folder or review group;
- a bulk database submission;
- a fixture load.

Include every member case and the group start/end. This directly distinguishes operational batches from logging batches in G1, G2, G3, and G6.

## Priority 1 — Declare workstation and resource capacity

For each resource, record:

- number of people or machines;
- number of independently processable items;
- fixture or slot capacity;
- whether parallel processing is allowed;
- whether one person may hold multiple active cases.

Capacity must be usable as a denominator in utilization and bottleneck calculations.

## Priority 1 — Record blocking, downtime, and release events

Capture:

- safety-check start and end in the repair-system clock;
- equipment or staffing outage;
- upstream batch release;
- work resuming after an outage;
- administrative processing windows.

These events distinguish G8’s outage from a scheduled release and prevent G6’s backlog from being confused with a resource’s service duration.

## Priority 1 — Supply numeric precision and rounding metadata

For each timestamp field, record:

- resolution in seconds;
- whether the value is truncated, floored, ceiled, or rounded to nearest;
- the applicable rounding direction;
- timezone;
- whether precision changed over time.

Raw values should be retained even when normalized values are also published.

## Priority 2 — Validate with short operational studies

For a representative interval, compare logged events with direct observation of:

- how many items are actually handled together;
- when each case physically starts;
- whether analysts multitask;
- when tickets are actually closed versus when the form is submitted.

This validation should focus first on G1, G2, G3, G5, G6, and G8.

# 7. Final adjudication

## High-confidence interpretations

The following are high-confidence **temporal or semantic diagnoses**:

- **G1:** a four-case simultaneous stored-time pattern, with physical parallelism made plausible by the four-item fixture. Actual per-item execution remains unconfirmed.
- **G2:** a common group start. Whether the ensuing work was serial or parallel is not high confidence.
- **G3:** a common folder/form completion or submission, not four proven simultaneous completions.
- **G4:** a serial, back-to-back lifecycle pattern with little evidence of bulk stamping.
- **G5:** a shared-account resource artifact. Individual analyst attribution is impossible.
- **G6:** administrative bulk stamping with zero observed service. This is high confidence as a data-semantic diagnosis.
- **G7:** quarter-hour timestamp coarsening.
- **G8:** an arrival backlog followed by a serial drain. The queue pattern is high confidence; capacity loss as its specific cause is only moderate confidence.

## Exclusions or interval censoring

- **G6 should be excluded from conventional execution-waiting, service-time, utilization, and throughput analysis.** Keep it for administrative event counts, queue-arrival analysis, and investigation of the underlying backlog. Do not treat the recorded 17:00 completion as an operational service timestamp.
- **G7 should be interval-censored**, using the actual rounding rule once documented. Until then, treat duration and waiting estimates as ranges or hypotheses, not point estimates.
- **G2 and G3 should initially be analyzed at group or folder level.** Their raw rows should be retained, but individual waiting and service metrics require confirmation of what their start and complete fields mean.
- **G1 should not be dropped**, but it must use four fixture slots rather than one worker slot in utilization models.
- **G5 should be retained after replacing the pooled login with badge/session-based actors and an explicit concurrency model.**
- **G4 is the strongest candidate for immediate conventional queue analysis.** G8 is suitable for within-repair-system waiting and service analysis, but not for precise outage attribution.

## Operational validation required before performance conclusions

- **G1:** establish whether four items really receive concurrent per-item execution.
- **G2:** establish whether the grouped cases are processed serially by T-B.
- **G3:** separate individual review completion from folder submission and validate the individual start fields.
- **G5:** establish who performed each case and whether any analyst multitasked.
- **G6:** establish whether ticket closure work occurred before 17:00 and why the batch was processed then.
- **G7:** obtain the exact start and completion rounding rules.
- **G8:** align the safety and repair clocks and distinguish an outage from a scheduled release.
- **G4:** verify capacity and hidden pauses, although it requires the least semantic repair.

The defensible overall conclusion is that these logs contain several different kinds of batches: **operational parallel work in G1, operational group starts in G2, folder-end logging in G3, serial batching in G4, shared-actor overlap in G5, administrative bulk stamping in G6, coarse timestamp buckets in G7, and queued serial recovery in G8**. Treating all identical or adjacent timestamps as the same physical phenomenon would produce materially wrong conclusions about waiting, service, utilization, throughput, conformance, and bottlenecks.
