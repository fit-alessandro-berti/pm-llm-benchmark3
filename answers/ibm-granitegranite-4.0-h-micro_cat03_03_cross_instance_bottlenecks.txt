## 1. Interaction-graph description

### Node types
- **Job**: J1, J2, J3, J4, J5, J6
- **Machine**: CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1
- **Batch**: B1, B2

### Edge types and qualifiers
- **requires**: Job  Component (e.g., J1 requires C1)
- **contains**: ComponentLot  Component (e.g., L7 contains C2, C5)
- **batches**: Batch  Job (e.g., B1 batches J1, J2, J3)
- **consolidates**: Shipment  Job (e.g., S1 consolidates J1, J4)
- **acquires**: Job  Tool (e.g., J3 acquires FIX-A)
- **releases**: Tool  Job (e.g., FIX-A releases J4)
- **quarantines**: Lot  Component (e.g., Quarantine Lot quarantines L7)
- **calibrates**: Tool  Job (e.g., CAL-3 calibrates TEST-1)
- **packs**: Job  Shipment (e.g., J1 is packed at 13:30)
- **dispatches**: Shipment  Shipment (e.g., S1 dispatches at 19:25)

### Higher-order motifs
- **Motif A – Batch Synchronization**: Jobs J1, J2, J3 batched in B1 are synchronized by OVEN-1. Batch B1 starts at 12:05, delaying all three jobs by 3 hours 55 minutes. This motif illustrates how batch operations create synchronization barriers.
- **Motif B – Tool Contention**: FIX-A is required for J3 and subsequently for J4. The tool is acquired at 09:00 and released at 11:40, causing a 2-hour wait for J4. This highlights tool contention as a significant bottleneck.
- **Motif C – Lot Release Coupling**: ComponentLot L7 is shared by J2 and J5. A quarantine event at 10:20 for C2 and C5 in L7 delays J2 and J5. This motif shows how component-lot release coupling can create delays that are not immediately visible in job-centric logs. 
- **Motif D – Shipment Consolidation**: Shipment S1 consolidates J1 and J4, with J1 packed early (13:30) but J4 only packed later (19:10). The shipment is dispatched at 19:25. This demonstrates how shipment consolidation can delay early-ready jobs, despite low machine utilization.

### Observations vs. Inferences
- **Direct observations**: Direct timestamps and events such as "J1 requires C1" and "J1 is packed at 13:30" are directly observed.
- **Inferred interactions**: For example, the waiting attributable to batch synchronization for J1 and J2 in B1 is inferred from the batch start time and job readiness times. Similarly, the delay associated with FIX-A for J4 is inferred from the acquire-release timestamps.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

### Delay for J1 and J2 in Batch B1
- **B1 start time**: 12:05
- **J1 readiness**: 08:10
- **J2 readiness**: 08:25
- **Waiting time for J1**: 12:05 - 08:10 = 3 hours 55 minutes
- **Waiting time for J2**: 12:05 - 08:25 = 3 hours 40 minutes
- **Counterfactual assumption**: The readiness times are accurate and represent when the jobs could have started processing. If the jobs began processing immediately upon readiness, the waiting times would be significantly shorter.

### Delay for J4 due to FIX-A
- **FIX-A acquisition**: 09:00
- **FIX-A release**: 11:40
- **J4 start time**: 09:50
- **Waiting time for J4**: 11:40 - 09:50 = 1 hour 50 minutes
- **Counterfactual assumption**: FIX-A is the only resource blocking J4. If another resource were available, J4 could start immediately after acquisition.

### Delay imposed on J1 by S1 consolidation
- **S1 consolidation**: J1 and J4 consolidated in S1
- **J1 packing time**: 13:30
- **J1 waiting time due to consolidation**: 13:30 - 08:10 = 5 hours 20 minutes
- **Counterfactual assumption**: If J1 had not been consolidated with J4, it would have been packed earlier, reducing the waiting time.

### Delay due to CAL-3 for J6
- **CAL-3 calibration start**: 16:35
- **CAL-3 calibration end**: 17:20
- **J6 test start**: 17:25
- **Waiting time for J6**: 17:25 - 17:20 = 5 minutes
- **Counterfactual assumption**: The calibration time is the only delay for J6. If the calibration were more efficient or if the test could start earlier, the waiting time would be reduced.

## 4. What flattening misses

### Job Viewpoint
- **What it makes visible**: Individual job timelines, start and end times for each job.
- **What it hides**: Synchronization delays between jobs in the same batch, tool contention, lot release events.
- **Misleading DFG**: Could suggest a lack of bottlenecks because each job individually appears to have reasonable processing times.

### Machine Viewpoint
- **What it makes visible**: Utilization rates and activity sequences for each machine.
- **What it hides**: Waiting times caused by tool acquisition/release, batch consolidation, and shipment consolidation.
- **Misleading DFG**: Might suggest machines are not bottlenecks when, in fact, they are indirectly impacted by tool and batch synchronization issues.

### Batch Viewpoint
- **What it makes visible**: Batch start and end times, batch-related waiting.
- **What it hides**: Individual job readiness times, tool contention, lot release events.
- **Misleading DFG**: May underrepresent the impact of batch synchronization on overall job delays.

### Tool Viewpoint
- **What it makes visible**: Tool acquisition and release times.
- **What it hides**: Tool contention across multiple jobs, batch synchronization delays.
- **Misleading DFG**: Might not capture the full extent of tool contention if not explicitly logged.

### ComponentLot Viewpoint
- **What it makes visible**: Lot release events, quarantine events, and their impact on jobs.
- **What it hides**: Individual job readiness times, batch synchronization delays.
- **Misleading DFG**: Might not clearly show the relationship between component-lot release and job delays.

### Shipment Viewpoint
- **What it makes visible**: Shipment creation, loading, and dispatch times.
- **What it hides**: Individual job packing times, batch synchronization delays.
- **Misleading DFG**: Might suggest shipments are not a bottleneck when, in fact, they can delay jobs significantly.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

### Narrative 1: J1, C1, L7, B1, S1
- **Temporal Precedence**: J1 requires C1, which is contained in lot L7. B1 batches J1, causing synchronization delays. S1 consolidates J1, leading to shipment S1.
- **Resource Feasibility**: C1 is available for J1, and B1 processes J1 without issue. S1 consolidates J1 and J4, showing no immediate resource conflict.
- **Policy Constraints**: B1's batch operation is a policy-driven requirement, ensuring J1 processes with C1. Shipment S1 consolidates J1 and J4, adhering to logistics policies.
- **Causal Speculation**: If B1 were eliminated, J1 could start processing immediately after C1 is ready, potentially reducing overall waiting times.

### Narrative 2: J2, C2, L7, B1, S2
- **Temporal Precedence**: J2 requires C2, contained in lot L7. B1 batches J2, causing synchronization delays. S2 consolidates J2, leading to shipment S2.
- **Resource Feasibility**: C2 is available for J2, and B1 processes J2 without issue. S2 consolidates J2 and other jobs, showing no immediate resource conflict.
- **Policy Constraints**: B1's batch operation is a policy-driven requirement, ensuring J2 processes with C2. Shipment S2 consolidates J2 and other jobs, adhering to logistics policies.
- **Causal Speculation**: If L7 were quarantined earlier, J2 could be released sooner, reducing waiting times in S2.

### Narrative 3: J3, FIX-A, L7, B1, S1
- **Temporal Precedence**: J3 requires FIX-A, which is acquired at 09:00 and released at 11:40. J3 is part of batch B1, causing synchronization delays. S1 consolidates J3, leading to shipment S1.
- **Resource Feasibility**: FIX-A is available for J3, and B1 processes J3 without issue. S1 consolidates J3, leading to shipment S1.
- **Policy Constraints**: FIX-A usage is a policy-driven requirement for J3, ensuring its processing. Shipment S1 consolidates J3 and other jobs, adhering to logistics policies.
- **Causal Speculation**: If FIX-A were available on-demand (not shared), J3 could start processing immediately after acquisition, reducing waiting times.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

### Intervention 1: Reduce Batch Synchronization
- **Target Mechanism**: Eliminate or reduce batch synchronization delays.
- **Expected Benefit**: Shorter waiting times for jobs in the same batch, potentially reducing total job processing time.
- **Affected Jobs**: J1, J2, J3 (B1); J4, J5 (B2).
- **Potential Harm**: May disrupt batch processing policies or increase operational complexity.
- **New Cost/Risk**: Potential increase in resource allocation for batch management.
- **Metric**: Reduction in batch synchronization time.
- **Evaluation Design**: Monitor job processing times before and after batch synchronization intervention; compare with baseline metrics.
