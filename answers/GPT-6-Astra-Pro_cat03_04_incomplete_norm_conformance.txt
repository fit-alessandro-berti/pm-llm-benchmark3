## 1. Conformance semantics

**The norm is a partial contract, not a whitelist.** I separate conformity of the recorded representation from conformity of the underlying business execution.

**Forbidden behavior** requires an occurrence matching an explicit prohibition and its identity/temporal scope. **Missing mandatory behavior** requires an applicable obligation and evidence of nonfulfilment: event-level missing links can be established immediately, whereas eventual obligations require sufficiently complete histories. **Unexpected but unregulated behavior** is absent from the specification, or explicitly unspecified; neither establishes a violation. **Typical-pattern absence** is an exception to a descriptive expectation, not noncompliance.

**Likely data-quality error** concerns incorrect identities, links, timestamps, or extraction. It can produce a genuine defect in the recorded representation without proving forbidden operational conduct. **Abstraction mismatch** occurs when the graph predicate differs from the business predicate—for example, adjacency versus eventual validation. **Insufficient evidence** means scope, ordering, provenance, maturity, or counting units remain unresolved; it is neither acquittal nor conviction.

Closed-world checking applies only to explicitly forbidden relations and scoped mandatory requirements. Everything else remains open-world. Unspecified does not imply approval under another policy. There are no supplied frequency tolerances: rarity cannot establish a violation, and high aggregate coverage cannot excuse an established mandatory omission.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

The relevant observation is succession along an individual object’s projected event history. Such edges do not establish causality, full lifecycle completion, or per-instance mandatory coverage. Moreover, the inspected PM4Py graph-comparison implementation unions activity-pair flows across object types; retain typed edges separately rather than relying exclusively on its raw flow diagnostics. 

**N1 is an eventual-response obligation, not an adjacency obligation.** The illustrative trace `Create Order → Review → Validate Order` satisfies eventual validation while lacking the summarized direct edge. Conversely, an open prefix ending at creation cannot establish that validation will never occur. The two missing direct relations therefore require scope and maturity checks, not automatic breach labels.

The supplied “unrecorded review” explanation needs qualification: if the review is absent from the underlying log while creation and validation are both present in the same projection, it cannot itself remove their adjacency. A review omitted only from the summary could explain the difference.

Counts also need reconciliation. Under a common cohort, one creation per Order, and a single total-order projection, **98 creation-to-validation plus 3 creation-to-review** cannot describe mutually exclusive immediate successors for 100 Orders. Repeated creation records, differing projections/cohorts, or approximate counts are hypotheses—not established explanations. The three reviewed Orders must not automatically be identified with the two missing-validation-edge Orders. Similarly, 72 invoice-path and 83 direct-closure observations need not be disjoint.

For N4, external-shipment scope is established, but strict adjacency versus prior packing remains ambiguous. An intervening scan could satisfy a packing-before-dispatch interpretation without satisfying literal adjacency. Do not dismiss the two gaps until that distinction is adjudicated.

N3 has the opposite danger: `Cancel → Review → Dispatch` would still violate its business prohibition despite lacking the direct forbidden edge. Check all later dispatches for the same Order, with validated package attribution.

### OTG

An interaction edge witnesses related object pairs somewhere in the log—not necessarily at the event where T1 or T2 requires them. PM4Py’s OTG discovery aggregates object-graph pairs by type and relationship, so its weights are not event-level coverage counts. 

Consequently, Package–Delivery weight 200 cannot cure the two Dispatch events lacking Delivery links. Pairs may interact at other events, and one event can involve multiple objects. Likewise, Order–Item weight 1450 cannot prove every Add Item event satisfies T1.

T6 additionally requires **business birth**, not first visibility. PM4Py derives cobirth from objects first encountered in the observed event sequence. Truncated histories or imports can therefore imitate prohibited birth. Verify actual creation events before concluding a process breach. 

Interaction and cobirth are different predicates: ordinary co-participation does not imply prohibited co-creation. The seven codeaths, customer links, and risk-review links remain unregulated here.

### ET–OT

This family exposes activity/object-type associations but does not establish correct identity, causal responsibility, or universal event coverage. The inspected discovery implementation increments weights for event–object relation rows; therefore **1450 minus 1448 is not automatically two events**. Two events with multiple Items could explain the difference without missing Orders; duplicate rows or multiple Orders could alter it further. 

Use distinct event IDs and test required types on each event. The additional context independently establishes two E6 gaps and one E8 gap; those findings do not depend on subtracting aggregate weights. Carrier attributes are potential repair evidence, not typed Delivery relations.

The unlinked payment is compatible with T3’s typical exception but violates E8’s recorded-link requirement unless explicitly classified as unapplied cash. Finance testimony alone does not activate that exception. E10 is a prohibited recorded association whose supplied join explanation strongly favors data corruption over operational misconduct.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

**Mandatory**

* N1 and N4: 98/100 and 198/200 show direct-edge gaps; actual omissions depend on scope, history, and temporal semantics.
* T1–T2 and E1–E8: aggregate edges are supporting evidence, not universal coverage. E6/E8 have established recorded gaps; E2/E3 require event-level checking.

**Forbidden**

* N3’s two observations and T6’s one cobirth become business violations when identity, ordering, and actual creation are verified.
* E10’s one association is forbidden in the recorded model; its likely origin is erroneous linkage.

**Typical**

* N2’s 83 direct closures and N5/T3’s payment patterns are descriptive, not minimum-frequency requirements.
* Four write-offs and fourteen successor-free Invoices are compatible with exceptions; due dates or other policies could create separate obligations.

**Unregulated**

* Invoice intermediates (72), review paths/interactions (3), customer interactions (100), codeath (7), and reopening (1) are not violations merely because unmodeled.
* N6 does not authorize an exception to N3: reopening itself is unregulated, but subsequent dispatch after cancellation remains subject to N3.

## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch

The six APIs below are exposed in the inspected PM4Py source; conformance accepts discovered graphs as well as logs. The sketch retains raw results, applies separate policy metadata, and performs event-level checks. It is not an executed audit of the unavailable underlying log. 

Default comparison thresholds are implementation settings, not supplied business tolerances. In particular, OTG/ET–OT graph fitness does not directly penalize additional edges as explicit prohibitions; a favorable score cannot establish compliance here. 

```python
from collections import Counter
from copy import deepcopy
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Inputs already supplied: ocel, norm_ocdfg, norm_otg, norm_etot.
api_names = (
    "discover_ocdfg", "discover_otg", "discover_etot",
    "conformance_ocdfg", "conformance_otg", "conformance_etot",
)
missing = [
    name for name in api_names
    if not callable(getattr(pm4py, name, None))
]
if missing:
    raise RuntimeError(f"This PM4Py build lacks the required APIs: {missing}")

# Reference graphs use PM4Py's native dictionary/tuple layouts.
# Forbidden edges belong in policy metadata, NOT as zero-weight expectations.
# Do not insert N3/T6/E10 into positive-reference graphs as required edges.
if any(
    w <= 0
    for weights in (norm_otg[1], norm_etot[3])
    for w in weights.values()
):
    raise ValueError(
        "Reference weights must be positive; encode prohibitions separately."
    )

observed = {
    "ocdfg": pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel),
    "otg": pm4py.discover_otg(ocel),
    "etot": pm4py.discover_etot(ocel),
}
raw_diagnostics = {
    "ocdfg": pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(observed["ocdfg"], norm_ocdfg),
    "otg": pm4py.conformance_otg(observed["otg"], norm_otg),
    "etot": pm4py.conformance_etot(observed["etot"], norm_etot),
}
# Preserve these outputs verbatim. Default numerical thresholds are NOT policy.

# Version this registry OUTSIDE the bare graphs. Status, scope, exceptions,
# timing semantics and approved tolerances cannot be inferred from membership.
# Each row: norm ID, family, typed key, status, scope/interpretation.
policy_rows = [
    ("N1", "ocdfg", ("Order", "Create Order", "Validate Order"),
     "mandatory", "New standard Order; eventual validation; deadline undefined"),
    ("N2", "ocdfg", ("Order", "Validate Order", "Close Order"),
     "typical", "Intermediate events permitted"),
    ("N3", "ocdfg", ("Order", "Cancel Order", "Dispatch Package"),
     "forbidden", "Same Order; any subsequent dispatch, not only adjacency"),
    ("N4", "ocdfg", ("Package", "Pack Package", "Dispatch Package"),
     "mandatory", "External shipment; clarify adjacency versus precedence"),
    ("N5", "ocdfg", ("Invoice", "Issue Invoice", "Record Payment"),
     "typical", "Credit terms, write-offs and credit notes permitted"),
    ("N6", "ocdfg", ("Order", "Reopen Order", "Close Order"),
     "unspecified", "Reopening outside this policy"),

    ("T1", "otg", ("Order", "object_interaction", "Item"),
     "mandatory", "Every Add Item interaction"),
    ("T2", "otg", ("Package", "object_interaction", "Delivery"),
     "mandatory", "Every Dispatch Package interaction"),
    ("T3", "otg", ("Invoice", "object_interaction", "Payment"),
     "typical", "Unapplied cash permitted"),
    ("T4", "otg", ("Order", "object_interaction", "Customer"),
     "unspecified", "Separate customer policy"),
    ("T5", "otg", ("Order", "object_interaction", "RiskCase"),
     "unspecified", "Risk review permitted"),
    ("T6", "otg", ("Order", "object_cobirth", "Payment"),
     "forbidden", "True business birth, not merely first observation"),
]

for rule_id, activity, object_type in [
    ("E1", "Create Order", "Order"),
    ("E2", "Add Item", "Order"),
    ("E3", "Add Item", "Item"),
    ("E4", "Pack Package", "Package"),
    ("E5", "Dispatch Package", "Package"),
    ("E6", "Dispatch Package", "Delivery"),
    ("E7", "Record Payment", "Payment"),
    ("E8", "Record Payment", "Invoice"),
]:
    scope = (
        "Every event except explicitly typed unapplied cash"
        if rule_id == "E8" else "Every event of this activity"
    )
    policy_rows.append(
        (rule_id, "etot", (activity, object_type), "mandatory", scope)
    )

policy_rows += [
    ("E9", "etot", ("Manual Compliance Review", "RiskCase"),
     "unspecified", "Legitimate rare interaction"),
    ("E10", "etot", ("Sensor Heartbeat", "Invoice"),
     "forbidden", "Every sensor event"),
]


def canonical(family, key):
    # These OTG relationships are symmetric; do not reverse directed ones.
    if family == "otg" and key[1] in {
        "object_interaction", "object_cobirth", "object_codeath"
    }:
        left, right = sorted((key[0], key[2]))
        return left, key[1], right
    return key


policy = {
    (family, canonical(family, key)): {
        "norm": rid, "status": status, "scope": scope
    }
    for rid, family, key, status, scope in policy_rows
}


def edge_weights(family, graph):
    if family == "ocdfg":
        # Preserve object type even if raw graph comparison pools types.
        # This weight counts event couples, NOT distinct affected objects.
        return {
            (ot, a, b): len(witnesses)
            for ot, arcs in graph.get("edges", {}).get(
                "event_couples", {}
            ).items()
            for (a, b), witnesses in arcs.items()
        }

    weights = graph[1] if family == "otg" else graph[3]
    result = Counter()
    for key, weight in weights.items():
        result[canonical(family, key)] += weight
    return dict(result)


references = {
    "ocdfg": norm_ocdfg, "otg": norm_otg, "etot": norm_etot
}
semantic_graph_rows = []

for family, graph in observed.items():
    weights = edge_weights(family, graph)
    keys = set(weights) | set(edge_weights(family, references[family]))
    keys |= {key for fam, key in policy if fam == family}

    for key in sorted(keys):
        rule = policy.get((family, key), {
            "norm": None,
            "status": "unspecified",
            "scope": "Open-world default",
        })
        present = weights.get(key, 0) > 0
        status = rule["status"]

        if status == "forbidden":
            label = (
                "forbidden_relation_candidate" if present
                else "not_observed"
            )
        elif status == "mandatory":
            label = (
                "aggregate_presence_not_instance_compliance" if present
                else "missing_candidate_requires_scope_and_history_check"
            )
        elif status == "typical":
            label = (
                "typical_pattern_present" if present
                else "typical_absence_not_breach"
            )
        else:
            label = (
                "unregulated_observation" if present else "no_obligation"
            )

        semantic_graph_rows.append({
            "family": family,
            "edge": key,
            **rule,
            "observed_weight": weights.get(key, 0),
            "classification": label,
        })

# Event-level checks use the event table, not a difference between edge weights.
# Relation presence does not prove correct business identity or join provenance.
eid, activity = ocel.event_id_column, ocel.event_activity
otype = ocel.object_type_column

if ocel.events[eid].isna().any() or ocel.events[eid].duplicated().any():
    raise ValueError("Event IDs must be unique and non-null for scoped checks.")

present_types = ocel.relations.groupby(eid)[otype].agg(set).to_dict()

# None are explicitly typed as unapplied cash in the supplied snapshot.
# Populate only from an authoritative exception field in a future implementation.
explicit_unapplied_ids = set()
event_checks = []

for event_id, event_activity in ocel.events[[eid, activity]].itertuples(
    index=False, name=None
):
    seen = present_types.get(event_id, set())

    for rid, family, key, status, scope in policy_rows:
        if family != "etot" or key[0] != event_activity:
            continue
        if rid == "E8" and event_id in explicit_unapplied_ids:
            continue

        absent_required = status == "mandatory" and key[1] not in seen
        forbidden_present = status == "forbidden" and key[1] in seen

        if absent_required or forbidden_present:
            event_checks.append({
                "event_id": event_id,
                "norm": rid,
                "classification": (
                    "recorded_mandatory_link_missing" if absent_required
                    else "recorded_forbidden_link_present"
                ),
                "business_verdict": "unresolved_pending_provenance",
                "incident_key": ("event", event_id),
            })

    # Check interaction scope at the triggering event, not anywhere in the OTG.
    for rid, trigger, required in [
        ("T1", "Add Item", {"Order", "Item"}),
        ("T2", "Dispatch Package", {"Package", "Delivery"}),
    ]:
        if event_activity == trigger and not required <= seen:
            event_checks.append({
                "event_id": event_id,
                "norm": rid,
                "classification": "scoped_interaction_witness_missing",
                "missing_types": sorted(required - seen),
                "business_verdict": "unresolved_pending_provenance",
                # Correlate E6/T2, for example; do not double-count incidents.
                "incident_key": ("event", event_id),
            })

# Deliberately unresolved: the four inputs do not define these business semantics.
review_queue = {
    "N1": (
        "Test eventual validation per standard Order; "
        "verify completeness/maturity."
    ),
    "N3": (
        "Test all post-cancellation dispatches with validated "
        "Order-Package attribution."
    ),
    "N4": (
        "Test each external Package's history; "
        "adjudicate adjacency versus precedence."
    ),
    "T6": (
        "Compare first-observed events with authoritative "
        "business-creation events."
    ),
    "lineage": (
        "Validate references, identities, duplicate relations "
        "and extraction windows."
    ),
    "frequency": (
        "No approved frequency bands: raw measure flags are descriptive only."
    ),
}

audit = {
    "pm4py_version": getattr(pm4py, "__version__", "unknown"),
    "policy_source": "Supplied partial specification",
    "observed_graphs": observed,
    "raw_diagnostics": raw_diagnostics,  # Unmodified PM4Py results.
    "policy": deepcopy(policy),
    "semantic_graph_diagnostics": pd.DataFrame(semantic_graph_rows),
    "event_diagnostics": pd.DataFrame(event_checks),
    "review_queue": review_queue,
}
```

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 scope and maturity — mandatory.** Undefined “standard” Orders and validation timing risk false breach declarations. Define applicable Order attributes, the response deadline or completion criterion, and pending-prefix treatment.
2. **N4 timing — mandatory.** Ambiguous adjacency risks either unnecessary alarms or missed unpacked shipments. Specify whether prior packing suffices, how intervening scans are treated, and whether repacking changes applicability.
3. **N3 attribution and cancellation effectiveness — forbidden.** Shared Packages and timestamp ties risk wrongful blocking or missed breaches. Define effective cancellation ordering, fulfilment attribution, and explicitly whether any reauthorization exception exists; none can currently be presumed.
4. **T6 business-birth semantics — forbidden.** First-observed events can falsely imply impossible creation. Tie the prohibition to authoritative creation transactions and identify incomplete-history cases as unresolved.
5. **T1–T2/E1–E8 cardinality — mandatory.** Aggregate totals can conceal missing participation. Specify at least one valid required object per applicable event, acceptable batching, identity checks, and relationship qualifiers.
6. **E8 unapplied-cash handling — mandatory.** Informal exceptions undermine consistent reconciliation. Require an explicit, evidenced classification and defined ownership of subsequent allocation; never fabricate Invoice links.
7. **N2/N5 frequency interpretation — typical.** Unspecified denominators invite unsupported statistical alarms. Define comparable cohorts, credit-term maturity, and descriptive baselines without silently converting them into hard quotas.
8. **Policy boundaries — explicitly unspecified.** Unclear ownership invites overreach. Document the handoff for T4/T5/E9, reopening, and codeath; state which separate policies, if any, govern them.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Immediate containment.** Prioritize N3’s two Orders: verify cancellation status and prevent further affected dispatches while attribution is checked. Prioritize T6’s pair through transaction-level exception review; do not freeze unrelated payments based on observed cobirth alone. Separately quarantine E10’s suspect linkage from downstream use while preserving evidence.

**Data repair.** Repair the reused-identifier join using source-qualified identities and inspect its wider impact. Restore Delivery links only from verified source mappings. Resolve the unlinked payment through genuine allocation evidence or documented unapplied-cash classification. Preserve original records, correction provenance, and before/after graphs.

**Process investigation.** Retrieve complete histories for the cancellation, cobirth, packing, and validation candidates. Reconcile creation-count inconsistencies and inspect Add Item events individually. Distinguish absent execution from absent recording before assigning operational responsibility.

**Norm revision.** Obtain owner approval for the clarifications above, version the policy metadata separately from graphs, and record which version governs each audit. Do not retrospectively redefine rules simply to improve fitness.

**Monitoring.** Track confirmed forbidden incidents, scoped mandatory coverage, pending obligations, and extraction defects separately. Correlate findings across model families to avoid counting one missing Delivery link twice. Monitor the fourteen Invoices against actual credit terms. Retain rare reviews, codeaths, and reopening as descriptive observations unless another applicable policy supplies grounds for escalation.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

Robust conclusions are the explicit prohibitions, the two recorded E6 gaps, the one recorded E8 gap, and the absence of grounds to condemn unregulated behavior. N1/N4 judgments depend on scope, temporal semantics, and completeness. E10 strongly implicates data quality; T6 may reflect observed rather than actual birth. Graph summaries cannot settle precise event coverage, identity, chronology, authorization, financial impact, or the business causes of the discrepancies.
