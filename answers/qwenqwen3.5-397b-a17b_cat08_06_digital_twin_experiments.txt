# Simulation-Based Digital Twin Design for Building Permit Process

## 1. Model scope and state variables

**Boundary:**
The digital twin covers the end-to-end permit lifecycle from **Submit application** to **Issue permit/Reject**. It excludes post-permit construction monitoring but includes the "Public-notice period" as a process activity with external dependencies.

**Level of Detail:**
Discrete Event Simulation (DES) with minute-level granularity. This resolution is required to capture resource calendar interruptions (e.g., council meetings, part-time shifts) and inspection travel time batching.

**Entities:**
*   **Case (Application):** Attributes include `case_id`, `case_type`, `district`, `architect_id`, `application_value`, `requires_fire`, `requires_environment`, `submission_timestamp`.
*   **Document Packet:** State variable tracking completeness (Pass/Fail/Pending).
*   **Objection Event:** Virtual entity generated probabilistically during Public Notice to extend duration.

**State Variables:**
*   **Queues:** FIFO queues for each activity (e.g., `Queue_Planning`, `Queue_Inspection`). Priority queues for "Aging Cases" (Intervention D6).
*   **Resource Status:** For each resource unit (e.g., `Inspector_1`): {Idle, Busy, Off-shift, On-leave}.
*   **Calendar State:** Global clock tracking holidays, council weeks, and peak seasons.
*   **Batching Buffer:** A holding state for Inspection Scheduling to accumulate cases by `district` and `architect_id` before dispatching (Intervention D3).

**Resources & Calendars:**
*   **Pools:** Clerks (5), Planners (8), Fire (2), Env (3), Inspectors (4), Legal (2).
*   **Calendars:** Specific shift patterns (e.g., Fire reviewers Tue-Thu). Legal pool has "Blackout periods" for council meetings.
*   **Capacity:** Dynamic capacity for Inspectors (Intervention D4 adds +1 FTE in peak months).

**Cross-Case Dependencies:**
*   **Architect Bursts:** Arrival process is clustered by `architect_id`.
*   **Inspection Batching:** Site inspections are not independent; travel time is reduced if multiple cases in the same district are visited sequentially.
*   **Resource Contention:** Planners and Legal officers share capacity across case types; high volume of Complex Variance can starve Simple Renovation if not prioritized.

**Estimation Methods:**
*   **Arrivals:** Fit a **Non-Homogeneous Poisson Process (NHPP)**. Split log by month and day-of-week to estimate $\lambda(t)$. Use the 1.7x peak season and 2.2x Monday multipliers provided.

*   **Rework:** Estimate rework probability as the frequency of loops in the DFG divided by total passes through the node.
*   **Applicant Delay:** Model "Receive missing documents" waiting time as a separate distribution (heavy-tailed, median 5 days) independent of municipal resources.
*   **Dependencies:** Correlate `architect_id` submission times to estimate burst inter-arrival times.

## 5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment

**Warm-up Period:**
*   **Duration:** 3 months (simulated time).
*   **Method:** The system starts empty. The first 3 months allow queues to reach steady-state levels typical of the municipality. Data from this period is excluded from KPI calculation.
*   **Justification:** Given the 140d+ tail for Complex cases, a shorter warm-up would bias lead time metrics downward.

**Replications:**
*   **Count:** 30 independent replications per experiment.
*   **Reasoning:** Central Limit Theorem application for confidence intervals on mean lead time.
*   **Random Seeds:** Distinct seed for each replication to ensure independence of arrival and service stochasticity.

**Run Length:**
*   **Duration:** 18 months (matching the historical log period) post-warm-up.
*   **Reasoning:** Captures full seasonal cycles (two peak seasons).

**Uncertainty & Sensitivity:**
*   **Confidence Intervals:** Report 95% CI for all KPIs (Lead Time, Cost, Utilization).
*   **Heavy Tails:** Use **Common Random Numbers (CRN)** across experiments to reduce variance when comparing interventions (e.g., same arrival stream for Baseline vs. D1).
*   **Sensitivity Analysis:** Vary key uncertain parameters (e.g., Objection Probability, Applicant Response Time) by $\pm 20\%$ to test robustness of the recommended intervention.

## 6. Validation strategy

1.  **Face Validation:** Present process flow and resource calendars to process owners (Clerks, Planners) to confirm logic matches reality.
2.  **Event-Log Replay (Trace Validation):** Run the simulation using the *actual* arrival timestamps from the historical log (deterministic arrival) and compare simulated activity end-times against actual end-times. This isolates service time logic errors.
3.  **Distributional Validation:** Compare the distribution of simulated Lead Times (Median, P90, Max) against the historical summary table (e.g., Simple clean path Median 12d).
4.  **Resource Utilization:** Compare simulated resource utilization rates against HR records or estimated workload.
5.  **Bottleneck Validation:** Verify that the simulation identifies Planning and Inspection as primary bottlenecks, consistent with the "Queue depends on district" notes.
6.  **Out-of-Sample:** Reserve the last 3 months of the log for validation. Calibrate on the first 15 months, predict the last 3, and compare.

## 7. Decision criteria

**Primary Metrics:**
*   **Lead Time (Median & P90):** Target < 30 days median for Simple, < 90 days P90 for Complex.
*   **SLA Compliance:** % of cases completed within statutory deadlines.

**Secondary Metrics:**
*   **Resource Utilization:** Target 70-85% for human resources (above 85% risks queue explosion).
*   **Rework Rate:** % of cases looping back (Target < 10%).
*   **Fairness:** Gini coefficient of wait times across Districts (Target < 0.2).
*   **Backlog:** Average number of cases waiting > 10 days.

**Selection Thresholds:**
*   An intervention is **viable** if it reduces P90 Lead Time by >5% without increasing Resource Utilization >90%.
*   An intervention is **preferred** if it improves Fairness (District Gini) without degrading overall Throughput.
*   **Robustness:** The chosen policy must perform within 10% of optimal in Stress Scenarios S1 and S2.
