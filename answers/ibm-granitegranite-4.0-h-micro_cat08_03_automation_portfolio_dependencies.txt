# Dependency and Prerequisite Map

1. **Upstream Data Quality Dependencies**: A1, A2, and A6 lay the foundation for downstream activities. Poor data quality at these points cascades through the entire process.
2. **Legal/Compliance Controls**: A3, A4, and A9 are critical for maintaining compliance controls. Automated KYC hit pre-classifier (A3) must have a robust escalation path to prevent control violations.
3. **Human Escalation Capacity**: A4, A7, and A10 require human intervention for exceptions. The compliance team's limited FTE capacity (3) is a constraint; automation must not overload them.
4. **External Valuation Dependency**: A7 depends on external valuers, which adds variability. This delay needs to be mitigated to prevent synchronization points.
5. **Core-Banking Integration Constraints**: A11 and A8 are the only modules that can be deeply integrated with the core banking system in the first six months due to IT constraints.

# Portfolio Alternatives

## 1. Quick-Win Portfolio
- **Modules**: A1, A5, A10
- **Optimization**: Reduces manual data entry and improves customer experience.
- **Sacrifice**: Minimal control enhancement; may not significantly reduce rework or waiting time.

## 2. Bottleneck/Synchronization Portfolio
- **Modules**: A3, A6, A7
- **Optimization**: Addresses critical bottlenecks like KYC delays, financial statement normalization, and collateral valuation.
- **Sacrifice**: Increases dependency on external valuers and requires careful handling of exceptions.

## 3. Compliance-First Portfolio
- **Modules**: A4, A9, A12
- **Optimization**: Strongly enhances control compliance and auditability.
- **Sacrifice**: May introduce some friction in the customer experience due to additional checks.

# Expected Process-Level Effects

## Quick-Win Portfolio (A1, A5, A10)
- **Cycle Time**: Slight reduction due to faster data entry and quicker customer onboarding.
- **Waiting Time**: Decreases for customers, especially through A10.
- **Rework**: Low; immediate data quality improvements.
- **Handovers**: Reduced due to streamlined processes.
- **Resource Demand**: Decrease in back-office workload, more specifically in KYC and document review.
- **Control Violations**: Minimal; but the risk of classification and product exposure errors remains.

## Bottleneck/Synchronization Portfolio (A3, A6, A7)
- **Cycle Time**: Significant reduction in KYC, financial normalization, and collateral valuation times.
- **Waiting Time**: Major reduction due to efficient handling of critical delays.
- **Rework**: Decreases as the quality of inputs improves.
- **Handovers**: Reduced, especially between compliance and credit teams.
- **Resource Demand**: Increases in credit analysts due to better input quality; requires monitoring.
- **Control Violations**: Reduced via strict KYC clearance and contract checks.

## Compliance-First Portfolio (A4, A9, A12)
- **Cycle Time**: Slight increase due to additional controls.
- **Waiting Time**: Consistent due to enhanced control checks.
- **Rework**: Lower overall due to stronger compliance.
- **Handovers**: Increased monitoring required for control remediation.
- **Resource Demand**: Steady, with a focus on compliance and legal teams.
- **Control Violations**: Significantly reduced through four-eyes principle enforcement and control remediation tools.

# Risks and Compensating Controls

## Risks
- **Auditability**: Ensuring that automated decisions are traceable is crucial. Implement detailed logging and audit trails for all automated actions.
- **False Positives/Negatives**: In A3, false negatives (missed entity mismatches) pose a significant risk. Implement a robust escalation protocol and regular review cycles.
- **Model Drift**: Automated classification and recommendation models may drift over time. Regular model retraining and validation are necessary.
- **Exception Handling**: Automation might shift workload to human operators. Ensure that exception handling processes are well-defined and staffed.
- **Four-Eyes Compliance**: Automated contract generation must maintain the four-eyes principle. Implement automated checks to ensure compliance with this rule.

## Compensating Controls
- **Audit Trails**: Maintain detailed logs of all automated decisions and manual overrides.
- **Human-in-the-Loop**: For high-risk modules (A3, A12), ensure that a human reviewer is part of the process.
- **Regular Reviews**: Conduct periodic reviews of automated processes to identify and mitigate drift or inefficiencies.
- **Fallback Mechanisms**: Ensure that manual processes are in place to handle errors or exceptions that automated systems cannot resolve.

# Recommended Phased Rollout

## Phase 1 (First Six Months)
- **Modules**: A1, A5, A10
- **Monitoring Metrics**: Increase in customer onboarding rate, reduction in duplicate applications, and improvement in customer satisfaction scores.
- **Gate Criteria**: Achieve at least a 20% reduction in duplicate applications and a 15% improvement in customer satisfaction.

## Phase 2 (Months 7-12)
- **Modules**: A3, A6
- **Monitoring Metrics**: Reduction in KYC delays, improvement in document normalization quality, and reduction in rework due to accurate financial statements.
- **Gate Criteria**: Achieve at least a 30% reduction in KYC delays and a 25% improvement in document normalization accuracy.

## Phase 3 (Months 13-18)
- **Modules**: A7, A9
- **Monitoring Metrics**: Reduction in collateral valuation delays, improvement in control compliance, and reduction in contract remediation loops.
- **Gate Criteria**: Achieve at least a 40% reduction in collateral valuation delays and a 35% reduction in contract remediation loops.

## Phase 4 (Months 19-24)
- **Modules**: A12
- **Monitoring Metrics**: Improvement in control compliance and auditability, reduction in control violations.
- **Gate Criteria**: Achieve a 50% reduction in control violations and improved auditability metrics.

# Final Recommendation

While the highest-frequency activity (Register application) might seem like an obvious choice for automation, it is not the most strategic first step. Automation of A1 (Auto-registration and duplicate detection) and A5 (Missing-document checklist and customer portal nudging) offers immediate benefits in terms of data quality and customer experience without introducing significant upstream dependencies. These quick-wins will lay the groundwork for more complex automations.

The **Bottleneck/Synchronization Portfolio** (A3, A6, A7) should be the second focus. This portfolio addresses the most critical delays in the process, significantly reducing waiting times and rework. It also enhances control compliance by improving the quality of inputs to credit analysis and collateral valuation.

Finally, the **Compliance-First Portfolio** (A4, A9, A12) should be pursued to ensure that automation does not compromise control and governance. While it may introduce some friction in the customer experience, the long-term benefits of reduced control violations and improved auditability make it a worthwhile investment.

**Automation should not begin with the highest-frequency activity alone** because it may overlook the deeper issues of data quality, control, and risk exposure that dominate the end-to-end process. By starting with a balanced approach that includes quick-wins, bottleneck solutions, and compliance enhancements, the bank can achieve significant process improvements while maintaining robust control and risk management.
