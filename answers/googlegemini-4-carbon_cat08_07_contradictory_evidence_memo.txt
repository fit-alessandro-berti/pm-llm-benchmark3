# 1. Executive memo

**To:** Executive Leadership Team  
**From:** VP of Operations & Process Excellence

**Subject:** Decision on Fiber-Installation Redesign (Pilot R): Targeted Hybrid Deployment  

### Recommendation
Management should adopt **Alternative E (Targeted Hybrid Deployment)**, reinforced with immediate operational guardrails. Specifically, we should **scale** merged validation/scheduling and coordinator ownership exclusively for simple, low-risk houses; **immediately suspend** post-billing activation checks across all segments and predictive survey skipping for medium/high-risk and multi-dwelling buildings; and execute a **60-day targeted measurement sprint** before considering broader rollout.

### Why Neither Full Scale Nor Full Rollback Is Defensible
Process-mining evidence shows that Pilot R did not uniformly improve or break the process—it bifurcated performance along building-complexity lines while shifting costs from upfront prevention to downstream rework:

* **Where Pilot R succeeded:** Simple, low-risk houses (34% of pilot volume) improved on every dimension: median cycle time dropped by 6.0 days, p90 tail duration dropped by 8.0 days, first-time-right (FTR) rose by 3 percentage points, and complaints fell. Rolling back (Alternative B) would needlessly forfeit proven efficiency and customer-experience gains in our cleanest segment.
* **Where Pilot R failed:** In medium-risk apartments (29%) and permit-sensitive older buildings (18%), skipping field surveys and omitting permit checks pushed unprepared orders into installation. Failed visits rose from 13% to 19%, second-visit team utilization surged from 66% to 92%, and p95 cycle time degraded from 45.0 to 62.0 days. Simultaneously, allowing billing to start before verified activation drove manual activations from 7% to 15% and 30-day complaints from 6.1% to 9.8%—creating unacceptable brand and revenue-dispute risk ("billing before working"). Scaling nationally as-is (Alternative A) would institutionalize severe rework bottlenecks and compliance exposure.

### Accounting for Distorted Signals
Two data biases pull our headline metrics in opposite directions. First, pilot regions contained a higher share of apartments and older buildings than the national baseline, making aggregate Pilot R failure rates look worse than a mix-adjusted rollout would. Second, cost tracking excluded downstream complaint handling after week 4; while visible upfront cost fell (EUR 184 to EUR 161), estimated full cost worsened (EUR 198 to EUR 207) and likely understates true tail expense. Furthermore, concentrating ownership in single coordinators cut 3+ handovers from 34% to 12%, but drove coordinator utilization to 93%, creating single-point-of-failure stalls during absences.

### The Core Strategic Trade-off
Adopting Alternative E trades short-term process uniformity and deferred cycle-time ambitions in complex buildings for immediate risk containment and verified unit-cost capture. Running dual routing rules (fast-track for simple houses; mandatory survey, permit, and pre-billing activation gates for complex/business/rural orders) introduces modest routing complexity today. However, it immediately captures hard savings on ~34%+ of volume, relieves overloaded revisit and activation teams, stops premature billing disputes, and buys a clean 60-day window to measure full downstream unit economics and fix our risk-scoring and coordinator-backup models before wider deployment.

# 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

### 1. High Fitness (0.94) vs. Low Precision (0.51)
* **Mechanism:** Fitness measures how well the discovered model replays observed event traces; precision measures how tightly the model restricts behavior to *only* observed paths rather than arbitrary combinations. In Pilot R, a large plurality of orders flowed cleanly through the merged coordinator path, making it easy for discovery algorithms to achieve 0.94 fitness. However, when complex orders broke down, they generated erratic combinations of skipped surveys, repeated visits without surveys, manual activations, and out-of-sequence billing starts. To accommodate these exceptions alongside the happy path, the discovered Petri net/process model introduced permissive loops and parallel/optional gateways—causing precision to collapse to 0.51 because the model now mathematically allows thousands of chaotic trace permutations.

### 2. Improved Median Duration (10.8d) vs. Worse Tail Duration (p90: 38.5d, p95: 62.0d)
* **Mechanism:** Pilot R transformed a unimodal process distribution into a strongly bimodal one. Merging validation/scheduling and skipping field surveys shaved days off the first 50%–60% of orders that succeeded on the first visit, pulling the median down by 3.4 days. Conversely, the 24% of orders that failed first-time-right entered congested recovery loops. Because second-visit teams were running at 92% utilization and coordinators at 93%, failed cases experienced non-linear queueing delays (consistent with queueing theory as utilization approaches 100%), stretching the slowest 5%–10% of cases to 38.5–62.0 days.

### 3. Fewer Handovers (12% with 3+) vs. Lower Organizational Resilience
* **Mechanism:** Social network mining distinguishes between the *handover-of-work* network and the *working-together/centrality* network. Assigning a single coordinator eliminated inter-departmental ping-pong, slashing multi-handover cases from 34% to 12%. However, replacing pooled functional queues with dedicated individual ownership removed structural redundancy. Tacit context lived with one coordinator running at 93% capacity; if that person took leave or hit an exception spike, cases could not dynamically rebalance across peers, stalling orders entirely.

### 4. Lower Visible Cost (EUR 161) vs. Higher Rework and Downstream Cost (EUR 207+)
* **Mechanism:** Accounting visibility is front-loaded while failure costs are back-loaded. Eliminating 40%+ of field surveys (survey utilization dropped to 52%) and merging front-office tasks yielded immediate, fully logged savings prior to billing start (saving EUR 23/case visibly). However, Pilot R shifted defect discovery from pre-installation to post-installation and post-billing. Second visits (+7pp), manual activations (+8pp), and post-billing complaint escalations (+3.7pp) accrued late in the lifecycle—and because complaint costs were truncated after week 4 in a 6-week pilot where p95 reached 62 days, even the EUR 207 estimate undercounts the true economic tail.

### 5. Strong Improvement for Simple Houses vs. Sharp Deterioration for Apartments/Old Buildings
* **Mechanism:** Process steps have segment-dependent value. For low-risk single-family houses, infrastructure is standardized; separate scheduling handoffs and cautious field surveys represent pure coordination overhead, so removing them reduced both median (-6.0d) and tail (-8.0d) times while *improving* FTR (+3pp, likely due to faster installs reducing customer reschedules). For apartments and older buildings, internal riser cabling, splice capacity, and municipal/landlord permits represent genuine technical uncertainty. Removing upfront surveys and permit checks blinded installation crews, causing systemic first-visit failures (-8pp to -11pp FTR) and +12d to +25d p90 blowouts.

# 4. Decision alternatives

### Alternative A: Scale Redesign R Nationally
* **Advantages:** Maximizes immediate front-end administrative and survey savings; captures fast median cycle times nationwide; maintains a single uniform operating model.
* **Risks:** Exports severe p95 tail blowouts (62d), revisit bottlenecks (92% utilization), and coordinator burnout (93% utilization) nationwide; institutionalizes premature billing before working service, inviting regulatory scrutiny, revenue write-offs, and NPS erosion; locks in negative unit economics if uncapped 60-day rework exceeds EUR 207.
* **When Reasonable:** Only if national volume were >85% simple low-risk houses *and* post-billing activation defects could be resolved instantaneously via automated software patches (neither holds here).

### Alternative B: Roll Back to the Baseline Process
* **Advantages:** Immediately halts premature-billing complaints, restores permit/survey compliance, relieves revisit/activation specialists, and returns p90/p95 tail predictability and FTR (82%) to known baselines.
* **Risks:** Destroys proven, substantial gains in simple houses (-6d median, -8d p90, +3pp FTR); reverts to high handover friction (34% with 3+ handovers) and EUR 184 visible upfront cost; demoralizes change teams by discarding valid innovations due to flawed targeting rules.
* **When Reasonable:** Only if IT/routing systems cannot segment orders at intake (making selective routing impossible) or if legal/regulatory authorities mandate an immediate halt to all pilot workflows.

### Alternative C: Scale Coordinator Ownership Only; Suspend Survey Skipping & Post-Billing Activation
* **Advantages:** Eliminates the two primary root causes of technical rework and customer billing anger while preserving handover reductions across the board.
* **Risks:** Scaling solo coordinator ownership nationally without fixing the 93% utilization ceiling and single-point-of-failure absence stalls will create national queueing bottlenecks; forfeits merged scheduling and safe fast-track gains on low-risk simple houses; overlooks missing permit gates in older buildings.
* **When Reasonable:** If building-risk data at order intake is too unreliable to distinguish simple houses from complex buildings, *provided* coordinator staffing/pod-pooling is adjusted first.

### Alternative D: Run a Controlled Regional Experiment with Better Segment/Risk Measurement
* **Advantages:** High causal rigor; fixes pre-week-3 attribute gaps and week-4 cost truncation before committing national rollouts; allows clean A/B testing of coordinator models and skip thresholds.
* **Risks:** Delaying operational fixes to run a pristine experiment leaves known harmful practices (billing before activation, missing permit checks on old buildings) running on live customers, incurring avoidable brand damage; delays capturing risk-free gains in simple houses.
* **When Reasonable:** For *uncertain* mechanisms (e.g., calibrating medium-risk survey rules or testing pod-based vs. solo coordinators), *after* halting clearly defective post-billing practices.

### Alternative E: Hybrid — Deploy R for Simple Low-Risk Houses, Add Controls for Apartments/Old Buildings, and Run Measurement Before Wider Scale
* **Advantages:** Captures proven cycle-time, FTR, and cost wins on simple houses (~34%+ of volume, likely higher nationally given pilot mix-shift); immediately stops rework and compliance bleeding in apartments, old buildings, and business accounts via hard survey/permit/activation gates; rebalances utilization across survey (52%) and revisit (92%) teams; creates a safe operational baseline from which to run high-value measurements.
* **Risks:** Requires reliable intake triage (address/building type) to route cases accurately; introduces dual-track process management complexity for coordinators and IT workflows in the interim.
* **When Reasonable:** Immediately optimal under current evidence—where segment divergence is stark, failure mechanisms are identifiable in alignment logs, and full economic tail data is still maturing.

# 6. Final recommendation

Management should execute **Alternative E (Targeted Hybrid Deployment)** immediately, governed by a 60-day stabilization and measurement phase:

* **Immediate Action (Days 1–15):**
  1. Enforce a hard system gate requiring a passed `Activation test` *before* `Billing start` across **100% of segments** (eliminating billing-before-working complaints and compliance risk).
  2. Enforce a mandatory system gate requiring verified `Permit check` completion prior to scheduling `Installation visit` for all older/permit-sensitive buildings and multi-dwelling units.
  3. Implement "buddy/pod backup" routing for coordinators in pilot regions and cap target coordinator utilization at **80%–85%** (shifting excess intake temporarily back to standard scheduling) to clear the p90/p95 backlog.

* **What to Pause:**
  * Suspend predictive `Field survey` skipping for all apartments, older buildings, business customers, and rural long-distance installs (immediately shifting idle survey capacity at 52% utilization to relieve revisit teams at 92% utilization).
  * Suspend all post-billing activation checks company-wide.

* **What to Scale:**
  * Scale merged `Address validation + Appointment scheduling` and coordinator ownership for verified **simple, low-risk single-family houses** across regions where building-risk intake attributes are complete, paired with pod-backup rules.

* **What to Measure Next (60-Day Sprint):**
  * Execute **M4** (60-day full downstream cost tracking), **M6** (offline/shadow validation of the survey-skip predictive model), **M3** (structured failure/manual-activation reason codes), **M7** (automated conformance alerting on premature billing and missing permits), and **M5** (A/B test of solo vs. small-pod coordinator backup). Follow with **M1** (stratified skip RCT) only if M6 demonstrates high predictive accuracy.

* **Thresholds Required to Justify Broader National Rollout:**
  Broader expansion of coordinator ownership to complex segments or re-introduction of selective survey skipping requires meeting **all five** quantitative gates over a full 60-day cohort:
  1. **Unit Economics:** Fully loaded 60-day cost (including revisits and complaints) **$\le$ EUR 192/case** (beating baseline EUR 198).
  2. **Quality & Rework:** Aggregate First-Time-Right **$\ge$ 82%** and 30-day complaint rate **$\le$ 6.0%** across target segments.
  3. **Tail Control:** Mix-adjusted p90 cycle time **$\le$ 30.0 days** and p95 **$\le$ 43.0 days**.
  4. **Conformance & Precision:** `< 0.5%` move-on-log violations for `Billing start` before `Activation test` and missing `Permit checks`, with discovered process precision recovering to **$\ge$ 0.75**.
  5. **Resource Resilience:** Coordinator and revisit-team steady-state utilization sustained at **$\le$ 82%**, with zero multi-day case stalls during coordinator absences.
