import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# 1. Load and format
df = pd.read_csv("event_log.csv", parse_dates=["time:timestamp"])

# Normalize/derive analytical columns
df["complexity_code"] = df["case_complexity"].map({"Low": 0, "Medium": 1, "High": 2, "Critical": 3})
df["is_substitution"] = df["temporary_substitution"].fillna(False).astype(bool)

log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    resource_key="org:resource"
)

# 2. PM4Py organizational roles
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log)
print("Discovered roles:", roles)

# 3. Activity-based resource similarity
similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
    log,
    similarity_threshold=0.5
)
print("Resource similarity:", similarity)

# 4. Handover of work and working together networks
handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log)
working_together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)

# Inspect edges, e.g. self-loops and cross-role handovers
print("Handover edges:", list(handover.edges(data=True)))
print("Working-together edges:", list(working_together.edges(data=True)))

# 5. Join discovered roles with complexity and substitution indicators
# Create resource-level profile from the raw event table
resource_profile = (
    df.groupby("org:resource")
    .agg(
        n_events=("concept:name", "count"),
        distinct_activities=("concept:name", "nunique"),
        median_complexity=("complexity_code", "median"),
        mean_complexity=("complexity_code", "mean"),
        n_critical=("case_complexity", lambda s: (s == "Critical").sum()),
        n_high=("case_complexity", lambda s: (s == "High").sum()),
        n_substitution_events=("is_substitution", "sum"),
        n_substitution_days=("temporary_substitution", "nunique")  # if date-like; otherwise count
    )
    .reset_index()
    .rename(columns={"org:resource": "resource"})
)

# Role membership table from PM4Py output
role_membership = [
    {"resource": member, "pm4py_role": role}
    for role, members in roles.items()
    for member in members
]
role_df = pd.DataFrame(role_membership)

# Join with complexity/substitution profile
role_df = role_df.merge(resource_profile, on="resource", how="left")

# Join with activity counts to show frequency vs authority
activity_counts = pd.crosstab(df["org:resource"], df["concept:name"])
role_df = role_df.merge(
    activity_counts,
    left_on="resource",
    right_index=True,
    how="left"
)
print(role_df)

# 6. Filtering for high-critical cases or substitutions to avoid over-interpreting frequency
high_risk_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log,

    ["High", "Critical"],
    retain=True
)
substitution_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(

    ["Yes", True],

)

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

These are not improper roles; they are informal or bridging roles that provide resilience and cross-functional coverage, but they carry authority and risk implications that formal role mining can obscure.

## R07_Giorgio as potential commercial shadow role

Evidence: R07_Giorgio is formally a Senior Service Agent in Front Office, yet he performed 72 A7 Pricing Exception Review events and 88 A8 Customer Negotiation events. His activity profile is 0.84 similar to Amira but 0.71 similar to Fatima. During weeks 3–4, when R06_Fatima was absent, R07 performed 31 A7 and 18 A8 events, but median pricing exception duration rose from 38 to 74 minutes.

Benefits: He provides continuity for pricing exceptions and negotiation on complex front-office cases. His front-office knowledge helps bridge commercial and service delivery.

Risks: Longer pricing duration during substitution suggests this is not identical expertise. His 20 A9 Supervisor Approval events also create potential authority ambiguity: is he making final approval decisions, preliminary judgments, or only facilitating approvals?

## R13_Mateo as cross-functional escalation broker

Evidence: R13_Mateo has one of the most distributed activity profiles. He touches A6, A7, A8, A9, A10, A11, A13, and A14, although not at high volume. He performed 44 A9 events, 42 A13 Reopen events, and 16 A6 Security Review events. In week 5, while R08_Hana was at an audit workshop, Mateo performed 21 preliminary A9 approvals, but 13 were later rechecked by Hana. In weeks 7–8, Mateo filtered low-risk security referrals to reduce false escalations; this reduced complaints but two critical cases waited more than two days.

Benefits: He prevents false escalations, coordinates across technical, commercial, security, and supervisory boundaries, and absorbs triage load from highly specialized resources.

Risks: His preliminary approval work can be mistaken for final authority. The recorded recheck behavior suggests his role is more about routing and preparation than formal decision-making. He can also become a bottleneck or single point of failure for critical coordination.

## R05_Erik as rare but critical security specialist

Evidence: R05_Erik has only 96 A6 events in 10 weeks, but his complexity profile is extreme: 5 low, 24 medium, 82 high, and 55 critical cases, with a median complexity of 0.86. His overall event count is low compared with front-office or technical roles.

Benefits: He provides a rare but mandatory security review control for high-risk and critical work.

Risks: A frequency-based analysis may treat him as marginal or group him with technical analysts because he also performs A3, A5, and A10 occasionally. If management reduces security capacity or allows too much prefiltering, critical cases may be delayed or improperly cleared.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

The following is illustrative PM4Py-style code. It formats a pandas dataframe, runs organizational role discovery and resource similarity, computes handover and working-together networks, and joins the role output with complexity and substitution indicators.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load and format event log
# ------------------------------------------------------------
df = pd.read_csv("event_log.csv", parse_dates=["time:timestamp"])

# Analytical columns
df["complexity_code"] = df["case_complexity"].map({
    "Low": 0,
    "Medium": 1,
    "High": 2,
    "Critical": 3
})
df["is_substitution"] = df["temporary_substitution"].fillna(False).astype(bool)

# PM4Py expects a formatted dataframe/EventLog
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    resource_key="org:resource"
)

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Organizational role discovery
# ------------------------------------------------------------
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log)
print("PM4Py discovered roles:", roles)

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Activity-based resource similarity
# ------------------------------------------------------------
similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
    log,
    similarity_threshold=0.5
)
print("Resource similarity:", similarity)

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Handover-of-work and working-together networks
# ------------------------------------------------------------
handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log)
working_together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)

print("Handover edges:", list(handover.edges(data=True)))
print("Working-together edges:", list(working_together.edges(data=True)))

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Join discovered roles with case complexity and substitution indicators
# ------------------------------------------------------------
resource_profile = (
    df.groupby("org:resource")
    .agg(
        n_events=("concept:name", "count"),
        distinct_activities=("concept:name", "nunique"),
        median_complexity=("complexity_code", "median"),
        mean_complexity=("complexity_code", "mean"),
        n_critical=("case_complexity", lambda s: (s == "Critical").sum()),
        n_high=("case_complexity", lambda s: (s == "High").sum()),
        n_substitution_events=("is_substitution", "sum")
    )
    .reset_index()
    .rename(columns={"org:resource": "resource"})
)

role_membership = [
    {"resource": member, "pm4py_role": role}
    for role, members in roles.items()
    for member in members
]
role_df = pd.DataFrame(role_membership)

role_df = role_df.merge(resource_profile, on="resource", how="left")

activity_counts = pd.crosstab(df["org:resource"], df["concept:name"])
role_df = role_df.merge(
    activity_counts,
    left_on="resource",
    right_index=True,
    how="left"
)

print(role_df)

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Filter high/critical cases and substitution episodes
# ------------------------------------------------------------
high_risk_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log,
    "case_complexity",
    ["High", "Critical"],
    retain=True
)

substitution_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log,
    "temporary_substitution",
    ["Yes", True],
    retain=True
)

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. Avoid over-interpreting activity-frequency clusters
# ------------------------------------------------------------
# Frequency profiles are not authority.
# Compare:
# - R14_Nora: very high A1/A2 frequency, but no decision authority.
# - R08_Hana: relatively low A9 frequency, but formal approval authority.
# - R05_Erik: low overall counts, but rare critical security gate.
#
# Therefore, use complexity, substitution, escalation_level, and
# network position as complementary evidence to role mining.
```

# 6. Operational interpretation

Management should use the discovered structure as a starting point for process governance, not as a replacement for formal HR roles.

## Staffing

- Keep automation, temporary support, front-office, technical, commercial, security, quality, supervisor, and coordinator roles distinct.
- Do not treat temporary agents as equivalent to permanent service agents simply because their activity profiles are similar.
- Ensure that rare but critical roles, especially R05_Erik and R08_Hana, are explicitly staffed for high/critical cases even though their event counts are low.

## Training

- Train temporary agents on complete Validate Entitlement notes; weeks 8–10 reopen evidence shows process quality problems from poor validation.
- Give R07_Giorgio formal commercial training and clarify pricing-approval boundaries if he is intended to remain a shadow or become a formal backup.
- Develop R04_Dina toward higher-complexity technical work under supervision before granting independent authority on high/critical cases.
- Train R13_Mateo on explicit escalation thresholds so his preliminary approvals and security filtering do not become de facto final decisions.

## Risk control

- Protect security review and supervisor approval as mandatory controls; do not let low event frequency or successful prefiltering justify removing them.
- Monitor substitution duration and rework rates: R07’s pricing substitution slowed from 38 to 74 minutes, and R13’s preliminary approvals required rechecking.
- Track critical-case waiting time where coordination filters down low-risk referrals, as in weeks 7–8 when two critical cases waited more than two days.
- Treat quality and KB updates as independent controls, not as closing formalities.

## Resilience

- Formalize shadow roles such as R07_Giorgio and R13_Mateo as documented backups, with clear limits and escalation paths.
- Ensure R05_Erik is not a single point of failure for security reviews; evaluate whether R03_Chen’s security-adjacent cases could support a controlled backup.
- Plan for automation failure for R14_Nora; front-office agents must be able to register and validate manually when automation is unavailable.

## Cautions about changing staffing based only on mined roles

1. **Temporary substitution is not proof of permanent competence.** R07_Giorgio covered pricing review but was slower; R13_Mateo prepared approvals that were later rechecked; temporary agents closed cases that later reopened because of incomplete notes. A mined role may suggest coverage, but not permanent readiness or authority.

2. **Activity-frequency clusters are not authority or risk exposure.** R14_Nora has very high counts but no decision authority; R05_Erik has low counts but is critical for security; R08_Hana has low overall volume but formal approval authority. Removing or redistributing work based only on volume or similarity could eliminate rare but essential process controls.
