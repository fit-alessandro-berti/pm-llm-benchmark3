# 2. Benign alternative explanations

- **PO four-eyes exceptions and fast Carla/Gita or Dan/Hugo approvals.** Benign explanations include (1) authorized emergency delegation for urgent medical supplies or weekend repairs and (2) a logged resource representing a shared account or Bot_PO mapping rather than the human approver. Support would include emergency authorization criteria, contemporaneous incident/work-order evidence, separate named approver in `approved_by` or access logs, and a different reviewer’s timely retrospective justification. Refute or weaken these explanations if cases are non-emergency, the same person created and approved the PO in the detailed audit trail, delegation was absent, or justification is late, missing, or authored by the same person.
- **Lena/Kim bank changes, rapid payment approvals, and the V-77x loop.** Benign explanations include (1) approved mass maintenance for the banking-platform change and (2) an operational correction loop to resolve rejected or mismatched vendor data. Support would include a maintenance ticket listing affected vendor IDs and effective dates, independent bank verification, change logs, and a matching payment approval trail. Refute or weaken them if bank destinations lack independent confirmation, changes fall outside the authorized migration batch, payment beneficiary differs from the verified vendor, or a loop closes without documented correction.
- **Same-person override/retrospective entries.** Benign explanations include (1) quarter-close spreadsheet migration preserving upload time rather than event time and (2) delegated staff entering records under a shared manager account. Support would include original spreadsheet metadata, immutable source timestamps, uploader identity, and a different human’s actual approval. Refute them if contemporaneous records show the same person made both decisions or if the required justification was never obtained within three working days.
- **Repeated subcontracting and high co-working counts.** Benign explanations include (1) expected clarification/escalation between a specialist and delegated approver and (2) correction after comments or automated exception feedback. Support would include case notes, assignment history, comments, and bot execution traces. Refute or weaken them if repeated returns lack substantive work, decisions are pre-filled, or the same person controls both sides of an approval despite nominal role changes.
- **Vendor Check followed by bank-data change by Lena/Marc.** Benign explanations include (1) vendor-master role design assigning both checks and maintenance to the same specialist and (2) routine onboarding or verified corrections. Support would include approved role design, independent second-level review, and vendor onboarding/change tickets. Refute or weaken them if there is no compensating review or the same actor both changes and validates unverified bank data.

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

**Would increase concern:**

- Independent audit logs confirm the same named person both created and approved a PO, or changed bank details and approved payment, without authorized emergency delegation or compensating independent review.
- No valid emergency trigger, purchase justification, timely different-person retrospective approval, or contemporaneous evidence exists; timestamped records show justification was created after payment or backdated.
- V-77x bank destinations cannot be independently verified, differ from verified vendor instructions, or were changed outside the approved migration batch; payment beneficiary, vendor legal entity, or invoice do not match.
- Manual uploads lack a traceable uploader, source file, approval record, or ticket; repeated fast approvals have no review evidence or substantive comments.
- Access logs show shared credentials, conflicting identities, unauthorized role grants, deletion/editing of approval history, or the same actor using separate nominal accounts.
- Repeated loops consistently return to the same decision-maker without a documented correction, and approval outcomes appear predetermined or bypass normal checks.

**Would reduce concern:**

- Immutable identity and application logs establish separate human decision-makers and show shared-resource or Bot_PO mapping caused the apparent overlap.
- Emergency declarations, dispatch/work-order evidence, delegation records, and retrospective justifications meet the policy timing and independence requirements.
- The V-77x maintenance ticket and batch manifest match each changed account, independent vendor verification, and subsequent beneficiary; the 9 cases are validly covered or corrected.
- Original spreadsheet metadata shows quarter-close_upload ingestion time, with independently timestamped source approvals and distinct authorized authors.
- Case comments, rejection messages, and version history explain subcontracting returns as substantive correction or clarification; ordinary comparator cases show similar routing.
- Reconciled results show violation and speed rates fall materially after correcting actor mapping, excluding bots/shared accounts, and stratifying for emergency category, workload, and source system.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

The event-log review identified control exceptions and concentrated processing patterns that warrant targeted validation; the current evidence does not establish collusion or other misconduct. The clearest control concern is the Lena/AP_Kim subgroup: nine cases appear to combine vendor bank-detail changes with payment approval by the same resource, while the wider subgroup has a high concentration in vendor family V-77x, a high-value mix, rapid approvals, and substantial manual-upload activity. This combination merits prompt review of the nine apparent segregation conflicts and reconciliation to the platform-migration records. The known banking-platform change and legitimate mass-maintenance activity are plausible explanations, but the missing link to maintenance tickets prevents confirmation from this log alone.

The 42 apparent PO create/approval conflicts and 17 same-resource override/retrospective records also require control testing. Emergency medical-supply and weekend-repair cases, delegated urgent approvals, and specialist routing plausibly explain much of the fast activity and recurring buyer/approver interaction. They do not remove the need to verify authorization and, where required, a timely retrospective justification by a different human. Twelve same-minute justifications were uploaded after quarter close, and shared-account, bot-mapping, and legacy-ERP issues may distort both actor attribution and event timing.

We recommend reconciling named actors, source timestamps, delegation records, vendor-change tickets, bank verification, and payment beneficiaries before drawing conclusions about individual conduct. Review the high-risk cases first, compare them with matched emergency and routine cases, and document any confirmed control failures separately from evidence of intent. Until source and identity limitations are resolved, network frequency and short elapsed times should be treated as screening signals, not proof of improper coordination.
