## 1. Materiality Framework

**Principle:** Technical alignment cost measures syntactic edit distance under a uniform, data-blind cost function. Business materiality measures control effectiveness and exposure. They must be assessed separately.

**Severity (Business Risk) 1-5:**

*   **5 - Critical control failure / irreversible financial diversion:** Pay Claim executed before or without legally/operationally required approval/fraud control, or on HIGH risk / bank-change diverted payment, on material/high amount or with evidence of bypass. Irreversible, regulatory breach, high fraud/customer harm, potential criminal exposure. Requires immediate investigation and containment.
*   **4 - High policy failure with fraud vector:** Data-aware policy violation (P1/P2) where Auto Approve used despite prohibition (amount >5k, not LOW, or recent bank change) or HIGH risk claim bypasses Fraud Screen. Payment is reversible only via recovery. Even small amount with recent bank change is high severity due to diversion risk.
*   **3 - Material procedural/timing failure:** Required control performed but in wrong order (Pay before Approve), or Auto Approve misused on moderate amount/medium risk without bank change. Controls eventually executed but effectiveness and auditability destroyed; payment may need reversal. Medium exposure.
*   **2 - Low direct financial exposure but evidence/auditability/ customer harm gap:** Substantive control was performed (or likely performed) but not reliably evidenced in core log; notification exists only in non-immutable correlated system; data-quality gap needing correction. No unauthorized payment, but regulatory/customer and resourcing risk and control cannot be proven.
*   **1 - No business risk / Harmless technical noise:** UI telemetry, retry/transport duplication, internal silent routing, pure naming/mapping difference, or model-scope mismatch where no payable claim was intended. Alignment cost >0 is artifact, not deviation. Requires hygiene, not control remediation.

**Dimensions evaluated for each case:** Control objective affected (P1-P3 = fraud/payment authorization; P4 = notification), Amount/exposure, Reversibility (payment to bank is irreversible vs notification retry is fully reversible), Timing (before vs after control), Customer/regulatory harm, Recurrence/systemic vs isolated, Evidence reliability, Model scope.

**Confidence vs Severity:** Severity asks *how bad would it be if the interpretation is true?* Confidence asks *how strongly does authoritative evidence support that interpretation?* Confidence is lowered by: missing external evidence, mutable correlations, label ambiguity, alternative optimal alignments existing, and informality (verbal check). We treat a `M(A)` as *no matching log event found* - not as proof the activity did not occur. We treat `L(A)` as *log event not mimicked by model* - not as proof it is a business activity. Confidence is therefore independent of `raw_cost`.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

*We never interpret `M(A)` as proof `A` did not happen; we seek independent evidence.*

**Case/Pattern CL-602 Missing Fraud Screen (480k HIGH + bank change)**
*   H1: Control intentionally bypassed under time pressure / verbal check culture. 
*   H2: Fraud screen performed in separate system but not logged/correlated (evidence gap). 
*   H3: Fraud role understaffed, queue skipped, approver believed verbal was sufficient.
*   **Discriminators:** Query fraud system for case ID/fraud case number; check fraud analyst workload/queue logs; interview approver + check for email/phone record; review segregation logs; check if 74 other HIGH+bank cases also missing screen (systemic vs isolated). Immutability of approval timestamp vs fraud system.

**Case/Pattern CL-604 Pay-before-Approve (timestamps 10:03 vs 10:20)**
*   H1: System allows payment dispatch independent of approval flag (no hard gate). 
*   H2: Clock skew between bank gateway and core (apparent order artifact). 
*   H3: Manual approval recorded late (after verbal approval) but actually occurred earlier.
*   **Discriminators:** System design review: is Pay Claim service checking approval status? Compare NTP-synchronized logs; check approval workflow audit trail (when task opened/completed vs recorded); bank gateway ACK time vs core event emission; review 4-case data-aware pattern for similar timing gaps.

**Pattern P-B CL-606 External Adjuster Gap (M Assess Loss)**
*   H1: Assessment done externally, not ingested (integration gap). 
*   H2: Assess Loss truly skipped on high-value claim (control failure). 
*   H3: Label mapping failure (`Assess Damage`-type synonym).
*   **Discriminators:** Retrieve external signed PDF and verify signature timestamp + case reference; check document-to-case identifier stability (2 failing cases); search core for alternative labels around 10:05; cross-check adjuster assignment system logs. Finding signed report before fraud screening favours H1.

**Pattern P-H CL-608 Mutable Notify Link**
*   H1: Notification correctly sent via portal, but core-CRM correlation implemented as mutable text. 
*   H2: Notification never sent; CRM entry manually added post-hoc. 
*   H3: Notification sent but outside 24h SLA (timing failure not content failure).
*   **Discriminators:** Extract portal immutable message store with message ID + hashes; compare CRM text field history/audit trail (was it edited?); check portal access logs (claimant login); verify +3h timestamp vs SLA; review CRM schema: is foreign key immutable? Test reproducibility across portal cases.

**Pattern P-E CL-609 / CL-610 Data-aware Auto-Approve Failure (cost 0)**
*   H1: Business rule never encoded; model permission mistaken as policy permission (design omission). 
*   H2: Rule exists but reads stale risk/bank-change data (data latency/bug). 
*   H3: Approver manually forces Auto path via override.
*   **Discriminators:** Inspect DMN/BRMS: does gate check amount<=5000 && risk==LOW && noBankChange7d? Query bank-change event timestamp vs Auto event (1 hour gap in CL-610 proves data existed); check risk scoring time vs Auto time; review Auto-approval configuration logs & override audit; replay 4 flagged cases with data-aware check.

**Pattern P-C CL-611 Label Synonym (Assess Damage vs Assess Loss)**
*   H1: Pure synonym, activity identical. 
*   H2: Two distinct activities with different SOPs (damage scoping vs loss quantification) and model conflates them.
*   **Discriminators:** Compare SOP definition + UI labels for both events; interview assessors what they did; check data mapping table change history after case; sample 10 `Assess Damage` cases to see if fraud/approve followed same as `Assess Loss` cases; check resource/role who performed.

**Pattern P-D CL-607/CL-612 Denied/Withdrawn Incomplete Model**
*   H1: Legitimate out-of-scope cases should be excluded via preprocessing. 
*   H2: Legitimate variants that should be added to model as early termination. 
*   H3: Model fitness artificially low due to logging `Deny/Withdraw` as business events when they are case-management dispositions.
*   **Discriminators:** Quantify volume: denied/withdrawn % of total log; review intake SOP whether denied leads are claims per policy; check downstream `Pay Claim` occurrence on denied/withdrawn (should be 0 if correct); workshop with Model Owner vs Process Owner on scope charter.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

The supplied cost function sums `1` for every visible `L` or `M` move and `0` for `S`/`T`. It is deliberately uniform, control-flow-only, and scope-blind. Business risk depends on *which* control is missing, *when* relative to an irreversible payment, *amount* at risk, *evidence reliability*, and *model fitness*. Five+ cases prove the divergence:

**1. Zero cost can hide critical data-aware fraud (CL-610, CL-609, CL-605 contrast).** CL-610 and CL-609 are fully synchronous (`raw_cost 0`) yet violative: CL-610 Auto-Approved 4,500 LOW immediately after a bank change, violating P1 (no bank change 7 days) and P2 (bank change requires Fraud+Manual). CL-609 Auto-Approved 18,000 MEDIUM, exceeding the 5,000 Auto limit and non-LOW risk. The process tree permits Auto as a control-flow choice; the *data condition* is invisible to the alignment. `T(tau)` in CL-605 also costs 0 and is correctly fitting, showing that zero-cost captures *control-flow* fit, not policy compliance. Relying on cost would prioritize neither CL-610 nor CL-609, though they represent unauthorized payment authorization - the most dangerous failure mode because alignment cannot see amount/risk/bank recency.

**2. Low cost drastically understates irreversible missing fraud control (CL-602).** CL-602 costs `1` (`M Fraud Screen`) identical to a UI autosave in CL-601, yet it involved a 480,000 HIGH-risk payment to a *newly changed* beneficiary with no auditable fraud evidence. The uniform cost treats all `M` equally; business risk weights a mandatory fraud control on high exposure + diversion vector as Severity 5. Missing evidence is not minor: without Fraud Screen, money laundering/fraud cannot be excluded, payment is irreversible, and regulatory breach is certain even if a verbal check was claimed.

**3. Moderate cost masks temporal irreversibility (CL-604).** CL-604 costs `3` (`L Pay` + `M Fraud` + `M Pay`). The alignment "repairs" the trace by moving Pay after Manual Approve, making it appear as a cheap edit. Business reality: bank accepted payment at 10:03 *before* manual approval at 10:20; bank acceptance is irreversible. P3 explicitly states Pay-before-approval is severe even if cheap to repair. Cost counts moves, not the arrow of time or the inability to undo a settlement. A single `L(Pay)` early is far worse than three telemetry `L`s in CL-601 (also cost 3).

**4. High cost is driven by model scope, not risk (CL-607, CL-612).** CL-607 costs `4` and CL-612 costs `4`, the highest in the sample, yet both represent *correct* business outcomes (denied pre-screen lead, claimant withdrawal) where no payment was made. The tree was designed for payable claims per P6; the cost penalizes the log for containing `Deny/Withdraw` plus the absence of downstream payable steps. The distribution evidence confirms most `cost >=4` cases are denied/withdrawn. Ranking by cost would prioritize scope mismatches over the 480k diverted payment at cost 1 - an inversion of risk.

**5. Duplicate telemetry and label synonyms inflate cost without customer harm (CL-601, CL-603, CL-611).** CL-601's cost 3 is three UI telemetry events (`Open Workbench`, `Autosave`, `Close`) per P5 - not business activities. CL-603's cost 1 is a retry duplicate with same message ID (74-case systemic pattern) - P4 allows portal/CRM duplicates to be deduplicated, customer was notified once. CL-611's cost 2 counts `L Assess Damage` + `M Assess Loss` as two deviations, yet the signed procedure and mapping table confirm they are synonyms for the same assessment; fraud/manual controls were executed. Costs count syntactic novelty; business counts *substantive control execution*.

**6. Missing evidence  missing activity, and evidence reliability matters (CL-606, CL-608).** `M` means no matching log event, not proof of omission. CL-606's `M Assess Loss` (cost 1) is complemented by an external licensed adjuster report at 10:05 before fraud/approval - the control *did* happen externally. Conversely, CL-608's `M Notify` (cost 1) appears resolved by a CRM portal message, but the link is a mutable text field, so evidence is unreliable. Uniform cost treats both as `1`, yet one is a logging integration gap (Severity 2) and the other is an auditability gap where customer notification cannot be proven. Hidden moves `T` (cost 0) and data granularity (amount, risk, bank change) are also invisible to cost.

**Design implication:** Cost-function design (uniform 1), scope (payable-only model), hidden moves, data conditions, activity granularity/synonyms, duplicated transport events, external-but-uningested evidence, and irreversibility all disconnect cost from risk. A risk-prioritized view must overlay P1-P4, amount/risk, timing, and evidence reliability - kept in separate columns from technical cost.
