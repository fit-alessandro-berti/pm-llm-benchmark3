## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1: Pre-pilot audit and shadow routing | No live reassignment; audit routing rules, capacity, and common support; shadow-match Orion/Nova cases | Confounding and positivity | Case, observational | No service change; maintain compliance and SLA | Variant/DFG comparison, wait/service, queue length, staffing, backlog, open/abandoned status | Identifies whether Orion/Nova comparisons have overlap and which mechanisms differ | Stop reassignment planning if overlap is poor or high-complexity Nova capacity is inadequate |
| Phase 2: Small randomized or quasi-random routing pilot | Randomize eligible cases within complexity × shift × load × compliance to Orion vs Nova; use a small prespecified fraction, not a blanket 50% | Direct effect of `resource_group` assignment | Case | Exclude high-risk cases if needed; require compliance; ensure Nova certification and night coverage; monitor backlog and quality | Cycle time, `queue_wait`, `review_service`, `rework`, quality outcome, conformance, case status | Within eligible strata, estimates causal effect of assignment under randomization/quasi-randomization | Roll back if backlog exceeds threshold, SLA breaches rise, appeals rise, or capacity cannot support assignment |
| Phase 3: Queue/compliance capacity intervention | Add compliance capacity, night staffing, or queue caps rather than changing the resource-group label | Queueing/compliance mediation | Shift/cell or queue | Maintain compliance; no group label change; monitor service quality | Queue length, wait before review, compliance wait, service time, cycle time, rework | Tests whether waiting/compliance capacity, not group label, is the actionable lever | Stop if wait does not fall or service/rework worsens |
| Phase 4: Staged scale-up only if supported | If pilot supports Nova or capacity improvement, move small tranches (e.g., 10–25%) via staggered rollout | Scale and heterogeneous effects | Case/team | Preserve Orion difficult/night capacity; monitor quality and backlog | Same as pilot plus backlog, appeals, open/abandoned cases | Stepped-wedge or staged rollout estimates real-world effect while limiting risk | Roll back specific strata if quality, backlog, or cycle time worsens |

Recommendation: Do not reassign 50% of Orion cases now. The observed Orion disadvantage is dominated by case mix, shift, compliance, and rework; within complexity strata Orion is not slower and may be slightly faster, but the evidence is too sparse and nonrandom for a causal claim. First run a pre-pilot audit and a small stratified randomized/quasi-random routing pilot within complexity × shift × load × compliance, with predefined high-complexity and night safeguards. In parallel, test a queue/compliance-capacity intervention, because waiting dominates cycle time and compliance/rework are plausible mechanisms. Scale only if the pilot shows Nova is not worse in difficult strata and capacity/backlog metrics remain safe; otherwise retain Orion for complex/night work and fix the bottleneck. Monitor abandoned/open cases, rework, appeals, and SLA breaches. This learns whether resource group matters while limiting operational risk and avoids a blanket reassignment that could fail or backfire.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Selection by completion/abandonment.** Only completed cases are present, so group comparisons may be biased if difficult Orion or Nova cases remain open or abandoned. Most valuable addition: all registered cases with status/censoring time, including open, abandoned, and time-to-abandonment.
2. **Unobserved case severity beyond coarse complexity.** `complexity` may not capture claim amount, medical necessity, or legal risk that drives both routing and cycle time. Most valuable addition: a validated case-risk score at `Classify` or detailed severity/claim characteristics.
3. **Routing rule and reviewer skill bundle.** `resource_group` combines assignment policy, reviewer certification, shift coverage, and team process. Most valuable addition: the actual routing score/reason and reviewer ID/certification/team for each review.
4. **Queue state and capacity at arrival/processing.** Wait time depends on backlog, staffing, shift handoffs, and load, not only group label. Most valuable addition: time-stamped queue length, staffing level, and backlog by step and group.
5. **Compliance/rework timing and measurement.** `wait_before_review_h` aggregates queue and compliance waiting, and `review_service_h` includes repeated review; the exact mechanism is hidden. Most valuable addition/design change: event-level timestamps for `Classify`, `Compliance Check`, `Request Info`, `Review`, and `Close`, ideally supplemented by a small randomized routing pilot.
