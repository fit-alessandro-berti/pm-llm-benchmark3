## 1. Decision rule

**Eligibility & Stage Gate**
- Only cases in `Triage` or `Diagnosis Started` are eligible. Cases that have reached `Diagnosis Complete` or later are automatically excluded, regardless of `p_now` or breach value.
- Intervention must occur before the SLA deadline minus 45 minutes (swarm duration) plus a 30-minute safety buffer.

**Alert Persistence & Minimum Evidence Change**
- Default cool-down: 24 hours for any case that has already received a swarm alert.
- Re-entry exception: Override cool-down only if **both** of the following are met:
  1. A materially new event is logged (e.g., diagnostic attachment, status change, customer reply), **or**
  2. `p = p_now  p_prev > 0.05` **and** time-to-SLA < 4 hours.
- Repeated alerts driven solely by model refresh cycles without new evidence are suppressed and routed to a model-review queue.

**Calibration Adjustment & Threshold Robustification**
- `p_now` is adjusted for segment-specific backtest bias before threshold comparison:
  - SME: `p_adj = p_now + 0.065` (underprediction)
  - Strategic Enterprise: `p_adj = p_now  0.085` (overprediction)
  - Public Sector / Non-Native: `p_adj = p_now` (flagged for higher variance)
- Decision threshold is not a fixed probability. Instead, a case proceeds to the prioritization score only if `p_adj > 0.45` **and** the conservative (lower-bound) ARR estimate yields a non-negative expected net benefit.

**Prioritization Score**
- `Score = (ARR_mid × Value)  €450  False-alarm burden  Expected Spillover Cost`
- Expected Spillover Cost = 6 untreated diagnosis-stage cases × 0.02 (mid spillover) × €20,000 (portfolio-average breach cost)  €2,400 per slot.
- Cases are ranked by `Score`. Only slots with `Score > 0` are activated. One slot is permanently reserved for unlisted regulatory/high-priority arrivals.

**Uncertainty Handling**
- ARR ranges are never treated as point estimates. Decisions use the lower bound for thresholding and the midpoint for scoring, with explicit downside caps.
- Non-monetary obligations (customer trust, regulatory exposure, specialist fatigue) are tracked via a qualitative override flag that can elevate a case if systemic risk or reputational exposure is detected.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Cool-down & Suppression**
- Default 24-hour suppression after any swarm alert.
- Automatic suppression for cases with `p < 0.03` and no new event log, regardless of `p_now`.

**Escalation & Re-entry Logic**
- Re-entry triggers: (1) new diagnostic log/attachment, (2) status change to a new stage, (3) `p > 0.05`, or (4) time-to-SLA < 2 hours with `p_now > 0.60`.
- When re-entry triggers, the case is treated as a fresh alert; cool-down resets.

**I-05 vs I-07 Comparison**
- **I-05:** Third alert, no new event, `p = +0.02`. Fails re-entry triggers. Policy: **Suppress**. Log for model-refresh tuning. Intervening would waste a slot, increase specialist fatigue, and add spillover without new information.
- **I-07:** Third alert, but new customer diagnostic log uploaded. `p = 0.03` but material event present. Policy: **Allow re-entry & prioritize**. The log changes the diagnostic hypothesis space, justifying a swarm despite the slightly lower `p_now`. Time-to-SLA (1.5h) further justifies urgency weighting.

**Temporal Decay & Queue Awareness**
- Urgency weight increases as SLA approaches, capped at 2× base score to prevent panic allocation.
- If >2 slots are requested within 1 hour, queue congestion triggers a spillover penalty multiplier (+20% to spillover cost) until demand normalizes.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-Based Prioritization & Starvation**
- High-value cases (I-04, I-08) consistently score higher, potentially starving lower-value cases (I-05, I-10, I-07) even when they face tighter deadlines or systemic risk.
- *Safeguard:* Implement a tiered threshold with a mandatory “urgency floor” (e.g., time-to-SLA < 2h automatically qualifies for score review) and a quarterly fairness audit to ensure lower-value segments aren’t systematically deprioritized.

**Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support**
- Sparse calibration yields high variance in both `p_now` and ARR estimates. Decisions risk over- or under-intervening.
- *Safeguard:* Apply Bayesian shrinkage toward the portfolio-mean ARR for this segment. Require manual specialist review before swarm allocation. Track actual vs predicted ARR separately for this segment.

**Differential Calibration Across Segments**
- SME underprediction and Strategic Enterprise overprediction create asymmetric decision thresholds if not corrected.
- *Safeguard:* Maintain segment-specific calibration layers (`p_adj`) with independent thresholds. Run monthly backtests per segment; if calibration drift exceeds ±0.05, pause automated allocation and trigger recalibration.

**Selective Intervention Changing Future Training Labels**
- Consistently intervening on high-risk cases prevents breaches, causing the training set to become dominated by “no breach” labels. The model will learn to underpredict risk for future cases, creating a negative feedback loop.
- *Safeguard:* Log counterfactual outcomes (predicted `p` vs actual breach under intervention). Maintain a holdout group (10–15% of eligible cases) that receives standard triage only. Use difference-in-differences to estimate true causal ARR and adjust thresholds accordingly.

**Spillover Harms Falling on Untreated Groups**
- Congestion from swarms delays diagnosis for untreated cases, disproportionately affecting lower-value or slower-moving queues.
- *Safeguard:* Cap daily swarms at 4. Rotate spillover burden by tracking untreated breach rates per segment; if any segment’s untreated breach rate rises >2% above baseline, pause new allocations and rebalance. Implement queue-aware scheduling that prioritizes swarms on cases whose diagnosis is blocking downstream resolution work.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational Monitoring**
- Daily dashboard: slot utilization, queue wait times, specialist interruption counts, false-alarm rate, and alert suppression volume.
- Trigger: If slot utilization <60% for 3 consecutive days, lower threshold or increase daily cap. If >90%, enforce stricter re-entry rules.

**Predictive Monitoring**
- Track Brier score, log-loss, and calibration curves per segment weekly.
- Monitor `p_now` drift using Population Stability Index (PSI). If PSI >0.1 for any segment, pause automated scoring and run manual calibration.
- Alert frequency vs. new event rate: if >70% of alerts lack new events, tighten cool-down or adjust model refresh cadence.

**Causal-Effect Monitoring**
- A/B test where feasible: randomly assign 10% of eligible cases to a “no-swarm” control group (with manager override for critical deadlines).
- Compute observed ARR = (breach rate control  breach rate swarm). Compare to predicted ARR ranges.
- If observed ARR consistently falls below the lower bound by >0.05, reduce intervention confidence and shift to conservative scoring.

**Capacity & Spillover Monitoring**
- Track untreated diagnosis-stage breach rates hourly during swarm windows.
- Measure aggregate delay imposed on non-intervened cases (target: 15 min average). If congestion exceeds 20 min, activate spillover penalty multiplier and pause new allocations.

**Fairness Monitoring**
- Weekly parity report: breach rates, alert suppression rates, and swarm allocation rates across segments and value tiers.
- If any segment’s breach rate diverges >3% from portfolio average, trigger a policy review and adjust thresholds or reserve slots for that segment.

**Recalibration & Threshold Adjustment Plan**
- **Monthly:** Full calibration review, segment-specific threshold updates, and ARR range validation.
- **Quarterly:** Policy stress-test using historical data under Optimistic/Base/Adverse scenarios; update spillover models and queue congestion parameters.
- **Trigger Rules for Threshold Change:** 
  1. Brier score degradation >0.05 over 14 days  lower intervention threshold by 0.02.
  2. Observed ARR consistently below lower bound  raise threshold by 0.03 or reduce daily cap.
  3. Untreated breach rate increase >2% due to spillover  pause allocations, rebalance queue, and adjust spillover cost parameter.
- All changes require explicit documentation of expected impact, uncertainty bounds, and rollback procedures.
