## 1. What is observed

Availability is coarse: `automation=1` only after branch-system go-live — Alpha/Beta in April, Gamma in July; Delta/Epsilon had no adoption by August. It marks opportunity to use the system, not the state of any case. Actual treatment intensity varied substantially: `automated_fast_path_pct` rose to 25–50% in adopter months and increased gradually, while remaining 4–6% in nonadopter months, consistent with incomplete uptake and learning. Adoption was not random: Alpha/Beta were selected because of high volumes and poor historical cycle times; Gamma followed a readiness pilot. These branches also experienced contemporaneous changes: a centralized fraud desk from March, routing that increased arrivals at Alpha/Beta and later Gamma, FTE changes, a May product-mix shift toward higher complexity, and possible local work-practice changes. Outcomes are branch-month summaries. `median_cycle_h` and `p90_cycle_h` hide case-level path differences; rework and escalation are aggregate rates; CSAT is a monthly mean with response rates varying from roughly 20–45% and no response counts. Queue states, fraud-desk waits, team-level compliance, and event-level sequences are unavailable.

Interference and treatment-version issues are central. The centralized fraud desk means one branch’s triage can affect another branch’s delays through a shared queue, violating a simple no-spillover assumption. The “treatment” also changes over time: availability, training, actual fast-path use, routing rules, and specialist/fraud-desk configuration are bundled. E1–E2 are therefore policy-package ITT estimands, not effects of a stable case-level technology.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary: cohort-specific staggered adoption / event study, e.g. group-time ATT or interaction-weighted estimator | Alpha/Beta cohort = April 2026; Gamma cohort = July 2026; event time relative to first availability | Not-yet-treated branches at each t; Delta/Epsilon as never-treated; use Gamma as control for Alpha/Beta before July | pre-outcome leads, `log(arrivals)`, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct`, month effects; optionally routing indicators | E1, E2, E4–E7 | no unmeasured time-varying confounders; no anticipation; conditional parallel trends; limited spillovers | event-study leads; placebo adoption dates; cohort-by-cohort effects; leave-one-branch-out; wild cluster inference | only three treated branches; one branch or month dominates |
| Alternative: matched / synthetic control | Single adoption date per treated branch | Weighted Delta/Epsilon and pre-treatment outcomes; Gamma donor only after July | lagged outcomes, arrivals, complexity, FTE | E1, E2, E4, E6 | donors unaffected by treatment; good pre-fit; stable relationship absent treatment | pre-period RMSPE, balance, placebo controls, post/pre RMSPE ratio | poor pre-fit; all branches share March/May shocks, so donors are contaminated |
| Alternative: interrupted time series with control series | level and slope break at adoption | Delta/Epsilon; pre-adoption own history | pre-trend, seasonality, arrivals, FTE, complexity, March/May shock indicators | E1, E2, dynamic level/slope effects | no concurrent level/slope shocks omitted; stable measurement | model residual autocorrelation, alternative windows, change-point placebo | confounds rollout with routing/product/staffing changes |
| Alternative: treatment-intensity dose-response model | continuous `automated_fast_path_pct`, with lags | all branches, or adopters only | branch and month effects, arrivals, FTE, complexity; perhaps availability indicator | E3, E4–E6 | dose not driven by unobserved performance/shocks; measured dose reflects treatment version | dosage leads, nonlinear dose curves, branch-specific slopes | uptake is endogenous: better teams or easier cases may use automation more |

The primary design is preferable because adoption is staggered, targeted, and accompanied by trends that differ across branches. A group-time/event-study design estimates separate effects for Alpha/Beta and Gamma, uses not-yet-treated branches as contemporaneous controls, and explicitly examines leads; this avoids the heterogeneity/timing bias of a single naïve two-way fixed-effects coefficient. It also keeps the initial estimand honest: branch availability is a policy bundle, not case-level automation. In this setting the bundle includes learning, changing fast-path intensity, routing shifts, and fraud-desk effects, so a simple post indicator would mix those components. Still, with only three treated branches, the design is fragile: one branch or one month can dominate. I would therefore use it as a disciplined descriptive-causal framework, report cohort-specific effects and placebo leads, and use wild cluster or randomization inference where feasible. The synthetic-control alternative is valuable for Gamma because its adoption is late and there are several untreated donors, but it is weak for Alpha/Beta, where only Delta/Epsilon remain clean donors and all branches share the March/May shocks. ITS can describe within-branch level/slope breaks, but without careful controls it confounds holidays, routing, and product mix. The intensity design is needed operationally, but because uptake is endogenous it should be secondary unless randomized in the next rollout.

**Arithmetic facts: treatment intensity rather than binary availability.** Using only adopter post-periods and demeaning within branch, a 10 percentage-point higher fast-path share is associated with approximately:

- median cycle: **-1.1h** per 10 pp, but with substantial branch/month confounding;
- p90 cycle: **+1.5h** per 10 pp;
- rework: **+1.0 pp** per 10 pp;
- escalation: **+0.2 pp** per 10 pp;
- CSAT: **+0.01 point** per 10 pp.

These slopes are descriptive. Higher uptake occurred over time while high complexity and FTE/routing conditions also changed, so they do not identify a causal dose response.

**Pre-trends and regression to the mean.** Alpha/Beta pre-period medians were fairly stable, but March had elevated escalation across branches when the fraud desk started. Gamma’s May–June median and p90 jumped sharply — median 45.9 and p90 101.0 in June versus roughly 38–40 and 75–81 in Jan–Apr — coinciding with higher complexity and a June FTE loss. Its July–August improvement is therefore partly movement back toward its earlier level. Delta and Epsilon also partially improved after June, supporting a common post-May reversion component. Regression to the mean is a serious explanation for Gamma’s apparent gain.

**Sensitivity / bounded conclusions.**

- **Median cycle:** raw adopter changes range from -3.4h to +1.9h; control-adjusted contrasts are about -4h. A bounded interpretation is: relative median improvement plausibly 0 to -5h versus controls, but no defensible absolute improvement for Alpha/Beta. A >10% universal reduction is unsupported.
- **p90 cycle:** control-adjusted contrasts suggest -6h to -7h relative improvement, but Alpha/Beta absolute p90 worsened about +10h. A bounded interpretation is: tail effect plausibly ranges from material harm (+10h absolute) to moderate relative improvement (-7h versus controls). The sign is not established.
- **Rework/escalation:** rework improvements are not durable in Alpha/Beta; escalation consistently rises. A safer range is rework -2 to 0 pp relative, escalation 0 to +1 pp relative.

**Causal interpretation.** A single statement such as “automation reduces cycle time by X%” is not defensible. The defensible claim is narrower: availability was followed by lower cycle-time measures relative to never-treated branches, but median and p90 behavior diverged, uptake was endogenous, and selected branches experienced simultaneous shocks.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

expand with an embedded evaluation

Next wave should be **Delta only**, with Epsilon held as the near-term control. Delta is smaller, lower-complexity, and less fragile operationally than Epsilon, so the immediate customer-harm risk is lower. Epsilon is the branch where upside may be larger, but it also has the worst high-complexity/rework/CSAT profile and would leave no clean comparator if expanded simultaneously. Within Delta, use a stepped-wedge or cluster-randomized phase-in by intake team or intake day, stratified by registration risk class. If withholding availability is infeasible, randomize the timing of training, fast-path offers, and audit feedback while keeping business-as-usual comparison teams; log all overrides. This preserves operational movement while creating a credible within-branch contrast.

Collect at least 6 months of pre-period and 6 months of post-period branch-month data; preferably use 12 months pre and post. Alongside monthly aggregates, collect daily/weekly case-level event data: registration time, triage type, automated recommendation, acceptance/override, specialist review, fraud-desk referral, information-request loops, rework, resolution, closure, risk class, routing source, and SLA status. Queue-state data must include specialist WIP/backlog, fraud-desk arrivals and waiting time by referring branch, productive FTE hours, and missed SLA counts.

Monitor spillovers at Alpha, Beta, Gamma, and Epsilon, especially fraud-desk wait times and escalation rates, because the centralized desk can transmit congestion across branches. Guardrails: stop or pause if p90 worsens >5% versus controls for two consecutive months, rework rises >2 pp, CSAT drops >0.1, SLA breaches rise materially, or fraud-desk waits increase. Scale to Epsilon only if Delta shows at least a 5% median-cycle improvement after risk/arrival/FTE adjustment, p90 is not worse than control, guardrails are not breached, and effects replicate across at least two independent cohorts or randomized blocks using randomization or wild-cluster inference.

# Load and validate panel
df = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(csv_text))
required = [
    "branch", "month", "automation", "arrivals", "fte",
    "high_complexity_pct", "automated_fast_path_pct",
    "median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h", "rework_pct",
    "escalation_pct", "csat_1to5"
]
missing = set(required) - set(df.columns)
assert not missing, f"Missing columns: {missing}"

df["month"] = pd.to_datetime(df["month"] + "-01")
df["month_label"] = df["month"].dt.strftime("%Y-%m")
df = df.sort_values(["branch", "month"]).reset_index(drop=True)

assert df.duplicated(["branch", "month"]).sum() == 0
expected_index = pd.MultiIndex.from_product(
    [sorted(df["branch"].unique()), sorted(df["month"].unique())],
    names=["branch", "month"]
)
actual_index = pd.MultiIndex.from_frame(df[["branch", "month"]])
assert len(expected_index.difference(actual_index)) == 0
assert df["arrivals"].gt(0).all()
assert df["fte"].gt(0).all()
assert df["automation"].isin([0, 1]).all()

# Derived variables
df["log_arrivals"] = np.log(df["arrivals"])
df["fast_pp10"] = df["automated_fast_path_pct"] / 10.0

adoption_date = {
    "Alpha": pd.Timestamp("2026-04-01"),
    "Beta": pd.Timestamp("2026-04-01"),
    "Gamma": pd.Timestamp("2026-07-01"),
    "Delta": pd.NaT,
    "Epsilon": pd.NaT,
}

# Relative-time indicators for staggered adoption.
# Never-treated branches have undefined event time.
df["rel_time"] = np.nan
adopt = df["adoption_date"]
is_post = adopt.notna() & df["month"].ge(adopt)
df.loc[is_post, "rel_time"] = (
    df.loc[is_post, "month"].dt.year - adopt.loc[is_post].dt.year
) * 12 + (
    df.loc[is_post, "month"].dt.month - adopt.loc[is_post].dt.month
)
for k in range(-6, 5):
    df[f"rel_{k}"] = ((df["rel_time"] == k) & df["rel_time"].notna()).astype(int)

# Print branch trends for five outcomes; no subplots or event-log assumptions.

    "median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h", "rework_pct",
    "escalation_pct", "csat_1to5"
]
print("=== Branch trends for five outcomes ===")
for y in outcomes:
    print(f"\n{y}")

    "Alpha": ("2026-01-01", "2026-03-01", "2026-04-01", "2026-08-01"),
    "Beta": ("2026-01-01", "2026-03-01", "2026-04-01", "2026-08-01"),
    "Gamma": ("2026-01-01", "2026-06-01", "2026-07-01", "2026-08-01"),
}
print("\n=== Adopter-specific pre/post contrasts ===")
for b, (pre0, pre1, post0, post1) in windows.items():
    sub = df[df["branch"] == b]
    pre = sub[(sub["month"] >= pre0) & (sub["month"] <= pre1)]
    post = sub[(sub["month"] >= post0) & (sub["month"] <= post1)]
    row = {"branch": b, "pre_n": len(pre), "post_n": len(post)}
    for v in [
        "automated_fast_path_pct", "arrivals", "high_complexity_pct", "fte",
        "median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h", "rework_pct",
        "escalation_pct", "csat_1to5"
    ]:
        row[f"{v}_pre"] = pre[v].mean()
        row[f"{v}_post"] = post[v].mean()
        row[f"{v}_delta"] = post[v].mean() - pre[v].mean()
    print(pd.DataFrame([row]).T.to_string(header=False))

    f"WARNING: branch-clustered inference has only {n_clusters} clusters. "
    "Cluster-robust SEs are likely unreliable; use wild-cluster bootstrap "
    "or randomization inference in any serious causal analysis."
)
print(
    "WARNING: median_cycle_h and p90_cycle_h are branch-month aggregates. "
    "Regressions below do not identify customer-level quantile effects or "
    "case-level process mechanisms. No event-log discovery is attempted."
)

# DESCRIPTIVE PANEL MODELS ONLY.
# CAUSAL ASSUMPTIONS REQUIRED FOR ANY CAUSAL READING, NOT VERIFIED HERE:
# - no unmeasured branch-by-month confounders;
# - no anticipation of adoption;
# - conditional parallel trends across branches;
# - no interference through the centralized fraud desk;
# - stable treatment version over time.
# These assumptions are likely incomplete here.

print("\n=== Descriptive panel model: automation availability ===")
for y in outcomes:
    formula = (
        f"{y} ~ automation + high_complexity_pct + log_arrivals + fte "
        f"+ C(branch) + C(month_label)"
    )
    model = smf.ols(formula, data=df).fit(
        cov_type="cluster",
        cov_kwds={"groups": df["branch"]}
    )
    print(

        f"p={model.pvalues['automation']:.3f}"
    )

print("\n=== Descriptive treatment-intensity model: fast-path percentage ===")
for y in outcomes:
    formula = (
        f"{y} ~ automation + fast_pp10 + high_complexity_pct "
        f"+ log_arrivals + fte + C(branch) + C(month_label)"
    )
    model = smf.ols(formula, data=df).fit(
        cov_type="cluster",
        cov_kwds={"groups": df["branch"]}
    )
    # fast_pp10 coefficient is per 10 percentage points of actual fast-path use.
    print(
        f"{y}: fast_pp10 coef={model.params['fast_pp10']:.3f}, "
        f"cluster SE={model.bse['fast_pp10']:.3f}, "

    )

print("\nDone. Treat all estimates as descriptive unless the explicit causal "

## 10. Final claim ladder

- Observed: Availability was staggered and targeted, actual fast-path use ramped from 4–6% to 25–50%, escalation rose in all adopters, and median versus p90 cycle-time behavior diverged.
- Conditionally causal: Under strong staggered-adoption assumptions, availability is associated with relative median-cycle improvements of roughly 0–5 hours versus controls, but this depends on untestable no-confounding and no-spillover assumptions.
- Suggestive: Higher actual fast-path use is associated with somewhat lower medians but worse tails/rework, consistent with process substitution and queue congestion rather than clean capacity creation.
- Not supported: The statement “automation reduces cycle time by X%” is not defensible as a general claim, nor are any strong CSAT or tail-improvement claims.
