# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

- **Automation Bot (Nora):** Core = A1/A2 only, executed at machine speed and volume; there is no peripheral activity because the bot has no discretionary authority — it is a boundary object, not a role in the human-organizational sense.

- **Front-Office Agents (Amira, Ben):** Core = A1, A2, A4, A12 — the "routine resolution" loop. Peripheral touches on A3/A5/A7/A8 are exception handling, not competence; the low counts and lower median complexity (0.29–0.34) indicate these are covered rather than owned. Authority is limited: no approval or override rights.

- **Temp Agents (Kai, Lina):** Core activities mirror front-office agents but the *absence* of any A3/A5/A7/A8/A9/A11 activity is itself diagnostic — these resources are structurally walled off from diagnostic/authority activities. The linkage to 17 reopened cases suggests their "core" work has a quality gap not visible in raw frequency.

- **Technical Specialists (Chen, Dina):** Core = A3, A5, A10 — full diagnostic-to-implementation ownership. Peripheral A6/A11/A14 touches suggest informal consultation. Case complexity differentiates them substantially even though activity-profile similarity is high (0.94); Chen's peripheral A6 involvement and higher critical caseload suggest an unofficial security-adjacent trust level that Dina has not yet earned (tenure 5.1y vs 1.2y).

- **Security Specialist (Erik):** Core = A6 only, and this is deliberately narrow — a certified, low-volume, high-stakes gate. Peripheral A3/A5/A10/A11/A14 activity at very low counts likely reflects consulted-but-not-owned involvement in complex remediation. Authority is concentrated, not distributed — the opposite pattern from front-office roles.

- **Commercial Specialist (Fatima):** Core = A7, A8. Peripheral A2/A9/A12 are incidental. The substitution episode (median duration 3874 min when Giorgio covers) is direct evidence that the core activity carries tacit skill not captured by activity counts alone.

- **Giorgio (hybrid):** Core is genuinely split — front-office core (A1/A2/A4/A12) plus a secondary, less certified commercial core (A7/A8/A9). The A9 approval events (20) are notable: Giorgio appears to exercise approval-adjacent authority despite not being a formal Supervisor — this is a peripheral activity with outsized organizational implication.

- **Supervisor (Hana):** Core = A9 only, but this is a high-authority veto/approval gate, not a throughput activity. Peripheral touches on A2/A7/A8/A11/A13 represent oversight and escalation review (e.g., rechecking Mateo's interim approvals) rather than production work — frequency is deliberately low because authority activities are rate-limited by design.

- **Incident Coordinator (Mateo):** No single "core" activity in the classic sense — the core *function* is routing/bridging across A6, A7, A9, A13, evidenced by moderate counts spread across almost every activity column. This is a coordination role whose value is structural connectivity, not throughput.

- **Quality Analysts (Ivan, Julia):** Core = A11, A14 — the quality gate and knowledge codification function. Peripheral A3/A5/A10 touches are light validation, not ownership. Their authority is "stop-the-line" (quality check can block closure) despite fairly low case complexity numbers.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

**R07_Giorgio — Commercial shadow role.**
Giorgio's activity profile (0.71 similarity to Fatima, 0.84 to Amira) plus the direct substitution episode (31 A7 + 18 A8 events during Fatima's absence, with median pricing-review duration nearly doubling) shows a resource who *can* execute commercial exceptions but does so at reduced efficiency/quality relative to the certified specialist. Benefit: provides surge capacity and continuity when Fatima is unavailable. Risk: without formal pricing certification, degraded cycle time and possibly inconsistent pricing decisions could go unnoticed if only volume is monitored; Giorgio's presence in A9 (20 events) also suggests informal encroachment on approval authority that may not be sanctioned.

**R13_Mateo — Cross-functional escalation broker.**
Mateo's footprint touches nearly every activity at low-to-moderate volume, which is the signature of a coordination/bridging role rather than a production role. Evidence: fragment #3 and #5 show Mateo routing Reopen and Security-adjacent cases to specialists and to Hana; the Week-5 episode where Mateo performed 21 preliminary A9 approvals (13 later rechecked) demonstrates both the value (keeping approvals flowing during Hana's absence) and the risk (provisional authority without full sign-off validity). The Weeks 7–8 filtering of low-risk security referrals reduced false-escalation complaints but also delayed two critical cases >2 days — a clear case of a beneficial triage function creating a new tail risk.

**R05_Erik — Rare but critical specialist.**
Erik's low similarity to every other resource (0.39 with Mateo, near-zero overlap elsewhere) and his concentration in A6 with the highest median complexity score (0.86) in the table mark him as a singleton expertise node. Benefit: guarantees a qualified security gate for critical cases. Risk: the Weeks 7–8 episode shows that when his referral volume is throttled by Mateo's triage, some critical cases wait excessively — Erik is both a bottleneck and a single point of failure; there is no evident backup with equivalent certification.

**Additional shadow/bridging roles:**

- **Automation boundary role (R14_Nora):** Sits at the front edge of every case, silently absorbing high-volume low-complexity intake. It is a "shadow" only in the sense that it is invisible to resource-based social-network analysis (no handovers *to* Nora from humans), and its precheck of entitlement means human A2 volume already reflects Nora's upstream filtering — a naïve frequency read would overstate human effort on A1/A2 relative to actual discretionary work.

- **Temporary-workforce risk role (R11_Kai, R12_Lina):** Structurally resembles front-office agents but the traceable link to 17 reopened cases from incomplete Validate Entitlement notes suggests a shadow "under-trained execution" role — high closure throughput masking a quality debt that surfaces later as A13 Reopen and rework by R01/R02/R13.

- **Quality gatekeeping role (R09_Ivan, R10_Julia):** Beyond simple QC throughput, their A14 Knowledge Base Update activity positions them as an informal organizational-learning bridge between technical/commercial resolution and future front-office/temp-agent guidance — a bridging function easily missed if A11 and A14 are treated as two unrelated activity clusters.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load and format the event log
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
df = pd.read_csv("event_log.csv")

df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
)

event_log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Discover organizational roles (activity-based grouping)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# NOTE: this groups resources by *co-occurrence of activities*,
# not by authority or decision rights -- treat as a starting
# hypothesis, not a final org chart.
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(df)
print(roles)  # inspect activity sets per discovered role-cluster

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Activity-based resource similarity
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
similarity_df = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(df)

# Flag high-similarity pairs for manual review rather than
# auto-merging them into one role
high_sim_pairs = similarity_df[similarity_df["similarity"] > 0.85]
print(high_sim_pairs)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Handover-of-work and working-together networks
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
how_network = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(df)
wt_network = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(df)
subcontract_network = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(df)

# Use handover network to spot bridging resources: nodes with
# high betweenness but modest raw activity volume (e.g., Mateo)
import networkx as nx
G = nx.DiGraph()
for (src, tgt), weight in how_network.items():
    G.add_edge(src, tgt, weight=weight)

betweenness = nx.betweenness_centrality(G, weight="weight")
bridging_candidates = sorted(betweenness.items(), key=lambda x: -x[1])[:10]
print("Potential bridging/broker resources:", bridging_candidates)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Join role-discovery output with case_complexity and
#    temporary_substitution indicators
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
resource_complexity = (
    df.groupby("org:resource")["case_complexity"]
      .agg(["mean", "median", "count"])
      .rename(columns={"mean": "avg_complexity",
                        "median": "median_complexity",
                        "count": "n_events"})
)

subst_flags = (
    df.groupby("org:resource")["temporary_substitution"]
      .agg(lambda s: (s == True).sum())
      .rename("n_substitution_events")
)

role_enriched = (
    resource_complexity
    .join(subst_flags, how="left")
    .fillna(0)
)

# Merge discovered role labels (if roles output resource->role map)
# role_enriched = role_enriched.join(roles_df.set_index("resource"))

print(role_enriched.sort_values("median_complexity", ascending=False))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Guardrails against over-interpreting frequency clusters
#    as formal authority
# ---------------------------------------------------------------
# a) Never rank/assign roles by raw event count alone --
#    always cross-tab with median_complexity and n_substitution_events.
# b) Treat high activity-based similarity (>0.85) as a *hypothesis*
#    requiring qualitative validation (titles, certifications),
#    not an automatic merge.
# c) Distinguish "executes activity" from "has decision authority":
#    e.g., filter A9/A6/A7 events and check whether the resource's
#    formal title/certification matches, or whether the event
#    coincides with temporary_substitution == True (indicating
#    provisional, not standing, authority).
provisional_authority = df[
    (df["concept:name"].isin(["A9 Supervisor Approval", "A6 Security Review"])) &
    (df["temporary_substitution"] == True)
]
print(provisional_authority.groupby("org:resource").size())
```

# 6. Operational interpretation

**Staffing.** Use the discovered structure to protect narrow, high-risk gates (Erik's A6, Hana's A9, Fatima's A7) from being treated as fungible with high-volume front-office throughput roles. Cross-training investment should target the demonstrated shadow capacity (Giorgio for commercial, Mateo for coordination/triage) to formalize backup coverage, but staffing models should reserve headcount growth for the bottleneck roles (Erik, Hana) rather than simply hiring more front-office/temp agents, since the Weeks 7–8 and Week 5 episodes show that volume elsewhere does not relieve pressure on these gates.

**Training.** Direct targeted training at (a) temp agents on Validate Entitlement completeness, given the traced reopen linkage, and (b) Dina/junior technical analysts on graduated complexity exposure so the Chen/Dina gap narrows through structured mentoring rather than informal routing avoidance. Consider certifying Giorgio formally in pricing review given his demonstrated (if imperfect) coverage capacity, converting a shadow role into a sanctioned backup.

**Risk control.** Track provisional-authority events (approvals/security reviews performed under temporary_substitution=True) as a standing KPI, since the data shows these are not equivalent in quality to standing-authority events (13/21 Mateo approvals rechecked; pricing cycle time near doubling under Giorgio). Monitor Mateo's referral-filtering behavior specifically, since it has a documented trade-off (fewer false escalations vs. delayed critical cases).

**Resilience.** The organization currently has single points of failure at Erik (security) and Hana (approval), with only informal, imperfect backups (Mateo, Giorgio). Formalizing secondary certification paths — without diluting the specialization that makes these roles valuable — would improve resilience against absence, attrition, or surge load.

**Two cautions on changing staffing from mined roles alone:**

1. **Mined similarity is not mined competence or authority.** High activity-profile similarity (e.g., Chen–Dina 0.94, Fatima–Giorgio 0.71) reflects *what* activities a resource touches, not the quality, risk exposure, or decision rights associated with those activities. Reassigning workload purely based on these similarity scores (e.g., treating Giorgio as a full Fatima substitute) risks embedding the performance degradation already observed in the substitution episode into standing policy.

2. **Temporary substitution performance is not proof of sustained capability.** Episodes where Mateo or Giorgio covered for absent specialists show the process *can* survive short gaps, but the accompanying side effects (rechecked approvals, longer cycle times, delayed critical cases) indicate these are stopgaps, not validated steady-state capacity. Using such episodes to justify permanent headcount reallocation (e.g., removing dedicated commercial or approval specialists) would likely convert a manageable, monitored risk into an unmonitored structural one.
