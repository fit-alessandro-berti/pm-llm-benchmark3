# 1. Evidence Ledger

**The three candidates below are hypotheses about recorded completion sequences—not claims that the underlying execution mechanism has been identified.** They share the principal approval safeguards but differ in how much unobserved behavior they admit.

For compact expressions and trace audits, use these abbreviations:

| Symbol | Activity              | Symbol | Activity              |
| ------ | --------------------- | ------ | --------------------- |
| R      | Register Order        | Q      | Expedite Review       |
| C      | Credit Check          | A      | Approve Configuration |
| E      | Engineering Review    | P      | Release Production    |
| S      | Supplier Confirmation | Z      | Archive Case          |

Thus, `R C E S A P Z` denotes a **complete seven-event trace**, not a fragment.

| S7                        | The export contains completions only; ties use ingestion order.                                                                                           | Strict physical precedence for every adjacent pair, or activity start times.                                             | Strong description of logging semantics.                                                     | Preserve supplied ordering for replay, but do not interpret every ordering as temporal dependency.                                         |

**Why bidirectional DFG edges do not prove concurrency.** Both `C → E` and `E → C` arise from a model that chooses between two entirely sequential routes:

$$
X\bigl(\operatorname{seq}(C,E),\operatorname{seq}(E,C)\bigr).
$$

They also arise from parallel checks with different completion orders, context-dependent scheduling, or tie-breaking. The same reasoning applies to the two directions between S and Q. Nor do bidirectional edges establish loops: no supplied trace repeats an activity.

Furthermore, the DFG permits the walk `R C Q A P Z` because all its edges occur somewhere. That walk nevertheless omits Engineering Review and violates Q’s prerequisite. **Edge-level evidence cannot safely be recombined without preserving case-level dependencies.**

# 2. Shared Assumptions

| Assumption                            | Meaning and consequence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A1 — Observation boundary             | Visible transitions represent the supplied recorded completions. A sequence constrains their recorded order; it does not automatically mean that the second activity started after the first completed.                                                                                        |
| A2 — Scope                            | These models describe completed production-order cases of the supplied kind. Cancellations, failed checks, rejected configurations, and other unobserved outcomes are outside scope—not declared impossible in the company.                                                                    |
| A3 — Common safeguards                | R occurs first; C and E occur before A; visible S and Q occur before A; Q additionally follows both C and E; A precedes P and Z follows P. D3, D4, and D6 are adopted as modeling constraints despite their lower confidence than D1–D2.                                                       |
| A4 — Supplier observation abstraction | An absent S may mean “not required,” “performed but not exported,” or potentially an actual omission. The control-flow models do not distinguish these explanations or certify external-component compliance. The missing external/stocked attribute prevents an enforceable data-aware guard. |
| A5 — Cardinality                      | R, C, E, A, P, and Z occur exactly once; S and Q occur at most once. This matches the sample but is a simplifying hypothesis. In particular, one case-level S is not proof that every external component received a separate confirmation.                                                     |
| A6 — Optionality and probabilities    | `?(T)` means `X(T, τ)`: either one visible occurrence or a silent alternative. It specifies possible observations, not their probabilities. Separately optional S and Q allow combinations; they do **not** assert statistical independence.                                                   |
| A7 — Validation timestamps            | Timestamps generated by `parse_event_log_string` are synthetic ordering aids. They must not be used to test overlap, waiting time, timestamp ties, or causal timing.                                                                                                                           |

**The seven absent Supplier events are not a measured 23.3% event-loss rate.** They are seven cases without a recorded S. As an illustrative—not inferred—allocation, five legitimate skips and two lost events would produce the observed 23 records from 25 actual Supplier completions, corresponding to an 8% loss rate. That is compatible with S5, but does not identify any particular case.

All three candidates fit the training language by construction. Consequently, their “underfitting risks” below concern **omitted mechanisms or legitimate future behavior**. The conservative candidate’s dominant statistical problem is overfitting through memorization.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

**Inductive bias.** Preserve exactly the seven observed variants, including the two singleton expedited variants. Do not infer new combinations merely because individual fragments have been observed. This is a defensible reference model for detecting novelty, but not a discovered universal operating procedure. Its exactness deliberately preserves potentially accidental correlations, including the fact that every observed Q co-occurs with S.

**Compact formal expression**

Using juxtaposition for sequence:

$$
M_C =
R\cdot
X(CES,\ ECS,\ CE,\ EC,\ CSE,\ CESQ,\ ECQS)
\cdot APZ.
$$

**Self-contained PM4Py code**

This uses PM4Py’s sequence and XOR process-tree syntax. ([PM4Py Documentation][1])

```python
import pm4py

labels = {
    "C": "Credit Check",
    "E": "Engineering Review",
    "S": "Supplier Confirmation",
    "Q": "Expedite Review",
}

middles = ["CES", "ECS", "CE", "EC", "CSE", "CESQ", "ECQS"]

branches = [
    "->(" + ",".join(repr(labels[c]) for c in middle) + ")"
    for middle in middles
]

tree_text = (
    "->('Register Order',"
    "X(" + ",".join(branches) + "),"
    "'Approve Configuration',"
    "'Release Production',"
    "'Archive Case')"
)

model_conservative = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_text)
```

**Behavior deliberately excluded**

* Every complete trace other than the seven observed variants, including lawful-looking recombinations such as `R C E Q A P Z`.
* Repetition, missing principal checks, early approval, and all alternative starts or endings.

**Known underfitting risks**

* Legitimate new interleavings and expedited stocked-component cases produce novelty alerts.
* Corrected or newly missing Supplier events can change a trace into an unlisted variant.
* The enumeration does not represent the actual enabling mechanism, context-dependent routing, or physical concurrency.

**Duplicate labels and scalability.** C and E appear in multiple **distinct tree leaves**. This does not mean that a case repeats those activities: XOR selects one branch. However, label identity must not be confused with node identity when editing or evaluating the model. Factoring out R and APZ reduces duplication, but enumeration still grows with the number and length of variants and can become factorial when many interleavings must be represented.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

**Inductive bias.** Replace whole-variant memorization with a small set of completion-order constraints. Permit either principal-check order; retain `C ≺ S` as a provisional gate because all 23 recorded Supplier completions follow Credit; and generalize the independently justified Q rules across check and supplier arrangements. This deliberately remains narrower than D5’s possible Engineering-only or intermediate-information interpretations. The Credit-to-Supplier relation is a **low-confidence descriptive hypothesis**, not a new compliance requirement.

**Compact formal expression**

$$
M_B =
R\cdot
PO\left(
\{C,E,?S,?Q\},
\{C\prec ?S,\ C\prec ?Q,\ E\prec ?Q\}
\right)
\cdot APZ.
$$

All children of the partial-order block must finish—including their silent alternatives—before A.

**Deciding sequence, concurrency, and partial order**

| Relationship                 | Decision            | Reason                                                                                              |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| C versus E                   | No precedence edge  | Both recorded orders occur. This permits interleavings without asserting observed physical overlap. |
| C versus S                   | Provisional `C ≺ S` | Supported by all 23 visible S occurrences, but not established as a causal rule.                    |
| E versus S                   | No precedence edge  | V5 explicitly places S before E; other variants place E before S.                                   |
| C/E versus Q                 | Both precede Q      | D6 and S6 explicitly require this barrier.                                                          |
| S versus Q                   | No precedence edge  | Both orders are observed and expressly permitted by S6.                                             |
| Entire review block versus A | Sequence            | Both principal checks and any visible optional reviews must finish before approval.                 |

A global sequence “both checks, then Supplier” would reject V5. Unrestricted parallelism among C, E, S, and Q would lose Q’s prerequisites. The partial order preserves precisely the selected asymmetric constraints. In POWL, precedence applies between child executions, and the stored relation must be a valid strict partial order. ([GitHub][2])

**Self-contained PM4Py code**

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def optional(label):
    return OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [Transition(label), SilentTransition()],
    )


def sequence(*nodes):
    result = StrictPartialOrder(list(nodes))
    # Store the transitive closure of the sequence relation.
    for i, source in enumerate(nodes):
        for target in nodes[i + 1:]:
            result.order.add_edge(source, target)
    return result


credit = Transition("Credit Check")
engineering = Transition("Engineering Review")
supplier = optional("Supplier Confirmation")
expedite = optional("Expedite Review")

review = StrictPartialOrder(
    [credit, engineering, supplier, expedite]
)
review.order.add_edge(credit, supplier)
review.order.add_edge(credit, expedite)
review.order.add_edge(engineering, expedite)

model_balanced = sequence(
    Transition("Register Order"),
    review,
    Transition("Approve Configuration"),
    Transition("Release Production"),
    Transition("Archive Case"),
)

model_balanced.validate_partial_orders()
```

**Behavior deliberately excluded**

* S before C, including `R E S C A P Z`; Q before either principal check; approval before required visible work.
* Repeated reviews, additional Supplier events, and outcomes outside the shared scope.

**Known underfitting risks**

* Engineering-only or intermediate-information enablement may legitimately produce S before C, which this model rejects.
* Case attributes may couple Q eligibility and Supplier necessity; the model does not capture those mechanisms.
* Per-component confirmations, rework, and lifecycle dependencies remain unrepresented.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

**Inductive bias.** Retain the approval and expedite safeguards, but do not elevate any observed principal-completion-to-Supplier relation into a universal gate. Supplier completion may occur anywhere after R and before A. This includes completing before both principal checks: D5 speaks about having **enough information**, not necessarily completing a check. Such traces are feasible under a hidden-information-milestone interpretation, but the available export cannot establish that the necessary information actually existed.

**Compact formal expression**

Here `∥` permits all interleavings of its children:

$$
M_P =
R\cdot
\left(
?S
\parallel
\left((C\parallel E)\cdot ?Q\right)
\right)
\cdot APZ.
$$

**Self-contained PM4Py code**

```python
import pm4py

tree_text = (
    "->('Register Order',"
    "+("
        "X('Supplier Confirmation',tau),"
        "->("
            "+('Credit Check','Engineering Review'),"
            "X('Expedite Review',tau)"
        ")"
    "),"
    "'Approve Configuration',"
    "'Release Production',"
    "'Archive Case')"
)

model_permissive = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_text)
```

**Boundary of reasonable generalization.** A supplier-first completion can be consistent with D5 when a principal check has already started, produced usable information, and remains unfinished while Supplier Confirmation completes. Those starts and information milestones are outside the visible alphabet. The model therefore represents an **existentially feasible completion sequence**, not authorization to start procurement without information.

It is not an unrestricted “anything goes” model: both checks remain mandatory; Q must follow both; all visible reviews precede approval; and production release requires approval.

**Behavior deliberately excluded**

* Early Q, approval without both checks, production release without approval, and S or Q after approval.
* Repetition, alternative terminal outcomes, and activities outside the supplied alphabet.

**Known underfitting risks**

* The model still excludes legitimate repeated checks, multiple component confirmations, or rework if these exist beyond the sample.
* It underrepresents actual start-time and data dependencies: a visible trace may be admitted even though its particular underlying execution violated an unobserved information prerequisite.

# 6. Unobserved Behavior Audit

The candidates have strictly nested **complete visible languages**:

$$
L(M_C)\subsetneq L(M_B)\subsetneq L(M_P),
\qquad
|L(M_C)|=7,\quad |L(M_B)|=12,\quad |L(M_P)|=18.
$$

These are language cardinalities, not estimated probabilities or PM4Py quality scores.

For the presence combinations `(S absent, Q absent)`, `(S present, Q absent)`, `(S absent, Q present)`, and `(S present, Q present)`, the balanced model contributes respectively **2, 3, 2, and 5** traces; the permissive model contributes **2, 6, 2, and 8**. All complete traces have six to eight visible events.

**Metric interpretation matters.** PM4Py’s token-replay generalization measure uses transition execution frequencies, so duplicated leaves and replay choices can affect it. It is not a substitute for validation on genuinely new cases. ([GitHub][3])

Likewise, the default Petri-net simplicity measure is based on arc degree. It need not rank human readability, model size, or the three inductive biases in the same order as the table. ([GitHub][4])

# 8. Validation Code

The script below independently reconstructs all models and the 30-case log. It normalizes historical `EventLog` and newer DataFrame parser returns, and converts each model to a Petri net with initial and final markings. These behaviors are documented in the official implementation. ([GitHub][5])

**Execution status:** I checked the mathematical language sizes and all 60 manually specified model/trace expectations independently. PM4Py was unavailable in this execution environment, so I have **not measured** its soundness or metric outputs. The script computes them and reports metric failures explicitly rather than substituting invented values.

The soundness API’s tuple result is unpacked explicitly; treating a nonempty `(False, diagnostics)` tuple as a Boolean would be incorrect. The fallback supports the newer Boolean-returning check. ([GitHub][4])

[Standalone validation script](sandbox:/mnt/data/process_model_audit/validate_models.py)

```python
"""Self-contained model audit. Requires PM4Py with POWL support and pandas.
Generated timestamps preserve order only; they are not empirical time evidence.
"""
from collections import deque
from itertools import permutations, product
import random
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import EventLog
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator
from pm4py.objects.petri_net import semantics

LABELS = dict(zip("RCESQAPZ", [
    "Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review",
    "Supplier Confirmation", "Expedite Review", "Approve Configuration",
    "Release Production", "Archive Case",
]))
VARIANTS = [
    (12, "RCESAPZ"), (7, "RECSAPZ"), (4, "RCEAPZ"),
    (3, "RECAPZ"), (2, "RCSEAPZ"), (1, "RCESQAPZ"),
    (1, "RECQSAPZ"),
]


def labels(code: str) -> tuple:
    return tuple(LABELS[c] for c in code)


def tree_sequence(*parts: str) -> str:
    return "->(" + ",".join(parts) + ")"


def powl_sequence(*nodes):
    po = StrictPartialOrder(list(nodes))
    # Include transitive closure, not just adjacent edges.
    for i, first in enumerate(nodes):
        for second in nodes[i + 1:]:
            po.order.add_edge(first, second)
    return po


def make_models():
    leaf = {c: repr(name) for c, name in LABELS.items()}
    branches = [tree_sequence(*(leaf[c] for c in code[1:-3]))
                for _, code in VARIANTS]
    model_conservative = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_sequence(
        leaf["R"], "X(" + ",".join(branches) + ")",
        leaf["A"], leaf["P"], leaf["Z"],
    ))

    c, e = Transition(LABELS["C"]), Transition(LABELS["E"])
    s = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR,
                     [Transition(LABELS["S"]), SilentTransition()])
    q = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR,
                     [Transition(LABELS["Q"]), SilentTransition()])
    core = StrictPartialOrder([c, e, s, q])
    for before, after in [(c, s), (c, q), (e, q)]:
        core.order.add_edge(before, after)
    model_balanced = powl_sequence(
        Transition(LABELS["R"]), core, Transition(LABELS["A"]),
        Transition(LABELS["P"]), Transition(LABELS["Z"]),
    )
    model_balanced.validate_partial_orders()

    checks = "+(" + leaf["C"] + "," + leaf["E"] + ")"
    optional_q = "X(" + leaf["Q"] + ",tau)"
    optional_s = "X(" + leaf["S"] + ",tau)"
    core_p = "+(" + optional_s + "," + tree_sequence(checks, optional_q) + ")"
    model_permissive = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_sequence(
        leaf["R"], core_p, leaf["A"], leaf["P"], leaf["Z"],
    ))
    return {
        "conservative": model_conservative,
        "balanced": model_balanced,
        "permissive": model_permissive,
    }


def as_event_log(obj):
    # Supports both historical EventLog and newer DataFrame parser results.
    return obj if isinstance(obj, EventLog) else pm4py.convert_to_event_log(obj)


def marking_key(marking):
    # Place objects, not place names: distinct places may have equal names.
    return frozenset((p, int(n)) for p, n in marking.items() if n)


def bounded_language(net, im, fm, max_visible=8, max_states=100_000):
    """Enumerate visible words using actual Petri-net firing semantics.

    Explore (marking, visible-prefix) pairs, including silent transitions.
    Report any visible-length truncation; abort on state-space overflow.
    """
    queue = deque([(im, ())])
    seen = {(marking_key(im), ())}
    accepted, clipped = set(), False
    final_key = marking_key(fm)
    while queue:
        marking, word = queue.popleft()
        if marking_key(marking) == final_key:
            accepted.add(word)
        for transition in semantics.enabled_transitions(net, marking):
            next_word = word if transition.label is None else word + (transition.label,)
            if len(next_word) > max_visible:
                clipped = True
                continue
            next_marking = semantics.execute(transition, net, marking)
            if next_marking is None:
                raise RuntimeError("Enabled transition could not fire")
            key = (marking_key(next_marking), next_word)
            if key not in seen:
                if len(seen) >= max_states:
                    raise RuntimeError("State cap reached: no exhaustive claim is valid")
                seen.add(key)
                queue.append((next_marking, next_word))
    return accepted, clipped, len(seen)


def mathematical_languages():
    """Independent specification oracle, not derived from the Petri nets."""
    result = {"conservative": {labels(code) for _, code in VARIANTS},
              "balanced": set(), "permissive": set()}
    for has_s, has_q in product([False, True], repeat=2):
        activities = "CE" + ("S" if has_s else "") + ("Q" if has_q else "")
        for middle in permutations(activities):
            pos = {a: i for i, a in enumerate(middle)}
            if has_q and not (pos["C"] < pos["Q"] and pos["E"] < pos["Q"]):
                continue
            word = labels("R" + "".join(middle) + "APZ")
            result["permissive"].add(word)
            if not has_s or pos["C"] < pos["S"]:
                result["balanced"].add(word)
    return result


# Expectations are manually specified in conservative/balanced/permissive order.
SUITE = {

    "RCEQAPZ": (False, True, True),

    "RCEQSAPZ": (False, True, True),
    "RCSEQAPZ": (False, True, True),
    "RECSQAPZ": (False, True, True),

    "RSCEAPZ": (False, False, True),
    "RSECQAPZ": (False, False, True),
    "RCQESAPZ": (False, False, False),  # Q before E
    "RCAPZ": (False, False, False),    # A without E
    "RCEPZ": (False, False, False),    # P without A
    "RCEASPZ": (False, False, False),  # S after A
    "RCEQQAPZ": (False, False, False), # repeated Q
    "RCECEAPZ": (False, False, False), # repeated principal checks

}


def main():
    print("PM4Py version:", pm4py.__version__)
    trace_strings = [",".join(labels(code))
                     for count, code in VARIANTS for _ in range(count)]
    log = as_event_log(pm4py.parse_event_log_string(trace_strings))
    assert len(log) == 30
    assert sum(len(trace) for trace in log) == 205

    expected_dfg_codes = {
        ("R", "C"): 19, ("R", "E"): 11,
        ("C", "E"): 17, ("E", "C"): 11,
        ("C", "S"): 9, ("E", "S"): 13, ("S", "E"): 2,
        ("E", "A"): 6, ("C", "A"): 3, ("S", "A"): 20,
        ("S", "Q"): 1, ("C", "Q"): 1,
        ("Q", "S"): 1, ("Q", "A"): 1,
        ("A", "P"): 30, ("P", "Z"): 30,
    }
    dfg, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    assert dict(dfg) == {(LABELS[a], LABELS[b]): n
                         for (a, b), n in expected_dfg_codes.items()}
    assert starts == {LABELS["R"]: 30}
    assert ends == {LABELS["Z"]: 30}

    models = make_models()
    oracle = mathematical_languages()
    languages, metric_rows, test_rows = {}, [], []
    for model_index, (name, model) in enumerate(models.items()):
        net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
        checker = getattr(pm4py, "check_soundness", None)
        if checker is not None:
            result = checker(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)
        else:
            result = pm4py.check_is_sound(net, im, fm)
        is_sound = result[0] if isinstance(result, tuple) else result
        if not is_sound:
            raise RuntimeError(f"{name}: soundness check failed: {result}")
        print(name, "sound:", is_sound,
              "places/transitions/arcs:", len(net.places), len(net.transitions), len(net.arcs))

        language, clipped, states = bounded_language(net, im, fm)
        if clipped:
            raise RuntimeError(f"{name}: length bound truncated exploration")
        assert language == oracle[name], f"{name}: code differs from formal specification"
        languages[name] = language
        assert all(tuple(e["concept:name"] for e in trace) in language for trace in log)
        print(name, "complete visible language size:", len(language), "states:", states)

        for code, expected in SUITE.items():
            actual = labels(code) in language
            assert actual == expected[model_index], (name, code, actual, expected)
            test_rows.append({"model": name, "trace": code, "accepted": actual})

        # Sample as a separate smoke test, not as a language-inclusion proof.
        random.seed(2026)
        sample = as_event_log(pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm, parameters={
            "noTraces": 100, "maxTraceLength": 12,
            "add_only_if_fm_is_reached": True,
        }))
        sample_words = {tuple(e["concept:name"] for e in trace) for trace in sample}
        assert sample_words <= language
        print(name, "sample cases/unique words:", len(sample), len(sample_words))

        metrics = {
            "fitness_alignments": lambda: pm4py.fitness_alignments(
                log, net, im, fm, multi_processing=False),
            "precision_alignments": lambda: pm4py.precision_alignments(
                log, net, im, fm, multi_processing=False),
            "generalization_tbr": lambda: pm4py.generalization_tbr(log, net, im, fm),
            "simplicity_petri_net": lambda: pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm),
        }
        for metric, compute in metrics.items():
            try:
                value, error = compute(), None
            except Exception as exc:
                value, error = None, f"{type(exc).__name__}: {exc}"
            metric_rows.append({"model": name, "metric": metric, "value": value,
                                "error": error})

    assert languages["conservative"] < languages["balanced"] < languages["permissive"]
    assert [len(languages[n]) for n in models] == [7, 12, 18]
    print(pd.DataFrame(metric_rows).to_string(index=False))
    print(pd.DataFrame(test_rows).to_string(index=False))
    print("All formal-language and manual-suite assertions passed.")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

**What the validation establishes—and what it does not.**

The bounded explorer uses actual enabled-transition and firing semantics, including silent transitions. It compares the resulting accepted words against a separately defined mathematical specification. For these loop-free candidates, eight visible events cover the entire language; truncation or state-limit failures prevent an exhaustive claim. The firing interface used is provided by PM4Py’s Petri-net semantics module. ([GitHub][6])

Sampled playout is an additional smoke test only. The final-marking option excludes incomplete runs, and the sampling length limit is deliberately above the candidates’ maximum visible length. Its frequencies are not fitted business probabilities. ([GitHub][7])

The alignment metrics evaluate replay fitness and enabled behavior at observed prefixes; they do not identify causal dependencies or physical concurrency. ([GitHub][8])

**No sampled playout proves language inclusion.** If loops are later introduced, bounded exploration generally establishes only bounded-language results unless additional termination and completeness arguments are supplied.

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

**Use `model_balanced` immediately for descriptive monitoring and explanation, with `model_permissive` as the sensitivity model.** Retain the conservative candidate as a versioned reference catalogue of observed behavior.

Do not use any candidate alone to certify that an external-component order satisfied Supplier requirements. Likewise, a violation of the balanced model’s `C ≺ S` edge should initially be reported as **“unexpected Supplier completion order—dependency unresolved,”** not as a confirmed compliance breach.

## Resolve the existing cases first

Reconcile all 30 cases against source records, prioritizing the seven supplier-absent cases, both expedited cases, the two `C S E` cases, and the eleven Engineering-first cases. These groups overlap.

For each missing S, assign a documented status: **not required; performed but lost in export; required but apparently omitted; or unresolved**. Preserve the original export and link any repaired event to its source evidence rather than silently replacing the original observation.

Resolve S1/S2 through the policy owner and workflow configuration: determine whether Credit-before-Engineering is a universal requirement, a historical policy, a product-specific branch, or merely normal practice.

## Collect information that separates the hypotheses

| Data to collect                                                                                                            | Ambiguity it resolves                                                                                                |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Stable case ID, activity-instance ID, source event ID, and source-system identifier                                        | Correlation errors, duplicate handling, and source-to-export reconciliation.                                         |
| Lifecycle `start` and `complete` timestamps for C, E, S, and Q                                                             | Strict sequence versus actual overlap; whether apparent Engineering-first order results from ties or real execution. |
| Timestamp precision, clock/timezone metadata, ingestion time, and tie indicator                                            | Whether recorded total order should be treated as strict chronology.                                                 |
| “Credit information ready” and “Engineering information ready” milestones, plus the milestone used to enable Supplier work | Whether D5 concerns a completed check, intermediate information, either source, or both.                             |
| Component IDs, external/stocked status, and the Supplier-required decision with its effective time                         | Genuine optionality versus hidden conditional obligation, including mixed-component orders.                          |
| Supplier confirmation IDs linked to components and a case-level completeness status                                        | Whether one visible S represents all required confirmations or only one component.                                   |
| Expedite eligibility, reason, authorization, and process-policy version                                                    | Whether Q/S combinations are truly transferable across contexts.                                                     |
| Resource/team assignment and workflow enablement records                                                                   | Context-dependent sequential routing versus parallel capability that happens to be scheduled sequentially.           |
| Integration acknowledgements, failed deliveries, retries, and backfill identifiers                                         | Event loss mechanisms and whether missingness depends on case type or activity order.                                |

A practical initial collection target is the **next 100 completed cases**, extended as necessary to cover external, stocked, mixed-component, and expedited cases. This is an operational starting point, not a statistical guarantee that the relevant rare behavior will appear.

## Prioritize maximally informative observations

| Future observation                                                             | Main discriminatory value                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `R C E Q A P Z`, with verified stocked components and complete logging         | Supports legitimate Q-without-S behavior; refutes the conservative co-occurrence restriction.                        |
| `R C S E Q A P Z` or `R E C S Q A P Z`                                         | Tests whether permitted mechanisms recombine as the balanced candidate assumes.                                      |
| `R E S C A P Z`, with reliable non-tied completions                            | Refutes the balanced Credit-completion gate while remaining compatible with permissive behavior.                     |
| `R S C E A P Z` or `R S E C A P Z`, with recorded information-ready milestones | Distinguishes intermediate-information enablement from a requirement that at least one principal check finish first. |
| Verified intervals with `max(C.start, E.start) < min(C.complete, E.complete)`  | Demonstrates overlapping check intervals and challenges a strictly sequential C/E execution hypothesis.              |
| External cases with source-confirmed S but no exported S                       | Directly identifies the observation-loss mechanism rather than inferring optionality from absence.                   |
| Repeated S events tied to different components                                 | Challenges the shared at-most-one case-level abstraction and motivates a component-aware model.                      |

There is an important identifiability limit: observing more `C E` and `E C` completion orders alone cannot distinguish `C ∥ E` from `X(CE, EC)`. A simple activity-level XOR `X(C,E)` is already contradicted by every trace because both checks occur; the unresolved XOR hypothesis concerns **alternative sequential routes**. Lifecycle evidence and branch/enablement metadata are necessary to distinguish these mechanisms.

Absence of overlap also does not prove that parallel execution is forbidden: resource availability can serialize tasks that are independently enabled.

## Validate prospectively rather than rewarding reconstruction

Freeze these model versions before assessing new cases. Report both case-weighted coverage and variant-level coverage, with separate results for component source, Q presence, policy version, and data-quality status. Variant-grouped checks on the seven existing variants can reveal sensitivity, but are not a replacement for genuinely new observations.

Maintain separate outcomes for **hard-rule violations, model-only novelty, and unresolved data quality**. A future Q-before-check trace should trigger policy and logging investigation—not automatic relaxation of D6 merely to improve fitness.

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

| Modeling decision                                                                    | Calibrated confidence                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| R first, Z last, and approval before release within the chosen scope                 | High: explicit high-confidence rules and unanimous observed support.                                        |
| Both principal checks before approval                                                | Medium-to-high as a normative restriction; fully supported descriptively in this sample.                    |
| Either recorded C/E completion order must be represented                             | High for this export.                                                                                       |
| C and E actually execute concurrently                                                | Low: testimony is contested and lifecycle evidence is absent.                                               |
| Credit completion universally precedes Supplier completion                           | Low-to-moderate as a generalization, despite holding for all 23 recorded S occurrences.                     |
| Supplier absence sometimes reflects legitimate optionality                           | Moderate from S3/S4; unidentified at the individual-case level.                                             |
| Allocation of the seven absences between genuine skips, logging loss, and violations | Low; no defensible case-level allocation is currently available.                                            |
| Q is optional, once at most, and follows both checks                                 | Medium-to-high as a policy-based hypothesis; empirical evidence alone is sparse.                            |
| Q may occur on either side of S                                                      | Medium-to-high within the stated policy scope, supported by both observed orderings and S6.                 |
| Supplier-first completion is legitimate through intermediate information             | Plausible but low-confidence; suitable for sensitivity analysis, not automatic authorization.               |
| No repetitions or additional process outcomes are needed                             | High only for reconstructing these 30 traces; low as a universal company-process claim.                     |
| The three specified languages contain 7, 12, and 18 complete traces                  | High mathematical confidence; independent enumeration agrees. PM4Py runtime outputs remain unmeasured here. |

**A materially different interpretation remains plausible.** Finance’s statement could be the authoritative current rule, while Engineering-first traces arise from timestamp coarsening, misclassified lifecycle events, or a different policy version. Verified evidence for that interpretation would change the recommendation toward a **policy-segmented sequential normative model**, with the discrepant export handled as data-quality or conformance evidence rather than as permission for concurrency.

Similarly, all seven supplier-absent cases could turn out to involve external components and lost events. That would not invalidate an observation-level silent alternative, but it would make treating that alternative as operational permission to skip Supplier Confirmation particularly dangerous.

The balanced candidate is therefore the most useful **current working hypothesis**, not the uniquely identified process. Its disputed Supplier gate should be easy to revise, while the observation model and the compliance specification remain explicitly separate.
