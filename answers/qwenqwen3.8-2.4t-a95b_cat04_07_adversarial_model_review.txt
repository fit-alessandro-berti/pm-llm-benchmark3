# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

This review is for a **model-governance board**, not for a single metric leaderboard. The reported metrics are treated as **evidence with known weaknesses**, not as ground truth.

I distinguish four intended use cases:

1. **Exploration / variant discovery**
   - Goal: understand possible behavior, find frequent paths, generate hypotheses.
   - Tolerance: higher recall and permissiveness can be acceptable.
   - Risk: low precision can mislead users into treating accidental behavior as legitimate.

2. **Conformance enforcement / compliance auditing**
   - Goal: detect violations of mandatory policy, support audits, block or flag noncompliant cases.
   - Tolerance: low false-positive rate is essential. A model must reject known-forbidden behavior even if that reduces fitness on positive logs.
   - Risk: overpermissive models hide violations; overfitting models reject legitimate unseen variants.

3. **Simulation / what-if analysis**
   - Goal: generate plausible future cases, estimate cycle times, test policy changes.
   - Tolerance: needs realistic branching and attribute dependence, not merely control-flow acceptance.
   - Risk: missing external/internal and affected-check attributes make simulation of Supplier Review and correction behavior unreliable.

4. **Stakeholder communication / change management**
   - Goal: make the process understandable to business users and auditors.
   - Tolerance: simplicity matters, but not at the cost of silently removing mandatory controls.
   - Risk: a simple model may be trusted for decisions it cannot support.

Key assumptions for this review:

- Activity labels are the exact strings shown in the models and probes.
- “Accepts” in the probe tables means the trace belongs to the model’s language after treating silent/tau moves as invisible. Alignment fitness is not equivalent to acceptance.
- The log lacks two attributes needed for full policy enforcement:
  - `external/internal` order type;
  - `affected_checks` after `Correct Dossier`.
- The behavior probes B1–B8 are policy test cases, not a complete oracle.
- The reported metrics are not fully comparable because seeds, preprocessing, alignment-cost settings, and held-out case identities were not supplied.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

## Strongest case for Model S

Model S is attractive because it is small, fast, robust to missing optional events, and easy for business users to read. # 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

## Strongest case for Model C

Model C is explicit. Every allowed trace is one of eight named variants. It does not allow skipping Credit Check or Technical Review. It rejects B3 and B4. ## Strongest case against Model C

Model C is brittle, hard to maintain, and likely memorizes training variants rather than learning policy structure. Its holdout fitness collapse from 0.973 to 0.744 is a major warning. The qualitative feedback confirms this: legitimate new combinations of known behavior are rejected.

Model C enumerates full traces instead of expressing reusable structure. For example, alternate ordering of Credit Check and Technical Review is represented by duplicating entire branches. Duplicate labeled transitions make impact analysis difficult: changing the semantics of `Credit Check` may require touching many branches.

Duplicate labels also increase alignment search complexity and reduce interpretability.

- B2: `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Credit Check, Auto Approval, Release Order`

- It does not allow repeated corrections.
- It does not allow correction followed by Auto Approval.
- It does not combine correction with Supplier Review or Expedite Review.
- It does not make the affected-check decision transparent.

### Holdout degradation

The drop to 0.744 holdout fitness is not merely a statistical inconvenience. It indicates that the model cannot replay many held-out cases without log moves or model moves. If those held-out cases are legitimate, Model C will create operational false alarms.

### Maintainability

Model C is difficult to update because each new policy-legal combination may require a new branch. Ownership is hard because the same activity appears in many places. Impact analysis is hard because a change to one activity’s meaning is distributed across duplicated branches.

### High precision may be memorization

The 0.941 training precision is impressive but potentially misleading. It was computed against observed positive traces only. A model that memorizes frequent variants can have high positive-trace precision while failing to reject forbidden behavior and failing to accept valid unseen combinations. Without a curated negative suite, precision does not demonstrate compliance quality.

# 7. Third Alternative

I propose a **structured WF-net / Petri-net backbone plus external attribute-conditioned policy checks**. This is preferable to a pure process-tree enumeration because it can reuse activities, express loops, avoid duplicate branches, and integrate with PM4Py alignment metrics.

The model is designed to be:

- safer than Model S because Credit Check and Technical Review are mandatory;
- more general than Model C because it generates valid combinations instead of enumerating them;
- maintainable because repeated checks are represented by a loop, not duplicated branches;
- honest about missing attributes because Supplier Review and correction-affected checks are handled by explicit post-hoc constraints, not silent optionalization of mandatory controls.

## 7.1 Compact formal description

Let:

- `R` = Register Dossier
- `C` = Credit Check
- `T` = Technical Review
- `D` = Correct Dossier
- `S` = Supplier Review
- `E` = Expedite Review
- `M` = Manager Approval
- `A` = Auto Approval
- `L` = Release Order

The control-flow backbone is:

```text
R
· (mandatory subprocess)
    (C  T)
    · ( D · ( C  T  (C  T)) )*
· (optional Supplier subprocess, started at R)
    S?
· (after mandatory subprocess exits)
    E?
· join Supplier and Expedite before approval
· (M  A)
· L
```

Meaning:

- `R` starts both the mandatory-check subprocess and the optional Supplier subprocess.
- `C` and `T` must both occur initially, in any order.
- After both mandatory checks are complete, the model may either exit the correction loop or perform `D`.
- After `D`, exactly one of the following redo patterns occurs:
  - redo `C` only;
  - redo `T` only;
  - redo both `C` and `T`.
- The correction loop may repeat.
- `E` is optional but can occur only after the mandatory subprocess has exited.
- `S` may occur before, during, or after mandatory checks, but before approval. This is a deliberately flexible choice because the stakeholder agreement does not fully specify Supplier ordering. If the business later requires Supplier only after mandatory checks, the Supplier start can be moved without changing the rest of the model.
- Exactly one of `M` or `A` occurs.
- `L` is last.

External declarative constraints are required because the log lacks attributes:

```text
If external/internal == external:
    Supplier Review is mandatory before approval.
If external/internal == internal:
    Supplier Review is not required; occurrence may be flagged for review.
If affected_checks is known:
    After Correct Dossier, repeated mandatory checks must equal affected_checks.
If affected_checks is unknown:
    Partial redo is allowed by the control-flow model but flagged as policy-undetermined.
```

## 7.2 PM4Py code constructing `model_third`

```python
import pm4py

model_third = (net, im, fm)
```

## 7.3 Declarative dictionaries and external checks

The Petri net captures control-flow structure. The following external checks capture attribute-dependent policy that cannot be enforced from the discovered log alone.

```python
MANDATORY_CHECKS = {"Credit Check", "Technical Review"}
APPROVALS = {"Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"}
OPTIONAL_REVIEWS = {"Supplier Review", "Expedite Review"}

def activities(case):
    return [event["concept:name"] for event in case]

def check_exactly_one_approval(case):
    acts = activities(case)
    approvals = [a for a in acts if a in APPROVALS]
    if len(approvals) == 1:
        return "OK"
    return f"VIOLATION: found {len(approvals)} approval events"

def check_mandatory_checks_before_approval(case):
    acts = activities(case)
    approval_indices = [i for i, a in enumerate(acts) if a in APPROVALS]
    if not approval_indices:
        return "VIOLATION: no approval found"
    first_approval = min(approval_indices)

    missing = []
    for m in MANDATORY_CHECKS:
        if m not in acts[:first_approval]:
            missing.append(m)

    if missing:
        return f"VIOLATION: missing mandatory checks before approval: {missing}"
    return "OK"

def check_expedite_after_mandatory(case):
    acts = activities(case)
    if "Expedite Review" not in acts:
        return "OK"

    expedite_idx = acts.index("Expedite Review")
    mandatory_indices = [acts.index(m) for m in MANDATORY_CHECKS if m in acts]

    if len(mandatory_indices) < 2:
        return "VIOLATION: Expedite Review without both mandatory checks"

    if max(mandatory_indices) > expedite_idx:
        return "VIOLATION: Expedite Review before both mandatory checks"

    approval_indices = [i for i, a in enumerate(acts) if a in APPROVALS]
    if approval_indices and expedite_idx > min(approval_indices):
        return "VIOLATION: Expedite Review after approval"

    return "OK"

def check_supplier_requirement(case):
    """
    Requires case attribute:
      case.attributes["external_internal"] in {True, False, None}
    True means external, False means internal, None means unknown.
    """
    ext = case.attributes.get("external_internal", None)
    acts = activities(case)
    has_supplier = "Supplier Review" in acts

    if ext is True and not has_supplier:
        return "VIOLATION: external order missing Supplier Review"
    if ext is False and has_supplier:
        return "REVIEW: internal order contains Supplier Review"
    if ext is None:
        return "UNKNOWN: external/internal attribute missing; Supplier requirement cannot be decided"
    return "OK"

def check_correction_affected_checks(case):
    """
    Requires case attribute:
      case.attributes["affected_checks"] = iterable containing zero or more of
      {"Credit Check", "Technical Review"} for each correction.

    This simple version assumes one affected-check set for the whole case.
    A production version should store a list aligned with each Correct Dossier event.
    """
    affected = case.attributes.get("affected_checks", None)
    acts = activities(case)

    if "Correct Dossier" not in acts:
        return "OK"

    if affected is None:
        return "UNKNOWN: affected_checks attribute missing; partial redo cannot be validated"

    expected = set(affected) & MANDATORY_CHECKS

    violations = []
    for i, act in enumerate(acts):
        if act == "Correct Dossier":
            repeated = set()
            for later in acts[i + 1:]:
                if later in MANDATORY_CHECKS:
                    repeated.add(later)
                if later in APPROVALS or later == "Release Order":
                    break

            if repeated != expected:
                violations.append(
                    f"after Correct Dossier at position {i}, repeated {sorted(repeated)} "
                    f"but affected_checks says {sorted(expected)}"
                )

    if violations:
        return "VIOLATION: " + "; ".join(violations)
    return "OK"

policy_checks = {
    "exactly_one_approval": check_exactly_one_approval,
    "mandatory_before_approval": check_mandatory_checks_before_approval,
    "expedite_after_mandatory": check_expedite_after_mandatory,
    "supplier_external_rule": check_supplier_requirement,
    "correction_affected_rule": check_correction_affected_checks,
}
```

## 7.4 Explicit semantics

### Mandatory checks

- Credit Check and Technical Review are both required initially.
- They may occur in either order.
- There is no silent skip for either mandatory check.
- A missing mandatory check in the log should reduce fitness and trigger a data-quality or compliance investigation, not be silently accepted.

### Optional Supplier Review

- Supplier Review is optional in the control-flow model because the external/internal attribute is missing.
- Supplier Review may occur after Register and before approval.
- If the case is later known to be external, an external check requires Supplier Review.
- If the case is known to be internal, Supplier Review may be flagged as review-worthy, depending on business policy.

### Expedite Review ordering

- Expedite Review is optional.
- Expedite Review can occur only after both mandatory checks have completed and after the correction loop has been exited.
- Expedite Review can occur before or after Supplier Review because Supplier Review is represented in a concurrent optional branch.

### Exactly-one approval

- The net contains a structural XOR between Manager Approval and Auto Approval.
- Exactly one approval transition must fire before Release Order.

### Correction and recheck behavior

- Correct Dossier can occur only after both mandatory checks have completed.
- Every Correct Dossier must be followed by one of:
  - Credit Check only;
  - Technical Review only;
  - both Credit Check and Technical Review.
- Repeating Credit Check or Technical Review without a preceding Correct Dossier is structurally impossible in the net.
- The model allows partial redo because P6 says only affected checks should repeat. However, because the log lacks `affected_checks`, the model cannot verify which partial redo is correct. That verification is delegated to the external check or manual review.

### Missing events

- Missing optional Supplier or Expedite events are represented by silent skips.
- Missing mandatory events are not represented by silent skips.
- This avoids solving missing mandatory data by silently treating every activity as optional.

## 7.5 Limitations caused by missing attributes

1. **Missing external/internal attribute**
   - The model cannot enforce Supplier Review as mandatory for external orders inside the control-flow net.
   - It must rely on case attributes or manual review.
   - If the attribute remains missing, Supplier-related compliance cannot be fully automated.

2. **Missing affected-check attribute**
   - The model allows Credit-only, Technical-only, and both-check redo after correction.
   - This is necessary to avoid overchecking, but it also means B6-style traces are accepted as structurally possible even when the business cannot confirm they were policy-correct.
   - Without `affected_checks`, partial redo remains policy-ambiguous.

3. **Supplier ordering ambiguity**
   - I allow Supplier Review to occur before or during mandatory checks because the stakeholder agreement does not forbid it.
   - If the business later says Supplier Review must occur only after mandatory checks, the net should be tightened by moving the Supplier branch start from `Register Dossier` to `exit_corrections`.

4. **No repeated optional reviews**
   - The model allows Supplier Review and Expedite Review at most once.
   - If repeated expedite or supplier reviews are legitimate, the model must be extended with loops.

5. **No resource or data-payload semantics**
   - The model does not encode who performed checks, what data changed during Correct Dossier, or why Auto Approval was chosen.

## 9.1 Reproducible case splits

Use deterministic hashing of case IDs so the same split is obtained across runs. Publish preprocessing code, activity normalization rules, case-ID handling, and seed/salt.

```python
import hashlib
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import EventLog

CASE_ID = "concept:name"
ACTIVITY = "concept:name"

def deterministic_case_split(log, test_fraction=0.2, salt="gov-board-2026"):
    indexed = []
    for i, case in enumerate(log):
        cid = str(case.attributes.get(CASE_ID, f"case_{i}"))
        indexed.append((cid, case))

    def hash_fraction(cid):
        h = hashlib.sha256(f"{salt}:{cid}".encode()).hexdigest()
        return int(h[:8], 16) / 0xFFFFFFFF

    test_ids = {
        cid for cid, _ in indexed
        if hash_fraction(cid) < test_fraction
    }

    train = EventLog([case for cid, case in indexed if cid not in test_ids])
    test = EventLog([case for cid, case in indexed if cid in test_ids])

    return train, test
```

```python
def variant_key(case):

def group_variants(log):
```

```python
import random

def drop_activity(log, activity_to_drop, probability, seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    new_log = EventLog()

    for case in log:
        new_case = Trace(attributes=dict(case.attributes))
        for event in case:
            if event[ACTIVITY] == activity_to_drop and rng.random() < probability:
                continue
            new_case.append(Event(dict(event)))
        new_log.append(new_case)

    return new_log

def duplicate_activity(log, activity_to_duplicate, probability, seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    new_log = EventLog()

    for case in log:
        new_case = Trace(attributes=dict(case.attributes))
        for event in case:
            new_case.append(Event(dict(event)))
            if event[ACTIVITY] == activity_to_duplicate and rng.random() < probability:
                new_case.append(Event(dict(event)))
        new_log.append(new_case)

    return new_log
```

Expected behavior:

- Dropping Supplier Review should not heavily penalize fitness if Supplier is optional, but external cases should be flagged by attribute checks.
- Dropping Credit Check or Technical Review should reduce fitness and trigger mandatory-check violations.
- Duplicating Credit Check or Technical Review without Correct Dossier should be flagged or rejected.

## 9.6 Unseen-combination tests

Hold out combinations that are valid under policy but absent from training:

- Technical/Credit order with Auto Approval.
- Supplier + Expedite + Auto Approval.
- Expedite before Supplier.
- Credit-only correction with Auto Approval.
- Technical-only correction with Manager Approval.
- Multiple corrections.

This directly tests whether the model generalizes structurally rather than memorizing variants.

## 9.9 Separate metric reporting

```python
import time
import statistics

def as_net(model):
    if isinstance(model, tuple) and len(model) == 3:
        return model
    return pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)

def evaluate_model(log, model):
    net, im, fm = as_net(model)

    sound = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=False)

    t0 = time.perf_counter()
    fitness = pm4py.fitness_alignments(log, net, im, fm)
    t1 = time.perf_counter()

    precision = pm4py.precision_alignments(log, net, im, fm)
    generalization = pm4py.generalization_tbr(log, net, im, fm)
    simplicity = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm, variant="arc_degree")

    return {
        "soundness": sound,
        "fitness": fitness,
        "precision": precision,
        "generalization": generalization,
        "simplicity": simplicity,
        "alignment_wall_time": t1 - t0,
    }
```

Do not collapse these into a single score unless the board explicitly approves weights and normalization.

## 9.10 Stakeholder comprehension protocol

Replace informal 1–5 scoring with a documented protocol:

- Recruit representative users: process owner, auditor, operations analyst, IT architect.
- Give fixed tasks:
  1. Identify whether a trace is allowed.
  2. Explain why a trace is forbidden.
  3. Describe how to add a new valid variant.
  4. Identify the effect of Correct Dossier.
- Measure:
  - task correctness;
  - time to answer;
  - confidence rating;
  - number of clarifying questions;
  - SUS-style usability score;
  - qualitative misunderstanding patterns.
- Randomize model presentation order.
- Record whether users mistakenly believe Model S enforces mandatory checks.

## 9.11 Decision rule

A model should be rejected for compliance enforcement if any of the following holds:

1. It accepts any hard negative trace, such as B3 or B4.
2. It allows mandatory checks to be skipped silently.
3. It fails soundness.
4. It cannot reject repeated mandatory checks without preceding Correct Dossier.
5. Its precision is computed only on positive traces and no negative suite is available.
6. Its holdout behavior is unstable across reasonable alignment-cost settings.
7. It requires manual exceptions for frequent legitimate variants.
8. It cannot be maintained under a documented change process.
9. Supplier or correction policy depends on missing attributes and there is no manual-review fallback.
10. Aggregate metrics are good but hard policy probes fail.

Good aggregate metrics should never override hard policy failures.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

## Model S

Model S should **not** be used for conformance enforcement, compliance auditing, or automated blocking.

It may be used only for:

- early exploration;
- missing-event diagnosis;
- informal stakeholder discussions;
- identifying candidate variants.

If used for exploration, it must be clearly labeled as **non-normative**. Users must be warned that it allows forbidden traces such as approval without mandatory checks.

Recommendation: **retire Model S as a compliance model**, but optionally retain it as an exploratory baseline.

## Model C

Model C should not be the primary production model. It is too brittle, too hard to maintain, and too likely to reject legitimate unseen combinations.

It may be temporarily useful as:

- a variant catalog;
- an audit artifact for known high-frequency traces;
- a regression baseline for exact variant checking.

However, it should not be used for general conformance enforcement because:

- it fails valid unseen combinations;
- it handles correction poorly;
- it duplicates labels and branches;
- it is hard to update;
- its high precision is not validated against negatives.

Recommendation: **retire Model C as the primary compliance model**, but keep it under version control as a historical variant reference if useful.

## Third alternative

The third alternative is the recommended path for operational and compliance use, subject to conditions.

Before deployment, it must satisfy:

1. Pass all hard negative probes with zero forbidden acceptances.
2. Pass all approved positive policy probes.
3. Be validated with a curated negative suite, not only positive logs.
4. Be checked for soundness and proper completion.
5. Use published alignment-cost settings.
6. Use reproducible train/holdout splits.
7. Be tested under missing-event and duplicate-event perturbations.
8. Be enriched with external/internal attributes or routed to manual review when the attribute is missing.
9. Be enriched with affected-check attributes or routed to manual review when partial correction cannot be validated.
10. Be documented with a change-management procedure for adding new valid behavior.
11. Be benchmarked for alignment runtime at expected log volume.
12. Be evaluated with a formal stakeholder comprehension protocol.

Until those conditions are met, the third alternative should be used in **shadow mode**: run it alongside current operations, collect exceptions, and refine policy rules before enforcement.

# 11. Dissenting View

The recommendation to replace Model C with `model_third` is overconfident. `model_third` still cannot enforce the two most politically sensitive rules because the log lacks external/internal and affected-check attributes. It therefore converts hard compliance questions into optional control-flow plus after-the-fact Python checks, which may be weaker than Model C’s explicit enumeration. Its silent redo transitions can silently assume Technical Review or Credit Check remains valid after Correct Dossier; in an audit, that is close to treating mandatory checks as optional. Model C’s low holdout fitness is not necessarily a defect: if unseen combinations are not approved, rejecting them is a conservative control. The third model also adds Petri-net complexity, invisible synchronization, and a validation harness that the organization may not be able to maintain. Until the missing attributes are populated and a curated negative suite proves that `model_third` never accepts forbidden traces, Model C plus a manual exception workflow may be the safer compliance artifact.

Additional evidence that would change my decision:

- A curated positive/negative benchmark showing `model_third` rejects all forbidden traces and accepts all policy-valid unseen combinations.
- Evidence that external/internal and affected-check attributes are available, reliable, and populated before approval.
- A stakeholder comprehension study showing users understand the third model’s flags and do not mistake optional Supplier behavior as compliance approval.
- Maintenance evidence: adding a new valid variant requires a small, local model change rather than a new enumerated branch.
- Runtime evidence: alignment with `model_third` is acceptable at production scale under fixed alignment-cost settings.
- Perturbation evidence: missing mandatory events are surfaced as violations or data-quality incidents, not silently absorbed.
