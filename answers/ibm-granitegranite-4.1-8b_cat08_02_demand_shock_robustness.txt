## 1. Stress scenarios  

| # | Scenario Description | Key Drivers |
|---|----------------------|-------------|
| **S1** | **Demand Mix Shift Toward Urgent & ComplexPanel** | Winter infection wave spikes the proportion of Urgent cases to 35% and Complexpanel to 30%, while Routine drops to 30%. Peakperiod load patterns dominate 24h operations. |
| **S2** | **AssayMachine Degradation / Outage** | M2 experiences a 20% throughput loss (runtime 15%) and occasional failure events (10% of attempts) during highload periods. M1 capacity unchanged. |
| **S3** | **Medical Validator / SpecialistReview Absence** | One medical validator is unavailable for an entire week; specialist review is also off by half due to scheduled maintenance (Fridaynight blackout). |
| **S4** | **Courier Arrival Compression / Delay** | Courier peak arrivals at 10:00h compress to a 30min window, causing sample intake queues to swell to 7h median (vs 1h). Nightshift deliveries delayed by 4h. |

Each scenario stresses a different link in the valuestream and forces the process to shift bottleneck focus.

## 2. Bottleneckmigration analysis  

### **Scenario S1 – Demand Mix Shift Toward Urgent & ComplexPanel**

| Step | NormalPeriod Bottleneck | PeakPeriod Bottleneck (Scenario) | Migration Rationale |
|------|---------------------------|-----------------------------------|----------------------|
| **Register  Receive sample** | Low utilization (68%  85%) | Intake still OK, but **Manual accession correction** queue spikes (14h) because Urgent cases often need rapid accession tweaks. |
| **Preanalytical check  Test preparation** | 74%  96% utilization | **Test preparation** becomes overloaded (batches cannot hold Urgent overrides); **Batch wait** (previously idle) now a 12h queue for Routine. |
| **Assay run** | M1 71%  98% utilization | **Urgentfirst dispatch** (I1) pushes Urgent into M1, creating **preanalytical split**: Complexpanel still go to M2, intermittently overloading it when M1 fails. |
| **Technical validation  Medical validation** | TV 76%  101% | **Medical validation** queue balloons (22h) because Complexpanel require extra validation; Urgent cases bypass partially, but many still need rapid medical signoff. |
| **Specialist review** | 43%  87% | **Specialist review** queue elongates (36h) for Complexpanel; Urgent cases rarely wait here. |

*Key migration*: Priority routing (I1) moves bottleneck from **Test preparation  Batch wait** to **Medical validation**. Relieving one station (e.g., adding a temporary medicalvalidation shift) risks overloading **Technical validation** due to increased repeat assays (more TV  MV handoffs).

---

### **Scenario S2 – AssayMachine Degradation / Outage (M2)**

| Step | Normal Bottleneck | Degraded Bottleneck | Migration Effect |
|------|-------------------|----------------------|------------------|
| **Test preparation  Assay run** | TV 5h  15h (peak) | **Assay run** on M2 becomes a 30h queue; M1 (Urgent/Complex) still OK. | **Batch wait** is released but **Technical validation** now sees more repeats (failed M2 assays). |
| **Repeat assay  Assay run** | 3h  8h | **Repeat loop** lengthens to 22h  pushes **Technical validation** queue upstream. | Relief at M2 (e.g., dedicated M1 to Complexpanel) shifts overload to **Technical validation** and **Medical validation**. |
| **Medical validation** | 3.5h  16h | **Medical validation** queue lengthens because of increased repeatassay handoffs. | Overloading TV if we add resources there; thus robust redesign must “buffer” repeat cycles at **Assay run** stage. |

*Migration pattern*: Removing capacity from M2 (by routing more Routine to M2) would merely push the delay downstream to TV/MV; therefore a **dedicated M1 for critical panels** (I3) is required, but only if spare capacity exists – otherwise repeat loops amplify risk.

---

### **Scenario S3 – Medical Validator / SpecialistReview Absence**

| Step | Normal Bottleneck | Absence Impact | Migration |
|------|-------------------|----------------|-----------|
| **Technical validation  Medical validation** | 8h  24h (peak) | **Medical validation** queue swells to 22h  Urgent cases miss 18h SLA, raising rework. | Adding a **temporary evening medicalvalidation shift** (I5) reduces immediate queue; however, the **Specialist review** queue (already 5h) may now receive more overflow, creating a new bottleneck at **Holdforspecialist**. |
| **Hold for specialist review  Medical validation** | 18h  48h (peak) | **Specialist review** loss of half capacity (87%  43%)  queue jumps to 36h, causing rework spillover into TV. | Crosstraining of technical validators (I4) can absorb some Specialist work, but if surge occurs, **Technical validation** becomes the new choke. |

*Migration insight*: Any “quickfix” at Medical validation must anticipate spillover to Technical validation; therefore **buffer capacity at TV** (e.g., extra TV staff) is a robust safeguard.

---

### **Scenario S4 – Courier Arrival Compression / Delay**

| Step | Normal Bottleneck | Courier Compression Impact | Migration |
|------|-------------------|---------------------------|-----------|
| **Registration  Receive sample** | Reg utilization 68%  85% | **Courier intake** queue spikes to 7h median  **Preanalytical check** queue inflates (9h vs 2h). | **Early preanalytical defect triage** (I6) rejects poor samples early, reducing intake load; however, if triage fails, **Manual accession correction** queue grows (14h). |
| **Manual accession correction  Test preparation** | 3.0h  14.0h (normal) | **Manual accession correction** becomes saturated, pulling back **Preanalytical check** workers (error rate  after >9h shifts). | Adding **eighthour registration automation** (I8) can offload registration clerks, but still leaves **Preanalytical check** as the new bottleneck if triage rejects too aggressively. |

*Migration cue*: Solutions that reduce intake frontend (automation, early triage) risk overloading the **Manual accession correction** step; thus a **dualtriage** approach (I6 + I8) is needed to keep both stations balanced.

## 3. Robust redesign package  

### **Core interventions (robust across multiple scenarios)**  

| Intervention | Why it is robust | Key assumptions |
|--------------|------------------|-----------------|
| **I3 – Dedicated M1 for Urgent & Complexpanel** | Provides a guaranteed highthroughput path for the cases that cause the most penalty (Urgent SLA) and complex validation demand. Keeps **Assay machines** from being starved when M2 degrades (S2) and prevents prioritystarvation of Routine (S1). Works without increasing total assay capacity, merely shifting workload. | Assumes spare capacity on M1 (10% buffer) and that M1 runtime is comparable to M2 for Complexpanel assays. |
| **I4 – Crosstrain two Technical Validators to perform Manual Accession Correction** | Reduces the **Preanalytical check  Manual accession correction** queue (S1, S4) and simultaneously eases **Technical validation** load when Medical validation is absent (S3). Crosstraining time (4weeks) must be scheduled before anticipated peaks. | Assumes minimal loss in validator accuracy during dual role and that the two validators are not otherwise needed for urgent signoffs during early training weeks. |
| **I5 – Temporary Evening MedicalValidation Shift** | Directly mitigates **Medical validator absence** (S3) and also supplies extra capacity during winter spikes when Routine & Urgent demand coexist, limiting queue buildup at **Medical validation**. Evening shift can be ramped up/down based on realtime queue length. | Assumes staff availability and that the shift does not interfere with existing nightshift operations. |
| **I6 – Early Preanalytical Defect Triage (2hour window)** | Lowers the **Courier intake  Preanalytical check** queue (S4) and cuts upstream rework loops (S1). Triage is performed by a junior analyst, keeping error rate low under normal utilization. | Requires clear triage criteria to avoid overly aggressive rejections that could later flood **Manual accession correction**. |
| **I8 – Automated Registration for orders with complete digital metadata** | Offloads **Registration clerks** during highintake periods (S1, S4), preventing the 9hour shift errorrate spike. Automation reduces frontend queue, giving more clerks capacity for **Preanalytical check**. | Assumes >80% of incoming orders have the required digital metadata ready; otherwise manual fallback needed. |

### **Why these are robust**  

* **Scenario crosscoverage** – Each intervention addresses at least two of the four stress scenarios:  
  * I3 solves S1 (Urgent/Complex demand) and S2 (M2 degradation).  
  * I4 solves S1 (preanalytical bottleneck) and S3 (Medical validator shortage).  
  * I5 solves S3 (Medical validator absence) and S1 (peak medical validation queue).  
  * I6 solves S4 (courier compression) and S1 (preanalytical queue).  
  * I8 solves S1 (registration overload) and S4 (intake spikes).  

* **Tradeoff awareness** – The plan explicitly acknowledges downstream overload risks (e.g., I4 may increase Technical validation load, hence the need for I5 to buffer it).  

* **No blanket capacity expansion** – Instead of simply staffing more, we **reallocate existing resources** and **introduce role flexibility**, preserving cost efficiency while improving resilience.

### **Potential failure points**  

| Intervention | Likely failure mode |
|--------------|---------------------|
| I3 | If M1 fails (unexpected hardware fault) and no backup alternatives exist  Urgent & Complexpanel cases stall. |
| I4 | Dualrole overload could reduce validation quality  higher repeatassay rate and increased rework. |
| I5 | Evening shift fatigue could cause quality drop; also may clash with existing nightshift processes. |
| I6 | Overaggressive triage may reject highvalue samples, leading to downstream manual corrections and a surge in **Manual accession correction** queue. |
| I8 | Insufficient digital metadata  manual fallback still requires clerks, negating frontend benefit. |

## 4. Scenariospecific policies  

| Policy | Trigger Condition | Active Scenario | Actions |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|---------|
| **P1 – UrgentFirst Dispatch Override** | **Preanalytical check  Test preparation** queue >10h *and* **Urgent case proportion** 30% of current batch | S1 (high Urgent mix) | Autoroute all detected Urgent orders directly to **M1** (if free) before batching; flag for **Manual accession correction** if accession data incomplete. |
| **P2 – M2 Degradation Alert** | **Assay run on M2** average cycle time >30% above baseline (historical 5h) for two consecutive hours | S2 | Switch **all Routine** orders to **M2** (if M1 has spare capacity) and **pause** new Complexpanel submissions to M2; alert operations lead. |
| **P3 – Medical Validator Absence** | **Medical validation** queue length >20h *or* scheduled absence for >4days | S3 | Deploy **temporary evening shift** (I5); if queue exceeds 30h, **preemptively release** nonurgent Routine results with “pending validation” flag and prioritize **Urgent** and **Complexpanel** for manual signoff. |
| **P4 – Courier Compression Alert** | **Courier intake** median queue >5h or **sample arrival** variance >30% of normal (peaked at 10:00h) | S4 | Activate **early triage** (I6) and **increase** preanalytical check staffing (overtime) for the next 4h; if triage rejects >20% of arrivals, **suspend** triage and revert to manual testing. |
| **P5 – BatchWait Prioritization Toggle** | **Batch wait** queue >8h *and* **Urgent case proportion** 25% | S1 (peak) | Disable routine batching for Urgent cases; allow “singletube” mode to M1 directly, preserving Urgent SLA. |

These policies are **dynamic**, triggered by realtime queue signals, and can be turned off once the stressor subsides.

## 6. Residual vulnerabilities  

1. **SpecialistReview Bottleneck** – Even with P3, the **specialistreview** step (absent every second Friday) remains a *calendardriven* vulnerability. If a major wave coincides with that absence, Complexpanel cases may still experience long review queues, risking delayed clinical decisions.  
2. **AssayMachine Failure Cascades** – While I3 shifts critical cases to M1, M1 itself could experience hidden degradation; the redesign assumes M1’s reliability exceeds M2’s, but without redundancy this remains a singlepoint risk.  
3. **CrossTraining Quality Drop** – I4 relies on dualrole precision. During peak periods, the additional load on technical validators may increase error rates, inadvertently raising repeatassay rates and offsetting rework reductions.  
4. **Automation Metadata Reliance** – I8’s effectiveness hinges on >80% completeness of digital metadata. Any surge in analog orders (e.g., during a courier delay) could revert to manual registration, negating frontend gains.  
5. **Policy Trigger Lag** – Realtime queue signals (e.g., P1, P4) may have a latency of 510minutes, allowing a bottleneck to develop before the policy activates, especially during sudden courier spikes.  

These vulnerabilities are **mitigated** by continuous monitoring (see Section7) and secondary fallbacks (e.g., a reserve of **parttime specialist reviewers** for Friday gaps).

## 7. Minimum monitoring dashboard (daily during peak periods)

| Leading Indicator | Definition | Threshold / Warning Band | Why it is leading |
|-------------------|------------|--------------------------|-------------------|
| **Courier Intake Queue** | Median time from sample arrival to **Preanalytical check** initiation | >5h (yellow) >8h (red) | Early sign of intake overload; triggers P4 or I6. |
| **Preanalytical Check  Manual Accession Correction** | Average queue length for accessioncorrection requests | >3h (yellow) >6h (red) | Indicates triage or registration issues; warns before TV bottlenecks. |
| **Test Preparation  Batch Wait** | Queue length for batching stage (expressed in minutes) | >10min (yellow) >20min (red) | Signals upcoming downstream pressure; activates P5 if Urgent proportion rises. |
| **AssayRun Latency (M1/M2)** | Average time from **Test preparation** to **Assay run** start per machine | >10min (yellow) >20min (red) | Early detection of M1/M2 degradation; invokes P2 if M2 latency spikes. |
| **Technical Validation  Medical Validation** | Queue length for TVtoMV handoffs | >5h (yellow) >8h (red) | Predicts Medical validator overload; prompts P3 or extra evening shift. |
| **Medical Validation Queue** | Total pending medical validations | >12h (yellow) >20h (red) | Directly maps to Urgent SLA risk; ensures P3 stays active. |
| **Specialist Review Queue** | Pending cases in specialist review (average wait) | >4h (yellow) >6h (red) | Forewarns Complexpanel delays; may require temporary additional reviewer. |
| **Rework Rate** | % of cases needing repeat assay or accession correction | >5% (yellow) >8% (red) | Indicates quality or capacity pressure; may trigger I4 or P4. |
| **Urgent SLA Breach Rate** | % of Urgent cases released >18h | >2% (yellow) >5% (red) | Core performance metric; drives P1 and P3 activation. |

*Lagging outcome metrics* (cycle time p90 per case type, overall rework %) are **monitored weekly** for trend analysis, but the dashboard focuses on *leading* signals that can be acted on within the same day.

### Summary
The proposed redesign blends **resource reallocation (I3)**, **crossfunctional training (I4)**, **temporary staffing (I5)**, **early triage (I6)**, and **automation (I8)**, supported by **dynamic policies (P1P5)** that react to realtime queue pressures. This structure anticipates the four stress scenarios, acknowledges bottleneck migrations, and provides a clear monitoring framework to keep the laboratory stable across winter infection waves, equipment issues, staffing gaps, and courier fluctuations. Residual risks (specialist review absence, assaymachine reliability, crosstraining quality, metadata completeness, and policy latency) are explicitly identified and mitigated through continuous observation and secondary fallbacks.
