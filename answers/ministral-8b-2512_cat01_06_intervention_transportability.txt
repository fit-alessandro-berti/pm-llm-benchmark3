## 6. Candidate Site B Intervention

### Process Specification:
- **Eligibility**: Digital claims **and** non-legal cases (62% of cases excluded due to originals; 40% excluded due to legal review).
- **Concurrent Tasks**: Identity Check **||** Document Check (only for eligible cases).
- **Statutory Prerequisites**:
  - Originals Receipt must precede Document Check for 62% of cases (cannot parallelize).
  - Legal Review remains sequential for 40% of cases.
- **Queue/WIP Controls**:
  - Limit concurrent Identity/Document tasks to 6 per reviewer (mirroring Site A’s rule).
  - Implement daily queue huddles to balance workload.
- **Resource Responsibilities**:
  - Cross-train 2 identity reviewers and 2 document reviewers to handle concurrent tasks.
  - Legal reviewers and assessors remain separate.
- **Handling Legal/Supplement Loops**:
  - If Legal Review issues a supplement, **defer** Document Check until originals are received (if required) or supplements are resolved.
  - Reassign Document Check tasks to a separate pool for supplement cases.
- **Logging Requirements**:
  - Record timestamps for all task starts/ends, including back-entry events.
  - Track overlap percentages by case type (digital vs. originals).

**Separating mechanism failure from implementation failure**:
The pilot tests two hypotheses:
1. **Mechanism failure**: Parallelization is impossible due to Site B’s constraints (e.g., originals requirement, legal review). This would show no overlap in eligible subgroups.

## 9. Recommendation: **Conditional Go**

### What to Transfer:
- **Parallelize Identity Check || Document Check** for **digital claims only** (38% of cases). This subgroup meets Site A’s core conditions (no originals requirement, no legal review).
- **Implement queue huddles** to manage workload, as Site A’s co-intervention was critical.
- **Cross-train 2 identity and 2 document reviewers** to handle concurrent tasks, mirroring Site A’s resource allocation.

### What Not to Copy:
- **Do not parallelize Document Check for cases requiring originals** (62% of cases). This violates statutory ordering.
- **Do not parallelize legal-review cases** (40% of cases). Legal review is a sequential bottleneck.
- **Do not assume IT locks will be bypassed**. Site B’s legacy system may require explicit workarounds (e.g., timestamp back-entry).

### Subgroup to Enter First:
1. **Digital claims, non-legal** (38% of cases): Highest transportability due to alignment with Site A’s context.
2. **Originals-receipt cases with digital identity**: If originals arrive quickly (e.g., <24h), parallelize Identity Check while waiting for originals.
3. **Legal cases**: Exclude from parallelization; monitor queue saturation separately.

### Expected Benefits:
- **Median cycle time reduction**: ~3–5 hours for eligible cases (digital, non-legal), driven by reduced waiting before assessment.
- **Tail improvement**: p90 may drop by ~10–15 hours if Document Check is the bottleneck.
- **Resource utilization**: Cross-trained reviewers can handle 20% more cases (mirroring Site A’s FTE increase from 18 to 20).

### Risks:
- **Bottleneck shift**: If legal-review cases increase waiting time, p90 may worsen.
- **Rework congestion**: Supplement cases may see higher rework rates (pilot must monitor this).
- **IT lock failure**: If parallelization is not truly concurrent, overlap metrics will mislead.

### Evidence to Trigger Expansion/Modification/Abandonment:
- **Expand**: If pilot shows:
  - Overlap rate > 50% in eligible subgroup.
  - Median cycle time improvement > 4 hours.
  - Rework rate stable or reduced.
- **Modify**: If pilot shows:
  - Overlap rate < 30% (parallelization ineffective).
  - Legal-review queue saturation (add legal reviewers).
  - Originals-receipt delays > 48h (defer Document Check).
- **Abandon**: If pilot shows:
  - No overlap in eligible subgroup (IT locks prevent concurrency).
  - Rework rate > 20% (harmful side effect).
  - Median cycle time improvement < 2 hours (no benefit).
