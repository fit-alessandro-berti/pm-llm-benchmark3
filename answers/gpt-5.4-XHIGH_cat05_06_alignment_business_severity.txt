## 1. Materiality Framework
Severity should be anchored to the business control objective before looking at edit distance. The highest materiality issues are failures of pre-payment fraud and approval controls, especially when money has already left, because exposure is large and reversibility is low. Amount, risk tier, and recent beneficiary-bank change increase exposure. Timing matters: a control recorded after payment is much worse than the same control missing in a low-value unpaid case. Customer/regulatory harm is relevant but usually secondary to payment-control failure here; notification defects matter mainly when they affect statutory notice, complaints, or auditability. Recurrence matters because a small single-case defect can still be a large systemic problem if it repeats. Evidence reliability matters separately: a missing event, mutable cross-system link, or label drift lowers confidence, but it does not by itself prove the business activity never occurred. Model scope also matters: denied and withdrawn claims can generate large raw costs if the model only covers payable claims.

| Severity | Business meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Harmless technical noise or fully fitting behavior. No meaningful control, financial, or customer harm. |
| 2 | Low materiality model/logging mismatch or incomplete evidence with limited direct business harm and no payment-control breach. |
| 3 | Moderate issue: meaningful evidence weakness, policy breach on moderate exposure, or customer-notice deficiency, but not the most critical pre-payment failure. |
| 4 | High issue: material policy/control breach with significant fraud or payment risk, but with some mitigating factor such as lower amount or incomplete proof of exact mechanism. |
| 5 | Critical issue: likely severe control failure on a paid claim, especially high-value/high-risk or payment-before-approval cases where loss/regulatory exposure is immediate and hard to reverse. |

Confidence is not severity. Severity rates consequence if the interpretation is true. Confidence rates how strongly the current evidence supports that interpretation. A case can be high severity and only medium confidence when the consequence would be serious if confirmed, but the evidence still allows multiple explanations. Conversely, a low-severity telemetry issue can have high confidence because the evidence is clear.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk
Alignment cost is a technical distance under a chosen move-cost function, not a business-risk metric. Here every visible log move and model move costs 1, regardless of whether it is an autosave event or a EUR 480,000 fraud-control failure. That design is useful for generic conformance search, but it cannot encode materiality on its own.

1. Uniform cost ignores control criticality. CL-601 and CL-604 both have raw cost 3. CL-601 is UI telemetry around a routine low-value claim. CL-604 is a likely payment-before-approval breach on EUR 90,000. Same technical distance, radically different business severity.
2. Control-flow cost ignores data conditions. CL-609 and CL-610 are raw cost 0 because the executed path fits the tree. Both still violate business policy; CL-610 is especially serious because a recent bank change should have forced Fraud Screen and Manual Approve. A perfectly synchronous alignment can still be a control failure.
3. Hidden moves are fitting by design. CL-605 contains a silent `tau` move with zero cost. That is not “missing work”; it is a routing construct introduced by model conversion. Zero cost here is correctly benign, showing that not all model internals have business meaning.
4. Extra events can be harmless technical noise. CL-603 has raw cost 1 because a notification retry created a duplicate business label, and the month-level evidence shows 74 similar cases from one connector. The cost is informative for integration hygiene, but not for claimant harm or compliance severity.
5. Missing events are not proof that the business activity did not occur. CL-606 has raw cost 1 for missing `Assess Loss`, yet a licensed adjuster signed an external report before approval. CL-608 has raw cost 1 for missing core-system notification, but there is a CRM/portal message within 3 hours. These are evidence-quality questions first, not automatic proof of operational failure.
6. Activity granularity and label design matter. CL-611 has raw cost 2 because `Assess Damage` and `Assess Loss` are treated as different labels. If they are semantically the same activity under different naming, the cost mostly measures dictionary drift, not control failure.
7. Model scope can dominate raw cost. CL-607 and CL-612 each have raw cost 4 because denial and withdrawal are not represented in the payable-claims tree. Those traces look highly deviant technically, yet they carry little or no payment exposure. In fact, the prompt states that most raw-cost `>= 4` cases are denied or withdrawn variants.
8. Irreversibility and timing are absent from the cost function. CL-602 has raw cost 1 even though it involves a paid EUR 480,000 HIGH-risk claim with a recent bank change and no durable fraud-screen evidence. CL-604’s severity comes from money moving before required approval, not from the number of edit operations.

Raw cost is still useful when used narrowly: it can help cluster technical deviation shapes, identify noisy connectors, and direct where to inspect logs or model scope. It becomes dangerous when treated as if it were automatically equivalent to compliance risk, financial exposure, customer harm, or evidential confidence.

def policy_overlay_flags(row: pd.Series) -> pd.Series:
    recent_bank_change = pd.notna(row["recent_bank_change_days"]) and row["recent_bank_change_days"] <= 7
    auto_allowed = (
        row["amount_eur"] <= 5000
        and row["risk_level"] == "LOW"
        and not recent_bank_change
    )
    manual_required = (
        row["amount_eur"] > 50000
        or row["risk_level"] == "HIGH"
        or recent_bank_change
    )
    return pd.Series(
        {
            "recent_bank_change": recent_bank_change,
            "auto_allowed_by_P1": auto_allowed,
            "manual_fraud_required_by_P2": manual_required,
            "policy_auto_breach": bool(row["auto_approve_used"] and not auto_allowed),
            "policy_manual_path_breach": bool(
                manual_required
                and (
                    not row["manual_approve_used"]
                    or not row["fraud_screen_logged"]
                    or row["payment_before_approval"]
                )
            ),
            "scope_variant": bool(row["denied"] or row["withdrawn"]),
            "noise_only": row["deviation_bucket"] in {"noise_ui_telemetry", "noise_retry_duplicate"},
            "external_evidence_gap": bool(
                (row["external_adjuster_doc"] and not row["external_doc_stable_id"])
                or (row["notify_external_logged"] and not row["notify_external_stable_link"])
            ),
        }
    )

summary = pd.concat([summary, summary.apply(policy_overlay_flags, axis=1)], axis=1)
