# 1. Objectives and constraints

The policy should make safe assignments first, protect threatened SLAs, and then balance throughput, continuity, workload, development, and specialist availability. There is no uniquely optimal allocation from this snapshot: activity durations, existing work commitments, downstream processing times, and supervisor availability are missing. The recommendation below is a concrete allocation to validate against those calendars before dispatch, not a guarantee that all work fits today.

**Safety and compliance gates**

- Skill 0 means the resource cannot perform that activity, including under supervision. A training preference does not confer authorization.
- Treat both high and urgent priority as requiring skill 2 or 3, unless a permitted trainee has explicit, scheduled supervision.
- Security requires skill 3, or skill 1–2 with explicit supervision by R7_Gita or R8_Hugo. C001 additionally requires a specialist to perform the review because of its case-specific requirement.
- Final Approval must be finalized by skill 2–3. Skill 1 may prepare material with a named authorized finalizer; preparation alone does not complete the activity.
- A supervised assignment must identify the performer, supervisor, scope, review/sign-off responsibility, and reserved supervision time. Merely naming an unavailable supervisor is insufficient.
- Apply any separately documented access or independence restrictions as eligibility gates. The supplied team labels alone do not establish a prohibition on cross-team assignments.

**Operational constraints**

- Maximum four new assignments per resource in this decision batch. For this proposal, use the more conservative check of four distinct case involvements, including retained assignments, supervision, and consultation. Two cases in a batch still count as two assignments.
- Avoid additional work for high-overtime-risk resources unless the SLA is urgent and no safer alternative exists. The current proposal requires no such exception.
- Check each resource's committed work, new execution time, handover time, and supervision time against their actual available calendar. Open-case counts are a workload signal, not an estimate of hours. Do not double-count a retained case as a newly opened case.
- At least 20% of normal/low assignments should support documented training priorities when SLA and supervision capacity permit. There are nine such cases here, so the target is at least two cases: ceiling(0.20 × 9) = 2. Count a case once, even if it has several learners. Genuine supervised performance on a defined activity segment qualifies; passive observation does not.
- Batch Quality Checks only when every included case has normal/low priority and more than 24 hours of SLA time remaining. Recheck at batch formation and release, and bound the batching delay.

**Preferences and trade-offs**

Minimize predicted SLA misses and severe lateness, avoid preventable errors and rework, reduce excessive workload and overtime exposure, preserve ownership when valuable, develop authorized skills, and retain coverage for scarce activities. Throughput matters alongside these objectives, rather than determining the policy alone.

For an initial, reviewable configuration, classify complexity ≥0.75 as high complexity. This is a proposed threshold, not a fact learned from the data. Give high-cost handovers on these cases a strong presumption of continuity. Keep a competent owner unless an alternative materially improves safety, deadline feasibility, or capacity. An owner at skill 1 can remain only where effective supervision and case-specific rules permit it. C001's specialist requirement overrides this preference; C016's competent owner can remain with expert assistance.

The historical 1.8× rework and 1.4× appeal associations justify caution after repeated reassignments. The 2.7-hour handover delay and 3.3-hour weak-skill service penalty are planning evidence, not deterministic additions to every case or causal estimates. They need adjustment for complexity, activity, priority, and case history.

# 2. Assignment policy

Use a rolling allocation process triggered by arrivals, completions, absence, material SLA deterioration, and scheduled review. Re-evaluation does not automatically mean reassignment: started work and recently stabilized ownership receive protection against unnecessary changes.

**Deadline risk and urgency.** For case j and candidate resource or pair a, estimate:

- W(j,a): time until the performer and any required supervisor can begin;
- H(j,a): transfer and coordination delay;
- S(j,a): activity duration, including learning and review time;
- D(j,a): remaining downstream time to the contractual SLA milestone.

Let T(j,a) be the resulting time-to-completion distribution. Define slack(j,a) = deadline(j) − now − Q90(T(j,a)), and p_late(j,a) = Pr(now + T(j,a) > deadline(j)). Use calendar-aware scheduling and account for overlaps between execution, transfer, and support work. Finishing the next activity before its case deadline does not establish SLA compliance if mandatory downstream activities remain.

For queue ordering, an illustrative urgency score is:

`U(j) = 0.40 × priority(j) + 0.45 × deadline_pressure(j) + 0.15 × aging(j)`

Use priority values urgent=1, high=0.7, normal=0.3, low=0.1. Normalize aging from ready-to-start waiting time. One deadline-pressure function is `1 / (1 + max(best_feasible_slack_hours, 0) / 4)`. Flag cases with no on-time feasible candidate separately for immediate recovery planning. These weights and the four-hour scale are starting settings to validate, not an empirically optimal formula. Aging prevents normal and low work from being indefinitely postponed.

**Candidate scoring.** Normalize these components to a common scale:

| Component | Scoring principle |
|---|---|
| SLA safety | Reward lower candidate-specific lateness probability and severity, with greater weight for urgent cases. |
| Skill match | Reward sufficient capability for the activity and complexity. Add specialist value where expertise is needed; competent workers can receive routine work. Model the execution and review cost of a supervised pair explicitly. |
| Continuity | Reward retaining useful case knowledge. Penalize transfers according to handover band, complexity, prior reassignments, and predicted delay; increase the penalty after two reassignments. |
| Workload fairness | Penalize high projected committed-hours/available-hours utilization, difficult-case concentration, and overtime exposure. Include supervision and coordination work. |
| Training | Reward a matching documented priority only when the learner performs a defined, authorized task with reserved support and feedback. |
| Resilience | Penalize consuming scarce skill capacity needed elsewhere in the queue or by likely arrivals. Value alternative coverage and avoiding dependence on one supervisor. |

After eligibility filtering, maximize a batch score such as:

`sum(w_sla × SLA_safety + w_skill × skill_match + w_cont × continuity + w_train × training − w_load × workload_cost − w_bottle × bottleneck_cost)`

Publish and version the weights, review them with workers, and test their sensitivity. Training and fairness cannot compensate for an unauthorized assignment. Evaluate the whole proposed schedule; selecting every case independently against unchanged workloads can create overload.

```text
allocate(queue, resource_state, decision_time):
    snapshot skills, owners, workload, calendars, and policy version
    distinguish existing commitments from additional demand

    for each case:
        generate individual and explicitly supervised candidate assignments
        reject skill-0 performers and activity-rule violations
        reject high/urgent trainees without effective supervision
        reject high-overtime-risk candidates unless urgent and no safer option exists
        estimate execution, downstream, transfer, and support requirements
        identify continuity-preserving options and candidate SLA risk

    identify scarce skills and demand across the planning horizon
    reserve explicit specialist capacity where forecast demand warrants it
    set training target = ceiling(0.20 × normal_low_case_count)
    identify feasible training opportunities before filling remaining capacity

    order cases by recovery-needed flag, urgency score, deadline, and queue age
    build a provisional schedule using candidate scores and joint capacity checks
    protect costly-to-transfer complex cases with capable incumbent owners
    preserve started work unless a documented exception requires change

    repair the schedule through local swaps and alternative sequencing:
        enforce at most four distinct case involvements per resource
        enforce performer and supervisor calendars and workload limits
        meet the training target where safe opportunities exist
        retain reserve capacity unless deadline risk justifies its release
        form compatible Quality batches only under the batching rule

    if the training target cannot safely be met:
        record specific blocking cases/capacity and the actual training share
        schedule a future opportunity; do not invent training or bypass skill rules

    if no safe assignment can meet an SLA:
        retain safety gates and one accountable owner; flag expected lateness
        seek qualified backup, reprioritize, or escalate to the coordinator
        record any permitted overtime or continuity exception and its rationale

    break materially equivalent choices by:
        incumbent ownership, lower projected utilization,
        fewer recent scarce-skill assignments, least recent allocation,
        then stable resource ID for reproducibility

    recheck live availability and atomically reserve capacity before dispatch
    record the choice, alternatives, evidence, explanation, and exceptions
```

For high-cost continuity cases, require a documented material benefit before changing a competent owner; small score fluctuations are insufficient. If modest changes in weights or duration estimates produce a different allocation, show the coordinator the alternatives rather than presenting one choice as inevitable.
