# 1. Current-process diagnosis

The process-mining evidence shows a classic “fast path trap”: the dominant path is fast and cheap in the front half of the process, but it creates downstream quality, compliance, and customer-experience problems that erode the apparent advantage.

## Main performance problem
- The **frequency directly-follows graph** shows the dominant path is:
  - `Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen`: 3,600 cases
  - `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval`: 2,550 cases
  - `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement`: 2,300 cases
  - `Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close`: 2,320 cases  
  So roughly half of all cases are pushed through a fast replacement path.
- The **performance directly-follows graph** makes that path look attractive:
  - `Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen`: median 0.5 hours
  - `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval`: median 0.4 hours
  - `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement`: median 1.8 hours
  - `Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close`: median 3.2 hours
- By contrast, reviewed paths are much slower:
  - `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote`: mean 22 hours, p90 60 hours
  - `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review`: median 26 hours, p90 88 hours
  - `Customer complaint/reopen -> Escalation board`: median 42 hours, p90 130 hours

The performance data therefore favors speed, but it is not enough to justify the current routing because it ignores rework, compliance, and segment-specific service expectations.

## Main quality and rework problem
- **Rework statistics** show that the fast path is not actually “clean”:
  - Customer complaint/reopen occurs in **14.1% of Fast approval cases**, versus **3.3%** for the `Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device` path.
  - Overall complaint/reopen rate is 9.2%, so Fast approval is materially above the process average.
  - Fast approval cases are **31% cheaper before rework**, but only **9% cheaper after** complaints, reshipments, and service credits.
- The **temporal profile deviations** identify `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` as a long-tail pattern linked to incorrect replacement part selection.
- Wrong replacement cost is not trivial:
  - EUR 95 expected cost for low-risk accessories
  - EUR 410 expected cost for high-value devices
- `Rework repair` is also higher for high-value devices:
  - 12.5% of repaired high-value devices
  - 4.0% of repaired low-risk devices

So the slow repair path is not merely “slower”; it is associated with materially better quality outcomes.

## Main compliance problem
- The **token-based replay summary** reports log fitness 0.91, but only **68% of traces fit** the normative model.
- **Alignment diagnostics** show frequent move-on-log deviations:
  - Fast approval before evidence completeness
  - Customer complaint/reopen after close
  - Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices
- These deviations map directly to normative rules:
  - **R1:** Claims above EUR 400 must have Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review before Fast approval.
  - **R2:** Replacement dispatch must not occur before evidence completeness is confirmed, except for Premium low-risk accessory claims.
  - **R3:** Any reopened claim must pass Manual eligibility review before final close or denial.
- The discovered model has **low precision** because many low-frequency paths combine auto approval, risk review, and repair in ways not allowed by policy. That means the process is not just noisy; it is structurally non-compliant in important places.

## Main customer-experience problem
Customer outcomes are highly segment-dependent:
- **Premium low-risk accessory**:
  - Fast path median 8 hours, manual path 46 hours
  - Complaint/reopen: 6% fast, 3% manual
  - High service-credit exposure if cycle time exceeds 24 hours
- **Standard low-risk accessory**:
  - Fast path median 13 hours, manual path 52 hours
  - Complaint/reopen: 8% fast, 4% manual
  - Customers tolerate 72-hour completion if status visibility is good
- **Premium high-value device**:
  - Fast path median 20 hours, reviewed/repair path 96 hours
  - Complaint/reopen: 22% fast, 5% reviewed/repair
  - High compliance risk if R1 or R2 is violated
- **Standard high-value device**:
  - Fast path median 28 hours, reviewed/repair path 120 hours
  - Complaint/reopen: 18% fast, 6% reviewed/repair
  - Cost of wrong replacement is high
- **Unclear defect / missing evidence**:
  - 22% of cases
  - Median cycle time 140 hours
  - Highest abandonment and repeated evidence requests

The customer-experience problem is therefore not simply “process is too slow.” For Premium low-risk accessories, the manual path may be too slow and trigger service credits. For high-value devices, the fast path is too risky and too complaint-prone. For unclear defects, the process is slow, repetitive, and abandonment-heavy.

## Main resource and bottleneck problem
- **Manual eligibility review**:
  - 7 reviewers, 36 productive hours per reviewer per week
  - EUR 34 per labor hour
  - Queue grows sharply when utilization exceeds 82%
  - The DFG shows substantial inflow: 1,400 from intake, 890 from Request missing evidence, 310 from Customer complaint/reopen
- **Fraud/risk review**:
  - Only 2 specialists
  - EUR 52 per labor hour
  - Bottleneck on Mondays and after marketing campaigns
- **Repair quote**:
  - 3 technicians
  - EUR 45 per labor hour
  - Delay mostly caused by waiting for part-price confirmation
- **Quality check**:
  - 2 inspectors
  - EUR 38 per labor hour
  - Underused except after repair campaigns
- **Escalation board**:
  - One daily 45-minute slot
  - Strong customer-satisfaction effect but poor scalability

## The core contradiction
The most frequent path is attractive because the **frequency DFG** and **performance DFG** show it is fast and cheap. But the **conformance findings**, **rework statistics**, and **customer segment outcomes** show that the same path produces more complaints, more compliance exceptions, and worse outcomes for high-value segments.

The slower reviewed path is not universally better either:
- It is better for high-value and complex claims.
- It can be too slow for Premium low-risk accessories.
- It can become a queue bottleneck when manual-review inflow rises.
- It does not solve the unclear-defect / missing-evidence problem, which has a 140-hour median cycle time and high abandonment.

The redesign task is therefore not to choose “fast” or “slow.” It is to separate the process into materially different operating models by segment, risk, evidence state, and downstream consequence.

# 2. Redesign A: Segment-aware fast lane with evidence-verified pre-dispatch gate

## Redesigned process flow

1. **Intake claim**
   - Capture segment, device value, defect type, evidence status, campaign flag, and customer digital-literacy indicators where available.

2. **Auto eligibility screen v2**
   - Replace the current outdated, non-segment-aware auto screen with a segment- and risk-aware decision layer.
   - Outputs:
     - Segment: Premium / Standard
     - Value band: low-risk accessory / mid-risk device / high-value device
     - Defect risk: low / medium / high
     - Evidence completeness: complete / incomplete / unclear
     - Campaign or fraud-risk flag

3. **Routing decision**
   - **Premium low-risk accessory, low defect risk:**
     - Fast approval is allowed.
     - Pre-dispatch quality gate is mandatory.
     - Dispatch replacement may occur under the R2 Premium low-risk exception, but evidence completion must be tracked after dispatch and closed within a short SLA.
     - If evidence later fails, trigger a recovery action: recall, replacement correction, service credit, or denial with explanation.
   - **Standard low-risk accessory, low defect risk:**
     - Evidence completeness must be confirmed before dispatch.
     - Fast approval is allowed only after evidence completeness.
     - Pre-dispatch quality gate is mandatory.
     - Then dispatch replacement and close.
   - **High-value device, high-risk defect, missing/unclear evidence, or reopened claim:**
     - No Fast approval.
     - Route to the reviewed/repair lane or evidence-first lane.
     - Reopened claims must pass Manual eligibility review before final close or denial, satisfying R3.

4. **Pre-dispatch quality gate**
   - Lightweight gate before replacement dispatch.
   - Automated checks:
     - Correct part number
     - Serial/IMEI match
     - Warranty status
     - Stock availability
     - Replacement cost band
     - Segment-specific exception rules
   - Sampled human check by quality inspectors for higher-risk low-value cases or when automated confidence is low.
   - Target gate time:
     - 6 hours for automated low-risk cases
     - Up to 18 hours where human inspection is required

5. **Dispatch replacement**
   - Only after gate passes.
   - For Premium low-risk cases using the R2 exception, dispatch can occur before full evidence completeness, but the case remains open for evidence completion and compliance close.

6. **Quality/compliance close**
   - Close only after:
     - Dispatch or repair completion
     - Evidence completeness confirmed where required
     - No open compliance exception
     - No unresolved gate failure

## Segments, paths, and activities targeted
- Targets the dominant fast path:
  - `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval`: 2,550 cases
  - `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement`: 2,300 cases
- Targets low-risk accessory segments:
  - Premium low-risk accessory: 18% of cases
  - Standard low-risk accessory: 25% of cases
- Targets specific failure modes:
  - Fast approval before evidence completeness
  - Dispatch replacement without correct part selection
  - `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` long tail
  - R2 violations for non-Premium low-risk claims
  - R1 violations where high-value claims are incorrectly fast-approved

## Mechanism of improvement
- **Segment-aware auto screening** prevents high-value and high-risk claims from entering the fast path.
- **Evidence completeness before dispatch** for Standard low-risk claims reduces R2 violations.
- **Pre-dispatch quality gate** catches wrong replacement selection before the customer receives the wrong item.
- **Preserved fast lane** keeps the speed advantage for low-risk accessories, especially Premium low-risk cases where service-credit exposure is high if cycle time exceeds 24 hours.
- **Use of underused quality inspectors** for the gate converts idle inspection capacity into a compliance and quality control point.

The improvement mechanism is not “slow everything down.” It is: keep the fast path fast where it is safe, but make the fast path evidence-aware and part-selection-aware.

## Deliberate sacrifices and unresolved issues
- Adds 6–18 hours to the fast path depending on gate design.
  - If the gate takes 18 hours, Premium low-risk cases may approach or exceed the 24-hour service-credit threshold.
- Updating auto-screen rules may increase manual-review inflow by 15–35%.
  - This can push manual eligibility review utilization above the 82% queue-growth threshold.
- Does not fully solve the unclear-defect / missing-evidence problem.
  - The 22% of cases with unclear defect or missing evidence still need a dedicated evidence-first redesign.
- Does not directly improve the high-value reviewed path.
  - High-value claims still need a separate lane to address R1, repair-quote delay, and fraud capacity.
- May increase front-end cost before rework.
  - The net cost effect is uncertain because the gate adds labor and time but should reduce wrong replacements, reshipments, and service credits.

# 3. Redesign B: Evidence-first triage with conditional fast approval and abandonment rescue

## Redesigned process flow

1. **Intake claim**
   - All claims enter a structured evidence front end before eligibility is finalized.

2. **Evidence portal / guided defect classification**
   - Customer or service agent completes a guided evidence flow:
     - Device type
     - Serial/IMEI
     - Purchase proof or warranty proof
     - Defect description
     - Photos or diagnostic output where relevant
     - Segment and value indicators
   - The portal produces an evidence completeness score.

3. **Auto eligibility screen v2 after evidence scoring**
   - The auto screen uses both eligibility and evidence completeness.
   - It classifies:
     - Low-risk accessory
     - Mid-risk device
     - High-value device
     - Unclear defect
     - Missing evidence
     - Fraud-risk flag

4. **Evidence-incomplete path**
   - If evidence is incomplete:
     - Send a specific evidence request with a clear checklist.
     - Provide status visibility and a completion deadline.
     - Offer fallback channels for customers with low digital literacy:
       - Phone
       - Chat
       - Service-center upload
       - Agent-assisted completion
   - After two failed evidence attempts:
     - Route to human triage, not automatically into generic Manual eligibility review.
     - Human triage decides:
       - Conditional fast approval for Premium low-risk accessory claims under R2 exception
       - Continue evidence collection
       - Route to reviewed/repair lane
       - Abandon claim with clear reason and customer communication

5. **Evidence-complete path**
   - **Low-risk accessory:**
     - Fast approval is possible.
     - Standard low-risk claims require evidence completeness before dispatch.
     - Premium low-risk claims may use the R2 exception but should still pass the pre-dispatch quality gate if Redesign A is deployed.
   - **High-value device or high-risk defect:**
     - Route to the reviewed/repair lane.
     - No Fast approval without Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review, satisfying R1.
   - **Unclear defect:**
     - Route to human technical triage or reviewed lane.
     - Avoid repeated generic evidence requests.

6. **Fulfillment or repair**
   - Replacement dispatch only after:
     - Evidence completeness confirmed, or
     - Valid Premium low-risk R2 exception
   - Repair path follows the reviewed/repair lane.

7. **Quality/compliance close**
   - Close only after:
     - Evidence completeness confirmed where required
     - Correct fulfillment or repair
     - No open compliance exception
     - Customer communication completed

## Segments, paths, and activities targeted
- Targets the `Request missing evidence` loop:
  - `Auto eligibility screen -> Request missing evidence`: 650 cases
  - `Manual eligibility review -> Request missing evidence`: 780 cases
  - `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review`: 890 cases
  - `Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned`: 320 cases
- Targets the 22% of cases with unclear defect / missing evidence:
  - Median cycle time 140 hours
  - Highest abandonment rate
  - Repeated evidence requests
- Targets the fact that **26% of cases entering Request missing evidence occur at least twice**.
- Targets R2 violations by making evidence completeness an explicit routing state.
- Targets manual-review inflow caused by missing evidence:
  - 890 cases from `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review`

## Mechanism of improvement
- **Front-loads evidence quality** instead of discovering missing evidence after eligibility or dispatch.
- **Reduces repeated evidence requests** by using a guided portal and specific checklist.
  - The prompt indicates a portal could reduce repeated evidence requests by 30–45%.
- **Reduces manual-review inflow** by resolving simple evidence gaps before they enter Manual eligibility review.
- **Improves customer experience** through status visibility, clear evidence requirements, and fallback channels.
- **Reduces abandonment** by adding human triage and low-digital-literacy fallback instead of repeated automated requests.
- **Improves compliance** by making evidence completeness a visible process state before dispatch.

This redesign is materially different from Redesign A because it changes the front end of the process. Redesign A mainly makes the existing fast lane safer; Redesign B changes how claims enter eligibility and how evidence uncertainty is handled.

## Deliberate sacrifices and unresolved issues
- May increase cycle time for evidence-incomplete claims.
  - Customers who cannot quickly provide evidence may experience longer waits.
- May increase abandonment for customers with low digital literacy if the fallback channel is weak.
- Adds portal and support workload.
  - The portal may reduce repeated requests, but it can shift effort to phone, chat, or human triage.
- Does not directly solve high-value fraud capacity or repair-quote delay.
- Does not by itself fix the high-value fast-path compliance problem unless combined with segment-aware routing.
- May make the process feel more bureaucratic if the evidence checklist is too strict or too generic.

# 4. Redesign C: Parallel reviewed/repair lane for high-value and complex claims

## Redesigned process flow

1. **Intake claim**
   - Capture segment, value, defect type, evidence status, campaign flag, and claim history.

2. **Auto eligibility screen v2**
   - Routes claims into one of three lanes:
     - Fast lane for low-risk accessories
     - Evidence-first lane for unclear or missing evidence
     - Reviewed/repair lane for high-value, high-risk, mid-risk complex, or reopened claims

3. **Reviewed/repair lane entry**
   - Entry criteria:
     - Device value above EUR 400
     - High-risk defect
     - Mid-risk device with ambiguous technical decision
     - Fraud-risk flag
     - Reopened claim
     - High complaint or escalation history
   - This lane is where R1 is enforced most strictly:
     - No Fast approval without Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review.

4. **Parallel workstreams**
   Instead of the current serial pattern:
   - `Manual eligibility review -> Fraud/risk review -> Repair quote`
   
   the redesigned lane runs three workstreams in parallel:

   ### Workstream 1: Manual eligibility review
   - Warranty terms
   - Segment entitlements
   - Claim history
   - Evidence completeness
   - Premium service expectations

   ### Workstream 2: Fraud/risk review
   - Risk score
   - Campaign flag
   - High-value device flag
   - Repeat claim pattern
   - Anomalous customer or device indicators

   ### Workstream 3: Fulfillment planning
   - Repair quote or replacement planning
   - Part-price confirmation with SLA
   - Part availability
   - Pre-dispatch quality gate for replacement
   - Quality check scheduling for repair
   - Customer communication plan

5. **Shared case file**
   - All three workstreams write to one case file.
   - No workstream should require the customer or another team to repeat information.

6. **Technical decision gate**
   - Decision is made only after:
     - Manual eligibility review complete, or
     - Fraud/risk review complete where required, and
     - Evidence completeness confirmed, and
     - Fulfillment plan is ready
   - Possible decisions:
     - Repair
     - Replace
     - Deny
     - Escalate
     - Conditional approval with customer action

7. **Premium high-value expedited queue**
   - Premium high-value claims receive:
     - Priority queue
     - Proactive status updates
     - Shorter internal SLAs
     - Direct escalation path if decision is delayed

8. **Repair path**
   - `Customer approval -> Repair device -> Quality check -> Quality/compliance close`
   - Quality check is scheduled before repair completion where possible.
   - High-value devices receive stricter quality verification because rework repair occurs in 12.5% of repaired high-value devices.

9. **Escalation board**
   - The escalation board is no longer the default overflow for all complaints.
   - It is reserved for:
     - High-value Premium exceptions
     - Compliance-critical decisions
     - Repeated rework cases
     - Cases with strong customer-satisfaction impact
   - Cases are pre-reviewed asynchronously before the daily 45-minute slot to improve scalability.

10. **Quality/compliance close**
   - Close only after:
     - Technical decision is compliant
     - Repair or replacement is complete
     - Quality check passed where required
     - R3 is satisfied for reopened claims
     - Customer communication is complete

## Segments, paths, and activities targeted
- Targets high-value segments:
  - Premium high-value device: 14% of cases
  - Standard high-value device: 21% of cases
- Targets high-value fast-path failure:
  - Premium high-value complaint/reopen: 22% fast path vs 5% reviewed/repair path
  - Standard high-value complaint/reopen: 18% fast path vs 6% reviewed/repair path
- Targets compliance rules:
  - R1: high-value claims must have Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review before Fast approval
  - R2: replacement dispatch must not occur before evidence completeness
  - R3: reopened claims must pass Manual eligibility review before final close or denial
- Targets slow serial reviewed path:
  - `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote`: mean 22 hours, p90 60 hours
  - `Manual eligibility review -> Fraud/risk review`: mean 18 hours, p90 44 hours
- Targets repair-quote delay:
  - Delay mostly caused by waiting for part-price confirmation
- Targets high-value rework:
  - Rework repair occurs in 12.5% of repaired high-value devices
- Targets escalation bottleneck:
  - `Customer complaint/reopen -> Escalation board`: 120 cases
  - Escalation board has only one daily 45-minute slot

## Mechanism of improvement
- **Parallelization** reduces serial waiting between Manual eligibility review, Fraud/risk review, and Repair quote.
- **Part-price confirmation SLA** attacks the main cause of repair-quote delay.
- **Shared case file** reduces repeated information requests and inconsistent decisions.
- **Quality check scheduling** uses underused inspectors to reduce high-value rework.
- **Escalation board pre-review** preserves the customer-satisfaction benefit while reducing dependence on a single daily slot.
- **Compliance gate before technical decision** reduces R1 and R2 violations for high-value claims.
- **Reopened-claim routing** ensures R3 compliance.

This redesign is materially different from A and B because it redesigns the reviewed/repair lane rather than the fast lane or evidence front end. It accepts that high-value and complex claims need more human judgment, but it makes that judgment faster, more parallel, and more compliant.

## Deliberate sacrifices and unresolved issues
- Higher labor cost:
  - Manual reviewers: EUR 34 per hour
  - Fraud/risk specialists: EUR 52 per hour
  - Repair technicians: EUR 45 per hour
  - Quality inspectors: EUR 38 per hour
- Higher capacity pressure:
  - Fraud/risk review has only 2 specialists and is already a bottleneck on Mondays and after campaigns.
  - Parallelizing fraud review may worsen the bottleneck if not carefully capacity-managed.
- Still slower than the fast lane.
  - The reviewed/repair path is not expected to become as fast as the low-risk fast path.
- Does not improve low-risk accessory speed.
- May create coordination overhead:
  - Parallel workstreams can produce inconsistent findings if the shared case file and decision rules are weak.
- Does not fully solve unclear-defect abandonment unless combined with Redesign B.
- The escalation board may remain a bottleneck if complaint volume is not reduced upstream.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

## Redesign A failure modes

### Failure mode 1: Auto-screen update overloads manual eligibility review
- Updating auto-screen rules may increase manual-review inflow by 15–35%.
- Manual eligibility review has 7 reviewers and queue growth becomes sharp above 82% utilization.
- If the rule update is too aggressive, the fast lane may shift cases into manual review faster than the team can absorb.
- Result:
  - Cycle time worsens for low-risk claims.
  - Premium low-risk cases may exceed the 24-hour service-credit threshold.
  - The apparent speed advantage of the fast lane disappears.

### Failure mode 2: Pre-dispatch gate becomes too slow
- The gate adds 6–18 hours depending on resource design.
- If the gate is designed at the 18-hour end, Premium low-risk accessory cases may approach or exceed the 24-hour service-credit threshold.
- The gate may reduce wrong replacements, but the service-credit cost may offset the benefit.
- Result:
  - Customer experience worsens for the segment that most needs speed.
  - The redesign may be perceived as adding bureaucracy without improving outcomes.

### Failure mode 3: Gate catches part errors but not evidence or root-cause errors
- The gate can catch wrong replacement selection, but it may not fix:
  - Incomplete evidence
  - Poor defect classification
  - Outdated part catalog
  - Incorrect warranty interpretation
- Result:
  - Complaints may shift from wrong replacement to other post-dispatch issues.
  - Compliance exceptions may remain if evidence completeness is still not enforced.

### Likely bottleneck migration
- From `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` to:
  - Manual eligibility review queue, or
  - Pre-dispatch quality gate / quality inspectors, or
  - Post-dispatch recovery work if evidence fails after Premium exception dispatch.

---

## Redesign B failure modes

### Failure mode 1: Portal increases abandonment for low digital literacy
- The evidence portal may reduce repeated evidence requests by 30–45%, but it may increase abandonment for customers with low digital literacy.
- The unclear-defect / missing-evidence segment already has the highest abandonment rate and a 140-hour median cycle time.
- If the fallback channel is weak, the portal may make the process more frustrating rather than clearer.
- Result:
  - Abandonment rises.
  - Customer trust decreases.
  - The apparent reduction in repeated evidence requests is offset by lost cases.

### Failure mode 2: Evidence completeness threshold is too strict
- If the portal requires too much evidence before any fast approval:
  - Low-risk accessory claims may wait unnecessarily.
  - Standard low-risk customers may exceed their 72-hour tolerance.
  - Premium low-risk customers may exceed the 24-hour service-credit threshold.
- Result:
  - The redesign improves evidence quality but harms speed and customer experience.
  - The fast path loses its main advantage.

### Failure mode 3: Repeated requests shift to phone, chat, or human triage
- The portal may reduce repeated automated evidence requests, but customers may shift to:
  - Phone
  - Chat
  - Service-center assistance
  - Human triage
- If support capacity is not planned, the bottleneck moves from the evidence loop to customer support.
- Result:
  - Cost may increase.
  - Cycle time may not improve.
  - The process may feel less automated but not more reliable.

### Likely bottleneck migration
- From `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` to:
  - Portal support / human triage, or
  - Manual eligibility review after portal completion, or
  - Fraud/risk review if evidence reveals higher risk.

---

## Redesign C failure modes

### Failure mode 1: Parallel fraud review overloads the two fraud specialists
- Fraud/risk review currently has only 2 specialists and is already a bottleneck on Mondays and after marketing campaigns.
- Running fraud review in parallel with manual eligibility and fulfillment planning may increase concurrency.
- If campaign periods or Monday peaks are not managed, fraud wait times may increase.
- Result:
  - High-value cycle time does not improve.
  - The reviewed lane becomes slower than the current serial path.
  - Premium high-value customers experience worse service.

### Failure mode 2: Part-price confirmation remains the real delay
- Repair-quote delay is mostly caused by waiting for part-price confirmation.
- If the redesign parallelizes manual and fraud review but does not fix part-price confirmation:
  - The technical decision still waits.
  - Customer approval delays remain.
  - The p90 of `Repair quote -> Customer approval` may not improve.
- Result:
  - The redesign adds coordination cost without materially reducing cycle time.

### Failure mode 3: Parallel workstreams create inconsistent decisions
- Manual eligibility, fraud review, and fulfillment planning may reach different conclusions if the shared case file and decision rules are weak.
- Examples:
  - Manual reviewer approves eligibility, but fraud reviewer flags risk.
  - Fulfillment planner prepares replacement, but evidence is incomplete.
  - Repair quote is ready, but part-price confirmation is missing.
- Result:
  - Rework increases at the technical decision gate.
  - Complaints may shift from fulfillment to decision inconsistency.
  - Compliance exceptions may persist if the decision gate is not enforced.

### Likely bottleneck migration
- From serial `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote` delay to:
  - Fraud/risk review wait, or
  - Part-price confirmation, or
  - Quality check scheduling, or
  - Escalation board if complaint volume is not reduced upstream.

---

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

The experiment sequence should not assume that the fastest path is best or that the reviewed path is always better. It should test segment-specific hypotheses and monitor whether improvements are real end-to-end gains or merely shifted downstream.

## Stage 0: Baseline instrumentation and segment tagging
Before piloting, ensure the process log can distinguish:
- Premium vs Standard
- Low-risk accessory vs mid-risk device vs high-value device
- Evidence complete vs incomplete vs unclear
- Fast path vs reviewed path
- Complaint/reopen within 30 and 60 days
- Post-close complaints
- Service credits
- Wrong replacement cost
- Compliance exceptions for R1, R2, and R3
- Resource utilization:
  - Manual eligibility review
  - Fraud/risk review
  - Repair quote
  - Quality check
  - Escalation board

This stage is necessary because the current contradiction cannot be resolved by average cycle time alone.

---

## Stage 1: First pilot — Redesign A for Premium low-risk accessories

### Why this first pilot
- Premium low-risk accessories are 18% of cases.
- They have high service-credit exposure if cycle time exceeds 24 hours.
- The current fast path is fast but still has 6% complaint/reopen.
- The auto-screen update can be deployed in two weeks.
- The pre-dispatch gate can be designed at the lower end, around 6 hours, for automated low-risk cases.
- This pilot tests whether the fast lane can be made safer without destroying its speed advantage.

### Pilot design
- Randomize or time-split Premium low-risk accessory claims:
  - Treatment: segment-aware auto screen + pre-dispatch quality gate
  - Control: current process
- Keep the R2 Premium low-risk exception, but require:
  - Pre-dispatch gate
  - Post-dispatch evidence completion tracking
  - Recovery action if evidence fails

### Metrics to monitor
- Median and p90 cycle time
- Complaint/reopen rate at 30 and 60 days
- Wrong replacement cost
- Service credits
- Manual-review inflow
- Manual-review utilization
- Pre-dispatch gate time, especially p90
- R2 violations
- Post-close complaints
- Total cost after rework, complaints, and service credits

### Stop/go thresholds
Go if:
- Complaint/reopen for Premium low-risk falls from 6% toward 4% or lower, or shows a clear relative reduction of at least 25–40%.
- Service credits are flat or decrease.
- Manual-review inflow increases by no more than 20–25%.
- Manual-review utilization stays below the 82% queue-growth threshold.
- Pre-dispatch gate p90 stays around 12 hours or lower.
- Post-close complaints do not increase.

Stop or rollback if:
- Manual-review utilization exceeds 85% or queue growth becomes severe.
- Service credits increase.
- Pre-dispatch gate p90 exceeds 18 hours.
- R2 violations do not decrease.
- Complaints simply move from pre-dispatch to post-dispatch.

---

## Stage 2: Evidence-first pilot — Redesign B for unclear defect / missing evidence

### Why this second
- The unclear-defect / missing-evidence segment is 22% of cases.
- It has a 140-hour median cycle time.
- It has the highest abandonment and repeated evidence requests.
- 26% of cases entering `Request missing evidence` occur at least twice.
- The current DFG shows a large loop:
  - `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review`: 890 cases
  - `Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned`: 320 cases
- This pilot tests whether evidence quality can be fixed at the front end without increasing abandonment.

### Pilot design
- Apply the evidence portal and guided defect classification to:
  - Unclear defect cases
  - Missing-evidence cases
  - Optionally Standard low-risk cases with incomplete evidence
- Include fallback channels:
  - Phone
  - Chat
  - Agent-assisted completion
- After two failed evidence attempts, route to human triage rather than automatic repeated requests.

### Metrics to monitor
- Repeated evidence request rate
- Abandonment rate
- Evidence completion time
- Manual-review inflow from missing evidence
- Complaint/reopen rate
- Customer satisfaction or effort score
- Portal completion rate
- Fallback channel usage
- Post-portal manual-review volume
- Post-portal fraud-review volume
- 30/60-day reopen rate

### Stop/go thresholds
Go if:
- Repeated evidence requests fall by roughly 30–45%.
- Abandonment does not increase by more than 5% relative to control.
- Manual-review inflow from missing evidence decreases.
- p90 cycle time for unclear cases improves.
- Complaint/reopen does not increase.
- Fallback channel usage is manageable.

Stop or redesign if:
- Abandonment increases by more than 10–15% relative to control.
- Low-digital-literacy customers show materially worse outcomes.
- Repeated evidence requests decrease but phone/chat/human-triage load increases sharply.
- Evidence completion time worsens without reducing downstream rework.
- Complaints shift from evidence requests to post-dispatch or post-close issues.

---

## Stage 3: High-value reviewed-lane pilot — Redesign C for high-value devices

### Why this third
- High-value devices are 35% of cases:
  - Premium high-value: 14%
  - Standard high-value: 21%
- The fast path is clearly worse for these segments:
  - Premium high-value complaint/reopen: 22% fast vs 5% reviewed
  - Standard high-value complaint/reopen: 18% fast vs 6% reviewed
- The current reviewed path is slow and serial:
  - `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote`: mean 22 hours, p90 60 hours
- This pilot tests whether the reviewed path can be made faster and more compliant without overloading fraud capacity.

### Pilot design
- Route high-value, high-risk, and reopened claims into the parallel reviewed/repair lane.
- Run:
  - Manual eligibility review
  - Fraud/risk review
  - Fulfillment planning / repair quote
  in parallel with a shared case file.
- Introduce:
  - Part-price confirmation SLA
  - Pre-dispatch quality gate for replacement
  - Quality check scheduling for repair
  - Premium high-value expedited queue
  - Escalation board pre-review

### Metrics to monitor
- R1 violations
- R2 violations
- R3 violations
- High-value complaint/reopen rate at 30 and 60 days
- Median and p90 cycle time for high-value reviewed claims
- Fraud/risk review wait time, especially p90
- Fraud/risk utilization
- Repair-quote delay
- Part-price confirmation time
- Rework repair rate for high-value devices
- Escalation board volume
- Escalation board cycle time
- Total cost after rework, complaints, and service credits

### Stop/go thresholds
Go if:
- High-value complaint/reopen falls materially, for example:
  - Premium high-value from 22% toward 12–15%
  - Standard high-value from 18% toward 10–12%
- R1 and R2 violations decrease.
- R3 violations decrease for reopened claims.
- p90 cycle time for high-value reviewed claims improves by at least 20%.
- Fraud utilization stays below a dangerous level, for example below 80–85%.
- Escalation board volume decreases or remains stable while customer satisfaction improves.

Stop or redesign if:
- Fraud utilization exceeds 85% or fraud wait p90 worsens significantly.
- Cycle time does not improve after 6–8 weeks.
- Part-price confirmation remains the dominant delay.
- Parallel workstreams create inconsistent decisions and increase rework.
- Complaints shift from fulfillment to technical decision or escalation.

---

## Sequencing logic

1. **Start with Redesign A for Premium low-risk accessories.**
   - It is the fastest to test.
   - It protects a high-service-credit segment.
   - It tests whether the fast lane can be made compliant without losing speed.
   - It uses the two-week auto-screen update and the lightweight pre-dispatch gate.

2. **Then test Redesign B for unclear defect / missing evidence.**
   - Once the fast lane is safer, reduce upstream noise from missing evidence.
   - This reduces manual-review inflow and abandonment.
   - It also creates cleaner evidence for the high-value lane.

3. **Finally test Redesign C for high-value devices.**
   - It is the most resource-intensive.
   - It depends on stable segment routing and better evidence quality.
   - It should not be launched before fraud and repair-quote capacity constraints are understood.

The sequence is not strictly linear. If evidence portal investment is already available, Redesign B could run in a non-overlapping segment alongside Redesign A. However, Redesign C should not be scaled until the upstream routing and evidence state are more stable.

---

## How to learn whether improvements are real rather than shifted downstream

For each pilot, monitor the full case lifecycle, not just the stage being changed.

### Check for downstream complaint shifting
- If Redesign A reduces pre-dispatch complaints but increases:
  - Post-close complaints
  - Service credits
  - Reshipments
  - Recovery actions  
  then the improvement may be shifted, not eliminated.

### Check for manual-queue shifting
- If Redesign A or B reduces fast-path errors but increases:
  - Manual-review inflow
  - Manual-review wait time
  - Manual-review utilization above 82%  
  then the speed gain may be offset by queue growth.

### Check for evidence-loop shifting
- If Redesign B reduces repeated evidence requests but increases:
  - Abandonment
  - Phone/chat support load
  - Human triage time
  - Post-portal manual review  
  then the portal may be displacing work rather than removing it.

### Check for fraud and repair-quote shifting
- If Redesign C reduces serial manual delay but increases:
  - Fraud wait time
  - Part-price confirmation delay
  - Quality check delay
  - Escalation board load  
  then the reviewed lane has not improved end-to-end.

### Use cohort-based end-to-end metrics
For each segment, track:
- 30-day reopen
- 60-day reopen
- Post-close complaint
- Total cost after rework
- Total cost after service credits
- Compliance exception count
- Customer satisfaction or effort
- Resource utilization in downstream stages

The goal is to determine whether the redesign improves the total outcome:
- faster where appropriate
- cheaper after rework
- more compliant
- better for the customer  
without simply moving the problem to another queue, another cost line, or another customer touchpoint.
