## 1. System boundary and assumptions

- **Environmental scope (inside boundary)**: I treat the order process from `Receive order` through `Close` (and `Cancel due to delay`) as the system. Environmental impact is measured using the enriched event attributes that are already in the log: operational electricity `energy_kwh` (production + heat treatment + inspection + rework loops), transport emissions `transport_kgco2e` (by `route_mode`), material waste `material_scrap_kg` (including rework), and packaging material `package_material_kg` (including packaging rework). I also include **obsolescence/waste** as a process outcome using the provided proxy: the share of batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days (noted to be 6% when large batches are used and forecasts are wrong).
- **Service/quality/resilience scope (inside boundary)**: service is captured by cycle time (median/p90), late delivery vs `promised_lead_time_hours`, cancellations due to delay, and partial shipments/backorders. Quality is captured via `rework_flag`, production/packaging rework loops, and Q1 conformance. Resilience is captured via the ability to keep at least one alternative route for Service-contract customers (R1) and the behavior of emergency/expedite paths during disruptions.
- **Outside boundary (tracked qualitatively)**: upstream supplier impacts and embodied impacts of machines/buildings; downstream customer-side impacts, use-phase, and end-of-life. These can change (e.g., higher scrap implies more upstream material burden), but they are not quantified by the prompt’s attributes, so I manage them through guardrails (do not increase scrap/returns/obsolescence).
- **Key uncertainties and assumptions**:
  - **Flattened cases**: the log is flattened by order (not line item). Any rules that depend on line-item properties (e.g., Q1’s “>8 kg”) require either existing hidden attributes or enhanced logging at line-item level; otherwise, apply Q1 using the best available proxy (e.g., part class/geometry if present).
  - **Transport estimates**: `transport_kgco2e` is model-based; relative comparisons by mode are strong (air is ~4–6× ground), but values should be recalibrated periodically.
  - **Returns visibility**: damage-related returns are referenced; if returns are not explicitly logged, add events so improvements that “look green” do not silently shift burden downstream.
  - **Definition of “unacceptable deterioration”**: do not materially worsen late delivery for Service-contract and Critical-downtime segments; do not increase rework/returns/cancellations; maintain route optionality for Service-contract lanes per R1.

## 2. Sustainability-related process indicators

Concise indicator set grounded in the log attributes and observed variants:

- **Energy (operations)**:
  - **kWh per order**: `energy_kwh/order` by `case_type × customer_type` and by major production path (CNC_A vs CNC_B; with/without `heat_treat`; with/without rework loops).
  - **Energy per successful shipment**: `energy_kwh` per closed (non-cancelled) order; and incremental `energy_kwh` attributable to `Rework production` loops (cases with `Inspection -> Rework production -> CNC production`).
  - **Energy-vs-batch frontier**: for CNC_A, track kWh/item vs `batch_size` buckets (e.g., 1–3 vs 12–20) alongside `Batch wait` time (given: 2.8 → 1.3 kWh/item, while Batch wait 4h → 38h).

- **Transport (distribution)**:
  - **kgCO2e per order**: `transport_kgco2e/order` by `route_mode` (ground/air/dedicated_van/consolidated_truck) and by segment.
  - **Mode share with slack**: % of `parcel_air` shipments where SP1 would prefer ground/truck (i.e., predicted on-time probability for low-carbon modes >85% given `promised_lead_time_hours`).
  - **Dedicated-van justification completeness**: SP2 conformance proxy (% of dedicated_van cases with an explicit “Critical-downtime justification” event).

- **Materials (waste and packaging)**:
  - **Scrap**: `material_scrap_kg/order` by machine group and by variant (especially CNC_B + rework loop variants with high scrap).
  - **Packaging**: `package_material_kg/order`, reinforced-vs-standard share, and packaging material attributable to `Rework packaging` loops.
  - **Obsolescence proxy**: % of batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days (by `batch_size`, machine group, and segment).

- **Rework and quality**:
  - **Rework rate**: % cases with `rework_flag`, and **loop count** of `Rework production` and `Rework packaging` events per case.
  - **Q1 conformance**: for Made-to-order heavy parts, share shipped without reinforced packaging unless certified low-risk; track correlation with returns/rework.
  - **Inspection rush proxy**: inspection queue time / workload vs later rework loops (prompt: rushed inspections contribute to rework and returns).

- **Batching and waiting**:
  - **Batch wait time**: distribution of `Batch wait` and heat-treatment queue times; “cap breach rate” by segment.
  - **Late risk vs batching**: late delivery as a function of `Batch wait` and heat-treatment waiting (prompt: heat-treatment batching wait increases late risk 1.7×).

- **Service**:
  - **Cycle time**: median and p90 cycle time by segment and by key variants (e.g., partial shipment; MTO batch wait vs immediate CNC).
  - **Late delivery**: late delivery rate by segment and by `route_mode` and `expedite_flag`.
  - **Partial shipment burden**: share of cases with `Partial shipment`, number of shipments per case (derived from ship events), and backorder wait time.

- **Resilience**:
  - **Route diversity (R1)**: per Service-contract lane/customer, count of distinct `route_mode`s used over a rolling window and a concentration index (avoid single-mode lock-in).
  - **Disruption response**: share of cases using `Emergency production` and/or `expedite_flag`, and the service impact during disruption windows.

## 3. Intervention options

### Option 1: Probabilistic green shipping recommender (SP1-first) (S1)
- **Target path/segment**: shipment choice from `Package standard/reinforced` to `Ship ground/air/dedicated_van/consolidated_truck`, especially Standard-stock Service-contract (SP1 violated in 22%).
- **Mechanism (environment)**: choose the lowest-CO2 mode that still meets the promise with \(P(\text{on-time}) \ge 0.85\), using historical transit reliability by route and mode; incorporate consolidation wait where applicable.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect**: maintains service via probability guardrail; improves resilience if coupled with R1 constraints (do not collapse all Service-contract lanes to one mode). Risk if prediction is miscalibrated.
- **Evidence to monitor**: SP1 conformance rate, mode share with slack, late delivery by segment, transport `kgCO2e/order`, and route diversity (R1).

### Option 2: Replace unnecessary air for Standard-stock Service-contract via priority pick + ground (S4)
- **Target path/segment**: Standard-stock Service-contract orders often shipped by air even when ground would still meet promise (prompt).
- **Mechanism (environment)**: reduce `parcel_air` usage (air emits ~4–6× more than ground) by cutting internal time (priority pick/pack) so ground fits within the same promised window.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect**: service preserved if internal lead time is reduced; keep air as an exception for tight promises/disruptions (do not “ban air”).
- **Evidence to monitor**: time from `Availability check` to `Package` for this segment, air share with sufficient slack, late delivery and p90 cycle time, transport `kgCO2e/order`.

### Option 3: Twice-daily consolidated-truck departures for non-urgent stock orders (S5)
- **Target path/segment**: Standard-stock Regular (largest share) and any stock orders with slack; shift from `Ship ground` to `Consolidated truck`.
- **Mechanism (environment)**: consolidation reduces transport `kgCO2e` by ~35–55% vs individual ground parcels, at the cost of a 12–24h consolidation wait.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect**: slight cycle-time increase for eligible orders; protect service by eligibility rules (only when slack allows) and keep ground as fallback to preserve resilience.
- **Evidence to monitor**: consolidation wait time, late deliveries among consolidated shipments, mode concentration by lane (R1), transport `kgCO2e/order`.

### Option 4: Reduce partial shipments by redesigning availability-check and allocation rules (S11)
- **Target path/segment**: `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment -> Backorder wait` variant (median 79h, late 24%, higher transport due to multiple shipments).
- **Mechanism (environment)**: reduce duplicate shipments and handling (packaging + transport + customer contact) by reserving inventory for complete fulfillment when the missing items are expected soon, and by proactively sourcing missing items before releasing a partial.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect**: improves service and reduces cancellations if rules avoid long holds; resilience improves by lowering operational noise during disruptions.
- **Evidence to monitor**: partial shipment rate, number of ship events per order, backorder wait distribution, late delivery/cancel rates, transport and packaging material per order.

### Option 5: Dynamic batching with segment-specific waiting-time caps for CNC and heat treatment (S2)
- **Target path/segment**: Made-to-order production (`Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC production`), and heat-treatment batching; avoid the “energy-efficient but late” extreme.
- **Mechanism (environment)**: capture part of the CNC_A energy-per-item benefit (2.8 → 1.3 kWh/item from small to 12–20 batches) while limiting waiting; similarly for heat treatment where efficiency improves at batch >10 but waiting increases late risk 1.7×.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect**: controls late risk via caps; resilience depends on scheduler robustness (avoid brittle rules that fail under disruption).
- **Evidence to monitor**: batch_size vs Batch wait vs late delivery, energy_kwh vs batch_size, heat-treatment wait vs late delivery, obsolescence proxy (% not shipped within 14 days).

### Option 6: Due-date-aware machine assignment: shift MTO from CNC_B to CNC_A when slack allows (S3)
- **Target path/segment**: Made-to-order cases routed to CNC_B (higher rework: 18% vs 9% on CNC_A), especially the high-impact CNC_B + rework variants (energy 32 kWh, scrap 1.8 kg).
- **Mechanism (environment)**: reduce rework-driven energy and scrap by preferring CNC_A for cases with due-date slack; reserve CNC_B for truly urgent capacity needs.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect**: quality improves (lower rework); service improves if rework drops, but watch CNC_A congestion (avoid creating a new bottleneck).
- **Evidence to monitor**: routing by slack and queue time, rework_flag by machine_group, energy/scrap per order by machine assignment, late delivery and cycle time.

### Option 7: Inspection quality gate + anti-rush controls for high-risk combinations (S7)
- **Target path/segment**: `Inspection -> Rework production -> CNC production` loops and downstream returns; prompt notes rushed inspections drive rework/returns.
- **Mechanism (environment)**: prevent defect escape and rework loops by adding enhanced inspection for high-risk machine/part combinations and by preventing “expedite” from shortening inspections.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect**: may add small inspection time per case but reduces total cycle time and lateness by avoiding rework; resilience improves because fewer emergency remakes are needed.
- **Evidence to monitor**: inspection queue vs rework loops, rework loop counts per case, energy/scrap attributable to rework, late delivery for high-risk cohorts.

### Option 8: Packaging-route compatibility checks + stricter Q1 enforcement with risk-based downgrades (S6 + Q1)
- **Target path/segment**: packaging rework loops (`Rework packaging`), and Made-to-order heavy parts where reinforced packaging reduces damage-related returns by 40% but doubles packaging material.
- **Mechanism (environment)**: eliminate incompatible pack/route selections that cause packaging rework; apply reinforced packaging when damage risk is high (Q1), but allow standard packaging when inspection certifies low risk to avoid unnecessary packaging material.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect**: reduces rework and returns (service/quality win); risk if downgrades are too aggressive (damage returns rebound).
- **Evidence to monitor**: rework packaging rate by route_mode/pack type, package_material_kg, Q1 conformance, damage/return events (add explicit logging if missing).

### Option 9: Split Critical-downtime into “true downtime” vs “precautionary urgent” with mode-specific SLAs (S9)
- **Target path/segment**: Critical-downtime Service-contract where the dominant emergency+dedicated-van path has very high transport emissions (52 kgCO2e) but excellent service (late 3%).
- **Mechanism (environment)**: reduce dedicated-van use for precautionary urgencies by using air/ground/stock exceptions when slack allows, while preserving fastest modes for true downtime.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect**: preserves service for true downtime; adds governance clarity; resilience improves when routing is explicit and auditable. Main risk is misclassification.
- **Evidence to monitor**: new “downtime justification” event, dedicated_van share by category, late delivery for true downtime, transport `kgCO2e/order`, escalation/expedite rates.

### Option 10: Replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes + backhaul/reuse (S8)
- **Target path/segment**: dedicated_van shipments with empty returns; clusters of Critical orders on similar routes.
- **Mechanism (environment)**: improve load factor via pooled emergency lanes (multi-stop/shared capacity) and use backhaul to return reusable crates for Service-contract loops where feasible.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect**: keeps speed while reducing emissions; resilience improves by diversifying carriers, but watch capacity constraints during disruptions.
- **Evidence to monitor**: route-level utilization proxies, transport kgCO2e per critical delivery, on-time for true downtime, route diversity (R1), packaging material per order (reusable loop effectiveness).

### Option 11: Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular Made-to-order customers (S10)
- **Target path/segment**: Made-to-order Regular, where the “immediate CNC + air” variant reduces late delivery (9%) but has high energy/transport; enable lower-impact variants by creating slack.
- **Mechanism (environment)**: offer an explicitly “green promise” option at order intake (longer promised lead time) to enable batching on CNC_A and low-carbon shipping without degrading actual on-time performance.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect**: avoids hidden service deterioration by aligning expectations; risk of rebound if customers later request expediting.
- **Evidence to monitor**: promised_lead_time_hours shifts, accept/decline and subsequent expedite_flag rates, variant mix changes, late delivery and cancellations, energy/transport per order.

### Option 12: Sustainability exception log + SP1/SP2/Q1 override governance (S12)
- **Target path/segment**: policy violations and missing justifications (SP1 violations; SP2 missing justification event in 31% of dedicated-van cases; Q1 violations tied to returns/rework).
- **Mechanism (environment)**: require explicit override events with standardized reasons and approvals to prevent silent drift and to learn from genuine exceptions.
- **Service/quality/resilience effect**: maintains flexibility (exceptions allowed) while making trade-offs transparent; resilience improves by making fallback decisions auditable rather than ad hoc.
- **Evidence to monitor**: override completeness and reason distribution, downstream service and quality outcomes for overrides, trend in SP1/SP2/Q1 conformance.

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

At least six “fix one thing, worsen another” risks, tied to the observed trade-offs:

- **Batching rebound (energy ↓, service ↓, transport ↑)**: pushing batch sizes toward the energy-optimal range (e.g., CNC_A batch 12–20) increases `Batch wait` (4h → 38h), which can increase lateness and trigger expediting (air/dedicated van), offsetting energy gains with higher transport emissions.
- **Heat-treatment batching displacement (energy ↓, late risk ↑)**: waiting to fill heat-treatment batches improves per-item efficiency, but the prompt shows a 1.7× higher late-delivery risk for cases waiting for a heat-treatment batch; downstream expediting may follow.
- **Consolidation rebound (transport ↓, customer/service reaction ↑)**: adding a 12–24h consolidation wait can increase late deliveries for borderline cases; customers may respond by demanding shorter promised lead times or requesting more expediting, shifting emissions elsewhere.
- **Air reduction displacement (transport ↓, operations ↑)**: replacing air with ground without fixing internal pick/pack time can increase late deliveries, which may drive more `Emergency production` and `expedite_flag` usage—raising energy and scrap upstream.
- **Packaging material trade-off (packaging ↓, returns/rework ↑)**: reducing reinforced packaging to cut `package_material_kg` can increase damage-related returns (reinforced cuts returns by 40% for heavy MTO), creating more rework/transport and higher total impact.
- **Quality gate rebound (rework ↓, cycle time ↑)**: stricter inspection reduces rework but can add time and create inspection-cell queues; if the organization then “rushes” inspections to recover speed, defects may escape again (prompt explicitly flags this dynamic).
- **Partial shipment “hold-to-complete” rebound (transport ↓, cycle time ↑)**: aggressively preventing partial shipments by holding orders can increase waiting time and cancellations; the policy must depend on expected backorder time and customer segment, not be absolute.
- **Obsolescence displacement (energy ↓ per item, waste ↑)**: large batches can produce items that are not shipped within 14 days (6% under forecast error), creating hidden waste that can outweigh per-item efficiency gains.
- **Customer behavior rebound (green promise ↓ impact, later expedite ↑ impact)**: offering carbon-aware promise negotiation may shift customers to longer promised lead times initially, but some may later request expediting; if those “green” orders become air shipments, net impact worsens.
- **Resilience concentration risk (single-mode optimization)**: optimizing to one low-carbon mode (e.g., consolidated trucks) can violate R1 in practice by eroding alternative route readiness; during disruptions, fallback may become air/dedicated van with higher emissions and worse service.

## 6. Balanced recommendation

Recommend a **coherent redesign package** that targets the highest-impact paths (air, dedicated vans, rework, partial shipments) while explicitly preserving service for Service-contract and true Critical-downtime cases and maintaining route diversity (R1):

- **A. Segment-based “service-safe decarbonization” for shipping**
  - Deploy **Option 1 (SP1-first shipping recommender)** with a conservative start: focus first on **Standard-stock Service-contract** where SP1 violations are high (22%) and the main opportunity is replacing unjustified air with ground while maintaining \(P(\text{on-time}) \ge 0.85\).
  - Implement **Option 2 (priority pick + ground)** operationally (warehouse staffing and pick sequencing) so the mode change does not translate into late deliveries.
  - Expand to **Option 3 (consolidated trucks)** for **non-urgent Standard-stock Regular** only when promise slack covers the 12–24h consolidation wait; keep ground/air as explicit fallbacks and monitor R1 route diversity.

- **B. Cut hidden waste drivers in Made-to-order (rework and avoidable expediting)**
  - Implement **Option 6 (CNC_B→CNC_A when slack allows)** to reduce rework (18%→9%) and the high-impact CNC_B rework variants (32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap).
  - Add **Option 7 (inspection quality gate)** for high-risk combinations and remove incentives to rush inspection under expedite; this directly targets the rework-production loop and downstream returns.
  - Layer **Option 5 (dynamic batching with waiting caps)** for CNC and heat treatment: capture energy efficiency where feasible, but cap waiting by segment (tighter for MTO Service-contract, looser for MTO Regular) to avoid turning energy savings into late deliveries and expediting.
  - For **Regular MTO only**, use **Option 11 (carbon-aware promise negotiation)** so lower-carbon plans are enabled by slack rather than by gambling on late delivery.

- **C. Reduce multi-shipment and packaging churn without increasing damage**
  - Implement **Option 4 (reduce partial shipments)** with a segmented rule: prevent partial shipments when the expected backorder is short (ship complete), but allow partials for true urgency with coordinated follow-up shipment planning to avoid repeated emissions.
  - Deploy **Option 8 (packaging compatibility + Q1 enforcement)** to reduce packaging rework and damage returns; use reinforced packaging where it demonstrably prevents returns (heavy MTO) and allow certified low-risk downgrades to avoid unnecessary packaging material.

- **D. Critical-downtime: preserve true urgency while shrinking the dedicated-van footprint**
  - Implement **Option 9 (critical triage)** to distinguish true downtime from precautionary urgent. Keep fast modes for true downtime, but use lower-carbon routing when the situation allows.
  - Where dedicated vans remain necessary, introduce **Option 10 (shared emergency lanes + backhaul/reuse)** to reduce empty returns and improve resilience through diversified capacity.

- **E. Governance so “green” decisions do not drift or become brittle**
  - Introduce **Option 12 (exception logging)** from day one so SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides are explicit, auditable, and learnable rather than informal.

This package is balanced because it (i) targets the largest emissions levers (air and dedicated vans) mainly where there is **observable slack or misrouting**, (ii) tackles **rework loops** that dominate energy/scrap in high-impact MTO variants, (iii) reduces **partial shipments** that are simultaneously bad for service and emissions, and (iv) preserves **critical service** and **route diversity** rather than pursuing lowest-emission paths that clearly violate service/resilience requirements.

## 7. Validation and governance plan

- **1) Pre/post evaluation design (avoid misleading averages)**
  - Evaluate at **segment × variant** level (e.g., Standard-stock Service-contract; MTO Service-contract; Critical true downtime vs precautionary) to avoid case-mix confounding.
  - Use a **difference-in-differences** or matched-cohort approach by lane/customer and promised lead time: compare similar orders before/after each rollout phase, keeping disruption periods as a separate stratum.
  - Primary outcomes: `transport_kgco2e`, `energy_kwh`, `material_scrap_kg`, `package_material_kg`, rework loop counts, obsolescence proxy, and service (late delivery, p90 cycle time) plus cancellations.

- **2) Conformance monitoring (SP1/SP2/Q1) with continuous alerts**
  - **SP1**: flag shipments where `route_mode` is air/van while the recommender (or rule baseline) predicts \(P(\text{on-time with ground/truck}) \ge 0.85\); monitor violation rate by segment (especially Standard-stock Service-contract).
  - **SP2**: require a `Critical-downtime justification` (or new triage category) event for every dedicated-van shipment; track missing-justification rate (currently 31%).
  - **Q1**: verify that Made-to-order heavy parts use reinforced packaging unless certified low-risk by inspection; track violations and their association with returns/rework loops.

- **3) Service and quality guardrails (do not “greenwash” with hidden harm)**
  - Weekly dashboards of median and p90 cycle time and late delivery by segment; set alert thresholds relative to baseline (e.g., no sustained increase for Service-contract and true Critical-downtime).
  - Track rework/return proxies: `rework_flag`, counts of `Rework production` and `Rework packaging`, and incremental energy/scrap for rework cases.
  - Specifically monitor the high-impact variants (CNC_B + rework; emergency + dedicated van) to confirm they shrink in share without increasing their service failures.

- **4) Resilience monitoring (R1 and disruption behavior)**
  - Compute route diversity per Service-contract lane/customer (rolling distinct mode count and concentration) to ensure R1 remains satisfied as consolidation and routing policies change.
  - During disruption windows, track shifts to `Emergency production` and `expedite_flag`, and evaluate whether the system can switch modes without a spike in late deliveries or emissions.

- **5) Exception governance and learning loop**
  - Implement a structured **exception log** with reason codes and approvals for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides; review exceptions weekly to distinguish true constraints (carrier disruption, capacity limits) from policy drift.
  - Use exception analytics to refine the shipping recommender and batching caps (e.g., adjust heat-treatment batching caps where late risk spikes).
  - Maintain a change-control cadence: roll out in phases (shipping first for known slack misuse; then production/inspection; then critical pooling), and only expand when guardrails remain stable.

- **6) Logging improvements (to reduce model uncertainty)**
  - Add explicit events for **critical triage**, **dedicated-van justification**, and **returns/warranty** if currently missing.
  - If possible, extend from order-level to **line-item-level** attributes (weight/geometry, item count) to enforce Q1 precisely and to compute per-item intensities rather than diluted order averages.
