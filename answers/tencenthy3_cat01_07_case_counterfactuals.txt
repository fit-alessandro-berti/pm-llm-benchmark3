## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104
Factual mechanism: Triage noted sanctions ambiguity but did not escalate; Identity and Document checks proceeded normally (I1 queue ~2 h, D1 queue ~13 h). Assessment (A1) later discovered the same ambiguity, S1 advised, and C1 performed a full review from scratch after a long compliance queue (Wed 08:00 C1 queue 8 h).

Minimal intervention: At Triage (Mon 08:50) send preliminary packet to S1 and reserve a C1 slot; allow C1 to perform a preliminary screen on partial identity/doc data while D1 finishes.

Temporal propagation: The packet lets S1 advise earlier (overlap with checks), but the critical path remains Document Check completion (Tue 05:40)  Assessment (A1 queue Tue 08:00 = 9 h, start ~Tue 15:10). Because C1 slot is reserved, final Compliance Review starts earlier (Wed 09:00 instead of 13:10) and is shortened by 1–3 h from preliminary work, bypassing part of C1 queue. Decision moves from Wed 18:00 to ~Wed 12:30.

Processfeasibility check: Foureyes satisfied (A1C1). Preliminary screen does not replace final review (rule 10). No originals, so IdentityDocument allowed. Resource clash absent: C1 reserved, S1 advisory may overlap (rule 11 allows advisory overlap).

Best/base/worst case: Best: –8 h, clean approve. Base: –6 h, same decision. Worst: +2 h if early packet incomplete forces C1 redo and reservation irritates other urgent cases.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: If C1 cannot meaningfully preprocess partial complex corporate docs, or if reserving the slot causes an urgent compliance case to breach SLAs, the intervention could be net negative.

### K-219
Factual mechanism: Triage routed to D1; Identity done Mon 16:20; originals arrived Mon 17:30; D1’s queue (11–13 h) delayed Document Check to Tue 09:30; Assessment Tue 20:00 after A2 queue.

Minimal intervention: At originals receipt, assign Document Check to D2 (queue D2 ~2–5 h) instead of waiting for D1.

Temporal propagation: D2 can start ~Mon 18:00 (postreceipt, small queue), finishing ~Tue 01:30 (standard 4–8 h, midrange). Assessment becomes eligible Tue 01:30 but waits A2 queue (Tue 08:00 queue 6 h, plus new arrivals) so starts ~Tue 14:00, ending ~17:00. Decision ready Wed 08:30 or earlier. The D1 queue shrinks by this case; D2 queue grows earlier.

Processfeasibility check: Originals Receipt precedes Document Check (rule 4). No highrisk/compliance needed. Foureyes N/A. D2 is generalist, allowed. If D2 hits handwriting difficulty, rule 6 could trigger Request More Info, creating new Document Check + Assessment.

Best/base/worst case: Best: –14 h, no rework. Base: –8 h, no rework. Worst: +20 h if D2 requests clarification and external rework (8–36 h) ensues, possibly altering decision.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: If handwriting proves illegible to D2 at high probability, or D2 queue explodes under substitution policy, the routing change increases cycle time and risk.

### K-337
Factual mechanism: Supplier delays caused RMI1 (Wed–Thu) and RMI2 (Fri–Mon). Three assessment/doc cycles; final Compliance Review Tue after C1 queue cleared.

Minimal intervention: After Assessment 2 (Fri 10:30) prohibit RMI2; require decision on current evidence, with explicit treatment of remaining discrepancy.

Temporal propagation: Removing RMI2 (Fri 10:30–Mon 09:00 external) and DocCheck3/Assess3 (Mon–Tue) collapses the tail. Assessment 2 becomes final; Compliance Review can start Fri (C1 queue Thu 08:00 = 5 h, normal) rather than waiting for Tue, completing Fri. Decision Fri/Mon instead of next Wed. Cycle cut ~4–6 days.

Processfeasibility check: Highrisk/highvalue still requires Compliance Review after latest Assessment (rule 5) – satisfied. Foureyes (A1 vs C1) intact. Intervention changes decision policy (rule 7 loop suppressed) but explicitly does not ignore discrepancy; conditional approval or rejection permitted.

Best/base/worst case: Best: –6 days, approvewithconditions, low loss. Base: –5 days, approvewithconditions, moderate audit risk. Worst: –4 days but reject or later appeal/litigation cost > process gain.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: If the unresolved discrepancy later proves material (e.g., supplier insolvency), suppressed loop would be judged negligent, making intervention harmful despite speed.

### K-411
Factual mechanism: Case lock at Triage created only Identity task; Document Check D2 started after Identity completed (Tue 15:00). Assessment waited until Wed due to queue growth.

Minimal intervention: At Triage create both tasks; controlled workaround lets I2 and D2 work concurrently with realtime timestamps.

Temporal propagation: With both tasks created, D2 can start after Triage + D2 queue (Tue 08:00 D2 queue 4 h  start ~13:00) and finish ~17:30. Identity I2 starts ~11:30 (I2 queue 2 h) ends 13:20. Both ready Tue 17:30. Assessment A2 still faces Wed queue (snapshot not given but factual shows growth), so start ~Wed 09:30, similar to factual. Gain limited to ~1.5 h earlier doc completion, but earlier eligibility may slightly advance assessment if queue permits.

Processfeasibility check: No originals, so concurrent checks allowed (rule 3). Foureyes N/A. Workaround must be logged realtime to avoid false concurrency (rule 11). Resource clash none: I2 and D2 different pools.

Best/base/worst case: Best: –4 h if assessment queue absorbs earlier. Base: –2 h. Worst: 0 h if assessment bottleneck dominates; side effect of crowding A2 queue.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: If the workaround violates audit constraints or if assessment queue is already saturated Tue evening, local gain vanishes and spillover appears.

**100case weekly rollout analysis.**  
If the K104 policy (early S1 packet + C1 reservation) is applied to 100 similar highrisk cases in one week, the single certified reviewer C1 becomes a severe bottleneck: provisional slot reservations would implicitly prioritize 100 cases over all others, displacing urgent compliance work and potentially violating SLAs for nontarget cases. Preliminary screens might save 1–3 h each, but the reservation logic could inflate the effective C1 queue by >200 h, creating systemic delay.  

The K219 substitution at scale shifts load from D1 (fast on complex) to D2 (generalist). With 100 handwrittenstandard cases, D2’s queue would absorb a large block; if even 20% trigger clarification rework (rule 6), additional Document Check + Assessment loops appear, increasing assessmentqueue pressure and externalresponse waits (8–36 h). D1 relief helps complex corporate cases, but net cycle may worsen.  

K337 suppression of rework loops across 100 cases yields large capacity release: avoided RMI loops remove many D1/A1 intervals and external delays, but weekend/response effects vanish unevenly; if unresolved discrepancies cluster, appeal/loss exposure rises nonlinearly.  

K411 concurrency for 100 digital cases creates earlier mass arrival at the assessment queue, migrating bottleneck from document/identity to assessment. Realtime timestamp logging is essential; otherwise false concurrency undermines audit and capacity measurement. Overall, local timing gains are real but easily erased by bottleneck migration and scarceresource interference.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is **K411 (parallelize verification)**. It addresses a clear structural defect (case lock) without altering compliance obligations, decision policy, or scarceresource reservation, and its expected cycle gain, while modest, is stable across queue assumptions.

The most dangerous intervention despite potential speed is **K337 (suppress rework loop)**. Removing the second information loop can cut days, but it explicitly changes the allowed decision policy and may convert a latent supplier discrepancy into realized loss, audit failure, or rejection. The speed benefit is real; the quality/compliance tail risk is severe.

A local case counterfactual is least transportable for **K104**: the reserved C1 slot produced local compliance savings, but with 100 cases the single certified reviewer becomes a systemwide bottleneck, and the “provisional slot” metaphor breaks under aggregate demand. Local gain assumes others’ delays are acceptable.

Most valuable additional data: (1) highgranularity C1 utilization and queuedisplacement logs to quantify reservation spillover; (2) D2 handwritingrework empirical rates; (3) supplierresponse distributions for K337type cases; (4) realtime assessmentqueue inflows to predict bottleneck migration under K411. These would calibrate the nonlinearities identified and improve pilot stopping rules.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **Structural** – Normative precedence and foureyes rules completely describe enforceable control flow; no hidden dependencies exist.
2. **Temporal** – Snapshot queue lengths reasonably predict waits within ±30% for the week.
3. **Resource** – C1 is the only certified compliance reviewer in the window and cannot be augmented.
4. **Behavioral** – Triage staff will reliably identify sanctions ambiguity and dispatch the early packet when instructed.
5. **Quality** – A preliminary compliance screen on partial complex documents yields 1–3 h later saving without missing material risk.
6. **Interference** – Reserving a C1 slot displaces other compliance cases proportionally to queue length.
7. **Behavioral** – D2’s higher clarification propensity on handwriting manifests only when writing is ambiguous; otherwise service matches ranges.
8. **Resource** – The controlled workaround for case lock permits true I2/D2 concurrency and is captured by realtime timestamps without violating audit rules.
