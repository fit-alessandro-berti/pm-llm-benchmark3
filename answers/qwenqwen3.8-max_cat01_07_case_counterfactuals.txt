
```text
Conditional and declarative constraints

- Four-eyes constraint: the final Assessment and final Compliance Review may not be performed by the same person.
- Resource exclusivity: a resource cannot perform two service intervals at the same time.
- Queue estimates are forecasts, not guarantees; waiting time may vary.
- Preliminary compliance work may reduce later compliance duration by roughly 1-3 h, but cannot substitute for the final compliance review.
- K-104 intervention adds preliminary escalation and a provisional C1 reservation; it does not remove the need for final compliance after final Assessment.
- K-219 intervention changes document routing from D1 to D2; D2 may start earlier but has a slower standard range and higher clarification risk on difficult handwriting.
- K-337 intervention changes the allowed decision policy: after Assessment 2, no second Request More Info loop is permitted; the decision must explicitly address the unresolved discrepancy by conditional approval or rejection, not silent acceptance.
- K-411 intervention changes task creation and concurrency control; real-time timestamps are required to distinguish true parallel work from false batch-entered concurrency.
- External rework response may take 8-36 h and is not directly controllable by internal resources.
```

The four interventions differ in what they change. K-104 mainly changes timing and preparatory routing: it adds an earlier escalation/preliminary compliance step and reserves later compliance capacity, but it does not change the final decision rule. K-219 changes routing by substituting D2 for D1; timing changes follow from queue position and resource speed, while quality risk changes because handwriting may be harder for D2. K-337 changes the decision policy: it removes an otherwise permitted rework loop and forces an explicit decision under unresolved evidence, so the counterfactual is not merely shorter but substantively different in risk and outcome. K-411 changes task creation and concurrency control; it allows legally permissible parallel verification but depends on logging discipline to make the concurrency real rather than apparent.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism: The case is high-risk and high-value, so final Compliance Review is mandatory. The factual path loses time in three places: the sanctions ambiguity is not escalated at Triage, D1’s long queue delays complex Document Check until Monday night, and Compliance Review begins late because the issue is only fully recognized after Assessment. C1 then reads much of the evidence from scratch.

Minimal intervention: The intervention adds an earlier escalation packet and provisional C1 reservation at Triage. It does not change I1, D1, or A1 assignments, and it does not allow final Compliance before final Assessment. The only structural addition is preliminary escalation/compliance preparation that may overlap with Identity and Document work.

Temporal propagation: Identity and Document timings should be held mostly fixed because the intervention does not change their queues or resources. The main propagation is that S1/C1 preliminary work can identify the sanctions ambiguity earlier, organize evidence, and reduce final Compliance Review duration. If Assessment still completes around Tuesday 19:10, a reserved C1 slot could allow final review Wednesday morning rather than Wednesday 13:10, and preliminary work could reduce the review from 4.5 h toward 3-4 h. Decision and Notify then move earlier by a few hours. The benefit is not simply “start earlier”; it depends on preliminary work being complete enough to shorten the final review.

Process-feasibility check: The path remains feasible. Identity and Document both complete before Assessment. Final Compliance occurs after the latest Assessment. Four-eyes is preserved because A1 performs final Assessment and C1 performs final Compliance. Preliminary work does not replace the final review. S1 advisory work may overlap with checks, and C1 is not performing two intervals simultaneously.

Best/base/worst case: Best case: preliminary work is complete, C1 is reserved Wednesday morning, final Compliance takes about 3 h, and the case finishes around midday Wednesday, improving cycle time by roughly 6-10 h. Base case: final Compliance starts late morning or early afternoon and finishes 3-5 h earlier than factual, giving a 3-6 h improvement. Worst case: preliminary packet is incomplete, C1 cannot be held, or Assessment overruns; the case finishes near the factual time or slightly later, while other urgent compliance cases are delayed by the reservation.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Evidence that C1 reservations consistently displace more urgent compliance work, that preliminary packets are often incomplete, that S1 cannot start before Assessment, or that final Compliance duration does not fall despite preliminary work would weaken or reverse the expected benefit.

### K-219

Factual mechanism: The case is low-risk and low-value but requires originals. Triage routes handwritten documents to D1 because handwriting may be difficult. D1’s queue delays the start until Tuesday 09:30, but D1 completes accurately in 4 h. The case then waits for Assessment and next-day Decision.

Minimal intervention: The intervention changes only document routing: once originals arrive, D2 takes the Document Check instead of D1. Identity, Originals Receipt, Assessment, and Decision remain governed by the same rules and queues.

Temporal propagation: Originals arrive Monday 17:30, so D2 could start much earlier than factual D1 if D2 queue permits. However, D2’s standard service range is longer, and handwritten documents increase the chance of clarification. If D2 starts Monday evening or Tuesday morning and takes 5-8 h, Document Check may finish Tuesday morning or early afternoon. If Assessment can absorb the earlier readiness, Decision may move earlier by a few hours. If Assessment remains queue-constrained, the case may still finish near Wednesday morning. If D2 requires clarification, the path adds external waiting, a new Document Check, and a new Assessment, which can dominate the earlier-start benefit.

Process-feasibility check: The path remains feasible. Originals Receipt precedes Document Check. Identity and Document both complete before Assessment. No compliance review is required because the case is neither high-risk nor high-value. If rework occurs, Rule 7 is satisfied by a new Document Check and new Assessment before Decision.

Best/base/worst case: Best case: D2 starts soon after originals arrive, completes overnight or early Tuesday, Assessment starts Tuesday afternoon, and Decision occurs late Tuesday or Wednesday morning, improving cycle time by perhaps 6-10 h. Base case: D2 finishes Tuesday afternoon, Assessment still starts late Tuesday, and Decision remains Wednesday morning, giving little or no cycle-time improvement. Worst case: D2 requests clarification; external response takes 8-36 h, then recheck and reassessment add another business day or more, worsening cycle time by roughly 24-72 h and possibly changing approval to conditional approval or rejection.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Evidence that D2’s handwriting clarification rate is materially higher than D1’s, that D2 cannot start soon after originals arrive, that A2 cannot start earlier even when checks finish earlier, or that rework rates increase would reverse the expected benefit.

### K-337

Factual mechanism: This high-risk, high-value case is delayed by slow and inconsistent external supplier responses. After Assessment 2, the factual path requests a second confirmation, which crosses a weekend and triggers Document Check 3 and Assessment 3 before final Compliance and Decision.

Minimal intervention: The intervention stops the process after Assessment 2. It does not remove the need for Compliance Review; it removes the second Request More Info loop and requires an explicit decision on the evidence already available. The decision may be conditional approval or rejection, but it may not silently ignore the unresolved discrepancy.

Temporal propagation: Assessment 2 completes Friday 10:30. The intervention removes the Friday-to-Monday external wait, Document Check 3, and Assessment 3. The case can move to Compliance Review after Assessment 2. If C1 can review Friday afternoon, Decision could occur Friday evening; if C1 or M1 capacity is unavailable, Decision may move to Monday or Tuesday. The saving is therefore not just the 2.5 h Assessment or 3.5 h Document Check; it is mainly the avoided weekend external delay and the avoided re-queuing of D1 and A1 work. Compliance may take longer than usual because the evidence contains an unresolved discrepancy, so the net saving is a range rather than a fixed number.

Process-feasibility check: The path is feasible under the modified decision policy. The high-risk/high-value rule is satisfied because Compliance Review occurs after the latest relevant Assessment, Assessment 2. Four-eyes is preserved because A1 performs the final Assessment and C1 performs the Compliance Review. Because no second Request More Info is issued, Rule 7’s rework requirements are not triggered. The intervention explicitly requires the decision to address the discrepancy, so compliance defensibility depends on the quality of the conditional-approval or rejection rationale.

Best/base/worst case: Best case: Compliance Review occurs Friday afternoon, Decision is made Friday evening, and cycle time falls by roughly 100-110 h. Base case: Compliance occurs Monday, Decision Monday or Tuesday, giving a reduction of roughly 60-90 h. Worst case: the decision becomes conditional approval with monitoring or rejection with appeal risk; cycle time is still shorter, but quality, loss exposure, or legal exposure worsens.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Evidence that unresolved discrepancies are frequently material, that conditional approvals generate expensive post-decision work, that appeals or audit challenges increase, or that C1/M1 cannot act soon after Assessment 2 would reduce the net value of the intervention.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: The case is low-risk, low-value, digital, and standard. The factual delay is caused by a system defect: Triage creates only the Identity task, and the case lock prevents Document task ownership until Identity completes. This forces sequential verification even though the process rules would allow concurrent Identity and Document checks when originals are not required.

Minimal intervention: At Triage, create both verification tasks and use a controlled workaround so I2 and D2 can own and execute their tasks concurrently. Require real-time timestamps so the system records true concurrency rather than later batch entries.

Temporal propagation: With both tasks created Tuesday 09:35, Identity and Document can enter their queues immediately. I2 and D2 can work in parallel, subject to queue position. Identity likely takes 1.5-3.5 h and Document Check likely takes 4-8 h for a standard digital case. Both checks may complete by mid-to-late Tuesday afternoon instead of Document Check finishing at 19:30. The case becomes ready for Assessment earlier. The final benefit depends on A2’s queue: if A2 can start earlier Tuesday evening or Wednesday morning, Decision moves earlier; if A2 is saturated, the case simply waits longer in the Assessment queue.

Process-feasibility check: The path is feasible because no originals are required, so Identity and Document may run concurrently. Both checks complete before Assessment. No compliance review is required. Real-time timestamps preserve the resource-exclusivity constraint by showing that I2 and D2, not the same resource, performed the overlapping intervals.

Best/base/worst case: Best case: both checks finish by Tuesday afternoon, Assessment occurs late Tuesday or early Wednesday, and Decision occurs Wednesday morning, improving cycle time by roughly 8-12 h. Base case: checks finish a few hours earlier, Assessment advances modestly, and Decision occurs around Wednesday midday, improving cycle time by 2-6 h. Worst case: Assessment queue does not move, benefit is near zero, or poor logging creates false concurrency and audit rework, possibly making the case slower.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Evidence that A2 cannot absorb earlier readiness, that the workaround produces timestamp exceptions, that I2/D2 queues are too congested to start concurrently, or that audit findings increase would weaken or reverse the expected benefit.

If applied to 100 similar cases in a week, K-104’s provisional C1 slots convert a first-come compliance queue into a reservation system. For high-risk/high-value cases this reduces waiting and makes final review more predictable, but C1 is a single bottleneck. Holding slots for target cases displaces other urgent compliance work, creates idle capacity if preliminary packets are incomplete, and can increase total compliance lead time unless preliminary screens genuinely save 1-3 hours per case. The net effect depends on the fraction of cases where early evidence is sufficient.

K-219’s D2 substitution changes the document pool mix. D2 starts earlier, but if many handwritten standard cases are shifted away from D1, D2’s queue grows and D1’s specialized capacity is underused. The system may gain a few hours per case when D1 is congested, but lose more hours when D2 requires clarification. Rework is the critical amplifier: each clarification adds external waiting, a new document check, and a new assessment, so a small increase in error probability can dominate the routing benefit.

K-337 avoids rework loops and therefore frees D1, A1, and external-response capacity. However, it replaces investigation with decision risk. In a 100-case rollout, weekend effects become important: stopping before a Friday RMI avoids weekend delay, but conditional approvals or rejections may generate appeals, post-decision monitoring, or later compliance challenges. The apparent capacity saving may migrate to legal, monitoring, or escalation queues.

K-411 increases simultaneous identity/document starts and pushes cases toward assessment earlier. If A2 capacity is fixed, the bottleneck migrates from verification to assessment, producing a queue earlier in the week. Real-time timestamps are essential: batch-entered concurrency can make reports look parallel while hiding waiting, violating resource constraints, and triggering audit rework. The safest rollout uses controlled pilots with queue dashboards.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-411’s controlled parallelization of identity and document verification. It aligns with the normative process for non-original digital cases, uses interchangeable resources, does not change the decision policy, and produces a measurable path change. Its main risks are implementation risks—false concurrency and assessment-queue congestion—rather than fundamental compliance risk. With real-time timestamps and a small pilot, the system can learn quickly.

The most dangerous intervention is K-337’s prohibition of the second rework loop. It can remove a weekend-delayed external loop and save many days, but it changes what decision may be made under unresolved evidence. The time saving is real only if the organization accepts conditional approvals or rejections with higher appeal, loss, or audit exposure. Because the decision is hard to reverse and the effect depends on external supplier behavior, legal risk, and compliance tolerance, this is not a simple process improvement.

The least transportable local counterfactual is also K-337, and to a lesser extent K-104. A single-case K-337 path assumes that one high-risk discrepancy can be decided safely without further confirmation. At scale, supplier inconsistency, weekend effects, and decision-policy changes alter the distribution of approvals, rejections, and post-decision work. K-104’s single-case benefit assumes C1 capacity can be reserved without harming other urgent cases; at scale, a single compliance bottleneck makes that assumption fragile.

The most valuable data are event-level timestamps for queue entry, queue exit, start, completion, and waiting reason; resource-level calendars and concurrent-work checks; compliance slot reservations and releases; rework reasons and external-response times; and outcome data such as appeals, losses, and audit findings.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural` The mandatory partial order applies: Register precedes Triage; Identity and Document completion precede Assessment; Decision precedes Notify; high-risk/high-value cases require Compliance Review before Decision.

2. `temporal` Queue snapshots and service-time ranges are forecasts, not guarantees; actual waiting and processing may vary materially.

3. `resource` C1 is the only fully certified compliance reviewer in the window, so any reservation or acceleration of C1 work creates system-wide interference.

4. `behavioral` S1 can produce a useful preliminary escalation packet at Triage, and C1 can use that preliminary work to shorten final review without compromising independence.

5. `quality` D2 has a higher probability than D1 of requiring clarification on difficult handwriting, so routing to D2 changes not only timing but also rework probability.

6. `interference` Reserving C1 capacity for K-104-like cases can delay or displace other urgent compliance cases, especially when C1’s queue is already high.

7. `behavioral` In K-337, Assessment, Compliance, and Decision makers are willing and authorized to make an explicit conditional approval or rejection without a second external confirmation.

8. `temporal` Avoiding a Friday Request More Info loop can remove weekend external-response delay, producing a calendar-time saving larger than the sum of internal processing hours.
