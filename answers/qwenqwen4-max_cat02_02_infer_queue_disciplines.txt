def served_before(row, t):
    return sum(max(0, min(e, t) - s) for s, e in row.segs if s < t)

           and r.skills <= sk and free_at(res, t):

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

**QA — FIFO + appointment insertion.**
- *Support:* A02 (chosen at 08:32 over p1/shorter A04 — kills priority and SPT); A05 (09:05 over p1 A08 and shorter A07); A07 (09:32 over p1 A08, which had already waited 52 min — kills aging 52); A03 and A06 (the two appointment jobs, both taken exactly at/after their due time).
- *Challenge:* A03 at 08:20 violates FIFO — **explained** by the appointment (a documented field), no undocumented exception needed. A04's 45-min and A08's 61-min waits contradict any priority claim, so "priority" must be dropped rather than patched. A06's +5 lateness is **bounded** by non-preemption (incumbent A05 finishes 09:20), not a violation. The 08:3008:32 gap is a 2-min unexplained idle: setup, handover, or A02 readiness — unresolved but immaterial to ordering.
- *Residual exception needed:* none for ordering; one micro-exception for the 2-min gap.

**QB — FIFO/resume + p1-only preemption.**
- *Support:* B01 resumed at 09:31 over higher-priority B03 and shorter B03; B01 resumed at 10:14 over B06 (p2) and B07 (6 min); B04 at 10:28 over p2 B06; B06 at 10:36; B02 preempting at 09:16 and B05 selected at 10:00 (the only two p1-favoring events). p1 mean wait = 8 min vs. QA 53, QC 43, QD 35 — QB is the only queue with an urgent class.
- *Challenge:* **B03 at 09:48** is the one episode no canonical rule explains: p1 B05 was eligible (arrival 09:46; scan delay makes it *earlier*, not later, so A1 cannot excuse it), SRPT would take B04 (8), strict priority B05, non-preemptive FIFO would keep B01. The reason field ("dispatcher reassignment") is positive evidence of a **manual override**, i.e., an undocumented exception rather than a rule. **B07** (+26) contradicts an appointment component: the next epoch after its 10:30 appointment was 10:36, so even a loose "next opportunity" tolerance is violated  QB appointments are inert or on a different policy version.
- *Note:* B01's three segments total exactly 45 = est, and every switch is seamless (0-min gaps), so measured preemption/resumption cost  0; any hidden setup/rework is below the 1-minute resolution (bound: 4 min across B01's four switch events).

**QC — SPT with two overrides.**
- *Support:* C02, C03, C04, C06 — four consecutive shortest-first selections against FIFO, priority and EDD; C01's note ("oldest, executed last among initial backlog") plus its 100-min wait is the classic SPT starvation signature.
- *Challenge:* **C05 at 10:54** violates SPT (C06 = 6 min was waiting). Explained by *either* p1 priority, *or* breach-triggered escalation (breach at 10:50), *or* least-slack — all equivalent here; no known constraint forces it, so a second mechanism is required. **C01 at 11:40** violates SPT *and* every deadline rule; it needs an anti-starvation cap (~100 min) or an undocumented readiness constraint on C07. C05's deadline was **infeasible at arrival** (earliest finish 10:58 > 10:50), so its lateness is not evidence of misdispatch; C07's lateness *is*: at 11:40 a feasible schedule existed (C07 11:40–11:58, C01 11:58–12:34, both on time).
- *Counterfactual cost of the escalation:* pure SPT from 10:10 gives C02, C03, C04, C06, C07, C01, C05  **one** breach (the unavoidable C05, late 104). Observed hybrid  **two** breaches (C05 late 44, C07 late 34). Escalating an already-doomed job without a feasibility check consumed 40 min of scarce capacity and destroyed a salvageable deadline.

**QD — skill routing + per-resource FIFO + appointment reservation.**
- *Support:* D05 (only D-F qualified; its 73-min wait is fully explained by D-F's 11:21–11:36 occupancy, the 12:00–12:20 meeting and the non-preemptive D03/D02 choices); D-M idle 12:03–12:30 with D05/D10 waiting is **skill-infeasible idleness**, not a priority failure; D04 at 11:25 over p1 D08 and D07 at 11:48 over p1 D10 are two clean FIFO-over-priority/SPT episodes; D06 starts exactly at 11:30.
- *Challenge:* **D10** waits 51 min although D-F was free 11:36–12:00 and D-X free at 11:48 — both qualified. The table note ("D-F still unavailable until 12:20") is **inconsistent with the resource table** (meeting starts 12:00). Either the availability record is wrong (then D-F's idle vanishes and D10's wait is partly forced), or D10 had an undocumented readiness gate, or electrical work is pinned to D-X by a routing preference — but D03 (electrical) ran on D-F at 11:21, which weakens strict pinning. **D-X idle 11:20–11:30** with D04/D07 eligible is non-work-conserving; appointment reservation explains it (starting D07's 28 min at 11:20 would have pushed D06 to 11:48), but "D-X actually available at 11:30" explains it equally well.
- *Exception budget:* skill/calendar constraints explain most of QD; **one** genuine undocumented element remains (D-F 11:36–12:00 / D10's gate).

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

**T1 — QB 09:48 (preemption of B01).**
- H_A: *p1-preemptive priority + FIFO/p1-first selection*  predicts **B05** (p1, 14).
- H_B: *manual dispatcher override on top of FIFO + p1 preemption*  predicts **B03** (whatever the dispatcher staged), with an override flag.
- H_C: *preemption triggered by p1 arrival but replacement chosen by FIFO among waiting*  also predicts B03 here, **but fails at 10:00** (it predicts B01, observed B05). H_C is therefore already disfavoured 1:1 by existing data.
- Discriminating observable: a **dispatch-decision record** with `t_decision`, `actor` (system vs. dispatcher id), `override_flag`, `reason_code`, and the **eligibility snapshot** at `t_decision`. If `t_decision`  09:46 (before B05's scan) the episode becomes decision-latency, not override.
- Quantified stakes: under H_A, B05 finishes 10:02 (12 min), B01 10:16 (12), B03 10:28 (+28); makespan unchanged at 11:02. So the override bought B03 28 min at the cost of 12 min each for a p1 job and the starved job — a testable welfare claim, not just a label.

**T2 — QC 11:40 (C01 vs C07).**
- H_A: *SPT + anti-starvation cap (~100 min)*  predicts **C01** (wait 100  cap), and predicts C07 next.
- H_B: *pure SPT with hidden readiness* (C07 not ready until 12:16)  predicts **C01** as the only eligible job.
- H_C: *SPT + breach escalation only*  predicts **C07** (18 < 36; C07 not yet breached, C01 not breached) — refuted by the observation, which is why an extra mechanism is unavoidable.
- Discriminating observable: per-job **`ready_time` / eligibility flags** (parts, customer approval, bay availability) plus the **aging counter/threshold parameter** in the policy config. If C07 was ready at 10:40 and no cap exists, H_A and H_B both fail and a manual expedite of C01 must be logged.
- Additional probe: at 11:34 the same rules diverge — H_A/H_B with C07 ready predict C06 (observed ), so the pair (11:34, 11:40) is only jointly consistent with a cap that fires between 94 and 100 min of C01 waiting. That narrow band is a falsifiable prediction for the next shift.

**T3 — QD 11:36–12:00 (D-F idle while p1 D10 waits).**
- H_A: *availability record wrong; D-F actually out from ~11:36*  predicts idle, and predicts D10's start at the first qualified free epoch (12:16 on D-X, observed ).
- H_B: *work-conserving skill-FIFO with a "no start that cannot finish before a known outage" guard*  blocks D05 (30 > 24) but still predicts **D10** (12  24)  refuted unless the guard is a blanket "no new starts within 24 min of an outage".
- H_C: *D10 not ready until ~12:16*  predicts idle and D05-only eligibility.
- Discriminating observable: **resource-state log** (actual meeting start/end, unplanned absence) + **job readiness timestamps** + any **reservation/booking record** naming D-F. Also a natural probe: a short electrical job arriving at 11:40 on the next shift — H_A idles, H_B dispatches it.

**T4 — QA 08:58 (A04) — the deliberately uninformative epoch.**
- H_A: *FIFO + appointment* and H_B: *priority with aging 45 min + appointment* both predict A04 (FIFO oldest, p1 oldest, shortest, waited 45).
- Existing partial discrimination: at 09:32, A08 (p1) had waited 52 min and lost to A07  any aging threshold 52 is refuted, so H_B survives only with a threshold in (52, 61] — an implausibly tuned parameter.
- Discriminating observable: the **ranking key / rule id** emitted per decision (or the aging parameter), plus one designed probe: a p1 job with the largest `est_min` arriving just after a p3 job, with no appointment due. FIFO picks the p3; priority/aging picks the p1.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations (ranked by plausibility)

1. **`est_min` is deterministic or back-filled from actuals (data-quality / leakage).** Evidence: 32/32 exact matches, including a fragmented 45-min job. Plausibility: very high. Effect: SPT/SEPT/SRPT and estimate-gaming hypotheses become untestable; QC's diagnosis must be stated as "duration-driven shortest-first, possibly via a duration-correlated proxy (repair type)" rather than "the dispatcher reads `est_min`". It also removes "noisy estimate" as an escape hatch for QA/QB/QD anomalies.
2. **QD resource-availability record is wrong (D-F out before 12:00, and/or D-X free only at 11:30).** Evidence: the D10 note contradicts the resource table; two idle blocks. Plausibility: high. Effect: removes QD's largest anomaly and its reservation evidence simultaneously — QD would collapse to "skill routing + per-resource FIFO" with the appointment component becoming unidentified (confidence in the appointment part drops, confidence in FIFO rises).
3. **Manual dispatcher overrides / undocumented expedites.** Evidence: QB's explicit "dispatcher reassignment" reason; QC's C05 at 10:54 and C01 at 11:40. Plausibility: high. Effect: QB becomes FIFO + p1-preemption with a human layer (6/6 rule-consistent epochs); QC becomes SPT with ad-hoc interventions rather than a designed hybrid — a very different governance conclusion (fix behaviour, not parameters).
4. **Hidden readiness/eligibility gates (parts, kitting, customer approval, bay).** Plausibility: medium-high, and *dangerous because unfalsifiable*: assigning C06 ready=11:34 and C07 ready=12:16 makes QC pure SPT (6/6), and D10 ready=12:16 makes QD fully work-conserving. Effect: would simplify diagnoses, but only eligibility logging can distinguish this from policy; without it, any queue can be "explained" by invisible constraints.
5. **Appointment semantics differ by queue / policy version.** Evidence: QA honors not-before + next-epoch insertion (A03 0, A06 +5); QD reserves capacity (D06 0, with 10 min idling); QB ignores it (B07 +26, exceeding the next-epoch bound of 10:36). Plausibility: medium-high. Effect: supports "undocumented hybrid, per-queue configuration" rather than one center-wide rule; alternatively QB's appointment field is decorative/unsynchronized data.
6. **SLA-triggered regime change in QC.** Evidence: the SPT violation occurs 4 min after the first breach. Plausibility: medium. Counter-evidence: SPT resumes at 11:34 while C07 is at risk, so it is a **one-shot escalation of the breached job**, not a persistent switch; and it is indistinguishable from plain p1 priority at that epoch. Effect: if true, QC = SPT with breach escalation; if false, QC = SPT with priority-1 exception. Both are hybrids; the trigger differs and matters for SLA design.
7. **Decision latency / staging (decision made before the recorded start).** Evidence: recurring 2-min offsets (A01 08:0008:02, B01 09:0009:02, B02 09:1409:16, B05 09:4609:48, QA 08:3008:32). Plausibility: medium. Effect: could convert QB 09:48 from "wrong job chosen" to "eligible set at decision time excluded B05", changing the diagnosis from override to latency. Not universal (0 gaps at 09:31, 10:00, 10:14, 10:28, 10:36), so it is at most intermittent.
8. **Arrival-scan delay (2 min).** Plausibility: certain to exist, **impact near zero** here: the delay is one-directional (physical arrival earlier), so it can only *add* jobs to eligible sets, never remove them. Every contradiction has margin >2 min except QB 09:48 (B05, margin 2), where the direction works *against* the excuse. It cannot rescue any priority/SPT hypothesis.
9. **Hidden preemption/rework cost.** Plausibility: low-medium. Evidence against: B01's segments sum exactly to 45 and switches are seamless; bound 4 min total. Effect: if real rework existed, QB's preemption would be more costly than it looks and the "cheap preemption" reading would be wrong — but this cannot reorder decisions.
10. **Partial skill capability (e.g., D-M doing the mechanical part of D07, D-X doing firmware).** Plausibility: low (D05's note explicitly says D-F is the *only* fully qualified resource). Effect: would enlarge QD eligible sets, make D-M's 12:03–12:30 idle and D07's delay policy failures rather than constraints, and shift QD toward "priority/SPT with skill preference".

# 6. Additional event data needed (prioritized, tied to the actual ambiguities)

**P0 — Provenance and decision identity (unblocks everything).**
- `est_min` provenance flag: was it captured at arrival or overwritten from actuals? Plus `estimate_history` (value, timestamp, author) — the only way to test SEPT vs. SPT vs. strategic under-estimation.
- `dispatch_decision` event: `decision_id`, `t_decision`, `t_start`, `resource`, `chosen_job`, **`eligible_set` snapshot** (job ids + why each excluded job was excluded), `ranking_key`, `rule_id`, `policy_version`, `override_flag`, `actor_id`. Targets: QB 09:48, QC 10:54/11:40, QD 11:36.
- `queue_entry_time` **and** `physical_arrival_time` **and** `scan_time` (three distinct fields), so the 2-min delay stops being an all-purpose excuse.

**P1 — Eligibility and readiness (kills the unfalsifiable explanations).**
- Per job: `ready_time`, `parts_status`, `authorization_status`, `bay/prerequisite` flags, with change timestamps. Specifically needed for C06/C07 (QC), D10/D07 (QD), B05 (QB), A02 (QA's 2-min gap).
- `qualification_matrix` with full vs. partial capability and any resource-pinning preference (electricalD-X?), plus `assignment_constraints` (why D03 went to D-F while D-X was free).

**P2 — Resource truth and appointment contract.**
- `resource_state` log: busy / idle / meeting / unplanned absence with **actual** start/end and reason; reconcile D-F 11:36–12:00 and D-X 11:20–11:30 against the calendar.
- Appointment as a **window**: `not_before`, `not_after`, `tolerance_min`, `reservation_flag` (was capacity held?), `honored/late_reason`. This separates QA's insertion rule, QD's reservation, and QB's apparent non-implementation.
- `current_estimated_remaining` at each decision epoch (not just at arrival) — required to separate SRPT from SPT and to score preemption triggers.

**P3 — Preemption, SLA and starvation mechanics.**
- `preemption_event`: `reason_code` (urgent arrival / reassignment / resource outage / parts), `initiator`, `setup_min`, `rework_min`, `resume_penalty`, `preempted_remaining`. Needed to price QB's two interruptions properly.
- SLA state at each decision: `deadline`, `predicted_finish_if_selected_now`, `feasible_flag`, `breach_flag`, `escalation_trigger`. This is the single most valuable addition for QC: it distinguishes breach-escalation from priority and exposes infeasible-at-arrival jobs (C05).
- `waiting_time_at_decision` and any `aging_counter`/`starvation_cap` parameter, plus `policy_version` effective-dated per queue (to test whether QB and QA/QD genuinely run different appointment code).

**Log shape for mining:** case = job; activities = `arrive  ready  dispatch_decision  start  (suspend  resume)*  complete`, with `org:resource`, `lifecycle:transition`, and the decision attributes above attached to `dispatch_decision`. PM4Py is the right tool for variants/DFG, resource-calendar conformance and waiting-time decomposition; **dispatching-rule inference is decision mining over eligibility sets**, which belongs in pandas (as in §2.5) — discovering the control-flow graph would only re-describe start order and would repeat exactly the error this task warns against.

# 7. Final synthesis

- **Most identifiable: QA.** Six non-forced epochs, a perfect 6/6 fit for FIFO-with-appointment-insertion, and two *clean* refutations of the rivals (08:32 kills SPT and priority; 09:32 kills priority-with-aging). Only one mechanism beyond FIFO is needed, and it is anchored to a documented field.
- **Least identifiable: QC.** The SPT base is clear (4/6), but the two overrides are each multiply explainable: at 10:54 priority-1, EDD, least-slack and LPT coincide; at 11:40 aging, hidden readiness and manual expedite coincide. With `est_min` possibly back-filled, even the base mechanism could be a duration-correlated proxy. QD is a close second — its constraint layer (skills, calendar) is nearly certain, but its *sequencing* rule rests on just two informative epochs, and three of its choices are 3-way/4-way confounded.
- **Most dangerous claim to infer: "QB runs preemptive priority."** Strict preemptive priority fits only 3/7 QB epochs; p2 never behaves as a priority class (B03 loses to a resumed p3 job at 09:31; B06 loses to p3 B04 at 10:28), and the one preemption that looks priority-like is labelled a *dispatcher reassignment* and actually **delayed** the p1 job by 12 min. Institutionalizing that label would license more interruption of long jobs, hide the manual override, and cement B01-style fragmentation (45 min of work spread over 88 min) on the strength of a single genuine urgent preemption. Runner-up: "QC is deadline/priority-aware" — QC produced a **preventable** breach (C07) precisely because it is deadline-blind, and its one deadline-looking choice escalated a job that was already infeasible at arrival.
- **Cross-queue pattern (hypothesis, not fact):** the center plausibly runs *oldest-first* as a default (QA, QB, QD), with three different overlays — appointment insertion/reservation (QA, QD), a p1 urgent class with preemption (QB only), and shortest-first with ad-hoc escalation/starvation relief (QC). Priority-1 mean waits (QB 8, QD 35, QC 43, QA 53 min) are the sharpest single statistic supporting that reading. The correct next step is not to relabel the queues but to log **eligibility sets and decision reasons**: with those, every diagnosis above becomes a testable one-line rule; without them, at least three of the four queues can be "explained" equally well by invisible constraints.
