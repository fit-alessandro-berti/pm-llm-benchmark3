5. **Sufficiency of CRM notifications.**  
   Prosecution: missing `Notify_Requester` in the onboarding log is a control failure.  
   Defense: stable CRM notification evidence can establish notification, especially after logging migration.

6. **Equivalence of QA_Clearance and Quality_Review.**  
   Prosecution: unmapped label change creates nonconformance.  
   Defense: procedure/UI rename with audit evidence of equivalent content establishes substantive compliance.

7. **Desirability of Request_More_Info cycles.**  
   Prosecution: unmodeled loops weaken control.  
   Defense: practice is desirable and did not cause premature activation.

8. **Identity and conflict-of-interest facts.**  
   Prosecution: aliases expose the same human requester/approver in SUP-712; undisclosed relationships create conflict risk in SUP-715.  
   Defense: M0 does not express these constraints; operational intent may have been benign.

9. **Eligibility of ambiguous Auto_Approve cases.**  
   The 9 cases with missing eligibility attributes are neither proved nor disproved as eligible.

10. **Whether informal normalization can create a permanent Auto_Approve route.**  
   Prosecution: no.  
   Defense: imminent permanent approval is at least an explanation, though not necessarily a justification.

### Burden of proof approach

Because this is a conformance review, not a criminal proceeding, I use a tiered burden:

#### To declare a case NONCOMPLIANT

The prosecution must establish, with **clear and convincing evidence**, that:

1. a binding rule or model applied to the case;
2. the rule was effective at the relevant time;
3. the event evidence reliably shows the deviation;
4. the deviation is material to control objectives; and
5. no valid exception, scope exclusion, or data defect explains the case.

A raw trace mismatch with M0 is **not enough by itself** if the mismatch is due to obsolescence, scope ambiguity, label mapping, duplicate logging, or missing mirrored events. However, mismatches involving **sanctions before activation**, **manual approval**, **spend controls**, or **segregation of duties** carry high materiality.

#### To accept a JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION

The defense must establish, with **documentary evidence**, that:

1. an authorized exception existed;
2. it was effective at the relevant time;
3. the case met all eligibility and scope conditions;
4. compensating controls were in place; and
5. the exception did not create disproportionate risk.

Practice, desirability, or operational convenience **cannot by themselves** create an exception. An expired directive requires a written extension or an independent legal/necessity doctrine. A disputed pilot can justify individual low-risk cases only if the evidence of authorization, eligibility, and monitoring is strong enough; otherwise the failure is assigned to the appropriate category, including NONCOMPLIANT or DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE.

#### Where evidence is insufficient

If the available evidence cannot distinguish noncompliance from harmless data incompleteness, the case should be classified as **DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE**, not as presumed compliant or presumed noncompliant.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest prosecution case is that CS-9 remains the only controlled normative standard, M0 remains the sanctioned control-flow model, and the operation substituted informal practices, expired directives, and weak data for binding controls.

### 1. CS-9 and M0 were never lawfully displaced

CS-9 is controlled and formally approved. M0 was not replaced by a revised POWL. PD-3 was only a steering-committee decision and was never incorporated into CS-9 or M0. The compliance observer later stated she had not approved weakening CS-9. That leaves the baseline intact.

From that premise, the prosecution can argue:

- Manual approval before activation remains mandatory unless a valid exception applies.
- Quality before activation remains mandatory unless a valid exception applies.
- Sanctions before activation is an unconditional hard control.
- Notification before closure remains part of the model and cannot be ignored because another system may contain a message.

### 2. SUP-703 is a clear severe deviation

SUP-703 is the strongest single prosecution case.

- It was a **HIGH-risk new foreign supplier**.
- It used `Auto_Approve` despite ineligibility.
- It activated before `Quality_Review`.
- It lacked the manual approval required by CS-9.
- The first purchase was EUR 180,000.

Even if PD-3 were accepted as an exception, this case fails every material eligibility condition. The prosecution can argue that this is not obsolete-model noise; it is a false use of an exception path for a supplier that should have received the most conservative controls.

### 3. SUP-708 violates the core sanctions control

SUP-708 activated a HIGH-risk supplier 40 minutes before sanctions clearance. There was no pilot or emergency authority. The fact that no transactions occurred before sanctions clearance is mitigation, not compliance. The control is preventive: activation should not occur before sanctions clearance.

The prosecution can argue that allowing “no harm occurred” to excuse activation before sanctions would convert a hard compliance control into a retrospective outcomes test. That is unacceptable for sanctions risk.

### 4. SUP-705 and SUP-714 show normalization of exceptions

SUP-705 used the emergency pattern after FD-7 expired. Operations said the emergency factually existed, but no written extension existed. FD-7 expressly expired automatically. If factual continuity alone can extend emergency powers, the time limit becomes meaningless.

SUP-714 used Auto_Approve two days after PD-3 expired. Operations believed permanent approval was imminent, but no permanent amendment existed. The prosecution can argue that this is exactly how temporary exceptions become permanent unauthorized practice.

The aggregate evidence supports this concern:

- 14 clearly ineligible Auto_Approve cases during the pilot period;
- 9 cases with missing eligibility attributes;
- 6 post-pilot Auto_Approve cases in the first week of August.

### 5. M0 silence does not excuse resource and conflict failures

SUP-712 fits M0’s control flow but violates CS-9 because the requester and manual approver were the same human through two aliases. M0’s failure to encode the resource rule is a modeling defect, but CS-9 still contains the rule. The prosecution can argue that M0’s silence does not legalize prohibited conduct.

SUP-715 fits M0, but the quality reviewer and manual approver are spouses in the same reporting line, with no recorded conflict-of-interest disclosure. Even if no explicit rule prohibits the relationship, the conflict policy requires disclosure. Absence of disclosure is a control failure.

### 6. Data weaknesses strengthen, rather than weaken, the prosecution

After the CRM migration, notification missingness rose from 2% to 29%. In SUP-706, the CRM message was entered two days later into a mutable text field. In SUP-702, the notification is in CRM but not in the onboarding log. In SUP-711, migration duplicated a register event.

The prosecution can argue that the operation cannot rely on data weakness to excuse missing controls. When a control’s evidence becomes unreliable, the auditor is entitled to raise data-quality findings and, where material, treat controls as not demonstrated.

### 7. Desirable practices still require controlled model change

SUP-707 includes repeated `Request_More_Info` cycles. Compliance agrees the behavior is desirable. But desirable behavior that is not modeled, bounded, or evidenced can still create audit opacity. The prosecution can argue that if the organization wants Request_More_Info, it should add a governed activity and loop, not simply depart from M0.

### Prosecution concession

The prosecution must concede that M0 has genuine defects:

- It has no `Request_More_Info` activity or loop.
- It has no low-risk auto-approval route.
- It has no emergency/provisional route.
- It lacks resource constraints present in CS-9.
- It does not address reactivation or rejected-lead scope.
- It assumes `Notify_Requester` appears in the onboarding log despite the CRM migration.
- It does not handle label renames such as `QA_Clearance`.

These defects mean some deviations are not misconduct. But they do not excuse ineligible high-risk auto-approval, activation before sanctions, post-expiry exceptions, or segregation-of-duties failures.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense case is that M0 is no longer a valid operational baseline because it is incomplete, incorrectly scoped, contradicted by documented temporary overlays, and applied to an event log that no longer captures the full process.

### 1. M0 is obsolete relative to governed operating reality

M0 contains neither:

- a `Request_More_Info` activity;
- nor any loop mechanism;
- nor an `Auto_Approve` route;
- nor an emergency provisional activation route;
- nor reactivation semantics;
- nor rejected-lead pre-screen semantics;
- nor the CS-9 separation-of-duties resource rule;
- nor CRM-based notification evidence.

A model that cannot express known, desirable, and documented behavior is not a complete compliance baseline. Conformance against M0 alone will overstate deviations.

### 2. SUP-702 is best understood as a justified pilot exception

SUP-702:

- occurred within the PD-3 window;
- involved a LOW-risk DE supplier in an approved corporate group;
- had sanctions before activation;
- had quality review on the next business day;
- had CRM notification with a stable `notification_id`;
- had complete pilot eligibility fields.

The defense can argue that this is exactly the narrow population PD-3 was meant to cover. The missing log notification is a data-integration issue, not proof that notification failed. The stable notification ID is stronger than a mutable text link.

The defense should not claim PD-3 amended CS-9. It can instead argue that PD-3 was a documented, time-bound exception, with compensating controls and low risk.

### 3. SUP-704 satisfies the emergency directive

SUP-704 occurred on 2026-07-11, within FD-7’s effective window.

- critical pump supplier;
- sanctions before activation;
- spend EUR 18,000, below the EUR 20,000 cap;
- daily monitoring exists;
- quality and approval occurred next business day.

This fits FD-7’s core bargain: provisional activation under strict emergency controls. Treating it as noncompliant would ignore the valid emergency overlay.

### 4. SUP-707 reveals a benign model defect, not misconduct

SUP-707 contains two `Request_More_Info` events before approval. Missing certificates were obtained. No activation occurred before requested evidence arrived. Forty-seven such cases show the same pattern.

The defense can argue that M0’s lack of a loop is a modeling defect. The business risk was reduced, not increased, by obtaining missing information. The correct remedy is model revision, not adverse conformance judgment.

### 5. SUP-709 is a scope defect, not necessarily a rejected-lead failure

SUP-709 failed sanctions pre-screen, so no quality review was performed. CS-9 itself acknowledges that rejected leads may use an abbreviated pre-screen process, but does not say whether those leads are governed by M0.

If a rejected lead is an intake object, not a full onboarding case, then SUP-709 is out of M0 scope. The correct fix is to define a separate rejection/pre-screen scope. The absence of that scope definition is a scope defect.

### 6. SUP-710 shows that reactivation is ungoverned by CS-9

SUP-710 is a reactivation of a supplier closed six months earlier. Prior quality certification remained valid. The log lacks `Register_Supplier` and `Quality_Review`, but CS-9 contains no explicit reactivation procedure.

The defense can argue that applying M0 to reactivation by default is a scope extension not supported by the normative text. A separate reactivation route, possibly relying on prior quality validity, is needed.

### 7. SUP-711 and SUP-713 are labeling/data artifacts

SUP-711 has duplicate `Register_Supplier` events from migration, but both rows share the same source transaction ID. This is a log-quality defect, not two registrations.

SUP-713 uses `QA_Clearance` instead of `Quality_Review`. The procedure and UI renamed the activity, and audit evidence shows equivalent content. The mapping was approved one week later, but the substance of the control was performed. This is a labeling/governance defect, not business misconduct.

### 8. SUP-706 is indeterminate because of CRM migration

After 2026-07-01, notification moved to CRM. Only 70% of notifications are mirrored. In SUP-706, the CRM contains a message, but the case link is a mutable text field entered two days later.

The defense can argue that this is not proof of non-notification. It is also not proof of notification. The correct classification is evidentiary indeterminacy, with remediation aimed at immutable notification identifiers.

### 9. Formal expiry matters, but factual context can affect severity

For SUP-705 and SUP-714, the defense should not argue that expired directives create lawful exceptions. They do not. But the defense can argue:

- SUP-705 involved low initial spend and the same emergency controls;
- SUP-714 involved an otherwise eligible low-risk supplier;
- these are governance failures, not necessarily fraud or reckless disregard.

This affects severity, not the validity of the exception.

### Defense concession

The defense must acknowledge serious limits on informal practice:

- SUP-703 cannot be defended because the supplier was ineligible and HIGH-risk.
- SUP-708 cannot be defended because activation occurred before sanctions and manual approval without any overlay.
- Expired pilots and emergencies cannot become permanent routes by habit.
- M0’s absence of resource rules does not excuse CS-9 violations in SUP-712 or conflict-of-interest failures in SUP-715.
- Missing or mutable notification evidence is a real control weakness, not a neutral inconvenience.

At system level, I conclude:

1. **The operation is not wholly noncompliant, but it is materially noncompliant in important respects.**

   - exception governance;

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The correct solution is not to enforce M0 blindly or to abandon CS-9. The organization should move to a layered normative architecture.

### Layer 1: Controlled policy baseline

CS-9 should remain the controlled parent policy. It should define:

- sanctions must occur before any activation;
- quality must occur before permanent activation unless a governed provisional route applies;
- manual approval is required unless an authorized Auto_Approve or emergency route applies;
- notification must occur before closure;
- separation of requester and approver;
- conflict-of-interest disclosure;
- evidence retention and immutable identifiers.

CS-9 should not contain transient pilot parameters. Those belong in policy overlays.

### Layer 2: Revised control-flow model

A revised POWL should represent:

- standard manual-approval onboarding;
- low-risk auto-approval route;
- emergency provisional route;
- rejected-lead abbreviated route;
- full rejection after checks;
- repeatable Request_More_Info behavior, at least as an approximation;
- notification before closure;
- separate reactivation and withdrawal models if needed.

This layer expresses **ordering, concurrency, and route choice**, not data eligibility or monetary caps.

### Layer 3: DECLARE/log-skeleton constraints

Some constraints are poorly expressed in POWL alone. These should be represented as DECLARE, log skeleton, or temporal constraints:

- `Precedence(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier)` for all routes.
- `Precedence(Quality_Review, Activate_Supplier)` for standard route.
- `Response(Activate_Supplier, Quality_Review, max=2 business days)` for provisional routes.
- `Response(Activate_Supplier, Manual_Procurement_Approval, max=2 business days)` for emergency route.
- `Succession(Notify_Requester, Close_Case)` or at least `Notify_Requester` before `Close_Case`.
- `Not Co-Occurrence(Auto_Approve, emergency_manual_pattern)` within the same case unless governed by valid overlay.
- Resource constraints:
  - requester != manual approver after canonical alias resolution;
  - approver/reviewer conflict disclosure required when relationship or reporting line triggers COI policy.

### Layer 4: pandas/data-aware eligibility checks

Data-aware rules should be implemented outside POWL, preferably as pandas/database checks:

- Auto_Approve eligibility:
  - risk = LOW;
  - country = DE;
  - corporate group status = APPROVED;
  - pilot effective date within window;
  - rule version assigned;
  - eligibility snapshot frozen at decision time.
- Emergency eligibility:
  - emergency ID present;
  - criticality flag;
  - activation timestamp within FD-7 window or valid extension;
  - spend cap <= EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval;
  - daily monitoring evidence;
  - quality and retrospective approval within two business days.
- Notification evidence:
  - immutable `notification_id`;
  - stable case foreign key;
  - sent timestamp;
  - created timestamp;
  - source system;
  - content hash.
- Data quality:
  - idempotency keys for register events;
  - source transaction IDs;
  - label mapping table;
  - alias master;
  - effective/record timestamps.

### Layer 5: Organizational controls

Some duties cannot be enforced by process mining alone:

- conflict-of-interest disclosures;
- segregation-of-duties access controls;
- exception approval workflow;
- compliance sign-off for pilots;
- emergency extension procedure;
- role-based training;
- internal audit sampling of Auto_Approve and emergency cases;
- periodic alias and user-access reconciliation.

### Policy-overlay governance for pilots and emergencies

Pilots and emergencies should be separate, versioned policy-overlay objects with:

- unique `rule_version_id`;
- parent policy being derogated;
- effective start and end timestamps;
- scope expression;
- eligibility conditions;
- compensating controls;
- spend caps;
- approval authority;
- compliance sign-off;
- automatic expiry;
- post-exception review.

No practice should extend an expired overlay. Factual emergency may support severity assessment, but not validity, unless a written extension exists.

### Explicit scope rules

#### Rejected leads

A rejected lead should be a distinct case type:

- `LEAD_PRESCREEN`;
- requires at least sanctions pre-check;
- may reject without quality;
- may notify and close;
- if accepted, convert to `MAIN_ONBOARDING` and require full checks.

This removes ambiguity such as SUP-709.

#### Reactivations

Reactivation should be a distinct case type:

- require current sanctions check;
- require validation of prior quality certification;
- allow quality skip only if certification is valid and risk allows;
- require manual approval unless a governed auto route applies;
- require notification before closure.

This addresses SUP-710.

#### Withdrawals

Withdrawal/dormancy should be separate:

- withdrawal request;
- review open obligations;
- deactivate supplier;
- notify requester;
- close case.

Activation controls should not be applied to pure withdrawal cases.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The revised model below uses POWL for control flow and Python dictionaries for the policy-overlay/data proposal. It deliberately does not try to make POWL enforce amount limits, dates, identities, risk scores, or authority validity. Those belong in data checks and organizational controls.

Important limitations:

1. **Request_More_Info is represented by a finite unrolled loop** up to five cycles. A true unbounded loop, if supported by the mining/conformance toolstack, would be preferable. If not, DECLARE-style existence/response constraints should supplement it.
2. **Route eligibility is not encoded in POWL.** POWL cannot know whether a case is LOW-risk, DE, approved corporate group, or emergency-critical. That must come from policy-overlay assignment.
3. **Time and spend constraints are outside POWL.** They are represented in the overlay table and data checks.
4. **Label equivalence is handled through mapping governance.** The model uses canonical labels; observed variants should be mapped before conformance checking.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition

try:
    from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import SilentTransition
except ImportError:
    class SilentTransition(Transition):
        def __init__(self, label=None):
            super().__init__(label=None)

from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def _silent():
    """
    Return a silent transition. Some PM4Py versions may not accept arguments.
    """
    try:
        return SilentTransition()
    except TypeError:
        return SilentTransition(label=None)


def make_rmi_block(max_cycles=5):
    """
    Finite approximation of a repeatable Request_More_Info mechanism.

    POWL is not the ideal place for an unbounded loop. This unrolls zero to
    max_cycles Request_More_Info repetitions as an XOR choice. For production,
    prefer a native loop construct if available, plus DECLARE/log-skeleton
    constraints for bounds and reason codes.
    """
    options = []

    # Zero RMI cycles.
    options.append(StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[_silent()]))

    # One to max_cycles RMI cycles.
    for n in range(1, max_cycles + 1):
        steps = [Transition(label="Request_More_Info") for _ in range(n)]
        block = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=steps)
        for a, b in zip(steps, steps[1:]):
            block.order.add_edge(a, b)
        options.append(block)

    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=options)


# --------------------------------------------------------------------
# MAIN ONBOARDING POWL
# --------------------------------------------------------------------

register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

# --------------------------------------------------------------------
# Route 1: standard manual-approval route
# - Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review concurrent.
# - Request_More_Info may occur concurrently with checks before approval.
# - Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier.
# - Notify_Requester before Close_Case.
# --------------------------------------------------------------------

s_std = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
q_std = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
checks_std = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[s_std, q_std])

rmi_std = make_rmi_block(max_cycles=5)

m_std = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
a_std = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
n_std = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

standard_branch = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[checks_std, rmi_std, m_std, a_std, n_std]
)
standard_branch.order.add_edge(checks_std, m_std)
standard_branch.order.add_edge(rmi_std, m_std)
standard_branch.order.add_edge(m_std, a_std)
standard_branch.order.add_edge(a_std, n_std)

# --------------------------------------------------------------------
# Route 2: low-risk auto-approval route
# - Sanctions_Check and Automated_Master_Data_Validation before Auto_Approve.
# - Auto_Approve before Activate_Supplier.
# - Quality_Review and Notify_Requester after activation; timing governed by overlay.
# --------------------------------------------------------------------

s_auto = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
md_auto = Transition(label="Automated_Master_Data_Validation")
pre_auto = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[s_auto, md_auto])

auto = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
a_auto = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")

q_auto = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
n_auto = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
post_auto = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[q_auto, n_auto])

auto_branch = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[pre_auto, auto, a_auto, post_auto]
)
auto_branch.order.add_edge(pre_auto, auto)
auto_branch.order.add_edge(auto, a_auto)
auto_branch.order.add_edge(a_auto, post_auto)

# --------------------------------------------------------------------
# Route 3: emergency/provisional route
# - Sanctions_Check before Activate_Supplier.
# - Activation before Quality_Review and retrospective Manual_Procurement_Approval.
# - Quality_Review and retrospective approval concurrent after activation.
# - Notify_Requester after retrospective completion.
# --------------------------------------------------------------------

s_em = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
a_em = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")

q_em = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
m_em = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
post_em = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[q_em, m_em])

n_em = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

emergency_branch = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[s_em, a_em, post_em, n_em]
)
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(s_em, a_em)
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(a_em, post_em)
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(post_em, n_em)

# --------------------------------------------------------------------
# Route 4: abbreviated rejected-lead route
# - Sanctions_Check pre-screen.
# - Reject_Supplier without Quality_Review.
# - Notify_Requester before Close_Case.
# --------------------------------------------------------------------

s_rej_abbrev = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
reject_abbrev = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
n_rej_abbrev = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

abbreviated_reject_branch = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[s_rej_abbrev, reject_abbrev, n_rej_abbrev]
)
abbreviated_reject_branch.order.add_edge(s_rej_abbrev, reject_abbrev)
abbreviated_reject_branch.order.add_edge(reject_abbrev, n_rej_abbrev)

# --------------------------------------------------------------------
# Route 5: full rejection after checks
# - Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review concurrent.
# - Reject_Supplier.
# - Notify_Requester before Close_Case.
# --------------------------------------------------------------------

s_rej_full = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
q_rej_full = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
checks_rej_full = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[s_rej_full, q_rej_full])

reject_full = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
n_rej_full = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

full_reject_branch = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[checks_rej_full, reject_full, n_rej_full]
)
full_reject_branch.order.add_edge(checks_rej_full, reject_full)
full_reject_branch.order.add_edge(reject_full, n_rej_full)

# --------------------------------------------------------------------
# Route choice and root model
# --------------------------------------------------------------------

route_choice = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        standard_branch,
        auto_branch,
        emergency_branch,
        abbreviated_reject_branch,
        full_reject_branch,
    ],
)

M1_onboarding = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[register, route_choice, close]
)
M1_onboarding.order.add_edge(register, route_choice)
M1_onboarding.order.add_edge(route_choice, close)

M2_reactivation.order.add_edge(quality_optional, m_react)
```
