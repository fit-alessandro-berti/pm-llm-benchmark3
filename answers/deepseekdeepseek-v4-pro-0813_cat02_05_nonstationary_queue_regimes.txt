def burst(label, arr):
    a = np.array(arr, dtype=float)

    var = a.var(ddof=1)

# 1. Data audit and estimands

## Queueing quantities and units

- Opening hours: 07:00–19:00 = 12 service hours per day.
- `arrivals` and `completed` are daily counts during opening hours.
- `median_service_min` and `p90_service_min` are active handling times in minutes, excluding waiting.
- `mean_wip` is mean cases in the system from 15-minute snapshots during opening hours.
- `staff_fte` is mean concurrently available full-time-equivalent capacity.
- `complex_pct` is percentage of arrivals following a multi-step complex variant.
- `peak_08_10_pct` is stated as percentage of daily arrivals from 08:00 through 09:59.

## Estimated quantities

For a regime:

- Arrival intensity:  
  \(\lambda_h = \text{daily arrivals}/12\) cases per opening hour.
- Completion intensity:  
  \(\mu_h = \text{daily completed}/12\) cases per opening hour.
- Service behavior: median and p90 active service minutes, not mean service time.
- WIP: median of daily `mean_wip` cases.
- Capacity pressure proxy:  
  \(\rho_{\min} \approx \lambda_h \times \text{median service}_h / c\)  
  \(\rho_{p90} \approx \lambda_h \times \text{p90 service}_h / c\)  
  where \(c = staff\_fte\). These are not true offered load because mean service time is not supplied; they are a sensitivity range assuming the mean service time lies between the median and p90. That assumption is plausible for moderately right-skewed service times but is not guaranteed.

## Key limitations and assumptions

Assumptions:
- Each day has 12 usable opening hours.
- `mean_wip` is representative of opening-hours system load.
- `arrivals` and `completed` are consistently measured across days.
- `complex_pct` measures an actual arrival-mix property.

Threats to naive inference:
1. **No 07:00 backlog or overnight carry-over**: Cases can remain overnight, so daily completed counts may include arrivals from prior days.
2. **Median service time is not mean flow time**: Little’s Law requires mean flow time, not median service time.
3. **Snapshot WIP is interval-censored**: 15-minute snapshots during opening hours omit overnight WIP and can miss short peaks.
4. **`peak_08_10_pct` inconsistency**: For the representative hourly vector on 2026-04-21, bins 08–09 and 09–10 sum to 11 arrivals, about 9.6% of the day’s 115 arrivals, not the 47% shown in the daily table. This suggests either the hourly vector is not from the same definition, the bins are shifted, or there is a data-quality issue.
5. **`complex_pct` may be coding or case-mix change**: The jump around 2026-04-20 may be real complexity, a coding change, or both.
6. **Daily rows are not independent**: Repeated weekdays, backlog carry-over, outage recovery, and staffing changes create serial dependence.
7. **Staffing intervention time not logged**: 2026-04-29 begins the intervention, but partial-day effects are possible.
8. **Aggregate service-time summaries hide distribution shape**: Median and p90 alone cannot support mean queueing estimates without assumptions.

- Fano factor \(F = \sigma^2/\mu\). A Poisson process has \(F \approx 1\).
- Arrivals are overdispersed, especially on 2026-04-21, where the hourly arrival series has two large spikes and a Fano factor above 10.
- Completion streams are smoother than arrivals on 2026-04-21, likely because work is scheduled or adjusted after arrival spikes.
- The representative days cannot establish a full stochastic model: they are only four days, with no uncertainty bands, and they are not enough to estimate seasonal stationarity, day-of-week effects, or arrival burst parameters by regime.
- The daily sequence is not stationary: there is an abrupt complex-mix shift around Apr 20, an outage Apr 15, and an intervention Apr 29. Daily arrivals also show strong weekly periodicity.

Change-point evidence from daily aggregates:
- Apr 15: outage; completed drops to 58, WIP jumps to 47.
- Apr 20: complex mix jumps from 23% to 41%, service p90 from 56 to 78, WIP from 13 to 37.
- Apr 29: staffing increases; completion count exceeds arrivals and WIP begins declining.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little’s Law: \(L = \lambda W\), where \(L\) is mean number in system, \(\lambda\) is mean throughput, and \(W\) is mean flow time. Here, `mean_wip` is used as \(L\), but median service time is not \(W\).

The main defensible interpretations are **approximate**, not exact, because the start/end backlog is unknown and the WIP data are opening-hours snapshots, not continuous time.

# 7. Operational implications

- **Staffing should be regime-specific**: Weekday high-complexity pre-intervention had arrivals of about 115/day with completions of only 101/day at 6 FTE. With intervention staffing around 7 FTE, completions exceeded arrivals. This supports adding capacity during high-complexity weekday periods.

- **Weekend high-complexity is not low-risk just because volume is low**: R5 had only about 47 arrivals/day, but WIP was 21.5 with 2.5 FTE and completions were below arrivals. The weekend should be monitored for backlog growth when complexity remains high.

- **Outage recovery is not a high-throughput regime**: Apr 16–17 completion counts above arrivals are likely catch-up from the Apr 15 outage. Using those days as a normal capacity benchmark would overstate sustainable throughput.

- **Peak smoothing may help more in the post-mix period**: The hourly vector for 2026-04-21 shows arrival spikes around hours 10–11 and 13–14. A pooled daily average would miss these spikes. Staff breaks, non-urgent work, and meetings should avoid those peaks.

- **Control for complexity coding**: The complex-mix jump may be partly a coding change. Operational decisions based on `complex_pct` need a stable definition.

- **Data collection recommendations**:
  - Record event logs with case IDs, start and end timestamps, waiting vs service time, staff assignment, and variant.
  - Record 07:00 backlog daily.
  - Log exact staffing intervention implementation time.
  - Record outage start/end times precisely.
  - Collect continuous or more frequent WIP snapshots, not only daily means.
  - Align the `peak_08_10_pct` definition with hourly vectors.

- **Policy that looks beneficial under pooled averages but is risky under segmented view**: Staffing to the pooled median arrivals and service time could look adequate because pooled WIP is about 23 cases and median service is about 32.5 min. Under a segmented view, high-complexity weekdays before intervention had WIP near 44 and service p90 near 83 min, with completions below arrivals. A pooled staffing plan would likely under-serve the high-complexity weekday regime and allow backlog to accumulate.

# 3. Burstiness / dispersion indicators and serial dependence
def burstiness(label, arr):
    a = np.asarray(arr, dtype=float)
    mu = a.mean()
    var = a.var(ddof=1)
    cv = np.sqrt(var) / mu
    fano = var / mu
    centered = a - mu
    denom = np.sum(centered ** 2)
    lag1 = np.sum(centered[:-1] * centered[1:]) / denom
    return pd.Series({
        "Fano": fano,
        "CV": cv,
        "lag1": lag1,
    })
