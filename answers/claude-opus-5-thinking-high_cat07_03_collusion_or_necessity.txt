# 2. Benign alternative explanations

For each pattern I give at least two benign explanations, plus the evidence that would **support** and the evidence that would **refute** each.

## 2.1 Buyer_Carla ↔ Approver_Gita: four-eyes violations, 8-minute approvals, 36 A→B→A loops

**Benign explanation A — genuine emergency handling in a declared rush channel.** Carla is described as specialized in emergency medical supplies; Gita is a *delegated approver for urgent cases*; 88% of the subgroup carries `emergency_flag=True`. An 8-minute PO-to-approval time is what a functioning expedite channel looks like when a clinical need is time-critical.
- *Supports:* per-case `emergency_flag=True` set **before** the approval timestamp (not backfilled); an approved delegation-of-authority document naming Gita as urgent-case delegate with an amount ceiling; requisitions traceable to clinical demand (ward/stock-out tickets); approval times clustering during declared incidents rather than uniformly; amounts within the delegated limit.
- *Refutes:* fast approvals on cases where `emergency_flag=False`; `emergency_flag` written after the approval event; amounts above the delegated ceiling; the same speed persisting for routine categories; emergency flag set by the same person who benefits from the shortcut.

**Benign explanation B — attribution artefact from the shared account and the bot mapping.** The log itself states that 23 emergency approvals carry `org:resource="Approver_Gita"` while `entered_by` records assistants, and that `legacy_erp` sometimes collapses Bot_PO and a human into one `org:resource`. Either defect fabricates "same resource created and approved" without any human ever self-approving.
- *Supports:* for violating cases, `entered_by` ≠ `approved_by` at the audit-trail level; the Create PO event carrying a bot technical user in the underlying ERP document; violations concentrated in `source_system="legacy_erp"`; the violation rate dropping to near zero when the log is re-derived from `entered_by`/`approved_by` instead of `org:resource`.
- *Refutes:* violations persisting after resolving `org:resource` to individual identities; violations present in modern (non-legacy) source systems; SSO/access logs showing one physical session performing both actions.

**Benign explanation C — capacity constraint, not preference.** Outside business hours or during a surge there may be only one authorized approver available for that category, so "repeated pair" reflects the roster, not choice.
- *Supports:* on-call rosters showing Gita as the only urgent-category approver in those windows; violations concentrated out-of-hours or in surge weeks.
- *Refutes:* other qualified approvers demonstrably available and idle at those timestamps.

## 2.2 Buyer_Dan ↔ Manager_Hugo: 10-minute approvals, weekend cases, 29 loops

**Benign explanation A — out-of-hours escalation for facilities repairs.** Burst pipes and building failures genuinely require a manager to authorize on a Saturday; 79% emergency flags and the "weekend cases" note fit this.
- *Supports:* timestamps falling on weekends/holidays; matching facilities work orders or incident tickets; Hugo's role description covering emergency overrides (it does: "exceptions / emergency overrides"); repair vendors from a pre-approved call-off list.
- *Refutes:* weekday, non-urgent purchases treated the same way; no corresponding incident ticket; new/one-off vendors rather than framework contractors.

**Benign explanation B — escalation loop = *more* review, not less.** Hugo → Dan → Hugo can mean the manager sent the case back for missing information and re-approved after correction, which is a strengthening of control.
- *Supports:* attribute deltas between the two Hugo events (amount, scope, vendor, attachments changed); a rejection/return event or comment in the workflow history; longer total cycle time for looped cases.
- *Refutes:* zero field changes between the two events (empty loop), sub-minute intervals, or loops that only ever terminate in approval regardless of content.

**Benign explanation C — role concentration by design.** With 180 events across 170 cases, Hugo is a low-volume exception handler; a manager who owns exceptions will mechanically appear paired with whichever buyer generates exceptions.
- *Supports:* Dan's exception rate being similar to other buyers' exception rates once routed to Hugo; Hugo pairing with other buyers at comparable rates.
- *Refutes:* Hugo approving Dan's cases at a materially higher rate than other approvers do for comparable cases, with no routing rule to explain it.

## 2.3 VendorMaster_Lena ↔ AP_Kim: bank changes, V-77x concentration, 17-minute payments, 83% manual

**Benign explanation A — the documented V-77x banking-platform migration.** The evidence explicitly records that V-77x changed banking platform and that several bank-detail changes were legitimate mass-maintenance operations. Mass maintenance is normally executed outside the standard interface, which explains both the `manual_upload` concentration and the vendor concentration; and if the new account details were validated once at the ticket level, downstream approvals could legitimately be quick.
- *Supports:* a change-management or maintenance ticket predating each bank change, with new IBANs matching a bank-issued confirmation letter for the whole vendor family; identical target account details across the affected cases (mass update, one destination per legal entity); Lena's changes falling inside the migration window; the vendor confirming the change through an independently verified channel (call-back to a number from the master vendor record, not from the change request); payment amounts matching approved POs and goods receipts.
- *Refutes:* bank changes outside the migration window; **distinct** destination accounts per case rather than a single migrated account; new account holder names not matching the vendor's legal name; changes to vendors outside V-77x hiding in the same batch; no ticket at all; the "revert" in the Lena→Kim→Lena loop restoring the *original* account shortly after payment.

**Benign explanation B — legitimate risk-based specialization.** AP_Kim is described as handling *payment approvals for higher-risk suppliers*. If V-77x is classified higher-risk (recent master-data change is itself a risk trigger), routing those payments to Kim is the control working as intended — the pair exists **because** of the risk, not in spite of it. Similarly, Bot_Match → AP_Kim (210 co-cases) shows Kim is the designated destination for exceptions.
- *Supports:* a documented routing rule assigning recently-changed or high-risk vendors to Kim; Kim handling comparable volumes of other high-risk vendors; the pairing predating the V-77x migration.
- *Refutes:* Kim handling V-77x specifically while other high-risk vendors go elsewhere; no documented routing rule; Kim's approvals for V-77x systematically faster than Kim's own approvals for other high-risk vendors (a within-person comparison that controls for individual working style).

**Benign explanation C — small-team segregation limits.** With only two master-data staff and a small payment-approval group, some overlap is arithmetically unavoidable, especially during absence.
- *Supports:* leave/roster records showing Marc absent on the relevant dates; a documented, approved compensating control for such periods.
- *Refutes:* alternative approvers available; overlap occurring routinely rather than during coverage gaps.

**Note on why this cluster still ranks highest:** each benign explanation above is *plausible and specific*, but unlike the emergency clusters, none of them is corroborated by anything already in the log — the very field that would corroborate it (the maintenance-ticket linkage) is missing (D3/R18). Prioritizing this cluster is therefore a statement about *evidential uncertainty on a high-consequence control path*, not an allegation.

## 2.4 Same-person retrospective justification (17 cases)

**Benign explanation A — migration artefact.** Twelve of seventeen share identical to-the-minute timestamps and came from a `quarter_close_upload` spreadsheet. Bulk loads typically stamp the loader's identity and the load time onto every row, which simultaneously manufactures the "same person" and the "9 minutes" findings.
- *Supports:* identical timestamps to the second across the batch; a technical/loader user in the underlying record; the original spreadsheet showing different named authors and earlier real dates; other unrelated activities from the same upload showing the same collapse.
- *Refutes:* distinct, plausible timestamps within the batch; the spreadsheet itself showing the override author as the justifier; the pattern also occurring in non-migrated cases.

**Benign explanation B — genuine administrative batching under a compliant process.** A team may legitimately draft justifications contemporaneously (on paper or in a ticket) and only key them into the ERP at quarter end. The *control* (independent justification within 3 working days) may have been satisfied even though the *log* cannot show it.
- *Supports:* dated source documents (signed forms, emails, ticket entries) within the 3-working-day window, authored by someone other than the overrider.
- *Refutes:* no underlying document, or documents dated after quarter close, i.e. the justification was genuinely reconstructed retrospectively — which is a real control failure (though still an exception, not proof of misconduct).

**Residual concern either way:** even under the most benign reading, 18–21% late retrospective justification in the emergency subgroups means the compensating control for emergency overrides is not reliably operating. That is a legitimate audit finding independent of anyone's intent.

## 2.5 Vendor Check + Change Vendor Bank Details by the same person (31 cases)

**Benign explanation A — intended role design.** Master-data specialists conventionally own the full vendor record lifecycle; there may simply be no rule forbidding this combination (the evidence itself notes this).
- *Supports:* the segregation-of-duties matrix showing this combination as permitted; role definitions in the ERP authorization concept.
- *Refutes:* an SoD matrix that prohibits it, i.e. a genuine violation rather than a rule that does not exist.

**Benign explanation B — sequencing artefact.** "Vendor Check" may occur at onboarding and the bank change months later in the same case; the "same person" is then just the same specialist touching one vendor twice over time.
- *Supports:* long intervals between the two events; different underlying tickets.
- *Refutes:* the two events occurring minutes apart with no intervening review.

## 2.6 Temp_Nina → Buyer_Carla (4-minute handovers, 65 cases)

**Benign explanation A — intake-and-route data entry.** A temp keys requests and the workflow immediately assigns the specialist buyer; 4 minutes is the signature of an automatic or near-automatic routing step, not of a decision.
- *Supports:* Assign Buyer being system-driven or rule-based; Nina having no approval authority in the authorization concept.
- *Refutes:* Nina appearing on approval or master-data activities; Nina's user having authorizations beyond data entry.

**Benign explanation B — genuine quarter-end backlog support.** The note "end-of-quarter support" and "emergency backlog" is a mundane staffing explanation.
- *Supports:* Nina's 190 events concentrated in the final weeks; a contract/onboarding record with a defined limited role.
- *Refutes:* activity spread evenly across the quarter with elevated privileges, or Nina's account remaining active after the assignment ended.

# 3. Case prioritization

Guiding principles: prioritize by **(consequence of the control × strength of unexplained evidence × cheapness of resolution)**, not by volume or by how unusual a person's working pattern looks. Resolve data-quality questions *first* where they can invalidate a whole finding, because that is the cheapest way to shrink the population.

## High priority

**H1 — VendorMaster_Lena + AP_Kim, vendor family V-77x (44 cases; core focus the 16 fastest and the 9 four-eyes cases).**
- *Reason:* the only cluster where five independent indicators compound on the highest-consequence control path (beneficiary bank change immediately preceding payment release): four-eyes breach (R2), 27x timing compression with no declared emergency channel (R7, only 16% emergency), 68% high-amount, 72% single-vendor-family concentration, 83% manual upload, plus an 11-case change→pay→change loop (R11) and a *missing* ticket linkage that prevents the benign migration explanation from being confirmed in-system. Small enough (44 cases, 16 critical) to review case-by-case within days.
- *Inspect first:* (1) the **field-level before/after values** of every bank-detail change — old and new IBAN/account number, bank name/country, account holder name — and whether the new details are **identical across cases** (consistent with mass migration) or **distinct per case** (not); (2) whether any account reverts to the original after payment in the 11 loop cases; (3) the V-77x banking-migration ticket, bank confirmation letter, and vendor's own written notification, with an **independent call-back** to a phone number taken from the pre-change master record; (4) the `manual_upload` files themselves — filename, uploader, upload timestamp, checksum, and whether the file also touched vendors outside V-77x; (5) `entered_by` vs `approved_by` on the 9 four-eyes cases plus ERP change documents (e.g. master-data change log) and authorization objects for Lena and Kim; (6) three-way match evidence — PO, goods receipt, invoice — and payment remittance advice for the affected payments; (7) any bank returns, duplicate payments or vendor complaints of non-receipt in the period; (8) access logs / IP / session data for the manual uploads.

**H2 — Shared-account and bot-mapping resolution (23 shared-account emergency approvals + all `legacy_erp` Create PO events; scoping affects the 42 R1 cases).**
- *Reason:* this is a *prerequisite*, not a parallel workstream. Until `org:resource` is resolved to real human identities, the headline four-eyes number (42 cases) is not interpretable, and both false positives and false negatives are possible. It is also a genuine control weakness in its own right — shared accounts defeat accountability and non-repudiation regardless of what the cases contain.
- *Inspect first:* the `legacy_erp` resource mapping table and its change history; user-master records for the "Approver_Gita" account (is it a named user, a shared login, or a delegation container?); `entered_by` vs `approved_by` vs `org:resource` for all 23 cases; SSO/authentication logs, session IDs, terminal/IP for the approval events; delegation-of-authority records for the assistants; and the technical user IDs behind Bot_PO/Bot_Match. Deliverable: a re-derived four-eyes population based on real identities.

## Medium priority

**M1 — Emergency four-eyes violations after re-derivation: Buyer_Carla/Approver_Gita and Buyer_Dan/Manager_Hugo (subset of the 42, plus the 84 and 62 fast-approval cases).**
- *Reason:* the mechanism (declared emergency channel, delegated approver, specialization) is credible and supported by the case attributes, so this is *most likely* a control-design question — whether the expedite channel has adequate compensating controls and approval ceilings — rather than an investigation. It is medium rather than low because self-approval on commitment of funds is still a real breach, 42 cases is non-trivial, and 31–42% of these cases are high-amount.
- *Inspect first:* delegation-of-authority documents and amount ceilings for Gita and Hugo; whether `emergency_flag` was set **before** the approval (compare flag-set timestamp with approval timestamp) and by **whom**; the underlying clinical/facilities urgency evidence (stock-out reports, incident/work-order tickets, weekend call-outs); a targeted sample of the **highest-amount** and **non-emergency-flagged** cases within the pair (these are the diagnostic minority — the ~12% of Carla/Gita cases without an emergency flag are more informative than the 88% with one); vendor identity and whether emergency purchases went to framework suppliers or to new vendors.

**M2 — Emergency-override justification integrity (17 same-person cases; and, more importantly, the 18%/21% late-justification rates in the two emergency subgroups).**
- *Reason:* the compensating control that legitimises emergency overrides is demonstrably not evidenced. Even under the fully benign migration reading, the organisation cannot show that independent justification occurred within 3 working days — a reportable control weakness. Medium because the evidence points strongly to a recording defect rather than an intent problem.
- *Inspect first:* the `quarter_close_upload` source spreadsheet — original authors, original dates, edit history/metadata; the 5 same-person cases that were **not** part of the batch (these are the cases where the "migration artefact" explanation does not apply, and they are the real sample); signed justification forms or ticket entries with contemporaneous dates; and the working-day calendar used to compute the 3-day window.

**M3 — Subcontracting loops as substance vs. rubber-stamping: Gita→Carla→Gita (36) and Hugo→Dan→Hugo (29), benchmarked against Aline→Elena→Aline (33) and Boris→Felix→Boris (30).**

Cheap to resolve analytically before spending audit hours.
- *Inspect first:* attribute deltas between the first and second event of the same resource (amount, vendor, quantity, attachments, comments); presence of rejection/return events or approval comments; intra-loop durations; and whether looped cases ever end in rejection.

**M4 — Vendor Check + Change Vendor Bank Details (31 cases) as a policy-design review.**
- *Reason:* the evidence explicitly notes this may reflect role design. This is an SoD-matrix question — is the combination prohibited at all? — and should be answered by reading the policy before reading any case.

# 4. Network and conformance analyses

Assumptions: the log is a pandas DataFrame `df` with the stated columns. Code is illustrative pseudocode in PM4Py style; exact signatures should be checked against the installed version, and some helpers below are plain pandas because the interesting questions (identity resolution, before/after field values, baseline comparison) are not process-mining primitives.

## 4.0 Preparation and identity resolution (do this first)

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

df = pd.read_csv("p2p_log.csv")
df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"], utc=True)

log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)

# Critical: build a resource view that does NOT trust org:resource blindly.
# Rationale: shared account "Approver_Gita" and the legacy_erp bot/human mapping error
# can both fabricate and mask four-eyes findings.
BOTS = {"Bot_PO", "Bot_Match"}

def effective_actor(row):
    # Prefer the fine-grained audit-trail identity when it exists and differs.
    for col in ("entered_by", "approved_by"):
        v = row.get(col)
        if pd.notna(v) and str(v).strip():
            return str(v)
    return row["org:resource"]

log["actor:resolved"] = log.apply(effective_actor, axis=1)
log["is_bot"] = log["org:resource"].isin(BOTS)
log["actor:human"] = log["actor:resolved"].where(~log["is_bot"])

# Quantify the mapping defect rather than assuming it away.
suspect_mapping = log[
    (log["source_system"] == "legacy_erp")
    & (log["concept:name"] == "Create Purchase Order")
    & (~log["is_bot"])
    & (log["approved_by"].notna())
]
print("Potentially mis-mapped bot/human Create PO events:", len(suspect_mapping))

# How often does org:resource disagree with the audit trail?
disagree = log[log["org:resource"] != log["actor:resolved"]]
print(disagree.groupby(["org:resource", "source_system"]).size().sort_values(ascending=False))
```

Every four-eyes result below should be produced **twice** — once on `org:resource` (the naive view) and once on `actor:resolved` (the resolved view) — and the difference reported. That delta is itself an audit finding about data integrity.

## 4.1 Four-eyes filtering

```python
def four_eyes(log_df, a1, a2, resource_key="org:resource"):
    return pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
        log_df, a1, a2,
        activity_key="concept:name",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        resource_key=resource_key,
    )

pairs = [
    ("Create Purchase Order", "Approve Purchase Order"),
    ("Change Vendor Bank Details", "Payment Approval"),
    ("Emergency Override", "Retrospective Justification"),
    ("Vendor Check", "Change Vendor Bank Details"),   # policy question, not necessarily a rule
]

summary = []
for a1, a2 in pairs:
    naive    = four_eyes(log, a1, a2, "org:resource")
    resolved = four_eyes(log, a1, a2, "actor:resolved")
    n_naive    = naive["case:concept:name"].nunique()
    n_resolved = resolved["case:concept:name"].nunique()
    summary.append((a1, a2, n_naive, n_resolved, n_naive - n_resolved))

print(pd.DataFrame(summary, columns=["act1", "act2", "naive", "resolved", "explained_by_identity"]))
```

*Interpretation:* a large `explained_by_identity` on the Create PO + Approve PO pair would substantially deflate R1. Two refinements matter more than the raw networks:

**(a) Normalize edge weights.** A raw co-case count rewards volume. Compute an observed/expected ratio so that Aline→Elena (large teams, many cases) is not mistaken for an anomaly and small-but-exclusive pairs are not hidden:

```python
import numpy as np
edges = pd.DataFrame(
    [(s, t, w) for (s, t), w in ho.items()], columns=["src", "dst", "w"]
)
out_tot = edges.groupby("src")["w"].sum()
in_tot  = edges.groupby("dst")["w"].sum()
N = edges["w"].sum()
edges["expected"] = edges["src"].map(out_tot) * edges["dst"].map(in_tot) / N
edges["lift"] = edges["w"] / edges["expected"]
# Exclusivity: what share of this approver's work comes from this one buyer?
edges["share_of_dst"] = edges["w"] / edges["dst"].map(in_tot)
```

*Interpretation:* high `lift` **plus** high `share_of_dst` **plus** a risky activity path is the combination worth attention. High weight alone is not (this operationalises the constraint that co-working frequency is not by itself suspicious).

**(b) Distinguish substantive loops from empty ones.** For each A→B→A subcontracting instance, test whether anything changed between A's two events:

```python
WATCH = ["amount_band", "vendor_id", "contract_type", "purchase_category"]

def loop_substance(log_df, a, b):
    rows = []
    for cid, cdf in log_df.sort_values("time:timestamp").groupby("case:concept:name"):
        r = cdf["actor:resolved"].tolist()
        for i in range(len(r) - 2):
            if r[i] == a and r[i+1] == b and r[i+2] == a:
                before, after = cdf.iloc[i], cdf.iloc[i+2]
                changed = any(before[c] != after[c] for c in WATCH)
                gap = (after["time:timestamp"] - before["time:timestamp"]).total_seconds() / 60
                rows.append((cid, changed, gap))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["case", "changed", "gap_min"])

for a, b in [("Approver_Gita", "Buyer_Carla"),
             ("Manager_Hugo", "Buyer_Dan"),
             ("VendorMaster_Lena", "AP_Kim"),
             ("Buyer_Aline", "Approver_Elena")]:   # baseline included deliberately
    res = loop_substance(log, a, b)
    print(a, b, len(res), "unchanged-loop share:", round((~res["changed"]).mean(), 2),
          "median gap(min):", round(res["gap_min"].median(), 1))
```

*Interpretation:* if the Gita/Carla loops show a *lower* unchanged-share than the Aline/Elena baseline, the "clarification loop" reading is supported and R9 can be downgraded. For Lena→Kim→Lena, additionally diff the bank-detail fields specifically (from the master-data change log, since the event log may not carry them) — a revert to the pre-change account is the single most decisive observation available.

The resource-and-deadline part of the rule is not expressible in standard DECLARE, so add an explicit predicate check:

```python
def justification_rule(log_df, max_business_days=3):
    rows = []
    for cid, cdf in log_df.groupby("case:concept:name"):
        cdf = cdf.sort_values("time:timestamp")
        ov = cdf[cdf["concept:name"] == "Emergency Override"]
        rj = cdf[cdf["concept:name"] == "Retrospective Justification"]
        if ov.empty:
            continue
        if rj.empty:
            rows.append((cid, "missing", None, None, None)); continue
        o, j = ov.iloc[0], rj.iloc[0]
        bdays = np.busday_count(o["time:timestamp"].date(), j["time:timestamp"].date())
        same_person = (o["actor:resolved"] == j["actor:resolved"])
        batch = (j["source_system"] == "quarter_close_upload")
        status = ("late" if bdays > max_business_days else
                  "same_person" if same_person else "ok")
        rows.append((cid, status, bdays, same_person, batch))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["case", "status", "business_days", "same_person", "batch_loaded"])

jr = justification_rule(log)
print(pd.crosstab(jr["status"], jr["batch_loaded"]))
```

*Interpretation:* the crosstab separates "control genuinely failed" from "control cannot be evidenced because of the migration" — and the `status == "missing"` bucket (overrides with **no** justification at all) may be more important than the 17 same-person cases, since it is not mentioned in the evidence provided and would be a straightforward gap.

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

## Evidence that would increase concern

**On the Lena/Kim + V-77x cluster (H1):**
- Bank-detail change records showing **distinct destination accounts per case** rather than one migrated account per legal entity, or an account holder name that does not match the vendor's registered legal name, or a bank country inconsistent with the vendor's domicile.
- Any case in the 11-instance Lena→Kim→Lena loop where the account is **reverted to the original value shortly after** `Release Payment`.
- Bank changes with **no maintenance ticket**, or with timestamps falling **outside** the V-77x banking-migration window, or `manual_upload` files that also silently touched vendors **outside** V-77x.
- Vendor bank details changed on the basis of an **inbound email or phone request** with no independent call-back, or a call-back placed to a number supplied in the change request itself.
- Payments without a matching PO and goods receipt, duplicate payments, split payments just below an approval threshold, or vendor complaints of non-receipt / bank returns in the period.
- Access logs showing the bank change and the payment approval originating from the **same session, workstation or IP**, or logins outside normal hours or from unusual locations.
- Authorization records showing Lena or Kim held privileges beyond their stated role, or a recently granted privilege that enabled the combination.
- Any relationship between the involved staff and V-77x beneficiaries disclosed in (or absent from) conflict-of-interest declarations, and any V-77x entity with recent incorporation, a virtual-office address, or ownership overlap.

**On the emergency clusters (M1):**
- `emergency_flag` written **after** the approval timestamp, or set by the same person who benefits from the expedited path.
- Fast approvals on cases with `emergency_flag=False`, or on `purchase_category` values unrelated to medical supplies / facilities repair.
- Approved amounts **exceeding** Gita's or Hugo's documented delegation ceiling, or no delegation-of-authority document at all.
- Emergency purchases routed to **new or single-source vendors** rather than framework suppliers, or a rising share of one vendor within the emergency channel.
- Absence of any underlying clinical stock-out report, incident ticket or work order for a sampled emergency case.
- Loop analysis showing the Gita/Carla and Hugo/Dan loops are **empty** (no field changed, sub-minute gaps) at a materially higher rate than the Aline/Elena and Boris/Felix baseline.
- Growth over time: the expedite channel's share of total spend increasing quarter on quarter, or emergency use clustering near period-end thresholds.

**On justification and data quality:**
- The five same-person justification cases **not** in the migration batch turning out to have genuine, distinct timestamps.
- The original `quarter_close_upload` spreadsheet showing the overrider as the true author, or documents dated only after quarter close.
- Emergency Override events with **no** Retrospective Justification at all.
- Four-eyes and fast-approval counts **persisting** after `manual_upload`, `legacy_erp` and `quarter_close_upload` are excluded and identities are resolved.
- The shared "Approver_Gita" account showing logins from multiple concurrent sessions or from users with no approval authority.

## Evidence that would reduce concern

**On the Lena/Kim + V-77x cluster:**
- A dated change-management ticket and a **bank-issued confirmation letter** for the V-77x platform migration covering exactly the affected vendors and dates, with the new account details **identical** across the batch and matching the letter.
- Independent vendor confirmation obtained by call-back to a number from the **pre-change** master record, plus the vendor confirming receipt of the payments in question.
- Complete three-way match (PO, goods receipt, invoice) and remittance advice consistent with the approved POs, with no duplicates and no unexplained variances.
- A documented routing rule that assigns recently-changed or higher-risk vendors to AP_Kim, plus evidence that Kim handles other high-risk vendors at comparable speed (making the 17-minute figure a property of Kim's queue, not of V-77x).
- Roster/leave records showing VendorMaster_Marc unavailable on the relevant dates, with a documented and approved compensating control for the coverage gap.
- The `manual_upload` files traceable to an authorized migration process with a named owner, approval, and checksum, touching only V-77x.

**On the emergency clusters:**
- An approved delegation-of-authority document naming Gita as urgent-case delegate and Hugo as override authority, with ceilings that **cover** the observed amounts.
- `emergency_flag` consistently set **before** approval, by requesters rather than approvers, and traceable to independent clinical or facilities incident records.
- Weekend/holiday timestamps for the Dan/Hugo cases matching logged facilities incidents and call-out records.
- Loop analysis showing the emergency loops involve **real field changes** and comments — i.e. genuine clarification, more scrutiny than the routine baseline.
- Emergency spend concentrated on pre-negotiated framework agreements with benchmarked pricing, and post-hoc price checks showing no premium beyond the expected urgency surcharge.
- Comparable expedite behaviour visible in peer organisations or in prior periods, indicating a stable designed channel rather than a recent drift.

**On data quality:**
- The `legacy_erp` mapping table demonstrably collapsing Bot_PO onto human resources, with the resolved-identity four-eyes count dropping sharply — quantifying how much of R1 was never a real violation.
- The `quarter_close_upload` spreadsheet showing **different named authors** and original dates within the 3-working-day window, with signed justification forms attached.
- `entered_by` ≠ `approved_by` on the 23 shared-account approvals, with SSO logs confirming two distinct humans.
- Confirmation that the Vendor Check + Change Vendor Bank Details combination is **permitted** by the segregation-of-duties matrix (converting 31 "violations" into a design observation).
- The V-77x maintenance-ticket linkage being recoverable from the master-data change log even though it is absent from the event log.

**A note on interpretation:** the absence of confirming evidence is not evidence of wrongdoing. Where documentation cannot be located, the correct finding is a **traceability and control-evidence gap**, which should be reported as such rather than escalated by default.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

**Observation.** Analysis of last quarter's procurement-to-payment log identified segregation-of-duties exceptions in a small share of cases: the purchase-order pair (42 cases, 1.8%), the vendor-bank-change/payment pair (9 cases, 0.4%), and emergency-override self-justification (17 cases, 0.7%). Several approval segments completed materially faster than the population median. These are control exceptions and process anomalies, not evidence of misconduct; no conclusion of fraud or collusion is drawn.

**Assessment.** Exceptions involving Buyer_Carla/Approver_Gita and Buyer_Dan/Manager_Hugo appear largely consistent with a designed expedite channel for emergency medical supplies and out-of-hours facilities repairs, supported by emergency-flag rates of 79–88% and documented specialist roles. High co-working frequency between routine buyer–approver pairs reflects team structure and is not treated as an indicator.

The subgroup warranting closest attention is the 44 cases covering vendor-master and payment activity on vendor family V-77x, of which 16 show a bank-detail change followed by payment approval within a median of 17 minutes against a 7.8-hour benchmark. These combine a segregation-of-duties exception on a high-consequence control path with high value (68%), vendor concentration (72%) and manual data entry (83%). Management indicates a legitimate banking-platform migration for this vendor family; the linkage between maintenance tickets and procurement cases is not recorded in the log, so that explanation could not be confirmed from system data alone.

**Limitations.** A shared approver account, a bot-to-human resource mapping error in the legacy ERP, and spreadsheet-migrated justification records carrying identical timestamps may create apparent exceptions where none occurred, and may equally mask real ones.

**Recommendation.** Verify the 16 V-77x cases through field-level before/after account details, vendor confirmation via independently sourced contacts, and payment-receipt confirmation. Resolve the mapping and shared-account defects and re-run the analysis. Confirm delegation-of-authority coverage for expedited approvals and reinforce timely, independent retrospective justification.
