# 1. Executive Verdict
The net is a workflow net (unique source/sink, all nodes on source-to-sink paths) but is **not sound**. The principal defect is **improper synchronization**: the waiver path (`p_waived`) bypasses the analysis completion check, allowing `t_authorize` to fire before `t_analyze` finishes. Business consequence: **cases may be authorized without mandatory analysis**, violating compliance. The net also risks deadlock if the standard review path is taken but the waiver path is not properly joined.

- **Repair C** is minimal but changes the business rule (analysis optional for waiver), which is explicitly flagged.

import pm4py

# Helper to fire a transition and print marking
def fire_and_print(net, marking, transition_name, label=None):
    enabled = pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, marking)
    if transition_name in enabled:
        new_marking = marking.copy()
        for arc in net.transitions[transition_name].in_arcs:
            new_marking[arc.source] -= 1
        for arc in net.transitions[transition_name].out_arcs:
            new_marking[arc.target] += 1
        print(f"Fired {transition_name} ({label}): {dict(new_marking)}")
        return new_marking
    else:
        print(f"Cannot fire {transition_name}: not enabled. Enabled: {enabled}")
        return None

# 7. Repair Trade-Offs and Residual Risks

- **Distinguishability of waiver/standard review**:
  - **Repair A/B**: Both paths remain distinguishable in traces (e.g., `t_waive_review` vs. `t_standard_review`).
  - **Repair C**: Waiver path skips analysis, which may be intentional but risks policy violation.

- **Silent transitions**:
  - `t_split`, `t_join_normal`, `t_join_waived` are silent. This is acceptable if the business process does not require logging these steps, but may reduce trace clarity.

- **Soundness  business alignment**:
  - Soundness ensures proper completion and no deadlocks/livelocks, but does not guarantee that the business rule (mandatory analysis) is enforced. **Repair C** is sound but violates the rule.

- **Regression testing**:
  - **Positive traces**:
    - `t_register  t_split  t_analyze  t_standard_review  t_join_normal  t_authorize`
    - `t_register  t_split  t_analyze  t_waive_review  t_join_waived  t_authorize`
  - **Negative traces**:
    - `t_register  t_split  t_waive_review  t_join_waived  t_authorize` (should fail in repaired net if analysis is incomplete).

- **Detecting analysis bypass**:
  - In the repaired net, check that `t_authorize` is never enabled unless `p_analysis_done` has a token. This can be verified by:
    1. Ensuring `join_waived` requires `p_analysis_done` (Repair B).
    2. Auditing traces for the absence of `t_analyze` before `t_authorize`.

# 8. Final Recommendation

**Deploy Repair B** (add `p_analysis_done  join_waived` arc) as the primary fix. It is the **minimal edit** (cost = 1) and fully preserves the business intent: analysis is mandatory regardless of review path. The waiver and standard review paths remain distinguishable, and soundness is achieved without introducing new risks.

**Fallback: Repair A** (rewire `p_waived` to `join_normal`). While slightly more invasive (cost = 2), it unifies synchronization logic, reducing complexity. However, it merges the two review paths at the join point, which may obscure trace variability. Use this if the organization prefers a single synchronization transition for maintainability.

Avoid **Repair C** unless the business explicitly decides to make analysis optional for waivers, as it violates the stated intent. Soundness alone is insufficient; the repaired net must be validated against the business rules via trace analysis.
