# 1. Cluster diagnoses

Preliminary note on method: "identical timestamp" is treated as a *logging observation*, not as evidence of simultaneous physical work. For each cluster I combine (a) the shape of start/complete equality, (b) whether service intervals are contiguous or overlapping, (c) source precision, (d) resource-identity reliability, and (e) the supplied contextual notes. Confidence is my subjective posterior after weighing these against the plausible alternatives.

# 2. Expected temporal signatures

The matrix below states what each *hypothesis* predicts for each *signature*, independent of the specific clusters. "Adjacency" = complete[i] equals start[i+1]. "Overlap" = one item's [start,complete] intersects another's. "Recording lag" = recorded_at − complete. "Behavior under smaller tolerance" = what happens to a naive batch/simultaneity claim if the equality tolerance is tightened toward the true source precision.

| hypothesis | start equality | end equality | adjacency | overlap | recording lag | source precision | resource-identity reliability | queue-arrival pattern | behavior under smaller time tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Genuine simultaneous batch | Equal | Equal | Not required | Full overlap by construction | Small, uniform | Fine precision still shows equality (true equality) | Single machine/worker, reliable | Arrivals may cluster before the run | Survives — equality persists even at sub-second tolerance |
| Start batching / common setup | Equal | Differ (fan out) | No | Partial (share the start instant only) | Varies per item | Equality at start survives fine precision if real | Reliable | Arrivals may pre-cluster | Start equality survives; ends stay distinct |
| End batching / bulk submission | Differ | Equal | No | Partial (share the end instant only) | One shared, larger lag; identical recorded_at | End equality is a submission artifact; fine precision would reveal it | Reliable | Arrivals spread; ends collapse | End equality collapses if true per-item completion is recovered |
| Sequential batching | Differ | Differ | Yes (chain) | None (disjoint intervals) | Small, per-item | Fine precision confirms distinct, contiguous stamps | Reliable | Arrivals often pre-cluster then drained serially | Survives — chain remains at tight tolerance |
| Concurrent work / multitasking | May differ | May differ | No | Yes (genuine overlap) | Per-item | Overlap real at fine precision | Reliable if truly one worker | Arrivals may interleave | Overlap survives; count of parallel items > 1 |
| Shared-account artifact | Any | Any | No | Yes (apparent) | Per-item | Precision irrelevant; the problem is the ID, not the clock | UNRELIABLE (many workers → one ID) | Arrivals independent | Overlap survives but is mis-attributed; splitting the account removes it |
| Timestamp coarsening | Equal (bucketed) | Equal (bucketed) | Spurious/forced | Spurious full overlap within a bucket | Recording lag often finer than lifecycle | Coarse precision; equality is a rounding artifact | Reliable | Arrivals finer than lifecycle stamps | Collapses — equalities dissolve into a ±precision interval; no crisp claim survives |
| Queue accumulation after capacity loss | Often equal (drain start) | May cluster | Possibly | Depends | Varies | Any | Reliable | Long arrival spread, then burst of service | Depends — arrival spread is the tell, not equality; survives on arrival evidence |

Key discriminators: (1) *adjacency without overlap* → sequential batching (G4, G8); (2) *start-only equality* → start batching (G2); (3) *end-only equality with shared recorded_at* → bulk submission (G3, G6); (4) *equalities that dissolve at fine tolerance but the source precision is coarse* → coarsening (G7); (5) *overlap under an unreliable ID* → shared-account vs. multitasking, undecidable from timestamps alone (G5); (6) *equality that survives fine precision on a machine that physically holds N items* → genuine simultaneity (G1).

# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

General mechanisms first, then per cluster.

- **Waiting time** is usually measured as start − queue_arrival. If `start` is a batch/setup marker (equal for many cases) or a bulk-entry instant, waiting is *systematically inflated* for early arrivals and *compressed/negative-risk* for late arrivals, and its variance is destroyed.
- **Service time** is complete − start. Adjacency/serial patterns give honest per-item service. Common-start or common-complete artifacts *shrink or zero out* service. Coarsening *quantizes* service to bucket multiples.
- **Utilization** = busy time / available time. Overlapping intervals under one resource ID inflate apparent utilization above 100% (impossible for one worker) — a red flag for shared accounts or coarsening.
- **Bottleneck detection** keys on high service time and/or long queues. Logging batches create *phantom* bottlenecks (huge apparent wait) or *phantom* fast stations (near-zero service), both misleading.

Per cluster:

- **G1**: If treated as 4 sequential items, service time is over-divided and utilization understated; correctly treated as one fixture cycle, per-item throughput is 4 items / 3 min. Waiting per item is fine (arrivals 08:54–08:58, start 09:00). Low bias risk if labeled as machine-level.
- **G2**: Common start 10:00 inflates waiting for the earliest arrival (09:42) and understates it for 09:54; service times (8–27 min) are probably *overstated at the front* because real per-item start ≥ 10:00. Bottleneck signal (long, growing service) may be partly a start-batching artifact.
- **G3**: Common complete 11:30 *compresses* service for late-started items (E04: 11:24→11:30 = 6 min looks fast) and *inflates* it for early ones (E01: 28 min). Throughput looks bursty (4 completes at once). Waiting is roughly honest (starts fan out). Do not trust per-case service ranking here.
- **G4**: Clean serial flow → service (~4 min) and waiting are trustworthy; utilization ≈ 100% during 12:05–12:25. This is the *reference-quality* cluster. Bottleneck detection valid.
- **G5**: Overlap under a shared pool inflates single-"resource" utilization above 1.0 and can invent a bottleneck. Service times per case (15–25 min) may be real, but *per-worker* utilization, throughput, and load are meaningless until the account is split. Waiting is roughly honest.
- **G6**: The archetypal double distortion. Because start=complete=17:00, **service ≈ 0** (station looks infinitely fast / never a bottleneck), while **waiting = 17:00 − queue_arrival** ranges from ~34 min to ~2h55m and is *entirely a data-entry artifact* (inflated waiting). Any SLA or bottleneck analysis using G6 is invalid; it will simultaneously hide the real processing cost and manufacture a huge queue.
- **G7**: Quarter-hour rounding quantizes every service to exactly 15 min and every start to 14:00, so service-time distribution collapses to a spike, variance vanishes, and waiting (start − arrival) is biased by up to ±15 min. Bottleneck ranking based on 15-min service is untrustworthy (it is the grid, not the work).
- **G8**: Serial handoff → service (~7 min) and utilization trustworthy, like G4. The apparent overlap with the safety check (15:00–15:35) would *falsely* suggest concurrency and inflate utilization if cross-system stamps were merged; keep clocks separate.

Why G6-like data creates *both* inflated waiting *and* near-zero service: waiting and service are measured against *different* reference points. Waiting anchors on the upstream `queue_arrival` (real, spread over hours), while service anchors on the collapsed local `start`/`complete` (a single 17:00 entry instant). The gap between a real upstream clock and a bulk local entry inflates waiting, while the equality of start and complete zeroes service — one artifact, two opposite-direction biases.

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

**Resource IDs trusted vs. treated as shared accounts.**
- If IDs are *trusted*: G5's overlapping intervals mean one worker (`analyst_pool`) ran up to three analyses at once → apparent utilization > 100 % and a "multitasking super-worker," which is physically implausible and should itself flag the ID as suspect. G1/G4/G8 are unaffected (dedicated machine/station).
- If IDs are *treated as shared*: G5 splits into up to three concurrent workers, utilization normalizes to ≤ 100 % per person, and the "overlap" becomes ordinary parallelism. But throughput/utilization per named resource become uncomputable. The safest conclusion: for `analyst_pool` do **not** report per-resource metrics; report only case-level service. G6's `T-F` and G2/G3's `T-B`/`T-C` are single, non-overlapping, so the trusted/shared distinction barely moves them.

**Timestamps exact vs. interval-censored by stated precision.**
- Exact: G1's 09:00/09:03 equality is *true* simultaneity; G7's 14:00/14:15 is a *true* simultaneous 15-min batch; G4/G8 adjacency is exact serial handoff.
- Interval-censored: each stamp becomes [t − p/2, t + p/2] (or the documented rounding window). Under censoring, G7's equalities *dissolve* — five items could each start anywhere in 13:52–14:07 and finish 14:07–14:22, so the "batch" may be an artifact of the 15-min grid and no simultaneity claim survives. G2/G3 (1-min) barely change (a few seconds of slack). G1 (1-sec) survives censoring, strengthening the genuine-batch reading. G4/G8 adjacency is robust because 1-sec censoring cannot manufacture minute-exact chains across five items by chance.

**At least three cases where one temporal pattern supports multiple physical explanations:**
1. **G1 (identical start+complete):** *genuine simultaneous fixture cycle* vs. *PLC writing one cycle-timestamp to all four items regardless of true micro-timing*. Same pattern, different physics; resolved only by knowing whether the PLC stamps per item or per cycle.
2. **G7 (all 14:00→14:15):** *true simultaneous batch of five measurements* vs. *five independent measurements coarsened by 15-min rounding*. The documented rounding rule makes both defensible; the raw stamps cannot decide.
3. **G5 (overlapping intervals, one ID):** *one analyst multitasking three cases* vs. *three analysts on a shared login*. Identical overlap signature; only badge-level identity (not retained) could separate them.
4. (Bonus) **G6 (start=complete=17:00):** *pure bulk data entry after real spread-out work* vs. *genuine end-of-shift crunch where closes really did cluster near 17:00*. The zero-service stamp is consistent with both; upstream arrival spread hints at entry, but does not prove absence of a real backlog.

# 6. Data-collection redesign

Prioritized (P1 = highest leverage):

- **P1 — Execution vs. recording separation.** Persist true per-item `start` and `complete` at the source, distinct from `recorded_at`. Forbid forms that write one submission time to all rows (root cause of G3/G6). Where a single submit is unavoidable, store both the submit time and a per-item execution time.
- **P1 — Resource/badge identity.** Replace shared logins (`analyst_pool`) with per-person badge/authentication captured on every event; retain it even when a shared workstation is used (fixes G5's un-attributable overlap).
- **P1 — Timestamp precision metadata + raw values.** Record the *raw* source timestamp and the documented precision/rounding rule as machine-readable fields (e.g., `precision_s`, `rounding=floor/ceil/nearest`). For legacy MES (G7), log the pre-rounding value if the device exposes it, or explicitly mark the field interval-censored.
- **P2 — Source clock governance.** Synchronize all sources to a common time reference (NTP) and log clock offset/skew per source, so cross-system overlaps (G8 vs. safety system) are reconcilable instead of guesswork.
- **P2 — Group/batch identifiers.** When a UI has a "begin group" action (G2) or a fixture holds N items (G1), emit an explicit `batch_id` / `fixture_id` and `batch_role` (start-group / end-group), so batching is declared rather than inferred from equal timestamps.
- **P2 — Workstation capacity metadata.** Store station concurrency capacity (RBT-7 = 4, single-server stations = 1). This lets analysts reject impossible readings (utilization > capacity) automatically.
- **P3 — Pause/resume (lifecycle) events.** Capture suspend/resume so multitasking and downtime are explicit; this disambiguates "concurrent work" from "one long interval" (G5) and reveals capacity-loss gaps behind queue accumulation.
- **P3 — Arrival provenance.** Keep the upstream `queue_arrival` clock identity so waiting time is computed against a trustworthy, comparable reference (critical for any G6-style reconstruction).

# 7. Final adjudication

**High-confidence, usable as-is:**
- **G4 (Pack)** and **G8 (Repair triage):** clean serial one-piece flow (minute-exact adjacency, no overlap, 1-sec precision). Waiting, service (~4 min / ~7 min), and utilization are trustworthy. (For G8, ignore the safety-system overlap as clock skew.)
- **G1 (Apply label):** high confidence as a *machine-level* genuine simultaneous batch (report per-fixture, not per-item). Only caveat is whether the PLC stamps per item vs. per cycle.

**Should be interval-censored or excluded from queue analysis:**
- **G7 (Manual measure):** interval-censor to ±15 min; do **not** use its 15-min service times or 14:00 starts for waiting/bottleneck analysis (they are the rounding grid).
- **G3 (Release review):** censor the common completion (11:30); per-case service times are not comparable. Waiting is usable.
- **G6 (Close ticket):** **exclude** start/complete from service analysis entirely (zero-service artifact) and treat waiting as unverified — it is inflated by bulk entry. Retain only for arrival-side backlog study after reconstruction.
- **G2 (Surface inspection):** treat the 10:00 start as a group-open marker; censor/adjust starts before computing waiting; completions are usable.

**Require operational validation before any performance conclusion:**
- **G5 (Root-cause analysis):** cannot separate multitasking from shared-account parallelism without badge identity; do not publish per-resource utilization/throughput until the login is de-shared or the concurrency is confirmed operationally.
- **G6 (again):** validate whether a real end-of-day backlog existed vs. pure data entry before making any queue/SLA claim.
- **G1 (edge):** confirm whether the PLC timestamps items or fixture cycles before treating item-level throughput as exact.

Bottom line: only G4 and G8 (and G1 at fixture granularity) meet the quality bar for direct performance analysis. G2/G3/G7 are salvageable with explicit interval censoring. G5 and G6 must be operationally validated, and G6's service field must never be used as-is — its identical timestamps are a logging batch, not simultaneous physical work.
