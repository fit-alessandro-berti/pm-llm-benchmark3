## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

### Facts
- **GPIP-4** is a board-approved global policy effective 2026-01-01 and applies to all Northbridge purchases.
- **SOP DE-AAC-PROC-17 Rev 6** is a controlled local procedure effective 2025-03-01 for Aachen maintenance and production-support purchases.
- **ECD-2** is an incident directive jointly signed by the COO and Incident Commander, effective only from **2026-07-14 09:00 CEST** to **2026-07-18 18:00 CEST**, and limited to “critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the current contamination event.”
- **CFO email D** is an ordinary email, not in the controlled policy repository, sent during the ECD-2 window.
- The ERP outage and document-system outage affect timestamp reliability but do not themselves create normative exceptions.
- The case facts include amounts, supplier status, purpose, recorded activity times, and some corroborating evidence.

### Assumptions
1. **GPIP-4 sets mandatory minimum integrity controls.**  
   In particular, segregation of duties, independent budget-owner/Finance Controller approval for purchases of at least EUR 50,000, and Compliance clearance for purchases above EUR 100,000 or from new suppliers are treated as integrity controls unless a valid CRO exception exists.

2. **ECD-2 is a temporary operational overlay, not a general emergency waiver.**  
   ECD-2 may relax ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding requirements for in-scope purchases within its window, but it does not automatically waive GPIP-4 integrity controls. The fact that GPIP-4 does not state whether a COO/Incident Commander directive counts as an A7 exception is treated as a defeater: absent CRO involvement, ECD-2 is not presumed to be a GPIP-4 exception.

3. **The CFO email is interpretive guidance, not a controlled exception.**  
   The CFO may interpret Finance controls, but the email is not treated as a CRO exception. It may clarify operational approvals, but it does not waive GPIP-4 segregation of duties, Compliance clearance, or the independent budget-owner requirement unless the source explicitly says so.

4. **“Before supplier commitment” means before a binding commitment is made**, not merely before ERP entry.  
   Where ERP timestamps are unreliable, stronger contemporaneous evidence such as supplier acknowledgments, call logs, signed PDFs, or recordings is used to determine the operative time.

5. **“New supplier” is treated as a supplier not in the approved-supplier register at the time of commitment.**  
   Therefore, an unapproved supplier triggers both the local approved-supplier rule and, where relevant, the GPIP-4 new-supplier Compliance rule.

6. **“Critical sterilization-line lots” is interpreted conservatively.**  
   A purchase is treated as in scope under ECD-2 if it is needed to contain the contamination event or restore the affected sterilization line within an urgent operational window, especially within 24 hours. Backup-line components are treated as in scope only if there is evidence they are needed within that urgent window. Where stock was expected to last three days, ECD-2 scope is doubtful.

7. **“Final business approver” includes the approval that authorizes commitment.**  
   If the requester or committer is one of the joint authorizers and no higher approval is required or obtained, segregation of duties is treated as breached.

8. **Burden of proof.**  
   Required pre-commitment approvals must be evidenced. Missing evidence of a required approval or exception is treated as inability to prove compliance, not as proof of compliance. Late approvals may mitigate severity but do not automatically cure a missing pre-commitment approval.

### Decision method
For each case, the assessment:
1. Determines the operative time of commitment using the best available evidence.
2. Determines whether the purchase was within ECD-2 scope and window.
3. Determines whether the purchase was an Aachen emergency under SOP B4.
4. Applies the rules in force at the time of the relevant act.
5. Checks whether a valid exception exists, especially a CRO exception under GPIP-4 A7.
6. Distinguishes satisfied rules, apparent deviations, failed exceptions, and evidential gaps.
7. Assigns a primary classification, severity, and confidence without using a single numerical score.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

### P-501 — Alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION
**Premise that must change:** SOP B4 must be read as allowing a Shift Lead to commit in an emergency and as permitting Plant_Approve and Finance_Approve to occur after commitment, even where the Plant Manager is reachable but has not formally delegated.  
**Why credible:** The purchase was for an affected sterilizer with predicted stoppage in four hours, and approvals were obtained later the same day.  
**Why weak:** B4 expressly addresses only PO timing and substitution of an absent Plant Manager by an actively delegated Shift Lead. B5 expressly preserves Finance_Approve in emergency. The Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate SHIFT-1. GPIP-4 A6 prevents later approval from automatically curing the missing pre-commitment control.

### P-502 — Alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION
**Premise that must change:** GPIP-4 A3 Compliance_Clearance for a new supplier must be treated as an ordinary supplier-onboarding requirement that ECD-2 C4 can override, and CFO D1 must be treated as satisfying GPIP-4 A2 Finance/budget requirements for purchases up to EUR 150,000.  
**Why credible:** ECD-2 C2 explicitly allows suppliers outside the approved register, C4 says ECD-2 takes precedence over supplier-onboarding requirements, and the CFO email says IC authorization is sufficient for operational approvals up to EUR 150,000.  
**Why weak:** GPIP-4 A4 says emergency waives competitive tendering only unless a CRO exception is issued. A3 is a board-level integrity control. D4 says the CFO email is not meant to remove essential integrity controls. No CRO exception exists.

### P-505 — Alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION
**Premise that must change:** CFO D1 must be treated as a valid exception to GPIP-4 A2 and SOP B2 Finance/Plant requirements for purchases up to EUR 150,000 during ECD-2, and the commitment time must be fixed at the supplier acknowledgment time of 17:59:31.  
**Why credible:** The CFO has authority to interpret Finance controls, the purchase was for the affected sterilizer, IC authorization occurred before commitment, and supplier acknowledgment shows commitment before ECD-2 expiry.  
**Why weak:** A2 requires both an independent budget owner and a Finance Controller. IC members are not expressly budget owners. A4 reserves formal exceptions to the CRO. D4 preserves essential integrity controls. No Plant_Approve is evidenced.

### P-507 — Alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION
**Premise that must change:** CFO D1 must be treated as a valid exception to GPIP-4 A2 Finance/budget requirements for purchases up to EUR 150,000 during ECD-2, and the signed PDF created at 12:40 must be treated as sufficient C3 recording despite ERP showing 14:30.  
**Why credible:** Compliance_Clearance occurred before commitment, IC authorization occurred before commitment, the PDF was created within four hours, and the document-system outage explains the late ERP entry.  
**Why weak:** A2 is a GPIP-4 control. The CFO email is not a CRO exception. IC members are not expressly independent budget owners. D4 limits removal of essential integrity controls.

### P-508 — Alternative: JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION
**Premise that must change:** D1 and D2 together must be read as a CFO-authorized exception for purchases above EUR 150,000, such that joint IC authorization plus Legal_Clearance satisfies all operational approvals, and IC members may be treated as independent budget owners for incident purchases.  
**Why credible:** Compliance_Clearance and Legal_Clearance occurred before commitment, SoD appears clean, the supplier was the only available source for a critical pump, and the amount exceeded EUR 150,000 with Legal clearance obtained.  
**Why weak:** A2 explicitly requires an independent budget owner and Finance Controller. IC members are not expressly budget owners. A4 reserves GPIP-4 exceptions to the CRO. D2 adds Legal clearance but does not expressly replace Finance/budget approval.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Proposed replacement hierarchy and exception clause**

1. **Supreme integrity controls.**  
   GPIP-4 controls on segregation of duties, independent budget-owner and Finance Controller approval for purchases of at least EUR 50,000, and Compliance_Clearance for purchases above EUR 100,000 or from new suppliers are mandatory minimums. Local procedures and incident directives may add stricter controls but may not weaken these controls.

2. **Exception authority.**  
   Only the Chief Risk Officer may issue a written, scoped, time-limited exception to GPIP-4. A COO/Incident Commander directive, CFO email, or local SOP does not create a GPIP-4 exception unless the CRO countersigns or later issues a written exception expressly covering the relevant scope and time.

3. **Incident directive scope.**  
   An incident directive may relax ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding steps only for purchases that are:
   - within the stated effective window;
   - expressly identified as critical to contain the incident or restore the affected line;
   - needed within 24 hours, or needed immediately where production stoppage is imminent.  
   “Critical sterilization-line lots” must be defined in the directive. Backup-line components are covered only if no suitable stock exists and the component is needed within the stated urgent window.

4. **Emergency procurement controls.**  
   During a valid incident directive:
   - two distinct Incident Command members who are not the requester or committer must jointly authorize the purchase before commitment;
   - verbal authorization must be recorded within four hours;
   - commitment may precede Create_PO;
   - an unapproved supplier may be used only if Compliance_Clearance is obtained before commitment, or a CRO exception expressly permits otherwise;
   - purchases of at least EUR 50,000 still require independent budget-owner and Finance Controller approval before commitment unless a CRO exception expressly permits otherwise;
   - purchases above EUR 100,000 or from new suppliers still require Compliance_Clearance before commitment unless a CRO exception expressly permits otherwise;
   - purchases above EUR 150,000 require Legal_Clearance before commitment.

5. **Segregation of duties.**  
   The requester, committer, or person who commits the company to a supplier may not be the final business approver of the same purchase. Incident Command authorization does not cure this requirement unless the CRO issues a written exception.

6. **Retrospective records and post-hoc approval.**  
   A system outage does not excuse a missing control. Where ERP or document-system timestamps are unreliable, contemporaneous evidence such as call logs, supplier acknowledgments, signed PDFs, or access logs may establish the operative time. Post-commitment approvals may mitigate consequences but do not automatically cure a missing pre-commitment approval. Retroactive CRO ratification is permitted only if expressly written, scoped, and time-limited.

7. **Emergency normalization safeguard.**  
   All emergency exceptions must be logged with scope, amount, supplier, authorizers, expiry, and CRO exception ID where applicable. Incident directives and exceptions expire automatically at the stated time. Verbal extensions are not valid. If the same control is waived more than twice in 30 days, or any emergency exception exceeds 24 hours, the CRO and Internal Audit must review the pattern before further reliance on the same exception.
