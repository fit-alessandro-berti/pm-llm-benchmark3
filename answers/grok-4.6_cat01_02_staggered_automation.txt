## 1. What is observed

The panel records branch-month availability, not case-level assignment. The automation flag turns from 0 to 1 in April 2026 for Alpha and Beta and in July 2026 for Gamma; Delta and Epsilon remain untreated through August. Availability does not imply use. `automated_fast_path_pct` is already 4–6 percent before go-live and then climbs gradually, reaching the high 40s in Alpha and Beta and 32 percent in Gamma by August. Intensity is therefore partial and delayed, and is better read from that share than from the binary flag.

Selection into rollout was targeted, not random. Alpha and Beta were chosen for high volume and historically poor cycle times. Gamma entered after a readiness pilot. Local managers could change work practices in the same months.

Several operational changes coincide with adoption. A centralized fraud desk opened in March for all branches; automated triage sent a larger share of borderline cases there, but desk waits were not recorded. A routing campaign added about 10 percent more arrivals to Alpha and Beta from April and about 5 percent more to Gamma from July. A May product change raised `high_complexity_pct` in several branches. Productive FTE also moved: Alpha lost two in May, Beta gained one in April, Gamma lost one in June, and Delta gained one in June.

Outcomes are aggregates. Median and p90 cycle times are branch-month summaries, not recoverable case-level distributions. Rework and escalation are monthly percentages without path traces. CSAT is a survey mean with response rates of about 20–45 percent and no branch-month response counts.

Interference and treatment-version notes. Routing reallocates arrivals toward treated branches, so control branches are not isolated and treated branches receive a different case pool. The shared fraud desk is a common resource; automated triage sends a larger fraction of borderline cases there, so one branch’s adoption can lengthen waits for others. Treatment is not a single version: availability, realized AFP share, local work-practice changes, staffing, and mix all differ by branch and month. `automation=1` is therefore a bundled, leaky intervention.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary: cohort event-study / staggered ATT (not naïve TWFE) | First month of availability; relative time \(e = t - G_b\); report cohort-time ATTs for April cohort and July cohort separately | Never-treated (Delta, Epsilon) as primary; Gamma as not-yet-treated for the April cohort only | `high_complexity_pct`, arrivals per FTE, FTE level; do not absorb the treatment with a single post dummy | E1, E3, E4, E5, E6, E7 | No anticipation; conditional parallel trends in calendar time; limited carryover from treated to untreated except via measured routing; treatment-effect heterogeneity not used to impute counterfactuals (avoid TWFE contamination) | Event-study leads Jan–Feb vs March; common March fraud-desk jump should appear in all arms; leave-one-control-out; compare April-only window (pre-product-change) with May–Jun contaminated window | Five clusters; targeted selection of the worst branches; concurrent routing and FTE moves not fully captured; SUTVA false via fraud desk |
| Alternative A: Gamma short-window DiD / ITS | July 1 switch; pre = May–Jun (already post-product-change, post-FTE loss); post = Jul–Aug | Delta and Epsilon over the same May–Aug window; Gamma’s own pre-period as ITS | Already balanced on complexity (27%) and FTE (23); optionally adjust arrivals | Late-adopter ATT for E3–E6 | No Gamma-specific July shock other than known +5% routing and the system itself; control summer recovery is the right counterfactual | Controls should show the same May–Jun deterioration and Jul–Aug easing without a July level jump in escalation of Gamma’s size | Only two post months; pilot selection; routing +5%; cannot speak to Alpha/Beta-type sites |
| Alternative B: intensity / dose model | Continuous `automated_fast_path_pct` (and its lag) rather than the binary flag | Within-branch over time, plus between-branch after month FE | Branch FE, month FE, complexity, arrivals/FTE | E2 | Conditional ignorability of realized use given FE and load/mix; no reverse causality from queues into use | Residual-vs-intensity; first-difference intensity vs first-difference outcomes; compare month-0 jump (availability) with later intensity slope | Use is endogenous to learning and congestion; month FE absorb common shocks but also much of the identifying calendar variation |

The primary design is preferable because assignment in these data is a staggered, branch-level availability switch, not a case-level dose and not a single interrupted series. Cohort-time ATTs respect that April adopters were the historically weak, high-volume sites, whereas Gamma is a later, piloted, lower-complexity site; pooling them into one TWFE post coefficient would mix different treatment versions and can use already-treated Alpha/Beta as controls for Gamma under dynamic effects. Never-treated Delta and Epsilon, plus Gamma before July, give the only credible calendar-time counterfactuals for the May complexity shock and the summer volume easing. Intensity models answer a scientifically important question—how much realized fast-path substitution matters—but they require a stronger selection-on-observables assumption than the data can support, and availability also changes escalation policy for cases that never take the fast path, so exclusion for an IV reading of E8 fails. Gamma’s July window is the cleanest single contrast, but it is the wrong population for a claim about expanding into the remaining weak or high-complexity branch. With five branches the primary design is an organizing frame for transparent cohort-time contrasts, not a precise estimator.

## 5. Quantitative evidence

Windows used below are transparent and pre-specified relative to known shocks. Early adopters: pre = Jan–Mar; first month = Apr (before the May product change and Alpha’s FTE loss); full post = Apr–Aug; mature = Jul–Aug. Gamma: pre-comparable = May–Jun (same complexity 27% and FTE 23 as post); post = Jul–Aug. Controls are scored on the matching calendar windows. Arithmetic facts are reported first; causal language is reserved for the interpretation sentences that follow each block.

### 5.1 Adopter-specific raw pre/post

| Alpha | Jul–Aug minus Jan–Mar | −3.30 | +6.93 | +1.30 | +3.13 | +0.067 |

| Beta | Apr only minus Jan–Mar | +1.40 | +2.67 | −2.40 | +2.13 | 0.000 |
| Beta | Jul–Aug minus Jan–Mar | +0.85 | +9.47 | −0.10 | +2.63 | 0.000 |

Fact: early-adopter medians barely move over the full post window; their p90s worsen by about 10 hours; first-month rework drops about 2pp then rebounds. Gamma’s two post months look much better on median, p90, rework, and CSAT, with a +1pp escalation rise. These are not causal effects.

### 5.2 Comparison with never-treated and not-yet-treated branches

Never-treated Apr–Aug minus Jan–Mar (average of Delta, Epsilon): median +5.22h, p90 +16.05h, rework +1.92pp, escalation +1.82pp, CSAT −0.085. Subtracting that common deterioration from the early-adopter raw changes gives descriptive DiD: Alpha median −5.62h, p90 −6.39h, rework −1.38pp, escalation +1.09pp, CSAT +0.106; Beta median −3.36h, p90 −6.24h, rework −2.48pp, escalation +0.71pp, CSAT +0.068.

Gamma versus never-treated on May–Jun → Jul–Aug (controls also ease as summer volume falls): DiD median −4.18h, p90 −7.65h, rework −2.10pp, escalation +0.93pp, CSAT +0.103.

Using Gamma as not-yet-treated for Apr–Jun versus Jan–Mar yields a similar early-cohort picture (Alpha median DiD −4.64h; Beta −3.64h). The March escalation jump is exactly +2.0pp in every branch, which is a common shock, not adoption. The next escalation jump occurs only in the adopting branch-month (April for Alpha/Beta, July for Gamma).

Interpretation, not arithmetic: relative to deteriorating or recovering controls, adopters look 3–6 hours better on the median and about 6–8 hours better on the p90. That is a descriptive DiD, not an ITT. Routing, FTE, mix, and the fraud desk remain in the residual.

### 5.3 Treatment intensity rather than the binary flag

AFP share is 4–6% when `automation=0` and rises monotonically after go-live (Alpha 25, 32, 39, 46, 48; Beta 27, 34, 41, 48, 50; Gamma 25, 32). First-month intensity (~25–27%) coincides with the rework drop and the +1pp escalation step. Later intensity (40–50%) coincides with Alpha/Beta p90 staying elevated (99–120h) while medians only improve once summer arrivals fall. Gamma never exceeds 32% AFP and shows the largest raw median and tail improvement. A simple reading “more AFP, better cycle time” is therefore false in the early-adopter series. Any intensity slope estimated with month FE will be identified mainly from within-branch ramps that are collinear with learning, load, and mix.

### 5.4 Pre-trends and regression to the mean

Jan–Mar medians are not exploding: Alpha 50.4, 46.9, 49.4; Beta 45.4, 45.9, 46.4. The early adopters are stably slower, not caught at a one-month spike. Parallel-trends in levels fail: Gamma, Delta, and Epsilon already sit 8–15 hours faster before anyone is treated, and March moves are uneven on medians (common +2pp on escalation). Regression to the mean is a real threat because Alpha and Beta were chosen for historically poor cycle times, but the three observed pre months do not show a pre-treatment crash that later mean-reverts. Any RTM would more likely operate over a longer unobserved history than over Jan–Mar.

### 5.5 Sensitivity / bounds for two outcomes

`median_cycle_h`. Across windows and controls, descriptive DiD clusters between about −1.5h (Beta April-only versus never-treated) and about −6.9h (Alpha mature versus never-treated Jul–Aug vs Jan–Mar). Raw own-pre/post is −0.4 to +1.9h for early adopters and −8.5h for Gamma’s short window. A bounded statement: if residual confounding from routing and FTE is no larger than the May common shock (controls +5 to +7h when complexity rises), a beneficial median ATT larger than about 7 hours is hard to defend, and a zero or small adverse effect is not ruled out for Beta’s typical case.

`p90_cycle_h`. Raw early-adopter change is +9.7 to +9.8h (harm). DiD versus never-treated is about −6.2 to −6.4h (relative benefit). Gamma raw −15.6h, DiD −7.7h. The sign flips with the counterfactual. Interval: from roughly +10h (own pre/post, early cohort) to −8h (DiD). Tail benefit is not identified; tail harm in the treated sites’ actual experience is a fact.

Do not collapse these into one “cycle-time effect.”

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**expand with an embedded evaluation**

Do not publish a single X% cycle-time claim, and do not flip Delta and Epsilon together. The next operational candidate is Epsilon, not Delta. Epsilon already has Alpha-like complexity (30–38%), the highest rework (16.2%), and a CSAT path that only partially recovered. It is the site where capacity relief, if real, would matter, and the site that tests whether anything learned from Alpha/Beta travels. Delta is faster, simpler, and already gained an FTE; it is the more valuable holdout for another quarter.

Assignment. Do not do another manager-selected branch switch. Inside Epsilon, randomize eligible cases (or rotating calendar days/teams) to automated versus manual triage for a minimum of eight weeks after a four-week frozen baseline. Keep Delta fully manual and do not retarget the routing campaign toward the newly treated site during the experiment. If legal or system constraints forbid case-level randomization, use a two-step week-level randomized start across Epsilon teams, still holding Delta out. Gamma may continue and be used only as a second treated cohort, not as a control.

Measurement. Keep the current branch-month KPIs but stop treating them as the causal file. Collect case-level event logs with timestamps for Register, Automated/Manual Triage, Specialist Review, Rework loops, fraud-desk handoff and return, Resolve, and Close. Record the risk class, the actual triage path, override reason, and productive minutes. On the fraud desk, record sending branch, wait, and handle time so spillovers are visible. For CSAT, store response counts and rates beside the mean; below 100 responses in a branch-month, do not interpret the mean.

Guardrails. Pause new enrollment if Epsilon’s weekly p90 exceeds its own four-week baseline by 10 hours for two consecutive weeks, if fraud-desk median wait rises more than 20% for any sending branch, or if CSAT with adequate n falls 0.15 points. Escalation rising by about 1pp is expected and is not by itself a stop rule.

Decision rule. Scale the same protocol to Delta only if (i) the randomized contrast shows a median improvement whose interval excludes zero after mix and load adjustment, (ii) p90 does not worsen, (iii) fraud-desk waits are stable, and (iv) CSAT with known n is non-inferior. If those fail, keep availability where it already exists, cap AFP growth in Alpha/Beta, and fix queueing before any further expansion. That path buys both operational caution and the first estimand the present panel cannot identify: the case-level effect of automated triage under a stable treatment version.

## 10. Final claim ladder

- `Observed:` Automation was switched on in the historically slow, high-volume branches first; realized automated-fast-path use rose only gradually; escalation stepped up by about one percentage point in the adopting branch-month; early-adopter p90 cycle times lengthened in raw terms while never-treated branches also deteriorated after the May complexity shock.
- `Suggestive:` Relative to never-treated and not-yet-treated branches, adopting sites’ medians look on the order of 3–6 hours better than the calendar-time path of controls, and Gamma’s first two months look better on median, tail, and rework; these contrasts remain bundled with routing, staffing, mix, and learning.
- `Conditionally causal:` If one is willing to treat the March fraud-desk jump as a common shock and the never-treated summer path as the counterfactual, turning availability on raises escalation by about 1pp and does not produce a first-order CSAT movement in the observed means; even this is an effect of a bundled branch policy, not of automating a case.
- `Not supported:` Automation reduces cycle time by X%, or improves tails, or improves customer satisfaction, in a way that justifies immediate uninstrumented expansion to Delta and Epsilon.
