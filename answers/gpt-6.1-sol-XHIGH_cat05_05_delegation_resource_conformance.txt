## 2. Rule Interpretation

Let H(e) be the attributed human, which may be unknown, and t(e) the UTC event timestamp. Let D(c), A(c), I(c), and V(c) be the sets of draft, approval, implementation, and verification events for case c.

- **RR1:** For every d in D(c) and a in A(c), H(d) must differ from H(a). Compare all relevant events, not just the first occurrence. A known overlap establishes a violation. An unknown relevant identity prevents a conformance conclusion for that pair; missing activities require a completeness assessment rather than an automatic pass.
- **RR2:** For every i in I(c) and v in V(c), H(i) must differ from H(v), using the same treatment of aliases, multiple events, and unknown identities.
- **RR3:** An ordinary HIGH-risk approval requires an event-time QM or a valid QM delegate. A valid grant requires an authentic grant established before use, coverage of t(e), the correct activity and change scope, satisfaction of any principal-absence/unavailability condition, an authorized principal, and a permitted delegation chain. Any known failed condition invalidates that grant; an unresolved necessary condition leaves authority unproved. LOW risk removes RR3's QM requirement, not RR1 or RR2.
- **RR4:** A credible HIGH-risk emergency can use IC or valid IC-delegate approval before implementation instead of initial QM approval. The exception also requires an authorized QM retrospective review within two business days. RR1 and RR2 still apply. A timely review cannot repair an unauthorized IC approval or prohibited subdelegation; a late or unauthorized review cannot complete the exception. An emergency flag alone does not establish a credible emergency or safety rationale.
- **RR5:** Apply RR1–RR4 to humans. A service-account event passes only through reliable individual attribution and the resulting human's authority. An unresolved account is neither a distinct independent person nor proof that a suspected person acted.
- **RR6:** Collaboration patterns generate investigative hypotheses. They cannot establish collusion or negate otherwise valid authority without corroborating evidence.

**Business-day convention.** I anchor retrospective review to implementation, exclude the implementation date, and count the next two Monday–Friday site business dates, with no holidays in this period. I use the end of the second business date in UTC as the deadline. Thus both Tuesday, August 11 implementations have a Thursday, August 13 deadline. CHG-505's Friday, August 14 review is late; CHG-510's Wednesday, August 12 review is timely. A policy preserving the implementation clock time would give August 13 at 13:10 and 14:30 respectively and yields the same findings here. Anchoring to approval instead also leaves these two timing findings unchanged. Record the site's chosen convention explicitly, especially for implementations outside business hours or on weekends.

**Principal working during leave.** Leave does not revoke Asha's QM role. If she personally approved CHG-504, her approval is authorized. Her account's 09:15–09:40 VPN session may indicate temporary availability or credential sharing. Determine whether the delegation's absence condition was suspended during that interval using the actual policy and human evidence. Do not infer that one urgent intervention cancels D-QM-01 for the entire leave period. No supplied Ben approval occurs during that VPN window. At 13:00, the leave record supports resumed absence, although a formal availability record would be stronger. Ben's own sick leave likewise does not automatically revoke his delegation; it undermines the account-to-human attribution in CHG-506.

**Later database registration.** A later entry alone cannot prove that a grant existed earlier. Here D-QM-01's signed August 9 PDF is independent contemporaneous evidence of a grant effective August 10. Its August 12 migration registration need not make CHG-501 unauthorized. Validate the signature, provenance, document version, and migration trail, and distinguish effective time from recording time. A newly created, backdated grant without such prior evidence would not authorize the earlier action.

The stated rules do not require every verifier to hold the job title Independent Verifier, prohibit every approver from verifying, or require pair rotation. Those may be useful additional controls, but should not be invented as existing RR violations. Eva's switch to Quality Engineer does not by itself establish that she lost verification competence. Grace's Site Director role neither grants QM authority nor proves she is unqualified to verify.

D-QM-01 explicitly grants production-equipment **approval**, so it is not assumed to authorize cooling-system retrospective reviews. An SOP explicitly defining cooling equipment within its scope and granting retrospective review could change the review-authority assessment; no such evidence is supplied. Likewise, if the site defines an off-duty weekend as satisfying D-QM-02's absence condition, retain that policy and the applicable availability evidence. The calendar alone does not settle the condition.

def delegation_result(grant, event):

    timestamp = event[TIME]
    if not (grant["start"] <= timestamp <= grant["end"]):
        failures.append("outside effective interval")

    if event[ACT] not in grant["activities"]:
        failures.append("activity not granted")

    # Supplied valid grants are direct from a standing principal.
    # Farid's conditional authority cannot become a direct onward IC grant.
    if grant["role"] not in active_roles(grant["principal"], timestamp):
        failures.append("no standing principal authority; invalid onward path")

        if absent == "UNKNOWN":
            unknowns.append("principal absence unproved")
        elif absent != "ABSENT":
            failures.append("principal available")
    if pd.notna(grant["incident"]):

            unknowns.append("CI-44 case linkage unproved")

    if pd.isna(grant["prior_evidence"]):
        unknowns.append("prior authentic grant evidence unproved")
    elif not grant["prior_evidence"]:
        failures.append("no prior grant evidence")
    if pd.notna(grant["signed_at"]) and grant["signed_at"] > timestamp:
        failures.append("signature postdates event")
    if pd.notna(grant["recorded_at"]) and grant["recorded_at"] > timestamp:
        notes.append("later DB entry; earlier signed artifact supports effective time")
    status = "INVALID" if failures else "UNKNOWN" if unknowns else "VALID"

def authority(event, target_role, human_override=None):
    human = event["human"] if human_override is None else human_override
    if pd.isna(human):
        return "UNKNOWN", "actual human unresolved"
    if target_role in active_roles(human, event[TIME]):
        return "VALID", "standing " + target_role
    candidates = delegations.loc[
        (delegations["delegate"] == human) & (delegations["role"] == target_role)
    ]
    outcomes = [(g["id"], *delegation_result(g, event))
                for _, g in candidates.iterrows()]
    if not outcomes:
        return "INVALID", "no standing role or supplied matching grant"
    statuses = {item[1] for item in outcomes}
    status = "VALID" if "VALID" in statuses else "UNKNOWN" if "UNKNOWN" in statuses else "INVALID"
    detail = " | ".join(f"{gid}:{state}:{why}" for gid, state, why in outcomes)
    return status, detail

## 6. Control Improvements

1. **Delegation creation and scope:** Require controlled grants approved before use, with principal/delegate person IDs, granted activities, explicit equipment/document/incident scope, effective start/end, absence conditions, revocation rules and permitted chain depth. Deny prohibited subdelegation automatically. Do not treat generic QM delegation as every QM function; explicitly grant or deny retrospective review and record equipment-scope hierarchies.
2. **Effective-time recording:** Keep effective time, signature time, database recording time, migration/import time and revocation time separately. Preserve historical role and delegation versions. Reconcile signed source grants during migration; route conflicting or genuinely retroactive grants to review rather than silently backdating authority.
3. **Principal availability:** Maintain timestamped availability and temporary-return records with a clear policy for when conditional delegation pauses/resumes. Integrate leave and incident-duty information while preserving standing role authority. Treat sick-leave account activity as an identity alert rather than conclusive misconduct or automatic role revocation.
4. **Alias management and SoD enforcement:** Use immutable human IDs with controlled alias histories. Enforce RR1 and RR2 across aliases and all relevant events. Block or escalate unresolved human attribution instead of treating a shared-account string as an independent verifier.
5. **Individual authentication:** Require action-bound individual authentication/signatures for approval, implementation and verification, including on shared terminals. Separate service automation from human decision-making, prohibit shared human credentials, lock unattended sessions and preserve token-to-action links. Establish who is authorized and qualified to verify under the applicable SOP.
6. **Emergency review:** Require the incident ID, credible safety rationale, explicit IC route and approved-before-implementation evidence. Compute deadlines from the controlled site calendar, assign a QM reviewer with adequate scope, and alert/escalate before the deadline. Prevent a timely but unauthorized review from completing the control; preserve failures and corrective actions rather than treating later review as retroactive authorization.
7. **Pair rotation and independence:** Consider risk-based rotation or independent sampling where coverage permits, with documented staffing exceptions. Do not retrospectively invent a pair-rotation violation. Consider additional approval/verification independence for HIGH-risk cases such as CHG-509, and assess whether reviewers challenge changes substantively.
8. **Network monitoring:** Monitor time-windowed, alias-normalized handovers and shared-case patterns against staffing, eligibility, workload and case mix. Distinguish unresolved nodes and provisional edges. Use concentrated pairs and reciprocity to select audits, then test review substance and outcomes; never issue automatic collusion findings from graph weights.
9. **Evidential retention:** Retain signed grant artifacts, document/version histories, migrations, event-time role records, site calendars, incident telemetry, approval/review artifacts, VPN/device/workstation logs, badge records and action-bound FIDO evidence with synchronized clocks and tamper-evident audit trails. Preserve relevant records promptly for CHG-503, CHG-504, CHG-506 and both emergency cases.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

This is a mixed control failure, not evidence of proven collusion. Five of ten cases have established rule failures: CHG-502 and CHG-508 self-approval, CHG-507 missing HIGH-risk authority, CHG-505 a late emergency review, and CHG-510 prohibited subdelegation plus self-approval. On the supplied scope, both emergency reviews also lack established QM authority. CHG-501 is a valid substitution obscured by registration timing; CHG-504 is provisionally authorized. CHG-503 and CHG-506 principally expose identity failures, while CHG-509 remains conditional on unproved principal absence. These categories overlap: resolving an identity gap could uncover further authority or SoD failures, and CHG-508 combines alias quality with a real separation failure.

Recurring pairs and reciprocal approvals justify targeted scrutiny, but same-cell assignments, workload and legitimate substitution remain plausible explanations. The most serious systemic weaknesses are inadequate individual attribution, conditional authority that is not checked at event time, and emergency review controls that fail to enforce both deadline and reviewer authority.
