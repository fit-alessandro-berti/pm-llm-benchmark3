## 2. Counterfactual continuations

Approval is a plausible modal outcome after a completed conformant review, but the probability of **approval plus timely execution** is not supplied. The 0.46 fuzzy-match confidence is neither an approval probability nor proof that the match is false.

1. **D-first / S4 review**
   - **Now:** start alternate screening; preserve the evidence package; confirm S4’s actual start and completion ETA; complete R17’s required post-evidence review.

## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

- **Mandatory controls are constraints, not negative weights.** A favorable forecast cannot offset a missing second review. The apparent €20,000–€35,000 deadline benefit of A or B is therefore not an admissible benefit that can be traded against regulatory exposure.

- **The business upside is conditional on both approval and timely execution.** A review that finishes after the deadline, rejects the payment, or results in a report does not deliver the stated €20,000–€35,000 deadline value. Conversely, a substantiated rejection avoids unlawful-execution exposure but can create the stated €15,000–€80,000 false-rejection harm.

- **The regulatory range is asymmetric and partly non-monetary.** An unlawful execution could plausibly cause €100,000–€500,000-plus loss, as well as licensing, enforcement, reputation, and customer-trust consequences. Those harms should not be treated as merely another line item that might be outweighed by a 10–15 minute speed advantage.

- **The prediction ranges do not establish intervention effects.** The 0.48–0.64 breach probability applies to “continue normal processing,” not automatically to D or C. The 0.55–0.75 approval prediction is observational and may not transport to a rescreened, resource-constrained intervention. No defensible post-intervention deadline probability is available for C or D.

- **D has operational advantages over simply starting E:** it can obtain information in 15–30 minutes without imposing a 6–24 hour customer delay, while preserving the option of E if the result identifies a need for more evidence. This is not strict dominance: the API is scarce, and screening may not resolve the match.

- **C is faster in its stated central scenario but creates a real spillover.** Its advantage over D is strongest when S4 will be late and S2 can genuinely finish sooner. Its disadvantage is the P-901 interruption, whose probability impact cannot be converted into monetary harm without P-901’s loss-at-risk and alternative-capacity information.

- **The supplied harm ranges may overlap.** For example, a late approval may combine deadline loss with customer dissatisfaction. Adding €20,000–€35,000 and €15,000–€80,000 mechanically would risk double counting.

- **Neither the 12-minute-old score nor the fuzzy match supports an automatic clearance.** Until a valid update is available, the 72 score remains relevant to the second-review requirement. The 0.46 match confidence should inform evidence assessment, not be converted directly into an execution or rejection probability.

## 4. Conformance constraints

**Common requirements:** Payment Registration and initial Sanctions Screening are complete, and R17 has performed the first review. Because the evidence cycle currently ends at Evidence Validated, R17 must complete the required return-to-Analyst Review before the independent decision. That is not permission for R17 to act as the second officer.

- **Action A — Rule 6:** the current score of at least 70 and unresolved match require Second Officer Review before approval or execution. **Rule 7:** R17 cannot substitute for the different second officer. **Rule 9:** there is no valid approval path. **Rule 10:** no emergency override is enabled. A numerical forecast cannot cure these missing prerequisites.

- **Action B — Rule 7:** self-certification directly violates four-eyes. It also cannot create the required independent review under Rule 6 or a valid approval path under Rule 9. Its shorter duration is operationally irrelevant to admissibility.

- **Action C — Rules 6, 7, 8, and 9:** S2 must be a different person from R17, independently assess the evidence, and reach and record a permitted disposition. Execution requires an actual valid approval. C is an ordinary review path, not an emergency override, so Rule 10 need not be invoked.

- **Action D — Rules 6, 7, and 9:** screening may improve evidence, but on the stated facts it does not remove the requirement for S4/S2 review. A screening result must not be treated as an approval or as permission to bypass four-eyes. If authoritative new evidence genuinely changes the risk and match conditions, the case should be reranked under those updated facts.

- **Action E — Rule 5:** a reasoned request may repeat the evidence cycle, followed by validation and Analyst Review. It does not suspend Rules 6–9 indefinitely: if the high score or unresolved match remains, the independent review is still required. A request should be specific rather than used indefinitely to avoid a decision.

- **Action F — Rule 8 and the documented evidentiary threshold:** rejection requires a substantiated, applicable basis and valid decision authority. Rule 6 expressly requires the second review before approval/execution; it should not be overextended into a blanket claim that every rejection requires that review. Nevertheless, no separate pre-review rejection exception is supplied, and the prompt does not establish F’s evidence threshold. F is therefore conditional, not confirmed valid. Required evidence would include the match source and corroboration, the applicable policy threshold, why the evidence meets it, and confirmation of authority to issue rejection at this stage.

- **Action G — Rule 10:** both a genuine incident identifier and recorded Compliance Director authorization are required. Neither exists. A missed SLA must not be relabeled retrospectively as an incident. If later obtained, the authorization’s scope must be checked; it should not automatically be assumed to waive four-eyes or authorize an otherwise unsupported payment. G remains unavailable until both conditions are actually satisfied.

- **All actions — Rule 11:** if an SAR is filed, its existence and contents must not be disclosed to the customer. Neither filing a report nor receiving evidence automatically authorizes execution.

## 5. Resource spillovers

**S2 versus S4**

- **S2 now:** C provides the clearest immediate route to independent review, but the 45-minute interruption raises P-901’s breach probability by an estimated **0.12–0.28**. These are probability-point changes, not € losses and not certainty that P-901 will breach. P-901 has its own 70-minute deadline, so its loss-at-risk and possible reassignment cost matter even though they are not supplied.
- **S4:** waiting avoids direct P-901 disruption. S4 should be asked for a firm review-start and completion ETA, not merely an availability forecast. Reserving S4 is preferable to interrupting P-901 speculatively.

**Effect of waiting for S4**

- If S4 becomes available at **35 minutes**, the optimistic record/execution lower bound is approximately **40–50 minutes**, assuming prompt review and using C’s 5–15 minute recording allowance. D-first becomes clearly stronger relative to C.
- If S4 becomes available at **65 minutes**, that lower bound becomes **70–80 minutes**, before accounting for any additional review or queue time. D-first remains primary on the supplied facts because it is resource-sparing and still nominally fits, but its timing confidence falls and C becomes a more serious alternative if S2 could start now and finish materially earlier.
- If S4 has not produced a credible start by about **65 minutes**, one should not assume that starting S2 then still allows a 40–90 minute completion before the customer deadline: that could place completion around **105–155 minutes**. A confirmed updated service estimate could change this, but waiting does not preserve C’s original 40–90 minute window.
- If S4’s actual completion ETA plus recording/execution time exceeds the case deadline, C may overtake D-first **only if S2 offers a credible earlier completion and the P-901 tradeoff is accepted**.

**Alternate-screening quota**

D consumes **one of three** expedited calls, leaving two. Its value is faster information without a 6–24 hour customer-evidence delay, but the remaining quota has option value for other cases. Repeated screening should not be used as a substitute for review, and the ranking should be revisited if a call is needed elsewhere.

**Waiting for an exception**

G may consume 20–60 minutes or longer without producing a valid incident or authorization. Director reachability is not authorization, and an incident identifier is not a permission slip. Time spent pursuing G should be bounded; it should not silently consume S2 capacity or crowd out D/C.

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence

**Primary recommendation:** Execute the **D-first composite** now: start one alternate-screening call, preserve the validated evidence package, ensure R17 completes the required post-evidence review, and reserve S4 for an independent decision. Do not approve or execute on the screening result. A valid approval must still be recorded before execution, and any SAR-based hold remains non-executable and confidential.

**Fallback:** Use **C** if S4 is unavailable or its credible completion ETA threatens the deadline and S2, starting now, offers a materially better feasible completion. Make the P-901 interruption an explicit operational tradeoff. If neither independent-review route is feasible, preserve the non-executable case and use F only if its rejection basis and authority are verified, or E if specific additional evidence is genuinely warranted.

**Confidence:** **Moderate overall.** Confidence is high that A and B are inadmissible and that an independent-review plan is required. Confidence is moderate regarding S4/S2 completion within the deadline, the post-screening approval probability, and the resource impact of interruption.

**Next event that should trigger immediate reranking:** obtain a **firm S4 review-start and completion ETA**. If no credible start is available by roughly 65 minutes, or S4’s projected completion plus recording time exceeds the deadline, immediately compare C against the verified rejection/hold alternatives—without reflexively interrupting P-901 or using A/B.
