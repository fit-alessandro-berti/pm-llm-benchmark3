## 1. Executive causal assessment

Provisional verdict: **not identifiable from the supplied evidence**. Confidence: **90/100 that the effect of assigning an otherwise comparable case to Orion, rather than Nova, is unidentified here**; this is not a probability that Orion is slower. Orion's crude mean is 21.96 hours versus Nova's 15.24, but Orion receives far more high-complexity, night, and compliance-required work. In every recorded complexity stratum, Orion's mean is lower. Standardizing the three observed stratum means to the pooled complexity mix reverses the crude difference to approximately 2.51 hours *in Orion's favor*. This is strong evidence against management's interpretation of the crude mean.

The strongest challenge to an entirely noncausal account is the consistency of Orion's shorter within-complexity waits, with slightly shorter review service too. Certified reviewers or better triage could genuinely help. Yet complexity is coarse, Orion has only two low-complexity cases and one day case, and the high-complexity Nova comparison has only two cases. Shift, load, unrecorded risk, reviewer skill, and completion selection remain unresolved. Neither complexity stratification nor observed waiting times identify a group effect. The total effect would include changed queues, reviewer mix, and rework, including spillovers to other cases. A controlled direct effect would hold those mediators fixed; indirect effects would run through them. Neither decomposition is identified, and fixing queue time may be operationally artificial.

Moving half of Orion's future work could help if Nova has unused, qualified capacity and Orion-specific handoffs are slow. It could fail if the observed gap is mostly routing mix. It could backfire by overloading Nova, weakening high-complexity or compliance coverage, increasing rework or appeals, and changing queues for both groups. A bulk transfer therefore has neither an identified benefit nor a stable effect implied by these 30 completed cases.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Orion is genuinely beneficial, masked by difficult-case routing | Certified staff and triage shorten waiting or service for comparable cases | All three complexity strata favor Orion; within-stratum waits are lower | Tiny cells, night concentration, and unmeasured severity permit selection explanations | Log pre-routing risk, eligibility, and queue state; compare exact/propensity-matched cases in common-support shift-load-route cells, then run a guarded randomized pilot. A null or reversed randomized effect weakens this hypothesis | Comparable Orion assignments retain shorter cycles, especially before review, with no quality loss |
| 2 | Shift and load, rather than group practice, drive most apparent delay | Night staffing and high-load queues determine available capacity | Orion has 13/14 night cases; night and high-load means exceed day and normal-load means | Orion is faster within each complexity stratum despite its night-heavy mix; night-only contrasts are very small | Reconstruct queue occupancy and certified capacity at each arrival; compare within complexity, shift, and queue-state bands or randomized shift blocks. Persistent group gaps at matched queue states weaken a pure conditions account | Group contrast shrinks toward zero when true pre-assignment congestion and staffing are balanced |
| 3 | Compliance handling or rework mediates a group effect | Waiting around mandatory checks, information requests, and repeated review changes cycle time | Orion has more required checks and a higher crude rework fraction; wait is large | Requirements are policy-driven; within High rework fractions are equal, and Orion's within-complexity waits are shorter | Capture check start/end, queue entry/release, request, response, and review intervals; compare compliant trace variants and estimate effects in a randomized routing subset without adjusting away mediators for the total effect. No group difference in stage delays or rework weakens mediation | A group difference localizes to check-adjacent wait or request/re-review frequency and attenuates a separately estimated direct contrast |
| 4 | Orion itself has a harmful workflow effect | Extra handoffs or slower internal practices add time for an identical eligible case, perhaps hidden by favorable within-stratum priority or selection | Its crude cycle and service means are higher; a routing label can represent different work practices | Every recorded complexity stratum favors Orion and its observed waits are shorter | Randomize a small eligible pool, compare baseline risk and queue states, and log reviewer handoffs; inspect routes only as possible mechanisms, since route conditioning biases total effects. Pre-routing time is a negative control. Faster Orion under randomization weakens the harmful-effect claim | Randomly assigned Orion cases have longer stage-specific handoffs or cycle time at comparable load |
| 5 | Completed-case selection or measurement creates the reversal | Slow or abandoned assignments are absent; aggregated waits merge stages and appeals miss quality defects | Only completed cases appear; wait is coarse; quality has two appeals | No direct evidence that missingness differs by group | Build an inception cohort of every assignment with open/abandoned status; estimate time-to-completion with censoring and compare stage-timestamp audit records. ## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Baseline instrumentation | Log every routed case, eligibility, routing inputs, actual reviewer, queue state, and real activity start/end times | Resolve case mix, censoring, and stage ambiguity | All new assignments | Enforce required checks and reviewer certification; monitor open cases | Timestamped variants, directly-follows counts, queue entry/exit, compliance/check time, review service, rework, pending cases, 30-day outcomes | Establish support and pre-intervention trends; diagnose whether Orion and Nova can fairly be compared | Pause analysis-driven reassignment if routing or event coverage is materially incomplete |
| 1. Capacity trial | Add a certified night review or compliance slot, or stagger the check-to-review handoff, using prespecified comparable shift blocks | Night queue and compliance-adjacent wait, without relabeling groups | Night-shift blocks | Maintain certification and required checks; measure displaced work and both groups' queues | Queue length and age, check-to-review lag, p50/p90 cycle time, rework, completion and appeals | A shift-block contrast tests whether added capacity reduces the suspected bottleneck | Roll back for any missed mandatory check or sustained p90 deterioration beyond a prespecified operational limit |
| 2. Limited routing pilot | Randomize 5–10% of newly arriving, mutually eligible cases 1:1 between groups within complexity, shift, requirement, and load blocks; otherwise use a documented quasi-random routing threshold | Total effect of assignment at feasible capacity | Eligible case, with shift-block analysis for spillovers | Cap Nova's extra workload; reserve certified coverage and high-risk escalation; track all randomized cases, including open ones | Actual route and handoffs, queue and service intervals, conformance, rework, completion survival, delayed quality | Intent-to-treat estimate within overlap, with explicit interference checks on nonpilot cases | Stop for policy/certification breach, excess overdue cases or queue spillover, or a prespecified adverse cycle/quality signal |
| 3. Controlled scale decision | Expand only a demonstrated beneficial policy in capacity-balanced steps; otherwise retain routing and address the measured bottleneck | Sustainable throughput and quality | Shift blocks | Continue risk-adjusted monitoring and an independent quality review | Both groups' queue tails, case-mix drift, route variants, incomplete cases, 30-day quality | Tests transport of the pilot effect as case volume changes | Revert the affected step if cycle tails, incomplete-case share, compliance, or quality cross prespecified bounds |

**Final recommendation.** Do not approve the proposed 50% bulk reassignment on the basis of Orion's higher crude mean. The observed reversal after complexity stratification makes the asserted benefit especially fragile, while sparse day and high-complexity overlap prevents a reliable counterfactual estimate. First capture all incoming cases and real stage boundaries, then test whether certified night or compliance capacity reduces the measured wait without changing routing. In parallel, create a small randomized routing comparison only where both groups can safely and legally process the same case, with capacity caps and case-level follow-up. Estimate intention-to-treat completion-time distributions from all assignments, accounting for censoring, and watch both groups' queues for spillovers. Require noninferior compliance and independently checked quality before scaling. If the pilot shows a stable benefit, expand in steps and remeasure; if it does not, improve the identified queue or handoff rather than moving work by label. The current data justify investigation and a bounded trial, not the claimed 50% improvement policy.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Residual case severity:** Obtain a pre-routing, case-level risk score that records difficulty within each complexity category.
2. **Who could be assigned where:** Log each case's pre-routing probability of Orion assignment under the active eligibility and capacity rule.
3. **Where time is spent:** Collect an event-level lifecycle log with real queue, compliance, request, and review boundary timestamps.
4. **Missing long cases:** Create an inception cohort of every routed case, including open and abandoned cases with status at extraction.
5. **Speed versus correctness:** Add an independent, blinded review-quality assessment for a sample of completed cases.
