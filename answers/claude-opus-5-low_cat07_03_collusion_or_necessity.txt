# 1. Risk indicators

Overall reading up front: the evidence is **most consistent with a mixture** — dominated by (a) informal specialization plus genuine emergency handling in the Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo channels, and (b) data-quality and system-mapping artefacts in the retrospective-justification and Bot_PO populations — with **one residual cluster (VendorMaster_Lena → AP_Kim, vendor family V-77x) that is not explained by either and warrants substantive testing**. Nothing here evidences misconduct; all items below are *control exceptions and risk indicators*, not findings of wrongdoing.

# 2. Benign alternative explanations

**A. Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo: four-eyes violations plus 8–10 minute approvals on emergency cases**

1. *Genuine emergency handling.* Carla is documented as specialising in emergency medical supplies and Gita as the delegated approver for urgent cases; Dan handles facilities repairs on weekends with Hugo as the exception manager. 88%/79% emergency_flag and low vendor concentration fit this precisely. Emergency channels are *designed* to be fast and to have thin staffing, which mechanically produces both repeated pairs and short intervals.
   - *Would support:* emergency_flag set before the override timestamp, not after; independent operational corroboration (ward stock-out tickets, facilities incident tickets, out-of-hours call logs) time-stamped before Create Purchase Request; amounts consistent with the operational need; a documented on-call roster naming Gita/Hugo for those windows.
   - *Would refute:* emergency_flag applied retroactively or after PO creation; emergencies clustering at month/quarter end rather than following incident patterns; the same vendor recurring across "emergencies"; emergency purchases of non-urgent commodity categories.
2. *Informal specialization / structural pairing.* With only one delegated urgent approver and one exception manager, the pool of possible counterparties is tiny. High co-case counts are then arithmetic, not choice. The subcontracting loop appears at nearly the same rate in the concededly routine Aline/Elena and Boris/Felix pairs, which is direct in-log evidence that A→B→A is normal here.
   - *Support:* compute the expected co-occurrence under a random-assignment null given each resource's marginal volumes and shift/region availability; if observed ≈ expected, the pairing carries no information.
   - *Refute:* Carla/Gita pairing persists on cases where other approvers were demonstrably available and eligible, or Gita is bypassed for other buyers' emergencies of equal urgency.
3. *Identity artefact.* Part of the 42 same-person Create+Approve cases may be Bot_PO-created POs collapsed onto the human approver by the legacy_erp mapping error — in which case no human ever violated four-eyes.
   - *Support:* the violating cases are disproportionately source_system="legacy_erp" and have distinct `entered_by` vs `approved_by`.
   - *Refute:* violations persist in non-legacy systems with genuinely identical `entered_by` and `approved_by`.

**B. Same-person Emergency Override + Retrospective Justification (17 cases)**

1. *Migration artefact.* 12 of 17 carry an identical minute-level timestamp under quarter_close_upload — a hallmark of bulk spreadsheet ingestion, where the loader's account and load time overwrite the true actor and time.
   - *Support:* the loader identity appears in a technical field; the spreadsheet source file exists with per-row author/date; the same timestamp signature appears on unrelated activities in the same load.
   - *Refute:* the spreadsheet itself shows the override actor as the author of their own justification, i.e. the artefact hides a real SoD breach rather than creating a fake one.
2. *Legitimate delegation recorded under a supervisor's account.* Assistants prepared the justification; the log shows the accountable manager.
   - *Support:* `entered_by` differs from `org:resource` on those events (this is already documented for 23 Gita approvals).
   - *Refute:* `entered_by` equals the override actor as well.
3. *Process-design failure rather than avoidance.* No one is assigned or reminded to write justifications, so they are swept up at quarter close. This also explains the 18–21% "late" rate.
   - *Support:* absence of any workflow task/reminder for Retrospective Justification; lateness spread evenly across all emergency cases regardless of buyer.

**C. VendorMaster_Lena → AP_Kim, bank change → payment, 17 min, V-77x, 83% manual**

1. *Mass-maintenance during the vendor's banking-platform migration.* The scenario states several bank changes were legitimate mass maintenance; concentration on one vendor family and manual upload are exactly what a migration looks like. Kim is separately assigned to higher-risk suppliers, so pairing is expected.
   - *Support:* an approved change ticket / migration project record covering the exact vendor IDs and date window; a bank-confirmation letter on vendor letterhead; the new IBANs are identical across all V-77x entities and match the vendor's published details; the changes were made in a single window rather than trickling case-by-case; payments went to the vendor's own verified account.
   - *Refute:* changes fall outside the migration window; distinct account numbers per case; accounts in a different country/bank than V-77x's domicile; changed shortly before an invoice and reverted afterwards; no ticket coverage.
2. *Missing ticket linkage is a data model gap, not concealment.* The log simply has no field joining maintenance tickets to procurement cases, so legitimate and illegitimate changes are indistinguishable in-log by construction.
   - *Support:* the linkage is absent for *all* bank changes, including uncontested ones, and other vendors show the same gap.
   - *Refute:* linkage exists generally and is missing specifically for V-77x.
3. *Role design in a two-person vendor-master team.* Vendor Check + Change Bank Details by the same person (31 cases) is likely unavoidable given headcount, and may not even be a defined SoD conflict.
   - *Support:* the official SoD matrix does not list this pair; no documented backup approver role exists.

**D. Bot-related patterns (Bot_PO, Bot_Match → AP_Kim, 52 loops)**

1. *Normal automation.* An automated matcher routing exceptions to a designated human and receiving corrections back is the intended design; 6-minute medians are machine latency.
2. *Attribution artefact.* Where bot and human collapse to one org:resource, apparent "self-approval" is a mapping defect.
   - *Support/refute:* reconcile the source_system mapping table against the RPA service-account inventory.

**E. Temp_Nina → Buyer_Carla, 4 min**

1. *Backlog data entry* at quarter end with immediate routing to the emergency specialist — expected.
2. *Queue/worklist automation* auto-assigning by purchase_category. Check whether Assign Buyer is system-derived rather than a human decision.

Do **not** read raw edge weights as risk. Normalise against a volume-based null so that "they work together a lot" is not itself the finding:

```python
import itertools, collections
cases = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["org:resource"].apply(set)
n = len(cases)
marg = collections.Counter(r for s in cases for r in s)
obs  = collections.Counter(tuple(sorted(p)) for s in cases for p in itertools.combinations(s, 2))
lift = {p: c / max(n * (marg[p[0]]/n) * (marg[p[1]]/n), 1e-9) for p, c in obs.items()}
```
Restrict attention to pairs with lift meaningfully > 1 **and** an independently risky activity pair. Also compute an *exclusivity* measure — for each emergency case, how many eligible approvers were on shift — since a pair can only be informative if alternatives existed.

The generic constraints are only a starting point; encode the actual norm explicitly, since the rule has a deadline, an actor condition and a working-day calendar that DECLARE alone cannot express:

```python
import numpy as np
def justification_conformance(case_df):
    ov = case_df[case_df["concept:name"] == "Emergency Override"]
    rj = case_df[case_df["concept:name"] == "Retrospective Justification"]
    if ov.empty:
        return None
    t0 = ov["time:timestamp"].min()
    if rj.empty:
        return {"status": "missing"}
    t1 = rj["time:timestamp"].min()
    days = np.busday_count(t0.date(), t1.date())
    same = set(ov["actor"]) & set(rj["actor"])
    return {"status": "ok" if (days <= 3 and not same) else
                      ("late" if days > 3 else "same_actor"),
            "busdays": days, "same_actor": bool(same),
            "src": rj["source_system"].iloc[0]}
```
Manually enforce: (i) response — every `Emergency Override` is eventually followed by `Retrospective Justification`; (ii) the 3-working-day bound; (iii) a *different human* actor. Report `missing`, `late` and `same_actor` separately — they are different control failures with different implications.

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

**Would increase concern**

- `entered_by` populated with assistant names distinct from the approver for the 23 shared-account approvals, plus an access record showing the shared account was a known, governed arrangement.
- A corrected legacy_erp mapping table that, once reapplied, eliminates most of the 42 Create+Approve violations.
- Incident, stock-out or facilities tickets time-stamped *before* each emergency request, and an on-call roster naming Gita and Hugo for the relevant windows.
- A null-model analysis showing observed Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo co-occurrence is close to what shift coverage and category specialisation predict, and that the A→B→A loop rate matches the routine baseline pairs.
- An SoD matrix that does not designate Vendor Check + Change Bank Details as a conflict, and documents the two-person team's compensating review.
- All emergency approvals within the approvers' delegated authority limits.
- Independent confirmation from the RPA service-account register that Bot_PO events were machine-generated and separately human-approved.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

**Observation — segregation-of-duties exceptions and expedited approvals in procure-to-pay**

Analysis of last quarter's procure-to-pay event log identified a small number of control exceptions. Same-resource execution of Create and Approve Purchase Order occurred in 42 cases (1.8%), and of Change Vendor Bank Details and Payment Approval in 9 cases (0.4%). We found no evidence of loss, misappropriation, or improper conduct, and this observation makes no such assertion. A control exception indicates that a designed preventive control did not operate as intended; it does not, on its own, indicate misconduct, and several exceptions appear attributable to recording and system-mapping issues rather than to individual behaviour.

Most expedited approvals occur in the emergency medical-supply and facilities-repair channels, where cases are predominantly emergency-flagged and vendor concentration is low. Rapid processing in these channels is consistent with their intended operating design, although we could not verify from the log alone that emergency flags were applied before commitment.

The subgroup warranting the closest follow-up is the vendor master-data and payment-approval activity associated with vendor family V-77x. Unlike the emergency channels, it shows no urgency rationale, a high proportion of high-value transactions, and predominantly manual uploads. Management has indicated that a vendor banking-platform migration occurred in the period, which would plausibly explain the pattern; however, the log contains no linkage between maintenance tickets and procurement cases, so this explanation could not be corroborated.

We recommend targeted verification of the V-77x bank-detail changes against migration tickets and vendor confirmations, remediation of the shared approver account and the legacy ERP resource-mapping defect, and reinforcement of the three-working-day retrospective-justification requirement.

Limitations: conclusions are constrained by shared-account usage, a known bot/human mapping error, and bulk quarter-close data migration, which affect resource attribution and timestamp reliability. Findings should be treated as indicative until these data-quality matters are resolved.
