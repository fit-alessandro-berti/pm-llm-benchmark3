### Causal Mechanisms and Transportability Explanation
The shared causal mechanism across both sites is the reduction of idle waiting time between sequential validation steps. By permitting `Identity Check` and `Document Check` to execute concurrently immediately after `Register`, the idle time that previously accumulated while waiting for the completion of the first sequential check is eliminated, compressing the median cycle time. 

However, several mechanisms are site-specific. Site A benefited from a digital document intake share of 90%, independent external data sources, and cross-trained reviewers operating under a modern case-management system with concurrent task ownership. In contrast, Site B features 62% physical document requirements (with an 18-hour median receipt delay), strict role specialization across four distinct FTE pools, and a legacy IT case-locking architecture that prevents simultaneous active task ownership. 

Variables such as `originals_required`, `IT_case_lock`, and `role_specialization` function as strong negative effect modifiers. Specifically, initiating a document check before physical originals arrive is legally and operationally impossible, rendering parallelization ineffective for 62% of Site B's volume. Furthermore, rigid role specialization prevents Site B staff from absorbing simultaneous task spikes, shifting the bottleneck from pre-assessment waiting to downstream assessment and legal review queues. 

Therefore, a similar control-flow diagram does not guarantee transportability. Process topology alone is insufficient; token routing is constrained by physical resource availability, legal prerequisites, and data-system locks. Without modifying Site B's intake prerequisites and resource allocation rules, direct structural copying will fail to reproduce Site A's gains.

### Reasoning Mapping Site A Evidence and Site B Constraints
Site A’s observed 9-hour median cycle time reduction was achieved under conditions of high digital share (90%), modern IT systems supporting concurrent task ownership, and cross-trained staff. Site B shares a 48% high-complexity baseline and similar base service times, but is heavily constrained by 62% physical document requirements (median 18h arrival delay) and strict role specialization. 

Scenario 1 (Optimistic Transfer) fails because parallelizing document checks before physical originals arrive creates operational paralysis and severe rework loops, inflating p90 cycle times. Scenario 2 (Digital-Claim-Only) restricts parallelization to the 38% digital subgroup, capturing a safe median improvement without violating physical prerequisites. Scenario 3 models the classic bottleneck shift: speeding up upstream verification without expanding downstream assessment and legal capacities (3 FTEs and 2 FTEs respectively) starves downstream queues, causing tail latency (p90) to worsen despite modest median gains. Scenario 4 captures IT-lock failure where manual workarounds neutralize concurrency benefits. Scenario 5 (Segmented Hybrid) synthesizes the realistic constraints: channeling digital claims into parallel verification while holding physical claims until originals arrive, paired with WIP limits, achieves balanced median and tail improvements while protecting quality.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

### Process Specification
- **Eligibility Rules:** Intervention is restricted to digital-first, non-public-sector, non-high-value claims (38% of volume). Physical-document claims (62%) and legal-review mandatory claims (40%) follow modified serial or conditional-split paths.
- **Concurrent Execution:** For eligible digital claims, `Identity Check` and `Document Check` tasks are created simultaneously immediately after `Register`.
- **Statutory Prerequisites:** For cases requiring physical originals, `Document Check` remains strictly dependent on completion of `Originals Receipt`. `Assessment` requires successful completion of both `Identity Check` and `Document Check`.
- **Queue and WIP Controls:** Implement a WIP cap of 4 open verification tasks per reviewer to prevent queue saturation and task hoarding.
- **Resource Responsibilities:** Specialized identity and document reviewers retain their roles, but shared queue-monitoring dashboards allow supervisors to dynamically reallocate review capacity when queue length disparities exceed 15 items.
- **Handling of Legal-Review and Supplement Loops:** If `Legal Review` issues `Request Supplement`, the case routes back to a designated pre-assessment triage node rather than executing unmanaged loops, resetting only the necessary verification subset.
- **Logging Requirements:** Disable manual timestamp back-entry in the legacy system; enforce automated system-generated event logging for all task state transitions.

### POWL-Style Description
```text
POWL (
    nodes: [Register, Identity_Check, Document_Check, Originals_Receipt, Assessment, Legal_Review, Request_Supplement, Approve, Reject],
    order: [
        Register -> Identity_Check,
        Register -> Originals_Receipt,
        Originals_Receipt -> Document_Check,
        (Identity_Check, Document_Check) -> Assessment,
        Assessment -> Legal_Review,
        Legal_Review -> Request_Supplement,
        Request_Supplement -> Document_Check,
        Assessment -> X(Approve, Reject),
        Legal_Review -> X(Approve, Reject)
    ],
    choices: [
        X(Approve, Reject)
    ],
    loops: [
        (Request_Supplement -> Document_Check)
    ]
)
```

### Pilot Causal vs. Implementation Separation
The pilot separates mechanism failure from implementation failure by deploying two distinct treatment arms alongside rigorous process mining. If Version 1 (parallelization without WIP control) shows no cycle time improvement but high rework, while Version 2 (parallelization with WIP limits) demonstrates a robust reduction in cycle time with stable rework, the failure of Version 1 is diagnosed as an *implementation* failure caused by queue saturation and resource contention, rather than a failure of the underlying parallelization *mechanism*. 

Furthermore, event-log trace analysis isolates whether delays stem from structural task dependencies (e.g., waiting on physical mail) or operational friction (e.g., IT case locks and manual back-entry). If system-enforced audit logs reveal that parallel tasks were executed sequentially due to legacy IT case locks, the poor performance is attributed to technical implementation constraints rather than invalid causal logic. By stratifying by document channel and monitoring resource utilization concurrently, management can definitively ascertain whether poor outcomes derive from faulty causal assumptions (e.g., parallelizing tasks that legally require serial execution) or poor operational execution (e.g., lack of queue discipline and system bottlenecks).

## 8. PM4Py model-construction code

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition
from pm4py.objects.conversion.process_tree import converter as pt_converter
from pm4py.objects.conversion.powl import converter as powl_converter
from pm4py.algo.analysis.workflow_lexical_analysis.algorithm import check_is_workflow_net
from pm4py.algo.analysis.soundness.algorithm import check_soundness
from pm4py.algo.simulation.playout.petri_net import algorithm as simulator

def build_models():
    # 1. Site A Before Process Tree (Sequential verification)
    # Tree: ->('Register', 'Identity Check', 'Document Check', 'Assessment', X('Approve', 'Reject'), 'Notify')
    root_a_before = ProcessTree(operator=ProcessTree.Operator.SEQUENCE)
    reg_a1 = ProcessTree(label='Register', parent=root_a_before)
    id_a1 = ProcessTree(label='Identity Check', parent=root_a_before)
    doc_a1 = ProcessTree(label='Document Check', parent=root_a_before)
    assess_a1 = ProcessTree(label='Assessment', parent=root_a_before)
    
    choice_a1 = ProcessTree(operator=ProcessTree.Operator.XOR, parent=root_a_before)
    app_a1 = ProcessTree(label='Approve', parent=choice_a1)
    rej_a1 = ProcessTree(label='Reject', parent=choice_a1)
    
    notify_a1 = ProcessTree(label='Notify', parent=root_a_before)
    root_a_before.children = [reg_a1, id_a1, doc_a1, assess_a1, choice_a1, notify_a1]

    # 1. Site A After Process Tree (Parallel verification)
    # Tree: ->('Register', +('Identity Check', 'Document Check'), 'Assessment', X('Approve', 'Reject'), 'Notify')
    root_a_after = ProcessTree(operator=ProcessTree.Operator.SEQUENCE)
    reg_a2 = ProcessTree(label='Register', parent=root_a_after)
    
    parallel_a2 = ProcessTree(operator=ProcessTree.Operator.PARALLEL, parent=root_a_after)
    id_a2 = ProcessTree(label='Identity Check', parent=parallel_a2)
    doc_a2 = ProcessTree(label='Document Check', parent=parallel_a2)
    parallel_a2.children = [id_a2, doc_a2]
    
    assess_a2 = ProcessTree(label='Assessment', parent=root_a_after)
    
    choice_a2 = ProcessTree(operator=ProcessTree.Operator.XOR, parent=root_a_after)
    app_a2 = ProcessTree(label='Approve', parent=choice_a2)
    rej_a2 = ProcessTree(label='Reject', parent=choice_a2)
    
    notify_a2 = ProcessTree(label='Notify', parent=root_a_after)
    root_a_after.children = [reg_a2, parallel_a2, assess_a2, choice_a2, notify_a2]

    # 2. Convert Site A models to Accepting Petri Nets
    net_a_before, im_a_before, fm_a_before = pt_converter.apply(root_a_before)
    net_a_after, im_a_after, fm_a_after = pt_converter.apply(root_a_after)

    # 3. Construct Site B candidate using POWL classes
    t_reg = Transition('Register', 'Register')
    t_orig = Transition('Originals Receipt', 'Originals Receipt')
    t_id = Transition('Identity Check', 'Identity Check')
    t_doc = Transition('Document Check', 'Document Check')
    t_assess = Transition('Assessment', 'Assessment')
    t_legal = Transition('Legal Review', 'Legal Review')
    t_supp = Transition('Request Supplement', 'Request Supplement')
    t_app = Transition('Approve', 'Approve')
    t_rej = Transition('Reject', 'Reject')
    t_notify = Transition('Notify', 'Notify')

    # Choice operator for Approve/Reject
    choice_dec = OperatorPOWL(operator=OperatorPOWL.XOR, children=[t_app, t_rej])

    # Strict partial order for Site B
    nodes_b = {t_reg, t_orig, t_id, t_doc, t_assess, t_legal, t_supp, choice_dec, t_notify}
    order_b = {
        (t_reg, t_id),
        (t_reg, t_orig),
        (t_orig, t_doc),
        (t_id, t_assess),
        (t_doc, t_assess),
        (t_assess, t_legal),
        (t_legal, t_supp),
        (t_supp, t_doc), # Supplement loop back to document check
        (t_legal, choice_dec),
        (t_assess, choice_dec), # For non-legal cases
        (choice_dec, t_notify)
    }

    powl_site_b = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=nodes_b, order=order_b)

    # 4. Convert Site B POWL model to Accepting Petri Net
    net_b, im_b, fm_b = powl_converter.apply(powl_site_b)

    # 5. Check soundness and workflow-net properties
    is_wf_a_before = check_is_workflow_net(net_a_before)
    is_sound_a_before = check_soundness(net_a_before)
    
    is_wf_b = check_is_workflow_net(net_b)
    is_sound_b = check_soundness(net_b)

    print(f"Site A Before - Workflow Net: {is_wf_a_before}, Sound: {is_sound_a_before}")
    print(f"Site B Model - Workflow Net: {is_wf_b}, Sound: {is_sound_b}")

    # 6. Optional playout
    simulated_traces = simulator.apply(net_b, variant_parameter=10, parameters={simulator.Parameters.NO_TRACES: 5})
    print("Sample Site B Traces:")
    for trace in simulated_traces[:3]:
        print([event['concept:name'] for event in trace])

    return net_a_before, net_a_after, net_b

if __name__ == "__main__":
    build_models()
```

### Model Limitations Commentary
The constructed Petri net and POWL models capture control-flow routing, basic sequence, concurrency, and explicit loops (such as the legal supplement cycle). However, PM4Py process models fundamentally **cannot** encode:
1. **Data-dependent legal requirements:** The statutory rule that 62% of cases require physical originals and 40% require legal review cannot be natively represented in pure control flow without complex data-attribute guards and free-choice constructs.
2. **Resource capacity and pooling constraints:** Static workflow nets treat resources as infinite tokens; they cannot simulate Site B's specific FTE limits (4 Identity, 7 Document, 3 Assessment, 2 Legal) or bottleneck starvation.
3. **Case locking and concurrency contention:** Database-level locking, race conditions, and legacy system workarounds are infrastructural realities absent from abstract token-game semantics.
4. **Queue service times and waiting distributions:** Standard process mining and Petri net playout assume immediate or stochastic firing delays without queuing theory dynamics (e.g., M/M/1 or M/G/c queue saturation).

## 9. Recommendation

**conditional go**

### Recommendation Rationale
Site B should adopt a *conditional go* recommendation, restricting the parallel verification intervention strictly to the 38% digital-first, non-high-value claim stratum. 

**What should be transferred:** The core operational concept of concurrent task initiation (`Identity Check` and `Document Check` executed simultaneously post-registration) is highly transferable, but *only* for digital claims where external data is instantly accessible. Furthermore, the WIP limit principle (capping open tasks per reviewer) and the adoption of automated event logging should be copied to remediate Site B's timestamp back-entry issues.

**What should not be copied:** Site B must *not* copy unconstrained parallelization for the 62% of cases requiring physical originals, nor should it eliminate sequential dependencies before mail intake is logged. Blindly copying Site A's aggregate process tree across all cases would cause operational paralysis, failed document checks, and severe rework loops.

**Which subgroup should enter first:** The pilot and initial rollout must target strictly the digital-first, non-public-sector stratum (38% volume). High-complexity, legal-review mandatory (40%), and physical-document cases must remain excluded until downstream assessment and legal capacities are expanded.

**Expected benefits and risks:** Expected benefits include a 10–18% reduction in median cycle time for digital claims and improved queue visibility. The primary risk is a severe bottleneck shift: accelerating upstream verification without expanding Site B's 3-person assessment and 2-person legal review pools will starve downstream queues, causing p90 cycle times to balloon and rework rates to surge.

**Trigger conditions for expansion, modification, or abandonment:** 
- *Expand* to physical claims only if Originals Receipt delays are decoupled from document checking via mailroom scanning automation, and downstream assessment capacity is increased by at least 1 FTE.
- *Modify* (tighten WIP caps or add triage filters) if the pilot rework rate exceeds 20% or if median assessment wait times increase by more than 20%.
- *Abandon* immediately if legacy IT case-locking causes persistent database deadlocks or if 90-day appeal rates increase by more than 25 relative percentage points.

## 10. Assumption register

1. **Digital Channel Independence** — *Why needed:* Assumes digital identity and document external data sources operate asynchronously without mutual blocking. — *How to test:* Measure API response correlation times during peak load. — *Consequence if false:* Parallel execution causes system timeouts and cascading API failures.
2. **Physical Mail Processing Latency** — *Why needed:* Assumes the 18-hour median Originals Receipt delay for 62% of cases is stable and bounded. — *How to test:* Audit mailroom intake timestamp deltas weekly. — *Consequence if false:* Document checks initiated prematurely fail validation, doubling rework rates.
3. **IT Case-Lock Workaround Stability** — *Why needed:* Assumes manual back-entry workarounds do not corrupt underlying event sequence logs beyond recovery. — *How to test:* Cross-audit system audit logs against manual timestamp entries. — *Consequence if false:* Process mining analyses yield false concurrency metrics and unidentifiable bottlenecks.
4. **Resource Specialization Elasticity** — *Why needed:* Assumes specialized FTE pools (4 ID, 7 Doc) can maintain throughput without cross-training if WIP limits are enforced. — *How to test:* Monitor queue length variance across specialized reviewer pools. — *Consequence if false:* Severe task starvation in the document review pool while identity reviewers sit idle.
