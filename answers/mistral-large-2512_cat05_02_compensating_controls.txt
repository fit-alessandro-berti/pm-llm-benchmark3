## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1’s four-eyes principle aims to prevent erroneous or fraudulent transfers by ensuring no single person controls both initiation and authorization. Decomposed objectives and control mappings:

| **Objective/Threat Scenario**               | **AV-2**               | **MG-4**               | **RA-7**               | **ED-1**               |
|---------------------------------------------|------------------------|------------------------|------------------------|------------------------|
| **1. Erroneous data entry** (e.g., wrong amount, duplicate invoice) | Prevents (duplicate, amount limit) | Detects (if reviewed) | Detects (audit) | Partially prevents (AV-2 pass required) |
| **2. Fraudulent vendor/bank account** (e.g., fake vendor, changed bank details) | Prevents (sanctions, bank ownership, recent changes) | Detects (if reviewed) | Detects (audit) | Partially prevents (AV-2 pass required) |
| **3. Collusion between preparer and releaser** | Fails (cannot detect collusion) | Fails (if manager is complicit) | Detects (audit may uncover patterns) | Fails (emergency conditions do not address collusion) |
| **4. Unauthorized or fabricated transactions** (e.g., no goods/services received) | Fails (no receipt validation) | Detects (if reviewed) | Detects (audit) | Partially prevents (manager approval required) |
| **5. Compromised user account** (e.g., stolen credentials) | Fails (cannot detect account compromise) | Detects (if manager is independent) | Detects (audit may uncover anomalies) | Partially prevents (manager approval required) |
| **6. Emergency circumvention** (e.g., false emergency to bypass controls) | Prevents (AV-2 pass required) | Prevents (manager approval required) | Detects (audit) | Prevents (conditions must be met) |

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating control package is evaluated against the following criteria:

| **Criterion**               | **Fully Equivalent**                                                                 | **Partially Equivalent**                                                                 | **Merely Mitigating**                                                                 | **Ineffective**                                                                 |
|-----------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Timing**                  | Preventive action occurs before irrevocable settlement.                             | Detective/corrective action occurs before irrevocable settlement.                       | Detective action occurs after settlement but within recall window.                   | No action or action occurs after recall window.                                |
| **Independence**            | Second actor is organizationally and hierarchically independent.                     | Second actor is independent but may have indirect conflicts (e.g., shared incentives).  | Second actor is not independent (e.g., direct supervisor).                           | No second actor or second actor is complicit.                                  |
| **Coverage**                | Addresses all material risks (e.g., fraud, error, collusion, account compromise).    | Addresses most risks but has gaps (e.g., no collusion detection).                       | Addresses only a subset of risks (e.g., only error detection).                       | Addresses no material risks.                                                   |
| **Overrideability**         | Overrides are rare, logged, and require independent justification.                   | Overrides are logged but may lack independent review.                                   | Overrides are frequent or lack justification.                                       | Overrides are routine or unlogged.                                             |
| **Recoverability**          | Detective/corrective action can fully recover funds or prevent loss.                 | Detective action can recover funds but with uncertainty (e.g., partial recall).         | Detective action can only detect, not recover.                                      | No recoverability.                                                              |
| **Evidence Quality**        | High-quality evidence (e.g., signed approvals, system logs, receipts).               | Evidence exists but may be incomplete or unsigned.                                      | Evidence is weak (e.g., rubber-stamp approvals).                                     | No evidence or evidence is fabricated.                                          |
| **Collusion Resistance**    | Controls are designed to detect or prevent collusion (e.g., random audits).         | Controls can detect collusion but are not designed to prevent it.                       | Controls are not designed to address collusion.                                     | Controls are vulnerable to collusion.                                           |

**Why "same expected loss" is not the only standard**:
- **Control objectives are not purely quantitative**: Some risks (e.g., collusion, reputational damage) are difficult to quantify but are critical to the control framework.
- **Timing matters**: A preventive control is inherently stronger than a detective one, even if both achieve the same expected loss reduction.
- **Independence is qualitative**: A manager who rubber-stamps approvals may technically satisfy "four-eyes" but fail the control objective.
- **Evidence quality affects accountability**: Weak evidence undermines the ability to investigate or recover from failures.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| **Control** | **Preventive**                                                                 | **Detective**                                                                 | **Corrective**                                                                 | **Hybrid**                                                                 |
|-------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **AV-2**    | Yes (blocks or warns before release)                                          | No                                                                            | No                                                                            | No                                                                         |
| **MG-4**    | Yes (if approval occurs before release)                                       | Yes (if approval occurs after release but before settlement)                  | No (unless approval triggers a recall)                                        | **Yes**: Manager approval after release but before settlement is **preventive for future losses** and **detective for the current transaction**. |
| **RA-7**    | No                                                                            | Yes (audit detects issues)                                                    | Yes (if audit triggers a recall within the window)                            | No                                                                         |
| **ED-1**    | Yes (conditions must be met before release)                                    | No                                                                            | No                                                                            | No                                                                         |

**Semantics of manager action after release but before settlement**:
- **Preventive**: The manager’s approval prevents future losses by ensuring the transaction is legitimate before it becomes irrevocable.
- **Detective**: The manager’s review detects issues with the current transaction (e.g., fraud, error) that may require recall.
- **Hybrid**: In practice, such approvals serve both purposes. They prevent the transaction from becoming irrevocable without scrutiny (preventive) while also detecting issues that may require corrective action (detective).

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

### **Evidence Collection Rules**
1. **Same-user rate**:
   - Flag cases where `Prepare_Payment` and `Release_Payment` are performed by the same user, even if nominally compliant (e.g., AP-204).
   - Alert if rate exceeds 5% of payments or 2% of payment value in a rolling 30-day window.

2. **Override rate**:
   - Log all AV-2 warnings and overrides, including the user, timestamp, and reason.
   - Alert if override rate exceeds 1% of payments or 0.5% of payment value in a rolling 30-day window.

3. **Late manager approval**:
   - Flag manager approvals occurring after `Release_Payment` but before settlement.
   - Alert if rate exceeds 10% of payments or 5% of payment value in a rolling 30-day window.

4. **Audit-before-recall-window**:
   - Flag audits occurring after the 24-hour recall window.
   - Alert if rate exceeds 5% of payments or 2% of payment value in a rolling 30-day window.

5. **Repeated resource pairs**:
   - Flag pairs of users who perform `Prepare_Payment` and `Release_Payment` together more than 3 times in 30 days.
   - Alert if any pair exceeds 10% of payments in a rolling 30-day window.

6. **Shared workstations**:
   - Log workstation IDs for `Prepare_Payment` and `Release_Payment`.
   - Flag cases where the same workstation is used for both steps by different users.

7. **Bank-change proximity**:
   - Flag payments where the vendor’s bank account was changed within 7 days of `Release_Payment`.
   - Alert if rate exceeds 1% of payments in a rolling 30-day window.

8. **Control outages**:
   - Log all AV-2 unavailability events.
   - Alert if AV-2 is unavailable for more than 1 hour in a 24-hour period.

### **Monitoring Rules**
- **Alerts vs. Proof of Misconduct**:
  - Alerts indicate potential control weaknesses or anomalies, not proof of misconduct.
  - Proof of misconduct requires additional investigation (e.g., interviews, document review).
- **Severity Tiers**:
  - **Critical**: Same-user handling + high-value payment + bank change (e.g., AP-202).
  - **High**: Repeated resource pairs + shared workstations (e.g., AP-204, AP-209).
  - **Medium**: Late manager approvals or audits (e.g., AP-201, AP-206).
  - **Low**: Emergency token timing issues (e.g., AP-206).
