# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

## Evidence, assumptions, and estimation conventions

The demand percentages are treated as expected proportions, not as observations of individual cases. Case types and priorities are therefore not assumed independent. A complex case contributes one Mechanical and one Electrical branch for ME, or one Electrical and one Software branch for ES.

The following assumptions are needed because the prompt does not fully specify a stochastic or operational model:

1. **Arrivals within a two-hour block are uniform.** This is a modeling assumption, not a supplied fact. It is used only to estimate P1 exposure before noon and to generate illustrative simulation arrivals.
2. **U1’s “rises by 8 percentage points” means P1 becomes 18% and 23% in the morning blocks.** The remaining probability is divided between P2 and P3 in proportion to their original shares. Renormalization is necessary for a valid categorical distribution.
3. **U2 doubles the E and ES category weights before normalization.** E and ES probabilities in the affected blocks increase; other categories decrease. Block arrival volumes remain unchanged. Thus, total arrivals need not remain exactly 42.
4. **Service ranges are bounded service times.** The capacity analysis uses midpoints. The illustrative simulation uses a mixture of independent uniform distributions within the ordinary and complex-tail ranges. This is a convenient distributional assumption, not an inference about the actual distribution.
5. **Tail draws are independent across activities and branches.** Actual tail correlation is unknown and could materially affect queueing and SLA attainment.
6. **The same-day calendar ends at each resource’s listed shift end.** No next-day schedule, vacation, or overnight processing is supplied. Unfinished cases are therefore censored rather than silently completed.
7. **The 30-minute overtime allowance is not automatically consumed.** It is included as a capacity option, but granting it during a shock would compete with other cases. The illustrative simulation does not use overtime.
8. **Preemption applies to diagnostic service, not Intake or Validation.** A preempted activity resumes with 10% of its then-remaining active service added. A case can be interrupted only once.
9. **Handover is charged on each changed-resource activity boundary.** In parallel execution, a new resource branch can incur its own Intake-to-diagnostic handover. This is an explicit accounting convention; the prompt does not say how a logical parallel handoff should be counted.
10. **Resource speed multipliers do not change with priority.** There is no supplied evidence for priority-dependent speed, fatigue, learning, or quality effects.
11. **No resource is made qualified for a new skill.** “Pooling” means pooling among the qualifications actually listed in the matrix.

For analytical estimates below:

- Delay is elapsed time from arrival to Validation completion.
- Tail delay is the 90th percentile unless stated otherwise.
- SLA percentages include all arrived cases; unresolved cases are failures, not excluded observations.
- “Mean completed delay” excludes cases unfinished at the end of the modeled day. It can be optimistic under overload.
- A right-censored lower bound assigns an unfinished case a delay of at least `20:00  arrival time`.

## Parallel versus sequential branches

Let \(T_M,T_E,T_S\) be the expected active branch times after the selected resource’s speed multiplier. The approximate critical-path comparison is:

\[
T_{\mathrm{parallel}}\approx \max(T_M,T_E,T_S)+8,
\]

\[
T_{\mathrm{sequential}}\approx T_M+T_E \quad\text{or}\quad T_E+T_S,
\]

plus any handover and family-transfer costs.

Examples:

- With nominal unit-speed branches, ME is approximately 32.7 minutes in parallel versus 67.3 minutes sequentially, before coordination.
- ES is approximately 46.5 minutes in parallel versus 71.2 minutes sequentially, before coordination.
- A fast Mechanical and Electrical pair can save roughly 26 minutes on an ordinary ME branch.
- A Chen software resource has a 1.25 multiplier. Even when the electrical branch is fast, parallel ES execution can remain attractive for P1 because the software branch is critical.

A parallel decision should not rely on these means alone. It should require:

1. qualified resources available immediately;
2. sufficient remaining deadline slack;
3. no higher-priority work that would be displaced;
4. tolerance for the additional coordination and handoffs.

## Decision variables

The operational decision variables are:

- resource eligibility and primary skill/family assignment;
- reserved P1 and software-capable capacity;
- dispatch score and tie-breaking rules;
- preemption eligibility and restart-cost approval;
- branch execution mode;
- handover-minimizing affinity rules;
- overflow triggers and overtime release;
- maximum protected utilization.

Staffing itself is held fixed at the six supplied resources. A recommendation for additional software qualification or extended hours is a capacity intervention, not something achievable merely by routing the supplied staff.

## Objectives

An SLA-risk objective is:

\[
\min F_1 =
\sum_k w_k\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{(D-\tau_k)_+}{\tau_k}\right]
+\alpha\,\mathbb{E}[D]
+\beta\,P_{90}(D),
\]

where \(k\) denotes priority class, \(\tau_k\) is its target, and illustrative weights could be \(w_{P1},w_{P2},w_{P3}=4,2,1\). Both class-specific and **all-priority-mixed** SLA attainment must be reported. Otherwise, protecting an easy P1 subset could conceal P1 failures among early Software cases.

A fairness-and-robustness objective is:

\[
\max_s\min_p J_{p,s},
\]

with an illustrative scenario-specific score

\[
J_{p,s}
=1-0.40\,\mathrm{SLA}_{p,s}
-0.30\,[u_{p,s}-0.90]_+
-0.20\,\mathrm{CV}_{p,s}
-0.10\,\min(P_{90}(D_{p,s})/1440,1).
\]

Here, \(s\) ranges over Base and U1–U5. The numerical weights are policy-design choices, not supplied facts.

Under the uniform-within-block assumption, expected P1 Software cases arriving before noon are:

\[

=1.0084.

That is approximately 1.01 of the 5.44 expected P1 cases, or **18.5% of P1 arrivals**. These cases cannot meet a 120-minute target because no listed Software resource is available before noon.

\[
P1\_SLA_{\mathrm{mixed}}
\le 1-\frac{1.0084}{5.44}

Under U1:

- morning arrivals: 27.2;

# 3. Candidate policies

All policies use these common rules unless overridden:

- **Eligibility:** only the skills in the supplied matrix count.
- **Assignment audit:** log the eligible set, selected resource, score components, and rejection reason.
- **P1 preemption:** permit at most one interruption per case. A P1 arrival must be projected at risk, another qualified resource must be available immediately, and the restart penalty is recorded. An interrupted case is not considered permanently lower priority.
- **Handover:** 7 minutes whenever a resource changes at an activity boundary.
- **Family transfer:** add 5 minutes when sequential work moves from Mechanical to Electrical/Software, unless the same cross-skilled resource retains the case.
- **Setup:** add 5 minutes when a resource’s previous diagnostic family differs from the next case’s family. This is not charged merely because one case runs M then E on the same resource.
- **P3 aging:** after 240 elapsed minutes, P3 enters the P2 dispatch band. It remains below protected P1 capacity in the hybrid.
- **Tie breaking:** oldest arrival, then case identifier.

## Policy A — Specialized cells with fixed queues

**Eligibility and staffing**

- Mechanical cell: Ada, Ben, Deepa.
- Electrical cell: Ada, Chen, Deepa, Farah.
- Software cell: Chen, Emil.
- Software has first claim on Chen; other Electrical work uses the remaining qualified capacity.
- No dynamic cross-family overflow or workload-balancing score.

**Dispatch**

Within each cell, choose the eligible resource with the earliest projected completion. A resource that is qualified for both M and E is assigned according to the case’s current diagnostic family rather than whichever total queue is shorter.

**Parallelization**

Use parallel execution only if both resources are immediately free and the P1 or P2 deadline slack exceeds:

\[
0.75\left(\text{remaining deadline slack}-8\right).
\]

P3 work is sequential unless doing so would clearly miss a hard operational cutoff.

**Reservations and overflow**

No reservation. A case waits if its family cell is busy.

**Advantages and weaknesses**

- Simple to audit and protects specialist learning.
- Avoids unnecessary cross-family setup.
- Cannot resolve the absence of morning Software coverage.
- Can leave Ada/Farah or Chen/Emil underused while another qualified resource is idle.

## Policy B — Full qualified-resource pooling

**Eligibility and staffing**

Every resource joins the pool for every skill for which it is listed as qualified.

**Dispatch**

For each ready case and eligible resource, estimate:

- remaining active service, including the reduced effective completion threshold after preemption;
- calendar-aware projected finish;
- setup and likely handover;
- outstanding eligible work per resource.

For a case \(c\) and resource \(r\), use:

\[
\mathrm{Score}_{c,r}
=
0.25\,\widehat{\mathrm{Finish}}_{c,r}
+
\frac{
\sum_{q\in Q_r}(1-f_q)\,T_{q,\mathrm{remaining}}
}{
m_{r,\mathrm{skill}(c)}
},
\]

where \(Q_r\) is the ready work eligible for \(r\), and \(f_q\) is recorded progress. Select the lowest score.

Priority bands are:

1. P1 with a feasible projected finish;
2. other P1 and aged P3;
3. ordinary P2;
4. non-aged P3.

A P1 case that cannot meet its deadline is not allowed to preempt infinitely many alternatives; its unattainability is reported rather than hidden.

**Parallelization**

Use the same deadline-slack test as Policy A, but do not require a particular family cell.

**Reservations and overflow**

No reservation. Overflow is automatic through the pool.

**Advantages and weaknesses**

- Strong base-case load balancing.
- Can move Intake/Validation work away from scarce M/E/S resources.
- Does not fix the morning Software coverage hole.
- Queue-pressure scoring can increase resource switching and family setup.

## Policy C — Reserved P1 capacity with controlled overflow

This is the recommended operating design.

**Base dispatch**

Use Policy B’s workload-balancing score, but:

- add a 5-minute affinity penalty when a ME or ES case would incur an avoidable family transfer;
- prioritize keeping ME/ES on the same cross-skilled resource when it remains feasible;
- protect P1 work that can still meet its deadline;
- do not use a nominally faster resource if the resulting setup and family transfer consume the time advantage.

**Reservations**

- Ada’s first 30 minutes of each hour from 08:00–12:00 are reserved for P1 Intake.
- Chen and Emil’s first 30 minutes of each hour from 12:00–16:00 are reserved for P1 Software or the Software branch of ES.
- A reserved block is released when there is no queued P1 work and there is no P1 arrival visible within the next 15 minutes.
- If arrival visibility is unavailable, retain the block rather than assuming it will remain empty.
- Breaks are protected and do not consume the reserved productive interval.

**Electrical overflow trigger**

Assign Ada to an urgent E or ME electrical branch when either:

- at least two urgent E-eligible cases are waiting and Ada has no P1 Intake or Validation obligation due within 30 minutes; or
- the oldest urgent E-eligible case has waited 45 minutes.

The dispatch log must show that setup time and the expected family transfer were included.

**Preemption**

Use a more conservative rule than full pooling:

- only preempt if another qualified resource is immediately available;
- only for a P1 case with negative or insufficient deadline slack;
- never more than once per lower-priority case;
- record the exact remaining service, 10% penalty, paused resource, and resuming resource.

**Parallelization**

- P1: parallelize if both resources are free and the software/electrical critical path fits within 75% of the remaining slack.
- P2: use the same test when no protected work is displaced.
- P3: prefer sequential execution unless parallel execution clearly accelerates the oldest waiting case without consuming protected capacity.
- If parallel execution is unavailable, ES remains sequential; no supplied resource is both Electrical- and Software-qualified.

**Fallback**

Fall back to specialized cells when the eligible-resource feed is unavailable or inconsistent. This is a safe operating mode, not a claim that specialization solves the structural capacity deficit.

- Delay ranges are illustrative day-level planning estimates, not confidence intervals or observed results.
- P1/P2 SLA is a **mixed-priority** percentage.
- P90 is completed-case P90; under overload, it must be read with the unresolved-case count.
- Maximum utilization is delivered work divided by the resource’s available scheduled minutes. A value near 100% leaves no recovery margin.
- The separate scenario analysis can show offered load above 100%, even if a same-day simulation only delivers approximately 100% before censoring.
- Utilization fairness is the coefficient of variation of resource utilization: 0 is equal, 1 is highly unequal. Equal utilization during a capability shortage is not evidence of satisfactory service.
- Resilience uses a 1–5 scale, where 1 means a structural coverage failure and 5 means multiple feasible recovery paths.
- Handover burden is expressed as changed-resource boundaries per case, plus indicative setup minutes per day.

# 5. Robust recommendation

## Primary recommendation: Policy C

Adopt the hybrid policy because it offers the best practical trade-off across the supplied scenarios:

- It performs at least as well as the other policies on the intended robustness objective, even if full pooling occasionally has a slightly lower base mean.
- It protects feasible P1 work without consuming all capacity exclusively for P1.
- It limits avoidable family switching and setup burden.
- It provides explicit starvation control and release rules for reservations.
- It responds to U2 by migrating capacity toward Electrical and Validation.
- It partially mitigates U4 by protecting urgent work, although it cannot eliminate tail overload.
- It fails transparently under U1, U3, and U5 rather than pretending that pooling created missing Software capacity.

Policy C does not numerically dominate every metric. Policy B may have lower base mean delay and better utilization CV. The recommendation is based on **worst-scenario service protection, P2 continuity, auditability, and controlled coordination burden**, not only base mean delay.

## Fallback mode

Use full qualified-resource pooling when:

- at least two urgent cases are waiting for the same skill;
- the oldest urgent case has waited more than 45 minutes;
- a skill is forecast to exceed 85% of its remaining available capacity over the next hour;
- the hybrid’s affinity penalty is preventing a materially better completion time.

Use fixed specialization only when the eligible-resource feed is unavailable or disputed. It is the safest fallback for audit consistency, not the preferred capacity-response policy.

## Explicit operating switches

| Trigger | Mode change |
|---|---|
| Two urgent P1 cases eligible for the same scarce skill | Temporarily disable low-priority family affinity; use full pooling |
| Oldest urgent work exceeds 45 minutes | Activate documented overflow, including Ada for urgent E/ME |
| Any qualified resource exceeds 85% of its next-hour effective capacity | Freeze new nonurgent handovers to that resource |
| Resource reaches 90% of remaining daily capacity | Require supervisor approval for optional setup-heavy work |
| Two protected P1 cases remain without immediate coverage | Escalate capacity intervention; do not promise that routing can meet SLA |
| No qualified Software resource exists in the next 60 minutes | Communicate a structural SLA risk; prioritize by clinical/technical urgency if available, not by this model |
| Dispatch scores conflict within 5 minutes of projected finish | Choose the option with lower handover/setup burden and older arrival |

## Capacity conclusion that routing cannot change

If the operational objective requires at least 90% mixed-priority P1 attainment under U1, U3, or U5, the supplied capacity is insufficient. Required interventions include one or more of:

- qualified Software coverage starting before noon;
- sustained additional Software capacity;
- a validated reduction in Software service demand;
- formally changed SLA expectations.

The policy should not conceal this by reporting only an easy P1 subset.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

## Gaming of estimates

Operators may understate expected service time to obtain a better dispatch score. A fallback estimate based on rolling median or P60 service should be auditable, and actual service must be compared with the estimate.

Priority credits should not be awarded for optimistic estimates. They should depend on observed completion and elapsed SLA outcome.

## P3 starvation

A strict P1-first, P2-second policy can indefinitely starve P3. The proposed 240-minute promotion into the P2 band provides a simple safeguard. Reserved capacity should not prevent all aged P3 work from progressing once the release condition is met.

Report P3 attainment and oldest-waiting age by skill, not merely overall utilization.

## Skill erosion

Cross-skill overflow is valuable but can reduce regular practice. A resource repeatedly used outside its primary family may need:

- a minimum primary-family allocation;
- supervision during unfamiliar work;
- a cap on consecutive overflow assignments;
- periodic qualification review.

“Qualified” must remain an explicit attribute, not an informal belief that a person can learn the task during an incident.

## Excessive preemption

Every interruption adds at least 10% of remaining service and can impose additional queue delay. A P1 interruption can make the combined P1/P2 outcome worse.

The once-per-case rule is a hard safety limit. A second preemption should be impossible in the dispatch engine, not merely discouraged.

## Coordination overhead

Parallel ME/ES execution can save substantial critical-path time but adds coordination and potentially two changed-resource boundaries. Its value falls when:

- one branch cannot start immediately;
- the sequential resource is cross-skilled;
- a family transfer is added;
- the case is P3 and can tolerate sequential processing.

The log should distinguish time saved on the critical path from coordination work performed.

## New bottleneck at Validation

A dispatch rule that sends every urgent case to the fastest diagnostic resource may leave only slower Validation resources. Ada’s Validation multiplier is 1.15; Farah’s is 1.20. Ben, Deepa, and Emil should therefore be protected for Validation when their diagnostic skills are not uniquely required.

Track Validation queue entry separately from case-level waiting.

## Fairness

Three notions must remain separate:

1. **Opportunity fairness:** comparable cases receive comparable access to qualified resources.
2. **Workload fairness:** utilization and overtime are reasonably distributed.
3. **Outcome fairness:** old, P3, and skill-constrained cases are not systematically abandoned.

The hybrid improves the first two without guaranteeing the third.

## Human interpretability

Operators should see a short explanation such as:

> “Selected Deepa: predicted finish 14:18, 1.25 Mechanical multiplier, no family setup, no P1 displacement; Ada would finish 14:21 but would add 5-minute family setup.”

A black-box optimizer that cannot produce this explanation is unsuitable for live incident dispatch.

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design

## Deployment indicators

| Indicator | Definition | Warning interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Queue delay by stage and skill | Assignment/start minus queue entry | Skill-specific starvation or hidden handoff queues |
| Case elapsed SLA | Validation completion minus arrival | Actual target attainment |
| Active service SLA | Sum of active service, including restart penalty | Separate service deterioration from queueing |
| P1 SLA, mixed priority | All P1 arrivals passing / all P1 arrivals | Prevents subset gaming |
| P90/P95 by arrival block | Completed-case and censored-aware results | Time-of-day failure |
| Unresolved-at-day-end rate | Unfinished weighted cases / all weighted cases | Hides same-day infeasibility |
| Resource utilization | Active work / available scheduled minutes | Overtime and fragility |
| Utilization CV | Standard deviation / mean utilization | Workload concentration |
| Handover minutes per case | Sum of 7-minute boundary delays | Routing inefficiency |
| Family-transfer rate | Additional 5-minute transfers / cases | Skill-mixing overhead |
| Setup minutes per diagnostic | Setup work / diagnostics | Consecutive skill switching |
| Parallel adoption and critical-path saving | Parallel eligible cases; elapsed saving | Avoiding ceremonial parallelism |
| Preemption count and restart burden | Interruption count; 10% penalty | Excessive priority churn |
| Reservation use and idle time | Protected versus released intervals | Over-reservation |
| Unfinished qualified work by hour | Waiting work by deadline feasibility | Structural coverage gaps |

## Required event attributes

Every event should include, where applicable:

- `case:concept:name`
- `concept:name`
- `start_timestamp`
- `time:timestamp`
- `org:resource`
- case priority and case type
- queue/skill
- SLA deadline and remaining slack
- eligible-resource set
- assignment decision and score components
- selected resource and actual skill used
- planned and actual service
- lifecycle: `start`, `complete`, `pause`, or `resume`
- pause/resume reason
- restart penalty
- handover reason and minutes
- family-transfer flag
- setup flag and minutes
- branch mode: parallel or sequential
- policy name and version
- scenario/shock flag
- arrival block
- clock basis: elapsed, active, or waiting

Queue-entry events must use a distinct concept such as `queue:Software`, so they are not mistaken for actual service.

## PM4Py analyses

1. **Case duration and waiting**
   - `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` on complete case intervals.
   - Label the result as whole-case elapsed span, not active service.
   - Recalculate stage-specific waiting separately from queue events.

2. **Service-time drift**
   - `pm4py.get_service_time` grouped by case and activity.
   - Compare planned, logged, and replayed estimates by resource, skill, and priority.
   - Monitor P50/P90 and tail probability indicators.

3. **Handover burden**
   - `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` on complete, resource-consistent events.
   - Count actual boundary changes, not merely all adjacent activity pairs.
   - Treat parallel branch changes separately.

4. **Bottleneck migration**
   - Prefix-token discovery by priority and case type.
   - Variant analysis by skill, resource, arrival block, and policy version.
   - Stage-specific queue metrics before aggregating to a case-level view.

5. **Policy drift**
   - Compare discovered control-flow variants for sequential versus parallel ME/ES.
   - Detect whether reservation release rules, aging, or preemption are being applied inconsistently.

Parallel cases and interrupted activities require explicit lifecycle filtering. Naively summing activity durations across resources can double-count parallel elapsed time; naively taking first-start to last-end can count queue and pause time as service.

# 8. Reproducible Python

The following is an executable illustrative discrete-event scaffold. It encodes the supplied tables, generates all six scenarios and three policies, repeats each policy/scenario over three seeds, prints compact comparison tables, and writes one simulated base-case event log.

The simulation is deliberately conservative:

- arrivals are uniformly distributed within blocks;
- fractional expected arrivals are represented by a final case with a fractional SLA/arrival weight;
- no next-day service or overtime is granted;
- unfinished cases are censored at 20:00;
- delay distributions exclude unfinished cases and are printed with completion coverage;
- all-P1 and all-P2 SLA rates include unfinished cases as failures;
- handover is charged per changed-resource branch boundary;
- a completed uninterrupted activity is logged; an activity that remains censored at 20:00 has no completion event.

The estimates in sections 2–4 are not represented as outputs from an executed run. The code provides an auditable way to test or replace those planning estimates.

```python
import math
from dataclasses import dataclass, field
from datetime import datetime, timedelta

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Supplied evidence and explicit modeling parameters
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

SERVICE = {
    "Intake":     (5, 8, 10, 14, 0.08),
    "Mechanical": (18, 28, 35, 55, 0.15),
    "Electrical": (22, 35, 40, 65, 0.18),
    "Software":   (25, 40, 45, 80, 0.20),
    "Validation": (10, 18, 22, 35, 0.12),
}

SPEED = {
    "Ada":   {"Intake":1.00, "Mechanical":1.00, "Electrical":1.35, "Validation":1.15},
    "Ben":   {"Intake":1.10, "Mechanical":1.00, "Validation":1.00},
    "Chen":  {"Intake":1.10, "Electrical":1.00, "Software":1.25, "Validation":1.10},
    "Deepa": {"Intake":1.00, "Mechanical":1.25, "Electrical":1.00, "Validation":1.00},
    "Emil":  {"Intake":1.10, "Software":1.00, "Validation":1.00},
    "Farah": {"Intake":1.00, "Mechanical":1.40, "Electrical":1.40, "Validation":1.20},
}

# Local clock minutes from midnight. A single day is modeled.
SHIFT = {
    "Ada": (480, 960), "Ben": (480, 960), "Chen": (480, 960),
    "Deepa": (600, 1080), "Emil": (720, 1200), "Farah": (480, 720),
}
BREAKS = {
    "Ada": [(735, 765)],       # 12:15-12:45
    "Ben": [(720, 750)],       # 12:00-12:30
    "Chen": [(750, 780)],      # 12:30-13:00
    "Deepa": [(840, 870)],     # 14:00-14:30
    "Emil": [(960, 990)],      # 16:00-16:30
    "Farah": [],
}

DEMAND = pd.DataFrame([
    ["08:00-10:00", 7,  .10, .55, .35, .30, .25, .15, .20, .10],
    ["10:00-12:00", 9,  .15, .55, .30, .25, .25, .15, .25, .10],
    ["12:00-14:00", 7,  .12, .58, .30, .22, .28, .18, .20, .12],
    ["14:00-16:00", 10, .18, .52, .30, .20, .30, .18, .20, .12],
    ["16:00-18:00", 6,  .10, .50, .40, .20, .25, .25, .15, .15],
    ["18:00-20:00", 3,  .05, .45, .50, .25, .20, .30, .10, .15],
], columns=[
    "block", "arrivals", "P1", "P2", "P3",
    "M", "E", "S", "ME", "ES"
]).set_index("block", drop=False)

PARAMS = {
    "handover_minutes": 7,
    "family_transfer_minutes": 5,
    "setup_minutes": 5,
    "parallel_coordination_minutes": 8,
    "restart_fraction": 0.10,
    "max_preemptions_per_case": 1,
    "p3_aging_minutes": 240,
    "p1_risk_margin_minutes": 12,
    "parallel_slack_fraction": 0.75,
    "pool_balance_weight": 0.25,
    "hybrid_affinity_penalty_minutes": 5,
    "overtime_per_resource": 30,       # encoded; simulation grants none
    "simulate_overtime": False,
    "it_outage": (570, 600),          # 09:30-10:00
    "sla_minutes": {"P1": 120, "P2": 480, "P3": 1440},
}
DIAGS = {"Mechanical", "Electrical", "Software"}
POLICIES = ["A_specialized", "B_full_pool", "C_hybrid"]
SCENARIOS = ["Base", "U1", "U2", "U3", "U4", "U5"]
SEEDS = [17, 29, 43]
DAY_END = 1200
BASE_DATE = datetime(2024, 1, 1)


def timestamp(minute):
    return BASE_DATE + timedelta(minutes=float(minute))


def calendar_intervals(resource):
    start, end = SHIFT[resource]
    return [(start + i * 30, start + (i + 1) * 30)
            for i in range((end - start) // 30)]


def capacity_minutes(resource, scenario):
    if scenario == "U3" and resource == "Chen":
        return 0
    cap = sum(b - a for a, b in calendar_intervals(resource))
    cap -= sum(b - a for a, b in BREAKS[resource])
    if scenario == "U5" and resource != "Chen":
        cap -= PARAMS["it_outage"][1] - PARAMS["it_outage"][0]
    return cap


def advance_calendar(resource, skill, start, requested):
    """Return (end clock, actual productive minutes). No overtime."""
    requested = float(requested)
    end_clock = float(start)
    remaining = requested
    outage = PARAMS["it_outage"]

    while remaining > 1e-9 and end_clock < DAY_END:
        if skill == "Intake" and scenario == "U5":
            if end_clock < outage[0]:
                end_clock = float(outage[0])
                continue
            if outage[0] <= end_clock < outage[1]:
                end_clock = float(outage[1])
                continue

        lo, hi = SHIFT[resource]
        t = max(end_clock, float(lo))
        if t >= hi:
            break

        available = None
        for a, b in calendar_intervals(resource):
            if a <= t < b:
                available = (a, b)
                break
        if available is None:
            end_clock = t
            continue

        bs, be = available
        for a, b in BREAKS[resource]:
            if a <= t < b:
                available = (b, b)  # resume only after this break
                break

        bs, be = available
        if be <= t:
            end_clock = t
            continue

        usable = min(be, hi, end_clock + remaining)
        worked = max(0.0, usable - t)
        end_clock = t + worked
        remaining -= worked

        if remaining > 1e-9 and end_clock <= be:
            end_clock = be

    return end_clock, requested - max(0.0, remaining)


def scenario_demand(scenario, rng):
    rows = []
    for block, row in DEMAND.iterrows():
        n = float(row.arrivals)
        if scenario == "U1" and block in ("08:00-10:00", "10:00-12:00"):
            n *= 1.7

        integer_n, fraction = int(n), n - int(n)
        lo, _ = block.split("-")
        hour, minute = map(int, lo.split(":"))
        block_start = hour * 60 + minute

        probs = row[["P1", "P2", "P3"]].to_numpy(dtype=float)
        probs /= probs.sum()
        if scenario == "U1" and block in ("08:00-10:00", "10:00-12:00"):
            p1_new = 0.18 if block == "08:00-10:00" else 0.23
            remainder = 1.0 - p1_new
            probs = np.array([
                p1_new,
                remainder * probs[1] / probs[1:].sum(),
                remainder * probs[2] / probs[1:].sum(),
            ])

        type_probs = row[["M", "E", "S", "ME", "ES"]].to_numpy(dtype=float)
        if scenario == "U2" and block in ("12:00-14:00", "14:00-16:00"):
            type_probs[[1, 4]] *= 2.0
        type_probs /= type_probs.sum()

        count = integer_n + int(fraction > 1e-9)
        for i in range(count):
            weight = 1.0 if i < integer_n else fraction
            if weight <= 0:
                continue
            priority = ["P1", "P2", "P3"][rng.choice(3, p=probs)]
            kind = ["M", "E", "S", "ME", "ES"][rng.choice(5, p=type_probs)]
            rows.append({
                "block": block,
                "arrival": block_start + rng.uniform(0, 120),
                "weight": weight,
                "priority": priority,
                "type": kind,
            })
    return rows


def draw_service(scenario, kind, rng):
    def draw(activity):
        lo, hi, tlo, thi, p = SERVICE[activity]
        if scenario == "U4" and activity in DIAGS:
            p = min(1.0, 2.0 * p)
        if rng.random() < p:
            return rng.uniform(tlo, thi)
        return rng.uniform(lo, hi)

    work = {"Intake": draw("Intake")}
    if kind == "ME":
        work.update({"Mechanical": draw("Mechanical"),
                     "Electrical": draw("Electrical")})
    elif kind == "ES":
        work.update({"Electrical": draw("Electrical"),
                     "Software": draw("Software")})
    else:
        work[{"M": "Mechanical", "E": "Electrical", "S": "Software"}[kind]] \
            = draw({"M": "Mechanical", "E": "Electrical", "S": "Software"}[kind])
    work["Validation"] = draw("Validation")
    return work


@dataclass
class Job:
    case_id: str
    arrival: float
    weight: float
    priority: str
    kind: str
    work: dict
    fraction: dict = field(default_factory=dict)
    queue_entry: dict = field(default_factory=dict)
    queue_total: float = 0.0
    queue_by_skill: dict = field(default_factory=dict)
    started_diags: list = field(default_factory=list)
    ready: bool = True
    parallel: bool = False
    first_diag_resource: str = None
    last_owner: str = None
    handover_count: int = 0
    handover_minutes: float = 0.0
    family_transfers: int = 0
    preempted: bool = False
    paused: set = field(default_factory=set)
    finish: float = None
    coord_owner: str = None

    def activities(self):
        return ["Intake"] + self.started_diags + ["Validation"]

    def next_activity(self):
        if self.fraction.get("Intake", 0) < 1:
            return "Intake"
        candidates = {
            "ME": ["Mechanical", "Electrical"],
            "ES": ["Electrical", "Software"],
            "M": ["Mechanical"],
            "E": ["Electrical"],
            "S": ["Software"],
        }[self.kind]
        for activity in candidates:
            if self.fraction.get(activity, 0) < 1:
                return activity
        return "Validation"


@dataclass
class Resource:
    name: str
    job: tuple = None
    last_family: str = None
    last_case: str = None
    worked: float = 0.0
    diagnostic_work: float = 0.0
    setup_work: float = 0.0
    coordination_work: float = 0.0
    restart_penalty: float = 0.0


class Simulator:
    def __init__(self, policy, scenario, seed):
        self.policy = policy
        self.scenario = scenario
        self.rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
        self.events = []
        self.resources = {r: Resource(r) for r in SPEED}
        self.jobs = []
        for i, item in enumerate(scenario_demand(scenario, self.rng), 1):
            work = draw_service(scenario, item["type"], self.rng)
            job = Job(
                case_id=f"{scenario}-{policy}-{seed}-{i:03d}",
                arrival=item["arrival"],
                weight=item["weight"],
                priority=item["priority"],
                kind=item["type"],
                work=work,
                fraction={a: 0.0 for a in work},
            )
            self.jobs.append(job)

    def emit(self, job, concept, lifecycle, start, end, resource):
        self.events.append({
            "case:concept:name": job.case_id,
            "concept:name": concept,
            "timestamp": timestamp(end),
            "start_timestamp": timestamp(start),
            "time:timestamp": timestamp(end),
            "org:resource": resource,
            "lifecycle": lifecycle,
            "case_weight": job.weight,
            "case_priority": job.priority,
            "case_type": job.kind,
            "queue:skill": concept.split(":", 1)[1]
                           if concept.startswith("queue:") else None,
            "branch_mode": None,
            "policy_version": self.policy,
            "scenario:shock": self.scenario,
        })

    def eligible(self, resource, activity, job, clock):
        if activity not in SPEED[resource]:
            return False
        if self.scenario == "U3" and resource == "Chen":
            return False
        if self.resources[resource].job is not None:
            return False

        # A: fixed family cells. B/C: all supplied qualifications.
        if self.policy == "A_specialized" and activity in DIAGS:
            cells = {
                "Mechanical": {"Ada", "Ben", "Deepa"},
                "Electrical": {"Ada", "Chen", "Deepa", "Farah"},
                "Software": {"Chen", "Emil"},
            }
            if resource not in cells[activity]:
                return False

        if self.policy == "C_hybrid":
            protected = False
            if activity == "Software" and resource in {"Chen", "Emil"}:
                protected = 720 <= clock < 960 and clock % 60 < 30
            if activity == "Intake" and resource == "Ada":
                protected = 480 <= clock < 720 and clock % 60 < 30
            if protected and not (
                job.priority == "P1" or
                (activity == "Software" and job.kind == "ES" and job.priority == "P1")
            ):
                return False
        return True

    def remaining(self, job):
        return sum(
            job.work[a] * max(0.0, 1.0 - job.fraction[a])
            for a in job.work
        )

    def setup_needed(self, resource, job, activity):
        if activity not in DIAGS:
            return False
        family = activity[0]
        r = self.resources[resource]
        return (r.last_family is not None and r.last_family != family
                and r.last_case != job.case_id)

    def project(self, job, resource, activity, clock):
        work = sum(
            job.work[a] * max(0.0, 1.0 - job.fraction[a])
            for a in job.work
        )
        cost = work / SPEED[resource][activity]
        if self.setup_needed(resource, job, activity):
            cost += PARAMS["setup_minutes"]
        return advance_calendar(resource, activity, clock, cost)[0]

    def band(self, job, clock):
        slack = PARAMS["sla_minutes"][job.priority] - (clock - job.arrival)
        if job.priority == "P1":
            infeasible_early_s = job.kind in {"S", "ES"} and clock < 720
            if slack < 0 or infeasible_early_s or (
                self.remaining(job) + PARAMS["p1_risk_margin_minutes"] > slack
            ):
                return 1
            return 0
        if job.priority == "P3" and clock - job.arrival >= PARAMS["p3_aging_minutes"]:
            return 1
        return {"P1": 0, "P2": 2, "P3": 3}[job.priority]

    def score(self, job, resource, activity, clock):
        projected = self.project(job, resource, activity, clock)
        if self.policy == "A_specialized":
            return projected

        queued = [
            q for q in self.jobs
            if q.ready and q is not job
            and any(self.eligible(resource, a, q, clock)
                    for a in q.work)
        ]
        pressure = sum(self.remaining(q) for q in queued) / SPEED[resource][activity]
        value = PARAMS["pool_balance_weight"] * projected + pressure
        if self.policy == "C_hybrid":
            family_transfer = (
                len(job.started_diags) > 0
                and job.started_diags[0] == "Mechanical"
                and activity in {"Electrical", "Software"}
                and job.first_diag_resource != resource
            )
            if family_transfer:
                value += PARAMS["hybrid_affinity_penalty_minutes"]
        return value

    def choose(self, job, activity, clock):
        candidates = [
            r for r in SPEED
            if self.eligible(r, activity, job, clock)
        ]
        if not candidates:
            return None
        return min(
            candidates,
            key=lambda r: (
                self.band(job, clock),
                self.score(job, r, activity, clock),
                -job.arrival,
                job.case_id,
            ),
        )

    def start(self, job, resource, activity, clock, parallel=False):
        r = self.resources[resource]
        job.ready = False
        job.parallel = parallel
        job.queue_entry[activity] = clock
        wait = max(0.0, clock - job.queue_entry[activity])
        job.queue_total += wait
        job.queue_by_skill[activity] = job.queue_by_skill.get(activity, 0.0) + wait
        self.emit(job, f"queue:{activity}", "complete",
                  job.queue_entry[activity], clock, "QUEUE")

        setup = self.setup_needed(resource, job, activity)
        self.emit(job, f"assignment:{activity}", "complete", clock, clock, resource)

        if job.last_owner != resource:
            delay = PARAMS["handover_minutes"]
            clock, worked = advance_calendar(resource, activity, clock, delay)
            job.handover_count += 1
            job.handover_minutes += worked

        if activity in DIAGS:
            if not job.started_diags:
                job.first_diag_resource = resource
            else:
                previous = job.started_diags[-1]
                if previous == "Mechanical" and activity in {"Electrical", "Software"}:
                    if job.first_diag_resource != resource:
                        clock, worked = advance_calendar(
                            resource, activity, clock,
                            PARAMS["family_transfer_minutes"]
                        )
                        job.handover_minutes += worked
                        job.family_transfers += 1

            job.started_diags.append(activity)
            if setup:
                end, productive = advance_calendar(
                    resource, activity, clock, PARAMS["setup_minutes"]
                )
                self.emit(job, "SETUP", "start", clock, end, resource)
                r.job = ("SETUP", job, activity, clock, end)
                r.setup_work += productive
                r.last_case = job.case_id
                r.last_family = activity[0]
                job.last_owner = resource
                return

            duration = job.work[activity] * SPEED[resource][activity]
            end, productive = advance_calendar(resource, activity, clock, duration)
            r.diagnostic_work += productive
            r.last_case = job.case_id
            r.last_family = activity[0]

        self.emit(job, activity, "start", clock, end if activity in DIAGS else clock,
                  resource)
        r.job = ("RUN", job, activity, clock,
                 end if activity in DIAGS else clock)
        job.last_owner = resource

    def dispatch(self, clock):
        progressed = True
        while progressed:
            progressed = False
            for job in sorted(self.jobs, key=lambda j: (j.arrival, j.case_id)):
                if not job.ready:
                    continue
                activity = job.next_activity()
                resource = self.choose(job, activity, clock)
                if resource is None:
                    continue

                activities = []
                if job.kind == "ME":
                    activities = ["Mechanical", "Electrical"]
                elif job.kind == "ES":
                    activities = ["Electrical", "Software"]
                elif job.kind in {"M", "E", "S"}:
                    activities = [{
                        "M": "Mechanical", "E": "Electrical", "S": "Software"
                    }[job.kind]]

                parallel = False
                if len(activities) > 1 and not job.started_diags:
                    parallel = all(
                        any(self.eligible(r, a, job, clock) for r in SPEED)
                        for a in activities
                    )
                    if parallel:
                        slack = PARAMS["sla_minutes"][job.priority] - clock + job.arrival
                        critical = max(
                            job.work[a] for a in activities
                        )
                        parallel = (
                            clock + critical + PARAMS["parallel_coordination_minutes"]
                            <= PARAMS["parallel_slack_fraction"] * slack
                        )

                self.start(job, resource, activity, clock, parallel=parallel)
                progressed = True

                if parallel and len(job.started_diags) == 1:
                    other = next(a for a in activities if a not in job.started_diags)
                    second = self.choose(job, other, clock)
                    if second is not None:
                        self.start(job, second, other, clock, parallel=True)
                break

    def preempt_for_arrivals(self, clock):
        arrivals = [
            j for j in self.jobs
            if j.priority == "P1" and abs(j.arrival - clock) < 1e-7
        ]
        for p1 in arrivals:
            risk = (
                clock - p1.arrival + self.remaining(p1)
                + PARAMS["p1_risk_margin_minutes"]
                >= PARAMS["sla_minutes"]["P1"]
            )
            if not risk:
                continue
            for resource_name, r in self.resources.items():
                if r.job is None:
                    continue
                kind, job, activity, start, old_end = r.job
                if kind != "RUN" or activity not in DIAGS or job.preempted:
                    continue

                other_available = any(
                    activity in SPEED[x] and x != resource_name
                    and self.resources[x].job is None
                    and not (self.scenario == "U3" and x == "Chen")
                    for x in SPEED
                )
                if not other_available:
                    continue

                f = job.fraction[activity]
                penalty = job.work[activity] * (1.0 - f) * PARAMS["restart_fraction"]
                job.fraction[activity] = min(1.0, f + PARAMS["restart_fraction"] * (1.0 - f))
                job.preempted = True
                job.paused.add(activity)
                r.restart_penalty += penalty
                self.emit(job, activity, "pause", start, clock, resource_name)
                self.emit(job, "PREEMPTION", "complete", clock, clock, resource_name)
                # r.job remains occupied. Its end time is recalculated
                # when the paused activity next receives service.

    def finish_task(self, resource_name, clock):
        r = self.resources[resource_name]
        kind, job, activity, start, end = r.job
        r.job = None

        if kind == "SETUP":
            self.emit(job, "SETUP", "complete", start, end, resource_name)
            return

        if kind == "COORD":
            r.coordination_work += max(0.0, end - start)
            self.emit(job, "COORDINATION", "complete", start, end, resource_name)
            job.ready = True
            return

        productive = max(0.0, end - start)
        r.worked += productive
        f_before = job.fraction[activity]
        if job.fraction[activity] < 1.0:
            r.diagnostic_work += productive
        job.fraction[activity] = 1.0
        self.emit(job, activity, "complete", start, end, resource_name)

        if activity == "Validation":
            job.finish = clock
            return

        if all(job.fraction[a] >= 1.0 for a in job.work if a != "Validation"):
            if len(job.started_diags) == 2:
                job.coord_owner = resource_name
                end, productive = advance_calendar(
                    resource_name, activity, clock,
                    PARAMS["parallel_coordination_minutes"]
                )
                self.emit(job, "COORDINATION", "start", clock, end, resource_name)
                r.job = ("COORD", job, activity, clock, end)
                r.coordination_work += productive
            else:
                job.ready = True
        # A nonparallel diagnostic remains paused/busy until its next
        # positive service interval completes.

    def run(self):
        if not self.jobs:
            return self.metrics([])
        clock = min(j.arrival for j in self.jobs)
        arrivals = sorted(j.arrival for j in self.jobs)

        while clock < DAY_END:
            self.preempt_for_arrivals(clock)
            self.dispatch(clock)

            completed_resources = [
                name for name, r in self.resources.items()
                if r.job is not None and r.job[4] <= clock + 1e-7
            ]
            if completed_resources:
                for name in completed_resources:
                    self.finish_task(name, clock)
                continue

            candidates = [r.job[4] for r in self.resources.values() if r.job is not None]
            candidates += [a for a in arrivals if a > clock + 1e-7]
            next_clock = min(candidates) if candidates else DAY_END
            if next_clock >= DAY_END:
                break

            for name, r in self.resources.items():
                if r.job is None or r.job[0] != "RUN":
                    continue
                kind, job, activity, start, old_end = r.job
                planned = job.work[activity] * SPEED[name][activity]
                remaining = planned * max(0.0, 1.0 - job.fraction[activity])
                new_end, worked = advance_calendar(
                    name, activity, clock, remaining
                )
                r.worked += worked
                job.fraction[activity] = min(
                    1.0, job.fraction[activity] + worked / planned
                )
                r.job = ("RUN", job, activity, clock, new_end)
            clock = next_clock

        # Cases and tasks with no completion event are right-censored.
        return self.metrics(self.events)

    def metrics(self, events):
        total_weight = sum(j.weight for j in self.jobs)
        completed = [j for j in self.jobs if j.finish is not None]
        completed_weight = sum(j.weight for j in completed)

        def weighted_rate(priority):
            cases = [j for j in self.jobs if j.priority == priority]
            met = sum(
                j.weight for j in cases
                if j.finish is not None
                and j.finish - j.arrival <= PARAMS["sla_minutes"][priority]
            )
            return met / sum(j.weight for j in cases) if cases else np.nan

        delays = [j.finish - j.arrival for j in completed]
        utilizations = np.array([
            self.resources[r].worked / capacity_minutes(r, self.scenario)
            for r in SPEED
        ])

        return {
            "policy": self.policy,
            "scenario": self.scenario,
            "arrival_weight": total_weight,
            "completed_weight": completed_weight,
            "completion_fraction": completed_weight / total_weight if total_weight else np.nan,
            "unresolved_weight": total_weight - completed_weight,
            "mean_delay": np.mean(delays) if delays else np.nan,
            "p90_delay": np.quantile(delays, 0.90) if delays else np.nan,
            "P1_SLA_all_arrivals": weighted_rate("P1"),
            "P2_SLA_all_arrivals": weighted_rate("P2"),
            "P3_SLA_all_arrivals": weighted_rate("P3"),
            "max_resource_utilization": float(utilizations.max()),
            "utilization_fairness_CV": float(
                utilizations.std(ddof=0) / utilizations.mean()
                if utilizations.mean() > 0 else np.nan
            ),
            "handover_changes_per_case": sum(
                j.weight * j.handover_count for j in self.jobs
            ) / total_weight if total_weight else np.nan,
            "handover_minutes_per_case": sum(
                j.weight * j.handover_minutes for j in self.jobs
            ) / total_weight if total_weight else np.nan,
            "family_transfers_per_case": sum(
                j.weight * j.family_transfers for j in self.jobs
            ) / total_weight if total_weight else np.nan,
            "preemptions_per_case": sum(
                j.weight * int(j.preempted) for j in self.jobs
            ) / total_weight if total_weight else np.nan,
            "setup_minutes_total": sum(r.setup_work for r in self.resources.values()),
            "coordination_minutes_total": sum(
                r.coordination_work for r in self.resources.values()
            ),
            "restart_penalty_minutes": sum(
                r.restart_penalty for r in self.resources.values()
            ),
        }
```

# 9. Limits and validation plan

## What cannot be concluded from the prompt alone

The supplied data do not establish:

- arrival variability within blocks;
- priority–type correlation;
- service-time distributions or tail dependence;
- whether service begins immediately after assignment or includes administrative work;
- actual handover/setup incidence;
- historical preemption behavior;
- task cancellation or rework;
- next-day carryover;
- demand suppression after long queues;
- quality effects from cross-skill work;
- actual operator routing behavior.

The average expected counts also do not guarantee that a queue can be cleared. In particular, the base and stressed Software workloads exceed qualified same-day capacity under the midpoint assumptions.

The policy comparison ranges are therefore hypotheses for testing, not measured performance claims. P90 values based only on completed cases are downward-biased under censoring.

## Backtest design

1. **Reconstruct historical arrivals and service.**
   - Preserve original event timestamps.
   - Separate ordinary and complex-tail observations.
   - Reconstruct qualified resources and actual calendars.
   - Identify interruptions, resumes, and whether the 10% penalty was actually charged.

2. **Backtest each policy on the same cases.**
   - Freeze arrival timestamps.
   - Use common random seeds for service and type draws.
   - Replay specialist, pool, and hybrid dispatch.
   - Include U1–U5 counterfactuals without changing underlying historical service draws.

3. **Separate model validation from policy evaluation.**
   - First compare predicted versus observed queue, service, and SLA distributions.
   - Only then compare policies.
   - Do not tune a dispatch policy until the service model is calibrated enough to reproduce queueing.

4. **Use time-block holdouts.**
   - Train on some arrival blocks and validate on others.
   - Include a shock day in the holdout.
   - Report all-priority P1, P2, and P3 results, not only a selected P1 subset.

5. **Audit interrupted and parallel cases separately.**
   - Compare critical-path saving with coordination and handoff cost.
   - Exclude censored activities from completed-service percentiles.
   - Report physical resource-minutes separately from elapsed case time.

## Safe pilot sequence

1. **Shadow mode first.** Produce recommendations without changing assignments. Operators record disagreements and hidden constraints.
2. **Pilot the hybrid’s P1 reservations and overflow trigger first.** Do not introduce broader preemption in the same change.
3. **Limit preemption to audited, feasible P1 cases.** Compare actual P1 and P2 outcomes; a P1 gain is not sufficient if P2 deterioration is material.
4. **Expand dynamic pooling only if robustness improves.** Include a full-pool counterfactual in the same evaluation.
5. **Use a go/no-go rule based on uncertainty.** Do not roll out because one seed has a better mean. Require no material P1 deterioration, bounded P90/censoring, and improved performance under resampled surge/absence scenarios.
6. **Escalate structural failures rather than tuning them away.** If U1/U5 remains dominated by missing Software capacity, the intervention is capacity or SLA redesign, not another dispatch weight.

Finally, validate that reported resource utilization is not gamed by leaving work unassigned. A policy that achieves low utilization by failing to start cases is not robust. Completion coverage, unfinished work, and oldest-case age must accompany every utilization and delay result.
