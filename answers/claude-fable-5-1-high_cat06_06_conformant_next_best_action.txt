## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

Four loss channels are compared: (1) deadline loss €20k–€35k (only realised as a *benefit* if the case is also approved, so its expected value is at most 0.75 × €35k ≈ €26k); (2) false rejection / prolonged hold €15k–€80k; (3) regulatory harm from unlawful execution €100k–€500k plus non-monetary consequences; (4) spillover to P-901 of the same order as (1).

Dominance arguments rather than a single utility:

- **A and B are strictly dominated.** Their maximum attainable benefit (€35k) is smaller than the *minimum* of the regulatory harm range (€100k), before weighting by any probability and before the non-monetary tail (licence conditions, personal liability, loss of the four-eyes control's credibility). The prompt itself describes the harm as highly asymmetric; no realistic probability of "getting away with it" reverses a comparison in which the best case of one side is below the worst case of the other. Non-monetary regulatory harm is treated lexicographically: it is not traded against operational loss at all.
- **Among conformant options, hold dominates rejection now.** With approval probability 0.55–0.75, rejecting now has an expected customer harm of roughly €8k–€60k and forfeits the deadline value anyway; waiting for a second review costs a probability-weighted deadline loss of about €9k–€23k and a small hold harm, while keeping a 55–75% chance of the right outcome. F only becomes attractive if the re-screen or the second officer produces evidence that pushes approval probability well below 0.5.
- **C versus Plan D+ is close and hinges on the portfolio.** C buys roughly a 0.15–0.25 reduction in P-884's breach probability (≈€4k–€9k expected, after discounting by approval probability) at the price of +0.12–0.28 breach probability for P-901 (≈€2k–€10k if P-901's deadline value is of similar magnitude). At institution level this is approximately a transfer of loss between two customers, not a reduction, and it degrades the quality of an interrupted control. That is why the unconditional interruption ranks second and is kept as a conditional fallback.
- **E** carries a near-certain deadline loss and hold harm, but zero compliance risk and the best information; it is dominated as the *next* action because a second officer will be available in 35–65 minutes, but it is exactly the right branch if that officer wants more evidence.
- **G** has expected value close to or below zero: low probability of a valid override, certain consumption of time and director attention, and a tail in which a manufactured incident creates channel (3) exposure.
- **Uncertainty about intervention effects.** The 0.48–0.64 breach forecast and 0.55–0.75 approval forecast are observational: they were learned on cases that went through the normal review. Skipping the review (A/B) does not "inherit" the 0.55–0.75 approval rate; that rate is a property of reviewed cases. Interrupting S2 (C) changes review duration and thoroughness in ways the historical data did not sample. Plan D+ intervenes least on the process itself, so its forecast transports best.

## 4. Conformance constraints

Rule mapping:

- **Rule 6 (second review when risk >= 70 or fuzzy match unresolved):** binds now on both grounds. Violated by A (no second review) and B (no *second* reviewer). Satisfied by C and by Plan D+ only when S2/S4 actually review. Not changed by D alone, by an SAR, or by a re-score unless *both* the score falls below 70 and the match is resolved and documented; even then, choosing to skip a review at a borderline value minutes before a deadline should be documented as a deliberate, evidence-based decision, not a threshold-gaming outcome.
- **Rule 7 (four-eyes):** violated by B outright. C and Plan D+ satisfy it because S2/S4 ≠ R17; the condition attached to C ("independently reviews") is exactly the substantive content of rule 7.
- **Rule 8 (decisions only after required reviews; SAR does not authorise execution):** constrains Plan D+, C and F. In branch (c) a Hold + SAR is the expected outcome; filing the report must not be treated as clearing the payment.
- **Rule 9 (execution only after a valid approval path):** violated by A, B and by any invalid G. The Approve → Payment Executed step in Plan D+ and C is conformant only after the second officer's recorded approval.
- **Rule 10 (override only with Compliance Director authorisation and valid incident identifier):** G is not enabled; both conditions are currently false.
- **Rule 11 (no SAR disclosure):** constrains every customer communication in every plan, including delay notices and a rejection message under F.
- **Rule 5 (evidence cycle):** E is a legitimate repetition of the cycle; Plan D+ can lead into it.
- **Rule 2 (screening):** D is a supplementary screening; it adds evidence but is not itself a decision step.

**Why A and B cannot be rescued by a favourable forecast.** Rules 6, 7 and 9 are deontic constraints, not cost terms: they define which traces are valid, and a valid trace is a precondition for execution rather than one input to a trade-off. Three further points: (i) the approval forecast of 0.55–0.75 is a prediction of what the *review* would conclude — it presupposes the review and cannot replace it; the control exists precisely for the 25–45% of cases in which the forecast would have been wrong. (ii) The forecast is observational; under the intervention "execute without review" the population changes and the estimate does not transport. (iii) Even taking the forecast at face value, the best-case benefit is below the worst-case regulatory harm (§3), so there is no numerical value of the forecast that would make A or B preferable. B is additionally worse than A because it records a control as performed when it was not, so it damages the integrity of the evidence base the process relies on.

**What would make F valid.** A documented, policy-defined evidence threshold for rejection that is actually met by the current record (for example, a fuzzy-match confidence above a defined rejection cut-off, an adverse re-screen result, or evidence the supplier is the listed party), a recorded rationale by the reviewer the policy designates for rejection decisions (if the policy requires the second officer's involvement for rejections too, R17 cannot reject alone), and a customer communication that discloses nothing about any report. Until those exist, F is a guess dressed as a control.

**What would make G valid.** A real, independently verifiable incident (system outage, force-majeure event, regulator instruction) with an identifier generated in the incident-management system for that reason; explicit authorisation by a Compliance Director recorded against P-884; and a record showing why the normal path could not be completed. A commercial deadline does not qualify, and creating an identifier to fit the payment is itself a violation.

## 5. Resource spillovers

**S2 versus S4.** S2 is available only by interrupting P-901 (70-minute deadline, high value). A 45-minute interruption raises P-901's breach probability by 0.12–0.28; S2's review of P-884 could run 35–75 minutes, so the realistic interruption may exceed 45 minutes, pushing spillover above the stated range. S4 arrives in 35–65 minutes with no spillover. Plan D+ uses the wait productively: the re-screen (15–30 min) completes before S4 can start, so the wait costs little information-wise, and S2 may finish P-901 naturally inside the same window, giving a second officer without any interruption. Whoever starts first reviews; the other stays on their own queue.

**Effect on P-901.** Interrupting at T0 leaves P-901 about 25 minutes of slack after S2 returns; interrupting at the T+30 checkpoint leaves ~40 minutes before P-901's deadline with a 45-minute absence — S2 would not return before P-901's deadline, so a late interruption is materially worse for P-901 than the 0.12–0.28 estimate. Therefore the fallback to C should only be exercised late if P-901's owner confirms P-901 is already complete, already breached, or has a soft deadline; otherwise the honest choice is to accept P-884's probable breach conformantly.

**API quota.** Three expedited calls remain. Plan D+ spends one, leaving two for P-901 or other high-risk cases today. A second call on P-884 should not be spent to "shop" for a clean result; a discordant first result is information for the second officer, not a reason to re-roll.

**Waiting for an exception.** G consumes 20–60+ minutes of the 90 with a low probability of a valid outcome and, if pursued instead of reserving an officer, can leave P-884 at T+60 with no override, no second review started, and the S4 window partly wasted. At most, a one-line heads-up to the Director that a high-value case may breach is acceptable; it must not displace the conformant path.

**Sensitivity to S4's arrival time.**

- *S4 at ≈35 minutes:* Plan D+ clearly dominates C. S4 starts just as the re-screen lands; execution is plausible around T+65–100; breach probability ≈0.4–0.55 versus ≈0.3–0.45 for C, a gain too small to justify P-901's extra 0.12–0.28. Ranking unchanged.
- *S4 at ≈65 minutes:* the S4 path cannot execute before roughly T+95–140; the deadline is effectively lost on that path. The decision then has to be taken *early* (by T+15, when the ETA is known), not at T+30, because a late interruption is worse for P-901. If P-901's owner confirms tolerance (P-901 nearly complete or its own deadline negotiable), C with D in parallel moves to rank 1 and Plan D+ becomes the fallback. If P-901 cannot tolerate it, Plan D+ stays at rank 1 with the deadline accepted as probably lost, and the mitigation shifts to renegotiating the customer deadline (permitted; no report disclosure) — E and F are unchanged in position.

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence

**Primary recommendation:** Plan D+. At T0, launch the alternate screening (one API call), refresh the risk score, reserve the earliest independent second officer (S4, or S2 as soon as P-901 completes without interruption), have R17 assemble the review package, and inform the customer generically that compliance processing is continuing. Second Officer Review by S4 or S2 with the re-screen result in hand; Approve → Payment Executed only after the recorded approval; Hold + SAR if the match is confirmed; Evidence Requested (Action E) if the officer needs more.

**Fallback:** Action C — interrupt P-901 for an immediate independent review by S2 — exercised only if (a) by T+15 S4's confirmed ETA is ≥55 minutes and P-901's owner confirms the interruption is tolerable, or (b) at the T+30 checkpoint no officer is confirmed to start by T+45 *and* P-901 is already complete or its deadline is soft. If neither holds, accept the probable deadline breach conformantly rather than execute A, B or an invalid G. Reject (F) only if the re-screen or second officer produces evidence meeting the documented rejection threshold.

**Confidence:** high (≈0.85) that ranks 5–7 belong at the bottom and that a conformant independent second review is the correct strategy; medium (≈0.6) on the ordering of Plan D+ above C, because it depends on S4's actual ETA and on P-901's true sensitivity, both of which are ranges rather than observations.

**Trigger for immediate reranking:** the first of these observations — S4's confirmed ETA (≥55 minutes flips the primary/fallback order if P-901 can tolerate it); S2 completing P-901 early (removes the spillover issue entirely and makes the second review start immediately); the alternate screening result (a confirmed match moves the case to Hold + SAR and makes F/hold the primary, a clear non-match shortens the review); the refreshed risk score falling below 70 together with a resolved match (changes rule 6's applicability, to be documented, not exploited); or the customer agreeing to extend the deadline (removes most of the pressure that made C attractive).
