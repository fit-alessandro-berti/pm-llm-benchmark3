## 2. Raw-versus-Normalized Interpretation

The raw-to-mapped fitness gap (e.g., 0.55  0.9 in March) primarily reflects **logging/semantic drift**: the system migration introduced label changes (`Eligibility Screen` vs. `Validate Eligibility`) and duplicated `Open Case` events, which violate the normative model but are artifacts of data capture, not process behavior. Label mapping recovers conformance because the *intended* control flow is preserved, but this does *not* imply the business process was unchanged—only that the *observed* deviations were largely syntactic.

Duplicated technical events (e.g., `Open Case`) and missing resources distort **temporal and organizational analyses** (e.g., queueing metrics may misattribute delays to phantom events). However, the post-May DFG and performance patterns (e.g., rising `Assess->RequestInfo` rates, longer cycle times) cannot be dismissed as logging drift alone because:
1. The mapped fitness remains depressed (0.86–0.88 vs. 0.92 baseline), suggesting *real* control-flow changes (e.g., more rework).
2. Performance metrics (cycle time, queueing) are robust to label mapping but correlate with known disruptions (Supplier Z, staffing cuts).
3. Seasonal and priority-driven effects (e.g., `high_complexity_pct` spikes in October) are *population* shifts, not logging artifacts.

Thus, while March’s fitness collapse is mostly semantic, later drift reflects **behavioral, population, and resource mechanisms**.

## 9. Provisional Attribution

The **March fitness collapse** is **almost certainly logging/semantic drift** (confidence: 95%) due to the system migration, as evidenced by the recovery of `mapped_model_fitness` to ~0.9 and the appearance of new labels (`Eligibility Screen`) and duplicated `Open Case` events. The **persistent 5–10pp gap in mapped fitness** (vs. baseline 0.92) suggests **minor behavioral drift** (e.g., inconsistent `Senior Assessment` logging) but is not strong evidence of major process changes.

The **May–July cycle-time deterioration** is **primarily driven by Supplier Z’s disruption** (confidence: 85%) and **staffing cuts** (confidence: 80%). Supplier Z’s incomplete bundles directly increased rework (`rework_variant_pct` +24pp), while staffing cuts exacerbated queueing (median cycle time +40%). The **priority rule (July)** likely **redistributed delays** (high-complexity cases faster, standard cases slower), but this is **plausible but unproven** (confidence: 60%) due to aggregation.

The **October–November spike in complexity and cycle time** is **seasonal population drift** (confidence: 85%), consistent with historical renewal patterns. However, the **December improvement** exceeds typical seasonality, suggesting the **overtime and document-quality campaign** had a **real but modest effect** (confidence: 70%).

**Interaction and mediation** complicate attribution:
- Supplier Z’s disruption **increased rework**, which **worsened queueing** under staffing constraints.
- The priority rule may have **masked** the full impact of staffing cuts on high-complexity cases.
- Missing resources post-migration **obscure** team-specific effects.

**Revisions needed with more data**:
- Event-level logs to segment by supplier, complexity, and priority.
- Pre-2026 baselines to quantify seasonal effects.
- Resource-specific metrics to isolate staffing impacts.

**Strongest conclusions**: March logging drift; Supplier Z  rework; staffing  queueing.
**Plausible but uncertain**: Priority rule’s delay redistribution; campaign effectiveness.
