# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

All durations are in minutes. For case \(c\), write arrival \(a_c\), fork release \(r_c\), branch start and end \(s_{ci}, e_{ci}\), and final start and end \(f_c, g_c\), with \(i\in\{A,B,C\}\).

- Upstream elapsed: \(U_c=r_c-a_c\). This is not necessarily all queueing.
- Branch queue: \(Q_{ci}=s_{ci}-r_c\); branch service: \(S_{ci}=e_{ci}-s_{ci}\); branch release-to-completion: \(L_{ci}=Q_{ci}+S_{ci}=e_{ci}-r_c\).
- Join readiness: \(J_c=\max_i e_{ci}\); elapsed fork–join stage: \(T_c=J_c-r_c=\max_i L_{ci}\). A critical branch has \(e_{ci}=J_c\); ties are possible, although none occur here.
- Branch synchronization wait: \(W_{ci}=J_c-e_{ci}=T_c-L_{ci}\).
- Downstream elapsed waiting: \(D_c=f_c-J_c\), interpreted as a final-station queue only if readiness immediately makes the case eligible for service and no other release rule applies. Final service: \(H_c=g_c-f_c\).
- Total flow: \(G_c=g_c-a_c=U_c+T_c+D_c+H_c\).

For any **one** branch, \(T_c=Q_{ci}+S_{ci}+W_{ci}\). Branch timelines overlap, so their queues, services and synchronization waits are descriptive branch-level quantities, not three additive pieces of case elapsed time. Indeed, \(\sum_i(Q_{ci}+S_{ci}+W_{ci})=3T_c\). Even summing synchronization waits alone counts overlapping intervals multiple times: in F02, A waits 10 minutes and C waits 35 minutes, with A's entire wait contained in C's. Their 45 branch-minutes cover only 35 distinct minutes on the clock. Adding those 45 minutes to the critical branch's 60-minute path double counts time already on that path.

# 3. Aggregate critical-path analysis

Each row describes 12 cases. Span is queue plus service from fork release; the stage is the maximum span. Maximums provide a transparent small-sample tail description.

| Branch | Service mean (min–max) | Queue mean (min–max) | Span mean / max | Last-completing cases | Mean synchronization wait | Influence assessment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| A | 50.00 (50–50) | 0.00 (0–0) | 50.00 / 50 | 2/12 | 11.00 | Second by this sample's stage influence; long service creates a common floor. |
| B | 37.50 (30–45) | 19.17 (5–30) | 56.67 / 75 | 8/12 | 4.33 | First: most frequently critical, with the largest mean and maximum span. |
| C | 20.17 (18–30) | 10.42 (5–35) | 30.58 / 57 | 2/12 | 30.42 | Third overall, but its queue creates two critical cases. |

A and C tie in critical frequency, so their ordering depends on the measure: A's span is larger on average, while C's exceptional queues control F04 and F08. B controls both 75-minute stages, F07 and F12, which also have the longest total flows, 130 and 125 minutes. A's service is longest on average, but its zero observed queue leaves it less often critical than B. C's long mean synchronization wait is mostly time spent finished, not evidence of slow C service.

Mean upstream, stage, downstream waiting and final service are respectively 19.58, 61.00, 14.67 and 20.00 minutes, summing to mean flow 115.25. These describe this small sample; they are not proof of causation. The counterfactuals provide an intervention-specific comparison.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

These are **mechanical scenarios**, not forecasts. For branch changes, assume the targeted queue or service changes as specified, all other branch timestamps and upstream duration stay fixed, and final service starts earlier by the resulting advancement of join readiness, retaining that case's original downstream wait and final service duration. This isolates max-based effects but does not reschedule shared resources or establish feasibility. Fractional minutes are allowed as model outputs despite minute-resolution observations.

For the final-station scenario, assume a redesign halves each observed downstream wait, with branch completions and final service unchanged. Extra capacity is one possible means, but the reduction is not a demonstrated consequence of adding a resource. Savings are in minutes; scenarios are independent and not combined.

| Case | A service −20% | B queue −50% | Downstream wait −50% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| F01 | 5 | 0 | 4 |
| F02 | 0 | 10 | 7.5 |
| F03 | 0 | 10 | 7.5 |
| F04 | 0 | 0 | 9 |
| F05 | 0 | 10 | 5 |
| F06 | 0 | 15 | 7.5 |
| F07 | 0 | 15 | 7.5 |
| F08 | 0 | 0 | 7.5 |
| F09 | 0 | 12.5 | 7.5 |
| F10 | 0 | 5 | 10 |
| F11 | 10 | 0 | 10 |
| F12 | 0 | 15 | 5 |
| **Mean** | **1.25** | **7.71** | **7.33** |
| **Total across cases** | **15** | **92.5** | **88** |
| **Cases with positive saving** | **2** | **8** | **12** |
| **Maximum resulting flow** | **130** | **115** | **122.5** |

A's 10-minute service reduction saves only 5 minutes in F01 because B then becomes last; it saves all 10 in F11, where A ties B after the change. It saves nothing in the other ten cases because B or C remains critical. B's queue reduction benefits its eight critical cases, capped when another branch catches up: F10 has 10 minutes removed from B's queue but only 5 minutes of stage benefit because A becomes critical. F01, F04, F08 and F11 gain nothing from reducing B's queue because another branch remains last. The downstream scenario directly reduces every case's measured wait by half; it has no branch-criticality restriction but assumes the interval is reducible waiting.

**Masking and feasibility:** If final starts instead remain fixed at observed times, every modeled branch improvement is absorbed into a longer downstream interval and total-flow savings are zero. Actual outcomes depend on scheduling, capacity and policies and can differ from either illustrative assumption; these are not guaranteed bounds. Even the fixed-wait scenario can be infeasible with recorded assignments: advancing F11's final start by 10 minutes to 11:40 overlaps F09 on F1, which runs until 11:50. The downstream-halving scenario also makes F09 and F11 overlap on F1. Conversely, capacity contention is not established as the sole cause of observed waiting: F01 is ready at 09:00 while both recorded final resources appear idle until its 09:08 start, subject to unobserved work or availability. Changes to branch timing can shift demand and synchronization to other branches; final capacity can change dispatch order, utilization and congestion. These secondary effects require a schedule-aware model or pilot.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

1. **Pilot reduction of B's queue**, examining dispatch, availability and handoffs at the stress-test step. The mechanical B scenario has the largest modeled mean benefit and improves the two longest stages; it also reduces synchronization waiting for A and C when B is last. Earlier B completion can make A or C critical, and higher B service utilization or added B capacity can shift congestion to F. Faster case completion alone does not prove higher sustainable throughput. Verify B resource availability, arrivals and waiting reasons, then measure completions per operating hour, B queue and service distributions, resource occupancy, critical-branch switches and downstream waits in a comparable pilot with schedule-aware analysis.

2. **Pilot a final-station dispatch or capacity change after diagnosing the downstream interval.** The half-wait scenario improves all 12 cases and reduces the sample's maximum flow, but capacity alone may not remove policy or scheduling delays. More capacity may lower F utilization per resource while potentially increasing throughput if F is constraining; otherwise it may be underused. It does not shorten the fork–join stage or branch synchronization waits, and B or occasional C queues may become binding. Measure readiness-to-start intervals, both resources' actual availability and occupancy including other work, dispatch rules, throughput and high-percentile flow before and during the pilot. Compare policy changes and added capacity under the same workload, watching for a new branch bottleneck or uneven resource use.

# 6. Reproducible Python

The code requires pandas and PM4Py. It prints per-case decomposition, branch summaries and counterfactuals, and PM4Py service-time summaries. All tied critical branches are retained in A/B/C order.

```python
from io import StringIO
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

raw = """case arrival fork_release A_start A_end B_start B_end C_start C_end final_start final_end final_resource
F01 07:55 08:10 08:10 09:00 08:15 08:55 08:20 08:38 09:08 09:28 F1
F02 08:05 08:25 08:25 09:15 08:45 09:25 08:30 08:50 09:40 10:00 F2
F03 08:20 08:40 08:40 09:30 09:00 09:45 08:45 09:03 10:00 10:20 F1
F04 08:35 08:55 08:55 09:45 09:05 09:35 09:30 09:52 10:10 10:30 F2
F05 08:50 09:10 09:10 10:00 09:35 10:10 09:15 09:35 10:20 10:40 F1
F06 09:05 09:25 09:25 10:15 09:55 10:30 09:40 10:00 10:45 11:05 F2
F07 09:20 09:40 09:40 10:30 10:10 10:55 09:45 10:03 11:10 11:30 F1
F08 09:35 09:55 09:55 10:45 10:00 10:30 10:20 10:50 11:05 11:25 F2
F09 09:50 10:10 10:10 11:00 10:35 11:15 10:15 10:35 11:30 11:50 F1
F10 10:05 10:25 10:25 11:15 10:45 11:20 10:30 10:50 11:40 12:00 F2
F11 10:20 10:40 10:40 11:30 10:50 11:20 10:45 11:03 11:50 12:10 F1
F12 10:35 10:55 10:55 11:45 11:25 12:10 11:00 11:18 12:20 12:40 F2
"""
df = pd.read_csv(StringIO(raw), sep=r"\s+")
time_columns = [c for c in df.columns if c not in ("case", "final_resource")]
for column in time_columns:
    df[column] = pd.to_datetime("2026-05-19 " + df[column])
branches = ("A", "B", "C")

def minutes(delta):
    return delta.dt.total_seconds() / 60.0

# Readiness, branch queues and synchronization waits are custom pandas
# calculations: a directly-follows performance graph orders events and
# does not by itself recover parallel synchronization waiting.
out = pd.DataFrame({"case": df["case"]})
out["upstream_elapsed"] = minutes(df["fork_release"] - df["arrival"])
ends = pd.DataFrame({b: df[b + "_end"] for b in branches})
join = ends.max(axis=1)
for b in branches:
    out[b + "_queue"] = minutes(df[b + "_start"] - df["fork_release"])
    out[b + "_service"] = minutes(df[b + "_end"] - df[b + "_start"])
    out[b + "_span"] = minutes(df[b + "_end"] - df["fork_release"])
    out[b + "_sync"] = minutes(join - df[b + "_end"])
out["join_ready"] = join
out["critical_branch"] = [
    "/".join(b for b in branches if ends.at[idx, b] == join.at[idx])
    for idx in df.index
]
out["fork_join_stage"] = minutes(join - df["fork_release"])
out["downstream_queue"] = minutes(df["final_start"] - join)
out["final_service"] = minutes(df["final_end"] - df["final_start"])
out["total_flow_time"] = minutes(df["final_end"] - df["arrival"])
assert (out["downstream_queue"] >= 0).all()
assert (out["total_flow_time"] == out["upstream_elapsed"] +
        out["fork_join_stage"] + out["downstream_queue"] +
        out["final_service"]).all()
for b in branches:
    assert (out[b + "_queue"] + out[b + "_service"] + out[b + "_sync"]
            == out["fork_join_stage"]).all()

summary = []
for b in branches:
    summary.append({
        "branch": b,
        "service_mean": out[b + "_service"].mean(),
        "service_min": out[b + "_service"].min(),
        "service_max": out[b + "_service"].max(),
        "queue_mean": out[b + "_queue"].mean(),
        "queue_min": out[b + "_queue"].min(),
        "queue_max": out[b + "_queue"].max(),
        "span_mean": out[b + "_span"].mean(),
        "span_max": out[b + "_span"].max(),
        # Every tied branch counts; counts can sum to more than cases.
        "critical_cases": sum(b in value.split("/") for value in out["critical_branch"]),
        "sync_mean": out[b + "_sync"].mean(),
    })

# Independent scenarios: other branches, downstream wait and final service
# are fixed for branch interventions; no resource rescheduling is modeled.
a_ends = ends.copy()
a_ends["A"] -= pd.to_timedelta(0.20 * out["A_service"], unit="min")
b_ends = ends.copy()
b_ends["B"] -= pd.to_timedelta(0.50 * out["B_queue"], unit="min")
savings = pd.DataFrame({
    "case": df["case"],
    "A_service_20pct": minutes(join - a_ends.max(axis=1)),
    "B_queue_50pct": minutes(join - b_ends.max(axis=1)),
    "downstream_50pct": 0.50 * out["downstream_queue"],
})
scenario_cols = list(savings.columns[1:])
scenario_summary = pd.DataFrame({
    "mean_saving": savings[scenario_cols].mean(),
    "total_saving": savings[scenario_cols].sum(),
    "positive_cases": (savings[scenario_cols] > 0).sum(),
    "max_new_flow": [(out["total_flow_time"] - savings[col]).max()
                     for col in scenario_cols],
})

# One interval event per branch/final activity. Branch resources are not
# supplied; only F's resource is recorded. Timestamps use the given date.
records = []
for _, row in df.iterrows():
    for b in branches:
        records.append({"case_id": row["case"], "activity": b,
                        "start_timestamp": row[b + "_start"],
                        "time:timestamp": row[b + "_end"],
                        "org:resource": None})
    records.append({"case_id": row["case"], "activity": "F",
                    "start_timestamp": row["final_start"],
                    "time:timestamp": row["final_end"],
                    "org:resource": row["final_resource"]})
interval_events = pd.DataFrame.from_records(records)
formatted = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    interval_events, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp", start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp"
)
# Explicit start key matters: PM4Py otherwise may use the completion key.
# Reported mean activity service times are in seconds.
service_seconds = pm4py.get_service_time(
    formatted, start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp"
)

pd.set_option("display.max_columns", None)
pd.set_option("display.width", 240)
pd.set_option("display.float_format", lambda x: f"{x:.2f}")
print("CASE DECOMPOSITION (minutes)")
print(out.to_string(index=False))
print("\nBRANCH SUMMARY (minutes)")
print(pd.DataFrame(summary).to_string(index=False))
print("\nCOUNTERFACTUAL SAVINGS BY CASE (minutes)")
print(savings.to_string(index=False))
print("\nCOUNTERFACTUAL SUMMARY (minutes)")
print(scenario_summary.to_string())
print("\nPM4PY MEAN SERVICE TIMES (seconds)")
print(service_seconds)
```

# 7. Limits of inference

These 12 cases describe one day; they do not establish representative rates, stable tail behavior or causal effects of a redesign. Timestamps show elapsed intervals, not delay reasons. Branch resource identities, capacities, calendars, availability, other workload and dispatch rules are missing, as are final-station off-log work and availability. An apparently idle final resource may be unavailable or serving an unrecorded case.

Start and end timestamps do not reveal pauses, preemption, rework or productive work within a service interval; a measured branch queue may include release constraints or other nonqueue time. `fork_release` must truly make every branch eligible, and the latest logged completion must be the actual join condition, for the decomposition to have the stated operational meaning. Final waiting could reflect batching, authorization, intentional release policy, missing prerequisites or recording conventions rather than a capacity queue. Minute precision and timestamp ordering limit certainty about short differences and ties. The counterfactuals assume changes to durations without accounting for shared-resource interactions, altered arrivals, service variability or changed routing; a pilot or validated resource-aware simulation is needed to estimate those effects.
