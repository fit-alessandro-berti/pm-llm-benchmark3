# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

**Working definitions grounded in this case:**

- **Resilience** is a *deliberate, bounded* degradation that preserves the critical function (hospital supply) **and** preserves or substitutes the critical controls, produces evidence in real time, and is reversible through planned reconciliation. It accepts slower, smaller, more expensive service — not absent controls.
- **Concealed noncompliance** is a deviation that removes a regulatory-mandated control *without a compensating control*, leaves no contemporaneous evidence, and surfaces only later as audit-remediation workload. Its defining feature is that the process *looked* complete while its assurance content was hollow.

**True resilience here:** B (manual authorization by trained staff), F (100% pharmacist release for controlled substances), I (explicit deferral to protect capacity), J (prioritized backlog drain), K (reconciliation as corrective action), D *on Day 0 only* (fresh snapshot), and A *insofar as* it accelerated logistics rather than skipped authorization. These deviations traded time and cost, not control integrity.

**Risky shortcuts:** C (unverified authorization — the purest concealed noncompliance in the log), G (dispatch without scan — the largest evidence destruction, ~3,250 parcels), and H *for controlled products* (phone confirmation cannot establish who received a narcotic). The conformance data indicts them precisely: these appear as **move-on-log** deviations — behaviors actively inserted by actors — whereas missing scans, temperature records, and PODs appear as **move-on-model** deviations — steps that *could not be executed* because the enabling system was gone. The first category reflects choices; the second reflects forced degradation. Choices deserve removal; forced gaps deserve designed substitutes.

**Ambiguous, risk-appetite-dependent:** A (acceptable only if the authorization gate is never crossed), D after Day 0 (staleness converts a tool into a hazard), E (resilient only if reconstruction fidelity is high — 12.5% rework says it wasn't), H for standard products (commercially tolerable, evidentially weak), and L (necessary containment, but a full remediation queue is itself evidence that real-time compliance failed).

**The throughput trap:** T2 volume (~2,240/day) matched or exceeded baseline while only 44% of traces conformed. Throughput was preserved by spending control margin — the organization kept pushing cases through instead of throttling intake. By the stated constraint, that is not resilience; it is risk transfer to patients (undetected excursions), regulators (unauthorized supply), and the future team that built the 2,300-case remediation queue.

# 4. Recovery process model

Proposed **Degraded Mode Operating Procedure (DMOP)** — a pre-authorized alternate process that activates on trigger and runs until a formal exit gate closes.

### Trigger conditions
- **L1 (Partial):** WMS down 30 min but intake/scanning alive  paper evidence for WMS-dependent steps only.
- **L2 (Full outage):** WMS + scanning + authorization unavailable  full DMOP.
- **L3 (Data-integrity compromise):** any forensic indication of data manipulation  L2 **plus** revalidation of all standing authorizations and physical counts before trusting restored data (directly addressing the T3 hybrid-state danger).

### Triage rules (published, not improvised)
- Priority: Hospital-critical  Pharmacy-standard  Wholesale-bulk, with a **hard floor**: pharmacy fill rate may not fall below 80% of its baseline while hospital protection is active.
- Within segment: controlled-substance and cold-chain orders route to compliance-gated paths regardless of urgency; recall-sensitive orders get double batch capture.
- Fast lanes may **parallelize** steps (pick while authorizing) but dispatch is blocked until authorization + release evidence exist.

### Minimum control floor by product/customer risk

| Class | Authorization | Pick/pack | Release | Dispatch/POD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Controlled substance | Named-checker manual auth, 100% | Dual sign-off, line-item count | Pharmacist 100%, no sampling | Tamper-evident, named recipient + ID captured; **no phone-only POD** |
| Cold chain | Manual auth, 100% | Pre-conditioned shipper, logger serial recorded | 100% temperature-doc check at pack + dispatch | Receiver confirms condition + temp readout |
| Recall-sensitive | Manual auth | Batch/expiry captured twice (pick + pack) | Sampled release + batch verification | Parcel-level batch-linked POD |
| Ambient (hospital fast lane) | Manual auth (parallel, gated) | Standard | Checklist signed by responsible person | Signed manifest or photo POD |
| Ambient (standard) | Simplified documented auth | Standard | Sampled release | Phone/photo POD permitted |

### Manual evidence capture
Pre-positioned contingency kits: carbonless triplicate forms (order/pick/dispatch-POD) with **mandatory lot/expiry fields**, unique incident case IDs that bridge to system records later, courier manifests with per-parcel signatures, and a standalone (WMS-independent) photo/upload channel. Rule: **every control performed manually must generate its evidence at the moment of performance**, never reconstructed.

### Backlog prioritization
Score = customer criticality × product risk × age × clinical urgency; rubric published in advance; deferral register reviewed every 24h with customer notification and a maximum deferral age before cancel-or-expedite decision.

### Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow (exit-gated)
On system restoration: freeze-and-count high-risk zones first (cold rooms, controlled-substance cage)  reconcile manual reservations  three-way match (paper form  spreadsheet  system)  scored backlog drain  remediation queue drain. **Incident is not closed** until: remediation queue < agreed threshold AND traceability/dual-control/temperature exception rates 1.5× baseline for 5 consecutive days.

### Compact notation (POWL-style; explained)
Operators: `` strict sequence; `×` exclusive choice; `` parallel; `` loop; `[GATE cond]` blocking gateway.

```
DMOP =  ( TRIGGER_EVAL  ×(L1, L2, L3)  TRIAGE_REGISTER 
         ( FULFILLMENT: ×(CONTROLLED_PATH, COLDCHAIN_PATH,
                            RECALL_PATH, FASTLANE_AMBIENT, STANDARD_AMBIENT)
                          -- each path = AUTH_FLOOR  PICK_EVIDENCE
                              RELEASE_FLOOR  [GATE evidence-complete]  DISPATCH_EVIDENCE  POD_FLOOR
            DEFERRAL:   DEFER_NONCRITICAL  DEFERRAL_REGISTER  (24h REVIEW) ) )
RESTORE = FREEZE_COUNT_HIGH_RISK  RECONCILE_RESERVATIONS  THREE_WAY_MATCH
           SCORED_BACKLOG_DRAIN  REMEDIATION_DRAIN
           [GATE queue<threshold  exceptions1.5×baseline 5d]  POST_INCIDENT_REVIEW
```

Each `*_FLOOR` is instantiated from the control-floor table; the GATE makes "dispatch before evidence" structurally impossible rather than merely discouraged.

# 5. Resilience indicators

**Leading (during incident — predict trouble before it lands):**
- *Control completeness:* real-time dual-control completion for controlled substances (target 100%); temperature-logger attachment rate for cold-chain dispatches (100%); manual-authorization evidence captured within 1h of receipt (98%) — measured as "evidence existed at time of dispatch," not "evidence exists now."
- *Evidence freshness:* snapshot age in hours by zone (alarm >4h); evidence-capture completeness per parcel at dispatch.
- *Service protection:* fill rate by segment, daily; **fairness ratio** = segment fill ÷ its own baseline (alarm if pharmacy ratio <0.85 while hospital >0.95 — exactly the T2 failure).
- *Backlog age:* p95 age of deferred orders (<48h); deferral register size.
- *Capacity:* compliance-checker queue depth (the B bottleneck made visible).

**Lagging (after recovery — verify true restoration):**
- *Traceability:* exception-rate recovery slope toward 0.8% baseline; mock-recall drill time-to-full-lineage.
- *Throughput:* cases/day vs. baseline band — reported **only alongside** control-completeness, never alone.
- *Hidden workload:* remediation queue depth and age; % closed within SLA; reconciliation hours per 1,000 disrupted cases (T4 showed ~14% of cases touching cleanup flows — this should trend to <3%).
- *Rework:* correction rate by cause code (auth / batch / temp / address).
- *Quality tails:* p90 cycle time by segment vs. baseline ratio (bulk p90 was still 120h vs. 72h baseline at T4).
- *Fairness:* cancellation and fill-rate deltas by segment; complaint rate from deferred customers.

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should accept — and pre-authorize — slower cycle times, deferred bulk orders, sampled release for low-risk ambient products, and paper-based evidence, provided that evidence is captured in real time and reconciled within SLA; that is the honest price of resilience, and this incident proves it is affordable (hospital-critical service survived at 91% fill with a +2h median). Management should **not** accept, under any declared emergency, the omission of authorization verification, the loss of dual control on controlled substances, missing temperature documentation on cold-chain shipments, or the treatment of a growing audit-remediation queue as evidence of compliance — retrospective reconstruction cannot retroactively prove a vaccine stayed cold or a narcotic reached the right hands, and a 2,300-case tail persisting past operational recovery is a liability, not a cleanup task. Three pieces of evidence should change this stance: if mock-regulator testing demonstrates that properly templated paper records fully satisfy traceability requirements, sampling latitude for ambient products can widen; if remediation finds **zero** temperature excursions, diversions, or unauthorized shipments attributable to the workaround paths, the current control floor can be judged sufficient rather than tightened; and conversely, a single confirmed unauthorized shipment, excursion, or diversion in the remediation population should trigger immediate suspension of the fast lane, 100% checks on the affected class, and a formal reportable-event assessment. The decision rule is simple: degrade speed and breadth deliberately, never degrade assurance silently.
