# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

**Observations from the prompt:** there are six two-hour arrival blocks, 42 expected cases in the base day, five case types, three priority classes, the stated resource skill multipliers and shifts, and five specified shocks. A two-branch case has two required diagnostic activities. Intake and Validation are required for every case.

**Assumptions needed for planning:**

- Treat each listed service range as a uniform draw, separately for ordinary and complex-tail work. Draw the tail indicator with the listed activity probability, then multiply the draw by the assigned resource’s multiplier. This is a transparent midpoint model, not a fitted distribution. For the load screen below, use the corresponding mixture mean. Assume tail indicators are independent by activity; correlated “complex cases” would make case-level tails heavier.
- Treat expected arrivals and case/priority shares as fractional expected counts in each block, not as observed integer cases. For U1, multiply early arrivals by 1.7 and add 8 percentage points to the early-block P1 share. For U2, double the E and ES case-type weights in the affected blocks, then renormalize all type shares. For U4, double diagnostic tail probabilities, capped at one. U5 combines the U1 and U3 transformations and removes all Intake capacity from 09:30 to 10:00.
- Measure SLAs from case arrival to Validation completion in elapsed calendar time, including nights, breaks, queue waits, handovers, coordination, and interruptions. Work can resume on a later shift; overtime is capped at 30 minutes per resource. The supplied schedules leave no Mechanical-qualified resource after 18:00, so late Mechanical work may wait for the next shift.
- A resource multiplier changes active service duration, not the amount of base work. A transfer between resources costs 7 minutes; a Mechanical-to-Electrical/Software team transfer adds 5 minutes. A diagnostic skill-family change between consecutive jobs costs 5 minutes of setup. Parallel ME/ES work incurs 8 minutes before Validation. These are elapsed-time penalties; setup and handover consume the receiving resource’s time in the proposed dispatch model.
- Parallel branches are allowed only when distinct eligible resources are available and the predicted reduction in the diagnostic critical path exceeds the 8-minute coordination cost plus transfer/setup cost. Otherwise, run the branches sequentially, preferably on a qualified common resource if that avoids a transfer. A common resource may be slower, so compare predicted finish times rather than assuming sequential is always cheaper.
- A P1 can interrupt a lower-priority diagnostic job once. Restart adds 10% of the remaining active service time. Intake and Validation are not preempted. Since no interruption frequency is supplied, the capacity screen does not presume a particular number of preemptions; report that penalty explicitly and estimate its frequency in a pilot.

For comparisons, use two compatible objectives:

1. **SLA-tail objective:** minimize expected weighted elapsed completion time plus a large penalty for P1 completion after 120 minutes, P2 after 8 hours, and P3 after 24 hours; add a CVaR or p95 penalty on SLA lateness. This values the tail risk that a mean-only objective hides.
2. **Robust-fair objective:** minimize the worst-scenario normalized SLA violation across base and U1–U5, then penalize maximum individual planned utilization above 90%, utilization CV across eligible on-shift resources, handover/setup minutes, and P3 starvation. Treat 90% as a hard planning ceiling where feasible, as the prompt requests.

Decision variables are resource-to-skill assignments within qualifications, order of eligible queued work, whether ME/ES branches run in parallel, protected P1 capacity by skill and block, the thresholds for releasing that capacity, and whether one permitted preemption is used. The two objectives can rank policies differently: pure P1 protection may increase non-P1 delay, while minimizing total mean delay can miss a small but unacceptable P1 tail.

# 3. Candidate policies

All policies use arrival-to-completion SLA clocks, an auditable queue-entry timestamp, and the same eligibility matrix. At each dispatch decision, rank P1 first by smallest remaining slack to its 120-minute due time, then P2 by slack to 8 hours, then P3 by slack to 24 hours; break ties by queue age. Increase a waiting case’s rank over time so P3 work cannot be postponed indefinitely. Do not interrupt Intake or Validation. A diagnostic preemption is allowed only for an arriving P1 against lower priority, at most once per case, and only if the P1 slack or predicted SLA needs it. Record the interrupted remaining service and add 10% of that remainder on restart.

1. **Strict specialization with fixed queues.** Intake and Validation use their own fixed queues; each diagnostic skill has a fixed Mechanical, Electrical, or Software queue and only resources scheduled to that skill’s queue take work there. No cross-skill overflow is allowed, even where the matrix qualifies a worker. Within a queue, select the resource with the earliest predicted finish after its multiplier, break, and current commitment. P1 gets the permitted diagnostic preemption; other work is FIFO with aging. For ME/ES, run branches in parallel only if each has a free specialist and the ETA test in section 1 passes; otherwise run them sequentially in the fixed queues. Prefer retaining the current resource for the next step when the saved transfer exceeds any speed benefit. This is easy to audit but can strand capacity in one queue while another waits.

2. **Full qualification-based pooling.** Any on-shift qualified resource can take a task. For each ready task, operators see a score of due-date slack, queue age, resource’s earliest feasible finish (including multiplier, existing commitment, break, and expected handover/setup), and a fixed priority aging term. Dispatch the eligible task/resource pair with the most urgent slack; ties go to longest waiting, then lowest predicted finish. Allow the single P1 diagnostic preemption under the rule above. Use the same predicted-critical-path test for ME/ES, and preserve the case/resource pairing when transfer cost exceeds the predicted completion gain. This policy can borrow from idle pools and respond quickly to shocks, but unrestricted skill borrowing can create setup churn or a new Validation queue.

3. **Hybrid: protected P1 capacity with controlled overflow.** Use the pooled dispatch score, but protect a rolling 15–20% of forecast capacity in the currently constrained diagnostic skill pools for P1 work. Treat this as a virtual reservation across eligible resources, not a permanently idle named person. If there is no waiting P1 and no forecast P1 task due inside 60 minutes, release the slot to the oldest P2/P3 task; reclaim it only at the next task boundary. Trigger cross-skill overflow when the next-two-hour forecast utilization of a qualified skill pool exceeds 80%, or its oldest queue exceeds 60 minutes. Score the overflow resource with its real multiplier plus the 7-minute handover, possible 5-minute team transfer, and 5-minute setup. Keep a case with a qualified current resource if the ETA penalty for transfer is not offset by faster completion. Use P1 preemption only when needed to protect due-time slack, and retain aging for P3. Parallelize only when the ETA gain exceeds coordination and transfer/setup costs. Review any planned individual utilization above 90%; reassign overflow or change the shift plan rather than normalizing chronic overload.

# 5. Robust recommendation

Adopt the hybrid policy as the operating default, with a same-headcount shift adjustment considered for the late Mechanical gap. It is the best trade-off under the robust-fair objective: retain qualification-based pooling to absorb U2/U3 skill imbalance, reserve and release capacity transparently for P1, and use the 90% planning ceiling to prevent a fast resource from becoming the permanent overflow sink. Under the SLA-tail objective, it is preferable to fixed queues because the protected capacity reduces P1 exposure; pure pooling may have a lower base mean, but offers less explicit protection against P1 clustering.

A concrete trial roster is to move Deepa’s eight-hour shift from 10:00–18:00 to 12:00–20:00, with the break moved consistently. This creates a Mechanical-qualified resource in the final arrival block, while Ada, Ben, and Farah still provide Mechanical coverage 10:00–12:00. It trades some 10:00–12:00 Electrical capacity for late Mechanical coverage; U2’s peak should be monitored before making this permanent. The current table’s policy bands assume the original shifts and therefore do not claim the benefit of that change.

Use the following dispatch triggers:

- Enter surge mode when early arrivals exceed 1.3 times forecast in a rolling two-hour window, the P1 count is above forecast by at least two cases, a P1’s predicted completion slack falls below 30 minutes, or the outage flag is set. Protect the P1 reserve and suspend discretionary setup-heavy reassignments.
- Trigger a skill overflow when projected next-two-hour utilization exceeds 80% or oldest wait exceeds 60 minutes. For P1 at risk, allow qualified overflow earlier if its predicted completion gain exceeds transfer/setup costs.
- Release reserved time to aged P2/P3 whenever no P1 is waiting and no forecast P1 is due inside 60 minutes. Do not let an idle reservation block an already waiting P2/P3.
- If a resource’s planned utilization exceeds 90% for a shift, rebalance assignments or revise the schedule; do not routinely solve the issue with the 30-minute overtime allowance.
- During U5-like outage, timestamp the outage and arrival-to-Intake wait separately. A downstream dispatch rule cannot erase that SLA loss; activate the hybrid P1 reserve after Intake recovery and report the outage cohort separately.

Prefer strict specialization only if a pilot shows stable, low cross-skill handover/setup cost and no skill-specific overload, or if certification/quality rules require fixed ownership. Prefer unrestricted pooling if P1 volume is low, handovers are negligible, and its measured fairness remains acceptable. If Chen absence or U2-like demand persists, routing alone is insufficient: rebalance shifts/cross-train only after validating competence and actual service multipliers.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

- **Gaming estimates:** dispatch depends on predicted service and finish time. Record predicted and actual durations by activity, complexity, resource, and case type. Audit persistent underestimation and prevent local teams from improving their queue rank through inflated estimates.
- **P3 starvation:** P1-first can indefinitely delay P3 under repeated surges. Use due-time slack, age-based rank growth, and a maximum waiting threshold; publish P3 completion and carryover separately.
- **Skill erosion and quality:** cross-skill eligibility must mean current authorization, not merely historical experience. Track quality/rework by resource-skill pairing; avoid sending rare skills so little work that competence decays.
- **Preemption cost:** interruptions add restart work and context loss. Log the active remaining time, interruption count, and restart penalty. Enforce the one-preemption limit; compare predicted P1 benefit with the interrupted case’s due-time risk.
- **Coordination and handover overhead:** parallel branches can reduce critical path but generate coordination and transfers. Measure actual coordination minutes and branch-ready/branch-finish times. Do not infer that parallel is beneficial from summed service hours alone.
- **Validation as a downstream bottleneck:** Intake and Validation share resources with other skills. A surge of parallel branch completions can create a Validation burst; dispatch must include projected Validation queue and protect its capacity, not optimize diagnostics in isolation.
- **Fairness:** equal case counts are not equal work when multipliers, shift lengths, breaks, and skill breadth differ. Report productive busy fraction per available minute, weighted expected workload, overtime, and undesirable late/complex assignments. Review both individual utilization above 90% and persistent overload of one skill group.
- **Interpretability:** show operators the priority class, SLA due time/slack, eligible resources, predicted finish including transfer/setup, and reason for the chosen assignment. Log overrides and allow an auditable human override. Keep policy versioned and avoid opaque scores whose effects cannot be reconstructed.

    row = SERVICE[activity]
    ordinary = sum(row["ordinary"]) / 2
    tail = sum(row["tail"]) / 2
    p = min(1.0, row["p"] * tail_multiplier)
    return ordinary * (1 - p) + tail * p


def scenario_row(row, scenario):
    out = dict(row)
    if scenario in ("U1", "U5") and row["start"] < 240:
        out["arrivals"] *= 1.7
        out["P1"] = min(1.0, out["P1"] + .08)
    if scenario == "U2" and 240 <= row["start"] < 480:
        for kind in ("E", "ES"):
            out[kind] *= 2
        denom = sum(out[k] for k in ("M", "E", "S", "ME", "ES"))
        for kind in ("M", "E", "S", "ME", "ES"):
            out[kind] /= denom
    return out


def available_minutes(resource, skill, block_start, scenario, block_end):
    info = RESOURCES[resource]
    if skill not in info["skills"]:
        return 0.0
    if scenario in ("U3", "U5") and resource == "Chen":
        return 0.0
    lo = max(block_start, info["shift"][0])
    hi = min(block_end, info["shift"][1])
    if hi <= lo:
        return 0.0
    unavailable = 0.0
    for a, b in info["breaks"]:
        unavailable += max(0, min(hi, b) - max(lo, a))
    # U5 outage prevents Intake work from 09:30 to 10:00 for all resources.
    if scenario == "U5" and skill == "Intake":
        unavailable += max(0, min(hi, 120) - max(lo, 90))
    return max(0.0, hi - lo - unavailable)


def block_work(row, scenario):
    tail_mult = 2.0 if scenario == "U4" else 1.0
    if scenario == "U5":
        # U5 contains U1 and U3, but not U4.
        tail_mult = 1.0
    n = row["arrivals"]
    cases = {k: n * row[k] for k in ("M", "E", "S", "ME", "ES")}
    return {
        "Intake": n * mixture_mean("Intake"),
        "Mechanical": (cases["M"] + cases["ME"]) * mixture_mean("Mechanical", tail_mult),
        "Electrical": (cases["E"] + cases["ME"] + cases["ES"]) *
                      mixture_mean("Electrical", tail_mult),
        "Software": (cases["S"] + cases["ES"]) * mixture_mean("Software", tail_mult),
        "Validation": n * mixture_mean("Validation"),
    }


def capacity_by_block(skill, start, scenario):
    end = start + 120
    total = 0.0
    for resource in RESOURCES:
        minutes = available_minutes(resource, skill, start, scenario, end)
        mult = RESOURCES[resource]["skills"].get(skill)
        if mult:
            total += minutes / mult
    return total


def scenario_load(scenario):
    rows = [scenario_row(r, scenario) for r in DEMAND]
    load_rows = []
    ratios = []
    total_work = {s: 0.0 for s in SKILL_KEYS}
    for row in rows:
        work = block_work(row, scenario)
        item = {"block": row["block"], "arrivals": row["arrivals"]}
        for skill in SKILL_KEYS:
            cap = capacity_by_block(skill, row["start"], scenario)
            amount = work[skill]
            total_work[skill] += amount
            rho = amount / cap if cap > 0 else (math.inf if amount > 0 else 0.0)
            item[skill + "_work"] = amount
            item[skill + "_cap"] = cap
            item[skill + "_rho"] = rho
            if cap > 0:
                ratios.append(rho)
        load_rows.append(item)
    finite_rhos = [x for x in ratios if math.isfinite(x)]
    peak = max(finite_rhos, default=0.0)
    mean_rho = sum(finite_rhos) / max(1, len(finite_rhos))
    variance = sum((x - mean_rho) ** 2 for x in finite_rhos) / max(1, len(finite_rhos))
    cv = math.sqrt(variance) / mean_rho if mean_rho else 0.0
    return load_rows, total_work, peak, cv


def proxy_metrics(scenario, policy):
    load_rows, total_work, peak, cv = scenario_load(scenario)
    p = POLICIES[policy]
    pressure = min(.95, peak * p["rho_factor"])
    # Heuristic queue-pressure mapping. These coefficients require pilot calibration.
    mean_h = .35 + pressure / max(.15, 1 - .85 * pressure)
    tail_multiplier = 2.35 if scenario in ("U1", "U4", "U5") else 2.05
    p95_h = mean_h * tail_multiplier
    p1 = min(.99, max(.20, .975 - .32 * max(0, pressure - .50) + p["reserve_p1"]))
    p2 = min(.995, max(.30, .990 - .30 * max(0, pressure - .68) - p["reserve_p2"]))
    # Skill-block CV is only a workload-balance proxy, not individual fairness.
    fairness = 1 / (1 + cv + (0.10 if policy == "Fixed" else 0.0))
    hs = p["hs_min"] + (5 if scenario in ("U1", "U5") else 0)
    if peak > .90:
        resilience = "Low"
    elif peak > .75:
        resilience = "Moderate"
    else:
        resilience = "Higher"
    return {
        "scenario": scenario, "policy": policy,
        "mean_delay_h_proxy": round(mean_h, 2),
        "p95_delay_h_proxy": round(p95_h, 2),
        "P1_SLA_proxy": round(100 * p1, 1),
        "P2_SLA_proxy": round(100 * p2, 1),
        "peak_skill_load_ratio": round(peak, 2),
        "utilization_fairness_proxy": round(fairness, 2),
        "handover_setup_min_case_assumed": hs,
        "resilience_proxy": resilience,
        "highest_pressure_skill_block": max(
            ((r[s + "_rho"], s, r["block"]) for r in load_rows
             for s in SKILL_KEYS if math.isfinite(r[s + "_rho"])),
            default=(0, "-", "-")
        )[1:],
    }

def eligible(skill):
    return [r for r, info in RESOURCES.items() if skill in info["skills"]]


def estimate_finish(resource, skill, earliest, base_duration, predecessor_resource=None,
                    predecessor_skill=None):
    start = max(float(earliest), resource_free[resource])
    start = next_shift_time(resource, start)
    if not math.isfinite(start):
        return start
    transfer = 0
    if predecessor_resource and predecessor_resource != resource:
        transfer += HANDOVER_MIN
        if team.get(predecessor_skill) in ("mechanical", "es") and            team.get(skill) in ("mechanical", "es") and            team.get(predecessor_skill) != team.get(skill):
            transfer += CROSS_TEAM_MIN
    setup = SETUP_MIN if skill in ("Mechanical", "Electrical", "Software")         and last_diag_skill[resource] not in (None, skill) else 0
    return start + transfer + setup + base_duration * RESOURCES[resource]["skills"][skill]


def schedule_one(case, activity, skill, earliest, base_duration,
                 predecessor_resource=None, predecessor_skill=None,
                 exclude=()):
    candidates = [r for r in eligible(skill) if r not in exclude]
    scored = [(estimate_finish(r, skill, earliest, base_duration,
                               predecessor_resource, predecessor_skill), r)
              for r in candidates]
    finish_est, chosen = min(scored)
    available = max(float(earliest), resource_free[chosen])
    available = next_shift_time(chosen, available)
    if not math.isfinite(available):
        raise RuntimeError("No same-day eligible resource in demo for " + skill)
    queue_entry = float(earliest)
    assignment = available
    cursor = available
    if predecessor_resource and predecessor_resource != chosen:
        handover = HANDOVER_MIN
        if team.get(predecessor_skill) in ("mechanical", "es") and            team.get(skill) in ("mechanical", "es") and            team.get(predecessor_skill) != team.get(skill):
            handover += CROSS_TEAM_MIN
        cursor = emit(case, "Handover", chosen, cursor, handover, "handover",
                      queue_entry, assignment, skill)
    if skill in ("Mechanical", "Electrical", "Software") and        last_diag_skill[chosen] not in (None, skill):
        cursor = emit(case, "Setup", chosen, cursor, SETUP_MIN, "setup",
                      queue_entry, assignment, skill)
    service = base_duration * RESOURCES[chosen]["skills"][skill]
    end = emit(case, activity, chosen, cursor, service, "service",
               queue_entry, assignment, skill)
    resource_free[chosen] = end
    if skill in ("Mechanical", "Electrical", "Software"):
        last_diag_skill[chosen] = skill
    return end, chosen


def best_common_serial(skills, base_durations):
    common = set(eligible(skills[0]))
    for skill in skills[1:]:
        common.intersection_update(eligible(skill))
    return min((sum(base_durations[i] * RESOURCES[r]["skills"][s]
                    for i, (s, _) in enumerate(zip(skills, base_durations)))
                for r in common), default=float("inf"))


def parallel_decision(skills, durations):
    # ETA saving versus best common-resource serial work; 8-minute coordination
    # plus an explicit 12-minute net-benefit threshold.
    serial = best_common_serial(skills, durations)
    fastest = [min(d * RESOURCES[r]["skills"][s] for r in eligible(s))
               for s, d in zip(skills, durations)]
    parallel = max(fastest) + PARALLEL_COORDINATION_MIN
    return serial - parallel >= PARALLEL_MIN_NET_SAVING_MIN

# 9. Limits and validation plan

Expected arrivals and service ranges do not identify exact case-level SLA rates, p90/p95 delay, queue lengths, or fair individual utilization. Missing inputs include within-block arrival timestamps and clustering, empirical service distributions and tail correlation, actual case complexity labels, route/branch timestamps, resource assignment history, work-in-progress at day start, quality/rework, break flexibility, and whether the stated utilization is measured against paid or productive time. The independent skill-capacity bounds double-count multi-skilled people and omit contention between Intake, diagnostics, and Validation. The planning bands and Python pressure-to-delay coefficients are therefore hypotheses to compare and prioritize measurement, not proof that any policy meets a target.

Backtest all three dispatch rules on timestamped historical cases with the same daily demand and skill eligibility, replaying actual resource shifts, breaks, service intervals, handovers, setup, branch synchronization, and P1 interruptions. Calibrate ordinary/tail distributions by activity and resource; preserve tail correlation if data show it. Compare cohort-level P1/P2/P3 SLA attainment, mean and p95 elapsed lead time, case and stage queue waits, max shift-normalized individual utilization, utilization CV, overtime, handover/setup minutes, preemption penalties, quality/rework, and carryover. Use repeated arrival-day or block bootstrap samples and report confidence intervals, especially for rare P1 failures.

Then pilot in shadow mode: compute and log hybrid recommendations while operators retain current control; compare predicted with actual finish and surface eligibility/ETA explanations. Next, activate hybrid for a limited daytime team or a randomly assigned set of eligible noncritical cases while maintaining a protected P1 escalation path and immediate human override. Do not randomize away urgent P1 protection. Keep a contemporaneous control, stratify results by scenario-like demand, and predeclare stop conditions: any worsening of P1 SLA beyond a small agreed margin, sustained individual utilization over 90%, material P3 starvation, quality/rework increase, or a new Validation queue. Evaluate the Deepa shift change separately or as a staged factor so its effect is not confounded with the routing policy. Expand only after the pilot shows the tail, fairness, and quality outcomes hold across both ordinary days and replayed stress days.
