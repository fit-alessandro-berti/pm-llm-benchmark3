## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

**Hard invariants**

- H1. Referential integrity: every relation references existing events/objects; no new events or objects may be invented.
- H2. No duplicate (event, object, qualifier) or (source, target, qualifier) rows.
- H3. O2O qualifiers are directional (rule 8); repairs must not flip direction.
- H4. An object cannot participate in a lifecycle step before it exists (an Item cannot be packed before its Add Item event).

**Strong defaults** (expected, but violable with documented cause)

- S1. Activity cardinalities from rules 1–4, 6, 7 (e.g., Pack Item: one Item, one Package; Dispatch Package: one Package, one Delivery).
- S2. An Item is packed in exactly one Package; a Package is carried by at most one Delivery.
- S3. Identifier equality between event and object attributes (scanner_ref prefix "PK8" ↔ PK-8; external_ref "N-421?" ↔ carrier_ref N-421) selects a candidate strongly, because ev06/ev07/ev09 show these fields are populated from the same source as the observed relations.
- S4. Lifecycle macro-order Create → Add → Pack → Dispatch → Confirm → Invoice → Pay; repairs respecting it are preferred.

**Weak heuristics** (adjust confidence, never decide alone)

- W1. Amount arithmetic: 130 + 100 = 230 and 100 + 130 = 230 are suggestive for INV-44/ORD-B and PAY-2/INV-44, but rule 6 explicitly demotes arithmetic to evidence, not proof.
- W2. Timestamp proximity (ev09 and ev10 both at 10:00; ev08 one minute after ev07).
- W3. Shared customer C17 across ORD-A, ORD-B, INV-44, ev11.
- W4. Route matching: both packages, both deliveries, and both hints are NORTH, so route is non-discriminating in this dataset and carries almost no weight.
- W5. `order_hint` on Items agrees with observed `contains` edges, so hints are calibrated as reliable here, but they are hints.

**Evidence against a repair**

- A1. The wildcard "N-42*" in ev11 matches both DEL-X and DEL-Y: positive evidence against any unique assignment.
- A2. Rule 5 says an order-only Confirm Delivery row can be legitimate; ev11's missing delivery is not necessarily damage.
- A3. Rule 7 allows unapplied cash; the missing invoice on ev14 may be real behavior, not loss.
- A4. Fees/discounts (rule 6) mean 230 could be 130 + fees on ORD-A alone; the invoice repair is not forced.
- A5. After repairing ev08, PK-8 carries items from two orders — unusual-looking though not forbidden; it counts mildly against P1 and must not be "fixed away".

**Combination procedure.** Hard invariants filter candidates. A cardinality gap under S1 creates a *slot*; identifier evidence (S3) nominates a filler; weak heuristics adjust confidence; counter-evidence caps it. Evidence lines combine only when independent: an O2O edge derived from an assumed E2O edge adds nothing. Confidence ≥ 0.8 with an identifier match → accept; 0.5–0.75 resting mainly on weak heuristics → provisional; genuinely ambiguous or legitimately absent → unresolved; tempting but counter-indicated → reject.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A — conservative / minimum change.** Adds only the identifier-backed repairs: P1 + P2 (ev08 ↔ PK-8, PK-8 packs IT-B1) and P3 + P4 (ev10 ↔ DEL-Y, DEL-Y carries PK-8). Everything resting mainly on arithmetic or customer identity stays unresolved.

- *ev08 and PK-8:* repaired via scanner_ref; PK-8 becomes a mixed-order package, accepted as legitimate.
- *ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:* ev10 → DEL-Y; DEL-X keeps only PK-7.
- *ev11 and deliveries:* left order-only; rule 5 says this can be genuine portal behavior, and N-42* cannot separate the candidates.
- *INV-44 and ORD-B:* not linked; the 100 gap is noted as possible fees or a partial log.
- *PAY-2 and INV-44:* not linked; PAY-2 is treated as unapplied cash.

Lifecycle coherence: ORD-A is complete; ORD-B ends after its item is packed and dispatched, with no confirmation or billing — incomplete but honest. Packages and deliveries are fully coherent. INV-44 remains 130 under-settled and PAY-2 is an orphan payment; both are flagged, not fixed. The bundle never needs the arithmetic assumption, so no edge depends on a rejected premise.

**Bundle B — consolidated-customer interpretation.** Everything in A, plus P8 + P9 (ev12 ↔ ORD-B, INV-44 bills ORD-B), P10 + P11 (ev14 ↔ INV-44, PAY-2 settles INV-44), and P5 + P6 (ev11 acknowledges DEL-X and DEL-Y together, per rule 5's joint-acknowledgment clause). P7 (ev11 ↔ ORD-B) stays unresolved even here, since the observed ORD-A link may be exactly what the portal recorded.

- *ev08 and PK-8 / ev10 and DEL-Y:* as in A.
- *ev11 and deliveries:* both deliveries attached at ~0.45–0.5 each, justified jointly (one portal acknowledgment for customer C17's shipments), not individually.
- *INV-44 and ORD-B:* linked; one consolidated invoice for both orders, which explains total = 230 without postulating fees.
- *PAY-2 and INV-44:* linked; the invoice is settled in two payments (100 + 130).

Lifecycle coherence: every object closes, and the story is jointly coherent — one customer, one consolidated invoice, two payments, one joint confirmation. That neatness is precisely why it is seductive; each added edge individually rests on weak heuristics (W1, W3), and coherence must not be mistaken for evidence.

**Bundle C — skeptical dispatch variant (for contrast, not recommended).** Reads "N-421?" as a garbled reference and ev10 as a bulk dispatch under DEL-X (DEL-X carries both packages; DEL-Y is an unused planned delivery; ev11 confirms DEL-X only). Coherent as a story, but it must explain away a near-exact identifier match, orphans DEL-Y, and contradicts the ev09 convention that external_ref equals the related delivery's carrier_ref. Confidence ≈ 0.1; retained only to show the data does not *force* the A/B dispatch reading.

**Selection.** Bundle A is selected for downstream use; Bundle B's extra edges are carried as provisional annotations in §2, so an analyst can toggle them without re-deriving the reasoning.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Order.** ORD-A: Create (ev01) → Add Item ×2 → Confirm Delivery (ev11) → Issue Invoice (ev12); complete without repair. ORD-B: Create (ev02) → Add Item (ev05); under Bundle A it stops there — an honest truncation, since invoicing may fall outside the log window. The `contains` edges match the Add Item events one-for-one, so Order–Item structure is doubly supported by independent recordings.

**Item.** All three items have Add Item and Pack Item events. IT-B1's Pack Item lacked a package (S1 gap); after P1 it conforms. No item is in two packages; S2 holds.

**Package.** PK-7: packs IT-A1, dispatched (ev09) — coherent. PK-8: after repair, packs IT-A2 and IT-B1 and is dispatched (ev10). The one-to-many `packs` from PK-8 crosses two orders — the one suspicious-looking relation the repair *creates* — retained because the scanner evidence outweighs the tidiness prior (A5 vs. S3). Weights (3.1 for one glass item, 2.9 for two cables) are a weak but non-contradicting sanity check.

**Delivery.** DEL-X: carries PK-7 and relates to ev09 — both rows observed, the cleanest doubly-attested relation in the log. DEL-Y: an orphan object with zero events before repair; after P3/P4 it has a Dispatch event and a carries edge. Its lifecycle still lacks confirmation because ev11 stays unresolved — under Bundle A, Confirm Delivery is an orphan event *with respect to the Delivery type*, flagged rather than fixed.

**Invoice.** INV-44: Issue (ev12), one Record Payment (ev13), bills ORD-A. Under Bundle A it remains 130 under-settled — a legitimate open-balance state; an invoice need not be fully paid inside the window.

**Payment.** PAY-1: doubly supported (E2O ev13 and O2O settles both observed). PAY-2: under Bundle A an orphan whose only event is its own Record Payment (unapplied cash); under Bundle B it settles INV-44.

**Circular-reasoning check.** P2, P4, P9, and P11 are O2O edges inferred *from* the corresponding E2O proposals and must never be cited as independent confirmation of them; their confidences sit at or below the parent E2O confidence and §2 marks the dependency. Conversely, PAY-1/INV-44 and DEL-X/PK-7 show genuine double support: both the E2O and O2O rows were observed, not inferred. A subtler circle to avoid: "ev11 should link DEL-Y because DEL-Y carries ORD-B's item, and ev11 should link ORD-B because DEL-Y is confirmed" — each half assumes the other; both stay unresolved.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**OC-DFG.** Unrepaired: PK-8's Package-typed path is Pack Item (ev07) → Dispatch Package (ev10); ev08 contributes nothing to any Package path, and the Delivery type shows only DEL-X's single Dispatch node — Confirm Delivery is invisible to the Delivery type entirely. Bundle A adds a second Pack Item on PK-8 (a Pack Item → Pack Item edge for the Package type) and gives DEL-Y a Dispatch node. Bundle B additionally creates Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery edges for both deliveries, and Issue Invoice → Record Payment gains a second Payment-side traversal.

**OTG (object-type graph).** Unrepaired: Order–Item, Item–Package (partial), Package–Delivery (via DEL-X only), Order–Invoice, Invoice–Payment (via PAY-1 only). Bundle A strengthens Item–Package and Package–Delivery (PK-8/IT-B1, DEL-Y/PK-8) without new type pairs. Bundle B adds no new pairs either but changes multiplicities: Invoice–Order and Invoice–Payment each become 1-to-2, and Delivery–Order co-occurrence appears at ev11.

**ET–OT graph.** Pack Item × Package: 2 → 3 (A). Dispatch Package × Delivery: 1 → 2 (A). Confirm Delivery × Delivery: 0 → 2 (B). Issue Invoice × Order: 1 → 2 (B). Record Payment × Invoice: 1 → 2 (B). Event counts never change; only participation counts do.

**Flattenings.** On *Order*: unrepaired, ORD-B's trace is ⟨Create Order, Add Item⟩ and ORD-A's is ⟨Create Order, Add Item, Add Item, Confirm Delivery, Issue Invoice⟩; Bundle A changes nothing; Bundle B extends ORD-B with Issue Invoice and *duplicates ev12* across both traces (convergence), inflating flattened Issue Invoice frequency from 1 to 2. On *Package*: PK-8 gains a second Pack Item under A. On *Delivery*: DEL-Y is absent unrepaired; under A it becomes ⟨Dispatch Package⟩; under B both deliveries become ⟨Dispatch Package, Confirm Delivery⟩ with ev11 duplicated. On *Invoice*: under B, ⟨Issue Invoice, Record Payment⟩ gains a second Record Payment. On *Payment*: PAY-2 stays a one-event trace in all bundles.

**Activity frequencies.** Event-based counts are invariant (no events added). Object-based counts diverge: Confirm Delivery touches 1 object unrepaired and under A, but 3 under B (ORD-A, DEL-X, DEL-Y); Record Payment's Invoice participation goes 1 → 2 under B. Repair-induced shifts appear only in the object columns — a useful audit signal.

**Equivalent-execution clustering around Order.** Unrepaired and under A, ORD-A and ORD-B fall into different variant clusters (one full lifecycle, one truncated), independent except for the shared package PK-8. Under B they share the invoice event (and possibly the confirmation), merging into one interconnected customer episode rather than two order cases — B changes not just edge counts but the case-level story.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# --- 0. Preserve originals -------------------------------------------------
events_orig, objects_orig = events.copy(), objects.copy()
relations_orig, o2o_orig = relations.copy(), o2o.copy()

# --- 1. Bundle A additions -------------------------------------------------
e2o_add = pd.DataFrame([
    {"ocel:eid": "ev08", "ocel:oid": "PK-8",  "ocel:qualifier": "package"},
    {"ocel:eid": "ev10", "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:qualifier": "delivery"},
])
o2o_add = pd.DataFrame([
    {"ocel:oid": "PK-8",  "ocel:oid_2": "IT-B1", "ocel:qualifier": "packs"},
    {"ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:oid_2": "PK-8",  "ocel:qualifier": "carries"},
])

# Carry event metadata (activity, timestamp) onto new relation rows if the
# relations table stores them denormalized, as pm4py's OCEL expects.
ev_meta = events_orig[["ocel:eid", "ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp"]]
e2o_add = e2o_add.merge(ev_meta, on="ocel:eid", how="left")
obj_meta = objects_orig[["ocel:oid", "ocel:type"]]
e2o_add = e2o_add.merge(obj_meta, on="ocel:oid", how="left")

# --- 2. Referential integrity and duplicates --------------------------------
assert e2o_add["ocel:eid"].isin(events_orig["ocel:eid"]).all(), "unknown event id"
assert e2o_add["ocel:oid"].isin(objects_orig["ocel:oid"]).all(), "unknown object id"
assert o2o_add["ocel:oid"].isin(objects_orig["ocel:oid"]).all()
assert o2o_add["ocel:oid_2"].isin(objects_orig["ocel:oid"]).all()

relations_new = pd.concat([relations_orig, e2o_add], ignore_index=True)
o2o_new = pd.concat([o2o_orig, o2o_add], ignore_index=True)
for df, keys in [(relations_new, ["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"]),
                 (o2o_new, ["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"])]:
    dups = df.duplicated(subset=keys)
    assert not dups.any(), f"duplicate rows: {df[dups]}"

relations_new["ocel:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(relations_new["ocel:timestamp"])

# --- 3. Rebuild OCEL ---------------------------------------------------------
# VERSION-DEPENDENT LINE: the OCEL constructor signature (and whether o2o is
# accepted as a keyword) varies across pm4py 2.7.x/2.8.x; check your version.
from pm4py.objects.ocel.obj import OCEL
ocel = OCEL(events=events_orig, objects=objects_orig,
            relations=relations_new, o2o=o2o_new)

# --- 4. Discovery ------------------------------------------------------------
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# --- 5. Flattenings ----------------------------------------------------------
flat_order = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Order")
flat_delivery = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Delivery")
print(flat_order.groupby("case:concept:name")["concept:name"].apply(list))
print(flat_delivery.groupby("case:concept:name")["concept:name"].apply(list))

# NOTE: pm4py.ocel_o2o_enrichment merely joins stored O2O rows onto events.
# Since PK-8->IT-B1 and DEL-Y->PK-8 were *inserted by us*, enrichment output
# reflects our hypothesis, not independent business confirmation. Do not use
# it as validation of the repair itself.
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Unique assignment of ev11 to DEL-X** (or DEL-Y) would fabricate a Dispatch → Confirm edge for one delivery and silently deny confirmation to the other, corrupting delivery-level cycle-time and SLA statistics, even though N-42* matches both carriers.
2. **Linking INV-44 to ORD-B on arithmetic alone** would invent a consolidated-billing variant. If the true story is 130 + fees = 230 on ORD-A, the repair merges two independent order executions into one customer episode, and every Order-flattened variant analysis inherits the error.
3. **Forcing PAY-2 onto INV-44** hides genuine unapplied-cash behavior; a finance analysis would report 100% settlement and zero open items, masking exactly the exception a process miner should surface.
4. **"Purifying" PK-8 by reassigning IT-A2 to PK-7** would contradict the directly observed scanner_ref PK8-1 on ev07 and destroy a real convergence point between the two orders — the mixed package is the finding, not the defect.
5. **Attaching PK-8 to DEL-X as a bulk dispatch** because timestamps and routes match would orphan DEL-Y forever, create a false one-delivery-many-packages pattern, and override a near-exact identifier match with two weak heuristics.
6. **Adding ORD-B to ev11 for symmetry** would duplicate the Confirm Delivery event across both order traces after flattening, inflating its frequency via convergence and making the two orders' variants artificially identical.
7. **Repairing until every lifecycle closes** biases conformance checking toward zero deviations: the discovered model certifies as compliant precisely the open behavior (unbilled ORD-B, unconfirmed DEL-Y, unapplied PAY-2) that the original data honestly reported.
