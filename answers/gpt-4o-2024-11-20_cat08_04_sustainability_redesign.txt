# 1. System Boundary and Assumptions

### Environmental Scope  
The environmental boundary includes **energy consumption**, **transport emissions**, **material waste (scrap and packaging materials)**, and **process-driven inefficiencies** such as rework, transport mode mismatches, and partial shipments. The goal is to assess the lifecycle impacts of operating the spare-parts distribution process, including both production and logistics stages.

### Inside the Boundary  
- **Energy**: Machine-specific energy consumption during production (e.g., CNC, heat treatment).  
- **Transport emissions**: Emissions from all transport modes (e.g., parcel air, ground, consolidated trucks, dedicated vans).  
- **Material waste**: Scrap rates from production and rework, along with packaging materials.  
- **Operational inefficiencies**: Rework, excessive batching queues, partial shipments, and emergency production.  

### Outside the Boundary  
- Upstream sourcing of raw materials.  
- Downstream product use and disposal by the customer.  
- Long-term lifecycle durability of parts, beyond processes observed in the event log.

### Uncertainties  
- Impact of customer perception on lead-time flexibility for sustainability adjustments.  
- Variance in return rates driven by factors not captured in the process path (e.g., customer handling).  
- Noise in forecasts for demand and production queues, leading to variable batching times and obsolescence risks.

# 3. Intervention Options

### S1. **Green Shipping Recommender**  
- **Target Paths/Segments**: Standard-stock, Service-contract segment where SP1 violations occur.  
- **Mechanism**: Optimize route mode selection (ground vs. air) by integrating promised lead time, customer criticality, and route reliability. Prioritize ground transport where slack exists.  
- **Service/Quality/Resilience Effect**: Reduces transport emissions while minimizing service delays. May slightly increase cycle time for less-urgent cases.  
- **Required Monitoring**: Proportion of orders switched from air to ground; late-delivery rates after intervention.

---

### S2. **Dynamic Batching with Waiting-Time Caps**  
- **Target Paths/Segments**: Made-to-order CNC/heat treatment processes with large waiting queues.  
- **Mechanism**: Enforce batch size-energy trade-offs while capping queue times by segment (e.g., no more than 24h for high-priority Service-contract orders, 48h for Regular).  
- **Service/Quality/Resilience Effect**: Reduces batching-related energy inefficiencies with controlled lateness risks. Avoids service deterioration if waiting-time caps are enforced.  
- **Required Monitoring**: Batch size, waiting times, and delivery punctuality.

---

### S3. **Shift CNC_B Load to CNC_A**  
- **Target Paths/Segments**: Made-to-order paths with CNC_B queue for Service-contract customers.  
- **Mechanism**: Prioritize CNC_A when due date slack allows to cut energy use and reduce rework risk (CNC_A has 9% rework vs. CNC_B's 18%).  
- **Service/Quality/Resilience Effect**: Slightly longer cycles in low-slack cases but reduces rework loops for high-priority items.  
- **Required Monitoring**: Usage rates of CNC_A vs. CNC_B, rework rates, and due-date compliance.

---

### S4. **Priority Pick + Ground for Standard-stock Air Cases**  
- **Target Paths/Segments**: Standard-stock Service-contract orders where air is used despite slack.  
- **Mechanism**: Replace low-urgency air shipping with prioritized picking and ground transport.  
- **Service/Quality/Resilience Effect**: Cuts air-shipping emissions without significant delays (especially for non-Critical-downtime).  
- **Required Monitoring**: Compliance with SP1, proportion of orders shifted off air.

---

### S5. **Consolidated Truck Departures Twice Daily**  
- **Target Paths/Segments**: Non-urgent Standard-stock and some Made-to-order orders.  
- **Mechanism**: Bundle ground shipments into fewer, larger truckloads to cut transport emissions by 35-55%.  
- **Service/Quality/Resilience Effect**: May slightly increase delivery cycle times for low-priority cases but improves resilience by creating more predictable routes.  
- **Required Monitoring**: Consolidation rates, cycle time, and emissions reductions.

---

### S6. **Packaging-Route Compatibility Checks**  
- **Target Paths/Segments**: Cases with frequent packaging rework (e.g., reinforced packs for parcel air/dedicated vans).  
- **Mechanism**: Add compatibility checks during availability and route-selection steps to prevent packaging-to-route mismatches.  
- **Service/Quality/Resilience Effect**: Reduces wasteful rework with negligible service impact. Protects quality by reducing returns.  
- **Required Monitoring**: Packaging rework rates and returns due to in-transit damage.

---

### S7. **Inspection Quality Gate Additions**  
- **Target Paths/Segments**: CNC_B-heavy paths prone to rework loops.  
- **Mechanism**: Enforce pre-packaging inspections focusing on high-rework machine/part combinations to prevent customer-facing defects.  
- **Service/Quality/Resilience Effect**: Reduces rework/returns but slightly increases inspection times.  
- **Required Monitoring**: Pre-rework inspection loops and related rework frequency.

---

### S8. **Shared Emergency Courier Lanes**  
- **Target Paths/Segments**: Critical-downtime cases using dedicated vans.  
- **Mechanism**: Replace single-customer dedicated vans with shared emergency transport for nearby locations.  
- **Service/Quality/Resilience Effect**: Cuts transport emissions without compromising service levels for true downtime-critical cases.  
- **Required Monitoring**: Dedicated van usage, customer delays, and emissions per transport event.

# 5. Rebound and Displacement Risks

1. **Batching delays**: Larger wait-based batches may reduce per-item energy but increase late deliveries and obsolescence.  
2. **Transport mode shifts**: Customers may escalate demand for air shipping if ground services slow significantly.  
3. **Packaging increases**: Reinforced packaging reduces damage but doubles material waste.  
4. **Rework cycles**: Missed inspections may create hidden loops, increasing waste.  
5. **Obsolescence risk:** Forecast errors for bulk batching could increase unused inventory.  
6. **Customer behavior**: Sustainability adjustments (e.g., extending lead times) could spur cancellations or demand for faster modes.

---

# 6. Balanced Recommendation

### Recommended Package:
- **Primary focus**: S1 (Green Shipping), S2 (Dynamic Batching), and S5 (Consolidated Trucking). Combined, these three absorb much of the transport and production inefficiencies.  
- **Secondary layers**: Complement with S3 (CNC_A shift) and S6 (Packaging checks) to cut rework, material waste, and energy at key points.  
- **Critical safeguards**: Use S4 (Priority Pick) and S8 (Shared Van Lanes) to maintain service for critical cases and avoid resilience risks.

This package balances environmental reductions with service guarantees by focusing higher efficiencies on moderate-priority cases while protecting critical paths.

# 7. Validation and Governance Plan

### Validation Metrics
- **Conformance monitoring**: Measure compliance with SP1 (ground shipping) and SP2 (dedicated vans).  
- **Impact tracking**: Compare pre/post metrics on transport emissions, energy per batch, and rework rates.  
- **Segment-level service**: Monitor late-delivery percentages by case/customer type.  

### Governance Plan  
- Add an **exception log** to track overrides of SP1, SP2, and Q1 policies for validation.  
- Implement monthly event-log audits, highlighting process paths or segments driving risks.  
- Adjust batching and transport thresholds iteratively to maintain equilibrium between sustainability and resilience.
