# 1. Assumptions and notation

**Notation.** For a case, `arr` = external S1 arrival, `s1s`/`s1e` = S1 start/end, `s2s`/`s2e` = S2 start/end. Queue entry into S2 is defined by the scenario as the S1 completion (`s2_queue_entry = s1e`). All times are 2026-05-12. Durations are in minutes. I use the flags `TIME` (a recorded clock value), `MISSING` (activity occurred, start not recorded), `OPEN` (started, not finished by 14:00), and `BLANK` (activity not yet started; case waiting).

Quantities per station:
- S1 wait `W1 = s1s − arr`; S1 service `X1 = s1e − s1s`; S1 sojourn `= s1e − arr`.
- S2 wait `W2 = s2s − s1e`; S2 service `X2 = s2e − s2s`; S2 sojourn `= s2e − s1e`.
- Total case sojourn (completed) `= s2e − arr`.

**A1 — Event-order / instantaneous-transition convention.** A case is assumed to enter the S2 queue at the instant S1 completes (no transport delay). This is stated in the scenario ("A case enters the S2 queue when its S1 activity completes").

**A2 — Rounding.** Every reported time `t` is a 5-minute round of a true time in `[t−2.5, t+2.5]` min. Consequently any single duration computed as a difference of two rounded times carries an uncertainty of about `±5` min (two independent `±2.5` rounding errors combine to `±5` in the worst case, `≈±3.5` if independent-Gaussian). I therefore treat all point durations as central estimates with a `±5` min bound and do **not** report sub-5-minute precision as meaningful. Rounding can also produce apparent resource overlaps or zero/near-zero gaps that are not real (A "apparent conflict" clause).

**A3 — Capacity.** S1 has 2 parallel single-task servers (I1, I2); S2 has 2 (C1, C2). A server does one case at a time. C2 is unavailable 10:55–11:10 for maintenance; during that window S2 effective capacity is 1.

**A4 — No bypass.** Every case must pass S1 then S2 (scenario). Therefore cases still in S1 or waiting for S1 at 14:00 have *not yet generated* any S2 demand except through their (future) S1 completion.

**A5 — Right censoring at 14:00.** For OPEN activities I observe only elapsed service `= 14:00 − start`, which is a **lower bound** on the final service time. OPEN cases are retained (never discarded); their contribution to WIP and station load is counted up to 14:00.

**A6 — MISSING-start imputation is bounded, never a single silent value.** For each MISSING start I compute a feasible interval `[lo, hi]` from four constraints: (i) queue entry (can't start before it is available: `arr` for S1, `s1e` for S2), (ii) same-resource predecessor completion (server must be free), (iii) the case's own completion (`start ≤ end`, i.e. non-negative service), and (iv) the same-resource successor start (must finish before the next job it actually did). I then report `[lo, hi]`, a midpoint central value, and the induced service range. These are treated as *estimates/bounds*, explicitly distinguished from observations.

**A7 — Utilization is computed from busy-time over available server-time, using OPEN elapsed time as a lower bound.** I never infer utilization from completed cases alone (that would ignore in-progress and queued work).

**A8 — Little's Law is applied only where inflow/outflow and the population are jointly observable over a bounded interval and the system is not dominated by censoring** (see §6).

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

**Waiting / service / sojourn.** Computed by the differences in §1. For a MISSING start, service `X = end − start` is a *range* `[end − hi, end − lo]`; the wait that precedes it is the complementary range. For OPEN, `elapsed = 14:00 − start` is reported as `X ≥ elapsed`.

**Lower / central / upper convention.**
- *Lower* estimate of a service uses the latest feasible start (`hi`) → shortest service, longest preceding wait.
- *Upper* estimate uses the earliest feasible start (`lo`) → longest service, shortest wait.
- *Central* uses the midpoint of `[lo, hi]`.
- Rounding adds an additional `±5` min band on top of these.

**WIP / queue length.** WIP at station = number of cases that have entered the station's scope but not left it. For S2, a case is "in S2 system" on `[s1e, s2e)` (waiting) ∪ in-service on `[s2s, s2e)`; "in S2 queue" only on `[s1e, s2s)`. For S1, "in S1 system" on `[arr, s1e)`. **Simultaneous-event convention (A2-derived):** at a rounded instant where an arrival/queue-entry coincides with a departure, I treat departures as occurring at `t⁻` and arrivals at `t⁺` (departure-before-arrival), so a shared timestamp does not double-count WIP. Peaks are therefore reported as bounds, not exact.

**Throughput / rates.** Arrival rate over a subperiod = (# cases with `arr` in the subperiod)/duration. Departure rate at a station = (# `s?e` in subperiod)/duration.

**Utilization.** For server `r` over interval `[a,b]` with availability `A_r` (excluding maintenance), `busy_r = Σ service-time of jobs r executed within [a,b]` (OPEN counted as elapsed lower bound; MISSING counted with its service range). `ρ_r = busy_r / A_r`, reported as `[ρ_lo, ρ_hi]`.

**Little's Law.** In interval `I`: `L̄ ≈ λ · W̄`, where `L̄` = time-average WIP, `λ` = throughput, `W̄` = mean sojourn of cases that flowed through. Checked only in the early "warm, uncongested, fully-observed" window (§6).

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

**Observation (early, 08:00–≈09:30).** Both stations keep up. S1 waits are 5–20 min; S2 waits 5–15 min. Neither queue grows; sojourn times are modest (P01–P04 ≤ 105 min). No bottleneck evident.

**Observation (mid, ≈09:30–11:30).** S1 waits grow rapidly (P08 50, P09 60, P10 70, P11 75, P12 85). S1 service is ~25–30 min per case with only 2 servers, so S1 admission cadence (a new S1 start roughly every 15–25 min alternating I1/I2) cannot absorb the arrival stream (arrivals every ~10–15 min early, spacing out later). This is the first sign that **S1 is throughput-limiting**: the S1 *start* times drift ever further behind arrivals.

**Observation (late, ≈11:30–14:00).** S2 becomes the visibly congested station: at 14:00, four cases (P16, P18, P19, P20) have completed S1 but are still waiting for S2, and two (P15, P17) are OPEN in S2. S2 utilization (0.87–0.95) exceeds S1's (0.78–0.85). The S2 queue is strictly growing at the horizon.

**Hypothesis (bottleneck migration).** The evidence is most consistent with a **time-varying bottleneck**: S1 is the binding constraint through the morning (it meters flow, building the large S1 waits), but because S2 service (~40–45 min) is *longer* than S1 service (~25–30 min) on equal 2-server capacity, once S1 finally pushes a burst of completions through (P14–P20 all complete S1 between 12:00 and 13:45), the accumulated demand overwhelms S2 and S2 becomes the terminal bottleneck. So: **S1-limited early/mid, S2-limited late.** A pure single-station diagnosis would be wrong.

**Maintenance interval (10:55–11:10).** During this 15-min window S2 runs at capacity 1 (only C1). C1 is occupied by P07 (10:20–11:00) then P09 (11:00–11:40); C2's next job P08 does not start until 11:15 — i.e., 5 min after C2 returns. So the outage plausibly delayed P08's start: P08 entered the S2 queue at 10:20 (s1e) and could not be served by C2 until 11:10, contributing to its 55-min S2 wait. The outage is a *local aggravator* of the late-morning S2 backlog, not the sole cause (the backlog persists and grows well past 11:10). This is a hypothesis consistent with, but not proven by, the timestamps (rounding, A2).

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

**Three imputation scenarios for the four MISSING starts** (using §3 intervals):

- **Optimistic (short service / late start = `hi`):** P05 X2=0, P07 X1=0, P11 X2=0, P15 X1=0. This minimizes computed service and maximizes upstream waits. It pushes S2 busy-time down (→ ρ_S2 ≈ 0.87) and inflates S1 waits.
- **Central (midpoint):** P05 X2≈15, P07 X1≈18, P11 X2≈13, P15 X1≈18 — close to the observed service norms (S1 ~25–30, S2 ~40 suggests these central values are on the low side; the true values likely sit in the upper half of the intervals given typical service).
- **Pessimistic (long service / early start = `lo`):** P05 X2=30, P07 X1=35, P11 X2=25, P15 X1=35. Maximizes busy-time (→ ρ_S2 ≈ 0.95, ρ_S1 ≈ 0.85) and minimizes waits.

**Censoring scenarios for OPEN / WAITING cases.** OPEN P15 (X2 ≥ 30), P17 (X2 ≥ 5), P21 (X1 ≥ 30) and waiting P16/P18/P19/P20/P22 all have monotonically growing waits. Under any completion assumption these can only *increase* the late S2 (and P22's S1) waits, never decrease them.

**Stable conclusions (robust to all scenarios):**
1. S2 is the terminal bottleneck at 14:00 (a growing S2 queue exists regardless of imputation — it is defined by blank starts, independent of any imputed value).
2. Sojourn times grow monotonically through the morning; congestion is real and increasing.
3. S1 waits dominate the *middle* of the window; S2 waits dominate the *end*.
4. The C2 outage coincides with and plausibly worsened the S2 backlog onset.

**Fragile / scenario-dependent conclusions:**
1. Exact utilization values (S1 0.78–0.85, S2 0.87–0.95) shift several points across imputations and OPEN treatment.
2. The precise split of P05/P07/P11/P15 between wait and service is genuinely unidentifiable within the stated intervals.
3. Whether S1 or S2 is the "primary" bottleneck *overall* depends on the weighting of the mid vs. late window; the defensible answer is the time-varying one, not a single station.

**Little's-Law check.** I apply it only to the early window **08:00–09:30 for S1**, where arrivals (P01–P06, 6 cases), completions, and the population are all fully observed and no start is MISSING/OPEN. There, λ ≈ 6/1.5h = 4/h, mean S1 sojourn W̄ ≈ (25+30+40+50+... early values) ≈ 0.6h, giving L̄ ≈ λ·W̄ ≈ 2.4, consistent with the observed 2–3 cases typically in S1. In the congested late window Little's Law is **not** applied: the system is non-stationary and dominated by censoring (WIP still rising, many departures beyond 14:00), so the stationarity assumption fails (A8).

    return pd.Timestamp(f"{DAY} {hhmm}")

    counts = []
    for g in grid:
        n = ((entry_series <= g) & ((exit_series > g) | exit_series.isna())).sum()
        counts.append(n)
    return pd.Series(counts, index=grid)

# S2 system: entry = s1_end (queue entry), exit = s2_end (NaT/OPEN/waiting -> still in system)
s2_entry = work["s1_end"]
s2_exit = work["s2_end"]
s2_wip = wip_series(s2_entry, s2_exit)
# S1 system: entry = arrival, exit = s1_end
s1_wip = wip_series(work["arrival_s1"], work["s1_end"])
print("\n=== WIP (count in station system) mean/peak ===")
print(f"S1  mean={s1_wip.mean():.1f}  peak={s1_wip.max()}")
print(f"S2  mean={s2_wip.mean():.1f}  peak={s2_wip.max()}")

# Utilization: busy server-minutes / available server-minutes
def busy_minutes(df_ev, resources):
    b = {}
    for res in resources:
        sub = df_ev[df_ev["org:resource"] == res]
        b[res] = ((sub["time:timestamp"] - sub["start_timestamp"]) / MIN).sum()
    return b
s1_busy = busy_minutes(ev, ["I1","I2"])
s2_busy = busy_minutes(ev, ["C1","C2"])
WINDOW = dmin(ts("08:00"), HORIZON)          # 360 min per server
C2_AVAIL = WINDOW - 15                        # maintenance 10:55-11:10
print("\n=== Utilization (busy / available, central imputation, OPEN excluded here) ===")
print(f"I1 rho ~ {s1_busy['I1']/WINDOW:.2f} | I2 rho ~ {s1_busy['I2']/WINDOW:.2f}")
print(f"C1 rho ~ {s2_busy['C1']/WINDOW:.2f} | C2 rho ~ {s2_busy['C2']/C2_AVAIL:.2f} (C2 avail={C2_AVAIL})")

# Arrival / departure rates by subperiod
def rate_table(series, label):
    bins = [ts("08:00"), ts("10:00"), ts("12:00"), ts("14:00")]
    lbl = ["08-10","10-12","12-14"]
    cut = pd.cut(series.dropna(), bins=bins, right=False, labels=lbl)
    return cut.value_counts().reindex(lbl).rename(label)
rates = pd.concat([
    rate_table(work["arrival_s1"], "S1_arrivals"),
    rate_table(work["s1_end"], "S1_departures"),
    rate_table(work["s1_end"], "S2_arrivals(qentry)"),
    rate_table(work["s2_end"], "S2_departures"),
], axis=1)
print("\n=== Arrivals / departures by 2h subperiod (counts) ===")
print(rates.to_string())

# 6) Case-level PM4Py semantics on a VALID projection: fully-observed COMPLETE cases only.

proj = ev[ev["case:concept:name"].isin(complete)].copy()

proj_af = pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate(proj_sw)

print("\n=== CASE-LEVEL (whole-case, NOT station-level) service/waiting/arrival/finish ===")

      .head(8).to_string(index=False))
