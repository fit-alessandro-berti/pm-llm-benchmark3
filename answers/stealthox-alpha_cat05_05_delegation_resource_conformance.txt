## 2. Rule Interpretation

**Formalization** (`did(h,a,c)` = human h performed activity a on case c at time t; identities post-normalization per RR5):

- **RR1 (drafter/approver):** `c h: did(h,Draft,c)  did(h,Approve,c)  VIOLATION`. Absolute; risk level irrelevant. Evaluated on canonical human keys.
- **RR2 (implementer/verifier):** `c h: did(h,Implement,c)  did(h,Verify,c)  VIOLATION`. A verification event whose actor cannot be resolved to a human **cannot satisfy** RR2 — it is fail-closed, not auto-passing.
- **RR3 (high-risk authority):** `Approve(c,h,t)  risk(c)=HIGH  QM(h,t)  d [Delegate(d,h)  tInterval(d)  Scope(c)ScopeSet(d)  Unavailable(Principal(d),t)  ¬SubDelegated(d)]`. `Unavailable` = documented leave/formal incapacity **at t**, judged on contemporaneous records. Demonstrated principal activity (e.g., VPN) does not restore formal availability, but it converts the question into one of **identity assurance**.
- **RR4 (emergency):** emergency HIGH change approved by IC/valid IC delegate requires `Retrospective_QM_Review` by QM or valid QM delegate with `t_review  BD²(t_implement)`, plus a credible emergency flag and safety rationale.
- **RR5 (identity):** service accounts are not humans; aliases of one human form an equivalence class collapsed before SoD checks.
- **RR6 (suspicion vs proof):** repeated pairs, reciprocity, shared stations, concentrated handovers are **indicators only**; a collusion finding requires convergent independent evidence.

**Business-day counting for RR4.** Day 0 = implementation day (not counted). Count forward over site business days (Mon–Fri minus the site-holiday calendar; 08-15/16 are ordinary weekend days, no holidays in period). Deadline = end of the 2nd business day. Example: implement Tue 08-11  day 1 Wed 08-12, day 2 Thu 08-13  **deadline 2026-08-13 23:59**. Sensitivity: even under an inclusive convention (implementation day = day 1), CHG-505 remains late and CHG-510 remains on time — the conclusions are convention-robust.

**Principal acting while on leave.** (a) A principal's *personal* act during own leave: authority is intact (the role persists); this is an HR/policy matter, not an RR breach — but because leave predicts absence, the act carries a heightened identity-assurance duty (MFA/FIDO/device corroboration). (b) A *delegate's* account used during the delegate's own certified sick leave: the capacity presumption is rebutted; unexplained account use is treated as **unattributed**, not as the delegate's act. (c) The delegation absence-condition is assessed on formal records at the event instant.

**Can a later database entry prove an earlier delegation?** No. Probative hierarchy: contemporaneous signed/registered instruments > system transaction logs > post-hoc migration records (lowest weight; consistency-only). A record created at t cannot, alone, establish existence at t_use < t. Here D-QM-01 validly rests on the 08-09 signed PDF; the 08-12 DB row adds nothing for the 08-10/08-11 approvals and would be **insufficient by itself**.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

Per RR6, none of the following is treated as proof; each is an indicator requiring discriminating evidence.

1. **Clara–Ben repeated pair (18 of 23 HIGH production approvals in 60 days; same production cell).**
   - *Benign:* cell-aligned staffing with no rotation policy naturally concentrates the pair; Ben is the designated QE cover.
   - *Adverse:* entrenched dyad rubber-stamping Clara's drafts; mutual dependency that suppresses challenge.
   - *Discriminate:* audit the 18 approvals for substantive depth (comments, rework, rejection rate vs site baseline); test whether approval latency differs when Asha approved instead.

2. **Reciprocity: Ben drafted 4 LOW training changes that Clara approved.**
   - *Benign:* small-team symmetry; training documents are routine.
   - *Adverse:* mutual non-challenge pact ("you sign mine, I sign yours"), scalable to HIGH cases.
   - *Discriminate:* compare rejection/challenge rates on reciprocal vs non-reciprocal pairs; interview both on how often they challenged each other.

3. **Clara drafted 100% of cases in the extract.**
   - *Benign:* she is the designated change author for the cell.
   - *Adverse:* single point of agenda control — every case enters the pipeline pre-shaped by one person, enabling the self-approvals seen in 502/510.
   - *Discriminate:* authorship metadata in the QMS (text provenance), backlog ownership records.

4. **Shared workstation WS-DM-04 / svc_qms (CHG-503).**
   - *Benign:* a colleague verified legitimately but the terminal design made individual re-authentication impossible — a control-design failure, not misconduct.
   - *Adverse:* Diego (or an accomplice) verified his own implementation under the service account, defeating RR2 deliberately.
   - *Discriminate:* badge granularity at the room door 12:20–12:40, terminal process logs, who else badged into the area that shift; compare with the CHG-509 pattern where badge+FIDO attribution worked.

5. **Asha's VPN window vs CHG-504 approval (09:22 inside 09:15–09:40).**
   - *Benign:* exactly what she says — one urgent personal handling during leave.
   - *Adverse:* someone else used her credentials, exploiting the leave window precisely because her presence was not expected.
   - *Discriminate:* FIDO/MFA possession logs, device fingerprint vs her usual endpoints, HR ticket for the "urgent case", badge records showing she was off-site.

6. **Same-day cluster 08-13: u_as at 09:22 (VPN window) and u_bk at 13:00 (office IP during Ben's sick leave, denied).**
   - *Benign:* coincidence — a genuinely urgent day handled by two absentees improvising.
   - *Adverse:* one actor holding or coordinating multiple principals' credentials to push HIGH approvals through while both authorities were formally away — the strongest collusion-shaped fact in the extract.
   - *Discriminate:* full authentication-factor logs for both accounts on 08-13, endpoint correlation, network flows, and whether the same human physically badged into the office across both events.

7. **Alias split b.koch / u_bk within CHG-508.**
   - *Benign:* convenience login; Ben did not realize the SoD appearance.
   - *Adverse:* deliberate use of a second identifier to defeat four-eyes automation.
   - *Discriminate:* who provisioned `b.koch`, whether the UI displays one identity, whether other cases mix aliases.

8. **D-IC-04: attempted email subdelegation FaridClara.**
   - *Benign:* urgency-driven misunderstanding of delegation law by non-experts.
   - *Adverse:* conscious construction of a paper authority chain to let the drafter approve her own emergency change; question who initiated it and why Clara did not simply route to Farid, who was valid until 18:00.
   - *Discriminate:* the email thread (initiator, wording, timing vs the 14:20 approval), Farid's actual availability 14:00–16:00.

9. **Saturday burst CHG-509 (draftverify in 3 hours, HIGH).**
   - *Benign:* planned weekend coverage under a pre-registered single-day delegation — the design worked.
   - *Adverse:* quiet-period processing to avoid scrutiny; the delegation's narrow one-day scope could be a pre-built lane for chosen changes.
   - *Discriminate:* submission queue timestamps (was it waiting?), Asha's true availability, whether other weekend lanes exist.

## 6. Control Improvements

- **Delegation creation:** register every delegation before its interval with a unique ID, machine-readable scope enums (asset class **and** activity type — "approval" vs "review" must be distinct), explicit subdelegation-permitted flag, and a cryptographic seal; reject email-only instruments at the workflow layer.
- **Effective-time recording:** write-ahead registration in an append-only store; migrations must carry provenance metadata and must never back-fill authority records; a delegation used before any contemporaneous record exists is auto-flagged.
- **Principal availability:** integrate HR leave calendars with the QMS so the absence condition is evaluated automatically at use time; provide a break-glass path (deputy QM roster) so urgencies never depend on leave exceptions.
- **Alias management:** golden identity registry; logs emit canonical IDs only; block issuance of local aliases that bypass SoD engines; periodic reconciliation of login IDs to HR identities.
- **Individual authentication:** abolish shared terminals for GxP-relevant actions; FIDO2 mandatory for Approve/Verify; step-up re-authentication at each critical activity; bind sessions to badge-present individuals; retain factor logs for investigation.
- **Emergency review:** start an SLA timer at implementation with auto-escalation at T1 business day and auto-assignment to a pre-declared backup QM; codify the business-day convention in the system, not in prose.
- **Pair rotation:** mandate rotation or second-line sampling when any drafter–approver pair exceeds a threshold (e.g., >50% of a risk class over a rolling quarter); track reciprocity metrics.
- **Network monitoring:** baseline handover/working-together networks; alert on new dominant edges, emerging reciprocity, service-account sinks, and off-hours bursts; route alerts to QA, not to the line.
- **Evidential retention:** WORM storage for delegations, badges, FIDO logs, and session records with retention exceeding investigation horizons; extend CCTV retention at shared stations or compensate with per-action biometric re-auth.
