# 1. Model scope and state variables
## Boundary
The digital twin simulates **case arrival to final decision (Issue permit / Reject application) including rework loops and post-decision appeal**, plus applicant and third-party delays. Included: portal submission, completeness loop, parallel reviews (Planning, Fire-safety, Environmental), Public-notice with latent objection process, inspection scheduling + inspection with geography, Decision drafting as join, Legal check with council calendar, rejections.
Excluded from mechanistic simulation but represented as exogenous delays/parameters: detailed document content quality, council political deliberation, court appeals beyond municipal reject, HR hiring/firing dynamics, IT outages. Emergency fast-track cases removed from log are out of scope and must not be used to promise SLAs for them.

Level of detail: **task-level discrete-event simulation with resource-constrained queues + calendars + case attributes + explicit cross-case couplings**. Not a simple replay of traces and not independent-case Monte Carlo. Resources are individual agents with shifts and skills; queues are per-activity with priority rules.

## Entities
- **Cases:** attributes `case_id, case_type {Simple, New residential, Commercial, Complex variance}, district {D1-D5+}, architect_id, application_value, requires_fire, requires_environment, requires_public_notice, prior_violations, submission_time, age, state`.
- **Tasks / Activities:** as in process tree, including silent `tau` routings and rework tasks `Completeness rework, Inspection rework`, plus latent `Objection occurrence, Applicant response, Consultant report arrival`.
- **Resources:** `clerk_1..5, planner_1..8, fire_1..2eq, env_1..3, inspector_1..4, scheduler_1.., legal_1..2, system (infinite), portal (infinite), applicant/third-party (infinite but with heavy-tailed delay)`.
- **Batches / Bundles:** inspection bundles keyed by `(district, week, inspector)`, architect submission bursts keyed by `(architect_id, week)`, objection clusters keyed by `(district, neighbourhood, time-window)`.
- **Calendars:** weekly shift calendars + exception calendar (public holidays, council-meeting weeks).

## State variables
- Global clock `t`; future event list; non-stationary arrival intensity `lambda(t, weekday, month, architect-burst-state)`.
- Per case: `current_node, enabled_tasks, join_counters (how many of required reviews arrived), remaining_required_branches, rework_count, waiting_start, service_start, priority_score, bundle_id`.
- Per queue: `FIFO + priority-ordered list, queue_length(t), waiting_time_sofar, starvation time`.
- Per resource: `status {off-shift, idle, busy, travel, reserved-for-other-duties}, current_case, remaining_service, location/district, next_available_time`.
- Per district/architect: `backlog, recent submission count, objection risk score, travel-time matrix`.
- System: `WIP by case_type/district/activity, completed, SLA breaches, utilization integrals`.

## Resources, queues, calendars
Single pooled queues per skill with skill constraints, not one global queue:
- Clerk queue -> 5 clerks, Mon-Fri 08-16, Mon one clerk reserved for portal support (reduced completeness capacity).
- Planner queue -> 8 planners, Mon-Fri 09-17, 2 specialists only Simple; others handle all but with case-type-dependent service.
- Fire queue -> capacity only Tue-Thu; models 2 FTE equivalent as 2 servers active 3/5 days.
- Environmental queue -> 3 servers with 40% unavailability (preemptive reservation); modelled as server vacations or reduced effective capacity.
- Inspection scheduling (office) + Site inspection (field Tue-Fri) as two-stage: schedulers batch, inspectors travel. Travel time sampled by district (D3/D5 longer) and reduced per-stop if batched.
- Legal queue -> 2 officers, zero capacity in council-meeting weeks except `urgent==True` (Complex variance appeal, Commercial deadline).
- System/portal infinite servers with small service.

## Cross-case dependencies (must be first-class)
1. **Architect bursts:** `architect_id` top-20 generate correlated arrivals (Hawkes / batch arrivals). Related cases share missing-document propensity and objection risk.
2. **Objection contagion:** public-notice objections are not independent; one objection raises extension probability for temporally/spatially proximate cases. Modelled as latent district-time risk factor.
3. **Inspection bundling:** scheduler can delay first-ready case to form district batch (trade waiting vs travel). This couples waiting times.
4. **Join synchronization:** Decision drafting waits for max of applicable branches (Planning + optional Fire/Env/Public-notice + Inspection). This creates induced waiting even with idle drafters.
5. **Resource contention with priority:** aging-case reservation (D6), Simple-only planners, Monday surge contention couple case types through shared queues.

# 2. Parameter-estimation plan
## PM4Py role: log/model handling only, not simulation
Use PM4Py for parsing, discovery, conformance and descriptive statistics. Keep simulation engine separate (SimPy / custom DES) consuming estimated parameters. This separation avoids confusing replay fitness with predictive capacity under interventions and calendars.

Planned PM4Py steps:
- `pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id='case:concept:name', activity_key='concept:name', timestamp_key='time:timestamp')` for case notion, sorting, start/end activity analysis.
- Variant analysis: `pm4py.get_variants()`-family / `pm4py.statistics.variants` to reproduce Variant and routing summary table, filter by `case_type` and attribute `requires_*`, compare median/p90 duration per variant group.
- DFG discovery: `pm4py.discover_dfg(log)` for control-flow frequencies and `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)` for median/mean sojourn between activities; use to calibrate branching probabilities and to locate rework arcs (Planning->Planning review, Inspection->Inspection rework, Legal->Drafting).
- Process-tree handling: attempt `pm4py.parse_process_tree(simplified_string)`; if syntax mismatch document mapping and instead discover `pm4py.discover_process_tree_inductive(log)` and compare to nominal tree via conformance (`pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_token_replay`, alignments for selected variants). Simplified string uses `->` sequence, `X` XOR, `+` parallel, `*` loop, `tau` silent — not directly executable without loop-body semantics and guard conditions.
- Time statistics: `pm4py.statistics.traces.pandas.case_statistics.get_median_case_duration`, case arrival rate (`case_statistics.get_cases_description`), start-activity arrival time series for non-stationary estimation.
- Resource / performance: derive directly with pandas after formatting because only completion timestamps exist for many activities: compute inter-completion sojourns, then split into waiting vs service using external service-time priors and calendar-aware correction (see below).

## Arrivals
- Aggregate intensity: estimate hourly/daily counts by `month x weekday x case_type`. Confirm 1.7x peak (Mar-May, Sep-Nov vs Jan-Feb) and 2.2x Mon vs Wed via Poisson regression / STL decomposition on daily submissions.
- Model as non-homogeneous Poisson process + batch component: base `lambda_0(month, weekday, hour)` fitted by maximum likelihood on counts; burst component: for each `architect_id`, estimate burstiness (Fano factor, same-week multiple submissions). Fit Hawkes or compound-Poisson: with prob `p_burst(architect_tier)` arrival generates 1-4 siblings within 7 days same district.
- Validate against 31,200/18mo  57/day average; peak days ~90-100.

## Service vs waiting split (critical: only completion times observed)
- Do not equate sojourn to service. Strategy: (a) use activities with short service priors (Completeness 25m median, Request 10m, Issue 5m) to infer waiting = sojourn - service - off-calendar time; (b) calendar-aware subtraction: if sojourn spans weekend/holiday/off-shift, subtract non-working time using reconstructed HR calendars; (c) for human tasks without start, estimate service distribution from median/p90 table + targeted time-study assumption (lognormal/gamma), estimate waiting as residual queueing delay validated against queue-length proxies (Monday spikes).
- Fit service: lognormal or Weibull for Planning (3.5h med, 14h p90), Fire (2.5h/9h), Env (4h/18h), Legal (3h/16h); deterministic+noise for Public-notice minimum 14d; zero service + heavy-tailed delay for Receive missing documents (5d median from request).
- Waiting: empirical queueing delay per activity as function of WIP and calendar state; calibrate queueing parameters so simulated median waiting matches 1.5d completeness, 6d planning, 4d fire, 8d env, 8d inspection, 7d legal.

## Branching / routing
- Estimate `P(requires_fire | case_type, application_value, district)`, `P(requires_env | district, case_type)`, `P(requires_public_notice | case_type)` directly from log attributes; note missing threshold/sensitivity attributes -> add residual uncertainty and sensitivity range.
- Estimate `P(missing docs loop | case_type, architect_tier)`, `P(legal check | case_type, variant)`, `P(reject | path)`, rework probabilities after Planning/Inspection/Legal/appeal from DFG arc frequencies conditioned on case_type. Use Laplace smoothing for rare 7% variants.
- Decision drafting join: estimate synchronization delay as max-branch sojourn; do not model as independent activity duration.

## Rework
- Mine loop-backs via DFG + variant inspection: count `Completeness check` repetitions, `Inspection rework` occurrences (9% variants), Complex variance appeal (8% long-tail). Because rework reasons are unstructured text, treat rework probability as aggregate per exit point + rework-count-dependent (first vs second loop different), with NLP-derived sub-types only as scenario factor, not calibrated probability.
- Distinguish municipal rework (planner/inspector/legal rework with service) vs applicant rework (Receive missing documents, readiness delay, no-show).

## Calendars
- HR calendars give prior availability; refine by observing event timestamps: infer effective capacity (e.g., no Fire completions outside Tue-Thu, no Legal completions in council weeks, no inspections Mon). Estimate env 40% reservation as random server unavailability calibrated to 8d median wait.
- Holidays/council meetings as exception calendar overlay that blocks resource eligibility and pauses Public-notice minimum clock differently from service queues.

## Dependencies
- Inspection travel: estimate extra sojourn for D3/D5 vs others conditional on inspector; estimate batching benefit from cases with same `(district, inspection week)` close scheduling times (reduced per-case travel, increased first-case wait).
- Objection proxy: since objection events missing, model Public-notice duration as mixture: `14d minimum + with prob p_obj(district, case_type, time) ~ extended Lognormal( up to 60d+)`. Estimate `p_obj` and extension from bimodal duration histogram, not as observed labels. Spatial correlation estimated from co-movement of long notices in same district-week.
- Architect effect: hierarchical model with architect random effect on missing-doc prob and arrival burst; top-20 vs rest.

# 3. Assumptions
## Arrivals
- Base arrivals follow NHPP with month and weekday multipliers as observed; within-day portal submissions concentrated 08-20h; Mon 2.2x Wed persists under baseline. Burst arrivals from same architect are correlated, not independent.
- 18-month log covers at least one full seasonal cycle; next-year seasonality repeats shape but level has +/-15% uncertainty. Policy D7 changes weekday shape, not total volume (unless stated).

## Service times
- Service distributions are stationary conditional on `(activity, case_type, resource skill)` except when intervention changes work content (e.g., D2 removes inspection service, D8 triages env). Median/p90 table anchors fits; tails are heavy (lognormal) not exponential. Using exponential would understate p90 and SLA breach.
- Observed sojourn = waiting + service + off-calendar + applicant delay. Where start missing, split is inferred with uncertainty, not measured. Sensitivity band +/-30% on service mean tested.

## Routing
- `requires_*` flags in log are taken as ground truth for past routing but are incomplete predictors (area threshold, env sensitivity missing). Future routing uses fitted probabilistic rules + attribute logic; Complex variance always requires public notice; Simple clean path often skips legal.
- Parallel branches (Planning + Fire + Env) can overlap in time subject to resource availability; Decision drafting is AND-join. Simplified `+` in tree is interpreted as parallel AND, `X` as XOR, `*` as redo-loop with max 2-3 iterations (empirical truncation).

## Resources/calendars
- HR calendars approximate true availability; effective capacity lower due to meetings, sick leave, multitasking. Fire Tue-Thu only, Inspectors field Tue-Fri, Legal blocked council weeks, Clerks reduced Mon, Env 60% net. System tasks infinite capacity. Travel time district-dependent and sequence-dependent (batching reduces per-stop).
- No overtime in baseline; interventions adding capacity (D4) respect same daily shift windows.

## Rework
- Rework probabilities conditional on case_type and loop count, not memoryless constant; Inspection rework 9% overall but higher for Commercial/Complex; Complex appeal rework creates very long tail (council-dependent). Rework service times similar to first pass but with +20% mean (context switching) unless data shows otherwise.
- Uncoded text reasons mean root-cause-specific rework reduction cannot be calibrated; interventions affecting rework (D1, D9) modelled as relative reduction scenarios with wide intervals.

## Applicant/third-party response
- Applicant delays (Receive missing docs median 5d heavy-tailed, inspection readiness, objection response) are separate infinite-server delays, not municipal service; they do not consume municipal resources but block case progress and inflate end-to-end duration. Modelled as lognormal/Pareto with case_type/architect dependence; pre-submission checker (D1) shifts effort pre-submission (increases time-to-submit slightly) while reducing in-process applicant delay.
- Consultant reports for env are third-party with highly variable delay folded into env waiting; triage (D8) reduces both service and external wait for low-risk.

## Cross-case dependencies
- Cases are not i.i.d. Architect bursts, objection spillover, inspection batches, and shared-queue contention induce positive correlation in waiting. Ignoring this understates peak congestion and p90. Twin explicitly simulates couplings; independent-case run is only a debug baseline, not a decision model.
- Same-architect same-district cases have correlated quality (missing docs) and correlated objection exposure; grouped site visits couple their inspection times.

## Non-stationary demand
- Demand non-stationarity (seasonal 1.7x, weekly 2.2x, architect bursts) drives queue instability; steady-state analysis invalid. Simulation must be transient with warm-up and time-varying arrivals. Peak-month capacity (D4) only helps if queue carryover across weeks is modelled.
- Policy/arrival interaction: smoothing (D7) and checker (D1) change arrival process itself, not just service.

## Missing data
- Missing start timestamps -> waiting/service split uncertain; missing objection events -> public-notice extension mechanism latent (proxy mixture, not observed labels); missing area/sensitivity attributes -> routing rules partially unidentified; unstructured rework reasons -> rework causes unidentified; removed emergencies -> tail slightly truncated; HR calendars not log-derived -> availability overestimated. All propagated as parameter uncertainty + structural sensitivity scenarios, not point estimates. No claim that twin is calibrated truth.

# 4. Experiment matrix
Factors: `arrival_level, case_mix, intervention, resource_level, calendar_stress, routing_rule, priority_rule, batching_rule`. Each experiment: 18-month simulated horizon + warm-up, 30+ replications, common random numbers across interventions for variance reduction.

| ID | Intervention / scenario | Key factor levels | Expected trade-off / hypothesis |
|---|---|---|---|
| B0 | Baseline (as-is, calibrated) | Current calendars, current routing, NHPP arrivals | Reference for all deltas; shows seasonal backlog, p90 tails |
| D1 | Pre-submission checker | P(missing loop) -30 to -50%, +0.5-2d pre-submit effort, +5% portal support load | Reduces 19% missing-doc variants (22d~14d) but shifts burden to applicant; risk: drop-off / delayed submission |
| D2 | Simple no-inspection if value<50k & no violations | Skip scheduling+inspection for ~60-70% Simple (~27% all cases) | Large waiting cut for Simple, frees inspectors for Complex; risk: quality/missed defects, reject shift downstream |
| D3 | District-batched inspection | Batch window 2-3d by district, travel -20-40% per stop, +1-2d first-case wait | Net gain if travel-heavy D3/D5; risk: penalizes first-ready Simple |
| D4 | +1 temp inspector peak months only | +25% inspection capacity Mar-May, Sep-Nov, Tue-Fri | Cuts 8d inspection wait in peaks; risk: underutilized off-peak, scheduling overhead, no help for legal/env bottlenecks |
| D5 | Parallel legal pre-check Commercial/Complex | Legal starts after Planning draft, not after Decision drafting (overlap 50-70%) | Cuts 68d/140d paths by ~5-10d; risk: wasted legal work if planning rework, extra legal load |
| D6 | Reserve 1 planner/day for aging cases | Priority: age>30d (Simple) / >60d (others) preempts FIFO | Improves p90/fairness, may increase median slightly; risk: starvation of fresh cases |
| D7 | Appointment slots to smooth Mon burst | Mon 2.2x1.3x Wed, spread to Tue-Thu, small applicant delay | Reduces completeness queue spike (1.5d wait); risk: applicant non-compliance, shift to Tue peak |
| D8 | Env triage by district/attributes | Fast-track 30% env (service -50%, external wait -40%), standard rest | Targets 8d env wait + 18h p90 service; risk: misclassification if sensitivity data incomplete |
| D9 | Objection early-warning | p(extended notice) -20%, extension mean -15%, +planner/legal upfront cost | Cuts 60d objection tail; risk: false alarms, extra work, cannot observe objections directly |
| D10 | Combined D1+D2+D3+D4 | As above jointly | Tests complementarity (applicant + inspection capacity + batching); risk: overestimates additive benefit, implementation complexity |
| C1 | D5+D6 combo (fairness/legal) | Parallel legal + aging priority | Addresses Commercial/Complex tails without extra hires |
| S1 | Stress: +20% demand surge + burst architects | Arrival level +20%, burst prob x1.5 | Tests robustness; expect non-linear blow-up in planning/env/legal queues |
| S2 | Stress: Legal/env outage (council backlog + consultant delay) | Legal capacity -50% 4 weeks, env external wait +100% | Exposes AND-join vulnerability; Decision drafting starves |
| S3 | Stress: Inspection sick-leave + D3/D5 travel shock | Inspector 3 FTE 6 weeks, travel +50% | Tests batching (D3) and temp (D4) resilience; fairness across districts |
| CAL | Calibration check: D2 under alternative violation prevalence | Violation rate 5% vs 15% | D2 benefit highly sensitive to eligibility; guards against over-claim |

All D-runs also evaluated under S1-S3 for robustness (e.g., D10+S1). Implementation cost/effort recorded qualitatively (IT, hiring, legal change) alongside simulated benefit.

# 5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment
## Warm-up
- System starts empty but real system never empty; need warm-up to fill WIP and queues. Approach: (1) initialize with WIP sampled from log (open cases by activity/district) + 60-day warm-up with peak-season arrivals discarded from statistics (Welch's method on WIP and waiting); (2) sensitivity: 0/30/90-day warm-up comparison; (3) for stress scenarios, warm-up under baseline then inject stress (no re-warm) to capture transient backlog carryover.
- Council calendar phase matters: start clock on same weekday/month as log start (Jan) to align seasonality; do not start in peak and measure immediately.

## Run length
- Horizon 18 simulated months matching log + 3-month tail to drain WIP (cases submitted near end still resolve; Complex 140d median needs drain). Report both truncated (submitted-in-window) and completed-case metrics to avoid censoring bias for long tails.

## Replications and seeds
- Minimum 30 replications per cell (50 for tails / Complex variance analysis). Independent streams per stochastic component (arrivals, service, routing, applicant delay) with seeded RNGs; common random numbers across B0 vs D* (same arrival traces, same service draws where applicable) to reduce variance of differences. Publish seed table; allow reproducibility with `numpy.random.SeedSequence`.
- Compute paired differences per replication (D - B0 on same seed) and CI on difference, not just overlapping independent CIs.

## Confidence / credible intervals
- Report mean + median + p75/p90/p95 + SLA breach % with 95% CIs (t or bootstrap percentile, 2000 resamples) for durations; Wilson CIs for probabilities (rework, reject). For heavy tails, emphasize medians and quantiles over means; report both mean and trimmed mean.
- Parameter uncertainty: bootstrap log cases (cluster bootstrap by architect/week to preserve bursts) to get parameter distributions; run outer Monte Carlo over 100 parameter draws x inner DES replications for key outputs (two-level uncertainty). Alternatively Bayesian posteriors for branching probs (Beta) and lognormal service (Normal-Inverse-Gamma) propagated.
- Sensitivity: one-way tornado (service mean +/-30%, p_obj +/-50%, travel +/-50%, burst intensity x0.5/x1.5), plus Morris screening for D10 interactions. Heavy-tailed applicant/env delays: test lognormal vs Pareto; report p90 sensitivity explicitly; use median-based comparisons and quantile regression to avoid mean dominance by extremes.
- Do not present point-optimal ranking; present intervals and probability each intervention beats baseline (e.g., P(D reduces p90 by >7d) = 80%).

# 6. Validation strategy
## Face validation
- Walkthrough with clerks, planners, inspectors, legal officers: does sequence, parallel reviews, join at Decision drafting, council blackout, batching logic match lived process? Review animation of 20 sampled simulated traces vs P001/P002-style real traces. Challenge D2/D5 legality and D3 travel assumptions. Document disagreements as model risks.

## Event-log replay validation
- Replay log traces on model with resources infinite (control-flow check): token replay / alignments fitness should be >0.9 for top 80% variants; deviations pinpoint missing guards (area threshold, sensitivity). Then replay with finite resources/calendars to see if simulated waiting reproduces observed queue spikes (Mon completeness, peak-month planning). Failure to reproduce indicates calendar/capacity miscalibration, not proof of correctness.

## Distributional validation
- Compare simulated vs empirical distributions stratified by `case_type x variant group`: median/p90 duration table (12d/24d Simple clean through 140d/260d Complex), activity sojourn histograms, public-notice mixture (14d spike + long tail). Use QQ-plots, KS / Anderson-Darling (with heavy-tail focus), coverage of prediction intervals. Critically, validate joint distributions (e.g., Fire+public-notice 48d/95d) not just marginals, to test parallel-join logic.

## Resource-utilization validation
- Compare simulated utilization/busy-time vs HR expectations and indirect proxies: completions per FTE per week, idle gaps (no Fire completions Fri-Mon, no inspections Mon), queue lengths before Planning/Legal/Inspection. Since start times missing, validate completions-per-day and WIP, not claimed service utilization alone; show band for inferred utilization given service uncertainty. Flag if simulation needs >100% utilization to match observed waits (indicates missing capacity or mis-split).

## Bottleneck validation
- Check that model reproduces known bottlenecks: Planning queue 6d, Env 8d, Inspection 8d, Legal 7d, applicant 5d; that shifting bottleneck appears seasonally (env/legal interact for Commercial, inspection for rework variants). Use bottleneck frequency analysis (which activity has longest waiting per case) simulated vs log-derived; test intervention directionality (e.g., removing inspection should relieve inspector queue but expose legal).

## Out-of-sample validation
- Temporal holdout: calibrate on first 12 months, predict next 6 months (peak season) arrival counts, variant mix, median/p90 per case_type, backlog evolution. Success = prediction intervals cover holdout with <15% median error for high-volume types; Complex variance allowed wider. Also cross-district holdout (train without D5, predict D5) to test travel/generalization. Failed holdout triggers recalibration and widens uncertainty, not tuning to test set. Document that validation does not confer optimality; decision use requires ongoing monitoring and recalibration after policy change.

# 7. Decision criteria
Decide on **dominance under uncertainty + robustness + fairness**, not single mean optimum. All deltas reported as paired difference vs B0 with 95% CI.

## Primary metrics (per case_type and overall, median and tail)
- End-to-end duration: median and p90/p95 days (submission to Issue/Reject). Thresholds for consideration: median -5d overall or -7d for targeted type AND no p90 worsening >3d; strong candidate if p90 -10d for Commercial/Complex without median harm.
- SLA compliance: e.g., Simple <21d, New residential <60d, Commercial <90d, Complex <180d (illustrative; municipality to confirm). Require +5pp compliance for targeted type, no type losing >2pp.
- Backlog/WIP: average WIP and peak-month queue days; require no sustained WIP growth under S1; threshold: peak WIP -15% for capacity interventions.

## Fairness
- By district (D3/D5 vs others) and architect tier (top-20 vs small): max-min gap in median wait for Planning/Inspection and overall p90. Flag if D3/D5 p90 >1.3x best district or small architects systematically worse. D6/D3 evaluated partly on gap reduction, not just average. Reject interventions that improve mean by sacrificing a district (>5d worsening for any district).

## Resource / efficiency
- Utilization 65-85% target for human pools (sustained >90% = fragile, <50% = waste for temp hire). Overtime/urgent-legal share, inspection km-travel proxy, rework rate, applicant burden (extra pre-submit days, missing-doc loops avoided). D1/D7 must report applicant cost; D4 cost per peak-day saved.

## Rework and quality guardrails
- Rework rate, reject rate, appeal rate must not increase >1pp (D2 no-inspection watch); any time saving accompanied by reject increase is flagged as risk-shifting, not improvement.

## Robustness
- Rank by worst-case across B0+S1+S2+S3: preferred intervention has positive median benefit in baseline and non-negative in all stresses (or smallest regret). Compute minimax regret on p90 and SLA. Combined D10 must beat best single by >3d median to justify complexity/cost.
- Decision rule: pilot if (a) paired CI excludes zero harm on primary + fairness, (b) holds under S1-S3, (c) cost/effort proportionate; otherwise monitor or redesign. Explicitly state simulation suggests candidates, does not prove optimality without pilot calibration.
