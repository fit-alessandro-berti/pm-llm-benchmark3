## 2. Rule Interpretation

### RR1 — Drafter/approver separation

Formal check:

- For each case, normalize resources to human identity where possible.
- Let `drafter` be the normalized human performing `Draft_Change_Request`.
- Let `approver` be the normalized human performing `Approve_Change`.
- Violation if `drafter == approver`.

Service accounts:

- If a draft or approval is performed by a service account, the event cannot satisfy human separation unless reliable session evidence maps it to a human.
- If mapping is weak or absent, the control is not proven.

Risk level:

- RR1 applies to LOW and HIGH cases. Low risk does not waive drafter/approver separation.

### RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation

Formal check:

- Let `implementer` be the normalized human performing `Implement_Change`.
- Let `verifier` be the normalized human performing `Verify_Implementation`.
- Violation if `implementer == verifier`.

Service accounts:

- A `svc_qms` verification is not human verification unless strongly attributed to a human.
- If attribution is weak, RR2 is not technically proven and may conceal self-verification.

Role requirement:

- The stated rules only require separation, not a specific verifier role. Therefore, technically, verification by a non-implementer can satisfy RR2.
- However, if the QMS requires the Independent Verifier role, Eva’s verification actions after 2026-08-11 23:59 and Grace’s CHG-507 verification become role-authorization or control-objective issues.

### RR3 — High-risk approval authority

For non-emergency HIGH-risk changes:

- `Approve_Change` must be performed by:
  1. QM standing at event time; or
  2. A valid QM delegate.

A QM delegation is valid only if all of the following are true:

- Effective interval covers the approval timestamp.
- Scope covers the case/change type.
- Delegated role covers the activity performed.
- Principal-absence or unavailability condition is satisfied where required.
- No prohibited subdelegation occurred.
- Evidence is contemporaneous or otherwise trustworthy.

For emergency HIGH-risk changes, RR4 provides a limited alternate approval path through IC authority.

### RR4 — Emergency substitution

For HIGH-risk emergency changes, where the emergency flag and safety rationale are credible:

- Implementation may proceed after approval by IC or valid IC delegate.
- A QM or valid QM delegate must perform `Retrospective_QM_Review` within two business days.

Business-day definition used here:

- Business day = Monday–Friday, excluding site holidays.
- 2026-08-15 and 2026-08-16 are weekend days.
- No site holiday exists in the period.
- Trigger time = later of emergency `Approve_Change` and `Implement_Change`.
- Deadline = end of the second business day after the trigger date, not counting the trigger date as day one.

Under this definition:

- CHG-510 trigger date 2026-08-11  deadline 2026-08-13 23:59 UTC. Its retrospective on 2026-08-12 is timely, but authority fails.
- CHG-505 trigger date 2026-08-11  deadline 2026-08-13 23:59 UTC. Its retrospective on 2026-08-14 is late.

If the site instead counts the trigger day as day one, deadlines are earlier; CHG-505 remains late.

### RR5 — Identity evidence

- Service account  human.
- Aliases must be normalized before SoD checks.
- Strong session evidence may map a service-account event to a human for conformance testing, but the use of shared credentials remains a control weakness.
- Weak session evidence leaves identity indeterminate.

### RR6 — Suspicion versus proof

Repeated collaboration, reciprocal approvals, shared workstations, leave conflicts, and concentrated handovers are indicators. They are not proof of collusion. Each suspicious pattern requires an alternative benign explanation and discriminating evidence.

### Action by a principal while on leave

Formal annual leave does not automatically strip a principal of standing authority. If Asha, as QM, personally and authentically approves a change while on leave, the approval can be technically authorized.

However:

- If a delegation is conditioned on the principal being absent, the principal’s actual availability may weaken the delegate’s authority.
- If authentication is weak or credential sharing cannot be excluded, the action becomes an identity/evidence issue rather than a clean authorized action.

For CHG-504, Asha’s VPN session coincides with the approval and she admits handling one urgent case. That supports authorization, but credential-sharing uncertainty lowers confidence.

### Later database entry proving earlier delegation

A database record created after the effective interval cannot by itself prove that a delegation existed earlier. It can only corroborate contemporaneous evidence.

- D-QM-01 has a signed PDF timestamped 2026-08-09 18:00, before the effective interval. If the signature and document integrity are authentic, the delegation may be valid despite the QMS database record being created during migration on 2026-08-12.
- The late database entry is a data-quality and audit-trail issue, not independent proof of the earlier delegation.
- D-QM-02 has a controlled database record created before the interval, making it stronger on record integrity.
- D-IC-04 is email only and violates D-IC-03’s no-subdelegation condition; it is invalid.

def check_delegation(actor, needed_role, event_ts, case_scope, activity, emergency):
    """
    Delegation logic:
    - interval must cover event
    - scope must cover case scope
    - allowed activity must cover event
    - emergency_only delegations require emergency
    - absence_required requires principal absence evidence
    - valid_record False means invalid regardless of interval/scope
    - availability_conflict is a warning, not automatic invalidation
    """
    if actor is None or pd.isna(actor):
        return {
            "delegation_ids": [],
            "final_valid": False,
            "record_valid_but_absence_condition_missing": False,
            "issues": ["actor_not_human_or_unattributed"],
        }

    relevant = delegations[
        (delegations["delegate"] == actor) & (delegations["role"] == needed_role)
    ]

    delegation_ids = []
    final_valid = False
    record_valid_but_absence_condition_missing = False
    issues = []

    if relevant.empty:
        return {
            "delegation_ids": [],
            "final_valid": False,
            "record_valid_but_absence_condition_missing": False,
            "issues": [f"no_{needed_role}_delegation_found"],
        }

    for _, d in relevant.iterrows():
        interval_ok = d["start"] <= event_ts <= d["end"]
        scope_ok = str(d["scope"]).lower() == str(case_scope).lower()
        activity_ok = activity in d["allowed_activities"]
        emergency_ok = (not d["emergency_only"]) or bool(emergency)
        record_ok = bool(d["valid_record"])

        principal_absence_ok = True
        if d["absence_required"]:
            principal_absence_ok = is_absent(d["principal"], event_ts)

        principal_availability_conflict = availability_conflict_signal(d["principal"], event_ts)

        if interval_ok and scope_ok and activity_ok and emergency_ok:
            delegation_ids.append(d["delegation"])

            if not record_ok:
                issues.append(f"{d['delegation']}_invalid_record_or_prohibited_subdelegation")

            if d["absence_required"] and not principal_absence_ok:
                issues.append(f"{d['delegation']}_principal_absence_not_proven")

            if principal_availability_conflict:
                issues.append(f"{d['delegation']}_principal_availability_conflict_{principal_availability_conflict}")

            if record_ok and principal_absence_ok:
                final_valid = True

            if record_ok and d["absence_required"] and not principal_absence_ok:
                record_valid_but_absence_condition_missing = True
        else:
            if not interval_ok:
                issues.append(f"{d['delegation']}_interval_mismatch")
            if not scope_ok:
                issues.append(f"{d['delegation']}_scope_mismatch")
            if not activity_ok:
                issues.append(f"{d['delegation']}_activity_mismatch")
            if not emergency_ok:
                issues.append(f"{d['delegation']}_emergency_condition_mismatch")

    return {
        "delegation_ids": sorted(set(delegation_ids)),
        "final_valid": final_valid,
        "record_valid_but_absence_condition_missing": record_valid_but_absence_condition_missing,
        "issues": sorted(set(issues)),
    }

## 6. Control Improvements

1. **Delegation creation and registration**
   - Require digital delegation creation in QMS before the effective interval.
   - Require principal digital signature and optional delegate acknowledgment.
   - Enforce mandatory fields: principal, delegate, role, effective interval, scope, activities, absence condition, subdelegation permission, emergency scope, incident reference.
   - Block delegations from being active unless stored in an authoritative, non-backdated registry.

2. **Effective-time recording**
   - Store immutable creation timestamp, effective start, effective end, and last modification timestamp.
   - Prevent migration or bulk-load processes from creating delegation records with assumed effective dates unless accompanied by validated documentary evidence.
   - Reconcile signed artifacts with database records after migrations.

3. **Principal availability and absence conditions**
   - Integrate HR leave data with delegation activation.
   - If delegation is conditioned on absence, require explicit absence statement or automatic activation based on leave status.
   - If principal performs work while on leave, require documented rationale and consider suspending or narrowing the delegation for the affected case.
   - Record weekend availability policy explicitly: whether weekend nonpresence satisfies “principal absent.”

4. **Alias management**
   - Maintain a unique human identity table with all aliases mapped to one canonical identity.
   - Prohibit approval systems from accepting unmapped second identifiers such as `b.koch` if they represent the same human as `u_bk`.
   - Run continuous reconciliation between AD/QMS/master-data aliases and process-log resources.

5. **Individual authentication**
   - Prohibit service accounts from performing human control activities unless step-up personal authentication occurs.
   - Require MFA/FIDO for high-risk approvals and verifications.
   - On shared workstations, force re-authentication before each QMS action.
   - Log terminal lock state, session handoff, and user switching.

6. **Emergency review controls**
   - Predefine emergency IC delegates and scopes.
   - Require incident identifier linkage, such as CI-44, in emergency change records.
   - Start automatic timers for retrospective QM review deadlines.
   - If QM is absent, require delegation scope broad enough to cover emergency retrospective review, or trigger escalation to an alternate QM.
   - Prohibit email-only emergency subdelegation unless explicitly permitted and registered.

7. **Pair rotation**
   - Implement rotation or random assignment for high-risk drafting and approval where operationally feasible.
   - Alert when the same drafter/approver pair exceeds a threshold, but treat alerts as indicators, not proof.
   - Require independent spot review for concentrated pairs.

8. **Network monitoring and process analytics**
   - Maintain PM4Py handover-of-work and working-together networks as risk indicators.
   - Monitor SoD violations involving aliases and service accounts.
   - Track approvals during leave or sick leave.
   - Track service-account use in verification and approval-like activities.
   - Combine network indicators with session, badge, and outcome evidence before escalating to collusion investigation.

9. **Evidential retention**
   - Retain VPN, badge, CCTV, QMS transaction logs, delegation PDFs, database audit trails, session logs, and incident-command records for a defined investigation window.
   - Preserve logs around leave periods and emergency incidents with elevated retention.
   - Ensure CCTV retention is sufficient for shared-terminal disputes.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

The apparent nonconformance is mixed. At least five cases contain clear or highly likely authority/segregation failures: CHG-502 self-approval, CHG-507 non-QM high-risk approval, CHG-508 alias-hidden self-approval, CHG-510 invalid emergency subdelegation plus self-approval, and CHG-505 invalid/late retrospective QM review. A substantial portion also involves identity or data-quality uncertainty rather than clean proof of misconduct: CHG-503 unresolved service-account verification, CHG-506 Ben’s account use during sick leave, CHG-504 Asha’s on-leave VPN approval, and CHG-509 weekend principal-absence ambiguity plus service-account verification. Valid or potentially valid substitution explains CHG-501 cleanly and CHG-509 conditionally. Organizational-pattern risk is present because of repeated Clara–Ben pairing, reciprocal low-risk approvals, shared workstations, and emergency delegation irregularities, but these indicators do not by themselves establish collusion.

Approximate judgment:

- **Genuine authority or SoD failure:** about half of the apparent cases.
- **Identity/data-quality uncertainty:** about one-third to two-fifths, with overlap because authority failures can also involve account misuse.
- **Organizational-pattern risk:** present across multiple cases and should trigger monitoring/rotation, but not automatic culpability.

Three highest-priority investigations:

1. **CHG-510** — invalid subdelegation, drafter/approver conflict, emergency authority abuse.
2. **CHG-506** — high-risk approval using Ben’s account during certified sick leave; possible credential sharing or unauthorized access.
3. **CHG-503** — unresolved `svc_qms` verification from Diego’s usual workstation; possible self-verification or shared-account control bypass.

.CHG-507 should also be remediated quickly through role-authority correction, but it is prioritized below the active emergency, credential, and shared-account risks.
