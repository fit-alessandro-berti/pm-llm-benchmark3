## 1. Modeling assumptions

- **Object types and capitalization**: Singular PascalCase is used consistently across `objects`, `relations`, and PM4Py calls: `Order`, `Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, and `Payment`.
- **E2O qualifier vocabulary**: Every event-to-object link carries an explicit role qualifier in `relations["ocel:qualifier"]`:
  - `creates`: `Order` instantiated by `Create Order`
  - `updates`: `Order` modified by `Add Item`
  - `adds`: `Item` attached to an `Order` by `Add Item`
  - `reserves`: `Item` whose stock is reserved by `Reserve Stock`
  - `operational_context`: `Order` explicitly noted as sharing an operational event (`Reserve Stock` E06 and `Pack Package` E07 for `P1`)
  - `packs`: `Package` assembled in `Pack Package`
  - `packed_into`: `Item` placed into a `Package` in `Pack Package`
  - `dispatches`: `Package` handed over in `Dispatch Package`
  - `carried_in`: `Delivery` executing `Dispatch Package`
  - `issues`: `Invoice` created by `Issue Invoice`
  - `bills`: `Order` referenced on `Issue Invoice`
  - `records`: `Payment` registered by `Record Payment`
  - `applies_to`: `Invoice` credited by `Record Payment`
  - `confirms`: `Delivery` verified by `Confirm Delivery`
  - `closes`: `Order` finalized by `Close Order`
- **O2O direction conventions**: Rows in `o2o` read left-to-right as `(ocel:oid) --[ocel:qualifier]--> (ocel:oid_2)`:
  - `Order` `contains` `Item`
  - `Package` `packs` `Item`
  - `Delivery` `carries` `Package`
  - `Invoice` `bills` `Order`
  - `Payment` `partially_settles` or `completes_settlement` `Invoice` (encoding `M1` as the initial partial settlement and `M2` as the second partial payment that completes settlement of `V1`)
- **Direct relation policy for logistics and financial events**: The model uses a **direct operational-involvement policy** rather than transitive propagation:
  - `Reserve Stock` (`E06`) and `Pack Package` (`E07`, for `P1`) link directly to `O100` and `O200` with `operational_context` because the process description explicitly states both orders share those events.
  - `Pack Package` (`E08`, for `P2`) links directly to `P2` and `I2` only; its link to `O100` remains structural in `o2o` (`O100 contains I2`, `P2 packs I2`).
  - `Dispatch Package` links to the `Package` and `Delivery`, and `Confirm Delivery` links only to the `Delivery`.
  - `Issue Invoice` links directly to `V1`, `O100`, and `O200` (explicitly billed), while `Record Payment` links only to the `Payment` (`M1` or `M2`) and `Invoice` (`V1`). Payment amounts (`100.0` and `150.0`) are stored as event attributes in `events`.
- **Preventing confusion between source facts and analytical propagation**: Observed E2O relations remain isolated in `relations`, whereas structural ownership and containment reside in `o2o`. Any downstream analytical projection (e.g., attributing `Confirm Delivery` or `Record Payment` back to `Order` or `Item`) is never written into the base `relations` table; if computed in an extension, it would require a separate derived DataFrame or an explicit `derived:` qualifier prefix.
- **Version-dependent PM4Py assumption**: The pipeline assumes a PM4Py release supporting OCEL 2.0 constructor keyword arguments `OCEL(events=events, objects=objects, relations=relations, o2o=o2o)` and top-level discovery/inspection functions (`ocel_get_object_types`, `ocel_object_type_activities`, `ocel_flattening`, `discover_ocdfg`, `discover_otg`, `discover_etot`, `discover_oc_petri_net`, `ocel_objects_interactions_summary`). The `OCEL(...)` instantiation is isolated in one helper block so older signatures (e.g., `OCEL(events=..., objects=..., relations=...)` with `o2o` attached afterward) can be adapted without touching any data semantics.

    assert len(objects) == 12, f"Expected 12 objects, got {len(objects)}"

    assert set(relations["ocel:eid"]) == event_ids, "Unlinked events detected"
    assert set(relations["ocel:oid"]) == object_ids, "Unlinked objects detected"

    # Activity and timestamp consistency between events and relations
    ev_check = relations.merge(

| `Pack Package` | `E07`, `E08` | `Package`, `Item` (plus `Order` on `E07`) |
| `Dispatch Package` | `E09`, `E10` | `Package`, `Delivery` |
| `Issue Invoice` | `E11` | `Invoice`, `Order` |
| `Record Payment` | `E12`, `E15` | `Payment`, `Invoice` |
| `Confirm Delivery` | `E13`, `E14` | `Delivery` |
| `Close Order` | `E16`, `E17` | `Order` |

### Consequential events for convergence and divergence

- **`E07` (`Pack Package` for `P1`)**: Touches `P1`, two `Item` objects (`I1`, `I3`), and two `Order` objects (`O100`, `O200`). This is the central **operational convergence** event: two independent order threads coalesce into a single physical package.
- **`E06` (`Reserve Stock`)**: Touches two `Item` objects (`I1`, `I3`) across two `Order` objects (`O100`, `O200`), introducing **cross-order convergence** before physical packing begins.
- **`E11` (`Issue Invoice` for `V1`)**: Touches one `Invoice` (`V1`) and two `Order` objects (`O100`, `O200`), creating **financial convergence** where two order lifecycles share a single billing document.
- **Divergence drivers**: `O100` exhibits **1-to-many divergence** across `Add Item` (`E02`, `E03`) and splits across two packages/deliveries via `o2o` (`P1`/`D1` and `P2`/`D2`), while `V1` exhibits **1-to-many financial divergence** across two `Record Payment` events (`E12`, `E15`).

## 4. Expected flattened behavior

### `Order` flattening (`O100`, `O200`)
- **Approximate trace shape**:
  - `O100` (length 7): `Create Order -> Add Item -> Add Item -> Reserve Stock -> Pack Package -> Issue Invoice -> Close Order`.
  - `O200` (length 6): `Create Order -> Add Item -> Reserve Stock -> Pack Package -> Issue Invoice -> Close Order`.
- **Copied shared events**: `E06` (`Reserve Stock`), `E07` (`Pack Package` for `P1`), and `E11` (`Issue Invoice`) are duplicated into both `O100` and `O200` traces.
- **Omitted lifecycle aspects**: Packing of `P2` (`E08`), both `Dispatch Package` events (`E09`, `E10`), both `Confirm Delivery` events (`E13`, `E14`), and both `Record Payment` events (`E12`, `E15`) disappear completely.
- **Likely DFG edges**: `Create Order -> Add Item` (2), `Add Item -> Add Item` (1, self-loop), `Add Item -> Reserve Stock` (2), `Reserve Stock -> Pack Package` (2), `Pack Package -> Issue Invoice` (2), `Issue Invoice -> Close Order` (2).
- **Misleading interpretations**: An analyst looking only at the `Order` DFG sees `Pack Package -> Issue Invoice -> Close Order` and might assume invoicing happened immediately after packing all items (`P2` is invisible), that there were two separate stock reservations/packings/invoices (because shared events are counted twice), and that orders closed without delivery confirmation or payment settlement.

### `Package` flattening (`P1`, `P2`)
- **Approximate trace shape**:
  - `P1` (length 2): `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package`.
  - `P2` (length 2): `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package`.
- **Copied shared events**: None (`P1` and `P2` share no events in `relations`).
- **Omitted lifecycle aspects**: Order creation, item addition, stock reservation, delivery confirmation (`E13`, `E14`), invoicing, payments, and order closure.
- **Likely DFG edges**: `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` (2).
- **Misleading interpretations**: The process looks like a trivial linear two-step workflow with no cross-order consolidation (`P1` combining `O100` and `O200` is erased) and no post-dispatch outcome (it appears packages are never confirmed delivered).

### `Delivery` flattening (`D1`, `D2`)
- **Approximate trace shape**:
  - `D1` (length 2): `Dispatch Package -> Confirm Delivery`.
  - `D2` (length 2): `Dispatch Package -> Confirm Delivery`.
- **Copied shared events**: None.
- **Omitted lifecycle aspects**: Everything upstream of dispatch (`Create Order`, `Add Item`, `Reserve Stock`, `Pack Package`) and all financial/closing steps (`Issue Invoice`, `Record Payment`, `Close Order`).
- **Likely DFG edges**: `Dispatch Package -> Confirm Delivery` (2).
- **Misleading interpretations**: Flattening suggests delivery is an isolated two-step silo, hiding that `D1` delivers items for both `O100` and `O200` whereas `D2` delivers only `I2` for `O100`, and hiding that `D2` is confirmed (`2026-04-07 09:00 UTC`) long after `Issue Invoice` and `M1`.

### `Invoice` flattening (`V1`)
- **Approximate trace shape**:
  - `V1` (length 3): `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment -> Record Payment`.
- **Copied shared events**: None across invoice cases (only 1 `Invoice` exists, though `E11` was shared with two `Order` objects).
- **Omitted lifecycle aspects**: All order creation, item reservation, packing, dispatch, delivery confirmation, and order closure events.
- **Likely DFG edges**: `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` (1), `Record Payment -> Record Payment` (1, self-loop).
- **Misleading interpretations**: With a single trace, the `Invoice` log skeleton and DFG look deterministic and detached from physical fulfillment—hiding that `M1` occurred before both deliveries were confirmed while `M2` occurred after `D2` was confirmed, and hiding that `V1` consolidates two orders.

### Why flattening on `Payment` is particularly weak
Each `Payment` object (`M1`, `M2`) participates in exactly one event (`Record Payment` at `E12` and `E15`, respectively). Flattening on `Payment` produces two length-1 traces (`[Record Payment]`, `[Record Payment]`). The resulting DFG contains **zero activity-to-activity edges** (only start/end arcs on `Record Payment`), the log skeleton yields only trivial presence constraints with no temporal ordering, and the view completely loses the fact that both payments settle the same invoice (`V1`) across multiple dates to pay for two consolidated orders.

## 5. Expected object-centric structures

- **OC-DFG (`discover_ocdfg`)**:
  - Produces a multi-layered directed graph where edges are typed and colored per object type.
  - The `Order` layer tracks `O100` and `O200` converging into `Reserve Stock`, `Pack Package` (`E07`), and `Issue Invoice` (`E11`), before closing independently (`Close Order`).
  - The `Item` layer links `Add Item -> Reserve Stock -> Pack Package` for `I1` and `I3`, and `Add Item -> Pack Package` directly for `I2` (skipping `Reserve Stock`).
  - The `Package` (`Pack Package -> Dispatch Package`) and `Delivery` (`Dispatch Package -> Confirm Delivery`) layers hand off at `Dispatch Package`, preserving `P1`/`D1` alongside `P2`/`D2` without collapsing them into `Order`.
  - The `Invoice` layer captures `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` with a `Record Payment -> Record Payment` self-loop (`V1` spanning `M1` and `M2`), while `Payment` appears at `Record Payment` with no outgoing transitions.
- **OTG (`discover_otg`)**:
  - Reveals the empirical coupling topology among object types inferred from co-occurring E2O relations: `Order` connects directly to `Item` (via `Add Item`, `Reserve Stock`, `Pack Package`), to `Package` (via `E07` on `P1`), and to `Invoice` (via `Issue Invoice` on `V1`).
  - `Package` bridges `Item`/`Order` to `Delivery` (via `Dispatch Package`), while `Invoice` bridges `Order` to `Payment` (via `Record Payment`).
  - Notably, `Payment` has no direct edge to `Order`, `Item`, `Package`, or `Delivery` in the OTG because `M1` and `M2` only co-occur with `V1`.
- **ET–OT graph (`discover_etot`)**:
  - Explicitly maps each activity to the set of object types it synchronizes: e.g., `Pack Package` bridges `{Package, Item, Order}` (driven by `P1`'s cross-order link on `O100`/`O200`), `Dispatch Package` bridges `{Package, Delivery}`, `Issue Invoice` bridges `{Invoice, Order}`, and `Record Payment` bridges `{Payment, Invoice}`.
  - Highlights single-type lifecycle boundaries (`Create Order`, `Confirm Delivery`, `Close Order`).
- **Object-centric Petri net (`discover_oc_petri_net`)**:
  - Synthesizes a typed Petri net where places carry typed tokens (`Order`, `Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, `Payment`) and shared transitions carry variable/multiple arcs where convergence or divergence occurs (such as `Pack Package` synchronizing `Package`, `Item`, and optionally `Order` tokens; `Issue Invoice` synchronizing `Order` and `Invoice`; and `Record Payment` cycling on `Invoice` while consuming/producing `Payment`).
  - Because OC-Petri net discovery generalizes from a small, finite sample (2 orders, 1 invoice, 2 payments) using per-type projections and heuristics, **it represents one plausible behavioral abstraction rather than a uniquely determined model**—for instance, whether `Order` tokens are shown as optional on `Pack Package` (reflecting `E07` vs `E08`) or whether concurrency between `Confirm Delivery` and `Record Payment` is strictly interleaved or parallel depends on the discovery abstraction.
- **Object-interactions summary (`ocel_objects_interactions_summary`)**:
  - Tabulates co-occurrence frequencies across events for object pairs.
  - Explicitly surfaces `P1`'s dense cross-cutting footprint (`P1` interacts with `O100`, `O200`, `I1`, `I3` at `E07`, and `D1` at `E09`), contrasting with `P2` (which interacts only with `I2` at `E08` and `D2` at `E10`).
  - Surfaces `V1` as the sole financial hub linking `O100` and `O200` (at `E11`) to `M1` (at `E12`) and `M2` (at `E15`), while `M1` and `M2` never interact directly with each other or with `O100`/`O200` in the E2O table.

## 6. Modeling alternatives

1. **Source-minimal E2O relations**:
   - *Policy*: Link each event strictly to its primary operational subject(s) without secondary context—e.g., `Reserve Stock` (`E06`) links only to `I1`, `I3`; `Pack Package` (`E07`) links only to `P1`, `I1`, `I3` (not `O100`/`O200`); `Issue Invoice` (`E11`) links only to `V1` (relying on `o2o` `V1 bills O100/O200` for order attribution).
   - *Impact on discovery*: `Order` flattening shrinks to `Create Order -> Add Item -> Close Order`, removing `Reserve Stock`, `Pack Package`, and `Issue Invoice` from `Order` DFGs. In the OTG and OC-DFG, direct `Order`–`Package` and `Order`–`Invoice` co-occurrence edges vanish; convergence of `O100` and `O200` becomes visible only by querying `o2o` rather than E2O discovery alone.

2. **Full operational involvement (chosen policy)**:
   - *Policy*: Record every object directly named as operationally participating in an event (`O100`/`O200` in `E06`, `E07`, `E11`; `D1`/`P1` in `E09`, etc.) using descriptive qualifiers (`operational_context`, `bills`), while leaving unstated downstream transitive ties (`P2`->`O100`, `D1`->`O100`/`O200`, `M1`->`O100`/`O200`) in `o2o`.
   - *Impact on discovery*: Captures true operational synchronization points (`E06`, `E07`, `E11`) directly in the OC-DFG, ET–OT, and interactions summary without inventing false direct E2O observations for `Confirm Delivery` or `Record Payment`.

3. **O2O-propagated analytical relations**:
   - *Policy*: Materialize transitive closures from `o2o` into `relations`—for example, attaching `O100` to `E08` (`Pack Package` `P2`), attaching `O100` and `O200` (and `I1`, `I2`, `I3`) to `Dispatch Package`, `Confirm Delivery`, and `Record Payment`.
   - *Impact on discovery*: `Order` flattened traces expand to include all logistics and payment events, turning `Order` DFGs into dense graphs with interleaved `Confirm Delivery` and `Record Payment` loops. However, it artificially couples `M1`/`M2` directly to `O100`/`O200` in the OTG and interactions summary, inflating interaction counts and creating severe risks of double-counting `payment_amount` across orders.

## 7. Failure modes

1. **Relation inflation**: Propagating `o2o` links indiscriminately into `relations` causes every downstream event to carry dozens of `Order` and `Item` rows, distorting OC-DFG arc frequencies and object-interaction metrics.
2. **Missing direct E2O links**: Omitting stated shared objects (such as dropping `O100`/`O200` from `E07` or `E11`, or dropping `V1` from `E12`/`E15`) severs process handoffs in the OC-DFG and makes `Order` or `Invoice` lifecycles appear disconnected.
3. **Treating O2O propagation as observation**: Analysts or automated KPI scripts may interpret derived E2O links as proof that the ERP payment module natively tracks order-level payments or that delivery confirmation explicitly inspected individual orders, masking actual system boundaries.
4. **Duplicate shared events after flattening**: Flattening on `Order` copies `E06` (`Reserve Stock`), `E07` (`Pack Package`), and `E11` (`Issue Invoice`) into both `O100` and `O200`. Naive aggregation (e.g., summing `E11` counts or packing workload per trace) double-counts shared work.
5. **Ambiguous payment allocation**: `V1` bills both `O100` and `O200`, and `M1` (`100`) plus `M2` (`150`) settle `V1`, with no per-order invoice line items provided. Attempting to allocate `100` and `150` across `O100` and `O200` (e.g., 50/50 split vs. item-count split) introduces fabricated financial figures.
6. **Overly short `Payment` traces**: Flattening on `Payment` yields 1-event traces (`M1` -> `[E12]`, `M2` -> `[E15]`), producing empty DFGs and uninformative log skeletons that can mislead users into thinking payment processing has no upstream preconditions or structure.
7. **Constructor / API version mismatch**: PM4Py versions differ on whether `OCEL(...)` accepts `o2o` as a constructor keyword argument and on exact return types of discovery helpers; failing to isolate the constructor risks crashing the pipeline even when the underlying DataFrames are valid.
8. **False total order for same-time events**: Although all 17 events here have distinct UTC timestamps, real operational logs frequently exhibit identical timestamps (e.g., batch jobs or second-level truncation). Directly-follows discovery (`discover_dfg`, `discover_ocdfg`) over tied timestamps turns arbitrary DataFrame row order into spurious causal edges.
