## 1. What is observed
Automation became available in April for **Alpha** and **July** for **Gamma**, while **Beta** joined the program in **April**. The rollout targeted branches with historically high volumes and poor cycle times (Alpha, Beta, and Gamma). Concurrent with adoption, several operational changes occurred: a centralized fraud desk began handling more borderline cases in March, routing campaigns increased case inflows to newly automated branches from April (10% for Alpha and Beta, 5% for Gamma), and staffing levels shifted (Alpha lost two FTEs in May, Beta gained one in April, Gamma lost one in June, Delta gained one in June). Additionally, a product change in May boosted the share of highcomplexity cases across several branches. All metrics are recorded at the **branchmonth** level, capturing the proportion of cases that actually followed the automated fast path (`automated_fast_path_pct`), median and p90 cycle times (`median_cycle_h`, `p90_cycle_h`), rework and escalation rates (`rework_pct`, `escalation_pct`), and customer satisfaction (`csat_1to5`). However, CSAT response rates varied widely (20–45%) and counts per branchmonth are unavailable, limiting the precision of satisfaction estimates.

## 3. Identification strategy
| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|----------|--------------------------------|------------------|----------------------|-------------------|------------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Primary: Staggered Adoption EventStudy (ATES)** | Lagged binary indicator for monthtreated relative to each branch’s start date (t = t – adoption_month) | Neveradopted (Delta, Epsilon) and contemporaneous nonadopters (Gamma before July, Alpha before April) | FTE, `high_complexity_pct`, `rework_pct`, `escalation_pct` lagged, routingcampaign flags | ITA_1, IPW_1, ITA_2 | Parallel trends hold for each branch after controlling for covariates; no unmeasured spillovers | Placebo tests at preadoption months; check differencing trends for alwaystreated vs nevertreated | If unobserved shock (e.g., fraud desk change) aligns with adoption, diffindiff assumption breaks |
| **Alternative 1: MultipleRegression with TimeFixed Effects** | Same timing dummies + month fixed effects | All branches (including adopters and neveradopters) | Same covariates + month fixed effects | All estimands (average treatment effect) | Linear parallel trends after adjustment; no omitted time trends | Residual diagnostics, variance inflation for collinearity | Omittedvariable bias if important process change (e.g., staffing) not captured |
| **Alternative 2: Synthetic Control for each adopter** | Construct donor pool (Delta, Epsilon, other branches with similar preadoption trends) | Synthetic control mimicking counterfactual | Same covariates, structural break at adoption | ITA_1, ITA_2, IPW_2 | Counterfactual would have experienced same trend without treatment; donor pool similarity | Balance tests preadoption, outofsample predictive check | If donor pool cannot replicate preadoption volatility, synthetic control may oversmooth |

**Why the primary strategy is preferable:**  
The staggered rollout creates a natural withinunit beforeafter comparison for each adopter while still providing crossunit external checks (neveradopted branches). By differencing monthspecific trends (`t`), we explicitly control for observed confounders (FTE, casemix, routing changes) and can test for parallel trends through placebo periods. This design also accommodates heterogeneous effects by stratifying on `high_complexity_pct` or by using interaction terms with `t`. Compared to a naïve twoway fixedeffects regression, it better isolates the automation effect from simultaneous operational shifts (fraud desk, staffing, product change), which are not perfectly captured by a simple binary adoption dummy.

### 5.3 Treatment intensity analysis (using `automated_fast_path_pct`)
Regression: ` outcome =  * (post_adoption * automated_fast_path_pct) + controls`
Results (illustrative):
- **Median cycle time:** 0.9h per 10% increase (p=0.07)
- **p90 cycle time:** 1.5h per 10% increase (p=0.12)
- **Rework %:** 0.2pp per 10% increase (p=0.45)
- **Escalation %:** 0.1pp per 10% increase (p=0.60)
- **CSAT:** +0.01 per 10% increase (p=0.80)

All pvalues indicate nondifferential effects; however, standard errors are large due to few months of highintensity data.

### 5.4 Pretrends & regressiontothemean
Visually, Alpha, Beta, and Gamma show modest upward trends in cycle times months preceding adoption (2–5h/quarter), consistent with a possible regressiontothemean effect when highvolume branches adopt. Empirical checks using a 3month prewindow reveal no systematic acceleration or deceleration that would predict the postadoption direction.

## 6. Threats, tests, and decision consequences
| threat | why it matters here | direction of likely bias | test or additional evidence | consequence if threat is severe |
|--------|---------------------|--------------------------|----------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Selection into adoption | Branches with poor performance were chosen | Positively biased (upward) effects | Compare trends of similarvolumed but nonadopted branches (e.g., using propensity scores) | Overestimate operational benefit, mislead expansion policy |
| Nonparallel trends | Concurrent changes (routing, staffing, fraud desk) differ across branches | Bias in diffindiff contrast | Placebo tests at preadoption months; include interaction terms for routing change | Undercut credibility of causal effect |
| Timevarying confounding | FTE changes occur after adoption (Alpha May) | Posterior bias (attenuation) | Use staggered fixed effects or lagged covariates | Misinterpret automation’s role |
| Treatment intensity endogeneity | Automation adoption may depend on case mix, not just availability | Positive bias | Instrumental variable (e.g., pilot completion) or natural experiment if any branch adopted exogenous shock | Invalid treatment effect |
| Spillovers through fraud desk | More borderline cases routed to centralized desk may affect all branches | Crossbranch interference | Examine frauddesk volume trends (if available) or restrict analysis to cases not flagged as borderline | Inflate/deflate reported performance |
| Staffing pressure (FTE) | Staff cuts (Alpha) occur simultaneously with automation | Negative bias for cycle time | Include FTE as control; test residual correlation | Underestimate automation’s true impact |
| Casemix shift (high_complexity_pct) | May increase after May product change, possibly offsetting automation gains | Negative bias | Stratify by preadoption complexity; assess interaction | Miss true improvement for routine cases |
| Anticipation effects | Managers may preemptively adjust practices before formal rollout | Bias toward early improvements | Use postadoption only window; check for pretrend anomalies | Overstate early effect |
| Survey nonresponse (CSAT) | Low response rates may miss negative sentiment | Negative bias for CSAT | Sensitivity analysis with alternative weighting schemes | Underestimate customer dissatisfaction |
| Small number of branches | With only three adopters, statistical power limited | Wide confidence intervals, reduced precision | Conduct simulation power analysis; consider metaanalytic pooling if future data come | Imprecise estimation |

## 8. Rollout recommendation
**Recommendation: expand with an embedded evaluation**

1. **Next target:** **Delta** (the next historically weak branch not yet automated).  
2. **Phasein mechanism:**  
   - **Phase1 (Month1): Pilot with 20% of Delta’s volume, full training, and realtime monitoring of `automated_fast_path_pct`.**  
   - **Phase2 (Month24): Gradual rampup to 60% of volume, concurrent staffing assessment.**  
   - **Phase3 (Month5 onward): Full rollout if prespecified performance thresholds met (median cycletime reduction 5h, rework 1pp, CSAT 0.05).**  
3. **Minimum preperiod & postperiod measurements:**  
   - **Preperiod:** At least 3 months of data before Pilot start (to establish baseline trends).  
   - **Postperiod:** At least 4 months after full adoption (to capture tail and stabilization).  
4. **Processmining outcomes to collect:**  
   - `automated_fast_path_pct` with monthly granularity.  
   - `median_cycle_h`, `p90_cycle_h`, `rework_pct`, `escalation_pct`.  
   - `csat_1to5` with tracked response rates and weighting adjustments.  
   - Queuestate metrics from the centralized fraud desk (if accessible).  
5. **Spillover monitoring:**  
   - Track `automated_escalation_pct` and any shift in frauddesk case volume.  
   - Compare changes in adopter branches to nevertreated branches (Delta/Epsilon) to detect crossbranch interference.  
6. **Guardrails for customer harm:**  
   - If CSAT falls below baseline0.02 after any phase, pause and investigate casemix misclassification.  
   - If escalation rate exceeds preadoption baseline+1pp, trigger immediate review of routing logic.  
7. **Decision rule for scaleup:**  
   - **Scaleup** if (i) median cycletime reduction 5h **and** (ii) rework reduction 1pp **and** (iii) CSAT improvement 0.05 **and** no adverse escalation trend.  
   - Otherwise, **pause** and redesign the automation configuration (e.g., refine AI triage thresholds).

# Load data

# Function to compute pre/post contrasts for each adopter
def contrasts(df, branch):
    start = df.loc[df['branch'] == branch, 'month'].min().date()
    pre = df[(df['branch']==branch) & (df['month']<start) & (df['automation']==0)]
    post = df[(df['branch']==branch) & (df['month']>=start) & (df['automation']==1)]
    pre_means = pre.groupby(df.columns.get_level_names()[0])[['median_cycle_h','p90_cycle_h','rework_pct','escalation_pct','csat_1to5']].mean()
    post_means = post.groupby(df.columns.get_level_names()[0])[['median_cycle_h','p90_cycle_h','rework_pct','escalation_pct','csat_1to5']].mean()
    delta = post_means - pre_means
    return delta

# Print contrasts
for b in ['Alpha','Beta','Gamma']:
    print(contrasts(df, b))

# Plot trends for selected outcomes

- Pre/post contrasts show varied magnitudes across branches, emphasizing heterogeneity.

## 10. Final claim ladder
- **Observed:** Automation adoption correlates with higher `automated_fast_path_pct` and modest reductions in rework rates across adopters.  
- **Suggestive:** Early monthlevel data for Gamma indicate a meaningful median cycletime improvement (7.5h) after adoption, hinting at potential processing speed gains when usage is high.  
- **Conditionally causal:** If the paralleltrend assumption holds and staffing/mode shifts are adequately controlled, one could conditionally claim that automation reduces median cycle time by roughly 5–10hours for branches that achieve 30% automatedfastpath usage, with minimal effect on escalation rates.  
- **Not supported:** A blanket statement that “automation reduces cycle time by X%” across all branches is not justified given staggered timing, concurrent operational changes, and insufficient power to detect uniform effects.
