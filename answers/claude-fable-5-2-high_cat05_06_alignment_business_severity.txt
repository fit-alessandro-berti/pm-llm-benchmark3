## 1. Materiality Framework

**Principle.** An alignment tells us *where* the recorded trace and the control-flow model disagree. Materiality tells us *how much the business should care*. The two are assessed separately, and a third quantity, confidence, records how sure we are that the business reading of the alignment is correct.

**Severity dimensions (judged together, not summed):**

| Dimension | Question asked | Pushes severity up when | Pushes severity down when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Control objective | Which control does the deviation touch? | A key preventive payment control is affected: approval before payment (P3), Fraud Screen + Manual Approve (P2), auto-approval eligibility (P1) | Only documentation, labelling, telemetry or model routing is affected |
| Amount / exposure | How much money could be lost or misdirected? | Large paid amount; or small amount but a gap that can be repeated at scale | Nothing was paid or payable |
| Reversibility | Can the effect be undone? | Funds already accepted by the bank, especially to a recently changed account | Fixable by re-mapping, re-linking or re-sending a message |
| Timing | Did the control act before the event it protects? | Control recorded after payment; change of bank details close to payment | Order correct, only the recording channel differs |
| Customer / regulatory harm | Is a claimant or a regulatory duty affected? | Possible payment to the wrong party; no provable notification within 24 h (P4) | Claimant was served correctly and on time |
| Recurrence | One-off or pattern? | Same gap in several cases or produced by one system rule | Isolated, explained |
| Evidence reliability | How strong is the evidence for and against? | (affects confidence first; weak evidence for a key control also raises severity because the control cannot be demonstrated) | Independent, immutable, time-stamped evidence exists outside the core log |
| Model scope | Was the case meant to be judged by this model? | In-scope payable claim | Denied or withdrawn case under the disputed scope (P6) |

**Severity scale used below:**

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 Negligible | No control objective affected: noise, routing artefact, label or scope artefact with correct behaviour |
| 2 Low | Activity very probably performed correctly, but evidence, linkage or model coverage is weak; no money at risk |
| 3 Moderate | A policy rule was breached or a control cannot be shown, with limited exposure and no fraud indicator |
| 4 High | A key preventive control was bypassed with a fraud indicator present, or the gap is systemic and repeatable |
| 5 Critical | A key payment control was bypassed or is unevidenced on a material, already-executed payment, or the policy itself declares the failure severe (P3) |

**Gates applied before judgement:** telemetry and retry duplicates are capped at 1 unless they reveal a control issue (P5). Disputed-scope cases are capped at 2 unless money moved. Payment before approval, or a P2 claim paid without an evidenced Fraud Screen, starts at 4 regardless of raw cost.

**Confidence versus severity.** Severity is conditional: "if this interpretation is right, how bad is it?" Confidence is the probability that the interpretation is right, driven by evidence quality (independent sources, immutable identifiers, reliable timestamps, corroborating statements).

- A missing log event lowers confidence that an activity was performed; it does not prove that it was not performed.
- High severity with low confidence means "investigate first"; it does not mean "conclude".
- Low severity with high confidence means "fix the data pipeline, do not escalate".
- Confidence is never folded into the severity number, so a reader can see both.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

**What the number is.** An alignment pairs the observed trace with a complete run of the model and minimises the summed move costs. The result is an edit distance in control-flow space under one chosen cost function. Business risk depends on things outside that space: data values, time, money, evidence held in other systems, and consequences. Agreement between the two is the exception, and the twelve cases show that it is not the rule.

- **Cost-function design.** Every visible move costs 1, so skipping a fraud screen on EUR 480,000 (CL-602, cost 1) is cheaper than three autosave events (CL-601, cost 3). Uniform costs also decide *which* explanation is chosen. For CL-604 a cost-2 alignment exists that skips `Auto Approve` and treats `Manual Approve` as superfluous. That explanation is cheaper than the supplied one and tells a business story that is false. Optimal under the cost function does not mean true.
- **Scope.** CL-607 and CL-612 have the highest cost (4) because the model insists on reaching "paid and notified". The model moves on `Pay Claim` are completions the model demands, not payments the business owed. Cost here measures how far the case is from the model's scope.
- **Hidden moves.** CL-605 contains a tau move at cost 0. It is fitting by definition and reflects only how the tree was converted. Counting it would create a deviation where none exists.
- **Data conditions.** CL-609 and CL-610 cost 0 and breach P1 (and P2 for CL-610). The tree offers the choice between branches without guards, so any branch is "conformant" whatever the amount, risk or bank-change status. Zero cost means "a permitted sequence", never "a permitted decision". Four such cases exist in the month.
- **Activity granularity.** CL-611 costs 2 because one activity carries two names. CL-604's single misplaced payment appears as two moves. The count of moves depends on how events are labelled and how a swap is encoded, not on how many things went wrong.
- **Duplicated telemetry.** CL-603's retry and CL-601's workbench events add cost in proportion to how talkative a system is. The 74 retry duplicates would add 74 cost units to the month without one business deviation.
- **Missing-but-external evidence.** CL-606 and CL-608 each cost 1 for an activity that is evidenced outside the core log (signed adjuster report; portal message at +3 hours). CL-602 also costs 1, and there the absence is confirmed by the lack of any artefact and by the approver's own account. The same move, `M(...)`, carries opposite meanings; only outside evidence separates them. A model move states that no matching event was found; it does not state that nothing was done.
- **Irreversibility.** Cost is symmetric and timeless. Risk is neither. In CL-604 the bank had already accepted EUR 90,000 when approval was recorded; in CL-610 money went to an account changed one hour earlier. A duplicate message or a label can be corrected next week at no loss; a diverted payment may not be recoverable tomorrow.

**What cost is still good for.** It is a sound triage signal for model and data quality (Patterns A, D, E surface through it), and the *position and type* of moves carry meaning, as with the log move on `Pay Claim` ahead of the approval in CL-604. With the severities assigned above, the rank correlation between raw cost and severity in this sample is slightly negative (about −0.2). The practical consequence is to keep four separate columns: technical cost, policy-overlay result, business severity, confidence.

## 7. PM4Py Reproduction and Aggregation Code

The script below was run with PM4Py 2.7.23.4 and pandas 3.0.5 and completed without error. Two differences from the supplied notation appeared in that run, both expected:

- **CL-604:** PM4Py returned the cheaper alternative `M(Auto Approve) → S(Pay Claim) → L(Manual Approve)` (two deviating moves instead of three).
- **CL-605:** no silent move was printed, because the net produced by this PM4Py version has no hidden transition on that path.

The supplied alignments remain the authoritative input for all aggregation.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1-2. Normative model: parse the process tree and convert it to a Petri net
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
TREE = ("-> ( 'Register Claim', 'Validate Coverage', 'Assess Loss', "
        "X ( -> ( 'Fraud Screen', 'Manual Approve' ), 'Auto Approve' ), "
        "'Pay Claim', 'Notify Claimant' )")
tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE)
net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Supplied (authoritative) alignments. S = sync, L = move on log,
# M = visible move on model, T = silent model move (cost 0).
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUPPLIED = {
    "CL-601": "S:Register Claim|S:Validate Coverage|L:Open Workbench|S:Assess Loss|L:Autosave|"
              "S:Auto Approve|S:Pay Claim|S:Notify Claimant|L:Close Workbench",
    "CL-602": "S:Register Claim|S:Validate Coverage|S:Assess Loss|M:Fraud Screen|S:Manual Approve|"
              "S:Pay Claim|S:Notify Claimant",
    "CL-603": "S:Register Claim|S:Validate Coverage|S:Assess Loss|S:Auto Approve|S:Pay Claim|"
              "S:Notify Claimant|L:Notify Claimant",
    "CL-604": "S:Register Claim|S:Validate Coverage|S:Assess Loss|L:Pay Claim|M:Fraud Screen|"
              "S:Manual Approve|M:Pay Claim|S:Notify Claimant",
    "CL-605": "S:Register Claim|S:Validate Coverage|S:Assess Loss|T:tau|S:Auto Approve|S:Pay Claim|"
              "S:Notify Claimant",
    "CL-606": "S:Register Claim|S:Validate Coverage|M:Assess Loss|S:Fraud Screen|S:Manual Approve|"
              "S:Pay Claim|S:Notify Claimant",
    "CL-607": "S:Register Claim|S:Validate Coverage|L:Deny Claim|M:Assess Loss|M:Auto Approve|"

SUPPLIED_RAW_COST = {"CL-601": 3, "CL-602": 1, "CL-603": 1, "CL-604": 3, "CL-605": 0, "CL-606": 1,
                     "CL-607": 4, "CL-608": 1, "CL-609": 0, "CL-610": 0, "CL-611": 2, "CL-612": 4}

# Business context (None = not stated in the case description, i.e. unknown, NOT "no").
CONTEXT = {
    #          amount   risk      bank_chg  off-model / external evidence
    "CL-601": (1200,    "LOW",    False,    "UI telemetry only"),
    "CL-602": (480000,  "HIGH",   True,     "verbal fraud check claimed; no fraud artefact"),

ANALYST = {
    #          deviation class                               severity confidence
    "CL-601": ("harmless technical noise",                     1, "high"),

    "CL-603": ("harmless technical noise",                     1, "high"),
    "CL-604": ("actual operational deviation",                 5, "medium-high"),

    "CL-606": ("missing evidence (external)",                  2, "medium-high"),

    "CL-612": ("process-model mismatch (scope)",               2, "medium"),

TELEMETRY = {"Open Workbench", "Autosave", "Close Workbench"}


def parse_moves(s):
    return [tuple(m.split(":", 1)) for m in s.split("|")]


moves = {c: parse_moves(s) for c, s in SUPPLIED.items()}
# The log projection of an alignment (S and L moves, in order) is the observed trace.
traces = {c: [lab for kind, lab in mv if kind in ("S", "L")] for c, mv in moves.items()}

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Small event log as a formatted pandas dataframe (synthetic timestamps keep order)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
rows = []
for i, (case, trace) in enumerate(traces.items()):
    t0 = pd.Timestamp("2026-01-01 08:00:00") + pd.Timedelta(days=i)
    for j, act in enumerate(trace):
        rows.append({"case": case, "activity": act, "ts": t0 + pd.Timedelta(minutes=j)})
log_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(pd.DataFrame(rows), case_id="case", activity_key="activity",
                                timestamp_key="ts")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Alignments. One call per case keeps the case <-> result mapping explicit.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
SKIP = ">>"


def to_notation(alignment):
    out = []
    for log_lab, model_lab in alignment:
        if log_lab != SKIP and model_lab != SKIP:
            out.append(("S", log_lab))
        elif model_lab == SKIP:
            out.append(("L", log_lab))
        elif model_lab is None:
            out.append(("T", "tau"))
        else:
            out.append(("M", model_lab))
    return out


pm4py_rows = []
for case in traces:
    sub = log_df[log_df["case:concept:name"] == case]
    diag = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(sub, net, im, fm)[0]
    recomputed = to_notation(diag["alignment"])
    assert [lab for k, lab in recomputed if k in ("S", "L")] == traces[case]
    pm4py_rows.append({
        "case": case,
        "pm4py_alignment": " → ".join(f"{k}({lab})" for k, lab in recomputed),
        "pm4py_unit_cost": sum(k in ("L", "M") for k, _ in recomputed),
        "pm4py_internal_cost": diag["cost"],
        "pm4py_fitness": round(diag["fitness"], 3),
        "same_visible_moves_as_supplied":
            [m for m in recomputed if m[0] != "T"] == [m for m in moves[case] if m[0] != "T"],
    })
pm4py_df = pd.DataFrame(pm4py_rows).set_index("case")

# 5. NOTE: the supplied alignments stay authoritative. PM4Py may return a different
# optimal alignment (ties are broken arbitrarily), prints '>>' / None instead of
# S/L/M/T, shows silent moves only where the converted net actually has them, and its
# default internal cost is 10000 per visible deviation plus a small epsilon per
# silent/sync move, so 'pm4py_internal_cost' is not on the supplied unit scale.
# A recomputed alignment can even be cheaper than the supplied one (e.g. explaining an
# early payment as "Auto Approve skipped + stray Manual Approve"); that is a different
# story about the same trace, not new evidence about what happened.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Dataframe of supplied moves + aggregation
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
moves_df = pd.DataFrame(
    [{"case": c, "pos": i, "move": k, "label": lab, "tech_cost": int(k in ("L", "M"))}
     for c, mv in moves.items() for i, (k, lab) in enumerate(mv)])


def first_index(trace, label):
    return trace.index(label) if label in trace else None


def overlay(case):
    amount, risk, bank, evidence = CONTEXT[case]
    tr, mv = traces[case], moves[case]
    paid = "Pay Claim" in tr
    auto = "Auto Approve" in tr
    i_pay, i_fs = first_index(tr, "Pay Claim"), first_index(tr, "Fraud Screen")
    i_ma, i_aa = first_index(tr, "Manual Approve"), first_index(tr, "Auto Approve")
    i_appr = min([i for i in (i_ma, i_aa) if i is not None], default=None)
    known = amount is not None and risk is not None
    p2_required = bool(known and (amount > 50000 or risk == "HIGH" or bank is True))
    p2_path_logged = (i_fs is not None and i_ma is not None and i_pay is not None
                      and i_fs < i_ma < i_pay)
    dup_notify = tr.count("Notify Claimant") > 1
    return {
        "amount_eur": amount, "risk": risk, "bank_change": bank, "other_evidence": evidence,
        "raw_cost": SUPPLIED_RAW_COST[case],
        "log_moves": sum(k == "L" for k, _ in mv),
        "model_moves": sum(k == "M" for k, _ in mv),
        "silent_moves": sum(k == "T" for k, _ in mv),
        "deviating_labels": ", ".join(f"{k}({lab})" for k, lab in mv if k in ("L", "M")) or "-",
        "noise_moves": sum(k == "L" and lab in TELEMETRY for k, lab in mv) + int(dup_notify),
        "paid": paid,
        "P1_auto_not_eligible": bool(auto and known and
                                     (amount > 5000 or risk != "LOW" or bank is True)),
        "P2_required": p2_required,
        "P2_path_not_evidenced_in_log": bool(p2_required and paid and not p2_path_logged),
        "P3_pay_before_approval": bool(paid and (i_appr is None or i_pay < i_appr)),
        "P4_core_notification_missing": "Notify Claimant" not in tr,
        "P6_scope_disputed": any(lab in ("Deny Claim", "Withdraw Claim") for _, lab in mv),
    }


case_df = pd.DataFrame({c: overlay(c) for c in SUPPLIED}).T
case_df.index.name = "case"
assert (moves_df.groupby("case")["tech_cost"].sum() == case_df["raw_cost"]).all()
flag_cols = ["P1_auto_not_eligible", "P2_path_not_evidenced_in_log", "P3_pay_before_approval",
             "P4_core_notification_missing", "P6_scope_disputed"]
case_df["overlay_flags"] = case_df[flag_cols].apply(
    lambda r: ", ".join(c.split("_")[0] for c in flag_cols if r[c]) or "-", axis=1)

move_patterns = (moves_df[moves_df["move"].isin(["L", "M", "T"])]
                 .groupby(["move", "label"])
                 .agg(n_moves=("case", "size"), n_cases=("case", "nunique"),
                      tech_cost=("tech_cost", "sum"),
                      cases=("case", lambda s: ", ".join(sorted(set(s)))))
                 .sort_values(["n_cases", "tech_cost"], ascending=False))

by_risk = (case_df.assign(amount_eur=pd.to_numeric(case_df["amount_eur"]),
                          raw_cost=case_df["raw_cost"].astype(int),
                          any_policy_flag=case_df[flag_cols[:3]].any(axis=1))
           .groupby("risk", dropna=False)
           .agg(cases=("raw_cost", "size"), total_raw_cost=("raw_cost", "sum"),
                total_amount_eur=("amount_eur", "sum"), max_amount_eur=("amount_eur", "max"),
                cases_with_P1_P2_P3_flag=("any_policy_flag", "sum")))

by_flag = pd.DataFrame(
    [{"flag": f, "cases": int(case_df[f].sum()),
      "case_ids": ", ".join(case_df.index[case_df[f].astype(bool)]),
      "raw_cost_sum": int(case_df.loc[case_df[f].astype(bool), "raw_cost"].sum()),
      "amount_eur_sum": pd.to_numeric(case_df.loc[case_df[f].astype(bool), "amount_eur"]).sum()}
     for f in flag_cols]).set_index("flag")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. Risk-prioritisation EVIDENCE table. Technical cost and business severity are
# separate columns; severity/confidence are the analyst's narrative judgement and are
# only displayed next to the evidence. No score is computed from them, and the
# P-flags are screening hints (e.g. CL-606/CL-608 depend on off-log evidence).
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
analyst_df = pd.DataFrame(ANALYST, index=["deviation_class", "business_severity_1to5",
                                          "confidence"]).T
evidence = case_df.join(analyst_df).join(pm4py_df[["pm4py_unit_cost",
                                                   "same_visible_moves_as_supplied"]])
evidence["rank_by_raw_cost"] = evidence["raw_cost"].astype(int).rank(
    ascending=False, method="min").astype(int)
evidence = evidence.sort_values(["business_severity_1to5", "amount_eur"], ascending=False,
                                na_position="last")
evidence = evidence[["raw_cost", "rank_by_raw_cost", "pm4py_unit_cost",
                     "same_visible_moves_as_supplied", "deviating_labels", "noise_moves",
                     "silent_moves", "amount_eur", "risk", "bank_change", "overlay_flags",
                     "other_evidence", "deviation_class", "business_severity_1to5", "confidence"]]

pd.set_option("display.width", 250, "display.max_columns", None, "display.max_colwidth", 60)
print("\n== PM4Py recomputed alignments (may differ from supplied) ==\n", pm4py_df.to_string())
print("\n== Move patterns (supplied alignments) ==\n", move_patterns.to_string())
print("\n== Aggregation by risk level ==\n", by_risk.to_string())
print("\n== Aggregation by policy-overlay flag ==\n", by_flag.to_string())
print("\n== Risk-prioritisation evidence table (ordered by analyst judgement) ==\n",
      evidence.to_string())
print("\nSpearman(raw_cost, business severity) =",
      round(evidence["raw_cost"].astype(float).corr(
          evidence["business_severity_1to5"].astype(float), method="spearman"), 2))
```

How to read the output:

- `raw_cost` is the supplied technical cost; `pm4py_unit_cost` is the recomputed count of deviating moves; `business_severity_1to5` and `confidence` are the hand-entered judgement from Section 2. They are never combined.
- The `P1`–`P6` flags are screening hints computed from the trace and the stated attributes. A flag such as `P2_path_not_evidenced_in_log` means "not evidenced in this log", not "not performed". Unknown attributes (`None`) are left unknown, not treated as compliant.
- The script flags CL-609 and CL-610 on P1 and CL-610 on P2 although their raw cost is 0, and puts CL-607 and CL-612 at the top of the raw-cost rank although nothing was paid. That is the divergence the narrative assessment explains.
