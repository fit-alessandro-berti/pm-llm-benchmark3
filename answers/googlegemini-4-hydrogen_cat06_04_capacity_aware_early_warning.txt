## 1. Decision rule

The policy separates **eligibility**, **evidence validity**, **calibration adjustment**, and **portfolio-level economic ranking** so that scarce Senior Diagnostic Swarm slots are spent only where causal impact exceeds both direct costs and queue externalities.

### A. Eligibility and Data-Integrity Gate
A case $i$ is eligible for a swarm only if all three conditions hold:
1. **Stage eligibility:** Current stage is `Triage` or `Diagnosis Started` (strictly prior to `Diagnosis Complete`).
2. **Temporal feasibility:** Remaining time to SLA deadline $T_{\text{SLA}, i} \ge 2.0\text{ h}$ (a 45-minute swarm plus handoff, resolution, and customer validation is rarely viable under 2.0 h; cases with $T_{\text{SLA}, i} < 2.0\text{ h}$ are routed to a lightweight fast-track check rather than a full swarm).
3. **Data integrity:** No critical live operational features are missing or imputed. If a critical feature fails (e.g., live queue telemetry), the alert is placed in **Data-Verification Hold** (15–30 minute telemetry check) before committing a swarm slot.

### B. Alert Persistence and Minimum Evidence Change
To prevent alert fatigue from 30-minute model refreshes, an alert on an eligible case triggers slot evaluation only if:
- It is the **first alert** in the current stage, **OR**
- A **material new event** occurred since the last evaluated alert (e.g., new customer diagnostic log, stage transition into `Diagnosis Started`, or calibrated risk jump $\Delta p_{\text{cal}} \ge +0.05$), **OR**
- The case enters `Diagnosis Started` with $T_{\text{SLA}, i} \le 3.5\text{ h}$ (window-closing urgency before `Diagnosis Complete`).

Alerts generated solely by time ticks without new evidence are suppressed.

### C. Segment Calibration and Causal-Effect Haircuts
Before scoring, convert observational `p_now` into a segment-adjusted calibrated probability interval $p_{\text{cal}, i}$ and conservative expected absolute risk reduction $\Delta p_i^{\text{adj}}$:
- **SME** (underpredicts by $0.04\text{–}0.09$): $p_{\text{cal}, i} = \min(1,\, p_{\text{now}, i} + 0.065)$ (range $+0.04$ to $+0.09$). Use midpoint of the supplied ARR range.
- **Strategic Enterprise** (overpredicts by $0.05\text{–}0.12$ post-workflow redesign): $p_{\text{cal}, i} = \max(0,\, p_{\text{now}, i} - 0.085)$ (range $-0.05$ to $-0.12$). Because baseline risk is inflated, scale midpoint ARR conservatively by $p_{\text{cal}, i} / p_{\text{now}, i}$ (and verify that lower-bound ARR still justifies intervention if false-alarm burden is high).
- **Public Sector** (acceptable calibration, moderate sample): $p_{\text{cal}, i} = p_{\text{now}, i}$ ($\pm 0.03$ uncertainty band). Use midpoint ARR.
- **Non-Native-Language Support** (small sample; uncertain calibration and effect): Treat $p_{\text{cal}, i} = p_{\text{now}, i} \pm 0.10$ and ARR across its full interval. Do not shrink estimates to zero solely due to sparsity; instead, require language-capable specialist participation so the intervention mechanism holds.

### D. Prioritization and Shadow-Price Rule
Rank all eligible, evidence-validated cases by **Net Expected Portfolio Value ($\text{NEV}_i$)**:

$$\text{NEV}_i = \Delta p_i^{\text{adj}} \cdot V_i - C_{\text{swarm}} - (1 - p_{\text{cal}, i}) \cdot B_i - \Lambda_{\text{spill}}(Q)$$

where:
- $V_i$ = value of avoiding breach;
- $C_{\text{swarm}} = €450$;
- $(1 - p_{\text{cal}, i}) B_i$ = expected false-alarm disruption burden (using full $B_i$ as a worst-case stress check);
- $\Lambda_{\text{spill}}(Q) = 6 \cdot \bar{s}(Q) \cdot \bar{V}_{\text{diag}}$ = congestion-dependent spillover shadow price imposed on six non-intervened diagnosis-stage cases. Using base spillover $\bar{s} = 0.02$ (range $0.01\text{–}0.03$) and typical untreated diagnosis-case breach value $\bar{V}_{\text{diag}} \approx €15{,}000\text{–}€20{,}000$, each slot carries a spillover shadow cost of **€1,800–€2,400** (base benchmark **€2,000**; rising to €3,000+ under heavy congestion).

**Intervention thresholds:**
1. **Risk-floor gate:** Require $p_{\text{cal}, i} \ge 0.38$ (prevents burning slots and inflicting high false-alarm disruption on cases likely to resolve naturally).
2. **Positive net value gate:** Intervene only if $\text{NEV}_i > 0$ (meaning expected direct value clears both €450 + expected burden *and* the ~€2,000 spillover shadow cost).
3. **Reserve option value:** When remaining daily slots $\le 1$ and an unlisted regulatory arrival is anticipated, require $\text{NEV}_i > \Omega_{\text{reserve}}$ (option value of holding the final slot, e.g., $\ge €2{,}500$), unless the case faces imminent `Diagnosis Complete`.
4. **Wait vs. Intervene in `Triage`:** If a case is in `Triage` with $T_{\text{SLA}, i} \ge 7.0\text{ h}$ and borderline $\text{NEV}_i$ or $p_{\text{cal}, i} < 0.40$, **Wait** rather than locking in spillover now; if $\text{NEV}_i$ is strongly positive and lower-bound direct benefit $> C_{\text{swarm}} + B_i$, **Intervene** now.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

### A. Cool-Down, Suppression, Escalation, and Re-Entry Rules
1. **Intra-stage cool-down (no swarm yet):** After an alert is evaluated and put on `Wait` or `Suppress`, suppress pure model-refresh re-alerts for **2 hours** unless a **state-change trigger** fires.
2. **State-change / re-entry triggers (override cool-down):**
   - **New external evidence:** Upload of customer diagnostic logs, new error signature, escalation ticket, or dependency failure.
   - **Stage transition:** Case moves from `Triage` $\to$ `Diagnosis Started` (re-enters evaluation because it is now entering the final intervention window).
   - **Material calibrated jump:** $\Delta p_{\text{cal}} \ge +0.05$ accompanied by non-missing telemetry.
   - **Deadline urgency gate:** Case in `Diagnosis Started` reaches $T_{\text{SLA}} \le 3.5\text{ h}$ without a prior swarm.
3. **Post-swarm cool-down (anti-reswarm rule):** Once a case receives a swarm, it is **locked out** from repeat swarms unless a genuinely new root cause or scope expansion occurs (e.g., customer environment change or secondary component failure verified by the triage lead) *and* the case remains pre-`Diagnosis Complete`.
4. **Escalation & non-swarm fallback:** Cases that pass the evidence gate but fail swarm ROI (low $V_i$ or $T_{\text{SLA}} < 2.0\text{ h}$) escalate to **Tier-B lightweight mitigation** (15-minute single-specialist review or automated log check, zero two-senior swarm spillover).
5. **Data-quarantine rule:** Alerts accompanied by missing/imputed critical telemetry (`I-06`) enter a **30-minute hold**, triggering an automated ops check rather than immediate slot consumption or permanent suppression.

### B. Explicit Comparison: I-05 vs. I-07
- **I-05 (`Triage`, 5.0 h, third alert, no new event, $\Delta p = +0.02$):**
  - *Diagnosis:* Classic **30-minute refresh echo**. No new diagnostic information has entered the system since alert 1.
  - *Policy action:* **Suppress** re-alerts under the intra-stage cool-down until `Diagnosis Started` transition, a new event, or a calibrated jump $\ge +0.05$. Even at $p_{\text{cal}} \approx 0.635$, its direct swarm EV ($€1{,}760$) does not clear base spillover ($\sim€2{,}000$), so re-alerting every 30 minutes only creates alarm fatigue.
- **I-07 (`Triage`, 1.5 h, third alert, new customer log uploaded, $\Delta p = -0.03$):**
  - *Diagnosis:* **Substantive new evidence.** Even though `p_now` ticked down slightly ($0.75 \to 0.72$), the newly uploaded customer log changes the underlying causal state and may reveal a fast-fixable root cause (and `p_cal` is $\sim 0.785$).
  - *Policy action:* **Break cool-down and re-evaluate** (unlike I-05). However, instead of allocating a 45-minute two-senior swarm (which yields only $€840$ direct EV against $€2{,}000$ spillover and cannot realistically finish resolution inside 1.5 h), route I-07 immediately to a **15-minute single-specialist / automated log triage**. This respects the new evidence without destroying portfolio net value.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

1. **Value-based prioritization starving lower-value cases (e.g., SMEs like I-05, I-07, I-10):**
   - *Risk:* Pure linear $V_i$ weighting systematically starves $€5\text{k}\text{–}€11\text{k}$ SME cases whenever $€25\text{k}\text{–}€100\text{k}$ enterprise cases are present, potentially violating baseline SLA equity or causing chronic SME churn.
   - *Safeguards:* (a) Apply concave value weighting (e.g., $\tilde{V}_i = V_i^{0.75}$ or capped strategic multiplier) when comparing Tier-2 cases; (b) pair swarm gating with a **dedicated Tier-B fast-track lane** (single-specialist / automated runbook) funded for low-value cases so rejecting a *swarm* never means *zero help*; (c) enforce a minimum segment intervention-access floor or SLA-breach ceiling per quarter.

2. **Epistemic uncertainty penalizing Non-Native-Language Support (`I-09`):**
   - *Risk:* Small validation samples widen confidence intervals; risk-averse rules that shrink uncertain ARR toward zero would systematically deny swarms to Non-Native-Language cases, permanently keeping their sample size small.
   - *Safeguards:* (a) Use an **optimism-under-uncertainty / UCB exploration bonus** when selecting borderline cases from sparse segments (e.g., evaluate at midpoint-to-upper-quartile ARR up to a bounded exploration budget of 1 slot/day); (b) require language-matched specialist or real-time translation tooling during the swarm so the causal effect is actually realized.

3. **Differential calibration across segments:**
   - *Risk:* Unadjusted `p_now` over-allocates scarce slots to overpredicted Strategic Enterprise cases (`I-04`, `I-08`) while under-serving underpredicted SME cases (`I-05`, `I-07`, `I-10`).
   - *Safeguards:* Maintain segment-specific post-hoc recalibration maps (updated weekly or immediately upon workflow changes) and decouple threshold gating ($p_{\text{cal}, i}$) from raw model scores.

4. **Selective intervention corrupting future training labels (confounding by indication):**
   - *Risk:* When swarms prevent breaches on high-value/Strategic cases (`I-04`, `I-02`), the observed outcome label becomes `no breach`. Future retraining treats those cases as safe, causing the model to **underpredict** risk on previously intervened profiles and erode future recall.
   - *Safeguards:* (a) Log intervention assignment, timestamp, propensity score, and specialist assessment at decision time; (b) train risk models strictly on **untreated counterfactuals** using inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) / doubly robust censoring, or define the target as `breach OR swarm_triggered_with_confirmed_root_cause` only when separating risk estimation from causal-effect models.

5. **Spillover harms systematically falling on untreated groups:**
   - *Risk:* Because high-value Strategic Enterprise cases trigger swarms, the 8–20 min delays and $+0.01\text{–}0.03$ breach risks land disproportionately on the untreated diagnosis queue—predominantly SME and Public Sector cases.
   - *Safeguards:* (a) Internalize the spillover shadow price $\Lambda_{\text{spill}}$ directly into the Strategic Enterprise slot gate; (b) **cap concurrent swarms** (e.g., max 1 active swarm per 6 diagnosis-queue cases) and block non-urgent `Triage` swarms during high queue congestion; (c) route displaced untreated cases to queue-protection buffers (e.g., automated pre-diagnosis data gathering while seniors are in a swarm).

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

| Dimension | Key Metrics & Signals | Estimation / Detection Method | Policy Change & Recalibration Triggers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Operational & Data Integrity** | Imputation/missingness rate on live queue & SLA features; alert suppression ratio; swarm duration; reserve-slot utilization. | Real-time telemetry dead-man switches; automated quarantine alerts (catching `I-06`-style failures before scoring). | Freeze automated alerting for any case missing critical live features; alert on-call engineering within 5 min if feature null rate $>2\%$. |
| **Predictive Calibration** | Segment-stratified Brier score, Expected Calibration Error (ECE), Observed/Expected (O/E) breach ratio across SME, Strategic Enterprise, Public Sector, and Non-Native-Language Support. | Rolling 14-day evaluation on **untreated** cases (IPTW-adjusted to remove selection bias); CUSUM drift tests after workflow redesigns. | Recalibrate segment offset ($p_{\text{cal}}$) if segment O/E ratio deviates by $>0.05$ over 50 untreated cases, or immediately upon any workflow redesign. |
| **Causal Intervention Effect** | Estimated CATE ($\widehat{\text{ARR}}$) by segment, stage (`Triage` vs. `Diagnosis Started`), and case type; true-positive root-cause discovery rate during swarms. | **Tie-breaker / Regression Discontinuity** around $\text{NEV} \approx 0$, plus bounded randomized exploration in sparse segments (Non-Native-Language) and doubly robust CATE modeling. | Tighten ARR assumptions or move a segment to lower-bound ARR if 30-day empirical CATE falls below the lower third of current priors. |
| **Capacity & Spillover** | Diagnosis queue wait-time delta per active swarm (benchmark: 8–20 min); breach-rate delta on concurrent untreated `Diagnosis Started` cases (benchmark: $+0.01\text{–}0.03$). | Interrupted time-series comparing diagnosis-stage throughput and breach rates during swarm vs. non-swarm hours, stratified by queue depth $Q$. | Increase $\Lambda_{\text{spill}}(Q)$ and activate a **max-1-swarm congestion cap** if empirical delay exceeds 20 min/slot or spillover exceeds $0.03$ per untreated case. |
| **Fairness & Feedback Loops** | Intervention-access rate, Tier-B mitigation rate, untreated breach-rate gap, and spillover-exposure share by segment and contract value tier. | Monthly fairness audit comparing parity-adjusted breach rates and queue-delay burden across SME, Public Sector, Strategic, and Non-Native-Language cohorts. | Raise the Tier-B support budget, adjust the concave value exponent $\tilde{V}_i$, or reserve protected capacity if any segment's spillover-adjusted breach rate rises $>3\%$ above baseline parity. |

**Governance cadence:** Run automated daily telemetry/calibration checks, weekly spillover and CATE updates, and an immediate out-of-band policy review whenever a major process change (like the Strategic Enterprise workflow redesign) or sustained negative net-benefit regime (Adverse scenario indicators) is observed.
