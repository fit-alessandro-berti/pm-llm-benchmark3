## 1. Feasibility model

Here, `X ≺ Y` means that X completes before or at the start of Y. A request and its external response are represented separately even when the log records them as one interval. Index (j) identifies a verification and assessment round.

```text
Register ≺ Triage
Triage ≺ Identity Check
Triage ≺ Document Check[1]
{Identity Check, Document Check[1]} ≺ Assessment[1]
Originals Receipt ≺ Document Check[j]                     if that check needs originals
Assessment[j] ≺ Request More Info[j]                      if Assessment issues it
Assessment[j] ≺ Compliance Review[j] ≺ Request More Info[j]
                                                          if Compliance Review issues it
Request More Info[j] ≺ Response[j] ≺ Document Check[j+1] ≺ Assessment[j+1]
Latest Assessment ≺ Decision ≺ Notify
Latest Assessment ≺ {Final Compliance Review or explicit Reaffirmation} ≺ Decision
                                                          if high risk or high value
```

```text
Register and Triage are the first two case activities, in that order.
Identity and document checks may overlap on distinct resources when no originals are required.
A case awaiting originals may proceed with Identity Check.
An issued information request requires a fresh document check and assessment.
A preliminary compliance screen cannot discharge final compliance.
The final assessor and final compliance reviewer must be different people.
C1 is the only certified final reviewer in this window. S1 can advise.
Each resource serves at most one case at a time. A reservation changes access to
a future slot. Service starts when the resource and all required inputs are ready.
Queue estimates are forecasts. Completion depends on actual service and dispatch.
```

K-411 changes the timing and release of verification tasks while retaining I2 and D2. K-219 changes document routing from D1 to D2 and may change rework. K-104 adds early advisory routing to S1 and changes when C1 prepares and gets capacity. K-337 changes the allowed decision policy by suppressing the second evidence request before issuance. It retains final compliance.

Cycle time runs from Register start to Notify completion. The scenarios illustrate sensitivity to slot availability and rework. Outcomes can fall outside the illustrated ranges. The logged D1 intervals for K-219 and K-337 overlap on Tue Apr 7 from 09:30 to 13:30. Each comparison uses the focal case's logged history and treats background workload as approximately fixed. One feasible shared-calendar reconstruction moves K-219's factual D1 check to Tue 14:30–18:30, preserving its logged assessment and notification times. The reported examples assume the stated resource windows are available or, for an explicit reservation, become available through dispatch.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism: The sanctions ambiguity was visible at triage. The case still passed through the D1 and A1 queues before the escalation reached S1. After S1 finished Tue Apr 7 at 21:20, the case waited until Wed 13:10 for its 4 h 30 min final review. Factual notification completed Wed Apr 8 at 18:40.

Minimal intervention: At Mon 08:50, send the known ambiguity and available evidence to S1, and book a provisional C1 window for after the expected assessment. Retain the identity, document and assessment intervals, including A1's Tue 15:10–19:10 assessment. In the prepared scenarios, S1 advises Mon 09:00–11:00 and C1 screens Mon 22:00–23:00, overlapping D1's document work. These intervals require S1 and C1 capacity in the existing workload.

Temporal propagation: The case still becomes eligible for final review Tue 19:10. The earlier advice removes the separate post-assessment wait for S1 if the issue has stayed the same. An earlier reservation can move the case forward in C1's queue, and reusable screening work can shorten C1's final service. The benefit reaches Notify only if M1 can use the earlier clearance.

Process-feasibility check: A1 assesses after both checks. C1 reviews A1's completed assessment and verifies the current sanctions evidence, including anything absent from the packet. C1 and A1 are different people. S1's advice and the preliminary screen have advisory status, and M1 acts after final clearance.

Best/base/worst case: With a usable packet and an early opening, C1 reviews Tue 20:00–23:00 and M1 decides and notifies Wed 09:00–09:40, saving 9 h. In the base case, C1 reviews Wed 09:30–13:00 and M1 decides and notifies Wed 13:10–13:50, saving 4 h 50 min. If urgent work overrides the reservation, the packet saves no review time and the next opening is Thu 08:00–12:30, M1 can decide and notify Thu 13:00–13:40, 19 h later than observed. Those times assume a decision on the available evidence. A material evidence gap could trigger another round.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Actual C1 calendars, urgent arrivals and reservation-release history could show that any earlier slot comes at a high displacement cost. Comparing packet contents with the final evidence, and recording which preliminary findings C1 reused, could show that early preparation saves little or routinely adds work.

### K-219

Factual mechanism: The case was routed to D1 at triage and became eligible for document service when its originals arrived Mon Apr 6 at 17:30. D1's check took 4 h once started, but the case waited 16 h for the start. The accurate check avoided rework. A2 took the case at Tue 20:00, and Notify completed Wed Apr 8 at 09:00.

Minimal intervention: Assign D2 at the 17:30 receipt, with the completed I2 identity check and the same originals. Retain the handwriting standard and the A2 assessment and M1 decision gates. Assignment puts the case into D2's queue. D2 starts at the first usable dispatched window in the scenario.

Temporal propagation: In the base case D2 starts Mon 22:30 and works until Tue 04:30, finishing 9 h before factual document completion despite two extra hours of service. A2 can use a Tue 13:00–15:30 window instead of its logged evening slot, and M1 can act that afternoon. An unchanged A2 or M1 slot can absorb all the readiness gain. If D2 flags illegible entries and A2 requests clarification, the external wait and the second document and assessment queues can more than erase the gain.

Process-feasibility check: D2 works after originals receipt. A2 starts after both checks. D2 can report uncertainty and A2 issues any formal Request More Info. After a response, a fresh D2 check and A2 assessment precede the decision. Risk and value stay below the mandatory compliance gates in this example.

Best/base/worst case: With D2 working Mon 20:30–Tue 01:00, A2 Tue 08:00–10:30, and M1 Tue 11:00–11:30, notification is 21 h 30 min earlier. The base schedule with M1 Tue 16:00–16:30 saves 16 h 30 min. In a rework scenario, D2 finishes Tue 06:30 and A2 requests clarification Tue 22:30. A response Wed 18:30 reaches D2 Thu 08:00–13:00 and A2 Thu 17:00–19:30. M1 decides and notifies Fri 08:30–09:00, 48 h later than observed. Reliable clarification can still support approval.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: D2's actual availability after receipt, the pairing of document completions with A2 openings, and reviewer-specific handwriting error and clarification rates would change the trade-off. A frequent long clarification tail or worse independent audit results would make even a consistent first-check gain a weak basis for rerouting.

### K-337

Factual mechanism: The first correction still left an inconsistency at Assessment 2. The second external response took 70 h 30 min from Fri Apr 10 at 10:30 to Mon Apr 13 at 09:00. The response enabled a third document check and assessment. C1 reviewed on Tue Apr 14, and Notify completed Wed Apr 15 at 09:10.

Minimal intervention: Preserve the path through A1's Fri 10:30 completion of Assessment 2. At that point, block issuance of the second request and have A1 document the unresolved totals, the verified amounts and the implications for approval. Send that assessment and the existing record to C1.

Temporal propagation: The case becomes eligible for final review on Fri Apr 10, instead of Tue Apr 14 after the third assessment. The intervention avoids the weekend response wait and saves the factual 3 h 30 min of D1 service and 2 h 30 min of A1 service in round three. C1 now reviews a less conclusive record and may need a longer slot. Weekend staffing, C1 dispatch and M1 availability govern how much of the earlier readiness reaches Notify.

Process-feasibility check: The issued first request has already been followed by Document Check 2 and Assessment 2. C1 performs final compliance after A1's Assessment 2, and M1 acts after C1. Conditional approval is feasible only when the verified evidence meets mandatory controls and an enforceable condition, such as a permitted exposure cap at the verified amount, addresses the residual risk. Reject if the discrepancy makes approval indefensible. The decision records how the discrepancy was handled.

Best/base/worst case: An open Fri 12:00–17:00 C1 window and M1 Fri 17:20–18:00 yield notice 111 h 10 min earlier. With C1 Mon Apr 13 at 12:00–17:00 and M1 at 17:20–18:00, notice is 39 h 10 min earlier. If C1 needs six consecutive hours for the unresolved finding and the first feasible window is Wed Apr 15 at 14:00–20:00, M1 may use Thu 08:30–09:10, 24 h later than observed. The Tue factual four-hour review window would be too short in that scenario. Either early schedule can end in rejection.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: The actual control threshold, materiality of the mismatch and enforceability of any condition could rule out approval on this record. Response timestamps for comparable second requests, actual C1 and M1 calendars, and later appeals, losses or successful corrections would change the judgment about the value of closing earlier.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: The case lock kept the D2 task from being created until I2 finished Tue Apr 7 at 13:20. D2 started at 15:00 and finished at 19:30. The case reached A2 Wed at 09:30 and completed Notify Wed Apr 8 at 14:30.

Minimal intervention: Create both tasks at Tue 09:35 and give I2 and D2 separate task ownership with controlled access to the case evidence. Record service starts and completions as they happen. Retain I2's 11:30–13:20 interval, D2's service duration, and the A2 and M1 gates in the illustrated schedules.

Temporal propagation: If D2 works Tue 12:00–16:30, actual overlap is 1 h 20 min and both checks are ready 3 h earlier. Readiness is the later of the two valid completions. Earlier assessment queue entry can secure an earlier A2 slot or still encounter the same overnight wait. The manager's dispatch determines the remaining gain.

Process-feasibility check: No originals are required. I2 and D2 work on separate tasks and preserve the case lock for writes to the authoritative case record. The join validates both results against the relevant evidence versions before enabling A2. Task and resource identifiers and contemporaneous timestamps establish that the observed overlap reflects work. M1 decides before notifying.

Best/base/worst case: If D2 works Tue 11:30–16:00, A2 Tue 16:45–19:15 and M1 Tue 19:35–20:05, notification is 18 h 25 min earlier. With D2 finishing Tue 16:30, A2 Wed 08:00–10:30 and M1 Wed 11:00–11:30, it is 3 h earlier. If D2 has no earlier opening or A2 and M1 keep their factual slots, the case can finish at the factual time. Reconciliation of a material change during overlap could make it later.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Task ownership and service traces could show that D2 seldom has earlier capacity or that the apparent overlap comes from timestamp entry. Evidence-version conflicts or a scheduling history showing that earlier readiness routinely yields the same A2 slot would reduce the expected benefit.

Treat the 100 cases as an arrival cohort for each policy separately. Recompute queue entry and dispatch for target and non-target cases. The single-case gains depend on which slots are available, and those slots cannot be reused for every case. A named resource has at most 168 service hours in a week even with continuous staffing. For example, 100 K-104 cases at the base scenario's one hour of screening plus 3.5 hours of final review would offer 450 C1 service hours over their eventual paths, before other cases. A weekly rollout under this capacity implies carryover.

For K-104, part of the gain comes from earlier access to C1. Reserving many windows can push urgent reviews back even when screening saves final service. Slots held for cases whose assessments slip consume dispatch flexibility. Actual urgent overrides, unused reserved time and reusable screening hours determine the net effect.

For K-219, applying the illustrated four-hour D1 and six-hour D2 service times to the cohort shifts 400 hours of demand off D1 and offers 600 hours to D2 before repeats. D1's complex cases can benefit while D2's standard cases lose time. Clarifications add supplier or customer waits and send cases through A2 again. K-337 removes the second supplier wait and frees repeated D1 and A1 service for cases that would otherwise loop. The saved elapsed time is especially large for requests that would cross a weekend. Earlier arrivals and more difficult reviews can move the bottleneck to C1. Appeals can add workload after notification.

K-411 advances the release of D2 work and can bunch completed checks at A2. The effect depends on available service by hour. An assessment backlog can absorb the verification gain, and an earlier assessment outflow can shift waiting to M1. Measure active service overlap, ready-to-start wait, unfinished work and notification tails for both cohorts and their neighbors. Batch-entered timestamps can create apparent overlap while physical service remains serial. Scale estimates need contemporaneous task events and occupancy checks.

## 7. Final synthesis

K-411 appears most robust. The case already qualifies for independent digital checks, and parallel task release preserves the evidence requirements and the authority of the final decision. Its gain depends on when D2 actually starts and whether the earlier join changes A2 or M1 dispatch. Because the workaround can be disabled for future cases, a pilot can measure real overlap, evidence integrity and queue effects with limited exposure.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite its potential speed. The second request supplied evidence used to approve the factual case. Closing on the earlier record can remove days of calendar time while making a rejection appropriate or requiring enforceable conditions based on the verified evidence. A faster notice is a poor outcome if the decision creates avoidable losses, appeals or an audit finding. C1 must review the discrepancy after Assessment 2 regardless of the proposed disposition.

K-104's local counterfactual is least transportable to a system-wide policy. One early C1 slot can improve one case by changing its position relative to others. A portfolio of reserved slots consumes the same certified review capacity that urgent cases need, and preparation has value only to the extent that C1 reuses it. The urgency mix, packet quality and fraction of cases ready at their reserved times determine the effect.

The most valuable data are timestamped queue entry and eligibility, priority changes, actual service starts and finishes, staffing calendars, and reservation bookings, releases and overrides. Record request dispatch, supplier receipt, evidence versions and the reasons for rework, alongside final decisions, audit corrections and appeals. Linked task and case identifiers make it possible to attribute waiting to specific resource queues and distinguish actual concurrency from delayed recording.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **structural** — Assessment requires a completed identity check and the applicable document check. A follow-up document check and assessment satisfy the repeat gate when the existing identity result remains valid.
2. **temporal** — All times are wall-clock times. Proposed openings, including evening and early-morning intervals, are conditional examples that require staffing and dispatch at those times.
3. **resource** — Each named person has one service slot at a time. The overlapping factual D1 intervals require a background scheduling adjustment in any shared calendar.
4. **behavioral** — K-104's preliminary screening carries forward some work in the prepared scenarios. Urgent work can override the reservation and incomplete evidence can erase that reuse.
5. **quality** — D2 can correctly verify some handwritten cases without clarification. Its error and clarification rates depend on the originals and are unknown here.
6. **quality** — In K-337, conditional approval depends on the verified record satisfying mandatory controls and the condition addressing residual risk. C1 must review either disposition.
7. **structural** — In K-411, independent task ownership and a version-checked join protect case integrity. Real-time service events reflect work performed by the identified resources.
8. **interference** — A single target route can change other cases' dispatch locally. At 100 arrivals, queue positions, utilization, repeat demand and carryover are jointly determined.
