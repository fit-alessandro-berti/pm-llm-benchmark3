## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

**Hard invariants.** Every E2O relation must reference an existing event and object; every O2O relation must reference two existing objects; qualifiers are directional; duplicate event-object or object-object rows are not allowed; object/event IDs are unique. An event must not be used as an object or vice versa. For interpretation, I treat observed E2O and O2O rows as currently trusted data, not as necessarily complete lifecycles.

**Strong defaults.** Create Order normally relates to one Order. Add Item normally relates to one Order and one Item. Pack Item normally relates to one Item and one Package, but the domain explicitly allows an item-only scanner-outage row. Dispatch Package normally relates to one Package and one Delivery, but bulk dispatch may cover multiple packages. Confirm Delivery is deliberately flexible: it may reference one delivery, several deliveries, or only an order/customer if the portal loses the carrier reference. Issue Invoice relates to one Invoice and one or more Orders. Record Payment relates to exactly one Payment and normally one Invoice, while unapplied cash is possible. A package may contain several items; a delivery may carry several packages; an invoice may bill several orders. I do **not** treat arithmetic equality as proof of billing or settlement.

**Weak heuristics.** Exact identifier matches are strong; prefix matches are weaker; malformed wildcard references are weaker still. Temporal order matters: dispatch should precede confirmation, invoice should not precede packing in a normal flow, and payments follow invoice issuance. Customer, route, and scanner-reference co-reference are supporting evidence. Existing O2O structure is useful, but it must not be circularly counted as independent confirmation of an E2O edge that was inferred first.

**Evidence against a repair.** Missing observed relations, malformed identifiers, cross-order consolidation without corroboration, arithmetic-only matches, and over-completion of every object lifecycle all argue against aggressive repair. I combine evidence by accepting exact or near-exact identifier plus lifecycle-consistent edges; marking plausible but malformed/wildcard or arithmetic-supported edges provisional or unresolved; and rejecting edges that mainly make the log tidy, unless an explicit bundle adopts a consolidation interpretation. Inferred O2O edges are not independent evidence for the E2O edge from which they were derived.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. Adding ev08–PK-8 can create a false cross-order package containing IT-A2 and IT-B1, producing a Pack Item  Pack Item self-loop for PK-8 and merging order-item contexts that may have been separate.
2. Treating ev10’s `N-421?` as a bulk dispatch to both DEL-X and DEL-Y can duplicate Dispatch Package on deliveries and overstate the Delivery–Package cardinality in the OTG.
3. Adding ev11 to both DEL-X and DEL-Y, or to ORD-B, can create duplicate Confirm Delivery events and inflate confirmation frequencies in Delivery and Order flattenings.
4. Adding INV-44 bills ORD-B solely because 130+100=230 can falsely merge ORD-A and ORD-B finance, even if INV-44 contains fees, discounts, taxes, or only a partial order relation.
5. Adding PAY-2 settles INV-44 solely because 100+130=230 can turn a legitimate unapplied cash record into an apparent second settlement and create a Record Payment  Record Payment self-loop on the invoice.
6. Deriving an O2O edge from an assumed E2O edge and then citing that O2O edge as independent confirmation double-counts evidence and artificially raises confidence.
7. Forcing every orphan object into a complete lifecycle, such as giving DEL-Y a confirmation or IT-B1 a package, removes exactly the missingness that should remain visible in a conservative OCEL repair.
