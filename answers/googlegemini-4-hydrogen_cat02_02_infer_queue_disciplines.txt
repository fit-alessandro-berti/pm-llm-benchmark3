# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

### Test 1: QC at `10:32` and `11:40` — *Linear aging-adjusted SPT vs. Batch-locked SPT + starvation threshold*
- **Ambiguous episode**: `C04` (`22m`, arr `10:07`) beat `C06` (`6m`, arr `10:25`) at `10:32`, then `C06` (`6m`) beat `C01` (`36m`, arr `10:00`) at `11:34`, and finally `C01` beat `C07` (`18m`, arr `10:40`) at `11:40`.
- **Competing explanations**:
  - **$H_1$ (Continuous wait-adjusted SPT, e.g., $\text{est} - \alpha \cdot \text{wait}$ with $\alpha \approx 1$)**: `C04` beats `C06` at `10:32` because `C04`'s 18-minute head start offsets its 16-minute duration disadvantage ($29 < 31$); `C06` beats `C01` at `11:34` ($31 < 36$); `C01` beats `C07` at `11:40` ($36 < 58$).
  - **$H_2$ (Initial-batch SPT `{C01..C04}` + hard starvation age cap, e.g., $\text{wait} \ge 100$ min)**: At `10:32` `C04` wins because `C06` was not in the `10:10` batch; at `11:34` `C01` has waited $94 < 100$ min so SPT picks `C06`; at `11:40` `C01` hits $100$ min wait and overrides `C07`.
- **Counterfactual next-job prediction**: Suppose `C06` had `est_min = 2` (same `10:25` arrival) and `C01` were not starving at `10:54` (suppose `C05` did not exist).
  - $H_1$ predicts `C06` ($25+2=27 < 29$) beats `C04` already at `10:32`.
  - $H_2$ predicts `C04` still runs at `10:32` (batch-locked) and `C01` runs at `10:54` ahead of `C06`.
- **Discriminating observable**: **Dispatch score / batch-lock indicator logged at decision time** (specifically whether `C06` was in the candidate ranking set at `10:32` and the computed priority metric for `C01` vs `C07` at `11:40`).

### Test 2: QD at `11:48` (`D-X`) — *Skill-filtered FIFO vs. Bottleneck-skill (least-flexible-job) routing*
- **Ambiguous episode**: `D-X` chose `D07` (`11:14`, P3, `mech+elec`, `28m`) over `D10` (`11:25`, P1, `elec`, `12m`).
- **Competing explanations**:
  - **$H_1$ (Global Skill-filtered FIFO)**: `D07` arrived earlier (`11:14 < 11:25`), so any eligible resource takes `D07` first.
  - **$H_2$ (Least-flexible-job / bottleneck-skill priority)**: `D07` requires `mech+elec` (only `D-X` is qualified), whereas `D10` only requires `elec` (also executable by `D-F`), so `D-X` prioritizes `D07` regardless of arrival order.
- **Counterfactual next-job prediction**: Suppose `D07` arrived at `11:28` (after `D10` at `11:25`) and `D-F` was in its `12:00–12:20` meeting.
  - $H_1$ predicts `D-X` starts `D10` at `11:48`.
  - $H_2$ predicts `D-X` still starts `D07` at `11:48`.
- **Discriminating observable**: **Candidate-job ranking log / skill-scarcity weight at dispatch** (or an `assigned_resource` timestamp recorded at job arrival showing whether `D10` was queued specifically for `D-F` vs pooled across `D-F`/`D-X`).

### Test 3: QB at `09:48` — *Manual dispatcher override vs. Unlogged `B05` readiness/staging delay*
- **Ambiguous episode**: `B05` (P1) arrives `09:46`; `B01` stops `09:48` (`"dispatcher reassignment"`), yet `B03` (P2, arr `09:20`) executes `09:48–10:00` before `B05` (`10:00–10:14`).
- **Competing explanations**:
  - **$H_1$ (Manual dispatcher override of P1 preemption)**: `B05` was ready at `09:48`, but the dispatcher intentionally pulled `B01` to run quick `B03` (`12m`, waiting 28 min) before `B05`.
  - **$H_2$ (Strict P1 preemption + unlogged `B05` staging delay)**: `B01` was stopped for another operational reassignment/handoff, while `B05` was not physically ready on the bench until `10:00` (despite `09:46` arrival scan).
- **Counterfactual next-job prediction**: If `B05` had been physically ready at `09:48` with `est_min = 14`:
  - $H_1$ still predicts `B03` at `09:48` if the dispatcher valued clearing `B03` first.
  - $H_2$ predicts `B05` runs `09:48–10:02` (exactly matching `B02` at `09:16`).
- **Discriminating observable**: **`job_ready_time` (physical bench-ready timestamp) and structured `preemption_trigger_id`** linking `09:48` to either `B03`, `B05`, or a resource-level event.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked from **most plausible** to **least plausible** given the supplied evidence:

1. **Unrecorded operational buffers, setup/wrap-up lags, and horizon guards (Plausibility: Very High)**
   - *Evidence*: `A1` has 2-minute idle gaps at `08:00–08:02` and `08:30–08:32` (after `A03`, before `A02`); `B1` has 2-minute lags `09:00–09:02`, `09:14` $\rightarrow$ `09:16`, and `09:46` $\rightarrow$ `09:48`; `D-X` is idle `11:20–11:30` before `D06`'s `11:30` appointment; `D-F` is idle `11:36–12:00` before its `12:00` meeting despite `D10` (`12m`) fitting mathematically.
   - *Impact on diagnosis*: Explains idle gaps cleanly without invoking priority inversions or malfunctioning dispatch software; strengthens **Appointment reservation** in `QD` (`D-X`) and cautions against assuming `D-F` used HoL blocking if a pre-meeting buffer existed.

2. **Ad-hoc manual dispatcher overrides and reactive SLA escalation (Plausibility: High)**
   - *Evidence*: Explicitly logged in `QB` (`09:48`: `"dispatcher reassignment"`) and strongly indicated in `QC` (`C05` running at `10:54` right after crossing its `10:50` deadline).
   - *Impact on diagnosis*: Could collapse complex "hybrid" rules into simpler baselines plus human exceptions. Most radically, if `A03` at `08:20` in `QA` was a one-off manual favor, `QA` reduces to **pure FIFO**; similarly `QB` reduces to **FIFO + manual P1 interventions**.

3. **Delayed arrival scans up to 2 minutes (Plausibility: Moderate — explicitly possible per scenario, but low explanatory power here)**
   - *Evidence & quantitative bound*: Because recorded `arrival` $\in [\text{physical}, \text{physical}+2]$, true arrival can be up to 2 minutes *earlier*. However, every contested FIFO decision has an arrival difference $\ge 3$ minutes (`A02`/`A03`: 4m; `A02`/`A04`: 9m; `B04`/`B06`: 30m; `D04`/`D08`: 11m; `D07`/`D10`: 11m). The only boundary crossing is `C05` (`10:11`) potentially arriving physically at `10:09` (before `C1` frees at `10:10`), which still leaves `C05` skipped at `10:18` and `10:32`.
   - *Impact on diagnosis*: Does **not** reverse any primary queue diagnosis; mainly warns that apparent 2-minute dispatch lags (`08:00–08:02`, `09:00–09:02`, `09:14–09:16`) are *not* explained by delayed arrival scans (which shrink rather than widen `start - recorded_arrival`).

4. **Post-hoc overwriting or flat-rate rounding of `est_min` (Data-quality explanation; Plausibility: Moderate)**
   - *Evidence*: Across all 32 jobs, `est_min` equals realized duration to the exact minute (`0` variance, even across `B01`'s 3 interrupted segments). In operational logs, zero estimation error across 32 heterogeneous repairs is suspicious and suggests either standard-time billing blocks, worker pacing, or ETL overwriting of `est_min` with realized minutes.
   - *Impact on diagnosis*: Critically affects **QC**.
