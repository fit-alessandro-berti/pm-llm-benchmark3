## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

- **Hard invariant.** Keep the given events, objects, and observed relations. Every E2O must reference an existing event and object, and both O2O endpoints must be existing objects. Each relation is unique for its endpoints and qualifier. Preserve the direction and meaning of an O2O qualifier, such as Delivery `carries` Package.
- **Strong default.** Expect one Order at creation, one Order and one Item at item addition, one Item and one Package at packing, and one Package and one Delivery at dispatch. Allow documented exceptions such as an item-only scan or bulk dispatch. Expect one Invoice and at least one Order at issue, and one Payment and usually one Invoice at payment recording. A confirmation can have zero, one, or several Delivery links.
- **Strong default.** Prefer links that support a plausible progression for the relevant objects. In this log, item addition precedes packing, packing precedes dispatch, confirmation follows dispatch, and an applied payment follows invoice issue. A close identifier match in the correct role has substantial weight. The observed mapping of `PK8-1` to PK-8 gives the `PK8-2` scan a local precedent. The nearly complete `N-421?` carrier reference favors DEL-Y.
- **Weak heuristic.** Shared route, timestamps, customer, reference prefixes, and exact amount matches narrow or rank candidates. Both deliveries have route NORTH and both dispatches occur at 10:00. Both orders have customer C17. The package weights provide little discrimination without item weights and packaging tare. The invoice matches the sum of declared values, and the payments match the invoice total. Fees, discounts, taxes, unapplied cash, and other adjustments leave room for different allocations.
- **Evidence against a repair.** A better competing identifier, a chronology conflict, or an additional participant without a plausible role lowers support. Using DEL-X for ev10 requires explaining why that scan resembles DEL-Y's carrier reference. Adding ORD-B to ev11 requires evidence about the portal acknowledgment's order scope beyond its shared shipment. The bank reference on ev14 identifies PAY-2 and provides no invoice identifier.

I check endpoint roles and existing O2O structure first, then compare candidates using the most discriminating source fields. Time, customer, route, and amounts reinforce compatible hypotheses. Correlated clues get joint weight. For example, PAY-2 matching ORD-A's declared value and matching the recorded invoice remainder are two views of related amounts. The confidence values are comparative judgments, and candidates that can coexist have no requirement to sum to one. The extract omits E2O qualifier labels, so the additions use descriptive role labels.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A — conservative/minimum-change repair.** Add R01–R04. IT-B1 is packed into PK-8 at ev08 and PK-8 is dispatched with DEL-Y at ev10. DEL-X retains its observed PK-7 dispatch at ev09. Keep ev11 at ORD-A scope with both carrier links open. Leave INV-44 linked to ORD-A at issue and in `bills`, with ORD-B's billing unknown. PAY-1 remains the documented 100 application and PAY-2 remains without an identified invoice. This yields plausible shipment histories for both packages and allows a partial view of billing, settlement, and delivery confirmation.

**Bundle B — joint confirmation and consolidated billing.** Keep R01–R04 and add R08–R09 and R11–R14. Interpret ev11 as one acknowledgment covering the two deliveries that each carry an ORD-A item. Continue to leave the direct ORD-B acknowledgment open. ORD-B's item can share PK-8 while the portal event is scoped to ORD-A. At 15:00 INV-44 bills both C17 orders, and the two distinct payments are applied at 15:30 and 16:00. The documented applications under this hypothesis total 230. The shipment and billing sequences fit together without requiring a separate confirmation for ORD-B before invoice issue.

A single-delivery reading of ev11 also fits. Either carrier reference matches the wildcard and each delivery carries an ORD-A item. A lost carrier identifier explains the recorded order-only row. Joint confirmation, consolidated billing, and application of PAY-2 each require their own evidence even though they coexist coherently in Bundle B.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

Under Bundle A, every object has at least one E2O link and every event relates to at least one object. DEL-Y is the only object with zero E2O links in the input. PAY-2 already has its own payment event and remains the only O2O-isolated object in A. Coverage of events and objects still allows gaps in the business lifecycles.

- **Order.** ORD-A has creation, two item additions, an order-level acknowledgment, and invoice issue. ORD-B has creation and one item addition, with billing and acknowledgment open. One-to-many containment is expected, and each Item has a single observed Order. The observed Add Item links and `contains` edges corroborate the three memberships.
- **Item.** Each Item has an addition followed by a pack scan and one assigned Package in A. The item identifiers are given by observed E2O. For IT-A1 and IT-A2 the package memberships are also directly recorded in O2O. IT-B1's package membership is inferred from ev08.
- **Package.** PK-7 has one item and PK-8 has two. Both have pack activity followed by one dispatch event. PK-8 combines ORD-A and ORD-B items, giving an indirect Order–Package many-to-many pattern across the two packages. This is plausible with consolidated packing. The original Pack Item rows and `packs` edges corroborate the original two memberships. The new PK-8 membership needs independent packing evidence to reach the same footing.
- **Delivery.** Each Delivery has one Package and a dispatch. Direct delivery confirmation remains unresolved. DEL-X's carriage has both an observed dispatch link and an observed `carries` edge. DEL-Y's dispatch and carriage are jointly inferred.
- **Invoice.** INV-44 has issue followed by the recorded PAY-1 event. Its relationship to ORD-A appears in distinct observed E2O and O2O records, as does its relationship to PAY-1. The 100 recorded application against 230 leaves 130 unexplained by recorded applications. B widens billing and settlement cardinalities in ways the domain allows.
- **Payment.** Each Payment has one Record Payment event, and PAY-1 has one documented Invoice. PAY-2 has an observed payment event and an unresolved invoice allocation. The PAY-1 application has support in both relation tables. In B, two Payments to one Invoice are consistent with installment settlement.

The observed pairs provide cross-table corroboration, with provenance independence unknown. Inferring `PK-8 packs IT-B1` from the assumed ev08 package link and then using that edge to confirm ev08 would count the same scan twice. The ev10 pair has the same dependency. Bundle B's billing and settlement pairs likewise encode one respective hypothesis in two tables.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**OC-DFG.** In the input, PK-8 has ev07 → ev10. A and B replace that instance with ev07 → ev08 → ev10, adding a Package `Pack Item → Pack Item` loop and preserving two Package `Pack Item → Dispatch Package` transitions across PK-7 and PK-8. The three Item `Add Item → Pack Item` transitions already exist. A gives DEL-Y a singleton Dispatch Package lifecycle. B adds two Delivery instances of `Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery`, with the same ev11 at the end of each. B also adds ORD-B's `Add Item → Issue Invoice` and INV-44's `Record Payment → Record Payment`. Payment-object lifecycles remain singleton events.

**OTG.** In the projection of directed O2O links by object type, A increases Package → Item support from two pairs to three and Delivery → Package from one to two. B additionally increases Invoice → Order and Payment → Invoice from one pair each to two. The type pairs already exist in the input, so a presence-only graph can look identical while relationship multiplicities change. An E2O co-participation projection gains an Item–Package witness at ev08 and a Package–Delivery witness at ev10 in A, then adds Order–Delivery interaction at ev11 in B. That interaction describes event participation and has a different meaning from a directed O2O edge.

**ET–OT graph.** A strengthens the Pack Item–Package and Dispatch Package–Delivery associations already observed in the input. B introduces a Confirm Delivery–Delivery incidence with one event and two Delivery objects, and strengthens Issue Invoice–Order and Record Payment–Invoice. Role-sensitive views may display the newly supplied qualifiers separately from unspecified input qualifiers.

**Flattenings.** With direct E2O flattening, the Order view has ORD-A's confirmation and invoice in all three logs. ORD-B gains ev12 in B, so ev12 appears once per billed order there. The Package view gains ev08 in PK-8 from A onward. The Delivery view has ev09 for DEL-X in the input, gains ev10 for DEL-Y in A, and contains two case rows for the same ev11 in B. The Invoice view has ev12 → ev13 in the input and A, and gains ev14 in B. The Payment view has one event per Payment throughout. Shared events create rows per case in the relevant flattening. Any flattening that follows O2O paths as well can assign additional events to cases and requires an explicit traversal rule.

**Activity frequencies.** Count events by distinct event ID, unique objects by distinct object ID within the activity, and total objects by event–object incidences within the activity. Across input/A/B, Pack Item has 3 events, 5/5/5 unique objects, and 5/6/6 incidences. Dispatch Package has 2 events, 3/4/4 unique objects, and 3/4/4 incidences. Confirm Delivery has 1 event, with unique objects and incidences both 1/1/3. Issue Invoice has 1 event, with both object measures 2/2/3. Record Payment has 2 events, 3/3/3 unique objects, and 3/3/4 incidences. INV-44 occurs in two payment events in B and remains one unique Invoice.

**Equivalent executions around Order.** In the input the orders lie in separate components if extraction uses observed co-participation and O2O. A's PK-8 repair joins their neighborhoods, so component extraction can group both orders, and rooted extraction can reuse the same packing and dispatch fragment for both. B adds the shared invoice and extends the neighborhood to PAY-2. The rooted signatures can still differ because ORD-A has two items and the direct ev11, while ORD-B has one item and gains a direct invoice event in B. Cluster assignments depend on traversal scope and whether equivalence preserves the root and relation qualifiers.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

This assumes the standard `ocel:*` columns and a PM4Py version exposing the three discovery calls. Empty strings represent unspecified observed E2O qualifiers where the input extract supplies none. Confidence and justification stay in the repair artifact. Use the curated O2O additions as the input to discovery. Co-occurrence enrichment could generate hypotheses for review.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.ocel.obj import OCEL

eid, activity, timestamp = "ocel:eid", "ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp"
oid, object_type, oid_2 = "ocel:oid", "ocel:type", "ocel:oid_2"
qualifier = "ocel:qualifier"

original = {
    "events": events.copy(deep=True),
    "objects": objects.copy(deep=True),
    "relations": relations.copy(deep=True),
    "o2o": o2o.copy(deep=True),
}
ev = original["events"].copy()
ob = original["objects"].copy()
e2o = original["relations"].copy()
links = original["o2o"].copy()

if qualifier not in e2o.columns:
    e2o[qualifier] = ""
e2o[qualifier] = e2o[qualifier].fillna("")

e2o_additions = pd.DataFrame(
    [("ev08", "PK-8", "package"), ("ev10", "DEL-Y", "delivery")],
    columns=[eid, oid, qualifier],
)
o2o_additions = pd.DataFrame(
    [("PK-8", "IT-B1", "packs"), ("DEL-Y", "PK-8", "carries")],
    columns=[oid, oid_2, qualifier],
)
e2o = pd.concat([e2o, e2o_additions], ignore_index=True)
links = pd.concat([links, o2o_additions], ignore_index=True)

for name, frame, key in (
    ("events", ev, [eid]),
    ("objects", ob, [oid]),
    ("E2O", e2o, [eid, oid, qualifier]),
    ("O2O", links, [oid, oid_2, qualifier]),
):
    if frame[key].isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError(f"Missing key value in {name}")
    if frame.duplicated(subset=key).any():
        raise ValueError(f"Duplicate key in {name}")

for name, values, allowed in (
    ("E2O event", e2o[eid], ev[eid]),
    ("E2O object", e2o[oid], ob[oid]),
    ("O2O source", links[oid], ob[oid]),
    ("O2O target", links[oid_2], ob[oid]),
):
    if not values.isin(allowed).all():
        raise ValueError(f"Dangling {name} reference")

# Materialize PM4Py's E2O metadata from the master tables.
ev[timestamp] = pd.to_datetime(ev[timestamp], errors="raise")
event_lookup = ev.set_index(eid)
object_lookup = ob.set_index(oid)
for column, expected in (
    (activity, e2o[eid].map(event_lookup[activity])),
    (timestamp, e2o[eid].map(event_lookup[timestamp])),
    (object_type, e2o[oid].map(object_lookup[object_type])),
):
    if expected.isna().any():
        raise ValueError(f"Missing master data for {column}")
    if column in e2o.columns:
        stored = e2o[column]
        if column == timestamp:
            stored = pd.to_datetime(stored, errors="raise")
        present = stored.notna()
        if not stored[present].eq(expected[present]).all():
            raise ValueError(f"Conflicting E2O metadata for {column}")
    e2o[column] = expected

# Version-dependent constructor. Check the installed signature for o2o support.
ocel = OCEL(events=ev, objects=ob, relations=e2o, o2o=links)

ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)
order_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Order")
delivery_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Delivery")
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

- Use of timestamp alone could merge the two 10:00 dispatches and assign PK-8 to the wrong carrier movement.
- Assigning ev11 to both carriers by wildcard alone could create two false Delivery completions from one order-level portal entry. Choosing one carrier arbitrarily could hide a real joint acknowledgment.
- Following PK-8's item membership back to ORD-B could manufacture a direct confirmation and shorten ORD-B's apparent fulfillment time.
- Enforcing one Order per Package could erase the plausible PK-8 consolidation or incorrectly reassign IT-B1 away from its observed order.
- Using the sum of declared values as a billing key could insert a false ORD-B invoice occurrence and distort time-to-billing statistics.
- Applying PAY-2 on the basis of the remainder alone could falsely close INV-44 and obscure unapplied cash.
- Treating every object reachable from an event as a direct participant could add payments to Order traces or packing events to Delivery traces. Those added event links would change directly-follows behavior and flattening counts.
- Using flattened row counts as original event counts could turn the one ev11 acknowledgment into two confirmations and the one ev12 issue into two invoice events.
