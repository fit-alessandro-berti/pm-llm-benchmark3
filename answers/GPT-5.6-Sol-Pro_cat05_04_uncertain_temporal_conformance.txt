## 1. Normalization Assumptions

All event evidence, source-clock metadata, SLA limits, temporal-profile parameters, and case-specific interpretive constraints below come from the supplied task. 

**Interval construction.** Let the displayed timestamp first expand to a recorded-clock interval (R=[r_{\min},r_{\max}]):

* HUB exact-second displays become ([t-0.5\text{s},t+0.5\text{s}]).
* A floor-to-minute display becomes ([t,t+59.999\text{s}]).
* SENSOR nearest-10-second displays become ([t-5\text{s},t+5\text{s}]).
* MOBILE-LON nearest-minute displays become ([t-30\text{s},t+30\text{s}]).

With `clock_offset = recorded_clock − true_clock` and offset (O=[a,b]), the corrected true-clock interval is

[
T_{\text{local}}=[r_{\min}-b,\ r_{\max}-a].
]

Thus the maximum positive offset moves the lower endpoint earlier, while the most negative offset moves the upper endpoint later. Resolution uncertainty is applied before this correction.

**Time-zone conversion.** Corrected local intervals are converted to UTC using the stated zone and seasonal offset: CEST (=) UTC+2, CET (=) UTC+1, EDT (=) UTC−4, and BST (=) UTC+1.

**DST folds.** Each T-405 local 02:xx interval initially has two UTC alternatives. The joint ingestion order

[
\text{Alert_Received}<\text{Investigation_Start}<\text{Containment_Complete}
]

leaves one feasible fold assignment: the alert is in the first 02:xx hour, while investigation and containment are in the second 02:xx hour. The order removes the other fold combinations but does not remove minute-floor or clock-offset uncertainty.

**Duration arithmetic.** For independently feasible event intervals (A=[a_l,a_u]) and (B=[b_l,b_u]),

[
B-A=[b_l-a_u,\ b_u-a_l].
]

For unions, I take the Cartesian product of alternatives, then merge only overlapping duration components. I do not replace a disjoint union with its convex hull. Where a joint fold scenario exists, durations are computed within the same scenario rather than recombining event projections arbitrarily.

**Clock-offset dependence.** The baseline is conservative: source offsets may take any admissible value at each event. I do not assume that two records from the same source share one perfectly common offset unless stated. A common-offset alternative is discussed for T-405 because it materially narrows its durations.

**Causal order.** A negative `Alert_Received → Investigation_Start` duration is not treated as an exceptionally fast pass. For ordinary lifecycle pairs, I condition on the named causal order and retain only nonnegative durations. If no ordered realization existed, the result would be internally inconsistent and therefore `INDETERMINATE`. For T-409, ordered realizations exist, so its raw negative lower bound is truncated to zero while confidence remains low.

**T-404 inferred start.** The signed 18–25 minute investigation duration is applied to the entire `Investigation_Complete` interval:

[
I_{\text{start}}=
[I_{\text{complete},l}-25\text{m},\
I_{\text{complete},u}-18\text{m}].
]

No midpoint duration is substituted.

**T-406 mobile delay.** The baseline treats the displayed mobile timestamp as an entry-time reading: the actual investigation occurrence may precede that corrected reading by 0–5 minutes. Therefore the five-minute delay expands only the lower endpoint earlier. An alternative interpretation—entry was late but the entered timestamp refers to occurrence—leaves occurrence at `12:08:30–12:19:30Z`; its consequences are shown in Section 4.

**T-407 missing receipt.** The network evidence gives a technical-delivery window, not a logged or definitionally equivalent `Alert_Received`. Consequently, S1 and temporal-profile pairs involving `Alert_Received` are `INDETERMINATE` under strict semantics. I nevertheless show a conditional numeric interval obtained by assuming:

1. technical delivery can serve as receipt; and
2. receipt cannot be after investigation start.

**Baseline classifications.** Section 3 uses zero SLA tolerance and (\zeta=2). SLA limits and temporal-profile bands are inclusive. “Certain” means over every feasible true-time realization under the stated baseline assumptions; it is not a probability statement.

### Material changes

**T-401.** Adding two minutes makes S1 a certain pass because the maximum feasible duration is 16m31.499s. The profile Alert→Investigation result needs (\zeta=3) to become a certain pass.

**T-402.** S1 changes from certain violation to possible violation at +2 minutes, but it does not become a certain pass even at +5 minutes because 20m31.499s remains feasible. S2 becomes a certain pass with +2 minutes. At (\zeta=1), both Alert→Investigation and Alert→Containment are certain profile violations; (\zeta=2) removes the latter and makes the former only possible.

**T-403.** S1 needs the full five-minute addition to become a certain pass. Alert→Investigation remains a possible profile violation even at (\zeta=3): its upper endpoint, 19m01.500s, is 1.5 s above the 19-minute upper band.

**T-404.** S2 is a boundary case: +2 minutes makes it a certain pass, while at zero tolerance its upper endpoint exceeds 45 minutes by 3 s. S1 requires +5 minutes. The profile’s wider (\zeta=2) band resolves Investigation→Containment, while Alert→Investigation needs (\zeta=3).

**T-405.** S1 becomes a certain pass only at +5 minutes in the eventwise-independent offset model. Alert→Investigation becomes a certain profile pass at (\zeta=3).

A reasonable **common LEGACY clock-offset** alternative materially narrows T-405 because a single systematic offset cancels in pairwise durations:

* Alert→Investigation becomes `14m00.001s–15m59.999s`.
* Investigation→Containment becomes `36m00.001s–37m59.999s`.
* Alert→Containment becomes `51m00.001s–52m59.999s`.

Under that alternative, T-405/S1 is still `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION` at zero tolerance, but becomes `CERTAIN_PASS` at +2 minutes. Its Alert→Investigation profile result changes to `CERTAIN_VIOLATION` at (\zeta=1), `CERTAIN_PASS` at (\zeta=2), and `CERTAIN_PASS` at (\zeta=3). This demonstrates why clock-offset correlation must be recorded rather than guessed.

**T-406.** Under the baseline occurrence-shifting interpretation, both SLA rules require +5 minutes for certain passage. Alert→Investigation remains a possible profile violation for all three (\zeta) values because the interval is both very broad and extends 31.5 s above the (\zeta=3) upper bound.

Under the alternative that the five-minute delay affects only `recorded_at`, not the occurrence estimate:

* Investigation_Start becomes `12:08:30–12:19:30Z`.
* S1 remains `PV, PV, CP` for 0, 2, and 5 minutes.
* S2 becomes `CERTAIN_PASS` even at zero tolerance, with duration `32m28.500s–43m31.500s`.
* Investigation→Containment becomes `CERTAIN_PASS` even at (\zeta=1).

Thus T-406/S2 is primarily a semantic/provenance uncertainty, not merely a clock-width problem.

**T-407.** Strict conclusions remain indeterminate at every tolerance and (\zeta), because changing a numeric threshold cannot create a missing receipt event. If technical delivery were formally accepted as `Alert_Received`, conditional S1 would be possible at 0 and +2 minutes and a certain pass at +5 minutes. Conditional Alert→Containment would pass at every tested (\zeta).

**T-408.** No tested SLA tolerance changes the two certain breaches. Profile results are much more sensitive: Investigation→Containment changes `CV → PV → CP`; Alert→Containment changes `CV → CP → CP`; and Containment→Close changes `CV → CV → CP`. This is exactly why (\zeta) is a statistical diagnostic setting rather than an SLA waiver.

**T-409.** S1 becomes a certain pass at +5 minutes, but Alert→Investigation remains a possible profile violation even at (\zeta=3). Its feasible duration includes zero, which is below the (\zeta=3) lower band of one minute, while larger values lie inside the band. Investigation→Containment becomes a certain profile pass at (\zeta=2).

def resolution_window(recorded: datetime, semantics: str) -> TimeInterval:
    """Apply display/rounding uncertainty before clock correction."""
    if semantics == "exact_second":
        return TimeInterval(
            recorded - timedelta(seconds=0.5),
            recorded + timedelta(seconds=0.5),
        )
    if semantics == "floor_minute":
        # The scenario explicitly states an upper endpoint of :59.999.
        return TimeInterval(
            recorded,
            recorded + timedelta(seconds=59.999),
        )
    if semantics == "nearest_10_seconds":
        return TimeInterval(
            recorded - timedelta(seconds=5),
            recorded + timedelta(seconds=5),
        )
    if semantics == "nearest_minute":
        return TimeInterval(
            recorded - timedelta(seconds=30),
            recorded + timedelta(seconds=30),
        )
    raise ValueError(f"Unknown resolution semantics: {semantics}")


def merge_time_intervals(
    intervals: Iterable[TimeInterval],
) -> List[TimeInterval]:
    """Merge only overlapping alternatives; retain genuine gaps."""
    result: List[TimeInterval] = []
    for interval in sorted(set(intervals)):
        if not result or interval.lo > result[-1].hi:
            result.append(interval)
        else:
            result[-1] = TimeInterval(
                result[-1].lo,
                max(result[-1].hi, interval.hi),
            )
    return result


def merge_num_intervals(
    intervals: Iterable[NumInterval],
) -> List[NumInterval]:
    result: List[NumInterval] = []
    for interval in sorted(set(intervals)):
        if not result or interval.lo > result[-1].hi:
            result.append(interval)
        else:
            result[-1] = NumInterval(
                result[-1].lo,
                max(result[-1].hi, interval.hi),
            )
    return result


def utc_candidates(
    local_interval: TimeInterval,
    tz_name: str,
) -> List[TimeInterval]:
    """
    Convert a corrected local interval to UTC.

    Both fold values are tried. For ordinary times they produce duplicate
    intervals and are deduplicated; for an ambiguous DST hour they remain
    separate alternatives.
    """
    zone = ZoneInfo(tz_name)
    candidates: List[TimeInterval] = []

    for fold in (0, 1):
        lo_aware = local_interval.lo.replace(tzinfo=zone, fold=fold)
        hi_aware = local_interval.hi.replace(tzinfo=zone, fold=fold)
        candidates.append(
            TimeInterval(
                lo_aware.astimezone(UTC),
                hi_aware.astimezone(UTC),
            )
        )

    return merge_time_intervals(candidates)


def normalize_row(row: pd.Series) -> List[TimeInterval]:
    meta = SOURCES[row["system"]]
    recorded = datetime.fromisoformat(row["recorded_value"])

    # 1. Display resolution.
    clock_window = resolution_window(
        recorded,
        meta["resolution"],
    )

    # 2. Clock correction.
    override = row["offset_override_s"]
    if isinstance(override, tuple):
        offset_min_s, offset_max_s = override
    else:
        offset_min_s, offset_max_s = meta["offset_s"]

    # offset = recorded_clock - true_clock
    true_local = TimeInterval(
        clock_window.lo - timedelta(seconds=offset_max_s),
        clock_window.hi - timedelta(seconds=offset_min_s),
    )

    # 3. Time-zone conversion, preserving fold alternatives.
    candidates = utc_candidates(true_local, meta["tz"])

    # 4. Baseline mobile-entry interpretation.
    lag_max_s = float(row["occurrence_lag_max_s"])
    if lag_max_s:
        candidates = [
            TimeInterval(
                interval.lo - timedelta(seconds=lag_max_s),
                interval.hi,
            )
            for interval in candidates
        ]

    return merge_time_intervals(candidates)

def sequence_is_feasible(
    intervals: Sequence[TimeInterval],
) -> bool:
    """
    Test whether t1 <= t2 <= ... can be selected with each ti inside
    its corresponding interval.
    """
    chosen = intervals[0].lo
    if chosen > intervals[0].hi:
        return False

    for interval in intervals[1:]:
        chosen = max(chosen, interval.lo)
        if chosen > interval.hi:
            return False

    return True

def duration_intervals(
    source_intervals: Sequence[TimeInterval],
    target_intervals: Sequence[TimeInterval],
    require_nonnegative: bool = True,
) -> List[NumInterval]:
    """
    Interval subtraction for every feasible alternative combination.
    """
    durations: List[NumInterval] = []

    for source_interval, target_interval in product(
        source_intervals,
        target_intervals,
    ):
        lower = (
            target_interval.lo - source_interval.hi
        ).total_seconds()
        upper = (
            target_interval.hi - source_interval.lo
        ).total_seconds()

        if require_nonnegative:
            if upper < 0:
                continue
            lower = max(0.0, lower)

        durations.append(
            NumInterval(lower, upper)
        )

    return merge_num_intervals(durations)

def classify_sla(
    feasible: Sequence[NumInterval],
    limit_s: float,
    indeterminate: bool = False,
) -> str:
    if indeterminate or not feasible:
        return "INDETERMINATE"

    if all(
        interval.hi <= limit_s
        for interval in feasible
    ):
        return "CERTAIN_PASS"

    if all(
        interval.lo > limit_s
        for interval in feasible
    ):
        return "CERTAIN_VIOLATION"

    return "POSSIBLE_VIOLATION"


def classify_band(
    feasible: Sequence[NumInterval],
    lower_s: float,
    upper_s: float,
    indeterminate: bool = False,
) -> str:
    if indeterminate or not feasible:
        return "INDETERMINATE"

    if all(
        interval.lo >= lower_s
        and interval.hi <= upper_s
        for interval in feasible
    ):
        return "CERTAIN_PASS"

    # Important for unions: every component must be wholly outside.
    if all(
        interval.hi < lower_s
        or interval.lo > upper_s
        for interval in feasible
    ):
        return "CERTAIN_VIOLATION"

    return "POSSIBLE_VIOLATION"


def format_utc(value: datetime) -> str:
    return (
        value.astimezone(UTC)
        .isoformat(timespec="milliseconds")
        .replace("+00:00", "Z")
    )


def format_time_union(
    values: Sequence[TimeInterval],
) -> str:
    return " U ".join(
        f"[{format_utc(interval.lo)}, "
        f"{format_utc(interval.hi)}]"
        for interval in values
    )


def format_duration_union(
    values: Sequence[NumInterval],
) -> str:
    return " U ".join(
        f"[{interval.lo:.3f}, "
        f"{interval.hi:.3f}] s"
        for interval in values
    )

def normalize_pm4py_diagnostics(
    raw_diagnostics,
    ordered_cases: Sequence[str],
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Isolate PM4Py-version-specific output assumptions here.

    Expected shape:
      one list per trace;
      each deviation resembles
      (source_activity,
       target_activity,
       elapsed_seconds,
       standardized_distance).

    Adapt only this function if a PM4Py version returns a DataFrame,
    mapping, or differently named fields.
    """
    rows = []

    if isinstance(
        raw_diagnostics,
        pd.DataFrame,
    ):
        result = raw_diagnostics.copy()
        result["_raw_shape"] = "dataframe"
        return result

    if isinstance(
        raw_diagnostics,
        Mapping,
    ):
        iterable = raw_diagnostics.items()

    elif isinstance(
        raw_diagnostics,
        list,
    ):
        iterable = zip(
            ordered_cases,
            raw_diagnostics,
        )

    else:
        return pd.DataFrame(
            [
                {
                    "case": None,
                    "raw": repr(raw_diagnostics),
                    "_raw_shape":
                        type(raw_diagnostics).__name__,
                }
            ]
        )

    for case, deviations in iterable:
        if deviations is None:
            continue

        if (
            isinstance(deviations, tuple)
            and len(deviations) >= 4
            and isinstance(deviations[0], str)
        ):
            deviations = [deviations]

        for deviation in deviations:
            if isinstance(
                deviation,
                Mapping,
            ):
                rows.append(
                    {
                        "case": case,
                        **dict(deviation),
                        "_raw_shape": "mapping",
                    }
                )

            elif (
                isinstance(
                    deviation,
                    (tuple, list),
                )
                and len(deviation) >= 4
            ):
                rows.append(
                    {
                        "case": case,
                        "source_activity":
                            deviation[0],
                        "target_activity":
                            deviation[1],
                        "elapsed_seconds":
                            deviation[2],
                        "standardized_distance":
                            deviation[3],
                        "_raw_shape": "tuple",
                    }
                )

            else:
                rows.append(
                    {
                        "case": case,
                        "raw": repr(deviation),
                        "_raw_shape":
                            type(deviation).__name__,
                    }
                )

    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

The diagnostic adapter isolates the documented PM4Py convention in which temporal-profile conformance returns per-trace deviations containing the source activity, target activity, elapsed time, and standardized distance. The interval classifications remain separate because PM4Py’s ordinary point-timestamp interface does not itself resolve these source-clock, fold, and manual-entry uncertainty sets. ([PM4Py][1])

Expected midpoint-versus-interval contrasts include:

* T-408 Investigation→Containment has a 55-minute midpoint. At (\zeta=2), a point checker can accept the boundary value, while the interval result is `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION` because 55m03s is feasible.
* T-402 Alert→Investigation has a midpoint around 18m15s and will be a point violation at (\zeta=2), while the interval conclusion is only `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION` because durations as low as 15m58.500s remain feasible.
* T-405’s midpoint is exactly 15 minutes after fold filtering, but it does not justify replacing the full 12–18 minute baseline interval.

## 7. Governance Recommendation

**Store normalized UTC without discarding the source representation.** Every event should have an immutable UTC occurrence interval or point, while retaining the raw timestamp string, source system, local time-zone identifier, explicit numeric UTC offset, and conversion version. A normalized UTC point must never overwrite the raw local value.

**Retain resolution and rounding semantics.** Record whether a source timestamp is exact, floored, rounded, truncated, user-entered, or inferred, together with its unit and uncertainty rule. A displayed minute from a flooring source must not later be interpreted as an exact `:00` event.

**Capture clock telemetry at event granularity.** Persist the estimated source-clock offset, lower and upper bounds, NTP status, synchronization age, drift estimate, stratum or reference, and whether an offset is shared across a batch of records. This allows analysts to distinguish eventwise-independent bounds from common systematic offsets.

**Represent DST folds explicitly.** Ambiguous local events should carry a fold field or explicit UTC offset. Ingestion should reject or quarantine a local timestamp in an ambiguous hour when neither fold nor offset is supplied. Inferring a fold from process order should be recorded as an inference with provenance, not silently written as observed fact.

**Separate occurrence, recording, and ingestion times.** Manual and mobile events should have at least:

* `occurred_at`
* `recorded_at`
* `ingested_at`
* `entry_delay_reason`
* device-clock offset and synchronization state

A delay in data entry should not automatically become uncertainty about occurrence, and vice versa.

**Use explicit lifecycle semantics.** Activities should carry a lifecycle attribute such as `start`, `complete`, `acknowledged`, `sent`, `delivered`, or `received`. In particular:

* `Investigation_Start` must not be inferred from `Investigation_Complete` without preserving the inference rule.
* SENSOR completion events must not be interpreted as starts.
* `Alert_Sent`, technical delivery, human receipt, and acknowledgment must remain distinct event types.

**Preserve inference provenance.** An inferred T-404 start should carry the source event identifier, signed work-record identifier, duration bounds, inference formula, author or system, and creation time. Downstream users must be able to exclude inferred events or recompute them under different assumptions.

**Add causal identifiers.** Alert transmission, delivery, receipt, investigation, and containment records should share incident IDs and, where applicable, message IDs, acknowledgment IDs, source-event IDs, and causal-parent IDs. Sequence numbers are useful but should not be substituted for event occurrence time without an explicit ordering guarantee.

**Adopt a four-state enforcement policy.**

* `CERTAIN_PASS`: close the timing test as satisfied.
* `CERTAIN_VIOLATION`: permit formal breach treatment, subject to ordinary audit controls.
* `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION`: trigger evidence collection or conservative operational escalation, but do not represent it as a proven legal breach.
* `INDETERMINATE`: treat as an evidential-control failure and obtain the missing activity or semantic evidence.

For hard-SLA enforcement, mere overlap with the violation region should not by itself support sanctions or contractual breach reporting. An organization may still use the upper duration bound for preventive operations, but the operational risk decision must be labelled separately from the legal conformance verdict.

For temporal-profile monitoring, interval overlap should normally produce an anomaly-review flag rather than enforcement. The profile is a statistical expectation, its verdict depends strongly on (\zeta), and—as T-402 and T-408 demonstrate—it is neither a substitute for nor an override of the hard SLA.
