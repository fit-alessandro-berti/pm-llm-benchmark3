## 1. Equivalence design space

**Activity-sequence equivalence.**  
Two executions are equivalent when the ordered sequences of activity labels are identical. Formally, extract the list \(L = [a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n]\) of activity names in timestamp order; \(e_1 \equiv_{\text{act}} e_2\) iff \(L(e_1) = L(e_2)\).  
Invariants preserved: pure control-flow skeleton (which activities occur and in which total order).  
Information discarded: all object identifiers, object types, multiplicities, E2O incidences, O2O relations and qualifiers, external flags.  
The relation is an equivalence (reflexive, symmetric, transitive) because string equality of sequences is.  
Concurrency / same-time events: if two events share a timestamp, sort them lexicographically by activity label before comparison; independent “Add Item” events that differ only in order are therefore treated as interchangeable only if their labels are identical (they are). In the given data all timestamps induce a total order, so no additional cases arise.

**Typed structural equivalence under identifier renaming.**  
Two executions are equivalent when there exists a type-preserving bijection \(\phi\) on the sets of local object identifiers (mapping EXT-marked objects only to other EXT-marked objects of the same type) such that:  
(1) the sequences of events become identical after each event’s related-object multiset is rewritten by \(\phi\), and  
(2) the multiset of O2O relations (including any qualifiers) is identical after \(\phi\).  
Operationally one can compute a canonical labelling by sorting objects of each type by the sorted list of (activity, role) pairs they participate in, then rewrite the event sequence and the O2O table.  
Invariants preserved: object-type multiplicities, E2O incidence patterns (which types participate in which activities), topology of O2O relations, presence of external objects, and the total order of events.  
Information discarded: the concrete local identifier strings.  
The relation is an equivalence (isomorphism of labelled multi-graphs after renaming).  
Concurrency: simultaneous independent events of the same label are represented by multisets of object-type tuples, so order among them is irrelevant; interleaving that changes topology (e.g., Pack–Dispatch–Pack versus Pack–Pack–Dispatch) is preserved.

**Business-semantic equivalence.**  
Two executions are equivalent when they realise the same high-level fulfilment motif after local identifiers have been normalised. Motifs are defined by the following discrete features:  
- logistics topology (number of Package and Delivery objects, whether packages are consolidated or split, presence of EXT objects on Pack/Dispatch);  
- finance topology (number of Invoice and Payment objects, presence of EXT objects on Issue Invoice/Record Payment, partial versus complete settlement);  
- exception qualifiers (replacement / removed_before_dispatch, return-inspect-repack cycle, partial_settles);  
- isolation (all objects local versus cross-order sharing).  
Formally one extracts a motif signature (see §2) and declares equivalence when signatures coincide.  
Invariants preserved: the business motif, key object-type multiplicities, EXT flags, and the presence/absence of designated O2O qualifiers and lifecycle cycles.  
Information discarded: concrete local identifiers, pure ordering of independent Add-Item events, absolute numeric attributes not encoded in the motif.  
By construction the relation partitions the set of executions into motif classes and is therefore an equivalence.  
Concurrency: independent Adds are abstracted to a multiset; the relative order of Pack and Dispatch is retained only when it alters the topology (consolidated versus interleaved shipments).

A fourth, coarser relation—lifecycle-motif equivalence that collapses all non-exception isolated executions—could be defined, but the three relations above already span the required design space.

## 2. Canonical signatures

The preferred relation is business-semantic equivalence. The following Python-like procedure produces a reproducible, rename-invariant signature.

```python
def canonical_signature(exec):
    # 1. Normalise local IDs (type-aware rank by participation)
    objects = renumber_local_ids(exec.objects, preserve_EXT=True)
    events  = rewrite_E2O(exec.events, objects)
    o2o     = rewrite_O2O(exec.o2o, objects)

    # 2. Extract multiplicities and flags
    n_item   = count(objects, type="Item")
    n_pack   = count(objects, type="Package")
    n_deliv  = count(objects, type="Delivery")
    n_inv    = count(objects, type="Invoice")
    n_pay    = count(objects, type="Payment")
    ext_log  = any(EXT in e.objects for e in events if e.act in {"Pack Package","Dispatch"})
    ext_fin  = any(EXT in e.objects for e in events if e.act in {"Issue Invoice","Record Payment"})
    has_repl = any(q in {"replaces","removed_before_dispatch"} for q in o2o.qualifiers)
    has_ret  = any(e.act in {"Return Item","Inspect Return"} for e in events)
    settle   = "partial" if any("partially_settles" in q for q in o2o.qualifiers) else "complete"

    # 3. Activity skeleton (collapse consecutive identical labels)
    skeleton = collapse_repeats([e.act for e in events])

    # 4. Motif label
    if has_ret:
        motif = "ReturnRepack"
    elif has_repl:
        motif = "Substitution"
    elif ext_log:
        motif = "SharedLogistics"
    elif ext_fin:
        motif = "SharedFinance"
    elif n_pack > 1 and n_deliv == 1:
        motif = "MultiPackConsolidated"
    elif n_pack > 1 and n_deliv > 1:
        motif = "MultiShipPartialPay"
    else:
        motif = "StandardIsolated"

    return (motif, n_item, n_pack, n_deliv, n_inv, n_pay,
            ext_log, ext_fin, has_repl, has_ret, settle, tuple(skeleton))
```

The tuple is hashable and invariant under local renaming while retaining every feature required by the business-semantic definition.

## 4. Cluster interpretations

**StandardIsolated (X1,X2)**  
Signature: single Package, single Delivery, single complete Payment, no EXT, no exception qualifiers.  
Business behaviour: classic “happy-path” order fulfilment—items packed together, shipped once, invoiced and paid once.  
Operational risks/opportunities: low complexity, high predictability; opportunity for automated straight-through processing.  
Traditional flattening (by Order) represents it well; flattening by Package or Delivery yields identical cases.  
Stable under reasonable data-repair (identifier renaming, re-ordering of independent Adds).

**MultiPackConsolidated (X3)**  
Signature: two Packages, one Delivery.  
Business behaviour: items packed separately (size, fragility, warehouse location) yet shipped together.  
Risks: packing bottlenecks; opportunities: optimised vehicle fill-rate.  
Order-centric flattening hides the multi-pack structure; Package-centric flattening fragments the delivery.  
Stable provided the single-Delivery topology is recovered correctly.

**MultiShipPartialPay (X4)**  
Signature: two Packages, two Deliveries, two Payments with partial-settlement qualifiers.  
Business behaviour: intentional multi-shipment (back-order, split fulfilment) together with progressive payment.  
Risks: partial-delivery customer complaints, cash-flow fragmentation; opportunities: revenue recognition per shipment.  
Any single-object flattening loses either the logistics split or the payment split.  
Stable if partial-settlement qualifiers are retained; collapses to MultiPackConsolidated if payments are aggregated.

**SharedLogistics (X5)**  
Signature: Pack/Dispatch events contain EXT objects.  
Business behaviour: cross-order consolidation of packages (shared container or truck).  
Risks: allocation of shipping cost, delay propagation across orders; opportunities: reduced logistics cost.  
Order flattening hides the shared package; the connected component of the O2O graph is essential.  
Stable only while EXT flags are preserved; disappears if external objects are stripped.

**SharedFinance (X6)**  
Signature: Issue Invoice / Record Payment contain EXT objects.  
Business behaviour: joint invoicing of several orders (framework contracts, consolidated billing).  
Risks: payment-allocation ambiguity, credit-risk contagion; opportunities: fewer invoices, lower administrative cost.  
Order flattening treats the invoice as local; Invoice-centric views are required.  
Stable under the same conditions as SharedLogistics.

**Substitution (X7)**  
Signature: presence of replaces / removed_before_dispatch qualifiers.  
Business behaviour: an item was replaced or withdrawn before dispatch (stock-out, customer change, quality issue).  
Risks: inventory adjustment, customer notification, possible re-pricing; opportunities: proactive substitution service.  
Activity-sequence or pure structural views treat it as StandardIsolated; the qualifier is the only distinguishing signal.  
Unstable if O2O qualifiers are dropped (then merges with StandardIsolated).

**ReturnRepack (X8)**  
Signature: Return Item – Inspect Return – Pack – Dispatch cycle.  
Business behaviour: customer return followed by re-fulfilment of the returned item.  
Risks: reverse-logistics cost, quality inspection overhead, delayed revenue; opportunities: secondary sales, warranty analytics.  
Order flattening mixes the original and the return path; a dedicated return object type or qualifier is needed.  
Stable provided the extra activities are not filtered out.

## 5. Borderline cases

**X1 versus X2.**  
Activity-sequence, typed-structural and business-semantic all declare equivalence (renaming + commutative Adds). A stricter “exact identifier sequence” relation would separate them; any useful object-centric equivalence discards pure local names, so the three definitions agree.

**X1 versus X5.**  
Activity-sequence places both in cluster A. Typed-structural and business-semantic separate them because of EXT objects on the package. A pure “label + multiplicity” definition that ignores EXT flags would merge them; the business risk of cross-order delay propagation justifies keeping them apart.

**X1 versus X6.**  
Identical activity sequence; structural difference only on the finance side (EXT invoice). Equivalence notions that look solely at logistics topology merge them; notions that treat financial sharing as a first-class motif keep them distinct—correct for payment-allocation analysis.

**X1 versus X7.**  
Activity labels coincide and the object-type multiplicities are almost identical; only the Dispatch E2O list and the O2O qualifiers differ. Activity-sequence and a qualifier-free structural isomorphism therefore equate them; business-semantic treats the replacement as a distinct exception motif. The disagreement is exactly the value placed on explicit O2O qualifiers.

**X3 versus X4.**  
Both contain two Pack events, yet X3 consolidates into one Delivery while X4 produces two Deliveries and two Payments. An equivalence that counts only Package multiplicity merges them; an equivalence that also inspects Delivery and Payment multiplicities (or the interleaving of Pack/Dispatch) separates them—reflecting the operational difference between one shipment and progressive fulfilment.

**X4 versus X8.**  
Both exhibit multiple Dispatch events. Activity-sequence and a naïve “multi-dispatch” count therefore look similar. The presence of the Return–Inspect cycle and the replacement relation “P82 replaces P81 for I82” make X8 an exception path; business-semantic therefore keeps them apart. Ignoring the return activities would erroneously place X8 with X4.

## 6. Relation to `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel`

The PM4Py key is a pair  
\(( \text{event sequence}, \text{object sequence} )\),  
where each event is the tuple \((\text{activity}, o_1, o_2, \dots)\) of the concrete object identifiers related to that event, and each object is the pair \((\text{oid}, \text{otype})\). Consequently the key preserves exact identifier strings, the total order of events, and the precise E2O incidences; O2O qualifiers are not part of the key.

Because raw identifiers appear, two executions that differ only by renaming receive distinct keys and form singleton clusters. Canonicalisation is therefore mandatory:

```python
import pm4py
from collections import defaultdict

def rename_invariant_cluster(ocel, lead_type="Order", max_objs=100):
    raw = pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, object_type=lead_type, max_objs=max_objs)
    # post-process: map each key to a canonical signature
    groups = defaultdict(list)
    for key, ocel_list in raw.items():
        # extract the process execution of the lead object
        for sub_ocel in ocel_list:
            sig = canonical_signature(sub_ocel)   # §2
            groups[sig].append(sub_ocel)
    return dict(groups)
```

Exact equivalence (the raw PM4Py clusters) is preferable when one must detect literal duplicates or when identifier schemes themselves carry audit meaning. Coarser business clustering is preferable for process discovery, performance comparison and exception mining. The parameter `max_objs` truncates large connected components; if an order shares packages or invoices with many other orders the extracted execution may be incomplete, producing spurious signature differences. In practice one therefore filters or expands the connected component before calling the function.

## 7. Robustness analysis

- **Ignore all O2O qualifiers.** Substitution (X7) collapses into StandardIsolated; MultiShipPartialPay loses its “partial” flag and may be merged with a multi-shipment motif that does not distinguish payment structure. ReturnRepack remains distinct because of the extra activities.  
- **Ignore external objects.** SharedLogistics and SharedFinance both become StandardIsolated; the remaining clusters are unchanged. Cross-order coupling is no longer visible.  
- **Collapse repeated same-label events.** The two Pack events of X3 and the two Pack/Dispatch pairs of X4 become single events; X3 moves closer to StandardIsolated while X4 still differs by the second Delivery and the second Payment. Activity-sequence clusters shrink.  
- **Treat Pack and Dispatch as a partial order.** The interleaving Pack–Dispatch–Pack–Dispatch of X4 may be regarded as concurrent with the consecutive Pack–Pack–Dispatch of X3; the two multi-pack clusters become harder to separate unless Delivery multiplicity is retained.  
- **Treat payment multiplicity as an amount attribute.** The structural distinction “two Payment objects” disappears; X4 is then distinguished from X3 only by the number of Deliveries. Business-semantic still separates them, but the signature loses the explicit partial-settlement marker.

In all four (plus the optional) perturbations the core motif distinctions that rely on activity presence (ReturnRepack) or on EXT flags remain robust; distinctions that rely solely on qualifiers or on fine interleaving become fragile.

## 8. Limitations and recommendation

- **Compliance monitoring** – prefer typed structural equivalence (or the exact PM4Py key after mild renaming). It guarantees that every object incidence and every qualifier is examined, which is required for audit trails.  
- **Operational segmentation** – prefer the business-semantic clusters defined above. They group executions that share the same logistics and finance topology, enabling capacity planning and SLA design.  
- **Root-cause analysis** – prefer the finest relation that still respects O2O qualifiers (typed structural including qualifiers). Only then can a replacement or a partial settlement be isolated as the distinguishing factor.  
- **Descriptive reporting** – a coarser lifecycle-motif equivalence that collapses StandardIsolated with Substitution (when qualifier quality is low) yields stable, easily communicated high-level categories.

No single equivalence is optimal for all four purposes. Compliance demands the preservation of every identifier and qualifier; operational and descriptive work needs abstraction of pure renaming and of immaterial concurrency; root-cause analysis needs a middle ground that retains exception signals while discarding noise. The design space of §1 therefore supplies a deliberate menu rather than a unique “correct” clustering.
