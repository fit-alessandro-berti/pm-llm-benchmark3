## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

**Hard invariants.** Every added E2O endpoint must name an existing event and object; every added O2O endpoint must name existing objects. Preserve O2O direction and the recorded qualifier: `DEL-X carries PK-7` means the delivery points to the package. Do not duplicate an identical relation. These are structural checks, not claims about business truth. No rule here makes an object lifecycle complete by force.

**Strong defaults.** A `Create Order` event ordinarily concerns one Order; `Add Item` one Order and one Item; `Pack Item` one Item and one Package; `Dispatch Package` one Package and one Delivery; an invoice concerns one Invoice and one or more Orders; a payment ordinarily concerns one Payment and one Invoice. These set expectations for cardinality. The observed events mostly follow them, and ev08, ev10, and ev14 are the conspicuous incomplete rows. However, the stated scanner outage, bulk dispatch, portal behavior, and unapplied cash exceptions mean a deviation is not itself proof of a missing edge.

**Weak heuristics.** Identifier similarity is strongest when a full identifier matches: ev10's `N-421` matches DEL-Y's carrier reference, while ev11's wildcard `N-42*` matches both deliveries and is less discriminating. `PK8-2` points toward PK-8 but is a scanner reference, not a declared foreign key. Matching time, route, customer, amounts, and chronological order raise or lower plausibility; none establishes a relation alone. Amount 230 equals the two declared order values and the invoice total, and PAY-2 is 130 after PAY-1 of 100, but fees, discounts, taxes, unrelated cash, and unapplied cash remain possible. Existing O2O structure helps: ORD-B contains IT-B1, PK-8 already packs IT-A2, and DEL-X carries PK-7. It provides lifecycle context but does not automatically prove a missing edge.

**Evidence against a repair.** Existing event relations only to ORD-A at confirmation and invoice issuance, only to ORD-A for INV-44's observed `bills` relation, and only to INV-44 for PAY-1 are evidence against adding symmetric-looking relations without further evidence. They may simply be incomplete. A missing endpoint in an otherwise normally binary event is evidence for repair; an expressly allowed exception (scanner outage, portal identifier loss, unapplied cash) tempers it. I combine evidence cumulatively but discount correlated clues: a scanner reference and a package association inferred from that same reference are not independent confirmations; neither is an assumed invoice relation followed by an O2O edge inferred from it.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A — conservative/minimum-change (selected).** Add R1–R4 only. In ev08, PK-8 is a plausible delayed scanner reconciliation for IT-B1; adding `PK-8 packs IT-B1` then gives the package a second item. Keep ev10 linked to PK-8 and add DEL-Y, with DEL-Y carrying PK-8. Leave ev11 related only to ORD-A: the wildcard and common route do not identify which delivery, and the portal may omit the carrier reference. Keep INV-44 billed to ORD-A alone and leave ev12 without ORD-B. Leave PAY-2 without an invoice edge and retain the possibility of unapplied cash. This yields credible item/package/dispatch chains while allowing incomplete confirmation and finance links.

**Bundle B — consolidated shipment and finance.** Accept R1–R4, add both DEL-X and DEL-Y to ev11, add ORD-B to ev12 and `INV-44 bills ORD-B`, and add INV-44 to ev14 plus `PAY-2 settles INV-44`. This interprets the portal wildcard as a combined acknowledgment of the two NORTH deliveries, the invoice as covering both same-customer orders, and PAY-2 as the remaining 130 balance after PAY-1. The bundle produces a coherent aggregate lifecycle: both orders' items are packed and dispatched, the two deliveries are acknowledged together, one invoice bills both orders, and two payments settle it. But the inference chain is weaker than the resulting neat lifecycle may make it appear. In particular, the amount equalities do not prove either invoice scope or payment allocation, and confirmation of DEL-X/Y does not establish that ORD-B was on either delivery. Bundle B should be retained as a scenario, not silently substituted for recorded fact.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

- **Order:** ORD-A has create, two item-add events, an order-level delivery confirmation, and an invoice event. ORD-B has create and item-add events. Bundle A leaves B without invoice or confirmation links, which is incomplete but plausible; its three observed `contains` edges (two for A, one for B) are not suspicious. Bundle B gives the same invoice event to both orders; that one-to-many invoice scope is allowed, though it is inferred. No order-to-delivery O2O relation is implied.
- **Item:** IT-A1, IT-A2, and IT-B1 each have an Add Item event and a Pack Item event. A1/A2 have both event and O2O package evidence. For B1, the repair pairs ev08 with PK-8 and adds the package edge; both derive largely from `PK8-2`, so do not count the two relation tables as independent corroboration. A false edge would make an apparent cross-order package.
- **Package:** PK-7 has Pack Item then Dispatch Package, and DEL-X carries it. PK-8 has the A2 pack event and dispatch; Bundle A assigns B1 too. A package with several items is reasonable. The possible cross-order contents of PK-8 are noteworthy, not a cardinality violation. The package is not orphaned in either bundle.
- **Delivery:** DEL-X is linked to ev09 and carries PK-7. Bundle A links DEL-Y to ev10 and PK-8. Both have dispatch lifecycles; neither needs a confirmation event to be valid. ev11 stays at order level in A. Bundle B links both deliveries to that event, an allowed joint acknowledgment, but does not independently prove which order each delivered to.
- **Invoice:** INV-44 has an issue event and one observed billed order, ORD-A. Bundle A leaves ORD-B unbilled by the observed relation set; it is not an orphan. Bundle B makes a one-invoice/two-order relation, allowed by the domain. The O2O edge and ev12–ORD-B E2O edge would be mutually consistent but not independent: both would be inferred from the same aggregate-amount/customer story.
- **Payment:** PAY-1 is recorded and settles INV-44. PAY-2 has a record event but no invoice relation in A, so it is an unapplied or unresolved payment, not a broken object. B assigns it to INV-44. Since `Record Payment` normally has one invoice, this is plausible; there is no evidence of a second invoice object, but cash could remain unapplied.
- **Events:** ev01–ev05 meet their normal object cardinalities. ev06 and ev07 have item/package pairs; ev08 is item-only until provisionally repaired. ev09 has package/delivery; ev10 has package only until repaired. ev11's order-only form is explicitly permitted. ev12 has invoice/order; ev13 payment/invoice; ev14 payment only until any allocation is inferred. No events or objects are invented. In Bundle A no event is wholly unreferenced, though ev08 and ev10 are incomplete before the repair and ev14 stays financially unresolved.

There is circular-reasoning risk in using ev08's inferred PK-8 E2O edge to assert `PK-8 packs IT-B1`, then citing that O2O edge as confirmation of the E2O edge. Both are one scanner-reference hypothesis. Similarly, a combined invoice hypothesis from amount/customer can motivate both an ev12–ORD-B edge and an INV-44–ORD-B edge; their agreement does not supply two independent proofs. PAY-2 to INV-44 and `PAY-2 settles INV-44` have the same dependency. Keep provenance for the original clue behind each addition.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

The unrepaired log has 14 events and 12 registered objects; repairs add relations, not events or objects. Event-count activity frequencies therefore stay fixed. The observed related-object incidences and distinct object IDs per activity can change. In the unrepaired view, Pack Item has five E2O incidences across ev06–08 and five distinct related objects; Bundle A adds one incidence to an already represented PK-8, so the distinct count rises only if counted per event-activity rather than globally, while total incidences rise to six. Dispatch Package rises from three to four incidences and gains DEL-Y as a distinct object. Bundle B adds three more E2O incidences (two deliveries to ev11, ORD-B to ev12, and INV-44 to ev14: four additions) beyond A; Confirm Delivery then has three related objects. If counting unique objects in the registry, the total remains twelve; if counting event-object occurrences, it increases with each E2O addition. State which measure is used.

For an OC-DFG, the repaired PK-8/package path can expose a `Pack Item` to `Dispatch Package` fragment for PK-8 through ev07/ev08/ev10. Bundle A does not connect `Confirm Delivery` to either delivery, so it does not create a delivery-level dispatch-to-confirm path. Bundle B can create `Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery` for both delivery objects. Adding ORD-B to ev12 can create a financial continuation on that order from item/order activity to `Issue Invoice`; assigning PAY-2 to INV-44 can extend the invoice object's `Issue Invoice → Record Payment` sequence. These links may make a process look more complete while actually duplicating the same invoice or payment event in order-level analyses.

In an OTG, Bundle A reinforces existing type patterns `Package → Item` (packs) and `Delivery → Package` (carries), without introducing new endpoint types. Bundle B also increases observed `Invoice → Order` and `Payment → Invoice` instances, but those type-level patterns already exist. The ET–OT graph similarly gains relation counts on existing `Pack Item–Package`, `Dispatch Package–Delivery`, and `Record Payment–Invoice` type pairs in A; Bundle B adds a `Confirm Delivery–Delivery` pair and reinforces `Issue Invoice–Order`. A projection that displays only type adjacency may hide the uncertainty and appear more certain than the source.

Flattening on **Package** can put IT-A2 and IT-B1 into PK-8's trace in A, thereby combining two orders' item activity. Flattening on **Delivery** gives DEL-Y a dispatch in A; B additionally gives it a confirmation. Flattening on **Order** keeps ORD-A's existing invoice and confirmation events. In B, ev12 appears in both order traces and ev11 may still be only tied to ORD-A unless ORD-B is separately added to ev11—which this bundle does not do. Thus invoice activity can be duplicated across order projections while confirmation is not. Flattening on **Invoice** or **Payment** can similarly create shared financial fragments in B. Order, Package, and Delivery flattenings can therefore disagree about apparent completion.

Event frequency remains a count of event rows. Activity frequency by unique related objects and total event-object incidences changes as above; a unique-object count is not an event count. Equivalent-execution clustering around Order may treat ORD-A and ORD-B as more alike in B because both acquire `Issue Invoice` and share INV-44, despite different item/order histories. The shared PK-8 already creates a possible cross-order bridge in A. Depending on flattening and whether shared events are copied, identical invoice/payment fragments can be duplicated and spuriously increase similarity; with Bundle A, the orders remain more distinct and B's financial execution may look unfinished.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. Assigning both deliveries to ev11 from `N-42*` can convert one portal acknowledgment into two delivery completions.
2. Assigning ev11 to ORD-B because it shares customer C17 with ORD-A confuses customer-level identity with order-level scope.
3. Adding ORD-B to INV-44 solely because 130 + 100 = 230 treats arithmetic as proof and may erase fees, discounts, taxes, or a different invoice scope.
4. Linking PAY-2 to INV-44 solely because 100 + 130 = 230 can falsely close an invoice when PAY-2 is unapplied cash.
5. Treating `PK8-2` as a guaranteed package foreign key can turn the stated scanner outage into false package contents and make PK-8 span two orders.
6. Treating parallel timestamps and route labels as proof can create a delivery-to-package or delivery-to-confirmation chain unsupported by a carrier identifier.
7. Reversing `DEL-Y carries PK-8` into `PK-8 carries DEL-Y` violates the qualifier's direction and changes object-type relationships.
8. Counting E2O and O2O edges inferred from the same clue as independent confirmation inflates confidence and can create convincing but circular lifecycles.
9. Forcing every payment, order, package, or delivery into a completed path hides legitimate exceptions and unresolved records, biasing discovered models toward clean but invented behavior.
