## 1. Decision rule

**Principle.** The unit of decision is a slot, not an alert. `p_now` is an observational forecast of breach under business as usual; it says nothing by itself about whether a swarm can change the outcome (I-03 is the proof). A slot is spent only when the plausible causal benefit to the treated case exceeds its full cost, including the harm it pushes onto untreated cases. Leaving a slot unused is a legitimate outcome.

**Step 1 – Hard gates (all must pass).**
- G1 Stage: the case is before `Diagnosis Complete` and is expected to still be there when the swarm starts.
- G2 Feasibility: time to deadline ≥ 45 min + a fast-but-plausible remaining path (e.g. the P10 historical time from end of diagnosis to `Closed` for that incident class). Otherwise the case goes to a rescue path, not a swarm.
- G3 Novelty: no earlier swarm on the case, and no earlier decline on unchanged evidence, unless a materially new event has occurred (defined in Section 4).
- G4 Data integrity: no decision-critical feature is imputed. If one is, the case is held for a manual check of at most 30 minutes.

**Step 2 – Robustified probability `p_adj` (an interval, never a point).**

| Segment / condition | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| SME | `p_now` + 0.04 to 0.09 |
| Strategic Enterprise | `p_now` − 0.05 to 0.12 |
| Public Sector | `p_now` ± 0.05 (moderate sample) |
| Non-Native-Language Support | `p_now` ± 0.10, no directional correction |
| Imputed critical feature | interval spans `p_prev` to `p_now` |

`p_adj` is used in three ways:
1. To open an alert episode: upper bound ≥ 0.35, or a one-prefix rise ≥ 0.10. This only places the case on the board.
2. To weight the false-alarm burden by the probability the alarm is false: (1 − `p_adj`) × burden.
3. To cap the effect (it cannot exceed baseline risk) and to shade the supplied effect range by roughly `p_adj`/`p_now` where the model overpredicts (Strategic, about ×0.8–0.9). SME ranges are not inflated beyond what was supplied.

Wide intervals (Non-Native-Language, imputed data) trigger a human check. They are not a score penalty, because that would systematically deprioritise poorly validated groups.

**Step 3 – Break-even test against the effect range.**

ARR* = [€450 + (1 − `p_adj`) × false-alarm burden + λ] / value of avoiding breach

λ is the spillover shadow price of one slot: 6 exposed cases × Δp per case × average value of an exposed case. I value an exposed case at €10k–€24.5k, the median and mean of the six listed cases left untreated under my portfolio. The 0.04 for a congested queue is my extrapolation from the 15–30 minute adverse delay.

| Queue state | Δp per exposed case | λ range | Planning value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light | 0.01 | €0.6k–1.5k | €1,000 |
| Normal | 0.02 | €1.2k–2.9k | €2,500 (deliberately cautious) |
| Congested | 0.04 | €2.4k–5.9k | €5,000 |

The queue state is read from observable signals (diagnosis-queue wait, backlog) and from the delay measured during the previous swarm.

- **Tier A:** ARR* is below the lower bound of the (shaded) effect range. The swarm is worthwhile even on pessimistic effects.
- **Tier B:** ARR* is inside the range. Worthwhile only if the effect is not at the low end.
- **Tier C:** ARR* is above the range. Not worthwhile at the current queue state; offer a lite action instead.

**Step 4 – Prioritisation and capacity.**
1. Regulatory, statutory or safety obligations take lexical priority, subject to G1.
2. Tier A comes before Tier B. Tier C never receives a slot at the current λ.
3. Within a tier, the case whose intervention window closes first goes first. Long-slack cases are scheduled later and re-confirmed at slot start, since waiting one or two refreshes is nearly free.
4. One slot is reserved until 15:00. It is broken earlier only for a Tier A case about to lose its window whose net value after λ clearly exceeds the reserve's option value (default: more than about €5k).
5. Before every slot, re-check stage, `p_adj` and measured congestion. No more than two swarms run back to back; then the specialists return to the queue for at least 30 minutes.
6. Every declined case receives a named non-swarm action. "No slot" never means "no action".

**Selected portfolio: I-09 (firm), I-04 (firm, subject to confirmation), I-02 (conditional on the congestion gate). One slot is reserved** for the possible regulatory incident until 15:00.

- **Why the reserve costs little.** The best fourth candidate is worth roughly zero after spillover, so the reserve is worth holding even if the regulatory incident is unlikely.
- **After 15:00.** The reserve goes to I-08 or I-01 if either then clears the break-even test at measured congestion. Otherwise it stays unused.
- **The judgement call.** In euro terms I-02 edges out I-01 for the third slot. In breach-count terms I-01 is better (midpoint effect 0.27 vs 0.15). An organisation that weights equal treatment over contract value could defensibly swap them.

**Arithmetic.**
- *Effects (low / mid / high):* I-09 0.18 / 0.24 / 0.30; I-04 0.10 / 0.19 / 0.28; I-02 0.08 / 0.15 / 0.22.
- *Optimistic benefit (upper half of each range):* I-09 €4.8k–6.0k, I-04 €11.4k–16.8k, I-02 €3.75k–5.5k; total €19.95k–28.3k.
- *Base benefit (midpoints):* €4.8k + €11.4k + €3.75k = €19.95k. With the two Strategic effects shaded ×0.85: €4.8k + €9.7k + €3.2k = €17.7k.
- *Adverse benefit (lower bounds, one case at zero):* €9.6k if I-02 fails, €8.0k if I-09 fails, €5.6k if I-04 fails.
- *Cost:* 3 × €450 = €1,350, plus expected false-alarm burden of about €1.7k (I-09 0.31 × €800, I-04 0.60 × €2,000, I-02 0.42 × €600). In the adverse case the failed case bears its full burden.
- *Spillover:* 3 slots × 6 cases × Δp, valued at €10k–€24.5k per breach. Δp is 0.01 in the optimistic case, 0.01–0.03 in base (central 0.02) and 0.03–0.05 in adverse.

**What the table implies.**
- **The third slot adds almost nothing at base.** A two-slot variant (I-09 + I-04) yields about +€6k to +€11k at base and about −€14k to −€0.5k in the adverse case. That is why I-02 is gated rather than firm.
- **Congestion is observable; effect failure is not.** The adverse outcome is driven mostly by congestion, which can be seen before each slot. Slots 2 and 3 are cancelled if the delay measured during slot 1 exceeds about 20 minutes in aggregate.
- **Part of the gain is a transfer.** Some of the monetary benefit comes from moving breach risk from lower-value untreated cases to higher-value treated ones. That is a fairness cost, not a free gain (Section 5).
- **Assumptions to treat with caution.** The spillover model is applied uniformly, though short-slack cases are probably hit harder than I-08 or I-10. If the six exposed cases are unlisted queue cases, the same formula applies with their values. As a rough subjective weighting (20% optimistic, 55% base, 25% adverse), the expected net is about +€5k to +€8k with roughly a one-in-four chance of a net loss.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**One episode per case.** A model refresh updates the existing episode: one ticket, one page, a visible alert counter and episode age. Three refreshes on the same evidence are one piece of evidence, not three.

**Material change (minimum evidence change).** Any one of:
- a new process event: stage transition, reassignment, reopening, a customer-supplied artefact such as a log, a vendor or dependency update, or a widened scope;
- a `p_adj` shift of at least 0.10 since the last decision, driven by features other than elapsed time and sustained for two refreshes unless accompanied by an event;
- repair of a previously imputed feature;
- the case reaching its last responsible moment (entry into `Diagnosis Started`, or slack falling below about 3 hours).

Not material: a refresh with unchanged events, a change under 0.05, or a change caused only by imputation.

**Cool-down.** After any decision (swarm or decline), the case is not reconsidered for 90 minutes or until a material change, whichever comes first. After a swarm, a second swarm on the same case needs a material new event plus duty-lead approval, with at most one repeat per case.

**Suppression.** Alerts without material change are logged but not paged. Suppression always comes with a scheduled re-review at the last responsible moment, so a suppressed case is never silently abandoned.

**Escalation.**
- Two consecutive rising alerts with no action and shrinking slack force an explicit duty-lead decision (I-02 and I-09 today).
- A swarmed case that is still high-risk after two refreshes escalates to non-swarm channels (management, vendor, customer communication), not to a second swarm.
- A high-risk case past the effective stage (I-03) goes straight to resolution-phase escalation.

**Re-entry.** A material change resets the episode and the case competes afresh through all gates and the break-even test. Re-entry grants a review, not a slot.

**I-05 versus I-07.**

| | I-05 | I-07 |
|---|---|---|
| Alert history | Third alert, no new event | Third alert, new customer log uploaded |
| Risk movement | 0.55 → 0.57, inside the noise band | 0.75 → 0.72, slightly lower |
| Novelty gate | Fails. The alerts are the 30-minute refresh re-reading the same facts. | Passes. The log is diagnostic material a senior can act on, so treatability may have changed even though risk did not rise. |

The point is that the direction of the probability change is not the test; the arrival of new, actionable evidence is.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-based prioritisation starving lower-value cases.** Today's slots go to two Strategic cases and one Non-Native-Language case, while every SME case waits even though SME risk is underpredicted and I-05 has the largest effect per slot. Safeguards:
- Report every allocation in both euros and breach counts, and require each funded case to be non-negative in breach-count terms (midpoint effect ≥ about 0.12 induced breaches per slot).
- Winsorise case value in the score at about the 90th percentile so that one €100k case cannot dominate.
- Set a rolling four-week floor: each segment's share of slots is at least 60% of its share of eligible Tier A/B alerts. A shortfall earmarks the next released slot.
- Run a starvation counter: a case or segment declined three times with positive direct net value gets a mandatory duty-lead review.
- Guarantee the lite action (15-minute senior review) to every declined alert.

**Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support.** Wide intervals must not become automatic deprioritisation, nor automatic favouring. Safeguards:
- Use the same effect quantile as for other segments; uncertainty triggers a human check, not a penalty.
- Check whether translation delays are being read by the model as risk signals.
- Ensure language support is present in the swarm so the effect is not degraded.
- Log every such case in full and pool estimates hierarchically with other segments until its own sample suffices.

**Differential calibration across segments.** A single raw threshold would over-alert Strategic and under-alert SME, a hidden transfer of capacity toward high-value customers. Safeguards:
- Apply segment-specific offsets with shrinkage, and always act on intervals.
- Recalibrate Strategic on post-redesign data only.
- Track alert recall and missed-breach rate per segment weekly.

**Selective intervention changing future training labels.** Swarmed cases that do not breach look like "low-risk" examples. The model then learns that treated profiles (high-value, Strategic) are safe and under-alerts them later. Safeguards:
- Log the treatment flag, the score at decision time, tier, propensity and the queue state for every alert.
- Never train on treated outcomes as if untreated: use untreated and randomised-control cases, or treatment-aware or inverse-propensity-weighted models.
- Record slot usage as a covariate for untreated cases, since their outcomes are contaminated by spillover.

**Spillover harm falling on untreated groups.** The specialists' ordinary queue work is displaced; if that work is mostly SME or Public Sector, those groups pay for Strategic swarms. Safeguards:
- Measure delay minutes and breach changes by segment during swarm windows.
- Route unavoidable delay to cases with the most slack, not the lowest value.
- Give protected queue status and backfill during swarms to untreated cases with under 3 hours of slack or `p_adj` above 0.6 (today I-05, I-06 and I-07).
- Cap spillover at about 20 minutes of aggregate delay per slot, and pause further swarms above it.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational monitoring.**
- Alerts and episodes per day, and the share of repeat alerts suppressed.
- Time from alert to decision and from decision to swarm start.
- Swarms wasted because the case reached `Diagnosis Complete` first.
- Human overrides and their stated reasons.
- Feature-feed health: a missing live field automatically downgrades the alert to "verify first".
- Uptake and outcomes of lite actions.

**Predictive monitoring.**
- Weekly reliability curves, Brier score and calibration-in-the-large with confidence intervals, by segment, stage, prefix length and language support.
- Computed on untreated and randomised-control cases, or propensity-weighted, since treated cases no longer reveal untreated risk.
- Feature drift checks, especially around workflow redesigns.
- Trigger: update a segment's offset when its interval excludes zero for two consecutive weeks or the offset exceeds 0.05.

**Causal-effect monitoring.** The supplied effect ranges are priors, not facts.
- Randomise at the margin: Tier B cases within about ±€1k of break-even receive the swarm with a known probability (for example 50%). Regulatory and Tier A cases are never randomised.
- Use capacity-rationed days as a secondary comparison: qualified cases that went unserved, with adjustment.
- Outcomes: breach, time to `Diagnosis Complete`, time to resolution, and a mechanism code per swarm (accelerated diagnosis, exposed missing information, redirected the case, no effect).
- Estimate effects with a hierarchical model by segment, stage and slack, so sparse cells borrow strength.
- Because swarms affect untreated cases, estimate spillover with switchback designs (blocks of days capped at two slots vs four) and by regressing queue delay and breach on slots used.

**Capacity monitoring.**
- Slots used, slots left unused, and how often the reserve was actually needed.
- The reserve's opportunity cost: net value of the best case it displaced.
- Aggregate queue delay measured per slot, diagnosis-queue P50 and P90 waits, and specialist utilisation.
- λ re-estimated weekly from measured delay and its link to breach.

**Fairness monitoring.**
- By segment, value tercile and language support: alert rate, selection rate given eligibility, declines with positive net value, breach rates treated and untreated, spillover minutes borne, calibration error.
- Monthly review against the floor and the starvation counter.

**Detecting policy-induced drift.**
- Maintain a shadow model trained only on untreated and control cases; a growing gap from the production model signals label contamination.
- Watch for falling apparent risk or discrimination among treated profiles.
- Watch for behavioural gaming: delayed marking of `Diagnosis Complete` to stay eligible, or uploads timed to trigger re-entry.

**When to change the threshold or allocation rule.** Triggers are pre-specified:
- Measured λ leaves its band for two weeks: update the planning values.
- The posterior for a segment-stage cell puts most of its mass below break-even: move that cell to Tier C. If its lower bound clears break-even: promote it to Tier A.
- Reserve used on fewer than 10% of days over eight weeks: release it earlier (for example 13:00). Used on more than 40%: make it a hard rule.
- A fairness floor breached two months running: raise the floor or lower the value cap.
- Realised portfolio results repeatedly resembling the adverse scenario: cut the default to two slots per day pending review.

Changes are made one at a time, backtested in shadow mode for two weeks, versioned, and approved by a named owner. They are not made on fewer than about 30–50 swarms per affected cell unless a safety or regulatory issue requires it.
