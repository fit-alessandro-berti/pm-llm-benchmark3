## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts and limits.** GPIP-4 is a board-approved, company-wide policy; the Aachen SOP is a controlled local procedure; ECD-2 is a signed incident directive with a defined window and stated subject; and the CFO message is an ordinary email. The Chief Risk Officer neither issued nor ratified an exception. Those facts establish no universal ranking for every issue. In particular, an unapproved supplier is not necessarily a *new* supplier, and an ERP entry is not necessarily the time of the underlying act.

**Interpretive assumptions used for the primary classifications.** I read GPIP-4's express pre-commitment integrity requirements as continuing unless an A7 exception is established. The COO/Incident Commander signatures and past overlay practice support ECD-2 as an operational directive, but do not establish a written exception *by the Chief Risk Officer*. I read the SOP's required Plant and Finance approvals as prospective authorizations, although B2 does not expressly say “before commitment”; this follows their approval purpose, B4's limited emergency changes, and B5. A Finance_Approve entry is provisionally evidence of Finance approval, but does not establish the signer's authority without the approval record. Incident Command membership alone does not establish independent budget-owner or Finance Controller authority. The CFO may interpret and arrange Finance controls within delegated authority, but the email alone does not remove A2's named approvers, A1's segregation, or A3's Compliance gate. “Critical” requires a direct, time-sensitive connection to containment; neither a rigid 24-hour cutoff nor Operations' label alone decides scope. Cafeteria equipment repair is provisionally within the SOP's “maintenance” scope, an interpretation of B's wording rather than a stated fact.

**Method and burden.** Test scope and controlling rules at the actual supplier commitment, then test each required prior act against that time. A later approval does not cure a missing prior approval. A later *record* can prove an earlier verbal act if authenticated and made within the applicable recording deadline. For exceptions, the proponent must establish the exception's authority, scope, timing, actors, and conditions with suitable evidence; uncertainty is not a presumed waiver. Conversely, do not infer a breach merely from a delayed ERP entry when stronger evidence supports timely action. Classify the whole case by its unresolved material requirement, while identifying separately each valid exception and each proof gap. “Noncompliant” below means a material deviation is established under these assumptions; it is not a finding of intent or of an irreversible commercial consequence.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

Keep four findings distinct: **policy breach** means a required act did not occur in time (for example, P-502's recorded late Compliance clearance); **logging defect** means a timely act was entered late (potentially P-507's 14:30 ERP entry); **late evidence of a timely act** means a later authenticated record proves an earlier authorization or commitment (P-507's signed 12:40 PDF, and possibly P-505's 17:59:31 acknowledgment); **inability to prove compliance** means the required act or authority may have existed but is not established (the budget-owner roles in several cases, P-508's C3 record). Do not convert the last category into a fabricated fact, or let it serve as an exception. ON-44 supports scrutiny of P-501/P-502 ERP chronology only; check later outages and clock drift independently.

**Immediate case actions.** For P-501 obtain Plant/Finance source records and active-delegation evidence; document the B4 basis and any late-approval consequence. For P-502 preserve the supplier call and acknowledgment, investigate why Compliance and Finance were postcommit, and have Compliance assess the new supplier and resulting exposure. For P-503 establish the backup-line need date and IC roles, determine whether PROC-3 participated in final approval, and escalate the missing A2/A3 gates. For P-504 settle SOP coverage and supplier-newness, review supplier eligibility, and correct any unauthorized commitment. For P-505 preserve the acknowledgment with headers and call evidence, audit ERP clock offset, locate the PO/C3 record, and review the late Finance approval. For P-506 document that the directive expired, determine whether a true B4 emergency existed, and address the late PO/Plant approval and unproved budget approval. For P-507 authenticate the PDF's signature and creation time, retain outage/queue logs, and investigate the missing A2/local approval evidence. For P-508 locate the C3 record and any Finance/budget/Plant authority instrument; preserve the precommit Compliance and Legal records. In each material breach, obtain a documented risk and consequence review by the relevant control owner; any later approval should be labeled remediation, not backdated compliance.

**Monitoring.** Reconstruct commitment from supplier communications as well as ERP data; store act time, record-creation time, and ingestion time separately. Configure precommit checks for amount, new-supplier status, approver identity/independence, and Finance/Compliance roles; make emergency route, directive version, covered lot, and expiry explicit fields. Alert on records near the four-hour and 24-hour deadlines, unapproved suppliers, self-approval, absent PO, and attempts to use an expired directive. Sample emergency purchases after the incident, including cases under EUR 25,000, to detect routine use of exceptional routes.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

> **Hierarchy and incident exceptions.** GPIP-4 applies to every purchase. A controlled site procedure may add safeguards. A dated incident directive may change only the local PO sequence, tender process, or supplier-onboarding sequence for specifically identified incident lots during its stated window, and only if it names the applicable conditions; silence does not waive a control. Any change to segregation of duties, the independent budget-owner and Finance Controller approvals at EUR 50,000 or more, or precommit Compliance clearance for purchases above EUR 100,000 or from a new supplier requires a written Chief Risk Officer exception identifying each affected clause, covered entities and lots, amount ceiling, alternate controls, issuer, start and expiry times. A CFO may issue written Finance role delegations consistent with GPIP-4, naming delegates and duration, but may not remove other GPIP-4 controls. Incident Command authorization must involve two distinct members before commitment; neither may approve their own request or supplier commitment as final business approver. Verbal authorization is effective only where the directive allows it and must be evidenced by an authenticated record created within four hours; a later system upload may prove the timely act but does not create it retrospectively. Later approval does not cure a missing precommit act. Extensions require a new written instrument before expiry; verbal continuation is ineffective. The CRO and Internal Audit shall review emergency-route volume and all exceptions daily during an incident and close the route at expiry, with any renewed use requiring fresh, lot-specific justification.
