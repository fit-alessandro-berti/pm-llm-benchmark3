# Process-Mining Impact Forecast: Organizational Restructuring

---

## **1. Mechanism map**
The restructuring introduces structural shifts with cascading process-mining effects:

| **Restructuring Action**               | **Direct Process-Mining Effect**                     | **Indirect Effect**                                  | **Counteracting Forces**                     |
|-----------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| **Centralized Technical Shared Service** | Reduced local-specialist handover loops (3641/100)   | Central queue forms bottleneck (4.0h median wait)    | Specialist utilization rises (15% increase) |
| **Shared Specialist Review Queue**     | Legal/Risk cases routed centrally (912/100)         | Longer waits for high-complexity cases (10.5h)       | Legal/Risk specialists near capacity         |
| **Standardized Approval Queue**         | Local supervisor removed; approvals centralized      | Role clarity declines (shared ownership unclear)      | Accountability may weaken                   |
| **Local Intake/Eligibility Retained**   | Fewer cross-team handovers (63/100)                 | Local expertise loss if early advice reduced         | East may gain from existing E_Tech overload |
| **Routing Rules**                       | Higher specialist utilization (15% increase)         | Resilience to absence drops (fewer local buffers)    | Fairness issues if teams lose regional knowledge |

**Key linkages**:
- **Handover reduction**  **Queue formation**  **Specialist utilization**  **Rework risk** (if expertise loss offsets gains).
- **Centralization**  **Role ambiguity**  **Resilience decline**  **Fairness gaps** (e.g., East vs. North/South).
- **Automation + Shared Service**  **Cycle-time compression** (but tail delays may worsen).

## **2. Scenario forecasts**
### **Optimistic Scenario**
- **Handover pattern**: Local-specialist loops eliminated; cross-team transfers nearly nonexistent (3/100). Legal/Risk cases routed centrally but with pre-screening to reduce 10.5h waits to 6h via queue prioritization.
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern**: Median wait after handover improves to 2.0h (centralized queues optimized). High-complexity cases handled in parallel via shared-service queues, reducing tail delays.
- **Cycle-time distribution**: 90th percentile drops from 22.8d to 12.0d (East benefits most; North/South converge). Median cycle time improves to 5.5d.
- **Rework and quality pattern**: Rework rate drops to 6% (centralized review reduces errors). SLA miss rate falls to 4% (standardized approvals).
- **Role clarity and accountability**: Shared roles create ambiguity initially, but clear documentation and training resolve this within 3 months. Local teams retain regional expertise for low-complexity cases.
- **Fairness implications**: East’s E_Tech specialization is preserved; North/South teams gain consistency. Customer segments with regional needs (e.g., language barriers) face no new delays.
- **Early warning signs**: Specialist utilization hits 95% within 2 months; local caseworker satisfaction rises to 4.5/5.

### **Central Scenario**
- **Handover pattern**: Mixed effects—local-specialist loops reduced (41/100), but cross-team handovers persist (3/100). Legal/Risk cases routed but with 10.5h waits for 12% of cases.
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern**: Median wait after handover stabilizes at 3.0h. High-complexity cases experience 15h waits in 10% of cases (tail latency).
- **Cycle-time distribution**: Median cycle time improves to 6.0d; 90th percentile remains at 18.0d (no improvement for East). Rework rate stays at 8%.
- **Rework and quality pattern**: Rework rate increases to 9% due to centralization bottlenecks. SLA miss rate rises to 7% (shared approvals lack flexibility).
- **Role clarity and accountability**: Role ambiguity persists for 6 months; local teams feel demoted. Legal/Risk specialists report burnout.
- **Fairness implications**: East benefits from E_Tech’s overcapacity, but North/South teams lose local expertise. Customer segments with regional needs face longer waits.
- **Early warning signs**: Specialist utilization plateaus at 90%; local caseworker turnover rises. Rework spikes in high-complexity cases.

### **Adverse Scenario**
- **Handover pattern**: Local-specialist loops re-emerge (50/100) as central queues become a bottleneck. Cross-team handovers increase to 5/100 due to inconsistent case notes.
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern**: Median wait after handover spikes to 4.5h. High-complexity cases experience 20h waits in 20% of cases. Central queues collapse during peak hours.
- **Cycle-time distribution**: Median cycle time worsens to 7.5d; 90th percentile rises to 25.0d. East’s cycle time degrades further.
- **Rework and quality pattern**: Rework rate jumps to 12%; SLA miss rate climbs to 10%. Quality Check rework doubles.
- **Role clarity and accountability**: Role ambiguity leads to confusion; local teams blame shared-service delays. Accountability is unclear.
- **Fairness implications**: East’s E_Tech is overwhelmed; North/South teams lose local expertise. Customer segments with regional needs face cascading delays.
- **Early warning signs**: Specialist utilization drops to 85%; local caseworker satisfaction falls to 3.5/5. Rework spikes in high-complexity cases.

## **4. Risks invisible in average cycle time**
Six critical risks masked by stable averages:

| **Risk**                          | **How It’s Hidden**                                      | **PM4Py Detection Method**                              |
|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| **Tail latency**                  | Median/mean cycle time may hide 10%+ of cases with 20h+ waits. | `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` (percentile analysis). |
| **High-complexity case skew**     | Average rework rate (8–12%) may mask 20% of cases with 30%+ rework. | `get_rework_cases_per_activity` (segmented by `case_complexity`). |
| **Local expertise erosion**       | `local_expertise_flag` may drop without tracking skill development. | `discover_organizational_roles` (compare pre/post role usage). |
| **Fairness gaps**                 | Team-specific wait times may vary without segmentation.   | Filter event log by `team` and `customer_segment`; compare `rework_flag`. |
| **Accountability collapse**       | Shared roles may reduce blame assignment, increasing errors. | Analyze case notes for ownership tags (`org:resource`). |
| **Exception handling failure**    | Standardized queues may fail for rare, ad-hoc cases.      | `discover_subcontracting_network` (identify ad-hoc hops). |

## **5. Pilot design**
### **Pilot Scope**
- **Phase 1 (2 months)**: Centralize **Technical Shared Service** and **shared approval queue** for 30% of cases (high-complexity only).
- **Phase 2 (4 months)**: Expand to **Legal/Risk Review** for 20% of cases.
- **Comparison Group**: Keep 30% of cases in local teams (control group) for benchmarking.

### **Pre/Post Observation**
- **Pre-pilot (1 month)**: Baseline PM4Py analysis (handover network, variants, rework).
- **Post-pilot (3 months)**: Compare:
  - Handover frequency/median wait by team.
  - Specialist utilization vs. local caseworker load.
  - Rework rate by case complexity and customer segment.

### **Segmentation**
- **Teams**: North, South, East (East as high-risk group).
- **Complexity**: Low (Intake/Eligibility), Medium (Technical Review), High (Quality Check/Risk).
- **Customer Segment**: Regional vs. national.

### **Guardrail Metrics**
- **Handover**: Median wait after handover  3h; rework rate  8%.
- **Specialist Utilization**:  90% for shared services.
- **Fairness**: Team-specific wait times  20% variance.
- **Resilience**: Cross-team handover frequency  2/100 cases.

### **Staff Feedback**
- **Weekly**: Short surveys on role clarity and queue fairness.
- **Monthly**: Focus groups on local vs. shared-service experiences.

### **Rollback/Adjustment Triggers**
- If **specialist utilization drops below 85%** or **rework rate spikes above 10%** in Phase 1.
- If **East’s cycle time worsens** or **North/South teams report skill loss**.

---

## **6. Recommendation**
**Proceed with a phased pilot**, conditional on:
1. **Monitoring leading indicators** (queue depth, role conflicts, local expertise flag changes) within 2 weeks.
2. **Avoiding premature full rollout** if:
   - Specialist utilization drops below 90% in Phase 1.
   - Rework rate exceeds 8% in high-complexity cases.
   - Customer satisfaction drops below 4.0/5 in any segment.

**Management must not infer success from:**
- Average cycle-time improvements alone (tail latency and fairness matter).
- Handover frequency reductions without tracking **wait times by team/segment**.
- Specialist utilization gains without verifying **local expertise retention**.

**Conditional on pilot success**, full rollout should include:
- **Automated alerts** for queue bottlenecks.
- **Local team training** to maintain regional knowledge.
- **Fairness audits** by customer segment.
