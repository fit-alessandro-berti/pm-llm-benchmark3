# 1. Executive Memo

**TO:** Executive Leadership Team  
**FROM:** Director of Operational Excellence & Process Analytics  
**DATE:** October 24, 2023  
**SUBJECT:** Strategic Recommendation for Redesign R Deployment in Fiber Installation  

### Strategic Context & Key Decision
Pilot Redesign R demonstrated a classic operational trade-off: it optimized velocity for straightforward orders while severely degrading execution quality, process compliance, and long-tail customer experience for complex orders. 

Management must decide whether to scale, roll back, or refine Redesign R. **We recommend adopting Alternative E (Hybrid Targeted Deployment)**: immediately scale Redesign R exclusively for the low-risk "Simple House" segment, while placing hard process gates and reinstating field controls for high-risk buildings, supported by targeted measurement (M6, M4, M7).

```
                      +-----------------------------------+
                      |   Fiber Order Process Mining      |
                      +-----------------------------------+
                                        |
                 +----------------------+----------------------+
                 |                                             |
   [Simple Low-Risk Segment]                      [Complex / Apartment Segment]
   - Cycle time: -6.0 days                        - Tail time: +12 to +25 days
   - First-Time-Right: +3pp                       - First-Time-Right: -8 to -11pp
   - Complaints: -1pp                             - Complaints: +5 to +7pp
                 |                                             |
   ACTION: Scale Redesign R                       ACTION: Pause Skip-Rules & Re-gate
```

### Core Metric Trade-offs & Findings
1. **Speed vs. Reliability:** Redesign R improved median cycle time by 3.4 days (14.2 to 10.8 days) and cut multi-handover cases from 34% to 12%. However, tail cycle time blew out catastrophically: p90 rose from 31.0 to 38.5 days, and p95 escalated from 45.0 to 62.0 days.
2. **Upfront Savings vs. Hidden Rework Costs:** Visible operational costs decreased from EUR 184 to EUR 161 per case. However, rework surged: First-Time-Right (FTR) installations dropped from 82% to 76%, second visits jumped from 11% to 18%, manual activations doubled (7% to 15%), and 30-day complaints rose from 6.1% to 9.8%. Total estimated unit cost already exceeds baseline (EUR 207 vs. EUR 198), even with incomplete post-week 4 complaint cost logging.
3. **Extreme Segment Variance:** 
   * **Simple Houses (34% of volume):** Highly successful. Cycle time dropped by 6.0 days, FTR increased by +3 percentage points (pp), and complaints fell by -1pp.
   * **Apartments & Old Buildings (47% of volume):** Severe failure. p90 cycle time worsened by +12.0 to +25.0 days, FTR plummeted by up to -11pp, and complaints surged by +5 to +7pp due to flawed predictive survey skipping and missing permit checks.

### Process Mining Diagnostics
Process discovery reveals that pilot fitness rose to 0.94 while precision plummeted from 0.79 to 0.51. The process mining alignment log confirms that the system permitted unconstrained operational execution: billing started prior to successful activation tests, and permit checks were frequently skipped for older apartment buildings. Furthermore, centralizing ownership reduced handovers but created severe resource bottlenecks: coordinator utilization hit 93% and second-visit field team utilization hit 92%, while survey teams sat underutilized at 52%.

### Operational Risk & Mitigation Strategic Alignment
Scaling Redesign R nationally without modification will trigger widespread customer dissatisfaction, escalate field rework costs, and introduce regulatory compliance exposure regarding building permits. A total rollback, however, forfeits EUR ~23 per simple case in direct efficiencies and delays cycle times unnecessarily for over one-third of our customer base. 

The hybrid strategy captures EUR ~1.5M in annual savings from low-risk cases while eliminating the operational bottlenecks and rework loops driving the tail degradation in complex structures.

# 3. Explanation of Metric Conflicts

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                            METRIC CONFLICT DYNAMICS                               |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. High Fitness / Low Precision   -->  Log follows flexible paths; model permits      |
|                                        invalid/unconstrained execution loops.     |
| 2. Faster Median / Worse Tail     -->  Simple cases accelerate; complex cases stall  |
|                                        in overloaded downstream queues.          |
| 3. Fewer Handovers / Low Resilience->  Single owner speeds happy path; context  |
|                                        is lost when coordinator is absent.       |
| 4. Lower Direct / Higher Total Cost->  Upfront survey savings are wiped out by   |
|                                        expensive second visits and support rework.|
| 5. House Win / Apartment Loss     -->  Standard topology succeeds without survey; |
|                                        complex topology fails without site check.|
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. High Fitness (0.94) vs. Low Precision (0.51)
* **Mechanic:** Fitness measures how much of the observed log can be replayed through the model; precision measures how tightly the model constrains unobserved behavior. 
* **Explanation:** The pilot model achieved high fitness because the discovered model incorporated new, relaxed pathways (such as skipping field surveys and delaying activation checks). However, precision dropped sharply to 0.51 because the discovered graph permits an overly broad set of execution combinations (e.g., `Installation Visit` $\rightarrow$ `Manual Activation` $\rightarrow$ `Billing Start` $\rightarrow$ `Complaint Escalation`). The workflow engine lost its strict control structures, allowing invalid execution paths to occur freely.

### 2. Improved Median (10.8d) vs. Worse Tail Duration (p95: 62.0d)
* **Mechanic:** Median reflects the central tendency (dominated by high-volume simple orders), whereas p90/p95 capture process exceptions and queuing dynamics.
* **Explanation:** Merging address validation with scheduling and skipping field surveys created an expedited highway for the 34% "Simple House" cohort, reducing their median cycle time by 6 days. However, when complex orders (apartments, permit-sensitive structures) bypassed upfront field surveys, they encountered site execution failures. These failed orders entered rework loops (`Failed Visit` $\rightarrow$ `Second Visit`, manual activations), routing into second-visit teams working at 92% utilization and coordinators working at 93% utilization. Queuing theory dictates that at $>90\%$ resource utilization, cycle times grow non-linearly, blowing out the p90/p95 tail.

### 3. Fewer Handovers (34% $\rightarrow$ 12%) vs. Lower Organizational Resilience
* **Mechanic:** Structural handover counts measure handoffs across formal team boundaries; operational resilience measures process throughput stability under resource variance.
* **Explanation:** Assigning a single installation coordinator eliminated inter-departmental ticket transfers, improving transit efficiency for standard cases. However, implicit knowledge became siloed within individual coordinators. Because working-together networks centralized around single coordinators without cross-coverage mechanisms, any coordinator absence or overload caused their assigned cases to stall completely, as no other resource possessed the contextual history to advance the order.

### 4. Lower Visible Direct Cost (EUR 161) vs. Higher Downstream Rework Cost (EUR 207+)
* **Mechanic:** Direct process costs track upstream execution steps; total cost includes downstream failure demand, support calls, and repeat field visits.
* **Explanation:** Skipping field surveys (dropping from 31% to 18% DFG share) directly reduced upfront labor costs by EUR 23 per order. However, this generated failure demand: FTR dropped by 6 percentage points, second visits increased from 11% to 18%, manual activations rose from 7% to 15%, and 30-day complaints jumped from 6.1% to 9.8%. The cost of a second field visit and manual provisioning far exceeds the cost of a preventive field survey. Furthermore, because pilot cost logging omitted downstream complaint handling after week 4, the true total cost of Redesign R is significantly higher than the reported EUR 207.

### 5. Simple House Improvements vs. Apartment / Old Building Deterioration
* **Mechanic:** Process standardization effectiveness depends on underlying structural asset homogeneity.
* **Explanation:** "Simple houses" feature standard drop-cable connections and clear rights-of-way. For this segment, predictive rules are accurate, and skipping physical surveys incurs minimal risk. Conversely, apartments and older buildings require complex MDU (Multi-Dwelling Unit) internal cabling, shared conduit access, and municipal permit clearances. Eliminating upfront site surveys for these segments resulted in technicians arriving without required permits or proper equipment, leading to aborted installs, manual activation workarounds, and billing disputes.

# 4. Decision Alternatives Evaluation

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                        STRATEGIC ALTERNATIVES MATRIX                              |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Option A: National Scale R        | HIGH RISK  | Uncontrolled tail delays & rework.  |
| Option B: Full Rollback           | LOW VALUE  | Forfeits EUR ~1.5M simple savings.  |
| Option C: Partial Scale (No Skip) | MED VALUE  | Preserves control, misses speed.    |
| Option D: Controlled Experiment   | MED VALUE  | Delays value during measurement.   |
| Option E: Segmented Hybrid        | RECOMMENDED| Max speed for simple; control for   |
|                                   |            | complex structures.               |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Alternative A: Scale Redesign R Nationally
* **Advantages:** Maximizes initial process velocity for simple cases; enforces single-point customer accountability; achieves immediate upfront operational labor savings in survey teams.
* **Risks:** Severe operational instability; tail durations (p95) will exceed 60 days nationally; second-visit field teams will cap out, causing massive backlog queues; high customer churn from premature billing disputes; potential legal non-compliance from missing municipal permits.
* **Conditions under which reasonable:** Reasonable only if the predictive rule for survey skipping had $>98\%$ precision across all building types, and second-visit capacity was unconstrained. (Currently invalid).

### Alternative B: Full Rollback to Baseline Process
* **Advantages:** Restores process precision (0.79) and structural quality control; reduces second visits back to 11% and complaints to 6.1%; eliminates permit compliance risk.
* **Risks:** Forfeits all efficiency gains; increases median cycle time back to 14.2 days; reinstates high handover rates (34% with 3+ handovers); degrades employee morale for coordinators.
* **Conditions under which reasonable:** Reasonable only if Redesign R showed uniform degradation across all customer segments and segment-specific routing was technically impossible. (Currently invalid).

### Alternative C: Scale Coordinator Ownership, Suspend Survey Skipping & Post-Billing Activation
* **Advantages:** Retains single-contact accountability; eliminates premature billing disputes and main drivers of low First-Time-Right scores.
* **Risks:** Maintains high field survey costs (EUR 184) for simple houses that do not require them; does not address coordinator capacity bottlenecks (93% utilization) or single-point-of-failure risks.
* **Conditions under which reasonable:** Reasonable if business rules cannot dynamically distinguish between low-risk simple houses and high-risk apartments during order entry.

### Alternative D: Run Controlled Regional Experimentation with Enhanced Measurement
* **Advantages:** Generates statistically rigorous, un-biased data; isolates regional demographic biases; collects complete 60-day downstream cost data.
* **Risks:** Delays value capture for 3-6 months; maintains customer frustration in active pilot regions; operational complexity of managing experimental cohorts.
* **Conditions under which reasonable:** Reasonable if management refuses to make capital allocation decisions without complete downstream cost quantification and randomized trials.

### Alternative E: Hybrid Targeted Deployment (RECOMMENDED)
* **Advantages:** Optimizes process performance based on order risk:
  * Scales Redesign R for "Simple house, low risk" (34% of volume), retaining -6.0 day speedup and FTR gains (+3pp).
  * Reinstates mandatory field surveys and permit checks for apartments, old buildings, and business customers.
  * Restores strict process gating: `Activation Test` must pass before `Billing Start`.
* **Risks:** Requires maintaining dual operational pathways in IT dispatch systems; requires managing coordinator capacity boundaries.
* **Conditions under which reasonable:** Optimal under current conditions where segment data clearly demonstrates divergent outcomes between simple and complex installation environments.

### Detailed Ranking & Decision Impact

#### Rank 1: M6 – Validate building-risk predictive rule against field-survey outcomes
* **Uncertainty Resolved:** Determines the precise false-negative rate of the predictive rule (i.e., how often the rule skips a survey when a physical site issue actually exists).
* **Decision Impact:** Directly dictates segment boundaries for Alternative E. If false-negative rates for medium-risk apartments exceed 5%, survey skipping must remain strictly restricted to low-risk single houses.

#### Rank 2: M4 – Measure full downstream cost for 60 days after Billing start
* **Uncertainty Resolved:** Quantifies true Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) by capturing long-tail support calls, truck rolls, and billing dispute write-offs missing after week 4.
* **Decision Impact:** Resolves whether the net financial impact of Redesign R is ROI positive. If 60-day downstream cost exceeds EUR 215/order, post-billing activation must be permanently banned across all segments.

#### Rank 3: M7 – Implement automated conformance monitoring for process guardrails
* **Uncertainty Resolved:** Identifies real-time non-conformance frequency (`Billing start` before `Activation test`, missing municipal permit logs).
* **Decision Impact:** Provides immediate operational control. Triggers hard IT system blocks that prevent premature billing, eliminating customer dispute complaints.

#### Rank 4: M3 – Add structured reason codes for Failed Visit, Manual Activation, and Complaints
* **Uncertainty Resolved:** Differentiates root causes of rework (e.g., customer absent vs. missing drop cable vs. lack of conduit access vs. missing permit).
* **Decision Impact:** Determines whether field failures require engineering intervention, customer communication fixes, or coordinator training.

#### Rank 5: M2 – Instrument start timestamps for Installation visit, Activation test, and Manual activation
* **Uncertainty Resolved:** Separates active task duration from queue waiting time in event logs.
* **Decision Impact:** Reveals whether the p95 tail duration (62 days) is caused by field execution delays or cases sitting idle in coordinator queues, guiding staffing allocation.

#### Rank 6: M5 – Test coordinator team-based ownership vs. individual ownership during absences
* **Uncertainty Resolved:** Quantifies productivity drop and case stalling caused by single-coordinator absence.
* **Decision Impact:** Informs whether to transition from pure single-coordinator ownership to paired "pod" structures (2-3 coordinators sharing context) to maintain resilience.

#### Rank 7: M1 – Conduct randomized pilot of survey skipping by building-risk stratum
* **Uncertainty Resolved:** Isolates the causal effect of survey skipping from regional building demographic bias.
* **Decision Impact:** Validates long-term expansion of skip rules into medium-risk segments once M6 rule calibration is complete.

#### Rank 8: M8 – Measure customer experience separately for "speed to appointment" vs. "working service"
* **Uncertainty Resolved:** Separates customer satisfaction gained from fast scheduling from dissatisfaction caused by technical day-one failures.
* **Decision Impact:** Refines marketing and customer notification timing but does not alter core process engineering decisions.

# 6. Final Recommendation

### Core Strategic Recommendation
Management should execute **Alternative E (Hybrid Targeted Deployment)**, supported immediately by Measurement Priorities **M6, M4, M7, and M3**.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                           TARGET STATE ARCHITECTURE                               |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Order Entry -> Dynamic Risk Stratification                                        |
|                                                                                   |
|  [Low Risk: Simple House] --------> Combined Schedule/Validate -> Install         |
|                                      -> Mandatory Activation Test -> Billing      |
|                                                                                   |
|  [High Risk: Apt / Old Building] -> Mandatory Permit Check & Field Survey          |
|                                      -> Scheduled Install -> Activation Test      |
|                                      -> Billing Start                             |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. Immediate Action (Next 0–30 Days)
* **Re-establish Process Guardrails:** Implement hard IT system blocks (M7) prohibiting `Billing Start` until a successful `Activation Test` timestamp is logged.
* **Re-instate Field Surveys & Permits:** Mandate physical field surveys and permit verification for all "Apartment, medium risk", "Old building, permit-sensitive", and "Business customer" orders.
* **Establish Paired Coordinator Pods:** Reorganize installation coordinators into 3-person pod teams with shared case visibility to remove single-point-of-failure risks (M5).

### 2. What to Pause
* **Pause Predictive Survey Skipping:** Immediately suspend survey-skipping algorithms for all non-single-house property types.
* **Pause Post-Billing Activation:** Ban early billing starts based solely on estimated network signal appearance.
* **Pause Uncontrolled Coordinator Case Allocation:** Cap individual coordinator active queue loading at 80% to eliminate tail-duration queue growth.

### 3. What to Scale
* **Scale Redesign R for Simple Houses:** Fully roll out merged address validation/scheduling and single-coordinator ownership for the "Simple house, low building risk" segment (34% of national volume).
* **Scale Merged Validation & Scheduling:** Expand combined task scheduling IT interface across all regions for standard drop connections.

### 4. What to Measure Next (Execution Roadmap)
* **Deploy Measurement M6 & M3 (Days 1–45):** Log structured failure codes for all second visits and validate predictive rule error rates against actual survey findings.
* **Deploy Measurement M4 (Days 1–60):** Track full 60-day post-billing downstream cost tracking across all pilot regions to establish true TCO.

### 5. Thresholds & Evidence Justifying Broader Rollout
Broader expansion of Redesign R features to medium-risk building segments will be authorized **only** when the following quantitative gates are satisfied:
