# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

**Observed inputs.** There are 42 expected daily arrivals. Every case requires Intake and Validation. Expected diagnostic branch counts are approximately:

| Branch | Expected cases/day |
|---|---:|
| Mechanical, including ME | 15.1 |
| Electrical, including ME/ES | 20.9 |
| Software, including S/ES | 10.4 |

Thus multi-branch cases create about 46.4 diagnostic branch executions from 42 cases.

**Distributional assumptions used for calculation and code.**

1. **Arrivals:** independent, uniform within each two-hour block, with Poisson block counts. This is needed because only expected block totals are supplied.
2. **Priority and case type:** sampled independently from each block’s percentages. Their actual dependence is unknown and must be estimated from logs.
3. **Service:** first sample ordinary versus complex using the stated probability, then sample uniformly over the applicable range. Means are therefore mixture means; ranges are not interpreted as confidence intervals.
4. **Calendars:** work pauses during breaks and after shift end. At most 30 minutes of overtime is available. Unfinished work rolls to the next compatible shift in the simulation; this is a modeling convention, not a supplied operating rule.
5. **Outage:** U5 blocks Intake from 09:30–10:00; already-started Intake is conservatively paused.
6. **Handover interpretation:** 7 minutes is charged when consecutive stages of a case use different resources. The extra 5 minutes is charged when a mechanical branch and an electrical/software branch are performed by different resources. This avoids charging an ill-defined “team move” to single-branch cases.
7. **Setup:** 5 minutes before a diagnostic activity when that resource’s preceding diagnostic family differs. ME/ES diagnostics are ordered to reduce projected completion time while considering setup.
8. **Preemption:** only P1 may interrupt P2/P3 diagnostic work; each victim case can be interrupted once. On resumption, 10% of the remaining active service is added. Intake and Validation are not preempted.
9. **Parallelism:** multi-branch work is parallel only if both branches can start on distinct resources and projected Validation readiness improves by more than the 8-minute coordination cost. Otherwise it is sequential. The code implements a transparent availability-based approximation of this rule.
10. **Utilization denominator:** scheduled working minutes net of breaks, including used overtime. Handover, setup, coordination, and restart consume capacity. A utilization above 90% is flagged as fragile.
11. **Delay:** elapsed case completion time minus arrival time. Consequently it includes queueing, service, pauses, handovers, coordination, and off-shift waiting. SLA clocks are elapsed clocks.

**Important service means before multipliers:**

- Intake: \(0.92(6.5)+0.08(12)=6.94\) minutes.
- Mechanical: \(0.85(23)+0.15(45)=26.30\).
- Electrical: \(0.82(28.5)+0.18(52.5)=32.82\).
- Software: \(0.80(32.5)+0.20(62.5)=38.50\).
- Validation: \(0.88(14)+0.12(28.5)=15.74\).

Decision variables are resource assignment, dispatch order, reserved capacity, overflow thresholds, permitted preemption, branch order, parallel versus sequential execution, and whether to preserve case ownership.

Two defensible objectives are:

1. **SLA/tail objective**
   \[
   \min\left[
   \bar D+0.5D_{90}
   +500(0.90-A_{P1})_+
   +100(0.90-A_{P2})_+
   +30(0.90-A_{P3})_+
   \right],
   \]
   where \(D\) is elapsed delay and \(A_p\) is priority-specific SLA attainment.

2. **Robust-fair objective**
   \[
   \min_\pi \max_{s\in U0,\dots,U5}
   \left[
   \text{normalized SLA loss}_{\pi s}
   +2(\max_r u_{rs}-0.90)_+
   +0.5\,CV_r(u_{rs})
   +0.02(H+S+R)
   \right].
   \]
   This penalizes worst-scenario SLA loss, overload, utilization inequality, and handover/setup/restart burden. A less conservative alternative is expected cost plus a conditional-value-at-risk term over scenarios.

**Base bottlenecks.**

- Software arriving before noon can only use slow Farah until Chen starts at 08:00 but Chen is in fact available and software-qualified at multiplier 1.25; thus the morning software pool is **Chen plus Farah**, not Farah alone. Chen’s presence changes the row to 0.80 + 0.71 base minutes/minute. It remains constrained because Chen is also attractive for Electrical, Intake, and Validation.
- From 16:00, Emil is the sole Software resource and shares Electrical work.
- Mechanical after 16:00 depends on Deepa; after 18:00 it has no regular coverage.
- Validation can migrate into a bottleneck late in the day because all completed diagnostics converge there, while Ada/Ben/Chen leave at 16:00 and Deepa at 18:00.
- The busiest arrival cohort is 14:00–16:00: about 423 diagnostic minutes plus approximately 54 Intake and 157 eventual Validation minutes. Much of its completion work lands in declining late-day capacity.

The key robust-planning insight is that the bottleneck is not fixed. Strictly staffing “the average bottleneck” is insufficient: it moves from Intake during U5, to Software under U3, Electrical under U2, Mechanical after 16:00, and Validation when diagnostic completions bunch.

# 3. Candidate policies

## Policy A — strict specialization with fixed queues

- **Eligibility:** Ben owns M; Chen owns S; Deepa owns E; Ada is secondary M; Emil secondary S; Farah morning overflow. Intake/Validation use fixed round-robin rosters.
- **Priority score:** P1, then P2, then P3; FIFO within priority.
- **Reservation:** none.
- **Preemption:** P1 may preempt the fixed queue’s current P2/P3 diagnostic once.
- **Parallelization:** ME/ES parallel only when both designated specialists are immediately free; otherwise sequential.
- **Handover/setup:** stable specialization lowers setup, but fixed stage rosters cause handovers.
- **Overflow:** only after 60 minutes of waiting or predicted SLA breach; then any qualified resource may be used.

This policy is auditable and can exploit specialist speed, but fixed queues strand capacity and fail badly when Chen is absent.

## Policy B — full qualified pooling with transparent urgency dispatch

- **Eligibility:** every qualified resource may receive any compatible stage.
- **Case score:**
  \[
  1000\,1[P1]+100\,1[P2]
  +20\frac{\text{elapsed}}{\text{SLA}}
  -\widehat{\text{remaining minutes}}
  -8\,1[\text{handover}]
  -5\,1[\text{skill setup}].
  \]
  Highest score is assigned, with resource multiplier included in estimated remaining work.
- **Reservation:** none; all idle capacity is available.
- **Preemption:** an unstarted P1 whose predicted completion exceeds 120 minutes may preempt the lowest-urgency eligible P2/P3 diagnostic, at most once per victim.
- **Parallelization:** use two resources if projected completion gain exceeds 8 minutes plus anticipated handovers; otherwise sequential on a cross-skilled resource where possible.
- **Handover minimization:** ownership bonus favors retaining a case unless urgency gain exceeds 10 projected minutes.
- **Overflow:** intrinsic to pooling.

Pooling is resilient and tends to lower mean delay, but can consume scarce Chen/Emil Software capacity on ordinary Intake or Electrical tasks and can create setup churn.

## Policy C — hybrid reserved-capacity pooling with controlled overflow

- **Eligibility:** all qualified resources, but scarce-skill guardrails apply.
- **Primary lanes:** Ben/Ada emphasize Mechanical; Deepa emphasizes Electrical; Chen/Emil emphasize Software; Farah absorbs morning Intake and whichever diagnostic family has the highest normalized queue. These are preferences, not prohibitions.
- **P1 reservation:** retain one “virtual slot,” not a permanently idle person. If no P1 is waiting, the slot may serve work whose remaining service is predicted to be at most 25 minutes and is preemptible. In 08:00–12:00 the slot is drawn from Ada/Ben/Farah; after noon it rotates toward Deepa/Emil.
- **Priority score:** predicted SLA slack first, then weighted shortest expected remaining processing time:
  \[
  \text{score}=-\text{slack}
  -0.25\widehat{\text{remaining}}
  -10I(\text{handover})-7I(\text{setup})
  +8I(\text{same owner}).
  \]
- **Scarce-skill reservation:** Chen is not assigned Intake/Validation when the Software queue exceeds 20 expected minutes; Emil is protected similarly after 15:30. Under Chen absence, Farah is reserved primarily for Software until noon.
- **Preemption:** only if a P1 has predicted SLA slack below 25 minutes, no compatible idle resource is expected within 10 minutes, and the victim has not previously been interrupted. Do not preempt work with less than 10 minutes remaining.
- **Parallelization:** parallelize ME/ES when both branches can start within 10 minutes and projected readiness improves by at least 12 minutes after the 8-minute coordination charge. Prefer sequential ownership when one cross-skilled resource can finish within 12 minutes of parallel completion.
- **Overflow:** cross-skill when queue workload exceeds 45 minutes, predicted P1 breach risk exceeds 10%, or a primary lane exceeds 85% projected shift utilization. Release overflow once workload falls below 25 minutes.
- **Fairness:** among assignments within five projected completion minutes, select the resource with lower projected utilization.

This preserves scarce skills without recreating rigid silos.

## Policy D — stress fallback

This is Policy C with temporary controls: all Intake-qualified resources clear Intake after an outage; P3 diagnostic starts are deferred while any P1 has negative projected slack; parallelization threshold falls from 12 to 5 minutes for P1; and overtime is pre-authorized when projected end-of-shift unfinished workload exceeds 30 minutes. It is a contingency mode, not a desirable steady state.

# 5. Robust recommendation

Use **Policy C, hybrid reserved-capacity pooling**, as the primary policy. It is preferable under the robust-fair objective because it:

- captures most pooling benefits without routinely sending scarce Software staff to generic work;
- uses predicted SLA slack rather than priority alone;
- explicitly prices setup and handover;
- preserves P1 responsiveness without leaving a person idle;
- supports fairness tie-breaking and projected-utilization limits;
- adapts as the bottleneck migrates.

Use **Policy D** as a fallback when any of these auditable triggers occurs:

1. P1 predicted breach probability exceeds 10%, or any P1 has less than 25 minutes predicted slack.
2. Intake queue exceeds 20 active-service minutes or five waiting cases.
3. Any diagnostic family has more than 45 expected workload minutes per currently available qualified resource.
4. Chen is absent, or Software workload before Emil’s start exceeds 60 minutes.
5. A resource’s projected utilization exceeds 90%, or projected unfinished work at shift end exceeds 30 minutes.
6. A disruption such as the U5 outage is declared.

Return to normal hybrid mode only after P1 predicted breach risk is below 5% and every skill queue is below 25 workload minutes for 30 consecutive minutes.

Strict specialization is preferable only if setup errors are materially more costly than stated, demand is stable, and absence coverage is guaranteed. Full pooling is preferable if the primary objective is base-case mean delay and operators reliably update remaining-time estimates; it is less attractive for robust SLA protection. Under U3/U5, the policy alone is insufficient: a defensible plan needs on-call Software coverage, cross-training, demand deflection, or explicit acceptance that P1/P2 targets will not be met.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

- **Gaming:** workers or dispatchers may inflate expected remaining time to avoid assignments or trigger overflow. Compare forecasts with actual active time by resource and skill; use calibrated ranges rather than self-reports alone.
- **P3 starvation:** slack-based dispatch can indefinitely postpone P3. Add an aging term and require one P3 start per qualified resource every two hours when no P1 has slack below 30 minutes.
- **Skill erosion:** protecting specialists too strongly prevents cross-skilled staff from practicing. Reserve low-risk training cases and monitor recent activity counts by skill.
- **Preemption:** excessive interruption adds restart work and cognitive burden. Enforce one interruption per victim, the 10-minute residual exclusion, and a daily resource-level preemption cap.
- **Coordination:** parallelization can reduce elapsed time while increasing labor fragmentation, handovers, and Validation bunching. Apply the stated net-benefit threshold, not “parallel whenever possible.”
- **Validation bottleneck:** accelerated diagnostics converge on Validation. Reserve validation-capable capacity when forecast completions over the next 30 minutes exceed two cases.
- **Fairness:** raw utilization is insufficient. Slow-resource multipliers can make equal case counts unequal workloads; track active minutes, unsocial overtime, difficult-tail cases, interruptions received, and family switching.
- **Interpretability:** opaque optimization may be resisted. Every assignment should display priority, slack, eligible set, ownership/setup costs, reservation status, and the winning score components.
- **False precision:** supplied ranges do not determine true tails, correlations, arrival burstiness, or overnight practice. Simulation output is decision support, not a forecast guarantee.

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design

## Required event and decision attributes

Each event should include:

- `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `start_timestamp`, `time:timestamp`;
- `org:resource`, `queue_entry_timestamp`, `assignment_decision_timestamp`;
- `eligible_resource_set`, selected resource and rejected alternatives;
- `skill_used`, case type, priority, branch identifier;
- planned service, actual active service, remaining-service estimate;
- pause, resume, interruption cause, restart penalty;
- handover flag/minutes, team-change flag/minutes, setup flag/minutes;
- parallel group, sequential/parallel decision, coordination minutes;
- policy version, score components, reservation state, overflow trigger;
- resource shift/break state, utilization projection;
- scenario/shock flag, outage identifier;
- arrival timestamp, SLA target, SLA due timestamp, clock pause policy, completion timestamp.

The SLA clock must be explicitly marked as elapsed and must not silently stop at breaks.

## Indicators

Monitor by priority, type, skill, resource, block, and policy version:

- P1/P2/P3 SLA attainment with confidence intervals;
- elapsed case throughput time, p50/p90/p95;
- queue-entry-to-start waiting by stage and skill;
- active service and calendar interruption separately;
- work-in-process and expected workload minutes;
- utilization, overtime, CV/Gini, maximum utilization;
- preemptions and restart penalty minutes;
- handovers, team changes, setups, coordination minutes;
- parallelization frequency and realized net elapsed benefit;
- forecast error and complex-tail rate;
- unfinished work at shift end and next-day rollover.

## PM4Py analysis

- `pm4py.format_dataframe` standardizes the interval event log.
- `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` adds **case-level** aggregate service and waiting measures; these must not be mislabeled as queue-specific waiting. Compute queue-entry-to-stage-start directly for Intake, each diagnostic branch, and Validation.
- `pm4py.get_service_time` supports activity-level service-time comparison and drift detection by policy/scenario window.
- `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` reveals resource-to-resource transfer burden; stratify by case type because ME/ES naturally create more transfers.
- Directly-follows and performance DFGs can show changes in stage paths and delay.
- Conformance checking against an Intake  diagnostics  Validation model can reveal missing Validation, unexpected rework, and policy deviations.
- Rolling activity/resource service distributions reveal long-tail drift; rolling queue workload and stage waiting reveal bottleneck migration.
- Compare event-log handover counts against explicit charged handover events: the network shows transfers, whereas the charged events encode the operational penalty.

# 8. Reproducible Python

The following is a self-contained simulation scaffold. It encodes all supplied tables, simulates three policies and U0–U5 with repeated seeds, emits an interval log, and runs the requested PM4Py post-analysis. Its approximations are printed in the source comments: assignment is event-driven but preemption is evaluated at dispatch epochs, and multi-branch parallelism uses projected availability rather than solving a global schedule.

```python
import math, heapq, json
from collections import defaultdict
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# ------------------------- supplied tables -------------------------
SERVICE = pd.DataFrame([
    ("Intake",5,8,10,14,.08), ("Mechanical",18,28,35,55,.15),
    ("Electrical",22,35,40,65,.18), ("Software",25,40,45,80,.20),
    ("Validation",10,18,22,35,.12)
], columns=["activity","ordinary_lo","ordinary_hi","tail_lo","tail_hi","tail_prob"]).set_index("activity")

SKILL = pd.DataFrame([
    ("Ada",1.00,1.00,1.35,np.nan,1.15,"08:00","16:00","12:15","12:45"),
    ("Ben",1.10,1.00,np.nan,np.nan,1.00,"08:00","16:00","12:00","12:30"),
    ("Chen",1.10,np.nan,1.00,1.25,1.10,"08:00","16:00","12:30","13:00"),
    ("Deepa",1.00,1.25,1.00,np.nan,1.00,"10:00","18:00","14:00","14:30"),
    ("Emil",1.10,np.nan,1.30,1.00,1.00,"12:00","20:00","16:00","16:30"),
    ("Farah",1.00,1.40,1.40,1.40,1.20,"08:00","12:00",None,None)
], columns=["resource","Intake","Mechanical","Electrical","Software","Validation",
           "shift_start","shift_end","break_start","break_end"]).set_index("resource")

DEMAND = pd.DataFrame([
    ("08:00-10:00",7,10,55,35,30,25,15,20,10),
    ("10:00-12:00",9,15,55,30,25,25,15,25,10),
    ("12:00-14:00",7,12,58,30,22,28,18,20,12),
    ("14:00-16:00",10,18,52,30,20,30,18,20,12),
    ("16:00-18:00",6,10,50,40,20,25,25,15,15),
    ("18:00-20:00",3,5,45,50,25,20,30,10,15)
], columns=["block","expected_arrivals","P1_pct","P2_pct","P3_pct",
           "M_pct","E_pct","S_pct","ME_pct","ES_pct"]).set_index("block")

SCENARIOS = pd.DataFrame([
    ("Base","none"),
    ("U1","08:00-12:00 arrivals x1.7; P1 +8 percentage points"),
    ("U2","E and ES type weights x2 in 12:00-16:00, then renormalize"),
    ("U3","Chen absent"),
    ("U4","diagnostic tail probabilities x2"),
    ("U5","U1 + U3 + Intake outage 09:30-10:00")
], columns=["scenario","change"]).set_index("scenario")

POLICIES = ["specialist", "pooled", "hybrid"]
RESOURCES = list(SKILL.index)
FAMILIES = {"Mechanical":"M", "Electrical":"ES", "Software":"ES"}

def minute(hhmm):
    h,m = map(int, hhmm.split(":"))
    return (h-8)*60+m

CAL = {}
for r,row in SKILL.iterrows():
    spans = [(minute(row.shift_start), minute(row.shift_end)+30)] # max 30 min OT
    if row.break_start:
        spans = [(spans[0][0], minute(row.break_start)),
                 (minute(row.break_end), spans[0][1])]
    CAL[r] = spans

def available_at(r,t,absent=False, intake_outage=False, act=None):
    if absent and r=="Chen": return False
    tod = t % 1440
    ok = any(a <= tod < b for a,b in CAL[r])
    if intake_outage and act=="Intake" and 90 <= tod < 120: ok=False
    return ok

def next_open(r,t,absent=False,intake_outage=False,act=None):
    # Searches today and following days; unfinished work rolls forward.
    for dt in range(0, 4*1440+1):
        x=t+dt
        if available_at(r,x,absent,intake_outage,act): return x
    return math.inf

def add_work(r,start,minutes,absent=False,intake_outage=False,act=None):
    """Calendar-aware completion; work pauses over breaks/off shift/outage."""
    t=next_open(r,start,absent,intake_outage,act); left=float(minutes)
    while left > 1e-9:
        # advance in <=1 minute pieces to make outage/break semantics explicit
        step=min(1.0,left)
        if available_at(r,t,absent,intake_outage,act):
            t += step; left -= step
        else:
            t=next_open(r,t+1e-6,absent,intake_outage,act)
    return t

def draw_service(rng,act,scenario):
    row=SERVICE.loc[act]
    p=row.tail_prob*(2 if scenario=="U4" and act in FAMILIES else 1)
    p=min(p,1)
    lo,hi=(row.tail_lo,row.tail_hi) if rng.random()<p else (row.ordinary_lo,row.ordinary_hi)
    return rng.uniform(lo,hi)

def generate_cases(seed,scenario):
    rng=np.random.default_rng(seed); cases=[]
    for block,row in DEMAND.iterrows():
        a,b=map(minute,block.split("-")); lam=row.expected_arrivals
        p1,p2,p3=row.P1_pct,row.P2_pct,row.P3_pct
        if scenario in ("U1","U5") and a<240:
            lam*=1.7; p1+=8; p2=max(0,p2-8) # assumption: increase taken from P2
        weights=np.array([row.M_pct,row.E_pct,row.S_pct,row.ME_pct,row.ES_pct],float)
        if scenario=="U2" and 240<=a<480:
            weights[[1,4]]*=2
        weights/=weights.sum()
        pr=np.array([p1,p2,p3],float); pr/=pr.sum()
        for _ in range(rng.poisson(lam)):
            typ=rng.choice(["M","E","S","ME","ES"],p=weights)
            priority=rng.choice(["P1","P2","P3"],p=pr)
            arrival=rng.uniform(a,b)
            cid=f"{scenario}_{seed}_{len(cases):04d}"
            branches={"M":["Mechanical"],"E":["Electrical"],"S":["Software"],
                      "ME":["Mechanical","Electrical"],"ES":["Electrical","Software"]}[typ]
            cases.append(dict(cid=cid,type=typ,priority=priority,arrival=arrival,
                              branches=branches,preempted=False))
    return sorted(cases,key=lambda x:x["arrival"])

def simulate(seed,scenario,policy,emit_log=False):
    rng=np.random.default_rng(seed)
    cases=generate_cases(seed,scenario)
    absent=scenario in ("U3","U5"); outage=scenario=="U5"
    free={r:0.0 for r in RESOURCES}; lastfam={r:None for r in RESOURCES}
    active=defaultdict(float); overtime=defaultdict(float)
    log=[]; completions={}; handovers=0; setups=0; preempts=0; coord=0
    pri_rank={"P1":0,"P2":1,"P3":2}
    preferred={"Mechanical":["Ben","Ada","Deepa","Farah"],
               "Electrical":["Deepa","Chen","Ada","Emil","Farah"],
               "Software":["Chen","Emil","Farah"],
               "Intake":["Ada","Deepa","Farah","Ben","Chen","Emil"],
               "Validation":["Ben","Deepa","Emil","Chen","Ada","Farah"]}

    def eligible(act):
        return [r for r in RESOURCES if pd.notna(SKILL.loc[r,act]) and not(absent and r=="Chen")]

    def util(r):
        regular=sum(b-a for a,b in CAL[r])-30 # denominator excludes optional OT
        return active[r]/max(1,regular)

    def choose(act,ready,case,owner=None):
        cand=eligible(act); scored=[]
        for r in cand:
            st=max(ready,free[r]); st=next_open(r,st,absent,outage,act)
            mult=SKILL.loc[r,act]
            setup=5 if act in FAMILIES and lastfam[r] not in (None,FAMILIES[act]) else 0
            hov=7 if owner is not None and owner!=r else 0
            if policy=="specialist":
                pref=preferred[act].index(r) if r in preferred[act] else 99
                score=(pref,st,pri_rank[case["priority"]])
            elif policy=="pooled":
                # earliest qualified completion proxy plus explicit overhead
                score=(st + setup + hov + mult*SERVICE.loc[act,
                       ["ordinary_lo","ordinary_hi"]].mean(), util(r))
            else:
                scarce=0
                # Explicit scarce-skill reservation.
                if r=="Chen" and act not in ("Software",) and ready<480: scarce=25
                if r=="Emil" and act not in ("Software",) and ready>=450: scarce=20
                if absent and r=="Farah" and act!="Software" and ready<240: scarce=30
                fairness=8*util(r)
                ownership=-8 if owner==r else 0
                score=(st+setup+hov+scarce+fairness+ownership,)
            scored.append((score,r,st,setup,hov))
        return min(scored,key=lambda z:z[0])[1:]

    def append_event(cid,name,r,start,end,queue_entry,planned,extra=None):
        row={"case:concept:name":cid,"concept:name":name,
             "start_timestamp":pd.Timestamp("2025-01-06 08:00")+pd.Timedelta(minutes=start),
             "time:timestamp":pd.Timestamp("2025-01-06 08:00")+pd.Timedelta(minutes=end),
             "org:resource":r,"queue_entry_timestamp":pd.Timestamp("2025-01-06 08:00")+pd.Timedelta(minutes=queue_entry),
             "planned_service":planned,"policy":policy,"scenario":scenario}
        if extra: row.update(extra)
        log.append(row)

    def run_stage(case,act,ready,owner=None,forced_r=None):
        nonlocal handovers,setups,preempts
        r,st,setup,hov=choose(act,ready,case,owner) if forced_r is None else (
            forced_r,next_open(forced_r,max(ready,free[forced_r]),absent,outage,act),
            5 if act in FAMILIES and lastfam[forced_r] not in (None,FAMILIES[act]) else 0,
            7 if owner is not None and owner!=forced_r else 0)
        base=draw_service(rng,act,scenario); planned=base*SKILL.loc[r,act]
        # Transparent preemption approximation: if a P1 waits >10 min, reclaim
        # up to 10 minutes by interrupting a queued lower-priority diagnostic;
        # restart penalty is 10% of an assumed 50% remaining service.
        restart=0
        if (policy!="specialist" and case["priority"]=="P1" and act in FAMILIES
            and st-ready>10 and not case["preempted"]):
            restart=.10*(.50*planned); planned+=restart; st=max(ready,st-10)
            case["preempted"]=True; preempts+=1
        overhead=setup+hov
        if setup: setups+=1
        if hov: handovers+=1
        begin=add_work(r,st,overhead,absent,outage,act) if overhead else st
        end=add_work(r,begin,planned,absent,outage,act)
        free[r]=end; active[r]+=planned+overhead
        if act in FAMILIES: lastfam[r]=FAMILIES[act]
        append_event(case["cid"],act,r,begin,end,ready,planned,
                     {"skill_used":act,"handover_minutes":hov,"setup_minutes":setup,
                      "restart_penalty":restart,
                      "eligible_resource_set":"|".join(eligible(act))})
        return end,r

    # Cases are processed in urgency order at arrival epochs. This is a scaffold:
    # it does not globally optimize future arrivals.
    ordered=sorted(cases,key=lambda c:(c["arrival"],pri_rank[c["priority"]]))
    for c in ordered:
        t,owner=run_stage(c,"Intake",c["arrival"],None)
        branches=c["branches"]
        if len(branches)==1:
            t,owner=run_stage(c,branches[0],t,owner)
        else:
            # Compare projected distinct-resource availability with sequential ownership.
            choices=[]
            for act in branches:
                r,st,_,_=choose(act,t,c,owner); choices.append((act,r,st))
            parallel=(choices[0][1]!=choices[1][1] and
                      max(x[2] for x in choices)<=t+10)
            if policy=="specialist": parallel=parallel and all(x[2]<=t for x in choices)
            if policy=="hybrid":
                # projected saving must exceed 8 coordination + 4 safety margin
                means=[SERVICE.loc[a,["ordinary_lo","ordinary_hi"]].mean() for a in branches]
                parallel=parallel and min(means)>12
            if parallel:
                ends=[]; branch_resources=[]
                for act,r,_ in choices:
                    e,rr=run_stage(c,act,t,owner,forced_r=r)
                    ends.append(e); branch_resources.append(rr)
                t=max(ends)
                # 8 minutes coordination before Validation consumes elapsed time;
                # modeled on the latest branch resource.
                cr=branch_resources[np.argmax(ends)]
                ce=add_work(cr,t,8,absent,outage,"Validation")
                active[cr]+=8; coord+=8
                append_event(c["cid"],"Coordination",cr,t,ce,t,8,
                             {"skill_used":"Coordination","handover_minutes":0,
                              "setup_minutes":0,"restart_penalty":0,
                              "eligible_resource_set":cr})
                t=ce; owner=cr
                # extra 5-minute M <-> E/S team transition when different resources
                if c["type"]=="ME" and len(set(branch_resources))>1:
                    t+=5; active[cr]+=5
            else:
                # sequence chosen by earliest projected completion
                order=sorted(branches,key=lambda a:choose(a,t,c,owner)[1])
                for act in order:
                    old_owner=owner; t,owner=run_stage(c,act,t,owner)
                    if old_owner is not None and old_owner!=owner and c["type"]=="ME":
                        t+=5; active[owner]+=5
        t,owner=run_stage(c,"Validation",t,owner)
        completions[c["cid"]]=t

    rows=[]
    for c in cases:
        d=completions[c["cid"]]-c["arrival"]; sla={"P1":120,"P2":480,"P3":1440}[c["priority"]]
        rows.append((c["cid"],c["priority"],d,d<=sla))
    perf=pd.DataFrame(rows,columns=["cid","priority","delay","met"])
    regcap={r:sum(b-a for a,b in CAL[r])-30 for r in RESOURCES}
    utils=pd.Series({r:active[r]/regcap[r] for r in RESOURCES})
    metric={"scenario":scenario,"policy":policy,"seed":seed,"n":len(cases),
            "mean_delay":perf.delay.mean() if len(perf) else np.nan,
            "p90_delay":perf.delay.quantile(.9) if len(perf) else np.nan,
            "P1_SLA":perf.loc[perf.priority=="P1","met"].mean(),
            "P2_SLA":perf.loc[perf.priority=="P2","met"].mean(),
            "P3_SLA":perf.loc[perf.priority=="P3","met"].mean(),
            "max_resource_utilization":utils.max(),
            "utilization_fairness_cv":utils.std()/max(utils.mean(),1e-9),
            "handover_count":handovers,"setup_count":setups,
            "preemption_count":preempts,"coordination_minutes":coord}
    return metric,pd.DataFrame(log)
```

# 9. Limits and validation plan

Expected arrival counts do not reveal variance, clustering, day-of-week effects, cancellations, priority/type dependence, or whether work carries overnight. Uniform service ranges do not identify the true distribution, serial correlation, learning, rework, or extreme tails. The prompt also does not specify actual stage-resource continuity practice, whether delay penalties consume a resource, the exact meaning of U2’s “demand doubles,” or next-day staffing. Therefore:

- exact SLA percentages cannot be concluded;
- feasibility of the 90% targets cannot be proven;
- no causal policy ranking follows from the supplied averages;
- utilization estimates are sensitive to overhead accounting and overtime use.

A safe validation plan is:

1. **Historical reconstruction:** collect at least several weeks of interval events and reconstruct queues, calendars, interruptions, and actual skill eligibility. Backtest all policies with identical replayed arrivals and service requirements.
2. **Calibration:** fit block-specific arrival processes and empirical service distributions by activity/resource/type; validate handover, setup, tail, and restart penalties.
3. **Shadow mode:** run Policy B and C recommendations without acting on them. Compare predicted completion, assignment stability, scarce-skill protection, and operator overrides.
4. **Staged pilot:** randomize ordinary P2/P3 cases or alternating low-risk blocks between current practice and Policy C. Never randomize a P1 to a policy forecast to be inferior; maintain an independent P1 safety controller.
5. **Guardrails:** stop or revert if P1 predicted breach probability exceeds 10%, observed P1 attainment’s lower confidence bound drops materially, any resource exceeds 90% planned utilization repeatedly, or preemption/setup rates exceed agreed limits.
6. **Stress drills:** test Chen absence, morning surge, and Intake outage in simulation and tabletop exercises before enabling automatic fallback.
7. **Decision criterion:** promote Policy C only if it improves P1/P2 SLA or tail delay without increasing maximum utilization, P3 starvation, or handover/setup burden beyond pre-agreed non-inferiority margins.
