## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

### 1.1 What I treat as fact

The following are taken as given because the case file states them directly: the text and status of sources A–D; the effective windows (GPIP-4 from 2026-01-01; SOP Rev. 6 from 2025-03-01; ECD-2 from 07-14 09:00 to 07-18 18:00); the CFO email timestamp of 07-15 07:20; the ERP outage ON-44 (07-14 08:30–13:00, recorded timestamps up to 12 minutes *later* than the real act); the recorded case events; and the organizational facts, including that the Chief Risk Officer (CRO) was unavailable and never ratified ECD-2, and that the Incident Command (IC) charter does not designate IC members as budget owners.

### 1.2 Decision method (assumptions, stated as such)

**M1 — Authority before wording.** A source can only displace a control that its issuer had authority to displace. GPIP-4 A7 reserves formal exceptions to the CRO. The COO can issue operational directives (organizational fact) but the same fact set says GPIP-4 "reserves formal exceptions to the Chief Risk Officer". I therefore assume ECD-2 is a valid *operational* overlay and an invalid *A7 exception*. Strong wording in C4 does not enlarge the signatories' competence. This is the single most consequential assumption in this report; §5 tests its negation.

**M2 — Specificity and self-limitation.** Where a special source states its own reach, I read it at its stated reach and no further. ECD-2's own operative scope statement (C4) is "ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding requirements". C5 records its silence on segregation of duties (SoD), Finance approval, Compliance clearance, the EUR 100,000 threshold and the CRO. Silence in an instrument of limited competence is not implied repeal; A4 makes the default explicit ("other controls remain").

**M3 — Later-in-time is not a trump.** ECD-2 (July) is later than GPIP-4 (January), and the CFO email is later still, but lex posterior operates only between instruments of equal rank and identical scope. Between a board-approved global policy and a COO/IC incident directive, rank and competence decide; recency only breaks ties.

**M4 — Dimension-specific hierarchy.** There is no single total ordering. I resolve conflicts issue by issue: *sequence/timing* and *supplier onboarding* are governed by ECD-2 (within scope and window); *financial dual approval* is governed by GPIP-4 A2 as interpreted, within the Finance domain only, by the CFO; *SoD* and *Compliance clearance* are governed by GPIP-4 alone.

**M5 — The CFO email is a competence-limited interpretation, not a waiver.** Assumption: because the CFO owns the Finance control framework (organizational fact) and A2 names functions rather than named post-holders, the CFO may designate who exercises the two A2 economic approvals. Accordingly I allow joint IC authorization to *substitute functionally* for the A2 pair, but only if all of the following hold: the act is inside the ECD-2 window; the amount is at or below EUR 150,000 (Legal added above it per D2); the two authorizers are distinct and are neither the requester nor the committer; the authorization precedes commitment; and it is recorded per C3. D3 and D4 are read as express limits: the email cannot touch Compliance clearance or SoD. Because D is uncontrolled documentation, reliance on it is itself a control weakness and caps confidence at "moderate" wherever it is load-bearing. §5 states the credible contrary reading.

**M6 — Emergency necessity is a scope and proportionality test, not a licence.** A4 and B4 both make emergency a *bounded* status. Necessity is assessed against the norm's purpose: urgency can justify inverting *sequence* (an order that exists to save time) but not deleting *independence* (a control that exists to stop self-dealing). A control that could have been satisfied without losing time — because the authorized approver was reachable, or because a clearance took ten minutes — is not excused by urgency.

**M7 — Time of act, not time of file.** Every rule is applied to the state of the world at the moment of the act assessed. A directive that began at 09:00 does not reach an 08:50 commitment; an email sent on 07-15 does not reach a 07-14 commitment; a directive that expired at 18:00 on 07-18 does not reach a 07-19 commitment. Expiry is treated as hard because a self-expiring instrument that can be extended verbally is not a control.

**M8 — Retrospective evidence versus retrospective authority.** Late *evidence* of a timely act is a records defect; late *authority* is not authority. A6 is applied literally: post-hoc approval mitigates severity and never cures a missing pre-commitment approval.

**M9 — Evidential ranking.** ON-44 changes weight, not norms. Where system timestamps conflict with telephone recordings, supplier acknowledgments, signed PDFs or access logs, the contemporaneous artefact wins, in both directions — including where it hurts the buyer. A known 12-minute lag or a nine-minute fast clock is applied as a stated interval, not silently ignored.

**M10 — Burden of proof.** Baseline controls are presumed applicable; the party invoking an exception must show authority, scope, window and evidence. An unproven exception yields NONCOMPLIANT, not INDETERMINATE. INDETERMINATE is reserved for cases where the *underlying primary fact* is genuinely balanced and reasonable analysts with this file would split on the fact rather than on the interpretation. On this file no case reaches that state, because each contested fact is either resolved by a contemporaneous artefact or is a fact the actor bore the burden of establishing and did not.

**M11 — Classification vocabulary.** COMPLIANT = every applicable rule satisfied on its own terms. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION = at least one deviation from a baseline rule, validly authorized in scope, window and purpose by a competent source, with residual defects that are documentary rather than substantive. NONCOMPLIANT = at least one substantive control was absent at the moment it was owed and no competent source excused it. Severity is graded Critical / High / Moderate / Low / Documentation-only, by exposure to the risk the control protects (patient safety, self-dealing, financial exposure), not by amount alone.

### Issue-specific orderings (the operative hierarchy)

- **Sequence of PO versus commitment:** ECD-2 C1 (in scope/window) > SOP B4 emergency limb > SOP B1. GPIP-4 is indifferent to this ordering except through A2's pre-commitment approval requirement.
- **Off-register / new supplier:** two separate questions. *Use* of the supplier: ECD-2 C2 > SOP B3. *Clearance* of the supplier: GPIP-4 A3 > everything present here.
- **Dual economic approval (≥ EUR 50k):** GPIP-4 A2 > CFO email as *interpreter of who performs it* > SOP B2 > ECD-2 (silent, incompetent).
- **Segregation of duties:** GPIP-4 A1 alone. No other source in this file has standing.
- **Compliance clearance:** GPIP-4 A3 alone, subject only to a genuine A7 exception, which does not exist here.
- **Emergency status and its consequences:** GPIP-4 A4 (defines the ceiling of what emergency does) > ECD-2 (window-bounded operational relaxation) > SOP B4 (local definition, used outside the window).
- **Evidence:** contemporaneous artefacts (call recordings, supplier acknowledgments, signed PDFs, access logs) > ERP timestamps, whenever ON-44 or a known clock skew is in play.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-508 — flips to NONCOMPLIANT (High).** Premise to change: read D1's "up to EUR 150,000" as a hard ceiling on the CFO's sufficiency statement, so that above it the ordinary A2 pair revives and D2's Legal requirement is merely *additional*. This is textually the more literal reading — D1 grants sufficiency in a bounded band, and D2 does not restate sufficiency — and it is normatively attractive because it means the single largest commitment in the incident (EUR 155,000, off-register supplier) received no approval from anyone holding a budget or Finance role. It is also supported by D4's own caution and by the fact that D never entered the controlled repository. **Why I did not adopt it:** D2 would be close to meaningless if the > 150k band simply reverted to full ordinary process, since Legal clearance is not a Finance control and adds nothing to A2. Reasonable analysts will split here; this is the weakest of my three justified findings, and I would not resist a supervisory conclusion that P-508 was noncompliant but excusable.

**P-505 and P-507 — both flip to NONCOMPLIANT (Moderate and Low–Moderate).** Premise to change: reject M5 entirely — hold that an ordinary, uncontrolled email cannot alter *who* performs a control that a board-approved policy requires, because A5's "no silent weakening" principle and A7's exception monopoly together mean any relaxation must be a controlled, written, CRO-issued instrument. On that premise, joint IC authorization is simply not an A2 approval, the IC charter's silence on budget ownership is decisive, and both cases lack any pre-commitment economic approval. This is credible: it is the reading an external auditor is most likely to take, and it is internally cleaner than mine because it needs no judgment about the boundary of the CFO's domain. **Why I retained M5:** A2 names *functions*, not post-holders; the CFO indisputably owns the Finance control framework; and treating a written instruction from the framework owner as void would leave the organization with no lawful way to operate A2 during a declared incident, which pushes actors toward undocumented workarounds. Note the asymmetry: rejecting M5 changes P-505, P-507 and P-508 but changes nothing about P-502 (the email postdated the act), P-503 (independence failed) or P-506 (the window had closed) — my noncompliance findings in those cases are robust to it.

**P-503 — could soften to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION only if two premises change together.** One must (a) accept Operations' C6 reading that backup-line contingency parts are "critical sterilization-line lots" despite a three-day stock horizon, and (b) accept that PROC-3's dual role is a documentation failure rather than a substantive one, on the theory that IC-1 was a genuinely independent second mind and PROC-3 merely executed. **Why this is weak:** even granting both, the case is still > EUR 100,000 with no Compliance_Clearance at any time, and A3 is beyond the reach of ECD-2 and of the CFO alike. The scope argument therefore cannot change the classification — at most it moves severity from Critical to High. This is worth stating plainly, because it shows the C6 ambiguity that dominates the file is *not* load-bearing for P-503.

**P-501 — flips to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION (Documentation-only).** Premise to change: hold that B4's substitution clause governs only *who signs Plant_Approve*, not *who may bind the supplier*, and that no source in the file reserves Commit_to_Supplier to the Plant Manager. On that reading SHIFT-1's commitment was not an unauthorized act at all; B2's approvals carry no express pre-commitment ordering below EUR 50,000 (A2's ordering rule starts at 50k, and this was 38k); both approvals arrived the same day; the emergency was genuine; and B6's form was timely. This is a genuinely strong argument and the closest call in the report. **Why I did not adopt it:** treating "who commits" as unregulated would let any shift employee create a binding obligation ahead of every approval whenever an emergency is asserted, which reads B4's careful delegation condition out of the SOP and inverts A6. The deciding fact is that the Plant Manager was reachable — necessity, the only thing that makes pre-approval commitment tolerable, was absent. A second, weaker variant would flip it the other way to a higher severity by arguing the ERP's ±12-minute lag makes the 08:50/09:00 boundary unsafe; that fails because the lag makes recorded times *late*, so the real commitment was at or before 08:50, i.e. even further from ECD-2's start.

**P-506 — flips to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION or INDETERMINATE if the extension is substantiated.** Premise to change: accept that an incident directive can be extended by the same authority that issued it without a fresh writing, given the organization's history of treating signed directives as temporary policy overlays, and treat the buyer's statement as evidence rather than assertion. If a written extension, an IC calendar entry, or a call recording of the overnight authorization surfaced, C1 would cure the PO sequence and D1 would cover the EUR 51,000 approvals, producing JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION; if the evidence were merely suggestive, INDETERMINATE would become honest. **Why it is weak as the file stands:** a self-expiring emergency instrument whose life can be extended by recollection is not a control at all, and the actor invoking the extension carries the burden. Note that even on the buyer's own account there was no joint IC authorization record, so C3 fails independently of the extension question.

**P-502 — the only interpretation that changes it is a maximalist supersession theory, and it is weak.** One would have to read C4 ("takes precedence over ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding requirements") as sweeping in the Compliance clearance *of* a new supplier, on the ground that clearance is part of onboarding. That is not implausible as a matter of ordinary language, and B7's undefined "ordinary local workflow steps" invites it. **Why it fails:** A3's new-supplier limb sits in the global integrity policy, not in local onboarding workflow; C5 records ECD-2's silence on it; the signatories lacked A7 competence; the CRO never ratified; and the organization has expressly never applied the overlay precedent to new-supplier Compliance clearance. Even at its strongest, this theory does not reach A2, so P-502 would remain noncompliant for the absent pre-commitment economic approvals.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

### 6.1 Four distinct findings, kept separate

**(a) Policy breach — a control was absent when it was owed.** P-502 (Compliance clearance on a new supplier, eight hours late); P-503 (SoD, Compliance, both A2 approvals); P-504 (off-register supplier without clearance, single-actor commitment, unfounded emergency label); P-506 (budget-owner approval after the binding act, PO after commitment, reliance on a lapsed directive); P-501 (commitment with no prior approval while the authorized approver was reachable and no delegation existed). These require corrective and disciplinary action, not better logging. No amount of additional evidence changes them, because the missing item is authority, not proof.

**(b) Logging or documentation defect — the act was correct, the record is not.** P-507's ERP authorization entry at 14:30 for an 08:55 act; P-505's absent Create_PO record; the absence of Plant/Finance *events* in P-505, P-507 and P-508 where the underlying authorization existed; P-508's unrecorded four-hour write-up. These are control-environment weaknesses that inflate apparent noncompliance and must be fixed in the record, with the underlying acts left classified on their merits.

**(c) Late evidence of a timely act — must not be penalized.** P-507's signed PDF created at 12:40 and queued behind a document-system outage proves C3's four-hour deadline was met despite the 14:30 ERP stamp. P-505's supplier acknowledgment at 17:59:31 proves the commitment beat the 18:00 expiry despite an 18:06 stamp on a nine-minute-fast clock. P-502's telephone recording fixes the 10:15 commitment and thereby confirms the 10:10 authorization preceded it. In all three, the ERP is the *worse* witness; ON-44 and the clock skew change evidential weight only and create no exception.

**(d) Inability to prove compliance — distinct from proven breach.** P-506's alleged overnight extension; the unknown identities behind P-505's and P-507's authorizations and commitments; whether P-508's sole-source claim was tested. Where the actor bore the burden of establishing the exception (P-506), non-proof resolves against the exception. Where the open fact would only *confirm* an otherwise coherent justification (P-505, P-507), I classify on the balance of the available evidence and mark the reversing fact explicitly.

### 6.2 Immediate case-level remediation

1. **P-503 — highest priority.** Retrospective Compliance and Finance review of the EUR 140,000 commitment within five working days; independent second-line review of PROC-3's authority and of IC-1's actual involvement; formal determination whether the pumps were in ECD-2 scope at all; consider contract review for a purchase committed without any independent approval. Treat as a reportable control failure regardless of outcome, and use it as the test case for whether "critical contingency" labelling by IC has any normative effect.
2. **P-502 — new-supplier exposure.** Complete the supplier qualification retroactively and document what the 18:30 clearance found; if it would have failed, escalate immediately (product-quality implications, since these were sterile filters used on the affected line). Record the pre-commitment gap as a breach even though the clearance eventually passed.
3. **P-504 — emergency-label misuse.** Withdraw or annotate the emergency form; onboard or replace the freezer supplier through the ordinary register process; confirm whether a facilities delegation existed for SHIFT-2; use this case in training as the canonical example of scope drift.
4. **P-506 — expiry discipline.** Obtain a written statement from IC on whether any extension was authorized; if none, record the reliance as unfounded and re-approve the EUR 51,000 spend under ordinary process; if a writing exists, reclassify on the record.
5. **P-501 — delegation hygiene.** Confirm and document whether the Plant Manager was consulted verbally at 08:45; reissue standing delegation instruments so that a reachable approver is never bypassed; the same-day approvals stand as mitigation only.
6. **P-505, P-507, P-508 — ratify the substituted approvals.** Have the CFO (and for P-508 the CRO, once available) confirm in a controlled document that joint IC authorization was intended to perform the A2 approvals in these specific cases, with amounts and dates. This converts a defensible-but-informal position into a recorded one. Separately, reconstruct actor identities to verify R9 independence, and create the missing Create_PO record for P-505.
7. **Across all cases in the ECD-2 window.** Issue one controlled retrospective note that supersedes the CFO email, states which controls were and were not relaxed, and lists every purchase relying on it. An uncontrolled email carrying the authority for six-figure commitments is itself the most systemic finding in this review.

### 6.3 Monitoring actions

- Conformance checking against the R1–R10 catalogue with **act-time** rule evaluation, not case-completion evaluation, so that late approvals never silently repair a trace.
- Automated SoD check on every purchase comparing the actor sets of Request_Purchase, Commit_to_Supplier, Emergency_Authorize and each approval; alert on any intersection, as in P-503.
- Threshold-and-supplier alert firing at commitment time: ≥ 25k, ≥ 50k, > 100k, > 150k, plus any supplier absent from the register, with the new-supplier check explicitly amount-independent (the P-504 gap).
- Directive-window guard: hard-block emergency pathways outside a declared window; require a fresh controlled document for any extension; alert on any emergency-form entry lacking a production or sterilization nexus.
- Timestamp-integrity monitoring: NTP drift alarms, reconciliation of ERP times against supplier acknowledgments and telephone logs for every commitment within 30 minutes of a threshold or a window boundary, and a standing rule that boundary cases are adjudicated on contemporaneous artefacts.
- Emergency-normalization dashboard: count, value and share of emergency-pathway purchases per month, per requester and per supplier, with review triggered when the share exceeds a set baseline, and a specific flag for repeat use of the same off-register supplier.
- Closure tracking of C3 recording latency (target ≤ 4h) and B6 form latency (≤ 24h), reported separately from substantive breach rates so that documentation defects are never conflated with control failures.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Proposed GPIP-4 clause A8 — Incident Overlays, Exceptions and Expiry (replaces the interaction of A4, A5 and A7 with local SOPs and incident directives).**

1. **Three-tier authority.** (a) *Non-waivable core*: segregation of duties, Compliance clearance where required, and the existence of two independent economic approvals above the dual-approval threshold. These may be modified only by a written CRO exception under this clause. (b) *Waivable sequence and onboarding*: the order of PO creation, supplier commitment and record creation, and use of an off-register supplier. These may be relaxed by a signed incident directive. (c) *Local detail*: named approver roles, forms and evidence formats, which the site may make stricter but never weaker.
2. **Who may relax what.** A COO/Incident-Commander directive may relax tier (b) only. The CFO, as owner of the Finance control framework, may designate *who* discharges the economic approvals but may not reduce their number below two, may not remove their pre-commitment character, and may not touch tier (a). Any relaxation of tier (a) requires a written CRO exception that states the clauses waived, the monetary ceiling, the named cases or case class, and an expiry timestamp. **A standing CRO deputy is designated in advance**, so that the CRO's unavailability can never be a reason to proceed without competent authority.
3. **Scope must be certified per purchase.** An incident directive applies to a purchase only if a named Incident Commander certifies, before commitment and in the record, that the purchase meets a defined criterion. The default criterion is: *required to prevent or contain patient-safety or product-quality harm, or to restore an affected production line, with a need arising within 48 hours of commitment*. Contingency, backup-line and buffer-stock purchases are outside the directive by default and require an express written extension of scope. Self-labelling by the requester or by IC alone, without the certification and criterion, has no normative effect.
4. **Thresholds are absolute and cumulative.** Compliance clearance is required before commitment for any purchase above EUR 100,000 **and for any purchase from a supplier not in the register, at any amount**. No incident directive, email or emergency status affects this. Two independent economic approvals are required before commitment at or above EUR 50,000; Legal clearance is additionally required above EUR 150,000. Where a directive designates substitute approvers, the substitution is valid only up to the ceiling stated in the CRO exception or CFO designation, and above that ceiling the ordinary role-holders must approve.
5. **Segregation of duties is absolute.** No person may occupy more than one of the roles requester, committer, and authorizer or approver of the same purchase. An emergency authorization signed by a person who also requested or committed is void, and the purchase is treated as having had no authorization. Where only one qualified independent person is available, the purchase requires the CRO deputy's written approval.
6. **New suppliers.** An incident directive may permit *use* of an off-register supplier. It never displaces clearance. Where time genuinely does not permit full clearance, an expedited clearance — a documented sanctions, ownership, conflict-of-interest and quality screen, achievable within two hours — must be completed before commitment, and full onboarding within ten working days. A commitment made without at least the expedited screen is a breach, irrespective of outcome.
7. **Retrospective records versus retrospective authority.** Verbal authorization is permitted only where the directive says so, must precede the commitment, and must be written up within four hours by one of the authorizers. Where a system outage prevents entry, the four-hour clock is met by creation of a signed artefact, and the outage must be logged; contemporaneous artefacts (call recordings, supplier acknowledgments, signed PDFs, access logs) govern over system timestamps whenever an outage or clock deviation is known. **Authorization created after the commitment is never authorization**; it may reduce severity only, and must be recorded as a deviation with a named accountable owner.
8. **Expiry is hard.** A directive lapses at its stated end time. Extension requires a new signed document issued before the lapse. No verbal, retrospective or implied extension has any effect, and commitments made after lapse are assessed under ordinary rules regardless of the continuing incident.
9. **Safeguards against emergency normalization.** (a) Every purchase using an incident pathway is subject to mandatory second-line review within 72 hours, and the review outcome is recorded on the case. (b) A single directive may not exceed 96 hours; a third consecutive extension requires board notification. (c) A quarterly ceiling on emergency-pathway spend, by count and by value; breaching it triggers automatic escalation and suspension of the pathway for non-safety purchases. (d) Emergency pathways are technically unavailable for purchase categories with no production, safety or quality nexus, and misuse of an emergency form is a disciplinary matter independent of the purchase's outcome. (e) Repeat use of the same off-register supplier under emergency status forces immediate full onboarding or a supplier block. (f) Emergency-pathway metrics per requester and per approver are reported to the audit committee, so that individual reliance on emergency status is visible rather than aggregate.
10. **Interpretive instruments.** No instruction outside the controlled policy repository has normative effect. Where an executive issues urgent guidance by email, it must be entered into the repository within 24 hours or it lapses; purchases already made in good-faith reliance on it are treated as documentation deviations rather than breaches, provided tier (a) was respected.
