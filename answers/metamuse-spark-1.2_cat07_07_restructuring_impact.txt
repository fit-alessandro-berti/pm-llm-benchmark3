# 1. Mechanism map

Restructuring actions -> Intermediate mechanisms -> Observable process-mining effects

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[Merge N_Tech/S_Tech/E_Tech into Technical Shared Service + Centralize Legal/Risk/QC] --> B[Nominal handover reduction: local loop 36->0]
    A --> C[Central queue formation: 1 pooled queue for 4 functions]
    A --> D[Remove 1 local supervisor -> Shared approval queue]
    A --> E[Routing rules: all high complexity + legal/risk/security -> Shared Service]

    B --> F[Measured: handover_of_work N_Tech/S_Tech/E_Tech disappears]
    B --> G[But: new handovers Local Caseworker -> Shared Service 41/100 + Shared Service -> Caseworker 36/100]

    C --> H[Specialist utilization 68-82% -> 85-95%+]
    H --> I[Queue wait: 3.2h -> 4.0h expected, hidden bottleneck risk 6-12h]
    C --> J[Loss of informal advice: local caseworker cannot tap N_Tech at desk]
    J --> K[More formal referrals + late rework loops]

    D --> L[Role clarity : org:resource no longer maps to team/location]
    L --> M[discover_organizational_roles entropy , activity_based_resource_similarity  within pool,  across pool vs locals]
    L --> N[Subcontracting: local caseworker -subcontracts-> Shared Service -returns-> same caseworker : pings without ownership transfer]

    E --> O[Local expertise loss: high-complexity cases routed away]
    O --> P[Learning deskilling: locals see fewer hard cases, rework_flag  later]
    O --> Q[Customer segment fairness: regional/language cases lose local conventions]

    C & D --> R[Resilience: Single pool SPOF vs cross-team cover]
    R --> S[If pool staffed: resilience to absence  ; If pool overloaded/absent: whole system stalls]
```

# 2. Scenario forecasts

### Optimistic Scenario (Management view holds, ~20% probability)

*Assumes pooled capacity is truly additive, triage is clean, local informal advice replaced by fast async advice.*

*   **Handover pattern:** `handover_of_work_network` shows intended frequencies realized: `caseworker->Technical Shared Service` 38-42/100, return 34-36/100, `cross-team handover` 2-3/100, `caseworker->Legal/Risk` 11-13/100. Subcontracting network shows clean two-step subcontract (caseworker -> shared -> caseworker) without multiple loops. `reassignment_count` stable at 1.0-1.2.
*   **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** `insert_case_service_waiting_time` median wait `caseworker->Shared Service` 3.5-4.5h, return 1.8-2.5h, `shared approval` 2.5-3.5h. Performance DFG variance  after `Specialist Review`. No hidden queue: waiting time distribution is unimodal.
*   **Cycle-time distribution:** Median 6.5-7.0d (stable/improved ~0.5d vs baseline), p90 drops: East 22.8d -> 18-19d, North/South stable. SLA miss 6.5% -> 5.5%. East gains most.
*   **Rework and quality pattern:** `get_rework_cases_per_activity` for `Specialist Review` 9% -> 7% due to better specialization. `Quality Check -> Rework` stable. `local_expertise_flag` cases keep same rework because locals still write thorough notes for shared queue.
*   **Role clarity and accountability:** `discover_organizational_roles` shows 2 clear roles: `Local Generalist` (Intake/Eligibility/Contact/Implementation) and `Central Specialist` (Specialist/Legal/Risk/Approval). Similarity within roles high, between roles low. Ownership clear in `concept:name` = `Supervisor Approval` replaced by queue but traceable via `org:resource` in queue.
*   **Fairness implications:** East catches up to South. High-complexity cases no worse than average. No systematic penalty for `customer_segment` with regional needs because queue has language/region routing attribute.
*   **Early warning signs:** Positive: Queue wait stays <5h in first 4 weeks, `handover_reason` = `standard_routing` >90%, E_Tech utilization volatility drops.

### Central Scenario (Most likely, ~50% probability)

*Nominal handovers fall but central wait + ownership costs offset. East benefits, North/South flat or slightly worse. Tail widens.*

*   **Handover pattern:** Measured handovers meet nominal target (41/100 to Shared Service), but `discover_subcontracting_network` reveals inflated loops: 15-20% of cases need 2+ trips `caseworker <-> Shared Service` (`reassignment_count` 1.4-1.6). `handover_reason` mix shifts: `incomplete_advice` , `need_clarification` , `missing_local_context` 12-18% of shared handovers. `cross-team handover` indeed 3/100 but replaced by `queue re-assignment` within shared pool (invisible to cross-team metric).
*   **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** Median wait `caseworker->Shared Service` 5.5-7.5h (not 4.0h), return 3.0-4.5h. `shared approval queue` 4.0-6.0h with bimodal distribution: 70% fast (2-3h), 30% stuck 12-24h. `get_service_time` service time stable but `service_waiting_time`  30-50%. East wait improves vs. its old 3.2h+high variability; North/South wait worsens.
*   **Cycle-time distribution:** **Median stable 7.0-7.8d** (average looks unchanged  managerial success narrative). **p90 worsens for North/South: 16.4d -> 19-21d, 18.2d -> 20-22d** while East p90 22.8d -> 19-20d improves. Aggregate average masks divergence (Simpson's paradox). SLA miss flat 6-8%.
*   **Rework and quality pattern:** Overall `rework_flag` 8-9% -> 10-12%. `Technical Shared Service -> local caseworker` rework 11-14% (vs. old local loop 8%). Legal/Risk wait 9.6h -> 11-13h, rework stays high 14-16%. `Quality Check` rework  because shared checker lacks local context.
*   **Role clarity and accountability:** `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` within Shared Service  (>0.6) indicating role blurring (Tech doing Legal triage). `working_together_network` shows locals interact only with *queue*, not person. `reassignment_count` correlate with `outcome` delays but no owner. Survey: "who owns this case?" unclear.
*   **Fairness implications:** Team fairness: East benefits (+1d), North/South penalty (-1 to -2d on p90). Customer fairness: `case_complexity=high` and `customer_segment=regional_specific` suffer +3-5d vs low-complexity. `priority=high` cases jump queue, low-priority starve.
*   **Early warning signs:** Median wait creeping to >6h by week 3, bimodal waiting histogram, `reassignment_count` >1.3, `handover_reason` = `clarification` rising, North/South p50 stable but p90 diverging, East vs North gap narrowing then inverting.

### Adverse Scenario (Bottleneck + deskilling, ~30% probability)

*Shared Service becomes overload, informal local help removed, feedback loops amplify.*

*   **Handover pattern:** `caseworker->Shared Service` frequency inflates to 48-55/100 (locals over-refer because early advice gone and risk-averse). Return + re-referral loops 20-30/100 extra. `handover_of_work_network` shows high in-degree to Shared Service but low out-degree throughput (queue buildup). `subcontracting` shows ping-pong: caseworker -> Shared -> caseworker -> Shared.
*   **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** Median wait 9-15h, p90 wait 30-48h before Specialist Review. `shared approval queue` median 7-10h. Performance DFG shows `Specialist Review` and `Quality Check` self-loops with huge waiting edges. Utilization Legal/Risk >100% (queue never drains). `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` shows afternoon pile-ups, Monday backlogs.
*   **Cycle-time distribution:** Median 8.5-10.5d (+1.5-2.5d), p90 26-32d. SLA miss 7% -> 12-15%. Throughput/month  2100 -> 1800 despite same staffing. East also stalls after initial relief because pool serves all teams.
*   **Rework and quality pattern:** Rework 15-20%. `get_rework_cases_per_activity` `Rework` after `Implementation` doubles. Mechanism: advice generic  implementation wrong  Quality Check (central, lacking context) misses  customer rework. `local_expertise_flag` negative cases rework +10pp vs. flag positive.
*   **Role clarity and accountability:** `discover_organizational_roles` collapses to 1.5 roles (everyone generalist or everyone bottleneck). `resource similarity` among Shared Service ~0.8 (interchangeable but overloaded). Accountability vacuum: cases sit in `shared approval queue` with `org:resource` = null/queue. `handover_reason` = `queue_timeout` / `auto_reassignment` appears.
*   **Fairness implications:** Strong unfairness: `case_complexity=high`, `required_specialty=legal/risk/security`, and vulnerable `customer_segment` bear all delay. Low-complexity cases improve slightly (faster approval) widening gap  fairness and compliance risk. Geographic fairness reverses then equalizes downward.
*   **Early warning signs within days:** Wait >8h by day 5, queue length monotonically increasing, `reassignment_count` >1.8, sharp drop in `local caseworker` activity on `Local Technical Assessment` (deskilling), spike in `filter_variants` with extra `Specialist Review` repetitions, staff feedback: locals stop developing tech skill, customer satisfaction drops even before rework recorded.

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

Average/median cycle time can stay flat or improve (especially via East improvement weighting average) while these risks accumulate:

**1. Tail latency explosion (p90/p95):** Median 7.4d may stay 7.4d yet p90 goes 16-18d -> 22-28d for 20% of cases. Performance DFG average edge hides bimodal waiting (70% fast, 30% stuck 24h+). Equity and SLA risk lives in tail, invisible to mean. Must monitor percentiles and waiting-time histogram, not mean.

**2. High-complexity cases systematically worse:** Average includes 60% low-complexity cases that benefit from standardized approvals. Segmented analysis `case_complexity=high` may show +4-6d even as overall average -0.3d. Management's "all high-complexity go to shared service" creates adverse selection. If not filtered, averages commit Simpson's paradox.

**3. Local knowledge loss and generic advice:** Average hides `local_expertise_flag` effect. Cases with `required_specialty` that previously got informal local-tech advice (3.2h wait, 9% rework) now get pooled advice plus return (7h wait, 14% rework) but average diluted by easy cases never needing advice. Immediate measure looks efficient; latent `rework_flag` appears 2-4 weeks later after `Quality Check`/`Customer Contact`.

**4. Fairness across teams and customer segments:** Aggregate improvement can be East's 22.8d -> 19d masking North/South deterioration. Similarly, `customer_segment` needing language/regional accommodation faces +2 handovers via queue. Fairness invisible unless stratified by `team`, `location`, `customer_segment`. Uniform average violates equity.

**5. Learning and deskilling (slow-burn resilience loss):** In weeks 1-6 locals still competent on hard cases (old skill). Routing away hard cases stops skill formation: `discover_organizational_roles` will show locals no longer perform `Local Technical Assessment`. Observable only after 2-3 months when experienced locals leave and novices have no learning path. Average productivity stable until shock (absence, peak demand).

**6. Accountability diffusion and ownership vacuum:** Cases sitting in `shared approval queue` / `Technical Shared Service` queue have `org:resource`=queue, not person. Handover network shows fewer edges but `working_together_network` shows no one `works together` for long. Delays become "queue time" not attributable. `handover_reason` shifts to `auto_reassignment`. Average cycle time attributes delay to "process" not role, reducing corrective action.

**7. Resilience to absence and exception handling brittleness:** Pre-change, absence of S_Tech affected only South via `cross-team handover` 6/100. Post-change, absence of 1-2 pooled specialists stalls all teams simultaneously. Variants for exceptions (`filter_variants` with `priority=high` or `required_specialty=security`) may need ad hoc local workaround that no longer exists. Under normal load average fine; under stress (flu, peak) system fails abruptly—unobservable in average under stable staffing.

**8. Hidden utilization and rework-inflated workload:** Specialist utilization metric 71% -> 82% looks efficient, but if measured as `events per resource`, it counts re-referrals as productive work. True useful work flat while `reassignment_count` inflates. Queueing time not counted in utilization, so bottleneck invisible until queue explodes.

# 5. Pilot design

**Principle:** Phased, segmented, reversible. Do not roll out to all teams/specialties at once.

**Pilot scope:**
*   **Where:** Team East only for first 8-12 weeks. Rationale: East already overloaded (E_Tech 82%, slowest cycle), most likely to benefit; North/South act as controls and preserve local expertise. Keep North/South fully local during pilot.
*   **What:** Centralize only `Technical Shared Service` for `case_complexity=high` originating in East. Keep `Legal/Risk` shared queue as-is (already centralized, near capacity) - do NOT add Technical triage to their queue during pilot. Keep one local supervisor in East (do not remove until pilot proven). Shared approval queue not introduced in pilot; approval stays local.
*   **Routing:** East high-complexity -> Technical Shared Service staffed by E_Tech + 0.5 FTE from N_Tech/S_Tech volunteering (not full merge). All other segments stay local.

**Comparison group:**
*   **Concurrent control:** North and South teams (same period, same `case_complexity`, `required_specialty`, `customer_segment`). Difference-in-differences.
*   **Historical control:** East own 3 months pre-pilot vs pilot, adjusting for seasonality via `extract_temporal_features_dataframe`.
*   **Within-pilot segmentation:** Compare East `high` vs `low` complexity; `local_expertise_flag=True` vs False; `customer_segment` regional.

**Pre/post observation window:**
*   Pre: 12 weeks baseline (stable).
*   Pilot: 8-12 weeks (minimum 6 weeks to observe rework). Warm-up 2 weeks excluded from evaluation.
*   Post: Daily leading, weekly lagging reviews. Full evaluation after 10 weeks.

**Segmentation (all PM analyses stratified):**
*   By `team` (North/South vs East pilot vs East control low-complexity)
*   By `case_complexity` (high/medium/low)
*   By `required_specialty` (none / tech / legal / risk / security)
*   By `customer_segment` (standard vs language/regional accommodation)
*   By `priority` and `location`

**Guardrail metrics (automatic alert  intervention):**
*   Leading guardrails (stop if breached 2 consecutive weeks): Median wait East high-complexity `caseworker->Shared Service` >7h; p90 wait >20h; `reassignment_count` mean >1.5; `rework_flag` East high >14%; queue depth growth >15% week-on-week.
*   Lagging guardrails: East p90 cycle >24d; SLA miss East >12%; customer satisfaction East regional segment drops >0.3; North/South unaffected (to detect spillover).
*   Fairness guardrail: Gap between `customer_segment` regional vs standard cycle >4d.

**Staff feedback collection:**
*   Weekly pulse: caseworkers (East + North), specialists (E_Tech, shared Legal/Risk), supervisors. Questions: informal advice loss, ownership clarity, queue usability, skill use.
*   Case note quality audit: sample 20 cases/week coded for `handover_reason` completeness.
*   Shadowing: 3 days observing queue pull behavior (cherry-picking?).

**Rollback or adjustment triggers:**
*   **Rollback to local:** If adverse scenario signals in first 4 weeks (wait >12h median, rework >16%, or guardrail breached 2 weeks). Immediate revert East to embedded E_Tech.
*   **Adjust:** If central scenario: cap `caseworker->Shared Service` referrals to true high-complexity only (reinstate local informal advice channel 2h/day office hours), split Legal/Risk triage from Technical (separate queue), add WIP limit to Shared Service (e.g., 15 cases/FTE).
*   **Proceed to next phase:** Only if optimistic/central-low: East p90 improves, median wait <6h, rework 11%, fairness gap not widened, staff reports role clarity  baseline. Then expand to South technical only, keep North as control one more cycle.

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: Do NOT proceed to full rollout. Pilot first with modified scope (East-only technical pool, no supervisor removal, no merging Legal/Risk/QC into same queue). Proceed to broader rollout only conditional on stratified monitoring.**

**Why conditional pilot:**
*   East is distinct: high utilization, slow p90, likely to benefit from pooling; North/South have stable local loops with low rework (8-9%) that pooling risks breaking. Full merge treats heterogeneous teams homogeneously.
*   Management estimate (41/100 handover at 4.0h) assumes additive capacity. Current Legal/Risk at 91-93% utilization indicates zero slack to absorb difficult QC + technical triage. Staff risk of hidden bottleneck is plausible and would not appear in average cycle time until queue saturated.
*   Expected +15% utilization gain is mechanical: removing informal advice and centralizing approvals *will* increase utilization, but queuing theory predicts wait  non-linearly above ~85%. The trade-off is not visible in handover counts.

**What management must NOT infer from early averages:**
1. **Do not infer success from median/mean cycle time falling in weeks 1-4.** Early median can fall due to East improvement + Hawthorne effect + cherry-picking of easy cases by queue pullers, while North/South tails and high-complexity cases deteriorate. Require p90/p95 and segmented medians.
2. **Do not infer fewer handovers = less coordination cost.** Nominal handover count  but `discover_subcontracting_network` + `reassignment_count` may . Count subcontracting loops, not handovers.
3. **Do not infer higher specialist utilization = efficiency.** Utilization  can be rework-inflated and queue-waiting is unmeasured. Measure `service_waiting_time` and queue length alongside utilization.
4. **Do not extrapolate East pilot success to North/South.** Fairness requires team-stratified inference. Average weighted by case volume over-represents North.
5. **Do not treat early stable rework as proof of quality.** Rework lags 2-6 weeks (`Quality Check` -> `Rework` -> `Close`). Early weeks understate loss of `local_expertise_flag` effect.

**Conditional path:**
*   If pilot shows optimistic metrics (guardrails met, East p90 -3d, rework 9%, wait <5h): Expand to South technical pool with WIP limits; keep Legal/Risk separate; evaluate supervisor queue separately.
*   If pilot shows central metrics (median flat, p90 divergence, wait 5-7h, rework 10-12%): **Modify** - retain 0.5 day/week embedded tech hours for informal advice, split queues, introduce ownership tagging (`org:resource` must be person within 4h of queue entry), and priority aging to prevent starvation.
*   If adverse signals: **Reject** full centralization; instead optimize locally (add 0.5 FTE to East, codify local best practice, improve case-note standard to reduce `cross-team handover` rework 18% -> 10%), and only share overflow via overflow queue with strict routing.

Full rollout without pilot risks converting a manageable East capacity problem into a systemic bottleneck, deskilling North/South, and hiding fairness and accountability losses behind a stable average.
