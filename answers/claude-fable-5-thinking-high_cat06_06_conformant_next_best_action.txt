## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

The four loss components are not on the same footing, and the ranking follows from dominance rather than a single expected-value sum:

- **Regulatory exposure dominates.** The unlawful-execution tail (€100k–€500k plus non-monetary consequences: licence risk, regulator relationship, personal accountability) exceeds the *entire* deadline value (€20k–€35k) by roughly an order of magnitude even before non-monetary terms. Any action that buys deadline value by accepting unlawful-execution probability is dominated: even a 10% chance of the low-end penalty (€10k expected) nearly cancels the maximum deadline benefit, and the realistic exposure is far larger. This is why A and B are ranked last regardless of how favorable the SLA forecast looks — see Section 4.
- **Deadline loss vs. false rejection.** These are commensurable and both bounded: €20k–€35k vs €15k–€80k. Because approval probability after a conformant review is 0.55–0.75, rejecting now (F) has expected false-rejection harm of roughly 0.55–0.75 × €15k–€80k ≈ €8k–€60k, which generally exceeds the expected deadline saving of trying the review first. Hence F ranks below the review strategies but above E and the nonconformant options: it is the correct *fallback* if the review path collapses, not the correct first move.
- **Spillover is a first-class cost.** Interrupting P-901 converts 0.12–0.28 additional breach probability on another high-value case into expected loss plausibly comparable to P-884's own deadline value. That is why P1 (which pays the spillover only if S4 is late) dominates C (which pays it unconditionally).
- **Uncertainty handling.** All predictions are observational; intervening (interrupting an officer, expediting a review) changes the data-generating process, so I treat the stated intervals as optimistic and widen them mentally toward the adverse end (approval probability toward 0.55, review time toward 75 min, S4 arrival toward 65 min). The 12-minute-stale risk score (72) is treated as ≥70 for rule purposes — one may not "wait out" a control trigger on staleness. Where intervals overlap heavily (P1 vs C), the tie-break is the option that preserves optionality and avoids irreversible costs: P1 keeps the P-901 interruption and further API calls in reserve.

**Why no forecast can rescue A or B.** Rules 6, 7, 9, and 10 are deontic constraints, not soft costs: they define which continuations are *permitted*, prior to any utility comparison. A favorable SLA forecast changes the expected operational loss, but expected loss is only compared *within* the permitted set. Concretely: (i) the constraint triggers (score ≥70, unresolved 0.46 fuzzy match) are facts about the case, not predictions, so no forecast makes them false; (ii) the harms the controls protect against (paying a sanctioned party, single-person fraud/error) are exactly the tail events that observational point forecasts are worst at pricing; (iii) B is additionally an integrity violation — it would create a record asserting a control was performed when it was not, which is worse than A's overt omission. Treating a SAR filing, a re-screen (D), or self-certification (B) as a substitute for the independent second review would be a category error: none of them is the control that rules 6–7 mandate.

**What would validate F:** an articulated, documented basis showing the unresolved match plus risk profile meets the institution's rejection threshold (e.g., match confidence above policy cutoff, corroborating adverse indicators, documented inability to resolve within the customer's window), recorded after the required review — ideally after the second officer concurs, which folds F into the tail of P1/C rather than a standalone shortcut.

**What would validate G:** an actual, independently justifiable incident (e.g., a documented operational emergency), a genuine incident identifier created through the incident process, and explicit Compliance Director authorization — all *before* execution. Manufacturing an incident to launder a deadline problem would convert G into a variant of A with an audit trail of intent.

## 5. Resource spillovers

- **S2 vs S4.** S2 is available only by imposing 0.12–0.28 extra breach probability on P-901, another high-value case with a *tighter* (70-min) deadline; the harm we would export to P-901 is of the same order as the value we are trying to save on P-884. S4 is free of spillover but arrives at an uncertain 35–65 min. The dominant design is conditional: reserve S4 now, and buy the S2 interruption only if, at the T+30–40 checkpoint, S4 has not materialized and the deadline is still worth chasing. If at that checkpoint the alternate screening has come back adverse (match strengthening), do not interrupt P-901 at all — the likely outcome is Reject/Hold, which S4 can record after P-901 finishes.
- **If S4 arrives at 35 min:** P1 strictly dominates C — same conformant review, no P-901 harm, screening result already in hand; execution possible by ~T+75–90. Ranking unchanged, gap between ranks 1 and 2 widens.
- **If S4 arrives at 65 min:** the S4 route almost certainly breaches the deadline (65 + 35–75 + 5–15 ≈ 105–155 min). The escalation clause fires: P1 collapses into C at T+30–40, still finishing by ~T+75–130. Effective ranking becomes C-with-prior-screening first; standalone waiting for S4 drops below F, because a knowingly late review forfeits the deadline value while still delaying P-901's officer pool.
- **API quota.** One of three expedited calls is a modest, justified spend for a €480k case where the match confidence (0.46) sits in the maximally informative middle range — the screening result has high value-of-information per call. Do not spend a second call on the same case.
- **Waiting-for-exception risk (G).** Its 20–60+ min director latency overlaps almost the whole remaining budget with a low success probability and zero salvage value on failure; pursuing G *instead of* securing a reviewer is the classic time-consuming trap the prompt flags, which is why G ranks below even E.

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence

**Primary recommendation — Plan P1:** immediately dispatch the alternate sanctions screening (one API call) and reserve S4, without interrupting P-901 yet. At T+30–40 minutes: if S4 is available, S4 performs the independent Second Officer Review informed by the fresh screening result; if S4 is not available and the screening is not adverse, interrupt P-901 and use S2. Execute only after a recorded Approve; if the review yields Reject or Hold (with or without a Suspicious Activity Report, never disclosed to the customer), accept the deadline loss deliberately.

**Fallback:** if by ~T+55–60 no second officer has started the review, or the refreshed screening strengthens the match, stop chasing the deadline and move to Action F — a documented rejection (or hold with SAR, per the second officer's judgment once available), explicitly recording that the unresolved match and control requirements, not throughput, drove the outcome.

**Confidence:** Medium-high (≈0.7) that P1 is the best available plan; high (≈0.95) that the correct plan is *some* conformant second-review strategy and that A, B, and G-as-currently-framed must not be taken.

**Immediate reranking triggers:** (1) the alternate screening returns — a cleared match shifts weight toward speed (earlier S2 escalation), a strengthened match shifts weight toward F/Hold+SAR; (2) S4's arrival time firms up at either 35 or 65 minutes; (3) any material change in the refreshed risk score (a drop below 70 with a resolved match would re-open the question of the mandatory-review triggers, though not retroactively excuse skipping one); (4) P-901's status changes such that interrupting S2 becomes cheaper or costlier than the 0.12–0.28 estimate.
