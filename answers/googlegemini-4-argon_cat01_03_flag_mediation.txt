## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

We adopt the **Separable Effects** (Robins & Richardson, 2010) and **Interventional Direct/Indirect Effects** (VanderWeele et al., 2014; Vansteelandt & Daniel, 2017) frameworks rather than classical Natural Direct and Indirect Effects (NDE/NIE). 

**Why:** NDE/NIE require cross-world counterfactual independence ($Y_{a, M_{a^*}} \perp\!\!\!\perp M_{a^*} \mid C$), which is violated here due to **exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding**: the flag ($A$) alters triage completion time ($L_1$) and shared queue states ($L_2$), which subsequently affect both downstream mediators (manual review waiting, escalation $M$) and total cycle time ($Y$). Furthermore, "setting `flag=0`" is ambiguous because `flag` bundles three distinct operational components: priority triage routing ($A_{pri}$), mandatory manual review routing ($A_{rev}$), and visible display to human reviewers ($A_{vis}$). Separable effects allow us to define policies (P0–P4) as explicit manipulations of these distinct operational components, while interventional analogues avoid unassailable cross-world assumptions by fixing mediator distributions to random draws from counterfactual populations conditional on pre-exposure covariates.

### Confounding, Feedback, and Interference Analysis
Three structural complications prevent naive causal decomposition:
1. **Exposure-Induced Mediator-Outcome Confounding:** `flag` routes cases to `priority_lane` (resources T1/T2), drastically shortening `triage_wait` and altering `triage_done_time`. Because `triage_done_time` determines when a case enters the downstream queue, it affects `U_shared_queue_state` and `manual_queue_wait` while also directly shifting downstream shift schedules/batches (`escalation_queue_wait` and `cycle_time`). Thus, `triage_done_time` is an exposure-induced confounder of the manual-review-to-cycle-time pathway.
2. **Treatment-Confounder Feedback:** Across sequential stages, a case's realization of `manual_queue_wait` alters `manual_done_time`, which updates `U_shared_queue_state` before the decision to trigger `escalation` and before `escalation_queue_wait` is realized.
3. **Interference (SUTVA Violation):** Because `review_resource` (M1, M2, M3) and senior escalation teams are shared, setting `flag=1` on case $i$ forces mandatory review and accelerates its arrival into the review pool via fast triage. This consumes M1/M2/M3 capacity (`U_other_cases_treatment`), directly inflating `manual_queue_wait` for concurrent flagged and unflagged cases $j \neq i$.

### Interpretation of Observed Contrasts vs. Causal Effects
The timestamps confirm a sharp operational reversal: flagged cases complete triage in 0.84 hours on average (0.25h wait + 0.59h service via dedicated resources T1/T2) versus 3.27 hours for unflagged cases (2.33h wait + 0.94h service via T3/T4). However, flagged cases average 17.06 hours total cycle time versus 8.21 hours for unflagged cases. This 8.85h penalty is driven by three downstream contrasts:
1. **Mandatory Review Occurrence:** 100% of flagged cases enter manual review versus 40% of unflagged cases.
2. **Manual Queue Bottleneck:** Conditional on entering review, flagged cases wait 6.55h (`triage-done-to-manual-start`) versus 2.13h for reviewed unflagged cases.
3. **Escalation Routing:** 60% of flagged cases escalate (adding ~9.17h of wait and service) versus 20% of unflagged cases.

These raw contrasts are **not automatically causal**. First, `flag=1` is perfectly collinear with `risk_score >= 0.64`, and 50% of flagged cases are High complexity (vs. 30% unflagged). High-complexity cases get routed exclusively to reviewer `M2` (5 of 6 escalated flagged cases and both escalated unflagged cases R09/R20 use `M2`), causing severe resource-specific queuing. Second, because flagged cases finish triage 2.43h earlier, they arrive at the manual queue early in the shift while `M2` is still blocked, mechanically converting triage wait savings into manual queue wait.

Detecting Unobserved Confounding & Timestamp Artifacts** | **Double Negative-Control Design:** Use `residual-to-close` (`last_done` to `close`) as a Negative-Control Outcome (NCO) and `arrival_load` (conditional on queue state) as a check on clock artifacts. | Adjust for `complexity`, final stage reached (`triage`, `manual`, or `escalation`), and time-of-day of last stage completion. | `residual-to-close` reflects automated/clerical wrap-up that should not be directly affected by `flag` or `U_underlying_risk` ($A \not\to Y_{NCO}$, $U \not\to Y_{NCO}$). | Regress `residual-to-close` on `flag` conditional on `complexity` and final stage (in log: mean is 1.12h for `flag=1` vs. 1.17h for `flag=0`). | If `residual-to-close` varies strongly with `flag` or exhibits exact 60-minute step functions tied to clock hours, it indicates automated batch closing or unlogged post-escalation work, invalidating cycle-time precision. |
| **6. Robustness to Hidden Risk (`U_underlying_risk`)** | **Rosenbaum Bounds / E-Value Sensitivity Analysis** on the escalation probability and cycle-time contrast between flagged and unflagged reviewed cases. | Match or stratify on `complexity=High` and `review_resource=M2` (comparing R01, R03, R10, R13, R18 vs. R09, R20). | Unobserved risk $U_{risk}$ increases odds of `flag=1` by at most $\Gamma$ and increases `escalation` or wait time by at most $\Delta$. | Compute the minimum strength of association ($\text{E-value}$) that `U_underlying_risk` must have with both `flag` and `manual_queue_wait` to explain away the 7.58h vs. 2.50h wait gap among High-complexity cases. | Because unflagged High-complexity cases on M2 (R09, R20) have **100% escalation** and **50% Reject** (higher than flagged High-complexity's 40% Reject!), a formal test showing $\hat{\beta}_{flag \to esc \mid High} \le 0$ proves `escalation` is driven by `complexity`, not the flag. |
| **7. Shared-Queue Interference Across Cases** | **Cluster-Randomized / Time-Block Switchback Design** alternating policy regimes (P0 vs. P2) across distinct 48-hour shift blocks $b \in \{1, \dots, B\}$. | Block-level covariates: total arrival volume, fraction of High-complexity arrivals, and day-of-week. | Partial interference: queue spillovers are contained within 48-hour blocks (or decay across shift boundaries after accounting for carryover backlog $Q_0$). | Test whether unflagged reviewed cases (`flag=0, manual_review=1`) experience shorter `manual_queue_wait` during P2 blocks than during P0 blocks (**Spillover Test**). | If unflagged review waits (`triage-done-to-manual-start`) do not decrease during P2 blocks despite a 50% drop in flagged review volume, interference is negligible and delays are structural/schedule-based. |

### Pilot Specification: Complexity-Stratified P2 with Shift-Aligned Guardrails
We select **Policy P2 (targeted at Medium complexity)** for a 4-week limited pilot: retain priority triage for all `flag=1` cases, retain mandatory review for `flag=1 & complexity=High`, and replace mandatory review with **discretionary triage routing** for `flag=1 & complexity=Medium`.

* **Eligibility:** All incoming cases with `flag=1` ($\text{risk\_score} \ge 0.64$) and `complexity=Medium` (approx. 25% of total volume; 50% of flagged volume).
* **Randomization / Assignment Rule:** **48-hour Time-Block Switchback Design** alternating between P0 (100% mandatory review for eligible cases) and P2 (triage worker T1/T2 decides whether to route eligible cases to manual review based on case file and `risk_score`). Within P2 blocks, randomly audit 25% of bypassed cases via a background shadow review to measure false-approval rates.
* **Queue-Capacity Guardrail:** If total queued cases awaiting `M1/M2/M3` exceed $Q_{max} = 5$ at any timestamp, or if any High-complexity case waits $> 4.0\text{ hours}$ for `manual_start`, cross-trained reviewer `M1` is dynamically reallocated to `M2`'s queue.
* **Primary and Secondary Outcomes:** 
  * *Primary:* Mean total cycle time for `flag=1` cases and `manual_queue_wait` across all reviewed cases (capturing spillover).
  * *Secondary:* False-approval rate in the 25% shadow-reviewed bypass sample; escalation queue wait.
* **Minimum Process Data Needed:** Exact timestamps for `arrival`, `triage_start/done`, `manual_start/done`, `escalation_start/done`, `close`; `triage_decision_reason`; shadow-audit outcome; and hourly resource shift-login logs for T1–T4, M1–M3, and seniors.
* **Stopping Conditions:** Terminate immediately if the shadow-audit reveals a $>2\%$ false-approval rate (`Reject`-worthy cases approved without review) or if `escalation_queue_wait` increases by $>25\%$.

## 8. Reversal conditions

Our preferred mechanism interpretation (that mandatory review routing for Medium-complexity cases and `M2` resource bottlenecks/shift schedules drive the flag penalty) and our policy recommendation (**Pilot P2 on Medium-complexity flagged cases**) would reverse under any of the following six empirical conditions:

1. **Hidden Risk (`U_underlying_risk`):** If the 25% shadow-audit of Medium-complexity flagged cases bypassing review under P2 reveals that $\ge 5\%$ contain severe unrecorded defects that should have been `Rejected` (despite 0% observed rejections for Medium complexity in the historical log), proving that mandatory manual review was actively preventing false approvals rather than rubber-stamping them.
2. **Queue Interference vs. Shift Schedules:** If high-resolution resource-availability logs reveal that manual reviewers `M1–M3` only begin shift work at 14:00 local time and senior escalation staff only process batches overnight (00:00–18:00), and that reviewers have idle capacity during their active shifts. This would prove that the 6.55h manual wait is an artifact of **clock-time shift scheduling** interacting with early morning triage completions (`08:42–12:42`), reversing our recommendation from P2 (volume reduction) to **P3/Shift Realignment** (moving reviewer shifts to 08:30).
3. **Decision Quality and Senior Value-Add:** If ground-truth post-audit data show that senior escalation teams overturn `M2`'s preliminary recommendations on $>30\%$ of escalated cases (e.g., saving valid high-value cases R01, R06, R10, R18 from erroneous rejection by `M2`), which would reverse any recommendation under **P4** to let `M2` reject cases directly without escalation.
4. **Timestamp Semantics:** If system telemetry reveals that `manual_start` and `escalation_start` do not record when a worker begins reviewing a case, but rather when an automated batch job transfers the dossier between IT systems (or overwrites `start` upon pause/resume), meaning "waiting time" (`triage-done-to-manual-start`) actually contains unlogged asynchronous investigation or client document collection.
5. **Positivity / Lack of Overlap in Resource Capabilities:** If `T3/T4` and `M1/M3` lack the legal certification or system permissions to process cases with `risk_score >= 0.64` or `complexity=High`, creating a structural positivity violation where cross-training (`P3`) or pooling lanes (`P1`) is legally or operationally impossible.
6. **Compensatory Behavior Change by Reviewers/Triage:** If removing mandatory manual review (`P2`) causes triage workers `T1/T2` to engage in defensive risk-aversion—either increasing their `triage_service` time from 0.59h to $>2.5\text{h}$ to perform ad-hoc investigations at the triage desk, or routing $>90\%$ of Medium-complexity flagged cases to manual review anyway—nullifying all downstream queue savings while destroying triage speed.

## 9. Final synthesis

* **What is directly observed:** Flagged cases (`flag=1`) complete triage 2.43 hours faster than unflagged cases (0.84h vs. 3.27h) because they are routed exclusively to fast triage resources (`T1/T2`). Yet their total cycle time is more than double that of unflagged cases (17.06h vs. 8.21h). This 8.85-hour reversal is arithmetically accounted for by three downstream differences: 100% of flagged cases enter manual review (vs. 40% unflagged), flagged cases wait 6.55 hours for manual review to start (vs. 2.13h for reviewed unflagged cases), and 60% of flagged cases enter senior escalation (vs. 20% unflagged), adding 9.17 hours of escalation wait and service. Notably, `Reject` decisions occur exclusively among High-complexity cases routed through `M2` and senior escalation (50% rejection rate), whereas Medium-complexity flagged cases have a 100% approval rate.
* **What is a plausible mechanism:** The flag acts as an operational policy bundle. Priority triage accelerates flagged cases out of triage early in the morning, dumping them into the manual review queue hours before reviewers (especially High-complexity specialist `M2`) work through their backlogs or start their afternoon shifts (no manual review starts before 14:06). Simultaneously, forcing 100% of Medium-complexity flagged cases into mandatory review consumes shared `M1/M3` capacity without yielding a single rejection.
* **What remains unidentified:** From observational log data alone, we cannot separate how much of the 6.55h manual wait is caused by shared-queue congestion versus fixed afternoon shift schedules, nor can we separate `flag` anchoring from `U_underlying_risk` because `flag=1` is deterministically collinear with `risk_score >= 0.64` and segregated by resource pools (`T1/T2` vs. `T3/T4`).
