## 1. Materiality Framework

I treat the supplied alignments as authoritative diagnostic inputs. The raw alignment cost is interpreted only as technical edit distance under a uniform visible-move cost function. Business severity is assessed separately.

**Severity considers:**

- **Control objective affected:** fraud screening, manual approval, payment sequencing, assessment evidence, notification evidence, or only logging/model hygiene.
- **Amount and exposure:** actual claim amount, potential fraud loss, and whether the payment was high value.
- **Reversibility:** paid claims are hard to reverse; denied, withdrawn, or unpaid cases are usually less severe unless customer harm occurred.
- **Timing:** payment before approval, notification outside 24 hours, or bank change immediately before payment increases risk.
- **Customer and regulatory harm:** wrongful denial, missing notification evidence, fraud exposure, or audit-trail weakness.
- **Recurrence:** isolated technical noise is less severe than repeated control bypass or systemic data-aware policy failure.
- **Evidence reliability:** immutable core-system evidence is stronger than verbal assertions, mutable CRM text fields, or unlinked external documents.
- **Model scope:** if the model was designed only for payable claims, denied and withdrawn cases may produce high alignment cost without operational failure.

**Severity scale used below:**

- **1:** negligible / technical noise / fitting model behavior.
- **2:** minor data, labeling, or scope issue; limited business impact.
- **3:** moderate control or evidence gap; remediation needed but compensating evidence may exist.
- **4:** serious policy or control failure with material exposure or customer harm potential.
- **5:** severe control failure, high fraud or financial exposure, irreversible payment, or critical missing evidence.

**Confidence differs from severity.** Severity asks: *if the interpretation is correct, how bad is it?* Confidence asks: *how reliable is the evidence for that interpretation?* A case can be high severity but medium confidence, for example where external evidence exists but is not immutably linked. A case can also be low severity but high confidence, such as a clearly identified retry duplicate.

| Priority | Remediation | Cases/patterns addressed | Preventive/detective/corrective | Expected risk reduction | Implementation caveat | Validation metric |

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment cost is a technical distance metric. It measures how many visible log or model moves are needed to reconcile the log with the model under a chosen cost function. It does not measure fraud exposure, customer harm, regulatory exposure, evidence reliability, or irreversibility.

**1. Uniform cost design ignores materiality.**  
The cost function assigns 1 to every visible move. Therefore, CL-601 receives cost 3 for three harmless UI telemetry events, while CL-602 receives cost 1 for a missing Fraud Screen on a EUR 480,000 HIGH-risk claim with a recent bank change. The technical cost is inverted relative to business risk.

**2. Scope mismatch creates high cost without operational failure.**  
CL-607 and CL-612 have costs of 4 because the model expects assessment, approval, payment, or notification for payable claims, while the real cases were denied or withdrawn. The high cost reflects model scope and lifecycle omission, not payment loss or control bypass.

**3. Hidden moves can be fitting and costless.**  
CL-605 contains T(tau,c=0), a silent routing transition introduced by Petri-net conversion. It is technically fitting and has no business meaning. A zero-cost alignment here correctly indicates no visible deviation, but zero cost alone does not prove policy compliance.

**4. Data conditions are invisible to pure control-flow alignment.**  
CL-609 and CL-610 have fully synchronous, zero-cost Auto Approve paths. Yet CL-609 violates P1 because the amount is EUR 18,000 and risk is MEDIUM, and CL-610 violates P1 and P2 because the beneficiary bank changed one hour before payment. Alignment cost cannot see these data-aware policy failures.

**5. Duplicated telemetry and external evidence change business meaning without changing severity proportionally.**  
CL-603 has cost 1 because of a duplicate notification event, but the duplicate is likely harmless retry noise. CL-606 and CL-608 each have cost 1, but one involves missing core assessment evidence for a high-value claim and the other involves weak notification evidence for a EUR 250,000 HIGH-risk claim. The business issue is evidence reliability, not edit distance.

**6. Irreversibility is not represented in alignment cost.**  
CL-604 has cost 3, but the payment was executed before manual approval. Once money leaves the account, the control failure is much more serious than a sequence-edit problem. Alignment cost cannot represent the fact that the harmful event has already become difficult to reverse.
