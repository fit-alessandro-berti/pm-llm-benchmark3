## 1. Decision rule

The policy should maintain two separate estimates:

- **Calibrated untreated breach risk, \(p_i^0\):** the risk if the swarm is not used.
- **Incremental swarm effect, \(d_i\):** a provisional, uncertain causal-effect range. It is not derived by subtracting anything from `p_now`, and the supplied ranges are treated as priors rather than known effects.

**Calibration treatment**

- **SME:** use \(p_{\text{now}}+0.04\) to \(p_{\text{now}}+0.09\), capped at 1. Thus I-05 is approximately 0.61–0.66, I-07 0.76–0.81, and I-10 0.50–0.55.
- **Strategic Enterprise:** use \(p_{\text{{now}}-0.12\) to \(p_{\text{now}}-0.05\). Thus I-02 is approximately 0.54–0.61, I-04 0.36–0.43, and I-08 0.31–0.38.
- **Public Sector:** retain the point estimate but attach a sampling interval because the calibration sample is only moderate.
- **Non-Native-Language Support:** do not make a favorable or unfavorable point correction. Use a widened, partially pooled uncertainty interval and include a zero/low-effect branch in adverse analysis.
- **Missing or imputed critical features:** treat the prediction as conditionally invalid until the field is restored or manually verified. This applies to I-06.

These calibrated probabilities inform urgency, plausibility, and false-alarm risk, but do not substitute for the intervention-effect estimate.

**Eligibility gates**

A case is eligible only if all of the following hold:

1. It is before `Diagnosis Complete`.
2. The swarm can start early enough to finish before its last safe start, allowing some time for resolution work after the 45-minute swarm.
3. There is a plausible diagnosis-related mechanism the swarm can affect.
4. No prior swarm has occurred, unless a senior reviewer confirms a materially new event that changes the diagnostic hypothesis.
5. Data needed to justify the alert are available or manually validated.

A legal, safety, or regulatory obligation may override economic ranking, but not the stage-effectiveness gate.

**Persistent alert and material-change rule**

- A first qualifying alert opens one persistent candidate record.
- Thirty-minute refreshes do not create additional evidence or additional claims on capacity.
- Re-notification requires at least one of:
  - a genuinely new customer or diagnostic artifact;
  - a relevant stage or ownership change;
  - correction of previously missing data;
  - an absolute calibrated-risk movement of at least 0.10 that persists for two refreshes;
  - entry into the case’s last-safe-start window.
- An unchanged candidate remains visible and may later be selected because its deadline becomes urgent, but repeated alert count is not a priority feature.

**Priority and portfolio rule**

For each eligible case, calculate conservative and midpoint direct values:

\[
D_i^L=d_{i,L}V_i-€450-B_i,\qquad
D_i^M=d_{i,\text{mid}}V_i-€450-B_i
\]

where \(B_i\) is the listed false-alarm burden. Deducting the full burden is conservative; sensitivity reporting may instead use \((1-p_i^0)B_i\) when burden occurs only for truly unnecessary interventions.

Then evaluate candidate portfolios, not just individual cases:

\[
U(A,s)=\sum_{i\in A}(d_{i,s}V_i-€450-B_i)
-H_s(|A|,\text{congestion})-\text{fairness/duty penalties}.
\]

The spillover charge for one slot is approximately:

\[
q_t \approx 6s_t\bar V_U+\text{cost of 8–20 queue-minutes},
\]

where \(s_t\) is the estimated per-case spillover and \(\bar V_U\) is the average breach value of the exposed untreated cases.

Allocation is lexicographic:

1. Mandatory regulatory/safety cases.
2. Cases at their last safe start.
3. Cases with positive lower-bound direct value and credible diagnostic actionability.
4. Higher midpoint portfolio value, adjusted for uncertainty and externality.
5. Cases that can safely wait are deferred to gather information and preserve option value.

Swarms are started sequentially. After each slot, pause further deployment if observed queue delay exceeds 20 minutes per slot, estimated spillover exceeds 0.03 per exposed case, or the next case has become stage-ineligible.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Stateful alert lifecycle**

1. **Open candidate:** first qualifying alert is recorded with its evidence, calibrated-risk band, driver, and last safe start.
2. **Acknowledged/cool-down:** after review, suppress duplicate notifications for 90 minutes—three model refreshes—unless a material event occurs.
3. **Persistent but quiet:** the case remains on the portfolio dashboard. Its deadline and stage continue updating, but unchanged predictions do not create new alerts.
4. **Escalation:** page the decision owner when:
   - a material diagnostic artifact arrives;
   - calibrated risk rises by at least 0.10 and persists for two refreshes;
   - missing data are corrected and the alert remains;
   - the case enters its last-safe-start window;
   - queue congestion materially changes the intervention’s externality.
5. **After a swarm:** suppress additional swarm recommendations for the remainder of diagnosis. A repeat requires both a materially new event and senior approval. A risk increase caused only by elapsed time is insufficient.
6. **Exit:** close the candidate at `Diagnosis Complete`, SLA resolution, cancellation, or documented determination that the diagnostic mechanism is not swarm-actionable.

**I-05 versus I-07**

- **I-05:** `p_prev` 0.55 to `p_now` 0.57 with no new event. The third alert is a duplicate refresh, not three independent pieces of evidence. Suppress further notifications. Because it has not received a swarm, it remains eligible for a first intervention if its deadline becomes urgent or new evidence arrives.
- **I-07:** `p_prev` 0.75 to `p_now` 0.72, but a new customer log was uploaded. The lower probability does not negate the fact that the evidence set changed. The case should be reopened and reviewed immediately. If it had already received a swarm, the reviewer would also need to attest that the log materially changes or invalidates the prior diagnostic hypothesis.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-based prioritization can starve lower-value cases.** Contract values may reflect bargaining power rather than customer harm. A purely euro-based score would repeatedly favor Strategic Enterprise cases and could exclude SMEs or public-service incidents.

Safeguards:

- Reserve at least one of every four rolling slots for a duty/urgency lane covering public safety, severe time pressure, and high expected breach reduction regardless of contract value.
- Use a capped or concave value contribution for allocation ranking—for example, cap the ranking contribution at €50,000—while retaining full value in economic reporting.
- Set a maximum wait rule so a repeatedly eligible lower-value case receives review before its last safe start.
- Report both economic value protected and expected breaches prevented; do not optimize only the former.

**Non-Native-Language Support uncertainty could become exclusionary.** Applying a large uncertainty penalty to I-09 and similar cases could systematically deny intervention to a subgroup simply because historical validation was inadequate.

Safeguards:

- Use hierarchical partial pooling rather than treating the small sample as zero effect.
- Require a language-capable reviewer or interpreter in the preflight, not as an extra burden placed on the customer.
- Maintain a governed exploration allocation among otherwise eligible cases so evidence can improve.
- Compare customer burden, intervention rate, diagnosis time, and missed breaches for this subgroup with matched Native-Language Support cases.

**Differential calibration can produce unequal false positives and false negatives.** The same `p_now` has different meaning across SME, Strategic Enterprise, and Public Sector cases.

Safeguards:

- Apply and publish segment-specific calibration mappings and uncertainty intervals.
- Monitor false-negative and false-positive rates conditional on stage, deadline, and intervention eligibility.
- Prohibit a universal probability threshold.
- Review segment corrections for proxy discrimination and avoid using them when sample sizes are too small to support a stable estimate.

**Selective intervention changes future labels.** Successfully treated high-risk cases may not breach. Training naïvely on those outcomes will make the model appear to overpredict and may teach it that intervention-triggering patterns are safe.

Safeguards:

- Log treatment assignment, decision time, stage, propensity, reason, and intervention completion.
- Maintain separate models for untreated risk and outcome under the deployed policy.
- Do not use treated outcomes as if they were untreated labels.
- Use randomized or quasi-random capacity-margin comparisons and doubly robust/off-policy estimators when retraining.

**Spillover may systematically harm untreated groups.** If high-value cases receive swarms while SMEs, language-support cases, or public-sector cases remain in the interrupted queue, the intervention transfers risk to those groups.

Safeguards:

- Record exactly which cases were exposed to each swarm’s queue interruption.
- Monitor cumulative added delay and breach uplift by segment, language context, value band, and public-duty status.
- Impose a congestion cap and pause swarms when exposed-case risk exceeds 0.03 per slot.
- Add backup diagnosis capacity or queue protection when swarm use repeatedly shifts burden to the same subgroup.
- Include spillover disparity in the allocation objective, not only in retrospective reporting.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational monitoring, real time and daily**

Track:

- selected, started, completed, cancelled, and repeated swarms;
- stage at selection and start, including cases that become `Diagnosis Complete` while waiting;
- selection-to-start latency and time left after the swarm;
- specialist interruption time and actual queue delay;
- data failures, imputation frequency, and manual overrides;
- customer burden and new-information yield;
- reserve-slot use, expiration, and cases that lost eligibility while a slot was reserved.

Pause further swarms during a block if observed delay exceeds 20 minutes per slot, estimated spillover exceeds 0.03 per exposed case, or critical model inputs fail.

**Predictive monitoring, weekly with monthly formal review**

Measure by segment, stage, deadline horizon, language-support status, and missing-data status:

- calibration-in-the-large, reliability curves, Brier score, and log loss;
- alert sensitivity, false-positive burden, and missed breaches;
- distribution of `p_prev` to `p_now` changes;
- feature drift and workflow-stage drift;
- residual calibration after the SME and Strategic corrections.

A segment recalibration is triggered when residual calibration error exceeds 0.05 for two adequately sized review windows or a confidence/credible interval excludes zero. Small groups should use hierarchical estimates and longer windows rather than unstable point corrections.

**Causal-effect monitoring**

Because treatment is selective and queue spillovers violate the no-interference assumption:

- Randomize among similarly ranked, non-mandatory cases at the capacity margin where genuine equipoise exists.
- Randomize or rotate swarm saturation across comparable queue-time blocks—for example, one versus two slots—to estimate direct and spillover effects jointly.
- Exclude mandatory regulatory cases from random withholding.
- Record treatment propensity and use doubly robust, time-to-event, or matched estimators as secondary analyses.
- Estimate effects separately by stage, time to deadline, alert driver, segment, and congestion.
- Measure diagnosis duration, SLA breach, customer disruption, rework, and untreated-queue outcomes—not just whether `p_now` falls after treatment.

Effect ranges should be widened or shifted toward zero when transportability fails. If the lower credible bound of portfolio net benefit remains below the estimated externality for two review periods, reduce the number of concurrent slots or tighten the evidence gate.

**Policy-induced drift detection**

Maintain an untreated or randomized sentinel sample and compare:

- pre-intervention risk distributions;
- outcomes for untreated versus treated cases after propensity adjustment;
- workflow paths before and after policy deployment;
- model calibration on untreated-risk labels versus outcomes under the intervention policy.

A model that appears to overpredict only among treated cases is not necessarily miscalibrated; the intervention may have changed the outcome. Retraining must preserve this distinction.

**Capacity monitoring**

Review weekly:

- average and tail queue delay per swarm;
- added breach probability among exposed cases;
- slot utilization and marginal benefit of the third and fourth slots;
- frequency with which the reserve is used;
- high-value candidates that expired while the reserve went unused.

If the reserve remains unused on more than half of days while eligible cases regularly lose their window, release it earlier than 15:00. If regulatory arrivals are common or severe, retain or expand it. If adverse congestion appears after two slots, cap deployment at two until staffing or queue conditions improve.

**Fairness monitoring**

Report, with uncertainty intervals:

- intervention rate among eligible cases;
- time to intervention and probability of expiring untreated;
- false-alarm burden and customer disruption;
- missed-breach rate;
- direct benefit and spillover exposure;

all stratified by segment, language-support status, value band, and public-duty status. A disparity larger than roughly 1.25 in burden or missed-breach rate after adjustment for stage, urgency, and actionability should trigger review, provided sample size is sufficient.

**Changing the allocation rule**

Change the evidence threshold, score cutoff, value cap, reserve policy, or congestion shadow price only after:

1. replaying the proposed rule on logged decisions;
2. performing off-policy or randomized-margin evaluation;
3. testing optimistic, base, and adverse spillover scenarios;
4. conducting a fairness impact review; and
5. running the change in shadow mode before deployment.

Emergency safety changes—such as disabling automatic action during a feature outage or reducing slots under severe congestion—may be made immediately and reviewed afterward.
