## 2. Rule Interpretation

I evaluate each event using the role, delegation, absence, and identity evidence available at the event timestamp. Current master data is not treated as retroactive proof of event-time authority.

Formalized rules:

- **RR1 — Drafter/approver separation**  
  For each case, let `H_draft` be the set of normalized human identities performing `Draft_Change_Request`, and `H_approve` the set performing `Approve_Change`. A violation exists if `H_draft  H_approve` is nonempty. If a service account performed either activity, it is included only if reliable session evidence maps it to a human; otherwise the case is flagged as an identity problem rather than treated as a clean human actor.

- **RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation**  
  For each case, let `H_implement` be the normalized human identities performing `Implement_Change`, and `H_verify` the normalized human identities performing `Verify_Implementation`. A violation exists if `H_implement  H_verify` is nonempty. A service-account verification with weak attribution does not satisfy the human separation control; it is flagged as unresolved.

- **RR3 — High-risk approval authority**  
  For HIGH-risk, non-emergency cases, `Approve_Change` must be performed by either:
  - the standing QM, or
  - a valid QM delegate.

  A QM delegation is valid only if all of the following are true at the approval timestamp:
  1. The delegation interval covers the event.
  2. The delegation scope covers the case scope.
  3. Any principal-absence or formal-unavailability condition is satisfied.
  4. No prohibited subdelegation occurred.
  5. The delegate’s identity is reliably established.

- **RR4 — Emergency substitution**  
  For HIGH-risk emergency cases, the initial approval may be performed by the IC or a valid IC delegate, provided the emergency flag and safety rationale are credible. This is an exception to the normal QM-approval requirement for the initial emergency approval only. A QM or valid QM delegate must then perform `Retrospective_QM_Review` within two business days.

  Business-day counting used here:
  - A business day is Monday through Friday, excluding site holidays.
  - 2026-08-15 and 2026-08-16 are weekend days; no holiday existed in the period.
  - The RR4 clock starts at the emergency `Approve_Change` timestamp.
  - The deadline is the end of the second subsequent business day.
  - Example: an emergency approval on Tuesday 2026-08-11 is due for retrospective QM review by end of Thursday 2026-08-13 under this interpretation. A review on Friday 2026-08-14 is late. A review on Wednesday 2026-08-12 is timely.

- **RR5 — Identity evidence**  
  A service account is not a human identity. It can support conformance only if session evidence maps the action to an authorized human. Strong evidence includes unique MFA/FIDO token, named session login, badge plus terminal-specific authentication, or similar. Weak evidence includes shared workstation, room-level badge only, no re-authentication, or unavailable CCTV. Aliases for the same human must be normalized before SoD checks.

- **RR6 — Suspicion versus proof**  
  Repeated pairs, reciprocal approvals, shared workstations, concentrated handovers, and unusual network edges are indicators requiring investigation. They are not proof of collusion by themselves.

Treatment of a principal acting while on leave:

- A recorded leave or absence is evidence that the principal is absent for delegation-condition purposes.
- If the principal nevertheless performs an action using their own role, the action is not automatically unauthorized solely because they are on leave. The key questions are whether the person retained event-time authority and whether the identity evidence reliably shows the principal personally acted.
- However, if credential sharing cannot be excluded, a principal-on-leave action becomes an identity/availability issue. It may be technically authorized if genuinely performed by the principal, but it is evidentially weaker.
- For delegate actions, if the delegation requires principal absence and there is credible evidence the principal was actively working at or near the delegate event time, the absence condition may be weakened. I evaluate this at the exact delegate event timestamp.

Treatment of later database entries:

- A database record created after an event cannot, by itself, prove that a delegation existed at the event time.
- A later record can corroborate an earlier delegation if there is independent evidence with a reliable earlier timestamp, such as a signed PDF, approved workflow state, email, or migration audit trail.
- If the only evidence is a late database record, I treat it as stale master data or weak evidence, not proof of event-time authority.
- For D-QM-01, the signed PDF timestamped 2026-08-09 is independent evidence preceding the 2026-08-11 and 2026-08-12 approvals, while the 2026-08-12 QMS record is a documentation/migration weakness.
- For D-QM-02, the controlled database record was created before the delegation interval, which is stronger.

def qm_authority(human, t, scope):
    flags = []

    # Standing QM authority.
    for person, start, end in STANDING_QM:
        if human == person and between(t, start, end):
            if between(t, ASHA_LEAVE_START, ASHA_LEAVE_END):
                flags.append("standing_QM_on_recorded_leave")
            if between(t, ASHA_VPN_START, ASHA_VPN_END):
                flags.append("standing_QM_VPN_session")
            return True, "standing_QM", flags

    failures = []
    for d in DELEGATIONS:
        if d["authority"] != "QM" or not d.get("valid", True) or d["delegate"] != human:
            continue
        if not between(t, d["start"], d["end"]):
            continue

        flags = []
        if d["scope"] != scope:
            flags.append("delegation_scope_mismatch")

        if d.get("absence_required", False):
            absent, why = principal_absence(d["principal"], t)
            if not absent:
                flags.append("principal_absence_not_evidenced")
            elif "assumption" in why:
                flags.append("principal_absence_weekend_assumption")
            else:
                flags.append("principal_absence_evidenced")

        blocking = {"delegation_scope_mismatch", "principal_absence_not_evidenced"}
        if not any(flag in blocking for flag in flags):
            return True, d["id"], flags

        failures.append((d["id"], flags))

    return False, None, failures

def ic_authority(human, t, scope, case):

        if d.get("incident"):

                failures.append((d["id"], flags))
            else:
                return True, d["id"], flags

        failures.append((d["id"], flags))

    return False, None, failures

def is_business_day(day):
    # Site calendar: Monday-Friday, no holidays in the relevant period.
    return day.weekday() < 5

def rr4_deadline(approval_ts):
    # Deadline: end of the second subsequent business day after approval.
    day = approval_ts.normalize()
    count = 0
    while count < 2:
        day += pd.Timedelta(days=1)
        if is_business_day(day):
            count += 1
    return day + pd.Timedelta(hours=23, minutes=59, seconds=59)

## 6. Control Improvements

- **Delegation creation and effective-time recording**
  - Require delegations to be created in the QMS before the effective start time, with mandatory fields for principal, delegate, interval, scope, absence condition, and subdelegation permission.
  - Prevent retroactive creation or modification of delegation effective dates without a controlled corrective-action record.
  - Preserve independent evidence such as signed PDFs, workflow approval stamps, and migration logs. During system migrations, require reconciliation of effective dates against source documents.

- **Principal availability and absence evidence**
  - Integrate HR leave, out-of-office records, and delegation activation logic.
  - If a delegation requires principal absence, the system should record the absence basis: annual leave, certified unavailability, documented nonworking weekend, or explicit out-of-office.
  - If a principal on leave actively uses the QM account, the system should flag the event and require a brief justification.

- **Delegate availability and sick leave**
  - Add a control that warns when a delegate’s account is used during certified sick leave or other formal unavailability.
  - Require temporary suspension or reassignment of delegations when a delegate becomes unavailable.
  - Require step-up authentication when a delegate account is used from an unusual location or during an absence interval.

- **Alias management**
  - Maintain a canonical identity map synchronized with HR/IAM.
  - Prohibit multiple active human aliases unless documented and time-boxed.
  - Normalize aliases in process mining, conformance checking, and audit reports before SoD checks.
  - Investigate historical cases where aliases may have hidden self-approval or reciprocal approval patterns.

- **Individual authentication and service-account control**
  - Prohibit human actions from being recorded under generic service accounts unless strong session attribution exists.
  - Require named user login plus MFA/FIDO for approval, verification, and retrospective review activities.
  - For shared terminals, require individual re-authentication before each high-risk action.
  - Log workstation, badge, token, IP, session start/end, and re-authentication events in a tamper-evident audit trail.

- **Emergency change control**
  - Require emergency changes to carry a structured incident ID, safety rationale, and IC authority record.
  - Link emergency approvals to the specific incident, such as CI-44, rather than only to a broad scope like `cooling_system`.
  - Automatically calculate and monitor the RR4 retrospective review deadline using the site business calendar.
  - Escalate if retrospective QM review is late or performed by someone without the correct QM delegation scope.

- **Segregation-of-duties expansion**
  - RR1 and RR2 cover drafter/approver and implementer/verifier, but CHG-509 shows a gap: the same person approved and verified. Consider adding a rule that the approver should not be the sole verifier for HIGH-risk changes.
  - Consider restricting closure authority for cases where the closer drafted or implemented the change, unless an independent review is recorded.

- **Pair rotation and independence**
  - Introduce rotation or recusal rules for HIGH-risk drafting and approval pairs, especially where one pair dominates a production cell.
  - Require periodic independent sampling of repeated drafter/approver pairs.
  - Avoid treating repeated collaboration as misconduct by itself; use it as a risk indicator.

- **Network monitoring and investigation workflow**
  - Use PM4Py handover-of-work and working-together networks as periodic monitoring evidence.
  - Investigate high-weight edges only when combined with other risk factors: self-approval, invalid delegations, service-account use, absence conflicts, or poor change outcomes.
  - Maintain an investigation playbook that distinguishes data quality issues, identity problems, valid substitutions, and unauthorized acts.

- **Evidential retention**
  - Retain CCTV, badge logs, VPN logs, MFA/FIDO assertions, workstation session logs, delegation documents, QMS audit trails, and emergency rationales for a defined investigation period.
  - Ensure shared-workstation rooms have sufficient logging to attribute high-risk actions to individuals.
  - Preserve original signed delegation artifacts and migration reconciliation records.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

The apparent nonconformance is mixed. Using the primary classifications:

- **Valid substitution with documentation or condition caveats:** CHG-501 and CHG-509.
- **Primary unauthorized authority or SoD failures:** CHG-502, CHG-505, CHG-507, CHG-508, and CHG-510.
- **Primary data/identity problems:** CHG-503 and CHG-506.
- **Indeterminate due to identity/leave uncertainty:** CHG-504.

Overall, a substantial portion of the apparent nonconformance is genuine authority failure or control bypass: self-approvals, an invalid emergency subdelegation, a HIGH-risk approval by a non-QM, and emergency retrospective reviews without the correct QM scope. Another meaningful portion is identity/data quality: service-account verification, alias normalization, late delegation records, weekend absence interpretation, and disputed account use during leave. There is also organizational-pattern risk from repeated Clara/Ben collaboration, reciprocal low-risk approvals, shared workstations, and service-account usage, but these patterns are indicators and not proof of collusion.

Uncertainty is highest where identity evidence is ambiguous: Asha’s leave-day VPN approval, Ben’s account use during sick leave, and the CHG-503 service-account verification. These cannot be resolved into authorized or unauthorized without stronger session, badge, MFA, or CCTV evidence.

The three highest-priority investigations are:

1. **CHG-510** — invalid emergency approval through prohibited subdelegation, drafter/approver self-approval, and retrospective QM review without valid cooling-system QM scope. This is the clearest safety-relevant authority failure.
2. **CHG-506** — Ben’s account used during certified sick leave to approve a HIGH-risk production change under a QM delegation. This requires credential-use, badge, workstation, and delegation-suspension investigation.
3. **CHG-503** — service-account verification on a workstation associated with the implementer, with weak individual attribution. This directly threatens implementer/verifier independence and requires session-level forensic evidence.
