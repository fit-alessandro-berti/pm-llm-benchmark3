# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

This review treats the uploaded task as the sole evidence base and does not infer hidden traces, hidden labels, or missing preprocessing choices beyond what is stated. 

I distinguish four intended uses:

| Use case                  | What the model is for                                                       | Acceptable trade-off                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Exploration               | Finding frequent behavior, variants, rough bottlenecks, and candidate rules | Higher fitness and tolerance for missing events can be acceptable, but the model must be labeled non-enforcing.                                            |
| Conformance enforcement   | Deciding whether a case violates policy                                     | Mandatory controls must not be optionalized. Precision against curated negative traces matters more than aggregate fitness.                                |
| Simulation / forecasting  | Estimating future paths, capacity, and effect of changes                    | The model needs realistic branching semantics, not merely replay of historic frequent variants. Both over-permissiveness and over-enumeration are harmful. |
| Stakeholder communication | Explaining what the process requires and where uncertainty exists           | Simplicity is valuable, but not if it hides mandatory controls or data gaps.                                                                               |

Assumptions I use:

| Assumption                                                                                                            | Why needed                                                               | Effect                                                                                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| A1. “Accepts” means exact language acceptance by the process-tree expression, not “can be aligned with nonzero cost.” | The behavior probes ask for model acceptance.                            | Alignment fitness may be high even when exact acceptance is false.                                           |
| A2. `+` is interpreted as parallel/AND, `X` as exclusive choice, `->` as sequence, and `tau` as a silent skip.        | This is standard process-tree semantics and matches the PM4Py reminders. | Model S executes each optional branch at most once, in arbitrary order.                                      |
| A3. Supplier Review requiredness cannot be fully judged without the external/internal attribute.                      | P3 says that attribute was missing from the discovery log.               | Absence of Supplier Review is not automatically compliant; it is data-incomplete.                            |
| A4. Correction semantics require extra data or a post-hoc rule.                                                       | P6 says the affected-check attribute is missing.                         | A model may allow correction/recheck patterns structurally but cannot certify that the right check repeated. |

The key governance point is that no single metric should decide the model choice. A high-fitness model may be unsafe if it fits by skipping mandatory controls. A high-precision model may be brittle if it memorizes frequent variants and rejects legitimate recombinations.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

The strongest case for Model S is that it is compact, fast, explainable, and robust to missing events. It naturally tolerates absence of Supplier Review, which is important because Supplier Review may be required only for external orders and the external/internal attribute was missing. It also permits different orderings of Credit Check and Technical Review and different combinations of Supplier Review, Expedite Review, and approval type without enumerating every variant. For exploratory process discovery, it is a useful “what activities tend to co-occur before approval?” model.

The strongest case against Model S is that it makes all four reviews optional and unordered. That is not merely noise tolerance; it changes the meaning of mandatory controls. P2 says Credit Check and Technical Review are mandatory before approval. P4 says Expedite Review must follow both mandatory checks. Model S violates both because each review is wrapped in `X(activity, tau)` inside a parallel block.

Concrete undesirable or policy-ambiguous traces admitted by Model S:

| Trace admitted by Model S                                                                          | Why admitted                                                  | Why problematic                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order                                                  | All review branches choose `tau`.                             | Skips Credit Check and Technical Review; forbidden by P2.                       |
| Register Dossier, Auto Approval, Release Order                                                     | All review branches choose `tau`; Auto Approval chosen.       | Same mandatory-check violation, with automated release path.                    |
| Register Dossier, Credit Check, Manager Approval, Release Order                                    | Technical Review branch chooses `tau`.                        | Skips Technical Review.                                                         |
| Register Dossier, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order                                   | Credit Check branch chooses `tau`.                            | Skips Credit Check.                                                             |
| Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order | Parallel block permits Expedite Review before the checks.     | Violates P4 because Expedite Review must follow both mandatory checks.          |
| Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Manager Approval, Release Order                                 | Credit and Technical choose `tau`; Supplier chosen.           | Supplier Review cannot substitute for mandatory checks.                         |
| Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Expedite Review, Auto Approval, Release Order                   | Credit and Technical skipped; Supplier and Expedite selected. | Combines missing mandatory checks with Expedite Review before mandatory checks. |
| Register Dossier, Technical Review, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Auto Approval, Release Order    | Parallel block permits this order.                            | Expedite Review occurs before Credit Check has completed.                       |

Permissiveness caused by intentional noise tolerance:

| Intentional tolerance                                      | Defensible?                                                                                                          |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Supplier Review optional                                   | Partly defensible because the external/internal attribute is missing and some Supplier Review events may be missing. |
| Allowing both approval types                               | Defensible because P5 allows exactly one of Manager Approval or Auto Approval.                                       |
| Allowing Credit Check and Technical Review in either order | Defensible and required by P2.                                                                                       |

Accidental semantic flaws:

| Flaw                                            | Why it is accidental, not acceptable tolerance                                          |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Credit Check optional                           | Directly contradicts P2.                                                                |
| Technical Review optional                       | Directly contradicts P2.                                                                |
| Expedite Review allowed before mandatory checks | Directly contradicts P4.                                                                |
| No Correct Dossier behavior                     | Misses P6 entirely.                                                                     |
| No suspicious-repeat logic                      | Cannot represent P7, although exact Model S also does not allow repeated checks at all. |

Model S may be retained for exploration, but it is unsafe for compliance enforcement.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

The strongest case for Model C is that it tries to encode concrete observed variants rather than allowing arbitrary skips. It requires Register Dossier first and Release Order last in all branches. It includes both possible orders of the two mandatory checks for some Manager Approval paths. It includes Supplier Review and Expedite Review variants, one correction variant, and one Auto Approval variant. Its high training precision indicates that, near the training log, it allows fewer traces than Model S.

The strongest case against Model C is that it is a branch enumerator masquerading as a policy model. It lists frequent variants but does not represent the underlying rules compositionally. This creates several problems.

First, it rejects unseen combinations of known behavior. The feedback says holdout cases combine Supplier Review, Auto Approval, and alternate check order in ways absent from training. Model C rejects those combinations not because policy forbids them, but because the branch was not enumerated.

Second, it has duplicate-label and impact-analysis problems. The same activities appear repeatedly across branches. The reported duplicate-label count is 33, and IT architecture explicitly objects that duplicate labels and branch enumeration make ownership and impact analysis difficult.

Third, its correction handling is too narrow. It has one correction branch:

`Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Correct Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order`

That branch assumes both checks repeat, in fixed order, followed by Manager Approval. F6 says correction may require repeating only Credit Check, only Technical Review, or both. Model C hides that decision rather than representing it.

Fourth, the holdout degradation is severe: 0.973 training fitness to 0.744 holdout fitness. That is consistent with overfitting, split sensitivity, or failure to represent legitimate recombinations.

Fifth, high precision may be an artifact. Because precision was computed only on observed positive traces and no curated negative suite was used, C’s high precision does not prove that it captures compliance semantics. It may simply memorize frequent training variants and reject everything else.

Finally, the “audit-ready” claim is not justified. A model is audit-ready when it can explain which policy rule is violated, handle legitimate but rare cases, and remain maintainable under policy changes. Model C is closer to a frozen variant catalog than a policy model.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

| Rank | Pathology                                            | Decision impact                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---: | ---------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|    1 | Fitness rewarded by optionalizing mandatory behavior | Very high. Model S’s excellent fitness is partly explained by making Credit Check and Technical Review skippable, which is unacceptable for compliance.                                                                |
|    2 | Positive-log-only precision                          | Very high. F5 says no curated negative behavior suite was used. C’s high precision does not prove that it rejects the right bad behavior, and S’s low precision does not identify which unsafe behaviors are admitted. |
|    3 | Missing-event bias                                   | Very high. Supplier Review may be missing, and the external/internal attribute is absent. A model can be rewarded or punished for behavior caused by data incompleteness rather than business reality.                 |
|    4 | Unseen-combination blindness                         | High. C rejects legitimate recombinations of known activities, especially Supplier Review + Auto Approval + alternate check order.                                                                                     |
|    5 | Train/holdout leakage or split sensitivity           | High. The teams claim the same 80/20 split but did not provide seeds, exact cases, or preprocessing. The large C holdout drop may depend heavily on split composition.                                                 |
|    6 | Alignment-cost sensitivity                           | High. Different costs for skipping mandatory checks, inserting Supplier Review, or moving duplicate labels can change fitness and diagnostics materially.                                                              |
|    7 | Silent-transition effects                            | High. S uses `tau` to skip four review activities. Silent skips can hide the difference between tolerated missing observations and true policy violations.                                                             |
|    8 | Generalization rewarded by excessive permissiveness  | Medium-high. S’s high token-based generalization may mean “admits many possible traces,” not “generalizes according to policy.”                                                                                        |
|    9 | Duplicated-label effects                             | Medium-high. C’s duplicate labels can create ambiguous alignments, inflated search space, and weak diagnostic ownership.                                                                                               |
|   10 | Runtime confounding by model size                    | Medium. C’s runtime is much slower, but the cause may be model size, duplicate labels, alignment settings, or conversion details.                                                                                      |
|   11 | Stakeholder-score methodology                        | Medium. The comprehension scores are directionally useful, but informal 1–5 scores without protocol are weak evidence.                                                                                                 |

The highest-impact pathologies are those that can reverse a governance decision: optional mandatory controls, absence of negative testing, and data incompleteness.

# 7. Third Alternative

I recommend a hybrid third alternative: a block-structured procedural backbone plus explicit declarative/data checks. The procedural model should encode the stable control-flow skeleton. The declarative checks should handle conditional Supplier Review and correction affected-check semantics, because the discovery log lacks the attributes needed to decide those rules purely from control flow.

Compact formal description:

```text
Register Dossier
then Credit Check and Technical Review, both mandatory, either order
then zero or more correction cycles:
    Correct Dossier
    then repeat Credit Check, Technical Review, or both
then optional Supplier Review and optional Expedite Review, either order
then exactly one of Manager Approval or Auto Approval
then Release Order
```

Main PM4Py model:

```python
import pm4py

model_third_string = """
->(
  'Register Dossier',
  +('Credit Check', 'Technical Review'),
  *(
    tau,
    ->(
      'Correct Dossier',
      X(
        'Credit Check',
        'Technical Review',
        +('Credit Check', 'Technical Review')
      )
    )
  ),
  +(
    X('Supplier Review', tau),
    X('Expedite Review', tau)
  ),
  X('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval'),
  'Release Order'
)
"""

model_third = pm4py.parse_process_tree(model_third_string)

net_third, im_third, fm_third = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)
pm4py.check_soundness(net_third, im_third, fm_third, print_diagnostics=True)
```

Declarative and data-aware policy checks:

```python
APPROVALS = {"Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"}
MANDATORY_CHECKS = {"Credit Check", "Technical Review"}

policy_constraints = {
    "first_activity": "Register Dossier",
    "last_activity": "Release Order",
    "mandatory_before_approval": ["Credit Check", "Technical Review"],
    "approval": {
        "exactly_one_of": ["Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"],
        "must_precede": "Release Order",
    },
    "supplier_review": {
        "required_if_case_attribute": {"external_internal": "external"},
        "missing_attribute_verdict": "data_incomplete_not_compliant_by_default",
    },
    "expedite_review": {
        "optional": True,
        "must_follow_all": ["Credit Check", "Technical Review"],
        "must_precede_any": ["Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"],
    },
    "correction": {
        "may_repeat": True,
        "each_correct_dossier_requires_at_least_one_recheck_of": [
            "Credit Check",
            "Technical Review",
        ],
        "rechecked_set_should_equal_affected_checks_when_known": True,
        "missing_affected_check_verdict": "conditional_or_needs_review",
    },
    "repeated_mandatory_check": {
        "forbidden_without_preceding_correct_dossier": True,
    },
}


def assess_policy_trace(trace, case_attrs=None):
    """
    Returns a conservative governance verdict for one case trace.

    trace: list[str], e.g. ["Register Dossier", "Credit Check", ...]
    case_attrs may include:
        {
          "external_internal": "external" | "internal" | None,
          "affected_checks_by_correction_index": {
              3: {"Technical Review"}
          }
        }
    """
    case_attrs = case_attrs or {}
    issues = []
    unknowns = []

    if not trace:
        return {"verdict": "forbidden", "issues": ["empty trace"], "unknowns": []}

    if trace[0] != "Register Dossier":
        issues.append("Register Dossier is not first")

    if trace[-1] != "Release Order":
        issues.append("Release Order is not last")

    approval_positions = [i for i, a in enumerate(trace) if a in APPROVALS]
    if len(approval_positions) != 1:
        issues.append(f"expected exactly one approval, found {len(approval_positions)}")
        approval_pos = None
    else:
        approval_pos = approval_positions[0]
        if approval_pos > len(trace) - 2:
            issues.append("approval does not precede Release Order")

    if approval_pos is not None:
        before_approval = trace[:approval_pos]
        for check in MANDATORY_CHECKS:
            if check not in before_approval:
                issues.append(f"{check} missing before approval")

        for i, activity in enumerate(trace):
            if activity == "Expedite Review":
                if not all(check in trace[:i] for check in MANDATORY_CHECKS):
                    issues.append("Expedite Review occurs before both mandatory checks")
                if i > approval_pos:
                    issues.append("Expedite Review occurs after approval")

        external_internal = case_attrs.get("external_internal")
        if external_internal == "external":
            if "Supplier Review" not in before_approval:
                issues.append("external case lacks Supplier Review before approval")
        elif external_internal is None:
            unknowns.append(
                "external/internal attribute missing; Supplier Review requiredness cannot be certified"
            )

    # Repeated mandatory checks require a preceding correction since the previous
    # occurrence of that same check.
    last_seen_check_pos = {}
    for i, activity in enumerate(trace):
        if activity in MANDATORY_CHECKS:
            if activity in last_seen_check_pos:
                previous = last_seen_check_pos[activity]
                if "Correct Dossier" not in trace[previous + 1 : i]:
                    issues.append(
                        f"{activity} repeated at position {i} without intervening Correct Dossier"
                    )
            last_seen_check_pos[activity] = i

    affected_map = case_attrs.get("affected_checks_by_correction_index", {})

    for i, activity in enumerate(trace):
        if activity != "Correct Dossier":
            continue

        # Look until the next correction, approval, or release.
        barriers = [
            j
            for j in range(i + 1, len(trace))
            if trace[j] in APPROVALS
            or trace[j] == "Release Order"
            or trace[j] == "Correct Dossier"
        ]
        end = min(barriers) if barriers else len(trace)
        rechecked = [a for a in trace[i + 1 : end] if a in MANDATORY_CHECKS]
        rechecked_set = set(rechecked)

        if not rechecked_set:
            issues.append(
                f"Correct Dossier at position {i} is not followed by a mandatory recheck"
            )

        affected = affected_map.get(i)
        if affected is None:
            unknowns.append(
                f"affected-check attribute missing for Correct Dossier at position {i}"
            )
        elif rechecked_set != set(affected):
            issues.append(
                f"after Correct Dossier at position {i}, rechecked {sorted(rechecked_set)} "
                f"but affected checks were {sorted(set(affected))}"
            )

    if issues:
        verdict = "forbidden"
    elif unknowns:
        verdict = "conditional_needs_data_or_review"
    else:
        verdict = "valid"

    return {"verdict": verdict, "issues": issues, "unknowns": unknowns}
```

Explicit semantics:

| Requirement                                 | Third alternative semantics                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mandatory Credit Check and Technical Review | Both must occur before approval. They may occur in either order.                                                                                           |
| Supplier Review                             | Optional in the procedural backbone only because the external/internal attribute is missing. A data check requires it when `external_internal = external`. |
| Expedite Review                             | Optional, but if present it must occur after both mandatory checks and before approval. It may occur before or after Supplier Review.                      |
| Exactly-one approval                        | The procedural model uses `X('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval')`; the policy checker also verifies exactly one approval.                                 |
| Correct Dossier                             | May repeat before approval. Each correction must be followed by Credit Check, Technical Review, or both.                                                   |
| Repeated mandatory check                    | Forbidden unless there is an intervening Correct Dossier.                                                                                                  |
| Affected-check semantics                    | If the affected-check attribute is present, the repeated check set must match it. If absent, the case is conditional, not fully certified.                 |

Limitations:

| Limitation                                                                           | Consequence                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Missing external/internal attribute                                                  | The third model cannot prove whether absence of Supplier Review is compliant. It must mark such cases data-incomplete unless another reliable source supplies the attribute.                       |
| Missing affected-check attribute                                                     | The model can allow structurally plausible correction/recheck behavior but cannot certify that only the affected check repeated.                                                                   |
| Correction placed before Supplier/Expedite block                                     | This is a simplifying assumption. If real corrections can occur after Supplier Review or Expedite Review, the backbone should be revised using evidence.                                           |
| Duplicate labels remain for Credit Check and Technical Review in the correction loop | This is much smaller and more semantic than Model C’s branch enumeration, but diagnostics should still distinguish initial checks from rechecks through event attributes or lifecycle annotations. |

# 9. Validation Experiments

The evaluation program should report metrics separately and include both model-language tests and policy-classification tests. It should not invent a single aggregate score.

Reproducibility requirements:

| Requirement                   | Implementation                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Published preprocessing       | Publish case filtering, event sorting, duplicate handling, lifecycle treatment, missing-activity policy, and attribute extraction.                                         |
| Reproducible splits           | Use fixed seeds and publish train/holdout case IDs.                                                                                                                        |
| Attribute-stratified holdout  | Stratify by Supplier presence, approval type, check order, correction presence, and Expedite presence. If external/internal later becomes available, stratify by it.       |
| Curated negative suite        | Include B3, B4, B8, correction-without-recheck, double approval, release-before-approval, approval-before-checks.                                                          |
| Missing-event perturbations   | Remove Supplier Review and possibly one mandatory check under controlled probabilities to see which models confuse missingness with compliance.                            |
| Duplicate-event perturbations | Inject repeated Credit Check or Technical Review with and without Correct Dossier.                                                                                         |
| Unseen-combination tests      | Generate valid combinations not present in training, such as Supplier + Auto Approval + alternate check order.                                                             |
| Alignment-cost sensitivity    | Re-run alignments under multiple costs for skips, insertions, repeated labels, and silent moves.                                                                           |
| Soundness/reachability        | Check the converted Petri nets and inspect whether key states are reachable.                                                                                               |
| Stakeholder comprehension     | Use documented tasks: identify allowed path, forbidden path, correction rule, Supplier condition, and update impact. Record accuracy and time, not only subjective scores. |
| Rejection rule                | Reject any compliance model that accepts critical negative traces, rejects core valid probes, or cannot explain Supplier/correction uncertainty.                           |

def prepare_event_log(df):
    """
    Publish this exact preprocessing with the evaluation.
    """
    df = df.copy()
    df[TIME_COL] = pd.to_datetime(df[TIME_COL], utc=True, errors="coerce")
    df = df.dropna(subset=[CASE_COL, ACT_COL, TIME_COL])
    df = df.sort_values([CASE_COL, TIME_COL, ACT_COL])
    formatted = pm4py.format_dataframe(

        case_id=CASE_COL,

        timestamp_key=TIME_COL,

def case_features(df):
    rows = []
    for case_id, g in df.groupby(CASE_COL):
        acts = list(g.sort_values(TIME_COL)[ACT_COL])
        approval = next((a for a in acts if a in {"Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"}), None)
        rows.append(
            {
                CASE_COL: case_id,
                "has_supplier": "Supplier Review" in acts,
                "has_expedite": "Expedite Review" in acts,

                "check_order": (
                    "credit_first"
                    if acts.index("Credit Check") < acts.index("Technical Review")
                    else "technical_first"
                )
                if "Credit Check" in acts and "Technical Review" in acts
                else "missing_check",
            }
        )
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)


def reproducible_case_split(df, seed=20260805, holdout_fraction=0.20):
    """
    Simple seed-controlled split.
    For final governance, replace with stratified splitting over case_features.
    """
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    case_ids = np.array(sorted(df[CASE_COL].unique()))
    rng.shuffle(case_ids)
    n_holdout = int(round(len(case_ids) * holdout_fraction))
    holdout_cases = set(case_ids[:n_holdout])
    train_cases = set(case_ids[n_holdout:])
    return train_cases, holdout_cases


def split_log(df, train_cases, holdout_cases):
    train_df = df[df[CASE_COL].isin(train_cases)].copy()
    holdout_df = df[df[CASE_COL].isin(holdout_cases)].copy()
    return prepare_event_log(train_df), prepare_event_log(holdout_df)

def perturb_remove_activity(df, activity, probability, seed):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    df = df.copy()

def perturb_duplicate_check_without_correction(df, probability, seed):

| Accepts B3 or B4 exactly                                                        | Reject for compliance use regardless of aggregate fitness.                              |

# 10. Governance Recommendation

Model S should be allowed only for exploration and stakeholder brainstorming. It should not be used for compliance enforcement, audit sign-off, automated monitoring, or case rejection because it admits traces that skip mandatory Credit Check and Technical Review and allows Expedite Review before those checks.

Model C should not be used as the compliance model either. It may be retained temporarily as a historical frequent-variant catalog, but its low holdout fitness, branch enumeration, duplicate labels, poor maintainability, and rejection of plausible valid recombinations make it unsafe for operational enforcement. I would retire it from production decision support unless it is explicitly labeled “training-variant inventory, not policy.”

The third alternative should proceed to validation as the candidate governance model. It is safer than S because it preserves mandatory checks and ordering constraints. It is more generalizable than C because it composes rules instead of enumerating branches. It is also more honest because it marks Supplier Review and correction affected-check questions as data-dependent rather than pretending the control-flow log can decide them.

Deployment conditions for the third alternative:

| Condition                  | Required before compliance deployment                                                                                                                   |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reproducible evaluation    | Seeds, preprocessing, split IDs, alignment settings, and PM4Py version published.                                                                       |
| Negative-suite performance | Must reject critical negative traces such as skipped checks, Expedite before checks, repeated check without correction, and double approval.            |
| Valid-probe performance    | Must accept B1, B2, B5, and B7 under the stated policy assumptions.                                                                                     |
| Data attributes            | External/internal and affected-check attributes should be captured or replaced by documented reliable proxies.                                          |
| Uncertainty handling       | Cases with missing required attributes must be routed to manual review or data-quality resolution.                                                      |
| Stakeholder validation     | Stakeholders must correctly explain the model’s mandatory checks, Supplier condition, correction rule, and approval choice under a documented protocol. |
| Runtime                    | Alignment and policy checks must be feasible at expected case volume.                                                                                   |

# 11. Dissenting View

A strong rebuttal is that the recommendation over-engineers a process where the available log is already known to be incomplete. Model S is honest about that incompleteness: it avoids false noncompliance alarms when events are missing, attributes are absent, or valid behavior changes faster than governance documentation. For exploration and operational monitoring, rejecting fewer cases may be preferable to burdening teams with manual reviews caused by data gaps. Model C, meanwhile, is valuable precisely because it reflects frequent observed behavior and has high training precision. A governance board could reasonably argue that unseen combinations should not be automatically normalized. If Supplier Review plus Auto Approval was absent from training, the safer audit stance may be to treat it as unapproved until formally added. The proposed third model risks granting legitimacy to combinations that are merely plausible, not proven. Its correction logic is also conditional on an affected-check attribute that does not exist, so it may create an illusion of rigor while still depending on manual review. In practice, a simple exploratory model plus a conservative variant whitelist might be easier to govern.

Evidence that would change my decision: a documented policy saying Model S is only exploratory and never enforcing; curated negative tests showing C rejects all critical violations while valid unseen combinations are truly unauthorized; stable external/internal and affected-check data proving the third model’s conditional logic can be automated; and reproducible splits showing C’s holdout degradation was an artifact rather than structural brittleness.
