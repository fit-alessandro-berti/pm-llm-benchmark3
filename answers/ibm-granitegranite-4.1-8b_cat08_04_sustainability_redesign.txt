# System boundary and assumptions
**Environmental scope:** The analysis focuses on the cradle-to-gate phase of spare-part orders, covering energy consumption at machines (CNC, heat treat, inspection), material usage (including scrap), transport emissions, and packaging material. Endoflife disposal of returned parts is outside the boundary.  

**Inside the boundary:**  
- Order receipt, availability check, picking/planning, CNC machining, heat treatment, inspection, optional rework, packaging, shipping, delivery confirmation, and closure.  
- All route modes (parcel ground, air, dedicated van, consolidated truck) and their associated COe emissions.  
- Batch processing decisions (batch size, wait time) and their impact on energy per unit and obsolescence.  
- Packaging choices (standard vs. reinforced) and rework loops.

**Outside the boundary:**  
- Freight after final delivery (customer use, disposal).  
- Environmental impact of material extraction upstream of the distributor.  

**Uncertainty remaining:**  
- Precise rebound effects when batch sizes are increased (forecast errors could lead to excess inventory).  
- Longterm compliance monitoring of dynamic batching caps as demand patterns evolve.  
- Exact emission factors for “dedicated van” empty runs, which vary by carrier contract.

# Rebound and displacement risks
1. **Batching rebound:** Increasing batch size lowers energy per unit but raises obsolescence risk; misforecasted demand could create excess inventory, offsetting energy savings with storage emissions.  
2. **Transport mode shift:** Switching many air shipments to ground may free capacity for other carriers, potentially increasing congestion or shifting emissions to road freight elsewhere.  
3. **Packaging overconsolidation:** Doubling reinforced packaging material improves damage resistance but adds weight, possibly raising transport emissions for heavy parts if ground shipping is used.  
4. **Rebound demand:** Lower perceived shipping costs (cheaper ground) might encourage order volume growth, offsetting perorder savings.  
5. **Rework displacement:** Improving inspection quality may reduce rework on CNC_B but could shift defects to heattreatment stages, affecting that stage’s scrap rates.  
6. **Servicelevel displacement:** Tightening promised lead times (S8) can push some Regular MTO customers to competitors offering faster air service, risking lost market share and increased overall emissions elsewhere.

# Balanced recommendation
**Redesign package (selected interventions):**  

1. **Dynamic batching with caps (S2)** for Madetoorder—targets the largest energy consumer while preserving service windows through segmentspecific wait limits.  
2. **Green shipping recommender (S1)** for Standardstock Servicecontract—to immediately cut air emissions without breaching the 85% ontime probability policy (SP1).  
3. **Consolidated truck scheduling (S4)** for nonurgent Standardstock Regular orders—captures a sizeable share of transport volume with a modest cycletime tradeoff and meets the 85% ontime threshold.  
4. **Packagingroute compatibility check (S5)**—prevents rework and reduces material waste, directly supporting quality policy Q1 and resilience R1 (alternative routes remain).  
5. **Inspection quality gate (S6)** for heavy Madetoorder parts—lowers scrap and rework, reinforcing service reliability for critical contracts.  
6. **Shared emergency courier lanes (S7)**—addresses the highimpact Criticaldowntime segment without excessive dedicatedvan usage, staying within sustainability policy SP2 when justified.  

**Why this balances:**  

- **Environmental impact:** Energy savings from batching and transport consolidation dominate the carbon footprint reduction; transportation emissions drop by ~3060% across segments.  
- **Service quality:** All interventions keep promisedleadtime adherence above the 85% threshold required by SP1 and do not exceed the latedelivery risk increase of 12percentage points (acceptable under the “no unacceptable deterioration” constraint).  
- **Quality:** Rework and material scrap are reduced via batching efficiency and inspection gates, meeting Q1 standards and lowering damagerelated returns.  
- **Resilience:** Alternative routing options remain (consolidated trucks and shared emergency lanes) satisfying R1; no single mode becomes a bottleneck.  

The package avoids the lowestemission but potentially servicedisruptive option (e.g., forcing all air shipments to ground for all segments without capacity checks) and respects the precise policy and evidence constraints provided.

# Validation and governance plan
1. **Conformance monitoring (Postimplementation):**  
   - **SP1 adherence:** Flag any Standardstock Servicecontract order where ground/consolidated shipment probability falls below 85% (using `route_mode`, `promised_lead_time_hours`). Automated alerts trigger a compliance review.  
   - **SP2 justification:** Ensure every dedicatedvan shipment carries a documented `Emergency justification` event; otherwise raise a deviation for corrective action.  
   - **Q1 packaging compliance:** Count `rework_flag` occurrences for >8kg parts; any increase >5% over baseline initiates a rootcause analysis.  

2. **Segmentlevel service monitoring:**  
   - Track `Late delivery` percentages, `promised_lead_time_hours` vs. observed, and average cycle time per segment (Standardstock, MTO, Critical).  
   - Set threshold alerts (e.g., >10% late deliveries for any segment) to prompt process adjustments.  

3. **Sustainability KPI dashboard:**  
   - Realtime `energy_kwh/order`, `transport_kgCOe/order`, `material_scrap_kg/order`.  
   - Compare against preintervention baselines; visual trend lines guide continuous improvement.  

4. **Exception governance log (S12):**  
   - Log all overrides or policy exemptions with reason, duration, and impact. Review quarterly to assess whether exceptions are justified or indicate systemic issues.  

5. **Continuous processmining feedback:**  
   - Rerun the eventlog mining weekly to surface emergent subpaths (e.g., new rushed inspections) and verify that interventions remain effective under evolving demand patterns.  

By coupling **automated conformance checks** with **periodic KPI reviews** and **exception logging**, the distributor can sustain the achieved environmental gains while dynamically adapting to service, quality, and resilience pressures.
