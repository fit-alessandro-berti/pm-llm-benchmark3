## 3. Intervention options

**O1 — Green shipping recommender (S1).** Target: SP1-violating air shipments (22% of Standard-stock Service-contract, ~420 orders; visible as `Package standard  Ship air`, 620 cases). Mechanism: at the ship decision, predict on-time probability from `promised_lead_time_hours`, historical route reliability and segment; default to ground/consolidated when p  85%, keep air otherwise. Service: preserved by design via the probability guard; residual risk if predictions miscalibrate. Quality: unaffected. Resilience: R1 alternate-route list maintained; air stays available as escape valve. Monitor: avoidable-air ratio, prediction calibration, late% for shifted cohort.

**O2 — Priority pick + ground substitution (S4).** Target: same segment; the enabler making O1 feasible without breaking 18h medians (Standard-stock Service-contract median 18h vs Regular 31h shows the SLA is picking-driven, not purely transport-driven). Mechanism: wave-planning slots Service-contract picks ahead; ground replaces air when slack suffices. Service: protects promise dates; monitor p90 (41h today). Quality: none. Resilience: positive — removes air-capacity dependence for routine SLAs. Monitor: pick-start timestamps vs receipt, dock-to-ship times.

**O3 — Dynamic batching with segment-specific wait caps (S2).** Target: `Plan production  Batch wait  CNC production` (2,910 of 3,780 production starts enter batch wait) and heat_treat. Mechanism: close a batch when either target size (8–12, near the 1.3 kWh/item optimum) or a wait cap (e.g., strict for Service-contract MTO, looser for Regular) is hit; avoid chasing size 12–20 everywhere because waits grow 4h38h and obsolescence hits 6%. Service: caps bound the 1.7× heat-treat late risk to tolerable levels. Quality: unchanged. Resilience: mild positive (smoother WIP). Monitor: batch-wait p90 vs caps, energy/item, 14-day obsolescence.

**O4 — Prefer CNC_A for Made-to-order when slack allows (S3).** Target: the `MTO  CNC_B  inspection  rework  ground` variant (32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap, 160h, 32% late). Mechanism: route to CNC_A when queue depth + processing fit the promise; CNC_B rework rate (18% vs 9%) means every avoided loop saves a full CNC + possibly heat-treatment re-run. Service: cycle time improves via fewer 160h spirals, though CNC_A machines slower — queue guardrails essential. Quality: direct improvement. Resilience: keep both machines qualified to preserve redundancy. Monitor: per-machine rework rates, queue projections accuracy.

**O5 — Targeted inspection quality gate (S7).** Target: high-rework machine/part combinations behind `Inspection  Rework production` (470) and rushed inspections that push defects downstream into packaging rework and customer returns. Mechanism: mandatory deeper inspection only for flagged combos; prevents multiple future energy/material-consuming loops. Service: slight queueing (inspection_cell congestion is documented) — scope narrowly. Quality: strongly positive. Resilience: neutral. Monitor: escaped-defect/return rate, inspection-cell queue p90.

**O6 — Packaging–route compatibility pre-check (S6).** Target: 550 `Rework packaging` loops (`Package standard  Rework packaging` 260; `Package reinforced  Rework packaging` 360) caused by route/geometry mismatch, plus part of Q1 violations correlating with return loops. Mechanism: validate pack type against `route_mode` and geometry **before** packing. Crucially, this does *not* remove reinforced packaging for MTO parts >8 kg — its 40% damage-return reduction outweighs doubled `package_material_kg`. Service: removes loop days; Quality: fewer damages; Resilience: neutral. Monitor: packaging-rework frequency, damage-return rate by pack/route pair.

**O7 — Scheduled consolidated-truck departures (S5).** Target: non-urgent Standard-stock ground volumes (`Package standard  Ship ground`: 5,100 cases), mainly Regular. Mechanism: consolidation cuts transport kgCO2e 35–55% vs individual ground parcels. Restriction: apply only where the 12–24h consolidation wait fits comfortably inside the promise (Regular-stock slack), never to Service-contract near-SLA cases. Service: none where eligibility filter works; late risk rises sharply if misapplied. Resilience: consolidation is a concentration point — retain immediate parcel fallback (supports R1). Monitor: share of consignments meeting cutoff, any post-consolidation air upgrades.

**O8 — Shared emergency courier lanes (S8).** Target: dedicated_van usage (620 shipments) lacking SP2 justification (31%), especially precautionary-urgent cases. Mechanism: pooled multi-stop courier lanes remove much of the empty-return inefficiency baked into the 52 kgCO2e/order figure. Service: SLA risk if lanes congest — restrict to non-critical-downtime urgencies; true downtime keeps sole-use vans. Resilience: dual-carrier contracts for lane capacity. Monitor: van usage with/without justification, lane on-time%, utilization.

**O9 — Triage Critical-downtime: true downtime vs precautionary (S9).** Target: 11% Critical-downtime segment (avg 38 kgCO2e/order — largest per-order transport footprint). Mechanism: confirmed line-down cases retain van/express; precautionary urgent cases get priority pick + next consolidated departure when SLA math holds. Service: protects the 15h median / 3% late of genuine emergencies; misclassification is the key risk — require confirmation attribute at order entry. Quality: neutral. Resilience: neutral-to-positive (frees van capacity for true crises). Monitor: triage accuracy vs retrospective confirmation, SP2 compliance.

**O10 — Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation (S10).** Target: Made-to-order Regular (18% late today; heavy emergency-production/expedite leakage: 760 emergency starts). Monitor: exception rates by type/approver, expiry follow-up.

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching  rescue-shipment displacement.** Pushing CNC/heat-treat batch sizes upward for energy/item gains raises `Batch wait` (438h) and risks breaching promises; missed dates get rescued by air or van whose emissions exceed everything the batching saved. Caps (O3) exist precisely to stop this loop.
2. **Consolidation  air-upgrade displacement.** The 12–24h consolidation wait invites deadline misses; observed behaviour elsewhere (SS-SC ordering air with available slack) suggests planner habit of upgrading after delay. Mitigation: Regular-stock-only scope, hard exclusion rule, and monitoring of air shipments logged *after* consolidation cutoffs.
3. **Packaging material-cutting  damage/return displacement.** Removing reinforced packaging to save doubled `package_material_kg` would raise damage-related returns (reduced 40% when reinforced is used for heavy MTO). A return journey plus replacement production typically dwarfs packaging mass — Q1 must stay enforced.
4. **Rework/inspection shortcuts  escaped defects.** Making inspection lighter to save inspection-cell energy and queue time causes defects to escape into returns or *field failures*, each triggering a replacement expedition (worst form: downtime-critical reshipment). O5 goes the opposite way deliberately.
5. **Obsolescence write-off displacement.** Building ahead into efficient batches creates finished stock; the 6% unshipped-within-14-days share turns saved production energy into scrapped embodied energy when forecasts slip. Batch caps plus demand-signal gating address it; the indicator set tracks it explicitly.
6. **Customer-behavior rebound.** If greener execution proves reliable, sales may re-tighten quoted dates (slack-consumption/"Jevons" dynamics on speed) or customers escalate Service-contract expectations, re-inflating air usage. O10's negotiated dates and locked quote logic counteract; monitor promise-date drift over quarters.
7. **Machine-loading displacement.** Over-routing MTO to CNC_A destroys the queue slack that justified the shift, recreating delays and pushing work back to CNC_B at 18% rework — guardrail thresholds must trigger automatic reversion.
8. **Governance gaming.** The O12 exception register can decay into rubber-stamping, normalizing SP1/SP2/Q1 violations; exception rates need standing review limits and expiring approvals.
9. **Out-of-boundary displacement.** Regional stocking or mode shifts may move impacts to inbound logistics or upstream inventory (outside this log) — flag for periodic broader-footprint audits rather than claiming pure wins.

## 6. Balanced recommendation

Deploy one integrated package in three waves, not scattered green actions:

**Wave 1 — Visibility and decision hygiene (months 0–2):** O12 exception register first (it creates the justification events SP2/Q1 need), then O1+O2 targeting the demonstrable misuse block: 22% SP1 violations on Standard-stock Service-contract and air-vs-ground gaps of ~15 kgCO2e/order, achieved while protecting an 18h-median SLA through priority picking rather than transport.

**Wave 2 — Production core (months 2–6):** O3 dynamic batching with segment wait caps (size target 8–12, not 12–20, to hold obsolescence at bay), O4 CNC preference rule with queue guardrails coupled to O5 inspection gates for whatever still runs on CNC_B — the combination captures the 32 kWh/1.8 kg/160h worst-variant improvements without simply relocating load. O11 partial-shipment policy follows, scoped to exclude downtime-critical lines.

**Wave 3 — Distribution and demand interface (months 4–9):** O7 twice-daily consolidation restricted to slack-rich Regular stock orders with guaranteed parcel fallback; O9 downtime triage plus O8 shared lanes absorbing the ~31% unjustified van share while sole-use vans remain reserved for verified line-down events; O10 negotiated promise dates for Regular MTO rolled out last, once execution reliability improves (offering longer dates credibly requires Waves 1–2 to land first).

**Why this balances the four criteria.** Environmentally it attacks the two dominant measured drivers — transport hotspots (vans, avoidable air, fragmented ground parcels  10.3 kgCO2e/order average) and MTO production losses (rework loops, scrap, inefficient batches) — while explicitly *keeping* reinforced packaging and guarding inspection depth so material savings aren't reclaimed by returns and escapes. Service-wise it improves the worst paths (MTO late rates of 18–32%, partial-shipment 24% lateness) instead of taxing the best one (Critical-downtime at 4% late stays untouched). Quality-wise rework enters via root causes (machine selection, rushed inspection, pack-route mismatch) rather than end-of-pipe filtering. Resilience-wise every concentration created (consolidation cutoffs, shared lanes, CNC_A loading) ships with an explicit fallback: alternative routes for Service-contract customers per R1, dual carriers, qualified second machine, immediate parcel mode.

## 7. Validation and governance plan

**Baseline and design.** Freeze current KPI values per segment before rollout; pilot with control groups (segment/region stratification using log attributes) since randomized assignment may not be feasible operationally. Evaluate at 4-week checkpoints with pre-agreed rollback thresholds.

**Conformance monitoring (automated on the enriched log).**
- *SP1*: compute eligibility from `promised_lead_time_hours` plus empirical route reliability distributions; alert when avoidable-air ratio rises or shifts to Service-contract Regular classes (currently 22% violation is the reference point).
- *SP2*: dedicated-van shipments must carry a justification event — target near-zero "missing justification" share (baseline 31%); O9 confirmation attributes feed this automatically.
- *Q1*: check reinforced packaging wherever Made-to-order weight >8 kg, allowing certified low-risk exemptions; correlate remaining violations with return loops monthly.
- Track exception-register health: counts by policy, approver concentration, approval latency, expired approvals acted upon.

**Service and quality dashboards (segment level).** Median/p90 cycle time vs promise attainment; late %, rescue-expedite rate (the early-warning signal for displacement), partial-shipment and cancellation rates; rework-loop frequency per machine/part family; packaging-rework occurrences; 14-day obsolescence rate by batch-size band; batch-wait p90 vs configured caps.

**Displacement watchdogs (required by the rebound analysis).** Air shipments occurring *after* consolidation cutoffs; expedite conversions following batch-cap breaches; damage-return trends after any packaging change; promise-date drift (are quotes quietly tightening?); CNC_A queue saturation events causing reversion to CNC_B.

**Resilience testing.** Quarterly tabletop/simulation exercises: carrier outage with R1 verification that an alternate route executed for 95% of affected Service-contract cases; consolidated-departure failure drill confirming parcel fallback absorbs load without service collapse.

**Model and assumption maintenance.** Recompute the DFG/conformance model quarterly — O12's structured exceptions should measurably raise model precision by collapsing the current combinatorial emergency/partial spaghetti. Refresh transport and grid emission factors annually and re-run the effect estimates; retire or intensify interventions whose realized curves deviate from the ranges in Section 4. Sustainability performance joins cost and service as a standing agenda item in operations review, with authority to tighten caps, revert routings, or expand pilots codified in advance — so adaptation happens through governed rules, not ad-hoc exceptions.
