## 1. Materiality Framework

A robust severity framework for process deviations cannot rely on technical edit distance. Instead, it must evaluate the actual business impact of the deviation. The framework consists of the following dimensions:
*   **Control Objective:** Does the deviation bypass a critical safeguard (e.g., fraud screening, manual approval)? Bypassing controls yields high severity.
*   **Amount/Exposure:** What is the financial value at risk? A EUR 480,000 deviation is inherently more severe than a EUR 800 deviation.
*   **Reversibility:** Can the action be undone? Irreversible actions (like releasing funds to an external bank) before required approvals constitute critical failures.
*   **Timing:** Did events occur out of sequence in a way that negates a control (e.g., paying before approving)?
*   **Customer/Regulatory Harm:** Does the deviation violate SLAs, compliance mandates (e.g., 24-hour notification), or wrongfully deny service?
*   **Recurrence:** Is the deviation an isolated anomaly or a systemic failure exposing the business to continuous risk?
*   **Evidence Reliability:** Is the system log missing an event that occurred externally, or did the event truly not happen? 
*   **Model Scope:** Is the case intentionally outside the model's design (e.g., denied or withdrawn claims)?

**Confidence vs. Severity:** 
Severity measures the potential negative impact of a deviation assuming it actually occurred. Confidence measures the degree of certainty that the deviation represents reality rather than a logging error, missing data integration, or a model mismatch. A high-severity issue (like a missing fraud check) might have low confidence if there is strong evidence the check happened in an unlinked external system.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

1.  **Pattern: Missing Assess Loss (CL-606)**
    *   *Hypothesis 1:* The assessment was skipped entirely by the handler.
    *   *Hypothesis 2:* The assessment was completed by an external adjuster but not synced to the core log.
    *   *Evidence Test:* Query the external adjuster database for report timestamps matching the case ID. A missing log event is not proof the activity did not occur.
2.  **Pattern: Duplicate Notify Claimant (CL-603)**
    *   *Hypothesis 1:* The claimant was erroneously spammed with multiple notifications.
    *   *Hypothesis 2:* A network transport retry logged the same notification twice.
    *   *Evidence Test:* Check the message IDs and transport logs for HTTP 5xx errors or timeouts preceding the retry.
3.  **Pattern: Out-of-Sequence Payment (CL-604)**
    *   *Hypothesis 1:* Log timestamps are skewed between the payment gateway and the approval system.
    *   *Hypothesis 2:* The payment was actually released out-of-band before the manual approval was granted.
    *   *Evidence Test:* Cross-reference bank clearing timestamps with system clock synchronization logs.
4.  **Pattern: Missing Notify Claimant (CL-608)**
    *   *Hypothesis 1:* The regulatory notification was never sent.
    *   *Hypothesis 2:* The notification was sent via the secure portal/CRM but is poorly linked to the core case.
    *   *Evidence Test:* Search CRM portal logs by claimant email or user ID rather than relying on the mutable text field link.
5.  **Pattern: Auto-Approve Policy Violation (CL-609, CL-610)**
    *   *Hypothesis 1:* The rule engine was misconfigured and allowed the approval.
    *   *Hypothesis 2:* The risk score or bank change data arrived in the system *after* the auto-approval executed.
    *   *Evidence Test:* Check the exact temporal order of the risk scoring/bank update timestamps versus the auto-approval timestamp.
6.  **Pattern: High-Cost Denials/Withdrawals (CL-607, CL-612)**
    *   *Hypothesis 1:* Handlers are abandoning claims non-compliantly.
    *   *Hypothesis 2:* The model is missing valid early-exit end states.
    *   *Evidence Test:* Review Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) to confirm if denial and withdrawal are valid process terminations.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Raw alignment cost is a measure of technical edit distance between a log and a model, which fundamentally fails to capture business risk for several reasons:

1.  **Data Conditions & Cost-Function Design:** In CL-610, the alignment is fully synchronous (cost 0), implying perfection. However, it violates critical data-aware policies (P1/P2) by auto-approving a payment after a bank change. The cost function only checks control flow, blinding it to severe fraud risk.
2.  **Scope & Irreversibility:** CL-604 has a cost of 3 (payment before approval) and CL-601 has a cost of 3 (UI telemetry). The cost function treats them equally. However, CL-604 violates P3 (irreversible payment before approval)—a critical failure—while CL-601 is harmless technical noise.
3.  **Missing-but-External Evidence:** CL-606 incurs a cost of 1 for a missing `Assess Loss` event. However, the activity occurred externally and is documented. The business risk is low (an audit gap), but the cost penalizes it the same as a bypassed fraud screen (CL-602, cost 1).
4.  **Activity Granularity & Duplicated Telemetry:** CL-603 incurs a cost of 1 due to a duplicate notification from a network retry. This is a technical artifact of system resilience, not a business deviation, yet it increases the raw cost.
5.  **Model Scope:** CL-607 incurs a massive cost of 4 because the model lacks a path for denied claims. The high cost reflects a deficiency in the model's design, not a high-risk process failure by the business.
