# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

## Queue QA
- **Supporting cases:**
  - `A01`, `A02`, `A04`, `A05`, `A06`, `A07`, `A08` start in exact arrival order (`08:00 < 08:04 < 08:13 < 08:21 < 08:29 < 08:35 < 08:40`).   - `A03` (`08:20`) directly supports an appointment-due override over FIFO (`A02`), Priority (`A04`), and SPT (`A04`).
- **Contradicting / challenging cases:**
  - `A06` (`09:05`  started at `09:20` for a `09:15` appointment): Challenges a *strict/protected* appointment rule. **Constraint vs. exception:** Explainable *without* an ad-hoc exception if QA's documented rule is **non-preemptive, zero-lookahead appointment dispatch** (appointments jump the queue only once `now >= appointment`, never reserving capacity ahead of time) OR if QA uses an **appointment tolerance window** (e.g., `+5` to `+10` min grace period so `09:20` is still compliant).
  - Unexplained 2-minute idle gaps before `A01` (`08:00–08:02`) and `A02` (`08:30–08:32`), absent after `A04`, `A05`, `A06`, `A07`. **Constraint vs. exception:** Likely minor operational friction (shift start / post-appointment reset / dispatch acknowledgment lag); does not alter queue order.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

## Test 1 — Queue QA at `09:05` (Appointment protection vs. non-lookahead / tolerance window)
- **Ambiguous episode:** `A1` becomes free at `09:05` with waiting jobs `A05` (arr `08:21`, P3, `15m`), `A06` (arr `08:29`, appt `09:15`, P2, `12m`), `A07` (arr `08:35`, P3, `9m`), and `A08` (arr `08:40`, P1, `25m`). `A05` was selected, pushing `A06` to `09:20` (+5 min late).
- **Competing explanations:**
  - **H1 (FIFO + Zero-Lookahead Due-Time Appointment Override):** Appointments jump FIFO only when `now >= appointment_time`; prior to `09:15`, `A1` blindly picks FIFO head regardless of expected overrun.
  - **H2 (FIFO + Windowed Appointment Protection, e.g., `[appt, appt + 10m]`):** Dispatch *does* check appointment feasibility against a 10-minute grace window (`09:25`), so `A05` (`09:05 + 15 = 09:20 <= 09:25`) was permitted.
- **Counterfactual next-job prediction:** Suppose `A05`'s `est_min` had been `25m` instead of `15m` (projected finish `09:30`, breaching `09:25`):
  - **H1 predicts:** Still select **`A05`** (FIFO head), starting `A06` at `09:30` (15 min late).
  - **H2 predicts:** Reject `A05` and instead select **`A07`** (`9m`, finishes `09:14`), start **`A06`** early, or **idle** until `09:15` to keep `A06` inside `[09:15, 09:25]`.
- **Single discriminating observable:** **Logged appointment tolerance window / dispatch-rule rejection flag** at `09:05` (specifically, whether the dispatcher system evaluated `now + est_min(A05) <= appt_window_end(A06)` before releasing `A05`).

## Test 2 — Queue QB at `09:48` (P1-only preemption + manual override vs. intake-delayed P1 preemption)
- **Ambiguous episode:** `B05` (P1, `14m`) is logged at `09:46`; at `09:48`, `B01` (P3) is preempted (`"dispatcher reassignment"`), yet `B03` (P2, arr `09:20`, `12m`) starts instead of `B05`, and `B05` starts at `10:00`.
- **Competing explanations:**
  - **H1 (P1-Only Preemption over FIFO + Ad-Hoc Manual Override for `B03`):** `B05` was visible and ready at `09:48`, but a supervisor manually overrode the rule to clear `B03` first (and `B05` was not allowed to preempt `B03`).
  - **H2 (Automatic P1 Preemption + Unlogged Intake/Triage Delay on `B05`):** `B05` was physically present/scanned at `09:46` but not yet *work-ready* (e.g., awaiting diagnostics/parts) until between `09:49` and `10:00`; meanwhile, `B03`'s reassignment at `09:48` was an independent P2 aging/escalation intervention, and `B05` became ready while `B03` was running.
- **Counterfactual next-job prediction:** Suppose `B05` had arrived and completed intake at `09:40` (unambiguously work-ready by `09:48`):
  - **H1 predicts:** If the dispatcher still prioritized `B03` manually, **`B03`** still runs at `09:48` (or `B05` runs only if the manual override wasn't triggered). More sharply, if another P1 arrived at `09:50` while `B03` (P2) was running, **H1 (P1 preempts P3 only)** predicts `B03` finishes uninterrupted, whereas **H2 (strict P1 preemption once ready)** predicts `B03` is immediately preempted.
- **Single discriminating observable:** **`dispatch_ready_time` (distinct from arrival-scan time) for `B05`** (or the eligibility set displayed on the dispatch console at `09:48`): if `B05` was not yet in the ready set at `09:48`, H2 wins; if `B05` was shown as ready and explicitly bypassed by a human override code, H1 wins.

## Test 3 — Queue QC at `10:32` (Continuous Aging-SPT vs. Batched/Wave SPT or C06 Intake Delay)
- **Ambiguous episode:** At `10:32`, `C04` (arr `10:07`, `22m`) is selected ahead of `C06` (arr `10:25`, `6m`) and `C05` (arr `10:11`, P1, dl `10:50`, `40m`).
- **Competing explanations:**
  - **H1 (Continuous Linear Aging-SPT, `est - wait`):** `C04` (`22 - 25 = -3`, or `arr+est = 10:29`) legitimately outranks `C06` (`6 - 7 = -1`, `arr+est = 10:31`).
  - **H2 (Pure SPT with Delayed Readiness/Estimate Entry for `C06`, or Pre-10:10 Wave Locking):** Pure SPT is used, but `C06` was either not dispatch-ready at `10:32` or locked into a later wave until the `10:54` SLA disruption reset the queue.
- **Counterfactual next-job prediction:** Suppose `C06` had arrived at `10:20` (12m wait by `10:32`, `arr+est = 10:26 < 10:29`) with the same `est_min = 6` and the same readiness delay of 8 minutes (ready at `10:28`, before `10:32`):
  - **H1 predicts:** **`C06`** beats `C04` at `10:32` (`10:26 < 10:29`).
  - **H2 (wave locking of pre-10:10 backlog)** predicts: **`C04`** still beats `C06` at `10:32` because `C04` belongs to the locked initial wave (`C02, C03, C04, C01`).
- **Single discriminating observable:** **Timestamp when `C06` entered the dispatch-eligible pool with its `est_min = 6` populated** (and whether a wave/batch ID existed for `C01–C04`).

## Test 4 — Queue QD at `11:36` on `D-F` (Static Push-Assignment vs. Shared Pull-Queue with HOL Blocking)
- **Ambiguous episode:** `D-F` finishes `D03` at `11:36`, has 24 minutes before its `12:00–12:20` meeting, cannot fit `D05` (`30m`), and idles instead of executing eligible P1 `D10` (`11:25`, electrical, `12m`).
- **Competing explanations:**
  - **H1 (Static Push-Routing at Arrival + Per-Resource FIFO):** At `11:25`, `D10` was permanently assigned to `D-X`'s personal queue (leaving only `D05` in `D-F`'s queue).
  - **H2 (Shared Skill-Based Pull-Queue + Strict HOL Blocking / Pre-Meeting Cutoff):** `D10` remained in a shared queue eligible for `D-F`, but `D-F` refused to skip past older `D05` (or had a policy prohibiting new jobs within 25–30 min of a scheduled meeting).
- **Counterfactual next-job prediction:** Suppose `D-F`'s meeting had been canceled at `11:35` (so `D-F` had unlimited time) and `D05` had been canceled at `11:35` (removing HOL blocking):
  - **H1 (static push to `D-X`) predicts:** `D-F` *still* idles at `11:36` while `D10` waits on `D-X` until `12:16`.
  - **H2 (shared pull-queue) predicts:** `D-F` immediately pulls **`D10`** at `11:36` (`11:36–11:48`).
- **Single discriminating observable:** **Assignment timestamp and target resource ID at queue entry** (whether `resource = D-X` was bound to `D10` at `11:25` vs. at `12:16` dispatch time).

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked from **most plausible** to **least plausible** given the supplied evidence:

1. **Rank 1 (Very High Plausibility — Data-Quality Defect): Post-hoc overwrite of `est_min` or flat-rate standard-time logging (`32/32` exact matches)**
   - *Evidence:* Every single job across QA, QB, QC, and QD has realized service time identically equal to `est_min`—even `B01` across three interrupted segments (`14 + 17 + 14 = 45 min`).
   - *Impact on diagnosis:* If `est_min` was overwritten by realized duration during ETL, we do **not** know the true ex-ante estimates dispatch saw. This most severely threatens **QC** (where SPT/aging-SPT relies on `est_min`: if `C06`'s initial estimate at `10:32` was actually `25m` and later revised to `6m`, `C04` beating `C06` at `10:32` ceases to contradict pure SPT!) and **QB** (any preemption/resumption overhead on `B01`—say 3–5 min per resume—would be hidden if true net work was shorter than `45m`, or if `est_min` was retroactively set to total logged work time).

2. **Rank 2 (High Plausibility — Operational): Unrecorded resource unavailability, setup/turnover, and pre-meeting cutoffs**
   - *Evidence:* Unexplained idle gaps appear at `QA` (`08:00–08:02`, `08:30–08:32`), `QB` (`09:00–09:02`, and 2m preemption gaps `09:14–09:16`, `09:46–09:48`), and `QD` (`D-X` idle `11:20–11:30`; `D-F` idle `11:36–12:00`). The prompt explicitly warns that absence of a logged interruption reason does not prove no operational reason existed.
   - *Impact on diagnosis:* If `D-F` took an unlogged break or enforced a 30-min pre-meeting cutoff at `11:36`, and `D-X` actually started floor work at `11:30` after a 10-min briefing, then **QD** simplifies cleanly to a standard **shared skill-based FIFO pull-queue** with no need to posit HOL blocking or static push-assignment.

3. **Rank 3 (High Plausibility — Operational): Intake/triage latency vs. arrival-scan time (readiness lag)**
   - *Evidence:* Arrival scans reflect gate/counter check-in (and can be delayed up to 2 min after physical arrival), but jobs may still require diagnostic triage, parts kitting, or bench staging before becoming dispatch-eligible.
   - *Impact on diagnosis:* A 3–10 minute readiness lag would resolve the two sharpest single-episode anomalies in the dataset: `B05` (logged `09:46`) not being ready at `09:48` in **QB** (restoring consistent P1 preemption once ready), and `C06` (logged `10:25`) not being ready/estimated by `10:32` in **QC** (making pure SPT + starvation cap + SLA-breach override far cleaner than continuous aging).

4. **Rank 4 (Moderate-to-High Plausibility — Policy Design): Soft appointment tolerance windows vs. hard appointment timestamps**
   - *Evidence:* `A06` (`09:15` appt) starts at `09:20` (+5m) in QA, while `D06` (`11:30` appt) is held exactly until `11:30` in QD, and `B07` (`10:30` appt) starts at `10:56` (+26m) in QB.
   - *Impact on diagnosis:* In **QA**, a `+5` to `+10` min appointment tolerance window changes the diagnosis from "zero-lookahead appointment override" to "window-aware FIFO with appointment compliance." In **QD**, a strict `[11:30, 11:30+w]` non-early-start rule explains `D-X`'s `11:20–11:30` idle gap without invoking unrecorded unavailability.

5. **Rank 5 (Moderate Plausibility — Data Quality): Arrival scan delays (up to 2 minutes)**
   - *Evidence:* Explicitly noted in scenario rules (`true_arrival` in `[logged - 2m, logged]`).
   - *Impact on diagnosis:* At `10:10` in **QC**, `C05` (logged `10:11`) may have physically arrived at `10:09–10:10`; if it was already visible, passing over `C05` at `10:10` adds a third refutation of Priority/EDF in QC, whereas if not yet scanned, `10:10` only tests `{C01–C04}`. In **QB**, scan delay widens the preemption latency for `B02` (`09:12–09:14`  `09:16`) and `B05` (`09:44–09:46`  `09:48`) to 2–4 minutes, reinforcing that preemption is not instantaneous (consistent with checkpoint/safe-stop costs). In **QA** (`A05` at `08:21` vs `08:20`) and **QD** (`D09` at `11:20`), 2-minute shifts do not alter any rule's winning choice.

6. **Rank 6 (Moderate-to-Low Plausibility — Behavioral): Strategic estimate inflation/deflation or unlogged VIP/SLA overrides**
   - *Evidence:* `B03` (`09:48`) has `"dispatcher reassignment"` without an explicit rule trigger; `C05` (`10:54`) and `B01` (`45m`) sit at extremes of `est_min`.
   - *Impact on diagnosis:* If technicians or dispatchers manipulate `est_min` (e.g., inflating `C01`/`C05` to avoid taking them, or deflating `C02`/`C03` to get quick wins), observed SPT behavior in **QC** may partly reflect cherry-picking rather than formal SEPT policy. Similarly, unlogged customer escalations (VIP phone calls) could explain `B03` at `09:48` and `A03` at `08:20` as human discretion rather than codified queue rules.

# 7. Final synthesis

- **Most identifiable queue: `QA`.**
  Across six decision points on a single general server, `QA` cleanly and repeatedly separates its **FIFO** baseline from Priority (`A04` and `A08` both lose to P3 jobs) and SPT (`A02` and `A05` beat much shorter jobs), with only a single well-isolated structural ambiguity: how appointments interact with FIFO (zero-lookahead due-time override vs. grace-window protection, weakly constrained because only `A03` is unconfounded with FIFO).

- **Least identifiable queue: `QB`.**
  In just seven jobs, `QB` violates strict 3-tier preemptive priority four times (`09:31`, `09:48`, `10:14`, `10:28`), violates pure FIFO three times (`09:16`, `09:48`, `10:00`), violates SPT/SRPT five times, and ignores appointments (`B07`). While a **P1-selective preemptive priority over FIFO** baseline fits six of seven jobs, the `09:48` reassignment (`B03` beating P1 `B05`) prevents clean separation between ad-hoc human discretion, unlogged intake latency, and a hidden aging/escalation rule. (`QC` is the second least identifiable because continuous aging-SPT, batched SPT + starvation cap, and post-hoc `est_min` overwrite remain hard to separate without ex-ante dispatch snapshots.)

- **Most dangerous claimed policy to infer from these observations alone: claiming `QB` operates under strict (3-tier) preemptive priority.**
  A superficial reading of `B01`'s two preemptions invites labeling `QB` as "preemptive priority." Relying on that claim for staffing, simulation, or SLA commitments would be operationally hazardous: `P2` jobs (`B03`, `B06`) repeatedly waited behind `P3` jobs (`B01`, `B04`), `P1` job `B05` was bypassed by `P2` job `B03` at `09:48`, and `B07`'s appointment was missed by 26 minutes. Almost as dangerous—for different reasons—would be certifying **`QC` as pure SPT (or proactive SLA-protecting scheduling)** without first auditing the `32/32` `est_min == realized` anomaly and the reactive post-breach switching that allowed both `C05` and `C07` to miss their deadlines. Across all four queues, the evidence points less to textbook single-rule disciplines than to **pragmatic, partially undocumented hybrids** whose exact boundaries can only be verified by logging ex-ante readiness, eligibility sets, immutable estimates, and override actors.
