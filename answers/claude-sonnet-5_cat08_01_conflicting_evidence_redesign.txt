# 1. Current-process diagnosis

The discovered process is dominated by one high-frequency, low-friction path: **Intake  Auto eligibility screen  Fast approval  Dispatch replacement  Quality/compliance close** (2,550 of 3,600 auto-screened cases reach Fast approval; 2,300 of those go straight to dispatch). This path is fast (median 0.4–1.8h per hop) and, on a unit-cost basis, 31% cheaper before rework. But five converging artifacts show that this cheapness is partly illusory and partly non-compliant:

1. **Rework/complaint asymmetry** – Fast approval cases reopen at 14.1%, versus only 3.3% for Repair quote  Customer approval  Repair device. Once rework, reshipment, and service credits are included, the "cheap" path's cost advantage collapses from 31% to 9%. The most frequent path is not simply fast-cheap vs. slow-expensive; it is fast-cheap-fragile vs. slow-expensive-durable.

2. **Compliance violations concentrated in the fast path** – Alignment diagnostics explicitly flag "Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices" and "Fast approval before Evidence completeness" as frequent log deviations. This directly breaches R1 (>EUR 400 claims need Manual/Fraud review before Fast approval) and R2 (no dispatch before evidence completeness, except Premium low-risk accessories). Segment data confirms the damage is segment-specific: Premium high-value fast path complaint rate is 22% vs 5% on the reviewed/repair path; Standard high-value is 18% vs 6%.

3. **Low conformance despite decent replay fitness** – Token-based replay shows fitness 0.91 but only 68% of traces fully fit the normative model, and model precision is separately flagged as low. This tells us the *frequent* behavior is not simply "slightly off" — a third of all cases follow structurally different, low-frequency variants (auto approval mixed with risk review and repair in disallowed combinations), which is exactly where hidden compliance and quality risk lives, not in the headline path average.

4. **Evidence loop is a hidden tax, not a rare exception** – Request missing evidence is re-entered at least twice in 26% of cases that touch it, and the Unclear defect/missing evidence segment (22% of volume) has the worst cycle time (median 140h) and highest abandonment. Auto-screen routes some of this straight into Fast approval risk (400 of 3,600 auto-screened cases go to Fraud/risk review, meaning the auto rules are already misclassifying non-trivial claims), confirming that "auto-screen rules are outdated and not segment-aware."

5. **Reopen loop reinjects cost into the slow lane, and is itself under-controlled** – R3 requires reopened claims to pass Manual eligibility review before close/denial, but Customer complaint/reopen  Escalation board (120 cases) bypasses that queue via a scarce, poorly scalable daily 45-minute slot. Meanwhile Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen is explicitly tied to a temporal/quality root cause (wrong replacement part selection), a systemic defect, not noise.

**The core contradiction, stated plainly:** the most frequent path optimizes first-pass unit economics for the *modal* case (low/mid-risk, correctly classified) but externalizes its failure cost onto compliance exposure, escalation-board capacity, and Premium/high-value complaint rates — segments where the underlying data (R1/R2, segment outcome tables) say the fast path is actively unsafe. The slow paths are not uniformly "better" either: Manual eligibility review  Repair quote is flagged as too slow specifically for Premium cases, and the evidence-request loop is slow *and* prone to abandonment. Any redesign has to differentiate by segment/risk rather than pick one lane as globally superior.

# 2. Redesign A: Risk-Tiered Compliance-Embedded Triage

**Flow.** Replace the single Auto eligibility screen with a two-parameter triage evaluated immediately after Intake: (claim value/device risk tier) × (segment). Low-risk accessory claims (Premium or Standard) continue to Auto screen  Fast approval largely unchanged. Mid/high-value device claims are *hard-routed* — not optionally, but by rule — to Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review before any Fast approval decision can fire, directly encoding R1. Premium low-risk accessory claims retain the R2 exception (fast dispatch without full evidence confirmation), but Standard low-risk and all mid/high-value claims require confirmed evidence before dispatch.

**Targets.** The R1/R2 deviations named in the alignment diagnostics, and the segment-specific complaint gap (Premium/Standard high-value: 22%/18% fast-path complaints vs 5%/6% reviewed-path). Leaves low-risk accessory volume (43% of cases) essentially untouched.

**Mechanism.** By making the routing logic itself compliance-aware (a two-week deployable auto-screen rule update per the prompt), most R1/R2 violations become structurally impossible rather than dependent on case-by-case discretion. This should reduce Fast-approval-driven reopens in the high-value segments toward the reviewed-path baseline (5–6%) without slowing the low-risk majority.

**Sacrifices / leaves unresolved.** The prompt explicitly warns that updating auto-screen rules can increase manual-review inflow by 15–35%; Manual eligibility review already has queueing risk above 82% utilization with only 7 reviewers. This redesign does *not* fix the wrong-part-selection defect driving DispatchComplaint, nor the evidence-request loop (26% repeat rate) — it only stops disallowed *bypassing* of review, it doesn't make review itself faster or higher quality. Premium high-value customers may now experience the "too slow for Premium" Manual review  Repair quote pattern more often, not less, unless paired with capacity changes.

# 3. Redesign B: Pre-Dispatch Quality Gate + Evidence Self-Service (Control-Insertion Redesign)

**Flow.** Keep intake and eligibility routing structurally as-is, but insert two new controls at different points: (a) an **evidence portal** immediately after Request missing evidence, letting customers upload documentation/photos directly, reducing repeat evidence cycles; (b) a **lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate** inserted between Fast approval/Repair quote decisions and Dispatch replacement/Repair device — but *only* for mid-risk and high-value devices, not low-risk accessories.

**Targets.** The two rework mechanisms that are structural, not routing, defects: (1) Request missing evidence recurring 2x in 26% of cases, contributing to the 140h median and high abandonment in the Unclear-defect/missing-evidence segment; (2) the long-tail Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen pattern explicitly linked to incorrect replacement part selection.

**Mechanism.** The portal directly attacks the 26% repeat-request rate (expected 30–45% reduction in repeat requests per the improvement note), shortening the slowest segment's cycle time and cutting abandonment from evidence friction. The pre-dispatch gate adds 6–18 hours but only on higher-risk devices where the wrong-replacement cost is EUR 410 (vs EUR 95 for low-risk) — so the added latency is deliberately not applied where the expected value doesn't justify it.

**Sacrifices / leaves unresolved.** The prompt flags that the portal "may increase abandonment for customers with low digital literacy" — an equity/CX risk concentrated in whichever segment skews toward that profile (plausibly overlapping with Standard/Unclear-defect claims). The pre-dispatch gate adds latency precisely to the high-value segment where Premium customers already have stricter expectations, so it can *increase* complaint risk from delay even as it decreases complaint risk from wrong parts — a trade this redesign does not resolve, only shifts. It does nothing to fix R1 bypass (auto-approval of high-value claims without risk review) — that structural compliance gap remains open.

# 4. Redesign C: Segmented Parallel-Lane Design with Resource Reallocation

**Flow.** Restructure the process into explicit parallel lanes rather than a single shared queue with branching: (1) **Premium/low-risk lane** — fast, lightly gated, R2 exception preserved, monitored via cycle-time SLA (flag if >24h, since service-credit exposure is explicitly tied to that threshold). (2) **Standard/low-risk lane** — same fast mechanics but with active status-visibility messaging, since the prompt notes Standard customers "tolerate 72-hour completion if status visibility is good" — trading speed for transparency rather than trying to match Premium's cycle time. (3) **High-value lane (both segments)** — mandatory Manual/Fraud review, Repair quote path, dedicated technician capacity, and priority access to Quality check — explicitly resourced using underused inspector capacity (2 inspectors, currently under-loaded except after repair campaigns). (4) **Reopen/escalation lane** — decouple Customer complaint/reopen from re-entering the general Manual eligibility review queue; instead route it to a dedicated small review pool (partially staffed by cross-trained Quality inspectors) so R3 compliance (mandatory Manual review before close/denial) is satisfied without competing with first-pass claims for the same 7 reviewers.

**Targets.** The mismatch between segment expectations and one-size-fits-all queueing: Manual review  Repair quote being "too slow for Premium," Standard's tolerance for slower-but-transparent handling, and Escalation board's poor scalability as a single daily slot.

**Mechanism.** Instead of changing *what* triggers review (Redesign A) or inserting new gates (Redesign B), this redesign changes *capacity allocation and lane isolation* so that high-stakes/high-value work no longer queues behind or in front of high-volume low-risk work, and reopened claims don't silently degrade first-pass SLA. This is the only redesign that directly uses the resource data (reviewer utilization threshold, inspector under-utilization, escalation board's single-slot constraint).

**Sacrifices / leaves unresolved.** This is organizationally the most disruptive (new staffing model, cross-training, lane governance) and does not, by itself, fix the auto-screen rule quality (Redesign A's problem) or the wrong-part-selection defect (Redesign B's problem) — it assumes those are handled elsewhere or accepts their current rates while reducing queueing-driven delay. It also raises fairness questions: Standard customers get an explicit "slower but transparent" tier, which is a deliberate, not accidental, service differentiation that could raise perception issues if not framed correctly to customers.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A (Risk-Tiered Compliance Triage)**
- Failure mode 1: If manual-review inflow grows toward the high end of the 15–35% estimate without added reviewer capacity, utilization crosses the 82% threshold and queue times explode — potentially making high-value claims *slower and non-compliant-feeling* even though technically compliant.
- Failure mode 2: Over-aggressive value/risk thresholds could misclassify borderline mid-value Standard claims into slow lanes unnecessarily, eroding the CX gains it's meant to produce, especially if the rule doesn't account for defect-type nuance.
- Bottleneck migration: Volume pressure shifts from Auto screen (cheap, unlimited capacity) to Manual eligibility review and secondarily to Fraud/risk review (already bottlenecked on Mondays/post-campaigns) — the queue simply moves upstream in the flow.

**Redesign B (Pre-Dispatch Gate + Evidence Portal)**
- Failure mode 1: Evidence portal could raise abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers exactly as flagged in the prompt, converting a rework problem into a lost-claim/customer-trust problem instead of solving it.
- Failure mode 2: The pre-dispatch gate, if resourced with existing technician/inspector capacity, could create a new queue at the gate itself if volume is underestimated — turning a 6-18h addition into something closer to the p90 tail seen elsewhere in the DFG.
- Bottleneck migration: Pressure shifts from "downstream complaint handling" (Customer complaint/reopen, Escalation board) to a new pre-dispatch checkpoint and to whichever team staffs it (likely Quality check inspectors, previously underused).

**Redesign C (Segmented Parallel Lanes)**
- Failure mode 1: Creating a visible "Standard = slower" lane risks a customer-perception backlash if not paired with genuinely good status visibility — the prompt's tolerance data assumes visibility is delivered, not just promised.
- Failure mode 2: Cross-training inspectors into reopen/escalation handling could degrade Quality check availability precisely when repair campaigns spike (their normal underused-except-after-campaigns pattern), reintroducing the Rework repair delay (12.5% high-value rework rate) elsewhere.
- Bottleneck migration: Pressure shifts from the single-slot Escalation board to the newly dedicated reopen-review pool, and from Manual eligibility review to whatever residual pool handles first-pass high-value claims once reopen work is siphoned off — net queueing may just relocate rather than shrink unless total review capacity is actually increased.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**Stage 0 — Instrumentation check (1–2 weeks, no process change):**
Before piloting anything, resolve why the discovered DFG has low precision and only 68% trace fitting — run a targeted conformance-checking pass isolating the low-frequency "auto approval + risk review + repair" variants to confirm they map to genuine misrouting rather than logging artifacts. This avoids piloting fixes against a partially misunderstood baseline.

**Stage 1 — First pilot: Redesign A (compliance-embedded triage), high-value segment only.**
Rationale: it directly targets the clearest, rule-based compliance violation (R1) with the fastest deployment path (~2 weeks) and the most unambiguous success criterion.
- Metrics to monitor: manual-review utilization (weekly, vs. 82% threshold), high-value complaint/reopen rate (vs. 22%/18% baseline), R1/R2 alignment-deviation rate in conformance checks, median/p90 cycle time for high-value claims by segment.
- Stop/go thresholds: Go if reviewer utilization stays below ~80% and high-value complaint rate trends toward the 5–6% reviewed-path baseline within 4–6 weeks. Stop/redesign if utilization sustains above 85% for 2+ consecutive weeks (queue-collapse risk) or if complaint rate doesn't move (signals the routing wasn't the actual driver).
- Sequencing logic: Only after this stabilizes should reviewer capacity or triage thresholds be adjusted — changing both routing and staffing simultaneously would make it impossible to attribute effects.

**Stage 2 — Second pilot: Redesign B's evidence portal only (not the pre-dispatch gate yet), targeted at the Unclear-defect/missing-evidence segment.**
- Metrics: repeat evidence-request rate (vs. 26% baseline), abandonment rate by (proxy) digital-engagement cohort, median cycle time for this segment (vs. 140h baseline).
- Stop/go thresholds: Go if repeat-request rate falls meaningfully (toward the 30–45% reduction estimate) without abandonment rising among any customer cohort; Stop/adjust (e.g., add assisted-portal support) if abandonment increases even as repeat requests fall — that would indicate the improvement is illusory, just converting rework into lost claims.

**Stage 3 — Pre-dispatch quality gate**, introduced only for high-value devices, after Stage 1 has already reduced the volume of high-value claims reaching Fast approval improperly (so the gate's added load is measured against a cleaner baseline).
- Metrics: wrong-replacement-linked complaint rate, added dispatch latency (vs. 6–18h estimate), inspector/technician utilization.
- Stop/go: Go if wrong-replacement complaints drop without pushing high-value cycle time past Premium's service-credit-relevant thresholds; Stop if gate queue times exceed the intended 6–18h band, indicating under-resourcing.

**Stage 4 — Redesign C (parallel lanes / resource reallocation)** only after A and B pilots establish real routing and rework improvements — since C's value proposition (relieving queueing pressure) is only meaningful once we know how much *genuine* volume needs to sit in the "high-value/reviewed" lane. Piloting C first would risk building organizational structure around a flow pattern (current miscompliant volumes) that Stage 1–3 are about to change.
- Metrics: escalation-board wait time, reopen-to-close cycle time under R3, Standard-lane CX satisfaction with status visibility, cross-trained-inspector availability for post-repair Quality check spikes.
- Stop/go: Go if reopen cycle time and escalation-board queueing both improve without inspector availability for standard Quality check dropping below pre-change levels; Stop/rebalance staffing if either Quality check delay or Rework repair rate for high-value devices rises, indicating the reallocation displaced rather than resolved the bottleneck.

**Distinguishing real improvement from downstream displacement:** at every stage, track not just the target metric but the *adjacent* queue/resource utilization named in the failure-mode analysis (Manual review utilization, Fraud/risk review load, Escalation board wait, Quality check availability). An improvement is only credited as "real" if the target metric improves **and** none of these adjacent-resource indicators degrade beyond their current baseline — otherwise the gain is provisionally treated as a shifted bottleneck, not a system-level improvement, and the next stage's design is adjusted before proceeding.
