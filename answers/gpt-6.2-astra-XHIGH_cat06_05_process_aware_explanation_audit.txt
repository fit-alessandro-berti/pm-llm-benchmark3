## 1. Leakage and temporal-consistency findings

The cutoff is the first Coverage Check Completed event, inclusive of that event. A value needs both a valid measurement at that point and a record the predictor could actually have read then. Process feasibility is a separate test. All three supplied prefixes end at coverage completion. None contains an observed downstream transition. The global ranking summarizes a model with access to the future. Its mean absolute contributions measure the size of attributions in that fit and do not establish a direction, cause or ranking for a clean model.

## 2. Process-model consistency checks

1. **Replay the scoring boundary.** For each claim, locate the first coverage completion and reconstruct the data visible immediately after that event. Use event time, effective time for mutable records, reliable sequence order, and ingestion or publication time. A backdated correction first received tomorrow fails today's replay. Test delayed uploads, multiple coverage records, a queue snapshot on each side of the cutoff, and same-day refreshes. Compare replayed features with logged online inputs where available.
2. **Test independence from the suffix.** Trace every input, including identifiers, aggregate filters and lookup keys, to its sources. Compute inputs on the truncated log and as-of dimension tables. Attach different legal continuations to identical prefixes. With the fitted pipeline and its explanation reference fixed, the feature vector, prediction and explanation should stay the same. Perturb later assignments, later claims and extraction date as well. A copied closure field can survive truncation of the event log, which is why this test also needs source lineage.
3. **Replay the prefix against a guarded process model.** Track enabled transitions and outstanding obligations. After coverage, the route may enable fast-track damage assessment or a first document request. Receipt requires an outstanding request, validation follows receipt, and another request can start a new cycle. Fraud opening requires its trigger. Allow investigation to overlap evidence or assessment. A decision must follow assessment on the appropriate path, and terminal closure requires final disposition. Use prefix alignment so unfinished work incurs no penalty. A strict, reliably ordered event already visible by t0 can still violate the model, for example an appeal with no qualifying decision in a complete recorded prefix. Flag the conformance or logging issue separately from any timing failure.
4. **Classify explanation matches at the cutoff.** Record whether each activity or pattern is observed, enabled as a next step under known guards, reachable only later, or forbidden from the relevant state. Mark guard status unknown where evidence is missing. In these cases registration and coverage are observed. A document request can be next on the evidence route, the request–receipt pair can finish only later, and an appeal requires several further steps. Damage assessment after terminal closure is forbidden under the supplied model. Specify whether a pattern means directly consecutive events or an ordered subsequence. A frequent directly-follows graph edge describes recorded order. Concurrency, missing events and timestamp ties limit its use as a normative rule.
5. **Handle timestamp ties as partial orders.** Use a trustworthy source sequence index where available, especially for events tied with the cutoff. Otherwise consider the permissible orderings and label a match or prefix membership indeterminate when it depends on tie-breaking. Exclude order-dependent inputs that cannot be established. In Z a conforming chronology could place assessment before closure, but the timestamps cannot prove it. If reliable independent evidence instead establishes closure first, investigate a process exception, an incorrect lifecycle label or a merged case.
6. **Test decision loops explicitly.** Accept a trace with a Partial or Deny decision, then an appeal, then a reconsidered settlement decision and eventual closure. Check decision attributes and event identity as well as activity labels. Flag an appeal after Approve or Withdrawn, a second decision without a qualifying appeal, or a decision after terminal closure. Check for duplicated source events before calling a repeated label a business violation.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

| Class | Interpretation and limits |
|---|---|
| Defensible predictive signal | Frozen initial damage complexity describes the intake assessment. A historically verified initial reserve could describe the early estimate of exposure. Either may help distinguish claims with different observed breach rates. Estimate their predictive contribution in a prefix-only fit. Editing an assessment or lowering a reserve would require separate evidence of any effect on settlement time. |
| Process-state or congestion signal | Prefix age measures time already spent. A valid queue snapshot measures workload at the relevant queue. The registration-to-coverage path establishes the current state, although its activity labels alone cannot distinguish the three identical prefixes. Queue load can motivate a capacity check, and remaining time can motivate a deadline review. The effect of moving a case or adding staff requires separate evaluation. |
| Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review | Region and, once verified, channel may capture service access, routing and case mix. A repaired prior-claim count would measure recorded history. Its link to complexity requires evidence. Test incremental predictive value and service burdens across groups before deciding whether to retain, restrict or replace each feature. A regional attribution provides no basis by itself for slower service or different treatment. |
| Drift/calendar proxy | Calendar week can index a historical operating regime. A week-52 signal may reflect seasonality, holidays, policy or their combination. Generalization requires tests across those conditions. Specific staffing or policy explanations require direct evidence. |
| Unusable until lineage is repaired | Quarantine the current prior-count, nightly count, mutable score and extraction-date experience. Verify channel and reserve. Assignment and actor role require distinct as-of features in place of final team and final role. Every repaired signal needs a new fit and validation. Completed duration, final disposition and eventual path belong to later stages. |

Predictive validity requires point-in-time availability and performance on later, held-out claims. A causal interpretation requires a defined intervention and evidence that addresses confounding. Operational actionability also requires a feasible action at the current process state and evidence about its benefits and harms. Fairness review concerns who bears the consequences of a decision, including when a proxy genuinely improves prediction. Correlation alone settles none of these questions.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Stop publishing affected scores and explanations as coverage-time results.** Inventory every input and transformation, including unranked features, the explanation background data and the sequence matcher. Isolate closure-derived fields in the outcome pipeline. Define the ten-day clock and evaluate on claims with ascertainable outcomes so a still-open claim is not prematurely labeled on time.
2. **Make historical availability reproducible.** Record feature definition and version, entity key, source event, effective time, first availability, revisions and snapshot age. Log the feature vector served at each prediction. Use backward as-of joins with explicit freshness and missing-value rules. Where historical versions cannot be reconstructed, omit the feature for the affected period.
3. **Rebuild from prefixes and enforce conformance.** Produce one example per claim at its first coverage completion using the same cutoff and code used in serving. Apply the suffix-invariance, guarded alignment, decision-loop and tie tests above to training, scoring and explanation generation. Include fixtures for a late correction, a future queue snapshot, a legal appeal loop and an unresolved tie. Alert on violations with source-event evidence.
4. **Retrain and recalibrate.** Split chronologically by scoring time, keeping each claim in one split and using only labels available at the training cutoff. Fit the clean model, calibrate on a later validation period, and evaluate on a subsequent held-out period. Check discrimination, reliability curves, Brier score and error rates at the operational threshold. Recompute each case probability, baseline and attribution together. Subtracting leaked contributions from the old probability cannot account for relearned dependencies.
5. **Test stability and proxy effects.** Use a reference population from the same prediction landmark. Compare local signs and rankings across time windows, refits and reasonable reference cohorts. Examine grouped attributions and ablations for correlated variables. Compare versions with and without region, channel and calendar inputs on calibration, false positives, missed breaches and access to any resulting service, including by relevant subgroup with uncertainty estimates. Evaluate capacity or routing interventions separately before making treatment claims.
6. **Monitor the deployed pipeline.** Track source schema and activity-label changes, event delays, tie rates, snapshot freshness, missing historical versions, prefix-conformance failures and shifts in routing or policy. Monitor case mix and attribution distributions promptly, then calibration and subgroup outcomes as labels mature. Assign owners to investigate changes, suppress invalid explanations and revalidate after feature or process revisions.
