## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The March raw fitness collapse does imply a major observability break, but it does not by itself prove the business process broke in March. Raw fitness falls from 0.93 to 0.55, whereas mapped fitness stays near 0.90. That pattern is exactly what we would expect if `Validate Eligibility` was renamed to `Eligibility Screen` and duplicated `Open Case` events were injected into some traces. Roughly 72-82% of the apparent post-March nonconformance is recovered by mapping labels and removing duplicated technical opens.

What that gap does not imply is “the process was unchanged.” A label mapping can restore conformance to an old control-flow model when the same step is recorded under a different name, but it says nothing about whether later steps became slower, loopier, or differently routed. Mapped fitness itself still drifts down to 0.86-0.89 after May.

Duplicated technical events mainly distort raw variants, DFG counts, and alignment costs. Missing resources affect a different layer: organizational mining, handover analysis, workload attribution, and team comparisons. Because missingness is concentrated in migrated activities and is not random, resource-based conclusions are especially fragile.

The post-May pattern cannot be dismissed as logging drift alone. In the normalized DFG fragment, `Assess -> Request Info` rises from 12 to 24 and then 29 per 100 cases, `CollectDocs -> Assess` drops, decision completion falls from 83 to 72, median and p90 cycle times deteriorate sharply, and `Assess` service time changes only modestly while queue waiting grows much more. That is real behavioral and performance drift layered on top of the March logging break.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 | calendar month | Preserve a global operational view and detect large system-wide breaks | all cases | Broad timing of change points | High aggregation can mask opposite movements across subpopulations |
| L1 | month x supplier group (`Z`, `non-Z`) | Separate supplier-driven effects from general drift | at least 80 cases per cell or pool adjacent months | Supplier shock, recovery, and residual backlog effects | Supplier assignment is not random |
| L1 | month x complexity bucket (`high`, `standard`) | Separate case-mix shifts from within-class behavior changes | at least 80 cases per cell | Renewal-season effects and escalation behavior | Complexity labeling may be imperfect |
| L2 | month x supplier group x complexity bucket | Primary causal-monitoring segmentation using the two strongest pre-treatment confounders | at least 50 cases per cell; otherwise two-month pooling | Distinguishes population shifts from supplier-specific behavioral drift | Small cells late in the year for rare combinations |
| L2 | post-2026-07 month x priority class x complexity bucket | Detect redistribution caused by the July priority rule | at least 50 cases per cell | Who benefits and who waits under prioritization | Priority class may be inferred rather than directly logged |
| L2 | month x region cluster x supplier group | Test whether Supplier Z effects are actually region-mediated | at least 60 cases per cell | Supplier vs geography discrimination | Regions may proxy hidden socioeconomic mix |
| L3 | month x assessor team on resource-complete subset | Diagnose local queueing or work redistribution only after data-quality checks | at least 70 cases and at least 85% resource completeness | Staffing-loss impact, local bottlenecks, handovers | Restricting to complete cases can select cleaner work |

The primary monitoring population should be month x supplier group x complexity bucket. It keeps the two most important pre-treatment drivers visible, avoids conditioning on outcomes or missing resources, and is still coarse enough to maintain workable support if low-volume cells are pooled across adjacent months.

Seasonality should be handled by regime-specific baselines now and month-of-year baselines once more history exists. In this dataset, October-December should not share thresholds with January-July. Multiple testing should be handled hierarchically: first label/schema monitors, then control-flow monitors, then performance monitors. Within each family, use false-discovery control or require corroboration from at least two related monitors before operational escalation, while allowing a single severe schema failure to trigger immediate data-quality action.

def load_tables():
    monthly = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(MONTHLY_CSV))
    dfg = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(NORM_DFG_CSV))
    return monthly, dfg


def validate_tables(monthly: pd.DataFrame, dfg: pd.DataFrame):
    monthly = monthly.copy()
    dfg = dfg.copy()
    monthly["month"] = pd.PeriodIndex(monthly["month"], freq="M")
    dfg["month"] = pd.PeriodIndex(dfg["month"], freq="M")

    if not monthly["month"].is_monotonic_increasing:
        raise ValueError("Monthly table is not sorted by month.")
    if not set(dfg["month"]).issubset(set(monthly["month"])):
        raise ValueError("DFG months must be a subset of monthly summary months.")

    pct_cols = [
        "high_complexity_pct",
        "supplier_z_pct",
        "fast_variant_pct",
        "rework_variant_pct",
        "missing_resource_pct",
        "duplicate_open_case_pct",
    ]
    for col in pct_cols:
        if not ((monthly[col] >= 0) & (monthly[col] <= 100)).all():
            raise ValueError(f"{col} should be between 0 and 100.")

    for col in ["raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness"]:
        if not ((monthly[col] >= 0) & (monthly[col] <= 1)).all():
            raise ValueError(f"{col} should be between 0 and 1.")

    if not (monthly["mapped_model_fitness"] >= monthly["raw_model_fitness"]).all():
        raise ValueError("Mapped fitness should not be below raw fitness in this supplied summary.")

    edge_cols = [c for c in dfg.columns if c != "month"]
    if not ((dfg[edge_cols] >= 0) & (dfg[edge_cols] <= 100)).all().all():
        raise ValueError("Normalized DFG rates should be between 0 and 100 occurrences per 100 cases.")

    return monthly, dfg


def baseline_relative_changes(monthly: pd.DataFrame, baseline_months=("2026-01", "2026-02")):
    work = monthly.copy()
    mask = work["month"].astype(str).isin(baseline_months)
    baseline = work.loc[mask].mean(numeric_only=True)
    numeric_cols = [c for c in work.columns if c != "month"]
    out = work[["month"]].copy()
    for col in numeric_cols:
        out[f"{col}_delta"] = work[col] - baseline[col]
    return baseline, out


def dfg_distance_table(dfg: pd.DataFrame, base_month="2026-01"):
    edge_cols = [c for c in dfg.columns if c != "month"]
    base = dfg.loc[dfg["month"].astype(str) == base_month, edge_cols].iloc[0]
    rows = []
    for _, row in dfg.iterrows():
        diff = row[edge_cols] - base
        rows.append(
            {
                "month": str(row["month"]),
                "l1_mean_abs_distance": diff.abs().mean(),
                "euclidean_distance": float(np.sqrt((diff ** 2).sum())),
                "largest_edge_shift": diff.abs().idxmax(),
                "largest_edge_shift_value": float(diff.loc[diff.abs().idxmax()]),
            }
        )
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)


def raw_to_mapped_recovery(monthly: pd.DataFrame):
    out = monthly[["month", "raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness"]].copy()
    out["fitness_gap"] = out["mapped_model_fitness"] - out["raw_model_fitness"]
    out["recovery_fraction_of_apparent_nonconformance"] = np.where(
        (1 - out["raw_model_fitness"]) > 0,
        out["fitness_gap"] / (1 - out["raw_model_fitness"]),
        np.nan,
    )

def simple_change_points(monthly: pd.DataFrame, metric: str, min_abs_change: float):
    work = monthly[["month", metric]].copy()
    work["delta"] = work[metric].diff()
    scale = np.nanmedian(np.abs(work["delta"].dropna()))
    threshold = max(min_abs_change, 2.5 * scale if np.isfinite(scale) else min_abs_change)
    flags = work.loc[work["delta"].abs() >= threshold, ["month", "delta"]].copy()
    flags["metric"] = metric
    flags["threshold"] = threshold
    return flags[["metric", "month", "delta", "threshold"]]

from typing import Dict, Iterable, Optional, Set

try:

def _require_pm4py():
    if pm4py is None:

def format_event_log(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    case_id_col: str = "case_id",
    activity_col: str = "activity",
    timestamp_col: str = "timestamp",
    resource_col: Optional[str] = "resource",
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    _require_pm4py()
    work = df.copy()
    rename_map = {
        case_id_col: "case:concept:name",
        activity_col: "concept:name",
        timestamp_col: "time:timestamp",
    }
    if resource_col and resource_col in work.columns:
        rename_map[resource_col] = "org:resource"
    work = work.rename(columns=rename_map)
    work["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(work["time:timestamp"], errors="coerce", utc=True)
    if work["time:timestamp"].isna().any():
        raise ValueError("Found unparsable timestamps.")
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(
        work,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    )


def filter_window(
    formatted_df: pd.DataFrame,
    start: str,
    end: str,
    mode: str = "traces_intersecting",
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    _require_pm4py()
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(
        formatted_df,
        start,
        end,
        mode=mode,
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    )


def discover_monthly_dfgs(
    formatted_df: pd.DataFrame,
    months: Iterable[str],
    mode: str = "traces_intersecting",
) -> Dict[str, Dict[str, object]]:
    _require_pm4py()
    out: Dict[str, Dict[str, object]] = {}
    for month in months:
        period = pd.Period(month, freq="M")
        start = str(period.start_time.date())
        end = str((period + 1).start_time.date())
        sliced = filter_window(formatted_df, start, end, mode=mode)
        dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(
            sliced,
            activity_key="concept:name",
            timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
            case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        )
        out[str(period)] = {
            "dfg": dfg,
            "start_activities": start_acts,
            "end_activities": end_acts,
            "cases": sliced["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
        }
    return out

def build_raw_and_mapped_logs(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    label_mapping: Dict[str, str],
    case_id_col: str = "case_id",
    activity_col: str = "activity",
    timestamp_col: str = "timestamp",
    resource_col: Optional[str] = "resource",
    dedup_activity_names: Optional[Set[str]] = None,
) -> Dict[str, pd.DataFrame]:
    raw_df = df.copy()
    mapped_df = df.copy()
    mapped_activity_col = f"{activity_col}_mapped"
    mapped_df[mapped_activity_col] = mapped_df[activity_col].replace(label_mapping)

    if dedup_activity_names:
        tech_mask = mapped_df[mapped_activity_col].isin(dedup_activity_names)
        dedup_subset = [case_id_col, mapped_activity_col, timestamp_col]
        mapped_nontech = mapped_df.loc[~tech_mask]
        mapped_tech = (
            mapped_df.loc[tech_mask]
            .sort_values([case_id_col, timestamp_col])
            .drop_duplicates(subset=dedup_subset, keep="first")
        )
        mapped_df = (
            pd.concat([mapped_nontech, mapped_tech], ignore_index=True)
            .sort_values([case_id_col, timestamp_col])
            .reset_index(drop=True)
        )

    raw_log = format_event_log(
        raw_df,
        case_id_col=case_id_col,
        activity_col=activity_col,
        timestamp_col=timestamp_col,
        resource_col=resource_col,
    )
    mapped_log = format_event_log(
        mapped_df,
        case_id_col=case_id_col,
        activity_col=mapped_activity_col,
        timestamp_col=timestamp_col,
        resource_col=resource_col,
    )
    return {"raw_log": raw_log, "mapped_log": mapped_log}

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest conclusion is that the March break is primarily logging and semantic drift, not a sudden collapse of the real process. The evidence is the raw-to-mapped gap: raw fitness falls by 0.38 from the Jan-Feb baseline, but mapped fitness falls only 0.03, while label count, duplicate `Open Case`, and missing resources all jump at the same time. I would assign high confidence, about 0.9+, to “migration-induced observability drift” as the main March mechanism.

The next strongest conclusion is that the post-May deterioration is real operational drift. I would assign high confidence, about 0.8-0.9, to supplier-driven rework beginning around 2026-05-05. Supplier Z share nearly triples relative to baseline, `Assess -> Request Info` and `RequestInfo -> Collect Documents` roughly double immediately, fast variants collapse, and cycle time rises. Logging alone does not explain normalized DFG changes plus worsening time profiles.

I would assign moderately high confidence, about 0.75-0.85, to staffing-driven queueing becoming a major amplifier after the 2026-06-15 departures. The key clue is that `Assess` service time changes only modestly, whereas reconstructed waiting before `Assess` increases much more and the p90 inflates faster than the median. That pattern is queueing, not merely slower service.

The July priority rule is plausible but less directly proven from aggregates. I would assign moderate confidence, about 0.6-0.7, that it redistributed delay rather than improving system-wide performance. It is temporally aligned with continued worsening, but only segment-level waits by complexity or priority can confirm that standard cases absorbed more delay.

The October-November deterioration is best attributed to recurring seasonal population drift interacting with the existing backlog and reduced capacity. Confidence is high, about 0.85, because volume, high-complexity share, and `Senior Assessment` all surge together in the supplied timeline. December improvement is real but mixed: moderate confidence, about 0.7, that overtime and the document-quality campaign helped, with the rest due to lower load and lower Supplier Z exposure.

I would revise this account if supplier-stratified mapped DFGs showed no within-supplier rework increase after May, if priority-stratified waits showed no redistribution after July, or if resource completeness audits showed that timing fields rather than resource fields were materially corrupted post-migration.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. `IF raw fitness collapses while mapped fitness and mapped DFG distance stay near baseline, THEN classify the incident as a logging/schema problem and repair mappings before changing operations, BECAUSE semantic relabeling and duplicate technical events create apparent nonconformance without equivalent business-process change.`
2. `IF Assess->Request Info and RequestInfo->Collect Documents rise together within Supplier Z and within fixed complexity bands, THEN trigger supplier remediation and temporary intake-quality controls, BECAUSE that paired edge increase is the signature of incomplete bundles causing real rework.`
3. `IF median and p90 waiting before Assess rise while Assess service time stays roughly stable, THEN treat capacity and queueing as the primary bottleneck and rebalance staffing or WIP controls, BECAUSE the extra cycle time is being created mainly before service rather than during service.`
4. `IF high-complexity or priority cases improve after 2026-07-01 while standard cases worsen, THEN review and retune the priority rule, BECAUSE the mechanism is delay redistribution rather than net capacity creation.`
5. `IF October-November volume, complexity, and Senior Assessment usage rise together but within-segment mapped control flow stays near prior renewal baselines, THEN classify the change as recurring seasonal population drift and answer it with seasonal staffing plans, BECAUSE the dominant mechanism is recurrent case-mix and load growth.`
6. `IF missing resource rates spike in migrated activities or specific teams, THEN quarantine resource-based bottleneck conclusions until completeness is restored, BECAUSE nonrandom missingness can counterfeit staffing or routing findings even when control-flow evidence is intact.`
7. `IF December-like improvements coincide with both active interventions and lower seasonal load, THEN hold attribution as mixed and run matched segment-level before/after evaluation before making permanent policy changes, BECAUSE seasonal easing can mimic intervention success.`
