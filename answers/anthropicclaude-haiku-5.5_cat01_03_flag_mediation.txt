## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

**Framework.** I use **interventional analogues** of direct and indirect effects, plus a **controlled direct effect** for the "bundle minus review" contrast. Natural direct and indirect effects are not identified here because they require cross-world values of mediators such as queue waits and reviewer judgment, which cannot be jointly observed. The data also violate no-interference, since one case's review occupies a reviewer that another case needs. The flag is treated as a bundle of two separable components: a **priority lane** and a **mandatory-review mandate**. I use separable-effect reasoning to ask which component carries the delay. The flag is also not manipulable independently of `risk_score`, so every contrast is a policy contrast over the routing rules and the score-to-flag threshold, not a literal "set flag = 0" intervention.

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | P0 vs. P_noflag (flag-triggered lane and mandatory review removed; unflagged rules applied at the same score) | Cases with score in a local band (0.58–0.80), future arrivals | Arrival-to-close cycle time | Total policy effect | Partly (policy-level, under a stable queue model) | Shared-queue interference; flag is a deterministic function of score, so positivity holds only in a narrow band |
| E2 | Priority lane on vs. off, holding score fixed | Flagged-band cases | Triage completion time (triage_done  arrival) | Total effect on the first mediator; controlled direct effect of lane | Yes, if lane can be toggled independently of display | Lane-off is not observed for flagged cases; arrival_load confounds triage wait |
| E3 | Review occurrence distribution under P0 vs. P2 (mandatory review removed), lane held fixed | Flagged cases | Cycle time | Interventional indirect effect via review occurrence | Partly | Review occurrence depends on U_reviewer_judgment and queue state; exposure-induced confounding with the outcome |
| E4 | Manual-queue wait under P0 vs. P3 (protected capacity), lane and occurrence fixed | Reviewed cases | Cycle time | Interventional indirect effect via review waiting | No at the case level (queue wait depends on other cases' treatments); yes as an aggregate queue-state effect | Interference: wait is a joint function of all cases' treatments; need queue-state conditioning |
| E5 | Reviewer assignment (M2 vs. other reviewers) with occurrence fixed | Reviewed cases | Manual service duration and downstream escalation | Controlled direct effect of reviewer; mediated effect on escalation | Partly | Reviewer assignment is non-random and tied to complexity and escalation risk |
| E6 | Escalation occurrence under P0 vs. P4 (criteria change), review fixed | Reviewed cases | Cycle time and decision | Interventional indirect effect via escalation | Partly | Escalation is downstream of review outcome and U_reviewer_judgment, a feedback loop |
| E7 | Senior-queue wait under P0 vs. P4 (added senior capacity) | Escalated cases | Escalation waiting and cycle time | Interventional indirect effect via escalation waiting | Partly (aggregate) | Senior capacity state is shared; escalations of other cases shift this wait |
| E8 | Mean cycle time across the arrival stream, P_k vs. P0 | All arrivals in a window | Cycle time (mean, p90) | Total policy effect, aggregate | Yes as a simulation estimand under a fitted model | Requires a queue model and calibration to timestamps; model-dependent |
| E9 | Decision quality under P_k vs. P0 (agreement with a blinded adjudicated reference) | Reviewed and unreviewed cases in the band | Concordance and missed high-risk rate | Total effect on decision quality; controlled direct effect of removing review | No with current data (no ground truth; escalation outcomes are themselves post-treatment) | No reference standard; outcome is observed only for escalated or reviewed cases |
| E10 | Effect of flag on unflagged spillover: unflagged manual wait under P0 vs. P3 | Unflagged cases | Manual wait and cycle time | Spillover (interference) effect | Partly | Unflagged cases are few (n=4 reviewed) and their arrivals are interleaved with flagged ones |

**Where interference and feedback arise.** Review and senior queues are shared, so one flagged case's review occupies capacity that other cases need. This makes queue wait a joint function of the policy, and cross-world mediator values are not defined at the case level. Escalation is conditioned on review, and the review decision is shaped by U_reviewer_judgment and U_underlying_risk. Conditioning on escalation therefore opens paths between the flag and the outcome through unmeasured risk. Escalated cases also consume senior capacity, which lengthens waits for later cases, so treatment at time t changes the mediator distribution for cases at t+k. This is treatment-confounder feedback. The queue state at each manual or escalation start is a time-varying confounder that depends on earlier flags. Reviewers may also adapt their judgment to visible queue length, which makes review occurrence depend on the congestion that the flag itself creates.

The figures show that the flagged-high-load stage times are close to the flagged-normal-load ones for triage, but manual-queue wait is longer under high load (7.4 vs 5.1 h). Load therefore acts mainly through the review queue.

**Stage handling.** Triage always occurs. Manual review and escalation are conditioned on occurrence. Residual time is measured from the end of the last completed stage (triage, manual, or escalation). Escalation always follows manual review in these data.

**Interpreting the contrasts.** Flagged cases start triage about 2.2 h sooner on average (0.24 vs 2.5 h), which is consistent with a priority lane. Flagged cases then wait about 6.5 h for review, versus about 1.9 h for the few unflagged reviews, and 6 of 10 are escalated, with a further 7 h wait. Triage acceleration is therefore small relative to the downstream delay. Several of these contrasts are confounded: flagged cases are concentrated on high complexity and high load, and flagged reviews share queues. The contrasts are descriptive. They do not show that the flag causes the downstream delay, because the same complexity and risk that produce the flag may also produce review and escalation.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Does the flag itself cause the delay, holding risk fixed? | **Local-threshold (regression discontinuity) around the score cutoff.** Compare cases just above and below the score threshold (0.62–0.64 in the data). Identifies the total effect of the flag bundle for marginal cases. | Score polynomial within band; calendar time; arrival_load | Continuity of unobserved risk at the cutoff; no manipulation of scores near the cutoff | Density test of scores near the cutoff; covariate balance (complexity, load) across the threshold | Imbalance in complexity or a bunching of scores at the cutoff would weaken the design. Strong imbalance would reverse the causal reading. |
| Does the lane speed triage, and is that speed the cause of later delay? | **Queue-state-aware triage analysis.** Model triage wait conditional on triage-queue length at arrival. Compare flagged vs unflagged within queue-length strata. | Queue length at arrival; arrival_load; triage resource; hour of day | Queue length is measured without error from timestamps; no unmeasured shifts in staffing | Placebo test: triage wait for unflagged cases during periods with high flagged volume | If triage speed-up disappears within queue strata, the priority lane is not the cause and the apparent gain comes from queue timing. |
| Does review occurrence mediate the delay? | **Interventional mediation analysis** with review occurrence as mediator and queue state as a time-varying confounder. Estimate E3 with g-computation or marginal structural models. | Score, complexity, arrival_load, queue state at triage_done, reviewer, hour | Sequential ignorability of review given observed history; no unmeasured confounding of review and outcome given history | Sensitivity of the indirect effect to an unmeasured confounder of review and outcome | Large sensitivity bound (small confounder strength reverses the effect) weakens the mediation claim. |
| Is the policy change itself the cause, or the flag per se? | **Randomized encouragement or policy-change design.** Randomly assign a review nudge (not the flag) or switch rules by time block to estimate the effect of review. | Block (shift); arrival_load; complexity | Encouragement affects review only through review occurrence (exclusion restriction) | Compare pre-period and post-period outcomes for unflagged cases, which should not change | Changes in unflagged cases would indicate spillover or a secular trend, undermining the policy-only reading. |
| Are the observed associations an artifact of timestamps or data generation? | **Negative-control outcome and exposure.** Negative-control outcome: arrival-to-register lag (not affected by flag). Negative-control exposure: a placebo flag using pre-policy scores. | Same as main model | Negative controls share confounding but not effect | Estimated effect on negative-control outcome should be zero | A non-zero placebo effect suggests confounding or a timestamp artifact and would reverse the main conclusion. |
| How strong must unobserved risk be to explain the gap? | **Sensitivity analysis for unobserved risk** using E-value or an omitted-variable bias formula. Bound how strong an unobserved risk factor would need to be to shift the flag's apparent effect to zero. | Observed proxies: complexity, load, resource | Unmeasured confounder is one-dimensional; bias-to-effect ratio is interpretable | Compare the bound to observed covariate effects (complexity) | If observed covariates explain most of the gap, the flag effect is small. If the bound requires implausibly strong unobserved risk, the causal reading is stronger. |
| How do queues propagate effects across cases? | **Interference-aware design.** Switchback experiment by time block (e.g., 4-hour blocks), with blocks as units and queue-state summaries as covariates. Estimate the policy effect on aggregate waits, not individual waits. | Block-level arrival_load and queue lengths; time of day | Blocks are independent conditional on covariates; no carryover | Carryover test: compare first hour after switch with later hours | Strong carryover (waits persist after switch) would show queue interference and invalidate block-level estimates. |
| Is decision quality preserved? | **Blinded second-reader audit.** A random sample of unreviewed flagged cases is re-reviewed blind to the flag. | Stratify by score band and complexity | Auditors are blinded; audit sample is random | Concordance rate between audit and original decision | Concordance below a pre-set threshold, or missed high-severity cases, would reverse any recommendation to remove review. |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 (current) | Flagged triage wait 0.2–0.3 h | Flagged review wait 6.5 h; escalation waits 4–10 h | Flagged 16 h; unflagged 8 h (observed) | Baseline; all rejects escalated | Flagged cases dominate review capacity | Observed, n=20, weak for causal claims | Keep only as control arm |
| P1 (suppress display only) | Unchanged if lane is automatic; reverts toward 2.5 h wait if lane depends on display | Changes only through reviewer behavior; uncertain sign | Range 2 to +2 h; not identified from current data | Less human context; transparency concerns; score still visible | Limited direct spillover | Weak; no display variation in data | Use as diagnostic arm for reviewer behavior, not as primary policy |
| P2 (keep priority lane, remove automatic mandatory review) | Unchanged (0.2–0.6 h) | Review occurrence for flagged cases falls (e.g., 100%  40–70%); manual queue shrinks | Flagged 3 to 8 h for cases no longer reviewed | Misses if removed reviews include high-risk cases; decision-quality risk | Positive: frees reviewer capacity for unflagged cases | Moderate if randomized; none yet | **Recommended pilot** |
| P3 (protected review capacity for flagged) | Unchanged | Flagged review wait falls to 2–3 h; unflagged waits rise; escalations reach senior queue earlier | Flagged 2 to 4 h; unflagged may rise | Decision quality maintained; senior congestion may worsen | Negative for unflagged cases; senior queue pressure | Weak to moderate | Use as a guardrail component, not standalone |
| P4 (change escalation criteria or add senior capacity) | Unchanged | Escalation waits (4–10 h) could halve with added capacity | Escalated flagged 3 to 6 h | Criteria change alters decision mix and reject rate; needs audit | Frees senior capacity for others | Weak (8 escalations) | Next step after P2 pilot; biggest single-case lever |

Policies do not have identical direct or indirect effects. P1 changes information. P2 changes whether review occurs. P3 changes queue order. P4 changes the senior queue. The shortest expected cycle time is not automatically best: P2 could shorten cycle time while increasing missed high-risk cases, and P3 could shorten flagged cycle time while lengthening unflagged waits.

**Selected pilot: P2 (randomized conditional review).**

- **Eligibility:** Flagged cases with score 0.62–0.80 at intake, excluding High complexity cases. Unflagged cases are not enrolled but are monitored for spillover.
- **Assignment rule:** Block randomization 1:1 within shift and arrival_load strata. Control: current mandatory review. Treatment: triage-led review, with review required only for a triage override reason, plus a 15% random audit.
- **Queue-capacity guardrail:** Pause enrollment if the manual queue exceeds a set length or if flagged review waits exceed 8 h for two consecutive days.
- **Primary outcome:** Arrival-to-close time for eligible cases (median and p90).
- **Secondary outcomes:** Triage completion time; manual wait for all cases (spillover); escalation rate; audit concordance with blinded second reader; 30-day adverse events; reviewer utilization.
- **Minimum process data:** Start and completion timestamps for triage, review, and escalation; resource IDs; queue length at each review and escalation start; assignment log; score, complexity, load, and outcome.
- **Stopping conditions:** Stop for harm if concordance falls more than 5 percentage points below control or any missed high-severity case is confirmed. Stop for futility if the interim estimate of cycle-time reduction is below 1 h. Stop for capacity if the queue guardrail triggers on more than 3 days.

    rows = []
    for _, r in w.iterrows():
        common = dict(
            **{"case:concept:name": r["case"]},
            flag=r["flag"], complexity=r["complexity"],
            arrival_load=r["arrival_load"], risk_score=r["risk_score"],
            outcome=r["outcome"])

        def add(activity, start, end, resource):
            rows.append({**common,
                         "concept:name": activity,
                         "start_timestamp": start,
                         "time:timestamp": end,
                         "org:resource": resource})

        add("Register", r["arrival"], r["arrival"], np.nan)
        if pd.notna(r["triage_start"]):
            add("Triage", r["triage_start"], r["triage_done"], r["triage_resource"])
        if pd.notna(r["manual_start"]):
            add("Manual Review", r["manual_start"], r["manual_done"], r["review_resource"])
        if pd.notna(r["escalation_start"]):
            # Escalation resource is not recorded in the source data
            add("Escalation", r["escalation_start"], r["escalation_done"], np.nan)
        add("Close", r["close"], r["close"], np.nan)
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

events = build_events(wide)

# 3. Format with PM4Py, supplying the start-timestamp key
if pm4py is not None:
    events = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        events,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")

# 4. Explicit stage durations (no zero-length stand-ins for absent stages)
def stage_table(w):
    out = []
    for _, r in w.iterrows():
        d = {"case": r["case"], "flag": r["flag"], "complexity": r["complexity"],
             "load": r["arrival_load"], "outcome": r["outcome"]}
        d["arr_to_triage_wait"] = hours(r["arrival"], r["triage_start"])
        d["triage_service"] = hours(r["triage_start"], r["triage_done"])
        last = r["triage_done"]
        d["triage_to_manual_wait"] = np.nan
        d["manual_service"] = np.nan
        if pd.notna(r["manual_start"]):
            d["triage_to_manual_wait"] = hours(r["triage_done"], r["manual_start"])
            d["manual_service"] = hours(r["manual_start"], r["manual_done"])
            last = r["manual_done"]
        d["manual_to_esc_wait"] = np.nan
        d["esc_service"] = np.nan
        if pd.notna(r["escalation_start"]):
            assert pd.notna(r["manual_start"]), "escalation without review"
            d["manual_to_esc_wait"] = hours(r["manual_done"], r["escalation_start"])
            d["esc_service"] = hours(r["escalation_start"], r["escalation_done"])
            last = r["escalation_done"]
        d["residual_to_close"] = hours(last, r["close"])
        d["total_cycle"] = hours(r["arrival"], r["close"])
        out.append(d)
    return pd.DataFrame(out)

st = stage_table(wide)
STAGE_COLS = ["arr_to_triage_wait", "triage_service", "triage_to_manual_wait",
              "manual_service", "manual_to_esc_wait", "esc_service",
              "residual_to_close", "total_cycle"]

# 5. Stage summaries and support counts
def summarize(df, label):
    if len(df) < 5:
        warnings.warn(f"Subgroup '{label}' has n={len(df)} (<5): descriptive only.")
    row = {"subgroup": label, "n": len(df)}
    for c in STAGE_COLS:
        s = df[c].dropna()
        row[c] = f"{s.median():.2f} (n={len(s)})" if len(s) else "NA"
    return row

groups = {
    "flag=0": st[st.flag == 0],
    "flag=1": st[st.flag == 1],
    "flag=1, complexity High": st[(st.flag == 1) & (st.complexity == "High")],
    "flag=1, complexity Medium": st[(st.flag == 1) & (st.complexity == "Medium")],
    "flag=1, high load": st[(st.flag == 1) & (st.load == "High")],
    "flag=1, normal load": st[(st.flag == 1) & (st.load == "Normal")],
    "flag=1, escalated": st[(st.flag == 1) & st.esc_service.notna()],
    "flag=1, not escalated": st[(st.flag == 1) & st.esc_service.isna()],
    "flag=0, escalated": st[(st.flag == 0) & st.esc_service.notna()],
    "flag=0, not escalated": st[(st.flag == 0) & st.esc_service.isna()],
}
summary = pd.DataFrame([summarize(df, k) for k, df in groups.items()])
print("\n=== Stage summaries (median hours; n = cases where stage occurred) ===")
print(summary.to_string(index=False))

print("\n=== Outcome by escalation (support) ===")
print(pd.crosstab(st.esc_service.notna(), st.outcome))

# 6. Frequency and performance DFGs for flagged vs unflagged cases
def discover_dfgs(ev_subset, label):
    if pm4py is None:
        return
    print(f"\n--- DFG: {label} ---")
    try:
        dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(ev_subset)
        print("Frequency DFG edges:")
        for (a, b), n in sorted(dfg.items(), key=lambda kv: -kv[1]):
            print(f"  {a} -> {b}: {n}")
    except Exception as exc:
        print("discover_dfg failed (check PM4Py version / DataFrame support):", exc)
    try:
        perf, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(ev_subset)
        print("Performance DFG (mean seconds between consecutive recorded events):")
        for (a, b), v in perf.items():
            print(f"  {a} -> {b}: {v/3600:.2f} h")
        warnings.warn(f"{label}: performance-DFG values are gaps between recorded events, "
                      "not stage waits. Do not read them as causal effects of the flag.")
    except Exception as exc:
        print("discover_performance_dfg failed:", exc)

flagged_ev = events[events["flag"] == 1]
unflagged_ev = events[events["flag"] == 0]
discover_dfgs(flagged_ev, "flagged cases")
discover_dfgs(unflagged_ev, "unflagged cases")

# 7. Case durations
case_dur = st[["case", "flag", "total_cycle"]].sort_values("total_cycle", ascending=False)
print("\n=== Case durations (hours) ===")

This code computes stage intervals explicitly from start and completion timestamps. It does not call `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, because that function's internal conventions for absent stages are not fully specified here. The code does not estimate natural direct or indirect effects.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk.** A blinded audit shows that unflagged or mid-band cases carry high severity at rates comparable to flagged cases. Then removing mandatory review (P2) is not safe, and the recommendation reverses toward keeping review.
2. **Queue interference.** Freed reviewer capacity under P2 does not reach unflagged cases, or protected capacity under P3 causes unflagged waits to rise by more than the flagged gain. Then the expected spillover benefit of P2 disappears and P3 or P4 becomes preferred.
3. **Decision quality.** Audit concordance for the no-review arm falls more than 5 percentage points below control, or a confirmed missed high-severity case appears. Then P2 is stopped and the recommendation shifts to P4.
4. **Timestamp semantics.** If the dash or start fields actually record queue entry rather than resource start, the triage speed-up or review wait is mislabeled. The stage decomposition and the pathway attribution then change.
5. **Positivity or lack of overlap.** Within the local score band (0.58–0.67) there are too few flagged and unflagged cases, or the threshold moves. The threshold design fails, and the causal claim about the flag rests on weaker evidence.
6. **Reviewer behavior change.** Reviewers respond to the flag being hidden (P1) or to visible queue length by shortcutting reviews. Then the apparent review occurrence and wait effects reflect behavior, not the rule, and P1 and P2 effects reverse.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed.** Flagged cases start triage about 2.2 h sooner (0.24 vs 2.5 h). All 10 flagged cases are sent to manual review, against 4 of 10 unflagged. Flagged cases wait about 6.5 h for review and 6 of 10 are escalated after a further 4–10 h wait. Mean cycle time is 16.5 h for flagged cases and 8.2 h for unflagged cases. All four rejections were escalated.

**Plausible mechanism.** The priority lane speeds triage, but the mandatory review and escalation rules move the delay downstream into shared review and senior queues. Complexity and load plausibly drive both the flag and the queue waits, so part of the gap reflects case mix rather than policy.

**Unidentified.** Whether the flag causes the downstream delay, or whether unmeasured risk drives both the flag and the review and escalation decisions, is not established. Decision quality is unknown because no reference standard exists. Queue interference and reviewer behavior change are not measured.

**Most useful next evidence.** A randomized conditional-review pilot (P2) in the mid-score band provides the cleanest test of the review mechanism while protecting decision quality through blinded audit. It also yields data on spillover and on whether the queue shifts downstream.
