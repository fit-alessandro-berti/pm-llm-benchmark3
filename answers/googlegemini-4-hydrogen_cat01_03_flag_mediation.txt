## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

I avoid Natural Direct/Indirect Effects (NDE/NIE) because (i) `flag` acts as a multi-component policy bundle rather than a single atomic manipulation, (ii) downstream congestion and triage speed create **exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding** (`priority_lane` and `triage_wait` alter when cases hit shared downstream queues, confounding `manual_review` and `escalation` with `cycle_time`), and (iii) NDE/NIE rely on untestable cross-world independence assumptions. Controlled Direct Effects (CDE) are used only where a policy explicitly fixes a routing gate (e.g., setting `manual_review` = 1 vs 0).

**Interference, exposure-induced confounding, and feedback (156 words):**
Interference arises because triage (`T1–T4`), manual review (`M1–M3`), and the scarce senior team share finite capacity (`U_queue_state_*`). Flagging case $i$ accelerates its exit from triage into the manual queue and increases its probability of manual review and escalation, lengthening `manual_queue_wait` and `escalation_queue_wait` for concurrent cases $j \neq i$ regardless of $j$'s flag. Exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding occurs because `flag` alters `priority_lane`, `triage_wait`, and the timing of entry into `U_queue_state_manual`—all post-treatment variables that affect both whether/when `manual_review` and `escalation` occur and downstream waiting times. Furthermore, `U_underlying_risk` and `complexity` confound every stage transition (`manual_review` $\to$ `escalation` $\to$ `cycle_time`). Finally, treatment-confounder feedback arises dynamically across arrivals: early flagged surges deplete `M1–M3` and senior-team capacity, increasing `U_queue_state_manual` and `U_queue_state_escalation` for subsequent arrivals, which can alter human discretionary referral and escalation thresholds.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| P0: Status quo | Fast flagged triage (`~12m` wait); slow unflagged triage (`~150m` wait) | Severe manual (`~405m`) and escalation (`~420m`) backlogs | Longest for flagged (`~987m` med); moderate for unflagged (`~345m` med) | Low false-negative risk for flagged; unflagged complex cases still wait long | Heavy negative spillover from flagged surges into `M1–M3` and senior queues | Directly observed (small $n=20$) | Do not maintain unchanged; bottleneck is downstream |
| P1: Suppress flag display only | Flagged triage slows toward `~150m`; unflagged may speed up slightly | Moderate reduction in Medium-complexity manual reviews (`~5/5 -> ~1/3`?), less burstiness | Mixed: flagged triage lengthens (+2h), manual queue shrinks (-2 to -5h?); net modest change | High: removes visual alert without fixing underlying `risk_score` blindness or triage overload | Eliminates artificial `T1/T2` vs `T3/T4` segmentation; shifts load back to standard triage | Low (no overlap at `risk_score >= 0.64`) | Reject: throws away rapid intake of genuinely risky cases |

| P4: Retain manual review, change escalation criteria or add senior capacity | Unchanged | Unchanged `manual_wait`; cuts `escalation_queue_wait` (`420m -> ~100–200m`) for High-risk cases | Cuts ~3–5h off escalated cases (`R01/03/06/10/13/18/20`); zero effect on non-escalated cases | High if criteria tightened blindly (`4/4` `Reject`s were escalated); Low if senior capacity added | Positive spillover to unflagged High-risk cases (`R09, R20`) sharing the senior queue | Moderate (6/8 escalations are High complexity) | Do not tighten criteria without audit; add senior capacity or balance `M2` load |

**Recommended Pilot Design (P2 — Targeted Removal of Automatic Mandatory Review) (233 words):**
- **Eligibility:** Flagged cases (`flag = 1`) with `complexity` $\in \{\text{Low}, \text{Medium}\}$ and `risk_score` $\in [0.62, 0.80)$ (excluding `High` complexity and `risk_score >= 0.80`, which remain mandatory review).
- **Assignment rule:** Two-stage cluster/time-block randomization by 8-hour shift over 4 weeks: shifts are randomized 1:1 to **P2-Pilot** (priority triage retained, `T1/T2` specialists use structured clinical/risk checklist to decide manual review discretionarily) vs **P0-Control** (priority triage + automatic mandatory review). Shift-level assignment captures shared-queue interference and avoids contamination.
- **Queue-capacity guardrail:** If concurrent manual-queue WIP exceeds 8 cases or oldest unreviewed `High`-complexity case wait exceeds 180 minutes, auto-trigger temporary manual-review overflow protection (pause non-essential reviews and alert shift lead) regardless of arm.
- **Primary and secondary outcomes:** *Primary:* shift-level median `cycle_time` across all cases (flagged + unflagged) and `manual_queue_wait`. *Secondary:* manual-review referral rate among eligible flagged cases, `escalation` rate, `Reject` rate, and **independent blind post-close audit defect rate** (100% audit of P2-approved cases that bypassed manual review + 20% random sample of P0 approvals).
- **Minimum process data needed:** Exact stage start/end timestamps, queue WIP snapshots at each stage entry, `risk_score`, `complexity`, resource IDs (`T*`, `M*`, senior), escalation reason codes, and blind audit ground-truth labels.
- **Stopping conditions:** Immediately halt pilot if P2 blind-audit severe-error/missed-rejection rate exceeds P0 by $>2$ percentage points, or if `High`-complexity `cycle_time` worsens by $>15\%$ over 3 consecutive shifts.

# 6. Print stage summaries and support counts across subgroups
def summarize_subgroups(df, group_col, label):
    print(f"\n=== Stage Summaries (minutes): {label} ===")
    for grp, gdf in df.groupby(group_col, dropna=False):
        n = len(gdf)
        if n < 10:
            warnings.warn(
                f"Small subgroup '{label}={grp}' (n={n} < 10): estimates have high variance.",
                UserWarning,

            k = len(valid)

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk (`U_underlying_risk`):** If unflagged cases (`flag = 0`) and flagged cases (`flag = 1`) at similar `risk_score` differ drastically in unmeasured severity—such that human triage under P2 would either refer $>85\%$ of flagged Medium cases anyway or miss genuine high-risk frauds among skipped cases—then removing automatic manual review (P2) would fail to save capacity or would unacceptably degrade decision quality.
2. **Queue interference:** If `M1–M3` are actually independent, non-fungible specialized teams (e.g., `M2` handles only High complexity while `M1/M3` cannot cross-cover) rather than a shared capacity pool, then freeing `M1/M3` capacity via P2 will produce zero spillover relief for `High`-complexity or escalated cases.
3. **Decision quality:** If post-close blind audits reveal that `manual_review` on flagged Medium-complexity cases (`R02, R08, R12, R15`) caught subtle defects that required corrective conditions before `Approve` (even though `outcome` reads `Approve`), then removing automatic manual review under P2 would be reversed in favor of P3 (adding capacity).
4. **Timestamp semantics:** If `triage_done`-to-`manual_start` (and `manual_done`-to-`escalation_start`) does not represent pure active queue waiting, but instead includes external customer-response waits, off-shift closures (`22:00–06:00`), or batch-release schedules, then adding reviewer capacity (P3/P4) would not shorten those gaps.
5. **Positivity or lack of overlap:** Because `flag = 1` occurs exclusively at `risk_score >= 0.64` and `Low` complexity occurs exclusively at `flag = 0` (`n = 20` across one 36-hour window), any conclusion extrapolated from `flag = 0` Medium cases to `flag = 1` Medium cases reverses if the relationship between `risk_score` and `manual_service`/escalation jumps discontinuously above `0.62`.
6. **Behavior change by reviewers (Goodhart / automation bias):** If removing automatic manual review (P2) or suppressing the flag (P1) causes `T1/T2` triage staff to defensively slow down triage (increasing `triage_service` from 36m to >70m) or over-refer borderline cases out of liability fear, downstream congestion would shift upstream or persist unchanged.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed (from the 20-case log):** Flagged cases (`n=10`) enter triage much faster than unflagged cases (`12m` vs `150m` median wait; `36m` vs `57m` triage service), yet finish with nearly triple the median cycle time (`987m` vs `345m`). Every flagged case enters manual review (`10/10` vs `4/10`), waits substantially longer for manual review (`405m` vs `105m` conditional median, rising to `420m` under high arrival load), and escalates more frequently (`6/10` vs `2/10`), adding another `~420m` wait plus `~120m` senior service. Flagged cases also carry no `Low`-complexity cases (`0/10` vs `4/10`) and concentrate heavily in `M2` (`5/10` flagged cases, all High complexity).

**Plausible mechanism:** The flag acts as a bundled policy: priority triage accelerates flagged intake and dumps cases into a shared, capacity-constrained manual review queue, where mandatory routing of `Medium`-complexity flagged cases (`5/5` vs `1/3` unflagged) amplifies congestion and feeds a scarce senior escalation team (`7/8` High-complexity cases escalate).

**Unidentified:** With $n=20$, zero `risk_score` overlap across the threshold (`0.61` vs `0.64`), unmeasured `U_underlying_risk`, and no ground-truth label for `Approve`/`Reject` accuracy, we cannot cleanly separate case-mix confounding from pure routing effects, identify cross-world natural direct/indirect effects, or quantify false-negative quality risk if reviews are skipped.

**Most informative next intervention:** A shift-randomized **P2 pilot**—retaining priority triage while replacing automatic mandatory manual review with structured discretionary referral for `Medium`-complexity flagged cases (`0.62 <= risk_score < 0.80`), paired with blind post-close audits—directly isolates the mandatory-review pathway while measuring both queue spillover and decision quality.
