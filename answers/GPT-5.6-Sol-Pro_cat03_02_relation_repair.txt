## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

Confidence values are calibrated judgments, not learned probabilities. They rank support within this dataset; candidates such as `ev11 -> DEL-X` and `ev11 -> DEL-Y` are correlated alternatives, so their scores must not be added.

* **Hard invariants.** Every endpoint must exist; no new event or object may be invented; an identical E2O or O2O tuple must not be duplicated; and directional O2O qualifiers must retain their stated direction. Thus `DEL-Y carries PK-8` is admissible, while reversing it under the same qualifier is not.
* **Strong defaults.** Activity-specific cardinalities, normal lifecycle order, and near-exact identifiers dominate the repair. A `Pack Item` should have one Item and one Package; a `Dispatch Package` one Package and one Delivery; and a `Record Payment` one Payment and usually one Invoice. `PK8-2`, `N-421?`, and `B-772` are strong evidence when they agree with object attributes and time order.
* **Weak heuristics.** A common customer, common route, matching timestamp, wildcard/prefix similarity, and amount arithmetic raise plausibility but do not settle identity. In particular, 130 + 100 = 230 supports consolidated billing and settlement, but fees, discounts, taxes, or unapplied cash remain possible.
* **Evidence against a repair.** A conflicting identifier, unnecessary cardinality inflation, lifecycle reversal, or a repair whose only purpose is to eliminate an orphan counts against it. `ev10 -> DEL-X`, for example, conflicts with the apparent `N-421` reference and leaves the matching `DEL-Y` unexplained.

I combine evidence by beginning with lifecycle/cardinality priors, adding identifier and temporal support, then applying customer, route, and amount only as tie-breakers. Existing O2O structure is useful when genuinely pre-existing, but an O2O edge added from an assumed E2O edge is not independent corroboration. Attribute fields that may derive from the same source are also discounted rather than double-counted.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A — conservative/minimum-change repair (selected).** Add `ev08 -> PK-8` and `PK-8 packs IT-B1`; add `ev10 -> DEL-Y` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`; add `ev14 -> INV-44` and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`. Leave `ev11` related only to the observed `ORD-A`; do not choose DEL-X, DEL-Y, both, or ORD-B. Leave `INV-44 -> ORD-B` unresolved. This yields a coherent two-item PK-8, two dispatched deliveries, and one invoice with two recorded payments, without asserting that either delivery was confirmed or that ORD-B was billed.

**Bundle B — consolidated customer fulfilment and billing.** Use Bundle A’s ev08, ev10, and PAY-2 repairs. In addition, relate ev11 to both DEL-X and DEL-Y and to ORD-B, interpreting `N-42*` as a bundled portal acknowledgement for customer C17. Relate ev12 to ORD-B and add `INV-44 bills ORD-B`. The bundle is internally coherent: both deliveries are acknowledged together, the invoice covers both orders, and PAY-1/PAY-2 exactly cover its stated total. Its weakness is dependence on several correlated clues—customer, prefix, and arithmetic—and it converts one ambiguous portal row into broad many-object behavior.

**Bundle C — single physical delivery with a corrupted carrier reference.** Relate ev08 to PK-8, but assign ev10 and PK-8 to DEL-X, treating `N-421?` as erroneous and DEL-Y as a planned or unused object. Relate ev11 only to DEL-X, retain its observed ORD-A link, leave ORD-B unadded, and use Bundle B’s consolidated invoice plus PAY-2 settlement. This produces a bulk DEL-X lifecycle containing two same-time dispatch rows followed by confirmation. It is coherent, but weaker than A or B because it requires both an identifier error and an unexplained DEL-Y.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Order.** ORD-A has `Create Order -> Add Item -> Add Item -> Confirm Delivery -> Issue Invoice`, a plausible directly related lifecycle. ORD-B remains `Create Order -> Add Item` under Bundle A, but its item proceeds indirectly through PK-8 and DEL-Y. That is incomplete, not orphaned. Bundle A deliberately does not manufacture confirmation or billing completion.

**Item.** All three Items have one Add Item and one Pack Item. Each has one containing Order. IT-B1 gains one Package, satisfying the strong packing default. The PK-8-to-IT-B1 O2O edge and ev08 E2O edge express the same repair and therefore are not two independent observations.

**Package.** PK-7 has one packed Item and one dispatch through DEL-X. PK-8 has two Pack Item events—IT-A2 and IT-B1—then dispatch through DEL-Y. One Package packing multiple Items is plausible; the notable feature is that PK-8 crosses Orders. This is one-to-many, not necessarily erroneous, but it couples ORD-A and ORD-B.

**Delivery.** DEL-X and DEL-Y each have one dispatch after Bundle A. Neither has a confirmed-delivery E2O relation. Dispatch-only lifecycles are plausible in an incomplete extract. DEL-Y was the only event-orphan object before repair and is no longer orphaned, but orphan removal was not the deciding evidence.

**Invoice.** INV-44 has `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment -> Record Payment`. It bills ORD-A in the selected repair and may or may not bill ORD-B. Two Payments to one Invoice are expected partial-settlement cardinality. The repeated `Record Payment` activity is legitimate, not a duplicate event.

**Payment.** PAY-1 and PAY-2 each have exactly one Record Payment event and one Invoice target after repair. PAY-2’s “settles” qualifier should be read cautiously: if the business uses it for any allocation, it is suitable; if it means final legal discharge, `applies_to` would be safer.

No event is relation-orphaned: every event already had at least one E2O relation. Pre-existing pairs such as ORD-A/IT-A1, PK-7/IT-A1, DEL-X/PK-7, INV-44/ORD-A, and PAY-1/INV-44 have both observed E2O co-participation and observed O2O structure, although their source-system independence is unknown. By contrast, none of the newly added O2O edges independently validates its E2O counterpart. Treating those generated pairs as confirmation would be circular reasoning; the same warning applies to Bundle B’s proposed `ev12 -> ORD-B` and `INV-44 bills ORD-B`.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**OC-DFG.** The unrepaired Package trace for PK-8 is `Pack Item(ev07) -> Dispatch Package(ev10)`. Bundle A changes it to `Pack Item(ev07) -> Pack Item(ev08) -> Dispatch Package(ev10)`, adding a Package-type self-loop `Pack Item -> Pack Item`; the aggregate `Pack Item -> Dispatch Package` count need not increase because its PK-8 predecessor merely changes from ev07 to ev08. INV-44 changes from `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment(ev13)` to `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment(ev13) -> Record Payment(ev14)`, adding `Record Payment -> Record Payment`. Order behavior is unchanged in A. In B, ORD-B gains `Add Item -> Confirm Delivery -> Issue Invoice`, increasing those Order edges, while both Delivery objects gain `Dispatch Package -> Confirm Delivery`.

**OTG and ET–OT.** In current PM4Py, the default OTG is built from derived interaction/lifecycle object graphs rather than by directly counting the business `ocel.o2o` table; ET–OT iterates E2O rows and counts each activity–object-type association. Thus A increases Item–Package, Package–Delivery, and Payment–Invoice interaction evidence and raises the ET–OT weights for `Pack Item–Package`, `Dispatch Package–Delivery`, and `Record Payment–Invoice`. B additionally creates `Confirm Delivery–Delivery`, raises `Confirm Delivery–Order` and `Issue Invoice–Order`, and can introduce Delivery–Delivery and Order–Delivery interaction edges through ev11. Explicit O2O additions remain important business data, but do not automatically become default OTG edges. ([GitHub][1])

**Flattenings.** Order flattening is unchanged from unrepaired to A; B duplicates shared ev11 and ev12 into both order cases. Package flattening gains ev08 in PK-8. Delivery flattening changes from one nonempty case to two in A; B gives both cases a common ev11, so one physical event appears in two traces. Invoice flattening gains ev14 and a payment self-loop. Payment flattening is unchanged because ev13 already belongs to PAY-1 and ev14 to PAY-2; the new Invoice and O2O links do not add Payment events.

**Frequency semantics.** Raw activity frequency by unique event ID never changes. Object-type event frequency can change when an old event gains a new type: under A, ev08 adds one Package-associated Pack Item and ev10 one Delivery-associated Dispatch. Unique-object and total-object measures diverge: adding ev14 to INV-44 raises total Invoice associations for Record Payment but not the number of distinct Invoices. In B, ev11 contributes one Confirm Delivery event, two distinct Delivery objects, and two total Delivery relations; adding ORD-B to ev11 leaves the unique event count for the Order type at one but raises unique and total Orders from one to two.

**Equivalent executions around Order.** Before repair, ORD-B’s E2O neighborhood is relatively separate. A’s `ev08 -> PK-8` connects IT-B1/ORD-B through PK-8 to IT-A2/ORD-A, so ancestor/descendant execution extraction may merge or enrich the two Order-centered contexts even though no Order E2O edge was added. B reinforces that coupling by sharing ev11 and ev12 directly, making both orders look more execution-equivalent and potentially duplicating customer-level events in each cluster description. Current PM4Py clustering is explicitly based on object-centered executions and their E2O descriptions, so this sensitivity should be tested rather than inferred from O2O alone. ([GitHub][2])

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

The current release constructor accepts an `o2o` dataframe, while `ocel_flattening` uses objects of the selected type as cases; the fallback below covers older constructors. ([GitHub][3])

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.ocel.obj import OCEL

# Immutable snapshots taken before any repair.
events_original = events.copy(deep=True)
objects_original = objects.copy(deep=True)
relations_original = relations.copy(deep=True)
o2o_original = o2o.copy(deep=True)


def normalize_ocel_frames(events_df, objects_df, relations_df, o2o_df):
    """Validate endpoints/metadata and return normalized copies."""
    ev = events_df.copy(deep=True)
    obj = objects_df.copy(deep=True)
    rel = relations_df.copy(deep=True)
    obj_rel = o2o_df.copy(deep=True)

    required_events = {"ocel:eid", "ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp"}
    required_objects = {"ocel:oid", "ocel:type"}
    if not required_events.issubset(ev.columns):
        raise ValueError(f"events missing {required_events - set(ev.columns)}")
    if not required_objects.issubset(obj.columns):
        raise ValueError(f"objects missing {required_objects - set(obj.columns)}")
    if ev["ocel:eid"].duplicated().any():
        raise ValueError("Duplicate event identifiers")
    if obj["ocel:oid"].duplicated().any():
        raise ValueError("Duplicate object identifiers")

    ev["ocel:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(ev["ocel:timestamp"], errors="raise")
    event_meta = ev.set_index("ocel:eid")[["ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp"]]
    object_type = obj.set_index("ocel:oid")["ocel:type"]
    event_ids, object_ids = set(event_meta.index), set(object_type.index)

    for column in [
        "ocel:eid", "ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp",
        "ocel:oid", "ocel:type", "ocel:qualifier"
    ]:
        if column not in rel.columns:
            rel[column] = pd.NA
    if rel[["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid"]].isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError("E2O rows require both event and object endpoints")
    if not set(rel["ocel:eid"]).issubset(event_ids):
        raise ValueError("E2O relation references an unknown event")
    if not set(rel["ocel:oid"]).issubset(object_ids):
        raise ValueError("E2O relation references an unknown object")

    present_ts = rel["ocel:timestamp"].notna()
    rel.loc[present_ts, "ocel:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(
        rel.loc[present_ts, "ocel:timestamp"], errors="raise"
    )
    expected = {
        "ocel:activity": rel["ocel:eid"].map(event_meta["ocel:activity"]),
        "ocel:timestamp": rel["ocel:eid"].map(event_meta["ocel:timestamp"]),
        "ocel:type": rel["ocel:oid"].map(object_type),
    }
    for column, mapped in expected.items():
        mismatch = rel[column].notna() & (rel[column] != mapped)
        if mismatch.any():
            raise ValueError(f"Inconsistent denormalized values in {column}")
        rel[column] = rel[column].fillna(mapped)

    for column in ["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"]:
        if column not in obj_rel.columns:
            obj_rel[column] = pd.NA
    if obj_rel[["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2"]].isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError("O2O rows require both object endpoints")
    if not set(obj_rel["ocel:oid"]).issubset(object_ids):
        raise ValueError("O2O relation has an unknown source object")
    if not set(obj_rel["ocel:oid_2"]).issubset(object_ids):
        raise ValueError("O2O relation has an unknown target object")

    e2o_key = ["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"]
    o2o_key = ["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"]
    if rel.duplicated(e2o_key).any():
        raise ValueError("Exact duplicate E2O relations")
    if obj_rel.duplicated(o2o_key).any():
        raise ValueError("Exact duplicate O2O relations")

    return ev, obj, rel, obj_rel


# A normalized but semantically unrepaired baseline.
events_base, objects_base, relations_base, o2o_base = normalize_ocel_frames(
    events_original, objects_original, relations_original, o2o_original
)

# Work only on copies of that baseline.
events_r = events_base.copy(deep=True)
objects_r = objects_base.copy(deep=True)
relations_r = relations_base.copy(deep=True)
o2o_r = o2o_base.copy(deep=True)

event_meta = events_r.set_index("ocel:eid")[["ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp"]]
object_type = objects_r.set_index("ocel:oid")["ocel:type"]

# Selected Bundle A E2O additions.
e2o_specs = [
    ("ev08", "PK-8", "package"),
    ("ev10", "DEL-Y", "delivery"),
    ("ev14", "INV-44", "applied_to_invoice"),
]


def make_e2o_row(eid: str, oid: str, qualifier: str) -> dict:
    if eid not in event_meta.index or oid not in object_type.index:
        raise KeyError(f"Unknown endpoint: {eid}, {oid}")
    row = {column: pd.NA for column in relations_r.columns}
    row.update({
        "ocel:eid": eid,
        "ocel:activity": event_meta.at[eid, "ocel:activity"],
        "ocel:timestamp": event_meta.at[eid, "ocel:timestamp"],
        "ocel:oid": oid,
        "ocel:type": object_type.at[oid],
        "ocel:qualifier": qualifier,
    })
    return row


relations_r = pd.concat(
    [relations_r, pd.DataFrame([make_e2o_row(*spec) for spec in e2o_specs])],
    ignore_index=True,
)

o2o_additions = pd.DataFrame([
    {"ocel:oid": "PK-8", "ocel:oid_2": "IT-B1", "ocel:qualifier": "packs"},
    {"ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:oid_2": "PK-8", "ocel:qualifier": "carries"},
    {"ocel:oid": "PAY-2", "ocel:oid_2": "INV-44", "ocel:qualifier": "settles"},
])
for column in o2o_r.columns:
    if column not in o2o_additions.columns:
        o2o_additions[column] = pd.NA
o2o_r = pd.concat([o2o_r, o2o_additions[o2o_r.columns]], ignore_index=True)

# Re-run referential-integrity, metadata, and exact-duplicate checks after append.
events_r, objects_r, relations_r, o2o_r = normalize_ocel_frames(
    events_r, objects_r, relations_r, o2o_r
)

# Diagnostic cardinalities: inspect rather than forcing every count to one,
# because bulk dispatch and portal acknowledgement are allowed exceptions.
cardinality = (
    relations_r.groupby(["ocel:eid", "ocel:activity", "ocel:type"])
    .size().rename("count").reset_index()
)
print(cardinality[cardinality["ocel:activity"].isin(
    ["Pack Item", "Dispatch Package", "Record Payment", "Confirm Delivery"]
)])

# VERSION-DEPENDENT CONSTRUCTOR:
# Current PM4Py accepts o2o=...; older releases may require assignment after init.
def build_ocel(
    ev: pd.DataFrame,
    obj: pd.DataFrame,
    rel: pd.DataFrame,
    obj_rel: pd.DataFrame,
) -> OCEL:
    try:
        return OCEL(events=ev, objects=obj, relations=rel, o2o=obj_rel)
    except TypeError:
        result = OCEL(events=ev, objects=obj, relations=rel)
        result.o2o = obj_rel
        return result


original_ocel = build_ocel(events_base, objects_base, relations_base, o2o_base)
repaired_ocel = build_ocel(events_r, objects_r, relations_r, o2o_r)

# Compare discovered views before and after repair.
models_original = {
    "ocdfg": pm4py.discover_ocdfg(original_ocel),
    "otg": pm4py.discover_otg(original_ocel),
    "etot": pm4py.discover_etot(original_ocel),
}
models_repaired = {
    "ocdfg": pm4py.discover_ocdfg(repaired_ocel),
    "otg": pm4py.discover_otg(repaired_ocel),
    "etot": pm4py.discover_etot(repaired_ocel),
}

order_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(repaired_ocel, "Order")
delivery_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(repaired_ocel, "Delivery")
print(order_flat[["case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"]])
print(delivery_flat[["case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"]])

# Deliberately do not call pm4py.ocel_o2o_enrichment as semantic proof:
# graph-derived co-occurrence/lifecycle relations are analytical inferences,
# not confirmation of business qualifiers such as packs, carries, or settles.
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. Assigning ev11 to both deliveries solely because `N-42*` matches both would create a shared confirmation event and a possibly false `Dispatch Package -> Confirm Delivery` edge for each Delivery.
2. Adding ORD-B to ev11 merely because customer C17 matches would duplicate one portal event across two Order traces and overstate synchronized fulfilment.
3. Treating 130 + 100 = 230 as proof that INV-44 bills both orders would suppress legitimate fees, discounts, taxes, or invoice adjustments.
4. Treating the same arithmetic as proof that PAY-2 settles INV-44 could convert unapplied cash into a completed invoice lifecycle and create a false payment self-loop.
5. Linking ev10 to both DEL-X and DEL-Y would violate the normal one-Delivery cardinality and imply one package was simultaneously dispatched through two deliveries.
6. Reversing O2O directions—for example storing `PK-8 carries DEL-Y`—would alter qualifier semantics and contaminate topology analyses.
7. Using newly inferred O2O edges as independent support for the E2O edges that generated them would inflate confidence through circular evidence.
8. Completing every object lifecycle would pressure the repair to invent confirmation for DEL-Y, billing for ORD-B, or direct payment-order edges even though incompleteness is allowed.
