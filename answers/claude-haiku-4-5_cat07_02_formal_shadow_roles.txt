# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

## Front-Office Service Agent (Tier 1): R01_Amira, R02_Ben

**Core activities:**
- **A2 Validate Entitlement** (805 combined events): High-frequency intake gating; first point of rule-based judgment (customer eligibility, scope).
- **A4 Provide Standard Response** (645 combined events): Primary delivery mechanism for routine requests; direct customer communication; assumes entitlement validation is complete.
- **A12 Close Request** (540 combined events): Case closure; customer notification; triggers quality-check downstream.

**Peripheral or occasional activities:**
- **A3 Diagnose Issue** (135 combined events): Low frequency; likely overflow or simple technical issues that don't require specialist; Amira slightly more (75 vs. 60) suggesting greater complexity awareness.
- **A8 Customer Negotiation** (67 combined events): Amira 42, Ben 25; Amira's higher frequency suggests informal escalation mentoring or higher-complexity case touch-points.
- **A13 Reopen Request** (12 combined events): Rework due to Tier 1 error or downstream discovery; low frequency suggests generally good quality.
- **A1 Register** (430 combined events): Minimal because R14_Nora automates most registration; Tier 1 agents handle only unautomatable or correction registrations.

**Case complexity profile:**
- Median complexity 0.29–0.34 (lowest in organization except automation and temps).
- 410–475 low-complexity cases each; 205–225 medium; <60 high; <10 critical.
- Amira's slightly higher median (0.34 vs. 0.29) and medium-case count (225 vs. 205) suggest she is the informal Tier 1 escalation point for edge cases; Ben handles higher-volume, lower-variance work.

**Authority and decision scope:**
- Authority: **rule-based judgment only** (entitlement, scope, eligibility).
- No sign-off power; cannot approve exceptions, pricing changes, security waivers, or supervisor-level decisions.
- Rework frequency (8–12 reopens) is low, indicating good judgment within scope.

**Substitution evidence:**
- Weeks 8–10: R11_Kai and R12_Lina performed A2/A4/A12 in identical profile (0.98 similarity to each other; same activity subset).
- Result: 17 reopened cases traced to **incomplete A2 notes**  suggests Tier 1 agents apply implicit, experience-based validation heuristics that temps lack.
- Implication: **Tier 1 authority is qualitative** (judgment, risk awareness), not just activity count.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

## Shadow Role 1: R07_Giorgio (Commercial Shadow / Senior Agent Hybrid)

**Definition:**
Giorgio is nominally a **Senior Service Agent** in the Front Office team but implicitly fills a **commercial and escalation co-pilot** role alongside his formal duty to handle high-volume standard responses.

**Evidence:**
- **Formal assignment**: Front-office team; 7.4y tenure; higher pay grade / seniority.
- **Core activities** (high frequency): A2 Validate (190), A4 Standard Response (150), A12 Close (130), A1 Register (90).
  - Identical activity profile to Tier 1 agents (Amira, Ben), except **breadth**.
- **Shadow activities** (commercial domain): A7 Pricing Exception Review (72 events), A8 Customer Negotiation (88 events), A9 Supervisor Approval (20 events).
  - Tier 1 agents (Amira, Ben) have minimal A7 (18, 5), A8 (42, 25), A9 (0, 0).
  - Giorgio's 72 A7 events are **second-highest in organization** (only Fatima 122 exceeds; no other non-specialist exceeds 10).
  - Giorgio's 88 A8 events and 20 A9 events suggest **informal escalation authority**.
- **Similarity evidence**: Giorgio–Amira similarity 0.84 (very high), but Giorgio–Fatima similarity 0.71 (lower than expected for a commercial agent, but higher than Tier 1 to commercial gap).
- **Case complexity**: Median 0.58 (between Tier 1 at 0.29–0.34 and Fatima at 0.67).
- **Substitution evidence (Weeks 3–4)**: Fatima absent. Giorgio performed 31 A7 + 18 A8 events.
  - Outcome: Pricing exception duration increased 3874 min (97% increase; Fatima's baseline efficiency lost).
  - Interpretation: Giorgio is **competent enough to prevent process breakdown but not optimized** for pricing domain (implicit, not formal, expertise).

**Organizational benefits of this shadow role:**
1. **Continuity during specialist absence** (weeks 3–4 demonstrate resilience without formal backup plan).
2. **Front-office escalation mentoring**: Giorgio's higher-complexity median (0.58 vs. 0.29–0.34 for Tier 1) suggests he mentors Amira/Ben on edge cases.
3. **Cross-functional understanding**: Giorgio's touch-points in commercial domain allow him to coach Tier 1 agents on pricing implications of entitlement decisions.

**Organizational risks of this shadow role:**
1. **Informality**: Giorgio's commercial authority (A7, A8, A9) is never explicitly documented; if Giorgio leaves, commercial escalation process lacks redundancy.
2. **Reduced efficiency under load** (weeks 3–4 duration increase): Giorgio cannot match Fatima's throughput. If Fatima absent for extended period (>2 weeks), pricing exception backlog will grow.
3. **Potential for unauthorized decisions**: If Giorgio's informal authority is unclear, he might approve exceptions outside policy limits (not observed in data, but latent risk).
4. **Liability / compliance**: Commercial decisions (pricing exceptions) may require formal sign-off audit trail; Giorgio's informal work may leave audit gaps.

**Alternative interpretation:**
Giorgio could be classified as a **dedicated "Senior Service Agent with Commercial Cross-Training"** role (i.e., his shadow work is intentional, not emergent). If so, he should:
- Have formal commercial certification or training record (not visible in provided data).
- Have explicit escalation authority in A7/A8/A9 (not documented).
- Have measurable commercial domain KPIs (efficiency, pricing accuracy, customer satisfaction).

The fact that weeks 3–4 substitution shows **efficiency loss** (74 min vs. 38 min) suggests Giorgio's commercial work is **secondary competence**, not primary; thus, **shadow role** classification is more accurate.

## Shadow Role 2: R13_Mateo (Cross-Functional Escalation Broker)

**Definition:**
Mateo is nominally an **Incident Coordinator** but functionally operates as a **process-wide escalation broker**, filtering, routing, and preliminary-triaging high-complexity and rework cases across technical, commercial, security, and supervisory domains.

**Evidence:**
- **Formal assignment**: Coordination team; 5.9y tenure; "escalation" in formal title.
- **Distributed activity profile**: 504 events across 13 activities; no dominant single activity (A9 44, A13 42, but then A2 50, A3 35, etc.).
  - Unlike specialists (Chen dominates A3/A5, Fatima dominates A7, Erik dominates A6), Mateo has **even distribution** across all process activities.
  - Naive clustering would fragment Mateo across multiple roles or label him "low-activity utility player."
- **Case complexity**: Median 0.77 (third-highest; equivalent to Hana at 0.79); 76 critical cases.
  - Despite low single-activity event counts, Mateo's case complexity is **disproportionately high**, indicating **selective engagement in hard cases**.
- **Substitution evidence**:
  - **Week 5 (Hana at audit)**: Mateo performed 21 preliminary A9 approvals; 13 rechecked by Hana (62% rechecked).
    - Interpretation: Mateo can **triage and preliminary vet** but lacks final strategic authority; Hana retains veto.
  - **Weeks 7–8 (high Enterprise Security load)**: Mateo performed **security filtering** (low-risk cases diverted away from Erik).
    - Outcome: False escalation complaints down, but 2 critical cases waited >2 days (bottleneck revealed).
    - Interpretation: Mateo **understands security domain** well enough to filter, but Erik remains non-substitutable.
- **Handover and working-together network insights** (inferred from activity distribution):
  - Mateo touches A1 (register, 25), A2 (validate, 50), A3 (diagnose, 35), suggesting Mateo **re-engages at every rework stage**.
  - A13 (reopen, 42) is Mateo's second-highest; rework coordination is core.
  - Mateo's touch-points in A6 (security, 16) and A7 (pricing, 10) suggest **cross-domain literacy** (not deep expertise, but enough to understand constraints).

**Organizational benefits of this shadow role:**
1. **Process-wide visibility**: Mateo sees cases across all domains; can identify systemic bottlenecks (e.g., Erik bottleneck, pricing delays) and escalate to process owner.
2. **Rework optimization**: Mateo's high A13 activity (42) and case complexity (0.77) suggest he is **skilled at diagnosing rework root causes** (validation error, technical design flaw, commercial miscommunication) and re-routing appropriately.
3. **Load balancing**: Mateo's week 7–8 security filtering demonstrates **intelligent triage** (reduces non-critical load on bottleneck resource Erik without compromising critical cases).
4. **Substitution resilience**: Mateo can partially cover for Hana (week 5, though with high rechecked rate) and can augment specialist capacity (week 7–8 security filtering).

**Organizational risks of this shadow role:**
1. **Single point of failure**: If Mateo is absent, rework and escalation routing may stall. (No substitution incident observed, suggesting Mateo is considered essential but perhaps under-staffed).

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

Below is illustrative Python pseudocode using PM4Py-style function names and parameters. This code is not a runnable script (as full PM4Py API details and data format would need verification), but it demonstrates the analytical workflow.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
from pm4py.objects.log.importer.xes import importer as xes_importer
from pm4py.objects.log.importer.csv import importer as csv_importer
from pm4py.util import constants
import pm4py

# =============================================================================
# 1. LOAD AND FORMAT DATAFRAME
# =============================================================================

# Assume CSV event log with columns:
# case:concept:name, concept:name, time:timestamp, org:resource,
# case_complexity, customer_segment, product_line, temporary_substitution, escalation_level

event_log_df = pd.read_csv('event_log_10weeks.csv')

# Format dataframe to PM4Py standard structure
# (ensure timestamp is datetime; ensure column names match PM4Py conventions)
event_log_df['time:timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(event_log_df['time:timestamp'])
event_log_df = event_log_df.sort_values('time:timestamp')

# Specify that the dataframe is already in event format
event_log_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(event_log_df,
                                       case_id_key='case:concept:name',
                                       activity_key='concept:name',
                                       timestamp_key='time:timestamp',
                                       resource_key='org:resource')

# Inspect formatted log
print("Formatted event log shape:", event_log_df.shape)
print("Activities:", event_log_df['concept:name'].unique())
print("Resources:", event_log_df['org:resource'].unique())

# =============================================================================
# 2. DISCOVER ORGANIZATIONAL ROLES (FREQUENCY & ACTIVITY PROFILE)
# =============================================================================

# Role discovery based on resource-activity matrix
# PM4Py discovers roles by clustering resources with similar activity patterns

roles_discovered = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(event_log_df)

print("\n=== DISCOVERED ORGANIZATIONAL ROLES (Frequency-Based) ===")
for role, resources in roles_discovered.items():
    print(f"Role {role}: {resources}")

# Extract role-activity matrix (resources × activities)
role_activity_matrix = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles_activity_profile(event_log_df)
print("\nRole-Activity Profile:")
print(role_activity_matrix)

# =============================================================================
# 3. COMPUTE RESOURCE-RESOURCE SIMILARITY (ACTIVITY-BASED)
# =============================================================================

# Activity-based resource similarity (cosine distance / profile similarity)
similarity_matrix = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(event_log_df)

print("\n=== ACTIVITY-BASED RESOURCE SIMILARITY MATRIX ===")
print(similarity_matrix)

# Identify high-similarity resource pairs
threshold = 0.90
similar_pairs = []
resources = similarity_matrix.index.tolist()
for i, r1 in enumerate(resources):
    for r2 in resources[i+1:]:
        sim = similarity_matrix.loc[r1, r2]
        if sim >= threshold:
            similar_pairs.append((r1, r2, sim))

print(f"\nResource pairs with similarity >= {threshold}:")
for r1, r2, sim in sorted(similar_pairs, key=lambda x: x[2], reverse=True):
    print(f"  {r1} — {r2}: {sim:.4f}")

# =============================================================================
# 4. ANALYZE CASE COMPLEXITY BY RESOURCE
# =============================================================================

# Group by resource and compute complexity statistics
resource_complexity = event_log_df.groupby('org:resource').agg({
    'case_complexity': ['mean', 'median', 'std', 'min', 'max', 'count'],
    'escalation_level': ['mean', 'max']
}).round(3)

print("\n=== CASE COMPLEXITY BY RESOURCE ===")
print(resource_complexity.sort_values(('case_complexity', 'median'), ascending=False))

# Identify low-complexity (generalist/support) vs. high-complexity (specialist) resources
low_complexity_resources = event_log_df.groupby('org:resource')['case_complexity'].median().nsmallest(5).index.tolist()
high_complexity_resources = event_log_df.groupby('org:resource')['case_complexity'].median().nlargest(5).index.tolist()

print(f"\nLow-complexity (support/generalist) resources: {low_complexity_resources}")
print(f"High-complexity (specialist/bottleneck) resources: {high_complexity_resources}")

# =============================================================================
# 5. DISCOVER HANDOVER-OF-WORK NETWORK
# =============================================================================

# Handover of work: transitions where one resource's activity is followed by another's
# (same case, different resource)

handover_network = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(event_log_df)

print("\n=== HANDOVER-OF-WORK NETWORK (Top Edges) ===")
# handover_network is a directed graph; print top handover paths
handover_edges = sorted(handover_network.edges(data=True), 
                        key=lambda x: x[2].get('weight', 0), 
                        reverse=True)[:20]
for source, target, attr in handover_edges:
    weight = attr.get('weight', 0)
    print(f"  {source}  {target}: {weight:.0f} handovers")

# Insight: High handover frequency between two resources suggests sequential work
# (e.g., validation  diagnosis  configuration). Can infer workflow stages.

# =============================================================================
# 6. DISCOVER WORKING-TOGETHER NETWORK
# =============================================================================

# Working together: resources that co-occur on the same cases (but not necessarily sequential)

working_together_network = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(event_log_df)

print("\n=== WORKING-TOGETHER NETWORK (Top Edges) ===")
working_together_edges = sorted(working_together_network.edges(data=True),
                                key=lambda x: x[2].get('weight', 0),
                                reverse=True)[:20]
for r1, r2, attr in working_together_edges:
    weight = attr.get('weight', 0)
    print(f"  {r1}  {r2}: {weight:.0f} cases together")

# Insight: High co-occurrence suggests cross-functional teams or shared case responsibility.

# =============================================================================
# 7. ANALYZE TEMPORARY SUBSTITUTION EPISODES
# =============================================================================

# Filter for temporary_substitution events
substitution_events = event_log_df[event_log_df['temporary_substitution'] == True]

print("\n=== TEMPORARY SUBSTITUTION EPISODES ===")
if len(substitution_events) > 0:
    sub_by_resource = substitution_events.groupby('org:resource').agg({
        'case:concept:name': 'count',
        'concept:name': lambda x: x.value_counts().index[0],  # most common activity
        'case_complexity': 'mean'
    }).rename(columns={'case:concept:name': 'substitution_events', 
                       'concept:name': 'primary_activity',
                       'case_complexity': 'avg_complexity'})
    print(sub_by_resource.sort_values('substitution_events', ascending=False))

# Analyze substitution effectiveness: compare case rework rate (A13 Reopen) for
# cases handled during substitution vs. normal period

normal_period_reopens = event_log_df[
    (event_log_df['temporary_substitution'] == False) &
    (event_log_df['concept:name'] == 'A13 Reopen Request')
].shape[0]

subst_period_reopens = event_log_df[
    (event_log_df['temporary_substitution'] == True) &
    (event_log_df['concept:name'] == 'A13 Reopen Request')

print(f"\nReopen rate (normal period): {normal_period_reopens} / {len(event_log_df[event_log_df['temporary_substitution'] == False])}")

# =============================================================================

# Resource-activity frequency matrix
resource_activity_freq = pd.crosstab(event_log_df['org:resource'], 
                                      event_log_df['concept:name'])

print("\n=== RESOURCE-ACTIVITY FREQUENCY (Top Resources by Total Events) ===")
resource_total_events = resource_activity_freq.sum(axis=1).sort_values(ascending=False)
print(resource_total_events.head(15))

# Identify activity concentration (Herfindahl index)
# High concentration = specialist (focus on few activities)
# Low concentration = generalist (spread across many activities)

def herfindahl_concentration(freq_series):
    """Compute Herfindahl index: sum of squared shares."""
    total = freq_series.sum()
    if total == 0:
        return 0
    shares = freq_series / total
    return (shares ** 2).sum()

concentration = resource_activity_freq.apply(herfindahl_concentration, axis=1)

print("\n=== ACTIVITY CONCENTRATION BY RESOURCE (Herfindahl Index) ===")
print("High (>0.3) = Specialist; Low (<0.15) = Generalist")
print(concentration.sort_values(ascending=False).round(3))

# Identify pure specialists (concentration > 0.4)
specialists = concentration[concentration > 0.40].index.tolist()
print(f"\nIdentified specialists (concentration >0.40): {specialists}")

# Identify generalists (concentration < 0.15)
generalists = concentration[concentration < 0.15].index.tolist()
print(f"Identified generalists (concentration <0.15): {generalists}")

# 9. ESCALATION AND BOTTLENECK ANALYSIS
# =============================================================================

# Identify bottleneck resources:
# - High case complexity (median)
# - Low activity substitution (no backup during absence)
# - High concentration in rare activities (only one performs activity)

print("\n=== BOTTLENECK RISK ASSESSMENT ===")

# Activity exclusivity: activities performed by only one resource
activity_resource_count = pd.crosstab(event_log_df['concept:name'], 
                                       event_log_df['org:resource']).applymap(lambda x: x > 0).sum(axis=1)

print("\nActivities by single resource (exclusive / rare):")
for activity, count in activity_resource_count.items():
    if count == 1:
        sole_performer = event_log_df[event_log_df['concept:name'] == activity]['org:resource'].unique()[0]
        event_count = event_log_df[event_log_df['concept:name'] == activity].shape[0]
        print(f"  {activity}: {sole_performer} ({event_count} events) - BOTTLENECK")

# =============================================================================
# 10. SERVICE TIME ANALYSIS (Process Duration by Resource)
# =============================================================================

# Insert case service time (time from first to last event per case)
event_log_with_service_time = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(event_log_df)

# Analyze service time by primary resource handling the case
# (e.g., Tier 1 agent who validated the request)

service_time_by_resource = event_log_with_service_time.groupby('org:resource').agg({
    'case:concept:name': 'count',
    'case_service_duration': ['mean', 'median', 'std']
}).round(2)

print("\n=== CASE SERVICE TIME BY PRIMARY RESOURCE ===")
print(service_time_by_resource.sort_values(('case_service_duration', 'median'), ascending=False))

# Identify slow resources (high service time) = potential bottlenecks
print("\nResources with high median service time (>1 day):")
slow_resources = service_time_by_resource[
    service_time_by_resource[('case_service_duration', 'median')] > 86400  # 1 day in seconds
].index.tolist()
for resource in slow_resources:
    print(f"  {resource}: {service_time_by_resource.loc[resource, ('case_service_duration', 'median')] / 3600:.1f} hours median")

# =============================================================================
# 11. ROLE STRUCTURE SYNTHESIS
# =============================================================================

# Combine insights: cluster resources into proposed roles

proposed_roles = {}

# Use similarity matrix + complexity + activity concentration to assign roles
for resource in event_log_df['org:resource'].unique():
    
    complexity_median = resource_complexity.loc[resource, ('case_complexity', 'median')]
    concentration_idx = concentration[resource]
    total_events = resource_total_events[resource]
    
    # Classification logic
    if resource == 'R14_Nora':
        role = 'Automation & Workflow Initiation'
    elif concentration_idx > 0.40 and complexity_median > 0.75:
        role = 'High-Complexity Specialist (Bottleneck)'
    elif concentration_idx > 0.40 and complexity_median > 0.60:
        role = 'Domain Specialist'
    elif concentration_idx > 0.30 and complexity_median > 0.60:
        role = 'Quality Gatekeeper / Escalation Authority'
    elif concentration_idx > 0.20 and complexity_median > 0.40:
        role = 'Mid-Level Technical / Commercial'
    elif concentration_idx < 0.15 and complexity_median < 0.35:
        if total_events > 500:
            role = 'Front-Office Tier 1 Service Agent'
        else:
            role = 'Support / Temporary Agent'
    else:
        role = 'Cross-Functional Coordinator'
    
    proposed_roles[resource] = role

print("\n=== PROPOSED ROLE STRUCTURE ===")
for resource, role in sorted(proposed_roles.items()):
    print(f"  {resource}: {role}")

# =============================================================================
# 12. OUTPUT SUMMARY TABLE
# =============================================================================

# Combine all metrics into summary dataframe for export

role_summary = pd.DataFrame({
    'Resource': event_log_df['org:resource'].unique(),
})

role_summary['Proposed_Role'] = role_summary['Resource'].map(proposed_roles)
role_summary['Total_Events'] = role_summary['Resource'].map(resource_total_events)
role_summary['Complexity_Median'] = role_summary['Resource'].apply(
```

This analysis proposes a **nuanced organizational role structure** that balances naive frequency clustering with case complexity, authority, and non-substitutability:

## Core Roles (Designed & Formal)
1. **Tier 1 Front-Office Service Agents** (Amira, Ben) – Routine entitlement validation, standard response delivery, closure.
2. **Technical Specialists** (Chen, Dina) – Diagnosis, configuration, implementation; Chen handles high-complexity / critical cases.
3. **Quality Gatekeepers** (Ivan, Julia) – Quality checks, knowledge-base updates, rework triage.
4. **Commercial Specialist** (Fatima) – Pricing exception review, customer negotiation.
5. **Risk & Security Specialist** (Erik) – Security review, compliance veto, critical bottleneck.
6. **Supervisor & Escalation Authority** (Hana) – Final approval authority across all domains.

## Shadow & Bridging Roles (Emergent, Valuable but Undocumented)
7. **Giorgio** – Senior agent with informal commercial backup capability (hybrid front-office/commercial).
8. **Mateo** – Cross-functional escalation broker, rework coordinator, intelligent load-balancer.
9. **Temporary Load-Balancing Agents** (Kai, Lina) – Routine low-complexity processing; boundary role (no authority, no judgment).
10. **Automation & Workflow Initiation** (Nora) – System-level gatekeeper; must be segregated from human-role classification.

## Key Insights
- **Activity frequency alone is insufficient** to determine organizational value or staffing decisions.
- **Case complexity + authority + non-substitutability** reveal true bottlenecks and critical roles.
- **Shadow roles are real and valuable** but risky if informal and undocumented.
- **Temporary substitution is a coping mechanism**, not proof of equal permanent competence.
- **Ambiguous assignments (Dina, Amira/Ben, Giorgio, Mateo, Ivan/Julia) require explicit clarification** before major staffing changes.

The proposed PM4Py analysis (Section 5) provides a systematic workflow to operationalize this discovery and avoid over-reliance on activity frequency alone.
