**1. Executive memo**

Management should adopt a controlled hybrid rollout (alternative E) rather than national scale, rollback, or full coordinator model. The pilot delivered clear gains for simple low-risk houses—median cycle time fell 6 days, first-time-right rose 3 pp, complaints declined 1 pp—while exposing material harm in apartments, old buildings, and business segments where p90 tails lengthened 12–25 days, complaints rose 5–7 pp, and manual activation doubled. Fewer handovers improved accountability on paper but coincided with 93 % coordinator utilization, higher second-visit load, and evidence of reduced resilience when coordinators were absent.

The process-mining artifacts confirm the redesign simplified the happy path but relaxed controls on activation sequencing and permit checks, producing high fitness (0.94) yet low precision (0.51) and frequent moves-on-log for billing-before-activation and missing permits. Visible cost improved while estimated total cost rose, indicating cost shifting rather than reduction.

A national scale (A) risks amplifying tail and rework exposure across heterogeneous regions. Full rollback (B) would forfeit proven benefits for the 34 % simple-house segment. Partial coordinator scaling without survey or activation changes (C) leaves the root causes of rework untouched. A new controlled experiment (D) adds delay without using existing segment differentiation.

Hybrid E therefore limits exposure, preserves the coordinator ownership mechanism where it works, and mandates segment-specific guardrails plus targeted instrumentation before any wider deployment. Immediate next steps are listed in section 6.

**2. Evidence table**

**Facts directly supported**  
- Median cycle time: 14.2  10.8 days; p90: 31.0  38.5 days (pilot data).  
- First-time-right: 82 %  76 %; complaints within 30 days: 6.1 %  9.8 %.  
- Cases with 3+ handovers: 34 %  12 %; coordinator utilization reached 93 % in two regions.  
- Pilot model fitness 0.94, precision 0.51; baseline fitness 0.87, precision 0.79.  
- Frequent alignments: billing start before activation test; installation visit repeated without field survey.  
- Simple-house segment (34 % of pilot): median –6 days, p90 –8 days, FTR +3 pp.  
- Apartment/medium-risk (29 %): median –2 days, p90 +12 days, complaints +5 pp.  
- Old-building/permit-sensitive (18 %): p90 +25 days, complaints +7 pp; missing permit checks observed.

**Interpretations or plausible causal explanations**  
- Coordinator ownership reduced handovers but concentrated load, lowering resilience during absences.  
- Survey-skip rule performed poorly on apartments and older stock, increasing failed visits and second visits.  
- Post-billing activation increased manual-activation volume and downstream complaints.

**Assumptions that remain uncertain**  
- Building-risk predictive rule accuracy against actual field-survey outcomes.  
- Full downstream complaint and revisit cost beyond week 4 of pilot.  
- Start-timestamp sparsity masks true activity durations for activation and second visits.

**3. Explanation of metric conflicts**

High fitness/low precision arises because the pilot model overfits the simplified coordinator path while permitting many unobserved or invalid combinations of skipped survey, manual activation, and billing-before-activation. Improved median but worse tail occurs because gains concentrate in simple houses while apartments and old buildings generate long rework loops. Fewer handovers yet lower resilience follows from centralization: context is not shared, so coordinator absence stalls cases. Lower visible cost but higher total cost reflects cost shifting to complaint handling and second visits not captured after week 4. Segment divergence stems from the skip rule and permit omissions applying unevenly across building types, producing opposite effects in the same pilot.

**4. Decision alternatives**

A. Scale nationally: Advantage—fast realization of median and handover gains. Risk—amplifies tail, rework, and SLA breaches across untested regions; violates constraint against scaling without tail/rework fixes. Reasonable only after segment guardrails and activation controls proven.  
B. Roll back: Advantage—stops deterioration in complex segments. Risk—forfeits documented 6-day median and 3 pp FTR gains in simple houses. Reasonable only if downstream cost overrun exceeds visible savings by a large margin.  
C. Scale only coordinator ownership: Advantage—keeps accountability improvement. Risk—leaves survey skipping and post-billing activation untouched, sustaining rework. Reasonable if new data show survey skip is not causal.  
D. New controlled experiment: Advantage—improves measurement completeness. Risk—delays action and repeats pilot coverage gaps. Reasonable if current segment data are judged too incomplete for any rollout.  
E. Hybrid (simple houses only + controls): Advantage—captures proven benefits while containing exposure; allows measurement before scale. Risk—requires operational segmentation discipline. Reasonable under current evidence.

**5. Value-of-information analysis**

Highest value: M1 (randomized survey-skip test by stratum)—directly tests causal driver of rework. M6 (validate risk rule)—resolves skip-rule uncertainty. M4 (60-day downstream cost)—quantifies true cost delta. M3 (reason codes)—explains failed-visit and complaint drivers. M7 (conformance monitoring on billing/activation and permits)—flags compliance-like failures in real time. M2 (start timestamps)—improves duration accuracy for tail analysis. M8 (separate CX measures)—clarifies NPS versus operational metrics. Lowest: M5 (coordinator vs shared ownership)—useful only after hybrid is running.

**6. Final recommendation**

Immediate action: Launch hybrid E in two pilot regions for simple low-risk houses only, with mandatory permit check retained for old buildings and activation-before-billing enforced. Pause: survey skipping and post-billing activation outside the simple-house segment. Scale: coordinator ownership only within the hybrid scope. Measure next: M1, M3, M4, M6, M7. Threshold for broader rollout: p90 cycle time  baseline +2 days, complaints  7 %, and first-time-right  80 % in the hybrid cohort for four consecutive weeks with full 60-day cost captured.
