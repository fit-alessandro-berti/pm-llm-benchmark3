## 1. Temporal Data Semantics

Order each case by `(time:timestamp, event_index)`; a smaller pair is earlier. `event_index` resolves equal timestamps within a case, not necessarily across cases. The state at an event is the result of applying all nonblank attribute updates up to and including that event. A blank means no update; a value stays unknown until first established. A sanctions-list update names a list entry, not necessarily a change to the case's jurisdiction. Evaluate each release separately. A later case attribute does not establish an earlier value unless independent evidence gives it an earlier effective time.

For this assessment, a change of jurisdiction, risk, or customer type is material when the value actually changes; an amount change is material for review freshness when it increases by more than 20% relative to the amount at the review. Compare each subsequent observed amount with that review's baseline, so cumulative increases can invalidate a review even when individual increments are small. Exactly 20% is not more than 20%. A decrease alone does not invalidate; it also does not undo an earlier invalidation. A review with unknown baseline amount has uncertain amount freshness if a later amount is observed. Unknown-to-known changes in other attributes create uncertainty about whether the underlying value changed. These are explicit operational interpretations of the otherwise undefined phrase “material data change.” Under R2, I treat a change in risk or customer type after review as requiring a relevant new enhanced review; R4's amount invalidation applies to amount-dependent obligations, not automatically to R2, which is triggered by risk or PEP status. Other defensible materiality policies can change findings.

Event time is when the event happened; effective time is when the underlying fact or exemption applies; record time is when it entered a system; evidence time includes when a signature or other corroboration was created. An insert after release does not prove late approval, and a backdated displayed effective or approval time does not itself prove timely approval. Evidence may support an earlier effective fact, but uncertainty must remain visible. The obligation trigger for R1 and R2 is release; exemption expiry is treated as inclusive at that instant, subject to the actual instrument's wording.

| R7 | Every event-time comparison | Reconstruct ordered events and updates | Earlier timestamp, or lower event_index at the same timestamp within a case | State at the particular event | Explicitly supported different effective time | Unresolvable cross-source ordering remains uncertain | Event timestamps, stable indices, source and effective-time metadata |

Pseudo-logic: let `a < b` mean earlier by timestamp, or, within the same case and timestamp, lower event_index. `S(e)` is state after applying e's effective updates. `AND`, `OR`, comparisons, and negation use three-valued logic, so false AND unknown is false and true OR unknown is true. `E(e)` denotes an Enhanced_Review completed at e. Quantification over events is restricted to the relevant case unless stated otherwise. `release(r)` is evaluated once for each release.

- **R4:** `invalid_amount(q,r)` is true if some amount observed at `u`, `q < u < r`, exceeds `1.20 * amount(S(q))`; false if all such comparisons are known and none does; unknown if an unknown baseline or intervening amount could change the result. A zero baseline followed by a positive amount counts as an increase over 20%; negative amounts require domain validation. An invalidated review stays invalid even if the amount later falls. `fresh(q,r)` also requires no known material non-amount change after q; unknown comparisons produce unknown. These predicates are evaluated relative to each candidate review, not only the last overall review.
- **R1:** `T1(r) = (jurisdiction(S(r)) != DE) AND (amount(S(r)) >= 100000)`. If true, require either `exists q: E(q) AND q < r AND fresh(q,r)` or `valid_exemption(r, R1)`. If the trigger is false, no R1 obligation arises. If a required proposition remains unknown and no independently sufficient alternative resolves it, the R1 result is indeterminate.
- **R2:** `T2(r) = (risk(S(r)) = HIGH) OR (customer(S(r)) = PEP)`. If true, require `exists q,p: E(q) AND q < p < r AND Compliance_Approve(p) AND relevant(q,p,r)`. Here relevance includes a review addressing the applicable risk/customer state and an approval of that enhanced packet. A known risk or customer change after q makes relevance false under the interpretation above; unclear linkage or changes make it unknown. R4 does not independently invalidate this non-amount-triggered obligation.
- **R3:** for release jurisdiction `j`, a qualifying addition `u` is after the latest preceding screen `s` and before r, and concerns j. The obligation is a screen `n` with `u < n < r`, covering j. Equivalently, where screening and list history are complete and a baseline screen exists, any addition after the latest screen before r is an outstanding rescreen obligation. If no baseline screen exists, R3's specific antecedent is not established; the absence of a baseline is a separate potential control gap, not automatically an R3 violation. An addition or screen whose cross-case ordering or coverage is unknown is evaluated as uncertain.
- **R5:** `valid_exemption(r,k)` requires scope matching `S(r)`, an explicit waiver of k, approval by Head_Compliance established by r, `effective_from <= time(r) <= expiry`, and supporting evidence of the approval's existence by r. A record may be inserted later; its insertion time alone neither proves nor disproves earlier approval. Conflicting or insufficient evidence makes validity unknown, rather than automatically true or false.
- **R6–R7:** build `S` by ordered, forward-only application of nonblank updates; retain unknowns. Classify a case noncompliant if a triggered obligation is definitely unmet; indeterminate if no definite deviation is established but a decisive trigger or fulfillment remains unknown; otherwise classify compliant, or justified exception if a valid exemption is actually needed to waive a triggered obligation. For multiple releases, any definite violation dominates, then uncertainty, then an actually used exception, then compliance.

## 3. Case Findings

Conclusions assume the supplied log and relevant list history are complete except where the facts explicitly flag uncertainty.

## 4. Ambiguities and Alternative Readings

- **TX-302:** The qualified signature and outage evidence support timely approval despite late insertion. If signature verification or scope evidence fails, the exemption's validity could become uncertain, but the existing fresh enhanced review independently satisfies R1. If that review were incomplete or stale and EX-77 were valid, the classification would be JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.
- **TX-305:** An immutable release payload proving an amount below 100,000 would exclude R1. A reliable contemporaneous value of at least 100,000 would activate it; a review document proving that the review covered that value and remained fresh could establish compliance, while evidence that no qualifying review occurred would establish noncompliance. The settlement amount alone is not proof of the release amount. A policy interpreting any enhanced review as sufficient despite unknown amount coverage could change the result.
- **TX-310:** A verifiable contemporaneous approval record or independently corroborated authority and timing for oral approval could make EX-88 valid and yield JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, provided its other scope and timing terms hold. Reliable proof that approval was first given after release would make the case NONCOMPLIANT. The later-created certificate undermines the displayed approval claim but does not alone disprove a separate oral approval; the interested witness alone is not treated as conclusive.
- **TX-311:** The index places the jurisdiction change after the review even though the timestamps match. It is a material change under the stated interpretation and would make that review stale for R1 if R1 were triggered. However, release state is DE, so R1 is not triggered. Evidence that DE was recorded in error or became effective only after release would leave US at release and make the post-review jurisdiction change and its actual effective time decisive. A different policy requiring review on threshold crossing independently of release would also change the result; that is not the stated R1.
- **TX-306 and TX-308:** If policy instead treats every amount change as material for R1, the post-review increase and decrease respectively could require another review. The stated interpretation applies R4's >20% increase criterion, under which neither invalidates the review. If R2 requires a fresh approval after every amount change, TX-306 would also need another approval; that additional requirement is not explicit in R2.
- **TX-309:** A later correction showing that the amount at enhanced review was at least 120,833.34 would make the 145,000 amount no more than 20% higher, potentially restoring review freshness if no earlier invalidating increase occurred. Conversely, a later drop would not cure the already observed invalidation under the selected interpretation.

```python
            value = row[a]
            if pd.isna(previous):
                uncertain = True
            elif value != previous:
                return True
            previous = value
    return None if uncertain else False

            # This sample's notes identify the standard-only packet; otherwise linkage
            # is assumed from order and should be replaced with explicit packet IDs.
            linked = any('standard review packet' not in str(x.get('note', '')).lower() for x in approvals)
            candidates_r2.append(tri_and(relevant, bool(linked)))
        review_ok = tri_or(*candidates_r1) if candidates_r1 else False
        r2_ok = tri_or(*candidates_r2) if candidates_r2 else False
        r1_trigger = tri_and(tri_not(eq(r['jurisdiction'], 'DE')),
                             None if pd.isna(r['amount']) else bool(r['amount'] >= 100000))
        r2_trigger = tri_or(eq(r['risk_level'], 'HIGH'), eq(r['customer_type'], 'PEP'))
        ex_ok = exemption_valid(r)
        r1_result = result(r1_trigger, tri_or(review_ok, ex_ok))
        r2_result = result(r2_trigger, r2_ok)
        # Only updates for the release jurisdiction can trigger R3. A screen's
        # jurisdiction is checked; simultaneous cross-case order is unknown.
        additions = [u for u in list_updates if pd.notna(r['jurisdiction'])
                     and u['list_jurisdiction'] == r['jurisdiction']]
        previous_screens = [s for s in prior if s[act] == 'Sanctions_Screen'
                            and eq(s['jurisdiction'], r['jurisdiction']) is True]
        covered_screen = previous_screens[-1] if previous_screens else None
        relevant_updates = []
        for u in additions:
            bu = before(u, r)
            bs = before(covered_screen, u) if covered_screen is not None else False
            relevant_updates.append(tri_and(bu, bs))
        if pd.isna(r['jurisdiction']):
            r3_trigger = None
        else:
            r3_trigger = tri_or(*relevant_updates) if relevant_updates else False
            if covered_screen is None and any(before(u, r) is not False for u in additions):
                r3_trigger = None  # no baseline screen: antecedent cannot be established
        r3_result = result(r3_trigger, False)
        preceding_updates = [u for u in additions if before(u, r) is True]
        last_update = max(preceding_updates, key=lambda u: (u[ts], u['event_index']), default=None)
        statuses = [r1_result, r2_result, r3_result]
        if 'FAIL' in statuses:
            classification = 'NONCOMPLIANT'
        elif 'REVIEW_REQUIRED' in statuses:
            classification = 'REVIEW_REQUIRED'
        elif r1_trigger is True and review_ok is not True and ex_ok is True:
            classification = 'JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION'
        else:
            classification = 'COMPLIANT'
        outputs.append(dict(
            case_id=cid, release_time=r[ts], release_index=r['event_index'],
            amount=r['amount'], risk_level=r['risk_level'], jurisdiction=r['jurisdiction'],
            customer_type=r['customer_type'], exemption_id=r['exemption_id'],
            unknown_amount=pd.isna(r['amount']), unknown_risk=pd.isna(r['risk_level']),
            unknown_jurisdiction=pd.isna(r['jurisdiction']), unknown_customer_type=pd.isna(r['customer_type']),
            last_standard=stamp(std), last_enhanced=stamp(enh), last_approval=stamp(approval),
            last_screen=stamp(screen), last_relevant_list_update=stamp(last_update),
            last_material_change=max(material_times, default=pd.NaT),
            last_uncertain_material_change=max(uncertain_material_times, default=pd.NaT),
            r1_trigger=r1_trigger, r1_review_ok=review_ok, exemption_valid=ex_ok,
            r1=r1_result, r2_trigger=r2_trigger, r2=r2_result,
            r3_trigger=r3_trigger, r3=r3_result, r4_latest_review_invalid=r4,
            classification=classification))

releases = pd.DataFrame(outputs)
# Join the register after reconstructing release state; the later insert does not
# turn into a release-time update. The joined fields are evidence, not proof by themselves.
releases = releases.merge(register[['exemption_id', 'evidence', 'approval_time', 'effective_from',
                                    'expires', 'signature_time', 'record_time']],
                          how='left', on='exemption_id', validate='many_to_one')
priority = {'COMPLIANT': 0, 'JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION': 1, 'REVIEW_REQUIRED': 2, 'NONCOMPLIANT': 3}
case_diagnostics = (releases.sort_values(['case_id', 'release_time', 'release_index'])
                    .groupby('case_id', as_index=False)
                    .agg(release_count=('release_time', 'size'),
                         classification=('classification', lambda s: max(s, key=priority.get)),
                         last_release=('release_time', 'last'),
                         any_unknown_amount=('unknown_amount', 'max'),
                         any_unknown_risk=('unknown_risk', 'max'),
                         any_unknown_jurisdiction=('unknown_jurisdiction', 'max'),
                         any_unknown_customer_type=('unknown_customer_type', 'max'),
                         last_standard=('last_standard', 'last'), last_enhanced=('last_enhanced', 'last'),
                         last_approval=('last_approval', 'last'), last_screen=('last_screen', 'last'),
                         last_relevant_list_update=('last_relevant_list_update', 'last'),
                         last_material_change=('last_material_change', 'last'),
                         last_uncertain_material_change=('last_uncertain_material_change', 'last'),
                         last_r1=('r1', 'last'), last_r2=('r2', 'last'), last_r3=('r3', 'last'),
                         last_r4_invalid=('r4_latest_review_invalid', 'last'),
                         exemption_id=('exemption_id', 'last'),
                         exemption_evidence=('evidence', 'last'),
                         exemption_signature_time=('signature_time', 'last'),
                         exemption_record_time=('record_time', 'last')))
pd.set_option('display.max_columns', None)
print(releases.to_string(index=False))
print(case_diagnostics.to_string(index=False))
```

`REVIEW_REQUIRED` is the code's coarse operational counterpart to an unresolved case, not a fifth narrative classification. The code does not establish the completeness of the source data, prove a review's scope, or resolve an evidential dispute. It assumes an approval after an enhanced review refers to that review unless its note says otherwise; real checking needs explicit review/approval linkage. For multiple releases, inspect the per-release table as well as the case aggregate: the aggregate's last-event evidence is not necessarily the evidence behind an earlier failure. A simple trace check such as “Enhanced_Review occurs before Release_Funds” can be useful as a preliminary screen, but cannot evaluate the conditional, stateful rules on its own.

## 6. Why Control-Flow-Only Conformance Is Insufficient

1. **Final-state leakage:** using settlement or final attributes at release can falsely apply EUR 150,000 to TX-305 or DE to an earlier TX-311 review.
2. **Post-hoc exemptions:** a `Record_Exemption` event says when a record was inserted, not whether an authorized approval existed at release; EX-77 and EX-88 need different evidential assessments.
3. **Material changes:** a review before release can be stale after a >20% amount increase, as in TX-309. A trace with both activities misses the review baseline and intervening values.
4. **Conditional activation:** R1 depends on jurisdiction and amount; R2 depends on risk or PEP. Neither is an unconditional requirement for every release, and R1's 100,000 threshold is inclusive.
5. **Missing data:** absent amounts and other fields cannot safely be treated as low risk or as the final value; a trace can appear complete while the trigger or review coverage remains unknown.
6. **Equal timestamps:** event_index makes the TX-311 jurisdiction change later than its review; a timestamp-only sequence may reverse or arbitrarily merge them.
7. **Approval linkage and type:** a standard review and approval, as in TX-307, cannot stand in for enhanced review followed by approval of that review.
8. **External evolving state:** R3 depends on list additions and screening coverage for the current jurisdiction, potentially across cases and systems, not merely the case's activity sequence.
9. **Scope and time of validity:** an exemption must match jurisdiction, customer type, amount, waived rule and time, as well as authority; the presence of an exemption event alone cannot show these facts.

## 7. Recommended Logging and Control Improvements

- Persist an immutable, signed release payload containing the amount, currency, jurisdiction, risk, customer type, relevant list version and the exact review, approval and exemption identifiers used. Preserve review-time snapshots and the reviewed packet's identity and contents.
- Record occurrence time, effective-from and effective-until times, database insertion time, signature creation and verification times, signer identity and authority, and source-system clock provenance separately. Preserve corrections as new events with reasons rather than overwriting history.
- Capture exemption scope, explicitly waived rules, limits, approval authority, expiry, evidence references and links to each release; retain verifiable signatures and outage/audit records.
- Log both old and new attribute values, change reason, source, actor and provenance, including whether the value was unknown; specify materiality and cumulative-change rules and link invalidation and replacement reviews to their triggering changes.
- Version the policy and rule configuration used at each decision, including thresholds, materiality, expiry boundaries and any interpretation of approval relevance.
- Give events stable, unique IDs and an authoritative per-case sequence; provide a global sequence or causality links for cross-system list updates and screens, along with time precision and clock synchronization. Log list entry jurisdiction, effective time, list version, screen coverage and result.
- Enforce release-time checks for triggered obligations, stale reviews, approval linkage, sanction-list changes and exemption validity. Route unknowns and conflicting evidence to a documented human decision instead of silently defaulting them.
