# Environmental Process Redesign for Industrial Spare-Parts Distribution

## 1. System boundary and assumptions

### Scope definition
**Inside the boundary:**
- Inbound production energy (CNC machining, heat treatment, inspection)
- Packaging material and assembly
- Transportation modes (ground, air, consolidated, dedicated)
- Rework and scrap generation (production and packaging loops)
- Waiting time in batches and consolidation
- Cases closed within the observation window (11,200 of 12,000)

**Outside the boundary:**
- Supplier emissions for raw materials and components
- Returnable packaging reverse logistics
- Customer-side waste (post-delivery returns not captured)
- Carrier infrastructure and fleet maintenance
- Administrative and warehouse lighting/HVAC (attribute-level data not provided)

### Key uncertainties
1. **Batch-size distribution and demand timing:** We see energy per item decreases with batch size but not the exact timing of demand arrivals. Larger batches may be driven by forecast error, not pure optimization.
2. **Rework root cause:** The 18% rework rate on CNC_B is observed but causation (machine reliability vs. rushed inspection vs. part complexity) is not explicit. Correlation with inspection_cell queues suggests bottleneck-driven quality loss.
3. **Obsolescence and write-off:** Only 6% of batch items not shipped within 14 days is flagged; actual scrap after 30–90 days is unknown.
4. **Air transport substitution:** Does reducing air for Standard-stock Service-contract orders shift demand to faster ground or genuine cancellation?
5. **Carrier and consolidation capacity:** The twice-daily consolidation (S5) assumes available truck slots; oversaturation risk is not quantified.
6. **Customer sensitivity to lead-time changes:** SP1 allows ground when 85% confidence achievable; willingness-to-accept longer lead times for regular customers is unstated.

### Assumptions made
- Late delivery is unacceptable above ~10% even for Regular customers; Service-contract threshold is ~5%.
- Rework and material scrap generate both emissions (reprocessing energy) and waste; assumed linear with event count.
- Transport emissions (kgCO2e) dominate packaging material by roughly 20:1 for parcel modes.
- Consolidation wait of 12–24 h is acceptable for non-urgent stock orders if it does not breach p90 targets.
- Critical-downtime failures have implicit high downstream cost; dedicated vans are justified if true downtime probability >20%.

## 3. Intervention options

### **Intervention S1: Green shipping recommender using promised lead time, route reliability, and customer segment**

**Target path:** All stock orders; secondary focus on Made-to-order Regular.

**Mechanism:**
- Build a decision rule that selects route_mode (ground, consolidation, air, van) based on:
  - Promised lead time vs. median p90 cycle time for the segment and part availability
  - Service-contract flag (stricter SLA)
  - Part weight and fragility (inspection certificate or prior damage rate)
  - Carrier reliability data (time-to-deliver variance)
- Implement as a real-time "recommend ground + consolidation if p(on-time) > 85%" gate before ship node.
- Override allowed only with explicit exception log (governance tracking for SP1/SP2).

**Environmental improvement:**
- Shift Standard-stock Service-contract from 22% air (7.8 kgCO2e) to 15–20% air; remainder to ground + consolidation.
- Estimated transport reduction: 30–40% for this segment (6,000 orders × ~3 kgCO2e saved = ~18 tCO2e/year).
- Avoid dedicated vans for non-critical orders.

**Service/quality/resilience effect:**
- **Risk:** Service-contract customers accustomed to fast air may complain if lead time increases by 18–24 h.
- **Mitigation:** Combine with priority-picking SLA (handle within 2 h of order arrival) to keep ground still <24 h.
- **Resilience upside:** Ground + consolidation is less volatile than air; reduces dependency on single carrier.

**Required monitoring:**
- Fraction of orders following recommender vs. exceptions.
- On-time delivery rate for ground-picked Service-contract orders (vs. baseline air).
- Exception override reasons and frequency (log compliance with SP1).

### **Intervention S2: Dynamic batching for CNC and heat treatment with maximum waiting-time caps by segment**

**Target path:** Made-to-order orders queued in Batch wait (3,020 cases, 25% of orders).

**Mechanism:**
- Replace fixed batch-size targets with a **time-capped dynamic batching rule:**
  - If queue wait exceeds 24 h (Service-contract) or 48 h (Regular), start production immediately.
  - Otherwise, wait for batch size to reach 12 (CNC_A) or 10 (heat treatment) before processing.
- Monitor batch_id fill rate and wait duration in real time.
- Separate queues by part type (standard vs. heavy/complex) to avoid mixing incompatible geometries.

**Environmental improvement:**
- Reduce average waiting time in batch queues from ~38 h to ~20 h (estimated 45% reduction).
- Shorter wait  lower obsolescence risk (6% of batch items not shipped within 14 days  ~3–4%).
- Reduced wait-induced late-delivery pressure  fewer emergency production  fewer rework loops.
- Energy per item may increase slightly for smaller batches, but net energy saved from fewer rework loops and emergency expedites.

**Service/quality/resilience effect:**
- **Benefit:** Late-delivery risk drops for MTO Regular (18%  ~12%) and MTO Service-contract (13%  ~8–9%).
- **Tradeoff:** Energy per item for CNC_A rises from 1.3 kWh (batch 12+) to ~1.8 kWh (batch 7–9 at time cap). Estimated net +2–3 kWh per order if rework avoided, but potentially 8 kWh if no rework occurs.
- **Quality:** Reduced inspection backlog (shorter queue  more thorough inspection)  lower rework rate.

**Required monitoring:**
- Batch fill rate (average items per batch started).
- Median and p90 batch wait time.
- Rework incidence before/after (especially for CNC_A path).
- Energy per order for MTO segment.

### **Intervention S3: Shift Made-to-order production from CNC_B to CNC_A when queue and due-date slack allow**

**Target path:** MTO orders currently routed to CNC_B (subset of the 3,780 + 760 = 4,540 production orders).

**Mechanism:**
- At "Plan production" node, check CNC_B queue length and part due-date slack.
- If CNC_A has available capacity and order due date is >72 h away, assign to CNC_A instead.
- CNC_B reserved for emergency production and high-priority rush jobs where time slack <36 h.
- Implement a load-balancing rule in the availability check or production planner.

**Environmental improvement:**
- CNC_B has 18% rework rate vs. 9% on CNC_A  fewer rework loops.
- Rework is material-intensive (observed 1.8 kg scrap on CNC_B path vs. 0.70 kg on CNC_A).
- Estimated rework reduction: 5–6 percentage points of the ~1,200 CNC_B orders/year  ~60–72 orders with rework avoided.
- Avoided rework energy: ~60 orders × (2.5 kWh rework cycle + 5 kg scrap handling) = ~150 kWh + reduced packaging rework.

**Service/quality/resilience effect:**
- **Benefit:** CNC_A is slower but more reliable; lower rework  shorter overall cycle time for repeat quality checks.
- **Risk:** If CNC_A queue is deep, pushing orders from CNC_B may increase CNC_A wait time, causing late deliveries.
- **Mitigation:** Only shift if CNC_A slack (capacity × slack hours) exceeds order processing time; otherwise queue CNC_B as normal.

**Required monitoring:**
- CNC_A vs. CNC_B utilization and queue length over time.
- Rework rate for orders shifted to CNC_A (should trend toward 9%).
- Late-delivery rate for shifted orders (must not exceed baseline 18% for MTO Regular).

### **Intervention S4: Replace air for Standard-stock Service-contract with priority pick + ground consolidation**

**Target path:** Standard-stock Service-contract (1,920 orders, 5.8% currently using air, ~110 orders/year).

**Mechanism:**
- Classify Standard-stock Service-contract orders into two tiers:
  1. **Tier 1 (85% of cases):** 4-h pick SLA + ground consolidation (2–4 h wait) + next-day or 36-h delivery.
  2. **Tier 2 (15% of cases):** Parts with <4-h availability confirmed  air only if no slot in consolidation queue.
- Require exception log entry for air shipping (aligns with SP1 policy).
- Consolidation departs 2× daily (06:00, 14:00) to bound waiting time.

**Environmental improvement:**
- Shift ~110 orders/year from air (18.5 kgCO2e) to ground (3.6 kgCO2e).
- Estimated annual reduction: 110 × (18.5  3.6) = ~1,650 kgCO2e (~1.65 tCO2e).
- Consolidation reduces per-order transport emissions by absorbing fixed-route overhead (~2–4 parcels per truck route).

**Service/quality/resilience effect:**
- **Risk:** Tier 1 orders now take 36 h total (vs. 18 h via air). Current baseline is 18 h median, p90 41 h, so 36 h is within historical variation but tighter on some orders.
- **Mitigation:** 4-h priority pick is critical; coordinate with warehouse to ensure reliable fast-pick slots.
- **Resilience:** Ground consolidation uses multiple carriers (if multiple truck routes); less dependent on air capacity.

**Required monitoring:**
- Fraction of Standard-stock Service-contract orders by tier and mode.
- On-time delivery for Tier 1 orders (must remain >95%).
- Transport emissions per order for this segment.
- Exception log entries (SP1 overrides).

### **Intervention S5: Consolidate truck departures twice daily for non-urgent stock orders**

**Target path:** Standard-stock orders assigned to ground or consolidated truck (6,040 + 850 = 6,890 orders, ~57% of all orders).

**Mechanism:**
- Establish two consolidation departure times: 06:00 and 14:00.
- Route orders arriving 00:00–06:00 to the 06:00 departure; 06:01–14:00 to the 14:00 departure.
- Orders arriving after 14:00 go to next-day 06:00 departure.
- Expect average consolidation wait: 12 h (median) and p90 ~24 h.
- Use dedicated consolidation hub or partner 3PL with fixed-schedule lanes.

**Environmental improvement:**
- Consolidation reduces transport emissions by 35–55% vs. individual parcel ground (through load aggregation and route optimization).
- Estimated modal split impact: 850 orders currently use consolidated_truck; target 2,000–2,500 orders (30% of stock).
- Annual savings: 1,650 orders × (3.6  2.0 kgCO2e) = ~2,640 kgCO2e (~2.64 tCO2e).
- Truck utilization improves (full routes vs. partial).

**Service/quality/resilience effect:**
- **Risk:** Consolidation wait of 12–24 h adds to cycle time. Baseline p90 for stock is 62 h; consolidation adds ~18 h average  p90 becomes ~70–75 h.
- **Mitigation:** 
  - Prioritize fast-pick orders (availability confirmed within 2 h) to offset wait.
  - Set maximum 24-h consolidation wait; if order is urgent, fall back to immediate ground parcel.
  - Document promised lead-time slack in order intake; only consolidate if slack 36 h.
- **Resilience:** Consolidation with fixed departure times is more predictable than on-demand parcel; queuing risk lower.

**Required monitoring:**
- % orders routed to consolidation vs. individual ground.
- Median and p90 consolidation wait time.
- p90 cycle time for stock orders (must not exceed 75 h).
- Late-delivery rate (must remain <7% for Regular, <5% for Service-contract).

### **Intervention S6: Improve packaging-route compatibility checks before shipment**

**Target path:** Packaging rework loop (260 + 360 = 620 rework-packaging events, ~5% of packaged orders).

**Mechanism:**
- Implement a **compatibility matrix** at the Package standard/reinforced node:
  - Define rules: route_mode (air requires crush-resistant) vs. package_type (standard, reinforced).
  - Define rules: part_weight (>5 kg) requires reinforced packaging unless damage_risk audit certifies otherwise.
  - Check part dimensions against parcel limits before packaging assignment.
- Add a **damage-risk scorecard** for Made-to-order heavy parts (>8 kg):
  - If part is first-time production or from rework-prone machine, upgrade to reinforced (aligns with Q1 policy).
  - If prior 10 similar orders had <2% damage rate, allow standard packaging.
- Prevent rework-packaging by catching incompatibilities before shipment.

**Environmental improvement:**
- Eliminate 70–80% of rework-packaging events (~400–500 orders/year).
- Avoided rework: repackaging energy (~0.2 kWh/order) + handling labor + potential second shipment.
- Avoided package material cycling: ~5–6 kg per rework event (650 events/year × 0.6 kg average = ~390 kg/year reduced).
- No additional primary packaging waste if reinforced packaging is only applied when genuinely needed (not oversized).

**Service/quality/resilience effect:**
- **Benefit:** Fewer rework loops  shorter cycle time (620 rework events represent ~620 × 8 h = 4,960 person-hours/year of rework).
- **Quality risk:** Reinforced packaging for >8 kg parts is standard safety; if applied correctly, damage rates should fall by ~40% (per the evidence).
- **Tradeoff:** Reinforced packaging doubles material (e.g., 0.5 kg  1.0 kg per order), but only for ~10–15% of orders. Net material increase ~0.05 kg/order, vastly offset by rework avoidance.

**Required monitoring:**
- Rework-packaging event count and reason (incompatibility vs. damage found).
- % orders with damage reported vs. packaging type.
- Cycle time for orders with packaging rework (should decline).

### **Intervention S7: Add inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations**

**Target path:** Inspection cell  rework production loop (470 rework-production events, ~4% of inspected orders; CNC_B has 18% rework).

**Mechanism:**
- Conduct a **root-cause analysis** of rework triggers (data not fully provided; infer from CNC_B high rate):
  - Hypothesis 1: rushed inspections miss defects because queue length exceeds 6 h.
  - Hypothesis 2: CNC_B machine calibration or tooling is less precise.
  - Hypothesis 3: part designs routed to CNC_B are more complex.
- Implement **process-conditional quality gates:**
  - For CNC_B orders: add a secondary inspection step if queue wait >4 h (flag high-rush conditions).
  - For Made-to-order heavy parts (>8 kg) or first-time geometries: mandatory secondary inspection (do not rush).
  - For parts with prior rework history: automated flagging and triage to experienced inspector.
- Use statistical process control (SPC) to monitor CNC_B defect rate; if it exceeds 10% on a moving average, escalate to machine maintenance.

**Environmental improvement:**
- Reduce rework events on CNC_B by ~30–40% (from 18%  12–13% rework incidence).
- Avoided rework material: ~60–80 orders × ~1.8 kg scrap per rework = ~108–144 kg/year.
- Avoided rework energy: ~60–80 orders × ~12–16 kWh = ~720–1,280 kWh/year.

**Service/quality/resilience effect:**
- **Benefit:** Lower rework rate  fewer repeat inspections  shorter cycle time.
- **Quality upside:** Secondary inspection catches defects earlier; reduces customer returns.
- **Operational risk:** Secondary inspection adds ~2–4 h per order, but only for flagged high-risk cases (~5% of production). Net impact: only applies to ~240 orders/year, so minimal cycle-time increase.
- **Resilience:** More thorough quality  fewer customer escalations and emergency returns.

**Required monitoring:**
- Rework incidence by machine (CNC_A vs. CNC_B) and part type.
- Inspection queue time and volume.
- Defect detection rate at secondary gate (% caught before shipment vs. customer returns).

### **Intervention S8: Replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes**

**Target path:** Critical-downtime orders routed to dedicated van (620 orders, 8% of all; transport: 52 kgCO2e/order).

**Mechanism:**
- Segment Critical-downtime orders into two classes:
  1. **True downtime (estimated 60–70% of cases):** customer has active production halt; requires guaranteed 4-h arrival. Keep dedicated van or same-day air.
  2. **Precautionary urgent (30–40% of cases):** customer is at risk but not yet down; willing to accept 12–18 h if cost/carbon is lower. Offer scheduled emergency courier lane (shared with 3–5 customers) departing 2× daily.
- Route precautionary orders to shared lanes (lower cost, lower emissions per order through load sharing).
- Require explicit "downtime confirmed" event in order to trigger dedicated van.

**Environmental improvement:**
- Shift 50–60% of dedicated vans to shared courier lanes.
- Shared lane emissions per order: ~25–30 kgCO2e (vs. 52 kgCO2e solo).
- Estimated reduction: 200–250 orders × (52  27) = ~5,000–6,250 kgCO2e (~5.0–6.3 tCO2e/year).

**Service/quality/resilience effect:**
- **Risk:** Precautionary orders may experience longer wait in shared dispatch queue; some may miss 12-h window if queue is backlogged.
- **Mitigation:** Shared courier contracted with SLA (95% on-time for 12–18 h window). Escalation: if queue exceeds 2 orders, auto-upgrade to dedicated van.
- **Resilience upside:** Shared lanes diversify carrier dependency; if one dedicated carrier fails, shared lanes provide fallback.
- **Quality:** No change; upside is reduced risk of failed downtime response if shared lanes are more reliable than dedicated.

**Required monitoring:**
- % True downtime vs. precautionary by order volume and ratio of confirmed downtime events.
- On-time delivery for shared-lane orders (12–18 h window).
- Escalation frequency (how often precautionary orders are bumped to dedicated).
- Carrier reliability for shared lanes (compare to dedicated baseline).

### **Intervention S9: Split Critical-downtime into true downtime and precautionary categories**

**Target path:** All 1,320 Critical-downtime Service-contract orders.

**Mechanism:**
- Introduce a **downtime severity classification** in the order intake:
  - **Severity A (True downtime):** Production line halted; customer has called emergency support. Lead time: <4 h target. Mode: dedicated van or emergency air.
  - **Severity B (Precautionary urgent):** Customer reports equipment at risk but still operating. Lead time: <18 h acceptable. Mode: priority stock + ground consolidation or shared emergency courier.
- Implement classification rule in "Receive order"  "Availability check" node:
  - Ask customer severity during intake; cross-check with account history.
  - If prior order frequency is <monthly, default to Severity B unless customer confirms downtime.
- Create separate downstream paths (packaging, routing) for each severity.

**Environmental improvement:**
- Reduce unnecessary dedicated vans and express air by ~40–50% (shift from 52 kgCO2e to 15–25 kgCO2e for Severity B orders).
- Estimated: 400–500 Severity B orders × (52  20) = ~12,800–16,000 kgCO2e (~12.8–16.0 tCO2e/year).
- Severity A (true downtime) remains high-emission; justified by downtime cost externality.

**Service/quality/resilience effect:**
- **Risk:** Misclassification (customer claims precautionary but is actually down)  delayed response  reputational harm.
- **Mitigation:** 
  - Implement service-level agreement (SLA) with escalation: if Severity B order breaches 18 h, auto-escalate to Severity A mode at no extra charge.
  - Build 2-h buffer into Severity B planning (target 16 h delivery).
  - Maintain call-in hotline for downtime confirmation.
- **Quality:** No change if classification is accurate.
- **Resilience:** Better segmentation allows targeted resilience investment (e.g., stock key Severity A parts locally).

**Required monitoring:**
- % of orders classified as Severity A vs. B.
- Actual customer downtime incidence (follow-up survey or account data).
- Escalation rate (Severity B  Severity A mid-flight).
- On-time delivery by severity.

### **Intervention S10: Introduce carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular made-to-order customers**

**Target path:** Made-to-order Regular (2,640 orders, 22% of all; highest MTO late-delivery risk at 18%).

**Mechanism:**
- At order intake (Receive order node), calculate two scenarios:
  1. **Fast path:** Priority queuing, no batching. Lead time: ~70 h. Emissions: ~24 kWh + 23 kgCO2e transport. Cost premium: 15–20%.
  2. **Green path:** Batch wait capped at 24 h (S2), ground consolidation (S5). Lead time: ~120 h. Emissions: ~16 kWh + 6 kgCO2e. Cost discount: 5–10%.
- Present both options to customer in order confirmation email.
- Incentivize green path: offer 10% discount, carbon offset certificate, or rebate if customer commits to 3 green orders/quarter.
- Log customer choice and actual usage to tune incentives.

**Environmental improvement:**
- Estimated modal shift: 30–50% of Regular MTO orders shift from fast to green path.
- Per-order savings: ~8 kWh + 17 kgCO2e per shifted order.
- 700–1,300 orders × 17 kgCO2e = ~11,900–22,100 kgCO2e (~12–22 tCO2e/year).

**Service/quality/resilience effect:**
- **Risk:** Regular customers expect fast delivery; green path (120 h) is 70% longer. Adoption may be low unless incentive is compelling.
- **Mitigation:** 
  - Target customers with lower downtime risk (e.g., planned maintenance parts vs. emergency repairs).
  - Use customer segment data (account frequency, order patterns) to identify price-sensitive vs. speed-sensitive customers.
  - Pilot with 20% of Regular MTO orders; measure adoption and satisfaction.
- **Quality:** No change.
- **Resilience:** More predictable cycle time (batching + consolidation is less volatile than priority queuing).

**Required monitoring:**
- % adoption of green vs. fast path by customer cohort.
- Discount/incentive cost vs. emissions avoided.
- Late-delivery rate for green-path orders (should meet 120 h target).
- Customer satisfaction (NPS or survey on green path choice).

### **Intervention S11: Reduce partial shipments by changing availability-check rules**

**Target path:** Partial shipment path (1,010 cases, 8.4% of orders; cycle time 79 h, late delivery 24%).

**Mechanism:**
- **Root cause:** Availability check identifies some items in stock but not all in a multi-line order.
- **Current behavior:** Ship available items, backorder remainder. Creates two shipments, extra cycle time, customer contact overhead.
- **Proposed rule change:**
  - If order contains 2–4 line items and stock availability is 50–90%, offer customer two options at intake:
    1. **Split ship:** Send available items now (72 h), backorder remainder (estimated 96 h wait for full order).
    2. **Hold and batch:** Wait for all items to be in stock or ready for consolidated production (estimated 120 h for full order).
  - Recommend option 2 if promised lead time allows; provide estimated delivery date and carbon savings.
  - Only split-ship if customer explicitly chooses speed and accepts two shipments.

**Environmental improvement:**
- Reduce partial shipments from 1,010 to ~300–400 cases (70% reduction).
- Avoided second shipment: 600–700 orders × 5 kgCO2e per shipment = ~3,000–3,500 kgCO2e (~3.0–3.5 tCO2e/year).
- Reduced packaging rework (second shipment requires new packaging): ~600 orders × 0.5 kg = ~300 kg material saved.

**Service/quality/resilience effect:**
- **Risk:** Customers expecting fast split-ship may resist hold-for-batch option.
- **Mitigation:** 
  - Present both options with delivery date and carbon/cost impact in order confirmation.
  - Track promised lead time; only recommend hold if slack 36 h.
  - For urgent orders, default to split-ship but log as exception.
- **Quality:** Bundled shipments reduce damage risk (fewer handling cycles).
- **Resilience:** Fewer shipments  lower logistics variance and fewer carrier handoffs.

**Required monitoring:**
- % of multi-item orders choosing split-ship vs. hold-and-batch.
- Actual lead-time impact of hold-and-batch (compare 120 h estimate to actual cycle time).
- Late-delivery rate for held orders (must not exceed ~8%).

### **Intervention S12: Create a sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides**

**Target path:** All orders (governance and compliance).

**Mechanism:**
- Implement a **decision-log system** at key decision nodes:
  - **SP1 override (green shipping recommender):** If order routed to air/express but promised lead time allows ground (>85% confidence), log override reason (e.g., "customer last-minute change," "weather disruption," "stock unavailable").
  - **SP2 override (dedicated van):** If order routed to dedicated van but not Critical-downtime Severity A, log justification.
  - **Q1 override (packaging rule):** If part >8 kg shipped with standard packaging without damage-risk audit, log reason.
- Link overrides to customer, account, order attributes.
- Generate weekly governance report: override count, reasons, cost/emissions impact.
- Use report to identify systematic gaps (e.g., "unavailability rule too conservative," "customer always chooses air").

**Environmental improvement:**
- Indirect: improves visibility and accountability, enabling iterative refinement of S1–S11.
- Direct savings: catching unjustified overrides (e.g., 22% of Standard-stock Service-contract air shipments may be preventable)  ~3–4 tCO2e/year if overrides are reduced to <5%.

**Service/quality/resilience effect:**
- **Benefit:** Governance creates organizational alignment; forces tradeoff discussions (speed vs. carbon vs. cost).
- **Risk:** Excessive logging overhead may reduce operational flexibility.
- **Mitigation:** Automate override detection; only log anomalies (e.g., air for low-slack orders) or high-cost exceptions.
- **Resilience:** Transparency helps identify failures; e.g., if stock unavailability is chronic, may justify local inventory investment.

**Required monitoring:**
- Override frequency and reason distribution.
- Cost and emissions impact of overrides.
- Trend over time (should improve as root causes are addressed).
- Process-mining conformance metrics (SP1/SP2/Q1 compliance rate).

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

### **Risk 1: Batching consolidation  obsolescence increase**

**Mechanism:**
- S2 (dynamic batching with time caps) and S5 (2× daily consolidation) both increase queuing and lead times.
- Longer lead times  higher risk that demand forecasts change (customer cancels, part becomes superseded, or market demand shift).
- Evidence: 6% of batch-produced items are not shipped within 14 days when forecasts are wrong.
- If batching wait increases from 24 h to 48 h on average, obsolescence risk may rise from 6% to 8–10%.

**Displacement:**
- Avoided energy/emissions from larger batches or consolidation are offset by write-off of unsalable inventory.
- Estimated: 1% increase in obsolescence rate × 3,780 MTO orders × 15 kWh (embodied in unshipped batch) = 567 kWh wasted per year.
- Emissions from write-off/recycling: ~100–200 kgCO2e per year (incineration or landfill).

**Mitigation:**
- Link demand-forecast accuracy to order intake. Offer shorter lead times (and higher cost) for orders with <2-week visibility.
- Implement **obsolescence reserve:** allocate 2–3% of batch-produced items for quality testing or sample builds to avoid overstock.
- Cap batch-wait time to 24 h (as in S2) to keep obsolescence within historical 6% baseline.
- Use batch-demand sensing: if waiting batch has <80% committed demand, split into smaller run.

---

### **Risk 2: Ground consolidation  missed service commitments and urgency rebound**

**Mechanism:**
- S4, S5, S10 shift orders from air/express to ground consolidation (12–24 h wait added).
- Consolidation requires customers to accept longer lead times.
- **Rebound:** Customers who perceive consolidation as unreliable may switch to purchasing from competitors with guaranteed next-day service, or place safety-stock orders (over-ordering) to avoid stock-outs.
- If 20% of shifted customers rebound (e.g., 2–3 Standard-stock Service-contract orders per customer per quarter = ~150 orders), they may switch mode back to air or place duplicate orders, negating transport savings.

**Displacement:**
- Lost orders: 150 orders × 7 kgCO2e (avoided via consolidation) = 1,050 kgCO2e saved is lost (offset by customer churn).
- Duplicate/safety-stock orders from other competitors: ~100 additional orders/year placed elsewhere, not captured in internal sustainability metrics.

**Mitigation:**
- **Contractual SLA:** Offer consolidation only with explicit on-time-delivery guarantees (95%+ for 36-h window) backed by rebate if missed.
- **Transparency:** Communicate consolidation schedule and transparency into tracking; give customers visibility 6 h before departure so they can escalate if urgent.
- **Tiered pricing:** Consolidation is cheaper (-5 to -10%) than air; capture willingness to wait with pricing rather than forcing all customers into slower mode.
- **Segment targeting:** Consolidation is best for Regular customers with flexible demand; keep Service-contract on fast mode to protect retention.

---

### **Risk 3: Rework reduction (S2, S3, S6, S7)  lower quality scrutiny / inspection cost-cutting**

**Mechanism:**
- If S7 adds secondary inspection or S2 improves process quality, rework may decline from 11% (MTO) to 6–8%.
- Cost savings from fewer rework cycles (~5–8 h labor per rework, plus material resourcing) may incentivize operational teams to cut corners elsewhere.
- **Rebound:** Inspection budgets are reduced (e.g., "inspections were slowing us down; now we can skip secondary checks on other parts"). Defect rates may creep up on non-flagged part types.
- Rework rate drifts back toward 10% within 12 months as discipline erodes.

**Displacement:**
- Avoided rework energy (estimated 2 to 3% from S2, S3, S7) is offset by new defects appearing in customer returns, which are not detected in-house.
- Customer returns generate reverse logistics (emissions), rework (high-priority energy), and brand damage.

**Mitigation:**
- **Institutionalize quality gate:** Make S7 (secondary inspection rule) and S2 (batch-timing discipline) part of documented standard operating procedures (SOP).
- **Continuous monitoring:** Track rework rate by machine and part type monthly; set control limits (e.g., alert if CNC_A rework drifts above 12%). Escalate to management if SPC signals.
- **Incentive alignment:** Tie cost savings from reduced rework to reinvestment in quality tools (e.g., automated defect detection, equipment maintenance).
- **Customer data:** Monitor returned-goods rate; if it increases, escalate and re-tighten inspection.

---

### **Risk 4: Shift from CNC_B to CNC_A (S3)  CNC_A bottleneck and late deliveries on CNC_A path**

**Mechanism:**
- S3 proposes shifting Made-to-order orders from CNC_B (high rework, 18%) to CNC_A (lower rework, 9%).
- If all CNC_B orders are redirected to CNC_A, CNC_A queue depth increases; cycle time on CNC_A path may grow from ~50 h to ~80–100 h.
- **Rebound:** MTO Regular orders on CNC_A now have longer cycle time (100 h) and higher p90 risk (35–45% late delivery vs. baseline 18%).
- Alternative: orders are queued so long that customers cancel or expedite via CNC_B anyway (defeating the purpose).

**Displacement:**
- Avoided rework emissions (S3: 0.8 kg scrap per order) are displaced by late-delivery surge, which may trigger emergency production or air shipping to meet promised dates.
- Estimated: 200 orders × (16 kWh emergency energy + 23 kgCO2e express) = 4,600 kgCO2e offset from S3 benefit (~45 kgCO2e).

**Mitigation:**
- **Conditional routing in S3:** Only shift CNC_B  CNC_A if CNC_A queue wait is <12 h. If CNC_A is overloaded, route to CNC_B with reinforced inspection (S7) instead.
- **Capacity balance:** Implement load-balancing: if CNC_A is trending toward >80 h lead time, increase capacity (e.g., extend shifts, invest in parallel equipment) or reduce intake.
- **Segment tiering:** Shift only Regular MTO orders with flexible lead-time slack (>120 h promised) to CNC_A; keep Service-contract and tight-deadline orders on CNC_B with S7 quality gates.

---

### **Risk 5: Multi-intervention interaction: consolidation + batching  cascade delays**

**Mechanism:**
- S2 (dynamic batching, max 24-h wait) and S5 (2× daily consolidation, 12–24 h wait) are sequentially applied to many orders.
- If an order hits both batch wait (24 h) and consolidation wait (24 h), total delay = 48 h added.
- **Rebound:** Orders that are consolidated but hit batch-wait saturation may be deprioritized or bumped to the slower consolidation slot (14:00 instead of 06:00).
- Median cycle time for MTO orders rises from 94 h to 140–160 h; p90 from 180 h to 220–240 h.
- Late-delivery risk increases from 18% (MTO Regular) to 25–30%, violating service expectations.

**Displacement:**
- Cycle-time increase (48+ h) may trigger customer cancellations or complaints, leading to service-recovery shipments (express air).
- Estimated: 5% of affected orders (~200 orders/year) switch to air recovery  200 × 23 kgCO2e = 4,600 kgCO2e emissions spike.
- Net benefit of S2 + S5 is erased for this segment.

**Mitigation:**
- **Execution sequence:** Implement S2 (batching with time caps) first and validate that MTO cycle time stabilizes <120 h.
  - Only then roll out S5 (consolidation) to non-MTO orders (stock, fast-pick items).
  - Do NOT apply S5 consolidation to MTO orders; use express ground instead (no batching overlap).
- **Queue separation:** Maintain separate fulfillment queues for stock (consolidation-eligible) vs. make-to-order (batch-eligible). Prevent cross-queue congestion.
- **Dynamic override:** If an order accumulates >48 h queuing delay (batch + consolidation), auto-escalate to non-batched, non-consolidated fast-ground path.

---

### **Risk 6: Carbon-aware negotiation (S10) adoption  lower-margin business model and customer cherry-picking**

**Mechanism:**
- S10 offers customers a "green path" discount (5–10%) or carbon-offset incentive for accepting 120-h lead time vs. 70-h fast path.
- **Rebound 1:** Only price-sensitive customers (low-margin, high-volume) adopt green path; high-value, low-volume customers continue on fast path.
- Over time, margin-weighted profitability declines; green path becomes a loss leader.
- **Rebound 2:** Competitors copy green-pricing model, driving down margins across the industry.
- Company is forced to cut costs (e.g., less rigorous quality inspection, reduced buffer stock) to maintain profitability.

**Displacement:**
- Cost-cutting undermines quality and resilience benefits of S2, S6, S7.
- Rework and returns increase; customer satisfaction declines.

**Mitigation:**
- **Tiered incentives:** Carbon discounts are highest (10%) for repeat adopters (e.g., customer places >50% of orders on green path). Single-order green adopters get 2–3% discount only.
- **Volume targets:** Set a target (e.g., 30% green adoption) and cap total discount budget. If adoption exceeds target, reduce incentive and instead communicate brand value ("we shipped 30% of orders carbon-neutral").
- **Cost management:** Ensure green path is genuinely lower-cost (batching + consolidation drive real labor and transport savings). Don't subsidize green path; let efficiency gains justify discount.
- **Premium positioning:** Complement green pricing with premium fast-track offering ("guaranteed 12-h delivery, air-shipped") at +20% markup. This attracts margin-rich customers and balances portfolio.

---

### **Risk 7: Exception logging (S12) governance  process rigidity and emergency bypass**

**Mechanism:**
- S12 mandates logging exceptions (SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides) with justification.
- **Rebound:** Operational teams view exception logging as bureaucracy. Pressure to "log" exceptions increases workload; teams start approving expedited shipments (air, dedicated van) verbally without logging, to avoid paperwork.
- Override rate appears to improve (fewer logged exceptions) but actual expedites continue unabated (hidden exceptions).
- Governance becomes performative (numbers improve in dashboards) but real environmental impact is unchanged.

**Displacement:**
- Exceptions that should be eliminated (unjustified air shipments for Service-contract orders) persist but are no longer tracked.
- Opportunities to address root causes (e.g., "customer always demands air because previous company promised it") are missed because exceptions are hidden.

**Mitigation:**
- **Simplify logging:** Make exception logging a single-click decision in the order system, not a separate form. Integrate into existing workflow.
- **Automated triggers:** Use process-mining rules to flag suspicious override patterns (e.g., "Service-contract order routed to air but promised lead time >24 h"). Auto-log these as exceptions; require confirmation/justification.
- **Feedback loop:** Monthly governance review includes anomaly detection (e.g., "why are 15% of CNC_B orders marked as emergency production?"). Escalate patterns to process owners.
- **Incentive alignment:** Tie operational bonuses or department KPIs to exception rate trends, not just cost/volume. Encourage teams to prevent need for exceptions.

## 6. Balanced recommendation

### Proposed coherent redesign package

**Phased implementation approach:**

#### **Phase 1 (Months 1–3): Foundation and quick wins**
**Objective:** Establish governance, baseline monitoring, and low-risk improvements.

**Interventions:**
1. **S12 (Exception log)** + **Conformance monitoring:** 
   - Deploy decision-log system for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides in order management system.
   - Begin weekly governance reporting on override patterns and root causes.
   - Baseline: measure current compliance with SP1 (green shipper), SP2 (dedicated van), Q1 (reinforced packaging) policies.
   
2. **S6 (Packaging compatibility):**
   - Implement packaging-route compatibility matrix at Package node.
   - Create damage-risk scorecard for >8 kg parts; use historical return data to set thresholds.
   - Target: reduce rework-packaging events from 620/year to ~300/year within 3 months.
   - **Benefit:** 350–400 kg material saved, minimal cycle-time impact, improves packaging quality.

3. **S7 (QA gate for high-rework combinations):**
   - Conduct root-cause analysis of CNC_B rework (18% rate) and inspection backlog.
   - Implement secondary inspection trigger: if CNC_B queue wait >4 h, add inspector queue monitoring.
   - If part is first-time or from prior rework history, flag for secondary inspection.
   - **Benefit:** Reduce CNC_B rework from 18% to ~12–13% over 3 months; avoid 100–150 kg scrap/year.

4. **S2 (Dynamic batching with time caps):**
   - Implement max-wait rules for production queues:
     - Service-contract MTO orders: max batch wait 24 h (vs. baseline ~38 h).
     - Regular MTO orders: max batch wait 36 h.
   - Trigger: start production immediately if queue wait exceeds cap, regardless of batch fill.
   - **Benefit:** Reduce MTO cycle time (median 94 h  80 h), lower late-delivery risk (18%  12–15%), avoid rework loops.
   - **Risk:** Energy per item may increase slightly (smaller batches), but offset by avoided rework. Monitor and adjust.

**Expected Phase 1 outcomes:**
- Transport emissions: no change (not yet targeted).
- Energy: slight increase (smaller batches), offset by rework reduction (~1%).
- Material scrap: 400 to 500 kg/yr.
- Cycle time: 12 to 18 h for MTO orders.
- Late delivery: 4 to 6 pp for MTO Regular and MTO Service-contract.
- Rework rate: 4 to 6 pp (CNC_B and packaging).

---

#### **Phase 2 (Months 4–6): Transport and logistics optimization**
**Objective:** Reduce transport emissions without major service deterioration.

**Interventions:**
5. **S1 (Green shipper recommender):**
   - Build decision rule: at Ship node, recommend route_mode based on promised lead time, customer segment, part characteristics.
   - Rule: "Ground + consolidation if p(on-time) > 85% given promised lead time."
   - Implement for Standard-stock orders first (lower risk than MTO).
   - Require exception log for air/express shipments.
   - **Benefit:** Shift 15–20% of Standard-stock Service-contract orders from air (18.5 kgCO2e) to ground (3.6 kgCO2e); save ~3–4 tCO2e/yr.

6. **S4 (Priority pick + ground for Service-contract):**
   - Implement 4-h priority-pick SLA for Standard-stock Service-contract orders.
   - Consolidate with S1: ground shipment leaves within 8–12 h of order arrival (pick + consolidation wait).
   - **Benefit:** Total delivery time ~24–30 h (vs. 18 h via air but similar customer experience due to faster pick).
   - Transport reduction: ~1.5 tCO2e/yr (as estimated in S4).

7. **S5 (2× daily consolidation for stock orders):**
   - Establish consolidation schedule: 06:00 and 14:00 departures for non-urgent stock.
   - Link to S1 recommender: orders eligible for consolidation are those with promised lead time >36 h slack.
   - **Benefit:** 35–55% transport emissions reduction for consolidated orders; target 2,000–2,500 stock orders (~30% of stock).
   - Expected savings: ~2.6 tCO2e/yr.
   - **Monitoring:** p90 cycle time for stock must remain <75 h (baseline 62 h + 12–24 h consolidation wait).

8. **S3 (Shift MTO from CNC_B to CNC_A when queue allows):**
   - Implement at Plan production node: if CNC_A queue wait <12 h and order due date >72 h away, route to CNC_A.
   - Otherwise, route to CNC_B but apply S7 (secondary inspection) if risk flagged.
   - **Benefit:** Shift ~30% of CNC_B orders (~250/yr) to CNC_A; reduce rework from 18% to 9%; avoid ~60 kg scrap/yr.
   - **Risk:** Monitor CNC_A queue; if trending >20 h wait, pause CNC_BA shifts.

**Expected Phase 2 outcomes:**
- Transport emissions: 7.1 to 8.6 tCO2e/yr (largest environmental improvement).
- Cycle time: +12 to +24 h for stock orders (consolidation wait), but still within p90 targets.
- Late delivery: +1 to +2 pp if consolidation SLA not met; mitigated by S1 lead-time logic.
- Rework: further 2 to 3 pp from CNC_B shift.

---

#### **Phase 3 (Months 7–9): Segmentation and customer engagement**
**Objective:** Further optimize through better segmentation and customer choice.

**Interventions:**
9. **S9 (Severity split for Critical-downtime):**
   - Classify Critical-downtime orders at intake: Severity A (true downtime, <4 h) vs. Severity B (precautionary, <18 h).
   - Route Severity A to dedicated van or emergency air (52 kgCO2e, justified).
   - Route Severity B to shared emergency courier lanes or priority stock + ground (25–30 kgCO2e).
   - **Benefit:** Shift 40–50% of Critical-downtime orders from dedicated van to shared lanes; save ~12.8–16.0 tCO2e/yr.
   - **Risk:** Misclassification (customer claims precautionary but is actually down); mitigated by 2-h buffer and escalation SLA.

10. **S10 (Carbon-aware negotiation for Regular MTO):**
   - Pilot with 20% of Regular MTO customers: offer "green path" option at 5–10% discount (lead time 120 h vs. 70 h fast).
   - Present both options in order confirmation; track adoption rate.
   - If adoption >30%, expand to 50% of Regular MTO customer base.
   - **Benefit:** Shift 700–1,300 orders/yr to slower, batched, consolidated mode; save ~12–22 tCO2e/yr in energy and transport.
   - Invest discount revenue in S2/S5 infrastructure (batching discipline, consolidation reliability).
   - **Risk:** Customer adoption may be low (<20%); pilot first before scaling.

11. **S11 (Reduce partial shipments):**
   - At Availability check, identify multi-line orders with 50–90% stock availability.
   - Offer customers: "Ship available now (72 h, 2 shipments)" vs. "Hold for full order (120 h, 1 shipment)."
   - Recommend hold-for-batch if promised lead time >120 h; offer as default, customer can override.
   - **Benefit:** Reduce partial shipments from 1,010 to ~300–400/yr; save ~3.0–3.5 tCO2e/yr and avoid second shipment handling.
   - **Risk:** Some customers expect fast split-ship; mitigate with transparent option and lead-time visibility.

**Expected Phase 3 outcomes:**
- Transport emissions: additional 18 to 30 tCO2e/yr (from S9, S10, S11).
- Energy: 1 to 2% (from S10 batching benefits).
- Customer satisfaction: **requires monitoring** (consolidation wait, longer lead times may affect NPS).
- Cycle time: +20–40 h for 30–50% of orders (S10 green-path adopters and held partial shipments), but within promised lead times for this segment.

---

#### **Phase 4 (Months 10–12): Validation, refinement, and institutionalization**
**Objective:** Confirm benefits, adjust based on data, and embed sustainable practices in standard operations.

**Validation activities:**
- Full process-mining reanalysis: compare event logs before/after all interventions.
- Conformance checks: SP1/SP2/Q1 compliance rates (target >95%).
- Service-level monitoring: late-delivery rates by segment (must remain within tolerance).
- Carbon accounting: sum of estimated transport, energy, material reductions vs. actual process-mining data.
- Rework and returns: track defect and rework rates (should improve or stay flat, not increase).
- Customer feedback: NPS, complaint escalations on consolidation wait and longer lead times.

**Refinement gates:**
- If Phase 2 consolidation drives p90 stock cycle time above 75 h, reduce consolidation target from 30% to 20% of stock orders.
- If Phase 3 S10 green-path adoption is <15%, increase discount to 10–15% or pilot with different customer cohort.
- If Phase 3 partial-shipment reduction is <50% (i.e., customers override hold-for-batch), reduce default to "split-ship" and make hold-for-batch opt-in.

**Institutionalization:**
- Codify S2, S6, S7 rules in updated SOPs for production, packaging, and inspection.
- Embed S1 recommender and S12 exception log in ERP/WMS systems as standard decision gates.
- Create quarterly governance review (management + operations + finance + sustainability teams) to monitor policies and adjust targets.
- Establish carbon reduction KPI for supply-chain team, linked to bonus/incentive structure.

### Exception governance framework

**Three-tiered exception handling:**

1. **Tier 1 (Green exceptions - autonomous):**
   - Order-level exceptions: 1–2 overrides per week (e.g., customer last-minute air request, system failure).
   - Approved by: Order fulfillment manager.
   - Logged automatically in exception log.
   - No escalation if <5 per week and total impact <1% of transport emissions.

2. **Tier 2 (Yellow exceptions - escalation):**
   - Systematic patterns: >10% of a customer's orders override SP1 (requesting air when ground is feasible).
   - Approved by: Supply-chain director and account manager together.
   - Logged + investigated for root cause (customer expectation misalignment, product issue, competitor pressure).
   - Action: customer conversation, policy negotiation, or segmentation change.

3. **Tier 3 (Red exceptions - governance board):**
   - Policy violations: >20% of an order segment violate SP2 (dedicated vans without Critical-downtime flag).
   - Approved by: Monthly governance board (supply chain, operations, finance, sustainability).
   - Logged + escalated to process re-design (e.g., is SP2 policy realistic?).
   - Action: policy update, process redesign, or investment (e.g., fleet capacity).

### Risk monitoring plan

**Six monthly risk reviews:**

1. **Obsolescence:** Track % of batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days. If >8%, adjust S2 batch-wait caps downward or improve demand forecasting.
2. **Service degradation:** If p90 cycle time for stock rises above 75 h or late-delivery rate exceeds segment target + 2 pp, reduce consolidation %, pause green-path expansion, or adjust lead-time promises.
3. **Quality drift:** If rework rate increases after Phase 1 (rebound from S6/S7 gains), escalate to quality team; review inspection processes and reinforce SOP discipline.
4. **CNC_A bottleneck:** If CNC_A queue median wait >18 h, revert S3 shifts and focus on CNC_B process improvement (S7) instead.
5. **Customer churn:** Track NPS and customer retention, especially for accounts moving to consolidation/green-path. If NPS declines >5 points or churn >2%, pause green-path incentives and refocus on service certainty.
6. **Carrier dependency:** If consolidation partner fails (capacity constraint, service breach), have fallback: increase individual ground parcels (higher emissions but maintains service). Monitor diversification.

**Annual sustainability review (end of Year 1):**
- Aggregate environmental performance: total kgCO2e/order by segment, trend vs. industry benchmarks.
- ROI analysis: cost of interventions vs. carbon reduction and margin impact.
- Policy effectiveness: are SP1/SP2/Q1 achieving intended outcomes, or are they barriers to service?

- Invest in long-term solutions (e.g., local consolidation hubs, equipment efficiency upgrades, demand-forecasting AI).
- Set ambitious targets (e.g., 40% transport emissions, 5% energy by Year 3) and allocate capital accordingly.

## Summary: Why this recommendation is balanced

This redesign package balances environmental impact, service, quality, and resilience by:

1. **Phased sequencing:** Foundation and quick wins (Phase 1) build organizational capability and confidence before tackling higher-risk transport and lead-time changes (Phases 2–3). Reduces execution risk.

2. **Targeted interventions:** Each of the 12 interventions addresses a specific process path with quantified impacts:
   - High-impact, low-risk improvements first (S2, S6, S7: rework reduction, 6 to 10 pp).
   - Medium-impact, medium-risk changes second (S1, S4, S5: transport consolidation, 7 to 8 tCO2e/mo).
   - Customer-facing, high-adoption-risk changes last (S10, S11: green pricing and partial-shipment reduction).

3. **Rebound and displacement mitigation:** Each intervention acknowledges risks (e.g., batching  obsolescence, consolidation  late deliveries, CNC_A shifts  queue bottlenecks) and includes mitigation strategies (time caps, SLA guarantees, load balancing, conditional logic).

4. **Segment differentiation:** Service-contract customers retain fast, reliable options; Regular customers are offered lower-cost green alternatives with transparency. Critical-downtime is split (true downtime keeps high speed/cost; precautionary shifts to cost-effective shared lanes). No one-size-fits-all sustainability policy.

5. **Data-driven governance:** Validation plan uses process-mining conformance metrics (SP1/SP2/Q1), environmental KPIs (transport, energy, scrap), and service metrics (on-time delivery, rework, cycle time) to continuously adjust. Exception logging captures deviations and root causes.

6. **Institutional embedding:** Interventions are codified in SOPs, ERP systems, and governance reviews, not left as ad-hoc initiatives. Sustainability KPIs are tied to operational bonuses and quarterly reviews, ensuring accountability.

7. **Realistic impact scope:** Estimated total environmental benefit is 30 to 40 tCO2e/year (~20–30% reduction from transport-dominant segments) and 1,000 to 1,500 kg material scrap/year. Service impact is managed: late-delivery risk remains within 5–8 pp for most segments, and rework/quality improves significantly.

This is not a "go green at all costs" recommendation; it is a pragmatic process redesign that uses operational efficiency improvements (batching, consolidation, quality) to drive sustainability gains while protecting customer service and profitability.
