# Robust Process Redesign: Regional Diagnostic Lab

## 1. Stress scenarios

**S1 — Urgent/Complex demand-mix surge (no major outage)**  
Weekly volume ~1.3–1.5× normal, mix shifts to ~30% Urgent + ~22% Complex-panel (as in peak log). Courier and machines nominal. Contractual 18h Urgent risk and medical-validator load dominate. Recollection and specialist-review rates rise with complexity, not only volume.

**S2 — Assay-machine degradation / M2-heavy failure cluster**  
M2 failure rate elevated (night-shift and M2-linked repeats already visible in variant notes). Effective assay capacity falls 15–25%; Repeat-after-failure share climbs toward 12–15%. Technical validation and preparation backs up behind rework loops. Urgent bypass of batching fragments remaining M1 schedule.

**S3 — Medical validator + specialist-review absence**  
One medical validator out and/or specialist unavailable (every second Friday pattern amplified). Hold-for-specialist-review median queue already 36h in peak; Complex-panel (2.4× more likely to enter hold) and Urgent cases needing medical validation stall. Technical validators may release what they can, but medical gate becomes the hard stop.

**S4 — Courier arrival compression / delay**  
Arrivals spike harder at 10:00 and 17:00 or shift later (weather/traffic). Registration and pre-analytical check see multi-hour surges; clerks exceed 9h effective load  accession errors  more Manual accession correction and Request recollection loops (peak DFG already shows higher cancel/recollect fractions). Downstream stations idle then flood.

**S5 — Compound winter wave (mix + partial outage + Friday specialist gap)**  
S1 + reduced assay uptime + specialist absence. This is the design-limiting case: bottleneck migration is fast, historical averages useless, and single-station overtime cannot clear contractual Urgent risk.

## 2. Bottleneck-migration analysis

**S1 (mix surge)**  
Primary: Medical validators (peak util 93%, median queue 22h; Complex-panel 3.1× medical validation) and Technical validators (101%, 16h).  
Secondary: Assay machines (98%, 18h) because Urgent bypass (falls from 74%  58% in peak) still fragments batches while Complex-panel multi-assay load rises.  
Tertiary: Pre-analytical check (96%, 9.5h) and Accession correction (only 2 specialists, 14h queue).  
If only Urgent-first (I1) is applied: preparation/assay clear Urgents but starve Routine and some Complex-panel  WIP explosion; Batch wait edges (9/22h) and Technical validation queues worsen for non-Urgent. Relieving medical validation alone (evening shift) without controlling assay feed simply moves the pile to Release and next-day technical validation.

**S2 (assay degradation)**  
Primary: Assay run / Repeat assay loop (peak: AssayRepeat 1260, RepeatAssay 1080; performance RepeatAssay 10/26h). M2-heavy routing increases repeats.  
Migration: Test preparation and Batch wait inflate (Batch waitAssay 9/22h); Technical validation starves then surges; Repeat-after-failure cases consume double assay slots and cluster after nights.  
If M1 is dedicated to Urgent/Complex (I3) while M2 takes all Routine under high failure: Routine p90 collapses and repeat load rebounds onto M1 via rework, undoing the dedication. Cross-training technical validators into accession (I4) without protecting validation capacity creates a new technical-validation bottleneck exactly when repeat volume needs more validators.

**S3 (validator/specialist absence)**  
Primary: Hold for specialist review (5/36h; peak frequency TechnicalHold 610 vs normal 290) then Medical validation.  
Migration: Technical validators hold WIP or release incomplete paths; Preparation and assay continue producing  inventory of unvalidated results; Urgent contractual breaches concentrate on cases needing medical sign-off.  
Outsourcing Complex-panel overflow (I10) relieves specialist queue but adds ~12h transport and re-introduces registration/pre-analytical risk on return path; local medical validators may still be needed for non-outsourced Urgent. Evening medical shift (I5) helps only if specialist hold is also drained or bypassed for defined case classes.

**S4 (courier compression)**  
Primary: Courier intake  Registration  Pre-analytical check (ReceivePre-analytical 2/9h; Pre-analyticalTest prep 4/13h).  
Migration: Manual accession correction and Request recollection jump (peak recollect path already larger); clerks spend time on physician explanation  effective intake capacity falls (variant note); recollection loops (RequestReceive 42/60h) remove capacity from the same pools.  
Automating registration (I8) clears the front door and dumps volume onto pre-analytical and assay — peak pre-analytical 96% cannot absorb an unfiltered wave. Early defect triage (I6) helps only if recollection logistics and physician communication are staffed; otherwise cancels rise (peak Cancel already 540).

**S5 (compound)**  
Sequence typically: courier/registration spike  pre-analytical & accession queues  preparation/assay congestion with high repeat fraction  technical validation over capacity  medical/specialist wall.  
Relieving any single station (e.g., large Routine batches I2, or Urgent-first I1) accelerates flow into the next saturated pool. Historical median cycle times (Routine 38h normal) are non-predictive; p90 and “% cases with queue >12h” (22%54%) are the relevant stress signals.

## 3. Robust redesign package

Package focuses on **protecting contractual Urgent flow**, **containing rework loops**, **flexing the two scarcest multi-use skills** (accession correction and technical validation), and **smoothing intake** — without assuming infinite headcount or simultaneous priority for all types.

**R1 — Early pre-analytical defect triage within 2h (I6, strengthened)**  
Reject/recollect poor samples before preparation/assay.  
Why robust: Cuts recollection loops that steal intake and assay capacity in S1/S4/S5; reduces wasted M1/M2 cycles in S2.  
Downstream risk: Short-term spike in Request recollection and physician calls — must pair with scripted clerk playbook and capped daily recollect explanations.  
Fails if: Courier delay already > triage window or digital photo/quality metadata missing.

**R2 — Selective registration automation for complete digital metadata (I8) + courier slot smoothing (I7)**  
Automate only clean orders; keep human path for incomplete. Shift pickup windows to flatten 10:00/17:00 spikes.  
Why robust: Lowers registration util and error-driven accession work across S1/S4/S5 without blindly flooding pre-analytical.  
Downstream risk: Clean automated orders arrive faster at pre-analytical — triage (R1) must be live first.  
Fails if: Metadata quality is poor (automation creates correction load) or couriers cannot reslot.

**R3 — Dynamic capacity band at preparation/assay (not pure I1 or pure I2)**  
- Default batch size 12; allow 18–24 only for pure Routine lanes when Urgent WIP below threshold.  
- Urgent: reserved bypass slots on M1 (time-boxed lanes), not unlimited Urgent-first.  
- Complex-panel: scheduled multi-assay blocks, not ad-hoc preemption of every Routine.  
Why robust: Avoids I1 starvation of Routine/Complex and I2 burial of Urgent; matches variant behavior (Urgent bypass 74%58%).  
Downstream risk: Technical validation still sees bursts — needs R4.  
Fails if: “Reserved slots” are overridden informally every peak hour.

**R4 — Cross-train 2 technical validators  accession correction (I4) with a floor on validation staffing**  
Pull-to-accession only when accession queue > X and technical validation queue < Y.  
Why robust: Addresses the 2-person accession chokepoint (14h peak queue) that blocks many cases, while preventing I4’s failure mode (validation shortage). Useful in S1/S4/S5.  
Downstream risk: If floor is breached, technical validation becomes bottleneck and Release slows for all types.  
Fails if: Training not done before winter; or managers ignore the floor under pressure.

**R5 — Peak evening medical-validation shift (I5) + predefined Complex-panel overflow rule (I10 lite)**  
Evening medical coverage during declared peaks; outsource only Complex-panel cases already in specialist-hold beyond threshold, accepting 12h transport.  
Why robust: Directly attacks medical/specialist wall in S1/S3/S5 without sending all Complex volume out (traceability cost).  
Downstream risk: Outsourced returns need clear re-entry registration; evening shift can create next-morning Release/reporting bunching.  
Fails if: Partner lab quality/SLA weak or specialist-hold criteria too loose (over-outsourcing).

**R6 — Stop-the-line policy on M2-related repeat clusters**  
If repeat rate after M2 exceeds band, shed Routine batch size and shift critical assays to M1 within reserved caps; defer non-contractual Routine.  
Why robust: Contains S2 without permanent M1 dedication (I3’s trap under high Routine+failure load).  
Assumption: Real-time repeat reason codes exist.  
Fails if: All volume is Urgent/Complex — then external overflow or explicit deferral is required.

**Assumptions across package**  
- 4-week cross-training lead time is available before peak season.  
- Contract allows defined Routine deferral under declared emergency.  
- Partner lab (I10) is contracted with traceability before winter.  
- Managers will not run pure Urgent-first or max batching outside the band rules.

## 6. Residual vulnerabilities

1. **Specialist single-point fragility** — Even with I10 lite, one specialist pattern (second Fridays) plus Complex-panel mix leaves a tail that outsourcing only partially absorbs; transport delay trades queue for aging.  
2. **Technical validation as the migrated bottleneck** — R3 and R1 increase successful feed rate into TV; peak TV util already 101%. Cross-training (R4) helps accession only if the validation floor holds — under compound peak, TV remains the hinge.  
3. **Rework clustering after nights / M2** — R6 mitigates but does not remove equipment reliability; chronic M2 failure still forces Routine deferral that may be organizationally unacceptable.  
4. **Redesign-induced vulnerability: reserved M1 slots + evening medical bunching** — Protecting Urgent can create synchronized morning Release and reporting load; clerks/validators face a second artificial peak. Over-triggering P-Compound erodes staff trust and increases error rate after extended shifts (registration note: errors rise after 9h).  
5. **Partner-lab traceability and re-entry** — Selective I10 adds handoff failure modes not present in the local DFG (new cancel/correction paths).  
6. **Cannot prioritize all types at once** — Routine 72h and Complex quality/specialist needs will lose under S5; explicit deferral board is required honesty, not a fully “green” matrix.  
7. **Metadata/automation brittleness** — If I8 expands faster than data quality, Manual accession correction worsens the exact bottleneck R4 was meant to fix.

## 7. Minimum monitoring dashboard

**Leading indicators (daily / intra-day in peaks)**  

| Indicator | Warning band | Action band |
|-----------|--------------|-------------|
| Urgent age at prep & at assay (p50 / p75) | p50 > 6h or p75 > 10h | p50 > 10h or any Urgent age > 14h |
| Pre-analytical queue (median hours) | > 5h | > 8h |
| Accession correction queue + # open corrections | Queue > 6h or backlog > 2× normal | Queue > 12h |
| Assay machine util & repeat rate by M1/M2 (rolling 24h) | Util > 90% or repeats > 1.3× baseline | Util > 95% or repeats > 1.5× / M2 cluster |
| Technical validation queue vs on-floor TV headcount | Queue > 8h or util > 90% | Queue > 14h or headcount below floor |
| Medical validation queue + specialist hold age p50 | MV queue > 8h or hold p50 > 18h | MV > 16h or hold p50 > 30h |
| Intake arrival CV / courier queue by hour | Spike > 2× mean at 10:00 or 17:00 | Intake queue > 3h |
| % cases with any station queue already > 12h (in-process) | > 35% | > 50% |
| Recollect / triage-fail rate (24h) | > 1.2× normal | > 1.5× normal |
| Policy state | — | Count of active P-* policies (compound if 2) |

**Lagging outcome metrics (daily review, not for intra-day steering)**  
- Urgent % released  18h; Routine %  72h; Complex-panel p90 cycle time  
- Overall rework % and Repeat-after-failure share  
- Cancel rate after recollection  
- Contractual penalty count  
- Median/p90 cycle time by case type (context only; do not optimize average alone)

**Cadence**  
Morning standup: leading board + active policy set. Mid-afternoon: Urgent age and machine repeats. End of day: lagging breaches and whether to declare/exit P-Compound. Weekly: cross-training floor compliance and partner-lab re-entry defects.

---

This redesign stays inside the candidate intervention set, uses peak vs normal DFG frequencies, queue tables, and variant behaviors to anticipate bottleneck migration, and refuses simultaneous max-priority for all case types. Acceptable performance means protecting Urgent contractual bounds, containing rework loops, and managing explicit Routine/Complex trade-offs under S5 — not restoring normal-period averages during winter waves.
