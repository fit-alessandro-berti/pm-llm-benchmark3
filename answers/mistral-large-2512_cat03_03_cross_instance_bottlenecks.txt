# **Object-Centric Analysis of Cross-Job Coupling in Manufacturing Delays**

## **1. Interaction-Graph Description**

### **Node Types**
- **Job (J1-J6):** Primary production orders.
- **Component (C1-C6):** Physical parts required by jobs.
- **ComponentLot (L7-L8):** Batches of components shared across jobs.
- **Machine (CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1):** Processing resources.
- **Tool (FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3):** Auxiliary resources required for processing.
- **Batch (B1-B2):** Grouping of jobs for shared processing.
- **Shipment (S1-S3):** Consolidation of jobs for dispatch.

### **Edge Types and Qualifiers**
| **Source** | **Qualifier** | **Target** | **Direct/Inferred** | **Notes** |
|------------|--------------|------------|---------------------|-----------|
| Job        | `requires`   | Component  | Direct              | Explicit dependency |
| Component  | `contained_in` | ComponentLot | Direct          | Lot membership |
| Job        | `batched_in` | Batch      | Direct              | Batch grouping |
| Batch      | `processed_on` | Machine   | Direct              | Machine usage |
| Batch      | `uses_tool`  | Tool       | Direct              | Tool requirement |
| Job        | `consolidated_in` | Shipment | Direct          | Shipment grouping |
| Job        | `waits_for`  | Tool       | Inferred            | Contention delays |
| Job        | `waits_for`  | ComponentLot | Inferred        | Lot release delays |
| Job        | `waits_for`  | Batch      | Inferred            | Batch synchronization |
| Job        | `waits_for`  | Shipment   | Inferred            | Consolidation delays |

### **Higher-Order Motifs**
1. **Batch-Tool-Machine Synchronization**
   - **Motif:** `(Job  Batch)  (Batch  Machine)  (Batch  Tool)`
   - **Example:** `J1, J2, J3  B1  OVEN-1  RACK-7`
   - **Effect:** Jobs wait for batch formation, which requires both a machine and a tool.

2. **ComponentLot-Component-Job Coupling**
   - **Motif:** `(ComponentLot  Component)  (Component  Job)  (Job  Batch)`
   - **Example:** `L7  C2  J2  B1`
   - **Effect:** Lot-level delays propagate to jobs via shared components.

3. **Tool-Machine-Job Contention**
   - **Motif:** `(Job  Tool)  (Tool  Machine)  (Machine  Job)`
   - **Example:** `J3  FIX-A  CNC-2  J4`
   - **Effect:** Tool unavailability blocks machine usage, delaying subsequent jobs.

## **2. Quantitative Delay Decomposition**

### **1. Batch Synchronization Delay (B1 on OVEN-1)**
- **Jobs:** J1, J2, J3
- **Ready Times:** J1 (08:10), J2 (08:25), J3 (11:55)
- **Batch Start:** 12:05
- **Waiting Time:**
  - J1: `12:05 - 08:10 = 3h 55m`
  - J2: `12:05 - 08:25 = 3h 40m`
  - J3: `12:05 - 11:55 = 10m`
- **Counterfactual:** If batching were not required, J1 and J2 could have started at their ready times.
- **Avoidable Waiting:** **3h 55m (J1), 3h 40m (J2)**

### **2. Tool Contention Delay (FIX-A for J4)**
- **J3 acquires FIX-A at 09:00, releases at 11:40.**
- **J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50.**
- **CNC-2 idle from 10:05–10:47 (42m).**
- **J4 starts processing at 11:45.**
- **Plausible Delay:**
  - If FIX-A were available at 09:50, J4 could have started at 10:05 (after setup).
  - Actual start: 11:45.
  - **Delay: 1h 40m**
- **Counterfactual:** No tool contention  J4 starts at 10:05.

### **3. Shipment Consolidation Delay (S1 for J1)**
- **J1 packed at 13:30.**
- **J4 packed at 19:10.**
- **S1 dispatches at 19:25.**
- **Waiting Time for J1:** `19:25 - 13:30 = 5h 55m`
- **Counterfactual:** If J1 could dispatch immediately after packing, no delay.
- **Consolidation-Induced Delay: 5h 55m**

### **4. Calibration Tool Delay (CAL-3 for J6)**
- **J5 tests from 16:10–16:35.**
- **CAL-3 recalibrated from 16:35–17:20.**
- **J6 tests from 17:25–17:50.**
- **J6 ready at 16:05.**
- **Plausible Delay:**
  - If CAL-3 were available immediately, J6 could have started at 16:35.
  - Actual start: 17:25.
  - **Delay: 50m**
- **Counterfactual:** No recalibration  J6 starts at 16:35.

### **5. ComponentLot Release Delay (L7 for J5)**
- **L7 quarantined at 10:20, released at 13:50.**
- **J5 ready at 14:40 (after release).**
- **B2 starts at 14:50.**
- **Plausible Delay:**
  - If L7 were released at 10:20, J5 could have been ready earlier.
  - **Minimum Delay: 3h 30m (10:20–13:50)**
- **Counterfactual:** No quarantine  J5 ready by 11:00 (assuming no other delays).

## **4. What Flattening Misses**

| **Viewpoint**      | **What It Shows**                                                                 | **What It Hides**                                                                 | **Misleading Conclusion**                                                                 |
|--------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Job**            | - Job-level process flow. <br> - Median activity times.                          | - Batch synchronization delays. <br> - Tool/lot dependencies. <br> - Shipment waits. | "No severe bottleneck" (hides cross-job coupling).                                      |
| **Machine**        | - Utilization rates. <br> - Activity sequences.                                   | - Why machines are idle (e.g., waiting for tools/batches). <br> - Job-level delays. | "Low utilization = no bottleneck" (ignores synchronization effects).                     |
| **Batch**          | - Batch processing times.                                                         | - Individual job waiting times. <br> - Tool/machine dependencies.                  | "Batches are efficient" (hides job-level delays).                                       |
| **Tool**           | - Tool usage patterns.                                                            | - Contention delays (no explicit "wait for tool" event). <br> - Impact on jobs.    | "Tools are underutilized" (ignores blocking effects).                                   |
| **ComponentLot**   | - Lot-level events (quarantine/release).                                          | - Job-level impact (inconsistent linking). <br> - Override policies.               | "Lot delays are rare" (misses propagation to jobs).                                     |
| **Shipment**       | - Shipment creation/loading/dispatch.                                             | - Job waiting times for consolidation. <br> - Early-packed job delays.             | "Shipments are fast" (hides internal waiting).                                          |

### **Why Low Machine Utilization Coexists with Long Job Waiting**
- **Synchronization Delays:** Machines wait for batches/tools, not because of capacity constraints.
- **Hidden Dependencies:** Flattened views ignore cross-job coupling (e.g., J1 waits for J4 in S1).
- **Policy-Induced Idleness:** Machines may be idle due to batching rules, not lack of demand.

## **5. Object-Lifecycle Evidence**

### **Narrative 1: Batch-Tool-Machine Synchronization (J1, J2, J3  B1  OVEN-1  RACK-7)**
- **Temporal Precedence:**
  - J1 ready at 08:10, J2 at 08:25, J3 at 11:55.
  - B1 starts at 12:05 (OVEN-1 + RACK-7).
- **Resource Feasibility:**
  - OVEN-1 available at 12:05.
  - RACK-7 available (no prior use recorded).
- **Policy Constraints:**
  - Batch policy requires all jobs to be ready before processing.
- **Causal Speculation:**
  - If batching were optional, J1/J2 could have started earlier.
  - RACK-7 availability may have been a limiting factor (not observed).

### **Narrative 2: Tool Contention and Machine Idleness (J3  FIX-A  CNC-2  J4)**
- **Temporal Precedence:**
  - J3 acquires FIX-A at 09:00, releases at 11:40.
  - J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50.
  - CNC-2 idle from 10:05–10:47.
  - J4 starts at 11:45.
- **Resource Feasibility:**
  - CNC-2 available but blocked by FIX-A.
- **Policy Constraints:**
  - No explicit "wait for tool" event  tool contention is inferred.
- **Causal Speculation:**
  - If FIX-A were released earlier, J4 could have started at 10:05.

### **Narrative 3: ComponentLot Release and Shipment Consolidation (L7  J2/J5  B1/B2  S1/S3)**
- **Temporal Precedence:**
  - L7 quarantined at 10:20, released at 13:50.
  - J2 proceeds via override (not delayed).
  - J5 ready at 14:40, B2 starts at 14:50.
  - J1 packed at 13:30, waits for J4 (packed at 19:10) in S1.
- **Resource Feasibility:**
  - L7 release is a hard constraint for J5.
  - S1 dispatch policy requires both J1 and J4.
- **Policy Constraints:**
  - Supervisor override bypasses lot delay for J2.
  - Shipment consolidation policy delays J1.
- **Causal Speculation:**
  - If L7 were released earlier, J5 could have started sooner.
  - If J1 could dispatch independently, no delay.

    "event_start_timestamp",
    "event_end_timestamp",
    "event_objects",
    "event_activity"
])

# Calculate waiting times between job readiness and batch start
batch_waiting_times = {}
for batch in ocel.objects["Batch"].unique():
    batch_events = ocel.events[ocel.events["event_objects"].apply(lambda x: batch in x)]
    if len(batch_events) > 0:
        batch_start = batch_events["event_start_timestamp"].min()
        for job in ocel.relations[ocel.relations["ocel:qualifier"] == "batches"]["ocel:object2"].unique():
            if job in ocel.relations[ocel.relations["ocel:object2"] == job]["ocel:object1"].values:
                job_ready = ocel.events[
                    (ocel.events["event_objects"].apply(lambda x: job in x)) &
                    (ocel.events["event_activity"] == "Ready for batch")
                ]["event_start_timestamp"]
                if len(job_ready) > 0:
                    waiting_time = (batch_start - job_ready.iloc[0]).total_seconds() / 3600
                    batch_waiting_times[(job, batch)] = waiting_time

print("\nBatch Waiting Times (hours):")
print(batch_waiting_times)

## **8. Falsification and Additional Data**

### **Observations That Would Weaken Top-Ranked Explanation (Batch Synchronization)**
1. **No correlation between batch size and waiting time** (e.g., small batches also have long delays).
2. **OVEN-1 utilization spikes during batch processing** (suggesting capacity, not synchronization, is the issue).
3. **RACK-7 is unavailable for most of the batch formation period** (tool, not batch, is the bottleneck).
4. **Jobs in the same batch have highly variable processing times** (suggesting machine variability, not batching).
5. **Supervisor overrides frequently bypass batching rules** (suggesting batching is not strictly enforced).
6. **ComponentLot delays affect jobs in the same batch differently** (suggesting lot, not batch, is the issue).

### **Additional Data to Discriminate Among Hypotheses**
1. **Tool acquisition/release timestamps** (to confirm contention).
2. **Batch formation rules** (e.g., minimum/maximum batch size).
3. **Shipment dispatch policies** (e.g., minimum jobs per shipment).
4. **ComponentLot quarantine reasons** (e.g., quality vs. administrative).
5. **Machine setup/cleanup times** (to separate processing from idle time).
6. **Supervisor override logs** (to quantify policy bypasses).
7. **Tool calibration schedules** (to assess CAL-3 availability).
8. **Job priority rules** (to understand sequencing decisions).
9. **Real-time machine status logs** (to distinguish idle vs. blocked).
10. **Component demand forecasts** (to assess lot sizing logic).

---

## **Conclusion**
The analysis reveals that **batch synchronization, shipment consolidation, and shared-tool contention** are the primary drivers of otherwise hidden cross-job coupling. Traditional flattenings obscure these dependencies by focusing on isolated views (e.g., machine utilization, job-level DFGs). Interventions should target **dynamic batching, tool reservation, and shipment policies**, while improving logging to expose latent dependencies. The object-centric approach is essential to diagnose delays that flattenings cannot capture.
