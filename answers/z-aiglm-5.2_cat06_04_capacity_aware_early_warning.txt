## 4. Repeated Alerts and Temporal Policy

**Cool-down:** After any alert (intervened or not), a 60-minute cool-down suppresses new alerts for the same case unless a qualifying material event occurs. This prevents the 30-minute refresh cycle from generating alert fatigue (the I-05 pattern).

**Suppression rule:** On the third or later alert within a single case lifecycle, suppress automatically unless a materially new event is logged (as defined in Section 1). Suppressed alerts are recorded but do not trigger swarm review.

**Escalation:** If a case has been alerted  3 times, is still eligible, and **has** had a material event, it is flagged for manual review by a shift lead rather than automatic REV scoring. This is because repeated alerts with new events may indicate a complex case where the model's effect estimate is especially unreliable.

**Re-entry:** A suppressed case re-enters the active queue when: (a) a material event triggers a new alert; (b) the case transitions stage (e.g., Triage  Diagnosis Started); or (c) the cool-down expires and `p_now` has increased by > 0.08 absolute since the last reviewed alert.

## 5. Fairness and Feedback-Loop Risks

### 5.1 Value-based prioritization starving lower-value cases

The REV formula inherently favors high-value cases. I-07 (€5k value) and I-05 (€7k) are deprioritized even though their adjusted breach probabilities are high. Over time, if low-value segments are systematically untreated, their breach rates will rise, damaging customer relationships in segments where lifetime value may exceed the per-incident value.

**Safeguards:**
- **Segment-level minimum intervention rate**: Track the fraction of alerted cases receiving swarms per segment per week. If any segment's intervention rate falls below 15% of its alert rate for two consecutive weeks, escalate to the policy review board.
- **Floor on urgency overrides**: Cases with < 2 h to deadline and p_adj > 0.70 receive a manual review regardless of value, even if REV is low. This prevents "letting low-value cases burn" simply because the formula says so.
- **Lifetime-value adjustment**: For repeat-customer segments, apply a 1.3× multiplier to the stated breach-avoidance value to approximate retention effects.

### 5.2 Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support

I-09 is in a sparse-calibration segment. Both the predicted probability and the intervention-effect estimate are unreliable. Intervening based on uncertain estimates risks both false alarms (customer disruption in a potentially sensitive cross-language context) and wasted slots.

**Safeguards:**
- **Widened decision interval**: For Non-Native-Language cases, require REV > €1,500 at the lower bound (vs. > €0 for other segments) before auto-approving. I-09's adjusted REV (€1,645) clears this bar, but barely.
- **Outcome flagging**: All Non-Native-Language interventions are tagged for manual outcome review within 7 days to build calibration data.
- **Consent check**: Before swarming, confirm the customer's language preference is available; if the swarm specialists cannot communicate in the customer's preferred language, the case is deferred.

### 5.3 Differential calibration across segments

SME predictions are systematically too low; Strategic Enterprise predictions are too high. If uncorrected, this causes both under-intervention (SME cases not flagged) and over-intervention (SE cases consuming slots unnecessarily). The current rule applies midpoint corrections, but these are themselves uncertain.

**Safeguards:**
- **Rolling recalibration**: Recompute segment bias estimates weekly using the last 4 weeks of outcome data. If the bias estimate for any segment moves by > 0.03 from the previous week, trigger an ad hoc recalibration review.
- **Conservative band**: Instead of a point correction, use the full bias range as a sensitivity band. For SE cases, compute REV with both the uncorrected and corrected p_adj; if the intervention decision flips, flag the case for manual review. I-04's REV is positive under both (uncorrected p = 0.48; corrected p = 0.395), so it is robust. I-08 flips (positive under uncorrected, negative under corrected), which supports excluding it.

### 5.4 Selective intervention changing future training labels

When we intervene on high-risk cases, some breaches are prevented. Future models trained on this data will see "no breach" for cases that would have breached without intervention, making the model underpredict risk for similar cases. This is a classic selection-bias / label-corruption problem.

**Safeguards:**
- **Treatment flagging**: Every swarmed case is tagged with a binary `swarmed` feature and the estimated p. The training pipeline must either (a) exclude swarmed cases from the breach-prediction training set, or (b) use inverse propensity weighting to correct for the intervention effect.
- **Shadow scoring**: Maintain a "no-intervention counterfactual" model that is trained only on un-intervened cases. Compare its predictions to the production model monthly; if they diverge by > 0.10 average absolute error on a held-out set, the production model is drifting due to treatment effects.
- **Effect-heterogeneity logging**: Record post-hoc whether each swarmed case actually breached and estimate the realized p. Feed this into a separate causal-effect model (see Section 6).

### 5.5 Spillover harms falling on untreated groups

Spillover delays affect cases sharing the diagnosis queue. If the queue is disproportionately composed of certain segments (e.g., SME cases tend to be queued longer), then spillover harms are systematically borne by those segments. This creates a hidden transfer from low-value/low-priority cases to high-value intervened cases.

**Safeguards:**
- **Spillover audit**: Log the segment composition of the diagnosis queue at each intervention time. If > 60% of spillover-affected cases are from a single segment for 3 consecutive intervention days, flag for review.
- **Spillover budget**: Set a weekly cap on aggregate spillover probability mass (e.g., total spillover p across all untreated cases must not exceed 1.5 expected additional breaches per week). If exceeded, reduce slot usage or expand specialist capacity.
- **Equity-adjusted REV**: In future iterations, subtract an equity penalty proportional to the concentration of spillover on any single segment: `penalty = 0.5 × max_segment_share × total_spillover_harm`.

### 6.3 Causal-effect estimation

The supplied p ranges are observational estimates, not causal effects. To estimate the true intervention effect:

1. **Encourage natural variation**: When slot capacity is not fully consumed (e.g., only 2 of 4 slots used on a given day), record the cases that were eligible but not swarmed as a "deferred" comparison group. These cases have similar risk profiles but no intervention, providing a quasi-control.
2. **Difference-in-differences**: Compare breach rates between swarmed and deferred cases, adjusted for `p_adj`, segment, and stage. Run weekly with at least 30 swarmed and 30 deferred cases for a meaningful estimate.
3. **Stratified effect estimation**: Estimate p separately by segment, stage at intervention, and time-to-deadline bucket. If any stratum's estimated effect falls below the lower bound of the supplied range, update that stratum's range.
4. **Instrumental variable (if feasible)**: Use slot availability (number of remaining slots at the time of alert) as an instrument for intervention probability. High-availability cases are more likely to be swarmed; if slot availability is plausibly uncorrelated with case severity (conditional on `p_adj`), this provides a cleaner causal estimate.

### 6.6 Decision-rule change protocol

The threshold and allocation rule should be revised when **any** of the following occur:
1. The adverse-scenario net impact (Section 3) is realized for 2 consecutive weeks  raise the REV minimum or reduce default slot count.
2. A segment's calibration bias shifts by > 0.03  update adjustment factors.
3. The causal-effect estimate for any stratum falls outside the supplied p range  update the range used in REV.
4. The spillover budget is exceeded  either reduce slot count or raise the REV minimum.
5. A regulatory or contractual change alters the breach definition  full recalibration and rule review.

Changes are staged: first adjust calibration factors, then REV modifiers, then slot-count defaults. Each change is logged with a rationale and a rollback trigger (e.g., if post-change Brier score worsens by > 0.02 within 2 weeks, revert).
