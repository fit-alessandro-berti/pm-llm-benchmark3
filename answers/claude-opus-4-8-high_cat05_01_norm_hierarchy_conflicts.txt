## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Method and burden of proof.** I assess each case by applying the applicable norms *at the time of each relevant act*, not at case completion. Compliance is a property of the sequence of decisions as they occurred, so a control that must precede commitment cannot be "cured" by a later event; it can only be *mitigated*. The party asserting that an emergency or directive waived an ordinary control bears the burden of showing (a) that the waiver was within the issuer's authority, (b) that the case fell within the directive's scope and time window, and (c) that the specific control was actually displaced rather than merely unmentioned. Silence in a directive is *not* a waiver.

**Treatment of source authority (not a single ladder).** I do not assume that the global, newest, or most strongly worded document automatically wins. Instead I use *issue-specific* precedence:
- On **segregation of duties (SoD)** and **Compliance clearance for new suppliers / high thresholds**, GPIP-4 is the controlling integrity floor because ECD-2 and the CFO email are silent on these, and GPIP-4 (A4) preserves non-tender controls absent a valid A7 exception.
- On **purchasing *timing* (PO vs commitment) and *supplier onboarding***, ECD-2 is more specific and later-in-time and expressly claims precedence (C1, C2, C4), so within its scope/window it can override B1 and B3.
- On **Finance controls**, the CFO has explicit interpretive authority (D3), so the CFO email can restructure *Finance* approval mechanics — but D3 and D4 both disclaim authority to waive Compliance or SoD.

**Specificity vs. generality.** A specific, scoped, time-limited emergency rule can override a general ordinary-workflow rule *on the dimension it addresses*. It does not thereby override integrity controls it never mentions.

**Later-in-time.** Later-in-time only breaks ties between instruments of comparable authority and overlapping scope. A late ordinary email (D) does not outrank a board policy (A) on matters A reserves.

**Emergency necessity.** Necessity can justify deviation from *timing/tendering/onboarding* controls but does not by itself dissolve SoD or Compliance. Necessity claims must be tested against each directive's own emergency definition (B4 six-hour test; ECD-2 scope).

**Retrospective evidence.** Late recording of a *timely* act is a logging matter, not a substantive breach, when stronger contemporaneous evidence (phone recordings, signed PDFs, supplier acknowledgments, access logs) fixes the real time. Per ON-44, ERP timestamps may lag reality by up to 12 minutes and are not conclusive against better contemporaneous evidence. The outage changes *evidential weight only*; it is not a normative exception.

**A7 exception question (assumption, flagged).** GPIP-4 (A7) reserves formal exceptions to the CRO and does not say a COO+Incident-Commander directive counts. The CRO was unavailable and never ratified ECD-2. I therefore *assume* ECD-2 is **not** a valid A7 exception to GPIP-4's reserved integrity controls (SoD, Compliance, thresholds). It functions as a valid *local operational overlay* for timing/onboarding (consistent with prior practice of treating signed directives as temporary overlays), but *not* as a waiver of A-reserved controls. This assumption is decisive for several cases and I mark where reversing it changes the outcome.

**Fact / interpretation / assumption / recommendation** are separated throughout: case timings are facts; precedence choices are interpretations; the A7 and scope readings are assumptions; Section 6–7 are recommendations.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-503 (NONCOMPLIANT → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION).** Premise that must change: that ECD-2 does not touch SoD/Compliance. If one reads ECD-2's C4 "takes precedence over ordinary purchasing… requirements" broadly, and treats IC authorizers as de facto budget owners (charter silent), then a single-signer overlap might be tolerated. *Weak*, because A1 is a core integrity control, C5 confirms ECD-2 never mentions SoD, and a 3-day stock buffer undercuts necessity. The alternative requires both an authority stretch and a scope stretch simultaneously.

**P-505 (INDETERMINATE → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION).** Premise: the ERP clock was 9 minutes fast and contemporaneous evidence (call 17:57, ack 17:59:31) governs. Under ON-44, contemporaneous evidence outranks ERP, so the real commit is pre-18:00 and inside the window. This is *credible*; it would make the timing valid, leaving only a post-commit Finance defect (mitigable). The counter-premise (ERP is authoritative) is *weak* given ON-44's explicit warning.

**P-507 (NONCOMPLIANT → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION as primary).** Premise: that A2's Finance requirement is satisfied constructively by IC authorization under CFO email D1 (IC sufficient ≤€150k). If D1 validly restructures *Finance* mechanics (D3 gives the CFO Finance authority), then the €110k IC authorization could count as the Finance leg. *Moderately credible* for the Finance leg only; still questionable because A2 wants an *independent* budget owner and Finance Controller, and D4 warns against removing essential controls. This would flip primary to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.

**P-501 (NONCOMPLIANT → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION).** Premise: the four-hour predicted stoppage satisfies B4 (imminent ≤6h), and a delegation of SHIFT-1 existed but was informal. If a pre-09:00 signed delegation surfaces, B4 substitution holds and commit-before-PO is locally permitted for a sub-threshold purchase. *Weak on current facts* (PM reachable and expressly did not delegate), but a single evidentiary document would reverse it.

**P-508 (JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION → NONCOMPLIANT).** Premise: A2 dual Finance control is non-negotiable and unmet. If the judge treats the absent Finance Controller/budget-owner approval as a hard integrity breach (not a curable defect), the strong Compliance/Legal/SoD posture cannot rescue it, flipping to NONCOMPLIANT. *Credible* under a strict A2 reading.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

**Taxonomy applied:**
- **Policy breach (substantive):** P-503 (SoD + missing Compliance/Finance), P-504 (scope + unapproved supplier + no auth), P-506 (out-of-window, no valid extension). These are real control failures, not evidence problems.
- **Logging defect (timely act, late record):** P-507 ERP auth 14:30 vs signed PDF 12:40 (document-system outage); P-502 joint-auth recording. Remediation: attach contemporaneous artifacts to the record; no substantive breach for the timing itself.
- **Late evidence of a timely act:** P-505 commit — supplier ack 17:59:31 and call 17:57 likely prove pre-expiry action despite ERP 18:06. Remediation: obtain certified clock-offset report to convert INDETERMINATE → determinate.
- **Inability to prove compliance:** P-505 (window edge) and P-506 (alleged verbal extension) — the missing artifact is dispositive.

**Immediate case-level remediation:**
- P-503: retroactive independent Compliance and Finance review; document SoD failure; require independent ratification or unwind if the pumps were non-critical (3-day stock).
- P-504: treat as out-of-scope ordinary purchase; obtain approved-supplier exception or re-source; corrective action for SHIFT-2.
- P-505: pull ERP time-sync logs; if clock 9 min fast, record commit as pre-expiry and back-fill the missing pre-commit Finance rationale.
- P-506: obtain any written ECD-2 extension; absent it, process as post-emergency ordinary purchase and validate A2 approvals were pre-commit (they were not — Finance 06:05 before commit 06:20 is actually pre-commit; the defect is reliance on lapsed emergency timing, so confirm ordinary rules were met).
- P-507 / P-508: secure the missing Finance Controller/budget-owner sign-off; log signed-PDF provenance for P-507.

**Monitoring:** track (a) % emergency buys lacking pre-commit Compliance where required, (b) SoD overlaps in IC-authorized buys, (c) commitments within ±15 min of window edges, (d) ERP-vs-contemporaneous timestamp deltas.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Emergency Procurement Precedence Clause (replacement).**

1. **Authority.** An incident directive jointly signed by the COO and Incident Commander is a *time-limited operational overlay*. It may override ordinary *timing* (PO-before-commit) and *supplier-onboarding* steps within its stated scope and window. It does **not** waive segregation of duties (A1), dual Finance control (A2), or Compliance clearance (A3) unless the Chief Risk Officer issues a written A7 exception naming those controls. If the CRO is unavailable, a designated alternate must ratify within 24 hours or the overlay lapses to ordinary rules.
2. **Scope.** "Critical" purchases must be defined by an objective test: components required within the next 24 hours to sustain or restore an *affected* sterilization/production line. Backup/contingency stock with >24h buffer is excluded unless expressly designated by two IC members with recorded justification.
3. **Thresholds & SoD (non-waivable floor).** For any purchase ≥ €50k: an independent budget owner and Finance Controller must approve before commitment; the requester/committer may never be an authorizer. IC members are **not** automatically budget owners.
4. **New suppliers.** New-supplier purchases always require Compliance_Clearance *before* commitment, even in emergencies; onboarding waivers (C2) suspend register-membership only, not Compliance vetting.
5. **Retrospective records.** A timely act supported by contemporaneous evidence (recorded call, signed PDF, supplier acknowledgment, access log) is deemed timely even if the ERP record is late; ERP timestamps are rebuttable by stronger contemporaneous evidence. Late approval mitigates but never cures a missing pre-commitment control (A6 restated).
6. **Expiry.** Overlays end at the stated time; extensions require a written, jointly signed record before the original expiry. No verbal extensions.
7. **Anti-normalization safeguard.** Each emergency-overlay purchase triggers a mandatory 5-business-day post-hoc integrity review; a site exceeding a set rate of overlay purchases per quarter, or any SoD/Compliance bypass, escalates automatically to the CRO and Audit, and repeat reliance on the same overlay for non-time-critical buys is prohibited.
