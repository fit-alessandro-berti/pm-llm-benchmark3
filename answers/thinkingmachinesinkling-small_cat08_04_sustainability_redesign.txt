5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. Batching rebound (energy vs waiting / obsolescence): Larger batches lower per-item energy (CNC_A 2.81.3 kWh) but increase batch wait (4h38h) and heat-treatment late risk (1.7×). If caps (S2) are too loose, waiting dominates; if too tight, energy savings are lost. Also, 6% of batch items become obsolete if forecasts are wrong—displacing operational savings with waste.

2. Transport displacement (consolidation vs speed): Consolidated trucks cut emissions 35–55% but add 12–24h. If applied to Service-contract orders without slack checks, late delivery rises; if customers then upgrade to air, total system emissions rebound.

3. Rework displacement (machine switch vs queue): Shifting MTO to CNC_A reduces rework (9% vs 18%) but may congest CNC_A. Without S2 caps, orders queue and miss deadlines, causing cancellation (410 cases) or emergency production—displacing quality gains into service failure and high-energy rush paths.

4. Packaging rebound (reinforced vs material / damage): Reinforced packaging doubles material and is required for >8 kg (Q1). If packaging-route checks (S6) are too strict and force reinforced for all, material waste rises; if too lenient, damage returns increase, causing second shipments (partial shipment path: 7.2 kgCO2e, 24% late) and hidden transport emissions.

5. Obsolescence / forecasting rebound: Dynamic batching relies on demand visibility. If batch sizes remain large to chase per-item energy savings but forecasts miss, produced items sit unshipped (>14 days). This displaces energy savings into scrap/obsolete inventory, especially in MTO where demand is less regular.

6. Customer / contract behavior rebound: If green shipping (S4) adds even small delays to Standard-stock Service-contract, some customers may upgrade to Critical or demand dedicated vans, increasing total transport emissions and eroding cost/revenue balance.

7. Dedicated-van / emergency rebound: Replacing vans with shared lanes (S8) saves emissions, but if shared capacity is insufficient during disruption, true Critical downtime fails (resilience loss). Also, splitting Critical (S9) requires correct classification; misclassification sends precautionary orders into low-emission but late paths, damaging service.

8. Partial-shipment / availability rebound: Tightening availability rules (S11) reduces second shipments but may increase backorder waits (220 cases) or cancellations; if customers respond by splitting orders or switching to faster channels, system emissions may not fall.

6. Balanced recommendation

Recommended redesign package: “Segment-differentiated service-protected green operations”

Component A — Standard-stock (39% Regular / 16% Service-contract): Deploy S1 (green recommender) + S4 (priority pick + ground instead of default air for Service-contract when slack >85%) + S5 (twice-daily consolidated trucks for non-urgent orders) + S12 (exception log). Rationale: Lowest-impact segment but high transport-mode leverage (air 18.5 kgCO2e, SP1 22% violation). Avoid eliminating air entirely—preserve it for true tight deadlines and maintain ground as alternative (R1). Consolidation requires backup parcel option to prevent resilience loss.

Component B — Made-to-order (22% Regular / 12% Service-contract): Deploy S2 (dynamic batching caps: Service max 24h, Regular max 48h; force small/instant if cap reached) + S3 (route to CNC_A when queue/slack allows) + S7 (inspection quality gate for high-rework combos) + S6 (packaging-route compatibility before shipment) + optional S10 (carbon-aware promise negotiation for Regular). Rationale: Largest combined footprint (18.5–20 kWh, 0.85–0.90 scrap, 10–11% rework). Batching must be optimized but capped to prevent 160h/32% late paths. CNC_A shift cuts hidden scrap; packaging and inspection fixes close rework loops. Promise negotiation allows larger batches without forcing rushed production.

Component C — Critical-downtime (11% Service-contract): Deploy S9 (true vs precautionary split) + S8 (shared emergency courier lanes; require SP2 justification event; keep dedicated only for true with justification). Rationale: Very high emissions (38 kgCO2e, 620 dedicated vans, 31% missing justification). Do not cut service for true downtime—protect it with shared lanes and explicit justification. Reduce precautionary overuse of emergency production and empty-return vans.

Component D — Governance and resilience backbone: Deploy S12 (exception log) + conformance monitoring for SP1/SP2/Q1 + maintain at least one alternative route per Service-contract order (R1). Rationale: Low DFG precision and complex emergency/partial paths mean hidden overrides are likely. Logging prevents displacement of environmental gains into unjustified air/van use.

Why this balances:
- Service: Critical true downtime protected; Service-contract stock gets faster priority pick rather than arbitrary air; MTO caps prevent 32% late paths; promise negotiation accepts reasonable delays for Regular.
- Quality: Inspection gate + packaging check + CNC_A shift directly address hidden scrap and rework loops (rework_flag, material_scrap_kg, packaging rework).
- Resilience: Alternative routes preserved (ground always available; shared lanes replace some vans but do not eliminate; backup parcel for consolidation); exception log ensures disruptions are visible rather than hidden in model noise.
- Environment: Largest gains come from transport mode (air/dedicated van reduction) and material/rework reduction—not from pushing lowest-emission options that violate service or resilience.

7. Validation and governance plan

Validation using event-log evidence after implementation:

Baseline and rollout
- Lock pre-implementation 12,000-order summary (segment-level median/p90, late %, energy, transport, scrap, rework, batch wait, conformance) as reference.
- Implement in phases: first S1/S4/S5 for stock; S2/S3/S6/S7 for MTO; S8/S9 for Critical; S12 globally.

Segment-level service monitoring (rolling 90-day event log)
- For each case_type × customer_type, compute: median / p90 cycle time; late delivery % (Confirm delivery timestamp vs promised_lead_time_hours); partial shipment %; cancellation %; backorder wait rate.
- Monitor for deterioration: if Standard-stock Service-contract median exceeds ~30h or late delivery exceeds 8%, trigger S4 adjustment; if MTO Service-contract late delivery exceeds 15%, tighten S2 caps.

Path-level process tracking (DFG edges)
- Track frequency of key paths: Stock  ground  Confirm; MTO  batch  CNC_A  heat  inspection  reinforced  ground; Critical  shared/lanes / dedicated with justification.
- Watch for new abnormal paths (e.g., batch wait  cancellation spike; emergency production increase due to S9 misclassification).

Conformance monitoring (SP1, SP2, Q1)
- SP1: For orders where promised lead time allows ground/consolidated with >85% historical reliability, measure % using parcel_air / dedicated_van incorrectly. Target >95% conformance (reduce 22% violation).
- SP2: For dedicated_van routes, check presence of justification event. Target 100% with justification; if missing, trigger exception log (S12).

Rebound / displacement monitoring

Resilience audit
