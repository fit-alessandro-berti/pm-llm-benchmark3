## 1. Assumption ledger

Reference origin for all "remaining hours" figures: Friday 18 Sep 2026, 15:30. Useful landmarks: Mon 21 Sep 08:00 = +64.5 h; Mon 12:00 = +68.5 h; Mon 20:00 = +76.5 h; Tue 22 Sep 08:00 = +88.5 h; Wed 23 Sep 12:00 = +116.5 h; Thu 24 Sep 12:00 = +140.5 h; Fri 25 Sep 12:00 = +164.5 h; Mon 28 Sep 12:00 = +236.5 h.

| Assumption | Chosen interpretation | Why it is defensible | Sensitivity if wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Join semantics | QA start = max(chemistry usable result, microbiology usable result), not the sum of branch durations | Rule 7 of the normative model states the join is governed by the slower branch | Using a sum would inflate the forecast by roughly 25–40 h; this is the single largest modelling error available |
| Incubation readiness window | Fri 14:00 + 46–54 h ⇒ **Sun 20 Sep 12:00 – 20:00**; the whole readiness window falls on Sunday | Direct arithmetic on the given band; no part of it reaches Monday | If incubation ran long (>58 h) readiness slips into Monday morning and micro becomes the binding clock again by 1–3 h |
| Weekend readout | Without on-call authorization, readout starts **Mon 08:00**, taking 0.5–1.5 h ⇒ micro usable **Mon 08:30–09:30** | Routine microbiology staffing is Mon–Fri 08:00–18:00; Sunday readiness cannot be read | If ad-hoc weekend reading is more common than stated, the smooth path is unchanged anyway (see next row) |
| Value of on-call authorization (p = 0.25–0.45) | On the **smooth** path it changes almost nothing, because QA does not work Sunday and cannot start before Mon 08:00 regardless | QA calendar: Mon–Fri 08:00–20:00 + Sat 09:00–13:00, no Sunday | Its real value is conditional: on a contamination path it starts the 20–30 h confirmation ~14–18 h earlier, moving that tail from Wed to Tue |
| Chemistry Friday completion | Treated as **essentially impossible** (<5%) under either queue discipline | Best case B-417 starts ~16:00–16:30 with only 1.5–2.0 h of lab left; P(triangular(1.5, 2.5, 4.5) ≤ 1.75 h) ≈ 0.02 | If overtime were authorized, chemistry could finish Friday ~19:00–21:00, but the join would still wait for micro on Monday — no effect on the terminal time |
| Queue discipline mixture | FIFO 55% ⇒ B-417 is 5th; risk-priority 45% ⇒ B-417 is 2nd (behind the urgent sample, ahead of the three ordinary ones) | "High priority but not critical" ranks below urgent, above ordinary | Discipline shifts the chemistry finish by ~3–5 h on Monday and decides *whether chemistry or microbiology is the binding clock* |
| Friday partial work carries over | Whatever service B-417 receives before 18:00 is preserved and resumes Monday 08:00 (no re-start penalty) | Stated as pause/resume, not abort | If restart were required, add 1–2 h to the priority-discipline branch only |
| QA service distribution | Median 1.5 h, P80 3.5 h ⇒ right-skewed (lognormal-like), mean ≈ 2.0–2.3 h | Fits both quantiles; a symmetric fit would contradict P80 | A heavier tail adds 1–3 h to QA congestion clearance, mostly inside the noise |
| QA backlog at the join | 5 now, plus ~3 arrivals during Fri 15:30–20:00 and Sat 09:00–13:00 (0.35/h × 8.5 h ≈ 3, overdispersed); ~4–5 cleared by the single Fri/Sat reviewer ⇒ **3–5 waiting Monday 08:00**, two reviewers ⇒ backlog clears ~Mon 11:00–13:00 | Arrivals only accrue in opening hours; weekend service capacity is real but thin | Overdispersion is the risk: a burst leaves 7–8 on Monday, pushing B-417's QA start to ~Mon 14:00–16:00 (+3 h) |
| Temperature excursion as a latent common factor | Not an independent event: I use a **two-world mixture** — World A "no excursion" (p ≈ 0.70): rerun 0.10, contamination 0.05, investigation 0.04–0.10; World B "excursion confirmed" (p ≈ 0.30): rerun ~0.20–0.30, contamination ~0.15–0.20, investigation 0.30–0.55 | The scenario explicitly forbids assuming independence; a shared latent cause is the simplest defensible coupling | Assuming independence would understate the joint tail (rerun *and* contamination *and* investigation together) by roughly a factor of 2–3 |
| Terminal event | Electronic Release in ~92–96% of paths; Batch Rejected ~3–6%, essentially only via confirmed contamination or a confirmed critical deviation | Rejection requires a *confirmed* critical defect, which needs the confirmation incubation or a lab investigation first | If excursion confirmation implies a critical defect more directly, rejection probability roughly doubles |
| Arithmetic shortcut | Working-hour offsets computed by hand-rolling the calendar (lab 10 h/day Mon–Fri; QA 12 h/day Mon–Fri + 4 h Sat); quantiles read from scenario envelopes, not from a Monte Carlo run | The task permits approximate arithmetic and scenario envelopes | Point values could be off by ±2–3 h; band widths are the honest output |

Overlaps, stated explicitly: S5 and S6/S7 are *modifiers* that can attach to any of S1–S4, so the bands are not mutually exclusive and should not be summed to 100%. S3 and S4 can co-occur (a temperature excursion raises both), and in World B their joint probability is materially higher than the product of the marginals. Roughly: P(no anomaly at all, i.e. pure S1∪S2) ≈ 0.45–0.60; P(at least one query/investigation) ≈ 0.25–0.40; P(at least one lab-side anomaly, rerun or contamination) ≈ 0.20–0.30.

## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

**Common to all scenarios — the two branch clocks from Friday 15:30**

*Chemistry, Friday afternoon.* Two analysts, busy for 0.4–0.9 h and 1.2–2.0 h. First free slot ≈ 15:55–16:25, second ≈ 16:45–17:30. Ahead of B-417 sit one urgent and three ordinary samples.
- Under **risk-priority (45%)** B-417 is second in line and can take the first free analyst at ~16:00–16:25. Service is triangular(1.5, 2.5, 4.5). To finish by 18:00 it would need ≤ ~1.6–2.0 h, i.e. P ≈ 0.02–0.06. So it almost always receives 1.6–2.0 h of partial service and is **paused at 18:00**, resuming Mon 08:00 with ~0.5–2.9 h left (central ~0.9 h) ⇒ chemistry usable **Mon 08:30–11:00**, central ~09:30.
- Under **FIFO (55%)** the urgent plus three ordinary samples come first. Friday's remaining ~2.0 h of two-analyst capacity (~4 analyst-hours) starts at most two of them and finishes none. Monday from 08:00 two analysts must clear roughly three to four predecessors at ~2.5 h each ⇒ B-417 starts ~Mon 10:30–12:30 and finishes **Mon 13:00–16:30**, central ~14:30.

*Microbiology.* Incubation started Fri 14:00; technically ready Fri 14:00 + 46–54 h = **Sun 12:00–20:00** — entirely inside the Sunday gap.
- Without on-call (p ≈ 0.55–0.75): readout begins **Mon 08:00**, 0.5–1.5 h ⇒ usable **Mon 08:30–09:30**.
- With on-call (p ≈ 0.25–0.45): dispatch 1–3 h + readout 0.5–1.5 h ⇒ usable **Sun 14:00 – Mon 00:30**. Because QA has no Sunday shift, this does **not** advance the smooth-path terminal time at all; its only value is (a) knowing the result 12–18 h earlier for planning and (b) starting a contamination confirmation on Sunday afternoon instead of Monday morning.

*Where a maximum replaces a sum.* Join time = max(chemistry, microbiology), never the sum. Chemistry consumes ~4–7 h of lab time and microbiology ~48 h of incubation, but they run concurrently, so the ~48 h incubation is not additive with chemistry; likewise the ~16.5 h Friday-evening pause and the ~48 h weekend pause are shared by both branches, so they are counted once, not twice.

---

**S1 — Smooth, risk-priority chemistry**
- Chemistry: wait ~0.5 h Friday + ~1.7 h service Friday, pause 18:00 → Mon 08:00, ~0.9 h residual ⇒ ready **Mon ~09:00** (range 08:30–11:00).
- Microbiology: 46–54 h incubation → Sun; readout Mon 08:00–09:30 ⇒ ready **Mon ~09:00**.
- **Join = max ≈ Mon 09:00–11:00.** The two clocks are nearly tied here, which is why small perturbations flip which branch binds.
- QA: 3–5 backlog batches at Mon 08:00, two reviewers, mean service ~2.1 h ⇒ backlog clears ~Mon 11:00–13:00. B-417 waits ~0.5–2 h, then service 1.0–2.5 h (median 1.5) ⇒ QA accept **Mon 12:15–15:00**.
- No rework.
- Electronic Release 0.25–0.75 h ⇒ **Mon 21 Sep 12:30–15:30 (+69 to +72 h)**; optimistic edge Mon 11:00 (+67.5 h), pessimistic edge Mon 18:00–20:00 (+74.5 to +76.5 h) if QA arrivals burst.

**S2 — Smooth, FIFO chemistry**
- Chemistry is the binding clock: ready **Mon 13:00–16:30**.
- Microbiology ready Mon ~09:00 and then simply waits at the join — idle branch time, not additive.
- **Join = max = chemistry ≈ Mon 14:30.** By then the Monday QA backlog has largely drained, so QA wait falls to ~0–1 h; QA service 1.0–2.5 h ⇒ accept **Mon 15:30–18:30**, release **Mon 15:45–19:00 (+72 to +75.5 h)**. Note the partial offset: a later join buys a shorter QA queue.
- Tail: if chemistry slips past ~Mon 18:00 (long service plus a crowded queue), QA's 20:00 close can push acceptance to **Tue 09:00–12:00 (+89.5 to +92.5 h)**.

**S3 — Chemistry rerun**
- Base chemistry finishes Mon 09:00–14:30 as in S1/S2; the borderline flag then triggers a priority rerun of 1.0–3.0 h that **cannot cross 18:00**.
- If the rerun starts before ~15:00 it completes **Mon 13:00–17:30**; if the base result lands after ~16:00, part of the rerun spills to **Tue 08:00–10:00**.
- **Join = chemistry** unambiguously; microbiology waits ~4–24 h at the join.
- QA: Monday-evening arrival ⇒ light queue, service 1.0–2.5 h ⇒ release **Mon 18:30 – Tue 12:00 (+75 to +92.5 h)**.
- Coupling: a rerun raises the deviation-investigation probability to 0.30–0.55, so ~1/3 of S3 mass actually terminates inside S6/S7, at **Tue 16:00 – Wed 23 Sep 12:00 (+96.5 to +116.5 h)** or later.

**S4 — Delayed microbiology readout / contamination confirmation**
- *Late-readout sub-case:* readiness at the 54 h edge (Sun 20:00) with no on-call still means Mon 08:00 readout — so it is only harmful if incubation exceeds ~58 h and lands after Mon 08:00; then readout is Mon 09:00–11:00 and the effect is a 1–3 h shift, not a new day.
- *Contamination sub-case (the material one):* indication read Mon ~09:00, then **20–30 h of continuous incubation** (calendar time, no calendar pause — incubation runs continuously) ⇒ technically confirmed **Tue 05:00–15:00**; second readout 0.5–1.5 h needs a microbiologist, so confirmed result **Tue 08:30 – Tue 16:30**, occasionally **Wed 08:30** if readiness falls after Tue 18:00.
- With on-call authorization the first readout happens Sunday afternoon, so the confirmation incubation starts ~14–18 h earlier and the confirmed result lands **Mon 09:00–17:00** — this is where on-call actually pays.
- **Join = microbiology**, dominating chemistry by 24–48 h.
- QA then reviews a batch with an adverse result: investigation probability is 0.30–0.55, and QA service runs long. Release or rejection **Wed 23 Sep 10:00–18:00 (+114.5 to +122.5 h)** centrally; with a laboratory investigation, **Thu 24 – Fri 25 Sep (+140.5 to +164.5 h)**.
- This scenario carries most of the **Batch Rejected** probability mass.

**S5 — QA Documentation Query**
- Join and QA start as in S1/S2; QA begins, raises the query, and pauses. The query adds **4–12 QA working hours**, and QA hours are 12/day Mon–Fri.
- Query raised Mon ~13:00 ⇒ resume between Mon 17:00 and Tue 13:00; QA re-review 1.0–2.5 h; release **Tue 12:00–20:00 (+92.5 to +100.5 h)** centrally, edge case **Mon 19:00 (+75.5 h)** if only 4 h are needed and QA is still open.
- Documentation completeness 0.82 is decent but not high, which is why 0.15–0.30 rather than <0.10 is the right band.

**S6 — Documentation-only Deviation Investigation**
- Adds **8–20 QA working hours** on top of the join and first QA touch: opened Mon ~13:00, closed between Tue 09:00 and Wed 17:00; then QA acceptance 1–2.5 h and release ⇒ **Wed 23 Sep 12:00 – Thu 24 Sep 12:00 (+116.5 to +140.5 h)** centrally.
- Almost always co-occurs with S3, S4, or a confirmed excursion; the 0.04–0.10 "clean batch" rate applies only to S1/S2.

**S7 — Laboratory Deviation Investigation**
- Adds **3–7 calendar days**, which spans at least one further weekend given a Monday/Tuesday opening. Opened Tue ⇒ closed Fri 25 Sep – Tue 29 Sep; QA closeout 2–4 h plus release 0.25–0.75 h ⇒ terminal **Fri 25 Sep – Wed 30 Sep (+165 to +265 h)**, with a severe edge beyond +300 h.
- Terminal event here is Electronic Release in roughly two of three cases and **Batch Rejected** in the rest.

**Final step in all scenarios.** Electronic Release is 0.25–0.75 h and runs continuously — it never adds a calendar pause and is negligible relative to band widths. Batch-rejection recording is 0.5–1.0 h and likewise continuous.

## 4. Forecast summary

| Quantile | Remaining hours from Fri 18 Sep 15:30 | Approximate clock time |
|---|---|---|
| **Plausible earliest** | ~66–68 h | **Mon 21 Sep, 09:30–11:30** — requires priority discipline, a short Friday chemistry service, a fast Monday readout, and an empty QA queue at 08:00 |
| **Central / P50-like** | **~70–78 h** | **Mon 21 Sep, ~13:30 – Mon 21 Sep, ~21:00**, i.e. most plausibly a Monday-afternoon release, with the top of the band spilling to early Tuesday |
| **P80-like** | **~92–105 h** | **Tue 22 Sep, ~12:00 – Wed 23 Sep, ~00:30** — driven by a chemistry rerun, a documentation query, or a congested QA queue |
| **P90-like / severe tail** | **~118–145 h**, with a 3–5% residual beyond **165 h** | **Wed 23 Sep, ~14:00 – Thu 24 Sep, ~16:00**; residual tail **Fri 25 Sep – Tue 29 Sep** or later under a laboratory deviation investigation |

Interpretation notes. There is effectively **zero probability of a terminal event before Monday**: the microbiology result cannot be usable in a QA sense before Mon 08:00 (no Sunday QA), and chemistry cannot finish Friday. So the distribution has a hard floor near +64.5 h and is strongly right-skewed above it — the mode sits close to the floor and the mean (~90–100 h) sits well above the median because of the investigation tail. Quoting a mean would be misleading.

**Most plausible terminal event(s):** *Electronic Release*, probability ~0.92–0.96, most likely on **Monday 21 September afternoon/evening**. *Batch Rejected*, probability ~0.03–0.06, and if it occurs it is most likely **Wednesday 23 September or later**, following a confirmed contamination or a laboratory deviation investigation.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. **Chemistry queue discipline (FIFO vs. risk-priority) and the Monday chemistry queue.** *Queue uncertainty* (with a service-time component). This is the top driver because it decides *which branch binds the join*: under priority, chemistry finishes ~Mon 09:30 and microbiology binds; under FIFO it finishes ~Mon 14:30 and chemistry binds. Swing on the central forecast: 3–6 h.
2. **Deviation-investigation path, especially the documentation-only vs. laboratory split.** *Path uncertainty*. Low probability (6–15% combined) but by far the largest magnitude — the laboratory branch alone moves the terminal time by 70–180 h and dominates the P90 region.
3. **Unknown temperature-excursion flag.** *Dependence uncertainty* (plus *observation uncertainty*, since it is unknown only because of a sensor gap). It does not directly add duration; it raises rerun, contamination, and investigation probabilities *together*, correlating the tails and roughly doubling the joint probability of a multi-day outcome relative to an independence assumption.
4. **QA queue state at the Monday-morning join.** *Queue uncertainty*, aggravated by stated overdispersion in the 0.35/h arrival process. A quiet weekend gives ~0–1 h QA wait; a burst gives 3–5 h and can push acceptance past the 20:00 close into Tuesday.
5. **Microbiology readout staffing and the on-call decision.** *Calendar uncertainty*. Note the asymmetry: on the smooth path on-call authorization is worth almost nothing because QA is closed Sunday, but conditional on a contamination indication it advances the terminal time by ~14–18 h. Its importance is therefore itself path-dependent.

Runner-up, not in the top five: chemistry service-time dispersion (triangular 1.5–4.5 h) is *service-time uncertainty* and matters mainly through whether the Friday partial service leaves a small or large Monday residual.

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

**The single most informative next observation is the Monday-morning microbiology readout result — specifically, whether it shows a contamination indication (jointly with resolution of the temperature-excursion flag, which the same investigation typically settles).**

- **If the readout is clean and no excursion is confirmed** (most likely, ~0.75–0.85): the entire S4 branch is eliminated, the investigation probability collapses from the 0.30–0.55 band to 0.04–0.10, and the forecast tightens sharply to **Mon 21 Sep 12:30–19:00 (+69 to +75.5 h)** with a P90 near +95 h. The distribution loses most of its right skew, and attention shifts entirely to the chemistry queue discipline.
- **If a contamination indication is present** (~0.05–0.12, higher if the excursion is confirmed): the microbiology clock becomes decisively dominant, 20–30 h of confirmation incubation plus a second readout are inserted, the investigation probability jumps to 0.30–0.55, and the central forecast moves out to **Wed 23 Sep (+114 to +123 h)** with meaningful mass into Thursday/Friday. Batch Rejected becomes a live outcome at ~25–35% conditional probability, and the value of on-call authorization jumps from near-zero to ~14–18 h saved.
- **If the excursion is confirmed but the readout is clean**: an intermediate update — the chemistry rerun probability rises toward 0.20–0.30 and the investigation probability toward 0.30–0.55, shifting the central forecast to roughly **Tue 22 Sep – Wed 23 Sep** without the microbiology confirmation delay.

**Secondary update trigger:** the **first chemistry service start for B-417 on Monday morning** (or, equivalently, an explicit statement of the discipline actually in force). Observing service begin at ~08:00–09:00 confirms the priority discipline and pins the join to microbiology at ~Mon 09:00; observing that B-417 is still queued at ~11:00 confirms FIFO, makes chemistry the binding clock, and shifts the central release ~3–5 h later into Monday evening — with the compensating benefit of a shorter QA queue on arrival. A useful tertiary trigger is the **observed QA backlog at Monday 08:00**: more than about seven waiting batches would add 3–4 h to the central estimate and materially raise the chance of spilling past QA's 20:00 close.
