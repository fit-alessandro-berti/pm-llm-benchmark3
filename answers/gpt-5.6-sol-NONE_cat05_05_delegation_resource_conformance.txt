## 2. Rule Interpretation

### RR1 — Drafter/approver separation

For case \(c\):

\[
\text{human}(\text{Draft}_c) \ne \text{human}(\text{Approve}_c)
\]

Comparison is made after alias normalization and reliable session attribution. If either event cannot be mapped to a human, technical conformance is unresolved rather than presumed.

### RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation

\[
\text{human}(\text{Implement}_c) \ne \text{human}(\text{Verify}_c)
\]

Again, the comparison is human-to-human, not raw user-ID-to-user-ID. A service account cannot independently satisfy RR2.

### RR3 — High-risk approval authority

A HIGH-risk approval is valid only if the attributed human is:

1. the event-time QM; or
2. a QM delegate whose interval contains the approval timestamp, scope covers the case, required principal absence/unavailability is established, and delegation lineage contains no prohibited subdelegation.

Role validity uses event time, never the current user master.

A signed delegation created before use can prove authority even where the migrated database row was created later. D-QM-01’s signed PDF is primary evidence; the 2026-08-12 database entry is migration corroboration, not retroactive creation of authority. A later database entry alone, without contemporaneous evidence, would not reliably prove an earlier delegation.

### RR4 — Emergency substitution

For a credible HIGH-risk emergency:

- initial approval may be by the IC or valid IC delegate rather than QM;
- the event must have a credible emergency flag and safety rationale;
- a QM or valid QM delegate must complete `Retrospective_QM_Review` within two business days.

I count business days using the stated site calendar, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and site holidays. The deadline is the end of the second business day after the triggering emergency approval/implementation day unless the governing SOP specifies an hour-based deadline. Thus, an event on Tuesday 2026-08-11 has a Thursday 2026-08-13 end-of-day deadline. CHG-505’s Friday 2026-08-14 review is late.

The reviewer’s authority is assessed at review time. A technically timely review by an unauthorized or unidentifiable actor would not meet the underlying control objective.

### RR5 — Identity evidence

- Alias-equivalent IDs are one human.
- Service-account actions require reliable session attribution.
- Workstation assignment or room entry alone is insufficient because it establishes opportunity, not necessarily operation.
- Badge plus individual FIDO authentication close in time is strong attribution, though audit linkage to the exact transaction should still be retained.

### RR6 — Suspicion versus proof

Resource networks, repeated pairs, reciprocal approvals, concentrated handovers, shared terminals, leave conflicts, and account usage anomalies are investigative indicators. They do not by themselves prove collusion, credential sharing, or misconduct.

### Principal action while on leave

Leave is evidence of absence for a delegation condition; it does not remove the principal’s standing authority. Therefore, if Asha personally approved CHG-504, the approval was authorized despite leave.

Her personal return may affect delegation conditions prospectively or during the period she was actually available, depending on the delegation wording and policy. D-QM-01 says “while Asha is absent,” so Ben’s authority should be evaluated case-by-case after evidence that Asha resumed urgent work. A single 25-minute VPN session should not automatically invalidate all remaining delegation days, but it creates an availability ambiguity requiring records of recall-from-leave, case assignment, and actual operator identity.

## 6. Control Improvements

1. **Delegation creation and effective time**
   - Require digitally signed, immutable delegation records before activation.
   - Store separately: creation timestamp, signature timestamp, effective start/end, migration timestamp, principal, delegate, scope, conditions, and subdelegation permission.
   - Block approvals where delegation scope does not match a controlled taxonomy.

2. **Principal availability**
   - Integrate leave, on-call, recall-from-leave, and temporary return-to-duty records.
   - Define whether a principal’s brief availability suspends a delegation globally, for assigned cases only, or not at all.
   - Capture case-specific principal-unavailability attestations where required.

3. **Alias management**
   - Maintain a canonical human identifier independent of login IDs.
   - Feed all aliases to the QMS SoD engine in real time.
   - Prevent self-approval across aliases, domains, renamed accounts, and migrated identities.

4. **Individual authentication**
   - Eliminate generic accounts for regulated approvals and verification.
   - If a service process is technically necessary, require per-transaction FIDO re-authentication and retain cryptographic human attribution.
   - Enforce screen lock, prohibit shared credentials, and record device/session/user bindings.

5. **Emergency authority**
   - Require incident ID, safety rationale, emergency declaration timestamp, IC identity, and delegation lineage.
   - Automatically reject prohibited subdelegation.
   - Start a business-calendar timer for retrospective QM review and escalate before expiry.
   - Require late-review deviation documentation rather than silently accepting lateness.

6. **Role history**
   - Keep effective-dated, append-only role assignments.
   - Make event-time role snapshots available in every approval audit record.
   - Test migration reconciliation so current roles cannot overwrite historical roles.

7. **Pair rotation and workload design**
   - Do not treat repeated pairs as misconduct.
   - Introduce risk-based rotation or periodic independent sampling for HIGH-risk approvals.
   - Monitor whether concentrated pairs show abnormally low challenge, rejection, or rework rates.

8. **Network monitoring**
   - Baseline handover and working-together networks by cell, shift, expertise, and staffing.
   - Alert only on context-adjusted changes, closed reciprocal clusters, unusually rapid approvals, or concentration combined with other control failures.
   - Require human review before opening misconduct allegations.

9. **Evidential retention**
   - Retain signed delegation originals, certificate validation, QMS transaction logs, FIDO events, badge records, workstation/EDR logs, incident records, and migration lineage under synchronized UTC time.
   - Document access controls and chain of custody.
   - Preserve approval comments and substantive verification evidence, not merely usernames.

10. **Additional SoD policy**
    - Consider explicitly separating approval from verification for HIGH-risk cases. CHG-509 demonstrates that RR1 and RR2 alone permit one person to approve and later verify.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

The log contains a mixed population rather than one systemic explanation.

- **Likely genuine authority/control failures:** CHG-502, CHG-507, CHG-508, and CHG-510. CHG-505 also has a clear late retrospective review and possible scope/identity defects.
- **Likely valid substitution:** CHG-501 is well supported by contemporaneous D-QM-01 evidence.
- **Identity/data-quality risk:** CHG-503 and CHG-506 are substantial accountability failures; CHG-504 has a narrower identity caveat. CHG-509 has strong verifier attribution but unresolved delegation absence.
- **Organizational-pattern risk:** Clara–Ben concentration and reciprocal approvals justify monitoring and sampling, but do not prove collusion.

The three highest-priority investigations are:

1. **CHG-506:** identify who used Ben’s account during certified sick leave and determine Asha’s actual availability.
2. **CHG-503:** determine the human verifier and whether Diego self-verified through `svc_qms`.
3. **Emergency controls for CHG-505/CHG-510:** establish CI-44 linkage, safety rationale, scope coverage, review authorship, and why prohibited subdelegation or late review was accepted.

Overall, there is meaningful genuine nonconformance, but identity-control weakness and incomplete event-time evidence materially limit conclusions about individual intent or collusion.
