## 1. Drift diagnosis table

Conventions used throughout. **O** marks something observed directly in the supplied tables or audit findings. **D** marks something derived by arithmetic from observed figures. **H** marks a hypothesis that has not yet been tested.

The drift types are defined relative to the deployed model, which scores cases immediately after `Initial Diagnosis Completed`:
- **Covariate drift** is a change in P(X) for the model's features.
- **Concept drift** is a change in P(breach | X) for the features available at diagnosis.
- **Process drift** is a change in the control flow or timing of the process itself.
- **Label delay/censoring** is a distortion in how the outcome Y is measured at the cutoff.
- **Population shift** is the arrival of a new sub-population (Partner Z) whose joint distribution, including variables the model cannot see, differs from the existing one.
- **Pipeline failure** means the computed X, the scoring prefix or the labels differ from what actually happened.

These types are not mutually exclusive. Process drift can cause concept drift (supplier delays) or a prior shift (routing policy).

**What would falsify each explanation**
- **Pipeline (April).** Falsified if April, rebuilt with normalised labels and consistent deduplication, and re-scored by the frozen model, still shows AUROC ≲ 0.72 and ECE ≳ 0.08. That would point to real operational disruption during the migration.
- **Process drift as the June explanation.** Falsified if V1, V2 and V3 breach rates in June, at matched maturity, differ from May, or if the mix-implied rate diverged from the observed one.
- **Concept drift (V3, from July).** Falsified if the V3 breach rate reverts to about 0.33 once V3 is stratified on pre-diagnosis covariates, meaning the change was in within-V3 composition and not in the outcome mechanism. In a narrower sense it is also refuted if P(breach | V3, actual part wait) is stable over time. The drift would then be "performance drift propagated into the model's feature space", and the fix is a lead-time feature rather than relearning the mechanism.
- **Label delay (October).** Falsified if, when compared at the same label age, October's breach rate matches Jan–Sep at that age and does not rise as labels mature. Also falsified if the event log shows October's long-path cases closing within their promise.
- **Population shift (Partner Z).** Partner Z is not a separate cause if, on mature labels, its within-variant breach rates and model calibration match non-Z cases with the same observed covariates. Its effect would then be purely a mix effect.
- **Covariate drift.** Falsified as a material cause if the domain classifier's AUROC stays near 0.5 on cleaned features, or if the model is well calibrated within the shifted regions.

## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

**Derived month-level diagnostics.** The mix-implied rate is Σ(variant share × matured variant breach rate). The Brier skill score (BSS) compares the model's Brier score with a constant predictor at that month's observed rate.

| Month | Maturity | Observed | Mean predicted | Predicted − observed | Mix-implied rate (D) | BSS (D) | Dominant signature |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Jan | 100% | 0.18 | 0.19 | +0.01 | 0.171 | 0.11 | Baseline |
| Feb | 100% | 0.19 | 0.19 | 0.00 | 0.172 | 0.16 | Baseline |
| Mar | 100% | 0.18 | 0.18 | 0.00 | 0.166 | 0.11 | Baseline; slow part-edge creep |
| Apr | 100% | 0.19 | 0.28 | +0.09 | ≈0.19 (mapped cases, renormalised) | −0.20 | Broken inputs, normal outcomes |
| May | 100% | 0.18 | 0.21 | +0.03 | 0.179 | 0.01 | Residual pipeline damage |
| Jun | 100% | 0.15 | 0.20 | +0.05 | 0.158 | −0.09 | Mix change; prior shift |
| Jul | 98% | 0.19 | 0.18 | −0.01 | 0.199 | 0.02 | Onset of V3 concept drift, masked in aggregate |
| Aug | 93% | 0.28 | 0.19 | −0.09 | 0.313 | 0.02 | V3 concept drift plus V3 mix; mild censoring |
| Sep | 82% | 0.31 | 0.21 | −0.10 | 0.341 (probably understated) | ≈0.00 | Supplier drift plus Partner Z plus censoring |
| Oct | 55% | 0.16 | 0.23 | +0.07 (spurious) | ≈0.33 if Sep rates hold (H) | n/a | Label maturity dominates |

The mix-implied rate runs about 0.01 below the observed rate in the baseline months, which is consistent with rare variants and rounding. That tolerance is what I use to judge fit in later months.

**Month-by-month narrative**

- **January–March (baseline).** Metrics are stable (AUROC 0.81–0.82, ECE ≤ 0.025) and the data-quality indicators are at their floor. One early signal is observed: the median part wait creeps from 28 h to 35 h. It does not yet affect outcomes (V3 is 0.30–0.32). The model's absolute skill is modest (BSS ≈ 0.11–0.16). This matters later, because a small calibration offset is enough to wipe that skill out.

- **April (pipeline incident).** Outcomes are normal: 0.19 at 100% maturity, variant rates close to baseline and a mix-implied rate of ≈0.19. The prediction side is broken: unknown activities at 17%, missing queue feature at 14%, 18% unmapped variants, mean predicted risk of 0.28, AUROC 0.67, and a Brier score worse than a constant predictor. The audit explains the mechanism. When the renamed diagnosis label was not recognised, prefixes were built at the wrong event, which leaks later events or inflates event counts, or were not built at all, so the scored population was a selected subset. Missing queue values were imputed, and duplicates inflated count features. The 18% "unmapped" variants and the drop in V1/V2/V3 shares are an artefact of UNKNOWN labels and duplicates, not process drift. A secondary hypothesis is a small real disruption during the migration, since variant rates are slightly elevated. It is secondary because the scale is minor compared with the metric collapse.

- **May (partial recovery).** The 6 May patch brings unknowns down to 2% and missing values to 3%, and AUROC recovers to 0.78. The remaining gap (+0.03 over-prediction, ECE 0.05) is consistent with residual unmapped events, cases that straddled the patch, and the cached "number of events before diagnosis" feature for April-started cases that was never recomputed. The observed outcome mix (43/34/22) is essentially back to baseline. This is a residual pipeline issue, not drift.

- **June (routing policy, a real process change).** V2 rises to 52%, taken from both V1 and V3. The within-path breach rates are unchanged (0.16/0.09/0.35) and the mix-implied rate of 0.158 matches the observed 0.15. The outcome-generating mechanism conditional on the path did **not** change, and the diagnosis → Remote Fix time is stable. The model still over-predicts (0.20 vs 0.15), because the routing decision happens *after* the prediction point and the model cannot see the new policy. Measured only on its own features, P(breach | X) fell. This is best described as a prior shift induced by process drift. A calibration-intercept update fixes it; retraining is not needed. AUROC of 0.79 confirms that ranking was preserved. The negative BSS shows how much damage the calibration offset alone does.

- **July (supplier onset, hidden by offsetting errors).** The part wait jumps to 65 h and V3's breach rate to 0.46. Aggregate calibration looks acceptable (0.18 vs 0.19, ECE 0.042), but this is an artefact of cancellation. The V2-heavy segment is still over-predicted from the June shift while V3 is under-predicted. AUROC slips to 0.76. July shows that aggregate calibration monitoring alone would have missed the onset.

- **August (supplier-driven concept and process drift, before Partner Z).** The part wait is 130 h and V3's breach rate is 0.64. V3's share also rises to 35% for a reason the timeline does not document. The aggregate rate increase from July (≈0.20 → 0.31, mix-implied) splits roughly into:
  - a within-V3 rate effect of about +0.05 to +0.07;
  - a mix effect of about +0.04 to +0.07, depending on decomposition order.

  The two effects interact, because a larger V3 share matters more when V3 is worse. Partner Z is absent and labels are 93% mature, so neither population shift nor censoring explains August. Censoring slightly understates the rate (0.28 observed vs ≈0.31 implied). The model under-predicts by about 0.09–0.12 and discrimination falls to 0.68. Within V3, whether a case breaches is now dominated by supplier timing, which the diagnosis-time features cannot observe.

- **September (three overlapping causes).**
  1. The supplier problem persists (148 h): the non-Z breach rate is 0.27, close to August's 0.28.
  2. Partner Z adds 24% of volume with 58% V3 and an observed breach rate of 0.45. This is probably understated, because Partner Z labels mature more slowly and its immature cases are likely the long ones.
  3. Maturity is 82%, so both the aggregate 0.31 and V3's 0.61 are probably understated.

  Partner Z explains about 0.04 of the 0.13 rise over baseline, roughly a third. The rest is the non-Z supplier effect. A rough check (H) suggests most of Partner Z's excess comes from its V3 share: 0.58·0.61 plus about 0.42·0.15 gives ≈0.42, against 0.45 observed. A within-variant effect from travel distance and device age may explain the remainder and must be tested. The rise in mean predicted risk to 0.21 and in missing values to 3% may be Partner Z covariates and Partner Z integration gaps (H).

- **October (measurement-dominated).** Only 55% of labels are mature, and the backlog preferentially holds back long-path cases. The observed 0.16 therefore comes mostly from quickly resolved, mostly V2, non-breach cases. The process evidence points the other way: a 155 h part wait, 41% V3 and 27% Partner Z imply a mature breach rate around 0.30–0.36 (H). The apparent over-prediction (0.23 vs 0.16) is therefore probably a real **under**-prediction. AUROC 0.64 and ECE 0.13 are computed on a selected, non-representative subset and cannot be compared with mature months.

**Why these are not one homogeneous failure.** Each of the four episodes has a different signature, and each needs a remedy that would harm the others if applied everywhere.

| Episode | Where the anomaly lives | Outcomes | Discrimination | Calibration direction | Proportionate remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| April | Inputs (schema, prefixes, features) | Normal | Collapsed | Over-prediction | Fix and recompute data; no model change |
| June | Process mix after the prediction point | Lower, fully explained by mix | Intact | Over-prediction | Intercept recalibration, or add a policy/routing feature |
| Aug–Sep | Process performance → P(Y\|X); new population | Genuinely higher, within the V3 path | Degraded | Under-prediction | New features (lead time, source, geography), then retrain; operational escalation |
| October | Label measurement | Artificially low | Not measurable | Spurious over-prediction | Wait, age-match, derive labels from the event log |

The remedies conflict. A single "recalibrate downward" fix learned from April, June and October would deepen the August–September under-prediction. A single "retrain on everything" fix would absorb April's corrupted features and October's censored labels. The direction of error flips (over, over, under, apparently over), which alone rules out one cause.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

The tests use two cohort definitions:
- **Case-start cohorts** are the prediction and label unit.
- **Event-calendar cohorts** describe operational conditions and data quality in a calendar month.

Every test reports bootstrap confidence intervals, with cases resampled within each month.

**T1. Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting**
- *Procedure.* For each case-start month, plot the share of cases labelled and the cumulative breach rate against label age (15/30/45/60/90/120 days after case start). Do this overall and by variant, by predicted parts need and by source. Re-evaluate every month's AUROC, Brier score, ECE and calibration intercept/slope using labels as they were known at the same age (for example 45 and 90 days). Reconcile labels from the outcome API with event-log labels: `Case Closed` vs promised date, and open cases already past their promise count as known breaches.
- *If October is dominated by label delay:* October's curve at age *a* overlays September's and August's curves at the same age, and the overlay sits below their mature values. At equal age, October's AUROC and ECE resemble those of earlier months at the same age. Event-log labels show many closed or past-promise cases missing from the API, which is the backlog as distinct from natural delay.
- *If October is a genuine improvement (e.g. supplier recovery):* October's age-matched breach rate is below earlier months at the same age. Its V3 curve flattens, and the part-wait edge shortens in the event log. The last condition is already contradicted by the 155 h part wait.
- *If August–September deterioration is real concept drift:* at a fixed vintage, degradation persists and grows as labels mature, instead of shrinking.

**T2. Recomputation after activity-label normalisation and duplicate cleaning**
- *Procedure.* Rebuild the whole Jan–Oct log with one canonical activity map (`Diagnostic Assessment Completed` → `Initial Diagnosis Completed`). Resolve UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY where the source code allows it. Apply one duplicate rule across both sources and all dates: same case and normalised activity within a small tolerance, keep the first, and flag rather than silently delete. Rebuild prefixes at the true diagnosis event, recompute all derived features (including "events before diagnosis" and the queue features), and re-score with the **frozen** production model.
- *If April is a pipeline failure:* re-scored April returns to baseline (AUROC ≈ 0.80, ECE ≈ 0.03, mean predicted ≈ 0.19). Scoring coverage returns to about 100%, unknowns fall to ≈0.3% and unmapped variants to ≈0%. May's residual gap also disappears.
- *If April includes a real operational disruption:* part of the degradation survives recomputation. It lines up with real timing changes, such as longer edge durations in the cleaned performance DFG and higher mature breach rates in specific variants.
- *If the inconsistent deduplication shifted features in Jan–Mar:* recomputing Jan–Mar changes count-based features and slightly changes baseline metrics. This means training and serving were built with different feature definitions, which must be corrected before any retraining.

**T3. Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source and month**
- *Procedure.* For each month × stratum, compute reliability curves, the calibration intercept and slope from a logistic regression of the outcome on logit(p), within-stratum AUROC, and the share of the Brier score that is calibration versus refinement. Use mature or fixed-vintage labels only. The realised variant is known only after prediction, so stratifying on it is *diagnostic*. For decisions that could be deployed, also stratify by a predicted path (for example, diagnosis codes implying parts, or a remote-eligibility flag).
- *If June is a pure mix or prior shift:* within-variant calibration intercepts in June are close to Jan–May, and the aggregate offset is fully explained by variant shares. Reweighting predictions by variant removes the aggregate ECE.
- *If August is V3 concept drift:* V3's calibration intercept becomes strongly positive (under-prediction) in Jul–Sep, while V1 and V2 stay near zero. Within-V3 AUROC falls because supplier timing adds noise the model cannot see.
- *If Partner Z has a specific effect:* in September, Partner Z's V3 intercept exceeds non-Z V3 at the same label age.
- *If the aggregate drop is only due to mix:* within-stratum AUROC is stable and only between-stratum composition changed.
- *July cancellation check:* V2 over-prediction and V3 under-prediction should both be visible in July despite the benign aggregate ECE.

**T4. Comparison of process variants and DFG/performance-DFG edges**
- *Procedure.* For each week and month, compute variant shares (with "open/incomplete" as an explicit category for recent cohorts), DFG edge frequencies, and performance-DFG edge medians, P90 values and full distributions for every edge. Test the differences against a Jan–Mar reference with bootstrap intervals or a quantile-regression interaction with month. Also look for new edges, self-loops and zero-duration edges, which are the signature of duplicates.
- *If the supplier is the cause:* only `Order Part → Part Received` shifts, with a long right tail and a rising share above the promise slack. Diagnosis → Remote Fix, Dispatch → Repair Completed and Part Received → Dispatch stay stable.
- *If Partner Z strains capacity or travel:* Dispatch → Repair Completed and Part Received → Dispatch lengthen, especially for Partner Z and rural cases. This would also explain V1's slight rise from 0.16 to 0.19 in Aug–Sep (H).
- *If April is a pipeline artefact:* April's DFG shows UNKNOWN nodes, duplicate self-loops (A → A at about 0 h) and diagnosis-label splitting. After T2 these disappear and April's edges match March and May.
- *For the unexplained August rise in V3 share:* break it down by diagnosis code and region. If parts-requiring diagnoses rose, it is covariate drift in case demand. If the same diagnoses are routed differently (for example, less remote routing), it is process or policy drift.

**T5. Has the outcome relationship changed conditional on the same path?**
- *Procedure.* Within V3, fit a model of P(breach | month, actual part wait, promise slack, pre-diagnosis covariates), or a survival model of time to resolution against the promised date. Test whether month effects remain after conditioning on the realised part wait. Repeat for V1 and V2 with their own edge durations. Also compare the distribution of promise slack (promised date − diagnosis time) by month and source.
- *If process-performance drift propagates into the model's feature space:* month effects vanish once the part wait is conditioned on. The breach mechanism, meaning how waiting translates into breaches, is stable, and what changed is the supplier. The remedy is a lead-time feature known at prediction time. Relearning the mechanism is not needed.
- *If there is a genuine mechanism or definition change:* month effects remain after conditioning. Examples are shorter promise windows, different promise rules for Partner Z, or undocumented changes to how the outcome is defined. Promise-slack distributions will reveal the definitional variant.
- *If June involves no concept drift:* conditional on path and covariates, June's month effect is zero.

**T6. Population reweighting and transport-style checks for Partner Z**
- *Procedure.*
  1. Estimate density-ratio weights (a source classifier on shared pre-diagnosis covariates plus predicted path) that make non-Z cases resemble Partner Z. Check overlap (positivity): if rural, old-device non-Z cases are rare, report that transport is not identifiable instead of extrapolating.
  2. Compare reweighted non-Z breach rates and model calibration with Partner Z's observed values at the same label age.
  3. In the other direction, standardise September to August's composition (source and variant) to obtain a "Partner-Z-free" September.
- *If Partner Z is a pure mix or covariate shift:* reweighted non-Z cases reproduce Partner Z's breach rate and calibration within the confidence intervals. Standardised September ≈ non-Z September ≈ August.
- *If Partner Z has an effect from omitted variables or partner-specific operations:* a residual gap remains after reweighting on the model's features, and shrinks when distance and device age are added to the weighting model. If it does not shrink, look at partner-specific operations or promise rules.
- *If the Partner Z gap is a label-maturity artefact:* the gap changes materially as Partner Z labels mature. Compare only age-matched Partner Z and non-Z labels.

**T7. Feature missingness and lineage audits**
- *Procedure.* For each feature, month and source, record: missing rate, rate of default imputation, pipeline and mapping version, cache write time relative to the event time, and parity between the online-served value and the offline-recomputed value for the same case and prefix. Also record scoring coverage (the share of cases with a valid diagnosis prefix that were scored) and the prefix anchor event actually used.
- *If there is a pipeline failure:* missing values concentrate in the new source before 6 May and possibly in Partner Z's integration in Sep–Oct. Served and recomputed values disagree, most visibly for "events before diagnosis" in April. Anchor events other than diagnosis appear in April.
- *If there is genuine covariate drift:* served and recomputed values agree, missingness is flat, and distributions shift smoothly with volume and source mix.

**T8. Covariate-shift test on cleaned features**
- *Procedure.* Train a domain classifier (baseline Jan–Mar vs month *t*) on the recomputed features, and compute PSI per feature and on the score distribution.
- *If covariate drift is benign:* the domain classifier separates the months, but the model remains calibrated within the shifted regions.
- *If concept drift is present:* separation is weak (as for non-Z cases in August), yet calibration fails. This shows the change is in P(Y|X).

**T9. Mix-versus-rate decomposition, and intervention check**
- *Procedure.* Decompose each monthly change in the mature breach rate into a mix term and a within-path rate term (Kitagawa/Oaxaca-style, reporting both decomposition orders), as in Section 2. Separately, determine whether the June routing policy, or any operational action, used the model's risk score. If it did, observed outcomes are affected by the model's own interventions.
- *If mix explains the change:* the rate term ≈ 0, as in June.
- *If concept drift explains it:* the rate term dominates, as in V3 from July to September.
- *If there is intervention feedback:* flagged high-risk cases receive expediting and breach less, so the model appears to over-predict among flagged cases only.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Stage 0: Immediate containment (days).**
- Freeze automated retraining and recalibration jobs. Label Sep–Oct metrics "provisional (maturity < 95%)" on every dashboard.
- Do **not** lower thresholds or recalibrate downward because October shows 0.16.
- Serve-time guards:
  - If a case has no valid diagnosis anchor, carries UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY in its prefix, or has an imputed critical feature, abstain or flag it for manual triage instead of emitting a default score.
  - Log scoring coverage.
- Operational overlay (not a model change) for cases whose diagnosis implies a part order: raise their visibility while the part wait exceeds a threshold (for example > 72 h rolling median), because the model is known to under-predict them.
- Communicate to users that risk is currently under-stated for parts-dependent and Partner Z cases. Frame Partner Z handling as closer monitoring, not a penalty.
- Escalate the supplier problem to procurement, since the largest deterioration is a real business change.

**Stage 1: Short-term recalibration (1–3 weeks), keeping the model's features and ranking.**
- Fit a segment-aware recalibration layer (intercept and slope, or isotonic with care) on **mature, clean** recent data: June–July, plus August once it is at least 97% mature, or with censoring-aware weighting (IPCW, inverse-probability-of-censoring weights using a censoring model conditioned on path and age).
- Segments: predicted parts need (yes/no) × source (Z/non-Z, the latter pooled with a prior because Partner Z data are thin).
- Recalibration can fix June-type prior shifts almost completely. It can shift the V3 segment's level for August–September. It **cannot** restore lost discrimination (AUROC 0.68 → 0.8), because the missing information (supplier timing, geography) is not among the inputs.
- The supplier regime keeps moving (65 → 155 h). Refit on a rolling window with recency weighting, and re-validate at fixed vintage each month.
- Acceptance criterion: calibration intercept ≈ 0 and slope ≈ 1 within each segment on the most recent mature month, measured at fixed vintage.

**Stage 2: Feature repair (2–6 weeks).**
- Make the canonical activity map, the uniform deduplication rule, prefix anchoring on the normalised label, and the recomputation of all cached features (April especially) a permanent part of the pipeline. Add lineage tests (served vs recomputed parity) to CI.
- Add features that are **known at prediction time**, so that future regime changes become covariate shifts the model can handle rather than concept drift:
  - predicted parts need (diagnosis-based);
  - rolling supplier lead-time statistics (for example the median and P90 part wait over the previous 14 days, strictly before the prediction time);
  - part or supplier identifier where available;
  - a flag for remote-routing policy eligibility;
  - source/partner;
  - rural travel distance and device age;
  - technician capacity and queue measures, now properly populated.
- Close Partner Z's integration gaps, attributing the residual missingness and unknowns by source.
- Verify promised-date rules by source. If Partner Z uses different SLA rules, the label semantics differ and must be recorded explicitly.

**Stage 3: Full retraining (once gates pass; realistically after the October labels mature, i.e. early next year).**

Retraining is allowed only when all of the following hold:
1. T2 and T7 pass for the whole training window.
2. Label maturity is ≥ 97% overall **and** ≥ 95% within the V3 and Partner Z strata. Alternatively, a censoring-aware learner (discrete-time survival against the promised date, or IPCW with a censoring model conditioned on path and age) is used.
3. Fixed-vintage degradation of the recalibrated incumbent persists for at least two cohorts.
4. The Stage 2 features are available historically, with no leakage.

Evaluation and deployment:
- Validate on rolling origins: train through month *t* and test on *t+1* at a fixed vintage.
- Compare against the recalibrated incumbent.
- Deploy as a shadow or challenger for at least one cohort, with the stratified metrics from T3.

**Which months are safe to train on**

| Month | Status now | Condition to become usable |
|---|---|---|
| Jan–Mar | Safe | Represents the pre-supplier, pre-routing regime. Check whether deduplication of the old source changes count features (T2). |
| Apr | **Unsafe** as stored | Only after full recomputation, re-scoring back near baseline, and cached features rebuilt. Otherwise exclude permanently. |
| May | Conditional | After verifying the residual 2% unknowns and 3% missing values. Exclude or recompute cases whose features straddle the patch. |
| Jun | Safe | Fully mature and clean. Represents the new routing regime. |
| Jul | Safe (almost) | Wait for about 100% maturity; the remaining 2% are long cases. Supplier onset. |
| Aug | Conditional | ≥ 97–98% maturity or censoring-aware weighting. This is the key month for the supplier regime without Partner Z. |
| Sep | **Unsafe** for binary-label training | Censoring-aware methods only, or after maturation. Partner Z labels are especially incomplete. |
| Oct | **Unsafe** | Monitoring and age-matched comparison only. |

Pooling Jan–Mar with Aug–Sep without regime features would teach the model an average of two supplier regimes. Once lead time is a feature, the model can learn one mechanism across regimes. This is also the only way it will behave correctly when the supplier recovers.

**Is a segment-specific or variant-specific model justified?**
- *Variant-specific models routed on the realised variant are not deployable.* The branch (Remote Fix vs Dispatch vs Order Part) happens **after** the prediction point.
- *A path-decomposed structure is justified if the path can be predicted at diagnosis.* The structure is P(breach | x) = Σ_v P(v | x) · P(breach | v, x). It fits the evidence well. Routing policy changes (June) act on P(v | x), supplier changes (July–October) act on P(breach | V3, x), and each component can then be recalibrated or retrained separately. Adopt it if a path classifier at diagnosis time has good discrimination (for example AUROC ≥ 0.85). Otherwise use a single model with a parts-need indicator and interactions with lead time.
- *A Partner-Z-specific model is not justified now.* There are about two months of slow-maturing labels (≈1,200–1,400 cases per month), so overfitting risk is high. The T6 evidence may also show that most of Partner Z's excess is variant mix. The better choice is a global model with source, distance and device-age features, monitored per source, plus a per-source calibration layer. Revisit once T6 shows a persistent within-variant Partner Z residual on mature labels and enough long-path Partner Z cases have matured.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

**Layered monitors.** Each monitor records its cohort basis, because event-calendar and case-start cohorts answer different questions.

| Monitor | Signals | Cohort basis | Example alert rule | Gated by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 Data quality & lineage | Activity vocabulary vs canonical map, UNKNOWN share, duplicate share (by source), per-feature missingness and imputation rate, scoring coverage, share of cases with a valid diagnosis anchor, served-vs-recomputed parity, pipeline and mapping version | Event-calendar (daily) plus case-start | Unknown share > 3× the trailing baseline or > 1%; coverage < 98%; parity mismatch > 0.5%; any new unmapped label | None (root layer) |
| M2 Label maturity | Maturity curve by case-start cohort and stratum, API-vs-event-log label reconciliation, Kaplan–Meier label-delay distribution, share of "known breach, open" cases | Case-start | Maturity at age *a* below the historical band; API backlog (event log closed, API missing) > 2% | M1 (label pipeline health) |
| M3 Process variants | Variant shares including "open", emergence of new variants, routing shares by diagnosis code | Case-start (with explicit open share) plus event-calendar | Share of any variant changes > 5 pp with a CI excluding 0 for 2 consecutive weeks | M1 (vocabulary green) |
| M4 Performance DFG | Median and P90 per edge, especially `Order Part → Part Received`, dispatch and remote edges; share of part waits exceeding the promise slack | Event-calendar for operations; case-start for outcome linkage | Edge median +25% vs the trailing 8 weeks with a CI excluding 0; P90 above the promise slack | M1 |
| M5 Population | Source/partner mix, geography, device age, volume per source, PSI per feature, domain-classifier AUROC | Case-start | New source > 5% of volume; PSI > 0.2 on a key feature; domain AUROC > 0.65 | M1 |
| M6 Predictive (discrimination) | AUROC and PR-AUC overall and by stratum; Brier and Brier skill score | Case-start, **fixed vintage** only | AUROC drop > 0.03 (CI) for 2 cohorts at a fixed vintage | M1 green and M2 maturity ≥ threshold, otherwise tagged "provisional" |
| M7 Calibration | Calibration intercept and slope, ECE, observed/expected ratio, by predicted path × source; early proxy of predicted risk vs "known-so-far" breaches at matched age | Case-start, fixed vintage, plus an age-matched early proxy | \|intercept\| > 0.3 logit or slope outside [0.8, 1.2] in any major stratum | M1 green; M2 decides provisional or final |
| M8 Intervention & deployment | Model version, threshold, share flagged, actions taken on flagged cases, override rates, policy and change calendar (migration, routing policy, onboarding, supplier notices), whether policies consume the score | Event log of deployments and decisions | Any undeclared change in the share flagged or in action rates; a policy change entering the calendar | None; it annotates all other monitors |

**Alert dependencies (decision logic)**

1. **M1 red → quarantine.** Suppress M3–M7 conclusions for affected cohorts, mark those cohorts ineligible for training, and route the alert to data engineering. No recalibration or retraining is triggered.
2. **M2 below the maturity threshold → provisional.** M6 and M7 for that cohort become provisional and cannot trigger retraining or recalibration. Only age-matched comparisons (T1) and event-log-derived labels are allowed. A label-delay alarm by itself only opens a backlog ticket and a "wait" state.
3. **M3/M4/M5 alert → annotate and stratify, do not retrain.** Check stratified calibration (M7) on the latest mature cohort.
   - If within-stratum calibration is intact, treat it as a mix or prior shift and recalibrate the intercept, or do nothing.
   - If a stratum is miscalibrated and the drift is in an edge (M4), recalibrate that segment, raise an operations alert and open a feature-repair ticket.
4. **Recalibration trigger:** M1 green, and M2 mature for the evaluation cohort, and M7 breach in at least one stratum, and M6 stable (AUROC within tolerance).
5. **Retraining trigger:** M1 green over the full training window, M2 mature (≥ 97% overall, ≥ 95% per key stratum) or a censoring-aware setup, M6 degraded at fixed vintage for at least 2 cohorts **after** recalibration, and a root cause assigned via M3–M5/M8 with the required features available.
6. **M8 events** open "expected change" windows. During these windows thresholds are interpreted relative to the declared change, for example routing policy → expect an M3 shift and an M7 intercept shift in the V2-heavy strata. Any post-change drift that differs from the expectation is escalated.

The code below extracts auditable monthly process evidence. It deliberately contains no drift detector. The month-to-month comparisons are the statistical tests in Section 3, run on its output.

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE, ACT, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
DIAG = "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
RENAMES = {"Diagnostic Assessment Completed": DIAG}   # extend from the migration mapping table
VARIANTS = {
    ("Case Created", "Triage", DIAG, "Dispatch Technician", "Repair Completed", "Case Closed"): "V1",
    ("Case Created", "Triage", DIAG, "Remote Fix", "Case Closed"): "V2",
    ("Case Created", "Triage", DIAG, "Order Part", "Part Received",
     "Dispatch Technician", "Repair Completed", "Case Closed"): "V3",
}


def load_events(path="field_service_events.csv", dup_tolerance="2min"):
    raw = pd.read_csv(path)                      # expects case_id, activity, timestamp; optional partner, promised_resolution
    raw["activity_raw"] = raw["activity"]
    raw["activity"] = raw["activity"].str.strip().replace(RENAMES)   # normalise before any prefix/feature logic
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(raw, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp")
    if "org:resource" not in log.columns:
        log["org:resource"] = "n/a"
    # One duplicate rule for all sources and dates (production only covered the new source after 20 April):
    # same case + normalised activity within the tolerance is flagged; the first occurrence is kept.
    log = log.sort_values([CASE, ACT, TS])
    gap = log.groupby([CASE, ACT])[TS].diff()
    log["dup_flag"] = gap.notna() & (gap <= pd.Timedelta(dup_tolerance))
    return log.sort_values([CASE, TS]).reset_index(drop=True)


def _median_h(perf, edge):
    v = perf.get(edge)
    return np.nan if v is None else (v["median"] if isinstance(v, dict) else v) / 3600


def case_table(clean, cutoff):
    pc = clean.groupby(CASE).agg(start=(TS, "min"))
    pc["closed"] = clean[clean[ACT] == "Case Closed"].groupby(CASE)[TS].min()
    diag = clean[clean[ACT] == DIAG].groupby(CASE)[TS].min().rename("diag")
    pc["has_diag_prefix"] = pc.index.isin(diag.index)
    tmp = clean.join(diag, on=CASE)
    pc["n_events_before_diag"] = (tmp[TS] < tmp["diag"]).groupby(tmp[CASE]).sum()  # diff against the cached feature
    if "partner" in clean.columns:
        pc["partner"] = clean.groupby(CASE)["partner"].first()
    if "promised_resolution" in clean.columns:   # label derived from the log, independent of the outcome API
        promise = pd.to_datetime(clean.groupby(CASE)["promised_resolution"].first(), utc=True)
        closed = pc["closed"]
        pc["label_state"] = np.select(
            [closed.notna() & (closed > promise), closed.notna(), promise < cutoff],
            ["breach", "no_breach", "breach_open"], default="censored")
    return pc


def monthly_evidence(log, first="2026-01", last="2026-10"):
    clean = log[~log["dup_flag"]]
    tz, cutoff = clean[TS].dt.tz, clean[TS].max()
    cases = case_table(clean, cutoff)
    rows, graphs = [], {}
    for m in pd.period_range(first, last, freq="M"):
        s0, s1 = (t.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S") for t in (m.start_time, m.end_time))
        # (a) Event-calendar cohort: events executed in the month, whatever the case start date.
        #     Describes workload and data quality in that month; edges crossing month borders are cut.
        ev = pm4py.filter_time_range(log, s0, s1, mode="events")
        # (b) Case-start cohort: cases created in the month, followed to the cutoff.
        #     This is the prediction/label cohort; recent months are right-censored (open cases).
        t0, t1 = m.start_time.tz_localize(tz), m.end_time.tz_localize(tz)
        pc = cases[(cases["start"] >= t0) & (cases["start"] <= t1)]
        cs = clean[clean[CASE].isin(pc.index)]
        dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(cs)
        perf, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(cs)
        shares = {}
        for trace, n in pm4py.get_variants(cs).items():
            key = VARIANTS.get(tuple(trace), "other" if "Case Closed" in trace else "open")
            shares[key] = shares.get(key, 0) + (n if isinstance(n, int) else len(n))
        row = {
            "month": str(m),
            "ev_events": len(ev),
            "ev_unknown_share": (ev[ACT] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean(),
            "ev_renamed_share": (ev["activity_raw"].str.strip() != ev[ACT]).mean(),
            "ev_dup_share": ev["dup_flag"].mean(),
            "cs_cases": len(pc),
            "cs_closed_share": pc["closed"].notna().mean(),
            "cs_missing_diag_prefix": 1 - pc["has_diag_prefix"].mean(),
            "cs_median_events_before_diag": pc["n_events_before_diag"].median(),
            "cs_freq_order_to_received": dfg.get(("Order Part", "Part Received"), 0),
            "cs_part_wait_median_h": _median_h(perf, ("Order Part", "Part Received")),
            "cs_diag_to_remote_median_h": _median_h(perf, (DIAG, "Remote Fix")),
            **{f"cs_share_{k}": v / max(len(pc), 1) for k, v in sorted(shares.items())},
        }
        if "partner" in pc:
            row["cs_partner_Z_share"] = (pc["partner"] == "Z").mean()
        if "label_state" in pc:
            row.update(pc["label_state"].value_counts(normalize=True).add_prefix("cs_label_").to_dict())
        rows.append(row)
        graphs[str(m)] = {"dfg": dfg, "performance_dfg": perf}
    weekly = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(clean, grouper_freq="W")   # event-calendar view
    return pd.DataFrame(rows).set_index("month"), weekly, graphs


if __name__ == "__main__":
    summary, weekly, graphs = monthly_evidence(load_events())
    summary.to_csv("monthly_process_evidence.csv")   # auditable input to the Section 3 tests
    weekly.to_csv("weekly_temporal_features.csv")
```

The two cohorts are read differently:
- **Event-calendar rows** (`ev_*`) answer "what did the system and process do in this calendar month?" They are the right basis for the April incident (unknowns, renamed labels, duplicates by processing date) and for operational performance.
- **Case-start rows** (`cs_*`) answer "how did cases that entered in this month behave?" They are the cohort the model scores and the labels refer to. For recent months they are right-censored, so `cs_share_open`, `cs_closed_share` and the `censored` label state must be reported alongside any variant share or breach rate.

The same function should also be run with `cs` split by partner and by diagnosis-derived parts need, to feed T3, T4 and T6.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

1. **Retraining or recalibrating on immature labels (treating October's 0.16 as real).**
   - *Risk.* Labels are missing mostly for long, V3 and Partner Z cases, so the model would learn that exactly these cases are low-risk. Risk estimates would fall to about 0.16–0.20 while the true mature rate is plausibly above 0.30. Missed breaches would concentrate in parts-dependent and rural cases, and the damage would be locked in until the next retrain.
   - *Mitigation.* Gate training on maturity per stratum (≥ 95–97%). Report only at fixed vintage. Derive labels from the event log (closure and past-promise open cases). Use survival or IPCW learners with a censoring model conditioned on path and age when recent data are essential. Enforce the alert-dependency rule that a label-delay alarm cannot trigger retraining.

2. **Calibrating around the April pipeline bug.**
   - *Risk.* Fitting a downward correction to April's inflated scores (0.28 vs 0.19), or retraining with the corrupted cached features, teaches the model to compensate for artefacts. When the pipeline is fixed, the compensation becomes a bias in the opposite direction. Corrupted "events before diagnosis" values can also create spurious feature importance.
   - *Mitigation.* Fix the data first (T2), then re-score with the frozen model. Quarantine April until recomputation matches baseline. Make served-vs-recomputed parity and vocabulary checks blocking gates, so that no calibration job runs on cohorts where M1 is red.

3. **Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift.**
   - *Risk.* Explaining August–September away as Partner Z or label delay leaves V3 under-predicted by a large margin (0.46–0.64 real vs the ~0.33 the model has learned), even though August had no Partner Z and 93% maturity. Operations lose the early warning precisely when breaches are rising. The supplier problem also goes unescalated.
   - *Mitigation.* Use the August non-Z evidence and the stratified calibration from T3 to confirm the drift. Recalibrate the parts-likely segment immediately. Add rolling supplier lead time as a feature so the model tracks the regime and also recovers when the supplier improves. Route performance-DFG edge alerts (M4) to procurement and operations, not only to data science.

4. **Attributing Partner Z's higher error or breach rate to unfair model behaviour without stratified evidence, or dismissing it.**
   - *Risk (first direction).* Declaring the model "biased against Partner Z" and suppressing or equalising its scores could hide real risk from rural customers and reduce proactive help for them. Most of the gap appears to be V3 mix and supplier delay, and the relevant harm is likely *under*-prediction for Partner Z.
   - *Risk (second direction).* Dismissing the gap as pure mix would ignore a real within-variant effect from travel distance and device age.
   - *Mitigation.* Evaluate fairness on mature, age-matched labels, stratified by variant and predicted path (T3), with reweighting and overlap checks (T6). Separate mix effects from within-variant effects before drawing any fairness conclusion. Add the missing covariates. State explicitly how predictions are used (prioritisation vs penalty), because that determines which error direction is harmful.

5. **Treating June's routing change as concept drift and retraining.**
   - *Risk.* A full retrain on post-June data changes the ranking unnecessarily, adds variance, and still does not encode the policy. The next routing change would break it again.
   - *Mitigation.* Confirm with T9 and T5 that path-conditional rates are unchanged. Apply an intercept or prior-shift correction. Add a routing or policy feature, or adopt the path-decomposed structure so that routing changes only update P(path | x).

6. **Over-reacting with a Partner-Z-only or variant-only model.**
   - *Risk.* A Partner Z model trained on about two months of slow-maturing labels overfits and inherits their censoring bias. A realised-variant model uses information that is not available at prediction time, which is leakage, and looks excellent offline but fails online.
   - *Mitigation.* Use a global model with source and geography features and a per-source calibration layer. Use only features available at diagnosis time (predicted path, not realised path). Reconsider segmentation only after T6 shows a persistent within-variant residual on mature labels.

7. **Mistaking intervention feedback for model error.**
   - *Risk.* If high-risk flags trigger expediting, or if the June routing policy consumed the score, flagged cases breach less and the model looks over-confident. Recalibrating downward would then undo the intervention's targeting.
   - *Mitigation.* Log interventions per case (M8). Evaluate calibration on untreated or pre-intervention outcomes, or keep a small randomised holdout without intervention. Model the treatment explicitly when estimating calibration.
