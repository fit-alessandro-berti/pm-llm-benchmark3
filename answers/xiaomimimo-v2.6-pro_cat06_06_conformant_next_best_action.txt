## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

**Ordering of objectives (lexicographic, not compensatory).** Conformance is a *constraint*, not a cost line. Only within the set of conformant (or lawfully excepted) paths do I minimise expected portfolio harm. This is not squeamishness: rules 6, 7, 9 and 10 exist because the harms they guard against are precisely the ones that cannot be priced — an unlawful crossborder payment to a sanctionedborder region produces licence, criminal and personal liability that no €20–35k operational saving can offset.

**Ranges, not points.** The stated bands overlap badly: benefit €20–35k sits *inside* the €15–80k customerharm band, and regulatory harm (€100–500k plus nonmonetary) is an order of magnitude larger and rightskewed. Point estimates would be false precision. I therefore use (i) interval dominance, (ii) explicit breakeven thresholds, and (iii) minimaxregret over the bands.

**The decisive dominance argument against A and B.** The mandatory Second Officer Review takes 35–75 min plus 5–15 min to record/execute = **40–90 min, which fits inside the 90minute clock**. Therefore the *incremental* operational benefit of executing at T+10–15 (Action A) or T+10–20 (Action B) over the conformant plan is only the sliver of probability by which the conformant plan overruns 90 min — call it **€0–5k**. In exchange, A/B absorb the *entire* regulatory tail. A trade of €0–5k of certain benefit against €15k–150k of expected regulatory harm plus unbounded nonmonetary harm is dominated under every monotone risk measure, before we even reach the point that the harm is irreversible and partly borne by individuals (R17 personally, in B’s case). This is why no favourable forecast can rescue A or B (see §4).

**F versus the informed review.** F looks prudent (“control the unlawfulexecution risk”) but it buys **no incremental compliance benefit**: in the Rank 1 path execution is gated by the mandatory second review in 100% of branches, so the probability of an unlawful execution is already 0. What F *does* change is the falserejection rate. Rejecting now is false with probability 0.55–0.75 (the case’s predicted approvability)  expected harm €8k–60k. Under review, a rejection occurs only with probability 0.25–0.45 and only a fraction (0.3–0.6) of those are wrong  €1k–22k. So F **strictly increases** expected customer harm while forgoing the 0.55–0.75 approval branch worth €20–35k. F is dominated except in one narrow state of the world: if the documented rejection threshold is actually met (i.e. the case was *not* approvable), F’s harm collapses toward zero and it moves up the ranking. That is why F ranks 4 and not lower, and why it can swap with E.

**E versus F.** E’s harm is a *slow, reversible* miss (€30–100k, dominated by forgone deadline value and hold harm); F’s harm is an *irreversible wrong decision* whose validity is itself unestablished. Where irreversible harm and uncertain authority are in play, recoverability is worth paying for. If, however, the customer’s hold harm is known to sit at the top of the band (€80k) *and* the rejection threshold is documented and met, E and F swap.

**Portfolio externalities and the breakeven on P901.** Rank 1 buys roughly P(ontime)  0.55–0.85 of extra ontime delivery versus Rank 2, worth €13–45k to P884 including holdharm avoidance. It pays 0.06–0.20 (earlyrelease variant) of extra breach probability on P901. Setting these equal: **V  €150k (range €50k–350k)** is the indifference point. Below it, preempting is netbeneficial for the book; above it, waiting for S4 is. Since V is *unknown*, the plan opens with a 3minute triage — a textbook valueofinformation step costing almost nothing against a €20k–80k decision swing.

**Uncertainty about intervention effects.** The 0.48–0.64 SLAbreach and 0.55–0.75 approval figures are *observational*, fitted under “continue normal processing.” They do not transport unchanged: pulling S2 under escalation changes the officer’s selection and possibly the stringency of review; rescreening adds evidence that shifts the approval rate in either direction; and timecompressed reviewers can anchor on the deadline. I therefore (a) use the ranges robustly, (b) never let the point estimate settle a control question, and (c) prefer plans whose ranking is stable across the whole band — which Rank 1 is, except at V  €150k–350k.

**Why A and B cannot be rescued by a favourable forecast.**
1. **Constraint, not tradeoff.** Rules 6, 7 and 9 define what counts as a *valid approval path*. A payment executed outside it is not “slightly worse”; it is legally void/illegal regardless of whether the underlying payment was substantively correct. Money cannot ratify a nullity.
2. **The forecast is about outcomes, not authority.** “Approval probability 0.55–0.75” describes what a review would conclude; it says nothing about who may conclude it. B’s defect is *identity* (R17 cannot secondreview R17), which is a structural separationofduties property, invariant to any number.
3. **The forecast is not even reliable.** It is observational and explicitly nontransporting under intervention; it is a range (0.55–0.75), and the action we would be forecasting is precisely an offpolicy, unobserved one. A control cannot be waived on the strength of an estimate whose validity is unknown.
4. **The rule’s purpose is exactly the unpriced tail.** Independent challenge exists to catch the 0.46 fuzzy match on a €480k transfer to a sanctionedborder region. Expected value cannot represent a €100–500k+ regulatory hit with nonmonetary consequences (licence, criminal exposure, personal liability).
5. **Dominance anyway.** As shown in §3, A/B’s incremental benefit over the conformant plan is €0–5k because the mandated review fits inside the remaining clock. They are strictly dominated *even if* the compliance tail were set to zero.
6. **Selfcertifying good numbers is the failure mode the rule targets.** A control that may be bypassed whenever the forecast looks favourable is not a control.

## 5. Resource spillovers

**The timevalue decay of the S2 option.** S2 is the only resource that can deliver a recorded outcome inside 90 minutes: pulled at T+0 the completion band is T+40–90 (ontime 0.85–1.0); pulled at T+15 0.75; at T+25 0.50; at T+35 0.35; and if we simply wait for S4 the completion band is T+75–155 (ontime 0.10–0.25). **Every 10 minutes of deferral costs roughly 12–15 points of ontime probability.** Consequence: the commonly tempting hedge “wait a bit, escalate to S2 only if S4 slips” is the *worst of both* — it pays (later) nearly all of the P901 spillover while forfeiting most of the S2 option’s value. The S2 option must be exercised **early and briefly, or not at all**. Rank 1 does exactly this: it starts S2 immediately but caps the engagement and gives it a T+20–25 exit, so the interruption is 20–45 min rather than a flat 45.

**P901 protection (mitigations, in order).** (i) 3minute triage to quantify V — if it is €200k–350k, do not preempt (see §3 breakeven). (ii) Stage P901 at a safe checkpoint and hand its pending items over before pulling S2. (iii) Scope S2’s task to the mandatory elements only (namematch adjudication + 70 escalation) and record the outcome within the engagement; the 5–15 min recording/execution is done by R17/ops, not S2. (iv) Release S2 the moment the second review is recorded, and immediately on the checkpoint branch if the triggers are extinguished. (v) **Secondorder spillover:** squeezing P901 into its last ~25 minutes creates pressure to shortcut *its* controls — P901’s own review/foureyes requirements stand and must not be traded for its 70minute deadline. Preemption also creates a queueingfairness precedent; log the rationale.

**Alternatescreening quota (3 calls left).** Use exactly one now: its information value is high (it is the only thing that can extinguish the rule6 match trigger), its opportunity cost is low, and it consumes no officer time — which is why it runs in parallel with S2 rather than before it. Reserve one call for a final preexecution check if the case proceeds, and one for contingency/other cases. **Do not** run repeats to “shop” for a sub40 score or a clean name result: serial rescreening in search of a clearance is control gaming, and any AutoClear reached that way is not a genuine rule3 path.

**Exceptionwaiting risk (G).** A 20–60minute director response is time bought with the 90minute clock and with no guaranteed output; the option decays to zero value at the deadline and, worse, manufactures pressure to invent an incident identifier. Keep G to an informational headsup to the director (“here is the plan and the clock”), and activate it only if a genuine incident actually blocks the review route.

**Sensitivity: S4 at 35 minutes vs 65 minutes.**
- **S4 at ~35 min.** Completion band T+75–125  P(ontime)  0.25 versus 0.9 for Rank 1. The gap is real but much smaller, and Rank 2 then delivers zero spillover. **The ranking flips** — Rank 2 becomes Rank 1 — if triage shows V  **€200k–350k**, or if P901 cannot be safely staged/handed over, or if P884’s ontime value is at the low end (€20k) and its hold harm small. A reasonable compromise in this state is “hold S2 in reserve, hard checkpoint at T+30, pull S2 only if S4 is unconfirmed.”
- **S4 at ~65 min.** Completion band T+105–155  P(ontime)  0.0–0.05: S4 then delivers a *decision*, not a delivery. Rank 1 is clearly first unless V  €400k+. A T+35 escalation to S2 would give T+75–125 (p0.25) — better than nothing, but it pays the spillover late and is strictly worse than pulling S2 at T+0.

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence

**Primary recommendation — Rank 1 (C  D fullparallel dual track).** Within three minutes: quantify P901’s atrisk breach cost and stage P901 at a safe checkpoint; pull **S2** ( R17) for a bounded, independently recorded Second Officer Review of the validated evidence; **simultaneously** fire one alternatescreening call and request a refreshed risk score; at **T+20–25** run the checkpoint — if the 0.46 match has been authoritatively dispositioned and a fresh score <70 (or <40 with no alert) is on file, close S2’s task and release S2 back to P901 and proceed to analyst approval/execution; otherwise let S2 record Approve  execute within 40–90 min, or Reject / Hold+SAR (a SAR filing never authorises execution). Communicate to the customer only in neutral terms — no SAR or alert disclosure.

**Fallback — Rank 2 (D  S4wait).** If the triage shows P901’s breach exposure  €200k–350k (or S2 cannot be safely staged), wait for S4 while running the same screening and score refresh, with a **precommitted** hard decision at T+35 (escalate to S2 only if S4 is unconfirmed *and* V is low) and acceptance that the 90minute deadline will probably be missed. Under no circumstance fall back to A, B or an unenabled G: if both officers are unavailable and the deadline becomes unrecoverable, the correct terminal action is a neutral “processing continues” message and the evidence loop — not execution.

**Confidence.** **0.65–0.75** that Rank 1 is the best available *conformant* plan (its ranking is stable across the stated bands except at V  €150k–350k); **0.55–0.65** on the exact timing profile (the 40–90 min band just fits the clock, with no slack for handover slippage); **0.85+** that A and B are strictly dominated and must never be selected on expectedloss grounds; **0.85** that G is unusable as things stand. The residual uncertainty is dominated by two unknowns: P901’s quantified breach cost and whether the second officer will approve an open 0.46 match at score 70.

**Trigger for immediate reranking — the alternatescreening disposition plus the refreshed risk score (expected T+15–30).** If it authoritatively dispositions the fuzzy match *and* the fresh score is <70 (or <40 with no alert), rule 6’s triggers are extinguished, the second review ceases to be mandatory, S2 is returned to P901 at once and the case proceeds to analyst approval and execution — this single observation dominates every other consideration. Secondary triggers, if observed first: a confirmed S4 availability time of ~35 min combined with a quantified V  €200k (promote Rank 2 to Rank 1); or a signal from S2 that the name match cannot be adjudicated on the current evidence (rerank toward Rank 3’s evidence loop, accepting the deadline loss rather than forcing a decision).
