### 1. Disruption timeline

*   **T0 (Baseline, Day -30 to Day -1):** 
    *   *Operational State:* Stable, highly automated, fully compliant.
    *   *Process Variants:* Standard normative flow (Receive order $\rightarrow$ Validate authorization $\rightarrow$ Inventory reservation $\rightarrow$ Pick $\rightarrow$ Pack $\rightarrow$ Quality/release check $\rightarrow$ Dispatch $\rightarrow$ Proof of delivery $\rightarrow$ Invoice close). Minor exceptions handled via standard compliance holds and backorder queues.
    *   *Risk Posture:* Low. Traceability exceptions at 0.8%, cold-chain documentation missing at 0.4%, controlled-substance dual-control missing at 0.2%. Median cycle times range from 8h (Hospital-critical) to 36h (Wholesale-bulk).
*   **T1 (Day 0 morning):** 
    *   *Operational State:* Acute disruption. Warehouse management system (WMS) goes offline due to a cyber incident. Order-to-delivery workflow grinds to a halt.
    *   *Process Variants:* Emergency triage initiated; shift to manual workarounds, printed inventory snapshots, phone confirmations, and spreadsheet pick lists.
    *   *Risk Posture:* Severe immediate spike in operational and compliance risk.
*   **T2 (Manual Workaround Phase, Day 0 afternoon to Day 2):** 
    *   *Operational State:* Emergency manual operation. 5,600 cases processed.
    *   *Process Variants:* Dominant flow shifts through `Emergency triage` $\rightarrow$ `Manual authorization check` / `Skip system authorization check` $\rightarrow$ `Manual inventory snapshot check` $\rightarrow$ `Manual pick list` $\rightarrow$ `Manual pack` $\rightarrow$ `Temporary release control` / `Dispatch without scan` $\rightarrow$ `Phone proof of delivery` $\rightarrow$ `Spreadsheet invoice hold`. 
    *   *Risk Posture:* High risk. Traceability exceptions surged to 9.5%, cold-chain temperature documentation missing climbed to 4.8%, and controlled-substance dual-control missing rose to 2.9%. Rework rate spiked to 12.5% and cancellations to 7.8%. Pharmacy-standard fill rate collapsed to 61% while Hospital-critical fill rate was artificially maintained at 91% via triage.
*   **T3 (Partial Recovery, Day 3 to Day 6):** 
    *   *Operational State:* Transitional recovery. Order intake returns, but inventory synchronization and barcode scanning remain unreliable. 11,400 cases processed.
    *   *Process Variants:* Introduction of `Backlog prioritization` and hybrid manual-digital execution. Fitness improves to 0.82.
    *   *Risk Posture:* Moderate-high risk. Traceability exceptions drop to 5.4%, cold-chain missing to 2.3%, controlled-substance missing to 1.4%. Cycle times remain significantly elevated (p90 for Pharmacy-standard at 90h).
*   **T4 (Stabilization, Day 7 to Day 21):** 
    *   *Operational State:* Systems mostly restored, but operations choked by reconciliation and audit remediation. 35,000 cases processed.
    *   *Process Variants:* Return to standard flow combined with `Reconcile manual reservation`, `Proof-of-delivery remediation`, and a massive `Audit remediation` tail. Fitness returns to 0.90 (76% fitting traces).
    *   *Risk Posture:* Latent/hidden risk posture. Operational metrics appear normalized (Hospital median cycle time back to 8h), but an extensive audit-remediation queue persists. Compliance debt accumulated during T2/T3 must be worked off offline, risking regulatory exposure and staff burnout.

### 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

In a pharmaceutical wholesale environment, differentiating resilience from concealed noncompliance is vital for patient safety and regulatory standing:

*   **True Resilience:** Adaptations that preserve operational continuity and patient protection *without permanently compromising regulatory anchors or audit trails*. Examples include **Hospital-critical fast lane (A)** (when managed properly), **Temporary release control (F)**, and **Backlog prioritization (J)**. These actions bend rigid digital processes to protect life-safety missions while preserving core safety thresholds (e.g., pharmacist sign-off on controlled substances).
*   **Risky Shortcuts (Concealed Noncompliance):** Adaptations that achieve throughput or convenience by quietly severing regulatory controls, creating hidden liabilities that emerge only during post-incident audits. Examples include **Skip system authorization check (C)**, **Dispatch without scan (G)**, and relying on stale **Manual inventory snapshot checks (D)**. These create severe compliance gaps (such as a 9.5% traceability exception rate and missing cold-chain documentation), transferring risk from operational uptime onto the regulatory ledger.
*   **Ambiguous Adaptations:** Practices whose value depends entirely on governance and execution strictness. **Manual pick lists (E)** and **Phone proof of delivery (H)** maintain operations, but without strict contemporaneous logging, they generate massive downstream rework (12.5% rework rate) and audit exposure. 

True resilience does not sacrifice compliance for speed; it improvises control mechanisms when digital systems fail, whereas noncompliance simply discards controls under the guise of an emergency.

### 4. Recovery process model

```
[Disruption Detected / WMS Offline]
       
       
[Trigger Condition Evaluated] (IT Down > 2h) [Activate Emergency Incident Command]
                                                            
                                                            
                                                [Emergency Triage & Segmentation]
                                                 Hospital-Critical (Fast Lane)
                                                 Pharmacy-Standard (Queued)
                                                 Wholesale-Bulk (Deferred)
                                                            
                                                            
                                                [Minimum Risk-Tiered Controls]
                                                 Controlled/Cold-chain: Mandatory 100% Pharmacist Release + Temp Log
                                                 Ambient: Offline Whitelist Check + Dual-Sign Pick List
                                                            
                                                            
                                                [Manual Execution & Evidence Capture]
                                                 Paper Pick List (Dual-Initialed)
                                                 Offline Barcode/Logbook Dispatch (No unrecorded handoffs)
                                                            
                                                            
                                                [System Restoration & Backlog Prioritization]
                                                 Risk-Weighted Release Matrix
                                                 Automated Bulk Reconciliation Scripts
                                                            
                                                            
                                                [Concurrent Audit Remediation & Closure]
```

*   **Explicit Trigger Conditions:** Activation occurs automatically when the primary WMS or order-to-delivery workflow experiences an unscheduled outage exceeding 2 hours, or upon formal declaration by the Incident Commander.
*   **Triage Rules:** Incoming orders are instantly categorized at intake:
    1.  *Hospital-critical:* Immediate priority; routed to manual fast lane with pre-approved emergency customer whitelists.
    2.  *Pharmacy-standard:* Processed subject to available capacity; non-critical orders deferred if warehouse capacity drops below 50%.
    3.  *Wholesale-bulk:* Automatically deferred to preserve cold-chain and hospital capacity.
*   **Minimum Controls by Product/Customer Risk:**
    *   *Controlled Substances & Cold-Chain:* Zero bypass permitted. Dual-control pharmacist sign-off and continuous temperature data logging (manual paper logs if digital fails) are mandatory prior to pack release.
    *   *Ambient/Standard:* Offline customer eligibility check against a cached weekly master list; manual dual-initial sign-off on pick lists.
*   **Manual Evidence Capture:** Elimination of unrecorded handoffs. `Dispatch without scan` is replaced by mandatory sequential logbook entry recording courier badge ID, vehicle plate, and exact timestamp, alongside signed paper/photographic proof of delivery.
*   **Backlog Prioritization:** Upon system return (T3/T4), backlogs are processed via a deterministic, rules-based queue: Hospital-critical backlog first, followed by cold-chain/controlled substances, and finally ambient standard orders, preventing ad-hoc or biased manual sorting.
*   **Reconciliation and Audit-Remediation Flow:** Integration of automated reconciliation scripts to map manual log sheets back to WMS tables upon restoration, paired with a dedicated compliance sprint team operating concurrently with stabilization rather than trailing for weeks afterward.

### 5. Resilience indicators

To measure both operational resilience and compliance integrity during future incidents, management must track a balanced scorecard of leading and lagging indicators:

*   **Throughput & Service Protection:**
    *   *Fill Rate by Segment (Leading):* Daily percentage of ordered units fulfilled, tracked separately for Hospital-critical, Pharmacy-standard, and Wholesale-bulk (Target: >90% for hospital, >75% for pharmacy during emergency).
    *   *Order Cycle Time (Lagging):* median and p90 hours from order receipt to dispatch across customer segments.
*   **Traceability & Control Completeness:**
    *   *Traceability Exception Rate (Lagging):* Percentage of cases missing any node in the digital DFG chain (Target: <2.0% during disruption).
    *   *Mandatory Control Compliance Rate (Leading/Lagging):* Percentage of cold-chain orders with complete temperature logs and controlled substances with verified dual-control sign-offs (Target: 100% absolute adherence, zero tolerance for bypass).
*   **Operational Friction & Rework:**
    *   *Rework / Correction Case Rate (Lagging):* Percentage of orders requiring repacking, rechecking, or manual correction (Target: <5.0%).
    *   *Audit-Remediation Backlog Age (Lagging):* Number of unresolved compliance exceptions lingering past system restoration (Target: zero exceptions older than 5 business days post-recovery).
*   **Fairness & Equity:**
    *   *Segment Fill-Rate Variance Ratio (Leading):* Ratio of Hospital-critical fill rate to Pharmacy-standard fill rate, ensuring triage does not starve standard pharmacies below equitable emergency thresholds (Target: Ratio $\le 1.3$).

### 7. Management trade-off statement

Management must explicitly accept that maintaining absolute regulatory compliance and patient safety during a cyber incident will require sacrificing raw operational speed and accepting a temporary degradation in non-critical throughput (e.g., lower fill rates for wholesale-bulk and pharmacy-standard segments). Management must **not** accept unrecorded dispatches, bypassed controlled-substance checks, or undocumented cold-chain handoffs under any circumstances, as the resulting regulatory liability and patient safety risks far outweigh short-term delivery gains. This recommendation would change only if statutory emergency decrees explicitly grant blanket liability immunity and suspend pharmaceutical distribution traceability laws during declared cyber emergencies—an exemption that does not currently exist.
