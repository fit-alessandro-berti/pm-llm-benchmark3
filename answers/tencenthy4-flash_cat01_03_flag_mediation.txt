## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

I treat the flag as part of a **policy bundle**, not as a magical causal switch. `do(flag=0)` while leaving the score visible is not the same as suppressing the flag display, removing the priority lane, removing mandatory review, adding review capacity, or changing escalation. Primary frameworks: **controlled direct effects** for explicitly manipulated policy components and **interventional indirect effects / separable effects** for stage-mediated pathways. I do **not** rely on natural direct/indirect effects as primary estimands because review findings and hidden risk are post-flag confounders, and shared queues violate single-unit stability.

Time-ordered process-mechanism diagram:

```text
At registration:
  U_underlying_risk, U_hidden_risk, complexity, arrival_load, U_queue_states
        -> risk_score -> flag
        -> policy bundle: priority_lane + manual-review rule + escalation rule

After registration:
  priority_lane -> triage_wait -> triage_start -> triage_service -> triage_done

  triage_done -> manual_review occurrence
       selected by: flag, complexity, U_hidden_risk, U_reviewer_behavior, triage_done

  if manual_review occurs:
       manual_review -> manual_queue_wait -> manual_service -> U_review_finding

  if escalation occurs:
       U_review_finding, flag, complexity, U_hidden_risk -> escalation
       escalation -> escalation_queue_wait -> senior_service

  last completed stage -> residual_to_close -> close

  manual_service, senior_service, flag, risk_score, complexity, U_hidden_risk
        -> decision_outcome
```

Interference arises because each flagged case sent to review or escalation increases `U_manual_queue_state` or `U_senior_queue_state`, changing later cases’ waits, including unflagged cases. Exposure-induced confounding occurs after triage: the flag affects review occurrence, but review findings and hidden risk also affect escalation and decision_outcome. Treatment-confounder feedback is plausible because queue length and reviewer workload influence subsequent routing, while prior routing creates that workload. Therefore estimates below are cohort-level policy/interventional analogues, not identified natural effects.

The observed pattern is consistent with a flag bundle that buys time at triage and loses it later. Flagged cases start triage about 2.3 hours sooner and have slightly shorter triage service, but far longer total cycles. All flagged cases enter manual review, versus 4/10 unflagged cases, and flagged manual waits are much longer among reviewed cases. Escalation is also more common among flagged cases; escalated flagged cases have median cycle time 21.9 h versus 8.8 h without escalation. Complexity tracks both flag and duration: flagged cases are medium/high complexity only. These contrasts are not automatically causal. The flag reflects an imperfect score, humans can route unflagged high-risk cases, load is coarse, and queues are shared. Manual-wait comparisons also condition on receiving review, a post-flag selection event.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Is there a local causal effect of being flagged near the score threshold? | Regression-discontinuity/local comparison around the flag cutoff, estimating jumps in triage timing, review, escalation, and cycle time | risk_score, complexity, hour-of-day, arrival_load; narrow bandwidth | Score not manipulated; threshold enforced; covariates smooth at cutoff | Pre-treatment variables and outcome trends should be smooth except at cutoff | Jump in complexity/hidden-risk proxies at cutoff, or jump in quality outcomes without process jump |
| Does priority triage remain beneficial after accounting for queues? | Queue-state-aware survival/time-to-event model for triage start/done using reconstructed queue lengths at arrival and stage transitions | queue length at arrival, triage resource, hour, complexity, score, load | Queue lengths measured accurately; no omitted time-varying urgency | Flag advantage should shrink when triage queue length is low or after conditioning on queue state | Flag gap persists at equal queue states, suggesting human differential treatment or hidden risk |
| What is the total effect of P2, P3, or P4? | Policy-change or randomized encouragement design, randomized by half-day/arrival block; compare P0 with one policy at a time | load stratum, day, expected arrival volume, complexity distribution | Randomization affects outcomes only through policy; block-level interference handled | Balance checks on pre-policy covariates; stage-specific effect pattern should match mechanism | No triage/review/cycle response despite strong assignment uptake, or large spillover contaminating blocks |
| Is manual occurrence or manual waiting the main mediator? | Mediation-oriented g-computation using interventional indirect effects: fix triage, shift review occurrence; then shift manual_queue_wait conditional on review | risk_score, complexity, load, triage timing, queue state before review | Sequential ignorability for measured confounders; no unmeasured review finding | Manual occurrence intervention should change review volume; P3 should mainly change wait, not occurrence | P3 reduces wait but cycle time barely changes, implying escalation/residual dominates |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 current system | Fastest observed triage for flagged cases | High manual and senior queue burden; large flagged delays | Flagged cycle much longer despite triage speed | High process compliance, but possible over-review/over-escalation | High: flagged routing congests shared queues | Direct descriptive evidence from log | Benchmark only; do not scale unchanged |
| P1 suppress flag display | Triage speed may persist if score visible; otherwise less priority separation | Likely less flag-triggered review/escalation, but human risk judgment remains | Could shorten cycles if downstream routing falls | Higher if compliance requires flag response or hidden risk is missed | Lower if fewer flag-driven escalations | Weak; no observed variant | Not first pilot unless goal is to test flag salience |
| P2 priority triage, no mandatory manual review | Keeps observed triage acceleration | Large immediate reduction in manual queue pressure | Likely largest cycle reduction for many flagged cases | Highest concern: some true high-risk cases skip review | Lower review spillover; possible senior queue shift if reviewers refer selectively | Moderate: manual occurrence strongly separates flagged cycles | Test only with quality audit; not preferred first if compliance critical |
| P3 protected flagged review capacity | Triage unchanged | Manual queue wait should fall for flagged cases | Moderate-to-large reduction while preserving review | Lowest process-compliance risk; quality still needs audit | Low if capacity is additive; high if taken from unflagged reviewers | Moderate: manual waiting is largest visible delay | Preferred limited pilot |
| P4 change escalation criteria/capacity | Triage and manual review unchanged | Senior queue wait or escalation volume falls | Useful only for escalated subset; smaller overall effect if review wait dominates | Stricter criteria risk under-escalation; capacity increase may induce demand | Senior team shared by all cases | Moderate: escalation wait large but service shorter | Secondary pilot add-on if senior queue remains binding |

Pilot selection: **P3**. Eligibility: all flagged registrations during 4–6 week windows, plus all unflagged cases in the same windows for spillover measurement. Randomization/assignment: randomize half-day blocks to P3 versus P0, stratified by expected load; all cases in a block receive the same regime to reduce interference. Queue-capacity guardrail: add one dedicated reviewer for flagged cases in P3 blocks without removing existing reviewer capacity; pause if unflagged manual p90 wait rises >30% or >1 hour. Primary outcomes: flagged arrival-to-close and manual_queue_wait. Secondary: triage wait/service, review occurrence, escalation occurrence/wait/service, unflagged delays, audit-based decision correctness/compliance. Minimum data: all stage start/end timestamps, resource IDs, queue length at arrival and stage entry/exit, risk_score, complexity, load, reason codes, and audited true-risk outcome. Stopping: stop for increased quality misses, compliance breaches, senior-queue deterioration, missed hidden high-risk cases, or timestamp/resource data-integrity failure.

# 2. Reshape to PM4Py-compatible event log
# -----------------------------
events = []

def add_event(row, activity, end_col, start_col=None, resource_col=None):
    end = row[end_col]
    if pd.isna(end):
        return  # absent stage: do not fabricate an event
    start = end if start_col is None or pd.isna(row[start_col]) else row[start_col]
    resource = row[resource_col] if resource_col and pd.notna(row[resource_col]) else None
    events.append({
        "case:concept:name": row["case"],
        "concept:name": activity,
        "time:timestamp": end,
        "start_timestamp": start,
        "org:resource": resource,
        "flag_num": row["flag"],
        "flag": row["flag"],
        "risk_score": row["risk_score"],
        "complexity": row["complexity"],
        "arrival_load": row["arrival_load"],
        "outcome": row["outcome"],
    })

for _, row in wide.iterrows():
    # Register and Close are observed instantaneous milestones, not service stages.
    add_event(row, "Register", "arrival")
    add_event(row, "Triage", "triage_done", "triage_start", "triage_resource")
    add_event(row, "Manual Review", "manual_done", "manual_start", "review_resource")
    # Escalation resource is not recorded; do not invent one.
    add_event(row, "Escalation", "escalation_done", "escalation_start", None)
    add_event(row, "Close", "close")

events_df = (
    pd.DataFrame(events)
    .sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "concept:name"])
    .reset_index(drop=True)
)

)

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk:** If audited true risk shows that unflagged cases receiving manual review/escalation were systematically higher-risk than flagged cases with similar scores, the apparent flag-driven downstream delay may be case-mix rather than policy response.

2. **Queue interference:** If unflagged cases do not slow when flagged review/escalation volume rises, the shared-queue mechanism weakens; protected capacity would then be less justified.

3. **Decision quality:** If P2 or stricter P4 substantially shortens cycles without increasing missed high-risk cases or compliance breaches, a less review-intensive policy could dominate P3.

4. **Timestamp semantics:** If triage_start, manual_start, or escalation_start are entered retroactively or reflect request time rather than actual work start, the waiting/service decomposition is invalid and the “fast triage, slow downstream” story may reverse.

5. **Positivity or lack of overlap:** If near-threshold or otherwise comparable flagged and unflagged cases are too rare, or if reviewer behavior makes some policy regimes impossible for certain risk/complexity strata, policy contrasts are not identified.

6. **Reviewer behavior change:** If reviewers under P3 reduce scrutiny because flagged cases are perceived as “already handled,” or compensate by escalating more, added review capacity may not improve total cycle or quality as predicted.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed:** Flagged cases have much shorter arrival-to-triage-start median time, 0.20 vs 2.50 h, and slightly shorter triage service, but far longer total cycles, 16.45 vs 5.75 h. All flagged cases had manual review; six were escalated, versus four and two unflagged cases. Manual waiting is the largest visible gap, 6.75 vs 1.75 h among reviewed cases. Escalation adds roughly seven hours of senior-queue waiting when it occurs.

**Plausible mechanism:** The flag operates as a policy bundle. It buys speed in the priority triage lane, then routes most cases into a shared manual queue and sometimes into a scarce senior queue. Manual-review waiting and escalation waiting plausibly offset the initial triage gain. Complexity and high load amplify this pattern.

**What remains unidentified:** The flag is not underlying risk. Hidden risk, complexity, reviewer judgment, and queue state jointly affect routing, waiting, and outcomes. Because queues are shared and review findings are post-flag confounders, natural direct/indirect effects are not identified. Approve/Reject is not decision quality.

**Most useful next evidence:** A cohort-randomized P3 pilot with protected flagged-review capacity, audited decisions, and reconstructed queue states would directly test whether manual waiting is the bottleneck while preserving review/compliance and measuring spillovers.
