## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

Conformance is a feasibility gate. Compare the remaining plans by how much they change the chance of **lawful execution by the deadline**, the risk of avoidable harm to the customer and the effects on other cases. The €20k–€35k is the value of timely execution when it can lawfully occur. An incremental deadline benefit would multiply that range by the change in the probability of lawful, timely execution. The available predictions do not estimate that change. The 0.48–0.64 normal-processing breach estimate and the 0.55–0.75 ultimate approval estimate describe different outcomes. They should not be multiplied to estimate on-time approval or transferred unchanged to an expedited plan.

The €15k–€80k range for false rejection or prolonged hold makes an unsupported rejection potentially more costly than missing the deadline. Count customer harm and deadline loss separately only where they represent distinct losses. For a lawful hold with a credible prospect of resolving the match, weigh that harm against the value of the additional evidence and the time it will take to receive it.

Unlawful execution could cause €100k–€500k in regulatory harm, alongside sanctions enforcement, audit consequences and reputational damage. Comparing the endpoints as if they were expected values would obscure both probability and the severity of the downside. The 0.46 is a match-confidence score with no stated calibration to the probability of a true match or an unlawful payment. Independent review and better screening evidence address different uncertainties. Where an early S4 slot can produce a timely disposition, D can improve the evidence without imposing the S2 interruption. Where waiting removes the credible chance of timely execution, C buys time at a portfolio cost that must be weighed explicitly.
