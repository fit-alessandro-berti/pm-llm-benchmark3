## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

1. **Upstream Data Quality Dependencies:**
   * **A5 (Missing-document checklist) $\rightarrow$ A6 (Financial normalization) $\rightarrow$ A8 (Committee pack):** Financial document review suffers a 24% exception rate and triggers 3,300 missing-document requests (with 2,800 looping back in the DFG and a 52% repeat rate). Automating normalization (A6) without fixing intake completeness (A5) will result in extraction failures on incomplete/low-quality files. Similarly, A8 (Committee packaging) is a synchronization node that requires reconciled KYC, normalized financials, credit notes, and valuations; automating A8 before A5 and A6 yields "garbage-in/garbage-out."
   * **A1 (Auto-registration) $\rightarrow$ A2 (Classification) $\rightarrow$ A7 (Collateral triage):** Fast registration (A1) without accurate classification (A2, which has an 8% error rate) misroutes Equipment finance (71% valuation requirement) and Working-capital (18% requirement) cases, delaying A7 collateral triggers.

2. **Legal and Compliance Controls:**
   * **A4 (Entity-mismatch) & A3 (KYC pre-classifier) $\rightarrow$ A9 (Contract generation) $\rightarrow$ A10 (E-signature) $\rightarrow$ A11 (Loan activation):** Customer signing cannot legally precede KYC clearance, yet provisional signing occurs in 6.5% of cases, and Resolve entity mismatch bypasses KYC re-verification to go directly to Contract preparation in 380 cases. Furthermore, Contract preparation has an 8.5% remediation loop (14% for Credit-line renewals) due to mismatches with Committee decisions. Therefore, A10 and A11 cannot be safely deployed until A9 enforces hard system blocks requiring clean KYC (aided by A4) and reconciled Committee terms.
   * **Four-Eyes Principle:** High-exposure SME cases have a 5.8% violation rate during month-end rushes where the same person completes Credit analysis and Committee packaging. A8 or A9 must embed role-based access controls (RBAC) checking user IDs across these steps.

3. **Human Escalation Capacity:**
   * **A3 (KYC pre-classifier) vs. A4 (Entity-mismatch assistant):** Compliance specialists have only 3 FTEs, with work heavily concentrated on two seniors. A3 processes 11,500 cases; if tuned conservatively to avoid false negatives, it will escalate a high volume of hits to those two specialists, worsening the 31.0h median wait at Resolve entity mismatch. A4 must be deployed first (or alongside A3) to expand the capacity of the 3-FTE specialist pool and reduce the 38% rework rate (1,160 loops back to KYC check).
   * **Upstream Speed vs. Credit Analysts (9 FTE):** Concurrency exists between KYC check and Financial document review. Accelerating front-end intake (A1, A2, A5) increases the arrival rate of clean cases at Normalize financial statements (20.0h wait) and Credit analysis (28.0h wait, 110m service time). A6 is a strict prerequisite to free up analyst capacity (5,200 cases $\times$ 75m) so they can absorb the smoother inflow.

4. **External Valuation Dependency:**
   * **Wait for external valuation (58.0h median wait, 19% late):** Because Committee packaging waits for valuation (1,980 DFG paths from Wait for external valuation $\rightarrow$ Committee packaging), internal credit speedups for Equipment finance (71% valuation rate) will still stall at Committee packaging unless A7 triggers valuation requests earlier (e.g., immediately after A2 classification rather than waiting for Credit analysis to finish, as shown by the current DFG `Credit analysis -> Collateral valuation request: 2400`).

5. **Core-Banking Integration Constraints:**
   * IT limits deep core-banking integrations to **two modules in the first six months**. Deep integration is required for modules that write/mutate core system-of-record entities: **A1** (customer/loan registration), **A9** (binding contract terms/control locks), and **A11** (loan activation/disbursement). Modules like **A4**, **A5**, **A6**, and **A7** operate primarily in workflow, document management, portal, or compliance side-systems and require only lightweight API/read integrations in H1.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

### Portfolio 1: Quick-Win / High-Frequency Portfolio (A1, A2, A5, A10)
* **Modules:** A1 (Auto-registration), A2 (Product/exposure classification), A5 (Missing-document checklist/portal), A10 (E-signature & reminders).
* **What it optimizes:** Front-end intake speed, back-office labor reduction (12 FTE pool), and end-of-pipe customer signing turnaround (44h wait). It is politically visible because it touches almost all 12,000 cases.
* **What it sacrifices:** Ignores the true critical-path bottlenecks (3-FTE Compliance specialists at 31h wait; 9-FTE Credit analysts at 20h–28h waits) and fails the Compliance mandate unless A2 is heavily rule-bound. Worse, accelerating intake (A1/A2) and signing (A10) without fixing middle-office capacity or contract controls will flood Credit analysts and increase the 6.5% illegal provisional signing rate.

### Portfolio 2: Bottleneck, Synchronization & Control Portfolio (A5, A6, A4, A9) — *Recommended*
* **Modules:** A5 (Missing-document checklist/portal), A6 (Financial-statement extraction/normalization), A4 (Entity-mismatch resolution assistant), A9 (Contract generation with control-rule checks).
* **What it optimizes:** End-to-end cycle time across parallel branches, critical-path synchronization before Committee/Contracting, scarce specialist capacity (Credit analysts and Compliance specialists), and hard regulatory controls at Contract preparation.
* **What it sacrifices:** Forgoes immediate automation of the highest-frequency step (A1: 11,880 cases) and leaves external valuation routing (A7: 58h wait on 2,400 cases) for Year 2. However, Back office (A1's pool) already has <65% utilization after 15:00, making A1 labor savings non-critical.

### Portfolio 3: Compliance-First Portfolio (A2, A3, A4, A9)
* **Modules:** A2 (Classification recommender), A3 (KYC hit pre-classifier), A4 (Entity-mismatch assistant), A9 (Contract generation with control checks).
* **What it optimizes:** Regulatory risk reduction, correct exposure routing (preventing missed Committee reviews), KYC auditability, and elimination of provisional signing and contract-term mismatches.
* **What it sacrifices:** Completely ignores the financial analysis branch (`Financial document review -> Request missing documents -> Normalize financial statements -> Credit analysis`). Because KYC and Financial document review run concurrently and synchronize at Credit analysis/Committee packaging, speeding up the KYC branch alone will leave cases waiting on the 18h/20h/28h credit analyst queues and 52% missing-document loops.

## 4. Expected process-level effects

### Portfolio 1: Quick-Win / High-Frequency (A1, A2, A5, A10)
* **Cycle time & Waiting time:** Reduces early waits (`Register application` 1.5h, `Classify product` 2.0h) and late signing waits (`Customer signing` 44.0h). However, end-to-end cycle time improves marginally because cases pile up at `Normalize financial statements` (20.0h), `Credit analysis` (28.0h), and `Resolve entity mismatch` (31.0h).
* **Rework:** A5 cuts the `Financial document review -> Request missing documents` loop (3,300 cases, 52% repeated), and A2 reduces the 8% classification error. However, the 38% rework at `Resolve entity mismatch` (1,160 loops to KYC) and 12% rework at `Contract preparation` remain untouched.
* **Handovers & Resource demand:** Frees up Back office (12 FTE), which already has excess afternoon capacity (<65% utilization after 15:00). Shifts a faster, cleaner wave of cases onto the 9-FTE Credit analysts (via `Financial document review -> Normalize financial statements: 5050`), severely overloading them.
* **Control violations:** **Worsens.** A10 accelerates `Contract preparation -> Customer signing` (7,300 cases) without A9's control gates, increasing the risk of the 6.5% provisional signing violation (signing before KYC clearance) and pushing unverified contracts into `Remediate control issue` (650 cases).

### Portfolio 2: Bottleneck, Synchronization & Control (A5, A6, A4, A9)
* **Cycle time & Waiting time:** Dramatically reduces end-to-end cycle time by attacking both concurrent middle-office branches:
  1. *Credit branch:* A5 + A6 compress the 18.0h wait at `Financial document review`, the 8.0h wait at `Request missing documents`, and the 20.0h wait + 75m service time at `Normalize financial statements`. This directly reduces the 28.0h queue before `Credit analysis` and the 26.0h synchronization wait before `Committee packaging`.
  2. *KYC branch:* A4 targets `Resolve entity mismatch` (on the critical path in 82% of cases when present), slashing its 31.0h median wait and 55m service time.
  3. *Contracting:* A9 cuts the 22.0h wait and 35m service time at `Contract preparation` (7,600 cases).
* **Rework:** Directly targets the three worst rework loops in the DFG:
  * `Request missing documents -> Financial document review` (2,800 loops, 52% repeated) via A5.
  * `Resolve entity mismatch -> KYC check` (1,160 loops, 38% rework) via A4.
  * `Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue` (650 cases, 8.5% overall / 14% Credit-line renewal) via A9.
* **Handovers & Resource demand:**
  * Relieves the **9-FTE Credit analyst** pool: A6 saves a major portion of the 75m normalization work across 5,200 cases, preventing A5's cleaner document inflow from overloading analysts during `Credit analysis` (8,900 cases, 110m).
  * Relieves the **3-FTE Compliance specialist** bottleneck (specifically the two over-relied-upon seniors) via A4, smoothing handovers from Compliance ops.
  * Smooths the **4-FTE Legal ops** month-end overload via A9, as upstream KYC (A4) and credit artifacts arrive more predictably and contract drafting is automated.
* **Control violations:** **Strongly improves.** A9 embeds hard preventive controls at `Contract preparation`: it blocks contract generation if KYC is uncleared (closing the `Resolve entity mismatch -> Contract preparation: 380` bypass and eliminating the 6.5% provisional signing rate), auto-populates approved `Committee decision` terms (fixing the 8.5%/14% contract mismatch loop), and checks that `Credit analysis` and `Committee packaging` were completed by different user IDs on High-exposure SME cases (stopping the 5.8% four-eyes month-end violation before contracts can be issued).

### Portfolio 3: Compliance-First (A2, A3, A4, A9)
* **Cycle time & Waiting time:** Reduces waits in the KYC branch (`KYC check` 4.0h, `Resolve entity mismatch` 31.0h) and `Contract preparation` (22.0h). However, because `Committee packaging` (26.0h wait) and `Contract preparation` must wait for the credit branch, overall cycle time remains dictated by the unaddressed 18.0h, 20.0h, and 28.0h waits in `Financial document review`, `Normalize financial statements`, and `Credit analysis`.
* **Rework:** Reduces KYC rework (11% and 38%), misrouting (8%), and contract remediation (12%), but leaves the massive 24% document review exception rate and 52% repeated missing-document loop intact.
* **Handovers & Resource demand:** Risk of A3 (KYC pre-classifier on 11,500 cases) generating false-positive escalations that offset A4's relief on the 3-FTE Compliance specialists. Leaves the 9-FTE Credit analysts completely unassisted. Legal ops (4 FTE) still faces starvation early in the month and overload at month-end because the credit bottleneck remains unaddressed.
* **Control violations:** **Strongly improves** (eliminates misclassified exposure paths via A2, secures KYC via A3/A4, and locks down contracts/four-eyes/KYC clearance via A9), but achieves this at the expense of operational throughput.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Automation Risk | Affected Modules | Compensating Control & Governance Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lack of Auditability** | A4, A6, A9 (and future A2, A3) | **Immutable Audit Log:** Every automated action must write a structured log entry containing: (1) model/rule version, (2) raw input snapshot, (3) machine suggestion/extraction confidence score, (4) human reviewer ID, (5) override reason code (if modified), and (6) final timestamped decision. |
| **False Negatives / Extraction Errors** | A6 (low-quality PDFs), A4 (entity resolution) | **Confidence Thresholds & Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop (HITL):** A6 highlights low-confidence OCR/normalized line items in amber, requiring mandatory Credit analyst sign-off before feeding the credit model. For A4, the assistant only aggregates registry/UBO evidence and proposes a match; one of the 3 Compliance specialists must execute final sign-off. |
| **False Positives & Escalation Flooding** | A4 (and future A3) | **Triage Queuing & Calibration:** Configure A4 to group related entity mismatches per application rather than firing multiple alerts. Monitor escalation rates weekly to ensure the two senior Compliance specialists are not flooded. |
| **Outdated Legal/Control Rule Library** | A9 (Contract generation) | **Rule Governance & Regression Testing:** Establish joint Legal ops + Compliance sign-off for all A9 template/rule updates. Run automated regression tests on historical `Credit-line renewal` (14% error rate) and `High-exposure SME` cases before pushing rule changes to production. |
| **Four-Eyes Principle Violations (5.8% at month-end)** | A9 (and upstream workflow) | **Hard System Segregation of Duties (SoD) Gate:** Program A9's control check (and the workflow transition into `Committee packaging`) to query the system log: if `User_ID(Credit analysis) == User_ID(Committee packaging)` for a High-exposure SME case, block contract generation and route to an independent senior analyst for re-verification. |
| **Provisional Signing (6.5%) & Premature Contract Prep (380 cases)** | A4, A9 | **Hard State-Machine Prerequisite Lock:** Remove the workflow permission allowing `Resolve entity mismatch -> Contract preparation` (380 cases) without a verified `KYC check = Cleared` token. Configure A9 so contracts cannot be generated or dispatched to `Customer signing` unless KYC status is `Cleared` and `Committee decision` terms are locked. |
| **Customer Friction & RM Resistance** | A5 (Missing-document portal) | **Dynamic Product-Specific Checklists & RM Co-Design:** Tailor A5 checklists by product (Working-capital vs. Equipment finance) and segment (Micro vs. SME) so customers only see relevant requests. Give RMs a pre-submission preview dashboard and allow an "RM Assisted Upload" mode so RMs feel empowered rather than bypassed. |
| **Model Drift** | A6, A4 | **Monthly Override & Rework Monitoring:** Track analyst override rates on A6 normalized fields and post-A4 KYC rework rates. If override rates exceed 15% or downstream `Normalize financial statements` rework rises, trigger model retraining. |

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

**Portfolio Selected:** **Portfolio 2 (A5, A6, A4, A9)**.  
**IT Constraint Alignment:** Only **A9** requires deep core-banking integration (reading approved credit limits/pricing and writing contract/control locks). **A5**, **A6**, and **A4** use portal, document-management, and compliance-workbench integrations, staying well within the limit of $\le 2$ deep core-banking integrations in the first six months (leaving one deep-integration slot as buffer or for core workflow SoD locks).

### Phase 1 (Months 1–3): Intake Stabilization & Specialist Bottleneck Relief
* **Modules Deployed:** **A5** (Missing-document checklist & portal nudging) + **A4** (Entity-mismatch resolution assistant).
* **Core-Banking Integrations Used:** 0 of 2 allowed in H1 (lightweight portal and KYC workbench integrations only).
* **Rationale:** A5 immediately stops RMs from sending incomplete instructions and cuts the 3,300 missing-document loops before we automate financial normalization. Simultaneously, A4 relieves the 3-FTE Compliance specialist bottleneck (31.0h wait, 82% critical path) and starts cleaning up KYC before A9 is introduced.
* **Monitoring Metrics:**
  * `Financial document review -> Request missing documents` frequency (baseline: 3,300) and repeat loop rate (baseline: 52%).
  * `Resolve entity mismatch` median waiting time (baseline: 31.0h), mean service time (baseline: 55m), and rework loop `Resolve entity mismatch -> KYC check` (baseline: 38% / 1,160 cases).
  * RM portal adoption rate and customer drop-off rate.
* **Gate Criteria to Phase 2:** Missing-document repeat rate drops below 25%; `Resolve entity mismatch` median wait drops below 15h; 100% audit trail capture verified on A4 specialist decisions.

### Phase 2 (Months 4–6): Control Hardening & Legal Ops Smoothing
* **Modules Deployed:** **A9** (Contract generation with control-rule checks, including automated four-eyes and KYC-clearance gates).
* **Core-Banking Integrations Used:** 1 of 2 allowed in H1 (deep integration with core banking & committee decision records to pull approved terms and enforce state locks).
* **Rationale:** Satisfies Compliance's mandate in H1. With A4 already stabilizing KYC entity mismatches, A9 can strictly block the 380 premature `Resolve entity mismatch -> Contract preparation` bypasses, eliminate the 6.5% provisional signing rate, and eradicate the 8.5% contract remediation loop (14% for Credit-line renewals) without bringing the process to a halt.
* **Monitoring Metrics:**
  * Provisional signing rate (baseline: 6.5% $\rightarrow$ target: 0%).
  * High-exposure SME four-eyes violation rate (baseline: 5.8% $\rightarrow$ target: 0%).
  * `Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue` loop rate (baseline: 8.5% overall, 14% renewals $\rightarrow$ target: <2%).
  * Legal ops (4 FTE) month-end queue time (baseline: 22.0h median wait).
* **Gate Criteria to Phase 3:** Zero unblocked provisional signings or four-eyes violations in month-end audit; cleaner document inflow from A5 confirmed ready for automated extraction.

### Phase 3 (Months 7–12): Credit Analyst Capacity Unlock & Synchronization
* **Modules Deployed:** **A6** (Financial-statement extraction and normalization).
* **Core-Banking Integrations Used:** 1 deep integration (connects normalized financial spreads to the internal credit scoring/analysis engine).
* **Rationale:** By Month 7, A5 has standardized incoming financial documents, minimizing the risk of A6 failing on incomplete files. Deploying A6 frees up the central 9-FTE Credit analyst pool (saving up to 75m across 5,200 cases and cutting the 20.0h wait), enabling them to process `Credit analysis` (28.0h wait) and `Committee packaging` (26.0h wait) much faster.
* **Monitoring Metrics:**
  * `Normalize financial statements` mean service time (baseline: 75m), median wait (baseline: 20.0h), and rework rate (baseline: 16%).
  * Downstream `Credit analysis` median wait (baseline: 28.0h) and `Committee packaging` median wait (baseline: 26.0h).
  * Credit analyst override rate on A6 extracted fields.
* **Year-End Gate Criteria (for Year 2 Portfolio - e.g., A7, A2, A8):** Analyst override rate on A6 <15%; end-to-end median cycle time reduced by >30% without increasing Credit analyst overtime or control exceptions.

## 7. Final recommendation

I recommend **Portfolio 2 (A5, A6, A4, A9)**, rolled out in three phases over the first year.

### Why Senior Management Should Reject Automating the Highest-Frequency Activity First (A1)
Senior management’s instinct to automate **A1 (Register application: 11,880 cases, 8 min service time)** is a classic local-optimization trap:
1. **Illusion of Bottleneck vs. True Utilization:** While Back office (12 FTE) has the most visible queue by case count, process-mining and resource data show its median wait is only **1.5h**, its rework is low (**4%**), and **utilization drops below 65% after 15:00**. Saving 8 minutes per case in a resource pool that already has afternoon slack yields zero improvement in end-to-end loan origination time.
2. **Exacerbating Downstream Bottlenecks:** In our process network, `Register application` feeds directly into middle-office branches that are severely constrained: **Compliance specialists (only 3 FTE, 31.0h wait, 38% rework)** and **Credit analysts (9 FTE, facing 18.0h, 20.0h, and 28.0h waits across document review, normalization, and credit analysis)**. Automating A1 simply pushes 11,880 cases faster into a middle-office traffic jam, shifting waiting time from a 1.5h front-office queue to a 28h–31h middle-office queue.
3. **Wasting Scarce IT Capacity:** A1 requires a deep core-banking integration to create customer/application records. Using one of our two H1 core-banking integration slots on a 1.5h wait starves high-impact control and bottleneck modules like **A9**.

### Why Portfolio 2 (A5, A6, A4, A9) is the Optimal Trade-Off
* **Synchronization & Parallel Branch Balance:** Because `KYC check` and `Financial document review` run concurrently and synchronize before `Credit analysis` (4,200 from KYC; 5,000 from Normalization) and `Committee packaging`, optimizing only one branch fails to move the critical path. Portfolio 2 accelerates **both** branches: **A4** unblocks the KYC critical path (present on the critical path 82% of the time it occurs, with a 31.0h wait), while **A5 + A6** break the 52% missing-document loop and slash the 75m/20.0h financial normalization burden on our 9 Credit analysts.
* **Compliance & Control Integrity by Design:** Rather than treating compliance as an afterthought or building a reactive remediation tool (A12), **A9** embeds preventive controls directly into `Contract preparation`. It eliminates the **6.5% illegal provisional signing rate**, blocks the **5.8% month-end four-eyes violations**, and stops the **8.5% contract-term mismatch loop** (14% in renewals), while relieving the 4-FTE Legal ops team during month-end peaks.
* **Defensible Year-2 Runway:** Once Portfolio 2 stabilizes document intake, financial normalization, KYC resolution, and contract controls in Year 1, the bank can safely deploy **A7 (Collateral valuation triage)**, **A2 (Classification)**, and **A8 (Committee pack auto-assembly)** in Year 2 on top of clean, compliant upstream artifacts.
