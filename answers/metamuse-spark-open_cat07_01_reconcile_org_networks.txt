# 5. Recommendation

**What should be changed immediately (low-regret, no headcount removal)**

* Document and instrument the exception-routing rule set currently in Mira's head: extract recent `Bot_Rules -> Mira -> {target}` decisions, codify into a visible routing matrix in the workflow system with explicit reason code (missing-info, medical complexity, legal exposure, complaint).
* Add lightweight four-eyes for high-risk actions she monopolizes: `Reopen Claim` and `Assign Specialist -> {Omar/Priya}` require secondary confirmation (Alice_TeamLead or alternate coordinator) logged as distinct event to restore segregation; does not require new hires.
* Create shared exception queue dashboard (replacing personal inbox) displaying age, flag, last handler, waiting-since-Mira-inbound to make bottleneck visible and delegable; include Bot_Ingest/Bot_Rules volumes and Vendor SLAs so automation performance remains contextualised.

**What should be monitored (weekly metrics, 4-8 weeks)**

* Tail SLAs: p50/p90 cycle time broken out by `exception_flag True/False`, `Reopen` and `Escalate Complaint` cohorts, with separate vendor waiting component.
* Router workload: handovers/day into Mira, queue length pre-Mira, `Temp -> Mira` escalation rate, subcontracting return rate, and fairness of onward distribution (events/case sent to each adjuster/specialist).
* Resilience: working-together overlap after introducing deputy, `discover_handover_of_work_network` betweenness trend (should decline from 0.77 toward ~0.4-0.5 as backup gains edges), and recurrence of p90 spike after any absence.
* Compliance trace: % of assignments with documented reason/target rationale.

**What should not be changed until more evidence is available**

* **Do not remove or reassign Mira_Coordinator** — operation depends on her broker buffer. Any elimination without replacement would recreate the 8.4->13.9 day tail delay at higher exception volume and push unresolved work onto specialists and `Temp -> Vendor_Verify` loops, increasing formal escalations as warned. If long-term automation desired, first run pilot where Bot_Rules proposes recommendation and Mira confirms, measuring accuracy of auto-routing before decommissioning.
* Do not permanently rebalance team leads' remit (e.g., force all triage via Alice/Bruno) nor restructure vendor contracts; Vendor_Verify/Pay correctly absorb bulk volume (1550/2850 cases) and their 1-2h service is not the exception bottleneck.
* Do not impose productivity quotas on short Mira events — many are appropriate rapid triage; penalizing event count would incentivize slower handling and distort SNA metrics.

Longer-term, after metrics stabilize, evaluate staffing: either train a second coordinator (job-shadow rotation with Alice/Bruno) or semi-automate low-discretion missing-info branch while preserving human broker for medical/legal/complaint judgement.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira_Coordinator looks central and peripheral at once because she acts as a dispatcher, not a teammate. In handover terms she connects bots, both branch teams, medical/legal specialists and temporary staff — so most exception cases pass through her. She rarely works *with* anyone on the same case for long, which is why co-work scores are low. She touches only 22% of claims but 64-71% of reopens and complaints, handling quick routing decisions that downstream specialists then spend hours resolving. When she was absent, routine claims were fine but the slowest exception cases jumped from 8 to 14 days, showing a single-person dependency for complex work. There is no evidence of misuse, but combining intake, reassignment and reopen rights in one person creates control risk and queueing delay. We recommend immediately documenting her routing logic, adding a second check on reopens and a shared queue, monitoring tail delays and fairness weekly, and not removing her role until that logic is safely backed up or gradually automated.
