## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### 1.1 Stipulated Facts (Evidentiary Ground Truth)
The following facts are established by objective documentary and transactional records:
1. **Codified Baseline**: Compliance Standard CS-9 (effective 2025-01-01) is the sole formally promulgated and controlled enterprise standard. It mandates that every supplier activation requires a `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` prior to activation, and requires a `Manual_Procurement_Approval` executed by a human distinct from the requester (Four-Eyes Principle / Segregation of Duties).
2. **Pilot Parameters (PD-3)**: The steering committee authorized Automation Pilot PD-3 with an explicit temporal window of 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31. Eligibility was strictly restricted to LOW-risk German (`DE`) suppliers within an approved corporate group, requiring pre-activation `Sanctions_Check` and automated master-data validation, while permitting `Quality_Review` within two business days post-provisional activation.
3. **Emergency Directive Parameters (FD-7)**: Flood Emergency Directive FD-7 was promulgated with a strict lifespan of 2026-07-10 00:00 through 2026-07-12 23:59, scoped strictly to critical flood-relief suppliers to prevent plant shutdown. It permitted provisional activation prior to `Quality_Review` and manual approval, subject to mandatory pre-activation `Sanctions_Check`, a spend ceiling of EUR 20,000, daily transaction monitoring, and retrospective `Quality_Review` within two business days. It expired automatically without written extension.
4. **Data Infrastructure Shift (Source N4)**: Effective 2026-07-01, `Notify_Requester` was migrated to an external CRM service. Only approximately 70% of notification events were mirrored into the onboarding log, driving apparent missingness from 2% to 29%. CRM records contain `notification_id` keys, but legacy integration links rely on mutable text fields.
5. **High-Risk Case Facts**:
   - `SUP-703`: A HIGH-risk foreign supplier was onboarded on 2026-06-20 using `Auto_Approve` via an operator override despite failing all PD-3 eligibility criteria, resulting in an immediate EUR 180,000 purchase.
   - `SUP-708`: A HIGH-risk supplier was activated 40 minutes *prior* to the execution of `Sanctions_Check` without any pilot or emergency justification.
   - `SUP-712`: An onboarding trace conformed perfectly to M0 control flow, but identity resolution confirmed that the requester and manual approver were the same human operating under two distinct system aliases.
   - `SUP-715`: Quality reviewer and manual approver are spouses in the same direct reporting line; no conflict-of-interest (COI) declaration was filed.
6. **Aggregate Quantities**: During June–July 2026, 312 cases executed `Auto_Approve` (289 eligible, 14 clearly ineligible, 9 indeterminate). Forty-seven cases executed `Request_More_Info` loops without early activation. Six cases executed `Auto_Approve` post-pilot in August 2026.

### 1.2 Disputed Propositions
The core legal, architectural, and operational disputes comprise:
1. **Authority of Steering Committee and Pilot Status**: Whether steering committee minutes and email communications (PD-3) constitute a legally valid temporary variance of CS-9, or whether the Compliance observer's post-facto disclaimer invalidates the pilot and renders all 312 auto-approvals unauthorized breaches.
2. **De Facto vs. De Jure Emergency Powers**: Whether the ongoing factual reality of flood recovery on 2026-07-13 legally extended FD-7 powers for `SUP-705`, or whether the automatic expiration rendered subsequent emergency activations unlawful.
3. **Scope of the Normative Model (M0)**: Whether M0 was intended to govern the entire intake funnel (including pre-screen lead rejections such as `SUP-709` and reactivations like `SUP-710`), or whether M0 only governs de novo onboardings accepted past initial intake.
4. **Desirable Deviations vs. Unauthorized Process Shifts**: Whether iterative due diligence loops (`Request_More_Info`, `SUP-707`) and operational UI renamings (`QA_Clearance`, `SUP-713`) represent benign model omissions or unapproved, non-auditable shadow processes.
5. **Evidentiary Weight of CRM Links**: Whether post-migration notifications linked via mutable text fields entered post-hoc (`SUP-706`) satisfy regulatory non-repudiation and evidentiary proof of completion.

### 1.3 Burden of Proof and Evidentiary Framework
- **For Declaring Noncompliance (Prosecution Burden)**:
  - The asserting party must prove by a *preponderance of clear, reliable evidence* that a case violated a codified standard (CS-9), exceeded the strict jurisdictional boundaries of an authorized variance (PD-3, FD-7), or breached non-control-flow governance rules (Segregation of Duties, Conflict of Interest, Sanctions law).
  - *Standard*: A control-flow mismatch against M0 is insufficient alone to establish business noncompliance if the mismatch arises from documented ETL duplication, semantic aliasing, or unmodeled desirable intake loops.
- **For Accepting an Exception or Variance (Defense Burden)**:
  - The asserting party bears the *affirmative burden* of proving all five elements of a valid exception:
    1. *Formal Authority*: Promulgated by an authorized body with appropriate governance jurisdiction.
    2. *Temporal Validity*: Execution occurred strictly between the effective start and expiry timestamps.
    3. *Objective Eligibility*: Complete, immutable data verifying the case satisfied all threshold criteria (risk tier, country, corporate group, emergency scope).
    4. *Compensating Controls*: Full adherence to safety guardrails (e.g., pre-activation sanctions screening, spend caps, daily monitoring).
    5. *Retrospective Settlement*: Timely execution of required retrospective quality and approval actions within the mandated time window (e.g., \(\le 2\) business days).
  - Failure to establish any single element voids the exception and reverts the case to noncompliant status.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

### 2.1 The Primacy of Codified Governance and the Myth of Informal Policy
Compliance Standard CS-9 is the sole formally controlled, binding standard of the enterprise. Under corporate governance principles, informal steering committee minutes, email exchanges, and verbal understandings do not possess the legal power to supersede a controlled compliance standard. The Compliance observer's explicit record that she had "not approved weakening CS-9" establishes that PD-3 was never a de jure amendment. By executing `Auto_Approve` across 312 cases in June and July, Operations initiated an uncontrolled departure from established enterprise risk baselines.

Even if PD-3 and FD-7 are evaluated as limited operational variances, their formal authority was bounded by rigid temporal and substantive guardrails. Operations systematically treated these temporary boundaries as negotiable suggestions rather than strict legal limits:
- **Temporal Transgression (SUP-714 & August Aggregate)**: On 2026-08-02, Operations executed `Auto_Approve` for `SUP-714` two days after PD-3 expired, with six additional auto-approvals in the first week of August. Operational belief that permanent approval was "imminent" is the classic definition of *normalization of deviance*. Operating without an active policy instrument is per se noncompliant.
- **Unauthorized Emergency Powers (SUP-705)**: FD-7 expired automatically at 23:59 on 2026-07-12. Activating `SUP-705` under emergency provisions on 2026-07-13 without an executive written extension was an ultra vires exercise of emergency authority. Factual hardship does not confer legal jurisdiction.

### 2.2 Critical Risk Failures and Systemic Exposure
The breakdown of procedural guardrails created catastrophic compliance failures that exposed the firm to severe legal, financial, and regulatory liabilities:
- **Catastrophic International Sanctions Breach (SUP-708)**: Activating a HIGH-risk foreign supplier 40 minutes *before* performing a `Sanctions_Check` is a direct violation of international sanctions laws and anti-money laundering regulations. Sanctions compliance is an absolute, non-derogable legal prohibition. The defense's argument that "no transactions occurred during the 40-minute window" is irrelevant; the enterprise was exposed to unlawful contractual binding and strict-liability regulatory fines the moment activation occurred unvetted.
- **Rogue Operator Override and EUR 180,000 Exposure (SUP-703)**: A high-risk foreign supplier was illegally flagged as "pilot" by an operator, completely bypassing manual procurement approval and pre-activation quality checks. This resulted in an immediate, unvetted EUR 180,000 spend exposure. Out of 312 pilot cases, 14 were clearly ineligible and 9 lacked eligibility attributes entirely, demonstrating that Operations deployed an automated bypass mechanism without basic software controls or validation gates.
- **Willful Evasion of Segregation of Duties (SUP-712)**: `SUP-712` perfectly conformed to M0's control flow, yet the requester and approver were the exact same individual concealing their identity behind multiple system aliases. This is a deliberate circumventing of the Four-Eyes Principle (CS-9), creating severe fraud exposure that M0 blindly passed.
- **Undisclosed Conflicts of Interest (SUP-715)**: A quality reviewer and manual approver in the same direct line of reporting were spouses who failed to disclose their relationship. This undermines the structural independence of the quality assurance and procurement controls.

### 2.3 Evidentiary Degradation and Auditability Failures
Operations cannot prove compliance for its unmodeled practices:
- In `SUP-706`, notification in the onboarding log was missing, and the CRM link was manually entered into a mutable text field two days after the fact. This fails the basic evidentiary requirements of immutability, non-repudiation, and audit traceability.
- Across the enterprise, post-migration notification missingness spiked to 29%, crippling compliance auditability.
- Desirable intentions (such as `SUP-707`'s extra requests for certificates) cannot retroactively legitimize unmanaged process loops that lack formal change control.

## 3. Defense Brief

### 3.1 Structural Inadequacy, Obsolescence, and Mis-Specification of M0
The prosecution's case rests on the premise that M0 represents an infallible, complete normative standard. In reality, M0 is an obsolete, brittle, and structurally defective model that fails to reflect legitimate operational requirements, forcing compliant business behavior to register as artificial process violations:
- **Omission of Due Diligence Loops (SUP-707 & 47 Aggregate Cases)**: M0 forces a rigid linear progression from registration to checks to decision. In modern procurement, requesting additional certificates (`Request_More_Info`) is essential due diligence. In all 47 cases where this occurred, suppliers were held until missing certifications arrived. M0's inability to express an information-gathering loop classifies thorough risk management as nonconformance.
- **Structural Defect in Pre-Screen Rejection (SUP-709)**: M0 enforces a strict partial order where `checks` (containing both `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review`) must fully complete before any `decision` (including `Reject_Supplier`) can fire. In `SUP-709`, a lead failed the initial sanctions pre-screen. Requiring an expensive, labor-intensive `Quality_Review` for a supplier already barred by sanctions is economically absurd and operationally irrational. M0 suffers from a severe intake scope and control-flow defect.
- **Reactivation Lifecycle Blindness (SUP-710)**: Re-onboarding an established supplier closed six months prior with active, unexpired quality certifications requires only a fresh sanctions check and procurement sign-off. M0 treats every transaction as a de novo registration, lacking any concept of supplier lifecycle states.
- **Ontological Rigidity and Schema Drift (SUP-713)**: Flagging `SUP-713` as noncompliant because the UI renamed `Quality_Review` to `QA_Clearance` (with verified identical audit content and formal mapping approved one week later) conflates nominal label variation with substantive noncompliance.

### 3.2 Legitimacy of Authorized Multi-Tiered Normative Overlays
Operational agility requires multi-tiered governance. The steering committee's PD-3 and Executive FD-7 were valid, authorized corporate instruments:
- **PD-3 Pilot Validity (SUP-702 & 289 Compliant Cases)**: PD-3 was formally enacted by the steering committee. Compliance participated and recorded that the pilot could proceed as a "monitored exception." `SUP-702` strictly satisfied every condition: LOW-risk DE supplier, approved corporate group, pre-activation sanctions check, and retrospective quality review completed the next business day. Notification was proven in CRM at +2 hours with a stable ID. Classifying 289 fully compliant pilot cases as violations misrepresents operational reality.
- **FD-7 Emergency Justification (SUP-704 & SUP-705)**: FD-7 was enacted to prevent catastrophic plant shutdown during severe flooding. `SUP-704` strictly conformed to FD-7 (critical pump, EUR 18,000 spend under the EUR 20k cap, retrospective approvals completed next day). For `SUP-705`, the physical flood crisis and plant danger continued into July 13. Operations acted in good faith to preserve plant operations, strictly respecting the spend cap (EUR 12,000) and completing retrospective approvals. It represents an administrative documentation delay during an active natural disaster, not systemic noncompliance.

### 3.3 Data Engineering, Pipeline Artifacts, and Technical Deficiencies
Apparent process violations are frequently artifacts of the data collection pipeline:
- **ETL Replay Duplication (SUP-711)**: The duplicate `Register_Supplier` event shares the exact same source transaction ID, caused by a database migration ingestion replay. The physical process occurred exactly once. Imputing noncompliance to the business process for an ETL pipeline flaw is methodologically invalid.
- **CRM Migration Splitting (SUP-706 & Source N4)**: Moving customer/requester communications to an enterprise CRM naturally decoupled notification event logging. The notification occurred at +3 hours. Technical integration delays should not be conflated with operational delinquency.

### 3.4 Conceding Material Business Misconduct
The defense explicitly distinguishes between model/data defects and genuine operational misconduct. The defense **concedes** that `SUP-703` (unauthorized pilot flag for an ineligible foreign supplier with EUR 180k spend), `SUP-708` (activation 40 minutes prior to sanctions clearance), and `SUP-712` (fraudulent self-approval via identity aliases) are severe business violations. However, these failures resulted from a lack of automated system validations and M0's complete blindness to resource rules—further proving that M0 is unfit as a modern governance mechanism.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

A robust compliance architecture cannot rely solely on a single control-flow graph. The revised normative architecture employs a **five-layer separation of concerns**:

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER 5: ORGANIZATIONAL & RESOURCE CONTROLS (IAM / HR / Governance Policy)    |
| - Identity Aliasing Resolution (Master Person ID)                             |
| - Four-Eyes Principle (SoD): Requester != Approver                            |
| - Conflict-of-Interest (COI) & Nepotism Disclosure Rules                     |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER 4: DATA-AWARE & ATTRIBUTE GUARDS (Pandas / SQL / Business Rules Engine) |
| - Auto-Approve Eligibility: Risk == 'LOW' & Country == 'DE' & CorpGroup == True|
| - Emergency Spend Cap: Spend <= EUR 20,000                                    |
| - SLA Timers: Post-Activation QA <= 2 Business Days                           |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER 3: POLICY OVERLAYS & LIFECYCLE CONTROLS (Temporal / Authority Registry) |
| - Time-Bounded Validity Windows [Start_Timestamp, End_Timestamp]              |
| - Formal Authority & Electronic Sign-off Binding                              |
| - Zero-Grace-Period Auto-Reversion upon Expiry                                |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER 2: CROSS-TRACK DECLARATIVE INVARIANTS (DECLARE / Log Skeleton)          |
| - Invariant: Response(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier) across ALL tracks   |
| - Invariant: ExactlyOnce(Notify_Requester) prior to Close_Case                |
| - Invariant: Precedence(Quality_Review, Manual_Procurement_Approval) [Std]    |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER 1: MULTI-TRACK CONTROL FLOW (PM4Py POWL Model M1)                       |
| - Intake Phase with Repeatable Request_More_Info Loop & Reactivation Bypass   |
| - Standard Route, Low-Risk Auto-Approve, Emergency Route, Rejection Routes    |
| - Unified Notification and Case Closure                                       |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 6.1 Distribution of Governance Rules across Architectural Tiers

1. **Layer 1: POWL Control-Flow Grammar**:
   - Models valid partial orders, concurrency (Sanctions and Quality in standard track), branching routes (Standard, Auto-Approve, Emergency, Reactivation, Early Rejection), and repeatable structures (`Request_More_Info` loop).
2. **Layer 2: DECLARE / Log-Skeleton Temporal Invariants**:
   - Enforces absolute cross-cutting rules that must never be violated regardless of branch or operational pressure:
     - `Response(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier)`: Sanctions check must always precede activation.
     - `Response(Activate_Supplier, Quality_Review)`: In fast-track/emergency routes, activation strictly mandates retrospective quality review.
3. **Layer 3: Policy-Overlay Governance Engine**:
   - Centralizes governance of variances (Pilots, Emergencies). Each case binds to a specific `Policy_Overlay_ID`. The engine automatically rejects overlay routes if the system timestamp is outside the registered `[Effective_Start, Effective_End]` window.
4. **Layer 4: Data-Aware Rules Engine (Pandas / SQL / Schemas)**:
   - Validates numerical thresholds, geographic constraints, and risk scores:
     - Auto-Approve: `Risk_Level == 'LOW' and Country == 'DE' and Corp_Group_Approved == True`.
     - Emergency: `Spend_Amount <= 20000.00 and Plant_Shutdown_Risk == True`.
     - SLA Enforcement: `Timestamp(Quality_Review) - Timestamp(Activate_Supplier) <= 2 Business Days`.
5. **Layer 5: Resource, IAM, and Conflict-of-Interest Governance**:
   - Resolves system aliases to an immutable `Master_Person_ID`.
   - Enforces Segregation of Duties: `Master_Person_ID(Requester) != Master_Person_ID(Approver)`.
   - Automated HR checks: Flag approval pairs sharing direct familial relationships or reporting lines lacking an approved Compliance COI waiver.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

### 7.1 Executable PM4Py POWL Construction
The Python code below constructs the revised normative POWL model (`M1`). It natively incorporates:
- Intake choice with a repeatable `Request_More_Info` loop and a silent intake bypass for reactivations;
- Standard track with concurrent `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` preceding manual approval;
- Low-risk fast-track (`Auto_Approve` with post-activation `Quality_Review`);
- Emergency provisional track (activation followed by retrospective quality and manual approvals);
- Fast-track sanctions pre-screen rejection and full-check rejection;
- Reactivation track utilizing valid historical quality credentials;
- Unified notification and case closure.

```python
"""
Revised Normative POWL Model (M1) for Supplier Onboarding Conformance
Constructed using PM4Py POWL objects.
"""

from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    OperatorPOWL,
    SilentTransition,
    StrictPartialOrder,
    Transition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def build_revised_powl_model() -> StrictPartialOrder:
    # =========================================================================
    # 1. INTAKE AND DUE DILIGENCE CLARIFICATION PHASE
    # =========================================================================
    register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
    req_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
    skip_info = SilentTransition()

    # Repeatable Request_More_Info loop (executes 0 or more times)
    info_loop = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[skip_info, req_info]
    )

    intake_standard = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, info_loop])
    intake_standard.order.add_edge(register, info_loop)

    # Intake Choice: Standard Intake with Loop vs. Silent Bypass (for Reactivations)
    skip_intake = SilentTransition()
    intake_phase = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR, children=[intake_standard, skip_intake]
    )

    # =========================================================================
    # 2. OPERATIONAL TRACKS (Approval, Fast-Track, Emergency, Rejection, Reactivation)
    # =========================================================================

    # Track A: Standard Approval Route
    # Concurrent Sanctions & Quality Review -> Manual Approval -> Activation
    s_std = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    q_std = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
    checks_std = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[s_std, q_std]
    )  # Concurrent execution
    m_std = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    act_std = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
    track_std = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[checks_std, m_std, act_std])
    track_std.order.add_edge(checks_std, m_std)
    track_std.order.add_edge(m_std, act_std)

    # Track B: Low-Risk Fast-Track Route (PD-3 Codified)
    # Sanctions Check -> Auto Approve -> Activation -> Post-Activation Quality Review
    s_auto = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    auto_app = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
    act_auto = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
    q_auto = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
    track_auto = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[s_auto, auto_app, act_auto, q_auto])
    track_auto.order.add_edge(s_auto, auto_app)
    track_auto.order.add_edge(auto_app, act_auto)
    track_auto.order.add_edge(act_auto, q_auto)

    # Track C: Emergency Provisional Route (FD-7 Codified)
    # Sanctions Check -> Activation -> Retrospective Quality Review & Manual Approval
    s_emg = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    act_emg = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
    q_emg = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
    m_emg = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    post_emg_checks = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[q_emg, m_emg]
    )  # Retrospective dual control
    track_emergency = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[s_emg, act_emg, post_emg_checks]
    )
    track_emergency.order.add_edge(s_emg, act_emg)
    track_emergency.order.add_edge(act_emg, post_emg_checks)

    # Track D: Fast-Track Sanctions Pre-Screen Rejection (Short-circuits QA)
    s_rej_early = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    rej_early = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
    track_early_reject = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[s_rej_early, rej_early])
    track_early_reject.order.add_edge(s_rej_early, rej_early)

    # Track E: Standard Full-Check Rejection
    s_rej_std = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    q_rej_std = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
    checks_rej_std = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[s_rej_std, q_rej_std])
    rej_std = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
    track_std_reject = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[checks_rej_std, rej_std])
    track_std_reject.order.add_edge(checks_rej_std, rej_std)

    # Track F: Reactivation Route (Valid Historical QA on file)
    # Sanctions Check -> Manual Procurement Approval -> Activation
    s_react = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
    m_react = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    act_react = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
    track_reactivation = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[s_react, m_react, act_react]
    )
    track_reactivation.order.add_edge(s_react, m_react)
    track_reactivation.order.add_edge(m_react, act_react)

    # Decision Hub: Exclusive Choice over all Valid Operational Routes
    operational_decision = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[
            track_std,
            track_auto,
            track_emergency,
            track_early_reject,
            track_std_reject,
            track_reactivation,
        ],
    )

    # =========================================================================
    # 3. NOTIFICATION AND CASE CLOSURE PHASE
    # =========================================================================
    notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
    close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

    # Master POWL Model M1
    M1 = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[intake_phase, operational_decision, notify, close]
    )
    M1.order.add_edge(intake_phase, operational_decision)
    M1.order.add_edge(operational_decision, notify)
    M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)

    return M1


# Instantiate model for runtime conformance checking
revised_powl_model = build_revised_powl_model()
```

### 7.4 Explicit Statement of POWL Limitations
While POWL excels at expressing partially ordered control flow, concurrency, loops, and exclusive choices without state-space explosion, **POWL cannot natively enforce**:
1. *Data Predicates*: POWL cannot verify that `Risk_Level == 'LOW'` or that `Spend <= EUR 20,000`.
2. *Temporal Invariants & Real-Time SLAs*: POWL cannot enforce that post-activation `Quality_Review` occurs within two business days, nor can it check whether the current system time falls within `[Effective_Start, Effective_End]`.
3. *Resource & Identity Constraints*: POWL cannot verify that the actor of `Register_Supplier` differs from `Manual_Procurement_Approval`.

**Architectural Solution**: These constraints are strictly enforced in the surrounding architecture via Layer 4 (Python/Pandas data pipeline guards) and Layer 5 (IAM / Segregation-of-Duties validation). The multi-tiered architecture ensures that control flow, data eligibility, temporal validity, and organizational integrity operate in synchronized harmony.
