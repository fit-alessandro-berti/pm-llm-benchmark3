Supporting skill-based routing plus resource logic: D02 goes to D-F for firmware; D03 goes to D-F for electrical; D05 waits for D-F because D-F is the only fully qualified resource; D10 uses D-X while D-F is unavailable. D04 before P1 D08 and D07 before P1 D10 support FIFO/resource-specific queueing rather than priority. Challenging cases: the D-F no-start from 11:36 to 12:00 is not explained by simple FIFO once D05 is eligible. It is explainable by a resource-calendar rule: D05’s estimate is 30 minutes, only 24 minutes remain before the meeting, and semicolon-style interruption cost shows the system tracks interrupted work. If no such meeting-fit rule existed, the idle period would require an undocumented hold. D06’s appointment also coincides with FIFO, so appointment scheduling is supported but not uniquely identifiable.

# 7. Final synthesis

QD is the most identifiable queue because skill eligibility, resource calendars, and resource-specific FIFO explain most choices; D05 and D10 are especially diagnostic. QA is fairly identifiable as FIFO plus exact-appointment override, though A06 is confounded. QB is a hybrid with credible urgent/P1 exceptions but late cases remain under-explained. QC is the least identifiable: early SPT-like choices are mixed with priority, deadline, aging, and possible policy switching.

The most dangerous policy to infer from these observations alone is **strict priority scheduling**, especially preemptive priority. P1/P2 jobs sometimes jump, but many high-priority jobs wait while FIFO, appointments, skills, and resource availability explain the observed starts. A close second would be declaring QC to be pure SPT/SEPT from C02, C03, and C06 alone; later decisions contradict that inference.
