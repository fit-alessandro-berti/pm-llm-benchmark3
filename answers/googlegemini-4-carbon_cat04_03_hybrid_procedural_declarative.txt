# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

## Material Modeling Assumptions
1. **Atomic Event Timestamps and Total Ordering:** Events carry point-in-time completion timestamps (`time:timestamp`) rather than start/complete lifecycle intervals. Within a trace, timestamps induce a strict total order (or deterministic tie-breaking via log position).
2. **Closed-World Case Scope:** Each trace represents a single onboarding instance from `Receive Onboarding Request` through `Close Onboarding`. Post-activation events (`Change Bank Details`, `Suspend Vendor`, and re-executed `Verify Bank Account`) are tracked within the onboarding case only up to `Release First Payment` and `Close Onboarding`.
3. **Final Outcome Semantics (R3):** In R3 ("before any final outcome"), the onboarding decision outcomes are `Reject Vendor` and `Activate Vendor` (as well as terminal `Close Onboarding`). Thus, all open clarification cycles must be closed by `Receive Clarification` prior to vendor activation or rejection, although clarifications may occur either before `Classify Risk` (as in T2) or after `Classify Risk` during due diligence/approval review.
4. **Scope of Sanctions Escalation & Legal Opinion (R4, R5, R7):** `Obtain Legal Opinion` is not a standalone routine activity; it is triggered exclusively in response to `Sanctions Escalation`, which itself can occur only after `Run Sanctions Screening` (either before or after `Classify Risk`, as seen in T6 and T7). Importantly, an unfavorable `Obtain Legal Opinion` may legitimately lead to `Reject Vendor` rather than `Activate Vendor`.
5. **Ambiguity of the 24-Hour Anchor in R6:** R6 states that *"emergency activation may occur before Obtain Legal Opinion, but only when jointly authorized outside the event log; the opinion must then arrive within 24 hours."* Operational SLAs of this type are commonly anchored to `Activate Vendor` ($t_{\text{opinion}} - t_{\text{activate}} \le 24\text{h}$), though a conservative reading anchors them to `Sanctions Escalation` ($t_{\text{opinion}} - t_{\text{escalation}} \le 24\text{h}$, matching the $12\text{h} + 2 \times 6\text{h} = 24\text{h}$ upper bound in R12). Our data-aware layer checks $t_{\text{opinion}} - t_{\text{activate}} \le 24\text{h}$ explicitly and logs both intervals (in T6, both hold: $10\text{h}$ and $17\text{h}$).
6. **Soft Normative Status of Temporal Expectations (R12):** Elapsed-time deviations exceeding $\zeta = 2.0$ standard deviations generate compliance `warning` flags for SLA review rather than `fail` verdicts, provided hard control-flow and resource/data obligations hold.

## Resolving the R5 vs. R6 Policy Conflict
R5 (Legal) imposes an unconditional control-flow invariant: after `Sanctions Escalation`, `Obtain Legal Opinion` must precede `Activate Vendor` under all circumstances. R6 (Operations) introduces an exception allowing `Activate Vendor` to precede `Obtain Legal Opinion` if jointly authorized out-of-band and followed by `Obtain Legal Opinion` within 24 hours—while explicitly noting that **neither an emergency flag nor the joint authorization exists in the event log**.

Because the conditioning evidence for R6 is unobservable in raw event data, silently merging R5 and R6 creates a hidden compliance hole: in an activity-only model, an authorized emergency activation is **observationally indistinguishable** from an unauthorized sanctions bypass. At least three governance resolutions are possible; we contrast the two primary ones below:

### Resolution A — Strict Legal Precedence / Fail-Closed Baseline *(Chosen for Implemented Model)*
* **Design:** Treat Legal's R5 rule as the authoritative automated baseline. Enforce `nonsuccession("Activate Vendor", "Obtain Legal Opinion")` in DECLARE, while leaving the procedural backbone structurally permissive so traces can still be parsed and diagnosed. In the Resource/Data layer, require explicit enriched case/event attributes (`case:is_emergency == True` and `case:joint_auth_id` verified by Legal + Operations) alongside $0 < t_{\text{opinion}} - t_{\text{activate}} \le 24\text{h}$ to grant an R6 override. When those attributes are absent from the log (the current state), fail closed (`fail`).
* **Compliance Risk:** **Operational friction and false-positive blocks.** Genuine, jointly authorized emergencies will fail automated conformance (`fail` on T6) until Operations and Legal digitize out-of-band emergency sign-offs into the event log. If overrides remain frequent and unlogged, compliance queues may experience alert fatigue. Conversely, regulatory exposure to strict-liability sanctions violations is minimized.

### Resolution B — Permissive Control-Flow with Time-Bounded Manual Audit *(Alternative)*
* **Design:** Relax R5 in DECLARE by omitting `nonsuccession("Activate Vendor", "Obtain Legal Opinion")` and keeping only R4 (`response("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion")`). Delegate R6 to the Resource/Data layer: whenever `Sanctions Escalation` $\prec$ `Activate Vendor` $\prec$ `Obtain Legal Opinion`, verify $t_{\text{opinion}} - t_{\text{activate}} \le 24\text{h}$. If satisfied and joint-authorization attributes are missing from the log, emit `warning` (pending manual verification of out-of-band sign-off) rather than `fail` (or `fail` if $>24\text{h}$).
* **Compliance Risk:** **Severe regulatory and sanctions exposure (false negatives).** Relaxing the automated control-flow gate means an *unauthorized* activation of a sanctions-flagged vendor passes control-flow checking cleanly and relies entirely on post-hoc manual reconciliation of off-system emails/signatures—after the vendor is already active in the ERP master file.

### Justification of Choice
We implement **Resolution A (Strict Legal Precedence / Fail-Closed)** because sanctions compliance carries strict regulatory liability; missing evidence of exception approval must never default to conformance pass in financial onboarding controls.

# 3. Procedural Backbone

## 1. Compact Formal Description
Using standard process-tree notation ($\to$ for sequence, $\wedge$ for concurrency, $\times$ for exclusive choice, $\circlearrowleft(D, R)$ for structured loop executing body $D$ followed by zero or more $(R \to D)$ iterations, and $\tau$ for silent step):

$$\begin{aligned}
M_{\text{proc}} =\; \to\big(&\text{Receive Onboarding Request},\\
&\wedge(\; B_{\text{core}},\; B_{\text{clar}},\; B_{\text{sanc}}\;),\\
&\text{Close Onboarding}\big)
\end{aligned}$$

where:
* **Core Lifecycle ($B_{\text{core}}$):**
  $$B_{\text{core}} = \;\to\Big(\wedge(\text{Capture Master Data}, \text{Run Sanctions Screening}, \text{Verify Bank Account}),\; \text{Classify Risk},\; \times(\text{Reject Vendor},\; B_{\text{accept}})\Big)$$
* **Accepted Path with Post-Activation Bank-Change Loop ($B_{\text{accept}}$):**
  $$\begin{aligned}
  B_{\text{accept}} = \;\to\Big(&\times\big(\text{Manager Approval},\; \to(\text{Enhanced Due Diligence}, \text{Compliance Approval})\big),\\
  &\text{Activate Vendor},\\
  &\circlearrowleft\big(\tau,\; \to(\text{Change Bank Details},\; \wedge(\text{Suspend Vendor}, \text{Verify Bank Account}))\big),\\
  &\text{Release First Payment}\Big)
  \end{aligned}$$
* **Cross-Cutting Clarification Envelope ($B_{\text{clar}}$):**
  $$B_{\text{clar}} = \circlearrowleft\big(\tau,\; \to(\text{Request Clarification}, \text{Receive Clarification})\big)$$
* **Cross-Cutting Sanctions Escalation Envelope ($B_{\text{sanc}}$):**
  $$B_{\text{sanc}} = \times\big(\tau,\; \to(\text{Sanctions Escalation}, \text{Obtain Legal Opinion})\big)$$
