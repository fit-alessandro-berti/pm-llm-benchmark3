## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

**Hard invariants** (a candidate that violates one is discarded)
- H1 Additive only: no observed relation is deleted or redirected; no new events or objects.
- H2 Referential and type integrity: both ends exist, and the object type fits the activity signature (no Order on `Record Payment`, no Package on `Confirm Delivery`, although packages are what is physically delivered).
- H3 O2O direction: `Order contains Item`, `Package packs Item`, `Delivery carries Package`, `Invoice bills Order`, `Payment settles Invoice`.
- H4 `Record Payment` has exactly one Payment; no duplicate (event, object) or (source, qualifier, target) rows.
- H5 Lifecycle order per object: an item is added before it is packed, a package is packed before it is dispatched, a delivery is dispatched before it is confirmed.

**Strong defaults** (overridable by the exceptions named in the domain rules)
- S1 `Pack Item` = one Item + one Package; `Dispatch Package` = one Package + one Delivery; `Record Payment` normally one Invoice.
- S2 An item sits in one package; a package travels on one delivery.
- S3 An identifier inside an event attribute that names an existing object (`PK8-2` → PK-8, `N-421` → DEL-Y) is the best available evidence for a missing E2O row.
- S4 Mirroring: every complete Pack/Dispatch/Invoice/Payment event in the observed data has a matching O2O row, so an added E2O pair gets the mirrored O2O edge. The mirror inherits the E2O confidence; it does not add to it.

**Weak heuristics**
- W1 Amount arithmetic (130 + 100 = 230 on the order side and on the payment side).
- W2 Same customer.
- W3 Time proximity and sequence numbers (09:04/09:05, B-771/B-772).
- W4 Lifecycle completeness. Deliberately the weakest: an orphan object is a reason to look, never a reason to link.

**Evidence against a repair**
- X1 Uncertainty markers in the source (`N-421?`, `N-42*`).
- X2 A documented legitimate exception already explains the record (scanner outage, lost carrier ID, unapplied cash).
- X3 Non-discriminating evidence: route NORTH and customer C17 fit every candidate, so they cannot choose between candidates. Gross weight is unusable without item weights.
- X4 Chained dependence: a repair that only makes sense if another unverified repair holds has its confidence multiplied down.
- X5 The repair breaches S2 or creates many-to-many structure.
- X6 Partial loss: ev11 and ev12 did store ORD-A, so the relation writer worked there; "it dropped only the second object" is a narrower failure story.

**Combination.** Hard invariants filter first. An identifier match sets the base level: about 0.85 when exact, 0.70 with a doubt marker, 0.4–0.55 for a wildcard fitting two objects. Amount, customer and time move a candidate in small steps and never above 0.70 alone. Route contributes nothing here. Lifecycle order and cardinality act as vetoes or penalties, not as positive evidence. Existing O2O structure is used to trace observed chains (ORD-A → IT-A1 → PK-7 → DEL-X) that depend on no repair. Confidences are subjective and ordinal; a bundle is less likely than its weakest edge.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A — conservative (P1–P4).** Only repairs where the event's own identifier names an existing object.
- ev08 and PK-8: add ev08 → PK-8 and `PK-8 packs IT-B1`.
- ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y: add ev10 → DEL-Y and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`, both flagged provisional; DEL-X untouched.
- ev11: left order-only, as rule 5 permits. The wildcard cannot separate DEL-X from DEL-Y.
- INV-44 and ORD-B: unresolved.
- PAY-2 and INV-44: unresolved; PAY-2 is treated as possibly unapplied cash.

Coherence: Item, Package and Delivery lifecycles are correctly ordered (packs at 09:04 and 09:05, dispatch at 10:00). Three tensions stay visible on purpose: ORD-B's item ships but the order is neither confirmed nor billed; INV-44 states 230 against a single 130 order; PAY-2 floats. The financial half of the story stays open. Chance that all four additions are right: roughly 0.6.

**Bundle B — consolidated C17 shipment and billing (A + P8, P9, P11–P14).**
- ev08 and PK-8: as A.
- ev10: as A.
- ev11: related to both DEL-X and DEL-Y, one acknowledgement for two deliveries. Assigning only DEL-X would be weaker here, because under P1–P4 half of ORD-A travels on DEL-Y, so an ORD-A confirmation covering DEL-X alone would describe a partial delivery. Assigning only DEL-Y ignores the fully observed chain to DEL-X.
- INV-44 bills ORD-A and ORD-B; ev12 → ORD-B.
- PAY-2 settles INV-44; ev14 → INV-44.

Coherence: one story covers everything. C17 places two orders two minutes apart, they ship as two parcels on two NORTH references, the portal acknowledges both, and one invoice is paid by two transfers. Remaining tension: ORD-B has `Issue Invoice` but no `Confirm Delivery` unless P10 is added, which I do not do. The bundle needs five uncertain, positively correlated steps; I put the whole conjunction at about 0.15–0.25.

**Bundle C — bulk dispatch with a mis-keyed reference (P1, P2, P5, P6, P8, P11, P12).**
- ev08 and PK-8: as A.
- ev10: related to DEL-X; `N-421?` is read as a typing error at a same-minute bulk dispatch. DEL-Y stays an orphan (booked but unused, or its events lie outside the extract).
- ev11: DEL-X only. Within this bundle that is unique, because DEL-Y carries nothing.
- INV-44 bills both orders.
- PAY-2: left unapplied.

Coherence: internally consistent, but it needs a mis-key, tolerates an object with no events, and leaves cash of exactly the missing amount unapplied. I rate it near 0.05; its use is to test how much the Delivery view depends on P3.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

Audit of Bundle A, with Bundle B differences noted.

- **Order.** ORD-A is complete without any repair: Create → Add ×2 → Confirm → Invoice. ORD-B stops at `Add Item` in A and jumps from `Add Item` to `Issue Invoice` in B. Neither is an orphan. Suspicious structure: through P2, Order–Package becomes many-to-many (ORD-A is spread over PK-7 and PK-8; PK-8 holds items of both orders). This is plausible for one customer on one route, but it is the most influential consequence of the repair.
- **Item.** All three items run `Add Item → Pack Item` in every bundle; the repair adds no item events and keeps one package per item.
- **Package.** PK-7: Pack → Dispatch. PK-8: Pack, Pack, Dispatch, correctly ordered. Each package is on exactly one delivery in A and B.
- **Delivery.** Unrepaired, DEL-Y is an orphan object. In A, both deliveries have one event and no confirmation: incomplete but legitimate. In B, ev11 is one-to-many across both deliveries. Filling DEL-Y is a side effect of P3, not a reason for it.
- **Invoice.** INV-44: Issue → Payment. In A the mismatches (230 versus 130, 100 paid) are left visible. In B the invoice has two orders and two payments; both one-to-many shapes are allowed by rules 6 and 7.
- **Payment.** PAY-1 is complete. PAY-2 is not an orphan (it has ev14), but in A it forms an isolated component, consistent with unapplied cash.

**Independent E2O and O2O support.** Only observed relations have both, for example ev06 with `PK-7 packs IT-A1`, ev09 with `DEL-X carries PK-7`, ev13 with `PAY-1 settles INV-44`. Every gap is missing from both tables at once: ev08/PK-8 with `packs`, ev10/DEL-Y with `carries`, ev12/ORD-B with `bills`, ev14/INV-44 with `settles`. Either O2O is derived from E2O upstream and both were lost together, or the relation never existed. No proposed repair has independent double support.

**Circular reasoning to avoid.**
1. P2 and P4 are derived from P1 and P3. Citing `PK-8 packs IT-B1` afterwards as confirmation of ev08 → PK-8 would be counting one clue twice.
2. P3 → P4 → "ORD-A's item rides DEL-Y" → P9 → "DEL-Y has a confirmed lifecycle, so P3 was right" is a loop. P9 is discounted for depending on P3 and gives nothing back.
3. P11 and P13 both rest on the figure 230. "The invoice is fully paid, so it covers both orders" and "it covers both orders, so the 130 is its payment" confirm each other with the same number. The one non-circular piece is observed: PAY-1 (100, equal to ORD-B's value) already settles INV-44. That is still arithmetic.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**OC-DFG**
- Unrepaired. Order: Create → Add (2), Add → Add (1), Add → Confirm (1), Confirm → Issue Invoice (1). Item: Add → Pack (3). Package: Pack → Dispatch (2). Delivery: one node, no edges; DEL-Y absent. Invoice: Issue Invoice → Record Payment (1). Payment: no edges.
- Bundle A. Package gains a `Pack Item → Pack Item` self-loop (PK-8); `Pack → Dispatch` stays at 2 but PK-8's edge now starts at ev08. Delivery start/end count for `Dispatch Package` rises from 1 to 2. Order, Invoice and Payment are unchanged.
- Bundle B. Delivery gains `Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery` with two objects from a single ev11. Order gains `Add Item → Issue Invoice` (ORD-B), a skip of confirmation that exists only because of the repair. Invoice gains a `Record Payment → Record Payment` self-loop.
- Bundle C would instead show `Dispatch → Dispatch` on DEL-X, whose direction is a sort artefact because ev09 and ev10 share a timestamp.

**OTG.** PM4Py builds it from event co-occurrence and lifecycle relations. A adds no type pair; weights change (Item–Package interaction 2→3, Delivery–Package 1→2). B adds a Delivery–Order interaction and the self-pairs Order–Order (ev12) and Delivery–Delivery (ev11), and raises Invoice–Order and Invoice–Payment from 1 to 2. The Delivery–Package co-death relation (PK-7 and DEL-X both ending at ev09) disappears in B, because deliveries then end at ev11.

**ET–OT graph.** A adds no edge: `Pack Item–Package` goes 2→3, `Dispatch Package–Delivery` 1→2. B adds `Confirm Delivery–Delivery` and raises `Record Payment–Invoice` and `Issue Invoice–Order` 1→2, the latter from a single event.

**Flattenings**
- Order: unrepaired and A give ORD-A ⟨Create, Add, Add, Confirm, Invoice⟩ and ORD-B ⟨Create, Add⟩. B gives ORD-B ⟨Create, Add, Invoice⟩, duplicating ev12 across both cases.
- Package: unrepaired, both cases ⟨Pack, Dispatch⟩. A and B: PK-8 becomes ⟨Pack, Pack, Dispatch⟩, so one variant splits into two.
- Delivery: unrepaired, one case ⟨Dispatch⟩. A: two such cases. B: two cases ⟨Dispatch, Confirm⟩ with ev11 duplicated and identical four-hour durations. C: one case ⟨Dispatch, Dispatch, Confirm⟩.
- Invoice: ⟨Invoice, Payment⟩ unrepaired and in A; ⟨Invoice, Payment, Payment⟩ in B.
- Payment: two cases ⟨Record Payment⟩ in every bundle. The repair is invisible in this view.

**Activity frequencies.** Event counts never change (for example `Confirm Delivery` = 1, `Dispatch Package` = 2). Unique-object and total-object counts do: `Pack Item` keeps 2 unique packages but package relations go 2→3 in A; `Dispatch Package` goes from 1 to 2 unique deliveries in A, while C keeps 1 unique with 2 total; `Confirm Delivery` goes from 0 to 2 deliveries in B; `Issue Invoice` from 1 to 2 orders in B. KPIs on flattened counts will report two invoices or two confirmations where one event occurred.

**Equivalent-execution clustering around Order.** Unrepaired, the object graph has a large ORD-A component, a small {ORD-B, IT-B1} component, and PAY-2 alone. In A, PK-8 joins the two order components. With connected components there is one execution containing both orders; with Order as leading type, ORD-B's execution gains PK-8 and DEL-Y, which also appear in ORD-A's execution, so ev07, ev08 and ev10 are shared. B additionally shares ev12 to ev14 and pulls PAY-2 in. The two order executions are never equivalent (two items versus one; confirmation present versus absent), and with two orders any cluster frequency is anecdotal. P1 alone decides whether the orders are independent executions at all.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.ocel.obj import OCEL

EID, ACT, TS = "ocel:eid", "ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp"
OID, OTYPE, QUAL, OID2 = "ocel:oid", "ocel:type", "ocel:qualifier", "ocel:oid_2"

# 0. Keep the unrepaired tables untouched
orig = {name: df.copy(deep=True) for name, df in
        {"events": events, "objects": objects, "relations": relations, "o2o": o2o}.items()}

# 1. Candidate repairs, tagged by bundle. Confidence stays in this side table,
#    it is never written into the OCEL tables.
E2O_CAND = pd.DataFrame([
    ("A", "ev08", "PK-8",   "package",  0.85),
    ("A", "ev10", "DEL-Y",  "delivery", 0.70),
    ("B", "ev11", "DEL-X",  "delivery", 0.55),
    ("B", "ev11", "DEL-Y",  "delivery", 0.40),
    ("B", "ev12", "ORD-B",  "order",    0.70),
    ("B", "ev14", "INV-44", "invoice",  0.60),
], columns=["bundle", EID, OID, QUAL, "confidence"])
O2O_CAND = pd.DataFrame([
    ("A", "PK-8",   "IT-B1",  "packs",   0.80),
    ("A", "DEL-Y",  "PK-8",   "carries", 0.65),
    ("B", "INV-44", "ORD-B",  "bills",   0.65),
    ("B", "PAY-2",  "INV-44", "settles", 0.55),
], columns=["bundle", OID, OID2, QUAL, "confidence"])
LAYERS = {"unrepaired": set(), "A": {"A"}, "B": {"A", "B"}}
O2O_SIGNATURE = {"contains": ("Order", "Item"), "packs": ("Package", "Item"),
                 "carries": ("Delivery", "Package"), "bills": ("Invoice", "Order"),
                 "settles": ("Payment", "Invoice")}


def build(bundle):
    ev, ob = orig["events"].copy(), orig["objects"].copy()
    rel, oo = orig["relations"].copy(), orig["o2o"].copy()
    ev[TS], rel[TS] = pd.to_datetime(ev[TS]), pd.to_datetime(rel[TS])
    otype = ob.drop_duplicates(OID).set_index(OID)[OTYPE]
    ev_idx = ev.drop_duplicates(EID).set_index(EID)

    add_rel = E2O_CAND[E2O_CAND["bundle"].isin(LAYERS[bundle])][[EID, OID, QUAL]].copy()
    add_oo = O2O_CAND[O2O_CAND["bundle"].isin(LAYERS[bundle])][[OID, OID2, QUAL]].copy()

    # 2. Checks on the additions before appending
    assert add_rel[EID].isin(ev[EID]).all(), "addition refers to an unknown event"
    assert add_rel[OID].isin(ob[OID]).all(), "addition refers to an unknown object"
    assert add_oo[OID].isin(ob[OID]).all() and add_oo[OID2].isin(ob[OID]).all()
    assert add_rel.merge(rel, on=[EID, OID]).empty, "E2O addition already present"
    assert add_oo.merge(oo, on=[OID, OID2, QUAL]).empty, "O2O addition already present"
    for src, tgt, q in add_oo.itertuples(index=False):
        assert (otype[src], otype[tgt]) == O2O_SIGNATURE[q], f"wrong direction: {src} {q} {tgt}"

    # 3. Append; fill the denormalised columns of the relations table
    add_rel[ACT] = add_rel[EID].map(ev_idx[ACT])
    add_rel[TS] = add_rel[EID].map(ev_idx[TS])
    add_rel[OTYPE] = add_rel[OID].map(otype)
    rel = pd.concat([rel, add_rel.reindex(columns=rel.columns)], ignore_index=True)
    rel = rel.sort_values([TS, EID], kind="stable").reset_index(drop=True)  # ev09/ev10 tie
    oo = pd.concat([oo, add_oo.reindex(columns=oo.columns)], ignore_index=True)

    # 4. Whole-table integrity and duplicate checks
    assert rel[EID].isin(ev[EID]).all() and rel[OID].isin(ob[OID]).all()
    assert oo[OID].isin(ob[OID]).all() and oo[OID2].isin(ob[OID]).all()
    assert not rel.duplicated([EID, OID]).any(), "duplicate E2O row"
    assert not oo.duplicated([OID, OID2, QUAL]).any(), "duplicate O2O row"

    # 5. Soft audits: report, do not repair
    per_event = rel.groupby([EID, ACT, OTYPE]).size().unstack(fill_value=0)
    pay = per_event.xs("Record Payment", level=ACT)
    assert (pay["Payment"] == 1).all(), "Record Payment needs exactly one Payment"
    multi_carrier = oo[oo[QUAL] == "carries"].groupby(OID2)[OID].nunique()
    multi_pack = oo[oo[QUAL] == "packs"].groupby(OID2)[OID].nunique()
    report = {
        "orphan_objects": sorted(set(ob[OID]) - set(rel[OID])),
        "events_without_objects": sorted(set(ev[EID]) - set(rel[EID])),
        "packages_on_several_deliveries": multi_carrier[multi_carrier > 1].to_dict(),
        "items_in_several_packages": multi_pack[multi_pack > 1].to_dict(),
        "objects_per_event": per_event,
    }

    # 6. VERSION-DEPENDENT: constructor keywords differ across PM4Py releases.
    #    If this fails, deep-copy an OCEL read with pm4py.read_ocel2_* and assign
    #    .relations and .o2o instead.
    ocel = OCEL(events=ev, objects=ob, relations=rel, o2o=oo)
    return ocel, report


def profile(ocel):
    out = {
        "ocdfg": pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel),
        "otg": pm4py.discover_otg(ocel),    # requires a recent PM4Py release
        "etot": pm4py.discover_etot(ocel),  # requires a recent PM4Py release
    }
    for ot in ["Order", "Delivery", "Package", "Invoice"]:
        flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, ot)
        out[f"variants_{ot}"] = (flat.sort_values("time:timestamp", kind="stable")
                                     .groupby("case:concept:name")["concept:name"]
                                     .agg(tuple).to_dict())
    return out


results = {}
for name in LAYERS:
    ocel_b, report_b = build(name)
    results[name] = {"ocel": ocel_b, "report": report_b, **profile(ocel_b)}

# 7. Expectations that follow from the selected bundle (structure, not business truth)
assert "DEL-Y" in results["unrepaired"]["report"]["orphan_objects"]
assert "DEL-Y" not in results["A"]["report"]["orphan_objects"]
assert results["A"]["variants_Package"]["PK-8"] == ("Pack Item", "Pack Item", "Dispatch Package")
assert results["A"]["variants_Order"] == results["unrepaired"]["variants_Order"]
assert results["B"]["variants_Delivery"]["DEL-Y"] == ("Dispatch Package", "Confirm Delivery")

# Deliberately NOT calling pm4py.ocel_o2o_enrichment: it derives object links from
# event co-occurrence or lifecycle order, so it would re-express the E2O rows just
# added and could be misread as independent confirmation of the business relation.
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Confirming ORD-B's delivery (P10).** Adding ORD-B to ev11 because the customer matches invents a confirmation nobody recorded and removes the only visible sign that ORD-B may have been billed before delivery was confirmed.
2. **Confirming both deliveries by wildcard.** If DEL-Y was still in transit at 14:00, B creates a false `Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery` edge and two identical four-hour lead times.
3. **Linking ev10 to DEL-Y to rescue an orphan.** If PK-8 actually left on DEL-X, the repair invents a second delivery. DEL-Y may be a booked but unused shipment.
4. **Applying PAY-2 to INV-44 on arithmetic.** Payment has no customer attribute. If this is unapplied cash or another customer's transfer, the repair shows INV-44 as paid in full, adds a `Record Payment` self-loop on Invoice, and hides an open receivable.
5. **Billing ORD-B on INV-44 on arithmetic.** If the extra 100 is something else, an unbilled order disappears from view and ev12 is counted twice in the Order flattening.
6. **Adding shortcut relations.** Attaching Pack, Dispatch or Payment events to Orders, or adding Order → Package O2O edges, produces Order-level edges such as `Add Item → Pack Item` that the source never recorded, and multiplies convergence.
7. **Merging ev09 and ev10 into a bulk dispatch.** Adding PK-8 to ev09 or PK-7 to ev10 puts a package on two deliveries and creates a many-to-many Package–Delivery relation from a timestamp coincidence.
