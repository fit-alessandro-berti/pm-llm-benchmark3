## 1. Decision rule

I treat the swarm as a scarce, congestion-coupled resource whose value depends on *causal* risk reduction under intervention, not on the observational breach score. The rule has five gates applied in order.

**(a) Stage eligibility (hard gate).**
A case is eligible only if it is at or before `Diagnosis Started` (i.e., before `Diagnosis Complete`). Any case at `Diagnosis Complete` or later is ineligible regardless of risk, because the stated intervention effect there is ~0.00–0.03 (indistinguishable from zero). This immediately removes I-03 even though it has the highest `p_now`.

**(b) Calibration-robustified probability.**
I never use raw `p_now`. I map it to a decision probability `p*` by shifting for segment miscalibration and then, for effect purposes, take a *conservative* posterior:
- SME: model underpredicts by 0.04–0.09 -> add ~+0.065 (raise risk).
- Strategic Enterprise: overpredicts by 0.05–0.12 -> subtract ~-0.085 (lower risk).
- Public Sector: broadly OK -> no shift, but widen the interval for moderate sample size.
- Non-Native-Language Support (NNL): keep `p_now` but attach a large uncertainty band and *do not* trust the point estimate; treat both calibration and effect as poorly identified.
For data-quality problems (imputed / missing features), I additionally discount confidence rather than the point value.

**(c) Minimum evidence change (alert-persistence gate).**
An alert only "counts" as actionable if there is a *material evidence change* since the last swarm consideration, defined as EITHER (i) a new process event / new artifact (e.g., a new diagnostic log), OR (ii) a probability jump `p_now - p_prev` that survives the data-quality check (not attributable to imputation). A refreshed score with no new event and no genuine jump does **not** re-qualify a case. This is what separates I-05 (stale re-alert) from I-07 (new log).

**(d) Net-value screen using causal effect, not the score.**
For each eligible case compute
`expected direct benefit ≈ Δrisk_causal × Value − €450 − false-alarm burden`,
where `Δrisk_causal` is the *supplied risk-reduction range* (which I explicitly treat as an uncertain causal estimate, using its midpoint for ranking and its lower bound for a downside check), and the false-alarm burden is charged in proportion to `(1 - p*)` (a swarm on a case that would not have breached anyway is where the disruption cost is incurred). A case must clear a *positive lower-bound-adjusted* expected value, not merely a high score.

**(e) Portfolio / spillover gate.**
Because each used slot adds 8–20 min queue delay and raises breach probability of ~6 untreated diagnosis-stage cases by ~0.01–0.03 each, I evaluate the *marginal* case against the *aggregate* spillover it induces. I stop adding slots when the marginal net benefit no longer clearly exceeds marginal spillover harm, and I hold one slot in reserve for the possible pre-15:00 regulatory incident unless a case has an unusually dominant, low-uncertainty payoff.

**Prioritization** is a tier, not a single threshold: Tier A = eligible + material evidence + robust positive net value even at the lower effect bound + high effect-per-slot; Tier B = eligible + positive at midpoint but fragile; Tier C = wait/monitor; Ineligible = stage or evidence gate fails.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Cool-down.** After a swarm on a case, no second swarm for a defined refractory window (default 4 h) *unless* a materially new event occurs. Repeat swarms are otherwise forbidden by policy.

**Suppression (stale re-alerts).** A refreshed alert with no new process event and `|p_now − p_prev|` explainable by model refresh noise is *suppressed* — it does not re-enter the queue and does not consume review attention beyond logging. Suppression is logged so we can audit how often the 30-min refresh generates non-actionable alerts.

**Minimum evidence change to re-qualify.** Re-entry requires (i) a new event/artifact, or (ii) a jump that passes the data-quality check (not imputation-driven), or (iii) a stage transition that changes eligibility.

**Escalation.** If a suppressed case *later* acquires new evidence, or if a genuinely re-qualifying case keeps re-alerting across cycles without resolution, it escalates to human review / an alternative remedy (e.g., reassigning ownership) rather than repeated swarms.

**Re-entry.** A suppressed case can re-enter the actionable queue only through the minimum-evidence-change test above, resetting its "material evidence" timestamp.

**I-05 vs I-07 (the decisive comparison).**
- **I-05**: third alert, *no new event since the previous alert*, `p_prev 0.55 -> p_now 0.57` (trivial drift). This is the model’s 30-min refresh re-surfacing the same case. Even though its raw reduction range is attractive (0.25–0.40) and SME upshift raises risk, **acting on it rewards alert noise and would consume a scarce slot on stale information**. -> **Suppress**; revisit only on a new event.
- **I-07**: also a third alert, but a **new customer diagnostic log** was uploaded — genuinely new information — even though `p_now (0.72)` is slightly *lower* than `p_prev (0.75)`. The lower score is not disqualifying because the *evidence* changed and SME underprediction means true risk is likely higher (~0.79). **I-07 qualifies** on evidence, subject only to the timing check (45-min swarm inside 1.5h window). This is exactly the case where "lower `p_now`" must not override "material new evidence."

The general principle: **persistence of an unchanged alert is not evidence; change in evidence is.**

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-based prioritization starving low-value cases.** Ranking by `Δrisk × Value` systematically favors Strategic Enterprise (I-04, I-08) over SME/Public Sector even when the latter have larger, better-identified effects. Safeguard: reserve a **minimum share of slots over a rolling window (e.g., ≥30% of weekly slots) for non-top-value segments**, and cap the fraction of slots any single high-value segment can consume. Track a per-segment "served vs. eligible" ratio and alert if it drifts.

**Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support (I-09).** Sparse validation means both `p` and `Δ` are unreliable; a naive expected-value rule will *under-serve* NNL (uncertain benefit) or *mis-serve* it (uncalibrated risk). Safeguard: do **not** let sparse-segment cases lose slots purely due to wide intervals — instead route them to a **dedicated review path** and deliberately over-sample NNL cases for effect estimation so the uncertainty shrinks over time, rather than letting uncertainty become a permanent exclusion.

**Differential calibration across segments.** Because SME underpredicts and SE overpredicts, an uncorrected threshold silently over-serves SE and under-serves SME. Safeguard: keep the **segment calibration shifts explicit and versioned**, recompute them regularly (see §6), and monitor decision rates per segment against post-hoc breach outcomes.

**Selective intervention changing future training labels.** If we swarm exactly the high-risk cases and (sometimes) prevent their breaches, those cases record "no breach," teaching the model they were low-risk — a self-defeating feedback loop that erodes the very signal we act on. Safeguards: (i) **record treatment assignment as a feature / hold treated cases out of, or reweight them in, the training label pipeline**; (ii) keep a small **randomized or hold-back control** (see §6) so some high-risk cases are deliberately not swarmed to preserve untreated labels and enable effect estimation; (iii) train on *counterfactual-aware* targets where possible.

**Spillover systematically harming untreated groups.** If swarms cluster on high-value SE cases, the 0.01–0.03 queue-delay spillover falls repeatedly on whichever segment tends to sit in the shared diagnosis queue (plausibly SME/Public Sector), compounding unfairness invisibly. Safeguard: **attribute spillover harm by segment** and include it in the fairness dashboard; if one group absorbs disproportionate spillover, throttle concurrent slots or stagger swarms to spread congestion.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational monitoring.** Track slots used vs. available, swarm timing vs. SLA windows, suppression rate (how many alerts the 30-min refresh generates that are non-actionable), and reserve-slot usage vs. the regulatory-incident preference. Alert if suppression rate spikes (indicates refresh noise) or if swarms routinely start too late to matter.

**Predictive (calibration) monitoring.** Maintain rolling reliability curves **per segment** (SME, SE, Public Sector, NNL) with confidence bands sized to sample count. Re-estimate the segment shifts (currently SME +0.065, SE −0.085) on a schedule and after any workflow redesign (the SE overprediction itself came from a redesign). Watch for the imputation pathway (I-06-type) inflating jumps; flag features with high missingness.

**Causal-effect monitoring (the core of "don’t trust the score as the effect").** The supplied Δ ranges are assumptions, not measurements. Estimate real effects by (i) maintaining a **randomized hold-back**: among eligible, actionable cases, occasionally *not* swarming a fraction to build a control arm; (ii) using **stage- and segment-stratified comparisons** of treated vs. untreated breach rates; (iii) reporting effect estimates with intervals and updating the per-case reduction ranges as evidence accumulates — especially for NNL, which we deliberately over-sample. If measured effect for a stage/segment collapses toward zero, remove it from eligibility (as I-03’s stage already is).

**Capacity / spillover monitoring.** Measure actual added queue delay per slot against the assumed 8–20 min, and the actual breach-rate change on untreated diagnosis-stage cases against the assumed 0.01–0.03. Re-fit the spillover model under different congestion levels; if congestion routinely pushes spillover to the adverse end, lower the max concurrent slots.

**Fairness monitoring.** Per-segment served/eligible ratios, per-segment realized benefit, and **per-segment absorbed spillover**. Trip an alarm if any protected/low-value segment’s service rate or net outcome drifts beyond a set band.

**When to change the threshold / allocation rule.** Change the rule when: (a) recalibration shifts a segment’s calibration materially; (b) causal-effect estimates diverge from the supplied ranges (tighten or drop eligibility); (c) spillover measurements exceed assumptions (reduce concurrent slots); (d) feedback-loop drift appears (treated-case labels degrading model AUC on high-risk cases); or (e) fairness bands are breached. All rule changes are versioned, and the hold-back control is preserved so future changes remain measurable rather than guessed.
