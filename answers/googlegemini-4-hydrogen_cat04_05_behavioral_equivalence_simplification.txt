# 1. Equivalence Notions

Let $\Sigma = \{\text{'Open Case'}, \text{'Analyze Revision'}, \text{'Check Revision'}, \text{'Approve Revision'}, \text{'Rework Case'}, \text{'Close Case'}\}$ be the set of visible activity labels, and let $\tau$ denote silent steps (both explicit `tau` leaves and internal routing transitions introduced during Petri-net translation). Let $\pi_{\Sigma} : (\Sigma \cup \{\tau\})^* \to \Sigma^*$ be the projection that erases $\tau$ and keeps visible labels in execution order.

Unless stated otherwise, **duplicate visible labels are distinguished only by label and sequence position, not by transition/node identity**: two occurrences of `'Check Revision'` in different loop iterations produce the same symbol `'Check Revision'` in the trace. (In both models, every visible label appears on at most one static leaf, so static label identity and label string coincide per firing step, while runtime multiplicity still distinguishes 1 occurrence from $k \ge 2$ occurrences.)

1. **Visible trace equivalence after hiding `tau` ($\equiv_{\text{vis}}$):**
   Let $L_{\text{vis}}(M) = \{\pi_{\Sigma}(\sigma) \mid \sigma \text{ is a complete execution of } M \text{ from initial state to proper termination}\}$.
   Models $M_1, M_2$ are visibly trace equivalent ($M_1 \equiv_{\text{vis}} M_2$) iff $L_{\text{vis}}(M_1) = L_{\text{vis}}(M_2)$.

2. **Bounded visible-language equivalence under P3 ($\equiv_{P3}$):**
   For a trace $w \in \Sigma^*$, let $|w|$ be its length and $\#_a(w)$ be the number of occurrences of $a \in \Sigma$ in $w$. Define the P3-bounded language:
   $$L_{P3}(M) = \{w \in L_{\text{vis}}(M) \mid |w| \le 12 \;\land\; \#_{\text{'Analyze Revision'}}(w) \le 3\}.$$
   Models $M_1, M_2$ satisfy bounded visible-language equivalence under P3 ($M_1 \equiv_{P3} M_2$) iff $L_{P3}(M_1) = L_{P3}(M_2)$. Because P3 truncates traces at length $12$ and $3$ analyses, $M_1 \equiv_{P3} M_2$ is only bounded evidence and never proves $M_1 \equiv_{\text{vis}} M_2$; conversely, $L_{P3}(M_1) \neq L_{P3}(M_2)$ immediately refutes $M_1 \equiv_{\text{vis}} M_2$.

3. **Business-relevant equivalence under P1 ($\equiv_{P1}$):**
   Under P1, all visible labels (including `'Approve Revision'` and its exact placement) remain observable, and every complete trace $w \in L_{\text{vis}}(M)$ is evaluated against the operational compliance predicate $\mathsf{Comp}_{P1}(w)$:
   - $w$ starts with `'Open Case'` and ends with `'Close Case'`;
   - Splitting the interior of $w$ at every `'Rework Case'` yields segments $s_1, \dots, s_k$ ($k \ge 1$) such that each segment $s_i$ contains exactly one `'Analyze Revision'`, at least one `'Check Revision'`, and at most one `'Approve Revision'`.
   - Moreover, because P1 keeps approval count and placement visible, two models are P1-equivalent ($M_1 \equiv_{P1} M_2$) iff $L_{\text{vis}}(M_1) = L_{\text{vis}}(M_2)$ **and** both models satisfy $\mathsf{Comp}_{P1}$ on all admitted traces (or agree on both compliant and non-compliant visible traces). Under P1, any trace admitted by one model and not the other—especially one violating per-revision checking or changing approval placement—breaks equivalence.

4. **Abstracted dashboard equivalence under P2 ($\equiv_{P2}$):**
   Define the dashboard abstraction $\alpha_{P2} : \Sigma^* \to \{0,1\}^4 \times \mathbb{N} \times \{0,1\}$ for a complete visible trace $w$ as:
   $$\alpha_{P2}(w) = \bigl(\text{first}(w)=\text{'Open Case'},\; \text{last}(w)=\text{'Close Case'},\; \#_{\text{'Analyze Revision'}}(w)\ge 1,\; \#_{\text{'Check Revision'}}(w)\ge 1,\; \#_{\text{'Analyze Revision'}}(w),\; \#_{\text{'Rework Case'}}(w)\ge 1\bigr).$$
   (Equivalently, even if the dashboard additionally retains the projection $\pi_{\{\text{Open Case}, \text{Analyze Revision}, \text{Rework Case}, \text{Close Case}\}}(w)$ alongside the Boolean flag $\#_{\text{'Check Revision'}}(w)\ge 1$, the induced equivalence is the same for loop-structured revision traces.)
   Models $M_1, M_2$ are dashboard-equivalent under P2 ($M_1 \equiv_{P2} M_2$) iff $\alpha_{P2}(L_{\text{vis}}(M_1)) = \alpha_{P2}(L_{\text{vis}}(M_2))$. Under this abstraction, repeated `'Check Revision'` occurrences collapse to a single Boolean, `'Approve Revision'` and `tau` are hidden, and interleaving between `'Analyze Revision'` and `'Check Revision'` is ignored.

For $k=1$ (single attempt, no rework), when `tau` is chosen ($a=0$), both models produce the exact same two traces: `['Open Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision', 'Close Case']` and `['Open Case', 'Check Revision', 'Analyze Revision', 'Close Case']`. Thus, a test suite that only observes auto-approved ($a=0$), single-attempt ($k=1$) cases will fail to separate Model A from Model B!
- **Role of visible `'Approve Revision'`:** Selecting the visible branch `'Approve Revision'` at $k=1$ acts as a synchronization probe: in Model A, `'Approve Revision'` can only appear *after* `'Check Revision'`, whereas in Model B `'Check Revision'` can appear *after* `'Approve Revision'` (witness **T3**).
- **Role of P2 abstraction:** Under P2, hiding `'Approve Revision'` removes the **T3** distinction, and collapsing multiple `'Check Revision'` occurrences to a Boolean removes **T1, T2, T4, T5**, explaining why P1 and P3 yield `not equivalent` while P2 yields `equivalent only under stated abstraction`.

# 5. Bounded Comparison Code

The script below parses both trees, converts them to accepting Petri nets, checks soundness, and runs an **exhaustive bounded breadth-first search (BFS)** over markings using `pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(...)`.

### Handling of BFS edge cases
- **Silent transitions (`t.label is None`):** Firing a silent transition does not increase the visible trace length or `'Analyze Revision'` count. Therefore, even when a prefix has already reached the P3 bound (`len(trace) == 12` or `analyze_count == 3`), silent transitions remain enabled to allow loop exits, parallel joins, and reaching the final marking `fm`.
- **Repeated markings & `tau`-cycles:** Visited states are keyed by `(marking_key(m), trace_tuple)`. Deduplicating on both marking and visible prefix prevents infinite looping on silent cycles and avoids redundant silent interleavings while guaranteeing every distinct reachable visible trace within the bound is discovered.
- **Final-marking equality:** A trace is recorded as complete whenever `marking_key(m) == marking_key(fm)` (canonicalized by keeping only places with positive token count).
- **Exhaustive BFS vs. `pm4py.play_out(...)`:** `pm4py.play_out(...)` performs stochastic/random simulation and is **not exhaustive**: it samples traces according to random transition/branch weights, may miss valid interleavings (especially deeper multi-rework traces), and can never prove set equality or give exact bounded trace counts. Exhaustive BFS enumerates every reachable visible trace satisfying the P3 bound.

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import Marking

TREE_A_STR = """->(
  'Open Case',
  *(
    ->(
      +('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'),
      X('Approve Revision', tau)
    ),
    'Rework Case'
  ),
  'Close Case'
)"""

TREE_B_STR = """->(
  'Open Case',
  +(
    'Check Revision',
    *(
      ->(
        'Analyze Revision',
        X('Approve Revision', tau)
      ),
      'Rework Case'
    )
  ),
  'Close Case'
)"""


def normalize_tree_str(s: str) -> str:
    return " ".join(line.strip() for line in s.splitlines() if line.strip())


def marking_key(m: Marking) -> frozenset:
    return frozenset((p, c) for p, c in m.items() if c > 0)


def fire_transition(m: Marking, t) -> Marking:
    new_m = Marking(m)
    for arc in t.in_arcs:
        new_m[arc.source] -= arc.weight
        if new_m[arc.source] <= 0:
            new_m.pop(arc.source, None)
    for arc in t.out_arcs:
        new_m[arc.target] = new_m.get(arc.target, 0) + arc.weight
    return new_m


def exhaustive_bounded_traces(
    net,
    im: Marking,
    fm: Marking,
    max_visible_len: int = 12,
    max_analyze: int = 3,
) -> set[tuple[str, ...]]:
    """
    Exhaustive BFS over (marking, visible_trace) within P3 bounds.
    Unlike pm4py.play_out(...), this explores every reachable path up to the bound.
    """
    fm_key = marking_key(fm)
    start_state = (im, ())
    queue = [start_state]
    visited = {(marking_key(im), ())}
    complete_traces: set[tuple[str, ...]] = set()

    head = 0
    while head < len(queue):
        m, trace = queue[head]
        head += 1

        if marking_key(m) == fm_key:
            complete_traces.add(trace)

        analyze_count = trace.count("Analyze Revision")
        for t in pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, m):
            if t.label is None:
                # Silent transition: always explore if state (marking, trace) is new
                next_trace = trace
            else:
                if len(trace) + 1 > max_visible_len:
                    continue
                if t.label == "Analyze Revision" and analyze_count + 1 > max_analyze:
                    continue
                next_trace = trace + (t.label,)

            next_m = fire_transition(m, t)
            state_key = (marking_key(next_m), next_trace)
            if state_key not in visited:
                visited.add(state_key)
                queue.append((next_m, next_trace))

    return complete_traces


def main():
    tree_a = pm4py.parse_process_tree(normalize_tree_str(TREE_A_STR))
    tree_b = pm4py.parse_process_tree(normalize_tree_str(TREE_B_STR))

    net_a, im_a, fm_a = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_a)
    net_b, im_b, fm_b = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_b)

    sound_a = pm4py.check_soundness(net_a, im_a, fm_a, print_diagnostics=True)
    sound_b = pm4py.check_soundness(net_b, im_b, fm_b, print_diagnostics=True)
    print("Model A sound:", sound_a[0] if isinstance(sound_a, tuple) else sound_a)
    print("Model B sound:", sound_b[0] if isinstance(sound_b, tuple) else sound_b)

    traces_a = exhaustive_bounded_traces(net_a, im_a, fm_a, max_visible_len=12, max_analyze=3)
    traces_b = exhaustive_bounded_traces(net_b, im_b, fm_b, max_visible_len=12, max_analyze=3)

    only_a = sorted(traces_a - traces_b, key=lambda tr: (len(tr), tr))
    only_b = sorted(traces_b - traces_a, key=lambda tr: (len(tr), tr))
    common = traces_a & traces_b

    print(f"\nP3 bounded counts -> |A|={len(traces_a)}, |B|={len(traces_b)}, |A & B|={len(common)}")
    print(f"Unique to A: {len(only_a)}; Unique to B: {len(only_b)}")
    print("P3 equivalent?:", traces_a == traces_b)

    print("\nSample traces unique to Model A (first 5):")
    for tr in only_a[:5]:
        print(" ", list(tr))

    print("\nSample traces unique to Model B (first 5):")
    for tr in only_b[:5]:
        print(" ", list(tr))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

# 6. Stochastic and Footprint Comparison

### 1. Behavioral footprints (`pm4py.discover_footprints(...)`)
Footprint discovery extracts pairwise relations (causal `->`, parallel `||`, choice `#`) from directly-follows pairs $(x > y)$:
- **What footprints detect here:** In Model A, `'Check Revision'` can precede `'Approve Revision'` (`CR -> App`), whereas `'Approve Revision'` can never directly precede `'Check Revision'` (even across iterations, `'Rework Case'` separates them). In Model B, `'Check Revision'` can directly precede and directly follow `'Approve Revision'`, yielding `'Check Revision' || 'Approve Revision'`. Footprints therefore detect a difference between Models A and B.
- **Why equal footprints would still not prove equivalence:** Footprints only examine *pairwise directly-follows adjacency* and cannot count event multiplicities or enforce loop-scoped invariants. If `'Approve Revision'` were omitted from both trees, Models A and B would produce **identical** footprint matrices (`Open -> AR`, `Open -> CR`, `AR || CR`, `AR || Rework`, `CR || Rework`, `AR -> Close`, `CR -> Close`) despite Model A checking $k$ times and Model B checking only once!

### 2. Stochastic language & Earth Mover's Distance (`pm4py.get_stochastic_language(...)`, `pm4py.compute_emd(...)`)
Process trees are non-stochastic; they define sets of valid traces, not probabilities. To compute a stochastic language and EMD, one must explicitly supply or estimate trace probabilities (for example, via playout with declared branching/loop-exit weights).
- **What EMD can add:** Given a declared probabilistic assumption (or two observed event logs), EMD quantifies *how much probability mass* differs between the two distributions using edit distance between traces. It helps prioritize common vs. rare behavioral divergences.
- **Why low EMD does not establish trace equivalence:**
  1. **Sensitivity to assumed probabilities:** If the loop-exit probability is high (say $p_{\text{exit}} = 0.95$) and `'Approve Revision'` is rare ($p_{\text{app}} = 0.05$), almost all probability mass sits on the 2 shared traces (`<Open, AR, CR, Close>` and `<Open, CR, AR, Close>`), driving EMD very close to $0$ even though $L_{\text{vis}}(A) \neq L_{\text{vis}}(B)$.
  2. **Sampling omission:** When estimated from finite playout (`pm4py.play_out`), rare divergent traces may receive empirical probability $0$.

```python
import pm4py

# Assuming tree_a, tree_b from Section 5
fp_a = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_a)
fp_b = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_b)
print("Footprint sequence diffs:", fp_a["sequence"] ^ fp_b["sequence"])
print("Footprint parallel diffs:", fp_a["parallel"] ^ fp_b["parallel"])

# IMPORTANT: Process trees have no inherent probabilities.
# Using pm4py.play_out(...) imposes PM4Py's default playout sampling assumptions
# (uniform choice/loop-exit weights, finite sample size), declared here as an
# illustrative prior only—not a property of the original trees.
log_a = pm4py.play_out(tree_a, parameters={"no_traces": 2000})
log_b = pm4py.play_out(tree_b, parameters={"no_traces": 2000})

slang_a = pm4py.get_stochastic_language(log_a)
slang_b = pm4py.get_stochastic_language(log_b)
emd_value = pm4py.compute_emd(slang_a, slang_b)
print("Illustrative playout EMD (not a proof of equivalence):", emd_value)
```

# 7. Complexity Comparison

We define **depth** as edge-depth from the root (root at depth `0`). Static structural counts are derived directly from the two tree definitions:

| Metric | Model A | Model B | Comparison |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Process-tree node count** | 12 (5 operators, 7 leaves) | 12 (5 operators, 7 leaves) | Equal size |
| **Edge-depth (root = 0)** | 4 (node-depth = 5) | 5 (node-depth = 6) | Model B is 1 level deeper (`X` sits inside `->` inside `*` inside `+`) |
| **Visible leaves** | 6 | 6 | Same visible alphabet |
| **Silent (`tau`) leaves** | 1 | 1 | Identical (`tau` inside `X('Approve Revision', tau)`) |
| **Duplicate visible labels** | 0 | 0 | No repeated static label in either tree |
| **Converted Petri-net simplicity** | Compute via `pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net_a, im_a, fm_a)` | Compute via `pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net_b, im_b, fm_b)` | Both have identical operator/leaf multisets (`->`$\times 2$, `*`$\times 1$, `+`$\times 1$, `X`$\times 1$, 7 leaves), yielding comparable structural Petri-net simplicity; exact float values require running the code below. |

### Python code for structural metrics and Petri-net simplicity

```python
from collections import Counter
import pm4py


def tree_metrics(node, depth: int = 0) -> dict:
    if not node.children:
        is_tau = node.label is None
        return {
            "nodes": 1,
            "max_depth": depth,
            "visible_leaves": 0 if is_tau else 1,
            "silent_leaves": 1 if is_tau else 0,
            "labels": [] if is_tau else [node.label],
        }

    total_nodes = 1
    max_d = depth
    vis = 0
    sil = 0
    labels = []
    for child in node.children:
        sub = tree_metrics(child, depth + 1)
        total_nodes += sub["nodes"]
        max_d = max(max_d, sub["max_depth"])
        vis += sub["visible_leaves"]
        sil += sub["silent_leaves"]
        labels.extend(sub["labels"])

    label_counts = Counter(labels)
    duplicates = sum(c - 1 for c in label_counts.values() if c > 1)
    return {
        "nodes": total_nodes,
        "max_depth": max_d,
        "visible_leaves": vis,
        "silent_leaves": sil,
        "duplicate_labels": duplicates,
        "labels": labels,
    }


for name, s in [("Model A", TREE_A_STR), ("Model B", TREE_B_STR)]:
    t = pm4py.parse_process_tree(" ".join(line.strip() for line in s.splitlines() if line.strip()))
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(t)
    metrics = tree_metrics(t)
    simplicity = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm)
    print(f"{name}: nodes={metrics['nodes']}, edge_depth={metrics['max_depth']}, "
          f"visible={metrics['visible_leaves']}, silent={metrics['silent_leaves']}, "
          f"duplicates={metrics['duplicate_labels']}, "
          f"net=(|P|={len(net.places)}, |T|={len(net.transitions)}, |F|={len(net.arcs)}), "
          f"simplicity={simplicity}")
```

# 8. Simplified Model

A single simplified model cannot preserve **all three** views simultaneously: preserving P1 (and P3) requires keeping the per-iteration parallel check, optional approval, and rework loop—which is already structurally minimal in Model A. Therefore, we select **P2 (executive dashboard view)** as the target semantics, where substantial simplification is genuinely justified.

### 1. Compact Process Tree

```text
->(
  'Open Case',
  'Check Revision',
  *(
    'Analyze Revision',
    'Rework Case'
  ),
  'Close Case'
)
```

Compared to Model A (12 nodes, edge-depth 4, 1 `tau`, `+`, `X`) and Model B (12 nodes, edge-depth 5, 1 `tau`, `+`, `X`), this simplified model has **7 nodes** (2 operators `->`, `*`; 5 visible leaves), **edge-depth 2**, **0 silent leaves**, **0 duplicate labels**, and eliminates both concurrency (`+`) and choice (`X`).

### 2. Self-contained PM4Py Code

```python
import pm4py

SIMPLIFIED_TREE_STR = """->(
  'Open Case',
  'Check Revision',
  *(
    'Analyze Revision',
    'Rework Case'
  ),
  'Close Case'
)"""

simplified_model = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    " ".join(line.strip() for line in SIMPLIFIED_TREE_STR.splitlines() if line.strip())
)

net_s, im_s, fm_s = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(simplified_model)
sound_s = pm4py.check_soundness(net_s, im_s, fm_s, print_diagnostics=False)
print("Simplified model sound:", sound_s[0] if isinstance(sound_s, tuple) else sound_s)
```

### 3. Semantics Preservation Table

| Property | Status | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `'Open Case'` first & `'Close Case'` last | **Exact** | Preserved by the outer `->` operator. |
| At least one `'Analyze Revision'` & at least one `'Check Revision'` | **Exact** | `'Check Revision'` executes once; `*( 'Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case' )` executes `'Analyze Revision'` at least once. |
| Exact count of `'Analyze Revision'` ($k \ge 1$) and whether `'Rework Case'` occurred ($k \ge 2$) | **Exact** | Loop iterations $k \ge 1$ match Models A and B 1-to-1, and even the projected sequence over `{'Open Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case', 'Close Case'}` is exact. |
| Repeated `'Check Revision'` occurrences ($k$ checks in A) | **Abstracted** | Collapsed to one `'Check Revision'`, preserving the P2 Boolean `check_occurred = True`. |
| `'Approve Revision'` and `tau` choices | **Abstracted / Lost** | Removed entirely as required by P2; approval counts and placements are no longer represented. |
| Interleaving of `'Analyze Revision'` and `'Check Revision'` | **Weakened / Lost** | Fixed sequentially as `'Check Revision'` followed by the analysis loop. |
| P1 operational compliance (every revision checked before rework/close) | **Lost** | Multi-attempt traces have only one upfront check, violating P1. |

### 4. Regression and Negative Traces

**Regression traces (accepted by `simplified_model` and preserving P2 summaries of Models A and B):**
1. `['Open Case', 'Check Revision', 'Analyze Revision', 'Close Case']` ($k=1$, `check_occurred=True`, `rework_occurred=False`).
2. `['Open Case', 'Check Revision', 'Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Close Case']` ($k=2$, `check_occurred=True`, `rework_occurred=True`).
3. `['Open Case', 'Check Revision', 'Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Close Case']` ($k=3$, `check_occurred=True`, `rework_occurred=True`).

**Negative traces (rejected by `simplified_model`):**
1. `['Open Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Close Case']` — violates P2 (missing `'Check Revision'`); rejected by `simplified_model`, Model A, and Model B.
2. `['Open Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision', 'Approve Revision', 'Close Case']` — valid in Models A and B under P1/P3, but rejected by `simplified_model` because `'Approve Revision'` is removed and `'Check Revision'` is fixed before the analysis loop.
3. `['Open Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision', 'Rework Case', 'Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision', 'Close Case']` — valid in Model A under P1/P3, but rejected by `simplified_model` because repeated checks are collapsed under P2.

# 9. Final Recommendation

- **For P1 (operational compliance):** **Use Model A only.** Model A is the only original model that enforces a dedicated `'Check Revision'` for every `'Analyze Revision'` before `'Approve Revision'`, `'Rework Case'`, or `'Close Case'`. Neither Model B nor `simplified_model` should be used for compliance verification or execution under P1.
- **For P2 (executive dashboard):** **Both Model A and Model B are equivalent under $\alpha_{P2}$, and `simplified_model` may replace either one purely as a dashboard reporting/analytics model.** Replacing Model A with `simplified_model` in production execution, however, would destroy operational compliance (P1), so `simplified_model` must be restricted to the P2 reporting layer.
- **For P3 (bounded testing):** **Models A and B are not equivalent even within the P3 bound**, and P3 should be used only as a falsification test (already catching discrepancies at length $\le 8$). Never treat bounded trace equivalence—or low EMD or matching playout samples—as proof of unbounded process equivalence: bounded checks truncate deep rework loops, and stochastic metrics can mask rare but compliance-critical counterexamples.
