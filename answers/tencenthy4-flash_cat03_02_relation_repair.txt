## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

| Rule / signal | Classification | How used here |
|---|---|---|
| Do not invent events or objects; every E2O/O2O endpoint must exist. | hard invariant | All proposed endpoints are existing IDs only. |
| O2O qualifiers are directional; `DEL-Y carries PK-8` is not `PK-8 carries DEL-Y`. | hard invariant | Proposed O2O additions preserve source-target direction. |
| Do not add exact duplicate relations. | hard invariant | Validation code checks duplicates. |
| `Create Order`, `Add Item`, `Pack Item`, `Dispatch Package`, `Issue Invoice`, `Record Payment` normal cardinalities. | strong default | These define expected missing “slots”: e.g., ev10 wants a Delivery, ev14 wants an Invoice. |
| `Pack Item` item-only rows, bulk dispatch, portal-only `Confirm Delivery`, and unapplied cash are legitimate exceptions. | strong default / exception | Used to avoid over-repairing ev08, ev11, and PAY-2. |
| Identifier prefixes such as `N-421?`, `N-42*`, `PK8-2`, `B-771`. | weak heuristic | Strongest when matching a unique object, but downgraded by `?`, `*`, or conflicting structure. |
| Time order and same/near timestamps. | weak heuristic | ev10 at 10:00 after ev07/ev08 supports dispatch; it does not uniquely identify carrier. |
| Customer, route, amount, and amount-sum matching. | weak heuristic | `230 = 130 + 100` and customer C17 support bundle-level invoice/payment hypotheses but do not prove them. |
| Existing O2O structure: `contains`, `packs`, `carries`, `bills`, `settles`. | weak-to-strong evidence depending on source | Existing O2Os are retained; new O2Os inferred from the same E2O event are not counted as independent confirmation. |
| Circular support: infer E2O, then infer O2O from it, then cite both as independent. | evidence against a repair | Treated as one hypothesis, not two independent signals. |

Combination strategy: first apply hard invariants; then ask whether a strong default leaves a conspicuous dangling object/event; then score candidates by identifier match, customer/route/time consistency, amount evidence, and fit with existing O2Os. Ambiguous markers (`?`, `*`) and explicitly allowed exceptional cases reduce confidence. Arithmetic is evidence only, never proof. When two repairs are nearly equally coherent, the conservative one leaves the edge unresolved.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A — conservative/minimum-change repair.**  
This bundle adds only P1–P2: ev10 relates to DEL-Y, and DEL-Y carries PK-8. This fills the clearest missing Dispatch slot because `N-421?` has a unique, route-consistent DEL-Y candidate. Bundle A keeps ev08 item-only because the domain explicitly permits item-only packing rows. It does not assign ev11 to DEL-X or DEL-Y: `N-42*` is compatible with both, and the portal may have retained only the order. It leaves INV-44 linked only to ORD-A and leaves PAY-2 unapplied. The resulting lifecycles are intentionally incomplete: ORD-B has no invoice or delivery confirmation, DEL-X has dispatch but no confirmation, and PAY-2 has a payment event but no settlement object. These are plausible incompletenesses, not errors requiring repair.

**Bundle B — consolidated C17 shipment/invoice interpretation.**  
Bundle B accepts the scanner-prefix and arithmetic evidence more aggressively. It adds ev08  PK-8 and O2O `PK-8 packs IT-B1`, so IT-B1 is interpreted as packed in PK-8 despite ev07 already using PK-8 for IT-A2. It also adds ev10  DEL-Y and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`. For ev11, it relates the event jointly to DEL-X and DEL-Y, because `N-42*` covers both carrier refs and ORD-A has items in both packages; DEL-X-only or DEL-Y-only would imply an arbitrary partial acknowledgement. It still does not add ORD-B to ev11 because the portal object observed is only ORD-A. Bundle B also adds ev12  ORD-B and `INV-44 bills ORD-B`, interpreting the invoice as covering both C17 orders, and ev14  INV-44 plus `PAY-2 settles INV-44`, interpreting the two payments as settling the 230 invoice. As a full bundle, this yields a coherent commercial story: two C17 orders, two packed items in PK-8 plus one in PK-7, two dispatches, one joint delivery acknowledgement, one consolidated invoice, and two settlement payments. Its risk is that several weaker signals are mutually reinforcing without being independent proofs.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Orders.** ORD-A has Create, Add Item ×2, Confirm Delivery, and Issue Invoice. Its delivery confirmation is not tied to a Delivery object in Bundle A, which is allowed. ORD-B has Create and Add Item only; Bundle A leaves it without invoice or confirmation. This is incomplete but not structurally impossible. In Bundle B, ORD-B gains an invoice event, making the two orders share INV-44; that is a suspicious but allowed one-Invoice-to-many-Orders pattern. If ev11 were also added to ORD-B, the bundle would become stronger but more speculative than B as stated.

**Items.** IT-A1 and IT-A2 have creation/ordering, packing, and package dispatch paths. IT-B1 has Add Item and O2O containment in ORD-B; its Pack Item event remains item-only in Bundle A. The suspicious cardinality in Bundle B is PK-8 packing both IT-A2 and IT-B1 from different orders. That is not forbidden, but it changes process semantics from order-specific packing to consolidated packing.

**Packages.** PK-7 has a coherent pack-dispatch lifecycle and DEL-X carries it. PK-8 has IT-A2 packing and, in Bundle A, dispatch on DEL-Y. In Bundle B, PK-8 has two Pack Item events and carries items from two orders. No reverse-direction `PK-8 carries DEL-Y` edge is proposed.

**Deliveries.** DEL-X is dispatched with PK-7 but has no Confirm Delivery edge in either A or B-as-stated. DEL-Y is orphaned in the unrepaired log; Bundle A gives it a dispatch lifecycle. Bundle B additionally ties ev11 to DEL-X and DEL-Y, creating a one-event-to-many-Delivery acknowledgement, explicitly allowed by domain semantics but not proven.

**Invoices.** INV-44 has Issue Invoice and PAY-1 settlement. In Bundle A its linked order value is 130 while total is 230; this is a mismatch but fees/taxes/discounts are allowed. Bundle B reduces the mismatch by adding ORD-B, but the evidence is arithmetic plus customer, not proof.

**Payments.** PAY-1 has a clean Record Payment and settlement. PAY-2 has its own event but no Invoice in Bundle A; this is acceptable as unapplied cash. Bundle B makes PAY-1 and PAY-2 two settlements of one invoice, a plausible cardinality but not forced.

**Independence and circularity.** Existing `contains` links are independently supported by Add Item E2Os plus observed O2Os. New `DEL-Y carries PK-8` is not independent of ev10  DEL-Y; it is a structured restatement of the same dispatch hypothesis. Similarly, `PK-8 packs IT-B1` in Bundle B is not independent of ev08  PK-8, and `PAY-2 settles INV-44` is not independent of ev14  INV-44. These should not be counted as separate confirmation.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**OC-DFG.** Event-level activity counts do not change because no events are invented. Unrepaired, ev10 contributes only Package-related behavior and ev11 only Order-related confirmation. Bundle A creates a Dispatch Package  Delivery object flow for DEL-Y. Bundle B additionally creates Pack Item  Package for ev08, Confirm Delivery  Delivery for DEL-X and DEL-Y, Issue Invoice  ORD-B, and Record Payment  INV-44 for PAY-2. Edges involving Delivery after Confirm Delivery appear in B but remain absent in A.

**OTG.** Unrepaired O2O edges lack `DEL-Y carries PK-8`. Bundle A adds that one directed edge. Bundle B adds `PK-8 packs IT-B1`, `INV-44 bills ORD-B`, and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`; the graph becomes more consolidated and many-to-one. Reversing any of these would create materially wrong OTG semantics.

**ET–OT graph.** Bundle A changes ev10 from Package-only to Package+Delivery at the object-type level. Bundle B changes ev08 from Item-only to Item+Package, ev11 from Order-only to Order+Delivery, ev12 from one Order object to two Order objects, and ev14 from Payment-only to Payment+Invoice. Because type-level counts may ignore object multiplicity, ev12’s type signature may look unchanged even though total object-relation counts increase.

**Flattenings.** On Order, A leaves ORD-B without invoice; B duplicates ev12 into both ORD-A and ORD-B cases. On Package, A gives PK-8 the ev10 dispatch; B also gives PK-8 ev08 with IT-B1. On Delivery, A gives DEL-Y ev10 only; B gives DEL-X and DEL-Y both ev10/ev11-style fragments, duplicating ev11 across delivery cases. On Invoice, A has one order and one payment; B has two orders and two payments. On Payment, A leaves PAY-2 as a one-event case; B connects it to invoice settlement semantics.

**Activity frequencies.** Event frequency remains fixed at 14. Object-activity relation count rises from 22 unrepaired to 23 in A and 28 in B. Unique objects touched by ev10 rise from one to two in A/B; ev11 rises from one to three in B; ev12 rises from two to three in B; ev14 rises from one to two in B. Flattened activity counts inflate where one event is duplicated across object cases, especially ev11 in B and ev12 in B.

**Equivalent-execution clustering around Order.** Unrepaired, ORD-A clusters as Create  Add Item ×2  Confirm Delivery  Issue Invoice, while ORD-B clusters as Create  Add Item. Bundle A largely preserves that separation. Bundle B moves ORD-B closer to ORD-A by adding Issue Invoice, so clustering may show a shared C17 consolidated-invoice execution. If ev11 were also added to ORD-B, the two orders would become even more similar, but that is a further non-conservative step not selected here.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. Adding ev08  PK-8 and `PK-8 packs IT-B1` can create a false mixed-order package, making ORD-B appear shipped with ORD-A goods.
2. Assigning ev11 uniquely to DEL-X ignores that `N-42*` matches DEL-Y too, and that ORD-A has items in both packages.
3. Adding ev11 to both deliveries and also to ORD-B would manufacture a delivery confirmation for ORD-B that the portal object does not support.
4. Adding ORD-B to INV-44 solely because `130 + 100 = 230` can create a false multi-order billing pattern; invoice totals may include fees, taxes, discounts, or corrections.
5. Adding PAY-2 to INV-44 can falsely imply full settlement and falsely connect bank_ref B-772 to the C17 invoice; unapplied cash is explicitly possible.
6. Linking ev10 to DEL-X to force DEL-X into a bulk dispatch would orphan DEL-Y and contradict the stronger `N-421?` identifier match.
7. Counting inferred O2O edges as independent corroboration of the E2O assumptions that generated them would make confidence look higher than the evidence warrants.
8. Reversing O2O direction, such as `PK-8 carries DEL-Y`, would corrupt OTG, flattening, and lifecycle interpretation even if the object pair is otherwise plausible.
