# 1. Mechanism map
## Restructuring actions -> mechanisms -> observable log effects
Central proposition: shared-service reduces *nominal* handovers counted as local loops, but replaces them with *queued* handovers that have different waiting-time, ownership, and learning properties.

| Restructuring action | Causal mechanism | Process-mining observable |
|---|---|---|
| Merge N_Tech, S_Tech, E_Tech into Technical Shared Service + centralize Specialist Review / Legal / Risk / difficult Quality Check | **Nominal handover reduction:** local caseworker <-> embedded tech loop (36+31 per 100) collapses into caseworker -> Shared Service (41) -> caseworker (36). Count looks flat/slightly lower, but topology changes from dense local dyads to star. | `discover_handover_of_work_network`: density falls within team, centrality of 3-5 shared resources spikes; `discover_working_together_network`: North/South intra-team edges weaken; `get_variants`: fewer `Local Technical Assessment <-> Local Customer Contact` loops |
| Same centralization + routing rules: all high-complexity + legal/risk/security to shared queues | **Central queue formation + specialist utilization:** utilization +15% target pushes Legal 93%->~100%+, Risk 91%->~100%+, Tech 68-82%->80-95%. Queueing theory: wait grows non-linearly near 90%+. Management estimate 4.0h + 10.5h median may be optimistic. | `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `get_service_time`, `discover_performance_dfg` on Specialist Review, Legal/Risk Review: service time stable, waiting time up, variance up; `extract_temporal_features_dataframe`: arrival bunching -> wait spikes |
| More cases correctly routed + advice returned via queue | **Rework (two opposing effects):** standardization + better triage reduces rework for standard cases; loss of context + inconsistent notes increases rework for complex/regional cases. Current legal/risk handover already 14% rework, cross-team 18%. | `get_rework_cases_per_activity` + `filter_event_attribute_values` on `rework_flag`, `handover_reason`: rework after `Technical Shared Service -> local caseworker` vs old `embedded tech -> same caseworker`; DFG: `Quality Check -> Rework -> Specialist Review` loops |

Summary chain: fewer local loops **=>** star network + higher utilization **=>** lower service variation for standard cases BUT higher waiting variance + context loss **=>** rework down for simple, up for complex **=>** local learning declines **=>** more future referrals **=>** further central load (reinforcing loop).

# 2. Scenario forecasts
## Optimistic scenario – standardization works
- **Handover pattern:** Handover-of-work confirms management table: caseworker -> Shared Service ~41/100, return ~36/100, cross-team 6->3/100, local supervisor loop disappears. Subcontracting network shows clean delegation-return without repeated bouncing (`reassignment_count` 0-1).
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** Shared Service median 3.5-4.0h as predicted, Legal/Risk ~10h but with low variance because triage is accurate and difficult Quality Check is well-scoped. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` shows waiting concentrated in Specialist Review only.
- **Cycle-time distribution:** Median 7.3d -> ~6.5d, p90 18d -> ~15d for all teams. East improves most: 8.6d -> 6.9d, p90 22.8d -> 17d. SLA miss -2pp.
- **Rework and quality pattern:** Rework 9% -> 7%: fewer unnecessary referrals, standardization catches errors. Rework after shared handover 8-9% (vs 14% before for legal/risk).
- **Role clarity and accountability:** Shared approval roles discovered cleanly via `discover_organizational_roles`: Intake vs Caseworker vs Shared Specialist vs Shared Approver distinct. Ownership clear because routing rules + case notes standardized.
- **Fairness implications:** All segments improve; high-complexity penalty small (+0.5d). No language/regional gap.
- **Early warning signs that confirm:** Return rate Shared Service -> same caseworker high without second referral; `local_expertise_flag` stable; approval queue wait <3h in first 2 weeks.

## Central scenario – average improves, tails and expertise degrade
- **Handover pattern:** Nominal count matches forecast, but subcontracting reveals 15-20% of shared advice requires clarification loop: Shared Service -> caseworker -> Shared Service again. `reassignment_count>=2` rises from baseline. Approval queue shows 49 in but only 40 out promptly, rest loop to specialist.
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** Median wait as forecast (4.0h tech, 10.5h legal/risk, 3.0h approval) but variance doubles. Performance DFG: Specialist Review waiting p50 stable, p90 +60%. Morning arrivals wait 2x afternoon.
- **Cycle-time distribution:** Mean/median flat to -5%: 7.3d -> 7.0d. But p90 flat or +2d; East median -1.0d (relief), North/South median +0.3d. Bimodal: 70% simple faster, 30% complex slower.
- **Rework and quality pattern:** Overall rework 9% -> 9.5%: simple-case rework down 2pp, high-complexity/legal rework up from 14% -> 16-18% due to missing local context. Quality Check -> Rework variant share rises for complex.
- **Role clarity and accountability:** Role discovery shows blurred approver/specialist cluster; caseworkers report "don't know who approved." Resource similarity: former N_Tech/S_Tech now similar to each other, dissimilar to caseworkers (skill split).
- **Fairness implications:** East net beneficiary; North/South net neutral/negative. `customer_segment` requiring regional knowledge and `required_specialty=legal/risk` see +1-2d vs average. Local satisfaction North 4.3->4.0, East 3.8->4.0.
- **Early warning signs:** Within days: shared queue length growing, `handover_reason=clarification` up, second referrals up, approval wait p90 >6h even if median 3h, East improvement masking North/South deterioration in aggregate.

## Adverse scenario – hidden bottleneck + de-skilling
- **Handover pattern:** Caseworker -> Shared Service inflates 41 -> 55-60/100 because loss of informal advice means caseworkers over-refer defensively. Legal/Risk queue 12 -> 18/100 (over-detection). Multiple bounces: 25% need 2+ shared touches.
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** Utilization >95% -> exponential wait: tech median 4h -> 8-12h, legal/risk 10.5h -> 18-24h, approval 3h -> 6-8h. Working-together network shows shared pool working together constantly but still backlogged; service time unchanged, waiting dominates.
- **Cycle-time distribution:** Median +1-2d (7.3d -> 8.5-9d), p90 +8-12d (18d -> 26-30d). East still slightly better than before on median but worse on tail; North/South markedly worse. SLA miss +4-6pp.
- **Rework and quality pattern:** Rework 9% -> 13-15%: advice applied without local context fails Quality Check; difficult Quality Check centralized without local knowledge misses regional conventions. Rework loop `Implementation -> Quality Check -> Rework -> Specialist Review` becomes top variant for complex cases.
- **Role clarity and accountability:** No clear owner: local caseworker thinks shared service owns, shared service thinks local owns. `handover_reason=ownership unclear / approval clarification` spikes; supervisor-approval activity fragmented across many shared approvers with low working-together continuity.
- **Fairness implications:** High-complexity, security/legal, and language-accommodation segments suffer severe delays and rework; simple low-risk cases look good, hiding inequity. North/South local expertise decays fastest; learning stalls for juniors.
- **Early warning signs:** Day 3-10: queue length monotonic growth, `reassignment_count` mean up, informal `local caseworker -> embedded tech` replaced by formal queue with no quick questions, staff feedback: "easier to route than to decide," approval pull rate < arrival rate.

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time
Average cycle time can stay flat or improve ~5% while all of the following worsen:

- **Tail latency explosion:** Median driven by 70% simple cases with slightly more automation (83 vs 88 handovers). Shared queues add variance: p50 4h but p90 12h+ when Legal 93%+Risk 91% pushed over 95%. Average hides that p90 18d->26d and SLA misses concentrate in few cases. Must monitor p90/p95 and performance DFG variance, not mean.
- **High-complexity cases subsidize the average:** Rule routes *all* high-complexity to shared service. Even if simple Eligibility is faster, complex cases incur + queue + context loss + clarification bounce. Segmented analysis by `case_complexity=high` and `required_specialty in {legal,risk,security}` may show +2-4d while low-complexity shows -1d. Pooling helps throughput but hurts the cases that matter most for risk/quality.
- **Local knowledge loss:** North/South strength was regional conventions reducing rework (6-9% vs 18% cross-team). Central advice without `local_expertise_flag` and inconsistent notes repeats the cross-team failure at scale. Observable only after months as `local_expertise_flag=False` share rises and complex-case rework creeps 14%->18%. Average initially benefits from correct routing, then degrades as locals stop learning.
- **Fairness across teams and customers:** East (E_Tech 82%, 8.6d/22.8d) gains from relief; North (6.8d) / South (7.4d) lose local advantage. Aggregation hides redistribution. Similarly, `customer_segment` needing language/regional accommodation faces more handovers (loss of local contact continuity) while standard segments improve. Need team x segment x complexity slices.
- **Learning and de-skilling:** If all hard cases routed away, juniors never do `Local Technical Assessment` on hard cases. Resource similarity will show caseworkers narrowing to Intake/Eligibility. Short-term quality up (experts decide), long-term resilience down and referral rate up (41->60/100). Invisible in 4-week average, visible in rising referral propensity and falling local resolution rate.
- **Accountability and role clarity:** Shared approval queue (49 in/43 out) standardizes *decision* but diffuses *ownership*. Log shows many approvers touching a case briefly (`working_together` fragmented, `subcontracting` shallow). Defects increase in `Quality Check` because no one owns end-to-end. Average wait 3.0h looks fine, but `handover_reason=approval clarification` and post-approval rework reveal churn.
- **Exception handling and resilience to absence/peak:** Local model fails one team at a time; shared model fails all teams together. Cross-team handovers 6->3 looks like improvement, but correlation of waits across teams rises. A sick day or surge in shared pool of 3-5 people blocks high-complexity work enterprise-wide. Average over a calm month misses this; need stress-period analysis and temporal features showing synchronized backlogs. Related: informal early advice that prevented referrals disappears – its absence never appears as a handover, only as higher formal referral volume later.

# 5. Pilot design
## Pilot scope
Pilot in **East only** for 8-12 weeks: East has clearest upside (overload) and limits blast radius for North/South local expertise. Keep North/South as controls with existing embedded tech + local supervisor. In East: pool E_Tech + 1 rotating tech from North/South (secondment, not permanent move) + dedicated Legal/Risk triage slot (protect from tech queue). Keep one East supervisor for low-value cases; route only medium/high-value East cases to shared approval queue. Do not centralize difficult Quality Check in pilot – keep local QC to isolate effect.

## Comparison group
Difference-in-differences: East-pilot vs North/South-control, pre vs post. Additionally within-East comparison: pilot-eligible (high-complexity / legal-risk-security) vs non-eligible (simple) to detect spillover. Match on seasonality and intake volume (Bot_Intake share).

## Pre/post observation window
- **Pre:** 8-12 weeks baseline (covers monthly cycle, absence cover examples). Freeze routing rules and collect handover network, performance DFG, rework, p50/p90.
- **Post pilot:** 8-12 weeks minimum; leading indicators daily, lagging at 4, 8, 12 weeks. Include 2-week wash-in excluded from primary comparison (learning routing rules). Follow-up 3 months after to catch learning effects.

## Segmentation by team, complexity, specialty, and customer segment
All comparisons sliced by `team` (N/S/E), `case_complexity` (low/medium/high), `required_specialty` (none/tech/legal/risk/security), `customer_segment` (standard / regional-accommodation / language-accommodation / high-value), `priority`, `location`. Report median + p90 + SLA miss per cell, minimum n disclosed. Fairness dashboard: gap high-low complexity, accommodation vs standard.

## Guardrail metrics
Halt/adjust if any for 2 consecutive weeks: shared tech wait p50 >5h or p90 >10h; legal/risk wait p90 >20h; bounces >10/100; rework after shared handover >12%; SLA miss +3pp vs baseline in any team/segment; high-complexity p90 +20%; `reassignment_count>=2` share +50%; staff overtime/utilization >90% sustained.

## Staff feedback collection
Weekly 15-min pulse for caseworkers, techs, approvers: clarity of ownership (1-5), ease of getting informal advice, note quality, fairness perception, time spent clarifying vs deciding. Collect `handover_reason` free-text audit + 5 case walkthroughs per week per team (complex + accommodation cases). Separate channel for specialists on triage load and context adequacy.

## Rollback or adjustment triggers
- **Rollback to local model** if: overall p90 +15% or rework +4pp or SLA miss +4pp, or any protected segment (high-complexity, accommodation) p90 +25% / satisfaction -0.5.
- **Adjust (not full rollback)** if: queue variance high but median ok -> add specialist capacity / split Legal/Risk from Tech queue / restore local informal office hours; if role clarity low -> reintroduce named approver + end-to-end owner field; if over-referral -> restore local tech office hours and tighten referral criteria.
- Pre-commit: decision gate at week 6 and week 12; no enterprise rollout unless East p50 *and* p90 improve or stay neutral *and* no segment degrades >10% *and* role-clarity pulse stable.

# 6. Recommendation
## Recommendation: pilot first with modified design – do not proceed to full rollout
The proposal has a plausible benefit concentrated in East and for standard cases, but full rollout risks converting localized strain into systemic bottleneck, trading a measured local-loop cost (3.2h/1.6h waits, 8-9% rework) for larger queued waits (4.0h/10.5h/3.0h medians with higher variance) at 93%/91% already-saturated Legal/Risk, plus loss of North/South local expertise and accountability.

Conditional path: **(a)** pilot East-only as above, **(b)** split Technical vs Legal/Risk vs difficult QC queues (do not combine), **(c)** preserve local informal advice (e.g., 30-min daily tech office hours, keep N_Tech/S_Tech embedded part-time), **(d)** keep one local supervisor per team for low-value/fast-track with named owner, use shared queue only for medium/high-value, **(e)** standardize case notes *before* pooling, **(f)** cap specialist utilization target at 85% not +15% indefinitely, add capacity if exceeded.

Proceed to phased expansion only if pilot meets guardrails for medians *and* tails *and* fairness *and* learning/clarity pulses. If East improves but North/South would lose, consider asymmetric model: shared service as overflow for East + complex overflow, not mandatory routing for all high-complexity in North/South.

## What management should not infer from early averages
Do not infer success from a stable or 5-10% improved average/median cycle time in weeks 1-4. Early averages are dominated by simple cases with slightly less intake friction, Hawthorne attention, and unused queue capacity before backlog accumulates. They hide: rising p90, high-complexity penalty, defensive over-referral that lags by weeks, de-skilling that lags by months, and East gains masking North/South losses. Require segmented p90, rework-after-handover, bounce rate, and fairness gaps to move together before claiming benefit. A lower nominal handover count is not efficiency if each handover now waits longer and bounces more.
