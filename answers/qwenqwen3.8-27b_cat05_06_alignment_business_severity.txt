## 1. Materiality Framework

The severity framework below is deliberately judgment-based. There is no single canonical ranking because alignment cost, business risk, evidence reliability, and remediation urgency are different dimensions. A case can have low technical alignment cost and high business risk, or high technical alignment cost and low business risk.

### Severity scale

| Severity | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 1 | No material business impact. Harmless technical noise, fitting hidden move, or non-business telemetry. |
| 2 | Minor gap with low exposure. Usually model-scope mismatch, label mismatch, or non-payable terminal event. Remediation is important but not urgent. |
| 3 | Moderate control, evidence, or policy gap. Moderate exposure, recurring pattern, or weak audit evidence. Requires structured remediation. |
| 4 | Severe control failure or high exposure with mitigants, lower confidence, or limited reversibility. Likely unauthorized payment, weak notification evidence, or data-aware policy bypass. |
| 5 | Critical control failure with high exposure, irreversibility, fraud/regulatory risk, or payment without required approval/fraud screening. Immediate investigation and control intervention required. |

### Materiality factors

1. **Control objective**  
   Does the deviation affect fraud prevention, authorization, payment integrity, notification, or auditability? A missing fraud screen is more material than a duplicate UI event.

2. **Amount and exposure**  
   EUR 480,000, EUR 250,000, and EUR 90,000 cases carry more financial exposure than EUR 800 or EUR 2,000 cases.

3. **Reversibility**  
   Payment is much harder to reverse than a missing log event or a model-scope mismatch. A payment before approval is more severe than a missing assessment event when external evidence exists.

4. **Timing**  
   Payment before approval is more severe than late notification within 24 hours. A bank change one hour before payment is more severe than a bank change outside the policy window.

5. **Customer and regulatory harm**  
   Missing or unreliable notification can create customer harm and regulatory exposure. Fraud-screen gaps on high-risk claims can create financial and compliance harm.

6. **Recurrence**  
   A single case is less material than a repeated pattern. The 74 retry-duplicate notification cases are individually low severity but operationally significant because they create conformance noise and may mask real notification failures.

7. **Evidence reliability**  
   A missing core event is not proof that the activity did not occur. External signed reports, CRM messages, fraud-system logs, bank timestamps, and call recordings can change the interpretation.

8. **Model scope**  
   The process tree was designed for payable claims. Denied and withdrawn claims may produce high alignment cost because the model expects payable steps that are not applicable.

### Confidence vs severity

- **Severity** is the business impact if the deviation is real.
- **Confidence** is how certain we are that the deviation is real and that the likely cause is correct.

A high-severity, medium-confidence case still requires immediate investigation. A low-severity, high-confidence case may only need data-quality or model cleanup. Confidence is not a substitute for severity, and severity is not a substitute for evidence.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment cost is an edit-distance measure under a chosen cost function. In this case, visible log/model moves were configured uniformly at cost 1. That makes the cost useful for finding structural mismatches, but it is not a business-risk score.

1. **Cost-function design ignores control objective and exposure**  
   CL-604 has raw cost 3, but it represents payment before required approval and missing fraud-screen evidence. A uniform cost function treats a missing fraud screen, a missing notification, and a UI event as similar in magnitude if they are visible moves. It does not weight fraud control, payment irreversibility, or EUR 90,000 exposure.

2. **Scope mismatch inflates cost without operational harm**  
   CL-607 and CL-612 have raw cost 4 because the model expects `Assess Loss`, `Auto Approve`, `Pay Claim`, and sometimes `Notify Claimant`, while the claim was denied or withdrawn. The high cost reflects model scope, not payment risk. The model was designed for payable claims, so non-payable terminal paths create expensive but low-severity deviations.

3. **Hidden moves can be fitting, not harmful**  
   CL-605 has a hidden `T(tau,c=0)` move before `Auto Approve`. The raw cost is 0, and the hidden move is technically fitting. Hidden moves are not automatically suspicious; in this case they are a Petri-net conversion artifact. Conversely, a zero-cost visible path can still be a policy failure if data conditions are ignored.

4. **Data-aware policy failures can have zero alignment cost**  
   CL-609 and CL-610 are fully synchronous with raw cost 0. CL-609 violates P1 because EUR 18,000 MEDIUM risk cannot use Auto Approve. CL-610 violates P1 and P2 because a beneficiary bank changed one hour before payment. The process tree does not encode amount, risk, or bank-change conditions, so perfect structural alignment can still be a serious control failure.

5. **Missing events are not proof that activities did not occur**  
   CL-606 has `M(Assess Loss,c=1)`, but an external signed assessment report exists. The missing core event may be a correlation or logging gap, not proof that assessment was skipped. Similarly, CL-602 has missing fraud-screen evidence, but a verbal check may have occurred. Business interpretation must consider external evidence before concluding operational failure.

6. **Duplicated telemetry creates cost without material risk**  
   CL-603 has raw cost 1 from a duplicate `Notify Claimant` event caused by a transport retry. The 74 monthly cases with the same pattern show this is a technical duplicate, not 74 separate notification failures. Uniform cost treats the duplicate as a deviation, but the business impact is low.

7. **Activity granularity and label mismatch distort cost**  
   CL-611 has raw cost 2 because `Assess Damage` and `Assess Loss` are treated as different activities. If they are the same business activity, the cost is an artifact of event-label mapping. CL-601 similarly has raw cost 3 from UI telemetry that is not a business activity at the process-model granularity.

8. **Irreversibility is not represented in edit distance**  
   A missing notification can often be corrected. A payment before approval may be irreversible or expensive to recover. CL-604’s raw cost of 3 is therefore materially misleading because it does not capture the irreversible financial and control impact of paying before approval.
