The pattern `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` violates precedence and should be flagged as impossible, whereas `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is permitted but only in the future. A declarative check separates “normatively illegal” from “legal but not yet enabled”.

- **Same-timestamp ambiguity check**: Require a reliable sequence index for any events sharing a timestamp. If the index is missing, the validator should treat the events as an unordered bag and refuse to instantiate directed DFG edges such as `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`. This prevents misordered edges from being learned as patterns.

- **Complete-trace detector**: Scan feature lineage to mark any field sourced from the full case (e.g., `num_document_requests_total`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`). Such features are excluded from prefix explanations regardless of statistical importance.

- **Enabled-activity check at `t0`**: After the first `Coverage Check Completed`, enabled activities are (a) `Damage Assessment Completed` for fast track, (b) `Documents Requested` for evidence cycle, and (c) possibly `Fraud Investigation Opened` if a trigger exists. `Appeal`, `Settlement Decision Recorded`, `Claim Closed` are not enabled. Explanations referencing non-enabled activities are future or impossible.

- **Repeated Settlement Decision validation**: Verify that a second `Settlement Decision Recorded` is preceded by `Appeal` and a first decision; otherwise it is a schema error. This distinguishes a legitimate appeal reconsideration from spurious duplicate events.

- **Footprint / DFG triangulation (with caution)**: A directly-follows graph may show frequent edges, but frequency is not normative. The suite should use DFG only as a diagnostic of observed behavior, not as a rule source; normative checks above override DFG frequencies.

Limitations: DFG edges can arise from timestamp rounding or missing indexes; alignment checks need a correct event order; declarative models must be kept in sync with documented process changes.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

**Defensible predictive signal**
- Examples: `queue_length_at_t0`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, `initial_damage_complexity`, `claimant_channel`, `initial_reserve` (if frozen).
- May claim: statistically associated with higher or lower breach risk at `t0`.
- May not claim: direct causation or that changing the feature alone will change the outcome.

**Process-state or congestion signal**
- Examples: `queue_length_at_t0`, repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (historical load).
- May claim: reflects operational state (congestion, backlog) at decision time.
- May not claim: individual claimant blame; causal effect of congestion on this specific case without further analysis.

**Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review**
- Examples: `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, possibly `initial_reserve` (socioeconomic proxy).
- May claim: predictive value derived from correlated environment factors (repair network, language).
- May not claim: region or channel is a causal “delay driver”; must undergo fairness and stability review before operational use.

**Drift/calendar proxy**
- Examples: `calendar_week`.
- May claim: certain weeks coincide with holidays/policy changes and show higher risk (drift marker).
- May not claim: the calendar week itself causes delay; interpret as contextual covariate.

**Unusable until lineage is repaired**
- Examples: `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_handler_team` (until t0 capture), `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (SQL fix), `same_day_document_count_snapshot` (cutoff fix), `risk_score_current`, `last_resource_role`, `final_decision`, all listed future sequence patterns.
- May claim: nothing in `t0` explanations until remediated.
- May not claim: any importance as early-warning signal.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Feature-store design with point-in-time correctness**: Store every feature with an effective timestamp and a `valid_as_of` field; reject features lacking event-time lineage. Include `team_assigned_at_t0`, `risk_score_at_t0`, `role_at_t0`.

2. **Fix lineage defects before reuse**: Correct the `prior_claim_count_before_t0` SQL window, change `same_day_document_count_snapshot` to a streaming t0-cutoff, and join employee experience as of event date, not extraction date.

3. **Prefix-only recomputation**: Retrain and recompute SHAP-like values using only prefix features  `t0`. Remove all complete-trace or post-outcome fields; recalibrate the model.

4. **Process-conformance tests in CI**: Automate declarative checks (precedence, appeal-enabled-only-after-Partial/Deny, repeated Settlement Decision legitimacy) and same-timestamp index validation; fail builds that emit impossible patterns like `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`.

5. **Calibration after feature removal**: After dropping leaked features, check probability calibration (e.g., Brier score) on a holdout; do not trust previous 0.82/0.71 scores.

6. **Explanation stability analysis**: Test that small admissible perturbations (queue length, elapsed time) produce consistent direction; flag unstable attributions from proxies.

7. **Proxy/fairness review**: Run subgroup analysis on `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, `initial_reserve`; assess disparate impact and robustness before any operational action.

8. **Monitoring for schema or process drift**: Track changes in `calendar_week` effects, new activity variants, and timestamp-quality regressions; alert if DFG edges deviate from normative model or if missing sequence indexes reappear.
