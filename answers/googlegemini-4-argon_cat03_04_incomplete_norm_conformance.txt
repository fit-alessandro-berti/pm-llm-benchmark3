## 1. Conformance semantics

Evaluating an object-centric event log (OCEL) against an intentionally incomplete normative specification requires separating mathematical graph differences from actual business compliance violations. A raw conformance check in tools like PM4Py compares discovered structural elements (edges in an Object-Centric Directly-Follows Graph, Object-Type Graph, or Event-Type to Object-Type bipartite graph) against a reference set. However, when the reference specification mixes open-world and closed-world semantics, raw set differences (`observed \ normative` and `normative \ observed`) do not map one-to-one to compliance breaches.

To interpret observations rigorously, we establish seven distinct conformance classes:

*   **Forbidden behavior:** The observed log contains an interaction, lifecycle relation, or control-flow transition explicitly marked as `forbidden` in the norm. Because the norm operates under a closed-world assumption for forbidden elements, any positive count (weight $\ge 1$) constitutes a prima facie violation, unless overridden by evidence of a data-extraction artifact.
*   **Missing mandatory behavior:** A relation marked `mandatory` is absent for an event or object lifecycle that falls unambiguously within the rule's stated scope. Under the partial closed-world assumption, mandatory relations must hold for 100% of in-scope instances. If an event occurs $N$ times and requires a link to an object type, observing fewer than $N$ links indicates missing mandatory behavior, subject to abstraction and data-quality reviews.
*   **Unexpected but unregulated behavior:** An observed relation does not appear in the normative tables, or is explicitly marked `unspecified`. Because the specification is open-world outside of its `mandatory` and `forbidden` rules, unregulated behavior is not noncompliant. It represents either standard domain complexity omitted from a high-level summary, newly emerging business processes, or benign cross-object associations governed by external policies.
*   **Typical-pattern absence:** An instance fails to follow a relation marked `typical`. Typical relations represent common, desirable happy paths (e.g., an invoice being paid), not invariants. Absence of a typical edge is expected for a subset of instances due to legitimate business exceptions (e.g., write-offs, open credit terms, or alternative paths) and does not constitute a compliance violation.
*   **Likely data-quality error:** An observed deviation (either a triggered `forbidden` edge or a missing `mandatory` edge) whose root cause lies in event extraction, transformation, or loading (ETL) rather than real-world business execution. Examples include cross-domain key collisions (joining unrelated tables on non-unique integers), dropped foreign keys, or un instantiated object records despite populated event attributes.
*   **Abstraction mismatch:** A discrepancy caused by the mathematical limitations of the chosen process mining model family rather than a violation of the underlying business intent. The primary example is encoding an "eventually-follows" temporal requirement ($\mathbf{F}(\text{Create}) \Rightarrow \mathbf{F}(\text{Validate})$) as a directly-follows edge ($\text{Create} \rightarrow \text{Validate}$) in an OC-DFG. If a legitimate or unmodeled event intervenes, the directly-follows edge breaks even though the business rule is satisfied.
*   **Insufficient evidence:** An observation where summary counts and aggregated graph weights are mathematically compatible with both compliant and noncompliant underlying event executions. Because summary graphs strip away trace-level context, object instance identities, timestamps, and attribute payloads, classifying these observations definitively requires drilling down into the raw OCEL tables.

**Impact of Open-World vs. Closed-World Assumptions:**
A purely closed-world assumption treats the normative model as an exhaustive specification: anything not explicitly permitted is forbidden, and every specified path is mandatory. Applying closed-world semantics to a partial enterprise specification creates massive false-positive alert fatigue, flagging legitimate actions like `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` or `Package object_codeath Delivery` as illicit. Conversely, a purely open-world assumption renders compliance checking toothless, as missing mandatory steps could be excused as unmodeled alternative paths. 

Our hybrid semantics resolve this: we apply **closed-world strictness** solely to `forbidden` assertions (their presence is strictly prohibited) and to `mandatory` expectations within their exact stated scopes (their absence is strictly prohibited). Everything else—including `typical` expectations, `unspecified` interactions, and completely unmentioned edges—is evaluated under an **open-world assumption**, treating structural differences as benign operational variance or model incompleteness unless independent evidence indicates a failure.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG (Object-Centric Directly-Follows Graph)
**What it can establish:** An OC-DFG projects the event log onto individual object types, establishing immediate temporal adjacency ($A \rightarrow_O B$) between event types for specific object instances. It can prove that for a given number of objects (when annotated with object counts), activity $B$ immediately followed activity $A$ with no intervening events attached to that same object. Consequently, it can definitively detect violations of forbidden immediate sequences—and, a fortiori, forbidden eventual sequences like **N3** (`Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`, observed for 2 Orders), because if $B$ directly follows $A$, $B$ also eventually follows $A$.

**What it cannot establish:** An OC-DFG cannot establish long-distance temporal dependencies (eventually-follows relations, $A \twoheadrightarrow_O B$), concurrency across different object types where objects do not share the intermediate events, or complete trace lifecycles. Furthermore, standard OC-DFG edge weights often conflate *event-pair frequencies* with *unique object counts* unless explicitly scoped.

**Danger of interpreting "eventually expected" rules as directly-follows rules:**
Norm **N1** states that every newly created standard Order should *eventually* enter validation, but approximates this with a directly-follows edge (`Create Order -> Validate Order`). In the observed log, 98 of 100 Orders satisfy this directly-follows relation, while 3 Orders exhibit `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review`. If an analyst treats the OC-DFG edge literally as a closed-world requirement, the 2 Orders missing `Create Order -> Validate Order` are flagged as noncompliant. However, if an Order is created, undergoes `Manual Compliance Review` (an event linked to the Order, as proven by the OTG edge `(Order, object_interaction, RiskCase)` of weight 3), and *then* undergoes `Validate Order`, its trace is $\langle \text{Create Order}, \text{Manual Compliance Review}, \text{Validate Order} \rangle$. The business rule ("eventually enter validation") is 100% satisfied, yet the directly-follows edge `Create Order -> Validate Order` is broken by the intervening review! (Note: Because $98 + 3 = 101 > 100$, at least one Order had both transitions—perhaps via multiple creation/validation loops or parallel object associations—proving that summary counts require trace-level inspection). Similarly, **N2** (`Validate Order -> Close Order`, 83 Orders) and `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72 Orders) reflect normal intermediate events interrupting a high-level summary edge. Conversely, for **N4** (`Pack Package -> Dispatch Package`, 198 of 200 externally shipped Packages), the missing 2 packages might represent an intervening WMS event (abstraction mismatch) or a genuine failure to pack before shipping (missing mandatory behavior). As for **N5** (`Issue Invoice -> Record Payment`, 92 of 110), the 4 `Write Off` edges and 14 terminal `Issue Invoice` events represent legitimate open-world business exceptions and right-censored open invoices.

### OTG (Object-Type Graph)
**What it can establish:** The OTG captures high-level structural coupling between object types across the entire log, specifically co-occurrence in events (`object_interaction`), simultaneous creation in an object's first event (`object_cobirth`), and simultaneous termination in an object's final event (`object_codeath`). It establishes whether two object classes ever interact and quantifies the total volume of such relationship instances.

**What it cannot establish:** The OTG is completely blind to *which* event types generated the interactions, the temporal ordering of events, and whether mandatory interactions occurred on every required occasion. 

**Counts, scopes, and cross-abstraction discrepancies:**
Consider **T1** `(Order, object_interaction, Item)` (weight 1450) and **T2** `(Package, object_interaction, Delivery)` (weight 200). Looking solely at the OTG, both mandatory rules appear fully satisfied with high positive weights. However, a relation can look completely conforming in the OTG while being suspicious or noncompliant in the ET–OT model. When we cross-reference T1 with ET–OT rules **E2** and **E3**, we see 1450 `Add Item -> Item` links but only 1448 `Add Item -> Order` links. Why does the OTG show 1450 `(Order, object_interaction, Item)` edges if 2 `Add Item` events lacked an `Order`? Because the OTG aggregates interactions across *all* event types (e.g., `Validate Order`, `Create Order`, or multi-item events where one Order interacts with multiple Items), masking the specific deficit in `Add Item`. Similarly, the OTG shows weight 200 for `(Package, object_interaction, Delivery)` (satisfying **T2** in aggregate), hiding the fact that 2 specific `Dispatch Package` events lacked a `Delivery` object in the ET–OT table! Conversely, the OTG successfully catches **T6** `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` (weight 1), a `forbidden` lifecycle coupling that neither the OC-DFG nor the ET–OT graph can detect on their own, because ET–OT does not track whether an event is the *first* event in an object's lifecycle. Finally, the OTG surfaces `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)` (weight 7). Under our open-world assumption for unspecified lifecycle relations, this is completely benign: when a delivery confirmation event closes out both the `Delivery` tracking record and the physical `Package` record simultaneously, an `object_codeath` edge is naturally generated.

### ET–OT (Event-Type to Object-Type Graph)
**What it can establish:** The ET–OT graph measures the exact cardinality of couplings between specific activity types and object types. By comparing the weight of `(EventType, ObjectType_A)` against `(EventType, ObjectType_B)`—or against total event frequency—it establishes whether mandatory object participations hold at the event execution level.

**What it cannot establish:** The ET–OT graph cannot establish temporal sequence (whether `Cancel Order` happened before or after `Dispatch Package`), nor can it establish direct object-to-object pairing when multiple objects of the same type participate in a single event (e.g., whether one `Add Item` event linked to 2 `Item`s and 1 `Order`, or whether two single-item `Add Item` events lacked an `Order`).

**Counts, scopes, and interpretation:**
In the ET–OT summary, `Create Order -> Order` (100, **E1**) and `Pack Package -> Package` (200, **E4**) show clean participation. However, `Add Item` shows 1450 `Item` links (**E3**) versus 1448 `Order` links (**E2**). If every `Add Item` event involves exactly one `Item` and one `Order`, then 2 `Add Item` events are missing their mandatory `Order` reference. (Alternatively, if a single `Add Item` event can attach multiple `Item`s to one `Order`, the event count might be 1448 and all events have an `Order`, which is why event-level cardinality inspection is needed—though in standard one-line-per-event logs, a gap of 2 indicates missing foreign keys). For `Dispatch Package`, we observe 200 `Package` links (**E5**) but only 198 `Delivery` links (**E6**). Context confirms that 2 `Dispatch Package` events lack a `Delivery` relation despite having a carrier reference in an event attribute. This pinpoints a classic ETL/data-quality failure: the business process obtained a carrier reference, but the log builder failed to instantiate the `Delivery` object. For `Record Payment`, we see 93 `Payment` links (**E7**) and 92 `Invoice` links (**E8**). While **T3** notes that unapplied cash is a typical exception, **E8** makes the `Invoice` link mandatory *unless* the event is explicitly typed as unapplied cash. Because the finance team confirmed unapplied cash occurs without explicit event typing in this snapshot, this discrepancy is a schema/labeling defect rather than an illicit financial transaction. Finally, `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` (weight 1, **E10**) violates an explicit `forbidden` rule, which additional context attributes to a reused numeric identifier during a database join—a textbook data-quality false positive that still requires pipeline remediation. Meanwhile, `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer` (weight 3) alongside `RiskCase` (weight 3, **E9**) is an unregulated, benign multi-object association.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

*   **Mandatory behavior (and its observed gaps):**
    *   *Conforming:* `Create Order -> Order` (100, **E1**); `Pack Package -> Package` (200, **E4**); `Dispatch Package -> Package` (200, **E5**); `Record Payment -> Payment` (93, **E7**); `(Order, object_interaction, Item)` (1450, **T1**); `(Package, object_interaction, Delivery)` (200, **T2**).
    *   *Gaps / Conditional Violations:*
        *   `Order: Create Order -> Validate Order` (98/100, **N1**): Classified as **Abstraction mismatch** (conforming to business intent) *if* the 2 remaining Orders eventually reached `Validate Order` after `Manual Compliance Review`; classified as **Missing mandatory behavior** *if* they never validated (unless cancelled prior to validation).
        *   `Package: Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` (198/200, **N4**): Classified as **Missing mandatory behavior** *if* 2 externally shipped packages bypassed packing; classified as **Abstraction mismatch** *if* an unmodeled warehouse event intervened between packing and dispatch.
        *   `Add Item -> Order` (1448 vs. 1450 `Item`s, **E2/E3**): Classified as **Missing mandatory behavior** (or **Likely data-quality error** due to dropped foreign keys) *if* 2 `Add Item` events have zero linked Orders; classified as **Conforming** *if* all `Add Item` events link to an Order and the count difference stems from batch events linking multiple Items to a single Order.
        *   `Dispatch Package -> Delivery` (198 vs. 200 `Package`s, **E6**): Formally **Missing mandatory behavior** in the graph representation, but operationally a **Likely data-quality error** since carrier attributes exist on the 2 events.
        *   `Record Payment -> Invoice` (92 vs. 93 `Payment`s, **E8**): Formally **Missing mandatory behavior** under E8's strict lexical scope (since the event lacks an unapplied-cash type label), but substantively **Typical-pattern absence** / **Likely data-quality error** (missing label) *if* verified by Finance as legitimate unapplied cash under **T3**.

*   **Forbidden behavior:**
    *   `Order: Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` (2 Orders, **N3**): **Forbidden behavior** *if* physical dispatch occurred after cancellation; **Likely data-quality error** *if* caused by asynchronous timestamp skew.
    *   `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` (1 instance, **T6**): **Forbidden behavior** *if* an operational bypass created an order and settlement in one atomic action; **Likely data-quality error** / **Abstraction mismatch** *if* an ETL aggregation merged separate checkout and payment events.
    *   `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` (1 instance, **E10**): Formally triggers a **Forbidden behavior** alert in the graph, but substantively classified as a **Likely data-quality error** caused by a foreign-key collision on a reused numeric ID.

*   **Typical behavior (and legitimate exceptions):**
    *   `Order: Validate Order -> Close Order` (83 Orders, **N2**): **Typical behavior**; the presence of `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72 Orders) represents expected, incompletely modeled intermediate fulfillment/billing steps.
    *   `Invoice: Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` (92/110 Invoices, **N5**) and `(Invoice, object_interaction, Payment)` (92, **T3**): **Typical behavior**; the 4 `Issue Invoice -> Write Off` transitions and 14 `Issue Invoice` events with no observed successor are **Typical-pattern absences** representing legitimate credit write-offs and open receivables.

*   **Unregulated / Unspecified behavior:**
    *   `Order: Reopen Order -> Close Order` (1 Order, **N6**): Explicitly **Unspecified**.
    *   `(Order, object_interaction, Customer)` (100, **T4**): Explicitly **Unspecified** (governed by external policy).
    *   `(Order, object_interaction, RiskCase)` (3, **T5**) and `Manual Compliance Review -> RiskCase` (3, **E9**): Explicitly **Unspecified**.
    *   `Order: Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review` (3 Orders), `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer` (3), `Invoice: Issue Invoice -> Write Off` (4), and `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)` (7): **Unexpected but unregulated behavior** permitted under the open-world assumption.

    meta = NORM_METADATA_REGISTRY.get(model_family, {}).get(element_key)

    # Open-world fallback for completely unmentioned edges (e.g., codeath, Customer in MCR)

            "element": element_key,
            "norm_id": "UNLISTED",

    status = meta["status"]

    temporal_note = meta.get("temporal_semantics")

    # Closed-world evaluation for FORBIDDEN relations
    if status == "forbidden":
        if observed_weight > 0:
            cls_label = (
                "Likely data-quality error (Forbidden edge triggered)"
                if dq_note
                else "Forbidden behavior"

            return {
                "model_family": model_family,
                "element": element_key,
                "norm_id": meta["id"],
                "status": status,
                "observed_weight": observed_weight,
                "classification": cls_label,
                "notes": dq_note or "Direct violation of closed-world prohibition.",
                "requires_escalation": True,
            }
        return {
            "model_family": model_family,
            "element": element_key,
            "norm_id": meta["id"],
            "status": status,
            "observed_weight": 0,
            "classification": "Conforming (Forbidden relation absent)",
            "requires_escalation": False,
        }

    # Closed-world evaluation within scope for MANDATORY relations
    if status == "mandatory":

            else:

            return {

                "element": element_key,

                "requires_escalation": True,

            "element": element_key,

            "status": status,
            "observed_weight": observed_weight,
            "classification": "Conforming (Mandatory expectation met in aggregate)",
            "requires_escalation": False,
        }

    # Open-world evaluation for TYPICAL relations
    if status == "typical":

            "element": element_key,

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

Leaving a normative specification ambiguous forces analysts to choose between alert fatigue (treating gaps as violations) and compliance blindness (excusing real failures as open-world exceptions). Below are six specific gaps in the current specification and how to resolve them:

1.  **Temporal Abstraction in N1 (Directly-Follows vs. Eventually-Follows):**
    *   *Operational Risk:* Any newly introduced intermediate step after order creation—such as fraud screening, credit checks, or `Manual Compliance Review`—breaks the `Create Order -> Validate Order` directly-follows edge, generating false-positive compliance alerts or hiding orders that genuinely get stuck in review and never validate.
    *   *Proposed Clarification:* Redefine N1 as a temporal eventually-follows constraint ($\text{Create Order} \twoheadrightarrow_{\text{Order}} \text{Validate Order}$) conditioned on the order not being cancelled first, or explicitly enumerate permitted intervening review steps (`Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review -> Validate Order`).
    *   *Target Status:* **Mandatory** (for the eventually-follows rule / composite path) and **Typical** (for the direct `Create Order -> Validate Order` shortcut).

2.  **Unapplied Cash Typing and Reconciliation (Conflict between T3 and E8):**
    *   *Operational Risk:* **T3** states that unapplied cash is a legitimate exception (`typical`), whereas **E8** mandates that every `Record Payment` event link to an `Invoice` unless explicitly typed as unapplied cash. Because the source system records unapplied cash under the standard `Record Payment` event type without a specialized subtype or flag, legitimate accounting actions trigger mandatory-edge violations, while actual orphan payments blend in with unapplied cash.
    *   *Proposed Clarification:* Either update the event taxonomy so unapplied cash is extracted as `Record Unapplied Payment` (preserving E8 as **Mandatory** for `Record Payment`), or downgrade E8 (`Record Payment -> Invoice`) to **Typical** while adding a **Mandatory** rule that every `Record Payment` must link to *either* an `Invoice` *or* a `Customer/SuspenseAccount` object.
    *   *Target Status:* **Mandatory** (for the disjunctive requirement `Invoice OR Customer`) and **Typical** (for `Record Payment -> Invoice`).

3.  **Intermediate Billing and Fulfillment Lifecycle on Orders (N2 Incompleteness):**
    *   *Operational Risk:* **N2** notes that intermediate invoice, review, or fulfillment events may occur between `Validate Order` and `Close Order`, yet `Issue Invoice` (observed on 72 Orders) is completely absent from the normative OC-DFG and ET–OT tables for `Order`. Moreover, 83 Orders transition directly from `Validate Order -> Close Order`, raising the risk that orders are being closed without ever issuing an invoice, or that orders are being re-validated multiple times without governance.
    *   *Proposed Clarification:* Specify the required lifecycle relationship between `Order` closure and `Invoice` issuance (e.g., whether a fulfilled standard Order must link to at least one `Issue Invoice` event before `Close Order`), and clarify whether multiple validations per Order are permitted.
    *   *Target Status:* **Typical** (for `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice -> Close Order` on billable orders) and **Forbidden** (for closing a shipped, non-free Order without an associated `Invoice`).

4.  **Object Lifecycle Termination (`object_codeath`) Governance:**
    *   *Operational Risk:* The observed OTG contains 7 `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)` edges, while 193 other packages and deliveries do not terminate together. Because the norm ignores `object_codeath`, it is unclear whether a `Package` lifecycle is supposed to terminate upon delivery confirmation, upon dispatch, or upon return window expiration. Inconsistent termination logic skews lifecycle duration metrics and lead-time KPIs.
    *   *Proposed Clarification:* Explicitly define whether `Package` and `Delivery` lifecycles are expected to end in the same delivery-confirmation event or whether `Package` tracking ends at dispatch.
    *   *Target Status:* **Explicitly Unspecified** (if the business does not govern object-lifecycle end events in this policy) or **Typical** (if joint termination at delivery confirmation is the standard happy path).

5.  **Multi-Object Cardinality on `Add Item` Events (E2, E3, T1):**
    *   *Operational Risk:* **E2** and **E3** state that every `Add Item` event must link to an `Order` and an `Item`, but do not specify cardinality constraints (e.g., exactly $1:1$ vs. $1:N$). When `Add Item -> Item` is 1450 and `Add Item -> Order` is 1448, analysts cannot tell from graph weights alone whether 2 events are missing an `Order` (a critical error) or whether 1448 valid events each linked to 1 `Order` while two of them bundled 2 `Item`s.
    *   *Proposed Clarification:* Define strict per-event cardinality bounds: each `Add Item` event must link to *exactly one* `Order` ($|O_{\text{Order}}| = 1$) and *at least one* `Item` ($|O_{\text{Item}}| \ge 1$).
    *   *Target Status:* **Mandatory**.

6.  **Governance of Reopened and Cancelled Order States (N3 and N6):**
    *   *Operational Risk:* **N6** leaves `Reopen Order -> Close Order` (1 Order) completely `unspecified`. If an order is cancelled, reopened via `Reopen Order`, and then has its package dispatched, a naive trace check might flag it under **N3** (`Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` is `forbidden`), or conversely, bad actors might use `Reopen Order` to bypass validation controls (**N1**) and ship unvalidated orders.
    *   *Proposed Clarification:* Define the exact state-machine semantics of `Reopen Order`: specify whether reopening a cancelled order resets the N3 prohibition on shipping, and whether a reopened order must pass through `Validate Order` again before dispatch or closure.
    *   *Target Status:* **Mandatory** (for `Reopen Order -> Validate Order` prior to fulfillment) and **Typical** / **Explicitly Unspecified** (for administrative reopen-to-close adjustments).

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

To address the findings efficiently without disrupting normal operations or overreacting to benign rare events (such as `Reopen Order -> Close Order` or `Manual Compliance Review`), remediation is structured into five workstreams, prioritizing the top-severity findings (**Rank 1: N3 Cancelled Order Dispatch** and **Rank 2/3: T6 Cobirth & E2 Orphan Items**).

### Immediate containment
1.  **Intercept Post-Cancellation Shipments (Rank 1 - N3):** Immediately query the OCEL and WMS for the 2 `Order` IDs exhibiting `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`. Check carrier tracking portals. If the packages are still in transit, issue an immediate carrier intercept/return-to-sender instruction. If already delivered, notify Accounts Receivable and Customer Support to initiate recovery or post-delivery billing reconciliation, and lock the affected order records against automated refunds until reconciled.
2.  **Verify Atomic Order-Payment Cobirth (Rank 2 - T6):** Isolate the single event generating `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)`. Verify with Treasury/Fraud control that this was not an unauthorized manual override creating a settled order without validation.
3.  **Quarantine Orphan Line Items (Rank 3 - E2/E3):** Identify the `Add Item` event(s) accounting for the 2-unit gap between `Item` (1450) and `Order` (1448) associations. Ensure these 2 items are not stuck in warehouse reservation queues or unbilled fulfillment states.

### Data repair
1.  **Purge Cross-Domain ID Collision (Rank 5 - E10):** Modify the OCEL extraction pipeline for `Sensor Heartbeat` events to scope foreign-key joins by object domain/table prefix, eliminating the join on the reused numeric `Invoice` ID. Remove the erroneous edge from the current OCEL snapshot.
2.  **Backfill Uninstantiated Delivery Objects (Rank 4 - E6/T2):** Update the ETL transformation script for `Dispatch Package` so that whenever a carrier reference attribute is present on the event, a corresponding `Delivery` object is deterministically instantiated and linked in the ET–OT relation table. Re-run extraction for the 2 affected events.
3.  **Restore Missing Order Foreign Keys & Unapplied Cash Labels (Rank 3 & Rank 7):** If the 2 `Add Item` events dropped their `Order` IDs during log extraction, repair the extraction join from the ERP line-item table. For the 93rd `Record Payment` event lacking an `Invoice` (**E8**), inspect the ERP accounting entry; if confirmed as unapplied cash, tag the event attribute/type explicitly as `Record Payment (Unapplied Cash)`.

### Process investigation
1.  **Root-Cause Analysis on N3 (Rank 1):** Compare exact system timestamps (including timezone offsets and batch-processing lags) for the 2 cancelled-then-dispatched orders. Determine whether the warehouse physically lacked real-time cancellation lockouts (a critical process failure requiring an ERP-to-WMS hard stop) or whether customer service logged a retroactive cancellation after the truck had physically departed.
2.  **Trace Verification for Validation & Packing Gaps (Rank 6 & Rank 8):** Pull full event sequences for the 2 Orders missing `Create Order -> Validate Order` (**N1**) and the 2 externally shipped Packages missing `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` (**N4**). Confirm whether the 2 N1 orders were simply routed through `Manual Compliance Review` before validation (closing the finding as benign), and determine whether the 2 N4 packages bypassed warehouse scan stations or experienced an unmodeled intermediate QA/repack event.
3.  **Cobirth Mechanism Review (Rank 2 - T6):** Trace the source system transaction for the single `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` instance to determine if a specific e-commerce checkout channel or API integration commits order headers and instant payment captures under a single shared event ID.

### Norm revision
1.  Update **N1** in the formal specification from a directly-follows expectation to an eventually-follows rule, or explicitly add `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review -> Validate Order` as a compliant variant.
2.  Resolve the **T3 / E8** conflict by formalizing how unapplied cash events are typed or by relaxing E8 to require linking to `Invoice OR Customer`.
3.  Add explicit event-level cardinality rules to **E2/E3** ($|O_{\text{Order}}| = 1$) and document `object_codeath` and `Issue Invoice` expectations so future automated checks do not flag them as unknown anomalies.

### Monitoring
1.  **Automated Hard Alerts:** Deploy continuous OCEL conformance monitors for **N3** (`Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` $> 0$), **E10** (`Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` $> 0$), and **E2** (`Add Item` where $|O_{\text{Order}}| = 0$).
2.  **Non-Escalation of Benign Variance:** Configure the monitoring layer (using the semantic registry pattern from Section 5) to suppress alerts on open-world, unregulated, or typical-exception patterns—specifically `Manual Compliance Review` links (`RiskCase`, `Customer`), `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, `Reopen Order -> Close Order`, `Issue Invoice -> Write Off`, and `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)`. Rare, unregulated business actions should be logged for periodic process discovery reviews rather than escalated as compliance incidents.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

*   **Conclusions that are robust:**
    *   The presence of open-world, unregulated edges—specifically `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72), `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer` (3) and `RiskCase` (3), `(Order, object_interaction, Customer)` (100), `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)` (7), and `Reopen Order -> Close Order` (1)—as well as typical-pattern absences on **N5** (`Issue Invoice -> Write Off` [4] and open invoices [14]), are **not** compliance violations.
    *   There are exact numerical deficits against mandatory expectations in the summary counts: 2 Orders lack the direct edge `Create Order -> Validate Order` (**N1**), 2 externally shipped Packages lack the direct edge `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` (**N4**), `Add Item` has 2 fewer `Order` links than `Item` links (**E2/E3**), `Dispatch Package` has 2 fewer `Delivery` links than `Package` links (**E5/E6**), and `Record Payment` has 1 fewer `Invoice` link than `Payment` links (**E7/E8**).
    *   Three explicitly forbidden edges are present in the extracted graphs: `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` (2, **N3**), `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` (1, **T6**), and `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` (1, **E10**).

*   **Conclusions that depend on the norm’s incompleteness:**
    *   Whether the 14 invoices without a successor in **N5** are compliant depends on unstated credit-term aging windows.
    *   Whether `Order: Validate Order -> Close Order` (83) and `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72) represent compliant billing lifecycles depends on unstated rules governing order re-validation and mandatory invoicing prior to closure (**N2**).
    *   Whether the single `Reopen Order -> Close Order` (**N6**) bypassed mandatory re-validation depends on unspecified state-reset rules.

*   **Conclusions that depend on suspected data-quality issues:**
    *   Treating `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` (**E10**) as a benign ETL artifact rather than a security/system breach depends on confirming the suspected numeric ID join collision.
    *   Treating the 2 missing `Delivery` links on `Dispatch Package` (**E6**) as an object-instantiation bug rather than unshipped/untracked freight depends on validating the carrier reference attributes on those 2 events.
    *   Treating the 1 missing `Invoice` link on `Record Payment` (**E8**) as legitimate unapplied cash (**T3**) rather than an accounting error depends on verifying that the source event simply lacked the unapplied-cash label.

*   **What cannot be decided from graph summaries alone:**
    *   **Trace-level eventual satisfaction vs. true omission:** Aggregated OC-DFG counts cannot prove whether the 2 Orders missing the direct edge `Create Order -> Validate Order` (**N1**) are a subset of the 3 Orders routed to `Manual Compliance Review` that *eventually* validated, or whether they never validated at all. Similarly, summary counts cannot prove whether the 2 Packages missing `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` (**N4**) had an intervening event or skipped packing entirely.
    *   **Event-level object cardinality:** Aggregated ET–OT weights (1450 `Item`s vs. 1448 `Order`s on `Add Item`) cannot distinguish between 2 orphan `Add Item` events with zero `Order` links and 1448 valid `Add Item` events where up to two events bundled multiple `Item`s under a single `Order`.
    *   **Physical vs. system ordering:** Without raw event timestamps and warehouse scan logs, the OC-DFG cannot prove whether the 2 `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` (**N3**) instances reflect physical post-cancellation shipments or asynchronous timestamp inversions.
