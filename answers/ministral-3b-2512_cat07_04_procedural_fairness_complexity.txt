# 1. Fairness dimensions

Procedural fairness in this case-management system requires examining multiple dimensions beyond raw outcome parity. Key dimensions include:

- **Outcome parity**: Raw approval rates differ substantially across groups (78% vs. 58%), but this alone does not imply unfairness since case-mix varies.
- **Adjusted outcome disparity**: After controlling for complexity variables, Group B and D still show reduced approval probabilities (-5.5 to -3.1 percentage points), suggesting *unexplained* disparities beyond documented complexity.
- **Procedural burden**: Groups using rights-preserving support steps (B/C) experience longer waits and more handovers, even when controlling for complexity.
- **Timeliness**: Median cycle times range from 8.1 to 15.9 days, with Group B/C/D consistently slower than Group A, and 90th percentiles exceeding 30 days for some groups.
- **Consistency of routing**: The system appears to implement legitimate routing (risk screening, language support, accommodation review) but with:
  - **Justified routing**: Higher-risk cases (Group D) go through Fraud Review (V3 path)
  - **Concerning routing**: Group B cases disproportionately routed through bilingual staff (V4) but then face backlog in regional officers
- **Quality/accessibility of support**: Language support and accommodation steps exist as rights-preserving but lack explicit service-level targets
- **Appeal/rework burden**: Higher for Groups B/C/D (13-24% reopen rates vs. 11% for A), potentially due to:
  - More complex cases (higher risk_score_band, incomplete docs)
  - Less favorable decision explanations (not captured in outcomes)
- **Resource-allocation fairness**:
  - Group B gets concentrated assignment to 7 bilingual workers + overloaded regional officers
  - Group C gets specialized accommodation specialists but also supervisor overlap
  - Group D gets risk-based routing but with regional backlogs

# 4. Plausible mechanisms

**Legitimate mechanisms**:
1. **Risk-based routing**: Group D's higher risk_score_band justifies Fraud Review (V3 path)
2. **Complexity-based support**: Language support (V4) and accommodation (V5) are rights-preserving steps
3. **Resource specialization**: Accommodation specialists and bilingual workers address legitimate needs

**Concerning mechanisms**:
1. **Unnecessary handovers**: Group B cases routed through bilingual  regional officer  bilingual
2. **Backlog amplification**: Regional officers handling both general and bilingual cases
3. **Support step burden**: Rights-preserving steps create **proportional delays** (e.g., 19.2d vs 5.8d)
4. **Historical bias**: Risk_score_band may encode past disparities (e.g., documentation completeness affected by language barriers)

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

**Measurement issues**:
- **Risk_score_band**: May encode historical disparities (e.g., applicants from disadvantaged regions)
- **Documentation completeness**: Likely affected by language barriers (Group B/C)
- **Case outcome**: Does not capture:
  - Quality of decision explanations
  - Dignity of applicant interactions
  - Appeal quality (only reopen rate is measured)

**Unobserved factors**:
- **Resource workload**: Daily granularity hides peak times
- **Applicant needs**: Not all disability accommodations are documented
- **Process flexibility**: No explicit service-level targets for support steps
- **Regional variation**: Two regions have backlogs not captured in averages
