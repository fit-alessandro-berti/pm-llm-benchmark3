## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

### Upstream data quality dependencies

- A1 and A2 are entry-level data-quality modules. A1 cleans registration; A2 prevents misrouting. If either is wrong, downstream automation receives polluted inputs.
- A6 needs complete financial documents. Without A5 or an equivalent document-completeness control, normalization may be starved or forced to work on low-quality PDFs.
- A9 needs committee decisions in structured form. If committee packaging is manual and inconsistent, contract generation will inherit those errors.
- A3 depends on historical KYC outcomes, current watchlists, and AML policy. A4 depends on registry data and the KYC hit output from A3.

### Legal/compliance controls

- KYC clearance is a legal prerequisite for customer signing. A3 and A4 are the primary first-wave control modules.
- A10 and A11 must be gated on KYC clearance. They should not be automated before KYC is reliable.
- A9 must enforce that contract terms match the committee decision. The legal rule library must be versioned and approved.
- Four-eyes compliance must be preserved. A8 can help, but if A8 is not selected, a manual role-separation control is required.
- All model suggestions, human overrides, and final decisions must be logged in an auditable trail.

### Human escalation capacity

- A3 and A4 reduce but do not remove compliance-specialist work. There are only 3 compliance specialists, so escalation SLAs and capacity planning are necessary.
- A6 requires analyst validation for nonstandard statements.
- A9 requires legal review for nonstandard contract terms.
- Automation should not simply shift work into an already constrained human pool without a release plan.

### External valuation dependency

- A7 depends on external valuers. The 58-h external wait and 19% late rate cannot be fully solved internally.
- A8/committee packaging waits for collateral valuation when required. If A7 is absent, external wait remains; if A8 is added before A7, it reduces packaging work but not the synchronization wait.

### Core-banking integration constraints

- A1, A3, A9, and A11 are the most likely to require deep core-banking integration.
- Only two modules can be deeply integrated in the first six months.
- In the recommended plan, A3 and A6 use the two deep-integration slots. A4 and A9 initially use workflow/API integration, with A9 deepened after the six-month window.

## 4. Expected process-level effects

### P1: Quick-win/volume

- **Cycle time:** Registration wait, missing-document delays, contract-prep wait, and signing wait shrink. End-to-end cycle improves only modestly because KYC, credit analysis, committee, and external valuation waits remain.
- **Waiting time:** Registration 1.5 h, missing-doc response, contract 22 h, and signing 44 h reduce. Entity-mismatch 31 h and external valuation 58 h are unchanged.
- **Rework:** Duplicate registration, missing-doc repeats, contract remediation, and signing reminders decrease. KYC and credit rework stay similar.
- **Handovers:** Fewer back-office and legal handovers. More customer/RM touchpoints.
- **Resource demand:** Back office and legal ops are freed. KYC/credit/committee utilization is unchanged. Faster contracts may create a signing bottleneck.
- **Control violations:** A9 improves contract-term compliance. A10 must be gated on KYC or it may worsen provisional signing. Four-eyes violations are not addressed.

### P2: Bottleneck/synchronization

- **Cycle time:** Reduces entity-mismatch wait, normalization wait, external valuation wait, and committee-pack wait. This disproportionately helps equipment-finance cases, where collateral valuation is required in 71% of cases, and high-exposure cases, where mismatch is on the critical path in 82% of cases.
- **Waiting time:** Mismatch 31 h, normalize 20 h, valuation 58 h, and committee pack 26 h reduce. Registration, KYC screen, contract, and signing waits remain.
- **Rework:** Mismatch 38%, normalize 16%, and committee-pack 15% rework decrease. Missing-document 52% repeat loops may still hurt A6. Contract remediation is unchanged.
- **Handovers:** Senior compliance-specialist handovers decrease. Valuer routing becomes more structured. Credit analysts receive cleaner inputs.
- **Resource demand:** The 3-FTE compliance specialist pool is relieved. Credit analysts spend less time normalizing but may see higher clean inflow. Committee cadence may become the next constraint.
- **Control violations:** A8 can enforce four-eyes. A4 improves KYC entity data. Contract and provisional-signing controls remain unchanged.

### P3: Compliance-first

- **Cycle time:** KYC, mismatch, contract, and remediation segments shorten. Overall cycle remains capped by credit analysis, external valuation, committee decision, and signing.
- **Waiting time:** KYC 4 h, mismatch 31 h, contract 22 h, and remediation loops reduce. Valuation 58 h and signing 44 h remain.
- **Rework:** KYC 11%, mismatch 38%, and contract 8.5% / 14% for renewals decrease. Financial-document and committee rework remain.
- **Handovers:** Fewer compliance-to-specialist and legal handovers. Credit and committee handovers are unchanged.
- **Resource demand:** Compliance specialists and legal ops are relieved. Credit may receive more clean KYC-cleared inflow, creating a downstream bottleneck if A6 is absent.
- **Control violations:** Strongest control profile. KYC clearance, contract terms, and remediation are addressed. Four-eyes still needs an explicit manual control.

### P4: Recommended hybrid

- **Cycle time:** Improves both parallel branches: the KYC branch via A3/A4, and the financial-document branch via A6. It also improves the contract handoff via A9. Because KYC and financial-document review are concurrent, automating only one branch leaves the other as a constraint.
- **Waiting time:** KYC 4 h, mismatch 31 h, normalize 20 h, and contract 22 h reduce. Credit-analysis wait may shorten due to better inputs, but credit-analysis judgment capacity still needs monitoring. Valuation 58 h and signing 44 h remain.
- **Rework:** KYC 11%, mismatch 38%, normalize 16%, and contract 8.5% / 14% for renewals decrease. Missing-document loops still need A5 or document-completeness checks. Committee-pack rework is unchanged.
- **Handovers:** Fewer compliance-specialist and legal handovers. Credit analysts receive standardized financial statements. Contract remediation cycles decrease.
- **Resource demand:** Compliance specialists are freed. Credit analysts spend less time normalizing, but clean inflow into credit analysis must be monitored. Legal ops month-end load is reduced. Back office remains unchanged.
- **Control violations:** Strong KYC and contract controls. Four-eyes is not automated, so role separation must remain a manual control policy.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Risk | Introduced/amplified by | Compensating control |
|---|---|---|
| Loss of auditable trail | All AI modules | Log model suggestion, human override, and final decision; version models; use immutable audit store; role-based access |
| False negatives in KYC/AML | A3 | Conservative thresholds; mandatory human review for low-confidence hits; random sample re-review; periodic backtesting |
| False entity matches | A4 | Registry confidence score; escalate low-confidence matches; senior-specialist sign-off; no auto-close |
| Misclassification of product/exposure | A2 | Default to stricter control path on uncertainty; human override; feedback loop; periodic reconciliation |
| Financial-statement extraction errors | A6 | Validation rules; outlier detection; analyst review of nonstandard/low-quality PDFs; no automatic credit-model input without validation |
| Contract-rule drift | A9 | Legal rule-library governance; versioned templates; scheduled legal review; nonstandard terms require human legal approval |
| Model drift and concept drift | A3, A4, A6, A9 | Retraining cadence; performance dashboards; drift alarms; rollback procedures; revalidation before release |
| Shifted queues/downstream bottleneck | A3, A6, A9 | Phase releases; monitor wait times and queue lengths; adjust staffing; do not accelerate into saturated resources |
| Four-eyes violations | A8/A9, month-end rush | Enforce role separation in the workflow; prohibit same-person analysis and packaging for high-exposure cases; automated conflict check; random audits |
| Customer/RM friction | A5, A10 | Soft nudges; flexible communication; RM feedback; opt-out/alternative channels |
| External valuer quality/SLA | A7 | Multi-vendor panel; SLAs; quality audits; fallback manual routing |
| Policy ambiguity surfaced | A12 | Assign policy owner; require business sign-off; document rationale; create escalation path |
| Unauthorized provisional signing | A10/A11 if wired incorrectly | Hard KYC gate before any signing/activation; flag provisional cases; suspend until remediation |

Automation does not remove compliance responsibility. Humans remain accountable for final decisions, especially in high-exposure SME cases and control-sensitive points.

## 7. Final recommendation

Recommend **P4: A3, A4, A6, A9**.

The highest-frequency activity — registration — should not be automated first. Registration has high volume, but its median wait is only 1.5 hours, the back office is underutilized after 15:00, and registration primarily feeds downstream bottlenecks. Automating it first would push more applications into KYC and credit-analysis queues without improving their throughput or quality.

Process-mining evidence points elsewhere:

- A4, entity-mismatch resolution, has only 1,750 cases but a 31-hour wait, a 38% rework rate, and is on the critical path in 82% of cases where it appears.
- External valuation creates a 58-hour synchronization wait.
- Financial-document review has a 52% repeated missing-document loop.
- Contract remediation is 8.5% overall and 14% for Credit-line renewal.

P4 addresses both concurrent workflow branches — KYC and financial-document review — and then secures the control-heavy contract handoff. It includes compliance-strengthening modules A3 and A9, and it avoids deploying downstream signing or activation bots before KYC and contract controls are reliable.

The trade-off is that P4 sacrifices immediate high-frequency labor savings and external-valuation/committee-pack automation in year one. But it builds the control and data-quality foundation needed before those later quick wins can be safe. Once the gates are passed, A1, A7, A8, A10, and A11 become strong second-year candidates.
