# Controlled Vendor Onboarding: Hybrid Compliance Model

---

# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

## Material Assumptions

1. **Concurrency model**: The three checks (Capture Master Data, Run Sanctions Screening, Verify Bank Account) may run in any order or overlapping, as long as all complete before Classify Risk.
2. **Clarification loops**: Request Clarification and Receive Clarification form re-entrant cycles; each cycle must complete before the next request or final outcome.
3. **Approval exclusivity**: Exactly one approval activity (Manager Approval or Compliance Approval) occurs in accepted cases; zero in rejected cases.
4. **Terminal outcome uniqueness**: Reject Vendor and Activate Vendor are mutually exclusive terminal decisions; a case ends with Close Onboarding after either rejection or first payment.
5. **Resource attributes**: Event logs contain a resource field; missing resources are treated as a compliance gap.
6. **Timestamps**: Sufficient granularity to compute elapsed time in hours; missing or zero-duration timestamps do not invalidate structure but do affect temporal profiling.
7. **Sanctions escalation optionality**: Sanctions Escalation is not mandatory; it occurs only when screening results trigger escalation policy (not defined here).
8. **Emergency authorization**: R6 refers to an out-of-band authorization mechanism not recorded in the event log.

---

## R5/R6 Conflict Resolution

### Conflict Statement

- **R5** (Legal): "after Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion must precede Activate Vendor under all circumstances."
- **R6** (Operations): "emergency activation may occur before Obtain Legal Opinion, but only when jointly authorized outside the event log; the opinion must then arrive within 24 hours."

These are **irreconcilable** if R5 is applied as an invariant on all cases. R6 explicitly permits a temporal inversion and backdating.

### Resolution Options

#### **Option A: R5 as strict invariant; R6 as advisory exception**
- Treat R5 as the mandatory procedural constraint (Obtain Legal Opinion must precede Activate Vendor in all cases in the event log).
- R6 is a documented business waiver; deviations are flagged as **non-conformant at procedural level** but logged as "emergency-approved" in a separate audit trail (not modeled).
- **Compliance risk**: Rejects T6 and any emergency activations without post-hoc evidence. Aligns with conservative legal policy.

#### **Option B: R6 as procedural; R5 as temporal guardrail**
- Permit Activate Vendor before Obtain Legal Opinion if and only if a "joint authorization" marker is present.
- Require Obtain Legal Opinion within 24 hours after Activate Vendor in such cases.
- Without the authorization marker, enforce R5 strictly.
- **Compliance risk**: Requires external attribute not in the event log; logs cannot prove authorization retroactively. Increases audit burden.

#### **Option C: Dual-track conformance**
- Run two separate conformance checks: one per legal requirement.
- Report both pass/fail outcomes; require human review to reconcile under emergency policy.
- **Compliance risk**: Ambiguous verdict; operationally unclear.

### **Chosen Resolution: Option A**

**Justification:**
- The event log does not contain "emergency authorization" or joint-authorization metadata (stated in R6).
- Strict procedural conformance is defensible under audit.
- R5 is encoded as a procedural precedence constraint; R6 is documented as out-of-scope for automated conformance.
- Violations are flagged and can be manually reviewed for emergency approval evidence offline.

**Compliance risk:**
- **False positives**: Legitimate emergency activations will be marked non-conformant unless retroactively authorized.
- **Mitigation**: Maintain an external emergency-approval ledger and reconcile with conformance findings in final audit.
- **T6 verdict**: Will fail procedural layer due to Activate Vendor before Obtain Legal Opinion, flagged as non-conformant to R5.

# 3. Procedural Backbone

## 3.1 Formal Description

### Petri Net Structure

**Places:**
- `p_init`: Initial place
- `p_request`: After Receive Onboarding Request
- `p_checks_started`: Three parallel checks enabled
- `p_master`: Capture Master Data complete
- `p_screening`: Run Sanctions Screening complete
- `p_bank`: Verify Bank Account complete
- `p_clarification_loop`: Clarification request–response cycle
- `p_classify`: All checks and clarifications complete
- `p_risk_classified`: Risk determined; split to three routes
- `p_reject_path`: Reject Vendor executed
- `p_low_risk_path`: Manager Approval executed
- `p_high_risk_base`: Enhanced Due Diligence path activated
- `p_sanctions_escalation_optional`: Sanctions Escalation may occur
- `p_legal_opinion_required`: Obtain Legal Opinion required (if escalation occurred)
- `p_high_risk_approval`: Compliance Approval executed (after Enhanced Due Diligence and optionally Obtain Legal Opinion)
- `p_activate_decision`: Activate Vendor or Reject Vendor ready
- `p_activated`: Vendor activated
- `p_post_activation`: Release First Payment and optionally Change Bank Details loop
- `p_final`: Close Onboarding and end

**Transitions:**
- `t_receive`: Receive Onboarding Request
- `t_master`: Capture Master Data
- `t_screening`: Run Sanctions Screening
- `t_bank`: Verify Bank Account
- `t_request_clarification`: Request Clarification (may repeat)
- `t_receive_clarification`: Receive Clarification
- `t_classify`: Classify Risk
- `t_escalation_optional`: Sanctions Escalation (optional)
- `t_legal_opinion`: Obtain Legal Opinion (conditional on escalation)
- `t_enhanced_dd`: Enhanced Due Diligence
- `t_manager_approval`: Manager Approval
- `t_compliance_approval`: Compliance Approval
- `t_reject`: Reject Vendor
- `t_activate`: Activate Vendor
- `t_change_bank`: Change Bank Details (optional)
- `t_suspend`: Suspend Vendor (conditional on change)
- `t_release_payment`: Release First Payment
- `t_close_reject`: Close Onboarding (after rejection)
- `t_close_activate`: Close Onboarding (after activation)

**Arc weights:** Standard (1 per arc unless noted).

**Mutual exclusion:**
- Transitions `t_reject` and `t_activate` share no precondition; only one fires at `p_activate_decision`.

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.process_tree import ProcessTree, Operator

# Define the procedural model as a process tree
# Top-level: sequence of phases

# Phase 1: Receive Request
receive = ProcessTree(label="Receive Onboarding Request", operator=None)

# Phase 2: Three concurrent checks (Master Data, Screening, Bank)
checks_parallel = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.PARALLEL,
    children=[
        ProcessTree(label="Capture Master Data", operator=None),
        ProcessTree(label="Run Sanctions Screening", operator=None),
        ProcessTree(label="Verify Bank Account", operator=None),
    ]
)

# Phase 3: Optional clarification loop (before Classify Risk)
# Loop: Request Clarification -> Receive Clarification, repeat until none requested
clarification_loop = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[
        ProcessTree(
            operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
            children=[
                ProcessTree(label="Request Clarification", operator=None),
                ProcessTree(label="Receive Clarification", operator=None),
            ]
        ),
        ProcessTree(operator=Operator.TAU, label=None),  # loop exit
    ]
)

# Phase 4: Classify Risk
classify = ProcessTree(label="Classify Risk", operator=None)

# Phase 5: Choice of three outcomes
# Route A: Reject Vendor -> Close Onboarding
reject_route = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
    children=[
        ProcessTree(label="Reject Vendor", operator=None),
        ProcessTree(label="Close Onboarding", operator=None),
    ]
)

# Route B: Low-risk (Manager Approval -> Activate Vendor)
manager_approval = ProcessTree(label="Manager Approval", operator=None)

# Route C: High-risk (Enhanced Due Diligence -> optional Sanctions path -> Compliance Approval)
enhanced_dd = ProcessTree(label="Enhanced Due Diligence", operator=None)

# Conditional Sanctions Escalation + Legal Opinion (optional but linked)
sanctions_escalation = ProcessTree(label="Sanctions Escalation", operator=None)
legal_opinion = ProcessTree(label="Obtain Legal Opinion", operator=None)

# Within high-risk route, Sanctions Escalation is optional
# If it occurs, Legal Opinion must follow before Activate Vendor
sanctions_optional = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[
        ProcessTree(
            operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
            children=[
                sanctions_escalation,
                legal_opinion,
            ]
        ),
        ProcessTree(operator=Operator.TAU, label=None),  # skip if no escalation
    ]
)

high_risk_route = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
    children=[
        enhanced_dd,
        sanctions_optional,
        ProcessTree(label="Compliance Approval", operator=None),
    ]
)

# Combine three routes into a choice
three_routes = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        reject_route,
        ProcessTree(
            operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
            children=[manager_approval, ProcessTree(label="Activate Vendor", operator=None)]
        ),
        ProcessTree(
            operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
            children=[high_risk_route, ProcessTree(label="Activate Vendor", operator=None)]
        ),
    ]
)

# Phase 6: Post-activation path (Release First Payment, optional Change Bank Details -> Suspend Vendor loop)
change_bank_suspend_loop = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[
        ProcessTree(
            operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
            children=[
                ProcessTree(label="Change Bank Details", operator=None),
                ProcessTree(label="Suspend Vendor", operator=None),
                ProcessTree(label="Verify Bank Account", operator=None),  # Re-verify after change
            ]
        ),
        ProcessTree(operator=Operator.TAU, label=None),  # skip if no change
    ]
)

post_activation = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
    children=[
        change_bank_suspend_loop,
        ProcessTree(label="Release First Payment", operator=None),
        ProcessTree(label="Close Onboarding", operator=None),
    ]
)

# Full model: receive -> checks -> classify -> three_routes (which includes activate and close)
# but only reject route includes close; accept routes must do post_activation
# Adjust structure: after three_routes, if not rejected, do post_activation

# Simplified: three_routes already includes Close Onboarding in reject path
# For accept paths, combine activate with post_activation

# Rebuild three_routes to separate activation and closing:
reject_and_close = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
    children=[
        ProcessTree(label="Reject Vendor", operator=None),
        ProcessTree(label="Close Onboarding", operator=None),
    ]
)

low_risk_activate = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
    children=[
        manager_approval,
        ProcessTree(label="Activate Vendor", operator=None),
    ]
)

high_risk_activate = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
    children=[
        enhanced_dd,
        sanctions_optional,
        ProcessTree(label="Compliance Approval", operator=None),
        ProcessTree(label="Activate Vendor", operator=None),
    ]
)

post_activation_refined = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
    children=[
        change_bank_suspend_loop,
        ProcessTree(label="Release First Payment", operator=None),
        ProcessTree(label="Close Onboarding", operator=None),
    ]
)

activate_then_post = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
    children=[
        ProcessTree(
            operator=Operator.XOR,
            children=[low_risk_activate, high_risk_activate]
        ),
        post_activation_refined,
    ]
)

three_outcomes = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        reject_and_close,
        activate_then_post,
    ]
)

# Top-level sequence
procedural_model = ProcessTree(
    operator=Operator.SEQUENCE,
    children=[
        receive,
        checks_parallel,
        clarification_loop,
        classify,
        three_outcomes,
    ]
)
```

## 3.3 Explanation

### Concurrency
- **Three checks** (Capture Master Data, Run Sanctions Screening, Verify Bank Account) are modeled as a PARALLEL operator, indicating all must complete before Classify Risk, but no ordering is enforced among them.
- This matches the requirement that they "may proceed concurrently."

### Choices
- **After Classify Risk**, a three-way XOR split:
  1. **Reject path**: Reject Vendor immediately, then Close Onboarding.
  2. **Low-risk path**: Manager Approval, Activate Vendor, then post-activation.
  3. **High-risk path**: Enhanced Due Diligence, optional Sanctions Escalation + Legal Opinion (as a sub-loop), Compliance Approval, Activate Vendor, then post-activation.
- The choice enforces **mutual exclusion** of rejection vs. activation (R1) and ensures only one approval occurs (R2).

### Clarification Loops
- A LOOP operator before Classify Risk allows multiple cycles of Request Clarification  Receive Clarification.
- The loop exit (TAU transition) enables skipping clarifications if none are needed.
- **Constraint**: Clarifications cannot occur after Classify Risk (by structural placement).

### Terminal Outcomes
- **Rejection path** ends with Close Onboarding.
- **Activation paths** proceed to Release First Payment and Close Onboarding (post-activation phase).
- **Mutual exclusion**: Only one of Reject Vendor or Activate Vendor occurs in any case.

### Post-Activation Behavior
- After Activate Vendor, an optional loop allows Change Bank Details  Suspend Vendor  (re-)Verify Bank Account.
- After the loop exits, Release First Payment and Close Onboarding execute.
- This satisfies R11 (Change Bank Details triggers Suspend Vendor and re-verification before later Release First Payment).

### Deliberate Approximations
1. **Sanctions Escalation model**: Represented as a LOOP with optional exit, not a standalone optional branch. In true Petri nets, an optional transition is clearer; ProcessTree's lack of a direct "optional" operator requires a loop with immediate exit.
2. **Conditional Obtain Legal Opinion**: Modeled within the sanctions loop (sequential), but R4 states only "if Escalation occurs, Opinion must occur" (no ordering constraint there). R5 adds the precedence. The loop structure satisfies both by making Legal Opinion follow Escalation whenever Escalation occurs.
3. **Post-activation loop for Change Bank Details**: A LOOP allows zero or more iterations, but R11 implies at most one occurrence (implied by "if Change Bank Details occurs... Suspend Vendor must follow"). The loop is conservative and allows future enhancements; in this scenario, only zero or one occurrence is expected.

### Soundness
- The process tree is **sound**: every path reaches Close Onboarding, and no deadlocks or livelocks are introduced.
- The LOOP operators are guarded by TAU (silent transition) exit, preventing infinite loops.

### DECLARE Model Limitations

1. **Scope qualification**: `noncoexistence` rules implicitly apply to all cases. For R2 (exactly one approval in accepted cases only), DECLARE cannot distinguish accepted from rejected cases. This requires external filtering.
2. **Ordering within responses**: `responded_existence` does not enforce *direct* succession; intermediate activities are allowed (e.g., "Request Clarification  [delay]  Receive Clarification" is valid). This is intentional for R3 (multiple cycles).
3. **Conditional precedence**: R5 applies *only* if Sanctions Escalation occurred. DECLARE `precedence` is unconditional. If Obtain Legal Opinion does not occur and Escalation did not occur, the precedence rule is vacuously satisfied (no violation). This is correct behavior.
4. **Post-activation loop**: `responded_existence(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` does not prevent Change Bank Details or Suspend Vendor in between. This is acceptable; R11 is handled by the procedural layer.

## Log Skeleton Explanation

### Rationale for Coverage

1. **Equivalence**: Empty set because no activity always occurs with another in all cases. (E.g., Manager Approval does not occur in high-risk or rejected cases.)

2. **Always Before**: Captures precedence relationships. Unlike DECLARE `precedence`, Log Skeleton `always_before` is symmetric and intended for declarative trace consistency checks. Every entry `(A, B)` means A never appears after B in any trace.

3. **Always After**: The reverse relation; redundant but included for clarity and defensive validation.

4. **Never Together**: Mirrors DECLARE `noncoexistence`. Pairs like (Reject Vendor, Activate Vendor) enforce mutual exclusion.

5. **Activity Frequency**: Specifies cardinality bounds for each activity. Key points:
   - Verify Bank Account may occur 1 or 2 times (due to re-verification after Change Bank Details).
   - Clarification activities (Request, Receive) allow 0–3 occurrences, modeling typical loop behavior.
   - Reject and Activate are exclusive: each occurs 0 or 1 times, but not both.

6. **Directly Follows**: Stricter than `always_before`; it specifies immediate succession. Used to detect skipped activities or out-of-order traces. Note: This is partial; not all legal activity pairs are listed (e.g., multiple sequential checks are allowed, but directly_follows omits intermediate pairs).

### Overlap with Other Layers

- **Overlap with Procedural**: `always_before` and `always_after` are stricter enforcements of the process tree's sequential and choice structures. If the procedural model is correct, log skeleton checks are redundant but defensive.
- **Overlap with DECLARE**: `noncoexistence` in Log Skeleton mirrors DECLARE. DECLARE is more expressive (supports weighted confidence), so DECLARE is authoritative; log skeleton is a consistency check.
- **Disagreement**: `directly_follows` is more strict than DECLARE rules. It disallows intervening activities between some pairs (e.g., (Release First Payment, Close Onboarding) must be adjacent), which is more conservative than DECLARE's looser response templates.

### Limitations

- **Concurrency ambiguity**: `always_before` and `directly_follows` are inherently sequential. Concurrent activities (Capture Master Data, Run Sanctions Screening, Verify Bank Account) have partial orderings in the process tree but no forced sequence in Log Skeleton. The skeleton treats any topological sort as valid.
- **Loop cycles**: Clarification loops are captured in `directly_follows` via ("Receive Clarification", "Request Clarification"), but Log Skeleton does not enforce the loop structure itself; only that both can repeat up to 3 times. The procedural layer is authoritative for loop semantics.

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
temporal_profile = {
    # R12 provided mappings
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (172800, 86400),    # 48h ± 24h in seconds
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): (43200, 21600),       # 12h ± 6h in seconds
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"): (259200, 129600),         # 72h ± 36h in seconds
}

zeta = 2.0  # Outlier threshold: 2 standard deviations
```

### Explanation

Each tuple key `(source_activity, target_activity)` maps to `(mean_seconds, std_dev_seconds)`:

1. **Request Clarification  Receive Clarification**: 
   - Mean: 48 hours = 172,800 seconds
   - Std Dev: 24 hours = 86,400 seconds
   - Threshold for outlier: 172,800 + 2×86,400 = 345,600 seconds (96 hours). Deviations beyond this are flagged as atypical but not failures.

2. **Sanctions Escalation  Obtain Legal Opinion**:
   - Mean: 12 hours = 43,200 seconds
   - Std Dev: 6 hours = 21,600 seconds
   - Threshold: 43,200 + 2×21,600 = 86,400 seconds (24 hours). Used to detect unreasonably delayed legal opinions.

3. **Activate Vendor  Release First Payment**:
   - Mean: 72 hours = 259,200 seconds
   - Std Dev: 36 hours = 129,600 seconds
   - Threshold: 259,200 + 2×129,600 = 518,400 seconds (144 hours). Captures legitimate variation in payment release.

### Why Warnings, Not Failures

Temporal deviations are treated as **warnings** rather than control-flow violations because:

1. **Business legitimacy**: Clarifications may be delayed due to vendor response times, which are outside operational control. Legal opinions may take longer than average due to case complexity.

2. **Variance acceptance**: Standard deviation models expected variation; a 2-sigma threshold captures ~95% of normal behavior. Deviations beyond 2-sigma are unusual but not impossible.

3. **Separation of concerns**: Control-flow (sequencing) is mandatory and modeled in Petri nets/process trees. Temporal characteristics are contextual and organization-specific; they should inform risk assessment but not block case progression.

4. **Post-hoc audit**: Temporal profiles are typically analyzed after case completion to identify bottlenecks, not during execution to block transitions. This reflects the compliance audit function, not real-time validation.

5. **External delays**: E.g., vendor response time (Request Clarification  Receive Clarification) depends on external factors; blocking the case would harm business. Flagging the delay allows operational teams to follow up.

### Temporal Profile Limitations

- **No causality** between elapsed times of different pairs. (E.g., a long clarification cycle may not affect legal opinion timing.)
- **Single-point estimate**: Assumes mean and std dev are stationary. Seasonality, regulatory changes, or staffing variations could shift the distribution.
- **Missing profiles**: Activities like Capture Master Data  Classify Risk lack explicit temporal bounds; they are checked only for control-flow sequencing.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

## 7.1 Segregation-of-Duties Validation

### R9 Requirement
- The resource performing Verify Bank Account must differ from the resource performing the applicable approval (Manager Approval or Compliance Approval).
- The resource performing Capture Master Data must differ from the resource performing Activate Vendor.

### Implementation

```python
import pandas as pd
from typing import Dict, List, Set, Tuple

def check_four_eyes_principle(log: pd.DataFrame, rules: List[Tuple[str, str]]) -> Dict:
    """
    Validate segregation-of-duties rules: no single resource performs both activities in a rule pair.
    
    Args:
        log: Event log DataFrame with columns ['case_id', 'activity', 'resource', 'timestamp']
        rules: List of (activity1, activity2) tuples to validate
        
    Returns:
        Dict with keys:
            'violations': List of (case_id, activity1, activity2, resource) tuples
            'missing_resource': Set of case_ids where 'resource' attribute is missing
            'pass': Bool (True if no violations and no missing attributes)
    """
    violations = []
    missing_resource_cases = set()
    
    # Check for missing resource attribute
    if 'resource' not in log.columns:
        return {
            'violations': [],
            'missing_resource': set(log['case_id'].unique()),
            'pass': False,
            'error': "Resource attribute is missing from event log."
        }
    
    for case_id in log['case_id'].unique():
        case_trace = log[log['case_id'] == case_id].sort_values('timestamp').reset_index(drop=True)
        
        # Check if any events in this case have NaN resource
        if case_trace['resource'].isna().any():
            missing_resource_cases.add(case_id)
            continue
        
        # For each rule pair, check if same resource performed both activities
        for activity1, activity2 in rules:
            events1 = case_trace[case_trace['activity'] == activity1]
            events2 = case_trace[case_trace['activity'] == activity2]
            
            if not events1.empty and not events2.empty:
                resource1 = events1.iloc[0]['resource']
                resource2 = events2.iloc[0]['resource']
                
                if resource1 == resource2:
                    violations.append({
                        'case_id': case_id,
                        'activity1': activity1,
                        'activity2': activity2,
                        'resource': resource1,
                        'violation_type': 'same_resource'
                    })
    
    return {
        'violations': violations,
        'missing_resource': missing_resource_cases,
        'pass': len(violations) == 0 and len(missing_resource_cases) == 0,
    }


def validate_r9(log: pd.DataFrame) -> Dict:
    """
    R9: Capture Master Data resource  Activate Vendor resource
         Verify Bank Account resource  Applicable Approval resource
    """
    # Determine applicable approval for each case
    cases_with_manager_approval = log[log['activity'] == 'Manager Approval']['case_id'].unique()
    cases_with_compliance_approval = log[log['activity'] == 'Compliance Approval']['case_id'].unique()
    
    violations = []
    missing_resource_cases = set()
    
    for case_id in log['case_id'].unique():
        case_trace = log[log['case_id'] == case_id].sort_values('timestamp').reset_index(drop=True)
        
        if case_trace['resource'].isna().any():
            missing_resource_cases.add(case_id)
            continue
        
        # Check Capture Master Data vs Activate Vendor
        master_data_events = case_trace[case_trace['activity'] == 'Capture Master Data']
        activate_events = case_trace[case_trace['activity'] == 'Activate Vendor']
        
        if not master_data_events.empty and not activate_events.empty:
            master_data_resource = master_data_events.iloc[0]['resource']
            activate_resource = activate_events.iloc[0]['resource']
            if master_data_resource == activate_resource:
                violations.append({
                    'case_id': case_id,
                    'rule': 'Capture Master Data  Activate Vendor',
                    'resource': master_data_resource,
                })
        
        # Check Verify Bank Account vs applicable approval
        bank_verify_events = case_trace[case_trace['activity'] == 'Verify Bank Account']
        
        if not bank_verify_events.empty:
            bank_resource = bank_verify_events.iloc[0]['resource']
            
            # Check against Manager Approval
            manager_events = case_trace[case_trace['activity'] == 'Manager Approval']
            if not manager_events.empty:
                manager_resource = manager_events.iloc[0]['resource']
                if bank_resource == manager_resource:
                    violations.append({
                        'case_id': case_id,
                        'rule': 'Verify Bank Account  Manager Approval',
                        'resource': bank_resource,
                    })
            
            # Check against Compliance Approval
            compliance_events = case_trace[case_trace['activity'] == 'Compliance Approval']
            if not compliance_events.empty:
                compliance_resource = compliance_events.iloc[0]['resource']
                if bank_resource == compliance_resource:
                    violations.append({
                        'case_id': case_id,
                        'rule': 'Verify Bank Account  Compliance Approval',
                        'resource': bank_resource,
                    })
    
    return {
        'violations': violations,
        'missing_resource': missing_resource_cases,
        'pass': len(violations) == 0 and len(missing_resource_cases) == 0,
    }
```

### Required Event Attributes
- `case_id`: Unique case identifier
- `activity`: Activity name (must match exact labels)
- `resource`: Person or system performing the activity
- `timestamp`: ISO 8601 or Unix timestamp (for ordering)

### Behavior When Attribute Missing
- If `resource` is missing: Mark the case as **non-compliant** due to insufficient audit trail.
- If `timestamp` is missing: Mark the case as **invalid** (cannot verify ordering).
- Return `'pass': False` and populate `'missing_resource'` set for downstream notification.

## 7.2 Emergency Authorization Check (R5/R6 Conflict)

### Scenario
Activate Vendor occurs before Obtain Legal Opinion. Under R5, this is a violation. Under R6, it is permitted if jointly authorized outside the event log.

### Implementation

```python
def check_r5_r6_conflict(log: pd.DataFrame, policy: str = 'strict_r5') -> Dict:
    """
    Evaluate the R5/R6 conflict for cases with Sanctions Escalation.
    
    Args:
        log: Event log DataFrame
        policy: 'strict_r5' (default) or 'allow_r6_with_evidence'
        
    Returns:
        Dict with verdict and explanation
    """
    violations = []
    warnings = []
    
    for case_id in log['case_id'].unique():
        case_trace = log[log['case_id'] == case_id].sort_values('timestamp').reset_index(drop=True)
        
        # Check if Sanctions Escalation occurred
        escalation_events = case_trace[case_trace['activity'] == 'Sanctions Escalation']
        if escalation_events.empty:
            continue  # No sanctions escalation; R5/R6 does not apply
        
        escalation_time = escalation_events.iloc[0]['timestamp']
        
        # Find Activate Vendor and Obtain Legal Opinion
        activate_events = case_trace[case_trace['activity'] == 'Activate Vendor']
        opinion_events = case_trace[case_trace['activity'] == 'Obtain Legal Opinion']
        
        if activate_events.empty or opinion_events.empty:
            continue  # Cannot evaluate if either is missing
        
        activate_time = activate_events.iloc[0]['timestamp']
        opinion_time = opinion_events.iloc[0]['timestamp']
        
        # Check ordering
        if activate_time < opinion_time:
            # Activate occurs before Opinion
            if policy == 'strict_r5':
                violations.append({
                    'case_id': case_id,
                    'violation': 'R5: Obtain Legal Opinion must precede Activate Vendor',
                    'activate_time': activate_time,
                    'opinion_time': opinion_time,
                    'emergency_authorization_required': True,
                })
            elif policy == 'allow_r6_with_evidence':
                # Check for external emergency-authorization marker (not present in current log)
                warnings.append({
                    'case_id': case_id,
                    'flag': 'Potential R6 emergency activation (no authorization marker in log)',
                    'activate_time': activate_time,
                    'opinion_time': opinion_time,
                    'action': 'Manual review of external authorization ledger required',
                })
        else:
            # Opinion precedes Activation (R5 compliant)
            # Check temporal window (R12 temporal profile)
            elapsed = (opinion_time - escalation_time).total_seconds() if hasattr(escalation_time, 'total_seconds') else (opinion_time - escalation_time)
            threshold_seconds = 43200 + 2 * 21600  # 24 hours (mean + 2*std_dev)
            if elapsed > threshold_seconds:
                warnings.append({
                    'case_id': case_id,
                    'flag': 'R12: Sanctions Escalation  Obtain Legal Opinion exceeds 2-sigma threshold',
                    'elapsed_hours': elapsed / 3600,
                    'threshold_hours': threshold_seconds / 3600,
                })
    
    return {
        'policy': policy,
        'violations': violations,
        'warnings': warnings,
        'pass': len(violations) == 0,
    }
```

### Behavior Under Both Resolutions

- **`policy='strict_r5'`** (chosen resolution):
  - Activate before Opinion  **violation**, flagged for manual emergency-approval review.
  - No external marker checked (it doesn't exist in the log).

- **`policy='allow_r6_with_evidence'`** (alternative, not chosen):
  - Activate before Opinion  **warning**, requires external authorization proof.
  - Expects a `authorization_id` or `emergency_marker` event attribute.
  - Absent attribute  escalates to manual review.

## 7.3 Post-Activation Bank Change and Re-verification (R11)

### Requirement
If Change Bank Details occurs after Activate Vendor, Suspend Vendor must eventually follow, and another Verify Bank Account must occur before Release First Payment.

### Implementation

```python
def check_r11_post_activation_change(log: pd.DataFrame) -> Dict:
    """
    Validate R11: If Change Bank Details occurs after Activate Vendor,
    Suspend Vendor must follow, and Verify Bank Account must recur before Release First Payment.
    """
    violations = []
    
    for case_id in log['case_id'].unique():
        case_trace = log[log['case_id'] == case_id].sort_values('timestamp').reset_index(drop=True)
        
        # Find Activate Vendor event
        activate_events = case_trace[case_trace['activity'] == 'Activate Vendor']
        if activate_events.empty:
            continue  # Case was rejected; R11 does not apply
        
        activate_idx = activate_events.index[0]
        
        # Find Change Bank Details after Activate Vendor
        change_bank_events = case_trace[(case_trace['activity'] == 'Change Bank Details') & 
                                         (case_trace.index > activate_idx)]
        
        if change_bank_events.empty:
            continue  # No bank change post-activation; R11 does not apply
        
        change_bank_idx = change_bank_events.index[0]
        
        # Check if Suspend Vendor occurs after Change Bank Details
        suspend_events = case_trace[(case_trace['activity'] == 'Suspend Vendor') & 
                                     (case_trace.index > change_bank_idx)]
        if suspend_events.empty:
            violations.append({
                'case_id': case_id,
                'violation': 'R11a: Change Bank Details must be followed by Suspend Vendor',
                'change_bank_idx': change_bank_idx,
            })
        else:
            suspend_idx = suspend_events.index[0]
            
            # Check if Verify Bank Account occurs between Suspend and Release First Payment
            release_events = case_trace[case_trace['activity'] == 'Release First Payment']
            if not release_events.empty:
                release_idx = release_events.index[0]
                
                # Verify Bank Account should occur between Suspend and Release
                reverify_events = case_trace[(case_trace['activity'] == 'Verify Bank Account') & 
                                              (case_trace.index > suspend_idx) & 
                                              (case_trace.index < release_idx)]
                if reverify_events.empty:
                    violations.append({
                        'case_id': case_id,
                        'violation': 'R11b: After Suspend Vendor, Verify Bank Account must occur before Release First Payment',
                        'suspend_idx': suspend_idx,
                        'release_idx': release_idx,
                    })
    
    return {
        'violations': violations,
        'pass': len(violations) == 0,
    }
```

### Required Event Attributes
- `case_id`, `activity`, `timestamp` (ordered)

### Behavior When Attributes Missing
- If `timestamp` is missing: Cannot determine post-activation sequencing; return non-compliant.
- If `activity` is malformed: Skip case as indeterminate.

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

## 9.1 Duplicated Enforcement Across Layers

### Mutual Exclusion (R1, R8)
- **Encoded in**: Procedural (XOR choice), DECLARE (`noncoexistence`), Log Skeleton (`never_together`)
- **Redundancy**: Procedural is authoritative; DECLARE and Log Skeleton are defensive checks.
- **Justification**: Layered redundancy detects modeling errors (e.g., if procedural model is misconfigured) and provides artifact-level validation.

### Precedence Relationships (R5, R7, R3)
- **Encoded in**: Procedural (transition ordering), DECLARE (`precedence`), Log Skeleton (`always_before`).
- **Redundancy**: Procedural model captures control-flow intent; DECLARE and Log Skeleton verify conformance.
- **Issue**: Log Skeleton `always_before` is stricter than DECLARE `precedence` for paths with loops or optional branches. E.g., in clarification loops, "Request Clarification always before Classify Risk" is true, but the procedural model allows multiple requests; Log Skeleton treats each as a separate always_before pair.

## 9.2 Constraints That Become Inconsistent Under Loops or Post-Activation Changes

### Clarification Loops (R3)
- **Procedural**: Loop allows multiple Request-Receive cycles before Classify Risk.
- **Log Skeleton** `directly_follows`: Includes ("Receive Clarification", "Request Clarification") for re-looping, but does not capture loop exit.
- **Contradiction**: If `directly_follows` enforces strict adjacency, it would prevent the loop from exiting cleanly (e.g., after one cycle, Receive could directly transition to Classify, violating direct_follows constraint if not explicitly listed).
- **Resolution**: `directly_follows` is incomplete; not all legal transitions are listed. It is treated as a sampled set, not an exhaustive specification. Conformance checkers using Log Skeleton should allow unlisted transitions for flexibility.

### Post-Activation Changes (R11)
- **Procedural**: Optional loop for Change Bank Details  Suspend Vendor  (re-)Verify Bank Account.
- **Log Skeleton** `activ_freq`: Verify Bank Account  {1, 2} (occurs 1 or 2 times).
- **Temporal Profile**: No explicit constraints on post-activation timing.
- **Blind spot**: If a case goes through the loop twice (Change Bank Details twice), Verify Bank Account might need to occur 3 times. The cardinality bound {1, 2} assumes at most one change; if design allows multiple changes, bounds should be {1, 2, 3, ...}.
- **Conservative assumption**: Design assumes Change Bank Details occurs at most once post-activation (per R11 statement "if Change Bank Details occurs..."). If this assumption is violated, Log Skeleton bounds become incorrect.

### R5/R6 Conflict Under Emergency Policy
- **Procedural (strict R5)**: Obtain Legal Opinion strictly precedes Activate Vendor.
- **Resource/Data layer (R6 awareness)**: Flags Activate before Opinion as *potential* emergency, requiring external verification.
- **Contradiction**: Procedural model marks all such cases as non-conformant; resource layer marks them as "pending external review." A single case has two verdicts.
- **Resolution by design**: Procedural verdict is tentative; resource layer verdict is final only after external authorization is verified. This requires human-in-the-loop reconciliation.

## 9.3 Vacuity in Declarative Rules

### Vacuous Precedence Rules
- **Example**: `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` applies even if neither activity occurs.
- **Vacuous satisfaction**: If a case never escalates (and thus never obtains legal opinion), the rule is vacuously true (no violation).
- **Is this correct?** Yes; the rule is: "IF Legal Opinion occurs THEN it precedes Activation." If Legal Opinion does not occur, the rule does not apply.
- **Potential issue**: If a case *should* have legal opinion (because escalation occurred) but does not, the violation is caught by a different rule: `responded_existence(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)`. So vacuity is expected and correct.

### Noncoexistence Rules Scope
- **Example**: `noncoexistence(Manager Approval, Compliance Approval)` implies these never co-occur.
- **Vacuous satisfaction**: Rejected cases (with neither approval) satisfy the rule vacuously.
- **Is this correct?** Yes; rejection is a valid outcome where no approval is needed. The rule does not require *either* approval to occur; it only forbids both.
- **Potential issue**: R2 states "exactly one of Manager Approval and Compliance Approval should occur in an accepted case." The DECLARE rule does not enforce "exactly one"; it only forbids "both." To enforce "exactly one," we need:
  - A procedural model that ensures one path leads to Manager Approval, another to Compliance Approval, and a third to Rejection.
  - An external classifier to identify accepted vs. rejected cases and verify cardinality.
- **Mitigation**: Procedural model is authoritative for "exactly one." DECLARE is a necessary but not sufficient check.

## 9.4 Missing Case Attributes

### Resource Attribute
- **Requirement**: R9 mandates segregation of duties; resource field is essential.
- **What happens if missing?** 
  - PM4Py's `filter_four_eyes_principle()` will fail or return no findings.
  - Custom resource validator returns `missing_resource` set and `pass=False`.
- **Blind spot**: Logs with incomplete resource data cannot be fully validated for segregation. Compliance audit must flag such cases as "insufficient data" rather than "pass."

### Emergency Authorization Attribute
- **Requirement**: R6 references "joint authorization outside the event log."
- **What happens if missing?**
  - No metadata to prove emergency authorization.
  - Strict R5 policy treats all Activate-before-Opinion cases as violations.
  - Alternative R6 policy requires manual cross-reference with external ledger (not automated).
- **Blind spot**: Without explicit authorization marker, automated conformance cannot distinguish legitimate emergencies from policy violations. This is by design; R6 is a business waiver, not a procedural rule.

### Case Outcome Classification
- **Requirement**: R2 states "exactly one approval in accepted cases; none in rejected cases."
- **What happens if missing?**
  - No automated way to distinguish accepted from rejected cases for DECLARE scope-limited rules.
  - Must rely on procedural model's XOR choice to ensure outcomes are mutually exclusive.
- **Blind spot**: DECLARE rules that are scope-limited (e.g., "in accepted cases, exactly_one approval") cannot be expressed without an external case-outcome attribute. Conformance tools must support scoped templates or require post-hoc filtering.

## 9.5 Timestamp-Only Limitations

### Temporal Precision
- **Issue**: Elapsed time computed from timestamps alone. Timestamps may be rounded, batched, or have seconds precision but business logic requires hour precision.
- **Example**: If two activities are recorded at the same second (or within seconds), elapsed time  0, which might violate a minimum-duration assumption (not expressed in current model).
- **Mitigation**: Temporal profile uses hour-scale means and std devs; sub-hour variations are absorbed into the std dev term.

### Causality Cannot Be Inferred
- **Example**: T7 has a legal opinion that arrives 34 hours after escalation (delayed). Temporal profile flags this as 2-sigma outlier. But *why* was it delayed?
  - Resource unavailability? Regulatory hold-up? Data complexity?
  - Timestamps alone cannot explain delays; case attributes (escalation_reason, complexity_score, assigned_reviewer) would help.
- **Blind spot**: Temporal anomalies detected but not diagnosed.

### Missing Intermediate Events
- **Example**: R3 clarification cycles. If clarification request and response are recorded asynchronously (request logged immediately, response batched hours later), the recorded timestamp gap may not reflect actual business elapsed time.
- **Blind spot**: Conformance checks based on recorded timestamps may misattribute delays to business process rather than logging/ETL latency.

## 9.6 Risk of Treating Support/Confidence Metadata as Normative Truth

### Current Design
All DECLARE rules have `support: 1.0, confidence: 1.0`, implying:
- **Support**: 100% of cases exhibit the rule.
- **Confidence**: 100% certainty in the rule's applicability.

### Misinterpretation Risk
- **If treated as discovery metadata**: "support: 1.0" means the rule was observed in all historical logs. This is descriptive, not prescriptive.
- **If treated as policy constraint**: "support: 1.0" and "confidence: 1.0" are imposed as hard requirements. This is correct and intended.
- **Danger**: Confusing derived metrics (from log mining) with designed policies (from requirements). A rule discovered in historical logs with 90% support is weaker than a rule defined by policy (100% required).

### Current Mitigation
- In this model, all rules are defined by requirements (R1–R12), not derived from log analysis.
- `support` and `confidence` are set to 1.0 to reflect mandatory policies, not statistical frequency.
- Documentation should clarify: "These are design-time rules, not learned patterns."

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

## 10.1 Canonical Procedural Representation

**Primary model**: ProcessTree (or equivalent Petri net).

**Justification**:
- Encodes control-flow structure: sequence, parallelism, choice, loops.
- Directly executable and simulatable (PM4Py supports simulation).
- Supports compliance checking via `conformance_lp()` or `conformance_alignments()`.
- Explicit representation of mutual exclusion and post-activation logic.

**What it covers**:
- R1, R2 (mutual exclusion of terminal outcomes and approvals)
- R3 (clarification loops before classification)
- R4 (conditional legal opinion response)
- R5 (precedence of legal opinion before activation, under strict R5 resolution)
- R7 (precedence of screening before escalation)
- R10 (cardinality: each terminal activity at most once)
- R11 (post-activation bank change and suspension loop)

**What it does not cover**:
- R9 (resource segregation): requires event attribute inspection
- R6 (emergency authorization): out-of-band policy, not modeled
- R12 (temporal profiles): timing variations, modeled separately

---

## 10.2 Authoritative Policy Layers

| Policy | Authoritative Layer | Reason | Fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Control flow** (R1, R2, R3, R5, R7, R10, R11) | Procedural (ProcessTree/Petri net) | Defines state transitions and guarding conditions. | DECLARE `precedence`, Log Skeleton `always_before` (defensive redundancy) |
| **Temporal expectations** (R12) | Temporal profile | Captures mean/std dev of activity-pair durations. Deviations are warnings, not hard blocks. | Manual review for process bottlenecks. |
| **Resource segregation** (R9) | Python resource/data-aware validation layer | Extracts and compares resource attributes; activity-only models cannot encode identity. | Manual four-eyes review or external authorization ledger. |
| **Emergency override** (R6) | External authorization ledger (out-of-scope for automated checks) | Provides evidence for waiver of R5 in urgent cases. | Strict R5 procedural enforcement (all Activate-before-Opinion cases fail, pending manual reconciliation). |
| **Conditional constraints** (R4: if escalation, then legal opinion) | DECLARE `responded_existence` + procedural optional branch | Both layers encode the dependency; DECLARE for audit, procedural for execution. | — |
| **Mutual constraints** (R1, R2, R8) | Procedural choice + DECLARE `noncoexistence` + Log Skeleton `never_together` | Redundant encoding across three layers for robustness. | Any single layer catches violations; others confirm. |

---

## 10.3 Conformance Check Execution Order

### Recommended Sequence

1. **Procedural conformance** (first gate)
   - Run PM4Py's `conformance_lp()` or `conformance_alignments()` against the ProcessTree.
   - Fast, deterministic, catches structural violations (R1, R2, R3, R5, R7, R10, R11).
   - **Output**: PASS or FAIL (with alignment details if FAIL).
   - **Decision**: If FAIL, escalate to operational team (case blocked pending process correction).

2. **DECLARE conformance** (validation layer)
   - Run `pm4py.conformance_declare(log, declare_model)`.
   - Redundancy check; should align with procedural verdict.
   - **Output**: PASS or FAIL (per-rule violations).
   - **Decision**: If FAIL and procedural is PASS, investigate modeling inconsistency. If both FAIL, take procedural verdict as authoritative.

3. **Log Skeleton conformance** (consistency layer)
   - Run `pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton(log, log_skeleton)`.
   - Checks activity-only constraints: ordering, co-occurrence, cardinality.
   - **Output**: PASS or FAIL.
   - **Decision**: Defensive check; should confirm procedural verdict. If disagreement, review Log Skeleton bounds (may be too conservative).

4. **Temporal profile analysis** (monitoring layer)
   - Run `pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(log, temporal_profile, zeta=2.0)`.
   - Flags outlier activity-pair durations.
   - **Output**: PASS (all within threshold) or WARNING (deviations flagged).
   - **Decision**: Warnings do not block; they inform audit and operational review. Escalate to process owner for root-cause analysis (bottleneck, complexity, unavailability).

5. **Resource and data-aware validation** (authorization layer)
   - Run custom Python checks: `validate_r9()`, `check_r5_r6_conflict()`, `check_r11_post_activation_change()`.
   - Inspects event attributes: resource, emergency authorization markers, etc.
   - **Output**: PASS, FAIL, or PENDING REVIEW (if external data needed).
   - **Decision**:
     - If FAIL (segregation breach, missing attribute): Case rejected; alert compliance officer.
     - If PENDING REVIEW (emergency authorization claimed): Cross-reference external ledger; issue final verdict after human review.
     - If PASS: Proceed to next layer.

6. **Aggregate verdict**
   - **PASS**: All five layers pass (or warnings only).
   - **FAIL**: Any layer fails; escalate with clear explanation of earliest violation and layer(s) affected.
   - **PENDING**: If R6 emergency review needed, mark case as "approved pending emergency-authorization evidence" and re-evaluate after evidence is added to audit trail.

### Rationale for Sequence

- **Procedural first**: Control-flow violations are structural and most likely to occur; fail fast.
- **DECLARE second**: Redundancy check validates procedural model; catches modeling errors.
- **Log Skeleton third**: Consistency verification; should align with procedural (lower priority).
- **Temporal last**: Monitoring and advisory; does not block; informs operational KPIs.
- **Resource last**: Requires data attribute inspection; most resource-intensive; executed conditionally (only if earlier layers pass).

---

## 10.4 Handling Contradictory Findings

### Scenario 1: Procedural PASS, DECLARE FAIL

**Example**: Procedural model allows a sequence that DECLARE rule forbids.

**Action**:
1. Investigate the rule: Is DECLARE rule correct? Is procedural model incomplete?
2. If DECLARE is obsolete, remove or update it.
3. If procedural is incomplete, revise the ProcessTree and revalidate.
4. Document the discrepancy in a compliance drift report.
5. **Decision**: Take procedural verdict as authoritative (it directly encodes the business process); DECLARE is advisory.

### Scenario 2: Procedural FAIL, DECLARE PASS

**Example**: ProcessTree rejects a trace due to sequencing, but DECLARE rules are satisfied.

**Action**:
1. Examine the procedural model: Is it over-constrained (too many hard constraints)?
2. Check DECLARE rules: Are they missing? (E.g., do they lack `precedence` rules that the ProcessTree enforces?)
3. If procedural is correct and DECLARE is incomplete, add missing rules to DECLARE.
4. If procedural is over-constrained, relax it (e.g., allow more interleaving of concurrent activities).
5. **Decision**: Take procedural verdict as authoritative; DECLARE must be updated to match.

### Scenario 3: Resource/Data FAIL, Procedural PASS

**Example**: Case satisfies control-flow but violates segregation of duties (R9).

**Action**:
1. Confirm the resource violation (same person performed Verify Bank Account and Manager Approval).
2. Block the case: Segregation of duties is a hard compliance requirement.
3. Notify compliance officer and business stakeholder.
4. **Decision**: Resource/data layer verdict is authoritative for non-procedural constraints. Override procedural PASS.

### Scenario 4: Temporal WARNING, All Procedural PASS

**Example**: Case satisfies control flow but legal opinion arrives 34 hours after escalation (T7).

**Action**:
1. Log the temporal outlier (>2 sigma).
2. Do not block the case; temporal variations are expected.
3. Flag for process owner review: investigate if there is a bottleneck in legal review.
4. **Decision**: Warning is informational; case proceeds. Operational team decides if process improvement is needed (e.g., SLA adjustment, resource allocation).

### Scenario 5: R5/R6 Conflict (Activate before Legal Opinion)

**Action** (under chosen strict R5 resolution):
1. Procedural, DECLARE, Log Skeleton all fail: Activate precedes Legal Opinion.
2. Resource/data layer checks for emergency-authorization marker: **Not present** in log.
3. Verdict: **Non-conformant to R5**; case flagged as violation.
4. Escalation:
   - Notify compliance and legal teams.
   - Require external evidence of joint authorization (R6 waiver).
   - If evidence is found, amend audit trail with authorization note and re-mark case as "approved under emergency policy."
   - If evidence is not found, case remains non-conformant; determine if retroactive correction is needed.

- **Yellow (WARNING)**: Temporal anomalies detected; operational review recommended; case proceeds.

- **Orange (PENDING REVIEW)**: External authorization or manual verification needed; case on hold.

### Exception Handling

- **If layers disagree** (e.g., procedural PASS but resource FAIL):
  - Highlight the disagreement clearly.
  - Explain which layer is authoritative (per section 10.2 table).
  - Provide links to root-cause analysis (modeling error? missing constraint? data quality issue?).
