# 1. Current-process diagnosis

**The core contradiction: the happy path is locally fast and cheap, systemically expensive and non-compliant.**

The frequency DFG shows the dominant route is Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen (3,600 of 5,000) -> Fast approval (2,550) -> Dispatch replacement (2,300) -> Quality/compliance close (2,320). On the performance DFG this looks excellent: Auto -> Fast approval median 0.4h, Fast approval -> Dispatch median 1.8h, Dispatch -> Close median 3.2h. The segment data confirms it: Premium low-risk accessories complete in 8h median on the fast path vs 46h manual.

But that path degrades every downstream outcome:

- **Quality/rework:** Customer complaint/reopen occurs in 14.1% of Fast approval cases vs only 3.3% of cases going Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device. Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen fires 260 times, and Fast approval -> Dispatch is followed by a long-tail complaint edge (Dispatch -> Complaint mean 96h, p90 180h) linked in the temporal profile to incorrect replacement part selection. Fast approval is 31% cheaper *before* rework but only 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and service credits — and that residual saving ignores the EUR 410 expected cost of a wrong replacement on high-value devices.
- **Compliance:** Token-based replay gives log fitness 0.91 but only 68% of traces fit — high fitness masks that nearly a third of cases deviate. Frequent move-on-log deviations are exactly on the fast path: Fast approval before evidence completeness (violates R2), Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices (violates R1: >EUR 400 needs Manual or Fraud/risk review before Fast approval), and Customer complaint/reopen *after* close. Low precision of the discovered model confirms many observed auto-approval/risk-review/repair combinations are not allowed by policy. Premium high-value fast-path cases carry 22% complaint/reopen (vs 5% reviewed/repair) plus high R1/R2 risk.
- **Performance is bimodal, not slow on average:** The manual/evidence side is where time actually lives. Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review has median 26h, p90 88h; Manual -> Repair quote p90 60h; Manual -> Request missing evidence p90 36h; complaint loops re-enter at Complaint -> Manual mean 40h and Complaint -> Escalation board mean 54h, p90 130h. Unclear defect / missing evidence cases (22% of volume) have 140h median cycle time, the highest abandonment (Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned: 320, plus 70 after Customer approval), and Request missing evidence recurs at least twice in 26% of cases entering it.
- **Customer experience is segment-asymmetric:** Slowing Premium low-risk accessories is actively harmful — service-credit exposure is high beyond 24h, and fast-path complaints are only 6% vs 3% manual, a small quality price for a 38h saving. The reverse holds for high-value devices (Premium 22% vs 5%, Standard 18% vs 6% complaint rates). Standard low-risk customers tolerate 72h if status visibility is good, so speed is being over-bought for them and under-protected for Premium.
- **Resources:** Manual eligibility review (7 reviewers) queues sharply above 82% utilization; Fraud/risk review has only 2 specialists and Monday/campaign spikes; the Escalation board (120 cases) is one daily 45-minute slot — effective but unscalable, and it is currently absorbing failures the fast path created. Quality check (2 inspectors) is underused except after repair campaigns, despite Rework repair hitting 12.5% of high-value repairs.

In short: the process optimizes the Auto -> Fast -> Dispatch edges by exporting cost, time, and compliance risk to complaint/reopen, escalation, rework, and post-close deviations that the edge-level performance view understates.

# 2. Redesign A: Segment- and Risk-Gated Triage — "Fast Lane with a Licence"

- **Redesigned process flow:** Replace the outdated, non-segment-aware auto-screen rules with explicit routing at Intake: (1) Premium/Standard low-risk accessories with complete evidence -> Fast approval -> Dispatch, retaining the R2 exemption only for Premium low-risk; (2) any claim >EUR 400 or high-value device -> mandatory Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review before any approval (hard R1 gate in the rules engine, not a guideline); (3) unclear defect -> structured evidence checklist issued at Intake *before* eligibility decision; (4) reopened cases forced through Manual eligibility review before close/denial (hard R3 gate). Fast approval ceases to be reachable from Auto screen for high-value segments.
- **Targets:** The Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval edge (2,550 cases) for high-value and >EUR 400 claims; the R1/R2/R3 deviations; Premium and Standard high-value segments (35% of volume combined).
- **Mechanism of improvement:** It attacks the 14.1%-complaint fast path where it is actually failing, by moving the control point upstream to the cheapest decision point (Auto screen cost EUR 0.20/case). High-value cases get the reviewed path whose complaint rate is 5-6% instead of 18-22%, and compliance is enforced by construction rather than detected in conformance checking. The fast lane is *preserved and protected* for low-risk accessories, where segment data shows it is genuinely the right trade.
- **Deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved:** High-value cycle time will rise toward the reviewed-path medians (96-120h) unless manual capacity is managed. The prompt warns rule updates may increase manual-review inflow by 15-35%, against a team that queues sharply above 82% utilization — this redesign accepts that risk and does not itself fix the evidence loop (26% repeat requests) or wrong-part dispatch selection. Premium high-value customers trade speed for correctness; service-credit exposure from slower handling is a real, accepted cost.

# 3. Redesign B: First-Time-Right Evidence Front-End — "Fix the Loop, Not the Gate"

- **Redesigned process flow:** Rebuild the front of the process around evidence completeness. At Intake, customers get a defect-type-specific evidence checklist with guided upload (the new evidence portal), photo/part-number validation, and real-time completeness confirmation *before* entering Auto or Manual eligibility. Request missing evidence becomes a single, structured, tracked request with proactive status notifications (exploiting Standard customers' tolerance for 72h completion with good visibility), an assisted/non-digital fallback channel for low-digital-literacy customers, and a rule that a second evidence request triggers Manual eligibility review with a named owner rather than another portal loop. Eligibility reviewers receive only evidence-complete cases.
- **Targets:** The Request missing evidence sub-graph (1,430 entries from Auto + Manual; 890 looping back to Manual, 320 abandoned), the 22% unclear-defect/missing-evidence segment at 140h median, and the Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review edge (p90 88h).
- **Mechanism of improvement:** This is an information-flow redesign, not a control redesign: it removes rework loops instead of gating decisions. The portal is expected to cut repeated evidence requests by 30-45%, which compounds because each loop currently costs a median 26h plus reviewer re-handling. Evidence-complete arrival also makes both the auto screen and manual review faster and makes R2 compliance ("no dispatch before evidence completeness") achievable without slowing the fast lane. Status visibility converts waiting time into tolerated time for Standard segments.
- **Deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved:** Intake becomes heavier and slower for everyone up front, and abandonment may rise for low-digital-literacy customers despite the fallback. It does nothing directly about wrong replacement part selection after Fast approval, the high-value fast-path compliance hole (a well-evidenced high-value claim can still be wrongly fast-approved), or Fraud/risk review capacity. Upfront effort is spent on cases that will be denied anyway.

# 4. Redesign C: Split Fulfilment Factories with a Pre-Dispatch Gate — "Slow the Shipment, Not the Decision"

- **Redesigned process flow:** Decouple the two fulfilment modes into separately managed lanes after the technical decision. *Replacement factory:* Fast approval remains available, but high-value dispatches (and any dispatch where part selection was auto-derived) pass a lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate — staffed by redeploying the underused Quality check inspectors — verifying part/replacement match before Dispatch replacement; low-risk accessories bypass the gate. *Repair factory:* Repair quote is parallelised with part-price confirmation (the stated cause of its delay), Repair device -> Quality check is protected, and high-value repairs (12.5% rework rate) get mandatory rather than sampled quality check. The Escalation board is reserved, by triage criteria, for Premium and high-value reopened cases instead of first-come scheduling.
- **Targets:** The Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen edge (260 cases, p90 180h, linked to wrong part selection), high-value replacement error cost (EUR 410 vs EUR 95 low-risk), Rework repair on high-value devices, and the Manual -> Repair quote delay (mean 22h, p90 60h; Premium temporal-profile deviation).
- **Mechanism of improvement:** It places the control at the point of the expensive, hard-to-reverse error — the physical shipment — rather than at eligibility. A 6-18h gate is cheap insurance against a EUR 410 wrong replacement plus a complaint loop that re-enters at 40-54h mean delay. It rebalances existing capacity (idle inspectors) instead of loading the already-critical manual reviewers, and it improves the repair path's speed (parallel part-price confirmation) rather than only policing the replacement path.
- **Deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved:** Raw dispatch speed drops 6-18h where the gate applies, which would breach Premium expectations if misapplied to low-risk accessories — hence the bypass. It leaves the eligibility compliance violations (R1, fast approval before evidence) formally in place; a compliant-but-gated wrong *decision* can still ship the wrong *entitlement*. It does not reduce evidence loops or unclear-defect cycle time.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A — Gated triage**
- Failure: manual-review inflow rises 15-35%, utilization crosses 82%, and the queue explodes — high-value p90 cycle times exceed even today's reviewed path, triggering Premium service credits beyond 24h expectations.
- Failure: borderline gaming and misclassification — claims valued just under EUR 400 or defect types coded as low-risk to stay in the fast lane, recreating R1 deviations in a less visible form; outdated-rule problems reappear as rule drift.
- Bottleneck migration: from Auto screen to Manual eligibility review and Fraud/risk review (2 specialists, Monday/campaign spikes), and onward to the daily Escalation board as dissatisfied slowed-down Premium customers reopen.

**Redesign B — Evidence front-end**
- Failure: portal abandonment rises among low-digital-literacy customers (transferring the 320 abandonments earlier and making them invisible as "never claimed"), while determined loopers still generate second requests because defect taxonomy, not upload mechanics, was the real problem.
- Failure: reviewers distrust portal completeness flags and re-request evidence anyway, so the 890-case loop persists with an extra step added — cycle time worsens with no quality gain.
- Bottleneck migration: from Request missing evidence loops to Manual eligibility review (now receiving more, better-prepared cases faster than 7 reviewers can clear) and to Repair quote, whose part-price waiting becomes the binding delay once evidence is fixed.

**Redesign C — Split fulfilment**
- Failure: the pre-dispatch gate, staffed by only 2 inspectors, becomes a queue during repair/replacement campaigns — the 6-18h estimate stretches, Premium low-risk cases are accidentally gated, and the gate degenerates into a rubber stamp under time pressure, catching nothing.
- Failure: part-price confirmation cannot actually be parallelised (supplier dependency), so the repair factory gains little speed while absorbing mandatory quality checks for high-value repairs (12.5% rework rate means more Rework repair -> Repair device cycles, 42 observed).
- Bottleneck migration: from Dispatch/complaint handling to Quality check / the new gate and to the 3 repair technicians; Escalation board demand falls, but Repair quote -> Customer approval waiting (median 21h, p90 80h, customer-side) becomes the visible constraint.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**Sequencing logic:** Order by reversibility and by where evidence is weakest, and never pilot two changes on the same segment simultaneously — otherwise edge-level improvements cannot be attributed and downstream shifting cannot be detected. Start with the cheap, rule-based change on the segment where the current process is most clearly dominated; use its reviewer-capacity learnings to size later steps.

**Pilot 1 (weeks 1-6) — Redesign A on Standard high-value claims only.** Standard (not Premium) high-value is chosen first: complaint rate 18% fast vs 6% reviewed, high wrong-replacement cost, but no 24h service-credit cliff, so a slowdown is survivable while learning. Enforce R1/R3 hard gates for this segment; hold Premium high-value and all low-risk as control.
- Metrics: end-to-end median *and p90* cycle time per case (not edge averages); complaint/reopen rate measured with a lag window covering the Dispatch -> Complaint p90 of 180h; R1/R2/R3 deviation count from conformance checking (fitting-trace % for the pilot segment); Manual review utilization and queue wait; abandonment; cost per closed case *including* reshipments and credits.
- Stop/go thresholds (ranges): **Stop/pause** if manual utilization sustains above ~82-85% for two weeks, pilot p90 cycle time exceeds the current reviewed-path level without complaint improvement, or abandonment rises materially. **Go to extend** if complaint/reopen moves directionally toward the 5-6% reviewed benchmark, fitting traces rise toward >85-90% for the segment, and utilization stays manageable.

**Pilot 2 (weeks 6-14) — Redesign B for unclear-defect cases**, with the assisted channel included from day one and a randomised or stepped-wedge comparison of portal vs current request letters. Metrics: repeat-evidence-request rate (baseline 26% with >=2 requests; target direction consistent with the 30-45% reduction range), abandonment split by channel/digital proxy, time from Intake to evidence-complete, and downstream reviewer handling time. Stop if abandonment for the assisted-needing subgroup rises more than a small agreed margin, even if the average improves — averages would hide the excluded group.

**Pilot 3 (weeks 12-20) — Redesign C gate for high-value dispatches**, staffed explicitly from Quality check capacity with a campaign-period staffing rule. Metrics: wrong-part complaint rate on gated vs bypassed dispatches, gate catch rate, gate waiting time distribution, inspector utilization, and repair rework rate for high-value devices against the 12.5% baseline.

**Learning whether improvements are real rather than shifted downstream:**
- Measure *whole cases to final END* (Quality/compliance close -> END is only 3,015 of 5,000 starts; track where the rest exit) and follow cohorts past close, because Customer complaint/reopen after close is a known deviation — a redesign that "closes" faster but reopens later must count as worse.
- Keep untargeted segments as concurrent controls and compare against baselines given in the prompt (14.1% vs 3.3% complaint rates, 26% repeat evidence, segment medians), not against pre/post averages alone.
- Re-run conformance (fitting-trace %, R1-R3 deviations) and the frequency/performance DFGs per pilot: success means specific edges shrink (Request missing evidence loops, Dispatch -> Complaint) without a compensating bulge in Escalation board, Claim abandoned, or a neighbouring review queue.
- Judge cost only on fully-loaded case cost (the 31% -> 9% lesson): any pilot claiming savings before the complaint-lag window closes is reporting the same illusion that made the current fast path look attractive.
