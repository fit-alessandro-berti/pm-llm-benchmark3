# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

Assumptions used in the reconstruction:

1. **Non-preemptive rules choose only when a resource becomes free.** A job that arrives during service cannot affect the decision until completion unless the policy permits preemption.
2. **Arrival uncertainty is ±2 minutes in the operational record.** Jobs with recorded arrivals near a decision boundary are treated cautiously. It does not materially change most episodes below.
3. **Appointment eligibility is uncertain.** Without documented tolerances, the appointment column predicts strongly only near the stated time; it does not establish whether a job is withheld before that time or remains preferred afterward.
4. **SPT uses `est_min` at arrival.** For resumed QB work, a true SPT policy would need the current estimated remaining time, which is unavailable.
5. **A listed skill combination is conjunctive.** For example, `firmware+electrical` requires both skills.
6. **“Skill-based” predictions identify the eligible subset/resource match, not necessarily a unique next job when several eligible jobs remain.**

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

## QA

**Supporting cases**

- **A03** is the clearest appointment case: it starts exactly at 08:20, ahead of older A02.
- **A06** starts five minutes after its 09:15 appointment, consistent with “next available after appointment” rather than exact-time service.
- **A02, A05, and A07** support FIFO as the secondary rule. At 08:30, 09:05, and 09:32 respectively, they are older than competing jobs and are selected despite higher-priority or shorter alternatives.
- **A04’s 08:58 start** is compatible with FIFO because it is then the oldest waiting job. Its priority and short estimate make this episode non-identifying.

**Challenges**

- **A03** also fits SPT, since its estimate is 10 versus A02’s 20. The exact appointment alignment makes appointment scheduling more plausible, but does not logically exclude SPT.
- **A06** is not selected at 09:05 despite being close to its appointment. This requires a finite appointment window, a no-early-service rule, or FIFO precedence until the slot becomes due.
- **A08** waits behind A05, A06, and A07 despite priority 1. This strongly rejects strict priority but does not challenge appointment-plus-FIFO.
- The **08:30–08:32 idle gap** is unexplained. Delayed scans are not sufficient because A02 was already known from 08:04. It requires a setup/decision lag or another undocumented operational exception.

## QB

**Supporting cases**

- **B02/B01** provide direct support for preemptive priority: B01 is stopped at 09:16 for an “urgent arrival,” and B02 is priority 1.
- **B05** starts at 10:00 before interrupted B01 and waiting B04, consistent with priority dominance.
- **B03** taking precedence over priority-3 B01 at 09:48 is compatible with priority, although B05 complicates that interpretation.
- The interrupted **B01** is resumed twice, indicating explicit resume logic and nonzero switching considerations rather than abandonment.

**Challenges**

- At **09:48**, B05 is apparently already waiting and has higher priority than B03. The explicit dispatcher reassignment supplies an undocumented override, but pure preemptive priority cannot explain the choice.
- At **10:14**, B01 resumes ahead of B06, which has higher priority. This requires a resume preference, a commitment rule, or a substantial preemption/resumption cost.
- At **10:28**, B04 is chosen ahead of B06 and near-appointment B07. This is a clear violation of strict priority and likely of SPT/appointment precedence.
- **B07’s 10:30 appointment** is not honored; it starts at 10:56. Appointment scheduling is therefore not a reliable primary mechanism in QB.

## QC

**Supporting cases**

- **C02** is the shortest member of the initial backlog and starts first.
- **C03** is selected next and is shorter than C01, C04, and C05.
- **C06** is selected at 11:34 as the shortest waiting job.
- The resulting early sequence **C02–C03–C04** broadly tracks increasing estimates among the initial jobs other than C01.

**Challenges**

- **C04 at 10:32** is selected despite C06 having an estimate of 6. This is a direct SPT violation not explained by arrival-scan delay, because C06 arrived seven minutes earlier. It requires temporary ineligibility, a prior dispatch commitment, estimate revision, batching, or an undocumented exception.
- **C05 at 10:54** is the longest waiting job, selected over C06. Its priority-1 status and already-missed 10:50 deadline provide a strong known rationale for an SLA escalation.
- **C01 at 11:40** is chosen over shorter, higher-priority, earlier-deadline C07. This requires aging, FIFO recovery, a maximum-wait rule, or another commitment not present in the table.
- **C05** was already urgent at 10:18 and 10:32 but was not selected. Therefore escalation is not simply “always choose earliest deadline/highest priority”; it may activate only after a threshold is crossed.

## QD

**Supporting cases**

- **D05** is the strongest skill-routing case: only D-F has both firmware and electrical skills, and the 12:00–12:20 meeting explains its delayed start.
- **D06** uses D-X at its appointment while D-F is occupied, consistent with routing to a compatible resource and reserving appointment capacity.
- **D07** is routed to D-X, preserving D-M for mechanical-only work such as D09.
- **D10** waits for D-X while D-F remains unavailable until 12:20, consistent with resource-state constraints.
- **D03** follows D02 on D-F and is both older and shorter than D05, consistent with a resource-local sequencing rule after eligibility filtering.

**Challenges**

- At **11:48**, D-X selects D07 ahead of priority-1, shorter D10. Skill eligibility does not explain this because both are eligible. Resource-local FIFO or prior assignment is needed.
- At **11:25**, D-M selects D04 instead of priority-1, shorter D08. This rejects strict global priority and SPT, but supports resource-local FIFO.
- D-X’s apparent **11:20–11:30 idleness** despite several compatible waiting jobs is not explained by skills alone. Appointment reservation for D06 is the most plausible known explanation, though a ten-minute reservation window is undocumented.
- **D09 before D07 on D-M** is not FIFO, because D07 arrived first. It is plausibly a routing decision that sends the cross-skilled D07 to D-X; without assignment logs, this remains inferred rather than observed.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

## Test 1: QA at 08:20 — appointment scheduling versus SPT

**Observed set:** A02 (estimate 20, no appointment) and A03 (estimate 10, appointment 08:20).  
**Observed choice:** A03.

- **Appointment explanation:** predicts A03 because its slot has become due.
- **SPT explanation:** also predicts A03 because 10 < 20.
- **FIFO explanation:** predicts A02.

**Discriminating observable:** the configured appointment tolerance and the dispatch system’s ranked candidate list at 08:20. A particularly useful future matched case would have an appointment job with a *longer* estimate than a waiting walk-in. Appointment scheduling predicts the appointment job; SPT predicts the walk-in.

## Test 2: QC at 10:54 — SLA escalation versus non-preemptive priority

**Observed set:** C01 (p3, 36, due 13:00), C05 (p1, 40, due 10:50), C06 (p3, 6, due 15:00), and C07 (p2, 18, due 12:00).  
**Observed choice:** C05.

- **Priority explanation:** predicts C05 because priority 1 is highest.
- **Deadline/SLA-escalation explanation:** predicts C05 because its deadline has already passed.
- **SPT explanation:** predicts C06.

**Discriminating observable:** the SLA risk score or escalation-state transition time. If C05 was promoted only when projected lateness crossed a threshold, that supports SLA escalation. If it had held the same effective rank since arrival, priority is more plausible. A future case where high priority and earliest deadline belong to different jobs would be especially diagnostic.

## Test 3: QD at 11:48 — resource-local FIFO versus global priority/SPT after skill filtering

**Observed set for D-X:** D07 (p3, 28, arrived 11:14) and D10 (p1, 12, arrived 11:25).  
**Observed choice:** D07.

- **Resource-local FIFO/prior assignment explanation:** predicts D07 because it is older or may already have been assigned to D-X.
- **Global priority explanation:** predicts D10.
- **Global SPT explanation:** also predicts D10.
- **Pure skill-based eligibility:** cannot discriminate because both are qualified.

**Discriminating observable:** assignment timestamp and resource-local queue position. If D07 was committed to D-X before D10 arrived, the choice is not a live priority violation. If both were unassigned and simultaneously reconsidered at 11:48, the evidence strongly favors FIFO/aging over priority and SPT.

## Test 4: QB at 10:14 — interrupted-job resumption versus continuing preemptive priority

**Observed set:** interrupted B01 (p3, unknown remaining time), B04 (p3, 8), B06 (p2, 20), and possibly appointment-held B07 (p3, 6).  
**Observed choice:** resume B01.

- **Strict preemptive priority:** predicts B06.
- **Resume-first with switching/setup cost:** predicts B01 if restarting it avoids sufficient setup loss.
- **Shortest remaining processing time:** cannot be evaluated without B01’s updated remaining estimate.

**Discriminating observable:** restart/setup cost and the current estimated remaining time for B01 at 10:14. A logged “resume bonus” or locked-work state would establish a hybrid; a small remaining time could instead make the choice compatible with shortest-remaining-processing-time.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

The ranking below reflects plausibility in these specific records.

1. **Known resource unavailability and skill constraints — very high plausibility.**  
   A1 calibration explains QA’s 08:52–08:58 gap; C1’s prior job explains QC’s delayed first decision; D-F’s meeting and the qualification matrix explain much of QD. Accounting for these increases confidence in appointment-plus-FIFO for QA, SPT-like selection at QC’s first genuine opportunity, and skill-based routing for QD. Ignoring them would create false policy violations.

2. **Manual dispatch override or prior commitment — high plausibility.**  
   QB explicitly records a dispatcher reassignment, proving that manual intervention exists. Similar unlogged commitments could explain QC’s C04 choice or QD’s D07 routing. If frequent, diagnoses should be reframed as “default policy plus discretionary override,” and confidence in any deterministic discipline should fall.

3. **Appointment tolerance/reservation windows — high plausibility.**  
   A03 starts exactly on time, A06 starts late, B07 is substantially late, and D-X appears reserved before D06’s appointment. Different queues may use different tolerances. A documented window would strengthen the appointment component in QA and QD; a narrow or nonexistent window would make D-X’s idle time and A06’s precedence require other explanations.

4. **SLA-risk or aging threshold that changes effective priority — medium-high plausibility.**  
   QC’s C05 becomes selected only after its deadline is missed, while C01 eventually runs after extreme waiting. A threshold policy explains both better than pure SPT. If confirmed, QC is a dynamic hybrid: SEPT under normal load, deadline escalation and anti-starvation overrides under risk.

5. **Preemption and resumption/setup costs — medium-high plausibility.**  
   QB repeatedly resumes B01 despite higher-priority or shorter waiting work. If restart costs are material, a preemptive-priority rule may only preempt for sufficiently urgent jobs and may give interrupted work a resume bonus. This would preserve the QB diagnosis while explaining apparent priority violations.

6. **Delayed or operationally unavailable arrival scans — medium plausibility, limited explanatory reach.**  
   Up to two minutes of delay can affect boundary cases such as whether B02 was visible just before 09:16 or B07 before 10:28. It cannot explain C06 being ignored at 10:32 after a 10:25 recorded arrival, nor most of the longer inversions. It should widen decision sets near boundaries but not be used as a universal excuse.

7. **Estimate noise or strategic misstatement — medium plausibility.**  
   `est_min` is dispatch-visible but may be noisy. If dispatch revised estimates after arrival, apparent SPT violations such as C04 over C06 or D07 over D10 could disappear. Strategic understatement could also distort observed order. Without estimate-version history, SPT can be supported but not proven.

8. **Unrecorded temporary ineligibility, setup, batching, or job readiness — medium-to-low plausibility.**  
   Parts, diagnostics, customer authorization, or workstation setup may make an arrived job not dispatchable. This could explain QA’s two-minute idle period, QC’s C04/C06 inversion, or QD’s resource idling. Because none of these states is supplied, they are hypotheses, not observations; relying on them extensively would make the policy unfalsifiable.

9. **Timestamp rounding and same-minute ordering — low but nonzero plausibility.**  
   Minute accuracy obscures event order within a minute, especially at B02’s 09:16 preemption or resource release/start transitions. It may alter whether a decision was immediate, but does not change the broad queue diagnoses.

| 12 | **Candidate ranking produced by the dispatch engine and final human-selected candidate** | Directly separates documented mechanism from manual deviation |

For robust inference, these fields should be collected over many decision opportunities, especially “conflict cases” where FIFO, priority, SPT, and appointment rules predict different jobs. Logging only service starts will continue to confound policy with eligibility.

# 7. Final synthesis

**QB is the most identifiable queue.** The explicit interruption of B01 for urgent B02 directly distinguishes preemptive from non-preemptive priority, although later dispatcher overrides and resume behavior show that the rule is hybrid rather than pure.

**QD is the least identifiable as a sequencing discipline.** Skill constraints and availability are clearly influential, but once eligibility is filtered, the evidence mixes local FIFO, appointment reservation, priority, SPT, and prior assignment. Parallel resources make start order alone particularly unsafe.

Across the queues, the most plausible interpretations are:

- **QA:** appointments with a tolerance/next-available rule, otherwise largely FIFO.
- **QB:** preemptive priority, modified by manual reassignment and resume/setup considerations.
- **QC:** SEPT/SPT under normal conditions, with deadline/priority escalation and likely anti-starvation behavior.
- **QD:** skill-based routing first, then resource-local sequencing and appointment reservation.

The most dangerous policy to infer from these observations alone is **non-preemptive priority**. Many high-priority jobs start late or behind lower-priority work, while apparent priority choices are often equally explained by deadlines, appointments, resource eligibility, or short estimates. More generally, QD demonstrates why a priority conclusion drawn from global start order without reconstructing resource-specific eligibility sets would be especially misleading.
