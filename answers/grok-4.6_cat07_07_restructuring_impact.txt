# 1. Mechanism map

The restructuring does not simply “remove handovers.” It **relocates** work from short, local, high-context loops into longer, shared, lower-context queues. Process-mining effects follow from that relocation, not from the org-chart change itself.

| Restructuring action | Organizational mechanism | Expected process-mining effect |
|---|---|---|
| Merge N_Tech, S_Tech, E_Tech into Technical Shared Service | Local specialist nodes disappear; one central resource pool appears | **Nominal handover reduction** of *same-person local loops* (`local caseworker → embedded tech → same caseworker`). Handover-of-work graph degree falls locally, but a new high-in-degree / high-out-degree hub appears. |
| Route all high-complexity and specialty cases to shared queues | Work is batched by rule rather than by informal local judgment | **Central queue formation**: waiting-time mass concentrates on `Technical Shared Service`, `Legal/Risk shared queue`, and `shared approval queue`. Service-time of specialists may fall (less idle, more batching); waiting-time of cases rises, especially tails. |
| Expected 15% higher specialist utilization | Idle slack of N_Tech/S_Tech is consumed; E_Tech overload is pooled | **Specialist utilization** of former local techs rises toward Legal/Risk levels (already 91–93%). Utilization is a resource metric, not a case metric: high utilization predicts longer queue tails, not shorter cycle times. |
| Broader detection of legal/risk cases (9 → 12 per 100) | More cases enter scarce specialist queues that are already near capacity | **Rework** can rise if referrals are later, thinner, or poorly specified. Current legal/risk handovers already have 14% post-handover rework and 9.6h median wait. |
| Keep Intake / simple Eligibility local; pull advice from shared queue | Case ownership stays local, expertise leaves | **Local expertise loss**: `local_expertise_flag` and activity-based resource similarity between caseworkers and tech activities decline. Informal pre-referral advice (currently absorbing demand before the 36/100 tech handovers) is not in the DFG as an activity; its disappearance shows up as *more* specialist referrals and *later* first specialist touch. |
| Remove one local supervisor; create shared approval for medium/high-value cases | Approval becomes a role, not a person known to the team | **Role clarity** declines if `org:resource` no longer maps stably to `team` + activity. Organizational-role discovery will show mixed membership: local caseworkers still do Implementation, but Supervisor Approval and Quality Check split across local and shared resources. Accountability for `outcome` / `rework_flag` becomes harder to attribute. |
| Fewer ad hoc cross-team transfers (6 → 3 per 100) | Formal queues replace absence cover | **Resilience to absence** can *worsen* even if cross-team handover frequency falls. Today, cover is rare and costly (8.9h, 18% rework) because notes are inconsistent. A shared queue is more resilient *if* case context travels; if not, absence of a local tech becomes a queue-length problem rather than a 6/100 exception. |
| Uniform routing by complexity/specialty, not by team | East’s overload is pooled; North/South lose local slack | **Fairness across teams/customer segments**: East cycle-time and SLA miss may improve (E_Tech 82%, 8.6d / 22.8d / 9.8% miss). North/South (strong local loops, 4.3 and 4.1 CSAT) may worsen on complex and regionally specific cases. Segments needing language/regional accommodation see more handovers even if average handover count falls. |

Causal chain (condensed):

```
centralize specialists
  → fewer local loops (nominal handover ↓)
  → larger shared queues (central wait ↑, utilization ↑)
  → later / thinner local advice (unnecessary referrals ↑ or quality of referral ↓)
  → rework and tail latency on high-complexity / specialty cases
  → role discovery: activities detach from teams
  → local learning atrophy (resource–activity similarity ↓ over time)
  → East may gain; North/South and non-standard segments may lose
```

Management’s expected table already encodes the tension: tech handovers *increase* (36 → 41) and waits *increase* (3.2h → 4.0h; return 1.6h → 2.2h). That is not handover reduction. It is **rerouting plus queueing**. The only nominal reductions are local intake→caseworker (88 → 83) and cross-team (6 → 3). Shared approval *creates* a new high-frequency handover (49/100 at 3.0h).

# 2. Scenario forecasts

Assumptions shared by all scenarios: routing rules are implemented as specified; Legal/Risk remain scarce; East starts more constrained than North/South; event-log attributes remain comparable.

## Optimistic

Conditions: shared-service work intake is well specified; local caseworkers keep doing light technical triage; queue SLAs are staffed; East’s overload is the binding constraint; notes/templates make context travel.

- **Handover pattern.** Local tech loops collapse. Handover-of-work network becomes a star around Technical Shared Service and the approval queue. Frequency of `local caseworker → Technical Shared Service` stays near 41/100 but *unnecessary* referrals fall after a few weeks. Cross-team ad hoc edges nearly disappear. Subcontracting network shows specialists returning work to the originating caseworker rather than retaining the case.
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern.** Median wait after tech handover stays near 4h because pooling absorbs East’s 82% utilization. Legal/Risk wait does not blow out (stays ~10h) because detection quality improves faster than volume (12/100). Service-time of specialists falls slightly (less context-switching across three local diaries).
- **Cycle-time distribution.** Mean and median cycle time improve modestly, driven by East (8.6d → closer to South). P90 improves for East (22.8d) and is stable for North/South. High-complexity tail does not thicken.
- **Rework and quality.** Post-handover rework after tech advice falls toward the old local-loop 8–9% because advice is standardized. Legal/Risk rework stays ~14% or slightly better. `local_expertise_flag` declines slowly but is offset by templates.
- **Role clarity and accountability.** Organizational roles become cleaner: “technical assessor” and “approver” are shared-service roles; “case owner” remains local. Working-together network still shows stable caseworker–supervisor pairs for low-value cases.
- **Fairness.** East and high-complexity cases gain the most. North/South CSAT holds. Customer segments with regional conventions see little change because Intake and Customer Contact stay local.
- **Early warning signs (should stay quiet).** No growth in `reassignment_count`; no rise in variants that insert extra `Local Customer Contact` after shared-service return; specialist queue age P90 stable; East–North cycle-time gap narrows without North P90 rising.

## Central (most likely)

Conditions: pooling helps East; North/South lose informal advice; Legal/Risk volume rises as estimated; shared approval standardizes but adds wait; context loss is partial, not catastrophic.

- **Handover pattern.** Nominal local-loop handovers fall, but **total specialist-touch handovers rise**. The DFG shows a thicker path `… → Specialist Review / Technical Shared Service → Local Customer Contact → …` because advice must be translated back to the customer. Management’s 41/100 tech send and 36/100 return materializes; a residual 5/100 are re-asks (`handover_reason = incomplete_advice` or similar). Shared approval at 49/100 becomes one of the highest-frequency human handovers, exceeding today’s local supervisor path (42/100).
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern.** Technical Shared Service median wait lands at 4–6h, not 4.0h, because the same pool also takes difficult Quality Check and some legal/risk triage. Wait is **heterogeneous**: simple technical questions are fast; high-complexity and multi-specialty cases wait much longer. Legal/Risk wait moves from 9.6h toward 10.5–14h. Shared approval median ~3h, but Friday/Monday batching produces a second mode.
- **Cycle-time distribution.** **Average cycle time is flat to slightly better** (East improvement offsets North/South). The distribution **spreads**: P50 may dip ~0.3–0.5d; P90 is stable or +1–2d; P95/P99 worsen for high-complexity and `{legal, risk, security}` cases. East P90 improves; North/South P90 worsen slightly.
- **Rework and quality.** Overall rework rate looks unchanged (~9–10%) while composition shifts: fewer “local misunderstanding” reworks, more “advice didn’t fit local constraint / customer convention” reworks. Rework after shared-service return exceeds the old 8% local-loop rate. `rework_flag` concentrates on high `case_complexity` and non-standard `customer_segment`.
- **Role clarity and accountability.** Role discovery still finds Intake and Caseworker as local roles, but Supervisor Approval and Quality Check become mixed. Working-together edges between a caseworker and a *named* specialist weaken; cases share a queue, not a colleague. `outcome` attribution to a resource becomes noisier (`org:resource` on Close is still the local caseworker, who did not make the technical or approval decision).
- **Fairness.** East is the winner on cycle time and SLA. North/South lose CSAT on complex cases. Segments that needed local tech presence (language, regional rules, on-site convention) see more handovers and more `Local Customer Contact` repeats. Legal/Risk requesters compete with newly detected cases; historical legal-heavy segments wait longer.
- **Early warning signs.** Rising `reassignment_count` on shared-service cases; growth of variants with two specialist touches; increasing waiting-time share of cycle time (`insert_case_service_waiting_time`); declining `local_expertise_flag` on cases that never enter the shared queue (loss of informal prevention); East utilization of the pool stays high while North/South local idle appears on caseworkers waiting for advice.

## Adverse

Conditions: central pool becomes a hidden bottleneck; informal local triage dies quickly; Legal/Risk and difficult QC share the same scarce people; approval queue becomes a political sink; notes do not carry local context.

- **Handover pattern.** Tech referrals overshoot 41/100 (local staff stop trying) and return loops multiply. Subcontracting network shows the shared service *retaining* cases or bouncing them across Legal/Risk/Technical queues (`handover_reason` = wrong_queue / needs_other_specialty). Cross-team handovers do not fall to 3/100: when the central queue is long, teams invent side-channels. Variant count rises.
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern.** Technical Shared Service median wait >6–8h; P90 measured in days. Legal/Risk, already at 91–93% utilization, tips into unstable queueing as volume goes 9 → 12+ per 100. Shared approval wait exceeds 3h systematically. Waiting time dominates cycle time for medium/high-value and high-complexity cases.
- **Cycle-time distribution.** Mean may still look acceptable if simple local cases (the majority) are unchanged or slightly faster. P90/P95 blow out. East’s improvement stalls because the shared pool inherits East’s old overload *plus* North/South demand *plus* QC/legal triage. Bimodal cycle times: easy local cases vs. anything that touches a shared queue.
- **Rework and quality.** Post-handover rework after central touches climbs toward the old cross-team 18% (same root cause: inconsistent context). Rework after Quality Check rises because difficult QC is now remote from the team that implemented. Repeat `Local Customer Contact` increases.
- **Role clarity and accountability.** Organizational roles blur: many resources perform fragments of Specialist Review, Quality Check, and Approval. No single role owns the case across the risk decision. Working-together network fragments. Staff report “the queue decided.” `reassignment_count` and multi-resource cases rise.
- **Fairness.** North/South customers lose local technical access they previously had; East does not gain enough to offset the new bottleneck. High-complexity, legal/risk, and accommodation-need segments systematically sit in the right tail. Low-complexity, digitally standard segments look “improved,” which can fool a global average.
- **Early warning signs.** Queue age growing faster than arrivals (unstable); specialist utilization >90% with rising service-time (fatigue/context switching); surge in `handover_reason` ∈ {incomplete, wrong_queue, absence_cover}; `local_expertise_flag` collapse; CSAT drop isolated to complex/regional segments; approval queue rework (send-backs) rising.

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

Average cycle time is dominated by the large mass of local, low-complexity cases that the proposal **does not move**. Those cases can stay flat or improve slightly (automation, 88 → 83 intake handovers) while the restructured tail deteriorates. At least the following can hide behind a stable or better mean.

1. **Tail latency (P90/P95/P99).** Management already forecasts *higher* median waits on the new high-frequency edges (tech 3.2→4.0h, return 1.6→2.2h, approval 2.4→3.0h). A mean can improve if East’s long cases shorten a little and simple cases are untouched, while the right tail of shared-queue cases lengthens. Report distributional statistics and waiting-time share, not the mean.

2. **High-complexity cases.** They are *explicitly* forced onto the central path. Their waiting time is structurally higher, and they lose the local loop that currently returns advice in 1.6h with 8% rework. A global average that mixes them with Bot_Intake-driven simple cases will not show this. Filter on `case_complexity` and compare performance DFGs.

3. **Local knowledge loss.** `local_expertise_flag` and regional conventions currently suppress rework. That knowledge is not an activity in the DFG; it is a missing handover. When it dies, the first observable is *more* correct-looking specialist referrals, which management may celebrate as “better routing.” Rework and extra `Local Customer Contact` appear later and may be attributed to “case mix.”

4. **Fairness across teams and segments.** East is capacity-constrained (E_Tech 82%, 11.5% rework, 9.8% SLA miss, 3.8 CSAT) and is the plausible beneficiary of pooling. North/South start from strong local loops (4.3 / 4.1 CSAT, lower rework). A company-level mean can improve solely because East improves, while North/South complex and regionally specific customers pay. Customer segments needing language or local accommodation can face more handovers even as `cross-team handover` falls from 6 to 3 per 100.

5. **Learning and skill atrophy.** If all hard cases route away, local caseworkers stop accumulating technical activity. `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` and role discovery will show caseworkers clustering only on Intake / Contact / Implementation. That is invisible in cycle time for months, then shows up as higher referral rates and weaker exception handling. Utilization of the center will look “efficient” while the organization becomes more dependent on it.

6. **Accountability and role clarity.** Shared approval standardizes decisions and can reduce some local idiosyncrasy. It also detaches the approver from the team and from Close. Average cycle time cannot detect send-backs with no owner, mixed organizational roles, or a working-together network that no longer has stable pairs. Quality incidents will be hard to attribute in the log (`org:resource` on Close ≠ decision-maker).

7. **Exception handling and resilience.** Today’s failure mode is rare, ugly cross-team cover (6/100, 8.9h, 18% rework). The proposal reduces that frequency by design. The new failure mode is a single saturated pool (tech + legal/risk triage + difficult QC) during absence, demand spikes, or holiday weeks. Temporal features (`extract_temporal_features_dataframe`) will show synchronized waiting-time spikes that a long-run average smooths away. Legal/Risk are already at 91–93% utilization; they have no spare capacity for “broader detection” (9 → 12 per 100) without a queue explosion.

8. **Hidden work and measurement substitution.** Informal local advice never hit the log. After centralization, that work becomes a logged handover, so “specialist utilization” and “correct routing” rise by construction. Measuring only those, plus average cycle time, systematically favors the proposal even when customer wait and rework on hard cases worsen.

# 5. Pilot design

Do not roll out all three teams, all case types, and shared approval in one step. The forecast is **heterogeneous by team** (East ≠ North/South) and **heterogeneous by case class** (complexity, specialty, segment).

**Pilot scope.**  
Phase 1 (8–12 weeks): route only **high-complexity** and `{legal, risk, security}` cases from **East + one strong local team (South)** into Technical Shared Service. Keep North as a local-specialist control. Do **not** yet fold difficult Quality Check or Legal/Risk triage into the same named queue as technical advice. Keep local supervisors; do not remove a supervisor or stand up the shared approval queue in Phase 1.

Phase 2 (only if Phase 1 guardrails hold): add shared approval for medium/high-value cases on the same two teams, still holding North as control.

Phase 3: consider North and the QC/legal-triage co-location only after Phase 2.

**Comparison group.**  
- **Control team:** North remains on the current embedded-specialist model (N_Tech stays local).  
- **Within-team controls:** low/medium-complexity South/East cases that remain local.  
- **Historical baseline:** 12–16 weeks pre-change, same seasonality window if volume is seasonal.  
This separates “East needed pooling” from “shared service is globally better.”

**Pre/post observation window.**  
- Baseline: ≥12 weeks, ≥2,000 cases per large team if volume holds (North ~820/month, South ~760, East ~520).  
- Pilot: 8–12 weeks after a 2-week hypercare (exclude hypercare from confirmatory stats).  
- Maturation check: compare weeks 1–4 vs. 5–12 of the pilot (referral overshoot often appears first).

**Segmentation (mandatory, not optional dashboards).**  
For every metric, cut by:

| Cut | Values |
|---|---|
| `team` | North (control), South (pilot), East (pilot) |
| `case_complexity` | low / medium / high |
| `required_specialty` | none, legal, risk, security, technical |
| `customer_segment` | including any language/regional accommodation flag |
| `priority` / value band | especially cases that would later hit shared approval |
| route | local-only vs. touched shared service |

Never report a single pre/post average.

**Guardrail metrics (weekly).**  
Hard guardrails (any two amber or one red → freeze expansion):

- Shared-service waiting-time P90 vs. baseline local-tech wait (3.2h median / implied tail): freeze if P90 > 2× baseline local-tech P90 for two consecutive weeks.
- Legal/Risk queue: freeze if utilization >95% or median wait >12h (above the 10.5h management estimate).
- Rework after shared-service return >12% (above old local loop 8–9%, approaching cross-team 18%).
- `reassignment_count` mean on pilot cases +0.3 vs. control.
- SLA miss on high-complexity or specialty cases +2 pp vs. that segment’s own baseline.
- North (control) metrics must remain stable (detects exogenous shocks).
- CSAT / complaint rate on regional/language segments: no decline vs. baseline.

Soft guardrails: variant count, extra `Local Customer Contact`, `local_expertise_flag` on *non-routed* cases, staff-reported time-to-informal-advice.

**Staff feedback collection.**  
Weekly 10-minute structured pulse for caseworkers, remaining local techs, shared-service specialists, and supervisors: time to get informal advice, quality of case context in the queue, ownership of send-backs, perceived skill use. Code comments to `handover_reason` categories. Do not rely on the event log for informal advice disappearance.

**Rollback / adjustment triggers.**  
- Rollback Phase 1 routing for low-complexity leakage if >15% of shared-service arrivals are low-complexity.  
- Split queues immediately if the same resources show high multi-activity membership (tech + legal triage + difficult QC) *and* wait P90 is red.  
- Restore a named local tech presence on South if South high-complexity P90 or CSAT worsens while East improves (do not “average” them).  
- Do not proceed to supervisor removal if approval send-back rate or role-discovery mixing is worse than baseline local supervisor loops.  
- Full rollback to embedded specialists if adverse-scenario early warnings appear on both pilot teams, not only North/South.

# 6. Recommendation

**Pilot first, and modify the design before any full rollout. Do not implement the proposal as a single package.**

Proceeding as specified bundles four different interventions with different risk profiles: (1) pooling overloaded East tech capacity, (2) stripping North/South of embedded tech, (3) merging technical advice with Legal/Risk and difficult QC in one queue, (4) replacing a local supervisor with a shared approval queue. (1) is the only change with a clear current-state justification in the log (E_Tech 82%, East 8.6d / 22.8d / 11.5% rework / 9.8% miss). (2)–(4) are where handover reduction is nominal, queues form, role clarity drops, and learning/fairness risks sit.

**Modify even the pilot:** keep Legal/Risk as a separate scarce queue; do not assign difficult QC to the same pool in Phase 1; do not remove a supervisor until the technical pool’s waiting-time distribution is stable; staff Legal/Risk *before* expanding detection from 9 to 12 per 100 cases. A 15% utilization increase on people already at 68–82%, next to colleagues at 91–93%, is not free capacity—it is a move toward the unstable region of a queue.

**Conditional monitoring rule.** Expansion is allowed only if, relative to North and to each team’s own baseline, *all* of the following hold after the maturation window: high-complexity and specialty P90 cycle time and waiting time are not worse; rework after shared return is not higher; low-complexity referral rate is not higher (informal advice still working); Legal/Risk wait is not worse; fairness cuts (team × segment) show East gaining and North/South not paying; role discovery still shows a clear local case owner.

**What management should not infer from early averages.**  
Do not treat a flat or improved global mean cycle time, higher specialist utilization, or fewer *local-loop* handovers as success. Those three numbers are the ones the design *mechanically* moves, including in the adverse scenario. They can improve because informal work became a logged queue, because East’s extreme tail pulled the mean, or because simple cases never entered the new path. Do not infer that “handovers went down” if the handover-of-work network merely replaced short local edges with fewer but slower central edges of higher weight. Do not infer resilience from a drop in cross-team handovers. Do not infer quality from standardized approval volume. Infer success only from **segmented distributions**, **post-handover rework**, **queue stability of scarce specialists**, **role/ownership clarity**, and **stable local skill on cases that remain local**.

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.algo.organizational_mining.roles import algorithm as roles_alg

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 0. Load, tag period / route, format
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
df = pd.read_csv("event_log.csv", parse_dates=["time:timestamp"])

# period: baseline vs pilot (or vs post). Adjust the cut date.
CUT = pd.Timestamp("2026-03-01", tz="UTC")
df["period"] = (df["time:timestamp"] >= CUT).map({False: "before", True: "after"})

df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    resource_key="org:resource",
)

# case-level attributes to broadcast if they live on events
CASE_ATTRS = [
    "team", "location", "required_specialty", "priority",
    "case_complexity", "customer_segment", "outcome",
    "rework_flag", "local_expertise_flag", "reassignment_count",
]

def cases_with(df, **eq):
    """Filter cases that have at least one event matching each attr=value."""
    out = df
    for k, v in eq.items():
        out = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(out, k, [v], level="case", retain=True)
    return out

def split_period(df, period):
    return pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(df, "period", [period], level="event", retain=True)

before, after = split_period(df, "before"), split_period(df, "after")

SHARED_ACTS = {
    "Specialist Review", "Technical Shared Service",
    "Legal Review", "Risk Review", "Shared Approval",
    "Difficult Quality Check",
}
LOCAL_TECH = {"N_Tech", "S_Tech", "E_Tech"}

def touched_shared(df):
    return pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        df, "concept:name", list(SHARED_ACTS), level="case", retain=True
    )

def local_only(df):
    return pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        df, "concept:name", list(SHARED_ACTS), level="case", retain=False
    )

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Handover-of-work, working-together, subcontracting
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def org_networks(log, name):
    how = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log)
    wt  = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)
    sub = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(log)
    # inspect edge weights; normalize per 100 cases
    n_cases = log["case:concept:name"].nunique()
    def per_100(net):
        return {e: 100.0 * w / n_cases for e, w in net.edges.items()}
    return {
        "name": name,
        "n_cases": n_cases,
        "handover_per_100": per_100(how),
        "working_together": wt,
        "subcontracting": sub,
        # ping-pong / local-loop signature: A->B and B->A both heavy
    }

for label, log in [("before", before), ("after", after)]:
    org_networks(log, label)
    for team in ["North", "South", "East"]:
        org_networks(cases_with(log, team=team), f"{label}-{team}")

# Compare specific edges of interest (string-match resources / groups)
# before: N_Case* -> N_Tech -> N_Case*   (local loop)
# after:  *_Case* -> Technical Shared Service -> *_Case*
# also: cross-team edges (resource team prefix != case team)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Variants and performance DFG
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def variant_and_dfg(log):
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)
    dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    pdfg, psa, pea = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)  # mean/median times on edges
    return variants, dfg, pdfg

# SLA: assume SLA hours depend on priority; attach to case start/end
def sla_miss_rate(log, sla_hours_by_priority):
    cases = (log.sort_values("time:timestamp")
                .groupby("case:concept:name")
                .agg(start=("time:timestamp", "min"),
                     end=("time:timestamp", "max"),
                     priority=("priority", "first"),
                     team=("team", "first"),
                     case_complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
                     customer_segment=("customer_segment", "first"),
                     outcome=("outcome", "last"),
                     rework_flag=("rework_flag", "max"),
                     reassignment_count=("reassignment_count", "max"),
                     local_expertise_flag=("local_expertise_flag", "max")))
    cases["cycle_h"] = (cases["end"] - cases["start"]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0
    cases["sla_h"] = cases["priority"].map(sla_hours_by_priority)
    cases["sla_miss"] = cases["cycle_h"] > cases["sla_h"]
    return cases

# Post-handover rework: cases whose first Rework timestamp is after first

def rework_after_touch(log, touch_activities):
    ev = log.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])
    rows = []
    for cid, g in ev.groupby("case:concept:name"):
        acts = list(zip(g["concept:name"], g["time:timestamp"]))
        t_touch = next((t for a, t in acts if a in touch_activities), None)
        t_rework = next((t for a, t in acts if a == "Rework"), None)
        rows.append({
            "case": cid,
            "touched": t_touch is not None,
            "rework_after_touch": bool(t_touch and t_rework and t_rework >= t_touch),
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)
```
