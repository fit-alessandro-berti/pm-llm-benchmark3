# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

Average (median) cycle time can remain stable or even improve while serious damage occurs in the distribution, in specific subpopulations, and in organizational capabilities. At least six such hidden risks are critical here:

### 4.1 Tail latency hidden by median improvement
The restructuring may reduce median cycle time for simple cases (`case_complexity` = low) by removing local supervisor loops, while high-complexity cases accumulate in the central Technical Shared Service queue. Because high-complexity cases are a minority (~20–30%), the overall median can drop even as the 90th and 95th percentiles rise sharply (e.g., from 22.8d to 30d+ for East). The `discover_performance_dfg` will reveal this only if analysts explicitly filter by `case_complexity` and examine arc percentiles, not just mean/median aggregates.

### 4.2 High-complexity case degradation masked by volume-weighting
All high-complexity cases are routed to the shared service. If that pool has only a fraction of prior embedded capacity, these cases experience disproportionate delay. The event log’s `case_complexity` column allows filtering (`pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values`), but if management reports only unfiltered averages, the severe tail for complex work is invisible. The `variant` analysis (`pm4py.get_variants`) will show a growing “long-loop” variant that is rare in count but catastrophic in customer impact.

### 4.3 Local knowledge loss invisible in time metrics
The loss of `local_expertise_flag` usage is an organizational-capability event, not a direct cycle-time event. Early on, local staff may still complete cases quickly by referring everything to the central pool, keeping median time flat. Over months, however, incorrect initial assessments (`Local Technical Assessment` errors) increase `rework_flag` and `reassignment_count`. These quality failures appear in the log under `rework_flag` and `outcome`, but if analysts only track `time:timestamp` differences, the root cause—loss of embedded expertise—is missed.

### 4.4 Fairness across teams and customer segments
North and South have strong local loops; East is already overloaded. A shared queue can “level” median times across `team` values, creating an appearance of fairness, while actually harming North/South (who lose local speed) and benefiting East only modestly (whose tail delays worsen). Moreover, `customer_segment` requiring regional language or accommodation (`location` conventions) may suffer more handovers (`cross-team handover` rebounds) without changing the overall average. The fairness gap is visible only when comparing `filter_event_attribute_values` by `customer_segment`, `location`, and `priority` simultaneously.

### 4.5 Learning and skill atrophy (long-lag invisible effect)
If all difficult technical assessments are routed away, local caseworkers (`N_Case1`, `S_Case1`, etc.) stop developing technical judgment. In the short term (`3–6 months`), this does not affect cycle time; it may even reduce it because local staff avoid “trying” hard assessments. In the long term, the organization loses resilience and innovation capacity. The log shows this indirectly through declining `local_expertise_flag` correlation with positive `outcome`, and through `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` showing local resources converging on only intake/eligibility activities—an organizational “deskilling” pattern invisible in average duration.

### 4.6 Accountability and exception-handling failure
Standardizing approvals in a shared queue can stabilize median approval time (`shared approval queue  implementation`), but it fragments ownership. When an exception occurs (e.g., an unusual `required_specialty` combination or a high-`priority` escalation), no local supervisor has full context. The case bounces (`reassignment_count` >1) between shared queues, but the average may still look acceptable because most standard cases flow smoothly. `discover_subcontracting_network` reveals the accountability gap: shared resources appear in dense subcontracting relationships across all `team` nodes, indicating diffuse ownership. `discover_organizational_roles` may show unstable role assignments over time. These patterns are not captured by `cycle_time` averages but by network topology and reassignment statistics.

### 4.7 Resilience to absence masked by normal-load averages
Under normal staffing, the shared pool meets targets. If one specialist is absent, the entire system slows because there is no local backup with embedded knowledge. The event log will show this only on absence days (`org:resource` missing) if analysts compare `time:timestamp` distributions conditionally. Average metrics computed over full months smooth over these spike events, hiding the single-point-of-failure risk.

Compare the three `team` groups across four dimensions using `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` and `pm4py.filter_variants`:
1. **`case_complexity`** (low / medium / high)
2. **`required_specialty`** (none / tech / legal / risk / security)
3. **`customer_segment`** and `location` (regional/custom vs. standard)
4. **`priority`** (low / medium / high)

### Pre/post observation window
- **Pre-pilot baseline:** 90 days of event-log data (`time:timestamp`) before rollout.
- **Pilot period:** 90 days after rollout (Day 1 = shared-service activation for East; shared-approval activation for South).
- **Evaluation holdback:** 30 days after pilot end to capture lagging `rework_flag` and `outcome`.

### Guardrail metrics (rollback/adjustment triggers)
Measured weekly using PM4Py functions; breach for **2 consecutive weeks** triggers action.

| Guardrail | Metric / Function | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Central queue bottleneck | Median wait (`insert_case_service_waiting_time`) at shared-service activities | > 6.0h |
| Tail latency risk | 90th percentile cycle time (`extract_temporal_features_dataframe`) for high-`case_complexity` cases in East | > 28d (baseline 22.8d + 20%) |
| Rework explosion | `get_rework_cases_per_activity` for `Specialist Review` or `Local Technical Assessment` in East/South | > 14% (from ~9–11.5%) |
| Expertise erosion | `local_expertise_flag` usage rate in East + South | Drop > 40% from baseline with concurrent `rework_flag` increase |
| Accountability breakdown | Mean `reassignment_count` per case in East | > 0.5 |
| Fairness breach | Difference in median cycle time (`filter` by `customer_segment`) between standard and regional/custom segments in East | > 25% relative increase |
| Resilience failure | Cycle-time divergence on days with shared-resource absence (`org:resource` missing) vs. presence | > 2x median wait increase |

### Staff feedback collection
- **Quantitative:** Weekly 3-question survey to all `org:resource` in East/South mapped to `team`:
  1. “I understand who owns the decision on my current cases.” (role clarity)
  2. “I can access technical advice quickly without formal queue entry.” (local expertise access)
  3. “The shared approval process improves fairness for customers.” (fairness/perception)
- **Qualitative:** Bi-weekly 30-minute focus groups by `team` and role (`Intake`, `Case`, `Tech`, `Supervisor` / shared-queue staff). Map themes to `discover_working_together_network` changes.

### Rollback or adjustment triggers
- **Full rollback (East to local model; South to full local):** Any two guardrails breached for 2 consecutive weeks **plus** staff survey score < 2.5/5 on role clarity.
- **Partial adjustment (retain shared approval but revert Technical Shared Service for East):** If queue bottleneck and tail-latency guardrails breach, but rework and fairness remain acceptable. This tests whether approval centralization is separable from technical centralization.
- **Expansion hold:** Even if pilot succeeds, do not expand to North until:
  - `discover_organizational_roles` shows stable shared-role definitions for 60 days,
  - `discover_subcontracting_network` shows sparse, clear subcontracting (not dense confusion),
  - `filter_event_attribute_values` on `customer_segment` shows no fairness gap >15%.

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: Pilot first—do not proceed to full organizational rollout. Use a phased, comparative pilot (East full; South hybrid; North control) with the guardrail framework above, and make continuation conditional on leading and lagging indicators, not on average cycle time alone.**

### Reasoning
The restructuring addresses real inefficiencies—`Legal` (93%) and `Risk` (91%) are near capacity; East (`E_Tech` 82%) is clearly overloaded; cross-team handovers (`6` per 100, `8.9h` wait, `18%` rework) are dysfunctional. Centralization could improve specialist utilization and standardize approvals. However, the mechanism map shows that the proposed design **replaces low-latency, high-context local loops with high-latency, low-context central queues**, creates a **single point of failure** for technical and approval work, and risks **deskilling** local staff (`N_Case`, `S_Case`, `E_Case`) by removing `local_expertise_flag` pathways.

The scenario forecasts demonstrate that **median cycle time is an unreliable success metric**: in the central and adverse scenarios, median can stay flat or improve for simple cases while the 90th percentile explodes, rework shifts to hidden activities, and fairness diverges across `team` and `customer_segment` values.

### What management should NOT infer from early averages
1. **Do not infer efficiency from a falling median cycle time.** Check `discover_performance_dfg` percentiles filtered by `case_complexity` and `priority`. If the 90th percentile for high-complexity cases rises, the system is failing its most valuable or vulnerable work.
2. **Do not infer quality improvement from flat overall `rework_flag`.** Filter `get_rework_cases_per_activity` by `concept:name`. Rework may migrate from visible `Specialist Review` to hidden `Local Technical Assessment` or post-`Implementation` failures.
3. **Do not infer fairness from equalized median times across `team`.** Equal medians can mean North/South slowed down while East improved slightly. Examine `filter_event_attribute_values` by `location`, `customer_segment`, and `priority`.
4. **Do not infer resilience from normal-week performance.** Test absence-day divergence in `time:timestamp` and `reassignment_count`. A shared pool that works at 90% capacity fails catastrophically at 70% staffing.
5. **Do not infer role clarity from organizational chart changes.** Use `discover_organizational_roles` and `discover_subcontracting_network` on the event log to see whether `org:resource` assignments are stable or diffuse.
6. **Do not infer specialist utilization gains are sustainable.** `get_service_time` may show 85%+ busy ratios that look efficient but actually represent overload (high `reassignment_count`, long `waiting` intervals, staff survey declines).

### Conditional path forward
- **Phase 1 (Months 1–3):** Execute the East/South/North pilot as designed.
- **Phase 2 (Month 4):** Evaluate all leading indicators (queue waits, variant patterns, network density, expertise flag usage) and staff feedback.
- **Phase 3 (Months 4–6):** If guardrails hold and `discover_organizational_roles` shows stable, clear shared roles, expand shared approval to North and consider technical shared service for South—**only if** `local_expertise_flag` pathways are preserved for informal advisory access.
- **Abort / Revert:** If adverse scenario signals appear (tail latency > guardrail, `reassignment_count` > 0.5, fairness gap widening, staff role-clarity collapse), revert East and South to embedded specialists and redesign the shared service with explicit capacity buffers, regional advisory slots, and clear ownership rules before any re-attempt.

The restructuring is **not rejected outright**, but it is **not approved for full rollout**. The only defensible management decision is to **treat the event log as the primary decision source**, not internal estimates of “nominal handover reduction” or projected utilization.

### Key analytical moves in the sketch
- **Before vs. after network comparison** (`discover_handover_of_work_network`, `discover_working_together_network`, `discover_subcontracting_network`) reveals the structural shift from intra-team loops to central hub-and-spoke patterns.
- **Performance DFG** (`discover_performance_dfg`) makes queue build-up visible as thicker/slower arcs entering shared-service activities.
- **Filtering by `team`, `case_complexity`, `required_specialty`, `customer_segment`, and `priority`** (`filter_event_attribute_values`) ensures analysis does not rely on global averages.
- **Variant analysis** (`get_variants`, `filter_variants`) detects new failure-mode loops (e.g., clarification cycles, misroutes).
- **Waiting/service time insertion** (`insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `get_service_time`) quantifies the bottleneck effect hidden in simple handover counts.
- **Resource similarity and role discovery** (`discover_activity_based_resource_similarity`, `discover_organizational_roles`) measure whether shared roles are coherent or diffused.
- **Rework mapping** (`get_rework_cases_per_activity`) connects quality failures back to specific `concept:name` activities—essential for identifying whether local expertise loss is driving hidden errors.

This framework should be executed separately for the **pilot treatment groups** (East full, South hybrid) and the **control group** (North local) to support the conditional recommendation in Section 6.
