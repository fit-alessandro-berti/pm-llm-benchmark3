## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

* **Lexicographic boundary vs. trade-off zone:** Mandatory compliance controls (**Rules 6, 7, 9, 10**) and statutory sanctions prohibitions are **hard constraints**, not pricing parameters. A naive expected-value calculation might note that if the probability of an actual sanctions hit is low (say, 0.05–0.15), `0.10 × €300,000 = €30,000` looks comparable to the `€20,000–€35,000` SLA loss. That reasoning is invalid in regulated payments for two reasons:
  1. **Process invalidity:** Normative rules forbid trading mandatory controls for SLA savings.
  2. **Asymmetric, non-ergodic tail risk:** Unlawful execution in a sanctioned-border corridor carries `€100,000–€500,000` in direct exposure *plus* non-monetary harms that cannot be linearly monetized—supervisory enforcement orders, loss of USD/EUR correspondent clearing access, personal officer liability, and mandatory lookback audits.
* **Dominance among conformant choices:**
  * **Plan D vs. Action E:** Action E forfeits `€20,000–€35,000` with near certainty and triggers `€15,000–€80,000` in prolonged-hold harm. Plan D risks only one scarce API call while preserving a positive probability of meeting the 90-minute deadline (if S4 frees up near 35m) or missing it by only 15–50 minutes (which induces far less supplier damage than a 6–24 hour hold), *and* retains Action E as a lossless fallback if the match stays unresolved. Thus, **D stochastically dominates E** on P-884 outcomes.
  * **Action E vs. Action F:** With a `0.55–0.75` baseline approval probability for an established supplier, immediate rejection (**F**) converts moderate temporary delay risk into a high-probability permanent false-rejection loss (`€15,000–€80,000`) *plus* failure to execute (`€20,000–€35,000`), while facing conformance ambiguity. Unless the customer strictly prefers immediate rejection to re-route funds elsewhere, **E dominates F**.
  * **Plan D vs. Action C:** Both protect compliance, so comparison hinges on **system-wide operational loss**. Action C improves P-884's odds of saving `€20,000–€35,000` modestly, but *still* can breach P-884's SLA (review + execution up to 90m+ without alternate screening) while inflicting an immediate `+0.12 to +0.28` breach probability increase on high-value P-901 (70m deadline). Plan D resolves match ambiguity while waiting for S4, protecting P-901 completely.
* **Observational vs. interventional uncertainty:** The `0.48–0.64` SLA breach and `0.55–0.75` approval figures reflect passive historical routing. Intervening via **C** compresses review under interruption stress (risking slower or more defensive decisions by S2), whereas intervening via **D** injects exogenous vendor intelligence before S4 starts, reducing review variance and lowering both false-clear and false-reject error rates.
