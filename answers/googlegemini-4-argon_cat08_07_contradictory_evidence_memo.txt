# 1. Executive memo

**To:** Executive Management Team  
**From:** VP of Process Excellence & Analytics  
**Date:** May 20, 2024  
**Subject:** Decision Memo: Strategic Next Steps for Fiber-Installation Redesign R  

### Recommendation
We should **immediately adopt a modified Alternative E (Hybrid Segmented Rollout with Hard Process Controls and Targeted Experimentation)**. Specifically, we must decouple the bundled interventions of Redesign R:
1. **Scale** combined validation/scheduling and single-coordinator ownership *only* for **simple, low-risk houses** (coupled with a shared-backup rule to mitigate coordinator bottlenecks).
2. **Immediately halt** post-billing activation across all segments, and **suspend** predictive field-survey skipping for apartments, older buildings, and business customers.
3. **Launch a 6-week measurement and controlled experimentation phase** to recalibrate the survey-skipping algorithm and resolve coordinator capacity constraints before considering wider national expansion.

### Why Neither Full Scale Nor Full Rollback Is Viable
Redesign R was tested as a monolithic bundle of four distinct changes: merged intake, single-coordinator ownership, predictive survey skipping, and post-billing activation. Process-mining evidence proves this bundle creates a sharp **speed-versus-reliability trade-off** across customer segments:

* **Why we cannot scale R as-is (Rejecting Alternative A):** While median cycle time fell by 24% (14.2 to 10.8 days) and visible pre-complaint cost dropped by EUR 23/case, the process tail and rework metrics degraded severely. The 95th percentile cycle time surged by 17 days (to 62.0 days), first-time-right (FTR) installation fell 6 percentage points (to 76%), and 30-day complaints jumped from 6.1% to 9.8%. Crucially, deferring activation checks until after billing start generated a toxic loop of manual activations (up from 7% to 15%), billing disputes, and customers being billed for non-working connections. Scaling this nationally will overwhelm second-visit field teams (already at 92% utilization) and erode brand equity.
* **Why we should not roll back to Baseline (Rejecting Alternative B):** A blanket rollback sacrifices genuine, structural gains in our largest clean segment. For **simple, low-risk houses** (34% of pilot volume), Redesign R reduced median duration by 6.0 days, cut p90 duration by 8.0 days, *improved* FTR by 3pp, and reduced complaints by 1pp. Rolling back throws away proven efficiency in uncomplicated installs because of control failures in complex ones.

### Navigating the Trade-off via Process-Mining Insights
Our conformance and frequency analyses isolate the exact mechanisms driving the conflict:
1. **Front-end simplification works when physical risk is low:** Merging address validation and scheduling (38% of pilot flow) drives the median cycle time improvement without harming downstream quality in simple single-family homes.
2. **Skipping surveys and permit checks breaks complex installs:** In medium-risk apartments (29% share) and permit-sensitive old buildings (18% share), skipping field surveys (down from 31% to 18% of scheduled cases) and omitting required permit checks directly shifts work from the underutilized survey team (52% utilization) to the overloaded second-visit team (92% utilization). This adds +12 to +25 days to p90 cycle times.
3. **Single-point ownership creates fragile bottlenecks:** Reducing cases with 3+ handovers from 34% to 12% improved happy-path speed, but drove coordinator utilization to 93%. Social network analysis shows extreme centralization: when a coordinator is overloaded or absent, exception cases stall completely, inflating the p95 tail.
4. **Post-billing activation is a pure compliance and CX failure:** Alignment diagnostics show frequent *Billing start before Activation test* deviations, shifting customer complaints from "waiting for appointment" to "installed but not working / billed prematurely."

### Path Forward and Decision Gates
By deploying the simplified workflow strictly to low-risk houses while restoring pre-billing activation gates and mandatory surveys/permits for complex buildings, we capture ~35% of the speed benefits immediately while capping downside tail risk. Over the next 6 weeks, we will instrument missing risk/cost data (Measurements M3, M6, M4, M7, M5) to test whether pooled pod-ownership and a retrained risk model allow us to safely bring apartments and rural installs into the redesigned flow.

# 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

Process-mining evidence often appears contradictory when a process bifurcates into a streamlined mainstream path and a chaotic exception path. Here is the structural reconciliation of the five core metric conflicts in Redesign R:

### 1. High Fitness (0.94) vs. Low Precision (0.51)
* **Mechanism:** In process mining, *fitness* measures the ability of the discovered model to replay the observed event log without blocking, whereas *precision* measures whether the model restricts behavior *only* to what is actually observed (avoiding underfitting/spaghetti freedom).
* **Why they conflict here:** Redesign R channeled 64% of scheduled appointments directly to installation and merged intake for 38% of cases. Because a high volume of simple cases followed this tight, linear happy path, the discovery algorithm easily captured the dominant backbone, boosting fitness from 0.87 to 0.94. However, when cases failed (19% failed visits, 19% manual activations, post-billing complaints, skipped permits), they entered unstructured, ad-hoc recovery loops at arbitrary points (both before and after *Billing start*). To accommodate these unstandardized exceptions alongside the happy path, the discovered Petri net/BPMN model had to include permissive parallel/choice gateways and self-loops—allowing thousands of theoretical trace combinations and collapsing precision from 0.79 to 0.51.

### 2. Improved Median Duration (-3.4 days) vs. Worse Tail Duration (p90 +7.5d, p95 +17.0d)
* **Mechanism:** The median tracks the 50th percentile case (the typical happy path), whereas p90 and p95 track the 10% and 5% worst exception cases.
* **Why they conflict here:** Merging *Address validation* and *Appointment scheduling* and skipping *Field survey* shaved days off the first 76% of cases that succeeded on the first visit (pulling the median down by 3.4 days). Conversely, the 24% of cases that failed first-time-right installation were hit by a compounding triple penalty: (a) they required a *Second visit* (and often a retrospective survey or missing permit check), (b) they entered queues for the second-visit team (92% utilization) and coordinators (93% utilization), where Little’s Law ($W_q \propto \frac{\rho}{1-\rho}$) dictates exponential waiting-time growth above 90% utilization, and (c) if a coordinator was absent, the case stalled completely. Thus, the distribution became heavily right-skewed (bimodal).

### 3. Fewer Handovers (3+ handovers down from 34% to 12%) vs. Lower Operational Resilience
* **Mechanism:** Handovers represent transfers of case ownership between distinct roles or individuals; resilience is the system's ability to absorb variance, capacity shocks, and staff absences without stalling.
* **Why they conflict here:** Assigning a single installation coordinator reduced ping-ponging between scheduling, survey, and dispatch teams, which cut coordination overhead on routine orders. However, in the baseline process, handovers went to *pooled functional queues*—if one agent was sick or busy, another agent pulled the ticket. Redesign R tied each order to a *named individual* running at 93% utilization and centralized the working-together network around them. When that specific coordinator took leave or became swamped with complex escalations, tacit context was locked in their inbox, causing *Failed visit $\rightarrow$ Second visit* delays to spike. Specialization efficiency was traded away for single-point-of-failure fragility.

### 4. Lower Visible Cost (EUR 161 vs. EUR 184) vs. Higher Rework and Downstream Cost (EUR 207+ vs. EUR 198)
* **Mechanism:** Visible cost captures standard, upfront operational activities recorded prior to billing closure; total economic cost includes post-billing remediation, second truck rolls, manual engineering overrides, and customer care escalations.
* **Why they conflict here:** Redesign R achieved an immediate EUR 23 savings on upfront unit cost by eliminating 13 percentage points of field surveys (saving a technician visit) and merging front-office validation/scheduling tasks. However, these upfront cuts functioned as a "false economy" in complex segments: every skipped survey that resulted in a failed installation required an aborted first install visit *plus* a second installation visit (up 7pp), often a manual activation by an expensive network specialist (up 8pp), and a post-billing complaint resolution cycle (up 3.7pp). Worse, because cost accounting truncated downstream complaint costs after week 4—and *Billing start $\rightarrow$ Complaint escalation* is a long-tail relation—the true total cost of Pilot R is likely well above EUR 207.

### 5. Strong Improvement for Simple Houses vs. Severe Deterioration for Apartments and Old Buildings
* **Mechanism:** Process standardization works when input variance is low, but fails when hidden technical or regulatory complexity requires specialized stage-gates.
* **Why they conflict here:** Simple, low-risk houses (34% of pilot) have direct drop-wire/duct access and no third-party landlord or historical-preservation constraints. For them, field surveys were genuinely redundant overhead; removing them yielded a pure 6-day median speed gain and a 3pp FTR increase (likely due to faster install before customer circumstances changed). Conversely, medium-risk apartments (29%) involve complex internal risers/MDU splice boxes, and old buildings (18%) require municipal or landlord permits. When the predictive rule skipped surveys in apartments and coordinators bypassed *Permit checks* in old buildings (a frequent move-on-model alignment violation), installers arrived on-site unable to drill or patch fiber, collapsing FTR by 8pp to 11pp and adding 12 to 25 days to p90 duration.

# 5. Value-of-information analysis

To transition from a defensive hybrid containment (Alternative E) to an optimized national model, we must invest in measurements that resolve high-cost uncertainties. Below is the ranking of **M1–M8** from highest to lowest decision value:

### Rank 1: M3 — Add structured reason codes for Failed visit, Manual activation, Complaint escalation, and Second visit
* **Uncertainty Resolved:** Disentangles how much of the rework surge (+6pp failed visits, +8pp manual activation, +3.7pp complaints) is caused by *skipped surveys/permits* versus *network provisioning bugs*, *customer no-shows*, or *coordinator scheduling errors*.
* **How It Changes the Decision:** Low cost, immediate diagnostic power. If >75% of failed visits in apartments stem from indoor cabling issues detectable by a survey, we permanently mandate surveys for MDUs. Conversely, if failures are driven by external splice-box records or customer readiness, re-enabling physical surveys won't fix FTR, and we must fix GIS database accuracy or customer reminders instead.

### Rank 2: M6 — Validate the building-risk predictive rule against field-survey outcomes
* **Uncertainty Resolved:** Quantifies the false-negative rate (predicting "safe to skip" when a physical blocker or permit requirement actually exists) and false-positive rate of the predictive skip algorithm across building types.
* **How It Changes the Decision:** Evaluates whether the predictive rule can be recalibrated (e.g., by raising the confidence threshold or adding building-age/MDU features) or must be scrapped. If recalibration achieves >90% precision on "safe-to-skip" subsets of medium-risk apartments and rural installs, we can expand survey skipping beyond simple houses (unlocking the idle 48% capacity of the survey team without overloading the second-visit team).

### Rank 3: M7 — Run conformance monitoring for Billing start before Activation test and Permit check absence
* **Uncertainty Resolved:** Determines whether premature billing and skipped permits are automated system routing flaws or manual compliance workarounds by overloaded coordinators trying to hit cycle-time targets.
* **How It Changes the Decision:** Directly governs workflow enforcement. By implementing real-time conformance guardrails (hard system blocks preventing *Billing start* until *Activation test = PASS*, and blocking *Appointment scheduling* on old buildings until *Permit check = COMPLETE*), we immediately eliminate the 8% *Billing $\rightarrow$ Complaint* tail and the +25d p90 explosion in old buildings.

### Rank 4: M4 — Measure full downstream cost for 60 days after Billing start
* **Uncertainty Resolved:** Resolves the truncated cost visibility after week 4, capturing the true financial impact of long-tail complaint handling, billing credits, SLA penalties, and early customer churn.
* **How It Changes the Decision:** Determines the true economic breakeven of Redesign R. Right now, estimated total cost is EUR 207 vs. EUR 198 baseline. If 60-day tracking reveals that complex-segment failures push true cost to EUR 225+, it cements the business case against aggressive survey skipping and justifies higher upfront verification spend. If 60-day costs in simple houses remain near EUR 161, it validates rapid national scaling of Track 1 (Simple Houses).

### Rank 5: M5 — Compare coordinator ownership with shared-team ownership under coordinator absence
* **Uncertainty Resolved:** Tests whether "pod-based" ownership (e.g., a primary coordinator backed up by a 4-person shared cell with unified case notes) eliminates the p95 absence/overload stall while preserving the speed and accountability of low handovers.
* **How It Changes the Decision:** Decides the target organizational design for national rollout. If pod-ownership keeps 3+ handovers below 15% while reducing coordinator utilization spikes and cutting p95 duration from 62d back toward <40d, we scale the pod model across all segments. If shared teams reintroduce baseline stagnation, we keep single-coordinator ownership but lower case-load caps (hiring more coordinators).

### Rank 6: M2 — Instrument start timestamps for Installation visit, Activation test, and Manual activation
* **Uncertainty Resolved:** Separates active *service/working time* from *queueing/waiting time* (since the current log only has completion timestamps). Specifically, shows whether the *Installation visit $\rightarrow$ Activation test* delay in apartments is technician on-site struggle or back-office specialist queue wait.
* **How It Changes the Decision:** Guides staffing and automation investment. If activation specialists are the bottleneck (long queue wait before a 10-minute manual activation), we add activation staff or automate provisioning scripts. If technicians are spending 4 hours on-site struggling with apartment wiring, we invest in pre-install surveys and technician training.

### Rank 7: M1 — Randomized pilot of survey skipping by building-risk stratum
* **Uncertainty Resolved:** Provides gold-standard, unconfounded causal estimates of survey skipping on FTR and cycle time, removing the regional demographic bias (higher apartment/old-building share in Pilot R) and pre-week-3 missing attribute noise.
* **How It Changes the Decision:** While methodologically rigorous, M1 ranks below M3 and M6 because **running a randomized skip trial right now on high/medium-risk buildings imposes known, severe harm on customers and second-visit teams (92% utilized)**. M1 should only be executed *after* M6 (offline model validation) and M3 (reason codes) have narrowed the ambiguity to borderline medium-risk strata where the expected value of skipping is plausibly positive.

### Rank 8: M8 — Collect customer experience separately for "speed to appointment" and "working service after installation"
* **Uncertainty Resolved:** Quantifies the exact NPS trade-off between faster initial scheduling and post-install reliability failures (which caused aggregate NPS to slip from +21 to +17).
* **How It Changes the Decision:** Lowest operational decision value because complaint logs and qualitative feedback *already* prove that customers detest "installed but not working" and "billing before activation." While split CSAT/NPS tracking is useful for executive reporting, we do not need a new survey instrument to know we must stop billing customers before their fiber works.

# 6. Final recommendation

Management should execute a **Phased Segmented Deployment (Modified Alternative E)** effective immediately. This decision protects our customer base and field capacity from tail-risk failures while locking in proven speed gains for uncomplicated orders.

### 1. Immediate Actions (Week 1)
* **Establish Two-Track Triage at Order Intake:** Enforce mandatory capture of `Building_Risk` and `Customer_Segment` at *Address validation* (resolving the pre-week-3 data gap).
* **Rebalance Field Workforce:** Temporarily cross-assign underutilized Field Survey technicians (currently at 52% utilization) to assist the overloaded Second-Visit team (at 92% utilization) and clear the backlog of stalled pilot cases.
* **Implement Coordinator Backup Pods:** Transition coordinators in the pilot regions from isolated individual queues (93% utilization) to 4-person "pods" with automatic ticket redistribution if a case sits idle for >24 hours or when a coordinator is absent.

### 2. What to Pause Immediately
* **Halt Post-Billing Activation Everywhere:** Restore a hard IT system gate across all segments: *Billing start* cannot execute until *Activation test* logs a verified `PASS` completion timestamp. Eliminate the heuristic that allows billing based on raw network signal appearance.
* **Suspend Predictive Survey Skipping for Non-Simple Segments:** Turn off the survey-skip rule for **Apartments (medium risk)**, **Old/permit-sensitive buildings**, **Business customers**, and **Rural long-distance installs**.
* **Enforce Mandatory Permit Conformance:** Put a hard workflow block on *Old building, permit-sensitive* orders so *Appointment scheduling* cannot proceed to *Installation visit* without a logged *Permit check* completion.

### 3. What to Scale Now
* **Scale "Fast-Track R" for Simple, Low-Risk Houses:** Across the pilot regions—and expanding to comparable suburban regions—scale the merged *Address validation + Appointment scheduling* step, single-coordinator (pod-backed) ownership, and survey skipping **exclusively for verified simple, low-risk single-family houses** (where Pilot R delivered -6.0d median, -8.0d p90, +3pp FTR, and -1pp complaints).

### 4. What to Measure Next (Weeks 1–6 Action Plan)
Deploy the high-VOI measurement package in the four pilot regions over the next 6 weeks:
1. **Week 1:** Deploy structured failure/exception reason codes (**M3**) and automated conformance blocking/monitoring for premature billing and missing permits (**M7**).
2. **Weeks 2–4:** Conduct offline retro-validation of the building-risk predictive rule against historical survey outcomes (**M6**), instrument start timestamps on field and activation tasks (**M2**), and run the A/B test of individual vs. pod-based coordinator ownership (**M5**).
3. **Week 6:** Complete the 60-day full downstream cost audit (**M4**) for all pilot cohorts to establish audited unit economics. (Hold **M1** randomized skip testing until M6 recalibration is complete).

### 5. Quantitative Thresholds for Broader National Rollout
Management will reconvene at **Week 7** to authorize national rollout across complex segments **only if** the modified process meets all five exit criteria over a minimum 4-week observation window:
1. **Tail Control:** Overall **p90 cycle time $\le$ 29.0 days** and **p95 cycle time $\le$ 42.0 days** (beating the baseline tail of 31d / 45d while preserving a median $\le$ 11.5 days).
2. **Quality & Rework:** Aggregate **First-Time-Right installation $\ge$ 83%** (and $\ge$ 80% within the Apartment segment), with **Second-visit rate $\le$ 11%** and **Manual activation $\le$ 8%**.
3. **Compliance & CX:** Conformance violations for *Billing start before Activation test* at **< 0.5%**, **30-day complaint rate $\le$ 6.0%**, and **NPS $\ge$ +22**.
4. **Resource Sustainability:** Coordinator and Second-visit team utilization stabilized inside the **75%–82% band** (preventing queueing non-linearities).
5. **Unit Economics:** Audited 60-day total cost (including all downstream revisit and complaint costs per **M4**) **$\le$ EUR 190 per order** (at least EUR 8 below the EUR 198 baseline).
