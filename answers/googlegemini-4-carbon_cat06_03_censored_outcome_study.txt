## 1. Target definition

The stakeholder request—“predicting 90-day default from the first few events of every credit application”—conflates an operational intake population (all applications) with a credit-exposure outcome (`default_90d`) that only exists after an application is accepted, signed, and activated. Treating rejected, withdrawn, or open applications as “non-default” introduces severe collider and outcome-definition bias: a high-risk applicant who is properly rejected has zero observed defaults not because they are creditworthy, but because credit exposure was prevented.

### Primary Target Formulation: Multi-State / Competing-Outcome Process Trajectory
To evaluate *every* credit application without fabricating counterfactual credit performance for unactivated cases, we formulate a two-stage / competing-outcome target over the full application cohort, paired with a conditional credit-risk model on activated accounts:

* **Unit of analysis:** Individual credit application (`case:concept:name`), evaluated at a designated early prefix milestone $t_p$.
* **Population:** All credit applications initiated within the study cohort window that remain active (not yet terminated or activated) at prediction time $t_p$.
* **Prediction time ($t_p$):** The timestamp of the first occurrence of `Credit Review` (or elapsed time $\Delta t = 24\text{ hours}$ from `Application Started` for time-based monitoring).
* **Primary Estimand (All Applications):** The joint cumulative incidence function (CIF) over horizon $\tau_{\text{orig}}$ (e.g., 60 days from $t_p$) of reaching one of four mutually exclusive process resolution states under the prevailing policy:
  1. $S_{\text{act}}$: `Account Activated`
  2. $S_{\text{rej}}$: `Rejected`
  3. $S_{\text{wdr}}$: `Withdrawn`
  4. $S_{\text{trf}}$: `Transferred`
  *(Applications still in progress at extraction or horizon $\tau_{\text{orig}}$ are right-censored for process resolution).*
* **Conditional Credit Risk Estimand (Activated Sub-path):** Conditional on reaching `Account Activated` at time $T_{\text{act}}$, the probability of default within 90 days post-activation under the prevailing underwriting and servicing regime:
  $$P(\text{Default}_{90\text{d}} = 1 \mid X_{\le t_p}, \text{State} = S_{\text{act}}, T_{\text{act}})$$
  Combined via the law of total probability, the observable *realized* bad-account rate per incoming application under the active policy is $P(S_{\text{act}} \mid X_{\le t_p}) \times P(\text{Default}_{90\text{d}} = 1 \mid X_{\le t_p}, S_{\text{act}})$.

**Why this formulation is operationally and statistically defensible:**
1. **Well-defined on all 48,000 cases:** Every application legitimately enters the competing-risks origination model without imputing fake non-defaults for rejections or withdrawals.
2. **Separates operational funnel from credit performance:** Operations teams can distinguish “high risk of default *if* activated” from “high likelihood of withdrawal or transfer,” while accounting for censoring on still-open cases and immature activated accounts.
3. **Honest Conditioning:** It explicitly reports factual risk under the *observed* policy rather than hiding sample selection inside an undocumented data filter.

### Credible Alternative Formulation: Policy-Specific Counterfactual Default Risk (“What if we activated everyone?”)
* **Target & Estimand:** The potential outcome $P(Y^{a=\text{activate}}_{90\text{d}} = 1 \mid X_{\le t_p})$ for *all* incoming applications, representing the 90-day default probability if an applicant were hypothetically approved, signed, and activated.
* **Why it answers a different question:** While the primary formulation predicts what will happen under the *existing* screening regime (useful for portfolio loss forecasting, collections staffing, and early escalation), credit-policy threshold design asks what would happen if underwriting rules were relaxed to approve currently rejected applicants ("Reject Inference").
* **Why it is secondary:** Identifying $Y^{a=\text{activate}}_{90\text{d}}$ requires **positivity** ($P(\text{Activate} \mid X) > 0$ for all covariates $X$) and **conditional exchangeability** (no unmeasured confounders influencing Credit Review decisions and default). Deterministic policy cutoffs violate positivity, and unrecorded underwriter judgment violates exchangeability. We explore this only via Inverse Probability of Selection Weighting (IPSW) sensitivity bounds rather than claiming point identification as our primary target.

## 2. Cohort construction

### Explicit Inclusion, Exclusion, and As-Of Rules
Let $t_{\text{cut}}$ denote the evaluation or training extraction timestamp (for the full snapshot, $t_{\text{cut}} = \text{2026-02-15}$).
1. **Cohort Entry (Inclusion):** All applications with `Application Started` between `2024-01-01 00:00:00` and `2025-12-31 23:59:59` ($N = 48,000$).
2. **Prefix Eligibility Rule:** For a prediction point $t_p$ (e.g., $k$-th event or first `Credit Review`), only cases that have reached $t_p$ without already having terminated (`Rejected`, `Withdrawn`, `Transferred`, or `Account Activated` prior to $t_p$) are at risk in the active prediction set.
3. **As-Of Observation Rule:** At any historical backtest cutoff $t_{\text{cut}}$, an outcome is treated as *known* only if `label_available_at` $\le t_{\text{cut}}$ (for credit outcomes) or the terminal origination event timestamp $\le t_{\text{cut}}$ (for process outcomes). Even if `default_90d` is populated in the static CSV, if `label_available_at` $> t_{\text{cut}}$, the case must be treated as immature/censored as of $t_{\text{cut}}$ to prevent look-ahead bias.
4. **Maturity Buffer Rule (Stage 2 Credit Model):** Since the 90-day window plus the 7–35 day external feed delay spans up to **125 days** post-activation, accounts activated after `2025-10-13` ($t_{\text{cut}} - 125\text{ days}$) cannot be assumed fully mature unless explicitly confirmed, and unconfirmed accounts within this window are administratively censored/immature rather than dropped blindly or assumed non-default.

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

### Taxonomy of Incomplete Outcomes
1. **Ordinary Administrative Right Censoring (5,100 still-open applications; newly activated accounts $< 90\text{d}$ old):** The process is ongoing at $t_{\text{cut}} = \text{2026-02-15}$. Conditional on application arrival date and observed prefix tempo, censoring time is administratively determined by $t_{\text{cut}}$.
2. **Delayed Reporting Labels (Subset of the 3,600 activated accounts with $90 < t_{\text{cut}} - T_{\text{act}} \le 125\text{ days}$):** The 90-day exposure window has elapsed, but the external bureau/servicing feed takes 7–35 days to arrive. Naively including activations up to $t_{\text{cut}} - 90\text{d}$ causes severe bias if default notifications arrive faster (via internal `collection_status`) or slower than non-default clearances.
3. **Selection Through Activation (9,100 Rejected + 5,700 Withdrawn):** Outcomes are *not* missing at random; credit exposure never occurred. Underwriter rejection depends on observed risk (`risk_score`, `case:requested_amount`) and unobserved underwriter scrutiny; applicant withdrawal depends on pricing/terms and processing delays (e.g., repeated document loops).
4. **Transfer as Competing Event / Partial Linkage (2,900 Transferred):** Specialist/partner transfers represent risk/product routing (informative selection). Only ~60% (~1,740 cases) exist in a linkage table under a new ID, and partner platforms operate under different underwriting/servicing rules. Merging the 60% linked transfers directly into standard activations introduces survivorship/linkage bias and regime mixing.
5. **Permanently Missing Outcome Feeds (Subset of 3,600 activated accounts, concentrated in `case:channel == partner` with $t_{\text{cut}} - T_{\text{act}} > 125\text{ days}$):** The account was activated long enough ago that the 90d + 35d window has passed, yet no label ever arrived. Dropping these cases creates selection bias if partner channels have systematically higher or lower default rates.

### Primary Analysis Strategy
* **Stage 1 (Origination Funnel):** Fit a **Cause-Specific Cox / Fine-Gray Competing Risks model** (or discrete-time multi-state hazard model) on all 48,000 applications from $t_p$ to resolution (`Activated`, `Rejected`, `Withdrawn`, `Transferred`), treating the 5,100 still-open cases as right-censored at `data_extract_time`. Keep `Transferred` as a distinct terminal competing state on the core platform rather than selectively stitching the 60% linked subset.
* **Stage 2 (Conditional 90d Default on Activated Accounts):**
  * Restrict maturity eligibility to accounts activated at least **125 days** prior to $t_{\text{cut}}$ (i.e., $T_{\text{act}} \le \text{2025-10-13}$ for final evaluation), eliminating the 7–35 day reporting-delay bias cleanly, OR use Inverse Probability of Censoring Weighting (IPCW) where censoring includes administrative immaturity.
  * For mature activated accounts ($T_{\text{act}} \le t_{\text{cut}} - 125\text{d}$) that still lack an outcome due to **permanently missing partner feeds**, estimate **Inverse Probability of Observation Weights (IPOW)**: model $P(\text{Feed Observed} = 1 \mid T_{\text{act}} \le t_{\text{cut}} - 125\text{d}, \text{Channel, Region, Source System, Prefix Features})$ and reweight observed mature accounts by $1 / \hat{P}(\text{Feed Observed})$.

### Sensitivity Analyses
1. **Manski Worst/Best-Case Bounds on Missing Partner Feeds:** Re-evaluate model calibration and subgroup bad rates among mature activated accounts ($T_{\text{act}} \le t_{\text{cut}} - 125\text{d}$) under extreme bounds where permanently missing feeds have (a) $0\%$ default, (b) $2\times$ the observed partner default rate, and (c) $100\%$ default.
2. **IPSW Counterfactual / Reject-Inference Sensitivity:** Estimate propensity of activation $\hat{e}(X) = \hat{P}(S_{\text{act}} \mid X_{\le t_p})$ from Stage 1, trim cases violating positivity ($\hat{e}(X) < 0.05$), and fit an IPSW-weighted default model to gauge how much estimated prefix feature effects shift when attempting to project onto the full intake population.
3. **Linked-Transfer Sensitivity Check:** On the 60% linked transfer subset, audit downstream activation and default rates separately as an out-of-model diagnostic to verify whether `Transferred` cases represent higher-risk edge cases offloaded from the main book.

### Testability of Assumptions
* **Testable from data:** Positivity overlap in observed features $X_{\le t_p}$ between `Activated`, `Rejected`, `Withdrawn`, and `Transferred` cohorts; whether missing outcome feeds after 125 days depend on `case:channel`, `source_system`, or `risk_score`; empirical distribution of `label_available_at` minus $T_{\text{act}}$ to verify the 125-day maturity threshold.
* **Partially testable:** Policy-shift stability of selection probabilities before vs. after `2025-07-01` (we can test shifts in $P(\text{Activate} \mid X)$ and observed $P(\text{Default} \mid X, \text{Activate})$, and exploit discontinuity around the policy change to check selection sensitivity).
* **Fundamentally untestable:** Missing at Random (MAR) conditional on observables for permanently missing partner feeds (e.g., if a partner’s IT feed specifically drops accounts entering bankruptcy); Conditional Exchangeability (no unobserved confounders) for counterfactual default of `Rejected` and `Withdrawn` applicants; whether the 40% unlinked transfers behave identically to the 60% linked transfers.

## 4. Prefix extraction

### Pre-Processing: Activity Canonicalization & Deduplication (Handling 15 Oct 2025 Migration)
Before cutting any prefixes, event logs must be harmonized without leaking future knowledge:
1. **Deterministic Activity Mapping:** Build a static dictionary mapping post-`2025-10-15` migrated activity names back to canonical pre-migration names (e.g., `KYC_Verification_v2` $\rightarrow$ `Identity/KYC Check`, `Doc_Req_Sent` $\rightarrow$ `Request Documents`, `Underwriting_Review` $\rightarrow$ `Credit Review`).
2. **Causal Deduplication:** The `2025-10-15` migration introduced occasional duplicate events. Deduplicate consecutive or identical `(case:concept:name, canonical_activity, time:timestamp)` records (or identical canonical activity within a $\le 5\text{-second}$ system-echo tolerance where `source_system` double-logged) **using only backward-looking rules** ($t_i - t_{i-1} \le \epsilon$). If tie-breaking timestamps, sort deterministically by `(time:timestamp, canonical_event_order_priority)`.

### Prefix Extraction Schemes (Preventing Future Look-Ahead)
We support two operational prefix definitions, strictly truncating all events with `time:timestamp` $> t_p$ and excluding any case whose terminal origination event (`Accepted`, `Rejected`, `Withdrawn`, `Transferred`, `Account Activated`) occurred *at or before* $t_p$:

1. **Fixed Event-Count Prefixes ($k$-event prefixes, e.g., $k \in \{2, 3, 4, 5\}$):**
   * Extract the first $k$ deduplicated events per case using `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(df, length=k)` (after filtering to cases with length $\ge k$).
   * Prediction timestamp $t_p$ is defined as the timestamp of the $k$-th event: $t_p = t_{(k)}$.
   * **Handling Short Cases:** Suppose a case is swiftly `Rejected` at event 3 (`App Started` $\rightarrow$ `KYC` $\rightarrow$ `Rejected`). For $k=3$, the outcome is already realized at event 3, so it is **excluded from the $k \ge 3$ active prediction risk set** (predicting an already-decided case inflates metrics artificially). Conversely, if a case is still open at $t_{\text{cut}}$ with only 2 events (`App Started` $\rightarrow$ `KYC`), it enters $k=2$ (with a right-censored outcome) and is simply not yet eligible to enter $k \ge 3$.
   * **Handling Repeated Document Loops:** In fixed-$k$ prefixes, a case cycling `Credit Review` $\rightarrow$ `Request Documents` $\rightarrow$ `Credit Review` consumes events $k=3, 4, 5$ inside rework loops, whereas a clean case may already reach `Accepted` at event 4. Therefore, we do *not* pool prefixes $k=1\dots K$ naively without conditioning on $k$ and loop count, or better yet, evaluate milestone-anchored prefixes (e.g., first arrival at `Credit Review` vs. second arrival at `Credit Review`).

2. **Elapsed-Time Horizon Prefixes ($\Delta t$ from `Application Started`, e.g., $\Delta t \in \{6\text{h}, 24\text{h}, 72\text{h}\}$):**
   * Set $t_p = t_{\text{start}} + \Delta t$ (provided $t_p \le t_{\text{cut}}$).
   * Slice all events for the case satisfying $t_e \le t_p$.
   * **Handling Short Cases & Loops:** Cases that already terminated prior to $t_{\text{start}} + \Delta t$ have resolved and leave the active prediction cohort at $\Delta t$. Cases stalled waiting for customer documents naturally retain their exact event history as of $t_p$, making elapsed-time prefixes cleaner for daily portfolio batch scoring because calendar exposure is standardized across looped and unlooped cases.

## 5. Process-aware features

All features are computed strictly as of prediction timestamp $t_p$ for case $i$.

### A. Static Case Attributes (Available at `Application Started`, $t_1 \le t_p$)
* `case:channel` (Direct, Broker, Partner — critical both for risk and missing-feed propensity).
* `case:region` (Geographic macro-region).
* `case:requested_amount` (Log-transformed requested credit facility and ratio to regional historical median).

### B. Prefix-Derived Intra-Case Features (Computed strictly from events $e_1, \dots, e_m$ with $t_{e_m} = t_p$)
* **Control-Flow & Milestone Sequence:**
  * Current activity `concept:name` at $t_p$ and immediate bigrams (e.g., `Identity/KYC Check -> Credit Review`).
  * Count of distinct canonical activities visited so far; prefix event length $m$ (especially informative in elapsed-time prefixes).
* **Rework & Friction Features:**
  * `doc_loop_count`: Number of occurrences of `Request Documents` (or cycles returning to `Credit Review`) in $[t_1, t_p]$.
  * `resource_handoff_count`: Number of distinct `org:resource` actors handling the prefix up to $t_p$, and boolean indicator of whether the same underwriter retained the case across loops.
* **Timing & Tempo Features:**
  * `elapsed_since_start`: $t_p - t_1$ (in hours).
  * `kyc_latency`: Transition duration $t_{\text{KYC}} - t_{\text{Start}}$ (automated instant pass vs. manual KYC hold).
  * `inter_event_max` and `inter_event_acceleration`: Maximum gap between consecutive prefix events and ratio of most recent step duration to prior step duration (captures applicant hesitation during document upload).
* **Dynamic Risk Trajectory:**
  * `risk_score_initial` (at earliest populated prefix event), `risk_score_latest` (last non-null value at or before $t_p$), and `risk_score_delta` (`latest - initial`, capturing score degradation upon KYC or document verification).
* **Cohort & Calendar Context:**
  * Day-of-week, hour-of-day (out-of-hours applications often exhibit elevated fraud/adverse selection), and explicit regime indicators (though raw post-regime flags cannot extrapolate to unseen future regimes, calendar seasonality and weekend submission indicators remain valid).

### C. Cross-Case & Historical Reference Features (Computed from global log state strictly $\le t_p$)
* **Work-In-Progress (WIP) / Queue Congestion:** Number of active (unterminated) applications currently sitting in `Identity/KYC Check` or `Credit Review` across the platform (and within the same `case:region`) at exact timestamp $t_p$. High queue congestion delays review and increases applicant withdrawal.
* **Causal Dynamic Resource Experience:** Reconstructed historical volume of prior events/cases processed by `org:resource` strictly within $[t_{\text{log\_start}}, t_p)$ (replacing the leaked static extraction-time experience).
* **Historical Variant Rarity:** Out-of-fold frequency of the observed canonical prefix trace within a trailing 30-day rolling window ending at $t_p$ (flags anomalous/unusual process paths).

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`final_limit`** | **Direct Target / Decision Leakage:** Populated only *after* final credit decision; null or zero for rejections, positive for approvals. | Only valid *after* `Accepted` timestamp; unavailable during early `Credit Review` prefixes. | **Drop** | Directly encodes approval vs. rejection outcome and underwriter’s final risk sizing after $t_p$. |
| **`decision_reason`** | **Direct Decision Leakage:** Populated at or after `Accept`/`Reject` (e.g., "Low Score", "Policy Pass"). | Only valid post-decision; never available at early warning $t_p$. | **Drop** | Deterministic proxy for Stage 1 outcome and strong selection collider for Stage 2. |
| **`collection_status`** | **Direct Target Leakage:** Updated post-activation during servicing; directly reveals delinquency and `default_90d`. | Never valid at origination prediction time $t_p$. | **Drop** | Future post-activation state; leaks the 90-day default outcome directly into features. |
| **Future events (`Contract Signed`, `Account Activated`, `Collections Contact`, `Default Recorded`)** | **Look-Ahead / Post-Prefix Leakage:** Present in raw CSV; using them in DFG/variant encoding or duration features leaks resolution and default. | Truncate event log strictly to `time:timestamp <= t_p` for features; use post-$t_p$ origination events *only* to construct targets/censoring as of $t_{\text{cut}}$. | **Repair** (Strict temporal split into prefix vs. label) | Required to define Stage 1 targets (`Account Activated` vs. `Rejected`), but must be stripped before feature extraction. |
| **`documents_total` (nightly snapshot)** | **Same-Day Intraday Look-Ahead:** Snapshot at 23:59 includes documents uploaded *after* $t_p$ on the same calendar day (e.g., requested after $t_p$ during Credit Review). | Only valid using the *previous* calendar day's snapshot ($\text{date}(t_p) - 1\text{ day}$), or reconstructed purely from timestamped prefix events $\le t_p$. | **Repair** (Lag by 1 day or rebuild from events) | Same-day document jumps often reflect post-$t_p$ underwriter requests or adverse risk escalations. |
| **Updated `risk_score`** | **Temporal Overwrite / Look-Ahead Risk:** Updated across multiple events; taking case-level `max`, `mean`, or final row leaks post-$t_p$ score recalculations. | Extract `risk_score` strictly from rows where `time:timestamp <= t_p` (forward-filling only within $[t_1, t_p]$). | **Repair** | Safe and highly predictive when restricted to values recorded on events $\le t_p$. |
| **Resource experience (at extraction time)** | **Survivorship & Future-Volume Leakage:** Reflects employee tenure/volume as of `2026-02-15`. Junior staff in 2024 who stayed through 2026 look "experienced" vs. those who quit or were fired. | Recompute cumulative event/case count handled by `org:resource` strictly in $(-\infty, t_p)$ from the event log. | **Repair** (Recompute causally; drop static field) | Static extraction-time attributes violate point-in-time fidelity and induce historical look-ahead bias. |
| **`default_90d` & `label_available_at`** | **Target & As-Of Maturity Leakage:** `default_90d` is repeated on early prefix rows in the CSV! Also, using labels in backtests where `label_available_at > t_cut` leaks future bureau feeds. | Strip `default_90d` and `label_available_at` from all prefix rows immediately; in any split with cutoff $t_{\text{cut}}$, mask `default_90d` to censored/immature unless `label_available_at <= t_cut`. | **Repair** (Isolate to target table + enforce `label_available_at <= t_cut`) | Leaving `default_90d` on prefix rows causes 100% target leakage; ignoring `label_available_at` creates optimistic backtest calibration. |
| **`source_system` & raw `concept:name`** | **Regime / Migration Artifact Bias:** After `2025-10-15`, renamed activities and migrated system tags correlate spuriously with cohort maturity and post-July policy rates. | Canonicalize activity names prior to prefix extraction; monitor `source_system` for drift rather than letting tree splits memorize migration dates. | **Repair** | Prevents models from overfitting to IT migration artifacts and misinterpreting immaturity near late 2025. |

## 7. Validation design

### Why a Random Case Split Is Misleading Here
A random train/test split (such as a naive call to `pm4py.split_train_test`) fails on four counts:
1. **Concurrent Queue & Macro Leakage:** Cases running in parallel on the same day share macro shocks, underwriting backlog, and `org:resource` states; training on a case from Nov 2025 helps predict a case from Oct 2025.
2. **Policy Regime Blending:** Mixing pre- and post-`2025-07-01` cases masks the credit-policy shift, yielding an average score that reflects neither the historical regime nor the current deployed policy.
3. **Migration Artifact Memorization:** Mixing post-`2025-10-15` cases into training allows models to memorize migration quirks rather than testing whether canonicalization generalizes across system changes.
4. **Right-Censoring Distortion:** Random splits scatter late-2025 immature cases across train and test instead of mimicking real deployment, where training uses only matured historical labels as of $t_{\text{train\_cut}}$ and predicts forward on newer cohorts.

### Temporal Backtesting & Rolling As-Of Split Architecture
We implement a **strict point-in-time rolling temporal validation** anchored by `Application Started` ($T_{\text{start}}$) and simulated extraction cutoffs ($t_{\text{cut}}$), respecting the 125-day activation-to-feed maturity lag:

1. **Regime-Shock Backtest Split (Testing Policy Shift Resilience):**
   * **Train Cohort:** Applications started `2024-01-01` to `2025-02-15`, evaluated strictly *as of* simulated cutoff $t_{\text{cut,1}} = \text{2025-06-30}$ (day before the policy change). Any case whose `label_available_at` $> \text{2025-06-30}$ is treated as censored/unobserved during training!
   * **Validation Cohort (Post-Policy Shift):** Applications started `2025-07-01` to `2025-09-15`, evaluated using outcomes matured as of `2026-02-15`. This directly quantifies performance degradation, selection-probability shift, and miscalibration caused by the `2025-07-01` credit-policy change, informing sample decay weights or post-July recalibration.
2. **Final Pre-Production Holdout Split (Testing Post-Migration & Current Policy):**
   * **Retrain / Recalibration Window:** Applications started `2024-01-01` to `2025-09-14` (applying higher importance weights or hierarchical regime indicators to post-`2025-07-01` cases, strictly enforcing `label_available_at <= 2026-02-15` and IPOW for missing feeds).
   * **Out-of-Time Test Cohort:**
     * *Stage 2 (Mature 90d Default Evaluation):* Applications started `2025-09-15` to `2025-10-10` whose activations occurred $\le \text{2025-10-13}$ (ensuring $\ge 125\text{ days}$ of full feed maturity by `2026-02-15`), plus IPCW-adjusted survival evaluation up to `2025-11-15`.
     * *Stage 1 (Origination Competing Risks & Migration Audit):* Applications started `2025-10-15` to `2025-12-15` (evaluating 60-day origination funnel resolution and verifying feature distribution stability across the `2025-10-15` system migration).

### Evaluation Metrics (Censoring- and Selection-Aware)
* **Stage 1 (Origination Competing Risks on All Eligible Applications):**
  * Cause-specific **Time-Dependent Concordance Index ($C(t)$)** and **Integrated Brier Score (IBS)** weighted via IPCW at $\tau = 30, 60\text{ days}$ for each state (`Activated`, `Rejected`, `Withdrawn`, `Transferred`).
* **Stage 2 (Conditional 90d Default on Activated Accounts):**
  * **IPCW / IPOW-Weighted ROC-AUC and PR-AUC** (essential given the 12.5% base default rate: $2,700 / 21,600$).
  * **Calibration:** Expected Calibration Error (ECE), reliability curves, and **Observed-to-Expected (O/E) Default Ratio** weighted for missing partner feeds—critical because portfolio loss provisions depend on calibrated probabilities, not just rank ordering.
* **Subgroup & Drift Diagnostics:**
  * Stratified PR-AUC and O/E calibration across `case:channel` (specifically auditing `partner` vs. `direct`), `case:region`, prefix loop depth (`doc_loop_count == 0` vs. $\ge 1$), and pre- vs. post-`2025-10-15` migration cohorts (Population Stability Index, PSI, on canonicalized features).

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

### What This Study Can and Cannot Claim
* **Valid Claims (Predictive Association Under Active Policy):** Given an application’s early prefix up to $t_p$, the models validly estimate (1) the probability of reaching activation vs. rejection/withdrawal/transfer, and (2) the expected 90-day default probability *conditional on the application passing subsequent underwriting and activating under the prevailing policy*. This supports early fraud/high-risk queue prioritization, senior underwriter routing, and expected loss forecasting on the active pipeline.
* **Invalid Claims (Counterfactual Causal Risk & Unconditional Intake Risk):** A low predicted conditional default score on an application that is typically rejected **cannot** be interpreted as proof that approving the applicant is safe. Because underwriters reject applicants using information acquired *after* $t_p$ (e.g., bank statements, manual credit bureau review, income verification), observed activated accounts with high-risk prefixes are "positive outliers" that passed manual scrutiny (selection/collider bias). This study does not identify causal effects of underwriting interventions without randomized policy holdouts.

### How Deployment Could Change the Data-Generating Process (Performative Feedback Loops)
1. **Selective Early Rejection / Performative Censoring:** If underwriters use high early-warning risk scores to fast-track rejections or demand burdensome extra documentation at `Credit Review`, high-score applicants will be rejected or withdraw at much higher rates. Future activated cohorts will exhibit *fewer* observed defaults among high-score prefixes, making the model appear over-pessimistic ("miscalibrated") in subsequent retraining if naive retraining ignores the changed intervention policy.
2. **Queue Reprioritization Altering Withdrawal Dynamics:** Routing low-risk applications to an "express lane" while routing high-risk applications to a slow specialist review queue alters cycle times. Good borrowers stuck in a borderline queue may withdraw to take competing loan offers (adverse selection), shifting both `Withdrawn` incidence and conditional default rates.
3. **Channel & Applicant Gaming (Goodhart’s Law):** If brokers or partner channels learn that multiple `Request Documents` loops or out-of-hours submissions penalize early warning scores, they may hold applications until all documents are bundled offline and submit during business hours—destroying the predictive signal of prefix tempo/rework features without changing underlying borrower creditworthiness.
4. **Partner Routing Shifts:** Using the score to divert borderline cases via `Transferred` to specialist platforms changes the selection boundary and increases unlinked follow-up loss.

    df = pd.read_csv(csv_path)

def build_as_of_case_outcomes(df: pd.DataFrame, as_of_cutoff: pd.Timestamp) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Constructs Stage 1 competing outcomes and Stage 2 maturity-aware targets as of cutoff."""
    df_obs = df[df["time:timestamp"] <= as_of_cutoff].copy()
    case_starts = df_obs.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min().rename("start_time")
    case_last = df_obs.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].max().rename("last_obs_time")
    channel = df_obs.groupby("case:concept:name")["case:channel"].first()

    # Identify earliest origination resolution event per case as of cutoff
    res_events = {"Account Activated", "Rejected", "Withdrawn", "Transferred"}
    res_df = (
        df_obs[df_obs["concept:name"].isin(res_events)]
        .sort_values("time:timestamp")
        .groupby("case:concept:name")
        .first()[["concept:name", "time:timestamp"]]
        .rename(columns={"concept:name": "orig_state", "time:timestamp": "orig_event_time"})
    )

    # Extract target & feed availability strictly respecting label_available_at <= as_of_cutoff
    lbl_df = (
        df_obs.dropna(subset=["default_90d"])
        .groupby("case:concept:name")[["default_90d", "label_available_at"]]
        .first()
    )
    cases = pd.concat([case_starts, case_last, channel, res_df, lbl_df], axis=1)
    cases["orig_state"] = cases["orig_state"].fillna("Censored_Open")
    cases["stage1_time_days"] = (
        cases["orig_event_time"].fillna(as_of_cutoff) - cases["start_time"]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 86400.0

    # Stage 2 classification: separate mature labels, delayed/immature, missing feeds, and unactivated
    label_known_as_of = cases["default_90d"].notna() & (
        cases["label_available_at"].isna() | (cases["label_available_at"] <= as_of_cutoff)
    )
    days_since_act = (as_of_cutoff - cases["orig_event_time"]).dt.total_seconds() / 86400.0

    cases["cohort_status"] = np.select(
        [
            (cases["orig_state"] == "Account Activated") & label_known_as_of,
            (cases["orig_state"] == "Account Activated") & (~label_known_as_of) & (days_since_act <= MATURITY_BUFFER_DAYS),
            (cases["orig_state"] == "Account Activated") & (~label_known_as_of) & (days_since_act > MATURITY_BUFFER_DAYS),
            cases["orig_state"].isin(["Rejected", "Withdrawn", "Transferred"]),
        ],
        [
            "Activated_Labeled",
            "Activated_Immature_Or_Delayed",
            "Activated_Permanently_Missing_Feed",  # Requires IPOW reweighting in Stage 2
            "Unactivated_Competing_" + cases["orig_state"].astype(str),
        ],
        default="Still_Open_Right_Censored",
    )
    cases["y_default_90d_as_of"] = np.where(label_known_as_of, cases["default_90d"], np.nan)
    return cases.reset_index()


def build_causal_prefix_features(df_clean: pd.DataFrame, k: int, as_of_cutoff: pd.Timestamp) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Extracts fixed-length prefixes without post-prefix or extraction-time leakage."""
    df_as_of = df_clean[df_clean["time:timestamp"] <= as_of_cutoff].copy()

    # Causal resource experience: cumulative prior events handled by resource strictly before current event
    df_as_of["causal_res_exp"] = df_as_of.groupby("org:resource").cumcount()

    # Strip forbidden outcome/post-decision columns BEFORE prefix slicing
    safe_cols = [c for c in df_as_of.columns if c not in FORBIDDEN_FEATURE_COLS]
    df_safe = df_as_of[safe_cols].copy()

    # Keep cases that have at least k events and extract exact k-event prefixes
    event_counts = df_safe.groupby("case:concept:name").size()
    eligible_ids = event_counts[event_counts >= k].index
    prefix_log = pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(df_safe[df_safe["case:concept:name"].isin(eligible_ids)], length=k)

    # Exclude cases already decided/terminated at or before prefix k (not at risk for early warning)
    decided_in_prefix = prefix_log[prefix_log["concept:name"].isin(TERMINAL_OR_POST_DECISION)]["case:concept:name"].unique()
    prefix_log = prefix_log[~prefix_log["case:concept:name"].isin(decided_in_prefix)].copy()

    # Engineer prefix-level control-flow, rework, timing, and risk trajectory features
    grp = prefix_log.groupby("case:concept:name")
    feats = pd.DataFrame({
        "t_p": grp["time:timestamp"].max(),
        "elapsed_hours": (grp["time:timestamp"].max() - grp["time:timestamp"].min()).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0,
        "doc_loop_count": prefix_log["concept:name"].eq("Request Documents").groupby(prefix_log["case:concept:name"]).sum(),
        "distinct_resources": grp["org:resource"].nunique(),
        "latest_causal_res_exp": grp["causal_res_exp"].last(),
        "risk_score_first": grp["risk_score"].first(),
        "risk_score_last": grp["risk_score"].last(),
        "case:channel": grp["case:channel"].first(),
        "case:region": grp["case:region"].first(),
        "case:requested_amount": grp["case:requested_amount"].first(),
    })
    feats["risk_score_delta"] = feats["risk_score_last"] - feats["risk_score_first"]
    return feats.reset_index(), prefix_log
