## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

### Upstream data quality and artifact readiness

- **Registration and classification:** A1 can provide cleaner, earlier inflow but does not improve classification, KYC, documents, or decision quality. A2 can reduce wrong control-path routing, but High-exposure classifications need human confirmation.
- **Document and credit lane:**  
  `Complete source documents (A5)  reliable extraction/normalization (A6)  credit analysis  credit-analysis notes`
  
  A5 primarily reduces avoidable loops; it should not be credited with eliminating customer-response time. A6 must retain source references and route low-quality PDFs to analysts.
- **KYC lane:** A3 and A4 can assist KYC operations, but neither supplies autonomous compliance clearance. A4 addresses only the entity-mismatch subset and does not certify all KYC data.
- **Committee convergence:** Committee packaging waits for **KYC status, normalized statements, credit-analysis notes, and collateral valuation when required**. Thus:
  - A4 and A6 improve only two inputs.
  - A7 can improve the collateral input but cannot create acceptable credit or KYC artifacts.
  - A8 must hold incomplete packs rather than auto-fill or infer missing content.
- **Downstream control chain:**  
  `Verified artifacts  Committee decision  A9 contract generation  A10 signing  A11 activation`
  
  A9 needs an authoritative, structured Committee decision, not merely a PDF pack. A10 needs both a clean contract and authoritative KYC clearance. A11 is last because it needs the entire control chain to be complete and machine-verifiable.
- A2 is not a hidden fifth prerequisite for the recommended portfolio: A5 and valuation triage can initially use validated deterministic rules and human confirmation. However, unreliable product/exposure data is a rollout gate, not something downstream modules should inherit silently.
- KYC and financial document review are often **concurrent despite sequential system records**. Automation and measurement should preserve those parallel lanes rather than serialize them or add their waiting times together. The 380 `Resolve entity mismatch  Contract preparation` transitions also require conformance investigation; they do not imply that KYC clearance before signing can be waived.

### Legal and compliance controls

- A3/A4 may recommend, retrieve evidence, and route cases, but compliance remains accountable for KYC disposition.
- A10 must fail closed for signing when KYC status is unknown or unresolved. A9 can prevent contract release on incomplete control data, but off-system provisional signing still requires monitoring.
- A9’s legal rule library needs named legal ownership, effective dating, testing, and rollback. Credit-line renewal requires particular attention because its contract-remediation rate is 14%.
- A8 must not weaken the High-exposure four-eyes requirement. A bot or service account cannot count as the second pair of eyes.
- A12 can route and evidence remediation, but ambiguous policy must be escalated to a policy owner rather than encoded as an arbitrary rule.

### Human escalation capacity

- The three compliance specialists, especially the two senior specialists on whom KYC operations depend, are a capacity constraint. A4 helps only if evidence preparation and triage reduce senior touch time; an assistant that generates more referrals can worsen the 31-hour mismatch wait.
- A3 needs explicit false-positive and escalation-capacity testing before scale.
- A5 and A6 should be scaled together. A5 increases clean inflow, while A6 can release analyst capacity; either alone can produce a misleading local improvement.
- Back-office savings do not automatically become bank-wide capacity: utilization already falls below 65% after 15:00. Released staff may be better redeployed to customer assistance and exception prevention.
- A9 shifts legal work from drafting toward rule maintenance and exceptions. The four legal-ops FTE can be underutilized during upstream KYC delays but overloaded at month end, so average utilization is not a sufficient capacity gate.

### External valuation dependency

A7 can improve requirement identification, internal handoffs, valuer selection, and parallel completion of independent artifacts. It cannot directly automate the external valuation wait. This matters especially for Equipment finance, where valuation is required in 71% of cases, versus 18% for Working-capital. Valuer independence, quality review, capacity, and dispute handling remain necessary. Even a successful A7 may expose Committee packaging or weekly Committee cadence as the next constraint.

### Core-banking integration constraint

A four-module portfolio is not permission for four deep integrations.

- For the recommended rollout, reserve the first-six-month deep-integration slots for **A4’s authoritative KYC/entity workflow** and **A9’s authoritative KYC/decision/contract-control integration**.
- Pilot A5 and A6 through existing portal, document, and workbench interfaces without core writeback. If a proposed scope requires another deep integration, defer that scope rather than bypass control reconciliation.
- The quick-win alternative would likely consume its slots on A1 and A10. The compliance-first alternative would need A4/A9 sequenced before deep A10 enforcement.
- A8 and A11 should not be treated as integration-light merely because they assemble documents or perform the final step; their control and data dependencies are substantial.

## 4. Expected process-level effects

Activity-level median waits are diagnostic, not additive. Cases follow different variants, customer and external waits behave differently, and KYC/document-review concurrency is understated by the system log.

### Quick-win portfolio: A1 + A2 + A5 + A10

- **Cycle time:** A1 improves a stable step—Register application is in position 2 for 98% of cases—but its eight-minute service time and 1.5-hour median wait limit end-to-end leverage. A5 and A10 offer larger potential segment-level improvements through fewer document loops and faster signing for adopters. High-exposure, mismatch, and collateral-heavy cases may see little end-to-end change.
- **Waiting time:** Targets registration waiting, internal document-request handling, reminder delay, and part of the 44-hour signing wait. The 31-hour mismatch wait, 20-hour normalization wait, 28-hour credit-analysis wait, and 58-hour external valuation wait remain. KYC-gated signing may legitimately increase waiting for cases that previously used provisional signing.
- **Rework:** Can reduce duplicate registration, classification misrouting, and some of the `Financial document review  Request missing documents  Financial document review` loops. It does not directly address the 38% mismatch exception rate, normalization errors, or the 650/510 contract-remediation transitions.
- **Handovers:** Fewer clerk registration touches, RM document chases, and signing reminders are plausible. New duplicate-review, classification-override, portal-assistance, and identity/consent handovers appear.
- **Resource demand:** The visible 12-FTE back-office queue may shrink, but afternoon slack means savings may simply increase idle capacity. Without A6, more complete inflow can overload the nine analysts. Legal ops, signing support, and activation can also receive earlier or burstier demand.
- **Control violations:** A KYC-gated A10 can reduce the 6.5% provisional-signing pattern, provided the KYC status itself is authoritative. A2 still needs High-exposure human review. Four-eyes violations and contract/decision mismatches remain largely unaddressed.

### Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4 + A5 + A6 + A9

- **Cycle time:** Best suited to mismatch-affected and document-heavy variants. A4 addresses a low-frequency activity that is nevertheless on the critical path in 82% of cases when present. A5/A6 reduce document and analyst friction, while A9 improves the downstream contract segment. Equipment-finance cases may still be dominated by valuation and Committee timing.
- **Waiting time:** A4 can reduce mismatch waiting only if it increases effective specialist capacity. A5 can reduce repeated internal loops, while customer-response delay remains partly behavioral. A6 can reduce normalization queueing if quality assurance does not consume the released analyst time. A9 may reduce contract waiting only if month-end legal exceptions are staffed.
- **Rework:** Targets the 3,300 outgoing and 2,800 returning missing-document transitions, repeated-document requests, normalization exceptions, mismatch-to-KYC returns, and contract remediation. A9 has particular relevance to overall contract remediation of 8.5% and the higher Credit-line renewal rate.
- **Handovers:** Evidence-based mismatch resolution can structure KYC-operations-to-specialist handoffs. A5/A6 reduce RM/back-office/analyst document ping-pong, and A9 reduces analyst/RM/legal reconciliation. New low-confidence-PDF, entity-review, and legal-rule exception queues must be explicitly owned.
- **Resource demand:** A5’s increased clean inflow is absorbed only if A6 releases sufficient analyst capacity; scaling must therefore be paired. A4 may initially increase senior-specialist training and review work. Back-office staff can move toward customer assistance, while legal staff move from drafting to rule curation and exceptions. Faster contract output can shift demand to Customer signing and Loan activation.
- **Control violations:** A4 retains human KYC accountability, and A9 can block release on unknown KYC/decision status and check contract terms against the Committee decision. More issues may be detected initially. The portfolio does not by itself eliminate off-system provisional signing or the 5.8% month-end four-eyes violation rate in High-exposure cases; separate monitoring and interim manual gates remain necessary.
- **Synchronization limitation:** Because Committee packaging waits for all required artifacts, improving KYC and statements may not shorten packaging for a case still awaiting valuation or credit-analysis notes. The valuation and Committee paths must remain on the dashboard rather than being declared solved.

### Compliance-first portfolio: A4 + A9 + A10 + A12

- **Cycle time:** Can improve mismatch, contract, signing, and remediation-heavy variants. A12 may initially lengthen individual cases by surfacing previously hidden issues, while avoiding later remediation and unsafe completion. Document, credit-analysis, valuation, and Committee delays remain.
- **Waiting time:** Targets mismatch waiting, contract preparation, signing, and poorly owned remediation queues. It leaves normalization and credit-analysis waiting largely unchanged. Unknown KYC or decision status should produce a hold rather than an artificial cycle-time improvement.
- **Rework:** Addresses mismatch-to-KYC loops, contract-remediation loops, signing remediation, and recurring control issues through root-cause ownership. Missing-document and financial-statement rework remain.
- **Handovers:** Informal KYC/legal/RM remediation can become a single evidence-bearing workflow. Initially, however, more formal handovers may occur among specialists, legal ops, RMs, and control owners.
- **Resource demand:** Compliance and legal effort shifts toward exception review, policy clarification, rule maintenance, and remediation. Legal month-end capacity remains a gate. Analysts and back-office staff receive less direct relief, while faster clean completions can increase activation demand.
- **Control violations:** This portfolio gives the strongest coverage of KYC-before-signing, contract/decision matching, audit evidence, and remediation ownership. A12 can detect and route four-eyes conflicts, but preventive workflow enforcement and adequate month-end staffing are still required. Rising detected-issue counts should initially be distinguished from rising underlying violation rates.

## 7. Final recommendation

Adopt the **bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4 + A5 + A6 + A9**, implemented through the staged rollout above.

- **A4** addresses a small but disproportionately important exception path and its specialist-capacity constraint, without delegating KYC accountability.
- **A5 + A6** form a deliberate capacity pair: fewer incomplete-document loops and less manual normalization, rather than simply pushing more clean cases into an already central analyst network.
- **A9** provides both a broad downstream benefit and an explicit preventive-control investment, addressing contract/decision consistency and making the portfolio more than a labor-saving program.
- The portfolio also contains early visible wins through A5 and assisted A6, so the political need for a tangible result does not require making registration the first move.

**A1 should not be automated first merely because it has the highest frequency.** Its stable position and high feasibility make it attractive, and its nominal labor case may be real. However, registration’s service and waiting times are small relative to the mismatch, normalization, credit, valuation, contract, and signing constraints; the visible back-office queue also coexists with afternoon slack. Automating A1 first would therefore risk accelerating inflow into unchanged bottlenecks.

This recommendation deliberately sacrifices some opportunities: external valuation remains a major Equipment-finance constraint, Committee cadence remains unchanged, and e-sign adoption is not directly addressed. A7 should be the leading next-wave candidate if collateral waiting remains dominant; A8 should follow only when all Committee inputs are consistently reliable; A10/A12 should be prioritized if signing or remediation controls remain dominant. A3 requires further specialist-capacity and model-risk evidence, A1 can be reconsidered when downstream absorption is demonstrated, and A11 should remain last until the full control chain is dependable.
