**Reading.** QD provides repeated anti-priority evidence among jobs eligible for the same server: D04 beats D08 at 11:25, and D07 beats D10 at 11:48. The sequence instead follows resource-specific arrival order. D06’s exact appointment start is not uniquely diagnostic because D06 is also the oldest D-X candidate at 11:30, although D-X’s idle period from 11:20 to 11:30 suggests a reservation. The major anomaly is D05: if D-F was truly free after 11:36, skill-eligible FIFO should have started it immediately.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

**QA**

- **Supporting cases:** A03’s 08:20 start over older A02 is consistent with appointment priority. The subsequent order—A02, A04, A05, A06, A07, A08—matches recorded queue-entry order. A04 at 08:58, A05 at 09:05, and A07 at 09:32 each have at least one non-FIFO competitor and show that strict priority or SPT would have selected someone else.
- **Challenges:** A04 waits behind A02 despite P1 and estimate 7; A08 waits behind several P3/P2 jobs despite P1. These strongly challenge any claim that numeric priority controls QA. A04 and A08 challenge SPT at 09:05 and 09:32.
- **Known-constraint reconciliation:** A06’s 09:20 start relative to a 09:15 appointment is explained by A05 occupying A1 until 09:20. A03’s appointment explains its overtaking of A02. No additional undocumented exception is necessary if QA is appointment-aware FIFO. If appointments are not causal, A03’s move instead requires a manual hold of A02 or a batch-release rule.

**QB**

- **Supporting cases:** B01’s resumptions at 09:31 and 10:14 favor queue age and an interrupted-job continuation rule. B04 at 10:28 beats P2 B06, and B06 at 10:36 beats P3 B07; both support ordinary FCFS. B02’s preemption of B01 supports a bounded urgent/P1 preemption.
- **Challenges:** B05 beats older B04 at 10:00, contradicting pure FIFO and SPT. B02’s preemption contradicts pure FIFO. At 09:48, B03 is selected while P1 B05 is waiting, contradicting strict priority unless B03 was precommitted or B01’s reassignment forced a fallback.
- **Known-constraint reconciliation:** The second interruption explicitly says “dispatcher reassignment,” so it is not legitimate to infer a queue rule from that stop. B07’s 10:30 appointment was not hard-reserved: B04 and B06 occupied B1 continuously until 10:56. Resumption setup costs could also rationalize retaining B01, but no cost fields are supplied. If B03 was selected before B05 arrived, the apparent priority violation disappears.

**QC**

- **Supporting cases:** C02 at 10:10 and C03 at 10:18 are the shortest visible estimates at their respective boundaries. C06 at 11:34 is also shortest. C05 at 10:54 is consistent with P1 priority and with overdue-job urgency, although those two explanations are observationally equivalent.
- **Challenges:** C04 over C06 at 10:32 contradicts SPT; C05’s delay at 10:18 and 10:32 weakens deadline-driven dispatch. C01 over C07 at 11:40 contradicts priority, SPT, and earliest-deadline behavior. C07 was still feasible at 11:40 but becomes late because C01 runs first.
- **Known-constraint reconciliation:** C05 cannot meet 10:50 if started at 10:18 under its 40-minute estimate; delayed scans could mean it arrived near 10:09, but the logged entry remains 10:11. The largest unresolved issue is advance assignment: C04 may have been selected before C06 arrived, and C01 may have been selected before C07 arrived. Without those events, QC requires undocumented exceptions to nearly every pure rule.

**QD**

- **Supporting cases:** Every assignment respects skills. D05 is assigned to the only fully qualified resource, D-F. The three server streams are close to exact FCFS: D-M serves D01, D04, D08, D09; D-F serves D02, D03, D05; D-X serves D06, D07, D10. D06 starts exactly at its 11:30 appointment on D-X.
- **Challenges:** D08 is P1 and shorter than D04 but follows D04 at 11:25. D10 is P1 and shorter than D07 but follows D07 at 11:48. These are direct challenges to priority and SPT, not merely consequences of skill constraints, because the compared jobs share an eligible server. D-F’s failure to start D05 at 11:36 is the strongest challenge to immediate skill-eligible FIFO.
- **Known-constraint reconciliation:** D-X’s 11:20–11:30 idle time could be an appointment/resource hold. D05’s delay could reflect a hidden D-F hold, setup, or advance reservation. If D-F really was unavailable until 12:20, the 11:36 anomaly disappears; if it was available, an undocumented deferral is required.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

**1. QB at 09:48: ordinary FCFS with a temporary hold versus priority-first dispatch**

- **Explanation A — FCFS with an operational hold:** B01 is temporarily ineligible because of dispatcher reassignment, so B03 is selected. The next prediction is B04 at 10:00, followed by B01’s resumption at 10:14 unless a P1 override changes the order.
- **Explanation B — priority-first:** B05, already waiting as P1, should be selected at 09:48. The next prediction is B03 at 10:00, with B01 later resuming.
- **Observed distinction:** B03 then B05 is consistent with A plus a priority override at the 10:00 boundary, rather than with B.
- **Discriminating observation:** Capture the 09:48 dispatch-decision timestamp, candidate set, B01 eligibility/hold state, B03 assignment timestamp, and whether B05 carried an explicit urgent flag. In particular, a B03 assignment made before 09:46 would explain the episode without an exception.

**2. QD at 11:30: appointment/FCFS versus priority/SPT**

- **Explanation A — appointment-aware skill-eligible FIFO:** D06 starts at 11:30; the next D-X job is D07 at 11:48, followed by D10.
- **Explanation B — priority or shortest-job dispatch:** D10, P1 with estimate 12, should start at 11:30 rather than D06; D07 would follow later.
- **Observed distinction:** D06 followed by D07 supports A and contradicts B.
- **Discriminating observation:** Record whether the appointment created a D-X reservation from 11:20 or only a release time for D06. Also record the 11:25 eligibility snapshot: if D10 was already visible and D-X was free, priority would predict D10, strengthening B’s implications; if D10 was unseen or D-X was reserved, A remains viable.

**3. QC from 11:34 to 11:40: SPT/aging hybrid versus deadline-aware dispatch**

- **Explanation A — SPT with an aging exception:** C06 starts at 11:34; C01 then starts at 11:40 by age, as observed; C07 follows.
- **Explanation B — deadline/EDF protection:** C07 should start at 11:34, because its estimate of 18 still fits before its 12:00 deadline. C01 or C06 would follow, so the observed 11:40 C01 start is less likely.
- **Observed distinction:** The trace favors A over deadline-aware B.
- **Discriminating observation:** Record when C01 was actually selected. If C01 was selected before C07’s 10:40 arrival, the trace is consistent with advance assignment and aging. If it remained unassigned until 11:40 while C07 waited, the queue used an explicit FIFO/aging override rather than simply executing an old assignment.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

| rank | plausible exception or data-quality issue | evidence and diagnostic effect |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Service start is not dispatch-decision time** | This is the largest systematic ambiguity. QC’s apparent SPT violations can be explained if C04, C05, or C01 were assigned earlier. QB’s B03 choice can be explained by advance assignment. Collecting actual decision events would materially improve every queue and could raise QC confidence more than any other single addition. |
| 2 | **Incomplete resource-state or administrative-hold data** | At 11:36 D-F appears free by the supplied availability table, but D05 waits until 12:20. A hidden hold, setup period, or broader D-F unavailability would turn QD from “mostly FIFO with one violation” into almost exact resource-specific FIFO. |
| 3 | **Manual operational overrides and incomplete reason codes** | B01’s reassignment is a known override. The absence of a reason elsewhere does not prove there was no operational reason, particularly for D05 or QC’s unusual boundaries. Dispatcher identity, reason codes, and override flags would distinguish policy from exception handling. |
| 4 | **Appointment semantics differ by queue** | A03 starts exactly at its appointment, A06 starts when A1 becomes free, B07 starts 26 minutes after its target, and D06 starts exactly at its target. If appointments are hard reservations, QB contradicts the rule and QD is stronger. If they are soft targets or permitted windows, the appointment component should be downgraded rather than treated as a dispatch override. |
| 5 | **Delayed arrival scans change the candidate set** | C05 could physically have arrived as early as 10:09, and D10 as early as 11:23. If dispatch knew them before their recorded scans, priority or deadline predictions change. If scans define visibility, the logged sets are correct. True queue-entry and scan times must be separated. |
| 6 | **Priority, urgency, and SLA are not identical** | B02 is explicitly described as an urgent arrival, whereas B05 has the same numeric priority but no urgent-reason code. C05’s P1 status and endangered deadline coincide, so they cannot be distinguished observationally. If an independent urgency flag exists, QB becomes a clearer priority hybrid; if not, the fields may have been conflated. |
| 7 | **The queue changes policy when an SLA becomes endangered** | QC could switch toward urgency around C05 or C07, but the switch appears reactive: C05 starts after its deadline, and C07 is displaced while still feasible. If explicit SLA-warning and policy-version events show a preventive switch, QC’s hybrid becomes more coherent. |
| 8 | **Estimate noise, strategic inflation, or unrecorded revisions** | Ex post, all durations equal the estimates, so there is no positive evidence of noise or strategic behavior. Nevertheless, dispatch-time estimates or current remaining times could have differed from the final observed duration. This matters most for SPT and for resumed B01. |
| 9 | **Preemption/resumption overhead is real but hidden** | B01 is resumed twice ahead of newer jobs. A substantial setup cost could make this rational without a continuation rule. No visible gap proves zero overhead because timestamps have one-minute granularity. Explicit setup and resume-duration events would distinguish economic continuation from policy. |

A practical minimum next release would therefore include one immutable event for every attempted dispatch, not only successful service starts. That single change would distinguish the candidate mechanisms far more sharply than another start-order reconstruction.

# 7. Final synthesis

- **QA:** Most identifiable as an ordering policy. It is primarily FIFO by queue entry, with A03 showing a plausible appointment override. Priority and SPT fail repeatedly among server-eligible jobs.
- **QB:** Best described as FIFO/FCFS with a bounded P1 or explicitly urgent preemption, continuation of interrupted work, and at least one operational reassignment/hold exception. The first urgent interruption alone would seriously mislead a strict-priority analysis.
- **QC:** Least identifiable. No pure FIFO, priority, SPT, deadline, or appointment rule fits all starts. The most defensible description is an SPT-like hybrid with P1/SLA overrides and either aging or advance assignments, but the supporting event evidence is insufficient to choose among those mechanisms confidently.
- **QD:** Primarily skill-based routing with resource-specific FIFO. D04 over D08 and D07 over D10 strongly reject priority and SPT. D05’s delayed start and D-X’s pre-appointment idle period remain unresolved resource-state or reservation issues.

**Most identifiable queue:** QA, because it has one server and an almost exact FIFO sequence apart from one explainable appointment event.

**Least identifiable queue:** QC, because service-start order and dispatch order may differ and every pure mechanism has multiple unexplained boundaries.

**Most dangerous policy inference:** A blanket **preemptive-priority** rule. B02’s interruption and C05’s selection make it superficially attractive, but the full decision sets show repeated bypasses of P1 work in QA and QD. Operationalizing that false inference could institutionalize starvation and unsafe preemption. Pure FIFO is also a poor SLA control—especially for QC—but it is less dangerous to infer than a system-wide priority/preemption rule from this evidence.
