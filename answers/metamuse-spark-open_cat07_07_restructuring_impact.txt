# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1.  **Tail latency explosion (p90/p95 decoupled from mean):** Management expects median wait +0.8h (3.24.0) to be offset by fewer loops, but pooled queue with heterogeneous service times creates convex waiting. Average can stay flat (simple cases 60% faster due to automation) while p90 jumps 1927d. Monitoring only mean rewards gaming simple cases and starving complex ones. PM4Py view: `discover_performance_dfg` mean edge stable, but distribution view via `get_service_time` percentiles shows long tail at TSS and Legal/Risk.
2.  **High-complexity / high-risk cases systematically penalized:** Routing rule concentrates `case_complexity=high` and `required_specialty{legal,risk,security}` into same queue. Their average wait rises 9.6~18h while low-complexity cases improve. Blended average hides that riskiest cases – where delay costs most – degrade. Stratified `filter_event_attribute_values(case_complexity=high)` required; SLA misses become concentrated.
3.  **Local knowledge loss and downstream rework:** Pre-change, embedded N_Tech/S_Tech gave early informal advice preventing formal referrals and encoding regional conventions. Post-change, that advice is absent  case notes poorer  central advice generic  Implementation fails regional accommodation  Rework. `rework_flag` may lag 1–2 steps (Quality Check/Implementation), so cycle time initially looks fine, then rework creates second cycle. `local_expertise_flag=0` cohort shows +8pp rework even as average rework briefly dips due to better detection.
4.  **Fairness across teams and customer segments (East vs North/South; language/region):** East (median 8.6d, E_Tech 82% utilized) may genuinely benefit from pooling (relief of overload), while North/South (6.8d/7.4d, strong local loops) lose. Average across all teams (~7.3d blended) shows improvement driven by East, masking North/South degradation. Similarly, customers needing regional/language accommodation suffer more handovers to central actors lacking context. Only segmented analysis by `team` and `customer_segment` reveals redistribution, not creation, of value.
5.  **Learning, de-skilling and motivation erosion:** When all hard cases are routed away, local caseworkers stop developing technical judgment. `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` will show locals converging to `Intake+Contact+Implementation` only, with zero `Technical Assessment`. Early months: utilization up (good). 6–12 months: locals cannot triage, defensive over-referral increases (4150/100), central load grows endogenously. Absence of learning is invisible in cycle time until turnover or absence removes central experts.
6.  **Accountability diffusion and exception handling fragility:** Shared approval queue standardizes the `concept:name=Supervisor Approval` event but obscures *who* is accountable. `org:resource` becomes `Shared_Approval_Queue` rather than `N_Supervisor`. `discover_subcontracting_network` and `discover_organizational_roles` show a flat star, not a hierarchy. For exceptions (priority=urgent, security hold, customer complaint), no clear owner chases  cases age in queue. Average cycle time stable because exceptions are few, but exception cycle time + variance spikes; `reassignment_count` and `handover_reason=escalation` capture accountability vacuum.
7.  **Resilience to absence / single point of failure (hidden fragility):** Pre-change, absence covered by cross-team handover 6/100 at 8.9h wait – poor but localized (only that team affected). Post-change, central TSS or Legal absence blocks *all* teams. Average cycle time over a month with 1–2 absence days looks okay, but `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` shows bimodality: normal days median 6d, absence days median 11d with queue spillover into next week. System appears efficient at high utilization (93–95%) precisely when slack for resilience is gone (“efficient but brittle”).

# 5. Pilot design

**Pilot scope:** Do not pilot organization-wide. Pilot = East team (520 cases/month) + Technical Shared Service (limited capacity) + Shared Approval queue for medium/high-value cases only. Keep North and South as embedded controls with current org. Duration 10–12 weeks active + 4 weeks baseline pre.

Rationale: East has highest pain (E_Tech 82%, median 8.6d) and most to gain, minimizing harm and maximizing learning; limits blast radius of central bottleneck. If pilot still creates bottleneck in East (smallest team), full rollout will be worse.

**Comparison group:** North and South remain control (difference-in-differences). Additionally within East, simple Eligibility cases kept local even if rule would route – internal control for routing-rule effect.

**Pre/post observation window:**
*   Baseline (pre): 8 weeks before pilot, stratified by seasonality. Capture `team`, `case_complexity`, `required_specialty`, `customer_segment`, `priority`.
*   Wash-in: weeks 1–2 pilot excluded from primary evaluation (learning curve).
*   Evaluation: weeks 3–10 primary, weeks 11–12 robustness. Continue lagging follow-up to week 16 for rework/SLA.
*   Include both `pm4py.format_dataframe` with `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `time:timestamp` and full attribute columns.

**Segmentation (required stratification – blinded overall average not sufficient):**
*   By `team`: North vs South vs East-pilot vs East-control (if East split)
*   By `case_complexity`: low/medium/high
*   By `required_specialty`: none / legal / risk / security / technical
*   By `customer_segment` and `location`: regional/language accommodation flag, customer conventions
*   By `priority`: urgent/normal
*   By `local_expertise_flag`: 1 vs 0 to test knowledge loss hypothesis

**Guardrail metrics (automatic alerts, not just reporting):**
*   Operational: TSS queue median wait >8h for 2 consecutive days; oldest case >72h in TSS or approval; `reassignment_count` mean >1.5
*   Quality: Rework rate +3pp vs baseline; Implementation rework >12%; Quality Check fail rate +4pp
*   Fairness: East pilot high-complexity p90 vs control North/South high-complexity p90 gap widens >20%; language-accommodation segment satisfaction  0.3 pts
*   Resilience: Cycle time variance on specialist absence day >1.8× baseline
*   Learning: Share of cases where local caseworker performs Technical Assessment =0 for >4 weeks

Breaching 2 guardrails  escalate; breaching 3  pause new intake to central queue.

**Staff feedback collection (mixed methods, not only survey):**
*   Weekly 15-min pulse with N_Tech/S_Tech/E_Tech + 2 caseworkers/team: “informal advice instances lost?”, “advice quality sufficient to implement?”, “owner clear?” – coded to `handover_reason`
*   Monthly role-clarity workshop: map perceived ownership vs `discover_organizational_roles` output; identify accountability gaps
*   Customer-facing staff log friction tags: `regional_convention_mismatch`, `language_barrier`, `rework_due_to_central_advice`
*   Protect psychological safety: North/South control staff incentivized to report honestly, not to game referral rates

**Rollback or adjustment triggers (pre-committed):**
*   *Rollback to embedded model within pilot:* If median cycle time for East high-complexity cases  >15% vs baseline for 3 consecutive weeks, or p90  >25%, or rework >15%  revert East to embedded, keep data.
*   *Adjustment (not full rollback):* If Legal/Risk queue wait >20h median  carve out Legal/Risk from TSS (separate queue, dedicated FTE) and re-evaluate; if approval queue starvation  restore local supervisor approval for urgent cases (<20% of volume)
*   *Stop rollout:* If fairness gap (North/South vs East high-complexity SLA miss) widens >5pp or local_expertise_flag rate drops >15pp with quality drop  do not expand; redesign with local technical liaison model (hybrid)
*   *Go criteria to expand:* Median stable or better *and* p90 not worse *and* rework not worse *and* no guardrail breach for 6 weeks *and* staff feedback role clarity  baseline  consider phased expansion to South first.

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: Pilot first with hybrid modification – do not proceed to full immediate rollout; reject pure centralization as designed.**

Condition: Pilot East-only for 10–12 weeks with North/South as controls under guardrails above. Decision to modify/expand contingent on segmented, distributional evidence – not on early average cycle time.

**What to do instead of full centralization:**
1.  Create *pooled* Technical Shared Service but retain *local technical liaisons* (0.5 FTE N_Tech/S_Tech embedded) to preserve informal advice and context translation. Routes high-complexity to central, but liaison pre-triages and enriches case notes.
2.  Separate queues: Technical advice (fast, <4h SLA) vs Legal/Risk/Quality Check (slow, >24h SLA). Do not stack heterogeneous work in one queue.
3.  Keep local supervisor approval for `priority=urgent` and language/regional accommodation cases; shared approval only for standardized medium/high-value approvals with clear owner tagging (`org:resource` must be person, not queue).

**Conditional on monitoring:**
*   Only expand if pilot shows (a) East median improvement sustained, (b) North/South control not degraded relative to baseline seasonality, (c) high-complexity p90 and SLA miss not worse, (d) rework not increased in `local_expertise_flag=0` cohort, (e) reassignment_count and clarification handovers stable.
*   If pilot shows bimodal benefit (East gains, high-complexity loses)  *modify* to selective routing (route only truly specialized cases, keep medium-complexity local with central consult – not handover).

**What management must not infer from early averages:**
*   Do not infer specialist efficiency from utilization . `get_service_time` stable + utilization up can coexist with `service_waiting_time` explosion; throughput per specialist may fall.
*   Do not infer success from median cycle time  5–10% in weeks 1–4. Early improvement is driven by simple-case automation and Hawthorne/attention effects; tail (p90), rework, and learning effects lag by 6–12 weeks.
*   Do not infer fairness or quality from blended metrics. East improvement can mask North/South degradation and language/regional segment harm. Always segment by `team`, `case_complexity`, `required_specialty`, `customer_segment`.
*   Do not infer role clarity from fewer nominal handovers. Fewer edges in `discover_handover_of_work_network` with higher `reassignment_count` and diffuse `discover_organizational_roles` means accountability worsened, not improved.
*   Do not extrapolate resilience from normal-week data. Absence or demand spike not in early window will reveal fragility at 90%+ utilization.

Proceeding without pilot risks converting a distributional and learning problem into a central bottleneck that is invisible in management’s mean-based dashboard until customer-sensitive and high-risk cases have already degraded.
