# 1. Objectives and constraints

The policy is deliberately multi-objective. It does not assume that throughput, fairness, training, continuity, and SLA safety can be collapsed into one “obvious” optimum. Instead, it defines hard compliance rules first, then uses a transparent scoring function to balance competing preferences.

## Objectives

1. **SLA protection**
   - Minimize expected SLA misses, especially for urgent and high-priority cases.
   - Give priority to cases with short SLA time remaining, high complexity, and customer impact.

2. **Quality and error reduction**
   - Reduce skill mismatch.
   - Reduce rework, appeals, and repeated diagnosis loops.
   - Use specialists where complexity and risk are high.

3. **Continuity and handover control**
   - Prefer the current owner when handover cost is high and the owner is capable enough.
   - Avoid pushing cases past a reassignment threshold, especially when rework risk is high.

4. **Workload fairness and overtime protection**
   - Balance new assignments across resources.
   - Avoid adding non-urgent work to resources with high overtime risk.
   - Respect the maximum of 4 new assignments per resource in the current batch.

5. **Skill development**
   - Use normal/low-priority cases with SLA buffer for documented training opportunities.
   - Require supervision where trainees are used.

6. **Throughput**
   - Keep work moving without creating hidden future rework or SLA failures.

7. **Resilience and bottleneck avoidance**
   - Avoid over-concentration on a single specialist.
   - Maintain cross-trained capacity.
   - Reduce dependence on fragile handover chains.

## Safety / compliance hard constraints

These are treated as filters, not trade-offs.

1. **Skill 0 is not allowed**
   - A resource with score 0 for the required skill cannot perform the activity.

2. **Security Review rule**
   - Security Review must be performed by skill level 3.
   - Skill level 1–2 may perform only under explicit supervision by R7_Gita or R8_Hugo.

3. **Final Approval rule**
   - Final Approval must be performed by skill level 2–3.
   - Skill level 1 may prepare but may not finalize.

4. **High-priority trainee protection**
   - No high-priority case may be assigned to a skill level below 2 unless supervised.

5. **Batch workload cap**
   - No resource may receive more than 4 new assignments in the current decision batch.

6. **Overtime risk protection**
   - Resources with `overtime_risk = high` should not receive additional work unless the case is urgent and no safer alternative exists.
   - Any exception must be logged and auditable.

7. **Quality Check batching rule**
   - Quality Check may be batched only when:
     - priority is normal or low, and
     - SLA time remaining is greater than 24 hours.

8. **Supervision must be explicit**
   - If a supervised pair is used, the supervisor must be eligible, available, and recorded.
   - Supervision should be visible in the event log, for example via `assignment_reason`.

9. **Auditability**
   - Every assignment must record:
     - chosen resource,
     - supervisor if any,
     - main reason,
     - accepted trade-off,
     - whether it supports training,
     - risk flags,
     - any deviation from policy.

## Preference trade-offs

These are soft objectives and can be balanced through scoring.

1. **Throughput vs quality**
   - Faster assignment may increase skill mismatch and later rework.

2. **Continuity vs skill fit**
   - Keeping a current owner avoids handover cost but may increase service time if the owner is weak.

3. **Training vs SLA risk**
   - Training is valuable only when SLA buffer and supervision are sufficient.

4. **Specialist use vs bottleneck risk**
   - Specialists reduce errors but can create queues if overused.

5. **Fairness vs urgency**
   - Urgent cases may temporarily increase workload for a low-risk resource, but this should be monitored.

# 2. Assignment policy

The policy is a **rule-filtered, explainable scoring policy**. It is dynamic because the scoring inputs can be updated from process mining: service times, handover costs, rework rates, workload, and bottleneck indicators.

## Policy logic

The policy proceeds in three stages:

1. **Hard eligibility filtering**
2. **SLA-aware candidate ranking**
3. **Explainable scoring and exception handling**

## Hard eligibility filtering

For each case and resource, the policy first checks:

```text
eligible =
    skill > 0
    AND activity-specific compliance rules are satisfied
    AND resource has enough remaining hours for estimated work
    AND resource has not reached 4 new assignments in the batch
    AND overtime-risk rule is satisfied
```

Activity-specific rules:

### Security Review

```text
eligible if:
    skill == 3
    OR (skill in {1, 2} AND supervisor in {R7_Gita, R8_Hugo} AND supervisor available)
```

### Final Approval

```text
eligible if:
    skill in {2, 3}
```

Skill 1 may prepare, but cannot finalize.

### Quality Check batching

```text
batching allowed if:
    priority in {normal, low}
    AND sla_remaining_hours > 24
```

### High-priority cases

```text
if priority in {urgent, high}:
    skill >= 2
    OR (skill < 2 AND explicit supervisor available and eligible)
```

## SLA urgency scoring

Urgency is computed from:

- priority,
- SLA time remaining,
- complexity,
- customer impact notes,
- reassignment history.

A simple explainable form:

```text
priority_rank:
    urgent = 3
    high   = 2
    normal = 1
    low    = 0

sla_pressure = max(0, 1 - sla_remaining_hours / 24)

urgency_score =
    0.40 * priority_rank / 3
  + 0.35 * sla_pressure
  + 0.15 * case_complexity
  + 0.10 * (sla_remaining_hours <= 4)
```

Urgent cases with very short SLA time are processed first.

## Skill-match scoring

Skill match rewards appropriate competence, not only maximum skill.

```text
skill_score =
    0.55 * (resource_skill / 3)
  + 0.25 * supervisor_quality
  + 0.20 * specialist_bonus_if_complex
```

Where:

- `supervisor_quality` is high if the supervisor has skill 3 or is an approved specialist.
- `specialist_bonus_if_complex` is applied when complexity is high and the task is error-sensitive.

The policy does not always choose the highest skill if that creates a bottleneck or violates workload fairness.

## Continuity / handover-cost scoring

Continuity is especially important when:

- handover cost is high,
- complexity is high,
- reassignment count is already high,
- the case has a repeated loop history.

```text
handover_penalty:
    low    = 0.0
    medium = 0.3
    high   = 0.7

continuity_score =
    0.45 * is_current_owner
  + 0.35 * (1 - handover_penalty)
  + 0.20 * (1 - min(reassignment_count, 3) / 3)
```

If the current owner is skill 0, continuity is not enough.

If the current owner is skill 1 on a high-risk task, continuity is allowed only with appropriate supervision.

## Workload and overtime fairness scoring

Workload fairness uses:

- current open cases,
- hours remaining today,
- new assignments already given in the batch,
- overtime risk.

```text
open_case_load = current_open_cases / max_current_open_cases
hour_load = 1 - hours_remaining_today / max_hours_remaining

overtime_penalty:
    low    = 0.0
    medium = 0.4
    high   = 0.9

workload_score =
    1 -
        0.35 * open_case_load
      + 0.25 * hour_load
      + 0.25 * overtime_penalty
      + 0.15 * new_assignments_in_batch / 4
```

A higher workload score means a more favorable assignment from a fairness perspective.

## Training / development scoring

Training is supported only when it is safe.

```text
training_score = 1 if all of the following are true:
    priority in {normal, low}
    AND sla_remaining_hours >= 12
    AND resource.training_priority == required_skill
    AND resource_skill in {1, 2}
    AND eligible supervisor is available
else:
    training_score = 0
```

The policy targets at least 20% of normal/low-priority assignments to support documented training priorities when SLA risk remains acceptable.

## Resilience / bottleneck avoidance

Resilience scoring penalizes assignments that increase fragility.

```text
resilience_score =
    1 -
        0.40 * is_sole_specialist_for_activity
      + 0.30 * team_queue_pressure
      + 0.30 * specialist_utilization_risk
```

Examples:

- If Security Review has only two skill-3 resources, assigning all security work to one of them is penalized.
- If a resource already has many urgent cases, adding another urgent case is penalized.
- If a team has a long queue, assigning more work to that team is penalized.

## Overall score

A possible explainable weighting:

```text
assignment_score =
    0.30 * urgency_score
  + 0.25 * skill_score
  + 0.20 * continuity_score
  + 0.15 * workload_score
  + 0.05 * training_score
  + 0.05 * resilience_score
```

The weights are not sacred. They should be reviewed using process-mining indicators. For example:

- If rework rises, increase skill and continuity weights.
- If specialist queues rise, increase resilience weight.
- If training target is missed, increase training weight for normal/low cases only.

## Pseudocode

```python
def assign_case(case, resources, batch_state, historical_indicators):
    candidates = []

    # 1. Hard eligibility filtering
    for r in resources:
        if not hard_eligible(case, r, batch_state):
            continue

        supervisor = None
        if case_requires_supervision(case, r):
            supervisor = choose_supervisor(case, r, resources, batch_state)
            if supervisor is None:
                continue

        # 2. Compute explainable scores
        urgency = sla_urgency_score(case)
        skill = skill_match_score(case, r, supervisor, historical_indicators)
        continuity = continuity_score(case, r)
        workload = workload_fairness_score(r, batch_state)
        training = training_score(case, r, supervisor)
        resilience = resilience_score(case, r, historical_indicators)

        total = (
            0.30 * urgency
          + 0.25 * skill
          + 0.20 * continuity
          + 0.15 * workload
          + 0.05 * training
          + 0.05 * resilience
        )

        candidates.append({
            "resource": r,
            "supervisor": supervisor,
            "score": total,
            "urgency": urgency,
            "skill": skill,
            "continuity": continuity,
            "workload": workload,
            "training": training,
            "resilience": resilience,
        })

    # 3. Exception handling
    if not candidates:
        return handle_exception(case, batch_state)

    # 4. Tie-breaking
    best = sorted(candidates, key=lambda x: (
        -x["score"],
        x["resource"].overtime_risk_rank,
        x["resource"].current_open_cases,
        -x["resource"].hours_remaining_today,
        x["resource"].new_assignments_in_batch,
        x["resource"].name
    ))[0]

    # 5. Record explainable decision
    record_assignment(
        case=case,
        resource=best["resource"],
        supervisor=best["supervisor"],
        reason=explain_assignment(case, best),
        trade_off=explain_trade_off(case, best),
        supports_training=best["training"] > 0,
        risk_flags=compute_risk_flags(case, best),
        scores=best
    )

    # 6. Update batch state
    batch_state.add_assignment(best["resource"], case)
    if best["supervisor"]:
        batch_state.add_supervision(best["supervisor"], case)

    return best
```

## Tie-breaking

If scores are close, use this order:

1. Lower overtime risk.
2. Lower current open cases.
3. Higher hours remaining today.
4. Fewer new assignments already in the current batch.
5. Lower handover cost.
6. Better team alignment.
7. Stable resource identifier for reproducibility.

## Exception handling

If no eligible resource exists:

1. **Urgent case**
   - Escalate immediately.
   - Consider a high-overtime-risk resource only if no safer alternative exists.
   - Log the exception.
   - Require supervisor review if skill is below the normal threshold.

2. **Non-urgent case**
   - Defer to next batch if safe.
   - Do not force a skill-0 assignment.
   - Do not force an unsupervised trainee assignment.

3. **Supervisor unavailable**
   - Use an alternate approved specialist if available.
   - If the performer is already skill 3, allow solo performance where allowed.
   - Otherwise defer or escalate.

4. **Training target missed**
   - If SLA buffer is acceptable, reassign a normal/low-priority case to a resource whose documented training priority matches the required skill.
   - Require supervision.

5. **High-complexity, high-handover case**
   - If the current owner is skill 1, do not change owner unless supervision is feasible.
   - If the current owner is skill 0, continuity is not acceptable.

# 4. Comparison with baseline policies

This comparison is qualitative and based on the snapshot plus the historical handover-cost evidence. It is not a claim that one policy dominates in all situations.

| Dimension | First-available routing | Specialist-only routing | Proposed explainable dynamic policy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expected throughput | High short-term throughput because cases are pushed to the least-loaded available resource | Moderate throughput; quality may be high, but specialist queues can delay completion | Moderate to good sustainable throughput; accepts some slower supervised work to reduce future rework |
| SLA risk | High for urgent and specialized cases; skill mismatch can delay completion and create rework | High for activities where only one or two specialists exist, especially Security and Approval | Lower for urgent cases because SLA urgency is explicit; some supervised normal cases may add service time |
| Error / rework risk | High because skill fit is ignored except skill 0 | Lower for specialized tasks, but delays can increase customer escalation and rework pressure | Lower overall because skill fit, continuity, and reassignment history are considered; trainee cases are supervised |
| Fairness of workload | Poor; low-load resources can be overloaded quickly | Poor; specialists become overloaded while others remain underused | Better; workload, hours remaining, overtime risk, and batch caps are explicit |
| Skill development | Accidental and uncontrolled | Low; specialists do expert work, trainees do little | Targeted; normal/low-priority cases are used for documented training with supervision |
| Continuity of case ownership | Low; current owner is ignored | Low to medium; current owner may be replaced by a specialist | High where handover cost and complexity justify it; especially for C001, C007, C016 |
| Resilience to absence or bottlenecks | Fragile because skill mismatch creates hidden dependencies | Fragile because critical activities depend on a few specialists | More resilient because load is spread, supervisors are explicit, and bottleneck indicators are auditable |
| Explainability | Easy to explain but weak: “least loaded available” | Easy to explain but brittle: “best specialist” | More complex but auditable: each decision has reasons, trade-offs, and risk flags |
| Main weakness | Creates future rework and SLA failures | Creates specialist bottlenecks and underuses cross-trained capacity | Requires supervision capacity and good process-mining feedback |

## Interpretation

- **First-available** is attractive when the immediate goal is to empty the queue. However, the historical evidence says it increases reassignments and skill mismatch. That can reduce effective throughput later.

- **Specialist-only** is attractive when quality is the dominant concern. However, the snapshot shows only two skill-3 security resources and one obvious approval specialist. Routing everything to the best specialist would likely create Security and Approval bottlenecks.

- **The proposed policy** is not a universal optimum. It is a defensible operating point for a service process that must balance SLA, fairness, training, continuity, and resilience. If the organization were in an extreme SLA crisis, it might temporarily increase specialist-only behavior. If the organization were prioritizing long-term capability, it might increase supervised training further. The policy should be adjusted using audit indicators, not fixed forever.

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators

The deployed policy should be audited using the event log. The indicators below should be computed regularly and used to adjust weights, thresholds, and training targets.

| Indicator | Definition / formula | Why it matters | Suggested target or alert |
|---|---|---|---|
| Handover frequency | Number of owner changes per case; edge weights in handover-of-work network | High handover frequency increases waiting, confusion, and rework | Lower than baseline; alert if cases with >2 owner changes rise |
| Reassignment count | Mean, median, P90, and share of cases with `reassignment_count > 2` | Historical evidence links >2 reassignments to higher rework and appeals | P90 should not exceed 2; urgent cases should rarely exceed 2 |
| Skill mismatch | Share of assignments where `required_skill` score is below the required minimum, or below 2 for urgent/high cases without supervision | Skill mismatch increases errors and rework | Near 0 for hard-rule violations; low soft mismatch rate |
| Workload distribution | Distribution of new assignments per resource; Gini coefficient; max assignments per batch | Detects overload and unfair concentration | Max 4 per batch; Gini below agreed threshold |
| Overtime risk exposure | Share of assignments to resources with `overtime_risk = high`, especially non-urgent cases | Protects workers and reduces burnout-related errors | 0 non-urgent high-OT assignments without logged exception |
| SLA misses | Share of cases completed after `sla_deadline`, by priority and activity | Direct customer impact | Urgent SLA misses should be minimal; investigate all urgent misses |
| Waiting time after handover | Use `insert_case_service_waiting_time` to compute waiting after owner changes | Measures hidden cost of handovers | Median waiting after high-complexity handover should stay below threshold |
| Rework / appeal after assignment | Share of cases with `Rework Correction` or appeal-related variants after assignment | Measures quality impact of assignment decisions | Lower than baseline; monitor by skill mismatch and reassignment count |
| Training assignment share | Share of normal/low-priority assignments where `assignment_reason` includes training or supervised development and matches documented training priority | Ensures development is happening without sacrificing SLA | At least 20% when SLA risk is acceptable |
| Specialist bottleneck indicators | Utilization of skill-3 resources; queue length and waiting time for Security Review, Final Approval, Technical Diagnosis; share of security cases assigned to R7/R8 | Detects over-concentration on specialists | No single specialist should absorb an unsustainable share of critical work |
| Deviations from assignment policy | Share of assignments violating hard rules or differing from recorded policy decision | Ensures auditability and trust | 0 unlogged hard deviations; soft deviations logged and reviewed |
| Continuity preservation | Share of high-complexity/high-handover cases kept with current owner when eligible | Tests whether continuity rule is working | High for eligible high-handover cases |
| Batch effectiveness | Share of eligible Quality Check cases batched; batch size; SLA impact of batching | Validates batching rule | Batch eligible cases when safe; monitor SLA impact |
