## 1. What is observed
Availability of automation is staggered: Alpha and Beta show `automation=1` from April, Gamma from July, Delta and Epsilon remain at 0 through August. Actual use, measured by `automated_fast_path_pct`, rises gradually after availability from ~4-6% to 25-27% in the first treated month and to 32-50% by August, indicating partial take-up and learning rather than immediate full conversion. Branch selection into rollout was targeted: Alpha and Beta had higher arrivals and historically poorer cycle times than Gamma, Delta and Epsilon. Contemporaneous operational changes affect all branches: a centralized fraud desk from March coincides with a ~2pp rise in `escalation_pct`; routing increased arrivals ~10% to Alpha/Beta from April and ~5% to Gamma from July; FTEs changed in four branches; high-complexity share rose in May-June in most branches. Outcome measurement is at branch-month level only: `median_cycle_h` and `p90_cycle_h` summarize unknown within-month distributions, `rework_pct` and `escalation_pct` are percentages without denominators for queueing, and `csat_1to5` means have 20-45% response without counts, so precision and nonresponse cannot be assessed.

## 3. Identification strategy
| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-primary: staggered event-study DiD with not-yet-treated controls | relative-time dummies k=-3..+4 around branch adoption date; separate cohorts Alpha/Beta Apr and Gamma Jul | not-yet-treated branches in each window (Delta+Epsilon always; Gamma as control for Apr cohort pre-Jul) | month FE for common shocks, branch FE, high_complexity_pct, arrivals/fte workload, pre-period matching on level/trend | E1, E2, E4, E5 (ITT) | parallel trends in absence of treatment conditional on controls; no anticipation; limited spillover; stable cohort effects | pre-trend plot k<0, placebo March fraud-desk effect, leave-one-control-out, common-shock subtraction May-Jun | time-varying confounding (staffing, complexity, routing) and fraud-desk queue correlated with treatment timing |
| B-alternative: synthetic control per adopter | adoption as single interruption Apr or Jul | weighted donor pool of Delta, Epsilon (+ Gamma pre-Jul for Alpha/Beta) matched on Jan-Feb outcomes, arrivals, complexity | pre-period outcomes and covariates for weights; no regression adjustment post | E1, E2 | donor pool spans treated counterfactual; no spillover to donors; pre-fit predicts post absence | pre-RMSPE, placebo-in-time and placebo-branch, weight stability | only 2 donors, poor fit given Alpha/Beta selected as outliers; overfit |
| C-alternative: interrupted time series with control series + dose model | level+slope change at Apr/Jul; second specification replaces binary with automated_fast_path_pct | own pre-history plus never-treated trend as control series | month indicators for Mar fraud desk and May complexity shock, workload, FTE | E1-E3, E7 dynamic/dose | pre-slope would continue absent treatment; dose exogenous conditional on FEs | residual autocorrelation, slope-change vs level-change, dose-response linearity check | May-June shock breaks slope extrapolation; dose is endogenous to learning and case selection |

Why primary is preferable for these data: With only five branches and two adoption dates, any estimator is fragile, but the staggered event-study makes the identifying comparison explicit and exploits the cleanest quasi-experiment in the data: Gamma's July adoption against Delta/Epsilon, and Alpha/Beta's April adoption against Gamma pre-July plus never-treated. Month fixed effects directly absorb the March fraud-desk jump and May-June complexity surge visible in all branches, which a single-branch ITS cannot separate from treatment. Disaggregating by relative time respects that `automated_fast_path_pct` grows from ~25% to ~50%, so early versus mature effects differ, avoiding the forbidden comparisons and negative weighting of naive two-way fixed effects under heterogeneous timing. Diagnostics are transparent even with N=40: pre-coefficients test parallel trends, and Gamma-as-control disciplines regression-to-the-mean claims for Alpha/Beta. Synthetic control and ITS remain valuable as robustness checks, but with two donors and a strong common shock they are less credible as primary. No method solves endogenous use, small clusters, or fraud-desk spillover; the primary design only bounds ITT under stated assumptions.

Within-adopter learning: Alpha median 48.3 (Apr, 25% use) ->53.5 (Jun,39%) ->43.3 (Aug,48%); Beta 47.3->49.0->45.0; Gamma 45.9 (Jun,4%) ->38.4 (Jul,25%) ->36.0 (Aug,32%). August is best median month for all adopters.

**5.2 Comparison vs not-yet/never-treated.** Gamma Jul adoption is cleanest. Change Shock->Mature: Gamma median -8.5h, Delta -3.4h, Epsilon -5.2h. DiD Gamma-Delta = -5.1h (~11% of Shock level); Gamma-Epsilon = -3.3h (~7%). p90 Shock->Mature: Gamma -15.6h, Delta -6.2h, Epsilon -9.7h. DiD -9.4h vs Delta, -5.9h vs Epsilon (~6-9%). For Apr cohort, Mature-Baseline DiD vs Delta: Alpha median -8.8h [ -3.1 minus +5.7 ], Beta -4.6h; p90 DiD Alpha -11.1h, Beta -8.6h (adopters rose less than Delta). Vs Epsilon: Alpha median -5.5h, Beta -1.3h. All adopters improved *relative* to controls, but absolute p90 remains above Baseline except Alpha median.

**5.3 Treatment-intensity.** Binary `automation` overstates exposure early (25%) vs late (50%). Cross-section Aug: fast_path 48,50,32,4,4% vs median 43.3,45.0,36.0,39.0,40.8 — no simple negative slope because branch levels differ. Within-branch: pooled adopter months show median falls ~4-10h as use rises 25pp from Apr to Aug, but May-Jun shock confounds. Descriptive OLS median on fast_path + branch+month FE (see code) yields negative coefficient, p90 coefficient smaller/positive in early months — consistent with median benefit, tail penalty early. This is associational: managers likely automated easier cases first.

**5.4 Pre-trends and regression to mean.** Jan-Feb to Mar: Alpha median 48.7->49.4 (+0.7), Beta 45.7->46.4 (+0.7), Gamma 36.5->39.6 (+3.1), Delta 33.6->36.2 (+2.6), Epsilon 40.4->40.2 (-0.2). Adopters were already slower, and Alpha/Beta selected for poor history, so part of later relative improvement could be mean reversion. Counterpoint: Mar-Jun deterioration is common to all branches (fraud desk + complexity), and Gamma, not selected for poor times, shows largest Shock->Mature drop, weakening pure mean-reversion story for Gamma but not for Alpha/Beta.

**5.5 Sensitivity / bounds.** Median: Gamma DiD -3.3 to -5.1h depending on Delta vs Epsilon control; Alpha vs controls -5.5 to -8.8h but with larger staffing/routing confounds, so bound to -2 to -6h after discounting 30-50% for workload/complexity. p90: Gamma DiD -6 to -9h, but absolute still +9h above Baseline; bound as -5 to -10h relative, 0 to -5h absolute vs clean baseline. Rework: Apr dip -2pp reverses; Gamma -2.0pp Shock->Mature vs Delta 0.0pp, Epsilon +0.3pp => DiD ~-2pp, fragile to definition. CSAT: Gamma +0.17 vs Jun, Delta +0.06, Epsilon +0.11 => DiD +0.06 to +0.11 on 1-5 scale; within survey noise and nonresponse, bound includes zero.

## 6. Threats, tests, and decision consequences
| threat | why it matters here | direction of likely bias | test or additional evidence | consequence if threat is severe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| selection into adoption | Alpha/Beta chosen for high volume/poor times | overstates benefit via mean reversion | compare pre-levels; placebo DiD Jan-Feb vs Mar; Gamma as less-selected check | ITT for Delta/Epsilon not transportable |
| nonparallel trends | fraud desk Mar + May complexity hit branches unevenly | ambiguous; controls rose more, inflates DiD | event-study pre-coeffs; adjust for complexity/workload | spurious relative gain |
| time-varying confounding (complexity) | high_complexity +5-8pp in Alpha/Epsilon, +5pp Gamma/Delta | masks benefit (upward bias on cycle time) | complexity-adjusted model; case-level stratification | underestimates true efficiency |
| treatment intensity vs binary | use 25%->50%, learning over time | binary ATT averages over doses, understates mature effect | dose-response, early vs mature split | premature scale judgment |
| spillover via fraud desk | auto-triage sends more borderline to central desk; waits unrecorded | biases p90 upward for adopters; congests controls too | collect fraud-desk arrival/wait by branch; queue-state data | misattribute queue harm to automation |
| staffing changes | Alpha -2, Gamma -1, Beta/Delta +1 FTE | Alpha/Gamma bias upward, Beta/Delta downward | arrivals/FTE control; hours/overtime logs | confounded capacity effect |
| case-mix / routing | +10% arrivals to Alpha/Beta, +5% Gamma | inflates volumes and possibly complexity | routing logs; compare per-FTE and complexity-adjusted | mistaken capacity creation |
| anticipation / readiness pilot | Gamma pilot pre-Jul; managers change practices | pre-period contaminated, attenuates effect | check Jun dip in use, interviews, practice logs | understates or overstates |
| survey nonresponse | 20-45% response, no counts | unknown sign; dissatisfied may exit | obtain response n, link response to case outcome, wave analysis | false comfort on CX |
| measurement aggregation | only branch-month medians/p90, no distributions or event logs | hides tail mechanism, overprecise SEs | case-level event data, quantile plots, SPC | median gain hides tail harm |
| small number of branches (N=5, T=8) | cluster-robust SEs with 5 clusters unreliable | overstates precision | wild bootstrap, leave-one-branch-out, synthetic placebo | false significance |
| dynamic treatment effects | benefit grows with fidelity; naive TWFE mixes early/late | TWFE can flip sign under heterogeneity | cohort-specific event study, never use single TWFE coefficient | single X% claim invalid |

## 8. Rollout recommendation
`expand with an embedded evaluation`

Do not issue a single X% cycle-time claim and do not expand blindly to both branches at once. Instead phase Delta first while holding Epsilon as concurrent control, then cross over, creating a stepped-wedge that preserves operations and identifies effects net of common shocks. Delta is the better next site because its volumes and complexity are lower and it gained FTE in June, leaving headroom to absorb extra referrals without the overload that confounded Alpha; Epsilon's high complexity (38%) and fragile satisfaction make it a riskier first move. Randomize order publicly if both must go, or explicitly sequence Delta August-September readiness then go-live, Epsilon 6-8 weeks later conditional on guardrails. Require minimum 8-12 weeks pre-period with stable measurement and 12-16 weeks post-period to separate early disruption from mature fidelity, given use took 3-4 months to reach 45%+. Collect case-level event logs with triage path, timestamps, complexity, fraud-desk handoff and wait, specialist queue length, rework loops, staffing hours and overtime, plus survey sampling frame and response counts. Monitor spillovers daily: fraud-desk arrivals/wait by origin branch, arrivals per FTE, escalation and p90 by complexity stratum. Guardrails: pause if p90 rises >10% vs control-adjusted baseline for two consecutive weeks, escalation exceeds +4pp, rework rises, or csat response-adjusted mean drops >0.15. Decision rule pre-registered: scale to Epsilon only if Delta shows complexity- and workload-adjusted median gain with no p90 or csat deterioration versus Epsilon, replicated in mature-use weeks; otherwise redesign triage thresholds and fraud-desk capacity before any further rollout.

modC = smf.ols('p90_cycle_h ~ automated_fast_path_pct + C(branch) + C(month) + high_complexity_pct + arrivals', data=df).fit(
    cov_type='cluster', cov_kwds={'groups':df.branch})
print("\nModel C (intensity, p90):\n", modC.summary())

# 6-8. warnings and assumptions as comments/prints
print("\nWARNING: only 5 branch clusters; cluster-robust SEs are unreliable (use wild bootstrap / placebo).")
print("WARNING: dependent variables are aggregated medians/p90/percentages; OLS treats them as continuous branch-month outcomes, not case-level durations.")
# CAUSAL ASSUMPTIONS (not tested by regression): parallel trends conditional on controls,
# no anticipation, no spillover via fraud desk/routing, exogenous dose, stable survey response.

## 10. Final claim ladder
- Observed: Median and p90 cycle times, rework, escalation and satisfaction all deteriorated across treated and untreated branches in March-June after the fraud desk, routing shift and complexity rise, then partly recovered in July-August, with the largest median and p90 recoveries in adopting branches at highest fast-path use.
- Suggestive: Mature automation use is associated with relatively faster medians (roughly 3-5 hours more recovery than never-treated branches for Gamma), but absolute p90 remains above pre-rollout levels and early post-adoption months showed tail worsening.
- Conditionally causal: If parallel trends conditional on complexity, workload and month shocks held and fraud-desk spillovers were modest, availability caused a modest median reduction and a 1-3pp escalation increase; these conditions are not verifiable with five branches and missing queue data.
- Not supported: A single statement such as automation reduces cycle time by X% for Delta/Epsilon, or any claim of sustained rework reduction, tail improvement, or customer-satisfaction gain.
