# 3. Risk assessment

* **Bottleneck risk (Moderate under normal operations; High under surge conditions):** With a 4.2-minute median service time and 3.5-minute mean pickup from `Bot_Rules`, Mira is not currently throttling daily flow. By contrast, `Vendor_Verify` (2.1h service time), `Vendor_Pay` (1.3h), and `Priya_Legal` (51 min) impose far heavier active/waiting durations per case. However, because 920 bot fallouts, 360 temp escalations, and 300 specialist returns funnel through one queue, an unexpected spike in `Fraud Screen` flags or `Vendor_Verify` discrepancies could rapidly saturate her single-threaded capacity.
* **Coordination risk (Moderate to High):** Mira routinely routes work directly to `Carla`, `Diego`, `Elise`, and `Farid` while bypassing `Alice_TeamLead` and `Bruno_TeamLead` (only 4–5% co-case overlap). Consequently, regional Team Leads may lack visibility into nearly half the work entering their adjusters' queues, only discovering complex cases late in the lifecycle at `Approve Settlement`. Conversely, abruptly severing Mira’s brokerage risks uncoordinated direct referrals from adjusters/temps to `Omar_Medical` and `Priya_Legal`.
* **Key-person risk (High — empirically demonstrated):** No other resource replicates Mira’s activity vector or cross-team connectivity. The 6-day absence provides direct operational proof: while standard straight-through/Team-Lead flows remained insulated, exception queues stalled without automated fallback or Team Lead takeover, pushing 90th-percentile exception cycle times from 8.4 to 13.9 days (+5.5 days).
* **Compliance/control risk (Moderate — structural vulnerability, not proven misconduct):** In a regulated claims environment, the compliance reviewer’s concern merits attention: Mira logs problem triggers (`Reopen Claim`, `Escalate Complaint`), resolves `Bot_Rules` fraud-screen exceptions, and exercises discretion in selecting specialists (`Omar`, `Priya`) and adjusters via an non-standard `Assign Specialist` step. There is **no evidence of improper steering or wrongdoing**, and major financial controls remain segregated (`Alice`/`Bruno` execute `Approve Settlement`; `Vendor_Pay` executes `Issue Payment`). Nevertheless, concentrating exception classification, fraud-screen overrides, and specialist selection in one unaudited manual role weakens segregation-of-duties (SoD) transparency.
* **Workload fairness risk (Low to Moderate):** Mira distributes volume relatively evenly between Team North (`Carla`: 350, `Elise`: 290) and Team South (`Diego`: 330, `Farid`: 280). However, because her cases carry high exception rates (0.48) and rework/missing-info burdens, invisible injections into adjuster queues—if uncounted by `Alice` and `Bruno` during routine assignments—could create hidden complexity imbalances, especially for junior adjusters (`Elise` and `Farid` already have 0.35–0.36 exception rates, slightly higher than senior adjusters at 0.29–0.33).
* **Data-quality risk (Moderate):** Three log anomalies threaten naive SNA conclusions: (1) `Assign Specialist` is logged by Mira but missing from the official 14-activity taxonomy, potentially inflating her handover/subcontracting counts relative to `Alice`/`Bruno`; (2) brief administrative touches distort unweighted centrality vs. working-together metrics; and (3) long asynchronous SLA turnaround times at `Vendor_Verify` and `Vendor_Pay` may be conflated with service time if `lifecycle:transition` (`start` vs. `complete`) is inconsistently recorded across internal vs. external systems.

# 5. Recommendation

### What should be changed immediately
1. **Eliminate the single point of failure via designated backup coverage:** Cross-train `Alice_TeamLead`, `Bruno_TeamLead`, or a designated senior adjuster (`Carla`/`Diego`) on Mira’s exception queue and specialist-dispatch procedures, establishing an explicit SLA failover rule in `Bot_Rules` whenever the exception desk queue exceeds an aging threshold or Mira is out of office.
2. **Standardize event logging and auditability for exception routing:** Formalize `Assign Specialist` in the process taxonomy (or align Team Lead and Coordinator UI screens so all manual assignments log identical events with standardized reason codes), and require structured reason codes whenever `Bot_Rules` `Fraud Screen` flags are manually updated.
3. **Establish lightweight segregation-of-duties (SoD) guardrails:** For high-severity cases (`Escalate Complaint`, `Fraud Screen` overrides, or high `amount_band`), implement rule-based specialist routing criteria or periodic post-hoc compliance sampling rather than relying solely on undocumented individual discretion.

### What should be monitored
1. **Queue aging prior to exception triage and specialist review:** Track real-time waiting time (90th percentile) between `Bot_Rules` / `Temp_1` / `Temp_2` handoffs and first human pickup on `exception_flag=True` claims.
2. **Automation and vendor-interface fallout rates:** Monitor the weekly rate of `Bot_Rules`  `Mira` fraud-screen loops and `Temp_1`  `Vendor_Verify`  `Mira` escalations to spot rule miscalibration or vendor verification defects that can be automated away.
3. **Complexity-adjusted adjuster workloads:** Track the volume and exception share of cases injected directly by Mira into junior (`Elise`, `Farid`) vs. senior (`Carla`, `Diego`) adjuster queues alongside `Alice` and `Bruno`’s routine assignments so Team Leads have full WIP visibility.

### What should not be changed until more evidence is available
1. **Do not remove or dismantle `Mira_Coordinator`’s exception-desk function:** Eliminating her role without first codifying her tacit triage logic into `Bot_Rules` and expanding `Alice`/`Bruno`’s cross-team capacity would recreate the 6-day absence failure mode (90th-percentile exception cycle times spiking to ~14 days) and likely flood `Omar_Medical` and `Priya_Legal` with unscreened, incomplete referrals.
2. **Do not force all exception traffic through regional Team Leads yet:** Until waiting/service-time and cross-region handover analyses clarify whether Mira balances regional capacity and shields `Alice` and `Bruno` to focus on `Approve Settlement` and `Vendor_Pay` releases, rerouting her 960 events to Team Leads risks creating a new settlement-approval bottleneck.

# 6. Short executive summary

`Mira_Coordinator` is neither a routine team lead nor merely a clerical "queue cleaner." While she touches only 22% of all claims—making her appear peripheral in day-to-day team co-working metrics—she handles 64% of reopened claims, 71% of complaints, and nearly half of complex exceptions. She acts as the operational bridge connecting automated screening (`Bot_Rules`), temporary staff, external verification fallouts, medical/legal specialists, and regional adjusters. Her value was proven during a recent six-day absence: routine claims flowed normally, but complex claims backed up severely, pushing worst-case turnaround times from 8.4 to 13.9 days.

This creates two main business risks: **key-person dependency** (no backup exists when she is away) and **control visibility** (her routing criteria and fraud-screen updates are largely undocumented, and direct assignments can bypass Team Lead workload oversight). Management should **not** eliminate her role today, as doing so would stall exceptions and overload scarce specialists. Instead, immediately train backup coverage, standardize reason-code logging for specialist and fraud-exception routing, give Team Leads visibility into her adjuster assignments, and tune automated rules to reduce avoidable manual handoffs.
