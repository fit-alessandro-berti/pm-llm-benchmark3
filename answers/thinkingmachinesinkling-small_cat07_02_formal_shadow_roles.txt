# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

**Routine Front Office (R01, R02)**  
Core: high-volume entitlement validation and standard response/closure (A2: ~390–415; A4: ~310–335; A12: ~260–280). These are routine, low-to-medium complexity, and tightly coupled to sequence fragment 1. Peripheral: light diagnosis (A3: 60–75), occasional pricing (A7: 5–18), customer negotiation touchpoints (A8: 25–42), and reopen cleanup (A13: 4–8). Authority is executional, not decisional: they do not hold approval rights (A9: 0) and rarely perform quality gatekeeping (A11: 3–6). Substitution risk: temporary agents can cover A1/A2/A4/A12, but R01/R02 provide the certification-backed validation that prevents reopen sequences (fragment 6).

**Technical Analysts (R03, R04)**  
Core: diagnosisconfigurationimplementation (A3: 255–260; A5: 235–240; A10: 215–220). Authority is technical; they decide configuration and implement. Peripheral: pre-check validation (A2: 18–24), security-adjacent review for complex cases (A6: 6–24, with Chen doing more critical cases), knowledge updates (A14: 11–14), and reopen handling (A13: 7–15). Complexity is central to role definition: Chen’s median is 0.71 with 42 critical cases versus Dina’s 0.48 with 9; therefore, they are not interchangeable at the critical tail despite 0.94 similarity.

Core: A6 Security Review (96 events, but linked to 55 critical cases), plus complex diagnosis (A3: 16), security-context implementation (A10: 18), and quality/knowledge updates that document security exceptions (A11: 8; A14: 9). Peripheral: negotiation on critical cases (A8: 4) and reopen (A13: 6). Authority is rare and high-stakes: R13 filters referrals to protect Erik’s time, implying that not all security-related cases belong in A6—only those passing a broker-filter. Substitution is impossible; weeks 7–8 show that when Erik’s capacity is mismanaged by proxy, critical cases wait >2 days.

**Commercial Specialist (R06)**  
Core: pricing exceptions and related negotiation (A7: 122; A8: 90), with preliminary approval preparation (A9: 16) and validation checks (A2: 22). The substitution episode (Fatima absent, R07 covering) demonstrates that core pricing work can be shadowed, but at a cost: duration doubles, indicating lower efficiency without formal commercial authority.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

Shadow and bridging roles are organizational adaptations—not errors or improprieties. They create resilience but also hidden dependencies.

**R07_Giorgio — Commercial Shadow / Hybrid Front-Office Agent**  
- **Shadow status**: He holds “entitlement, pricing” certifications, yet his A7/A8 volume is ~60% of Fatima’s and he is not formally titled Commercial Specialist. During Fatima’s 6-day absence (weeks 3–4), he performed 31 pricing and 18 negotiation events.  
- **Benefits**: Provides continuity; prevents pipeline collapse when commercial specialists are unavailable; handles medium-complexity pricing cases that do not require Fatima’s full authority.  
- **Risks**: Duration doubled (3874 min), suggesting lower fluency without dedicated pricing focus. If management treats him as a permanent substitute rather than a shadow, they overestimate commercial capacity and may under-staff Fatima’s role. He also creates a “shadow premium”: complex cases are routed to him because of his hybrid profile, but he lacks Fatima’s critical-case volume (36 vs 25 critical, but lower pricing specialization).

**R13_Mateo — Cross-Functional Escalation Broker**  
- **Bridging status**: He touches security (A6), pricing (A7), negotiation (A8), approval (A9), technical (A3/A5), quality (A11), and coordination (A13). His similarity to Erika (0.39) is low precisely because he is not a specialist; his similarity to Hana (0.54) is moderate because both handle escalations, but with different authority.  
- **Benefits**: Prevents specialists from being interrupted by low-risk referrals (weeks 7–8: false escalation complaints decreased because Mateo filtered referrals). Covers Hana during audit (week 5). Coordinates reopen cases (fragment 5) that span technical, commercial, and supervisory domains.  
- **Risks**: Authority ambiguity is dangerous: he performed 21 preliminary approvals, 13 of which required Hana’s recheck. If broker workload increases, either approval quality drops or supervisor bottleneck worsens. He is also a hidden dependency for critical paths (median complexity 0.77).

**R05_Erik — Rare but Critical Specialist (Invisible Anchor)**  
- **Shadow/critical status**: Not a shadow in the replacement sense, but a “shadow resource” for organizational planning because his event count (96 A6, 55 critical) is too low for standard staffing formulas.  
- **Benefits**: Protects the critical tail; his scarcity is a feature (only true security cases reach him after brokerage). Knowledge updates and quality checks tied to security ensure compliance is documented.  
- **Risks**: Zero substitution pathway. Weeks 7–8 demonstrate that if brokerage (Mateo) is over-aggressive in filtering, critical cases wait >2 days; if under-aggressive, Erik is overwhelmed. He must not be diluted with routine A1/A2/A12 work; his 0.86 median complexity demands protected time.

**Additional shadow/bridging observations**  
- **R14_Nora — Automation Boundary Shadow**: She shadows the front-office validation step but cannot handle complexity >0.11 median. If bot validation is trusted blindly by temporary agents, incomplete notes propagate to human agents (reopen sequence 6).

Their substitution caused 17 reopen events, proving that shadow execution without shadow expertise creates downstream cost.

import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load / format the dataframe for PM4Py analysis
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Assumes CSV with headers: case:concept:name, concept:name, time:timestamp,
# org:resource, case_complexity, customer_segment, product_line,
# temporary_substitution, escalation_level
df = pd.read_csv("service_event_log.csv")

# PM4Py formatting expects standard column names (may vary by version;
# use format_dataframe to enforce consistency)
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    resource_key="org:resource"
)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Discover organizational roles (activity-frequency / sequence based)
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# This yields clusters of resources with similar activity profiles.
roles_df = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(
    df,
    resource_key="org:resource",
    activity_key="concept:name"
)
# Inspect, but do NOT treat high-frequency clusters as authority.
# Cross-reference with case complexity before labeling.

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Activity-based resource similarity (cosine over normalized counts)
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
sim_df = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
    df,
    resource_key="org:resource",
    activity_key="concept:name"
)
# Filter pairs with high similarity but different complexity exposure.
# Example: R03/R04 = 0.94; do NOT assume interchangeability
# because Chen’s critical count (42) is 4.7× Dina’s (9).

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Handover-of-work and working-together networks
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
    df,
    resource_key="org:resource",
    activity_key="concept:name"
)
working = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(
    df,
    resource_key="org:resource"
)
# Inspect cross-functional edges: R13R05 (routing security), 
# R13R08 (approval handover), R07R06 (shadow pricing).

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Join role discovery with complexity and substitution indicators
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Aggregate per resource for decision-making, not pure clustering.
resource_stats = df.groupby("org:resource").agg({
    "case_complexity": lambda s: s.mode()[0] if not s.empty else "Unknown",
    "temporary_substitution": "max",  # True if any substitution
    "concept:name": "count",
    "product_line": lambda s: s.nunique()
}).rename(columns={"concept:name": "event_count"})

# Map discovered roles back to statistics
merged = roles_df.merge(resource_stats, left_on="org:resource", 
                        right_index=True, how="left")

# Separate substitution episodes from baseline roles
substitution_episodes = df[df["temporary_substitution"] == True]
# Check whether substituted activities have same complexity mix.
sub_complexity = substitution_episodes.groupby("org:resource")["case_complexity"].mean()

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Inspect sequences to avoid authority confusion
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
variants = pm4py.get_variants(df)  # or custom aggregation
# For key fragments:
# Fragment 1 (botfrontclose): dominated by R14 and R01/R02/R11/R12
# Fragment 3 (security): includes R05 after R13 filtering
# Fragment 4 (pricingapproval): R06R07 shadow or R06R08 authority

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. Explicit guardrails: do NOT over-interpret frequency clusters
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# (a) Weight intensity by median case complexity.
# (b) Build a decision-authority matrix: which resources hold A9, A7, A6?
# (c) Flag temporary substitution events; do NOT add them to role core.
# (d) Compare handover volume to working-together volume:
#     High handover + low working-together = broker / shadow.
#     High working-together + high handover = core specialist team.
# (e) Reject clusters that merge low-complexity high-volume agents 
#     (R11/R12) with certified generalists (R01/R02) solely on A2/A4 counts.

# Optional: filter to critical cases and re-run role discovery

critical_df = df[df["case_complexity"] == "Critical"]  # or score-based
