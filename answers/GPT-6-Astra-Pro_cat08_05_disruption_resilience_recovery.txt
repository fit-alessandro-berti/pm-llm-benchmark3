## 1. Disruption timeline

**The wholesaler demonstrated partial, uneven resilience—not complete recovery.** Critical hospital supply was protected better than other service segments, but the adaptations also weakened traceability and transferred work into reconciliation and audit queues. A recovered hospital cycle-time median therefore does not establish either portfolio-wide service recovery or restored control effectiveness.

**Interpretation.** At T4, pharmacy and bulk p90 cycle times remain approximately **51% and 67% above baseline**, respectively, while traceability exceptions remain **2.75 times baseline**. The hospital median’s return to eight hours masks both long-tail delays and residual control weakness. These comparisons are descriptive; changed case mix could explain part of them.  

**Temporal and counting limitations matter.** The T2 bulk p90 of 180 hours exceeds the manual phase’s duration. The summaries therefore cannot safely be interpreted as orders both started and completed entirely within each phase. Analysis needs arrival cohorts, event-level operating modes, observation cutoffs and treatment of unfinished orders. Unequal phase lengths also prevent interpreting case totals as directly comparable throughput rates.  

DFG frequencies are transition counts, not automatically unique-case counts or complete accounting balances. For example, T4’s 750 `Cancel order → END` transitions divided by 35,000 cases give approximately 2.1%, not the reported 3.0% cancellation rate. Different windows, cohort definitions, omitted transitions or data problems could explain this; the supplied evidence does not resolve it.

## 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

**Resilience means maintaining the mission through a controlled alternative—not merely moving products faster.** An alternative can deviate substantially from the normal activity sequence while preserving its authorization, safety, custody and accountability functions. Conversely, a fast order can represent deferred risk rather than successful adaptation.

The strongest resilience candidates are **B’s manual verification, J’s explicit prioritization, and K/L’s reconciliation and assurance work**. Their value depends on trustworthy evidence and adequate staffing. **I** is defensible capacity rationing when deferral is bounded. **A** is mission-aligned when urgency changes queue position without removing required controls. The remaining manual execution patterns—**D, E, F, G and H**—are conditional substitutes whose quality depends on freshness, coverage and evidence strength. The source supports both their operational benefits and their limitations. 

**C is the clearest shortcut to retire:** its justification is an assumption about authorization rather than verification. The undocumented form of G is similarly unacceptable as a planned fallback. Management may accept slower electronic posting or a different evidence medium; it should not equate those choices with accepting absent authorization or an untraceable handoff. Product/customer risk appetite can govern service sequencing and permissible evidence methods, but it cannot by itself establish that an omitted required control was satisfied.  

### What the DFGs establish—and what they do not

T2 contains 1,800 `Manual pack → Dispatch without scan` transitions and 1,650 `Manual pack → Temporary release control` transitions. Thus approximately **52.2% of these two listed outgoing transition occurrences** follow the direct route. That is a strong signal of bypassing a **recorded intermediate release activity**, but it is not proof that 52.2% of orders received no physical check: DFG aggregation cannot recover complete traces, unlogged checks or product-specific eligibility. 

Likewise, the fall in fitness to 0.71 and fitting traces to 44% signals major behavioral change, not a direct estimate of unsafe shipments. `Phone proof of delivery` may be a legitimate alternative activity for an appropriate order, whereas a missing temperature-control record raises a different, substantive assurance question. Activity-label deviations and control failures must be assessed separately. 

For each apparent missing control, distinguish:

* **Control performed, evidence captured contemporaneously, entered later:** potentially valid execution with recording delay.
* **Control reportedly performed, but evidence cannot be verified:** an unresolved assurance gap.
* **Control not performed, or performed only after the required decision:** a substantive deviation that later documentation cannot erase.

Missing temperature documentation does not prove a temperature excursion; missing authorization evidence does not prove the customer was unauthorized. Neither absence can simply be treated as harmless. “Concealed” here describes risk hidden by logs or dashboards—not demonstrated intent to mislead.

### The deferred-work mechanism

`Spreadsheet invoice hold → END` occurs 2,100 times during T2, while T4 contains 2,300 `Invoice close → Audit remediation` transitions. These are not equivalent forms of successful completion, nor do they identify a linked cohort of the same orders. They show why observed trace endings and financial closure cannot substitute for assurance closure.  

The most defensible interpretation is that some service was preserved by **shifting verification and correction work downstream**. That can be reasonable where original controls were preserved and only posting was delayed. It becomes concealed noncompliance where the deferred work was actually a prerequisite for safe release. The supplied temporal findings support a long post-close tail, but do not quantify its labor cost or establish which adaptation caused each exception. 

## 4. Recovery process model

The following is a proposed design, not a reconstruction of what already occurred. It assumes designated incident, quality/compliance and order owners, with explicitly allocated capacity. The file supplies no jurisdiction-specific exemptions or validated numerical thresholds for data freshness, backlog age or restoration testing; those limits must be approved before an incident rather than improvised during one.

### Mode triggers and exit gates

| Mode transition                                          | Explicit trigger or gate                                                                                                                                                                   | Required response                                                                                                                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Normal → controlled emergency operation**              | WMS unavailable, or a critical authorization, stock, scanning or transaction-integrity check fails.                                                                                        | Declare the incident; identify affected functions; suspend unsafe automated actions; activate only trustworthy fallback sources and approved manual variants.      |
| **Emergency processing → affected-order hold**           | Authorization cannot be verified; required stock/lot identity is uncertain; a required pre-dispatch control is absent; or an approved source exceeds its freshness limit.                  | Hold the affected order or stock scope. Escalate urgent needs and investigate a verified alternative fulfillment route. Do not let priority waive the failed gate. |
| **Manual → partial digital recovery**                    | A specific component passes integrity and functionality checks.                                                                                                                            | Restore that component within a defined scope. Keep one authoritative reservation record and prevent duplicate entry or replay across manual and digital channels. |
| **Partial recovery → stabilized processing**             | End-to-end test orders, stock/lot reconciliation and scan-to-ledger checks pass over a pre-agreed observation window; required control capture works.                                      | Expand normal processing in stages, retain fallback capability and revert affected functions when checks fail. Intake availability alone is insufficient.          |
| **Operational restoration → incident assurance closure** | Legacy discrepancies have accountable dispositions, critical gaps are resolved or recorded as confirmed deviations with completed response actions, and agreed backlog conditions are met. | Close the incident’s assurance work separately from technical restoration. Never convert an irrecoverable historical omission into “control performed.”            |

Where no controlled fallback or verified alternative supplier/facility is available, record and escalate the unmet supply need. Alternative capacity is a contingency option to establish—not a resource demonstrated by the supplied evidence.

### Order-processing flow

Arrows indicate sequence; bracketed questions are decision gates. Reconciliation is a linked workstream initiated by manual transactions, not a task postponed until all deliveries finish.

```text
Receive order
  -> Assign persistent order and order-line IDs
  -> Triage documented urgency, customer need, product risk and age
  -> Verify customer/product authorization using a trusted source
  -> Check product/recall eligibility and relevant restrictions
  -> Confirm stock and create an exclusive, recorded reservation
  -> Pick with item/lot/quantity evidence
  -> Pack with required handling evidence
  -> Complete release decision and product-specific pre-dispatch controls
  -> [All controls due at this stage satisfied and evidenced?]
       No  -> Hold -> restore the missing control or use a verified alternative
       Yes -> Dispatch with contemporaneous custody/handoff evidence
             -> Monitor required transport conditions
             -> Obtain product-appropriate proof of delivery
             -> Record operational delivery completion
             -> Invoice close, or remain in an explicit invoice-hold state

For every manual transaction:
  Preserve original evidence
    -> Reconcile against the clean restored system
    -> [Records and control evidence agree?]
         Yes -> Verify the reconciled record and preserve provenance
         No  -> Open a discrepancy/control-deviation task
                 -> Assess product/customer impact
                 -> Correct records and take necessary response actions
                 -> Verify disposition
    -> Update assurance status independently of delivery/invoice status
```

Cancellation is a separate outcome requiring reservation release and reconciliation of any already-executed actions. It must not be counted as successful delivery. Similarly, an invoice hold remains an open state rather than an artificial successful `END`.

### Minimum controls by risk

These are proposed internal control floors derived from the process’s authorization, release and traceability requirements—not assertions of external legal requirements.

| Scope                                          | Required emergency controls                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Every order**                                | Verified customer/product eligibility; identifiable items, quantities and relevant lots; controlled reservation; attributable pick/pack checks; a documented release decision; courier/handoff evidence; appropriate delivery proof. Record who performed each required control and when.                      |
| **Ambient**                                    | All common controls remain applicable. Lower temperature-handling complexity does not justify assumed authorization or undocumented dispatch.                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Cold-chain**                                 | Verified handling and transport arrangements; identified temperature-evidence source; required pre-dispatch checks; contemporaneous temperature capture through the relevant custody period; defined response to missing evidence or excursions. Sampling must not replace required temperature documentation. |
| **Controlled-substance**                       | Documented checks by two distinct authorized people where dual control is required; secured custody and handoff; recipient authorization; complete quantity and identity linkage. A single pharmacist release check does not itself establish dual control.                                                    |
| **Recall-sensitive**                           | Reliable lot identity, a sufficiently current recall-status source, and an order–lot–recipient linkage adequate to locate affected deliveries. A morning stock snapshot alone does not establish current recall eligibility.                                                                                   |
| **Hospital-critical overlay**                  | Faster access to verification, release staff and verified transport alternatives—not relaxed product controls.                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Pharmacy-standard / Wholesale-bulk overlay** | Explicit service commitments, communication of deferral and age escalation. Permit reassessment when documented customer need is more urgent than its default segment label suggests.                                                                                                                          |

Apply the **union of applicable requirements** to mixed-risk products and mixed-product orders. One compliant line must not mask an uncontrolled line.

### Triage, backlog and capacity rules

Prioritize control-eligible orders by documented consequences of delay, time to required supply and waiting age. Product risk determines the necessary expertise and controls; it should not automatically outweigh every urgent lower-risk order.

Make every deferral and priority override attributable. Introduce aging escalation and customer-group service floors, with explicit incident-command approval when exceptional critical demand makes a floor temporarily infeasible. Preserve original receipt time so reprioritization cannot reset waiting age.

Allocate capacity to **current critical supply, aging backlog, and reconciliation/remediation**. The allocation may change each shift, but remediation labor must remain visible. Otherwise hospital protection can repeatedly consume the staff needed to reduce the next day’s control and backlog risks.

### Evidence capture and assurance closure

Use serialized paper forms or controlled offline records that capture order/line IDs, item/lot/quantity, source version, actor and reviewer identities, event occurrence time, recording time, evidence reference, courier/recipient details and applicable product-control results. Retain correction history.

For inventory, use the initial snapshot only as an opening balance. Record every reservation, pick, cancellation and adjustment against one controlled ledger; escalate mismatches and perform risk-based physical checks. Freshness limits should reflect stock movement and product risk.

For event-log ingestion, preserve **original activity, normalized meaning, operating mode, execution method and evidence status**. Do not relabel a reconstructed manual handoff as a scanner event that never occurred.

Finally, distinguish:

**Operational delivery complete → commercial status → assurance status.**

A case may be delivered while reconciliation remains open. A historical failure can eventually be investigated and its corrective actions completed, but its record must remain “confirmed deviation,” not “retrospectively compliant.” Missing evidence for an already-dispatched high-risk product requires an impact assessment and justified containment or customer response—not merely insertion of a missing event.

## 5. Resilience indicators

Measure indicators by **operating mode, arrival cohort, customer group and product risk**, using a fixed observation cutoff. Product-specific percentages need product-specific eligible denominators; cold-chain and controlled-substance rates must not be applied to all orders or added together.

| Indicator                           | Leading signal                                                                                                                           | Lagging measure and interpretation                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Throughput**                      | Control-eligible work awaiting each stage versus staffed capacity; projected release bottlenecks.                                        | Unique orders or order lines dispatched and delivered per operating hour. Report total output alongside output verified to meet due controls. Keep dispatch, delivery and assurance completion separate.             |
| **Service protection**              | Critical demand approaching its required supply time without confirmed stock, release capacity or transport.                             | Fill rate, on-time fulfillment and median/p90 cycle time by segment. Define fill consistently—units, lines or complete orders—and do not compare a new definition directly with the reported 91%/61% figures.        |
| **Traceability**                    | Orders approaching handoff with missing lot, quantity, custody or recipient-link fields.                                                 | Dispatched orders with at least one required traceability gap divided by eligible dispatched orders. Separate late-entered verified evidence from unverifiable links.                                                |
| **Control completeness**            | Missing or expired prerequisites at the point where they become due; unavailable independent reviewer or temperature-evidence mechanism. | Completeness by product and control: authorization, release, temperature and dual control. A future delivery proof is not “missing” before delivery, but absent pre-dispatch authorization is a gate failure.        |
| **Backlog age**                     | Open orders approaching an escalation threshold; oldest deferred order; backlog inflow exceeding clearance.                              | Open count/quantity, age median/p90/maximum, and completed waiting time. Measure age from original receipt and show canceled orders separately rather than making them disappear from service assessment.            |
| **Assurance debt**                  | New unresolved obligations per shift versus verified closures; unowned critical tasks; oldest missing-evidence task.                     | Open affected cases **and** open control tasks, age, severity and remediation labor hours. | **Rework and correction**           | Reservation conflicts, duplicate identifiers, inconsistent quantities and failed reconciliation checks.                                  | Distinct corrected-case rate, correction events per case, repacking and labor hours. Distinguish detected pre-release errors from errors discovered after dispatch.                                                  |
| **Fairness across customer groups** | Projected service-floor breaches; aging orders repeatedly bypassed; undocumented priority overrides.                                     | Fill, cancellation and waiting-time differences across groups, also compared within similar urgency/product-risk strata. Assess whether disadvantages are justified, proportionate and temporary.                    |
| **Resource resilience**             | Availability of trained compliance staff, dual-control coverage, trusted fallback data, evidence devices and qualified transport.        | Activation time, mode reversions, specialist hours diverted from ordinary work and downstream delay. Show whether a fast lane simply relocates the bottleneck.                                                       |
| **Risk-aware conformance**          | Live violations of required precedence, reviewer independence, source validity and evidence obligations.                                 | Evaluate traces against both the normal model and an approved emergency model. Report critical-control violations separately from aggregate fitness and activity-label mismatches.                                   |

The T2 fill-rate difference is **30 percentage points** between Hospital-critical and Pharmacy-standard. This is a meaningful distributional warning, not proof of unfairness: need, stock availability, product mix and baseline fill rates are not supplied. The reported pharmacy deterioration nevertheless warrants explicit review rather than being hidden behind hospital results.  

Two measurement safeguards are essential. First, preserve unfinished orders through open-age reporting and, where appropriate, censored time-to-completion analysis; cancellation is a competing outcome, not successful completion. Second, do not improve emergency-model fitness merely by declaring every workaround acceptable: manual variants must satisfy independently defined control obligations.

Baseline rates are comparators, **not permission to miss required controls**. The proposed dispatch rule is no unapproved absence of a control due before dispatch; historical defects and recording delays should remain separately visible.

Evaluation should test the proposed mechanisms rather than assume them. For example, compare traceability and total labor burden for controlled manual handoffs against the current scan-free variant, accounting for product/customer mix and incident stage. Examine whether authorization capacity limits critical fulfillment and whether aging rules reduce pharmacy cancellations without unacceptable critical-supply loss.

Do not experimentally remove required controls to obtain a cleaner comparison. The appropriate optimization target is **safe fulfillment plus total recovery burden**, not dispatch speed alone.

## 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should accept authorized manual methods, transparent posting delays and bounded deferral of genuinely noncritical demand when these protect urgent supply. It should not accept assumed authorization, absent required release or product controls, undocumented custody, or invoice closure presented as assurance recovery. The evidence supports retaining the hospital-priority principle, not granting it an exemption from controls. Verified contemporaneous records showing that apparently missing controls actually occurred would justify reclassifying some deviations as recording failures. Evidence of actual unauthorized release, temperature failure, untraceable controlled products, persistent risk-adjusted pharmacy disadvantage or a growing assurance backlog would justify tighter holds and withdrawal of affected workarounds. Expansion of emergency processing should depend on demonstrated control equivalence, sustainable staffing and a declining, visibly managed recovery burden—not the hospital median alone.
