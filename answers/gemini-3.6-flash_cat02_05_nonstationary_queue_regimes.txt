# 1. Data audit and estimands

## Operational Context and System Definitions
The service center operates under a fixed daily schedule from **07:00 to 19:00** (a 12-hour or 720-minute operating window). System metrics are aggregated daily over a four-week period (28 consecutive days, 2026-04-06 through 2026-05-03).

The primary queueing estimands and operational parameters are defined as follows:
*   **Operating Window ($T$)**: $T = 12\text{ hours/day} = 720\text{ minutes/day}$.
*   **Arrival Volume ($A_d$) & Intensity ($\lambda_d$)**: Total count of case arrivals during opening hours on day $d$. Nominal hourly arrival rate is $\lambda_d = A_d / 12\text{ cases/hour}$.
*   **Completion Volume ($C_d$) & Throughput ($\mu_d$)**: Total count of completed cases during opening hours on day $d$. Nominal hourly completion rate is $\mu_d = C_d / 12\text{ cases/hour}$.
*   **Work-In-Progress ($L_d$ or `mean_wip`)**: Time-weighted average number of active and waiting cases in the system during open hours, calculated from $N=48$ discrete 15-minute snapshots ($t \in \{07:15, 07:30, \dots, 19:00\}$).
*   **Service Time ($S$)**: Active handling time spent by staff working on a case, excluding waiting time in queue. Summarized per day via median ($S_{\text{med}}$) and 90th percentile ($S_{\text{p90}}$).
*   **Staffing Capacity ($C_{\text{fte}}$)**: Mean concurrently available full-time equivalent staff (`staff_fte`).
*   **Case Complexity (`complex_pct`)**: Percentage of daily arrivals following a multi-step complex variant.
*   **Intraday Peak Concentration (`peak_08_10_pct`)**: Percentage of daily arrivals occurring between 08:00 and 09:59.
*   **Flow Time / Turnaround Time ($W$)**: Total elapsed time a case spends in the system from arrival to completion ($W = W_q + S$, where $W_q$ is queue wait time). *Note: $W$ is not directly recorded in the daily aggregate table.*

## Analytical Limitations and Threats to Naive Inference

Naive statistical modeling or unsegmented queueing analysis on this dataset faces severe inferential threats:

1.  **Off-Hours Carry-Over Backlog and Boundary Censoring**: The system closes at 19:00 while cases remain in queue or in progress ($L_{19:00} > 0$). These cases carry over overnight without active processing. Consequently, daily completed counts ($C_d$) include cases that arrived on prior days ($d-1, d-2$), violating the closed-system assumption that arrivals and completions within a single 12-hour daily window form a self-contained queueing system.
2.  **Substitution of Service Time for Flow Time**: Active service time ($S_{\text{med}} \approx 24\text{--}36\text{ min}$) represents purely touch time. Treating $S_{\text{med}}$ as a proxy for total turnaround time $W$ ignores queueing delay $W_q$. In periods of high queue congestion, total flow time $W$ exceeds active service time $S$ by several hours.
3.  **Non-Stationarity from Unobserved Case-Mix Shift**: A major structural shift occurs around 2026-04-20, where `complex_pct` jumps from $\sim 20\%$ to $\sim 44\%$. This shift causes median active service time to increase by $40\%$ (25 min to 35 min) and p90 service time to increase by $75\%$ (46 min to 84 min). Pooling data across this boundary masks the underlying shift in work content per case.
4.  **Transient Outage Disruption and Forced Catch-Up Distortion**: On 2026-04-15, a 180-minute outage halted completions ($\mu_{11:00\text{--}14:00} = 0$). This generated a transient backlog spike ($L_d = 47$). Subsequent days (2026-04-16) showed elevated throughput ($C_d = 129$) supported by elevated staffing (7.0 FTE). Treating 2026-04-16 as an equilibrium baseline misinterprets temporary backlog draining as higher steady-state productivity.
5.  **Intraday Demand Burstiness and Non-Poisson Arrivals**: Hourly arrival vectors reveal extreme non-Poisson clustering. On 2026-04-21, 34 out of 115 daily arrivals (29.6%) occurred in a single hour (10:00–11:00). Uniform hourly queueing models ($\text{M/M/}c$) severely underestimate waiting times when arrival processes exhibit high Fano factors ($D > 9$).
6.  **Violation of Little’s Law Equilibrium**: Applying Little's Law ($L = \lambda W$) to daily aggregates assumes flow balance ($\lambda \approx \mu$) and stationary WIP. Between 2026-04-20 and 2026-04-28, daily arrivals consistently exceeded completions ($\lambda > \mu$), causing WIP to accumulate systematically from 37 to 50. Calculating $W = L / \lambda$ during queue buildup yields invalid flow-time estimates.
7.  **Confounding and Aggregation Bias (Simpson's Paradox Reversals)**: Aggregating across weekdays and weekends, or across low- and high-complexity regimes, generates misleading correlations. For example, higher staff levels (7.0 FTE) coincide with higher WIP (43–52) during outage recovery and intervention periods. Naive regression would erroneously conclude that adding staff increases queue backlog.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

## Alternative Segmentation: Operational Capacity-Demand Balance Matrix
Instead of chronological boundaries, this alternative scheme segments the system into four **Operational Capacity-Demand States** based on instantaneous system physics: Workload Demand Intensity vs Active Staffing Supply.

```
                        DEMAND INTENSITY (Arrivals & Case Complexity)
                        Low Demand / Low Complex    High Demand / High Complex
                     +----------------------------+----------------------------+
       Standard Staff|  STATE A: Balanced Baseline|  STATE B: Saturated Queue  |
       (5.5 - 6.0 FTE|  (R1 Weekdays)             |  (R4 Unassisted Weekdays)  |
CAPACITY             +----------------------------+----------------------------+
SUPPLY  Augmented    |  STATE C: Outage Recovery  |  STATE D: Managed Draining |
        (6.5 - 7.0 FTE|  (04-16 Recovery)          |  (R5 Intervention Weekdays)|
                     +----------------------------+----------------------------+
```

### State Definitions and Operational Rules
1.  **State A (Balanced Baseline)**: Workload demand within nominal capacity. $\lambda_d \le 105$, `complex_pct` $< 30\%$, FTE $\in [5.5, 6.0]$. Characterized by stable WIP ($L \le 25$), near-zero overnight queue accumulation, and $S_{\text{med}} \le 26\text{ min}$.
2.  **State B (Saturated / Queue Building State)**: Workload demand exceeds standard staffing capacity. $\lambda_d > 105$, `complex_pct` $> 40\%$, FTE $\in [5.5, 6.0]$. Characterized by $\lambda_d > \mu_d$, service times spiking ($S_{\text{med}} \ge 34\text{ min}$, $S_{\text{p90}} \ge 78\text{ min}$), and WIP accumulating at $10\text{--}15$ cases/day.
3.  **State C (Outage Interruption & Transient Recovery)**: Sudden capacity loss or recovery phase. Characterized by `outage_min` $> 0$ or single-day completion surges ($\mu_d > 125$) driven by elevated carry-over WIP ($L > 45$) following a disruption.
4.  **State D (Augmented Draining State)**: High demand / high complexity paired with expanded staffing (FTE $\ge 6.5$). Characterized by $\mu_d > \lambda_d$, high throughput ($\mu_d \approx 120$), and declining WIP trajectory.

### Comparison of Segmentation Schemes

| Dimension | Primary Chronological Segmentation | Alternative State-Matrix Segmentation |
|---|---|---|
| **Operational Focus** | Causal history, chronological change points, intervention audit. | Real-time queueing state, dynamic capacity balance, automated routing rules. |
| **Strengths** | Directly evaluates the impact of specific events (April 15 outage, April 20 mix shift, April 29 staffing intervention). | Highlights structural state transitions; applies cleanly to real-time control without needing fixed date boundaries. |
| **Weaknesses** | Treats transition days as discrete block cutoffs despite continuous carry-over backlogs. | Blurs temporal context; mixes post-outage recovery with planned staffing intervention if parameters overlap. |
| **Primary Use Case** | Executive performance audit, post-mortem process mining, policy assessment. | Automated workforce management, dynamic shift scheduling, SLA breach warning system. |

## Four Operational Paradoxes Created by Naive Pooling

### 1. The Productivity Fallacy (Simpson's Paradox Reversal)
*   *Pooled Observation*: Daily completion rates are highest on days with very high WIP ($C_d = 129$ on 2026-04-16 when WIP = 52; $C_d = 126$ on 2026-04-30 when WIP = 36).
*   *Naive Deduction*: High queue backlog drives higher staff productivity.
*   *Segmented Reality*: High throughput on 2026-04-16 was caused by adding staff (7.0 FTE) to clear carry-over backlog from the previous day's outage. Staff productivity per FTE hour was actually lower due to queue management overhead. Pooling confuses forced backlog draining with baseline operational efficiency.

### 2. The Case-Mix Masking Reversal
*   *Pooled Observation*: Active service median appears stable around $31\text{ min}$ across the month.
*   *Segmented Reality*: The service center experienced two distinct operating regimes: $S_{\text{med}} = 25\text{ min}$ in Weeks 1–2 vs $S_{\text{med}} = 35\text{ min}$ in Weeks 3–4. Pooling conceals a $40\%$ increase in task processing time caused by `complex_pct` jumping from $20\%$ to $44\%$.

### 3. The Capacity Headroom Illusion
*   *Pooled Observation*: Mean arrival rate is $92.5\text{ cases/day}$ ($7.7\text{ cases/hour}$). With 6.0 FTEs working 12 hours (72 gross staff-hours/day), capacity seems ample.
*   *Segmented Reality*: On high-complexity weekdays (Regime 4), arrivals averaged $114\text{ cases/day}$ ($9.5\text{ cases/hour}$), requiring $114 \times 35\text{ min} = 66.5\text{ hours}$ of active touch time. Accounting for staff breaks and administrative overhead ($\sim 80\%$ max labor utilization = 57.6 effective hours), effective workload exceeded maximum supply, driving steady queue growth.

### 4. Dilution of Peak Intraday Arrival Strain
*   *Pooled Observation*: Average peak arrival fraction (`peak_08_10_pct`) is $32.4\%$.
*   *Segmented Reality*: Weekday peak arrival fraction increased from $29.6\%$ in Regime 1 to $45.9\%$ in Regime 4. On 2026-04-27, $50\%$ of daily arrivals (60 cases) arrived in the first two operating hours. Pooling dilutes this morning arrival surge with weekend low-peak data ($18\%$).

### Operational Significance of Overdispersion
A standard Poisson arrival process assumes $D = 1.0$. On 2026-04-21, $D = 9.67$ demonstrates extreme overdispersion driven by batch arrivals. Standard stationary queueing equations ($\text{M/M/}c$) underpredict waiting times during these arrival surges.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little's Law states $L = \lambda W$, where $L$ is time-average WIP, $\lambda$ is long-run arrival rate, and $W$ is mean flow time. Applying Little's Law to 12-hour daily aggregates requires:
1.  **Conservation of Flow**: Long-run total arrivals equal total completions ($\lambda = \mu$).
2.  **Stationarity**: Mean WIP $L$ must be stable over the estimation window ($L_{\text{start}} \approx L_{\text{end}}$).
3.  **Boundary Consistency**: Minimal carry-over backlog relative to total volume.

### 1. Dynamic Shift Scheduling and Peak-Smoothing Strategies
*   **Peak-Aligned Staffing**: Shift baseline staffing from uniform daily coverage to staggered schedules. Concentrating staff during peak arrival windows (08:00–10:00 and 13:00–14:00, where $D=9.67$) prevents morning queue accumulation.
*   **Smoothing Demand**: Implement appointment slotting and intake throttling for non-urgent arrivals to flatten intraday surges, targeting a Fano factor $D < 2.0$.

### 2. Queue Isolation and Case-Mix Triage
*   **Dual-Queue Architecture**: Separate processing paths for simple (`complex_pct` $\approx 0$) and complex cases. Assigning simple cases to an "Express Lane" prevents simple tasks from waiting behind multi-step complex cases ($S_{\text{p90}} = 84\text{ min}$).
*   **Complexity-Based Staff Rostering**: Monitor `complex_pct` dynamically. When complex cases exceed $35\%$ of daily arrivals, adjust base staffing from 6.0 to 7.0 FTE to prevent queue accumulation.

### 3. Outage Protocols and Automated Backlog Recovery
*   **Trigger-Based Overtime**: Establish an automated protocol: if 12:00 WIP exceeds 35 cases, deploy 1.0 FTE float capacity for the afternoon shift.
*   **Post-Outage Isolation**: Following IT outages (e.g., 2026-04-15), dedicate extra FTE capacity (7.0 FTE) exclusively to clearing carry-over backlog, preventing new arrivals from experiencing extended wait times.

### 4. Process Mining and Data Architecture Enhancements
To move beyond daily aggregate estimates, instrument the service center system to capture granular event logs:
*   Log exact micro-timestamps for: `Case_Start`, `Queue_Entry`, `Handling_Start`, `Handling_End`, and `Queue_Exit`.
*   Record worker IDs and pause intervals to measure active service time vs wait time directly.
*   Track overnight carry-over state explicitly by logging WIP at 07:00 opening.
