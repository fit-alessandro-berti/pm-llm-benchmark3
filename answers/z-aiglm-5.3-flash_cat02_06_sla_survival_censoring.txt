# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

**Survivor / length bias.** A "completed cases only" view keeps the 13 resolutions (mean  49.8 h; median 41 h) and silently discards **19 cases**: all 9 opens (S09 92 … S31 23), all 3 transfers, and all 7 W/R. The discarded set is where time lives: the entire observed long tail (S09 92, S11 81, S25 79, S26 104) is open, and two late resolutions (S13 110) survive into the sample only because they happen to have finished. Conclusion "median 41 h, comfortable against 72" is an artifact of selection on completing. Against it: the **deterministic** accounting gives  9/32 = 28.1% certain breaches (and 50% of cases provably failed to *receive resolution by 72* once W/R before 72 are counted under broad E1).

**Informative censoring.** Transfers concentrate exactly where slowness concentrates: S08 escalates then transfers at 68 h (one hour inside the SLA), S32 at 96 h; both are P1/P2 escalations. If transfers are triggered by stalled escalations or scarce experts, censoring-at-random (CAR) fails: censored cases carry above-average latent slowness, so T1 understates E1. Similar concern for young opens: S11 (age 81, gap 55) and S25 (79, 48) show long silence windows; if stale cases are preferentially overlooked or force-closed around cutoffs, even *administrative* censoring correlates with frailty. **Hypothesis H-IC (testable with timestamps):** censoring hazard  in {Escalated, long gap} states.

**Competing risks done naively.** Hand computation (code is authoritative): treating W/R as ordinary censoring, KM puts Pr[resolve  72]  0.68; the Aalen–Johansen CIF treating W/R as *competing* events puts it  0.31, with CIF_w  0.15 and CIF_r  0.10 of the risk set consumed by early exits before 72. An A-vs-B contrast therefore mixes policy effect with calendar era, case mix, and compliance self-selection; it is intent-to-treat at best, and *non-randomized* ITT at that.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

Method-by-method fit to *these* data (n = 32, heavy ties, 6 of 32 censored before , 3 ambiguous transfers):

| Method | Verdict here |
|---|---|
| Kaplan–Meier (single endpoint) | Appropriate **only** for E3-type "any-disposition-free" survival. Using it for resolution (censoring W/R) is the §3 trap. Also degrade gracefully to exact finite-population accounting where censoring is absent. |
| **Aalen–Johansen CIF (primary)** | Correct machinery for E1/E2/E4: keeps W/R as competing causes, admits scenario-specific transfer coding, and lets me report *deterministic* known parts (9 certain breaches) beside model-implied masses. With n = 32 the curve is lumpy; I therefore pair it with **bounds** and treat CIs (bootstrap or exponential-Greenwood) as indicative only. |
| Cause-specific hazards (Cox) | Specified for growth-stage reuse (priority, cohort, stage toggles). Explorable descriptively now; trustworthy inference needs more cases. |
| Subdistribution hazards (Fine–Gray) | **Not recommended as primary at n = 32**: variance blowup, dependence on a fitted censoring model, and unstable with cause-specific counts of 3–4 per cause. Revisit when event counts grow ~10×. |
| Restricted mean survival time ( = 96) | Useful robust scalar for E3 (mean undisposed lifetime within window). Must respect a minimum risk-set size (cap  automatically and disclose the cap). Avoids proportional-hazards assumptions entirely — attractive given the visibly crossing-regime hazard structure (fast exits early, stalls late). |
| **Landmarking (secondary; operations backbone)** | Dynamic prediction at t  {24, 48, 60} using *attained state* (stage, has-escalated-so-far, inactivity gap). Naturally avoids the immortal-time/leakage problems of §5 because predictors are conditioned on survival-to-landmark and measured pre-landmark. Directly yields actionable "probability of breaching from where this case sits now." |
| Multi-state model | Conceptually the right home (§5 diagram) but **blocked by data**: stage-entry times are interval-censored (often unbounded), so intensities are only weakly identified today. Specify now, estimate after timestamps arrive (§9). |

**Primary recommendation.** Dual-number reporting under E1 and E3: (i) the deterministic partition (certain breach / certain clear / unknown, overall and by cohort/priority), and (ii) scenario-banded AJ estimates at 72 h (E1 = 1  CIF_res(72); E3 = overall AJ survival at 72), with T1/T2/T3 bands and worst-case bounds. This is honest about what is *observed* versus *assumed*, which matters more than estimator elegance at n = 32.

**Secondary.** Landmark models at 24/48/60 h for alerting; RMST(96) as a summary; cause-specific Cox as exploratory. PM4Py supplies variant/stage features feeding the landmark covariates.

# 7. Operational interpretation

**From curves to alerts.** Landmark models at 24/48/60 h produce P(unresolved at 72 | stage, gap, priority, elapsed). Alert tiers (illustrative thresholds, to be calibrated, not causal parameters):

| Trigger (mid-flight state) | Probabilistic reading | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Stage = Escalated, gap > 36 h (cf. S11 55, S25 48, S26 72-gap) | elevated Pr[T_disposition > 72]; empirically these dominate the certain-active set | human chase of the escalation owner; escalate-out decision deadline |
| Stage = Review, age > 48, no gap reset | moderate breach risk | queue jockeying / batch review |
| Approaching transfer with age  60–70 (cf. S08 @68) | high chance of exiting scope pre-deadline | transfer-readiness packet + external SLA contract triggered *before* handoff |

Workload: forecast expected undisposed volume at 72 h =  Pr(overdue-aging)  staffing/expedite slots. Governance: monthly AJ CIF breach curves with scenario bands; transfer-reconciliation registry (external outcomes fed back, shrinking the T-band); frozen, versioned threshold changes; pre-registered calibration reports.

**Calibration & fairness.** Group-wise Brier score and reliability curves by priority (P1/P2/P3) and cohort; equalized-opportunity-style parity on alert misses/false alarms; watch the P1 asymmetry discovered here (3/8 certain-breach, 0 hygiene exits, high transfer share): if P1 alerts over-fire because escalations are noisy alarms, capacity diverts from P3 cases whose 72-h resolution share is lowest (2/10 certain). Explicitly reject *causal* phrasing: "cases with an escalation stall breach more often"  "canceling escalations fixes SLAs."

def base_records(frame):
    """Endpoint atoms. Codes: res=1, W=2, R=3, transfer=4, censor=0.

    recs = []

                rows.append((t, 1, "res"))
            elif ty in ("W", "R", "X"):
                rows.append((t, 1, ty))                # competing causes
            else:
                rows.append((t, 0, None))
    return rows

def aj_estimate(rows, tau):
    """Inline Aalen-Johansen. rows: (t, delta, cause). Returns dict of
    CIF per cause at tau and overall survival S(tau). Hand-rolled so the
    script needs no survival library (lifelines-equivalent outline in
    header comment)."""
    times = sorted({round(t, 6) for t, d, _ in rows if d == 1})
    S, cif, out = 1.0, {}, {}
    for tt in times:
        n = sum(1 for t, *_ in rows if t >= tt)
        dc = {}
        for t, d, c in rows:
            if abs(round(t, 6) - tt) < 1e-9 and d == 1:
                dc[c] = dc.get(c, 0) + 1
        if n <= 0:
            break
        for c, d in dc.items():
            cif[c] = cif.get(c, 0.0) + S * d / n
        S *= (1.0 - sum(dc.values()) / n)
        out["t"] = tt; out["n_risk"] = n
        if tt > tau:
            break
    return {"S_tau": S, "n_risk_last": out.get("n_risk"),
            **{f"CIF[{c}]": v for c, v in cif.items()}}

def cif_at(est, cause, tau):
    return est.get(f"CIF[{cause}]", 0.0)

def rmst_active(rows, cap=RMST_CAP, min_risk=MIN_RISK):
    """Restricted mean undisposed time: area under overall AJ survival."""
    pts, S = [(0.0, 1.0)], 1.0
    times = sorted({round(t, 6) for t, d, _ in rows if d == 1})
    for tt in times:
        n = sum(1 for t, *_ in rows if t >= tt)
        if n < min_risk:
            break
        d_tot = sum(d for t, d, _ in rows if abs(t - tt) < 1e-9)
        S *= (1 - d_tot / n)
        pts.append((tt, S))
        cap_eff = tt
    cap_eff = min(cap, pts[-1][0])
    area, prev_t, prev_s = 0.0, 0.0, 1.0
    for t, s in pts:
        if t > cap_eff: break
        area += (t - prev_t) * (prev_s + s) / 2
        prev_t, prev_s = t, s
    if prev_t < cap_eff: area += (cap_eff - prev_t) * prev_s
    return area, cap_eff

    certain clear / unknown for broad E1 and for E3 active-at-72."""

                c += 1; ca += 1                    # ... but E3 certain-exited

                E1_lb=b / len(sub), E1_ub=(b + u) / len(sub),
                E3_known_active=ba, E3_unknown=ua,
                E3_lb=ba / len(sub), E3_ub=(ba + ua) / len(sub))

# 9. Limitations and additional data

Prioritized data requests, each with what it unlocks:

1. **Exact per-event timestamps** (highest value). Converts stage entry from interval-censored/unbounded to observed; enables the §5 multi-state intensities, escalational staleness clocks (S11-type 55 h silences become dated), business-hours decomposition (tests **A1**), and separating waiting-time from touch-time. Without them, every state-transition quantity stays a bound.
2. **External outcomes after transfer.** Even sparse linkage (did S08/S21/S32 resolve externally, when?) collapses the T1–T3 scenario band into measurement, resolving the widest interval in the study and settling whether transfers are losses or hand-offs.
3. **Submission dates and exact cutoff datetime.** Permits era adjustment (cohort B is later-born), arrival-volume context for the 104/92 h stragglers, seasonal/weekday hazard terms, and temporal validation splits that respect the A/B boundary rather than pooling eras.
4. **Time-varying workload.** Queue lengths, staffed capacity per stage: candidate confounders for the escalation-stall signal and natural instruments/exclusion restrictions for policy evaluation; enables congestion-sensitive alert thresholds (alerts during crunches otherwise generate thrash).
5. **Assignment mechanism and rollout compliance ledger.** Case-level start dates for the AutoCheck policy plus eligibility flags turn H-COMP into either a stepped-wedge/difference-in-discontinuities design or an IV story; without it, cohort contrasts remain non-randomized ITT with unknown confounding direction.
6. **Sample size and design.** n = 32 with 3–4 events per cause makes any hazard model decorative; ~10× volume would activate cause-specific/subdistribution regressions and subgroup calibration with acceptable variance.
7. **Smaller items:** reason codes for withdrawal/rejection (distinguishing customer-driven churn from quality-driven closure changes E1's interpretation); repeat-request linkage (within-customer clustering); external SLA contracts per receiving organization (makes T2 a measurable governance endpoint rather than a scenario).

**Standing assumptions to revisit:** A1 (contiguous clock hours), A2 (terminal-anchor semantics of `duration_or_age_h`), A3 (prefix chronology) are all plausible and internally consistent (all terminal rows show `last_event_gap_h` = 0), but each should be confirmed against source extraction logic before results are operationalized.
