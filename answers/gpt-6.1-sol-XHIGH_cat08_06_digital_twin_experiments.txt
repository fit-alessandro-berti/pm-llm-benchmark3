## 1. Model scope and state variables

Build a calendar-aware, stochastic discrete-event simulation with shared resources and shared events between cases. PM4Py supplies log preparation, process discovery, conformance diagnostics, and validation summaries; a separate simulation engine executes queues, calendars, dependencies, and interventions. The process tree is a starting control-flow specification, not the complete executable twin.

The primary boundary runs from an offered application through submission, municipal review, and the first issue/reject decision. For D1 and D7, extend the boundary upstream to include applicant preparation and appointment delay. For appeals, continue to final resolution as a separate episode linked to the original case. Report both first-decision duration and final-resolution duration; otherwise reducing one while increasing appeals could look like improvement. Include applicant and consultant response, notice periods, inspection travel, and relevant council dates. Exclude construction after permission, except where prior violations determine eligibility or a later audit measures the safety of an inspection exemption.

Use minutes for active work and scheduling, with an explicit municipal time zone and dated calendars. Portal submissions can occur continuously. Statutory notice periods use their applicable calendar-day/business-day rules; human work consumes staffed working time. Track these clocks separately.

## 2. Parameter-estimation plan

### Audit and prepare the log

Obtain event semantics, lifecycle conventions, observation boundaries, and application snapshots before interpreting timestamps. Preserve repeated activities and raw ordering, normalize activity names, distinguish automated from human work, and resolve time zones. Identify duplicate events, delayed uploads, resource aliases, missing IDs, and incomplete or left-truncated cases. Keep unresolved uncertainty visible.

Audit whether these reflect posting timestamps, exceptional work, or example-data simplification; do not silently add weekend capacity.

There is a larger capacity consistency problem. If all 31,200 cases require Planning review and its median **active service** is 3.5 hours, at least half contribute 3.5 hours, giving at least 54,600 planner-hours over 18 months. Eight planners provide roughly 25,000 weekday hours before holidays, drafting, or rework. The stated p90 strengthens this discrepancy. Thus the volume, service-time definition, and staffing cannot all describe a sustainable operation with the stated relatively short durations. Resolve possible elapsed-time/service confusion, units, partial case coverage, outsourcing, or omitted staffing. Until resolved, retain competing parameter interpretations and label results conditional. Do not manufacture waiting-time adjustments to hide an overloaded model.

### Arrivals and case attributes

Estimate submissions from each case's first genuine submission event, using exposure-corrected counts by month, day of week, hour, type, and district. Do not use average inter-arrival time as the sole demand model. Fit a seasonal marked non-homogeneous Poisson model as a starting point, then test overdispersion and autocorrelation against clustered alternatives.

One useful structure is a background arrival stream plus architect-level burst processes. Estimate burst initiation, batch size, within-batch timing, and correlated marks from architect/week/project groups. Jointly calibrate both components so total arrivals reproduce 31,200 over 18 months and the top 20 architects' 31% share. Do not add a separate 31% burst stream on top of an already calibrated total. Retain residual overdispersion through a Cox/negative-binomial specification or block-resampled arrival weeks if supported.

Estimate joint case attributes, rather than independently drawing district, value, required reviews, and type. Preserve the supplied 45/28/17/10% type mix as a calibration target, allowing mix to change by season or scenario. Normalize seasonal/day weights against actual exposure: the 1.7 peak-season multiplier and Monday/Wednesday ratio of 2.2 are relative effects, not absolute rates. The other months and weekdays need estimates or explicit assumptions. Eighteen months provide limited evidence for annual trends and only limited repeated seasonal observations.

2. Start-to-completion elapsed span.

Infer the remaining effort/wait split only under stated priors or scenario ranges. Do not estimate many interchangeable service and queue parameters solely to fit case durations.

Queue waiting should emerge from demand, readiness, resources, calendars, and dispatch rules. Add explicit non-resource release delays only where justified, such as applicant readiness, a booking lead time, or a notice publication gate.

Estimate routing conditionally on case attributes and policy period. Mandatory Fire, Environmental, and notice rules override probabilistic routing. Estimate how previous rework changes later service and routing; do not assume every loop is an independent identical retry.

## 5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment

### Initialization and run length

This is a transient, seasonal system. There is no single stationary warm-up period that justifies discarding the same number of days from every run.

For a live decision, initialize on a specified date with actual WIP: case attributes, age, current stage, partial work, outstanding external responses, booked inspections, and shared groups. Sample residual service/response times conditional on elapsed time rather than restarting a full duration. Use information available at the initialization date, avoiding future-event leakage. No artificial warm-up is needed if this state is credible; include uncertainty in reconstruction.

If only a synthetic initial state is possible, run 12–24 months of baseline seasonal history before the intervention, extending until phase-matched backlog and age distributions cease depending materially on the empty start. Compare corresponding months across cycles, not a smoothed flat average. If workload exceeds effective capacity, an annual stable state will not appear; report that result and use explicit transient starting backlogs instead of declaring convergence.

Use at least a 24-month policy horizon to observe two peak cycles. Follow the fixed intake cohorts beyond that horizon, initially allowing a further 12 months and extending when the heavy tail requires it. Retain unfinished cases as right-censored and report their count, age, and unresolved-work status. Do not compute duration quantiles from completed cases alone. If a p90 is not identifiable because too many cases remain unresolved, report a bound or “not yet estimable.” Include rollout and hiring ramps; do not start D4 with fully trained extra capacity unless that is the actual plan.

### Replications and random numbers

Use approximately 30 pilot replications to estimate variance and computational cost. Set the main replication count before confirmation, typically 100 and more for noisy tails. Choose it to achieve useful precision, such as a paired p90-difference interval half-width below 5% of baseline p90 and an SLA-difference half-width below 2 percentage points. If a practical maximum is reached, publish the wider intervals and defer close decisions. A pilot-informed fixed main sample avoids unadjusted repeated significance checks.

Use independent replication seeds with paired common random numbers across policies. Generate the offered-project world once per replication, including correlated marks and shared shocks. Key later draws by replication, case/group, activity, attempt, and draw purpose; omitting policy from a common draw key allows meaningful pairing even when a policy skips work. Keep policy-specific mechanisms on separate named streams. D1/D7 transform entry timing from that shared world rather than creating unrelated demand samples. Preserve a seed/parameter/calendar manifest.

### Intervals and uncertainty layers

Distinguish three sources:

- **Simulation noise:** paired replication confidence intervals for policy differences. Use replication-level resampling for quantiles, SLA rates, and fairness metrics; do not pretend all cases are independent observations.
- **Parameter uncertainty:** an outer loop of fitted-parameter draws or season-aware cluster/block bootstraps, with inner simulation replications. For example, use 100–200 parameter sets and allocate enough inner runs to distinguish parameter variation from simulation noise. Report combined uncertainty intervals. Call them credible intervals only if they come from an explicit Bayesian model.
- **Structural uncertainty:** alternative effort/wait splits, arrival clustering, objection explanations, missing routing rules, and calendar interpretations. Report results separately or combine only with defensible weights; an arbitrary mixture is not a posterior probability.

For log resampling, preserve architect/project clustering and calendar-season structure. Whole-case independent bootstrap samples would lose the dependencies that matter here. Use simultaneous or multiplicity-adjusted intervals when selecting among many policies, and confirm finalists on unused seeds and validation periods.

### Heavy tails and sensitivity

Use survival methods for censored external delays and case durations; compare empirical-body plus tail models with plausible lognormal/mixture alternatives. Do not winsorize long cases merely to stabilize results. Apply only genuine administrative deadlines or abandonment rules as truncation mechanisms, and distinguish their outcomes from completed permits.

Screen sensitivity to service means/tails, dependence strength, no-shows, travel savings, adoption, objection correlation, mandatory-review imputation, fire capacity interpretation, and meeting/duty-block placement. Then investigate interactions among the dominant factors. Report p95/p99, extreme backlog, and “still open after one year” rates where estimable. Robustness should cover plausible adverse assumptions, not just alternate random seeds. Cost estimates and staffing needs should carry their own uncertainty.

## 6. Validation strategy

### Face and rule validation

Walk through concrete cases with clerks, planners, reviewers, inspectors, legal staff, and applicant representatives. Include a clean Simple case, missing documents, concurrent Commercial reviews, a revision that invalidates Fire results, objection-linked neighbors, an inspection bundle with a no-show, council closure, an urgent exception, and an appeal. Check skill eligibility, statutory timers, priority, travel, and D2 eligibility. Obtain specific agreement on what starts a notice period and what invalidates prior approvals.

Verify invariants: no decision before valid prerequisites, no unqualified resource, no ordinary work in a closed calendar, no double-booked inspector, nonnegative work/wait intervals, conserved resource time, and no mandatory review silently omitted. Check that a shared shock affects all linked cases and that bundle dispatch respects the maximum hold. Assess conformance against the expanded executable specification as well as the nominal tree.

### Event-log replay validation

Use observed arrivals, observed case attributes, and known routing choices to test whether the model can reproduce feasible event sequences and readiness. Compare PM4Py alignments/variants with historical traces, accounting explicitly for abstract loop labels, missing events, and incomplete cases. Completion ordering does not prove that parallel activities were serialized.

Use two replay modes: a constrained replay to diagnose control flow and calendar feasibility, and a stochastic replay with actual arrivals but simulated work/resources to evaluate the timing mechanism. Feeding historical waits back into the engine could produce a convincing replay while bypassing the queue model; it is not predictive validation. The supplied calendar conflicts should appear as audit exceptions.

### Distributional validation

Compare observed and simulated counts, route shares, missing-document cycles, review combinations, rejection/appeal rates, and durations. Use medians, p90/p95, survival curves, and monthly cohort completion rates by type, variant, district, and architect-size group. Check the seven supplied variant-group shares and quantiles, while recognizing that they overlap type classifications and are not an independent arrival specification.

Also compare Monday queue spikes, seasonal backlog, applicant-response tails, notice extensions, and joint outcomes of related cases. An aggregate duration match can hide wrong queueing mechanisms and unfair subgroup behavior. Pre-agree practical tolerances, for example central-duration error around 10%, route-share error around 3 percentage points, and tail error around 15–20% when observation uncertainty supports those tolerances. These are proposed acceptance ranges, not evidence that the model meets them.

### Resource-utilization and bottleneck validation

Compare busy worker-hours with independent HR/time-study totals, known absence/duty calendars, and inspection route records. Calculate utilization against eligible staffed hours, separating service, travel, interruptions, and non-permit duties. Completion-only logs cannot establish active utilization by themselves. Revisit the Planning workload inconsistency before accepting a calibrated baseline.

Check whether queues grow in the observed locations and seasons: clerk backlog after Monday bursts, Environmental accumulation, inspection travel effects in D3/D5, and Legal congestion around council weeks. Compare throughput, queue-age distributions, and correlations between adjacent bottlenecks. Use small capacity perturbations to inspect whether the predicted bottleneck migration is operationally credible.

### Out-of-sample and intervention validation

Fit on the earliest approximately 12 months and test on the subsequent 6 months, with rolling-origin checks where feasible. Preserve policy/calendar changes and guard against using later outcomes to infer earlier case eligibility. The holdout is shorter than a year, so seasonal extrapolation remains uncertain; supplement it with seasonal hindcasts and report that limitation.

Validate a policy change on an observed natural experiment or a controlled pilot when possible, preferably with cluster allocation for architects/neighborhoods where spillovers exist. D2 requires eligibility and safety evidence; D9 requires actual objection/liaison measurements. Historical predictive accuracy does not validate their causal effects. Begin in shadow mode, compare new arrivals and backlog weekly, and recalibrate on a defined schedule with versioned parameters and drift alerts.

## 7. Decision criteria

Select a feasible, robust Pareto set rather than proclaiming an exact optimum. Rank finalists on total applicant journey, municipal capacity, quality, fairness, and cost. Apply hard legal/safety constraints first; then compare benefits and uncertainty. The following are proposed decision thresholds for municipal agreement, not supplied statutory SLAs.

|---|---|---|

Do not accept a hidden increase in upstream waiting or appeal-resolution tails. |

If weights are unavailable, use scenario-specific results and maximum regret. |

Measure fairness with common case-mix weights and sufficient follow-up; show the unadjusted figures too. Architect appointment access and batching should not reward only frequent submitters. Report variation in intervention uptake, including applicants who cannot use the checker or appointment system.

For D2, the event log cannot establish the risk of defects that an omitted inspection would have detected. Require verified eligibility and a pilot/audit sufficient for the municipality's chosen safety bound before broad rollout. For D8, ensure triage never removes a mandatory review. For D5, preserve final legal responsibility. These are conditions arising from the proposed policies, not benefits assumed by the simulation.

A practical decision package contains paired effect intervals, cohort/SLA results, seasonal backlog plots, utilization and bottleneck changes, costs, fairness, and sensitivity rankings. If unresolved service/calendar interpretations change the preferred policy, the immediate decision is to resolve those inputs or run a discriminating pilot. Favor reversible pilots for close alternatives; no precise ranking is justified by an uncalibrated twin.

def observed_timing_columns(frame: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    out = frame.copy()
    end = pd.to_datetime(out["time:timestamp"], utc=True)
    previous = end.groupby(out["case:concept:name"]).shift(1)
    out["recorded_event_gap_h"] = (end - previous).dt.total_seconds() / 3600

    # Optional columns must come from audited lifecycle pairing/readiness.
    def timestamps(name):
        if name in out:
            return pd.to_datetime(out[name], utc=True)
        return pd.Series(pd.NaT, index=out.index, dtype="datetime64[ns, UTC]")

    start = timestamps("start_timestamp")
    ready = timestamps("ready_timestamp")
    service_span = (end - start).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    queue_span = (start - ready).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    out["service_elapsed_h"] = service_span.where(service_span >= 0)
    out["queue_wait_elapsed_h"] = queue_span.where(queue_span >= 0)
    # Elapsed service may include nights, interruption, or external work.
    # Active work requires work segments or an independently justified measure.
    if "reported_active_work_minutes" in out:
        effort = pd.to_numeric(out["reported_active_work_minutes"], errors="coerce")
        out["service_work_h"] = (effort / 60).where(effort >= 0)
    else:
        out["service_work_h"] = np.nan
    return out

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Policy:
    name: str
    settings: dict = field(default_factory=dict)


class KeyedStreams:
    """Reproducible named draws; common keys deliberately omit policy name."""
    def __init__(self, replicate_seed: int):
        self.seed = replicate_seed

    def rng(self, *key) -> np.random.Generator:
        payload = repr((self.seed, key)).encode("utf-8")
        entropy = int.from_bytes(blake2b(payload, digest_size=16).digest(), "little")
        return np.random.default_rng(entropy)
        # Generate one vector per key, or include an explicit draw counter.


@dataclass
class Case:
    case_id: str
    offered_minute: float
    attributes: dict
    group_id: str
    version: int = 0
    attempts: dict = field(default_factory=dict)
    status: str = "offered"
    submitted_minute: float | None = None


@dataclass
class World:
    cases: list[Case]
    neighborhood_shocks: dict
    initial_wip: list[Case] = field(default_factory=list)


@dataclass
class Calendar:
    origin: pd.Timestamp  # UTC origin; production windows are localized first.
    weekly: dict         # weekday -> [(opening minute, closing minute), ...]
    closed_dates: set[str] = field(default_factory=set)
    overrides: dict = field(default_factory=dict)  # date -> dated working windows

    def next_window(self, now: float) -> tuple[float, float]:
        """Next eligible window as simulation minutes, including interruptions."""
        today = (self.origin + pd.Timedelta(minutes=now)).normalize()
        for offset in range(730):
            day = today + pd.Timedelta(days=offset)
            date = day.date().isoformat()
            windows = self.overrides.get(date)
            if windows is None:
                windows = [] if date in self.closed_dates else self.weekly.get(
                    day.weekday(), []
                )
            for opening, closing in windows:
                left = (day + pd.Timedelta(minutes=opening) - self.origin).total_seconds() / 60
                right = (day + pd.Timedelta(minutes=closing) - self.origin).total_seconds() / 60
                if right > max(now, left):
                    return max(now, left), right
        raise ValueError("Extend the calendar or resolve unavailable capacity")

        """Joint seasonal background + architect clusters + correlated marks/shocks."""

    def entry_delay_minutes(self, case: Case, policy: Policy,
                            rng: np.random.Generator) -> float: ...

class MunicipalDispatcher:
    def __init__(self, env, specs: list[WorkerSpec]):
        self.env = env
        self.specs = specs
        self.locks = {s.worker_id: simpy.Resource(env, capacity=1) for s in specs}
        self.waiting_jobs = []

    def execute(self, job: Job):
        """Implement a skill-aware, aging-aware shared queue.

        Choose a qualified worker atomically; consult next_window before work.
        Process only within dated windows. Pause resumable work at closure,
        release the worker, and requeue remaining work; do not charge nights
        as effort or hold an unused worker through off-duty periods.
        A site visit requires a feasible contiguous appointment/travel slot.
        Record readiness, queueing, work segments and resource assignments.
        Worker specs include Monday support, two Simple-only planners,
        Environmental duty blocks, holidays/council closures and peak hires.
        Urgent council exceptions need a job-specific calendar eligibility view.
        """
        raise NotImplementedError("Implement the calendar-aware dispatcher")
        yield self.env.timeout(0)  # identifies this interface as a generator

class RoutingAndRework:
    @staticmethod
    def required_reviews(case: Case) -> list[str]:
        a = case.attributes
        # Missing structural flags must be supplied by a documented imputation
        # or conservative scenario before simulation; missing is not False.
        reviews = ["Planning review"]
        if a["requires_fire"] or a["area_triggers_fire"]:
            reviews.append("Fire-safety review")
        if a["requires_environment"] or a["sensitive_district"]:
            reviews.append("Environmental review")
        return reviews

    @staticmethod
    def inspection_exempt(case: Case, policy: Policy) -> bool:
        a = case.attributes
        return bool(
            policy.settings.get("D2", False)
            and a["case_type"] == "Simple renovation"
            and a["application_value"] < 50_000
            and a.get("prior_violations") is False
            and not a.get("mandatory_inspection_exception", False)
        )

    def execute(self, twin, case: Case):
        """Implement the versioned workflow, not independent trace sampling.

        1. Completeness/document-response loop, changed by D1.
        2. Spawn required review jobs concurrently; use env.all_of(events).
        3. Apply valid Planning rework; invalidate affected versioned results.
        4. Required notice waits on a shared neighborhood shock/release time.
        5. InspectionBroker handles scheduling/inspection/rework unless D2
           eligibility and the independent audit draw permit exemption.
        6. Draft only after all current-version required results exist.
        7. D5 can spawn legal pre-check early; final sign-off remains guarded.
        8. Legal revisions invalidate/repeat the affected work; issue/reject;
           appeals reopen a linked episode and can add further rework.

        Municipal work uses dispatcher.execute(Job(...)). Applicant and
        consultant waits use env.timeout(...) without holding workers.
        Sample each attempt with keyed case/activity/version/purpose streams.
        D6 priorities and D8/D9 work changes use the same shared dispatcher.
        """
        raise NotImplementedError("Implement approved routing and rework rules")
        yield twin.env.timeout(0)


class ArrivalGenerator:
    def __init__(self, world: World):
        self.world = world

    def run(self, twin):
        # The fitted world already contains non-stationarity and joint clusters.
        for case in sorted(self.world.cases, key=lambda c: c.offered_minute):
            yield twin.env.timeout(max(0, case.offered_minute - twin.env.now))
            twin.env.process(twin.admit(case))


class Twin:
    def __init__(self, inputs, policy, world, streams):
        self.env = simpy.Environment()
        self.inputs, self.policy = inputs, policy
        self.world, self.streams = world, streams
        self.dispatcher = MunicipalDispatcher(self.env, inputs.worker_specs(policy))
        self.inspections = InspectionBroker(self)
        self.routes = RoutingAndRework()
        self.related = defaultdict(list)
        for case in world.cases + world.initial_wip:
            self.related[case.group_id].append(case)
        self.events = []
        self.results = {}  # (case_id, activity, document_version) -> result event

    def emit(self, case, activity, lifecycle, resource=None):
        self.events.append({
            "case:concept:name": case.case_id,
            "concept:name": activity,
            "sim_minute": self.env.now,
            "lifecycle:transition": lifecycle,
            "org:resource": resource,
            "document_version": case.version,
        })

    def notice(self, case):
        shock = self.world.neighborhood_shocks.get(case.attributes["neighborhood_id"])
        release = self.inputs.notice_release_minute(
            case, self.env.now, shock, self.policy,
            self.streams.rng(case.case_id, "notice", case.version),
        )
        yield self.env.timeout(max(0, release - self.env.now))
        # notice_release_minute enforces the statutory minimum and legal calendar.

    def admit(self, case):
        delay = self.inputs.entry_delay_minutes(
            case, self.policy, self.streams.rng(case.case_id, "entry")
        )
        yield self.env.timeout(delay)  # D1/D7 can move elapsed time upstream
        case.submitted_minute = self.env.now
        case.status = "open"
        self.emit(case, "Submit application", "complete", "portal")
        yield self.env.process(self.routes.execute(self, case))

    def run(self, horizon):
        # Initial-WIP routing must resume its state and conditional residual work,
        # not start completeness again. Implement a corresponding resume method.
        if self.world.initial_wip:
            raise NotImplementedError("Add state-aware WIP resumption")
        self.env.process(ArrivalGenerator(self.world).run(self))
        self.env.run(until=horizon)
        return self.inputs.summarize(self, horizon)


def run_experiments(fitted, policies, scenarios, seeds, horizon):
    rows = []
    for scenario_name, scenario in scenarios.items():
        inputs = fitted.for_scenario(scenario)
        for seed in seeds:
            streams = KeyedStreams(seed)
            world = inputs.generate_world(
                horizon, streams.rng("offered_world", scenario_name)
            )
            for policy in policies:
                # Fresh state, shared potential demand/shocks, paired random keys.
                twin = Twin(inputs, policy, deepcopy(world), streams)
                metrics = twin.run(horizon)
                rows.append({"scenario": scenario_name, "policy": policy.name,
                             "seed": seed, **metrics})
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)
