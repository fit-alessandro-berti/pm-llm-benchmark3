**Final recommendation** (178 words)  
The data strongly suggest that Orion’s higher average cycle time is driven by a case mix rich in complex, complianceheavy work and not by inferior performance. In fact, Orion appears faster within every complexity stratum. Blindly shifting 50% of Orion’s cases to Nova risks increasing aggregate cycle time because Nova handles those same strata more slowly.  

Instead, I recommend a phased learningandimprovement plan. First, conduct deep observational diagnostics (Phase1) to confirm where groups overlap and which process variants differ. Second, run a **pilot randomized assignment of a subset of mediumcomplexity, noncompliance, dayshift cases** (Phase2) to isolate the pure group effect on service time and rework. This pilot limits operational risk and will credibly estimate whether Orion’s reviewer skill genuinely improves productivity. Third, **target the compliance step directly** (Phase3) by adding capacity to Orion’s nightshift compliance queue; this addresses the largest share of controllable wait time without disrupting the groupshift alignment. Only after these steps – and if the evidence then indicates a causal harm from Orion – should a larger reassignment be considered, and even then it should be accompanied by shiftmatching constraints to avoid confounding the outcome.

## 8. Residual uncertainty  

1. **Unmeasured case severity within complexity categories**  
   *Most valuable additional data field:* A numeric severity or claimamount field at case arrival, or a detailed checklist score assigned during Classification.  

2. **Shift effect confounded with resource group**  
   *Most valuable data:* Cases that were crossstaffed (e.g., Orion working day shift, Nova working night) to create withingroup shift variation. Alternatively, record the shift as a separate, prerouting variable (arrival time window).  

3. **Censoring due to unobserved noncompleted cases**  
   *Most valuable data:* A snapshot of all registered cases with status (open/abandoned/completed) and the time at which they left the active pool.  

4. **Timevarying queue load and arrival dynamics**  
   *Most valuable data:* Eventlevel timestamps for every activity (Register, Classify, etc.) to reconstruct queue length at the moment of each assignment, enabling a proper queuingtheory counterfactual.  

5. **Residual confounding from unobserved reviewer skill**  
   *Most valuable data:* Persistent reviewer identifiers linked to each activity, allowing withinreviewer comparisons and skillmixing analyses that strip out individuallevel heterogeneity.
