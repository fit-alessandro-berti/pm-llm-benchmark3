## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:** The 58.5 h cycle is dominated by three queue waits — D1 backlog (doc starts 11 h after triage), A1 backlog (assessment starts 9.5 h after doc completion), C1 backlog (review starts 18 h after assessment) — plus a serialized 2 h escalation and a 4.5 h from-scratch review. The same sanctions ambiguity was touched at triage, assessment, and escalation, never in parallel.

**Minimal intervention:** Add two artifacts at triage (S1 packet, provisional C1 window) and shift existing work earlier. No activity is deleted and no ordering changes: the final review still follows the final assessment (rule 5), and the prelim screen is explicitly non-substitutive (rule 10). Four-eyes is untouched (A1 vs C1).

**Temporal propagation:** (i) The escalation's 2 h leaves the post-assessment critical path because S1 advisory work may overlap checks (pool notes) — it now runs Monday against dead queue time. (ii) Prelim reading transfers 1–3 h out of Wednesday's review (4.5  1.5–3.5 h) at the cost of 1–2 h of C1 touch-time Tuesday. (iii) The reservation converts part of the 18 h compliance wait into a scheduled Wednesday-morning window (~5 h saved credibly; holding Tuesday-evening capacity idle from Monday could in theory save ~18 h but is implausibly wasteful). (iv) The D1 (11 h) and A1 (9.5 h) waits are untouched and cap the total gain at roughly a third of the ~23 h post-assessment segment. Assessment itself may shorten ~0.5 h because A1 receives the ambiguity pre-framed — a minor effect.

**Process-feasibility check:** Lawful under rules 5 and 10; S1 overlap is permitted; the true cost center is C1's single-threadedness (rule 11): every prelim hour and every reserved fragment displaces queued work, including the two urgent compliance cases added Mon 16:00 — the reservation either holds (urgent cases delayed) or is overridden (benefit lost, admin wasted).

Worst: thin packet forces re-reading and the slot is wasted or overridden  0 to +3 h for the case, with measurable harm to displaced cases.

Applied to 100 similar cases in one week, the four policies interfere through shared pools, and the single-case arithmetic stops transporting. K-104's reservation policy is the most fragile at scale: C1 is a single server whose backlog runs 205 h across the week, and holding provisional slots for every flagged case converts forecast uncertainty into idle fragments. Near saturation, queueing delay grows superlinearly, so even a well-meant reservation regime can double waits for unreserved urgent cases such as the two added Mon 16:00. Preliminary reading also adds C1 touch-time; if packets are incomplete, net C1 hours rise and the policy defeats itself.

K-219's D2-first rule is a substitution, not a capacity increase: D2 is 1–2 h slower per standard case and carries a clarification propensity, so shifting 100 cases moves roughly 100–200 h of work from D1 to D2, inflates D2's queue, and risks D1 idling unless complex arrivals fill it. K-337 is the mirror image on rework: suppressing second loops removes about 6 h of internal second-pass work plus one external round-trip per case, but at scale it creates an unmanaged downstream queue — condition monitoring and appeals — and teaches suppliers that inconsistencies get decided anyway, degrading future evidence quality. K-411's parallelization removes serialization dead time but delivers earlier, burstier arrivals into the assessment pool; with assessment queues at 4–12 h and one assessor in training, the bottleneck migrates from verification to assessment, and gains plateau at the assessment wait unless capacity follows.

Calendar structure matters: external responses of 8–36 h that begin on Friday absorb the weekend (factual: ~70 h elapsed), so K-337-type suppression saves disproportionate calendar time, while K-104 reservations made late in the week decay over the weekend. Finally, K-411's value depends on timestamp truth: real-time event logs make concurrency auditable, whereas batch entry manufactures false concurrency, corrupts the process model, and converts a benign speedup into a compliance finding. Across all four, local counterfactuals that hold queues fixed systematically understate interference.

| process-path outcome | Share of cases with genuine ID/doc overlap; triagedocument-task-creation lag | Escalation-advice timestamp relative to assessment start; final review duration vs logged prelim hours; slot utilization |

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-411's parallel task creation with real-time timestamps. It restores a concurrency the normative process already permits, costs nothing in service hours, is fully reversible, and improves the evidentiary quality of the event log itself. Its benefit is modest (1–6 h) precisely because the binding constraint sits downstream, but it is positive or neutral under almost every assumption tested.

The most dangerous intervention despite its speed is K-337's suppression of the second information loop. It roughly halves a 214 h cycle, but it does so by changing the decision policy: the organization stops buying information and starts pricing risk with a known-open discrepancy. The saved hours are certain; the quality cost is fat-tailed, irreversible per case, and invisible to any pilot short enough to be practical. Precedent and supplier-behavior drift compound it.

The least transportable local counterfactual is K-104's reserved C1 slot. For one case, a reservation is trivially feasible and clearly beneficial; replicated across 100 cases on the only certified reviewer, it becomes a capacity-allocation regime that manufactures idle fragments and displaces urgent work — the local counterfactual assumes away exactly the scarcity it exploits. K-219 is transportable only conditional on a clarification rate nobody has measured.

The most valuable additional data: resource-level service-interval logs with event-time timestamps (to verify true concurrency and rule 11); per-pool queue-length histories rather than snapshots; D1-vs-D2 clarification and rework rates stratified by document type (especially handwriting); C1 preliminary-versus-final effort per case; external response-time distributions conditioned on weekday of issue (weekend crossing); and post-decision outcomes — appeals, condition breaches, losses — needed to price K-337-style policies at all.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **Structural** — K-411's case lock is an IT artifact; rule 3 already permits concurrency with no originals, so the workaround changes the realization, not the declared process model.
2. **Temporal** — Queue snapshots behave like fluid backlogs (start  arrival + current backlog), and the evening/overnight work evidenced in factuals (D1 overnight, A2 Tue 20:00–22:30, M1 18:00 decisions) is available on weekdays but not weekends.
3. **Resource** — C1 is a single, non-overlapping server (rule 11); any prelim hour or reservation displaces other compliance work roughly one-for-one and creates idle fragments.
4. **Behavioral** — Triage staff produce escalation packets of usable quality, and D2's clarification propensity on handwritten documents is genuinely higher than D1's, which is why the original routing existed.
5. **Quality** — Preliminary compliance reading transfers 1–3 h of effort to the final review without anchoring the reviewer or double-counting work.
6. **Quality** — In K-337, the residual inconsistency is more likely benign than material, making conditional approval defensible; this is unverifiable at the moment of decision.
7. **Interference** — Single-case counterfactuals hold other cases' queues approximately fixed except where the intervention directly loads a shared pool (C1 reservation, D2 load, A2 arrivals).
8. **Behavioral** — External suppliers' 8–36 h response times are exogenous within the pilot window and do not yet adapt to suppressed follow-ups or changed routing; at scale they likely would.
