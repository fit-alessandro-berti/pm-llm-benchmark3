# 1. Mechanism map

The restructuring changes the operating model from **local embedded specialists** to **central shared-service queues**. This shifts process behavior from direct handoffs between known local resources toward pooled queues with routing rules. The process-mining effects are not automatically good or bad; they depend on queue behavior, case mix, and local context.

| Restructuring action | Organizational mechanism | Observable process-mining effect |
|---|---:|---|
| Merge N_Tech, S_Tech, E_Tech into centralized Technical Shared Service | Pooling may increase specialist utilization and reduce local-specialist loops, but may also create a central queue and weaken local technical availability | Change in handover-of-work edges: `local caseworker  embedded tech specialist` becomes `local caseworker  Technical Shared Service`; wait after handover; specialist service time and utilization |
| Centralize Specialist Review, Legal/Risk Review, difficult Quality Check | Single queue simplifies routing but creates shared congestion risk | Waiting time before `Specialist Review`, `Quality Check`; queue-length proxy from event timestamps; rework after central review |

| Shared routing rules for high-complexity and required specialties | Standardizes referral decisions; can reduce unnecessary referrals or create over-referral | Routing compliance; frequency of `local caseworker  Specialist Review`; handover reasons |
| Remove one local supervisor, create shared approval queue | Standardizes decisions but may reduce case ownership and local accountability | Approval queue wait; `reassignment_count`; handover reason “unclear ownership”; role discovery |

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1. **Tail latency**: Mean or median cycle time can remain stable while p90/p95 increase substantially. Central queues may process standard cases well but delay complex or specialty-heavy cases. Monitor quantile cycle times and waiting-time distributions.

2. **High-complexity cases**: Average cycle time hides worsening for high-complexity cases. High-complexity cases may face multiple central queues, more handovers, and longer waits. Segment by `case_complexity` to detect this.

3. **Local knowledge loss**: A stable average can coexist with erosion of local expertise. Local staff may stop handling difficult technical work, reducing early informal advice and increasing future referrals. Monitor local staff participation, `local_expertise_flag`, and technical activity share.

4. **Fairness across teams and customer segments**: Overall average cycle time can improve while North/South stay stable or worsen, East improves, and language/regional customer segments face longer waits. Fairness must be measured by stratified cycle time, rework, and SLA miss.

5. **Learning and deskilling**: If all hard cases are routed away, local workers learn less. This may not appear in short-term cycle time but weakens future performance, innovation, and organizational resilience. Monitor resource similarity and role drift.

6. **Accountability and ownership**: Shared queues can show stable throughput while ownership becomes unclear. Cases may wait in queues without a responsible resource. Reassignment count and unclear handover reasons can rise even if average cycle time remains stable.

7. **Exception handling**: Centralized routing often works well for standard cases but poorly for exceptions. Average cycle time may hide increased waits for rare specialties, cross-regional cases, or cases with incomplete information.

8. **Queue backlog stability**: Average cycle time can remain stable while backlog grows. A central queue can absorb temporary demand until it saturates; once saturated, cycle time can deteriorate sharply. Monitor queue length and wait-time variance early.

- Legal/risk or approval queues show spillover congestion.
- Staff report serious role clarity or fairness problems.

If partial success occurs, modify rather than abandon: keep local technical champions, preserve local handling for regional/language-sensitive cases, improve case documentation before central routing, or add capacity to shared queues.

# 6. Recommendation

**Pilot first, do not proceed immediately to full rollout.**

The restructuring has a plausible logic: it may reduce nominal handovers, improve specialist utilization, and help East, where E_Tech is already overloaded. But it also creates central bottleneck risk, may weaken local expertise, and may reduce role clarity and fairness.

Proceed conditionally only if the pilot shows:

- Central technical queue waits remain within guardrail limits.
- High-complexity p90/p95 cycle time does not worsen materially.
- Rework and SLA miss rates do not increase, especially for high-complexity and regional cases.
- Fairness gaps across teams, locations, and customer segments do not widen.
- Staff report acceptable role clarity and ownership.

If the pilot shows central queue saturation or tail-latency degradation, **modify** the model. Reasonable modifications include:

- Keeping local technical specialists for certain high-complexity or regional cases.
- Preserving local triage before central Technical Shared Service.
- Adding capacity to legal/risk and approval queues.
- Improving case-note standards before central routing.

If the adverse scenario appears and does not respond to adjustments, **reject full rollout**.

Management should **not infer success from early average cycle time**. Early averages may improve or remain stable while tail waits, rework, fairness, and backlog risks are building. The evaluation should rely on stratified leading and lagging process-mining indicators, not on a single aggregate average.

        u, v, d = edge
        rows.append({
            'source': u,
            'target': v,
            'weight': d.get('weight', d.get('count', 1))
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

hw_before_edges = edge_table(hw_before).assign(period='before')
hw_after_edges = edge_table(hw_after).assign(period='after')

def case_outcomes(frame):

        start=('time:timestamp','min'),
        end=('time:timestamp','max'),
        team=('team','last'),
        complexity=('case_complexity','last'),
        specialty=('required_specialty','last'),
        customer_segment=('customer_segment','last'),
        rework_flag=('rework_flag','max'),
        reassignment_count=('reassignment_count','max')
    )
    case['cycle_hours'] = (case['end'] - case['start']).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    case['sla_miss'] = case['cycle_hours'] > 168  # example: 7-day SLA

        rework_rate=('rework_flag','mean'),
        sla_miss_rate=('sla_miss','mean'),

)
