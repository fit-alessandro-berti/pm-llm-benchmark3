```python
def pandas_separation(log, a1, a2, actor_col="org:resource"):
    """cases where a1 and a2 were done by disjoint actor sets (four-eyes kept)
       and cases where at least one actor did both (violation)"""
    keep, viol = set(), set()
    for case, g in log.groupby("case:concept:name"):
        s1 = set(g.loc[g["concept:name"] == a1, actor_col])
        s2 = set(g.loc[g["concept:name"] == a2, actor_col])
        if not s1 or not s2:
            continue                      # not evaluable (activity missing)
        (viol if (s1 & s2) else keep).add(case)
    return keep, viol
```

# 2. Rule Interpretation

Notation: `H(x)` = human-normalized actor of event x (after alias resolution and RR5 attribution); `case(x)`, `t(x)` = its case and UTC timestamp; `risk(c)`, `scope(c)`, `emg(c)` = case attributes. All comparisons use **UTC** event time.

**RR1 (drafter/approver separation).** `c: H(Draft_Change_Request@c)  H(Approve_Change@c)`.
Applies to **every** risk class and every scope — the rule has no risk qualifier, so a LOW-risk self-approval is still a breach (CHG502, CHG508). If either activity is missing from the trace, the case is *not evaluable / control gap*, not compliant. If either actor is an unattributed service account, the test is suspended under RR5 and the case is INDETERMINATE.

**RR2 (implementer/verifier separation).** `c: H(Implement_Change@c)  H(Verify_Implementation@c)`.
Same identity treatment. Letter-of-the-rule compliance is necessary but not sufficient for the control objective: if the verifier is the *approver* (CHG509: Eva approves and, via attributed svc_qms, verifies) or is unqualified (CHG507: Site Director verifies), RR2 is formally met while the independent-verification objective is hollow. I record both layers.

**RR3 (high-risk approval authority).** For `risk = HIGH`, `Approve_Change` at `t` is authorized iff `H(x)` is (i) the QM with a standing role covering `t`, or (ii) the delegate of a **valid** delegation `d`:

```
Valid(d, h, t, a, c) 
   (1) h = d.delegate                                        [identity, after RR5]
  (2) t  [d.start, d.end]                                  [event-time, UTC, inclusive]
  (3) a  d.functions                                       [function scope]
  (4) scope(c)  d.scope                                    [change-type/incident scope]
  (5) if d.instrument requires principal absence:
        FormallyUnavailable(d.principal, t)                   [see absence rule below]
  (6) ¬ prohibited subdelegation: d is issued by its principal,
       or the parent instrument permits onward transfer       [D-IC-04 fails here]
  (7) AuthenticInstrument(d) with effective-time proof       [bitemporal rule below]
```
Failure of (1)–(2) or (6)  **UNAUTHORIZED**. Failure of (5) or (7) only  **INDETERMINATE** (the authority may exist but is not evidenced). Condition (5) is engaged **only** when the instrument imposes it ("when required by the delegation"): DQM01 and DQM02 impose it; DIC03 does not.

**RR4 (emergency substitution).** For `risk = HIGH  emg(c)` with a *credible* emergency flag and documented safety rationale:

```
(a) H(Approve_Change@c) holds IC authority at t  (standing IC, or valid IC delegation scoped to the incident)
(b)  x: activity(x) = Retrospective_QM_Review
         H(x) is QM or valid QM delegate with scope over c     [RR3 test, QM function set]
         t(x)  Deadline2BD(t(Implement_Change@c))

    If (b) fails, the emergency change was executed without the required QM assurance:
    the RR4 shield does not apply and the change is treated as UNAUTHORIZED on the assurance leg.
```
Trigger = `Implement_Change` timestamp (the moment the emergency change takes effect); if absent, `Approve_Change`. Sequence is also checked: implementation must follow approval.

* **Inclusive:** trigger day counts as business day 1  deadline = 23:59 of the 1st business day after the trigger.
* **Generous (robustness test):** counting starts at the next full business day after incident stabilisation  deadline = 23:59 of the 3rd business day after the trigger.

**RR5 (identity evidence).** Service accounts are non-human and hold no role; an action by `svc_qms` supports conformance only if session evidence (individual authentication — personal credential/FIDO — plus application-session binding, corroborated by badge/terminal) maps it to **one** authorized human and excludes others. Unknown/unmapped principals  case marked not-evaluable.

**RR6 (suspicion versus proof).** Repeated pairs, reciprocity, shared workstations, concentrated handovers and absence conflicts contribute to a **suspicion score** only. Collusion requires at least two *independent* corroborating evidence classes (e.g. technical concealment + communications + motive/benefit), and anomaly must be assessed against a base rate (roster, cell assignment, number of *available* qualified approvers). Neither repeated collaboration alone nor a network edge weight is proof.

**Action by a principal while on leave.**
1. Standing role authority is **not** suspended by leave: roles are interval-based, so Asha's 20260813 09:22 approval by a standing QM is *technically* conforming (RR3).
2. But it creates (i) a leave-policy deviation, and (ii) an identity-assurance exception: credential sharing cannot be excluded, so the human actor must be re-attested with second-channel re-authentication. If the actor is not Asha, the act becomes UNAUTHORIZED.
3. **Effect on delegations:** formal absence/unavailability is determined by the **HR leave record**, not by momentary connectivity. A principal's brief VPN activity therefore does **not** retroactively void a delegation exercised while the principal was formally absent (DQM01 stays valid for 0813 13:00 notwithstanding the 09:15–09:40 VPN window). However, if an instrument conditions authority on *actual* unavailability and the principal demonstrably was available **at the moment of the delegated act**, the condition must be re-verified and the delegated act is INDETERMINATE pending that verification.
4. Symmetrically, a delegation to a delegate who is himself absent (Ben, sick from 0813 12:00) does not become *invalid* under RR3's test, but it becomes a control-design gap: there is then no available authorized approver in scope.

**Can a later database entry prove an earlier delegation? No — not by itself (bitemporal rule).**
Let `vt` = valid time (the interval the delegation was in force) and `tt` = transaction time (when the record entered the system). A record with `tt > t(event)` cannot on its own establish `vt  t(event)`; otherwise any authority could be manufactured retroactively. It is admissible as proof of an earlier valid time **only** if (i) the underlying instrument has an independent trusted timestamp predating the event (signed PDF 20260809 18:00), (ii) the instrument is unaltered and provenance-controlled (document-control ID, hash), and (iii) an auditable migration trail explains the late `tt`.
* **DQM01** meets (i)–(iii) on the facts given (signed PDF 0809 18:00; DB row 0812 09:15 created "during migration")  valid at 0811 09:00 and 0812 11:30 (for the latter even `tt` precedes the event). Residual caveat: the migration trail itself is unverified evidence.
* **DQM02** has `tt < vt` (record created before the interval)  forward-proven, evidence strongest of the three.
* **DIC03** signed **and registered** before use  contemporaneous.
* **DIC04** (email only) is void on the merits (prohibited subdelegation), so its evidential quality is moot.

# 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

Per RR6, every indicator below is a **hypothesis generator**. For each I give a benign and an adverse reading plus discriminating evidence. Nothing here is treated as proof.

**(1) Clara–Ben repeated pair (18 of 23 HIGH production changes over 60 days; in this extract CHG501, 503, 506).**
* Benign: the two are assigned to the same production cell; Ben is the only QE in the cell; with no pair-rotation policy the pairing is the *expected* outcome of the roster. Asha (QM) is a scarce resource (3/23), so delegations concentrate approvals in Ben by design.
* Adverse: familiarity-based rubber-stamping — Ben approves Clara's content with nominal challenge, creating a de-facto single author of HIGH-risk production changes.
* Discriminating: review duration per approval (10–40 minutes in this extract — compare to change complexity and number of attachments); Ben's historical **rejection/amendment rate** (a rate at or near zero is adverse); independent technical re-review of a sample of the 23 changes; edit history between Draft and Approve (were drafts amended after approval?); deviations/CAPA linked to the approved changes; interviews with both plus their manager; base rate of *available* qualified approvers at each approval time.

**(2) Reciprocal behaviour (Ben drafts LOW-risk training changes that Clara approves — 4 cases).**
* Benign: LOW-risk training documents are small; both are cell document owners; reciprocity is ordinary workload relief with no policy prohibiting it (RR1 is satisfied if different humans draft and approve).
* Adverse: quid-pro-quo pattern — "you approve mine, I approve yours" — which, combined with (1), means each can move content through the other's lane and the SoD control is formally intact but substantively defeated. Note also a **data discrepancy**: this extract's only Ben-drafted training change (CHG508) was approved by **Ben himself**, not Clara — the summary's "four" is not reproducible from the 10-case sample, so the population data must be re-derived before any conclusion.
* Discriminating: full 60-day extract including all training-document changes with normalized identities; document content diffs and materiality; whether the reciprocal approvals occurred shortly after reciprocal favours (temporal cross-correlation); any benefit evidence (performance ratings, workload relief, shift swaps); interviews.

**(3) Shared workstation / service account at WSDM04 during Diego's own implementation (CHG503).**
* Benign: WSDM04 is the room's practical terminal; `svc_qms` is the routine QMS automation identity; Diego badge-entered because it is his room and he was the implementer; an on-shift verifier legitimately used the closest terminal and the service account is how the QMS writes verification records.
* Adverse: control evasion — the implementer verified his own work through a non-personal account precisely because no individual re-authentication is enforced at shared terminals, defeating RR2 and leaving no accountable actor.
* Discriminating: application-level session logs (which credential/token authenticated before the write); WSDM04 OS login records and 802.1X identity at 12:30; `svc_qms` credential custody and whether it requires an upstream personal credential; FIDO/token events at 12:25–12:35; badge granularity (room vs. terminal) and full entry list; whether `svc_qms` verifications correlate with Diego's implementations across the 60-day population (that correlation would be adverse); imaging/forensics of the terminal.

**(4) Absence conflicts — Asha acting during leave (0813 09:22) and u_bk used during certified sick leave (0813 13:00).**
* Benign: dedicated staff handling one urgent item during leave with management knowledge; a person on sick leave coming in briefly to finish an urgent approval; an approved "worked while on leave" arrangement simply not recorded.
* Adverse: credential sharing or account takeover — a third party manufacturing approvals under the identities of the only two people with HIGH-risk approval reach during the QM's absence window (both anomalies fall inside the 08100814 delegation window, i.e. exactly when Ben's delegated authority was the bottleneck).
* Discriminating: hardware-token/FIDO binding on the VPN session and on the QMS approval (possession factor at 09:22 and 13:00); 802.1X/NAC identity of the 13:00 workstation and its physical desk assignment; geolocation and concurrent-session analysis (can one human plausibly do both?); helpdesk password-reset/self-service tickets on 0813; signed statements from Asha and Ben plus corroboration (manager, calendar, phone records); content analysis of the two approvals (who drafted, who benefits, was anything changed after approval); comparison of typing cadence/behavioural biometrics if available.

**(5) Implementation concentration and role stacking around Diego (implements 8/10 cases; principal of DIC03 used to approve his own implementation in CHG505; his workstation hosts the unattributed verification in CHG503).**
* Benign: one maintenance lead in a small cell; no rule bars the IC from implementing under an IC-delegate approval; the approval (Farid) and verification (Eva) were genuinely other people.
* Adverse: single point of control over execution combined with influence over the authority chain and over at least one unattributed verification — an opportunity structure for undetected unauthorised work.
* Discriminating: roster evidence of alternative implementers on 0811/0813; work-order records and independent field observation; post-change deviation rate for Diego's implementations vs. others; whether Farid exercised any real challenge before approving at 13:00 (review artefacts); the outcome of the CHG503 attribution work.

**(6) Authority routing to the drafter and compressed review times (CHG510: DIC04 created 14:00, Clara self-approves 14:20; CHG502 30 min; CHG505 40 min; CHG508 30 min).**
* Benign: a real cooling emergency with Farid believing he could delegate to the nearest competent person; the drafter was the person on the scene; short intervals reflect pre-agreed content discussed at shift handover before formal entry.
* Adverse: authority-shopping and pro-forma approval — the instrument was created to place the approval in the drafter's hands while a lawful approver (Farid, validly delegated 12:00–18:00) was on duty and available; the subdelegation's prohibition was ignored deliberately.
* Discriminating: the email text and headers (did Farid assert a right to subdelegate? who advised him?); incident-command log and radio/phone records 13:30–14:30; evidence that any substantive review occurred (checklists, attachments, version history between Draft and Approve); whether Farid was asked and refused; whether QM was notified of the emergency path; training records showing whether Farid/Clara had been told of the no-subdelegation clause.

**(7) Off-hours concentration (CHG507 on Sunday 0809; CHG509 on Saturday 0815).**
* Benign: weekend coverage for genuine production pressure; a training-document update scheduled for the weekend to avoid production hours.
* Adverse: work performed when qualified oversight (QM) is least available — both cases show approvals by people lacking QM authority at the time.
* Discriminating: shift rosters and on-call records for those dates; production/incident records justifying weekend work; whether the HIGH risk rating for CHG509 is genuine (risk-rating rationale); who requested the weekend work.

**(8) Structural availability gap (organisational, not individual).**
Between 0810 and 0814 the **only** in-scope approver for cooling-system HIGH-risk changes was Asha (on leave). DQM01 covers production equipment only; DIC03 covers one incident only. A control that cannot be operated by anyone on duty is a design failure that generates *apparent* violations even without bad intent — and it also creates the pressure that produces rushed or fabricated authority (CHG510). Discriminating evidence: the site's delegation matrix coverage analysis and any prior deviation/CAPA on delegation gaps.

**Bottom line for RR6:** the pair concentration, reciprocity, shared workstation, absence conflicts and handover concentration are **cumulatively notable and worth a structured investigation**, but no case is supported by two independent corroborating evidence classes. Sample size (10 cases against a stated 23+ population) and absent base rates prevent any collusion conclusion.

def pandas_sep(log, a1, a2, actor_col="org:resource"):
    """cases with disjoint actor sets (four-eyes kept) and cases where one actor did both"""
    keep, viol, noteval = set(), set(), set()
    for case, g in log.groupby("case:concept:name"):
        s1 = set(g.loc[g["concept:name"] == a1, actor_col])
        s2 = set(g.loc[g["concept:name"] == a2, actor_col])
        if not s1 or not s2:
            noteval.add(case)                      # activity missing -> not evaluable
        elif s1 & s2:
            viol.add(case)
        else:
            keep.add(case)
    return keep, viol, noteval

def is_bd(d):
    return d.weekday() < 5 and d not in HOLIDAYS


def deadline(ts_event, n=2, convention="event_day_excluded"):
    d, c = ts_event.date(), 0
    if convention == "next_business_day":                      # generous reading
        while not is_bd(d):
            d += timedelta(days=1)
        d += timedelta(days=1)
    while c < n:
        if is_bd(d):
            c += 1
            if c == n:
                break
            if convention == "event_day_excluded":
                d += timedelta(days=1)
                continue
        if convention == "event_day_included":
            d += timedelta(days=1)
        elif convention == "event_day_excluded":
            if not is_bd(d):
                d += timedelta(days=1)
        else:
            d += timedelta(days=1)
    return datetime.combine(d, time(23, 59, 59))

def together_fb(log):
    rows = []
    for case, g in log.groupby("case:concept:name"):
        rs = sorted(set(g["org:resource"]))
        for i in range(len(rs)):
            for j in range(i + 1, len(rs)):
                rows.append(dict(source=rs[i], target=rs[j], weight=1))
    if not rows:
        return pd.DataFrame(columns=["source", "target", "weight"])
    return (pd.DataFrame(rows).groupby(["source", "target"], as_index=False)["weight"].sum()
            .sort_values("weight", ascending=False))


def handover_fb(log):
    rows = []
    for case, g in log.groupby("case:concept:name"):
        g = g.sort_values("time:timestamp")
        prev = None
        for _, r in g.iterrows():
            if prev is not None and prev != r["org:resource"]:
                rows.append(dict(source=prev, target=r["org:resource"], weight=1))
            prev = r["org:resource"]
    if not rows:
        return pd.DataFrame(columns=["source", "target", "weight"])
    return (pd.DataFrame(rows).groupby(["source", "target"], as_index=False)["weight"].sum()
            .sort_values("weight", ascending=False))


def net(fn, fb, log, name):
    try:
        n = fn(log, activity_key="concept:name", resource_key="org:resource",
               case_id_key="case:concept:name", weight_threshold=0.0, output_mode="df")
        if isinstance(n, pd.DataFrame) and {"source", "target", "weight"} <= set(n.columns):
            return n[["source", "target", "weight"]].sort_values("weight", ascending=False), "pm4py"
    except Exception as exc:
        print("   [%s failed: %s -> pandas fallback]" % (name, exc))
    return fb(log), "pandas fallback"

# 6. Control Improvements

1. **Delegation creation.** Delegations may be created only through a controlled workflow by the *principal* (dual approval or QA counter-signature), with an immutable instrument ID, hash and an explicit **"subdelegation permitted: yes/no"** field that the system enforces by refusing to accept an instrument whose parent prohibits onward transfer (this alone would have blocked DIC04/CHG510). Template language must state *function scope* (which activities: Approve vs. Retrospective\_QM\_Review) and *change scope* (equipment class/incident ID) explicitly, and auto-expire on the delegate's own absence.
2. **Effective-time (bitemporal) recording.** Store `valid_from/valid_to` **and** `recorded_at`/`recorded_by` on every delegation, plus the instrument's independent trusted timestamp. Reject (or route to QA) any delegation whose `recorded_at` post-dates its `valid_from` unless an auditable migration ID and instrument hash are attached — this converts "created during migration" from a suspicion into a traceable, testable fact. Nightly exception report: any authority exercised under a delegation whose record post-dates the act.
3. **Principal availability.** Make the "principal absent / formally unavailable" condition *machine-checkable*: the delegation workflow should require a linked leave/travel/on-call record, and the approval screen should re-validate the condition at the moment of use, blocking or escalating when no absence record covers the timestamp (this is the CHG509 defect). Distinguish "annual/sick/travel absence" from "non-working day" in the data model and state which one the policy accepts. Publish a **coverage matrix**: for every change scope and risk, at least two available approvers at all times — 08100814 had none for cooling-system HIGH-risk changes.
4. **Alias management.** Maintain a governed, signed identity master (HR-provisioned) with `human_id` as the join key for all QMS actions; require re-attestation of the alias map quarterly and on joiner/mover/leaver events. All SoD analytics and the QMS itself must evaluate over `human_id`, never over raw login names. Report "unknown/unmapped principal" as a hard exception.
5. **Individual authentication.** Prohibit interactive use of `svc_qms` (or any service identity) at shared terminals; make service identities non-interactive and constrain them to automation. Enforce phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2) for **every** authority-bearing action, with the assertion logged so that the action record contains the authenticating human, device and token ID (this is what made CHG509 resolvable and CHG503 unresolvable). Disable or broker shared logins on WSDM04-class terminals; log workstation-level (not just room-level) badge/seat binding. Automatic session step-up and alerting when a credential is used during a recorded leave or sick-leave interval.
6. **Emergency review.** Make the retrospective QM review a system-enforced gate: the case cannot move to `Close_Change` without a review by a principal whose *scope* covers the change type, and the system should propose the correct reviewer automatically. Auto-compute the deadline from the site calendar at the moment `Implement_Change` is recorded and escalate at 50% and 90% of the window. Require a structured safety rationale and incident ID at `Draft_Change_Request` for any emergency flag; require the emergency flag to be re-confirmed by an independent person (never the drafter).
7. **Pair rotation and SoD design.** Introduce a soft pair-rotation policy for HIGH-risk changes (e.g. rotating approver pool per cell per month) and a **complementary-separation matrix** that goes beyond the two letter rules: also separate drafterimplementer and **approververifier** (CHG509 shows the current rule set lets one human approve and then verify). Where staffing makes rotation infeasible, define a documented compensating control (mandatory QA sampling of that pair's changes, e.g. 20–30%) rather than leaving concentration unmanaged.
8. **Network/pattern monitoring.** Run the handover/working-together/pair-frequency analytics monthly with **base-rate normalisation** (cell size, roster, number of available approvers) and track drift, not raw counts. Monitor specifically: (a) approvals occurring within *n* minutes of drafting for HIGH-risk changes, (b) approvals by accounts during their owner's absence, (c) service-account writes at shared terminals, (d) reciprocal approval pairs, (e) self-handling across *normalized* identities. Output to QA as *triage indicators* with a mandatory human adjudication step (RR6) — never as automated misconduct findings.
9. **Evidential retention.** Retain, per authority-bearing act: the authenticated session assertion, device/token IDs, the delegation instrument with its trusted timestamp and hash, the absence record consulted by the system, and the full version history of the change record (draft  approval diffs). Retain badge, VPN, NAC/802.1X, FIDO and CCTV records for at least as long as the change-control record (GMP retention), with CCTV treated as time-synchronised and tamper-evident. Preserve a read-only audit trail of identity-map changes so that alias normalisation at the time of an event can be reconstructed later.

# 7. Executive Conclusion

**Systemic judgment.** The apparent non-conformance splits roughly as follows across the ten cases: **genuine authority/SoD failure accounts for about half** (CHG502, CHG507, CHG508, CHG510 as outright breaches, plus the out-of-scope retrospective-review legs of CHG505 and CHG510); **identity and data quality account for roughly a quarter** (CHG503, CHG506, and part of CHG504 — here the rules may well have been honoured by a person, but the plant cannot demonstrate *by whom*); **one case is genuinely indeterminate** (CHG509, where a delegation's express absence condition is simply unevidenced); and **one case is cleanly conforming through a valid substitution** (CHG501). The single most important systemic lesson is that the deviations are **not** primarily a misconduct story: they are produced by (i) delegation instruments whose scope is narrower than the authority staff assume (DQM01 cannot touch cooling-system changes at all, so during the QM's leave *no one* could lawfully perform the RR4 retrospective reviews — a designed-in impossibility), (ii) subdelegation that is prohibited but not technically blocked, (iii) identity controls that do not survive shared terminals, service accounts and borrowed credentials, and (iv) SoD rules written as two pairwise tests that are satisfied in letter while the independent-verification objective is hollowed out (approver = verifier; drafter = implementer).

Organisational-pattern risk (Clara–Ben concentration, the reciprocal training-change pair, Diego's implementation concentration, authority routed to the drafter) is **real and worth structured review, but unproven**: no indicator is supported by two independent corroborating evidence classes, the sample is 10 cases against a stated 23+ population, and no base rates are available. Per RR6, none of it is treated as evidence of collusion.

**Uncertainty.** Confidence is high on the documentary failures (void subdelegation, alias self-approval, non-QM approval, out-of-scope reviewers) and low-to-medium on every identity attribution question (who used `u_as` at 09:22 and `u_bk` at 13:00; who typed into WSDM04 at 12:30) and on two interpretive points that could move CHG505 and CHG509 in either direction: whether "cooling\_system" is inside "production\_equipment", and whether a weekend satisfies "principal absent".

**Three highest-priority investigations**
1. **Credential forensics on the two HIGH-risk approvals of 20260813** (`u_as` 09:22 during annual leave; `u_bk` 13:00 one hour into certified sick leave from an office IP, denied by the account owner). Both fall inside the QM-absence window when delegated approval reach was the bottleneck. Priority evidence: FIDO/token assertions, VPN client telemetry, 802.1X identity of the workstation, helpdesk reset tickets, endpoint forensics and signed statements. Outcome determines whether two HIGH-risk changes are validly approved or were approved by an unidentified, unauthorized human.
2. **Emergency-change authority chain (CHG510 and CHG505).** Obtain the DIC04 email and its context, the CI44 incident-command log and the change-to-incident link, the safety rationale behind both emergency flags, and evidence of the substantive review performed 10–40 minutes after drafting. This tests whether the void subdelegation was negligence under incident pressure or deliberate authority-shopping, and whether the RR4 shield was used as cover.
3. **Service-account and shared-terminal attribution (CHG503) plus the scope/availability gap.** Recover application session logs and token data at WSDM04 to establish whether Diego verified his own implementation (RR2 evasion), and in parallel fix the structural defect: no in-scope QM authority existed for cooling-system HIGH-risk changes during 08100814, and `Retrospective_QM_Review` scope was never mapped to DQM01's wording. Also re-derive the 60-day pair statistics over the full population with normalized identities before any pattern-based enquiry is opened.
