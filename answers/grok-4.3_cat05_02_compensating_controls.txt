## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition
1. Unauthorized single-actor initiation and release of a fraudulent transfer to a controlled or collusive beneficiary (AV-2 prevents via sanctions/duplicate/bank checks but fails against collusion or compromised accounts; MG-4 detects/prevents if independent and pre-settlement; RA-7 detects post-facto with limited correction; ED-1 fails to address if conditions mask collusion).  
2. Erroneous payment details (wrong amount, vendor, or bank) executed without independent review (AV-2 prevents via limit/PO match but not commercial reasonableness; MG-4 prevents if evidence reviewed pre-settlement; RA-7 detects after release; ED-1 mitigates only when all preconditions including independent MG-4 hold).  
3. Use of a recently altered or compromised bank account (AV-2 detects bank-change flag and can block; MG-4 can override or confirm ownership; RA-7 provides post-settlement detection only; ED-1 explicitly requires no bank change in prior 7 days).  
4. Override or bypass of automated checks by a single actor (AV-2 emits warning but does not prevent override; MG-4 can ratify only if independent and evidence-based; RA-7 detects override patterns; ED-1 prohibits override without full MG-4).  
5. Emergency-process abuse without genuine operational necessity (AV-2 still runs but ED-1 preconditions may be logged retroactively; MG-4 and RA-7 provide detection; ED-1 itself fails when token timing or independence is violated).

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test
A compensating package is fully equivalent when every threat in the decomposition is addressed by at least one control that is (a) preventive or hybrid before irrevocable settlement, (b) performed by an independent actor at L3+, (c) covers the exact attributes AV-2/MG-4 claim, (d) non-overrideable without separate independent approval, (e) supported by contemporaneous evidence, and (f) leaves no material collusion or recovery gap. It is partially equivalent when one or two threats retain only detective coverage or late timing. It is merely mitigating when independence or timing fails but loss probability is observably low. It is ineffective when a high-impact threat (e.g., bank-change fraud) has no effective control before settlement. “Same expected loss” is insufficient because control objectives also require deterrence, regulatory traceability, and preservation of organizational separation culture; identical expected loss achieved via weak post-settlement detection still violates the independence objective.

## 5. Counterfactual and Residual-Risk Analysis
AP-202: Most plausible loss is fraudulent new-vendor payment via relative-controlled account. AV-2 warning and MG-4 override were the only barriers; both failed due to conflict. Independent second releaser would likely have refused override. Residual exposure remains total loss.  
AP-204: Collusion or coordinated error on fabricated receipt. Distinct resources satisfied nominal rule but shared workstation removed independence. Truly independent releaser would have examined receipt. Residual risk of undetected overpayment persists.  
AP-206: Emergency-process abuse via retroactive token. Independent releaser would have questioned timing. MG-4 provided partial prevention; residual risk is procedural non-compliance without immediate loss.  
AP-208: Bank-account fraud on changed details without AV-2. No control addressed ownership. Independent releaser would have demanded proof. High unrecoverable exposure remains.  
AP-209: Repeated pairing creates collusion vector similar to AP-204. Independent releaser outside the pair would break pattern. Residual risk is undetected future fraud.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations
Collect and alert on: same-user release rate (alert  proof), override rate with supervisor conflict flag, manager ratification after Release_Payment but before settlement, audit completion before 24-hour recall window, repeated resource-pair frequency, shared-workstation logins on payment events, bank-change proximity to payment, and any AV-2 outage. Alerts trigger case review; only contemporaneous evidence plus independent corroboration constitutes proof of misconduct.
