## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition  

| Objectives / Threat Scenarios | Primary Control(s) | How AV2, MG4, RA7, ED1 Address It |
|---|---|---|
| **O1 – Prevent a single actor from both preparing and releasing** (the core foureyes breach). | **MG4** (independent manager approval) – must occur before release/settlement. **ED1** – allows emergency singleoperator only under strict preconditions. | *AV2* only checks data quality; it does **not** enforce identity separation. *MG4* directly enforces identity separation when performed prerelease. *ED1* explicitly relaxes the rule but only under tightly controlled conditions. *RA7* is retrospective; it cannot prevent the initial breach. |
| **O2 – Detect payment fraud or erroneous amounts** (duplicate invoice, sanctionslist hits, limit exceedance). | **AV2** – automated validation before release. | *AV2* provides strong preventive detection; can block or warn. *MG4* may review after the fact but is not aware of AV2 warnings unless communicated. *RA7* detects after settlement; *ED1* bypasses AV2 if emergency token is active (but only for small amounts). |
| **O3 – Ensure commercial reasonableness and receipt verification** (goods/services received, invoice legitimacy). | **MG4** (requires manager to review invoice, receipt, bank details). | *AV2* only verifies technical fields, not commercial reasonableness. *RA7* may catch missing receipts later. *ED1* does not add commercial review. |
| **O4 – Limit collusion between preparer and releaser** (conflict of interest, shared accounts). | **MG4** – manager must be L3+, no unresolved COI. | *AV2* does not assess relationships. *RA7* audits later but cannot stop collusion. *ED1* explicitly bars the preparer/releaser from being the same person unless emergency, thus reducing collusion probability. |
| **O5 – Recover or recall erroneous payments after release** (bank recall window). | **RA7** – retrospective audit and recall within 24h. | *AV2* prevents release of obviously wrong data. *MG4* can stop before release. *ED1* allows emergency release that may later be recalled if conditions met. No other control provides recall capability. |

*Threshold for sufficiency*: A compensating package is **fully equivalent** when it satisfies **all** relevant objectives under the required timing (preventive before the risky step, detective after but before loss, corrective within the loss window). **Partially equivalent** means one or more objectives are only partially covered (e.g., detective only after settlement). **Mitigating** indicates the control only reduces likelihood without guaranteeing prevention. **Ineffective** means the control fails to address any of the relevant threat scenarios.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test  

| Criterion | Fully Equivalent | Partially Equivalent | Mitigating | Ineffective |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Timing** – Controls must act *before* the risk event (prerelease) when possible; detective after release before loss; corrective within loss window. | Controls act at required moments (MG4 before release, AV2 before release, RA7 within 24h). | MG4 sometimes occurs *after* release (AP207) – still detective, but not preventive. | AV2 only checks *before* release; any postrelease flaw remains unmitigated. | RA7 only after settlement for highrisk cases (AP202) – no preventive effect. |
| **Independence** – Sufficiently independent actors (different personas, no conflict). | MG4 with independent managers, ED1 with emergency token. | AP204 has distinct preparer/releaser but manager m02 may share implicit info with vendor – partial independence. | Sameuser approvals (AP201) lack independence. | No independent reviewer for some cases (AP208 manager after settlement). |
| **Coverage** – All relevant checks (duplicate, sanctions, limit, receipt). | AV2 covers technical checks; MG4 covers receipt/commercial; RA7 covers retrospective. | AP202’s warning was overridden, leaving coverage gap. | AV2 unavailable (AP208)  coverage missing for bankchange verification. |
| **Overrideability** – Ability to temporarily bypass controls only under strict emergency. | ED1 allows singleoperator only under strict preconditions; overrides must be logged. | AP202 used an override credential without emergency token  misuse. | AP204’s manager ratification after release is a postrelease detective only. |
| **Recoverability** – Possibility to recall or reverse erroneous payment within policy window. | RA7 provides recall within 24h for sameuser emergency payments. | AP202’s payment was not recalled  recovery ineffective. |
| **Evidence Quality** – Clear, auditable logs of who performed what and when. | All logs present (Prepare, Automated Validate, Release, Manager Ratify, Audit). | AP208’s “Validation_Service_Unavailable” lacks reason, reducing evidence quality. |
| **Collusion Resistance** – Reduces opportunity for preparer/releaser to collude. | MG4 and ED1 enforce separation; repeated u12/u13 pairs raise concerns but not eliminated. | AP204’s repeated pairing is a known weakness. |

**Why “same expected loss” is insufficient** – Controls may reduce *probability* and *impact* differently. A control that cuts probability by 90% but leaves a residual 5% exposure at highvalue loss may be preferable to one that only lowers impact (e.g., recall capability) without affecting likelihood. Thus, risk = likelihood × impact must be evaluated jointly.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison  

| Control | Timing (relative to risk event) | Function | Classification |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AV2** (Automated validation before release) | **Preventive** – runs *before* Prepare_&_Release commit. | Checks duplicates, sanctions, limits, etc. | Preventive |
| **MG4** (Independent manager approval) | **Preventive** when executed **before** Release_&_Settlement (ideal). **Detective** if performed after Release (still gives oversight). **Corrective** if it triggers rejection/reversal. | Manager must review and approve. | Hybrid (preventive/detective/corrective depending on timing). |
| **RA7** (Retrospective audit & recall) | **Detective** – occurs **after** settlement but **before** loss becomes permanent (within 24h). **Corrective** only when recall is still possible. | Audits payment, seeks evidence of fraud, attempts recall. | Detective (with limited corrective). |
| **ED1** (Emergency singleoperator permission) | **Preventive** under strict preconditions; allows **singleoperator** *only* when emergency token active and other safety nets (AV2, manager, audit) satisfied. | Grants exception to foureyes. | Preventive (exception) + detective (requires checks) |
| **Manager Ratify (postrelease)** | **Detective** – discovers defects after release; **Corrective** if reversal possible. | Independent manager signs after release. | Detective (mainly) |
| **Audit_Review** | **Detective** (identifies irregularities) and **Corrective** (if defect discovered before settlement, can block it). | Conducts audit within policy window. | Detective (mainly) |

**Semantic choice** – A manager action *before* settlement is **preventive** because it stops a bad payment before it becomes irrevocable. The same action *after* release is **detective** because it uncovers a flaw that has already occurred. When the action includes the power to *void* the payment, it also carries corrective capacity. The terminology used here acknowledges this layered role.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations  

| Metric / Indicator | Recommended Monitoring Rule | Alert Threshold (illustrative) | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sameuser Prepare & Release rate** (percentage of payments where preparer = releaser) | Compute daily % of payments with identical preparer/releaser. | >10% (statistical deviation from organizational baseline) | Potential circumvention of foureyes; requires investigation. |
| **Override rate of AV2 warnings** | Track occurrences where a warning was overridden (no block). | >1% of AV2 warnings per month | Indicates cultural tolerance; may require stricter governance or training. |
| **Late manager approval** (manager ratify after release, beyond 12h) | Flag manager ratify timestamps later than 12h postrelease. | Any occurrence on >5% of payments | Suggests detectiveonly oversight; may need stricter prerelease approval. |
| **Auditbeforerecallwindow** (audit completed before 24h postrelease) | Verify audit timestamp <24h after Release_&_Settlement. | Missing for any sameuser emergency payment | Increases unrecoverable exposure; alert for compliance failure. |
| **Repeated resource pairs** (same two users appearing together >10 times in 30 days) | Track cooccurrence of preparer & releaser IDs. | >10 cooccurrences | Potential collusion cell; warrant review of conflictofinterest policies. |
| **Shared workstation usage** (same MAC/IP for >3 consecutive Prepare/Release events) | Log workstation identifiers with each event. | >3 consecutive shared events by same users | Strong indicator of coordinated attempts to bypass foureyes. |
| **Bankchange proximity** (bank account updated within 7days of payment release) | Flag payments where bankchange occurred 7days prior. | Any for payments >EUR50,000 | Risk of unauthorized account switch; requires extra validation. |
| **Control outage** (AV2 unavailable or manager ratify missing) | Record timestamps of AV2 service status and manager ratify completeness. | AV2 unavailable >1hour per day or manager ratify missing >5% of cases | Reduces preventive capacity; trigger compliance investigation. |

**Alert vs Proof** – An alert signals a *potential* control weakness (e.g., high override rate). Proof of misconduct requires further audit (e.g., reviewing logs, interviewing personnel). Alerts should trigger investigations, while proof leads to disciplinary actions.

**Explanation of PM4Py behavior & caveats**  

- The code uses **pandas** to reconstruct the event log from the provided CSV string.  
- It then applies a basic **foureyes** filter by matching `Prepare_Payment` and `Release_Payment` traces on `case` and `time:timestamp`.  
- The custom **filter_four_eyes_principle** from PM4Py is **not invoked** here because the data layout (no global event log view) would require conversion to an XES or Petri net structure first. Instead, we emulate the filter manually.  
- In a realworld PM4Py workflow, you would export the CSV to **XES**, load it with `pm4py.read_xes('log.xes')`, then call `filter_four_eyes_principle(log, ['Prepare_Payment','Release_Payment'])`. This ensures that the foureyes filter respects trace order and handles missing timestamps automatically.  
- PM4Py version differences may affect the exact name of the filter or its parameters; always verify the latest API documentation.  
- The script above **produces evidence flags** (e.g., whether an emergency token was active, AV2 coverage, manager timing, etc.) that can be used by the LLM judge to reason about each case without committing to a final compliance verdict.
