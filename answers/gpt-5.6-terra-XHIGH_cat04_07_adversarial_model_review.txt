# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

This review distinguishes four uses that should not be governed by one aggregate score:

- **Exploration and descriptive mining:** A model may deliberately tolerate incomplete recording and novel combinations. High fitness and broad language coverage are useful; low precision is acceptable only when the model is clearly labelled as non-normative.
- **Conformance enforcement and compliance monitoring:** The model must reject or escalate traces that violate mandatory controls. A false acceptance of “approval without checks” is substantially worse than a false rejection caused by a missing event. Conditional policy requirements must not be represented merely as unconstrained optional activities.
- **Simulation and operational planning:** Besides control flow, this requires calibrated routing probabilities, timestamps, queues, resources, and missingness assumptions. Neither submitted model establishes these.
- **Stakeholder communication and impact analysis:** The model must map one business activity to an understandable and stable control concept. Duplicate labels and branch-specific copies are especially problematic here.

The following interpretation assumes standard PM4Py process-tree semantics:

- `->` is sequence.
- `X` is exclusive choice.
- `+` is parallel/AND execution.
- `tau` is a silent transition.
- Thus, in Model S, each `X(activity, tau)` makes that visible activity optional, and the four optional choices may occur in arbitrary order before approval.

Two policy facts cannot be recovered from the activity sequence alone:

1. Whether an order is external or internal.
2. Which checks a particular `Correct Dossier` affected.

Therefore, an activity-only model cannot make a definitive compliance decision for every trace. It should return **valid**, **invalid**, or **indeterminate/requires evidence** rather than silently treating missing attributes as permission.

Fitness/precision trade-offs are consequently use-case dependent. Model S’s low precision may be tolerable in exploratory discovery, but not for enforcement. Model C’s low holdout fitness might be tolerable for a deliberately narrow historical-variant registry, but not where legitimate approved combinations are expected to evolve.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

**Strongest case for Model S**

Model S is compact, fast, and easy to explain. It naturally covers alternate Credit/Technical order, Supplier/Expedite combinations, and Auto Approval combinations that Model C omitted. Its high holdout fitness and token-based generalization are consistent with F4: it can accommodate the held-out cases containing Supplier Review, Auto Approval, and alternate mandatory-check order. For exploratory analysis, especially where Supplier events are known to be missing, its broad language may be useful as a descriptive “possible observed behavior” model.

It also avoids Model C’s branch explosion and duplicate-labelled copies. This explains the strong simplicity, runtime, and informal comprehension results.

**Strongest case against Model S**

Model S confuses **observational tolerance** with **business permission**. Its silent alternatives make Credit Check and Technical Review optional. Its parallel block gives Expedite Review no required precedence after those checks. These are not merely noise-tolerance choices; they are direct policy violations under P2 and P4.

Under the stated standard semantics, Model S admits at least the following undesirable or policy-ambiguous traces:

| Trace admitted by Model S | Why it is undesirable or ambiguous |
|---|---|
| `Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order` | B3. Both mandatory checks are skipped. |
| `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Manager Approval, Release Order` | Technical Review is skipped. |
| `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order` | Credit Check is skipped. |
| `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` | B4. Expedite Review occurs before both mandatory checks. |
| `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Supplier Review, Expedite Review, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order` | Expedite Review occurs before Technical Review; Supplier Review may appear before either mandatory check. |
| `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order` in an external-order context | Supplier Review is absent even though P3 requires it for external sourcing. The trace alone cannot prove internal status. |
| `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Auto Approval, Release Order` | All mandatory checks are skipped and Expedite Review is unconstrained. |

There is an important distinction between intended and accidental permissiveness:

- **Potentially intentional:** Making Supplier Review optional may be a pragmatic response to missing Supplier events or unrecorded sourcing attributes. Allowing new combinations of Supplier, Expedite, and approval may be desirable in exploration.
- **Accidental semantic flaws:** Making Credit Check and Technical Review optional, allowing Expedite before them, and failing to encode correction/recheck logic are incompatible with the stated policy.
- **Selective, not general, noise tolerance:** Model S accepts missing checks, but rejects B6 because it has no `Correct Dossier` path and no repeated-check behavior. It is permissive in unsafe places and rigid in a legitimate rework scenario.
- **P7 handling is accidental:** Model S rejects B8 only because it has no loop or repeated Credit Check occurrence, not because it recognizes and explains the missing correction event.

Model S therefore should not be described as “robust” without qualification. It is robust to some missing events by weakening mandatory-control semantics.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

**Strongest case for Model C**

Model C explicitly requires Credit Check and Technical Review in every listed branch, has exactly one approval in every branch, and places Expedite Review after the mandatory checks whenever it appears. It rejects B3 and B4. It also rejects repeated mandatory checks without a correction branch, which is directionally consistent with P7.

Its high reported training precision is consistent with a model that tightly restricts behavior to a small set of observed pathways. If the listed variants had been formally approved, fully observed, stable, and intended as a closed policy catalogue, this narrowness could support an audit trail of known procedures.

**Strongest case against Model C**

Model C is a variant list disguised as a process model. It encodes combinations by enumerating complete linear branches instead of expressing reusable rules such as:

- Credit and Technical may occur in either order.
- Supplier is conditionally required by sourcing type.
- Expedite is optional but follows both mandatory checks.
- Either approval may occur after the review phase.
- A correction may trigger a targeted subset of rechecks.

That choice creates several defects.

- **Unseen combinations are rejected even when policy permits them.** B2, B5, and F4-style cases are rejected because the relevant combinations were absent from the enumerated branches.
- **Auto Approval is dramatically underrepresented.** The only Auto branch is `Register, Credit, Technical, Auto, Release`. It cannot accommodate alternate check order, Supplier Review, Expedite Review, or correction.
- **Supplier Review is not conditional on sourcing type.** Some branches contain it and some do not, but no data condition determines which is required.
- **Expedite/Supplier ordering is incomplete.** C includes Supplier followed by Expedite, but no branch with Expedite followed by Supplier after mandatory checks.
- **Correction logic is rigid and incomplete.** It supports only one correction, only after `Credit, Technical`, only with both checks repeated, only in `Credit, Technical` order, and only followed by Manager Approval. It rejects B6 even when only Technical Review was affected.
- **Repeated corrections are impossible.** P6 allows `Correct Dossier` to repeat; C has no loop and no scalable rework structure.
- **Duplicate labels obscure business meaning.** The same visible activity is replicated across branches. A “Technical Review” event may align to many branch-specific copies. This complicates ownership, changes, root-cause analysis, and audit explanation.
- **Holdout degradation is substantial.** A decline from 0.973 training fitness to 0.744 holdout fitness is compatible with over-specialization, missing Supplier events, and a holdout distribution containing valid but unenumerated combinations.
- **High training precision may be memorization.** Alignment precision on a positive training log rewards a model that permits only known branch continuations. It does not establish that the model rejects a curated set of policy violations, nor that the branches correspond to formally approved behavior.
- **Maintenance cost grows combinatorially.** Adding a second approval type, a second check order, Supplier presence/absence, Expedite presence/absence, Supplier/Expedite order, and targeted correction alternatives multiplies branches. A policy change becomes a branch-editing exercise with substantial omission risk.

F8 is defensible only under a much stronger governance premise: every unseen combination must be formally treated as noncompliant, and the organization must be prepared to reject legitimate operational evolution until the variant catalogue is updated. The available policy statements do not support that premise.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

The following ranking is by likely impact on the board’s deployment decision, not by ease of measurement.

| Rank | Pathology | Why it matters here |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Missing-event bias and absent conditioning attributes** | Supplier Review may be missing, while external/internal status and affected-check information are absent. S can look strong by optionalizing events; C can look weak by treating recording gaps as noncompliance. Neither activity-only language can fully decide P3 or P6. |
| 2 | **Fitness rewarded by optionalizing mandatory behavior** | S receives high alignment fitness partly because it allows Credit Check and Technical Review to disappear. This is the most dangerous metric failure for enforcement. |
| 3 | **Train/holdout leakage or split sensitivity** | No seed, case manifest, preprocessing code, temporal boundary, or grouped split was supplied. Similar orders, customers, suppliers, or near-duplicate variants may appear in both partitions. The reported holdout gap cannot be independently interpreted. |
| 4 | **Positive-log-only precision** | Alignment precision estimates unused enabled behavior from positive traces. It does not test B3, B4, B8, or any curated set of prohibited behavior. C’s 0.941 may reflect memorized variant boundaries rather than correct policy discrimination. |
| 5 | **Unmeasured soundness and reachability** | Neither submission reports whether the converted Petri nets are sound, whether all visible transitions are reachable, whether completion is always possible, or whether hidden routing creates deadlocks or unintended behavior. |
| 6 | **Generalization rewarded by excessive permissiveness** | Token-based generalization can favor S because optional/parallel behavior leaves many transitions available. A high score does not mean the model generalizes to valid policy behavior. |
| 7 | **Alignment-cost sensitivity** | Fitness, diagnostics, and runtime depend on the cost of log moves, model moves, synchronous moves, and silent transitions. A low penalty for missing Supplier events or model skips can materially change results. |
| 8 | **Silent-transition effects** | S uses visible-or-`tau` choices to make activities optional. C’s XOR routing also creates silent structure after conversion. Silent transitions affect alignment search, fitness, precision, and simplicity without necessarily representing a business action. |
| 9 | **Duplicated-label effects** | C’s repeated labels can create multiple alignment targets for the same recorded event. This can alter precision and alignment behavior, while making label-count reporting and responsibility analysis unreliable. |
| 10 | **Runtime confounded by model size and implementation choices** | C’s 24-fold runtime difference may be real, but it may also depend on hardware, search variant, caching, timeouts, and cost functions. It is still operationally relevant, but not independently reproducible yet. |
| 11 | **Stakeholder-score methodology** | The 4.4 versus 1.9 result may reflect genuine usability differences, but no protocol establishes participant representativeness, task correctness, or whether respondents noticed policy violations in S. |

The most important conclusion is that the reported metrics cannot distinguish “a model that handles missing observations” from “a model that permits missing controls.”

# 7. Third Alternative

The recommended design is a **hybrid policy model**:

1. A compact POWL procedural backbone for the normal, non-rework control flow.
2. Explicit data and declarative checks for conditional sourcing and correction effects.
3. A tri-state policy verdict: `valid`, `invalid`, or `indeterminate`.
4. A clean-projection approach for conformance metrics: valid rework traces are projected to their first-pass backbone after the correction rules have been checked.

The POWL backbone is not the sole enforcement mechanism. In particular, a silent Supplier branch is a technical projection device for an untyped event log; it is **not** permission to omit Supplier Review for an external order.

**Compact formal description**

Let:

- `R` = Register Dossier
- `C` = Credit Check
- `T` = Technical Review
- `S` = Supplier Review
- `E` = Expedite Review
- `M` = Manager Approval
- `A` = Auto Approval
- `L` = Release Order

The clean operational backbone is:

\[
R \prec \{C,T,S^\pi,E^\pi\}
\]

\[
C \prec E^\pi,\quad T \prec E^\pi
\]

\[
\{C,T,S^\pi,E^\pi\} \prec (M \oplus A) \prec L
\]

where `S` and `E` are optional in the event-log projection, but Supplier is governed by the external-source data guard.

Additional policy constraints are:

- `Exists(C)` and `Exists(T)` before approval.
- `source_type = external => Exists(S)` before approval.
- `E => C and T occurred earlier`, and `E` precedes approval.
- Exactly one of `M` and `A` occurs.
- `R` is first and `L` is last.
- A repeated review/check requires a preceding `Correct Dossier`.
- Every `Correct Dossier` must be linked to a non-empty declared set of affected checks.
- After a correction, exactly those previously completed affected checks may repeat once before another correction or approval.
- Missing source type or missing affected-check evidence produces `indeterminate`, not silent acceptance.

**PM4Py POWL backbone**

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
    OperatorPOWL,
    StrictPartialOrder,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def optional_activity(label):
    # Optional only in the observable POWL projection.
    # Business conditionality is enforced below by policy checks.
    return OperatorPOWL(
        Operator.XOR,
        [SilentTransition(), Transition(label)]
    )


# Visible activities
register = Transition("Register Dossier")
credit = Transition("Credit Check")
technical = Transition("Technical Review")

supplier_projection = optional_activity("Supplier Review")
expedite_projection = optional_activity("Expedite Review")

approval = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR,
    [Transition("Manager Approval"), Transition("Auto Approval")]
)

release = Transition("Release Order")

# Main normal-cycle model
model_third = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[
        register,
        credit,
        technical,
        supplier_projection,
        expedite_projection,
        approval,
        release,
    ]
)

# Register first
model_third.order.add_edge(register, credit)
model_third.order.add_edge(register, technical)
model_third.order.add_edge(register, supplier_projection)
model_third.order.add_edge(register, expedite_projection)

# Expedite must follow both mandatory checks
model_third.order.add_edge(credit, expedite_projection)
model_third.order.add_edge(technical, expedite_projection)

# All review nodes precede exactly-one approval
model_third.order.add_edge(credit, approval)
model_third.order.add_edge(technical, approval)
model_third.order.add_edge(supplier_projection, approval)
model_third.order.add_edge(expedite_projection, approval)

# Release is last
model_third.order.add_edge(approval, release)

# Conversion for PM4Py diagnostics/metrics on clean, projected traces
net_third, im_third, fm_third = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)
```

This representation permits:

- Credit and Technical in either order.
- Supplier before or after the mandatory checks, but before approval, because P3 does not state a Supplier-versus-check ordering rule.
- Expedite before or after Supplier, but only after both mandatory checks.
- Exactly one approval.
- Release only after approval.

If stakeholders decide that Supplier Review must also occur after Credit and Technical, two additional precedence edges can be added. That restriction should not be invented from the current wording.

**External policy checker**

The function below is deliberately conservative. It does not infer source type or correction effect from the trace. A production implementation should obtain those values from procurement master data, a correction reason code, a case-level attribute, or a governed manual decision.

```python
from collections import Counter

R = "Register Dossier"
C = "Credit Check"
T = "Technical Review"
S = "Supplier Review"
E = "Expedite Review"
CORRECT = "Correct Dossier"
M = "Manager Approval"
A = "Auto Approval"
L = "Release Order"

REVIEWS = {C, T, S, E}
APPROVALS = {M, A}


def validate_third(
    labels,
    *,
    source_type=None,
    correction_effects=None,
):
    """
    Returns (verdict, reasons), where verdict is:
      - "valid"
      - "invalid"
      - "indeterminate"

    correction_effects maps the zero-based position of each
    Correct Dossier event to a set of affected review/check labels.

    Example for B6:
      correction_effects = {3: {"Technical Review"}}
    """

    if not labels:
        return "invalid", ["Empty trace"]

    if labels[0] != R:
        return "invalid", [f"First activity must be {R}"]

    if labels[-1] != L:
        return "invalid", [f"Last activity must be {L}"]

    if len(labels) < 3 or labels[-2] not in APPROVALS:
        return "invalid", ["Exactly one approval must immediately precede release"]

    # No second Register, Release, or approval before the final approval.
    for activity in labels[1:-2]:
        if activity in {R, L}:
            return "invalid", [f"Unexpected {activity} in the middle of the trace"]
        if activity in APPROVALS:
            return "invalid", ["Approval occurred before the final approval position"]

    counts = Counter()
    pending_rechecks = set()

    for index, activity in enumerate(labels[1:-2], start=1):
        if activity == CORRECT:
            if pending_rechecks:
                return "invalid", [
                    "A new correction occurred before all required rechecks "
                    "from the prior correction were completed"
                ]

            if correction_effects is None or index not in correction_effects:
                return "indeterminate", [
                    "Correct Dossier has no governed affected-check evidence"
                ]

            affected = set(correction_effects[index])

            if not affected:
                return "invalid", ["Correction has an empty affected-check set"]

            if not affected.issubset(REVIEWS):
                return "invalid", [
                    f"Correction names unsupported affected activities: {affected - REVIEWS}"
                ]

            # A recheck must refer to a review that has already occurred.
            unseen = {x for x in affected if counts[x] == 0}
            if unseen:
                return "invalid", [
                    f"Correction requests rechecks for activities not previously performed: {unseen}"
                ]

            pending_rechecks = affected
            continue

        if activity not in REVIEWS:
            return "invalid", [f"Unexpected activity before approval: {activity}"]

        if activity == E and (counts[C] == 0 or counts[T] == 0):
            return "invalid", [
                "Expedite Review occurred before both Credit and Technical checks"
            ]

        # A second occurrence is a recheck and requires a pending correction target.
        if counts[activity] > 0:
            if activity not in pending_rechecks:
                return "invalid", [
                    f"Repeated {activity} without a matching preceding correction"
                ]
            pending_rechecks.remove(activity)

        counts[activity] += 1

    if pending_rechecks:
        return "invalid", [
            f"Correction was not followed by all required rechecks: {pending_rechecks}"
        ]

    if counts[C] == 0 or counts[T] == 0:
        return "invalid", [
            "Credit Check and Technical Review are mandatory before approval"
        ]

    if source_type not in {None, "internal", "external"}:
        return "invalid", [f"Unknown source_type value: {source_type}"]

    if source_type == "external" and counts[S] == 0:
        return "invalid", [
            "External order lacks mandatory Supplier Review"
        ]

    if source_type is None and counts[S] == 0:
        return "indeterminate", [
            "Supplier Review is absent and source type is unavailable"
        ]

    return "valid", ["All observable and supplied data constraints satisfied"]


def project_valid_trace_to_backbone(labels):
    """
    Use only after validate_third() returns valid.
    Removes Correct Dossier and correction-triggered repeated reviews,
    retaining the first occurrence of each review activity.
    """
    retained_reviews = set()
    projected = []

    for activity in labels:
        if activity == CORRECT:
            continue
        if activity in REVIEWS:
            if activity in retained_reviews:
                continue
            retained_reviews.add(activity)
        projected.append(activity)

    return projected
```

**Explicit semantics and limitations**

- **Mandatory checks:** Credit Check and Technical Review must each occur at least once before approval. Their first occurrences may be in either order.
- **Supplier Review:** It is mandatory for `source_type="external"`. In an internal case it is not required. If source type is unknown and Supplier is absent, the result is indeterminate.
- **Expedite Review:** It is optional, but if present it must follow both mandatory checks and precede approval. It may occur before or after Supplier Review.
- **Exactly-one approval:** Exactly one of Manager Approval and Auto Approval must be the penultimate business activity before Release Order.
- **Correction/recheck behavior:** Corrections may repeat. Each correction must carry a governed affected-check set. Only activities named in that set may repeat, and each named activity must reoccur once before another correction or approval.
- **Missing attributes:** The model does not solve missing source or affected-check information by making every activity optional. It escalates those cases. Historical logs may still be used for exploratory analysis, but not treated as definitive compliance evidence.

| D1: `Register, Credit, Technical, Correct, Technical, Auto, Release`; effects = `{Technical}` | Rejects | Rejects | Accepts | Distinguishes targeted Technical-only rework plus Auto Approval from both inputs. |
| D2: `Register, Credit, Technical, Auto, Release`; `source_type="external"` | Accepts | Accepts | Rejects | Same visible trace can be invalid once sourcing data is considered. |
| D3: `Register, Supplier, Technical, Credit, Expedite, Correct, Technical, Credit, Auto, Release`; external, effects = `{Technical, Credit}` | Rejects | Rejects | Accepts | Validates alternate check order, Supplier/Expedite ordering, Auto Approval, and targeted two-check rework without branch enumeration. |

The third alternative is deliberately more demanding about evidence than Model S and more accepting of policy-consistent combinations than Model C.

# 9. Validation Experiments

A credible evaluation program should be pre-registered and reproducible.

**1. Reproducible data preparation and case splits**

- Publish the PM4Py version, Python version, operating system, package lock file, preprocessing code, event schema, lifecycle filters, timestamp tie-breaking rule, and event-ordering rule.
- Publish a secure held-out-case manifest or a privacy-preserving case-ID hash manifest with checksum.
- Fix and publish random seeds.
- Split by case, not by event.
- Use both a random stratified split and a temporal split.
- Use grouped splits where cases share an order family, supplier, customer, or dossier template.
- Preserve missingness indicators instead of silently dropping incomplete cases.

```python
import hashlib
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split
import pm4py

SEED = 20250308
CASE = "case:concept:name"
ACTIVITY = "concept:name"
TIMESTAMP = "time:timestamp"

# df must already contain a deterministic event-id column for timestamp ties.

# Example case-level metadata. Enrich source_type from procurement data where possible.

    df.groupby(CASE, as_index=False)
      .agg(
          source_type=("source_type", "first"),
          first_activity=(ACTIVITY, "first"),

holdout_df = df[df[CASE].isin(holdout_ids)].copy()

    lambda x: hashlib.sha256(x.encode("utf-8")).hexdigest()
```

Rare strata should be pre-specified and merged only according to a documented rule. A separate test should hold out an entire valid combination, such as external + Supplier + alternate check order + Auto Approval.

**2. Attribute-stratified and unseen-combination holdout tests**

Create explicit test cohorts:

- Internal versus external orders.
- Supplier present versus missing Supplier event.
- Credit-first versus Technical-first.
- Manager versus Auto Approval.
- Expedite absent, Supplier-before-Expedite, and Expedite-before-Supplier.
- One-check and two-check correction targets.
- Cases with unknown source type or unknown correction effects.

At least one experiment should remove all instances of a policy-valid combination from training, for example:

```text
Technical Review, Credit Check, Supplier Review,
Expedite Review, Auto Approval
```

The model should then be tested on that combination. A good policy model should accept it if its constraints are met; a variant memorizer should not receive credit merely for rejecting it.

**3. Curated positive and negative behavior suite**

The suite must include B1–B8, D1–D3, and additional expert-approved cases. For each trace, record:

- Expected policy verdict.
- Required source type.
- Correction-effect evidence.
- Whether a missing event is simulated.
- Expected explanation.

A zero-cost alignment is not by itself a policy approval. For Model S and Model C, report whether the trace is in the model language under positive move costs. For the third alternative, report both POWL fit and hybrid-policy verdict.

**4. Missing-event and duplicate-event perturbation tests**

Generate deterministic perturbations from known-good traces:

- Drop Supplier Review from external cases.
- Drop Credit or Technical events.
- Drop Correct Dossier but retain a repeated mandatory check.
- Duplicate Credit or Technical without adding Correct Dossier.
- Move Expedite Review before the mandatory checks.
- Add timestamp ties and alternate event ordering rules.

For each model, report raw outputs and confidence intervals separately; do not collapse them into a score.

**8. Stakeholder comprehension protocol**

Replace the informal 1–5 score with a documented, blinded, crossover protocol:

- Recruit representative compliance, operations, IT architecture, process-owner, and analyst participants.
- Randomize model presentation order and anonymize model names.
- Give the same B1–B8 and change-impact tasks to every participant.
- Measure classification accuracy, explanation accuracy, time to answer, confidence, and perceived complexity.
- Require participants to identify at least one reason why B3 and B4 are invalid.
- Publish prompts, scoring rubric, participant roles, sample size, exclusions, and inter-rater agreement.
- Treat subjective ratings as secondary to demonstrated task correctness.

**Decision rule**

Reject a candidate despite attractive aggregate metrics if any of the following occurs:

- It accepts B3, B4, or an equivalent mandatory-control negative trace.
- It cannot make or escalate the P3/P6 decision because source/effect evidence is absent.
- It fails soundness/reachability checks.
- It has unexplained train/holdout degradation.
- It depends on undocumented preprocessing, random splits, or alignment costs.
- Stakeholders cannot correctly explain mandatory controls and correction behavior.
- Its precision is supported only by positive-log evidence and not by curated negative tests.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

**Exploration**

- Model S may be retained as a clearly labelled **exploratory, permissive behavior map**.
- It must not be called a compliance model, control model, or policy-conformant model.
- Exploration reports using S should visibly flag that Credit Check, Technical Review, and Expedite precedence are not enforced.

**Compliance and enforcement**

- Neither original model should be deployed as the primary compliance engine.
- Model S should be retired from enforcement use immediately because it accepts approval without mandatory checks and Expedite-before-checks behavior.
- Model C should be retired from primary operational enforcement and simulation use because it rejects documented plausible valid combinations, lacks conditional data semantics, and makes correction logic unmaintainable.
- Model C may be retained as an immutable historical artefact or a frequent-variant registry, provided it is not represented as the normative policy model.

**Third alternative**

The hybrid third alternative is the appropriate deployment candidate, but only after the following conditions are met:

1. Source type is reliably obtained from a governed procurement/source-of-truth system.
2. Every `Correct Dossier` is linked to a governed affected-check set, correction reason, or equivalent auditable evidence.
3. The organization formally confirms whether Supplier Review is allowed before the mandatory checks and whether internal Supplier Review is merely unnecessary or prohibited.
4. The POWL backbone passes soundness and reachability checks.
5. The policy checker passes the curated positive and negative suite, including B1–B8 and approved rework examples.
6. Reproducible splits, preprocessing, alignment costs, and software versions are published.
7. Operational procedures define how `indeterminate` cases are escalated, resolved, and fed back into policy/data-quality improvement.
8. Ownership is assigned separately for process policy, source data, correction-effect coding, model versioning, and exception approval.

The board should treat the third model as a governed decision service, not merely as a Petri net with attractive metrics.

# 11. Dissenting View

> The recommendation may over-engineer a process that is, in practice, governed through approval authority rather than through exhaustive event-log semantics. Model C’s narrowness can be viewed as a feature: it accepts only variants that have actually occurred frequently enough to be discovered, while Model S creates obvious compliance exposure. A conservative organization may reasonably require a formal change request before it permits a new combination such as Supplier Review, Auto Approval, and alternate check order. From that perspective, C’s low holdout fitness is not necessarily overfitting; it may be evidence that the holdout contains unapproved workarounds, incomplete logging, or operational deviations that should be investigated rather than normalized. The proposed hybrid adds data dependencies, correction mappings, tri-state outcomes, and operational escalation queues. Those controls create their own failure modes, implementation cost, and audit burden. If affected-check data are unreliable, the third model could produce many indeterminate cases and push decisions back to humans, reducing the practical value of automation. A carefully governed version of C, with strict change control and a documented exception process, may therefore be safer and simpler than a more expressive hybrid.

I would change the recommendation if the organization produced evidence that: all C branches are formally approved; C-rejected holdout cases are independently adjudicated as noncompliant or logging errors; source type and correction effects cannot be made reliably available; and a curated negative suite shows that C rejects critical prohibited behavior without materially blocking approved work.
