# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy

## 2.1 Conceptual separation

| Concept | Operational test |
|---|---|
| **Disparity** | Any difference in outcome, path or time between groups (raw or adjusted). Descriptive only. |
| **Legitimate routing** | The difference is fully explained by policy-mandated triggers (risk band  Fraud Review, accommodation request  Accommodation Review, language request  Language Support), applied consistently, with timeliness comparable to equivalent steps. |
| **Unjustified procedural disparity / disparate impact** | Differences persist among comparable cases, or arise in queue time, handovers or escalation beyond what the mandated step requires. Intent is not required. This is a system-design finding. |
| **Discrimination** | Differential treatment of otherwise-similar cases because of group membership. It needs stronger evidence: within-caseworker comparisons, blind re-review, audit or vignette tests, and exclusion of mediator and proxy explanations. The event log alone cannot establish it. |
| **Uncertainty** | Reported explicitly through confidence intervals, sensitivity bounds and "unmeasured" lists. |

## 2.2 Matching or stratification variables

- **Pre-treatment covariates:** region, submission month or week, channel, household_size_band, prior_case_count, program or claim type (if available), and initial documentation completeness at submission, measured before any request.
- **Questionable covariates:** treat risk_score_band and documentation_completeness as potentially tainted. Fit three specifications and compare them: (a) all covariates, (b) without risk band, (c) with the risk band's underlying inputs instead.
- **Use a 2×2 need-based design** instead of group labels alone:
  - B with language need vs B without vs A.
  - C with accommodation request vs C without vs A.
  - This separates "belonging to group X" from "using a support step".
- Use coarsened exact matching or overlap weighting with a doubly robust outcome model. Cluster standard errors by caseworker and region. Report overlap diagnostics and discard non-overlapping cases explicitly.
- Add workload at arrival (daily queue length per team) as a covariate in timeliness models. It is a mechanism, not a nuisance.

## 2.3 Path and variant comparison

- Compare variant mix by group within matched strata, not just overall.
- Use a Kitagawa/Oaxaca-style decomposition of the cycle-time gap into **composition** (different variant mix, e.g., B in V4) and **within-variant** effects (same variant, slower for one group).
- Within-variant checks, for example V2 by group, and V1 by group, since V1 is the fast path.
- Compute routing conformance at case level: share of high-risk cases with Fraud Review, of accommodation requests with Accommodation Review, and of language-need cases with Language Support Contact. Check both under-routing and over-routing.
- Compare **observed vs expected** Request Documents frequency given the incomplete-documentation share. For example, B has 58% (V2+V4) against 46% incomplete, a +12 pp excess. A has 25% against 21%, +4 pp. This is suggestive only, since the variants shown are not exhaustive.

## 2.4 Resource-assignment comparison

- Caseload distribution by caseworker and group (Gini, top-k concentration, cases per worker per day). For example, roughly 57% × 1250  710 B cases need language support, handled by 7 staff.
- Caseworker fixed or random effects: does the group effect on approval, time or escalation shrink or persist within caseworker?
- Handover counts, ping-pong loops (general  specialist  general), and handovers per case by group, region and role.
- Dual-role load: for the 2 accommodation specialists who also supervise, compare their supervisor-review queue time before and after accommodation load rises.
- Exploit natural experiments (leave, vacancies, team reorganizations) to estimate the causal effect of capacity on waits.

## 2.5 Waiting-time decomposition

Decompose each case's duration into service time (work), queue or waiting time (between steps), and applicant-caused time (waiting for documents). Then:

- Attribute excess time by step and group: support-step queue, Request Docs queue, Supervisor queue, verification queue (channel effect).
- Separate **inherent step time** from **queue time** for support steps. The inherent step is justified, while queue time is a capacity choice.
- Use business-hours calendars where possible.
- **Clock-stop issue:** time awaiting applicant documents is partly legitimate, but may be prolonged by language barriers, so report it separately.
- Use Decision  Notification as a **negative control**. It is clerical, shows a small gap (0.5 vs 0.8d), and is a sanity check for time bias.

## 2.6 Sensitivity analysis for possibly biased covariates

- **Specification curve:** with and without risk_score_band, with and without documentation_completeness, using the initial completeness rather than the final one.
- **Tipping-point analysis:** how much group-dependent over-scoring of risk would be needed to erase the residual gap?
- **E-values or Rosenbaum-style bounds:** how strong would an unmeasured confounder need to be?
- **Selective-label caution:** fraud findings only exist where review occurred, so do not use them as unbiased ground truth.
- **Blind re-adjudication** of a random sample of decisions, with group, language, and accommodation flags masked. It tests whether merits-equivalent files reach equivalent outcomes.
- **Negative-control outcomes** unlikely to depend on group bias (e.g., Decision  Notify timing).
- **Mediation framing:** distinguish "direct" from "through barriers" effects. Adjusting for documentation completeness, for example, removes barrier-mediated disadvantage from the estimate.

## 2.7 Subgroup checks by region and channel

- Stratify by region, especially the two backlog regions and the two regions with extra handovers. Test whether B and C gaps are driven by where those groups apply or occur within each region.
- Stratify by channel (in-person vs online), particularly in B, where in-person is 38%. Channel may be a consequence of access barriers, so adjusting for it may over-adjust.
- Check interaction terms (group × region, group × channel) with hierarchical or shrinkage estimators, to avoid over-reading small cells.
- Correct for multiple comparisons and suppress or flag cells below a minimum count.
- Check intersectional cases (language need and accommodation, high risk and language need). Groups may overlap in reality.

# 4. Plausible mechanisms

1. **Capacity bottleneck (structural, legitimate need but unjust outcome).** A few specialists carry all support demand: roughly 100 support cases per specialist in B and in C, against about 70 cases per caseworker for A across 52 staff. Queues form at the support steps.
2. **Legitimate complexity.** High-risk routing creates longer fraud paths for D. Incomplete documentation creates legitimate Request Docs loops.
3. **Missing service targets.** SLAs do not cover support steps, so support-step queues have no owner and can be deprioritized in favor of measured work. Case prioritization may favor cases that protect aggregate SLA metrics.
4. **Role overload and dual roles.** Specialists who also do supervisor review face competing queues, which slows both Accommodation Review and Supervisor Review.
5. **Fragmented ownership and handovers.** The general  specialist  general pattern means no one owns the case end to end. This loses information, causes repeated contact and rework, and may contribute to appeals.
6. **Documentation barriers.** Language barriers and accessibility issues cause incomplete or rejected documents that would be fine with assistance. This yields more Request Docs, longer waits, and lower approval that looks "explained" by documentation_completeness while being partly caused by access barriers.
7. **Defensive or discretionary escalation (potentially concerning).** Caseworkers may escalate cases with limited-English or accommodation needs because they feel uncertain, or because of stereotypes. This would show as a Supervisor Review excess after adjustment, varying across caseworkers.
8. **Risk-score feedback loop.** Prior-case count and risk band may reflect past enhanced scrutiny of certain populations. Higher scrutiny produces more flags and more prior cases, which produces higher scores and more fraud reviews.
9. **Regional backlog confounding.** Groups may be concentrated in backlogged regions, so the delay is geographic but produces group-level disadvantage.
10. **Under-provision or mis-flagging of support.** If many needing support do not receive it, they may proceed on the standard path with worse understanding and outcomes. If needs flags are inconsistently recorded, routing looks erratic.
11. **Weak decision explanation.** If notices are not accessible, applicants are more likely to appeal, and less able to cure deficiencies earlier. This increases appeal and rework.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

- **Measurement bias.** risk_score_band may encode historical disparities, since prior-case count and past flags reflect past scrutiny. Conditioning on it can absorb real bias (understating disparity for D) or legitimate risk. documentation_completeness is affected by language and access barriers, as well as by how strictly staff check. It is a mediator as well as a confounder. Channel may be partly a consequence of access constraints.
- **Missing applicant needs.** Needs that were never identified, never recorded, or never met are invisible. The "needs language support" and "accommodation requested" flags depend on self-report and on staff recording. Type and intensity of need (simple interpretation vs complex accommodation) are not captured. Intersecting needs (language and disability) are not modelled.
- **Unobserved resource workload.** Daily granularity hides intra-day queues, skills, experience, part-time status, leave, and whether "waiting" is real idle queueing or other work. We cannot cleanly separate understaffing from poor assignment. Caseworker experience and seniority are unknown.
- **Appeal quality.** Appeal rate reflects willingness and ability to appeal, not quality. Without appeal outcomes (overturn, partial remedy), appeal review time, and representation, appeal differences cannot be interpreted as error or fairness. Appeal may also be suppressed in some groups.
- **Historical bias in risk scores.** High-risk routing is "legitimate" only if the score is valid and equally calibrated across groups. No calibration or false-positive data by group is given. Fraud-review findings are a selective label.
- **Outcome limits.** case_outcome does not capture explanation quality, dignity, respectful communication, or whether the applicant understood the process. A fair result can coexist with a burdensome or disrespectful process.
- **Model limits.** The adjusted model is "preliminary", with no confidence intervals or clustering reported, and the cycle-time metric (mean vs median) is unclear. Variant data shows only top paths. Timestamps are single-point, so service vs waiting time cannot be separated without start times. Groups may overlap, which complicates group-level inference.
- **Causal limits.** Non-random assignment, unmeasured complexity, and possible over- or under-adjustment mean that the residual gaps are neither proven bias nor proven to be zero.

| **Auditing risk_score_band and documentation_completeness** | Test calibration and false-positive rates by group. Examine the inputs of the score. Test whether documentation completeness judgments differ by language or channel. Re-review a blind sample. Evaluate alternatives such as human-in-the-loop review for borderline flags. | Changing the score may temporarily weaken fraud controls or create inconsistency. Audits need independent expertise and resources. Loosening documentation rules might increase errors. Findings may be contested. |
| **Proactive needs identification and ownership of routing rules** | Standardize needs flags, add intake prompts, and track whether supported applicants actually receive support. | Additional intake burden and data-quality overhead. Must avoid coercive or stigmatizing disclosure. |
| **Staged evaluation** | Pilot changes in a few regions with comparison regions, tracking outcomes and staff workload. | Slower rollout, and unequal treatment during the pilot (mitigate by providing capacity first to the worst-affected regions). |

None of these options removes or shortens the support steps by reducing their substance. The aim is to cut queue and friction around them, not the rights they protect.

# 7. PM4Py analysis plan

The code is indicative. Function signatures and output column names vary by PM4Py version, so check them before use. Comments are given as docstrings to keep the code self-contained.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf

CASE, ACT, TS, RES = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp", "org:resource"
SUPPORT = ["Language Support Contact", "Accommodation Review"]

def load(path):
    """Load and format the event log."""
    df = pd.read_csv(path)
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS)
    return df.sort_values([CASE, TS])

def by_group(df, g):
    """Case-level filter on one applicant group."""
    return pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        df, "applicant_group", [g], level="case", retain=True)

def case_table(df):
    """One row per case: duration, handovers, flags, static covariates."""
    g = df.groupby(CASE)
    c = g.agg(start=(TS, "min"), end=(TS, "max"), n_events=(ACT, "size"),
              n_resources=(RES, "nunique"),
              grp=("applicant_group", "first"), region=("region", "first"),
              channel=("channel", "first"), risk=("risk_score_band", "first"),
              docs=("documentation_completeness", "first"),
              hh=("household_size_band", "first"), prior=("prior_case_count", "first"),
              lang=("language_preference", "first"),
              accom=("disability_accommodation_requested", "first"),
              outcome=("case_outcome", "first"), appeal=("appeal_flag", "max"),
              main_res=(RES, lambda s: s.mode().iat[0])).reset_index()
    c["days"] = (c["end"] - c["start"]).dt.total_seconds() / 86400
    c["approved"] = (c["outcome"] == "Approved").astype(int)
    c["month"] = c["start"].dt.to_period("M").astype(str)
    acts = df.groupby(CASE)[ACT].agg(set)
    for a in ["Supervisor Review", "Request Missing Documents", "Fraud Review",
              "Language Support Contact", "Accommodation Review"]:
        c["has_" + a.split()[0].lower()] = c[CASE].map(acts.map(lambda s: a in s)).astype(int)
    hand = df.groupby(CASE)[RES].apply(lambda s: (s != s.shift()).sum() - 1)
    c["handovers"] = c[CASE].map(hand)
    return c

def variant_mix(df, top=10):
    """Variant share by group, plus within-variant median duration."""
    rows = []
    for g in sorted(df["applicant_group"].unique()):
        v = pm4py.get_variants(by_group(df, g))
        n = sum(v.values())
        rows += [(g, k, cnt / n) for k, cnt in v.items()]
    mix = pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["grp", "variant", "share"]).pivot(
        index="variant", columns="grp", values="share").fillna(0)
    return mix.sort_values(mix.columns.tolist(), ascending=False).head(top)

def within_variant_gap(df, variant_tuple):
    """Keep one variant and compare durations by group (composition vs within-variant)."""
    sub = pm4py.filter_variants(df, [variant_tuple], retain=True)
    d = case_table(sub)
    return d.groupby("grp")["days"].describe(percentiles=[.5, .9])

def step_waits(df):
    """Median/90th percentile wait between consecutive activities, by group, region, channel."""
    d = df.sort_values([CASE, TS]).copy()
    d["next_act"] = d.groupby(CASE)[ACT].shift(-1)
    d["next_ts"] = d.groupby(CASE)[TS].shift(-1)
    d["wait_d"] = (d["next_ts"] - d[TS]).dt.total_seconds() / 86400
    d = d.dropna(subset=["next_act"])
    d["edge"] = d[ACT] + " -> " + d["next_act"]
    keys = ["edge", "applicant_group", "region", "channel"]
    return d.groupby(keys)["wait_d"].agg(["count", "median",
           lambda s: s.quantile(.9)]).reset_index()

def perf_dfg_by_group(df):
    """Frequency and performance DFGs per group (perf DFG defaults to the mean;
    use step_waits for medians/percentiles)."""
    out = {}
    for g in df["applicant_group"].unique():
        sub = by_group(df, g)
        dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_dfg(sub)
        perf, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(sub)
        out[g] = (dfg, perf)
    return out

def service_waiting(df, start_col=None):
    """Insert service/waiting/sojourn time per case. Needs a start timestamp per event;
    with only one timestamp, service time will be ~0. Check df.columns after the call."""
    start_col = start_col or TS
    d = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
        df, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE,
        start_timestamp_key=start_col)
    st = pm4py.get_service_time(df, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS,
                                case_id_key=CASE, start_timestamp_key=start_col)
    return d, st

def slow_tail(df, q=0.9):
    """Keep cases above the 90th-percentile duration (for path/resource inspection)."""
    durs = pm4py.get_all_case_durations(df)
    cut = float(np.quantile(durs, q))
    return pm4py.filter_case_performance(df, cut, float(max(durs)))

def support_delay(df, a, b, lo_days=3):
    """Cases with a slow edge a->b (e.g., Language Support Contact -> Eligibility Assessment)."""
    return pm4py.filter_paths_performance(
        df, (a, b), min_performance=lo_days * 86400, max_performance=10**9, keep=True)

def resource_networks(df):
    """Handover, working-together, similarity networks and roles, per group."""
    res = {}
    for g in df["applicant_group"].unique():
        sub = by_group(df, g)
        res[g] = dict(
            handover=pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(sub),
            together=pm4py.discover_working_together_network(sub),
            similarity=pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(sub),
            roles=pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(sub))
    return res

def caseload_concentration(cases):
    """Cases per caseworker by group, and Gini coefficient."""
    t = cases.groupby(["grp", "main_res"]).size().rename("n").reset_index()
    def gini(x):
        x = np.sort(x.values); n = len(x)
        return (2 * np.arange(1, n + 1) - n - 1).dot(x) / (n * x.sum())
    return t.groupby("grp")["n"].agg(["count", "mean", "max", gini])

def pingpong(df, role_of):
    """Count general -> specialist -> general loops per case (role_of: resource -> role)."""
    d = df.assign(role=df[RES].map(role_of))
    def loops(s):
        r = s.tolist(); r = [x for i, x in enumerate(r) if i == 0 or x != r[i - 1]]
        return sum(1 for i in range(len(r) - 2)
                   if r[i] == "general" and r[i + 2] == "general" and r[i + 1] != "general")
    return d.groupby(CASE)["role"].apply(loops)

def declarative_checks(df):
    """Log skeleton / Declare per group, plus explicit policy rules."""
    skel = {g: pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(by_group(df, g), noise_threshold=0.05)
            for g in df["applicant_group"].unique()}
    decl = {g: pm4py.discover_declare(by_group(df, g), min_support_ratio=0.3)
            for g in df["applicant_group"].unique()}
    return skel, decl

def rule_conformance(df, cases):
    """Case-level routing conformance: trigger -> required activity, and ordering."""
    c = cases.copy()
    c["hi_risk"] = (c["risk"] == "High").astype(int)
    c["need_lang"] = (c["lang"] != "majority").astype(int)
    c["need_acc"] = (c["accom"] == 1).astype(int)
    out = {
        "highrisk_no_fraud": c[(c.hi_risk == 1) & (c.has_fraud == 0)].groupby("grp").size(),
        "lowrisk_fraud": c[(c.hi_risk == 0) & (c.has_fraud == 1)].groupby("grp").size(),
        "lang_need_no_support": c[(c.need_lang == 1) & (c.has_language == 0)].groupby("grp").size(),
        "acc_req_no_review": c[(c.need_acc == 1) & (c.has_accommodation == 0)].groupby("grp").size(),
        "supervisor_without_trigger": c[(c.has_supervisor == 1)].groupby("grp").size()}
    return out

def adjusted_models(cases):
    """Specification curve with caseworker-clustered SEs: approval, cycle time, escalation."""
    base = "C(grp, Treatment('A')) + C(channel) + C(hh) + prior + C(region) + C(month)"
    specs = {"full": base + " + C(risk) + C(docs)",
             "no_risk": base + " + C(docs)",
             "no_docs": base + " + C(risk)",
             "minimal": base}
    out = {}
    for name, rhs in specs.items():
        for y, fam in [("approved", "logit"), ("days", "ols"),
                       ("has_supervisor", "logit"), ("has_request", "logit")]:
            f = f"{y} ~ {rhs}"
            m = (smf.logit if fam == "logit" else smf.ols)(f, data=cases).fit(
                disp=0, cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": cases["main_res"]})
            out[(name, y)] = m.params.filter(like="grp")
    return out

def need_based_contrast(cases):
    """B with/without language need vs A; C with/without accommodation vs A."""
    cases = cases.assign(seg=np.select(
        [(cases.grp == "B") & (cases.lang != "majority"), cases.grp == "B",
         (cases.grp == "C") & (cases.accom == 1), cases.grp == "C"],
        ["B_need", "B_noneed", "C_accom", "C_noaccom"], default=cases.grp))
    return smf.ols("days ~ C(seg, Treatment('A')) + C(risk) + C(docs) + C(channel) "
                   "+ C(region) + C(month)", data=cases).fit(
        cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": cases["main_res"]}).params

def e_value(rr):
    """E-value for a risk ratio (use 1/rr if rr < 1)."""
    rr = max(rr, 1 / rr)
    return rr + np.sqrt(rr * (rr - 1))

def region_channel_checks(cases):
    """Subgroup table: group x region x channel, with counts to suppress small cells."""
    t = cases.groupby(["region", "channel", "grp"]).agg(
        n=(CASE, "size"), approve=("approved", "mean"), median_days=("days", "median"),
        sup=("has_supervisor", "mean"), req=("has_request", "mean"),
        handovers=("handovers", "mean")).reset_index()
    return t[t.n >= 30]

def run(path):
    df = load(path)
    cases = case_table(df)
    return dict(mix=variant_mix(df), waits=step_waits(df),
                conformance=rule_conformance(df, cases),
                models=adjusted_models(cases), contrast=need_based_contrast(cases),
                load=caseload_concentration(cases),
                subgroup=region_channel_checks(cases),
                networks=resource_networks(df), decl=declarative_checks(df))
```

**How the outputs feed the assessment:**

- `variant_mix` and `within_variant_gap` separate composition from within-variant effects.
- `step_waits` and `service_waiting` isolate queue time at support, documentation, supervisor and verification steps.
- `rule_conformance` tests routing consistency, including the 57% vs 24% and 62% vs 31% gaps and the V3 and V4 path questions.
- `resource_networks`, `caseload_concentration` and `pingpong` quantify resource concentration and handover loops.
- `adjusted_models`, `need_based_contrast` and `e_value` provide the specification curve and sensitivity analysis.
- `region_channel_checks` tests for regional and channel heterogeneity.

# 8. Bottom-line assessment

**Indeterminate on discrimination, with probable procedural unfairness in how support steps are resourced.**

- **Supported by the evidence:**
  - Most of the raw approval and cycle-time gaps are associated with case mix. They should not be read as discrimination.
  - Residual disparities remain after adjustment: lower approval for B (5.5 pp), more Request Docs and Supervisor Review for B and C, longer adjusted cycle time for C (+4.1d) and B (+2.6d), and higher appeal for D (+5.7 pp) and B (+4.4 pp).
  - Rights-preserving steps are attached to long queues (3.8d and 4.7d), concentrated on very few staff, and not covered by service targets. These are structural procedural burdens on applicants using legitimate rights, and they appear more likely to reflect system design and capacity than individual bias.
- **Not established:**
  - Whether the residual approval gaps reflect unequal treatment of equivalent cases. Unmeasured complexity, tainted covariates (risk band, documentation completeness) and over-adjustment are all live explanations, and no confidence intervals are provided.
  - Whether Supervisor Review and Request Docs excesses are protective or burdensome.
  - Whether D's routing is fair, since it depends on the unvalidated risk score.
  - Routing consistency. The 57% vs 24% and 62% vs 31% contrasts, the V3 path, and the missing notification in V4 may be artifacts of partial variant reporting.
- **Overall judgment:** the process is not demonstrably procedurally fair. There is moderate evidence of unjustified procedural burden for Groups B and C, particularly in queue time, escalation and handovers. The evidence for Group D is weaker and depends mainly on risk-score validity and appeal outcomes. Findings that would change this judgment are:
  - case-level conformance showing consistent routing;
  - within-caseworker and blind re-review evidence of equal treatment;
  - shown calibration of the risk score across groups;
  - queue times falling to parity once capacity is added.

Immediate steps that do not depend on resolving the causal questions are to set support-step timeliness targets, relieve the dual-role bottleneck, reduce handovers, and audit routing conformance, the risk band, and documentation standards.
