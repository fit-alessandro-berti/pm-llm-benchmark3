## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

| Objective / threat scenario | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Prevent one operator from unilaterally initiating and authorizing payment** | Partly prevents through programmed checks, but is not an independent human authorization and cannot establish who controls a compromised account. | Prevents if an independent, qualified manager performs substantive review before release or irrevocable settlement. | Detects after release; corrects only while cancellation, recall, or recovery remains viable. | Conditionally permits single-operator processing but requires layered controls; does not itself eliminate concentration-of-authority risk. |
| **2. Detect erroneous or duplicate payments** | Strong prevention for duplicate invoices, operator limits, and basic PO mismatch. Coverage is incomplete for receipt and commercial substance. | Can prevent if supporting invoice, receipt, amount, and bank details are substantively reviewed. | Detects errors after release and may support recall. | Requires AV-2, pre-settlement MG-4, and timely audit, creating layered coverage. |
| **3. Prevent payment to an altered, sanctioned, or wrongly owned bank account** | Strong for sanctions, ownership, and recent bank changes when operational and not overridden. | Can prevent through independent bank-detail review, although quality depends on evidence and reviewer diligence. | May detect the issue, but correction deteriorates sharply after the recall window. | Disallows recent bank changes and requires a clean, non-overridden AV-2 pass. |
| **4. Establish legitimate commercial basis and receipt of goods/services** | Fails to establish receipt, collusion, or commercial reasonableness; basic PO matching is only partial evidence. | Potentially strong if invoice, receipt/service report, commercial basis, and bank details are actually reviewed. | Can detect fabricated or absent support, but possibly too late to recover funds. | Relies principally on substantive MG-4 review here; AV-2 alone is insufficient. |
| **5. Resist fraud, credential misuse, and collusion** | Can block known rule violations but is overrideable and cannot determine who controlled an account. It is weak against collusive fabrication that passes its checks. | Adds resistance only if genuinely independent, conflict-free, credential-secure, and substantive. | Can identify patterns and investigate misconduct, but usually after exposure. | Adds procedural constraints, not immunity from shared credentials, collusion, or rubber-stamping. |
| **6. Preserve the opportunity to stop or recover an improper payment** | Prevents release if it blocks; warnings are weaker, especially when overridden. | Approval after release but before settlement may still stop settlement and is therefore partly preventive/corrective, depending on system authority. | Detective and partly corrective when completed early enough for cancellation or recall. | Requires approval before settlement and audit within one business day, but the package is strongest when approval precedes release. |
| **7. Create reliable attribution, accountability, and audit evidence** | Produces machine evidence of checks, warnings, blocks, and overrides, but not human identity assurance. | Adds accountable human judgment if reviewer identity, level, independence, evidence viewed, and decision are recorded. | Provides retrospective evidence and pattern detection. | Emergency token and required control records provide an exception trail, but only if activated and logged before preparation as specified. |
| **8. Limit routine normalization of emergency or concentrated processing** | Does not establish necessity or prevent misuse of emergency pathways by itself. | Independent review can challenge necessity and substance. | Monitoring can detect repeated emergency use, same-user behavior, and recurring pairs. | Strict eligibility conditions constrain emergency use; any failed condition means ED-1 permission is unavailable. |

The central objective is not merely two different usernames. It is to prevent unilateral action, introduce independent challenge before value becomes irrecoverable, cover material payment risks, and preserve reliable attribution and recovery options.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating package should be assessed through the following structured test.

### A. Gateway: scope and permission

1. Was there a completed payment?
2. If the same person prepared and released it, were **all** ED-1 conditions satisfied?
3. Was the exception necessary and authorized, rather than merely convenient?
4. Were system identities attributable to independent human actors?

Failure of an ED-1 condition makes the transaction technically nonconforming; it does not automatically establish that every control objective failed.

### B. Timing

Assess each control relative to:

- preparation;
- release;
- irrevocable bank settlement;
- the normal 24-hour recall window.

A control before release is genuinely preventive. A substantive approval after release but before settlement can still prevent economic loss if it has authority and operational capacity to stop settlement, but it is weaker than pre-release approval because the transaction has already entered execution. Audit after the recall window is mostly detective.

### C. Independence and authority

Determine whether the reviewer:

- is L3 or above;
- differs from the preparer/releaser;
- has no unresolved conflict;
- did not lend or share credentials;
- has actual authority to block or cancel;
- performed an independent rather than scripted review.

Reporting-line proximity is not automatically disqualifying, but direct supervision, credential use, personal relationships, and incentive conflicts require scrutiny.

### D. Coverage

Map controls to the actual loss mechanisms:

- duplicate invoice;
- sanctions;
- bank ownership or recent account change;
- operator limit;
- PO match;
- receipt of goods/services;
- commercial reasonableness;
- credential compromise;
- collusion;
- emergency necessity.

A clean AV-2 result is strong only for its enumerated checks. It does not prove receipt, commercial legitimacy, or absence of collusion.

### E. Overrideability and control integrity

Evaluate whether:

- a warning or hard block occurred;
- the warning was overridden;
- the override credential belonged to the user who exercised it;
- override reasons and independent authorization exist;
- the validation service was fully operational;
- manual fallbacks were signed and reliable.

A readily overridden warning provides materially less assurance than a hard block.

### F. Recoverability

Ask whether an error could still be:

- stopped before settlement;
- canceled internally;
- recalled within 24 hours;
- recovered later through legal or contractual action.

Detective controls are not equivalent to preventive ones where recovery is uncertain or impossible.

### G. Evidence quality

Require evidence of what was reviewed, not merely an event name. Strong evidence includes:

- immutable timestamps;
- reviewer identity and level;
- documents opened or checks completed;
- decision rationale;
- system health and rule-set version;
- bank response and settlement status;
- audit findings and recall actions.

A bare `Manager_Ratify` event may be little more than a rubber stamp.

### H. Collusion resistance

Assess separation not only by username but also by:

- shared workstation or session;
- repeated preparer/releaser pair;
- common reporting or incentive structure;
- unusual throughput;
- shared credentials;
- fabricated supporting evidence;
- rotation and challenge behavior.

Distinct users may satisfy FEP-1 formally while leaving the control objective only partly met.

### Sufficiency levels

| Level | Test |
|---|---|
| **Fully equivalent** | Material threats are covered by independent, substantive, non-overridden controls before irreversible loss; evidence is strong; attribution and collusion resistance approximate a true independent second releaser; recoverability remains available. |
| **Partially equivalent** | Most material risks are covered, but timing, scope, independence, or collusion resistance is weaker than true four-eyes. Residual risk is bounded and visible. |
| **Merely mitigating** | Controls reduce some risk but leave a material causal path open—for example, post-settlement ratification, unavailable AV-2, or audit after recall. |
| **Ineffective** | Controls are absent, bypassed, conflicted, too late, unreliable, or irrelevant to the dominant loss mechanism. |

“Same expected loss” is not the only standard. Governance may also value deterrence, attribution, legal or regulatory compliance, tail-risk limitation, procedural legitimacy, resilience against common-mode compromise, and avoidance of a single person possessing end-to-end authority. Two packages with similar average losses can differ substantially in catastrophic-loss exposure, detectability, accountability, and susceptibility to collusion.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control | Preventive role | Detective role | Corrective role | Principal limitations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FEP-1 independent releaser** | Independent challenge before release; prevents unilateral authorization | May notice anomalies during authorization | Usually no separate corrective function unless the releaser causes rework or rejection | Can be defeated by collusion, credential sharing, rubber-stamping, or nominally distinct but dependent actors |
| **AV-2 hard block** | Strong for covered rule violations; stops release | Records the detected anomaly | Enables correction of invoice or bank data before resubmission | Limited scope; no receipt, commercial-reasonableness, collusion, or account-control assurance |
| **AV-2 clean pass** | Preventive assurance for covered checks | Produces evidence that listed checks found no issue | None by itself | A clean pass is not proof of transaction legitimacy |
| **AV-2 warning and override** | Weak prevention because release remains possible | Strongly signals an exception | May prompt remediation if escalated before settlement | Override authority and credential integrity are critical; self-override can nullify the control |
| **MG-4 before release** | Strongly preventive when independent and substantive | Detects anomalies during review | Can require correction before release | Rubber stamps and conflicted reviewers provide little assurance |
| **MG-4 after release but before settlement** | **Hybrid**: detective with respect to the already executed release, but economically preventive/corrective if the manager can stop settlement | Detects an error after authorization | Can cancel or stop the payment before irrevocability | Weaker than pre-release approval; classification depends on actual settlement hold and stop authority |
| **MG-4 after settlement** | Not preventive | Detective/ratifying only | Usually weak; legal recovery may remain exceptional | Cannot reliably reverse the loss |
| **RA-7 within one business day and recall window** | Not preventive of release | Strong retrospective detection | Partly corrective through cancellation, recall, or rapid recovery | Recall is not guaranteed; audit quality and completion time matter |
| **RA-7 after recall window** | None | Detective | Weak and uncertain | Primarily supports investigation, discipline, and future remediation |
| **ED-1** | A permission framework that mandates preventive layers and constrains emergency use | Requires rapid audit and exception evidence | Preserves pre-settlement intervention and recall opportunities | It is ineffective as permission if any mandatory condition fails |

For classification semantics, `Release_Payment` marks internal authorization, while settlement marks economic irrevocability. Thus an MG-4 action between release and settlement is detective relative to the authorization defect, corrective because it can stop an already initiated payment, and preventive relative to final economic loss. I therefore call it **hybrid**, while recognizing that it is inferior to approval before release.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

### Evidence collection

1. **Human identity and independence**
   - Maintain identity-to-account mappings, manager level, reporting relationships, conflicts, and temporary delegations.
   - Record workstation, session, device, IP, MFA event, and credential owner for preparation, override, release, and approval.
   - Preserve evidence of role rotation and whether a repeated pair is an approved payment cell.

2. **Manager review quality**
   - Record whether the manager opened and reviewed the invoice, bank evidence, PO, receipt/service report, and exception rationale.
   - Capture decision notes, elapsed review time, authority to stop settlement, and whether the payment was actually held pending review.
   - Distinguish `Manager_Approve` from retrospective `Manager_Ratify`.

3. **AV-2 integrity**
   - Preserve rule-set version, service-health status, each individual check result, warning/block reason, overrides, override credential, and justification.
   - Require dual authorization for high-risk overrides, especially recent bank changes and operator-limit warnings.
   - Log manual fallback evidence and prohibit unsigned checklists.

4. **Emergency authorization**
   - Record token issue time, activation time, scope, issuer, reason, expiry, and the first activity it covers.
   - Synchronize clocks and preserve raw source logs so a logging-delay claim can be tested.
   - Enforce ED-1 eligibility automatically rather than relying only on retrospective review.

5. **Settlement and recovery**
   - Capture release, bank submission, settlement, cancellation, recall initiation, bank response, and recovery timestamps.
   - Record both audit start and completion. ED-1 requires completed audit within one business day.

### Monitoring rules

| Metric / rule | Suggested monitoring treatment |
|---|---|
| **Same-user rate** | Track completed same-user prepare/release cases by operator, unit, emergency status, and period. Alert on unexplained concentration or use without a valid pre-existing token. |
| **Override rate** | Monitor warning overrides by user, warning type, credential owner, vendor, and amount band. Immediate escalation for self-overrides or another person’s credential. |
| **Late manager approval** | Flag approval after release; escalate separately if after settlement. Measure time from release to approval and verify stop authority. |
| **Audit-before-recall-window** | Track audits completed within 24 hours and within one business day. Alert when a same-user emergency case misses either relevant threshold. |
| **Repeated resource pairs** | Identify high-frequency preparer/releaser pairs, reciprocal pairs, and unusually exclusive pairing. Compare with documented team design and rotate reviewers where practical. |
| **Shared workstations** | Alert when nominally independent actors use the same workstation/session or overlapping credentials, especially for repeated pairs. |
| **Bank-change proximity** | Escalate payments within seven days of a bank change; prohibit ED-1 and require out-of-band bank ownership verification. |
| **Control outages** | Monitor AV-2 availability, payments released during outages, unsigned fallback checks, and post-outage reconciliation. High-risk releases should be blocked rather than merely reported. |
| **Credential anomalies** | Alert when an override or approval is exercised with credentials assigned to another person, from an unexpected device, or without expected MFA. |
| **Receipt anomalies** | Correlate duplicate service descriptions, reused documents, metadata inconsistencies, and post-payment receipt creation; route to substantive review. |

These are risk signals, not automatic findings of misconduct. Repeated pairing may be an approved two-person cell; a shared workstation may be operationally legitimate; late logs may reflect clock or ingestion faults. Each alert requires corroboration before concluding collusion, credential sharing, or fraud.

```python
    if col is None:
        raise RuntimeError(

    return set(df[col].dropna().astype(str))

blocked_attempt_cases = set(
    events.loc[events[ACT_COL].eq("Attempt_Release"), CASE_COL]
) - completed_cases
blocked_attempt_cases &= set(
    events.loc[events[ACT_COL].eq("Automated_Validate_Block"), CASE_COL]
)

# Compact structured facts derived from the supplied case/control table.
facts = pd.DataFrame([
    ["AP-201", 1800,   2190, False, False, False, "pass",        None,                    True],
    ["AP-202", 480000, None, False, True,  False, "warning_override", None,                False],
    ["AP-203", 75000,  1460, True,  False, True,  "pass",        "2026-05-04T09:55:00Z", True],
    ["AP-204", 220000, 730,  False, False, False, "pass",        None,                    False],
    ["AP-205", 12000,  None, False, False, False, "blocked",     None,                    True],
    ["AP-206", 95000,  1095, False, False, True,  "pass",        "2026-05-04T13:02:00Z", True],
    ["AP-207", 35000,  3285, False, False, True,  "pass",        "2026-05-04T13:50:00Z", True],
    ["AP-208", 900000, 240,  True,  False, True,  "unavailable", None,                    True],
    ["AP-209", 42000,  1825, False, False, False, "pass",        None,                    True],
], columns=[
    CASE_COL, "amount_eur", "vendor_age_days", "bank_change_lt_7d",
    "new_vendor", "emergency_token_reported", "av_result",
    "token_active_time", "no_defect_reported"
])
facts["token_active_time"] = pd.to_datetime(
    facts["token_active_time"], utc=True, errors="coerce"
)

def first_time(case, activities):
    rows = events[
        events[CASE_COL].eq(case) & events[ACT_COL].isin(activities)
    ]
    return rows[TIME_COL].min() if not rows.empty else pd.NaT

def first_resource(case, activity):
    rows = events[
        events[CASE_COL].eq(case) & events[ACT_COL].eq(activity)
    ].sort_values(TIME_COL)
    return rows.iloc[0][RES_COL] if not rows.empty else None

rows = []
for case in facts[CASE_COL]:
    prep_t = first_time(case, ["Prepare_Payment"])
    release_t = first_time(case, ["Release_Payment"])
    settlement_t = first_time(case, ["Bank_Settlement"])
    manager_t = first_time(case, ["Manager_Approve", "Manager_Ratify"])
    audit_t = first_time(case, ["Audit_Review"])

    # AP-201 has a supplied settlement fact not represented by an event.
    if case == "AP-201":
        settlement_t = pd.Timestamp("2026-05-04T10:00:00Z")

    prep_r = first_resource(case, "Prepare_Payment")
    release_r = first_resource(case, "Release_Payment")

    rows.append({
        CASE_COL: case,
        "prepare_time": prep_t,
        "release_time": release_t,
        "settlement_time": settlement_t,
        "manager_time": manager_t,
        "audit_time": audit_t,
        "prepare_resource": prep_r,
        "release_resource": release_r,
        "completed_payment": pd.notna(release_t),
        "blocked_attempt_no_completion": case in blocked_attempt_cases,
        "nominal_fep_violation": case in nominal_violating_cases,
    })

evidence = pd.DataFrame(rows).merge(facts, on=CASE_COL, how="left")

evidence["token_before_prepare"] = (
    evidence["emergency_token_reported"]
    & evidence["token_active_time"].notna()
    & (evidence["token_active_time"] <= evidence["prepare_time"])
)
evidence["emergency_basic_eligibility"] = (
    evidence["token_before_prepare"]
    & (evidence["amount_eur"] < 100000)
    & (evidence["vendor_age_days"] >= 180)
    & ~evidence["bank_change_lt_7d"]
    & evidence["av_result"].eq("pass")
)
evidence["av_full_pass"] = evidence["av_result"].eq("pass")
evidence["av_coverage_gap"] = evidence["av_result"].isin(
    ["warning_override", "unavailable"]
)
evidence["manager_before_release"] = (
    evidence["manager_time"].notna()
    & evidence["release_time"].notna()
    & (evidence["manager_time"] < evidence["release_time"])
)
evidence["manager_before_settlement"] = (
    evidence["manager_time"].notna()
    & evidence["settlement_time"].notna()
    & (evidence["manager_time"] < evidence["settlement_time"])
)
evidence["manager_after_settlement"] = (
    evidence["manager_time"].notna()
    & evidence["settlement_time"].notna()
    & (evidence["manager_time"] >= evidence["settlement_time"])
)
evidence["audit_within_24h_of_release"] = (
    evidence["audit_time"].notna()
    & evidence["release_time"].notna()
    & ((evidence["audit_time"] - evidence["release_time"])
       <= pd.Timedelta(hours=24))
)
# Calendar-day approximation only: actual "business day" logic needs a holiday
# calendar. This is intentionally retained as preliminary evidence.
evidence["audit_by_next_calendar_day"] = (
    evidence["audit_time"].notna()
    & evidence["release_time"].notna()
    & (evidence["audit_time"].dt.date
       <= (evidence["release_time"] + pd.Timedelta(days=1)).dt.date)
)

# Repeated preparer/releaser pairs among completed payments.
pair_counts = (
    evidence[evidence["completed_payment"]]
    .groupby(["prepare_resource", "release_resource"])
    .size()
    .rename("pair_count")
    .reset_index()
)
evidence = evidence.merge(
    pair_counts,
    on=["prepare_resource", "release_resource"],
    how="left"
)
evidence["repeated_pair_flag"] = evidence["pair_count"].fillna(0).ge(2)
evidence["bank_change_proximity_flag"] = evidence["bank_change_lt_7d"]
evidence["control_outage_flag"] = evidence["av_result"].eq("unavailable")

# ED-1 preliminary flag includes observable/table facts only. It does not prove
# manager level, independence, substantive review, or audit completion quality.
evidence["ed1_preliminary_observed_conditions"] = (
    evidence["emergency_basic_eligibility"]
    & evidence["manager_before_settlement"]
    & evidence["audit_by_next_calendar_day"]
)

display_cols = [
    CASE_COL, "completed_payment", "blocked_attempt_no_completion",
    "nominal_fep_violation", "emergency_basic_eligibility",
    "ed1_preliminary_observed_conditions", "av_full_pass",
    "av_coverage_gap", "manager_before_release",
    "manager_before_settlement", "manager_after_settlement",
    "audit_within_24h_of_release", "audit_by_next_calendar_day",
    "bank_change_proximity_flag", "repeated_pair_flag",
    "control_outage_flag"
]

print("EVIDENCE FOR JUDGMENT — NOT THE FINAL COMPLIANCE VERDICT")
print(pm4py_semantics_note)
print("Nominal violating completed-payment cases:",
      sorted(nominal_violating_cases))
print("Attempted but blocked, without completed release:",
      sorted(blocked_attempt_cases))
print(evidence[display_cols].sort_values(CASE_COL).to_string(index=False))
```

PM4Py’s high-level filtering API has changed across releases, and some versions may interpret the four-eyes filter as retaining conforming cases rather than violations or may return an EventLog instead of a DataFrame. The code therefore makes the required call, converts its result where possible, and cross-checks it against an explicit resource-intersection calculation. The pandas result remains preliminary evidence: it cannot establish substantive manager review, human independence, collusion, business-day holidays, or ultimate control-objective sufficiency.
