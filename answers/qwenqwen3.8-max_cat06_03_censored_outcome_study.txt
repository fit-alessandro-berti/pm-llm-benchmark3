## 1. Target definition

**Primary target: observed-policy “activated-default” cumulative incidence.**  
The target is not “default for every application” in the unrestricted credit-risk sense, because default is only observable after an account is activated. Instead, the primary target is the probability that an application, starting from its early prefix, will follow the observed historical process into **Account Activated** and then enter **Default Recorded within 90 days of activation**.

- **Unit of analysis:** one credit application case (`case:concept:name`).
- **Prediction time `T0`:** the earlier of  
  1. the timestamp of the third canonical early event, or  
  2. 48 hours after `Application Started`,  
  provided the case has not already reached `Account Activated`, `Rejected`, `Withdrawn`, or `Transferred`. Cases that terminate or activate before this early prefix are not treated as early-warning prediction cases; they are represented in the cohort flow as early terminal/activated cases.
- **Outcome horizon:** default must occur within 90 days after `Account Activated`. Because activation itself may occur after `T0`, evaluation can use an administrative maximum follow-up, for example 180 days from `T0`, or simply administrative censoring at the extraction date.
- **Population:** applications started between 1 January 2024 and 31 December 2025 that reach the early prefix and are still under consideration at `T0`.
- **Estimand:** for prefix history `H(T0)`, estimate

\[
P(\text{Activated-Default by follow-up end} \mid H(T0), \text{observed credit policy})
\]

where “Activated-Default” means activation occurred and default occurred within 90 days of activation. Rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and open cases are not labeled “non-default.” They are competing states or censored observations.

This formulation is operationally defensible because it predicts the future process outcome that is actually observable under the lender’s current decision process. It is statistically defensible because it avoids conditioning on activation as though activation were random and avoids assigning fake credit outcomes to applications that were never exposed to credit.

**Important interpretation boundary:** the primary score estimates expected funded default under the observed historical approval process. It is not the counterfactual credit risk of a rejected applicant if that applicant had been funded.

**Alternative target 1: conditional default among eventually activated accounts.**  
This target is

\[
P(\text{default within 90 days of activation} \mid \text{activated}, H(T0)).
\]

It answers a different question: post-approval credit risk among accounts that eventually become activated. It is useful for portfolio monitoring after approval, but it is not directly an early-warning target for all applications because activation is a future, policy-dependent, selective event. Conditioning on activation can induce collider/selection bias.

**Alternative target 2: policy-specific counterfactual default risk.**  
One could define

\[
P(\text{default within 90 days if activated under policy } \pi \mid H(T0)).
\]

This would answer “what would default be if this application were funded under a specified acceptance policy?” It requires strong causal assumptions: no unmeasured confounding, positivity, correct modeling of the activation policy, and stable outcome measurement. It should not be the primary target unless the lender explicitly wants causal policy analysis and accepts those assumptions.

## 2. Cohort construction

**Inclusion rules**

1. Application started between 1 January 2024 and 31 December 2025.
2. At least one valid event with a parseable timestamp.
3. Case identifier is valid and unique after event-level deduplication.
4. Events used for features must occur on or before the case-specific prediction time `T0`.
5. Outcome information is considered only as of the analysis cutoff: 15 February 2026.

**Exclusion rules**

1. Duplicate events caused by migration are removed at the event level, not by removing whole cases.
2. Events after 15 February 2026 are excluded.
3. Features populated after `T0` are excluded from the feature table.
4. Cases activating or terminating before the early prefix are excluded from the primary early-warning risk set, but they remain in the cohort-flow accounting.

**As-of rules**

- Administrative censoring date: 15 February 2026.
- Non-default activated labels are mature only if:

\[
\text{activation time} + 90 \text{ days} + 35 \text{ days maximum feed delay} \leq \text{15 Feb 2026}.
\]

- Default labels are accepted when a default event or `default_90d = 1` is observed on or before the cutoff and within 90 days of activation. If the default occurred near the horizon but the feed has not arrived by the cutoff, the case is treated as delayed/censored rather than confirmed non-default.
- Transfers are treated as competing/missing follow-up in the primary analysis. The linkage table is used only in sensitivity analyses because only about 60% of transfers are linkable.

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

The data contain several distinct missingness/censoring mechanisms. They must not be collapsed into “non-default.”

**1. Ordinary right censoring**  
Still-open applications and activated accounts whose 90-day window has not finished are censored at the earlier of administrative cutoff or last reliable observation. They contribute risk time but no outcome label.

**2. Delayed labels**  
The external outcome feed can arrive 7–35 days after the 90-day horizon. A case whose horizon has passed but whose feed has not arrived is not a confirmed non-default. The primary conservative rule is to censor such cases until `label_available_at` shows the label is known. A sensitivity analysis can use reporting-delay nowcasting based on historical feed-lag distributions.

**3. Selection through activation**  
Default is observed only if the account is activated. Activation is not random; it depends on the prefix, risk score, policy period, channel, and resource behavior. The primary multi-state formulation avoids conditioning on activation. If a conditional activated-only model is used, activation selection must be addressed, for example with inverse probability of activation weighting or explicit two-stage modeling, but those approaches require stronger assumptions.

**4. Transfer as competing event or missing follow-up**  
Transferred cases may reappear under a new identifier, but linkage exists for only about 60%. In the primary analysis, transfer is an absorbing competing state or missing-follow-up state. In sensitivity analysis, linked transfers can be rejoined where the linkage is trustworthy, and unlinked transfers can be treated as censored or bounded.

**5. Permanently missing outcome feeds**  
Partner-originated activated accounts have elevated permanently missing outcome probability. This is not ordinary administrative censoring; it may be informative. Primary analysis should flag these cases and avoid treating missingness as random. Sensitivity analyses should include pattern-mixture bounds, worst-case/best-case scenarios, or exclusion/reweighting of partner-originated accounts.

**Primary analysis strategy**

Use a multi-state or cause-specific survival model with states such as:

- pre-activation in process;
- activated;
- activated-default;
- activated-no-default mature;
- rejected;
- withdrawn;
- transferred;
- censored/open.

The model estimates the cumulative incidence of activated-default while treating rejection, withdrawal, and transfer as competing or missing-follow-up states. Inverse probability of censoring weights can be used for delayed labels and informative missing feeds when covariates explain the missingness.

**Sensitivity analyses**

1. Complete-case activated-only analysis with inverse probability of activation weighting.
2. Treat transfers as censored rather than competing.
3. Link transfers where linkage exists and compare results with the primary unlinked analysis.
4. Bound partner-originated missing outcomes under best-case and worst-case default rates.
5. Vary the maximum feed delay from 7 to 35 days.

**Assumption testability**

| Assumption | Testability |
|---|---|
| Administrative censoring independent conditional on prefix/state/calendar | Partially testable through covariate balance and lag diagnostics |
| Delayed feed timing independent of default given covariates | Partially testable using historical feed-lag distributions |
| Transfer missingness ignorable given linkage/covariates | Partially testable by comparing linked vs unlinked transfers |
| Activation selection ignorable given observed prefix | Fundamentally untestable without external/randomized data |
| Partner missingness missing-at-random | Largely untestable; requires sensitivity analysis |

## 4. Prefix extraction

Prefix extraction must be strictly as-of `T0`.

**Canonicalization**  
Post-migration activity names are mapped to canonical names before prefix construction. For example, renamed versions of `Credit Review`, `Identity/KYC Check`, `Request Documents`, `Account Activated`, and `Default Recorded` are mapped to stable names. A `migration_period` indicator is retained.

**Duplicate handling**  
Migration duplicates are removed only when they are implausible duplicates, for example same case, same canonical activity, same resource, same source system, and same timestamp rounded to one second. Legitimate repeated document loops are preserved.

**Fixed event-count prefixes**  
For a fixed prefix length `k`, take the first `k` canonical events after deduplication. If a case has fewer than `k` events before terminal closure or censoring, use all available events and flag `short_prefix`. Do not pad or impute future events.

**Elapsed-time prefixes**  
For an elapsed-time prefix, include all events with timestamps less than or equal to:

\[
\text{Application Started time} + \Delta
\]

where `Delta` might be 48 hours, 72 hours, or another business-defined window. If the case terminates before the elapsed window, the prefix ends at the terminal event and the case is flagged.

**Short cases**  
Cases that terminate or activate before the desired prefix length are not silently forced into the early-warning training set. They are either excluded from the primary early-warning risk set or modeled in a separate short-prefix/diagnostic stratum.

**Repeated document loops**  
Repeated `Request Documents` and `Credit Review` events are preserved. Rework is informative and should become a feature, for example number of document loops before `T0`.

**Activity renaming**  
Renaming is handled before feature construction. The original raw activity name and a schema-version indicator can be retained for drift monitoring, but modeling uses canonical names.

**Nightly snapshot features**  
A nightly snapshot such as `documents_total` is unsafe if it includes uploads later than the nominal prediction time. It may be used only if a snapshot timestamp exists and is less than or equal to `T0`. Otherwise it should be lagged by one day or dropped.

## 5. Process-aware features

Features must be available at `T0` and must not depend on future events or future labels.

**Static case attributes**

| Feature | Availability |
|---|---|
| `case:channel` | Known at application start |
| `case:region` | Known at application start |
| `case:requested_amount` | Known at application start, unless later updated; use start value |
| `source_system` | Known at application start |
| Application start date/time | Known at start |
| Policy-period indicator | Derived from start date relative to 1 July 2025 |
| Migration-period indicator | Derived from start date relative to 15 October 2025 |

**Prefix-derived features**

| Feature | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Number of events before `T0` | Process intensity |
| Activities observed before `T0` | Control-flow state |
| Prefix variant or sequence hash | Process pattern |
| Time since application start | Elapsed process time |
| Time since last event | Inactivity/queue risk |
| Duration between first and last prefix event | Process speed |
| Number of `Credit Review` repeats | Rework |
| Number of document-request loops | Rework/document friction |
| Latest `risk_score` before `T0` | Last known risk assessment |
| Slope or change in `risk_score` before `T0` | Risk trajectory |
| Number of distinct resources | Handoff intensity |
| Last resource or queue before `T0` | Operational context |
| Day-of-week and hour-of-day features | Calendar effects |

**Features requiring historical reference data**

These are allowed only if the reference data is itself as-of `T0`.

| Feature | Safe construction rule |
|---|---|
| Resource experience at event time | Count prior events/cases for that resource before `T0`; do not use extraction-time experience |
| Resource workload last 7/30 days | Count events assigned to resource before `T0` |
| Activity frequency by channel/region | Use historical events before `T0` |
| Historical matured default rate by resource/branch | Use only outcomes whose label maturity occurred before `T0` |
| Queue waiting benchmarks | Use historical cases whose relevant events occurred before `T0` |

Outcome-derived historical aggregates are high-risk. They should be built only from matured labels and should be lagged to avoid using recent outcomes that would not have been known at prediction time.

## 6. Leakage audit

| Feature or data element | Leakage or bias risk | Valid availability rule | Keep/repair/drop | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `final_limit` | Populated only after final credit decision; reveals future decision and exposure | Use only if decision and limit are known before `T0`; usually false for early prefixes | Drop for early-warning model | Credit limit is a post-prefix decision outcome |
| `decision_reason` | Populated at or after Accept/Reject; reveals future disposition | Use only if decision event occurs before `T0`; usually false | Drop for early-warning model | Decision reason is a future-state explanation |
| `collection_status` | Updated after activation and may directly reveal default | Use only for post-activation monitoring models, not early application-stage model | Drop for early-warning model | It is an outcome pathway variable |
| Future events such as `Contract Signed`, `Account Activated`, `Collections Contact`, `Default Recorded` | Direct future process/outcome leakage | Include only events with timestamp <= `T0` | Drop future events | Prediction must use only prefix history |
| `documents_total` | Nightly snapshot may include uploads later than nominal prediction time | Use only with snapshot timestamp <= `T0`; otherwise lag one day or reconstruct from event-level uploads | Repair or drop | Daily snapshots are not inherently as-of |
| Updated `risk_score` | Later risk updates may incorporate post-prefix information | Use last value with event/valid-from timestamp <= `T0` | Keep last known pre-prefix value; drop future updates | Risk score is valid only as of its observation time |
| Resource experience stored at extraction time | Uses future accumulated experience; leaks future operational context | Recompute experience from events before `T0` | Repair if possible; otherwise drop | Experience must be historical, not extraction-time |
| `default_90d` and `label_available_at` | Label fields can leak outcome if used as features | Use only for outcome definition and maturity checks | Drop from feature table | Labels are not predictors |
| `data_extract_time` | Reflects data availability, not process state at prediction time | Do not use as predictive feature | Drop | Extraction time creates artificial temporal shortcuts |

## 7. Validation design

Random case splitting is misleading here because it mixes policy regimes, migration periods, label-maturity states, and calendar-driven process behavior. A random split can place future post-policy cases in training and past pre-policy cases in testing, or vice versa, and can therefore produce misleading performance estimates.

**Primary temporal split**

Use case start time, not event time or random case ID.

| Split | Case start window | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Training | 1 January 2024 through 30 June 2025 | Pre-policy-change learning |
| Validation | 1 July 2025 through 14 October 2025 | Post-policy-change, pre-migration validation |
| Test | 15 October 2025 through 31 December 2025 | Post-migration drift and robustness testing |

Because of label maturity, the post-migration test set will contain many censored cases. It should be evaluated with censoring-aware metrics and with matured-only subsets where available. In production, this test set should be revisited as delayed labels mature.

**Rolling backtesting with label maturity gates**

For each monthly cohort:

1. Freeze the model version.
2. Score cases at `T0` using only prefix data.
3. Evaluate only cases whose label maturity time is before the evaluation date.
4. Continue monitoring censored cases with survival/competing-risk metrics.
5. Re-evaluate when delayed feeds arrive.

This avoids pretending that immature cases are non-default.

**Policy-change handling**

- Report performance separately before and after 1 July 2025.
- Compare calibration and score distributions across policy periods.
- Consider separate post-policy models, reweighting, or recalibration if post-policy matured data are sufficient.

**Migration handling**

- Monitor duplicate-event rates before and after 15 October 2025.
- Validate canonical activity mapping.
- Consider excluding or separately modeling the first one to two weeks after migration if event quality is unstable.

**Metrics appropriate for the primary target**

Because the target is a censored, competing-risk process outcome, use:

- Cause-specific cumulative incidence prediction error.
- Inverse probability of censoring weighted Brier score.
- Time-dependent AUROC for activated-default.
- Calibration curves for predicted activated-default probability.
- Expected calibration error or reliability diagrams.
- Calibration slope and intercept.
- AUPRC for the activated-default event, given imbalance.
- State-probability calibration across competing states.
- Coverage of predictive uncertainty intervals or conformal quantiles adapted to censoring.

**Subgroup evaluation**

Evaluate performance by:

- Policy period.
- Migration period.
- Channel.
- Region.
- Requested amount band.
- Partner vs internal source.
- Short-prefix vs full-prefix cases.
- Resource or branch, where sample size permits.

**Distribution-shift monitoring**

- Population stability index for key prefix features.
- Variant drift using `pm4py.get_variants`.
- Activity-frequency drift.
- Resource workload drift.
- Transfer-rate and withdrawal-rate drift.
- Missing-feed rate by source system.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

**What the study can claim**

- It can estimate the historical probability that an application following the observed process will become activated and then default within 90 days.
- It can identify early process patterns associated with higher activated-default risk.
- It can quantify uncertainty and monitor calibration/drift.
- It can support prioritization, early review, or process monitoring under the existing policy.

**What the study cannot claim**

- It cannot claim that rejected, withdrawn, or transferred cases are true non-default credit outcomes.
- It cannot estimate the counterfactual default risk of rejected applicants if they had been funded without strong causal assumptions.
- It cannot separate all selection effects from underlying credit risk.
- It cannot guarantee validity after another credit-policy change, partner-routing change, or collections-policy change.
- It cannot fully correct permanently missing partner outcome feeds without unverifiable assumptions.

**Predictive association vs selection effects vs counterfactual risk**

A high score may reflect true credit risk, but it may also reflect that certain prefixes are more likely to be approved, routed, delayed, or transferred. Predictive association is not the same as counterfactual default risk under a different approval policy.

**Deployment feedback effects**

Deployment can change the data-generating process. Examples:

1. If the model routes high-score cases to manual review, activation rates and the mix of activated accounts will change.
2. If the model triggers additional document requests, withdrawal rates and process durations may change.
3. If the model influences pricing, limits, or collections intensity, default occurrence or default recording may change.
4. If high-score cases are routed to partners, outcome-feed missingness may increase.

Therefore, the model should be monitored as part of a controlled decision system, not assumed to be a stable measurement device.

## 9. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation skeleton

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Configuration: as-of design is the main leakage control.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
CSV_PATH = "applications_events.csv"
CASE_ID = "case:concept:name"
ACTIVITY = "concept:name"
TIMESTAMP = "time:timestamp"

ANALYSIS_CUTOFF = pd.Timestamp("2026-02-15", tz="UTC")
HORIZON_DAYS = 90
MAX_FEED_DELAY_DAYS = 35

PREFIX_LEN = 3
ELAPSED_PREFIX_HOURS = 48

POLICY_CHANGE = pd.Timestamp("2025-07-01", tz="UTC")
MIGRATION = pd.Timestamp("2025-10-15", tz="UTC")

# ASSUMPTION: post-migration renamed activities can be canonicalized.
ACTIVITY_MAP = {
    "KYC Check": "Identity/KYC Check",
    "Credit Review v2": "Credit Review",
    "Docs Request": "Request Documents",
    "Activated": "Account Activated",
    "Default": "Default Recorded",
}

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Load and standardize event data.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
df = pd.read_csv(
    CSV_PATH,
    parse_dates=["time:timestamp", "label_available_at", "data_extract_time"],
)

df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id=CASE_ID,
    activity_key=ACTIVITY,
    timestamp_key=TIMESTAMP,
)

df[ACTIVITY] = df[ACTIVITY].replace(ACTIVITY_MAP)
df = df.sort_values([CASE_ID, TIMESTAMP])

# Repair obvious migration duplicates, but preserve true rework loops.
df["_ts_round"] = df[TIMESTAMP].dt.floor("s")
df = df.drop_duplicates(
    subset=[CASE_ID, ACTIVITY, "_ts_round", "org:resource", "source_system"],
    keep="first",
)

# Keep only events observed by the administrative cutoff.
df = df[df[TIMESTAMP] <= ANALYSIS_CUTOFF].copy()

terminal_activities = {"Rejected", "Withdrawn", "Transferred"}
activation_activity = "Account Activated"
default_activity = "Default Recorded"

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Case-level anchors: start, activation, terminal, default, maturity.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
case = df.groupby(CASE_ID).agg(
    start_time=(TIMESTAMP, "min"),
    last_event_time=(TIMESTAMP, "max"),
    n_events=(TIMESTAMP, "size"),
)

case["activation_time"] = (
    df.loc[df[ACTIVITY] == activation_activity]
      .groupby(CASE_ID)[TIMESTAMP]
      .min()
)

term = (
    df.loc[df[ACTIVITY].isin(terminal_activities)]
      .groupby(CASE_ID)
      .agg(
          terminal_time=(TIMESTAMP, "min"),
          terminal_activity=(ACTIVITY, "first"),
      )
)
case = case.join(term)

case["default_time"] = (
    df.loc[df[ACTIVITY] == default_activity]
      .groupby(CASE_ID)[TIMESTAMP]
      .min()
)

# Non-default labels are trusted only after horizon plus worst-case feed delay.
case["horizon_end"] = case["activation_time"] + pd.Timedelta(days=HORIZON_DAYS)
case["maturity_time"] = case["horizon_end"] + pd.Timedelta(days=MAX_FEED_DELAY_DAYS)

mask = (
    df["default_90d"].notna()
    & df["label_available_at"].notna()
    & (df["label_available_at"] <= ANALYSIS_CUTOFF)
)
known_label = df[mask].groupby(CASE_ID)["default_90d"].last()
case["known_default_90d"] = known_label

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Cohort state construction.
# Rejected/withdrawn/transferred are not coded as non-default.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def outcome_state(row):
    if pd.notna(row.terminal_activity):
        return row.terminal_activity.lower()
    if pd.isna(row.activation_time):
        return "open_censored"
    if pd.notna(row.default_time) and row.default_time <= row.horizon_end:
        return "activated_default"
    if row.known_default_90d == 1:
        return "activated_default"
    if pd.notna(row.maturity_time) and row.maturity_time <= ANALYSIS_CUTOFF:
        return "activated_no_default_mature"
    return "activated_censored_immature"

case["outcome_state"] = case.apply(outcome_state, axis=1)
case["label_matured"] = case["outcome_state"].isin(
    ["activated_default", "activated_no_default_mature"]
)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Prediction time and prefix extraction.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
prefix_end = df.groupby(CASE_ID)[TIMESTAMP].apply(
    lambda s: s.sort_values().iloc[PREFIX_LEN - 1]
    if len(s) >= PREFIX_LEN
    else s.max()
)
case["prefix_end_time"] = prefix_end
case["elapsed_prefix_time"] = case["start_time"] + pd.Timedelta(
    hours=ELAPSED_PREFIX_HOURS
)

case["prediction_time"] = case[
    ["prefix_end_time", "elapsed_prefix_time"]
].min(axis=1)

# If activation or terminal closure happens earlier, cap prediction time.
case["prediction_time"] = case[
    ["prediction_time", "terminal_time", "activation_time"]
].min(axis=1)

# Primary early-warning risk set: not already activated/terminal at T0.
case["eligible_for_early_warning"] = (
    case["prediction_time"].notna()
    & (
        case["terminal_time"].isna()
        | (case["terminal_time"] > case["prediction_time"])
    )
    & (
        case["activation_time"].isna()
        | (case["activation_time"] > case["prediction_time"])
    )
)

# Build prefix events only. This prevents future events from entering features.
df2 = df.merge(
    case[["prediction_time"]],
    left_on=CASE_ID,
    right_index=True,
    how="left",
)
prefix_events = df2[df2[TIMESTAMP] <= df2["prediction_time"]].copy()

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Leakage guard.
# documents_total is dropped unless a separate as-of snapshot table is joined.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
forbidden = [
    "final_limit",
    "decision_reason",
    "collection_status",
    "default_90d",
    "label_available_at",
    "data_extract_time",
    "documents_total",
]

present_forbidden = [c for c in forbidden if c in prefix_events.columns]
prefix_features = prefix_events.drop(columns=present_forbidden, errors="ignore")

leakage_audit = {
    "forbidden_nonnull_values_in_prefix": int(
        prefix_events[present_forbidden].notna().sum().sum()
    ) if present_forbidden else 0,
    "future_event_rows": int((df2[TIMESTAMP] > df2["prediction_time"]).sum()),
}

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Temporal split by case start time.
# Random splits are only a weak baseline.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
train_cases = case.index[case["start_time"] < POLICY_CHANGE]
valid_cases = case.index[
    (case["start_time"] >= POLICY_CHANGE)
    & (case["start_time"] < MIGRATION)
]
test_cases = case.index[case["start_time"] >= MIGRATION]

split_summary = pd.DataFrame(
    {
        "split": ["train", "validation", "test"],
        "n_cases": [
            len(train_cases),
            len(valid_cases),
            len(test_cases),
        ],
        "matured_cases": [
            case.loc[train_cases, "label_matured"].sum(),
            case.loc[valid_cases, "label_matured"].sum(),
            case.loc[test_cases, "label_matured"].sum(),
        ],
    }
).set_index("split")

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# PM4Py process audit.
# These objects support control-flow features and drift monitoring.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
event_log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)
prefix_log = pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(event_log, PREFIX_LEN)
dfg, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_dfg(event_log)
variants = pm4py.get_variants(event_log)

# pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe could be applied to prefix_events
# only after confirming that every timestamp is <= prediction_time.
#
# pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time is intentionally avoided here because
# service/waiting times to future activities can cross the prediction boundary.

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Cohort and audit outputs.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
cohort_counts = (
    case["outcome_state"]
    .value_counts()
    .rename_axis("outcome_state")
    .reset_index(name="n_cases")
)

print(cohort_counts)
print(split_summary)
print(leakage_audit)
print(
    {
        "prefixes": len(prefix_log),
        "variants": len(variants),
        "start_activities": start_activities,
    }
)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Modeling hook:
# Connect this table later to a multi-state or cause-specific survival model.
# - activated_default and activated_no_default_mature are labeled outcomes.
# - rejected/withdrawn/transferred are competing states, not non-defaults.
# - activated_censored_immature and open_censored are right-censored.
# - delayed labels can be handled with IPCW or reporting-delay nowcasting.
# pm4py.split_train_test may be used as a convenience baseline only.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
```
