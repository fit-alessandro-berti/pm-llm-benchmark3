## 2. Rule Interpretation

**RR1 (drafter/approver separation).** For each case c: human(Draft_Change_Request, c) ≠ human(Approve_Change, c), where human() is the alias-normalized, session-attributed identity. Applies to all risk levels; the rule text has no risk qualifier.

**RR2 (implementer/verifier separation).** For each case c: human(Implement_Change, c) ≠ human(Verify_Implementation, c). If the verifier identity cannot be resolved (unattributed service account), the check cannot be passed — RR5 makes reliable attribution a precondition for conformance, so an unresolved verification is itself nonconformant even before asking whether RR2 was breached.

**RR3 (high-risk approval authority).** For every Approve_Change on a HIGH-risk case at time t: the actor holds the QM role at t, OR a delegation D exists with (i) delegate = actor, (ii) t within D's effective interval, (iii) the change's scope within D's scope, (iv) the principal absent/formally unavailable at t where D requires it, and (v) no prohibited subdelegation in the authority chain. All five conjuncts must hold at t.

**RR4 (emergency substitution).** For a HIGH-risk emergency change, Approve_Change by the IC or a valid IC delegate is acceptable only if: (a) the emergency flag and safety rationale are credible (a manual judgment), and (b) a QM or valid QM delegate performs Retrospective_QM_Review within two business days of implementation. **Business-day counting:** the implementation day is day 0; the deadline is end-of-day (23:59:59 UTC) of the second following business day per the site calendar (Sat/Sun off, no holidays in the period). Implementation on Tue 08-11 → deadline Thu 08-13 23:59 UTC. Note that a review on Fri 08-14 is late under any reasonable counting convention (it is the third business day), so this case does not hinge on the convention chosen. The reviewer must satisfy the same event-time authority test as RR3, applied to the review activity and the change's scope.

**RR5 (identity evidence).** svc_qms is never a conforming resource by itself. Attribution requires reliable session evidence (multi-factor, individually bound — e.g., FIDO token). Badge-into-room plus a shared unauthenticated terminal is circumstantial, not reliable. Aliases are normalized before all checks.

**RR6 (suspicion vs. proof).** Frequency-based indicators (repeated pairs, reciprocity, shared workstations, concentrated handovers) generate hypotheses, never conclusions. Every indicator gets at least one benign and one adverse reading (Section 4).

**Principal acting while on leave.** Leave does not strip a standing role. If Asha genuinely performed the 08-13 approval, it is a valid act by the principal QM; the open question is purely identity, not authority. Her brief action also does not make her "available" so as to void Ben's delegation: D-QM-01's condition is her recorded absence, which persisted 08-10 – 08-14; a 25-minute VPN session does not restore availability.

**Late database entries.** A delegation is proven by when the authorizing instrument was executed, not when a database row was created. D-QM-01's signed PDF is timestamped 2026-08-09 18:00 — before the interval and before every use — so the 08-12 DB backfill during migration is a data-quality/recording defect, not evidence of retroactive fabrication. Conversely, a DB record alone with no earlier instrument could NOT prove an earlier delegation; here the PDF is the primary evidence and its cryptographic/signature integrity should be independently confirmed. Conformance verdicts that depend on D-QM-01 carry this (modest) evidentiary caveat.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

**Repeated pair (Clara drafts → Ben approves, 18/23 HIGH-risk).**
Benign: they staff the same production cell, there is no pair-rotation policy, and during Asha's absence Ben was the only valid QM delegate for production equipment — structure, not scheming, concentrates the pair. Adverse: an entrenched drafter-approver dyad in which approval has degraded to rubber-stamping, or worse, coordinated pushing of favorable changes. Discriminating evidence: approval cycle times and rejection/rework rates for Ben-on-Clara approvals versus other combinations (near-zero rejections and minutes-long approvals favor rubber-stamping); substantive content of approval comments; whether the 18 changes cluster around any asset, vendor, or batch with a benefit trail.

**Reciprocal pattern (Ben drafts 4 LOW-risk training changes → Clara approves).**
Benign: small site, few qualified approvers, training documents are routine. Adverse: reciprocity is the classic lubricant of mutual non-scrutiny ("you approve mine, I approve yours"), and CHG-508 shows Ben will self-approve when an alias lets him. Discriminating evidence: content review of the four training changes for substantive scrutiny; whether reciprocation is time-correlated (tit-for-tat within days); comparison with reciprocity rates among other resource pairs.

**Shared workstation (CHG-503, WS-DM-04).**
Benign: shared terminals in production areas are common; anyone in the room could have run the verification; svc_qms may even run scheduled jobs. Adverse: Diego verified his own implementation behind the service account, defeating RR2 while leaving no personal fingerprint. Discriminating evidence: OS/application session logs, keystroke/HID timing versus Diego's badge exit, room access log for other entrants, whether the verification content required knowledge only the implementer had.

**Absence conflicts (Asha's VPN on leave; Ben's account during sick leave).**
Asha — benign: a diligent QM handling one urgent case from vacation, exactly as she states; adverse: credential sharing to keep a leave record clean while approvals continue, which would also quietly undermine the "principal absent" premise of Ben's delegation. Ben — benign: he briefly worked past his sick-leave start or misremembers timing; adverse: a colleague at an office workstation used his session for CHG-506 (and possibly the CHG-505 review the next day), meaning at least one HIGH-risk approval was made by an unidentified person. Discriminating evidence: VPN device fingerprint and MFA method for Asha's session; office badge and CCTV records for 08-13 13:00 and 08-14 16:00; whether Ben's workstation was left unlocked; endpoint logs tying the sessions to a physical seat.

**Credential compromise as a unifying hypothesis.**
Three independent identity anomalies inside five days (Asha's leave-time VPN, Ben's sick-leave activity, an unattributed service-account verification) can each be innocent, but jointly they indicate the site cannot reliably bind QMS actions to persons. That is itself a systemic finding independent of whether any individual colluded: under RR5, several HIGH-risk conformance verdicts here rest on identity evidence the site cannot currently produce.

**What would count as proof, per RR6.** Collusion should be asserted only with converging evidence of coordination plus benefit or concealment: communications showing agreement, log manipulation, financially or operationally traceable benefit, or a pattern of SoD-evasion mechanics (like the CHG-508 alias use) repeated deliberately. None of that is in the present record; the honest verdict on the network patterns is "elevated monitoring warranted, misconduct unproven."

def in_intervals(person, t, table):
    return any(a <= t <= b for a, b in table.get(person, []))

def has_role(person, t, role_table):
    iv = role_table.get(person)
    return bool(iv) and iv[0] <= t and (iv[1] is None or t <= iv[1])

def delegated_authority(person, t, scope, role):
    hits = []
    for did, principal, delegate, s, e, r, scopes, needs_abs, valid in DELEGATIONS:
        if delegate != person or r != role:
            continue
        ok, why = True, []
        if not valid:
            ok = False; why.append("invalid instrument (prohibited subdelegation / email only)")
        if not (s <= t <= e):
            ok = False; why.append("outside effective interval")
        if scope not in scopes:
            ok = False; why.append(f"scope '{scope}' not covered")
        if needs_abs and not in_intervals(principal, t, ABSENCE):
            ok = False; why.append("principal-absence condition not evidenced")
        hits.append((did, ok, "; ".join(why) or "valid"))
    return hits

def busday_deadline(impl_ts, n=2):
    # site calendar: Sat/Sun off, no holidays; deadline = EOD of 2nd business day

## 6. Control Improvements

1. **Delegation creation.** Require delegations to be executed in the controlled QMS before their effective start, with machine-readable fields (principal, delegate, role, scope taxonomy, interval, subdelegation flag, absence condition). Prohibit email-based delegation outright; make the QMS reject approvals whose actor has no active delegation record, which would have blocked D-IC-04 and CHG-507 at the point of entry.
2. **Effective-time recording.** Version the delegation and role tables bitemporally (valid time + transaction time) so migrations can never make a record's creation date masquerade as its effective date, eliminating the D-QM-01 ambiguity class.
3. **Principal availability.** Integrate HR absence and on-call calendars with the QMS; evaluate "principal absent" automatically at approval time and log the evaluation. Define explicitly whether weekends count as formal unavailability (the CHG-509 gap) and whether a principal acting during leave suspends the delegation.
4. **Alias management.** One human, one identity: enforce a single QMS account per person, run periodic IAM reconciliation to detect duplicates like b.koch/u_bk, and make the SoD engine key on the person identifier, not the login string.
5. **Individual authentication.** Ban service accounts from performing regulated activities, or force step-up individual authentication (FIDO/biometric) at the moment of the GxP action; enforce short screen-lock timeouts on shared terminals. This addresses CHG-503, CHG-506, and the residual doubt on CHG-504.
6. **Emergency review.** Automate the RR4 clock: on any emergency implementation, open a mandatory Retrospective_QM_Review task with a business-day deadline, restrict its completion to users holding event-time QM authority for the change's scope, and escalate to the Site Director on breach. CHG-505 breached all three dimensions silently.
7. **Pair rotation.** Introduce approver rotation or a random second-review sampling for HIGH-risk changes so no drafter-approver pair exceeds a concentration threshold (e.g., 50% over 60 days) without triggering an independent look — directly targeting the 18/23 pattern without presuming misconduct.
8. **Network monitoring.** Run the handover-of-work and working-together analyses on a scheduled basis with alerting on concentration and reciprocity metrics, treating outputs as triage signals routed to quality compliance, not as verdicts (RR6 discipline built into the tooling).
9. **Evidential retention.** Retain, time-synchronized and tamper-evident, the artifacts this investigation lacked: terminal session logs bound to seats, badge and CCTV records covering QMS workstations, VPN device fingerprints and MFA method per session, and signed delegation instruments — with retention at least matching the change-record retention period.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

Of the ten cases, the clear genuine authority failures are CHG-507 (HIGH-risk approval by someone with no QM role or delegation at event time), CHG-510 (HIGH-risk emergency approved through a prohibited subdelegation by its own drafter), CHG-505's failed compensating control (retrospective QM review late, out of the reviewer's delegated scope, and performed under a sick-leave account), and the self-approvals CHG-502 and CHG-508. Identity and data-quality problems account for most of the remaining nonconformance: CHG-506 and CHG-503 turn entirely on who actually acted, CHG-508's breach was hidden by an alias, CHG-507 would be missed by anyone reading the current user master, and D-QM-01's late database entry would falsely incriminate CHG-501 and CHG-503's approvals if the signed PDF were ignored. Organizational-pattern risk (the Clara-Ben concentration and reciprocity) is real but unproven as misconduct and is partly a structural artifact of a single-delegate arrangement during the QM's leave. Roughly: about half the apparent deviations are genuine authority/SoD failures, a third are identity or master-data quality defects, and the remainder is pattern risk requiring monitoring rather than sanction — with the caveat that the identity defects cap the confidence of every other verdict, since several HIGH-risk conformance conclusions rest on attributions the site cannot currently prove.

The three highest-priority investigations: (1) the CHG-506 approval under Ben's account during certified sick leave from an office IP that he denies — a possible credential compromise touching a HIGH-risk approval, which also contaminates the CHG-505 review made under the same account the next day; (2) the CHG-503 service-account verification from Diego's workstation — a possible defeat of implementer/verifier separation on a HIGH-risk production change, and in any event a verification with no attributable human; (3) the CHG-510 authority chain — how an email subdelegation expressly prohibited by D-IC-03 was accepted in practice, including Farid's role in issuing it and why the QMS allowed a drafter to approve her own emergency change.
