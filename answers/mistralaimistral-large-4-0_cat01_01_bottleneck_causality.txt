## 1. Executive causal assessment

Provisional verdict: **mainly confounded**. Confidence: **70/100**. The crude Orion–Nova difference is +6.72 h (21.96 vs 15.24), but this is driven by case mix: Orion has 42.9% high-complexity vs Nova 12.5%, 92.9% night vs 25%, 64.3% compliance-required vs 25%, and 92.9% high arrival load vs 50%. Within observed complexity strata, Orion is faster: Low 7.50 vs 9.64 (2.14 h), Medium 17.17 vs 19.38 (2.21 h), High 31.57 vs 35.00 (3.43 h). Complexity-standardized using pooled case mix gives Orion 17.14 vs Nova 19.65 (2.51 h). The reversal is mainly a queue-wait phenomenon: complexity-standardized wait is 9.65 h for Orion vs 11.80 h for Nova, while review service is nearly identical (5.80 vs 6.07 h). Interpreted causally, the total effect is the crude +6.72 h; a direct effect would be any residual group effect after blocking queue_wait/review_service/rework; a mediated effect would run through those mechanisms. The data are consistent with a confounded total effect and a possible beneficial queue mechanism, but do not identify either. Strongest evidence against a harmful Orion effect: within-complexity reversal, similar service times, similar rework/appeal rates. Strongest evidence for caution: shift and arrival load are nearly collinear with group (all night cases are high-load; Orion has only one day case), compliance is tied to complexity, only completed cases are observed, and cells are tiny (e.g., High Nova n=2). Therefore the proposed 50% reassignment is not supported as a causal improvement. It may fail or backfire by moving difficult night/high-load cases to a day-oriented group with less night capacity, increasing queue waits and cycle times, while removing Orion’s apparent queueing advantage. A safer path is a queue/compliance capacity intervention plus a small stratified randomized routing pilot.

Final recommendation: Do not blindly reassign 50% of Orion cases to Nova. The crude Orion disadvantage is largely a case-mix artifact: Orion receives more high-complexity, night, high-load, compliance-required cases, and within complexity strata Orion is faster, mainly due to shorter queue waits. Moving half of Orion’s work to Nova could backfire by shifting difficult night/high-load cases to a day-oriented group with less night capacity, increasing queue waits and cycle times, and possibly harming quality. Instead, first run a queue/compliance capacity pilot at night/high load, because queue wait is the main process lever. In parallel, run a small stratified randomized routing pilot among cases where both groups have coverage, measuring wait, service, rework, and 30-day outcome. Use process mining to monitor variants and directly-follows relations. Only scale reassignment if the pilot shows Nova can handle comparable cases without increasing wait, rework, or appeals. This approach learns while limiting operational risk.

    appeal_rate=('outcome_30d', lambda x: (x == 'Appeal').mean())
).round(3))

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. Unobserved case risk within complexity strata: observed complexity is only Low/Medium/High, but routing may depend on finer-grained difficulty. Most valuable additional data: a continuous or multi-dimensional case-risk score recorded at classification, or detailed claim attributes (coverage type, claim amount, document completeness, fraud indicators).
2. Selection into completed-case sample: only completed cases are present; abandoned/open cases may differ by group. Most valuable additional data: a case-level indicator for abandonment/open status and time-to-abandonment, or the full intake log including non-completed cases.
3. Time-varying queue state and arrival load: wait_before_review_h includes queueing after classification and around compliance, but we do not observe queue length, staffing, or arrival timestamps. Most valuable additional data: event timestamps for Register, Classify, Compliance Check, Review, Request Info, Close, plus resource availability/staffing schedule.
4. Reviewer skill and within-group heterogeneity: resource_group is a bundle; Orion has more certified reviewers but we do not observe individual reviewer identity or skill. Most valuable additional data: reviewer ID and certification/skill level for each review activity, enabling within-reviewer comparisons.
5. Post-process quality outcome: 30-day appeal is coarse and may be affected by post-process factors. Most valuable additional data: a validated quality measure (e.g., error rate, reopen rate, compliance audit result) collected before post-process factors, or randomized audit of closed cases.
