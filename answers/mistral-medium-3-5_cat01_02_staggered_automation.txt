## 1. What is observed
The data distinguish **availability** of automation (`automation=1`) from **actual use** (`automated_fast_path_pct`), which increased gradually post-rollout (e.g., Alpha’s fast-path share rose from 4% to 48% over 5 months). Contemporaneous changes complicate attribution: **routing shifts** (10% more cases to Alpha/Beta, 5% to Gamma), **staffing fluctuations** (e.g., Alpha lost 2 FTEs in May), **case-mix shifts** (e.g., Alpha’s `high_complexity_pct` rose from 28% to 36%), and a **centralized fraud desk** (March 2026) that interacted with automation (borderline cases routed more often). Outcomes include **cycle-time summaries** (median, p90), **rework/escalation rates**, and **CSAT** (mean 1–5, with 20–45% response rates and no branch-month counts). ## 3. Identification strategy

### Primary Strategy: **Staggered Adoption with Synthetic Control**
| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|----------|----------------------------------|------------------|----------------------|-------------------|-------------------------|-------------------|--------------------|
| Synthetic Control (SCM) | Month of `automation=1` | Weighted average of Delta/Epsilon | Pre-adoption outcomes, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct` | E1, E2 | No unmeasured time-varying confounders; parallel trends in absence of treatment | Pre-trend test; placebo tests (e.g., fake adoption dates) | Poor match for treated branches; extrapolation bias |

### Alternative 1: **Difference-in-Differences (DiD) with Matched Controls**
| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|----------|----------------------------------|------------------|----------------------|-------------------|-------------------------|-------------------|--------------------|
| DiD with matching | `automation=1` post-adoption | Delta/Epsilon (matched on pre-trends) | `fte`, `high_complexity_pct`, `arrivals` | E1, E3 | Parallel trends; no anticipation | Pre-trend test; covariate balance | Heterogeneous effects; small sample (only 3 adopters) |

### Alternative 2: **Interrupted Time Series (ITS) with Treatment Intensity**
| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|----------|----------------------------------|------------------|----------------------|-------------------|-------------------------|-------------------|--------------------|
| ITS with `automated_fast_path_pct` | Continuous (`automated_fast_path_pct`) | None (within-branch) | Time trends, `fte`, `high_complexity_pct` | E3, E7 | No unmeasured time-varying confounders; linear trend | Residual autocorrelation test | Confounding by contemporaneous changes (e.g., staffing) |

**Why SCM is preferable**:
The staggered rollout and small number of branches (N=5) make **Synthetic Control** a strong choice because it explicitly constructs a counterfactual for each adopter using pre-adoption data and covariate balancing. Unlike DiD, SCM avoids relying on parallel trends across all non-adopters (Delta/Epsilon may not be comparable to Alpha/Beta). ITS is useful for treatment intensity but cannot isolate the effect of availability (`automation=1`) from intensity (`automated_fast_path_pct`). SCM’s main limitation—extrapolation—can be mitigated by restricting the post-period to 3–5 months and validating with placebo tests.

## 8. Rollout recommendation
**Recommendation**: `expand with an embedded evaluation`

**Next branches**: Delta first, then Epsilon (lower volume, better historical performance).
**Phase-in mechanism**:
- **Step 1**: Pilot in Delta for 3 months with **50% of cases** routed to automation (randomized at case level).
- **Step 2**: If no harm (see guardrails), expand to 100% of Delta cases for 3 months.
- **Step 3**: If Delta shows **median cycle-time reduction 5%** and **p90 cycle-time increase 5%**, expand to Epsilon.

**Measurement requirements**:
- **Pre-period**: 6 months of baseline data (Delta/Epsilon already have 8 months).
- **Post-period**: Minimum 6 months per phase.
- **Process-mining outcomes**:
  - Case-level cycle time (not branch-month medians).
  - Queue lengths at **Specialist Review** and **fraud desk** (to detect spillovers).
  - `rework_pct` and `escalation_pct` by case complexity.
  - **Abandonment rate** (if available).
- **Spillover monitoring**: Track fraud-desk wait times and Delta/Epsilon’s `p90_cycle_h` during Delta’s pilot.

**Guardrails for customer harm**:
- **Stop rule**: If Delta’s `p90_cycle_h` increases by **>10%** or `csat_1to5` drops by **>0.2**, pause and investigate.
- **Fallback**: Revert to manual triage for high-complexity cases if `rework_pct` rises by **>2pp**.

**Decision rule for scale-up**:
- **Green light**: Delta shows **median cycle-time 5%**, **p90 cycle-time 5%**, and **no CSAT drop**.
- **Yellow light**: Mixed results  extend pilot with adjustments (e.g., exclude high-complexity cases).
- **Red light**: Harm detected  pause and redesign (e.g., improve automation rules for borderline cases).

**Why this approach**:
- **Causal learning**: Randomized case-level assignment in Delta isolates automation’s effect from confounding.
- **Operational safety**: Phased rollout limits risk; guardrails prevent widespread harm.
- **Spillover detection**: Monitoring fraud-desk metrics ensures no hidden congestion.

- **Suggestive**: Higher automation usage (`automated_fast_path_pct`) is **associated with lower median cycle time but higher p90 cycle time** in Alpha, suggesting **process substitution with tail harm**.
- **Conditionally causal**: If we assume no unmeasured time-varying confounding and parallel trends, automation **may reduce median cycle time by ~5% in branches like Gamma**, but this is **not generalizable** to Alpha/Beta.
- **Not supported**: A claim that "automation reduces cycle time by X%" **across all branches** is not defensible due to heterogeneous effects, confounding, and small sample size.
