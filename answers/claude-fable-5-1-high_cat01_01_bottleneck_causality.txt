## 1. Executive causal assessment

Provisional verdict on the claim "assignment to Orion causes longer completion times": `mainly confounded`. Confidence: 70/100 that the crude +6.7 h gap is not a harmful Orion effect; confidence is much lower (roughly 40/100) that Orion has a genuine *beneficial* effect, because the sign of any residual effect is not identifiable from 30 completed cases.

Strongest evidence for the verdict: the routing engine deliberately sends difficult cases to Orion (42.9% High vs 12.5%; compliance 64% vs 25%), and within every recorded complexity stratum the sign reverses: Orion is faster by 2.1 h (Low), 2.2 h (Medium) and 3.4 h (High). A Kitagawa-style split of the crude gap gives about +9.0 h from case-mix composition and about -2.3 h from within-stratum differences. The within-stratum advantage sits almost entirely in `wait_before_review_h` (-1.6, -1.9, -3.3 h), not in `review_service_h` (-0.4, -0.2, -0.1 h), and the gap widens with complexity, consistent with Orion's larger certified-reviewer pool shortening the queue for cases only certified staff may take.

Strongest evidence against: stratification is on a coarse 3-level label while routing uses finer "difficulty"; shift and arrival load are nearly collinear with Orion (13/14 night, high load), so an Orion effect cannot be separated from a night effect inside Orion; there is zero overlap for Medium/High day cases; two strata have n=2; and only completed cases are visible.

The 50% reassignment will most likely fail or backfire: it moves night, compliance-heavy, High/Medium cases to a day-oriented group with fewer certified reviewers and only two observed High cases, predicting roughly +2 to +3.5 h per moved case before any congestion effects at Nova. It could succeed only if Orion's current queue is capacity-driven and Nova's day capacity has real slack, which the data cannot show.

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (weeks 1-2) | Freeze routing; instrument the log: timestamps for every activity incl. Compliance Check start/end and Request Info, reviewer id and certification, queue length and eligible free reviewers at Classify, reassignment events, open/abandoned cases | Measurement and selection (H6), queue-state reconstruction (H2, H3) | none (observational) | No operational change; data-quality review | Performance DFG with real timestamps; waiting vs service split per activity; conformance of compliance requirement vs execution; censoring counts | Makes the wait/service/compliance decomposition verifiable and enables survival analysis and matching | Not applicable; proceed once >= 90% of events have timestamps and reviewer ids |
| 1 (weeks 3-8) | Quasi-random routing pilot: for Low and Medium cases arriving in shift-overlap hours (both groups staffed), assign by odd/even case id or arrival minute; keep High/compliance cases on the existing rule | Isolates group effect from routing and shift (H1-H4); Low cases act as negative control | case | Exclude High; cap pilot at about 20% of eligible volume; certified-reviewer availability check before each Nova assignment; weekly quality review | Per-arm wait, service, rework, variant distribution; DFG comparison between arms; balance check on complexity, load, shift | First unconfounded estimate of the Orion-vs-Nova contrast at equal complexity and shift; tests whether the within-stratum reversal is real | Stop if the Nova arm's median cycle time exceeds the Orion arm's by > 15% for two consecutive weeks, or any SLA breach rate doubles |
| 2 (weeks 5-12, parallel) | Capacity intervention without relabeling: add certified-reviewer hours to Nova day shift and pre-schedule Compliance Check slots so compliance-required cases are not blocked before Review | Certified-pool queue and compliance waiting (H2, H5) | shift-day (stepped-wedge: switch weekdays on in a staggered sequence) | Do not move cases; track compliance conformance; monitor reviewer overtime | Wait_before_review by complexity before/after; time between Classify and Compliance Check; eligible-reviewer count vs wait curve | Tests whether wait is capacity-driven: if Nova's High/Medium wait falls to Orion's level when certified capacity is added, the "Orion effect" is a capacity effect | Roll back a switched-on day if its wait does not fall within 3 weeks or rework rises > 5 points |
| 3 (weeks 13-20) | Conditional, staged reassignment: if phases 1-2 show Nova at parity for a stratum, move 10-15% of Orion's cases in that stratum only, prioritising shifts where Nova has certified staff | Load balancing where evidence supports it | complexity stratum x shift | Never move High/compliance cases until Nova's certified capacity is proven; keep a 20% randomised hold-out | Compare moved vs hold-out cases; Orion's queue length and remaining-case wait (does Orion get faster?); appeals at 30 and 90 days | Estimates the system-level effect of reassignment including congestion relief at Orion, which no observational analysis here can give | Rollback if moved cases are > 1 h slower than hold-out at equal complexity, or system-wide mean cycle time rises for two weeks |

Final recommendation: do not implement the 50% reassignment on the strength of the aggregate table. The +6.7 h crude gap is mostly case-mix composition (about +9 h), and within every complexity level Orion is 2-3 h faster, with the advantage concentrated in waiting time for complex cases, which is exactly the work management wants to move. That pattern does not prove Orion is beneficial (shift and load are collinear with Orion, strata have as few as two cases, unobserved difficulty is unadjusted, only completed cases are seen), but it makes the proposal's premise unlikely and the most probable outcomes neutral-to-worse: complex night cases arriving at a day-oriented group with fewer certified reviewers would predictably wait longer, with an unmeasured quality risk. Instead, instrument the log, run a small quasi-random pilot on Low/Medium cases in overlap hours with Low cases as a negative control, and add certified capacity and compliance scheduling at Nova. Move volume only where those phases demonstrate parity, in staged 10-15% steps with a randomised hold-out and explicit rollback thresholds.

MIN_CELL = 3

CX_ORDER = ["Low", "Medium", "High"]


def warn_support(label, n):
    if n < MIN_CELL:
        print(f"  WARNING: {label} rests on {n} case(s) (<{MIN_CELL}); treat as anecdotal.")


def banner(title):
    print("\n" + "=" * 78 + f"\n{title}\n" + "=" * 78)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 1. load
df = pd.read_csv(io.StringIO(CSV)).rename(columns={"group": "resource_group"})
df["complexity"] = pd.Categorical(df["complexity"], CX_ORDER, ordered=True)
df["rework_flag"] = (df["rework_count"] > 0).astype(int)
df["appeal_flag"] = (df["outcome_30d"] == "Appeal").astype(int)
# residual = everything not in wait or review service (Register, Classify,
# Compliance Check service, waiting for requested info, Close)
df["other_h"] = (df["cycle_time_h"] - df["wait_before_review_h"] - df["review_service_h"]).round(2)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 2. crude
banner("2a. CRUDE comparison (association only - case mix differs)")
crude = df.groupby("resource_group").agg(
    n=("case_id", "size"),
    mean_cycle=("cycle_time_h", "mean"),
    median_cycle=("cycle_time_h", "median"),
    mean_wait=("wait_before_review_h", "mean"),
    mean_service=("review_service_h", "mean"),
    mean_other=("other_h", "mean"),
    high_pct=("complexity", lambda s: 100 * (s == "High").mean()),
    night_pct=("shift", lambda s: 100 * (s == "Night").mean()),
    compliance_pct=("compliance_required", lambda s: 100 * (s == "Yes").mean()),
    rework_pct=("rework_flag", lambda s: 100 * s.mean()),
    appeal_pct=("appeal_flag", lambda s: 100 * s.mean()),
).round(2)
print(crude.to_string())
crude_diff = crude.loc["Orion", "mean_cycle"] - crude.loc["Nova", "mean_cycle"]
print(f"\nCrude Orion - Nova mean cycle time: {crude_diff:+.2f} h")

banner("2b. COMPLEXITY-STRATIFIED comparison")
strat = df.groupby(["complexity", "resource_group"], observed=True).agg(
    n=("case_id", "size"),
    mean_cycle=("cycle_time_h", "mean"),
    median_cycle=("cycle_time_h", "median"),
    mean_wait=("wait_before_review_h", "mean"),
    mean_service=("review_service_h", "mean"),
    mean_other=("other_h", "mean"),
    rework_pct=("rework_flag", lambda s: 100 * s.mean()),
).round(2)
print(strat.to_string())
for (cx, g), row in strat.iterrows():
    warn_support(f"stratum complexity={cx}, group={g}", int(row["n"]))

print("\nWithin-stratum Orion - Nova differences (h):")
for metric in ["mean_cycle", "mean_wait", "mean_service", "mean_other"]:
    wide = strat[metric].unstack("resource_group")
    d = (wide["Orion"] - wide["Nova"]).round(2)
    print(f"  {metric:13s}: " + ", ".join(f"{cx}={v:+.2f}" for cx, v in d.items()))

print("Stratification removes confounding by *recorded* complexity only; it does not")
print("adjust for shift/load (collinear with Orion), unobserved case risk, or selection.")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 3. standardization
banner("3. COMPLEXITY-STANDARDIZED contrast (direct standardization)")
means = strat["mean_cycle"].unstack("resource_group")
weights = {
    "pooled complexity mix (all 30 cases)": df["complexity"].value_counts(normalize=True),
    "Orion's own complexity mix": df.loc[df.resource_group == "Orion", "complexity"].value_counts(normalize=True),
    "Nova's own complexity mix": df.loc[df.resource_group == "Nova", "complexity"].value_counts(normalize=True),
}
for name, w in weights.items():
    w = w.reindex(CX_ORDER).fillna(0)
    std = means.mul(w, axis=0).sum()
    print(f"\nWeights = {name}: " + ", ".join(f"{cx}={w[cx]:.3f}" for cx in CX_ORDER))
    print(f"  standardized mean cycle: Nova={std['Nova']:.2f} h, Orion={std['Orion']:.2f} h, "
          f"Orion-Nova={std['Orion'] - std['Nova']:+.2f} h")
# Kitagawa/Oaxaca-style split of the crude gap
w_nova = weights["Nova's own complexity mix"].reindex(CX_ORDER).fillna(0)
orion_at_nova_mix = means["Orion"].mul(w_nova).sum()
print("\nDecomposition of the crude gap (Nova mix as reference):")
print(f"  composition (case-mix) part : {crude.loc['Orion', 'mean_cycle'] - orion_at_nova_mix:+.2f} h")
print(f"  within-stratum part         : {orion_at_nova_mix - crude.loc['Nova', 'mean_cycle']:+.2f} h")
print(f"  sum (= crude gap)           : {crude_diff:+.2f} h")
print("Standardized contrasts are descriptive; they are causal only under exchangeability")
print("given complexity, positivity, and consistency, none of which is verified here.")

banner("4. OVERLAP / POSITIVITY: cases per complexity x shift x resource_group")

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. Residual confounding by difficulty finer than the 3-level complexity label: the routing engine's actual selection criterion may order cases within a stratum, and the direction of the resulting bias for Orion is unknown. Most valuable addition: export the routing engine's inputs and decision score (or rule id) for every case, so the true propensity can be modelled or matched on.

2. Inseparability of resource group from shift and arrival load: 13 of 14 Orion cases are night/high-load and Orion has no Medium or High day cases, so no Orion-vs-Nova contrast exists at equal operating conditions for the cases management wants to move. Most valuable change: a short quasi-random routing window in shift-overlap hours (or a few weeks of Orion day staffing) that creates observed Orion-Day and Nova-Night cases at Medium/High complexity.

3. Meaning of `wait_before_review_h`: it mixes group-level queueing, compliance waiting, eligibility waiting for certified reviewers and possibly prioritisation, and its steep complexity gradient shows it is not a pure queue. Most valuable addition: per-activity start and complete timestamps (including Compliance Check and Request Info) plus the count of eligible free reviewers at assignment time, enabling queue-state reconstruction and a true waiting/service split.

4. Selection into the completed-case sample: open, abandoned, escalated or reassigned cases are invisible, and if they are concentrated in one group (for example Nova's hard cases escalated to Orion) the stratified comparison is biased in an unknown direction. Most valuable addition: the full case population with status (open/closed/abandoned) and reassignment history, allowing censoring-aware (survival) comparisons.

5. Quality trade-off: one appeal per group in 30 days cannot detect whether moving complex cases to a group with fewer certified reviewers raises rework or appeals, and 30-day outcomes are influenced by post-process factors. Most valuable addition: reviewer id and certification per Review event together with a longer-horizon quality outcome (90-day appeal or reopening, audit-error rate), which also enables within-reviewer comparisons of the skill channel.
