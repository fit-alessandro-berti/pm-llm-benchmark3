# 1. Assumptions and notation

**A1 — Event-order convention for simultaneous rounded timestamps.** When a completion (resource freed / case leaves a queue) and a start (resource seized / case enters service) share the same 5-minute rounded stamp, the completion is processed first. This is the standard "free-before-seize" convention and avoids spurious capacity violations purely from rounding.

**A2 — Rounding band.** Every reported timestamp `t` represents an actual instant in `[t  2.5 min, t + 2.5 min]`. Differences of two rounded timestamps therefore carry up to ±5 min of uncertainty on the *true* elapsed duration. I report point estimates from rounded values and, where it matters, ±5 min sensitivity bands.

**A3 — Station/queue model.** S1 is a 2-server queue (I1, I2); S2 is a 2-server queue (C1, C2). The "S2 queue" for a case begins at S1 completion. FCFS within each resource is *not* assumed unless stated; I only use resource-availability constraints for bounding missing starts.

**A4 — Capacity / availability.** Each single-task resource serves at most one case at a time. C2 is unavailable 10:55–11:10 (15 min). No bypass of either station.

**A5 — Apparent resource conflicts are artefacts.** Where rounded timestamps make a resource look double-booked at a boundary (e.g., P07 S2 ends 11:00 and P09 S2 starts 11:00 on C1), I treat this as rounding, not genuine overlap.

**A6 — MISSING-start handling.** For a missing start on resource R with completion `e`, I bound the start `s` by `[max(prev_completion_on_R, queue_entry), e]` where `queue_entry` is the case's arrival (S1) or S1 completion (S2). A *central* estimate uses the midpoint of the feasible interval; *optimistic* (short service) uses `s = e` (service  0, minimal busy time); *pessimistic* (long service) uses the lower bound (maximal service consistent with the previous job and queue entry). The true service time is nonnegative and bounded above by the feasible interval width.

**A7 — OPEN handling.** For an OPEN activity, the *elapsed* service at 14:00 is `14:00  start` (known start) or `14:00  central_missing_start` (missing start); the *final* service time is right-censored ( elapsed). OPEN cases are included in WIP and in busy-time lower bounds but excluded from completed-service throughput.

**A8 — Blank S2 (waiting).** A completed S1 with blank S2 means the case is waiting for S2 at 14:00; its S2 waiting time is right-censored ( `14:00  S1_end`) and S2 service has not begun.

**A9 — Little's Law applicability.** Applied only in subperiods that are approximately stationary in arrival rate and where the queue is not undergoing a regime change; results are consistency checks, not estimates.

Notation: `Wq` = queue waiting time, `Svc` = active service time, `Soj` = station sojourn = `Wq + Svc`, `Lq` = queue length, `L` = station WIP (in queue + in service), `` = arrival rate into station, `TH` = throughput (completions/unit time), `` = utilization = busy time / available capacity time.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

For fully observed case `i` at station X with arrival-to-station `a`, start `s`, end `e`:
- `Wq = s  a`
- `Svc = e  s`
- `Soj = e  a`

For a MISSING start on resource R:
- Lower bound on start: `s_lo = max(prev_completion_on_R, a)` (queue entry cannot precede arrival/S1-end; resource cannot start a new case before finishing the previous one).
- Upper bound: `s_hi = e` (nonnegative service).
- Central: `s_c = (s_lo + s_hi)/2`.
- `Wq  [s_lo  a, s_hi  a]`, `Svc  [0, e  s_lo]`.

For OPEN (known start `s`, no end by 14:00):
- Elapsed service = `14:00  s`; final service  elapsed.
- If start is MISSING and OPEN, combine A6 and A7 (rare here; does not occur).

Queue length `Lq(t)` = cumulative arrivals into station  cumulative service starts, evaluated with A1 ordering at ties. Station WIP `L(t) = Lq(t) + (number in active service at t)`. In-service count bounded by capacity (2 per station, except C2 single during outage).

Resource busy time =  service durations on that resource. For missing-service cases, busy time is interval-valued. Utilization:
- S1 resources: busy / 360 min.
- C1: busy / 360 min.
- C2: busy / (360  15) = 345 min available (or /360 of window; both reported).

Throughput by subperiod = completions in subperiod / subperiod length. Arrival rate into S1 = external arrivals / length; into S2 = S1 completions / length.

Little's Law: `L  ·W` checked as `L_observed` vs `·W_observed` with bands from rounding and missingness.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

**Evidence (not hypothesis):**
- S1 service times  20–30 min (mean  28); S2 service times  25–50 min (mean  40). Per-server capacity: S1  1/28  0.036/min; S2  1/40  0.025/min. With 2 servers each: S1 capacity  0.071/min (4.3/hr), S2 capacity  0.050/min (3.0/hr). S2 has the lower theoretical capacity.
- S1 queue grows through the morning (peak  8–9) and only declines after arrivals slow (~11:00 onward). S1 waiting times climb from 5 min to ~100 min.
- S2 queue is small until ~12:00, then grows (peak  6 at 13:25–13:45). S2 waiting times climb from 5 min to 85 min (censored).

**Interpretation:**
- **Early (08:00–10:00): S1 is the binding constraint.** Arrival rate  7/hr exceeds S1 capacity  4.3/hr; the S1 queue builds rapidly. S2, fed by S1 completions, is under-loaded and its queue stays near zero.
- **Middle (10:00–12:00): transitional.** Arrivals slow to  4/hr (close to S1 capacity). S1 queue stabilizes; S2 queue begins building because S1 output (now catching up) feeds S2 faster than S2 can process, and C2's 10:55–11:10 outage removes 15 min of C2 capacity exactly when P08 is queued for C2 (P08 S2 starts 11:15, immediately after maintenance ends).
- **Late (12:00–14:00): S2 becomes the binding constraint.** Arrivals nearly stop (1 in 2 hr), S1 drains its queue (5 completions), but S2 cannot keep up: 5 new S1 completions enter S2 vs only 3 S2 completions. S2 queue grows to ~6 and 4 cases are still waiting at 14:00. S2's longer service times plus the accumulated backlog plus the C2 outage make S2 the dominant bottleneck in the afternoon.

**Maintenance effect:** The C2 outage (10:55–11:10) coincides with low S2 queue (P06 just finished 10:50; P08 queued). Its direct throughput loss is modest (one C2 job delayed ~15 min), but it permanently removes 15 min of C2 capacity that is never recovered, contributing to the afternoon S2 buildup. The outage did *not* cause an apparent conflict — P06 ended 10:50 and P08 started 11:15, both consistent with the maintenance window.

**Hypotheses (less certain):**
- If S2 service times are at the high end of their observed range (~45–50), S2 would have been the bottleneck earlier than 12:00; if at the low end (~30–35), the afternoon S2 buildup is milder. The data favor the higher end (P12–P14 are 45, 50, 45).
- The longer S2 services on C2 (P13 = 50) may reflect a different case mix or resource skill differential; with only the given data this is speculative.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

Three scenarios for the four MISSING starts (P05-S2, P07-S1, P11-S2, P15-S1) and the OPEN/waiting censoring:

**Optimistic (short service for missing; OPEN cases finish immediately at 14:00):**
- Missing services  0 (start = end). 
- I1 busy = 275 min (76%); C1 busy = 270 (75%); C2 busy = 245 (71% of avail).
- S1 utilization 78%; S2 utilization 73%.
- Missing-start cases had near-zero service, implying long queue waits on already-congested resources — slightly *increases* apparent queue waits, *decreases* busy time.
- S1 bottleneck conclusion strengthens (more idle capacity at S2).

**Central (midpoint starts; OPEN elapsed service only):**
- P07-S1 start  09:42, service  18; P15-S1 start  11:57, service  18; P05-S2 start  10:00, service  15; P11-S2 start  12:12, service  13.
- I1 busy  310 (86%); C1  285 (79%); C2  258 (75% of avail).
- S1  83%; S2  77%.
- Bottleneck: S1 early, S2 late (as in §5).

**Pessimistic (max service for missing; OPEN continues past 14:00):**
- Missing starts at the lower feasible bound: P07-S1 start 09:25 (service 35); P15-S1 start 11:40 (35); P05-S2 start 09:45 (30); P11-S2 start 12:00 (25).
- I1 busy = 345 (96%); C1 = 300 (83%); C2 = 270 (78% of avail).
- S1  88%; S2  81%.
- S1 looks near-saturated throughout; S2 still lower. The "S1 early / S2 late" pattern survives, but S1's dominance is stronger and lasts longer.

**Stable conclusions (hold across all three scenarios):**
1. S1 is the primary bottleneck in the first half (queue builds from near-zero to 8–9; waits grow 5100+ min).
2. S2 becomes the binding constraint in the afternoon (4 cases still waiting at 14:00; S2 queue grows while S1 queue shrinks).
3. C2 outage had a real but modest direct effect; no spurious conflict.
4. Total sojourn for completed cases grows monotonically with arrival time (congestion, not stationarity).
5. Utilization of both stations is high (75%) under all scenarios; the system is capacity-constrained, not data-constrained in its *qualitative* dynamics.

**Unstable / scenario-sensitive:**
- Exact utilization split between S1 and S2 (S1 78–88%, S2 73–81%).
- Whether S2's bottleneck onset is "around 11:00" (pessimistic) or "around 12:00" (optimistic).
- Final service times of P15, P17, P21 (right-censored); only lower bounds available.

**Little's-Law consistency check (A9), central scenario, S1, 09:00–11:00:**
- _S1 = 10 arrivals / 120 min = 0.083/min.
- Mean S1 sojourn for P08–P17  109 min.
- Predicted L = W  0.083 × 109  9.1.
- Observed mean L_S1 over 09:00–11:00 (from cumulative arrivals  completions, central)  7–8.
- Agreement within ~15%, acceptable given non-stationarity and ±5 min rounding. Little's Law is *not* applied to S2 over the full window (regime change at ~12:00 violates stationarity) nor to sub-30-min intervals (too few events).

import numpy as np
from datetime import datetime, timedelta

try:
    import pm4py
    HAVE_PM4PY = True

# ------------------------------------------------------------------

DATE = "2026-05-12 "
raw = [

df = pd.DataFrame(raw, columns=cols)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Status-aware timestamp parsing (MISSING / OPEN / blank differ)
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def parse_ts(s):
    """Return (status, datetime_or_None).
    status in {TS, MISSING, OPEN, BLANK}."""
    if s is None or s == "" or (isinstance(s,float) and pd.isna(s)):
        return "BLANK", None
    if s == "MISSING":
        return "MISSING", None
    if s == "OPEN":
        return "OPEN", None
    return "TS", datetime.strptime(DATE+s, "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M")

def enrich(row):
    out = {}
    for st in ["s1_start","s1_end","s2_start","s2_end","arrival_s1"]:
        stt, dt = parse_ts(row[st])
        out[st+"_status"] = stt
        out[st+"_dt"] = dt
    return pd.Series(out)

df = pd.concat([df, df.apply(enrich, axis=1)], axis=1)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Build a PM4Py-compatible interval-event DataFrame.
#    Only usable (start or end known) observations become events.
#    MISSING start -> NaT start (interval with open left); OPEN end -> NaT end.

events = []
for _, r in df.iterrows():

    # S1 event
    s1s = r["s1_start_dt"]; s1e = r["s1_end_dt"]

            "org:resource": r["s1_resource"] if r["s1_resource"] else None,
            "start_status": r["s1_start_status"],
            "end_status":   r["s1_end_status"],
        })
    # S2 event (only if S1 completed, i.e., case reached S2)
    s2s = r["s2_start_dt"]; s2e = r["s2_end_dt"]
    if r["s2_start_status"]!="BLANK" or r["s2_end_status"]!="BLANK":
        events.append({
            "case:concept:name": cid,
            "concept:name": "S2_Calibration",
            "start_timestamp": s2s if r["s2_start_status"]=="TS" else pd.NaT,
            "time:timestamp":   s2e if r["s2_end_status"]=="TS" else pd.NaT,

ev = pd.DataFrame(events)

if HAVE_PM4PY:
    pm4py.format_dataframe(ev, case_id="case:concept:name",

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Station-specific queue/service/WIP/utilization via pandas.
#    Direct pandas is safer than pm4py.convert_log_to_time_intervals
#    here because (a) we have MISSING starts (open-left intervals) and
#    OPEN ends (open-right intervals) that interval conversion would
#    drop or mis-handle, and (b) we need per-resource, per-station
#    queue accounting with a custom tie-break convention (A1).
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def minutes_between(a,b):
    if a is None or b is None or pd.isna(a) or pd.isna(b):
        return np.nan
    return (b-a).total_seconds()/60.0

# --- feasible bounds for MISSING starts (A6) ---
def feasible_missing(row, station, resource_col, end_col, queue_entry_dt):
    """Return (lo, hi) datetime bounds for a missing start."""
    res = row[resource_col]
    end = row[end_col]
    # previous completion on same resource
    same_res = df[(df[resource_col]==res) & (df.index!=row.name)]
    prev_ends = []
    for _,o in same_res.iterrows():
        oe = o[end_col+"_dt"] if o[end_col+"_status"]=="TS" else None
        if oe is not None and oe <= end:
            prev_ends.append(oe)
    lo = max([queue_entry_dt] + prev_ends) if prev_ends else queue_entry_dt
    hi = end
    return lo, hi

# Build a long-form station record for point estimates (central scenario)
records = []
for _,r in df.iterrows():
    arr = r["arrival_s1_dt"]
    # S1
    s1s_st = r["s1_start_status"]
    s1e = r["s1_end_dt"] if r["s1_end_status"]=="TS" else None
    if s1s_st=="TS":

    elif s1s_st=="MISSING":

        s1s = lo + (hi-lo)/2  # central
    else:
        s1s = None
    if s1s is not None and s1e is not None:
        s1_wq = minutes_between(arr,s1s); s1_svc = minutes_between(s1s,s1e)
    elif s1s is not None and r["s1_end_status"]=="OPEN":

    else:
        s1_wq=np.nan; s1_svc=np.nan
    records.append({"case":r["case"],"station":"S1",
                    "arrival":arr,"start":s1s,"end":s1e,
                    "resource":r["s1_resource"],
                    "wq":s1_wq,"svc":s1_svc,
                    "start_status":s1s_st,"end_status":r["s1_end_status"]})

        qentry = s1e
    else:
        qentry = None

    s2e = r["s2_end_dt"] if s2e_st=="TS" else None

        s2_wq = minutes_between(qentry,s2s); s2_svc=minutes_between(s2s,s2e)
    elif s2s is not None and s2e_st=="OPEN":
        s2_wq=minutes_between(qentry,s2s); s2_svc=np.nan

    elif s2s is None and qentry is not None:
        # waiting or not reached
        s2_wq=np.nan; s2_svc=np.nan
    else:
        s2_wq=np.nan; s2_svc=np.nan
    records.append({"case":r["case"],"station":"S2",
                    "arrival":qentry,"start":s2s,"end":s2e,
                    "resource":r["s2_resource"],
                    "wq":s2_wq,"svc":s2_svc,
                    "start_status":s2s_st,"end_status":s2e_st})

rec = pd.DataFrame(records)

# Waiting / service summaries

# Utilization bounds (optimistic vs pessimistic for missing starts)
def busy_bounds(station):
    sub = rec[rec.station==station].dropna(subset=["resource"])
    # observed service
    obs = sub[sub.start_status=="TS"].groupby("resource")["svc"].sum()
    # missing-start service bounds: 0 (optimistic) .. (end-start_lo) (pessimistic)
    miss = sub[sub.start_status=="MISSING"]
    miss_lo = miss.groupby("resource")["svc"].apply(lambda s: 0)  # optimistic
    # pessimistic = end - feasible_lo ; approximate via (end-arrival_to_station) capped
    # We recompute properly:
    pe = {}
    for _,m in miss.iterrows():
        # feasible width already encoded: central svc*2 is the upper bound
        pe[m.resource] = pe.get(m.resource,0) + (m.svc*2 if not pd.isna(m.svc) else 0)
    res_all = sorted(sub.resource.dropna().unique())
    out=[]
    for rsc in res_all:
        o = obs.get(rsc,0)
        p = pe.get(rsc,0)
        avail = 360
        if station=="S2" and rsc=="C2":
            avail = 345
        out.append((station,rsc,round(o,1),round(o+p,1),avail,
                    round(100*o/avail,1),round(100*(o+p)/avail,1)))
    return out

print("\n=== Resource busy-time / utilization bounds ===")
for st in ["S1","S2"]:
    for row in busy_bounds(st):
        print(row)

# WIP over time (1-min grid, central scenario, free-before-seize at ties)
def station_wip(station, step=5):
    sub = rec[(rec.station==station)].copy()
    t0 = datetime.strptime(DATE+"08:00","%Y-%m-%d %H:%M")
    t1 = datetime.strptime(DATE+"14:00","%Y-%m-%d %H:%M")
    times = [t0+i*timedelta(minutes=step) for i in range(int((t1-t0).total_seconds()//60//step)+1)]
    arr = sub["arrival"].dropna().tolist()
    starts = sub["start"].dropna().tolist()
    ends = sub["end"].dropna().tolist()
    rows=[]
    for t in times:
        n_arr = sum(a<=t for a in arr)
        n_start = sum(s<=t for s in starts)
        n_end = sum(e<=t for e in ends)
        in_queue = max(n_arr-n_start,0)
        in_service = max(n_start-n_end,0)
        rows.append((t, in_queue, in_service, in_queue+in_service))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["t","Lq","Lsvc","L"]).set_index("t")

wip_s1 = station_wip("S1"); wip_s2 = station_wip("S2")
print("\n=== S1 WIP peaks (central) ===")
print(wip_s2.sort_values("L",ascending=False).head(5))
print("\n=== S2 WIP peaks (central) ===")
print(wip_s2.sort_values("L",ascending=False).head(5))

**Notes on PM4Py usage:** `insert_case_service_waiting_time` and `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` aggregate *across a case's full trace*, so they are only meaningful on the projection of cases with complete, observed S1 and S2 intervals (P01–P14, no MISSING/OPEN). They are explicitly labeled case-level. `convert_log_to_time_intervals` is not relied upon because it would silently discard MISSING-start (open-left) and OPEN-end (open-right) intervals, which are central to this analysis; the direct pandas reconstruction above preserves their status semantics.
