## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

- **Hard invariants:** Reference existing event and object IDs; preserve observed relations, events, and attributes; introduce no events or objects. Avoid duplicate relation triples. Respect qualifier direction: `carries` goes from Delivery to Package. Multiple qualifiers on one event–object pair are not automatically duplicates.
- **Strong defaults:** Apply the stated activity cardinalities: one Order for creation; one Order and Item for addition; one Item and Package for packing; one Package and Delivery for ordinary dispatch; one Invoice and at least one Order for invoicing; one Payment and normally one Invoice for payment recording. Bulk dispatch, scanner outages, and unapplied cash remain legitimate exceptions. Packing before dispatch and dispatch before confirmation are lifecycle expectations, not universal timestamp axioms.
- **Weak heuristics:** Scanner/carrier similarity, timestamp proximity, compatible routes/customers, and amount equality rank candidates. `PK8-2` particularly supports PK-8; `N-421?` favors DEL-Y but contains explicit uncertainty. NORTH and C17 discriminate poorly because several objects share them. Comparing amounts assumes compatible currencies and accounting bases, which are not specified.
- **Evidence against a repair:** A competing identifier, unnecessary extra participants, unsupported transitive participation, or an incompatible lifecycle weakens a proposal. Fees and discounts weaken the inference that INV-44 must bill both orders. Unapplied cash weakens the inference that PAY-2 must settle it. An unused object does not itself justify attaching it somewhere.

Combine evidence by explanatory fit and the assumptions required, without treating correlated observations as independent votes. The invoice's total and ev12's amount probably repeat one fact. PAY-2's bank reference identifies the already-linked Payment, not its invoice. Existing E2O/O2O agreement is useful independent corroboration when both were originally observed; newly inferred companion edges are dependent conclusions.

Confidence numbers below are qualitative judgments, not calibrated statistical probabilities. Bundle A prefers the specific scanner/carrier clues and leaves financial and confirmation assignments open. Its accepted relations remain defeasible. E2O uses the declared neutral qualifier `involved`; this supplies no additional business-role evidence.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A: conservative/minimum-change repair.** Select P01–P04, adding two E2O and two O2O relations. ev08 packs IT-B1 into PK-8, making it a shared package containing items from both orders. ev10 dispatches PK-8 through DEL-Y, favored over DEL-X by the carrier reference. Keep ev11's observed ORD-A relation only: acknowledgment of X, Y, both, or just the order remains possible. Do not add INV-44–ORD-B or PAY-2–INV-44. Invoice charges beyond A's declared value and unapplied PAY-2 are possible explanations, not asserted facts. The resulting package lifecycles are coherent; delivery completion and financial allocation remain incomplete.

**Bundle B: shared delivery and consolidated finance.** Select P01/P02, P05/P06/P07, and P09–P12. ev08 still uses PK-8. Both dispatch events reference DEL-X, which carries both packages; the uncertain N-421? is treated as an unreliable reference. DEL-Y remains unobserved in event lifecycles. Conditional on that dispatch interpretation, ev11 acknowledges X, which carries both packages containing A's items. Y is weaker within B because no package is assigned to it, but outside B the wildcard does not distinguish them. A joint X/Y acknowledgment and an order-only acknowledgment remain credible alternatives.

INV-44 bills both orders, and both payments settle that invoice. The matching sums support this coherent scenario without proving it or allocating individual payments to individual orders. Packing precedes dispatch, confirmation follows dispatch, and invoicing precedes payments. B requires more assumptions and is less supported, particularly at ev10. It retains two dispatch events rather than merging them, and does not extend ev11 to ORD-B merely because B shares the package. It also does not manufacture activity for DEL-Y.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

- **Order:** A has creation, two additions, confirmation, and invoice issue; B has creation and one addition. Missing packing/dispatch in these order projections is expected because those events need not directly reference orders. The original Add Item E2O links independently agree with all three original `contains` links. B's absent billing/confirmation is unresolved, not a violation.
- **Item:** All three have addition followed by packing. A's two package memberships have original E2O/O2O support. IT-B1's repaired membership has only one inferential basis expressed twice.
- **Package:** PK-7 has packing then dispatch. PK-8 has two packing events then dispatch. Multiple items per package, including items from different orders, violate no supplied rule. Its repaired second membership is uncertain. PK-7's delivery connection has original E2O/O2O agreement; PK-8's repaired delivery connection is dependent evidence.
- **Delivery:** X and Y each have one dispatch event under A and no delivery-linked confirmation. These are plausible partial lifecycles. Y was entirely isolated in the observed relation graphs; eliminating that isolation is a consequence, not evidence for P03. B instead leaves Y isolated and gives X two package dispatches followed by confirmation.
- **Invoice:** INV-44 has issue followed by one observed payment. Original ev12 and `bills ORD-A` agree, as do ev13 and PAY-1's `settles` edge. The difference between total 230 and A's declared 130 is suspicious but explicable. B introduces ordinary many-orders-to-one-invoice billing and two payments to one invoice.
- **Payment:** Each Payment has one recording event. PAY-2 is event-linked, so it is not an isolated object; only its allocation is absent. This fits the allowed unapplied-cash case. A does not claim the invoice is fully paid.

Every event retains at least one object: there are no objectless orphan events. A restores the normal ev08/ev10 participant counts without imposing delivery participation on ev11. No hard one-package-per-order, one-payment-per-invoice, or one-event-per-delivery constraint is justified.

Inferring `PK-8 packs IT-B1` from assumed ev08 membership, then using that edge to “confirm” ev08, would be circular. The same applies to the repaired dispatch and B's invoice/payment companion edges. Their separate storage does not provide independent evidence.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**OC-DFG.** Originally PK-8 projects as `Pack Item → Dispatch Package`. A and B add a preceding second packing occurrence, producing `Pack Item → Pack Item`, while retaining the dispatch edge. A gives Y a dispatch activity but creates no Delivery directly-follows edge. B gives X `Dispatch Package → Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery`; the dispatch self-loop depends on handling equal timestamps and should be flagged as a tie artifact rather than evidence of rework. B also adds Invoice `Record Payment → Record Payment` and Order B `Add Item → Issue Invoice`. No original event is removed; support counts can change even when activity-pair sets remain unchanged.

**OTG.** Separate co-event interaction from qualified O2O semantics. A adds instance interactions PK-8–IT-B1 and PK-8–DEL-Y; their type pairs Item–Package and Package–Delivery already exist. B substitutes PK-8–DEL-X and adds ORD-A–DEL-X, ORD-B–INV-44, PAY-2–INV-44, and ORD-A–ORD-B through their shared invoice event. In an object-type projection, Order–Delivery is new; same-type Order–Order interaction may be suppressed. Inspect whether the installed OTG implementation uses co-events, explicit O2O, or a projection: typed O2O additions should not automatically be credited to a graph computed only from E2O.

**ET–OT graph.** A increases support for Pack Item–Package and Dispatch Package–Delivery without introducing new activity/type pairs. B additionally introduces Confirm Delivery–Delivery and increases Issue Invoice–Order and Record Payment–Invoice support. An unweighted graph conceals most of these changes.

**Flattenings.** Order A remains `Create, Add, Add, Confirm, Issue` throughout. Order B changes from `Create, Add` to `Create, Add, Issue` only in B. Package PK-8 acquires a second Pack occurrence in both repairs. Delivery flattening changes from X's single Dispatch and Y's empty lifecycle to one Dispatch each in A; B gives X two Dispatch rows and Confirm, leaving Y empty. Invoice changes from `Issue, Record` to `Issue, Record, Record` only in B. Payment flattenings remain one Record per payment. Ordinary flattening follows direct E2O participation; it does not propagate package events into order cases through O2O paths.

**Frequencies.** The activity totals remain 2 Create, 3 Add, 3 Pack, 2 Dispatch, 1 Confirm, 1 Issue, and 2 Record events: 14 throughout. E2O incidences rise from 22 to 24 in A and 27 in B. Count distinct event–object pairs rather than counting qualifier duplicates. For `(distinct events, unique objects, total incidences)` by activity/type: Pack/Package changes `(2,2,2) → (3,2,3)` in both repairs; Dispatch/Delivery changes `(1,1,1) → (2,2,2)` in A or `(2,1,2)` in B. B's Issue/Order changes `(1,1,1) → (1,2,2)`, and Record/Invoice changes `(1,1,1) → (2,1,2)`. Unique-object and event counts therefore tell different stories.

**Equivalent-execution clustering around Order.** A leaves direct order traces unchanged but connects B to A's package neighborhood. B also couples the orders through a shared delivery and invoice and extends B's trace. Neighborhood-based signatures can therefore change; trace-only signatures change only for B. The orders still differ in item count and direct confirmation participation, so neither repair guarantees that they become equivalent executions. A clustering implementation's neighborhood and equivalence definitions must be stated.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

Assume canonical OCEL column names. Preserve snapshots, validate identities and directions, and report defeasible cardinality deviations separately. Constructor and discovery interfaces should be checked against the installed release. Confidence and justification belong in a separate provenance artifact; they are not evidence supplied by PM4Py. Do not use `pm4py.ocel_o2o_enrichment` to certify `packs`, `carries`, `bills`, or `settles`.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.ocel.obj import OCEL

# Snapshot all original tables before any normalization or repair.
original = {
    "events": events.copy(deep=True),
    "objects": objects.copy(deep=True),
    "relations": relations.copy(deep=True),
    "o2o": o2o.copy(deep=True),
}
ev = original["events"].copy(deep=True)
ob = original["objects"].copy(deep=True)
r = original["relations"].copy(deep=True)
o = original["o2o"].copy(deep=True)

EID, OID, OID2 = "ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2"
ACT, TIME, TYPE, QUAL = (
    "ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp", "ocel:type", "ocel:qualifier"
)
ekeys, okeys = [EID, OID, QUAL], [OID, OID2, QUAL]
for frame in (r, o):
    if QUAL not in frame.columns:
        frame[QUAL] = ""
    frame[QUAL] = frame[QUAL].fillna("")

assert ev[[EID, ACT, TIME]].notna().all().all()
assert ob[[OID, TYPE]].notna().all().all()
assert not ev.duplicated(EID).any(), "Duplicate event ID"
assert not ob.duplicated(OID).any(), "Duplicate object ID"
ev[TIME] = pd.to_datetime(ev[TIME], errors="raise")

def check_links(rel, objrel):
    assert rel[ekeys].notna().all().all()
    assert objrel[okeys].notna().all().all()
    assert rel[EID].isin(ev[EID]).all(), "Unknown E2O event"
    assert rel[OID].isin(ob[OID]).all(), "Unknown E2O object"
    assert objrel[OID].isin(ob[OID]).all(), "Unknown O2O source"
    assert objrel[OID2].isin(ob[OID]).all(), "Unknown O2O target"
    assert not rel.duplicated(ekeys).any(), "Duplicate E2O triple"
    assert not objrel.duplicated(okeys).any(), "Duplicate O2O triple"

check_links(r, o)
before_e2o = set(r[ekeys].itertuples(index=False, name=None))
before_o2o = set(o[okeys].itertuples(index=False, name=None))

# Selected Bundle A only. Alternative bundles must start from the snapshots.
eadd = pd.DataFrame([
    {EID: "ev08", OID: "PK-8", QUAL: "involved"},
    {EID: "ev10", OID: "DEL-Y", QUAL: "involved"},
])
oadd = pd.DataFrame([
    {OID: "PK-8", OID2: "IT-B1", QUAL: "packs"},
    {OID: "DEL-Y", OID2: "PK-8", QUAL: "carries"},
])
r = pd.concat([r, eadd], ignore_index=True)
o = pd.concat([o, oadd], ignore_index=True)
check_links(r, o)
assert before_e2o <= set(r[ekeys].itertuples(index=False, name=None))
assert before_o2o <= set(o[okeys].itertuples(index=False, name=None))

# Canonical event/object tables supply denormalized relation metadata.
# Preserve other relation columns and do not duplicate merge suffixes.
r = r.drop(columns=[ACT, TIME, TYPE], errors="ignore")
r = r.merge(ev[[EID, ACT, TIME]], on=EID, how="left",
            validate="many_to_one")
r = r.merge(ob[[OID, TYPE]], on=OID, how="left",
            validate="many_to_one")
check_links(r, o)

# These qualifier directions are defined by this dataset's vocabulary.
otype = ob.set_index(OID)[TYPE].to_dict()
directions = {
    "contains": ("Order", "Item"),
    "packs": ("Package", "Item"),
    "carries": ("Delivery", "Package"),
    "bills": ("Invoice", "Order"),
    "settles": ("Payment", "Invoice"),
}
for source, target, qualifier in o[okeys].itertuples(index=False, name=None):
    if qualifier in directions:
        assert (otype[source], otype[target]) == directions[qualifier]

# Count objects, not relation rows: distinct qualifiers do not inflate counts.
counts = r.groupby([EID, TYPE])[OID].nunique().unstack(fill_value=0)
counts = counts.reindex(index=ev[EID], fill_value=0)
normal = {
    "Create Order": {"Order": (1, 1)},
    "Add Item": {"Order": (1, 1), "Item": (1, 1)},
    "Pack Item": {"Item": (1, 1), "Package": (1, 1)},
    "Dispatch Package": {"Package": (1, None), "Delivery": (1, 1)},
    "Issue Invoice": {"Invoice": (1, 1), "Order": (1, None)},
    "Record Payment": {"Payment": (1, 1), "Invoice": (1, 1)},
}
soft_deviations = []
for eid, activity in ev[[EID, ACT]].itertuples(index=False, name=None):
    for typ, (minimum, maximum) in normal.get(activity, {}).items():
        count = int(counts.at[eid, typ]) if typ in counts.columns else 0
        if count < minimum or (maximum is not None and count > maximum):
            soft_deviations.append((eid, activity, typ, count))
# ev14's missing invoice is an allowed case, not an automatic failure.
# Confirm Delivery deliberately has no mandatory Delivery count.
print("Default-cardinality deviations:", soft_deviations)

# VERSION-DEPENDENT constructor line: confirm named o2o support and
# canonical column defaults in the installed PM4Py release.
ocel = OCEL(events=ev, objects=ob, relations=r, o2o=o)

ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)
flat_order = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Order")
flat_delivery = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Delivery")
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. Assigning ev11 to both deliveries automatically can turn one uncertain portal acknowledgment into two confirmed delivery lifecycles.
2. Assigning ev10 to both X and Y as a hedge creates simultaneous multi-delivery participation unsupported by ordinary dispatch semantics.
3. Putting both packages on X solely because dispatch timestamps match can create a false common shipment and a dispatch self-loop.
4. Treating 230 = 130 + 100 as proof of consolidated billing creates false Order–Order coupling through ev12.
5. Treating payment sums as proof of settlement can hide unapplied cash and imply invoice completion without an allocation reference.
6. Propagating packing, delivery, and payment events to every reachable order produces artificial order traces and duplicates shared events across flattened cases.
7. Mapping PAY-1 to B and PAY-2 to A by amounts alone invents order-level allocations within a possibly consolidated invoice.
8. Filling DEL-Y's lifecycle simply to eliminate an orphan, or reversing `carries`, replaces missing information with false structure. Generated O2O edges must never be counted as independent corroboration of their generating assumptions.
