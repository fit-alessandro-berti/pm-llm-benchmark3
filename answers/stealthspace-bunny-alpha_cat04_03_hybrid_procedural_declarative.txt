# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

## Material assumptions

1. **Cases are evaluated individually.** Declarative, skeleton, resource, and data checks must not combine events from different cases.
2. **Completed cases reach one of the specified outcomes.** Incomplete or live cases may have pending obligations; absence of a future event is not, by itself, proof of violation.
3. **The three initial checks have no required mutual order.** Their event timestamps can differ even though the process permits them to proceed concurrently.
4. **“Co-occur” in R8 means occurring in the same case**, not necessarily having overlapping execution intervals. Activity-only formalisms cannot reliably establish interval overlap.
5. **R2 and R8 are enforced as requirements.** Their wording as “should” is treated normatively, not as an advisory recommendation.
6. **R2 is conditional:** an accepted case has exactly one applicable approval; a rejected case has neither.
7. **The implemented clarification protocol treats a request and its response as an immediate request/response pair.** Multiple pairs are allowed before an outcome. This satisfies R3 in the modeled workflow but is stronger than R3 where unrelated work might be allowed between a request and its response.
8. **The chosen bounded implementation requires an opinion before any terminal outcome after escalation, including rejection.** R4 only says “eventually,” and R5 explicitly addresses activation. Requiring the opinion before rejection is therefore a disclosed strengthening.
9. **A legal opinion is optional when no Sanctions Escalation has occurred.**
10. **The procedural model ends at `Close Onboarding`.** It does not invent a reactivation or reinstatement path for R11. R11 is checked externally because the post-activation recovery semantics are underspecified.
11. **A timestamp equal to another event’s timestamp is not sufficient to establish “after.”** Sequence information is used for ordering; timestamps are used for elapsed-time calculations.
12. **The 24-hour emergency deadline in R6 is a hard exception condition, not a temporal-profile warning.** It is different from the advisory 48/12/72-hour profiles in R12.
13. **Missing attributes do not establish compliance.** In particular, missing resource or authorization data yields an inconclusive external check, not an automatic pass.
14. **Temporal-profile support and confidence values are policy metadata, not statistical proof.** The dictionaries below use them to document enforcement intent.

## Two defensible resolutions of R5/R6

### Resolution A — Legal precedence is absolute

R5 is implemented literally:

```text
Sanctions Escalation  Obtain Legal Opinion  Activate Vendor
```

Operations’ emergency statement is treated as an aspiration or a request for a different policy version, not as permission to violate the implemented policy. An activation that bypasses the opinion fails the procedural model.

This is the **chosen implementation** because it does not infer an undocumented exception from an event sequence.

**Compliance risk:** legitimate emergency activations may be falsely rejected. This can create operational pressure to conceal exceptions, activate through another interface, or delay urgent vendor onboarding. Legal approval may also be performed after a business decision has already taken effect.

### Resolution B — An explicit, jointly authorized emergency exception

Create a separately versioned policy, such as `vendor-onboarding-emergency-v2`, in which R5 is amended to:

```text
Sanctions Escalation  [Obtain Legal Opinion before activation]
                       OR
                       [documented joint emergency authorization,
                        followed by Obtain Legal Opinion within 24 hours]
```

The authorization must be bound to the case, vendor, activation event, and policy version. The exception must be supported by an external authorization registry or signed authorization record; the current event log cannot establish it.

This resolution is operationally plausible, but it intentionally relaxes the absolute legal-precedence control. It introduces legal and financial exposure between activation and the opinion, and depends on reliable, non-replayable authorization evidence.

**The implemented model below uses Resolution A.** The code in Section 7 shows how Resolution B can be validated as a separate policy, without silently enabling it.

# 2. Requirement-to-Formalism Mapping

| Requirement | Primary formalism | Exact encoding | Why this formalism | Redundancy or limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R1** | Procedural model | The rejection, low-risk, and high-risk branches are mutually exclusive. | XOR directly represents mutually exclusive outcomes. | Also guarded by DECLARE `noncoexistence`, `exactly_one`, and skeleton `never_together`. DECLARE `exactly_one` strengthens “not both” to “one outcome per completed case.” |
| **R2** | Procedural model; conditional external validator | Low branch: one `Manager Approval`; high branch: `Enhanced Due Diligence  Compliance Approval`; reject branch: no approval. | The route structure establishes exactly which approval is applicable. | The approval set must **not** use whole-case `exactly_one`, because that would incorrectly require an approval in rejected cases. Whole-case DECLARE enforces only absence of the forbidden combinations. |
| **R3** | Procedural model, authoritative for the chosen atomic clarification protocol | Reusable request/response loops before classification and before activation/rejection. | Loops and transitions naturally enforce ordering. | DECLARE uses immediate `response` and `chainresponse`; the skeleton uses count equivalence. All three are weaker or different outside the chosen protocol. |
| **R4** | Procedural model, as a bounded interpretation of “eventually” | After optional escalation, the required legal step must be completed before continuing to any final outcome. | A completed accepting trace then contains the required opinion. | A finite trace cannot prove future eventual occurrence. Outside the bounded model, an obligation monitor is required. DECLARE precedence is stronger than bare eventual response. |
| **R5** | Procedural model | Escalation, when present, precedes the mandatory legal step; that step precedes activation. | Direct control-flow precedence is unambiguous. | Also duplicated in DECLARE and the skeleton. Under Resolution B, these encodings must be removed or parameterized, not silently bypassed. |
| **R6** | Separate policy/authorization validator | Detect an activation after escalation but before an intervening opinion; verify external joint authorization and `opinion_time  activation_time  24 hours`. | Requires case attributes, authorization provenance, and deadlines absent from the activity sequence. | Under Resolution A this exception is disabled. Under Resolution B absence of evidence is `policy-dependent`, never an inferred pass. |
| **R7** | DECLARE, backed by the process | `precedence(("Run Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation"))`. | Direct trace-order constraint. | Also in the skeleton. It does not establish why escalation was necessary or whether escalation was repeated. |
| **R8** | Procedural model; DECLARE for independent detection | Disjoint routes and `noncoexistence` for Manager/Compliance and Manager/EDD. | These are forbidden combinations within a case. | “Same case” is assumed. Actual simultaneous execution cannot be recovered from event timestamps alone. |
| **R9** | Resource-aware validator | Compare `resource` for `Verify Bank Account` with the sole applicable approval, and `Capture Master Data` with `Activate Vendor`. | Requires performer identity, which the process and ordinary DECLARE cannot express. | DECLARE and the skeleton can check ordering only, not identity inequality. |
| **R10** | Process plus skeleton cardinality | Single terminal-route construction; skeleton `activ_freq` allows zero or one occurrence of the three named activities. | Cardinality is natural in a skeleton and structurally enforced in the backbone. | Zero occurrences are allowed by the skeleton for incomplete cases. The process requires one primary outcome for a completed case. |
| **R11** | Separate event-log validator | For every post-activation `Change Bank Details`, require eventual suspension and, if a later payment exists, a fresh subsequent verification before that payment. | Requires a conditional relation over event occurrences and a post-activation scope. | The main process stops at closure. Coexistence or pairwise precedence alone cannot distinguish the initial verification from a fresh one. |
| **R12** | Temporal profile | Three directed activity pairs with the specified means and standard deviations, `zeta=2.0`. | Separates statistical elapsed-time expectations from mandatory ordering. | A deviation is a warning only. It cannot prove a control-flow or authorization violation. |

# 3. Procedural Backbone

## 1. Compact formal description

For places \(p,q\), use these abbreviations:

```text
ask(p,q) =
    p  Request Clarification  Receive Clarification  q
    OR
    p    q

option(p,q,A) =
    p  A  q
    OR
    p    q
```

The principal net is:

```text
start
   Receive Onboarding Request
   ask-loop / start-checks
   Capture Master Data  
    Run Sanctions Screening  concurrent-check join
    Verify Bank Account   
   ask-loop
   Classify Risk
   ask-loop
   optional Sanctions Escalation
   ask-loop
   optional Obtain Legal Opinion
   XOR:
      Reject Vendor  Close Onboarding

      Manager Approval
         ask-loop
         Activate Vendor
         Release First Payment
         Close Onboarding

      Enhanced Due Diligence
         ask-loop
         Compliance Approval
         Activate Vendor
         Release First Payment
         Close Onboarding
```

The implementation also allows a clarification pair before the initial checks.

## 2. Self-contained PM4Py code

This uses an **accepting Petri net**, avoiding any implicit POWL edge inference.

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri.petrinet import PetriNet, Transition
from pm4py.objects.petri.utils import accepting_petri_net, add_arc_from_to


@accepting_petri_net
def build_procedural_model():
    net = PetriNet("controlled_vendor_onboarding_legal_first")

    def place(name):
        return net.add_place(name)

    def visible(name):
        return net.add_transition(name)

    def invisible(name):
        t = Transition(name)
        t.visible = False
        net.add_transition(t)
        return t

    def wire(before, activity, after):
        add_arc_from_to(before, activity)
        add_arc_from_to(activity, after)

    def attach_clarification(entry, exit_place):
        """Exclusive request/response pair or silent skip; repeatable at entry."""
        request = visible("Request Clarification")
        response = visible("Receive Clarification")
        skip = invisible("tau_skip_clarification")
        resume = invisible("tau_clarification_complete")

        add_arc_from_to(entry, request)
        add_arc_from_to(request, response)
        add_arc_from_to(response, resume)
        add_arc_from_to(resume, exit_place)

        add_arc_from_to(entry, skip)
        add_arc_from_to(skip, exit_place)

    def attach_optional(entry, exit_place, activity_name):
        """Exclusive named activity or silent skip."""
        activity = visible(activity_name)
        skip = invisible("tau_skip_" + activity_name)

        add_arc_from_to(entry, activity)
        add_arc_from_to(activity, exit_place)
        add_arc_from_to(entry, skip)
        add_arc_from_to(skip, exit_place)

    start = place("start")
    request_received = place("request_received")
    checks = place("start_checks")
    classification_entry = place("classification_entry")
    classified = place("classified")
    escalation_entry = place("escalation_entry")
    legal_entry = place("legal_entry")
    decision = place("decision")
    manager_completed = place("manager_completed")
    approved = place("approved")
    edd_completed = place("edd_completed")
    compliant = place("compliant")
    closed = place("closed")

    wire(
        start,
        visible("Receive Onboarding Request"),
        request_received
    )

    # Clarification is possible before the initial checks and is repeatable.
    attach_clarification(request_received, checks)

    # Three tokens are created here, allowing unordered check completion.
    wire(checks, visible("Capture Master Data"), checks)
    wire(checks, visible("Run Sanctions Screening"), checks)
    wire(checks, visible("Verify Bank Account"), checks)

    # Clarification after all checks and before classification.
    attach_clarification(checks, classification_entry)
    wire(
        classification_entry,
        visible("Classify Risk"),
        classified
    )

    # Clarification after classification.
    attach_clarification(classified, escalation_entry)

    # Run Sanctions Screening has necessarily completed here.
    attach_optional(
        escalation_entry,
        legal_entry,
        "Sanctions Escalation"
    )

    # Clarification between escalation/skip and the legal opinion/skip.
    attach_clarification(legal_entry, decision)
    attach_optional(
        legal_entry,
        decision,
        "Obtain Legal Opinion"
    )

    # Exactly one primary route consumes the decision token.
    wire(decision, visible("Reject Vendor"), closed)
    wire(decision, visible("Manager Approval"), manager_completed)
    wire(decision, visible("Enhanced Due Diligence"), edd_completed)

    # Clarification after approval/EDD, but before the final outcome.
    attach_clarification(manager_completed, approved)
    attach_clarification(edd_completed, compliant)
    wire(compliant, visible("Compliance Approval"), approved)

    wire(approved, visible("Activate Vendor"), approved)
    wire(approved, visible("Release First Payment"), closed)

    # No post-activation maintenance arcs are invented in this base model.
    return net


procedural_model = build_procedural_model()

# For a case represented by an EventLog:
# aligned_log = pm4py.align_log(log, procedural_model)
```

## Behavioral interpretation

- **Concurrency:** The three initial checks have independent input/output arcs to one place. Any check may finish first.
- **Choices:** The three outcome routes are exclusive because they consume the same single token in `decision`.
- **Clarification loops:** A request/response pair can be followed by a silent skip. Revisiting the same entry permits multiple cycles. Under the selected protocol, another request or a final outcome cannot intervene between a request and its response.
- **Legal control:** After an escalation, the selected legal-first policy must take the legal transition before the decision route. It may also be taken without escalation.
- **High-risk route:** The code permits clarification between EDD and Compliance Approval. Both must complete before activation.
- **Terminal outcomes:** A completed accepted case has one activation and one payment. A rejected case has no approval, payment, or activation.
- **Post-activation behavior:** Deliberately excluded. The base net terminates at `Close Onboarding`; Section 7 handles R11 externally. A future reinstatement/reactivation design would require additional policy decisions and must respect the at-most-once activation rule in R10.

### Meaning and direction of every encoded rule

| Rule | Direction and semantics |
|---|---|
| `existence["Receive Onboarding Request"]` | Every evaluated case must contain receipt. |
| `existence["Close Onboarding"]` | These evaluations assume a completed case and require closure. An open case needs separate pending handling. |
| `init["Receive Onboarding Request"]` | Receipt is the initial activity. |
| `exactly_one["primary_outcome"]` | Exactly one of rejection and activation occurs. This is stronger than R1 alone, but matches the completed backbone. |
| `noncoexistence[(Reject, Activate)]` | Rejection and activation cannot both appear anywhere in one case. It does not forbid a rejection immediately after activation; that is handled by `exactly_one`. |
| `noncoexistence[(Reject, Manager)]` and `(Reject, Compliance)` | Enforce the “neither approval in a rejected case” part of R2. |
| `noncoexistence[(Manager, Compliance)]` and `(Manager, EDD)` | Enforce R8 for the whole case, not merely immediate succession. |
| `responded_existence[(Request, Receive)]` | If a request occurs, a response must exist somewhere. Unlike `response`, it does not establish adjacency. |
| `responded_existence[(Receive, Request)]` | A received clarification must correspond to some request in the case. |
| `response[(Request, Receive)]` | A request must be followed **immediately** by its response in the event sequence. This implements the chosen atomic clarification protocol. |
| `chainresponse[(Request, Receive, Request)]` | A request-response pair may be followed by another request, with no intervening event. |
| `chainresponse[(Request, Receive, Classify)]` | A request-response pair may be followed by classification, with no intervening event. Other legitimate terminal choices are supplied by the procedural branch, not by this declarative pair alone. |
| `precedence[(Run Screening, Sanctions Escalation)]` | If escalation occurs, screening must have occurred earlier. Neither activity is mandatory solely because of this rule. |
| `precedence[(Sanctions Escalation, Legal Opinion)]` | If an opinion occurs, escalation must precede it. With an escalation, the chosen bounded policy also requires the opinion. |
| The three `precedence` rules starting at `Classify Risk` | If a listed terminal/approval/EDD activity occurs, classification must precede it. |
| `precedence[(EDD, Compliance)]` and `(Manager, Activate)` | Approval-related ordering, allowing intervening activities. |
| `precedence[(Compliance, Activate)]` | An approval precedes activation; an approval is not mandatory where absent. |
| `precedence[(Capture, Activate)]` and the two `Verify`/approval rules | These are ordering-only guards for R9. They do **not** compare performers. |
| `precedence[(Activate, Release)]`, `(Release, Close)`, `(Reject, Close)` | Terminal-flow ordering, conditional on the target activity occurring. |

`response` and `altresponse` both concern an immediate successor, but `altresponse(A, B, C)` permits alternative successors B or C. `chainresponse(A, B, C)` specifies a two-step immediate chain.

`noncoexistence` forbids both activities anywhere in the case. `nonsuccession(A, B)` would forbid only an immediate A-to-B transition, so it is not used for R1.

R11 is not encoded here: ordinary DECLARE cannot express “a **new** verification after a change and before any later payment,” conditioned on a prior activation.

## Interpretation and overlap

- In `equivalence`, orientation is immaterial: the two activities must have equal occurrence counts.
- Every `always_before` pair is written as **source before target**.
- Every `always_after` pair is the reverse of an `always_before` pair: the source must already have occurred when the target occurs.
- `never_together` is unordered. The three pairs encode R1 and R8.
- `activ_freq` encodes the at-most-once constraints in R10 and adds the normal single-closure expectation. It deliberately does not require activation or rejection in every partial case.
- `directly_follows` is empty because the skeleton is an exploratory, cross-implementation model. The selected clarification protocol is stricter in the process and DECLARE layers.
- `("Change Bank Details", "Suspend Vendor")` is only a weak skeleton guard: if both occur, the change must precede suspension. It cannot require suspension when the change is absent, distinguish post-activation changes, or enforce a fresh verification.
- `equivalence` detects T5’s one request versus zero responses, but it does not determine whether matching counts are in the correct order or precede a final outcome.

For accepted-case R2, a projected subcase could use `exactly_one` on the two approval activities. Applying that projection to a rejected case would be wrong.

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"):
        (48 * 3600.0, 24 * 3600.0),
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"):
        (12 * 3600.0, 6 * 3600.0),
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"):
        (72 * 3600.0, 36 * 3600.0),
}

zeta = 2.0
```

For a matched pair with elapsed time \(d\), use:

\[
z = \frac{d-\mu}{\sigma}
\]

A two-sided profile deviation has magnitude greater than `zeta`; the conceptual range is:

\[
\mu - 2\sigma \le d \le \mu + 2\sigma
\]

These ranges are:

| Pair | Mean | Two-sided range |
|---|---:|---:|
| Request  Receive | 48 h | 0–96 h |
| Escalation  Opinion | 12 h | 0–24 h |
| Activation  Payment | 72 h | 0–144 h |

A deviation is a **warning**, not a control-flow violation. R12 explicitly says these expectations are not sufficient to reject automatically.

The R6 deadline is different: if an emergency exception is enabled, `opinion_time  activation_time  24 hours` is a hard exception condition. For T6, the deadline is hour 37, not the profile’s escalation-based upper expectation.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

## Required event schema

Normalize native PM4Py attributes into a dataframe with at least:

- `case_id`
- `activity`
- `resource`
- `timestamp`
- a stable event identifier and tie-breaking event order

Optional but useful case/event attributes include:

- `vendor_id`
- `risk_classification`
- `accepted`
- `authorization_id`
- `policy_version`

The `resource` requirement is a material assumption; the notation `Activity@resource` is not enough if the ingestion pipeline discards that attribute.

The pseudocode below uses stable sequence order for ordering and timestamps for elapsed time.

```python
def result(status, rule, evidence=None):
    return {
        "status": status,       # pass, fail, warning, not evaluated, policy-dependent
        "rule": rule,
        "evidence": evidence or {},
    }


def check_outcome_sod_and_route(case_df, accepted):
    """
    Checks R1, R2, R8, R9, and event-count portions of R10.
    'accepted' must be supplied by an authoritative case classification.
    """
    required = {"case_id", "activity", "resource"}
    missing = required - set(case_df.columns)
    if missing:
        return result("not evaluated", "R2/R8/R9/R10",
                      {"missing_attributes": sorted(missing)})

    if case_df["resource"].isna().any():
        return result("not evaluated", "R9",
                      {"missing_resource": True})

    def events(name):
        return case_df.loc[case_df["activity"].eq(name)].copy()

    reject = events("Reject Vendor")
    activate = events("Activate Vendor")
    pay = events("Release First Payment")
    manager = events("Manager Approval")
    compliance = events("Compliance Approval")
    edd = events("Enhanced Due Diligence")
    verify = events("Verify Bank Account")
    capture = events("Capture Master Data")

    # Terminal contradiction and at-most-once checks.
    for name, frame in [
        ("Reject Vendor", reject),
        ("Activate Vendor", activate),
        ("Release First Payment", pay),
    ]:
        if len(frame) > 1:
            return result("fail", "R1/R10",
                          {"activity": name, "count": len(frame)})

    if len(reject) and len(activate):
        return result("fail", "R1",
                      {"reject": len(reject), "activate": len(activate)})

    approvals = pd.concat([manager, compliance], ignore_index=True)

    # The route's accepted/rejected interpretation comes from case policy,
    # not merely from whichever approval happens to be present.
    if accepted and len(approvals) != 1:
        return result("fail", "R2",
                      {"approval_count": len(approvals)})

    if not accepted and len(approvals) != 0:
        return result("fail", "R2",
                      {"approval_count": len(approvals)})

    if len(manager) and (len(compliance) or len(edd)):
        return result("fail", "R8",
                      {"manager": len(manager),
                       "compliance": len(compliance),
                       "edd": len(edd)})

    if not accepted:
        return result("pass", "R2/R8")

    # Exactly one approval exists here, so it is the applicable approval.
    approval = approvals.iloc[0]
    approval_resource = approval["resource"]

    if verify["resource"].eq(approval_resource).any():
        return result("fail", "R9",
                      {
                          "rule_part": "verification_approval",
                          "resource": approval_resource,
                      })

    if activate["resource"].isin(capture["resource"]).any():
        return result("fail", "R9",
                      {"rule_part": "capture_activation"})

    return result("pass", "R9")
```

If the PM4Py event attribute is `org:resource`, normalize it to `resource`. A fast, non-authoritative adaptation of the built-in filter is:

## Emergency-policy check

Required external authorization fields include:

- authorization ID and policy version;
- case ID and vendor ID;
- activation event ID or unambiguous activation timestamp;
- authenticated authorizer identities and required authorization roles;
- issue time and validity interval;
- revocation status;
- an integrity-verifiable signature or registry proof.

The organization must configure what “jointly authorized” means. For example, a policy could require distinct designated Legal and Operations authorizers.

```python
def check_r6_emergency(
    case_df,
    authorization_registry,
    policy,
    as_of,
    closed=False,
):
    """
    policy example:
      {
        "version": "vendor-onboarding-legal-first-v1",
        "r5_absolute": True,
        "allow_emergency": False,
        "deadline_hours": 24
      }

    Resolution B would use:
      {
        "version": "vendor-onboarding-emergency-v2",
        "r5_absolute": False,
        "allow_emergency": True,
        "deadline_hours": 24
      }
    """
    required = {"case_id", "activity", "timestamp", "event_order"}
    missing = required - set(case_df.columns)
    if missing:
        return result("not evaluated", "R6",
                      {"missing_attributes": sorted(missing)})

    activations = case_df.loc[case_df["activity"].eq("Activate Vendor")]
    if len(activations) != 1:
        return result("not evaluated", "R6",
                      {"reason": "activation cardinality is not established"})

    activation = activations.iloc[0]
    activations_before = case_df.loc[
        case_df["event_order"].lt(activation["event_order"])
    ]

    escalations = activations_before.loc[
        activations_before["activity"].eq("Sanctions Escalation")
    ]
    opinions = activations_before.loc[
        activations_before["activity"].eq("Obtain Legal Opinion")
    ]

    for escalation in escalations.to_dict("records"):
        intervening_opinion = opinions.loc[
            opinions["event_order"].gt(escalation["event_order"])
        ]

        # An opinion between escalation and activation is the normal path.
        if len(intervening_opinion):
            continue

        # Only a policy permitting an exception can waive the R5 failure.
        if not policy.get("allow_emergency", False):
            return result(
                "not evaluated",
                "R6",
                {
                    "reason": "emergency exception disabled",
                    "candidate_breach_is_enforced_by": "R5 procedure",
                },
            )

        grant = authorization_registry.verify(
            case_id=case_df["case_id"].iloc[0],
            vendor_id=case_df.get("vendor_id"),
            policy_version=policy["version"],
            authorization_time=activation["timestamp"],
            escalation_event_id=escalation.get("event_id"),
        )

        if grant["status"] == "missing":
            return result("policy-dependent", "R6",
                          {"reason": "external authorization unavailable"})

        if grant["status"] in {"invalid", "expired", "revoked", "out_of_scope"}:
            return result("fail", "R6", {"grant": grant})

        if grant["status"] == "unverifiable":
            return result("policy-dependent", "R6",
                          {"reason": "joint authorization cannot be verified"})

        future_opinions = case_df.loc[
            case_df["activity"].eq("Obtain Legal Opinion")
            & case_df["event_order"].gt(activation["event_order"])
        ]

        deadline = (
            activation["timestamp"]
            + pd.Timedelta(hours=policy["deadline_hours"])
        )

        if future_opinions.empty:
            if as_of > deadline or closed:
                return result("fail", "R6",
                              {"reason": "opinion deadline exceeded"})
            return result("not evaluated", "R6",
                          {"status": "pending", "deadline": deadline})

        opinion = future_opinions.sort_values("event_order").iloc[0]
        elapsed = opinion["timestamp"] - activation["timestamp"]

        if elapsed > pd.Timedelta(hours=policy["deadline_hours"]):
            return result("fail", "R6", {"elapsed_hours": elapsed})

        return result("pass", "R6", {"elapsed_hours": elapsed})

    return result("pass", "R6", {"reason": "exception not triggered"})
```

The activation and opinion timestamps must be timezone-aware and preferably UTC-normalized. A gap in the event sequence suggests a possible emergency bypass, but does not prove that an emergency occurred or was authorized.

## R11 occurrence-sensitive validator

The following policy interprets R11 as follows:

- A changed bank detail is a `Change Bank Details` occurring after activation.
- Each such change requires an eventual later suspension.
- If a later payment exists, a **fresh** verification must occur after the change and before every such later payment.
- If no later payment exists, only the suspension obligation is enforced.

```python
def check_r11(case_df):
    required = {"activity", "event_order", "timestamp"}
    missing = required - set(case_df.columns)
    if missing:
        return result("not evaluated", "R11",
                      {"missing_attributes": sorted(missing)})

    c = case_df.sort_values(["event_order", "timestamp"]).copy()
    closed = c["activity"].eq("Close Onboarding").any()

    activations = c.loc[c["activity"].eq("Activate Vendor")]
    if len(activations) != 1:
        return result("not evaluated", "R11",
                      {"reason": "exactly one activation is required"})

    activation_order = activations.iloc[0]["event_order"]

    changes = c.loc[
        c["activity"].eq("Change Bank Details")
        & c["event_order"].gt(activation_order)
    ]

    suspensions = c.loc[c["activity"].eq("Suspend Vendor")]
    verifications = c.loc[c["activity"].eq("Verify Bank Account")]
    payments = c.loc[c["activity"].eq("Release First Payment")]

    for change in changes.to_dict("records"):
        co = change["event_order"]

        if not suspensions["event_order"].gt(co).any():
            status = "fail" if closed else "not evaluated"
            return result(status, "R11",
                          {"change": co, "reason": "suspension pending/absent"})

        for payment in payments.to_dict("records"):
            po = payment["event_order"]
            if po <= co:
                continue

            fresh_verification = (
                verifications["event_order"].gt(co)
                & verifications["event_order"].lt(po)
            ).any()

            if not fresh_verification:
                return result(
                    "fail", "R11",
                    {
                        "change": co,
                        "later_payment": po,
                        "reason": "missing fresh bank verification",
                    },
                )

    return result("pass", "R11")
```

The initial verification cannot satisfy this check because it occurs before the activation and before the change. The validator deliberately does not require a particular order between suspension and verification, since R11 does not impose one.

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

1. **Duplicated enforcement**
   - R1 is enforced by the procedural XOR, DECLARE `noncoexistence`/`exactly_one`, and skeleton `never_together`.
   - R5 is duplicated in the process, DECLARE precedence, and skeleton `always_before`.
   - R7 is duplicated in the process and both declarative representations.
   
   These are useful defense-in-depth checks, but they are not independent evidence. Conflicting findings must identify the authoritative layer rather than being resolved by majority vote.

2. **Different R3 semantics**
   - The selected process requires an immediate response.
   - DECLARE `response` also requires adjacency.
   - Skeleton equivalence checks only counts.
   
   For example, a conceptual trace with `Request  Capture Master Data  Receive Clarification` satisfies equivalence but fails the selected process and DECLARE. The atomic handoff is a disclosed procedural restriction. If work between request and response is legitimate, the policy needs a separate relaxed variant with occurrence-sensitive prefix/suffix checks.

3. **“Eventually” is not fully expressible as a finite-log success condition**
   - R3, R4, and R11 contain future obligations.
   - A finite trace can show a violation if a prohibited final outcome already occurred.
   - It can also show a pending obligation, but cannot prove that a future response/opinion/suspension will occur.
   - The process makes selected bounded versions of these obligations executable; live monitoring still needs deadline and pending-state logic.

4. **Post-activation behavior is outside the canonical process**
   - The base net ends at closure.
   - Adding a `Change Bank Details` transition to that net after `Close Onboarding` would make the original accepting trace fail.
   - A simple sequence such as change  suspend  verify  payment leaves unresolved whether a suspended vendor may receive payment without reinstatement.
   - R11 should therefore remain an external policy check until the organization defines repair, reinstatement, and terminal semantics.
   - A blanket `never_together(Activate, Change)` would avoid modeling work but would contradict R11’s explicit allowance for post-activation changes.

5. **Vacuity in declarative rules**
   - `precedence(A, B)` does not require either activity.
   - `Sanctions Escalation  Obtain Legal Opinion` is vacuous when there is no escalation.
   - A rule for Manager Approval is vacuous in a high-risk case.
   - Skeleton `always_before` can be vacuous for optional activities.
   - Whole-case approval `exactly_one` would be both vacuous and incorrect in rejected cases unless the accepted-case projection is explicit.

6. **Missing case and event attributes**
   - Without `case_id`, cross-case aggregation can create false ordering and resource conflicts.
   - Without `resource`, R9 cannot be evaluated.
   - Without risk or accepted/rejected case status, route validation may be ambiguous.
   - Without stable event order and timestamps, ordering and deadlines are unreliable.
   - Without external authorization provenance, R6 cannot be decided.

7. **Timestamp-only limitations**
   - Event timestamps do not reveal execution intervals.
   - Different timestamps may represent delayed event recording rather than delayed task execution.
   - A sequence cannot prove true concurrency.
   - Equal timestamps cannot establish “before” or “after.”
   - The log does not state whether a missing emergency attribute means “not emergency” or “attribute not captured.”

8. **Resource limitations**
   - The visible `resource` does not prove who legally authorized an action.
   - Shared service accounts can hide the true performer.
   - The checks do not detect all organizational conflicts of interest, only the two explicit inequalities in R9.
   - T6 uses the same resource for escalation and EDD, but no stated rule prohibits that; R8 prohibits activities, not reuse of a performer.

9. **Temporal-profile limitations**
   - A profile is descriptive, not a policy exception.
   - One case cannot establish the population mean or standard deviation.
   - Pending clarification cycles can be right-censored observations.
   - Zero or near-zero standard deviations require special handling.
   - Calendar hours, operational hours, and time zones can change elapsed-time results.
   - A long activation-to-payment interval may be operationally unusual but remains conformant to R12.

10. **Support/confidence metadata is not normative truth**
    - `support=1.0` here documents that a rule was deliberately installed from policy.
    - It does not estimate the probability that the rule is correct.
    - `confidence` does not convert an advisory temporal expectation into a hard constraint.
    - High metadata values must not be used to vote away a contradictory hard-control finding.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

1. **Canonical procedural representation**
   - Deploy the legal-first accepting Petri net as `procedural_model`.
   - Version it explicitly, for example `vendor-onboarding-legal-first-v1`.
   - Do not add an emergency branch to this model under the same version.
   - Keep post-activation maintenance outside the base case lifecycle until reinstatement semantics are specified.

2. **Authoritative policy layer**
   - Use the procedural model for mandatory backbone, route, terminal, and selected bounded clarification/control-flow obligations.
   - Use the resource/data layer as authoritative for R9, conditional R2 interpretation, R6, and occurrence-sensitive R11.
   - Use DECLARE and Log Skeleton primarily as independent diagnostics and exploratory conformance views.
   - Use temporal profiles as warnings only.

3. **Conformance-check order**
   1. Validate schema, case identity, timestamps, event order, and resource availability.
   2. Resolve the active policy version, especially whether an emergency exception exists.
   3. Run procedural conformance and identify the first unreachable or contradictory control-flow state.
   4. Run terminal/route, resource, and emergency-policy checks.
   5. Run R11 occurrence checks and classify unresolved future obligations as pending.
   6. Run DECLARE and Log Skeleton diagnostics.
   7. Compute temporal-profile warnings.
   8. Aggregate findings without allowing warnings or high-confidence metadata to override hard failures.

4. **Handling of missing evidence**
   - Missing resource data should block an automatic SoD pass.
   - Missing emergency authorization should surface as `policy-dependent` in an exception policy and fail closed for automatic activation admission.
   - Missing timestamp data should make the affected temporal/deadline check `not evaluated`, not silently use ingestion time.

5. **Contradictory findings**
   - Present the rule ID, policy version, authoritative source, actual event/resource evidence, expected condition, and severity.
   - For T6, show both “legal opinion missing before activation” and “candidate emergency authorization unavailable”; do not present the latter as proof of authorization.
   - Never resolve a conflict merely because more layers report a violation.
   - Never remove conflicting events to make a filtered log conform.
   - For live cases, distinguish `fail` from `pending`: an already-prohibited final decision is a failure, while an obligation that may still be satisfied is pending.
