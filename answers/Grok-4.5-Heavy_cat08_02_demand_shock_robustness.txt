**1. Stress scenarios**

Five scenarios are defined that vary case-mix composition and disruption type (not merely total volume). They are grounded in the observed normal-to-peak differences in case mix, DFG flow ratios, performance edges, utilizations, and variant behaviors.

**S1 – Sustained Urgent + Complex mix shift (winter infection wave)**  
Weekly volume ≈ 1.4× normal, mix ≈ 40 % Routine / 30 % Urgent / 22 % Complex-panel / 8 % Repeat-after-failure. Equipment and core staffing nominal. Matches the supplied peak-period summary and DFG. Complex-panel cases are 3.1× more likely to require medical validation and 2.4× more likely to enter specialist hold; Urgent cases retain the 18 h contractual penalty.

**S2 – Assay-machine degradation or partial outage**  
M2 experiences elevated failure rate or is partially unavailable, concurrent with elevated volume and mild-to-moderate mix shift. Amplifies the documented clustering of repeats after night shifts and M2 usage. Assay → Repeat already rises from ≈ 7 % (normal) to ≈ 17 % (peak DFG).

**S3 – Medical-validator or specialist-review absence**  
Specialist unavailable (recurring every-second-Friday pattern or unplanned) plus reduced medical-validator capacity, during elevated Complex/Urgent mix. Specialist-review median queue already reaches 36 h and Hold → Medical edge 48 h in the peak data.

**S4 – Courier arrival compression or multi-hour delay**  
Route arrivals compressed into tighter windows around the existing 10:00/17:00 spikes, or delayed by several hours, while volume is elevated. Courier intake already reaches 88 % utilization and 7.5 h median queue in peak; spikes load registration and pre-analytical check directly and threaten Urgent 18 h SLAs.

**S5 – Combined cascade stress**  
S1 mix + elevated pre-analytical defect rate + accession-specialist pressure (only two specialists). Tests multi-station interaction and amplification of recollection loops.

**2. Bottleneck-migration analysis**

**Baseline peak cascade (primarily S1 and elements of S5)**  
Courier/registration spikes feed Pre-analytical check (96 % util, 9.5 h median queue). Defect rate roughly doubles (Request recollection ≈ 4.2 % → ≈ 8 %), generating long recollection loops (Request → Receive median 42 h → 60 h) that re-consume registration-clerk time explaining errors to physicians and reduce net intake capacity. Surviving cases hit Accession correction (only two specialists, 14 h queue) or proceed to Test preparation (89 % util, 11 h queue). Batch waits rise sharply (Test prep → Batch frequency 760 → 1180; Batch → Assay median 9 h → 22 h). Assay machines reach 98 % util / 18 h queue; Urgent bypass of batching falls from 74 % to 58 %, increasing schedule fragmentation. Assay → Technical validation lengthens (5 h → 15 h) and Technical validators hit 101 % util / 16 h queue. A higher fraction routes to Medical validation or Hold for specialist review; Medical validators reach 93 % / 22 h and Specialist 87 % / 36 h. Result: overall rework 11.8 %, cases with ≥1 queue >12 h = 54 %, Complex and Repeat p90 cycle times 210 h / 240 h.

**Migration under relief attempts**  
- Early-station relief (I6 early triage, I7 smoothing, partial I8 automation) increases throughput into Prep / Assay / Tech-val stations already at 89–101 %. Consequence: longer batch waits, weaker Urgent bypass effectiveness, higher machine contention, and migration of queue into technical and medical validation. Peak DFG already shows elevated relative flow into Batch, Repeat and Tech → Med.  
- Pure Urgent-first dispatch (I1) shortens the Urgent path but starves Routine (which tolerate batching) and Complex-panel (multi-assay + medical). WIP accumulates at preparation; assay schedules fragment further; Complex cases arrive later at the already long specialist hold. Simultaneous prioritization of all case types is impossible.  
- Larger fixed batches (I2) improve Routine setup efficiency but lengthen waits for any Urgent that lose bypass privilege under congestion. Downstream assay and validation remain saturated.  
- Dedicating M1 to Urgent/Complex (I3) protects critical cases yet routes Routine volume onto higher-failure M2. Variant behavior shows repeats cluster after M2; each repeat consumes assay capacity twice and feeds additional load into Technical validation (already 101 %). Rework rises.  
- Cross-training Technical validators into Accession (I4) clears the 14 h accession queue and unblocks many cases, but reduces available Technical-validation capacity. Queue migrates from Accession into Technical validation and onward into Medical/Hold. Safe only under explicit utilization guardrails.  
- Evening medical-validation shift (I5) drains Medical and Hold queues but, if upstream Assay/Tech remain congested, merely accelerates release of already-delayed cases or leaves the separate Specialist resource as the new choke.  
- Outsourcing Complex overflow (I10) relieves Specialist and Medical load for retained cases yet inserts a fixed 12 h transport delay plus traceability overhead; net effect can be negative for time-sensitive Complex or if partner capacity is constrained.

**Scenario-specific notes**  
- S2: Repeats surge → double capacity consumption on remaining machines → Technical validation overload → spill into Medical/Hold even for non-Complex cases. Priority rules become harder to honor without starving Routine.  
- S3: Specialist Hold and Medical become absolute bottlenecks; upstream relief simply piles more cases into the scarce medical/specialist pool. Limited I10 becomes critical; uncontrolled upstream acceleration worsens the pile-up.  
- S4: Intake and Pre-analytical absorb the surge first; recollection loops amplify because clerks are busy with arrival peaks; late-arriving Urgent samples immediately threaten the 18 h SLA and force reactive priority that starves other types.  
- S5: Accession and Pre-analytical interact multiplicatively; each unblocked accession case that later fails pre-check re-enters the loop, consuming the scarce specialist capacity twice.

These sequences demonstrate why single-station optimization or average-cycle-time focus fails under the observed mix shifts and resource interactions.

**3. Robust redesign package**

The package reduces variance and early defects, protects scarce multi-skill resources, and adds flexible rather than permanent capacity. It deliberately avoids pure prioritization or pure batch-size changes that migrate bottlenecks or starve case types. It is grounded in the DFG frequency shifts, performance edges, utilization spikes, and variant behaviors.

**Core always-on / baseline elements**  
- **Early pre-analytical defect triage (I6)**: Rapid triage immediately after receipt rejects or flags poor-quality samples within 2 h. Targets the observed rise in pre-analytical → request recollection (≈ 4 % → 8 %) and the long recollection loops (42 h / 60 h). Cuts loops early, frees registration clerks from repeated physician explanations, reduces overall rework, and prevents defective cases from consuming prep, assay and validation capacity. Downstream overload risk is low if triage criteria are accurate; main risk is excessive early cancels. Assumption: reliable visual/metadata criteria can be defined quickly. Failure mode: high false-reject rate or physician resistance.  
- **Courier arrival smoothing (I7)**: Stagger or reschedule pickups to dampen the documented 10:00 / 17:00 spikes. Reduces courier-intake median queue (1.0 h → 7.5 h) and the subsequent pre-analytical surge. Stabilizes flow under both volume and mix shifts. Assumption: courier partners can adjust routes. Failure: rigid clinic collection times.  
- **Selective registration automation (partial I8)**: Automate registration for orders arriving with complete digital metadata (target ≥ 40–50 %). Frees clerks for exception handling and recollection coordination. Paired with triage and smoothing so that downstream stations are not suddenly flooded. Assumption: sufficient digital completeness. Failure: poor metadata quality simply moves errors to accession or pre-analytical.  
- **Managed cross-training for accession (modified I4)**: Cross-train two technical validators for accession corrections (4-week training). Deployment rule: only when accession median queue > 6 h AND technical-validation utilization < 90 %. Protects the two dedicated accession specialists (peak util 91 %, queue 14 h) whose work unblocks many cases, without depleting the already overloaded technical validators (peak 101 %). Assumption: training succeeds and dual-skill staff remain available. Failure: skill decay, burnout, or simultaneous high load forcing a choice.

**Flexible / peak-ready elements**  
- **Dynamic priority-aware batching (replaces pure I1 and pure I2)**: Default batch size 12–16. Larger Routine-only batches (up to 24) allowed only when Urgent and Complex queues are below thresholds. Urgent and Complex use priority lanes or small dedicated batches; a minimum capacity floor (≈ 25–30 %) is reserved for Routine so they are never fully starved. Preserves batching efficiency for Routine while protecting Urgent SLAs and Complex needs, and reduces the fragmentation observed when Urgent bypass rates fall (74 % → 58 %).  
- **Temporary evening medical-validation shift (I5)**: Pre-arranged overtime or temporary staff. Activated under mix-shift or absence triggers. Directly addresses the medical-validation queue explosion (2 h → 22 h) driven by higher Urgent and Complex volumes.  
- **Selective Complex-panel outsourcing (limited I10)**: Partner-lab overflow activated only when specialist-review queue exceeds 24 h. Accepts the 12 h transport delay in exchange for protecting the scarce specialist resource (peak queue 36 h). Requires strict traceability protocol. Not used for pure Urgent cases.

**Why robust**  
Targets the primary sources of instability visible in the data—arrival variance, early defect loops, scarce specialist skills (accession, medical, specialist review), and mix-driven load on validation—rather than optimizing historical average cycle time or adding undifferentiated capacity. Dynamic rules and activation thresholds prevent permanent over-commitment; explicit capacity floors stop starvation of any single case type. Pairing upstream relief with downstream flex prevents classic bottleneck migration.  

**Key assumptions**  
Training completes successfully; courier and partner-lab cooperation; temporary staff/overtime quality maintained; digital metadata quality sufficient; real-time queue/utilization monitoring available.  

**Where it may fail**  
Simultaneous multi-station failures (e.g., M2 outage + specialist absence + courier collapse) exceed flexible capacity; training lag or dual-skill burnout; partner-lab quality or transport failures; unreliable triage criteria.  

Explicitly not adopted as primary: pure I1 (starves Routine/Complex), pure I2 (delays Urgent), pure I3 (routes Routine onto higher-failure M2 → more repeats), pure I9 (adds waiting for Routine).

**4. Scenario-specific policies**

Policies activate and deactivate on measurable triggers so the process does not remain in high-cost mode.

**P1 – Mix-shift / high Urgent+Complex mode**  
Triggers ON: Urgent share > 25 % or Complex share > 18 % sustained ≥ 2 days, OR medical-validation median queue > 12 h, OR specialist-review median queue > 18 h.  
Actions: Activate I5 evening medical shift; tighten priority for Urgent/Complex in preparation and assay (still respecting Routine capacity floor); lower I10 threshold (specialist queue > 18 h).  
Triggers OFF: All three metrics below thresholds for 48 consecutive hours.

**P2 – Assay degradation / high-repeat mode**  
Triggers ON: Assay-machine utilization > 95 % AND repeat rate > 12 %, OR confirmed M2 degradation/outage, OR batch-wait median > 15 h with rising repeats.  
Actions: Prefer remaining capacity on M1 for Urgent and Complex; enlarge Routine batches only if they clear without further fragmenting M1; accelerate technical validation of repeats; temporarily pause large-batch experiments; intensify monitoring of night-shift and M2-linked repeats.  
Triggers OFF: Utilization < 90 % and repeat rate < 8 % for 48 h.

**P3 – Validator / specialist absence mode**  
Triggers ON: Specialist unavailable or medical-validator available FTE such that projected utilization > 95 %, especially under elevated Complex/Urgent mix.  
Actions: Immediate activation of I5 if not already on; early trigger of limited I10 for Complex-panel cases; apply managed I4 rule carefully if dual-trained staff exist; hold pure Routine cases that would otherwise enter specialist review if clinically acceptable.  
Triggers OFF: Full staffing restored and queues trending down for 24–48 h.

**P4 – Courier disruption mode**  
Triggers ON: Courier-intake median queue > 4 h, OR arrival variance exceeds historical peak spikes (both major arrivals compressed into ≤ 2 h windows), OR multi-hour route delays affecting > 20 % of expected samples.  
Actions: Enforce maximum I7 smoothing; temporary overtime at registration and pre-analytical; accelerate I6 triage on arriving backlog; open a separate fast lane for known Urgent samples at intake if feasible.  
Triggers OFF: Intake queue < 2 h and arrivals re-stabilized for 48 h.

**Cross-cutting managed-I4 rule**  
Continuously evaluated; deploys cross-trained staff to accession only under the dual condition (accession queue > 6 h AND tech-val util < 90 %). Overrides only under extreme P3 if accession is completely blocked.

These policies ensure interventions that can overload downstream stations are used only when the targeted bottleneck is active and are withdrawn promptly.

**6. Residual vulnerabilities**

- Simultaneous multi-resource disruption remains fragile: e.g., M2 outage (S2) + specialist absence (S3) + courier delay (S4) can still cascade beyond the flexible capacity of I5/I10 and dynamic rules because assay and specialist redundancy is limited.  
- Night-shift / M2-linked repeat clustering is mitigated but not eliminated; dynamic rules help but do not fix root machine reliability.  
- **Redesign-induced vulnerability**: Trigger complexity, dual-role deployment (mod I4), and outsourcing (I10) increase coordination and traceability overhead. During high-stress peaks this raises the risk of rule mis-application, handoff errors, or data-quality issues that did not exist in the simpler baseline process.  
- Physician push-back on early rejects (I6) or courier changes (I7) could erode compliance.  
- Training lag: the 4-week cross-train means managed I4 is unavailable for the first peak after decision.  
- Digital metadata quality assumed sufficient for I8; if not, automation may increase manual accession corrections downstream.

**7. Minimum monitoring dashboard**

**Leading indicators (daily / shift-level during peaks)**  
- Case-mix percentages (Urgent, Complex): warning if Urgent > 25 % or Complex > 18 % sustained.  
- Pre-analytical recollect rate: target < 5 %, warning > 7 %, critical > 9 %.  
- Median queues: Intake/Courier > 3 h warn; Pre-anal > 4 h; Accession > 6 h (triggers mod I4); Prep > 6 h; Assay > 8 h; Tech-val > 6 h; Med-val > 8 h; Specialist-review > 12 h (triggers I10 consideration).  
- Arrival variance: hourly receipt standard deviation or peak-hour concentration (e.g., > 40 % of daily volume in a 2 h window).  
- Assay repeat rate (especially post-M2 / night): warning > 10 %, critical > 15 %.  
- Tech-val available FTE after any cross-train deployment; dual-skill utilization.  
- Specialist availability (binary or hours available).  
- Percentage of orders auto-registered (I8 uptake).

**Lagging outcome metrics (track but do not drive daily decisions alone)**  
- Cycle-time medians and p90 by case type (especially Urgent > 18 h breaches, Routine > 72 h).  
- Overall rework percentage.  
- Percentage of cases with any queue > 12 h.  
- SLA penalty count / release delays.  
- Cancel rate after recollection request.

Thresholds are calibrated to the normal-versus-peak baselines supplied; qualitative green/amber/red bands support rapid operational huddles. Leading indicators are prioritized so that policies activate before cycle times fully degrade.
