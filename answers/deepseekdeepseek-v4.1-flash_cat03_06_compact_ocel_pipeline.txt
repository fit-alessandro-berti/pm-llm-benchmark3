## 1. Modeling assumptions

- Object types and capitalization: `Order`, `Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, `Payment`.
- Objects: `O100`, `O200` as `Order`; `I1`, `I2`, `I3` as `Item`; `P1`, `P2` as `Package`; `D1`, `D2` as `Delivery`; `V1` as `Invoice`; `M1`, `M2` as `Payment`.
- E2O qualifier vocabulary:
  - Order lifecycle: `created`, `item_added_to`, `reservation_shared_by`, `packed_for_order`, `dispatched_for_order`, `billed_order`, `delivered_for_order`, `order_closed`
  - Item lifecycle: `item_added`, `stock_reserved`, `item_packed`
  - Package lifecycle: `package_packed`, `package_dispatched`
  - Delivery lifecycle: `dispatch_delivery`, `delivery_confirmed`
  - Invoice lifecycle: `invoice_issued`, `payment_against`
  - Payment lifecycle: `payment_recorded`
- O2O direction convention: source object  target object.
  - `Order contains Item`
  - `Package packs Item`
  - `Delivery carries Package`
  - `Invoice bills Order`
  - `Payment partially_settles Invoice`
- Logistics and financial direct Order/Item relations:
  - We use a hybrid policy. Packing events relate directly to the packed Items, Packages, and the Order(s) being fulfilled. Dispatch and delivery confirmation events relate directly to Packages/Deliveries and to the Order(s) operationally served, but not to Items after packing. Issue Invoice relates to the Invoice and both billed Orders. Record Payment relates only to the Payment object and the Invoice; it does not directly relate to Orders.
- Preventing propagation/source confusion:
  - Structural facts are stored only in `o2o` using explicit containment/carrying/billing/settlement qualifiers.
  - E2O qualifiers such as `packed_for_order`, `dispatched_for_order`, and `delivered_for_order` denote explicit operational scope, not replacement structural ownership. Any ItemOrder or PackageOrder inference must be read through `o2o`, not treated as a direct source-system foreign key.
- Version-dependent PM4Py assumption:
  - The constructor line `OCEL(events=events, objects=objects, relations=relations, o2o=o2o)` is assumed available. If a PM4Py version uses a different OCEL constructor/API, adjust only that construction line and the corresponding OCEL function names, not the underlying DataFrames or semantics.

- `Confirm Delivery`  `Close Order`

Misleading interpretations:
- Shared events are duplicated across Order traces, so `O100` and `O200` appear to have separate packing, dispatch, and confirmation steps even when the same event/package/delivery is involved.
- The flattened Order view hides that `P1` and `V1` are cross-order objects, making convergence look like parallel duplicated work.

### Package flattening

Approximate trace shapes:
- `P1`: `Pack Package`  `Dispatch Package`
- `P2`: `Pack Package`  `Dispatch Package`

Copied shared events:
- `P1` packing is shared with `O100`, `O200`, `I1`, and `I3`, but Package flattening sees one event only.

Omitted lifecycle aspects:
- Delivery confirmation is not directly attached to Packages in this model.
- Invoice and payment lifecycles are absent.
- Item identities inside the package are not visible.

Likely DFG edges:
- `Pack Package`  `Dispatch Package`

Misleading interpretations:
- The Package appears to end at dispatch. It does not show delivery confirmation, order settlement, or item content.

### Delivery flattening

Approximate trace shapes:
- `D1`: `Dispatch Package`  `Confirm Delivery`
- `D2`: `Dispatch Package`  `Confirm Delivery`

Copied shared events:
- `D1` dispatch is shared with `P1`, `O100`, and `O200`; Delivery flattening collapses this to one activity occurrence.

Omitted lifecycle aspects:
- Packing, item contents, order billing, and payments are absent.
- The carried Package identity is not visible in the flattened activity sequence.

Likely DFG edges:
- `Dispatch Package`  `Confirm Delivery`

Misleading interpretations:
- Deliveries look like simple dispatch-confirm objects, even though `D1` carries a cross-order package `P1` and `D2` carries `P2`.

### Invoice flattening

Approximate trace shape:
- `V1`: `Issue Invoice`  `Record Payment`  `Record Payment`

Copied shared events:
- `Issue Invoice` is shared with `O100` and `O200`, but Invoice flattening sees one invoice object.

Omitted lifecycle aspects:
- Orders, items, packages, deliveries, and payment allocation details are absent from the activity sequence.
- `M1` and `M2` identities are lost unless event attributes are retained.

Likely DFG edges:
- `Issue Invoice`  `Record Payment`
- `Record Payment`  `Record Payment`

Misleading interpretations:
- The two `Record Payment` events look like an unbounded loop or repeated activity, not two partial payments by distinct Payment objects.
- The total paid amount and remaining balance are not visible unless attributes are aggregated.

### Why flattening on Payment is weak

Payment flattening produces one trace per Payment object:
- `M1`: `Record Payment`
- `M2`: `Record Payment`

There are no sequence edges, no Invoice context, and no Order context. The DFG is just a single activity. This view cannot analyze settlement behavior, partial payment allocation, or invoice closure; it only confirms that each Payment object participated in exactly one event.

## 5. Expected object-centric structures

### OC-DFG

The OC-DFG should show activity nodes connected through shared object types:
- `Create Order` connects to `Order`.
- `Add Item` connects to `Order` and `Item`.
- `Reserve Stock` connects to `Order` and `Item`, with `O100`, `O200`, `I1`, and `I3` creating convergence.
- `Pack Package` connects to `Order`, `Item`, and `Package`; `P1` bridges `O100`, `O200`, `I1`, and `I3`.
- `Dispatch Package` connects to `Order`, `Package`, and `Delivery`; `P1` and `D1` bridge both Orders.
- `Issue Invoice` connects to `Order` and `Invoice`; `V1` bridges `O100` and `O200`.
- `Record Payment` connects to `Invoice` and `Payment`; `V1` connects separately to `M1` and `M2`.
- `Confirm Delivery` connects to `Order` and `Delivery`.
- `Close Order` connects to `Order`.

Expected insight: the process is not a single case notion. `P1` and `V1` are cross-order convergence objects, while `M1` and `M2` are financial leaves attached to `V1`.

### OTG

The object type graph should contain edges roughly like:
- `Order`  `Item`
- `Order`  `Package`
- `Order`  `Delivery`
- `Package`  `Item`
- `Package`  `Delivery`
- `Order`  `Invoice`
- `Invoice`  `Payment`

Expected insight: `Invoice` is the financial bridge between fulfillment objects (`Order`) and settlement objects (`Payment`). `Package` is the physical bridge between `Item` and `Delivery`.

### ET–OT

The event-type/object-type graph should show:
- `Pack Package` touching `Order`, `Item`, and `Package`.
- `Dispatch Package` touching `Order`, `Package`, and `Delivery`.
- `Record Payment` touching `Invoice` and `Payment`.
- `Reserve Stock` touching `Order` and `Item`.

Expected insight: multi-object events such as `Pack Package` and `Dispatch Package` are the main sources of convergence and divergence. They are exactly where flattened per-object views become misleading.

### Object-centric Petri net

The object-centric Petri net should contain places for object types and transitions for activities, with tokens corresponding to `O100`, `O200`, `I1`, `I2`, `I3`, `P1`, `P2`, `D1`, `D2`, `V1`, `M1`, and `M2`.

Expected interpretation:
- `Reserve Stock` consumes/produces tokens across `O100`, `O200`, `I1`, and `I3`.
- `Pack Package` for `P1` involves `I1`, `I3`, `O100`, and `O200`.
- `Dispatch Package` for `D1` involves `P1`, `O100`, and `O200`.
- `Issue Invoice` for `V1` involves `O100` and `O200`.
- `Record Payment` transitions connect `M1`/`M2` to `V1`.

This Petri net should not be treated as having one uniquely determined interpretation. Different token-flow policies, especially for shared events, can yield different valid object-centric executions.

### Object-interactions summary

Expected high-level interactions:
- `Order`–`Item`: frequent because of Add Item, Reserve Stock, and Pack Package.
- `Order`–`Package`: from Pack and Dispatch.
- `Order`–`Delivery`: from Dispatch and Confirm Delivery.
- `Package`–`Delivery`: from Dispatch.
- `Package`–`Item`: from Pack.
- `Invoice`–`Order`: from Issue Invoice.
- `Invoice`–`Payment`: from Record Payment.

Specific interactions:
- `P1` interacts with `I1`, `I3`, `O100`, and `O200` during `Pack Package`, and with `D1` during `Dispatch Package`.
- `V1` interacts with `O100` and `O200` during `Issue Invoice`, and with `M1` and `M2` during `Record Payment`.
- `O100` and `O200` co-occur in `Reserve Stock`, `Pack Package`, `Dispatch Package`, `Issue Invoice`, and `Confirm Delivery`.
- `M1` and `M2` do not interact directly; they interact only through `V1`.

## 6. Modeling alternatives

1. Source-minimal relations:
   - Only relate events to objects explicitly named in the event sentence.
   - Example: `Dispatch Package P1 in D1` relates only to `P1` and `D1`; no direct `O100`/`O200` links.
   - Effect: Order flattening becomes shorter and omits dispatch/confirmation unless O2O propagation is added manually. OC-DFG still shows Package–Delivery interactions, but Order–Delivery edges weaken.

2. Full operational involvement:
   - Relate downstream events to all objects operationally affected by them, including Orders and Items reachable through O2O.
   - Example: `Confirm Delivery D1` relates to `D1`, `P1`, `I1`, `I3`, `O100`, and `O200`.
   - Effect: flattened traces become richer but heavily duplicated. Relation inflation can make every event appear to touch many object types, obscuring true source-system links.

3. O2O-propagated analytical relations:
   - Keep E2O source-minimal and compute derived relations by traversing `o2o` for analysis.
   - Example: an Order-level view is built by starting from Order objects and following `contains`, `packs`, `carries`, `bills`, and `partially_settles`.
   - Effect: source data stays clean, but standard PM4Py flattening may not use O2O propagation automatically. Results depend on the propagation policy and can vary between analytics.

4. Qualifier-separated hybrid relations:
   - Keep structural O2O authoritative and use E2O qualifiers to distinguish source roles from operational scope.
   - Example: `packed_for_order` is an operational E2O relation, while `contains` is the structural O2O relation.
   - Effect: preserves richness while making provenance inspectable. The trade-off is a larger qualifier vocabulary and more responsibility on the analyst to respect qualifier semantics.

## 7. Failure modes

1. Relation inflation:
   - Relating every downstream event to every reachable Order and Item creates dense relation tables where object-centric discovery overstates coupling. For example, adding `I1`, `I2`, `I3`, `O100`, and `O200` to all logistics events would hide the fact that only `P1` truly combines two Orders.

2. Missing direct E2O links:
   - If `Reserve Stock` is linked only to Items and not Orders, Order flattening loses the shared reservation point. If it is linked only to Orders and not Items, Item-level analysis loses stock reservation.

3. Treating O2O propagation as observation:
   - Inferring `O100 contains I2` is structural, but inferring `O100 participated in Dispatch D2` from `O100 contains I2` and `P2 packs I2` and `D2 carries P2` is analytical. Mistaking that inference for a directly recorded event relation can create false provenance.

4. Duplicate shared events after flattening:
   - `e6`, `e7`, `e9`, `e11`, and `e13` are shared across multiple Orders. Flattening by Order duplicates these events in multiple traces, making it look like separate work occurred for each Order.

5. Ambiguous payment allocation:
   - `M1` and `M2` both settle `V1`, but the process description does not state how much of `V1` each Order owes. Any Order-level payment allocation is an assumption, not a fact in the data.

6. Overly short Payment traces:
   - Payment flattening yields one-event traces per Payment object. This is too short for sequence discovery and can be mistaken for a trivial process rather than an artifact of object granularity.

7. Constructor/API version mismatch:
   - Different PM4Py versions may expect different OCEL constructor parameters, object column names, or discovery function names. Changing the constructor line is acceptable, but changing the semantics of `events`, `objects`, `relations`, or `o2o` would invalidate the model.

8. False total order for same-time events:
   - Although this example has no timestamp ties, same-timestamp events should not be treated as having a meaningful total order unless a deterministic tie-breaker such as `ocel:eid` is specified. Otherwise DFG edges can depend on arbitrary sort order.

9. Timezone-naive timestamps:
   - Mixing timezone-aware and timezone-naive timestamps breaks merge consistency and can reorder events across UTC boundaries. All timestamps here are UTC-aware.

10. Orphan relations:
    - A relation row pointing to a missing event ID or missing object ID breaks referential integrity and makes OCEL construction unreliable.

11. Activity/timestamp drift:
    - If `relations` repeats an activity or timestamp that differs from `events`, flattening and object-centric discovery can disagree about the same event.

12. Wrong object-type capitalization:
    - PM4Py object-type filters are string-based. `Order` vs `order` would create separate object types and break flattening by `Order`.
