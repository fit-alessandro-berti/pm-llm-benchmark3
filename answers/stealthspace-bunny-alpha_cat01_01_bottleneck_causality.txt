## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Verdict: `mainly confounded`; confidence: 88%**—in that classification, not in a harmful Orion effect.

The crude Orion mean is 21.96 hours versus Nova’s 15.24 hours, a difference of 6.73 hours. However, Orion is much more often high-complexity (42.9% versus 12.5%), night-shift (92.9% versus 25%), and compliance-required (64.3% versus 25%). Those are strong arguments against reading the aggregate comparison causally.

The strongest counterevidence to a simple case-mix explanation is a reversal in **every** complexity stratum: Orion averages 7.50 versus Nova’s 9.64 hours for low complexity, 17.17 versus 19.38 for medium, and 31.57 versus 35.00 for high. Orion also has slightly shorter observed review service within each stratum.

Nevertheless, this neither identifies a benefit nor eliminates residual confounding. Most Orion observations are night/high-load, there is no well-populated three-way complexity–shift–group overlap, and group represents a bundle of reviewer composition, shift coverage, and operating conditions. Waiting, compliance, and rework are candidate mediators; completed-case selection could distort the contrast in either direction.

A 50% transfer might ease queues and redistribute difficult work. Without eligibility and capacity controls, however, it could overload Nova’s compliance or review steps, increase rework or abandonment, or worsen quality. Use a small, randomized, complexity-stratified routing pilot and a separate capacity test, with all-case completion and quality outcomes, rather than an immediate quota.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

These hypotheses are nonexclusive. Rank reflects evidential priority, not an estimated causal probability.

| rank | hypothesis | process mechanism | evidence supporting it | evidence contradicting it | discriminating analysis or falsification test | predicted observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Routing and measured case mix mainly confound the aggregate contrast** | Difficulty determines assignment and independently increases work, compliance, and waiting | Orion has much more high-complexity, night, high-load, and compliance-required work; overall mean reverses after complexity standardization | Orion remains faster within every measured complexity stratum, so measured complexity alone is not a complete explanation | In a prospective cohort, perform exact or regularized propensity/entropy balancing on preassignment complexity, risk, shift, load, compliance, and documented eligibility. Diagnose overlap first, then estimate a covariate-adjusted contrast and sensitivity bounds for residual risk | Balanced, well-supported comparisons with an Orion–Nova difference near zero would favor mixing; a persistent positive or negative residual contrast would weaken this explanation. Neither result alone proves causality |
| 2 | **Orion has a genuine harmful queue or coordination effect** | Night coverage, incoming work, or handoffs consume review-readiness time even after controlling case complexity | Orion’s crude wait is 5.14 hours longer, and waiting dominates the crude timing margin; difficult workflows have several possible handoffs | Orion waits less within every complexity stratum, with only slightly—not dramatically—lower service; observed cases do not identify a coordination delay | Reconstruct queue states from timestamps, reviewer roster, concurrent workload, and arrivals. Compare eligible cases at matched shift/load and use randomized capacity changes. Estimate total assignment effects separately from queue-mediated effects | A reproducible excess Orion delay within supported operating conditions, reduced by extra capacity, would favor harm. | 3 | **Orion’s reviewer capacity is beneficial but masked by case mix** | More certified staffing or reviewer composition enables faster, equally reliable review of difficult cases | Orion is faster within strata and has slightly shorter service within every stratum; rework is not higher within strata | Individual reviewers and proficiency are unobserved; night burden and completion selection may hide harm; quality equivalence is not established | Compare within route and shift where both groups are eligible, with reviewer-level proficiency/certification measures. ## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Repair measurement and establish baseline | Reconcile aggregate discrepancies; introduce prospective intake, routing-decision, event, reviewer, and completion-status logging without changing routing | Selection, queue/service separation, and completion selection | None initially; all newly arriving cases included | No displacement of work; document unknown fields rather than imputing operational facts | Classification-to-routing delay; queue-state intervals; compliance and review intervals; repeated reviews; open/abandoned/closed cases; reason codes | Establishes real support, identifies measurement bottlenecks, and supplies an uncontaminated baseline cohort | Do not begin a causal pilot if case eligibility, completion status, or routing decisions cannot be recorded |
| 1. Capacity-first pilot | Randomize or step in protected compliance/review capacity or queue-drain support while retaining normal group assignment | Waiting and compliance throughput rather than the resource-group label | Cluster-day, work session, or eligible batch to limit interference | Capacity must not simply be removed from another queue; maintain documented qualifications and coverage floors | Queue before first review and around compliance separately; service by review instance; rework; compliance completion; all-case closure and abandonment | A randomized capacity manipulation provides stronger evidence for a queue mechanism than observational correlations. It does not automatically identify a formal front-door direct effect | Stop or reverse the added-capacity step if it worsens untreated queues, service reliability, completion, or prespecified quality outcomes |
| 2. Small routing experiment | Randomize a small, power-calculated share of demonstrably eligible cases to Orion or Nova, stratified by complexity and balanced by shift/load | Total resource-group effect and any group-specific operational disadvantage | Case where routing is independent; otherwise cluster/session to account for queue interference | Do not route unsupported complexity to a group without verified qualification; monitor spillovers to untreated work; retain fallback staffing | All-case time to close, completion probability, queue, service, rework, compliance, reviewer, and independently measured quality | Intention-to-treat comparison estimates the operational T effect under the pilot system. Assignment may inform actual receipt only after compliance and interference checks | Predefine stopping thresholds for material queue displacement, abandonment, appeals/audit failures, or unsupported case complexity |
| 3. Prudent replication | Expand only after an interim causal and safety review; use staged, auditable routing rules rather than a fixed half-volume quota | Confirmed, transportable operational benefit | Staged quota or eligible routing cohort, reviewed as operating conditions evolve | Recalculate overlap and staffing; prohibit selective inclusion of easy cases; publish denominator and completion status | Heterogeneous effects by complexity, shift, load, reviewer, and compliance; confidence bounds and quality noninferiority | Separates robust total benefit from subgroup averages and tests whether pilot effects survive operational implementation | Roll back if benefits disappear after balancing, harm thresholds are crossed, or effects arise only by moving easier or more complete cases |

**Final recommendation.** Do not authorize the proposed 50% reassignment on the current evidence. The aggregate disadvantage reverses after crude complexity standardization, while shift/load and completion selection remain inadequately controlled. Treat the routing rule and its staffing consequences as the exposure, not “Orion” as an intrinsic productivity trait.

First repair the aggregate audit, begin a prospective process census, and add event and reviewer timestamps. Then pilot a small, cluster-randomized share of cases for which both groups are demonstrably eligible, stratifying by complexity and balancing operating conditions. Estimate intention-to-treat effects on time to closure and completion probability for all arrivals, not completed cases alone, with prespecified quality safeguards. In parallel, randomize or step in protected compliance/review capacity to test a queue mechanism without changing the routing label.

Use favorable pilot results—persistent cycle-time benefit, no displacement of untreated work, and no quality or completion harm—to justify gradual, monitored replication. If benefits disappear with balanced case mix, or Nova queues, rework, or appeals rise, stop expansion. This design can distinguish an Orion effect from routing mix and identify actionable waiting constraints without betting half the future portfolio on a reversed descriptive comparison.

def discover_group_dfg(group_name):
    traces = ",".join(
        df.loc[df["group"] == group_name, "trace"].tolist()
    )
    log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(
        traces, sep=">", noise=1.0
    )
    if not log or "concept:name" not in log[0][0]:
        raise RuntimeError("Unexpected PM4Py parse_event_log_string output")

    # Frequency-based DFG only: no event timestamps are read or supplied.
    discovered = pm4py.discover_dfg(
        log,
        variant="parallel",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
    )
    dfg = discovered[0]
    count_column = "count" if "count" in dfg.columns else "frequency"
    normalized = (
        dfg.groupby(level=[0, 1])[count_column]
           .sum(min_count=1)
           .rename("frequency")
           .reset_index()
    )
    normalized.columns = ["from", "to", "frequency"]
    normalized["frequency"] = normalized["frequency"].fillna(0).astype(int)
    return normalized


group_dfgs = {group: discover_group_dfg(group) for group in GROUPS}

for (source, target), row in direct_follows.iterrows():

            f"Nova={row['Nova']}, Orion={row['Orion']} contributing cases"
        )

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Residual case-mix confounding and lack of overlap:** the most valuable addition is a **pre-routing case-risk score**, recorded for every arrival before resource-group assignment.

2. **Differential completion selection:** the most valuable addition is a **census outcome field and time** distinguishing closed, abandoned, and still-open cases at a common follow-up horizon.

3. **Queueing versus active service:** the most valuable design change is a **timestamped process event log** linking arrival, compliance, individual review instances, reviewer identifiers, and repeated reviews.

4. **Compliance and rework mechanisms:** the most valuable additional field is **elapsed compliance-step time for each performed check**, separating execution capacity from the requirement to perform the step.

5. **Reviewer composition and bundle effects:** the most valuable design change is a **reviewer-to-roster identifier** linked to certification, experience, coverage, and concurrent assigned workload.
