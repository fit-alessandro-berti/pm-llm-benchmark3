1. Disruption timeline
T0  (Day -30  -1)  Baseline  
• All systems nominal; 91 % of traces fit normative model.  
• Median cycle times: Hosp-critical 8 h, Pharm-standard 23 h.  
• Control gaps all < 1 %.  Risk posture: controlled and auditable.

T1  (Day 0 a.m.)  Shock / blackout  
• WMS, scanning and temperature IoT offline.  
• Throughput instantly falls to near zero; no alternative flow yet.  
• Risk posture: supply risk and control risk simultaneously high.

T2  (Day 0 p.m.  Day 2)  Manual-workaround phase  
• Dominant variant: STARTEmergency triage(A/B/C)Manual inventory snapshotManual pick listManual pack(F/G)Phone PODSpreadsheet invoice hold.  
• Hospital fast lane preserves 91 % hospital fill rate, but only 44 % of traces conform.  
• Control gaps explode: traceability 9.5 %, cold-chain temp 4.8 %, CS dual-control 2.9 %.  
• Risk posture: life-saving service partly protected; compliance largely degraded and invisible until later.

T3  (Day 3  6)  Partial system return  
• Order-intake restored, but scanning & inventory sync unreliable.  
• Hybrid flows mix manual and electronic. Conformance rises to 59 %.  
• Control gaps cut roughly in half, yet still 3–5 × baseline.  
• Risk posture: still elevated; hidden debt (missing scans, reconstruction work) accumulates.

T4  (Day 7  21)  Stabilisation & remediation  
• Core systems back; backlog-prioritisation (J) and reconcile-manual-reservation (K) dominant.  
• Operational KPIs almost baseline, but 2 330 cases spend extra time in Audit-remediation.  
• Conformance 76 %. Residual risk: delayed discovery of cold-chain & authorisation gaps, audit workload.

3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance
In a disruption, true resilience preserves the mission (patient supply) while still meeting the minimum immutable controls (authorisation, chain-of-custody, temperature, dual-control).  
• True resilience: A (with B), B, E (when paired with later scan), F (for CS), I, J, K, L.  
• Concealed non-compliance: C, G. These delivered product but silently discarded required controls.  
• Ambiguous / conditional: D, H. They are resilient only for a short window or low-risk products, otherwise drift into non-compliance.  
The key difference is whether a compensating control exists, is documented, and is time-bounded.

4. Recovery process model (future incidents)

Notation:  sequence; [ ] decision; { } parallel; ( ) event.

Trigger
(Any of)  
• WMS offline > 10 min OR scanning error-rate > 20 % OR cybersecurity containment triggers.

Phase 1 – Immediate triage  
1. Receive order  [Is Hospital-critical?]   
   a. YES  Fast-lane queue  Manual authorisation (B)  Minimum controls set  
   b. NO  Standard queue

2. Minimum controls matrix  
   • Controlled-substance  dual human check + paper chain-of-custody  
   • Cold-chain  attach Temp-Log label + infrared spot check  
   • Ambient  basic quantity & customer licence verified

Phase 2 – Manual execution  
{Pick preparation}  
• If WMS down  6 h  use printed live snapshot (D)  
• If > 6 h  suspend new picks, move to Inventory-verify sub-process

Pick  Pack  Temporary Release (F)  
Dispatch  
• Offline mobile scanner writes to local SD card; if unavailable, shipment held.

Proof of Delivery  
• Offline app e-sign (CS/Cold) OR Phone POD (Ambient)

Phase 3 – Backlog & reconciliation  
Backlog-prioritisation (risk-weighted FIFO)  automated diff-report compares manual logs vs system once online  auto-generate Reconcile tasks (K).

Phase 4 – Audit-remediation (time-boxed)  
Outstanding control gaps routed to Compliance queue with 5-day SLA  if unresolved, escalate to Quality & Legal.

5. Resilience indicators

Leading (real-time)  
• Critical-order fill %  
• Outstanding Manual Authorisation queue time  
• Cold-chain Temp-Log completion rate  
• Offline-scan completion rate  
• Backlog age percentile by segment  
• Fairness index = (median CT Pharmacy ÷ median CT Hospital)

Lagging  
• Traceability exception rate  
• Dual-control missing rate  
• Rework / correction rate  
• Audit-remediation ageing (> 5 d)  
• Customer cancellation rate  
• Staff overtime hours (fatigue proxy)

6. Recommendations for future disruptions

1. Deploy offline-capable mobile scanning & temp-log printers (Med effort / High benefit / Low residual risk)  
2. Codify triage & backlog-priority rules in SOP and train twice yearly (Low / High / Low)  
3. Build “minimum-controls matrix” checklists for manual mode; laminate at each station (Low / Medium / Low)  
4. Automate inventory diff-report and reconciliation script (Med / Medium / Low)  
5. Implement time-boxed audit-remediation dashboard visible to execs (Low / Medium / Low)  
6. Phase-out informal “skip system authorisation” shortcut; lock in system so manual release needs dual sign-off (Low / High / Very low)  
7. Create surge roster of cross-trained compliance staff (High / Medium / Med)  
8. Negotiate contingency SLAs with non-critical customers to formalise “defer” expectations (Low / Medium / Low)

7. Management trade-off statement
Management should accept controlled, documented adaptations that preserve patient care while meeting the minimum immutable controls, even at the cost of speed for lower-priority orders. It should reject shortcuts—such as shipping without authorisation or scan—that provide short-term throughput but embed invisible compliance, diversion, and recall risk that surface later as audit findings or patient harm. Evidence that compensating controls are in place, time-bounded, and fully reconciled within five days is required to classify an adaptation as resilient. Absence of such evidence must trigger immediate cessation or escalation.
