## 1. Decision rule

A case can receive a swarm only before the event log records Diagnosis Complete. Before dispatch, the lead verifies that the specialists have a specific diagnostic action to take and can start early enough for a 45-minute swarm to affect the remaining SLA path. Record a latest useful start using the expected diagnosis window, deadline, and time needed for downstream resolution and validation. Check swarm history separately from alert history. A prior swarm blocks automatic dispatch and requires the exception review in section 4.

Open one actionable alert per case. A first alert can lead to immediate dispatch if the evidence and time window are credible. If the evidence is weak and there is time, confirm it at the next 30-minute refresh or through a human check. Keep an unresolved case on the worklist while it remains actionable. Issue a fresh notification only if a verified event changes the action, effect estimate, or time window, or if a comparable calibrated risk shift of at least 0.10 persists across two refreshes and changes the decision. Crossing a latest-start escalation point also warrants notification. Treat feature failures as verification tasks and check them directly when waiting would cost the opportunity to act.

For the same model version and workflow, use working calibration bands of +0.04 to +0.09 for SME and −0.12 to −0.05 for Strategic Enterprise, clipped to [0, 1]. For example, I-05 becomes 0.61–0.66 and I-08 becomes 0.31–0.38. For Public Sector use the raw prediction as the center with uncertainty appropriate to its sample size. For Non-Native-Language Support, retain the raw score as a provisional signal, widen the uncertainty in review, and seek evidence of an actionable blocker through the support channel. These bands reflect known calibration issues and are provisional proxies for untreated risk. Use them to assess urgency, interpret changes, and check whether the proposed reduction is plausible given the amount of baseline risk. A swarm’s effect must come from separate causal evidence and case review.

For discretionary starts, use a planning score of **breach value × (75% of the effect midpoint + 25% of the lower effect estimate) − €450 − the listed false-alarm burden**. The lower-bound weight is a provisional risk preference. Charging the full burden budgets for customer disruption conservatively. Update the effect estimate when the lead confirms the blocker, timing, or evidence quality. Compare feasible sets of starts by total score less the incremental harm to untreated cases and the opportunity cost of committing reserved capacity. In close decisions favor the earliest latest useful start, then the longest continuous eligible wait. A documented statutory or critical-service obligation can take precedence while the swarm remains feasible. Before each dispatch, check current queue slack and protect untreated cases whose latest safe start would be lost unless alternate coverage is arranged. Halt additional discretionary starts if congestion invalidates the planning assumptions.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

Assign one owner to each alert episode and target acknowledgment within 10 minutes. Keep the case on the worklist through 30-minute refreshes. Apply a 60-minute cool-down to duplicate pushes after acknowledgment, and continue suppressing unchanged alerts after it expires. Escalate to the duty lead if acknowledgment is overdue and the case is time-critical, if the case reaches its latest-start decision point, or if a verified event invalidates the current plan. Recheck eligibility immediately before dispatch and expire the swarm pathway at Diagnosis Complete or when too little time remains. An unstarted case can re-enter allocation when a slot frees, the congestion estimate changes, or new evidence changes its feasible action.

**I-05** is an unchanged case after repeated refreshes. Retain its original worklist age and review it in the regular allocation without a fresh push. **I-07** has a new customer log. Review the log now even though the risk fell from 0.75 to 0.72. The log could expose a tractable cause or lead directly to ordinary diagnostic work, and either outcome changes the decision. If I-07 already received a swarm, require the owner to show that the log supports a materially different action, estimate the incremental benefit of a second swarm, and obtain duty-lead approval with a fresh queue check. Apply the same second-swarm rule to any case. Repeated scores alone cannot satisfy it.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

- High breach values can crowd out lower-value cases with substantial avoidable risk. Track access and wait from first eligibility, compare similarly urgent and actionable cases, and use latest useful start and wait age to break near ties. If an access gap persists, give under-served cases with positive net benefit protected consideration in a marginal slot and audit the outcomes. Route urgent cases that miss a swarm to the normal incident escalation path.
- For Non-Native-Language Support, provide a rapid language-assisted diagnostic review with the same response target as other cases. Require the same evidence of a tractable blocker. Keep uncertainty visible and collect outcomes from both offered and untreated cases so sparse estimates do not become a recurring reason for exclusion.
- Audit underprediction for SME and overprediction for Strategic Enterprise by stage and time to deadline. Apply versioned corrections with uncertainty bounds, compare calibration and access across groups around the allocation margin, and review small groups manually when quantitative estimates are unstable.
- A successful swarm can turn a likely breach into a recorded non-breach. Log all eligible decisions, offers, actual starts, timing, and assignment probabilities where available. Preserve the intervention and queue exposure in training data. Estimate untreated calibration and treatment benefit separately so changes in which cases receive help do not masquerade as model improvement or deterioration.
- Record which untreated cases share the affected queue, how many swarms each is exposed to, and their accumulated delay and estimated extra breach risk. Break this down by segment, language-support need, and case value. When harm concentrates in a group or a shared queue, protect staffing or move discretionary starts to lower-congestion periods, and check whether the exposure gap closes.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

- **At dispatch and daily**, log feature freshness, evidence, stage and deadline, raw and corrected risk, effect assumptions, score, decision reason, alert count, acknowledgment, latest useful start, and actual treatment timing. Track duplicate pushes, missed windows, false-alarm complaints and disruption, specialist time, queue backlog, delay to untreated work, reserve use, and opportunities that expired while the reserve was held. Review available coverage before each start. A threatened untreated latest safe start or estimated aggregate displacement above 20 minutes for the next slot triggers a supervisor check and a hold on discretionary releases pending coverage or lower congestion.
- **Weekly and on mature outcome windows**, monitor missingness and feature drift, calibration-in-the-large, reliability by risk band, and Brier score by segment, stage, and remaining SLA time. Use comparable untreated cases at a defined queue exposure, with weighting or standardization where assignment is estimable. Compare those results with all-case outcomes and track shifts in who is treated, when, and at what congestion. This helps detect policy-induced drift even when overall breach rates improve.
- **Estimate effects prospectively** by randomizing offers among comparable discretionary cases near the allocation margin when both choices are acceptable. Start follow-up at eligibility and use assigned offer for the primary analysis. In comparable queue-shift blocks, vary the allowed number of discretionary starts within safe limits to estimate spillover on untreated cases and the total effect on portfolio breaches. Track crossovers, timing, queue exposure, congestion, and spillover across shifts. Estimate uncertainty at the queue or shift level. For observational estimates, use logged assignment and exposure, check overlap and sensitivity to unmeasured confounding, and report uncertainty by subgroup. Keep mandatory cases outside lotteries and report which cases the estimates apply to.
- **Review the allocation rule and fairness monthly**, with earlier review after a redesign, sustained data failure, or queue or fairness guardrail breach. Report counts and uncertainty for review, offer, treatment, wait, and spillover rates at comparable need. A sustained calibration error of five percentage points in adequately sized mature windows triggers recalibration review. Repeated negative marginal net benefit, concentrated harm, or worsening delays triggers review of the minimum net-benefit requirement, effective capacity, or reserve policy. Replay proposed changes on logged decisions where there is support for the comparison, test them prospectively under the queue guardrails, and version and roll back changes if portfolio or subgroup outcomes worsen.
