## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

I evaluate actions in two stages.

**Stage 1: mandatory-conformance filter.**  
An action that cannot produce a lawful payment execution does not earn the operational benefit merely because it is fast. Action A and Action B may appear to avoid €20k–€35k deadline loss, but they do so by violating mandatory controls. Their downside is €100k–€500k plus non-monetary regulatory, reputational, audit, and control-integrity harm. Even if the monetary ranges partially overlap at extreme values, the asymmetry and non-monetary consequences dominate. Therefore A and B are ranked last regardless of any favorable forecast.

**Stage 2: compare lawful and exception paths.**  
Among lawful paths, the key tradeoff is between P-884’s deadline value and P-901’s spillover risk.

- **C** likely gives P-884 the best lawful chance to execute within 90 minutes because S2 is immediately available and the modeled review-plus-record time is 40–90 minutes. But it transfers risk to P-901, increasing breach probability by 0.12–0.28 for another high-value case with a 70-minute deadline.
- **D composite** avoids immediate P-901 harm and uses alternate screening to reduce uncertainty. Its weakness is that S4 availability is 35–65 minutes, and second review plus recording may push completion beyond 90 minutes. Thus it has lower expected deadline value but better resource fairness and optionality.
- **E** is conformant but almost guarantees deadline loss. It is dominated by D composite because D can gather information while waiting for S4 rather than waiting 6–24 hours for customer evidence.
- **F** is safe from unlawful execution but rejects with a non-trivial false-rejection probability because the conformant approval probability is 0.55–0.75. It is not primary while a second-officer path is realistically available within the next hour.
- **G** has option value only if the exception becomes valid. Since neither director authorization nor incident identifier is currently available, it should not consume the critical window ahead of conformant second-review paths.

The ranking therefore favors a conformant second-review strategy that preserves P-901 while gathering information, with C as the authorized fallback if S4 is too late. The observational probabilities—normal-processing SLA breach 0.48–0.64, approval probability 0.55–0.75, P-901 breach increase 0.12–0.28—should not be treated as stable under intervention. Interrupting P-901, running alternate screening, and waiting for S4 are different interventions, so the ranking uses ranges, decision points, and fallbacks rather than a single expected-value calculation.

## 4. Conformance constraints

**Action A — Execute payment immediately**  
Violates mandatory rules:

- Rule 6: risk score 72 and unresolved fuzzy sanctions match require Second Officer Review before Approve or Payment Executed.
- Rule 7: first analyst and second officer must be different people; no second officer has reviewed.
- Rule 9: Payment Executed is conformant only after a valid approval path.
- Rule 10: emergency override is not enabled because no director authorization or valid incident identifier exists.

A favorable numerical forecast cannot make A acceptable. The process constraint is not a soft penalty term; execution without a valid approval path is nonconformant. The utility of a shortcut is not lawful utility.

**Action B — Let R17 self-certify the second review**  
Violates:

- Rule 6: no independent second-officer review occurs.
- Rule 7: four-eyes principle is breached because R17 is the first analyst.
- Rule 9: self-certification does not create a valid approval path.

B cannot become recommended through forecast because it fabricates a control. It is worse than a simple delay because it corrupts the audit trail and may expose the institution to control-failure findings in addition to unlawful execution risk.

**Action C — Interrupt P-901 and request S2 review**  
Potentially conformant if:

- R17 completes the post-evidence analyst review or otherwise routes the validated evidence correctly.
- S2 is different from R17.
- S2 independently reviews the evidence and risk context.
- Approval is recorded before execution.
- The case is not executed unless the approval path is valid.

Interrupting P-901 is an operational resource decision, not a rule violation, but it creates spillover that must be explicitly accepted.

**Action D — Alternate screening, then second review**  
Potentially conformant if:

- Alternate screening is used only to reduce uncertainty or update evidence; it does not substitute for mandatory second review while the current triggers remain.
- If the screening output authoritatively resolves the fuzzy match and produces a valid updated risk score below 70, the second-review trigger may cease to apply, but this must be documented and accepted by policy.
- Otherwise, S4 or S2 must perform independent second review before approval or execution.
- The reviewer must be different from R17.

**Action E — Request additional customer evidence**  
Conformant under Rule 5, which permits the evidence cycle to repeat. It does not authorize execution and may cause deadline loss.

**Action F — Reject now**  
Potentially conformant if:

- R17 completes the post-evidence analyst review.
- Internal policy establishes that an unresolved fuzzy match at confidence 0.46, combined with risk score 72 and the evidence validation record, meets the documented threshold for rejection.
- The rejection rationale is recorded.
- If the policy requires second-officer sign-off for rejection at this risk level, that sign-off must be obtained.

If the rejection threshold is not established, F is not clearly valid; the safer conformant path may be hold/SAR or second review.

**Action G — Seek emergency override**  
The override itself is an exception path, not currently enabled. For it to become valid, the following evidence is required:

- Compliance Director authorization.
- A valid incident identifier.
- A documented justification that the emergency conditions are satisfied.
- Scope and duration of the override.
- Compensating controls and audit record.
- Confirmation that the override does not authorize disclosure of any SAR.

Until both director authorization and valid incident identifier exist, G cannot be used as a lawful execution path.

**SAR constraint**  
If the case is held while a Suspicious Activity Report is filed, Rule 11 prohibits disclosure of the SAR to the customer. This applies to D, C, E, F, and any hold branch.

S2 is immediately available but only by interrupting P-901. That creates a direct externality: P-901 has a 70-minute deadline and a 45-minute interruption increases its breach probability by 0.12–0.28. Because P-901 is also high-value, this spillover cannot be ignored. S4 avoids immediate P-901 harm but has uncertain availability, 35–65 minutes, and may still cause P-884 to miss its deadline.

**Effect on P-901.**  
C is attractive for P-884 because its modeled completion window is 40–90 minutes. But it may make P-901 worse off by a material amount. If P-901’s expected deadline value is comparable to P-884’s €20k–€35k, the expected spillover harm could be several thousand euros or more, and the non-monetary queue effects may be larger. Therefore C should be used only when S4 is too late and the organization explicitly accepts the P-901 tradeoff.

**Alternate-screening API quota.**  
The vendor has only three expedited API calls left today. Using one for P-884 may have opportunity cost if other cases need urgent screening. If API capacity is tight, the ranking could shift toward a simpler conformant plan: R17 post-evidence review, wait for S4, then S4 second review, without alternate screening. However, if the screening is likely to resolve the fuzzy match or materially reduce uncertainty, the API call is valuable.

**Risk of consuming time while waiting for an exception.**  
G is not currently enabled. Director response time is 20–60 minutes if reachable, and a valid incident identifier must still be created and justified. Pursuing G before a conformant second-review path is exhausted can consume the S4 window and delay the S2 fallback. It should not be the primary action.

**How ranking changes with S4 availability.**

If P-901 interruption is not authorized, then the ranking becomes more conservative: D composite may still be preferred over C for resource fairness, but E or F may become more relevant depending on rejection threshold and hold tolerance.
