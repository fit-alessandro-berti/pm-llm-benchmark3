| Package    | P1, P2 |
| Delivery   | D1, D2 |

**Each activity and the object types it touches** (derived from `relations` E2O + `o2o` O2O)

| Activity | Object types touched (via E2O) | Additional types via O2O |
|----------|-------------------------------|--------------------------|

**Consequential event for convergence/divergence**: Event **e6** (`reserve_stock`) is especially consequential. Its E2O relation set (`I1`, `I3`, `O100`, `O200`) is the sole sourcesystem fact that jointly links both Orders to the same stockreservation moment. This creates an immediate convergence in the objectcentric view (both orders share an event) and forces downstream divergence (I1 follows O100path, I3 follows O200path via P1). Event **e7** (`pack_package` for P1) is equally pivotal, as it explicitly fuses items from two Orders into one Package, marking the first operational merge point.

## 4. Expected flattened behavior

**Order flattening**
- **Approximate trace shape**: Two traces (one per Order), each roughly 6–8 events long. Because E2O directly links `reserve_stock` (e6) and `pack_package` P1 (e7) to both orders, both traces will contain those shared events.
- **Copied shared events**: `e6` (reserve_stock) and `e7` (pack P1) appear in **both** O100 and O200 traces. `e11` (issue_invoice) also appears in both.
- **Omitted lifecycle aspects**: Packagelevel dispatch (`dispatch_package`, `carried_by`) is omitted unless the OrderE2O link is explicitly modeled; `confirm_delivery` events are absent (they link only to Delivery in E2O). Payment settlement details are also omitted.
- **Likely DFG edges**: `create_order  add_item  reserve_stock  pack_package  dispatch_package  close_order`. Shared events create fork/merge patterns: `reserve_stock` is a fork feeding both orders; `pack_package` (P1) is a merge point where the two traces reconverge.
- **Misleading interpretations**: A naïve reader might conclude that O100 and O200 are independently complete, when in fact they share P1 and its associated events. The flattening can mask the crossorder coupling that is central to the business story.

**Package flattening (P1 and P2 separately)**
- **Approximate trace shape**: Single trace per Package. P1 trace includes events e1e9 (and e11, e12, e15 via Invoice/Payment crosslinks). P2 trace includes e1e4, e8e10.
- **Copied shared events**: For P1, `e6` (reserve_stock) and `e7` (pack) are *not* duplicated across orders in the Package trace; they appear once. However, because E2O linked e6/e7 to both O100 and O200, the Package trace indirectly carries the “dualorder” context through the event attributes.
- **Omitted lifecycle aspects**: Orderspecific additions (e2, e3, e4, e5) are omitted; the trace starts at Order creation but quickly focuses on the Package. Confirmation and closure events for the Orders are omitted.
- **Likely DFG edges**: `create_order  add_item  reserve_stock  pack_package  dispatch_package`. For P2, the trace is shorter (no reserve event).

**Delivery flattening (D1 and D2)**
- **Approximate trace shape**: Two very short traces. D1: `dispatch_package` (e9)  `confirm_delivery` (e13). D2: `dispatch_package` (e10)  `confirm_delivery` (e14).
- **Copied shared events**: None; each Delivery trace is independent.
- **Omitted lifecycle aspects**: All Orderlevel events (creations, item additions, invoice, payments) are omitted. The trace captures only the logistical movement from packing to confirmation.
- **Likely DFG edges**: `dispatch_package  confirm_delivery`. No complex branching.

**Invoice flattening (V1)**
- **Approximate trace shape**: Single trace for the Invoice: `issue_invoice` (e11)  `record_payment` (M1, e12)  `record_payment` (M2, e15). Trace length 3.
- **Copied shared events**: `e11` appears once (the invoice itself). Payment events are unique per payment.
- **Omitted lifecycle aspects**: All Order creation, item handling, packaging, and delivery events are omitted. The trace is purely financial.
- **Likely DFG edges**: `issue_invoice  record_payment  record_payment`. Edge labels may distinguish `partially_settles` vs `completes_settlement`.

**Why flattening on Payment would be particularly weak here**: A flattening on the `Payment` object type would produce traces consisting almost exclusively of the two `record_payment` events (M1 and M2), each appearing in isolation or paired with the Invoice. The resulting DFG would show trivial edges (`record_payment  record_payment`) and would completely miss the logistical backbone (Order  Item  Package  Delivery) that gave rise to the need for payment. Moreover, because M1 is a partial payment and M2 completes settlement, the Payment trace would be fragmented across two separate object instances, and without the contextual amount attribute (present in `events` but not propagated as a structural relation), the distinction between “partial” and “full” settlement would be lost, leading to a misleadingly simple “payment  payment” loop that obscures the underlying orderfulfillment flow.

## 5. Expected objectcentric structures

**OCDFG** (`pm4py.discover_ocdfg`):
- The graph will have nodes for each object type (`Order`, `Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, `Payment`).
- A distinct edge will emerge from `Order`  `Item` via `add_item` activities, and from `Item`  `Package` via `pack_package`.
- **Crucially**, the OCDFG will show **two incoming edges** to the `Package` node `P1` from both `O100` and `O200`, reflecting that P1 fuses items from separate Orders. The `Invoice` node `V1` will have incoming edges from both Orders via the `bills` qualifier, and outgoing edges to `Payment` nodes `M1` and `M2` labeled `partially_settles` and `completes_settlement` respectively.
- The `Delivery` nodes `D1` and `D2` will have edges from `Package` (`carries`), and `D1` will additionally connect to `Order` O100 (analytically) and `D2` to O100 (since I2 originates in O100), while the O200 link for D1 is mediated through P1’s I3.

**OTG** (`pm4py.discover_otg`):
- The ObjectType Transition Graph will succinctly capture the lifecycle state changes per object type.
- Expected transitions: `Order`  `created` (e1, e4), then `Order`  `closed` (e16, e17). `Item`  `added_to` (e2e5), then potentially `reserved_for` (e6). `Package`  `packed_with` (e7, e8), then `dispatched_in` (e9, e10). `Delivery`  `carried_by` (e9, e10), then `confirmed` (e13, e14). `Invoice`  `issued_for` (e11), then `recorded_against` (e12, e15). `Payment`  `recorded_against` (e12, e15).
- The OTG will highlight that `Package` `P1` has transitions from two distinct `Order` types, marking it as a **crossorder hub**.

**ET–OT graph** (`pm4py.discover_etot`):
- This graph explicitly links each event (`ocel:eid`) to the object types it touches.
- Event **e6** (`reserve_stock`) will have edges to **both** `Order` types and **both** `Item` types (I1, I3), visually capturing the “jointly shared” nature.
- Event **e7** (`pack_package` for P1) will have edges to `Package` `P1`, `Item` `I1`, `Item` `I3`, and **both** `Order` types, reinforcing the modeling choice of direct E2O links for operationally shared events.
- Event **e11** (`issue_invoice`) will connect to `Invoice` `V1` and **both** `Order` types.
- Event **e12** (`record_payment` M1) and **e15** (`record_payment` M2) will each connect to `Payment` and `Invoice` only, keeping the paymentorder distance visible but not overconnected.

**Objectcentric Petri net** (`pm4py.discover_oc_petri_net`):
- The net will have places representing object states (e.g., `O100_created`, `O200_created`, `I1_reserved`, `I3_reserved`, `P1_packed`, `V1_issued`, `V1_paid`).
- Transitions correspond to the 17 events. Notably, transition **e6** will have input arcs from the initial markings of both `O100` and `O200` (and from I1, I3), and output arcs to their reserved markings—modeling the concurrent reservation.
- Transition **e7** (pack P1) will have input arcs from the reserved places of I1 and I3 (which may be in different Orders) and output to the `P1_packed` place, illustrating how a single transition can merge tokens from two distinct Order tracks.
- Transition **e12** (M1) and **e15** (M2) will have input from `V1_issued` and output to `V1_paid`; M1’s transition may have a “partial” guard, M2 a “complete” guard, reflecting the partialsettles / completes_settlement split.
- The net may exhibit **conflict**: firing e6 for O100 and O200 simultaneously may create two token paths that converge at e7, a structure that PM4Py’s discoverer will render but which has multiple valid interpretations (e.g., whether reservation is truly simultaneous or sequential).

**Objectinteractions summary** (`pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary`):
- The summary table will list, for each object type, the number of interactions (E2O + O2O rows) and the partner types.
- `Order` will show high interaction count: with `Item` (via add_item, contains), with `Package` (via combines_order for P1), with `Delivery` (via dispatched_for, confirmed), and with `Invoice` (via bills). The interaction degree for `Order` will be the highest, driven by the twoorder structure.
- `Package` `P1` will show interactions with **two** `Order` types, **two** `Item` types, and one `Delivery` type—its crossorder nature is reflected in a multitype interaction row.
- `Invoice` `V1` will interact with two `Order` types and two `Payment` types.
- `Payment` types `M1`, `M2` will each interact primarily with `Invoice` and secondarily with nothing else (low degree), matching the design choice to keep payment E2O minimal.

## 6. Modeling alternatives

1. **Sourceminimal E2O policy** (the one adopted above):
   - E2O contains only relations explicitly narrated or jointly shared (reserve, pack P1, invoice). Confirmations link only to Delivery; payment settlement qualifiers live in O2O.
   - *Discovery impact*: OTG and OCDFG must analytically propagate OrderDelivery and OrderPayment paths. Flattened Order traces will include shared events (e6, e7) but omit lifecycle steps like confirm delivery, making the “shared” nature explicit yet requiring graph traversal to see full ordertocash flow.

2. **Full operational involvement policy**:
   - Every event is E2Olinked to every object it touches, even inferentially. Confirm Delivery would link to both Orders (since D1 carries P1 which contains items from both). Payment events would link to Orders via the invoiceorder chain. P1 would link to O100, O200, I1, I3, D1, etc.
   - *Discovery impact*: E2O becomes dense, risking **relation inflation**. DFGs discovered on flattened Order logs would show many duplicated shared events, inflating activity counts and potentially creating spurious loops. OTG would have highdegree nodes, obscuring the distinction between sourcesystem facts and analytical propagation. The business story remains the same, but the model’s “noisetosignal” ratio increases.

3. **O2Opropagated analytical relations policy**:
   - Keep E2O sourceminimal (as adopted). However, enrich O2O qualifiers to encode analytical hierarchies: e.g., `contains` could be transitive (Order contains Package via Item), and `bills` could have a `settles` subqualifier chain. O2O would effectively become a “logic layer” that mirrors what other tools discover analytically.
   - *Discovery impact*: OCDFG and Petrinet discovery would show more compact traces because the analytical relations are prewired into the structural model. Flattened traces on Order might omit some shared events (since they’re now “implied” by O2O), reducing trace length but also reducing explicit visibility of the operational shared moments (e6, e7) unless the analyst explicitly reads the O2O qualifiers. The underlying event log semantics are unchanged; only the discovery pipeline’s starting assumptions shift.

Each alternative preserves the same raw business facts (17 events, 12 objects, relationships) but changes *which* relations become part of the E2O/O2O base, thereby steering which discovery results highlight convergence, divergence, or independence.

## 7. Failure modes (at least eight)

1. **Relation inflation** – Adding too many E2O rows (e.g., linking every confirm delivery to both Orders) bloats the relations table, causes OTG/OCDFG to overconverge, and masks the true singlesource events that drive order separation.

2. **Missing direct E2O links** – If the `reserve_stock` E2O rows for O100 and O200 were omitted, the OTG would fail to see the initial joint reservation, and the OCDFG would treat O100 and O200 as independent from the start, losing the convergence point that explains why P1 fuses items from two orders.

3. **Treating O2O propagation as observation** – Interpreting O2O qualifier `contains` as “observed eventobject link” rather than structural containment would cause E2O to be repopulated with derived relations (e.g., Order  Item via contains), breaking referential integrity because the same ItemOrder pairing would appear both as a source event (`add_item`) and as a propagated O2O fact.

4. **Duplicate shared events after flattening** – Flattening on Order copies `e6` and `e7` into both O100 and O200 traces. If the flattening routine does not deduplicate pertrace activity IDs, the resulting DFG would count `add_item` and `pack_package` twice, skewing frequencybased discovery (e.g., overestimating the prevalence of those activities).

5. **Ambiguous payment allocation** – M1 (100) and M2 (150) both bear qualifier `recorded_against` V1 in E2O. Without the `amount` attribute in `events` or explicit `partially_settles`/`completes_settlement` in O2O, a flattened Payment trace cannot distinguish partial from full settlement, leading to the mistaken interpretation that the invoice was paid in one go.

6. **Overly short Payment traces** – Flattening on the `Payment` object type yields traces of length 1 (just the `record_payment` event). The resulting DFG shows a trivial loop, completely erasing the OrderItemPackageDelivery context that necessitated payment. Analysts may wrongly conclude that payment is the only relevant activity.

7. **Constructor/API version mismatch** – If PM4Py is upgraded and `pm4py.ocel_flattening` changes its signature (e.g., `type`  `object_type`), the pipeline breaks at the flattening step. Isolating the import line (as noted in assumption 6) is essential; failing to do so produces cryptic `TypeError`s that are mistaken for datamodel bugs.

8. **False total order for sametime events** – Even though this example has no timestamp ties, a generalpurpose pipeline might sort events by timestamp and assume a total order for DFG discovery. If two events (e.g., two `add_item` events) shared an exact timestamp, an enforced order could impose a spurious causal edge, distorting the OTG and Petrinet structure. Awareness of timestamp granularity and UTCaware comparisons is required to avoid this mode.

## 8. Final semantic checks (at least six assertions a reviewer could inspect)

1. **Eventrelation consistency** – For every row in `relations`, the `ocel:eid` value appears in `events` with an identical `ocel:activity` and `ocel:timestamp`. A manual crosscheck of all 31 relation rows against the 17 event entries should show exact matches.

2. **ObjectID coverage** – Every `ocel:oid` and `ocel:oid_2` in `relations` and `o2o` exists in the `objects` DataFrame. Counting unique IDs: `objects` has 12 oids; `relations` references only those 12 (I1I3, O100O200, P1P2, D1D2, V1, M1M2); `o2o` references the same set. No stray IDs should appear.

3. **Payment amount reconciliation** – The `amount` column in `events` contains 100 (M1) and 150 (M2). The `amount` of the Invoice event `V1` is 250. Verifying `100 + 150 == 250` confirms that the financial totals are internally consistent and that M1 is truly partial, M2 truly completes the settlement.

4. **Sharedevent E2O coverage** – Event `e6` (`reserve_stock`) has exactly four E2O rows: `I1`, `I3`, `O100`, `O200`, all with qualifier `reserved_for`. Event `e7` (`pack P1`) has exactly six rows: `P1`, `I1`, `I3`, `O100`, `O200` (with `packed_with`/`included_in`/`combines_order`). Verifying these counts confirms the “jointly shared” and “combines two Orders” design choices are fully encoded.

5. **No duplicate relation rows** – The `relations` DataFrame’s row count equals `relations.drop_duplicates().shape[0]`. A reviewer can sort by all columns and visually confirm no two rows are identical; this guards against accidental replay of the same eventobject pair.

6. **O2O direction integrity** – In the `o2o` DataFrame, `ocel:oid` is always the container (`Order`, `Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, `Payment`) and `ocel:oid_2` the contained (`Item`, `Package`).
