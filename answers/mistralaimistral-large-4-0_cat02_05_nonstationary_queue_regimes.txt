# 1. Data audit and estimands

**Queueing quantities and units.** The service center is open 07:00–19:00, so I use a **12-hour opening day** convention. All daily counts are during opening hours. I define:

- **Arrival intensity**  = daily arrivals / 12, in cases per opening hour.
- **Completion intensity**  = daily completed / 12, in cases per opening hour.
- **Service time** = active handling time in minutes, excluding waiting. The table gives daily medians and p90s, **not** mean case flow time.
- **WIP** L = `mean_wip`, the mean number of cases in the system from 15-minute snapshots. I assume these snapshots are during opening hours.
- **Capacity** = `staff_fte`, mean concurrently available FTE.
- **Capacity pressure proxy** _med = (daily arrivals × mean daily median service minutes) / (720 × staff FTE). This is a **median-based offered-load proxy**, not true utilization. For right-skewed service times, median  mean, so _med understates true offered load.
- **Implied flow time** W = L / , in minutes if  is per hour. This is total system time, i.e. waiting plus service, **not** service time.

**Labeled assumptions.**

- **A1.** The 15-minute WIP snapshots are during opening hours. Needed to interpret `mean_wip` as L during the same window as arrivals/completions.
- **A2.** No abandonment or case loss; every arrival either completes or remains in the system. Needed to interpret cumulative arrivals  completions as backlog change.
- **A3.** Daily median/p90 service times are active handling times, not flow times.
- **A4.** `staff_fte` is available capacity; breaks/shrinkage are already reflected or ignored.
- **A5.** The rise in `complex_pct` is treated as a regime shift, but it may be a real case-mix shift, a coding change, or both.
- **A6.** The staffing intervention began during 2026-04-29, but exact implementation time is unknown; I treat Apr 29–May 1 as an intervention regime, not a clean causal contrast.

**Threats to naive inference.**

1. **Weekday/weekend composition.** Pooling 20 weekdays and 8 weekends creates artificial overdispersion and hides very different arrival rates and staffing.
2. **Case-mix shift.** `complex_pct` jumps from about 22% before Apr 20 to about 43% after Apr 20; service times and WIP rise at the same time.
3. **Outage/recovery nonstationarity.** Apr 15 has a 180-minute outage; Apr 16–17 are recovery days with completions exceeding arrivals.
4. **Carry-over backlog.** The 07:00 backlog is not supplied. Completed cases need not have arrived the same day, so daily completions are not cohort completions.
5. **Staffing intervention timing.** The intervention began during Apr 29, but exact time is not logged; staffing changes are confounded with backlog recovery and weekday/weekend mix.
6. **Aggregated service-time quantiles.** Daily medians and p90s cannot be pooled into case-level quantiles, and median service time must not be substituted for mean flow time.
7. **Serial dependence.** Daily rows are not independent; there is day-of-week structure and autocorrelation.
8. **Possible coding change.** The complex-variant increase may be partly measurement change, not only real case-mix change.

- **Weakness of the primary:** Some cells are small, especially R3 (n=1) and R6 (n=3), so confidence is lower for those regimes.

- **Intervention.** Apr 29–May 1 shows completions above arrivals and falling WIP, which looks like a success. - **Complex mix.** The pooled correlation between `complex_pct` and service time may suggest complex cases cause delay, but the mix shift coincides with higher arrivals, higher WIP, and possible coding changes. The data cannot separate real mix shift from measurement change.

**Hourly dispersion for representative days.** Using sample variance over the 12 hourly bins:

Fano factor > 1 indicates overdispersion relative to a Poisson count process; Fano < 1 indicates underdispersion. The Apr 21 arrival vector is extremely bursty: one 09:00–10:00 bin has 34 arrivals. The Apr 15 completion vector is also overdispersed because of the 11:00–14:00 outage zeros. The Apr 25 completion vector is smooth, with Fano below 1.

**Daily dispersion and serial dependence.** For daily arrivals:

| Window | n | Mean | Sample variance | Fano | CV | Lag-1 autocorrelation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| All days | 28 | 89.96 | 902.18 | 10.03 | 0.334 | 0.395 |
| Weekdays only | 20 | 108.00 | 71.47 | 0.662 | 0.078 | 0.511 |
| Weekends only | 8 | 44.88 | 32.98 | 0.735 | 0.128 | 0.433 |

The overall daily Fano of about 10 is a mixture effect: weekday and weekend arrival rates are very different. Within weekdays, daily arrivals are actually underdispersed relative to Poisson. The positive weekday lag-1 autocorrelation (0.51) may reflect gradual demand growth, the mix shift, or backlog carry-over. The weekend lag-1 estimate is based on only 8 points and is unreliable.

**Change-point evidence.** A simple binary-segmentation heuristic on `complex_pct` finds the best single split after 2026-04-19: left mean 22.4%, right mean 43.3%. On `median_service_min`, the best single split is after 2026-04-14: left mean 25.8 min, right mean 33.4 min, aligning with the outage/recovery and subsequent high-mix period. `outage_min > 0` occurs only on Apr 15. The staffing intervention is known to begin during Apr 29, but exact timing is not logged.

**Why four representative days cannot establish a full stochastic model.** The four hourly vectors are selected illustrations, not a random sample. Twelve hourly bins per day give very unstable variance and Fano estimates. The daily table has no case-level timestamps, no hourly WIP, no abandonment, no service-time distribution, and no exact intervention time. Therefore these data can support regime descriptions and pressure indicators, but not a calibrated queueing model or a causal estimate of the intervention.

# 6. Little’s-Law applicability matrix

Little’s Law, L = W, is an identity for averages over a defined window. It requires that L, , and W refer to the same window and that the boundary WIP is stable enough. It does **not** require Poisson arrivals or exponential service. Here, the main problems are unknown 07:00 backlog, nonstationarity, and using arrivals or completions as the throughput proxy when they differ.

| Window/regime | Boundary stability | Throughput proxy | WIP comparability | Carry-over risk | Stationarity | Implied flow-time calculation | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pooled all 28 days | No; arrivals 2519 vs completions 2409, net +110 | Ambiguous | Poor across regimes | High | No | 239 min arrivals / 250 min completions, not meaningful | **Not defensible** |
| R1 weekday baseline | Approximate; arrivals 701 vs completions 697 | Arrivals or completions nearly equal | Good within regime | Moderate; unknown start backlog | Approximate | 146 min arrivals / 147 min completions | **Approximate only** |
| R2 weekend baseline | Approximate; arrivals 167 vs completions 166 | Arrivals or completions | Good, but Apr 18–19 may carry residual backlog | Moderate-high | Approximate | 237 min arrivals / 239 min completions | **Approximate only** |
| R3 outage day | No; arrivals 105 vs completions 58; outage 180 min | Completions not representative | WIP includes shock accumulation | High | No | 322 min arrivals / 583 min completions, contrast only | **Not defensible** |
| R4 recovery | No; arrivals 211 vs completions 241 | Completions include backlog clearance | WIP includes outage backlog | High | No | 297 min arrivals / 260 min completions, not a clean cohort flow time | **Not defensible** |
| R5 high-mix weekday pre-intervention | No; arrivals 798 vs completions 704, net +94 | Arrivals and completions differ | WIP rising within regime | High | No; backlog building | 280 min arrivals / 317 min completions, descriptive only | **Approximate only** |
| R6 intervention weekdays | No; arrivals 345 vs completions 363, net 18 | Completions include backlog clearance | WIP declining | High | No; only 3 days, timing unknown | 232 min arrivals / 220 min completions, descriptive only | **Approximate only** |
| R7 high-mix weekend | Approximate; arrivals 192 vs completions 180 | Arrivals and completions close | Moderate | Moderate; weekend carry-over | Approximate | 315 min arrivals / 336 min completions | **Approximate only** |

**Important interpretation.** The implied flow times above are total time in the system, waiting plus service. They must not be compared directly with `median_service_min`. For example, R1 has an implied flow time of about 146 minutes but a median service time of 25 minutes; the difference is queueing/waiting, but because only the median service time is observed, the mean service time and mean waiting time cannot be separated.

# 7. Operational implications

**Staffing.** Do not staff from the pooled average of 5.14 FTE. Weekday high-mix periods need about 6–7 FTE, while weekends need about 2.5–3 FTE. R5 has _med  0.93 and WIP/FTE  7.5, so it is near or beyond sustainable capacity even before considering that median service understates mean service. R7 has lower absolute WIP but _med  0.85 and WIP/FTE  7.6, so weekends are also capacity-stressed per FTE.

**Backlog recovery.** Track cumulative arrivals  completions, not just daily completions. Apr 16’s high completions were recovery from the Apr 15 outage, not a repeatable throughput level. The high-mix pre-intervention period accumulated about +106 net cases, and the intervention period cleared only about 18. A backlog-clearance surge should be temporary and should not be used as the new steady-state staffing target.

**Peak smoothing.** `peak_08_10_pct` rises from about 29.6% in R1 to 45.9% in R5. The 08:00–10:00 peak is more intense in high-mix weekdays. Consider appointment smoothing, staggered intake, or additional early-window capacity. The pooled peak share of 32.25% hides this shift.

**Intervention assessment.** The Apr 29–May 1 staffing increase coincided with falling WIP and completions above arrivals, but service times remained elevated (median 32.7 min, p90 74 min) relative to baseline (25.0 and 47.0). The intervention may have helped clear backlog, but these data do not prove it restored baseline service speed.

**Data collection.** Log the exact intervention start time; record 07:00 backlog; collect case-level timestamps so PM4Py can compute arrival/finish rates and temporal features; collect hourly WIP; collect abandonment; collect mean service time, not only median/p90; and separate coding changes from real case-mix changes.

**A policy that looks beneficial under pooled averages but is risky under a segmented view.** A pooled view might say weekends have low WIP (17.4 vs 34.9) and therefore can absorb staff cuts. The segmented view shows weekends have longer implied flow time (about 279 vs 232 minutes) and higher WIP per FTE (6.6 vs 5.7). Cutting weekend staffing would likely worsen weekend delay per case even though absolute weekend WIP is smaller.
