## 1. Decision rule
Deploy a gated, spillover-aware policy rather than a single `p_now` threshold.

1. Eligibility gate
- The case must still be before `Diagnosis Complete`; otherwise the swarm is not action-effective.
- A case is not eligible for a repeat swarm unless a materially new event has occurred since the last decision: new customer artifact/log, stage transition, ownership/queue change, or a calibration-adjusted risk movement of at least 0.08.
- If a key live feature is missing or imputed, the alert is provisional. It can consume a slot only after a rapid human check or after the signal persists to the next refresh, unless time to SLA is under 2 hours.

2. Probability robustification
- SME: use an adjusted risk band of `p_now + 0.04` to `p_now + 0.09`.
- Strategic Enterprise: use `p_now - 0.12` to `p_now - 0.05`.
- Public Sector: use `p_now` as centered, with ordinary uncertainty.
- Non-Native-Language Support: keep `p_now` as centered, but widen uncertainty and use the lower half of the effect range for prioritization until more evidence exists.
- Missing-feature cases: widen both risk and effect uncertainty; do not let a single imputed-field jump alone trigger a swarm.

3. Priority calculation
- Compute a direct-value band: `effect range x value of avoiding breach - false-alarm burden - 450`.
- Charge each swarm a base queue externality of about `EUR 1.5k` in normal congestion. If live queue monitoring suggests externality above about `EUR 2.5k` per slot or more than 20 minutes aggregate delay, stop adding swarms.
- Intervene only when the case is eligible and the spillover-adjusted action value remains positive under a conservative view:
`conservative value ~= lower-bound effect x breach value - false-alarm burden - 450 - base spillover charge`.
- Sort by conservative value first, then by: time to SLA, closeness to `Diagnosis Complete`, presence of materially new evidence, and public/regulatory obligations not well represented by euros.

4. Alert persistence
- `A` tier: conservative value positive and no data-quality blocker. Act now.
- `B` tier: midpoint positive but conservative case not yet clear. Wait for persistence, human check, or a lower-congestion window.
- `C/D` tier: suppressed, ineligible, or negative after externalities. Do not intervene.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy
- Cool-down after a non-action decision: suppress same-case alerts for 90 minutes unless there is a material new event, a stage transition, or a calibration-adjusted risk move of at least 0.08.
- Cool-down after an actual swarm: no second swarm unless the case is still pre-`Diagnosis Complete` and a materially new diagnostic event occurs; require human approval.
- Escalation rule: break cool-down if time to SLA drops below 2 hours, if the case moves into `Diagnosis Started` with unresolved blockers, or if new customer evidence plausibly changes diagnosis direction.
- Re-entry rule: better evidence can justify re-entry even if `p_now` falls slightly; probability movement alone is not the only signal.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks
- Value-based prioritization can starve lower-value cases. Safeguard: do not run a pure-euro policy. Maintain a protected review lane for Public Sector, regulatory, or very-high-risk cases, and audit intervention rates by value decile so lower-value cases are not silently ignored.
- Non-Native-Language Support has high uncertainty. Safeguard: uncertainty should trigger human review and exploration, not automatic exclusion. For near-threshold cases in this segment, allocate a small learning budget over time so the policy does not permanently under-serve the segment because evidence is sparse.
- Calibration differs by segment. Safeguard: use segment-specific calibration bands and thresholds, not a single global risk score. If a segment's calibration drift exceeds tolerance, demote it to manual-review-first until recalibrated.
- Selective intervention changes future labels. Safeguard: log recommendation, decision, timing, queue state, and whether a swarm occurred; train future models with intervention indicators and use causal estimators rather than treating observed post-policy outcomes as if they were natural labels.
- Spillover harm may systematically fall on untreated groups. Safeguard: attribute queue-delay harm by the segment and stage of untreated cases, not just by selected cases. If repeated swarms are pushing delay onto the same untreated group, cap concurrent swarms and raise the externality charge for additional interventions.
- Public obligations may be underpriced by the supplied euro values. Safeguard: allow a documented manual override when public-service or regulatory harm is materially larger than the contractual value field suggests.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan
- Operational monitoring, daily: number of eligible alerts, swarms used, reserved-slot usage, specialist interruption time, time from `Triage` to `Diagnosis Complete`, untreated queue delay, and actual SLA breaches. Trigger an operations review if observed aggregate delay exceeds 20 minutes per slot or the reserve is exhausted before 15:00 on multiple days.
- Predictive monitoring, weekly: segment- and stage-level calibration, Brier score, reliability plots, calibration slope/intercept, alert persistence patterns, and missing-feature rates. Track SME underprediction and Strategic Enterprise overprediction separately rather than averaging them away.
- Causal-effect monitoring, monthly: estimate intervention effect by stage, segment, time-to-SLA, and repeated-alert status using logged-policy data and doubly robust estimation. For near-threshold `B`-tier cases, use small randomized threshold perturbations or randomized ordering when capacity is tight so effect learning does not depend only on observational selection.
- Capacity and spillover monitoring, weekly: estimate marginal harm of the first, second, third, and fourth swarm as a function of queue length and specialist utilization. If marginal spillover exceeds marginal direct value for the last used slot over two consecutive review windows, raise the action bar or reduce simultaneous swarms.
- Fairness monitoring, monthly: intervention rate, false-alarm burden, realized benefit, missed-breach rate, and untreated spillover burden by segment, language-support context, and value band. Review whether uncertain segments are being under-selected because of evidence scarcity rather than true lack of benefit.
- Policy-induced drift detection: compare feature distributions, stage timing, and breach labels before and after policy rollout; run a shadow evaluation that includes intervention indicators and queue state. If the policy materially changes process timing, retrain the prediction model and the intervention rule together rather than recalibrating only one side.
- Rule-change triggers: change thresholds or tier boundaries when any of the following persists for two consecutive review periods: segment `ECE > 0.05`, realized marginal net benefit of `B`-tier swarms is non-positive, untreated spillover harm exceeds 30% of direct gains, or a segment's intervention share materially diverges from its eligible share without documented justification.
