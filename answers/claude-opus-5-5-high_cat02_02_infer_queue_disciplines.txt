# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

## Conventions, observations and assumptions

- **A1 (decision epochs):** A decision happens when a resource becomes free, or when a job arrives while another is in service (a possible preemption). Unless a gap is noted, the observed start is taken as the selection.
- **A2 (scan delay):** A recorded arrival r means the true arrival lies in [r−2, r]. Scan delay can make a job eligible earlier than recorded, never later. It therefore cannot excuse skipping a job whose recorded arrival is before the decision.
- **A3 (estimates):** As the prompt says, est_min is treated as visible to dispatch at arrival. **Observation O1:** every realized duration equals est_min exactly, and B01's three segments add up to 45 = est. This is flagged as a possible sign that estimates were backfilled (Section 5).
- **A4 (skills in QD):** A job needs all of its listed skills on a single resource. That gives: mechanical → D-M or D-X; electrical → D-F or D-X; firmware → D-F; firmware+electrical → D-F only; mechanical+electrical → D-X only.
- **A5 (visibility):** Priority, appointment and deadline are assumed visible at arrival. If they are not, "priority ignored" becomes "priority unknown" (Section 5).
- **A6 (QD appointment binding):** The D06 appointment is assumed to be pre-bound to D-X. This is only needed for the slot-reservation reading.
- **Observation O2:** 2-minute start or stop lags appear at 08:00→08:02 (A01), 08:30→08:32 (A02), 09:00→09:02 (B01), 09:14→09:16 (B02 preemption) and 09:46→09:48 (B01 stop). Starts that were pre-staged (A03 at 08:20) or back-to-back have no lag. QA and QB may therefore have a manual dispatch step of about 2 minutes.

✓ means the rule matches the observed choice; ✗ means it does not.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

## QA: FIFO plus an override for appointments that are due
- **Supports:** A03 at 08:20 (appointment outranks both FIFO and P1), A02 at 08:30, A05 at 09:05, A06 at 09:20, A07 at 09:32, and no preemption when A04 and A08 arrived.
- **Challenges:**
  - A06 started 5 min after its appointment. *This is explained by known mechanics.* The rule acts only at completions and only on appointments that are already due; at 09:05 A06 was not yet due. It is equally explained by an appointment window of at least ±5 min, which is an undocumented tolerance, not a new mechanism.
  - A04 and A08 (P1) waited 45 and 61 min. These challenge management's presumed priority claim, not this diagnosis. They require an undocumented exception only if priority was supposed to be active; otherwise the explanation is that priority was not visible or not used (Section 5).
  - A1 was idle from 08:30 to 08:32. This matches the 2-min manual-dispatch lag seen elsewhere (O2) and is at the resolution of the data. No exception is needed.
  - Early arrival of an appointment job (A03 at 08:08): whether early jobs can be served early cannot be tested, because A1 had no free decision point between 08:08 and 08:20.

## QB: two-class hybrid (P1 preempts or jumps; P2 and P3 FIFO; resume first)
- **Supports:** B02 at 09:16 (P1 preemption, logged as "urgent arrival"), B05 at 10:00 (P1 jumps ahead of the older B01 and B04), B01 resumed at 09:31 and 10:14, B04 at 10:28, B06 at 10:36, and no preemption by the P2 or P3 arrivals (B04, B06, B07).
- **Challenges:**
  - B03 at 09:48 *requires an undocumented exception*, and the log itself labels it "dispatcher reassignment". The stop comes 2 min after B05's recorded arrival, the same latency as the 09:16 preemption, which suggests B05 triggered it. Yet the slot went to B03, which had waited 28 min. A plausible reading is SLA rescue for B03 while B05 still had slack (B05 started after 14 min).
  - B03 being passed over at 09:31 contradicts pure priority, but it is explained by resume-first. That fits the absence of any visible resumption overhead (the segments add up to exactly 45 min), since cheap resumption makes keeping one job open acceptable.
  - B07's appointment was not honoured (26 min late). This contradicts any appointment claim for QB. It is explained if QB simply ignores appointments, or if the appointment refers to something other than the service start (an assumption).

## QC: SPT family with aging, plus P1 escalation
- **Supports:** C02 at 10:10, C03 at 10:18 (SPT, which here coincides with feasible-EDD and with priority excluding C05), and C06 at 11:34.
- **Challenges:**
  - C04 over C06 at 10:32 *needs an undocumented mechanism*: either look-ahead dispatch (the choice made at 10:18, before C06 arrived) or aging. Under the earliest-key rule the margin is 2 min, inside the scan uncertainty.
  - C05 at 10:54 *needs an escalation exception*. Its deadline had already passed (10:50) at a real-time decision, although the note says "at risk". That wording fits better with a look-ahead decision at 10:32, when the deadline was endangered but not yet breached. This is weak textual evidence.
  - C01 over C07 at 11:40 *needs an undocumented aging or anti-starvation rule*. C01 had waited 100 min. No deadline, priority or SPT rule predicts this choice.
  - The absent escalation for C07 (2 min of slack at 11:40, deadline then missed) shows that escalation is not a general "any SLA at risk" switch. It is either limited to P1 or triggered by a breach.

## QD: skill-constrained strict FIFO
- **Supports:** D03 at 11:21, D04 at 11:25 (skips P1 D08), D06 at 11:30, D08 at 11:35 and D07 at 11:48 (skips P1 D10).
- **Challenges:**
  - D-X idle from 11:20 to 11:30. *This can be explained by known constraints* if the D06 appointment was pre-bound to D-X (A6) and D-X does not backfill. It is equally explained by D-X actually starting at 11:30, which is a data-quality problem with the availability table.
  - D-F idle from 11:36 to 12:00 while D10 (P1) waited. *This needs an undocumented rule:* strict FIFO where the head job D05 cannot finish before the meeting, and no job may skip it. The alternative is unrecorded D-F work, such as meeting preparation or a firmware setup for D05. The prompt warns that a missing reason does not prove there was none.
  - D05 waiting 73 min, although D-F could have completed it from 11:21 to 11:51, contradicts intelligent skill-based routing. It is consistent with FIFO, because D03 was earlier in the queue.
  - Skill-aware routing and FIFO give the same prediction at 11:48, and appointment and FIFO give the same prediction at 11:30. So the "skill-based routing" label is supported only as an eligibility constraint, not as an optimizing discipline.

# 7. Final synthesis

- **Most identifiable: QA.** Non-preemptive FIFO with an override for appointments that are due explains all 7 informative decisions. It is the only rule that explains both 08:20 (appointment beats FIFO and P1) and 08:30 (FIFO beats P1 and SPT). The main open question is the appointment tolerance.
- **Least identifiable: QB.** Its primary mechanism is itself contested. FIFO, preemptive priority and a two-class hybrid each fit a different subset of decisions, and the two preemptions have different, human-mediated reasons. The only combination that fits 8 of 9 decisions was built from those same decisions.
- **QD** is mostly skill-constrained FIFO. Its idle periods can be explained equally well by strict head-of-line blocking or by resource states that were not recorded.
- **QC** is SPT-like in direction, but the exact variant (look-ahead, aging, escalation trigger) is not identified.

Across all four queues, priority is used only in QB, and only for P1. SPT, appointment and skill rules often coincide with FIFO, because the eligibility sets are small.

**Most dangerous claim to infer from these data alone: "QC follows SPT."** It looks well supported, but three things weaken it:
1. Every SPT-consistent choice depends on est_min, and est_min matches the realized duration for every job, so the evidence may be circular.
2. The discriminating 10:32 decision sits within the scan-delay error.
3. SPT, EDD and priority coincide exactly at the decisions where deadlines mattered.

If SPT were institutionalized on this evidence, deadline control would rest on an estimate field that may not exist in real time and on escalation behaviour that failed C05 by 44 min and C07 by 34 min. A close second is reading QB's single "urgent arrival" preemption as a general preemptive-priority policy. That would promise P2 jobs a protection they never received and assume P1 protection that B05 did not get at 09:48.
