## 1. Modeling assumptions

- **Object types and capitalization:** singular Title Case types: `Order`, `Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, `Payment`.
- **E2O qualifier vocabulary:** `order_created`, `order_receives_item`, `order_shared_operation`, `order_closed`, `billed_order`, `item_added`, `item_reserved`, `item_packed`, `package_packed`, `package_dispatched`, `delivery_dispatched`, `delivery_confirmed`, `invoice_issued`, `invoice_payment`, `payment_recorded`.
- **O2O direction conventions:** the first object is the container/source/agent and the second is the contained/carried/billed/settled target:
  - `Order --contains--> Item`
  - `Package --packs--> Item`
  - `Delivery --carries--> Package`
  - `Invoice --bills--> Order`
  - `Payment --partially_settles|completes_settlement--> Invoice`
- **Settlement assumption:** M1 settles 100 units and M2 settles 150 units; M2 is modeled as `completes_settlement` of V1 under the assumption that 250 units settle the invoice.
- **Relation richness:** the relations passed to PM4Py are source-minimal plus explicitly stated shared involvement. Dispatch, delivery confirmation, and payment events are **not** propagated to Orders or Items merely because O2O paths exist. Exceptions are E06, E07, and E11, where the narrative explicitly states shared operational involvement of O100 and O200.
- **Preventing confusion with source facts:** analytically propagated relations are not inserted into `relations`. If such a view is needed later, it should be separated and qualifiers should be prefixed, e.g. `derived:...`, so source observations remain distinguishable from analytical closure.
- **Version-dependent PM4Py assumption:** the pipeline isolates `OCEL(events=..., objects=..., relations=..., o2o=...)` as one constructor line. If a PM4Py version differs, adapt only that line or set the O2O table after construction without changing the DataFrames’ semantics.

Consequential events:

- **E06 Reserve Stock** is a convergence point: one event jointly touches I1, I3, O100, and O200.
- **E07 Pack Package P1** is especially consequential: P1 combines items from two orders, so flattening on Order copies the same packing event into O100 and O200.
- **E11 Issue Invoice V1** is another convergence point: one invoice bills both O100 and O200.
- **E12 and E15 Record Payment** create financial divergence at Payment-object level but convergence at Invoice-object level because both payments relate to V1.
- **E13 and E14 Confirm Delivery** split logistics completion across D1 and D2 at different times.

## 5. Expected object-centric structures

- **OC-DFG:** should show activity frequencies with object-type context. O100 and O200 share Reserve Stock, Pack Package P1, and Issue Invoice V1. P1 appears in a packing event involving I1, I3, O100, and O200. V1 appears in one invoice issuance and two payment events involving M1 and M2. Dispatch and Confirm Delivery remain tied to Package and Delivery objects, not directly to Orders or Items, because no propagated relations were inserted.

- **OTG:** should summarize the object-type backbone: OrderItem via `contains`, PackageItem via `packs`, DeliveryPackage via `carries`, InvoiceOrder via `bills`, and PaymentInvoice via settlement qualifiers. At instance level, this is what makes P1 bridge O100 and O200 through I1 and I3, and makes V1 link both orders to M1 and M2.

- **ET–OT graph:** should show the activity signatures. Add Item touches Item and Order; Reserve Stock touches Item and Order; Pack Package touches Package, Item, and Order because of E07; Dispatch Package touches Package and Delivery; Issue Invoice touches Invoice and Order; Record Payment touches Payment and Invoice; Confirm Delivery touches Delivery only. This highlights that E11 and payment activities are financially convergent on V1.

- **Object-centric Petri net:** one possible discovered net should represent concurrent or interleaved lifecycles for O100, O200, I1, I2, I3, P1, P2, D1, D2, V1, M1, and M2. It may show synchronization around E06 and E07 for O100/O200 and I1/I3, followed by separate dispatch/confirm paths for P1/D1 and P2/D2, and a V1-centered payment path with M1 and M2. This interpretation is not uniquely determined: PM4Py’s object-centric Petri net discovery abstracts from the data and different parameters or versions may produce different inscriptions or concurrency patterns.

- **Object-interactions summary:** should reveal strong interactions between O100 and O200 through E06, E07, and E11; between P1 and I1/I3; between P2 and I2; between D1/P1 and D2/P2; and between V1/M1/M2. It should make clear that M1 and M2 are separate Payment objects that converge on the same Invoice V1, even though flattened Order or Payment views do not show that.

## 6. Modeling alternatives

1. **Strict source-minimal E2O relations, as implemented here.**  
   Events relate only to the objects explicitly mentioned or explicitly stated as shared. This keeps the source record honest. Order flattening is lean, and object-centric discovery must rely on O2O relations to connect later logistics/financial lifecycle aspects.

2. **Full operational involvement.**  
   E09/E13 could be related not only to P1/D1 but also to I1/I3, O100, and O200. E10/E14 could be related to I2 and O100. E12/E15 could be related to V1, O100, and O200. This would make flattened Order traces include dispatch, confirmation, and payment steps, producing longer DFGs and stronger apparent order-level completion. The business story is unchanged, but the E2O table would now encode propagated operational reachability rather than direct event mentions.

3. **O2O-propagated analytical relations with explicit derived qualifiers.**  
   One could materialize closure relations such as `derived:delivery_of_order` or `derived:payment_allocated_to_order`. If kept in a separate OCEL or filtered view, this supports richer analytics while preserving a source-only view. If mixed into the same `relations` table without clear qualifiers, PM4Py will treat derived links as observed facts, inflating OC-DFG edges and making flattening look more complete than the source system actually observed.

4. **Order-centric direct linking policy.**  
   One could attach every downstream event directly to Orders and omit many Package/Delivery/Payment E2O links. Flattening on Order would then look complete, but Package, Delivery, and Payment views would weaken, and the distinction between P1 combining two orders and P2 containing only I2 would be less explicit unless O2O relations remain rich.

## 7. Failure modes

- **Relation inflation:** relating every descendant event to all ancestors through O2O can make every order appear to experience all logistics and payment events directly.
- **Missing direct E2O links:** omitting O100/O200 from E07 or V1 from E12/E15 would break explicitly stated shared/invoice facts.
- **Treating O2O propagation as observation:** derived links inserted into `relations` can make discovery believe settlement was observed at Order or Item level when it was only inferred through V1.
- **Duplicate shared events after flattening:** E06, E07, and E11 appear once in each relevant Order trace; frequency counts can overstate how many distinct events occurred.
- **Ambiguous payment allocation:** M1 and M2 settle V1, but the model does not allocate them to O100 versus O200 or to particular items.
- **Overly short Payment traces:** M1 and M2 each have one-event traces, so DFG and log-skeleton discovery on Payment is weak and mostly confirms the existence of `Record Payment`.
- **Constructor/API version mismatch:** older or newer PM4Py versions may use a different OCEL constructor signature or visualization API; adapt the isolated construction and visualization calls rather than changing the data model.
- **False total order for same-time events:** this example has no timestamp ties, but PM4Py sorts by timestamp; if ties occur, row order can create an artificial total order unless explicit tie-breakers or lower-granularity timestamps exist.
- **Timezone mistakes:** mixing UTC-aware and timezone-naive timestamps can shift event order or break validation.
- **Inconsistent qualifiers:** using one generic qualifier for all relations loses role information; using a derived qualifier without documenting it can mislead reviewers.
- **Duplicate relation rows:** repeated E2O rows distort activity frequencies and object-interaction counts even if the underlying event is unique.
- **Type capitalization drift:** mixing `Order` and `order` creates separate object types in PM4Py and fragments discovery.

## 8. Final semantic checks

- O100 contains exactly I1 and I2; O200 contains exactly I3.
- P1 packs I1 and I3, while P2 packs only I2; P1 therefore bridges items from O100 and O200.
- D1 carries P1 and D2 carries P2.
- V1 bills both O100 and O200; M1 is `partially_settles` V1 and M2 is `completes_settlement` V1.
- E06 has relation set I1, I3, O100, O200 and represents one shared stock reservation.
- E07 has relation set P1, I1, I3, O100, O200, while E08 has only P2 and I2 under the source-minimal policy.
- E12 records amount 100 and E15 records amount 150 as event attributes, not as objects.
- E13 and E14 are related only to Delivery objects, not directly to Orders, Items, or Payments.
- Every event E01–E17 has at least one E2O relation, and every relation references an existing event and object.
- All relation rows repeat the exact activity and UTC timestamp of their matching event row.
