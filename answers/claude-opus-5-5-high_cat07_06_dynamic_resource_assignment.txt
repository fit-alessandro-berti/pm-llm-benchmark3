# 1. Objectives and constraints

## 1.1 Decision setting

- **Decision unit:** one assignment batch, the current queue C001–C016, planned against the hours each worker has left today. Cases with a long SLA (for example 18 h or more) may legitimately carry over to the next shift, and that option is part of the decision.
- **Decision output:** for each case, one of three forms:
  - a performer;
  - a performer plus supervisor (for training or when skill is below the level required);
  - a preparer plus finaliser (for Final Approval).
  
  Each assignment also carries reason codes and a short explanation.
- **Interpretation choices:**
  - "High-priority" in rule 2 covers both *urgent* and *high*.
  - Continuing a case you already own is not a "new assignment" under rule 1, but it does consume hours, so it counts in the workload and overtime checks.

## 1.2 Objectives (to be balanced, not collapsed into one number)

| # | Objective | Operational proxy |
|---|---|---|
| O1 | SLA protection | Minimise probability-weighted SLA misses, weighted by priority (urgent > high > normal > low) |
| O2 | Quality / low rework | Minimise expected rework and appeals, driven by skill fit vs complexity and by the number of handovers |
| O3 | Short-term throughput | Maximise the number of cases completed today while keeping reserve capacity |
| O4 | Continuity of ownership | Minimise handovers weighted by handover-cost band; never push a case above 2 reassignments without strong reason (evidence: more than 2 reassignments gives 1.8× rework and 1.4× appeals) |
| O5 | Workload fairness and wellbeing | Spread projected utilisation evenly; minimise work given to high-overtime-risk workers |
| O6 | Skill development | At least 20% of normal/low assignments serve a *documented* training priority, done safely |
| O7 | Resilience | Keep spare specialist capacity in the bottleneck skills (Security, Approval); avoid one person holding all the work of one skill; widen the bench of people who can cover each skill |

These objectives conflict. Specialists raise O2 and hurt O5/O7. Training helps O6 and costs some O3. Continuity helps O4 and can hurt O2 when the owner is weak. The policy therefore has three layers: hard constraints, then soft constraints that need a logged justification to break, then weighted preferences.

## 1.3 Hard constraints (safety / compliance: never traded off)

| ID | Constraint | Source |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Skill level 0 means the person may not perform, prepare, or finalise that activity | skill matrix |
| H2 | Security Review is performed by skill 3, or by skill 1–2 only under explicit supervision by R7_Gita or R8_Hugo (and the supervisor is not the performer) | rule 6 |
| H3 | Final Approval is finalised by skill 2–3. Skill 1 may only *prepare*, and a skill 2–3 finaliser must be named | rule 7 |
| H4 | Urgent/high cases are not given to skill < 2 unless a named supervisor with enough skill and spare hours is attached | rule 2 |
| H5 | At most 4 *new* assignments per resource per batch; supervision duties count toward the supervisor's hours | rule 1 |
| H6 | Quality Check is batched only if priority is normal/low and SLA remaining > 24 h | rule 8 |
| H7 | No *planned* overtime. Overtime happens only as a logged, voluntary exception (for example, an urgent case that is infeasible otherwise) | wellbeing / labour agreements |

## 1.4 Soft constraints (strong defaults; breaking one needs a reason code)

| ID | Constraint | Typical justified exception |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Avoid adding work to overtime-risk = high resources | Urgent case with no safer feasible alternative; or keeping an already-owned case where moving it is worse and no overtime is needed (carry-over allowed) |
| S2 | Prefer continuity for high-complexity / high-handover-cost cases | Owner at skill 0/1 with no workable supervision; or supervised continuity would clearly break the SLA |
| S3 | Training share ≥ 20% of normal/low assignments | Only if the SLA risk is acceptable and supervision capacity exists |
| S4 | Avoid pushing a case above 2 reassignments | Owner unavailable, or owner is the cause of the loop |
| S5 | Keep at least one Security and one Approval specialist with ≥ 1.5 h unallocated (reserve) | Urgent demand consumes the reserve |

## 1.5 Preference trade-offs (weighted, by priority class)

These cover SLA fit, skill fit, continuity, workload/overtime, training, and resilience. Weights depend on the case class: urgent work leans on SLA and skill; normal/low work gives more weight to fairness and development. The weights are *governed parameters*. They are reviewed with team leads and worker representatives, and they are not treated as a discovered optimum.

## 1.6 Data-quality findings that constrain the policy

- **R5_Eva and R10_Jonas:** both list *security* as training priority but have Security = 0. Rule 6 allows supervised Security work only at levels 1–2, so they **cannot perform C015 even under supervision**. They can only shadow until someone formally certifies them at level 1.
- **R4_David and R8_Hugo:** both list *approval* as training priority but have Approval = 0. They cannot even *prepare* approvals.

These documented training priorities cannot be served inside the current rules. The mismatch should go to skill governance (a certification path) instead of being worked around by the routing algorithm.

# 2. Assignment policy

## 2.1 Overview

The policy works as a pipeline:

1. Take a state snapshot.
2. Apply a **hard filter**.
3. Order cases **most-urgent / most-constrained first**.
4. Score each candidate on **six transparent components**.
5. Choose greedily, with reserve protection.
6. Run a **batch-level repair** for quotas and fairness.
7. Generate **reason codes and an explanation**, and log them.

A greedy, rule-ordered procedure is used on purpose. It can be explained case by case ("you got this because …"). A global optimiser (for example an integer linear program) can be run in shadow mode to check how much performance the greedy procedure gives up.

## 2.2 Pseudocode

```text
INPUT  queue Q; resources R with skill[r][s], hours_left[r], open[r], ot_risk[r],
       training_priority[r], team[r]; parameters θ (weights, penalties, thresholds)
OUTPUT decisions D = {case -> (performer, supervisor|finaliser|None, reason_codes, explanation)}

# ---------- 0. State ----------
refresh availability (absence, meetings), hours_left, open cases, ot_risk
for each c in Q, r in R:
    est[c][r] = base_service[act(c)] * cx_factor(complexity(c)) * skill_mult[skill[r][s(c)]]
    # base_service, cx_factor, skill_mult learned from the log (Section 7); e.g. skill_mult = {3:1.0, 2:1.15, 1:1.45}
    handover_delay[c][r] = 0 if r == owner(c) else hdelay[band(c)] * (0.4 if warm_handover_possible else 1.0)
    # hdelay ≈ {low:0.25h, medium:1.0h, high:2.7h} from historical evidence

# ---------- 1. Eligibility filter (hard constraints) ----------
function CANDIDATES(c):
    C = []
    for r in available R:
        k = skill[r][s(c)]
        if k == 0: continue                                            # H1
        if r != owner(c) and new_count[r] >= 4: continue               # H5
        if act(c) == "Security Review":
            if k == 3: C.add((r, None))
            elif k in {1,2}:
                for sup in {R7_Gita, R8_Hugo} - {r}:
                    if spare(sup) >= sup_cost(c): C.add((r, sup, "SUPERVISED"))     # H2
        elif act(c) == "Final Approval":
            if k >= 2: C.add((r, None))
            elif k == 1:
                for f in R with skill[f][Approval] >= 2 and spare(f) >= finalise_cost(c):
                    C.add((r as PREPARER, f as FINALISER))                        # H3
        else:
            if priority(c) in {urgent, high} and k < 2:
                for sup in R with skill[sup][s(c)] == 3 and spare(sup) >= sup_cost(c):
                    C.add((r, sup, "SUPERVISED"))                                  # H4
            elif k == 1 and complexity(c) >= 0.5:
                for sup in qualified supervisors: C.add((r, sup, "SUPERVISED"))    # policy choice
            else:
                C.add((r, None))
        if act(c) == "Quality Check" and batching_requested(c):
            require priority(c) in {normal, low} and sla_left(c) > 24h                  # H6
    drop candidates whose total hours (performer + supervisor) exceed hours_left - buffer,
         UNLESS sla_left(c) allows carry-over to the next shift                          # H7
    return C

# ---------- 2. Case ordering ----------
for c in Q: slack0[c] = sla_left(c) - min_r(est[c][r] + handover_delay[c][r])
order Q by (priority_rank desc, slack0 asc, |CANDIDATES(c)| asc)     # urgent, tight, constrained first

# ---------- 3. Scoring components (each in [0,1] or a penalty) ----------
SLA(c,x):        slack = sla_left(c) - (queue_ahead[x] + handover_delay + est_total)
                 p_on_time = Φ(slack / σ_act)                     # σ from service-time dispersion
SKILL(c,x):      q = {3:1.0, 2:0.75, 1:0.45 if supervised}[k]
                 SKILL = q - 0.3 * complexity(c) * (3 - k)/3
CONT(c,x):       1 if performer == owner(c) or owner remains case owner in a co-work pair
                 else 1 - {low:0.2, medium:0.5, high:0.9}[band(c)]
                      - 0.5 * [reassign_count(c) + 1 > 2]
                 if complexity(c) >= 0.75 and band(c) == high and owner eligible:     # S2
                      non-owner candidates get -1.0 unless reason code SLA_INFEASIBLE or OWNER_SKILL_LOW
LOAD(c,x):       util = (committed_hours[r] + est)/hours_left[r]
                 LOAD = 1 - util - 0.2*max(0,(open[r]-median_open)/median_open)
                      - {high:0.6, medium:0.15, low:0}[ot_risk[r]]                      # S1
                 (the high-OT penalty is waived only if priority(c)==urgent and no other
                  candidate reaches p_on_time >= τ_urgent; or r == owner and no overtime needed)
TRAIN(c,x):      1.0 if act(c) == training_priority[r] and supervised and priority(c) in {normal,low}
                     and slack >= 2*est
                 0.3 if shadowing only (exposure; not counted as a training assignment)
                 0   otherwise
RES(c,x):        - scarcity(s) * [k == 3 and an eligible k == 2 candidate exists]
                 - 0.3 * [r would hold > 50% of today's hours in skill s]
                 - 0.5 * [assignment breaks the specialist reserve S5 and c is not urgent]
                 + 0.2 * [assignment widens the bench (supervised cross-skilling)]
                 scarcity(s) = specialist_demand_hours(s) / specialist_supply_hours(s)

SCORE(c,x) = Σ_k w_k[class(c)] * component_k

# weights by class (starting values, governed and reviewed; not "optimal")
#            SLA   SKILL  CONT  LOAD  TRAIN  RES
# urgent    .35    .25   .15   .10    .00   .15
# high      .30    .25   .20   .10    .00   .15
# normal    .15    .20   .20   .20    .15   .10
# low       .10    .15   .15   .25    .25   .10

# ---------- 4. Greedy selection ----------
for c in ordered Q:
    C = CANDIDATES(c)
    if C empty: EXCEPTION_NO_CANDIDATE(c); continue
    if priority(c) == urgent and max_x p_on_time(c,x) < 0.5: EXCEPTION_SLA_INFEASIBLE(c)
    x* = argmax SCORE with TIE-BREAK
    commit x*: update committed_hours, new_count, supervisor hours, queue_ahead, reserve
    record top-2 contributing components + any soft-constraint deviations as reason codes

TIE-BREAK (in order):
    1. higher p_on_time
    2. current owner
    3. lower projected utilisation
    4. fewer new assignments in this batch
    5. same team as current owner (lower coordination cost)
    6. longest time since last assignment of this activity (rotation)
    7. seeded random draw (seed logged)

# ---------- 5. Batch-level repair ----------
repeat until no improving move:
    if training_share(normal/low) < 20% and SLA risk acceptable:
        try swaps/deferrals that create supervised training assignments (never on urgent/high)
    if dispersion(util) > θ_fair or any high-OT resource has non-justified new work:
        try swaps that reduce it without breaking H1–H7 or raising Σ SLA risk above θ
    accept a move only if hard constraints hold and total score does not drop more than ε

# ---------- 6. Exceptions ----------
EXCEPTION_NO_CANDIDATE     -> escalate to team lead with the list of blocking constraints
EXCEPTION_SLA_INFEASIBLE   -> options shown to lead: specialist-led pair, split task,
                              negotiate deadline with customer, voluntary (logged) overtime
SUPERVISOR_UNAVAILABLE     -> pause the training assignment; reroute to a competent performer;
                              a trainee never finalises alone
ABSENCE / SICKNESS         -> re-run the policy for the affected cases; owned high-complexity
                              cases get a warm-handover package (notes, customer context)
WORKER_DECLINES / FLAGS    -> accepted with reason (fatigue, conflict of interest, missing context);
                              no penalty; logged for audit
MANUAL_OVERRIDE            -> allowed for anything except H1–H7; reason code mandatory
DATA_INCONSISTENCY         -> e.g. training priority on a skill at level 0: exposure only,
                              ticket raised to skill governance

# ---------- 7. Explanation & logging ----------
write assignment_reason = reason codes (e.g. CONTINUITY, SLA_URGENT, SPECIALIST_REQUIRED,
      TRAINING_SUPERVISED, LOAD_RELIEF, OT_PROTECT, RESERVE_KEEP, SOFT_DEVIATION_S1 ...),
      supervisor/finaliser id, policy_version, score components, decision timestamp
```

## 2.3 Socio-technical design choices

- **Reason codes, not raw scores**, are shown to workers. Scores remain available for auditors.
- **Warm handover** (a synchronous 10–15 minute briefing, with the former owner staying reachable) is treated as a *policy lever*. It reduces the handover delay term, so the policy can use it where the log shows it works.
- **Supervision is real work.** It is budgeted as about 25–35% of the task time for the supervisor, split across a start briefing, a midpoint checkpoint and sign-off. It is logged with the supervisor's ID so the audit can check it happened.
- **Right to flag.** Workers can flag an assignment (fatigue, missing context, conflict of interest) without justifying it numerically. The flag rate is itself an audit signal.
- **No individual performance ranking** is derived from the assignment data.

## 4.3 Where the baselines are legitimately better

- **First-available** is trivial to explain and cheap to run. In low-risk, homogeneous work (for example C004/C013-style eligibility cases) it performs well. The proposed policy effectively acts like first-available for low-risk routine cases through its LOAD term.
- **Specialist-only** minimises immediate error on the handful of truly critical tasks. The proposed policy deliberately behaves like it for C001, C002, C008 and C015.
- **Costs of the proposed policy:**
  - It needs calibrated estimates and a clean skill matrix.
  - It needs explicit logging of supervisors and reason codes.
  - It needs governance of its weights.
  - It can be gamed, for example by inflating the overtime flag or declining unattractive work.
  - Its reserve capacity looks like idleness to managers who look only at utilisation.

  Its advantage exists only if these costs are paid.

A practical rollout path:

1. Run the policy in **shadow mode** next to the current practice.
2. Compare the recommended decisions with the actual ones and their outcomes.
3. Do a **stepped rollout by team**.
4. Treat the close-call cases as natural experiments.

# 5. Explanation to workers

> **How today's work is shared out, and why**
>
> We assign work using a small set of rules everyone can see. The aim is to be fast and fair to you, to your colleagues and to our customers. It is not a score of anyone's performance.
>
> **What always comes first (non-negotiable)**
> - Some tasks need specific authorisation. Security Reviews are done by a Security specialist, or by a trained colleague with Gita or Hugo supervising. Final Approvals are signed off by someone at "competent" level or above.
> - Urgent and high-priority cases are never given to someone still learning that task without a named supervisor.
> - Nobody gets more than four new cases in one round, and we don't plan overtime into anyone's day.
>
> **What we then balance**
> 1. **Deadlines.** Cases close to their deadline are placed first, with someone who can finish them in time.
> 2. **Keeping your cases with you.** If you already know a case, especially a complex one, we try to keep it with you. Our own data shows that every extra handover adds waiting and makes rework more likely. When a case does have to move, the previous owner gives a short briefing and stays reachable.
> 3. **Right skill for the job.** We match the difficulty of the case to experience. That doesn't mean specialists get everything; that would overload a few people and leave others without variety.
> 4. **Your workload.** We look at your remaining hours and open cases. If your overtime risk is high, we try not to add anything new today. Sometimes that means a case you'd normally handle moves to a colleague. That is to protect your day, not a judgement about your work.
> 5. **Learning.** Roughly one in five routine (normal or low-priority) cases is used for supervised learning in the area you and your lead agreed on. Your supervisor's time is planned in, so learning is not something you do "on top".
> 6. **Keeping capacity in reserve.** We leave some specialist time free so a sudden urgent case doesn't knock everyone's plan over. If you see some spare time in your schedule, that's often why.
>
> **What you'll see on each assignment**
> A short reason, for example: *"Kept with you: you own this complex case"*, *"Moved to reduce your load today"*, *"Supervised learning: diagnosis, with Celine"*, or *"Specialist required: Security Review"*. Where it was a close call, the note says so.
>
> **Your voice**
> - If an assignment doesn't work for you (you're overloaded, you lack context, there's a conflict of interest, or you're not comfortable doing it without support), flag it. No justification in numbers is needed and there is no penalty. Flags go to your team lead and help us improve the rules.
> - If your skill profile or training goals look wrong, tell us. We already found that some training goals (for example Security for colleagues currently at "not allowed" level) can't be met under the current rules. We're setting up a certification path instead of quietly ignoring the goal.
> - The rules and their weights are reviewed every month with team representatives. Assignment data is used to improve the process, not to rank individuals.

**Example individual notes for today:**
- **Bruno:** *"C010 moved to Farid today to protect your hours, since your overtime risk is high. Tomorrow morning there's a quality-check learning case (C012) with Jonas, if your workload allows."*
- **Celine:** *"Hugo will perform the Security Review on C001 because of the 3-hour deadline. You remain the case owner and the customer's contact. A 15-minute briefing at the start is enough."*
- **David:** *"C003 stays with you because another change of owner would likely restart the diagnosis loop. C014 has gone to Jonas to free your time. No overtime is expected; C003 can continue tomorrow if needed."*

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators

Indicators are computed monthly per team and per resource, and weekly for the leading indicators. Each one is split by priority class, complexity band, and policy version. The event log should be extended with these fields:

- `supervisor`, `finaliser`
- `policy_version`, `decision_timestamp`
- `recommended_resource` (what the policy proposed)
- `override_reason`
- `score_components`
- `start_timestamp` (for service times)

| Indicator | Definition (from the log) | Alert / target |
|---|---|---|
| **Handover frequency** | Performer changes between consecutive events of a case, divided by the number of cases. Also a cost-weighted version (low 1, medium 2, high 4) and a ping-pong rate (A→B→A patterns in the handover network) | Rising trend; any ping-pong on high-complexity cases |
| **Reassignment count** | Distribution of the final `reassignment_count`; share of cases with count > 2; median waiting after an owner change by handover band | Share above 2 reassignments ≤ 5%; target no high-complexity case above 2 |
| **Skill mismatch** | Share of events where performer skill < 2 on urgent/high cases without a logged supervisor. Also: Security with skill < 3 and no R7/R8 supervisor; Approval finalised by skill < 2; any skill-0 execution | **Hard violations = 0**; soft mismatch (competent on complexity > 0.75) monitored |
| **Workload distribution** | Assigned hours per resource per day divided by available hours; spread across people (Gini, or coefficient of variation) within each team; new assignments per batch (max ≤ 4); open-case trajectory from `workload_snapshot` | Gini within team ≤ 0.25; any resource above 90% planned utilisation on more than 2 days a week |
| **Overtime risk exposure** | Share of new assignments going to high-overtime resources; actual hours worked past shift end; share of high-overtime assignments with a valid justification code (URGENT_NO_ALTERNATIVE or continuity) | Unjustified exposure = 0; overtime hours trending down |
| **SLA misses** | Share of cases finishing after `sla_deadline`, by priority. Split by assignment reason, by owner change vs continuity, and by supervised vs unsupervised. Also time-to-start for urgent cases | Urgent misses ≤ 2%; urgent time-to-start ≤ 15 min |
| **Rework / appeal after assignment** | Share of assignments followed by a Rework Correction (or appeal) within 14 days in the same case. Compared by skill level, supervision, handover count, and complexity band, *stratified* (see Section 7) | Trainee-supervised rework no worse than competent + 20%; appeals on enterprise/VIP cases tracked individually |
| **Training assignment share** | Supervised training assignments that match the worker's documented priority, divided by all normal/low assignments. Also: completion rate, SLA impact, who gets the opportunities (equity across workers and teams), and progress to the next skill level | ≥ 20%; no worker with a documented priority left without an opportunity for more than 4 weeks |
| **Specialist bottleneck indicators** | Waiting time before Security Review and Final Approval (from the performance DFG); queue length on those activities; specialist utilisation; share of specialist hours spent on work a level-2 could have done; hours of reserve kept; spread of work within each skill (bus factor) | Security/Approval waiting-time 90th percentile within target; reserve kept on at least 80% of days |
| **Deviations from policy** | Override rate (actual ≠ `recommended_resource`) by reason code and by lead; share of soft-constraint deviations with valid codes; worker flag/decline rate; replay conformance (recomputing the policy from snapshots gives the logged recommendation); unauthorised batching (Quality batches breaking rule 8) | Hard-rule deviations = 0; overrides reviewed if > 10% or concentrated on one lead or worker |
| Supplementary: continuity effectiveness | Service and waiting time plus rework for owner-kept vs owner-changed cases, split by band; effect of warm vs cold handover | Used to recalibrate the handover delay parameters |
| Supplementary: explanation quality | Share of assignments with a complete reason code; worker survey on perceived fairness and understandability | Reason codes complete on 100% |

**Interpretation cautions:**
- **Indicators can be gamed (Goodhart's law).** Pair each one with a counter-indicator. For example, training share goes with trainee rework, and low handover frequency goes with SLA misses on weak-owner cases.
- **Assignment is not random.** Specialists receive harder cases, so raw comparisons of rework by skill are confounded. Always stratify by complexity and priority.

**Learning and auditing notes:**

- **Where the parameters come from.** Service multipliers by skill level, handover delays by band, and rework odds by reassignment count are estimated *within complexity and priority strata*. This is needed because the historical assignment was not random: specialists got the hard cases. Where confounding remains, the estimates are validated with a simulation (a digital-twin replay of the queue under all three policies) or with a shadow-mode or stepped rollout, not with a naive before/after comparison.
- **What the resource networks show.**
  - The handover network, together with ping-pong detection, shows where continuity breaks down.
  - The working-together network shows real collaboration clusters, which feed the choice of supervisor pairs.
  - Activity-based similarity and discovered roles show who can actually cover which activities. Comparing that with the formal skill matrix reveals shadow roles, stale skill levels, and bench gaps (for example, Security depends on 3 people and Approval on 1 specialist).
- **Filtering.** `filter_event_attribute_values` (by `case_priority`, `team`, `complexity_band`, `assignment_reason`) and `filter_case_performance` split the performance DFGs, so policy effects are judged per segment and not on the average.
- **Governance loop.** The KPI panel and deviation report go to a monthly review with team leads and worker representatives. Weights are changed only through a versioned policy release, and each release records the indicators it is expected to move.
