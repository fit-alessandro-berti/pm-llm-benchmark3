## 1. What is observed

The panel records five branches over eight months. `automation` is an *availability* flag: it marks the month a branch could route cases through automated triage, not the share that did. Actual treatment intensity is `automated_fast_path_pct`, which rises gradually after switch-on (Alpha 25→48, Beta 27→50, Gamma 25→32) and sits at a 4–6 baseline in non-adopters, so "treated" branch-months contain a large and changing minority of automated cases — the treatment is a dose, not a state.

Selection is explicit and non-random: Alpha and Beta were chosen for high volume and poor historical cycle times; Gamma entered after a readiness pilot; Delta and Epsilon, the best pre-period performers, never adopted. Adoption timing is therefore correlated with both level and trend of the outcomes.

Adoption coincides with several other shocks. A centralized fraud desk started for *all* branches in March, visible as a uniform ~+2pp step in `escalation_pct`. From April a routing campaign added ~10% arrivals to Alpha/Beta (~5% to Gamma after July). A May product change raised `high_complexity_pct` in most branches. FTEs moved in four branch-months. Arrivals fall in August everywhere.

Measurement is aggregated and partly opaque: `median_cycle_h` and `p90_cycle_h` are branch-month summaries, not case-level distributions; `csat_1to5` is a mean over an unknown number of respondents with 20–45% response rates; fraud-desk waiting time — a plausible component of cycle time — is unrecorded; complexity is only a registration-time risk class.

Interference note: because the fraud desk is shared, "never-treated" branches are not untreated with respect to the system; E1–E5 are therefore contrasts against a possibly-degraded control, biasing measured benefits **upward**. Treatment-version note: automation in April (25% fast path, untrained staff) and in August (50%, trained) are different treatments; E1 averages them.

## 3. Identification strategy

**Primary: staggered-adoption event study with not-yet-treated controls (Callaway–Sant'Anna / Sun–Abraham style group-time ATTs), estimated descriptively given 5 clusters.**

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 (primary) Group-time ATT, cohort × calendar month, aggregated to event time | cohort g ∈ {Apr, Jul, never}; ATT(g,t) for each post month | not-yet-treated (Gamma pre-July) + never-treated (Delta, Epsilon) | month effects, `high_complexity_pct`, arrivals/FTE load, March fraud-desk step | E1, E3, E7 | Parallel trends conditional on covariates within cohort; no anticipation; no interference across branches | Pre-period ATT(g,t) for t<g; placebo March adoption date; leave-one-control-out; compare Gamma vs Delta/Epsilon separately | Only 5 clusters and 2 controls, both of which are cleaner performers *and* fraud-desk-contaminated; conditional parallel trends untestable with 3 pre-periods |
| S2 Synthetic control / matched control per adopter | single treated unit, donor pool weighted on pre-period outcome path | weighted combination of Delta, Epsilon (+ Gamma pre-July for Alpha/Beta) | pre-period `median_cycle_h`, `p90_cycle_h`, complexity, load | E1, E3 per branch | Donor pool spans the treated pre-path; no donor is treated or spilled-over; shock structure stable | Pre-period RMSPE; in-space placebos on Delta/Epsilon; in-time placebo at March | Donor pool of two units cannot reproduce Alpha's level; placebo inference has ≤4 permutations — no meaningful p-value |
| S3 Interrupted time series / comparative ITS per branch | level + slope break at branch switch-on month | own pre-period trend, with control-branch series as concurrent benchmark | seasonal/common-shock proxy (control mean), complexity, load | E1, E3, E7 | Pre-trend extrapolates; break coincides only with automation; no simultaneous unmodelled shock | Test break at a placebo date; check whether the same break appears in controls (it does in Jul–Aug) | Confounded 1:1 with the routing campaign (Apr) and the product/complexity change (May); 3–6 pre-points cannot pin a trend |
| S4 Intensity/dose panel model (`automated_fast_path_pct` as continuous regressor) | continuous dose, branch + month FE | within-branch variation over time | complexity, arrivals/FTE, month FE | E2 (association only) | Dose is as-good-as-random given FE and covariates — implausible | Compare dose coefficient with and without covariates; check reverse causality (do busy months suppress uptake?) | Dose is endogenous to staffing, learning and manager effort; coefficient absorbs the learning curve and the August demand drop |

**Why S1 is preferable (≈200 words).** The data have exactly the structure that breaks naïve two-way fixed effects: two adoption cohorts (April, July), never-treated units, and effects that plainly change with time-since-adoption as fidelity rises from 25% to 50%. Under TWFE, already-treated Alpha and Beta would serve as controls for Gamma's July switch-on, and their *improving* post-period trend would enter with a negative weight, contaminating the Gamma estimate by an amount that has nothing to do with automation. A group-time ATT restricted to not-yet-treated and never-treated comparisons eliminates that forbidden comparison and produces one interpretable number per cohort per month, which is exactly what leadership needs to see because the two cohorts are *not* exchangeable: April adopters got a 10% demand shock and (Alpha) an FTE loss on top of automation, while Gamma adopted after the May complexity shock had already landed in every branch.

S1 is also the only design that makes the identifying weakness visible rather than hiding it: the ATT(g,t) for pre-periods is reported, and here it is not flat (Alpha and Beta were selected *because* they were deteriorating). S2 and S3 are run as robustness, not as the headline; with five clusters no design delivers valid frequentist inference, so all estimates are reported as ranges.

That is the opposite of what a homogeneous automation effect would produce, and it tracks the co-shocks (Alpha/Beta absorbed +10% routing, a complexity rise and, for Alpha, −2 FTE; Gamma adopted after the complexity shock).

Interpretation (causal, conditional): once the common deterioration in the untreated branches is netted out, all three adopters look better than controls on median, p90 and rework, by roughly 3–9% on cycle time. But Alpha/Beta's controls deteriorated partly *because of the May complexity shock that hit controls harder in relative terms*, and Alpha/Beta simultaneously absorbed 10% more arrivals — so this DiD both over- and under-states the effect in ways I cannot sign confidently.

### 5.3 Treatment-intensity analysis

Using only treated branch-months (n=12) and within-branch variation, regressing `median_cycle_h` on `automated_fast_path_pct` with branch fixed effects gives a slope of roughly **−0.25 h per percentage point of fast-path share** (Alpha: 25%→48.3h, 48%→43.3h; Beta 27%→47.3h, 50%→45.0h; Gamma 25%→38.4h, 32%→36.0h). Scaled: moving from 4% to 50% fast-path share is associated with ≈ −11 h (−20%) on the median. For `p90_cycle_h` the same within-branch slope is much weaker and non-monotone (Alpha's p90 *peaked* at 39% intensity), consistent with substitution rather than end-to-end capacity creation.

Caveat that nearly voids this: intensity rises monotonically with calendar time, so the slope is collinear with (a) the August arrivals drop, (b) staff learning of *everything*, not just automation, and (c) any unrelated August improvement — the never-treated branches improved in Jul–Aug with intensity fixed at 4%. Adding a linear month trend to the intensity model roughly halves the coefficient. Treat −0.10 to −0.25 h/pp as the defensible band.

### 5.4 Pre-trends and regression to the mean

Alpha and Beta were selected *because* of poor historical cycle times, which is the textbook setup for regression to the mean: Alpha's Jan–Mar medians (50.4, 46.9, 49.4) are noisy around ~49, and any post-period value near 47–48 is unremarkable. Gamma's pre-period is *not* flat — it deteriorated sharply in May–Jun (36.5 → 45.9) after the complexity shock, and Jul–Aug partly reverts toward its Jan–Feb level of ~36.4. **A large share of Gamma's headline −18.5% is mean reversion to its own pre-May baseline, not automation.** The DiD in 5.2 mitigates this only to the extent that the controls experienced the same reversion — Delta and Epsilon did revert, but only partially (Delta 32.8 Feb → 39.0 Aug, still above baseline; Gamma returned essentially to baseline). The gap between "Gamma fully reverted, controls partially reverted" is the honest residual, and it is about 3–4 h on the median. Escalation shows a clean common step in March for all five branches (+2pp) — a useful placebo confirming that shared shocks do move these series in lockstep, and that adopter-specific escalation increments (~+1pp) are small.

### 5.5 Sensitivity / bounded conclusions

**Median cycle time.** Estimates across specifications: Gamma tight DiD −9%; Alpha/Beta wide DiD −6% and −11% relative to controls but with raw changes of −1% and +4%; intensity model −8% to −20% at 50% fast-path share; ITS on Gamma alone −18.5%. If I discount for (i) mean reversion in Gamma, (ii) fraud-desk contamination of controls (biases toward finding benefit), and (iii) the possibility that fast-path cases are simply the easy ones being relabelled, the plausible range is **0% to −12%, centred near −5%**, with the lower bound of "no effect on the median" not excluded.

**Tail (p90).** Raw p90 rose 10% at both April adopters and fell 15.5% at Gamma. Netting the common shock: Alpha/Beta ≈ −5% vs controls, Gamma ≈ −8%. But Alpha's p90 rose in absolute terms while carrying +20% arrivals and −2 FTE, so the DiD result is entirely load-adjustment-dependent: drop the load control and Alpha's tail effect flips sign. Range: **−9% to +10%; the sign is not established.** The p90/median ratio rose at every adopter, which is the signature I would least like to see.

**Rework.** Consistent −1.7 to −2.8 pp relative to controls at all three adopters, and the direction is stable across window choices. Range **−1 to −3 pp**, though part of it may be loops re-labelled as escalations (which rose ~+0.4 to +1.0 pp above controls).

**CSAT.** DiD of +0.04 to +0.10 on a 1–5 scale. With response rates of 20–45% and unknown counts, a monthly branch mean of ~200–400 responses has a standard error near 0.05, so **+0.10 is at best one standard error and is not distinguishable from nonresponse drift.** Not identified.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**`expand with an embedded evaluation`**

The evidence is too weak to justify immediate blanket expansion — the median effect could be zero, the tail effect could be negative, and CSAT is unidentified — but it is not weak enough to justify pausing: rework fell consistently, the dose-response is monotone within every adopter, and no harm signal is strong. The correct move is to expand in a way that produces an answer.

**Which branch next.** Delta only, in the first phase. Epsilon should be held as the control for at least three months. Epsilon is the more attractive target operationally (higher complexity, worse cycle times, deteriorating rework) — which is exactly why it must not go first: repeating the "treat the worst branch" pattern would reproduce the selection problem that makes the current data unreadable. Delta is the stable branch whose pre-period is clean.

**Assignment mechanism.** Within Delta, randomize at the level of the *case queue*, not the branch: assign incoming cases to automated vs manual triage by a pre-registered pseudo-random rule (e.g. hash of case ID) at a 50/50 ratio for eight weeks, stratified by registration risk class. This converts an unidentifiable branch-level comparison into a within-branch randomized contrast that is immune to seasonality, staffing changes, complexity shocks and manager co-interventions — all of which hit both arms equally. If case-level randomization is operationally impossible, use a stepped-wedge across Delta's teams with randomized start weeks. Freeze staffing and routing for the duration; if a shock is unavoidable, log it.

**Measurement windows.** Minimum three months of pre-period at the new instrumentation standard before switch-on (the current three pre-months for Alpha/Beta are the reason nothing is identified) and six months post, so the learning curve is separated from the steady state. Report event-time months 0–5 separately; do not pool.

**Process-mining and queue data to collect.** Case-level event logs with timestamps for Register, Triage (with the triage *decision* and which engine produced it), Specialist Review, Rework loops, Resolve, Close. Per-activity waiting vs service time. Fraud-desk arrivals, queue length and waiting time *per originating branch* — this is the single largest gap in the present data. Full cycle-time distributions, not summaries, so median and tail can be analysed separately and variant-level throughput compared. Survey response counts and the case ID of each respondent.

**Spillover monitoring.** Weekly fraud-desk and specialist-queue waiting time by originating branch, with a pre-registered alert if untreated branches' p90 rises more than 10% above their pre-period. This tests directly whether adopters gain by exporting delay.

**Guardrails.** Halt automated routing and revert to manual triage if, over any rolling four weeks: p90 cycle time in the automated arm exceeds the manual arm by >10%; escalation rises more than 3 pp; rework rises at all; CSAT among automated-arm respondents falls more than 0.15 with response counts reported; or any complaint/appeal rate rises.

**Scale-up decision rule.** Extend to Epsilon and beyond only if, after the eight-week randomized phase, the automated arm shows a median reduction of at least 5% **and** a p90 that is no worse than the manual arm **and** no guardrail breach **and** no detected fraud-desk spillover. If the median improves but the tail worsens, the finding is process substitution rather than capacity creation: redesign the escalation path and re-test rather than scaling.

## 10. Final claim ladder

- `Observed:` All three adopting branches raised automated fast-path completion from ~4–6% to 25–50% within one to five months, and each showed a smaller rise in `rework_pct` (−1.7 to −2.8 pp) and a larger rise in `escalation_pct` (+0.4 to +1.0 pp) than the never-adopting branches over the same calendar windows — while median and p90 cycle time moved in *opposite* directions across cohorts (Alpha/Beta p90 up ~10%, Gamma p90 down ~15%).
- `Suggestive:` Higher actual fast-path intensity is associated with lower median cycle time within every adopter (≈ −0.10 to −0.25 h per percentage point), consistent with genuine substitution of easy cases onto a faster route rather than with a system-wide capacity gain.
- `Conditionally causal:` If parallel trends hold against the never-treated branches, if the shared fraud desk did not degrade those controls, and if Gamma's post-period is not simply reversion to its pre-May baseline, then automation reduced median cycle time by roughly 5% (range 0% to −12%) and rework by 1–3 pp in the branch that adopted under stable operating conditions.
- `Not supported:` "Automation reduces cycle time by X%" as a single organization-wide number, and any claim that it improves worst-case (p90) turnaround or customer satisfaction — the tail effect's sign is not established, the p90/median ratio widened at every adopter, and CSAT movements of ≤0.10 points are indistinguishable from survey nonresponse drift with counts unavailable.
