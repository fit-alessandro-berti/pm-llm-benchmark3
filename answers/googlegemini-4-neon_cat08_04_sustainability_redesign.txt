# 1. System boundary and assumptions

## Environmental scope (Inside the boundary)
This analysis uses an **operational gate-to-customer-delivery boundary** anchored to the observed process-mining event log (`Receive order` through `Confirm delivery` and `Close`), covering all 12,000 orders across the five product–customer segments. Direct and process-induced environmental impacts inside the boundary include:
* **Manufacturing and inspection energy (`energy_kwh`):** Electricity and thermal energy consumed in `CNC_A`, `CNC_B`, `heat_treat`, and `inspection_cell`, including repeated energy use from `Rework production` (470 transitions) and unbatched `Emergency production` (760 transitions). Across the 12,000 orders, Made-to-order (MTO) accounts for ~87% of total logged energy consumption.
* **Production material scrap (`material_scrap_kg`):** Metal and part waste generated during machining, heat treatment, and rework loops. MTO accounts for ~85% of total logged scrap.
* **Packaging material (`package_material_kg`):** Material consumed during `Package standard`, `Package reinforced` (which doubles packaging mass), and `Rework packaging` (620 total rework events).
* **Outbound transport emissions (`transport_kgco2e`):** Direct freight emissions across `parcel_ground`, `parcel_air`, `dedicated_van`, and `consolidated_truck`, including second-leg emissions from `Partial shipment` (1,010 orders). `Critical-downtime Service-contract` orders represent only 11% of volume but generate ~41% of total outbound transport emissions.
* **Inventory obsolescence waste (partially captured):** Finished goods from batch production that remain unshipped beyond 14 days (6% of batch-produced items) represent wasted embodied energy and material inside the fulfillment boundary.

## Outside the boundary
The following lifecycle stages are excluded from direct event-log quantification, though they inform risk assessment qualitatively:
* **Upstream embodied impacts:** Raw-material extraction, metal refining, packaging manufacturing, and inbound supplier transport.
* **Downstream customer-site impacts:** Environmental and economic consequences of unscheduled industrial plant downtime (e.g., spoiled customer production runs, idle machinery, emergency generator use) if a `Critical-downtime` part arrives late.
* **Reverse logistics and end-of-life (EoL):** Return transport emissions for damage-related customer returns, recycling vs. landfill disposal of `material_scrap_kg`, obsolete batch stock, and spent packaging.
* **Facility overhead:** Warehouse lighting, HVAC, IT infrastructure, and machine standby power during `Batch wait` or `Backorder wait`.

## Key assumptions and remaining uncertainties
1. **No forced single-metric conversion:** The log reports `energy_kwh`, `transport_kgco2e`, `material_scrap_kg`, and `package_material_kg` in native physical units without grid emission factors (kgCO2e/kWh) or embodied material factors (kgCO2e/kg). Trade-offs across categories are therefore evaluated on a multi-criteria basis rather than collapsed into an unsupported composite CO2e figure.
2. **Order-level flattening hides line-item detail:** Each case is flattened to an order containing one or more line items. This obscures item-level weights (needed to audit Quality Policy Q1’s >8 kg threshold), exact split ratios in `Partial shipment`, and per-item energy/scrap attribution.
3. **Empty backhaul accounting uncertainty:** Dedicated vans often return empty; it is uncertain whether `transport_kgco2e` already allocates empty return legs or only outbound distance.
4. **Incomplete post-delivery and truncation visibility:** Of 12,000 orders, 11,200 reach `Close` and 530 reach `Cancel due to delay`, leaving ~270 orders in-flight or unclosed (e.g., `Rework production` has 470 incoming vs. 390 outgoing to `CNC production`). Furthermore, customer returns triggered by Q1 violations or rushed inspections are noted in process evidence but do not appear as explicit post-`Confirm delivery` DFG activities.
5. **Low model precision under disruption:** Emergency paths and partial shipments combine in ad-hoc ways during disruptions, meaning historical averages likely understate tail-risk variance during carrier or machine outages.

# 3. Intervention options

Eleven candidate interventions from the log-grounded backlog (S1–S11) are evaluated below. (S12, the sustainability exception log, is incorporated as a cross-cutting governance mechanism in Sections 6 and 7.)

### S1. Green shipping recommender (promised lead time, route reliability, segment)
* **Target path or segment:** Outbound shipping mode decision (`Package standard/reinforced -> Ship ground / Ship air / Consolidated truck / Dedicated van`) across all segments, especially the 1,420 `Ship air` and 620 `Dedicated van` shipments.
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Calculates probabilistic on-time delivery using `promised_lead_time_hours`, remaining slack, and empirical carrier transit distributions, enforcing SP1 (>85% on-time threshold) to divert low-urgency air shipments (4–6x CO2e) to `parcel_ground` or `consolidated_truck` (-35–55% CO2e vs. ground).
* **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Protects segment SLAs by preserving air/expedited shipping whenever slack is genuinely tight. Enhances R1 resilience if integrated with carrier disruption feeds so the recommender automatically switches to validated backup routes rather than failing late. Poorly calibrated reliability estimates could increase late deliveries.
* **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** SP1 conformance rate, predicted vs. actual transit times by `route_mode`, `expedite_flag` override frequency, and late-delivery rates on mode-shifted orders.

### S2. Dynamic batching for CNC and heat treatment with segment-specific maximum wait-time caps
* **Target path or segment:** `Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC production -> Heat treatment` (3,020 batch-wait orders across MTO Regular [22%] and MTO Service-contract [12%]).
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Captures lower energy per item (`CNC_A` falls from 2.8 to 1.3 kWh/item at batch 12–20; `heat_treat` is most efficient above batch 10) while enforcing hard wait-time caps (preventing 38h+ delays) and prohibiting speculative make-to-stock batch padding that drives the 6% 14-day obsolescence rate.
* **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Mitigates the 1.7x late-delivery penalty associated with heat-treatment batching and reduces `Cancel due to delay` (530 total cases), particularly for MTO Service-contract (currently 13% late) and MTO Regular (18% late). Maintains flexibility to release urgent batches early during disruptions.
* **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `batch_size` distributions, `Batch wait` median/p90 duration by segment, `energy_kwh` per item, late-delivery rate on heat-treated orders, and 14-day unshipped inventory rate by `batch_id`.

### S3. Shift Made-to-order production from `CNC_B` to `CNC_A` when queue and due-date slack allow
* **Target path or segment:** MTO machining path (`Plan production / Batch wait -> CNC_B`), specifically targeting the high-impact `MTO -> batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground` variant (5% of all orders; 32.0 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap, 160h cycle time, 32% late).
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Halves the MTO machining rework rate (`CNC_B` 18% vs. `CNC_A` 9%), directly preventing double-machining energy and heavy `material_scrap_kg` waste (1.8 vs. 0.7 kg/order).
* **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Improves first-pass quality, cuts extreme 160h rework tail delays, and reduces congestion at `inspection_cell`. However, unconstrained shifting could overload `CNC_A`, extending wait times and hurting MTO lead times; retaining `CNC_B` for peak surge preserves capacity resilience.
* **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Queue times and utilization on `CNC_A` vs. `CNC_B`, `Inspection -> Rework production` rates by `machine_group`, `material_scrap_kg`, `energy_kwh`, and MTO p90 cycle times.

### S4. Replace air for Standard-stock Service-contract orders with priority pick + ground (when slack allows)
* **Target path or segment:** `Standard-stock Service-contract` orders (16% of orders; 7.8 kgCO2e/order vs. 4.2 for Regular; 22% SP1 violations) traversing `Pick from stock -> Package standard -> Ship air` (where air averages 18.5 vs. 3.6 kgCO2e for ground).
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Compresses internal warehouse handling time via priority picking so orders meet `promised_lead_time_hours` via `parcel_ground` despite ground transit being ~24h slower than air, avoiding the 4–6x emission penalty of `parcel_air`.
* **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Preserves Service-contract SLA (5% late; median CT 18h, p90 41h) when slack is verified, while keeping air authorized when slack is insufficient or ground lanes are disrupted (satisfying R1). Risk of starving Regular stock picking queues if priority labor is not buffered.
* **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Internal picking/packing lead time (`Availability check -> Ship`), SP1 violation rate in `Standard-stock SC`, air-to-ground volume shift, `transport_kgco2e`, and late-delivery rates for both Service-contract and Regular stock orders.

### S5. Consolidate truck departures twice daily for non-urgent stock orders
* **Target path or segment:** `Standard-stock Regular` orders (39% of volume; median CT 31h, p90 62h) shifting from `Package standard -> Ship ground` (5,100 orders) to `Consolidated truck` (currently 850 orders).
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Reduces outbound `transport_kgco2e` by ~35–55% per shifted order compared to individual `parcel_ground` shipments via higher load factors. Twice-daily departures compress the typical 12–24h consolidation wait toward the lower end.
* **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Adds moderate pre-dispatch wait time; acceptable for Regular orders with ample slack (currently 7% late) if picking waves align with truck cutoffs, but risky for tight-slack orders. Diversifies carrier dependence away from parcel hubs (aiding R1), though missed cutoffs add half-day penalties.
* **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Wait time between `Package standard` and `Consolidated truck`, share of Regular stock orders consolidated, `transport_kgco2e`, and Regular stock late-delivery and p90 cycle time.

### S6. Pre-packaging route and geometry compatibility checks
* **Target path or segment:** `Package standard` and `Package reinforced` transitions into `Rework packaging` (260 + 360 = 620 packaging rework events) and Q1 policy violations.
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Eliminates wasted `package_material_kg` from unpacking and repacking route- or geometry-mismatched orders, prevents unnecessary double-packaging on low-risk items, and enforces Q1 on heavy MTO parts (>8 kg) to avoid high-impact replacement production and return transport.
* **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Protects the 40% reduction in damage-related returns from reinforced packaging on heavy MTO parts, removes pre-shipment rework delays that trigger last-minute `expedite_flag` air upgrades, and stabilizes dispatch schedules.
* **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `Rework packaging` event counts (from both standard and reinforced paths), `package_material_kg` per order, Q1 conformance rate, damage-related return rate, and post-rework expedite frequency.

### S7. Targeted quality gate and capacity de-bottlenecking at `inspection_cell` for high-rework combinations
* **Target path or segment:** `CNC production / Heat treatment -> Inspection -> Rework production` (470 rework transitions) and queued `inspection_cell` operations, prioritizing high-rework `CNC_B` / MTO part combinations.
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Catches machining defects *before* energy-intensive `Heat treatment` and `Package reinforced`, preventing wasted thermal energy, scrapped rework material, and downstream customer returns caused by rushed inspections under queue pressure.
* **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Directly elevates product quality and prevents multi-day rework loops (160h CT) and escaped defects. Adds minor inspection time on targeted high-risk parts, so de-bottlenecking `inspection_cell` capacity is necessary to avoid extending MTO cycle times.
* **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `inspection_cell` queue time and throughput, defects caught pre- vs. post-`Heat treatment`, `Inspection -> Rework production` rate, `material_scrap_kg`, and escaped-defect/return rates.

### S8. Replace eligible dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes
* **Target path or segment:** `Package reinforced -> Dedicated van` (620 orders; the `Critical -> emergency production -> reinforced pack -> dedicated van` path represents 8% of volume at 52.0 kgCO2e/order).
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Consolidates emergency deliveries across shared regional courier corridors and eliminates empty return legs ("dedicated vans often return empty"), lowering per-order `transport_kgco2e` without dropping to slow parcel ground.
* **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Preserves high-speed delivery on dense industrial corridors while retaining dedicated vans for remote sites or severe carrier disruptions (preserving R1). Introduces slight transit-time variance from multi-stop consolidation if deployed on ultra-tight plant-down windows.
* **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Mode split between `Dedicated van` and shared emergency lanes, empty-return utilization metrics, `transport_kgco2e` for Critical-downtime, and Critical-downtime late delivery (baseline 4%) and p90 cycle time (baseline 30h).

### S9. Triage `Critical-downtime` into true plant-down vs. precautionary urgent categories
* **Target path or segment:** `Critical-downtime Service-contract` orders (11% of orders; 38.0 kgCO2e/order avg), targeting the 31% of `Dedicated van` shipments missing SP2 justification (~192 orders) and `Emergency production` (760 orders).
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Enforces SP2 at `Receive order` by requiring explicit downtime verification. Precautionary urgent orders shift to cleaner fast paths (priority stock/ground, shared emergency courier, or scheduled CNC slots instead of low-batch `Emergency production`), cutting both 52 kgCO2e dedicated-van runs and 7.0 kWh emergency production runs.
* **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** *Strengthens* service and resilience for genuine plant-down emergencies by freeing `Emergency production` and dedicated vans from precautionary crowding. Must include fast-track verification so genuine emergencies are never delayed by administrative friction.
* **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** SP2 justification compliance rate, order split between true plant-down and precautionary urgent, `Emergency production` and `Dedicated van` volumes, and Critical-downtime late delivery and customer escalation counts.

### S10. Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular Made-to-order customers
* **Target path or segment:** `Made-to-order Regular` orders (22% of volume; 94h median CT, 180h p90, 18% late; 18.5 kWh, 8.0 kgCO2e), especially orders taking `MTO -> CNC immediately -> air` (6% of orders; 24.0 kWh, 23.0 kgCO2e).
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Offers Regular MTO customers transparent, slightly longer "green" delivery windows synchronized with `CNC_A` batches, heat-treatment cycles, and ground/consolidated shipping—creating upstream lead-time slack that unlocks S2, S3, and S5 while reducing immediate unbatched machining and air freight.
* **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Improves delivery reliability (cutting the 18% late rate and `Cancel due to delay`) by replacing aggressive, frequently missed promises with realistic commitments, while keeping a paid rush tier for genuinely urgent Regular demand. Risk of order defection if extended windows are poorly priced or communicated.
* **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Green-window opt-in rate, `promised_lead_time_hours` distribution, shift away from `CNC immediately + air`, `Cancel due to delay` rate, and MTO Regular late-delivery rate.

### S11. Revise availability-check rules to reduce partial shipments
* **Target path or segment:** `Availability check -> Pick from stock -> Partial shipment` (1,010 orders; 7% of total volume follows `Stock -> partial shipment -> ground + backorder` with 79h CT, 24% late, 7.2 kgCO2e vs. 3.6 kgCO2e for single ground).
* **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Prevents duplicate outbound transport legs and double packaging (`package_material_kg`) by briefly holding multi-line stock orders when missing lines have imminent replenishment within `promised_lead_time_hours`, rather than defaulting to automatic split shipments.
* **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Lowers duplicate-shipment costs, customer contact volume, and backorder coordination errors. However, rigid hold rules could delay an urgently needed in-stock line item or push orders into `Backorder wait -> Cancel due to delay` (60 cases); `Critical-downtime` and explicit customer split requests must remain exempt.
* **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `Partial shipment` and `Backorder wait` transition counts, `Backorder wait -> Cancel due to delay` rate, `transport_kgco2e` and `package_material_kg` per multi-line order, and stock late-delivery rate.

# 5. Rebound and displacement risks

Sustainability interventions optimized in isolation routinely displace impact across time, process steps, or customer behavior. Seven concrete risks emerge from this event log:

1. **Batching-to-Expediting Rebound (Energy vs. Transport):** Expanding batch sizes on `CNC_A` (up to 12–20) and `heat_treat` (>10) lowers machining energy per item, but pushes median `Batch wait` from 4h to 38h and increases heat-treatment late-delivery risk by 1.7x. When buffered orders approach `promised_lead_time_hours`, planners routinely rescue them via `Ship air` (4–6x CO2e) or `Dedicated van` (52 kgCO2e). The resulting transport emission spike can overwhelm the saved kWh, or—if not expedited—trigger `Cancel due to delay` (already 530 cases), turning partially completed orders into pure waste.
2. **Obsolescence Displacement (Energy Efficiency vs. Material Waste):** If planners inflate `batch_size` using speculative demand forecasts to reach energy-optimal runs on `CNC_A` or `heat_treat`, the observed 6% 14-day unshipped obsolescence rate increases. Writing off obsolete metal spare parts wastes the upstream embodied material, machining energy, and heat-treatment energy far more severely than running slightly smaller make-to-order batches.
3. **Transport Consolidation & Priority-Pick Displacement (Warehouse Queue & Carrier Cutoff Risk):** Shifting `Standard-stock SC` to priority pick + ground (S4) and `Standard-stock Regular` to twice-daily consolidated trucks (S5) compresses internal picking margins. Without dedicated picking capacity buffers, priority Service-contract picks crowd out Regular picks, causing Regular orders to miss consolidated truck cutoffs and slip past lead-time promises—triggering compensatory `expedite_flag` parcel-air shipments or repeat customer follow-ups.
4. **Packaging Reduction-to-Damage Return Displacement:** Attempting to minimize `package_material_kg` by cutting back on `Package reinforced` (which doubles packaging mass) risks violating Q1 on heavy MTO parts (>8 kg) and forfeiting the 40% reduction in damage-related returns. A single damaged MTO return triggers reverse freight, a replacement manufacturing cycle (~16–24 kWh and ~0.7–0.9 kg scrap), duplicate packaging, and urgent reshipment—vastly outweighing the saved packaging material.
5. **Rework & Machine-Shift Bottleneck Displacement:** Routing MTO volume away from high-rework `CNC_B` (18% rework) onto `CNC_A` (9% rework) (S3) and adding quality gates (S7) without dynamic capacity balancing can saturate `CNC_A` and `inspection_cell`. Log evidence already shows that `inspection_cell` queues trigger rushed inspections, allowing defects to escape into `Rework packaging` or post-delivery returns—shifting visible internal scrap into higher-impact downstream failures.
6. **Customer Behavior & Gaming Rebound ("Urgency Inflation" and Hoarding):** Tightening SP2 rules via Critical-downtime triage (S9) or extending lead times via green MTO promises (S10) and consolidated shipping (S5) can alter customer ordering behavior. Service-contract accounts may misclassify precautionary orders as "true plant-down" to secure `Emergency production` and `Dedicated van` service, while Regular customers facing longer or consolidated delivery windows may inflate order quantities to hold defensive buffer stock—raising system-wide production volume and obsolescence.
7. **Partial-Shipment Hold-to-Cancellation Displacement:** Holding multi-line stock orders under S11 to avoid duplicate ground freight (7.2 vs. 3.6 kgCO2e) can strand an immediately needed in-stock component behind a slow backorder. If replenishment slips, the entire order enters `Backorder wait -> Cancel due to delay` (60 cases in baseline) or provokes an emergency air order from the customer for the in-stock part, worsening both service and emissions.

# 6. Balanced recommendation

Rather than pursuing the lowest-emission path regardless of operational damage (e.g., banning air freight, eliminating dedicated vans, maximizing batch sizes unconditionally, or stripping reinforced packaging), the distributor should implement a **coherent four-pillar redesign package** that targets the two primary environmental hotspots—**outbound express transport** and **MTO energy/rework scrap**—while explicitly embedding service, quality, and resilience guardrails.

## The Integrated Redesign Package

### Pillar 1: Zero-Regret Waste & Quality Elimination (`S6 + S7 + queue-gated S3`)
* **Actions:** Introduce automated pre-packaging route/geometry checks enforcing Q1 (`S6`); add targeted pre-`Heat treatment` quality checks and de-bottleneck `inspection_cell` capacity (`S7`); steer MTO orders from `CNC_B` to `CNC_A` only when `CNC_A` queue time and due-date slack permit (`S3`).
* **Why it balances goals:** Eliminating 620 `Rework packaging` loops and cutting `CNC_B`'s 18% rework rate reduces `energy_kwh`, `material_scrap_kg`, `package_material_kg`, and late deliveries *simultaneously*. Preserving `Package reinforced` for >8 kg MTO parts protects the 40% damage-return reduction, while retaining `CNC_B` for peak surges preserves capacity resilience.

### Pillar 2: Slack-Aware Transport Optimization (`S4 + S1 + selective S5 + guarded S11`)
* **Actions:** Replace unjustified `parcel_air` in `Standard-stock Service-contract` (22% SP1 violations) with priority pick + `parcel_ground` whenever slack allows (`S4`), governed by the green shipping recommender enforcing SP1's >85% on-time threshold (`S1`). Launch twice-daily consolidated truck departures for `Standard-stock Regular` orders with sufficient slack (`S5`), and hold multi-line stock orders from `Partial shipment` (`S11`) only when backordered items replenish within a short threshold inside `promised_lead_time_hours` (exempting `Critical-downtime` and explicit customer split requests).
* **Why it balances goals:** Captures major transport CO2e reductions without imposing blanket mode bans. `parcel_air` remains authorized whenever slack is insufficient or ground carriers are disrupted—directly safeguarding Service-contract SLAs (<=5% late) and Resilience Policy R1.

### Pillar 3: Tiered Emergency Triage & Cleaner Express Capacity (`S9 + S8`)
* **Actions:** Split `Critical-downtime` at intake into **Tier 1 (True Plant-Down)** and **Tier 2 (Precautionary Urgent)** with mandatory SP2 justification (`S9`). Route Tier 2 orders to priority stock/ground, scheduled fast-track CNC, or shared emergency courier lanes (`S8`), while deploying `S8` on dense corridors for eligible Tier 1 shipments and reserving `Dedicated van` + `Emergency production` for verified extreme emergencies and remote sites.
* **Why it balances goals:** Directly addresses the ~41% transport emission share of Critical-downtime and the 31% missing SP2 justifications while *improving* true emergency resilience: removing precautionary noise frees dedicated vans and emergency CNC slots for genuine factory stoppages (protecting <=4% late and <=30h p90 CT) and avoids severe downstream customer downtime impacts.

### Pillar 4: Synchronized MTO Demand & Production Buffering (`S10 + S2 + S12`)
* **Actions:** Offer voluntary carbon-aware promise windows to `MTO Regular` customers (`S10`) aligned with dynamic `CNC_A` and `heat_treat` batch cycles governed by segment-specific maximum wait-time caps and a strict ban on speculative forecast batch padding (`S2`). Track all overrides of SP1, SP2, Q1, and batch caps in a unified sustainability exception log (`S12`).
* **Why it balances goals:** `S10` creates legitimate lead-time slack up front so `S2` can capture batch energy gains (2.81.3 kWh/item on `CNC_A`) without triggering the 1.7x heat-treat late penalty, 6% obsolescence waste, or panic-air rebound.

## Recommended Rollout Sequence
1. **Phase 1 (Months 1–3 — Foundations & Quick Wins):** Deploy `S6`, `S7`, `S4`, and `S12` (stop packaging/inspection rework, eliminate obvious SC air violations with priority pick, and upgrade event-log tracking).
2. **Phase 2 (Months 4–6 — Triage & Dynamic Routing):** Deploy `S1`, queue-gated `S3`, `S9`, and guarded `S11` (operationalize SP1/SP2 logic, steer MTO to `CNC_A`, curb avoidable split shipments).
3. **Phase 3 (Months 7–12 — Network & Demand Synchronization):** Deploy `S2`, `S5`, `S8`, and `S10` (roll out dynamic batch caps, twice-daily consolidated trucks, shared emergency courier corridors, and Regular MTO green promise-date negotiation once baseline flows stabilize).

# 7. Validation and governance plan

## 1. Event-log instrumentation upgrades (Pre-validation prerequisites)
Before evaluating post-implementation impact, extend the event log to resolve current boundary and precision gaps:
* **Line-item linkage:** Add `line_item_id` and `part_weight_kg` linked to `order_id` so Q1 (>8 kg) conformance and `Partial shipment` splits can be audited accurately.
* **New process events and attributes:** Log `Emergency triage tier` (`S9`), `SP2_justification_code`, `Promised_window_type` (`S10`), `Packaging_check_result` (`S6`), `Override_reason_code` (`S12`), post-delivery `Customer_damage_return`, and 14-day `Batch_obsolescence_flag`.
* **Disruption tagging:** Record carrier disruption windows and active alternative routes to measure R1 conformance and isolate disruption-induced low model precision from steady-state behavior.

## 2. Post-implementation validation methodology
Validate each phase using **controlled pilots** (e.g., stepped-wedge rollout by regional lane for `S4`/`S5`/`S8`, and by MTO part family for `S2`/`S3`/`S7`) combined with before/after process-mining conformance, variant, and bottleneck comparisons against the 12,000-order baseline:
* **Environmental verification:** Confirm statistically meaningful reductions in segment-normalized `transport_kgco2e`, `energy_kwh`, `material_scrap_kg`, `package_material_kg`, and 14-day batch obsolescence—checking explicitly for **cross-path rebound** (e.g., verifying that `S2` batching does not raise `expedite_flag` or `Ship air` downstream).
* **Multi-criteria release gates (Non-negotiable guardrails):** Do not scale a pilot to full network rollout if any guardrail breaches threshold:
  * `Critical-downtime` late delivery >4% or p90 cycle time >30h.
  * `Standard-stock Service-contract` late delivery >5% or `Regular` late delivery >8% (watching for priority-pick displacement).
  * `MTO` late delivery or `Cancel due to delay` (>4.4% baseline) worsens.
  * Damage-related customer returns or 14-day obsolescence (>6% baseline) increases.
  * Discovered model precision worsens further during disruption windows.

## 3. Automated conformance monitoring (SP1, SP2, Q1, R1)
Run automated weekly conformance checks directly on the enriched event log:
* **SP1 (Shipping mode):** Flag every `parcel_air` order whose pre-shipment slack exceeded the >85% ground/priority-pick on-time threshold. Target: reduce `Standard-stock SC` violations from 22% to <5%.
* **SP2 (Dedicated van justification):** Enforce a mandatory workflow capture at dispatch and audit weekly for missing or invalid justifications. Target: reduce missing justifications from 31% to 0%, and monitor the Tier 1 vs. Tier 2 ratio by customer account to detect urgency inflation.
* **Q1 (Heavy MTO packaging):** Cross-check `part_weight_kg > 8` against `Package reinforced` (or logged inspection certification) and correlate any non-conformance with post-delivery damage returns.
* **R1 (Carrier resilience):** Audit monthly that every `Service-contract` region maintains 1 active alternative `route_mode`/carrier tested during live or simulated disruption events.

## 4. Segment-level service and rework monitoring
Publish a weekly process-mining scorecard stratified across all five segments (`Standard-stock Regular/SC`, `MTO Regular/SC`, `Critical-downtime SC`) tracking:
* Median and p90 cycle time, late-delivery %, and `Cancel due to delay` rate.
* `Partial shipment` and `Backorder wait` rates (monitoring `S11` hold side effects).
* `CNC_A` vs. `CNC_B` queue times, `inspection_cell` wait times, `Rework production` loop counts, and `Rework packaging` counts.

## 5. Exception governance (`S12`) and continuous recalibration
* **Structured Sustainability Exception Log (`S12`):** Every manual override of `S1` recommendations, SP1/SP2/Q1 rules, `S2` batch wait caps, or `S11` partial-shipment holds requires a standardized reason code (*carrier disruption*, *verified plant-down escalation*, *machine breakdown*, *inventory inaccuracy*, *commercial exception*).
* **Monthly Cross-Functional Governance Review:** Representatives from Production, Logistics, Quality, Account Management, and Process Mining review conformance and exception-log trends monthly:
  * **Systemic operational overrides** (e.g., repeated SP1 overrides on a degrading ground lane) trigger parameter updates in `S1`, wait-cap tuning in `S2`, or capacity adjustments at `CNC_A`/`inspection_cell`.
  * **Behavioral or commercial overrides** (e.g., repeated unjustified Tier 1 claims by specific accounts) trigger account-level SLA reviews, transparent CO2/cost reporting, or tiered expediting fees—preventing gradual erosion of sustainability gains over time.
