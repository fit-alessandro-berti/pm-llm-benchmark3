## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### Stipulated facts (established by the provided materials)

- CS-9 (effective 2025-01-01 onward) requires: (i) Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review before activation; (ii) Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier; and (iii) requester and manual approver must be different humans (SoD), which is not represented in M0.
- M0’s control-flow requires: Register_Supplier → (Sanctions_Check ∥ Quality_Review) → XOR( (Manual_Procurement_Approval → Activate_Supplier), Reject_Supplier ) → Notify_Requester → Close_Case.
- PD-3 pilot (effective 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31, not incorporated into CS-9 or M0) states: (i) for LOW-risk DE suppliers in an approved corporate group, Auto_Approve may substitute for Manual_Procurement_Approval; (ii) Sanctions_Check and automated master-data validation must occur before activation; (iii) Quality_Review may occur up to two business days after provisional activation. A compliance observer described it as a “monitored exception,” later disputing that she approved weakening CS-9.
- FD-7 emergency directive (effective 2026-07-10 00:00 through 2026-07-12 23:59) states: (i) provisional activation may precede Quality_Review and manual procurement approval; (ii) Sanctions_Check must occur before activation; (iii) spend cap EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval; (iv) daily monitoring and Quality_Review within two business days are required. FD-7 expired automatically; no written extension is provided.
- From 2026-07-01, Notify_Requester moved to CRM; ~70% are mirrored into the onboarding log. CRM linkage is reliable when `notification_id` is present; older linkage via mutable text fields is weak evidence.
- Operations uses one or more Request_More_Info cycles; compliance agrees it is desirable; M0 has no such loop/activity. Aggregate evidence indicates none of these cases activated before the requested evidence arrived.
- Observed traces and contextual facts for SUP-701..SUP-715 are as stated, including the audit’s identification of SUP-703 and SUP-708 as potentially severe.

### Disputed propositions / unproved assumptions (must not be treated as established)

- **Authority and hierarchy**: whether PD-3 minutes/email constitute valid authority to weaken CS-9 versus merely authorizing a monitored, time-bounded exception; and what approvals are required for such weakening.
- **Expiry and extension**: whether FD-7 was extended (SUP-705) or whether “continued emergency” is an operational narrative without legal/policy authority.
- **Scope**: whether rejected leads (SUP-709) are governed by M0/CS-9 onboarding or are an intake/pre-screen process outside M0; and whether reactivation (SUP-710) is governed by onboarding rules or a separate procedure.
- **Identity and relationships**: reliability of alias resolution (SUP-712) and whether spouse/reporting-line relationships (SUP-715) trigger enforceable controls beyond the stated disclosure requirement.
- **Logging vs performance**: whether missing Notify_Requester events (SUP-702, SUP-706) reflect nonperformance or a known data collection break; and whether mutable CRM linkage is sufficient for audit.
- **Semantic mapping**: whether QA_Clearance (SUP-713) is fully equivalent to Quality_Review at execution time versus being a distinct control that was only later mapped.

### Burden of proof approach (for consistent case and system judgments)

- **Declaring NONCOMPLIANT** requires: (i) the case is in scope for the relevant normative regime; (ii) the applicable rule set version (CS-9 plus any authorized overlay) is identified for the case time; (iii) the deviation is a true control breach (not only a label/mapping or logging gap); and (iv) the evidence is sufficiently strong for the claimed severity (clear and convincing evidence for severe findings).
- **Accepting JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION** requires: (i) explicit authority reference (overlay rule id) with immutable effective window; (ii) an immutable eligibility snapshot at decision time (risk/country/group/criticality/spend); (iii) evidence of compensating controls and deadlines met; and (iv) auditable linkage (stable IDs, immutable timestamps).
- **DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE** is preferred over presuming failure when a required event is plausibly missing due to known collection changes (e.g., CRM mirroring), unless there is affirmative evidence that the action did not occur.
- **MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT** applies where the behavior is policy-aligned or explicitly desirable but cannot be expressed in M0 (e.g., Request_More_Info), or where scope boundaries are undefined (e.g., intake rejections vs onboarding, reactivations).

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest prosecution case treats CS-9 as the controlling normative source and M0 as its operationalization, with pilots/emergencies only valid when formally authorized, time-bounded, and evidenced case-by-case. On that view, the operation is materially noncompliant because (a) exceptions were normalized beyond their authority bounds, (b) core preconditions were violated in severe cases, and (c) auditability degraded without compensating controls.

### Formal authority, expiry, and “exceptions do not become policy”

- **CS-9 is controlled and effective** (2025-01-01 onward). PD-3 was not incorporated into CS-9 or M0, and even its compliance observer later denied approving any weakening. A steering-committee minute/email can at most authorize a monitored exception; it does not automatically amend a controlled compliance standard. Therefore, deviations that rely on PD-3 must carry strong authority evidence and strict eligibility proof; otherwise they remain noncompliant with CS-9.
- **FD-7 was explicitly time-bounded** and expired automatically. Continuing emergency-like bypasses after 2026-07-12 (SUP-705) without a written extension is a governance failure: “the flood still existed” is not a substitute for documented authority.
- **Desirable outcomes are not retroactive amendments**. Operations’ preference for a permanent auto-approval branch and the perceived success of the pilot do not legitimize post-pilot Auto_Approve (SUP-714; six August cases) absent a controlled amendment or extension.

### Material deviations and high-risk exemplars

- **SUP-703 (severe pilot misuse)**: a HIGH-risk foreign supplier was processed through Auto_Approve despite ineligibility, followed by EUR 180,000 spend. This is a direct violation of CS-9’s approval and pre-activation quality intent and a breakdown of any “low-risk only” rationale. It demonstrates that the Auto_Approve pathway is not reliably constrained in practice and can be used to bypass critical controls.
- **SUP-708 (core control breach)**: Activate_Supplier occurred ~40 minutes before Sanctions_Check. CS-9 and FD-7 both require sanctions clearance before activation; this breach is material regardless of whether transactions happened during the gap. The control objective is to prevent establishing a supplier relationship without sanctions clearance.
- **SUP-712 (SoD violation)**: even though the trace fits M0, CS-9 explicitly requires requester and manual approver to be different humans. Aliases resolving to the same person indicate a genuine compliance breach that M0 cannot detect—an under-specified model is not an excuse for violating the standard.

### Deviations “hidden” by M0’s incompleteness (prosecution acknowledges defects)

The prosecution must concede that M0 is incomplete: it omits SoD and conflict-of-interest controls, lacks Request_More_Info loops, does not encode pilot/emergency variants, and is undermined by notification logging changes. However, these defects do not explain away the most concerning findings:

- **Ineligible Auto_Approve usage** is evidenced both in the aggregate (14 clearly ineligible cases; 9 missing attributes) and in SUP-703. This is not a modeling problem; it is an eligibility control failure.
- **Post-expiry drift** is evidenced by SUP-714 and the six August Auto_Approve cases: governance and enforcement failed to shut off a time-bounded exception.
- **Emergency continuation without authority** (SUP-705) shows normalization of bypasses beyond the directive’s window.
- **Auditability degradation**: notification missingness rose to 29% after CRM migration. Even if notifications happened, the organization bears responsibility for maintaining auditable evidence for required activities; “not logged” is itself a control weakness when closures occur.

### Why enforcement is warranted now

Given severe exemplars (SUP-703, SUP-708), documented eligibility drift, and auditability gaps, the prosecution concludes that CS-9/M0 should be enforced, exceptions should be strictly gated and documented, and the organization should treat missing evidence as a control failure until the data pipeline is repaired.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense case separates “trace nonconformance to M0” from “business misconduct,” arguing that many apparent deviations are explained by (a) legitimate overlays (pilot/emergency) that were not incorporated into M0, (b) logging gaps introduced by system migration, and (c) scope categories not represented by M0 (intake rejections, reactivations). On this view, strict enforcement of M0 would produce false positives and punish desired, risk-reducing behaviors.

### M0 is obsolete/incomplete relative to real governing overlays

- **PD-3 and FD-7 are governing overlays in their windows**. Even if not folded into CS-9, they were issued by organizational authorities (steering committee; emergency directive) and explicitly define alternate control-flow and obligations. A conformance regime that ignores time-bounded overlays will misclassify compliant/justified behavior as noncompliant.
- **SUP-702 (pilot-eligible)**: LOW-risk DE supplier in approved group on 2026-06-15 used Auto_Approve; sanctions preceded activation; quality occurred next business day; CRM shows notification +2h with stable `notification_id`. Under PD-3, this is consistent (provisional activation allowed; quality within 2 days). M0’s “quality before activation” and “manual approval required” constraints are simply not the applicable expectations in that pilot context.
- **SUP-704 (emergency-eligible)**: on 2026-07-11 under FD-7, sanctions preceded activation; spend was EUR 18,000 under cap; monitoring exists; quality and approval occurred next business day. M0 is expected to flag this, but the directive intentionally changes the order to preserve continuity during crisis.

### Trace deviation vs logging/model problems

- **Request_More_Info is desirable and control-strengthening**. SUP-707 shows requests used to obtain missing certificates; aggregate evidence indicates none activated before evidence arrived. M0 cannot represent the loop; this is a model deficiency, not a compliance failure.
- **Notification evidence is degraded by system migration**. Since Notify_Requester moved to CRM and only ~70% are mirrored, missing notify events in the onboarding log are not reliable proof of nonperformance. SUP-702 has strong CRM evidence (stable `notification_id`); SUP-706 has weaker CRM linkage (mutable text field) and should be treated as an evidence-quality problem rather than assumed non-notification.
- **Label drift is a mapping issue**. SUP-713 uses QA_Clearance; audit documents show equivalence to Quality_Review and mapping was approved shortly after. Penalizing this as noncompliance would conflate semantic equivalence with labeling lag.
- **Duplicate Register_Supplier** (SUP-711) is a migration artifact (same source transaction ID). Treating it as process deviation misattributes ETL defects to operations.

### Scope categories not represented by M0

- **Rejected leads (SUP-709)**: the process owner says rejected leads are intake cases; the model owner says M0 begins after pre-screen acceptance; scope is ambiguous. Under a plausible intake scope, sanctions pre-screen rejection should not require Quality_Review.
- **Reactivation (SUP-710)**: reactivation lacks Register_Supplier and Quality_Review; prior quality certification remained valid; CS-9 contains no explicit reactivation procedure. This is a scope/model gap: a separate normative path is needed rather than forcing reactivation into new-onboarding semantics.

### Defense acknowledges genuine risk and limits of informal practice

The defense must concede: (i) ineligible pilot selection and severe spend (SUP-703) is not defensible; (ii) activation before sanctions (SUP-708) is unacceptable under any overlay; (iii) post-pilot Auto_Approve (SUP-714; six August cases) lacks authority; and (iv) SoD failures (SUP-712) are real violations that M0 cannot detect. The proper conclusion is not “ignore CS-9,” but “update the normative architecture and evidence collection so that enforcement targets real breaches rather than model/data artifacts.”

### System-level judgment (operations vs model/data)

- **Operational compliance**: The operation is **materially noncompliant in targeted, high-impact areas**—ineligible Auto_Approve selection (aggregate 14 cases + SUP-703), post-pilot continuation (SUP-714; six August cases), unauthorized post-expiry emergency continuation (SUP-705), activation before sanctions (SUP-708), and SoD failure (SUP-712). These require enforcement, remediation, and potentially disciplinary review for severe cases.
- **Model validity and evidence adequacy**: M0 is **obsolete/incomplete** for 2026: it cannot express pilot/emergency variants, Request_More_Info loops, resource rules, or scope categories (intake rejections, reactivations), and it assumes notification logging that was broken by the CRM migration. Conformance judged solely against M0 will overstate deviation and misdirect audit effort.

### Where each side’s argument is rejected

- I reject the **prosecution’s** claim (implicit in strict M0 enforcement) that most deviations demonstrate misconduct: SUP-702 and SUP-704 are consistent with time-bounded overlays; SUP-707 reflects desirable diligence; SUP-711 and SUP-713 are data/mapping defects; SUP-706 is evidence-fragile due to the CRM migration.
- I reject the **defense’s** attempt to treat obsolescence as a blanket excuse: SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, and SUP-714 remain noncompliant because they violate core controls or exceed authority bounds. Time-bounded exceptions do not become policy through repeated use.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

### Layered architecture (what belongs where)

1. **Revised control-flow (POWL)**
   - Encode structural variants: standard manual route (sanctions ∥ quality before activation), low-risk auto route, emergency/provisional route (sanctions before activation; retrospective approval/quality), and rejection route.
   - Include an explicit, repeatable Request_More_Info mechanism (loop) to avoid penalizing diligence.
   - Require notification before closure, but do not overconstrain its exact position relative to retrospective activities when overlays permit provisional activation.

2. **Declarative constraints (DECLARE / log-skeleton style)**
   - Use for conditional/temporal obligations that POWL cannot enforce well:
     - If auto/pilot route: Quality_Review within 2 business days after activation.
     - If emergency route: retrospective approval + quality within 2 business days; monitoring obligations.
     - Always: Notify_Requester occurs before Close_Case; inserted Request_More_Info can repeat without breaking compliance.

3. **Data-aware eligibility and threshold checks (pandas / rules engine)**
   - Auto-approval eligibility: `risk=LOW`, `country=DE`, `approved_group=True`, completeness of eligibility fields, and effective window checks.
   - Emergency eligibility: `critical_relief=True`, directive id, effective window, spend cap enforcement (`<= 20000`) until retrospective approval.
   - Detect and classify: ineligible route selection, missing eligibility attributes, deadline breaches, spend cap breaches.

4. **Resource, identity, and conflict-of-interest controls (organizational controls + data checks)**
   - SoD requester ≠ approver (CS-9), enforced via canonical person identity and alias resolution with provenance.
   - Delegation controls: require delegation evidence when an approval is performed under delegation.
   - COI controls: disclosure-required relationships; mitigation steps (alternate reviewer/approver) recorded and audited.

5. **Policy-overlay governance**
   - Maintain a **policy-overlay registry** with rule/version IDs, authority chain, immutable effective windows, scope predicates, compensating controls, and audit evidence requirements.
   - Each case must record the overlay `rule_id` at decision time; overlays expire automatically unless explicitly extended (preventing SUP-705/SUP-714 type drift).

6. **Explicit scope rules**
   - Define case types: `intake_prescreen`, `new_onboarding`, `reactivation`, `withdrawal`.
   - Route evaluation and normative expectations depend on case type:
     - Intake pre-screen rejection may be sanctions-only.
     - Reactivation may reuse valid certification subject to explicit validity evidence and policy.
     - Withdrawal/cancellation must be explicit to distinguish from incomplete/noncompliant execution.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

def T(label: str) -> Transition:
    return Transition(label=label)

def info_loop() -> OperatorPOWL:
    # Approximation of 0..n Request_More_Info:
    # PM4Py POWL LOOP supports exactly 2 children. With a silent "body" and a visible "redo",
    # the redo can repeat any number of times, yielding any number of Request_More_Info events.
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[SilentTransition(), T("Request_More_Info")])

# --- Standard manual-approval route (CS-9 baseline) ---
reg_s = T("Register_Supplier")
san_s = T("Sanctions_Check")
qua_s = T("Quality_Review")
pre_s = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[san_s, qua_s, info_loop()])  # sanctions || quality + optional info requests
man_s = T("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
act_s = T("Activate_Supplier")
not_s = T("Notify_Requester")
clo_s = T("Close_Case")

standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[reg_s, pre_s, man_s, act_s, not_s, clo_s])
standard_route.order.add_edge(reg_s, pre_s)
standard_route.order.add_edge(pre_s, man_s)
standard_route.order.add_edge(man_s, act_s)
standard_route.order.add_edge(act_s, not_s)
standard_route.order.add_edge(not_s, clo_s)

# --- Low-risk auto-approval route (pilot/permanent policy overlay) ---
reg_a = T("Register_Supplier")
san_a = T("Sanctions_Check")
mdv_a = T("Master_Data_Validation")
inf_a = info_loop()
aut_a = T("Auto_Approve")
act_a = T("Activate_Supplier")
qua_a = T("Quality_Review")       # may occur before or after activation; deadlines handled outside POWL
not_a = T("Notify_Requester")
clo_a = T("Close_Case")

auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[reg_a, san_a, mdv_a, inf_a, aut_a, act_a, qua_a, not_a, clo_a])
auto_route.order.add_edge(reg_a, san_a)
auto_route.order.add_edge(reg_a, mdv_a)
auto_route.order.add_edge(reg_a, inf_a)
auto_route.order.add_edge(reg_a, qua_a)
auto_route.order.add_edge(san_a, aut_a)
auto_route.order.add_edge(mdv_a, aut_a)
auto_route.order.add_edge(inf_a, aut_a)
auto_route.order.add_edge(aut_a, act_a)
auto_route.order.add_edge(san_a, act_a)      # sanctions before activation
auto_route.order.add_edge(mdv_a, act_a)      # validation before activation
auto_route.order.add_edge(act_a, not_a)      # notify after activation (may precede quality)
auto_route.order.add_edge(qua_a, clo_a)      # close after quality
auto_route.order.add_edge(not_a, clo_a)      # close after notification
auto_route.order.add_edge(act_a, clo_a)      # close after activation

# --- Emergency / provisional activation route (time-bounded overlay) ---
reg_e = T("Register_Supplier")
san_e = T("Sanctions_Check")
act_e = T("Activate_Supplier")               # provisional by overlay, not by label
man_e = T("Manual_Procurement_Approval")     # retrospective
qua_e = T("Quality_Review")                  # retrospective
not_e = T("Notify_Requester")
clo_e = T("Close_Case")

emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[reg_e, san_e, act_e, man_e, qua_e, not_e, clo_e])
emergency_route.order.add_edge(reg_e, san_e)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(san_e, act_e) # sanctions before activation even in emergency
emergency_route.order.add_edge(act_e, man_e)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(act_e, qua_e)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(act_e, not_e)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(man_e, clo_e)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(qua_e, clo_e)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(not_e, clo_e)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(act_e, clo_e)

# --- Rejection / pre-screen route ---
reg_r = T("Register_Supplier")
san_r = T("Sanctions_Check")
rej_r = T("Reject_Supplier")
not_r = T("Notify_Requester")
clo_r = T("Close_Case")

reject_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[reg_r, san_r, rej_r, not_r, clo_r])
reject_route.order.add_edge(reg_r, san_r)
reject_route.order.add_edge(san_r, rej_r)
reject_route.order.add_edge(rej_r, not_r)
reject_route.order.add_edge(not_r, clo_r)

# Exclusive choice among variants; eligibility/authority is enforced via overlays and data checks.
M1 = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[standard_route, auto_route, emergency_route, reject_route])
```

### Event and data-collection proposal (traceability and auditability)

- **Stable identifiers**
  - Immutable `case_id`, `supplier_id`, `request_id`.
  - Immutable per-row `event_id` and `source_tx_id` for deduplication (addresses SUP-711).
  - Immutable `notification_id` from CRM at send time, mirrored into the onboarding log (addresses SUP-702/SUP-706).

- **Immutable timestamps**
  - `event_time` (business time), `record_time` (ingestion), `effective_time` (overlay effective), all immutable.
  - Corrections must be appended as correction events; never overwrite mutable text link fields without retaining history.

- **Policy linkage and eligibility snapshots**
  - Record `rule_id`/`rule_version` at decision/activation time.
  - Store an immutable `eligibility_snapshot_hash` (risk, country, approved_group, critical_relief, spend forecast) to prevent post-hoc edits and to explain eligibility determinations (addresses aggregate “missing attributes”).

- **Control evidence fields**
  - Master-data validation: `md_validation_id`, `md_validation_passed`, input data hash.
  - Emergency spend controls: `initial_spend_amount`, `cap_applied_flag`, `retro_approval_id`, `monitoring_event_ids`.
  - Deadlines: computed fields `quality_deadline_met`, `retro_deadline_met` with supporting timestamps.

- **Identity, SoD, delegation, and COI**
  - Canonical `person_id` + `alias_id` mapping with provenance; store `requester_person_id`, `approver_person_id`, `quality_reviewer_person_id`.
  - Delegation evidence: `delegation_id`, validity window, approver role evidence.
  - COI: `coi_disclosure_id` (or explicit null + attestation), mitigation decision id.

- **Scope routing**
  - Add explicit `case_type` ∈ {intake_prescreen, new_onboarding, reactivation, withdrawal}.
  - For reactivation: store `prior_quality_cert_id`, `cert_valid_through`, and decision on whether re-check is required.
  - For withdrawal: log `Withdraw_Request` to distinguish abandoned cases from noncompliant incomplete execution.

- **Conformance reporting**
  - Produce separable outcomes: `control_flow_fit` (POWL), `data_rule_pass` (eligibility/deadlines/caps), `resource_rule_pass` (SoD/COI), and `evidence_completeness` (notification linkage, identity provenance), rather than collapsing all findings into a single deviation score.
