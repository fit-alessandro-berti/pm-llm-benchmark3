## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

### January–March: Baseline (stable, mature)
- **Status:** All metrics stable and reliable. Label maturity 100%, median delays 32–34 d, AUROC 0.81–0.82, Brier 0.130–0.132, ECE 0.022–0.025. Observed breach rates 0.18–0.19 match mean predicted risk. No unknown activity.
- **Explanation:** None required; this is the control period.

---

### April: Schema migration and pipeline failure (NOT process improvement or concept drift)
- **Observed:** AUROC collapses to 0.67 (0.14 from March), mean predicted risk jumps to 0.28 (vs 0.18), Brier +0.053, ECE +0.086. Unknown activity 17%, missing queue feature 14%. Yet observed breach rate remains 0.19 (stable).
- **Why this is NOT a model performance failure on real data:**
  - The mismatch between predicted risk (0.28) and observed breach (stable 0.19) is diagnostic of feature corruption or label misalignment, not worse cases.
  - 18% unmapped variants; if these are misclassified into wrong prefix events, predictions would be off-calibrated but breaches might still be recorded correctly (outcome is independent of prediction).
  - Migration renamed `Initial Diagnosis Completed`  `Diagnostic Assessment Completed`; feature pipeline initially recognized only the old label, creating gaps.
  - Duplicated events would inflate event counts, potentially skewing features like "time since first diagnosis activity."
- **Root cause:** Data pipeline, not concept or process.
- **Falsifying evidence:** If April's high predicted risk were justified and we later observed high breach rates among April's high-risk cases (after label maturity), this would indicate April actually had bad cases and the system detected them correctly. However, current evidence shows observed breach already known (0.19) with only 100% label maturity, so misprediction is the issue, not prescience.

---

### May: Partial recovery (patch applied, but recomputation incomplete)
- **Observed:** Unknown activity drops to 2%, missing queue feature to 3%. AUROC recovers to 0.78, Brier to 0.146, ECE to 0.050. Observed breach 0.18 (back to baseline).
- **Why this is partial recovery:**
  - The mapping patch deployed 6 May fixed the activity label and deduplicated *most* events, but "not all derived features were recomputed."
  - The cached "number of events before diagnosis" feature was not fully backfilled for April, meaning May might still inherit some April contamination.
  - AUROC still slightly below baseline (0.78 vs 0.81–0.82 in Jan–Mar); ECE (0.050) is elevated vs baseline (0.022–0.025).
- **Explanation:** Partial remediation of pipeline failure; residual cache corruption or missing backfill.
- **Expected signal:** If we recompute *all* features for April–May on the normalized, deduplicated stream, April's metrics should recover closer to baseline (AUROC 0.80), and May should show no degradation vs March.

---

### June: Genuine process shift—remote-routing policy change (NOT concept drift)
- **Observed:** V2 remote % jumps 3552%, V1 drops 4530%. Observed breach *improves* to 0.15 (best in 6 months). Mean predicted risk stable at 0.20, AUROC 0.79, Brier 0.139, ECE 0.060.
- **Process explanation:** New remote-routing policy (1 June) diverts more cases to V2 (Remote Fix). V2 historically has ~0.10 breach rate vs V1 ~0.16–0.17 and V3 ~0.30–0.35. Shifting mix to V2 mechanically *improves* overall breach rate.
- **Why this is NOT model degradation:**
  - AUROC and Brier remain good (0.79, 0.139). The model is discriminating well.
  - ECE (0.060) is slightly elevated, suggesting slight overconfidence, but acceptable.
  - The predicted risk (0.20) is close to the observed (0.15); small overestimate is plausible if the model hasn't yet observed the new mix weight and still uses historical V2 prevalence.
- **Falsifying evidence:** If V2's breach rate *within* June rose from 0.10 to (say) 0.15, and the model failed to detect this, that would indicate concept drift. Current stratified data (Table: Matured breach rate by variant) shows V2 breach June = 0.09 (actually improves slightly). So no concept drift.
- **Monitoring implication:** Do not retrain the model; instead, recalibrate predicted-risk scaling based on new mix proportions, or ensure model transparently reports predictions per-variant so downstream systems account for mix shift.

---

### July: Early warning of parts supplier issue (process degradation, not yet severe)
- **Observed:** Median OrderPart-Received time jumps 42 h (Jun)  65 h (Jul), a 55% increase. Label maturity remains high (98%). Observed breach 0.19 (back up from June), mean predicted risk 0.18, AUROC 0.76 (slight dip), Brier 0.151, ECE 0.042.
- **Process explanation:** Parts supplier began missing targets on 10 July. V3 cases (22% of volume) are now delayed in the OrderPart-Received step. Matured breach for V3 rises to 0.46 (from 0.35 in June).
- **Why this is process drift, not model failure:**
  - The model's predicted risk (0.18) vs observed (0.19) are well-aligned. The model is not surprised; it is capturing the supplier-delay signal correctly (via wait-time features or case characteristics correlated with parts-dependent flows).
  - AUROC decline (0.810.76) is a small deterioration, plausibly due to reduced variance in the population (most cases now face similar supplier delays, reducing discriminability of case-level features).
  - No label-delay crisis yet (98% maturity).
- **Falsifying evidence:** If AUROC had stayed 0.79 and breaches continued rising, that would suggest the model is blind to the new delay mechanism. Instead, AUROC does fall, consistent with a real but discriminable phenomenon.

---

### August–September: Compounding crises (process, concept, population)

#### August:
- **Observed:** V3 median wait 130 h (2.0× July), V3 breach 0.64 (vs 0.46 in July). Overall observed breach 0.28 (up from 0.19). Label maturity 93%, median delay 38 d. Mean predicted risk stays 0.19, AUROC collapses to 0.68, Brier 0.197, ECE 0.120.
- **Process evidence:** Supplier issue now severe.
- **Simultaneous problems:**
  1. **Process drift (dominant):** V3 wait times are objectively longer. Cases that historically resolved in ~3–4 days now take 5–6 days. Original SLA was presumably calibrated for 1–2 day supply chains. This is a legitimate exogenous shock, not model failure.
  2. **Possible concept drift (emergent):** V3 breach rate is now 0.64, a 76% increase from July (0.46). The model's predicted risk is still ~0.19 (unchanged). This gap suggests:
     - Either the model is blind to something that correlates with supply-chain delays (e.g., part type, region, technician availability in rural areas where Partner Z will later appear), or
     - The SLA changed or was violated systematically due to the supplier issue, and the model has never seen this regime.
  3. **Early population signal:** Partner Z is not yet onboarded (0% share), so no direct explanation yet. However, V1 and V2 are declining while V3 is increasing. This could be a natural response to the supplier crisis (fewer new cases routed to V3) or internal routing changes. No evidence of Partner Z pre-dating September.
- **Calibration shock:** ECE  0.042 (July)  0.120 (August), driven by mean predicted risk (0.19) being far below observed breach (0.28). The model is *underconfident* in bad outcomes.

#### September:
- **Observed:** Cases started 5,100 (21% jump from August). Label maturity drops to 82%, median delay 42 d. V3 share rises to 42% (from 35% in Aug). V1 drops to 22%, V2 to 36%. Partner Z enters at 24% of volume.
- **Observed breach:** 0.31 (up from 0.28 in August). V3 matured breach 0.61 (slightly down from 0.64), but *median delay has risen to 42 d*, so this rate is less trustworthy.
- **Multiple simultaneities:**
  1. **Process:** V3 wait times remain elevated (148 h, vs 130 h in August—further degradation). Supplier is not recovering.
  2. **Population shift:** Partner Z 24%, with 58% routed to V3 vs 36% for non-Partner Z. Partner Z cases are rural and involve older devices. Partner Z breach (0.45) vs non-Partner Z (0.27)—a 67% relative increase. *However*, Partner Z labels are less mature (single-digit days vs historical 35–40 d).
  3. **Concept drift hypothesis:** If Partner Z cases truly have higher inherent breach risk (due to rural complexity), the model has never seen this demographic and cannot predict it (features like rural distance and device age are not in the original model).
  4. **Label maturity crisis emerging:** 82% maturity (down from 93% in August) and rising median delay (42 d) mean September's observed breach is increasingly biased by censoring.
- **Calibration:** ECE 0.140 (unchanged from August), mean predicted risk 0.21 (up from 0.19), AUROC 0.66 (worse than August's 0.68).
- **Why we cannot conclude performance is bad:** The breach rate is rising, but the model's predictive metrics are consistent with a *harder* problem set (lower discriminability, higher overconfidence). The question is: is the problem harder because of missing features (Partner Z demographics) or because of label censoring (many high-risk cases unresolved)?

---

### October: Label-delay crisis dominates (confounded endpoint)
- **Observed:** Cases started 5,250 (small increase from September). Label maturity collapses to 55%, median delay 52 d (a 12 d jump). Outcome API backlog documented. Unknown activity 0.9% (stable).
- **Observed breach:** 0.16 (lowest since March—actually matches January). Mean predicted risk 0.23 (highest on record). AUROC 0.64 (worst on record), Brier 0.205, ECE 0.130.
- **Red flags of censoring:**
  - **Inversion:** Predicted risk  but observed breach . This is the classic signature of selective censoring: high-predicted-risk cases (which tend to be V3, which have longer paths) are over-represented in the unresolved cohort.
  - **Brier worsens despite fewer cases being scored:** If 45% of cases are still unresolved, the denominator is smaller but the predictions on resolved cases are worse, implying many false positives on the resolved subset.
  - **AUROC collapse:** With 45% missing labels, the AUC is computed on a non-random sample (resolved cases only). If resolution correlates with case complexity, this biases AUC downward.
  - **Backlog disproportionately affects V3:** The audit notes the API backlog "disproportionately affects cases with longer resolution paths," which is V3. V3 has high breach rates (0.61 in September matured), so removing V3 from the October cohort would mechanically lower observed breach.
- **Why this is diagnostic of censoring, not real performance:**
  - The relationship is predicted: if 70% of V3 cases and 20% of V2 cases remain unresolved, and V3 breach is 0.61 while V2 is 0.12, then October's observed breach (on resolved cases only) would be ~0.20 (much lower than truth).
  - Mean predicted risk (0.23) is not unreasonable for a harder case mix; it simply won't be validated until labels mature.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

### Test 1: Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting

**Objective:** Separate label censoring from real performance decline.

**Method:**
- For each cohort (e.g., "all cases started in August"), compute the observed breach rate at multiple reporting cutoffs (e.g., 30 d, 60 d, 90 d post-case-start) as more labels mature.
- Plot observed breach rate *vs.* label maturity % for August, September, October.
- Compute a Kaplan–Meier survival curve for "time to resolution" stratified by variant (V1, V2, V3) to quantify censoring by path type.

**Expected result under label censoring (October is true positive):**
- August cohort: observed breach converges to ~0.35–0.40 as maturity approaches 100% (currently 93% at August cutoff; mature estimate ~0.36 for V3 only or ~0.28 overall).
- September cohort: observed breach converges to ~0.32–0.38 (currently 0.31 at 82% maturity; V3 mature rate 0.61 suggests mix-weighted true rate ~0.38–0.40).
- October cohort: observed breach should *rise* as maturity increases (currently 0.16 at 55%; true rate predicted ~0.24–0.28 if V3 censoring resolved).
- Kaplan–Meier: V3 median resolution time ~150+ h (Aug–Sep) vs V2 ~20 h, V1 ~60 h; survival curves show V3 >> others in tail.

**Expected result under real performance decline:**
- Observed breach rates remain stable across cutoffs (no censoring bias).
- Kaplan–Meier curves superpose across months (resolution time unchanged).

**Falsification:** If October's observed breach *falls* below 0.16 at future maturity, censoring hypothesis is wrong; real performance did improve (unlikely given September data).

### Test 2: Recomputation of derived features post-patch and deduplication

**Objective:** Isolate April's pipeline corruption from real model failure.

**Method:**
1. Retrieve April and May raw event logs from both the old (pre-migration) and new (post-migration) systems.
2. Normalize all `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` events to `Initial Diagnosis Completed` label on the new system.
3. Apply duplicate-removal logic (keep first occurrence within a case per activity type, or use a canonical deduplication rule).
4. Recompute all prefix-level features for April and May (e.g., "number of activities before diagnosis," "time since case creation," "time to diagnosis," queue-depth indicators, activity-sequence embeddings if used).
5. Re-score the April and May cases with the model using recomputed features.
6. Compare new scores to original scores; compare new AUROC/Brier/ECE to original.

**Expected result under pipeline failure (April corrupted, May partially):**
- April's corrected AUROC rises from 0.67 to 0.80 (baseline level), Brier  0.053, ECE  0.086.
- May's corrected metrics improve marginally (already recovered due to patch), confirming residual contamination is minimal.
- Observed breach rates for April–May remain ~0.18 (unchanged), since outcome labels were likely correct; only feature space was corrupted.

**Expected result under real concept drift starting in April:**
- Corrected AUROC stays low (0.70), indicating model's intrinsic blind spot persists.
- Observed breach matches predicted risk post-correction; no inversion.

**Falsification:** If corrected AUROC remains poor but observed breach is high and stable, something else changed in the case mix or outcome definition in April (not just features).

### Test 3: Stratified calibration and discrimination by variant, source, and month

**Objective:** Identify which process streams experienced concept or covariate drift.

**Method:**
1. For each month and each variant (V1, V2, V3), compute:
   - Calibration plots (predicted risk decile vs. observed breach in that decile).
   - AUROC (discrimination) within each variant.
   - Brier score per variant.
   - Expected Calibration Error (ECE) per variant.
2. Compare July vs. August for V3 only (to isolate supplier impact):
   - If discrimination (AUROC) is stable but calibration (ECE) worsens in August, the model sees the same *kind* of case but predictions are overconfident (consistent with process degradation without concept drift).
   - If discrimination worsens in August, unobserved heterogeneity is emerging (possible concept drift or population shift).
3. Stratify September and October by Partner Z vs. non-Partner Z:
   - Compute AUROC and calibration separately.
   - If Partner Z has much worse AUROC/calibration, and features like rural distance and device age are absent, this is diagnostic of concept drift for Partner Z segment.

**Expected result under process drift only (JulyAugust):**
- V3 AUROC(July)  V3 AUROC(August) (discrimination stable).
- V3 Brier worsens (calibration), driven by ECE (overconfidence).
- V1 and V2 remain stable month-to-month.

**Expected result under concept drift (AugustSeptember for Partner Z):**
- Partner Z AUROC << non-Partner Z AUROC (within same month).
- Adding rural distance and device age as features improves Partner Z AUROC significantly.
- Removing Partner Z from training and retraining degrades performance on Partner Z validation set less than on non-Partner Z (indicating a true population mismatch, not just noise).

**Falsification:** If V3 AUROC improves in August (contradicts process degradation), or if Partner Z AUROC is not significantly worse than non-Partner Z (contradicts concept drift hypothesis), revise explanations.

### Test 4: Process variant analysis—DFG and performance-DFG by month

**Objective:** Verify that process-level metrics explain performance changes.

**Method:**
1. Use `pm4py.discover_dfg()` and `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg()` on monthly subsets to extract:
   - Event transitions (e.g., `Initial Diagnosis Completed  Order Part  Part Received  Dispatch Technician`).
   - Median and percentile times for each edge (e.g., median OrderPart-Received time).
2. Compute variant distribution (`pm4py.get_variants()`) to verify our variant mix table.
3. Correlate performance-DFG metrics (e.g., "median OrderPart-Received time in month M") with monthly AUROC and observed breach rate.
4. For V3 specifically, test whether the increase in `OrderPart-Received` time from July (65 h) to August–September (130–148 h) fully explains V3 breach rise (0.460.64), or whether residual acceleration remains unexplained.

**Expected result under process drift (supplier degradation):**
- Median OrderPart-Received time rises abruptly in July and continues rising August–September, correlating with V3 breach rate rise.
- Residual unexplained breach rise (after controlling for wait time) is small (< 0.05 or 10% relative increase).
- V2 performance-DFG (diagnosisremote fix) remains stable (3.8–4.6 h), explaining June's V2 surge and low observed breach.

**Expected result under concept drift:**
- Residual breach rise after controlling for wait time is large ( 0.10), suggesting factors beyond delay matter (e.g., repair difficulty).
- V2 performance-DFG might vary (e.g., older devices take longer remote fixes), detecting unobserved complexity.

**Falsification:** If OrderPart-Received time does *not* rise in July (contradicts audit data), reexamine the audit or pipeline.

### Test 5: Conditional outcome relationship analysis—does the causal effect change?

**Objective:** Isolate concept drift from covariate drift; determine if the *outcome model* changed or only the feature distribution.

**Method:**
1. For all cases on the same path (e.g., V3 with both "Order Part" and "Part Received" events), build a linear regression: `breach  log(order_to_received_time) + technician_id + device_age + ...` for July and for August–September.
2. Test whether the coefficient on `log(order_to_received_time)` is significantly different between July and Aug-Sep:
   - If coefficient(July)  coefficient(Aug-Sep), then a 1-hour increase in wait time has the same predictive effect; the *model* hasn't changed, only the feature distribution.  **Covariate drift, not concept drift.**
   - If coefficient(Aug-Sep) >> coefficient(July), then high wait time is suddenly much more predictive of breach; unobserved confounders (e.g., rush repairs, technician exhaustion, part quality) are now active.  **Concept drift.**
3. Similarly, for Partner Z cases (Sep–Oct), regress breach on available features and compare coefficient signs/magnitudes to non-Partner Z:
   - If coefficients flip sign or magnify, this suggests Partner Z operates under different rules.  **Concept drift for Partner Z segment.**

**Expected result under pure process drift (JulyAug-Sep):**
- Coefficient on wait time stable; intercept might shift if baseline breach rate rises due to harder case mix, but the marginal effect of additional delay is the same.

**Expected result under concept drift + process drift (Aug-Sep Partner Z):**
- Partner Z coefficients differ from non-Partner Z (e.g., device age coefficient is negative for Partner Z, positive for non-Partner Z, indicating older devices are *easier* for rural technicians).

**Falsification:** If coefficients are identical and residuals are well-calibrated, then the model is fundamentally sound; any observed performance change is due to label delay or score-distribution shift, not a changed outcome model.

---

### Test 6: Population reweighting / inverse-probability-of-treatment weighting (IPTW)

**Objective:** Measure how much of September's breach rise is due to Partner Z demographic shift vs. genuine degradation.

**Method:**
1. Estimate the propensity score P(Partner Z | observable case features at diagnosis) for all September and October cases.
2. For non-Partner Z cases, weight each by `1 / (1 - propensity)` to rebalance to a population with Partner Z prevalence matching June (~0%).
3. Recompute observed breach rate on reweighted September and October.
4. Compare reweighted breach to original:
   - If reweighted breach << original breach, Partner Z's higher breach is a population effect.
   - If reweighted breach  original breach, population shift is not the issue; real performance degradation exists.

**Expected result under population shift (Partner Z population effect):**
- Reweighted September breach  from 0.31 to ~0.26–0.28 (approaches non-Partner Z rate 0.27).
- Reweighted October breach still biased by label maturity, but trend should be downward if population reweighting is valid.

**Expected result under concept drift + process (Partner Z is a proxy for harder cases):**
- Reweighted breach remains high ( 0.28); the issue is not Partner Z identity but the underlying case complexity they introduce.
- This would suggest the model should be retrained on Partner Z cases to learn the new outcome distribution.

**Falsification:** If propensity-score model is poorly calibrated (many non-Partner Z cases mispredicted as Partner Z), IPTW fails; instead, use explicit stratification or sensitivity analysis.

### Test 7: Feature missingness and lineage audit

**Objective:** Confirm that feature corruption in April is isolated and identify any ongoing generation issues.

**Method:**
1. For each month and each feature (e.g., "time_since_diagnosis," "queue_depth," "device_age"), compute:
   - % of cases with missing (NULL or NaN) values.
   - Median, std, min, max of the feature for non-missing cases.
   - Frequency of feature values (e.g., histogram).
2. Track the lineage: which events are required to compute each feature? If `time_since_diagnosis` requires the `Initial Diagnosis Completed` event, verify that this event is captured in all cases post-April (should be ~100% after patch).
3. Compare April's feature distributions to March and May. Identify features with sudden shifts:
   - Sudden spike in missingness  data quality issue.
   - Sudden shift in median (e.g., median queue depth doubles)  covariate drift or mix change.

**Expected result under successful pipeline fix:**
- April feature missingness rates drop from 14% (missing queue feature) back toward ~1% (baseline).
- Feature distributions (median, std) for April post-fix are within 10–20% of March (allowing for natural sampling variance).
- May feature distributions match March closely.

**Expected result under ongoing pipeline issue:**
- Missingness remains elevated in May or beyond, or new features appear with high missingness.
- Distributions remain shifted.

**Falsification:** If April's recomputed feature distributions *match* March but AUROC still stays low, the issue is not feature generation; it's the outcome or model.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

### Staged decision tree:

```
START (September 2026)

 IMMEDIATE CONTAINMENT (Days 0–3)
   STOP production retraining or auto-retraining triggers.
   Alert: October's observed breach is NOT trustworthy (55% label maturity).
   Communicate: "System is under audit; reported Oct breach 0.16 is misleading."
   Enable: Diagnostic-test suite (Tests 1–7 above) in parallel.
   Safety: Freeze model at June 30 state (post-pipeline-fix, pre-Partner Z).
     Rationale: June is the last stable month with 100% label maturity and clean data.

 SHORT-TERM RECALIBRATION (Days 4–14)
  
   IF Test 1 (label-maturity curves) shows October's true breach will be 0.28
    AND Test 2 (recomputation) confirms April/May features are now clean:
     RECALIBRATE (do not retrain full model).
         Step A: Recompute feature statistics on June 1 – September 30 cases (clean pipeline, post-routing-change).
         Step B: If September's observed breach (0.31, mostly matured by mid-Oct) is
                  validated 0.29 and explained by V3 supplier delays + Partner Z routing,
                  apply Platt scaling or isotonic regression on Sept cohort only:
                    output_prob_new = f(output_prob_old)
         Step C: Test on held-out June–Aug cases. If calibration improves without harming AUROC, deploy.
         Step D: Do NOT report Oct-derived calibration as final; wait for label maturity.
  
   IF Test 3 (stratified calibration) shows V3 discrimination is intact but only
    calibration (ECE) worsened:
     RECALIBRATE V3 specifically.
         Apply variant-specific Platt scaling to V3; leave V1, V2 unchanged.
         Recalibration formula: risk_v3_new = logit^{-1}(a + b * logit(risk_v3_old)).
         Retrain a, b on V3 cases from June–Sep with stable maturity (90%).
  
   IF any test fails to support the above, escalate to feature repair (Step 2 below).

 FEATURE REPAIR & SHORT-TERM RETRAINING (Days 15–30)
  
   IF Test 4 (DFG/process analysis) shows V3 breach rise is NOT fully explained by
    OrderPart-Received time (residual unexplained breach  0.08):
     CONCEPT DRIFT SIGNAL in V3.
         Do NOT retrain on August–September yet (immature labels in Sep, confounded in Aug).
         Instead: Request process/operations team to identify *why* breach rose beyond delays
         (e.g., part quality, technician skill, prioritization). Add derived features or flags
         if available (e.g., "part-type XYZ failure rate," "technician-tenure binned").
         Plan to retrain in Step 3 (full retraining) with engineered features if available.
  
   IF Test 5 (conditional outcome) shows coefficients for wait time differ significantly
    (Aug-Sep coefficient >> July coefficient):
     Unobserved confounders active (e.g., supply-chain rush = technician stress).
         Do NOT train on Aug-Sep without accounting for this (else model learns spurious correlation).
         Option A: Collect new features (e.g., "parts expedited flag," technician load).
         Option B: Use instrumental-variable regression to recover causal effects (advanced; skip if time-critical).
  
   IF Test 6 (IPTW / Partner Z reweighting) shows reweighted Sep breach is
    significantly lower (e.g., 0.27 vs 0.31):
     Partner Z is a population shift; model is not *per se* failing.
         Option A: Build a Partner Z–specific model (if 24%+ volume justifies it).
         Option B: Add partner/region as a feature and retrain on combined June–Sep.
         Do NOT assume Partner Z breach is a permanent feature; monitor if it persists.
  
   IF Test 7 (feature audit) shows ongoing missingness or drift:
     Address pipeline issue before any modeling change.
         E.g., if device_age is increasingly NULL post-June, restore data source or imputation.
  
   OUTPUT of this phase:
     - Decide: Feature addition? (device_age, rural_distance, partner_origin, technician_tenure, part_expedited_flag)
     - Decide: Segment-specific model? (V3-only, Partner Z–only, region-specific)
     - Prioritize: Which features are fastest to collect and validate?

 FULL RETRAINING (Days 31–60, if justified)
  
   TRAINING COHORT DESIGN:
     Safe periods (100% label maturity, known-stable pipeline):
       January 1 – May 31, 2026: Mature labels, clean pipeline.
       June 1 – July 31, 2026: Clean pipeline, routing change documented; treat routing as feature.
       August onwards: RISKY due to supplier degradation, population shift, immature labels.
    
     If adding engineered features (e.g., partner_origin, part_expedited):
       Retrain on Jan–July (8 months, ~28k cases), stratifying by variant.
       Validate on Aug 1–31 (full month, ~4k cases) *after* Aug labels mature (late October cutoff).
    
     If using Partner Z segment model:
        Train main model on Jan–July non-Partner Z (Partner Z didn't exist yet).
        Collect Sep–Oct Partner Z cases; wait until labels mature (November cutoff).
        Train Partner Z model on Sep–Oct + Oct–Nov if needed.
        Hybrid: Ensemble or routing-based ensemble (use main model for non-Partner Z, Partner Z model otherwise).
  
   VARIANT-SPECIFIC RETRAINING?
     Rationale for variant-specific model:
       V1 breach ~0.16–0.19 (low, stable).
       V2 breach ~0.09–0.11 (very low).
       V3 breach ~0.30–0.64 (high, volatile).
       They may have different feature importances and thresholds.
         E.g., for V2, predicted risk ~0.05–0.10 might be the realistic range;
         model calibrated on overall data could waste capacity on V2.
    
     Recommendation: YES, build V3-specific model if V3 is growing >25% of mix.
       V3-specific training cohort: Jan–July V3 cases only (~5.5k cases, assuming ~22% of total).
       V3-specific features: include parts-wait-related features (supplier, part type, order lead time).
       Validate: V3 August cases (with mature labels from late-Oct cutoff).
       Threshold: If V3-specific AUROC  0.80 and unified AUROC  0.75 on V3 validation, deploy V3-specific.
    
     If building separate models: reweight training such that each variant contributes
       proportionally to recent case volume (V3 now ~40%, V2 ~35%, V1 ~25%).
  
   EVALUATION:
     Hold-out validation: Jan 1 – Feb 28 (never used in any training or calibration).
      Compute: AUROC, Brier, ECE, calibration plots.
    
     Temporal validation: Mar 1 – Mar 31 (used for April/May recomputation tests,
      but labels were mature; can be a secondary check).
    
     Out-of-sample on Aug (once labels mature): Does new model improve AUROC?
       E.g., old model AUROC(Aug) = 0.68; new model AUROC(Aug) = 0.75?
  
   GO/NO-GO Decision:
      If new model meets baseline (AUROC  0.81, Brier  0.132) on Jan–Feb validation: DEPLOY.
      If new model is marginally better (e.g., 0.79 AUROC): DEPLOY with caution; monitor closely.
      If new model is worse: DO NOT DEPLOY. Return to recalibration or investigate additional issues.

 DEPLOYMENT & ONGOING MONITORING (Days 61+)
    If deploying recalibrated model (Step 1 outcome):
      Monitor monthly: AUROC, Brier, ECE, variant-level breaches, label-maturity curves.
        Alert if AUROC drops below 0.75 for two consecutive months.
   
    If deploying retrained model (Step 3 outcome):
      A/B test: Shadow new model on 10% of cases for 2 weeks.
        Compare: predicted risk distribution, downstream routing decisions, case SLA compliance.
        If shadow metrics are in line with expectations, gradually roll out to 100% (50%100% over 2 weeks).
        Keep old model in shadow mode for 30 days post-deployment; roll back if needed.
   
    Post-deployment: Implement monitoring architecture (Section 5 below).
```

### Key decisions:

| Scenario | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| April detected  complete feature recomputation by mid-May | **Do not retrain**; fix pipeline. | Retraining on corrupted features would bake in the bug. Recomputation is faster and safer. |
| June's V2 surge + improved breach | **Monitor, do not retrain**; mix shift is favorable. | Model is discriminating well. Recalibration for mix (if desired) is optional. |
| July V3 delays emerge | **Monitor + process-level diagnostics**; do not retrain yet. | Process degradation is explained. Retraining after 1 month may over-fit to transient shock. |
| August V3 breach 0.64, but labels 93% mature | **Recalibrate V3 only** if Test 3 shows calibration issue. **Do not retrain** on August cases yet. | 93% maturity is borderline; waiting for 98%+ is safer. If V3 discrimination is still good, calibration fix is lower-risk than retraining. |
| September Partner Z 24%, breach 0.45 (vs 0.27), labels 82% mature | **Do not retrain**; labels too immature. **Plan** for Partner Z–specific model post-label-maturity. **Investigate** why Partner Z is harder (process, demographics, case complexity). | Cannot reliably train on 82% maturity. Partner Z labels will be mature by end of November; design Partner Z model in parallel. |
| October labels 55% mature, API backlog | **Do not use October metrics for any model decision**. **Re-report October at December cutoff** after labels mature. | 55% censoring is too severe and biased toward easier cases. Any model trained on Oct labels would overconfidently predict low breach on hard cases. |
| End of Phase 2 (Feature Repair): new features identified (device_age, partner_origin, part_expedited) | **Retrain on Jan–July only**. Validate on Aug with mature labels (late Oct cutoff). | Jan–July is 100% clean, 100% mature, and covers all variants. Aug is the first month with known *external* shock (supplier) that model hasn't seen; good validation for out-of-distribution generalization. |

## 5. Monitoring architecture

### High-level design:

```

          MONITORING FRAMEWORK (Monthly cadence)                 

                                                                 
  1. PREDICTIVE PERFORMANCE MONITOR                              
      AUROC (per variant, per source, overall)                 
      Brier score                                              
      Alert: AUROC < 0.75 for 2 consecutive months            
      Action: Trigger diagnostic (Test 3–5) if alert fires     
                                                                 
  2. CALIBRATION MONITOR                                          
      ECE (Expected Calibration Error)                         
      Calibration plots (decile-wise)                          
      Alert: ECE > 0.10 or Brier > 0.18                       
      Action: Inspect for censoring (label maturity) or        
          covariate drift; consider Platt scaling if benign      
                                                                 
  3. LABEL MATURITY & OUTCOME API MONITOR                        
      % cases with matured labels (outcome known) at cutoff    
      Median label delay (days from case close to outcome      
       recorded in system)                                       
      Percentile distribution (25th, 50th, 75th, 95th)         
      Alert: Maturity < 80% OR median delay > 45 days         
      Action: Flag that current metrics are provisional;       
          suppress model retraining; re-report metrics at        
          future cutoff when maturity  95%                      
                                                                 
  4. DATA QUALITY MONITOR                                        
      % unknown/unmapped activities                            
      % missing features (per feature)                         
      Duplicate event rate (same activity, same case, <5 min)  
      Feature value distributions (median, std, range)         
      Alert: Unknown activity > 2% OR missing feature > 5%     
              OR feature median drifts >30% from baseline       
      Action: Page data eng; check event-source system or      
          pipeline; do NOT retrain until cleared                 
                                                                 
  5. PROCESS VARIANT MONITOR                                     
      % cases per variant (V1, V2, V3, unmapped)               
      Median elapsed time per DFG edge (by variant)            
       e.g., median(Diagnosis  Dispatch) for V1                
             median(Order Part  Part Received) for V3          
      Alert: V3 median OrderReceived > 100 h OR V1            
              variant share drops >10 points month-to-month     
      Action: Investigate process change (supplier issue,      
          routing policy, external shock); update stratified     
          performance tracking                                   
                                                                 
  6. PERFORMANCE-DFG MONITOR                                     
      Median end-to-end case resolution time (by variant)      
      Median time to breach (i.e., P(breach | resolved by t)  
       for t = 12 h, 24 h, 48 h, 72 h)                         
      Alert: Median resolution time  > 20% month-on-month    
              OR median-time-to-breach for V3 drops abruptly    
              (possible SLA redefinition or external shock)     
      Action: Cross-check with process-variant monitor;        
          is slowdown due to longer diagnosis, parts wait, or    
          repair? Inform SLA team                                
                                                                 
  7. POPULATION MONITOR                                           
      % cases per source/partner (e.g., Partner Z, Partner A)  
      Breach rate per source (stratified, matured labels only) 
      Alert: New source > 10% volume OR breach rate for        
              source  > 0.10 points month-on-month             
      Action: Stratify model evaluation by source; compute     
               source-specific AUROC; plan segment-specific     
               model if breach gap persists                     
      Feature audit: Do new sources introduce missing          
          features (e.g., rural distance for Partner Z)?         
                                                                 
  8. BUSINESS OUTCOME MONITOR                                    
      Observed breach rate (overall and by variant/source)     
      Breach rate on cohort with 100% label maturity          
      SLA compliance (% of cases resolved on time)             
      Alert: Observed breach > 0.25 (or project-specific       
              threshold) on mature cohort                       
      Action: Trigger root-cause analysis; if model is not     
          the bottleneck (e.g., supplier, technician capacity),  
          escalate to ops; if model is under-predicting risk,    
          retrain or add features                                
                                                                 
   ALERT DEPENDENCIES & SUPPRESSION RULES 
                                                                
    IF (Label Maturity Alert FIRES)                             
       THEN suppress {Predictive Perf Alert, Calibration Alert} 
       REASON: Metrics not comparable; wait for full maturity  
                                                                
    IF (Data Quality Alert FIRES)                               
       THEN suppress {Predictive Perf, Calibration, Process}    
       REASON: Do not retrain or redeploy until data is fixed   
                                                                
    IF (Process Variant Alert FIRES) AND (no Data Quality Alert)
       THEN DO NOT automatically suppress Predictive Perf       
       REASON: Process change is real and expected; performance 
              may degrade, but model is still valid             
       ACTION: Recalibrate or investigate concept drift         
                                                                
    IF (Outcome API Delay Alert FIRES)                          
       AND (AUROC drops, ECE rises, predicted risk  while     
            observed breach )                                  
       THEN this is CENSORING; re-report at future cutoff      
       DO NOT retrain; DO NOT recalibrate                       
       (calibration on censored cohort would over-estimate     
        precision and lead to false confidence)                
                                                                
    IF (Breach Alert on mature cohort) AND (no other alerts)    
       THEN escalate to ops/product; may not be model issue     
                                                                
  
                                                                 
  REPORTING CADENCE:                                             
   Weekly: Data quality, process variance (early warning)      
   Monthly: Full metrics suite; board-ready summary            
   Quarterly: Trend analysis, model retraining plan            
   Ad-hoc: When any critical alert fires                       
                                                                 

```

### Python code sketch for monthly process-evidence extraction:

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.conversion.log import converter
from pm4py.algo.discovery.dfg import algorithm as dfg_discovery
from pm4py.algo.discovery.performance_dfg import algorithm as perf_dfg_discovery
from pm4py.algo.filtering.log import time_filter
from datetime import datetime, timedelta
import numpy as np

def audit_monthly_process_evidence(csv_path, month_str='2026-08'):
    """
    Extract monthly process evidence for audit: variant distribution,
    DFG performance metrics, label maturity, data quality.
    
    Args:
        csv_path: Path to field_service_events.csv with columns:
                  [case_id, timestamp, activity, resource, outcome, outcome_timestamp]
        month_str: 'YYYY-MM' for cohort selection (all cases STARTED in this month)
    
    Returns:
        dict with keys: variants, dfg_edges, perf_dfg_edges, label_maturity, data_quality
    """
    
    #  STEP 1: READ & NORMALIZE 
    df = pd.read_csv(csv_path)
    df['timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(df['timestamp'])
    df['outcome_timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(df['outcome_timestamp'], errors='coerce')
    
    # Normalize the renamed diagnosis activity (April 2026 schema migration)
    # Both 'Initial Diagnosis Completed' and 'Diagnostic Assessment Completed' map to same concept
    df['activity'] = df['activity'].replace({
        'Diagnostic Assessment Completed': 'Initial Diagnosis Completed'
    })
    
    # Replace generic 'UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY' with NULL; these will be flagged in data quality
    df.loc[df['activity'] == 'UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY', 'activity'] = np.nan
    
    #  STEP 2: SELECT MONTHLY COHORT (cases started in month_str) 
    start_date = pd.to_datetime(month_str)
    end_date = start_date + pd.DateOffset(months=1) - pd.Timedelta(days=1)
    
    df_month = df[(df['timestamp'] >= start_date) & (df['timestamp'] <= end_date)].copy()
    
    # Group by case_id to identify case start (first event) and case end
    case_starts = df_month.groupby('case_id')['timestamp'].min()
    df_month['case_start'] = df_month['case_id'].map(case_starts)
    
    # Filter: only cases that started in this month
    df_cohort = df_month[df_month['case_start'] == df_month.groupby('case_id')['timestamp'].transform('min')]
    
    print(f"Cohort {month_str}: {df_cohort['case_id'].nunique()} cases, {len(df_cohort)} events")
    
    #  STEP 3: HANDLE DUPLICATES & MISSING ACTIVITIES 
    # Duplicates: same case_id, activity, timestamp (within 1 sec)
    df_cohort = df_cohort.sort_values(['case_id', 'timestamp', 'activity'])
    df_cohort['is_duplicate'] = df_cohort.duplicated(
        subset=['case_id', 'activity', pd.Timestamp(df_cohort['timestamp']).round('1s')],
        keep='first'
    )
    df_cohort_dedup = df_cohort[~df_cohort['is_duplicate']].copy()
    
    dedup_rate = df_cohort['is_duplicate'].sum() / len(df_cohort) if len(df_cohort) > 0 else 0
    print(f"  Duplicates removed: {df_cohort['is_duplicate'].sum()} events ({dedup_rate:.1%})")
    
    # Drop rows with missing activity (diagnostic test: how many are lost?)
    missing_activity = df_cohort_dedup['activity'].isna().sum()
    unknown_activity_rate = missing_activity / len(df_cohort_dedup) if len(df_cohort_dedup) > 0 else 0
    df_cohort_dedup = df_cohort_dedup.dropna(subset=['activity'])
    
    print(f"  Unknown/missing activities: {missing_activity} ({unknown_activity_rate:.2%})")
    
    #  STEP 4: CONVERT TO PM4PY LOG FORMAT 
    df_pm4py = df_cohort_dedup[['case_id', 'timestamp', 'activity']].copy()
    df_pm4py.columns = ['case:concept:name', 'time:timestamp', 'concept:name']
    df_pm4py = df_pm4py.sort_values(['case:concept:name', 'time:timestamp'])
    
    log = converter.apply(df_pm4py, parameters={
        converter.Variants.TO_EVENT_LOG: {
            converter.TO_EVENT_LOG_TRACE_ID: 'case:concept:name',
            converter.TO_EVENT_LOG_ACTIVITY_KEY: 'concept:name',
            converter.TO_EVENT_LOG_TIMESTAMP_KEY: 'time:timestamp'
        }
    })
    
    #  STEP 5: DISCOVER VARIANTS (process types) 
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)
    variant_counts = {str(v): len(traces) for v, traces in variants.items()}
    
    # Classify into V1, V2, V3 based on activity sequence
    # V1: contains both Dispatch and Repair
    # V2: contains Remote Fix, no Dispatch
    # V3: contains Order Part and Part Received
    def classify_variant(activities_tuple):
        activities = set(activities_tuple)
        if 'Order Part' in activities and 'Part Received' in activities:
            return 'V3_Parts'
        elif 'Remote Fix' in activities and 'Dispatch Technician' not in activities:
            return 'V2_Remote'
        elif 'Dispatch Technician' in activities and 'Repair Completed' in activities:
            return 'V1_Standard'
        else:
            return 'Unmapped'
    
    variant_classification = {}
    for variant_tuple in variant_counts.keys():
        variant_list = eval(variant_tuple) if isinstance(variant_tuple, str) else variant_tuple
        class_ = classify_variant(variant_list)
        variant_classification[class_] = variant_classification.get(class_, 0) + variant_counts[variant_tuple]
    
    variant_pct = {k: v / sum(variant_classification.values()) * 100 
                   for k, v in variant_classification.items()}
    
    print(f"  Variant distribution:")
    for v, pct in sorted(variant_pct.items(), key=lambda x: x[1], reverse=True):
        print(f"    {v}: {pct:.1f}%")
    
    #  STEP 6: DISCOVER DFG & PERFORMANCE DFG 
    dfg = dfg_discovery.apply(log)
    perf_dfg = perf_dfg_discovery.apply(log)
    
    # Extract key performance metrics: Order -> Part Received time (V3 specific)
    order_part_times = []
    for case in log:
        activities_in_case = [e['concept:name'] for e in case]
        timestamps_in_case = [e['time:timestamp'] for e in case]
        
        order_idx = None
        received_idx = None
        for i, act in enumerate(activities_in_case):
            if act == 'Order Part':
                order_idx = i
            elif act == 'Part Received':
                received_idx = i
        
        if order_idx is not None and received_idx is not None and received_idx > order_idx:
            elapsed_h = (timestamps_in_case[received_idx] - timestamps_in_case[order_idx]).total_seconds() / 3600
            order_part_times.append(elapsed_h)
    
    if order_part_times:
        median_order_part = np.median(order_part_times)
        print(f"  Order -> Part Received: median {median_order_part:.1f} h, "
              f"p50={np.percentile(order_part_times, 50):.1f}, "
              f"p95={np.percentile(order_part_times, 95):.1f}")
    else:
        median_order_part = None
        print(f"  Order -> Part Received: no V3 cases in this cohort")
    
    #  STEP 7: LABEL MATURITY & OUTCOME TIMING 
    reporting_cutoff = end_date + pd.Timedelta(days=30)  # Assume 30 days after cohort end
    
    df_cohort_with_outcome = df_cohort_dedup.drop_duplicates(subset='case_id')
    df_cohort_with_outcome['has_outcome'] = df_cohort_with_outcome['outcome_timestamp'].notna() & \
                                             (df_cohort_with_outcome['outcome_timestamp'] <= reporting_cutoff)
    
    label_maturity = df_cohort_with_outcome['has_outcome'].sum() / len(df_cohort_with_outcome)
    
    # Label delay: days from case close to outcome recorded
    label_delays = []
    for idx, row in df_cohort_with_outcome.iterrows():
        if row['has_outcome']:
            case_close_time = df_cohort_dedup[df_cohort_dedup['case_id'] == row['case_id']]['timestamp'].max()
            delay_days = (row['outcome_timestamp'] - case_close_time).days
            label_delays.append(delay_days)
    
    if label_delays:
        median_label_delay = np.median(label_delays)
        print(f"  Label maturity: {label_maturity:.1%}, median delay {median_label_delay:.0f} days")
    else:
        median_label_delay = None
        print(f"  Label maturity: {label_maturity:.1%}, no outcomes recorded yet")
    
    #  STEP 8: DATA QUALITY METRICS 
    data_quality = {
        'duplicate_rate': dedup_rate,
        'unknown_activity_rate': unknown_activity_rate,
        'cases_with_outcome': df_cohort_with_outcome['has_outcome'].sum(),
        'total_cases': len(df_cohort_with_outcome),
        'label_maturity': label_maturity,
        'median_label_delay_days': median_label_delay,
    }
    
    # Feature missingness: check for key derived features
    # (These would typically be computed by the feature pipeline,
    #  but we can check data source here)
    # Example: if 'resource' (technician) is missing, diagnostics will be incomplete
    if 'resource' in df_cohort_dedup.columns:
        missing_resource = df_cohort_dedup['resource'].isna().sum()
        print(f"  Missing resource (technician): {missing_resource / len(df_cohort_dedup):.2%}")
        data_quality['missing_resource_rate'] = missing_resource / len(df_cohort_dedup)
    
    #  RETURN AUDIT SUMMARY 
    result = {
        'month': month_str,
        'cohort_size_cases': df_cohort_dedup['case_id'].nunique(),
        'cohort_size_events': len(df_cohort_dedup),
        'variant_distribution': variant_classification,
        'variant_percentages': variant_pct,
        'median_order_part_received_hours': median_order_part,
        'label_maturity': label_maturity,
        'median_label_delay_days': median_label_delay,
        'data_quality_metrics': data_quality,
    }
    
    return result


#  EXAMPLE USAGE 
if __name__ == '__main__':
    months = ['2026-06', '2026-07', '2026-08', '2026-09', '2026-10']
    
    audit_results = {}
    for month in months:
        print(f"\n{'='*60}")
        print(f"MONTH: {month}")
        print(f"{'='*60}")
        audit_results[month] = audit_monthly_process_evidence(
            'field_service_events.csv',
            month_str=month
        )
    
    #  COMPARATIVE SUMMARY 
    print(f"\n{'='*60}")
    print("COMPARATIVE SUMMARY")
    print(f"{'='*60}")
    
    summary_df = pd.DataFrame([
        {
            'Month': month,
            'Cases': result['cohort_size_cases'],
            'Events': result['cohort_size_events'],
            'V1 %': result['variant_percentages'].get('V1_Standard', 0),
            'V2 %': result['variant_percentages'].get('V2_Remote', 0),
            'V3 %': result['variant_percentages'].get('V3_Parts', 0),
            'Order->Part (h)': result['median_order_part_received_hours'],
            'Label Maturity': f"{result['label_maturity']:.0%}",
            'Label Delay (d)': result['median_label_delay_days'],
        }
        for month, result in audit_results.items()
    ])
    
    print(summary_df.to_string(index=False))
    
    # Key diagnostic signals:
    # - If Order->Part time jumps in July (65h), blame supplier  no retrain
    # - If V3 % rises sharply in Sep (42%), check if Partner Z is driving it
    # - If Label Maturity < 80% in Sep-Oct, metrics are provisional
    # - If unknown_activity rises in Apr (17%) then recovers in May (2%), pipeline fix was partial
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

### Risk 1: Retraining on immature labels (October disaster)

**Failure mode:** October's observed breach is 0.16 (improved from Sept 0.31), and someone concludes "great! Model is better now." They retrain on Oct cases (taking Oct observed breach as ground truth) to boost that improvement. Unbeknownst to them, 45% of Oct cases are still unresolved. When those cases resolve in November–December, the true breach rate is revealed to be ~0.28. The model, trained on Oct's false negatives, becomes overconfident in low-risk predictions and misses many breaches. By February, live predictions are severely miscalibrated.

**Consequences:**
- Users who should have gotten high-risk notifications (and extra resources) get low-risk scores.
- Unmet SLAs spike unexpectedly. Business loses trust in the system.
- Expensive emergency retraining or rollback needed.

**Mitigation:**
- **Label-maturity circuit breaker:** Do not train on any cohort with <90% label maturity at reporting cutoff.
- **Wait-and-retest protocol:** If Oct metrics look too good to be true (predicted risk  but observed breach ), flag as suspicious and wait for November cutoff before acting.
- **Kaplan–Meier survival curves:** Use these to estimate true breach rate *even if labels are incomplete*, by imputing unresolved cases with a censoring model. Train cautiously on censoring-adjusted labels, or don't train at all.
- **A/B test before deployment:** Even if Oct model looks good on paper, shadow it on 5–10% of live cases for 2–4 weeks before rolling out to 100%. Monitor live breach rate; if it diverges from historical, pull the model.

### Risk 2: Calibrating around a pipeline bug (April trap)

**Failure mode:** April's predicted risk is 0.28 while observed breach is 0.19. Someone looks at this and thinks, "The model is overconfident. I'll apply Platt scaling to bring down the predicted probabilities." They refit the calibration function on April data and deploy. Months later (now in June), the model now under-predicts risk for *all* cases (because the calibration was learned on corrupted April features). Low-risk scores are handed out too liberally. True breach rates climb because the model is now blind.

**Consequences:**
- Model performance degrades even though users don't know April was corrupted.
- Delayed discovery (metrics look fine in May after April effects are diluted).
- When root cause is finally found (pipeline investigation in August), it's too late; the calibration fix was already deployed and damage is done.

**Mitigation:**
- **Feature-lineage audit before calibration:** Before any recalibration, audit that features are computed correctly. Run Test 2 (recomputation post-patch) first.
- **Staging gates:** Do not deploy calibration changes to production without first validating on a known-clean cohort (e.g., recomputed April scores compared to June data).
- **Temporal stability check:** If considering calibration, check whether calibration parameters learned on one month transfer to adjacent months. If Jan calibration  Feb calibration significantly, something changed; investigate before deploying.
- **Adversarial review:** Have someone (not the model owner) review "why is April's calibration so different?" before acting.

### Risk 3: Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift in V3 (August silent failure)

**Failure mode:** August's V3 breach is 0.64 (up from 0.46 in July), but the team chalks it up entirely to the July 10 supplier delay (which is real). They decide: "This is process drift, not concept drift; no need to retrain." However, unbeknownst to them, when the supplier delays hit, technicians began cutting corners—ordering expedited parts at premium cost, rushing repairs without full diagnostics, or deploying less-experienced technicians to rural areas to manage workload. These behaviors are *correlated* with the delay but not *caused by it alone*. The model, trained on pre-delay data, doesn't know that a 100 h wait is not just a wait—it's a signal of downstream chaos.

By October, even though the supplier situation hasn't worsened further, the unobserved behaviors (expedited orders, rushed repairs, skill mismatch) persist, and the model still can't predict them.

**Consequences:**
- True breach rate remains elevated; model continues to under-predict.
- Team keeps blaming the supplier and waiting for it to recover.
- When supplier eventually recovers, breach rate doesn't drop as expected (because the true cause was unobserved behavior), and debugging is much harder.
- Business pressure to "just accept higher breaches" or "throw more resources at V3" grows, instead of addressing root causes.

**Mitigation:**
- **Residual unexplained breach test (Test 4 variant):** After controlling for observed wait times (e.g., via regression), quantify how much breach rise remains. If residual rise >0.08, investigate further; don't assume it's noise.
- **Domain expert review:** Include operations or technical leadership in model diagnostics. Ask: "We see V3 breach up 36%; we can explain 20% via longer wait times. What could explain the other 16%?" (Answers: part quality, technician skill, SLA change, etc.)
- **Feature engineering:** Identify and collect new features that might capture the unobserved behavior. E.g., "Was part expedited?" "Technician tenure?" "Time since diagnosis to dispatch." Even imperfect proxies help.
- **Temporal feature:** Add a feature flag "Is this after July 10?" and retrain; if model can use that flag to improve V3 AUROC, it suggests a regime change rather than noise.

### Risk 4: Attributing Partner Z's higher breach to unfair model without stratified evidence (discrimination trap)

**Failure mode:** Partner Z arrives in September with 24% volume and 0.45 breach vs. 0.27 for others. Someone reads this and concludes: "The model is biased against Partner Z. Partner Z cases are harder, but we didn't train on rural customers. We need to retrain fairly." They retrain on a mix heavily weighted toward Partner Z cases (over-sampling Partner Z to match their 24% prevalence with 50% of training weight). 

However, they didn't check: Is Partner Z actually harder (true concept drift), or is Partner Z just routed to V3 more often, and V3 is hard for exogenous reasons (supplier delays)? After retraining, the model now over-predicts breach on non-Partner Z cases and under-predicts on Partner Z cases (because the retraining implicitly lowered thresholds for Partner Z patterns). Live predictions degrade for the majority population.

**Consequences:**
- Model becomes unfairly *low-risk* toward Partner Z (demographic bias in the opposite direction).
- Non-Partner Z cases are over-flagged for intervention, increasing operational cost.
- Regulators or users notice the "fairness" adjustment didn't actually work (breach on Partner Z still high) and trust erodes.
- The real problem (V3 is hard, V3 is driven by process issues or intrinsic complexity, not Partner Z identity) is still unaddressed.

**Mitigation:**
- **Stratified performance tests (Test 3) before claiming discrimination:** Compute AUROC and calibration separately for Partner Z vs. non-Partner Z within the same month. If Partner Z AUROC is low and non-Partner Z is high (same model), that's discrimination evidence. If *both* have low AUROC in August–Sep (before Partner Z arrived), the issue is not Partner Z identity; it's the case mix (variant shift, supplier degradation, etc.).
- **Variant-stratified analysis:** Compute Partner Z breach rate *within V2 only*, and non-Partner Z breach rate *within V2 only*. If they're similar, V2 is fair; then compute V3 breach by source. This isolates whether Partner Z is inherently harder or just routed harder.
- **Feature audit:** Check if Partner Z cases are missing key features (e.g., device_age, rural_distance). If features are missing, that's a data collection issue, not a model bias issue; fix the data, don't retrain.
- **Sensitivity analysis:** Retrain a candidate fairness-aware model and test it on a clean July cohort (before Partner Z) to see if it regresses performance on the historical population. If yes, the fairness adjustment is gaming metrics; if no, it may be safe.
- **Involve domain experts:** Ask operations: "Are Partner Z cases *truly* harder (need more time, more specialized technician), or are they just routed harder?" The answer guides strategy.

### Risk 5: Over-relying on label-maturity curves without auditing censoring bias (August-September estimate disaster)

**Failure mode:** By late October, the team has maturity curves from Test 1. They see that Aug maturity was 93% and Sep maturity was 82%, and they interpolate: "By early November (30 days after Sep cohort start), Sep should reach 95% maturity; we can trust Sep metrics soon." They hold off retraining until November.

But the censoring is *not random*. Longer-path cases (V3) are overrepresented in the unresolved 18% of Sep cases. When the remaining 18% resolve in November, the true Sep breach (0.31 at 82% maturity) rises to 0.39 (at ~100% maturity). August's 0.28 (at 93% maturity) might also rise to 0.33.

If the team had trained a model in late September on the 82%-matured cohort, they'd be training on a cohort biased toward lower breach (easier, faster-resolving cases). The model would under-predict breach in production.

**Consequences:**
- Maturity curves are based on speed of label arrival, not on realistic breach rate estimation.
- Business is misled about true performance trends.
- Delayed discovery of real degradation.

**Mitigation:**
- **Stratified label-maturity curves:** Break maturity down by variant. V3 will have lower maturity (longer paths) than V2 or V1. Plot separate curves.
- **Censoring-adjusted breach rate:** Use Kaplan–Meier or logistic regression with censoring to estimate what Sep's true breach would be, even at 82% maturity. Compare this estimate to the observed (82% maturity) rate. If they're very different, censoring is severe; wait longer.
- **Process-stratified test:** Compute observed breach rate on *only the subset of cases that have resolved* within X hours (a fast-resolving subset with low censoring). Track this over time. If fast-resolving breach rises, true breach is rising; if flat, improvement may be illusory.
- **Disclose uncertainty:** Report metrics as: "Sep observed breach 0.31 at 82% label maturity; estimated true breach 0.35–0.39 (assuming MCAR censoring or V3 sensitivity model)." Flag to business that true rate is unknown and could be higher.

### Risk 6: Attributing process variance to model failure and over-retraining

**Failure mode:** June sees observed breach drop to 0.15 (best in 6 months). August sees it rise to 0.28 (up 87%). The team sees this volatility and thinks, "Our model is unstable; we should retrain monthly to adapt." They set up an auto-retraining pipeline that retrains the model every month if breach rises >0.10 points.

August arrives. Breach = 0.28. AUROC = 0.68. Model retrains on June + July (training data) to predict August's new breach level. New model AUROC = 0.72 (slightly better). They deploy it.

By November, they've retrained 4 times (Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov), and the model is now a patchwork of different regimes. It performs well on recent data but fails when the business environment stabilizes (e.g., supplier issue resolves, Partner Z operations mature). The model is over-fit to transient shocks and has zero stability.

**Consequences:**
- Model volatility erodes user trust.
- Retraining is expensive and error-prone (each month, new opportunities for bugs).
- Accumulated technical debt (every retraining adds variants and branches to code).
- When the environment *stabilizes*, the model is now brittle and must be rebuilt from scratch.

**Mitigation:**
- **Root-cause audit before retraining:** For any month-to-month change in breach rate >0.10, run Tests 1–7 before deciding to retrain. Identify whether the change is:
  - Process drift (supplier delay, routing change): Do NOT retrain; this is exogenous.
  - Population shift (Partner Z arriving): DO NOT retrain immediately; wait for labels to mature and stratify evaluation.
  - Concept drift (unobserved feature became important): YES, retrain (after engineering that feature if possible).
  - Label delay / censoring: DO NOT retrain; wait for label maturity.
  - Covariate drift (feature distribution shifted, but outcome model unchanged): Recalibrate, don't retrain.
- **Retraining cadence:** Plan retraining quarterly or semi-annually, not monthly. Monthly retraining is only justified if there's strong evidence of concept drift (not confirmed).
- **Change-control gate:** Every retraining requires sign-off from someone other than the modeler. Checklist: "Is this root-cause justified? Are we confident labels are mature? Have we stratified by variant/source? Have we backtested on held-out data?"
- **Monitor model stability:** Track whether model's feature importances (or coefficients) are stable month-to-month. If they're bouncing around, the model is over-fitting to noise; freeze it and investigate the data, not the algorithm.

## Conclusion

This audit reveals a **multi-layered deterioration** from April to October 2026, driven by distinct mechanisms:

1. **April (isolated pipeline failure)**: Schema migration + incomplete deduplication. Fixable by recomputing features; no model change needed.

2. **May (partial remediation)**: Patch partially effective; residual cache corruption. Needs complete backfill; then retest.

3. **June (legitimate routing shift)**: Business policy change to more V2 (remote). Improves observed breach; no model degradation. Monitor but don't retrain.

4. **July (early supplier degradation)**: Parts wait times rise 55%. V3 breach up 32%. Process degradation, discriminable by model. No retraining needed; monitor for further shock.

5. **August–September (overlapping process + concept + population)**: 
   - Process: V3 wait times  (supplier crisis worsens).
   - Concept: Residual V3 breach rise suggests unobserved confounders (expedited ordering, rushed repairs, technician stress).
   - Population: Partner Z enters September with 58% routed to V3, rural demographics, higher breach (0.45 vs 0.27); but labels immature (82%).
   - **Decision**: Do NOT retrain on Aug–Sep labels (immature). Plan feature engineering (expedited flag, technician tenure, rural distance). Plan Partner Z–specific model post-label-maturity.

6. **October (label censoring crisis)**: 55% of cases unresolved; API backlog disproportionately affects V3. Predicted risk  while observed breach  (diagnostic of censoring). **Do NOT report or train on October metrics.**

**Recommended path forward:**
- Immediate: Complete April/May pipeline audit (Test 2). Disable any auto-retraining that uses immature labels.
- Week 1: Run all diagnostic tests (Tests 3–7) to confirm root causes.
- Week 2–3: Engineer new features (expedited-flag, technician-tenure, rural-distance). Collect data if not already available.
- Week 4–5: Retrain on Jan–July (clean, mature, 100% data). Validate on Aug with labels matured (late Oct cutoff).
- Week 6–7: Deploy retraining or recalibration based on validation results.
- Ongoing: Implement monitoring architecture (Section 5); stratify by variant and source; wait for Nov–Dec label maturity before judging Aug–Sep and plan Partner Z model.

**Key principle**: Distinguish *exogenous process shocks* (supplier, policy) from *model failure* (unobserved features, censoring bias). Not all deterioration is a modeling problem; acting on the wrong diagnosis causes more harm than the original issue.
