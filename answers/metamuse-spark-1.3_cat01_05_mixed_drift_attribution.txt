## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The raw-to-mapped fitness gap isolates a semantic artifact: raw fitness collapses from 0.93 in February to 0.55 in March while mapped fitness stays at 0.90, recovering 0.35 of 0.38 loss. This implies the March non-conformance is dominated by renaming `Validate Eligibility` to `Eligibility Screen` plus duplicated `Open Case`, not by a new business path. It does not imply the business process was unchanged after March. Mapping recovers conformance mechanically by declaring the labels equivalent and deleting duplicates before alignment; that assumption must be validated independently via attribute, ordering, and temporal evidence. If the new screen actually changed eligibility logic, mapping would hide real behavioral drift behind a syntactic fix. Duplicated technical events inflate fitness cost, variant count, and DFG nodes if left in, but barely affect median cycle time; missing resources do not affect control-flow fitness at all but cripple resource, workload, handover, and queue analyses and can bias performance attribution if missingness correlates with team or complexity. Post-May patterns cannot be logging alone because they persist after normalization: mapped fitness falls 0.91 to 0.86, Assess->RequestInfo rises from 12 to 29 per 100, rework variants double, and queue waiting grows while Assess service grows only 0.6h. A label map cannot create rework loops or waiting time.

### 3.3 Cycle-time decomposition (median; p90 in parentheses)

Reference deterioration Jul vs base: +13.0h median (+36h p90); Oct-Nov vs base: +17h median (+48-53h p90).

- **Service time (Assess activity):** +0.6h (~12% rise 4.85.4h). If one Assess per case, contributes ~0.6h median (~5% of Jul gap); with rework ~1.3 Assesses/case adds ~0.8h extra. Share: **5-10% median, <10% p90**. Evidence strong (direct measurement), but service definition may exclude Senior Assessment time.
- **Control-flow / rework (extra loops):** Rework share +17.5pp Jul, each loop adds Collect+Assess service + intermediate waits. Even at 5-8h per loop, 0.17 extra loops  1-1.5h direct service + induced waiting counted under queue. Share of median gap as direct work: **10-20%**; as trigger for queue: larger (see mediation).
- **Case mix (complexity/volume):** High-complexity 20.5%28% Jul (+7.5pp), 45% Nov (+24.5pp). Supplier Z cases already more complex pre-May. Complex cases have longer paths and more Senior steps. Alone cannot explain May jump (complexity +4.5pp vs rework +12.5pp) but explains ~30-40% of Oct-Nov elevation vs Sep. Share Jul: **10-20%**; Oct-Nov: **25-40%**.
- **Queueing (waiting before Assess):** Residual after service/mix/rework-service. Reconstructed wait increased much more than service. Consistent with capacity loss + higher arrivals + rework recirculation (re-entrant queue). Share Jul-Aug: **60-75% of median gap, 70-80% of p90 gap**; Oct-Nov similar despite higher mix. P90 grows 2.7× median, classic queueing tail.

Ranges overlap because reworkqueue and mixrework are mediated; sums need not equal 100%. December improvement (-6.7h vs Nov) likely 50%+ from lower arrivals/mix, remainder from overtime/quality reducing queue and loops — inseparable in aggregates.

### 3.4 Candidate change points

- Logging drift: **2026-03-01** (rename, duplicates, labels 911, raw fitness cliff, missingness jump); **2026-03 persistence / no second fix** (no return to 9 labels or 1% missingness through Dec confirms persistent schema, not transient).
- Behavioral drift: **2026-05-05** (Assess->RequestInfo 1324 per 100, rework 13%24%); **2026-10-01** (SeniorAssessment 722 per 100, Decision edge drop, second DFG regime; alternative 2026-07-01 priority as secondary behavioral/queue-discipline point).
- Performance drift: **2026-05 to 2026-06** (median +4.3h MayJun, p90 +11h, onset of queue regime); **2026-10-01** (second level shift median +6.3h SepOct, p90 +20h on higher volume/complexity). June-15 staffing loss sits between, consistent with gradual queue build rather than single-day jump.

### 3.5 One vs multiple change points

Data reject one global change point. March logging shift leaves performance/behavior flat; May behavioral/performance shift leaves logging metrics flat; July priority and June staffing modulate performance without new labels; October population shift creates new DFG/queue regime while logging flat and supplier recovering. Formal test would find different breakpoints per artifact family. Monitoring must use mechanism-specific windows, not one partition.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 overall | month | SLA / global surveillance, volume planning | 300 cases/month (all months satisfy) | Crude trend, change-point screening | Overaggregation hides class redistribution and Simpson's paradox |
| L1 provenance | raw label version (Screen vs Validate), duplicate flag | Isolate logging artifact; validate mapping | 50 cases per label variant per month | Raw vs mapped conformance; duplicate impact | Conditioning on duplicate (post-treatment of migration) biases causal estimates of duration |
| L2 population | supplier (Z vs non-Z) × complexity (high/standard) | Separate mix from behavior; fair comparison | 100 cases per cell per month; pool to quarter if <100 | Z effect, complexity effect, standardized rates | Complexity may be mediator (Zcomplexityrework); stratifying masks indirect effect; complexity definition drift |
| L3 policy | priority class (high fast-tracked vs standard) post-Jul; team (remaining vs left) | Test redistribution and capacity mechanisms | 100 per class per period; team 30 cases/week | Pre/post priority DID; team workload equity | Conditioning on priority (caused by complexity) induces confounding; team conditions on post-leaving assignment |
| L4 behavioral | rework (0 vs 1 RequestInfo) × Senior (yes/no) | Quantify loop cost and Senior path; outcome analysis | 50 per path per period for duration comparison | Loop penalty, Senior approval rate, temporal profile per path | Conditioning on rework/Senior (mediators) blocks total-effect estimation; use for decomposition, not for total causal effect |

Primary monitoring population: **L2-standardized non-Z standard-complexity cases plus directly standardized overall rate**. Rationale: non-Z standard cases are least affected by supplier quality shocks and seasonal complexity surges, providing a stable sentinel for true behavioral/queue drift (staffing, priority, adaptation). Reporting both sentinel crude and mix-standardized overall avoids overaggregation while keeping cells large (standard non-Z remains majority even in Oct-Nov). High-complexity and Z cells monitored secondarily for equity and supplier SLA, not as primary drift alarm, because their volatility would generate false global alarms each season.

## 9. Provisional attribution

Strongest conclusions concern March and May. The March collapse in raw fitness (0.93 to 0.55) with mapped recovery to 0.90, stable normalized DFG distance of 3 per 100, and coincident label, duplicate, and missingness jumps make a logging-semantic migration the near-certain cause of the apparent conformance break (confidence 90-95%). No performance or rework change accompanies it, so treating March as real process deterioration would be an error. The May rework surge (Assess->RequestInfo 12 to 24 per 100, rework 13% to 24%) aligned with Supplier Z jumping 18% to 43% makes supplier-driven loops highly plausible (confidence 80-85%), especially given incomplete bundles directly predict Request Info. Only plausible, not proven, because Z serves a non-random, already-complex subpopulation, so complexity confounds the estimate and no Z-stratified rework rate is supplied. Staffing-driven queueing after 06-15 is plausible and likely large (confidence 75-80%): service rises only 0.6h while queue waiting dominates and deterioration continues through August as Z share falls, fitting capacity loss with re-entrant load. Priority redistribution (confidence 50-60%) and seasonal population drift in October (confidence 80-85% for mix, lower for pure recurrence without prior-year data) interact: the October DFG distance of 64 is driven by Senior Assessment (4 to 22 per 100), consistent with more complex renewal cases, but whether priority amplified standard-case waiting cannot be separated in aggregates. Mediation prevents clean shares: mix causes rework, rework causes queueing, queueing causes WIP and censoring that depresses the Decision edge. December improvement is most uncertain, mixing season-end, overtime, and campaign effects. I would revise this account if Z-stratified rework, class-specific waiting, or prior-year seasonal data showed non-Z rework rising first, standard-class waiting flat after July, or no October complexity peak historically.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. `IF mapped fitness falls while raw-to-mapped gap stays flat and Assess->RequestInfo is stable, THEN audit the normative model and mapping before any operational intervention, BECAUSE divergence without rework signals model or synonym drift rather than behavioral drift.`
2. `IF Assess->RequestInfo rises >5 per 100 in Z cases but stays flat in non-Z standardized cases, THEN trigger supplier quality review and document-completeness checks, BECAUSE Z-specific rework implicates bundle quality rather than general assessment policy.`
3. `IF queue waiting before Assess rises at constant rework-per-case and constant complexity with stable service time, THEN escalate to capacity planning and overtime authorization, BECAUSE wait-per-visit growth under fixed load indicates resource shortfall rather than loop or mix effects.`
4. `IF standard-complexity waiting rises while high-complexity waiting falls after the priority rule with unchanged total arrivals, THEN review queue fairness and SLA trade-offs rather than adding global capacity, BECAUSE redistribution signatures indicate discipline effects, not uniform overload.`
5. `IF volume and high-complexity share rise on the renewal calendar with stable within-stratum rework and waiting, THEN treat the excursion as seasonal population drift and compare to same-month prior year, BECAUSE crude deterioration under stable standardized rates reflects case mix, not new behavior.`
6. `IF missing-resource rate exceeds 3% clustered in migrated activities or teams, THEN freeze workload and handover analyses and fix provenance at source, BECAUSE non-random missingness biases organizational mining and conditions on a collider.`
7. `IF elevated rework and Senior Assessment persist after supplier recovery and seasonal volume normalizes, THEN initiate behavioral-adaptation review of assessment criteria and Request Info reasons, BECAUSE persistence beyond its triggers suggests a new norm rather than transient backlog.`
