| E7 | P0 vs P4: current escalation practice vs changed escalation criteria/capacity | Cases reaching manual review/escalation | Final decision quality: false-approval, false-rejection, reversal or audit failure | Total effect on decision quality; not a mediation estimand | Partial; observed `outcome` is not ground truth | No external quality labels; decision thresholds may shift without observed outcome changes |

I use interventional mediation effects and controlled direct policy effects. I do not use natural direct/indirect effects because the exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounders—manual review and escalation causing queue congestion, resource assignment, and reviewer judgment—make cross-world independence implausible. This is an explicitly underidentified decomposition; the estimands above are policy-relevant targets, not uniquely identified natural effects.


Interference arises because manual and escalation queues are shared: a flagged case that enters manual review or escalation changes backlog and resource availability for other cases, violating the no-interference part of SUTVA. Exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding arises because flag assignment induces manual review/escalation, which alters queue state and resource assignment; those same mediator states are also affected by unobserved risk and complexity and influence later outcomes. Treatment-confounder feedback may arise if the flag policy changes queue congestion enough to change arrival_load, future triage priorities, or reviewer behavior.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Within-risk-score or local-threshold: Does flag affect delay holding risk roughly constant? | Compare cases within narrow risk_score bins or regression discontinuity around the flag threshold | risk_score bandwidth, complexity, arrival_load, time-of-day | Threshold is not manipulated; no sorting; local exchangeability | McCrary density test; covariate continuity at threshold; placebo thresholds | If delay discontinuity is flat, the flag-longer effect is mostly risk selection, not flag treatment |
| Queue-state-aware: Do flagged cases increase downstream wait via shared queues rather than severity? | Add queue backlog/occupancy at arrival to triage, manual, escalation; compare flag coefficient before/after conditioning | complexity, risk_score, arrival_load, queue length, time block | Queue state is measured; no unobserved priority; no residual interference | Compare flag effect with and without queue state; placebo lagged backlog | Large attenuation after conditioning means shared-queue interference explains much of the delay |
| Policy-change or randomized encouragement: What is total effect of flag policy bundle? | Cluster-randomize display-suppression or non-mandatory manual review by day/team; use assignment as instrument | cluster, time block, risk strata | Randomization works; partial interference between clusters; compliance measured | Balance checks; spillover estimates; compliance-adjusted ITT | ITT effect smaller than observational effect shows confounding/spillover exaggerated flag effect |
| Mediation-oriented: How much of flag effect is through manual review occurrence/wait? | Interventional mediation via randomized mediator distributions; or sequential g-estimation with time-varying queue state | risk_score, complexity, arrival_load, queue state, intermediate confounders | No unmeasured mediator-outcome confounders; positivity; no cross-world interference | Sensitivity analyses for mediator-outcome confounding; bounds | If indirect via manual queue is small, focus should shift to escalation or case mix |
| Negative-control outcome or exposure: Is residual confounding from hidden risk present? | Use a negative-control outcome unaffected by flag but sharing risk/queue confounders, or a below-threshold flag-like exposure | complexity, arrival_load, time block | Control outcome shares confounders but has no flag effect | Flag association with negative control; balance of control exposure | A non-null negative-control association indicates hidden confounding and weakens causal attribution |
| Sensitivity analysis for unobserved risk: How strong must hidden risk be to remove flag effect? | E-value/bias formulas for U_underlying_risk on flag and outcome | observed covariates | Specified U relationships; no interactions | Report required E-value and bounds | Low required E-value means plausible hidden risk reverses inference; high value supports robustness |
| Interference-aware: Are flagged cases causing spillover to unflagged cases? | Cluster-randomize by time blocks/teams; define exposure mapping such as share of concurrent flagged cases; estimate direct/indirect effects | cluster, case-level covariates, exposure share | Partial interference; exposure mapping correctly captures spillover | Compare control outcomes by high/low cluster-level flag proportion; spillover tests | Substantial spillover invalidates individual-level estimates and requires cluster/pooled policy evaluation |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 | Fast for flagged; median arrival-to-triage-start 0.2h | High manual queue; median 6.75h triage-to-manual wait | Long for flagged; median total 16.45h | Uncertain | High shared-queue congestion | Observational only | Baseline; do not continue unaudited |
| P1 | Slower or unchanged depending whether staff infer risk from risk_score/complexity | May fall if display drives routing; likely lower manual queue | Ambiguous, possibly lower | High if visible flag loss reduces risk action | Possibly lower, but workaround risk | Weak | Diagnostic only; not standalone |
| P2 | Retained fast triage | Large reduction in automatic manual-review demand; manual wait likely drops | Likely improves by removing mandatory review and reducing queue | Moderate for low/medium flagged cases if review is needed | Positive for unflagged; less manual congestion | Moderate | Pilot with risk-based override |
| P3 | Unchanged fast triage | Lower flagged manual wait if net new capacity; unflagged may worsen if carve-out | Improves flagged, mixed systemwide | Low/positive if review preserved | Potential negative if reallocated from unflagged | Moderate for queue bottleneck | Add if audit confirms manual wait is dominant bottleneck |
| P4 | Unchanged | Escalation queue changes with criteria/capacity; direction uncertain | Ambiguous; may improve if unnecessary escalations removed | High if stricter criteria miss true positives | Shifts demand between manual/escalation | Weak without audit data | Defer until escalation decision quality reviewed |

Select **P2** for a limited pilot. Eligibility: flagged cases with risk_score 0.65–0.85 and complexity Medium; exclude risk_score  0.90, complexity High, or cases meeting existing mandatory-review rules. Assignment: cluster-randomize half-day blocks at the team level to P0 mandatory manual review versus P2 discretionary manual review, retaining priority triage in both arms. In the P2 arm, require a documented no-review justification and allow senior or automated override within 2 hours. Queue-capacity guardrail: add protected manual slots for high-risk flagged cases and stop or alter the pilot if unflagged median manual wait rises >20% or high-risk flagged cycle time worsens >10%. Primary outcome: total cycle time. Secondary outcomes: manual review rate, escalation rate, manual queue wait, and blind audit decision quality. Minimum process data: timestamps, risk_score, complexity, arrival_load, queue backlog, resource ID, override reason, and short-term audit/adverse-event labels. Stopping conditions: quality non-inferiority violation, severe queue spillover, override frequency >10%, or pre-specified sample reached.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk:** If external audit or adverse-event data show that unflagged or discretionary-review cases have materially worse outcomes, the P2 recommendation reverses toward mandatory or protected manual review.  
2. **Queue interference:** If cluster-randomized or queue-state analysis shows large spillover to unflagged cases, and the flag effect disappears after conditioning on shared backlog, the downstream wait is not mainly flag-specific.  
3. **Decision quality:** If faster triage or discretionary review increases false approvals or audit failures, the cycle-time gain is not acceptable and P3/P4 become preferable.  
4. **Timestamp semantics:** If timestamps reflect local clock time, batch entry, or inconsistent wait/service boundaries, the stage medians may be biased and could change direction after timestamp reclassification.  
5. **Positivity or lack of overlap:** If there are too few unflagged high-risk cases or too few flagged low-risk cases in key strata, the comparisons lack support and policy inference should be restricted.  
6. **Behavior change by reviewers:** If reviewers compensate—for example, manually upgrading unflagged high-risk cases or rarely using a discretionary no-review option—the pilot arms become contaminated and the evidence uninformative.

## 9. Final synthesis

Directly observed: flagged cases are triaged faster—median arrival-to-triage-start 0.2h versus 2.5h for unflagged—but have higher manual-review occurrence, longer manual queue waits, more escalations, and longer total cycle time. The early acceleration is real in the timestamps, while total time is created downstream.

Plausible mechanism: the flag is a policy bundle. It triggers priority triage and near-mandatory manual review. Priority triage compresses early wait, but mandatory review increases manual queue demand, and subsequent escalation adds senior-team wait. Shared queues amplify these effects across flagged and unflagged cases.

Remains unidentified: how much of the flagged-longer duration is caused by the flag bundle versus risk/complexity case mix, hidden risk, queue interference, reviewer judgment, and timestamp/resource behavior. Natural mediation decompositions are not credible without stronger ignorability or randomization.

Most useful next evidence: a cluster-randomized P2 pilot retaining priority triage but replacing automatic mandatory review with discretionary, risk-based review, with quality audits and queue guardrails. It targets the largest observed downstream driver—manual-review occurrence and waiting—while preserving triage acceleration and producing decision-quality data to separate speed, congestion, and safety.
