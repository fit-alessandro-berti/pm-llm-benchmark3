## 2. Rule Interpretation

- **RR1 — Drafter/approver separation:** For each case, normalize aliases to human identities. Let `D(c)` be the set of human drafters and `A(c)` the set of human approvers. Violation if `D(c)  A(c)  `. If an actor is an unresolved service account, technical conformance is indeterminate unless reliable session evidence maps it to a human.
- **RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation:** Let `I(c)` be human implementers and `V(c)` verifiers. Violation if `I(c)  V(c)  `. Same service-account resolution rule applies.
- **RR3 — High-risk approval authority:** A HIGH-risk non-emergency `Approve_Change` is conformant only if the approver is a QM at the event timestamp or a valid QM delegate. A QM delegation is valid only if: (i) event time lies inside the effective interval; (ii) scope covers the case scope/change type; (iii) any required principal absence/unavailability is met at event time; (iv) no prohibited subdelegation occurred; and (v) there is adequate pre-event or independent evidence. A later database entry alone cannot prove an earlier delegation; it may corroborate a contemporaneous signed/registered record.
- **RR4 — Emergency substitution:** For HIGH-risk emergency cases, an IC or valid IC delegate may approve/allow implementation as an exception only when the emergency flag and safety rationale are credible. A QM or valid QM delegate must perform `Retrospective_QM_Review` within two business days. Count business days by excluding the event day, counting Monday–Friday and excluding site holidays; no holiday existed in this period, so 2026-08-15 and 2026-08-16 are weekend. For an emergency approval/implementation on 2026-08-11, the deadline is end of 2026-08-13; for 2026-08-11 events, a review on 2026-08-14 16:00 is late.
- **Principal on leave:** Leave does not by itself strip a principal’s standing authority if identity is reliably established and the role interval remains active. However, leave creates an availability conflict and weakens any absence-based delegation condition. If the principal personally acts, the action is evaluated as the principal; if a delegate acts under an absence-conditioned delegation, absence must be supportable at that event time.
- **Service accounts:** A service account is not a human identity. It can support conformance only through reliable session/FIDO/badge/workstation evidence mapping the action to an authorized human. If mapping is unreliable, the correct result is data/identity problem or indeterminate, not automatic violation.
- **RR5 — Identity evidence:** Aliases belonging to one human are merged before SoD checks. Unattributed service-account actions are flagged separately and are not automatically assigned to the workstation owner.
- **RR6 — Suspicion versus proof:** Repeated resource pairs, reciprocal approvals, shared workstations, concentrated handovers, and absent-user activity are risk indicators. They should trigger investigation, not automatic collusion findings. Collusion requires corroborating evidence of coordinated intent, concealment, or benefit.

## 6. Control Improvements

- **Delegation creation:** Require structured, pre-approved delegation records with principal, delegate, type, exact scope, effective interval, availability condition, and explicit non-subdelegation rules. Reject email-only or retroactively created delegations for high-risk approvals.
- **Effective-time recording:** Use controlled, append-only QMS records with e-signatures and audit timestamps. Do not rely on a later database migration as the sole proof of an earlier delegation; require contemporaneous signed evidence or trusted electronic records.
- **Principal availability:** Integrate HR leave/unavailability status into the workflow at event time. If a principal acts while on leave, require strong re-authentication and an urgency justification; if a delegate acts, verify the absence condition dynamically.
- **Alias management:** Maintain a single identity master mapping all aliases to a human identity before SoD checks. Alert when draft/approve or implement/verify activities resolve to the same human through different aliases.
- **Individual authentication:** Eliminate shared human accounts and prohibit service-account use for high-risk approvals/verifications unless a named human is strongly authenticated in the session. Use FIDO/MFA, workstation lock, and per-action re-authentication for high-risk activities.
- **Emergency review:** Enforce automatic two-business-day retrospective QM review deadlines, with escalation and explicit evidence of emergency rationale. Validate that the retrospective reviewer’s QM delegation scope covers the emergency change type.
- **Pair rotation:** Introduce pair-rotation and independent-review sampling for HIGH-risk production changes. Monitor repeated drafter–approver pairs and reciprocal approvals, but use them as risk indicators, not automatic findings.
- **Network monitoring:** Periodically review handover and working-together networks for unusual concentration, reciprocal approvals, and shared-service-account edges. Require corroborating evidence before any collusion determination.
- **Evidential retention:** Preserve badge, VPN, FIDO/token, workstation login, CCTV where lawful, e-signature, workflow comments, and delegation records. Define retention periods sufficient for retrospective audits.

The apparent nonconformance is a mixture of genuine authority/SoD failures, identity/data-quality problems, and organizational-pattern risk. Clear authority failures exist in CHG-502, CHG-507, CHG-508, CHG-509, and CHG-510, with CHG-505 also nonconformant on the retrospective QM review timing/scope unless stronger scope evidence exists. CHG-503, CHG-504, and CHG-506 are primarily identity/evidence problems: the resources may be technically conformant, but the human attribution is not strong enough for a high-risk environment. The Clara–Ben repeated-pair and reciprocal approvals are a systemic indicator requiring review, but they are not proof of collusion.

Uncertainty is highest for CHG-503, CHG-504, and CHG-506. The three highest-priority investigations are: (1) CHG-506 account use during Ben’s certified sick leave and possible credential misuse; (2) CHG-503 `svc_qms` verification from shared WS-DM-04, including possible self-verification by Diego; and (3) emergency authority and retrospective QM review for CHG-505/CHG-510, including CI-44 scope, invalid D-IC-04 subdelegation, and D-QM-01 scope limitations.
