# Disruption, Resilience, and Recovery Analysis — Pharmaceutical Wholesaler Cyber Incident

## 1. Disruption timeline

**T0 (Day -30 to Day -1) — Baseline.**
Operational state: fully system-supported order-to-delivery flow. The dominant variant is the straight-through normative path (Receive order -> Validate authorization -> Inventory reservation -> Pick -> Pack -> Quality/release check -> Dispatch -> Proof of delivery -> Invoice close), with modest, controlled loops for compliance holds (700), backorders (900), and repack/recheck (450). Risk posture: strong — fitness 0.96, 91% fitting traces, traceability exceptions 0.8%, cold-chain documentation gaps 0.4%, dual-control misses 0.2%. Cycle times: Hospital-critical median 8h/p90 15h; Pharmacy-standard 23h/49h; Wholesale-bulk 36h/72h. This is the reference envelope against which everything else must be judged.

**T1 (Day 0 morning) — Impact.**
The warehouse management system goes down. Electronic authorization, inventory reservation, scanning, quality/release gating, and proof-of-delivery capture all lose their system backbone simultaneously. The organization must choose between stopping (protecting compliance but abandoning hospitals) and improvising (protecting supply but degrading evidence).

**T2 (Day 0 afternoon to Day 2) — Manual workaround phase.**
Operational state: fully improvised. The DFG shows an entirely new process shape: Emergency triage becomes the de facto front gate (5,100 of 5,600 orders), splitting into Manual authorization check (2,500), Skip system authorization check (1,600), and Hospital-critical fast lane (1,000); 500 orders are deferred at intake. Downstream, Manual pick list (3,920), Dispatch without scan (2,600 handoffs, of which 1,800 bypass even the Temporary release control), Phone proof of delivery, and Spreadsheet invoice hold replace system steps.
Risk posture: severely degraded on paper and in substance — fitness 0.71, only 44% fitting traces; traceability exceptions jump ~12x to 9.5%; cold-chain documentation gaps 12x to 4.8%; controlled-substance dual-control misses ~14x to 2.9%; rework 12.5%; cancellations 7.8%. Move-on-model deviations confirm that Quality/release checks, scans, proofs of delivery, and temperature records are genuinely missing, not just recorded differently.
The mission-critical signal: Hospital-critical median cycle time degrades only 8h -> 10h (p90 15h -> 21h) and fill rate holds at 91%, while Pharmacy-standard collapses (median 23h -> 54h, fill rate 61%) and Wholesale-bulk stretches to 96h median. The organization deliberately sacrificed standard segments to protect hospitals — that asymmetry is the clearest evidence of intentional, graceful degradation rather than chaos.

**T3 (Day 3 to Day 6) — Partial recovery.**
Operational state: hybrid. Order intake returns but inventory sync and scanning are unreliable, so manual and electronic variants coexist. Metrics recover partially: fitness 0.82, 59% fitting; traceability exceptions 5.4%; rework 9.0%; cancellations 5.2%. Hospital-critical is nearly back to baseline (median 9h); Pharmacy-standard (38h) and Wholesale-bulk (84h) still carry the backlog. Risk posture: the most dangerous phase in one respect — stale printed inventory snapshots (pattern D) are now 3-6 days old, and teams may still be trusting them while system data is only partially reliable.

**T4 (Day 7 to Day 21) — Stabilization.**
Operational state: normal systems mostly restored, but the DFG shows a persistent "repair layer": Backlog prioritization (1,500), Reconcile manual reservation (1,400, of which 300 end in cancellation), Proof-of-delivery remediation (1,200), and Audit remediation (2,300 cases after Invoice close). Fitness recovers to 0.90/76% but not to baseline; traceability exceptions (2.2%), cold-chain gaps (0.9%), dual-control misses (0.5%), rework (5.8%) all remain elevated. Median cycle times look nearly recovered (Hospital-critical 8h), which is exactly the trap: operational KPIs signal "back to normal" while a long compliance tail (Invoice close -> Audit remediation, flagged as a temporal-profile deviation) continues. Roughly 6.6% of stabilization cases pass through audit remediation after they have already been invoiced and closed. Risk posture: operationally green, compliance amber — hidden workload and residual evidence gaps persist beyond apparent recovery.

## 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

The distinction in this case is not "deviated from the baseline model" versus "conformed." During T2, conformance fitness of 0.71 with 44% fitting traces is not itself the problem — a rigid process that conformed perfectly by halting would have failed hospitals. The distinction is: **did the adaptation preserve the *function* of the control through an equivalent compensating mechanism with contemporaneous evidence, or did it preserve *throughput* by silently removing the control and hoping to reconstruct evidence later?**

Three tests separate them, all applicable to this log:

1. **Control-equivalence test.** Does a competent substitute perform the control's function at the time of the transaction? B (trained compliance staff with offline lists) passes; C (assumed standing authorization) fails — nothing checked eligibility, an assumption did.
2. **Contemporaneous-evidence test.** Is evidence captured when the event happens, even if on paper? F and H partially pass; G fails outright — 2,600 dispatches with no custody record cannot be evidenced later, only asserted. The move-on-model deviations (missing quality checks, scans, PODs, temperature records) show real absence of events, not just logging differences.
3. **Visibility test.** Was the deviation declared, bounded, and queued for reconciliation — or invisible until an audit found it? I and J were explicit, visible decisions (deferral, prioritization). The danger pattern in T4 is the inverse: cycle-time medians recovered to near-baseline by Day 7 while 2,300 audit-remediation cases and 1,400 inventory reconciliations continued behind them. Metrics that look recovered while a compliance tail persists are the signature of concealed (or at least under-reported) noncompliance risk.

**True resilience:** A (the prioritization itself), B, F (the concept), I, J, K, D-in-its-first-24-hours. These sacrificed speed or convenience for lower-risk segments while keeping controls functionally alive for the highest-risk products and customers, and the segment data proves it worked (hospital fill 91%, controlled-substance dual-control misses contained at 2.9% versus what full abandonment would have produced).

**Risky shortcuts / concealed noncompliance:** C and G unambiguously. C removed the authorization control entirely for 1,600 orders; G removed chain of custody for 2,600 dispatches. Both traded a regulated control for throughput, which the question's own constraint correctly flags as non-resilience. E's "reconstruct scans later" and H's late-entered phone PODs sit close to this line because reconstruction substitutes inference for evidence.

**Ambiguous, depending on risk appetite:** D (stale snapshots — fine at hour 6, reckless at day 4; the risk is time-dependent, and the log cannot tell us exactly when teams should have stopped trusting it), H (adequate for ambient pharmacy orders, inadequate for controlled substances), A's authorization bypass component (defensible for a hospital's emergency ambient order, indefensible for controlled substances), and L (a legitimate safety net at small scale, a concealment mechanism if it becomes the routine way controls get "done").

One more constraint the data enforces: after-the-fact remediation (L) must not be scored as compliance. A temperature excursion that was never recorded did or did not happen physically; back-filling documentation resolves the paperwork, not the patient risk. For cold-chain product, the honest T2 posture is "4.8% of shipments have unknowable temperature history," and some of those may warrant customer notification or product disposition decisions, not just record repair.

## 4. Recovery process model

The redesign is a pre-authorized **Degraded Operations Mode (DOM)** with three levels, entered and exited by explicit criteria, in which every T2 improvisation becomes a designed, evidence-preserving variant.

**Notation:** BPMN-style bullets. `[Gateway]` = decision point, `<<control>>` = mandatory control that may change form but never disappears, `(evidence: ...)` = the artifact that must exist for the case to close cleanly.

**Trigger conditions (entry/exit, declared by an incident commander and logged):**
- **DOM-1 (system degraded):** WMS or scanning unreliable but partially available. Entry: error/timeout rate on core transactions > defined threshold for 2 hours. Corresponds to T3.
- **DOM-2 (system down):** WMS unavailable. Entry: confirmed outage > 4 hours or declared cyber incident. Corresponds to T2.
- **DOM-3 (data untrusted):** systems up but integrity suspect (relevant post-cyber-incident). Entry: security team flags possible data tampering. Manual verification overlays electronic records.
- **Exit** requires all of: system stability > 24h, inventory reconciliation variance < threshold, and compliance sign-off — not merely "systems are back." Exit from DOM starts, not ends, the reconciliation phase.

**Process flow under DOM-2 (the deep-outage mode):**

- START -> **Receive order** (paper/phone/portal cache)
- -> **[Triage gateway]** using a pre-published priority matrix (this codifies A, I, J):
  - **P1:** Hospital-critical, or any life-critical product -> fast lane (front of every queue; controls still apply)
  - **P2:** Pharmacy-standard with clinical urgency flag
  - **P3:** Pharmacy-standard routine -> accepted with extended promise date (explicit customer notification, evidence: notification log)
  - **P4:** Wholesale-bulk -> deferred to backlog queue with committed review date and a maximum deferral age (e.g., 72h) before mandatory customer contact
- -> **[Authorization gateway]** — replaces the B/C split with a risk-tiered rule; the control never disappears, only its form changes:
  - Controlled-substance, cold-chain, recall-sensitive, or new/changed customer -> **<<Manual authorization check>>** by compliance staff using offline lists refreshed daily in peacetime (evidence: signed checklist with checker ID, timestamp)
  - Ambient product AND customer on the pre-ratified **standing-authorization whitelist** (approved by compliance quarterly, with hard product exclusions) -> **<<Whitelist authorization>>** (evidence: whitelist version + order match record). This is the designed, bounded replacement for pattern C.
- -> **<<Inventory check>>** against snapshot with time-to-live (codifies D):
  - Snapshot age < 24h: check against snapshot
  - 24-72h: snapshot + physical confirmation for controlled/cold-chain/recall-sensitive picks
  - > 72h: physical cycle-count confirmation required for all picks
- -> **Manual pick** using pre-printed DOM pick sheets with mandatory batch/lot, quantity, picker ID, timestamp fields; sheet photographed at completion (evidence: photo to shared drive/phone; fixes E)
- -> **Manual pack** -> **[Release gateway]** (fixes F's coverage and closes G's bypass — there is no path around this gateway):
  - Controlled-substance: **<<100% pharmacist dual-control check>>** (evidence: two signatures)
  - Cold-chain: **<<100% check incl. temperature log/indicator verification>>** (evidence: temp record attached; battery-powered data loggers in every cold-chain tote as standard DOM kit)
  - Recall-sensitive: **<<100% lot verification>>**
  - Ambient: risk-based sample (e.g., 10%)
- -> **<<Dispatch with manual custody record>>** (replaces G): paper manifest with tote IDs, lots, courier signature, timestamp; photographed (evidence: signed manifest photo)
- -> **<<Proof of delivery>>** (modifies H):
  - Controlled/cold-chain: signed paper POD photographed at delivery; phone confirmation supplementary only
  - Ambient/standard: phone POD acceptable, logged same-day with caller ID and time
- -> **Invoice hold ledger** (single controlled register, not scattered spreadsheets) -> END of operational flow; case tagged `DOM` with a per-case evidence checklist.

**Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow (codifies K and L; runs as a managed workstream, not an afterthought):**
- Starts **during** the outage: a reconciliation cell logs every DOM case and its evidence checklist in real time, so the remediation queue is born complete rather than discovered.
- On DOM exit: (1) inventory reconciliation with variance reporting (target: complete within 5 business days); (2) evidence back-entry — transcribing photographed sheets/manifests/PODs into the system, flagged as `manually-captured`, never as normal scans; (3) gap classification per case: *evidence recovered* / *evidence unrecoverable — assess disposition* (for cold-chain: quarantine or customer notification decision; for controlled substances: regulatory reportability assessment); (4) a burn-down chart of the remediation queue reported to management alongside operational KPIs until the queue reaches zero. Recovery is declared complete only when the audit queue closes, not when cycle times normalize.

**Minimum controls by product/customer risk (the non-negotiable floor in any DOM level):**

| Risk class | Authorization | Release check | Custody at dispatch | POD | Temperature |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Controlled-substance | Manual, 100%, dual sign-off | 100% pharmacist dual control | Signed manifest, mandatory | Signed paper, photographed | n/a |
| Cold-chain | Manual, 100% | 100% incl. temp verification | Signed manifest, mandatory | Signed paper preferred | Data logger per tote, mandatory |
| Recall-sensitive | Manual, 100% | 100% lot verification | Signed manifest, mandatory | Phone acceptable | n/a |
| Ambient / whitelisted customer | Whitelist match | Sample | Manifest (may be batched) | Phone acceptable | n/a |

If a case cannot meet its floor, the designed outcome is *hold or defer*, never *ship without the control*.

## 5. Resilience indicators

**Leading indicators (during the incident — detect emerging risk before it becomes an audit tail):**
- **Critical-segment service protection:** Hospital-critical fill rate (target >= 90%, as achieved) and median cycle time versus baseline (alert if p90 exceeds baseline p90 by > 50%). Measured per shift.
- **Control-floor compliance rate:** % of shipped DOM cases meeting their minimum-control floor from Section 4, by risk class, measured daily. Any controlled-substance or cold-chain case below floor triggers same-day escalation. This is the direct real-time replacement for discovering 2.9% dual-control misses after the fact.
- **Evidence-capture completeness at ship:** % of dispatches with a signed custody manifest and % of picks with photographed pick sheets (target 100%; in T2 this was effectively 0% for the 1,800 orders that bypassed release and shipped without scan).
- **Snapshot/data staleness:** age of inventory snapshot in hours and pick-error rate against it (proxy: same-day correction rate); triggers the escalating physical-count rules.
- **Deferral queue age and fairness:** oldest deferred order age (cap 72h before mandatory customer contact); deferral and cancellation rates by customer group — if two Pharmacy-standard customers with similar orders have materially different deferral rates, the triage rule is being applied informally (the risk flagged for I and J).
- **Compliance-capacity utilization:** manual-authorization throughput versus demand; when demand exceeds capacity, that is the moment C-type skipping historically emerged — the indicator should trigger activating the backup checker pool, not silent skipping.

**Lagging indicators (recovery quality — prevent the "looks recovered but isn't" trap of T4):**
- **Traceability exception rate** by phase versus 0.8% baseline, with a defined return-to-baseline deadline (e.g., within 10 business days of DOM exit; the incident took > 21 days and never fully returned, ending at 2.2%).
- **Control-completeness recovery:** cold-chain documentation gap and dual-control miss rates back to <= 0.4% / <= 0.2%, plus the count of *unrecoverable* evidence gaps (the number that matters for regulators and product disposition).
- **Audit-remediation backlog burn-down:** queue size, age distribution, and closure rate, reported until zero. The 2,300-case tail with ~200 apparently still open at period end must be visible on the same dashboard as cycle times, so operational recovery cannot be declared while it persists.
- **Reconciliation outcome quality:** % of manual reservations reconciled cleanly versus ending in cancellation (300 of 1,400 = 21% cancelled in T4 — a customer-harm signal); inventory variance found at reconciliation.
- **Rework and cancellation normalization:** return to <= 3.5% / <= 2.1%, by segment — segment-level view prevents hospital recovery from masking continued pharmacy degradation (Pharmacy-standard median was still 28h vs. 23h baseline at T4).
- **Fairness after the fact:** distribution of total delay, deferral, and cancellation across Hospital-critical / Pharmacy-standard / Wholesale-bulk, compared against the published triage policy; deviations indicate informal rule application.
- **Time-to-full-recovery:** measured to the close of the audit queue, not to cycle-time normalization — the incident's true duration was > 21 days, not 7.

## 6. Recommendations for future disruptions

Prioritized; effort = Low/Medium/High.

1. **Ban zero-evidence dispatch; deploy manual custody manifests and paper POD kits.** (Effort: Low — pre-printed forms, phone-photo procedure, courier briefing.) Benefit: eliminates the single largest traceability gap (G, 2,600 unscanned handoffs) and hardens H for controlled products; directly shrinks the audit-remediation tail. Residual risk: paper can be lost or illegible; mitigated by photograph-at-signature.
2. **Pre-ratify the standing-authorization whitelist and risk-tiered authorization rule.** (Effort: Medium — compliance review, quarterly refresh, offline distribution.) Benefit: removes the incentive for ad-hoc authorization skipping (C, 1,600 orders) by giving teams a fast lawful path for low-risk ambient orders; frees scarce manual-check capacity (B) for controlled/cold-chain. Residual risk: whitelist staleness (a customer loses a license mid-quarter); mitigated by daily peacetime sync of revocations to the offline list.
3. **Codify Degraded Operations Mode with entry/exit triggers, triage matrix, and the minimum-control floor; train and drill it.** (Effort: Medium-High — playbook, twice-yearly drill including a full manual shift.) Benefit: converts T2's improvisation into rehearsed procedure; protects the hospital fill rate result while preventing the control collapse; makes J's prioritization and I's deferrals fair and auditable by writing the rules down in advance. Residual risk: playbooks decay; drills mitigate.
4. **Stand up the reconciliation/audit cell as a real-time function with its own dashboard.** (Effort: Medium.) Benefit: eliminates the hidden-workload problem — the remediation queue is created during the outage and burned down visibly, so management sees true recovery status instead of prematurely normalized cycle times. Residual risk: cell competes for staff during the incident; mitigate with cross-trained back-office staff.
5. **Cold-chain DOM kit: battery data loggers in every cold-chain tote plus a 100% manual release rule.** (Effort: Low-Medium — hardware purchase, SOP.) Benefit: closes the worst substantive (not just documentary) gap — 4.8% of cold-chain shipments in T2 had unknowable temperature history, which is a patient-safety issue no back-fill can repair. Residual risk: logger failure; mitigated by indicator strips as secondary evidence.
6. **Time-boxed inventory snapshot procedure with escalating physical verification.** (Effort: Low.) Benefit: prevents D's time-dependent decay from driving the 12.5% rework rate and phantom-stock cancellations discovered at reconciliation. Residual risk: physical counts slow picking in extended outages — an accepted, explicit trade.
7. **Resilient IT measures: hardened/offline-capable WMS snapshot exports (automatic morning snapshot to an isolated store), plus recovery-sequence testing with the business.** (Effort: High.) Benefit: shortens T2 and makes D reliable by construction; reduces dependence on all manual patterns. Residual risk: cyber incidents can outrun any single technical safeguard — the DOM playbook remains necessary.

## 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should explicitly **accept** that during a severe disruption, service to Pharmacy-standard and Wholesale-bulk customers will be deliberately degraded — longer cycle times, deferrals, and some cancellations — to protect hospital-critical supply, and that fitness to the normative process model will fall, because designed deviation under a declared Degraded Operations Mode is the mechanism of resilience, not a failure of it. The T2 evidence justifies this: a 91% hospital fill rate during a total WMS outage is a result worth paying for in bulk-order delay. Management should **not accept** three things at any throughput price: shipment of controlled-substance, cold-chain, or recall-sensitive product without its minimum control floor (real authorization, 100% release check, custody evidence, verifiable temperature record); dispatch of any product with zero contemporaneous custody evidence; and the declaration of "recovery" while an audit-remediation and reconciliation backlog persists — after-the-fact record repair is a safety net, not a substitute for real-time control, and a reconstructed temperature record does not un-break a cold chain. The **evidence that would change this recommendation**: if post-incident sampling showed the whitelisted ambient orders shipped under pattern C had a materially elevated rate of genuine authorization failures (not just missing paperwork), the whitelist scope should be narrowed or withdrawn; conversely, if drill data showed the 100% manual release floor makes hospital-critical cycle times breach clinically dangerous thresholds during an outage, management should invest in more trained release capacity (the B constraint) rather than relax the floor — the floor moves only if regulators formally accept an alternative control, not because throughput demands it.
