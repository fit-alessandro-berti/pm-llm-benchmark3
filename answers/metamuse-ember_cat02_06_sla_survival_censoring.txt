# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

[Assumption A1] Time origin is `Submit`, because the SLA is measured from `Submit`. [Assumption A2] Every case in the extract enters the risk set at `Submit`; there is no observed delayed entry or left truncation. For terminal rows, `duration_or_age_h` is the event time; for `open` rows it is the right-censoring time at the single observation cutoff. The horizon is $\tau=72$ hours. Follow-up in the extract extends to 110 hours.

[Obs] The 32 cases comprise: 13 resolved (9 by 72h, 4 after 72h: S03 76h, S20 74h, S28 88h, S13 110h); 3 rejected; 4 withdrawn; 3 transferred (S08 68h, S21 26h, S32 96h); and 9 open (4 already aged >72h: S09 92h, S11 81h, S25 79h, S26 104h; 5 aged <72h: S31 23h, S15 35h, S10 47h, S29 51h, S24 67h).

**E1 — Primary, $F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)=P(T\le72,\ \text{event}=\text{Resolve})$**, the cumulative incidence (CIF) of internal resolution by 72h in the presence of competing exits. Its complement, $1-F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)$, is “not internally resolved by 72h”. This is the correct customer-facing SLA estimand if the promise is internal resolution by 72h. [Est, primary AJ, §4] $F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)\approx30.4\%$, so $\approx69.6\%$ were not internally resolved by 72h. Purely logical crude bounds, making no censoring assumption, are $[9/32,14/32]=[28.1\%,43.8\%]$, where the upper bound assumes all 5 open cases aged <72h resolve by 72h. Transferred cases are competing exits under the primary definition (§2).

**E2 — Backlog, $S_{\text{any}}(72)=P(T_{\text{terminal}}>72)$**, the probability of still having no terminal disposition of any kind at 72h. [Est] $\approx38.0\%$. The complement, $1-S_{\text{any}}(72)\approx62.0\%$, is “any terminal disposition by 72h” (resolve, withdraw, reject, or transfer internally). E2 answers staffing/backlog questions, not customer success. Note a case withdrawn at 27h counts as *not resolved* in E1 but *not pending* in E2; timely legitimate rejection/withdrawal must not automatically be labelled an SLA breach without a disposition policy — §7 therefore reports the stacked components, not only E1.

**E3 — Net (hypothetical) resolution, $1-S^{net}_{\text{Resolve}}(72)$**, estimated by Kaplan–Meier censoring all competing events. [Est] $\approx36.1\%$. This describes the resolution process *if* withdrawal/rejection/transfer were removed. It is a process-diagnosis estimand, not a real-world customer probability, and it overstates E1 (36.1% vs 30.4%).

**E4 — Post-breach conditional, $P(\text{Resolve in }(72,\infty]\mid\text{no terminal by }72)$**, for escalation decisions after 72h. Only 9 cases are at risk just before 72h, and only 1 case remains at 110h, so E4 is defined but not reliably estimable from this extract; any number would be tail-driven. Report it only descriptively with risk sets.

**Decision mapping:** E1  SLA reporting/customer promise; E2  workload and queue staffing; E3  diagnosing whether the resolution machinery itself is slow; E4  post-breach case handling. Using one number for all four decisions is the core ambiguity to avoid.

| Case | E1 (resolve by 72) | E2 (pending at 72) | E3 (net) |
|---|---|---|---|
| S03, resolved 76h | Not resolved by 72; contributes to late resolution only | Pending at 72 (terminal after horizon) | Non-event by 72; event at 76 |
| S09, open 92h | Known not resolved by 72: already breached; censored at 92 only for post-72 follow-up | Pending at 72 and at cutoff | Censored at 92 |
| S06, withdrawn 27h | Competing failure: never adds to $F_{\text{Resolve}}$ | Not pending at 72 (terminal before horizon) | Censored at 27 for the net hazard |
| S08, transferred 68h | Under T1: not internally resolved by 72 (competing exit); under T2/T3: unknown external outcome (§2) | Under T1: internally terminated, not pending; under T2: censored at 68 | Censored at 68 in the net analysis |

An “already-observed breach” is a case known, at or before 72h, not to have internally resolved by 72h (resolved after 72h, open >72h, or a competing exit before 72h under T1). A case open at 35h (S15) is *not* yet a breach; it is right-censored before the horizon.

# 2. Event and censoring encoding

Codes below are for E1 unless stated. Time is always `duration_or_age_h`.

| observed_status | E1 code | E2 code (any terminal) | E3 net code | Censoring rule (primary) | Sensitivity alternatives |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| resolved | 1 = Resolve event | 1 | 1 | None; event at duration | None |
| rejected | 2 = competing | 1 | 0 (censored at event) | Competing event, not ordinary censoring | Count as timely disposition in a disposition estimand; censor only for E3 |
| withdrawn | 2 = competing | 1 | 0 (censored at event) | Competing event | As for rejected; stress test: dormancy reclassification of opens (§6) |
| transferred | T1: 2; T2: 0; T3: 1 or 2 (bounds) | T1: 1; T2: 0 | 0 | See T1–T3 below | T1–T3 below |
| open | 0 | 0 | 0 | Right-censored at age (administrative cutoff) | Informative-censoring stress tests (§6); IPCW only with richer covariates |

**Transferred — three plausible treatments.**

**T1 (primary): competing internal exit.** Internal resolution becomes impossible because observation stops. Defensible for the internal-SLA question. Consequence: S08, S21, S32 never contribute to $F_{\text{Resolve}}$.

**T2: right-censoring at transfer.** The estimand becomes resolution *anywhere*, with the external time unobserved. [Assumption] Censoring is independent given observed covariates — strong, because all 3 transfers are P1 and escalated (S08, S21, S32), i.e. censoring is concentrated in the hardest stratum. This is the most likely to be informative.

**T3: informative-censoring bounds / pattern-mixture.** No single point assumption: worst case = never externally resolved by 72h (equivalent to T1 for E1 at 72h); best case = externally resolved at transfer; or a delta-adjustment in which the post-transfer external resolution hazard is $\delta\times$ the internal hazard of comparable pending cases, $\delta\in\{0,0.5,1,2\}$. $\delta$ is unidentifiable from internal data alone; T3 is reported as a range, not a point estimate. Simply deleting transfers is not a fourth valid option — it is shown only as a bias demonstration.

Withdrawal/rejection are competing events for E1, not non-informative censoring: censoring them would silently change the estimand to E3. For E3 they are censored, but that censoring may itself be informative (e.g. a case about to be rejected is unlikely to resolve), which is why E3 is secondary. Open cases are retained in every analysis; dropping them is never the primary analysis.

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

**Survivor / conditioning-on-the-future bias.** [Obs] The 13 resolved cases have mean duration 49.8h and median 41h, and 4/13 (30.8%) resolved after 72h. This conditions on eventually resolving, which requires surviving withdrawal, rejection and transfer. It excludes all 7 reject/withdraw cases, all 3 transfers and all 9 opens. The resulting “30.8% late” is not comparable to E1’s $\approx69.6\%$ not resolved by 72h, because the denominators and the event definitions differ.

**Informative censoring through transfer.** All transfers are P1 and escalated. Removing or censoring them (T2) preferentially removes P1 cases with the worst internal trajectories: only 2/8 P1 cases resolved internally in the extract, versus 8/14 P2 and 3/10 P3 cases. Any analysis that deletes transfers therefore flatters P1 and escalated cases.

**Competing risks.** P3 cases exit early by rejection/withdrawal (6/10). Treating those exits as censoring (KM) inflates the apparent resolution probability (E3 36.1% vs E1 30.4%) and hides the disposition mix that operations actually sees.

**Cohort imbalance and non-random assignment.** Cohort A has 15 cases (P1 3, P2 7, P3 5; escalated 6/15) and Cohort B 17 (P1 5, P2 7, P3 5; escalated 6/17). Early `AutoCheck` (second activity) occurs for 12/17 B cases but 0/15 A cases; 5 B cases (S17, S20, S23, S26, S30) are non-compliant with the new pathway. Assignment is by era, not randomisation, so cohort contrasts confound the policy with case mix, calendar period, workload and compliance selection.

**Open cases below vs above 72h are different populations.** S10, S15, S24, S29, S31 (<72h) are censored before the horizon: their 72h status is genuinely unknown and a censoring assumption is required. S09, S11, S25, S26 (>72h) are *known* E1 failures and, conditional on being pending at 72h, are a selected slow/dormant subgroup (last-event gaps 31, 55, 48, 72h). Pooling them into “still open” without the horizon distinction mixes unknown futures with certain breaches. S26 (age 104h, gap 72h) and S11 (81h, gap 55h) look dormant; S31 (23h, gap 3h) looks recently active — the same `open` label covers very different states.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

| Method | What it targets | Key assumption / caution | Fit here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kaplan–Meier (single event) | Net survival if competing events were removed | Competing events act as independent censoring | Secondary only (E3). Alone it overstates E1 and hides competing mix |
| Aalen–Johansen / CIF | Real-world cumulative incidence per cause; CIFs + $S$ sum to 1 | Independent administrative censoring for opens; transfer assumption explicit | **Primary** for E1/E2. Non-parametric, matches the stacked operational question |
| Cause-specific hazards (Cox) | Instantaneous rate of one event among those still event-free | Proportional hazards; competing events censored (possibly informatively) | Secondary/descriptive. Only 3 rejects, 4 withdrawals, 3 transfers — regression coefficients would be unstable/separated |
| Subdistribution hazards (Fine–Gray) | Direct modelling of the CIF | Proportional subdistribution hazards; HR is not a simple hazard ratio | Not primary. With $n=32$ and separation, it adds assumption without adding information; keep as sensitivity in larger data |
| Restricted mean time (RMST) | $\int_0^\tau S(t)dt$: expected time spent pending up to $\tau$ | Choice of $\tau$; tail beyond ~88h has 5 at risk | **Secondary.** [Est] RMST pending to 72h $\approx50.4$h — interpretable in hours for staffing, no proportional-hazards assumption |
| Landmarking | Conditional outcomes given still pending at landmark $L$ | Landmark population is selected; do not compare landmark groups as if randomised | Secondary for E4 and for stage-at-$L$ questions. At $L=72$ only 9 at risk, so descriptive only |
| Multi-state model | Transition intensities and state occupancy across process states | Needs exact state-entry/exit times | Conceptual target model (§5), not fittable from total durations alone |

**Recommendation.** Primary: Aalen–Johansen estimation of $F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)$, $F_{\text{competing}}(72)$ and $S_{\text{any}}(72)$ overall and stratified by cohort and priority, with numbers at risk printed under every estimate and bootstrap (case-resampling, stratified by cohort) confidence intervals — expected to be wide, which is itself the honest answer at $n=32$. Secondary: E3 net KM, RMST-pending to 72h, and a descriptive 72h landmark. No multivariable Cox/Fine–Gray model is primary: escalation is deterministic for P1 (8/8) and absent for P3 (0/10), so priority and escalation are not separately identifiable, and events-per-cause are in single digits. If a regression is demanded for planning, it should be penalised, pre-registered as exploratory, and reported with sensitivity analyses rather than as a causal effect.

Stage hazards are the quantities of interest — e.g. the hazard of `Resolve` while in `Review`, of `Transfer` while in `Escalate`, of `Reject` while in `Screen` — but [Assumption A3] the extract gives only total duration and activity *order*, not state-entry times. Stage-specific sojourn times and transition intensities therefore cannot be estimated from these data; only total-time hazards conditional on last observed stage (landmark) are defensible now. Early rejects (S05 9h, S23 11h, both `Submit>Screen>Reject`) are consistent with a screening-stage rejection hazard, but with 3 rejects this is a hypothesis, not an estimate.

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

| # | Analysis | What varies | Headline [Est] |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Primary (T1) | Transfer = competing; withdraw/reject = competing; opens censored | $F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)=30.4\%$, $S_{\text{any}}(72)=38.0\%$ |
| S2 | T2 | Transfer = independent censoring (“anywhere” resolution) | $F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)\approx31.3\%$; but eventual external resolution is not identified — the estimate assumes transferred cases behaved like those who stayed |
| S3 | T3 bounds | Transfer = resolved at transfer (best case) vs never (worst case); $\delta\in\{0,0.5,1,2\}$ pattern-mixture | Best-case $F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)\approx37.9\%$: a $\approx$7.5pp range from transfer handling alone |
| S4 | Net / disposition | Withdraw/reject censored (E3) vs counted as timely disposition | Net $36.1\%$ vs any-terminal-by-72 $62.0\%$: conclusions about “SLA success” flip depending on whether legitimate early disposition counts as success |
| S5 | Informative open censoring | Opens with gap >24h reclassified as withdrawn at last event (age  gap: S11 at 26h, S26 at 32h, S25 at 31h, S09 at 61h) as a stress test; or post-censor hazard multiplied by $\delta$ | $F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)$ moves modestly but $S_{\text{any}}(72)$ and the backlog story change materially; this tests dormancy, it does not assert it |
| S6 | Cohort/policy | Crude cohort AJ vs priority-standardised vs per-protocol (early-AutoCheck) | Crude: A $29.2\%$ vs B $31.6\%$ (+2.4pp for B). Priority-standardised to the pooled mix: A $\approx31.8\%$ vs B $\approx31.0\%$ — the sign reverses, within noise |

**Robust conclusion:** a majority were not internally resolved by 72h under every transfer treatment (resolved-by-72 stays in the 30–38% range). **Reversible conclusions:** any B-over-A benefit (S6 and the transfer mix: B has 2 transfers vs A’s 1), any “escalation harms/helps” claim (escalated cases are exactly the P1/transfer/open cases), any “rework helps” claim (3/3 resolved but selected), and the priority ranking (P2 $F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)\approx43\%$ > P1 $\approx25\%$ > P3 $\approx20\%$, driven largely by competing exits, not resolution speed). None of these should be reported as findings.

# 7. Operational interpretation

Convert survival outputs into decisions, not causal claims. **Alerts:** use landmark rules, not scores — e.g. at 24h check that a case has left `Screen` (S31 was still in `Screen` at 23h); at 48–72h flag cases still in `Review`/`Escalate` with a large inactivity gap (the >24h-gap opens S09, S11, S25, S26 are the archetype of dormant breaches). An alert predicts *who is likely to breach*; whether acting on the alert helps requires an experiment (randomised or stepped-wedge alert rollout). **Workload:** E2 says that per 100 submissions roughly 30 resolve, 32 exit by a competing disposition and 38 are still pending at 72h — staff Review capacity and transfer follow-up against the pending/state-occupancy numbers, and treat `Transfer` as an external SLA handover with its own acknowledgement clock, never as internal success. **Governance:** the dashboard denominator is all `Submit`s; report the stacked AJ components (resolved / withdrawn / rejected / transferred / still pending) by cohort and priority with numbers at risk, and ban “% late among resolved” as a KPI. **Calibration and fairness:** compare predicted $F_{\text{Resolve}}(72)$ against stratum-specific AJ estimates (calibration-in-large) by priority and cohort — currently the strata are P1 $\approx25\%$, P2 $\approx43\%$, P3 $\approx20\%$ with wide uncertainty — and, once a predictive alert exists, report sensitivity for late resolution and alert burden per 100 cases within each priority×cohort stratum at a fixed alert rate. Two equity risks need audit: P3 cases are never escalated in this extract (0/10), so an escalation-based alert system would systematically under-serve P3; and P1 cases are disproportionately transferred, so counting transfers as closures would mask the worst-served group.

        dR = int(((enc["time"] == t) & (enc["code"] == 1)).sum())
        dC = int(((enc["time"] == t) & (enc["code"] == 2)).sum())

# 9. Limitations and additional data

In priority order, by which assumption each item would relax:

1. **Exact event timestamps for every activity, plus case start dates and the cutoff date.** Without state-entry times, stage sojourn times, time-varying escalation/rework indicators and transition intensities are unidentifiable (§5), calendar/workload effects cannot be modelled, and the independence of administrative censoring cannot be checked. This is the single largest limitation.
2. **Post-transfer outcomes and transfer reasons.** External resolution times, the receiving organisation's SLA, and whether transfer is a customer success would identify T2/T3 instead of leaving them as bounds. Currently the “anywhere” estimand is not identified.
3. **Assignment mechanism and policy rollout records.** Randomisation or a credible quasi-experiment, rollout dates per case, and reasons for the 5 non-compliant B cases are needed before any cohort contrast can be read causally; at present cohort, compliance and era are entangled.
4. **Time-varying context:** queue lengths, staffing and reviewer workload, shift/holiday calendars, case complexity, channel and customer attributes. These are the likely confounders of priority, escalation and dormancy, and are entirely absent.
5. **Operational definitions:** which withdrawals/rejections are legitimate timely dispositions vs SLA failures; whether the 72h clock pauses or is in business hours; how priority is assigned and audited. Priority is currently taken as given, yet it drives escalation deterministically.
6. **A larger, longitudinal extract** with repeated cases per customer and follow-up beyond a single cutoff, so that tail estimates (currently resting on 1 case at 110h) and calibration/fairness checks by priority×cohort have usable precision; interval-censored or multi-state fitting would then become feasible.

Until items 1–3 are supplied, the defensible deliverables are the estimand definitions, the Aalen–Johansen estimates with explicit transfer assumptions and bounds, and the process-state representation — not causal claims about `AutoCheck`, escalation or rework.
