## 1. Interaction-graph description

The relevant structure is a typed, event-mediated interaction graph rather than six isolated Job cases. Nodes include:

- **Jobs:** J1–J6  
- **Components and lots:** C1–C6, L7, L8  
- **Production coordination objects:** B1, B2, S1–S3  
- **Capacity/state objects:** CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1, FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3  
- **Events:** readiness, quarantine/release, acquire/release, cure, process, test, pack, dispatch.

Important edge qualifiers are `requires`, `contains`, `batches`, `consolidates`, `uses`, `acquires`, `releases`, `quarantines`, `releases lot`, and event-to-object `relates_to`.

Compact edge list, where `--` denotes a typed relation rather than causality:

```text
J1 --requires--> C1                 L7 --contains--> C2, C5
J2 --requires--> C2                 L8 --contains--> C1, C3
J3 --requires--> C3
J4 --requires--> C4
J5 --requires--> C5
J6 --requires--> C6

B1 --batches--> J1, J2, J3          B2 --batches--> J4, J5
S1 --consolidates--> J1, J4         S2 --consolidates--> J2, J3
S3 --consolidates--> J5, J6

BatchCure(B1, 12:05)
  --relates_to--> B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1, J2, J3, C1, C2, C3

BatchCure(B2, 14:50)
  --relates_to/uses--> B2, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J4, J5

Acquire/Release(J3)
  --relates_to--> J3, FIX-A

MachineProcess(J4, 11:45)
  --relates_to--> J4, C4, CNC-2, FIX-A

QuarantineLot(10:20), ReleaseLot(13:50)
  --relates_to--> L7, C2, C5, J2, J5   [secondary/integrated source]

FinalTest(J5), FinalTest(J6)
  --relates_to--> TEST-1, CAL-3, respective Job
```

Three important higher-order motifs are:

1. **Batch synchronization hyperedge:**  
   `J1/J2/J3 – B1 – OVEN-1 – RACK-7 – C1/C2/C3`.  
   B1 is not merely a shared machine use. It is an AND-join: the batch cannot start until the required jobs, components, rack, oven, and operating policy are jointly feasible. J1 and J2 can therefore wait for J3 even if their own job-local path looks complete.

2. **Cross-batch tool-state motif:**  
   `J4 – B2 – RACK-7 – OVEN-1` and `J1/J2/J3 – B1 – RACK-7 – OVEN-1`.  
   RACK-7 connects batches that otherwise contain disjoint jobs. B2’s delayed start is consistent with a tool-state constraint even while OVEN-1 is idle.

3. **Fixture-mediated machine idleness motif:**  
   `J3 – FIX-A – J4 – CNC-2 – C4`.  
   J4’s process requires both CNC-2 and FIX-A. The relevant feasible condition is not “CNC-2 idle,” but “CNC-2 idle and FIX-A available and J4 ready.” The absence of an explicit wait event makes this dependency inferred from acquire/release and process relations.

4. **Lot-policy motif:**  
   `J2 – C2 – L7 – C5 – J5`, with quarantine and release events.  
   L7 creates a material-control coupling between J2 and J5. However, J2’s supervisor override means the lot state is not a deterministic physical barrier in the observed data.

5. **Downstream consolidation motif:**  
   `J1 – B1 – S1 – J4 – B2`.  
   J1 and J4 need not compete for the same machine to become coupled: S1 creates a downstream join in which J1’s dispatch depends on J4’s packing completion.

Direct observations include object membership, the B1 cure event’s multi-object relations, J3’s FIX-A hold, J4’s process relation to FIX-A and CNC-2, CAL-3 use, and S1 membership. Inferred edges include “J1 waits for J3,” “J4 waits for FIX-A,” and “RACK-7 caused B2’s delay.” Those inferences are plausible resource-feasibility explanations, not directly recorded causal links.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

The following are conditional decompositions, not causal proof. Several delays overlap and must not be added indiscriminately.

1. **J1’s B1 waiting.**  
   Observed ready-to-batch-start time:

   \[
   12{:}05 - 08{:}10 = 3\text{ h }55\text{ min}=235\text{ min}
   \]

   If the relevant synchronization point was J3 becoming ready at 11:55, then J1’s waiting attributable to waiting for the latest job readiness is:

   \[
   11{:}55 - 08{:}10 = 3\text{ h }45\text{ min}=225\text{ min}
   \]

   The remaining 10 minutes is between J3 readiness and the batch start.  
   **Counterfactual assumption:** J1 could have entered an otherwise equivalent cure process at or near 11:55, rather than being held for B1’s full membership.

2. **J2’s B1 waiting.**

   \[
   12{:}05 - 08{:}25 = 3\text{ h }40\text{ min}=220\text{ min}
   \]

   Relative to J3’s 11:55 readiness:

   \[
   11{:}55 - 08{:}25 = 3\text{ h }30\text{ min}=210\text{ min}
   \]

   Again, 10 minutes remain after the latest stated readiness.  
   **Counterfactual assumption:** J2 was technically eligible to cure once the final batch member, J3, was ready.

3. **J4’s delay plausibly associated with FIX-A.**  
   J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50. CNC-2 is idle from 10:05 to 10:47, while J3 retains FIX-A until 11:40. The overlap during which CNC-2 is idle but FIX-A is unavailable is at least:

   \[
   11{:}40 - 10{:}05 = 95\text{ min}
   \]

   J4 actually starts at 11:45, five minutes after fixture release. Thus, a reasonable tool-associated interval is 95 minutes, with another five minutes plausibly being handoff/setup. J4’s total wait from reaching CNC-2 is:

   \[
   11{:}45 - 09{:}50 = 115\text{ min}
   \]

   **Counterfactual assumption:** J4 required FIX-A before starting, an equivalent fixture could have been supplied, and CNC-2 was usable from 10:05. The 95 minutes are not proof that FIX-A was the sole reason for waiting.

4. **J4’s B2/RACK-7 exposure.**  
   J4 is ready for B2 at 13:10; B2 starts at 14:50:

   \[
   14{:}50 - 13{:}10 = 100\text{ min}
   \]

   J5 becomes ready at 14:40, so J4 waits 90 minutes for J5’s readiness. RACK-7 remains in cleaning/quarantine until 14:35:

   \[
   14{:}35 - 13{:}10 = 85\text{ min}
   \]

   These 85 and 90 minutes overlap substantially; they should not be summed.  
   **Counterfactual assumption:** OVEN-1 was usable at 13:10 and a clean, qualified rack would have allowed a smaller or earlier batch. The data support competing explanations: waiting for J5, waiting for RACK-7, or both.

5. **J1’s shipment-consolidation delay in S1.**  
   J1 is packed at 13:30; S1 dispatches at 19:25:

   \[
   19{:}25 - 13{:}30 = 5\text{ h }55\text{ min}=355\text{ min}
   \]

   J4 is packed at 19:10, 340 minutes after J1. If S1 had dispatched J1 on a benchmark similar to S2’s 25-minute last-pack-to-dispatch delay, J1 might have dispatched at 13:55:

   \[
   19{:}25 - 13{:}55 = 330\text{ min}
   \]

   **Counterfactual assumption:** J1 could have been split from S1 without violating customer, route, cost, or compliance constraints.

6. **J6’s CAL-3-associated delay.**  
   J6 is ready at 16:05 and starts testing at 17:25:

   \[
   17{:}25 - 16{:}05 = 80\text{ min}
   \]

   TEST-1 is idle from 16:35 to 17:25 while CAL-3 is recalibrated from 16:35 to 17:20. The idle-machine interval is:

   \[
   17{:}25 - 16{:}35 = 50\text{ min}
   \]

   **Counterfactual assumption:** After J5 finishes at 16:35, a valid replacement CAL-3 or pre-calibrated tool would let J6 start immediately. Under that assumption, 50 minutes is plausibly associated with calibration/tool state; the earlier 30 minutes reflect J5’s test priority and tool use.

7. **L7 control interval.**

   \[
   13{:}50 - 10{:}20 = 210\text{ min}=3\text{ h }30\text{ min}
   \]

   J5 did not proceed until after release.  
   **Counterfactual assumption:** J5 was otherwise ready during some or all of that interval. Since J5’s exact material-ready time is not supplied, 210 minutes is the quarantine duration, not a measured J5 delay.

## 4. What flattening misses

| Viewpoint | What it makes visible | What it hides or duplicates | Potentially misleading conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job | Standard routing, rework frequency, local elapsed times. | Batch cure may be duplicated across J1/J2/J3; lot events can disappear; no explicit tool or shipment-partner wait. | “Jobs are independent and broadly normal because no duration is a major outlier.” Shared waits can shift all jobs together. |
| Machine | Recorded utilization, setup/process/release sequence, event gaps. | Whether an idle machine had a ready job with all tools, material, and policy approvals. | “CNC-2 or TEST-1 had spare capacity, so capacity cannot matter.” The relevant constraint may be FIX-A or CAL-3. |
| Batch | Batch membership, load/cure/unload, batch-level duration. | Divergent job-ready times and individual pre-batch waits; one batch event may collapse three job histories. | “B1 cured normally, therefore J1/J2 were not delayed.” Their delay occurs before the batch event. |
| Tool | Fixture holds, rack status, calibration cycles. | Whether another job was actually ready, alternative tooling, operator constraints, and inferred rather than logged holds. | “High tool occupancy proves a tool bottleneck.” Occupancy alone does not show that a ready job was blocked. |
| ComponentLot | Quarantine/release, shared material genealogy, potentially J2–J5 coupling. | Inconsistent Job linkage, component-level eligibility, supervisor overrides, and source-system timing differences. | “L7 uniformly blocked J2 and J5.” J2’s override directly contradicts a simple uniform-gate interpretation. |
| Shipment | Shipment creation, load, dispatch, shipment cycle time. | How long each packed job waits for other members; S1’s early-ready J1 is collapsed into one shipment case. | “Shipments dispatch quickly after load.” That says little about J1’s 5 h 55 min pack-to-dispatch wait. |

Low machine utilization can coexist with long job waiting because machine use is only one condition in a feasibility conjunction:

\[
\text{Job ready} \land \text{material released} \land \text{tool available/valid} \land \text{batch/dispatch policy satisfied} \land \text{machine available}.
\]

If FIX-A, CAL-3, RACK-7, L7 policy, or shipment consolidation fails, the machine can be idle while the job waits. A gap between machine events is therefore not a direct waiting-time measurement.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**Narrative A: J1 through B1 into S1.**  
- **Temporal precedence:** J1 is ready at 08:10; B1 starts at 12:05; J1 is packed at 13:30; S1 dispatches at 19:25. C1 belongs to L8, although no L8 timing event is supplied.  
- **Resource feasibility:** B1’s cure relates J1, C1, B1, OVEN-1, and RACK-7.  
- **Policy constraints:** B1 groups J1 with J2/J3; S1 groups J1 with J4.  
- **Causal speculation:** J1 is plausibly delayed first by batch synchronization and later by shipment consolidation. The evidence does not prove that J3 caused the batch start or that S1 could legally have split J1.

**Narrative B: J4’s fixture, batch, and shipment path.**  
- **Temporal precedence:** J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50; J3 releases FIX-A at 11:40; J4 starts machine processing at 11:45; J4 is B2-ready at 13:10; B2 starts at 14:50; J4 is packed at 19:10.  
- **Resource feasibility:** J4’s process requires CNC-2 and FIX-A; B2 uses OVEN-1 and RACK-7.  
- **Policy constraints:** B2 waits for its members and qualified rack state; S1 cannot dispatch until its chosen members are packed.  
- **Causal speculation:** FIX-A plausibly explains part of J4’s early delay, and B2/RACK-7 plausibly explain part of its later delay. The 19:10 packing time cannot be attributed solely to those earlier constraints without missing lifecycle events.

**Narrative C: J5’s lot, batch, calibration, and S3 path.**  
- **Temporal precedence:** L7 is quarantined at 10:20 and released at 13:50; J5 is B2-ready at 14:40; B2 starts at 14:50; J5 is final-test-ready at 16:00; J5 tests 16:10–16:35; S3 eventually dispatches 35 minutes after its last member is packed.  
- **Resource feasibility:** J5 needs C5 from L7, B2’s OVEN-1/RACK-7 combination, then TEST-1 and CAL-3.  
- **Policy constraints:** Lot release governs progression unless overridden; B2 batching and S3 consolidation add joins.  
- **Causal speculation:** L7 may have delayed J5, but the evidence is weaker than for B1 because J5’s earlier readiness is unknown and J2’s override shows release policy is negotiable.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

| Intervention | Target mechanism and expected benefit | Helped / potentially harmed | New cost or risk | Perverse metric | Object-centric evaluation design |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Maximum-hold or micro-batch rule for early-ready jobs | Reduce B1-like synchronization; lower J1/J2 pre-batch waiting. | Helps J1/J2 and future early-ready jobs; may delay J3 or reduce batch efficiency. | Validation burden, smaller loads, energy cost, recipe-risk. | Minimize early-job wait, causing unsafe or uneconomic micro-batches. | Randomize eligible batch windows; compare ready-to-cure time, yield, rework, oven/rack use, and downstream shipment effects for all linked jobs. |
| Add or accelerate turnaround of RACK-7 | Reduce B2-like rack-state blocking. | Helps J4/J5 and future batches; may displace cleaning staff or maintenance work. | Capital, cleaning capacity, qualification risk. | Maximize OVEN-1 utilization, encouraging shortened quarantine. | Record rack acquire/clean/release events; compare batches with spare-rack availability against similar recipes and track quality failures. |
| Add substitute FIX-A or enforce earlier release discipline | Reduce CNC-2 idle-with-ready-job intervals. | Helps J4 and later CNC jobs; may interrupt J3 or create setup churn. | Extra fixture cost, traceability and setup errors. | Maximize CNC busy time, encouraging premature fixture release. | Analyze fixture-to-job allocation episodes, not machine gaps; measure J4-like ready-to-start delay and J3 quality/throughput spillovers. |
| Pre-calibrate spare CAL-3 or move recalibration before demand peak | Reduce J6-like TEST-1 idle time. | Helps J6 and later test-ready jobs; could delay J5 or consume calibration labor. | Calibration inventory, expiration-control risk. | Test throughput, potentially leading to overdue calibration. | Compare final-test-ready jobs exposed to valid-spare availability; include calibration validity, retest rate, and TEST-1 idle intervals. |
| Promise-aware shipment splitting or dispatch cutoff | Reduce S1-style packed-job waiting. | Helps J1 and early-ready members; may harm freight consolidation, J4’s shipment plan, or carbon/cost targets. | More shipments, route complexity, customer coordination. | Minimize pack-to-dispatch time, causing excessive partial shipments. | Assign treatment at shipment-policy level; compare individual pack-to-dispatch, on-time delivery, freight cost, carbon, and partner-job delay. |
| Lot partial-release rules with auditable overrides | Reduce avoidable L7 gating while preserving quality control. | Helps J5-like jobs; may expose customers to quality escapes or disadvantage non-overridden jobs. | Compliance, inspection, and fairness risk. | Minimize quarantine duration, encouraging inappropriate release. | Track lot, component, Job, override approver, and quality outcome together; compare approved partial releases with matched non-releases. |
| Repair event-to-object logging and source reconciliation | Improve observability rather than directly changing operations. | Helps analysis of J2/J5 and future interventions; may burden operators and systems teams. | Data-entry burden; fabricated links if automated matching is poor. | Maximize “Job-linked events,” encouraging indiscriminate relation propagation. | Audit sampled event-object links against source systems; report relation completeness, conflict rate, and change in inferred dependency graph before operational conclusions. |

# Assumes an OCEL object named `ocel` already exists.

    print("\nNetworkX conversion unavailable in this PM4Py version:", exc)

# 6. Waiting calculations must use explicit readiness, start, release, and
# blocking-state events. Do NOT infer waiting just from machine event gaps.

# for explicit "ready", "batch start", "tool release", "pack", and "dispatch" events.
explicit_boundaries = pd.DataFrame(
    [
        ("J1", "ready_for_B1", "08:10", "B1_start", "12:05"),
        ("J2", "ready_for_B1", "08:25", "B1_start", "12:05"),

        ("J1", "packed", "13:30", "S1_dispatch", "19:25"),
        ("J6", "CAL_recalibration_start", "16:35", "J6_test_start", "17:25"),
    ],
    columns=["object_id", "start_label", "start_time", "end_label", "end_time"],
)

base = pd.Timestamp("2025-01-01 ")
explicit_boundaries["start_ts"] = pd.to_datetime(base.strftime("%Y-%m-%d ") + explicit_boundaries["start_time"])
explicit_boundaries["end_ts"] = pd.to_datetime(base.strftime("%Y-%m-%d ") + explicit_boundaries["end_time"])
explicit_boundaries["elapsed_minutes"] = (
    explicit_boundaries["end_ts"] - explicit_boundaries["start_ts"]
).dt.total_seconds() / 60.0

print("\nExplicit-boundary intervals; these are not machine-gap estimates:")
print(explicit_boundaries[["object_id", "start_label", "end_label", "elapsed_minutes"]])

## 8. Falsification and additional data

Observations that would weaken the top-ranked batch-synchronization/RACK-7 explanation include:

1. Corrected readiness records showing J1 or J2 was not truly cure-ready until near 12:05.
2. Evidence that B1 had a mandatory 12:05 recipe, staffing, or customer slot independent of J3’s readiness.
3. Evidence that J1/J2 legally could not be cured separately or in a smaller qualified batch.
4. A rack-status audit showing RACK-7 was actually available and B2 was delayed solely for J5 or another unrecorded prerequisite.
5. Historical data showing early-ready members of similar batches do not systematically wait for late-ready members.
6. Evidence of undocumented OVEN-1 maintenance, qualification, or operator absence from 08:10–12:05 or 13:10–14:50.
7. Evidence that the B1/B2 timestamps are logging times rather than physical readiness/start times.
8. Proof that a different rack or oven could not have been used because of recipe compatibility, making “shared RACK-7” an incomplete explanation.

Additional discriminating fields or event types include:

1. Explicit `Job ready for batch`, `Job blocked`, and blocking-reason events.
2. Planned versus actual batch membership, batch-capacity limits, recipe compatibility, and scheduler decision timestamps.
3. OVEN-1 reservation, maintenance, operator-availability, warm-up, and qualification states.
4. RACK-7 acquire, clean-start, clean-complete, quarantine reason, release, and alternative-rack eligibility events.
5. FIX-A and CAL-3 reservation, acquisition, handoff, validity-expiry, calibration-result, and substitute-tool availability records.
6. Component-lot genealogy, component-level release status, inspection outcomes, override approvals, and approver identity.
7. Consistent source-system identifiers linking L7 events directly to J2, J5, C2, and C5.
8. Shipment assignment time, dispatch cutoff, route/customer constraints, split-shipment eligibility, and freight cost.
9. Job-level Pack, staging, Load, and Dispatch relation events so packed-job wait can be measured.
10. Quality, rework, scrap, and customer-service outcomes to test whether shorter synchronization waits create harmful spillovers.
