## 2. Path scenarios

Dates below are in 2026. These are overlapping event families, not a mutually exclusive partition. For example, a rerun can coincide with Monday microbiology readout, a query, and an investigation. The smooth row is a conservative clean subset: no confirmed excursion, normal tests, and no query or investigation. Its probability is smaller than the probability of eventual straightforward release.

The time columns describe completion after the indicated work, generally Electronic Release. Rejection can terminate an abnormal path earlier once its critical defect has been established. Tail ranges are adverse planning envelopes, not hard maxima.

The investigation probability bands are assumption-dependent, particularly because the excursion uplift is unspecified. To make their scale auditable, one illustrative model uses:

- Excursion probability 0.30.
- Chemistry rerun probabilities 0.10 without an excursion and 0.267 with one: marginal probability approximately 0.15.
- Microbiology indication probabilities 0.05 without an excursion and 0.167 with one: marginal probability approximately 0.085.
- Conditional independence of the two test anomalies **only within each flag state**, as an arithmetic shortcut. Even this shortcut produces positive unconditional dependence; additional dependence within a flag state remains possible.

In that model, the probability of at least one investigation trigger—excursion, rerun, or contamination indication—is approximately

\[
1-0.70(1-0.10)(1-0.05)=0.402.
\]

Using investigation probabilities 0.07 without a trigger and 0.425 with one gives an overall investigation probability near 0.21. A 70% documentation-only split gives about 15% documentation investigations and 6% laboratory investigations. Using a 0.225 query probability gives a clean-path probability near \(0.70\times0.90\times0.95\times0.93\times0.775=0.43\), if queries are independent for this illustration. Neither that independence nor those point probabilities are claimed to be known.

## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

For absolute branch-result times \(T_C\) and \(T_M\), the mandatory join is

\[
T_J=\max(T_C,T_M).
\]

QA starts after that join, its opening calendar, and its queue allow. Rework then follows its own calendar, with possible return to QA. **Chemistry and microbiology are not summed.** A chemistry delay hidden underneath a later microbiology result adds no time at the join; once chemistry becomes later, it takes control of that join.

**Shared chemistry calculation.** The first analyst becomes free Friday 15:54–16:24; the other becomes free 16:42–17:30. Under the chosen priority rule, the first free analyst takes the urgent sample, and B-417 can normally start around 16:42–17:30. Thus it waits approximately 1.2–2.0 calendar hours from now and receives only 0.5–1.3 h of Friday service. Even its 1.5 h minimum service cannot finish by 18:00 under this rule. Unfinished work then experiences the 62 h closure from Friday 18:00 to Monday 08:00.

The triangular chemistry mean is approximately 2.83 h, used here only to construct representative schedules. With midrange current residuals, B-417 starts around Friday 17:06, receives 0.9 h of work, and finishes its remaining 1.93 h around Monday 09:56. A useful priority-path envelope is Monday 08:12–12:00.

Under FIFO, all four waiting samples precede B-417. With representative service times, the first two queued jobs resume Monday and finish around 09:00 and 10:00; the next two finish around 11:50 and 12:50. B-417 then starts around 11:50 and finishes around 14:40. That is approximately 68 h of elapsed waiting from the forecast origin, followed by its own service. Favorable FIFO draws could finish around Monday 10:25; several 4.5 h jobs can leave B-417 unfinished at Monday 18:00 and completing around Tuesday 09:55. These are illustrative bounds under the assumed service range for the jobs ahead and no new preemption.

**Shared microbiology calculation.** At 15:30, 1.5 h of incubation has elapsed, leaving 44.5–52.5 h until technical readiness. This gives Sunday 12:00–20:00, not a Friday or Saturday result. Authorized on-call readout produces a recorded primary result roughly Sunday 13:00–23:00. Without authorization, recording completes Monday 08:30–09:30. QA has no Sunday shift, and chemistry also needs Monday, so on-call authorization alone cannot produce a routine weekend release. It matters much more if confirmation is needed.

**Shared QA queue calculation.** There are 8.5 reviewer-hours of capacity before Monday: 4.5 on Friday and 4 on Saturday. Those same opening hours generate approximately \(0.35\times8.5=3\) new arrivals on average, with substantial uncertainty. Under the illustrative 2.5 h mean service model, the initial five batches represent about 12.5 reviewer-hours and the arrivals another 7.5. Subtracting 8.5 hours of capacity leaves roughly 11–12 reviewer-hours of work at Monday opening, equivalent to around four or five average batches. This is a workload illustration, not a precise queue prediction; current service progress and arrival timing are unknown.

Two Monday reviewers provide roughly 0.8 batches/hour of service capacity against 0.35 arrivals/hour, suggesting that an ordinary backlog can drain. The later a normal chemistry result arrives, the more time QA has had to clear it. Conversely, an early microbiology result does not guarantee immediate QA. I use approximately 1–5 opening hours of ordinary QA waiting, with 6–12+ hours in adverse queue scenarios, then 1.5 h median review or around 3.5 h at its supplied P80. Late Monday work may pause at 20:00 and resume Tuesday 08:00. Arrival overdispersion and uncertain residual review times preclude a reliable queue percentile from the supplied information alone.

**S1: Smooth path.** Chemistry follows the priority/FIFO mixture above: about 1–2 h waiting with partial Friday service under priority, or a wait into Monday under FIFO, then 1.5–4.5 h total service. Microbiology completes its primary incubation and staffed readout without confirmation. The join is typically Monday 09:00–15:30: microbiology dominates a favorable chemistry case; chemistry often dominates FIFO. After ordinary QA waiting and review, add 0.25–0.75 h for Electronic Release. There is no optional rework. An exceptionally favorable Sunday readout, Monday 08:12 chemistry completion, zero QA wait, and 0.5–1.0 h review could release around Monday 09:00–10:00. The early QA service value is an explicitly favorable assumption, since no minimum was supplied.

**S2: Chemistry rerun.** Start with the same chemistry queue and first service, then add a priority rerun of 1–3 laboratory working hours, with mean approximately 1.93 h. Allow a small additional rerun queue delay rather than assuming priority implies instantaneous service. A representative priority schedule moves chemistry from Monday 09:56 to around noon; a representative FIFO schedule moves it from about 14:40 to 16:35 before any extra rerun waiting. Late or long reruns cross Monday 18:00 and resume Tuesday 08:00. Microbiology follows the normal primary schedule, so the join usually becomes the rerun completion. Add the future QA queue, review, and final release/recording. A rerun also raises investigation risk to the supplied 0.30–0.55 abnormal-path band; any such investigation adds S6 or S7, rather than being silently included as a fixed average rerun surcharge.

**S3: Delayed primary microbiology readout.** Chemistry still waits and receives service as in S1. Its favorable priority result can be ready early Monday, but the microbiology branch consists of Sunday technical readiness, about 12–20 h waiting until Monday 08:00, and 0.5–1.5 h readout. The join is \(\max(T_C,\text{Monday 08:30–09:30})\). Under FIFO, chemistry may still be the later branch. Apply the queue present at that particular join, QA review, and the final step. There is no additional rework in this scenario alone. Compared with Sunday on-call readout, the routine staffing delay is substantial at the branch level but often largely hidden by QA closure or chemistry spillover.

**S4: Microbiology confirmation.** Chemistry follows S1 unless the correlated rerun also occurs, in which case use S2. With on-call primary readout Sunday 13:00–23:00, another 20–30 h of incubation becomes ready roughly Monday 09:00–Tuesday 05:00. A result ready after Monday 18:00 waits until Tuesday 08:00 for readout. Without on-call authorization, the Monday 08:30–09:30 primary readout starts confirmation; technical readiness is then Tuesday 04:30–15:30, with recorded confirmation roughly Tuesday 08:30–17:00. The earliest favorable recorded confirmation is about Monday 09:30. The join takes the maximum of confirmed microbiology and completed chemistry/rerun, typically giving control to microbiology. Add QA waiting and review, then release if cleared or rejection recording if a critical defect is confirmed. If an investigation is opened, use its further delay explicitly; indication alone is neither a usable normal result nor proof of a critical defect.

**S5: Documentation Query.** Chemistry waiting/service and microbiology incubation/readout first determine the same join as S1, S2, or S4; there is no need to repeat the tests merely because documentation is missing. The initial QA queue and review occur, then the query adds 4–12 working hours. For example, a query opened Monday 17:00 has only 3 working hours available that evening: a 4 h response completes Tuesday 09:00, whereas a 12 h response completes Tuesday 17:00 under the assumed QA calendar. Add any return queue and approximately 0.5–1.5 h resumed review, then the final recording. This shifts otherwise Monday completions toward Tuesday or Wednesday. A separate investigation can overlap this event family; for central planning I count sequential work when the query must be resolved before QA can proceed.

**S6: Documentation-only investigation.** Establish the join from the actual chemistry and microbiology paths, then incur the queue and initial QA review. The investigation adds 8–20 working hours, followed by resumed review and final release or rejection recording. If opened Monday 17:00, an 8 h investigation finishes Tuesday 13:00; a 20 h investigation finishes Wednesday 13:00. A query can add another 4–12 working hours and additional requeueing. This supports a Tuesday-to-Thursday central range, with Friday or the following Monday possible if work or queues reach Friday closure. Saturday provides only 4 QA working hours, and Sunday provides none. The supplied additions may be partly concurrent in an actual investigation; counting them sequentially is a conservative shortcut when their ordering is unknown.

**S7: Laboratory investigation.** First complete the applicable chemistry queue/service and microbiology incubation/readout/confirmation, take their maximum, and incur initial QA waiting and review. Once the laboratory investigation opens, add its 3–7 calendar days, then respect any staffed result completion and QA reopening. An investigation opened Monday afternoon could finish Thursday afternoon through the following Monday afternoon; one opened Tuesday after confirmation could finish Friday through the following Tuesday. A seven-day duration includes the intervening weekend; it is not seven working days. Additional query work or a late return to QA explains the end-of-September/early-October tail. Add resumed QA and 0.25–0.75 h release, or 0.5–1.0 h rejection recording when a critical defect establishes the terminal decision. A clear critical defect can end the path before all clearance work would have been performed.

## 4. Forecast summary

These are approximate predictive planning bands for the overall mixture, rather than statistically calibrated quantiles. The scenario table describes conditional paths; its central ranges are not themselves unconditional P50 estimates.

| Forecast quantity | Remaining calendar hours from Friday 18 Sep 15:30 | Approximate terminal date/time | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plausible earliest completion | About 65.5–66.5 h | Mon 21 Sep 09:00–10:00 | Favorable priority chemistry, an available microbiology result, zero QA queue, short QA, and no rework. Routine Monday microbiology readout moves the favorable lower edge to roughly 09:15. |
| Central / P50-like range | About 72–90 h | Mon 21 Sep 15:30–Tue 22 Sep 09:30 | Normal branches dominate much of the probability mass, while FIFO chemistry, QA backlog, and closure push some otherwise ordinary cases into Tuesday. |
| P80-like range | About 100–145 h | Tue 22 Sep 19:30–Thu 24 Sep 16:30 | Allows confirmation, documentation queries, and a material proportion of documentation-only investigations. |
| P90-like planning range | About 140–240 h | Thu 24 Sep 11:30–Mon 28 Sep 15:30 | Highly sensitive to how much probability enters laboratory investigations and to overlapping rework. |
| Severe tail, beyond the central P90 interpretation | About 280–336+ h | Wed 30 Sep morning–Fri 2 Oct afternoon or later | Laboratory investigation after a delayed join, additional documents, calendar closures, or unusually long QA queues. This is an adverse scenario envelope, not a claimed P99 or maximum. |

The upper-quantile ranges need particular caution. In the illustrative mixture, laboratory investigations have about 6% probability, so P90 can remain within the documentation/congestion tail even though some batches take a week or more. If the laboratory-investigation share reaches or exceeds roughly 10%, P90 can move directly into that multi-day laboratory path. The unspecified excursion uplift could cause exactly that change. Conversely, low investigation probabilities and a rapidly clearing QA queue place P80 nearer Tuesday and P90 nearer Thursday.

**Electronic Release is the most plausible terminal event.** Batch Rejected is a material alternative following confirmation of a critical defect, but the problem supplies no probability that an indication becomes a critical defect. It would be unjustified to equate contamination probability, rerun probability, or investigation probability with rejection probability. Rejection can shorten the terminal duration relative to a long investigation seeking clearance.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. **Whether QA opens an investigation and whether it needs laboratory work — mainly path uncertainty.** This determines whether the added work is 8–20 working hours or 3–7 calendar days. Queries and repeated QA can compound the delay. It has the largest effect on the upper forecast ranges.
2. **Resolution of the temperature-excursion flag — observation and dependence uncertainty.** A confirmed flag jointly increases rerun, contamination, and investigation risk. Modeling independent branch anomalies with unconditional probabilities misses their clustering and can misstate both clean-path mass and joint rework tails.
3. **Microbiology confirmation and weekend authorization — path and calendar uncertainty.** Normal incubation is technically ready Sunday; staff availability determines when its clock yields a usable result. On-call work has limited smooth-path benefit but can start confirmation substantially earlier.
4. **QA backlog at the actual join — mainly queue uncertainty, amplified by service-time uncertainty.** Five batches now do not imply five ahead on Monday. Overdispersed arrivals, uncertain residual service, and two Monday reviewers determine whether waiting takes hours or crosses another closure.
5. **Chemistry priority implementation and spillover — queue, service-time, and calendar uncertainty.** Priority versus FIFO changes when B-417 starts, while long jobs and a rerun can cross Friday or Monday closure. Chemistry is often hidden behind microbiology in favorable cases, but can become the controlling branch.

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

The largest forecast update would be an **investigation-disposition event stating whether additional laboratory work is required**. It directly selects the longest remaining path. If laboratory work is required, move the forecast toward an additional 3–7 calendar days from investigation opening, followed by staffed completion and resumed QA. If the investigation is documentation-only, retain an additional 8–20 working hours. If no investigation is required, remove that major tail while retaining any query, test confirmation, and queue delay. A finding of a confirmed critical defect instead changes the forecast toward rejection after the necessary decision and recording, rather than completion of all clearance work.

One secondary update trigger is **resolution of the temperature-excursion flag**. Confirmation shifts probability toward correlated test anomalies and investigations, especially the upper ranges; ruling it out reduces that shared risk without eliminating ordinary reruns or contamination. Neither value changes the already-running primary incubation's Sunday readiness or the routine Sunday QA closure.
