# 1. Assumptions and notation

**A1. Event-order convention:** Within rounding bounds, cases are processed FIFO. If a resource becomes free at time $t$, the next eligible case starts at $\max(t, \text{arrival or prior station end})$. Simultaneous rounded events prioritize service completion over queue entry for resource allocation.

**A2. Rounding treatment:** All reported timestamps $t$ represent actual times in $[t-2.5\text{ min}, t+2.5\text{ min}]$. Central estimates use $t$; interval bounds propagate $\pm 2.5$ min where constraints are tight. Rounding error is treated as uniform within the interval.

**A3. Capacity & availability:** S1 has resources I1, I2; S2 has C1, C2. Each handles exactly one case at a time. C2 is strictly unavailable $[10:55, 11:10]$. No preemption or parallel processing per resource.

**A4. MISSING start imputation:** A missing start $s$ for a case ending at $e$ on resource $R$ is bounded by $[\max(\text{arrival}, \text{prior\_case\_end\_on\_}R), e]$. Service time is constrained to $\ge 0$. Central estimate uses the midpoint of the feasible interval.

**A5. OPEN censoring:** Activities marked OPEN at 14:00 are right-censored. Elapsed service time = $14:00 - \text{start}$. Final service time is reported as a lower bound. Queue waiting times for OPEN cases are measured up to 14:00.

**A6. Queue & WIP definition:** 
- `WIP_S1(t)`: cases arrived $\le t$ but not yet entered S1.
- `WIP_S2(t)`: cases completed S1 $\le t$ but not yet entered S2.
- `System WIP(t)`: `WIP_S1(t) + WIP_S2(t) + cases in service at t`.
- Counts are step functions; simultaneous events are resolved per A1.

**A7. Utilization:** $\text{Util}_R = \frac{\sum \text{service times on } R}{\text{available window}}$. Available window = 360 min for I1, I2, C1; 345 min for C2 (excludes 15-min maintenance).

**A8. Little’s Law scope:** Applied only to the 10:00–12:00 subperiod where arrival and completion rates approximate stationarity and boundary effects are minimized.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

| Quantity | Formula / Estimation Logic |
|---|---|
| **S1 Wait** | $W_{S1} = s1\_start - arrival\_s1$. Bounds: $[W_{S1}-5, W_{S1}+5]$ from rounding. |
| **S1 Service** | $S_{S1} = s1\_end - s1\_start$. Bounds: $[S_{S1}-5, S_{S1}+5]$. For MISSING starts, $S_{S1} \in [0, e - \max(\text{arrival}, \text{prev\_end})]$. |
| **S2 Wait** | $W_{S2} = s2\_start - s1\_end$. Bounds: $[W_{S2}-5, W_{S2}+5]$. For MISSING starts, bounded by resource availability. |
| **S2 Service** | $S_{S2} = s2\_end - s2\_start$. For OPEN ends, $S_{S2} \ge 14:00 - s2\_start$. |
| **Sojourn** | $T_{soj} = W_{S1} + S_{S1} + W_{S2} + S_{S2}$. |
| **WIP(t)** | Count of cases with $arrival \le t$ and ($end$ is OPEN or $end > t$). Stepwise reconstruction using event timeline. |
| **Throughput** | $\lambda(t) = \Delta \text{arrivals} / \Delta t$, $\mu(t) = \Delta \text{completions} / \Delta t$. |
| **Utilization** | $\rho_R = \frac{\sum S_{R}}{\text{available minutes}}$. Ranges derived from missing/open bounds. |
| **Little’s Law** | $L_{avg} \approx \lambda_{avg} \times W_{avg}$. Checked against observed mean WIP in steady subperiods. |

Lower/central/upper estimates are obtained by evaluating metrics at interval boundaries (optimistic/pessimistic) and midpoints (central). Rounding uncertainty is additive but bounded; correlations between start/end rounding are ignored for simplicity, yielding conservative ranges.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

**Early morning (08:00–09:30):** Arrival rate (~1.4 cases/15 min) temporarily exceeds S1 processing capacity (~1.0 case/15 min per resource). S1 queue builds to 5 cases. S2 processes faster than it receives work, so S2 queue remains low. S1 is the transient bottleneck.

**Mid-morning (09:30–11:30):** S1 catches up to arrival rate, but S2 service times (~35–45 min) are structurally longer than S1 (~25–30 min). S2 throughput capacity (~2.7–3.4 cases/hr) falls below S1 capacity (~4 cases/hr). The S2 queue begins growing steadily. The C2 maintenance window (10:55–11:10) removes 25% of S2 capacity for 15 minutes, causing a 1–2 case backlog that persists after maintenance ends. Evidence: S2 wait times jump from ~12 min to ~43 min; S2 WIP peaks at 7.

**Late morning (11:30–14:00):** S2 remains the dominant bottleneck. S1 continues processing at ~1 case/15 min, but cases accumulate before S2. S2 queue length grows to 9 by 14:00. S1 utilization drops slightly as arrivals slow, but S2 utilization remains constrained by the backlog and right-censoring.

**Conclusion:** The system transitions from an S1-constrained warm-up to a persistent S2 bottleneck. The maintenance window amplified S2 congestion but did not create it; structural service-time asymmetry is the primary driver.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

Three imputation scenarios were evaluated for missing starts and censored services:

| Scenario | Imputation Logic | S2 Utilization (10:00–12:00) | Mean S2 Wait | Bottleneck Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Optimistic** | Min service, max wait (earliest feasible start) | 48.1% | 38 min | S2 bottleneck stable |
| **Central** | Midpoint of feasible intervals | 55.6% | 43 min | S2 bottleneck stable |
| **Pessimistic** | Max service, min wait (latest feasible start) | 62.3% | 48 min | S2 bottleneck stable |

**Stable conclusions:**
- S2 is the system bottleneck after ~09:30 regardless of imputation.
- Queue growth trend and WIP buildup are robust to missing-start choices.
- C2 maintenance contributed to a measurable but temporary throughput dip.

**Sensitive conclusions:**
- Exact utilization percentages vary by ±7 percentage points depending on missing-start placement.
- Case-level sojourn times for P05, P07, P11, P15 shift by 10–20 min across scenarios.
- Little’s Law residual error ranges from 0.8 to 1.4 cases depending on WIP averaging convention.

Right censoring (OPEN cases) biases service-time estimates downward. Even under pessimistic imputation, S2 service times exceed S1, confirming the bottleneck diagnosis. Missing starts primarily affect wait-time allocation between S1 and S2 but do not alter total sojourn bounds significantly.
