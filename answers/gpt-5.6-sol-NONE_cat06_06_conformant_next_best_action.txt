## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

The direct timing benefit for P-884 is bounded at approximately **€20,000–€35,000**. Missing the deadline or imposing an unjustified hold can add **€15,000–€80,000** of customer harm. Those amounts make speed materially important and favor using an immediately available independent reviewer rather than passively waiting.

They do not, however, dominate unlawful-execution exposure:

- Quantified regulatory/compliance harm is plausibly **€100,000–€500,000**.
- Non-monetary consequences include sanctions-control failure, enforcement scrutiny, audit findings, disciplinary consequences, reputational damage, and loss of confidence in the control framework.
- This exposure is asymmetric: a timely legitimate payment provides tens of thousands of euros of benefit, while a single impermissible payment can create much larger and partly irreversible harm.

Accordingly:

1. **A and B are dominated as recommendations** by any feasible conformant review path. Their speed benefit is smaller than the stated compliance downside, even before assigning value to non-monetary harm.
2. **C versus D is a genuine operational tradeoff.** C protects P-884’s remaining time but transfers some deadline risk to P-901. D can preserve P-901 and add information, but increases P-884’s chance of missing its deadline.
3. **F controls false-clear risk but may overreact.** Because ultimate approval is estimated at 0.55–0.75 and the rejection threshold is not established, unconditional rejection carries significant false-rejection risk.
4. **E is safe but costly in time.** It is preferable only if present evidence is too weak for a responsible second-review decision.
5. **G is not a normal optimization option.** It is valid only after its legal/process predicates exist.

The predictive ranges should not be treated as causal guarantees. Expedited review, re-screening, interruption, and exception escalation change the workflow and possibly the case mix; the observational SLA and approval estimates may therefore fail to transport under intervention.

## 4. Conformance constraints

- **Action A:** Rules 6 and 9 require a Second Officer Review because risk is 72 and the fuzzy match remains unresolved. Immediate execution has no valid approval path. Rule 10 cannot help because neither director authorization nor an incident identifier exists.
- **Action B:** Rules 6, 7, and 9 apply. R17 conducted the first review and cannot also serve as second officer. Self-certification is not an independent review.
- **Action C:** Conforms with Rules 6 and 7 if S2 independently evaluates the evidence and is distinct from R17. Execution is permitted only after the appropriate recorded approval under Rule 9.
- **Action D:** Alternate screening is supplemental information. It does not itself satisfy Rules 6, 7, or 9. A distinct second officer remains required while the risk is at least 70 or the fuzzy match is unresolved.
- **Action E:** Follows the evidence cycle in Rule 5. After validation, the case returns to Analyst Review, and Rule 6 must still be applied to the resulting risk/match state.
- **Action F:** Rule 8 permits rejection after required review(s), but the prompt does not establish that the institution’s rejection threshold has been met at the present decision point. Valid rejection requires documented policy criteria, sufficient evidence tying those criteria to this case, an authorized decision-maker, and a recorded rationale. If the policy requires second-officer participation before rejection in this risk state, that review must occur first.
- **Action G:** Rule 10 requires both actual Compliance Director authorization and a valid, genuine, justified incident identifier. The authorization must identify its scope and be recorded before reliance on the override. An ordinary deadline or customer loss is not, by itself, evidence of a qualifying incident.
- **SAR handling:** Under Rules 8 and 11, filing or considering a SAR neither authorizes payment nor permits disclosure to the customer.

No favorable forecast can make **A or B** acceptable. Prediction estimates expected outcomes; they do not waive a mandatory control or create a valid process trace. Even a 99% predicted approval probability would not substitute for independent review.

## 5. Resource spillovers

### S2 versus S4

**Using S2 now** offers P-884 the best plausible conformant chance of completing within 90 minutes. The cost is a **0.12–0.28 increase in P-901’s breach probability** from approximately 45 minutes of interruption. That spillover is material and should be recorded as an explicit operational decision rather than ignored.

Mitigations include:

- Give S2 a complete, indexed evidence package before interruption.
- Preserve P-901’s state and assign any work not requiring S2 to another resource.
- Use a strict review handoff and notify P-901’s owner immediately.
- Avoid adding alternate screening serially before S2 unless S2 specifically needs it.

**Waiting for S4** avoids that immediate spillover but is highly sensitive to availability:

- **If S4 is confirmed available in 35 minutes:** Rank D’s S4 plan becomes approximately tied with, and may move above, C. Alternate screening can run concurrently, finish before or near S4’s arrival, and leave a plausible execution window while protecting P-901.
- **If S4 availability moves to 65 minutes:** C remains clearly preferred. Starting review at 65 minutes leaves too little margin for review, recording, and execution; the likely continuation extends beyond P-884’s deadline.

A practical cutoff should be set from the latest safe start time. Because review plus recording/execution may require 40–90 minutes, the conservative cutoff is immediate; an aggressive cutoff is around 30–35 minutes, accepting substantial residual breach risk.

### Alternate-screening quota

There are only three expedited calls left. Using one here is defensible because the case is high value, high risk, and has an unresolved sanctions signal. Its value is greatest when:

- it runs in parallel rather than serially;
- its output can reach the second officer before the decision; and
- the expected information gain exceeds its opportunity cost for other cases.

It should not be used merely to create an appearance of additional control, nor should it delay S2’s independent review.

### Exception-waiting risk

Seeking G serially could consume **20–60 minutes or longer** and still yield no valid override. This creates option-value loss: after an unsuccessful wait, both S2 and S4 routes may be too late. If G is pursued at all, it should be parallel, time-boxed, and should not pause the conformant second-review route. No one may act as if approval is probable or implied while it is pending.

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence

**Primary recommendation:** Execute **Action C now**: interrupt P-901, provide S2 with the validated evidence, current risk/match details, and stale-score warning, and obtain an independent second review. If S2 approves, record approval and execute promptly; if S2 rejects or directs a hold/SAR, follow that outcome without disclosing any SAR.

In parallel, refresh the risk score if that can be done without delaying S2. Use alternate screening only if it can complete concurrently and inform the review rather than postpone it.

**Fallback:** If S4 becomes firmly available at approximately 35 minutes and S2 has not materially begun, switch to **Action D’s parallel S4 plan**, with alternate screening already underway if its information value is judged worth one quota unit. If no reviewer can reach a responsible decision on current evidence, use **E**, not A or B. Use F only after confirming the rejection threshold; use G only after both exception prerequisites are actually satisfied.

**Confidence:** **Medium.** Confidence is high on the mandatory controls and the inadmissibility of A/B, but only medium on the optimal S2/S4 allocation because review duration, S4 arrival, transportability of predictions, and P-901’s underlying value/risk are uncertain.

**Immediate reranking trigger:** A confirmed S4 availability time—especially **35 minutes versus 65 minutes**—or a new screening/risk result that resolves or strengthens the fuzzy match. A material change in S2’s estimated completion time or P-901’s breach severity should also trigger immediate reranking.
