## 1. Decision rule

To ensure decision-theoretic coherence under uncertainty, the intervention policy is governed by a **Net Expected Value (NEV) Rule** adjusted for calibration and operational constraints. A case is eligible for intervention only if it satisfies the following hierarchy:

1.  **Stage Eligibility:** The case must be in `Triage` or `Diagnosis Started`. Cases at `Diagnosis Complete` or later are ineligible (intervention effect  0).
2.  **Alert Persistence Filter:**
    *   **Suppress:** Repeated alerts without material evidence change (e.g., no new logs, status change, or parameter shift) for >2 hours.
    *   **Allow:** Repeated alerts with material evidence change (e.g., new diagnostic log, customer escalation).
3.  **Calibration Adjustment:** Before scoring, adjust `p_now` based on segment reliability:
    *   **SME:** Add +0.065 (underprediction correction).
    *   **Strategic Enterprise:** Subtract -0.085 (overprediction correction).
    *   **Public Sector:** No adjustment.
    *   **Non-Native-Language:** Flag as "High Uncertainty"; require senior approval or higher NEV threshold.
4.  **Prioritization Score:** Calculate NEV for eligible cases:
    $$ \text{NEV} = (\text{Adj\_Risk\_Reduction} \times \text{Value}) - \text{Cost}_{\text{swarm}} - \text{Burden}_{\text{false\_alarm}} - \text{Spillover\_Cost}_{\text{slot}} $$
    *   `Adj_Risk_Reduction`: Midpoint of the provided effect range.
    *   `Spillover_Cost_slot`: Estimated as $0.02 \times 6 \times \text{Avg\_Queue\_Value}$ (approx. €2,400 per slot based on €20k avg case value).
5.  **Capacity Constraint:** Select top $N$ cases where $N \le (\text{Total Slots} - \text{Reserve})$. Reserve 1 slot for regulatory incidents.

The policy distinguishes between "noise" and "signal" in repeated alerts to prevent capacity exhaustion on stale cases.

*   **Cool-down:** A case is ineligible for a second swarm alert within 120 minutes unless a material event occurs.
*   **Suppression Logic (I-05):** I-05 has triggered a third alert with no new customer logs or status changes since the first. The model refresh is likely reacting to time-decay rather than new risk. **Action:** Suppress intervention. Re-evaluate in 2 hours.
*   **Escalation/Re-entry Logic (I-07):** I-07 has triggered a third alert, but a **new diagnostic log** was uploaded. This constitutes a material event, resetting the evidence baseline. Despite the slightly lower `p_now`, the new data suggests the risk model is re-evaluating a changed state. **Action:** Allow intervention due to urgency (1.5h SLA) and evidence change.
*   **Material Event Definition:** New attachment, customer status update (e.g., "escalated"), or stage transition.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

*   **Value-Based Prioritization (Starvation Risk):** Selecting I-08 (€100k) over I-05 (€7k) maximizes economic value but may systematically deprioritize SME cases. If SME breaches are less costly but more frequent, they may accumulate unresolved technical debt.
    *   *Safeguard:* Implement a "Fairness Floor." Ensure at least 20% of slots per week go to non-Strategic segments, even if NEV is lower, to maintain service level equity.
*   **Non-Native-Language Uncertainty (I-09):** The model is uncalibrated for this segment. Intervening on I-09 could be a false positive (wasting capacity) or a false negative (missing a breach).
    *   *Safeguard:* Exclude Non-Native cases from automated slot allocation. Require manual review by a specialist familiar with the language context before intervention.
*   **Differential Calibration:** Strategic cases are overpredicted (risk appears higher than reality), while SME cases are underpredicted. Our calibration adjustments attempt to correct this, but residual bias remains.
    *   *Safeguard:* Segment-specific thresholds. Use a higher `p` threshold for Strategic cases (to account for overprediction) and lower `p` threshold for SME cases.
*   **Feedback Loops:** If we intervene on high-risk cases, they resolve without breach. Future training data will show "High Risk + Intervention = No Breach," potentially teaching the model that these cases are "safe" and reducing future alert scores (label leakage).
    *   *Safeguard:* Maintain a "Shadow Intervention" log for untreated cases to estimate counterfactual breach rates. Retrain models using causal inference techniques (e.g., uplift modeling) rather than simple outcome prediction.
*   **Spillover Harm:** The spillover risk (delay) falls on *untreated* diagnosis-stage cases. If untreated cases are disproportionately from a specific segment (e.g., SME), that segment bears the external cost of protecting Strategic cases.
    *   *Safeguard:* Monitor breach rates of the *untreated* pool by segment. If untreated SME breach rates rise significantly, reduce Strategic intervention frequency.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

*   **Operational Monitoring:** Track slot utilization vs. breach rate daily. Alert if slots used < 70% of capacity (underutilization) or if breach rate > 15% of portfolio.
*   **Predictive Monitoring:** Weekly calibration check. Compare `p_now` vs. actual breach rate by segment. If Strategic breach rate < 5% (given p~0.4), increase overprediction correction.
*   **Causal-Effect Monitoring:** Run a "Holdout" test monthly. Randomly withhold intervention on 10% of eligible cases (where safe) to measure true intervention lift vs. observational prediction.
*   **Capacity Monitoring:** Track queue delay times for non-intervened cases. If average diagnosis time for untreated cases increases >10%, reduce spillover estimate in the NEV rule.
*   **Fairness Monitoring:** Quarterly audit of breach rates by segment. If SME breach rate exceeds Strategic breach rate by >2x, trigger a policy review to adjust the "Fairness Floor."
*   **Recalibration Trigger:** If the Net Impact (Section 3) turns negative for two consecutive weeks, revert to a "Conservative" policy: Reduce active slots to 2, increase reserve to 2, and raise the NEV threshold by 20%.
