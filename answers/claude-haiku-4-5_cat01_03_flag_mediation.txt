# Process-Mining Analysis: Flag-Induced Delays and Mediation Pathways

## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1_total_policy | P0 vs. P1 (suppress flag display) | All cases arriving under current flag regime | Total cycle time from arrival to close | Total policy effect (P0 outcome minus P1 outcome) | Partially | Requires counterfactual removal of information; reviewer behavior change is uncertain. |

| E7_outcome_quality | P0 vs. P1 | All cases, stratified by true underlying_risk | decision_outcome  {Approve, Reject} vs. true approval-rate by risk quintile | Controlled direct effect of flag on decision quality, holding risk_score and complexity constant | No | True underlying_risk is unobserved; risk_score is noisy; no ground truth for correctness of decisions provided. |

**Framework**: I use a mix of controlled direct effects (CDE), natural direct/indirect effects (NDE/NIE), and interventional indirect effects (via policy manipulation rather than isolated mediator intervention) because the flag is embedded in a decision rule and should not be manipulated in isolation. The threshold between "direct" (via priority triage routing) and "indirect" (via downstream queue congestion and escalation) is procedurally defined but causally ambiguous given unobserved case-level risk.

### Threats to identification (180 words):

**Interference (spillovers)**: The shared manual-review and escalation queues mean that assigning a case to priority triage or mandatory review directly affects other cases' waiting times. A case with a high flag may displace unflagged cases in queue, artificially inflating unflagged cases' cycle times. This violates SUTVA (stable unit treatment value assumption). Comparing flagged vs. unflagged cases overstates the causal effect of the flag because we cannot separate the direct effect of flag-driven routing from cross-case queue congestion.

**Exposure-induced confounding**: If the flag triggers mandatory manual review, and review reveals case-specific risk not captured by `risk_score`, then the decision to escalate is confounded by review findings. The pathway `flag  manual_review  escalation  cycle_time` is confounded by `U_case_difficulty`, which becomes visible only during review.

**Treatment-confounder feedback**: A reviewer who sees a flagged case may behave differently (slower, more careful), inflating manual service time. This behavior change is collateral to the flag-as-information and cannot be separated without knowing reviewer decision rules.

### Narrative explanation (100–180 words):

**Observed contradictions supporting flag-delay paradox**:

Flagged cases achieve triage acceleration (0.42 vs. 2.86 hours to triage start) yet suffer 3 hours longer total cycle time (19.6 vs. 16.6 hours). This is **consistent with downstream delay mechanisms**:

1. **Mandatory manual review queue**: Flagged cases enter the review queue almost universally (9/9 flagged cases vs. 5/11 unflagged), creating a policy-induced queue burden. Review waiting time is 5.92 hrs for flagged vs. 1.0 hr for unflagged (median).

2. **Escalation propensity**: Flagged cases are escalated more frequently (5/9 vs. 2/11), and escalation queues are severely congested (median 5.4 hrs waiting). This is not automatic; it results from reviewer judgment after manual review, so escalation is itself a downstream consequence of the flag.

3. **Lack of queue capacity adjustment**: There is no evidence that manual-review or escalation capacity was expanded when the flag policy was introduced. Shared queues mean flagged cases directly delay unflagged cases, yet the benefit of faster triage is internal to the flagged cohort.

**Why this is not automatically causal**: Observed timing differences are consistent with mediation, but confounding by unmeasured case difficulty cannot be ruled out. Flagged cases may have genuinely higher underlying risk (not fully captured by `risk_score`), making them legitimately harder to review (longer manual service) and more likely to warrant escalation. The delay could reflect appropriate scrutiny rather than queue congestion.

### Recommended Limited Pilot: Policy P2 + P3 (250 words)

**Policy**: Retain priority triage for flagged cases, drop mandatory manual review (P2), and add one dedicated senior reviewer for flagged cases (P3).

**Eligibility**: All cases with risk_score  0.70 (flag=1) arriving during business hours (08:00–17:00 M–F) for 4 weeks. Unflagged cases (flag=0) proceed under current routing.

**Assignment**: Randomize flagged cases 1:1 to (1) current mandatory review pathway or (2) discretionary review pathway (reviewers may review at discretion). Flagged cases assigned to (2) may also be manually referred by triage staff if complexity or arrival-load signals warrant it.

**Queue-capacity guardrail**: Designate one senior reviewer (M1 or M2) to the dedicated flagged track. If dedicated reviewer queue exceeds 3 cases (>6 hrs of estimated work), escalate next arrivals to main discretionary-review queue or approve-without-review pathway.

**Primary outcomes**:
- Total cycle time (close  arrival) for flagged cases under each pathway.
- Manual-review rate (proportion undergoing review) by treatment arm.
- Escalation rate and escalation-queue wait.

**Secondary outcomes**:
- Approval rate by treatment arm.
- Triage wait and manual-queue wait by treatment arm.
- Unflagged-case cycle time (spillover indicator).
- Staff satisfaction and workload (survey).

**Minimum process data**: Case timestamps (current + manual review assignment decision time), reviewer identity, queue length snapshots, escalation reasons (text coded into decision-quality categories).

**Stopping conditions**:
- If treatment arm (2) approval rate exceeds control arm (1) by >8 percentage points (possible false-approval signal), halt treatment arm and audit 10 recent cases.
- If unflagged-case median cycle time increases >2 hrs vs. pre-pilot baseline (spillover deterioration), halt and review queue rebalancing.
- If cycle time reduction in treatment arm is <1.5 hrs (policy ineffective), end early and pivot to P3 (capacity) alone.

**Analysis plan**: Intent-to-treat comparison of median cycle times, stratified by complexity. Report with 95% confidence intervals (exact binomial for rates, quantile regression for times).

## 8. Reversal conditions

The following six conditions would reverse my preferred mechanism interpretation or policy recommendation:

1. **Hidden risk unmeasured**: If true underlying risk driving escalation is 3–5 times stronger than observed risk_score predicts, then escalation delays are justified by genuine case difficulty, not flag-bias-induced over-escalation. Implication: **P4 (change escalation criteria) is ineffective**, and reducing escalation without addressing hidden risk increases false-approval rates. Recommendation shifts to P3 (add capacity) as the only harm-reducing intervention.

2. **Queue interference dominates direct effects**: If peer-case flag status predicts >50% of focal-case cycle-time variance (after conditioning on focal-case characteristics), then spillover effects overwhelm direct effects of flag routing or review policy. Implication: **Targeted policies (P2, P3, P4) produce zero net benefit** because capacity reallocated to flagged cases is exactly offset by increased congestion for unflagged cases. Only system-wide capacity expansion (hypothetical P5 with truly new FTE) helps. Recommendation shifts to "do not fragment queues; expand total capacity first."

3. **Reviewer decision quality degrades under deprioritization**: If decision-audit data show that unflagged complex cases (deprioritized after flag policy) have higher false-approval rates than flagged cases, then the cycle-time reduction from deprioritization (P0 vs. baseline) comes at unacceptable quality cost. Implication: **P0 is welfare-reducing despite shorter cycle times**. Recommendation reverses to "prior to flag introduction may have been preferable" or "accept current system and add unconditional capacity expansion."

4. **Flag display is causally inert; behavior is driven by risk_score directly**: If removing flag display (P1) produces no behavioral change in reviewer decisions or escalation rates (i.e., reviewers decide based on risk_score alone), then the problem is not information but capacity. Implication: **P1 has zero effect and should not be piloted**. Recommendation shifts to "flag is a symptom, not a cause; focus on P3/P4 (capacity/criteria redesign) instead."

5. **Positivity/overlap violation in risk_score**: If risk_score distributions for flagged and unflagged cases do not overlap substantially (e.g., no unflagged cases with risk_score >0.70), then local-threshold RDD design (Section 5) is invalid and flag effect is not identifiable from observational data. Implication: **All causal claims about flag's direct effect on triage are unfounded**. Recommendation shifts to "require randomized flag assignment in flagged high-risk region to identify causal effect; observational analysis cannot inform policy."

6. **Escalation criteria are objective and driven by decision_outcome alone**: If escalation decisions are deterministic (e.g., "always escalate if rejected, never if approved") and do not respond to flag status or reviewer discretion, then the pathway flag  escalation is entirely mechanically determined by the underlying decision quality, not by behavior change. Implication: **P4 (change escalation criteria) is misdirected** because escalation criteria are not actually changeable without redefining approval standards. Recommendation shifts to "focus on improving approval decision accuracy (reduce false rejections and approvals); escalation will naturally decrease if approval quality improves."

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed**: Flagged cases achieve a 2.4-hour median triage-wait reduction (0.42 vs. 2.86 hours) via priority-lane routing, confirming that the priority triage mechanism works mechanically. However, flagged cases experience a 3-hour longer total cycle time (19.6 vs. 16.6 hours), driven by downstream delays: a 5.9-hour median queue wait before manual review (vs. 1.0 hour for unflagged cases entering review discretionally) and a 5.4-hour escalation-queue wait for cases that escalate (5/9 flagged vs. 2/11 unflagged). The net effect is a "triage speedup, post-triage slowdown" pattern.

**Plausible mechanism**: The flag triggers mandatory manual review (policy), which floods the shared review queue. Manual service times are similar for flagged and unflagged cases (~2.8 hours), suggesting that review difficulty or reviewer effort is case-driven rather than flag-driven. Escalation occurs more frequently among reviewed flagged cases (55% of reviewed flagged vs. 18% of reviewed unflagged), and escalation queues are severely congested. The observed delays appear consistent with congestion, not reviewer bias or frivolous escalation—but unmeasured case difficulty is a competing explanation (genuinely harder cases may warrant longer review and more-frequent escalation, coincidentally correlated with flag status).

**Unidentified**: (1) The true causal effect of the flag on cycle time, disentangled from confounding by unmeasured case risk. (2) Whether escalation delays are justified (cases legitimately hard) or wasteful (over-escalation by reviewers). (3) The queue-discipline mechanism: FIFO vs. priority within queues, batch effects, and concurrent processing are unobserved. (4) Reviewer behavior change in response to flag visibility; P1 (suppress flag) cannot be evaluated without a behavioral pilot. (5) The spillover effects on unflagged cases, which SUTVA violation may render aggregate comparisons invalid.

**Most useful next evidence**: Implement the **limited pilot of P2 + P3 (discretionary review + dedicated flagged-case capacity)** for 4 weeks. This design permits causal identification of the review-occurrence mediation (via randomization to mandatory vs. discretionary) while controlling for escalation bottleneck (via capacity protection). If cycle time reduction is 4 hours without approval-rate degradation, then mandatory review is the primary causal bottleneck and P2 is justified. If reduction is <1.5 hours despite capacity protection, then escalation is the bottleneck and P4 (escalation-criteria redesign or senior-staff expansion) is the next lever. If unflagged-case cycle times worsen (spillover), then queue rebalancing or system-wide capacity expansion is required.
