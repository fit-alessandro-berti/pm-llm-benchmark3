# 1. Risk indicators

Overall, the evidence is most consistent with a mixture of emergency handling, informal specialization, and data-quality/control-design problems. Collusion is a hypothesis for targeted testing, not an established conclusion. High co-working frequency alone is not probative: the Aline/Elena and Boris/Felix pairings are large, routine, low-value, low-emergency populations with ordinary approval times.

# 2. Benign alternative explanations

The alternatives below should be tested against the underlying records rather than accepted or rejected from the event-log counts alone.

**Carla → Gita: fast approvals and PO four-eyes exceptions.**

- Specialized emergency-medical-supplies routing may intentionally send urgent requests to Carla and delegated urgent approver Gita. Support would include emergency requisitions, medical-priority codes, delivery deadlines, a valid delegation, and approvals occurring during documented on-call coverage. Refutation would be ordinary urgency descriptions, repeated non-emergency use, or approvals outside the delegate’s authority.
- The shared `Approver_Gita` account and legacy mapping may make a bot, assistant, or delegated approver look like the same human. Support would be a consistent match to `entered_by`, immutable user IDs, session logs, and Bot_PO execution records. Refutation would be a single human login making both decisions without an approved exception.

**Dan → Hugo: weekend speed, loops, and exceptions.**

- Facilities failures can require weekend repair escalation, with Hugo serving as the authorized manager and Dan as the specialist buyer. Support would be incident/work-order tickets, outage or safety impact, weekend schedules, contractor call-outs, and retrospective approval within three working days. Refutation would be weekday routine purchases or repeated approvals without service evidence.
- The loop may be a normal clarification cycle over scope, price, or repair completion. Support would be distinct comments, revised quotes, and changed PO fields between handoffs. Refutation would be identical records, no intervening work, or approval immediately after an unreviewed resubmission.

**Lena → Kim: bank changes, fast payment approvals, V-77x concentration, and loops.**

- The V-77x banking-platform migration may have required legitimate bulk maintenance, with Kim handling exceptions after a machine or manual match. Support would be a migration project ID, vendor notices, independently confirmed bank letters, approved maintenance tickets, old/new account reconciliation, and a complete payment trail. Refutation would be changes lacking a ticket or independent callback, bank details that do not match vendor master documentation, or payments released before verification.
- Manual-upload and correction loops may reflect a temporary operational workaround or source-system linkage failure. Support would be controlled upload manifests, batch IDs, maker/checker metadata, import logs, and later reconciliation to the procurement case. Refutation would be unexplained uploads, shared credentials, edits after payment release, or the same person controlling both change and payment without documented authorization.

**Same-person retrospective justifications.**

- The 12 identical-minute events may be a quarter-close spreadsheet migration that preserved load time rather than the time of the business action. Support would be the original spreadsheet, file metadata, migration logs, row-level source timestamps, and a different author in the original records. Refutation would be contemporaneous system audit entries showing the same person created and justified the override.
- A legitimate emergency may have been documented in a batch after the fact, with an approved temporary procedure. Support would be policy evidence allowing batch entry and original emergency tickets. Refutation would be late or absent justification, no emergency trigger, or the same actor selecting, approving, and documenting the exception.

**VendorMaster role overlap and ordinary pair frequency.**

- Lena/Marc performing both vendor checks and bank maintenance may be the designed master-data role, and Marc/Iris may be ordinary onboarding/payment work. Support would be role descriptions, access-control matrices, supervisory review, and normal vendor onboarding artifacts. Refutation would be access exceeding policy or concentration in unusual vendors and high-risk payments.
- Aline/Elena and Boris/Felix are likely team allocation and routine correction patterns. Support would be stable team assignment, low/medium amounts, low emergency rates, ordinary cycle times, and independent approval comments. There is no basis to infer collusion from their co-case counts alone.

# 4. Network and conformance analyses

First normalize timestamps to one timezone, booleans such as `emergency_flag`, actor identifiers, and case-level attributes. Retain both `org:resource` and the more authoritative `entered_by`/`approved_by`; do not overwrite the former because the mismatch itself is evidence of a mapping problem.

Illustrative PM4Py setup and four-eyes filtering:

    raw_df.copy(),
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",

log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)

# PM4Py releases differ slightly in keyword names; verify with help().

    log, "Create Purchase Order", "Approve Purchase Order",

    log, "Change Vendor Bank Details", "Payment Approval",
    case_id_key="case:concept:name", resource_key="org:resource",

For the known identity defects, repeat the calculation with an actor field derived from immutable audit-user IDs, and report the difference from `org:resource`. Also test the normative constraints directly per trace so that activity order and the emergency exception are explicit:

```python
def actor(e):
    return e.get("audit_user_id") or e.get("entered_by") or e.get("org:resource")

def four_eyes(trace, first, second):
    a = {actor(e) for e in trace if e["concept:name"] == first}
    b = {actor(e) for e in trace if e["concept:name"] == second}
    return bool(a & b)

po_cases = [t for t in log if four_eyes(t, "Create Purchase Order", "Approve Purchase Order")]
bank_cases = [t for t in log if four_eyes(t, "Change Vendor Bank Details", "Payment Approval")]
```

For the emergency rule, use a Declare-style response constraint plus a resource-aware validator. Declare can test that every `Emergency Override` is followed by a `Retrospective Justification`; the three-working-day and different-human conditions should be checked explicitly because resource inequality and working-calendar semantics may not be represented by a default template:

```python
declare_model = pm4py.discover_declare(log)  # or construct a Declare model
declare_model.add_response(
    "Emergency Override", "Retrospective Justification",
    max_working_days=3,
)
declare_result = pm4py.conformance_declare(log, declare_model)

def retrospective_ok(trace):
    overrides = [e for e in trace if e["concept:name"] == "Emergency Override"]
    justifications = [e for e in trace if e["concept:name"] == "Retrospective Justification"]
    return all(
        any(
            0 <= working_days(o["time:timestamp"], j["time:timestamp"]) <= 3
            and actor(o) != actor(j)
            for j in justifications
        ) for o in overrides
    )
```

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

Evidence that would increase concern:

- Immutable login, session, badge, or API records showing one human actually performed both sides of a prohibited pair, including after correcting shared-account and legacy-ERP mappings.
- Bank-change audit history showing edits without a maintenance ticket, independent callback, vendor-authenticated request, or second-person approval; bank details that lead to an account unrelated to the vendor’s verified records would be especially significant.
- Payment approval or release occurring before bank verification, invoice matching, or required retrospective justification, particularly for high-amount V-77x cases.
- PO version history showing no substantive review, identical comments, repeated same-minute approvals, backdated justifications, or approvals outside delegated authority and on-call coverage.
- Vendor, employee, or access-log relationships linking the actors to each other or to the beneficiary, or repeated routing around available independent approvers.

Evidence that would reduce concern:

- Valid emergency tickets, business-impact evidence, documented delegation, on-call rosters, and independent retrospective approval within three working days.
- Original quarter-close files and migration logs proving that the 12 identical-minute justifications are load timestamps, with different authors and timely source timestamps.
- Vendor-platform migration documentation, approved bulk-maintenance manifests, independent confirmation of every V-77x bank change, and complete reconciliation to payment records.
- Source-system mapping tests showing that apparent four-eyes violations disappear when immutable `entered_by`, `approved_by`, user IDs, bot run IDs, and event IDs are used.
- PO comments, revised quotes, changed fields, and work-order records demonstrating substantive clarification loops and genuine emergency specialization.
- Access-control evidence that role overlap is policy-approved and that an independent person still reviewed or released the payment.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Review of the procurement-to-payment event log indicates a mixed control-risk picture. The Carla–Gita and Dan–Hugo patterns show rapid approval and some apparent purchase-order four-eyes exceptions, but their high emergency rates, medical-supply and weekend-repair specialization, and delegated escalation roles provide plausible operational explanations. The most concerning subgroup is VendorMaster_Lena–AP_Kim: bank-detail changes and payment approvals are tightly timed, concentrated in vendor family V-77x, associated with high-amount cases and manual uploads, and include apparent four-eyes violations and feedback loops. This warrants targeted corroboration; it does not establish collusion or fraud.

The 17 same-person retrospective justifications and 23 shared-account emergency approvals also represent control and evidence-quality exceptions. Twelve justifications were batch-entered after quarter close, and legacy-ERP mappings sometimes combine bot and human activity, so the event log cannot reliably establish individual responsibility in every case. Audit should first obtain immutable user and session logs, source-system audit histories, upload manifests, emergency tickets, delegation records, vendor bank-change documentation, independent confirmation, and payment-release evidence. It should then recalculate the exceptions using corrected actor mappings and working-time intervals. Ordinary Aline–Elena and Boris–Felix co-working patterns appear consistent with routine team specialization and should remain monitoring populations unless corroborating evidence emerges. Findings should distinguish verified control failures, data-quality defects, and unresolved indicators requiring follow-up.
