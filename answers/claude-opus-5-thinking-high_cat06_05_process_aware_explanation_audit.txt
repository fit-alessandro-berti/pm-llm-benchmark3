## 1. Leakage and temporal-consistency findings

Finding-type vocabulary used below:

- **Target/outcome leakage** — the feature is a function of the label or of the outcome that defines it.
- **Post-t0 (future) information** — computed from events that occur after `t0`; not repairable by better joins, only by redefinition.
- **Mutable-feature / as-of-join defect** — the underlying fact exists before `t0`, but the stored value is the current or closure-time value with no `valid_from` timestamp (a lineage defect, therefore repairable in principle).
- **Process impossibility / ordering artifact** — the pattern contradicts the normative control flow, or depends on an ordering the source system cannot justify.
- **Correlated proxy** — admissible at `t0` and predictive, but carrying non-operational content (region, channel, socio-demographics) that requires fairness and robustness review rather than deletion.
- **Drift/calendar proxy** — admissible at `t0` but confounded with regime changes (policy, holiday capacity).
- **Legitimate prefix signal** — derivable from the realized prefix or from contemporaneous system state at `t0`.

## 2. Process-model consistency checks

The suite below is organized as executable gates in the feature/explanation pipeline. Each gate fails closed: a feature or an explanation that cannot be certified is quarantined rather than shipped with a caveat.

**T1 — Prefix-time availability (point-in-time correctness).**
Every feature row must carry `(value, valid_from, source_event_id)`. The gate asserts `valid_from ≤ t0` for every feature used in scoring and in the explanation. Two complementary implementations:
- *Static*: parse the feature SQL/DAG and reject any aggregation whose time predicate does not reference `t0` (explicitly banning `extraction_date`, `current_date`, `max(event_time)`, closure-time warehouse columns, and nightly-batch snapshot tables for intra-day prediction points). This gate alone catches `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `employee_experience`, and `final_handler_team`.
- *Dynamic*: replay the event stream to `t0` in an isolated store containing only events with `timestamp ≤ t0` (plus tie-safe ordering, see T3) and recompute every feature there. Any feature whose recomputed value differs from the served value is a lineage defect. This is the "shadow prefix recomputation" test and is the single most valuable gate.

**T2 — Activity-order feasibility.**
Check each observed and each explanation-referenced ordering against the normative model rather than against frequency.
- *Footprint / behavioural-profile check*: derive the eventually-follows and concurrency relations from the normative model and compare with the log's relations. `Claim Closed → Damage Assessment Completed` violates the model's ordering; `Appeal → Settlement Decision Recorded` and `Damage Assessment Completed → Settlement Decision Recorded` do not. Limitation: footprints are pairwise and lose expressiveness with loops (repeated evidence cycles) and duplicate labels (two decision events), so they screen but do not decide.
- *Alignment-based conformance* against a Petri net / accepting automaton of the normative process gives per-event diagnostics (log moves, model moves) and is the authoritative check for a full trace. Limitations: results depend on the model's fidelity and on the cost function; a "violation" may indicate an out-of-date model rather than a bad case, so alignment output feeds a review queue, not an automatic reject.
- *Prefix alignment* is mandatory for anything evaluated at `t0`: ordinary alignment penalizes an unfinished case for its missing future. Use prefix alignment to decide only "is this prefix a legal partial execution", and to compute the set of enabled activities at `t0`.
- *Declarative (Declare/LTL) checks* express the semantic qualifications that flow models express awkwardly: `init(Claim Registered)`, `precedence(Coverage Check Completed, Damage Assessment Completed)`, `precedence({Partial, Deny} decision, Appeal)`, `response(Appeal, Settlement Decision Recorded)`, `not-succession(Claim Closed, Damage Assessment Completed)`, `end(Claim Closed | Withdrawn)`. Limitation: activation semantics matter (vacuous satisfaction can hide problems), and constraints must be versioned with the policy.
- *DFG caveat, stated explicitly*: a frequent directly-follows edge is not a normative rule. DFGs flatten concurrency, so the parallel Fraud Investigation manufactures spurious edges into and out of evidence collection and assessment, and interleaving noise creates edges no execution ever intended. The sequence explainer in this audit is operating on exactly such a flattened view, which is why an impossible edge could acquire the highest pattern importance. Pattern features must be validated against the model, not against their own support.

**T3 — Same-timestamp ambiguity.**
Detect all timestamp ties within a case. Resolution order: (1) reliable ingestion sequence index; (2) if absent, enumerate the linearizations of the tied block and intersect with the model-permitted set; (3) if exactly one permitted linearization remains, use it and mark the case as "order-repaired"; (4) if several remain, mark "order-ambiguous". Then run an **order-sensitivity test**: recompute every order-dependent feature and every pattern feature under all admissible linearizations. Any feature or explanation whose value flips is disqualified from use until the ordering is resolved at the source. Case Z's headline pattern fails this test outright: no permitted linearization places `Claim Closed` before `Damage Assessment Completed`.

**T4 — Complete-trace dependence detection.**
- *Differential test*: for a sample of cases compute `f(prefix ≤ t0)` and `f(full trace)`. Report the disagreement rate; any non-zero rate for a feature declared prefix-safe is a defect. `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `last_resource_role` disagree in essentially every case; `final_handler_team` disagrees in ~35% — the important lesson is that a *low* disagreement rate is not a pass, because the disagreeing cases are systematically the hard ones.
- *Suspicion screen*: rank features by mutual information with the label and flag any feature whose single-feature AUC is implausibly high for information available at `t0`; also flag features whose prefix version has near-zero variance (a sign the served version must be using something else).
- *Provenance requirement*: each feature declares the maximum event timestamp it touched; the gate asserts that maximum `≤ t0`.

**T5 — Enabled vs impossible vs future-possible at `t0`.**
Compute the marking of the normative model after the prefix `Claim Registered → Coverage Check Completed` and classify every activity an explanation mentions:
- *Enabled at `t0`*: Damage Assessment Completed (fast track), Documents Requested (first evidence cycle), Fraud Investigation Opened (given a trigger).
- *Future-possible*: Documents Received, Documents Validated, Settlement Decision Recorded, Claim Closed; Appeal is *conditionally* future-possible — reachable only through a Partial or Deny decision, so it is two dependent steps away and must not be treated as a present fact.
- *Impossible*: Claim Closed before a decision or withdrawal, Damage Assessment Completed after Claim Closed, Appeal after Approve, a second Claim Registered.

The publication rule follows: an explanation may cite realized prefix facts and contemporaneous system state; it may cite enabled activities only as branch points ("the case may enter an evidence cycle"); it may cite future-possible activities only as predicted hazard, never as evidence; and citing an impossible ordering is an automatic hard failure that blocks the explanation and opens a data-quality ticket.

**T6 — Legitimacy of repeated Settlement Decision events.**
For every case with two or more `Settlement Decision Recorded` events, assert: a preceding `Appeal`; a first decision in {Partial, Deny}; distinct decision identifiers with a monotone `decision_seq`; and distinct timestamps or a reliable index. If all hold, the repetition is a legitimate reconsideration and must not be reported as a conformance violation or deduplicated. If the appeal is missing, or the first decision was Approve, or the two records are timestamp-identical with equal payloads, classify as duplicate ingestion or as a genuine violation and route accordingly. This gate also requires that the coarse label be refined in the feature layer (`decision_1_type`, `decision_2_type`) so that downstream models never conflate "decided once" with "decided twice", and so that reconsideration cases are not silently collapsed into a single-decision variant.

**T7 — Explanation-integrity gate.** Before an explanation is shown: sum of attributions plus base value reconciles with the score; every cited feature passed T1–T4; every cited activity passed T5; no cited feature is on the outcome-derived denylist. Unattributed residual mass above a threshold (say 10% of the score) blocks publication.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

**(a) Defensible predictive signal** — `initial_damage_complexity`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0` *after* the SQL repair, `initial_reserve` *after* timestamp verification, registration-time documentation completeness, prior claimant/broker response latency computed before `t0`, `fraud_trigger_raised_before_t0`.
*May be claimed*: these are admissible at `t0`, are stable functions of the past, and can legitimately support risk stratification and triage prioritization ("this claim belongs in the high-attention cohort"). Elapsed time may be framed mechanistically as slack consumed against the 10-day target.
*May not be claimed*: that they cause breach, or that acting on them changes the outcome. Complexity and prior history are not levers; changing the recorded complexity code changes nothing about the claim. Importance ranking is not effect size, and none of these has been identified under an interventional design.

**(b) Process-state / congestion signal** — `queue_length_at_t0`, plus the features it should be expanded into: work-in-progress per handling pool, arrivals in the last 24 hours, staffed capacity at `t0`, SIU and assessor backlog, ratio of queue to capacity.
*May be claimed*: this is the strongest class. It is contemporaneous rather than predictive-of-itself, it has an established mechanism (waiting time grows with utilization), and it is the only class that is genuinely operationally actionable — routing, overtime, temporary reallocation, changing the order of service. It is also the right place to invest, because it supports counterfactual questions management can act on.
*May not be claimed*: a precise causal magnitude from SHAP. Queue length is confounded with demand shocks and with case-mix (a spike in complex claims raises both the queue and the risk), so the honest statement is "elevated risk under congestion, with congestion a plausible and partly causal channel". Quantifying the effect requires a design — natural experiments around capacity changes, instrumenting with staffing rosters, or an explicit queueing/simulation model. Data-quality caveat: the ±5 minute tolerance must be enforced per row, and the queue measured must be the queue this case actually waits in.

**(c) Proxy requiring fairness / robustness review** — `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, handler team and role identifiers, and any feature strongly correlated with them.
*May be claimed*: predictive association, and — importantly — usefulness as a diagnostic that some parts of the network or some intake channels are served worse.
*May not be claimed*: that region or channel is "a delay driver" (the team's stated interpretation), that they justify adverse treatment of a claimant, or that they are actionable. The standards diverge sharply here: predictive validity is a statistical property, whereas legitimacy of use is a normative and often regulatory question about whether the feature encodes a permissible operational basis.
*Handling*: do not delete reflexively. Blind deletion destroys the ability to measure disparity, invites proxy re-entry through correlated features, and can degrade subgroup calibration through omitted-variable effects. Instead: decompose into operational mediators (repair-network density, distance to an approved repairer, intake language, broker documentation behaviour) and prefer the mediators; keep the sensitive attribute in the *evaluation and monitoring* set regardless of whether it is in the model; test subgroup calibration and error-rate parity; check whether high-risk scores lead to help (extra capacity) or harm (deprioritization), since the fairness verdict depends on the downstream action; and require human review before any adverse use.

**(d) Drift / calendar proxy** — `calendar_week`, and implicitly any period identifier.
*May be claimed*: that model performance and process behaviour differ across periods, and that the model should therefore be evaluated and monitored over time.
*May not be claimed*: that "week 52 is operationally worse" is a transferable rule. Week 52 here is a stand-in for two distinct and separable regimes — a holiday capacity trough and an operating-policy change — and the two will not coincide again. Left as a raw week index, the feature is a temporal-overfitting vehicle and a drift hazard.
*Handling*: replace with explicit regime variables (`policy_version`, holiday/working-day capacity, planned FTE), evaluate with time-blocked or rolling-origin validation, and report performance per regime rather than pooled.

**(e) Unusable until lineage is repaired** — `final_handler_team`, `risk_score_current`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (pre-fix), `employee_experience`, `initial_reserve` (pending verification).
*May be claimed*: nothing, currently. The critical distinction from class (f) below is that these features describe facts that *plausibly existed before `t0`* and are recoverable given history tables and as-of joins — the defect is in the plumbing, not in the concept.
*Not repairable at all (drop, do not "fix")* — `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_decision`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `num_document_requests_total`, `last_resource_role`, and all five sequence patterns. These are irreducibly post-`t0`: their prefix-restricted versions are either constant or empty, so no engineering makes them admissible at this prediction point. Conflating "repairable lineage defect" with "future information" is the error that keeps leakage alive across remediation cycles.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

**P0 — Stop the bleeding (immediately).**
1. Suspend publication of these explanations and of the affected scores. Denylist the irreducibly post-`t0` features (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_decision`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `num_document_requests_total`, `last_resource_role`, `final_handler_team` as currently sourced) and all five sequence patterns at the serving layer, so that no downstream consumer can request them at this prediction point.
2. Retrain a prefix-only baseline and publish the honest performance delta side by side with the old numbers. Set the expectation explicitly with stakeholders: the metric drop is the correction, not a regression.
3. Retrospectively assess decisions already taken on leaked explanations, especially any that deprioritized or escalated claims.

**P1 — Feature-store design and historical timestamps (the root cause).**
4. Make point-in-time correctness a structural property, not a review item: every feature is stored as `(entity, value, valid_from, valid_to, source_event_id)` and every training or serving read is an as-of join at `t0`. Ban `extraction_date`, `current_date` and closure-populated warehouse columns from feature definitions by lint rule, and ban nightly-batch snapshot tables as inputs to intra-day prediction points.
5. Convert all slowly-changing dimensions to type-2 history: handler team, role, employee experience, risk score, reserve, product and policy version. Where history was never retained, mark the feature unusable rather than approximating availability from an agreement rate — the `final_handler_team` 65% is the cautionary example.
6. Register each feature with a declared maximum touched event timestamp and an owner; require offline/online parity tests so the served value equals the recomputed value.

**P2 — Prefix-only recomputation and process-conformance tests in CI.**
7. Build the prefix replay harness (T1 dynamic gate) and run the prefix-vs-full-trace differential test (T4) on every feature at every release. Fail the build on any non-zero disagreement for a feature declared prefix-safe, and treat a small disagreement rate as more dangerous than a large one because it hides in the hard cases.
8. Add the conformance gates as tests: prefix-alignment legality of every scored prefix, footprint and declarative checks for ordering, the tie-detection and order-sensitivity test (T3), the enabled/impossible/future-possible classifier for explanation references (T5), and the repeated-decision legitimacy check (T6). Version the normative model and the declarative constraint set alongside the code, and record which model version certified each explanation.
9. Add the explanation-integrity gate (T7): attribution reconciles with the score, no denylisted feature cited, residual mass under threshold, and a hard block on any explanation referencing a process-impossible ordering.

**P3 — Statistical and normative validation of the repaired model.**
10. Recalibrate after feature removal and report calibration explicitly (reliability curves, ECE, Brier decomposition) overall and per regime and subgroup. Leakage removal typically shifts both discrimination and calibration; a repaired model with a lower AUC and honest probabilities is the deliverable.
11. Validate with time-blocked and rolling-origin splits, never random splits, so that regime alignment (policy change, holiday period) cannot be memorized. Report per-regime performance.
12. Measure explanation stability: rank agreement of global importances across bootstrap resamples, seeds and time windows; local-attribution stability under small perturbations of admissible inputs and under alternative admissible event linearizations; and agreement between attribution methods. Unstable rankings must not be narrated as findings. Where feasible, prefer explanation methods that respect the temporal structure of the prefix over post-hoc attributions on a flattened feature vector.
13. Run proxy and fairness analysis on the repaired model: mediation of region and channel into operational mediators, subgroup calibration and error-rate parity, disparity attribution across the retained features, and an assessment of whether the downstream action is help or harm. Keep sensitive attributes in the monitoring set even when excluded from the model, and record the regulatory basis for every retained proxy.
14. Separate the three registers in every published artifact: predictive (associational, validated out of time), causal (requires a design — natural experiments around capacity or policy changes, or an explicit queueing/simulation model — and currently supported only for congestion, tentatively), and actionable (levers management controls: routing, capacity, early parallelization of SIU work, front-loading document requests). Train the model team on the distinction, since the original interpretations column shows the failure mode is conceptual as much as technical.

**P4 — Ongoing monitoring.**
15. Monitor schema and lineage drift: alert when a feature's `valid_from` distribution shifts relative to `t0`, when a source table's refresh cadence or population time changes, when a new column appears in the scoring set without a lineage record, and when the rate of timestamp ties or missing sequence indices rises.
16. Monitor process drift: variant and DFG distributions, evidence-cycle repetition rates, appeal and reconsideration rates, conformance-violation rates against the versioned normative model, and queue/capacity regimes. Trigger a model and explanation re-audit when the process changes, since a control-flow change can silently turn a previously safe feature into a leaking one, and re-review the normative model itself rather than assuming every new violation is a data error.
17. Track explanation-gate outcomes as a first-class metric: the share of scored cases quarantined, the reasons, and the residual attribution mass. A rising quarantine rate is an early warning about the pipeline, not a nuisance to be suppressed.
