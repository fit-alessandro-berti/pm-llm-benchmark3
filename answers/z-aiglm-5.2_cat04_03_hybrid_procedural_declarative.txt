# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

Material assumptions:

- An "accepted case" is one that reaches `Activate Vendor`; a "rejected case" is one that reaches `Reject Vendor`. The two terminal outcomes are mutually exclusive (R1).
- "Applicable approval" in R9 means `Manager Approval` for the low-risk route and `Compliance Approval` for the high-risk route. The high-risk route is the only route that may produce `Sanctions Escalation`, but R7 only requires that escalation follows screening, not that it implies the high-risk branch.
- The clarification loop (R3) is interleaved with the procedural backbone; it is not anchored to a single gateway. It is therefore modeled declaratively (alt-response) rather than as a block-structured loop.
- The emergency authorization in R6 ("jointly authorized outside the event log") cannot be reconstructed from event attributes. R6 is therefore *data-conditional* and not activity-only expressible.
- "Several clarification cycles are possible" implies `Request Clarification` and `Receive Clarification` may occur multiple times; occurrence-count caps apply only to terminal and approval activities (R10).

**R5/R6 conflict.** R5 (Legal): after `Sanctions Escalation`, `Obtain Legal Opinion` must precede `Activate Vendor` under all circumstances. R6 (Operations): emergency activation may precede the opinion, provided it is jointly authorized outside the log and the opinion arrives within 24 hours. These are incompatible as written.

Three defensible resolutions:

- **Resolution A — Strict R5 (conservative, log-only).** Treat any `Activate Vendor` occurring before `Obtain Legal Opinion` when `Sanctions Escalation` has occurred as a hard control-flow violation. R6 is set aside because its enabling condition (external joint authorization) is not observable in the event log.
  - *Compliance risk:* legitimate emergency activations are flagged as violations; the model is safe but may over-reject.
- **Resolution B — Strict R6 with required external attribute.** Introduce a case-level boolean `emergency_authorized`. If set, `Activate Vendor` may precede `Obtain Legal Opinion`, but the opinion must arrive within 24 h of escalation (which, conveniently, equals the temporal-profile upper bound `12 + 2·6 = 24` h at zeta = 2.0). If the attribute is absent, default to denial.
  - *Compliance risk:* relies on an authoritative external system; missing attribute causes false positives, but the audit trail is explicit.
- **Resolution C — Conservative-default with external override (chosen).** In the absence of `emergency_authorized = True`, the model treats R5 as authoritative: `Activate Vendor` before `Obtain Legal Opinion` after `Sanctions Escalation` is a hard violation. When `emergency_authorized = True`, the same pattern is downgraded to a *temporal* obligation: opinion must arrive within 24 h (encoded by the temporal profile at zeta = 2.0). The activity-only DECLARE layer still encodes a *global* precedence approximation that is too strong and is therefore non-authoritative; the authoritative check is the resource/data-aware layer.
  - *Compliance risk:* a real emergency logged without the external flag will be misclassified as a violation; a wrongfully granted flag will silently downgrade a breach to a warning. The choice favors the auditor's ability to recover evidence over operational flexibility.

For the remainder of this document, **Resolution C** is implemented.

from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

def _leaf(label: str) -> ProcessTree:
    return ProcessTree(label=label)

def _seq(children: list) -> ProcessTree:
    pt = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE)
    pt.children = children
    for c in children:
        c.parent = pt
    return pt

def _xor(children: list) -> ProcessTree:
    pt = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR)
    pt.children = children
    for c in children:
        c.parent = pt
    return pt

def _par(children: list) -> ProcessTree:
    pt = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.PARALLEL)
    pt.children = children
    for c in children:
        c.parent = pt
    return pt

# Leaves
receive = _leaf("Receive Onboarding Request")
cmd     = _leaf("Capture Master Data")
rss     = _leaf("Run Sanctions Screening")
vba     = _leaf("Verify Bank Account")
cr      = _leaf("Classify Risk")
rv      = _leaf("Reject Vendor")
ma      = _leaf("Manager Approval")
av      = _leaf("Activate Vendor")
rfp     = _leaf("Release First Payment")
co      = _leaf("Close Onboarding")
edd     = _leaf("Enhanced Due Diligence")
ca      = _leaf("Compliance Approval")

# Parallel three-check block
three_checks = _par([cmd, rss, vba])

# Three mutually exclusive outcomes
reject_branch  = _seq([rv, co])
low_branch     = _seq([ma, av, rfp, co])
high_branch    = _seq([edd, ca, av, rfp, co])
outcomes       = _xor([reject_branch, low_branch, high_branch])

# Backbone
procedural_model = _seq([receive, three_checks, cr, outcomes])
```

## 3.3 Explanation

- **Concurrency:** `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, `Verify Bank Account` run in a `PARALLEL` block; `Classify Risk` requires all three (block completion).
- **Choices:** `XOR` over three terminal branches encodes R1/R2 mutually exclusive outcomes at the backbone level.
- **Clarification loop:** deliberately excluded from the tree (see approximation above); enforced by DECLARE alt-response.
- **Terminal outcomes:** `Close Onboarding` is reachable from both the reject branch and the accepted branches (after `Release First Payment`), matching "Close Onboarding follows either rejection or first payment."
- **Post-activation behavior** (`Change Bank Details`, `Suspend Vendor`): excluded from the tree and handled by DECLARE + resource layer to avoid forcing it on every accepted case.

        # Conservative approximation of R5 (too strong globally; authoritative check is the data layer)
        (("Obtain Legal Opinion", "Activate Vendor"),         {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0}),
    ],
}
```

| DECLARE rule | Direction / semantics | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `noncoexistence(Reject Vendor, Activate Vendor)` | Symmetric: never both in same trace. | Enforces R1. |
| `noncoexistence(Manager Approval, Compliance Approval)` | Symmetric exclusion. | R2/R8. |
| `noncoexistence(Manager Approval, Enhanced Due Diligence)` | Symmetric exclusion. | R8. |
| `exactly_one(Activate Vendor)` | Exactly one occurrence. | R10 (note: across cases this is 0-or-1; encoded as exactly_one because in any *executed* accept branch it must occur). |
| `exactly_one(Release First Payment)` | Exactly one occurrence. | R10. |
| `exactly_one(Reject Vendor)` | Exactly one occurrence in reject branch. | R10 (same caveat). |
| `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` | Each A eventually followed by B (no ordering). | R4. |
| `response(Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor)` | Each change eventually followed by suspend. | R11 (partial). |
| `altresponse(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` | Each A eventually followed by B before next A. | R3; the "before final outcome" portion is approximated. |
| `precedence(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` | Each B preceded by A. | R7. |
| `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` | Each Activate Vendor preceded by Obtain Legal Opinion. | **Approximation of R5; too strong on no-escalation cases.** Authoritative check is the data layer. |

Distinguishing notes:

- **Response vs precedence:** `response(A,B)` = A triggers a future B; `precedence(A,B)` = B requires a prior A. We use response for triggering obligations (R4, R11) and precedence for prerequisites (R7, R5-approx).
- **Alt-response vs chain-response:** `altresponse(A,B)` allows other activities between A and B but forbids a second A before B; `chainresponse(A,B)` would require B *immediately* after A. R3 explicitly permits other activities between request and response, so alt-response, not chain-response.
- **Noncoexistence vs nonsuccession:** noncoexistence is symmetric and case-wide (R1, R8); nonsuccession would forbid B *after* A only. R1/R8 are symmetric, so noncoexistence.

Overlap and disagreements:

- `never_together` mirrors DECLARE `noncoexistence` for R1/R8 — redundant; Log Skeleton is authoritative because it is symmetric and un-oriented.
- `always_before (Sanctions Escalation, Run Sanctions Screening)` mirrors DECLARE `precedence` for R7 — same direction.
- `always_before (Obtain Legal Opinion, Sanctions Escalation)` is **weaker** than DECLARE's R5-approximation: it only says "if Opinion occurs, Escalation occurred before" — it does **not** require Opinion before Activate Vendor. The data layer is authoritative for R5.
- `always_before (Release First Payment, Activate Vendor)` and `always_after (Receive Onboarding Request, Close Onboarding)` reinforce the procedural backbone.
- `directly_follows {(Reject Vendor, Close Onboarding)}` is the only strict directly-follows constraint; the parallel three-check block is intentionally *not* in `directly_follows` because the order varies (T1 vs T2).
- `activ_freq` for `Verify Bank Account = {1,2}` permits the second verification required by R11; this is a *necessary* but not *sufficient* condition — the resource/data layer must check that the second occurs *after* `Change Bank Details` and *before* the later `Release First Payment`.

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"):  (48 * 3600, 24 * 3600),   # (172800, 86400)
    ("Sanctions Escalation",  "Obtain Legal Opinion"):   (12 * 3600,  6 * 3600),   # (43200, 21600)
    ("Activate Vendor",       "Release First Payment"):  (72 * 3600, 36 * 3600),   # (259200, 129600)
}

zeta = 2.0
```

Why deviations are warnings, not control-flow violations:

- R12 explicitly states these expectations are "normative enough to flag but not automatically reject." The temporal profile is therefore a soft conformance layer: a deviation raises a `warning`, not a `fail`.
- Notably, for `Sanctions Escalation  Obtain Legal Opinion`, the upper bound at zeta = 2.0 is `12 + 2·6 = 24` hours, which coincides exactly with R6's 24-hour deadline. Under Resolution C with `emergency_authorized = True`, exceeding 24 h remains a warning but, combined with the R6 policy, can be elevated by the resource/data layer to a hard breach.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Expected event-log columns (at minimum):
#   case:concept:name, concept:name, time:timestamp, org:resource
# Optional case-level attribute (joined on case id):
#   case:emergency_authorized  (bool) — required only for R6 override

APPROVAL_ACTIVITIES = {"Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval"}

def events_for_case(df: pd.DataFrame, case_id) -> pd.DataFrame:
    return df[df["case:concept:name"] == case_id].sort_values("time:timestamp").reset_index(drop=True)

def first_resource(df_case: pd.DataFrame, activity: str):
    sub = df_case[df_case["concept:name"] == activity]
    if sub.empty:
        return None
    return sub.iloc[0]["org:resource"]

def check_r9(df_case: pd.DataFrame) -> list:
    """Segregation of duties.
    Required attributes: org:resource on every event.
    If resource missing -> treat as fail (cannot demonstrate compliance)."""
    violations = []
    vba_res = first_resource(df_case, "Verify Bank Account")
    cmd_res = first_resource(df_case, "Capture Master Data")
    av_res  = first_resource(df_case, "Activate Vendor")
    approval_res = None
    for a in APPROVAL_ACTIVITIES:
        r = first_resource(df_case, a)
        if r is not None:
            approval_res = r
            break
    if vba_res is None or approval_res is None:
        # not evaluable if approval/verification did not occur; ignore unless they did
        if vba_res is not None and approval_res is not None:
            violations.append("R9a: missing resource on approval or verification")
    elif vba_res == approval_res:
        violations.append(f"R9a four-eyes: VBA and approval by same resource {vba_res}")
    if cmd_res is not None and av_res is not None:
        if cmd_res == av_res:
            violations.append(f"R9b four-eyes: Capture Master Data and Activate Vendor by same resource {cmd_res}")
    # If resource attribute itself missing entirely:
    if "org:resource" not in df_case.columns:
        violations.append("R9: org:resource attribute missing — cannot verify segregation of duties")
    return violations

def check_r5_r6(df_case: pd.DataFrame, emergency_flag: bool | None) -> tuple[str, list]:
    """
    Resolution C.
    Returns (verdict, reasons).
    Required attributes:
       - org:resource (for completeness, not used here)
       - case:emergency_authorized (bool) — optional; if absent -> None -> default R5 strict
    """
    reasons = []
    activities = df_case["concept:name"].tolist()
    if "Sanctions Escalation" not in activities:
        return "pass", []
    if "Obtain Legal Opinion" not in activities:
        reasons.append("R4: Sanctions Escalation without Obtain Legal Opinion")
        return "fail", reasons

    esc_idx  = activities.index("Sanctions Escalation")
    opin_idx = activities.index("Obtain Legal Opinion")
    act_idx  = activities.index("Activate Vendor") if "Activate Vendor" in activities else None

    if act_idx is None:
        return "pass", []   # rejected case, no activation

    if opin_idx < act_idx:
        return "pass", []   # R5 satisfied trivially

    # Activate before Legal Opinion after Sanctions Escalation:
    if emergency_flag is True:
        # R6 downgrade: opinion must be within 24h of escalation
        esc_t  = pd.to_datetime(df_case.iloc[esc_idx]["time:timestamp"])
        opin_t = pd.to_datetime(df_case.iloc[opin_idx]["time:timestamp"])
        delta_h = (opin_t - esc_t).total_seconds() / 3600.0
        if delta_h > 24.0:
            reasons.append(f"R6: emergency authorized but opinion arrived after 24h ({delta_h:.1f}h)")
            return "fail", reasons
        reasons.append(f"R6: emergency authorized, opinion within 24h ({delta_h:.1f}h) -> warning only")
        return "warning", reasons

    # No emergency flag -> R5 strict
    reasons.append("R5: Activate Vendor before Obtain Legal Opinion after Sanctions Escalation; no emergency flag")
    return "fail", reasons

def check_r11(df_case: pd.DataFrame) -> list:
    """Change Bank Details -> Suspend Vendor -> second Verify Bank Account -> Release First Payment."""
    reasons = []
    activities = df_case["concept:name"].tolist()
    if "Change Bank Details" not in activities:
        return []
    # Only post-activation changes are scoped by R11
    if "Activate Vendor" not in activities:
        return []
    act_idx = activities.index("Activate Vendor")
    cbd_idx = activities.index("Change Bank Details")
    if cbd_idx < act_idx:
        return []   # change happened before activation — not in R11 scope
    # Suspend Vendor after change
    if "Suspend Vendor" not in activities:
        reasons.append("R11: Change Bank Details not followed by Suspend Vendor")
        return reasons
    susp_idx = activities.index("Suspend Vendor")
    if susp_idx < cbd_idx:
        reasons.append("R11: Suspend Vendor before Change Bank Details")
        return reasons
    # Another Verify Bank Account after Suspend, before later Release First Payment
    later_vba = [i for i, a in enumerate(activities) if a == "Verify Bank Account" and i > susp_idx]
    if not later_vba:
        reasons.append("R11: no second Verify Bank Account after Suspend Vendor")
        return reasons
    later_rfp = [i for i, a in enumerate(activities) if a == "Release First Payment" and i > susp_idx]
    if later_rfp and min(later_rfp) < min(later_vba):
        reasons.append("R11: Release First Payment before the post-suspend Verify Bank Account")
    return reasons

# Four-eyes filter helpers (PM4Py), if used at log scale:
# sub = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, "Verify Bank Account", "Manager Approval")
# (returns cases where the two activities are performed by different resources)
```

Notes on missing attributes:

- `org:resource` missing  R9 is *not evaluable*; treat as fail because compliance cannot be demonstrated.
- `case:emergency_authorized` missing  default to R5 strict (Resolution C). This is the conservative default.

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

- **Duplicated enforcement across layers.** R1 (Reject/Activate exclusion), R7 (screening before escalation), R8 (approval mutual exclusion) are encoded in both DECLARE and Log Skeleton. This redundancy is intentional: LS handles symmetry cleanly, DECLARE handles response/precedence cleanly. The risk is that the two layers disagree in edge cases (e.g., LS `always_before` is weaker than DECLARE `precedence` for R5; we deliberately did *not* mirror the strong R5-approximation in LS).
- **Constraints that become inconsistent under loops or post-activation changes.** `response(Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor)` is fine, but `precedence(Release First Payment, Verify Bank Account)` would fire on the *first* Release First Payment, which has no preceding second verification. R11 therefore cannot be expressed as a global DECLARE precedence; the resource layer must count occurrences after `Suspend Vendor`. Similarly, R3's alt-response is safe under multiple cycles, but combining it with `exactly_one(Release First Payment)` is fine; combining it with `exactly_one(Receive Clarification)` would be wrong — that is why we used `activ_freq` {0,1,2,3} in LS for clarifications.
- **Vacuity in declarative rules.** `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` is vacuously satisfied on no-escalation cases (Activate Vendor occurs, but the constraint does not require Legal Opinion if Sanctions Escalation did not happen — except that global precedence *does* require it). This is the over-strong approximation disclosed in §2. Similarly, `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` is vacuous on traces without escalation (T1, T3, T4, T5, T8).
- **Missing case attributes.** `emergency_authorized` is not in the event log. R6 cannot be evaluated activity-only. Our conservative default (Resolution C, no flag  R5 strict) avoids silent policy weakening but penalizes legitimate emergencies that are not properly flagged externally.
- **Timestamp-only limitations.** The temporal profile uses elapsed time between two activity occurrences; it cannot express deadlines relative to case start, nor distinguish "early" from "late" except via mean ± zeta·sd. R12's "normative enough to flag" is therefore a necessary softening — temporal deviations must not be auto-rejections.
- **Support/confidence as normative truth.** In our DECLARE model, `support` and `confidence` are both set to 1.0 because these are *policy* rules, not discovered patterns. Treating discovered support/confidence as normative would conflate "frequent" with "allowed"; e.g., a frequently observed four-eyes violation would acquire high support and could be misread as compliant. The values are metadata for the conformance engine, not evidence of correctness.
- **Resource/data conditions unrecoverable from activity sequences.** R9 (segregation of duties) and R6 (external authorization) cannot be inferred from activity labels alone; two traces with identical activity sequences may differ in compliance solely because of `org:resource` or `emergency_authorized`. Any activity-only model that pretends to enforce them is necessarily unsound.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

- **Canonical procedural representation:** the ProcessTree in §3.2 is the canonical control-flow model. It captures the stable backbone (init, parallel three checks, classify, three-way XOR outcome). It deliberately omits the clarification loop, escalation/legal-opinion pair, and post-activation bank-detail cycle, which are not block-structured.
- **Authoritative policy layer:** the resource/data-aware layer in §7 is authoritative for R5, R6, R9, and the "second Verify Bank Account" portion of R11. DECLARE and Log Skeleton are *secondary*; they catch coarse activity-only violations but cannot adjudicate conditional or resource-sensitive policy.
- **Conformance check ordering (per case):**
  1. **Log Skeleton** — cheap, fast, symmetric pair/frequency checks (R1, R8, R10, R7). Surfaces terminal contradictions early.
  2. **DECLARE** — catches response/precedence/alt-response violations (R3, R4, R7, R11-partial). DECLARE's R5-approximation is flagged as non-authoritative.
  3. **Procedural alignment** — replay/align against the ProcessTree to detect activities outside the backbone (T8's `Reject Vendor` after activation).
  4. **Temporal profile** — emits warnings (never auto-failures); the Sanctions Escalation pair doubles as R6's 24-hour deadline check when `emergency_authorized=True`.
  5. **Resource/data layer** — authoritative for R5/R6 (with `emergency_authorized`), R9, and R11's counting portion. Runs last because it requires the richest attributes.
- **Surfacing contradictions to users:** findings should be presented per layer with explicit severity labels (`fail`, `warning`, `policy-dependent`, `not evaluated`) and a single *overall verdict* that the resource/data layer adjudicates when layers disagree. The R5/R6 disagreement must be visible to the user: e.g., for T6, the UI should state "DECLARE precedence violated (Activate Vendor before Obtain Legal Opinion); resource/data layer verdict depends on `emergency_authorized`, which is absent — defaulting to R5 strict; user may attach external authorization to downgrade to warning." This makes the policy conflict auditable rather than silently resolved.
