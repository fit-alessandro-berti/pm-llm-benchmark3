# 1. Objectives and constraints

I recommend a **constraint-first, rolling assignment policy**: preserve qualified ownership where it is valuable, use supervised learning deliberately, and reserve scarce expertise for work that needs it. Assignment should include a feasible execution plan—not merely attach a resource name to a case.

This is a defensible policy, not a demonstrated optimum. The snapshot supports checking skills, assignment counts, ownership changes, and planned training coverage. It does **not** establish how many cases will finish today or meet their deadlines: activity durations, existing commitments, resource calendars, and downstream case requirements are missing.

## Objectives

Optimize several outcomes together: completed cases without avoidable rework; SLA protection, especially in the tail of the waiting-time distribution; sustainable workload; meaningful skill development; continuity; and capacity to recover from absence or unexpected arrivals. A faster activity completion is not necessarily a better outcome if it causes another diagnosis loop, an appeal, or an overloaded specialist.

## Safety and operational constraints

| Category                                    | Treatment                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Authorization and supervision: hard**     | Skill 0 cannot execute the activity, including under supervision. High/urgent work cannot go to skill 1 without supervision. As an additional policy safeguard, require supervision for **all** skill-1 execution.                                                |
| **Security: hard**                          | Skill 3 may execute independently; levels 1–2 require explicit supervision by Gita or Hugo. Treat C001’s “needs specialist” note as a stricter, case-specific requirement for a specialist lead.                                                                  |
| **Approval: hard**                          | Only levels 2–3 may finalize. Level 1 may prepare, with a separate authorized finalizer. Neither supervision nor a training objective authorizes a level-0 participant to perform approval work.                                                                  |
| **Capacity: hard**                          | Maximum four new activity commitments per resource in this batch. Conservatively count retained-owner activity assignments, supervision, and substantive trainee participation toward this limit. Also check actual available hours and overlapping appointments. |
| **Overtime protection: guarded exception**  | Exclude high-overtime-risk resources from additional execution or supervision unless SLA risk is urgent and no safer feasible alternative exists. Record the alternatives rejected and the exception approval.                                                    |
| **Quality batching: hard eligibility rule** | Only normal/low-priority Quality Checks with more than 24 hours remaining may be batched. Recheck eligibility when the batch starts.                                                                                                                              |
| **Training: conditional batch requirement** | At least 20% of normal/low assignments must support documented priorities when compatible with acceptable SLA risk. Here there are **nine** such assignments, so the target is **two distinct assignments**, not two participants on one case.                    |

Continuity, degree of specialization, workload equalization, and specialist reserve are preference trade-offs **within** these restrictions. They cannot compensate for an unauthorized assignment.

## Explicit assumptions

**A1 — Complexity threshold.** Use complexity ≥0.75 as an initial definition of “high complexity.” This is a proposed policy threshold, not a supplied fact. It identifies C001, C007, and C016.

**A2 — Ownership convention.** In the recommendation below, the named lead becomes or remains the accountable case owner. A supervisor or learner does not automatically become a co-owner. Organizations that separate activity responsibility from case ownership should log and audit both.

**A3 — Operational feasibility.** Cross-team work is permitted, subject to access checks and worker acceptance. “Hours remaining today” is an upper bound on working time, **not necessarily uncommitted capacity**. Assignments therefore require duration and calendar validation before release.

The supplied historical evidence motivates caution, not deterministic arithmetic. The 1.8× rework and 1.4× appeal figures are associations without baseline probabilities or adjustment details. Likewise, the 2.7-hour handover median and 3.3-hour weak-skill service penalty should not be mechanically subtracted, added, or interpreted as causal effects for an individual case.

# 2. Assignment policy

## Candidate plans and explainable scoring

For each case, generate plans consisting of an executor or lead, any learner, an authorized supervisor/finalizer where required, and a proposed start window.

Let \(F^{90}_{ia}\) be the estimated 90th-percentile **case completion time** under plan \(a\), including existing commitments, handover, supervision, and downstream work. Define:

$$
\text{slack}_{ia}=\text{SLA deadline}_i-F^{90}_{ia}.
$$

A next-activity completion estimate is insufficient when the deadline applies to the whole case.

For dispatch ordering, one possible urgency score is:

$$
U_i=p_i+
2\,\operatorname{clip}\left(
\frac{b-\max_a\text{slack}_{ia}}{b},0,2
\right)+\text{aging bonus}_i,
$$

where \(p_i=4,3,2,1\) for urgent, high, normal, and low priority, and \(b>0\) is a published safety buffer. These are proposed settings to calibrate, not empirically estimated coefficients. Aging prevents normal and low-priority work from being postponed indefinitely.

Without duration estimates, use priority and remaining SLA time provisionally, while marking predicted slack as **unknown**.

Among eligible plans, minimize a decomposable cost:

$$
J_{ia}
=w_RR_{ia}+w_EE_{ia}+w_HH_{ia}
+w_WW_{ia}+w_BB_{ia}
-w_KK_{ia}-w_DD_{ia}.
$$

| Component                        | Explainable interpretation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **SLA risk \(R\)**               | Estimated breach probability and expected lateness. A simpler fallback is a penalty for insufficient predicted slack. Report uncertainty alongside the score.                                                                                                            |
| **Effort \(E\)**                 | Expected person-hours, including supervision, handover, and expected corrective work. This rewards effective throughput rather than assignment volume.                                                                                                                   |
| **Skill match \(K\)**            | Competent execution is sufficient for ordinary work; specialist expertise receives extra value for complex or explicitly specialist-required work. Do not interpret skill 3 as “three times faster.” Supervision does not turn a trainee into an independent specialist. |
| **Continuity cost \(H\)**        | Zero ownership-change penalty for retention; otherwise increase the penalty with handover band, complexity, and accumulated reassignments. Apply an additional warning when a change would move the case above two reassignments.                                        |
| **Workload/overtime cost \(W\)** | Projected committed hours divided by available hours, concentration of difficult work, mentoring load, and overtime exposure. Open-case counts are a secondary proxy, not a substitute for effort.                                                                       |
| **Development benefit \(D\)**    | Credit only for substantive, documented work matching the learner’s recorded priority, with an agreed learning objective and feedback. Shadowing a prohibited activity does not satisfy this assignment target.                                                          |
| **Bottleneck cost \(B\)**        | Penalize consuming scarce qualified capacity on work others can safely do. Include both specialist execution and supervision commitments, and the consequences of losing one qualified resource.                                                                         |

Weights should be published and agreed with worker representatives and process owners. Safety gates, the assignment cap, and the conditional training requirement remain outside the weighted score. The current recommendation is a rule-based application of this framework, not the output of a fitted numerical optimizer.

## Algorithm

```text
At each dispatch cycle, material state change, or relevant completion:

1. Refresh case readiness, priorities, deadlines, ownership, qualifications,
   calendars, current commitments, and training consent.

2. Generate independent, supervised, and prepare/finalize candidate plans.
   Reject plans violating authorization, Security, Approval, access,
   supervision, assignment-count, or overtime-exception rules.

3. Estimate each plan's effort, completion-time distribution, handover burden,
   and effect on other already-promised work.
   Reserve time for every participant, not just the primary executor.

4. Protect urgent work and feasible high-complexity/high-handover continuity.
   Rank cases by SLA urgency and aging; retain feasible current owners unless
   an alternative has a material, explainable advantage.

5. Select a joint batch, not independent winners for each case.
   Include at least ceil(0.20 × normal/low assignments) genuine training
   assignments when the joint schedule satisfies the agreed risk tolerance.

6. Compare remaining plans using the published score components.
   Reject dominated plans; consider small swaps to reduce bottlenecks.

7. Break near-ties by:
   lower SLA risk → ownership retention → lower normalized workload →
   less recent access to training → auditable rotating tie-break.

8. Validate Quality batching, all resource calendars, and all assignment caps.
   Commit reservations atomically; record reasons and rejected alternatives.

9. If infeasible, defer nonurgent release, remove optional training involvement,
   or escalate for authorized capacity. Never silently relax skill rules.
   Record any training shortfall rather than changing its denominator.

10. Replan unfinished work when conditions change, but avoid routine
    reassignment of started work. Require a material benefit and explicit
    handover justification before changing an established assignment.
```

For training, a proposed initial guard is that the paired plan’s estimated case-completion p90 remains before the deadline with a buffer, without materially worsening urgent cases. This is a risk tolerance—not a guarantee.

Supervision must specify who is available, when, for which decisions, and who signs off. Being assigned to the same case is not enough.

# 4. Comparison with baseline policies

## First distinguish raw rules from deployable comparators

The literal first-available policy is not safety-compliant. Ignoring its uncheckable duration condition, it would favor Jonas for C002 because he has four open cases and nonzero Approval skill—but his Approval level is 1, so he cannot finalize. For C001, it would favor Celine among resources with nonzero Security skill, despite the specialist requirement.

Specialist-only routing avoids these particular qualification failures, but it does not inherently protect continuity, mentoring capacity, or overtime. It also routes all Final Approvals to Farid because he is the only Approval level-3 resource, despite Eva and Bruno being independently authorized at level 2.

Raw baselines should therefore be evaluated in shadow mode or simulation. Live comparators must honor the same organizational constraints and be labelled **constrained first-available** and **constrained specialist-only**, rather than silently altering their definitions.

## Expected trade-offs

These are hypotheses supported by the supplied scenario and policy structure, not measured performance results.

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators

The supplied columns are a useful starting point, but they are insufficient for a complete audit. Add an **assignment ledger** containing one record per decision, linked to execution records through `assignment_id` and `activity_instance_id`.

Record decision time, ready/start/complete times, participant roles, supervisor acceptance and sign-off, skill and overtime status **at assignment time**, expected effort, reserved time, ownership before/after, training objective and completion, batch identifier, policy version, score components, and override reason. Define whether `team` refers to the case team or executing resource’s team.

| Indicator                            | Definition and interpretation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Execution handover frequency**     | Resource changes between adjacent, reliably ordered execution events divided by comparable adjacent-event pairs; also report per case and activity transition. Separate required role transitions from avoidable movement.                                                         |
| **Ownership reassignment**           | Count explicit changes between two nonempty accountable owners. Report mean, p90, share above two changes, and changes on high-complexity/high-cost cases. Do not sum a cumulative `reassignment_count` repeated on every event.                                                   |
| **Skill mismatch and authorization** | Share of executed work violating the rule applicable to that activity and participant role. Separate prohibited level-0 work, missing supervision, unauthorized Security execution, and unauthorized finalization. Unknown historical qualifications are “unknown,” not compliant. |
| **Workload distribution**            | Distribution of time-weighted open cases and committed/executed effort divided by available working hours. Include mentoring, difficult-case concentration, and interrupted work. Report dispersion within comparable roles as well as across teams.                               |
| **Overtime-risk exposure**           | Assignment share and reserved-effort share directed to high-overtime-risk resources at decision time. Separately report documented exceptions and actual work beyond scheduled availability.                                                                                       |
| **SLA misses**                       | Case completion after the applicable case deadline, stratified by priority, complexity, policy version, and routing type. Use the business-defined completion event—not simply the last event observed. Report still-open overdue cases separately from not-yet-due cases.         |
| **Rework/appeal after assignment**   | New rework or appeal within a predefined follow-up window after the assignment. Distinguish pre-existing loops and document-related corrections from new failures. Report follow-up completeness and case-mix-adjusted comparisons.                                                |
| **Training assignment share**        | Distinct normal/low assignment packages with substantive work matching a documented training priority divided by all normal/low packages. Report planned and realized shares separately, alongside feedback completion and later assessed competence.                              |
| **Specialist bottlenecks**           | Ready-to-start waiting p50/p90/p95, queue size and age, reserved/available specialist hours, supervision load, and share of work concentrated on each qualified resource. Test capacity after removing one resource.                                                               |
| **Policy deviations**                | Per-rule violations, unauthorized overrides, missing reason codes, cap breaches, unavailable supervisors, stale qualifications, and unlogged post-assignment changes. Separate valid exceptions from breaches and unverifiable decisions.                                          |
| **Batching compliance**              | For every actual Quality batch, check priority and remaining SLA at batch formation/start. Measure any extra holding time, execution benefit, and defects. Count each case separately toward assignment and outcome indicators.                                                    |

PM4Py’s handover network describes resource succession, while its working-together network connects resources participating in the same case. Neither alone establishes an ownership change or proves that supervision occurred. Keep the ownership ledger and explicit supervision records authoritative. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][1])

Audit at three levels: immediate alerts for safety violations, batch-level checks for capacity and training, and periodic team-level outcome review. Include worker feedback about interruptions, unsupported learning, and administrative burden.

For causal evaluation, compare against pre-assignment characteristics and use chronological validation. An unadjusted increase in rework among specialist-assigned cases could reflect specialists receiving harder cases. Do not turn that association into an individual performance judgment.

# 7. PM4Py implementation sketch

Use separate views for **execution**, **assignment decisions**, and **case outcomes**. Do not insert reassignment decisions into an execution-only process model as though they were service activities, and do not encode a supervised pair as a single resource such as `"Amal+Celine"`.

Several API semantics matter:

**Timing.** `get_service_time` needs meaningful start and completion timestamps. Its default start key is the completion timestamp key, which does not recover unobserved service duration. Recorded start–complete spans also need interpretation when work contains pauses. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][2])

**Case waiting.** `insert_case_service_waiting_time` provides case-level timing enrichment, but its result must match the log’s execution semantics. For parallel or jointly performed work, do not equate summed person-hours with elapsed case service time; use interval-union or explicit state-based calculations where appropriate. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][3])

**Process and batch discovery.** Performance DFGs expose elapsed time along observed activity succession. Completion-only gaps are not pure queue waiting. `discover_batches` detects temporal patterns; it does not establish that batching was authorized or beneficial. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][4])

**Filtering.** Use `filter_event_attribute_values(..., level="case")` to retain whole cases matching an attribute condition. Event-level filtering can remove intermediate events and create misleading paths. `filter_case_performance` filters first-to-last observed duration, not case-specific SLA compliance. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][5])

The following Python sketch accepts real execution data; it does not fabricate a historical log from the queue snapshot.

```python
from typing import Any
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE = "case:concept:name"
ACT = "concept:name"
END = "time:timestamp"
RES = "org:resource"
START = "start_timestamp"  # Additional, validated activity-start field.


def process_audit_views(
    executions: pd.DataFrame,
    closed_case_ids: set[str],
    *,
    long_case_hours: float,
    intervals_verified: bool = False,
) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """
    Input contract:
      - One row per genuine completed execution/activity instance.
      - Assignment/administrative events are excluded.
      - Each row has one actual executing resource.
      - Timestamps have validated timezone semantics.
      - closed_case_ids comes from an authoritative outcome registry.
      - Known duplicate events and unresolved ordering problems are
        quarantined upstream, not arbitrarily ordered here.

    Outputs are descriptive views, not a complete policy-conformance verdict.
    """
    required = {CASE, ACT, END, RES}
    missing = required - set(executions.columns)
    if missing:
        raise ValueError(f"Missing columns: {sorted(missing)}")
    if long_case_hours <= 0:
        raise ValueError("long_case_hours must be positive.")

    d = executions.copy()
    if d.empty:
        return {"notes": ["No execution records; no indicators computed."]}
    if d[list(required)].isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError("Quarantine records with missing core attributes.")

    d[END] = pd.to_datetime(d[END], utc=True, errors="raise")
    d = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        d, case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=END
    )

    # Intentionally completion-only: these DFG timings are completion gaps.
    points = d.drop(columns=[START], errors="ignore").copy()
    out: dict[str, Any] = {"notes": []}

    out["handover_network"] = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
        points, beta=0
    )
    out["working_together_network"] = (
        pm4py.discover_working_together_network(points)
    )
    out["activity_similarity"] = (
        pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(points)
    )
    out["discovered_roles"] = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(points)

    out["performance_dfg"] = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
        points, business_hours=False, perf_aggregation_key="median"
    )

    # Whole-case cohort: this means "ever observed high/urgent."
    # For "priority at assignment", select cases through the decision ledger.
    if "case_priority" in points.columns:
        out["ever_high_priority_cases"] = (
            pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
                points, "case_priority", ["urgent", "high"],
                level="case", retain=True
            )
        )

    closed = points[points[CASE].isin(closed_case_ids)].copy()
    if not closed.empty:
        out["closed_case_variants"] = pm4py.get_variants(closed)
        out["long_observed_cases"] = pm4py.filter_case_performance(
            closed,
            min_performance=long_case_hours * 3600,
            max_performance=float("inf"),
        )

    if intervals_verified:
        if START not in d.columns:
            raise ValueError("Verified intervals require start_timestamp.")

        d[START] = pd.to_datetime(d[START], utc=True, errors="raise")
        if d[START].isna().any() or (d[START] > d[END]).any():
            raise ValueError("Invalid activity intervals.")

        out["median_recorded_activity_span"] = pm4py.get_service_time(
            d, aggregation_measure="median",
            start_timestamp_key=START, timestamp_key=END
        )

        # Conservative guard for straightforward case-time decomposition.
        c = d[d[CASE].isin(closed_case_ids)].copy()
        c = c.sort_values([CASE, START, END])
        if not c.empty:
            running_end = c.groupby(CASE)[END].cummax()
            previous_end = running_end.groupby(c[CASE]).shift()
            overlaps = c[START] < previous_end

            if not overlaps.any():
                out["case_times"] = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
                    c, start_timestamp_key=START, timestamp_key=END
                )
            else:
                out["notes"].append(
                    "Overlapping case work: use interval/state-based "
                    "case timing; do not treat person-hours as elapsed time."
                )
        batch_input = d
    else:
        out["notes"].append(
            "Service/waiting decomposition unavailable without verified "
            "start-complete intervals."
        )
        batch_input = points

    # Include ALL Quality executions so unauthorized batches are not hidden.
    quality = batch_input[batch_input[ACT].eq("Quality Check")].copy()
    out["quality_batch_candidates"] = (
        pm4py.discover_batches(
            quality, merge_distance=15 * 60, min_batch_size=2
        )
        if len(quality) >= 2 else []
    )
    # Fifteen minutes is a detection parameter, not a permitted holding time.
    # Join detected candidates to assignment/batch-start records to audit
    # priority, deadline, actual grouping, and assignment-count compliance.

    return out
```

Role discovery and activity-profile similarity can suggest possible backup or mentoring relationships. They must **not** replace the authorized skill matrix: frequent activity execution or a similar profile is not evidence of permission or competence. Network views can be shown with `pm4py.view_sna(...)` or saved using `pm4py.save_vis_sna(network, "handover.html")`. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][1])

Complete the audit by joining the assignment ledger to **effective-dated** skill, training-priority, overtime, and calendar records. Replay the policy version applicable at each decision against its original candidate set and workload snapshot. Compare actual execution and supervision with the accepted plan; do not infer compliance from the text of `assignment_reason`.

To learn timing models, use activity, complexity, skill level, supervision arrangement, prior loops, and workload **known at assignment time**. Estimate uncertainty and use broader activity/skill-group estimates when resource-specific samples are sparse. Keep later outcomes out of the input features, validate on later time periods, and reassess after skill changes.

The deployment criterion should be a balanced result: **acceptable safety and SLA performance, sustainable effort, controlled ownership changes, and demonstrated learning—not simply the largest number of assignments or the shortest average wait.**
