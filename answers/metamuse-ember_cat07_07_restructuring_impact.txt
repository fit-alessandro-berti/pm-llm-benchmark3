# 1. Mechanism map

The restructuring does not simply remove handovers. It moves work, waiting and accountability from local loops to a central queue. Several effects are therefore artefacts of logging and aggregation, not necessarily reductions in real work.

| Restructuring action | Mechanism | Observable process-mining effect | Counter-mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Merge N_Tech, S_Tech, E_Tech into Technical Shared Service (TSS) | Pooling of scarce capacity | **Nominal handover reduction:** `discover_handover_of_work_network` shows fewer distinct resource-to-resource edges if TSS is logged as one resource. Internal triage, assignment and peer review inside the pool become invisible self-loops. | Waiting moves from between resources to *inside* an activity. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` / `get_service_time` will show rising waiting for Specialist Review even if handover count falls. |
| Centralize Specialist Review, Legal Review, Risk Review and difficult Quality Check in one queue | **Central queue formation** plus task bundling | Longer service-waiting for Specialist Review, performance DFG bottleneck on that activity, more consecutive Specialist/Legal/Risk activities per case. | Local specialists previously absorbed triage and informal advice. That work now enters the formal queue, increasing arrivals from 9 to 12 per 100 for Legal/Risk. |
| Mandatory routing: all high-complexity and all legal/risk/security to shared queue | Adverse selection into the bottleneck | Variant share with Specialist Review rises; filter by `case_complexity` and `required_specialty` shows disproportionate waits for exactly those cases. | Loss of early informal filtering: cases that would have been resolved locally are formally referred. |
| Remove one local supervisor, create shared approval queue | Standardisation, diffusion of ownership | `discover_subcontracting_network` and `discover_organizational_roles` show a generic approver role, reduced Supervisor Approval variant, increased caseworker  shared approval edges (42  49 per 100). | No named owner for rework, exceptions, or Close. Rework loops become caseworker  TSS  approval  caseworker with no local arbiter. |
| Keep Intake / simple Eligibility local, pull advice from shared queue | Local front-end, remote expertise | Working-together network within North/South/East decays; activity-based resource similarity between caseworkers and techs falls. | Case notes remain inconsistent across locations (current cross-team problem: 6 per 100, 8.9h wait, 18% rework). Central advice without local context increases clarification loops. |

Specific mechanisms:

- **Nominal handover reduction:** Management counts 36 local tech referrals + 31 returns as replaced by 41 TSS referrals + 36 returns. This is not a reduction in legs; it is a relabelling. If TSS members share one `org:resource` value, PM4Py will under-count handovers within the pool and over-state standardisation. Real work and queueing must be measured by waiting time, reassignments (`reassignment_count`), and service time, not edge counts.
- **Central queue formation:** Legal is at 93% and Risk at 91% utilisation. Queueing delay is non-linear above ~85% utilisation. Adding triage, broader detection (9  12 per 100, +33%) and difficult Quality Check to the same pool risks utilisation >100%, backlog ageing, and priority inversion where high-complexity cases - the stated target - wait longest.
- **Specialist utilization:** Pooling North (71%) and South (68%) with East (82%) can balance load and help East. A management goal of +15% utilisation, however, treats high utilisation as efficiency. For scarce specialties it removes slack for absence, peaks, and learning. Utilisation must be read with waiting time, not alone.
- **Rework:** Standardisation may reduce rework for simple, rules-based cases. Loss of informal clarification, regional conventions and language/accommodation knowledge increases rework for complex cases. Early signal is not final `rework_flag` but rising returns from TSS/Approval, `get_rework_cases_per_activity` for Rework, and Quality Check failures.
- **Local expertise loss:** `discover_working_together_network` should show thinning North-North and South-South loops; `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` will show caseworkers becoming less similar to former local techs. Local techs may lose case context, caseworkers lose technical exposure.
- **Role clarity:** `discover_organizational_roles` (Pink-type clustering) will likely erase the local Supervisor role and create a large, heterogeneous TSS cluster. Subcontracting network will show caseworkers subcontracting to a pool, not a person. Accountability for outcome, rework and customer contact becomes ambiguous.
- **Resilience to absence:** Pooling helps if members are cross-trained and notes are standardised. It harms resilience if it creates a single point of failure, removes redundant local cover, and cross-team cover already fails due to inconsistent notes. Measure: resources per activity, concentration of cases per resource, handovers after absence flags.
- **Fairness across teams/customer segments:** A single FIFO queue can starve teams with older backlogs (East: median 8.6d, p90 22.8d) or penalise segments with longer service needs. North/South may lose the local advantage that produced 4.3/4.1 satisfaction vs East 3.8. Fairness must be segmented by `team`, `customer_segment`, `priority`, `case_complexity`, `required_specialty`, and accommodation needs - not averaged.

# 2. Scenario forecasts

## Optimistic scenario - pooling works, Legal/Risk ring-fenced

Conditions: TSS staffed at pooled FTE, Legal/Risk not absorbed, named local liaison retained, notes standardised before go-live, priority queue for high-complexity.

- **Handover pattern:** Handover network retains a local loop via named liaison, but formal TSS edges stabilise at ~38-41 per 100. Cross-team handovers fall 6  3 per 100. Subcontracting becomes more predictable, not diffuse.
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** Service waiting for Specialist Review median ~4h as estimated, p90 <12h. Legal/Risk wait stays ~9-10h because FTE added.
- **Cycle-time distribution:** Median improves modestly system-wide, p90 improves most in East (22.8d  ~17-18d). North/South median unchanged; distribution narrows.
- **Rework and quality pattern:** Rework falls 11.5%  ~9% in East, stable ~8% North/South. Quality Check pass rate improves due to better routing.
- **Role clarity and accountability:** Named case owner and named TSS liaison per case preserve accountability. Roles discovered remain distinct for caseworker, tech, approver.
- **Fairness implications:** East gains without North/South losing; gaps by team and segment narrow.
- **Early warning signs:** Absence of these signs: waiting p90 flat, reassignments stable, working-together within teams not collapsing, TSS utilisation 75-85% with slack.

## Central scenario - most likely given current data

Conditions: Neutral headcount, Legal/Risk triage absorbed by TSS, notes not fully standardised, supervisor removed but shared approval rules immature.

- **Handover pattern:** Nominal local edges fall, but total legs per case do not. More Specialist Review  Legal/Risk  Quality Check sequences, more returns to caseworker, rising `reassignment_count`.
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** TSS wait median 6-8h (vs 4.0h estimated), p90 18-24h. Legal/Risk wait 14-18h median. Waiting is hidden as service waiting, not handover wait, so management dashboard based on handover network alone looks fine.
- **Cycle-time distribution:** System median flat or slightly better because simple cases flow faster. p90 and p95 worsen, driven by high-complexity and legal/risk cases. East improves in median but tail remains long; North/South deteriorate in p90 from 16.4d/18.2d upward.
- **Rework and quality pattern:** Rework bifurcates: down for low-complexity, up 3-5pp for high-complexity, regional and accommodation cases. Cross-team cover rework remains ~18% pattern.
- **Role clarity and accountability:** Staff report unclear ownership of rework and approval delays. `discover_organizational_roles` shows merged, unstable roles; supervisor cluster disappears before shared approver cluster stabilises.
- **Fairness implications:** East benefits relative to baseline, North/South lose local expertise benefit. Within teams, high-priority or legal/risk segments experience disproportionate delay; segments needing language/regional accommodation face extra handovers.
- **Early warning signs:** Within 2-4 weeks: service waiting p90 rising while median cycle flat, Legal/Risk queue age >48h growing, reassignment_count 2 share rising, variant share for high-complexity cases growing, informal/shadow advice re-appearing in interviews.

## Adverse scenario - central bottleneck and deskilling

Conditions: Headcount cut in expectation of +15% utilisation, mandatory routing with no local triage, Legal/Risk fully centralised at >95% utilisation, supervisor removed early.

- **Handover pattern:** Apparent handover reduction, real variant explosion: loops Specialist Review  Rework  Specialist Review, Approval  Implementation  Rework, and ad hoc cross-team cover returns. Performance DFG shows high-frequency rework edges.
- **Queue/waiting-time pattern:** Queue explodes non-linearly. Specialist Review wait median >12h, p90 >2 days; Legal/Risk backlog ages in weeks. Cases are parked in queue while median is protected by fast-tracking simple cases.
- **Cycle-time distribution:** Bimodal distribution: simple cases faster, complex cases much slower. Overall median may still improve (Simpson's paradox from case-mix). p90/p95 and SLA misses rise sharply, especially East's already vulnerable tail and North/South high-complexity.
- **Rework and quality pattern:** Rework up across complex cases, Quality Check failures up, Close outcome worsens. Local caseworkers, stripped of hard cases, make weaker initial assessments, increasing referrals further - a reinforcing loop.
- **Role clarity and accountability:** Diffuse ownership: shared approval, shared specialist, shared queue - no one owns end-to-end. Subcontracting network shows many-to-many dependencies; staff stop developing technical skill.
- **Fairness implications:** Teams/segments with articulate, standard cases win the queue; complex, regional, non-standard, or accommodation cases are systematically delayed. Fairness gaps by `customer_segment` and `team` widen even if average improves.
- **Early warning signs:** First week: backlog count and age rising, utilisation >90% sustained, service time per specialist falling (rushed reviews) while waiting rises, caseworker  TSS referrals per 100 climbing beyond 41, staff feedback of shadow work and unclear ownership.

# 3. Leading and lagging indicators

Leading indicators are available in days/weeks from timestamps and assignments, before outcomes close.

| Horizon | Indicator | Process-mining measure | Segmentation / interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leading | Service waiting for Specialist Review, Legal/Risk Review | `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, median/p90 waiting per activity | By team, complexity, specialty, segment. Rising p90 while median flat = central queue forming. |
| Leading | Handover wait TSS  caseworker, caseworker  Legal/Risk,  shared approval | Consecutive-event timestamp differences grouped by sourcetarget activity/resource; compare to table (3.2h local, 9.6h Legal) | Expected 4.0h / 10.5h / 3.0h. Exceedance for 2 weeks = bottleneck. |
| Leading | Queue age / backlog proxy | Cases started Specialist Review not yet completed >24/48h; cases assigned to TSS not started | By complexity/specialty. High-complexity queue age is guardrail. |
| Leading | Specialist utilisation and concentration | `get_service_time` per resource, cases/day per resource, distinct resources per activity, share of cases per top resource | Utilisation >90% for Legal/Risk or >85% TSS sustained = fragile, not efficient. |
| Leading | Reassignments and clarification loops | Mean `reassignment_count`, share 2; return edges TSScaseworker per 100 | Rising reassignments precede rework. |
| Leading | Handover / subcontracting change | `discover_handover_of_work_network`, `discover_subcontracting_network` frequency per 100 | Nominal fall without wait fall = pooling artefact. |
| Leading | Early variant shift | `get_variants`, `filter_variants` share of high-complexity and legal/risk variants | Variant growth = exception handling burden. |
| Leading | Working-together decay | `discover_working_together_network` edge weights within North/South | Rapid decay = local expertise loss in progress. |

Lagging indicators require case closure and outcome accumulation.

| Horizon | Indicator | Process-mining measure | Segmentation / interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lagging | Cycle time distribution | `extract_temporal_features_dataframe`, median, p90, p95, p99 per case | Never median alone. Compare ratios p90/median by team. |
| Lagging | Performance DFG bottleneck | `discover_performance_dfg` median service and waiting per activity/edge | Identifies true bottleneck activity after queue stabilises. |
| Lagging | Rework and quality | `get_rework_cases_per_activity`, `rework_flag` rate, Quality Check fail rate, `outcome` | By complexity, specialty, segment, team. |
| Lagging | SLA misses | Duration per case vs SLA threshold, joined to `outcome`; SLA miss rate by segment | Fairness in SLA attainment, not just speed. |
| Lagging | Role and learning change | `discover_organizational_roles`, `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` over months | Loss of local role clusters, deskilling, reduced resource diversity per activity. |
| Lagging | Fairness gaps | Difference in median/p90 cycle, SLA miss, rework between teams, segments, priority groups | Pre-specify gap thresholds; East vs North/South must be reported separately. |
| Lagging | Resilience / absence impact | Cycle and waiting in weeks with absence of key TSS/legal resources; cases per resource concentration | Tests single-point-of-failure. |

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1. **Tail latency:** East already shows median 8.6d vs p90 22.8d. A pooled queue can improve median by processing simple cases quickly while p95/p99 cases wait in Specialist/Legal queues for weeks. Average and median hide this; p90/p95/p99 by segment reveal it.
2. **High-complexity adverse queue:** Mandatory routing sends all high-complexity cases to the same central queue. If capacity is fixed, these cases - with longest service times - create the longest waits, precisely where delay costs most. Filtering by `case_complexity` shows divergence invisible in overall average.
3. **Local knowledge loss:** Regional conventions, customer history, language/accommodation needs and inconsistent notes are context. Central advice stripped of this context generates clarification, rework and cross-team handovers that appear later and may be attributed to caseworkers, not the redesign.
4. **Fairness across teams and customer segments:** East may improve while North/South decline; standard-language, standard cases may improve while accommodation cases decline. Aggregated averages mask redistribution of delay. Segmented SLA, rework and satisfaction gaps are required.
5. **Learning and deskilling:** If all hard cases are routed away, caseworkers never develop technical skill, local techs never develop case ownership. Resource similarity and working-together networks will show narrowing over months, long after early cycle time looks stable. Future resilience and quality decline.
6. **Accountability and role clarity diffusion:** Shared approval and shared specialist queues mean no named owner for delay, rework or customer contact. Even if process completes, `discover_organizational_roles` and subcontracting patterns show unclear ownership, increasing escalation and blame-shifting.
7. **Exception handling and variant explosion:** Averages are dominated by the happy path. Exceptions - security, legal, regional, priority - generate rare variants with many loops. `get_variants` and performance DFG frequency for Rework/Quality Check reveal a long tail of variants that average cycle time hides.
8. **Resilience and shadow work:** Removing local redundancy and pushing utilisation to >90% removes slack for absence. Staff may create off-log informal advice, local shadow queues, or workarounds. Event logs then show cleaner process while real work is hidden, understating handovers and over-stating standardisation. Staff feedback and shadow-work audits are needed to detect this.

# 5. Pilot design

## Pilot scope
Pilot **technical pooling only** first, in East, where E_Tech is at 82% and p90 is worst. Do **not** pilot Legal/Risk/Security centralisation or supervisor removal in phase 1 - those are separate mechanisms with capacity risk at 93%/91% utilisation. Retain named local liaison and named accountable supervisor in pilot. Log TSS members with preserved individual IDs (e.g. TSS_E_Tech) to avoid pooling artefact. Standardise case notes and referral template before go-live.

## Comparison group
South as concurrent control (stable local model, similar size to North, not undergoing change in phase 1). North as staggered second site if gate passes. Use difference-in-differences accounting for case-mix, not simple before/after. Within East, also track eligible cases that remain local during transition excluded period.

## Pre/post observation window
12 weeks baseline pre (approx 600+ East cases, 900+ South cases at current volumes), 2-week transition excluded, 12 weeks post, with follow-up to case closure to avoid censoring bias. Analyse cases started in window and closed by cut-off, plus backlog age at cut-off.

## Segmentation by team, complexity, specialty, and customer segment
Pre-specify all reporting by: `team` (East vs South vs North), `case_complexity` (low/medium/high), `required_specialty` (technical/legal/risk/security/none), `customer_segment`, `priority`, `location`. Report East separately; do not pool North/South/East into a single average for go/no-go.

## Guardrail metrics
Pre-registered thresholds, reviewed weekly:
- Specialist Review service waiting p90 by complexity
- Legal/Risk waiting p90 (must not worsen even if not centralised, due to spillover)
- p90/p95 cycle time and SLA miss rate for high-complexity cases
- Rework rate and Quality Check fail rate by segment
- `reassignment_count` 2 share
- TSS utilisation and backlog age >48h count
- Fairness gap in p90 cycle / SLA miss between East and South, and between customer segments
- Cross-team handover frequency and rework after handover

## Staff feedback collection
Weekly 15-minute diary for caseworkers, TSS, approvers: informal advice given/received, unclear ownership episodes, shadow work, note quality. Monthly interviews and role-clarity survey. Log off-system handovers explicitly so PM4Py analysis can be corrected. Include customers needing accommodation in feedback sampling.

## Rollback or adjustment triggers
Rollback or pause and add capacity / restore local triage if any guardrail breaches for 2 consecutive weeks, e.g.:
- High-complexity p90 service waiting rises >20% vs baseline or >18h median
- Legal/Risk p90 wait >12h or backlog age rising despite no centralisation
- High-complexity SLA miss rises >3pp or rework rises >3pp
- Fairness gap (p90 cycle, SLA miss) between segments/teams widens >15%
- TSS utilisation >90% sustained or single-resource concentration >40% of cases
- Staff report systematic loss of accountability or shadow queues become dominant
Adjustment triggers (add FTE, ring-fence Legal/Risk, restore supervisor) precede full rollback for single-metric breaches.

# 6. Recommendation

**Modify and pilot first. Do not proceed to full rollout, and do not reject the idea outright.**

The technical pooling mechanism is plausible for East, where E_Tech overload (82%, 8.6d median, 22.8d p90, 11.5% rework) suggests local capacity failure. Pooling North/South slack could help East without harming others *if* liaison, notes, and accountability are preserved.

The other two mechanisms are not justified on current evidence: centralising Legal/Risk/Security at 91-93% utilisation with +33% arrivals, and removing local supervisors for a shared approval queue, create foreseeable queueing and accountability risks. These should be deferred until capacity, priority rules, and named ownership are designed and separately piloted.

Recommendation is conditional on pilot gates in Section 5: proceed to North/South technical pooling only if East improves on p90 and high-complexity guardrails without fairness widening, rework rising, or utilisation exceeding safe levels. If central-scenario warning signs appear, modify - add capacity, restore local triage, keep named supervisor - rather than expand.

Management should **not** infer from early averages that:
- Stable median cycle time means no bottleneck; check p90/p95, service waiting, and backlog age, especially for high-complexity.
- Fewer counted handovers means less work; pooling into one resource ID and moving waiting inside activities hides work and queueing.
- Higher specialist utilisation means higher efficiency; above ~85-90% for scarce specialties it predicts delay, rushed reviews, and fragility.
- East improvement means system improvement; North/South may simultaneously lose local expertise benefits, masking redistribution.
- Low early rework means quality is preserved; rework lags closure and learning/deskilling effects take months.
- Standardised shared approval means clearer decisions; without named ownership, standardisation can coincide with diffused accountability.

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py import format_dataframe, discover_handover_of_work_network
from pm4py import discover_working_together_network, discover_subcontracting_network
from pm4py import discover_activity_based_resource_similarity, discover_organizational_roles
from pm4py import discover_dfg, discover_performance_dfg
from pm4py import insert_case_service_waiting_time, get_service_time
from pm4py import filter_event_attribute_values, get_variants, filter_variants
from pm4py import get_rework_cases_per_activity, extract_temporal_features_dataframe
import pandas as pd

# Preserve individual TSS member IDs in log, e.g. TSS_N_Tech, to avoid pooling artefact.
# Columns expected: case:concept:name, concept:name, time:timestamp, org:resource,
# team, location, required_specialty, priority, case_complexity, customer_segment,
# handover_reason, reassignment_count, local_expertise_flag, outcome, rework_flag

def load(log_path):
    df_raw = pd.read_csv(log_path)
    df = format_dataframe(df_raw, case_id="case:concept:name",
                          activity_key="concept:name",
                          timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
                          resource_key="org:resource")
    return df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])

def handover_wait(df):
    # Consecutive events per case: wait attributed to source->target activity/resource
    df = df.copy()
    df["next_time"] = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].shift(-1)
    df["next_activity"] = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["concept:name"].shift(-1)
    df["next_resource"] = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["org:resource"].shift(-1)
    df["handover_wait_h"] = (df["next_time"] - df["time:timestamp"]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    return df.groupby(["concept:name", "next_activity", "org:resource", "next_resource"])["handover_wait_h"].agg(["count", "median", lambda s: s.quantile(0.9)])

def compare(before, after, label):
    print(label)
    print("Handover network:", discover_handover_of_work_network(before), discover_handover_of_work_network(after))
    print("Working together:", discover_working_together_network(before), discover_working_together_network(after))
    print("Subcontracting:", discover_subcontracting_network(before), discover_subcontracting_network(after))
    print("Resource similarity:", discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(before), discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(after))
    print("Roles:", discover_organizational_roles(before), discover_organizational_roles(after))
    print("DFG:", discover_dfg(before), discover_dfg(after))
    print("Performance DFG:", discover_performance_dfg(before, variant="case"), discover_performance_dfg(after, variant="case"))
    print("Service time:", get_service_time(before), get_service_time(after))
    print("Variants:", get_variants(before), get_variants(after))
    print("Rework:", get_rework_cases_per_activity(before, activity="Rework"), get_rework_cases_per_activity(after, activity="Rework"))
    print("Handover waits:", handover_wait(before), handover_wait(after))

before = load("before.csv")
after = load("after.csv")

# Service / waiting inside activities - reveals central queue hidden by handover counts
before_w = insert_case_service_waiting_time(before, resource_key="org:resource", timestamp_key="time:timestamp")
after_w = insert_case_service_waiting_time(after, resource_key="org:resource", timestamp_key="time:timestamp")

# Temporal features for cycle-time distribution and SLA
before_t = extract_temporal_features_dataframe(before)
after_t = extract_temporal_features_dataframe(after)

# Segmentation: team, complexity, specialty, customer_segment, priority
for team in ["East", "South", "North"]:
    b = filter_event_attribute_values(before, "team", [team])
    a = filter_event_attribute_values(after, "team", [team])
    compare(b, a, f"Team {team}")

for comp in ["high", "medium", "low"]:  # adapt to actual values in case_complexity
    b = filter_event_attribute_values(before, "case_complexity", [comp])
    a = filter_event_attribute_values(after, "case_complexity", [comp])
    compare(b, a, f"Complexity {comp}")

for spec in ["legal", "risk", "security", "technical"]:
    b = filter_event_attribute_values(before, "required_specialty", [spec])
    a = filter_event_attribute_values(after, "required_specialty", [spec])
    compare(b, a, f"Specialty {spec}")

for seg in sorted(before["customer_segment"].dropna().unique()):
    b = filter_event_attribute_values(before, "customer_segment", [seg])
    a = filter_event_attribute_values(after, "customer_segment", [seg])
    compare(b, a, f"Segment {seg}")

# Variant filtering for happy path vs exception paths
top_variants = get_variants(before)
before_happy = filter_variants(before, top_variants[0][0])
after_happy = filter_variants(after, top_variants[0][0])

# SLA misses: duration per case vs threshold, by outcome and segment
def sla_by_segment(df_temporal, df_raw):
    cases = df_temporal.groupby("case:concept:name").agg(duration=("case_duration", "max") if "case_duration" in df_temporal else ("duration","max"))
    meta = df_raw.drop_duplicates("case:concept:name").set_index("case:concept:name")
    joined = cases.join(meta[["team","case_complexity","required_specialty","customer_segment","priority","outcome","rework_flag"]])
    joined["sla_miss"] = joined["duration"] > pd.Timedelta(days=14)  # replace with actual SLA
    return joined.groupby(["team","case_complexity","customer_segment"])["sla_miss"].mean()

print(sla_by_segment(before_t, before))
print(sla_by_segment(after_t, after))
```

Interpretation rule in code review: do not report handover network counts alone. Report together: handover network, performance DFG waiting, service waiting after `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, p90/p95 cycle from temporal features, rework per activity, and segmented SLA - for East vs South, high vs low complexity, legal/risk/security vs technical, and each customer segment.
