### 1. Stress scenarios

To ensure the diagnostic-lab process remains robust, we evaluate it across four distinct stress scenarios that combine demand mix shifts with operational disruptions. These scenarios expose vulnerabilities that historical averages mask.

*   **Scenario A: The Winter Infection Wave (Volume Surge + Urgent/Complex Mix Shift + Courier Compression)**
    *   *Parameters:* Total volume increases by ~50%, but the case mix shifts heavily toward Urgent (29%) and Complex-panel (20%). Routine drops to 42%. Simultaneously, courier arrival compression creates massive intake spikes at 10:00 and 17:00.
    *   *System Impact:* Registration clerks, pre-analytical check, and assay machines are flooded. Urgent priority rules fragment machine schedules, and Complex-panel cases choke medical validation and specialist review.
*   **Scenario B: Equipment Failure & M2 Degradation (Capacity Loss + Quality Decay)**
    *   *Parameters:* M2 (the faster but less stable assay machine) suffers a 40% capacity degradation due to unprogrammed outages, forcing excess workload onto M1 or causing high-volume batch queues. 
    *   *System Impact:* Test preparation benches become choked with unassayed batches. Repeat-after-failure cases spike (clustering after night shifts and M2 usage), consuming assay capacity twice and triggering a cascading failure into technical and medical validation.
*   **Scenario C: Specialist & Medical Validator Absence (Downstream Constraint)**
    *   *Parameters:* Simultaneous absence of specialist-review personnel (compounded by the regular alternating Friday absence) and medical validators during a high-Urgent period.
    *   *System Impact:* Technical validation clears work, but cases bottleneck at Medical validation (peak median queue reaching 22h+) and Specialist review (peak median queue hitting 36h+). Work-in-process (WIP) backs up into technical validation, halting release mechanisms and jeopardizing contractual 18-hour Urgent SLAs.
*   **Scenario D: Upstream Data & Quality Failure (Pre-Analytical Cascades)**
    *   *Parameters:* A high influx of poorly labeled samples or incomplete digital metadata, increasing manual accession correction needs and pre-analytical defects.
    *   *System Impact:* Pre-analytical check failure rates surge. Accession correction specialists (only 2 available) face massive queues (peak median 14h+). Recollection loops multiply, reducing available intake capacity as clerks spend excessive time explaining errors to physicians.

### 2. Bottleneck-migration analysis

Analyzing the DFG, queue metrics, variants, and performance edges reveals that bottlenecks do not remain static; they migrate dynamically through the value stream as interventions are applied.

*   **From Registration to Pre-Analytical Check:** 
    *   *Mechanism:* If we automate registration (I8) or add overtime for clerks (raising utilization from 85% to unconstrained levels), we push raw case volume faster into the pre-analytical check. 
    *   *Downstream Overload:* Pre-analytical utilization jumps from 96% toward gridlock (median queue >9.5h). Defective samples bypass basic checks or create massive recollection loops (`Pre-analytical check -> Request recollection`: 780 peak cases), starving downstream test preparation of clean work.
*   **From Test Preparation to Assay Machines (M1/M2):**
    *   *Mechanism:* If we implement strict Urgent-first dispatch (I1) or increase batch sizes for routine samples to gain preparation efficiency (I2), preparation benches clear faster.
    *   *Downstream Overload:* This uncoordinated release floods Assay machines M1/M2 (peak utilization already at 98%). Bypassing batch waits for Urgent cases (58% of peak) fragments machine schedules. Consequently, batch waits for Routine cases balloon (`Batch wait -> Assay run` median jumps to 22h), and repeat assays surge due to rushed, suboptimal runs.
*   **From Assay Run to Technical & Medical Validation:**
    *   *Mechanism:* If we dedicate M1 to critical cases and M2 to routine cases (I3) to protect Urgent SLAs, or clear assay backlogs via expanded shifts, assay output accelerates.
    *   *Downstream Overload:* Technical validators (peak utilization 101%) and Medical validators (peak utilization 93%) are instantly overwhelmed. In Scenario C, cases stuck in `Technical validation -> Hold for specialist review` (median 48h in peaks) halt downstream release. Relieving technical validation via cross-training (I4) simply shifts the gridlock to medical validators and specialist review, where complex panels require multidisciplinary sign-off.
*   **The Recollection Loop Trap:**
    *   *Mechanism:* Interventions that rush sample preparation increase pre-analytical error detection.
    *   *Downstream Overload:* The DFG shows `Pre-analytical check -> Request recollection` leads to a 42-to-60-hour cycle time penalty before samples re-enter the system. Clerks consumed by physician communication regarding re-collections lose registration capacity, creating an invisible upstream capacity deficit.

### 3. Robust redesign package

To stabilize the diagnostic process without relying on fragile historical averages or adding unsustainable capacity everywhere, we propose a robust redesign package built on three structural pillars:

1.  **Early Defect Triage & Digital Gatekeeping (Combining I6 & I8):**
    *   *Action:* Implement an automated digital metadata registration gate combined with a strict, early 2-hour pre-analytical defect triage at sample intake. 
    *   *Why Robust:* It prevents garbage-in, garbage-out dynamics. By filtering out poor samples before they consume preparation and assay capacity, we protect downstream bottlenecks (M1/M2 and validation) from processing doomed iterations.
    *   *Assumptions & Failure Points:* Assumes referring clinics can rapidly fix digital metadata or resubmit samples. If clinics fail to engage, intake queues at the pre-analytical boundary will swell, requiring strict physician-side feedback loops.
2.  **Capped Dynamic Batching with Reserved Urgent Corridors (Mitigating I2 & I3 flaws):**
    *   *Action:* Enforce dynamic batching rules for Routine samples (batch size capped at 16, not 24, to prevent excessive wait times) while establishing a non-negotiable, dedicated hourly mini-batch slot for Urgent and Complex-panel cases on M1. M2 handles routine overflow with mandatory stability checks.
    *   *Why Robust:* It balances preparation efficiency with SLA preservation, preventing Urgent cases from starving Routine cases indefinitely while stopping priority overrides from fracturing machine schedules.
    *   *Assumptions & Failure Points:* Relies on M1 maintaining high uptime. If M1 suffers an outage, the dedicated corridor fails, requiring dynamic fallback policies.
3.  **Cross-Functional Validation & Tiered Specialist Review (Combining I4 & I5):**
    *   *Action:* Cross-train technical validators in accession correction and secondary data checks (I4) and establish a flexible, on-call evening medical-validation shift (I5) specifically triggered during peak volume or specialist absences.
    *   *Why Robust:* It directly addresses the migration of bottlenecks from the laboratory floor to the sign-off desk during infection waves. 
    *   *Assumptions & Failure Points:* Assumes technical validators can pass medical validation competency assessments. If certification bottlenecks occur, cross-training yields limited throughput gains for Complex-panel cases.

### 4. Scenario-specific policies

Static operating rules fail during disruptions. The following policies activate automatically based on real-time threshold triggers:

*   **Policy 1: Winter Wave & Urgent Surge Protocol (Trigger: Urgent queue > 6h OR daily Urgent volume > 35% of total)**
    *   *Activation:* Automatically spin up the temporary evening medical-validation shift (I5). Restrict Routine batching windows to maximum 4-hour holds to prevent Routine median cycle times from blowing past 72 hours.
    *   *Deactivation:* When Urgent queue drops below 3h for 48 consecutive hours.
*   **Scenario B Policy: M2 Assay Machine Degradation Protocol (Trigger: M2 failure rate exceeds baseline by >50% OR assay queue > 12h)**
    *   *Activation:* Cease all routine batching on M2. Route 100% of Urgent and Complex-panel cases exclusively to M1. Divert non-critical Routine overflow to the external partner lab (I10) to preserve internal machine capacity for repeatable assays and prevent repeat-after-failure feedback loops.
    *   *Deactivation:* Upon engineering sign-off confirming M2 stability over a 24-hour run.
*   **Scenario C Policy: Specialist Absence & Review Gridlock Protocol (Trigger: Hold for specialist review queue > 18h OR Friday afternoon staffing deficit)**
    *   *Activation:* Implement triage categorization for Complex-panel cases: route single-anomaly cases to standard medical validators with pre-approved clinical algorithms, reserving true specialist review only for multi-pathology anomalies. Authorize qualified technical validators to clear low-risk hold clearances.
    *   *Deactivation:* When specialist-review queue falls below 8h.
*   **Scenario D Policy: Upstream Data Defect Spike Protocol (Trigger: Manual accession correction queue > 8h)**
    *   *Activation:* Deploy cross-trained technical validators (I4) to assist the 2 accession correction specialists for 4-hour shifts. Simultaneously institute a soft-rejection auto-notification to clinics for metadata errors rather than holding physical tubes in limbo.
    *   *Deactivation:* When accession correction queue normalizes below 3h.

### 6. Residual vulnerabilities

Even with a robust redesign and dynamic policies, structural vulnerabilities remain:

*   **Vulnerability 1: Cross-Training Competency Bottlenecks (Created by Redesign):** 
    *   While cross-training technical validators for accession correction and basic validation (I4) relieves mid-process bottlenecks, it concentrates operational knowledge. If key senior validators fall ill during a winter wave, the system faces an acute cognitive bottleneck where quality checks slow down drastically to maintain patient safety, risking 18-hour Urgent SLAs.
*   **Vulnerability 2: Courier Dependency & External Transport Friction:**
    *   Offloading Complex-panel overflow to a partner lab (I10) introduces a 12-hour transport delay and external tracking dependencies. If winter weather disrupts regional courier routes, samples sent off-site become untraceable black boxes, breaking internal LIMS chain-of-custody metrics.
*   **Vulnerability 3: Physician-Side Latency in Recollection Loops:**
    *   Early pre-analytical defect triage (I6) successfully identifies bad samples within 2 hours, but it offloads the delay to external clinics. If referring physicians take 48 hours to respond to recollection requests, the internal lab's WIP metrics look clean, but true patient-diagnosis cycle times degrade severely without being captured in standard laboratory KPIs.

### 7. Minimum monitoring dashboard

To manage operations proactively during peak winter waves without relying on misleading historical averages, the operations team must monitor the following daily leading indicators alongside lagging outcome metrics.

#### Leading Indicators (Daily Operational Triggers)
1.  **Pre-Analytical Defect & Accession Correction Queue (Hours):**
    *   *Threshold / Warning Band:* Green < 4h | Yellow (Warning) 4h–8h | Red (Action) > 8h.
    *   *Why:* Predicts downstream starvation or sudden surges in recollection loops.
2.  **Assay Machine M1/M2 Queue Depth & Utilization (% / Hours):**
    *   *Threshold / Warning Band:* Green utilization < 85%, queue < 4h | Yellow utilization 85%–95%, queue 4h–10h | Red utilization > 95%, queue > 10h.
    *   *Why:* Early warning of assay gridlock before batch waits blow out Routine SLAs.
3.  **Specialist Review & Medical Validation WIP Count:**
    *   *Threshold / Warning Band:* Green < 20 cases | Yellow 20–40 cases | Red > 40 cases (or queue > 12h).
    *   *Why:* Identifies impending sign-off blockages before Urgent contractual penalties are breached.

#### Lagging Outcome Metrics (Performance Verification)
1.  **Urgent Case SLA Breach Rate (% > 18h):** Target: < 1% of total urgent volume.
2.  **Routine Case p90 Cycle Time:** Target: < 72 hours.
3.  **Overall System Rework & Repeat Rate (%):** Target: Maintained below historical normal average (7.5%), preventing peak surge inflation above 10%.
