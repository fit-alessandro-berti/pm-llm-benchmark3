## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 is not merely a resource-inequality rule. It is intended to reduce several related threats. A distinct preparer and releaser is a proxy for independent challenge, but its effectiveness depends on genuine independence, meaningful review, system integrity, and timing.

| Control objective / threat scenario | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. **Single-person unauthorized payment**: one person creates and authorizes a fraudulent or erroneous transfer | Preventive for covered conditions: duplicate, sanctions, bank ownership/change, limit, PO basics. Does not establish actor identity or intent. | Preventive if independent and before release/settlement, with substantive review. Weak if rubber-stamped or conflicted. | Detective after payment; potentially corrective only before recall/recovery capacity expires. | Allows an exception only if multiple preventive/detective conditions are met; does not eliminate this objective. |
| 2. **Erroneous bank account / account-takeover payment** | Strong preventive evidence when fully operational and passed, especially for bank ownership and recent changes. Warning/override weakens protection. Cannot establish who controlled a compromised account. | Can independently challenge bank details if actually reviewed. | Can detect misdirection after release; recall may be possible within 24 hours. | Requires no bank change in previous seven days and clean AV-2 pass, thereby narrowing exposure. |
| 3. **Duplicate or excessive payment** | Preventive for duplicate invoices and amount against operator limit, subject to configuration and override. | Can identify unusual amount or duplicate indicators if invoice/supporting material is reviewed. | Detects after the event; recovery depends on timing and bank status. | Requires amount below EUR 100,000 and clean AV-2 pass, reducing but not eliminating risk. |
| 4. **Fictitious goods/services or fabricated receipt** | Largely fails to address it. Basic PO matching is not proof of goods/service receipt or commercial reasonableness. | Strong if manager substantively reviews invoice, receipt/service report, business need, and independent operational evidence. | May detect it later, but normally after settlement; recovery uncertain. | Does not itself prove receipt. It relies materially on meaningful MG-4 review. |
| 5. **Collusion between nominally distinct actors** | May catch transaction anomalies, but not collusion where data appears valid. | Only useful if the manager is genuinely independent and not part of the collusive group. | Can detect patterns, but a routine audit may miss sophisticated collusion. | Does not solve collusion by itself; emergency use must still have independent approval and audit. |
| 6. **Compromised account / credential misuse** | Can identify some transaction anomalies but cannot establish who controlled the user account or credential. An override can itself be a compromise vector. | Can challenge the transaction, but cannot prove the true account controller unless identity evidence exists. | Can detect suspicious activity after the fact. | Conditions do not prove that the token holder or operator was the true actor. |
| 7. **Emergency operational continuity without uncontrolled payment risk** | Provides pre-release automated controls where operational. | Provides human independent challenge before release or settlement. | Provides prompt post-event assurance and possible recall. | Defines a narrowly bounded exception: token before preparation, low amount, established vendor, no recent bank change, clean AV-2, independent approval before settlement, and prompt audit. |
| 8. **Failure to stop or recover an erroneous payment once released** | Can prevent release only if it blocks, or an authorized override is not improperly used. | Before settlement, can stop or reverse an as-yet revocable payment instruction. | Detective and partly corrective if audit is timely enough for recall/recovery. | Requires next-business-day audit, but that is not necessarily within 24 hours or before recall expiry in every timing scenario. |

Important implications:

- A clean AV-2 pass is strong only for AV-2’s enumerated checks. It is not evidence of receipt, commercial reasonableness, absence of collusion, or absence of account compromise.
- A manager action after `Release_Payment` but before `Bank_Settlement` can still prevent final loss if the payment remains stoppable; it is weaker than a pre-release approval because the release action has already been authorized by the same operator.
- An audit is not automatically corrective. It becomes partly corrective only where its timing leaves a realistic opportunity to stop, recall, freeze, or recover funds.
- ED-1 is an exception mechanism with cumulative conditions. It is not a waiver of FEP-1’s anti-fraud objective.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating-control package should be evaluated against the control objective, not by simply counting activities or controls. The following test distinguishes full equivalence, partial equivalence, mitigation, and ineffectiveness.

### A. Assessment dimensions

| Dimension | Question | Strong condition | Weak condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | Did the control act before an irreversible commitment? | Before release, or at least before bank settlement with a demonstrably effective stop mechanism. | Ratification after settlement; audit after recall window. |
| **Independence** | Was the reviewer independent of the initiator and releaser? | Separate individual, sufficient authority, no unresolved reporting/conflict relationship, independent credentials. | Direct supervisor with performance-control relationship, shared credentials, shared workstation without controls, or suspected coordination. |
| **Coverage** | Does the package address the material risk mechanism? | Controls address bank, duplicate, amount, receipt/service evidence, commercial rationale, and authority as applicable. | AV-only package used to infer goods receipt or absence of collusion. |
| **Overrideability** | Could a warning/block be bypassed, and was the bypass independently controlled? | Override requires independent authorized approver, documented rationale, and heightened review. | Operator self-overrides; use of another person’s credential; unresolved credential sharing. |
| **Recoverability** | If prevention fails, can the payment still be stopped or recovered? | Review/audit occurs while settlement can be stopped or recall remains viable. | Audit occurs after 24 hours and after settlement, with no evidence of recovery action. |
| **Evidence quality** | Is there reliable evidence that the control actually operated substantively? | Immutable logs, reviewed invoice/bank/receipt evidence, manager attestation with supporting record, system health evidence. | Bare event labels, unsigned checklist, missing review record, ambiguous timestamps. |
| **Collusion resistance** | Does the package resist coordinated circumvention? | Genuine role separation, independent manager challenge, pair-pattern monitoring, credential controls, workstation segregation. | Same recurring pair, shared workstation, manager relationship conflict, no review of underlying evidence. |
| **Exception eligibility** | For emergency use, are all ED-1 predicates met? | All seven conditions evidenced, including token active before preparation. | Token created late, unavailable AV-2, bank changed recently, amount above threshold, or late manager action. |

### B. Classification logic

#### Fully equivalent

A package is **fully equivalent** to FEP-1’s relevant objective where it provides a comparably independent, timely challenge before irreversible loss and covers the material risk pathway.

Normally, that requires:

1. a properly bounded exception or alternative authorization design;
2. AV-2 fully operational and clean for the risks it covers;
3. a genuinely independent, substantive manager review before release or, at minimum, before irrevocable settlement with evidenced power to stop the transfer;
4. adequate review of matters AV-2 does not cover, especially receipt/service evidence and business rationale where material;
5. no unresolved override, credential, or collusion concern;
6. prompt audit within a viable recall/recovery horizon where post-event assurance is part of the package; and
7. reliable evidence of the foregoing.

ED-1 is designed to define one such route, but its stated conditions must actually be met. Even a technically complete ED-1 package may be only partially equivalent where the transaction’s risk mechanism is outside the package’s coverage.

#### Partially equivalent

A package is **partially equivalent** where it materially lowers risk and includes meaningful independent challenge, but has a limitation in timing, scope, evidence, or residual collusion exposure.

Examples:

- independent substantive manager approval occurs after `Release_Payment` but before settlement;
- AV-2 passes and an independent manager reviews documents, but goods receipt remains inherently difficult to validate;
- a timely audit occurs, but only after settlement and its ability to recall is uncertain;
- nominally separate preparer/releaser users exist, but repeated pairing and shared workstation evidence creates unresolved independence concerns.

#### Merely mitigating

A package is **merely mitigating** where controls reduce some loss modes but do not provide a sufficient substitute for independent authorization.

Examples:

- clean AV-2 alone, without independent human challenge, for a same-user completed payment;
- retrospective audit after the recall window;
- after-the-fact manager ratification after settlement;
- an override initiated by the same operator using a supervisor’s credential;
- a manager who is nominally separate but did not review invoice, bank details, and supporting receipt.

#### Ineffective

A package is **ineffective** for the relevant objective where material control elements are absent, unavailable, overridden without independence, too late to matter, unsupported by reliable evidence, or defeated by the same risk it is supposed to control.

Examples:

- AV-2 unavailable, no valid pre-settlement independent approval, and late audit;
- emergency permission invoked despite clear failure of several ED-1 conditions;
- nominal two-person approval where evidence supports coordinated handling and the service evidence is fabricated.

### C. Why “same expected loss” is not the only standard

Expected-loss comparison can inform risk assessment, but it is insufficient as the sole standard because:

- **Fraud deterrence matters.** Genuine segregation changes incentives and raises the difficulty of misconduct, even if observed monetary loss is currently low.
- **Tail risk matters.** Rare, high-impact fraud may have low average expected loss but be unacceptable.
- **Control integrity matters.** A control package relying on shared credentials or conflicted supervision may normalize bypass behavior even when no loss occurs.
- **Evidence and accountability matter.** Strong evidence permits investigation, attribution, remediation, and regulatory defensibility.
- **Risk distribution matters.** A low average loss may conceal concentrated harm to a particular vendor, business unit, or payment type.
- **Operational resilience matters.** Emergency exceptions must permit continuity without becoming an ordinary convenience route.
- **Conduct obligations may be rule-based.** Certain authorization and sanctions requirements may not be reducible to a monetary expected-loss calculation.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control | Primary role | When it is preventive | When it is detective | When it is corrective | Key limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AV-2 | Preventive automated validation | When it blocks release or requires a valid, independently governed response before release | When it emits a warning that is subsequently investigated | Usually not corrective itself; it can enable correction by stopping a transaction before settlement | Limited to specified checks; does not prove receipt, business purpose, non-collusion, or true account control. |
| MG-4 | Independent authorization / challenge | Strongly preventive when substantive and before release | If it reviews a transaction already released but can only identify an issue | Hybrid/corrective if performed before settlement and the manager can stop or reverse the instruction | Independence, evidence reviewed, authority to act, and timing determine effectiveness. |
| RA-7 | Retrospective audit and recall | Not normally preventive | Its core role: detect defects after the transaction | Partly corrective only where audit results arrive soon enough to stop settlement, recall funds, freeze an account, or otherwise recover value | After settlement and recall expiry, it may improve accountability but not recover loss. |
| ED-1 | Controlled exception framework | Preventive only insofar as all predicates force safe conditions before same-user execution | Includes a prompt audit condition that provides detection | Relies on RA-7 for any recovery component | It is not itself an independent reviewer and does not waive the four-eyes objective. |

### Manager action after release but before settlement

A `Manager_Ratify` after `Release_Payment` but before `Bank_Settlement` is best classified as a **hybrid control**:

- **Preventive with respect to final loss** if the manager can genuinely stop, cancel, or reverse the pending payment before settlement.
- **Detective with respect to the already-completed release action** because the release occurred without prior independent authorization.
- **Corrective in a narrow operational sense** if the manager identifies a defect and successfully cancels a released-but-unsettled payment.

The classification should therefore not depend only on the event label “ratify.” It depends on whether the manager actually reviewed substantive materials, was independent, acted before the payment became irrevocable, and possessed effective stop authority.

For this assessment:

- AP-201 and AP-207 have potentially meaningful hybrid manager intervention.
- AP-208’s manager action after settlement is materially detective/ratificatory only; it does not provide meaningful prevention of the settled transfer.
- AP-202’s manager action two days later is too late to be treated as preventive or meaningfully corrective.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

### A. Evidence collection

1. **Preserve authorization and identity evidence**
   - Immutable logs for preparation, release, override, manager approval, cancellation, settlement, and recall attempts.
   - MFA, device, IP/session, credential-vault, and privileged-access records.
   - Explicit evidence of who used an override credential, especially when the credential belongs to another employee.
   - Prohibit shared service accounts from being treated as independent human authorization.

2. **Strengthen manager-review evidence**
   - Require manager attestations identifying documents reviewed: invoice, bank-account evidence, PO, receipt/service report, commercial rationale, and emergency basis.
   - Record whether approval was pre-release, post-release/pre-settlement, or post-settlement.
   - Record whether the manager had actual bank-stop or cancellation authority.

3. **Evidence emergency eligibility**
   - Emergency-token issuance time, issuer, reason, expiry, and system-clock source.
   - Emergency incident records, such as outage ticket and start time.
   - Vendor tenure and bank-change history at the exact time of preparation.
   - Evidence that AV-2 was operational and passed without override.
   - Evidence that the independent manager approval and RA-7 review met required timing.

4. **Bank-account and vendor verification evidence**
   - Bank-account ownership validation result and source.
   - Independent callback evidence for bank changes, especially near payment time.
   - Bank-change requester, approver, and confirmation-channel evidence.
   - Vendor-master change audit trail and linkage screening.

5. **Goods/services evidence**
   - Receiving report, service acceptance, operational confirmation, maintenance record, or other independent delivery evidence.
   - Avoid treating invoice-plus-PO match as proof of service receipt.
   - Preserve the source and author of a receipt; test for fabricated or reused evidence.

6. **Recovery evidence**
   - Settlement timestamp, recall request timestamp, bank response, recovery amount, and recovery status.
   - Distinguish “audit was performed” from “audit identified and stopped/recovered a defect.”

### B. Monitoring rules

These rules should generate **alerts for review**, not conclusions of misconduct.

| Metric / rule | Suggested monitoring logic | Why it matters | Alert is not proof because |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Same-user rate** | Percentage of completed payments where preparer equals releaser; split by normal vs emergency route, business unit, vendor type, and amount band. | Detects FEP-1 exceptions and normalization of convenience bypasses. | A valid ED-1 exception can be legitimate. |
| **ED-1 eligibility failure rate** | Same-user completed payments missing any ED-1 predicate: late token, amount threshold breach, new vendor, recent bank change, AV outage/warning override, late manager approval, late audit. | Identifies high-risk exception misuse. | Source data, timestamp alignment, or emergency workflow logging may be incomplete. |
| **Override rate** | AV warnings/blocks divided by validation events; stratify by operator, manager, vendor, bank change, and reason code. | High override activity can signal weak preventive discipline or fraud risk. | Some warnings may be legitimate and correctly escalated. |
| **Self-override / credential mismatch** | Flag when override actor differs from credential owner, or when preparer performs override intended for manager authorization. | Directly tests override independence and account compromise risk. | Technical delegation may exist, but it must be documented and approved. |
| **Late manager approval** | Flag manager action after release, after settlement, or outside defined pre-settlement stop window. | Distinguishes meaningful prevention from after-the-fact ratification. | A post-release approval may still stop an unsettled payment if stop authority is real. |
| **Audit-before-recall-window rate** | Measure audits completed within 24 hours of release and before practical recall expiry; separately measure next-business-day compliance. | Determines whether RA-7 can be partly corrective rather than merely detective. | Bank recall mechanics vary; timestamps may not show actual recall feasibility. |
| **Repeated resource pairs** | Count recurring preparer/releaser pairs; compare concentration to expected assignment design; flag unusually persistent pairs. | Recurrent pairs can reduce independence and facilitate collusion. | A documented payment cell may legitimately create repeated pairs. |
| **Shared-workstation correlation** | Flag distinct-user approvals from the same workstation, device session, IP, or overlapping session window; especially when paired repeatedly. | Tests whether formal user separation corresponds to real operational separation. | Shared operations rooms and approved jump hosts may be legitimate. |
| **Bank-change proximity** | Flag payments to accounts changed within 7 days, especially within 24 hours, with warning overrides, emergency use, or high value. | Bank changes are a common diversion mechanism and explicitly constrained by ED-1. | Legitimate vendor changes occur and require verification, not automatic rejection. |
| **Control outages** | Monitor validation unavailability, manual fallback use, unsigned checklists, and releases during outage. | Outages can convert automated prevention into unstructured manual risk acceptance. | A documented, independently reviewed fallback can be effective. |
| **Receipt/service anomalies** | Reused receipts, absent receiving reports, manager approvals without evidence review, unusual service vendors, or mismatch between service dates and payment dates. | Addresses a major risk AV-2 does not cover. | Documentation anomalies require investigation; they do not establish fraud alone. |
| **Settlement-after-release exposure** | Measure elapsed time from release to settlement and whether a manager stop action was possible. | Determines whether post-release review can be genuinely preventive/hybrid. | Time alone does not prove a stop mechanism was available. |

### C. Recommended policy refinements

1. **Define “approval before settlement” operationally.** Require evidence that the manager had effective stop authority and that the payment was actually placed on hold or released only after approval where possible.

2. **Make ED-1 fail closed for critical predicates.** A same-user emergency payment should not proceed where:
   - AV-2 is unavailable;
   - bank account changed within seven days;
   - amount is EUR 100,000 or more;
   - no independent manager is available before settlement; or
   - emergency token was not demonstrably active before preparation.

3. **Tighten override governance.**
   - Overrides should be performed by the authorized independent approver, using their own credential.
   - Require a reason code, evidence attachment, and heightened verification for recent bank changes and limit warnings.
   - Alert immediately on delegated/shared credential use.

4. **Require independent service-receipt validation for higher-risk services.**
   - High-value consulting, repair, and intangible-service payments should not rely solely on PO match and invoice/receipt artifacts produced within the same operational chain.

5. **Address shared-workstation risk.**
   - Separate user sessions, device authentication, and role-based access.
   - Rotate pairings or document the operating design of any dedicated two-person payment cell.
   - Periodically test whether releasers can explain and evidence their independent review.

# 2. Parse UTC timestamps and apply PM4Py dataframe formatting.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

# and metadata for PM4Py operations.

    case_id="case:concept:name",

    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",

    ["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "concept:name"]

# exposed against an event log object. Depending on PM4Py version, the exact

# 3. Obtain nominal violating completed-payment cases via PM4Py.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Important version note:

    "Prepare_Payment",

    pm4py_result_df = pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(pm4py_four_eyes_result)
    pm4py_case_ids = sorted(
        pm4py_result_df["case:concept:name"].dropna().unique().tolist()

    pm4py_case_ids = []

# actual Release_Payment are "completed-payment candidates" for FEP-1.

    event_df[event_df["concept:name"].isin(["Prepare_Payment", "Release_Payment"])]
    .pivot_table(
        index="case:concept:name",
        columns="concept:name",
        values="org:resource",
        aggfunc="first",

    completed_cases["Prepare_Payment"] == completed_cases["Release_Payment"]

        completed_cases["nominal_fep1_violation"], "case:concept:name"

    has_attempt = "Attempt_Release" in activity_set
    has_actual_release = "Release_Payment" in activity_set
    return has_block and has_attempt and not has_actual_release

    case_activity_sets["activities"].apply(blocked_attempt)

# Case-level event timestamps and resources.
def first_event_time(case_id, activity):
    x = event_df[
        (event_df["case:concept:name"] == case_id)
        & (event_df["concept:name"] == activity)
    ]["time:timestamp"]
    return x.min() if not x.empty else pd.NaT

    x = event_df[
        (event_df["case:concept:name"] == case_id)
        & (event_df["concept:name"] == activity)

    return x.iloc[0] if not x.empty else pd.NA

    <= summary.loc[has_exact_token_time, "prepare_time"]

summary["ed1_manager_before_settlement_flag"] = pd.Series(

)
has_settlement_and_manager = (

)

    < summary.loc[has_settlement_and_manager, "settlement_time"]

# Conservative ED-1 evidence result:
# all known conditions must be true; unknown does not become "eligible".
ed1_components = [
    "token_before_prepare_flag",

    "ed1_vendor_age_flag",
    "ed1_no_recent_bank_change_flag",
    "ed1_av_clean_flag",
    "manager_independent_flag",
    "audit_within_24h_of_release_flag",
]

summary["ed1_preliminary_eligibility"] = "UNKNOWN"
all_true_known = summary[ed1_components].eq(True).all(axis=1)
any_false = summary[ed1_components].eq(False).any(axis=1)

summary.loc[all_true_known & ~any_false, "ed1_preliminary_eligibility"] = "SUPPORTED"
summary.loc[any_false, "ed1_preliminary_eligibility"] = "NOT_SUPPORTED"

# Add manager-before-settlement only where a settlement is recorded.
summary.loc[
    summary["settlement_time"].notna()
    & summary["ed1_manager_before_settlement_flag"].eq(False),
    "ed1_preliminary_eligibility",

# Repeated pair evidence: count actual completed payments by preparer/releaser.
pair_counts = (
    completed_cases.groupby(["Prepare_Payment", "Release_Payment"])
    .size()
    .rename("pair_count")
    .reset_index()
)

summary = summary.merge(
    pair_counts,
    on=["Prepare_Payment", "Release_Payment"],
    how="left",
)

summary["repeated_pair_flag"] = summary["pair_count"].fillna(0).ge(2)

# The supplied narrative says u12/u13 used a shared workstation. This fact is
# not derivable from the event CSV, so it is represented as a supplied evidence
# flag rather than an inferred fact.
summary["shared_workstation_evidence_flag"] = summary["case:concept:name"].isin(
    ["AP-204", "AP-209"]

# Additional evidence flags for review queues.
summary["high_risk_bank_change_flag"] = (
    summary["bank_changed_within_7d"] & summary["completed_payment"]
)
summary["control_outage_flag"] = summary["av_unavailable_flag"]
summary["same_user_completed_flag"] = (
    summary["completed_payment"]
    & summary["nominal_fep1_violation"].fillna(False)
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
print("\n=== PRELIMINARY CONTROL EVIDENCE ONLY — NOT A FINAL COMPLIANCE VERDICT ===")

print(pm4py_case_ids)

print("\nTransparent pandas nominal same-user completed-payment cases:")
print(pandas_nominal_violators)

print("\nAttempted but blocked releases (not completed payments):")
print(blocked_cases[["case:concept:name"]].to_string(index=False))

evidence_columns = [
    "case:concept:name",

PM4Py-version caution:

- `filter_four_eyes_principle` is called exactly as requested, but PM4Py releases may differ in accepted input object types and in whether a filter returns matching/violating versus retained/conforming traces under a given interface.
- The code therefore converts the formatted dataframe to an event log and also computes a transparent pandas cross-check based on the actual `Prepare_Payment` and `Release_Payment` resources.
- The pandas result should be used to validate interpretation of the PM4Py filter output in the installed version.
- The code deliberately treats AP-205 separately because it contains `Attempt_Release`, not an actual `Release_Payment`; therefore it is not a completed-payment FEP-1 case.
