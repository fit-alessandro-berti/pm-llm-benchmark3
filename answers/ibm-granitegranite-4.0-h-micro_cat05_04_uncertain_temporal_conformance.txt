## 1. Normalization Assumptions

### 1.1 Clock Offset
- **Clock offset** for each system is calculated as `recorded_clock - true_clock`.
- The offset is bounded by the provided bounds unless otherwise specified.
- Clock offsets are applied **before** timestamp resolution adjustments.

### 1.2 Timestamp Rounding
- Timestamps are rounded according to system-specific semantics.
- For SENSOR, rounding is to the nearest 10 seconds.
- For MOBILE-LON, rounding is to the nearest minute.
- For LEGACY-BER, timestamps are ambiguous due to a DST fold; both possible UTC times are retained.

### 1.3 Time Zone Conversion
- Time zones are converted to UTC using IANA time zone data.
- Europe/Berlin (OPS-BER, LEGACY-BER) requires consideration of daylight saving time (DST) changes.
- America/New_York (VENDOR-NY) requires conversion from EDT (UTC-4) to UTC.
- Europe/London (MOBILE-LON) requires conversion from BST (UTC+1) to UTC.

### 1.4 DST Ambiguity
- LEGACY-BER exhibits a DST fold on 2025-10-26, where local times from 02:00 to 02:59 could belong to either standard or daylight time.
- MOBILE-LON operates in BST (British Summer Time, UTC+1) during the event.

### 1.5 Inferred Start Times
- For cases where an event start time is missing (e.g., Investigation_Start in T-404), a plausible range is inferred based on operational records or expert judgment.
- The inferred start time is adjusted for the system-specific rounding and clock offset.

### 1.6 Mobile Entry Delay
- In T-406, a user-entered timestamp may have been delayed by up to 5 minutes. This delay is treated as **additional uncertainty** in the recorded timestamp, not the occurrence time.

### 1.7 Alert Sent vs. Alert Received
- `Alert_Sent` is distinct from `Alert_Received` and lacks a direct log of receipt acknowledgment. Its inclusion depends on logical assumptions rather than direct evidence.

### 1.8 Inconsistencies and Ambiguities
- All sources of uncertainty and ambiguities are explicitly noted. These include clock offsets, rounding, DST changes, missing start events, and inferred start times.

## 7. Governance Recommendation

### Timestamp Storage
- **UTC Storage**: All timestamps should be stored in UTC to avoid ambiguity and ensure uniformity across systems.
- **Raw Timestamp Retention**: Retain raw timestamps with their source metadata (system, rounding, clock offset) for audit and forensic purposes.

### Source Offset Telemetry
- **Clock Offset Telemetry**: Implement logging of clock offsets for all systems to monitor drift and ensure timely corrections.
- **Clock Synchronization**: Regularly synchronize clocks using NTP or similar protocols to minimize offset errors.

### Resolution Metadata
- **Resolution Metadata**: Include metadata detailing the resolution method for each timestamp (e.g., rounding, DST handling) to facilitate traceability.

### Start/Complete Semantics
- **Clear Semantics**: Define clear semantics for "start" and "complete" events to avoid ambiguity, especially for user-entered or inferred timestamps.

### DST Fold Handling
- **DST Fold Policy**: Establish a policy for handling DST folds, such as always using the most recent DST rules or a specific fold selection strategy.

### Causal Identifiers
- **Causal Identifiers**: Use unique identifiers (e.g., correlation IDs) to trace the causal relationship between events, especially in complex incident scenarios.

### Policy for Interval Overlap
- **Overlap Sufficiency**: Implement a policy that clearly defines when interval overlap is sufficient for enforcement, considering the context and severity of the incident.

### Additional Recommendations
- **Documentation**: Maintain comprehensive documentation of all normalization assumptions, assumptions, and evidence sources.
- **Training**: Provide training for incident responders on interpreting normalized timestamps and understanding the implications of uncertainties.
- **Continuous Monitoring**: Continuously monitor and review the effectiveness of timestamp normalization and incident response processes to adapt to evolving operational needs.
