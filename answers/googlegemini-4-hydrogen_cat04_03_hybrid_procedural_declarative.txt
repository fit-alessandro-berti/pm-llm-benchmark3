# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

### Material Assumptions
1. **Completed-Case Scope for Structural Conformance:** Conformance rules in the procedural, DECLARE, and Log Skeleton layers assume closed/completed cases (ending in `Close Onboarding`). In-flight cases would otherwise fail liveness obligations (e.g., `response` and `always_after`).
2. **Clarification Scope (R3):** `Request Clarification` / `Receive Clarification` cycles may occur at any point after `Receive Onboarding Request` and before a primary terminal decision (`Reject Vendor` or `Activate Vendor`), including concurrently with initial checks (as in T2) or after `Classify Risk` during due diligence.
3. **Post-Activation Bank Changes (R11 & R10):** Because R10 restricts `Release First Payment` to at most once, "before any later `Release First Payment`" in R11 applies to `Change Bank Details` occurring after `Activate Vendor` and prior to `Release First Payment` (forcing `Suspend Vendor` and a fresh `Verify Bank Account` before payment). Post-payment bank detail changes are outside the onboarding payment gate and are deliberately excluded from the procedural payment loop (or require post-onboarding master-data maintenance).
4. **R6 24-Hour Anchor Ambiguity:** R6 states *"emergency activation may occur before Obtain Legal Opinion ... the opinion must then arrive within 24 hours."* Two interpretations exist: within 24 hours of `Activate Vendor` (`t_opinion - t_activate <= 24h`) or within 24 hours of `Sanctions Escalation` (`t_opinion - t_escalation <= 24h`). In T6 (`Escalation@6`, `Activate@13`, `Opinion@23`), both deltas (`10h` and `17h`) are `<= 24h`. We formalize the literal anchor `t_opinion - t_activate <= 24h` while also monitoring `t_opinion - t_escalation <= 24h` (which matches R12's `12h + 2*6h = 24h` upper bound).
5. **Unlogged Attributes:** The event log lacks `risk_level` (for `Classify Risk`), `emergency` (boolean flag), and `joint_authorization` (two-party sign-off metadata). Activity-only formalisms cannot verify whether low/high-risk routing matches actual risk scores or whether premature activation was authorized.
6. **Sanctions Escalation Scope:** Per R7, `Sanctions Escalation` may occur any time after `Run Sanctions Screening` (including concurrently with `Classify Risk` or `Enhanced Due Diligence`).

### Resolving the R5 / R6 Conflict
R5 (Legal) and R6 (Operations) are directly contradictory when `Sanctions Escalation` occurs: R5 forbids `Activate Vendor` before `Obtain Legal Opinion` *under all circumstances*, whereas R6 allows `Activate Vendor` before `Obtain Legal Opinion` during an authorized emergency if the opinion arrives within 24 hours.

Three candidate resolutions exist:

- **Resolution A — Legal-Strict (R5 Prevails):** Enforce R5 unconditionally in the procedural and policy layers (`Obtain Legal Opinion` must precede `Activate Vendor` whenever `Sanctions Escalation` occurs). Because the log lacks `emergency` and `joint_authorization` attributes, R6 cannot be audited from the event log and is disallowed in the automated gate.
- **Resolution B — Operations-Permissive with Compensating Controls (R6 Exception):** Relax the procedural gate so `Obtain Legal Opinion` may follow `Activate Vendor` (preserving R4: `Sanctions Escalation` eventually followed by `Obtain Legal Opinion`), while the external policy layer checks `t_opinion - t_activate <= 24h` and places any premature activation on an audit hold until out-of-band joint authorization is verified.
- **Resolution C — Schema Remediation (Architectural Fix):** Extend the log schema with an explicit `Emergency Joint Authorization` event or mandatory `emergency_flag` + `auth_signers` case attributes before allowing the bypass.

**Implemented Choice & Compliance Risk:**
We choose **Resolution A (Legal-Strict)** for the implemented procedural model and default policy engine, while parameterizing **Resolution B** in the external resource/data layer for audit-mode comparison.
- *Compliance/Operational Risk of Resolution A:* Legitimate emergency vendor activations (such as T6) will be blocked or flagged as hard control-flow violations, risking SLA breaches or incentivizing staff to transact off-system.
- *Compliance Risk of Resolution B (for contrast):* Because `emergency` and `joint_authorization` are absent from the log, an unauthorized bypass of Legal review looks identical in the event log to a genuine emergency. Relying on Resolution B without out-of-band reconciliation exposes the organization to sanctions violations and unrecoverable payments.

# 3. Procedural Backbone

### Formal Description
We use a 1-bounded, sound **Accepting Petri Net** $(P, T, F, \ell, M_0, M_f)$:
- **Concurrency:** `Receive Onboarding Request` produces tokens into `p_cmd_in`, `p_rss_in`, `p_vba_in` (parallel checks) and `p_clar_ready` (clarification gate). `Classify Risk` acts as an AND-join over `p_cmd_out`, `p_rss_out`, `p_vba_out`.
- **Choices & Synchronization (R5/R7):** `Run Sanctions Screening` also populates `p_sanctions_decision`, creating an XOR choice between silent bypass `tau_no_esc` (producing `p_legal_gate`) and `Sanctions Escalation` (requiring `Obtain Legal Opinion` to produce `p_legal_gate`). Both `Activate Vendor` and `Reject Vendor` consume `p_legal_gate`, ensuring that under Resolution A, `Obtain Legal Opinion` precedes `Activate Vendor` whenever `Sanctions Escalation` occurred.
- **Clarification Loops (R3):** `Request Clarification` consumes `p_clar_ready` and produces `p_clar_pending`; `Receive Clarification` restores `p_clar_ready`. Because both `Activate Vendor` and `Reject Vendor` consume `p_clar_ready`, no unanswered clarification can remain open at a primary outcome, and no new clarification can start afterward.
- **Terminal Outcomes (R1, R2, R8):** `p_classified` branches via XOR into `Reject Vendor` (to `p_close_ready`), `Manager Approval` (low-risk, to `p_approved`), or `Enhanced Due Diligence -> Compliance Approval` (high-risk, to `p_approved`).
- **Post-Activation Behavior (R11):** `Activate Vendor` produces `p_activated`, from which `Release First Payment` can fire (to `p_close_ready -> Close Onboarding -> p_end`), or `Change Bank Details -> Suspend Vendor -> Verify Bank Account` (a second transition sharing the visible label `"Verify Bank Account"`) loops back to `p_activated` before `Release First Payment`. *Deliberately excluded:* `Change Bank Details` occurring *after* `Release First Payment` is excluded from the onboarding net because R10 forbids a second `Release First Payment`; post-payment bank changes belong to ongoing vendor master-data maintenance rather than first-payment release gating.

### Rule Semantics and Key Distinctions

| Template | Rule Key | Direction & Exact Semantics |
|---|---|---|
| `init` | `"Receive Onboarding Request"` | Case must begin with `Receive Onboarding Request`. |
| `exactly_one` | `Receive`, `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, `Classify Risk`, `Close Onboarding` | Each occurs exactly once per completed case. (`Verify Bank Account` is omitted because R11 permits `>= 2`.) |
| `existence` | `"Verify Bank Account"` | Must occur at least once (`>= 1`), accommodating R11 re-verification. |
| `precedence` | `(A, B)` for `Verify Bank Account -> Classify Risk`, `Classify Risk -> {Reject, Manager, EDD}`, `Run Sanctions Screening -> Sanctions Escalation`, `Activate Vendor -> Change Bank Details` | **Target-triggered backward check:** Whenever `B` occurs, `A` must have occurred earlier. Does *not* require `B` to occur if `A` occurs. |
| `response` | `(A, B)` for `Receive -> Classify Risk`, `{Manager, Compliance} -> Activate`, `{Reject, Release} -> Close` | **Source-triggered forward check:** Whenever `A` occurs, `B` must eventually occur afterward. Does *not* prevent `B` from occurring without `A` (e.g., `Activate Vendor` can follow `Manager Approval` or `Compliance Approval`). |
| `succession` | `(Capture/Run) -> Classify Risk`, `EDD -> Compliance`, `Sanctions Escalation -> Obtain Legal Opinion`, `Activate -> Release First Payment`, `Change Bank Details -> Suspend Vendor` | **Bidirectional:** Both `response(A, B)` and `precedence(A, B)` hold. Note `Verify Bank Account -> Classify Risk` is `precedence` only (not `succession`), because a post-activation R11 `Verify Bank Account` has no `Classify Risk` after it. |
| `altsuccession` | `("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification")` | Combines `altresponse` (every `Request` is followed by `Receive` before another `Request`) and `altprecedence` (every `Receive` is preceded by `Request` without another `Receive` in between). |
| `noncoexistence` | `(Reject, Activate)`, `(EDD, Manager)`, `(Compliance, Manager)`, `(Manager, Reject)`, `(Compliance, Reject)`, `(EDD, Reject)`, `(Reject, Release First Payment)` | `A` and `B` can never appear in the same trace in **any** order. |
| `nonsuccession` | `("Activate Vendor", "Request Clarification")`, `("Reject Vendor", "Request Clarification")` | `B` cannot occur **after** `A`, though `B` *may* occur **before** `A`. |

**Critical Template Distinctions:**
- **`response(A, B)` vs `precedence(A, B)`:** `response(A, B)` constrains the *future* when `A` happens (`Compliance Approval -> Activate Vendor`), whereas `precedence(A, B)` constrains the *past* when `B` happens (`Classify Risk -> Manager Approval`). Putting `precedence(("Manager Approval", "Activate Vendor"))` would falsely reject high-risk cases (T2).
- **`altresponse(A, B)` vs `chainresponse(A, B)`:** `chainresponse` requires `B` to be the *immediately next* event after `A`. For R3, `chainresponse(("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"))` would falsely reject valid traces where parallel checks interleave between request and reply. `altresponse` allows intervening events while still prohibiting consecutive unanswered requests.
- **`noncoexistence(A, B)` vs `nonsuccession(A, B)`:** `noncoexistence` forbids `A` and `B` anywhere in the same trace (used for R1 and R8). `nonsuccession(("Activate Vendor", "Request Clarification"))` permits `Request Clarification` *before* `Activate Vendor` (as in T2) while forbidding new clarification requests *after* activation (R3).
- **Normative `support=1.0, confidence=1.0`:** Because `declare_model` encodes mandatory policy rules rather than mined heuristics, both metadata fields are fixed to `1.0` so conformance thresholds up to `1.0` evaluate all rules. Note that PM4Py's DECLARE template set omits `absence2` (`<= 1`) and 3-ary conditional precedence (`if Escalation then Opinion before Activate`), leaving R10 (`<= 1`) and R5 to Log Skeleton `activ_freq` and the Procedural Petri Net.

# 5. Log-Skeleton Model

### PM4Py Log-Skeleton Dictionary (`log_skeleton`)

In PM4Py / Verbeek's Log Skeleton formalism, pair orientations are:
- **`always_before`**: `(target, prerequisite)` — whenever `target` occurs, `prerequisite` must have occurred before it (inverse tuple order of DECLARE `precedence`).
- **`always_after`**: `(trigger, follower)` — whenever `trigger` occurs, `follower` must occur after it (same tuple order as DECLARE `response`).
- **`directly_follows`**: `(A, B)` — whenever `A` occurs, `B` must be the *immediately following* event (not merely an allowed DFG edge).

### Overlap and Disagreement Across Layers
- **Tuple Orientation Disagreement:** Log Skeleton `always_before` reverses DECLARE `precedence` (`always_before` stores `("Classify Risk", "Verify Bank Account")` meaning *"before `Classify Risk`, `Verify Bank Account` occurs"*, whereas DECLARE `precedence` stores `("Verify Bank Account", "Classify Risk")`).
- **R10 vs DECLARE:** `activ_freq` natively enforces R10 (`{0, 1}` for `Activate Vendor`, `Reject Vendor`, `Release First Payment`), which PM4Py's DECLARE schema lacks (`absence` means `0`, not `<= 1`).
- **Finite-Set Loop Limitation:** `activ_freq` forces an arbitrary finite bound (`{0, 1, 2, 3}` for clarifications, `{1, 2}` for `Verify Bank Account`), whereas the Procedural Petri Net and DECLARE `altsuccession` permit unbounded cycles.
- **R3 & R5 Blind Spots:** `equivalence(("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"))` plus `always_after` only checks counts and eventual completion—it cannot forbid `Request -> Request -> Receive -> Receive` (unlike DECLARE `altresponse` and the Petri net). Similarly, `always_before` cannot express conditional R5 precedence without breaking unescalated cases.

# 6. Temporal Profile

### PM4Py Temporal Profile (`temporal_profile`)

Hours from R12 are converted to seconds (`1 hour = 3600 seconds`), with `zeta = 2.0` (flagging deviations outside `[mean - 2*sd, mean + 2*sd]`):

```python
temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (172800.0, 86400.0),   # 48h +/- 24h -> [0h, 96h]
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): (43200.0, 21600.0),      # 12h +/- 6h  -> [0h, 24h]
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"): (259200.0, 129600.0),        # 72h +/- 36h -> [0h, 144h]
}
zeta = 2.0
```

### Why Temporal Deviations Are Warnings, Not Control-Flow Violations
1. **Business Variance vs. Structural Legality:** Elapsed time depends on external vendor responsiveness (`Receive Clarification`), legal complexity (`Obtain Legal Opinion`), and payment batch runs (`Release First Payment`). A slow legal opinion (e.g., 34 hours in T7) does not render the onboarding legally invalid if the opinion was still obtained before activation; rejecting the case automatically would block legitimate business.
2. **Symmetric Statistical Bounds vs. Asymmetric SLAs:** A Gaussian profile with `zeta = 2.0` treats unusually fast completion identically to slow completion (e.g., `Activate -> Release` in 2 hours in T7 yields `z = -1.94`, close to `0h`), even though early completion is rarely a compliance failure.
3. **Separation of Concerns (R12 vs. R6):** R12 explicitly defines these thresholds as normative expectations to flag, whereas R6's 24-hour emergency legal opinion deadline is a policy SLA tied to out-of-band authorization—handled in the policy validation layer rather than conflated with statistical process monitoring.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

Activity-only formalisms cannot observe `org:resource`, missing case-level attributes (`emergency`, `joint_auth_signers`, `risk_level`), or conditional temporal SLAs.

### Required Attributes & Missing-Attribute Handling
- **R9 (Segregation of Duties):** Requires event attribute `org:resource` (non-null, non-empty string) on `Verify Bank Account`, `Manager Approval`, `Compliance Approval`, `Capture Master Data`, and `Activate Vendor`. **Missing-attribute policy:** Fail closed—raise `INDETERMINATE_MISSING_RESOURCE` rather than assuming distinct actors.
- **R5/R6 (Emergency Policy):** Requires event `time:timestamp` plus case attributes `emergency` (bool) and `joint_auth_signers` (list of `>= 2` distinct authorizers). **Missing-attribute policy:** Under Resolution A, missing attributes are moot because premature activation fails R5; under Resolution B, if `Obtain Legal Opinion` arrives within 24 hours after premature activation, missing `emergency`/`joint_auth_signers` triggers `policy-dependent` (`AUDIT_HOLD_MISSING_AUTHORIZATION`).

### Executable-Style Python Validation Layer

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

def validate_resource_and_policy_layer(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    policy_mode: str = "legal_strict",  # "legal_strict" (Res. A) or "ops_emergency" (Res. B)
    case_metadata: dict | None = None,  # {case_id: {"emergency": bool, "joint_auth_signers": list}}
) -> dict[str, dict]:
    """
    Validates R9 (four-eyes principle) and R5/R6 (sanctions escalation vs. activation policy).
    Expected columns in df: 'case:concept:name', 'concept:name', 'time:timestamp', 'org:resource'.
    """
    case_metadata = case_metadata or {}
    df = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"]).reset_index(drop=True)
    all_cases = set(df["case:concept:name"].unique())

    # 1. PM4Py built-in Four-Eyes filter on clean-resource subset (keeps conforming cases)
    # Identify cases missing org:resource on SoD-relevant activities first (fail-closed).
    sod_acts = {
        "Verify Bank Account", "Manager Approval", "Compliance Approval",
        "Capture Master Data", "Activate Vendor",
    }
    sod_df = df[df["concept:name"].isin(sod_acts)]
    missing_res_cases = set(
        sod_df[sod_df["org:resource"].isna() | (sod_df["org:resource"].astype(str).str.strip() == "")]
        ["case:concept:name"].unique()
    )

    valid_res_df = df[~df["case:concept:name"].isin(missing_res_cases)]
    ok_vba_mgr = set(pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(valid_res_df, "Verify Bank Account", "Manager Approval")["case:concept:name"].unique())
    ok_vba_comp = set(pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(valid_res_df, "Verify Bank Account", "Compliance Approval")["case:concept:name"].unique())
    ok_cmd_act = set(pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(valid_res_df, "Capture Master Data", "Activate Vendor")["case:concept:name"].unique())
    pm4py_sod_conforming = ok_vba_mgr & ok_vba_comp & ok_cmd_act

    results = {}
    for cid in sorted(all_cases):
        cdf = df[df["case:concept:name"] == cid]
        findings = []
        status = "pass"

        # --- Check R9: Segregation of Duties ---
        if cid in missing_res_cases:
            status = "fail"
            findings.append("R9_MISSING_RESOURCE: org:resource missing on SoD-critical activity (fail-closed).")
        elif cid not in pm4py_sod_conforming:
            status = "fail"
            # Pinpoint exact resource collision (handles multiple Verify Bank Account events)
            vba_res = set(cdf[cdf["concept:name"] == "Verify Bank Account"]["org:resource"])
            mgr_res = set(cdf[cdf["concept:name"] == "Manager Approval"]["org:resource"])
            comp_res = set(cdf[cdf["concept:name"] == "Compliance Approval"]["org:resource"])
            cmd_res = set(cdf[cdf["concept:name"] == "Capture Master Data"]["org:resource"])
            act_res = set(cdf[cdf["concept:name"] == "Activate Vendor"]["org:resource"])
            if vba_res & mgr_res:
                findings.append(f"R9_SOD_VIOLATION: Verify Bank Account and Manager Approval share resource {vba_res & mgr_res}.")
            if vba_res & comp_res:
                findings.append(f"R9_SOD_VIOLATION: Verify Bank Account and Compliance Approval share resource {vba_res & comp_res}.")
            if cmd_res & act_res:
                findings.append(f"R9_SOD_VIOLATION: Capture Master Data and Activate Vendor share resource {cmd_res & act_res}.")

        # --- Check R5 / R6: Sanctions Escalation vs. Activation Policy ---
        esc_rows = cdf[cdf["concept:name"] == "Sanctions Escalation"]
        act_rows = cdf[cdf["concept:name"] == "Activate Vendor"]
        op_rows = cdf[cdf["concept:name"] == "Obtain Legal Opinion"]

        if not esc_rows.empty and not act_rows.empty:
            t_act = act_rows["time:timestamp"].iloc[0]
            if op_rows.empty:
                status = "fail"
                findings.append("R4_R5_VIOLATION: Sanctions Escalation occurred and Vendor activated without Obtain Legal Opinion.")
            else:
                t_op = op_rows["time:timestamp"].iloc[0]
                if t_act < t_op:  # Premature activation before legal opinion
                    if policy_mode == "legal_strict":
                        status = "fail"
                        findings.append("R5_POLICY_VIOLATION: Activate Vendor preceded Obtain Legal Opinion after Sanctions Escalation.")
                    elif policy_mode == "ops_emergency":
                        hours_to_opinion = (t_op - t_act).total_seconds() / 3600.0
                        if hours_to_opinion > 24.0:
                            status = "fail"
                            findings.append(f"R6_SLA_VIOLATION: Opinion arrived {hours_to_opinion:.1f}h (>24h) after emergency activation.")
                        else:
                            meta = case_metadata.get(cid, {})
                            signers = meta.get("joint_auth_signers", [])
                            if meta.get("emergency") is True and len(set(signers)) >= 2:
                                findings.append(f"R6_EMERGENCY_APPROVED: Opinion arrived in {hours_to_opinion:.1f}h with verified joint auth.")
                            else:
                                if status != "fail":
                                    status = "policy-dependent"
                                findings.append(
                                    f"R6_AUDIT_HOLD: Opinion arrived in {hours_to_opinion:.1f}h (<=24h), "
                                    "but 'emergency' flag and joint authorization are absent from the event log."
                                )

        results[cid] = {"status": status, "findings": findings}
    return results
```

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

1. **Duplicated Enforcement Across Layers:** R1, R4, R7, and R8 are enforced simultaneously by the Procedural Petri Net, DECLARE, and Log Skeleton. While this provides defense-in-depth when exporting single-formalism views, it inflates violation counts on traces like T8 unless findings are deduplicated by root-cause requirement.
2. **Inconsistency Under Loops and Post-Activation Changes (R3 & R11):** Adding R11's post-activation `Change Bank Details -> Suspend Vendor -> Verify Bank Account` invalidates naive global constraints: `Verify Bank Account` cannot stay in DECLARE `exactly_one` (must downgrade to `existence`), and `("Verify Bank Account", "Classify Risk")` cannot be in DECLARE `succession` or Log Skeleton `always_after`—only `precedence` / `always_before` holds. Similarly, Log Skeleton `activ_freq` forces arbitrary finite caps on R3 clarification loops.
3. **Vacuity in Declarative Rules:** Binary DECLARE constraints (`precedence`, `response`, `noncoexistence`, `altsuccession`) and Log Skeleton (`always_before`, `always_after`) are vacuously satisfied whenever their trigger/target is absent. For instance, DECLARE gives `pass` to T6 on `succession(("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"))` and would pass `precedence(("Verify Bank Account", "Classify Risk"))` even on a malformed trace that skipped `Classify Risk` entirely unless `exactly_one("Classify Risk")` is also enforced.
4. **Missing Case & Event Attributes:** The event log omits `emergency`, `joint_authorization`, and `risk_level`. Consequently, no automated model can distinguish an authorized emergency activation (R6) from an unauthorized bypass, nor verify that `Manager Approval` was only used for genuinely low-risk vendors rather than misrouted high-risk ones.
5. **Timestamp-Only Limitations:** `temporal_profile` computes raw wall-clock seconds (`t_B - t_A`). It ignores weekends/holidays, timezone offsets, queuing vs. active work time, and *unpaired* events (T5's unanswered `Request Clarification@4` produces zero temporal deviations because `Receive Clarification` never occurs).
6. **Treating Support/Confidence Metadata as Normative Truth:** If `declare_model` were discovered empirically from historical logs containing routine policy workarounds (e.g., frequent premature activations or SoD overrides), a rule could exhibit `support = 0.95, confidence = 0.98` while violating statutory compliance, whereas rare legitimate paths (e.g., R11 bank-change suspensions) would show low support and be pruned. Normative models must fix `support = 1.0, confidence = 1.0` by policy decree, never by historical frequency.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

1. **Canonical Procedural Representation:** Deploy the **Accepting Petri Net (`procedural_model`)** as the canonical control-flow artifact for alignment-based conformance checking (`pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments`). Unlike Process Trees, it natively captures R5 synchronization, R3 clarification state, and R11 re-verification without structural approximations.
2. **Authoritative Policy Layers:**
   - **Control-Flow Topology, Synchronization & Unbounded Loops (Backbone, R1–R5, R7, R8, R11):** Authoritative = **Procedural Petri Net** (with DECLARE `altsuccession` and `nonsuccession` used as lightweight streaming/explainable guards).
   - **Strict Cardinality Caps (R10):** Authoritative = **Log Skeleton `activ_freq`** (`{0, 1}`).
   - **Segregation of Duties & Conditional Emergency Policy (R6, R9):** Authoritative = **Resource/Data Policy Layer** (`validate_resource_and_policy_layer`).
   - **Latency Monitoring (R12):** Authoritative = **Temporal Profile** (`zeta = 2.0`), strictly advisory (`warning`).
3. **Recommended Execution Order:**
   1. *Schema & Completeness Pre-Check* (verify `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `time:timestamp`, `org:resource`, and case closure).
   2. *Fast-Fail Cardinality & Exclusion Screen* (`Log Skeleton` + `DECLARE` for instant root-cause tagging of R1, R2, R3, R8, R10).
   3. *Full Structural Alignment* (`Accepting Petri Net` alignments for exact deviation localization across concurrency, R5, and R11).
   4. *Resource & Out-of-Band Policy Validation* (R9 SoD checks and R5/R6 emergency-policy routing).
   5. *Temporal Profile Evaluation* (R12 SLA anomaly warnings on structurally valid or policy-reviewed cases).
4. **Surfacing Contradictory Findings to Users:**
   - Never collapse multi-layer results into a single opaque score. Group findings by **severity tier** (`Hard Control-Flow/SoD Fail` > `Policy-Dependent Audit Hold` > `Temporal Warning`) and **deduplicate by Requirement ID (R1–R12)** so a single root cause (e.g., T8's `Reject Vendor` after `Activate Vendor`) is reported once with links to all failing formalisms.
   - Surface policy conflicts explicitly in the audit UI: when `policy_mode` switches between Legal-Strict (Resolution A) and Ops-Permissive (Resolution B), display the exact affected cases (such as T6) as **policy-dependent audit holds** requiring external joint-authorization upload before clearing.
