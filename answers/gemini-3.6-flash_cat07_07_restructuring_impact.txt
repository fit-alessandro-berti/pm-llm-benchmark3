### Detailed Causal Mechanics

*   **Nominal Handover Reduction vs. Operational Queue Formation:** Management anticipates reduced handovers by removing ad-hoc cross-team reassignments (dropping from 6 to 3 per 100 cases). However, converting direct local transfers into queue-based handovers alters the *type* of handover. Direct transfers involve synchronous context sharing; queue handovers are asynchronous. The event log will show fewer total unique resource-to-resource handovers per case, but longer queue waiting times (`insert_case_service_waiting_time`) at the centralized Specialist Review node.
*   **Specialist Utilization & Bottleneck Dynamics:** Pools of specialists increase nominal capacity utilization (aiming for +15%). However, because Legal and Risk specialists are already operating near critical capacity (93% and 91% respectively), routing all high-complexity technical cases into the same shared queue creates a combined multi-resource bottleneck. Queueing theory dictates that as utilization approaches 90%+, queue waiting times increase non-linearly.
*   **Rework and Local Expertise Loss:** Local specialists (N_Tech, S_Tech) currently conduct informal pre-screening ("triage"). Removing local specialists forces caseworkers to submit raw, unvetted requests to the shared service. This increases the rejection/clarification rate. In process logs, this registers as explicit rework loops (`Specialist Review` $\rightarrow$ `Local Customer Contact` $\rightarrow$ `Specialist Review` or `Quality Check` $\rightarrow$ `Rework`).
*   **Role Clarity and Organizational Networks:** The organizational network will shift from distinct, highly cohesive clusters (high within-team `working_together_network` density) to a hub-and-spoke model. Activity-based resource similarity (`discover_activity_based_resource_similarity`) between local caseworkers and specialists will drop, indicating functional silo expansion and loss of shared context.
*   **Resilience to Absence:** Centralization improves structural resilience against single-point-of-failure absences (such as E_Tech being away) by distributing load across a pool. However, systemic resilience decreases if the central queue lacks internal priority routing for time-sensitive cases.
*   **Fairness across Teams and Customer Segments:** East Team performance (baseline SLA miss 9.8%, median cycle time 8.6d) will likely improve due to load offloading. Conversely, North Team (baseline SLA miss 6.5%, CSAT 4.3) will experience performance degradation due to the loss of dedicated local technical support and regional context.

### Scenario 1: Optimistic (High Pooling Efficiency, Low Context Loss)
*   **Handover Pattern:** Formal handovers to Technical Shared Service stabilize at ~38/100 cases. Reassignment ping-pong stays low ($<5\%$). Cross-team ad-hoc transfers drop from 6 to 2 per 100 cases.
*   **Queue/Waiting-Time Pattern:** Centralized queuing efficiently smooths peaks. Technical Shared Service wait time averages 2.5 hours (lower than management’s estimated 4.0h due to optimal load distribution). Legal/Risk queue wait time remains elevated at 8.5–9.0h but does not grow.
*   **Cycle-Time Distribution:** Overall median cycle time drops across all teams to 6.2 days. The 90th percentile narrows from 22.8d to 15.0d in East, while North increases slightly from 16.4d to 17.0d. Tail dispersion decreases across the organization.
*   **Rework and Quality Pattern:** Rework rate stays stable at ~8.5% globally. Early standardized templates prevent low-quality submissions to the shared queue.
*   **Role Clarity and Accountability:** Clear boundary between local execution and specialist advice. High role distinctiveness in `discover_organizational_roles`.
*   **Fairness Implications:** Regional performance gaps narrow. East team SLA miss rate drops from 9.8% to 6.8%, achieving parity with North (6.7%) and South (6.9%).
*   **Early Warning Signs:** Immediate drop in East team waiting times within week 2; low `reassignment_count` ($<1.1$ per case) in central queues.

### Scenario 2: Central (Expected Baseline with Context Friction)
*   **Handover Pattern:** Handover frequency to Technical Shared Service matches management expectations (41/100 cases). However, return handovers require clarification in 15% of cases, creating secondary loops (`Shared Queue` $\rightarrow$ `Local Caseworker` $\rightarrow$ `Shared Queue`).
*   **Queue/Waiting-Time Pattern:** Technical Shared Service median wait increases to 4.5 hours. Legal/Risk queue wait rises from 9.6h to 11.2h due to additional triage load offloaded onto scarce legal resources.
*   **Cycle-Time Distribution:** Overall median cycle time remains flat at ~7.2 days across the enterprise. However, the distribution skews: East median cycle time improves (8.6d $\rightarrow$ 7.2d), while North regresses (6.8d $\rightarrow$ 7.6d). The enterprise 90th percentile cycle time widens from 18.5d to 21.0d.
*   **Rework and Quality Pattern:** Rework increases moderately in North and South (from 8.2% to 10.5%), driven by missing local nuances in technical specifications. Enterprise rework rises to 10.1%.
*   **Role Clarity and Accountability:** Local caseworkers report ambiguity over who owns case completion once a case enters the shared approval queue. Handover reason logs show increased "status inquiry" handovers.
*   **Fairness Implications:** East gains at the direct expense of North and South. Complex cases in North experience a 25% increase in end-to-end throughput time.
*   **Early Warning Signs:** Rising wait times in the Legal/Risk queue by week 3; spike in `reassignment_count` for cases with `case_complexity = 'High'`.

### Scenario 3: Adverse (Bottleneck Spillover & Expertise Loss)
*   **Handover Pattern:** Formal handovers to central queues exceed expectations (52/100 cases) because local caseworkers, lacking embedded technical advice, escalate ambiguous cases to clear their own queues. Return loops multiply (handover frequency TSS $\rightarrow$ Caseworker exceeds 50/100 cases).
*   **Queue/Waiting-Time Pattern:** Severe queue degradation. Technical Shared Service wait time jumps to 8.5 hours. Legal/Risk wait time explodes to 22.0 hours as Legal/Risk specialists (already at $>90\%$ capacity) become overwhelmed by unvetted submissions.
*   **Cycle-Time Distribution:** Median cycle time degrades to 9.1 days enterprise-wide. The 90th percentile cycle time explodes to 28.5 days. Bimodal cycle time distribution emerges: simple automated cases finish in $<2$ days, while complex cases drag out past 25 days.
*   **Rework and Quality Pattern:** Global rework rate spikes to 15.2%. Lack of local pre-screening results in high rates of rejection at `Quality Check`, pushing cases into repeated `Rework` steps.
*   **Role Clarity and Accountability:** Diffusion of responsibility. Caseworkers attribute delays to the "black hole" shared queue; shared specialists claim inputs are poorly prepared. Network analysis shows fragmented `working_together_network` clusters with weak inter-node ties.
*   **Fairness Implications:** Severe degradation for high-complexity cases and specialized customer segments (e.g., regional language/regulatory requirements). SLA miss rate in East spikes to 14.2%, North to 12.1%, South to 13.0%.
*   **Early Warning Signs:** Wavelength of queue growth at `Specialist Review` exceeding processing rate by week 2; steep decline in `local_expertise_flag` usage; worker complaints regarding approval queue stalled cases.

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1.  **Tail Latency Amplification (p90/p99 Blowup):**
    A stable enterprise average cycle time (e.g., 7.2 days) can conceal a severe worsening of the tail distribution. High-complexity cases requiring multiple specialist inputs may experience exponential queue delays, causing the 90th percentile to expand from 18 days to 28+ days, even as high volumes of simple, automated cases pull down the mean.

2.  **Queue Stagnation of High-Complexity Cases:**
    When specialists are evaluated on throughput or utilization targets, they are incentivized to process simple cases first. High-complexity cases (`case_complexity = 'High'`) sit in shared queues without clear assignment ownership. Average cycle time masks this because high-complexity cases represent a smaller volume percentage (~15–20%) compared to low-complexity cases, despite carrying the highest operational and financial risk.

3.  **Erosion of Local Informal Filtering ("Watercooler Triage"):**
    In the baseline model, embedded specialists (e.g., `N_Tech`) perform informal sanity checks on casework before formal ticket creation. Centralization forces all inquiries into formal handovers. While total recorded handovers between caseworkers may nominally drop, total system friction increases. The loss of informal pre-screening manifests as higher rejection rates later in the flow, which is hidden in early-stage cycle time averages.

4.  **Regional and Segment Fairness Disparity:**
    Aggregating performance metrics masks localized degradation. For example, East Team's performance may improve significantly (benefiting from central capacity), while North Team's performance degrades due to losing dedicated technical resources. Similarly, specialized customer segments requiring regional context suffer higher rework, but their impact is diluted in top-level median metrics.

5.  **Skill Atrophy and Learning Deficits in Local Caseworkers:**
    When local caseworkers offload all technical assessment and risk evaluation to shared queues, their exposure to complex decision-making drops. Over time, their capability to draft complete technical specifications deteriorates. This long-term degradation in frontline competency leads to a gradual increase in rework loops that will not show up in short-term post-restructuring cycle time metrics.

6.  **Diffusion of Accountability and Ping-Pong Handover Loops:**
    In a shared service model, cases often bounce back and forth between local caseworkers and central queues due to incomplete information (`Caseworker` $\rightarrow$ `Shared Queue` $\rightarrow$ `Caseworker` $\rightarrow$ `Shared Queue`). Each individual segment of handoff may report acceptable individual service times, keeping the mean cycle time reasonable, but total case touchpoints and rework counts double, causing hidden staff friction and severe customer dissatisfaction.


### 1. Pilot Scope & Comparison Group Structure
*   **Experimental Group:** Team East (to test offloading relief for highly utilized E_Tech) and a 50% randomized sample of Team North cases (to test transition impact on a high-performing unit).
*   **Control Group:** Team South (retains embedded S_Tech and local supervisor setup) along with the non-selected 50% of Team North.
*   **Observation Window:** 8 weeks pre-pilot baseline observation + 10 weeks pilot execution.

### 2. Stratified Analysis Dimensions
Log data must be evaluated across multi-dimensional strata:
*   *Team:* North vs. South vs. East.
*   *Complexity:* Low vs. Medium vs. High.
*   *Specialty:* General vs. Technical vs. Legal/Risk.
*   *Customer Segment:* Standard vs. Regional/Complex.

### 3. Quantitative Guardrail Metrics
The pilot must be paused or adjusted if any of the following guardrail thresholds are violated:
1.  **Queue Waiting Time:** `Specialist Review` queue median wait time exceeds **6.0 hours** (baseline is 3.2h local / 9.6h legal).
2.  **Tail Latency:** 90th percentile cycle time for `High` complexity cases exceeds **22.0 days** across pilot teams.
3.  **Rework Rate:** Rework flag rate at `Quality Check` exceeds **12.0%** for North/East pilot cases.
4.  **SLA Miss Rate Disparity:** The SLA miss gap between North pilot cases and South control cases widens by $>3.0$ percentage points.
5.  **Reassignment Spike:** Percentage of cases with `reassignment_count >= 2` exceeds **8.0%**.

### 4. Qualitative & Context Feedback Collection
*   **Weekly Caseworker & Specialist Pulse Surveys:** Capture qualitative friction, perceived role clarity, and queue ping-pong issues.
*   **Bi-weekly Handover Reason Audits:** Sample cases with multiple handovers to categorize root cause (e.g., missing specs, incorrect initial triage, ambiguous authority).

### 5. Rollback and Adjustment Triggers
*   **Hard Rollback Trigger:** If Legal/Risk queue waiting time exceeds **18.0 hours** for two consecutive weeks, OR if the overall SLA miss rate in the pilot group exceeds **12.0%**.
*   **Soft Adjustment Trigger:** If rework spikes in North while East cycle times improve, institute a "Triage Liaison" role (re-assigning 1 local tech specialist per team as an informal pre-screener before central routing).

# 6. Recommendation

### Formal Decision: **PILOT FIRST WITH STRUCTURAL MODIFICATIONS**

Management must **not** proceed directly to full rollout. The restructuring plan must be modified to address queue capacity and context loss risks before launching a phased pilot.

### Critical Modifications Required Prior to Pilot Launch:
1.  **Maintain Local Technical Triage ("Pre-Flight Check"):** Rather than routing all high-complexity cases straight to the shared queue, require local caseworkers to complete a standardized technical intake checklist.
2.  **Decouple Legal/Risk from Technical Shared Queue:** Do not combine general technical assessments with Legal/Risk reviews in a single queue pipeline. Legal and Risk specialists are already operating at 91–93% capacity; forcing additional traffic through them will trigger queue collapse.
3.  **Capacity Cap on Shared Approval Queue:** Retain local supervisor sign-off authority for medium-value cases; restrict the central approval queue strictly to high-value/high-risk cases to prevent supervisory queue congestion.

### What Management Must NOT Infer From Early Averages:
*   **Do NOT interpret a flat or slightly improved overall median cycle time as success.** Early gains in median cycle time are often driven by offloading backlog in lagging teams (East) and rapid processing of low-complexity cases. This masks significant degradation in tail latency (p90/p99) and severe queue buildup for high-complexity cases.
*   **Do NOT interpret an initial reduction in handovers as increased efficiency.** A fall in direct inter-person transfers paired with an increase in case wait times indicates that handovers have merely shifted from visible active collaboration to hidden queue delays.
*   **Do NOT mistake high specialist utilization ($>85\%$) for operational efficiency.** In service processes, high resource utilization directly causes exponential queuing delays. Raising specialist utilization targets to $>85\%$ will degrade overall process throughput and SLA compliance.

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.conversion.log import converter as log_converter
from pm4py.algo.transformation.log_to_features import algorithm as log_to_features

def run_process_mining_evaluation(file_path: str):
    """
    Performs comprehensive process mining analysis using PM4Py to evaluate 
    the operational impact of organizational restructuring across teams, 
    complexity levels, and specialist queues.
    """
    # ==========================================
    # 1. LOAD AND PREPROCESS EVENT LOG
    # ==========================================
    df = pd.read_csv(file_path)
    
    # Ensure correct data types
    df['time:timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(df['time:timestamp'])
    df['reassignment_count'] = df['reassignment_count'].fillna(0).astype(int)
    df['rework_flag'] = df['rework_flag'].fillna(False).astype(bool)
    
    # Format dataframe to PM4Py standards
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id='case:concept:name',
        activity_key='concept:name',
        timestamp_key='time:timestamp'
    )
    
    # Segment logs into Pre-Restructuring and Post-Restructuring (or Control vs Pilot)
    # Assuming log contains a 'phase' attribute or splitting by timestamp
    pre_df = df[df['phase'] == 'PRE']
    post_df = df[df['phase'] == 'POST']
    
    print("=== PM4Py Restructuring Analysis Suite ===")
    
    # ==========================================
    # 2. HANDOVER & ORGANIZATIONAL NETWORKS
    # ==========================================
    print("\n--- 1. Social Network Analysis ---")
    
    # Handover of Work Network
    hw_net_pre = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(pre_df)
    hw_net_post = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(post_df)
    print(f"Pre-Restructuring Handover Transitions Count: {len(hw_net_pre)}")
    print(f"Post-Restructuring Handover Transitions Count: {len(hw_net_post)}")
    
    # Working Together Network
    wt_net_pre = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(pre_df)
    wt_net_post = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(post_df)
    
    # Subcontracting Network (Detecting Ping-Pong / Delegation Loops)
    sub_net_pre = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(pre_df)
    sub_net_post = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(post_df)
    
    # Resource Similarity & Organizational Role Discovery
    roles_pre = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(pre_df)
    roles_post = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(post_df)
    print(f"Discovered Roles (Pre): {len(roles_pre)} | Discovered Roles (Post): {len(roles_post)}")

    # ==========================================
    # 3. DIRECTLY-FOLLOWS GRAPHS & PERFORMANCE
    # ==========================================
    print("\n--- 2. Directly-Follows Graphs (DFG) & Performance ---")
    
    # Frequency DFG
    dfg_pre, start_pre, end_pre = pm4py.discover_dfg(pre_df)
    dfg_post, start_post, end_post = pm4py.discover_dfg(post_df)
    
    # Performance DFG (Transition Durations)
    perf_dfg_pre, p_start_pre, p_end_pre = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(pre_df)
    perf_dfg_post, p_start_post, p_end_post = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(post_df)

    # ==========================================
    # 4. QUEUE AND WAITING TIME ANALYSIS
    # ==========================================
    print("\n--- 3. Service and Waiting Time Analysis ---")
    
    # Inject Service and Waiting Times into Event Log Dataframe
    df_with_times = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(post_df)
    
    # Calculate Service Times
    service_times = pm4py.get_service_time(df_with_times)
    
    # Extract temporal features for regression/queue analytics
    temporal_features = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(df_with_times)
    
    # Filter for Central Specialist Review Node
    specialist_cases = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        df_with_times, 
        'concept:name', 
        ['Specialist Review'], 
        level='case', 
        retain=True
    )
    
    # Calculate Median and p90 Waiting Time for Specialist Review
    spec_df = specialist_cases[specialist_cases['concept:name'] == 'Specialist Review']
    median_wait = spec_df['waiting_time'].median() / 3600.0  # Convert seconds to hours
    p90_wait = spec_df['waiting_time'].quantile(0.90) / 3600.0
    print(f"Specialist Review Queue - Median Wait: {median_wait:.2f}h | p90 Wait: {p90_wait:.2f}h")

    # ==========================================
    # 5. SUBGROUP SEGMENTATION ANALYSIS
    # ==========================================
    print("\n--- 4. Team, Complexity, and Segment Disparity ---")
    
    teams = ['North', 'South', 'East']
    complexities = ['Low', 'Medium', 'High']
    
    for t in teams:
        team_df = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(post_df, 'team', [t], level='case', retain=True)
        
        # Calculate Cycle Time Percentiles
        case_durations = team_df.groupby('case:concept:name')['time:timestamp'].agg(lambda x: (x.max() - x.min()).total_seconds() / 86400.0)
        p50 = case_durations.median()
        p90 = case_durations.quantile(0.90)
        
        # Rework per activity
        rework_counts = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(team_df)
        rework_rate = (team_df['rework_flag'].sum() / len(team_df)) * 100.0
        
        print(f"Team: {t:5s} | Median Cycle: {p50:.2f}d | p90 Cycle: {p90:.2f}d | Rework Rate: {rework_rate:.1f}%")
        
        for comp in complexities:
            sub_df = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(team_df, 'case_complexity', [comp], level='case', retain=True)
            if not sub_df.empty:
                sub_durations = sub_df.groupby('case:concept:name')['time:timestamp'].agg(lambda x: (x.max() - x.min()).total_seconds() / 86400.0)
                print(f"    Complexity: {comp:6s} | Count: {len(sub_durations)} | Median: {sub_durations.median():.2f}d | p90: {sub_durations.quantile(0.90):.2f}d")

    # ==========================================
    # 6. VARIANT & REWORK LOOP ANALYSIS
    # ==========================================
    print("\n--- 5. Process Variants & Path Instability ---")
    
    variants_pre = pm4py.get_variants(pre_df)
    variants_post = pm4py.get_variants(post_df)
    
    print(f"Unique Case Variants (Pre): {len(variants_pre)}")
    print(f"Unique Case Variants (Post): {len(variants_post)}")
    
    # Detect ping-ponging between local caseworker and central queue
    # Filter variants that contain repeated Specialist Review
    rework_variants = pm4py.filter_variants(
        post_df, 
        filter_variants_list=[v for v in variants_post if v.count('Specialist Review') > 1]
    )
    print(f"Cases with Multiple Specialist Review Loops: {len(rework_variants['case:concept:name'].unique())}")

if __name__ == "__main__":
```
