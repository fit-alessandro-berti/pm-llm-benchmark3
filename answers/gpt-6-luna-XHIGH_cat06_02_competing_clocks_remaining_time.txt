## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

**Smooth path.**

- **Chemistry waiting and service:** Under FIFO, four waiting samples plus work already in progress make Friday completion unlikely. B-417 may start late Friday only if risk-priority advances it as an analyst becomes free; unless it starts by about 16:30 and draws a short service, it cannot finish by 18:00. More typically it starts or resumes Monday. Allow about 2–5 hours of queue exposure on Monday and 1.5–4.5 analyst hours of service, interrupted at 18:00 if necessary. A reasonable result window is Monday late morning through afternoon.
- **Microbiology incubation and readout:** From the 14:00 Friday start, the 46–54-hour incubation ends Sunday 12:00–20:00. With no authorized on-call reader (probability 0.55–0.75), readout waits for Monday 08:00 and takes 0.5–1.5 hours. With authorization, it can instead be read Sunday after a 1–3-hour dispatch/readout. Thus a normal microbiology result is likely available Sunday afternoon/evening on the on-call path, or Monday morning otherwise.
- **Parallel join:** Join time is the later of the usable chemistry result and usable microbiology result, not their sum. In most smooth paths chemistry is later or close to the Monday microbiology readout. Weekend on-call helps only if chemistry is also ready.
- **QA waiting and service:** At a Monday join, allow about 1–5 QA staffed hours of queue wait, with 6–10 hours a plausible crowded tail, then roughly 1.5 hours typical review (up to about 3.5 hours at the stated 80th percentile). Monday work ends at 20:00; a late queue position can therefore resume Tuesday. Arrivals at 0.35/hour continue during open time, but two Monday reviewers should usually out-capacity the mean arrival rate.
- **Optional rework/investigation:** None in this path. This is a conditional smooth-path decomposition, not an assertion that all anomalies are independent.
- **Final step:** Electronic Release adds 0.25–0.75 hours continuously, so the terminal time follows QA acceptance without waiting for an opening.

**Chemistry rerun path.**

- **Chemistry waiting and service:** The primary sample still faces the same queue and calendar. If it is processed Monday, a borderline result adds a priority rerun of 1–3 analyst hours. If that rerun cannot finish by 18:00, it resumes Tuesday at 08:00; a late primary result can therefore push the chemistry branch into Tuesday even though the rerun is prioritized. Rerun probability is higher if the temperature excursion is confirmed.
- **Microbiology incubation and readout:** Usually the normal Sunday technical readiness and Sunday-on-call or Monday-morning readout described above.
- **Parallel join:** Take the later of the completed chemistry rerun and usable microbiology result. The rerun may be hidden under a slower microbiology clock, or may become the controlling branch if it spills into Tuesday.
- **QA waiting and service:** At the resulting Monday/Tuesday join, allow a few staffed hours of queue wait and 1.5–3.5 hours of review. A late Monday join may carry to Tuesday because QA closes at 20:00.
- **Optional rework/investigation:** If the rerun is a trigger for investigation, use the investigation path below; it is correlated with excursion and contamination risk. Otherwise no additional work is assumed.
- **Final step:** Add 0.25–0.75 hours for electronic release after acceptance.

**Delayed microbiology / contamination-confirmation path.**

- **Chemistry waiting and service:** Chemistry follows the same Monday queue and service estimate; a chemistry result ready before the delayed microbiology result is simply waiting at the join.
- **Microbiology incubation and readout:** If the primary readout happens Sunday under on-call coverage and shows an indication, the extra 20–30 hours of continuous incubation makes confirmation technically ready around Monday/Tuesday, followed by 0.5–1.5 hours of staffed readout. If there is no on-call reader, the first read is Monday 08:00 onward; confirmation incubation then makes the follow-up technically ready roughly Tuesday 04:00–Wednesday afternoon. A result ready before 18:00 can be read that day; a later one waits until the next 08:00 opening. This typically moves the usable result to Tuesday or Wednesday.
- **Parallel join:** Use the maximum of the chemistry result time and the final microbiology confirmation time. Do not add the two branch durations; chemistry may finish while confirmation incubates.
- **QA waiting and service:** QA can start only after that join. Add a few staffed hours of uncertain queue wait and 1.5–3.5 hours review; for a Wednesday join, two reviewers are available, though the queue remains uncertain.
- **Optional rework/investigation:** Contamination increases investigation probability. If opened, use either 8–20 QA working hours for documentation-only investigation or 3–7 calendar days for laboratory investigation, not both as a default.
- **Final step:** Add 0.25–0.75 hours for release after QA acceptance. A confirmed critical defect would instead require rejection recording, but no defect rate is supplied.

**QA query / investigation path.**

- **Chemistry waiting and service:** Typical Monday result; allow for a Monday close pause or Tuesday completion under FIFO or rerun conditions.
- **Microbiology incubation and readout:** Typically usable by Monday morning on the no-confirmation path; confirmation can move the join to Tuesday/Wednesday.
- **Parallel join:** Again take the later usable branch result. A query or investigation starts only after QA can review the joined batch.
- **QA waiting and service:** At the first review, add the future queue wait and about 1.5 hours typical review (up to 3.5 hours at the 80th percentile).
- **Optional rework/investigation:** A documentation query adds 4–12 QA working hours before resumption. A documentation-only investigation adds 8–20 QA working hours. Those hours can cross the 20:00 close and overnight/weekend closures, and resumed QA review may face another queue. A laboratory investigation instead adds 3–7 calendar days; its opening probability rises in anomaly/excursion paths. Query and investigation can co-occur, but should not be treated as independent probabilities.
- **Final step:** Once QA accepts, add 0.25–0.75 hours. If a critical defect is confirmed, substitute 0.5–1.0 hours of rejection recording.

**Laboratory-investigation severe tail.**

- **Chemistry waiting and service:** Usually completed by Monday/Tuesday, possibly after a rerun.
- **Microbiology incubation and readout:** Usually Sunday/Monday on a clean result, Tuesday/Wednesday if confirmation is needed.
- **Parallel join:** The slower usable branch controls initial QA start.
- **QA waiting and service:** Add the queue and first review as above; a flag, rerun, or contamination indication can raise investigation likelihood.
- **Optional rework/investigation:** If the investigation needs laboratory work, add 3–7 calendar days from its opening, likely Monday–Wednesday. Some work can proceed continuously, but final usable findings and QA closure are still needed.
- **Final step:** After the investigation and QA acceptance, electronic release adds 0.25–0.75 hours; confirmed critical defect, if any, leads to rejection recording instead.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. **Whether an anomaly opens a laboratory investigation, and its 3–7-day duration — path uncertainty.** This is the largest source of a multi-day right tail; the conditional investigation rates and conditional laboratory-work share are broad.
2. **Unknown temperature excursion — dependence uncertainty.** Confirmation raises both rerun and contamination chances and can also raise investigation probability, clustering delays in the same batches rather than creating independent risks.
3. **Chemistry queue discipline and service placement around 18:00 — queue and calendar uncertainty.** FIFO leaves four waiting samples ahead, while risk-priority could advance B-417; a late start loses the weekend and can also delay a rerun.
4. **Microbiology readout authorization and contamination confirmation — calendar and path uncertainty.** Incubation itself completes during the weekend, but routine readout is unavailable until Monday; confirmation adds another 20–30 hours and needs a second staffed readout.
5. **QA queue at the future join and follow-up workload — queue uncertainty.** Friday/Saturday staffing may clear some current work, but overdispersed arrivals leave an uncertain Monday backlog; a query or investigation then re-enters that queue and must fit QA hours.

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

The most informative single next observation is **the microbiology primary readout, including whether it is normal or indicates contamination**. A normal readout available Sunday with authorized coverage (or Monday morning without it) confirms that the branch is done and leaves chemistry/QA as the likely controls. An indication starts 20–30 more hours of incubation and another staffed readout, usually moving the join to Tuesday or Wednesday; in a confirmed-excursion state it also raises the chance of investigation.

A strong secondary update trigger is **confirmation or clearance of the upstream temperature-excursion flag**. Confirmation should move probability jointly toward chemistry rerun, microbiology confirmation, and QA investigation tails; clearance should reduce those correlated risks, while leaving ordinary queue and calendar uncertainty intact.
