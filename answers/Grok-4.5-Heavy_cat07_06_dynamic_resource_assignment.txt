# 1. Objectives and constraints

**Primary objectives** (to be balanced, not lexicographically optimized):
- Protect short-term SLA performance and throughput for urgent/high-priority work.
- Reduce error, rework and appeal risk through adequate skill match and continuity of ownership.
- Develop skills over time (documented training opportunities).
- Maintain workload fairness and limit overtime exposure.
- Preserve continuity of case ownership where handover costs and historical risk are high.
- Build resilience by avoiding specialist bottlenecks, single points of failure and extreme concentration of critical skills.

**Hard / safety / compliance constraints** (non-negotiable filters applied before any scoring):
- Skill eligibility: required skill score ≥ 1. Security Review requires skill 3, or skill 1–2 only under explicit supervision by R7_Gita or R8_Hugo. Final Approval requires skill 2–3 to finalize (skill 1 may prepare under supervision but cannot be the assigned finalizer). High-priority (urgent/high) cases may not be given to skill < 2 without supervision.
- At most 4 new assignments per resource in the current decision batch.
- Resources with overtime risk = high are excluded unless the case SLA remaining is critically short (≤ ~4–6 h) and no safer eligible alternative exists.
- For high-complexity cases (≥ ~0.7) that also carry high handover cost (or reassignment_count ≥ 2), continuity is preferred unless the current owner has skill 0/1 and supervision is infeasible.
- Quality Check may be batched only when priority is normal/low and SLA remaining > 24 h.
- Overall training share target: at least 20 % of normal/low-priority assignments should match a resource’s documented training priority when SLA risk remains acceptable.

**Soft preferences / trade-off dimensions** (scored and weighted):
- SLA urgency and capacity.
- Skill-match quality.
- Continuity / handover-cost avoidance.
- Workload fairness and overtime exposure.
- Training / development contribution.
- Resilience (load spreading across teams and avoidance of specialist saturation).

No single objective dominates; the policy deliberately accepts documented trade-offs rather than claiming a unique optimum.

# 2. Assignment policy

**High-level algorithm** (explainable, sequential, multi-criteria):

```
1. Sort the queue by descending urgency:
   primary key = SLA remaining (ascending),
   secondary key = priority rank (urgent > high > normal > low).

2. Maintain live state for each resource:
   assigned_count (this batch), estimated remaining hours, current open cases.

3. For each case in sorted order:
   a. Eligibility filter (hard constraints):
      - required_skill ≥ 1 (and activity-specific rules for Security / Approval)
      - high-priority → skill ≥ 2 or supervised pair
      - overtime-risk = high → exclude unless SLA remaining critical and no alternative
      - assigned_count < 4
      - hours remaining > complexity-proxy threshold
      - if supervised needed, identify eligible supervisors (R7/R8 for Security)

   b. If no eligible candidate → escalate (or force specialist with explicit justification).

   c. Score every remaining eligible candidate (each component normalized ≈ [0,1]):
      - SLA_urgency_score   (case urgency × resource capacity bonus)
      - skill_match_score   (skill/3; supervised slightly discounted; training-match bonus)
      - continuity_score    (1.0 if current owner; reduced by handover-cost band and complexity)
      - workload_OT_score   (inverse open cases + hours; heavy penalty for high OT risk)
      - training_score      (1.0 if training_priority matches required skill, normal/low priority, SLA comfortable)
      - resilience_score    (penalty for already-loaded specialists / bottleneck roles; team-spread bonus)

   d. Dynamic weights (default shown; boost by case class):
      SLA 0.30 (↑0.40 if SLA < 6 h),
      Skill 0.20 (↑0.25 if high complexity),
      Continuity 0.20 (↑0.30–0.35 if high-cx + high-handover or reassignment_count ≥ 2),
      Workload/OT 0.15,
      Training 0.10 (↑0.20 when filling the 20 % quota on remaining normal/low cases),
      Resilience 0.05.

   e. Select the highest total score. Record supervised pair when used.
   f. Update resource state (assigned_count++, open-cases estimate, hours estimate).

4. Tie-break order: current owner > lower open cases > higher hours remaining > lower OT risk > training match > team balance.

5. Post-batch check: if training share of normal/low cases < 20 % and residual SLA risk is acceptable, re-score remaining unassigned normal/low cases with boosted training weight.

6. Exception handling: any forced override (e.g., specialist under OT pressure) is logged with the explicit justification for later audit.
```

The policy is deliberately socio-technical: every score component maps to an observable reason that can be shown to a worker (“we kept you because reassignment count was already 3 and complexity high”).

# 4. Comparison with baseline policies

**First-available routing** (lowest open cases + sufficient hours, ignore specialization except skill 0):
- Throughput / waiting time: best short-term average.
- SLA risk: acceptable on average but higher variance on complex/urgent work.
- Error/rework risk: highest (frequent skill mismatch, especially Security/Approval).
- Fairness: good load balance but ignores OT risk and training.
- Skill development: accidental at best.
- Continuity: poor (many handovers).
- Resilience: fragile; specialists still become bottlenecks when skill is finally respected, and reassignments accumulate.

**Specialist-only routing** (highest skill, ties broken by open cases):
- Throughput: poorer (queues form behind R7/R8 for Security and the few Approval finalizers).
- SLA risk: low for the cases that obtain a specialist, high for those waiting.
- Error/rework risk: lowest immediate error rate.
- Fairness: poor (specialists overloaded, others idle).
- Skill development: none.
- Continuity: ignored.
- Resilience: worst (single points of failure on Security and Approval).

**Proposed dynamic policy**:
- Throughput: intermediate; capacity is respected but not maximized at the expense of other goals.
- SLA risk: protected on the critical tail (urgent Security/Approval kept with skill-3 owners or pure specialists).
- Error/rework risk: substantially lower than first-available by enforcing skill floors and continuity on high-complexity / high-reassignment cases; higher than pure specialist-only only where training or OT constraints intervene.
- Fairness: strongest (hard OT filter, max-4 cap, load spreading).
- Skill development: explicit 20 %+ target on normal/low work.
- Continuity: deliberately preserved on the highest-risk cases (C007, C016, most owners with adequate skill).
- Resilience: best of the three—specialists are used but not saturated, teams are diversified, and high-OT resources are protected.

No policy dominates on every dimension; the proposed policy is the only one that systematically surfaces the trade-offs and keeps them auditable.

# 5. Explanation to workers

We assign work so that the most time-critical and complex cases get the right skill and, where possible, stay with the person who already knows them. At the same time we deliberately give a portion of the quieter, lower-risk work to colleagues who have asked to develop that skill, and we avoid pushing extra load onto people already at high overtime risk.

When we move a case away from its current owner it is almost always for one of three visible reasons: the owner’s skill is too low for the urgency or complexity, the owner is already at high overtime risk, or a clear training opportunity exists on a case whose deadline is comfortable. We never move a high-risk case just to “balance numbers.”

You can always see the main reason recorded with the assignment. If the reason feels wrong, raise it—the policy is designed to be questioned and improved, not hidden.

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators

Using the event log columns (especially org:resource, assignment_reason, reassignment_count, handover_cost_band, workload_snapshot, required_skill, case_priority, sla_deadline, case_complexity) the following indicators should be monitored continuously:

- Handover frequency and average handover_cost_band per activity / complexity band.
- Reassignment_count distribution (flag cases exceeding 2).
- Skill-mismatch rate: fraction of events where resource skill < required skill (or < 2 on high-priority cases).
- Workload distribution: open-case and hours-remaining Gini or coefficient of variation; fraction of assignments given to high-OT-risk resources.
- Overtime-risk exposure: percentage of new assignments landing on high-OT resources, stratified by urgency.
- SLA miss rate and mean lateness, overall and for cases that changed owner.
- Rework / appeal rate after assignment, stratified by skill match and by reassignment_count.
- Training-assignment share: percentage of normal/low-priority cases assigned to a resource whose training_priority matches the required skill.
- Specialist bottleneck indicators: queue length and waiting time in front of Security and Approval specialists; utilization of R7/R8 versus other resources.
- Policy-deviation rate: fraction of assignments whose recorded assignment_reason or chosen resource violates the eligibility filters or the scored ranking (detectable by replaying the policy on the log).

These indicators can be computed with standard process-mining filtering, SNA and performance DFG techniques and should be reviewed both automatically (alerts) and in periodic human audits.

import pm4py
import pandas as pd

# 1. Load & format
df = pd.read_csv("event_log.csv")
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    resource_key="org:resource")

# 2. Enrich with service / waiting times
df = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df)

# 5. Filter slices for audit indicators

    df, "reassignment_count", [3,4,5,...], level="event")

skill_mismatch = df[df["org:resource_skill"] < df["required_skill"]]  # after joining skill matrix

# 6. Training share & workload fairness
training_events = df[df["assignment_reason"].str.contains("training", na=False)]
training_share = len(training_events) / len(df[df["case_priority"].isin(["normal","low"])])

# 7. SNA visualisation for resilience discussion
