# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

Assumptions used in the reconstruction:

* A job is **definitely visible** once its recorded arrival time is at or before the decision time.
* Because arrival scans may be delayed by up to two minutes, a job recorded up to two minutes after a decision is marked as only **possibly physically present**; I do not assume the dispatcher necessarily saw it.
* For interrupted B01, I approximate remaining expected work from recorded estimated work minus completed service time. B01 has 45 estimated minutes and receives 14 + 17 + 14 minutes of service.
* Service-start time is used as the observable decision point, but this is imperfect: the actual dispatch decision may have been made earlier.
* “Skill-based” in QA, QB, and QC mostly means “no discrimination,” because all listed jobs require general skill. In QD it is a real constraint.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

**QA**

Supporting cases:

* **A03** strongly supports appointment-aware dispatching. At 08:20 it jumps ahead of older A02 and higher-priority A04 because its appointment time has arrived.
* **A02, A05, and A07** support FIFO among ordinary jobs. A02 is chosen at 08:32 over priority-1 A04; A05 is chosen at 09:05 over priority-1 A08; A07 is chosen at 09:32 over A08.
* **A1 calibration 08:52-08:58** explains the otherwise idle gap after A02.

Challenging cases:

* **A06** has a 09:15 appointment but starts at 09:20. This does not overturn the appointment diagnosis, but it means appointments are probably windows or soft targets rather than exact hard start times.
* **A08** is priority 1 but waits until 09:41. This is not a violation of the inferred QA rule; it is a violation only of a pure priority interpretation.
* **A04** waits from 08:13 to 08:58 despite priority 1 and est7. The delay is explainable by the appointment handling of A03, subsequent FIFO handling of A02, and the 08:52-08:58 calibration break. It still rules out strict priority.

**QB**

Supporting cases:

* **B02** supports limited preemptive urgent handling. B01 is interrupted at 09:16 immediately after B02 arrives, and the interruption reason explicitly says “urgent arrival.”
* **B05** supports some priority-1 preference because it is served at 10:00 before B04 and before B01’s final resumption.
* **B01** supports a resumption/continuity component. It is resumed at 09:31 and 10:14, even though other lower-estimate or higher-priority work is waiting.
* **B04** before **B06** at 10:28 supports FIFO-like behavior once B01 and the urgent jobs have been cleared.

Challenging cases:

* **B03** is the central contradiction. At 09:48 it is selected over B05, B04, and B01. The recorded “dispatcher reassignment” acknowledges an exception, but the rule behind that exception is undocumented.
* **B07** challenges appointment scheduling. Its 10:30 appointment is not honored; it starts only at 10:56 after B06.
* **B06** challenges pure FIFO less than it challenges appointment scheduling: B06 is served before appointed B07, but B06 is older and higher priority.
* **B01’s resumptions** challenge pure preemptive priority and pure SPT, but they are explainable if the dispatch policy includes resumption costs or a “finish interrupted work” rule.

**QC**

Supporting cases:

* **C02** at 10:10 supports SPT because C01, the oldest job, is skipped and C02 has the shortest visible estimate among the clearly visible initial jobs.
* **C03** at 10:18 also supports SPT against FIFO, since C01 is still waiting.
* **C01** being delayed until 11:40 supports the idea that long jobs are penalized by an SPT-like rule.
* **C06** at 11:34 supports SPT because it is the shortest remaining job.
* **C05** at 10:54 supports an SLA/priority exception, but only reactively: it is selected after its 10:50 deadline has already passed.

Challenging cases:

* **C04** at 10:32 contradicts pure SPT because C06, est6, is already visible. This needs a batching, aging, or earlier-dispatch-decision explanation.
* **C05** contradicts strict priority and proactive deadline control because it is ignored at 10:18 and 10:32 despite priority 1 and a 10:50 deadline.
* **C07** contradicts proactive deadline control. At 11:40, starting C07 would likely finish it before 12:00; instead C01 is selected, making C07 late.
* **C01** before C07 at 11:40 contradicts SPT, priority, and earliest-deadline-first. This requires either aging/FIFO, hidden ineligibility, or a policy that reacts only after a deadline is already missed.

**QD**

Supporting cases:

* **D05** strongly supports skill-based routing. It requires firmware+electrical and is explicitly noted as serviceable only by D-F.
* **D07** also supports skill-based routing. Mechanical+electrical work can only be fully handled by D-X among the listed QD resources.
* **D06** supports appointment scheduling: D-X starts it at 11:30, and the resource appears effectively reserved for it.
* **D10** supports availability-aware skill routing because it is handled by D-X while D-F is still unavailable until 12:20.
* **D01, D04, D08, and D09** show a local mechanical stream on D-M, mostly FIFO with a later priority-compatible choice at 11:35.

Challenging cases:

* **D04** before **D08** contradicts pure priority and SPT. It can be explained if D04 was assigned before D08 became visible, but that requires an unobserved dispatch-decision timestamp.
* **D-F idle 11:36-12:00** is not fully explained by the recorded meeting alone. D05 cannot finish before the 12:00 meeting from 11:36, but D10 could. This likely requires a meeting buffer, preassignment of D10 to D-X, or unrecorded unavailability.
* **D05** waits a long time despite being p2 and uniquely D-F-qualified. The delay is partly explained by D-F serving D02 and D03 and then attending the meeting, but it contradicts a scarce-skill-first rule.
* **D10** waits behind D07 on D-X despite priority 1 and shorter work. This is explainable under unique-skill routing for D07 or local FIFO, but not under pure priority.

# 6. Additional event data needed

Priority collection plan:

1. **Queue-entry time and dispatch-visible time, separate from scan time.**
   This is essential for borderline cases such as A05 around 08:20, C05 around 10:10, and B05 around 09:48. The current arrival timestamp is not enough because scans may be delayed.

2. **Actual dispatch-decision timestamp.**
   Service start is not necessarily the decision time. This field would test whether D04 was assigned before D08 arrived, whether B03 was reassigned before B05 was visible, and whether QC decisions were pre-batched at 10:10.

3. **Eligibility-set snapshot at every dispatch decision.**
   For each decision, record all jobs the dispatcher could see, their current priority, appointment status, deadline slack, skill eligibility, and whether they were blocked. This would prevent retrospective inference from incomplete waiting sets.

4. **Resource state timeline.**
   Needed fields: available, busy, calibrated, meeting, reserved, on break, blocked by setup, and unavailable-with-reason. This is especially important for A1’s calibration, C1’s prior job, D-X’s apparent reservation before D06, and D-F’s idle period before the 12:00 meeting.

5. **Appointment tolerance and appointment policy.**
   Record earliest-start time, target time, latest-acceptable start, whether early starts are allowed, and whether resources may idle to protect appointments. This would distinguish QA’s soft appointment handling from QD’s stronger reservation behavior and QB’s weak appointment behavior.

6. **Current estimated remaining processing time.**
   For unstarted jobs, record the estimate visible at dispatch time, not only the original `est_min`. For interrupted jobs, record remaining estimate after each segment. This is crucial for testing SPT in QB and QC.

7. **Preemption reason and resumption policy.**
   For every stop, record whether the stop was caused by priority escalation, appointment reservation, resource interruption, dispatcher override, missing information, or planned split work. Also record whether interrupted work retains its queue position.

8. **Priority semantics and escalation history.**
   Record whether priority is static or dynamic, who changed it, and whether priority 1 always means immediate dispatch or only higher ranking at the next ordinary decision. This matters for A08, B05, C05, D08, and D10.

9. **Deadline/SLA state at decision time.**
   Needed fields: deadline, predicted completion if selected now, predicted completion if not selected now, late flag, at-risk flag, and SLA escalation threshold. This would clarify whether QC is reactive-late, proactive-deadline, or only accidentally deadline-aware.

10. **Skill matrix version and job-skill decomposition.**
    For QD, record whether a job requires all listed skills simultaneously, whether partial qualification is allowed, and whether a resource is uniquely qualified. This would confirm D05-to-D-F and D07-to-D-X routing.

11. **Policy version and dispatcher override code.**
    A field such as `policy_version`, `rule_id`, `override_flag`, and `override_reason` would separate documented rules from manual exceptions. This is critical for QB’s “dispatcher reassignment” and QC’s possible SLA-triggered changes.

12. **Assignment-created time versus service-start time.**
    This is different from the dispatch-decision timestamp if a job can be reserved for a future resource or appointment. It would explain D-X waiting for D06 and possibly QA’s handling of appointments.

# 7. Final synthesis

QA is the most identifiable queue as a dispatch discipline: the 08:20 A03 episode is hard to explain without appointment-aware dispatching, and the remaining ordinary jobs mostly follow FIFO once calibration and appointment timing are considered.

QC is the least identifiable. Its sequence contains real SPT evidence, but also major contradictions: C04 before shorter C06, C05 ignored until after its deadline, and C01 before at-risk C07. Several hybrids remain plausible: SPT with batching, SPT with aging, reactive SLA escalation, or undocumented holds.

QD is the clearest case of skill-based routing, but not of a simple priority rule. Skill eligibility and appointment reservation explain much of the pattern, while local ordering still looks hybrid and partly undocumented.

The most dangerous claimed policy to infer from these observations alone would be **pure priority or preemptive priority**. Several high-priority jobs are served early, but many are not: A08 waits behind lower-priority jobs, B05 is skipped at 09:48, C05 is ignored until after its deadline, D08 waits behind D04, and D10 waits behind D07. Treating the system as reliably priority-driven would falsely reassure management that urgent jobs are protected, while the evidence actually points to appointments, skill constraints, resumption costs, batching, and manual exceptions.
