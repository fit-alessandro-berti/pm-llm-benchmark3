# 1. Assumptions and notation

- **A1 — Observation and arrival conventions.** The analysis window is \([08{:}00,14{:}00]\). S1 arrivals use the external-arrival timestamps. S2 queue entry is the recorded S1 completion. No arrivals before 08:00 or activity completions after 14:00 are supplied.
- **A2 — Rounding.** Every reported operational or arrival time is treated as rounded to five minutes, with actual time in approximately \([t-2.5,t+2.5)\) minutes. The cutoff at 14:00 is treated as exact. Consequently, an observed difference of \(d\) minutes has the elementary envelope \([\max(0,d-5),d+5]\). These are **outer envelopes, not confidence intervals**; shared timestamps and resource-order constraints can tighten them.
- **A3 — Simultaneous rounded events.** Intervals are represented as \([start,end)\). At a timestamp, departures are processed before arrivals, so a resource released at \(t\) can accept a new case at \(t\). The WIP table uses the state **after** all events at a grid time. A snapshot “at 14:00” means immediately before censoring activities at the cutoff.
- **A4 — Capacity.** S1 and S2 each normally have two single-task resources. The primary calculation enforces non-overlap on each named resource. Because all logged endpoints are rounded, this is a modeling constraint, not proof that no undocumented assistance occurred. The supplied data show no decisive resource conflict.
- **A5 — Missing starts.** A missing start is not replaced silently. Its bounds use queue entry, completion, the preceding and following activity on the same resource, the other station, and nonnegative service. The central imputations below are explicitly chosen, valid five-minute-grid values—not estimated observations.
- **A6 — Censoring.** For an OPEN activity, service elapsed by 14:00 is integrated into within-window busy time and WIP, but it is **not** treated as final service. Waiting censored at 14:00 is a lower bound on eventual queue waiting. No service continuing after 14:00 is imputed.
- **A7 — Logical event order.** S1 completion precedes S2 entry; S2 start precedes S2 completion. No mandatory station is bypassed.
- **A8 — Maintenance.** C2 is unavailable for the nominal 15-minute interval 10:55–11:10. Its utilization denominator is therefore \(2(360)-15=705\) resource-minutes; station busy-time remains 565 minutes in the central reconstruction.
- **A9 — No FIFO assumption.** A case can wait while another case uses an available resource. The process-mining evidence does not establish FCFS, dispatching by priority, or batching.
- **A10 — Interpretation of ranges.** “Central” is a reproducible reference reconstruction. “Outer rounding envelope” means applying the five-minute difference uncertainty separately to observed durations. “Resource-constrained envelope” additionally imposes event order and availability. Neither envelope is a probability statement.
- **A11 — Little’s Law.** A formal station-level check requires approximately stationary flow, a stable WIP definition, and arrival/departure counts that adequately describe the boundary-crossing population. The short, strongly time-varying blocks here do not meet that standard; an algebraic diagnostic is not treated as validation.

Notation:

- \(Q_s(t)\): cases waiting for station \(s\).
- \(B_s(t)\): cases in active service at station \(s\).
- \(L_s(t)=Q_s(t)+B_s(t)\): station WIP.
- \(L(t)=L_1(t)+L_2(t)\): whole-system WIP among cases that have arrived, including cases waiting at S1.
- “Elapsed” refers to time observed before the cutoff; “final” would require completion data that are absent for OPEN cases.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

For case \(i\), station \(s\), and corresponding timestamps:

\[
W_{is}=start_{is}-entry_{is},
\]

\[
D_{is}=end_{is}-start_{is},
\]

\[
T_{is}=end_{is}-entry_{is}=W_{is}+D_{is}.
\]

Here \(entry_{i1}=arrival_{i1}\) and \(entry_{i2}=end_{i1}\).

For a completed case:

\[
T_i=end_{i2}-arrival_{i1}
    =T_{i1}+W_{i2}+D_{i2}.
\]

For an unfinished case at 14:00, \(T_i(14{:}00)=14{:}00-arrival_{i1}\) is **elapsed case time**, not completed-case sojourn.

The missing-start constraints used are:

\[
start_{i,s}\in
[\text{queue entry}_i,\ \text{completion}_i]
\cap
[\text{preceding same-resource end},\ \text{following same-resource start}],
\]

intersected with five-minute rounding uncertainty where appropriate. These constraints imply:

| Missing start | Resource-order interval | Effective imputation bounds | Central choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| P07, S1, I1 | I1 finishes P05 at 09:25; next I1 activity starts at 10:05 | 09:27.5–09:57.5, or reported-grid 09:30–09:55 | 09:45 |
| P15, S1, I1 | I1 finishes P13 at 11:40; completion is 12:15 | 11:37.5–12:12.5, or grid 11:40–12:10 | 11:55 |
| P05, S2, C1 | C1 finishes P03 at 09:45; completion is 10:15 | 09:47.5–10:12.5, or grid 09:50–10:10 | 09:50 |
| P11, S2, C2 | C2 finishes P08 at 12:00; completion is 12:25 | 11:57.5–12:22.5, or grid 12:00–12:20 | 12:00 |

The central S1 missing services are consequently 15 and 20 minutes; the central S2 missing services are both 25 minutes. These choices are reference imputations, not claims about the actual starts.

For interval \(I=[a,b)\), of length \(H\) minutes:

\[
ArrivalRate(I)=\frac{A(I)}{H/60},
\qquad
DepartureRate(I)=\frac{D(I)}{H/60}.
\]

For busy time:

\[
Busy_s(I)=\int_a^b B_s(t)\,dt.
\]

For two resources:

\[
U_s(I)=\frac{Busy_s(I)}{2H},
\]

using \(705\) rather than \(720\) for S2’s C2-adjusted capacity over the full window.

Time-average WIP is:

\[
\overline L_s(I)=\frac{1}{H}\int_a^b L_s(t)\,dt.
\]

Equivalently, busy time is the sum of clipped service intervals. A service interval extending beyond the observation window contributes only its intersection with the window.

The code evaluates every reported or imputed event on a five-minute grid, with the event-order convention in A3. It also calculates case-based WIP areas as an internal consistency check.

A Little’s-Law diagnostic would be:

\[
\overline L_s(I)\approx\lambda_s(I)\,\overline W_s(I),
\]

but is not accepted here as a validation unless the interval is demonstrably approximately stationary. Observed arrival and departure counts must include boundary-crossing cases appropriately; selecting only completed cases would bias the check.
