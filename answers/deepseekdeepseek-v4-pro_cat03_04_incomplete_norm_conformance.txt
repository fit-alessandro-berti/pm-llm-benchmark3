## 1. Conformance semantics  
The classification of observed deviations must respect the norm’s intended scope, the abstraction level at which each rule is expressed, and the openworld/closedworld boundaries stated in the norm.

| Deviation class | Definition | Openworld vs. closedworld influence |
|---|---|---|
| **Forbidden behavior** | An observed relation that directly matches a norm entry marked *forbidden*. The norm is **closed** for these relations; any occurrence, regardless of frequency, is a violation. | Closed for the specific relation; no exception can legitimise it. |
| **Missing mandatory behavior** | A **mandatory** relation is absent from the log under the exact conditions that the norm’s scope demands. The norm is closedworld within the stated scope: if the scope applies, the relation must exist. However, the **abstraction** of the observation (e.g., directlyfollows vs. eventual) must be matched to the rule. | Closed within scope; but if the scope is not clearly fulfilled (e.g., “standard Order”), the conclusion weakens. |
| **Unexpected but unregulated behavior** | A relation that is neither mandatory nor forbidden, nor covered by a typical expectation. | **Typicalpattern absence** | A **typical** relation is missing in a significant number of cases, but the norm itself anticipates exceptions. | **Likely dataquality error** | An observed relation that contradicts technical plausibility (e.g., identifier reuse) or is flagged by domain experts as a join artefact. Even if the relation violates a *forbidden* rule, contamination by bad data must be ruled out before drawing a process violation conclusion. | Both open and closed rules are affected; data quality must be verified first. |
| **Abstraction mismatch** | The norm expresses a business rule at a higher level (e.g., “eventually validated”) while the observation reports a stricter derived relation (directlyfollows). Absence of the latter does **not** automatically imply noncompliance if the higherlevel rule could still be satisfied. | Openworld for the higherlevel rule; the lowerlevel relation is only a proxy. |
| **Insufficient evidence** | The observation is ambiguous (e.g., a missing mandatory link could be legitimate unapplied cash that is not labelled). ## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family  

### OCDFG  
**What it can establish:** Directlyfollows relations between lifecycle events on the same object, suitable for detecting forbidden sequences (e.g., CancelDispatch) and checking whether mandatory ordering patterns are present at the granularity of adjacent events.  
**What it cannot establish:** It cannot confirm that an *eventually expected* activity (e.g., Validate Order) occurs if other events intervene, because OCDFG only reports immediate successors. It also cannot distinguish concurrency from genuine nonoccurrence, nor does it guarantee that a missing directlyfollows means the later event never happens.  
**Counts and scopes:** For N1, 98 of 100 orders show CreateValidate. The two missing occurrences may be the three orders that passed through Manual Compliance Review – validation could occur after review, but the directlyfollows is broken. The norm itself admits this approximation, so the absence is an abstraction mismatch rather than a certain violation. For N3, any CancelDispatch directlyfollows is unambiguous proof of the forbidden path. For N4, 198 of 200 externally shipped packages show PackDispatch; the two missing may stem from an unrecorded pack event or a correlation lapse – the directlyfollows gap raises an alarm, but the underlying mandatory rule (packing must happen) may still be satisfied. The severe findings (N3, N4) are robust at the DFG level; N1 requires lifecyclelevel verification.  
**Danger of misreading “eventually expected” as “directlyfollows”:** The mandatory rule “every Order must eventually be validated” is approximated by the directlyfollows edge only for the common case. Treating a missing DFG edge as a mandatory violation without checking the whole lifecycle would generate false positives. Hence the N1 deviation is rated as abstraction mismatch, not missing mandatory.

### OTG  
**What it can establish:** Objecttoobject relationships (interaction, cobirth, codeath) across the entire event log. It can verify that certain pairs of objects must or must not cooccur, regardless of the event type that connects them.  
**What it cannot establish:** It does not reveal *which event* created the relationship; thus violation of a mandatory rule tied to a specific interaction (“Add Item must interact with Order”) can only be inferred if the edge is completely absent. An edge present once may satisfy the rule even if a particular event contribution is missing – but the norm T1T2 are phrased at the interaction level. Here, all mandatory OTG edges are observed (ItemOrder weight 1450, PackageDelivery weight 200) except for the forbidden cobirth. The one OrderPayment cobirth (T6) is a clear forbidden occurrence, robustly identified by OTG. The 7 PackageDelivery codeath edges are unregulated; the norm says nothing about codeath, so they are permitted.  
**Counts and scopes:** The cobirth count of 1 is sufficient evidence of a violation; no frequency threshold is mentioned. The OTG does not explain why two Add Item events missing Order link appear only in ETOT – the OTG edge (Order, Item) exists globally, so from the OTG perspective alone no violation would be flagged. This illustrates that conformance must be checked at the right model family: ETOT is required for perevent object compliance.

### ET–OT  
**What it can establish:** Direct mapping of event types to the objects they involve. It is the most precise model for checking whether a specific event type always (or never) touches certain object types.  
**What it cannot establish:** It cannot enforce ordering dependencies between event types, nor can it distinguish legitimate exceptions (e.g., unapplied cash) if the exception is not explicitly recorded as a distinct event type.  
**Counts and scopes:** Add ItemOrder with 1448 out of 1450 events violates E2 (mandatory) directly. Dispatch PackageDelivery with 198 out of 200 events violates E6, and Record PaymentInvoice with 92 out of 93 violates E8 – both missing one case. The Sensor HeartbeatInvoice edge (1) is a direct violation of E10 (forbidden). These are unambiguous at the ETOT level. The Manual Compliance Review event touching Customer is not forbidden; T4 only states OrderCustomer is unspecified, and the link here is incidental.  
**Conformance at one abstraction, suspicious at another:** The two Add Item events missing an Order cannot be detected by OTG alone (because the global ObjectOrderItem interaction exists). This demonstrates that a process may appear compliant in a coarser view while having local violations. Similarly, two missing DispatchDelivery edges might be hidden in OTG if PackageDelivery interactions are created via other events, but ETOT exposes them directly.

## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch  
The code runs standard discovery and conformance checks, storing raw diagnostics, then applies a semanticclassification layer using external metadata. The mandatory/typical/forbidden statuses are maintained outside the bare graph structures (in dictionaries) to reflect the normative interpretation.

```python
import pm4py

# --- Discovery ---
ocel = ...  # the observed object-centric event log

ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg   = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot  = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# --- Normative metadata (must be maintained externally) ---
norm_ocdfg = {
    ('Order', 'Create Order', 'Validate Order'): 'mandatory',   # N1
    ('Order', 'Validate Order', 'Close Order'): 'typical',      # N2
    ('Order', 'Cancel Order', 'Dispatch Package'): 'forbidden', # N3
    ('Package', 'Pack Package', 'Dispatch Package'): 'mandatory',# N4
    ('Invoice', 'Issue Invoice', 'Record Payment'): 'typical',   # N5
    ('Order', 'Reopen Order', 'Close Order'): 'unspecified'      # N6
}
norm_otg = {
    ('Order', 'object_interaction', 'Item'): 'mandatory',       # T1
    ('Package', 'object_interaction', 'Delivery'): 'mandatory', # T2
    ('Invoice', 'object_interaction', 'Payment'): 'typical',    # T3
    ('Order', 'object_interaction', 'Customer'): 'unspecified', # T4
    ('Order', 'object_interaction', 'RiskCase'): 'unspecified', # T5
    ('Order', 'object_cobirth', 'Payment'): 'forbidden'         # T6
}
norm_etot = {
    ('Create Order', 'Order'): 'mandatory',                # E1
    ('Add Item', 'Order'): 'mandatory',                    # E2
    ('Add Item', 'Item'): 'mandatory',                     # E3
    ('Pack Package', 'Package'): 'mandatory',              # E4
    ('Dispatch Package', 'Package'): 'mandatory',          # E5
    ('Dispatch Package', 'Delivery'): 'mandatory',          # E6
    ('Record Payment', 'Payment'): 'mandatory',            # E7
    ('Record Payment', 'Invoice'): 'mandatory',            # E8
    ('Manual Compliance Review', 'RiskCase'): 'unspecified',# E9
    ('Sensor Heartbeat', 'Invoice'): 'forbidden'           # E10
}

# --- Raw conformance diagnostics ---
diag_ocdfg = pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(ocel, ocdfg, norm_ocdfg)
diag_otg   = pm4py.conformance_otg(ocel, otg, norm_otg)
diag_etot  = pm4py.conformance_etot(ocel, etot, norm_etot)

# --- Semantic classification layer (preserves raw output) ---
def classify_diagnostics(raw_diags, norm_dict):
    """Attach semantic class to each deviation based on normative status."""
    findings = []
    for diag in raw_diags:
        edge = diag['edge']
        status = norm_dict.get(edge, 'unspecified')
        if status == 'forbidden' and diag['observed']:
            diag['semantic_class'] = 'forbidden_behavior'
            diag['severity'] = 'high'
        elif status == 'mandatory' and diag['missing']:
            # check for abstraction mismatch placeholder
            diag['semantic_class'] = 'missing_mandatory'
            diag['severity'] = 'high'
        elif status == 'typical' and diag['frequency_below_threshold']:
            diag['semantic_class'] = 'typical_absence'
            diag['severity'] = 'low'
        else:
            diag['semantic_class'] = 'unregulated_or_benign'
            diag['severity'] = 'info'
        findings.append(diag)
    return findings

classified_ocdfg = classify_diagnostics(diag_ocdfg, norm_ocdfg)
classified_otg   = classify_diagnostics(diag_otg, norm_otg)
classified_etot  = classify_diagnostics(diag_etot, norm_etot)
```

- For the two Dispatch Package events lacking Delivery (Rank 3): Quarantine the shipments in the tracking system until Delivery object linkage is confirmed or created.

- Repair the Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice link by correcting the join logic and removing the polluted edge.  
- For the unlinked Record Payment (Rank 4): if confirmed as unapplied cash, relabel the event type and update the E8 exception list; otherwise, retroactively link to the correct Invoice.

- **Robust conclusions:** The two orders with CancelDispatch (N3), the one OrderPayment cobirth (T6), and the Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice link (E10) are unambiguously forbidden; their presence, regardless of frequency, constitutes a violation. The missing mandatory edges E6 (DispatchDelivery) and E2 (Add ItemOrder) are robustly identified from ETOT and indicate noncompliance under the current norm, unless dataquality remedies reveal hidden correlations.  
- **Depend on norm’s incompleteness:** Whether the 3 missing CreateValidate DFG edges (N1) amount to noncompliance depends entirely on whether those orders eventually reached validation – a fact the norm does not clarify as the primary rule. The significance of the 18 invoices without payment (N5) hinges on an undefined operational boundary for “typical”.  
- **Depend on suspected dataquality issues:** The Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice violation is almost certainly a join artefact; the unlinked Record Payment (E8) may be legitimate unapplied cash. Both require data forensics before drawing processlevel conclusions.  
- **Cannot be decided from graph summaries alone:** Graphderived metrics cannot confirm eventual order validation, genuine unpacked dispatching, or the correctness of unappliedcash exceptions. Lifecycle state, event attributes, and operational logs are needed to resolve these.
