## 1. Normalization Assumptions

### Interval model and clock-offset direction

All intervals below are feasible-time bounds, not confidence intervals or probability distributions.

For a recorded timestamp with displayed-clock interval \(R=[r_L,r_U]\) and clock offset:

\[
\text{offset} = \text{recorded clock} - \text{true clock} \in [a,b]
\]

the feasible true-time interval is:

\[
T=[r_L-b,\; r_U-a]
\]

Timestamp-resolution uncertainty is applied first, then the clock-offset bound is applied.

The primary analysis treats offsets as potentially varying between events because no per-event offset correlation or continuous telemetry is supplied. That is conservative. If a common system offset were proven stable, some same-system durations would narrow because the common offset partly cancels; it does not materially change the baseline classifications below.

### Resolution semantics used

| System | Recorded-clock interval before clock correction |
|---|---|
| HUB | displayed second ±0.5 s |
| OPS-BER | displayed minute through `:59.999` |
| SENSOR | displayed value ±5 s because of nearest-10-second rounding |
| VENDOR-NY | displayed minute through `:59.999` |
| LEGACY-BER | displayed minute through `:59.999`, separately for each DST fold |
| MOBILE-LON | displayed minute ±30 s because of nearest-minute rounding |

The `:59.999` endpoints are practical representations of a floor-to-minute upper bound; they should be read as just before the next minute.

### Time zones

* Berlin timestamps on 2026-06-01 use CEST, UTC+02:00.
* New York timestamps on 2026-06-01 use EDT, UTC-04:00.
* London timestamps on 2026-06-01 use BST, UTC+01:00.
* All reported normalized times are UTC and end in `Z`.

### Duration construction

For events \(A \in [A_L,A_U]\) and \(B \in [B_L,B_U]\), the unconstrained duration \(B-A\) is:

\[
[B_L-A_U,\;B_U-A_L]
\]

For unions of event intervals, duration is computed as the union of pairwise duration intervals. I do not replace a union with a convex hull unless the intervals overlap.

Where semantic causality is adopted, such as “Alert_Received must not be after Investigation_Start,” negative durations are excluded and the lower duration bound is truncated at zero. This is an assumption, not a fact inferred from a timestamp alone.

### Case-specific assumptions

* **T-404:** I treat the signed work record as evidence that actual investigation work lasted 18–25 minutes from `Investigation_Start` to `Investigation_Complete`. Therefore:

  \[
  Start = Investigation\_Complete - [18,25]\text{ minutes}
  \]

  If the record instead describes administrative elapsed time or a different lifecycle state, the inferred-start findings become indeterminate.

* **T-405:** I treat the supplied ingestion sequence as a hard ordering constraint for fold selection: `Alert_Received < Investigation_Start < Containment_Complete`. It eliminates all but one fold combination. It does not make the surviving minute-level records exact. If ingestion order is not reliable as evidence of occurrence order, T-405 should be treated as DST-ambiguous and its duration conclusions become weaker.

* **T-406:** Primary analysis interprets “entered up to five minutes late” as uncertainty about occurrence time, not merely record time. The displayed mobile record is a record-time interval, and the actual occurrence may precede it by 0–300 s:

  \[
  T_{occurrence}=T_{record}-[0,300]\text{ s}
  \]

  I also show the alternative “late recording only” interpretation in sensitivity analysis. That alternative does not shift the estimated occurrence earlier.

* **T-407:** `Alert_Sent` is not treated as identical to `Alert_Received`. Therefore, the primary S1 and alert-based profile findings are **INDETERMINATE**. A conditional proxy analysis uses delivery time:

  \[
  Alert\_Received_{proxy}=Alert\_Sent+[0,1200]\text{ s}
  \]

  and, separately, the plausible but unlogged causal assumption that receipt was no later than investigation start.

* **T-409:** The ordinary OPS-BER offset bound is replaced by the stated weak engineering estimate of \([-12,+12]\) minutes. A causal `Alert_Received  Investigation_Start` condition is used only to avoid interpreting an apparent pre-alert start as a meaningful negative response duration. Without that condition, the raw duration range is \([-481.500,1021.499]\) s.

### Baseline decision parameters

* SLA tolerance addition: **0 minutes**.
* Temporal-profile baseline: **zeta = 1**.
* SLA thresholds:
  * S1: 900 s
  * S2: 2700 s
  * S3: 129600 s
* A `CERTAIN_PASS` or `CERTAIN_VIOLATION` is certain only conditional on the stated evidence model, clock bounds, and semantics. It is not proof that the metadata itself is correct.

No midpoint is used for the primary findings.

The primary-indeterminate T-407 alert-based pairs remain indeterminate at every zeta. Under the conditional sent-plus-latency proxy, Alert  Investigation_Start is `PV` for zeta 1–3, while Alert  Containment is `CP` for zeta 1–3.

### Material interpretation changes

* **T-401:** Alert  Investigation_Start becomes a certain profile pass only at zeta=3. Its SLA S1 becomes certain pass with a +2 minute tolerance.
* **T-402:** S1 changes from certain violation to possible violation at +2 minutes. The profile result for Alert  Investigation_Start changes from certain deviation to possible deviation at zeta=2, not to a pass.
* **T-403:** The external alert uncertainty prevents a certain S1 pass until +5 minutes; Alert  Containment becomes profile-compliant at zeta=2.
* **T-404:** The inferred-start uncertainty is decisive. S2 is certain pass with +2 minutes; its profile Investigation_Start  Containment becomes certain pass at zeta=2.
* **T-405:** Once fold order is accepted, increasing zeta to 3 makes Alert  Investigation_Start a certain profile pass. This does not remove the need for a 5-minute SLA grace to make S1 certain pass.
* **T-406:** The semantic treatment of mobile late entry materially changes S2. The occurrence-time model is `PV` at zero tolerance, whereas the record-time-only model is already `CP`.
* **T-408:** The legal S2 and S3 breaches remain certain under all stated tolerances. Its profile deviations disappear only as the statistical band becomes substantially wider: zeta=3 for Investigation_Start  Containment and Containment  Close.
* **T-409:** Better tolerance can make S1 pass, but that is a policy change rather than evidence improvement. Hour-specific clock telemetry would be preferable.

def merge_timestamp_intervals(intervals):
    """Merge overlapping timestamp intervals, preserving disjoint unions."""
    if not intervals:
        return []
    ordered = sorted(intervals, key=lambda x: x[0])
    merged = [list(ordered[0])]
    for lo, hi in ordered[1:]:
        if lo <= merged[-1][1]:
            merged[-1][1] = max(merged[-1][1], hi)
        else:
            merged.append([lo, hi])
    return [tuple(x) for x in merged]


def merge_numeric_intervals(intervals):
    """Merge overlapping numeric duration intervals, preserving disjoint unions."""
    if not intervals:
        return []
    ordered = sorted(intervals, key=lambda x: x[0])
    merged = [list(ordered[0])]
    for lo, hi in ordered[1:]:
        if lo <= merged[-1][1]:
            merged[-1][1] = max(merged[-1][1], hi)
        else:
            merged.append([lo, hi])
    return [tuple(x) for x in merged]


def local_to_utc(local_naive: pd.Timestamp, tz_name: str, fold: int) -> pd.Timestamp:
    """Attach a ZoneInfo timezone and fold, then convert to UTC."""
    dt = local_naive.to_pydatetime().replace(
        tzinfo=ZoneInfo(tz_name),
        fold=fold,
    )
    return pd.Timestamp(dt.astimezone(timezone.utc))


def displayed_clock_bounds(recorded: str, resolution: str):
    """
    Return displayed-clock bounds before clock correction.
    Upper bounds use .999 seconds for floor-to-minute semantics.
    """
    t = pd.Timestamp(recorded)
    if resolution == "exact_second":
        return [(t - pd.Timedelta(seconds=0.5), t + pd.Timedelta(seconds=0.5))]
    if resolution == "floor_minute":
        return [(t, t + pd.Timedelta(seconds=59.999))]
    if resolution == "nearest_10_seconds":
        return [(t - pd.Timedelta(seconds=5), t + pd.Timedelta(seconds=5))]
    if resolution == "nearest_minute":
        return [(t - pd.Timedelta(seconds=30), t + pd.Timedelta(seconds=30))]
    raise ValueError(f"Unknown resolution: {resolution}")


def normalize_record(row) -> list[tuple[pd.Timestamp, pd.Timestamp]]:
    """
    Apply resolution uncertainty first, convert local time to UTC,
    then correct for offset = recorded_clock - true_clock.
    """
    meta = SOURCE[row.system]
    offset_lo = (
        float(row.offset_lo_override)
        if pd.notna(row.offset_lo_override)
        else meta["offset_lo"]
    )
    offset_hi = (
        float(row.offset_hi_override)
        if pd.notna(row.offset_hi_override)
        else meta["offset_hi"]
    )

    recorded_ts = pd.Timestamp(row.recorded)

    # Only the specified LEGACY-BER hour is ambiguous.
    if (
        row.system == "LEGACY-BER"
        and recorded_ts.date().isoformat() == "2025-10-26"
        and recorded_ts.hour == 2
    ):
        folds = [0, 1]
    else:
        folds = [0]

    normalized = []
    for local_lo, local_hi in displayed_clock_bounds(
        row.recorded,
        meta["resolution"],
    ):
        for fold in folds:
            utc_lo_clock = local_to_utc(local_lo, meta["tz"], fold)
            utc_hi_clock = local_to_utc(local_hi, meta["tz"], fold)

            # true = recorded - offset, so lower uses offset_hi and upper uses offset_lo
            true_lo = utc_lo_clock - pd.Timedelta(seconds=offset_hi)
            true_hi = utc_hi_clock - pd.Timedelta(seconds=offset_lo)
            normalized.append((true_lo, true_hi))

    return merge_timestamp_intervals(normalized)

def duration_intervals(left, right, causal=False):
    """
    Return all feasible (right - left) duration intervals in seconds.
    If causal=True, enforce left <= right and truncate the lower duration at zero.
    """
    result = []
    for a_lo, a_hi in left:
        for b_lo, b_hi in right:
            lo = (b_lo - a_hi).total_seconds()
            hi = (b_hi - a_lo).total_seconds()

            if causal:
                if hi < 0:
                    continue
                lo = max(0.0, lo)

            if lo <= hi:
                result.append((lo, hi))
    return merge_numeric_intervals(result)


def classify_sla_max(duration_intervals_seconds, maximum_seconds):
    if not duration_intervals_seconds:
        return "INDETERMINATE"
    if all(hi <= maximum_seconds for _, hi in duration_intervals_seconds):
        return "CERTAIN_PASS"
    if all(lo > maximum_seconds for lo, _ in duration_intervals_seconds):
        return "CERTAIN_VIOLATION"
    return "POSSIBLE_VIOLATION"


def classify_profile_band(duration_intervals_seconds, lower, upper):
    if not duration_intervals_seconds:
        return "INDETERMINATE"

    all_inside = all(lo >= lower and hi <= upper for lo, hi in duration_intervals_seconds)
    no_feasible_pass = all(hi < lower or lo > upper for lo, hi in duration_intervals_seconds)

    if all_inside:
        return "CERTAIN_PASS"
    if no_feasible_pass:
        return "CERTAIN_VIOLATION"
    return "POSSIBLE_VIOLATION"


def ts_text(ts):
    return ts.isoformat().replace("+00:00", "Z")


def timestamp_interval_text(intervals):
    return " U ".join(f"[{ts_text(lo)}, {ts_text(hi)}]" for lo, hi in intervals)


def duration_interval_text(intervals):
    return " U ".join(f"[{lo:.3f}, {hi:.3f}] s" for lo, hi in intervals)

    ("T-407", "Alert_Received", "Investigation_Start"),
    ("T-409", "Alert_Received", "Investigation_Start"),

def duration_for(case_id, left_activity, right_activity):
    left = events.get((case_id, left_activity))
    right = events.get((case_id, right_activity))
    if left is None or right is None:
        return []
    causal = (case_id, left_activity, right_activity) in causal_duration_pairs
    return duration_intervals(left, right, causal=causal)

def lossy_midpoint(intervals):
    """
    Intentionally lossy: midpoint of the full feasible hull.
    This is only for PM4Py's point-timestamp API comparison.
    """
    lo = min(x[0] for x in intervals)
    hi = max(x[1] for x in intervals)
    return lo + (hi - lo) / 2

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency

The following describes the minimum type of additional evidence needed to turn each baseline `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION` or `INDETERMINATE` result into a certain result. “Certain” could mean certain pass or certain violation; the required evidence must narrow the duration wholly to one side of the relevant boundary.

| Findings needing resolution | Minimum additional evidence | Evidence type | Why it is sufficient |
|---|---|---|---|
| T-401 S1; T-401 Alert  Investigation_Start profile | Event-level OPS-BER offset at the start instant, or an independently synchronized start timestamp with second-level resolution | Stronger synchronization evidence | The present range [718.500, 991.499] s straddles both 780 s and 900 s. A tighter relative alert/start bound would settle both. |
| T-402 S1 under added tolerance; T-402 S2; T-402 Investigation_Start  Containment profile | Start timestamp with sub-minute resolution plus an hour-specific offset measurement | Stronger synchronization evidence | T-402 S1 is certainly violated at zero tolerance, but becomes possible at +2 min. S2 needs the start interval narrowed around the 45-minute boundary. |
| T-402 Alert  Investigation_Start at zeta 2 or 3 | Same OPS start synchronization evidence | Stronger synchronization evidence | At zeta 2 and 3, part of the start-duration interval is inside the widened profile band and part is outside. |
| T-403 S1; T-403 alert-based profile pairs | Vendor-side event receipt/creation evidence tied to UTC, with a documented offset or signed server timestamp | Stronger synchronization evidence | The broad 7-minute vendor alert interval is the dominant uncertainty. HUB timestamps are already sufficiently precise. |
| T-404 S1, S2; inferred-start profile pairs | Original investigation-start event, or a signed record explicitly identifying actual start and actual completion times; alternatively a narrower signed work duration | Missing activity evidence and duration evidence | The inferred start is only as good as the semantic meaning and [18,25]-minute range of the work record. |
| T-405 S1; Alert  Investigation_Start profile | Original UTC timestamp or stored DST fold plus offset telemetry / NTP state for the LEGACY clock | DST-fold, synchronization, and provenance evidence | Ingestion ordering resolves the fold assignment only under a semantic assumption. | T-406 S1; S2; Alert  Investigation_Start and Investigation_Start  Containment profiles | A server-side start event, a mobile submission receipt time, or a policy-certified statement whether the user-entered timestamp denotes occurrence time versus entry time | Semantic evidence plus stronger synchronization evidence | S2 is `PV` only under the occurrence-earlier-by-up-to-5-minutes interpretation. Record-time-only treatment makes S2 a certain pass. |
| T-407 S1 and both alert-based profile pairs | Receipt acknowledgement, delivery confirmation tied to the same incident, or a durable message identifier proving that Alert_Sent led to Alert_Received at a known time | Missing activity evidence and semantic evidence | Delivery latency is not proof of receipt, and Alert_Sent is not definitionally Alert_Received. ## 7. Governance Recommendation

1. **Store canonical UTC alongside original values.**  
   Persist:
   * normalized UTC instant,
   * raw displayed timestamp text,
   * original IANA time zone,
   * UTC offset at capture,
   * DST fold where applicable,
   * source system identifier.

   Never overwrite raw timestamps with normalized values.

2. **Retain timestamp-resolution metadata.**  
   Store whether a value is exact-to-second, floor-to-minute, nearest-minute, nearest-10-seconds, user-entered, inferred, or server-generated. A value displayed as `10:14` should not be treated operationally as `10:14:00`.

3. **Capture event-level clock health and offset telemetry.**  
   Record NTP/PTP offset, synchronization state, stratum/source, uncertainty, and monotonic-clock diagnostics near each event. Daily diagnostics are insufficient when the SLA boundary is minutes or seconds away, as shown by T-409.

4. **Use authoritative server-side lifecycle events.**  
   Record explicit:
   * `Alert_Sent`,
   * `Alert_Delivered`,
   * `Alert_Received`,
   * `Investigation_Start`,
   * `Investigation_Complete`,
   * `Containment_Start`,
   * `Containment_Complete`,
   * `Close_Incident`.

   Each should have a documented business meaning. “Started work,” “completed investigation,” and “entered a timestamp later” are not interchangeable.

5. **Require start/complete semantics and provenance.**  
   A work record used to infer a start time should identify:
   * which lifecycle states it measures,
   * whether duration is actual work, wall-clock elapsed time, or administrative time,
   * signer identity,
   * record creation time,
   * immutable source reference.

6. **Persist DST fold explicitly.**  
   For local timestamps in repeated hours, store IANA zone plus fold (`0` or `1`) or an explicit UTC offset. Do not rely on ingestion order as the permanent mechanism for resolving a DST ambiguity.

7. **Use causal identifiers for distributed alerts.**  
   Alert messages should carry immutable IDs that link sent, delivery, receipt, acknowledgement, escalation, and investigation records. This is necessary to distinguish T-407-style `Alert_Sent` evidence from actual `Alert_Received` evidence.

8. **Separate occurrence time from record-entry time.**  
   Mobile and manually entered records should have at least:
   * asserted occurrence time,
   * entry/submission time,
   * server receipt time,
   * editor identity,
   * reason for backdating or delayed entry.

   Policy should state which time controls SLA measurement.

9. **Retain uncertainty rather than coercing timestamps to points.**  
   Downstream conformance systems should support interval or bounded-uncertainty timestamps, alternative DST folds, and provenance labels. Point-only process-mining logs may be useful for exploratory diagnostics, but they should be labeled as lossy approximations.

10. **Adopt an enforcement policy for interval overlap.**  
    The organization should decide explicitly whether:
    * only `CERTAIN_VIOLATION` triggers an automatic legal/SLA breach;
    * any `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION` triggers a manual investigation;
    * a conservative contractual policy treats any feasible violation as a breach; or
    * a burden-of-proof policy requires evidence that the deadline was exceeded.

    A recommended operational policy is:
    * `CERTAIN_VIOLATION`: automatic breach workflow;
    * `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION`: evidence-preservation and manual review;
    * `INDETERMINATE`: data-quality remediation and no conclusive enforcement absent further evidence;
    * `CERTAIN_PASS`: pass only conditional on documented source bounds and semantics.
