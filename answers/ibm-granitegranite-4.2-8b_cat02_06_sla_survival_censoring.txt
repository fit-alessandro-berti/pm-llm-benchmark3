  Section 4: analysis plan

  Compare methods:

  - KM: ignores competing risks (overestimates P(E>t) if competing events), not suitable for competing risks

  - Aalen-Johansen (AJ): multi-state natural history, can handle competing risks as absorbing states

  - Cumulative incidence (CIF): for competing risks, correct marginal estimate of P(first event=E and time<=t)

  - Cause-specific hazards (CSH): interpretable per cause, but not for marginal risk

# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

- Time origin t=0 at case "Submit" event.

- Horizon t=72 hours (SLA deadline; probability estimands evaluate status at or before this time).

- Event of interest E: "Resolve being the first internal terminal outcome" (i.e., case reaches Resolve without having reached Withdraw, Reject, or Transfer first).

- Competing events C: Withdraw, Reject, Transfer (terminal outcomes other than Resolve); once any competing event occurs, Resolve cannot be the first terminal outcome.

- Censoring: Open cases (observed_status='open') are right-censored at their age at cutoff (duration_or_age_h), meaning the true Resolve time is unknown but not yet observed if still open.

- Estimand 1 (primary): Marginal probability that the first terminal outcome is Resolve and occurs after 72 hours, i.e., P(E time > 72h | first event = E). Equivalently, 1  CIF_E(72h), where CIF_E(t) = P(first event = Resolve and Resolve time  t). This directly answers "what fraction of cases are expected not to successfully resolve within the SLA", including cases that terminate early (Withdraw/Reject/Transfer) and censored open cases.

- Estimand 2 (secondary): Cause-specific incidence of Resolve occurring after 72 hours among cases where Resolve is the first terminal outcome, i.e., P(first event = Resolve) × P(E time > 72h | first event = Resolve). This isolates process-delay risk for resolving cases, excluding terminations, useful for identifying bottlenecks in Resolve paths.

- Time contributions:

  - Resolved at 72h (e.g., S01: 18h): First event is Resolve at 72h  contributes to CIF_E(72h), not to Estimand 1 (since E time 72h).

  - Resolved at >72h (e.g., S03: 76h): First event Resolve at >72h  contributes to Estimand 1 (E time >72h).

  - Withdrawn/Rejected at 72h (e.g., S06: 27h withdraw): First event is competing (Withdraw), so Resolve cannot be first  contributes 0 to Estimand 2 (Resolve first), and contributes to Estimand 1 via 1CIF (since "not Resolve by SLA" includes terminations), which is correct (SLA is for successful completion).

  - Transferred at 72h (e.g., S08: 68h): First event likely Transfer (prefix ends with Transfer), so Resolve not first  same as above, contributes to Estimand 1 (not resolving within SLA), and 0 to Estimand 2.

  - Transferred at >72h (e.g., S32: 96h): First event Transfer >72h; SLA deadline passed, so definitely not Resolve 72h  contributes to Estimand 1 (not resolving on time), but Resolve could occur < Transfer time? No—prefix ends with Transfer, so Resolve would appear before Transfer in prefix if occurred, thus Transfer first implies Resolve time > Transfer time 72h, so contributes to Estimand 1.

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

Naive completed-case analysis considers only resolved cases (ignored open, withdraw, reject, transfer), leading to survivor bias because open cases (e.g., S09 age=92h, S10 age=47h, S15 age=9h) often have longer paths or gaps and are more likely to breach SLA or be unresolved. Example:

- S10: open at age=47h (last_event_gap=19h), prefix "Submit>Screen>Review>Escalate"; likely still pending resolution. Naive analysis ignoring open misses that this case may resolve >72h or be censored before resolution.

- S06: withdrawn at 27h (shorter than SLA), but naive resolved-only analysis would not include it, underestimating the proportion of cases that terminate early (a key part of "not resolving within SLA").

- Competing risks: Withdraw/Reject/Transfer at <72h (e.g., S05 reject 9h, S12 withdraw 15h) are frequent in both cohorts. Ignoring them treats them as censored or irrelevant, overestimating P(E>72h) because they will never resolve by SLA as Resolve first.

- Informative censoring: Open cases at cutoff <72h (e.g., S10 age=47h <72h) may be more likely to have delays due to recent inactivity (last_event_gap=19h), so censoring before horizon is informative (higher risk of E>72h). Naive KM on resolved + open as uncensored ignores censoring informativity.

- Cohort imbalance: Cohort B has the policy change but rollout incomplete; resolved cases in B may overrepresent fast resolves (due to AutoCheck), while open cases in B show longer gaps (e.g., S24 age=67h, S25 age=79h), creating bias if only completed cases are used.

- Difference below/above 72h: Cases with age <72h (e.g., S11 open age=81h? No, S11 age=81h >72h; S10 age=47h <72h) are censored before horizon, while open cases with age >72h are censored after. Naive analysis ignoring censoring treats all open as uncensored, misplacing risk timing.

- T2 might show lower SLA breach risk than T1 if many transfers happen <72h (e.g., S08 68h, S21 26h), leading to overoptimistic alerts.

- Landmarking at "Escalate" would exclude cases that escalate after 72h-mark (immortal time), potentially underestimating hazard after Escalate.

# 7. Operational interpretation

- Do not present risk scores as causal; instead, report CIF(72h) as "proportion of cases expected to reach Resolve within 72 hours", and Estimand 1 = 1  CIF(72h) as "proportion not resolving within SLA on time" (includes terminations and delays).

- Alert rule: If for a variant (e.g., priority P3 in cohort A), 1  CIF(72h) > 0.35 (35%), trigger "SLA tail risk review" for new cases of that priority/cohort; threshold chosen via recent process check (e.g., last 10 cases observed rate).

- Workload action: Prioritize cases with prefix containing "Escalate" + "Review" (e.g., S03 pattern) or last_event_gap > 24h (for open), since these have higher Resolve hazard and risk of E>72h.

- Governance: Update CIF model monthly with new cases; validate by comparing predicted 1CIF(72h) to observed breach rate in next cutoff window.

- Fairness checks (by priority and cohort):

  - Stratify CIF(72h) by P1 (highest), P2, P3; check if P1 has lower 1CIF (better Resolve) than P3, which is expected, but ensure no systematic disadvantage (e.g., P1 cases delayed due to policy); report CIF difference with 95% CI.

  - Cohort B vs A: since policy change, compare CIF; if B has higher 1CIF, flag for investigation (policy may slow Resolve), but account for possible higher priority mix.

- Calibration: Use recent open cases (cutoff within last week) to estimate observed 1CIF at cutoff: for open cases <72h age, if still open at cutoff <72h, count as breach (E>72h); compare to model's predicted 1CIF; adjust threshold if needed.

No risk scores like "case risk = 0.4"; instead, "variant X has expected 30% not resolve within SLA".

# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

- **Time origin**: \(t = 0\) at the `Submit` event for each case. All times (including `duration_or_age_h`, `last_event_gap_h`, and horizon) are measured from `Submit` in hours.

- **Horizon**: \(t = 72\) hours (the SLA deadline; "by 72 hours" means "Resolve occurs at or before 72 hours from Submit").

- **Event definitions**:
  - **Primary event of interest (E)**: "Resolve being the *first* internal terminal outcome". That is, the case reaches `Resolve` without having previously reached `Withdraw`, `Reject`, or `Transfer` (prefixes end with the terminal event observed, e.g., `Submit>...>Resolve` vs `Submit>...>Transfer`).
  - **Competing events (C)**: `Withdraw`, `Reject`, and `Transfer` (terminal outcomes other than `Resolve`). Once any competing event occurs, `Resolve` cannot be the first terminal outcome.
  - **Censoring**: `open` cases (observed_status = `'open'`) are right-censored at their age at cutoff, i.e., at \(t_{\text{cutoff}} = \text{duration\_or\_age\_h}\). No event (E or C) is observed after the cutoff for these cases.

- **Estimands** (defined *before* estimator selection; use only observed data; no "breaching" language without clarification):

  > **Estimand 1 (primary)**:  
  > \(P(\text{first terminal outcome is Resolve} \;\land\; \text{Resolve time} > 72\text{ h})\).  
  > Equivalently, \(1 - CIF_E(72)\), where \(CIF_E(t) = P(\text{first event} = \text{Resolve} \;\land\; \text{Resolve time} \leq t)\).  
  > *Interpretation*: "Proportion of cases expected *not* to successfully resolve within the SLA (i.e., either Resolve occurs after 72 h *or* a competing terminal event occurs first, which inherently means they do not Resolve by 72 h)." This directly answers operational queries like "What fraction of new cases are likely to miss the SLA deadline for successful completion?" and includes terminations (`Withdraw`/`Reject`/`Transfer`) correctly (they never Resolve by 72 h if they terminate first), and accounts for uncertainty from open cases.

  > **Estimand 2 (secondary)**:  
  > \(P(\text{first terminal outcome is Resolve}) \times P(\text{Resolve time} > 72\text{ h} \mid \text{first event} = \text{Resolve})\).  
  > *Interpretation*: "Proportion of resolving cases (those where Resolve is first) that experience a delay beyond 72 h." This isolates process-delay risk among cases that do attempt resolution, excluding terminations, useful for identifying bottlenecks in Resolve paths (e.g., rework or escalation timing).

  - **Why these two?**  
    Estimand 1 matches operational SLA compliance risk ("will this case meet the Resolve deadline?"), including all realistic outcomes (terminations and delays). Estimand 2 answers process-focused questions ("why do resolving cases sometimes delay?"), avoiding double-counting terminations. Both use the natural first-event definition consistent with prefixes (terminal events appear at the end of the prefix).

  - **Time contribution of each case type** (per case ID in data):
    - `resolved` at \(t \leq 72\) h (e.g., S01: 18 h): First event is Resolve at \(\leq 72\) h  contributes to \(CIF_E(72)\), not to Estimand 1 (\(P(E>72)\)).
    - `resolved` at \(t > 72\) h (e.g., S03: 76 h): First event Resolve at \(>72\) h  contributes to Estimand 1 (since Resolve is first and occurs after SLA deadline).
    - `withdrawn`/`rejected` at \(t \leq 72\) h (e.g., S06: 27 h withdraw): First event is competing  never Resolve as first, so contributes 0 to Estimand 2 but contributes to Estimand 1 (since "not Resolve by SLA" includes terminations; this is correct per operational definition).
    - `transferred` at \(t \leq 72\) h (e.g., S08: 68 h transfer): First event likely Transfer (prefix ends with Transfer), so same as above for Estimand 1; contributes 0 to Estimand 2.
    - `transferred` at \(t > 72\) h (e.g., S32: 96 h transfer): First event Transfer \(>72\) h; since prefix ends with Transfer, Resolve cannot be first if Transfer occurs (process paths show Transfer after escalation), so also contributes to Estimand 1 (not Resolve by 72 h), 0 to Estimand 2.
    - `open` at cutoff \(T_c = \text{duration\_or\_age\_h}\):
      - If \(T_c \leq 72\) h (e.g., S10: 47 h open, last_event_gap=19 h): Right-censored before horizon. Contributes to censoring; if Resolve were to occur \(> T_c\), it may or may not be \(>72\) h (depends on unknown post-cutoff time). Correctly handled as censoring (not ignored).
      - If \(T_c > 72\) h (e.g., S09: 92 h open): Right-censored after horizon. Contributes to censoring; if no competing event by 72 h and Resolve occurs after 72 h, it contributes to Estimand 1.
      - *No case is dropped*: All `open` cases are included as censored observations.

> **Key distinction clarified**:  
> - **Competing events** (`Withdraw`/`Reject`/`Transfer` when observed as terminal) are *not* censoring—they are absorbing first events that prevent Resolve from being first. Modeling them as competing risks (vs. censoring) avoids underestimating tail risk (e.g., treating a `Withdraw` at 27 h as censored would incorrectly allow it to possibly Resolve by 72 h).
> - **Censoring** (`open` at cutoff) is *only* from unknown status at cutoff; it does not imply "no competing risk" (e.g., an open case could still `Withdraw` later, but we only observe up to cutoff).
> - **Already-observed breaches**: A case resolved at 76 h (`S03`) is not "censored" or ignored; it directly contributes to Estimand 1 (Resolve \(>72\) h). A case `transferred` at 68 h also directly contributes to Estimand 1 (since it terminated before 72 h), not as censoring.

# 2. Event and censoring encoding

| observed_status | default event_E (Resolve first?) | default competing (C) | default censor | T1 (primary: Transfer = competing) | T2 (sensitivity: Transfer = censoring) | T3 (sensitivity: Transfer = competing iff \(t_{\text{transfer}} \leq 72\)h) |
|------------------|----------------------------------|------------------------|----------------|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| resolved        | yes                             | no                    | no             | yes (cause 0)                    | yes (cause 0)                          | yes (cause 0)                                               |
| withdrawn       | no                             | yes (cause 1)         | no             | yes (cause 1)                    | yes (cause 1)                          | yes (cause 1)                                               |
| rejected        | no                             | yes (cause 2)         | no             | yes (cause 2)                    | yes (cause 2)                          | yes (cause 2)                                               |
| transferred     | no                             | yes (cause 3)         | no             | **yes (cause 3)** if \(t_{\text{trans}} \leq 72\)h (since prefix ends with Transfer, likely first terminal); for simplicity in primary: always competing (cause 3) | **censor** at \(t_{\text{trans}}\) (assume may Resolve internally post-transfer; unknown, so treat as right-censoring) | yes (cause 3) if \(t_{\text{trans}} \leq 72\)h; censor if \(t_{\text{trans}} > 72\)h (SLA deadline passed, no Resolve by 72h possible if Transfer first) |
| open            | unknown                        | no                    | yes (censored at \(T_c = \text{duration\_or\_age\_h}\)) | yes/no unknown, censored | yes (censored) | yes (censored) |

- **Cause coding** (for competing risks):  
  - Cause 0 = Resolve (E),  
  - Cause 1 = Withdraw,  
  - Cause 2 = Reject,  
  - Cause 3 = Transfer (only used in T1 and T3; in T2, Transfer  censor).

- **Censoring rule (same for all estimates)**:  
  `open` cases are right-censored at \(T_c = \text{duration\_or\_age\_h}\). No case is treated as uncensored `open`.

- **Transfer assumptions (three plausible, explicitly labeled)**:
  - **T1 (primary, conservative)**: Transfer is always a competing terminal event (cause 3). Reason: Prefixes end with `Transfer` (e.g., `Submit>...>Transfer`), internal system stops observing, and process logic suggests Transfer occurs before Resolve if it appears in the path. Thus, for `transferred` cases, Resolve cannot be first—correct for internal SLA (which measures Resolve within the system). This avoids underestimating breach risk.
  - **T2 (optimistic censoring sensitivity)**: `transferred` cases are right-censored at transfer time \(t_{\text{trans}}\), i.e., not competing. Reason: In reality, transferred cases may resolve internally after handoff (external org may return), so internal Resolve time could still be \(\leq 72\) h if resolved quickly post-transfer. This yields an upper bound for Estimand 1 (\(P(E>72)\)), useful for "best-case" planning (e.g., staffing assumptions). Note: this assumes no Resolve occurs *before* transfer (which prefixes confirm, since `Transfer` is last), so only post-transfer Resolve is possible.
  - **T3 (cautious time-dependent)**: `transferred` cases are competing (cause 3) if \(t_{\text{trans}} \leq 72\) h (i.e., transfer occurs before SLA deadline); if \(t_{\text{trans}} > 72\) h, treat as censored (since SLA deadline has passed, Resolve by 72 h is impossible if Transfer is first). Reason: For transfers after 72 h, the SLA decision ("met/breached") is already determined by the transfer time (case did not Resolve by 72 h), so censoring is appropriate for the horizon. For transfers before 72 h, competing is safer (internal system typically does not Resolve after Transfer). This balances realism and horizon focus.

  > *Why not treat Transfer uniformly as censoring?*  
  > Because if Transfer occurs at \(t < 72\) h (e.g., S08 68 h), the case *definitely* did not Resolve by 72 h if Transfer is first (prefix order), so censoring would incorrectly allow Resolve \(\leq 72\) h. Thus, competing is the default minimal safe choice; T2/T3 provide documented sensitivities.

- **Competing vs. censoring**:  
  - `Withdraw`/`Reject` are always competing (cause 1/2), never censoring—they are terminal and observed as first events in prefixes.
  - `open` is always censoring—never competing or event-observed.
  - `resolved` is always event E (cause 0); no censoring.

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

A naive analysis that only uses `resolved` cases (ignoring `open`, `withdrawn`, `rejected`, `transferred`) is biased in multiple ways, illustrated with concrete case IDs:

1. **Survivor bias from open cases**:  
   - S10 (A, P2, `open`, age=47 h <72 h, prefix `Submit>Screen>Review>Escalate`, last_event_gap=19 h). At cutoff (47 h), it is still pending. If naive analysis ignores open, it misses that this case could resolve in, say, 30 h (total 77 h >72 h), contributing to Estimand 1. Naive resolved-only analysis would not count it, underestimating tail risk.  
   - S09 (A, P3, `open`, age=92 h >72 h). Naive analysis might incorrectly exclude it if it assumes only resolved cases are "complete", but it may Resolve after 72 h; ignoring it overestimates compliance rate.

2. **Ignoring competing risks**:  
   - S06 (A, P2, `withdrawn`, 27 h). Naive resolved-only analysis treats only resolved cases as "successful", ignoring that `withdrawn` cases never Resolve and thus *always* contribute to "not resolving by SLA" (Estimand 1). Excluding them would underestimate \(P(E>72)\), e.g., in cohort A, 2/12 terminal non-resolve cases (`S05`, `S06`, `S12`, `S14`, `S30`) are short (<72 h), so they are key to accurate SLA risk.

3. **Informative censoring from open cases**:  
   - S10 (`open`, age=47 h <72 h, gap=19 h): Last event was at \(47 - 19 = 28\) h, so at cutoff 47 h it has been 19 h since last activity. Such cases are more likely to have Resolve delays (e.g., awaiting next review), so censoring at 47 h is informative—right-censoring before 72 h means higher risk of \(E > 72\) h compared to open cases with no gap. Naive KM on only resolved + open as uncensored ignores this, leading to biased survival estimates (e.g., KM would overestimate compliance if open cases with gaps are omitted).
   - S31 (B, P1, `open`, age=3 h <72 h, gap=3 h): Very recent open case; likely to resolve quickly, but if last event was recent and no activity, could also stall. Censoring at 3 h carries information about activity pattern.

4. **Cohort imbalance not addressed**:  
   - Cohort B has the policy change, but rollout is incomplete and not randomized. Naive analysis comparing A vs B on resolved-only cases could be biased if resolved cases in B are overrepresented by fast resolves (due to AutoCheck), while open cases in B show longer gaps (e.g., S24 67 h, S25 79 h, S26 104 h). Ignoring open cases masks that B might have higher delay risk among pending cases.

5. **Misinterpreting "completed" vs. "resolved"**:  
   - A `resolved` case at 76 h (`S03`) *breached* the SLA (since >72 h), but naive "completed-case" analysis might only look at resolved 72 h and call it "on time", missing breach signal. Conversely, ignoring open cases misses uncertainty.

> **Summary of bias**: Naive resolved-only analysis excludes censoring (open), treats competing terminals (`Withdraw`/etc.) as irrelevant or censored, ignores informative censoring from open gaps, and fails to stratify by cohort policy effects—all leading to either under- or over-estimation of SLA tail risk and incorrect operational recommendations.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

- **Primary approach**: Cause-specific cumulative incidence (CIF) for Resolve (cause 0) using the **Fine-Gray model** or Turnbull's method (implemented in `lifelines.CumulativeIncidenceFitter`). Reason:
  - CIF directly estimates \(CIF_E(t) = P(\text{first event}= \text{Resolve} \land \text{Resolve time} \leq t)\), so \(1 - CIF_E(72)\) is Estimand 1. It naturally handles competing causes (1–3) and right censoring (open), avoiding KM's bias (KM ignores competing risks and overestimates \(P(E>t)\) when competing events occur).
  - Interpretable at the horizon: CIF(72) gives the marginal probability of resolving on time (first Resolve 72 h), and \(1 - CIF(72)\) gives Estimand 1 directly.
  - No landmarking needed; works with all cases (terminal + open), no leakage from censoring.
  - Easy to stratify by `cohort` and `priority` (e.g., fit separate CIF per cohort/priority).

- **Secondary approach**: Aalen-Johansen (AJ) matrix estimator for a multi-state model (states: `Start`, `Screen`, `Review`, `AutoCheck`, `Escalate`, `Resolve`; absorbing terminals `Withdraw`, `Reject`, `Transfer`). Reasons:
  - Provides cause-specific hazards and stage-wise risks (e.g., hazard of Resolve after `Escalate` vs before), supporting Estimand 2 and process insights.
  - Naturally incorporates time-varying features (e.g., `has_escalated` after `Escalate`), avoiding immortal-time leakage if features are time-aware (see Section 5).
  - Can extract restricted mean survival time (RMST) to 72 h, but CIF is preferred for horizon-aligned marginal risk.
  - Used only for secondary exploration; primary remains CIF due to simplicity and correctness for Estimand 1.

- **Why not other methods?**
  - **Kaplan-Meier (KM)**: Ignores competing risks (treats `Withdraw`/`Transfer` as censoring), leading to overestimation of \(P(E>72)\) when many early terminations exist (e.g., 5/16 terminal non-resolve cases in A, 6/16 in B <72 h). Not suitable for Estimand 1.
  - **Cause-specific hazards (CSH) or subdistribution hazards**: Useful for stage risks but give conditional (given no competing event yet) estimates; CIF is marginal and directly answers Estimand 1. Fine-Gray (subdistribution) aligns well with competing risks, but CIF is simpler for reporting at horizon.
  - **Landmarking**: Could be used (e.g., landmark at `Review`), but requires assuming no immortal time (risk of leakage if landmark time >72 h for some cases). Since we have no timestamps per activity, landmarking adds uncertainty; AJ avoids this by modeling natural history. Primary stays horizon-focused (CIF), landmark used only in secondary if stratified by landmark time <72 h.
  - **RMST**: Sensitive to censoring after 72 h; CIF is more appropriate for "probability by 72 h".

- **Steps (primary CIF, T1)**:
  1. Encode: `time = duration_or_age_h` (event time for terminal, cutoff for open); `cause = 0` for `resolved`, `1` for `withdrawn`, `2` for `rejected`, `3` for `transferred` (T1), `NaN` for `open`; `censored = (observed_status == 'open')`.
  2. Use `lifelines.CumulativeIncidenceFitter()` to fit CIF for cause 0, passing `time`, `cause`, and `censored`. (Fallback: if `lifelines` unavailable, report KM with explicit caveat that it ignores competing risks; but prefer CIF where possible.)
  3. Compute \(CIF_E(72)\) via `ci.survival_function_at_time(72)` (survival = \(1 - CIF\)), so Estimand 1 = \(1 - CIF(72)\).
  4. Stratify CIF fit by `cohort` (A/B) and `priority` (P1/P2/P3) separately (small groups checked for stability).
  5. **Sensitivity**: Fit CIF for T2 (`transferred` = censor) and T3 (`transferred` competing iff \(t \leq 72\)h) using same encoder; compare \(1 - CIF(72)\) at cohort/priority levels.

- **Secondary (AJ)**:
  1. Define states: `Start` (Submit), `Screen`, `Review`, `AutoCheck`, `Escalate`, `Resolve`; terminals `Withdraw`, `Reject`, `Transfer` as absorbing.
  2. From prefix, extract state sequence (e.g., `Submit>Screen>Review`  states `[Screen, Review]` after Start; `Escalate` may follow `Review` or `Screen`; `AutoCheck` may follow `Submit`). Note: `Submit` is start, first observed state is e.g., `Screen` or `AutoCheck`.
  3. Use PM4Py to parse prefixes into a log (see Section 8), then `pm4py.get_variants` or `get_prefixes_from_log` to derive state sequences.
  4. Fit AJ matrix with time-independent covariates (e.g., `cohort`, `priority`) and time-varying covariates (e.g., `has_escalated` set after `Escalate` appears), extracting cause-specific CIF or hazards.
  5. Compare Resolve hazard after `Escalate` vs before for priority groups; validate with Estimand 2.

> **Justification for choice**: CIF (primary) directly estimates Estimand 1 with correct competing-risk handling, uses all data (no dropping of open), and is interpretable at the SLA deadline. AJ (secondary) provides process-aware stage risks for Estimand 2 without requiring landmarking assumptions. Both avoid naive KM bias; sensitivity analyses cover transfer assumptions.

# 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards

- **Features derived from `prefix` only** (no invented timestamps; use prefix string pattern matching):
  - `has_escalate`: `1` if `'Escalate'` in prefix, else `0` (observed immediately after `Submit`, so time-varying at first Escalate but baseline in prefix; safe for CIF covariate since prefix known at first step; see leakage note below).
  - `review_repeat`: `1` if prefix contains `Review` followed later by another `Review` (e.g., `Review>Escalate>Review`), else `0`. Use regex `\bReview\b.*\bReview\b` on prefix string (simple; captures rework loops).
  - `has_rework`: `1` if `'Rework'` in prefix, else `0` (e.g., `Review>Rework>Review`).
  - `has_autocheck`: `1` if `'AutoCheck'` in prefix (common in cohort B), else `0`.
  - `escalate_position`: index (1-based) of first `'Escalate'` in prefix after `Submit` (e.g., `Submit>Screen>Escalate`  2, `Submit>Screen>Review>Escalate`  3). Useful for timing intuition; prefix-level, no per-activity times.
  - `inactivity_gap`: for `open` cases only, `last_event_gap_h` (time since last recorded event to cutoff); for terminal cases, `0`. Used as covariate only for open cases or via interaction (but not for baseline Resolve hazard in CIF).

- **Time-varying vs. baseline classification** (critical for avoiding immortal time):
  - **Baseline (time = 0)**: `cohort` (A/B), `priority` (P1/P2/P3). Known at `Submit`, no leakage (policy change is cohort-level, not case-level randomized).
  - **Time-varying (after first occurrence)**: `has_escalate` should not be used as a baseline covariate for Resolve hazard if modeled pre-escalate (e.g., in a hazard model starting at `Submit`). Instead:
    - For CIF (primary), include `has_escalate` as a *time-constant* covariate (allowed; CIF handles covariates at start), but **only use it if we condition on "escalate has not occurred yet"**—but since prefix is observed from start, `has_escalate` is known immediately (e.g., `Submit>Escalate>` would be rare; typically `Escalate` appears after `Screen`/`Review`). Thus, strictly speaking, `has_escalate` occurs post-start, so using it as baseline in a hazard model could leak future information. **Safer approach**: 
      - In CIF (which estimates marginal incidence), include `has_escalate` as a covariate; CIF is robust to time-dependent covariates if they are observed at first event (Turnbull's method supports covariates). For horizon-focused Estimand 1, baseline covariates are fine because CIF integrates over time.
      - In AJ model, define covariates as state-specific: e.g., `is_in_escalate_state` = `1` if current state is `Escalate`, or `has_seen_escalate` = `1` after first `Escalate` event (derived from prefix sequence). This avoids using `has_escalate` as baseline at `Submit` for cases that haven't escalated yet.
    - **Practical rule**: When adding features to CIF or AJ:
      - For CIF (primary): use `has_escalate`, `review_repeat`, `has_rework` as covariates (time-constant for the case; observed from prefix). CIF naturally accounts for when events happen via cause specification, so no immortal time issue for marginal CIF.
      - For AJ: use state-embedded features (e.g., `is_after_escalate` = `1` if prefix contains `Escalate` before Resolve), derived per state transition.
  - **Post-treatment**: `review_repeat` and `has_rework` typically occur after `Escalate` or `Review`, so they are relevant for Resolve hazard after escalation; included in AJ stage hazards (e.g., hazard of Resolve from `Review` vs `Escalate` state).
  - **Leakage risk mitigation**: 
    - Never use `has_escalate` as a baseline covariate in a hazard model that predicts Resolve time from `Submit` without conditioning on "escalate has occurred". Instead, in AJ, define covariates per state (e.g., `has_escalated` only included when in `Escalate` or later states). In CIF (marginal incidence), baseline covariates are acceptable because CIF estimates first-event incidence, not hazard at arbitrary time; however, for secondary analysis, AJ with state-covariates is safer.
    - `inactivity_gap` for open cases is only used for open-case features (e.g., interaction with `priority` in AJ), not as baseline for all cases.

  - **States** (non-absorbing): `Start` (`Submit`, implicit), `Screen`, `Review`, `AutoCheck`, `Escalate`, `Resolve` (absorbing).
  - **Transitions** (observed in prefixes):
    - `Screen`  `Review` or `Escalate` or `AutoCheck` (e.g., `Submit>Screen>Review`, `Submit>Screen>Escalate`, `Submit>AutoCheck>Screen`).
    - `Review`  `Rework` (if `Rework` in prefix), `Resolve`, `Reject`, `Withdraw`, `Escalate`.

    - `Rework` (state `J`) delays Resolve; `has_rework` covariate relevant post-Review.

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

Compare three analyses varying transfer treatment, cohort policy effect, and censoring (only document changes; no numerical results since data small):

1. **T1 (primary, Transfer = competing always)**:
   - `transferred`  cause 3 (competing); `open` censored.
   - CIF(72) computed; Estimand 1 = \(1 - CIF(72)\).
   - Stratify by `cohort`/`priority`; report point estimate + 95% CI via bootstrapping (n=32 small, use percentile bootstrap).
   - Example: In cohort A, if CIF(72)  0.7 (70% resolve on time), Estimand 1  0.3 (30% not resolve by SLA).

2. **T2 (Transfer = censoring)**:
   - `transferred`  censored at \(t_{\text{trans}}\); `cause` = NaN.
   - CIF estimates \(P(\text{first}= \text{Resolve} \land \text{Resolve}\leq t)\) ignoring Transfer as competing; thus \(1 - CIF(72)\) underestimates tail risk (since transferred cases may Resolve internally).
   - Sensitivity output: typically lower Estimand 1 than T1 (e.g., if T1: 30%, T2: 20% for some priority).
   - Use for "optimistic" planning (e.g., staffing assumptions if transfers often resolve quickly).

3. **T3 (Transfer = competing iff \(t_{\text{trans}} \leq 72\)h)**:
   - `transferred` with \(t_{\text{trans}} \leq 72\)h  cause 3 (competing); \(t_{\text{trans}} > 72\)h  censored.
   - Balances realism: transfers before SLA deadline compete (correct), transfers after deadline are already "missed" so censoring appropriate.
   - Likely Estimand 1 similar to T1 but slightly lower than T1 for late transfers (e.g., S32 96h), since late transfers are censored not competing.
   - Sensitivity output: between T1 and T2; more realistic than T2 for deadline-focused risk.

4. **Cohort policy sensitivity (secondary)**:
   - Cohort B has policy change, but rollout incomplete. No assignment data, so cannot adjust.
   - Sensitivity: (a) treat B as identical to A (no effect); (b) stratify only (fit CIF separately for A/B); (c) use observed priority mix within B (e.g., B has more P2) and check if CIF differs by priority.
   - Qualitative check: if B shows higher open-case age (e.g., S24 67h, S25 79h) but fewer Resolve delays, CIF(72) might be similar or slightly lower; if rework increases in B, CIF(72) might be lower. Sensitivity reports the range (A vs B estimates) without causal claim.

**What conclusions could reverse?**
- T2 could show lower Estimand 1 (e.g., 20% vs T1's 30%) if many transfers happen <72h (e.g., S08 68h, S21 26h), leading to over-optimistic alert thresholds if T2 used.
- Landmarking at "Review" (without AJ) might underestimate Resolve hazard after Escalate due to immortal time (cases that escalate after landmark time not included), so primary CIF/AJ preferred.
- If cohort B truly had full policy compliance with no bias, T1/T3 might differ from A; but without assignment data, only descriptive stratification is valid (no causal claim in output).

# 7. Operational interpretation

- **No risk scores presented as causal effects**. Instead:
  - Report for each group (e.g., "Cohort A, Priority P3"):  
    > "Based on current data, approximately X% of cases are expected not to reach Resolve within 72 hours (Estimand 1), including cases that terminate early (`Withdraw`/`Reject`/`Transfer`) and pending open cases. This estimate uses all observed cases (including open) and treats transfers before 72 hours as competing (T1), which is conservative for SLA compliance."
  - Avoid phrases like "case risk = 0.4"; use "proportion of cases expected to miss SLA deadline".

- **Convert to alerts and actions**:
  - **Alert rule (simple, threshold-based)**:  
    > If for a new case's group (e.g., cohort + priority), the estimated Estimand 1 (\(1 - CIF(72)\)) from the latest CIF fit > 0.35 (35%), flag the case for priority review within 24 hours. Threshold (0.35) chosen as midpoint between observed high-risk signs (e.g., prefixes with `Escalate` + `Review` repeat, or open cases with `last_event_gap_h` > 24 h), validated against recent cutoff data (e.g., last 5 open cases: if >2 still open >24 h gap, observe if they Resolve >72 h).
  - **Workload action**:  
    > Prioritize cases with prefix containing `Escalate` followed by `Review` (e.g., `Review>Escalate>Review`) or `last_event_gap_h` > 24 h (for open cases) for quicker review/rework handling. This targets likely delay paths per AJ secondary analysis.
