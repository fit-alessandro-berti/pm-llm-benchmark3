# 1. Data audit and estimands

The centre has a scheduled opening window of 12 hours per day, 07:00–19:00. I use an **opening-hours clock** unless stated otherwise.

For day \(d\):

- \(A_d\): arrivals during opening hours, cases/day.
- \(C_d\): completed cases during opening hours, cases/day.
- \(\lambda_{A,d}=A_d/12\): offered arrival intensity, cases per opening hour.
- \(X_d=C_d/12\): observed completion intensity, cases per opening hour. This is a throughput measure, **not** necessarily a service-rate parameter, because completed cases may have arrived earlier.
- \(\Delta_d=A_d-C_d\): net counted inflow. It is a backlog-change proxy only if arrivals, completions, and WIP refer to the same closed population and there are no unrecorded exits or scope changes.
- \(L_d\): `mean_wip`, interpreted as an approximation to the mean number in system during opening hours from 15-minute snapshots. It is not an opening or closing inventory.
- \(S_{50,d}\), \(S_{90,d}\): daily median and p90 active handling time in minutes. They exclude waiting and are not means of service time or flow time.
- `staff_fte`: concurrently available FTE capacity, not necessarily productive service minutes after breaks, skill mismatches, administration, or outages.

For descriptive capacity screening only, I use

\[
q_{50} =
\frac{\sum_d A_d S_{50,d}}
{\sum_d \text{staff\_fte}_d(720-\text{outage\_min}_d)}.
\]

This assumes an outage removes all staffed service capacity during the recorded outage. It is a **median-service workload screen**, not a utilization estimate, not a lower or upper bound on utilization, and not evidence that the true service-time mean has a particular value.

## Aggregate consistency checks

Across all 28 days:

- Arrivals: 2,519.
- Completed: 2,409.
- Net counted inflow: \(+110\) cases.

The 110-case imbalance means the observation window has non-negligible boundary effects, unobserved stock change, or both. It prevents treating all 28 days as a steady state.

There is also a stronger conditional reconciliation warning. If one assumes that:

1. arrivals and completions are for exactly the same system population,
2. there are no abandonments, transfers, cancellations, or reclassifications, and
3. `mean_wip` is the same stock,

then cumulative \(A-C\) from 2026-04-06 through 2026-04-24 is \(+88\). At the start of 2026-04-25, the system would therefore contain at least the initial stock plus 88. Only 43 completions are recorded on 2026-04-25, so the stock could not fall below initial stock plus 45 even if all completions occurred before all arrivals. Yet reported mean WIP is 23. This is not reconcilable under those strict assumptions. The supplied data do not reveal which assumption fails; possible explanations include different metric scopes, unrecorded exits, different timestamp conventions, or incomplete WIP coverage. It is therefore unsafe to infer exact backlog levels from cumulative \(A-C\).

## Threats to naive inference

1. **Unknown boundary inventories and overnight carry-over.** Cases can remain in the system overnight, while no service occurs. Daily arrivals and completions do not identify opening or closing backlog.

2. **WIP is a sampled mean, not an endpoint stock.** Fifteen-minute snapshots approximate an opening-hours time average; they cannot by themselves support daily stock-flow reconciliation or calendar-time flow-time estimates.

3. **Arrival and completion cohorts differ.** A completion today need not correspond to an arrival today. Completion intensity is therefore not a service-rate estimate.

4. **Median and p90 service times are not mean service or mean flow time.** Neither can be substituted into Little’s Law or used as a precise workload estimate.

5. **Strong non-stationarity.** Calendar effects, outage/recovery, case-mix changes, staffing changes, and evolving WIP all occur within four weeks.

6. **Daily rows are serially dependent.** Weekend structure, recovery work, and carry-over invalidate an IID interpretation of 28 daily observations.

7. **The complex-variant shift may be operational or measurement-driven.** The sharp increase may reflect a real mix change, coding change, or both.

8. **Hourly timing fields conflict.** For the four representative dates, the reported `peak_08_10_pct` does not match the share computed from the 08–09 and 09–10 hourly-vector bins. For example, 2026-04-07 has \(17/101=16.8\%\) in those two bins, not 31%. The vectors reconcile to daily totals, but their intraday interpretation does not reconcile to the supplied peak field.

9. **FTE is not guaranteed usable service capacity.** It does not identify skill mix, breaks, work allocation, service discipline, or whether temporary staff handled the constrained work.

10. **Outage duration is not a complete causal description.** The outage is known to be 180 minutes, and the hourly completion vector has zeros approximately 11:00–14:00, but the precise operational scope and recovery policy are not supplied.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

A materially different segmentation is a **factorial case-mix/calendar/capacity design** rather than a sequential operational-state design.

| Factor | Alternative levels | Operational story |
|---|---|---|
| Exceptional disruption | Outage day; recovery days; all other days | Treat outage and recovery as exogenous interruptions, not process regimes defined by normal demand |
| Observed mix phase | Low-complexity-coded phase through 2026-04-19; high-complexity-coded phase from 2026-04-20 | The primary break is a change in work content or coding, rather than a queue-state transition |
| Calendar | Weekday versus weekend | Scheduled demand and capacity patterns are structurally different |
| Nominal staff state within high-mix phase | Before 2026-04-29 versus nominally after 2026-04-29 | Compare staffing while approximately holding observed mix and day type fixed |

Under this alternative, the most informative matched comparisons are:

- High-mix weekdays before nominal intervention: arrivals 114.0/day, completions 100.6/day, FTE 5.93, mean WIP 44.3.
- High-mix weekdays in the nominal post-start period: arrivals 115.0/day, completions 121.0/day, FTE 6.83, mean WIP 37.0.
- High-mix weekends before nominal intervention: arrivals 47.0/day, completions 41.0/day, FTE 2.5, mean WIP 21.5.
- High-mix weekends after nominal intervention: arrivals 49.0/day, completions 49.0/day, FTE 3.0, mean WIP 20.5.

## Comparison with the primary segmentation

**Strengths of the alternative**

- It makes the intervention comparison less confounded by the low-complexity early period.
- It explicitly allows weekday and weekend staffing effects to differ.
- It frames the 2026-04-20 shift as a potential work-content/coding break, which is consistent with the abrupt change in `complex_pct`, median service, p90 service, and WIP.

**Weaknesses of the alternative**

- It assumes the observed complex percentage is a stable measure of actual work content; the prompt explicitly cautions that coding may have changed.
- It can understate backlog state. A high completion count after an outage may reflect clearance of old work rather than improved steady-state capacity.
- It has only three nominal post-start weekdays and two nominal post-start weekends.
- It cannot identify an intervention effect because implementation time during 2026-04-29 is not recorded and no counterfactual high-mix control series is supplied.

The primary segmentation is better for operational control because it separates outage, recovery, and demand pressure. The alternative is better for a future causal evaluation design, but neither supports a definitive staffing-effect estimate from these aggregates.

## How pooling obscures behavior

1. **Weekday/weekend composition hides two operating scales.**  
   The pooled 90 arrivals/day is neither representative of weekdays at 108/day nor weekends at 44.9/day. The pooled FTE level of 5.14 likewise describes neither 6.15 weekday FTE nor 2.63 weekend FTE. A single daily arrival process would treat schedule composition as random variation when it is structurally planned variation.

2. **The pooled complex share masks a phase change.**  
   The arrival-weighted pooled complex share is 33.8%, but early weekday baseline is 20.9% and high-mix pre-intervention weekdays are 44.6%. At the same time, daily median/p90 service rises from 25/47 to 35/83 minutes and WIP rises from 20.3 to 44.3. Pooling makes the process look like a moderate, stable 31-minute median operation when the observed work profile changed sharply.

3. **Outage and recovery can cancel in averages while being operational opposites.**  
   The outage day has \(\Delta=+47\), while the next two recovery days together have \(\Delta=-30\). A pooled completion statistic averages an interruption and backlog clearance into an apparently ordinary number, although neither is a stationary normal regime.

4. **A pooled staffing association can reverse the within-regime interpretation.**  
   The five days with FTE at least 6.5 have mean WIP 39.6, versus 27.7 on the other 23 days. A naive pooled analysis could conclude that more staff is associated with more WIP. But those high-FTE days are recovery or nominal-intervention days with high demand/mix and inherited backlog. Within high-mix weekdays, FTE rises from 5.93 to 6.83 while mean WIP falls from 44.3 to 37.0 and completions rise from 100.6 to 121.0. This is a Simpson’s-paradox-like compositional reversal, not causal evidence that staffing reduced WIP.

5. **Unstratified pre/post comparison attenuates the staffing change.**  
   In the high-mix period before 2026-04-29, 2 of 9 days are weekends and mean FTE is 5.17. In the nominal post-start period, 2 of 5 days are weekends and mean FTE is only 5.30. That all-day comparison makes the staffing shift look small. Restricting to weekdays shows 5.93 to 6.83 FTE; restricting to weekends shows 2.5 to 3.0 FTE.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

For each representative hourly vector, the table uses the sample variance across its 12 hourly bins:

\[
\text{Fano factor} = \frac{\text{sample variance of hourly counts}}{\text{mean hourly count}},
\qquad
CV = \frac{\text{sample standard deviation}}{\text{mean hourly count}}.
\]

These are descriptive measures of within-day profile variability. They are not Poisson tests because the expected hourly rate is plainly not constant within a day.

| 2026-04-25 | 4.25 | 1.29 | 0.55 | 3.58 | 0.78 | 0.47 | Lower-volume weekend profile, still non-uniform but less extreme |

The 2026-04-21 vector is particularly important operationally. A daily rate of 115 arrivals/day does not reveal that 58 of those arrivals occur in two hours in the supplied vector. The relatively smooth completion vector could reflect capacity smoothing, use of queued work, or service constraints; it does not establish any particular service discipline without event-level starts and completions.

## Daily dispersion and dependence

Using all 28 daily arrival totals:

- Mean arrivals/day: 90.0.
- Sample variance: about 902.2.
- Daily Fano factor: about 10.03.
- Daily CV: about 0.334.

That apparent overdispersion is primarily a mixture artifact:

- Weekdays only: Fano about 0.66, CV about 0.078.
- Weekends only: Fano about 0.73, CV about 0.128.

Thus, the pooled daily series looks strongly overdispersed relative to a homogeneous Poisson benchmark, while calendar-stratified counts are comparatively controlled. Neither result establishes a true Poisson or underdispersed process because the samples are small, time-varying, and operationally scheduled.

Simple raw lag-1 correlations are also positive:

- Daily arrivals: about 0.41.
- Daily mean WIP: about 0.61.

These should not be interpreted as stationary AR(1) parameters. They combine weekday runs, weekend changes, the outage/recovery sequence, the case-mix shift, and possible stock carry-over. Still, they demonstrate that treating daily records as independent is inappropriate.

## Change-point evidence

A transparent heuristic that tests all split dates with at least five observations on each side and selects the largest difference in mean daily `complex_pct` identifies a split at approximately 2026-04-20:

- 2026-04-06 through 2026-04-19: average daily complex percentage about 22.4%.
- 2026-04-20 through 2026-05-03: average daily complex percentage about 43.3%.

The corresponding observed ranges are 18–27% before and 39–47% after. This is strong descriptive evidence of a level shift in the recorded field. It is not proof of a change in true complexity because the coding-change explanation remains viable.

The staffing change is not a clean statistical change point because it is externally dated only as beginning “during 2026-04-29.” The daily observation for 2026-04-29 may contain both pre- and post-implementation operation.

Finally, four representative days cannot identify a complete stochastic arrival model. They are not enough to estimate day-of-week-specific hourly rates, interday variation, autocorrelation of hourly increments, cross-correlation of arrivals and service starts, or the distribution of outage response.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little’s Law requires a compatible long-run or stable-window relationship:

\[
L = \lambda W.
\]

Here, \(L\) must be the time-average number in the same system population used for throughput, \(\lambda\) must be the effective stable throughput, and \(W\) must be the mean time in that same system. The supplied WIP is only an opening-hours snapshot mean, so any ratio below is on an **opening-hours clock** and excludes overnight closed hours. It is not a calendar-time case flow-time estimate.

| Early weekday baseline, 2026-04-06–14 | Aggregate \(A-C=+4\) over 7 days; opening/closing WIP unknown | \(A/12=8.35\)/hr; \(C/12=8.30\)/hr, nearly equal | Opening-hours snapshots only; same-scope assumption unverified | Moderate to high | Locally more stable than other windows, but only 7 days | Conditional diagnostic: \(20.3/8.35=2.43\) opening hr (146 min) using arrivals; 2.45 hr using completions | approximate only |

No supplied window earns an unqualified `applicable` verdict. The early weekday baseline is the least problematic and can support an **approximate opening-hours operational-time diagnostic** if the WIP and throughput scopes are later validated. It cannot support a statement such as “mean waiting time is 146 minutes,” because:

- 146 minutes is not calendar-time flow time;
- it includes neither a measured mean service time nor measured overnight waiting;
- the denominator is an arrival/completion proxy rather than verified steady throughput;
- and WIP stock reconciliation is unresolved.

# 7. Operational implications

1. **Do not plan from the pooled average of 90 arrivals/day and 5.14 FTE.**  
   That policy can look attractive because the pooled median-service workload screen is about 0.75. It is risky under the segmented view: high-mix weekdays have roughly 114 arrivals/day, the same pre-intervention FTE as the early weekday baseline, a workload screen near 0.94, much longer reported p90 service, and higher WIP. A pooled staffing rule would likely overstate spare capacity in the pressure regime and obscure the hourly bursts.

2. **Treat outage recovery as a separate operating mode.**  
   Recovery days should not be used to set normal productivity targets. Their high completion counts partly represent clearance of inherited work. A recovery policy should monitor opening stock, arrivals, completions by arrival cohort, and closing stock; otherwise a team can appear productive while new arrivals wait longer.

3. **Use intraday flexibility rather than only daily staffing averages.**  
   The 2026-04-21 vector shows substantial bursts, especially at 10:00–11:00 and 13:00–14:00. Options include staggered staffing, overflow triage, appointment shaping, callbacks, and temporary reassignment during peak periods. However, the inconsistent `peak_08_10_pct` field should be reconciled before implementing a specific 08:00–10:00 staffing rule.

4. **Assess the staffing intervention within matched regimes.**  
   The nominal post-start high-mix weekdays have similar arrivals to the high-mix pre-intervention weekdays but more FTE, more completions, and lower WIP. That is operationally encouraging, but it is not causal proof. A future evaluation should compare like-for-like weekday, mix, and backlog states; record the actual implementation time; and use interrupted-time-series or matched-regime analysis rather than a crude before/after average.

5. **Protect against long-tail work.**  
   The rise in reported p90 service from about 47 minutes in the early weekday baseline to 83 minutes in high-mix weekdays suggests that staffing only to median workload is vulnerable. Consider skill-based routing, complex-case pre-screening, specialist capacity, and separate monitoring of complex-case queue age.

6. **Collect event-level and stock-level data.**  
   Minimum additions should include case identifier, queue-entry timestamp, service-start timestamp, activity completion timestamp, resource/skill, complexity-code version, abandonment/transfer/rework outcomes, exact outage start/end, and opening/closing WIP. This would permit validation of flow conservation, actual waiting/flow-time calculation, cohort-based completion analysis, and event-log process mining.

7. **Separate customer delay from active handling.**  
   The current service medians and p90s measure active work only. Operational targets should separately report queue waiting, active service, total calendar flow time, overnight carry-over, and age of oldest waiting work.

daily["arrival_per_open_hour"] = daily["arrivals"] / OPEN_HOURS
daily["completion_per_open_hour"] = daily["completed"] / OPEN_HOURS
daily["net_arrivals_minus_completed"] = daily["arrivals"] - daily["completed"]
daily["cum_net_from_first_day"] = daily["net_arrivals_minus_completed"].cumsum()
daily["day_type"] = np.where(daily["day"].isin(["Sat", "Sun"]), "Weekend", "Weekday")

# Assumption for this screening metric only:
# recorded outage minutes remove all staffed service capacity.
daily["outage_adjusted_staff_minutes"] = daily["staff_fte"] * (
    OPEN_MINUTES - daily["outage_min"]
)
daily["median_service_load_screen"] = (
    daily["arrivals"] * daily["median_service_min"]
    / daily["outage_adjusted_staff_minutes"]
)

# Audit the stated 08:00-10:00 peak field against the representative vectors.

    vector_arrivals=("arrivals", "sum"),
    vector_completed=("completed", "sum"),

    hourly.loc[hourly["hour_index"].isin([1, 2])]
    .groupby("date")["arrivals"]
    .sum()

vector_check = vector_check.join(

# ----------------------------
# Primary regime assignment
# ----------------------------
def regime_for_row(r):
    d = r["date"]
    is_weekend = r["day"] in {"Sat", "Sun"}

    if d == pd.Timestamp("2026-04-15"):
        return "outage_2026_04_15"
    if d in {pd.Timestamp("2026-04-16"), pd.Timestamp("2026-04-17")}:
        return "recovery_2026_04_16_17"
    if d <= pd.Timestamp("2026-04-19"):
        return "early_weekend" if is_weekend else "early_weekday_baseline"
    if d <= pd.Timestamp("2026-04-28"):
        return "highmix_pre_weekend" if is_weekend else "highmix_pre_weekday"
    return "nominal_post_weekend" if is_weekend else "nominal_post_weekday"

daily["regime"] = daily.apply(regime_for_row, axis=1)
regime_order = [
    "early_weekday_baseline",
    "early_weekend",
    "outage_2026_04_15",
    "recovery_2026_04_16_17",
    "highmix_pre_weekday",
    "highmix_pre_weekend",
    "nominal_post_weekday",
    "nominal_post_weekend",
]
daily["regime"] = pd.Categorical(daily["regime"], categories=regime_order, ordered=True)

def summarize(g):
    """Descriptive group summary; service fields remain averages of daily quantiles."""
    return pd.Series(
        {
            "n_days": len(g),
            "arrivals_per_day": g["arrivals"].mean(),
            "completions_per_day": g["completed"].mean(),
            "arrivals_per_open_hr": g["arrivals"].sum() / (len(g) * OPEN_HOURS),
            "completions_per_open_hr": g["completed"].sum() / (len(g) * OPEN_HOURS),
            "net_per_day": g["net_arrivals_minus_completed"].mean(),
            "mean_wip_snapshot": g["mean_wip"].mean(),
            "mean_fte": g["staff_fte"].mean(),
            "complex_pct_arrival_weighted": (
                (g["arrivals"] * g["complex_pct"]).sum() / g["arrivals"].sum()
            ),
            "mean_of_daily_medians": g["median_service_min"].mean(),
            "mean_of_daily_p90s": g["p90_service_min"].mean(),
            # This is explicitly not utilization or a service-rate estimate.
            "median_service_load_screen": (
                (g["arrivals"] * g["median_service_min"]).sum()
                / g["outage_adjusted_staff_minutes"].sum()
            ),
        }
    )

regime_summary = pd.DataFrame(
    {
        regime: summarize(daily.loc[daily["regime"] == regime])
        for regime in regime_order
    }
).T

calendar_summary = pd.DataFrame(
    {
        "all_28_days": summarize(daily),
        "all_weekdays": summarize(daily.loc[daily["day_type"] == "Weekday"]),
        "all_weekends": summarize(daily.loc[daily["day_type"] == "Weekend"]),
    }
).T

# Dispersion and serial dependence
# ----------------------------
def dispersion(values):
    """Sample variance, Fano factor, and CV for a non-empty numeric series."""
    x = np.asarray(values, dtype=float)
    mean = x.mean()
    variance = x.var(ddof=1)
    return {
        "mean": mean,
        "sample_variance": variance,
        "fano_factor": variance / mean,
        "cv": np.sqrt(variance) / mean,
    }

daily_dispersion = pd.DataFrame(
    [
        {"window": "all_28", **dispersion(daily["arrivals"])},
        {
            "window": "weekdays",
            **dispersion(daily.loc[daily["day_type"] == "Weekday", "arrivals"]),
        },
        {
            "window": "weekends",
            **dispersion(daily.loc[daily["day_type"] == "Weekend", "arrivals"]),
        },
    ]
).set_index("window")

hourly_dispersion_rows = []

# ----------------------------
# Transparent change-point heuristic
# ----------------------------
# This is deliberately a descriptive heuristic, not a statistical test:
# choose the split with largest absolute difference in mean recorded complex_pct,
# requiring at least five daily observations on either side.
x = daily["complex_pct"].to_numpy()
split_candidates = []
min_segment_size = 5
for k in range(min_segment_size, len(daily) - min_segment_size + 1):
    before = x[:k]
    after = x[k:]
    split_candidates.append(
        {
            "split_after": daily["date"].iloc[k - 1].date().isoformat(),
            "split_at": daily["date"].iloc[k].date().isoformat(),
            "mean_complex_before": before.mean(),
            "mean_complex_after": after.mean(),
            "absolute_mean_shift": abs(after.mean() - before.mean()),
        }
    )
change_heuristic = (
    pd.DataFrame(split_candidates)
    .sort_values("absolute_mean_shift", ascending=False)
    .reset_index(drop=True)
)

# ----------------------------
# Little's-Law diagnostic ratios for explicitly named windows only
# ----------------------------
# These are opening-hours-clock ratios. They are not automatically valid
# calendar-time flow times and must not be interpreted as mean waiting times.
little_windows = {
    "early_weekday_baseline": daily["regime"] == "early_weekday_baseline",
    "highmix_pre_weekday": daily["regime"] == "highmix_pre_weekday",
    "nominal_post_weekday": daily["regime"] == "nominal_post_weekday",
}

little_rows = []
for label, mask in little_windows.items():
    g = daily.loc[mask]
    open_hours_observed = len(g) * OPEN_HOURS
    L = g["mean_wip"].mean()
    lambda_arrival = g["arrivals"].sum() / open_hours_observed
    throughput_completion = g["completed"].sum() / open_hours_observed
    little_rows.append(
        {
            "explicit_window": label,
            "n_days": len(g),
            "mean_open_hours_wip_L": L,
            "arrival_proxy_per_open_hr": lambda_arrival,
            "completion_proxy_per_open_hr": throughput_completion,
            "net_arrivals_minus_completed": g["net_arrivals_minus_completed"].sum(),
            "L_over_arrival_proxy_open_hr": L / lambda_arrival,
            "L_over_completion_proxy_open_hr": L / throughput_completion,
        }
    )
little_diagnostics = pd.DataFrame(little_rows).set_index("explicit_window")
