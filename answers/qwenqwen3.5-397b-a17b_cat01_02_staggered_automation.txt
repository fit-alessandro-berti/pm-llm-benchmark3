## 1. What is observed

Automation availability (`automation=1`) began in April 2026 for Alpha and Beta, and July 2026 for Gamma, while Delta and Epsilon remained untreated throughout the observation window. However, availability did not equal usage; `automated_fast_path_pct` grew gradually from baseline levels (~4%) to approximately 50% in treated branches, indicating a significant learning curve rather than immediate substitution. Rollout was non-random; Alpha and Beta were selected specifically for historically poor performance and high volume, introducing selection bias. Contemporaneous changes complicate isolation: a centralized fraud desk launched in March affecting all branches, routing campaigns increased arrivals to treated branches by 10% from April, staffing fluctuated (e.g., Alpha lost two FTEs in May), and a product change increased case complexity across most branches in May. Outcome measurement relies on branch-month aggregates; cycle times are medians and percentiles, not case-level durations, and customer satisfaction (`csat_1to5`) suffers from variable response rates (20-45%) without known response counts per branch-month. These factors create a high-noise environment where external shocks affect treated and control branches differently, obscuring the specific contribution of the automated triage system.

The primary strategy employs a staggered Difference-in-Differences (DiD) design with event-study indicators, utilizing Delta and Epsilon as the control group. This approach accommodates the varied adoption dates (April vs. July) and allows visualization of pre-trends to assess the parallel trends assumption. Given the small number of clusters (N=5 branches), standard errors must be clustered at the branch level, though statistical power is limited. We adjust for time-varying confounders like `high_complexity_pct` and `fte` to isolate the automation signal from staffing and case-mix shocks. This is preferable to Interrupted Time Series because universal shocks (Fraud desk, Product change) affected all branches; without a control group, ITS would attribute these external delays to automation. Synthetic Control is theoretically sound but practically unstable with only two untreated donors, risking overfitting. The staggered DiD leverages all available data points while explicitly modeling the timing heterogeneity inherent in the rollout.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**expand with an embedded evaluation**

Delta should be the next branch for expansion, but not immediately for all cases. We recommend a **step-wedge cluster randomized design** within Delta. Instead of a full switch-on, automate 50% of incoming cases randomly in Month 1, increasing to 100% by Month 3. This creates an internal control group within the branch, mitigating the branch-level confounding (staffing, local demand) that plagued the Alpha/Beta rollout.

**Minimum Measurements:** Collect 3 months of pre-period data and 3 months of post-period data at the **case level**, not just branch-month aggregates. Specifically, capture timestamps for `Register`, `Triage_Start`, `Triage_End`, and `Resolve`. This allows us to separate triage duration from resolution duration.

**Process-Mining Outcomes:** We must collect queue-state data, specifically the waiting time at the **Centralized Fraud Desk**. The previous rollout obscured whether automation simply shifted bottlenecks downstream.

**Spillover Monitoring:** Monitor Beta and Gamma during Delta's rollout. If Delta's automation increases fraud desk wait times, it will degrade Beta/Gamma performance. Establish a guardrail: If Fraud Desk wait times increase by >10%, pause Delta's expansion.

**Guardrails for Customer Harm:** Set a hard limit on `p90_cycle_h`. If the 90th percentile cycle time exceeds 120 hours for two consecutive weeks, revert to manual triage for high-complexity cases.

**Decision Rule:** Scale-up to Epsilon only if Delta's case-level analysis shows a reduction in *total* cycle time (not just triage time) and no significant increase in rework rates compared to the internal control group. This prioritizes causal learning over speed of deployment, ensuring we do not scale a system that merely displaces bottlenecks.

## 10. Final claim ladder

*   **Observed:** Automation availability coincided with stable median cycle times in treated branches, while untreated branches experienced significant increases during the same period.
*   **Suggestive:** Automated triage may have mitigated the negative impact of external shocks (product change, fraud desk) rather than generating absolute efficiency gains.
*   **Conditionally causal:** If case-mix and staffing changes are fully controlled, automation availability is associated with a 10-15% relative improvement in median cycle time compared to the counterfactual of no automation.
*   **Not supported:** The claim that "automation reduces cycle time by X%" in absolute terms, or that it improves tail performance (P90) or customer satisfaction.
