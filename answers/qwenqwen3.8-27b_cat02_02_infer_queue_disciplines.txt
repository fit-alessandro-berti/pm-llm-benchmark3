# 7. Final synthesis

The **most identifiable queue is QA**. It has a single resource, and the pattern is reasonably coherent: non-appointment jobs follow FIFO, while appointments A03 and A06 receive special treatment. The main uncertainty is whether appointments are exact, windowed, or merely prioritized, and whether the small 2-minute gaps are meaningful.

The **least identifiable queue is QB**. B01’s interrupted long service, explicit interruption reasons, and mixed choices among B02, B03, B04, B05, and B06 make it impossible to separate FIFO, preemptive priority, SPT, and operational exceptions using the supplied data alone. QC is also weakly identifiable, but its initial SPT pattern is clearer than QB’s.

The **most dangerous policy to infer from these observations alone is pure priority, especially preemptive priority**. A few episodes look priority-like: B02 preempting B01, B05 being selected at 10:00, C05 being selected as P1, and D08 being selected at 11:35. But many higher-priority jobs wait behind lower-priority jobs: A04 and A08 in QA, D10 in QD, C07 in QC, and B01/B04 in QB. Inferring a global priority rule would therefore be misleading. A secondary dangerous inference is pure SPT for QC, because C04 and C01 contradict the visible shortest-job pattern. The safer conclusion is that each queue is best described as a constrained hybrid: QA is FIFO plus appointment; QB is FIFO with urgent/dispatcher preemptions; QC is SPT with P1/SLA and FIFO exceptions; QD is skill-based routing with FIFO and appointment/resource constraints.
