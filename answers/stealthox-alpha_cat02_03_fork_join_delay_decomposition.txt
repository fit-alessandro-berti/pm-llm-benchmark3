# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

**Notation.** For case index `c` and branch index `i  {A, B, C}`, let `AR` = arrival, `FR` = fork_release, `S_i` / `E_i` = branch start/end, `FS` / `FE` = final start/end. All quantities in minutes.

| Component | Formula | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Upstream elapsed | `u_c = FR_c  AR_c` | Entry  permission to start branches |
| Branch queue | `q_{c,i} = S_{c,i}  FR_c` | Own-queue waiting after fork release |
| Branch service | `s_{c,i} = E_{c,i}  S_{c,i}` | Active work |
| Join ready (unlogged, derived) | `J_c = max_i E_{c,i}` | AND-join release time |
| Synchronization wait | `w_{c,i} = J_c  E_{c,i}` | Completed-branch waiting; `w = 0` for the **critical branch** |
| Fork–join stage elapsed | `g_c = J_c  FR_c` | Wall-clock span of the parallel stage |
| Downstream queue | `d_c = FS_c  J_c` | Waiting before F **if** model assumptions hold |
| Final service | `f_c = FE_c  FS_c` | F active work |
| Total flow time | `T_c = FE_c  AR_c` | End-to-end |

**Exact (no-double-count) identity:**

```
T_c = u_c + g_c + d_c + f_c
    = u_c + max_i ( q_{c,i} + s_{c,i} + w_{c,i} ) + d_c + f_c
```

because `q + s + w = (E_i  FR) + (J  E_i) = J  FR = g_c` for **every** branch.

**Why summing synchronization waits double counts.** All three branch paths occupy overlapping sub-intervals of the *same* wall-clock window `[FR_c, J_c]`; elapsed time accrues once, not three times. Concretely:

- Summing full branch paths: `_i (q+s+w)_c,i = 3 · g_c`. Example F07: each path = 75 min, sum = 225, but the stage consumed only 75 min of elapsed time. Only the **max** (the critical branch, the one with `w = 0`) may be counted once.
- Even summing sync waits alone is wrong: `_i w_{c,i} = 3J_c  _i E_{c,i}  g_c` in general (F07: 25+0+52 = 77  75).

**Stated assumptions used throughout:** (AS-1) `join_ready = max` branch end, per the prompt's rule; (AS-2) branch resources are separate (no cross-branch contention observable); (AS-3) `final_start  join_ready` is treated as downstream waiting, with the caveat (§7) that part may be policy; (AS-4) deterministic tie-break for "critical branch": priority order **A < B < C**, i.e., the earliest-listed branch attaining the max (no exact ties occur in the raw data; ties do occur inside counterfactuals and are handled by this rule — savings are unaffected).

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

**Modeling assumptions:** (AC-1) branch improvements shift branch ends without altering any queue gap of other branches or the downstream gap (separability); (AC-2) service durations otherwise conserved; (AC-3) final service unchanged, so per-case saving = reduction in `join_ready` (CF1, CF2) or in `d` (CF3); (AC-4) half-minute results from halving odd queues are kept exact (real deployment rounds to the minute grid; effect  0.5 min).

**CF1 — A service 20% (5040).** *Direct:* helps only F01 (capped at 5 by B's 5-min lead) and F11 (full 10). *Zero-benefit cases:* 10 of 12 — wherever B (or C) completes  10 min later, A remains non-critical. This is the quantitative proof that **largest mean service  most influential branch**. *Secondary:* A-resource idle grows; if A staff/equipment are shared with upstream preparation (unobservable here), fork_release itself might improve — an upside not credited under AC-1.

**CF2 — B queue 50%.** *Direct:* 92.5 min total; benefits 8 cases, including the tail (F07 130115, F12 125110). *Zero-benefit:* F01, F04, F08, F11 — exactly the cases where A or C is critical; their constraint is untouched. F10 gains only 5 because A sits 5 min behind. *Secondary:* B's work content is unchanged (utilization constant; waiting falls). If B's queue originates in shared upstream preparation rather than B-station capacity, the benefit is overstated. Post-CF2 the critical label migrates to **A in ~8 cases** (bottleneck migration) — further gains then require attacking A or the stage floor.

**CF3 — downstream capacity / queue removal.** *Direct:* 176 min, the largest single lever, and uniquely **every case benefits** (no zero-benefit cases) because `d > 0` everywhere. Decomposition of the lever: in 4 cases (F05, F08, F09, F11) `final_start` coincides exactly with the assigned F-resource becoming free  plausibly contention-bound ( 60 min recoverable by capacity); in the other 8 cases the F-pool was already idle when the case waited (e.g., F01 waits 8 min with F1 idle; F06 waits 15 min although F2 freed exactly at join_ready)  plausibly policy/handoff-bound (~116 min, recoverable only by process change, not capacity). *Secondary:* F-pool utilization rises (busy 240 of ~424 available resource-minutes today,  57%); removing buffers exposes variability; if the gap contains a mandatory quality gate, deleting it creates compliance risk.

**Combination (CF2+CF3, additive under AC-1 since they touch disjoint segments):** 268.5 min total, mean T 115.3  92.9 (19.4%), tail max 130  100. Beyond that, returns diminish sharply without touching A (the new floor).

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

**R1 — Pull-dispatch B at fork release (attack the dominant queue).**
Reserve B capacity or prioritize B so `B_start  fork_release` (queue  ~0; CF2's full-queue analogue is worth ~120 min total, mean 10, tail stage 7550).
- *Throughput:* essentially unchanged today (arrivals at ~15-min cadence pace the line; F-pool   0.67), but it buys headroom for arrival-rate growth.
- *Flow-time tail:* strongest effect here — the two 75-min stages (F07, F12) collapse to ~50–55.
- *Utilization:* B busy time unchanged; B WIP/time-in-system falls; C's already-large sync idle (mean 30.4 min) grows further  redeploy C staff to the F station during slack.
- *New bottlenecks:* A becomes critical in ~8/12 cases (the 50-min floor); plan an A cycle-time effort as the *follow-on*, not the first move (CF1 shows A-first yields only 15 min now).
- *Synchronization effects:* join_ready tracks A's end more often; expect the critical-label mix to invert.
- *Pilot/measure first:* two-week dispatch-rule trial on one B cell; log `B_start  fork_release` distribution and queue *reasons*; verify B's queue is B-station-caused, not upstream-caused (if upstream, the queue relocates rather than disappears).

**R2 — Instrument, then compress, the joinF handoff (split policy from congestion).**
Step 1: add reason-coded timestamps at `join_ready` (waiting-for-resource vs waiting-for-release/handoff). Current evidence predicts a ~60/116 split (contention vs policy). Step 2a (policy-dominated): replace any timed/batched release with release-on-ready. Step 2b (contention): add a floating third F server staffed from C's sync slack.
- *Throughput:* neutral-to-positive; with a third server F-pool utilization falls to ~50%, protecting against bursts.
- *Flow-time tail:* benefits all 12 cases (unique among the levers); mean up to 14.7.
- *Resource utilization:* cross-training absorbs C's idle instead of adding headcount.
- *Possible new bottlenecks:* earlier F starts raise F demand slightly; if arrivals tighten, the queue re-forms upstream of F — monitor.
- *Synchronization effects:* none on the fork-join itself; but removing the post-join buffer removes the "shock absorber" that currently hides stage variability.
- *Pilot/measure first:* two-week baseline of reason-coded waits; then pilot immediate release on low-risk case types; confirm with QA that no mandatory gate lives inside the gap before deleting it.

# 7. Limits of inference

- **Causality.** Timestamps establish order, not cause. "Queue," "synchronization," and "policy" labels are inferences from gap positions; no queue-entry events, dispatch decisions, or reason codes exist. The B-queue reduction counterfactual assumes queues are removable slack, which is unverified.
- **Branch resource capacity.** No resource IDs or counts exist for A/B/C, so utilization, pooling, and capacity limits are unidentifiable. A's uniformly zero queue could reflect ample capacity *or* a dedicated-server policy; C's long sync waits could permit staff redeployment *or* mask off-screen work.
- **Preemption / interruption.** With only start/end instants, service may contain idle-with-case-open (breaks, rework pauses, waiting for materials). True busy time per branch is bounded below by `end  start`, not equal to it; service-time distributions are therefore optimistic.
- **Is the pre-F wait really a queue?** Unidentifiable from these timestamps alone. Evidence is mixed *within* the log: in F05, F08, F09, F11 `final_start` equals the moment the assigned F resource frees (contention-consistent), yet in F01 (waits 8 min, F1 idle), F03, F06 (F2 free exactly at join_ready, still waits 15), F07, F10, F12 the resource was already idle (policy/transport/setup-consistent). The 8–20 min gap is thus a case-mixture of queue, handoff, and possibly a timed release rule; only reason-coded logging can separate them. Consequently CF3's 176 min is an upper bound, not a promise.
- **Join semantics.** `join_ready = max(end)` is stipulated, not logged. If the real join requires an additional condition (sign-off, batch), some "synchronization wait" is misattributed to branches.
- **Sample and precision.** One day, 12 cases, minute-grain clocks: no arrival-process or variability model is identifiable; critical-branch labels with 7-min margins (F01, F04, F08, F10) can flip on rounding; all percentages carry wide sampling error.
- **Counterfactual separability.** Savings assume queues, services, and the downstream gap are independent and that behavior (dispatching, staffing, arrival timing) does not adapt.
