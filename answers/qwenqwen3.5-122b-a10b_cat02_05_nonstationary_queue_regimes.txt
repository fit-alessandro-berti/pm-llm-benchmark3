# 1. Data audit and estimands

### Definitions and Units
*   **System Boundary:** Open queueing system operating 07:00–19:00 (12 hours/day). Cases may enter before 07:00 (carry-over) and exit after 19:00.
*   **Arrivals (`arrivals`):** New cases entering the queue during opening hours. Unit: Count/day.
*   **Throughput (`completed`):** Cases exiting the queue during opening hours. Unit: Count/day.
*   **Service Time (`median_service_min`, `p90_service_min`):** Active handling time per case. Excludes waiting time. Unit: Minutes.
*   **Work-in-Progress (`mean_wip`):** Average number of cases in the system (queue + active) measured via 15-minute snapshots. Unit: Cases.
*   **Capacity (`staff_fte`):** Mean concurrent Full-Time Equivalent staff available. Unit: FTE.
*   **Complexity (`complex_pct`):** Percentage of arrivals classified as multi-step variants. Unit: %.
*   **Peak Concentration (`peak_08_10_pct`):** Share of daily arrivals occurring between 08:00–10:00. Unit: %.

### Limitations of Aggregates
1.  **No Case-Level Traceability:** We cannot link specific arrivals to completions; cross-day dependencies are inferred only via WIP and net flow.
2.  **Snapshot WIP:** `mean_wip` is averaged over snapshots, not continuous integration. It assumes linearity between points.
3.  **Service Definition:** `median_service_min` describes processing effort, not total lead time. Waiting time must be derived indirectly.
4.  **Missing Backlog:** Initial WIP at 07:00 is unknown. Net daily flow ($\text{Arrivals} - \text{Completed}$) approximates WIP change only if overnight carry-over is constant or negligible.
5.  **Representative Days:** Hourly vectors are illustrative samples, not exhaustive distributions for every day in the period.

### Threats to Naive Inference
1.  **Carry-Over Bias:** Assuming `Completed`  `Arrivals` ignores backlog accumulation or depletion (evident 04-16 where Completed > Arrivals).
2.  **Mix Confounding:** Increased `complex_pct` artificially inflates `median_service_min`. Treating this as general inefficiency is incorrect.
3.  **Non-Stationarity:** Demand spikes (e.g., 04-21 bursty vector) violate steady-state assumptions required for standard queueing formulas.
4.  **Outage Distortion:** 04-15 represents a structural break, not a typical low-throughput day. Including it skews baseline metrics.
5.  **Latency Lag:** Changes in staffing (04-29) may take days to reflect in WIP reduction; immediate correlation is unreliable.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little's Law ($L = \lambda W$) requires stability ($\lambda_{in} \approx \lambda_{out}$) and defined boundaries.

| Window / Regime | Boundary Stability | Throughput Proxy ($\lambda$) | WIP Comparability ($L$) | Carry-Over Risk | Stationarity Assessment | Implied Flow-Time ($W=L/\lambda$) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R1 (Baseline)** | High | Use `completed` (~99) | Consistent | Low | Approx. Steady | $19 / 99 \times 1440 \approx 276$ min | `applicable` |
| **R2 (Recovery)** | Low | Use `completed` (>Arrivals) | Inflated by backlog | High | Transient | $37 / 103 \times 1440 \approx 517$ min | `not defensible` (Transient) |
| **R3 (High Mix)** | Medium | Use `completed` (~101) | High, stable-ish | Medium | Drifting | $43 / 101 \times 1440 \approx 613$ min | `approximate only` |
| **R4 (Intervention)** | Medium | Use `completed` (~115) | Decreasing trend | Medium | Improving | $30 / 115 \times 1440 \approx 376$ min | `approximate only` |
| **Pooled (All)** | None | Use Mean `completed` | Mixed Scale | Unknown | Non-Stationary | N/A | `not defensible` |

*Note on Flow Time Calculation:* If using `mean_wip` (cases) and `completed` (cases/day), $W$ comes out in days. Multiply by 1440 to get minutes.
*Rationale:* In R1, system is roughly balanced. In R2, backlog drain makes $\lambda$ unrepresentative of new work. In R3, mix change alters the definition of "case," making $W$ incomparable across regimes.

# 7. Operational implications

### Staffing & Capacity
*   **Targeted Peaks:** The `peak_08_10_pct` rose from 29% to 44%. A fixed 07:00 start is insufficient. Recommendation: Stagger shifts so peak coverage aligns with 08:00–10:00 surge.
*   **Intervention Timing:** Staffing increased on 04-29, but WIP remained high for several days. Expect lag effects. Do not reduce staff immediately upon seeing slight WIP dip.

### Backlog Management
*   **Segregate Recovery:** During R2 (Recovery), staff were boosted to clear backlog. This should be planned explicitly when outages occur, rather than reacting ad-hoc.
*   **Complexity Buffer:** R3 showed that higher complexity requires proportionally more capacity. Simply adding 1 FTE (6.07.0) helped R4, but R3 accumulated backlog with 6.0 FTE against 44% complexity.

### Policy Risk (Pooled vs. Segmented)
*   **Risky Policy:** "Reduce average wait time by 10% across all cases."
*   **Why:** Under pooled averages, this looks achievable by trimming the long-tail (simple cases). However, under a segmented view, the "long tail" is driven by the 40% complex cases. Cutting resources there to meet an aggregate target will crash the complex queue specifically, increasing risk of SLA breaches for high-value clients.
*   **Better Policy:** "Maintain WIP < 30 for simple cases; allow WIP < 50 for complex cases."

### Data Collection
*   **Start-of-Day Snapshot:** Log exact WIP at 07:00 to distinguish carry-over from new arrivals.
*   **Case-Type Timestamps:** Record entry and exit timestamps for `complex` vs `simple` variants separately to validate if complexity truly drives service time or just coding.
