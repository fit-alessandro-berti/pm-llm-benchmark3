## 1. Materiality Framework

Raw alignment cost answers a narrow question: "How many edit operations, under a uniform unit cost function, are needed to reconcile this trace with the control-flow model?" It says nothing about *which* control was broken, *how much money* moved, whether the deviation is *reversible*, whether the evidence is *trustworthy*, or whether the case is even *in scope*. My severity framework therefore ignores `raw_cost` as a ranking key and instead scores each case against eight dimensions.

**Severity dimensions (drivers of the 1–5 score):**
1. **Control objective breached.** Which safeguard failed — segregation of approval and payment (P3), risk-tiered fraud screening (P2), amount/data-conditioned auto-approval (P1), or notification (P4)? Approval-before-payment and fraud-screen controls protect against loss and fraud; label/telemetry issues protect nothing.
2. **Amount / financial exposure.** EUR 480,000 unscreened is categorically worse than EUR 800 auto-approved, regardless of edit distance.
3. **Reversibility.** Money already paid (bank accepted funds) is largely irreversible; a mislabeled activity or a missing log write is fully reversible/correctable.
4. **Timing / sequence.** Payment *before* the applicable approval is the worst temporal pattern; a notification arriving a few hours late but within SLA is benign.
5. **Customer / regulatory harm.** AML/fraud exposure, unpaid-but-owed claimants, denied-lead handling, and audit-trail integrity all carry regulatory weight.
6. **Recurrence.** A pattern occurring in 74 cases via one connector is a systemic issue even if each instance is individually trivial.
7. **Evidence reliability.** A control assertion resting on a mutable text field, a verbal check with no artifact, or a document without a stable case identifier is weak evidence and must not be scored as "clean."
8. **Model scope.** If a case (denied lead, withdrawal) was never in the model's intended domain, high raw cost reflects a *scope/modeling* gap, not an operational failure.

**Severity scale (1 = harmless, 5 = severe control failure):**
- **5** — Irreversible financial control failure: payment made without the required approval/screen, or a genuine data-aware policy breach that let money out the door.
- **4** — Serious but not yet proven-irreversible control gap, or a policy breach with meaningful exposure requiring immediate investigation.
- **3** — Real control/evidence weakness: required assurance step lacks a durable artifact, or notification integrity depends on mutable evidence.
- **2** — Modeling/scope or label mismatch that pollutes conformance but reflects no operational wrongdoing; still needs cleanup.
- **1** — Harmless technical noise (UI telemetry, retry duplicates, silent routing transitions).

**Confidence is orthogonal to severity.** Severity = "how bad is this if the evidence is true?" Confidence = "how sure am I that my interpretation is the true state of the world?" A case can be **high-severity / low-confidence** (CL-602: catastrophic *if* no fraud screen occurred, but the approver claims a verbal check — I cannot fully prove the negative from a missing event). A case can be **low-severity / high-confidence** (CL-601 telemetry — certainly benign). A missing model move (`M`) is evidence that the *log* lacks an event, **not** proof the *activity* did not happen; I therefore separate "the control was not performed" from "the control was not recorded," and let that distinction drive confidence, not severity.

## 3. Repeated Deviation Patterns

**Pattern A — Retry-duplicated notifications (harmless telemetry).**
- *Cases:* CL-603, plus the monthly evidence of **74 cases** with `L(Notify Claimant)` after a synchronous notify sharing the same message ID, all tied to **one retrying connector**.
- *Business risk:* Negligible — no double payment, no missed customer notice; only conformance noise and inflated cost.
- *Cause:* Connector-level retry re-emitting the same event (same message ID = same logical notification).
- *Confidence:* High (identical message ID + single connector is a clear signature).
- *Owner:* Integration/Platform engineering.

**Pattern B — External-adjuster assessment gaps (missing-but-external evidence).**
- *Cases:* CL-606, and **9 high-value cases** with `M(Assess Loss)` + an external-adjuster document; **2 lack a stable document-to-case identifier**.
- *Business risk:* Low-to-medium — the assessment likely occurred off-system; risk is *audit traceability*, elevated where the doc-to-case link is unstable.
- *Cause:* External adjuster reports not ingested as core `Assess Loss` events.
- *Confidence:* Medium-High where a stable identifier exists; **lower for the 2 without one** (cannot firmly bind document to case).
- *Owner:* Claims Operations + Data Engineering (ingestion).

**Pattern C — Activity-label mapping mismatch.**
- *Cases:* CL-611 (`Assess Damage` vs `Assess Loss`).
- *Business risk:* None operationally; risk is *systematic false positives* if the synonym recurs across many cases while the mapping table lags.
- *Cause:* Mapping/reference table updated after cases were logged.
- *Confidence:* High.
- *Owner:* Data Governance / Master-data.

**Pattern D — Out-of-scope denials and withdrawals.**
- *Cases:* CL-607 (denied), CL-612 (withdrawn); evidence notes **denied/withdrawn cases account for most alignments with raw_cost ≥ 4**.
- *Business risk:* Low as operational deviations; high as a *measurement distortion* — the model penalizes legitimate outcomes it never intended to cover (P6 dispute).
- *Cause:* Single log stream feeds a payable-only model; no denial/withdrawal branches.
- *Confidence:* Medium (governance decision, not a fact).
- *Owner:* Process Owner + Model Owner (scope adjudication).

**Pattern E — Data-aware auto-approval failures (the dangerous zero-cost class).**
- *Cases:* CL-609 (amount/risk breach), CL-610 (recent bank change); evidence: **4 cases with fully synchronous Auto Approve despite violating P1/P2**.
- *Business risk:* **High** — fraud/AML exposure and improper payments that the control-flow model is *structurally blind to* (amount, risk, and bank-change conditions are not encoded).
- *Cause:* Business policy (P1/P2) lives outside the process tree; the automation gate does not enforce data conditions.
- *Confidence:* High (conditions are stated in the case data).
- *Owner:* Fraud/Financial-crime + Controls Engineering.

**Pattern F — Approval/payment sequence and evidence failures (highest severity).**
- *Cases:* CL-602 (no fraud-screen artifact on a EUR 480k HIGH bank-changed claim), CL-604 (payment before approval on EUR 90k), CL-608 (EUR 250k notify proof on a mutable field).
- *Business risk:* Severe — irreversible payouts without proven controls and weak audit evidence.
- *Cause:* Payment released ahead of/without durable approval evidence; portal/verbal artifacts not persisted immutably.
- *Confidence:* High for CL-604 (timing proven); Medium-High for CL-602/CL-608 (missing/weak evidence, not proof of omission).
- *Owner:* Internal Audit / Financial-crime / Controls.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

For each, note: a **missing event is not proof the activity did not occur.**

**CL-602 (no fraud-screen evidence, EUR 480k HIGH, bank changed).**
- H1: Fraud Screen genuinely skipped — a real control failure.
- H2: Fraud Screen performed off-system (verbal/manual) but never logged — recording gap.
- *Discriminating evidence:* fraud-tool query logs / analyst worklists timestamped before approval; the approver's claimed verbal check should have a corroborating artifact (email, case note, call record). Absence across *all* systems moves probability toward H1.

**CL-604 (payment 10:03, approval 10:20).**
- H1: True out-of-order execution — payment released before approval cleared (P3 breach).
- H2: Clock skew / event-timestamp mislabeling between banking and approval systems.
- *Discriminating evidence:* reconcile source-system clocks; check the payment authorization reference vs approval ID; confirm whether the bank settlement at 10:03 corresponds to an *instruction* sent before 10:20. Skew is testable and, if excluded, H1 stands.

**CL-606 (missing `Assess Loss`, external adjuster signed at 10:05).**
- H1: Assessment performed externally, just not written to the core log — traceability gap only.
- H2: No genuine internal assessment; external report substituted without core validation.
- *Discriminating evidence:* stable document-to-case identifier binding the signed report to CL-606; adjuster credential/licensing check; whether downstream steps referenced the report. A stable link supports H1.

**CL-608 (EUR 250k, notify link in a mutable text field).**
- H1: Claimant was notified via secure portal within SLA; only the *evidence medium* is fragile.
- H2: The portal link was edited/backfilled; notification integrity is compromised.
- *Discriminating evidence:* immutable portal/CRM delivery receipt with server-side timestamp; field-edit audit history showing no post-hoc change; claimant read/acknowledgment. An unaltered receipt supports H1.

**CL-609 / CL-610 (zero-cost policy breaches).**
- H1: Auto-approval gate simply does not enforce P1/P2 data conditions — systemic control-design defect.
- H2: One-off misconfiguration or manual override for these specific cases.
- *Discriminating evidence:* the automation rule configuration at execution time; the count of similar breaches (evidence already shows **4** synchronous auto-approvals violating P1/P2), which favors H1 (systemic) over H2 (isolated).

**CL-611 (`Assess Damage` vs `Assess Loss`).**
- H1: Pure synonym/label mismatch; activity performed correctly.
- H2: A genuinely different activity masquerading under a similar name.
- *Discriminating evidence:* the signed procedure and UI both name it `Assess Loss`; the mapping table update history; whether the same actor/tool/output fields match a normal assessment. Matching artifacts confirm H1.

**Pattern D / CL-607, CL-612 (scope).**
- H1: Denials/withdrawals are legitimately out of the payable-claim model — high cost is a scope artifact.
- H2: They should be in scope and the model is incomplete.
- *Discriminating evidence:* the documented intended scope of M0, and whether denial/withdrawal require downstream controlled steps. This is a governance decision, discriminated by policy, not by more log data.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Raw alignment cost is an edit distance under a chosen cost function; here costs were **configured uniformly at 1 per visible move, with no business weighting**. That design choice alone severs cost from risk in at least six demonstrable ways.

1. **Cost-function design is business-blind (CL-601).** CL-601 carries the highest raw cost in the set (3) yet is the *cleanest* case — three `L` moves are all UI telemetry. Because every move costs 1 regardless of meaning, noise and catastrophe are priced identically. A uniform cost function cannot encode that a workbench autosave and an unscreened half-million-euro payment are not the same thing.

2. **Data conditions are invisible, so zero cost can mean maximum risk (CL-610, CL-609).** CL-610 (EUR 4,500, LOW risk, **but bank changed one hour before payment**) and CL-609 (EUR 18,000, MEDIUM, auto-approved) both align at `raw_cost = 0`. The control-flow model literally cannot see amount, risk level, or bank-change history (P1/P2 live outside the tree), so a fraud-shaped, irreversible payout scores as *perfect conformance*. Zero cost here is the highest-risk signal in the entire set — the opposite of what cost implies.

3. **Cheap repairs hide irreversible exposure (CL-602).** A single `M(Fraud Screen)` (cost 1) sits atop a EUR 480,000, HIGH-risk, bank-changed claim with no fraud evidence. The model "repairs" the missing screen cheaply, but the money is gone and the control that should have caught fraud is unproven. Low cost ↔ severe, irreversible risk.

4. **Hidden (silent) moves are fitting and correctly cost nothing (CL-605).** The `T(tau)` routing transition before Auto Approve is a Petri-net conversion artifact, not a deviation. Here cost=0 is *informative and correct* — a reminder that not every zero is dangerous, and that severity must be assigned by semantics, not by the number itself.

5. **Activity granularity / label mismatch inflates cost with zero risk (CL-611).** `Assess Damage` vs `Assess Loss` produces `L+M = cost 2` purely because a mapping table lagged. The activity was genuinely performed on a EUR 62,000 HIGH case with a correct manual path. Cost measures naming friction, not control failure.

6. **Scope mismatch inflates cost on legitimate outcomes (CL-607, CL-612).** Denied (CL-607, cost 4) and withdrawn (CL-612, cost 4) cases dominate the high-cost tail *only because* a payable-claim model forces phantom `M(Auto Approve)/M(Pay Claim)` moves onto traces that correctly ended early. High cost here reflects a modeling-scope gap, not operational wrongdoing.

**Two further principles:**
- **Duplicated telemetry (CL-603 + 74 cases):** a retry re-emitting the same message ID adds cost per instance, so a single connector defect can dominate aggregate cost while representing one trivial engineering fix.
- **Missing-but-external evidence and irreversibility (CL-606, CL-608, CL-604):** an `M` move means the *log* lacks an event, not that the *activity* did not occur (CL-606's external assessment; CL-608's portal notification). Conversely, CL-604's `L(Pay Claim)…M(Pay Claim)` split — same nominal cost class — encodes an *irreversible* out-of-order payment. Identical or near-identical costs, opposite business meaning.

In short: cost counts edits; risk weighs objective, exposure, reversibility, timing, evidence quality, recurrence, and scope. They coincide only by accident under a uniform cost function.

    ("CL-608", 1,  250000, "HIGH",   "M_notify",                   "P4_weak_evidence",        "weak_evidence",       3, "Medium"),
