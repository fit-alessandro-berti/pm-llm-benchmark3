# 1. Network-by-network interpretation
## handover-of-work network
- **What it measures in PM4Py:** `discover_handover_of_work_network` counts direct succession of resources within cases: A completes an event, B completes chronologically next event in same case. Edge frequency and out/in-degree and betweenness reflect routing volume and position on paths, not effort or authority.
- **What it says about Mira_Coordinator:** Out-degree 0.82, in-degree 0.51, betweenness 0.77 - highest in human network. She succeeds Bot_Rules on 920 exception routings and fans out to Omar_Medical, Priya_Legal, all four adjusters, plus receives returns from specialists and Temp_1/2. This implies a brokerage / dispatcher position: many case paths go through her.
- **What it does NOT imply:** It does not prove she does substantive work, controls outcomes, or causes delay. Short routing, assignment, and reopen events create many handovers by design. High betweenness is expected for any exception desk even if value-adding. It also does not prove bottleneck alone - need waiting-time and absence evidence. Bot_Ingest -> Bot_Rules 4040 and TeamLead -> Vendor_Pay edges show the dominant volume is still automated start and routine Team North/South -> payment paths that bypass Mira entirely.

## working-together network
- **What it measures:** `discover_working_together_network` measures co-occurrence in same case, normalized. High weighted degree means repeatedly working the same case pool with same colleagues over full case duration.
- **What it says about Mira:** Weighted degree 0.16 despite case percentile 0.91. Pairwise co-case rates with Carla 8%, Diego 7%, Elise/Farid 6%, Alice 5%, Bruno 4% - far below Alice-Carla 27% or Bruno-Diego 26%. This implies her involvement is brief and diffuse: touches many cases once or twice for routing/clarification then disappears for several events, so she does not accumulate sustained co-presence with any one team.
- **Why apparent contradiction is reconcilable:** Handover rewards *succession frequency across many distinct partners*; working-together rewards *repeated shared cases with same partners*. A triage broker will be high on first, low on second. Low score does not mean unimportant or isolated; nor does it mean independent. Team leads and senior adjusters look cohesive because they co-stay on routine North/South lanes. Mira looks peripheral because she sprays across lanes.
- **Does NOT imply:** Lack of collaboration, low workload, or queue-cleaner triviality. Process owner label "just a queue cleaner" misreads this metric.

# 3. Risk assessment
## Bottleneck risk
Moderate-high but selective. Evidence calibrated: not volume bottleneck for 78% routine flow, but critical-path broker for exception/reopen/complaint segment. Absence test shows queueing, not immediate collapse. Risk is tail latency and accumulation, not median slowdown. Automation Bot_Rules and Vendor_Pay/Verify are larger volume/waiting bottlenecks overall.

## Coordination risk
High if lost, medium if retained without support. Current design relies on tacit knowledge of where hard cases should go. Benefit: prevents misrouting to wrong team/specialist and reduces formal escalations. Cost: implicit protocol, single routing logic. Failure mode is misassignment and rework loops Mira -> junior -> Mira.

## Key-person risk
High. Concentration of exception know-how, 91st percentile case coverage with no evident substitute - leads did not absorb load during absence. Even if service time low, unavailability creates high-variance delay for regulated complaints/reopens where SLA breach matters most.

## Compliance/control risk
Medium, not proven misconduct. Pattern of same actor flagging issue and selecting reviewer violates segregation-of-duties principle in regulated claims. Could enable bias or concealment, but log alone shows no outcome manipulation. Must be framed as control gap, not allegation. Need to check whether Approve Settlement remains with Alice/Bruno and whether Mira can both reopen and assign without second approval.

## Workload fairness risk
Medium-low visibility. Seniors Carla/Diego carry high cycle contribution and 870/820 cases vs juniors 610/590; Mira's reassignments 350/330 to seniors vs 290/280 to juniors plus 24%/22% lead-junior support pairs suggest possible preferential routing of complex but viable cases to seniors. Temps handle low-exception Validate/Close with escalation to Mira - fair if by skill, risky if temps parked on dead-end tasks without learning path. Requires fairness analysis by amount_band/priority, not assumption.

## Data-quality risk
Medium. Automated bots 4-7 sec and vendor hours vs human minutes mix different timestamp semantics; routing micro-events inflate centrality; Assign Specialist may not be logged as formal activity in original taxonomy; lifecycle:transition filtering essential. Conclusions about discretion depend on complete logging of who decided what; missing decision rationale fields limit auditability.

## What should not be changed until more evidence is available

# 6. Short executive summary
Mira handles only one in five claims, but two-thirds of reopened and difficult complaints pass through her. She does not do the long medical, legal or damage work herself; she briefly routes hard cases to the right specialist and later brings them back, which explains why she looks central in handover maps but distant in teamwork maps. This routing keeps ordinary claims flowing and prevents specialists being overwhelmed, but it creates dependency: when she was absent six days, routine work was unaffected while difficult cases queued and very slow cases grew much slower. Her broad discretion to both spot problems and choose who fixes them is useful but needs checks, clear backup, and better recording of decisions. We do not recommend removing the role. We recommend documenting her routing rules, adding a trained backup, and monitoring queues and fairness before any redesign.
