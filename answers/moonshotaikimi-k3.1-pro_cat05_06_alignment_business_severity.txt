## 1. Materiality Framework

The unit of assessment is the **business control objective**, not the alignment move. The same technical move can represent harmless noise, missing evidence, an incomplete model, or an actual payment-control failure. The severity scale below is a proposed triage framework, not a canonical ranking.

Six categories must remain distinct:

- **Process-model mismatch:** The trace is a legitimate business variant that the model cannot represent, such as denial or withdrawal. This does not by itself establish an operational deviation.
- **Event-label mismatch:** A business activity may have occurred under a different technical name. Semantic equivalence must be established before relabelling it.
- **Missing evidence:** A model-only move means that no matching event was found in the supplied log. It does **not** prove that the activity never occurred. External documents and other systems may provide compensating evidence, with varying reliability.
- **Actual operational deviation:** Independent evidence shows that the business sequence or operation was wrong, such as bank acceptance before recorded approval.
- **Data-aware policy failure:** The control flow fits, but attributes such as amount, risk, or bank-change recency make the selected branch or payment non-compliant. These can have zero alignment cost.
- **Harmless technical noise:** UI telemetry, connector retries, and hidden routing transitions need not represent business work. Noise should still be investigated if it reveals duplicate customer communication, unauthorized access, or another meaningful control issue.

Severity considers:

1. **Control objective:** Payment authorization, fraud prevention, and payee integrity generally outweigh model fit or logging aesthetics. Notification and denial/withdrawal handling can nevertheless create significant customer or regulatory harm.
2. **Amount and exposure:** Amount is potential exposure, not established loss. A EUR 480,000 evidence gap can warrant more urgent investigation than a EUR 1,200 logging gap, but amount alone does not determine severity.
3. **Reversibility:** A payment already accepted by a bank is less reversible than an unreleased payment or a correctable log gap. Recovery potential affects containment urgency, not whether the control was breached.
4. **Timing and sequence:** Payment before applicable approval, screening after payment, bank-change recency, and the 24-hour notification requirement have meanings that a simple edit count cannot capture.
5. **Customer and regulatory harm:** Possible misdirected funds, missing notification, improper denial, or unauditable approval can matter even where there is no immediate financial loss.
6. **Recurrence:** A repeated defect can be systemically important even if each instance is small. Recurrence changes remediation priority; it does not prove harm in every case.
7. **Evidence reliability:** Bank records, immutable approval logs, signed and linked reports, structured message identifiers, and mutable text links do not deserve equal evidentiary weight.
8. **Model scope:** Costs from denied, withdrawn, or potentially reopened claims must be segmented from payable-claim conformance. Scope disputes should not be resolved merely by filtering traces until costs look favorable.

| Severity | Proposed business interpretation |
|---|---|
| **5 — Critical** | Confirmed severe payment sequencing failure, or a credible payment/fraud-control failure whose exposure and irreversibility require immediate containment. |
| **4 — Major** | Confirmed policy-invalid payment approval or other major control failure, but without the same confirmed high-risk sequencing or exposure as severity 5. |
| **3 — Significant** | Material unresolved customer, regulatory, or control-evidence issue, with some compensating evidence but a credible possibility of non-compliance. |
| **2 — Limited** | Logging, linkage, label, or model-scope weakness without demonstrated payment or customer harm. |
| **1 — Negligible/no material deviation** | Harmless noise, fitting hidden routing, or a substantively compliant path with only technical observations. |

**Confidence is separate from severity.** Confidence describes the strength of evidence for a specific proposition. CL-602 has high confidence that fraud-screen evidence is absent, but only medium confidence that the screen itself was omitted. CL-601 has high confidence and low severity. Conversely, a severity-5 case can require precautionary containment while the root cause remains uncertain.

Except for CL-608, precise notification timings are not supplied. A synchronous notification proves presence in the control-flow trace, not compliance with the 24-hour deadline; no late-notification breach is inferred merely from missing timestamps.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Under the supplied cost function, the technical quantity is effectively:

\[
C(a)=\sum_{m\in a}c(m),
\]

with synchronous and hidden moves costing zero and each visible log-only or model-only move costing one. It measures how many technical edits a selected alignment uses. It does not include amount, payee integrity, timing, recoverability, customer harm, evidence quality, or recurrence.

1. **The cost function has no business weighting.** CL-602 costs only **1**, yet concerns EUR 480,000, HIGH risk, a bank change one day earlier, and no independent evidence of a mandatory fraud control. CL-601 costs **3** solely because of UI telemetry. A uniform cost of one implicitly treats a UI event and a missing Fraud Screen as equally expensive technical edits; that is not a statement about controls.

2. **Equal costs can conceal opposite materiality.** CL-601 and CL-604 both cost **3**. CL-601 has no material business deviation. CL-604 has bank acceptance before recorded manual approval, an unevidenced Fraud Screen, and a P3 issue involving an already accepted EUR 90,000 payment. The model’s cheap sequence repair does not restore the approval that should have preceded payment, and its model-only Pay Claim does not represent a second payment.

3. **Zero cost is not compliance.** CL-609 and CL-610 are fully synchronous and cost **0**. CL-609 violates P1 on amount and risk; CL-610 violates P1 and P2 because of the bank change and pays without mandatory fraud/manual controls. The process tree can express that Auto Approve is structurally possible, but not whether its data preconditions hold. Conversely, CL-605’s zero cost includes `T(tau)`, which is a **fitting hidden routing move**, not suspicious invisible behavior. Zero cost therefore has different meanings in different contexts.

4. **Scope can dominate high costs.** CL-607 and CL-612 each cost **4**, but neither contains an intended or actual payment. The model inserts payable-claim activities after a legitimate denial or withdrawal. These costs are informative about model scope and missing terminal branches. They are not evidence that assessment, approval, or payment was operationally omitted. The monthly observation that denied and withdrawn cases dominate raw costs 4 confirms that a high-cost queue would be heavily influenced by scope.

5. **Activity granularity and labels affect edit count.** CL-611’s `L(Assess Damage)` plus `M(Assess Loss)` costs **2**, although both moves may concern one activity recorded under two labels. That could be one mapping problem rather than two business failures. Equivalence still needs validation because a differently named event could represent only part of the required activity.

6. **Repeated events are not necessarily repeated business activities.** CL-603’s second notification costs **1**, but the shared message ID and transport evidence point to a retry. The 74-case pattern is important for connector repair and cost interpretation, not proof of 74 customer-harm incidents. Similarly, CL-601’s three telemetry events inflate cost without adding business work.

7. **A missing core event is not proof of inactivity.** CL-606’s model-only Assess Loss coexists with a signed external-adjuster report, making an evidence-integration gap more likely than an omitted assessment. CL-608’s model-only notification coexists with an allowed-channel portal message at +3 hours, but the mutable link makes its evidence weaker than a stable signed report. The same `M` notation therefore covers materially different evidence situations.

8. **Cost does not encode irreversibility or precautionary exposure.** CL-604’s bank acceptance and CL-610’s payment to a recently changed beneficiary account require urgent containment because recovery may be limited. CL-602 also warrants critical investigation despite uncertainty about whether a screen occurred: the potential exposure is large, and a mandatory fraud control cannot be evidenced. By contrast, correcting a label or filtering telemetry is highly reversible.

Alignment cost remains useful for **locating traces, measuring model fit under a fixed scope, detecting recurring logging patterns, and evaluating model repairs**. It becomes misleading when used as a proxy for compliance, expected loss, customer harm, or evidentiary confidence. Technical `raw_cost`, business severity, and confidence should therefore remain separate fields in both analysis and code.
