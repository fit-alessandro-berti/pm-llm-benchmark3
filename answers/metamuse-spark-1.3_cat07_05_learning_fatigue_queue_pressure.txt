# 2. Integrated explanation

## How the pieces combine across a shift

No single mechanism fits. The observed U-shape for errors (high early, low middle, high late) and monotonic fall for service time is the sum of three overlapping gradients that move in different directions:

**Start of shift (positions 1–5, ~22 min in): slow and error-prone because of what is worked, not who is working.** Shifts inherit backlog: Early inherits night backlog (queue 61), Late inherits day backlog (70), Night inherits urgent exceptions (56% high-complexity first hour). Complexity 0.72 and high-priority 41% mean longer `get_service_time` (18.8 min) even for skilled workers. Seniors disproportionately take these carryover cases, masking their speed. Juniors assigned complex early work show 5.1% early error — a selection effect, not just inexperience. Warm-up also matters: stabilization by positions 6–15 (15.2 min, 2.8%) reflects re-orientation, system/login, handover sensemaking. But without adjusting for complexity, warm-up looks larger than it is.

**Middle of shift (positions 16–50, ~150–260 min in): genuine learning + favorable mix creates the sweet spot.** Complexity falls to 0.54–0.50, queue eases to 31–33, priority normalizes. Within this window, within-resource speed-up persists even holding WIP low and complexity low (Seniors 15.911.8 min with flat 2.1%2.2% error; Mids similar). This is real practice effect, rhythm, and reduced setup cost — true warm-up/learning. Errors bottom at 2.5–2.7% and rework at 4.8–5.0% because pressure is moderate and Quality Check still interleaved. This is the baseline against which late deterioration should be judged, not the contaminated start.

**Late shift (positions 51+, ~380–460 min in): queue pressure + batching + deferred quality dominate, not continued learning and not pure fatigue.** Demand accumulates, especially Day 3875 queue. Mean queue 6288 forces a strategic response: shorten service (11.410.8 min) by truncating Document Review, curtailing Customer Callback, and batching (Junior_H/I 9–18 consecutive short events). Mild pressure at 30–70 WIP gives small speed-up with small error cost (11.6 min/3.0% for low complexity). Beyond ~70 WIP the tradeoff steepens sharply (9.4 min/5.8% low complexity; 16.8 min/10.9% high complexity). Fatigue lowers the threshold for failure — after 6+ hours, vigilance for exceptions and Bot_Risk verification degrades — but fatigue alone would predict slowing, not speeding. The data show speeding, so pressure-induced rushing is the proximal driver, fatigue is a moderator. Circadian and shift context shape where this binds: Day late and Late shifts hit the high-WIP risk zone; Night stays slower (15.2 min) because work is exception-heavy and less batchable despite moderate queue (2955).

## Why service time falls while errors rise only in some conditions

Service time falls throughout because *three* forces push the same direction: (a) complexity falls, (b) practice increases, (c) pressure incentivizes rushing/batching. Errors follow a different logic: they fall when (a) and (b) dominate (middle shift, low WIP), and rise only when (c) dominates and verification is cut. Hence the divergence:

- Under low WIP later, speed-up is safe: Seniors 2.2%, Mids 2.9% error — learning without rushing preserves quality.
- Under high WIP later, the same speed-up is unsafe: Seniors 4.9%, Mids 6.8%, Juniors 12.4%, Night 8.2% — truncated checks, batched Document Reviews, and uncorrected Bot_Risk outputs inflate `error_flag` and 7-day `rework_flag` (8.2%13.7%).
- Juniors show both ends: early errors from difficult assignment + inexperience, late errors from pressure sensitivity. Seniors show resilience under low pressure but not immunity under high pressure, proving the late rise is systemic, not dispositional.
- Deferred Quality Check creates a measurement illusion: late service looks fast because defects are not yet counted; the cost appears downstream as burst corrections in last 45 min and as Rework Correction cases, not in concurrent service time. Removing that delay would make late service look slower but total system time more honest.

In short: early slowness = case-mix + warm-up; middle improvement = learning + favorable mix; late speed + error spike = queue-pressure rushing + batching workarounds amplified by fatigue and automation over-reliance, concentrated in high-WIP, low-experience, Day/Late contexts.

# 3. Proposed analyses

## Analytical principles
Always compare within-resource, within-shift, and within-complexity. Never compare raw Junior late vs Senior early. Treat `sequence_position_within_shift`, `minutes_since_shift_start`, `wip_at_start`, clock hour, and `case_complexity_score` as separate covariates, not proxies.

## Required cuts
- **within-resource comparisons:** For each `org:resource`, compare first 20 events vs later events, split by WIP. This removes between-worker skill mix. Expect Seniors flat quality under low WIP, Juniors elevated throughout.
- **within-shift position effects:** Plot mean service/error vs `sequence_position_within_shift` bins (1–5, 6–15, 16–30, 31–50, 51–75, 76+) separately for Low/Med/High WIP and low/high complexity. If position effect disappears within strata, it was confounding.
- **WIP/queue stratification:** Non-linear test around 70. Estimate service and error curves by `wip_at_start` deciles and `queue_length_at_start`. Distinguish `wip_at_start` (active) vs queue (waiting).
- **complexity adjustment:** Risk-adjust with `case_complexity_score` + `priority` + `activity_family`. Report raw and adjusted trends side-by-side to show how much of 18.810.8 min is mix.
- **shift and time-of-day controls:** Add `shift_id` (Early/Day/Late/Night) and clock hour fixed effects. Contrast same tenure (e.g., 60 min in) across shifts. Control weekend/night backlog carryover.
- **batching detection:** Identify sequential same-resource same-activity bursts with short gaps and low variance via `pm4py.discover_batches`. Quantify share of late speed-up attributable to batches.
- **resource-experience interactions:** Interact `resource_experience_months` / group (Senior/Mid/Junior/Night) with WIP and position. Test whether juniors degrade faster under pressure.
- **rework/error downstream analysis:** Link `error_flag` to `rework_flag` within 7 days and to Rework Correction + Quality Check bursts. Measure `insert_case_service_waiting_time` to show deferred QC inflates waiting, not service.

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

## Worker-level conclusions are unfair without adjustment
Raw late-shift error rates punish whoever worked the worst system conditions. Juniors show 12.4% error under high WIP vs 4.4% under low WIP; Seniors 4.9% vs 2.2% — the gap is the system, not character. Attributing Day 7.5% or Late 6.8% last-two-hours errors to individuals ignores that Day queue rose 3875 by management design (staffing vs demand) and that Junior_H/I batching was a workaround to that pressure. Night 5.1% error with 15.2 min service reflects urgent exceptions (56% complex first hour), not shirking. Any league table without `case_complexity_score`, `priority`, `wip_at_start`, `sequence_position`, `shift_id`, and `activity_family` controls is misleading.

## Selection bias
Seniors take complex carryover early, especially after weekend/night backlog; juniors get batched low-complexity late work under pressure. This creates Simpson’s paradox: seniors look slow early, juniors look fast late but error-prone. Random comparisons across resources or across early vs late positions without within-resource, within-complexity stratification will invert true skill. Forced ranking would then reward avoidance of hard cases.

## Measurement error
`service time` = `time:timestamp - start_timestamp` is sensitive to batch logging, multitasking, and delayed QC. Batched Document Reviews with very short recorded times understate true effort and overstate productivity. Deferred Quality Check understates late service cost and overstates throughput; cost reappears as `rework_flag` and Rework Correction elsewhere, often attributed to a different worker/shift. `error_flag` depends on detection effort — more QC finds more errors, so diligent teams can look worse. Bot_Risk instant times hide downstream human correction work when queue >70.

## Queue pressure created by management
WIP >70 is not a worker choice. It results from arrival rates, staffing, shift handover backlogs (Early 61, Late 70), and Day demand peaks left uncovered. Asking workers to go faster (10.8 min) while holding error rates constant is contradictory: the table shows 9.4 min/5.8% tradeoff is structural. Capping shifts, deferring QC, or penalizing errors without adding peak senior cover or smoothing arrivals merely transfers pressure to the next shift or to rework.

## Automation quality
Bot_Risk shifts risk, not removes it. When humans correct Bot_Risk more often under queue >70, the error is a joint human-automation failure under time scarcity, not human inattention alone. Auditing only human `error_flag` while ignoring bot confidence, override rate, and verification time rewards blind acceptance of bot outputs. Automation must be monitored as a contributor, with human verification time protected under high WIP.

## Punitive use of error rates
Punishment based only on raw late-shift rates (Intervention 8) is prohibited by evidence: it conflates fatigue, pressure, circadian context, mix, and batching; incentivizes under-reporting, cherry-picking, and avoidance of Night/Late/Day-peak shifts; and disproportionately harms juniors and night staff who handle the hardest assignment mix with least support. Use event-log indicators for system learning — WIP-stratified, risk-adjusted feedback, coaching, and workload rebalancing — with transparency, appeal, and focus on process redesign, not individual penalty. Never remove Quality Checks to make numbers look better; that trades visible throughput for invisible harm and future rework.

All views filterable by `shift_id`, `org:resource` group, `activity_family`, `priority`, and WIP band. Show raw *and* complexity-adjusted values.

## Indicator set (minimum eight, expanded to ten)

**1. Mean and p50/p90 service time (by activity, resource group, position bin).**
`pm4py.get_service_time` + `sequence_position_within_shift` bins. Overlay `case_complexity_score` adjusted trend. Goal: detect rushing (sudden drop with high WIP) vs learning (gradual drop with flat errors). Alert if p50 drops >15% while queue rises.

**2. Waiting time and queueing (case waiting, queue length).**
`pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` mean waiting before Document Review, Risk Classification, Quality Check; `queue_length_at_start` and `wip_at_start` time series. Goal: prevent hiding delay in waiting while service looks fast. Alert if QC waiting > SLA or Day queue trajectory 3875 repeats.

**3. Live WIP and queue pressure gauge.**
Current `wip_at_start`, queue, arrivals/finishes via `pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate`. Threshold line at 70 with time-over-threshold counter. Goal: trigger Intervention 3 response protocol. Show by shift (Early/Day/Late/Night start vs end queues).

**4. Error rate and rework within 7 days (risk-adjusted).**
`error_flag.mean()` and `rework_flag.mean()` overall + `pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity` for Risk Classification, Document Review, Quality Check. Show crude and complexity/priority-adjusted. Stratify low/high WIP. Goal: catch 5.8%/10.9% high-WIP risk zone early. Never rank individuals on crude rate.

**5. Complexity and priority mix.**
Mean `case_complexity_score`, high-complexity share, high-priority share by hour and by position bin. Goal: contextualize early 0.72/41% vs late 0.43/19% and Night 56% / Early 48% first-hour spikes. Flag cherry-picking or overload.

**6. Batching indicators.**
`pm4py.discover_batches`: count of batches, mean batch size, share of events in batches (especially Document Review Junior_H/I pattern 9–18), within-batch service variance and gap time. Goal: quantify workaround share of throughput. Alert on new bursts.

**7. Handover / queue ownership changes and carryover.**
Handover count between `org:resource` / shifts, backlog carryover (end queue  next start queue: e.g., Day 75  Late 70), cases with Quality Check or Close Case crossing shift boundary, Rework Correction reopen rate. Via `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg` handover arcs + `filter_paths_performance`. Goal: make deferred work visible; evaluate Interventions 2 and 6.

**8. Worker-load fairness and experience exposure.**
Events per `org:resource` per hour, `sequence_position_within_shift` distribution, complexity-weighted load (sum `case_complexity_score`), high-WIP exposure share by group (Senior/Mid/Junior/Night/Bot_Risk), overtime minutes. Dotted chart `pm4py.view_dotted_chart` + `pm4py.view_events_distribution_graph`. Goal: ensure juniors not concentrated in high-WIP batches and seniors present at peaks (Intervention 7). Fairness metric: complexity-adjusted load balance, not just count.

**9. Automation interaction (Bot_Risk).**
Bot_Risk share of Risk Classifications, human override/correction rate by WIP band (notably >70), verification time, bot-confidence vs correction. Goal: detect automation brittleness under pressure and protect review time.

**10. End-of-shift quality burst monitor.**
Quality Check events in last 45–120 min, correction yield, Close Case vs deferred QC ratio. Goal: prevent illusory throughput from delayed QC; link to downstream rework.

## Dashboard logic and governance
- Each panel shows three layers: crude value, WIP-stratified value, and complexity-adjusted value, with annotations for shift and batch flags.
- Drill-down: click WIP>70 point  see contributing resources, activities, batches, and Bot_Risk corrections.
- Fairness rule displayed: no individual error ranking; only system-level and risk-adjusted team trends with confidence intervals and case counts (8200/2800 etc. as denominator context).
- Evaluation mode for interventions: pre/post comparison using `pm4py.filter_time_range` and matched WIP/complexity controls, tracking both intended (service, queue) and unintended (rework, waiting, fairness) effects.
