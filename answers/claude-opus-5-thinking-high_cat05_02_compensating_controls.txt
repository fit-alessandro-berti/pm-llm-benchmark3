## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 is written as a resource-distinctness rule, but the rule is only an implementation of a bundle of objectives. Decomposing it is what makes it possible to say that a same-user case can still satisfy the objective, and that a two-user case can fail it.

**O1 — Prevent unilateral diversion of funds (single-actor fraud).**
One person creates or edits a payee/bank record and then authorizes payment to it.
- AV-2: *partially preventive*. Bank-account-ownership and bank-change-within-7-days checks and sanctions screening attack exactly this pattern, and a block is a hard stop. But the checks are only as good as the reference data, and they are overrideable.
- MG-4: *preventive* if the approval happens before release and the manager actually inspects the invoice and the bank details; *interdictive* (loss-preventing but not release-preventing) if it happens between release and settlement.
- RA-7: *detective*, and *corrective only inside the ~24h recall window*.
- ED-1: *does not address it*; ED-1 is a permission that presupposes O1 is handled by conditions 4, 5 and 6, and it explicitly denies that the objective disappears.

**O2 — Prevent unilateral error (wrong payee, wrong amount, duplicate, wrong currency, double keying).**
- AV-2: *strongly preventive*. Duplicate-invoice, operator-limit and purchase-order matching are mechanical checks that a second human often performs worse than software. AP-205 is the demonstration.
- MG-4: *preventive/interdictive*, and it adds judgment AV-2 lacks.
- RA-7: *detective*, *corrective inside the recall window* — error cases are the ones most likely to be genuinely recoverable, because a legitimate but mistaken counterparty often cooperates.
- ED-1: *does not address it*, though conditions 2 and 5 cap the size of an uncaught error.

**O3 — Independent substantive verification of entitlement (goods/services actually received, commercial reasonableness, contract and price conformity).**
- AV-2: *fails to address it* by explicit stipulation. A basic purchase-order match is a documentary consistency test, not a receipt test.
- MG-4: *the only control that can address it*, and only when the review covers invoice, bank details **and** supporting receipt. A rubber stamp gives zero coverage here while producing an approval event in the log.
- RA-7: *detective*; this is where audit is genuinely strong (AP-204's fabricated receipt was found by audit, not by AV-2 and not by the second releaser).
- ED-1: *does not address it*.

**O4 — Deter and detect collusion between two nominally distinct actors.**
This is the objective FEP-1 silently assumes away: two user IDs are not two independent minds.
- AV-2: *fails to address it* (stipulated).
- MG-4: *partially preventive* if the manager is outside the colluding unit and reviews substance; *fails* if the manager is inside the same reporting line or approves on trust (AP-202's m09).
- RA-7: *detective and the main line of defence*, especially through resource-pair and workstation analytics rather than single-case review.
- ED-1: *fails to address it*; and by legitimising single-operator flow it reduces the number of relationships that pair analytics can observe.

**O5 — Prevent concentration of authorization capability (credential sharing, override abuse, coerced or conflicted approval).**
- AV-2: *partially preventive* — a hard block cannot be waved away by the operator alone; but the override path converts a preventive control into a discretionary one, and if the override credential is in the operator's hands the control has been structurally defeated (AP-202).
- MG-4: *preventive only if independence is real*. "Not in a relationship that creates an unresolved conflict of interest" is doing heavy lifting: a manager who sets the operator's performance rating cannot be the independence guarantee for that operator.
- RA-7: *detective* on override rates and credential-attribution anomalies.
- ED-1: *aggravating unless conditions hold* — condition 5 ("passed without override") exists precisely to close this gap.

**O6 — Preserve a stop/recall opportunity before irrevocability.**
- AV-2: *preventive*, best-positioned in time (before release).
- MG-4: *preventive before release; interdictive if before settlement; purely ceremonial after settlement*.
- RA-7: *corrective only if it starts before the recall window closes*. "Within one business day" and "within 24 hours" are not the same clock, and AP-201 shows the gap.
- ED-1: conditions 6 and 7 are exactly this objective, expressed as deadlines.

**O7 — Accountability, non-repudiation and deterrence.**
Two named humans on the record create attributable responsibility, which deters before it detects.
- AV-2: *weakly supportive* — it produces reliable machine evidence, but no human accountability.
- MG-4: *supportive*, but only if the approval record states what was reviewed; an approval event with no review artefact is evidence of a click, not of scrutiny.
- RA-7: *supportive and reinforcing* — a credible 100% audit of same-user payments is itself a deterrent even when it cannot recover funds.
- ED-1: *neutral to negative* — it dilutes attribution unless the emergency token itself is strongly authorised and time-stamped by an independent system.

**O8 — Maintain integrity when an account or workstation is compromised.**
- AV-2: *fails to establish who controlled a compromised account* (stipulated).
- MG-4: *partially preventive*, because an out-of-band human review is a second channel an account takeover does not automatically own — unless the same workstation session serves both.
- RA-7: *detective at best*; forensic attribution after the fact is exactly what the fact pattern in AP-202 says is unresolved.
- ED-1: *does not address it*; single-operator flow maximises the value of a compromised session.

Two structural conclusions follow. First, **no combination of AV-2 and RA-7 can substitute for FEP-1**, because O3, O4 and O8 are untouched by AV-2 and only retrospectively touched by RA-7. Second, **MG-4 is the only true substitute component**, and its substitution value is a function of timing, independence and review depth — not of its existence in the log.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

### 2.1 Seven dimensions, each with a gating condition

A compensating package is scored on all seven; some dimensions are *gates* (failure caps the achievable tier regardless of the others), because control strength is limited by its weakest structural element, not by an average.

| # | Dimension | Question | Gate condition (failure caps the tier) |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **Timing** | When, relative to Release_Payment and to bank settlement, does the independent human judgment occur? | Independent human review after settlement caps the package at *merely mitigating*. |
| D2 | **Independence** | Is the second actor outside the preparer's reporting line, incentive structure, physical session and social relationship, and at least L3? | A conflicted or same-line approver caps the package at *ineffective/mitigating*, and the approval must not be counted as coverage. |
| D3 | **Coverage** | Which of O1–O8 are actually tested by the package, evidence by evidence? | Zero coverage of O3 (entitlement) caps at *partially equivalent* for any substantive-risk payment. |
| D4 | **Overrideability / integrity** | Can the party being controlled weaken or bypass the control? Was the control fully operational? | Operator-exercised override, or a degraded/unavailable AV-2 without a signed compensating manual check, caps at *ineffective*. |
| D5 | **Recoverability** | Does a defect discovered by the package still have a realistic stop, recall or recovery path? | Detection guaranteed only after the recall window means the package has *no corrective capacity*, so it cannot exceed *partially equivalent*. |
| D6 | **Evidence quality** | Is there an artefact showing *what* was checked (invoice, bank record, goods/service receipt, PO), not merely that an event occurred? | Approval without review artefacts is treated as unproven coverage, not as coverage. |
| D7 | **Collusion resistance** | Would the package still work if the two nominally distinct actors were cooperating, or if one session controlled both identities? | A package whose only human element sits inside the suspect unit gets no O4 credit. |

### 2.2 Tier definitions

**Fully equivalent** (treat as conforming to the objective; nominal FEP-1 violation is a documentation matter):
- an independent L3+ manager approved **before Release_Payment**, with recorded review of invoice, bank details and supporting receipt (D1, D2, D6 all strong); **and**
- AV-2 fully operational and clean **without override** (D4); **and**
- coverage includes O1, O2, O3 and O6 (D3); **and**
- a corrective path remained open through settlement, plus RA-7 inside the recall window (D5); **and**
- for same-user cases, the ED-1 preconditions are satisfiable on evidence, including the amount cap that bounds tail loss.
Collusion resistance (D7) is *structurally weaker* than genuine four-eyes even here, so "fully equivalent" means equivalent for O1–O3 and O5–O7, with a residual O4/O8 gap that the amount cap and 100% audit are accepted as bounding. AP-203 and AP-207 sit here.

**Partially equivalent** (technically nonconforming but tolerable):
- independent human review occurred **after release but before settlement**, or before release but without full substantive scope; **and**
- AV-2 clean without override; **and**
- exposure is bounded (small amount, established vendor, no bank change); **and**
- some corrective path existed for at least part of the window.
The residual gap is real but bounded and the case should be logged as a policy exception with follow-up, not as a control failure. AP-201 sits here; AP-206 sits here with an unresolved eligibility question.

**Merely mitigating** (the package reduces expected loss without addressing the objective):
- only detective coverage, or manager involvement after irrevocability, or coverage limited to what AV-2 mechanically checks while the risk actually lies in O3/O4/O8;
- the case is nonconforming and needs remediation, but there is no positive evidence of a defect. A distinct-user case with no manager review and no receipt verification also lands here, which is why AP-209 cannot be waved through on nominal conformance.

**Ineffective** (no meaningful compensation):
- the compensating control was degraded, overridden by the controlled party, performed by a conflicted actor, or timed entirely after irrevocability; or
- the package's coverage does not intersect the case's dominant risk. AP-202 and AP-208 sit here.

A separate outcome, **loss prevented before completion**, is not a tier of equivalence at all: the preventive control terminated the transaction, so the four-eyes objective was never put to the test on a completed payment (AP-205).

### 2.3 Why "same expected loss" is not the only admissible standard

Expected loss is one legitimate lens, but adopting it as *the* standard would be wrong for at least eight reasons:

1. **Outcome luck.** AP-208 produced no loss and AP-202 produced a total loss with structurally similar weaknesses (no reliable bank-ownership evidence at release time, no independent pre-settlement judgment). Judging by realised loss would rank a €900,000 uncontrolled release as fine. Control adequacy is an *ex ante* property of the control system.
2. **Truncated observation.** Expected loss must be estimated from detected defects, and the very controls we are assessing determine what gets detected. Packages with weak O3 coverage systematically *under-report* their own loss rate, so their estimated expected loss is biased downward exactly where they are weakest.
3. **Tail shape, not mean.** Two packages can share a mean while differing enormously in variance. A €480,000 unrecoverable single event and a stream of small recoverable errors are not interchangeable for solvency, audit opinion, or reputational risk.
4. **Recoverability is a distinct dimension.** A defect caught at hour 3 and a defect caught at hour 30 can have the same probability but very different loss given detection. Expected loss can hide this by averaging; control design must not.
5. **Deterrence and behavioural effects are endogenous.** Segregation of duties changes the probability that anyone attempts fraud. A compensating package with the same measured expected loss under honest behaviour may have a much higher loss under adversarial adaptation — an override credential in operators' hands invites the very behaviour it was meant to control.
6. **Duty-based and assurance obligations.** Segregation of duties is a stated policy, often an external audit and regulatory expectation. An organisation must be able to *demonstrate* control, and a package that yields the same expected loss but no evidential trail fails the assurance objective even if it succeeds financially.
7. **Correlated failure and single points of dependence.** AV-2-heavy packages concentrate reliance on one system; AP-208 shows what a single outage does. A four-eyes structure degrades gracefully; a validated-automation structure fails all at once across every case in the outage window.
8. **Non-financial objectives.** Sanctions exposure, fraud-detection duty, insider-relationship conflicts and the ability to attribute responsibility after an incident are not fully priced in euros.

The practical rule I apply: **expected loss determines severity and prioritisation; coverage, timing, independence, integrity and recoverability determine classification.**

### Manager action after Release_Payment but before settlement: my chosen semantics

I classify it as **hybrid, and specifically "interdictive"** — detective with respect to the release decision, preventive with respect to the loss event.

The reasoning is that control type should be defined relative to a named harm event, and there are two candidate harm events here. Relative to *the unauthorised release itself*, the control is unambiguously detective: the instruction already exists, the segregation-of-duties objective has already been formally breached, and nothing the manager does can un-issue the release. Relative to *the irrecoverable loss of funds*, the same action is genuinely preventive, because the ADDITIONAL FACTS establish settlement as the point of irrevocability for internal-control purposes, and a manager acting before it can still stop the money. Since the dominant business objective of FEP-1 is preventing an erroneous or fraudulent *transfer of value*, and since the loss event is the one stakeholders care about, I give substantial preventive credit — but not full credit.

Three consequences of these semantics, applied consistently above:

1. **It is strictly weaker than pre-release approval,** for reasons that are not merely formal. The window (24 min in AP-201, 35 min in AP-207) is an interval in which an unreviewed instruction is live and could settle early, be batched, or be irrevocably processed by an intermediary. The approver also faces a status-quo bias: reversing a released payment is costlier and more embarrassing than declining to release one, which measurably raises rubber-stamp risk. And the operator retains an information advantage during the gap.
2. **The credit is contingent on settlement genuinely not having occurred and on the reversal path being real.** AP-201 and AP-207 earn the credit; AP-202 (two days later) and AP-208 (after settlement) earn none. Interdictive credit therefore requires evidence that a stop instruction would have been actioned, not merely that a timestamp precedes settlement.
3. **The same time-relative logic applies to RA-7,** which is why I treat AP-201's audit at ~24.9 hours as purely detective while AP-206's at ~19 hours and AP-207's at ~22 hours retain corrective value. "Within one business day" can be satisfied while the 24-hour recall window is missed; the two deadlines should be separated in policy, and the binding one is the bank's.

## 5. Counterfactual and Residual-Risk Analysis

Qualitative bands: negligible / low / moderate / high / very high. Any number below is explicitly labelled illustrative and is not derived from data in the log.

**AP-202 (€480,000, realised loss).**
*Most plausible loss mechanism:* an insider-facilitated payee substitution. A new vendor is onboarded or its bank record is changed two hours before payment; the operator prepares and releases to an account later linked to an employee's relative, defeating AV-2 by applying an override credential belonging to the manager who rates his performance.
*Which control breaks the chain:* AV-2 already did its job — it raised the bank-change and operator-limit warnings. The chain is broken only if **the override is structurally unavailable to the operator** and requires the independent authenticated action of an L3+ manager outside the operator's reporting line, before release. Nothing else in the package could have worked: RA-7 at +8 days is post-recall by a wide margin, and m09 is disqualified by conflict.
*What remains exposed even with fixes:* collusion between operator and a genuinely independent manager; vendor-master integrity upstream of payment; and forensic attribution of who controlled the override credential, which the fact pattern says is unresolved and may stay that way.
*Under a truly independent second releaser:* the loss probability drops sharply (illustratively from near-certain to low, perhaps 10–20% — an illustrative figure only), because an independent releaser inspecting an unoverridden AV-2 warning about a two-hour-old bank change on a new vendor with no receipt confirmation would have to ignore four simultaneous red flags. Residual risk concentrates in the collusion scenario, and the €480,000 size makes a bank-callback verification on an independently sourced number the necessary second layer.

**AP-204 (€220,000, confirmed fabricated receipt).**
*Most plausible loss mechanism:* fictitious or inflated services. A consulting invoice is supported by a fabricated service receipt; both members of a habitual two-person cell approve inside a shared session, so nobody independently tests whether the service was received.
*Which control breaks the chain:* only **MG-4 with mandatory receipt and deliverable verification by someone outside the cell, before release**. AV-2 is stipulated to be blind to receipt, and the two-user structure is exactly what failed. Secondarily, mandatory rotation of the releaser and a rule that shared-workstation sessions cannot supply both signatures.
*What remains exposed:* whether this was collusion or negligent mutual rubber-stamping is unresolved, and the other 36 joint payments are unexamined; recovery is uncertain because detection came 4 days out, well past the recall window.
*Under a truly independent second releaser:* the outcome depends on whether "independent" includes substantive receipt testing. A distinct-ID releaser *already existed* and did not help — this case is the proof that FEP-1's formal test is not self-sufficient. Only a releaser who is organisationally distant **and** required to sight the service acceptance materially reduces risk; that combination would likely cut the loss probability from high to low-moderate.

**AP-208 (€900,000, no loss realised).**
*Most plausible loss mechanism:* payment to a fraudulently altered bank account. The vendor is 8 months old, its bank details changed three days earlier, and the one control specifically designed to catch bank changes inside seven days was offline. A single operator released with no signed manual substitute and no independent human judgment before irrevocable settlement.
*Which control breaks the chain:* an **outage protocol** that either defers non-critical payments or mandates dual release plus documented out-of-band bank verification (callback to a number from the contract, not the invoice) with a signed checklist, plus **pre-settlement independent approval**, plus a hard amount ceiling for degraded-validation mode far below €900,000.
*What remains exposed:* even now, no contemporaneous bank-ownership evidence exists for the release moment, so the assertion of legitimacy rests on the absence of a complaint rather than on evidence. If this account had been compromised, the money would have been unrecoverable — ratification at +24h and audit at +4 days both land after any recall.
*Under a truly independent second releaser:* moderate improvement only. A second human cannot replace the bank-ownership check that AV-2 was performing; without out-of-band verification, an independent releaser looking at the same unverifiable data reduces risk from high to moderate at best. This is the case where the compensating package needed a *different kind* of control, not a second signature.

**AP-201 (€1,800, no defect).**
*Most plausible loss mechanism:* a small unilateral error or a low-value fictitious purchase — the classic same-user convenience risk, on a low-risk office-supply payment to a 6-year vendor.
*Which control breaks the chain:* AV-2 already covers duplicate, limit and PO matching, and m04's independent pre-settlement review covers the rest; the marginal control is really the **management rule** that same-user flow requires an emergency basis, since there was none.
*What remains exposed:* receipt of goods was never independently verified, and if the same-user shortcut is tolerated as convenience it will recur on larger payments where the same package would be inadequate. RA-7 arriving at ~24.9 hours means that on a future case the recall window would already be gone.
*Under a truly independent second releaser:* negligible change in expected loss on this case — exposure is €1,800 — but a meaningful change in precedent. The right response is to fix the exception process and the audit clock, not to escalate the case.

**AP-206 (€95,000, no defect).**
*Most plausible loss mechanism:* abuse of the emergency channel — an operator initiates a single-handed payment and the emergency justification is constructed around it, using the urgency of a production outage to bypass the second signature.
*Which control breaks the chain:* an **independently issued, tamper-evident emergency token verified as active before Prepare_Payment**, plus MG-4 sequenced *after* AV-2 so the approver actually sees the validation result. m11's pre-release approval is the substantive protection here, and it worked.
*What remains exposed:* the 2-minute token discrepancy is unresolved between benign clock/logging delay and precondition failure; the approval's depth is unrecorded and, having occurred one minute before the AV-2 result, cannot have incorporated it.
*Under a truly independent second releaser:* small improvement in loss probability (the manager already supplied independent pre-release judgment) but a large improvement in evidential clarity, since the emergency-token question would simply not arise. The cheap fix is clock discipline and sequencing, not a new control.

**AP-209 (€42,000, no defect found).**
*Most plausible loss mechanism:* the AP-204 mechanism repeated at lower value — mutual rubber-stamping inside a habitual pair sharing a workstation, with no independent test of service receipt and no manager anywhere in the flow.
*Which control breaks the chain:* **releaser rotation plus a workstation/session-distinctness check**, so that two IDs cannot be exercised from one session, and risk-based MG-4 review for payments above a threshold.
*What remains exposed:* everything O3, O4 and O8 covers is untested; the audit found no defect, but its scope is unknown and AP-204 shows this pair's output can contain fabricated support that only substantive audit detects.
*Under a truly independent second releaser:* this is the sharpest illustration of the section's theme. The case *already has* two releaser IDs; what it lacks is independence in substance. Substituting a rotated releaser from outside the cell would move the case from INDETERMINATE toward conforming with little added cost — which is why the right output here is targeted evidence collection, not a sanction.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

### 6.1 Evidence to collect as standing requirements

1. **Approval artefacts, not approval events.** Every MG-4 record must enumerate what was inspected (invoice ID, bank record version and its last-change date, goods/service receipt or acceptance, PO) and must be captured as an attestation, not a status flag. Rule: an approval with no artefact list is scored as *no coverage*.
2. **Independence register.** Machine-readable reporting lines, performance-rating relationships, and declared personal relationships between operators, releasers and approvers, evaluated at the timestamp of the approval. AP-202 is unarguable only because we know m09 rates u23.
3. **Override provenance.** Which credential authorised each override, which authenticated session used it, and from which device. Override credentials must be non-delegable and non-shareable by construction, ideally with a second-factor bound to the manager.
4. **Emergency token records.** Issuer identity, issue time from an authoritative clock, business justification, linked incident ticket, and validity window — with documented clock-skew tolerance so the AP-206 question is answerable rather than debatable.
5. **Bank-master change lineage.** Requester, approver, old and new details, verification method (out-of-band callback to a number from the contract), and time since change at the moment of release.
6. **Control-health telemetry.** AV-2 uptime, per-check pass/fail/unavailable status, and the specific checks exercised per payment, so "full pass" is decomposable rather than a single word.
7. **Session and device attribution.** Workstation, IP, and session identifiers for each event, enabling detection of two user IDs exercised from one session.
8. **Settlement irrevocability timestamps and recall-attempt outcomes**, so corrective capacity can be measured rather than assumed.
9. **Audit scope records.** Whether an audit tested entitlement (receipt/deliverable) or only documentary consistency. "No defect found" is uninterpretable without scope — this is what keeps AP-209 indeterminate.

### 6.2 Monitoring rules

Each rule states a signal, a suggested trigger, and the required response. Thresholds are starting calibrations to be tuned on the actual population, not derived from these nine cases.

| # | Monitor | Suggested trigger | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Same-user rate** (same resource on Prepare and Release, as % of completed payments, by operator, unit and month) | Unit rate above ~2%, or any operator above ~5%, or any same-user payment lacking a valid pre-Prepare token | Exception report to control owner; 100% RA-7 within the recall window; capacity review if the driver is staffing rather than emergency |
| M2 | **Override rate and concentration** (overrides per 100 warnings, by operator, by override credential, by manager whose credential was used) | Any operator-executed override on their own payment (zero tolerance); credential used from a session not belonging to its owner; unit rate above ~1% | Immediate credential suspension and investigation of custody; treat the underlying payment as unvalidated |
| M3 | **Late manager approval** (approval after Release, and after settlement) | Any approval after settlement (zero tolerance); post-release approvals above ~10% of same-user cases; median release-to-approval gap rising | Post-settlement approvals reclassified as ceremonial and the case reported as nonconforming; gap trend escalated as a rubber-stamp risk indicator |
| M4 | **Audit-before-recall-window** (hours from release to *audit start*, against the bank's 24h recall clock, not only "one business day") | Any same-user or overridden payment whose audit starts after 24h; queue ageing above ~18h | Re-scope RA-7 scheduling to an hours-based SLA with weekend cover; AP-201's ~24.9h is the archetype of a policy that reads compliant and is operationally too late |
| M5 | **Repeated resource pairs** (concentration of preparer–releaser pairs; e.g. a pair's share of either member's volume) | A pair exceeding ~25–30% of either member's payments, or absolute counts like u12/u13's 37 in 30 days | Mandatory rotation, targeted substantive audit of a sample of the pair's payments, and a demand for the organisational-design document; **alert only** |
| M6 | **Shared workstation / session overlap** (two payment identities from one device or overlapping session) | Any Prepare and Release from the same session; same device within a short interval without an intervening logout | Treat nominal four-eyes as unproven for those cases; enforce session distinctness technically |
| M7 | **Bank-change proximity** (hours between last bank-detail change and release; combined with vendor age and amount) | Change within 7 days (AV-2's own window), and especially within 24h on a new vendor or above a value threshold | Mandatory out-of-band verification plus independent pre-release approval; block rather than warn above a value threshold |
| M8 | **Control outages** (AV-2 unavailable, per-check degradation, with payments released during the window) | Any release during an outage; any outage release above a degraded-mode ceiling (well below €100,000); any unsigned manual checklist | Automatic queue-and-hold by default; named senior authorisation with signed checklist for exceptions; full post-outage sweep of every payment released during the window |
| M9 | **Composite risk score** combining M1–M8 with amount, vendor age, receipt evidence and ED-1 eligibility | Two or more concurrent indicators on one payment | Pre-release hold. AP-202 and AP-208 each carried four or more simultaneous indicators; correlated indicators, not single ones, are the actionable pattern |
| M10 | **ED-1 eligibility audit** (per-condition pass/fail, per case) | Any invoked emergency where any of the seven conditions fails or is unverifiable | Reclassify as an unauthorised same-user payment for reporting purposes even where substantive risk was low (AP-201, AP-206, AP-208) |

### 6.3 An alert is not proof of misconduct

This distinction should be written into the monitoring policy, not left to the reviewer's tact:

- **Alerts are statements about structure and base rates**, not about persons. "This pair handles 37 payments together" describes an organisational arrangement that removes an assumed independence; it is equally consistent with a deliberately designed two-person payment cell (the missing design document) and with collusion. The correct output is a request for evidence.
- **The burden of proof differs by consequence.** Requiring rotation, extra approval or a targeted audit is a control decision and needs only a plausible risk signal. Alleging fraud affects a person's employment and legal position and needs corroborated, individually attributable evidence — session logs, credential custody, document provenance, benefit flows.
- **Base rates matter.** Even a well-tuned monitor generates mostly false positives in a population where fraud is rare. Publishing the false-positive rate of each monitor protects both the programme's credibility and the people it flags.
- **Control failures should be reported without attribution where attribution is unproven.** AP-208 is a critical control failure with no identified wrongdoer; AP-202's control verdict is certain while the question of whether m09 shared the credential is explicitly unresolved and must be reported as such. AP-209 must be reported as *indeterminate pending evidence*, not as a suspected fraud, and u12/u13's denial of collusion in AP-204 remains untested rather than disproved.
- **Separate the tracks.** Monitoring output feeds process remediation immediately; disciplinary or forensic processes start only on a defined escalation with documented independence, and their conclusions — not the alerts — are what may be recorded against individuals.

        ed1_token_before_prepare = "UNVERIFIED_TIME"
    elif pd.isna(tok):
        ed1_token_before_prepare = "NO_TOKEN"
    elif pd.notna(t_prep) and tok <= t_prep:
        ed1_token_before_prepare = "OK"
    else:
        ed1_token_before_prepare = "TOKEN_AFTER_PREPARE"

    ed1_amount_ok = bool(f["amount_eur"] < 100_000)
    ed1_vendor_ok = bool(f["vendor_days_active"] >= 180)
    bank_change_d = f["bank_change_days_ago"]
    ed1_no_recent_bank_change = bool(pd.isna(bank_change_d) or bank_change_d > 7)
    ed1_av_clean = bool(f["av2_status"] == "full_pass" and not override)

    # --- manager timing (ED-1 condition 6) ----------------------------------
    if pd.isna(t_mgr):
        mgr_timing = "NO_MANAGER"
    elif pd.notna(t_rel) and t_mgr <= t_rel:
        mgr_timing = "BEFORE_RELEASE"
    elif pd.notna(t_settle) and t_mgr <= t_settle:
        mgr_timing = "AFTER_RELEASE_BEFORE_SETTLEMENT"
    elif pd.isna(t_settle):
        mgr_timing = "AFTER_RELEASE_SETTLEMENT_UNKNOWN"
    else:
        mgr_timing = "AFTER_SETTLEMENT"
    mgr_before_av_result = bool(
        pd.notna(t_mgr) and pd.notna(t_av) and t_mgr < t_av
    )
    ed1_mgr_before_settlement = mgr_timing in {
        "BEFORE_RELEASE", "AFTER_RELEASE_BEFORE_SETTLEMENT"
    } and bool(f["manager_independent"]) is True

    # --- audit timing (ED-1 condition 7) plus the recall clock --------------
    if pd.notna(t_audit) and pd.notna(t_rel):
        audit_h = round((t_audit - t_rel).total_seconds() / 3600.0, 1)
        bdays = int(
            np.busday_count(
                t_rel.date().isoformat(), t_audit.date().isoformat()
            )
        )
        audit_within_1_bday = bdays <= 1
        audit_within_recall = audit_h <= RECALL_WINDOW_H
    else:
        audit_h, audit_within_1_bday, audit_within_recall = (
            np.nan, None, None
        )

    ed1_conditions = {
        "c1_token_before_prepare": ed1_token_before_prepare,
        "c2_amount_under_100k": ed1_amount_ok,
        "c3_vendor_180d": ed1_vendor_ok,
        "c4_no_bank_change_7d": ed1_no_recent_bank_change,
        "c5_av2_clean_no_override": ed1_av_clean,
        "c6_indep_mgr_before_settlement": ed1_mgr_before_settlement,
        "c7_audit_within_1_business_day": audit_within_1_bday,
    }
    ed1_failed = [
        k for k, v in ed1_conditions.items()
        if v is False or v in {"NO_TOKEN", "TOKEN_AFTER_PREPARE"}
    ]
    ed1_unverified = [
        k for k, v in ed1_conditions.items()
        if v is None or v == "UNVERIFIED_TIME"
    ]
    if not completed:
        ed1_status = "NOT_APPLICABLE_NO_RELEASE"
    elif not same_user:
        ed1_status = "NOT_INVOKED_DISTINCT_USERS"
    elif ed1_failed:
        ed1_status = "FAILS:" + ",".join(sorted(ed1_failed))
    elif ed1_unverified:
        ed1_status = "UNVERIFIED:" + ",".join(sorted(ed1_unverified))
    else:
        ed1_status = "ALL_CONDITIONS_SATISFIABLE"

    rows.append(
        {
            "case": case,
            "completed_payment": completed,
            "same_user_prepare_release": same_user,
            "system_account_in_pair": case in system_actor_cases,
            "blocked_before_completion": case in blocked_cases,
            "amount_eur": f["amount_eur"],
            "av2_status": f["av2_status"],
            "operator_override": override,
            "av_coverage_flag": (
                "COVERED_CHECKS_ONLY" if f["av2_status"] == "full_pass"
                else "PREVENTED" if f["av2_status"] == "blocked"
                else "NEUTRALISED_BY_OVERRIDE"
                if f["av2_status"] == "warning_overridden"
                else "NO_COVERAGE_OUTAGE"
            ),
            "ed1_token_flag": ed1_token_before_prepare,
            "manager_timing": mgr_timing,
            "manager_independent": f["manager_independent"],
            "manager_substantive_review": f["manager_substantive_review"],
            "manager_approved_before_av_result": mgr_before_av_result,
            "audit_hours_after_release": audit_h,
            "audit_within_1_business_day": audit_within_1_bday,
            "audit_within_24h_recall_window": audit_within_recall,
            "bank_change_days_ago": bank_change_d,
            "bank_change_within_7d": (not ed1_no_recent_bank_change),
            "vendor_days_active": f["vendor_days_active"],
            "receipt_evidence": f["receipt_evidence"],
            "ed1_status": ed1_status,
        }
    )

flags = pd.DataFrame(rows).set_index("case").sort_index()

# --- repeated preparer/releaser pairs (log-derived + tabulated fact) --------
pair_rows = []
for case in completed_cases:
    p = sorted((prep_res.get(case, set()) - {"SYSTEM"}))
    r = sorted((rel_res.get(case, set()) - {"SYSTEM"}))
    pair_rows.append(
        {
            "case": case,
            "pair": f"{p[0] if p else 'NA'}->{r[0] if r else 'NA'}",
        }
    )
pairs = pd.DataFrame(pair_rows).set_index("case")
pair_counts = pairs["pair"].value_counts()
pairs["pair_count_in_log"] = pairs["pair"].map(pair_counts)
pairs["repeated_pair_in_log"] = pairs["pair_count_in_log"] > 1
pairs["pair_count_per_case_facts_30d"] = facts["facts_pair_count_30d"]
flags = flags.join(pairs, how="left")

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. Report
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
print("=" * 100)
print("EVIDENCE FOR JUDGMENT - NOT A COMPLIANCE VERDICT")
print("=" * 100)

print("\n[pm4py environment notes]")
for note in PM4PY_NOTES:
    print("  -", note)
print(f"  - filter_four_eyes_principle polarity: {pm4py_polarity}")
print(f"  - cases returned by pm4py helper : {pm4py_returned_cases}")
print(f"  - pandas ground-truth same-user   : {pandas_violations}")
if pm4py_returned_cases is not None and pm4py_polarity.startswith("unrecog"):
    print("  - WARNING: helper output could not be reconciled; pandas result used.")

print("\n[A] Nominal FEP-1 status (completed payments only)")
print(f"  completed payments            : {completed_cases}")
print(f"  nominal violations (same user): {nominal_violations}")
print(f"  system/shared account in pair : {system_actor_cases or 'none'}")

print("\n[B] Attempted but blocked releases (never completed; FEP-1 as written "
      "binds completed payments)")
print(f"  blocked/cancelled cases       : {blocked_cases}")
print(f"  of which same-user attempts   : {blocked_same_user}")

print("\n[C] Emergency eligibility (ED-1) and AV-2 coverage")
print(flags[[
    "same_user_prepare_release", "amount_eur", "av2_status",
    "operator_override", "av_coverage_flag", "ed1_token_flag", "ed1_status",
]].to_string())

print("\n[D] Manager timing / independence and audit timing")
print(flags[[
    "manager_timing", "manager_independent", "manager_substantive_review",
    "manager_approved_before_av_result", "audit_hours_after_release",
    "audit_within_1_business_day", "audit_within_24h_recall_window",
]].to_string())

print("\n[E] Bank-change proximity, vendor age, receipt evidence, repeated pairs")
print(flags[[
    "bank_change_days_ago", "bank_change_within_7d", "vendor_days_active",
    "receipt_evidence", "pair", "pair_count_in_log", "repeated_pair_in_log",
    "pair_count_per_case_facts_30d",
]].to_string())

print("\n[F] Monitoring aggregates (indicators, not conclusions)")
n_completed = len(completed_cases)
print(f"  same-user rate over completed payments : "
      f"{len(nominal_violations)}/{n_completed} = "
      f"{len(nominal_violations) / n_completed:.0%}")
print(f"  operator-executed override count       : "
      f"{int(flags['operator_override'].sum())}")
print("  audits starting after the 24h recall window: "
      + str(sorted(
          flags.index[flags["audit_within_24h_recall_window"] == False]  # noqa: E712
      )))
print("  manager action after settlement / none     : "
      + str(sorted(flags.index[flags["manager_timing"].isin(
          ["AFTER_SETTLEMENT", "NO_MANAGER"])])))
print("  AV-2 not providing coverage at release     : "
      + str(sorted(flags.index[flags["av_coverage_flag"].isin(
          ["NO_COVERAGE_OUTAGE", "NEUTRALISED_BY_OVERRIDE"])])))
print("  repeated preparer/releaser pairs           : "
      + str(sorted(flags.index[flags["repeated_pair_in_log"] == True])))  # noqa: E712

print("\n" + "=" * 100)
print("READING THE OUTPUT")
print("- Flags describe timing, coverage, independence and eligibility only.")
print("- A same-user case may still meet the control objective (see AP-203, "
      "AP-207) and a distinct-user case may still fail it (see AP-204).")
print("- 'No defect found' is uninterpretable without audit scope; absence of "
      "a flag is not assurance.")
print("- Repeated pairs, shared workstations and override events are ALERTS "
      "requiring evidence, never proof of misconduct.")
print("- Final classification requires the qualitative analysis in Sections "
      "1-6 of this report.")
print("=" * 100)

### PM4Py behaviour I cannot guarantee across versions

1. **`filter_four_eyes_principle` polarity.** Across releases this helper has been documented both as returning the cases *violating* the principle and as a filter that *retains* cases (with a `positive`-style parameter in some builds). This is not hypothetical: on pm4py 2.7.23.4 the bare positional call returns the *conforming* cases (`AP-204`, `AP-209`), i.e. the complement of the same-user set, so a script that reported its output directly as "violations" would invert every finding in this report. Rather than trusting either reading, the script computes the same-user set in pandas, compares it to whatever the helper returns, and prints which interpretation matched. If neither matches, it says so and uses the pandas result.
2. **Accepted input type and keyword names.** Some versions accept a formatted DataFrame; others expect an `EventLog` from `pm4py.convert_to_event_log`. Keyword names for the key parameters (`activity_key`, `resource_key`, `timestamp_key`, `case_id_key`) were added and renamed over time, and older builds accept positional arguments only. The script tries a bare positional call first, then the fully keyworded call, and reports failures instead of crashing.
3. **Return container.** The result may be a DataFrame or an `EventLog`; case identifiers are extracted differently in each, so both paths are handled.
4. **`format_dataframe` side effects.** It standardises `case:concept:name` / `concept:name` / `time:timestamp`, and in several versions adds helper columns such as `@@index` and `@@case_index`; it may also emit deprecation warnings, and some versions normalise or localise timestamps. Timestamps are therefore parsed with `utc=True` beforehand, and `org:resource` is left untouched because it is already the standard resource key.
5. **Module path.** The helper has been exposed as `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` and, in some versions, under `pm4py.algo.filtering.*` / `pm4py.filtering`. Only the top-level name is called here, inside a guard.
6. **Availability.** If pm4py is absent or any call fails, the script degrades to the pandas ground truth and prints the reason, so the evidence tables are still produced.

Two substantive limitations of the automated check itself, independent of pm4py: it cannot see settlement times that exist only in the facts table (AP-202 and AP-204 are recorded as unknown rather than assumed), and it cannot evaluate independence, review depth, collusion or entitlement — those come from the independence register, approval artefacts and audit scope listed in Section 6, which is exactly why the output is labelled evidence rather than verdict.
