## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 is a means, not the objective. Its purpose is to stop one person from both initiating and authorizing an erroneous or fraudulent transfer. The objective breaks into the following threat scenarios, each with different preventive and detective needs.

- **T1: Single-person initiation and authorization.** One human prepares and releases a bad payment with no independent authority in between. This is the core FEP-1 objective.
- **T2: Payee redirection or bank-account substitution.** Funds go to an account controlled by someone other than the vendor, often after a recent bank-detail change.
- **T3: Duplicate or mis-keyed payment.** The same invoice is paid twice, or an amount or payee is entered wrongly.
- **T4: Fictitious or inflated goods and services.** A payment is made without real delivery, or with a fabricated or inflated receipt. This is a commercial-substance failure that account checks cannot see.
- **T5: Collusion or rubber-stamping between two humans.** Two distinct IDs act as one unit, for example a habitual pair with a shared workstation, so "distinct users" does not give independence.
- **T6: Control bypass through overrides or delegated credentials.** A warning is overridden, or an approval is given by someone with a conflict of interest or a credential shared with the preparer's line.
- **T7: Emergency-path misuse or control outage.** Single-operator release is used without valid preconditions, or while AV-2 is unavailable.
- **T8: Irrevocability and timing.** The loss becomes final before anyone detects it, which depends on the bank recall window.

Which control addresses which threat:

| Threat | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 Single-person authorization | Partial preventive: checks only what it covers; does not establish authority | Preventive if independent and before settlement; hybrid if after release | Detective; corrective only within recall window | Permits the single path; does not prevent misuse; fails if preconditions fail |
| T2 Payee redirection | Preventive for ownership and 7-day changes; warning is overrideable | Preventive if bank details are actually reviewed | Detective; recall possible only early | Gated by condition 4 (no bank change in 7 days) |
| T3 Duplicate payment | Preventive (duplicate check) | Preventive/detective if the reviewer checks duplicates | Detective | Not an ED-1 concern except as a general check |
| T4 Fictitious goods/services | Fails: does not verify receipt or commercial reasonableness | Preventive only if the approver inspects receipt against the system of record | Detective, late, may find fabricated receipts | No protection |
| T5 Collusion or rubber-stamping | Fails: no collusion detection | Fails unless independence and substance are verified | Detective via pattern analytics only | Not applicable |
| T6 Override or credential misuse | Fails if override is allowed without independent authority | Fails if the approver is conflicted or the credential is shared | Detective | Not applicable |
| T7 Emergency misuse or outage | Must be operational; an outage removes preventive coverage | Preventive only if before settlement | Detective | Is itself the gate; conditions must be verified, not assumed |
| T8 Irrevocability | Not applicable | Preventive if before settlement | Corrective only within about 24 h | Not applicable |

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

**Dimensions.** For each threat present in a case, rate each relevant control on seven dimensions: timing (before release, before settlement, or after settlement), independence (level, reporting line, conflicts, credential ownership), coverage (does it test the threat at all), overrideability (can it be bypassed, and by whom), recoverability (can the effect still be undone), evidence quality (documented substantive review versus checkbox), and collusion resistance (is the second human truly separate).

Each dimension is rated Strong, Adequate, Weak, or Absent.

**Equivalence classes** (assessed per threat, not per case average):

- **Fully equivalent.** For each threat present, a control rates Strong on timing, independence, coverage, and evidence, with no override path below independent authority. It stops the threat before irrevocable effect without relying on the preparer's cooperation. A distinct releaser is not always required for equivalence; a substantive, independent, pre-settlement gate can be equivalent.
- **Partially equivalent.** The threat is covered before irrevocable effect, but one decisive dimension is Adequate or Weak. Examples: review depth undocumented, approval precedes the automated evidence, level undocumented, or a hold depends on unverified system enforcement.
- **Merely mitigating.** The package detects or partly corrects the threat, but cannot stop irrevocable effect, or independence is weak. Loss is reduced, not prevented.
- **Ineffective.** The control ran but could not have stopped the threat (coverage gap), was bypassed, lacked independence, or was absent when required.

**Decision procedure.**
1. Identify which threats are plausible in the case (for example, a bank change implies T2).
2. For each threat, determine whether any control acts before irrevocable settlement.
3. Test independence and override paths.
4. Test evidence sufficiency. A control with no reliable evidence cannot be relied upon.
5. Assign the class using the weakest decisive dimension.
6. Map to the case label:
   - PREVENTED if the threat was blocked before completion.
   - OBJECTIVE_MET if every present threat is fully equivalent.
   - OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET if the threats are covered before irrevocable effect with documented gaps. This is the "technically nonconforming but tolerable" category.
   - OBJECTIVE_FAILED if any present threat is ineffective or merely mitigating with realized loss or no prevention, or with an override by a non-independent authority.
   - INDETERMINATE if decisive evidence is missing and the rating cannot be assigned responsibly.

**Why expected loss is not the only standard.**
- **Irrevocability and tail risk.** Expected loss averages over outcomes. A single unrecoverable payment can be catastrophic even if most payments are safe. AP-208 was legitimate, yet its control design was unsafe.
- **Outcome luck masks failure.** A legitimate payment through a failed control is evidence of luck, not control effectiveness. The objective concerns authority separation, not the result of one payment.
- **Authority separation is a binary property.** A control that lacks independence has failed its objective even if no loss occurred. AP-202's override by a conflicted approver is a failure regardless of other outcomes.
- **Low-frequency, high-impact fraud.** Sparse data cannot estimate its frequency. Judgment must rest on mechanism and control design.
- **Assurance and auditability.** Controls are relied upon for assurance only if they produce reliable evidence. Missing evidence is a finding in its own right.
- **Portfolio effects.** Repeated pairing (AP-204, AP-209) shows a systemic weakness that no single case reveals. Per-case expected-loss estimates miss it.
- **Deterrence.** A same-user path that works in practice invites misuse, whatever the expected loss in the current sample.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Dimension | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary semantics | Preventive gate; becomes detective when a warning is overridden | Preventive if before release or settlement and independent; hybrid if after release and before settlement | Detective; corrective only within recall window | Conditional permission on who may act alone; not a control on outcomes |
| Typical timing | Before release | Before release (strongest) or before settlement | After settlement, usually | Token before Prepare; approval before settlement |
| Can stop irrevocable effect? | Yes, if it blocks and cannot be overridden by a non-independent authority | Yes, if the settlement hold is enforced | Rarely | No; it only permits |
| Can correct? | No | Can cancel before settlement | Only within the recall window | No |
| Typical failure | Coverage gap (receipts, collusion); overrides; outage | Rubber-stamping; conflicts; credential sharing; approval before evidence | Late audit; recall window missed | Preconditions unmet or misdated |
| Evidence produced | Check results; override log | Approval record; review artifacts | Audit findings | Token log; conditions checklist |

**Semantics of a manager action after Release_Payment and before settlement.** I classify this as hybrid: preventive with respect to settlement, detective with respect to release. The rule is that semantics follow the event the control can gate. The approval is preventive only if three things hold: the system holds the settlement until approval, the approval is substantive, and the manager can reject. If settlement is automatic, or the approval is a checkbox, the same action is merely detective, and the package is weaker. In this review, AP-201 and AP-207 depend on the settlement hold, which must be verified rather than assumed.

**ED-1 semantics.** ED-1 is not a detective or preventive check. It is a conditional permission. It changes who may act alone, and it is valid only if its seven conditions are actually met. Its risk lies in the preconditions it relies on, not in its own operation.

## 5. Counterfactual and Residual-Risk Analysis

The qualitative judgments below are not numerical estimates.

**AP-202 (EUR 480,000, bank changed two hours earlier).**
- Mechanism: payment redirected to a related party after a bank-detail change.
- Control that broke the chain: none effective. AV-2 detected the change, but the override was cleared under a credential tied to a conflicted approver.
- Still exposed: funds gone after recall window; relationship between vendor and employee unresolved.
- Under a truly independent second releaser (an L3 approver without a conflict, using their own credential, required to verify bank details by call-back): the warning would have forced a review by someone outside the preparer's line. Likelihood of stopping the payment is high.

**AP-204 (EUR 220,000, fabricated receipt).**
- Mechanism: a fictitious service is paid with a fabricated receipt; the pair passes it together.
- Control that broke the chain: none. AV-2 does not check receipts; no approval is evident for this amount.
- Still exposed: pair-based collusion; late audit; funds possibly unrecoverable.
- Under a truly independent second releaser (different workstation, different reporting line, required to match receipt against the system of record): the fabricated receipt would likely be caught at release. Residual exposure is collusion with a third party, which requires investigation rather than design alone.

**AP-208 (EUR 900,000, bank changed three days earlier, AV-2 unavailable).**
- Mechanism: payment to a recently changed account while automated bank-ownership validation was down.
- Control that broke the chain: none. The approval came after settlement.
- Still exposed: unverified bank ownership; irrevocable settlement.
- Under a truly independent second releaser with a signed manual checklist and call-back verification before settlement: the release would have been held until bank ownership was confirmed. The outcome was luck, and the design must change.

**AP-201 (EUR 1,800, same user, no emergency).**
- Mechanism: convenience-driven single-user release with an error or fraud undetected at release.
- Control that broke the chain: m04's pre-settlement review, which is independent.
- Still exposed: depth of review; late audit; no emergency basis.
- Under a truly independent second releaser: the difference is small in expected loss, because m04's gate already performs the authorization function. The main gain is removing the same-user path, which removes reliance on the gate's diligence.

**AP-207 (EUR 35,000, emergency with conditions).**
- Mechanism: legitimate emergency repair payment processed by one operator.
- Control that broke the chain: m15's pre-settlement review and the two-hour hold.
- Still exposed: reliance on the hold being enforced; emergency-token misuse.
- Under a truly independent second releaser at release: little change in loss; the main effect would be delay. ED-1 exists for this case, so the residual question is whether the conditions are verified each time.

**AP-209 (EUR 42,000, distinct IDs, repeated pair).**
- Mechanism: a pair cell that could rubber-stamp; no defect found.
- Control that broke the chain: AV-2 for vendor and bank checks, and the timely audit.
- Still exposed: undetected pair collusion, detectable only through pattern analysis.
- Under a truly independent releaser: reduces collusion exposure materially, though this single case may show no difference.

**AP-206 (EUR 95,000, late token, approval before AV-2).**
- Mechanism: single-user release under an unverified emergency basis.
- Control that broke the chain: m11's pre-settlement approval.
- Still exposed: approval given without automated results; ED-1 precondition question.
- Under a truly independent second releaser: little change, since the approval already gated settlement. The concern is process ordering and token integrity.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

**Metrics and alerts.** Thresholds are illustrative and should be calibrated on full population data. Values below are from this curated extract and are not rates.

- **Same-user rate.** Share of completed payments where preparer equals releaser, by amount band, team, and approver. Extract: 6 of 8 completed payments. Alert on any same-user payment outside a documented ED-1 window.
- **Override rate.** Overrides as a share of AV-2 warnings. Extract: 1 of 1 warning overridden. Alert on any override where the credential is not held by the overriding person, or where the holder is the overriding person's line manager or rater.
- **Late manager approval.** Approval after release or after settlement. Extract: AP-201 and AP-207 (after release, before settlement), AP-202 and AP-208 (after settlement or unknown settlement). Alert when approval follows settlement or precedes automated evidence (AP-206).
- **Audit before recall window.** Audit later than 24 h after release or settlement, where this is a proxy for the bank's recall window. Extract: 4 of 8 audited beyond 24 h (AP-201 at 24.9 h; AP-202, AP-204, AP-208 well beyond).
- **Repeated resource pairs.** Pair frequency and asymmetry of roles. Extract: u12u13 appears twice; facts report 37 joint payments in 30 days. Alert on pairs above a calibrated frequency, especially with high-value payments or one-sided approvals.
- **Shared workstations.** Same host for preparer and releaser. Extract: AP-204 and AP-209 per facts. Alert on any shared host for a completed payment.
- **Bank-change proximity.** Payments within 7 days of a vendor bank change. Extract: AP-202 (2 hours), AP-208 (3 days). Alert and require call-back verification; no override path.
- **Control outages.** AV-2 unavailable, token activation after prepare, release attempts after a block. Extract: AP-208 (AV-2 down), AP-206 (late token), AP-205 (release attempt after block). Alert on any outage during which non-emergency payments complete.

**Alert versus proof.** An alert is a risk indicator that routes a case to review. It does not establish misconduct. A shared workstation may reflect a designed team, a late token may be a logging delay, and a repeated pair may reflect legitimate workflow. Proof requires an evidence chain: source-system records, bank confirmations, credential and identity logs, receipt verification against the system of record, documented interviews, and testing of alternative explanations. Pair alerts in particular should be handled with care to avoid unfairly targeting individuals.

- Credential custody and use logs for the override credential in AP-202.

- Manager-approval policy thresholds for amounts of EUR 220,000 and below.

**Recommendations.**
- Restrict the same-user path to ED-1, enforced by the system rather than by convention.
- Make AV-2 outage fail closed for non-emergency payments.
- Remove override paths that are available to subordinates or to credential holders who are not the overriding person.
- Hold settlement until an independent substantive approval is recorded for all releases above a defined threshold.
- Require receipt verification against the system of record before release.
- Rotate pair assignments and separate workstations for preparers and releasers.
- Audit every same-user and emergency payment within 24 hours.

    for case in sorted(completed):
        g = ev[ev["case:concept:name"].eq(case)]
        prep = set(g.loc[g["concept:name"].eq("Prepare_Payment"), "org:resource"]) - SHARED_SERVICE_ACCOUNTS
        rel = set(g.loc[g["concept:name"].eq("Release_Payment"), "org:resource"]) - SHARED_SERVICE_ACCOUNTS
        overlap = sorted(prep & rel)
        rows.append({"case:concept:name": case, "preparers": sorted(prep),
                     "releasers": sorted(rel), "shared_human": overlap,
                     "nominal_violation": bool(overlap)})
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)


# ---- PM4Py cross-check (version-sensitive; never trusted over the oracle) ----
def pm4py_four_eyes(ev):
    try:
        import pm4py
    except ImportError:
        return None, "pm4py not installed; pandas oracle is the reference"
    if not hasattr(pm4py, "filter_four_eyes_principle"):
        return None, "pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle not exposed in this version; oracle only"
    try:
        df = pm4py.format_dataframe(ev.copy(), case_id="case:concept:name",
                                    activity_key="concept:name",
                                    timestamp_key="time:timestamp")
    except Exception as exc:
        return None, f"pm4py.format_dataframe failed: {exc!r}"
    attempts = [
        lambda: pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(df, "Prepare_Payment", "Release_Payment"),
        lambda: pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df),
                                                 "Prepare_Payment", "Release_Payment"),
    ]
    last_exc = None
    for attempt in attempts:
        try:
            res = attempt()
            res_df = res if isinstance(res, pd.DataFrame) else pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(res)
            return set(res_df["case:concept:name"].unique()), "pm4py filter executed"
        except Exception as exc:
            last_exc = exc
    return None, f"pm4py filter failed: {last_exc!r}"


# ---- Per-case timeline from the log ----
def build_timeline(ev):
    rows = []
    for case, g in ev.groupby("case:concept:name", sort=True):
        g = g.sort_values("time:timestamp")
        act = g["concept:name"]
        acts = set(act)

        def first_ts(mask, g=g):
            s = g.loc[mask, "time:timestamp"]
            return s.iloc[0] if len(s) else pd.NaT

        def first_res(mask, g=g):
            s = g.loc[mask, "org:resource"]
            return s.iloc[0] if len(s) else None

        prep_m = act.eq("Prepare_Payment")
        rel_m = act.eq("Release_Payment")
        aud_m = act.eq("Audit_Review")
        mgr_m = act.str.startswith("Manager_")
        set_m = act.eq("Bank_Settlement")
        av_m = act.str.startswith("Automated_Validate") | act.eq("Validation_Service_Unavailable")

        if "Validation_Service_Unavailable" in acts:
            av_log = "unavailable"
        elif "Automated_Validate_Block" in acts:
            av_log = "block"
        elif "Automated_Validate_Warning" in acts:
            av_log = "warning_override" if "Override_Warning" in acts else "warning"
        elif "Automated_Validate" in acts:
            av_log = "full_pass"
        else:
            av_log = "not_run"

        prep_ts, rel_ts = first_ts(prep_m), first_ts(rel_m)
        aud_ts, mgr_ts, av_ts = first_ts(aud_m), first_ts(mgr_m), first_ts(av_m)
        audit_hours = ((aud_ts - rel_ts).total_seconds() / 3600
                       if pd.notna(aud_ts) and pd.notna(rel_ts) else np.nan)
        rows.append({
            "case": case,
            "prep_res": first_res(prep_m), "rel_res": first_res(rel_m),
            "prep_ts": prep_ts, "rel_ts": rel_ts,
            "av_log": av_log, "av_ts": av_ts,
            "mgr_ts": mgr_ts, "mgr_res": first_res(mgr_m),
            "settle_log_ts": first_ts(set_m),
            "audit_hours": audit_hours,
            "attempt_release": "Attempt_Release" in acts,
            "approval_before_av": bool(pd.notna(mgr_ts) and pd.notna(av_ts) and mgr_ts < av_ts),
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)


def manager_timing(r):
    if pd.isna(r["mgr_ts"]):
        return "no_manager_event"
    if pd.isna(r["rel_ts"]):
        return "no_release"
    if r["mgr_ts"] < r["rel_ts"]:
        return "before_release"
    if pd.isna(r["settle_ts"]):
        return "after_release_settlement_unknown"
    if r["mgr_ts"] <= r["settle_ts"]:
        return "after_release_before_settlement"
    return "after_settlement"


def _tri(x):
    """Normalize to True/False/None."""
    if x is None or (not isinstance(x, str) and pd.isna(x)):
        return None
    return bool(x)


def _pf(v):
    return "UNVERIFIED" if v is None else ("PASS" if v else "FAIL")


def ed1_eval(r):
    c = {}
    c["1 emergency token active before Prepare"] = _pf(_tri(r["token_before_prepare"]))
    c["2 amount < EUR 100k"] = "PASS" if r["amount_eur"] < ED1_AMOUNT_LIMIT_EUR else "FAIL"
    age = r["vendor_age_days"]
    c["3 vendor active >= 180 d"] = ("UNVERIFIED" if pd.isna(age)
                                     else ("PASS" if age >= ED1_MIN_VENDOR_AGE_DAYS else "FAIL"))
    bc = _tri(r["bank_change_7d"])
    c["4 no bank change in 7 d"] = "UNVERIFIED" if bc is None else ("FAIL" if bc else "PASS")
    c["5 AV-2 full pass, no override"] = (
        "PASS" if (r["av2_fact"] == "full_pass" and not _tri(r["av2_override"])) else "FAIL")
    ind = _tri(r["manager_independent"])
    lvl = _tri(r["manager_l3_documented"])
    if not isinstance(r["manager_id"], str) or pd.isna(r["mgr_ts"]) or ind is False:
        c6 = "FAIL"
    elif pd.isna(r["settle_ts"]) or ind is None:
        c6 = "UNVERIFIED"
    elif r["mgr_ts"] > r["settle_ts"]:
        c6 = "FAIL"
    elif lvl is None:
        c6 = "UNVERIFIED"          # independence documented, L3 level not
    else:
        c6 = "PASS" if lvl else "FAIL"
    c["6 independent MG-4 before settlement"] = c6
    h = r["audit_hours"]
    c["7 RA-7 audit within proxy window"] = ("UNVERIFIED" if pd.isna(h)
                                             else ("PASS" if h <= RECALL_WINDOW_HOURS else "FAIL"))
    vals = list(c.values())
    if "FAIL" in vals:
        overall = "NOT_ELIGIBLE"
    elif "UNVERIFIED" in vals:
        overall = "ELIGIBLE_PENDING_EVIDENCE"
    else:
        overall = "ELIGIBLE_ON_AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE"
    return c, overall


def assemble(ev):
    tl = build_timeline(ev)
    facts = load_facts()
    m = tl.merge(facts, on="case", how="left")
    m["settle_ts"] = m["settlement_fact"].fillna(m["settle_log_ts"])
    m["mgr_timing"] = m.apply(manager_timing, axis=1)
    completed = m["rel_ts"].notna()
    pair_counts = Counter(zip(m.loc[completed, "prep_res"], m.loc[completed, "rel_res"]))
    m["pair"] = list(zip(m["prep_res"], m["rel_res"]))
    m["pair_count_in_log"] = [pair_counts.get(p, 0) if c else 0
                              for p, c in zip(m["pair"], completed)]
    m["pair_fact_count"] = [PAIR_FACT_COUNTS.get(p) for p in m["pair"]]
    return m


def main():
    ev = load_events(CSV_TEXT)

    oracle = nominal_oracle(ev)
    violating = set(oracle.loc[oracle["nominal_violation"], "case:concept:name"])

    pm_cases, pm_msg = pm4py_four_eyes(ev)
    print("== PM4Py cross-check ==")
    print(pm_msg)
    if pm_cases is not None:
        if pm_cases == violating:
            print("PM4Py result agrees with pandas oracle:", sorted(pm_cases))
        else:
            print("PM4Py DISAGREES with oracle. pm4py:", sorted(pm_cases),
                  "oracle:", sorted(violating),
                  "-> verify the filter's semantics (violating vs. compliant) in this version.")

    m = assemble(ev)
    rows = []
    for _, r in m.iterrows():
        ed_c, ed_overall = ed1_eval(r)
        completed = pd.notna(r["rel_ts"])
        blocked = bool(r["attempt_release"] or (r["av_log"] == "block" and not completed))
        pf = r["pair_fact_count"]
        rows.append({
            "case": r["case"],
            "completed": completed,
            "nominal_fep1_violation": r["case"] in violating,
            "blocked_attempt": blocked,
            "amount_eur": r["amount_eur"],
            "ed1_overall": ed_overall,
            "ed1_failed": ";".join(k for k, v in ed_c.items() if v == "FAIL"),
            "ed1_unverified": ";".join(k for k, v in ed_c.items() if v == "UNVERIFIED"),
            "av_fact": r["av2_fact"],
            "av_log": r["av_log"],
            "av_consistent": r["av2_fact"] == r["av_log"],
            "override_flag": bool(_tri(r["av2_override"])) or r["av_log"] == "warning_override",
            "mgr_timing": r["mgr_timing"],
            "late_manager_flag": r["mgr_timing"] in ("after_release_settlement_unknown",
                                                     "after_settlement"),
            "approval_before_av_flag": bool(r["approval_before_av"]),
            "audit_hours": round(float(r["audit_hours"]), 1) if pd.notna(r["audit_hours"]) else None,
            "audit_over_recall_proxy": (bool(r["audit_hours"] > RECALL_WINDOW_HOURS)
                                        if pd.notna(r["audit_hours"]) else None),
            "bank_change_7d": _tri(r["bank_change_7d"]),
            "pair": f"{r['prep_res']}->{r['rel_res']}" if completed else None,
            "pair_count_in_log": int(r["pair_count_in_log"]),
            "repeated_pair_flag": bool(r["pair_count_in_log"] >= 2 or
                                       (pf is not None and not pd.isna(pf) and pf >= PAIR_FACT_THRESHOLD)),
            "shared_workstation": _tri(r["shared_workstation"]),
            "label": "EVIDENCE_FOR_JUDGMENT",
        })
    flags = pd.DataFrame(rows)

    print("\n=== EVIDENCE FOR JUDGMENT ONLY - NOT THE FINAL COMPLIANCE VERDICT ===")
    with pd.option_context("display.max_columns", None, "display.width", 250):
        print(flags.to_string(index=False))

    print("\n=== Blocked or attempted releases (not completed; not FEP-1 completions) ===")
    print(flags.loc[flags["blocked_attempt"], ["case", "av_log", "completed"]].to_string(index=False))

    print("\n=== Nominal FEP-1 violations among completed payments (pandas oracle) ===")
    print(oracle.loc[oracle["nominal_violation"]].to_string(index=False))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
