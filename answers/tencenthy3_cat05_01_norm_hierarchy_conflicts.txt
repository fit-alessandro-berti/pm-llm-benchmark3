- **Later-in-time / specificity:** ECD-2 (specific, time-bound, signed) defeats B on the dimensions it expressly names (PO timing, supplier register) within its window/scope, but later-in-time does not automatically defeat GPIP-4 where C5 is silent.
- **Emergency necessity:** A declared emergency or factual urgency does not itself void written controls; it only activates defined waivers (B4, C1–C4). A6 prevents retroactive curing.
- **CFO email D:** Treated as a valid interpretation of *Finance* controls (D3) so that joint IC authorization substitutes for Finance/budget pre-approval up to EUR 150,000, but D does not and cannot waive A3 or A1 (D3, D4).
- **ECD-2 scope (C6):** Restrained reading — “critical sterilization-line lots” covers goods needed within ~24h to maintain/restore directly affected sterilization; backup-line goods with multi-day stock are probably outside.
- **Burden of proof:** Lies with the purchasing actor to show a pre-commitment control was satisfied or a valid exception applied. Missing evidence  NONCOMPLIANT or INDETERMINATE, not presumed compliant.
- **Retrospective evidence:** Contemporaneous artifacts (telephone recordings, signed PDFs, supplier acknowledgments, access logs) rebut late/disordered ERP timestamps when an outage or clock fault is known.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-501  possible JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** Premise change: ECD-2’s “precedence over ordinary purchasing timing” (C4) is read to *retroactively ratify* a commitment made 10 minutes before the directive’s 09:00 start, treating the whole incident response as a single continuous act; and/or B4 “actively delegated Shift Lead” is interpreted loosely where an imminent 4-hour stoppage made formal delegation impractical. Credible because the emergency was genuine and the amount was low-risk (<50k, no GPIP-4 trigger). Weak because A6 expressly says later approval/event does not auto-cure, ECD-2 was not effective at 08:50, and no delegation existed.

**P-502  possible JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION / COMPLIANT.** Premise change: “supplier-onboarding requirements” in C4 is interpreted to include GPIP-4 A3 Compliance_Clearance for new suppliers, so the post-commitment clearance at 18:30 is permitted. Credible because Compliance clearance for a new supplier is onboarding-like and ECD-2 explicitly targeted the contamination emergency. Weak because C5 expressly notes ECD-2 does not mention Compliance clearance, and A4 reserves all non-tendering controls unless a CRO exception exists.

**P-504  possible JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** Premise change: B4 “imminent safety harm” is broadened to food-safety/spoilage, the cafeteria is deemed production-support (employee safety), and ECD-2 C2/C6 is stretched to any contamination-event continuity need. Credible only if “site continuity” is accepted as sterilization-adjacent. Weak because the record explicitly states no sterilization or production effect, and Internal Audit’s narrow C6 reading would exclude it.

**P-508  possible JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** Premise change: D2 (“above EUR 150,000, obtain Legal clearance as well”) is read as implying that, above the D1 cap, joint IC authorization plus Legal clearance *replaces* Finance Controller approval, i.e., the CFO implicitly waived Finance for >150k when Legal is present. Credible because the CFO owns Finance and may have intended a streamlined high-value path. Weak because D4 states the email is “not meant to remove essential integrity controls” and Finance pre-approval is a core integrity control; nothing expressly waives A2 above 150k.

*(Additional note: P-503 could shift to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION only if prior overlay practice is held to make COO+IC signing a de facto A7 for sterilization contingencies and if Operations’ broad C6 view prevails—weak given CRO reservation and zero Compliance event. P-506 could shift if an oral extension were credited—weak against A7’s written requirement.)*

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Replacement Exception Governance Clause (resolves authority, scope, thresholds, segregation, new suppliers, retrospective records, expiry, and anti-normalization):**

1. **Hierarchy.** GPIP-4 integrity controls (A1 segregation, A2 budget+Finance pre-approval, A3 Compliance pre-clearance) are baseline and may be suspended only by a *written, scoped, time-limited exception issued or expressly ratified by the CRO*. An incident directive signed by COO + Incident Commander may suspend *local timing and supplier-onboarding* controls (PO-before-commit, approved-supplier register) for a defined emergency scope/window, but does **not** suspend A1, A2, or A3 unless the CRO ratifies in writing within 24 hours.
2. **Scope definition.** “Critical sterilization-line lots” means goods required within **24 hours** to maintain, restore, or safely contain directly affected sterilization operations. Backup-line goods with >24h stock are excluded unless expressly listed by Incident Command.
3. **Thresholds.** For any purchase EUR 50,000, independent budget owner + Finance Controller approval are required pre-commitment. During a ratified ECD window, the CFO may designate joint IC authorization as substituting for *Finance* approval up to EUR 150,000; above EUR 150,000, Legal clearance is required **in addition**, and Finance Controller approval remains mandatory.
4. **New suppliers / >EUR 100k.** Compliance_Clearance is mandatory pre-commitment. During a ratified ECD window, commitment may occur up to 4 hours before clearance only if clearance is recorded within that 4 hours and the CRO ratifies; otherwise it is a deviation.
5. **Records.** Verbal authorizations must be recorded within 4 hours using any contemporaneous medium (signed PDF, recording, supplier acknowledgment). ERP timestamps are rebuttable by such evidence. **No oral extensions** of an ECD; expiry is firm.
6. **Anti-normalization safeguard.** All emergency purchases EUR 25,000 are auto-flagged for post-incident audit. If >20% of monthly purchase value uses emergency routing for three consecutive months, the CRO must report to the board and suspend non-ratified emergency delegation.
