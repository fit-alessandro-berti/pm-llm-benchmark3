## 1. Interaction-graph description

Model the evidence as a typed multigraph G = (V, E). Nodes are typed: **Event** (typed by activity), **Job**, **Component**, **ComponentLot**, **Machine**, **Tool**, **Batch**, **Shipment**. Edges carry qualifiers; the analytically important ones are:

- `requires` (JobComponent), `contains` (ComponentLotComponent) — direct O2O relations.
- `batches` (BatchJob), `consolidates` (ShipmentJob) — direct O2O relations.
- `executes-on` (Job/BatchMachine) — direct, from co-related process events.
- `holds / acquires / releases` (JobTool) — **inferred** from acquire/release event qualifiers; the log has no `Wait for Tool` activity, so tool-induced waiting is never an event.
- `gates` (ComponentLotJob) — **inferred**; lot events are co-related to jobs only in some integration pipelines (the Job flattening drops them entirely).
- `waits-for-partner` (JobJob via Shipment) — **inferred** from timestamp arithmetic, not from any event.
- `precedes` (EventEvent) — temporal ordering only; precedence  causation.

**Higher-order motifs (more than two object types):**

- **M1 – Batch barrier:** {J1, J2, J3} –B1– {OVEN-1, RACK-7} –{C1, C2, C3}. A single `Batch Cure` event at 12:05 binds nine objects; three jobs' timelines are welded to one clock.
- **M2 – Tool block:** J3 –FIX-A J4 –CNC-2 (with C4). A hold by one job manifests as a wait by another job at a machine; the machine record shows only a partial idle gap.
- **M3 – Lot gate:** L7 –{C2, C5} {J2, J5}  B2. Lot state propagates into batch formation; J2 bypasses via override, J5 complies, and the whole batch start shifts.
- **M4 – Consolidation coupling:** {J1 (packed 13:30), J4 (packed 19:10)} –S1 dispatch 19:25. Asymmetric readiness is coupled at dispatch.
- **M5 – Calibration wedge:** {J5, J6} –CAL-3– TEST-1, with a 45-min recalibration inserted between their tests.

Compact edge list (direct = D, inferred = I):

```text
J1: requires C1 (D) | member-of B1 (D) | member-of S1 (D) | cured-in B1@OVEN-1/RACK-7 (D)
J2: requires C2 (D) | C2 in L7 (D) | member-of B1 via override (D+I) | member-of S2 (D)
J3: requires C3 (D) | C3 in L8 (D) | member-of B1 (D) | holds FIX-A 09:00–11:40 (I) | paces B1 (I)
```

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

Each item is an arithmetic gap under a stated counterfactual; none is causal proof.

1. **J1 in B1:** batch start 12:05  ready 08:10 = **3 h 55 min**. Counterfactual: per-job curing on readiness (oven available, size-1 batch)  wait  0. Assumes readiness timestamps are trustworthy and J1 had no alternative value-adding work in the window.
2. **J2 in B1:** 12:05  08:25 = **3 h 40 min**. Same counterfactual and caveats.
3. **Coordination cost of B1:** J3 (ready 11:55) paces the batch; total job-waiting injected by one synchronization event = 3 h 55 + 3 h 40 + 0 h 10 = **7 h 45 min**. Counterfactual: no batching  this pool vanishes, but oven changeover frequency rises.
4. **J4 at FIX-A/CNC-2:** staging wait = process start 11:45  CNC-2 arrival 09:50 = **1 h 55 min**. FIX-A was held by J3 until 11:40, leaving a 5-min setup  up to **~1 h 50 min** tool-attributable. Counterfactual: a second FIX-A  J4 starts when CNC-2 is free. Caveat: CNC-2's recorded idle gap is only 10:05–10:47 (42 min), so the machine-side record does not show continuous availability; either unrecorded setup/activity fills 10:47–11:45 or the FIX-A attribution is an overestimate. Treat as an upper bound.
5. **J1 at S1:** dispatch 19:25  pack 13:30 = **5 h 55 min** packed dwell. Counterfactual: J1 dispatches alone under S2's observed policy (dispatch = last pack + 25 min)  dispatch  13:55, saving **~5 h 30 min**. Assumes dispatching early has no contractual penalty.
6. **J6 at CAL-3:** test start 17:25  ready 16:05 = **1 h 20 min**. Counterfactual: CAL-3 continuously available (no recalibration, or a second unit)  J6 starts  16:05–16:10 (J5 began 5 min after ready) and finishes  16:35 instead of 17:50, saving **~75–80 min**. TEST-1's idle 16:35–17:25 confirms the machine was not the constraint.
7. **J4 at B2 (joint attribution):** B2 starts 14:50; J4 ready 13:10  **1 h 40 min**. Counterfactual A (L7 never quarantined, RACK-7 still busy until 14:35): start  ~14:35–14:45  lot contributes little. Counterfactual B (RACK-7 free earlier, J5 still ready 14:40): start  14:40–14:50  similar. Counterfactual C (both relaxed): start  13:10–13:20  J4's wait  0–10 min. J4's wait is jointly produced by L7's release (via J5) and RACK-7 turnover; the split is indeterminate from current data.
8. **Spillover of CAL-3 onto J5 via S3:** J6's extra ~80 min pushes J6's pack time; S3 dispatches 35 min after the last pack  J5's dispatch slips by up to ~80 min despite J5 testing at 16:10–16:35. Bounded inference: exact pack times for J5/J6 are not given.

## 4. What flattening misses

- **Job flattening.** Visible: per-job activity sequences, the two rework loops, medians per edge. Hidden/duplicated: the single 12:05 cure event is duplicated into J1, J2, J3 traces (inflating frequency 3×); lot quarantine/release absent (pipeline drops them); tool waiting has no activity; packed-then-waiting dwell invisible. Misleading conclusion: "no job exceeds 1.8 SD" and "PackDispatch median 1.1 h" — yet J1's packed dwell is 5 h 55 m. Medians over 6 jobs bury exactly the waits managers complain about.
- **Machine flattening.** Visible: utilization and idle gaps. Hidden: which jobs or batches the idle time serves; a waiting job generates no machine event. Misleading conclusion: "max idle 48–73 min, utilization 58%  no bottleneck," while J4 simultaneously waited 1 h 55 m at CNC-2.
- **Batch flattening.** Visible: membership and cure timing of B1, B2. Hidden: per-job readiness spread inside the batch; J2's override; RACK-7's cleaning (a tool event). Misleading conclusion: "Batch–Machine interactions = 2, low frequency  minor" — those two events coordinate five of six jobs.
- **Tool flattening.** Visible: acquire/release intervals (FIX-A 09:00–11:40), CAL-3 recalibration, RACK-7 cleaning. Hidden: the queue of jobs wanting the tool (no queue object exists). Misleading conclusion: FIX-A "busy 09:00–11:40" reads as productivity; it is precisely the hold that idles CNC-2 and delays J4. Tool-busy  value-adding.
- **ComponentLot flattening.** Visible: quarantine 10:20, release 13:50 on L7. Hidden: impact on J2/J5 because lot events are inconsistently linked to jobs; the override is in another system entirely. Misleading conclusion: "quarantine closed at 13:50, no further impact" — while J5's flow was gated and B2's start shifted.
- **Shipment flattening.** Visible: CreateLoadDispatch per shipment (0.8 h + 0.3 h medians). Hidden: how long already-packed jobs wait for partners; J1 waited 5 h 55 m. Misleading conclusion: "shipment cycle  1.1 h  logistics is fast" — the flattening measures the shipment's lifecycle, not the jobs' dwell inside it.

**Why low utilization coexists with long waiting:** utilization is the machine-busy share of time; the binding constraints here are non-machine objects — batch barriers, exclusive tools, lot holds, consolidation policy. A machine idles precisely while a job waits for a tool or a batch partner: the machine's idle gap and the job's wait are two views of the same synchronization slack. Worse, much of this waiting is recorded as *elapsed time between* events rather than as any activity, so no machine-side counter ever sees it.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**Narrative A — Tool  Machine  Batch  Shipment (FIX-A  J4  B2  S1  J1).** *Temporal precedence:* J3 acquires FIX-A 09:00, releases 11:40; J4 reaches CNC-2 09:50; J4's process starts 11:45; J4 ready for B2 13:10; B2 cures 14:50; J4 packs 19:10; S1 dispatches 19:25. *Resource feasibility:* CNC-2 shows idle capacity (gap 10:05–10:47); FIX-A is the infeasible resource until 11:40. *Policy:* FIX-A is exclusive-use; no `Wait for Tool` activity exists, so the wait is inferential. *Causal speculation:* J4's staging wait is largely FIX-A-induced (upper bound ~1 h 50 m), and via B2 and S1 a tool hold at 09:00 ends up delaying J1's dispatch at 19:25 — but the 10:47–11:45 hole in CNC-2's record forbids a clean causal claim.

**Narrative B — Lot  Component  Job  Batch  Tool (L7  J5  B2/RACK-7  J4).** *Precedence:* L7 quarantined 10:20, released 13:50; J5 ready 14:40; RACK-7 cleaning until 14:35; B2 starts 14:50; OVEN-1 idle 12:55–14:50. *Feasibility:* oven free from 12:55; rack free 14:35; J5 ready 14:40 — all three constraints release within 15 minutes of the start. *Policy:* lot quarantine is mandatory, yet a supervisor override admitted J2 into B1 with quarantined-lot C2 at 12:05. *Speculation:* B2's start is gated by max(J5 readiness, RACK-7 availability); both bind nearly simultaneously, so the lot's contribution to J4's 1 h 40 m wait cannot be separated from the rack's without more data.

**Narrative C — Tool  Machine  Job  Shipment (CAL-3  J6  S3  J5).** *Precedence:* J5 ready 16:00, J6 ready 16:05; J5 tests 16:10–16:35; CAL-3 recalibrated 16:35–17:20; J6 tests 17:25–17:50; S3 dispatches 35 min after last pack. *Feasibility:* TEST-1 idle 16:35–17:25; CAL-3 unavailable. *Policy:* every Final Test requires CAL-3; the recalibration wedge sits between the two tests. *Speculation:* if recalibration was triggered by J5's test, J5's presence delays J6, and J6's delay propagates to J5's own dispatch via S3 — a genuine circular coupling. If recalibration was calendar-scheduled, J6's wait is pure policy. The log cannot distinguish the two.

**Narrative D — compliance (brief).** J2's B1 entry at 12:05 used C2 from quarantined L7 (quarantine 10:20–13:50), legitimized only by an override stored outside this event log. Temporally coherent, resource-feasible, policy-violating — and invisible in every flattening shown.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

**I1. Decouple batch synchronization** (dynamic batch window; allow size-1 cure on demand).
- *Target mechanism:* batch barrier (rank 1). *Expected benefit:* removes up to 3 h 55 m (J1-type) and 3 h 40 m (J2-type) per cycle. *Helped:* early-ready members (J1, J2). *Harmed:* pace-setters like J3 (no gain); OVEN-1 hourly throughput may fall; later arrivals may instead wait for oven availability. *New cost/risk:* energy and changeover per cure; recipe drift. *Perverse metric:* OVEN-1 utilization % — maximizing it recreates large batches and the barrier. *Evaluation design:* object-centric — per-job e2o delta (job-ready  Batch Cure start) stratified by batch size, with batch membership from `batches` edges; compare before/after distributions and partner waits.

**I2. Second FIX-A unit, or reservation-with-timeout.**
- *Target:* exclusive-tool contention (rank 3). *Benefit:* J4-type staging waits (~1 h 50 m) shrink. *Helped:* J4; effective CNC-2 capacity. *Harmed:* none directly; risk of reservation hoarding starving others. *Cost/risk:* capital; idle duplicate. *Perverse metric:* FIX-A utilization — incentivizes holding the spare "warm." *Evaluation:* count Job–Tool–Machine contention motifs in the object graph before/after; track J4's arrivalprocess-start e2o delta.

**I3. Dispatch-on-ready shipment policy** (split shipments or firm cutoffs with partial loads).
- *Target:* consolidation coupling (rank 2). *Benefit:* J1 dispatches ~5 h 30 m earlier. *Helped:* early-packed jobs (J1, J5-type). *Harmed:* late partners (J4-type) lose the consolidated ride; freight cost per unit rises. *Cost/risk:* parcel proliferation, packaging waste. *Perverse metric:* shipment fill ratio or cost-per-shipment — forces re-consolidation. *Evaluation:* job-level PackDispatch e2o versus partner's pack time via `consolidates` edges; simulate counterfactual dispatch = own pack + 25 min and compare against realized.

**I4. Logging integration (no operational change).**
- *Target:* integration artifacts (rank 6). *Benefit:* correct attribution — link lot events to jobs, add `Wait for Tool`/queue activities, ingest override records, keep batch events single-instance in object space. *Helped:* future J5/J4-type diagnoses; planners. *Harmed:* no operational harm; teams may feel surveilled. *Cost/risk:* event volume, extraction latency. *Perverse metric:* "events logged per job" — invites trivial-event inflation. *Evaluation:* re-run the interactions summary; assert L7–J2/J5 edges now appear in the Job flattening; measure reduction of unexplained inter-event gaps.

**I5. Sub-lot quarantine / split release for L7-type lots.**
- *Target:* lot-release coupling (rank 4). *Benefit:* J5-type jobs not gated by an unrelated component's defect (up to 3 h 30 m here), with knock-on relief to J4 via B2. *Helped:* J5, J4. *Harmed:* QA assurance if contamination is lot-wide. *Cost/risk:* finer traceability, per-sub-lot testing. *Perverse metric:* quarantine-duration KPI — incentivizes premature releases. *Evaluation:* ETOT around lot objects; verify whether Release Lot still precedes job readiness; track quality escapes as the counter-metric.

**I6. Reschedule CAL-3 recalibration outside test windows, or add a second calibrator.**
- *Target:* tool contention at test (rank 3). *Benefit:* J6-type waits (~1 h 20 m) and the S3 spillover to J5 (up to ~80 min) shrink. *Helped:* J6, J5, S3. *Harmed:* maintenance displaces into production time; skipping condition-based recalibration risks quality escapes. *Cost/risk:* capex or schedule friction. *Perverse metric:* TEST-1 utilization — deferring recalibration inflates it. *Evaluation:* CAL-3 ETOT timeline; per-job Final-Test-start-minus-ready; S3 dispatch vs last pack.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations that would weaken the top-ranked explanation (batch synchronization):**

1. Readiness timestamps proven to mark *staging*, not completion of prior steps — the 3 h 55 m would then include unrecorded upstream work, not batch-induced waiting.
2. J1/J2 demonstrably perform other value-adding operations during 08:10–12:05 elsewhere (multi-object e2o shows parallel activity) — the "wait" is not lost time.
3. Across a larger sample, batch start  previous cure end + fixed rack turnaround, regardless of last-member readiness — the constraint is RACK-7 turnover, not synchronization policy.
4. Size-1 or size-2 batches show similar per-job waits — batching is not the driver.
5. Rework jobs dominate cycle time in a larger sample — quality, not synchronization, is the main loss.
6. J1's early dispatch has no downstream value (no SLA, no customer penalty) — the 5 h 55 m consolidation dwell costs nothing.
7. Peak-hour machine utilization exceeds 85% on busy days — capacity binds intermittently and the single-day picture misleads.

**Additional fields/event types that would discriminate among hypotheses:**

1. Explicit queue events with a queue object (`Wait for Tool`, `Queue at Machine`, with enter/exit timestamps).
2. Tool state log: reservation, hold reason, cleaning start/end, calibration state transitions for FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3.
3. Lot genealogy: sub-lot IDs, defect scope within L7, quarantine trigger and test results.
4. Batch formation policy log: window close rule, min batch size, trigger type (time vs quantity), and the full override record for J2 integrated into the OCEL.
5. Shipment cutoff schedule, carrier pickup times, and per-job due dates/SLA clauses.
6. Job priority classes and planned vs actual release times.
7. Setup/changeover durations per machine–tool pair (to fill CNC-2's 10:47–11:45 hole).
8. Rework reason codes and inspection outcomes linked to the two rework cycles.
9. Operator shift roster (some "waits" may be shift-boundary artifacts).
10. Timestamp recording-latency metadata (to trust 5–10 min margins such as J3's 11:5512:05).
