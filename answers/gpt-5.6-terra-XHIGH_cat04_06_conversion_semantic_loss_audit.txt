# 1. Source-Semantics Baseline

Let the visible labels be abbreviated as follows:

- `R` = Register Change
- `A` = Assess Risk
- `E` = Prepare Evidence
- `L` = Legal Review
- `C` = Configure Trial
- `I` = Inspect Trial
- `D` = Correct Defect
- `X` = Release Change
- `H` = Archive Change
- `` = the silent skip-legal branch

A witness is a visible linearization of the partial-order behavior. For absence-of-order invariants, a negative witness can be a converter that rejects one required linearization by imposing an extra order.

| Invariant ID | Invariant | Positive witness | Negative witness | Observable after hiding tau? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | `R` occurs first. | `R A E C I X H` | `A R E C I X H` | Yes |
| S2 | `A` and `E` are unordered after `R`; both `A < E` and `E < A` linearizations are required. | `R A E C I X H` and `R E A C I X H` | A result that rejects `R E A C I X H` by enforcing `A < E` | Yes, through alternative linearizations; this does not prove real-world simultaneous duration |
| S3 | Legal Review is optional. If selected, `L` occurs after `A`; if skipped, the silent branch is taken. | With review: `R A L E C I X H`; skipped: `R A E C I X H` | `R E L A C I X H`, or a result that makes `L` mandatory | Partly: presence or absence of `L` is visible, but the silent skip decision is not |
| S4 | Verification cannot begin until both `A` and `E` are complete. | `R A E C I X H` | `R A C I E X H` | Yes |
| S5 | Optional Legal Review and the verification loop have no source edge between them; they may be interleaved after their own prerequisites hold. | `R A E L C I X H` and `R A E C L I X H` | A result that requires all `L` occurrences before all verification activity | Yes, through required alternative linearizations; not as a duration-overlap claim |
| S6 | Every verification body executes `C` before `I`. | `R A E C I X H` | `R A E I C X H` | Yes |
| S7 | The loop has binary scope: a repeated verification body is separated by `D`. Its intended language is `(C I) (D C I)*`. | `R A E C I D C I X H` | `R A E C I C I X H`, or `R A E C I D X H` | Yes |
| S8 | At least one verification iteration occurs before release. | `R A E C I X H` | `R A E X H` | Yes |
| S9 | `X` waits for both the optional-legal branch and the verification loop. If `L` is selected, it must complete before `X`; if skipped, the silent branch must have completed. | `R A E L C I X H` and `R A E C I X H` | `R A E C I X L H`, or `R A E X H` | Partly: the selected `L` branch is visible; silent branch completion is not |
| S10 | `H` occurs after `X` and is the visible end of a completed execution. | `R A E C I X H` | `R A E C I H X`, or treating `R A E C I X` as complete | Yes |
| S11 | Visible business labels are unique in the source model. Routing and skip behavior are not business activities. | One visible transition per source label | Two semantically distinct tasks both rendered as, for example, `Legal Review` | Yes, but generated models may introduce duplicate labels and need inspection |

The source POWL is authoritative. A successful conversion call, diagram rendering, or serialization does not itself establish these invariants.

# 2. Candidate Conversion Pipelines

| Pipeline | PM4Py calls | Expected benefit | Expected risk | Failure condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary: POWL  accepting Petri net  BPMN** | `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(source_powl)`; `pm4py.convert_to_bpmn(net, im, fm)`; `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(bpmn)` for round trip | Uses the documented direct POWL-to-accepting-net conversion and retains explicit initial and final markings for validation. The accepting net is suitable for soundness checks and bounded trace comparison. | BPMN is a routing notation with gateways, start/end events, and layout choices. A visually plausible BPMN can still alter or obscure concurrency, optional silent routing, loop scope, or joins. | Null initial/final marking, conversion exception, failed BPMN round trip, non-workflow net, unsoundness, or failed witness/trace-language checks. |
| Secondary: POWL  accepting Petri net  process tree  BPMN | `pm4py.convert_to_process_tree(net, im, fm)`; if successful, `pm4py.convert_to_bpmn(tree)`; then BPMN-to-Petri round trip | A process tree can be compact and easy to explain if the behavior is genuinely block structured. | The dependency pattern is not obviously process-tree structured: `A` enables Legal Review while `A` and `E` jointly enable verification, and Legal Review remains unordered with verification. A tree converter may reject this or distort the partial order. | Any exception from `convert_to_process_tree`, a `None` tree, a BPMN conversion failure, or any validation mismatch. A process-tree failure quarantines this secondary pipeline; it does not invalidate the direct Petri/BPMN pipeline. |

The primary pipeline is the recommended candidate because it does not require the source behavior to be representable as a process tree.

```python
from pathlib import Path
import pm4py

from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (

from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Authoritative source POWL
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

register = Transition(label="Register Change")
assess_risk = Transition(label="Assess Risk")
prepare_evidence = Transition(label="Prepare Evidence")

legal_review = Transition(label="Legal Review")
skip_legal = SilentTransition()
optional_legal = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[legal_review, skip_legal],
)

configure_trial = Transition(label="Configure Trial")
inspect_trial = Transition(label="Inspect Trial")
trial_body = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[configure_trial, inspect_trial])
trial_body.order.add_edge(configure_trial, inspect_trial)

correct_defect = Transition(label="Correct Defect")
verification_loop = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[trial_body, correct_defect],
)

release_change = Transition(label="Release Change")
archive_change = Transition(label="Archive Change")

source_powl = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[
        register,
        assess_risk,
        prepare_evidence,
        optional_legal,
        verification_loop,
        release_change,
        archive_change,
    ]
)

source_powl.order.add_edge(register, assess_risk)
source_powl.order.add_edge(register, prepare_evidence)
source_powl.order.add_edge(assess_risk, optional_legal)
source_powl.order.add_edge(assess_risk, verification_loop)
source_powl.order.add_edge(prepare_evidence, verification_loop)
source_powl.order.add_edge(optional_legal, release_change)
source_powl.order.add_edge(verification_loop, release_change)
source_powl.order.add_edge(release_change, archive_change)

# Output directory and helper functions

def save_visualization(filename, save_function, *args):
    """
    Save a visualization, but record environmental failures such as missing
    Graphviz rather than pretending that the artifact was produced.
    """
    path = output_dir / filename
    try:
        save_function(*args, file_path=str(path))
        print(f"Saved visualization: {path}")
    except Exception as exc:
        visualization_failures.append((filename, exc))
        print(f"Visualization failed for {filename}: {type(exc).__name__}: {exc}")


def visible_label_collisions(net):
    """
    Report duplicate visible labels. Duplicate labels are not automatically
    semantic errors, but they weaken label-based trace comparisons.
    """
    by_label = {}
    for transition in net.transitions:
        if transition.label is not None:
            by_label.setdefault(transition.label, []).append(transition)

    return {
        repr(label): len(transitions)
        for label, transitions in by_label.items()
        if len(transitions) > 1
    }


def diagnose_accepting_net(name, net, initial_marking, final_marking):
    """
    Run diagnostics only when an accepting-net tuple is complete.
    """
    print(f"\n=== Diagnostics: {name} ===")

    if net is None:
        print("QUARANTINE: net is None.")
        return

    if initial_marking is None or final_marking is None:
        print("QUARANTINE: initial and/or final marking is None.")
        return

    try:
        workflow_net_result = pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net)
        print(f"Workflow-net check: {workflow_net_result}")
    except Exception as exc:
        diagnostic_failures.append((name, "workflow-net", exc))
        print(f"Workflow-net diagnostic failed: {type(exc).__name__}: {exc}")

    try:
        soundness_result = pm4py.check_soundness(
            net,
            initial_marking,
            final_marking,
            print_diagnostics=True,
        )
        print(f"Soundness result: {soundness_result}")
    except Exception as exc:
        diagnostic_failures.append((name, "soundness", exc))
        print(f"Soundness diagnostic failed: {type(exc).__name__}: {exc}")

    collisions = visible_label_collisions(net)
    if collisions:
        print(f"Visible-label collisions: {collisions}")
    else:
        print("Visible-label collisions: none")


def bpmn_to_accepting_net(name, bpmn_model):
    """
    Convert one produced BPMN model back into an accepting Petri net.
    The result is quarantined if it does not contain all three parts.
    """
    if bpmn_model is None:
        print(f"{name}: no BPMN model to round-trip.")
        return None

    try:
        converted = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(bpmn_model)
    except Exception as exc:
        print(
            f"{name}: BPMN-to-Petri conversion failed and is quarantined: "
            f"{type(exc).__name__}: {exc}"
        )
        return None

    if not isinstance(converted, tuple) or len(converted) != 3:
        print(f"{name}: unexpected Petri conversion result: {converted!r}")
        return None

    net, initial_marking, final_marking = converted

    if net is None or initial_marking is None or final_marking is None:
        print(
            f"{name}: conversion returned incomplete accepting-net parts; "
            "quarantining result."
        )
        return None

    return net, initial_marking, final_marking
```

# 4. Preservation and Loss Audit

The statuses below are deliberately pre-execution assessments. A successful conversion object is not enough to claim `validated preserved`. After executing the validation code, an `ambiguous` result should be replaced by `validated preserved`, `approximated`, `lost`, or `conversion failed` according to evidence.

| Source invariant | POWLPetri | PetriBPMN | PetriTree | BPMN round trip | Evidence or uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `R` occurs first | expected preserved | expected preserved | ambiguous | ambiguous | The accepting net should encode the unique start marking and enable `R` first. BPMN start-event rendering does not itself prove this. |
| `A` and `E` remain unordered | expected preserved | expected preserved | ambiguous | ambiguous | The Petri net should permit both linearizations. A trace pair can show absence of enforced order but cannot establish physical simultaneity. |
| Legal Review is optional through XOR with `` | expected preserved | expected preserved | ambiguous | ambiguous | The BPMN may show an exclusive gateway with an unlabeled route. The silent branch has no visible task label. |
| `L` is unordered with verification once prerequisites allow both | expected preserved | expected preserved | ambiguous | ambiguous | This cross-dependency is the main process-tree risk: `A` enables Legal Review while `A` and `E` jointly enable verification. |
| Verification waits for both `A` and `E` | expected preserved | expected preserved | ambiguous | ambiguous | The Petri net should contain synchronization before the verification-loop entry. A BPMN diagram can visually hide this behind gateways. |
| `C` precedes `I` in each iteration | expected preserved | expected preserved | ambiguous | ambiguous | This is visible and should be caught by negative witness `I C`. |
| Binary loop scope `(C I)(D C I)*` | expected preserved | expected preserved | ambiguous | ambiguous | Process trees support loops in principle, but success would not prove that `D` remains only a redo separator. |
| At least one verification iteration | expected preserved | expected preserved | ambiguous | ambiguous | Loop rendering may look like zero-or-more repetition even where the underlying net requires the first body. |
| Release waits for optional-legal completion and verification completion | expected preserved | expected preserved | ambiguous | ambiguous | The join before `X` is a critical safety property. If Legal Review is selected, `L` must not occur after `X`. |
| Archive follows release and completes the process | expected preserved | expected preserved | ambiguous | ambiguous | Finality depends on the actual final marking after round trip, not merely on an end event drawn after Release Change. |
| Visible activity labels remain distinguishable | expected preserved | ambiguous | ambiguous | ambiguous | The source labels are unique. Generated BPMN or round-tripped nets may duplicate labels or introduce technical routing labels. |
| Initial and final markings remain usable | expected preserved | ambiguous | ambiguous | ambiguous | BPMN has start/end events rather than externally supplied Petri markings. The returned round-trip markings must be checked, not assumed. |
| Routing is not mistaken for work | expected preserved | ambiguous | ambiguous | ambiguous | BPMN gateways, events, and silent Petri transitions may be rendered as unlabeled nodes or paths. They are control-flow artifacts, not business activities. |

Further audit observations:

- **True concurrency versus arbitrary interleaving:** Petri-net semantics can represent independently enabled transitions. A trace language records interleavings, not wall-clock overlap. Accepting both `A E` and `E A` is evidence against a forced sequence, but it does not establish real operational concurrency such as staffing or elapsed-time overlap.

- **No Legal Review-to-verification edge:** The source intentionally has no edge between `optional_legal` and `verification_loop`. A conversion that serializes Legal Review before verification, or verification before Legal Review, has imposed behavior not stated by the source.

- **Silent optional behavior:** The skip branch is represented by `SilentTransition()`. After hiding ``, the important observable behavior is that a trace with `L` and a trace without `L` are both possible. However, trace evidence alone cannot distinguish an intentional silent XOR from an implementation that simply deleted the Legal Review branch.

- **Binary-loop semantics:** The intended loop is not merely “repeat trial.” It is specifically `(Configure Trial; Inspect Trial) (Correct Defect; Configure Trial; Inspect Trial)*`. `Correct Defect` cannot be omitted between repeated trial bodies and cannot appear as a terminal loop action.

- **Join before Release Change:** The release join must wait for both the completed optional-legal branch and the completed verification loop. When Legal Review is skipped, that branch completes silently; when selected, `L` must precede `X`.

- **Activity-label uniqueness:** Label-based footprints and visible-trace comparison are defensible only if labels identify intended visible activities. Duplicate generated labels make a trace comparison a lossy quotient of the actual transition system.

- **Initial and final markings:** The accepting Petri net has explicit `im` and `fm`. BPMN does not expose the same marking interface. A round trip must produce non-null markings and must satisfy exact final-marking acceptance.

- **Gateway and routing-node ambiguity:** BPMN gateways may be rendered as diamonds or as technical routing nodes. Their geometry is not a business-level semantic statement. An unlabeled path may represent ``, not an omitted activity.

# 5. Round-Trip Validation Code

Run this after the conversion code in Section 3. It compares the source-derived accepting Petri net with every available BPMN round-trip net.

The bounded exploration records a trace only when the current marking is exactly equal to the supplied final marking. It hides transitions whose label is `None`, limits consecutive silent firings, limits visible activity count, and limits the number of `Correct Defect` occurrences.

```python
from collections import deque
from pm4py.objects.petri_net import semantics


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Bounds
#
# MAX_REDO = 2 allows up to three intended verification-body iterations:
#   C I
#   C I D C I
#   C I D C I D C I
#
# MAX_VISIBLE is also required because a malformed converted net could repeat
# visible activities without using Correct Defect.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

MAX_VISIBLE = 16
MAX_REDO = 2
MAX_CONSECUTIVE_TAU = 48
LOOP_SEPARATOR_LABEL = "Correct Defect"


def marking_signature(marking):
    """
    A local state signature. Place object identity is appropriate here because
    it is used only within one Petri net's exploration.
    """
    return tuple(
        sorted(
            (id(place), int(tokens))
            for place, tokens in marking.items()
            if tokens
        )
    )


def explore_completed_visible_traces(
    net,
    initial_marking,
    final_marking,
    max_visible=MAX_VISIBLE,
    max_redo=MAX_REDO,
    max_consecutive_tau=MAX_CONSECUTIVE_TAU,
):
    """
    Enumerate distinct completed visible traces within the stated bounds.

    - Transitions with label None are hidden.
    - Completion requires exact marking equality: marking == final_marking.
    - Silent cycles are bounded and dominated states are pruned.
    - Visible traces are bounded by max_visible.
    - Correct Defect occurrences are bounded by max_redo.

    The returned set contains tuples of visible labels.
    """
    if net is None or initial_marking is None or final_marking is None:
        raise ValueError("A complete accepting Petri net is required.")

    completed = set()
    queue = deque()
    queue.append((initial_marking, tuple(), 0, 0))

    # For a fixed marking and visible trace, a lower consecutive-tau count
    # dominates a higher one because it leaves at least as much tau budget.
    best_tau_count = {
        (marking_signature(initial_marking), tuple(), 0): 0
    }

    while queue:
        marking, visible_trace, redo_count, tau_run = queue.popleft()

        # Exact accepting-marking test. A merely dead marking is not accepted.
        if marking == final_marking:
            completed.add(visible_trace)
            continue

        enabled = pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, marking)

        for transition in sorted(
            enabled,
            key=lambda t: (
                t.label is not None,
                str(t.label),
                id(t),
            ),
        ):
            label = transition.label
            is_tau = label is None

            if is_tau:
                if tau_run >= max_consecutive_tau:
                    continue
                next_visible_trace = visible_trace
                next_redo_count = redo_count
                next_tau_run = tau_run + 1
            else:
                if len(visible_trace) >= max_visible:
                    continue

                if label == LOOP_SEPARATOR_LABEL and redo_count >= max_redo:
                    continue

                next_visible_trace = visible_trace + (label,)
                next_redo_count = redo_count + (
                    1 if label == LOOP_SEPARATOR_LABEL else 0
                )
                next_tau_run = 0

            next_marking = semantics.execute(transition, net, marking)

            if next_marking is None:
                raise RuntimeError(
                    "An enabled transition could not be executed. "
                    f"Transition: {transition!r}"
                )

            state_key = (
                marking_signature(next_marking),
                next_visible_trace,
                next_redo_count,
            )

            known_tau_run = best_tau_count.get(state_key)
            if known_tau_run is not None and known_tau_run <= next_tau_run:
                continue

            best_tau_count[state_key] = next_tau_run
            queue.append(
                (
                    next_marking,
                    next_visible_trace,
                    next_redo_count,
                    next_tau_run,
                )
            )

    return completed


def safe_footprints(name, net, initial_marking, final_marking):

def print_trace_set_difference(title, left, right, limit=10):
    """
    Prints traces in left - right.
    """
    difference = left - right
    print(f"{title}: {len(difference)} trace(s)")

    for trace in sorted(difference, key=lambda x: tuple(map(str, x)))[:limit]:
        print("  ", " ; ".join(map(str, trace)))


def report_witnesses(model_name, completed_traces, positives, negatives):
    missing_positive = {
        name: trace
        for name, trace in positives.items()
        if trace not in completed_traces
    }

    admitted_negative = {
        name: trace
        for name, trace in negatives.items()
        if trace in completed_traces
    }

    print(f"\nWitness report for {model_name}")

    if missing_positive:
        print("Missing required positive witnesses:")
        for name, trace in missing_positive.items():
            print(f"  {name}: {' ; '.join(trace)}")
    else:
        print("All listed positive witnesses are accepted.")

    if admitted_negative:
        print("Admitted forbidden completed witnesses:")
        for name, trace in admitted_negative.items():
            print(f"  {name}: {' ; '.join(trace)}")
    else:
        print("No listed negative witness is accepted as completed.")

    return missing_positive, admitted_negative


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
```

# 6. Visual-Semantics Review

A BPMN rendering can be behaviorally acceptable while still being easy to misread.

- **Parallel gateways:** A generated parallel split/join may correctly encode token concurrency, but a stakeholder may infer that Assess Risk and Prepare Evidence must be performed by different people at the same time. The source only guarantees absence of order, not resource allocation or elapsed-time overlap.

- **Exclusive gateways:** The optional legal branch may render as an exclusive gateway with an empty or visually insignificant path. A reader can misread the empty path as a missing diagram element rather than the intentional silent skip branch.

- **Loop visualization:** A back edge from Correct Defect may visually resemble an optional retry, a generic rework loop, or a zero-or-more loop. The intended behavior is more precise: one Configure/Inspect iteration is mandatory, and every additional iteration must be preceded by Correct Defect.

- **Silent routing nodes:** BPMN gateways, intermediate routing events, and unnamed sequence flows may correspond to Petri-net silent transitions. They should not be interpreted as unmodelled business activities or as omitted approval work.

- **Join before Release Change:** A join gateway immediately before Release Change can appear as a simple merge in a compact layout. If it is actually a synchronization gateway, Release Change must wait for verification and for the completed legal branch.

- **Graph layout:** Left-to-right, top-to-bottom, or vertical placement is not semantic ordering. In particular, a BPMN renderer may place Legal Review visually before or after verification even though the source gives no edge between them.

Recommended annotations for the BPMN artifact:

1. Label the legal XOR gateway with “Legal review required?” and label the empty path explicitly as “Skip legal review ()”.
2. Add a note near the verification loop: `Configure Trial  Inspect Trial; repeat only via Correct Defect`.
3. Add a note near the release join: “Release waits for verification and legal-branch completion.”
4. Add a note near Assess Risk and Prepare Evidence: “No required order; either may complete first.”
5. Publish the invariant table and bounded-validation report beside the BPMN, rather than treating the diagram alone as the specification.

# 7. Rejection Criteria

Reject or quarantine a conversion result when any of the following applies:

1. `convert_to_petri_net` or `convert_to_bpmn` raises an exception for the primary pipeline.
2. A converted accepting net has a null net, initial marking, or final marking.
3. The resulting Petri net is not a workflow net or fails soundness diagnostics.
4. BPMN cannot be converted back into a complete accepting Petri net.
5. A completed round-trip trace reaches a marking other than the exact required final marking, or Release Change is treated as final without Archive Change.
6. A required positive witness is absent, including either `A E` or `E A` ordering after registration.
7. A forbidden witness is admitted, especially Release Change before verification, Inspect Trial before Configure Trial, or Archive Change before Release Change.
8. Legal Review becomes mandatory, impossible, or can occur after Release Change when selected.
9. A repeated Configure/Inspect body is admitted without Correct Defect, or Correct Defect is allowed without a following verification body.
10. Release Change no longer waits for the selected Legal Review branch and the verification loop.
11. Generated visible labels collide or change so that source activities cannot be mapped unambiguously to derivative activities.
12. The process-tree conversion fails or cannot pass the same round-trip checks. This quarantines the process-tree pipeline, not necessarily the direct Petri/BPMN pipeline.
13. Reachability or bounded exploration cannot be completed within agreed resource limits and no alternate validation evidence is available.
14. A BPMN visualization requires undocumented interpretation of gateways, silent routing, or loop boundaries to appear correct.

# 8. Canonical Representation Recommendation

The canonical stored model should be the authoritative POWL definition, together with a versioned semantic invariant specification and stable activity identifiers.

The stakeholder-facing model should be an annotated BPMN diagram generated from the validated accepting Petri net. BPMN is appropriate for communication, review, and presentation, but it should not become the editable semantic master.

Recommended artifact roles:

- **Authoritative:** Version-controlled POWL source, including the explicit partial-order edges, XOR-with-silent-branch structure, loop children, and invariant table.
- **Executable derivative:** The accepting Petri net with explicit initial and final markings.
- **Stakeholder derivative:** BPMN generated from the accepting Petri net, accompanied by gateway and loop annotations.
- **Optional derivative:** A process tree only if the conversion succeeds and independently passes the same validation suite. It should never replace POWL as the source of truth.

Each derivative should carry validation evidence:

- Source-model version or hash.
- PM4Py version and conversion call path.
- Workflow-net and soundness results.
- Confirmation that initial and final markings are non-null.
- Visible-label mapping and collision report.
- Positive and negative witness results.
- Bounded completed-trace comparison results and explicit bounds.
- Reachability and footprint diagnostics where available.
- A statement of unresolved limits, especially loop bounds and silent behavior.

Future edits should be made in POWL only. The workflow should be:

1. Edit and review the POWL source and invariant table.
2. Regenerate the accepting Petri net and BPMN.
3. Re-run diagnostics and bounded regression traces.
4. Review the updated BPMN as a presentation artifact.
5. Never use BPMN-to-Petri-to-POWL round trips as an editing workflow.

This avoids round-trip drift in which BPMN layout, gateway choices, or silent routing artifacts become accidental source semantics.

# 9. Residual Uncertainty

Even if every listed test passes, the validation does not establish all possible claims.

1. Bounded visible-trace exploration does not prove unrestricted language equivalence for arbitrarily many verification iterations or arbitrarily long silent paths.
2. Trace equivalence after hiding `` does not prove branching bisimulation or preserve every internal routing decision.
3. A successful PM4Py conversion and round trip do not prove that another BPMN engine, workflow engine, or human reader will assign identical semantics to the BPMN.
4. Petri-net concurrency does not prove real-world simultaneous execution, staffing feasibility, duration overlap, legal responsibility, or data availability.
5. Soundness does not prove that every source invariant is preserved; a net can be sound while still imposing an unintended sequence or admitting unwanted traces.
6. Unique visible labels make trace comparison more useful, but they do not prove that generated nodes correspond one-to-one with the intended business activities.
7. A process-tree conversion failure does not prove the source is invalid; it may only show that the source behavior is outside the supported or exact block-structured subset of the converter.
