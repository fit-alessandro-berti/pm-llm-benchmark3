## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

Global calendar anchors (elapsed hours measured from Friday 18 Sep 15:30): Mon 08:00 = 64.5 h; Mon 12:00 = 68.5 h; Mon 18:00 = 74.5 h; Tue 12:00 = 92.5 h; Wed 15:30 = 120 h; Thu 15:30 = 144 h; Fri 25th 15:30 = 168 h; Mon 28th 15:30 = 240 h.

**Where a maximum is used instead of a sum.** Chemistry and microbiology run in parallel between now and the QA join. The join time is max(chemistry-result time, microbiology-result time), never their sum. Everything downstream of the join (QA wait, QA service, query/investigation loops, release) is sequential and is added. Within the chemistry branch, waiting and service are added; within microbiology, incubation and readout are added, with the readout snapped forward to the next microbiologist availability window.

**Scenario A — smooth path**

- Microbiology: incubation technically ready 46–54 h after Fri 14:00 → Sunday 12:00–20:00. No routine weekend staffing; with authorization (p 0.25–0.45) readout Sunday 13:00–23:00, otherwise readout starts Mon 08:00, takes 0.5–1.5 h → result Mon 08:30–09:30. Either way this branch is ready by Monday ~09:30.
- Chemistry: B-417 cannot start early enough to finish Friday (assumption ledger, row 1). Under risk-priority (~45 %): B-417 starts Mon 08:00 (or carries a small Friday head start if Analyst A began it at ~16:45), service triangular(1.5, 2.5, 4.5) → finish Mon ~09:30–12:30, central ~10:30–11:00. Under FIFO (~55 %): Monday opens with residuals of the two Friday-started samples (~0.5–3 h each) plus ordinary #2 and #3 ahead of B-417 on two analysts; B-417 starts ~11:30–13:00 and finishes ~13:30–17:30, central ~15:00. Blending disciplines: chemistry result Monday ~10:30–15:00.
- Parallel join: max(Mon 08:30–09:30 micro, Mon 10:30–15:00 chem) → chemistry is the slower branch in most realizations; join Monday ~10:30–15:00. This is the central irony of the case: the weekend calendar makes microbiology look dominant, but by Monday morning microbiology is already read and chemistry's queue is the binding clock.
- QA: Monday-08:00 queue ≈ 3–6 batches (ledger row 5). Two reviewers, effective service ~2 h each → gross throughput ~1/h; Monday arrivals 0.35/h → net drain ~0.65/h. By a join at ~11:00–14:00, queue ahead of B-417 ≈ 1–4 → wait ~1–4 h; QA service 1–3.5 h (median 1.5). QA acceptance Monday ~14:00–19:00.
- Final: Electronic Release 0.25–0.75 h, available continuously → terminal Monday ~14:30–19:30 (~71–77 h); congested realizations spill past QA's 20:00 close into Tuesday ~09:00–11:00 (~89–91 h).

**Scenario B — chemistry rerun**

- Same as A until the first chemistry result. Borderline result (p 0.10–0.20) triggers a priority rerun of triangular(1.0, 1.8, 3.0) h. First result Mon ~10:30–15:00; rerun finishes ~12:30–17:30 (a rerun starting after ~15:00 with a slow draw risks hitting the 18:00 close and finishing Tuesday morning ~09:00–11:00).
- Microbiology is unchanged (Mon ≤09:30); the join now equals the rerun finish — a pure maximum shift.
- QA: joins later in Monday when the queue has partially drained (wait ~1–3 h) but only ~1 reviewer-shift remains before 20:00; QA acceptance Monday 17:00–20:00 or Tuesday 09:00–12:00.
- Terminal: Monday ~18:00 – Tuesday ~12:00 (~74.5–92.5 h); tail Tuesday evening ~100 h. Note also that a rerun raises the deviation-investigation probability to 0.30–0.55, so Scenario B frequently chains into Scenario D timing.

**Scenario C — contamination indication and confirmation**

- Readout Mon 08:30–09:30 shows an indication (p 0.05–0.12 unconditional). Confirmation needs 20–30 h continuous incubation from ~Mon 09:00 → technically ready Tue 05:00–15:00. Readout: if ready before 18:00 Tuesday, read same day (+0.5–1.5 h) → confirmed result Tue ~08:30 (for early readiness) to ~16:30; a late-ready draw slips to Wed ~08:30–09:30.
- Chemistry (Mon 10:30–15:00) is now the faster branch; the join is the microbiology confirmation — the maximum has flipped sides.
- QA: with a contamination history the investigation probability is 0.30–0.55 regardless of the confirmation outcome. If confirmation is negative (indication not confirmed): QA Tue/Wed, likely doc-only investigation (8–20 wh ≈ 1–2 QA working days) → terminal Wed–Thu (~114–145 h). If confirmed: this is the main Batch-Rejected route — rejection recording 0.5–1.0 h after the critical-defect confirmation and mandatory investigation → Wed at the earliest, more plausibly Thu–Fri or into the next week if laboratory work is required (~170–240 h).
- Terminal band: earliest Tue ~18:00 (~99 h, negative confirmation, no investigation — low probability); central Wed 10:00–20:00 (~114–124 h); tail Fri 25th – Mon 28th (~170–240 h).

**Scenario D — QA documentation query / investigation (branches normal)**

- Join as Scenario A (Mon ~10:30–15:00). QA starts Mon ~13:00–17:00.
- Documentation Query (p 0.15–0.30, plausibly nearer the top given completeness score 0.82): +4–12 QA working hours before review resumes. A 4-wh query raised at ~14:00 Monday can be answered and re-reviewed by Monday close (~19:00–20:00). A 12-wh query consumes the rest of Monday (to 20:00 = up to 6 wh) plus Tuesday morning → resume Tue ~10:00–14:00, acceptance Tue ~12:00–17:00.
- Deviation Investigation (p 0.04–0.10 with normal branches): doc-only (60–80 % of investigations) adds 8–20 wh ≈ Tue afternoon – Thu; laboratory (20–40 % of investigations) adds 3–7 calendar days from the Monday/Tuesday open → Thu 24th at the earliest, centrally Fri 25th – Mon 28th.
- Terminal: earliest Mon ~19:00 (~75.5 h); central Tue 12:00 – Wed 12:00 (~92.5–116.5 h); tail Thu ~145 h (long doc-only) out to ~240 h (laboratory investigation).

**Scenario E — temperature excursion confirmed (dependence scenario)**

- The flag (p 0.20–0.40) is upstream of both branches: conditional on confirmation I move the rerun probability toward/above 0.20, the contamination-indication probability toward/above 0.12, and the investigation probability to 0.30–0.55 even if both results end up numerically normal.
- Timing is therefore a probability-weighted overlay of B, C, and D occurring together rather than a new clock: chemistry rerun Monday afternoon (join shift ~+2–4 h), possible contamination confirmation through Tue/Wed (join shift ~+24–48 h), and a near-mandatory investigation at QA (+1–2 working days doc-only, +3–7 calendar days laboratory).
- Terminal: earliest Tue ~15:00 (~96 h, excursion confirmed but everything else clean and only a fast doc-only investigation); central Wed–Thu (~115–145 h); tail Fri 25th – Mon 28th or beyond (~170–240+ h). This scenario carries most of the joint-anomaly probability mass precisely because the events are correlated — multiplying the marginal anomaly probabilities as if independent would understate it several-fold.

## 4. Forecast summary

All figures are ranges; remaining time is measured from Friday 18 Sep 2026, 15:30.

- **Plausible earliest completion:** ~68–70 h → Monday 21 Sep ~11:30–13:30. Requires risk-priority dispatch (or a Friday partial start) in chemistry, a fast Monday QA queue, short QA service, and no anomalies. Nothing can realistically finish before Monday midday because chemistry cannot complete before Monday and QA is closed Sunday.
- **Central / P50-like range:** ~71–78 h → Monday 21 Sep ~14:30–21:30 (effectively Monday afternoon/evening; realizations that miss QA's 20:00 close land Tuesday ~09:00–11:00, ~89–91 h, which sits just above the median).
- **P80-like range:** ~89–100 h → Tuesday 22 Sep ~09:00–20:00. Driven by one moderate complication: a documentation query, a chemistry rerun that slips the join, or QA congestion from overdispersed weekend/Monday arrivals.
- **P90-like / severe-tail range:** ~115–170 h → Wednesday 23 Sep midday to Friday 25 Sep evening, driven by contamination confirmation and/or a deviation investigation; the extreme tail (laboratory investigation of 3–7 calendar days, or confirmed contamination with rejection processing) extends to ~200–240 h, i.e., Sunday 27 – Monday 28 Sep.
- **Most plausible terminal event(s):** Electronic Release, with overall probability ~90–97 %. Batch Rejected carries ~1–5 % (concentrated in Scenario C/E realizations with confirmed contamination or a critical investigation finding) and, when it occurs, mostly materializes in the Wednesday-to-next-week tail, not before.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. **Temperature-excursion flag resolution (dependence uncertainty, with an observation-uncertainty component).** It is currently unobservable (sensor gap) and it simultaneously moves the rerun, contamination, and investigation probabilities in the same direction; it controls whether the forecast is unimodal-Monday or acquires a heavy multi-day tail.
2. **QA path branching — documentation query and deviation investigation (path uncertainty).** The 15–30 % query and 4–55 % (state-dependent) investigation probabilities, with add-ons ranging from 4 working hours to 7 calendar days, create the widest single spread in terminal time.
3. **Contamination indication and its 20–30 h confirmation loop (path uncertainty, amplified by calendar uncertainty).** A single Monday-morning readout outcome moves the join by one to two full days and carries most of the rejection probability.
4. **Chemistry queue discipline and Monday queue position (queue uncertainty).** FIFO versus risk-priority shifts the chemistry result — and hence the join, since chemistry is usually the slower branch by Monday — by roughly 3–5 hours, which is what separates a Monday-evening release from a Tuesday-morning one.
5. **QA queue state at the join (queue uncertainty, with service-time uncertainty in the tail).** The overdispersed 0.35/h arrival process over ~8.5 weekend-adjacent reviewer-hours makes the Monday queue anywhere from ~2 to ~9 batches; combined with the long right tail of QA service (P80 = 3.5 h), it determines whether B-417 clears QA before the Monday 20:00 close.

(Notable non-driver: the weekend microbiologist authorization (p 0.25–0.45) barely changes the forecast, because chemistry cannot finish before Monday and the join is a maximum — accelerating the already-faster branch is wasted effort unless chemistry overtime is also arranged.)

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

**Primary: resolution of the temperature-excursion flag.** If engineering confirms the excursion, the forecast should shift sharply pessimistic: rerun and contamination probabilities move to the tops of their bands and become positively correlated, the deviation-investigation probability jumps to 0.30–0.55 even with normal results, the central estimate migrates from Monday evening (~72–78 h) toward Wednesday–Thursday (~115–145 h), and the rejection probability multiplies. If the flag is cleared, the anomaly probabilities collapse to the bottoms of their bands, Scenario A absorbs most of the probability mass, and a Monday release (~71–78 h) becomes a fairly confident central call with a thin Tuesday shoulder. Because the flag is upstream of both parallel branches and the QA decision, no other single observation moves as many clocks at once.

**Secondary trigger: the Monday ~08:30 microbiology readout.** A clean readout removes the only path that can delay the join by more than a few hours (the 20–30 h confirmation loop) and eliminates most of the rejection mass, tightening the forecast onto the Monday/Tuesday chemistry-and-QA range; a contamination indication immediately re-centers the forecast on Wednesday or later and raises the investigation probability to 0.30–0.55. (A close third is observing which queue discipline the chemistry lab actually applies at Monday 08:00, which resolves ~3–5 h of join-time spread within the smooth path.)
