# Sustainability Redesign for Spare-Parts Distribution

## 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Environmental scope (inside the boundary).** I use a gate-to-customer operational boundary anchored on the attributes the process-mining team actually captured, so every claimed effect is auditable against the event log:

- **Direct process energy** at production and treatment steps: `energy_kwh` on CNC_A, CNC_B, heat_treat, and inspection_cell events (Scope 2 electricity equivalent).
- **Outbound transport emissions**: `transport_kgco2e` per shipment leg by `route_mode` (parcel_ground, parcel_air, dedicated_van, consolidated_truck). This is the outbound distribution slice of Scope 3.
- **Material losses**: `material_scrap_kg` from production/rework and `package_material_kg` from standard vs. reinforced packaging (embodied-material proxy, not full cradle-to-grave LCA).
- **Waste from process inefficiency**: rework loops (`rework_flag`, Inspection -> Rework production -> CNC production) and obsolescence from over-batching (items not shipped within 14 days).

**Outside the boundary (explicitly excluded).** Upstream raw-metal extraction and supplier Scope 3; embodied emissions of CNC/heat-treat capital equipment; inbound logistics to the distributor; end-of-life of the delivered part; customer-side downtime energy; grid carbon-intensity time variation (except where S10 makes it a decision variable); and the full LCA of packaging materials beyond mass. Empty-return van kilometers are noted qualitatively (evidence says dedicated vans "often return empty") but are not separately metered in the log today — closing that gap is a validation action.

**Assumptions.**
- The per-order averages and per-path figures in the tables are representative of steady-state case mix (39/16/22/12/11%); they are directional, not precise LCA numbers.
- `transport_kgco2e` already reflects mode and distance, so mode substitution is the primary transport lever available to us.
- "Environmental impact" is multi-criteria (energy + transport CO2e + scrap + packaging + avoided-waste-from-rework/obsolescence), not a single number. No single ground-truth optimum exists; I optimize for a balanced frontier subject to the SP/Q/R policy constraints.
- Service, quality, and resilience are hard constraints (per the stated policies), not free variables to be traded down to zero.

**Residual uncertainty.** Model precision is low because emergency and partial-shipment paths recombine during disruptions; grid carbon intensity, empty-van backhaul, forecast error driving obsolescence, and true damage-return rates for packaging changes are only partially observed. Effect estimates below are therefore given as directions and ranges, not point values.

## 2. Sustainability-related process indicators

A concise, log-computable indicator set, each tied to attributes/paths and sliced by the five segments so trade-offs stay visible:

**Energy**
- E1 — Energy per shipped item = `energy_kwh` / items, by `machine_group` and `batch_size` (captures the CNC_A 2.8 -> 1.3 kWh batching curve).
- E2 — Energy wasted on rework = `energy_kwh` attributed to Rework production and repeated CNC production events / total energy.

**Transport**
- T1 — Transport CO2e per order by `route_mode` and segment (`transport_kgco2e`).
- T2 — Air/dedicated-van share where slack existed = fraction of parcel_air or dedicated_van shipments whose `promised_lead_time_hours` could still be met (>85% on-time prob) by ground/consolidated (directly maps to SP1).
- T3 — Consolidation fill / empty-return proxy for consolidated_truck and dedicated_van legs.

**Materials & packaging**
- M1 — Scrap intensity = `material_scrap_kg` / order, by machine and rework state (CNC_B vs CNC_A).
- M2 — Packaging mass intensity = `package_material_kg` / order, and share of reinforced vs standard.
- M3 — Packaging rework rate = Rework packaging events / shipments (route/geometry incompatibility).

**Rework & returns**
- R1 — Rework loop rate = Inspection -> Rework production frequency, by machine/part combo (CNC_B 18% vs CNC_A 9%).
- R2 — Return-from-damage rate, linked to Q1 packaging compliance.

**Batching**
- B1 — Median `Batch wait` by segment and the resulting E1 gain (waiting-vs-energy curve).
- B2 — Obsolescence rate = batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days (currently ~6%).

**Service**
- S1 — Late-delivery rate and p90 cycle time by segment (must not regress vs. baseline).
- S2 — Promise-attainment probability used in SP1 decisions.

**Quality**
- Q1i — Q1 compliance rate (MTO >8 kg reinforced-or-certified) and its correlation with R1/R2.

**Resilience**
- RS1 — Alternative-route availability per Service-contract shipment during disruption (R1).
- RS2 — Emergency-path concentration = share of Critical volume dependent on a single mode/lane (single-point-of-failure indicator), plus model-precision/variant-entropy on disruption paths.

## 3. Intervention options

Eight-plus options, each with target, mechanism, service/quality/resilience effect, and the evidence to monitor.

**S1 — Green shipping recommender (SP1 enforcement).**
- *Target:* all segments at the Package -> Ship decision, especially where air/van is chosen with slack.
- *Mechanism:* recommend ground/consolidated when promised lead time is met with >85% probability, cutting T1 (air is 4–6x ground CO2e).
- *Service/quality/resilience:* neutral-to-positive service if the 85% gate is honored; no quality effect; resilience improves if it always surfaces a backup mode (supports R1). Risk only if the reliability model is over-optimistic.
- *Evidence to monitor:* T2, S2 attainment, late-delivery by segment, SP1 conformance.

**S4 — Replace air with priority-pick + ground for Standard-stock Service-contract.**
- *Target:* the specific finding that these orders are "often shipped by air even when promised lead time still allows ground plus priority picking"; SP1 is violated in 22% of these cases.
- *Mechanism:* substitute mode (air -> ground) while protecting lead time via priority picking; T1 drops sharply (this segment averages 7.8 kgCO2e/order, well above Standard-stock Regular's 4.2).
- *Service/quality/resilience:* service preserved by picking priority; only apply where slack exists (do not blanket-ban air). Quality unaffected; resilience fine as long as air remains the fallback.
- *Evidence:* T2 for this segment, on-time %, SP1 violation rate falling from 22%.

**S5 — Twice-daily consolidated truck departures for non-urgent stock.**
- *Target:* Standard-stock Regular ground parcels (the 42% low-impact path).
- *Mechanism:* consolidation cuts transport CO2e ~35–55% vs individual ground parcels.
- *Service/quality/resilience:* adds a 12–24h consolidation wait, so exclude Critical and tight-slack orders; two daily windows cap the wait. Resilience improves (fewer, planned departures) unless it becomes the only lane.
- *Evidence:* T3 fill rate, cycle-time delta on affected orders, late-delivery must not rise.

**S2 — Dynamic batching with segment waiting-time caps (CNC + heat treat).**
- *Target:* MTO batch-wait paths and heat-treatment queues.
- *Mechanism:* exploit the E1 curve (2.8 -> 1.3 kWh) and low heat-treat energy at batch >10, but cap `Batch wait` per segment so energy savings don't create lateness/obsolescence.
- *Service/quality/resilience:* protects service via caps (heat-treat batching carries 1.7x late risk; MTO batch path already 21% late). Directly attacks B2 obsolescence (6%). Resilience neutral.
- *Evidence:* B1, B2, E1, late-delivery on batch paths.

**S3 — Shift MTO from CNC_B to CNC_A when queue/due-date slack allows.**
- *Target:* MTO -> CNC_B -> ... -> rework path (5% share, scrap 1.8 kg, energy 32 kWh — "rework dominates impact").
- *Mechanism:* CNC_A has 9% rework vs CNC_B's 18%; fewer rework loops cut E2, M1, and R1 simultaneously.
- *Service/quality/resilience:* quality improves; watch that CNC_B is faster, so shifting load can add cycle time — gate on slack. Resilience: keep CNC_B as surge capacity, don't strand it.
- *Evidence:* R1 by machine, M1/E2, queue-driven cycle-time.

**S7 — Inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combos.**
- *Target:* Inspection -> Rework production loops (470) and rushed inspections in inspection_cell queues.
- *Mechanism:* right-sized inspection prevents defects that cause rework and packaging/customer returns, cutting hidden scrap, rework energy, and return transport.
- *Service/quality/resilience:* quality up; small added inspection time offset by avoided 160h+ rework paths. Resilience neutral-to-positive (fewer chaotic loops improve model precision).
- *Evidence:* R1, R2, E2, inspection queue length vs rework rate.

**S6 — Packaging-route compatibility check before shipment.**
- *Target:* Rework packaging loops (packaging incompatible with `route_mode`/geometry).
- *Mechanism:* prevent re-pack, saving `package_material_kg` (M2), packaging rework time (M3), and duplicate handling.
- *Service/quality/resilience:* service up (fewer re-pack delays), quality up, resilience neutral. Must respect Q1 (don't downgrade reinforced where required).
- *Evidence:* M3 rate, M2 intensity, Q1 compliance.

**S8 — Shared emergency courier lanes replacing some dedicated vans.**
- *Target:* Critical -> dedicated van path (transport 52 kgCO2e, "often return empty"); SP2 justification missing in 31% of van cases.
- *Mechanism:* shared/backhaul lanes cut empty-return CO2e (T1/T3) for genuinely urgent orders.
- *Service/quality/resilience:* **only** for cases that don't need point-to-point exclusivity; retain dedicated vans for true downtime (do not treat all vans as unjustified). Resilience risk if shared lanes are less reliable — keep dedicated as fallback (R1).
- *Evidence:* SP2 justification rate, T1/T3 on Critical, on-time % for true-downtime subset.

**S9 — Split Critical-downtime into true-downtime vs precautionary-urgent.**
- *Target:* the 11% Critical segment (avg 38 kgCO2e/order) where not every order is a real line-stop.
- *Mechanism:* route only genuine downtime to premium van/air; precautionary-urgent can take ground/consolidated with priority — large transport reduction on the highest-emission segment.
- *Service/quality/resilience:* protects true downtime fully; precautionary orders accept modest slack. Enables correct SP2 justification. Resilience improved by not overloading emergency lanes.
- *Evidence:* re-classification accuracy, T1 by sub-segment, late-delivery on true-downtime (must stay ~4%).

**S10 — Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular MTO.**
- *Target:* MTO Regular (22%, 18% late, 18.5 kWh, high scrap).
- *Mechanism:* negotiate slightly longer promise dates enabling batching (S2) and ground/consolidated (S1/S5); reduces energy per item and transport, and reduces late-delivery by setting realistic promises.
- *Service/quality/resilience:* service *perception* can improve (fewer broken promises); apply only to Regular, never Service-contract/Critical. Rebound risk if longer promises raise obsolescence — pair with demand check.
- *Evidence:* promise vs actual, B2 obsolescence, T1/E1 for the segment.

**S11 — Tighten availability-check rules to reduce partial shipments.**
- *Target:* Stock -> partial shipment -> ground + backorder path (7%, 79h, 24% late, double transport legs).
- *Mechanism:* better availability logic avoids second shipments — cuts duplicate transport (7.2 vs 3.6 kgCO2e) and packaging.
- *Service/quality/resilience:* fewer split deliveries improve service perception, but a stricter gate could hold orders — cap the wait and allow partial when downtime-critical. Resilience: keep partial as an option during shortages.
- *Evidence:* partial-shipment rate, backorder-cancel rate (currently 60), transport legs/order.

**S12 — Sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides.** (Governance enabler.)
- *Target:* all policy overrides.
- *Mechanism:* makes green-vs-service overrides explicit and auditable; no direct emission cut but prevents silent policy erosion and improves model precision on disruption paths.
- *Evidence:* override volume/reasons, conformance recovery on SP1/SP2/Q1.

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

At least six ways a local green gain can worsen another part of the system:

1. **Batching rebound (energy vs obsolescence/waiting).** Pushing batch sizes up to chase the 1.3 kWh/item point inflates `Batch wait` (up to 38h) and the 6% obsolescence rate. Energy saved per item can be dwarfed by whole parts scrapped when forecasts are wrong — net material and embodied-impact loss. Mitigation: S2 waiting caps + demand check before large batches.
2. **Transport mode-shift rebound.** Aggressive S1/S4/S5 ground substitution can trip late deliveries on tight-slack orders, triggering *expedite* air recovery shipments — a second, higher-emission leg that erases the saving. Mitigation: honor the 85% attainment gate; never down-mode Critical/true-downtime.
3. **Consolidation waiting displacement.** S5's 12–24h wait, if applied too broadly, converts on-time stock orders into late ones, increasing partial shipments/backorders (each adding a transport leg). Mitigation: scope S5 to non-urgent with slack only.
4. **Packaging trade-off (material vs returns).** Cutting reinforced packaging to save `package_material_kg` risks damage returns (reinforced cuts MTO-heavy returns 40%), and a return is a full reverse-plus-reship transport + rework burden. Q1 must gate any packaging downgrade. Conversely, over-reinforcing everything doubles material needlessly.
5. **Rework/quality displacement.** Speeding inspection or over-loading CNC_A to "green up" throughput can raise missed-defect rework (the CNC_B 18% lesson), converting saved time into scrap (1.8 kg on the rework path) and return transport. Mitigation: S7 gate sized to queue load.
6. **Customer-behavior / promise rebound.** Carbon-aware longer promises (S10) or slower ground defaults may push customers to pre-emptively flag orders as "urgent" or Critical to jump the queue, re-inflating air/van volume — exactly what S9's true-downtime split defends against. Mitigation: require justification (SP2) + exception log (S12).
7. **Resilience displacement.** Replacing dedicated vans with shared lanes (S8) or collapsing to a single consolidated lane removes the alternative route R1 requires for Service-contract customers during carrier disruption. Mitigation: always retain a fallback mode; monitor RS1/RS2.
8. **Machine-load displacement.** Shifting all MTO to CNC_A (S3) strands CNC_B and removes surge capacity, hurting resilience and possibly lengthening queues (rebounding into lateness -> expedite). Mitigation: slack-gated shift, keep CNC_B for overflow.

## 6. Balanced recommendation

Adopt a **single coherent redesign package**, sequenced so that service/quality/resilience guardrails are installed *before* the emission levers are pushed. Isolated "greenest option" moves are explicitly avoided because the evidence shows each one displaces impact elsewhere.

**Guardrail layer (install first):**
- **S12 exception log** + **S9 Critical split** + **S6 packaging-route check** + **S7 inspection gate.** These make policy overrides visible, stop misclassified urgency from flooding premium lanes, and cut the rework/return/packaging waste that would otherwise sabotage every transport saving. They improve model precision on the messy disruption/partial paths.

**Transport layer (highest CO2e leverage, service-gated):**
- **S1 green shipping recommender** as the engine, with **S4** (Standard-stock Service-contract air->priority-pick+ground, fixing the 22% SP1 violations) and **S5** (twice-daily consolidation for non-urgent stock) as its concrete policies, and **S8** shared emergency lanes only for the precautionary-urgent subset that S9 defines. Transport is the biggest, cheapest win (air is 4–6x ground; Critical van legs hit 52 kgCO2e) and, when gated by the 85% attainment rule, carries minimal service risk. Dedicated vans and air are preserved for true downtime and tight-slack Service-contract orders — we do **not** ban premium transport wholesale.

**Production layer (energy/material, slack-gated):**
- **S2 dynamic batching with per-segment waiting caps** and **S3 CNC_B->CNC_A on slack**, paired with **S10 carbon-aware promises for Regular MTO only.** Batching captures the energy curve while caps and demand checks contain obsolescence and lateness; the CNC shift attacks the rework-dominated path; S10 buys the slack that makes batching and ground shipping feasible for the segment that is 18% late.
- **S11** availability-rule tightening to reduce double-leg partial shipments, with a wait cap so it never strands downtime-critical orders.

**Why this balances the four objectives.** Emission reductions come predominantly from *transport mode substitution where slack exists* and from *eliminating waste* (rework, re-pack, obsolescence, duplicate shipments) — categories that improve service and quality rather than trading them away. Every lever that could hurt service (batching, consolidation, ground substitution, promise extension) is bounded by an explicit slack/attainment cap and applied only to segments that can absorb it. Resilience is protected by always retaining an alternative route (R1), keeping CNC_B as surge capacity, and logging exceptions so the system degrades gracefully during disruption. Critical-downtime true line-stops keep their premium protection; only precautionary-urgent volume is greened.

## 7. Validation and governance plan

**A. Pre/post comparison on the enriched log.**
- Compute the Section-2 indicators (E1–E2, T1–T3, M1–M3, R1–R2, B1–B2, S1–S2, Q1i, RS1–RS2) per segment for a baseline window, then re-compute after rollout. Judge success on the *multi-criteria frontier*: total impact (energy + transport CO2e + scrap + packaging + avoided waste) must fall **while** late-delivery and p90 cycle time per segment do not regress beyond agreed thresholds.
- Use A/B or staged rollout (by region/segment) so mode-shift and batching effects are attributable, given low model precision on combined disruption paths.

**B. Conformance monitoring (continuous).**
- **SP1:** track the share of air/van shipments where ground/consolidated met lead time with >85% probability; target the 22% Standard-stock Service-contract violation rate toward near-zero, alerting on regressions.
- **SP2:** measure presence of the Critical-downtime justification event on every dedicated-van/shared-lane case; drive the 31% missing-justification rate down; feed misses into S9 reclassification.
- **Q1:** monitor MTO >8 kg reinforced-or-certified compliance and its correlation with R1/R2; block packaging down-moves that would violate Q1.
- Rising rework/return, obsolescence (B2), or expedite-recovery shipments are treated as **rebound alarms** that pause the corresponding lever.

**C. Segment-level service monitoring.**
- Dashboards of late-delivery and p90 cycle time for all five segments plus the S9 true-downtime vs precautionary split. Hard stop: true-downtime late-delivery must not rise above its ~4% baseline; Service-contract on-time must hold.

**D. Resilience checks.**
- RS1: verify at least one alternative route remains available per Service-contract shipment; run periodic carrier-disruption drills and confirm fallback modes fire (R1). RS2: watch for lane/mode concentration after S5/S8 consolidation.

**E. Exception governance (S12).**
- Every SP1/SP2/Q1 override writes a structured event (reason, segment, slack, approver). Weekly review of override volume and reasons; recurring override patterns become either new policy or new automation rules. This log also enriches the event data, improving discovered-model precision on the emergency/partial-shipment variants over time.

**F. Uncertainty closure.**
- Add instrumentation for empty-van backhaul, grid carbon intensity (for S10), and true damage-return rates, so the currently qualitative Section-4 directions can be tightened into defensible ranges in the next iteration — without ever presenting them as precise LCA figures that the data does not support.
