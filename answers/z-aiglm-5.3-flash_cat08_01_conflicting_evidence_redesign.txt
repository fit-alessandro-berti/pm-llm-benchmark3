# Warranty Process Redesign Advisory — Process-Mining Synthesis

---

## 1. Current-process diagnosis

### The central contradiction: the modal path is an aggregation illusion

The most frequent completed path — `Intake  Auto screen  Fast approval  Dispatch replacement  Quality/compliance close` (2,300 of 5,000 cases, 46%) — looks excellent locally: auto screen waits are negligible (mean 0.8h), fast approval takes ~1h, and dispatch is quick. But three artifacts show this is misleading:

1. **Rework statistics**: Fast-approval cases are 31% cheaper *before* rework but only **9% cheaper after** accounting for complaints, reshipments, and service credits. The cost advantage collapses by roughly two-thirds once downstream failures are priced in.
2. **Segment outcomes**: The aggregate 14.1% reopen rate on Fast approval masks enormous heterogeneity — 6% for Premium low-risk accessories vs **22% for Premium high-value devices** and 18% for Standard high-value. The slow reviewed/repair path runs 3–6% reopen. So the fast path is *not* universally worse: it is worse precisely where failure costs are highest (wrong-part exposure of EUR 410 for devices vs EUR 95 for accessories).
3. **Alignment diagnostics**: The named deviations — *Fast approval before evidence completeness*, *Dispatch without risk review for high-value devices*, *Customer complaint/reopen after close* — cluster on exactly this modal path. Token-based replay shows fitness 0.91 but only **68% of traces fitting**, and precision is low, meaning the discovered model permits many fast/review/repair combinations policy disallows.

The root cause links both artifacts: auto-screen rules are stated to be **outdated and not segment-aware**, which simultaneously explains why 1,400 intake cases (28%) bypass to manual review, why high-value devices get dispatched without risk review (violating R1/R2), and why the modal path is simultaneously the cheapest-front-stage and worst-conformance path.

### Key performance problems (from the perf-DFG)

- **The evidence loop is the dominant cycle-time sink, driven by waiting, not work.** `Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility review` averages **34h (p90 88h)**; `Intake  Manual review` is 7.5h mean but re-entry legs stretch far longer. The 22% unclear/missing-evidence segment has a **140h median cycle time** and the highest abandonment (320 + 70 = 390 abandonments  7.8% of all cases).
- **Reviews are structurally saturated.** Summing DFG inflows, Manual eligibility review occurs ~2,600 times (1,400 intake + 890 returns from evidence + 310 from reopens) against only **7 × 36 = 252 productive reviewer-hours/week**, with a queue cliff above 82% utilization (~207 effective hours). Any redesign pushing inflow here collides with a wall.
- **Failures surface late.** Complaint/reopen arrivals arrive 50–96h (median) after their trigger, at p90 150–180h. Late detection is expensive: it burns service credits on Premium (>24h threshold) and monopolizes the escalation board, which has **one 45-minute daily slot** (450 min/week capacity vs 120 complaints routed to it in the log).
- **Fulfillment quality gaps**: `Quality check  Rework repair` affects **12.5% of repaired high-value devices**; `Dispatch  Complaint/reopen` (260 cases) is explicitly linked to incorrect replacement-part selection per the temporal profile.

### Conformance and data-hygiene observations

- Balance checks: evidence requests issued (650 + 780 = 1,430) exactly equal resolutions (890 + 320 + 220) — internally consistent. But fraud/risk review takes 980 entries and returns only 760, and only 3,625 of 5,000 cases reach a logged END (close 3,015 + denied 220 + abandoned 390), leaving ~1,375 endpoints unaccounted — consistent with the 32% non-fitting traces. This implies **silent terminations or logging gaps that bias both cost attribution and reopen-window measurement**, and it means any KPI baseline set on current data understates some tail activity.
- `Fast approval  Quality/compliance close` (350 cases, skipping any recorded fulfillment act) is anomalous given R2 permits a no-dispatch close only for Premium low-risk accessories — worth auditing, not redesigning around blindly.

**Bottom line:** the organization is not choosing between "the fast path" and "the slow path"; it is running one undifferentiated decision layer over five segments whose fast/slow trade-offs differ by an order of magnitude. The three redesigns below therefore intervene at three different lifecycle stages — front-stage triage/control, mid-loop evidence handling, and back-stage fulfillment — each addressing a different measured failure mode, each explicitly trading something away.

## 2. Redesign A: Segment-Aware Triage and Control Right-Sizing

*(Front-stage: rewrite who gets checked, how hard, and when)*

**Redesigned flow.**
Intake  **attribute-aware auto screen (new rule pack keyed on claim value, segment, defect type)**  three lanes:
1. *Express lane*: low-risk accessories (Premium + Standard)  auto/fast approval  dispatch (retained for both segments — their fast-path reopens are 6%/8% and Premium loses credits beyond 24h, so slowing them would be wrong).
2. *Gated lane*: all claims > EUR 400 and/or high-value devices  hard system block: no Fast approval and no dispatch release until Manual eligibility review **or** Fraud/risk review completes (direct, codified enforcement of R1 and R2; the log-allowed "dispatch without risk review for high-value" move becomes structurally impossible).
3. *Suspect lane*: fraud indicators  Fraud/risk review as today.
Reopens continue to Manual review per R3 — untouched.

**Targets.** All high-value device traffic (~35% of volume) and removes the outdated-rule chaos producing 1,400 spontaneous manual referrals. Keeps 43% (accessory) volume near-totally untouched.

**Mechanism of improvement.** Moves control effort from late, expensive correction (reopens arriving after 50–96h, consuming 40–54h-mean review/escalation loops) to early, cheap, rule-consistent filtering. Expected: high-value reopen rates migrate from 18–22% toward the 5–6% observed on reviewed paths, eliminating a large share of the ~260 dispatch-caused reopens and their EUR 410-class error costs. Codifying gates should also *raise model precision*, making future mining reliable.

**Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved issues.**
- Updated rules may push **15–35% more cases into manual review**, onto an already saturated desk (2,600 annual visits vs ~207 effective weekly hours at the 82% cliff). Mitigation attempted via queue priorities (Premium first) and shaving trivial 400-EUR-boundary reviews — but the capacity risk is accepted, not eliminated.
- Adds lead time to Standard high-value fast-path cases (currently a 28h path; gating realistically lands them nearer the lower bound of the reviewed path). We accept longer Standard-device cycles to buy compliance and rework reduction.
- Does not solve the evidence loop at all — B does that.

## 3. Redesign B: One-Shot Evidence Completeness Engine

*(Mid-loop: kill the ping-pong that traps 22% of volume)*

**Redesigned flow.** At intake (all segments), an attribute-conditioned **intake checklist** pre-computes everything the eventual review will need, based on defect type and value band. Instead of iterative drip requests (`Manual  Request evidence  Manual`, 780 + 890 traversals), the process issues **one consolidated evidence request** with a guided capture UI (new evidence portal; stated potential to cut repeat requests 30–45%). While evidence is outstanding, downstream preparation runs **in parallel** (e.g., part-price confirmation starts immediately for probable repair decisions — attacking the noted cause of Repair-quote delay rather than waiting sequentially). A **decision clock** replaces indefinite limbo: after N days of non-response, claims are routed to an assisted-callback attempt, then decisively adjudicated or withdrawn — no infinite parked state. Abandonment becomes an *explicit, monitored exit* rather than silent attrition.

**Targets.** Exclusively the unclear/missing-evidence cohort (22% share, 140h median, highest abandonment), plus anyone trickling into the 26%-repeat-request statistic.

**Mechanism of improvement.** Removes waiting-dominated loop legs (34h mean, 88h p90 re-entry) and — critically — **frees scarce reviewer-hours**: 890 returns into Manual review are largely re-reads of half-complete files; each avoided return releases capacity that Redesign A's stricter gates will otherwise absorb. Converting abandonment from accident to managed outcome lets Standard customers (who tolerate 72h *with good visibility*) be retained via proactive status updates instead of churning silently.

**Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved issues.**
- Explicitly accepts the warned **digital-literacy exclusion risk**: portal-only evidence may push vulnerable customers to abandon claims. Countermeasure (assisted channel, human callback at fixed checkpoints) recovers some but not all; residual exclusion is accepted and tracked.
- Consolidated mega-requests trade *number of touches* for *single-response size*: time-to-full-completion for some customers may lengthen even as total rework falls. This is a visible, explainable dip we accept.
- Auto-adjudication must never bypass R3 (reopened claims still pass manual review before close/denial) — enforced, but it caps how aggressively the decision clock can deny borderline cases.
- Does nothing for wrong-part selection in devices (that is C).

## 4. Redesign C: Diagnose-Before-Dispatch with Pre-Flight Part Validation

*(Back-stage: make device fulfillment right-the-first-time without adopting the full 96–120h reviewed path)*

**Redesigned flow.** For high-value devices (Standard first, Premium second):
1. After approval, a structured **defect-code  device-model  part-number match validation** executes *before* dispatch (a lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate, 6–18h depending on configuration).
2. Resourcing uses the **underused Quality-check inspectors** (stated idle except post-repair campaigns) at EUR 38/h — a deliberate use of measured slack capacity against a EUR 410 expected-error exposure; inspector workload is leveled against the marketing-calendar waves identified as fraud-desk crunch times.
3. For **Premium high-value devices**, dispatch becomes a validated **advance-replacement / courier exchange** from a small pooled inventory: the gate validates the part while the unit is en route, aiming to keep end-to-end under the 24h service-credit cliff *without* skipping R1/R2 controls (a fast-track, templated 15-minute review covers the mandate).
4. Reactive firefighting is converted into a structured path: supervisor-resolution authority for routine disputes with documented audit trail; the daily escalation board is reserved for genuinely high-exposure cases, protecting its ~450-minute weekly capacity.
5. Every gate catch and complaint gets tagged back into the defect-code library feeding the A-ruleset — closing the learning loop rather than letting corrections remain invisible labor.

**Targets.** High-value device fulfillment (both tiers), the 260 `Dispatch  Complaint/reopen` traversals, the 12.5% high-value rework rate, and the temporal-profile long tail explicitly attributed to incorrect replacement selection.

**Mechanism of improvement.** Shifts failure detection from a noisy, late customer-complaint signal (arriving at 96h mean, 180h p90) to an early, cheap, deterministic check costing hours. Where Redesign A stops bad claims earlier, C makes *good* claims execute correctly: it converts part of the expensive reviewed path's quality benefit (5–6% reopen) into the fast lane's structural speed, rather than forcing everyone through the slow path.

**Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved issues.**
- Adds 6–18h to device dispatches — knowingly eroding the fast path's speed advantage for devices where reopening risk justifies it; Premium units ride the pool/courier design specifically to stay under 24h, but some marginal cases will breach the credit threshold and pay credits. Accepted.
- Creates a **dependency on parts inventory**: a validated order still waits for the physical part; gate-passing  instant fulfillment. Pool capital ties up working cash and mis-sized pools cause stockouts that silently restore old behavior.
- Does not help accessories (already low error exposure) and can *increase* rework-technician load short-term as latent mis-dispatches get caught before shipping instead of after.

## 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A**
- *Failure 1 — Capacity cliff:* +15–35% manual-review inflow pushes utilization past 82%; queues deteriorate nonlinearly, silently recreating the 34–44h wait legs on the *early* stage instead of late rework.
- *Failure 2 — Gating theater:* under queue pressure, reviewers rubber-stamp (quality of review decays while conformance metrics look perfect) or staff dispatch outside the system to unblock Premium credits — Goodhart dynamics where R1/R2 metrics improve but behavior worsens.
- *Bottleneck migration:* from auto-screen (frictionless) and late complaints  **to the Manual eligibility review desk and Fraud specialists on Mondays/post-campaign**. Watch `IntakeManual` and `Req.EvidManual` wait distributions weekly.

**Redesign B**
- *Failure 1 — Digital exclusion:* portal-only evidence raises abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers — turning 390 silent abandons into larger, fairer-complaint-generating attrition.
- *Failure 2 — Mega-request stall:* one giant request may sit unanswered longer than three small nudges did; median time-to-complete can rise even as total rework drops, and a harsh auto-adjudication clock denies borderline claims  reputational/regulatory fallout.
- *Bottleneck migration:* from repeated reviewer reads  **to the assisted-outreach/callback team and the convergence edge back into Manual review**: if the 890-return edge doesn't actually shrink, the reviewer wall is unchanged. Verify that edge count, not just the repeat-request %.

**Redesign C**
- *Failure 1 — Gate congestion at waves:* inspectors are slack only *between* campaigns; dispatch bursts queue at the gate, stretching p90s exactly when promotional volume peaks — the pre-dispatch line inherits the fraud desk's Monday problem.
- *Failure 2 — Parts/inventory dependency:* a passed gate with an out-of-stock part halts fulfillment; undersized premium pools revert to unvalidated ad-hoc dispatch (old behavior with worse optics), and failed courier pickups spawn a brand-new complaint class.
- *Bottleneck migration:* from post-dispatch complaint handling/escalation board  **to pre-dispatch validation + parts provisioning**, with secondary pressure on repair technicians when gate failures trigger requotes.

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**Stage 0 — Instrumentation hygiene (before any pilot).** Fix END-token logging (~1,375 cases currently lack a reconciled endpoint) and the fraud-review in/out imbalance (980 vs 760); extend reopen observation windows (current signals arrive at 50–180h medians, so any short window censors failures); tag every event with segment/value-band/defect-type; build **segment-split process models** rather than one low-precision monolith. No credible go/stop decisions without this.

**First pilot — Redesign A, gated lane for Premium high-value devices (14% of volume), weeks 1–6.**
Rationale: smallest, sharpest signal (22% reopen, explicit R1/R2 stakes), rule update deploys in two weeks, and Standard high-value serves as a natural **holdout control** for difference-in-differences.
*Metrics:* reopen rate on gated cohort (baseline 22%); R1/R2 violations in scope (baseline: recurring alignment deviation); `IntakeManual` and re-entry wait p90s (baselines 18h/88h); manual-review utilization vs the 82% cliff; Premium cases breaching the 24h credit threshold; gated-path cycle-time p50/p90 vs the 20h fast baseline.
*Stop/go (qualitative-threshold form):*
- **Go/scale:** cohort reopen trending **30…50%**; violations near zero; review utilization holding under the cliff with wait medians no more than ~25% worse than baseline.
- **Adjust:** gated-cohort time-over-24h affecting a persistent double-digit-share of premiums (credit cliff), or review wait p90 **doubling** and staying there.
- **Stop/revert:** reopen improving but overall volume-weighted complaint rate flat (evidence of displacement without gain).

**Pilot 2 — Redesign B on the unclear-defect cohort, randomized portal vs assisted channel, weeks 5–12.** Sequenced second deliberately: B's freed reviewer-hours are what let A's increased inflow fit under the capacity cliff; running B first also cleans defect codes that A's rules depend on. *Metrics:* repeat-request incidence (baseline 26%  expect 30%), `Req.EvidManual` count (890 baseline) — **must shrink** — cohort median cycle (140h  target  ~100h), abandonment (390 baseline; guardrail: assisted-channel arm not worse than control ±noise). *Stop/go:* proceed if repeat-requests and the 890-edge both fall materially and abandonment is contained; recalibrate request size or the decision clock if completion latency balloons.

**Pilot 3 — Redesign C for Standard high-value fulfillment, weeks 10–16**, expanding to Premium with the swap pool only after gate stability. Sequenced last because it consumes defect-code quality produced by A+B and reuses inspectors whose availability depends on campaign timing. *Metrics:* gate-catch rate, dispatchreopen (baseline 260 traversals, 96h-mean arrivals), wrong-part cost proxy, added lead-time distribution against the 6–18h envelope, requote load on 3 technicians. *Go:* dispatchreopen falls in proportion to gate catches (see below); *stop:* gate failures marginal (<low single-digit %) while lead-time penalty lands on everyone — then the gate isn't paying for itself in this tier.

**Sequencing logic.** Front-stage control (cheap, fast deploy, defines cleaner segments)  mid-loop evidence (releases capacity the stricter gating consumes)  back-stage execution (depends on clean defect data and slack inspector timing). Each stage lowers the risk of the next.

**Learning whether improvements are real rather than shifted downstream:**
1. **Event-pair accounting:** every eliminated deviant edge must reappear as its intended corrective event (e.g., fewer `DispatchReopen` *plus* nonzero gate-catch records = real fix; reopen dropping with no compensating record = censoring or suppression).
2. **Holdout diff-in-diff:** Standard high-value (vs gated Premium), and non-portal vs portal arms, isolate treatment effects from seasonal/marketing waves that the fraud-bottleneck evidence shows matter.
3. **Full-window funnel per segment:** measure from intake to *verified* close plus a 60-day reopen look-back, per segment, on p50 **and** p90 — averages hide exactly the credit-breaching tails that motivated this redesign.
4. **Conformance drift monitoring:** token-replay fitness/precision per segment each week; a rising fitness with static precision warns of rubber-stamping (failure mode A-2).
5. **Automated temporal-profile alerts** on the edges predicted to become the *next* bottleneck (review desk under A; outreach and the 890-edge under B; gate queue and parts provisioning under C), so migration is detected as it forms, not after quarterly reporting.
