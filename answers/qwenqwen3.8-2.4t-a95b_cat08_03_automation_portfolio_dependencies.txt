# 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

The automation candidates are not independent. The process-mining evidence shows a chain of data-quality, control, and synchronization dependencies. A useful conceptual map is:

**A1/A2  A3/A4  A5/A6  A7  A8  A9  A10  A11**, with **A12** spanning all stages as a control/remediation layer.

However, the operational process should not be forced into a purely sequential system flow. The evidence says **KYC check and financial document review are often concurrent in practice**, even though the information system records them sequentially. Automation should therefore allow parallel work after classification, especially KYC and document collection.

## Upstream data quality dependencies

- **A1 Auto-registration** depends on complete intake fields and duplicate rules. If it is automated before classification and KYC are ready, it simply pushes more cases faster into downstream bottlenecks.
- **A2 Classification recommender** depends on registration/entity data quality. It is the gatekeeper for:
  - correct KYC intensity,
  - correct document checklist,
  - collateral requirement,
  - committee route,
  - exposure class,
  - contract template.
- **A4 Entity-mismatch assistant** depends on KYC hit identification, entity master data, registry evidence, and compliance specialist judgment. It should not operate as a black-box auto-clear function.
- **A5 Missing-document checklist** depends on A2. If product/exposure classification is wrong, the checklist will be wrong. It also depends on document repository status and RM/customer communication capability.
- **A6 Financial-statement extraction** depends on A5. Extraction cannot fix missing documents; it can only reduce manual normalization when documents are present and readable.
- **A7 Collateral triage** depends on A2 because collateral need varies by product: 71% of equipment finance and 18% of working capital. It also depends on valuer performance data.
- **A8 Committee pack auto-assembly** depends on many upstream artifacts: KYC clearance, normalized financials, valuation, and credit notes. It should not be automated before those artifacts are reliable.
- **A9 Contract generation** depends on clean committee decision terms, KYC clearance, product template, and control rules. It is highly sensitive to upstream data quality.
- **A10 E-signature** depends on A9 and KYC clearance. Customer signing cannot legally occur before KYC clearance.
- **A11 Loan activation bot** depends on all preceding controls being complete. It is the least suitable early automation if upstream control reliability is weak.

## Legal/compliance control dependencies

Several controls must be embedded into automation rather than treated as afterthoughts:

1. **KYC clearance must precede customer signing.**
   - Relevant candidates: A3, A4, A9, A10.
   - The process shows provisional signing in 6.5% of cases, with 1.1% requiring remediation. Automation must not normalize this behavior.

2. **Contract terms must match committee decision.**
   - Relevant candidates: A8, A9, A12.
   - Contract-remediation loops are 8.5% overall and 14% for credit-line renewal. A9 should include hard validation of decision-to-contract term mapping.

3. **Four-eyes principle for High-exposure SME cases.**
   - Credit analysis and committee packaging should not be completed by the same person.
   - Violations occur in 5.8% of High-exposure cases during month-end rush.
   - A9 and A12 can flag role conflicts, but committee-packaging systems and user-role data must support the check.

4. **Auditability of model suggestions and human overrides.**
   - Relevant candidates: A2, A3, A4, A6, A9.
   - Every automation module that makes a recommendation should log:
     - input data version,
     - model/rule version,
     - suggestion,
     - human decision,
     - override reason,
     - final outcome.

## Human escalation capacity

Automation will change human workload, not eliminate it.

- **Compliance specialists**: only 3 FTE, with high dependency from KYC ops to two senior specialists. A3 or A4 can help, but only if false-positive rates are controlled. Otherwise, automation increases escalation volume.
- **Credit analysts**: 9 FTE and central in the working-together network. A5 and A6 may increase clean inflow to analysts. Without WIP limits or normalization support, analysts may become the next bottleneck.
- **Legal ops**: 4 FTE, underutilized when upstream KYC is slow, but overloaded at month end. A9 can smooth contract preparation but may create a faster exception queue if upstream data is inconsistent.
- **Relationship managers**: often trigger missing-document loops through incomplete instructions. A5 should include RM nudging and escalation, not only customer-facing automation.
- **Back office**: 12 FTE with visible queues, but utilization drops below 65% after 15:00. This suggests room to shift exception handling, document chasing, and remediation work into afternoon slots.

## External valuation dependency

- **A7 Collateral valuation triage** can reduce internal delay and improve routing, but the external valuer remains outside direct control.
- The activity **Wait for external valuation** has a 58-hour median wait and 19% late rate.
- Automation should support:
  - early valuation request,
  - parallel valuation where risk allows,
  - valuer SLA tracking,
  - automated reminders,
  - exception routing for late valuations.
- However, committee packaging will still wait for valuation where required, so A7 alone does not eliminate synchronization risk.

## Core-banking integration constraints

IT can deeply integrate only **two modules in the first six months**. The recommended use of those slots is:

1. **A2 Product/exposure classification recommender**  
   Reason: it determines the downstream control path and is needed before many other modules can be trusted.

2. **A9 Contract generation with control-rule checks**  
   Reason: it is high value, control-critical, and needs reliable access to decision terms, KYC status, and contract data.

Other candidates can be introduced through lighter integration patterns:

- **A4**: case-workbench/API-based assistant for compliance specialists.
- **A5**: portal/document-management nudging, with minimal core writes.
- **A12**: dashboard/workbench reading event logs, not necessarily requiring deep core writes.
- **A10/A11**: should wait until upstream control data is reliable and integration capacity is available.

## Portfolio comparison

| Portfolio | Modules | What it optimizes | What it sacrifices |
|---|---|---|---|

## Portfolio notes

### Quick-win portfolio: A1, A5, A10, A12

This portfolio is attractive if senior management wants a visible win quickly. It includes:

- **A1** to reduce registration effort,
- **A5** to reduce missing-document loops,
- **A10** to reduce customer signing delay,
- **A12** to satisfy the requirement that at least one module strengthens controls.

However, it leaves **A2 classification risk** unresolved. Since classification errors misroute cases, accelerating registration without improving classification can increase rework in KYC, document review, and contract preparation. It also leaves the 31-hour entity-mismatch delay unresolved.

### Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A2, A4, A5, A9

This portfolio is more process-level. It recognizes that:

- **A2** is a high-frequency activity that determines control paths.
- **A4** addresses a low-frequency but high-impact critical path.
- **A5** reduces the missing-document loop that drives rework and customer delay.
- **A9** reduces contract rework and enforces key legal/control gates.

It sacrifices some direct labor savings from A1 and defers A6/A7/A10/A11. Those sacrifices can be mitigated through manual triage, backlog management, and phased follow-on automation.

### Compliance-first portfolio: A2, A3, A9, A12

This portfolio is strongest for regulators and internal audit. It focuses on:

- classification accuracy,
- KYC hit triage with human escalation,
- contract control checks,
- remediation workbench.

The trade-off is that it does not directly solve the entity-mismatch critical path or missing-document loops. It may improve control metrics while leaving cycle time and customer frustration high.

# 6. Recommended phased rollout

## Recommended first-year portfolio

**A2. Product/exposure classification recommender**  
**A4. Entity-mismatch resolution assistant**  
**A5. Missing-document checklist and customer portal nudging**  
**A9. Contract generation with control-rule checks**

This is the **bottleneck/synchronization portfolio**. It is recommended because it addresses high-frequency routing, low-frequency critical-path delays, rework loops, and contract-control risk. It also satisfies the compliance requirement that at least one first-wave module strengthen controls; in fact, A2, A4, and A9 all strengthen controls if designed correctly.

## Deep-integration plan for first six months

Because IT can deeply integrate only two modules in the first six months:

1. **A2 Product/exposure classification recommender**  
   Deep integration with application intake/core banking to read registration data and write recommended product/exposure/control path.

2. **A9 Contract generation with control-rule checks**  
   Deep integration with committee decision data, KYC status, contract templates, and contract storage.

The other two recommended modules should use lighter integration:

- **A4**: case workbench/API integration with KYC system and entity evidence sources.
- **A5**: document portal/checklist engine with document repository and notification capability.

This avoids consuming deep-integration capacity on A1 or A11 before control prerequisites are stable.

# 7. Final recommendation

## Recommended portfolio

I recommend the **bottleneck/synchronization portfolio**:

1. **A2. Product/exposure classification recommender**  
2. **A4. Entity-mismatch resolution assistant**  
3. **A5. Missing-document checklist and customer portal nudging**  
4. **A9. Contract generation with control-rule checks**

This portfolio is the strongest process-level choice because it does not simply automate the most frequent task. It automates the points where the process is most fragile:

- **A2** controls routing into KYC, document review, collateral, committee, and contract paths.
- **A4** addresses a low-frequency but critical synchronization failure: entity mismatches that block KYC and signing.
- **A5** reduces missing-document loops, repeated requests, and customer delay.
- **A9** reduces contract rework and enforces legal/control gates before signing.

It also satisfies the constraint that the first wave include control-strengthening automation. A9 directly enforces contract controls, while A2 and A4 strengthen KYC/routing controls.

## Why the highest-frequency activity should not be automated first

Senior management’s instinct to automate **A1 auto-registration** is understandable. Registration has high frequency, very high automation feasibility, and direct labor visibility. However, frequency alone is misleading.

Register application:

- appears early in the process,
- has relatively low median waiting time,
- has relatively low rework compared with classification, KYC, entity mismatch, missing documents, and contract remediation,
- and, if automated alone, accelerates cases into downstream activities that are already constrained.

The process evidence shows that the larger problems are not merely registration effort. They are:

- classification errors that misroute cases,
- entity mismatches that sit for long periods and block signing,
- missing-document loops that create repeated work,
- contract-remediation loops,
- external valuation synchronization,
- and control violations around KYC, four-eyes, and contract terms.

Automating A1 first would likely increase throughput into:

- **Register application  Classify product and exposure**: 11,680 directly-follows cases,
- **Classify product and exposure  KYC check**: 7,250 cases,
- **Classify product and exposure  Financial document review**: 3,940 cases.

Without A2, A4, A5, and A9 in place, that faster inflow can create more misroutes, more KYC exceptions, more document loops, and more contract-control failures. In other words, A1 can optimize a local activity while worsening the end-to-end process.

The better high-frequency win is **A2 classification**. It has nearly the same volume as registration but far greater process-level importance because it determines the correct control path. It gives management a visible high-frequency automation success while also reducing downstream risk.

## Defensible trade-off

The recommended portfolio sacrifices some easy direct labor savings from A1 and defers some attractive later modules such as A6, A7, A10, and A11. That trade-off is justified because:

- **A2** prevents downstream misrouting before volume increases.
- **A4** attacks a low-frequency but critical-path delay with severe waiting and rework.
- **A5** reduces the document loops that create customer friction and analyst rework.
- **A9** enforces legal and control integrity at the contract stage, where failures are costly.

A1 can still be considered later, preferably after classification, entity-resolution, and document-completeness controls are stable. If a visible quick win is politically necessary in the very short term, A1 could be limited to duplicate detection in a controlled pilot, with throttling and downstream WIP limits. But it should not be the anchor of the first-year automation portfolio.

The final recommendation is therefore: **do not start with the highest-frequency activity in isolation. Start with the highest-leverage process controls and synchronization points.**
