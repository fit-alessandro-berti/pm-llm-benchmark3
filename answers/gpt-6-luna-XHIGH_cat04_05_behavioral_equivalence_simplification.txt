# 1. Equivalence Notions

I use PM4Py process-tree semantics: `->` is sequence, `+` is parallel (both children execute, with either interleaving where possible), `X` is exclusive choice, and `*` is a binary loop. A loop executes its first child at least once; each additional iteration executes its second child and then its first child again.

- **Visible trace equivalence after hiding `tau`:** compare the sets of complete runs from the initial to final marking after erasing silent transitions. A trace is the ordered sequence of visible labels. Repeated labels are distinguished by their positions and multiplicities in that sequence, but events have no separate identity beyond their label and position. Thus two `Check Revision` occurrences remain two occurrences; they are not interchangeable with one occurrence.
- **Bounded visible-language equivalence under P3:** compare only complete visible traces with at most 12 visible events, including `Open Case` and `Close Case`, and at most three `Analyze Revision` events. Hide `tau` before applying the bounds. Equality within this finite subset is evidence only about this bound, not a proof about unbounded traces.
- **Business-relevant equivalence under P1:** retain the visible event order and occurrences of Analyze, Check, Approve, and Rework. Segment a run into revision attempts at each `Rework Case`; associate an analysis and its check by their attempt segment, and require the check to occur before that attempt's following Rework or the final Close. Approval occurrences and their positions are retained. P1 does not explicitly say the check must follow the analysis, only that it belongs to that revision and precedes the boundary. If “own check” additionally means *after* its analysis, that stricter ordering must be stated: Model A permits Check before Analyze because those activities are parallel.
- **Abstracted dashboard equivalence under P2:** map each complete trace to `(Open first, Close last, number of Analyze events, whether any Check occurred, whether any Rework occurred)`. Hide Approve and `tau`, collapse all Check occurrences to one Boolean, and discard the precise Analyze/Check interleaving. The dashboard admits only outputs with at least one Analyze and one Check.

# 2. Structural Comparison

| Feature | Model A | Model B | Semantic consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loop scope | The loop's first child contains parallel Analyze and Check, followed by the optional approval choice. | The loop contains Analyze followed by optional approval. Check is a sibling of the whole loop under `+`. | A repeats a check with each revision attempt; B executes one case-level check. |
| Minimum and maximum Check occurrences | At least one and unbounded maximum: one in every loop iteration. | Exactly one: both children of the outer parallel operator must execute, and the Check child runs once. | B cannot check each of multiple revisions. |
| Possible Check placement | Within its attempt, either before or after Analyze; it finishes before that attempt's approval choice, Rework, or Close. | Interleaved with the entire loop: before the first Analyze, between loop events, or after the last approval, but before Close. | A scopes a check to a revision; B permits a global check at many positions. |
| Optional approval occurrences | Zero or one per attempt, after both parallel activities finish; with `n` attempts, 0 through `n` visible approvals. | Zero or one per analysis, after that Analyze; with `n` attempts, 0 through `n` visible approvals. | Both preserve approval count and placement as visible behavior, though the check's relation to approval differs. |
| Concurrency | Analyze and Check are parallel within each attempt. | The Check runs in parallel with the whole revision loop. | The permitted interleavings differ, even for one attempt. |
| Join before Close | The loop must finish; every attempt's Analyze and Check and its optional approval must finish before the next Rework or Close. | Both the one Check and the complete loop must finish before Close. | Close is a join after all required parallel work in either model. |

# 3. Verdicts

| View | Verdict | Confidence | Decisive reason | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 operational view | not equivalent | High | A has one Check per revision attempt; B has one Check for the entire case. Their visible approval and check placements also differ. | A permits Check before Analyze. If P1 requires a post-analysis check, neither original guarantees that order. |
| P2 dashboard view | equivalent only under stated abstraction | High | Both produce exactly one or more analyses and checks; for `n` analyses, Rework occurs iff `n > 1`. Those are precisely the dashboard fields after hiding approvals and collapsing checks. | Their underlying traces, Check counts, and interleavings are different. |
| P3 bounded testing view | not equivalent | High | The witnesses below are complete, have at most 12 visible events and at most three analyses, and distinguish the languages. | This bounded result says nothing by itself about equality beyond the bound. |

# 4. Counterexample Traces

For compactness, let `O` = Open Case, `A` = Analyze Revision, `C` = Check Revision, `P` = Approve Revision, `R` = Rework Case, and `Z` = Close Case. Every row is a complete visible trace; silent `tau` choices are omitted.

| Trace ID | Trace | Model A | Model B | First decisive semantic difference | Relevant view |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| A1 | `O, A, C, R, A, C, Z` | yes | no | The second `C`: A checks both attempts; B has only one Check branch. | P1, P3 |
| A2 | `O, A, C, P, R, A, C, P, Z` | yes | no | Again, the second `C`; approvals do not create another Check in B. | P1, P3 |
| B1 | `O, A, P, C, Z` | no | yes | B can interleave its one Check after the loop's approval; A completes its parallel Analyze/Check pair before approval. | P1, P3 |
| B2 | `O, A, P, C, R, A, Z` | no | yes | B can place its one Check after the first approval and before Rework; A requires the attempt's Check before its approval and has no Check-free attempt. | P1, P3 |

Each A trace uses the `tau` branch of the approval XOR on no attempt, so no visible approval is required. A visible `Approve Revision` is retained when the other XOR branch is chosen; it is not hidden with `tau`. Each B trace chooses the visible approval branch in the first attempt. B1 and B2 demonstrate why retaining approval placement matters under P1. All four witnesses also satisfy the P3 bounds. In particular, A1 and B2 each contain two analysis attempts.

# 5. Bounded Comparison Code

The following code performs exhaustive breadth-first exploration of the converted accepting Petri nets for the stated bounds. It explores silent transitions without consuming visible length, deduplicates a state only when both its marking and visible prefix match, and records a trace only upon exact equality with the final marking. The converted process-tree nets are bounded, so repeated marking/prefix pairs stop silent cycles. This is exhaustive for the specified finite visible bounds, unlike random playout.

```python
from collections import deque
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.utils import petri_utils

TREE_A_TEXT = """->(
  'Open Case',
  *(
    ->(
      +('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'),
      X('Approve Revision', tau)
    ),
    'Rework Case'
  ),
  'Close Case'
)"""

TREE_B_TEXT = """->(
  'Open Case',
  +(
    'Check Revision',
    *(
      ->(
        'Analyze Revision',
        X('Approve Revision', tau)
      ),
      'Rework Case'
    )
  ),
  'Close Case'
)"""

tree_a = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE_A_TEXT)
tree_b = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE_B_TEXT)
net_a, im_a, fm_a = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_a)
net_b, im_b, fm_b = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree_b)

print("A sound:", pm4py.check_soundness(net_a, im_a, fm_a, print_diagnostics=True))
print("B sound:", pm4py.check_soundness(net_b, im_b, fm_b, print_diagnostics=True))

MAX_VISIBLE = 12
MAX_ANALYSES = 3
ANALYZE = "Analyze Revision"


def marking_key(marking):
    # Place names and token counts give a stable key for a marking.
    return tuple(sorted((place.name, count) for place, count in marking.items()))


def bounded_complete_language(net, im, fm):
    """All complete visible traces within both bounds, not sampled traces."""
    initial_trace = ()
    todo = deque([(im, initial_trace)])
    seen = {(marking_key(im), initial_trace)}
    complete = set()

    while todo:
        marking, trace = todo.popleft()

        # Only exact final-marking equality makes a run complete.
        if marking == fm:
            complete.add(trace)
            continue

        for transition in pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, marking):
            next_marking = petri_utils.execute(transition, marking)
            label = transition.label

            # Petri-net silent transitions have no visible label (tau).
            if label is None or label == "tau":
                next_trace = trace
            else:
                if len(trace) >= MAX_VISIBLE:
                    continue
                next_trace = trace + (label,)
                if label == ANALYZE and next_trace.count(ANALYZE) > MAX_ANALYSES:
                    continue

            state = (marking_key(next_marking), next_trace)
            if state not in seen:
                seen.add(state)
                todo.append((next_marking, next_trace))

    return complete


lang_a = bounded_complete_language(net_a, im_a, fm_a)
lang_b = bounded_complete_language(net_b, im_b, fm_b)
print("Complete traces unique to A:")
for trace in sorted(lang_a - lang_b):
    print(trace)
print("Complete traces unique to B:")
for trace in sorted(lang_b - lang_a):
    print(trace)
```

# 6. Stochastic and Footprint Comparison

Footprints can add a coarse diagnostic about ordering, concurrency, and directly-following relations. For example, a footprint comparison may reveal that Check can be interleaved with the loop in B, while A places each Check inside an iteration. Equal footprints would still not prove trace equivalence: a footprint summarizes relations and can miss occurrence counts, loop correlations, and longer ordering constraints. A differing footprint is evidence of a distinction; an equal one is not a proof of equal languages.

```python
footprints_a = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_a)
footprints_b = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_b)
print("Footprints equal:", footprints_a == footprints_b)
```

A stochastic language and Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) can compare distributions, but these process trees specify no transition probabilities. The following is an empirical comparison from playout samples; the simulator's choices induce a distribution that is not a business-supplied probability model. Sampling is not exhaustive, and different playout limits or simulator policies can change the estimate.

```python
log_a = pm4py.play_out(tree_a, num_simulations=10000)
log_b = pm4py.play_out(tree_b, num_simulations=10000)
stochastic_a = pm4py.get_stochastic_language(log_a)
stochastic_b = pm4py.get_stochastic_language(log_b)
print("Sample-based EMD:", pm4py.compute_emd(stochastic_a, stochastic_b))
```

A low EMD can coexist with a trace that is possible in one model and impossible in the other, especially when the distinguishing trace receives little sample mass. EMD compares weighted distributions under its chosen distance; it does not establish equality of supports or trace languages. No probabilities are assigned here beyond the empirical frequencies generated by playout.

# 7. Complexity Comparison

Counting every process-tree operator and leaf as one node, both original trees have 12 nodes. Depth below counts edges from the root to the deepest leaf. Each has six structurally visible leaves with six distinct labels, one silent `tau` leaf, and zero repeated visible leaf labels. “Duplicate-label count” here means the excess multiplicity among visible leaves in the finite tree syntax; runtime repetitions from looping are reported separately.

| Measure | Model A | Model B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Process-tree nodes | 12 | 12 |
| Maximum depth (edges) | 4 | 5 |
| Visible leaves | 6 | 6 |
| Silent leaves | 1 | 1 |
| Duplicate visible leaf labels | 0 | 0 |
| Runtime labels repeatable by looping | Analyze, Check, Approve, Rework | Analyze, Approve, Rework |
| Runtime Check occurrences | One per iteration; unbounded | Exactly one |

These counts follow directly from the displayed trees. In both, Open and Close occur once; the optional approval may occur zero or one time per iteration. Model A's loop repeats its Check leaf, while Model B's single Check leaf is outside the loop.

The code below computes the structural counts and asks the installed PM4Py version for the converted-net simplicity scores. I do not assign exact simplicity scores without running that version and conversion.

```python
from collections import Counter


def tree_stats(tree):
    visible_labels = []
    silent_leaves = 0
    node_count = 0
    max_depth = 0

    def visit(node, depth):
        nonlocal node_count, silent_leaves, max_depth
        node_count += 1
        max_depth = max(max_depth, depth)
        children = list(node.children)
        if not children:
            if node.label is None:
                silent_leaves += 1
            else:
                visible_labels.append(node.label)
        else:
            for child in children:
                visit(child, depth + 1)

    visit(tree, 0)
    counts = Counter(visible_labels)
    return {
        "nodes": node_count,
        "max_depth_edges": max_depth,
        "visible_leaves": len(visible_labels),
        "silent_leaves": silent_leaves,
        "duplicate_visible_leaf_labels": sum(n - 1 for n in counts.values()),
    }


print("A tree stats:", tree_stats(tree_a))
print("B tree stats:", tree_stats(tree_b))
print("A Petri-net simplicity:", pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net_a, im_a, fm_a))
print("B Petri-net simplicity:", pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net_b, im_b, fm_b))
```

# 8. Simplified Model

I target P2, since it intentionally hides approvals, collapses repeated checks, and discards Analyze/Check interleaving. The compact process tree below fixes one Analyze followed by one Check per attempt, with Rework between attempts.

```python
import pm4py

simplified_model = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
    "->( 'Open Case', *( ->( 'Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision' ), "
    "'Rework Case' ), 'Close Case' )"
)
```

| Property | Preservation in the simplified model |
|---|---|
| Open first and Close last | Exact. |
| At least one Analyze and one Check | Exact. The first loop iteration contains both. |
| Number of Analyze events | Exact for every dashboard output. |
| Whether any Rework occurred | Exact: it occurs iff there are at least two analyses. |
| Whether any Check occurred | Exact after P2's Boolean collapse; the simplified model has at least one. |
| Repeated Check count | Abstracted away, as P2 requires. The simplified tree has one Check per attempt. |
| Analyze/Check interleaving | Weakened to Analyze then Check; P2 discards this ordering. |
| Approve Revision occurrences | Lost from the model, as P2 hides them. |
| Silent behavior | Lost; P2 hides it. |
| P1 approval placement and check concurrency | Lost; this model is not a replacement for P1. |

The simplified tree has 8 nodes, maximum depth 3 edges, five visible leaves, and no silent leaves. Compared with each original's 12 nodes, it removes four nodes; its depth is one less than A's and two less than B's. It has one fewer visible leaf and one fewer silent leaf. Thus it is structurally simpler under these counts, though a Petri-net simplicity score should be measured using the code above with `simplified_model` converted in the same PM4Py version.

Regression traces accepted by the simplified model (and representing the corresponding P2 dashboard outputs) are:

- `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case`
- `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Rework Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case`
- `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Rework Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Rework Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case`

Negative complete traces for the simplified model and P2 requirements are:

- `Open Case, Analyze Revision, Close Case` — no Check occurred.
- `Open Case, Check Revision, Close Case` — no Analyze occurred.

# 9. Final Recommendation

Model A is the closer choice for P1 because it places one Check in every revision iteration and preserves approval occurrences. Its parallel Analyze/Check pair allows Check-before-Analyze; if the compliance rule requires the check to follow the analysis, revise that pair to the sequential expression `->( 'Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision' )`. Model B is not suitable for repeated-revision compliance because it checks only once per case.

For P2, either original is suitable after the stated abstraction, and they are equivalent as dashboard outputs. The simplified model is a clearer replacement for either one in a P2-only dashboard implementation; it should not replace the P1 operational model because it removes approvals and commits to an Analyze-then-Check order.

For P3, the originals are not equivalent even within the requested bound. Bounded comparison is exhaustive for the stated limits in the code, but matching bounded languages would still not prove unbounded equivalence.
