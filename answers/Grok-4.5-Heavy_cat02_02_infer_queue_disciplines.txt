# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

**QA**  
Supporting: A03 (appointment taken at exact window over older A02 and higher-prio A04); A02 then taken over A04; A05 taken over A06 and A08; A07 taken over A08. All consistent with FIFO once appointments are inserted.  
Contradicting: none major under the hybrid. Apparent “violations” of priority (A04, A08 delayed) and SPT (short A04 delayed) are explained by the primary FIFO rule. The 2-minute start offsets are within claimed arrival-scan delay.

**QB**  
Supporting: B02 (p1) immediately preempts B01; B05 (p1) is selected at 10:00 over residual B01 and lower jobs.  
Contradicting: 09:31 resume of lower-priority residual B01 instead of switching to B03; later selection of B04 over higher-prio B06; B07 appointment ignored. The interruption notes (“urgent arrival”, “dispatcher reassignment”) supply explicit operational exceptions; residual resume preference and possible 2-minute scan delay on B05 can explain the rest without abandoning the primary preemptive-priority diagnosis.

**QC**  
Supporting: C02 (shortest) first among initial backlog; C03 (next-shortest) next even over p1 C05; later C06 (shortest remaining) after the critical C05.  
Contradicting: C04 selected at 10:32 over both shorter C06 and higher-prio C05; C05 later preferred over remaining short jobs; C01 executed last among the initial backlog. The 10:32 anomaly cannot be fully absorbed by the claimed 2-minute scan delay (gap is ~7 min). Deadline-at-risk note on C05 and the possibility of a policy switch once an SLA is already missed provide a coherent secondary component; pure SPT is therefore only partial.

**QD**  
Supporting: exclusive-skill jobs D02 and D05 assigned only to D-F; D07 assigned to the only fully qualified resource D-X; D04 chosen over higher-prio/shorter D08 (FIFO within mechanical); D06 taken at its appointment time.  
Contradicting: D-F idles from 11:36 to 12:00 despite eligible D10 (p1) and exclusive D05; D07 chosen over p1 D10. The idle can be explained by a known future meeting (avoid starting a 30-min job that would be interrupted) plus a preference to reserve the specialized resource for its exclusive job while routing general electrical work to D-X. Priority is therefore only weak/secondary.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

**Episode 1 – QC at 10:32 (C04 chosen)**  
Two strongest competing explanations: (a) pure/near-pure SPT (would have selected C06), versus (b) SPT applied only to the initial backlog present before 10:25 / hybrid batch processing (selects C04).  
Discriminating additional observable: exact queue-visibility or system-entry timestamp of C06 (distinct from the recorded arrival scan) together with the est_min value that was actually displayed to the dispatcher at the decision instant. If C06 was invisible or carried a higher displayed estimate, (b) is supported; if it was fully visible with est 6, pure SPT is falsified and a deeper hybrid or data-quality issue remains.

**Episode 2 – QD at 11:25 (D-M chooses D04)**  
Two strongest competing explanations: (a) FIFO within skill-eligible (selects D04), versus (b) priority (or SPT) within skill-eligible (selects D08).  
Discriminating additional observable: the precise set of jobs marked eligible/visible to D-M’s dispatcher at 11:25, plus any skill-match score or “preferred resource” flag. If D08 carried a lower skill-match or was already soft-assigned elsewhere, priority can still be reconciled; if both were fully eligible and equal, FIFO is strongly preferred and pure priority is ruled out for this resource.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked by plausibility given the supplied notes and data constraints:

1. **Dispatcher manual overrides / reassignments** (highest). Explicitly recorded for B01’s second interruption and implied by “appointment not honored”. Would turn an apparent pure-rule violation into an intentional hybrid without changing the primary diagnosis for the automatic cases.

2. **Known future resource unavailability / interruption avoidance**. Explains D-F’s idle before the recorded meeting (avoid starting D05) and possibly A1’s calibration window. Alters diagnosis only by adding an availability-constraint layer on top of skill-based or FIFO rules.

3. **Arrival-scan delays at or beyond the claimed 2-minute bound, or delayed eligibility visibility**. Can partially rescue the QC 10:32 anomaly and some QB timing. If systematically larger than claimed, it weakens all timing-based discrimination (FIFO vs SPT vs priority) and lowers every confidence score.

4. **Noisy or strategically inaccurate est_min values**. Realized service times sometimes match estimates closely, sometimes not. If estimates used at decision time differed from the table values, SPT diagnoses (especially QC) become less reliable; the primary labels would shift toward priority or FIFO.

5. **Policy change once an SLA is already endangered**. Explicitly plausible for C05 and consistent with the note. Converts the QC diagnosis from “pure SPT” into the hybrid already listed; does not affect the other queues.

6. **Unrecorded skill preferences or soft constraints** (lowest among operational). Could explain why D-F did not take general electrical work. Would make the QD secondary component more complex but would not overturn skill-based routing as primary.

Data-quality explanations (scan delay, missing availability records) are plausible but secondary to the explicit operational notes already present.

# 6. Additional event data needed

Prioritized collection plan tailored to the residual ambiguities:

1. Exact queue-entry / system-visibility timestamp for every job (distinct from the possibly delayed arrival scan). Highest priority for resolving QC 10:32 and several QB borderline cases.
2. Snapshot of the eligible/visible job set presented to the dispatcher at every service-start or preemption decision, including current remaining est_min and any soft-assignment flags.
3. Resource calendar with precise unavailability intervals and reasons (meetings, calibration, prior-job overrun) known in advance to the dispatcher.
4. Preemption decision log: reason code, estimated remaining time of the preempted job, and any switching-cost or threshold parameter used.
5. Appointment tolerance windows (earliest/latest acceptable start) rather than single point times.
6. Skill-match matrix or preference scores used by the routing engine (especially for multi-skilled resources such as D-X and D-F).
7. Policy-version or active-rule identifier at each decision epoch (to detect mid-day switches when SLAs become endangered).
8. Realized versus displayed est_min at the moment of selection (to test strategic or noisy estimates).

These fields would convert most current observational equivalences into decisive tests.

# 7. Final synthesis

QA is the most identifiable queue: repeated, consistent selection of the oldest eligible job once appointments are satisfied produces clear contradictions to priority and SPT across multiple independent decision points.  
QC is the least identifiable: the early SPT signal is strong, yet the 10:32 anomaly and subsequent priority insertion for the already-missed deadline allow several hybrid and data-quality interpretations of roughly equal plausibility.  
The claimed policy that would be most dangerous to infer from these observations alone is pure priority scheduling. It is strongly violated in QA, only partially and selectively present in QB, subordinated to SPT in QC, and subordinated to skill-plus-FIFO in QD; any system-wide claim of priority would therefore be an over-generalization that the decision-set evidence does not support.
