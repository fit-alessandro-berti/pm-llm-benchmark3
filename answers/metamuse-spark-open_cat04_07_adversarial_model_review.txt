# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

**Governance question:** Which model, if any, can be trusted to describe, predict and enforce the dossiers process; what do the reported numbers actually prove; and what must a replacement do differently.

I distinguish four use cases with different tolerable error trade-offs:

*   **Exploration / discovery.** Goal: make sense of incomplete logs, find frequent patterns, communicate with business. Tolerates **high fitness, low precision**: admitting extra behaviour is acceptable if flagged as “inferred”. Must not *hide* mandatory rules, but may visualize uncertainty.
*   **Conformance enforcement / compliance.** Goal: decide compliant vs non-compliant for audit, automatic approval, SLA. Requires **high precision and controlled fitness** against a *specified* normative policy, not against observed log frequency. Low holdout fitness is not “strictness”; it is prediction failure. Unseen-but-valid behaviour must not be rejected. Needs curated negative tests.
*   **Simulation / what-if.** Goal: generate plausible future traces. Needs realistic optionalities, data-conditioned branching, correctly ordered dependencies, soundness (no deadlocks, proper termination). Over-permissive parallelism breaks resource and time simulation.
*   **Stakeholder communication / maintenance.** Goal: impact analysis, ownership, change. Requires block-structured, duplicate-free, declarative annotation of uncertain parts. Branch-enumerated models fail regardless of numeric scores.

Stakeholder agreement P1–P7 is the normative anchor, *not* the training log. The discovery log is known to lack `external/internal` and `affected_check` attributes and to have missing `Supplier Review` events (F4). Both models must therefore be judged against policy, not against how well they replay a biased 80/20 split.

Key assumption: The behaviour probes B1–B8 are *policy test cases* (B5 explicitly as plausible valid, B6 as conditional, B8 as suspicious), not ground truth for learning. A model that memorises frequent training variants and rejects a plausible policy combination fails generalisation, even if training precision looks excellent.

Fitness/precision trade-off is use-case dependent:
For exploration, 0.95+ fitness with 0.55 precision can be defensible *if* extra behaviour is documented as extrapolation. For enforcement, precision <0.7 is unacceptable, but precision achieved by enumerating 8 training traces (Model C) is *artifactual*: it measures memorisation, not correctness. No single weighted average of fitness+precision+simplicity+generalisation defines deployability.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

All reported numbers share the undocumented split/ cost/ preprocessing provenance – comparability not established.

**Ranked by likely decision impact (1 = most dangerous for governance):**

**1. Fitness rewarded by optionalizing mandatory behaviour.** S’s `X(Credit,tau)` makes mandatory steps silent-skip. Any trace missing mandatory checks still aligns with zero model moves (tau). In presence of missing events (F4: Supplier missing, possibly Credit/Technical missing), training fitness 1 without proving compliance. Decision impact: governance may deploy S believing 0.992 fitness means compliant, while it admits B3/B4. Pathology applies to C less, but C’s X-branch enumeration also inflates fitness via alternative paths.

**2. Train/holdout leakage & split sensitivity.** Same 80/20 install but no seed, preprocessing code, held-out IDs, stratification, or alignment-cost settings reported. With small log and rare combos (`Supplier+Auto+TR,CC` in F4), a single random split can swing holdout fitness by >0.2. C’s 0.744 could be split artefact; S’s 0.981 could be leak (e.g., temporal leakage, duplicate cases). Without reproducible splits, performance claim is unverifiable and not generalisable.

**3. Missing-event bias.** Discovery log lacks `external/internal` flag, so absence of `Supplier Review` conflates *internal order (legitimately absent)* with *external order with missing event* and *logging error*. Both models treat absence as evidence of optionality, but neither distinguishes cause. S leans into bias (all optional); C partially (requires Supplier only in enumerated branches). Holdout cases with Supplier+Auto reflect different missingness regime, punishing C unfairly and rewarding S’s over-permissiveness. Without perturbation tests, metrics are confounded.

**4. Positive-log-only precision (F5).** Reported precision only vs observed positives. Model that enumerates training traces (C) trivially scores 0.94; permissive model (S) scores 0.58. Neither tells whether B3/B4/B8 forbidden traces are correctly rejected (negative suite) or whether B5 valid traces are accepted. For compliance, negative precision is the decisive statistic – absent. Auditor must disregard reported precision until curated negative suite is evaluated (Section 9).

**5. Duplicate-label and silent-transition effects.** C’s 33 duplicates cause alignment to nondeterministically choose among identically labelled transitions in different branches, inflating/deflating fitness/precision depending on cost model. S’s 4× `tau` create free model moves: alignment can skip mandatory checks at near-zero cost, inflating fitness and masking violations. Neither team reports cost for log-move vs model-move vs `tau`, or version handling of duplicates – numbers not comparable. Runtime 0.44s vs 0.018s partly reflects this.

**6. Alignment-cost sensitivity.** Without published cost settings, a checker can make any model fit by setting model-move cost  log-move cost (or vice versa). Mandatory violations (missing Credit) become “cheap” in S, correction events become “expensive” in C. Probe B6 (single `TR` repeat) could flip accept/reject with small cost change. Metrics without sensitivity analysis are not decision-ready.

**7. Generalisation rewarded by excessive permissiveness.** Token-based generalisation 0.907 for S is tautological: parallel `+` can generate exponentially many traces, so token replay finds few unexpected tokens. Conversely C 0.412 punishes enumeration, not policy incorrectness. Metric reverses causality: correct generalisation should accept unseen *policy-valid* combos (B2/B5) and reject forbidden (B3/B4), not admit everything.

**8. Simplicity arc-degree confounding by model size.** Conversion `X(->(...),->(...))`  Petri net expands each branch to separate subnet, exploding places/arcs. Using simplicity to prefer S would select the least informative model.

**9. Runtime confounding.** 0.440s median for C correlates with duplicates and branching, but also with unreported hardware, caching, parallelism, log length, alignment algorithm (A* vs ILP), timeout handling. Cannot infer deployment cost without controlled benchmark. However magnitude 24× indicates operational risk for batch conformance.

**10. Stakeholder comprehension methodology.** Informal 1–5 score without protocol (sample, blinding, tasks, inter-rater , question wording). S’s 4.4 likely measures familiarity with simple boxes vs unfamiliarity with branching enumeration, not understanding of *policy* (F1). High comprehension of a wrong model is *negative* value for compliance (users confident in forbidden traces). Needs documented comprehension test (Section 9).

# 7. Third Alternative

**Strategy chosen:** **Block-structured procedural POWL backbone + DECLARE/resource constraints + explicit uncertainty/post-hoc checks.** Rationale: P1–P5 are precedence and choice constraints best expressed as partial order; P3/P6 uncertainty due to missing attributes must not be silently resolved by optionalizing or enumeration, but surfaced for human attestation.

### 7.1 Compact formal description

```
model_third = ->(
  Register Dossier,
  LOOP(
    body = StrictPartialOrder(
            nodes = { Credit Check, Technical Review, Supplier_opt, Expedite_opt, Approval },
            edges = {
              Credit -> Supplier_opt,   Technical -> Supplier_opt,
              Credit -> Expedite_opt,   Technical -> Expedite_opt,
              Credit -> Approval,       Technical -> Approval,
              Supplier_opt -> Approval, Expedite_opt -> Approval
            }
          ),
    redo = ->( Correct Dossier, RecheckChoice )
  ),
  Release Order
)

Supplier_opt = X(Supplier Review, tau)   // optional interval, not free-floating
Expedite_opt = X(Expedite Review, tau) // optional, but if present after both checks
Approval     = X(Manager Approval, Auto Approval)  // exactly one
RecheckChoice = X( Credit Check, Technical Review, +(Credit Check,Technical Review) )
              // transparent choice: only Credit, only Technical, or both; Supplier/Expedite may be re-executed if in scope, logged as redo attribute
```

Procedural backbone enforces: Register first, Release last (P1); mandatory Credit+Technical in any order, before Approval (P2); Supplier and Expedite *iff present* must be *after* both mandatory checks and before Approval, mutually unordered (P3/P4); exactly one approval (P5); correction loop with selective repeat (P6). Suspicious repetition without `Correct` is *not* modelled as dead path but as post-hoc violation (P7) to keep model sound and auditable.

Declarative overlay (non-procedural) handles:
* `NotCoExistence(Manager Approval, Auto Approval)` (redundant with XOR but auditable)
* `Precedence(Credit Check, Expedite Review)` and `Precedence(Technical Review, Expedite Review)`
* `NotRepeatWithoutCorrection` as constraint, not enumeration

Missing-data uncertainty: `Supplier_opt` remains optional in control-flow, but decision rule `SupplierRequired = external==true` is externalised as data attribute check; when attribute missing, case routed to *Attestation Queue* rather than auto-conformed. Similarly `RecheckChoice` records affected checks as explicit XOR, but `affected_check` attribute may be missing – then auditor attestation required.

### 7.2 Self-contained PM4Py code assigning `model_third`

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# --- atomic transitions (single labelled occurrence, no duplicates in backbone) ---
register  = Transition(label="Register Dossier")
credit    = Transition(label="Credit Check")
technical = Transition(label="Technical Review")
supplier  = Transition(label="Supplier Review")
expedite  = Transition(label="Expedite Review")
manager   = Transition(label="Manager Approval")
auto      = Transition(label="Auto Approval")
release   = Transition(label="Release Order")
correct   = Transition(label="Correct Dossier")

# Re-check transitions (label-equal but documented as loop iteration;
# kept as distinct objects for POWL construction; duplicate count = 3 justified, documented)
credit_r    = Transition(label="Credit Check")
technical_r = Transition(label="Technical Review")

# --- optional wrappers (explicit optionality, not silent permissiveness) ---
supplier_opt = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[supplier, SilentTransition()])
expedite_opt = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[expedite, SilentTransition()])

# --- exactly-one approval ---
approval = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[manager, auto])

# --- inner partial order for one pass: Credit || Technical -> {Supplier_opt, Expedite_opt} -> Approval ---
# In POWL, unordered = no edge. Supplier and Expedite unordered between themselves.
inner_po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[credit, technical, supplier_opt, expedite_opt, approval])
inner_po.order.add_edge(credit, supplier_opt)
inner_po.order.add_edge(technical, supplier_opt)
inner_po.order.add_edge(credit, expedite_opt)
inner_po.order.add_edge(technical, expedite_opt)
inner_po.order.add_edge(credit, approval)
inner_po.order.add_edge(technical, approval)
inner_po.order.add_edge(supplier_opt, approval)
inner_po.order.add_edge(expedite_opt, approval)

# --- selective recheck choice (transparent per F6) ---
only_credit    = credit_r
only_technical = technical_r
both_checks    = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[credit_r, technical_r])  # no edge => parallel/any order, both required
# both_checks has no edge between credit_r/technical_r -> both in any order

recheck_choice = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[only_credit, only_technical, both_checks])

# redo = Correct Dossier -> recheck_choice (Supplier/Expedite redo handled as attestation if needed)
redo_seq = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE, children=[correct, recheck_choice])

# Loop: body = inner_po, redo = redo_seq
# LOOP semantics: execute body, then optionally (redo, body) repeatedly
loop_body = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[inner_po, redo_seq])

# --- outer partial order: Register -> Loop_body -> Release ---
model_third = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, loop_body, release])
model_third.order.add_edge(register, loop_body)
model_third.order.add_edge(loop_body, release)
model_third.order.add_edge(register, release)  # transitive, ensures Register first, Release last

# Optional: verify soundness after conversion
# net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)
# print(pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True))
```

*Why this avoids enumeration:* Credit/Technical order freedom via *absence of edge* (not two branches). Supplier/Expedite optionality via single `X(label,tau)` each (not `tau` on mandatory). Approval exactly-one via single XOR. Correction loop uses documented 3-way XOR for selective repeat, not 8+ enumerated total orders. Net duplicate count = 2 (justified redo labels), not 33.

def posthoc_checks(trace):
    """Trace as list of activity labels. Returns violations list."""
    violations = []
    # P7: repeat without correction
    for i, act in enumerate(trace):
        if act in ("Credit Check","Technical Review"):
            if act in trace[:i]:
                # was there a Correct Dossier since previous occurrence?
                prev_idx = max([j for j,jact in enumerate(trace[:i]) if jact==act], default=-1)
                if "Correct Dossier" not in trace[prev_idx+1:i+1]:
                    violations.append(f"P7 suspicious repeat of {act} without Correct Dossier at position {i}")
    # P4 ordering if Expedite present
    if "Expedite Review" in trace:
        for pre in ("Credit Check","Technical Review"):
            if pre not in trace or trace.index(pre) > trace.index("Expedite Review"):
                violations.append(f"P4 Expedite before {pre}")
    # P2 mandatory
    if not ({"Credit Check","Technical Review"} <= set(trace)):
        violations.append("P2 missing mandatory Credit/Technical before approval")
    return violations

### 7.4 Explicit semantics

*   **Mandatory checks (P2):** Procedurally mandatory: `inner_po` contains `credit` and `technical` without `tau` alternative; partial order has no skip path. Any trace without both fails to parse. `X` not used.
*   **Optional Supplier Review (P3):** `supplier_opt = X(Supplier,tau)` inside the interval `after both mandatory, before Approval` (edges enforced). Control-flow accepts both, but *normative* requirement conditioned on `external==True` externalised to `supplier_policy`. Missing attribute  acceptance with warning, not silent permissiveness.
*   **Expedite ordering (P4):** Edges `Credit->Expedite_opt`, `Technical->Expedite_opt`, `Expedite_opt->Approval` enforce must-follow-both and must-precede-approval. Mutual unordered with Supplier via absence of edge between `supplier_opt`/`expedite_opt`.
*   **Exactly-one approval (P5):** Single XOR node `approval`; `NotCoExistence` declarative redundant for audit trail. No `tau` path skips approval.
*   **Correction/recheck (P6/P7):** LOOP allows `Correct Dossier` iteration. Redo is *selective* XOR of three documented recheck patterns, satisfying F6 transparency. `NotRepeatWithoutCorrection` enforced post-hoc, not by deleting loop language, so soundness preserved and auditor can distinguish “model forbids” vs “policy violation”.

### 7.5 Limitations caused by missing attributes

*   **external/internal missing:** Model cannot decide if absent `Supplier Review` is compliant (internal) vs omitted (external with missing event). Backbone will accept internal trace without Supplier; external trace with missing Supplier will align successfully but `posthoc_checks` flags missing attribute for human adjudication. Historical log estimates of Supplier frequency are biased by missing events (F4) – training log cannot be used to learn `P(Supplier|external)`. Resolution requires future log enrichment, not model complexity.
*   **affected_check missing:** Cannot auto-validate whether redo repeated correct subset. `RecheckChoice` forces processor to *declare* which checks were affected; without attribute we can only check structural presence of Correct before repeat (P7) and flag mismatched redo scope for manual review. Duplicate-label-free backbone prevents hidden enumeration of all redo permutations.
*   **Supplier missing events:** Token-based fitness/generalisation historically inflate optional model; backbone’s optional Supplier will still replay missing-event traces but flagged in attestation queue. Governance must publish missing-event rate before using any model for enforcement.

### 9.10 Decision rule for rejection despite good aggregates

*   Rejects 1 unseen-valid policy combination (unseen fitness <1.0)

*   Unsound, contains undocumented duplicates >5, or median alignment >0.2s without scaling justification
*   Cannot represent P6 selective repeat transparently or routes >30% cases to ambiguous attestation without plan to capture missing attributes

# 11. Dissenting View

**Rebuttal (198 words):**

The report over-engineers governance and undervalues what the business actually asked for. Model S delivers what operations need: 0.992 fitness, 0.981 holdout, 0.018s alignment, and 4.4 comprehension – users understand it, IT can run it, and it tolerates the missing Supplier events we know exist (F4). Demanding a curated negative suite and attribute-enriched logging before any enforcement is academic purism that will leave auditors with no model for months. Model C, for all its ugliness, at least reflects how the process *is* run: those eight variants are the approved paths – anything else *should* be treated as non-compliant until a change board approves it (F8). The “enumeration is unmaintainable” critique is IT vanity; we maintain larger rule engines daily. The third alternative adds a POWL diagram no processor has seen, a LOOP with selective recheck that reintroduces duplicate labels, and a declarative overlay that cannot be executed end-to-end in PM4Py – it trades one complexity for another while still requiring manual attestation for 30% of cases due to missing attributes. Simpler to keep S for exploration and C for enforcement, with a one-line fix: make Credit/Technical mandatory in S, keep C as authoritative, and revisit when data improves.

**What would change the decision:** Publish a reproducible 5-fold holdout where S, with Credit/Technical forced mandatory, still exceeds C on *negative-suite rejection* (0% false positives) and runtime at scale, and show auditor-validated evidence that the “unseen valid” combos B2/B5 occur at >5% frequency in the wild (not just synthetic plausibility). Conversely, if a cost-sensitivity test shows `model_third` maintains 0% negative acceptance and 100% unseen-valid acceptance across cost matrices while keeping median alignment under 0.05s and attestation queue under 10% after adding `external` capture in a pilot, I would switch to endorsing third as both exploration and enforcement candidate.
