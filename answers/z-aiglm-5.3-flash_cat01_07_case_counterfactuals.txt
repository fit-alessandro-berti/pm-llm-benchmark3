ComplianceReview   -> Decision

Assessment        -> RequestMoreInfo       (optional)
ComplianceReview  -> RequestMoreInfo       (optional)

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:** The binding delay was not work volume but information sequencing. Triage spotted the sanctions ambiguity at 08:50 yet escalated nothing; the knowledge was rediscovered by A1 on Tuesday evening, forcing S1 advice (Tue night) and then a compliance review on Wednesday in which C1 re-read nearly everything ("most evidence had to be read from scratch"). C1's Monday backlog (20h, rising urgency) meant even a prepared file would not have been reviewed earlier than Tuesday — so the gains come from *compression of C1's review*, not from pulling C1 forward dramatically.

**Minimal intervention:** Prepare the packet at triage; let S1 advise Monday morning (S1 is otherwise idle and legally able to overlap); schedule one short preliminary screen by C1 in a Tuesday trough; reserve — not consume — a Wednesday-morning final-review slot. Nothing in the core chain (I1, D1, A1 timings) is touched, preserving minimal change.

**Temporal propagation:** S1's Monday memo reaches A1 *before* Assessment, so the assessment resolves rather than merely discovers the ambiguity; that memo and the preliminary screen then act as a pre-index for C1, cutting the final review from ~4.5h to ~2–2.5h. Because rule 5 forces final compliance after the latest assessment (ends Tue 19:10), the earliest possible completion is Tuesday evening; the reserved Wednesday 09:00 slot is the realistic base. Downstream, Decision and Notify slide from 18:00/18:40 to midday Wednesday. The queue estimates are forecasts: if the two urgent Monday-afternoon compliance cases overflow their windows, they plausibly cannibalize the reserved slot.

**Process-feasibility check:** Orderings hold: RegisterTriage first; original-free case  parallel verification legal (rule 3); preliminary screen allowed (rule 10) and explicitly non-substitutive; final review after latest assessment (rule 5); four-eyes intact (A1 vs C1). Resource exclusivity respected: C1 performs only one interval at a time; S1's advisory status covers the overlap.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best — reserved Tuesday-evening slot after assessment ends 19:10: final review Tue 19:30–21:30, notify ~22:00 (**~19h**). Base — Wed 09:00–11:30 review, decision ~12:15 (**~6h**). Worst — packet materially incomplete; C1's final review re-expands to ~4.5h and/or the reservation is displaced by urgent cases; notify near Wed 18:40 (**~0h**, plus collateral delay to the urgent cases).

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Historical data showing preliminary screens rarely reduce final-review effort (i.e., preliminary  duplicated work); C1 slot ledgers showing no free contiguous window before Wednesday even with reservations; or incident data showing that pre-baked condition drafts anchored reviewers into premature conclusions.

### K-219

**Factual mechanism:** The 48h cycle was manufactured by a defensive routing choice. Originals landed Monday 17:30, but the file sat overnight-plus in D1's queue, starting only Tue 09:30. D2 had capacity hours earlier. The bottleneck was positional (which queue you stand in), not intrinsic difficulty.

**Minimal intervention:** Change only the assignment: on Originals Receipt, enqueue at D2. No reordering of Register/Triage/Identity; rule 4 is honored since Originals Receipt (17:30) precedes the check.

**Temporal propagation:** Using the Mon-16:00 snapshot (D2  5h), D2 should reach the file around 22:30–23:00 Monday and, at 4–8h for standard work weighted toward the upper band for handwriting, finish Tue 02:30–06:30 — roughly 7–10h before D1's factual 13:30 completion. Whether this translates into an earlier *decision* depends on the assessment queue: the case's own history shows A2 was not free until ~Tue 20:00 even for a 13:30-ready file, so the assessment start is uncertain between ~14:00 and 20:00. The critical nonlinearity is quality: D1 was chosen *because* handwriting is hard. A D2 clarification request converts a 12h gain into a 20–50h loss, because rule 7 mandates fresh Document Check *and* Assessment after any RMI, and external suppliers respond in 8–36h.

**Process-feasibility check:** All hard prerequisites preserved; no compliance layer applies (low risk/value); four-eyes vacuously satisfied. Feasibility is unconditional; *desirability* is probabilistic.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best — D2 quick start, clean read, A2 picks up Tue 14:00–17:00, M1 signs same evening: **~12h**. Base — no rework, decision Wed 08:30 unchanged: **0 to 6h** (readiness gained, sign-off slot unchanged). Worst — clarification raised Tue night: external wait + re-check + re-assessment  **+18 to +50h**, worst around Thursday–Friday completion.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** D2's historical clarification rate on handwritten documents materially above ~30% (expected value flips negative); instances of D1 being immediately available Monday evening (undercutting the queue argument); or evidence that D2's misses (false negatives on handwriting nuance) caused downstream corrections.

### K-337

**Factual mechanism:** This is the one intervention that rewrites the *decision function*, not the schedule. Loops exist because the assessment treats "known but unconfirmed figures" as blocking. Each loop costs a fresh Document Check and Assessment (rules 7), and loop 2 additionally paid a weekend tariff (Fri 10:30  Mon 09:00, ~70.5h). From Assessment 2's end (Fri 10:30), the factual path spent ~95h reaching notification.

**Minimal intervention:** Delete exactly the second RMI edge and require the discrepancy to be represented *inside* the decision — as a condition or a rejection. Everything upstream (through Thu 17:00 DC2, Fri 10:30 Assess2) is untouched.

**Temporal propagation:** Removing RMI2 eliminates not just 70.5h of waiting but the entire forced re-execution chain (DC3 Mon 3.5h, Assess3 Tue 2.5h). The new final assessment is Assessment 2 (Fri 10:30), so rule 5 now pulls Compliance Review forward to Friday afternoon (or Monday, if C1's Friday calendar is full — the snapshot table gives no visibility past Thursday, so this is genuinely uncertain). Decision then lands Friday evening or Monday 08:30, versus the following Tuesday. Crucially, rule 6 lets *compliance* initiate RMI too: if C1 judges the open discrepancy disqualifying, it can reinstate the loop, erasing the savings — the intervention relocates the rework veto rather than abolishing rework.

**Process-feasibility check:** Sequence valid: Assess2 (final)  ComplianceReview  Decision  Notify; four-eyes holds (A1 vs C1); high-risk/high-value triggers the review; C7 forbids silent approval. The one soft spot is organizational, not logical: a manager signing a conditional approval on materially inconsistent evidence is operating near the boundary of defensible practice.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best — C1 Friday slot, same-evening sign-off: **~88h** (notify Fri ~18:10 vs Tue 09:10). Base — Monday-morning review + decision: **~25h**. Worst — C1 exercises its own RMI Monday or governance mandates Tuesday sign-off: **~0 to 5h**, plus a litigated or reversed decision later (cost invisible in this trace).

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Audit policy treating confirmed-evidence gaps as mandatory escalation; loss history showing conditional approvals on disputed supplier data producing disproportionate claims; or compliance-behavior data showing C1 systematically demands confirmation whenever discrepancies persist (making the counterfactual collapse onto the factual path).

### K-411

**Factual mechanism:** Pure infrastructure failure: the legacy lock created one task, serializing two checks that rule 3 explicitly allows to run together. The direct serialization cost is modest (~3.2h: docs could have overlapped identity), but the indirect cost depended on which assessment slot the earlier-ready file could catch.

**Minimal intervention:** Create both tasks at triage; authorize a logged lock override so D2's task owns the case during the overlap window; require event-time stamps. Identity timing deliberately left untouched (minimal change).

**Temporal propagation:** Both verifications start from the same Tue-morning queues (I2  2h, D2  4h at 08:00). I2 proceeds factually (11:30–13:20); D2 takes the file around 12:00 and finishes ~16:30, i.e., verification-complete moves 19:30  ~16:30. Now the counterfactual forks on the assessment queue, not on the case: if A2 has a Tuesday ~20:00 opening (consistent with this resource's observed availability patterns elsewhere this week), the case clears assessment that evening and decision arrives Wed 08:30; if A2's next slot is the factual Wed 09:30 (queue had grown overnight per the note), then three hours of earlier readiness buy *nothing* — classic readiness-vs-completion divergence. Real-time timestamps matter only for *measurement*: batch entry at 17:00 would record both tasks as sequential, hiding genuine concurrency.

**Process-feasibility check:** Concurrency legal (no originals, rule 3); both checks precede Assessment (rule 2); no compliance layer required; unary capacity holds since I2 and D2 are different resources; the workaround must produce an auditable exception token, otherwise C8 fails even if work truly overlapped.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best — assessment caught Tue evening: **~6h** (23.5h cycle). Base — 50/50 slot lottery: **2.5 to 3h expected**. Worst — slot-gated at Wed 09:30 plus a logging defect triggering task re-entry: **~0 to +3h**.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Workaround deemed impermissible under system-control policy (feasibility fails outright); assessment staffing fixed such that start times never respond to earlier readiness; or audit finding retro-stamped (false) concurrency in pilot logs.

At 100 cases per week, four dynamics dominate. **Reserved compliance capacity becomes the binding constraint**: C1 processes everything alone, and holding provisional slots for cases that may not reach assessment punctually converts useful foresight into phantom load; whether preparation helps C1 at all depends on the packet-quality distribution — poor packets turn a claimed 1–3h saving back into from-scratch reading. **Substitution reshuffles rather than removes document work**: shifting tricky-standard files to D2 frees D1 for the complex corporate segment where it is uniquely faster, but raises the clarification rate, feeding external-supplier loops back into the front of the process. **Deleted rework reappears elsewhere**: suppressing loops relieves D1, A1, and M1 mechanically, yet conditional approvals generate appeals and compliance-initiated information requests — the workload migrates to the least elastic stage and returns asynchronously, sometimes weeks later. **The assessment queue is the absorption layer**: parallelizing verification produces midday arrival bursts; if assessor slots are the limiting factor (as the Wed snapshot's 5h A2 backlog suggests), most of the local gain disappears as waiting, hurting followers in the queue rather than the targeted case. **Weekends amplify targeting**: a loop opened Friday inherits ~70h of dead external time, so suppressions timed Friday are the cheapest in the portfolio — policy should weight by initiation day, not average across cases. Finally, **timestamp regime decides what is knowable**: batch entry will systematically understate K-411-type concurrency and overstate cleanliness elsewhere, so logging discipline is a precondition, not a nicety, for evaluating any rollout. ## 7. Final synthesis

On balance, **K-104 appears most robust**. Its downside is capped near zero (an unused reservation simply dissolves), it upgrades decision quality by surfacing the sanctions ambiguity once instead of twice, and it respects every hard ordering rule including four-eyes. Its fragility is administrative, not logical: reservations must carry release clauses. **K-337 is the most dangerous despite offering the largest headline savings**, because it does not merely save time — it rewrites the decision function for a high-risk, high-value case with a known evidentiary gap, exposing the organization to appeal risk and to compliance legitimately reinstating the very loop that was suppressed. The intervention hides its cost outside the process window, which is precisely why process mining traces flatter it. **The least transportable local counterfactual is K-104's reservation mechanics**: exploiting anticipatory slack works when one case negotiates privately with one reviewer, but at 100 cases the single-server compliance queue converts reservations into displacement, so the case-level result inverts under scaling. A related caution applies to K-411, whose gains evaporate when the assessment queue, not verification, governs completion. The most valuable additional data are: (i) per-case arrival and service distributions rather than point queue forecasts, especially for C1; (ii) C1's interruption calendar — urgent insertions explain most timing noise; (iii) external-supplier response-time distributions conditioned on weekday (Friday loops cost a weekend); (iv) timestamp provenance distinguishing event-time from entry-time, without which genuine versus paper concurrency cannot be adjudicated; and (v) reason-coded rework triggers, which determine whether suppression policies eliminate waste or relocate it. *(~255 words)*

## 8. Assumption audit

1. Internal resources work weekday schedules permitting evening/overnight processing, but weekends are idle except external correspondence — this locates where cycle-time savings accrue. `temporal`
2. Pool-level queue forecasts carry ±20% uncertainty and act as shared congestion backgrounds; staff belong to exactly one pool, and C1 remains the sole certified reviewer throughout. `resource`
3. Case attribute flags (high risk, high value, originals-required) are correctly assigned at registration and remain stable for the case's life. `structural`
4. Non-target cases continue arriving and prioritizing as in the snapshot week, except for the explicitly modeled interactions (urgent C1 bookings, assessment-queue contention). `interference`
5. Role-holders behave per professional norms: managers sign promptly after compliance completes, and compliance refrains from manufacturing information requests absent genuine concern. `behavioral`
6. Document complexity classifications are accurate — handwriting genuinely degrades D2's reliability and corporate complexity favors D1 — and the implied error/clarification rates (e.g., 20–35% for K-219) are stable enough to project. `quality`
7. Service durations for successive activities within a case are roughly independent, aside from the documented specialist advantages; no systematic fatigue or backlog correlation. `temporal`
8. There are no Hawthorne or gaming effects: participants in pilots behave as in steady state, and logged timestamps correspond to actual work times. `interference`
