# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

**Time origin.** The clock starts at `Submit` for every case (elapsed-time SLA "measured from Submit"). All prefixes begin with `Submit`, so the origin is uniform. Time is measured in hours of elapsed (wall-clock) time, not active processing time.

**Horizon.** The SLA horizon is \(t^\* = 72\) h. We analyze the survival/incidence functions on \([0, t^\*]\) and just beyond, since "tail risk" concerns behavior around and after 72 h.

**Follow-up time per case.** `duration_or_age_h` is the follow-up time: for terminal rows it is time-to-event; for `open` rows it is the administrative-censoring age at the frozen cutoff. We do **not** have per-activity timestamps, only the total and the `last_event_gap_h`.

**Events of interest.** The operational success event is reaching `Resolve`. The other terminal states — `Withdraw`, `Reject`, `Transfer` — are *competing events* (they preclude an internal `Resolve`), not neutral censoring. `open` cases are genuinely right-censored (still under observation, no terminal state yet).

**A key definitional distinction.** "Probability of breaching the SLA" is ambiguous because "breach" can mean (a) *not resolved within 72 h regardless of what else happened*, (b) *still administratively pending (no disposition) at 72 h*, or (c) *the specific hazard of failing to resolve given competing exits*. These are different estimands with different estimators and different operational meanings. I define the origin and event **before** choosing an estimator.

I use these estimands throughout:

- **E1 — Non-resolution by 72 h (composite "breach").**
  \(E1(t) = 1 - P(\text{Resolve} \le t)\), i.e. the probability the case has *not* been resolved by time \(t\), treating Withdraw/Reject/Transfer as *events that make resolution impossible* (they contribute to non-resolution). Concretely, \(E1(72) = 1 - \mathrm{CIF}_{\text{Resolve}}(72)\) where the CIF is estimated with competing risks. This matches the plain-English reading "the case did not get resolved on time" and is the most decision-relevant single number for SLA compliance reporting.

- **E2 — Cumulative incidence of resolution, split around 72 h.**
  \(\mathrm{CIF}_{\text{Resolve}}(72)\) = probability of an *on-time* resolution, and \(\mathrm{CIF}_{\text{Resolve}}(\infty) - \mathrm{CIF}_{\text{Resolve}}(72)\) = probability of a *late* resolution (resolution after the deadline). This separates "resolved on time" from "resolved but breached" and is the estimand for measuring recoverable vs. lost work.

- **E3 — Probability of no terminal disposition at 72 h (all-cause pending).**
  \(P(T > 72)\) where \(T\) is time to *any* terminal state (Resolve, Withdraw, Reject, Transfer). This is the all-cause Kaplan–Meier survivor function of "still pending." It answers "how many cases are still sitting in the system, undecided, at the deadline?" — a workload/queue estimand.

- **E4 — Cause-specific hazards / competing-risk decomposition.**
  Separate cause-specific hazards \(\lambda_k(t)\) for \(k \in \{\text{Resolve, Withdraw, Reject, Transfer}\}\), and the corresponding Aalen–Johansen CIFs. This is the estimand for *understanding mechanism* (is late-resolution risk driven by escalation, by transfers leaving, etc.).

**Contribution of the four illustrative cases (S03 resolved @76, S09 open @92, S06 withdrawn @27, S08 transferred @68):**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Estimand → decision mapping.**
- SLA compliance reporting / contractual breach rate → **E1** (composite non-resolution by 72), because a breach is "not resolved on time" irrespective of reason, but E1 must be reported alongside E4 so a high breach rate driven by legitimate rejections is not confused with operational failure.
- Recoverability / backlog triage ("how many can we still save late?") → **E2** (late-resolution incidence).
- Staffing and queue management ("undecided inventory at the deadline") → **E3** (all-cause pending).
- Root-cause and policy analysis → **E4** (cause-specific hazards + CIFs).

# 2. Event and censoring encoding

Let the *primary* event be **Resolve**. Competing events remove a case from the Resolve risk set. `open` = administrative right censoring. The table below gives the encoding; the last columns give sensitivity alternatives, with Transfer treated under (at least) three assumptions.

| observed_status | Example cases | Resolve event indicator \(\delta_R\) | Competing-risk code \(k\) | Censoring rule (all-cause KM, E3) | Sensitivity alternatives |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| resolved | S01,S02,S03,S13,... | 1 at `duration_or_age_h` | \(k=\)Resolve | event at t | none needed; but split on/late at 72 for E2 |
| rejected | S05,S14,S23 | 0 | \(k=\)Reject (competing) | terminal event at t (leaves pending) | (a) competing event (primary); (b) treat as censoring if rejection is "administrative noise" you want to condition away; (c) include as non-resolution "breach" under E1 |
| withdrawn | S06,S12,S22,S30 | 0 | \(k=\)Withdraw (competing) | terminal event at t | (a) competing event (primary); (b) censoring if withdrawals are exogenous customer choices unrelated to service delay; (c) non-resolution under E1 |
| open | S09,S10,S11,S15,S24,S25,S26,S29,S31 | 0 | censored | censored at age t | primary: administrative censoring; sensitivity: for cases with huge `last_event_gap_h` (S11 gap 55, S26 gap 72, S25 gap 48) consider *informative*-censoring bounds (see below) |
| transferred | S08,S21,S32 | 0 | **see three assumptions** | **see three assumptions** | — |

**Transfer under (at least) three plausible assumptions.**

- **T1 — Transfer as right censoring (independent/non-informative).** At the transfer time the internal system stops observing; treat as a censoring point identical to administrative censoring. *Interpretation:* the transferred case's eventual resolution is treated as *missing at random* w.r.t. its latent resolution time. This is the most common default but is likely **wrong** here: transfers are correlated with escalation and P1 (S08, S21, S32 are all `escalated` and all P1), so the censoring is plausibly *informative* — transferred cases are systematically harder/longer.

- **T2 — Transfer as a competing terminal event (never internally resolved).** The internal outcome is definitively "not Resolve." This *lowers* \(\mathrm{CIF}_{\text{Resolve}}\) and *raises* E1 (composite non-resolution). Appropriate if the SLA is about *internal* resolution and a transfer is, by definition, an internal SLA breach/handoff.

- **T3 — Transfer as a partially-observed outcome with imputed resolution (bounding).** Because the eventual external resolution is unknown, run *bounding* analyses: optimistic (all transfers eventually resolve, and count them resolved at transfer time or at an assumed external SLA), pessimistic (no transfer ever resolves), and an imputed/tilted version using an assumed external resolution hazard. This produces an interval on E1/E2 rather than a point estimate and directly confronts the informative-censoring concern.

A fourth, defensible option: **T4 — cure/mixture**, where a fraction \(\pi\) of transfers are "cured" (resolved externally) and \(1-\pi\) not; sweep \(\pi \in [0,1]\) to recover the T2/T3 endpoints. I fold this into the sensitivity analysis (§6).

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

A "naive" analysis that keeps only `resolved` cases and computes, say, mean duration or the fraction of *resolved* cases finishing under 72 h is biased in several compounding ways.

- **Survivor / selection bias (conditioning on the outcome).** Restricting to resolved cases conditions on the very event we are estimating. Fast resolvers are over-represented and slow-but-eventual resolvers are under-counted if the freeze happens before they finish. E.g. keeping S01(18), S02(41), S04(29) and dropping the still-pending S09(92), S11(81), S26(104) makes the process look far faster than it is. S13 resolved at 110 h and S03 at 76 h show real long tails that a "% of resolved under 72" metric silently hides (they *are* breaches).

- **Ignoring right censoring understates tail risk.** Open cases S09(92), S11(81), S25(79), S26(104) have already *passed* 72 h with no Resolve — these are *observed* non-resolutions at 72, i.e. breaches that are certain regardless of censoring. Dropping them removes exactly the mass that defines tail risk. Meanwhile S10(47), S15(35), S24(67), S29(51), S31(23) are open but *below* 72 — their SLA fate is *not yet decided*; they must be carried as at-risk, not scored as either success or breach. Distinguishing "active open below 72" (undetermined) from "active open above 72" (already breached, fate-of-resolution still censored) is essential.

- **Competing risks mislabeled as neutral.** Withdrawals (S06@27, S12@15, S22@44, S30@70) and rejections (S05@9, S14@63, S23@11) are not "cases that would have resolved if we'd waited." They *removed themselves* from the Resolve risk set. A KM that censors them treats them as if they *could* still resolve, biasing \(\mathrm{CIF}_{\text{Resolve}}\) upward. They should be competing events (or explicitly justified as censoring), never silent drops.

- **Informative censoring via transfers and inactivity gaps.** Transfers (S08@68, S21@26, S32@96) are all P1/escalated — the *hardest* cases leave observation exactly when they are getting hard. Treating them as neutral censoring (T1) biases estimates *optimistically*. Similarly, open cases with very long `last_event_gap_h` — S11(gap 55), S25(gap 48), S26(gap 72) — may be silently stalled/abandoned; their censoring is plausibly informative of *longer* latent times.

- **Cohort imbalance / confounding.** Cohort B follows a policy change (auto-screening + escalation) but assignment was **not randomized** and rollout compliance was incomplete. B's resolved cases (S16@16, S18@12, S27@39) look fast, but B also has long opens (S25@79, S26@104) and transfers (S21, S32). Comparing raw completed-case speeds A vs B conflates the policy effect with (i) censoring differences, (ii) case-mix/priority differences, and (iii) partial compliance. Any A-vs-B claim from completed cases alone is confounded.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

I match each method to the estimands of §1 and then recommend, rather than list.

- **Kaplan–Meier (KM).** Correct for **E3** (all-cause "still pending" survivor \(P(T>t)\)) and for a *single-cause* survival only if there are no competing risks. Using KM for "time to Resolve" while censoring Withdraw/Reject/Transfer **overstates** resolution probability because it assumes the censored cases could still resolve. So KM is *right* for E3, *wrong* as the primary Resolve estimator here.

- **Aalen–Johansen / cumulative incidence (CIF).** The correct nonparametric estimator for **E1 and E2** under competing risks. It gives \(\mathrm{CIF}_{\text{Resolve}}(72)\), \(\mathrm{CIF}_{\text{Withdraw}}\), etc., that sum (with survival) to 1. This is the workhorse for the composite-breach and on-time/late decomposition.

- **Cause-specific Cox hazards.** Model \(\lambda_k(t\mid x)\) for each cause separately — the right tool for **E4** and for understanding *mechanism* / etiologic effects of covariates (e.g., does escalation raise the Resolve hazard or the Transfer hazard?). Covariates must be baseline or handled time-varying (see §5).

- **Fine–Gray subdistribution hazards.** Directly models the covariate effect on the CIF (the *probability* of an on-time resolution), which is what a manager cares about ("does cohort B raise the chance of on-time resolution?"). Use it as the *predictive/decision* complement to cause-specific Cox; interpret carefully (subdistribution risk sets keep competing-event cases "at risk," which is a modeling convention, not reality).

- **Restricted Mean Time (RMST / RMTL).** With horizon \(\tau=72\), report restricted mean time-to-resolution or restricted mean *time lost* to breach. Robust when hazards are non-proportional (likely here given rework/escalation), avoids reliance on a single \(t^\*\), and is easy to communicate ("expected on-time hours gained under B").

- **Landmarking.** To use post-baseline process events (Escalate, Rework, Review-repeat) without immortal-time bias: pick landmarks (e.g. 24 h, 48 h), keep only cases still at risk (pending) at the landmark, and use covariate *status as of the landmark*. This yields dynamic predictions ("given still pending and escalated at 48 h, P(breach)").

- **Multi-state model (Markov/semi-Markov).** The most faithful representation: states = process stages, transitions include Resolve/Withdraw/Reject/Transfer as absorbing. Estimates transition intensities and stage-specific sojourns, and *naturally* handles time-varying stage membership, censoring, and competing exits. It is the structurally correct model but is data-hungry.

**Recommendation.**
- **Primary: Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence** stratified by cohort and (coarsened) priority, reporting E1 = \(1-\mathrm{CIF}_{\text{Resolve}}(72)\) and E2 (on-time vs late Resolve). Rationale: it directly answers the SLA breach question under the *actual* competing-risk structure, needs no timestamp data beyond the supplied durations, and does not require proportional hazards. With \(n=32\) it is the most defensible nonparametric choice.
- **Secondary: cause-specific hazards + landmarking**, plus **RMTL** for communication. Cause-specific hazards give mechanism (escalation/transfer vs resolution), landmarking legitimately incorporates the post-start process features from §5, and RMTL provides a proportional-hazards-free summary. A full multi-state model is proposed as the *aspirational* model once event timestamps (§9) are available; with only totals it is under-identified.

Given \(n=32\) with heavy censoring, everything is reported with wide uncertainty (log-log or bootstrap CIs), and A-vs-B contrasts are treated as *descriptive/associational*, not causal.

# 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards

From the `prefix` strings we can derive covariates. The critical discipline: classify each feature as **baseline** (known at/near Submit, safe as a fixed covariate), **time-varying** (must enter via time-dependent Cox / landmarking / multi-state), **post-treatment** (a consequence on the causal path — do not adjust for it if estimating a policy effect), or **leakage** (using future/outcome-adjacent info that would not be available at prediction time).

| Feature (from prefix) | Definition | Classification | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| priority (P1–P3) | given field | **baseline** | safe fixed covariate |
| cohort (A/B) | given field | **baseline** (assignment), but confounded | policy exposure; not randomized |
| auto-screen ordering (`AutoCheck` before/after `Screen`, presence of AutoCheck early) | position of AutoCheck in prefix | **baseline-ish** | B tends to `Submit>AutoCheck>...`; captures the policy operationally, but is itself post-Submit — treat as time-0 routing flag only if it always occurs at start |
| current stage / last activity | final element of observed prefix (for open cases) | **time-varying** | e.g. S09/S26 currently at `Review`; S11 at `Escalate`. Use as landmark covariate, not baseline |
| escalated (Escalate appears) | boolean | **time-varying / post-treatment** | **classic immortal-time trap**: Escalate happens *after* Submit. A case cannot be "escalated" at t=0. Using it as a baseline covariate makes escalated cases look artificially long-lived before escalation (they had to "survive" to be escalated). Enter via time-varying covariate or landmark |
| repeated Review (Review appears ≥2×) | count of `Review` | **time-varying** | S03, S07, S13 have Review loops; indicates back-and-forth. Time-varying |
| rework_count / Rework present | given field / `Rework` in prefix | **time-varying / post-treatment** | S07,S13,S27 reworked; rework occurs mid-flow |
| escalation timing | position of Escalate in prefix / hazard of escalation | **time-varying** | only meaningful with timestamps; here we only have ordering |
| path pattern / variant | full activity sequence | **mixed** | early portion is baseline-ish; later portion is post-treatment/leakage |
| inactivity gap (`last_event_gap_h`) | given field | **time-varying, near-outcome (leakage risk)** | for terminals it is 0 by construction (event just happened) → **using it as a predictor of the terminal outcome is leakage** because it is deterministically tied to whether a terminal event occurred. Only meaningful for *open* cases as a *current* stall signal; even then it evolves over time |
| prefix length (# events) | count | **time-varying** | grows with time; not baseline |

**Leakage / immortal-time summary.** `Escalate`, `Rework`, repeated `Review`, prefix length, and `last_event_gap_h` are all realized *after* the time origin. Treating any as a fixed baseline covariate induces immortal-time bias (the case is guaranteed to "survive" until the covariate-defining event) and/or outcome leakage (`last_event_gap_h=0` for all terminals perfectly separates terminal from open). The safe designs are **time-varying Cox**, **landmarking**, or a **multi-state model** where these are transitions/states rather than baseline features.

**Proposed state-transition diagram (Mermaid).**

```mermaid
stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> Submitted
    Submitted --> Screening: Screen / AutoCheck
    Screening --> UnderReview: Review
    Screening --> Escalated: Escalate
    Screening --> Reject: Reject (absorbing)
    Screening --> Withdraw: Withdraw (absorbing)
    UnderReview --> UnderReview: Review loop / Rework
    UnderReview --> Escalated: Escalate
    UnderReview --> Reject: Reject (absorbing)
    UnderReview --> Withdraw: Withdraw (absorbing)
    UnderReview --> Resolve: Resolve (absorbing, success)
    Escalated --> UnderReview: Review
    Escalated --> Resolve: Resolve (absorbing, success)

    Screening --> Resolve: Resolve (fast path)
    Resolve --> [*]
    Reject --> [*]
    Withdraw --> [*]
    Transfer --> [*]
```

`Resolve`, `Reject`, `Withdraw` are internal absorbing states. `Transfer` is absorbing *from the internal system's viewpoint* but externally the true fate is unobserved (the informative-censoring hinge).

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

I vary the four axes and state which conclusions can flip. All contrasts are associational given the non-randomized cohort.

**Axis 1 — Transfer treatment (T1 vs T2 vs T3/T4).**
- *T1 (censoring):* \(\mathrm{CIF}_{\text{Resolve}}\) is highest (most optimistic), E1 lowest. Transfers vanish from the risk set as if they might resolve.
- *T2 (competing event, never resolves):* \(\mathrm{CIF}_{\text{Resolve}}\) drops, E1 (composite breach) rises. Because transfers cluster in P1/escalated cases, this *widens* any apparent A/B gap only if transfer rates differ (B has S21,S32; A has S08 — comparable counts, so the *cohort* conclusion is fairly robust to this, but the *absolute* breach level is not).
- *T3/T4 (bounding, cure fraction \(\pi\)):* produces an interval on E1. **Reversible conclusion:** whether the overall on-time resolution probability is "acceptable" can flip between T1 and T2; the *ranking* of priorities (P1 worst) is more stable because P1 dominates both transfers and long opens under all treatments.

**Axis 2 — Withdrawal/Rejection as competing vs censoring.**
- As *competing events* (primary), they cap \(\mathrm{CIF}_{\text{Resolve}}\). As *censoring*, KM-style resolution probability rises. **Reversible:** the *level* of E1 shifts; but since withdrawals/rejections are spread across both cohorts and priorities, the *A-vs-B qualitative comparison* is relatively insensitive. A claim like "most breaches are legitimate rejections vs stalled cases" *can* reverse depending on this coding — hence E1 must always be reported next to the E4 cause decomposition.

**Axis 3 — Informative censoring for long-gap opens.**
- Baseline: treat all opens as non-informative administrative censoring.
- Stress test: for opens with large `last_event_gap_h` (S11=55, S25=48, S26=72), tilt their latent resolution times *longer* (pessimistic) or assume near-abandonment. **Reversible:** the estimated tail (E1 at and beyond 72) rises materially under the pessimistic tilt because these are exactly the >72 h opens; a conclusion of "tail risk is modest" can reverse to "tail risk is severe." The optimistic tilt barely changes results because these cases are already past 72.

**Axis 4 — Cohort/policy effect.**
- Naive (unadjusted) A vs B; vs priority-stratified; vs a stratified/adjusted cause-specific model; vs an *as-treated* (compliance-restricted) analysis. **Reversible:** an apparent "B resolves faster" signal (driven by fast B resolveds S16,S18,S27) can attenuate or reverse once you (i) stop dropping B's long opens (S25,S26) and transfers, (ii) stratify by priority, and (iii) account for incomplete rollout compliance (intention-to-treat vs as-treated). With \(n=32\) the cohort effect is likely *not* statistically distinguishable from noise under any coding — a key honest conclusion.

**Robust vs fragile conclusions.**
- *Robust:* P1 cases carry the most tail risk (they dominate long opens S11,S25 and all transfers); open cases already past 72 h (S09,S11,S25,S26) are certain non-resolutions-at-72 regardless of coding.
- *Fragile:* the absolute breach rate, and any claim that the policy (cohort B) improved SLA performance.

# 7. Operational interpretation

**From survival outputs to alerts.**
- Use a **landmark/dynamic prediction**: for each *still-pending* case, at its current age and stage, compute the model-implied probability of *not* resolving by 72 h (E1-type, conditional on current survival). Fire a **tiered alert** when the conditional breach probability crosses thresholds (e.g. amber at 0.5, red at 0.75) or when *time-remaining-to-72* is small relative to the stage's typical remaining sojourn. Cases like S10(47, escalated), S24(67), S29(51) — pending and approaching 72 — are the actionable amber set; S09/S11/S25/S26 are already past 72 and belong to a *breach-recovery* queue (E2 late-resolution), not a prevention queue.

**Workload actions.**
- Convert **E3 (pending inventory at 72)** into staffing: expected number still undecided at the deadline drives reviewer allocation.
- Use **cause-specific hazards** to route: high transfer hazard among escalated P1s suggests either earlier transfer decisions or added internal capacity for P1.
- Use **RMTL** (restricted mean time lost) as a single KPI per cohort/priority for capacity planning.

**SLA governance without causal overclaiming.**
- Report breach rate as **E1 with its competing-risk decomposition (E4)** so governance sees *why* (rejections vs stalls vs transfers), not just a headline number.
- Explicitly label all cohort/priority contrasts as **associational**; do not present the B-vs-A difference as "the policy caused X" given non-randomization and incomplete compliance. Provide both intention-to-treat and as-treated views.

**Calibration and fairness checks.**
- **Calibration:** on held-out or bootstrap data, bin predicted breach probabilities and compare to observed CIF-based breach frequencies (reliability curve); report calibration-in-the-large and slope. With \(n=32\), use temporal/landmark bootstrap and wide intervals; avoid over-tuned thresholds.
- **Fairness/consistency by priority and cohort:** check that predicted risk is calibrated *within* each priority and cohort (no systematic over/under-prediction for, e.g., P3 in cohort A). Monitor that alerting does not simply reallocate attention away from a subgroup that is being under-served. Report subgroup breach rates and alert precision/recall separately for P1/P2/P3 and A/B.
- **Guardrail:** because escalation/rework are on the causal path, never let the alert model's *features* create a feedback loop (e.g. down-prioritizing cases *because* they were escalated) that becomes self-fulfilling.

# ---------- Encoding: competing-risk cause + censoring flag (primary coding) ----------
# cause in {resolve, withdraw, reject, transfer, censored}
status_to_cause = {
    "resolved": "resolve", "withdrawn": "withdraw",
    "rejected": "reject", "transferred": "transfer", "open": "censored",
}
df["cause"] = df["observed_status"].map(status_to_cause)

def encode_scenario(df, transfer_mode):
    """Return (time, event_code) where event_code:
       'resolve','withdraw','reject','transfer','censored'.
       transfer_mode in {'censor','compete','optimistic_resolve'}."""
    out = df.copy()
    if transfer_mode == "censor":                 # T1: transfer = right censoring
        out.loc[out["cause"] == "transfer", "cause"] = "censored"
    elif transfer_mode == "compete":              # T2: transfer = competing event
        pass                                      # already 'transfer'
    elif transfer_mode == "optimistic_resolve":   # T3 upper-bound: transfers resolve
        out.loc[out["cause"] == "transfer", "cause"] = "resolve"
    else:
        raise ValueError(transfer_mode)
    return out

# ---------- Aalen-Johansen / cumulative incidence (manual, no library needed) ----------
def cumulative_incidence(sub, cause_of_interest):
    """Nonparametric CIF for one cause with competing risks, using KM overall
       survival * cause-specific hazard increments. 'censored' rows censor.
       Returns dict t->CIF and the CIF value at/just after HORIZON."""
    d = sub.sort_values("time")
    times = sorted(d["time"].unique())
    n_at_risk = len(d)
    surv_prev = 1.0          # S(t-) all-cause survival (any terminal event)
    cif = 0.0
    cif_at_h = 0.0
    curve = {}
    for t in times:
        at_t = d[d["time"] == t]
        d_any = (at_t["cause"] != "censored").sum()          # any terminal event
        d_cause = (at_t["cause"] == cause_of_interest).sum()  # events of interest
        if n_at_risk > 0 and d_any > 0:
            cif += surv_prev * (d_cause / n_at_risk)          # AJ increment
        # update overall survival for all-cause terminal events
        if n_at_risk > 0:
            surv_prev *= (1 - d_any / n_at_risk)
        curve[t] = cif
        if t <= HORIZON:
            cif_at_h = cif
        n_at_risk -= len(at_t)                                 # remove events+censored
    return curve, cif_at_h

# ---------- All-cause KM survivor (E3: still pending) ----------
def km_pending(sub):
    d = sub.sort_values("time")
    n = len(d); S = 1.0; S_at_h = 1.0
    for t in sorted(d["time"].unique()):
        at_t = d[d["time"] == t]
        d_any = (at_t["cause"] != "censored").sum()
        if n > 0 and d_any > 0:
            S *= (1 - d_any / n)
        if t <= HORIZON:
            S_at_h = S
        n -= len(at_t)
    return S_at_h  # P(no terminal disposition by 72) approx (open = censored)

# ---------- Estimands across 3 transfer scenarios, overall + stratified ----------
scenarios = ["censor", "compete", "optimistic_resolve"]
print("=== E1/E2: cumulative incidence of RESOLVE and composite non-resolution ===")
for sc in scenarios:
    enc = encode_scenario(df, sc)
    _, cif_res = cumulative_incidence(enc, "resolve")
    e1 = 1 - cif_res                          # E1: prob not resolved by 72 (composite)
    print(f"[transfer={sc:18s}] CIF_resolve(72)={cif_res:0.3f}  "
          f"E1 non-resolution(72)={e1:0.3f}")

print("\n=== E3: all-cause pending survivor at 72 (open cases NOT dropped) ===")
print(f"P(pending at 72) [primary coding] = {km_pending(df):0.3f}")

print("\n=== Cohort x (coarse) priority stratified, transfer=compete (primary) ===")
enc = encode_scenario(df, "compete")
enc["prio_grp"] = enc["priority"].map({"P1": "P1", "P2": "P2P3", "P3": "P2P3"})
for (coh, pg), sub in enc.groupby(["cohort", "prio_grp"]):
    if len(sub) == 0:
        continue
    _, cif_res = cumulative_incidence(sub, "resolve")
    print(f"cohort={coh} prio={pg:5s} n={len(sub):2d}  "
          f"CIF_resolve(72)={cif_res:0.3f}  E1={1-cif_res:0.3f}")

print("\n=== Sensitivity: cohort A vs B, E1 under transfer=censor vs compete ===")
for sc in ["censor", "compete"]:
    enc = encode_scenario(df, sc)
    row = {}
    for coh, sub in enc.groupby("cohort"):
        _, cif_res = cumulative_incidence(sub, "resolve")
        row[coh] = 1 - cif_res
    print(f"transfer={sc:8s}  E1(A)={row['A']:0.3f}  E1(B)={row['B']:0.3f}  "
          f"diff(B-A)={row['B']-row['A']:+0.3f}")

# ---------- Cause-specific CIF decomposition (E4), primary coding ----------
print("\n=== E4: cause-specific CIF at 72 (transfer=compete) ===")
enc = encode_scenario(df, "compete")

# We only have activity ORDER (no timestamps); we synthesize an ordering index as
# event position, NOT wall-clock time, to avoid fabricating durations.
def build_pm4py_variant_feature(df):
    try:
        import pm4py
    except Exception as e:
        print("\n[pm4py unavailable:", e, "] Falling back to pandas variant counts.")
        variants = df["prefix"].value_counts()
        print("Top variants (pandas fallback):")
        print(variants.head(10).to_string())
        # control-flow feature: has an Escalate step (post-start -> NOT baseline)
        df["has_escalate"] = df["prefix"].str.contains("Escalate")
        return

    # Build an event-log DataFrame; timestamp = integer position (order only).
    rows = []
    base = pd.Timestamp("2000-01-01")
    for _, r in df.iterrows():
        acts = r["prefix"].split(">")
        for i, a in enumerate(acts):
            rows.append({
                "case:concept:name": r["case"],
                "concept:name": a,
                # position-based pseudo-time (order preserved; not real durations)
                "time:timestamp": base + pd.Timedelta(hours=i),
            })
    log_df = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    log_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        log_df, case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp")
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(log_df)
    print("\n=== PM4Py control-flow feature: variant counts ===")
    for v, occ in sorted(variants.items(), key=lambda kv: -(len(kv[1]) if isinstance(kv[1], list) else kv[1])):
        cnt = len(occ) if isinstance(occ, list) else occ
        print(f"  {cnt:2d}  {v}")

build_pm4py_variant_feature(df)

# 9. Limitations and additional data

- **Sample size.** \(n=32\) with heavy censoring and 3–4 P1 events per stratum makes every estimate imprecise. All conclusions are provisional; report bootstrap CIs and resist thresholds tuned to this sample.
- **Priority for stronger inference (ranked):**
  1. **Exact per-event timestamps.** The single highest-value addition: enables real stage sojourns, time-varying covariates, correct immortal-time handling, escalation-*timing* hazards, and a genuine multi-state model instead of position-only ordering.
  2. **External outcomes after Transfer.** Resolves the T1/T2/T3 ambiguity that currently dominates the sensitivity range; converts bounding intervals into point estimates for E1/E2.
  3. **Cutoff and start dates (calendar).** Needed to characterize administrative censoring precisely, check for calendar-time confounding with the policy rollout, and detect whether long `last_event_gap_h` opens are stalled vs. simply recent.
  4. **Assignment mechanism for cohorts + rollout-compliance records.** Required to move from associational A-vs-B to any (quasi-)causal claim (e.g. instrument or as-treated/ITT contrast); without it the policy effect is not identifiable.
  5. **Time-varying workload / queue state.** Explains informative censoring and non-proportional hazards (busy periods lengthen tails) and improves alert calibration.
  6. **Resource/handler and reason codes** for Withdraw/Reject/Transfer, to justify whether each is truly a competing event or (for some withdrawals) exogenous censoring.
- **Structural caveats that data cannot fully fix:** transfers create *fundamentally* partially observed outcomes (external system boundary), so E1/E2 will always carry an irreducible assumption about external resolution unless outcome-sharing is established; and `last_event_gap_h` is outcome-adjacent (0 for all terminals) and must never be used as a baseline predictor.
