## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation
The gap between raw and mapped fitness reveals that roughly 40% of the conformance loss stems from label renaming and technical event duplication rather than genuine process deviation. Mapping `Eligibility Screen` back to `Validate Eligibility` and deduplicating `Open Case` restores structural alignment with the normative model, but it does not imply the underlying business process remained unchanged. A recovered fitness score only confirms that the event log can be reconciled with the old schema; it cannot distinguish between a stable process with improved logging and a genuinely altered process that happens to map back to the original control flow. Moreover, the mapped fitness still declines from 0.92 to ~0.86, indicating residual control-flow drift that label normalization cannot explain.

Duplicated technical events inflate activity counts and distort direct-frequent-graph (DFG) edge weights, while missing resource identifiers break workload attribution and queueing analysis. These artifacts primarily compromise resource-centric and performance-oriented mining, but they do not fully account for the post-May structural shifts. Therefore, while the March fitness collapse is predominantly a semantic/provenance issue, the subsequent deterioration reflects genuine behavioral adaptation and performance degradation driven by operational constraints.

## 9. Provisional attribution
The March fitness collapse is overwhelmingly a logging/semantic artifact (confidence: 90%). The sudden appearance of `Eligibility Screen`, duplicated `Open Case` events, and missing resource IDs align precisely with the system migration, and the mapped fitness recovery confirms that label normalization restores ~40% of conformance loss. However, the mapped fitness still declines to ~0.86, indicating genuine process drift that cannot be explained by schema changes alone.

From May to July, the dominant mechanisms are supplier-driven rework and staffing-induced queueing (confidence: 80%). The sharp rise in `Assess -> Request Info` transitions, the doubling of rework variants, and the disproportionate p90 cycle-time expansion align with Supplier Z’s incomplete bundles and the departure of four assessors. The priority rule activated in July likely redistributed delays, protecting high-complexity cases while starving standard ones, which explains the aggregate cycle-time peak without a corresponding service-time increase. This interaction (supplier disruption × staffing cut × priority rule) is highly plausible but difficult to isolate without case-level priority flags.

The October–November deterioration is primarily seasonal population drift (confidence: 85%), driven by the annual renewal cycle’s volume and complexity surge. The spike in senior assessment routing and the temporary p90 peak are consistent with historical patterns, though they amplify existing queueing constraints. The December improvements reflect a combination of load normalization, overtime capacity, and the document-quality campaign (confidence: 75%), though partial recovery suggests underlying behavioral adaptation or residual backlog.

Mediation prevents clean attribution: supplier disruption increased rework, which compounded staffing shortages, while the priority rule masked standard-case delays. I would revise this account if event-level trace data revealed that rework loops were primarily driven by internal assessor behavior rather than supplier bundles, or if resource allocation logs showed that missing IDs were systematically biased toward specific teams, distorting queueing estimates.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw model fitness drops >15% while mapped fitness remains stable, THEN investigate label schema or ETL pipeline, BECAUSE sudden raw/mapped divergence indicates logging or semantic drift rather than process change.
2. IF `Assess -> Request Info` rate exceeds 22 per 100 cases for two consecutive months, THEN audit supplier document completeness and assessor feedback loops, BECAUSE sustained rework elevation signals supplier disruption or behavioral adaptation.
3. IF p90 cycle time exceeds 2.8× median cycle time, THEN examine queue waiting distributions and WIP levels, BECAUSE disproportionate tail expansion indicates queueing bottlenecks or priority-induced starvation.
4. IF high-complexity share rises >10% above seasonal baseline, THEN verify renewal calendar alignment and check senior assessment routing, BECAUSE case-mix shifts drive service time and control-flow changes that mimic performance drift.
5. IF missing resource IDs exceed 5% in migrated activities, THEN trigger data engineering validation and impute resources for workload analysis, BECAUSE resource missingness breaks queueing attribution and inflates perceived delays.
6. IF mapped model fitness declines below 0.85 for three months, THEN run alignment analysis and update the normative model, BECAUSE persistent conformance loss indicates genuine behavioral drift or process redesign.
7. IF fast variant share drops >8% while rework share rises >5%, THEN segment by supplier and complexity class to isolate drivers, BECAUSE variant shifts often reflect compounding effects of supplier quality, staffing capacity, and priority rules.
