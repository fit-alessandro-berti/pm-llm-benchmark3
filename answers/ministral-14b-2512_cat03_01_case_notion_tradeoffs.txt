# **Analysis of OCEL Flattening Strategies for Order-Fulfillment Process**

---

## **1. Analytical Assumptions**

### **Case Notion**
A *case notion* here refers to the **temporal and logical grouping of events** around a central object type, where:
- **Convergence** occurs when multiple objects (e.g., orders, packages) are artificially merged into a single trace, obscuring their distinct lifecycles (e.g., `D2` consolidating `P2` and `P3` from `O1`, `O2`, `O3`).
- **Divergence** occurs when a single object’s lifecycle is split across multiple traces (e.g., `O3` appearing in `P3`, `P4`, `D2`, `D3`, `V2`, `M2`, `M3`), leading to fragmented process views.

### **Copied Shared Events**
- **Evidence**: Shared events (e.g., `e16` for `D2` involving `O1`, `O2`, `O3`) are **not inherently duplication** but reflect **business consolidation** (e.g., `D2` carrying `P2` and `P3` from multiple orders).
- **Duplication**: When flattening on an object type, shared events are **replicated** (e.g., `e16` appears in traces for `O1`, `O2`, `O3`), which can inflate frequency counts and distort DFG edges.
- **Treatment**: We treat them as **both evidence and duplication**, requiring explicit handling (e.g., filtering or weighting).

### **Permitted Analytical Transformations**
- **Relation Propagation**: We **do not** silently propagate `O2O` relations (e.g., `O1  I11  P1  D1` is not inferred unless explicitly modeled).
- **Explicit Propagation**: If used, it must be **documented as a transformation** (e.g., "flattening on `Package` with propagated `Order` relations").

## **3. Concrete Distortion Analysis**

### **(1) Cross-Order Package Distortion (Flattening on `Package`)**
- **Affected Flattening**: `Package`
- **Events/Objects**: `P3` (`e13`, `e16`, `e24`), `P4` (`e14`, `e17`, `e27`)
- **Distortion**:
  - `P3`’s trace includes `O2` and `O3` events (`e13`: `O2`, `O3`, `I21`, `I31`), but the DFG will show a **merged flow** where `O2` and `O3`’s packing steps appear indistinguishable.
  - **Harmful**: Loses the ability to distinguish between `O2` and `O3`’s packing logic.
- **Context-Dependent?**: Useful for **logistics optimization**, but misleading for **order-specific analysis**.

### **(2) Consolidated Delivery Distortion (Flattening on `Delivery`)**
- **Affected Flattening**: `Delivery`
- **Events/Objects**: `D2` (`e16`, `e24`), involving `O1`, `O2`, `O3`
- **Distortion**:
  - `D2`’s trace includes `e16` (dispatch) and `e24` (confirm), but the DFG will show a **single path** for all three orders, hiding that:
    - `O1`’s items (`I12`) were dispatched separately (`e16` vs. `e15` for `D1`).
    - `O2`/`O3`’s items (`I21`, `I31`) were consolidated.
  - **Harmful**: Makes it appear as if all orders follow the same delivery path.
- **Context-Dependent?**: Useful for **fleet management**, but **misleading for order-level SLA analysis**.

### **(3) Consolidated Invoicing Distortion (Flattening on `Invoice`)**
- **Affected Flattening**: `Invoice`
- **Events/Objects**: `V2` (`e19`, `e22`, `e25`), covering `O2`/`O3`
- **Distortion**:
  - `V2`’s trace shows `e22` (`M2`: partial payment) and `e25` (`M3`: completion), but the DFG cannot distinguish:
    - Whether `M2` was applied to `O2` or `O3` first.
    - If `O2` and `O3` had different payment terms.
  - **Harmful**: Obscures **order-level revenue recognition**.

### **(4) Partial Payment Distortion (Flattening on `Payment`)**
- **Affected Flattening**: `Payment`
- **Events/Objects**: `M2` (`e22`), `M3` (`e25`) for `V2`
- **Distortion**:
  - `M2` and `M3` appear as separate events, but the DFG cannot show:
    - That `M2` was a **partial settlement** (only `V2`’s first half).
    - That `M3` completed it, but the **order-level impact** is lost.
  - **Informative**: Highlights **payment fragmentation**, but **context-dependent** for financial reporting.

### **(5) Near-Singleton Lifecycle Distortion (Flattening on `Item`)**
- **Affected Flattening**: `Item`
- **Events/Objects**: `I41` (`e10`, `e14`, `e17`, `e27`)
- **Distortion**:
  - `I41`’s lifecycle is **short** (only 4 events), but flattening on `Item` makes it appear as a **complete process** when it’s just a **sub-process** of `O4`.
  - **Harmful**: Overstates item-level autonomy; **misleading for inventory planning**.

### **(6) Shared Event Duplication (Flattening on `Order`)**
- **Affected Flattening**: `Order`
- **Events/Objects**: `e16` (dispatch) appears in `O1`, `O2`, `O3`
- **Distortion**:
  - `e16` is **replicated** in three traces, inflating frequency counts.
  - **DFG Edge**: `Dispatch  Confirm` appears **three times**, but represents **one physical delivery**.
  - **Harmful**: Overestimates dispatch frequency; **context-dependent** for capacity planning.

### **(7) Lifecycle Truncation (Flattening on `Delivery`)**
- **Affected Flattening**: `Delivery`
- **Events/Objects**: `D1` (`e15`, `e23`)
- **Distortion**:
  - `D1`’s lifecycle is **complete** (`Dispatch`  `Confirm`), but flattening on `Delivery` **hides** that it’s tied to `O1`’s closure (`e28`).
  - **Harmful**: Makes delivery appear **decoupled from order completion**.

### **(8) Cross-Object Relation Loss (Flattening on `Payment`)**
- **Affected Flattening**: `Payment`
- **Events/Objects**: `M1` (`e21`) for `O1`, `M4` (`e26`) for `O4`
- **Distortion**:
  - `M1` and `M4` appear as **isolated payments**, but their relation to `Invoice`/`Order` is lost.
  - **Harmful**: Cannot answer: *"Which orders were paid late?"* without cross-referencing.

## **4. Traditional vs. Object-Centric Analysis Portfolio**

### **Recommended Flattened Logs**
| **Flattening** | **Safe Questions**                                                                 | **Unsafe Questions**                                                                 | **Required OC-DFG/OTG**                                                                 |
|----------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Order**      | Order lifecycle duration, item addition patterns, payment timing per order.       | Cross-order consolidations (e.g., `P3`, `D2`).                                       | **OC-DFG**: To show order-level interactions with packages/deliveries.                  |
| **Item**       | Item-level lead time, packing delays.                                               | Order-level aggregation (e.g., total order value).                                   | **OTG**: To see item-package-delivery relations.                                      |
| **Package**    | Package assembly time, delivery consolidation efficiency.                           | Order-specific packing logic.                                                        | **ET-OT Graph**: To map packages to their constituent orders.                          |
| **Delivery**   | Delivery confirmation rates, fleet utilization.                                    | Order-level SLAs.                                                                     | **Object Interaction Summary**: To see which orders are in each delivery.               |
| **Invoice**    | Invoice issuance delays, payment timing.                                            | Order-level revenue attribution.                                                     | **OC-DFG**: To link invoices to partial payments.                                      |
| **Payment**    | Payment method distribution, partial vs. full settlements.                          | Order-level payment impact.                                                          | **ET-OT Graph**: To map payments to invoices/orders.                                   |

### **Reconciliation Strategy**
- **Contradictions**: If flattened logs show conflicting frequencies (e.g., `Order` flattening reports 4 orders, but `Delivery` flattening shows 3 deliveries), resolve via:
  - **OC-DFG**: Show which orders share deliveries.
  - **OTG**: Visualize object interactions (e.g., `D2`  `O1`/`O2`/`O3`).
- **Example**: If `Order` flattening shows `O1` closes at `e28` but `Delivery` flattening shows `D1` confirms at `e23`, the **OC-DFG** reveals that `D1`’s confirmation is **not directly linked** to `O1`’s closure in the flattened view.

## **5. Lead-Object Decision**

### **Decision: B. Use a Primary Lead Object (`Order`) Plus Explicit Secondary Lifecycles**
#### **Decision Criteria**
1. **Business Relevance**: Orders are the **primary business case** (customers care about order fulfillment, not package logistics).
2. **Minimal Distortion**: Flattening on `Order` preserves **most of the lifecycle** without severe convergence issues (unlike `Delivery` or `Package`).
3. **Secondary Lifecycles**: Use **OC-DFG/OTG** to analyze:
   - `Package` for logistics.
   - `Invoice` for billing.
   - `Payment` for finance.

#### **Strongest Supporting Evidence**
- **Order-centric questions** (e.g., "Why was `O3` delayed?") are **best answered** by `Order` flattening.
- **Cross-object relations** (e.g., `P3` spanning `O2`/`O3`) are **explicitly modeled** in the OC-DFG, not hidden.

#### **Strongest Counterargument**
- **Convergence risk**: `Order` flattening still **duplicates shared events** (e.g., `e16` for `O1`/`O2`/`O3`), but this is **manageable** via filtering.
- **Divergence risk**: Some order lifecycles are **split** (e.g., `O3` appears in `P3`, `P4`, `D2`, `D3`), but the **OC-DFG** can reconstruct this.

#### **Residual Risk**
- **Overlap in shared events**: If `e16` is counted multiple times, **frequency metrics are inflated**.
  - **Mitigation**: Use **unique event IDs** or **weighted counts**.

#### **Conditions for Change**
- If the **primary question is logistics** (e.g., "How do we optimize package consolidation?"), switch to `Package` as lead + `Order` as secondary.
- If the **primary question is financial** (e.g., "How do partial payments affect cash flow?"), use `Invoice`/`Payment` as lead.

## **7. Sensitivity and Validation Plan**

### **(1) Removing/Downgrading Copied Shared Events**
- **Action**: Filter events with multiple related objects (e.g., `e16` with `O1`/`O2`/`O3`).
- **Impact**:
  - Reduces **event duplication** in flattened logs.
  - May **break convergence** (e.g., `D2`’s consolidation is no longer visible).
- **Validation**: Compare DFG edges before/after filtering.

### **(2) Comparing Direct E2O vs. Propagated Relations**
- **Action**: Run flattening with and without propagating `O2O` relations (e.g., `I11  P1  D1`).
- **Impact**:
  - **Direct E2O**: Preserves strict event-object links.
  - **Propagated**: May reveal **hidden dependencies** but risks **overfitting**.
- **Validation**: Check if propagated relations **artificially inflate DFG edges**.

### **(3) Changing the Lead Object**
- **Action**: Flatten on `Package` instead of `Order`.
- **Impact**:
  - **Logistics focus**: Better for delivery optimization.
  - **Order focus**: Loses order-level context.
- **Validation**: Compare **order completion times** vs. **package dispatch times**.

### **(4) Frequency Metrics Comparison**
- **Action**: Compare:
  - **Event-based frequency** (e.g., 10 `Dispatch` events).
  - **Unique object-based frequency** (e.g., 3 `Delivery` objects).
  - **Total object participation** (e.g., `D2` involves 3 orders).
- **Impact**:
  - Event-based counts **overestimate** due to duplication.
  - Object-based counts **underestimate** convergence.
- **Validation**: Use **OC-DFG** to reconcile discrepancies.

---
**Final Note**: This analysis demonstrates that **no single flattening is "correct"**—each reveals different truths. The **hybrid approach (lead object + OC-DFG/OTG)** is most defensible for end-to-end analysis.
