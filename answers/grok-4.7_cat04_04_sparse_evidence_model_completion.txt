# 1. Evidence Ledger

Both directions in the DFG do not prove concurrency. Credit Check immediately precedes Engineering Review in 17 cases, and Engineering Review immediately precedes Credit Check in 11. That pair of arcs says both adjacent completion orders occur. The same pair is produced by an exclusive choice between two sequences, by the linear extensions of one partial order, and by truly overlapping work whose log records only completions. S7 adds a fourth route: if the two completions share a timestamp, ingestion index alone chooses the arc. A directly-follows graph sums adjacent pairs and drops the rest of the case, non-adjacent order, and time. It cannot separate those mechanisms. D7 is exactly this limit. In the models below, an absent edge between the two checks means both completion orders are admitted. It does not mean the activities overlap in time.

# 2. Shared Assumptions

All three candidates are untimed, unweighted languages over completion labels. An event is the completion of an activity. No model contains start events, durations, resources, or the missing sourcing attribute. Equal frequencies are not assumed: V1 and V6 are both conforming under a language that contains them, even though their counts are 12 and 1. None of the models is a stochastic DFG.

The shared skeleton follows D1, D2, and the 30/30 suffix. Every accepted trace starts with Register Order and ends with Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case, with nothing between those three. That immediate succession is stronger than the literal wording of D2. It is shared because no observed directly-follows arc leaves that chain. A future Notify-style activity inserted before Archive Case would be rejected by every candidate. That is a shared underfitting risk.

Credit Check and Engineering Review each occur exactly once and complete before Approve Configuration (D3). There is no loop. A second Credit Check would be rejected by every candidate. S1 is not encoded. The 11 Engineering-Review-first completions are treated as data, not as defects.

Supplier Confirmation and Expedite Review occur at most once. Optionality, where a model uses it, is an exclusive choice between the activity and a silent transition. That is a control-flow abstraction. It merges at least three stories: a stocked component that legitimately skips Supplier Confirmation (S4), an external component whose confirmation event was dropped (S5), and an external component that should have been confirmed and was not (S3). The models do not repair traces, and a conforming skip cannot be read as a procurement violation until those stories are separable.

The absence counts are compatible with a mixture, and they do not identify the mixture. Let \(s\) be the share of cases that truly need no Supplier Confirmation, and let \(m\) be the probability that a required Supplier Confirmation is dropped, assumed independent of sourcing. The observed absence rate is \(7/30 \approx 0.233\), and \(s + (1-s)m \approx 0.233\). At the stated band, \(m = 0.05\) implies \(s \approx 0.19\), and \(m = 0.10\) implies \(s \approx 0.15\). A pure-loss account (\(s = 0\)) needs \(m \approx 0.23\), about twice the stated upper guess. A pure-skip account is the other boundary, \(m = 0\), \(s \approx 0.23\). Conditional missingness would change the algebra. No candidate estimates \(s\) or \(m\).

The conservative language is exactly the seven observed strings. The balanced language is a 12-trace superset. The permissive language is a 14-trace superset of the balanced language. Nesting is deliberate: each step adds one named hypothesis.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

### Inductive bias

The conservative hypothesis is a closed variant catalogue. If a completion string occurred, it is legal; if it did not occur, it is illegal, except for the shared skeleton that all observed strings already obey. High-confidence rules are satisfied because every listed string satisfies them. They are not used to add strings. Rare variants are kept: V6 and V7 were observed, so frequency is not a deletion rule. The bias favors precision on this sample and refuses every recombination of local orders. It is the right hypothesis when the operating question is “has this exact completion string been seen?”

### Formal expression

```text
sequence(
  Register Order,
  xor(
    sequence(Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation),          # V1
    sequence(Engineering Review, Credit Check, Supplier Confirmation),          # V2
    sequence(Credit Check, Engineering Review),                                  # V3
    sequence(Engineering Review, Credit Check),                                  # V4
    sequence(Credit Check, Supplier Confirmation, Engineering Review),           # V5
    sequence(Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Expedite Review),  # V6
    sequence(Engineering Review, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Supplier Confirmation)   # V7
  ),
  Approve Configuration,
  Release Production,
  Archive Case
)
```

The common skeleton is factored. The language is identical to an exclusive choice of the seven full traces. Factoring does not add or remove behavior.

### Code

```python
import pm4py

_MIDDLES = [
    "->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation' )",
    "->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', 'Supplier Confirmation' )",
    "->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review' )",
    "->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check' )",
    "->( 'Credit Check', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Engineering Review' )",
    "->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Expedite Review' )",
    "->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', 'Expedite Review', 'Supplier Confirmation' )",
]
_CONSERVATIVE_TREE = (
    "->( 'Register Order', X( "
    + ", ".join(_MIDDLES)
    + " ), 'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case' )"
)
model_conservative = pm4py.parse_process_tree(_CONSERVATIVE_TREE)
```

This is a process tree. The same activity label is a separate leaf in each branch that uses it, so the Petri-net image has several transitions named Credit Check. Each case still executes at most one of them, because the exclusive choice selects one middle and each middle contains each label at most once. Duplicate labels do not encode rework. They do make the net larger, they make simplicity worse, and they give the aligner several transitions for one label. Alignments remain well-defined.

Scalability stops at the variant dictionary. This log has 7 middles and 25 labeled leaves after factoring (21 in the middles, plus the four skeleton labels), versus 49 leaves if each full trace were copied. A few dozen variants remain explainable. Hundreds of variants produce a tree whose size tracks the log, with up to one transition copy per activity per variant, and alignment search pays for the duplicate labels. New interleavings add branches. Nothing in the tree states the constraint those branches would share. Unfolding the balanced language into twelve sequences would be language-equivalent to section 4 and would already hide the precedence hypothesis. Factorial growth in optional unordered activities is the point at which the catalogue stops being a model of a rule.

Missing Supplier Confirmation is not abstracted here. V3 and V4 are memorized strings. The model does not say why Supplier Confirmation is absent, and it does not contain a silent skip that could fire on a new context.

### Behavior deliberately excluded

- Every string outside the seven variants, including recombinations that obey D1–D6.
- Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Expedite Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case. After the two checks, the catalogue allows Supplier Confirmation or Approve Configuration. Expedite Review matches neither middle. D6 would have allowed it.
- Register Order, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case. After Register Order, Engineering Review, every matching middle expects Credit Check.
- Any repeat, any missing check, any Expedite Review before both checks, any Supplier Confirmation or Expedite Review after Approve Configuration, and any start or end other than D1.
- A sourced guard. External versus stocked is not in the language.

### Known underfitting risks

The catalogue underfits any stable rule whose linear extensions were only partly sampled. Five completions that the balanced model treats as the same partial order are conformance violations here. V6 with a dropped Supplier Confirmation becomes Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Expedite Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case and is rejected, while V1 with a dropped Supplier Confirmation becomes V3 and is accepted. Robustness to S5 is accidental and uneven. The frozen singleton orders also underfit the next Expedite Review case if its only difference is swapping Expedite Review with Supplier Confirmation relative to V6 or V7. True overlap, rework, and a sourcing guard are outside the hypothesis. The model overfits in the complementary sense: deletion artifacts and one-off ingestion orders, once seen, become permanent law.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

### Inductive bias

The balanced hypothesis is a completion-language partial order. Both check orders are legal because both occur, and a single sequence is inconsistent with the log. Supplier Confirmation, when present, completes after Credit Check, because that precedence is exceptionless in 23/23 relevant cases, including the 8 Supplier Confirmation cases in which Engineering Review had already completed first (V2 seven times, V7 once). Engineering Review stays unordered with Supplier Confirmation because V5 and the modal paths show both completion orders. Expedite Review is optional, once, after both checks and before approval, and unordered with Supplier Confirmation, because V6 and V7 exhibit both orders and D6 does not tie Expedite Review to Supplier Confirmation. Optionality of Supplier Confirmation is the disclosed abstraction of S3–S5, not a finding that 23% of cases are stocked. The missing edge between the checks admits interleavings. Under D7 it does not assert overlap. S2 is the reason this representation is plausible, not the reason it is proven.

### How sequence, partial order, and concurrency were chosen

Decision rule used for these four activities:

1. If every observed case that contains both activities completes \(X\) before \(Y\), and a rule or that exceptionless pattern supports it, add a precedence edge. The edge constrains admitted completions. It does not assert that \(Y\)’s start waits for \(X\)’s completion.
2. If both completion orders occur, do not add a sequence edge. Do not treat the two DFG arcs as proof of overlap (D7, S7, DFG-BIDIR).
3. Use a process-tree AND only when the block is atomic relative to every other activity. V5 places Supplier Confirmation between the checks, so an AND-block of Credit Check and Engineering Review followed by Supplier Confirmation rejects observed behavior. The checks are therefore separate nodes of one partial order, not children of `+`.
4. Use exclusive choice for optionality, and only for optionality, in this candidate: activity versus silent skip.
5. Credit Check and Engineering Review: both orders, 19 and 11, and S1 is contradicted. No edge.
6. Credit Check and Supplier Confirmation: Credit Check completes first in 23/23 cases that contain Supplier Confirmation. Edge. This remains a completion constraint. It does not prove that Credit Check’s completion enables Supplier Confirmation’s start, because a long supplier lead time can finish after a fast credit check even when the credit check did not enable it.
7. Engineering Review and Supplier Confirmation: both orders occur (Supplier Confirmation later in 21 of the 23, earlier in V5). No edge.
8. Expedite Review and either check: D6, S6, and 2/2 traces agree. Both checks precede Expedite Review.
9. Expedite Review and Supplier Confirmation: both orders occur once. No edge.
10. Expedite Review and Supplier Confirmation may each be skipped. Skipping one does not require skipping the other. Coupling them would memorize an \(n = 2\) coincidence that D6 does not state. Independence is the more cautious generalization and is itself uncertain (section 10).

Sequence, in the strict sense, is reserved for the skeleton: Register Order before the middle, and Approve Configuration before Release Production before Archive Case. Concurrency, in the wall-clock sense, is not the representation chosen for any pair.

### Formal expression

```text
partial_order, every node once:
  RO, CC, ER, (SC xor τ), (EX xor τ), AC, RP, AR
cover edges:
  RO ≺ CC
  RO ≺ ER
  CC ≺ (SC xor τ)
  CC ≺ (EX xor τ)
  ER ≺ (EX xor τ)
  ER ≺ AC
  (SC xor τ) ≺ AC
  (EX xor τ) ≺ AC
  AC ≺ RP ≺ AR
edges deliberately absent:
  CC and ER
  ER and (SC xor τ)
  (SC xor τ) and (EX xor τ)
```

Precedence is the transitive closure. The silent side of each exclusive choice is a synchronization point in the net and contributes no visible label. Finite language, 12 traces: both check orders with no Supplier Confirmation and no Expedite Review (2, both observed); the same with Expedite Review (2, neither observed); Supplier Confirmation and no Expedite Review (3, all observed: V1, V2, V5); both optional activities present (5, of which V6 and V7 are observed).

### Code

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

def _optional(label):
    visible = Transition(label=label)
    skip = SilentTransition()
    return OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [visible, skip])

_ro = Transition(label="Register Order")
_cc = Transition(label="Credit Check")
_er = Transition(label="Engineering Review")
_sc = _optional("Supplier Confirmation")  # merges S3, S4, and S5; see assumptions
_ex = _optional("Expedite Review")
_ac = Transition(label="Approve Configuration")
_rp = Transition(label="Release Production")
_ar = Transition(label="Archive Case")

model_balanced = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[_ro, _cc, _er, _sc, _ex, _ac, _rp, _ar])
model_balanced.order.add_edge(_ro, _cc)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(_ro, _er)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(_cc, _sc)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(_cc, _ex)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(_er, _ex)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(_er, _ac)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(_sc, _ac)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(_ex, _ac)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(_ac, _rp)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(_rp, _ar)
```

One node carries each visible label, so this candidate does not duplicate labels. The XOR nodes are atomic, which is harmless because each has only a single visible activity or a skip. The checks themselves are not wrapped in an operator, so Supplier Confirmation can sit between them.

### Behavior deliberately excluded

- Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check in that completion order. The earliest edge is Credit Check before Supplier Confirmation. This is the trace that distinguishes the balanced hypothesis from D5’s symmetric reading.
- Expedite Review before either check has completed. The earliest edge is the check that has not yet completed, under D6/S6.
- Supplier Confirmation before Credit Check, Supplier Confirmation or Expedite Review after Approve Configuration, a missing check, a repeated activity, and any break in the Approve Configuration–Release Production–Archive Case suffix.
- S1’s sequence. Engineering-Review-first completions stay inside the language.

### Known underfitting risks

The Credit Check before Supplier Confirmation edge underfits D5 if either check truly enables procurement and a slow credit check eventually produces the completion Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check. Long supplier lead times can also hide that enabling pattern inside the 23 observed “Credit Check completes first” strings, so the edge can be descriptively right on this extract and still mechanistically wrong. Expedite Review without Supplier Confirmation is admitted and may underfit a real coupling (“expedite only when a supplier is involved”) that the two observed cases are too few to prove; the complementary overfitting risk is treating independence as settled. The silent skip underfits the distinction between stocked parts and lost events: both produce the same completion string, so a conformance pass does not clear an external order. Rework, overlap, and any activity inside the suffix remain outside the language. The two singleton Expedite Review orders are generalized rather than memorized; if one of them is an ingestion-index accident, the partial order keeps the accident’s alternative as well as the real order.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

### Inductive bias

The permissive hypothesis takes D5 symmetrically and keeps every tighter constraint that D1–D4 and D6 actually state. Supplier Confirmation, when present, completes after Credit Check or after Engineering Review (or after both). The unobserved completion Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check is admitted because the written rule says either check can supply enough information, and because a completion log cannot see a start that was enabled by Engineering Review if Supplier Confirmation then outlasts Credit Check. The rest of the balanced partial order is unchanged. This candidate is the sensitivity model for the gate on Supplier Confirmation, not a flower model of the middle.

The four branches below are a partition of that language, not four overlapping guesses. P0–P2 together have the same language as the balanced partial order. P3 is the only strict addition.

### Formal expression

```text
sequence(
  Register Order,
  xor(P0, P1, P2, P3),
  Approve Configuration,
  Release Production,
  Archive Case
)
P0 = partial_order( CC || ER, both ≺ (EX xor τ) )                         # no SC
P1 = partial_order( {CC, ER} ≺ SC, {CC, ER} ≺ (EX xor τ), SC || EX )       # SC after both
P2 = partial_order( CC ≺ SC ≺ ER ≺ (EX xor τ) )                            # SC after only CC
P3 = partial_order( ER ≺ SC ≺ CC ≺ (EX xor τ) )                            # SC after only ER
```

Language size 14. The two traces in P3 are Register Order, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case and the same string with Expedite Review immediately after Credit Check. When Supplier Confirmation completes before Credit Check, Expedite Review cannot move in front of Supplier Confirmation, because Expedite Review still waits for both checks. The “before or after Supplier Confirmation” permission in S6 is already fully used in P1; P3 does not add a further Expedite Review slot.

### Code

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

def _ex_optional():
    return OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [Transition(label="Expedite Review"), SilentTransition()])

def _po(nodes, edges):
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=nodes)
    for src, tgt in edges:
        po.order.add_edge(src, tgt)
    return po

def _p0():
    cc, er, ex = Transition(label="Credit Check"), Transition(label="Engineering Review"), _ex_optional()
    return _po([cc, er, ex], [(cc, ex), (er, ex)])

def _p1():
    cc, er = Transition(label="Credit Check"), Transition(label="Engineering Review")
    sc, ex = Transition(label="Supplier Confirmation"), _ex_optional()
    return _po([cc, er, sc, ex], [(cc, sc), (er, sc), (cc, ex), (er, ex)])

def _p2():
    cc, er = Transition(label="Credit Check"), Transition(label="Engineering Review")
    sc, ex = Transition(label="Supplier Confirmation"), _ex_optional()
    return _po([cc, er, sc, ex], [(cc, sc), (sc, er), (er, ex)])

def _p3():
    cc, er = Transition(label="Credit Check"), Transition(label="Engineering Review")
    sc, ex = Transition(label="Supplier Confirmation"), _ex_optional()
    return _po([cc, er, sc, ex], [(er, sc), (sc, cc), (cc, ex)])

_choice = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [_p0(), _p1(), _p2(), _p3()])
_ro = Transition(label="Register Order")
_ac = Transition(label="Approve Configuration")
_rp = Transition(label="Release Production")
_ar = Transition(label="Archive Case")
model_permissive = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[_ro, _choice, _ac, _rp, _ar])
model_permissive.order.add_edge(_ro, _choice)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(_choice, _ac)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(_ac, _rp)
model_permissive.order.add_edge(_rp, _ar)
```

This is POWL. The same construction method as the balanced model is used so the two languages are easy to compare; the conservative candidate is the process tree. Credit Check, Engineering Review, and Expedite Review each appear as distinct transition objects in four branches, and Supplier Confirmation in three. Duplicate labels are alternative copies under the exclusive choice, not repeats inside one case. Each branch contains each label at most once, so a case still cannot execute Expedite Review twice. The copies cost simplicity and give the aligner several transitions per label. The structure does not grow with the number of historical cases. It grows only if a new order pattern is added as a branch. P0 and P1 keep unordered checks compact instead of enumerating their linear extensions.

The skip of Supplier Confirmation is again the S3/S4/S5 abstraction: P0 fires for a recorded absence, whatever the cause. P1–P3 do not repair a missing event; they only change where a present Supplier Confirmation may sit.

### Boundary between generalization and forbidden behavior

Reasonable generalization stops at the precedences D1–D6 state, with D5 read symmetrically and with the observed strict suffix retained. Inside that boundary the model adds the unobserved P3 completions and, by sharing P0–P2 with the balanced language, the five balanced recombinations.

The following stays forbidden:

- Supplier Confirmation before both checks have a completion in front of it (Supplier Confirmation as the first work step). D5 requires information from at least one check.
- Expedite Review before Credit Check has completed, or before Engineering Review has completed, or after Approve Configuration (D6, S6).
- A missing Credit Check or Engineering Review (D3), a missing Approve Configuration or Release Production (D2), or a missing skeleton event (D1).
- Any event between Approve Configuration, Release Production, and Archive Case.
- Any repeated label, any activity outside the eight names, and any second Expedite Review.
- A free interleaving of all four middle activities. That flower would admit the forbidden prefixes above. Breadth of imagination is not the boundary; the written precedences are.

### Behavior deliberately excluded

- Register Order, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case. Earliest violation: Supplier Confirmation before either check.
- Register Order, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Engineering Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case. Earliest violation: Expedite Review before Engineering Review.
- Register Order, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case. Expedite Review precedes Credit Check. P3 allows Expedite Review only after that Credit Check.
- Rework loops and S1’s forced check sequence.

### Known underfitting risks

P3 is a small language change and a large hypothesis change. If the 23/23 completion pattern is a real completion constraint, P3 underfits precision: it will accept a trace the next hundred cases may never show. Conversely, if the true gate is “both checks complete before Supplier Confirmation starts,” even P1 is tight on completions only where the log shows it, and P2 (which fitness requires, because of V5) already rejects that stronger gate. The candidate still underfits rework, sourcing guards, overlap, suffix insertions, and Supplier Confirmation that truly starts with no check completed. Duplicate XOR structure underfits explainability: a stakeholder reading the net sees four copies of Credit Check and has to be told they are alternatives. The nondeterministic reading of D5 is carried by the partition; if D5 meant something weaker (“procurement may prepare a file early”), this language is still too small, and that weaker reading is not supported by the rule text used here.

Overfitting and underfitting sit on opposite sides of the same edges. The catalogue overfits variant identity and underfits recombinations. The balanced edge Credit Check before Supplier Confirmation overfits a duration-confounded completion pattern if D5 is mechanistically true, and it underfits that same pattern’s unseen linear extension. None of the three overfits by adding loops or extra labels.

# 8. Validation Code

The script rebuilds the 30-case log, checks it against the published DFG, converts each candidate to an accepting Petri net, runs the soundness check, computes the four metrics, draws a playout sample, and scores a hand-written positive and negative suite with alignments. It also enumerates visible completions by firing the net. These nets are acyclic and small, so enumeration can terminate and, when the truncation flags stay false, can show equality with the intended finite language. That is not a general inclusion test. Sampled `play_out` is reported separately and does not prove language inclusion: a sample can miss a fireable trace, and a sample that only hits the oracle does not prove the oracle is complete. If a future edit introduced a silent loop, the depth and state caps would fire and the enumeration would be incomplete. Metric and playout APIs differ slightly across pm4py 2.7 releases; failures are printed rather than swallowed as successes. No numeric result is claimed in advance.

```python
"""Validate the three completion-language hypotheses. Targets pm4py 2.7+."""

import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri_net import semantics as petri_semantics
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

VARIANTS = [
    (12, ["Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review", "Supplier Confirmation", "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"]),
    (7, ["Register Order", "Engineering Review", "Credit Check", "Supplier Confirmation", "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"]),
    (4, ["Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review", "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"]),
    (3, ["Register Order", "Engineering Review", "Credit Check", "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"]),
    (2, ["Register Order", "Credit Check", "Supplier Confirmation", "Engineering Review", "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"]),
    (1, ["Register Order", "Credit Check", "Engineering Review", "Supplier Confirmation", "Expedite Review", "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"]),
    (1, ["Register Order", "Engineering Review", "Credit Check", "Expedite Review", "Supplier Confirmation", "Approve Configuration", "Release Production", "Archive Case"]),
]

EXPECTED_DFG = {
    ("Register Order", "Credit Check"): 19,
    ("Register Order", "Engineering Review"): 11,
    ("Credit Check", "Engineering Review"): 17,
    ("Engineering Review", "Credit Check"): 11,
    ("Credit Check", "Supplier Confirmation"): 9,
    ("Engineering Review", "Supplier Confirmation"): 13,
    ("Supplier Confirmation", "Engineering Review"): 2,
    ("Engineering Review", "Approve Configuration"): 6,
    ("Credit Check", "Approve Configuration"): 3,
    ("Supplier Confirmation", "Approve Configuration"): 20,
    ("Supplier Confirmation", "Expedite Review"): 1,
    ("Credit Check", "Expedite Review"): 1,
    ("Expedite Review", "Supplier Confirmation"): 1,
    ("Expedite Review", "Approve Configuration"): 1,
    ("Approve Configuration", "Release Production"): 30,
    ("Release Production", "Archive Case"): 30,
}

def build_log():
    strings = []
    for count, activities in VARIANTS:
        strings.extend([",".join(activities)] * count)
    log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(strings)
    if len(log) != 30:
        raise AssertionError("expected 30 cases, got %s" % len(log))
    return log

def build_conservative():
    middles = [
        "->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation' )",
        "->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', 'Supplier Confirmation' )",
        "->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review' )",
        "->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check' )",
        "->( 'Credit Check', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Engineering Review' )",
        "->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Expedite Review' )",
        "->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', 'Expedite Review', 'Supplier Confirmation' )",
    ]
    tree = "->( 'Register Order', X( " + ", ".join(middles) + " ), 'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case' )"
    return pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree)

def _optional(label):
    return OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [Transition(label=label), SilentTransition()])

def build_balanced():
    ro = Transition(label="Register Order")
    cc = Transition(label="Credit Check")
    er = Transition(label="Engineering Review")
    sc = _optional("Supplier Confirmation")
    ex = _optional("Expedite Review")
    ac = Transition(label="Approve Configuration")
    rp = Transition(label="Release Production")
    ar = Transition(label="Archive Case")
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[ro, cc, er, sc, ex, ac, rp, ar])
    for src, tgt in (
        (ro, cc), (ro, er), (cc, sc), (cc, ex), (er, ex),
        (er, ac), (sc, ac), (ex, ac), (ac, rp), (rp, ar),
    ):
        po.order.add_edge(src, tgt)
    return po

def _ex():
    return _optional("Expedite Review")

def _po(nodes, edges):
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=nodes)
    for src, tgt in edges:
        po.order.add_edge(src, tgt)
    return po

def build_permissive():
    def p0():
        cc, er, ex = Transition(label="Credit Check"), Transition(label="Engineering Review"), _ex()
        return _po([cc, er, ex], [(cc, ex), (er, ex)])

    def p1():
        cc, er = Transition(label="Credit Check"), Transition(label="Engineering Review")
        sc, ex = Transition(label="Supplier Confirmation"), _ex()
        return _po([cc, er, sc, ex], [(cc, sc), (er, sc), (cc, ex), (er, ex)])

    def p2():
        cc, er = Transition(label="Credit Check"), Transition(label="Engineering Review")
        sc, ex = Transition(label="Supplier Confirmation"), _ex()
        return _po([cc, er, sc, ex], [(cc, sc), (sc, er), (er, ex)])

    def p3():
        cc, er = Transition(label="Credit Check"), Transition(label="Engineering Review")
        sc, ex = Transition(label="Supplier Confirmation"), _ex()
        return _po([cc, er, sc, ex], [(er, sc), (sc, cc), (cc, ex)])

    choice = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [p0(), p1(), p2(), p3()])
    ro = Transition(label="Register Order")
    ac = Transition(label="Approve Configuration")
    rp = Transition(label="Release Production")
    ar = Transition(label="Archive Case")
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[ro, choice, ac, rp, ar])
    po.order.add_edge(ro, choice)
    po.order.add_edge(choice, ac)
    po.order.add_edge(ac, rp)
    po.order.add_edge(rp, ar)
    return po

def trace_names(trace):
    return [event["concept:name"] for event in trace]

def fitness_is_perfect(result):
    if isinstance(result, (int, float)):
        return float(result) >= 0.999
    if not isinstance(result, dict):
        return False
    for key in ("log_fitness", "logFitness", "average_trace_fitness", "averageFitness",
                "perc_fit_traces", "percFitTraces", "percentage_of_fitting_traces"):
        if key in result:
            value = float(result[key])
            if "perc" in key.lower() or "percentage" in key.lower():
                return value >= 99.9
            return value >= 0.999
    raise KeyError("unrecognized fitness payload: %r" % (sorted(result),))

def mark_key(marking):
    return frozenset((getattr(place, "name", id(place)), marking[place]) for place in marking)

def enumerate_visible_language(net, im, fm, max_depth=24, max_states=20000):
    """Exhaustive only if both truncation flags stay false. Silent loops hit the caps."""
    traces = set()
    seen = set()
    dead_non_final = []
    truncated = {"depth": False, "states": False}
    visits = {"n": 0}

    def walk(marking, prefix, depth):
        if visits["n"] >= max_states:
            truncated["states"] = True
            return
        if depth > max_depth:
            truncated["depth"] = True
            return
        key = (mark_key(marking), tuple(prefix))
        if key in seen:
            return
        seen.add(key)
        visits["n"] += 1
        enabled = list(petri_semantics.enabled_transitions(net, marking))
        if not enabled:
            if marking == fm:
                traces.add(tuple(prefix))
            else:
                dead_non_final.append(tuple(prefix))
            return
        for transition in enabled:
            follower = petri_semantics.execute(transition, net, marking)
            label = transition.label
            if label:
                walk(follower, prefix + [label], depth + 1)
            else:
                walk(follower, prefix, depth + 1)

    walk(im, [], 0)
    return sorted(traces), truncated, dead_non_final

def check_dfg(log):
    discovered = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    if isinstance(discovered, tuple):
        dfg = discovered[0]
        starts = discovered[1] if len(discovered) > 1 else None
        ends = discovered[2] if len(discovered) > 2 else None
    else:
        dfg, starts, ends = discovered, None, None
    if dict(dfg) != EXPECTED_DFG:
        missing = set(EXPECTED_DFG) - set(dfg)
        extra = set(dfg) - set(EXPECTED_DFG)
        print("DFG MISMATCH missing", missing, "extra", extra)
    else:
        print("DFG matches the published frequency DFG")
    print("start_activities", starts)
    print("end_activities", ends)

def safe(name, fn):
    try:
        value = fn()
        print(name, value)
        return value
    except Exception as exc:
        print(name, "FAILED", type(exc).__name__, exc)
        return None

OBSERVED = [tuple(activities) for _, activities in VARIANTS]
BALANCED_ONLY = [
    tuple("Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Expedite Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case".split(", ")),
    tuple("Register Order, Engineering Review, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case".split(", ")),
    tuple("Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Expedite Review, Supplier Confirmation, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case".split(", ")),
    tuple("Register Order, Engineering Review, Credit Check, Supplier Confirmation, Expedite Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case".split(", ")),
    tuple("Register Order, Credit Check, Supplier Confirmation, Engineering Review, Expedite Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case".split(", ")),
]
PERMISSIVE_ONLY = [
    tuple("Register Order, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case".split(", ")),
    tuple("Register Order, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case".split(", ")),
]
NEGATIVE = [
    tuple("Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Approve Configuration, Expedite Review, Release Production, Archive Case".split(", ")),
    tuple("Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Release Production, Archive Case".split(", ")),
    tuple("Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case".split(", ")),
    tuple("Register Order, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case".split(", ")),
    tuple("Register Order, Credit Check, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case".split(", ")),
]

def expected_accept(model_name, trace):
    if trace in NEGATIVE:
        return False
    if trace in OBSERVED:
        return True
    if trace in BALANCED_ONLY:
        return model_name in ("balanced", "permissive")
    if trace in PERMISSIVE_ONLY:
        return model_name == "permissive"
    return None

def main():
    log = build_log()
    check_dfg(log)
    models = {
        "conservative": build_conservative(),
        "balanced": build_balanced(),
        "permissive": build_permissive(),
    }
    oracles = {
        "conservative": set(OBSERVED),
        "balanced": set(OBSERVED) | set(BALANCED_ONLY),
        "permissive": set(OBSERVED) | set(BALANCED_ONLY) | set(PERMISSIVE_ONLY),
    }
    suite = OBSERVED + BALANCED_ONLY + PERMISSIVE_ONLY + NEGATIVE
    for name, model in models.items():
        print("\n===", name, "===")
        net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
        safe("soundness", lambda: pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True))
        safe("fitness", lambda: pm4py.fitness_alignments(log, net, im, fm))
        safe("precision", lambda: pm4py.precision_alignments(log, net, im, fm))
        safe("generalization_tbr", lambda: pm4py.generalization_tbr(log, net, im, fm))
        safe("simplicity", lambda: pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm))

        def playout():
            try:
                return pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm, parameters={"no_traces": 80, "max_trace_length": 16})
            except TypeError:
                return pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm)

        simulated = safe("play_out", playout)
        if simulated is not None:
            sampled = set(tuple(trace_names(tr)) for tr in simulated)
            print("play_out distinct traces", len(sampled), "(sample, not a language proof)")
            outside = sampled - oracles[name]
            print("sampled traces outside intended oracle", len(outside))
            for item in sorted(outside):
                print("  outside", " | ".join(item))

        enumerated, truncated, dead = enumerate_visible_language(net, im, fm)
        print("enumeration truncated", truncated, "dead_non_final", len(dead), "traces", len(enumerated))
        if not truncated["depth"] and not truncated["states"]:
            enum_set = set(enumerated)
            print("oracle minus enumerated", sorted(oracles[name] - enum_set))
            print("enumerated minus oracle", sorted(enum_set - oracles[name]))
        else:
            print("enumeration incomplete; do not treat it as language equality")

        passed = 0
        for trace in suite:
            one = pm4py.parse_event_log_string([",".join(trace)])
            result = pm4py.fitness_alignments(one, net, im, fm)
            accepted = fitness_is_perfect(result)
            expect = expected_accept(name, trace)
            ok = accepted == expect
            passed += int(ok)
            print(("PASS" if ok else "FAIL"), name, "accepted" if accepted else "rejected", " | ".join(trace))
        print(name, "suite", passed, "/", len(suite))

if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

Use the balanced model for immediate completion-log conformance. It accepts every observed case, it states the constraints that are actually stable on this extract, and a stakeholder can point at each edge. Use the permissive model for sensitivity analysis: replay the same cases and any new cases on both nets and count how often the only difference is a P3 firing. That difference is the operational cost of taking D5 symmetrically. If the operating question is instead “has this exact string been seen?”, switch the deployed language to the conservative tree and keep the balanced model as the explanation of which alarms are mere recombinations.

Do not deploy S1 as a hard gate on this evidence. It rejects 11 of 30 completions. If finance wants it as a to-be control, run it as a shadow rule and report the violations; do not silently replace the as-is model.

Collect the following before refitting:

1. A case-level, and preferably line-level, sourcing attribute with values external, stocked, and mixed, plus the decision `supplier_confirmation_expected`.
2. An integration acknowledgement for Supplier Confirmation: sent, acknowledged, or known-missing. Pair it with the sourcing attribute. External and known-missing supports S5. Stocked and no expected confirmation supports S4. External, expected, and neither an event nor an acknowledgement is a true control failure under S3.
3. Lifecycle timestamps `start` and `complete` for Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, and Expedite Review. Completions stay as they are.
4. A boolean `timestamps_tied`, true when a directly-follows pair was ordered by ingestion index rather than by distinct completion times (S7).
5. `expedite_requested` as a case attribute, so optionality of Expedite Review can be checked against a decision rather than against a silent skip.
6. The next 50–100 cases, stratified by sourcing. Thirty cases with two singletons are enough to build these hypotheses and not enough to settle them.

Traces and timestamp patterns that discriminate the hypotheses:

- Overlapping `start`/`complete` intervals for the two checks support a true concurrent mechanism (S2). Disjoint intervals whose order always matches S1 support a sequence and would demote the partial order to a model of logging noise. Both completion orders with no overlap support a data-driven sequence or an exclusive assignment, which is a different model again from either concurrency or the present partial order.
- A large share of the 19/11 split marked `timestamps_tied` means the check-order edge must stay absent. A sequence model would be fitting the ingestion index.
- `sourcing = external` and no Supplier Confirmation event, with the acknowledgement, separates missingness from optionality. If essentially all seven current absences are external and unacknowledged, remove the skip and treat those cases as non-conforming. If they are stocked, keep the skip and attach the attribute as a guard.
- `sourcing = stocked` together with a Supplier Confirmation event challenges S4’s skip story.
- The completion Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check is the single most informative new string for balanced versus permissive. Its `start` of Supplier Confirmation relative to `complete` of Credit Check is the mechanistic half of that test: a start before Credit Check completes supports D5 even in cases whose completions still show Credit Check first.
