# 1. Data audit and estimands

## Queueing quantities and conventions
- **System:** single queue, service center open 07:00-19:00, 12 opening hours per day. No service while closed. Cases can carry over overnight and over days.
- **Estimands:**
  - `lambda(t)` arrival intensity in cases per opening-hour, derived as `arrivals / 12`. Opening-hours convention only; true instantaneous rate varies intraday.
  - `mu_out(t)` completion intensity in cases per opening-hour, derived as `completed / 12`. This is observed throughput, not service capacity and not the reciprocal of service time.
  - `S` active handling time per case, excluding waiting. Only `median_service_min` and `p90_service_min` supplied. Mean, variance, and conditional-on-variant distributions are unobserved.
  - `L` mean number in system, via `mean_wip` from 15-min snapshots during opening hours. Includes both waiting and in-service.
  - `W` mean flow time in system (waiting + service) during opening hours, unobserved directly. Only inferrable via Little's Law under strict conditions.
  - `c` concurrently available capacity via `staff_fte`. Skill, breaks, non-queue work, and outage-reduced effective capacity are unobserved.
  - Covariates: `complex_pct`, `peak_08_10_pct` (share of arrivals 08:00-09:59), `outage_min`.
- **Units:** counts per day converted to per opening-hour; times in minutes converted to hours where needed for `L = lambda * W`.
- **Windows:** daily aggregates 2026-04-06 to 2026-05-03 (28 days). Representative hourly vectors for 4 days with 12 bins 07-08 to 18-19.

## Limitations of aggregates
Daily `arrivals` and `completed` need not refer to same cases. `median/p90 service` are not mean flow time. `mean_wip` snapshots weight long-staying cases. `staff_fte` daily mean hides intraday rostering. Backlog at 07:00 not supplied per day. Exact intervention time on 2026-04-29 not logged.

## At least five threats to naive inference
1. **Overnight carry-over and boundary backlog:** Opening and closing WIP unknown per day. Daily `arrivals - completed` does not equal change in system if day starts/ends non-empty. Outage and congested periods violate flow conservation within a day.
2. **Throughput-service confounding:** `completed` rises in recovery (2026-04-16: 129) not because service got faster but because backlog + extra staff drain queue. Treating it as sustainable capacity overstates `mu`.
3. **Case-mix and coding change:** `complex_pct` jumps from ~18-27% to ~39-47% around 2026-04-20. Median service jumps 25-26 to 34-36 min contemporaneously. Cannot separate real work increase vs recoding without case-level variant labels.
4. **Intraday non-stationarity and burstiness:** Daily means hide hourly peaks. Representative 2026-04-21 shows 34 arrivals in one hour vs daily mean 9.6/hour. Daily rate understates peak congestion and waiting.
5. **Capacity endogeneity:** `staff_fte` increases to 7.0 on 2026-04-16 and from 2026-04-29. Staffing responds to backlog/outage/mix, so correlation between staff and WIP/service time is not causal in pooled regression.
6. **Temporal dependence:** Daily rows are serially dependent via backlog, rostering week cycle, and persistent mix shift. Standard errors assuming independence are invalid; pooled means mix weekday/weekend and pre/post change.
7. **Censoring and truncation:** Service times for cases still in system at 19:00 or at end of observation are not represented correctly in daily median/p90 of completed/active handling; p90 inflation may reflect selection.

**Assumptions introduced:**
- A1: Opening hours are exactly 12h every date including weekends; needed to convert to hourly rates.
- A2: Hourly vectors sum to daily totals and are correctly aligned to bins; needed for burstiness metrics.
- A3: Outage 11:00-14:00 on 2026-04-15 eliminated completions but arrivals continued; needed to interpret zeros.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

## Alternative S2: three-state mix/capacity story
- S-low (normal mix): 2026-04-06 to 04-19 excluding 04-15 to 04-17, i.e. all low-complex days regardless of weekday/weekend, with weekday/weekend as sub-variation.
- S-transient (outage+recovery): 2026-04-15 to 04-17 as single transient block, deficit then surplus net -18 cases over 3d.
- S-high (new mix + intervention response): 2026-04-20 to 05-03 as single high-complex block, with staffing intervention as covariate inside it rather than a regime cut (pre 04-20 to 04-28 vs post 04-29 to 05-03 compared via difference).

Operational story: the fundamental change-point is case mix / arrival shape on ~04-20, not staffing. Staffing from 04-29 is a response whose effect is estimated within S-high. Outage is a nuisance transient.

Strengths vs primary: more parsimonious; emphasizes that 04-16 to 04-17 recovery staffing and 04-29 intervention are same lever at different times; avoids over-splitting 9+5 days of high-complex into two regimes with only 5 post-intervention days; better for testing whether service-time elevation persists after controlling for staff.
Weaknesses vs primary: pools weekday/weekend within S-low and S-high, obscuring that weekend arrival rate is ~40% of weekday and staffing ratio differs; pools declining-WIP intervention days with growing-WIP pre-intervention days, understating dynamics; merges outage deficit and recovery surplus which have opposite Little's Law implications.

## Alternative S3: calendar/rostering segmentation (weekday vs weekend only)
- Weekday regime (20d) vs Weekend regime (8d), ignoring outage/mix/intervention except as outliers.

Story: weekly roster drives everything; apparent trends are weekday composition effects.

Strengths: captures strongest periodic structure; simple for staffing templates.
Weaknesses: fails change-point evidence: weekday median 25 -> 35, complex 20% -> 44%, peak 30% -> 46% cannot be explained by day-of-week; pools R-A weekdays (L~20) with R-D weekdays (L~44) and R-E (L~37), yielding misleading average L~34 and service ~31 min that represents no actual day well. Outage day distorts weekday mean completion downward while recovery distorts upward, canceling in pooled weekday mean and hiding both.

Comparison: Primary is preferred for operations because it separates demand, service-content, capacity, and backlog mechanisms. S2 is a serious competitor if the question is whether complex_pct is real work vs coding artifact, because it frames the test as low vs high mix. S3 is useful as null model to demonstrate Simpson's paradox but indefensible as sole model.

# 4. Pooled versus segmented statistics

Pooled summaries use 28d totals: arrivals 2519 mean 90.0/d, completed 2409 mean 86.0/d, overall mean_wip 29.9, median_service range 24-36, staff mean ~5.3 FTE mixing 6-7 weekdays and 2.5-3.0 weekends. These obscure:

## 1. Weekday/weekend composition
- Pooled arrival 90/d (7.5/h) represents neither weekdays (~105-115/d in respective regimes) nor weekends (~42-49/d). Pooled staffing 5.3 FTE represents neither 6-7 nor 2.5-3.0.
- Example: pooled L 29.9 / pooled lambda 7.5/h gives 4.0h implied flow, versus segmented R-A weekday 2.4h and R-Aw weekend 4.0h and R-D weekday 4.7-5.3h. Pooled flow overstates baseline weekday wait by ~65% and understates congested weekday wait.
- Staffing inference reversal: pooled regression of completions on staff would show higher staff associated with higher WIP (weekday vs weekend, R-E vs R-A), suggesting staff increases WIP, which is backlog confounding.

## 2. Complex-case mix and service-time shift
- Pooled median ~32 min (range) or mean of medians ~31.5 min hides bimodality: R-A median 24-26, R-D/R-E median 32-36. Pooled p90 ~68 min hides 44-50 vs 72-88 split. p90/median rises 1.9 to 2.4, indicating shape change, not just location shift.
- Pooled view suggests gradual drift; segmented view shows step change on 04-20: complex_pct 25% (04-15) -> 27% (04-16) -> 25% (04-17) -> 24% (04-18 weekend) then 41% (04-20 Monday). Change-point heuristic on complex_pct flags 04-20 as break (pre-mean ~22%, post-mean ~43%). Treating service capacity as constant across pool underestimates required FTE post-04-20 by ~30-40% if using median 25 min.
- If complex increase is coding artifact, pooled service inflation is spurious; segmented view isolates the hypothesis to post-04-20 data where case-level validation is needed.

## 3. Staffing intervention and outage masking
- Pooled completions 86/d vs arrivals 90/d suggests mild backlog growth +110 over 28d (~3.9/d). This nets out -47 on 04-15, +29 on 04-16 to 04-17, -~94 over R-D weekdays (7d *13.4), +~18 over R-E weekdays (3d *6). Pooling cancels transient deficit/surplus and chronic imbalance, implying near-stability while R-D was explosively unstable.
- Intervention assessment: comparing pooled pre/post means without segmentation mixes outage recovery throughput (10.0/h in R-C) with intervention throughput (10.1/h in R-E) and baseline (8.3/h), suggesting intervention added little. Segmented comparison R-D weekdays 8.4/h vs R-E weekdays 10.1/h at same arrival ~9.5-9.6/h and same high mix shows +1.7/h (+20%) gain and WIP decline 50->32, a material effect. Conversely naive pooled comparison of R-C vs R-E would claim no effect, another reversal.
- Outage-inclusive pooled completion rate understates true service capacity; recovery-inclusive pooled rate overstates sustainable capacity. Either error biases staffing by ~15-20%.

Robust summaries preferred: report regime medians/ranges, not 28d means. E.g., R-A arrivals median ~101, R-D weekday median ~115, R-E weekday median ~116; R-A WIP 18-23, R-D WIP 37-50.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

## Representative hourly vectors
Sums match daily totals, supporting A2. Opening-hour mean per hour: 2026-04-07 8.42, 04-15 8.75, 04-21 9.58, 04-25 4.25.

Dispersion (population variance/mean = Fano factor, CV = sd/mean for 12 hourly counts, arrivals):
- 04-07 ordinary: var ~24.9, Fano ~3.0, CV ~0.59. Moderately overdispersed vs Poisson (Fano=1). Peak 20 at 10-11, smooth decay.
- 04-15 outage-day arrivals: Fano ~3.8, CV ~0.66. Arrivals still overdispersed but completions show structural zeros 12-15h: completion Fano >> arrivals, CV >1. This is capacity intermittency, not demand burstiness.
- 04-21 bursty: var ~88.9, Fano ~9.3, CV ~0.98. Two spikes 34 (10-11) and 24 (13-14) with troughs 8,5 between. Peak_08_10_pct for that regime ~47% vs 30% baseline quantifies concentration. Completion vector smoother (7-13 range), indicating queue absorbs bursts as waiting.
- 04-25 weekend: Fano ~1.2, CV ~0.53, closest to Poisson, smoother profile 1-8 range.

Interpretation: burstiness increased post-mix-change. Daily rate alone cannot predict waiting because intraday CV nearly doubled (0.59 to 0.98). Same daily total with Fano 9 vs 3 implies far higher peak-hour utilization and nonlinear waiting. Pooled queueing model assuming stationary Poisson arrivals at daily mean would drastically underpredict delays in R-D.

## Daily sequence dependence
- Level non-stationarity: arrivals weekday baseline ~100, R-D ~114, R-E ~115; WIP baseline ~20, R-D ~44; complex ~20% -> ~44%. Visual and mean-shift evidence for change points at 04-15 (outage) and 04-20 (mix).
- Serial dependence: backlog carry-over induces positive lag-1 autocorrelation in WIP and completions. High WIP days cluster: 47,52,35 then 37,41,46,44,43 then 49,50,43,36. Lag-1 autocorrelation of mean_wip is strongly positive (~0.7-0.8 heuristic), of arrivals moderate due to week cycle and regime persistence, of `arrivals-completed` negative after outage (deficit followed by surplus). Daily points not independent.
- Overdispersion daily: weekday arrivals variance across 28d far exceeds Poisson for mean ~100 (range 92-121 plus weekends), index of dispersion >>1 due to regime mixing and week cycle, not within-regime randomness.
- Change-point heuristic (transparent, not ground truth): cumulative sum on complex_pct or rolling 3d mean of median_service flags 04-20; cumulative backlog `cumsum(arrivals-completed)` shows kink at 04-15 (-47 jump) then steady climb 04-20 to 04-28 then decline post-04-29. Simple rule like |daily complex - global mean|>10pp sustained 3d triggers same dates.

## Why four days cannot establish stochastic model
Four hourly vectors are selected exemplars, not random sample; no replication within regime (only one bursty weekday, one ordinary weekday, one weekend, one outage). Cannot estimate arrival-process distribution, time-varying rate function, service-time distribution conditional on variant, or autocorrelation function intraday. Cannot separate day-of-week vs regime effects. Cannot fit MAP or Hawkes vs periodic Poisson. At best they illustrate mechanisms and bound burstiness; formal inference needs raw event logs with per-case arrival, start-service, end-service timestamps.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Principle: `L = lambda * W` requires long-run stable system with conserved flow, same window for L and lambda, stationary throughput proxy, and W including waiting+service in same time units. Median service cannot substitute for W or for mean S. Use arrivals as lambda only if arrivals ~= completions over window and backlog stable; use completions if measuring throughput during drain but then W refers to completed cohort, mismatched to snapshot L.

# 7. Operational implications

## Staffing
- Do not roster post-04-20 demand with R-A staffing template (6.0 weekday / 2.5 weekend). Work content per arrival rose ~40% in median and more in tail; arrival rate rose ~14% weekdays. Required FTE scales with `lambda * E[S] / utilization target`, so +14% volume * +~35% mean service implies ~50%+ workload increase before burstiness penalty. R-E 7.0 weekday / 3.0 weekend stabilized but only 3 weekdays observed; sustain at least that until WIP returns to ~20 and stays there 5+ days.
- Treat R-C throughput 120/d as ceiling under overtime + backlog availability, not sustainable capacity. Planning to 120/d with normal roster will fail.
- Weekend staffing needs review: R-Aw and R-D weekends show longest implied flows (4-6h) despite low volume, suggesting 2.5 FTE is bottleneck and allows weekday backlog to persist. R-E 3.0 FTE balanced weekends; retain 3.0.

## Backlog recovery
- Prioritize draining R-D accumulated ~90-100 cases before declaring new steady state. With R-E surplus ~6/d weekdays + balance weekends, recovery takes ~2 weeks if demand stays 115/d. Do not reduce FTE when WIP first hits 30; it is still 50% above baseline.
- Sequence recovery day vs night: overnight carry means morning opening queue conditions peak 08-10 performance. Consider early-shift overlap 08-10 to protect against burst amplification.

## Peak smoothing
- Peak_08_10_pct rose 30% to 46-50% weekdays. Even with same daily total, burstiness (Fano 3->9) nonlinearly increases waiting. Investigate cause of 10-11 and 13-14 spikes on 04-21 pattern (batch releases, upstream process, appointment template, coding of complex arrivals). Options: stagger appointment slots, cap hourly arrivals, split complex vs simple streams if complex cases cluster.
- Do not average peaks away: daily capacity plan meeting daily mean 9.5/h fails peak hours needing 20-30/h handling via queue. Need intraday roster flex or triage.

## Data-collection recommendations
- Log per-case arrival, service-start, service-end, variant flag, server ID; log outage and intervention timestamps to minute; snapshot backlog at 07:00 and 19:00 daily.
- Validate complex_pct definition change on 04-20: audit coding rules, sample cases pre/post, estimate true work-content change vs label change. Until validated, report capacity scenarios under both interpretations.
- Collect hourly staffing actually serving queue vs nominal FTE, and abandonment/reneging if any.

## Pooled-beneficial but segmented-risky policy
- **Policy to avoid:** Reduce weekday FTE from 6.0 to ~5.0 because pooled completions/daily staff looks efficient when including recovery days (120/d at 6.75 FTE) and because pooled service median ~31 min seems modest. Under segmented view this cuts capacity just as work content rose 40% and burstiness doubled; R-D already unstable at 6.0 FTE with growing WIP and 5h flows. Cut would accelerate backlog growth and push peaks into overtime. Similarly, using pooled flow 4.0h as SLA target would loosen weekday baseline (2.4h achievable) while legitimizing congested 5h waits as normal.
