## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

A trace mismatch is evidence about an observation relative to a model. It is not, by itself, proof of a policy breach, an authorized exception, or personal misconduct. Conversely, a trace that fits M0 does not establish compliance with rules that M0 cannot represent.

**Established facts and model semantics.** M0 requires registration, completion of both checks, one decision branch, notification, and closure. Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review are concurrent: either observed order is allowed, and neither is optional. The entire checks block must finish before either decision branch begins. On the approved branch, manual approval must precede activation; the rejected branch also follows both checks. M0 has no information-request loop, auto-approval route, provisional route, reactivation route, or resource constraints. Each modeled activity occurs once on its selected path.

CS-9 is an effective, controlled standard. Its activation prerequisites and requester–approver separation are explicit. It permits abbreviated rejected-lead screening but leaves the relationship of those leads to M0 unresolved. The pilot decision and emergency directive have finite windows. Continuing operational need does not itself extend either window. The CRM migration, duplicate registration, and substantive equivalence of QA_Clearance are positive evidence of observation or representation defects. The aggregate information-request evidence supports the usefulness of a behavior omitted from M0.

**Disputed or unproved propositions.**

- PD-3 is a real steering-committee decision, not merely an operator's preference. However, the supplied record does not establish that the committee had delegated power to derogate from CS-9. The observer's statements conflict; neither her attendance nor her subsequent objection determines the committee's authority. Failure to incorporate PD-3 into CS-9 is a governance concern, but meeting minutes are not inherently incapable of authorizing an exception.
- I treat FD-7 as the issued, operative emergency directive described in the question, with prima facie authority for its stated derogations. Its issuer and delegation should still be retained as audit evidence. If that authority were successfully challenged, the exception judgment would change. This differs from PD-3 because FD-7's authority is not contested in the supplied record, whereas PD-3's authority to weaken CS-9 expressly is.
- SUP-702's eligibility and next-business-day quality review are supported. Its required automated master-data validation is not evidenced. The absence of that activity from this trace does not prove that an automated service failed to perform it.
- Initial emergency spend is not evidence of total spend before retrospective approval. A notification appearing in CRM establishes more when its identifier and case association are stable than when its association was entered later in a mutable field.
- Whether SUP-709 is an M0 case, and whether SUP-710 requires fresh onboarding checks, remain unresolved. A valid old certificate does not automatically establish an authorized reactivation procedure.
- SUP-715 establishes a relationship and the absence of a recorded disclosure. It does not establish that the relationship itself is prohibited, or conclusively establish that no disclosure occurred through another accepted channel.

**Burden of proof.** For an adverse compliance finding, the reviewer should establish the applicable rule and version, case scope, reliable event meaning and chronology, and the relevant identity or attribute facts. I use an ordinary evidence-based, more-likely-than-not standard for organizational findings, while distinguishing a substantiated control failure from allegations of deliberate wrongdoing. Severity concerns exposure; it does not substitute for confidence in the evidence.

An exception claimant must supply positive evidence of competent authorization, applicability at the initiating decision, and required safeguards. Eligibility alone is insufficient. Missing authorization or safeguard evidence prevents certification of an exception. Where a real but contested authorization exists, that does not automatically prove individual misconduct: report the baseline departure and the unresolved exception qualification separately. Missing events in a demonstrably incomplete collection channel are not treated as proof of missing business actions.

For FD-7, I accept the supplied directive and case-specific evidence as supporting the observed activation exception, while limiting assurance about total spend to what was actually supplied. For PD-3, authority and validation remain material unresolved conditions. The resulting judgments are conditional where the evidence warrants that qualification; they are not a compromise between opposing preferences.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest prosecution case is that real control breaches coexist with data and model defects. CS-9's central safeguards have not become optional because operations learned a different workflow. The standard requires completed sanctions and quality checks before activation and manual procurement approval before every activation. Informal expectations of a future amendment cannot supply present authority.

SUP-703 is the clearest misuse of the pilot: a high-risk, new foreign supplier cannot satisfy PD-3's LOW-risk, DE, approved-group conditions. Selecting “pilot” is not authorization. Manual approval was replaced, quality followed activation, and the first purchase was EUR 180,000. That amount increases exposure; it is not a breach of FD-7's EUR 20,000 cap, since FD-7 did not govern this June case. The prosecution need not prove that sanctions were skipped—they were performed—to establish the other gate failures.

SUP-708 crossed the sanctions gate by 40 minutes and also activated before manual approval. No pilot or emergency applied. The absence of transactions before sanctions clearance mitigates realized financial exposure but does not erase activation of a high-risk supplier without the required controls. Internal Audit's concern about SUP-703 and SUP-708 is supported by concrete control failures, not merely by low model fitness.

SUP-705 began provisional activation after FD-7 expired. A continuing flood emergency is a factual justification for urgently seeking renewed authority, not evidence that the expired directive remained effective. Its EUR 12,000 initial spend is relevant mitigation but cannot cure the missing authorization. SUP-714 similarly used the pilot substitution after PD-3 expired. Eligibility and an imminent anticipated amendment do not extend July authority into August.

For SUP-702, even a sympathetic reading must confront both the disputed derogation authority and the unevidenced automated validation. A stable CRM notification resolves one observation defect; it does not establish the required pre-activation validation or resolve the normative conflict. The prosecution should seek those records rather than asserting that all pilot cases are legitimate or that all are misconduct.

M0 also understates noncompliance. SUP-712 fits its control flow but violates CS-9 because two account aliases resolve to one human requester and approver. An event sequence cannot establish independent authorization. SUP-715 raises a separate disclosure issue that M0 cannot detect. The prosecution may investigate whether the required disclosure was made; it cannot invent a rule banning spouses or shared reporting lines.

The aggregates show that exceptional behavior has spread beyond disciplined exception management. Fourteen of the 312 June–July auto-approval cases are clearly ineligible for PD-3, and nine cannot be assessed because attributes are missing. The 289 apparently eligible cases still require validation, timing, and authority evidence. Six August uses demonstrate persistence after the pilot window. Their complete traces must show whether Auto_Approve actually substituted for required manual approval before all six are declared breaches. These figures are evidence of weak guardrails and possible normalization of exceptions, not a denominator from which an overall breach rate can be calculated.

Desirable results do not retrospectively amend policy. Obtaining certificates, experiencing no loss, staying below an emergency amount, or expecting permanent automation may mitigate consequences or justify proposing a revision. They do not retroactively authorize an ineligible pilot or expired emergency route. Nevertheless, the prosecution must concede that SUP-707 exposes a missing beneficial loop, SUP-711 is an established duplicate, SUP-713 records an equivalent quality procedure, and SUP-709/SUP-710 raise genuine scope problems. It must also concede that notification missingness rose from 2% to 29% alongside a collection design that mirrors about 70% of notifications. Treating every absent notification as business nonperformance would be indefensible.

The appropriate enforcement claim is therefore targeted: preserve the substantive activation gates and enforce valid, time-limited exceptions; do not use every literal M0 mismatch as an adverse finding.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense is that M0 is an incomplete representation of both policy and observation, and therefore an unreliable sole adjudicator. It remains a useful standard-route skeleton, but its deviation count combines materially different phenomena.

First, concurrency must be respected. Quality before sanctions is not inherently a deviation: both orders linearize M0's unordered checks. The problem in SUP-708 is that activation occurred before sanctions and manual approval, not that Quality_Review came first. A revised implementation must test completed prerequisites, not impose an arbitrary total ordering on concurrent checks.

Second, M0 omits legitimate information gathering. SUP-707 obtained missing certificates before approval; the 47 aggregate cases did not activate before requested evidence arrived. Compliance endorses requesting information. An exact sequence language that rejects these requests penalizes a safeguard and needs an explicit repeatable mechanism. This supports revising the model, not abandoning the evidence gate.

Third, real decisions can create time-bound overlays without permanently rewriting the base standard. PD-3 described concrete eligibility and compensating controls, and a compliance observer initially described a monitored exception. If an authorized steering committee can grant such exceptions, SUP-702 is a strong candidate: it satisfies the supplied eligibility facts and quality deadline. A subsequent observer's characterization cannot unilaterally invalidate delegated committee authority. The defense must nevertheless produce that delegation and pre-activation master-data validation; it cannot infer either from good outcomes.

FD-7 more clearly explains SUP-704's provisional sequence. Sanctions preceded activation; the critical pump supplier was activated within the directive's window; initial spend was below the cap; monitoring and next-business-day retrospective controls exist. An exception that permits provisional activation is not faithfully represented by a model that invariably demands quality and manual approval beforehand. Obligations triggered during the directive may be completed after its expiry: SUP-704's later reviews are different from SUP-705's new activation after expiry.

Fourth, the observation layer changed. For SUP-702, a stable notification identifier corroborates an action absent from the onboarding log. For SUP-706, the mutable association is weaker evidence, but it still defeats a confident assertion that no notification occurred. The 29% missingness after migration is close to the nonmirrored share implied by 70% coverage. That supports a collection explanation at system level, while leaving individual linkage and timing to be verified. Neither deleting Notify_Requester from the norm nor fabricating missing events is justified.

Fifth, the universe of cases is disputed. CS-9 allows abbreviated screening of rejected leads; M0 requires both checks even before rejection. SUP-709 may therefore be a lawful intake rejection or an out-of-scope lead rather than an incomplete onboarding. SUP-710 is a reactivation with a still-valid prior quality certification, not plainly a new supplier. Demanding a new registration and repeated quality event without resolving reactivation scope risks enforcing a requirement that the policy has never specified. Historical evidence must be linked and assessed, not assumed to carry forward automatically.

Finally, representation defects have direct evidence. SUP-711's duplicate comes from migration and shares the source transaction ID. SUP-713 performed equivalent quality work under a renamed label; later approval of the mapping need not mean that the underlying quality activity was unauthorized. Correcting an event vocabulary is different from retrospectively legalizing omitted work.

These arguments do not excuse SUP-703, SUP-708, or use of expired authority. Nor do they answer SUP-712's identity failure. A permanent auto-approval branch remains a proposal, and factual emergency conditions require a new competent directive once the previous one expires. The defense establishes that M0's raw fitness is a poor misconduct metric; it does not establish that operations are compliant overall.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The normative artifact should be a versioned bundle: a control-flow model, executable policy predicates, evidence requirements, and an authority register. A case conforms only when all applicable layers pass. Discovery may identify candidate behavior; frequency cannot authorize it.

**Control flow and scope.** Keep the standard route's concurrent sanctions and quality checks and its manual-approval-before-activation sequence. Add a repeatable information-request thread before approval, allowing requests to interleave with checks. Model rejection, withdrawal, and notification explicitly. A full onboarding rejection follows the standard checks; an abbreviated failed-pre-screen rejection is a separately guarded intake route without activation. Do not globally make quality optional just to fit SUP-709.

Define new onboarding, pre-screen intake, reactivation, withdrawal, and post-activation offboarding as explicit case kinds with entry/exit rules. Record the point at which a lead becomes accepted onboarding. For intake, distinguish a registry entry from acceptance rather than letting the label Register_Supplier decide scope. Withdrawals before activation may terminate with a reason and notification; termination after activation belongs to offboarding, not a withdrawal shortcut that erases review obligations.

Reactivation should verify existing supplier identity, current risk, sanctions, and independent manual approval. A proposed carry-forward rule may accept a valid historical quality review only with unchanged relevant conditions, permitted age, substantive coverage, and immutable provenance. Otherwise require a fresh quality review. This rule needs approval; its inclusion in a proposed model does not automatically legalize SUP-710.

Retain separate pilot and emergency branches as historically executable overlays and as patterns for future authorized policies. For pilot activation, require sanctions and automated validation before activation, auto-approval eligibility, and timely quality completion. For emergency activation, require prior sanctions, capped provisional spending, retrospective approval, timely quality, and daily monitoring. Quality and retrospective manual approval need not be forced into one relative order absent a policy requirement.

Provisional notification may occur after activation and before retrospective reviews; closure waits for both notification and required reviews. This avoids treating SUP-702's early CRM message as missing merely because quality completed the next day. Approving this revised notification placement is a model-governance decision; no supplied CS-9 clause independently requires waiting for retrospective quality before notifying.

**Data-aware controls.** Resolve eligibility from a signed, time-specific snapshot, not current mutable supplier attributes. Reject missing risk, country, group-membership, or group-approval evidence as insufficient for automatic-route selection; route to manual handling rather than interpreting null as LOW risk. Calculate business-day deadlines using a versioned business calendar, timezone, and explicit activation anchor. Test completed checks against operational activation, using lifecycle completion rather than a task's start timestamp.

FD-7's spend control must evaluate aggregate spend until retrospective approval, not only the first purchase. Define whether commitments count in the governing policy rather than silently assuming it, retain EUR amounts or auditable conversions, and link all relevant transactions. Monitor each required day in the directive's monitoring period. The supplied directive sets a two-business-day quality deadline but does not set the same deadline for retrospective manual approval; a new approval deadline may be proposed, not attributed to FD-7.

The authorization window governs initiation. Quality and other triggered obligations may remain due after the window closes. Expiry on July 12 does not cancel SUP-704's outstanding reviews; it blocks SUP-705's July 13 initiation. Similarly, an eligible July 31 pilot activation may have a valid quality deadline in August, while an August 2 activation cannot use the expired pilot.

**Resources and conflicts.** Enforce requester ≠ manual approver on canonical human identities across aliases. Service identities must remain distinguishable from humans. Authorized delegation needs principal, delegate, scope, validity, and approval evidence; delegation is not an automatic exemption from separation of duties. Add timely conflict disclosure and required assessment or recusal under the actual conflict policy. Do not create a blanket spouse or shared-reporting-line prohibition unless adopted prospectively by competent authority.

**Overlay governance.** Record issuer, delegated competence, approved document, permitted derogations, effective interval, recorded approval time, safeguards, and revocation/expiry. Only an authorized overlay can override specified baseline rules; sanctions remain mandatory before activation under both supplied exceptions. Overlapping FD-7 and PD-3 windows do not permit operators to combine convenient fragments. Select one coherent applicable derogation set, or require express authorization for composition, and retain all baseline rules not displaced.

An extension or permanent auto policy requires a new competent decision, a new version, effective dates, tested eligibility gates, a rollback plan, and monitored outcomes. Route switches, especially near expiry, should be logged and checked at activation. Historical policies remain immutable. Later evidence may prove that authority already existed, but a later prospective amendment cannot silently backdate it.

**Allocation across tools.**

| Layer | Appropriate implementation | Limits and purpose |

Report unknown separately from false. Business enforcement must happen before activation, not only in a retrospective dataframe. |

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The following executable construction uses fresh node instances on every branch. The sequence helper adds all ordered pairs, so its strict partial orders explicitly include transitive relations. No external files are read.

This is a **candidate structural envelope**, not a declaration that every branch is currently authorized. External policy selection must disable PD-3 after July 31 and FD-7 after July 12 for new activations. Permanent automation, revised intake/withdrawal scope, and reactivation carry-forward remain pending approval where the source materials do not already establish them. Unguarded conformance to this union is insufficient for compliance.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def activity(label):
    return Transition(label=label)


def sequence(*nodes):
    result = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for i, earlier in enumerate(nodes):
        for later in nodes[i + 1:]:
            result.order.add_edge(earlier, later)
    return result


def parallel(*nodes):
    return StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))


def choice(*nodes):
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=list(nodes))


def optional(node):
    return choice(SilentTransition(), node)


def information_requests():
    # POWL LOOP(do, redo): do, followed by zero or more (redo, do).
    # A silent do makes the visible language Request_More_Info*.
    # Response correlation and outstanding-evidence gates are external.
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.LOOP,
        children=[SilentTransition(), activity("Request_More_Info")],
    )


def standard_route():
    checks_and_information = parallel(
        activity("Sanctions_Check"),
        activity("Quality_Review"),
        information_requests(),
    )
    decision = choice(
        sequence(
            activity("Manual_Procurement_Approval"),
            activity("Activate_Supplier"),
        ),
        activity("Reject_Supplier"),
    )
    return sequence(
        checks_and_information,
        decision,
        activity("Notify_Requester"),
    )


def pilot_route():
    activation_track = sequence(
        parallel(
            activity("Sanctions_Check"),
            activity("Automated_Master_Data_Validation"),
            information_requests(),
        ),
        activity("Auto_Approve"),
        activity("Activate_Supplier"),
        activity("Notify_Requester"),
    )
    # Quality can complete before or after activation/notification.
    # The enclosing parallel block joins before Close_Case.
    return parallel(activation_track, activity("Quality_Review"))


def emergency_route():
    activation_track = sequence(
        parallel(activity("Sanctions_Check"), information_requests()),
        activity("Activate_Supplier"),
        activity("Notify_Requester"),
    )
    # Only sanctions must precede activation under this overlay.
    # Both reviews still complete before the branch can close.
    return parallel(
        activation_track,
        activity("Quality_Review"),
        activity("Manual_Procurement_Approval"),
    )


def abbreviated_rejection_route():
    return sequence(
        parallel(activity("Sanctions_Check"), information_requests()),
        activity("Reject_Supplier"),
        activity("Notify_Requester"),
    )


def withdrawal_route():
    # Represents completed work before withdrawal, not interruption
    # of running tasks. No activation occurs on this branch.
    return sequence(
        parallel(
            information_requests(),
            optional(activity("Sanctions_Check")),
            optional(activity("Quality_Review")),
        ),
        activity("Withdraw_Request"),
        activity("Notify_Requester"),
    )


def reactivation_route():
    quality_evidence = choice(
        activity("Quality_Review"),
        activity("Verify_Current_Quality_Certification"),
    )
    return sequence(
        activity("Validate_Existing_Supplier_Identity"),
        parallel(
            activity("Sanctions_Check"),
            quality_evidence,
            information_requests(),
        ),
        activity("Manual_Procurement_Approval"),
        activity("Activate_Supplier"),
        activity("Notify_Requester"),
        activity("Close_Case"),
    )


new_routes = {
    "STANDARD": standard_route(),
    "PD3_PILOT": pilot_route(),
    "FD7_EMERGENCY": emergency_route(),
    "INTAKE_REJECT": abbreviated_rejection_route(),
    "WITHDRAWAL": withdrawal_route(),
}

new_supplier_case = sequence(
    activity("Register_Supplier"),
    choice(*new_routes.values()),
    activity("Close_Case"),
)

reactivation_case = reactivation_route()
M1 = choice(new_supplier_case, reactivation_case)

# M1 is the candidate POWL. Route identifiers and policy predicates
# are maintained outside it; accepting its activity language alone
# does not constitute a policy-conformance decision.
```

**Meaning and limitations.** On the standard route, sanctions and quality remain concurrent and both precede manual approval or full-process rejection. The information loop permits zero or more requests interleaved with checks. It records requests, not evidence arrival: linked responses must prevent approval/activation while decision-critical requests remain unresolved. Rejection or withdrawal may legitimately terminate unresolved requests with a documented reason. The loop cannot model every conversational dependency or task cancellation; this is an explicit approximation, not a claim that arbitrary retries are safe.

Pilot quality is concurrent with the activation track, allowing both pre-activation quality and the authorized deferred pattern. The proposed track requires master-data validation before auto-approval, which is deliberately stronger than PD-3's explicit minimum of before activation. Emergency quality and manual approval can complete on either side of activation; sanctions always precede it. Branch completion ensures retrospective tasks finish before closure, even if notification occurred earlier. Deadlines, results, cumulative spend, and the meaning of provisional status remain external. For example, executing Sanctions_Check is not evidence that its outcome allowed activation.

The abbreviated rejection branch requires the external intake/failed-screen predicate; it is not a way for accepted onboardings to bypass quality. Withdrawal handles completed activities before termination; an interrupting workflow would need additional lifecycle/cancellation semantics. Reactivation's certification branch requires an approved carry-forward rule and an actual verification event linked to historical evidence; historical SUP-710 must not be enriched with a fabricated verification event.

For decisive assessments, first select the authorized route and historical rule set from trusted context, then perform alignment and overlay checks. If route eligibility is unknown, evaluate the alternatives and report unknown qualification; do not choose the route with the best alignment and call it authorized. Shared activity labels across alternatives also mean an alignment alone cannot establish which authority was invoked.

Normalize the underlying register as:

- `policy_version`: policy/version ID, document hash, status, issuing authority ID, valid-from/to, immutable recorded-at, approved-at, revocation reference, timezone/calendar references, and superseded version.
- `policy_authority`: authority ID, issuer and role, delegation ID, empowered policy domain, allowed derogations, validity, signed evidence ID. This supplies organizational competence rather than merely a document title.
- `policy_scope` and `policy_predicate`: separate rows for case-kind membership and typed predicates such as risk, country, group approval, and critical-relief purpose, with evidence requirements and null-handling rules.
- `policy_derogation`: one baseline rule ID displaced by one overlay rule ID per row. Unlisted baseline rules remain applicable.
- `policy_obligation`: rule ID, trigger, required action/state, deadline type and anchor, calendar, amount/currency limit where relevant, and evidence class. Do not assign retrospective approval a source deadline that FD-7 never supplied.
- `case_policy_binding`: case ID, selected route ID, policy/rule/model versions, eligibility-snapshot ID, selection and activation times, authority evidence IDs, and the assessed result: satisfied, violated, or unknown. Bindings are append-only; changes record who changed the route and why.

Represent inclusive date windows as half-open timestamp intervals once the governing business timezone is established: PD-3 ends at the start of August 1; FD-7 ends at the start of July 13. Store UTC timestamps alongside the original timezone/offset. The materials do not specify that their date boundaries were UTC, so implementation must not silently make that assumption. Deadline calendars and policy validity are separate from when evidence was entered.

**Event and data collection.**

Use an append-only event ledger with `event_id`, `case_id`, `supplier_id`, `case_kind`, canonical activity ID, original label, lifecycle, occurred/start/completed timestamps, immutable recorded/ingested timestamps, source system, source transaction ID, source event ID, model/rule/version IDs, route ID, actor account ID, canonical human/service ID, and evidence references. Keep occurrence time separate from record time. Corrections append a superseding record; they do not overwrite old values. Preserve raw events alongside a versioned analytical view.

Collect dedicated evidence tables linked to that ledger:

- **Eligibility snapshots:** risk and assessment version, country, corporate-group ID and approved status, critical-relief justification, relevant effective times, snapshot creation/signature, and decision references. Preserve what the decision-maker knew then, not just today's supplier master data.
- **Checks and automated decisions:** outcome, completion timestamp, reviewed evidence, validation service/run ID, rule version, and signed result. Record failed as well as successful checks. Auto_Approve must reference both eligibility and the applicable authority; the automation service's identity cannot masquerade as a human manual approver.
- **Notifications:** immutable `notification_id`, authoritative case/supplier association, recipient, sent timestamp, defined event status, CRM source event ID, and delivery evidence if the notification rule actually requires delivery. Generate the stable association when sending. Retain linkage changes and their actors. Reconcile CRM against onboarding and monitor mirroring coverage. For old mutable associations, preserve the original state and supporting evidence; classify uncertain joins rather than manufacture notification events.
- **Information requests and responses:** request ID, requested item, criticality, requester, request time, response IDs and arrival times, accepted evidence, and resolution/rejection/withdrawal reason. Gate approval and activation on required resolved evidence, not on the presence of Request_More_Info alone.
- **Actors and delegation:** alias-to-human mappings with effective and recorded times, identity evidence, principal/delegate IDs, authorized scope, delegation approval, and validity. Evaluate separation on resolved humans at the historical decision. Keep sensitive relationship/disclosure evidence access-controlled.
- **Conflicts:** declaration ID, relationship/conflict type, applicable policy version, disclosure occurrence and recording times, assessment, recusal or authorized handling, and evidence location. A missing event in onboarding should prompt a declaration-register join before an adverse finding.
- **Emergency spending and monitoring:** linked purchase/transaction IDs, supplier/case allocation, amounts and currency conversion evidence, posting times, retrospective approval time, per-day monitoring records, exceptions, and investigator resolution. Record cap evaluation over the full relevant spend population.
- **Historical quality reuse:** original review/certification ID, content/version, supplier scope, validity and change assessment, permitted reuse rule, and new verification evidence. Separate a fresh review from verification of an existing certificate.

Deduplicate SUP-711 only under a documented source-specific identity rule backed by the migration evidence, retaining both raw rows and a canonical-event mapping. Normalize SUP-713 through an evidence-backed label map with substantive-validity dates, mapping approval/recorded dates, and procedure hashes; do not pretend the mapping was recorded before it was. Enrich SUP-702 from its stable CRM identifier, while leaving SUP-706 uncertain until its linkage is established. These are data corrections, not policy amendments.

Finally, report three outputs separately: structural conformance under the selected scope/model, policy-rule results with evidence and unknowns, and data-quality exceptions. This permits SUP-712 to fail despite structural fitness, SUP-711/SUP-713 to pass after justified normalization, and SUP-702 to remain uncertified until its exception conditions are evidenced. It preserves enforceable safeguards while making legitimate process and observation changes reviewable.
