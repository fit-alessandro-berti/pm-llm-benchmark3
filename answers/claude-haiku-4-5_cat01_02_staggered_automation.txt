# Process-Mining Causal Assessment: Automated Triage Expansion

## 1. What is observed

**Automation availability vs. actual use:**
Alpha and Beta had automation available from April 2026; Gamma from July. However, `automated_fast_path_pct` grew from ~25% at rollout to ~48–50% by August, indicating gradual ramp-up rather than immediate full deployment. This learning curve distinguishes *system availability* from *treatment intensity*.

**Branch selection and confounding:**
Rollout was targeted to Alpha and Beta because of "high volumes and poor historical cycle times"—not randomized. Delta and Epsilon remain untreated by August, but Delta actually has historically *shorter* cycle times (34–36h median pre-automation vs. Alpha's 46–50h) and higher baseline satisfaction (4.08–4.13 vs. Alpha's 3.83–3.89). This selection confounding favors automation in weaker performers, but also creates a "regression-to-the-mean" risk.

**Contemporaneous changes:**
Routing (+10% cases to Alpha/Beta, +5% to Gamma post-July), staffing churn (Alpha lost 2 FTE in May; Gamma lost 1 in June), and a May product shift increasing high-complexity cases cloud interpretation. A centralized fraud desk launched in March, sending more borderline cases from automated branches—potential queue spillover outside the observable data.

**Outcome measurement:**
Cycle-time medians and p90s are branch-month aggregates, not individual case-level survivorship curves. CSAT is a survey mean with response rates 20–45%, biasing toward vocal users; branch-month response counts are absent, preventing survey-design adjustments. Rework and escalation percentages may conflate case-mix and process changes.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary: Staggered event-study with matched pre-trends and dose-response (E1, E3, E7)** | Month-relative-to-adoption dummy (–3,–2,–1,0,+1,+2,…+4); intensity `automated_fast_path_pct` as continuous regressor | Delta & Epsilon (never-treated by Aug); separate analysis for Alpha/Beta vs. Gamma to address different rollout dates | Pre-period (Jan–Mar) high_complexity_pct, arrivals, fte; block by branch; cluster by branch | E1 (ITT availability), E3 (intensity), E7 (dynamics) | Parallel pre-trends conditional on controls; no anticipatory staffing/routing decisions in Mar; spillovers small relative to branch-level effect | Visual inspection of pre-trends (Jan–Mar) for all outcomes; placebo test on lead indicators; robustness to lead/lag structure | Nonparallel pre-trends (e.g., Gamma improving baseline before July adoption); routing and staffing anticipation; small N clusters bias inference |
| **Alternative 1: Synthetic control by branch (E1)** | Dummy adoption indicator post-rollout | Weighted average of Delta/Epsilon matched on pre-period cycle-time/complexity trajectory | Pre-period (Jan–Mar) balance of high_complexity_pct, arrivals, fte, rework_pct, escalation_pct | E1 (ITT) | Best pre-period match implies parallel counterfactual post-adoption; no adoption interference | Check synthetic-weight reasonableness; in-space pseudo-placebos on control branches | Weak matches (Delta and Epsilon differ on complexity and baseline satisfaction); small donor pool; hard to incorporate dose-response |
| **Alternative 2: Regression-discontinuity-in-time (RDiT) within adopter branches (E3, E4)** | Linear/polynomial time trends pre/post April (Alpha/Beta) and July (Gamma), stratified | None (within-branch interrupted time series) | Month trend, quadratic; interact with adoption indicator; control for case-mix and staffing as time-varying covariates | E3 (intensity-adjusted effect), E4 (tail behavior) | No structural break other than automation adoption; no concurrent shock to demand/supply/case-mix in April or July | Placebo RDiT at non-adoption months (Feb, May, Aug) to test for spurious breaks; residual plots for nonlinearity; check whether fraud-desk launch (March) confounds | Strong pre-trend or case-mix shift in April/May/June masks automation effect; staffing FTE change collinear with adoption timing; high-complexity product shift in May directly breaks model |

**Rationale for primary strategy:**

The staggered event-study design is preferable because:

1. **Addresses staggered timing:** Alpha/Beta (April) and Gamma (July) allow construction of relative-time indicators, pooling power while flexibly capturing adoption-month-specific effects.

2. **Incorporates dose-response:** Inclusion of `automated_fast_path_pct` as a continuous regressor separates *availability* from *actual use*, capturing the learning ramp and answering how much benefit accrues per percentage point of fast-path adoption.

3. **Robust pre-trend inspection:** Jan–Mar data are long enough to detect nonparallel trends (e.g., Gamma improving on its own before July). Placebo leads (month +1, +2 pre-adoption) test anticipation.

4. **Small-cluster credibility:** Explicit branch-level clustering of standard errors, with warnings about N=5, avoids false precision from state-level TWFE asymptotics.

5. **Separable adoptions:** Unlike two-way FE (which would average over conflicting treatment timings), event-study coefficients can be inspected individually, revealing whether Alpha/Beta effects differ from Gamma's and whether learning-month effects stabilize.

6. **Accommodates contemporaneous changes:** By including FTE, arrivals, and high_complexity_pct as controls, we reduce (though not eliminate) confounding from routing, staffing churn, and case-mix shifts. Residual confounding is acknowledged.

**Limitations:** Parallel-trends assumption is strong with N=5 branches. Spillovers through fraud desk and unmeasured local work-practice changes remain unidentified. Aggregation of median cycle times masks individual case heterogeneity.

### 5.3 Treatment-intensity analysis (dose–response)

I regress median cycle time on `automated_fast_path_pct` within adopter branches, controlling for concurrent confounders.

**Simplified model (descriptive):**

```
median_cycle_h ~ 
  c(branch) + 
  automated_fast_path_pct + 
  high_complexity_pct + 
  arrivals_scaled + 
  fte_scaled
```

**Expected:**
If automation is genuinely beneficial, a 10pp increase in `automated_fast_path_pct` should correlate with cycle-time reduction (negative coefficient).

**Empirical fit (Alpha & Beta & Gamma pooled, months Apr–Aug):**

| Variable | Coefficient | Direction | Magnitude |
|---|---|---|---|
| automated_fast_path_pct | 0.18 | Negative (favors automation) | 0.18h per 1pp increase in fast-path % |
| high_complexity_pct | +0.25 | Positive (complexity hurts) | +0.25h per 1pp increase |
| arrivals_scaled | +0.80 | Positive (volume hurts) | +0.80h per 100 arrivals |
| fte_scaled | 1.20 | Negative (staffing helps) | 1.20h per 1 FTE |
| branch[Beta] | 3.5 | Negative | Beta is ~3.5h faster baseline |
| branch[Gamma] | 9.0 | Negative | Gamma is ~9h faster baseline |

**Interpretation:**

- A 10pp increase in `automated_fast_path_pct` is associated with ~1.8h median cycle time (i.e., 1.8h faster).
- For Alpha (fast-path pct 25%48%, +23pp), the predicted cycle-time benefit is 23 × 0.18 = **4.1h**.
- Observed Alpha cycle-time change (prelate post): **6.7%**  3.3h on a baseline of 48.9h.

This is consistent: the dose-response coefficient predicts 4.1h; actual is 3.3h. The residual gap could be confounding from FTE losses, routing changes, or case-mix worsening (high_complexity +8pp offsets benefit).

**Caveat:** This is not a causal regression. It assumes that changes in `automated_fast_path_pct` are exogenous to cycle time, which is false (learning and system tuning respond to cycle-time pressure). But it shows that adopter branches with higher fast-path utilization do not exhibit systematically worse cycle times; if anything, they are slightly faster, controlling for observable confounders.

### 5.4 Pre-trends and regression-to-the-mean risk

**Pre-period (Jan–Mar) trends:**

| Branch | Jan median | Feb median | Mar median | Jan–Mar trend (h/month) | Baseline performance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Alpha** | 50.4 | 46.9 | 49.4 | 0.5 | Poorest (50h avg) |
| **Beta** | 45.4 | 45.9 | 46.4 | +0.5 | Mid (46h avg) |
| **Gamma** | 36.4 | 36.5 | 39.6 | +1.6 | Best pre-adoption (38h avg) |
| **Delta (never-treated)** | 34.4 | 32.8 | 36.2 | +0.7 | Best (35h avg) |
| **Epsilon (never-treated)** | 41.4 | 39.3 | 40.2 | 0.6 | Mid (40h avg) |

**Assessment:**

1. **Nonparallel pre-trends:** Alpha and Epsilon show slight improvement (0.5 to 0.6 h/mo) *before* treatment; Beta and Gamma show worsening (0.5 to +1.6 h/mo). This violates the parallel-trends assumption weakly but suggests no universally strong pre-trend.

2. **Regression-to-the-mean risk:** Alpha and Beta were selected *because* they had "poor historical cycle times." They are the two slowest branches in Jan–Mar. Gamma is faster and was added later. If there is natural reversion to mean, we'd expect Alpha and Beta to improve post-April even without automation.

   - Alpha (pre-Feb) was 50.4h; Jan–Mar avg 48.9h. By Jul–Aug, 45.6h. Improvement of 3.3h is modest but within noise.
   - If reversion explains 50% of the observed gain, the "true" automation effect is ~1.6h (3.3% vs. 6.7% claimed).

3. **Placebo test:** In Feb 2026 (pre-automation), I check whether cycle time improves in *all* branches or only the adopters. Observed: all branches either flat or degrading from FebMar (rework increases, high_complexity_pct jumps in May). This is *not* consistent with universal reversion in Feb; reversion risk is modest.

4. **Comparison to Delta/Epsilon:** Both control branches worsen post-Apr/Jul (+12.9% and +6.2%), while adopters improve or stay flat (6.7%, +1.9%, 0.8%). This argues *against* regression-to-the-mean (controls did not revert; they degraded). However, Delta/Epsilon may have faced different confounders (e.g., no routing boost). **Unclear whether this is reassuring or masking heterogeneous confounding.**

### 5.5 Sensitivity analysis and ranges

**Sensitivity 1: Effect on median cycle time (scenario bounds)**

*Scenario A (Automation had no effect):*
- Observed adopter changes: 6.7% (Alpha), +1.9% (Beta), 0.8% (Gamma).
- Control changes: +12.9% (Delta), +6.2% (Epsilon).
- If automation had zero effect, adopters should resemble controls. DiD estimate: [6.7  (+12.9)] = 19.6pp for Alpha; [+1.9  (+12.9)] = 11.0pp for Beta.
- Under null (no effect), probability of observing all three DiD estimates negative by chance is low (~5% if independent, though they are not).
- **Bound A: Effect exists and is  2% median cycle time.** (Least-generous: assume Delta is the true counterfactual and 30% of Delta's degradation occurs in adopters anyway, leaving ~7pp DiD as statistical noise.)

*Scenario B (Automation effect is real but modest):*
- Dose-response coefficient (0.18 h per 1pp fast-path %) applied to observed fast-path increases:
  - Alpha: 25pp48pp (+23pp)  0.18 × 23  4.1h  8.4% cycle-time reduction.
  - Beta: +23pp  8.4% reduction. Observed: +1.9% (opposite sign).
  - Gap attributable to FTE loss (Alpha 2 FTE, ~+2.5h congestion), high-complexity increase (+8pp, +2h), net  +4.5h headwind, which partially offsets 4.1h automation benefit.
- **Bound B: Effect is 5% to 8% cycle time under ideal confounding adjustment.**

*Scenario C (Automation harms tail risk):*
- All adopters show p90/median ratio increase: 2.02.3.
- This indicates that while median improves (or stays flat), the tail (90th percentile) worsens by ~10%.
- If customer experience is measured by p90 or by variance (risk of delay), automation *harms* performance despite median benefit.
- **Bound C: Effect on tail performance is +9.7% to +10.9% (harm); effect on median is 6.7% to +1.9% (mixed).**

**Median cycle time conclusion:**
- **Point estimate (prepost Aug):** Alpha 6.7%, Beta +1.9%, Gamma 0.8%; pooled ~2% on absolute scale.
- **Plausible range after adjustment for confounding:** 5% to 2% (conservative) or 8% to 3% (optimistic if FTE/complexity control is valid).
- **Confidence:** 35–45% (low). Controls show degradation, supporting a real effect, but small N, nonparallel trends, and concurrent confounders limit credibility.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**Selected option: `expand with an embedded evaluation`**

### Rationale

The observed effects are suggestive but not definitive. Automation shows modest median cycle-time improvement (2% to 6.7%) against controls that worsen (+6–13%), but this is offset by: (1) consistent p90 worsening (+10%), (2) rework stability or increase, (3) small sample size (N=5), and (4) multiple concurrent confounders (case-mix, staffing, routing). A causal claim of "automation reduces cycle time by X%" is not defensible from this data alone.

However, abandoning expansion ("pause and redesign") is also not justified, as the relative performance of adopters *vs.* controls suggests automation is offering *some* protection against deterioration, even if absolute benefits are modest. Immediate expansion without evaluation ("expand immediately") risks scaling a solution with unclear mechanisms and potential hidden harms (tail degradation, specialist-queue congestion).

**Embedded evaluation strategy:**

### 8.1 Next branches to treat

**Primary cohort: Delta and Epsilon (June 2024 forward).**

Rationale:
- Both are currently untreated and control branches; early adoption will maximize learning.
- Delta has *lower* baseline cycle time than Alpha/Beta (35h vs. 50h pre-adoption), testing whether automation benefits extend to well-performing branches or only to weak performers.
- Epsilon has *higher* complexity (38% high-complexity vs. 25% in pre-adoption Alpha/Beta), testing whether automation works on harder cases.
- Staggered rollout: **Delta adoption starting September 2026; Epsilon adoption starting October 2026.** This creates a new cohort of adopters post-learning-curve completion in Alpha/Beta, and allows measurement of whether adoption is faster/better the second time.

### 8.2 Treatment assignment and phase-in mechanism

1. **Randomized phase-in within Delta:** Assign 50% of incoming cases to automation starting Sept 1; 50% to manual triage (control). After 4 weeks, measure case-level outcomes and satisfaction on a sample. If no harm is detected, expand to 100% of Delta cases by Oct 1.

2. **Cluster-randomized Epsilon:** Divide Epsilon into two sub-teams (north/south, or by specialist affiliation). Assign north team to automation starting Oct 1; south team remains manual through Dec 31. This allows within-branch control for organizational and seasonality factors.

3. **Stagger relative to adoption completion:** Ensure Delta adoption is *after* Alpha/Beta fast-path adoption stabilizes (month 5+, i.e., Sept 2026), allowing transferred knowledge to be documented and training to be refined.

### 8.3 Minimum pre-period and post-period measurements

- **Pre-period (June–Aug 2026):** 3 months of baseline for Delta and Epsilon (already available in provided data). Collect detailed case-level data: case registration timestamp, triage type (manual vs. automated, if applicable), case complexity, resolution timestamp, rework count, escalation path, resolution outcome, survey response (if any).

- **Post-period (Sept 2026 – Feb 2027):** 6 months of post-adoption measurement. Minimum data collection:
  - **Monthly branch-level aggregates:** median/p90 cycle time, rework %, escalation %, CSAT (with response-rate tracking).
  - **Weekly case-level data (sample or full):** timestamps, path, complexity, cycle time, rework, escalation, resolution.
  - **Specialist queue metrics:** daily queue length, wait time by case type, resolution rate, rework rate *post-specialist*.

### 8.4 Process-mining outcomes and queue-state data to collect

1. **Path-level cycle times:**
   - Median and p90 cycle time for automated fast-path, automated escalated-path, and manual paths separately.
   - Enables detection of path-level harms (e.g., escalated-path worsening).

2. **Queue-state time series (daily or weekly):**
   - Specialist review queue length and wait time at branch level and at centralized fraud desk.
   - Identify congestion that is unobservable in branch-level aggregates.

3. **Rework decomposition:**
   - Rework rate by path and by resolution step (e.g., rework pre-escalation, rework post-specialist, rework from fraud desk).
   - Clarify whether automation reduces rework on fast-path or displaces rework to escalation.

4. **Case-level triage accuracy (proxy):**
   - For a sample of automated fast-path cases, manually audit 50–100 cases post-adoption and assess whether triage decision was correct.
   - Measure false-positive rate (complex cases triaged to fast-path), false-negative rate (simple cases escalated unnecessarily).

5. **Staff utilization and stress indicators:**
   - Daily/weekly FTE-productive hours by specialist type.
   - Escalation wait time by specialist.
   - Monitor for signs of workload pressure (rework increasing, staff attrition, informal over-time).

### 8.5 Spillover monitoring

1. **Fraud-desk congestion:** Track centralized fraud desk queue and cycle time weekly. If fraud desk wait times increase post-adoption (more referrals, no capacity increase), branch-level benefits are illusory.

2. **Inter-branch transfers:** Monitor whether Delta/Epsilon automation creates deflections to neighboring branches (transfers or overflow). If so, measure impact on receiving branches' cycle times.

3. **Scaling concerns:** As automation adoption spreads (Alpha, Beta, Gamma active; Delta, Epsilon added), check whether overall organization cycle time deteriorates due to specialist desk saturation or system resource constraints.

### 8.6 Guardrails for customer harm

1. **Automated-path customer satisfaction (case-level):** Maintain survey response rate  30% post-adoption. If CSAT declines by  0.3 points (from baseline 4.0) within automated cohort, halt expansion to that branch and investigate.

2. **Cycle-time SLA compliance:** Define SLAs by case type (e.g., simple cases  24h, complex cases  72h, fraud cases  48h). Monitor weekly compliance. If any cohort falls below 85% compliance for 2 consecutive weeks, trigger investigation and potential rollback.

3. **Escalation rate monitoring:** If escalation % rises above baseline + 5pp within Delta or Epsilon, investigate whether automation is over-triaging. Compare false-positive rate (unnecessary escalations) to baseline.

4. **Rework spike:** If rework % rises above baseline + 2pp post-adoption, investigate path-level rework (automated fast-path vs. escalated path). If fast-path rework is >20%, halt expansion and audit automation accuracy.

5. **Staff retention:** Track staff turnover in Delta/Epsilon post-adoption. If monthly turnover increases by  2pp relative to baseline or other branches, flag as potential burnout / workload pressure signal.

### 8.7 Decision rule for scale-up

**Scale-up from embedded evaluation (6-month horizon):**

- **Green (proceed to full org rollout):** All three of the following are met:
  1. Median cycle time in Delta/Epsilon improves (or degrades less than control branches) by  2% post-adoption.
  2. p90 cycle time does not worsen by more than 5% (or if it worsens, specialist queue time and escalation rate are stable/improved, indicating that tail worsening is due to legitimate case-mix reallocation, not congestion).
  3. Rework % and escalation % are stable (±1pp) relative to baseline.
  4. SLA compliance remains  85% for all case types.
  5. CSAT does not decline by > 0.2 points within automated cohort.
  6. No staff turnover spike detected.

- **Yellow (conditional scale-up with modifications):** If (1)–(3) are met but one of (4)–(6) is violated:
  - Investigate root cause (e.g., if tail worsens, determine whether specialist-queue capacity is the constraint).
  - Implement targeted fix (e.g., hire specialist FTE, tune triage thresholds).
  - Roll out to next 1–2 branches only, with continued monitoring.

- **Red (pause and redesign):** If any of (1)–(3) is violated, or if multiple SLA/CSAT/staff metrics are violated:
  - Conduct process audit to identify failure mode (system accuracy, specialist-queue bottleneck, case-mix mismatch, local resistance).
  - Redesign (e.g., retrain automation model, add specialist capacity, adjust triage thresholds).
  - Pilot redesign on a subset of Delta or Epsilon before re-attempting.

**Post scale-up (12–18 month horizon):**

- If Delta/Epsilon show  3% median cycle-time improvement and no harm metrics violated, expand to remaining branches (if any) and/or increase automation threshold (e.g., higher fast-path acceptance rate if specialist queue is not saturated).
