# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

**Position in one paragraph.** Neither submission should be chosen on its scores. Model S wins seven of the eight reported rows, yet it admits release without any credit or technical check. Model C wins the precision row, yet it rejects traces the policy calls valid and its precision is mostly a by-product of listing training variants. I propose a third model: a small partial-order (POWL) backbone in which the mandatory checks are truly mandatory, plus backbone-independent policy rules and an explicit "cannot be certified" state for the two attributes the log lacks.

**Use cases I distinguish, and how each changes the acceptable trade-off**

| Use case | What an error costs | Acceptable fitness/precision trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Exploration (what happens?) | A missed pattern costs insight, not money | High fitness with low precision is tolerable, provided nobody reads "the model replays it" as "it is allowed" |
| Conformance enforcement / audit (what is allowed?) | A false accept is an undetected control failure; a false reject is investigation effort and loss of trust | Precision must be measured against policy, not against the log. Fitness below 1 is expected and desirable if the log contains violations. Zero false accepts on hard rules outranks any fitness figure |
| Simulation / what-if | Wrong language gives wrong forecasts | Needs both: no forbidden paths and no missing valid ones, plus branching data. S would simulate skipped credit checks; C cannot simulate any combination it has not seen |
| Stakeholder communication | A misread model produces wrong decisions | Readers must be able to predict what the model permits; perceived ease is not enough |

**Assumptions**

- A1. Operators have PM4Py process-tree semantics: `->` sequence, `X` exclusive choice, `+` interleaved parallelism, `tau` silent step. "Accepts" means exact language membership (an alignment with no log move and no visible model move).
- A2. Where P1–P7 and the probes are crisp, they are the normative reference. The log is evidence of what happened, including possible violations and missing events (F4); it is not evidence of what is allowed.
- A3. The reported metrics are taken as honestly computed but unverifiable (no seeds, preprocessing, cost settings or holdout case list).
- A4. **Reviewer checks.** I rebuilt both expressions and my proposal in PM4Py 2.7.23.4 and computed only quantities that need no project data: language membership of the probes, net sizes, soundness, arc-degree simplicity, and two sanity checks on a synthetic log consisting solely of Model C's own eight sequences. These are marked *[reviewer check]*. I report no fitness, precision or generalization figure for the project log, because I do not have it.
- A5. Five policy points are open and I treat them as open rather than guessing silently:
  - Q1: may Supplier Review start before both mandatory checks are complete? (P3 gives no ordering.)
  - Q2: may a correction happen before both initial checks are done, or after Supplier/Expedite Review?
  - Q3: can a correction legitimately require no re-check at all?
  - Q4: does an Expedite Review stay valid if a check is repeated afterwards?
  - Q5: is Auto Approval restricted by case data (amount, sourcing, urgency)?

**Why I do not aggregate.** A simple vote gives S seven rows to one. That count is misleading because at least five of S's wins (both fitness rows, generalization, simplicity, runtime) follow from one property: few transitions, everything optional. They are one fact counted five times, and two of the rows are not reproducible. Any weighting would need the use case first; for enforcement, a single admitted forbidden trace outweighs all of them.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

**What S actually says.** After Register Dossier, any subset of {Credit Check, Technical Review, Supplier Review, Expedite Review} in any order, each at most once; then exactly one approval; then Release Order. That is 130 traces. By enumeration *[reviewer check]*, only 36 satisfy P2 and P4 even when Supplier Review may sit anywhere, and only 20 if Supplier Review must also follow the checks. At least 94 of 130 (72%) break a crisp policy.

**Strongest case for S**

- It is honest about its purpose (F7): an exploration map should replay nearly everything, and it does.
- It is small, has no duplicate labels, is sound, and its top-level skeleton is right: Register first, exactly one approval, Release last.
- It captures two true facts that C misses: the mandatory checks have no fixed order, and Supplier and Expedite Review are optional and combine freely with either approval.
- It accepts every probe the policy calls valid (B1, B2, B5, B7).
- Its tolerance of missing events matches a real data problem (F4).

**Strongest case against S**

- It cannot detect the two failures compliance cares most about: a skipped mandatory check and an expedite decision taken before the checks (F1).
- Its tolerance is selective. Approval, Register and Release are not optional, so a missing approval event is not tolerated while a missing Credit Check is. "Robust to missing events" is therefore not a principle of the model, only a side effect on four activities.
- It has no Correct Dossier and no repetition. It is too permissive and too strict at once: under S, B3 (no checks at all) scores fitness 1.0, while the legitimate rework trace that C lists as its seventh branch scores 0.727 *[reviewer check]*. Its fitness ranks a control bypass above honest rework.
- "Exploration only" labels do not survive contact with dashboards. A model with a 4.4 comprehension score will be reused as the reference picture.

**Concrete admitted behaviour that is undesirable or ambiguous**

| # | Trace admitted by S | Problem | Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order (B3) | Both mandatory checks skipped (P2) | Noise tolerance: skip on a mandatory activity |
| 2 | Register Dossier, Auto Approval, Release Order | Automatic approval with no evidence of any check | Noise tolerance, same mechanism |
| 3 | Register Dossier, Credit Check, Manager Approval, Release Order | Technical Review skipped (P2) | Noise tolerance, same mechanism |
| 4 | Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order (B4) | Expedite before the checks (P4) | Accidental: Expedite Review was placed inside the same parallel block as the checks; no missing event is involved |
| 5 | Register Dossier, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order | Expedite between the checks (P4) | Accidental, same flaw |
| 6 | Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Auto Approval, Release Order | A fast lane with no checks: two flaws compound | Both |
| 7 | Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order | Supplier Review before the checks: policy is silent (Q1) | Policy-ambiguous, admitted without anyone having decided |
| 8 | Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order, for an external order | Supplier Review missing where P3 requires it | Data gap; no control-flow model can see this, but S gives no hint that it is unchecked |

**Intentional tolerance versus accidental flaws.** The skips on Supplier and Expedite Review are correct modelling: P3 and P4 make them optional. The skips on Credit Check and Technical Review are the intentional tolerance, and they conflate "the event was not logged" with "the step may be skipped". The model cannot tell these apart, so every missing check is silently forgiven. The placement of Expedite Review and the absence of Correct Dossier have nothing to do with noise; they are modelling errors. S also rejects B8, which looks right, but only because it forbids every repetition, including legitimate ones: right answer, wrong reason.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

**What C actually says.** Exactly eight traces, no loop, no silent step, no concurrency.

**Strongest case for C**

- Every one of its eight traces is policy-valid (the seventh on condition that the correction affected both checks). It admits no forbidden probe.
- A finite whitelist is easy to audit line by line, and "unknown means review" (F8) is a legitimate stance in a regulated process.
- It is the only submission that contains Correct Dossier at all.
- It correctly rejects B3, B4 and B8.

**Strongest case against C**

- *Branch enumeration.* C lists sequences instead of stating rules. Of the 20 correction-free traces that satisfy P1–P5 under the strict reading, C contains 7 (35%). Stating the same policy by enumeration needs 20 branches without corrections, 80 more for one correction round and 320 more for two, since each round has four possible re-check sequences. The rule-based alternative in section 7 needs 13 visible transitions.
- *Accidental correlations.* Enumeration freezes coincidences of the training log into rules nobody stated: Auto Approval is allowed only with Credit Check first and with neither Supplier nor Expedite Review; Expedite Review only with Credit Check first; Technical-Review-first only with Manager Approval and never with Expedite Review. None of these is in P1–P7.
- *Duplicate labels.* 48 visible leaves for 9 activities. Every branch starts with the same label, so local replay faces an eight-way blind choice at the first event; this is what breaks token-based metrics for C (section 2). A change to one activity must be made in up to nine places, and ownership per branch is unclear (F3).
- *Unseen combinations.* B2 and B5 are valid by policy and are rejected. F4 reports exactly such cases in the holdout.
- *Correction handling.* One correction at most, only after Credit Check then Technical Review, always re-checking both in that fixed order, always ending in Manager Approval. P6 says corrections may repeat and that only affected checks are redone; F6 says the choice is not represented. C contradicts both.
- *Holdout degradation.* The drop from 0.973 to 0.744 is the signature of memorisation. It is also suspicious in size. Under C, B2 costs two alignment moves (fitness 0.80) and B5 three (0.75) *[reviewer check]*. A holdout mean of 0.744 would mean the average held-out case is as far from C as B5, which a random split of the same population should not produce. Either the split was not random, or the two figures are different statistics, or preprocessing differed. This must be explained before anyone relies on either number.
- *Maintainability.* Each newly approved combination is a new branch, and each policy change touches many branches. F2 and F3 are structural consequences, not teething problems.
- *Precision as an artefact.* Alignment precision asks how much model behaviour is unobserved in the log. A model that is a list of observed variants answers "almost none" by construction. The 0.941 says C is close to its training log; it says nothing about closeness to policy.

**On "audit-ready" and F8.** C rejects B2 for the same reason it rejects B3: not on the list. Its diagnostics cannot rank severity; under C, B5 (plausibly valid) and B3 (no checks at all) both score 0.75 *[reviewer check]*. An audit model that cannot distinguish a harmless reordering from a skipped control will flood reviewers and teach them to dismiss alerts. F8 also presumes the whitelist is complete with respect to approved behaviour, and the probes show it is not. Finally, C is as blind as S to the data rule: an external order without Supplier Review is simply its first branch.

# 5. Behavior-Probe Matrix

Neither expression contains a loop, and the silent steps in S are plain skips, so membership is decidable by inspection; there is no parsing or loop uncertainty for B1–B8. I confirmed each entry with alignments *[reviewer check]*. The only caveat is definitional: "accepts" is exact membership, not "fitness above a threshold". Under a threshold rule both models would wave through traces with fitness near 0.9, such as B8.

| Probe | Model S accepts? | Model C accepts? | Policy assessment | Consequence for review |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Yes | Yes (branch 1) | Valid | No discrimination |
| B2 | Yes | No: Auto Approval exists only after Credit Check then Technical Review | Valid | False reject by C; shows C encodes an accidental correlation |
| B3 | Yes: both checks skipped via silent steps | No | Forbidden | False accept by S; disqualifying for enforcement (F1) |
| B4 | Yes: Expedite Review is unordered relative to the checks | No | Forbidden | False accept by S caused by structure, not by noise tolerance |
| B5 | Yes | No: no branch combines Technical-Review-first, Supplier Review, Expedite Review and Auto Approval | Plausibly valid | False reject by C; the kind of case F4 reports in the holdout |
| B6 | No: Correct Dossier is not in the model and Technical Review cannot repeat | No: the only correction branch re-checks both, in fixed order | Policy-dependent | Neither model can express the decision (F6). S's rejection is an omission, not a judgement |
| B7 | Yes | Yes (branch 3) | Valid external case | No discrimination; neither model verifies that the order was external |
| B8 | No: each activity occurs at most once | No | Suspicious/forbidden | Both correct. S is correct only because it forbids all repetition |

On the seven crisp probes each model is wrong twice. S's two errors are false accepts of forbidden behaviour; C's two are false rejects of valid behaviour. The probes do not rank the models; they show that each fails in the direction that matters for its own claimed use.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

Ranked by likely impact on the board's decision, highest first.

| Rank | Pathology | Mechanism and evidence here | Direction of bias |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fitness rewarded by optionalising mandatory behaviour | Making Credit Check and Technical Review skippable turns every skipped or unlogged check into a perfect replay. B3 scores 1.0 under S | Inflates S on both fitness rows; turns its main defect into its headline number |
| 2 | Positive-log-only precision | No negative traces were used (F5). Precision penalises unobserved behaviour, not forbidden behaviour, and rewards a model for allowing any violation that happens to be in the log | Inflates C (memorisation scores as precision); makes S's 0.584 uninterpretable |
| 3 | Missing-event bias, including missing attributes | Supplier Review events may be missing (F4) and the sourcing attribute is absent. A missing event and a skipped step look identical. No reported metric can see an external order released without Supplier Review | Favours S, which absorbs all gaps; hides a whole class of violations from both models |
| 4 | Train/holdout leakage and split sensitivity | No seed, no case list, one split. A random case split puts the same frequent variants on both sides, so holdout fitness mostly re-measures training fitness and tests only the tail. If variant selection or thresholds were tuned after looking at the holdout, it is no longer a holdout. C's 0.229 drop is too large for a clean random split | Unknown sign; makes the most decision-relevant row (holdout fitness) untrustworthy |
| 5 | Generalization rewarded by excessive permissiveness | The token-based formula rewards few, frequently fired transitions. A flower-like model maximises it. It uses no unseen data | Inflates S, deflates C, for reasons unrelated to real generalization |
| 6 | Duplicated-label effects | Token replay cannot resolve C's eight-way choice on the first event; generalization of about 0.37 arises even on a log C fits perfectly *[reviewer check]*. The reported count of 33 matches no obvious definition | Deflates C on generalization; the count is noise |
| 7 | Silent-transition effects | Silent moves are almost free in alignments, so skipping a control costs nothing. Silent steps are invisible in the duplicate count and usually in the diagram shown to stakeholders | Favours S on fitness, duplicate count and comprehension |
| 8 | Alignment-cost sensitivity | With uniform costs, skipping Credit Check costs the same as a doubled Correct Dossier event. Fitness is normalised by each model's shortest run. Settings were not reported | Compresses severe and trivial deviations into similar scores; small cross-model distortions |
| 9 | Stakeholder-score methodology | Informal, self-reported, unknown sample and rendering. It measures perceived ease, not whether readers can predict what the model allows | Favours S; may reward the model whose defects are hardest to see |
| 10 | Runtime confounded by model size | Runtime tracks net size, share of non-fitting traces and hardware. Both figures are operationally fine | Favours S; should not influence the decision |

Two further points. The simplicity row could not be reproduced at all (section 2), so it belongs with rank 4 as a reproducibility failure rather than as a measurement. And pathologies 1, 5, 7 and 10 all stem from the same design choice in S, which is why its wins must not be counted separately.

# 7. Third Alternative

**Strategy.** A block-structured procedural backbone written in POWL, plus backbone-independent policy rules, plus two data rules that return an explicit "undetermined" result while the attributes are missing. The outcome per case is one of four verdicts instead of fits/does not fit. This answers the F2-versus-F8 dispute: unseen behaviour is neither silently accepted (S) nor automatically condemned (C); it is routed to review unless it breaks a stated rule.

## 7.1 Compact formal description

```text
model_third = PO(
  nodes = { REG, CC, TR, CORR, SUP, EXP, APPR, REL },
  order = { REG<CC, REG<TR, CC<CORR, TR<CORR,
            CORR<SUP, CORR<EXP, SUP<APPR, EXP<APPR, APPR<REL } )

CORR = LOOP( tau,
             ->( 'Correct Dossier',
                 X( 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review',
                    PO{ 'Credit Check', 'Technical Review' } ) ) )
SUP  = X( 'Supplier Review', tau )
EXP  = X( 'Expedite Review', tau )
APPR = X( 'Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval' )
```

Equivalent process tree: `->( REG, +(CC, TR), *(tau, ->(CD, X(CC, TR, +(CC, TR)))), +(X(SR, tau), X(ER, tau)), X(MA, AA), REL )`.

Facts about the model *[reviewer check]*: sound workflow net; 13 visible transitions for 9 labels (Credit Check and Technical Review each appear three times: initial check, single re-check, joint re-check); its correction-free language is exactly the 20 traces that satisfy P1–P5 under the strict reading; each correction round multiplies the options by four. It accepts all eight of Model C's sequences, so nothing C legitimately covers is lost.

## 7.2 PM4Py code for the main model

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

REG, CC, TR, SR, ER, CD, MA, AA, REL = (
    "Register Dossier", "Credit Check", "Technical Review", "Supplier Review",
    "Expedite Review", "Correct Dossier", "Manager Approval", "Auto Approval", "Release Order")


def build_third_model(supplier_review="optional"):
    """supplier_review: 'optional' (sourcing unknown), 'required' (external), 'absent' (internal)."""
    register, release = Transition(label=REG), Transition(label=REL)

    # Initial mandatory checks: both required, no skip, no order between them.
    credit, technical = Transition(label=CC), Transition(label=TR)

    # One correction round = Correct Dossier, then an explicit re-check decision:
    # credit only | technical only | both (any order). There is no "re-check nothing" branch.
    recheck_both = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[Transition(label=CC), Transition(label=TR)])
    recheck = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                           children=[Transition(label=CC), Transition(label=TR), recheck_both])
    correct = Transition(label=CD)
    correction_round = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[correct, recheck])
    correction_round.order.add_edge(correct, recheck)
    # LOOP(tau, round) = zero or more correction rounds.
    corrections = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP,
                               children=[SilentTransition(), correction_round])

    expedite = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                            children=[Transition(label=ER), SilentTransition()])
    approval = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                            children=[Transition(label=MA), Transition(label=AA)])

    nodes = [register, credit, technical, corrections, expedite, approval, release]
    if supplier_review == "optional":
        supplier = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                                children=[Transition(label=SR), SilentTransition()])
    elif supplier_review == "required":
        supplier = Transition(label=SR)
    elif supplier_review == "absent":
        supplier = None
    else:
        raise ValueError(supplier_review)
    if supplier is not None:
        nodes.append(supplier)

    model = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=nodes)
    model.order.add_edge(register, credit)
    model.order.add_edge(register, technical)
    model.order.add_edge(credit, corrections)        # corrections start only after
    model.order.add_edge(technical, corrections)     # both initial checks are done
    model.order.add_edge(corrections, expedite)      # P4: expedite after all (re-)checks
    model.order.add_edge(expedite, approval)
    model.order.add_edge(approval, release)
    if supplier is not None:
        model.order.add_edge(corrections, supplier)  # open question Q1: delete this edge to relax
        model.order.add_edge(supplier, approval)     # no edge supplier<->expedite: either order
    return model


model_third = build_third_model("optional")            # main model while sourcing is unknown
model_third_external = build_third_model("required")   # for use once the sourcing attribute exists
model_third_internal = build_third_model("absent")

net_third, im_third, fm_third = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)
```

POWL rather than a process tree is deliberate. If stakeholders answer Q1 with "Supplier Review may run alongside the checks", the change is the removal of one edge, and the result is no longer block-structured. In Model C the same change means writing new branches.

## 7.3 Declarative dictionary and explicit policy checks

The rules below are independent of the backbone, so a rule breach is a breach whatever the alignment says. The DECLARE dictionary holds the constraints whose PM4Py semantics I rely on. It is not sufficient alone: it cannot express "exactly one of two approvals", "Release Order is last", or P7 (it does not flag B8 *[reviewer check]*). The function `policy_checks` is therefore the authoritative rule set.

```python
_c = {"support": 1.0, "confidence": 1.0}
declare_third = {
    "init": {REG: _c},
    "exactly_one": {REG: _c, REL: _c},
    "existence": {CC: _c, TR: _c},
    "noncoexistence": {(MA, AA): _c},
    "precedence": {
        (CC, MA): _c, (TR, MA): _c, (CC, AA): _c, (TR, AA): _c,   # P2
        (CC, ER): _c, (TR, ER): _c,                               # P4
    },
}   # usage: pm4py.conformance_declare(log, declare_third)

VIOLATION, REVIEW, UNDETERMINED = "VIOLATION", "REVIEW", "UNDETERMINED"


def policy_checks(trace, sourcing=None, affected=None):
    """trace: list of activity labels in order.
    sourcing: 'external' | 'internal' | None (attribute missing).
    affected: None, or one set per Correct Dossier (in order), each a subset of {CC, TR}.
    Returns a list of (rule, severity, message)."""
    out = []
    pos = {a: [i for i, x in enumerate(trace) if x == a] for a in set(trace)}
    approvals = sorted(pos.get(MA, []) + pos.get(AA, []))
    gate = approvals[0] if approvals else len(trace)

    # H1 / H2 (P1)
    if not trace or trace[0] != REG or len(pos.get(REG, [])) != 1:
        out.append(("H1", VIOLATION, "Register Dossier must be the single first event"))
    if not trace or trace[-1] != REL or len(pos.get(REL, [])) != 1:
        out.append(("H2", VIOLATION, "Release Order must be the single last event"))
    # H3 (P5): exactly one approval event in total
    if len(approvals) != 1:
        out.append(("H3", VIOLATION, f"{len(approvals)} approval events; exactly one required"))
    # H4 (P2): both mandatory checks before the approval
    for a in (CC, TR):
        if not any(i < gate for i in pos.get(a, [])):
            out.append(("H4", VIOLATION, f"{a} not performed before approval"))
    # H5: the approval decision must be the last step before release
    if approvals and any(x != REL for x in trace[gate + 1:]):
        out.append(("H5", VIOLATION, "activity other than Release Order after approval"))
    # H6 (P4): expedite after both mandatory checks, before approval
    first_done = max(min(pos.get(CC, [len(trace)])), min(pos.get(TR, [len(trace)])))
    for i in pos.get(ER, []):
        if i < first_done:
            out.append(("H6", VIOLATION, "Expedite Review before both mandatory checks"))
        elif any(x in (CC, TR, CD) for x in trace[i + 1:gate]):
            out.append(("H6b", REVIEW, "re-check or correction after Expedite Review (Q4)"))
    if len(pos.get(ER, [])) > 1:
        out.append(("H6c", REVIEW, "Expedite Review repeated"))
    # H7 (P7): a repeated mandatory check needs a Correct Dossier since its previous occurrence
    for a in (CC, TR):
        idx = pos.get(a, [])
        for prev, cur in zip(idx, idx[1:]):
            if CD not in trace[prev + 1:cur]:
                out.append(("H7", VIOLATION, f"{a} repeated without preceding Correct Dossier"))
    # H8 / D2 (P6): what was re-checked after each correction
    cds = pos.get(CD, [])
    for k, i in enumerate(cds):
        end = cds[k + 1] if k + 1 < len(cds) else gate
        redone = {x for x in trace[i + 1:end] if x in (CC, TR)}
        if affected is not None and k < len(affected):
            if redone != set(affected[k]):
                out.append(("D2", VIOLATION,
                            f"correction {k + 1}: re-checked {sorted(redone)}, affected {sorted(affected[k])}"))
        else:
            if not redone:
                out.append(("H8", REVIEW, f"correction {k + 1} not followed by any mandatory re-check (Q3)"))
            out.append(("D2", UNDETERMINED, f"correction {k + 1}: affected-check attribute missing"))
    # H9: supplier review multiplicity / position (policy is silent on position: review only)
    srs = pos.get(SR, [])
    if len(srs) > 1:
        out.append(("H9", REVIEW, "Supplier Review repeated"))
    if srs and srs[0] < first_done:
        out.append(("H9b", REVIEW, "Supplier Review before both mandatory checks (Q1)"))
    # D1 (P3): supplier review conditioned on sourcing
    has_sr = any(i < gate for i in srs)
    if sourcing == "external" and not has_sr:
        out.append(("D1", VIOLATION, "external order without Supplier Review before approval"))
    elif sourcing == "internal" and srs:
        out.append(("D1", REVIEW, "Supplier Review on an internally sourced order"))
    elif sourcing is None:
        out.append(("D1", UNDETERMINED, "sourcing attribute missing: P3 cannot be certified"))
    return out


def verdict(trace, fits_backbone, sourcing=None, affected=None):
    """Three-valued outcome plus an explicit 'not certifiable' state."""
    sev = {s for _, s, _ in policy_checks(trace, sourcing, affected)}
    if VIOLATION in sev:
        return "VIOLATION"
    if REVIEW in sev or not fits_backbone:
        return "REVIEW"                    # unconfirmed behaviour: neither accepted nor condemned
    if UNDETERMINED in sev:
        return "CONFORMING_UNCERTIFIED"    # control flow fine, a data-dependent rule is not checkable
    return "CONFORMING"
```

Consistency of the two layers *[reviewer check]*: every trace of `model_third` up to 12 events (580 traces) raises no VIOLATION and no REVIEW from `policy_checks`. The backbone is therefore a subset of the rule-clean behaviour, and the gap between the two is exactly the REVIEW zone.

## 7.4 Explicit semantics

| Requirement | Semantics in the third alternative |
|---|---|
| Mandatory checks (P2) | Credit Check and Technical Review are plain, non-skippable, unordered nodes that both precede everything except Register Dossier. A missing check is a model move on a mandatory activity and rule H4. Tolerance for unlogged events lives in triage (was it performed but not recorded?), not in the model's language |
| Optional Supplier Review (P3) | Optional in the main model only because the sourcing attribute is missing. Rule D1 returns UNDETERMINED until it exists; afterwards `model_third_external` makes it mandatory and `model_third_internal` removes it. At most once; after the check phase in the backbone (Q1), anywhere before approval in the hard rules |
| Expedite Review ordering (P4) | Optional, at most once, after the whole check-and-correction phase, before approval, unordered relative to Supplier Review. Before the checks is a VIOLATION (H6); followed by a later re-check is REVIEW (H6b, Q4) |
| Exactly-one approval (P5) | Mandatory exclusive choice, immediately before Release Order. Zero or two approvals is H3; any activity between approval and release is H5. No restriction on which approval combines with which reviews, because no policy states one (Q5) |
| Correction and re-check (P6, P7) | Zero or more rounds. Each round is one Correct Dossier followed by an explicit three-way decision: re-check credit, technical, or both. ## 7.5 Limitations

- **Sourcing attribute missing.** The main model cannot detect an external order released without Supplier Review, and neither can S or C. The difference is that this model says so on every case instead of implying coverage. If Supplier Review events are also missing from the log (F4), even the attribute will not settle it without reconciliation against the source system.
- **Affected-check attribute missing.** The model accepts any non-empty re-check choice after a correction. It can therefore accept a case that re-checked the wrong check. Until D2 can be evaluated, correction cases are never fully certified.
- **Open questions are encoded conservatively, not resolved.** Supplier Review before the checks (Q1), corrections before both initial checks or after the reviews (Q2), corrections without re-check or two corrections in a row (Q3), and re-checks after Expedite Review (Q4) all fall outside the backbone and land in REVIEW. If such cases are frequent and legitimate, the review queue will be large until the backbone is extended.
- **Duplicate labels remain.** Credit Check and Technical Review each appear three times. This is deliberate (initial check versus re-check roles) but token-based replay is unreliable on this model too. Evaluate it with alignments only.
- **No resource or timing rules.** Segregation of duties and Auto Approval eligibility are not modelled because the question gives no data for them.
- **Untested on the project log.** Everything above is derived from policy and from the two expressions. Section 9 is the test.

## 9.1 Reproducible splits with seeds and published preprocessing

- Publish the preprocessing function, the count of events and cases before and after each step, the PM4Py version, and SHA-256 hashes of the sorted train and holdout case identifiers.
- Run at least 30 seeds. Report mean, standard deviation and worst seed for every measure, not one split.
- Add a temporal split (oldest 80% of cases for training) to expose drift, which a random split hides.

```python
import hashlib
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from pm4py.algo.conformance.alignments.petri_net import algorithm as alignments
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import Trace, Event

CASE, ACT, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
ACTIVITIES = [REG, CC, TR, SR, ER, CD, MA, AA, REL]
STD = 10000   # PM4Py default cost of one log move or visible model move


def prepare(df):
    """Every step is part of the published protocol; counts are logged, nothing is dropped silently."""
    report = {"events_in": len(df), "cases_in": df[CASE].nunique()}
    df = df[df[ACT].isin(ACTIVITIES)].sort_values([CASE, TS], kind="stable")
    report.update(events_out=len(df), cases_out=df[CASE].nunique())
    return df, report


def _sha(ids):
    return hashlib.sha256("\n".join(map(str, sorted(ids))).encode()).hexdigest()


def seeded_case_split(df, seed, train_frac=0.8, stratify_col=None):
    """Case-level split; optional stratification on a case attribute such as sourcing."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    strata = (df.groupby(CASE)[stratify_col].first() if stratify_col
              else pd.Series("all", index=sorted(df[CASE].unique())))
    train_ids = []
    for _, grp in strata.groupby(strata, dropna=False):
        ids = sorted(grp.index)
        rng.shuffle(ids)
        train_ids += ids[: int(round(train_frac * len(ids)))]
    train_ids = set(train_ids)
    train, test = df[df[CASE].isin(train_ids)], df[~df[CASE].isin(train_ids)]
    manifest = {"seed": seed, "train_frac": train_frac, "stratify": stratify_col,
                "train_sha256": _sha(train_ids), "test_sha256": _sha(set(df[CASE]) - train_ids),
                "pm4py": pm4py.__version__}
    return train, test, manifest


def temporal_split(df, train_frac=0.8):
    start = df.groupby(CASE)[TS].min().sort_values()
    train_ids = set(start.index[: int(train_frac * len(start))])
    return df[df[CASE].isin(train_ids)], df[~df[CASE].isin(train_ids)]
```

## 9.3 Unseen-combination tests

Hold out whole variants, so that no holdout variant occurs in training. This measures what the holdout row was supposed to measure. A model derived from training data (such as C rebuilt on each split) should be re-derived inside each split.

```python
def variants_of(df):
    return df.groupby(CASE)[ACT].agg(tuple)


def variant_disjoint_split(df, seed, test_frac=0.2):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    v = variants_of(df)
    uniq = sorted(set(v))
    rng.shuffle(uniq)
    held = set(uniq[: max(1, int(round(test_frac * len(uniq))))])
    test_ids = set(v[v.isin(held)].index)
    return df[~df[CASE].isin(test_ids)], df[df[CASE].isin(test_ids)]
```

## 9.6 Curated negative trace suite, missing-event and duplicate-event perturbations

- The suite is owned by compliance and the process owner, versioned, and written before results are seen. Seed it with B1–B8, T1–T8 and the eight traces of section 3; each entry is labelled accept, reject or policy-dependent with the policy clause that justifies it.
- Perturbations are generated from known-valid traces: delete each mandatory activity, duplicate an event, swap two neighbours. Expected outcomes come from the rules, not from any model.
- Report false accepts and false rejects as lists of trace names, not as a rate.

```python
def accepts(model, traces):
    """dict name -> bool; accepted iff the optimal alignment has no log move and no visible model move."""
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    out = {}
    for k, tr in traces.items():
        res = alignments.apply_trace(Trace([Event({ACT: a}) for a in tr]), net, im, fm)
        out[k] = res["cost"] < STD
    return out


def suite_confusion(model, suite):
    """suite: dict name -> (trace, expected), expected in {'accept', 'reject', 'policy-dependent'}."""
    got = accepts(model, {k: tr for k, (tr, _) in suite.items()})
    out = {"false_accepts": [], "false_rejects": [], "policy_dependent": {}}
    for k, (_, exp) in suite.items():
        if exp == "reject" and got[k]:
            out["false_accepts"].append(k)
        elif exp == "accept" and not got[k]:
            out["false_rejects"].append(k)
        elif exp == "policy-dependent":
            out["policy_dependent"][k] = got[k]
    return out


def perturbation_suite(valid_traces, seed):
    """Mutants of known-valid traces; the expectation is set by the policy rules."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    mutants = {}
    for name, tr in valid_traces.items():
        for a in (CC, TR, MA, AA, REG, REL):
            if a in tr:
                mutants[f"{name}|drop:{a}"] = [x for x in tr if x != a]
        i = int(rng.integers(0, len(tr)))
        mutants[f"{name}|dup@{i}"] = tr[: i + 1] + [tr[i]] + tr[i + 1:]
        j = int(rng.integers(0, len(tr) - 1))
        swapped = list(tr)
        swapped[j], swapped[j + 1] = swapped[j + 1], swapped[j]
        mutants[f"{name}|swap@{j}"] = swapped
    labelled = {}
    for k, tr in mutants.items():
        sev = {s for _, s, _ in policy_checks(tr)}
        labelled[k] = (tr, "reject" if VIOLATION in sev
                       else "policy-dependent" if REVIEW in sev else "accept")
    return labelled
```

def weighted_alignment(model, trace, model_move_cost, log_move_cost, tau_cost=1):

## 9.8 Stakeholder comprehension with a documented protocol

- Participants: at least five each from compliance, operations, IT and process ownership; none from the modelling teams.
- Material: the three models in the same notation and tool, order randomised, origin hidden.
- Task: for 16 traces (the probes plus adversarial traces), predict whether the model allows the trace; then name the model element that would have to change for two given policy changes.
- Measures: prediction accuracy, time, and stated confidence, so that confident wrong answers are visible. The 1–5 self-rating may be collected but is reported separately.
- Pass mark, fixed in advance: for example at least 80% accuracy on the forbidden traces. A model that readers believe forbids B3 when it allows it fails, whatever its rating.

## 9.9 Decision rule: rejection despite good aggregate metrics

A model is rejected for enforcement, regardless of any score, if any of the following holds:

1. it is unsound or cannot execute an activity that exists in the process;
2. it accepts any trace labelled reject in the curated suite (zero tolerance);
3. it rejects any trace labelled accept in the curated suite;
4. its worst-seed holdout share of fitting rule-clean traces is below a floor agreed in advance with operations;
5. its results cannot be reproduced by a third party from the published seeds, code and case hashes;
6. it fails the comprehension pass mark on forbidden traces.

```python
def gate(structural, hard_negatives, positives, split_reports, min_holdout_fit_pct):
    """split_reports: one quality_report per seed, computed on the rule-clean cases of each holdout."""
    reasons = []
    if not structural["sound"] or structural["activities_not_executable"]:
        reasons.append("unsound model, or a process activity that the model cannot execute")
    if hard_negatives["false_accepts"]:
        reasons.append(f"accepts forbidden traces: {hard_negatives['false_accepts']}")
    if positives["false_rejects"]:
        reasons.append(f"rejects policy-valid traces: {positives['false_rejects']}")
    worst = min(r["fitness_test"]["percentage_of_fitting_traces"] for r in split_reports)
    if worst < min_holdout_fit_pct:
        reasons.append(f"worst-seed holdout fitting-trace share {worst:.1f}% is below the agreed floor")
    return ("REJECT", reasons) if reasons else ("ELIGIBLE", [])
```

Applied to the probes alone, criteria 1 and 2 already reject S (no Correct Dossier; accepts B3 and B4) and criterion 3 rejects C (rejects B2 and B5). The third alternative passes criteria 1–3 on the probes; criteria 4–6 are untested.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

**Model S: exploration only, under conditions; retired from any enforcement, simulation or compliance-reporting role.**
- Every rendering carries the statement "replay tolerance, not policy: this model allows release without Credit Check or Technical Review".
- Its fitness figures may not be quoted as conformance rates.
- It should be superseded for exploration as well once the third alternative is validated, since deviations from a correct backbone are more informative than perfect replay on a permissive one.

**Model C: retire as a normative model. Keep its eight sequences as positive regression tests.**
- It may not be used for compliance: it rejects policy-valid behaviour, cannot express selective re-checks, and cannot rank the severity of what it rejects.
- All eight sequences are accepted by the third alternative, so nothing is lost by retiring it.

**Compliance: neither original. Two steps.**
- *Immediately:* rules H1–H7 can run as detective controls. They restate P1, P2, P4, P5 and P7, need no discovered model, and depend on none of the open questions.
- *After validation:* the third alternative, as backbone plus rules plus verdict states.

**Conditions the third alternative must meet before deployment**

1. Q1–Q5 are answered in writing by compliance and the process owner, and the backbone is updated accordingly.
2. Zero false accepts and zero false rejects on the curated suite, which must have grown beyond the probes in this review.
3. The programme in section 9 is run with published seeds and reproduced by someone outside both discovery teams, including an explanation of Model C's holdout drop and of the non-reproducible simplicity figures.
4. The sourcing and affected-check attributes are obtained, or the audit function formally accepts that P3 and P6 are reported as uncertified. No report may present CONFORMING_UNCERTIFIED as conforming.
5. A shadow period in which the REVIEW volume is measured; operations confirms it can absorb the queue, and a sample of REVIEW cases is adjudicated to tune the backbone.
6. The comprehension protocol is passed.
7. One named owner, version control, and a rule that any change to the backbone must rerun the curated suite.

# 11. Dissenting View

The recommendation replaces two measured models with an unmeasured one. Whatever the flaws of the reported figures, S and C were at least run against the real log; the third alternative has been tested only against eight probes and the reviewer's own adversarial traces, which the reviewer's own design unsurprisingly passes. It embeds five unanswered policy questions as conservative guesses, so its first contact with production data may send a large share of ordinary cases to review. Worse, with both data attributes missing, almost every case ends as "conforming, uncertified", a label that compliance cannot act on and operations will learn to ignore. The design also has three artefacts to keep consistent: a partial-order model, a declarative dictionary and custom rule code. That is harder to govern than a flat list of approved sequences which any auditor can read. Whitelisting is standard practice in regulated processes, and Model C could be repaired cheaply by adding the approved variants that the probes revealed. Model S, for its part, never claimed to enforce anything. Finally, several of the reviewer's criticisms rest on recomputations in an environment that may differ from the teams' own. Retiring both models on this basis is premature; extending C and keeping S for exploration would be the lower-risk path.

**Evidence that would change my decision**

- *Towards keeping C:* the complete set of approved behaviour turns out to be small and stable (a few dozen variants, corrections rare and uniform), and an extended whitelist shows zero false rejects on the curated suite and a stable holdout result across seeds.
- *Towards keeping S for longer:* a documented need for pure exploration with no reuse as a reference, plus evidence that most missing-check cases are logging gaps rather than skipped checks.
- *Against the third alternative:* a shadow run in which the REVIEW queue exceeds what operations can handle and adjudication shows most of it is legitimate; answers to Q1–Q5 that make the backbone as branch-heavy as C; or a comprehension test in which readers predict its behaviour no better than C's.
- *Against my metric criticisms:* reproduction of the teams' simplicity, duplicate-count and holdout figures from their own code and case lists, with a definition that makes them consistent with the two expressions.
