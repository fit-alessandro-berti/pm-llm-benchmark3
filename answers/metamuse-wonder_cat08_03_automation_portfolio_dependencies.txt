## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

**Upstream data quality dependencies**
- A1 -> A2 -> routing: Registration feeds classification. Automating A1 before fixing classification errors (8%) and document checklists simply delivers misrouted cases faster. A2 should remain a *recommender* with logged override, especially for High-exposure SME.
- A5 -> A6 -> Credit analysis -> Committee packaging: Financial document review -> Request missing documents (3,300) -> Financial document review (2,800) is the dominant rework loop. A6 extraction on incomplete or low-quality PDFs fails or creates analyst checking work. A5 first raises complete-at-first-review rate; A6 then normalizes; only then is Normalize -> Credit analysis (5,000) a reliable feed.
- Concurrency caveat: Financial document review and KYC are recorded sequentially (Classify -> KYC: 7,250; Classify -> Financial document review: 3,940) but often run concurrently. Do not design automation as a strict serial gate between them; design parallel tracks that *synchronize* at Committee packaging and at signing.

**Legal / compliance controls**
- Committee packaging waits for four artifacts: KYC status, normalized financial statements, collateral valuation if required, and credit-analysis notes. A8 cannot create the pack faster than the slowest input.
- Customer signing legally requires prior KYC clearance. Provisional signing (6.5%, 1.1% remediated) must be made technically impossible in any A10/A9 workflow, not merely discouraged.
- Contract terms must match Committee decision (Committee decision -> Contract preparation: 3,650; Contract preparation -> Customer signing: 7,300, inflated by remediation returns). A9's rule library is therefore a prerequisite for safe A10 and A11.
- Resolve entity mismatch -> Contract preparation: 380 is a red flag variant: it suggests some cases bypass or shortcut back through KYC. Any A4 design must force Resolve -> KYC re-check (already 1,160) where clearance was affected, and prohibit direct progression with unresolved identity.

**Human escalation capacity**
- Compliance specialists: only 3 FTE, dependent on 2 seniors. A3/A4 do not remove this pool; they *shift* it from routine searching to exception adjudication. If hit volume routed to them rises faster than routine work falls, waiting (already 31.0h for Resolve) worsens. Cap auto-referral rates and protect senior time.
- Credit analysts: 9 FTE, central in working-together network. A6 saves 75 min/case but also increases throughput into Credit analysis (110 min service, 28.0h wait, judgment-heavy for High-exposure). Keep High-exposure analysis manual; redeploy saved normalization time to analysis and to Committee packaging quality, and monitor queue, not just minutes saved.
- Back office 12 FTE and Legal ops 4 FTE: Back office slack after 15:00 and Legal ops month-end overload mean labor saving  FTE removal. Expect demand to shift to exception handling, customer chasing quality, and month-end smoothing.

**External valuation dependency**
- Credit analysis -> Collateral valuation request (2,400) -> Wait for external valuation (2,350, 58.0h) -> Committee packaging (1,980). The bank cannot automate the valuer, but can: (a) trigger valuation in parallel with Credit analysis once A2 confirms requirement, rather than after it; (b) triage by product — mandatory for 71% Equipment finance, selective for 18% Working-capital; (c) route by valuer latency/quality and escalate the 19% late. A7 is orchestration, not elimination of the wait.

**Core-banking integration constraints**
- Only two deep integrations in months 0–6. Reserve them for A6 (document/credit-model data) and A4 (KYC/entity status in core workflow). A5 runs largely in portal/CRM, A9's deep contract/core link lands in months 7–12. A1, A10, A11 are comparatively easy integrations — which is precisely why they tempt a wrong sequence.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

**Portfolio Q — Quick-win: A1, A9, A10, A11**
Optimizes visible, high-frequency labor (11,880 registrations; 7,600 contracts; 7,100 activations) and political momentum. Sacrifices process performance and safety: pushes work into Compliance and Analyst bottlenecks, automates signing/activation before KYC and contract controls are reliable. Fails the spirit of the compliance condition despite A9.

**Portfolio B — Bottleneck / synchronization: A5, A6, A4, A7**
Optimizes end-to-end cycle time by attacking loops (missing documents), the scarce analyst pool, the 31h entity-mismatch critical path, and the 58h external valuation synchronization point. Sacrifices headline labor savings and the registration quick win; benefits appear as waiting/rework reduction, harder to claim as FTE savings. Only partially satisfies the control-strengthening requirement (via A4).

**Portfolio C — Compliance-first: A2, A3, A9, A12**
Optimizes control integrity: classification to correct control path, KYC hit triage with audit, contract-decision matching, structured remediation. Directly addresses provisional signing, 8.5%/14% contract remediation, and four-eyes enforcement support. Sacrifices cycle time on the two biggest waiting pools — document loops and normalization — and loads the 3-FTE specialist pool with A3/A4-type exceptions without A5/A6 relief upstream. Also consumes both deep integrations on compliance tooling.

## 4. Expected process-level effects

**Portfolio Q — Quick-win**
- *Cycle time:* Little end-to-end gain; possibly worse. Registration saves minutes against waits of 18–58h elsewhere.
- *Waiting time:* Shifts queues: Classify/KYC (4.0h) and Credit analysis (28.0h) waits rise as A1 inflow accelerates.
- *Rework:* Unchanged document loop (3,300/2,800) and entity loop (1,160); contract remediation may fall via A9 but defects arrive faster.
- *Handovers:* More, faster handovers into scarce pools; Legal ops month-end overload intensifies.
- *Resource demand:* Back office demand falls (already slack after 15:00); Compliance specialists and analysts overloaded.
- *Control violations:* **Worsens risk:** A10+A11 layered on 6.5% provisional signing and incomplete controls; A11 executes errors.

**Portfolio B — Bottleneck / synchronization**
- *Cycle time:* Largest reduction. A5 cuts repeated review cycles; A4 removes 31h critical-path waits in 82% of affected cases; A7 parallelizes/triages the 58h valuation wait for Equipment finance.
- *Waiting time:* Falls at Financial document review (18.0h), Normalize (20.0h), Resolve (31.0h), Wait for external valuation (58.0h); Committee packaging (26.0h) improves because its four inputs arrive earlier and reconciled.
- *Rework:* Missing-document repeats (52%) and Resolve -> KYC loops fall; analyst re-checking of poor extractions is the main offset — controlled by confidence thresholds.
- *Handovers:* Fewer RM -> Back office -> Analyst chasing loops; RMs instead own complete customer instructions.
- *Resource demand:* Analyst time shifts from normalization to judgment; specialist time shifts to genuine exceptions; total exception volume must be monitored, not assumed away.
- *Control violations:* Neutral-to-positive via A4, but does not by itself fix provisional signing or contract-decision mismatch — its weakness.

**Portfolio C — Compliance-first**
- *Cycle time:* Modest gain at Contract preparation; may *lengthen* KYC in the short term as A3 surfaces hits previously handled informally.
- *Waiting time:* Resolve/KYC waiting improves only if specialist capacity holds; document and valuation waits (18–58h) largely untouched.
- *Rework:* Contract remediation loops (650/510) and misrouting rework fall; recorded remediation may initially rise as A12 makes issues visible — a good sign, not failure.
- *Handovers:* Clearer escalation handovers Compliance ops -> specialists with audit trail; more formal, slightly slower.
- *Resource demand:* Increases demand on 3 specialists and Legal ops rule maintenance; analyst bottleneck persists.
- *Control violations:* Strongest reduction: hard KYC-before-signing logic in A9/A12 design, contract-decision matching, four-eyes checks for High-exposure packaging.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

- **Auditability:** Every automated step logs model/rule version, suggestion, confidence, human override and final decision. No silent auto-clearance in A2/A3/A4. Logs retained to compliance standard; overrides sampled weekly.
- **False negatives (A3/A4 — highest severity):** Pre-classifier may only *prioritize and pre-populate*, never finally clear a hit. Random sample of auto-low-risk hits receives full manual review; false-negative rate is a stop-gate metric. Compliance retains accountability — automation is decision support.
- **False positives (A3/A5/A9):** Excess referrals flood 3 specialists; rigid document checklists frustrate customers and RMs. Compensate with referral-rate caps, RM feedback loop on checklist items, and customer-tested portal wording.
- **Model/rule drift (A2/A6/A9):** PDF quality, product mix and legal rules change. Scheduled revalidation, extraction-accuracy sampling by document source, versioned legal rule library owned by Legal ops with expiry dates, and drift alarms on override rates.
- **Exception handling shift:** Automation removes routine work and concentrates hard cases. Staffing models must assume *higher average handling time per remaining case* for specialists and analysts; measure exception queue age separately from routine queue.
- **Four-eyes compliance:** System-enforced separation: for High-exposure SME, Credit analysis and Committee packaging cannot be completed by same user ID; hard block at month-end too, since violations (5.8%) cluster under rush. A8, when built, must not let the analyst assemble their own pack unchecked.
- **KYC-before-signing:** Hard system gate in signing workflow; provisional signing route removed, exceptions only via documented compliance waiver. A10 reminders blocked until clearance flag set.
- **Garbage-in/garbage-out (A8/A9):** Contract/pack generation refuses, rather than papers over, missing or inconsistent artifacts; refusal routes to A12-type remediation queue.
- **External valuer quality (A7):** Dual metrics — latency *and* dispute/revision rate — so routing does not reward fast, poor valuations.

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

Recommended portfolio: **Portfolio B amended — A5, A6, A4, A9** (A7 delivered initially as a manual triage/parallel-trigger discipline; full A7 module and A8, A3 scaling in year 2). This keeps four modules, attacks loops and synchronization, and satisfies compliance via A9 (with A4 strengthening KYC integrity).

**Phase 0 — Instrument and guardrail (Months 0–2, no deep build)**
- Baseline dashboards from process-mining artifacts; remove provisional-signing pathway; enforce four-eyes system check; start valuation parallel-trigger SOP for Equipment finance.
- *Metrics:* Provisional signing %, four-eyes violations, baseline waits/rework per DFG edge.

**Phase 1 — Loops and critical path (Months 1–6; deep integrations #1 A4, #2 A6 pilot)**
- A5 checklist/portal nudging (light integration) by product, co-designed with RMs to limit friction. A4 assistant for specialists: pre-fetch entity evidence, propose match, human decides.
- *Gate to proceed:* Repeated missing-document rate down from 52%; Resolve median wait falling from 31h; no rise in specialist exception queue age; customer complaint/friction within tolerance.

**Phase 2 — Analyst capacity (Months 4–9)**
- A6 extraction/normalization with confidence thresholds; low-confidence PDFs route to analysts unchanged (no forced automation). Redeploy saved analyst time to Credit analysis queue.
- *Gate:* Extraction accuracy on sampled statements at agreed threshold by document type; Credit analysis wait (28h) not deteriorating despite higher clean inflow; analyst overtime stable.

**Phase 3 — Control-strengthening downstream (Months 7–12; deep integration now available)**
- A9 contract generation with Committee-decision matching and KYC-clearance hard gate; A12-lite remediation queue embedded in A9 refusals.
- *Gate:* Contract remediation below 8.5% baseline (and renewal below 14%) in pilot segments; zero contracts generated with incomplete KYC; legal rule library sign-off current.
- Explicitly **deferred:** A11 activation bot and full A10 push until two consecutive quarters with provisional signing ~0 and remediation at target; A1 revisited only if downstream waits have fallen enough to absorb faster inflow.

## 7. Final recommendation

**Recommend the amended bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A5, A6, A4, A9, in that order.**

The highest-frequency activity should **not** be automated first. Register application (11,880 cases) looks attractive on frequency, but it has 8-minute service time, 1.5h wait, 4% rework, and sits in the one pool (Back office, 12 FTE) with demonstrable slack after 15:00. Automating it first buys minutes, spends political capital, and accelerates cases into the real constraints: 3 Compliance specialists facing a 31h entity-mismatch critical path, and 9 Credit analysts trapped in a 3,300/2,800 missing-document loop and 75-minute manual normalization. That is local optimization that degrades end-to-end cycle time.

Conversely, A4 has one-seventh the frequency of registration but dominates outcomes for its cases (82% critical path), A5/A6 attack the rework loops and synchronization inputs that starve Committee packaging, and A9 converts a downstream high-frequency activity into a control point — fixing contract-decision mismatch and hard-gating KYC before signing — rather than merely a faster bot. A11 and A1 remain legitimate year-2 candidates, but only after the controls and upstream data quality they depend on are demonstrably reliable. The trade-off accepted is slower headline labor savings and a less photogenic first win, in exchange for shorter end-to-end cycle time, fewer loops and handovers, protected scarce experts, and a portfolio compliance can defend.
