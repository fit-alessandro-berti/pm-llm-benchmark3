# 6. Residual vulnerabilities

- **Redesign-induced priority inversion**: The dynamic time-decay priority rule (Intervention A) can cause Complex-panel cases to be delayed behind Urgent cases when M1 is fragmented by late-arriving Urgent samples. This creates a hidden SLA breach risk for Complex-panel orders that is not captured by the 18h Urgent penalty metric.
- **Single-point specialist dependency**: Specialist review remains unavailable every second Friday. Even with partner lab outsourcing (P2), traceability complexity and 12h transport delay create audit risks and potential result reconciliation failures during overlapping peaks.
- **Fatigue-driven validation errors**: The evening medical-validation shift (Intervention C) assumes linear productivity. In practice, fatigue increases validation error rates, which feed back into Repeat-after-failure loops and consume assay capacity twice, partially negating the queue relief.
- **Courier network fragility**: Staggered intake (Intervention D) depends on courier route flexibility. If regional transport collapses (e.g., winter storms), the lab loses the ability to smooth arrivals, causing sudden pre-analytical compression that overwhelms the triage gate.
- **Recollection loop externalization**: Early rejection (Intervention B) shifts error resolution to physicians. If clinician response times slow, recollection coordination consumes clerk time again, recreating the intake bottleneck under a different name.
