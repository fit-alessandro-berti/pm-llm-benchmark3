## 1. Feasibility model

Partial-order relation set:

```text
Register < Triage
Triage < Identity Check
Triage < Originals Receipt              if originals required
Originals Receipt < Document Check      if originals required
Triage < Document Check                 if originals not required
Identity Check < Assessment
Document Check < Assessment
Assessment < Decision                   if no final Compliance Review required
Assessment < Compliance Review (final) < Decision
                                              if high risk or high value
Request More Info < Document Check (next) < Assessment (next)
Assessment (next) < Compliance Review (final, new or reaffirmed) < Decision
                                              if high risk or high value
Decision < Notify
Escalation Advice (preliminary) and Compliance Screen (preliminary):
  may be parallel with Identity Check / Document Check after Triage,
  do NOT satisfy final Compliance Review and do NOT precede final Assessment
Identity Check || Document Check        allowed if no originals required
Identity Check || Escalation Advice (advisory) allowed
```

Conditional and declarative constraints:

```text
if high risk or high value:
  exists Compliance Review (final) after latest Assessment and before Decision
else:
  Compliance Review is not mandatory for Decision
if originals required:
  Originals Receipt < Document Check
  Identity Check may precede Originals Receipt
if Request More Info occurs:
  new Document Check and new Assessment are required before Decision
  high risk/high value then requires new or explicitly reaffirmed final Compliance Review
four-eyes: resource(final Assessment) != resource(final Compliance Review)
preliminary compliance screen != final Compliance Review
no resource performs two service intervals at the same time
queue snapshots and service-time ranges are forecasts, not guarantees
Decision < Notify in all cases
```

Timing only: K-411 mainly re-times task creation and allows already-permitted concurrency between Identity Check and Document Check. Routing changes: K-104 adds preliminary escalation and preliminary compliance nodes and reserves C1 capacity, while the final mandatory order is unchanged; K-219 reassigns Document Check from D1 to D2. Decision-policy change: K-337 forbids a second Request More Info loop and forces Decision on incomplete evidence, changing the admissible terminal decision rather than only compressing time.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism: Identity Check completed Mon 12:50 and Document Check completed Tue 05:40, but the sanctions ambiguity was not escalated at Triage. It was rediscovered at Assessment Tue 15:10-19:10, Escalation Advice ran Tue 19:20-21:20, and C1 then started Compliance Review only Wed 13:10, reading most evidence from scratch and finishing Wed 17:40. The critical path was therefore Document Check plus Assessment queue plus late escalation plus C1 queue and de novo reading.

Minimal intervention: At Triage, send a preliminary packet to S1 and create a provisional C1 reservation, allowing S1 advice and preliminary C1 reading to overlap Identity and Document Checks. No mandatory final step is removed: final Compliance Review remains after final Assessment and before Decision, performed by C1 and therefore different from A1.

Temporal propagation: S1 advice becomes available during the long D1 queue rather than after Assessment, so Assessment no longer needs to wait for escalation logic, but Assessment still cannot start before Document Check completes Tue 05:40 and still faces the Tue Assessment queue that produced the factual 15:10 start. The main saving is downstream: if preliminary C1 reading is reusable, final Compliance service falls by roughly the guidance range of 1-3 h, and if the provisional slot is genuinely earlier than Wed 13:10, the C1 wait also falls. Earlier readiness for Compliance therefore translates into earlier Decision only when the reserved slot, not merely the packet, moves.

Process-feasibility check: Preliminary compliance may occur before Assessment, but it cannot replace final compliance; the path Register  Triage  parallel checks and preliminary work  Assessment  final Compliance  Decision  Notify satisfies the partial order and four-eyes rule because A1 assesses and C1 reviews. S1 advisory work may overlap other checks, and I1, D1, S1, and C1 are distinct resources, so overlap is feasible provided each resource's own intervals do not overlap.

Best/base/worst case: Best case, the packet identifies the sanctions issue on Mon, preliminary C1 reading is complete, the reserved slot is Tue evening or Wed morning, final compliance takes about 2 h, and Decision moves roughly 10-16 h earlier. Base case, preliminary work saves 1-3 h and the slot moves only a few hours earlier, giving 5-10 h earlier Decision on Wed. Worst case, the packet is incomplete, C1 must re-read, the reserved slot is missed or unusable, and the case is 0-4 h later while other urgent compliance cases lose C1 time.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Queue logs showing the provisional C1 slot did not start earlier than Wed 13:10; C1 preliminary notes that were not reused; a packet missing the document versions later assessed; or records showing displaced urgent cases suffered larger delays than K-104 gained.

### K-219

Factual mechanism: Triage routed the handwritten originals case to D1, but D1 did not start Document Check until Tue 09:30, about 16 h after originals arrived Mon 17:30. D1 completed in 4 h, Assessment followed Tue 20:00-22:30 after Assessment queue waiting, and M1 decided Wed 08:30. The long gap between Originals Receipt and Document Check start was the main controllable delay.

Minimal intervention: Assign Document Check to D2 immediately on Mon evening receipt of originals, leaving Identity Check, Originals Receipt precedence, Assessment, Decision, and Notify unchanged. No compliance review is required because the case is neither high risk nor high value.

Temporal propagation: D2 can start Mon evening rather than Tue 09:30, but its standard service is slower, about 4-8 h versus D1's factual 4 h, so completion might be Mon 22:00 to Tue 02:30, roughly 11-15 h earlier than factual readiness. That earlier readiness does not guarantee immediate Assessment: the Tue Assessment queue, about A2 6 h and A1 9 h at 08:00, will absorb part of the gain, and M1 availability may further quantize Decision into Tue evening or Wed morning. Hence final cycle improvement is plausibly smaller than the Document Check start improvement, with a discrete downside if D2 requests clarification.

Process-feasibility check: Originals Receipt still precedes Document Check, Identity Check still precedes Assessment, Document Check still precedes Assessment, and Decision still precedes Notify. D2 is a distinct resource from I2 and A2, so no resource overlap is created if D2's Mon evening interval is genuinely free; if D2's queue at 17:30 is still about 5 h, the start must be delayed accordingly rather than assumed immediate.

Best/base/worst case: Best case, D2 starts Mon about 18:00, reads handwriting accurately, finishes before Tue 03:00, Assessment runs Tue daytime, and Decision is Tue evening, about 12-18 h earlier. Base case, D2 starts later or takes 6-8 h, Assessment queue absorbs several hours, and Decision improves by about 5-14 h. Worst case, D2 misreads handwriting, Request More Info triggers external collection of 8-36 h plus repeat Document Check and Assessment, and the case finishes 10-30 h later than factual.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: D2 handwriting error or clarification rates close to or above D1's on comparable cases; D2 queue release times showing no Mon evening capacity; Assessment queue data showing Tue daytime assessment was unavailable; or post-pilot rework rates high enough that expected rework time exceeds the start-time saving.

### K-337

Factual mechanism: Supplier totals were inconsistent. After Document Check 1 and Assessment 1, a first Request More Info loop ran Wed to Thu; one inconsistency remained after Document Check 2 and Assessment 2 Fri 10:30, triggering a second loop over the weekend until Mon 09:00, then Document Check 3 Mon 13:00-16:30, Assessment 3 Tue 08:00-10:30, Compliance Review Tue 15:30-19:30, and Decision Wed 08:30. Calendar time was dominated by external response and weekend waiting, not by pure service time.

Minimal intervention: After Assessment 2, prohibit the second Request More Info loop and require Compliance Review followed by Decision using available evidence. The Decision must explicitly approve with enforceable conditions or reject in light of the unresolved discrepancy; it may not silently ignore the discrepancy or issue the same unconditional approval as factual.

Temporal propagation: Deleting the second loop removes the Fri 10:30 to Mon 09:00 external wait, Document Check 3, the overnight wait to Assessment 3, and the wait to Tue Compliance Review. Compliance Review can move to Fri afternoon or Mon morning if C1 capacity is available, and Decision can follow Fri evening or Mon morning. The saving is therefore mostly calendar and queue waiting rather than service-time saving, which explains why the apparent gain is measured in days even though the removed service work is only a few hours.

Process-feasibility check: The counterfactual path still has Document Check 2 and Assessment 2 before Compliance Review, final Compliance Review before Decision, and Decision before Notify. Four-eyes remains satisfied because A1 performed Assessment 2 and C1 performs Compliance Review. However, feasibility is not equivalent to defensibility: the path is process-feasible only as a conditional approval or rejection that confronts the discrepancy, not as an unchanged unconditional approval.

Best/base/worst case: Best case, Compliance Review runs Fri, M1 approves with a tight enforceable cap or holdback, no appeal or loss materializes, and cycle time falls about 90-115 h. Base case, C1 or M1 availability pushes Decision to Mon, saving about 60-95 h, with a conditional decision and residual risk. Worst case, the discrepancy is material, Compliance cannot defend the decision, the case is appealed, reversed, rejected and reapplied, or losses occur, so risk-adjusted total time and cost exceed factual despite a much shorter recorded cycle.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Evidence that the unresolved amount is small relative to exposure and reliably bounded by conditions; supplier history showing the second confirmation would not have changed totals; Compliance and Legal opinion that conditional approval is defensible; or appeal and loss data from similar forced decisions showing no increased adverse outcomes.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: At Triage the legacy system created only the Identity task. Identity Check ran Tue 11:30-13:20, a case lock prevented Document Check ownership until after Identity completion, Document Check ran Tue 15:00-19:30, Assessment waited until Wed 09:30-12:00 as the Assessment queue grew, and Decision followed Wed 14:00. Verification was artificially serialized although the case had no originals requirement and digital standard documents.

Minimal intervention: At Triage create both Identity and Document tasks, allow I2 and D2 to work concurrently under a controlled workaround, and require real-time start and completion timestamps rather than later batch entry. Assessment, Decision, and Notify are unchanged.

Temporal propagation: If both resources are available from roughly the factual Identity start time, Document Check readiness moves from Tue 19:30 to roughly Tue 15:00-16:00, about 4 h earlier. But Assessment cannot begin before both checks complete and then faces the Wed Assessment queue; the case may joins that queue earlier and perhaps be assessed Wed morning rather than at 09:30, yet the gain is compressed by queue position and M1 Decision availability. Thus a 4 h verification gain plausibly becomes a 3-6 h final gain, not a guaranteed 4 h or more.

Process-feasibility check: Rule 3 expressly permits concurrent Identity and Document Checks when no originals are required. I2 and D2 are distinct resources, so true overlap is feasible; same-resource overlap or identical start and completion stamps would indicate false concurrency. No Compliance Review is required for this low risk and low value case, and Decision still follows Assessment and precedes Notify.

Best/base/worst case: Best case, both checks start Tue morning, Document finishes early afternoon, Assessment is pulled into Tue evening or early Wed, and Decision improves by about 5-8 h. Base case, resource queues delay one check slightly and Assessment queue absorbs part of the gain, giving 3-6 h earlier Decision. Worst case, the workaround still serializes work in practice, or the case arrives to a congested Assessment queue no earlier in effective order, giving only 0-2 h improvement while adding logging risk.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Resource rosters showing I2 and D2 were not simultaneously free; timestamp audits showing batch entry or same-person execution; Assessment queue release data showing the case could not have started earlier even with Tue readiness; or evidence that simultaneous verification increased Document Check errors or rework.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 early escalation and preliminary compliance | S1 and C1 work moved earlier; final C1 service may fall if preliminary reading is reusable | Assessment issue discovery for this case; final Compliance reading time | Compliance queue for other cases when C1 is reserved; S1 queue at Triage | Urgent compliance cases compete for sole C1; other high risk cases may lose escalation attention | Mixed, negative if reservation displaces urgent finals | Sole certified C1; incomplete packets waste reserved capacity |
| K-219 assign D2 instead of D1 | Document demand shifts from D1 to D2 and from Tue to Mon evening; D1 freed Tue | D1 queue after Mon; this case's Document start wait | D2 queue, especially if many cases substitute | Other standard cases assigned to D2 wait longer; complex corporate cases may benefit from freed D1 | Mixed, depends on D2 error and rework rate | Discrete rework loop if handwriting is misread |
| K-337 suppress second rework loop | Avoids Document Check 3 and Assessment 3; Compliance and Decision moved earlier | Document and Assessment queues after Fri for other cases | Compliance queue on Fri or Mon; post-decision appeal and audit workload | Other cases gain D1 and A1 slightly earlier; future cases face appeal or reapplication load | Positive for measured cycle, negative for risk-adjusted system | Weekend external wait dominates; decision risk is deferred, not removed |
| K-411 parallelize verification | Identity and Document demand peak simultaneously at Triage; total service roughly unchanged | This case's verification waiting time | Assessment queue earlier in week; simultaneous I and D workload | Cases behind this one in Assessment queue may be displaced; other verification cases may lose I2 or D2 if peaks collide | Mildly positive if concurrency is real | Bunching at Assessment; timestamp overlap can be false |

Analyzing each intervention singly, all four move work earlier, but none removes mandatory work, so benefit depends on whether earlier readiness meets an idle downstream resource or merely joins its queue earlier. Reserving C1 for K-104 is the sharpest interference: C1 is the sole certified reviewer, early-week queues are 16-20 hours, and a provisional slot for evidence not yet final displaces urgent cases whose final reviews cannot be pre-worked as easily. Preliminary reading may save 1-3 hours later, but an incomplete packet consumes that saving in re-reading, while displaced cases lose unrecoverable calendar time. This is queue displacement, not capacity creation.

Substituting D2 for D1 in K-219 moves demand from D1's long queue to D2's shorter Monday queue and frees D1 on Tuesday for complex corporate work. The nonlinearity is quality: D2 is slower standard work and much slower on difficult handwriting, so a small rise in clarification probability creates a discrete second Document Check and Assessment loop, plus 8-36 hours of external collection. Case-level saving is thus bimodal, not a mean shift, and at 100 cases D2's queue will refill.

K-411 and K-104 both push cases sooner into Assessment, already queued 4-12 hours and growing mid-week, so earlier verification yields diminishing final gains as cases bunch at A1/A2 and the bottleneck migrates downstream. K-337 avoids a rework loop but transfers supplier risk to Compliance, Decision, and later appeal or loss, outside the measured cycle; its large gain is mainly deleting a Friday-to-Monday external wait, not saving service hours.

Finally, timestamps are not concurrency. Parallel task creation without genuinely available resources, or batch entry later, will show overlap without capacity or audit value and will overstate pilot benefit.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-411 parallel verification. It uses a concurrency already permitted for non-original, standard cases, changes no compliance threshold, keeps the same identity and document resources, and its benefit, while modest, does not depend on optimistic service-time or external-response assumptions. Its main failure mode, poorly logged false concurrency, is detectable and correctable through real-time timestamp validation, and it is easily reversible case by case. K-104 is second because preliminary escalation and compliance preparation preserve the final Assessment-then-Compliance order and four-eyes rule; it can save real reading time, but its value is hostage to packet completeness and to displacement of the sole compliance reviewer.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite the largest apparent speed gain. Suppressing the second Request More Info loop does not resolve the supplier discrepancy; it forces Decision to price, condition, or reject on incomplete evidence. The measured cycle shortens by days, mainly by deleting a weekend external wait, but the risk is exported to appeal, loss, and audit outcomes that may arrive later and cost more than the saved days. A conditional approval can be defensible only if the condition is enforceable and explicitly tied to the unresolved amount.

Local counterfactuals are least transportable for K-104 and K-219 at scale. Reserving C1 and substituting D2 for D1 look beneficial only because other cases are held fixed; at 100 cases the reserved and substituted queues refill, bottlenecks migrate to Assessment, and average gains shrink toward zero. The most valuable additional data are resource-level queue release times and actual start/completion timestamps for every task, including who worked, whether work was truly concurrent, preliminary versus final compliance minutes, and post-decision appeal or rework outcomes, linked by case so local speed can be separated from displaced delay and deferred risk.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. structural — The partial order given in normative rules is complete: mandatory final Compliance Review after latest Assessment for high risk or high value cases, and new Document Check plus Assessment after any Request More Info.
2. temporal — Queue snapshots approximate resource release times, and overnight or weekend availability follows the factual pattern rather than a strict business-hours calendar.
3. resource — D1 is faster on complex corporate documents, D2 is a generalist with higher handwriting risk, C1 is the only certified compliance reviewer, and S1 advisory work can overlap other checks.
4. behavioral — Staff act on preliminary packets without anchoring the final decision, and applicants or suppliers do not change response behavior merely because a loop is prohibited.
5. quality — D2's handwriting disadvantage increases clarification probability but does not make accurate reading impossible, and the supplier discrepancy in K-337 is material enough that it cannot be silently ignored.
6. interference — Single-case counterfactuals hold other cases approximately fixed, whereas system-wide rollout requires separate accounting for queue displacement, bottleneck migration, and displaced urgent cases.
7. temporal — External rework collection takes 8-36 h and weekend waiting is calendar time, so deleting a Friday Request More Info loop saves days even when service-time saving is small.
8. structural — Preliminary compliance work may save 1-3 h of final review but never satisfies the final Compliance Review or the four-eyes requirement by itself.
