**1. System boundary and assumptions**

Environmental scope is gate-to-gate order-fulfillment impacts captured in the enriched event log: machine `energy_kwh` (CNC_A/B, heat_treat, inspection_cell), `transport_kgco2e` by `route_mode` (parcel_ground, parcel_air, dedicated_van, consolidated_truck), `material_scrap_kg` from production/rework, and `package_material_kg`.  

Inside the boundary: Availability check through Ship/Confirm, all rework loops (production and packaging), batching decisions (`batch_id`/`batch_size`), packaging choice, route selection, partial shipments, emergency production, and cancellations due to delay.  

Outside the boundary: upstream raw-material extraction and primary part manufacturing (beyond observed scrap), customer-side use/installation, end-of-life of parts or packaging, facility HVAC/lighting/overhead, employee commuting, and embodied carbon of capital equipment. Empty-return emissions for dedicated vans are only partially observable.  

Assumptions: log attributes are reliable relative proxies; `transport_kgco2e` already embeds mode-specific factors; `promised_lead_time_hours` plus historical reliability support probabilistic SP1 checks. Residual uncertainty remains on absolute LCA characterization factors, precise empty-return rates, disposal impacts of the observed 6 % obsolescence, and future carrier-disruption frequency that affects resilience policy R1.

**2. Sustainability-related process indicators**

All indicators are directly derivable from the supplied event attributes, DFG frequencies, and path statistics:

- Energy: average `energy_kwh`/order and per item by `case_type`, `machine_group` and `batch_size`; total energy on high-volume paths (MTO batch-wait vs immediate).  
- Transport: average `transport_kgco2e`/order by `route_mode` and segment; share of air / dedicated_van / consolidated_truck; SP1 conformance rate (ground or consolidated when P(meet LT) > 85 %).  
- Materials: average `material_scrap_kg`/order; `package_material_kg` by packaging type; obsolescence rate (% of batch-produced items unshipped within 14 days).  
- Rework: `rework_flag` rate; frequency of Rework-production and Rework-packaging loops in the DFG; scrap attributable to rework.  
- Batching: `batch_size` distribution; median Batch-wait by size and segment; energy-per-item versus wait trade-off; heat-treatment batch late-delivery multiplier (1.7×).  
- Service: median and p90 cycle time; late-delivery % by segment and path; partial-shipment rate.  
- Quality: rework rate by machine (CNC_A 9 % vs CNC_B 18 %); damage/return proxy via packaging rework and Q1 conformance; inspection defect-escape rate.  
- Resilience: % of Critical cases carrying explicit SP2 justification; alternative-route availability for Service-contract customers (R1); cancellation-due-to-delay rate; volume and outcome of sustainability exceptions.

**3. Intervention options**

Eight-plus options are analysed below, each mapped to observed paths/segments and grounded in the DFG, path performance table, batching evidence, transport evidence and policy findings.

**S1 – Green shipping recommender**  
Target: primarily Standard-stock Service-contract (16 % share, avg transport 7.8 kgCO₂e, 22 % SP1 violations) and MTO Regular with slack; decision point Package → Ship.  
Mechanism: at shipping, combine `promised_lead_time_hours`, historical route reliability and segment to recommend ground or consolidated_truck whenever P(meet LT) > 85 % (SP1). Prefer consolidation when feasible.  
Service/quality/resilience: controlled late risk via the probability threshold; priority picking may be paired for Service-contract; air remains available as R1 backup. Quality neutral.  
Monitor: SP1 conformance rate, `route_mode` distribution by segment/slack, `transport_kgco2e`, late % on recommended vs overridden cases, `expedite_flag` correlation.

**S4 – Priority pick + ground for Standard-stock Service-contract**  
Target: the air subset of Standard-stock Service-contract (concentrated in the 5 % Stock → standard-pack → air path).  
Mechanism: enforce SP1; insert priority picking so ground still meets LT with high probability; air only when remaining slack is insufficient.  
Service/quality/resilience: cycle time rises toward the ground median (≈31 h) but stays inside most promised windows; late risk controlled by the priority buffer; air option retained for true urgency (R1). Quality neutral.  
Monitor: air share and SP1 violation rate in the segment, cycle-time/late % after priority pick, `transport_kgco2e`.

**S5 – Twice-daily consolidated-truck departures for non-urgent stock**  
Target: Standard-stock Regular (and high-slack Service-contract) currently using individual ground (Package standard → Consolidated truck already 850 cases).  
Mechanism: fixed twice-daily consolidation windows for orders with sufficient LT slack; evidence shows 35–55 % lower `transport_kgco2e` versus individual parcels at the cost of a 12–24 h wait.  
Service/quality/resilience: cycle +12–24 h only for selected high-slack orders; late risk rises only if selection is poor; flexibility slightly reduced for Regular customers but R1 alternatives remain. Quality neutral.  
Monitor: consolid share, consolidation-wait time, `transport_kgco2e` reduction, late % consolid vs ground.

**S2 – Dynamic batching with segment-specific maximum waiting-time caps**  
Target: MTO paths (Plan production → Batch wait → CNC/heat; energy 16–32 kWh). Caps tighter for Service-contract, looser for Regular.  
Mechanism: target batch sizes ≥10–12 (CNC_A energy/item falls from 2.8 kWh to 1.3 kWh; heat treatment lowest above 10) while hard-capping Batch wait (e.g., 12–24 h Regular). Limits the 6 % obsolescence observed on oversized batches.  
Service/quality/resilience: wait and late risk controlled versus the current 38 h / 1.7× late multiplier; capacity preserved for the 760 emergency-production cases. Quality neutral-to-positive (more stable runs).  
Monitor: `batch_size` distribution, energy vs size, Batch-wait duration, late % by segment, % items unshipped >14 d.

**S3 – Prefer CNC_A over CNC_B when queue and due-date slack allow**  
Target: MTO CNC production (especially the 5 % path that ends in rework).  
Mechanism: CNC_A rework rate 9 % versus CNC_B 18 %; fewer Rework-production → CNC loops (470 cases) cut both energy and the high-scrap path (1.8 kg).  
Service/quality/resilience: slight cycle increase possible if A queues, but slack check prevents late; overall late lower because rework paths currently show 32 % late. CNC_B retained for rush/emergency (resilience). Quality clearly improved.  
Monitor: `machine_group` assignment versus slack, `rework_flag` and `material_scrap_kg` by machine, cycle/late for MTO.

**S6 – Packaging–route compatibility checks before shipment**  
Target: Package standard/reinforced → Ship and the Rework-packaging loops (260 + 360 cases).  
Mechanism: pre-check geometry/weight/`route_mode` compatibility; eliminates most packaging rework and aligns with Q1 (reinforced for >8 kg). Reinforced already reduces damage returns 40 %.  
Service/quality/resilience: fewer rework delays; better protection; resilience neutral.  
Monitor: packaging `rework_flag`, `package_material_kg`, Q1 conformance, DFG Rework-packaging frequency.

**S7 – Inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations**  
Target: Inspection → Rework production (470) and the high-rework CNC_B/MTO combinations; addresses the noted Inspection_cell queue problem that lets defects escape.  
Mechanism: additional or slowed inspection for known high-risk combos reduces false passes, cutting rework loops, scrap and later returns.  
Service/quality/resilience: small added inspection time offset by elimination of long rework paths (160 h median, 32 % late); quality markedly better; selective application preserves Critical speed.  
Monitor: post-inspection `rework_flag` by machine, scrap, return proxies, inspection duration, DFG loop frequency.

**S9 + S8 – Split Critical-downtime into true versus precautionary; replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes**  
Target: Critical-downtime Service-contract (11 %, avg transport 38 kgCO₂e; dedicated-van path 8 % at 52 kgCO₂e).  
Mechanism: true downtime retains dedicated van or emergency production; precautionary uses priority ground/stock or shared multi-stop courier lanes (better load factor, fewer empty returns). SP2 justification required.  
Service/quality/resilience: true-Critical late % (currently 3–4 %) protected; precautionary accepts modest cycle increase still inside promised windows; shared lanes improve scalability while R1 alternatives remain. Misclassification is the main residual risk.  
Monitor: new true/precautionary attribute, % dedicated vs shared, transport and late % by subcategory, SP2 justification completeness.

**S11 – Reduce partial shipments by tightening availability-check rules**  
Target: Partial-shipment path (7 %, 24 % late, dual transport 7.2 kgCO₂e).  
Mechanism: predictive or hold-for-full rules when slack exists → single shipment, lower total transport/energy/handling and fewer Backorder-wait → Cancel cases.  
Service/quality/resilience: overall late risk falls; risk of higher cancellations if rules are over-strict; Service-contract resilience improved by avoiding splits.  
Monitor: partial frequency, Backorder-wait, dual-shipment transport, cancel rate, late %.

**S12 – Sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides**  
Target: all segments, especially the observed 22 % SP1 and 31 % SP2 gaps.  
Mechanism: forces documented justification and considered alternatives; enables audit and learning.  
Service/quality/resilience: indirect improvement by making trade-offs explicit and supporting R1.  
Monitor: exception volume by policy/segment, post-override outcomes, conformance trend.

**5. Rebound and displacement risks**

1. **Batching energy versus waiting/obsolescence**: larger batches lower energy per item but, if caps are too loose, Batch wait rises (4 h → 38 h), late risk multiplies 1.7× on heat treatment, and the observed 6 % obsolescence produces extra scrap or disposal emissions; later rush re-production can erase the original gain.  
2. **Transport-mode shift versus service/resilience**: ground or consolid cuts kgCO₂e (air 4–6×, consolid 35–55 %), yet lower reliability or consolidation wait can increase late deliveries, triggering subsequent expedite_flag, emergency production or dedicated vans—displacing emissions to higher-impact modes. Service-contract R1 alternatives may be stressed under carrier disruption.  
3. **Packaging material versus damage returns**: more aggressive use of reinforced packaging (to cut the 40 % damage-return reduction) doubles `package_material_kg`; under-use relative to Q1 produces returns that generate secondary transport and rework energy/scrap.  
4. **Rework reduction versus capacity queues**: shifting to CNC_A and tighter inspection gates cut scrap and loops, but added inspection time or A-queue growth can push load into emergency paths or lengthen Batch wait elsewhere, displacing energy into higher-impact variants.  
5. **Consolidation wait versus later expedites**: the 12–24 h consolid wait can push borderline orders into lateness, forcing air or dedicated transport later and recreating the emissions that were avoided.  
6. **Critical split / shared-lane customer or capacity risk**: classifying true downtime as precautionary, or capacity contention on shared lanes during multi-site spikes, can produce unacceptable customer downtime, emergency-production spikes, or loss of R1 alternative-route availability.  
7. **Partial-shipment reduction versus cancellations/stock-outs**: stricter availability rules cut dual shipments but can raise Cancel-due-to-delay volume or force more Made-to-order/emergency production (higher energy and scrap).  
8. **Carbon-aware promise or green-recommender behavioural rebound**: longer negotiated dates or ground recommendations for Regular MTO may be refused, increasing cancellations, competitor switching, or internal pressure to override SP1 more frequently.

**6. Balanced recommendation**

Implement a coherent redesign package, not isolated green tactics:

- Deploy the green shipping recommender (S1) together with priority-pick + ground enforcement for Standard-stock Service-contract (S4) and twice-daily consolidation for high-slack Regular stock (S5). This directly attacks the 22 % SP1 violations and the high-transport air and dedicated-van volumes while preserving air and dedicated options for true Critical and tight-slack Service-contract cases.  
- Introduce dynamic batching with hard segment-specific wait caps (S2) and preferential routing of MTO work to CNC_A when slack permits (S3). Energy and scrap fall without unconstrained waiting or loss of emergency capacity.  
- Add packaging–route compatibility checks (S6) and an inspection quality gate on high-rework combinations (S7). These close the observed rework-packaging and rework-production loops that currently dominate material impact.  
- Split Critical-downtime into true versus precautionary categories (S9) and migrate suitable precautionary and some justified Critical movements onto shared emergency courier lanes (S8). True-downtime service levels and R1 alternative routes stay intact.  
- Tighten availability-check rules to reduce partial shipments (S11) and institute a mandatory sustainability exception log for every SP1/SP2/Q1 override (S12).  

The package is deliberately segment-aware: Critical and Service-contract receive protected fast paths; Regular and high-slack orders absorb the larger environmental gains. Rework, returns and obsolescence are explicitly controlled rather than ignored. No pure lowest-emission option that would violate service or resilience thresholds is selected. The net result is material reductions in energy, transport CO₂e and scrap while cycle-time, late-delivery and resilience indicators remain within acceptable bounds for each segment.

**7. Validation and governance plan**

After phased rollout (by warehouse or region):

- Re-mine the enriched event log with the original attributes plus new fields (true/precautionary flag, recommender suggestion versus actual route, exception_id, shared-lane indicator).  
- Conformance dashboards: SP1 violation rate (target well below the current 22 % on Standard-stock Service-contract), SP2 justification completeness (below the current 31 % missing), Q1 compliance on heavy MTO.  
- Segment-level service monitoring: median/p90 cycle time, late-delivery %, energy/transport/scrap by `case_type` × `customer_type`; Critical true-downtime late % must not deteriorate.  
- Path-level comparison: pre/post statistics on the high-volume variants (Stock–standard–ground, MTO–batch–CNC_A–ground, Critical–shared/dedicated).  
- Exception governance: weekly review of the S12 log—volume, reasons, post-override outcomes (late, rework, kgCO₂e); escalate systemic patterns.  
- Rebound triggers: automatic pause or rollback if any segment’s late % or Critical late % exceeds a pre-agreed threshold, or if obsolescence or cancel rates rise materially.  
- Resilience stress test: periodic carrier-disruption simulations confirming that Service-contract orders retain at least one alternative route (R1).  
- Statistical evaluation after sufficient volume (several thousand cases) before full scale-up.
