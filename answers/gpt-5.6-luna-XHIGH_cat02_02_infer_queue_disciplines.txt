# 1. Queue-by-queue diagnosis

The diagnosis treats a job as waiting when its recorded arrival is no later than the service start. Because a dispatch decision may have occurred before the recorded start, and because scans can be delayed by up to two minutes, some eligibility sets are necessarily ranges rather than exact sets. For a combined skill requirement, a resource is eligible only if it has both skills.

|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| QA | FIFO with a soft appointment override | Appointment scheduling; the A03 choice is also consistent with SPT | 78 | At 08:20, A03 starts exactly at its 08:20 appointment ahead of older A02; A02 then starts next | At 08:20, A03 violates pure FIFO, and A03 is also the shorter job; therefore the episode does not uniquely identify an appointment rule | No appointment tolerance or dispatch timestamp is recorded, and estimates may make an appointment choice observationally equivalent to SPT |
| QB | Preemptive priority, with exceptions | FIFO-by-age for resumed work and manual dispatcher reassignment | 70 | Low-priority B01 is stopped for urgent B02 at 09:16; high-priority B05 is chosen at 10:00 over lower-priority work | At 09:48, B03 (priority 2) is selected while B05 (priority 1) is already waiting; at 10:28, B04 (priority 3) precedes B06 (priority 2) | The explicit reassignment reason, unknown remaining-time estimate for B01, and absent decision timestamps prevent separation of policy from manual intervention |
| QC | An undocumented hybrid: SPT/SEPT for ordinary work with urgency or aging overrides | Priority/deadline override plus an apparent initial-batch or FIFO-aging component | 55 | C02, then C03, are selected as short jobs ahead of the older long C01; C01 is last among the initial backlog | At 10:32 C04 is selected while shorter C06 is waiting, and at 11:40 C01 is selected ahead of shorter, higher-priority, earlier-deadline C07 | The order is compatible with dynamic SPT, a precomputed batch, aging, or hidden queue holds; no dispatch or eligibility log resolves this |
| QD | Skill-based routing | FIFO among compatible jobs, with appointment reservation and resource-availability constraints | 86 | D05 waits for D-F, the only fully qualified resource; D07 goes to D-X; mechanical-only work is handled by D-M | D04 precedes priority-1 D08 on D-M, D07 precedes priority-1 D10 on D-X, and D06's appointment beats D10's priority | The skill constraints explain most assignments but do not identify the within-resource tie-breaker or whether appointments are hard reservations |

Here, “appointment” means a rule that reserves or favors a job near its stated time. The data do not say whether the stated times are exact appointments, allowable windows, or soft targets. “Skill-based” is primarily an eligibility rule; by itself it does not specify an ordering among jobs that are all eligible. Priority predictions below use priority 1 as highest and FIFO as the tie-break. SPT uses the visible arrival estimate, not realized duration. For a partially completed B01, its current remaining estimate is not supplied, so SPT predictions involving B01 are conditional.

# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

The predictions in the tables are for the eligible jobs plausibly visible at the decision. “Any eligible” under skill-based means that skills constrain the set but do not determine its order. Where no appointment is due, an appointment policy needs an unspecified fallback rule; that limitation is itself evidence against over-interpreting the episode.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

### QA

Support comes from A03, A02, A05, A06, A07, and A08. A03 is the clearest appointment case: it is early, waits until exactly 08:20, and is selected ahead of A02. A02 then becomes the next job, and A05, A07, and A08 follow mostly in arrival order despite A08 having priority 1 and A07 being shorter than A05. A06 starts at 09:20, close to but five minutes after its 09:15 appointment, which is compatible with a soft appointment blocked by A05's non-preemptive service.

The main challenge is A03 itself: its 10-minute estimate is half A02's 20-minute estimate, so SPT predicts the same choice. A04 and A08 also show that a priority-1 label does not dominate the queue. Those are not violations of the FIFO-plus-appointment diagnosis; they are violations of pure SPT, pure priority, or hard appointment interpretations. The known calibration interval explains the 08:58 start but provides no reason to favor A04 over other waiting work once A1 becomes free. No skill-based explanation is available because every QA job requires general skill and uses A1.

### QB

Support comes from B01, B02, and B05. B02's urgent arrival causes B01, a priority-3 job, to stop at 09:16, which is strong evidence for preemption tied to urgency or priority. B05, priority 1, is selected at 10:00 ahead of B01 and B04. B01 resumes after each higher-priority or reassigned piece of work, suggesting a resumption rule or an age-preserving unfinished-job record.

The strongest challenges are B03 and B04. B03 starts at 09:48 although B05, priority 1, was recorded at 09:46 and is eligible; B04 starts at 10:28 although B06 has priority 2 and B07 has an appointment at 10:30. B07 eventually starts at 10:56, so the appointment is not a hard exact-time rule. B03's selection can plausibly be explained by the recorded dispatcher reassignment, but B04 has no recorded reason. These episodes require an undocumented dispatcher exception, a different priority scope, or a stale/incomplete visible queue to preserve a strict preemptive-priority interpretation.

### QC

Support comes from C01, C02, C03, C04, C05, and C06. C02 is chosen ahead of much older C01 at 10:10, and C03 is chosen ahead of C01 and C04 at 10:18; both are consistent with shortest estimated work. C01 is explicitly the oldest initial job but is executed last among that initial backlog, also suggesting that short jobs were favored. C05's 10:54 selection is consistent with a priority/deadline override: it is priority 1 and its 10:50 deadline is already at risk.

The challenges are C06 and C07. C04 is selected at 10:32 while C06, estimated at only six minutes, is waiting. C01 is selected at 11:40 while C07 is shorter, priority 2, and due at 12:00. The latter selection causes C07 to miss its deadline, so a hard earliest-deadline or strict-priority rule is not supported. The C04 choice can be explained by a dispatch made before C06 was visible, an initial-batch schedule, or an unrecorded hold; the C01 choice additionally requires aging, batch protection for the initial backlog, or an undocumented exception. Since all QC work is general skill on one resource, skills do not explain any of these order changes.

### QD

Support comes from nearly every job-resource pairing. D01, D04, D08, and D09 are mechanical and are handled by D-M; D02 and D03 use D-F for firmware/electrical work; D07 uses D-X for its combined mechanical/electrical requirement; D05 waits until D-F returns from its meeting because D-F is stated to be the only fully qualified resource; and D10 uses D-X while D-F is unavailable. These are strong eligibility and availability observations rather than mere start-order observations.

The within-resource challenges are D04 before priority-1 D08, D06 before priority-1 D10, and D07 before priority-1 D10. D04 and D07 are both the older compatible jobs, so FIFO among the feasible set explains them. D06 starts exactly at its appointment, so appointment reservation is also plausible. No known skill or availability constraint forces D04 ahead of D08 or D07 ahead of D10; if those choices were not FIFO tie-breaks, they require an undocumented rule. Thus QD's routing diagnosis is strong, while its local ordering rule remains less certain.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

### Test 1: QA at 08:20, A03 versus A02

The two strongest explanations are (a) FIFO with a soft appointment override and (b) pure SPT.

| explanation | prediction immediately after A03 completes at 08:30 | observed next start |
|---|---|---|
| FIFO plus appointment override | A02, the oldest non-appointment job | A02 starts at 08:32 |
| Pure SPT | A04, estimated at 7 minutes, ahead of A07 at 9, A06 at 12, A05 at 15, and A08 at 25 | Not observed; A04 waits until 08:58 |

The observed next job strongly favors the hybrid over pure SPT, but the inference still needs the appointment reservation flag and the actual dispatch-decision timestamp. Those fields would show whether A03 was selected because its slot was protected or because the dispatcher saw it as the shortest job.

### Test 2: QB at 09:48, B03 versus B05 and unfinished B01

At 09:48 the recorded candidate set includes B01 with unfinished work, B03 at priority 2, B04 at priority 3, and B05 at priority 1.

| explanation | next-job prediction at the 09:48 decision or at 10:00 after B03 | implication |
|---|---|---|
| Strict preemptive priority | B05 should be selected immediately at 09:48; if B03 was already committed, B05 should be next at 10:00 | The observed B03 start is a violation unless B05 was not visible or an override existed |
| Dispatcher reassignment plus resume/FIFO fallback | B03 can be inserted for the reassigned slot; after it ends, B05 can be selected, with B01 resuming when the override ends | This matches B03 at 09:48, B05 at 10:00, and B01 at 10:14 |

The discriminating observables are an immutable dispatch/reassignment event, the exact time B05 became visible, whether “urgent” is a priority class, and the remaining-work estimate for B01. If B05 was visible before 09:48 and no override was logged, strict priority becomes untenable.

### Test 3: QD at 11:30, D06 versus D10

On D-X, D06 is priority 2 with an 11:30 appointment, while D10 is priority 1 and shorter. D07 is older but lower priority and longer.

| explanation | prediction at 11:30 | next X-job prediction after D06 ends at 11:48 |
|---|---|---|
| Appointment-first, then FIFO among feasible jobs | D06 at 11:30 | D07, the older compatible job |
| Priority-first | D10 | D10, priority 1, should precede D07 |

D06 followed by D07 is evidence for appointment reservation plus FIFO, but it is not conclusive because D06 is also the oldest of the three X-compatible jobs. The appointment type, tolerance, reservation state, and the complete eligible set at 11:30 would discriminate the explanations.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

| rank | exception or alternative | plausibility | effect on the diagnosis |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dispatch decisions were made before a recorded arrival scan, or a scan was delayed; the service-start timestamp is not the decision timestamp | High, because the prompt explicitly permits scan delays and because C04 versus C06 is otherwise difficult to explain | It could remove apparent SPT or priority violations in QA, QB, or QC by shrinking the visible candidate set at the actual decision time; it would lower confidence in all order-based inferences |
| 2 | Manual dispatcher override or reassignment | High for QB, where the second B01 stop explicitly says “dispatcher reassignment”; plausible elsewhere because absence of a reason is not proof of no reason | It supports a hybrid QB diagnosis and prevents treating B03 as a clean priority observation; unlogged overrides would make QC's hybrid even more likely |
| 3 | Appointments are soft reservations or windows rather than exact hard starts | High, because no tolerance is supplied and A06 and B07 start late | It explains A03 and D06 as appointment signals without requiring exact punctuality, while weakening appointment-based conclusions for A06 and B07 |
| 4 | Resource or skill eligibility was transiently different from the static table | High for QD and possible for QC if a hidden hold existed; D-F's meeting and D-X's availability are explicit examples of changing state | It strengthens QD's skill-routing diagnosis and could explain why a short or high-priority job was not considered; the missing state log prevents proving this for QC |
| 5 | A precomputed batch, aging threshold, or policy switch when an SLA becomes endangered | Medium to high for QC: the initial jobs follow a short-job-like order, C05 is selected when its deadline is already missed, and C07 is then held behind C01 | It favors an undocumented hybrid rather than dynamic SPT or earliest deadline; a threshold would need its trigger and policy version to be observed |
| 6 | Estimates were noisy, stale, or strategically inaccurate; after preemption, the original estimate was incorrectly reused as remaining work | Medium, explicitly because `est_min` is only the arrival estimate | It weakens SPT conclusions, especially QA's A03 choice and every comparison involving partial B01; it cannot alone explain D05's resource assignment |
| 7 | Unrecorded interruption, setup, or queue hold | Medium, because the prompt says an absent interruption reason does not prove that none existed | It could explain the gaps around QC and the late starts, but without a reason it should be treated as a hypothesis rather than retrofitted to every violation |
| 8 | Priority, deadline, or appointment fields were entered or changed after arrival | Low to medium | It could reconcile selected jobs with a hidden effective urgency, but it would reduce confidence in the supplied attributes and requires audit history to establish |

# 6. Additional event data needed

The collection should be event-sourced at each dispatch decision, not limited to service starts or completions.

| priority | fields to collect | ambiguity resolved |
|---:|---|---|
| P0 | Physical arrival time, queue-entry time, scan time, time the job became visible to dispatch, dispatch-decision time, service-start time, and clock-synchronization status | Separates true FIFO violations from delayed scans and from decisions made before the recorded start; directly tests A02/A03, B05/B03, and C04/C06 |
| P0 | Full eligibility set at every decision, including each excluded job and exclusion reason; resource ID, location, current job, free/busy state, meeting/calibration/maintenance state, and skill/certification snapshot | Distinguishes ordering from skill or availability constraints, especially for D-M, D-F, and D-X and for any alleged QC hold |
| P0 | Policy version, rule activation time, queue configuration, tie-break rule, manual override flag, dispatcher identity, and override reason | Tests whether a queue changes policy, uses an undocumented hybrid, or was manually reassigned; essential for QB and QC |
| P0 | Appointment type (hard, soft, window, target), tolerance window, reservation flag, lateness policy, and whether an appointment blocks other jobs | Separates appointment scheduling from FIFO or SPT for A03, A06, B07, and D06 |
| P1 | Priority history, effective urgency class, deadline/SLA history, remaining slack at each decision, and any escalation threshold | Tests whether C05 was selected by priority, deadline risk, or an SLA-triggered policy change, and why C07 was not |
| P1 | Estimate visible at arrival, estimate revisions, current estimated remaining time, actual work remaining, and whether estimate updates are supplied to dispatch | Makes SPT/SEPT testable and avoids using original `est_min` as B01's post-preemption remaining time |
| P1 | Start, stop, preempt, resume, and completion events with work completed, setup/changeover cost, preemption reason, resume priority, and whether the interruption was operational or administrative | Separates preemptive priority from a dispatcher reassignment and quantifies the cost of stopping B01 |
| P2 | Skill requirement at dispatch, resource skill matrix version, simultaneous multi-skill requirements, and whether cross-training or temporary authorization was active | Verifies D05's “only fully qualified” claim and identifies changes that could make a job eligible for another resource |
| P2 | Actual service duration, queue waiting duration, deadline outcome, appointment outcome, and audit trail for edits to arrival, priority, estimate, and appointment fields | Validates the inferred policy against outcomes and detects strategic or retrospective data changes |

The first collection priority is the dispatch-decision event with its candidate set and policy version. Without that event, a start sequence is only a partial observation of a decision process and cannot reliably distinguish FIFO, SPT, priority, appointment reservation, and manual intervention.

# 7. Final synthesis

QD is the most identifiable queue: the resource-skill matrix and availability exceptions explain the routing of almost every job, with FIFO and appointment behavior plausible within the feasible set. QC is the least identifiable: its choices mix short-job behavior, urgency, and apparent age or batch protection, while C04 versus C06 and C01 versus C07 remain unexplained by any single clean rule.

Overall, QA looks like FIFO with a soft appointment exception, QB like preemptive priority with dispatcher and resumption exceptions, QC like an undocumented hybrid with SPT-like batching and urgency or aging overrides, and QD like skill-based routing with local FIFO and appointment effects. The most dangerous claimed policy to infer from these observations alone is strict preemptive priority. A few priority-1 selections and the B02 interruption are insufficient: appointments, deadlines, skill eligibility, dispatcher overrides, and delayed visibility can generate the same pattern, while several priority-1 jobs are demonstrably delayed or bypassed.
