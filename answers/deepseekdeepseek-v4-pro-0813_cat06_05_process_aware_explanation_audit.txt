## 2. Process-model consistency checks

A concrete validation suite should include:

- **Prefix-time availability gate.** For every feature, require an `as_of` timestamp or source event timestamp and enforce `as_of  t0`. Features with only extraction-date lineage are rejected.
- **Activity-order feasibility.** Define the allowed lifecycle: `Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed -> Damage Assessment Completed` or document cycles, then `Settlement Decision Recorded`, then `Claim Closed`; `Appeal` only after `Partial` or `Deny`, followed by a possible second `Settlement Decision Recorded`. Check each trace and each explanation edge against this model.
- **Same-timestamp ambiguity.** For events with equal timestamps, require a reliable ingestion sequence index. If missing, test both orders and flag any order that changes the semantic pattern. `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is a classic artifact of missing ordering.
- **Complete-trace feature detection.** Build a lineage catalog identifying whether each feature is a function of the full trace or of the prefix. Features such as `total_case_duration_hours`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `num_document_requests_total`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_handler_team`, `last_resource_role`, and final decisions are complete-trace.
- **Prefix-feature projection.** Before training and explaining, project each case’s event log to `{events: timestamp  t0}`. Compute all counts, variants, and roles only from that prefix.
- **Activity enablement at `t0`.** For the reviewed prefix `Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed`, enabled activities are `Damage Assessment Completed` in the fast track or `Documents Requested` in evidence cycles; `Fraud Investigation Opened` may be enabled if a trigger is already known. `Settlement Decision Recorded`, `Appeal`, and `Claim Closed` are merely future-possible or impossible from the current state.
- **Repeated Settlement Decision semantics.** A second `Settlement Decision Recorded` is legitimate only after an `Appeal`, and the prior decision must have been `Partial` or `Deny`. Without this, a repeated decision is a duplicate or data error.
- **DFG as exploratory, not normative.** A frequent DFG edge is not automatically a rule. DFGs cannot resolve same-timestamp ambiguity and can be driven by system ingestion order. They should be used only as data-quality indicators and then checked against the normative lifecycle.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

### Defensible predictive signal

- `initial_damage_complexity` is known at `t0` and may indicate longer appraisal or inspection effort.
- `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` is known at `t0` and reflects process age and remaining slack.
- `initial_reserve` may be defensible if timestamped before `t0`; it can indicate case exposure or expected resource intensity.

**May claim:** these features are associated with predicted breach risk.  
**May not claim:** they directly cause a breach or are individually actionable.

### Process-state or congestion signal

- `queue_length_at_t0` is a current operational-load signal.
- A repaired prefix document count would indicate how much evidence work has already occurred at `t0`.

**May claim:** current congestion or accumulated process work is predictive of slower case handling.  
**May not claim:** a specific queue value deterministically causes this case to breach without capacity, priority, or routing context.

### Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review

- `claimant_region` likely proxies repair-network coverage, language, channel, and socioeconomic conditions.
- `claimant_channel = Broker` may proxy case mix, documentation completeness, or access.
- `final_handler_team` even if repaired to `t0` team may proxy routing rules, claim type, or operational segment.
- `prior_claim_count_before_t0` after repair may be legitimate history, but must be checked for proxy effects.

**May claim:** these features improve prediction across subpopulations.  
**May not claim:** region, channel, or assigned team is a direct or inherent cause of delay.

### Drift/calendar proxy

- `calendar_week` is known at `t0`, but its risk association is probably driven by holidays, staffing, or policy changes.

**May claim:** risk varies across calendar periods because of external operating conditions.  
**May not claim:** a calendar week itself causes delay.

### Unusable until lineage is repaired

- `total_case_duration_hours`
- `appeal_seen_anywhere`
- `final_handler_team` in current warehouse form
- `num_document_requests_total`
- `full_trace_variant_id`
- `prior_claim_count_before_t0` in current SQL form
- `same_day_document_count_snapshot` in current snapshot form
- `risk_score_current`
- `last_resource_role`
- `final_decision`
- Full-trace sequence patterns including `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded`, `Documents Requested -> Documents Received`, `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened`, `Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded`, and especially `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`

Some of these can be repaired to prefix versions: document counts, prior claims, `t0` resource role, `t0` handler team, and as-of risk score. Until then they should not enter a `t0` model or explanation.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Immediately remove confirmed leakage.** Remove full-trace features and future outcomes: `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `num_document_requests_total`, `final_handler_team`, `final_decision`, `last_resource_role`, and current forms of `same_day_document_count_snapshot` and `risk_score_current`. Retrain on prefix-only features before any new explanations are issued.

2. **Build a temporal feature store.** Store every feature with `entity_id`, `feature_name`, `as_of_time`, `source_event_time`, `ingestion_time`, and value. Use point-in-time joins to `t0`. Add bi-temporal history for mutable fields such as risk score, handler team, resource role, and queue length.

3. **Enforce historical feature timestamps.** Populate timestamps from source events or database versioning. For mutable warehouse fields, do not rely on “current” values. For any feature lacking an as-of timestamp, treat it as unusable for `t0` prediction unless repair is proven.

4. **Recompute features from the prefix only.** Use event sequences truncated to `{timestamp  t0}` for counts, trace variants, document activity, prior claims, and roles. Create reusable prefix-projection queries and prevent `COUNT`, `MAX`, `last`, or closure-field extraction without explicit time bounds.

5. **Run process-conformance tests before model training and explanation.** Validate each trace against the lifecycle, especially:
   - impossible edges such as `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`;
   - same-timestamp events without sequence index;
   - repeated `Settlement Decision Recorded` without a legitimate prior Appeal;
   - activities used in explanations that are future or impossible from the `t0` prefix.

6. **Recalibrate after removing leaked features.** After retraining the model, recalibrate probabilities with a temporal holdout or cross-validation. Reevaluate thresholds because breach probabilities may shift materially after leakage removal.

7. **Check explanation stability.** Recompute SHAP-like explanations on prefix-only features over bootstrap and time-slice splits. Flag features whose contributions are unstable or path-dependent. Do not present one global ranking as causal or operationally final.

8. **Run proxy and fairness analysis.** For `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, handler/team assignment, prior claims, and any reserve variable, test indirect impact and subgroup performance. Document which features are proxies for access, language, network coverage, or socioeconomic factors. Use fairness review before using them in operational decisions.

9. **Monitor for schema and process drift.** Track feature distributions, event-order anomalies, same-timestamp frequency, missing sequence-index rate, and calendar/holiday policy changes. Re-run leakage and conformance checks after any upstream process or database change.
