## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

**Where a maximum, a minimum or a sum is used**

- The chemistry and microbiology branches run concurrently from Friday afternoon. The join time is **J = max(C, M)**, where C is the chemistry usable-result time and M is the microbiology usable-result time. Branch durations are never added.
- B-417's chemistry start is the **minimum** of the two analysts' free times. B-417's QA start is the time the next of two reviewers becomes free, which is also a minimum, after the batches ahead of it have cleared.
- Stages *after* the join are sequential, so they are summed in *calendar* time: QA wait, review, any query or investigation loop, closure review, then release or rejection.
- The weekend pause is a common shock to both branches. It pushes both to Monday, which is why the join lands on Monday whichever branch is slower.
- Release and rejection are competing terminal outcomes. The path decides which one occurs.

**Why a naive mean-sum fails.** Summing unconditional means gives roughly 50 h incubation + 1 h readout + ~6 h chemistry + ~2.5 h QA + expected rework. That lands around Monday 00:00–04:00, when no lab and no QA staff are working. It also double-counts parallel time and hides a 14-hour overnight cliff.

**S1 – Smooth path**

*Chemistry, waiting and service*
- Friday: analyst 2 frees at 15:54–16:24 and takes the urgent sample under both disciplines. Analyst 1 frees at 16:42–17:30.
  - **Priority (≈ 45%):** analyst 1 starts B-417 at 16:42–17:30. It gets 0.5–1.3 h of work done by 18:00, then pauses for about 62 h over the weekend. It resumes on Monday at 08:00 with 0.2–4.0 h remaining (mean ≈ 1.9 h). C = Mon 08:15–12:00, central 09:15–10:45.
  - **FIFO (≈ 55%):** analyst 1 takes ordinary sample #1 on Friday. On Monday the resumed urgent and ordinary #1 samples finish at about 09:00 and 09:55. Ordinary #2 and #3 go next. B-417 starts when the first of those finishes, at about 11:00–12:30 (range 10:00–14:30). C = Mon 13:15–15:30 central (range 11:30–18:00). About 5% spill to Tuesday 08:00.
- Under FIFO, B-417's chemistry sojourn from request is about 70 calendar hours. Only about 2.8 h of that is service. About 62 h is calendar pause, and about 5–6 h is queue waiting (Friday plus Monday).
- *Overtime exception scenario:* even if chemistry finished on Friday evening, nothing changes. Microbiology is not ready until Sunday and QA is closed on Sunday, so overtime buys zero.

*Microbiology, incubation and readout*
- Incubation runs continuously. It is ready Sun 20 Sep 12:00–20:00.
- **No authorization (55–75%):** the plate waits for Monday 08:00, and M = Mon 08:30–10:30 (including backlog).
- **Authorized (25–45%):** M = Sun 13:00–23:00. QA is closed on Sunday, so this only guarantees that microbiology is not the constraint at Monday 08:00.

*Parallel join*
- J = max(C, M).
- Under priority, chemistry is later than microbiology in about 85–90% of cases. Chemistry finishes before about 09:00 only if the remaining work is under about 1 h, with probability ≈ 8–10%. So J ≈ Mon 09:30–10:45.
- Under FIFO, chemistry is later in essentially all cases. J ≈ Mon 13:15–15:30.
- Conclusion: the microbiology branch and staffing calendar dominate in the sense that they push the whole process into Monday. Once there, chemistry spill-over is usually the slower clock. Microbiology is a floor at Monday 08:30–10:30 (none on the on-call path).

*QA waiting and service*
- Friday 15:30–20:00, one reviewer: about 1.8 reviews completed (range 1–3) and about 1.6 arrivals (range 0–4).
- Saturday 09:00–13:00, one reviewer: about 1.6 completed and about 1.4 arrivals.
- Monday 08:00 backlog ≈ 5 + 3.0 − 3.4 ≈ 4–5, with P10–P90 of about 2–8. Overdispersion includes a possible Monday-morning burst from other batches whose weekend plates are read that morning.
- From Monday 08:00, two reviewers drain the queue at a net ≈ 0.8 − 0.35 ≈ 0.45 batches/h. The queue ahead is Q(t) ≈ 4.6 − 0.45·(hours since 08:00): about 3.7 at 10:00, 1.7 at 14:30 and 0.6 at 17:00. The FIFO wait is ≈ Q / 0.8 h.
  - Priority chemistry, join about 10:00: wait ≈ 4.5 h, QA start ≈ 14:30.
  - FIFO chemistry, join about 14:30: wait ≈ 2 h, QA start ≈ 16:30.
  - So the QA queue absorbs most of the chemistry discipline difference. A ~4.5 h difference in join time becomes a ~2 h difference in QA start.
- Review time has median 1.5 h and P80 3.5 h, so acceptance is Mon ~15:30–19:30.
- The QA day ends at 20:00. Starting at 14:30, B-417 misses 20:00 about 10% of the time. Starting at 16:30, it misses about 20%. Heavy queues can push the start past 18:00 in about 15–25% of cases.
- Overall about 25–35% of S1 cases pause at 20:00 and resume Tuesday 08:00, with acceptance Tue 08:30–10:30.

*Rework and investigation:* none, by definition.

*Final step:* Electronic Release adds 0.25–0.75 h and runs continuously. Terminal time is Mon 16:00–20:30, or Tue 08:45–11:00 for the spill-over cases.

**S2 – Chemistry rerun**

*Chemistry*
- **Priority:** the first result is Mon 09:15–10:45. A rerun of 1.0–3.0 h (mean 1.9 h) starts at once, so C = Mon 11:00–13:30 (tail 15:00).
- **FIFO:** the first result is Mon 13:15–15:30. The rerun ends at 15:00–18:00 if it fits. If the first result comes after about 15:00 and the rerun is long, it pauses at 18:00 and finishes Tue 08:30–10:00. That happens in about 35–50% of FIFO reruns.

*Microbiology:* the same as S1. It is now even less likely to bind, so the join is J = C, set by chemistry.

*QA*
- Join around 12:00: queue ≈ 2.6, QA start ≈ 15:15, accepted Mon 16:45–19:30, with about 30% spilling to Tuesday.
- Join around 16:30: queue ≈ 0.8, QA start ≈ 17:30. About half spill to Tuesday 08:00 and are accepted 08:30–10:30.
- Tuesday join: the queue is near steady state (0–2), so acceptance is Tue 10:30–13:00.

*Rework:* the rerun is a trigger, so the investigation probability is 0.30–0.55 and those cases move to S6/S7. A query at about 22% adds 4–12 working hours.

*Final step:* release Mon 17:30 – Tue 12:00 central. Under dependence, a rerun raises the posterior probability of E (about 0.3 → 0.5). That in turn raises the chance of a contamination indication, so S2 and S3/S4 co-occur more often than independence implies.

**S3/S4 – Delayed microbiology through contamination indication and confirmation**

*Microbiology*
- The indication is seen at the primary readout: Mon 08:30–10:30 without on-call, or Sun 13:00–23:00 with on-call.
- Confirmation needs another 20–30 h of continuous incubation, then a 0.5–1.5 h readout during weekday staffing.
  - **Without on-call:** confirmation is ready Tue 04:30–16:30. Readout is Tue 08:30–18:00; plates ready before 08:00 are read 08:30–09:30.
  - **With on-call:** confirmation is ready Mon 09:00 – Tue 05:00. Readout is Mon 09:30–18:00 if the plate is ready before about 17:00, otherwise Tue 08:30–09:30.
  - Authorization therefore saves about 0–20 h here. This is where its main value lies.

*Chemistry:* finished on Monday under either discipline, or Tuesday morning after a FIFO rerun. It is not binding.

*Join:* J = M = confirmation readout, centrally Tue 09:00–15:00. Microbiology is now clearly the dominant clock.

*QA:* on Tuesday the queue is near steady state. Utilization per reviewer is about 0.44, so the wait is 0–3 h including overdispersion. Review has median 1.5 h.

*Rework:* the contamination trigger gives an investigation probability of 0.30–0.55, likely weighted toward the laboratory type.

*Final step*
- **S3, confirmation clears:** release Tue 12:00–18:30 with no investigation. With a documentation-only investigation, it is Wed 10:00 – Thu 11:00. With a laboratory investigation, it is Fri 25 – Tue 29 Sep.
- **S4, confirmation positive:** QA disposition (0.5–2 h) plus rejection recording (0.5–1.0 h) gives Tue 13:00–19:00. If rejection waits for an investigation's root cause, it becomes Wed–Thu for a documentation-only investigation, or Fri 25 – Tue 29 Sep for a laboratory investigation. Central range: Tue 13:00 – Wed 12:00.

**S5 – Documentation query**
- Chemistry, microbiology, join and QA start are as in S1, with QA starting about Mon 14:00–17:00. The query is raised after about 0.5–1.5 h of review.
- The query adds 4–12 QA working hours. Only about 3–5 h of working time remains on Monday, and the overnight closure runs 20:00–08:00. The answer therefore arrives between about Mon 20:00 (effectively Tuesday 08:00) and Tue 16:00.
- The review resumes with 0–1 h wait and 0.5–1.5 h of work. Acceptance is Tue 09:00–18:00, and release adds 0.25–0.75 h.
- The central terminal range is Tue 10:00–17:00. The earliest case needs a priority join, an immediate QA start and a 4-hour answer, giving about Mon 16:00.

**S6 – Documentation-only investigation**
- **Clean-lab origin:** the investigation opens during the Monday review, about 15:00–17:00. It adds 8–20 QA working hours: about 4 h on Monday, 12 h on Tuesday, then Wednesday. An 8-hour investigation closes about Tue 12:00; a 20-hour one closes about Wed 12:00. Closure review takes 0.5–2 h, so release is Tue 13:00 – Wed 14:30, centrally Tue 18:00 – Wed 12:00.
- **Triggered origin** (from S2–S4): the investigation opens about Tue 11:00–15:00. It closes Wed 09:00 – Thu 09:00, and release is Wed 10:00 – Thu 11:00.
- The overnight closures convert "8–20 working hours" into 1–2 calendar days.

**S7 – Laboratory investigation**
- The investigation opens Monday afternoon (clean or rerun origin) or Tuesday (contamination origin). Adding 3–7 calendar days means it closes between Thu 24 Sep afternoon and Tue 29 Sep afternoon.
- Closure review then follows the QA calendar:
  - weekday closures are reviewed within hours;
  - closures on Friday after 20:00 go to Saturday 09:00–13:00, with a single reviewer;
  - closures between Saturday 13:00 and Monday 08:00, about 35–45% of the closure window, are all reviewed on Monday 28 Sep from 08:00.
- This creates a terminal-event lump on Mon 28 Sep 09:00–12:00. The outcome is mostly release. Rejection is substantial when the investigation was triggered by contamination.

**How the central band arises.**
- The probability of finishing on Monday is about 30–45%, centrally about 0.38. So the median most likely falls at Tuesday's first QA hours, 08:30–10:30.
- If the QA backlog is light, or QA expedites B-417 because it is high priority, the median moves to Monday 16:00–20:00.
- If QA expedites, the smooth path completes Mon 12:30–14:00 under priority chemistry, or Mon 15:00–17:30 under FIFO chemistry. The Monday completion probability then rises to about 55–65%.

**Checkpoints on the cumulative forecast.**
- By Mon 20:00 (76.5 h): about 30–45% complete.
- By Tue 11:00 (91.5 h): about 55–65%.
- By Tue 20:00 (100.5 h): about 75–82%.
- By Wed 20:00 (124.5 h): about 87–93%.
- Beyond Thu 24 Sep 20:00 (148.5 h): about 4–12%.

**Most plausible terminal event.** Electronic Release, with probability about 91–97%. It most often happens Monday late afternoon or Tuesday morning.

Batch Rejected has probability about 3–9% (central about 5–6%). It mainly comes from a confirmed microbiology contamination. It can happen Monday afternoon at the earliest (only via weekend on-call), typically Tuesday afternoon to Wednesday, and next week if rejection awaits a laboratory investigation.

**Calculation shortcuts I used**
- QA review time as a lognormal fit to the stated median and P80.
- A fluid (deterministic-rate) queue approximation, with overdispersion carried as ranges.
- Triangular means and simple CDF values for chemistry.
- Uniform readings of the stated ranges.
- Point conditional probabilities for E, which I constructed myself.
- No lunch breaks or shift handovers.
- An approximately exclusive scenario partition to read percentiles off the mixture.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. **Temperature-excursion flag and the dependence it induces.** *Type: dependence uncertainty, rooted in observation uncertainty (sensor gap).*
   - E is a common cause. It raises rerun and contamination together, so joint anomalies are about 1.5–2× more likely than under independence.
   - On its own, a confirmed E lifts the investigation probability from 0.04–0.10 to 0.30–0.55.
   - The overall investigation probability therefore ranges from about 0.12 to 0.32. This moves P80 by about a day and P90 by several days.
   - If the excursion was plant-wide, other batches in the QA queue are affected too. QA congestion and B-417's rework tail are then positively correlated.

2. **QA queue at the join time, with a heavy-tailed review against the 20:00 cut-off.** *Type: queue uncertainty, interacting with the calendar.*
   - The Monday backlog (2–8), the review tail (P90 about 5.5 h) and whether B-417 is expedited decide whether the majority path ends Monday evening or Tuesday morning.
   - That is a ~14-hour cliff at the median.

3. **Investigation occurrence and type, especially a 3–7 day laboratory investigation.** *Type: path uncertainty.*
   - It generates the whole beyond-P90 mass.
   - Its effect is magnified by weekend lumping onto Monday 28 Sep.
   - The documentation-only vs laboratory split is itself uncertain (60–80% vs 20–40%).

4. **Microbiology contamination indication and its confirmation.** *Type: path uncertainty, with calendar uncertainty from on-call authorization and weekday readout staffing.*
   - It makes microbiology the dominant clock and pushes the join to Tuesday.
   - It is the main route to rejection.
   - Weekend authorization changes this path by up to about 20 h, but barely affects the smooth path.

5. **Chemistry queue discipline, the chemistry service tail and Monday spill-over (including reruns).** *Type: queue uncertainty plus service-time uncertainty.*
   - FIFO vs priority shifts chemistry completion from about Mon 10:00 to about Mon 14:30.
   - The draining QA queue absorbs more than half of that shift.
   - It still matters at the Monday 20:00 QA cut-off, and for FIFO reruns that spill into Tuesday.

Material but below the top five:
- whether a documentation query occurs and how long the response takes (path uncertainty);
- the Monday microbiology readout backlog (queue uncertainty, rarely binding);
- the working-hour calendar used for queries and investigations (calendar uncertainty).

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

**Primary: resolution of the upstream temperature-excursion flag**, when engineering confirms or refutes it. It acts on every rework branch at once, because it is the common cause. It can be observed soon, possibly before the weekend analysis finishes. And it moves both the centre and the tail of the forecast.

- **Confirmed (prior 0.20–0.40):**
  - rerun rises to about 0.20–0.35, and contamination to about 0.12–0.25;
  - even normal results carry a 0.30–0.55 investigation probability, so the overall investigation probability becomes about 0.40–0.60 and the laboratory-investigation probability about 0.10–0.20;
  - rejection rises to about 8–15%, and the smooth-path probability falls to about 20–30%;
  - P50 moves to Tue midday – Wed, P80 to Thu 24 – Fri 25 Sep, and P90 to Mon 28 Sep or later;
  - it also makes weekend on-call authorization and pre-assembled deviation documentation much more valuable.
- **Refuted (0.60–0.80):**
  - rerun falls to about 0.08–0.14, contamination to about 0.03–0.07, and investigation to about 0.06–0.14;
  - the smooth path rises to about 55–65%;
