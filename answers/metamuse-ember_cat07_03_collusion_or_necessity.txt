# 3. Case prioritization

## High-priority clusters

1. **Lena–Kim, V-77x, bank-detail change followed by payment approval**
   - Include first the 9 same-resource Change Vendor Bank Details + Payment Approval cases and the 16 cases with approximately 17-minute intervals; then review the remainder of the 44-case Lena–Kim subgroup.
   - **Reason:** high-risk activity pair, 68% high amount, 72% V-77x concentration, 83% manual upload, only 16% emergency-flagged, and split across regions.
   - **Inspect first:** `vendor_id`, old/new bank details, `entered_by`, `approved_by`, `org:resource`, `source_system`, timestamps, `amount_band`, vendor-master ticket, independent bank-verification letter, vendor callback evidence, goods receipt, invoice match, payment-release record, and underlying authenticated user/session logs.

2. **Non-emergency or late-justified subsets of Carla–Gita and Dan–Hugo**
   - Prioritize fast PO approvals where `emergency_flag=False`, retrospective justification is late, or no different-person justification exists.
   - **Reason:** emergency handling may explain the overall pattern, but it cannot explain cases lacking an emergency basis or timely independent review.
   - **Inspect first:** emergency declaration, clinical or repair evidence, procurement category, approval comments, delegated-authority evidence, `entered_by`, `approved_by`, `lifecycle:transition`, goods receipt, and signed retrospective justification.

3. **The 23 shared-account Gita emergency approvals**
   - Cross-reference these with the 42 Create + Approve exceptions and the late-justification cases.
   - **Reason:** identity ambiguity affects both the four-eyes conclusion and accountability.
   - **Inspect first:** `org:resource`, `entered_by`, `approved_by`, authenticated user ID, delegation record, session/device logs, and source-system mapping for `legacy_erp`.

## Medium-priority clusters

1. **Remaining Carla–Gita and Dan–Hugo fast emergency cases**
   - Use a stratified sample by amount, vendor, purchase category, and justification timeliness rather than reviewing only the fastest cases.
   - **Reason:** plausible emergency handling, but 18–21% late retrospective justification requires control testing.
   - **Inspect first:** emergency request, delivery/need date, approval rationale, goods receipt, invoice, and different-person justification.

2. **Five same-person retrospective justifications not explained by the quarter-close batch**
   - Separate these from the 12 batch-migrated entries.
   - **Reason:** the batch-migrated cases may be timing artefacts, but the remaining cases may represent genuine rule failures.
   - **Inspect first:** original timestamps, spreadsheet audit history, signed justification date, override approver, and justification author.

3. **Vendor Check + Change Vendor Bank Details by the same person**
   - Review the 31 cases where this coincides with payment influence, high amount, or manual entry.
   - **Reason:** not itself a stated four-eyes violation, but relevant to vendor-master control design.
   - **Inspect first:** vendor-master role matrix, onboarding checklist, bank verification, and payment approver.

4. **Lena–KimLena subcontracting loops**
   - Review the 11 loop cases, especially where payment was followed by another bank-detail change.
   - **Reason:** could be corrections, but post-payment bank changes need explanation.
   - **Inspect first:** correction comments, change history, payment recall or reissue records, and before-and-after bank details.

## Low-priority or monitoring-only clusters

1. **Aline–Elena and Boris–Felix routine pairings**
   - Monitor through aggregate analytics; inspect a small random sample for control design.
   - **Reason:** high volume is explained by workload, timing is normal, emergency and manual-entry shares are low.
   - **Inspect first:** variant distribution, amount distribution, approval comments, and any fast/high-value outliers.

2. **Marc–Iris ordinary onboarding/payment cases**
   - Monitor in aggregate.
   - **Reason:** 41 co-cases, 1.8-hour median, 10% emergency, 21% manual entry; no comparable concentration of risk indicators.
   - **Inspect first:** outliers by amount, vendor, or source system.

3. **Bot_MatchAP_Kim and Temp_NinaBuyer_Carla**
   - Monitor automation and backlog-entry controls.
   - **Reason:** bot routing and end-of-quarter data entry are plausible operational explanations, though data-entry accuracy should be sampled.
   - **Inspect first:** Bot_Match exception reasons, matching scores, data-entry error rates, and correction history.

# 4. Network and conformance analyses

The purpose is to compare each pattern with workload and case mix, not merely to rank raw co-occurrence counts.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.importer.event_log.importer import importer

df = importer.apply("procurement_event_log.csv")

log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)

all_cases = set(df["case:concept:name"].unique())
```

### Filtering four-eyes violations

```python
def four_eyes_violations(log, activity_1, activity_2, resource_key="org:resource"):
    # Some PM4Py versions return cases satisfying the principle. Verify the
    # semantics of the installed version and invert if necessary.
    compliant_log = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
        log, activity_1, activity_2, resource_key=resource_key
    )
    compliant_cases = {
        trace.attributes["case:concept:name"] for trace in compliant_log
    }
    return all_cases - compliant_cases

po_violations = four_eyes_violations(
    log, "Create Purchase Order", "Approve Purchase Order"
)

bank_payment_violations = four_eyes_violations(
    log, "Change Vendor Bank Details", "Payment Approval"
)

# Sensitivity analysis: org:resource may collapse bots, assistants and
# approvers. Repeat using entered_by/approved_by and, where available,
# authenticated user ID rather than display resource.
```

Vendor Check + Change Vendor Bank Details should be analysed separately because it is not stated as a normative four-eyes rule.

### Handover, working-together and subcontracting networks

```python
handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
    log, resource_key="org:resource"
)
together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(
    log, resource_key="org:resource"

    log, resource_key="org:resource"

handover_metrics = pm4py.discover_network_analysis(handover)
together_metrics = pm4py.discover_network_analysis(together)
subcontracting_metrics = pm4py.discover_network_analysis(subcontracting)
```

For every edge, calculate a workload-normalized measure, for example:

- co-cases divided by each resource's distinct cases;
- share of the smaller resource's cases;
- comparison with the resource's expected pairing share under team assignments;
- median service and waiting time on the edge;
- proportion that is emergency, manual, high-amount, or concentrated in one vendor.

This prevents Aline–Elena's 510 cases from being treated as inherently more suspicious than a much smaller pairing that represents a large share of one resource's work.

Subcontracting loops should be extracted as ABA sequences and joined to case attributes and comments to distinguish correction from rubber-stamping.

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

## Evidence that would increase concern

- For V-77x, no maintenance ticket, change request, or case-level linkage authorizing specific `vendor_id` bank changes.
- Bank changes supported only by a spreadsheet, email, or manual upload, with no independent bank-verification letter or vendor callback on a previously known contact.
- Payment released before bank verification, goods receipt, or invoice matching.
- Old and new bank details show payment redirected to an account inconsistent with the vendor's legal name or prior account.
- `entered_by`, session logs, device IDs, IP addresses, or authenticated user IDs show the same underlying person performing bank change and payment approval despite different `org:resource` values.
- High-value purchases repeatedly split just below approval thresholds, or repeated bank changes for the same vendor across regions.
- Emergency-flagged fast approvals without clinical, safety, operational, or contractual evidence of urgency.
- Retrospective justifications genuinely authored by the override approver after the 3-working-day deadline, excluding batch-migration artefacts.
- Missing or altered `lifecycle:transition` history, deleted attachments, or approval comments that are generic and contain no substantive review.

## Evidence that would reduce concern

- A documented V-77x mass-maintenance ticket enumerating affected vendors, old and new bank accounts, effective dates, and independent verification.
- Distinct authenticated users for bank change and payment approval, confirmed through `entered_by`, `approved_by`, and access logs rather than shared `org:resource`.
- Vendor confirmation on verified letterhead, callback to a pre-existing vendor contact, or bank confirmation matching the vendor's legal identity.
- Goods receipts, delivery records, invoices, and three-way-match evidence supporting the payments.
- Emergency requests, clinical need, repair tickets, weekend rosters, or contractual deadlines explaining fast Carla–Gita and Dan–Hugo approvals.
- Signed retrospective justifications by a different person within 3 working days, with original timestamps predating the quarter-close migration.
- A corrected `legacy_erp` mapping showing that Bot_PO created the PO while a distinct human approved it.
- Delegation records showing that assistants acted under documented authority when using the shared Gita account.
- Regional splits explained by V-77x entities, tax registrations, or regional vendor-master records.
- Evidence that apparent loops contain substantive clarification, correction, and rechecking rather than repeated approval without review.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Internal Audit reviewed procurement-to-payment event-log patterns for repeated resource pairings, four-eyes exceptions, unusually fast approvals, and handover loops. The review identified control exceptions requiring targeted follow-up, but it did not establish misconduct by any individual. Most repeated pairings are consistent with documented workload specialization or emergency handling. The long-standing Buyer_Aline–Approver_Elena and Buyer_Boris–Approver_Felix pairings show routine timing, low emergency incidence, and low manual entry. Buyer_Carla–Approver_Gita and Buyer_Dan–Manager_Hugo pairings are faster and include more exceptions; however, their concentration in emergency medical supplies and weekend facilities repairs, low vendor concentration, and low manual entry make urgent operational handling plausible and case-specific testing necessary.

The most concerning subgroup is VendorMaster_Lena–AP_Kim in vendor family V-77x. It combines bank-detail changes followed rapidly by payment approval, many high-amount cases, vendor concentration, and predominantly manual_upload sourcing, with limited emergency justification. This warrants priority review of bank-change authorisation, vendor-master tickets, payment approvals, and access logs, without presuming intent.

Interpretation is limited by data-quality issues: shared or mapped identities, batch-entered justifications, missing linkage to V-77x maintenance tickets, and legacy_erp mapping errors may create false exceptions or obscure real ones. Audit recommends validating identities and timestamps, tracing a risk-based sample from each cluster to source documents, and strengthening four-eyes, bank-change, and emergency-justification controls before any broader conclusion.
