## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The March collapse of `raw_model_fitness` from 0.93 to 0.55, against a `mapped_model_fitness` of 0.90, is close to conclusive evidence that the raw drop is an artefact of representation, not of behaviour. Two mechanical operations — renaming `Eligibility Screen` back to `Validate Eligibility` and deleting duplicated `Open Case` events — recover 0.35 of fitness. Alignment cost is dominated by unmapped labels: an unknown activity forces a log-move at every occurrence, so a rename of one high-frequency step alone can move fitness by tens of points. What the gap *implies* is that most March-April non-conformance is attributable to naming and to a technical event, and that any narrative treating March as an operational breakdown is wrong.

What it does *not* imply is that the process was unchanged. A mapping is an assumption of semantic identity: it asserts that `Eligibility Screen` means what `Validate Eligibility` meant. The migration could have changed the screen's content, its automation level, or who performs it, and the mapping would hide exactly that. Recovered conformance is evidence about the *model-log* relation, not about work practice; it should be corroborated by unchanged temporal profiles, resource patterns, and downstream rework for migrated cases.

Duplicated technical events and missing resources damage different analyses asymmetrically. Duplicates inflate event counts, DFG edge weights, self-loop and repetition metrics, and alignment cost, but barely touch case-level cycle time. Missing resources (1.1%→~6%, concentrated in migrated activities and non-random) leave control-flow analysis intact but bias handover/social-network, workload, and resource-performance analyses — precisely the analyses needed to test the staffing hypothesis.

Post-May patterns cannot be dismissed as logging drift because mapped fitness declines *further* (0.91→0.86) while label count, duplicate rate, and missingness stay flat, and because the strongest signals — `Assess->Request Info` rising 12→29 per 100 cases and median cycle time +42% — are label-invariant quantities computed after normalization.

Two things stand out. (a) The fitness, label, missingness and duplicate metrics all move together, once, in March, and then flat-line — a single logging step. (b) The rework, supplier, cycle-time and complexity metrics move on completely different dates and continue moving. These are not the same drift.

### 3.2 Normalized DFG distance

**Measure.** Let r_m(e) be the rate per 100 cases of edge e in month m over the seven supplied edges. I use the unnormalized L1 distance
`D1(m, Jan) = Σ_e |r_m(e) − r_Jan(e)|`, in units of "edge occurrences per 100 cases". I report L1 rather than a probability-normalized (e.g. Jensen-Shannon or earth-mover) distance because the fragment is not a distribution: rows do not sum to a constant, and the supplied edges omit `Collect Documents->Assess` re-entries, `Decision->Close`, `Open Case` edges, and all infrequent paths. Normalizing seven partial edges into a simplex would fabricate structure. I also report D1 excluding the `Assess->Senior Assessment` edge, because that edge is contaminated by the known recording inconsistency.

| month | D1 vs Jan | D1 excl. Senior | dominant contributing edges |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 2026-03 | 3 | 2 | none material — March is at baseline behaviourally |
| 2026-05 | 40 | 37 | Assess->RequestInfo (+12), RequestInfo->CollectDocs (+12), Decision (−7) |
| 2026-07 | 59 | 54 | RequestInfo loop (+17/+17), Decision (−11), CollectDocs->Assess (−6) |
| 2026-09 | 28 | 25 | RequestInfo loop (+8/+8), Decision (−4) |
| 2026-10 | 64 | 46 | Senior Assessment (+18), RequestInfo loop (+14/+14) |
| 2026-12 | 36 | 26 | Senior Assessment (+10), RequestInfo loop (+9/+9) |

**Assumptions and caveats.** Omitted edges are assumed to have changed no more than the observed ones; if the missing mass drifted, all distances are underestimates. The rates are already label-mapped and duplicate-stripped, so D1 measures *behaviour*, not logging — which is exactly why March's D1 ≈ 3 (inside the noise band) is so informative. Rates are monthly aggregates of heterogeneous subpopulations; a constant D1 is compatible with two subpopulations moving in opposite directions.

Interpretation: behavioural distance is bimodal in cause — a rework ridge peaking in July, and a senior-assessment spike appearing only in October. July is the maximum *rework* deviation; October is the maximum *overall* deviation. These are different change points.

### 3.3 Cycle-time decomposition

Target: baseline 31.2h → July 44.2h (+13.0h, +42%) and → November 49.0h (+17.8h, +57%). Only bounded, order-of-magnitude shares are defensible from aggregates.

Anchors: recorded `Assess` service median 4.8h → 5.4h (+12.5% per execution). Expected assessments per case ≈ 1 + rework_rate: ~1.12 (Jan), ~1.29 (Jul), ~1.29 (Nov). Loop iterations add a `Request Info` + `Collect Documents` + re-`Assess` sequence, which is document-turnaround-dominated and therefore large relative to a 5h service step.

| component | Jul share of +13.0h | Nov share of +17.8h | basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| service time (per-execution) | **4-7%** (~0.6-0.9h) | 3-5% (~0.7h) | Direct: 0.6h × ~1.12-1.29 executions. Explicitly small; the "assessors are slower" story is not supported. |
| control-flow / rework (extra loop iterations) | **25-40%** (~3.3-5.2h) | 20-32% (~3.6-5.7h) | Rework variant +17.5pp; each loop adds ≥1 document round-trip. Includes the queueing *inside* the loop, so it overlaps with the queueing term — I treat "queueing attributable to loop-induced demand" as part of this bucket. |
| case mix (complexity, senior path) | **8-15%** (~1.0-2.0h) | **30-42%** (~5.3-7.5h) | Jul: high_complexity +7.5pp. Nov: +24.5pp and senior-assessment rate 4→22/100, adding a whole extra activity plus a second queue. |
| queueing / capacity (residual) | **40-58%** (~5.2-7.5h) | 25-42% (~4.5-7.5h) | Residual after the above. Corroborated by: reconstructed waiting rose "much more" than service; p90 grows faster than median (+53% vs +42% in Jul); p90/median ratio 2.19→2.35→2.47. Convex response to a −4 assessor capacity cut with +17% volume. |
| priority-rule redistribution | ~0 net, non-zero variance | ~0 net, non-zero variance | By construction the rule moves waiting between classes; it inflates the standard-class tail and deflates the high-complexity tail. Consistent with the widening p90/median, but **not separately identifiable** without class-stratified waiting. |

Shares are ranges, deliberately overlapping, and sum to roughly 100% only at their midpoints. The rework and queueing buckets are mutually mediated: rework raises demand, which raises utilization, which raises waiting for everyone including non-rework cases. Any decomposition presenting these as additive and disjoint is over-claiming.

### 3.5 One global change point or several?

Several — the data argue against a single global change point rather strongly. The series decouple:

- March moves the **logging** block (fitness, labels, duplicates, missingness) and moves nothing else: normalized D1 ≈ 3, median cycle time 31.2h (unchanged), rework 12% (unchanged).
- May moves the **control-flow** block (rework, `Request Info` loop, supplier share) with only a modest performance response (+17% median), and leaves the logging block flat.
- June-August moves the **performance** block (median +24% over three months, p90 +31%) while rework plateaus — i.e. degradation continues after its supposed behavioural cause stops growing.
- October moves the **population** block (complexity +15pp in one month, volume +13%) and the senior-assessment path, with rework rising again as a downstream consequence.

A single change-point model fitted to any one series would pick a different date per series (≈March for fitness, ≈May for rework, ≈June for cycle time, ≈October for complexity). That divergence *is* the evidence. The honest model is multiple mechanism-specific change points with different onset shapes: two step changes (March logging, May supplier), one gradual ramp (June-August capacity), one recurring seasonal shift (October), and one confounded mixed intervention (December). No formal change-point test has been run; the above are candidate dates for one (PELT/BinSeg on each series separately, or a Bayesian online change-point detector per metric), not test results.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 — global | month | Headline trend; executive reporting; anchor for all normalized comparisons | none (n ≥ 600/month always satisfied) | Overall trajectory; seasonality | Maximum aggregation bias — Simpson-type reversals; this level caused the original ambiguity and must never be used for attribution |
| L1 — logging regime | pre-migration (≤2026-02) / mixed (2026-03) / post-migration (≥2026-04); plus a per-case `label_variant` flag | Separate representation change from behaviour; decide whether mapping is admissible | ≥200 cases per regime; March split into old-label/new-label sub-logs (140/500 — both above threshold) | Raw vs mapped conformance; rename validation | Conditioning on the label the migration produced can bake the mapping assumption into every downstream result; always re-check the mapping, do not inherit it |
| L2 — supplier | Supplier Z / other, × month | Isolate the document-quality mechanism from composition | ≥100 cases per supplier-month (Z: 14-46% of 600-800 = 90-360, mostly OK; pool other suppliers) | Within-Z vs within-other rework; composition vs within-group decomposition | Supplier is a proxy for region and customer type; conditioning on it partly conditions on complexity |
| L3 — complexity | high / standard, × supplier × month | Handle the dominant population driver and the senior-assessment path | ≥80 cases per cell; collapse to quarter if a cell falls below | Composition-free cycle time; senior-path rate | **Complexity is a drifting stratifier** (inconsistent historical recording, plus the October surge). Conditioning on a post-treatment-recorded flag can induce collider bias |
| L4 — priority class | priority-eligible / not, × pre/post 2026-07-01 | Test the redistribution hypothesis | ≥150 cases per class per period (needs ≥2 months either side) | DiD on waiting time; queue-order analysis | Priority class is defined *by* complexity, so this is not an independent stratifier; the October complexity surge changes class sizes and thus the rule's own effect |
| L5 — resource/team | team; assessor tenure band; `resource_known` flag | Test the capacity mechanism; quantify missingness bias | ≥50 cases per team-month; teams below threshold pooled as "other"; always report the `resource_unknown` cell rather than dropping it | Workload vs waiting; handover change after the departures | ~6% non-random missingness concentrated in migrated activities; conditioning on `resource_known` selects a non-representative subsample |
| L6 — case-level covariate adjustment (not a hard split) | Regression / standardization on supplier, complexity, month-load, priority class, with month fixed effects | Recover per-mechanism effects without shattering the log into tiny cells | n ≥ 30 effective per covariate pattern; use direct standardization to the January mix rather than exhaustive stratification | Marginal effect estimates with uncertainty; Oaxaca composition-vs-behaviour split | Model-dependent; post-treatment adjustment (e.g. controlling for rework when estimating the supplier effect) blocks the very mediation path of interest |

**Primary monitoring population.** The **standard-complexity, non-Supplier-Z segment** should be the primary monitoring population, with the full log monitored alongside it.

Rationale: it is the largest and most stable stratum (roughly 45-60% of cases in every month, so never below ~300 cases — no small-cell problem), and it is the segment *least* exposed to the two known exogenous shocks. It therefore functions as a quasi-control. If rework, waiting, or fitness deteriorate *there*, the explanation cannot be "Supplier Z bundles" or "more complex cases", and must be systemic — shared capacity, the priority rule spilling delay onto non-priority work, or genuine behavioural adaptation. That is exactly the discrimination the aggregates currently fail to make. It also carries a specific, testable prediction from the priority hypothesis: after 2026-07-01 this segment's waiting should rise *even if* its own volume and mix are unchanged.

It must not be the *only* monitored population — it is by construction insensitive to supplier and seasonal drift, which is why the Supplier-Z and high-complexity strata are monitored in parallel with their own baselines.

## 7. Monitoring architecture

No universal threshold is used: each monitor has its own reference window, its own statistic, and a threshold calibrated to that statistic's own historical variability.

| monitor | statistic/artifact | reference window | alert logic | diagnostic follow-up | likely false-positive source | owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 Label vocabulary | Set of distinct activity labels; count and per-label frequency share | Trailing 30 days, plus a frozen approved-label allow-list | **Any** unseen label, or any known label whose share moves >5pp week-over-week → immediate page. Zero tolerance is correct here because the space is small and discrete | Diff the label set; check release notes and schema version; propose a mapping and re-run raw+mapped conformance before touching any behavioural dashboard | A legitimate new business activity; a staggered rollout producing both labels at once (as in March) | Data engineering |
| M2 Schema & attribute contract | Presence/type of case id, activity, timestamp, resource, supplier, complexity; % null per attribute per activity | Trailing 90 days per activity | Missingness exceeds max(2×trailing median, +1.5pp absolute) for any attribute-activity pair | Missingness model (logistic on activity/team/channel); quantify bias on resource-based KPIs; suppress resource dashboards until resolved | Genuinely optional fields on a new case type; batch backfills | Data engineering |
| M3 Technical/duplicate events | Rate of consecutive identical events per case; rate of `Open Case` and other non-business events per case | Trailing 60 days | Duplicate rate >1% or any step >0.5pp week-over-week | Confirm whether duplicates are emitted at source or introduced in ETL; add to the normalization pipeline; re-run conformance both ways | Legitimate retries; genuine repeated activities (a real second `Collect Documents` is not a duplicate) | Data engineering |

| M9 Service time | Median and IQR of `Assess` (and `Senior Assessment`) duration, per complexity band and per assessor tenure band | Trailing 90 days within band | Band median shifts >20% or +1.5h; or the cross-assessor spread doubles | Separate "harder cases" from "slower work": check within-band complexity, new-joiner ramp-up, tooling changes | Lifecycle/timestamp semantics changing what "duration" means; multitasking inflating recorded durations | Assessment team lead |
| M10 Case mix / population | Monthly shares of high-complexity, Supplier Z, region, channel; population stability index (PSI) vs a seasonal reference | Same month prior year for seasonal drivers; trailing 90 days for supplier/channel | PSI >0.10 = investigate, >0.25 = alert, **against the seasonally matched reference**, so a normal renewal season does not fire | Determine whether the shift is expected recurrence (matches prior-year shape) or novel; if novel, re-baseline downstream monitors | Recurring seasonality misread as drift — this is precisely the October trap | Business owner |

| M12 Outcomes & fairness | Approve/reject rate, decision rate per 100 cases, senior-assessment rate, SLA breach %, all stratified by complexity, supplier and priority class | Trailing 90 days within stratum; seasonally matched for annual comparisons | Any stratum's approval rate moving >5pp, or the between-class SLA gap widening, sustained 2 periods | Check whether load or the priority rule is changing decision quality (e.g. rushed approvals under backlog); review a sample of borderline decisions | Case-mix shift mechanically changing approval rates — always report standardized rates alongside crude ones | Business owner / risk |
| M13 Backlog & recovery | Open cases aged >p90; queue age distribution; time-to-drain estimate after a shock | Trailing 90 days | Backlog >1.5× baseline for 3 consecutive weeks, or a drain estimate exceeding one month | Distinguishes an active shock from hysteresis after a resolved shock (the September situation) | Seasonal arrivals; deliberate deferral policies | Capacity planning |

**Seasonality.** Any monitor whose driver is seasonal (M5, M8, M10, M12, M13) compares against a *seasonally matched* reference — same calendar month in prior years, or an STL-decomposed series where alerts fire on the remainder, not the raw level. Monitors on representation (M1-M4, M11-gap) use trailing windows because logging has no legitimate seasonality; a "seasonal" schema change is always a real change. Crucially, each seasonal monitor also emits a *recurrence verdict*: "matches prior-year shape within tolerance" (expected) vs "same direction, larger magnitude" (amplified — investigate the amplifier) vs "no prior-year analogue" (new drift). That verdict, not the level, is what distinguishes recurrence from drift.

**Multiple testing.** With ~13 monitors × several strata × weekly evaluation, naive 5% thresholds produce alerts constantly. Controls: (a) tier the monitors — M1-M4 are contract violations evaluated at near-zero tolerance and are exempt from FDR because they are deterministic checks, not statistical tests; (b) for the statistical monitors (M5-M13), apply Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control at q=0.10 **within each family** (performance, behaviour, population) rather than globally, so a population alert cannot mask a performance alert; (c) require **persistence** — two consecutive periods, or a CUSUM/EWMA crossing rather than a single-period exceedance — which suppresses most isolated false positives at modest detection delay; (d) restrict routine stratified evaluation to the pre-registered L2/L3 segments with adequate support, so the number of tests is fixed in advance rather than grown by exploratory slicing; (e) log every alert with its verdict so alert precision can be measured and thresholds recalibrated quarterly.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 1. validate
def validate(rows: Sequence[Dict[str, object]]) -> List[str]:
    """Sanity checks. Returns a list of human-readable problems (empty == OK)."""
    problems: List[str] = []
    months = [r["month"] for r in rows]

    if months != sorted(months):
        problems.append("months are not in chronological order")
    if len(set(months)) != len(months):
        problems.append("duplicate month keys present")

    for r in rows:
        m = r["month"]
        for pct_col in ("high_complexity_pct", "supplier_z_pct", "fast_variant_pct",
                        "rework_variant_pct", "missing_resource_pct",
                        "duplicate_open_case_pct"):
            v = r[pct_col]
            if not 0 <= v <= 100:
                problems.append(f"{m}: {pct_col}={v} out of [0,100]")
        for fit_col in ("raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness"):
            v = r[fit_col]
            if not 0 <= v <= 1:
                problems.append(f"{m}: {fit_col}={v} out of [0,1]")
        if r["mapped_model_fitness"] < r["raw_model_fitness"] - 1e-9:
            problems.append(
                f"{m}: mapped fitness below raw fitness — normalization should "
                f"not reduce conformance; check the mapping"
            )
        if r["p90_cycle_h"] < r["median_cycle_h"]:
            problems.append(f"{m}: p90 < median — impossible")
        if r["fast_variant_pct"] + r["rework_variant_pct"] > 100:
            problems.append(f"{m}: fast + rework variant shares exceed 100%")
        if r["cases"] <= 0:
            problems.append(f"{m}: non-positive case count")

    # DFG table consistency
    for m, rates in DFG_RATES.items():
        if len(rates) != len(DFG_EDGES):
            problems.append(f"DFG {m}: expected {len(DFG_EDGES)} rates, got {len(rates)}")
        if any(x < 0 for x in rates):
            problems.append(f"DFG {m}: negative rate")
        # Loop-closure sanity: RequestInfo->CollectDocs should not exceed
        # Assess->RequestInfo by more than rounding.
        d = dict(zip(DFG_EDGES, rates))
        if d["RequestInfo->CollectDocs"] > d["Assess->RequestInfo"] + 1:
            problems.append(f"DFG {m}: more RequestInfo->CollectDocs than Assess->RequestInfo")
        if m not in {r["month"] for r in rows}:
            problems.append(f"DFG month {m} absent from monthly summary")
    return problems

def baseline_stats(rows: Sequence[Dict[str, object]],
                   baseline_months: Sequence[str] = ("2026-01", "2026-02")
                   ) -> Dict[str, float]:
    """Mean of each numeric column over the baseline months."""
    base_rows = [r for r in rows if r["month"] in baseline_months]
    if not base_rows:
        raise ValueError("no baseline rows matched")
    return {c: statistics.fmean([r[c] for r in base_rows]) for c in NUMERIC_COLS}


def baseline_relative(rows, base) -> List[Dict[str, object]]:
    """Absolute delta vs baseline for pp-style columns, % change for levels."""
    PP_COLS = {"high_complexity_pct", "supplier_z_pct", "fast_variant_pct",
               "rework_variant_pct", "missing_resource_pct",
               "duplicate_open_case_pct", "raw_model_fitness",
               "mapped_model_fitness", "distinct_activity_labels"}
    out = []
    for r in rows:
        rec: Dict[str, object] = {"month": r["month"]}
        for c in NUMERIC_COLS:
            if c in PP_COLS:
                rec[c + "_delta"] = round(r[c] - base[c], 3)
            else:
                rec[c + "_pct_chg"] = round(100.0 * (r[c] - base[c]) / base[c], 1)
        # Tail-shape indicator: robust to level shifts, sensitive to queueing.
        rec["p90_over_median"] = round(r["p90_cycle_h"] / r["median_cycle_h"], 3)
        out.append(rec)
    return out


def month_over_month(rows) -> List[Dict[str, object]]:

    out = []
    for prev, cur in zip(rows, rows[1:]):
        rec: Dict[str, object] = {"month": cur["month"]}
        for c in NUMERIC_COLS:
            rec[c + "_mom"] = round(cur[c] - prev[c], 3)
        out.append(rec)
    return out


# ------------------------------------------------ 3. DFG distance measures
def l1_distance(a: Sequence[float], b: Sequence[float],
                skip: Sequence[int] = ()) -> float:
    """Sum of |rate_a - rate_b| over edges, in occurrences per 100 cases.

    Deliberately NOT normalized to a probability distribution: the supplied
    fragment is incomplete, so its rates do not sum to a constant and any
    simplex normalization would invent structure that is not in the data.
    """
    return round(sum(abs(x - y) for i, (x, y) in enumerate(zip(a, b))
                     if i not in set(skip)), 3)


def cosine_distance(a: Sequence[float], b: Sequence[float]) -> float:
    """Shape-only companion measure: insensitive to a uniform volume scaling."""
    dot = sum(x * y for x, y in zip(a, b))
    na = sum(x * x for x in a) ** 0.5
    nb = sum(y * y for y in b) ** 0.5
    return round(1.0 - dot / (na * nb), 5) if na and nb else float("nan")


def dfg_distance_table(reference: str = "2026-01") -> List[Dict[str, object]]:
    ref = DFG_RATES[reference]
    senior_idx = DFG_EDGES.index("Assess->SeniorAssessment")
    rows = []
    for m in sorted(DFG_RATES):
        cur = DFG_RATES[m]
        contrib = {e: round(c - r, 1)
                   for e, c, r in zip(DFG_EDGES, cur, ref) if abs(c - r) >= 1}
        rows.append({
            "month": m,
            "l1_all_edges": l1_distance(cur, ref),
            "l1_excl_senior": l1_distance(cur, ref, skip=[senior_idx]),
            "cosine_dist": cosine_distance(cur, ref),
            "top_moving_edges": contrib,
        })
    return rows


# ----------------------------------------- 4. raw-to-mapped fitness recovery
def fitness_recovery(rows) -> List[Dict[str, object]]:
    """How much conformance the label mapping + duplicate removal recovers.

    recovery_abs  = mapped - raw
    recovery_frac = (mapped - raw) / (1 - raw)  == share of the raw
                    conformance GAP that is explained by representation alone.
    """
    out = []
    for r in rows:
        raw, mapped = r["raw_model_fitness"], r["mapped_model_fitness"]
        gap = 1.0 - raw
        out.append({
            "month": r["month"],
            "raw": raw,
            "mapped": mapped,
            "recovery_abs": round(mapped - raw, 3),
            "recovery_frac_of_gap": round((mapped - raw) / gap, 3) if gap > 1e-9 else None,
            "residual_gap_after_mapping": round(1.0 - mapped, 3),
        })
    return out


# ------------------------------------------------- 5. candidate change points
def change_points(rows, k: float = 3.0) -> Dict[str, List[Dict[str, object]]]:
    """Simple, explainable rule — NOT a formal change-point test.

    For each metric: compute month-over-month first differences, take the
    median and the median absolute deviation (MAD) of those differences over
    the whole series, and flag any month whose difference exceeds
    median +/- k * (1.4826 * MAD). MAD is used instead of the standard
    deviation because the series are short (12 points) and contain the very
    outliers we are trying to detect, which would inflate an SD-based band.

    Metrics are grouped by the mechanism they are most sensitive to, so the
    output shows whether different mechanisms have different change points.
    """
    GROUPS = {
        "logging": ["raw_model_fitness", "distinct_activity_labels",
                    "missing_resource_pct", "duplicate_open_case_pct"],
        "behavioral": ["rework_variant_pct", "fast_variant_pct",
                       "mapped_model_fitness"],
        "performance": ["median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h"],
        "population": ["cases", "high_complexity_pct", "supplier_z_pct"],
    }
    diffs_by_col = {
        c: [(cur["month"], cur[c] - prev[c]) for prev, cur in zip(rows, rows[1:])]
        for c in NUMERIC_COLS
    }
    result: Dict[str, List[Dict[str, object]]] = {}
    for group, cols in GROUPS.items():
        flags = []
        for c in cols:
            series = [d for _, d in diffs_by_col[c]]
            med = statistics.median(series)
            mad = statistics.median([abs(d - med) for d in series])
            scale = 1.4826 * mad
            if scale < 1e-9:                    # degenerate: mostly-constant series
                scale = max(abs(med), 1e-6)
            lo, hi = med - k * scale, med + k * scale
            for month, d in diffs_by_col[c]:
                if d < lo or d > hi:
                    flags.append({
                        "metric": c, "month": month, "delta": round(d, 3),
                        "band": (round(lo, 3), round(hi, 3)),
                        "known_event": KNOWN_EVENTS.get(month, "-"),
                    })
        flags.sort(key=lambda f: f["month"])
        result[group] = flags
    return result

CAVEATS = """
CAVEATS — read before quoting any number above.
 1. The normalized DFG table covers 7 edges only and is explicitly "not a
    complete DFG". Edges for Open Case, Decision->Close, Senior Assessment
    ->Decision splits, and all infrequent paths are absent. Every distance
    reported here is therefore a LOWER BOUND on true behavioural change.
 2. All metrics are monthly aggregates over heterogeneous subpopulations
    (supplier, complexity, priority class, team). Aggregate stability is
    compatible with large offsetting changes in subgroups (Simpson's
    paradox). The priority rule is explicitly stated to have effects the
    monthly aggregate cannot reveal.
 3. Variant labels were normalized ONLY for the mapped-fitness series;
    other monthly columns come from operational tables. Cross-column
    comparisons therefore mix two normalization regimes.
 4. Change points below are flagged by a MAD-based first-difference rule on
    12 monthly points. They are CANDIDATES for a formal test (PELT, binary
    segmentation, Bayesian online CPD) applied to event-level data, not
    test results. With n=12 no method has real power against gradual drift.
 5. Alignment of a flagged month with a known calendar event is temporal
    coincidence, not causal identification. Several events overlap
    (May supplier / June staffing / July priority; October season /
    December intervention), so their effects are not separately estimable
    from these aggregates.
 6. Medians and p90s cannot be decomposed additively. The cycle-time
    component shares reported in the narrative are ranges derived from
    bounding arguments, not from a fitted model.
 7. Resource missingness is ~6% and explicitly non-random; any resource-
    based conclusion drawn from these aggregates is biased by an unknown
    amount and requires event-level missingness modelling.
"""

CASE_ID, ACTIVITY, TIMESTAMP = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"

# Semantic mapping applied ONLY to a copy of the log. Keeping this as data
# (not code) makes the assumption auditable: it asserts that Eligibility
# Screen MEANS Validate Eligibility, which is a hypothesis, not a fact.
LABEL_MAP = {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}
TECHNICAL_EVENTS = {"Open Case"}


def prepare_log(df, case_col="case_id", activity_col="activity",
                timestamp_col="timestamp"):
    """(1) Format a raw dataframe into a PM4Py-conformant event log."""
    import pm4py
    out = df.copy()
    out = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        out, case_id=case_col, activity_key=activity_col,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_col,
    )
    return pm4py.convert_to_event_log(out) if False else out  # keep as dataframe


def slice_window(log, start: str, end: str, mode: str = "traces_contained"):
    """(2) Slice a time window.

    mode='traces_contained' keeps only cases entirely inside the window —
    the right choice for cycle-time comparison, because it avoids truncation
    bias from cases still open at the window edge. mode='events' keeps events
    only, which is right for DFG/throughput but breaks case-level durations.
    Under a growing backlog these two give materially different answers, so
    the mode must always be stated alongside any result.
    """
    import pm4py
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(log, start, end, mode=mode)


def normalize_labels(log, label_map=None, drop_technical=True):
    """(7) Produce a NORMALIZED COPY; the original log is untouched.

    Returns (normalized_log, report) where report records exactly how many
    events were renamed or dropped — never normalize silently.
    """
    label_map = LABEL_MAP if label_map is None else label_map
    norm = log.copy()
    report = {"renamed": {}, "technical_dropped": 0, "consecutive_dupes_dropped": 0}

    for old, new in label_map.items():
        n = int((norm[ACTIVITY] == old).sum())
        if n:
            report["renamed"][f"{old} -> {new}"] = n
    norm[ACTIVITY] = norm[ACTIVITY].replace(label_map)

    if drop_technical:
        mask = norm[ACTIVITY].isin(TECHNICAL_EVENTS)
        report["technical_dropped"] = int(mask.sum())
        norm = norm[~mask]

    # Remove only CONSECUTIVE identical events within a case: a genuine repeat
    # of Collect Documents after Request Info is real rework and must survive.
    norm = norm.sort_values([CASE_ID, TIMESTAMP])
    dupe = (norm[ACTIVITY] == norm.groupby(CASE_ID)[ACTIVITY].shift(1))
    report["consecutive_dupes_dropped"] = int(dupe.sum())
    return norm[~dupe].reset_index(drop=True), report


def monthly_dfgs(log, month_col="month"):
    """(3) Discover a DFG per month. Returns {month: (dfg, start, end)}."""
    import pm4py
    work = log.copy()
    work[month_col] = work[TIMESTAMP].dt.to_period("M").astype(str)
    return {m: pm4py.discover_dfg(g) for m, g in work.groupby(month_col)}


def normalized_dfg_rates(dfg, n_cases: int):
    """Convert raw DFG edge counts into occurrences per 100 cases.

    This is the single most important normalization in the whole analysis:
    raw counts confound volume with behaviour, and volume rose 29% by
    November for reasons that have nothing to do with how work is done.
    """
    if not n_cases:
        return {}
    return {edge: 100.0 * cnt / n_cases for edge, cnt in dfg.items()}


def dfg_l1_distance(rates_a, rates_b):
    """Transparent L1 distance over the union of edges (per 100 cases).

    Missing edges are treated as rate 0, which assumes the edge is truly
    absent rather than merely unobserved — reasonable for a complete DFG
    from a full log, but NOT for the partial fragment used in Part A.
    """
    edges = set(rates_a) | set(rates_b)
    return sum(abs(rates_a.get(e, 0.0) - rates_b.get(e, 0.0)) for e in edges)


def monthly_variants(log):
    """(4) Variant distribution per month, plus concentration measures."""
    import pm4py
    import math
    work = log.copy()
    work["month"] = work[TIMESTAMP].dt.to_period("M").astype(str)
    out = {}
    for m, g in work.groupby("month"):
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(g)
        counts = {k: (len(v) if hasattr(v, "__len__") else v)
                  for k, v in variants.items()}
        total = sum(counts.values()) or 1
        probs = [c / total for c in counts.values()]
        top20 = sum(sorted(counts.values(), reverse=True)[:20]) / total
        out[m] = {
            "n_variants": len(counts),
            "top20_share": top20,
            "entropy_bits": -sum(p * math.log2(p) for p in probs if p > 0),
            "counts": counts,
        }
    return out


def temporal_profile_reference(log, ref_start: str, ref_end: str):
    """(5) Fit a temporal profile on a CLEAN reference window.

    The reference must predate the migration (2026-01..2026-02); fitting on a
    contaminated window would encode the drift into the baseline.
    """
    import pm4py
    ref = slice_window(log, ref_start, ref_end, mode="traces_contained")
    return pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(ref)


def temporal_profile_deviations(log, profile, zeta: float = 6.0):
    """Conformance against the temporal profile; zeta = allowed z-score.

    zeta is high (6) on purpose: durations are heavy-tailed, and a lower
    threshold makes the deviating-case share track volume rather than drift.
    """
    import pm4py
    return pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(log, profile, zeta=zeta)


def declarative_models(log, skeleton_noise: float = 0.05,
                       declare_support: float = 0.9):
    """(6) Optional declarative views — good at catching ORDERING drift that
    a DFG smooths over (e.g. 'Assess always before Approve' breaking)."""
    import pm4py
    return {
        "log_skeleton": pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(log, noise_threshold=skeleton_noise),
        "declare": pm4py.discover_declare(log, min_support_ratio=declare_support),
    }


def dual_conformance(raw_log, net, im, fm, label_map=None):
    """(7) Raw vs normalized conformance side by side, original preserved.

    The GAP (mapped - raw) is the logging-drift signal; the LEVEL of mapped
    fitness is the behavioural-drift signal. Reporting only one of them is
    what made the March collapse look like an operational breakdown.
    """
    import pm4py
    norm_log, report = normalize_labels(raw_log, label_map)
    raw_fit = pm4py.fitness_alignments(raw_log, net, im, fm)
    map_fit = pm4py.fitness_alignments(norm_log, net, im, fm)
    r, m = raw_fit["log_fitness"], map_fit["log_fitness"]
    return {
        "raw_fitness": r,
        "mapped_fitness": m,
        "recovery_abs": m - r,
        "recovery_frac_of_gap": (m - r) / (1 - r) if r < 1 else None,
        "normalization_report": report,
    }


def segmented_analysis(log, segment_cols=("supplier", "complexity"),
                       min_cases: int = 80):
    """Stratified DFG + rework + cycle time, enforcing a minimum-support rule.

    Cells below min_cases are reported as suppressed rather than silently
    dropped — a suppressed cell is a known unknown, a dropped one is a lie.
    """
    import pm4py
    results, suppressed = {}, []
    for key, g in log.groupby(list(segment_cols)):
        n_cases = g[CASE_ID].nunique()
        if n_cases < min_cases:
            suppressed.append({"segment": key, "n_cases": n_cases})
            continue
        dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(g)
        results[key] = {
            "n_cases": n_cases,
            "rates_per_100": normalized_dfg_rates(dfg, n_cases),
        }
    return {"segments": results, "suppressed": suppressed,
            "min_cases_rule": min_cases}

## 9. Provisional attribution

**Strongest conclusions (high confidence, ~0.9+).** The March conformance collapse is a logging/semantic event, not an operational one. Three independent lines converge: the mapped series barely moves (0.93→0.90), the normalized DFG distance for March is 3 occurrences per 100 cases — inside the January-February noise band — and the behavioural and performance metrics (median 31.2h, rework 12%) are flat. A migration that changed real work would not be repaired by a rename and a delete. Equally strong: the May rework increase is genuinely behavioural at the log level, since it appears in label-invariant, post-mapping rates (`Assess->Request Info` 12→24 per 100 cases) and its timing matches 2026-05-05 to the month.

**Plausible but not established (~0.6-0.75).** That Supplier Z *caused* the rework rather than merely growing as a share is likely but confounded — supplier share and rework rose in the same month, and Supplier Z cases were already more complex. The September reversal (supplier 38→24%, loop 29→20/100) is the best evidence for causation, because a pure composition story requires the rework-prone group to shrink by exactly the right amount, which is possible but less parsimonious. Similarly, the staffing hypothesis for June-August is well supported in *shape* — waiting grew far more than service (4.8→5.4h only), degradation continued while rework plateaued, and the p90/median ratio widened from 2.19 to 2.44 — but the effect size cannot be separated from backlog carry-over from May.

**Where mediation or interaction blocks clean attribution.** Three places. First, rework and queueing are causally chained: more loops means more assessments means higher utilization means longer waits for everyone. Assigning separate additive shares to them is not defensible, which is why my cycle-time decomposition uses overlapping ranges. Second, the priority rule has essentially no aggregate signature by design — it moves delay between classes. The widening tail is consistent with it but equally consistent with plain overload; I have no class-stratified evidence and I decline to claim an effect. Third, December is quadruply confounded: seasonal decline, overtime, quality campaign, and fewer working days all land together. I attribute the December improvement to "seasonal ebb plus intervention, shares unknown" and nothing sharper.

**A specific caution.** The October `Senior Assessment` jump (7→22 per 100) is larger than the complexity share increase (26→41%) can explain by composition alone, but the timeline says this distinction was historically recorded inconsistently — so part of it may be improved recording riding along with the seasonal surge. I hold this at roughly 50/50 between behaviour and logging.

**What would revise the account.** (a) If within-Supplier-Z rework was flat across 2026-05-05, the supplier mechanism collapses into pure composition. (b) If class-stratified waiting shows no crossover after 2026-07-01, drop the priority-redistribution hypothesis entirely. (c) If prior-year logs show no October complexity spike, the seasonal story becomes a novel population drift needing its own cause. (d) If the March sub-log comparison shows the 500 `Eligibility Screen` cases behaving differently downstream from the 140 old-label cases, the mapping is unsafe and my strongest conclusion weakens. (e) If the normative model turns out never to have permitted `Senior Assessment`, much of the residual mapped-fitness decline is a modelling artefact rather than drift.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. `IF raw conformance drops sharply while mapped conformance stays within its normal band AND the activity-label vocabulary or duplicate-event rate changed in the same period, THEN classify the event as logging/semantic drift, freeze behavioural alerting for that period, and route to data engineering for a mapping and a re-run — do not open an operational investigation, BECAUSE alignment cost is dominated by unmapped labels and technical events, so a rename can move fitness by tenths without any change in how work is performed.`

2. `IF mapped conformance declines while the label vocabulary, duplicate rate, and attribute-missingness are all stable, THEN treat it as real behavioural drift, decompose the alignment deviations by activity, and check the normative model's version date before escalating, BECAUSE representation-invariant conformance loss must originate either in changed behaviour or in a normative model that no longer encodes current policy, and those two require opposite responses.`

3. `IF the loop rate (Assess->Request Info per 100 cases) rises by more than 6 per 100 sustained over two periods, THEN decompose the rise into composition and within-group components by supplier and complexity before contacting any supplier, BECAUSE an aggregate rework increase can be produced entirely by growth of a rework-prone subpopulation, and acting on the aggregate would escalate to a supplier whose own per-case quality never changed.`

4. `IF cycle time deteriorates while recorded service time is nearly flat and reconstructed waiting rises steeply, THEN classify it as a capacity/queueing problem and escalate to capacity planning with the utilization series rather than to the assessment team, BECAUSE waiting responds convexly to utilization while service time reflects task difficulty, so a large waiting-to-service ratio identifies a demand-capacity imbalance rather than slower work.`

5. `IF the p90/median cycle-time ratio widens by more than 10% while the median moves little, THEN immediately split waiting time by priority class and test for a crossover, BECAUSE a queue-discipline change redistributes delay between classes and is nearly invisible in aggregate central tendency but shows up as tail dispersion — and if standard cases are absorbing the delay, that is a service-equity issue an aggregate SLA will never surface.`

6. `IF a population metric shifts sharply but matches the same calendar period in prior years in both direction and magnitude, THEN label it expected recurrence, re-baseline downstream performance thresholds to the seasonal reference, and suppress the derived performance alerts, BECAUSE seasonal case-mix shifts mechanically move cycle time and variant shares without any process change, and treating recurrence as drift both wastes investigation and desensitizes the alerting system.`

7. `IF a driver is known to have been removed (supplier recovered, capacity restored, season ended) but the affected metric has recovered less than about two-thirds of the way to its pre-shock level after two periods, THEN open a backlog/hysteresis investigation and measure time-to-drain rather than declaring the incident closed, BECAUSE queueing systems recover with a lag governed by residual utilization, so incomplete recovery signals either a persistent capacity shortfall or a behavioural adaptation that outlived its original cause.`
