## **2. Integrated explanation**

The observed patterns emerge from **interacting, non-linear effects** where no single mechanism dominates. The system operates as a **dynamic feedback loop** influenced by:

### **Early Shift (06:00–14:00): The "Backlog Clearing" Phase**
- **Case-mix/selection bias**: Early shifts inherit **night/weekend backlogs** (high complexity, high priority), forcing workers to prioritize complex cases first. Seniors, who handle complex cases, show **stable service times (11.8 min)** but **low errors (2.2%)**, suggesting they adapt quickly.
- **Learning/warm-up**: Juniors and mid-level staff exhibit **service time reductions (19.6  13.7 min)** in the first 30 events, but **error rates rise under high WIP (12.4%)**, indicating that learning is **context-dependent** (e.g., fatigue sets in when queue pressure is high).
- **Circadian effects**: Early shifts have **higher complexity share (48%)**, but service times are **slower (12.7 min)**—likely due to **fatigue from prior night shifts** (night staff’s **17.5  14.9 min** suggests this).

### **Mid Shift (09:00–17:00): Stabilization and Batching**
- **Queue pressure moderates**: As WIP decreases (30–70), service times **slightly drop (11.6 min)** but errors **increase (5.6%)** for high-complexity cases. This suggests **workarounds** (e.g., Juniors batch Document Review tasks) **reduce individual efficiency** but **increase error risk** when pressure is high.
- **Batching emerges**: Juniors’ **9–18 event sequences** with **15.2 min service times** indicate **sequential processing**, likely to **minimize handoffs** but **increase fatigue-induced errors** (e.g., misclassifications corrected later).

### **Late Shift (14:00–22:00): Queue Pressure Dominates**
- **Service time collapses (10.8 min)** but **errors spike (7.9%)**—not due to fatigue alone, but **because high WIP forces rushed decisions**. The **queue length rises (62  88)**, creating **higher error rates** (especially for Juniors, who show **12.4% errors under high WIP**).
- **Circadian effects persist**: Late shifts inherit **day-shift backlogs**, leading to **higher complexity share (35%)** and **slower service times (11.4 min)** despite lower WIP.

### **Night Shift (22:00–06:00): Urgency and Automation**
- **Night staff handle fewer cases but more urgent exceptions**: Service times are **longer (15.2 min)** due to **lower throughput**, but errors are **moderate (5.1%)**—likely because **complexity is higher (56%)**, and workers prioritize correctness over speed.
- **Bot_Risk interactions**: Automated risk classification is **instant**, but **human corrections spike when queue pressure is high (13.7% rework)**, suggesting **automation fails under stress** and **workers compensate by over-correcting**.

### **Why Service Time Falls While Errors Rise in Some Conditions**
- **Service time drops** when **queue pressure is high (WIP > 70)** because workers **prioritize speed over quality** (e.g., Juniors’ **9.4 min service time but 5.8% errors** under low-complexity conditions).
- **Errors rise** when:
  - **Complexity + WIP intersect** (e.g., **16.8 min + 10.9% errors** for high-complexity cases under high WIP).
  - **Batching creates fatigue** (e.g., Juniors’ **13.7 min service time** followed by **12.4% errors**).
  - **Automation fails under stress** (e.g., Bot_Risk corrections when `queue_length_at_start > 70`).

The system is **not purely learning-driven** (e.g., Juniors’ late-shift errors suggest fatigue) nor purely queue-pressure-driven (e.g., early-shift Seniors handle complex cases efficiently). Instead, it is a **network of interacting factors** where **case-mix, experience, and workload dynamics** create a **non-linear performance surface**.

## **5. Ethical and operational cautions**

### **Worker-Level Conclusions Are Unfair**
- **Experience-based assumptions**: Juniors’ **12.4% errors under high WIP** may reflect **selection bias** (e.g., they handle more complex cases later in shifts). Seniors’ **stable performance** could be due to **case-mix preferences**, not innate ability.
- **Queue pressure as management artifact**: If interventions like **WIP caps** are imposed without analyzing **root causes** (e.g., case-mix distribution), they may **create new inefficiencies**.

### **Measurement Errors and Bias**
- **Case-mix confounding**: Early shifts have **higher complexity**, but service times are **slower**—this could be due to **selection bias** (seniors take complex cases) or **learning**.
- **Automation quality**: Bot_Risk’s **instant service time** may mask **human error correction costs**, leading to **over-punitive interventions** (e.g., penalizing late-shift errors).

### **Punitive Use of Error Rates**
- **Late-shift errors  worker fault**: Errors spike when **WIP > 70** (not just fatigue), and **Juniors** (not Seniors) show the worst performance under pressure.
- **Rework  error**: Some rework is **corrective** (e.g., Quality Check delays), not **failure**.

### **Operational Risks**
- **Interventions may backfire**: Forcing random assignments could **disrupt workflows**; capping shifts may **reduce throughput**.
- **Queue pressure is systemic**: If **WIP > 70** is a result of **demand spikes**, interventions like **alerts** may not solve the root cause.
