## 3. Robust redesign package

Design principle: **shape the inflow, stop defects early, protect lanes, cap WIP, stay elastic.** Capacity is added only as trigger-gated elasticity, never as standing capacity everywhere.

- **R1 – SLA-risk-based dispatch + station WIP caps (replaces I1).** Priority = remaining time to deadline (Urgent 18h, internal Complex target ~96h, Routine 72h), with a Complex quota at prep/assay so machine blocks aren't shattered and Complex isn't starved. Hard WIP caps per station; when caps bind, escalation rather than queueing. *Robust because*: works in S1–S3, S5 without knowing which disruption hits. *Assumptions*: reliable timestamps; dispatch automation. *Downstream overload*: deliberately raises Routine queues (capped: no Routine case > ~96–120h without escalation — an explicit, governed trade-off, not an accident). *Failure mode*: sustained S5 starves Routine; next bottleneck is MV/specialist where Urgent+Complex converge  pair with P-A.
- **R2 – Early pre-analytical defect triage 2h (I6) + fast-track recollection + auto-cancel hygiene.** Kills the 42–60h loops before they consume assay capacity and clerk time; recollection is 2.9x normal at peak. *Downstream overload*: cancellations rise (already 42% of recollections)  registration handles re-orders/cancellations; triage consumes pre-check capacity at 96%  pair with R3. *Assumption*: physicians redraw promptly; *failure mode*: demand doesn't disappear — cancelled cases may return later as Repeat-after-failure.
- **R3 – Courier smoothing (I7) + arrival-shaped staffing.** Staff pre-check/intake ~90 min before the 10:00/17:00 waves; add micro-pickup slots. *Robust*: directly targets S4, helps S1. *Downstream overload*: pushes feed into night prep/assay where repeats cluster  mandatory night QC audits and R5 monitoring. *Synergy*: smoother feed improves batch efficiency without larger batches.
- **R4 – Automated registration for digital-complete orders (I8).** Removes part of the 5–8h registerreceive edge and error-driven accession load (140/wk at peak, only 2 specialists). *Sequencing matters*: relief lands on pre-check (96%) and assay (98%) — activate only alongside R3/R5. *Failure mode*: systematic metadata errors propagate silently; keep a manual audit sample.
- **R5 – Assay lane design: M1 dedicated to Urgent+Complex (I3) with protected slots; M2 carries Routine with QC gates and predictive maintenance.** The variant evidence (repeats cluster after M2/night shifts) makes M2 maintenance a rework lever, not just an uptime lever. *Downstream overload*: TV now receives two distinct release streams — schedule TV staffing to the lane pattern. *Failure mode*: M1 becomes a single point of failure (see §6); revert trigger if M2-routed repeat rate doubles.
- **R6 – Routine-only dynamic batching (I2).** Batch 24 **or** 4h timer, whichever first; Urgent never mixed; dispatch rate capped to assay absorption. *Downstream overload*: batch releases arrive as bursts into assay/TV — the cap prevents a TV wave. *Monitoring*: keep urgent bypass 85%.
- **R7 – Elastic cross-training pool (I4, trigger-based).** Cross-train 2 TVaccession and 2 prepregistration/intake; redeploy only on triggers with a **TV staffing floor** (TV already runs at 101% — a standing transfer, as I4 proposes, would trade an accession queue for a worse TV queue). *Failure mode*: in compound scenarios the floor itself is insufficient; this is borrowed capacity, not new capacity.
- **MV/specialist protection (process, not headcount).** Protected MV slots for Urgent; pre-assembly of specialist-review materials *before* entering hold, to compress the 48h holdMV dwell for Complex.

**Explicitly rejected:** I9 (holding Routine releases) — it adds waiting at the last step, conflicts with visibility and the 18h logic, and saves little; I1 in strict form; I2 without a timer; I4 as a standing transfer.

## 6. Residual vulnerabilities

1. **M1 single point of failure (caused by the redesign).** Dedicating M1 to Urgent+Complex concentrates both critical classes on one machine; an M1 outage during S1 leaves no fast lane. Mitigation (emergency swap protocol, P-B) exists but erodes batching and lane purity when used.
2. **Triage-induced demand distortion (caused by the redesign).** I6 will raise early cancellations; the "missing" demand can resurface as Repeat-after-failure orders or physician escalations, and rejection friction may degrade data quality at source.
3. **Routine starvation is a designed outcome, not an accident.** In sustained S5, Routine p90 will exceed the already-observed 120h. This needs explicit clinical/governance sign-off and a hard escalation cap (~96–120h), otherwise it becomes invisible harm.
4. **TV borrowing.** Every redeployment from TV (101% at peak) is borrowed time; the staffing floor may not hold in compound scenarios.
5. **Partner co-variance (P-B).** The partner lab serves the same region in the same winter wave; 12h transport plus their queue can exceed the hold queue it replaces, and chain-of-custody gaps create compliance exposure.
6. **Order-level log blindness.** The log is flattened per lab order, so a Complex panel with multiple tubes counts once at registration but consumes several tube-level prep/assay operations. As Complex share rises toward 20–24%, prep/assay load is systematically underestimated — capacity math calibrated on order counts will be optimistic exactly when it matters.
7. **Night-quality coupling.** R3 smoothing pushes work into night shifts where repeats already cluster; without night QC investment, queue risk converts into rework risk.
8. **Labor elasticity ceiling.** The 9h overtime error threshold caps shift extension; S5 likely exceeds it, feeding the accession queue.
9. **Median-monitoring trap.** Urgent median was 17h in peak — "acceptable on average" while p90 sat at 33h. Any dashboard built on medians will hide exactly the failures that incur penalties.
