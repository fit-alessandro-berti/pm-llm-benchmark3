## 1. Conformance semantics

**Forbidden behavior** is present whenever a relation explicitly marked `forbidden` occurs within its stated scope. The norm closes the world only there: if `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` (N3) appears for the same Order, or `Order` and `Payment` share `object_cobirth` (T6), or a `Sensor Heartbeat` touches `Invoice` (E10), the observation is non-compliant regardless of frequency. The only legitimate debate is whether the event is a data artifact; the normative classification remains `forbidden`.

**Missing mandatory behavior** applies only when a relation marked `mandatory` is expected within an explicitly defined scope and at the correct abstraction level. The world is open everywhere else. For example, `Create Order -> Validate Order` (N1) is mandatory for “every newly created standard Order,” but the norm notes that the OC-DFG uses a directly-follows approximation of an “eventually” rule. Therefore, absence in the direct adjacency graph is insufficient, by itself, to declare non-compliance; we must ask whether validation is missing eventually, whether the Order is “standard,” and whether an unrecorded intermediate event (e.g., review) breaks adjacency. Similarly, `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` (N4) applies only to packages “intended for external shipment”; if the two missing cases are internal transfers, there is no gap.

**Unexpected but unregulated behavior** covers relations absent from the normative tables. `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72 Orders), `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer` (3 events), and `Package`–`Delivery` `object_codeath` (7) fall here. Because the norm is open-world except for forbidden and mandatory-in-scope edges, these are neither violations nor confirmations of compliance. They indicate process reality that the norm deliberately does not govern.

**Typical-pattern absence** refers to `typical` relations that are common but not required. `Validate Order -> Close Order` (N2) is typical, yet 83 direct closures and intermediate invoices or reviews are both legitimate. `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` (N5) is typical (92/110), but the remaining 18—including 4 `Write Off` and 14 with no observed successor—are legitimate exceptions (credit terms, disputes, open terms, log truncation). Absence here is not a deviation class; it is an expected variance.

**Likely data-quality error** is diagnosed when a forbidden or mandatory relation conflicts with domain logic and is explained by a technical artifact rather than business behavior. The `Sensor Heartbeat`–`Invoice` link (E10) is suspected to arise from a reused numeric identifier join. The two `Dispatch Package` events lacking a `Delivery` relation (E6, 198/200) both carry a carrier reference in an event attribute, suggesting the object link is missing, not the delivery itself. **Abstraction mismatch** occurs when a rule is defined at one granularity (e.g., “eventually validate”) but measured at another (directly-follows adjacency). **Insufficient evidence** applies when counts are small, scope is ambiguous, or snapshot boundaries truncate lifecycles. The 2 missing `Add Item`–`Order` links (E2, 1448/1450), the 1 `Reopen Order -> Close Order` (N6), and the 14 invoices with no successor may reflect log truncation, rare legitimate variants, or extraction noise. Without event-level inspection, these remain unclassified beyond “potential gap within scope.”

**Open-world vs. closed-world assumptions.** The norm closes the world only for relations marked `forbidden` and for `mandatory` columns within the stated scope. For all other relations—`typical`, `unspecified`, and any relation not listed—the absence of an edge is not evidence of non-compliance, and the presence of an unlisted edge is not evidence of error. This prevents treating `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` or `Reopen Order -> Close Order` as violations merely because they are absent from the normative edge set.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG
The OC-DFG can establish direct-follows adjacency frequencies between activities for each object type, but it **cannot** establish temporal ordering beyond adjacency, eventual consistency, or the presence of intermediate activities that were not recorded or extracted. For `Order`, 98 of 100 show `Create Order -> Validate Order` (N1). The norm states that every newly created standard Order should “eventually” enter validation, and the summary explicitly approximates this with a directly-follows expectation. Because direct adjacency is an abstraction, the two missing edges **cannot** be declared violations without first verifying that validation never occurred and that the Orders are “standard.” If an unrecorded review event sits between creation and validation, the direct-follows arrow disappears while the mandatory rule remains satisfied. This is the central danger of interpreting an “eventually expected” business rule as a directly-follows rule: it converts legitimate intermediate steps or extraction gaps into false positives.

`Validate Order -> Close Order` (N2, 83 cases) is `typical`. The OC-DFG also records `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72 cases) and `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review` (3 cases). These are unregulated intermediates. The presence of 83 direct closures does not prove that intermediate steps are forbidden; it merely shows that direct closure is common. Conversely, the absence of a direct arrow for the remaining 17 validated orders does not indicate non-compliance, because N2 permits intermediates.

`Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` (N3, 2 cases) is `forbidden` within the same Order scope. Because the norm closes the world for forbidden relations, these 2 cases are violations regardless of abstraction issues—there is no “intermediate event” that can legitimize dispatching a cancelled order.

`Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` (N4, 198/200) is `mandatory` for packages intended for external shipment. The OC-DFG cannot determine shipment intent; it only sees adjacency. The two missing edges could represent internal packages (out of scope) or missing `Pack` events. Thus, the OC-DFG establishes a potential gap but **cannot** establish non-compliance without scope clarification.

`Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` (N5, 92/110) and `Issue Invoice -> Write Off` (4 cases) are `typical`. The 14 invoices with no observed successor may be open at the snapshot boundary. The OC-DFG cannot distinguish “not yet paid” from “never paid”; it can only report adjacency absence.

`Reopen Order -> Close Order` (N6, 1 case) is `unspecified`. The OC-DFG records it, but the norm assigns no status, so it is unregulated.

### OTG
The OTG captures object-level interactions (`object_interaction`), co-birth (`object_cobirth`), and co-death (`object_codeath`), weighted by frequency. It **cannot** establish event-level membership, temporal sequence, or mandatory presence per event. For example, `Order`–`Item` (`object_interaction`, 1450) is mandatory for `Add Item` (T1). The weight confirms that the interaction is widespread, but it does not reveal that 2 `Add Item` events lack an `Order` link in the ET-OT layer (1448/1450). This discrepancy highlights that OTG and ET-OT measure different things: OTG measures object interaction over lifetimes; ET-OT measures event-object pairing. A relation can appear “complete” in OTG but “incomplete” in ET-OT if extraction rules or event attributes differ.

The ET-OT layer links event types to object types with per-event weights. ## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

**1. Temporal abstraction for mandatory rules (N1).**
The norm states that validation is “eventually” expected but summarizes it with a directly-follows OC-DFG relation. **Operational risk:** False-positive escalation of 2/100 Orders that may have validated after an unrecorded intermediate step. **Clarification:** Specify whether conformance requires direct adjacency, adjacency within N steps, or eventual occurrence within a defined window; state whether intermediate unrecorded events are permitted. **Status:** Keep `mandatory`, but add an explicit `abstraction_clause` (e.g., “direct-follows is an approximation; eventual consistency within process completion is required”).

**2. Scope criteria for package shipment (N4).**
N4 applies to “packages intended for external shipment,” but the object model does not distinguish external from internal intent in the `Package` type alone. **Operational risk:** The 2 missing `Pack -> Dispatch` edges may be misclassified as violations when they are in-scope internal transfers. **Clarification:** Add an object attribute (`shipment_type`) or subtype (`ExternalPackage`) to determine scope unambiguously. **Status:** `mandatory` with an explicit `scope_condition`.

**3. Exception representation for payments (E8 / N5).**
E8 allows exceptions “explicitly typed as unapplied cash,” yet “none are so typed in this snapshot.” **Operational risk:** The 1 `Record Payment` without an `Invoice` and the 18 open/write-off invoices may be misclassified because the exception typing mechanism is undefined. **Clarification:** Define the event subtype or attribute value that marks “unapplied cash,” and specify how `Write Off` and credit-note events are represented in the ET-OT and OC-DFG layers. **Status:** `mandatory` with a formally defined `exception_type`; the absence of typed exceptions should be `unspecified` rather than assumed non-compliant.

**4. Lifecycle synchronization rules (`object_cobirth` and `object_codeath`).**
T6 forbids `Order`–`Payment` `cobirth`, but the norm says nothing about `codeath` (e.g., `Package`–`Delivery`, 7 cases). **Operational risk:** Shared termination events may indicate duplicate linking or correct co-completion; without a rule, analysts cannot distinguish error from design. **Clarification:** Explicitly state whether `codeath` is permitted, forbidden, or `typical` for each object pair, and define conditions (e.g., shared confirmation event). **Status:** Explicitly `unspecified` for `Package`–`Delivery` `codeath` unless a separate lifecycle policy governs it.

**5. Statistical interpretation and tolerance.**
The norm states no minimum frequencies or statistical tolerance bands. **Operational risk:** Small counts (2/200, 1/100) trigger binary escalation that may be sampling noise rather than systemic failure. **Clarification:** State whether conformance is evaluated per-instance (binary) or requires aggregate thresholds; if binary, specify that small counts still require investigation but not necessarily process redesign. **Status:** Explicitly `unspecified` regarding statistical tolerance, with a recommendation to maintain monitoring bands separately.

**6. Cross-family consistency (OTG vs. ET-OT).**
The OTG reports 200 `Package`–`Delivery` interactions, while ET-OT reports 198 `Dispatch Package` events with `Delivery`. **Operational risk:** Analysts may conclude conformance from OTG alone while missing event-level gaps visible only in ET-OT. **Clarification:** Specify reconciliation rules: if OTG and ET-OT disagree, which layer takes precedence? Define whether interaction weights include events other than the primary event type (e.g., updates or confirmations). **Status:** This is a meta-norm clarification; it should be `unspecified` in the business norm but `mandatory` in the analysis protocol.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

### Immediate containment (prioritize highest severity)
- **N3 (`Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`, 2 cases):** Implement a system-level gate preventing dispatch for any Order whose latest state is `Cancel`. This is a hard `forbidden` rule with direct business and financial risk.
- **E10 (`Sensor Heartbeat`->`Invoice`, 1 case):** Immediately isolate the sensor heartbeat feed from any join pipeline that uses numeric identifiers shared with financial objects. Prevent corruption of invoice data.

### Data repair
- **E10:** Repair the join mapping; verify that no invoice records were incorrectly updated or linked.
- **E6 (`Dispatch Package`->`Delivery` missing, 2 cases):** Inspect the 2 dispatch events with carrier references; either insert the missing `Delivery` object links or formalize an attribute-based tracking exception.
- **N4 (`Pack Package` missing, 2 cases):** Verify whether the 2 packages have missing `Pack` events or are internal shipments; repair logs or scope classifications accordingly.
- **E2 (`Add Item`->`Order` missing, 2 cases):** Inspect event attributes; repair object links if present in raw data.
- **E8 (`Record Payment`->`Invoice` missing, 1 case):** Confirm with finance staff; if unapplied cash, apply the correct event type; if erroneous, repair the link or correct the invoice reference.

### Process investigation
- **N3:** Determine whether the 2 cancellations were applied retroactively (system state error) or whether dispatch occurred despite an active cancellation (process bypass).
- **T6 (`Order` `cobirth` `Payment`):** Investigate the integration stream that produced the co-birth; check for timestamp rounding or batch-merge errors.
- **N1 (`Create Order`->`Validate Order` missing, 2 cases):** Examine the full lifecycle of the 2 Orders to determine if validation occurred eventually or if they are non-standard orders outside scope.
- **N5 (`Issue Invoice` open/write-off, 18 cases):** Confirm that the 4 `Write Off` cases have authorization; verify whether the 14 open invoices are genuinely unpaid or truncated by the snapshot cutoff.

### Norm revision
- Clarify N1’s temporal abstraction and scope (“standard Order”).
- Add explicit scope markers for N4 (`external shipment`).
- Formalize exception types for E8 and document `Write Off` representation.
- Define `codeath` rules (at minimum declare `unspecified`).
- Add statistical interpretation guidance or reference an external monitoring policy.
- Document cross-family reconciliation rules (OTG vs. ET-OT).

### Monitoring
- **Forbidden patterns:** Alert in real time for N3, T6, and E10 occurrences.
- **Mandatory gaps:** Monitor direct-follows and event-level rates for N1, N4, E2, E6, E8; investigate any non-zero rate rather than ignoring small counts.
- **Typical patterns:** Track the rate of `Issue Invoice`->`Record Payment` and `Validate Order`->`Close Order` over time; sudden shifts may indicate process change, not violations.
- **Data-quality indicators:** Monitor OTG/ET-ET discrepancies, missing object links despite attribute presence, and reused identifier conflicts.

**Priority justification:** N3 and E10 are prioritized because `forbidden` rules, by the norm’s own closed-world design, allow no legitimate exception. The remaining findings—especially those marked `typical` or `unspecified`—are not escalated merely because they are rare. The 1 `Reopen Order -> Close Order`, the 3 `Manual Compliance Review` events, and the 100 `Order`–`Customer` interactions require no immediate enforcement action because the norm explicitly does not govern them.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

**Robust conclusions:** The presence of `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` (N3, 2 cases) is a robust normative breach within the stated scope, regardless of data quality, because the rule is `forbidden` and applies to the same Order. The presence of `Sensor Heartbeat` linked to `Invoice` (E10) is robustly `forbidden`; even if it is a data artifact, it requires containment and repair. The `typical` nature of `Validate Order -> Close Order` (N2, 83) and `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` (N5, 92/110) is robust: absence of these edges is not a violation, and their presence confirms common but non-required behavior.

**Depend on the norm’s incompleteness:** All mandatory-gap conclusions—N1 (2 missing direct follows), N4 (2 missing pack-to-dispatch), E2 (2 missing add-item-to-order), E6 (2 missing dispatch-to-delivery), and E8 (1 missing payment-to-invoice)—depend on unresolved scope, abstraction, and exception-typing ambiguities. Until N1’s “eventually” is formalized, N4’s “external shipment” is object-level explicit, and E8’s “unapplied cash” is typed, these gaps remain “potential within-scope mandatory gaps” rather than confirmed violations.

**Depend on suspected data-quality issues:** The E10 `Sensor Heartbeat` link is highly likely a reused-identifier error; the 2 E6 missing `Delivery` links are likely extraction failures given present carrier attributes; the 2 E2 missing `Order` links and 2 N4 missing `Pack` events may also be log or extraction omissions. The T6 `cobirth` event is likely an integration artifact. These classifications change remediation paths (repair data vs. redesign process) without changing the normative status of the relations.

**What cannot be decided from graph summaries alone:** Whether the 2 missing validations in N1 eventually occurred; whether the 14 open invoices are truly unpaid or truncated at the snapshot boundary; whether the 2 packages in N4 are internal; whether the 1 payment in E8 is legitimate unapplied cash; and whether the 7 `Package`–`Delivery` `codeath` edges represent correct co-termination or duplicate linking. These require event-level inspection, attribute verification, and cross-reference with source systems—none of which are available in the aggregated OC-DFG, OTG, and ET-OT summaries.
