## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

I treat the repair problem abductively: the observed log constrains possible explanations, but it does not identify a single correct one. The rules below are used to generate and rank candidate relations, not to prove them.

**Hard invariants**

- *Referential integrity*: proposed relations may only refer to events and objects already present in the dataset. No new events or objects are invented.
- *Directionality of O2O*: `DEL-X carries PK-7` is not equivalent to `PK-7 carries DEL-X`. Proposed O2O edges must preserve the intended source/target direction.
- *No exact duplicates*: a relation already present should not be re-added as a repair unless the original relation is being explicitly corrected.
- *Payment object cardinality*: each `Record Payment` event should be linked to exactly one Payment object. This is already satisfied by `ev13`/`PAY-1` and `ev14`/`PAY-2`. The invoice link remains defeasible.

**Strong defaults**

- `Create Order` normally has one Order.
- `Add Item` normally has one Order and one Item.
- `Pack Item` normally has one Item and one Package, but an item-only row is possible during a scanner outage.
- `Dispatch Package` normally has one Package and one Delivery, but bulk dispatch can cover several packages.
- `Confirm Delivery` may reference one or more Deliveries, or only an Order/Customer when carrier data is lost.
- `Issue Invoice` normally has one Invoice and one or more Orders.
- `Record Payment` normally has one Payment and one Invoice, but unapplied cash is possible.
- Lifecycle order is expected: order creation before item addition, packing after item addition, dispatch after packing, delivery confirmation after dispatch, invoice after order, payment after invoice. Timestamp violations are suspicious but not decisive.

**Weak heuristics**

- Identifier similarity: `N-421?` suggests `DEL-Y` because `DEL-Y.carrier_ref=N-421`; `PK8-2` suggests `PK-8`.
- Timestamp proximity: events close in time are more likely to belong together, but same-timestamp events may also be bulk operations.
- Route/customer alignment: `NORTH` route and customer `C17` make consolidation plausible, but do not prove it.
- Amount alignment: `130`, `100`, and `230` are suggestive, but arithmetic coincidence is not proof of invoicing or payment application.
- Existing O2O structure: observed O2O edges can corroborate E2O edges when they arise from separate evidence.

**Evidence combination**

Hard invariants first eliminate impossible repairs. Strong defaults identify missing slots, such as `ev10` lacking a Delivery. Identifier similarity is then given the highest evidential weight, followed by route/customer/time. Amounts are treated only as weak corroboration. If a candidate O2O edge would be derived solely from a candidate E2O edge, I downgrade it and avoid treating it as independent confirmation. Where two explanations are plausible, I prefer leaving the relation unresolved rather than forcing completeness.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

### Bundle A: conservative / minimum-change repair

Bundle A accepts only the strongest missing relation: **P-01**, linking `ev10` to `DEL-Y` as provisional. It deliberately avoids completing lifecycles where the evidence is ambiguous.

- **ev08 and PK-8**: left unresolved. The row is treated as a possible scanner-outage or reconciliation gap. I do not add `ev08 -> PK-8` or `PK-8 packs IT-B1`, because doing so would place an ORD-B item into a package already associated with an ORD-A item without independent consolidation evidence.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y**: Bundle A adds `ev10 -> DEL-Y` because `N-421?` matches DEL-Y’s carrier reference. It does not assign PK-8 to DEL-X, nor does it merge `ev09` and `ev10` into a bulk dispatch. The O2O edge `DEL-Y carries PK-8` is left unresolved because it would be largely derivative from the E2O addition.
- **ev11 and the delivery objects**: left unresolved. The event remains linked only to `ORD-A`. If forced to rank delivery candidates, DEL-X is slightly more plausible than DEL-Y because DEL-X has an observed `carries PK-7` edge, but the wildcard `N-42*` and the possibility of order-level portal confirmation make alternatives too plausible to reject.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B**: no relation added. The invoice remains linked to ORD-A only. The total 230 may reflect fees, taxes, partial consolidation, or a missing invoice, but the data does not distinguish these.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44**: no relation added. PAY-2 remains possible unapplied cash or payment toward an unseen invoice.

**Coherence**: Bundle A yields a plausible but incomplete log. ORD-A has a fairly complete lifecycle, ORD-B remains open after item addition, PAY-2 is unapplied, and DEL-Y is weakly introduced. This is acceptable because incompleteness is less harmful than fabricated lifecycle completion.

### Bundle B: consolidated same-customer fulfillment

Bundle B is a plausible alternative if the organization sometimes consolidates packing, delivery, invoicing, and payment for the same customer.

- **ev08 and PK-8**: Bundle B upgrades P-03 and P-04 to provisional. `ev08` is linked to `PK-8`, and `PK-8 packs IT-B1` is added. This creates a mixed-order package, which is operationally plausible for the same customer and route but weakens case isolation.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y**: Bundle B keeps P-01 and upgrades P-02: `DEL-Y carries PK-8`. DEL-X continues to carry PK-7. The two dispatch events are treated as separate deliveries rather than one bulk dispatch.
- **ev11 and the delivery objects**: Bundle B treats `ev11` as a customer/route-level confirmation and provisionally links it to both `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y` via P-05 and P-06. It does not add `ev11 -> ORD-B` because the observed event explicitly contains ORD-A only.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B**: Bundle B upgrades P-08 and P-09. INV-44 is treated as billing both ORD-A and ORD-B, and `ev12` is linked to ORD-B. This is supported by customer alignment and the coincidence of 230 with 130 + 100, but not proved by it.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44**: Bundle B upgrades P-10 and P-11. PAY-2 is treated as a second settlement of INV-44. Again, the amount pattern is corroborative, not decisive.

**Coherence**: Bundle B gives ORD-B a much fuller lifecycle and makes the invoice/payment pattern look complete. However, it introduces several many-to-many relations: one package packs items from two orders, one invoice bills two orders, and two payments settle one invoice. It is coherent under consolidation semantics, but it is more fragile and more likely to distort downstream process mining.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

The selected repair is **Bundle A**.

**Order**

ORD-A has a plausible lifecycle: creation, two item additions, packing through its items, dispatch of PK-7, order-level delivery confirmation, invoice, and one observed payment. ORD-B has creation and item addition, but its lifecycle remains incomplete after `ev08`. This is acceptable: ORD-B may be backordered, cancelled later, billed elsewhere, or simply incompletely logged. Bundle A does not create suspicious order-to-order relations. In Bundle B, ORD-B becomes complete, but the invoice becomes one-to-many with respect to orders, which is plausible under consolidation but less conservative.

**Item**

IT-A1 is well supported: it is contained by ORD-A, packed in PK-7, and PK-7 is carried by DEL-X. IT-A2 is contained by ORD-A and packed in PK-8; its dispatch is present, but its delivery linkage remains weak because Bundle A does not add `DEL-Y carries PK-8`. IT-B1 remains linked to ORD-B and to `ev08`, but not to a package. This orphan-like tail is intentional: the scanner-outage explanation is credible.

**Package**

PK-7 has a clean lifecycle: packs IT-A1, is carried by DEL-X, and is dispatched by `ev09`. PK-8 packs IT-A2 and is dispatched by `ev10`; Bundle A adds DEL-Y only as an event-related object, not as an O2O target. Therefore PK-8 is not falsely mixed with ORD-B. The missing O2O edge means the package-delivery relationship is underrepresented, but that is preferable to an unsupported O2O edge.

**Delivery**

DEL-X is supported by `ev09` and the observed O2O edge `DEL-X carries PK-7`. DEL-Y receives only the provisional E2O link from `ev10` in Bundle A. It remains weakly connected and has no confirmed delivery relation. This is a deliberate under-repair. Bundle B would make DEL-Y fuller, but only by accepting dependent O2O and ambiguous confirmation relations.

**Invoice**

INV-44 remains linked to ORD-A and PAY-1. The total 230 is not explained by ORD-A’s declared value alone, but this mismatch can reflect fees, taxes, partial payment, or missing relations. Bundle A does not use arithmetic to force ORD-B into the invoice. Bundle B would make INV-44 a consolidated invoice, but that creates a one-to-many invoice-order relation supported mainly by amount and customer alignment.

**Payment**

PAY-1 is well supported by `ev13` and `PAY-1 settles INV-44`. PAY-2 remains linked only to `ev14`. This can represent unapplied cash, a payment to a missing invoice, or a data capture gap. Bundle B would link PAY-2 to INV-44, making the invoice appear more fully settled, but the evidence is not strong enough for the conservative repair.

**Independent support and circularity**

Several observed relations are independently supported by E2O and O2O evidence: `ORD-A contains IT-A1`, `ORD-A contains IT-A2`, `PK-7 packs IT-A1`, `DEL-X carries PK-7`, `INV-44 bills ORD-A`, and `PAY-1 settles INV-44`. The proposed `ev10 -> DEL-Y` edge lacks independent O2O confirmation in Bundle A. If I added `DEL-Y carries PK-8` solely because I first assumed `ev10 -> DEL-Y`, then cited that O2O edge as independent proof of the E2O edge, the reasoning would be circular. The same circularity risk exists for invoice repair: inferring `INV-44 bills ORD-B` from the total, then using payment amounts to confirm the invoice total. Bundle A avoids this by leaving dependent edges unresolved.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**Unrepaired log**

In the unrepaired log, `ev10` relates only to PK-8, so Dispatch Package does not visibly involve DEL-Y. `ev11` relates only to ORD-A, so Confirm Delivery has no Delivery object. `ev14` relates only to PAY-2, so the second payment is disconnected from invoicing. The OC-DFG will miss transitions such as `Pack Item -> Dispatch Package` for IT-B1, `Dispatch Package -> Confirm Delivery` via Delivery, and `Record Payment -> Record Payment` via INV-44 for the second payment. The OTG lacks DEL-Y entirely as a carrier object and lacks any invoice/payment edge for ORD-B. Order flattening leaves ORD-B incomplete. Delivery flattening gives DEL-Y no events. Invoice flattening shows only one payment. Payment flattening leaves PAY-2 isolated.

**Bundle A**

Bundle A adds only `ev10 -> DEL-Y`. In the OC-DFG, Dispatch Package now has visible Delivery involvement for one event, but no new object-to-object transition is created because no O2O edge is added. The OTG is unchanged. The ET–OT graph gains a relation between the activity `Dispatch Package` and object type `Delivery`. Delivery flattening now gives DEL-Y at least one event, but no carries relation and no confirmation. Order, Invoice, and Payment flattenings are essentially unchanged. Activity frequencies based on event counts remain identical, but object-involvement counts change: Dispatch Package now touches one additional Delivery object. Equivalent-execution clustering around Order changes little because ORD-B remains incomplete.

**Bundle B**

Bundle B substantially changes the object-centric view. The OC-DFG may gain edges such as `Add Item -> Pack Item` for IT-B1, `Pack Item -> Dispatch Package` through PK-8, `Dispatch Package -> Confirm Delivery` through DEL-X/DEL-Y, and `Record Payment -> Record Payment` through INV-44 when both payments settle the same invoice. The OTG gains `PK-8 packs IT-B1`, `DEL-Y carries PK-8`, `INV-44 bills ORD-B`, and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`. The ET–OT graph gains Pack Item to Package for `ev08`, Confirm Delivery to Delivery, Issue Invoice to a second Order, and Record Payment to Invoice for the second payment.

Flattening effects are larger. Order flattening gives ORD-B packing, dispatch, delivery-related, invoice, and payment traces. Delivery flattening gives DEL-Y a dispatch and possibly confirmation. Invoice flattening shows one invoice connected to two orders and two payments. Payment flattening connects PAY-2 to INV-44. Activity frequencies based on events remain unchanged, but frequencies based on unique or total object involvement increase: Dispatch Package touches more packages/deliveries, Confirm Delivery touches deliveries, Issue Invoice touches two orders, and Record Payment touches the same invoice twice. Equivalent-execution clustering around Order may make ORD-A and ORD-B appear more similar, but the shared package and shared invoice can also create cross-case interference, making cases look artificially merged or duplicated.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# 1. Copy original tables before repair.
events_rep = events.copy()
objects_rep = objects.copy()
relations_rep = relations.copy()
o2o_rep = o2o.copy()

# 2. Selected Bundle A additions.
selected_e2o = pd.DataFrame([
    {
        "ocel:eid": "ev10",
        "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y",
        "ocel:qualifier": "dispatched_on",
        "confidence": 0.70
    }
])

selected_o2o = pd.DataFrame(
    columns=["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier", "confidence"]
)

# 3. Append E2O additions without creating exact duplicates.
if not selected_e2o.empty:
    e2o_key = ["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"]
    existing_e2o_keys = set(
        relations_rep[e2o_key].itertuples(index=False, name=None)
    )
    selected_e2o = selected_e2o[
        ~selected_e2o[e2o_key].apply(tuple, axis=1).isin(existing_e2o_keys)
    ]
    relations_rep = pd.concat(
        [relations_rep, selected_e2o],
        ignore_index=True
    )

# 4. Append O2O additions without creating exact duplicates.
if not selected_o2o.empty:
    o2o_key = ["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"]
    existing_o2o_keys = set(
        o2o_rep[o2o_key].itertuples(index=False, name=None)
    )
    selected_o2o = selected_o2o[
        ~selected_o2o[o2o_key].apply(tuple, axis=1).isin(existing_o2o_keys)
    ]
    o2o_rep = pd.concat(
        [o2o_rep, selected_o2o],
        ignore_index=True
    )

# 5. Referential integrity checks.
event_ids = set(events_rep["ocel:eid"])
object_ids = set(objects_rep["ocel:oid"])

missing_e2o_events = relations_rep[
    ~relations_rep["ocel:eid"].isin(event_ids)
]
missing_e2o_objects = relations_rep[
    ~relations_rep["ocel:oid"].isin(object_ids)
]

missing_o2o_source = o2o_rep[
    ~o2o_rep["ocel:oid"].isin(object_ids)
]
missing_o2o_target = o2o_rep[
    ~o2o_rep["ocel:oid_2"].isin(object_ids)
]

if not missing_e2o_events.empty:
    raise ValueError("E2O relations reference unknown event IDs.")
if not missing_e2o_objects.empty:
    raise ValueError("E2O relations reference unknown object IDs.")
if not missing_o2o_source.empty:
    raise ValueError("O2O relations reference unknown source object IDs.")
if not missing_o2o_target.empty:
    raise ValueError("O2O relations reference unknown target object IDs.")

# 6. Duplicate checks.
duplicate_e2o = relations_rep.duplicated(
    subset=["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"]
)
duplicate_o2o = o2o_rep.duplicated(
    subset=["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"]
)

if duplicate_e2o.any():
    raise ValueError("Duplicate E2O relations detected after repair.")
if duplicate_o2o.any():
    raise ValueError("Duplicate O2O relations detected after repair.")

# 7. Construct or update an OCEL object.
# VERSION-DEPENDENT: PM4Py's OCEL constructor/importer API varies by version.
# Some versions expect dataclass objects rather than dictionary records.
ocel = pm4py.objects.ocel.obj.OCEL(
    events=events_rep.to_dict("records"),
    objects=objects_rep.to_dict("records"),
    relations=relations_rep.to_dict("records"),
    o2o=o2o_rep.to_dict("records")
)

# 8. Discover object-centric graphs.
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# 9. Flatten for selected object types.
order_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type="Order")
delivery_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type="Delivery")

# 10. Deliberate non-use of automatic O2O enrichment.
# We do not call pm4py.ocel_o2o_enrichment here because graph-derived O2O
# edges would not prove business semantics such as "carries", "bills",
# or "settles" in this ambiguous dataset.
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Linking `ev08` to PK-8 too eagerly** could create a mixed-order package. That would introduce `Pack Item -> Dispatch Package` behavior for ORD-B and make ORD-B appear fulfilled through a package primarily evidenced for ORD-A.

2. **Adding `DEL-Y carries PK-8` as if independent** could create circular support: the E2O edge `ev10 -> DEL-Y` would be used to infer the O2O edge, and then the O2O edge might be cited as confirmation of the E2O edge.

3. **Assigning `ev11` to a single delivery** would hide the ambiguity of `N-42*`. If `ev11` is actually a customer-level or bulk confirmation, a unique delivery link would falsely complete one delivery and leave the other artificially unconfirmed.

4. **Adding `INV-44 bills ORD-B` because 130 + 100 = 230** would treat arithmetic coincidence as proof. This could turn two separate order-to-cash lifecycles into one consolidated invoice lifecycle, changing Order flattening and performance metrics.

5. **Linking PAY-2 to INV-44 solely because amounts align** could falsely mark the invoice as more fully settled. If PAY-2 is unapplied cash or belongs to a missing invoice, the repair would conceal a real accounting exception.

6. **Treating `ev09` and `ev10` as one bulk dispatch without evidence** could merge distinct carrier references and deliveries. This may reduce apparent dispatch frequency, hide DEL-Y, and distort Delivery-level throughput analysis.
