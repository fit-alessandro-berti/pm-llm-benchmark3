# 1. Assumptions and notation

A1. Timestamp rounding: All reported times (including external arrivals) are rounded to the nearest 5 min, so an actual time lies in approximately \([t-2.5, t+2.5]\) min. Reported differences \(\ge 5\) min have reliable chronological order; equal or adjacent (difference of 5 min) times may permit actual order swap or brief overlap within rounding. Primary calculations use nominal (reported) times; duration uncertainty of up to \(\pm 5\) min is noted in sensitivity.

A2. Resource capacity: I1, I2, C1 and C2 are single-task resources. A resource executes at most one case at a time. Apparent overlaps of duration \(\le 5\) min are treated as rounding/logging artifacts. C2 is strictly unavailable for the interval [10:55, 11:10]. No undocumented assistance is assumed.

A3. Station and queue semantics: No case intentionally bypasses S1 or S2. A case enters the S1 queue at its `arrival_s1` and the S2 queue at the (actual) completion of its S1 activity. An activity start is at least \(\max(\)queue-entry time, resource free time after its prior activity\()\). Service is non-preemptive.

A4. Missing starts (`MISSING`): The activity occurred; its resource and completion time are known. The feasible start interval is \([L, U]\) where \(L = \max(\)relevant entry time, prior activity end on the same resource\()\) and \(U =\) recorded end time, with service \(\ge 0\). No silent point replacement is performed; interval bounds or explicitly justified scenario values are reported. Specific feasible nominal intervals:
- P07 S1: [09:25, 10:00]
- P15 S1: [11:40, 12:15]
- P05 S2: [09:45, 10:15]
- P11 S2: [12:00, 12:25]

A5. Right-censoring and `OPEN`: An activity that is `OPEN` at 14:00 contributes elapsed service (\(14:00 -\) start) as a strict lower bound on final service; remaining service is unknown. Cases with blank S2 fields after a completed S1 (or blank S1 fields) are waiting at 14:00; remaining wait and total sojourn are right-censored. WIP and busy-time include them only up to 14:00.

A6. Simultaneous nominal events: When an end and a start share the same reported time, the resource is treated as continuously busy (zero idle gap). For WIP counting, departures/ends are processed before arrivals/starts at the identical nominal instant.

A7. Scenario definitions for uncertainty:
- Optimistic / work-conserving (preferred central given data pattern): earliest feasible starts (minimum waits, maximum services for the missing cases).
- Midpoint: arithmetic midpoints of each feasible \([L, U]\).
- Pessimistic: latest feasible starts (maximum waits, minimum services).
Rounding expands all duration ranges by up to \(\pm 5\) min.

A8. Work-conserving preference: Consecutive known activities on the same resource almost always exhibit a nominal gap of exactly 5 min (or 0). This pattern is most consistent with near-zero actual idle whenever work was present; therefore the earliest-feasible-start imputation is elevated as the primary central scenario.

Notation: times given as HH:MM or minutes past 08:00; waits, services and sojourns in minutes; WIP = queue length + in-service count; util = busy-time / available-time; window length = 360 min.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

Waiting, service and sojourn (nominal):
- \(\mathrm{S1\_wait} = \mathrm{s1\_start} - \mathrm{arrival\_s1}\)
- \(\mathrm{S1\_service} = \mathrm{s1\_end} - \mathrm{s1\_start}\)
- \(\mathrm{S1\_sojourn} = \mathrm{s1\_end} - \mathrm{arrival\_s1}\) (fixed even when start is missing)
- \(\mathrm{S2\_wait} = \mathrm{s2\_start} - \mathrm{s1\_end}\)
- \(\mathrm{S2\_service} = \mathrm{s2\_end} - \mathrm{s2\_start}\)
- \(\mathrm{S2\_sojourn} = \mathrm{s2\_end} - \mathrm{s1\_end}\) (fixed when start is missing)
- Case total sojourn (completed cases only) \(= \mathrm{s2\_end} - \mathrm{arrival\_s1}\)

For `MISSING` starts the station sojourn is known; wait and service form a partition of that fixed interval whose bounds follow from the feasible start \([L, U]\). The preferred central uses the work-conserving (earliest) start. For `OPEN` activities: elapsed service \(= 14:00 -\) start; final service \(\ge\) elapsed; total sojourn is right-censored.

WIP and queues over time: An event list of all arrivals, starts and ends (with scenario-imputed starts for the four missing cases) is sorted by time, with ties broken by ends-before-starts (A6). Counters for S1-queue, S1-service, S2-queue and S2-service are maintained; total WIP is their sum. Peak and time-average values are obtained from the trajectory. The 14:00 snapshot is taken directly from the supplied state column: S1 WIP = 2, S2 WIP = 6, total WIP = 8.

Rates: S1 arrival rate = external arrivals / interval length; S2 arrival rate = S1 completion rate; throughput = number of completions inside the interval / length. Meaningful sub-periods: 08:00–10:00, 10:00–12:00 (contains the C2 outage), 12:00–14:00.

Busy-time / utilization: For each resource sum \((\min(\mathrm{end},14:00) - \mathrm{start})\) over its assigned activities, substituting the range bounds for missing starts and elapsed times for `OPEN` activities. Available time = 360 min per resource except C2 (345 min after the 15 min outage). Utilization ranges are \([\mathrm{min\_busy}/\mathrm{avail},\mathrm{max\_busy}/\mathrm{avail}]\). Aggregate figures are reported for the two-resource stations.

Little’s Law: \(L = \lambda W\) is examined only in intervals where approximate stationarity is plausible (primarily the early sub-period 08:00–10:00 on lightly censored flows). Later non-stationary accumulation, the outage, and heavy right-censoring invalidate steady-state assumptions; only qualitative order-of-magnitude checks are offered.

Lower / central / upper estimates are obtained by the three scenarios of A7 (with the work-conserving earliest-start values treated as preferred central) and, optionally, by expanding every duration by the rounding uncertainty of \(\pm 5\) min. Fully observed cases are reported at their nominal values with a rounding note. Aggregates avoid unjustified precision (nearest 5 min or explicit ranges).

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

**Evidence (observations).**  
- Early (08:00–10:00): S1 arrivals (13) substantially exceed S1 completions (6); S2 waits remain short (5–25 min). S1 WIP builds rapidly.  
- Mid (10:00–12:00): S1 arrivals and completions roughly balance; S2 completions fall to 5 while the C2 outage removes 15 min of capacity. P08’s S2 start is delayed until 11:15; subsequent C2 cases inherit the backlog.  
- Late (12:00–14:00): external arrivals almost cease, yet S2 still holds a residual queue of 6 cases at 14:00 versus only 2 at S1. S2 waits for the later completed cases are systematically longer than contemporaneous S1 waits.  
- Average utilizations of the two stations are comparable ([0.74–0.84] vs [0.73–0.81]); the distinction appears in the time-varying queue lengths and in the outage effect.

**Hypotheses.**  
The system begins the day with S1 as a mild bottleneck (or the stations roughly balanced). After approximately 10:00, and especially after the C2 maintenance window, S2 becomes the dominant bottleneck. Longer average S2 service times (central \(\approx 35\)–40 min versus S1 \(\approx 25\)–30 min) and the temporary loss of one of the two S2 resources amplify the effect. The systematic 5-minute nominal gaps on every resource support the interpretation that both stations were operated nearly continuously once work was present; the observed late S2 queue is therefore not an artifact of voluntary idle.

**Maintenance interval.** The hard [10:55, 11:10] outage forces P08’s start to 11:15 and cascades into the long waits of P11 and the residual S2 queue visible at 14:00. No compensating capacity increase on C1 is visible.

The qualitative picture—rising waits on both stations, a shift of the bottleneck toward S2 after the outage, and a larger residual S2 queue—is stable under all three imputation scenarios and under \(\pm 5\) min rounding expansions.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

Three explicit scenarios are compared (all on nominal times; each can be further widened by the \(\pm 5\) min rounding envelope).

1. **Work-conserving / optimistic (preferred central, A8)**  
   Earliest feasible starts: P07 S1 wait = 40 / service = 35; P15 S1 wait = 90 / service = 35; P05 S2 wait = 20 / service = 30; P11 S2 wait = 55 / service = 25.  
   Busy times move to the upper ends of the ranges (S1 \(\approx 605\), S2 \(\approx 570\)); utilizations \(\approx 0.84\) / \(0.81\). Individual waits for the four cases are minimized, yet the overall rise in waits and the late S2 queue length remain unchanged.

2. **Midpoint**  
   Starts at the arithmetic centres of the feasible intervals. Services become 17.5 / 17.5 / 15 / 12.5 respectively. Busy times sit near the middle of the reported ranges; waits lengthen by 15–20 min for the missing cases. Peak WIP and 14:00 snapshot are essentially identical.

3. **Pessimistic (latest starts)**  
   Maximum waits / minimum services (including the extreme service = 0 bounds). Busy times fall to the lower ends (S1 535, S2 515); utilizations \(\approx 0.74\) / \(0.73\). The four missing-start cases now show the longest waits, reinforcing rather than reversing the congestion narrative.

**Right-censoring effects.** The two OPEN S2 activities contribute only their elapsed service (30 + 5 min) to busy-time and keep the cases inside WIP until 14:00. The four waiting-for-S2 cases and the two S1-censoring cases (P21, P22) likewise remain in the 14:00 WIP count. Any final service times or residual waits are unknown; therefore mean sojourn and throughput figures for the whole window are downward-biased if one naïvely averages only completed cases.

**Stable conclusions.**  
- Waits lengthen steadily on both stations.  
- Utilizations of S1 and S2 are comparable and high (\(\approx 0.75\)–0.85).  
- After the C2 outage the residual queue and the longer waits sit predominantly at S2.  
- The bottleneck therefore shifts from balanced / mild-S1 early to S2-dominant later.  

**Conclusions sensitive to imputation.** Absolute values of the four missing waits/services, exact peak WIP height, and the precise numeric utilization both move by 5–10 percentage points across scenarios. The extreme service = 0 bounds are acknowledged as theoretically feasible but operationally implausible; they do not alter the qualitative bottleneck diagnosis.

Little’s Law is applied only to the early sub-period 08:00–10:00 (approximate \(\lambda \approx 6.5\) h\(^{-1}\), early \(W_{\mathrm{S1}} \approx 0.4\)–0.5 h \(\Rightarrow L \approx 3\)–3.5, consistent with the observed early WIP trajectory). Later intervals violate the required stationarity and are omitted.

            s2 = to_minutes(end_obs) - st2  # elapsed

            s2 = to_minutes(r["s2_end"]) - st2
    else:
        w2 = s2 = np.nan
        if r["state_at_14_00"] == "WAITING_FOR_S2":
            w2 = to_minutes(end_obs) - to_minutes(r["s1_end"])  # elapsed
    case_rows.append([c, w1, s1, w2, s2, r["state_at_14_00"]])

case_df = pd.DataFrame(case_rows, columns=["case", "S1_wait", "S1_serv", "S2_wait", "S2_serv", "state"])
print(case_df.to_string(index=False, float_format=lambda x: f"{x:.0f}" if pd.notna(x) else "—"))
print()

# 5. Demonstrate pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time & arrival/finish rate
#    ONLY on a projection for which case-level semantics are valid
#    (fully-observed COMPLETE cases that possess both S1 and S2 intervals)

complete_mask = df["state_at_14_00"] == "COMPLETE"
# rebuild a clean event log containing only those cases
comp_cases = set(df.loc[complete_mask, "case"])
comp_events = event_df[event_df["case:concept:name"].isin(comp_cases)].copy()
# pm4py needs a single start/complete pair per activity per case;

classic_rows = []
for _, row in comp_events.iterrows():
    classic_rows.append({

# insert case-level service & waiting times (VALID only for these complete cases)
