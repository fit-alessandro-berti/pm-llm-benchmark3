P-505: locate or recreate the PO; obtain the C3 recording; document clock skew via NTP logs. P-506: written post-facto CFO/CRO review; reaffirm in writing that no verbal extensions exist. P-507: close the evidence file (PDF hash, queue logs); document budget-owner disposition. P-508: retroactive Finance/budget-owner review; CRO review of the >150k emergency path.

**Monitoring:** an emergency-purchase dashboard (count, spend share, scope-validity rate, late-record rate); a hard-stop or alert for any >100k or new-supplier commitment lacking pre-commitment clearance; workflow enforcement that excludes requester/committer from the authorizer set; a 4-hour recording SLA metric; monthly ERP clock-synchronization attestation; mandatory repository filing within 24 hours for any CFO-type control interpretation; quarterly Internal Audit sampling of emergency labels against B4/ECD-2 scope definitions; tracking of repeat emergency requesters as a normalization indicator.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Draft — Emergency Procurement Override Clause (EPOC-1):**

1. **Two-tier hierarchy.** (a) *Integrity controls* — segregation of duties (no requester or committer may be an approver, including as one member of a joint authorization), pre-commitment Compliance clearance for any new supplier or any purchase above EUR 100,000 — are waivable only by a written, scoped, time-limited exception signed by the Chief Risk Officer and filed in the controlled repository. (b) *Operational controls* — PO sequencing, approved-supplier register, plant/finance approval routing, tendering — may be suspended by a written incident directive signed by the COO and Incident Commander (and the CRO when reachable), which must enumerate each suspended control by rule ID. **Silence means not suspended.**
2. **Mandatory directive contents:** an operational scope definition (e.g., "items required to contain the event or restore the affected line within 24 hours, supported by a documented engineering assessment"); a per-purchase amount cap; dual authorization by two named Incident Command members who are neither requester nor committer; verbal authorization valid only if recorded within 4 hours in a designated outage-resilient log (signed PDF plus transmitted copy); automatic expiry after at most 7 days with **no verbal renewal**; repository filing within 24 hours of issuance.
3. **Thresholds.** Above EUR 100,000 or any new supplier: an expedited Compliance clearance lane (target 2 hours) remains pre-commitment. Above EUR 150,000: additionally Legal clearance and contemporaneous Finance notification. No role substitutes for Compliance.
4. **CFO authority.** The CFO may re-route Finance approvals prospectively by written, repository-filed notice with explicit caps; the CFO may never waive segregation of duties or Compliance controls.
5. **Expiry and transition.** Acts after expiry follow baseline rules. Goods compliantly committed within the window may be received after expiry without new approvals.
6. **Evidence rule.** During system outages, contemporaneous non-ERP records (signed PDFs, telephony logs, supplier acknowledgments, access logs) control over ERP timestamps; recovery entries must be annotated as such.
7. **Anti-normalization safeguard.** Every directive-invoking purchase is auto-flagged for independent retrospective review within 10 business days. A monthly Audit Committee report tracks emergency volume, scope-validity rate, and recording-latency rate. Any site exceeding 15% emergency spend in a quarter, or breaching the 4-hour recording duty in more than 10% of emergency cases, triggers a formal review and automatic lapse of directive privileges pending that review. Knowingly mislabeling scope (e.g., "site continuity" for non-qualifying purchases) is treated as misconduct, not as a process error.
