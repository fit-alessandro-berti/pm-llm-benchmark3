# 1. Disruption timeline

**T0 (Day -30 to -1): Baseline, steady state.**
Normal 9-step flow dominates (START...Invoice close, 36,800 of 42,000 cases, ~88% clean completion). Traceability exceptions 0.8%, cold-chain documentation gaps 0.4%, controlled-substance dual-control gaps 0.2%. Fitness 0.96 / 91% fitting traces. Risk posture: low, controls are systemic and automatic rather than dependent on individual judgment.

**T1 (Day 0 morning): Trigger event.**
WMS becomes unavailable. No volume yet, but the automated backbone for authorization, inventory truth, scanning, and release checks disappears instantly. This is the point at which control design shifts from system-enforced to human-enforced.

**T2 (Day 0 afternoon–Day 2): Manual workaround phase, high-risk improvisation.**
Dominant variants bypass or replace normal gates: Emergency triage  (Manual authorization check *or* Skip system authorization check)  Manual inventory snapshot check  Manual pick list  Manual pack  (Temporary release control)  Dispatch without scan  Phone proof of delivery  Spreadsheet invoice hold. Hospital-critical fill rate held at 91% while Pharmacy-standard fill rate collapsed to 61% — evidence of deliberate triage, not random degradation. Traceability exceptions spike to 9.5%, cold-chain documentation gaps to 4.8%, controlled-substance dual-control gaps to 2.9%, rework 12.5%, cancellations 7.8%. Fitness drops to 0.71 (44% fitting). This is the period of maximum operational stress and maximum concealed-risk accumulation.

**T3 (Day 3–6): Partial recovery, unstable ground truth.**
Order intake returns but inventory sync/scanning remain unreliable — a dangerous hybrid state where the system *looks* authoritative but manual reconciliation is still silently happening (Reconcile manual reservation branch not yet present here, appears in T4). Metrics improve but remain elevated (traceability exceptions 5.4%, cold-chain gaps 2.3%, dual-control gaps 1.4%, rework 9.0%). Fitness 0.82 (59%). Risk posture: moderate — teams may be tempted to declare "back to normal" prematurely.

**T4 (Day 7–21): Stabilization with a hidden compliance tail.**
Core process variants return close to baseline shape (Validate authorizationInventory reservationPickPackQCDispatchPODInvoice close dominant again), but new remediation-specific edges appear: Backlog prioritization, Reconcile manual reservation, Proof-of-delivery remediation, Audit remediation. Traceability exceptions fall to 2.2%, still ~3x baseline; dual-control gaps 0.5%, cold-chain gaps 0.9% — all still above baseline. Fitness 0.90/76%, better but not baseline. Crucially, operational cycle-time metrics look almost fully recovered (Hospital-critical median 8h = baseline) while the audit-remediation queue persists — **operational recovery and compliance recovery are decoupled**, which is the central finding of the case.

# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

**True resilience** = a control objective is preserved through an alternate mechanism, and that mechanism is visible, bounded in time, and leaves evidence. In this case: **B** (manual authorization by trained staff), **F** (manual release control, at least for controlled substances), **I** (deliberate deferral of noncritical orders), **J** (explicit criticality-based backlog ordering), and **K/L** (structured reconciliation/remediation) all substitute a control with an equivalent-effort human process rather than deleting the control. Fill-rate data (91% hospital vs 61% pharmacy) shows this was a designed trade-off, not accidental throughput preservation.

**Risky shortcuts / concealed noncompliance** = throughput or speed is preserved by *removing* a control point with no compensating mechanism, and the gap is only surfaced later (if at all) through audit. **C** (skip authorization) and **G** (dispatch without scan) fit this pattern exactly — they explain the largest deviations in the DFG (move-on-log deviations) and the sharpest jumps in traceability exceptions (0.8%9.5%) and dual-control gaps (0.2%2.9%). These preserved cycle times but manufactured hidden liability, which is precisely the pattern the constraints warn against: throughput preservation is not resilience if critical controls disappear.

**Ambiguous / risk-appetite-dependent**: **A** (fast lane bypassing authorization for hospitals) is defensible under a "protect life-critical supply" ethic but is indistinguishable in the log from C unless a compensating check is added — organizations with low regulatory tolerance should treat it as noncompliant; those with strong patient-safety-first policies may accept it with post-hoc verification. **D** (snapshot inventory) is resilient early, noncompliant-by-neglect later — the same adaptation crosses the line as time passes, which is a key abductive insight: risk classification is not static, it is a function of *duration* and *staleness*. **H** (phone POD) is acceptable resilience for low-risk product/customer combinations but marginal-to-noncompliant for controlled substances given weak evidentiary strength.

The single most important structural insight: **operational KPIs (cycle time, fill rate) recovered faster than compliance KPIs** (traceability exceptions, dual-control gaps, audit queue). A recovery is not "done" when Day 7-21 cycle times match baseline; it is done when K/L work clears and control-completeness metrics return to baseline.

# 4. Recovery process model

**Trigger conditions (enter Incident Mode):**
- WMS/core system unavailability > 30 minutes, OR
- Authorization/inventory/scan subsystem confirmed unreliable, OR
- Manual workaround usage detected in >5% of live cases

**Exit conditions (return to Normal Mode) — require BOTH:**
- Core systems restored and validated (test transactions clean) for 4 continuous hours, AND
- Control-completeness metrics (traceability exceptions, dual-control gaps, cold-chain doc gaps) within 1.5x baseline for a rolling 24h window
(This decouples "systems are up" from "compliance is restored" — addressing the T3/T4 gap.)

**Triage rules (at Emergency triage node):**
1. Hospital-critical  fast lane, but must pass **Expedited-B** (manual authorization, target 5 min/order) — never route to Skip-C.
2. Controlled-substance / Recall-sensitive / Cold-chain, any customer  mandatory Manual authorization + Temporary release control + enhanced evidence capture (no exceptions, regardless of customer tier).
3. Pharmacy-standard / Wholesale-bulk, Ambient  may be deferred (Defer noncritical order) with automatic customer notification and promised re-sequencing window.
4. Any order lacking a same-day inventory snapshot refresh  routed to Reconcile-first queue rather than Pick.

**Minimum controls by product/customer risk (floor, never waived):**
| Risk tier | Minimum control during outage |
|---|---|
| Controlled-substance | Dual-control sign-off (2 named individuals) + chain-of-custody signature POD |
| Cold-chain | Manual temperature log at pack + dispatch, timestamped |
| Recall-sensitive | Lot/batch capture at pick, retained even if scan unavailable |
| Hospital-critical (any product) | Fast-lane triage + expedited authorization, never skipped |
| Ambient/Pharmacy/Wholesale, non-critical | Manual authorization OR verified standing-authorization list (time-boxed, 72h validity) |

**Manual evidence capture standard (applies to E, G, H):**
- Every manual step produces a durable artifact at time of action: photographed pick list/label, paper log with timestamp+initials, or phone-confirmed POD logged into a structured (not free-text) spreadsheet with mandatory fields (order ID, product risk tier, customer, timestamp, confirming person).
- No "dispatch without scan" without an equivalent manual manifest entry captured at the moment of courier handoff (closes the G gap).
- Inventory snapshot (D) is time-stamped and auto-expires; refresh required every 4-6 hours or orders route to hold.

**Backlog prioritization (J, formalized):**
- Explicit, published scoring: Customer criticality (Hospital > Pharmacy > Wholesale) × Product risk (Controlled/Cold-chain/Recall-sensitive weighted higher) × Age in queue.
- Backlog prioritization decisions logged with the scoring rationale to prevent "informal/unfair" ambiguity noted in J's risk column.

**Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow (K, L redesigned):**
- Structured as its own tracked sub-process, not an afterthought:
 `Invoice close -> Evidence completeness check -> [complete: Close] / [incomplete: Audit remediation queue]`
- Audit remediation queue has its own SLA (e.g., 95% closed within 10 business days), owner, and aging dashboard — reported separately from operational recovery metrics so leadership cannot mistake "cycle times normal" for "compliance normal."
- Reconcile manual reservation (K) triggers automatic re-validation of authorization and inventory for any order picked against a stale snapshot (D) or skip-check (C) — creating a forced closure loop for T2/T3 shortcuts rather than leaving them permanently unresolved.

**Process-tree style summary (POWL-like notation,  sequence, × exclusive choice,  parallel,  loop):**
```
Recovery = 
  Trigger-detection 
  Triage(× [Hospital-fast-lane(Expedited-B)],
           [High-risk-product(Mandatory-controls)],
           [Standard(Manual-B or time-boxed-standing-auth)],
           [Noncritical(Defer  Backlog-queue)]) 
  Fulfillment(Manual-pick-with-evidence  Manual-release-control) 
  Dispatch(Manifest-capture-required) 
  Evidence-completeness-check(× [Complete  Invoice-close  END],
                                 [Incomplete  Audit-remediation  until closed]) 
   Reconciliation(Stale-snapshot/skip-check re-validation)
```

# 5. Resilience indicators

**Leading indicators (predict risk during an active incident):**
- % of live orders on manual workaround paths (target: alert if >10%, matches T2 experience)
- Age of inventory snapshot in use (hours since last refresh)
- % of orders routed through any "skip control" path (C, G) — target near 0%
- Compliance-staff queue length/wait time for manual authorization (B) — detects capacity bottleneck before it forces skip-shortcuts

**Lagging indicators (evaluate after event/period):**
- **Throughput:** cases processed vs. baseline rate, by segment
- **Service protection:** fill rate by customer tier (Hospital vs Pharmacy vs Wholesale) — fairness signal if gap is too extreme or one tier's protection comes entirely at another's expense
- **Traceability:** exception rate vs. baseline (0.8%) — trend to closure, not just point-in-time
- **Control completeness:** cold-chain documentation gap, dual-control gap vs. baseline (0.4%, 0.2%)
- **Backlog age:** time from Defer/Backorder to resolution; audit-remediation queue age distribution (surfaces the "hidden tail")
- **Rework/cancellation:** rate vs. baseline (3.5%/2.1%) — proxy for downstream cost of upstream shortcuts
- **Fairness across customer groups:** variance in cycle-time degradation and cancellation rate between Hospital/Pharmacy/Wholesale — flags whether prioritization (I, J) was proportionate or excessive
- **Compliance-operations decoupling gap:** difference between "days to operational KPI recovery" and "days to control-completeness KPI recovery" — this is the single most diagnostic new metric this case reveals

# 6. Recommendations for future disruptions

| Priority | Recommendation | Effort | Expected benefit | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Eliminate pure "skip control" paths (C, G); replace with mandatory lightweight manual equivalent | Medium (procedure + training) | Removes largest traceability/authorization gaps at their source | Some throughput loss during first hours of outage |
| 2 | Time-box manual inventory snapshot validity (D) with forced refresh/hold | Low (policy + alerting) | Prevents late-stage rework/backorder spike seen Day 1-2 | Requires reliable manual refresh capability |
| 3 | Formalize backlog prioritization scoring (J) and publish criteria | Low | Improves fairness, defensibility, audit trail | None significant |
| 4 | Build structured manual-evidence capture tool (mobile form) for E/G/H instead of paper/phone/spreadsheet | Medium-High (tooling) | Drastically reduces later reconstruction burden and audit remediation volume | Requires pre-provisioned offline-capable devices |
| 5 | Separate "operational recovery" and "compliance recovery" declarations and dashboards | Low | Prevents premature "all clear" signaling to leadership/regulators | Requires cultural buy-in to report two recovery dates |
| 6 | Extend Temporary release control (F) explicitly to cold-chain, not just controlled substances | Low | Closes documented gap in T2 (4.8% cold-chain doc miss) | Slightly slower manual release throughput |
| 7 | Pre-negotiate customer communication protocol for deferral (I) | Medium | Reduces Pharmacy-standard dissatisfaction/cancellations without changing prioritization | None significant |
| 8 | Dedicated audit-remediation team/SLA with staffing surge plan | Medium-High | Prevents long hidden tail (2,300 cases) from persisting past Day 21 | Requires budget for temporary surge staff |

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should **accept** short-term degradation in Pharmacy-standard and Wholesale-bulk cycle times, higher cancellations for noncritical orders, and continued reliance on manual authorization/release checks (B, F) even at the cost of compliance-staff bottlenecks — these are the visible, bounded price of protecting hospital supply and maintaining real controls under duress. Management should **not accept** silent authorization bypasses (C) or scan-less dispatch (G) as standing practice, nor should it treat cycle-time recovery in Week 3 as proof that compliance risk is closed while an audit-remediation queue and reconciliation backlog remain open — doing so converts a temporary incident into a durable, hidden liability (potential regulatory exposure on controlled-substance/cold-chain records). The recommendation would shift toward greater tolerance of temporary throughput loss (e.g., accepting slower Hospital-critical fast-laning) if evidence showed that closing the authorization gate added materially more than a few minutes per order; conversely, it would shift toward stricter real-time controls if the current audit-remediation queue reveals unresolved controlled-substance or cold-chain gaps beyond an acceptable closure SLA (e.g., >10 business days), since that would indicate the "temporary" shortcuts left permanent, unrecoverable evidence gaps rather than merely delayed paperwork.
