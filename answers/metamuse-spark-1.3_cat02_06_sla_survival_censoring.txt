# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions
## Time scale and observation
- Time origin T0: instant of `Submit` for each case. All durations are elapsed hours from `Submit`.
- Horizon tau = 72 hours. SLA success requires `Resolve` at time <=72.
- Observed time T_obs = `duration_or_age_h`: time to terminal event if `observed_status` in resolved, withdrawn, rejected, transferred; otherwise age at freeze if `open`.
- `last_event_gap_h`: time from last recorded prefix activity to T_obs. For terminal rows it is 0 by construction here; for open rows it measures current inactivity.
- Freeze is a single calendar cutoff. Cases have different ages at freeze, so censoring times vary. No calendar dates or exact event timestamps are supplied.

## At least three estimands, two required
We distinguish what the business means by breach:

- E1 - SLA non-resolution by 72: P(not resolved by 72) = 1 - CIF_resolve(72), where CIF_resolve(t)=P(T_resolve <= t, resolve precedes any competing terminal). Withdrawal, rejection and, under most operational readings, transfer before 72 count as failure to resolve. This is the SLA-governance estimand: did the internal system deliver resolution within 72h. It treats competing terminals as breach, not as censored.
- E2 - Still without terminal disposition at 72: P(no terminal by 72) = S_any(72), where event = any of resolve, withdraw, reject, and transfer if transfer is defined as internal terminal. This is the workload / queue estimand: how much work remains past the horizon and needs staffing, review, or customer communication.
- E3 - Late-resolution incidence: CIF_resolve(t) for t>72 and increment CIF_resolve(inf)-CIF_resolve(72), i.e. probability of eventual resolution after the SLA. Also of interest: P(eventually resolve | still open at 72). This is the continued-effort value estimand: is late work worthwhile or should cases be closed, transferred, or renegotiated.

E1 best matches SLA reporting, penalties, and cohort-policy comparison on attainment. E2 best matches capacity alerts and triage of backlogs. E3 best matches decisions to keep working versus withdraw, reject, or transfer after breach.

Right censoring, competing events, and observed breaches are different:
- Right censoring (`open` with age <72): 72h status unknown. Case was event-free to censoring time, then unobserved.
- Competing event (withdraw/reject and arguably transfer before resolution): 72h E1 status known as non-resolved; E2 status known as having disposition. They remove the case from risk for later resolution.
- Already-observed breach: either resolved after 72, open with age >72 and still unresolved at 72, or competing terminal before 72 (for E1), or any terminal after 72 (for E2, still open at 72). No extrapolation needed to classify 72h status, though eventual outcome beyond freeze remains unknown for opens.

## How four exemplar cases contribute
- S03: resolved at 76h. E1: known breach; contributes to risk set through 72 as event-free, then resolve event at 76; CIF_resolve(72) does not count it. E2: event-free at 72, event at 76. E3: late resolution.
- S09: open at 92h, prefix Submit>Screen>Review, gap 31h. E1: known breach because observed without resolution through 72 and censored after horizon; at-risk through 72, censored at 92. E2: same, event-free at 72, censored at 92. Despite censoring, 72h classification is known. Eventual fate unknown.
- S06: withdrawn at 27h. E1: known breach / non-resolution; competing event at 27 that precludes later resolve. E2: terminal event at 27, no longer at risk after 27. Must not be coded as non-informative censoring without discussion.
- S08: transferred at 68h. E1: under competing / non-resolve reading, known breach at 68; under censor-at-transfer reading, at-risk to 68 then censored just before horizon, 72h status imputed. This 4-hour difference before tau makes the assumption material. E2: terminal at 68 if transfer is a disposition; censored at 68 if transfer is censoring. Eventual external resolution unknown.

Assumption A1: origin is Submit and 72h is strict <=72 success. Needed because no timestamp definitions are given. Assumption A2: freeze censoring is the only source of `open`; no loss to follow-up other than transfer.

# 2. Event and censoring encoding
## Mapping table
Columns: observed_status -> code for E1 competing-risk analysis, code for E2 any-disposition analysis, censoring rule, sensitivity alternatives.

- resolved with duration <=72: E1: event of interest at duration; E2: terminal event at duration; Censoring: none, fully observed; Sensitivity: none, always success for E1.
- resolved with duration >72 e.g. S03, S13, S20, S28: E1: event of interest after horizon; at-risk through 72, event at duration; E2: terminal event after horizon; event-free at 72; Sensitivity: none for 72h classification.
- open with age >72 e.g. S09, S11, S25, S26: E1: censored at age, but known non-resolved at 72; contributes to risk set through 72; E2: censored at age, but known event-free at 72; Sensitivity: bound eventual CIF beyond age; assess informative stall via gap.
- open with age <72 e.g. S10 47, S15 35, S24 67, S29 51, S31 23: E1: censored before horizon, 72h status unknown; at-risk to age then removed; E2: censored before horizon, disposition unknown; Sensitivity: best/worst imputation, IPCW, landmark re-prediction; do not drop.
- withdrawn e.g. S06 27, S12 15, S22 44, S30 70 and rejected e.g. S05 9, S14 63, S23 11: E1: competing event at duration, counted as non-resolution breach; never censored; E2: terminal event at duration; Sensitivity: alternative composite success/failure definition if business counts early withdraw/reject as neutral closure rather than breach; cause-specific vs subdistribution framing.
- transferred e.g. S08 68, S21 26, S32 96: see three-plus treatments below; E2 depends on whether transfer is terminal.

All open cases must be retained in risk sets until their censoring age. Dropping them is not a sensitivity, it is bias.

## Transferred under at least three plausible assumptions
- T-censor: non-informative right censoring at transfer. Internal observation ends but case is assumed to have same future resolve hazard as those still observed with same covariates. Example S08 censored at 68, S21 at 26, S32 at 96. Simple but strong and likely wrong if transfer selects difficult or escalated cases. Allows standard Kaplan-Meier, but Kaplan-Meier then estimates a hypothetical net survival if transfer were abolished, not actual SLA attainment.
- T-competing: absorbing competing event that precludes internal resolve. For E1 it is a breach via non-resolution; for E2 it is a disposition. Matches internal-system accountability: sent away is not resolved. Example S08 and S21 are E1 failures at transfer times; S32 is event-free at 72 then competing at 96. Preferred for E1 governance reporting.
- T-informative / unknown-outcome: transfer is informative censoring with unobserved external resolve. Sensitivity bounds: worst-case all transfers before 72 never resolve externally and best-case all resolve immediately after transfer; or external follow-up requested. S08 at 68 is pivotal: worst-case retains breach, best-case flips to success just after horizon. S21 at 26 has larger uncertainty window. S32 at 96 does not affect 72h E1/E2 classification but affects late CIF.
- Additional T-exclude sensitivity: drop transfers entirely to show selection effect. Not recommended as primary because all three transfers are escalated P1/P1/P2 and escalated cases are enriched, so exclusion induces survivor bias. Use only to demonstrate fragility.
- Additional T-composite: transfer + resolve as positive navigation versus withdraw/reject as negative closure, if external organization is a valid resolution path. Requires external outcome data, currently missing.

Assumption A3: transfer time equals last prefix time; no post-transfer internal events. Needed because last_event_gap_h=0 for transfers.

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased
Naive analysis here means restricting to the 13 resolved cases, computing share resolved >72, e.g. 4/13=30.8% breach and 9/13=69.2% within SLA, and comparing cohorts or priorities on that subset.

## Survivor bias with concrete IDs
Only cases that survived to resolve are conditioned on. S05 rejected at 9h, S12 withdrawn at 15h, S06 withdrawn at 27h, S14 rejected at 63h, S30 withdrawn at 70h are removed, yet for E1 they are SLA failures. S08 transferred at 68h is removed, yet it occupied capacity for 68h and did not resolve. Conditioning on future resolve selects less difficult paths: e.g. S01 Submit>Screen>AutoCheck>Resolve in 18h versus S13 with rework plus escalate resolving at 110h. The latter survived competing risks long enough to resolve late; many similar cases may have withdrawn or remain open. Completed-case analysis also inverts timing: S03 76h and S13 110h are called breaches, but S09 open 92h, S11 open 81h, S26 open 104h, which are also breaches for E1, are invisible.

## Informative censoring
Open cases are not random. Long gaps signal stall: S26 gap 72h on age 104h, S11 gap 55h on 81h, S25 gap 48h on 79h, S09 gap 31h on 92h suggest no recent progress. Short gaps S15 gap 2h on 35h, S31 gap 3h on 23h, S24 gap 4h on 67h, S29 gap 6h on 51h suggest active cases. Treating both as non-informative and dropping them equates a stalled 104h case with an active 23h case. Transfers are escalated: S08 yes, S21 yes, S32 yes, all with Escalate>Transfer pattern. If escalation marks difficulty, censor-at-transfer overestimates future resolve hazard for the remaining risk set.

## Competing risks
Kaplan-Meier that censors withdraw/reject/transfer estimates P(no resolve if competing events could be prevented), which is higher than actual CIF_resolve. Example: by 70h observed competing terminals S05, S12, S06, S23, S22, S14, S08, S21, S30 have removed 9 cases from resolve risk. Censoring them pretends they could still resolve late, inflating late-resolve probability and understating E1 breach. Aalen-Johansen correctly partitions probability among resolve, withdraw/reject/transfer, and still-open.

## Cohort imbalance and non-random rollout
Cohort B started after automated-screening and escalation-policy change but assignment was not randomized and compliance incomplete. Prefixes show non-compliance and mixture: B still contains Submit>Screen>AutoCheck>Review>Resolve S17, Submit>Screen>Review>Resolve S20, Submit>Screen>Reject S23, Submit>Screen>Review>Withdraw S30 without leading AutoCheck, while A contains Submit>Screen>AutoCheck>Resolve S01 and Submit>Screen>AutoCheck>Review S15. Priority mix also differs and priority correlates with escalation and transfer. Comparing raw resolve shares confounds policy with case-mix and with differential censoring: B has 5 opens vs A 4 opens, B has 2 transfers vs A 1, ages differ. Causal claims about policy require adjustment and compliance data.

## Active open below versus above 72h
- Below 72 and active: S31 23h gap 3h, S15 35h gap 2h, S29 51h gap 6h, S24 67h gap 4h, S10 47h gap 19h. These are censored before tau; E1 and E2 at 72 unknown. They contribute at-risk time but no 72h classification. Dropping them understates denominator; imputing them as successes or failures without bounds hides uncertainty. Proper handling: keep in risk set to censoring time, report bounds.
- Above 72: S11 81h, S09 92h, S25 79h, S26 104h, plus S32 transfer 96h. These are known E1 breaches and known E2 event-free at 72 despite being censored/terminal later. They must be counted as breaches in E1 denominators even though eventual resolve beyond freeze is unknown. Naive complete-case drops all of them.

Numerically, known resolved by 72 is only 9 of 32. Known non-resolved by 72 for E1 is at least 9 competing terminals before/at 72 plus 4 late resolves plus 4 opens above 72 plus S32 still at risk at 72 = 18, with 5 censored-before-72 unknown. Hence E1 breach lies in about 56-72% depending on those 5, far above the 31% breach among resolved-only. E2 still-open at 72 lies in about 28-44%. Exact numbers depend on transfer coding, but qualitative gap persists under all codings.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan
## Comparison for these data, n=32 with heavy censoring and competing events
- Kaplan-Meier (KM): estimates S(t)=P(T_event>t) for a single event with non-informative censoring. Here useful only for E2 if transfer is defined as censoring, or for time to any disposition if transfers counted as events. Invalid as primary for E1 if withdraw/reject/transfer are censored, because that answers a hypothetical net survival, not actual SLA attainment. With n=32 confidence bands are very wide; stratified KM by cohort/priority has cells as small as 8. Use as descriptive secondary for E2, with Greenwood intervals, and explicitly state censoring assumption.
- Aalen-Johansen / cumulative incidence (CIF): primary for E1. Nonparametric partition of overall survival into CIF_resolve, CIF_withdraw/reject, CIF_transfer, and still-open. Handles competing events without independence assumption between causes. Directly yields 1-CIF_resolve(72) for breach and CIF components for governance. Needsates that censoring (opens) is non-informative conditional on strata; address via sensitivity for informative gaps/transfers. With small n, report point estimates plus exact or bootstrap intervals and bounds for censored-before-72.
- Cause-specific hazards (Cox or nonparametric): models instantaneous rate of each cause among those still at risk. Good for etiology, e.g. does escalation-policy era increase resolve rate versus transfer rate. Does not directly give breach probability without combining hazards via Aalen-Johansen. Use as explanatory secondary by cause, with priority/cohort strata, acknowledging low power and non-random assignment.
- Subdistribution hazards (Fine-Gray): models CIF directly for prediction of E1 breach in presence of competing events. Useful if building a risk score for P(breach) that must account for withdraw/reject removing resolve opportunity. Harder to interpret etiologically and unstable at n=32 with many covariates. Consider only as exploratory prediction model with penalization, not primary inference.
- Restricted mean survival time (RMST) / restricted mean time lost: mean time spent unresolved or mean time to disposition truncated at 72h or at e.g. 110h max. Interpretable for operations: expected hours within SLA window without resolution, or expected delay. Robust to proportional-hazards violations and useful when hazards cross due to AutoCheck reordering. Recommend as secondary summary alongside CIF, e.g. RMST_unresolved to 72h by cohort/priority.
- Landmarking: dynamic prediction at landmark times e.g. 24h, 48h given survival and current stage plus gap. Essential to use post-baseline Escalate, Rework, Review counts without immortal-time bias. E.g. predict P(breach | still open at 24h, stage, gap). Preferred secondary for alerting. Simple, transparent, fits small data, avoids joint-model complexity.
- Multi-state model: full process-aware representation with states Submitted, Screened, AutoChecked, InReview, Escalated, Rework loop, plus absorbing Resolve/Withdraw/Reject/Transfer. Estimates transition hazards and state-occupation probabilities, naturally handles time-varying paths and competing absorptions. Ideal conceptually for prefix data, but data-hungry: many transitions observed 1-2 times in 32 cases, exact transition times missing. Recommend as structural framework and for simulation / what-if, with pooled transitions and descriptive transition counts now, and as primary only after exact event timestamps are collected.

## Recommended primary and secondary
- Primary: Aalen-Johansen CIF for E1 breach 1-CIF_resolve(72) with T-competing for transfers and withdraw/reject as competing, opens as censored, stratified by cohort and priority descriptively, plus overall RMST to 72h. Justification: directly answers SLA attainment, respects competing risks, retains all opens, makes transfer assumption explicit, feasible at n=32 without strong parametric assumptions.
- Secondary: landmark CIF / cause-specific analysis at 24h and 48h using current stage and inactivity gap, plus E2 KM for still-open workload. Justification: enables process-aware alerts without leakage, separates workload (E2) from attainment (E1), and exposes informative censoring via gap.
- Exploratory: multi-state transition counts and Fine-Gray with at most 1-2 covariates to avoid overfit. Report that cohort-policy effect is associational, not causal, given non-random rollout and incomplete compliance.

Assumption A4: censoring by freeze is conditionally non-informative within priority/cohort for primary intervals; relaxed in sensitivity.

# 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards
## Candidate features from prefixes, gaps, and admin data
- Path pattern / variant: e.g. fast-track Submit>Screen>AutoCheck>Resolve S01 vs Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Resolve S16; standard review Submit>Screen>Review>Resolve S02/S20; escalated Submit>Screen>Escalate>Resolve S04 vs Submit>AutoCheck>Escalate>Resolve S18; rework loops Submit>Screen>Review>Rework>Review>Resolve S07 and longer S13 with Escalate. Variant is known only retrospectively; using full variant at baseline is leakage.
- Current stage at time t: Submitted, Screened, AutoChecked, InReview, Escalated, Rework. Time-varying, valid in landmark or multi-state if evaluated at landmark using only history to t.
- Repeated review count and rework_count: rework_count 1 for S07, S13, S27, else 0. Cumulative count is time-varying; baseline value 0 for all, so baseline use has no signal and post-baseline use needs time-varying design.
- Escalated flag and escalation timing: escalated=yes for S03, S04, S08, S10, S11, S13, S18, S21, S25, S28, S29, S32. Timing unknown beyond order. Treating yes as baseline creates immortal-time bias: cases must survive long enough to escalate, so escalated cases appear longer even if escalation has no effect. Example S10 escalated but open at 47h versus S01 never escalated resolved at 18h cannot be compared at baseline.
- AutoCheck position: AutoCheck before Screen in many B cases vs after Screen in A. This reflects era/policy and order change. It is post-treatment relative to cohort assignment, so adjusting for it blocks part of policy effect; stratify or mediate rather than simply covary if estimating policy effect.
- Inactivity gap last_event_gap_h: 0 for terminals, 2-72 for opens. Strong dynamic predictor of stall. Baseline unknown; use as landmark time-varying covariate, e.g. gap at landmark. Large gaps S26 72h, S11 55h, S25 48h flag informative censoring / likely future breach.
- Priority P1/P2/P3: true baseline, available at Submit. Valid for stratification and adjustment. P1 enriched for escalation/transfer.
- Cohort A/B: baseline era indicator, not randomized treatment. Valid for descriptive stratification, not causal without assignment/compliance data. Do not interpret cohort coefficient causally.

## Baseline vs time-varying vs leakage
- Baseline safe: priority, cohort era, submission characteristics if available.
- Time-varying valid with landmark/multi-state: current stage, counts to date, gap to date, escalated-to-date.
- Post-treatment / mediator: AutoCheck order, escalation occurrence when evaluating cohort policy; handle via mediation or separate descriptive analysis.
- Leakage if misused: full prefix, future Escalate/Rework/Transfer, total rework_count at baseline, total duration. Any model that predicts 72h breach using whether the case eventually escalated or the full variant leaks future information unavailable at alert time.

# 6. Sensitivity analysis
Compare at least three coherent analyses; report how E1 breach, E2 still-open, and cohort contrast change.

- SA1 primary competing-risk: transfers + withdraw/reject as competing, opens censored. E1 breach = 1-CIF_resolve(72). E2 event = any terminal including transfer. This penalizes transfer as internal failure. Cohort contrast reflects actual attainment under internal accountability.
- SA2 censor-at-transfer (non-informative): transfers censored, withdraw/reject still competing. KM resolve and CIF_resolve rise because S08 censored at 68 and S21 at 26 are removed rather than failed; S32 censored at 96. E1 breach falls relative to SA1. If transfers are difficult escalated cases, SA2 is optimistic. The gap SA1-SA2 quantifies transfer-assumption leverage; with 3 transfers in 32 the swing can be several percentage points and can reverse a small B-vs-A advantage if B has more transfers.
- SA3 informative-stall bounds + best/worst imputation for censored-before-72: keep SA1 competing structure but bound the 5 opens censored before 72 S10, S15, S24, S29, S31. Lower breach bound imputes all 5 resolve by 72; upper bound imputes none resolve or all remain open. Separately, reclassify long-gap opens S11, S25, S26, S09 as high-risk still-open with elevated breach hazard via landmark or IPCW weighted by gap/priority. This widens intervals and can reverse apparent priority or cohort ordering if one cohort has more early-censored active cases S31 23h, S15 35h versus stalled cases.
- SA4 definitional (withdraw/reject neutral vs breach): SA1 counts withdraw/reject before 72 as E1 breach. Alternative counts early withdraw/reject as neutral closure excluded from breach denominator or as separate CIF component with E1 redefined as P(resolve by 72 | not withdrawn/rejected early). Since A has 4 withdraw/reject before 72 S05, S12, S06, S14 and B has 3 before 72 S23, S22, S30 excluding S32, redefinition shifts levels and can flip cohort ranking if one cohort withdraws faster.
- SA5 landmark-only active prediction: predict breach conditional on still open at 24/48h using stage+gap, ignoring eventual variant. Tests whether cohort/priority effects persist after conditioning on survival and stage. Escalation effect typically attenuates versus baseline-escalated model because immortal time is removed; a baseline model that naively flags escalated=yes as high risk will look stronger than the landmark model.

Qualitative conclusions that could reverse: B better than A on raw resolve share; escalation causes delay; P1 worse than P3; transfer policy improves SLA. All are sensitive to transfer coding, gap informativeness, and competing-risk handling. Report ranges, not points, and flag S08 68h, S21 26h, and the five early-censored opens as influential observations.

# 7. Operational interpretation
## From survival outputs to actions without causal overclaim
- Governance (E1): report 1-CIF_resolve(72) as SLA breach rate with interval and competing-risk decomposition stacked CIF curves by resolve/withdraw/reject/transfer/still-open. Use for targets and era comparison, labeled associational for cohort B vs A. Do not present cohort difference as policy effectiveness without randomization/compliance adjustment. Show sensitivity SA1 vs SA2 vs bounds so leadership sees transfer-policy leverage.
- Workload (E2): report S_any(t) and number still open at 24/48/72h by priority and stage. Use to staff review and escalation capacity. Example: cases still in Review/Escalated at 48h with gap >12h route to senior queue; stalled opens like S26, S11 patterns trigger outreach, not auto-close.
- Alerts (landmark E1/E3): at 24h and 48h landmarks, publish P(breach | still open, current stage, gap, priority). Use thresholds tuned for precision/recall on historical landmarks, e.g. review if P(breach)>0.6, escalate review if >0.8, with priority-specific thresholds. Present as risk stratification, not causal: high risk given escalation does not mean escalation causes breach; escalation may mark difficulty or may help. Never display escalated-future or full-variant features at alert time.
- Late-work decision (E3): for breached but still open cases, show P(eventual resolve | still open at 72, stage/gap) to prioritize continued effort versus transfer/withdraw. E.g. short-gap Review cases like S24 pattern merit continued review; long-gap Escalated cases like S25/S11 patterns merit audit before further effort.

## Calibration and fairness checks by priority and cohort
- Calibration: compare predicted landmark breach probabilities to observed CIF within priority, cohort, and stage bins; plot calibration curves with small-n intervals; check RMST predictions. Recalibrate separately if B-era AutoCheck changes hazard shape.
- Fairness / equity: monitor breach, late-resolve, transfer, and withdraw/reject CIFs by P1/P2/P3 and cohort; check alert rates, precision, and workload burden do not disproportionately penalize P3 routine cases or over-transfer P1 urgent cases. Because P1 is escalated/transferred more often, censor-at-transfer models can mask P1 breach; always show competing-risk decomposition by priority. Document that priority is triage, not blame, and that thresholds may need priority-specific calibration to equalize opportunity for timely resolve.
- Governance guardrails: log alert overrides, track compliance of escalation policy by cohort, audit transfers for gaming breach metrics, and require external outcome capture before rewarding transfer rates.

Assumption A5: alert thresholds assume capacity to act; without staffing, prediction alone does not improve SLA.

def risk_at(df_enc, tau=TAU):
    at_risk = (df_enc["T"] >= tau).sum() + ((df_enc["T"] < tau) & (df_enc["E2_event"] == 0)).sum()*0  # still at risk at tau = T>=tau
    # simpler: count T>=tau (event-free at tau) + events/censors counted correctly
    n_at_risk_tau = (df_enc["T"] >= tau).sum()
    n_cens_before = ((df_enc["T"] < tau) & (df_enc["E2_event"] == 0)).sum()
    n_term_before = ((df_enc["T"] < tau) & (df_enc["E2_event"] == 1)).sum()
    return n_at_risk_tau, n_cens_before, n_term_before

def manual_km(times, events, tau=TAU):
    # times, events (1=event,0=censored); returns S(tau); fallback if lifelines missing
    d = pd.DataFrame({"T": times, "E": events}).sort_values("T")
    n = len(d); surv = 1.0
    for t, g in d.groupby("T"):
        if t > tau: break
        at = (d["T"] >= t).sum()
        ev = g["E"].sum()
        if at > 0: surv *= (1 - ev / at)
    return surv

# 9. Limitations and additional data
## Prioritized missing data for stronger inference
- Exact event timestamps for every prefix activity: needed to estimate stage hazards, escalation timing, dwell times, and to implement time-varying Cox, multi-state, or joint models without immortal-time bias. Currently only total time and order are known.
- External outcomes after transfer: did S08, S21, S32 resolve externally and when. Needed to choose between censor, competing, and composite transfer treatments and to audit transfer gaming.
- Cutoff and start calendar dates plus submission dates: needed to assess staggered entry, secular trends, seasonality, and whether censoring ages depend on submission recency versus stall. Also needed for IPCW and for distinguishing administrative censoring from informative stall.
- Time-varying workload and staffing: queue lengths, reviewer capacity, handoffs. Needed to adjust hazards and to convert E2 predictions into feasible actions.
- Assignment mechanism and policy rollout compliance: why cases are in B versus A, which B cases actually received AutoCheck-first and new escalation policy, and which A cases crossed over e.g. S01, S15. Needed for any causal interpretation of cohort contrast; without it report associational differences only, stratified by priority.
- Baseline severity, customer, product, and channel covariates: needed to adjust for confounding between priority, escalation, and breach, and for fairness audits.
- Withdraw/reject reasons and SLA pause/renegotiation rules: needed to decide whether early withdraw/reject is breach, neutral, or censored, and whether clock should pause during customer wait or rework.
- Rework and review loop details and gap histories over time, not just final gap: needed for landmark features that update as gaps evolve, and to test whether long gaps predict breach versus eventual late resolve.
- Larger sample and follow-up of current opens beyond freeze: needed to narrow bounds driven by 5 early-censored opens and to stabilize CIF, RMST, and priority-stratified estimates which are currently very uncertain at n=32.

With these data, primary Aalen-Johansen plus landmark prediction can be upgraded to a multi-state model with transition-specific hazards, IPCW for informative transfer/stall, and a compliant policy evaluation with mediation of AutoCheck order.
