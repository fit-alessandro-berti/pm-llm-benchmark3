## 1. Decision rule

Do not deploy a single threshold on `p_now`. Use a gated, portfolio-level rule, re-run at each 30-minute refresh and on any material event.

**Step 0 – Calibrate, do not trust the raw score.**  
Produce a calibrated interval `[p_low, p_high]` before any ranking:

| Segment | Adjustment to `p_now` |
|---|---|
| SME | +0.04 to +0.09 (model underpredicts) |
| Strategic Enterprise | 0.05 to 0.12 (model overpredicts post-redesign) |
| Public Sector | no shift, ±0.02 sampling tolerance |
| Non-Native-Language Support | no point shift; widen interval to at least ±0.10 and flag “low-evidence” – do not automatically down-rank, require robust-positive economics plus a human/language review |

The calibrated `p` is used for urgency, false-alarm exposure, and monitoring — **never** as the intervention effect. Benefit uses only the supplied absolute risk-reduction (ARR) range, treated as an uncertain causal estimate, not a known effect.

**Step 1 – Hard eligibility (all must hold).**  
(a) Stage is Triage or Diagnosis Started, and a 45-min swarm can plausibly finish before `Diagnosis Complete`; post-`Diagnosis Complete` cases are ineligible regardless of `p`. (b) Time to SLA > ~1h so acceleration can still matter, or a human confirms a faster path exists. (c) No prior swarm on the case without a materially new event. (d) Not in alert cool-down (Section 4). (e) Data-quality check passed, or uncertainty is explicitly priced and the decision is unchanged across the plausible range.

**Step 2 – Evidence / minimum-evidence-change rule.**  
An alert is actionable only if at least one holds: (i) first alert with `p_cal  +0.10` vs `p_prev`; (ii) two consecutive alerts above the case’s segment watch level; (iii) a material new event (new customer log, stage change, missing-info resolved, queue feature restored); or (iv) crossing a time-critical boundary (<3h to SLA while still pre-`Diagnosis Complete`). A repeat refresh with `|p_cal| < 0.05` and no new event is logged, not re-alerted.

**Step 3 – Prioritisation among eligible cases.**  
Rank by a conservative (robust) net score, then adjust for window-closing urgency:

> Robust score  `ARR_low × Value  €450  False-alarm burden`

using the **low** end of ARR and the full burden (deliberately conservative). A case is a candidate only if this score is > 0, or if a non-monetary duty applies (regulatory, safety, `p_cal,high  0.75` with a closing window — these get human review, not an automatic slot). Within candidates, promote cases whose eligibility window is about to close (Diagnosis Started) over early Triage cases with similar scores, and demote cases whose alert may be a data artefact until verified. Finally apply the portfolio check in Step 4 — a positive case-level score is necessary, not sufficient.

**Step 4 – Portfolio / spillover / reserve check before committing slots.**  
Each slot used adds delay and roughly +0.01–0.03 breach probability to each of ~6 untreated diagnosis-stage cases. Commit a slot only if the candidate’s robust score exceeds the expected spillover cost of a marginal slot under the base model (Section 3,  €700–2,200 per slot depending on untreated-case value). Hold **one of four slots in reserve** for a possible regulatory arrival before 15:00; release it only on defined triggers (Section 4), not merely because a marginal positive case exists. Stop adding slots if observed queue delay exceeds ~20 min/slot that day.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Cool-down / suppression.** After an alert is evaluated (whether or not a swarm follows), suppress re-notification for 120 minutes or until `Diagnosis Complete`, whichever is sooner. During suppression the model still scores silently every 30 min — suppression affects *paging and slot eligibility*, never monitoring.

**Re-entry (any one resets the alert as “new”).** (a) Material event: new customer log/evidence, stage transition, missing information supplied, failed data feed restored. (b) Calibrated risk moves  +0.08 since the suppressed alert, or  +0.05 on two consecutive refreshes. (c) Time boundary crossed: <3h to SLA while still eligible, or <1h (human review only). (d) A swarm was done and a post-swarm reassessment shows a *different* blocker — repeat swarm then requires senior approval; otherwise repeat swarms are forbidden.

**Escalation inside suppression.** If silently monitored `p_cal` rises  +0.10 cumulatively, or stays 0.70 (calibrated) for 3 consecutive refreshes, escalate to a human owner with a low-cost action (chase information, re-triage, queue reprioritisation) before any swarm is reconsidered. This prevents “suppressed” becoming “forgotten.”

**Reserve release and expiry.** Reserved slot is held until 13:30 or a regulatory arrival, then released in the order I-04  validated I-06  confirmed I-07. Any case passing `Diagnosis Complete` instantly loses eligibility and frees its claim; any “wait” decision expires at the next refresh and must be re-earned.

**I-05 vs I-07 — the core contrast.** I-05 (third alert, no new event, p 0.550.57,  +0.02) is the *same* alert re-rendered by the 30-min refresh: SME-adjusted risk is genuinely concerning, so it gets silent monitoring plus a low-cost owner check, but it must not re-compete for a slot — otherwise the refresh cadence, not evidence, allocates capacity, and repeat-alerting high-frequency cases would systematically crowd out first alerts. I-07 (third alert, but a **new customer log** uploaded, p 0.750.72) is new evidence wearing a repeat alert’s clothing: the small p *fall* does not disqualify it, because the log may change what a swarm would do. It therefore re-enters eligibility immediately — but re-entry only restores the *right to compete*; with 1.5h left and €5,000 value it still ranks below the selected three on robust score, earning instant human log review and a conditional reserve claim rather than an automatic slot. Same alert count, opposite handling — driven by evidence change, not alert count or raw p.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-based prioritisation starving low-value cases.** The robust score is proportional to value, so SMEs (I-05, I-07, I-10) will structurally lose to Strategic cases, and a pure € rule would also have over-served I-08 while starving I-07. Safeguards: (1) a **duty floor** — any eligible case with `p_cal,high  0.75` and a closing window (I-07 today) gets a same-hour human review and a documented low-cost intervention even when it loses the slot competition; (2) a rolling cap — no segment may take >60% of slots over the last 20 allocations without a documented review; (3) a daily “highest-risk non-selected” slot review where the top calibrated-risk untreated case is explicitly reconsidered value-blind by a second decision-maker; (4) publish internally the count of high-risk cases that received *no* action by value band.

**Non-Native-Language Support uncertainty.** I-09’s segment has sparse calibration and effect data; the two failure modes are ignoring it (discrimination by ignorance) or over-trusting a point estimate. Policy: widen intervals rather than shift points, decide on the robust (low-ARR) score — I-09 passes comfortably — include language-support capability *inside* the swarm so the intervention itself is not degraded, and oversample this segment in outcome/effect data collection until its calibration sample is adequate. Track burden/disruption per intervention here separately; if false-alarm burden concentrates in this group, require pre-swarm customer-contact protocol changes.

**Differential calibration.** Applying raw `p_now` thresholds would over-treat Strategic (overpredicted 0.05–0.12) and under-treat SME (underpredicted 0.04–0.09), compounding the value bias. Safeguard: segment-specific calibration is mandatory in the decision rule (Section 1), calibration error is reported per segment at each review, and allocation audits compare *selection rate among eligible cases* by segment against *calibrated-risk distribution*, not against raw case counts.

**Selective intervention changing future labels.** A swarm that works makes a high-risk case end as “no breach,” which naive retraining reads as a model false alarm; conversely untreated cases keep their natural outcomes. Over time the model learns that alerted-and-treated profiles are safe, and effects get baked into “risk.” Safeguards: log intervention, slot, propensity/score components, ARR assumption, and decision-maker override for every case; never train the risk model on post-intervention outcomes as if untreated — use intervention as a feature/censoring indicator, maintain a hold-out of marginal cases (below), and report calibration separately for treated and untreated cohorts.

**Spillover harms falling on untreated groups.** The 8–30 min/slot delay lands on whoever shares the diagnosis queue — plausibly the same lower-value/SME cases that already lose slot competitions, a double penalty. Safeguards: spillover is charged to the *decision* (Section 3 hurdle), untreated cases whose wait increased receive compensatory queue-priority points in the next cycle, daily slot use per queue is capped, and monitoring breaks queue-delay and subsequent breach rates down by segment/value band with a stop rule (Section 6). If spillover harm concentrates on a protected/sparse segment, the per-slot spillover hurdle for that queue rises automatically.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational monitoring (daily dashboard).** Slots used/reserved/released and why; time from alert to decision; eligibility-window misses (cases reaching `Diagnosis Complete` while “wait”); overrides of the rule with reasons; reserve-slot performance (did the regulatory case arrive; was the released slot used well).

**Predictive monitoring (weekly, by segment and stage/prefix).** Calibration intercept/slope and mean `p_cal  observed breach` for SME, Strategic, Public Sector, Non-Native-Language Support; Brier score; alert volume and repeat-alert rate. Trigger: |calibration error| > 0.05 on 100 resolved cases in a segment, or >0.08 on 50, forces re-estimation of the Section 1 adjustments before further automated ranking for that segment. Small segments report intervals, not points, and are never recalibrated on <30 outcomes without pooling/shrinkage.

**Causal-effect estimation (the critical gap).** The supplied ARRs are not causal estimates. Build evidence prospectively: (a) for *marginal* cases near the selection boundary (robust score within ±€500 of the last selected case), randomise or use a stepped-wedge/lottery when capacity binds — ethically defensible precisely where the decision is genuinely uncertain; (b) otherwise exploit the reserve-release timing and cool-down as natural variation, with propensity logging for adjustment; (c) pre-register the primary endpoint (breach avoided) and secondary endpoints (diagnosis duration, queue delay imposed). Re-estimate ARR by segment/stage quarterly; if a segment’s estimated ARR lower bound falls to 0.03, it drops out of automatic eligibility.

**Capacity / spillover monitoring.** Measure actual added wait per used slot (target: detect departure from 8–20 min toward 15–30), breach-probability change in the six-case untreated exposure set, and specialist opportunity cost. Trigger: measured spillover > +0.03/case/slot or delay >25 min/slot over a rolling week raises the Step-4 hurdle and cuts daily slot release from 3 to 2 until congestion clears.

**Fairness monitoring (weekly).** Selection rate, human-review rate, false-alarm burden incurred, added queue delay, and breach rate — each by segment, value band, and language-support status, normalised by eligible-case calibrated risk. Trigger: any group’s selection rate <0.8× or added-delay burden >1.25× the portfolio average (risk-adjusted) over a rolling month prompts allocation-rule review and a documented corrective action (cap, floor, or hurdle change).

**Policy-induced drift detection and change control.** Because the policy changes both outcomes and future training data, track: treated-vs-untreated outcome gaps at fixed `p_cal`; feature-missingness rates (I-06-type failures) as a first-class alert-quality metric; and champion–challenger evaluation of any new threshold in shadow mode for 2 weeks before activation. Change the rule only through a monthly review (weekly exception for safety/calibration breaches) against pre-set criteria: sustained negative base-case net impact over 2 review periods, a causal ARR re-estimate crossing an eligibility bound, a calibration/fairness trigger above, or spillover breaching its stop rule. Every change is versioned with its expected effect stated in advance, so the next review can judge the policy — not just the model — on its own predictions.
