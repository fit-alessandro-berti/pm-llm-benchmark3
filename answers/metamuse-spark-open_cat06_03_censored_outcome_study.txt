## 1. Target definition
**Prediction time :** A landmark early in the case, defined *before* the final credit decision is known. Operationalized as (a) fixed length: after k events (k=2,3,5) from `Application Started` and (b) elapsed-time: 24h / 72h / 7 days after start. We evaluate both; primary reporting at  = after 3 events or 72h, whichever yields at least `Identity/KYC Check` started but excludes `Contract Signed`/`Account Activated` if they occur earlier.  must be  `time:timestamp` of prefix events only.

**Unit of analysis:** One credit application = one `case:concept:name`. Prediction is at the *prefix* level, target is at the *case* level as-of a cutoff.

**Primary target (factual under observed policy) — defensible for "every application":**  
Multi-state competing-outcome + factorization.

For every application alive at , we predict the **factual probability under the currently deployed activation policy ** that the case will ever reach `Account Activated` *and* then default within 90 days of activation, accounting for delayed feed:

`(prefix) = P_{}[ Activated within observation window W AND default_90d=1 within 90d of activation | prefix at  ]`

Decomposed for interpretability and uncertainty:
` = P_{}(Activated | prefix) * P(default_90d=1 | Activated, prefix)`

The joint  is well-defined for *all* 48,000 starts, is 0 for cases that are Rejected/Withdrawn/Transferred-never-activated under , and is estimable without counterfactuals. It answers: "If we keep operating as we did, what is the chance this *application* ends as a realized default loss?" This aligns with the business triage of every incoming prefix (route to fast-track, review, partner) while explicitly separating selection from credit risk.

Estimation is as a competing-risk landmark model: categories as-of case-history cutoff C:
1. `Activated  default_90d=1` (mature)
2. `Activated  default_90d=0` (mature)
3. `Rejected`
4. `Withdrawn`
5. `Transferred` (with partial linkage)
6. `Activated but censored / not mature / permanently missing feed`
7. `Still-open` (right-censored)

Calibration, abstention and conformal sets are reported on this 5+ outcome space; the conditional `(prefix)=P(default_90d=1 | Activated, prefix)` is reported as a *secondary* model trained only on activated-mature cases with explicit selection warning.

**Why not counterfactual as primary?** `*(prefix)=P(Y(1)=1 | prefix)` = default if *we forced activation* (`do(Activated)`). This is what "default for every application" literally implies if we treat rejected as having a latent default outcome. It requires strong ignorability / no unmeasured confounding (`Y(1)  Activated | prefix`) plus stable policy and no interference, and an activation-policy specification. With a 1-Jul-2025 threshold shift and unobserved underwriting notes, this is fundamentally untestable. We keep it as a **sensitivity-only counterfactual** labeled `P_counterfactual_under_[policy_preJul2025]`, estimated via IPW/ doubly-robust with bounds, never as the default dashboard metric.

**Credible alternatives and different question:**

*Alternative A — Conditional default | eventually activated:* Population = only eventually activated & mature (21,600). Estimand ``. Answers pricing/reserving: "Given we *will* book this loan, what is 90-day default risk?" Operationally useful for limit-setting but **cannot** be applied to every prefix without selection bias: `P(default|prefix, Activated)` is inflated/deflated vs `P(default|prefix)` because activation screens on risk. Naively deploying  to rejected cases imputes risk where exposure never happened.

*Alternative B — Time-to-default after activation with right censoring:* `(t)=P(T_default  [t,t+dt) | Tt, prefix)`, `T` days since activation, censored by extraction, transfer, early closure. Answers "when" to intervene in collections, requires survival assumptions (independent censoring, competing risk of closure) and still conditions on activation. Different horizon than fixed 90-day binary.

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy
We distinguish five mechanisms, often conflated:

1. **Ordinary right-censoring:** Still-open cases (5,100) and recent starts where ` + window > C`. Event time not observed by C. Assumption: censoring time independent of future outcome given prefix covariates and calendar time (testable partially, see below). Handled via landmark competing-risk with inverse probability of censoring weighting (IPCW) or as `censored` category in multi-state.

2. **Delayed labels (label maturity):** `default_90d` defined 90d post-activation but feed arrives +7–35d. A case activated on 2025-12-01 has default horizon 2026-03-01, feed until 2026-04-05. As of C=2026-02-15 it is immature. This is *not* non-default. Primary rule: impose **35-day embargo**: a case is only considered label-mature if `label_available_at  C` *and* `activation_time + 125d  C`. Otherwise excluded from supervised loss, kept for censoring model.

3. **Selection through activation:** Rejected/withdrawn never expose `Y`. `default_90d` is **undefined**, not 0. This is selective observation, not censoring. Requires either (a) factual  with =0 for these cases, or (b) counterfactual * with untestable ignorability. We do (a) as primary.

4. **Transfer as competing event / missing follow-up:** Transfer = competing terminal outcome *under our ledger*; but 40% unlinked means default could occur on partner platform and be unobserved to us. Primary: treat transfer as competing state with known linkage indicator. Sensitivity: bounds [all transferred = non-default vs transfer-default rate = activated rate] and pattern-mixture using linked subset.

5. **Permanently missing outcome feed:** Partner-originated activated accounts where feed never arrives even after 125d. Missingness likely MNAR (elevated risk). Primary: model missingness indicator `M` and report  with IPCW + missing-feed weight; flag partner-originated as high-uncertainty subgroup with wider prediction intervals. Sensitivity: tipping-point analysis varying `P(default|M=1)` from 0.5× to 3× observed rate.

**Primary analysis strategy:** Landmark () multi-state classification with **IPCW for censoring + embargo** + **factorization**. Steps:
- Estimate `G(t|prefix, calendar) = P(Censored > t | prefix)` on all prefixes using a censoring survival model (only pre- covariates). Weight each mature observation by `1/G`.
- Fit (i) activation/competing-risk model `P(state | prefix)` on all 48k with censored cases weighted, and (ii) conditional default model `P(default | Activated, prefix)` on activated-mature with additional missing-feed weights.
- Report joint  = (i)*(ii) with calibrated probabilities and uncertainty intervals; decision threshold applied to , not  alone.

**Sensitivity analyses (mandatory):**
- Worst/best-case imputation for immature: set all 3,600 to 0/1 to bound prevalence.
- Policy-stratified : train/test separately pre/post 2025-07-01; report delta.
- Counterfactual bounds under Manski/Heckman: IPW with exclusion restriction `case:channel, case:region` proxies, and partial identification intervals without ignorability.

**Assumption testability:**
- *Testable:* Distribution of feed delay (7–35d) via observed `label_available_at - (activation+90d)`; covariate dependence of censoring via C-index of censoring model; activity renaming effect via pre/post variant frequency.
- *Partially testable:* Whether withdrawal/transfer depends on prefix risk (fit competing-risk model; check calibration but cannot prove no unmeasured confounder).
- *Fundamentally untestable:* Counterfactual default of rejected if activated (`Y(1)`); MNAR permanent missingness mechanism; whether policy change interacts with unobserved applicant quality beyond prefix.

**B. Prefix-derived (control-flow + time + rework):**
- Control-flow: prefix length k, activity counts, last activity, variant id (low-freq bucketed), presence of `Request Documents`, `n_doc_requests`.
- Timing: `elapsed_time_since_start`, `mean/median inter-event time`, `time_since_last_event`, `hour/day-of-week of last event`; `remaining_sojourn_proxy` via `pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe` (only waiting/service *up to* , never using future `insert_case_service_waiting_time` beyond  — interpreted as queue experienced so far).
- Rework/Complexity: `rework_count`, `credit_review_reentries`, `documents_requested_total_to_date`, `loop_entropy`.
- Sequential risk: trajectory of `risk_score` *up to  only* — first, last, delta, slope, volatility; never `risk_score` updated after .

**C. Historical reference / cohort-calendar (requires past data only):**
- Resource/queue at : `org:resource` workload: number of open cases assigned to resource in preceding 7d *strictly before * (computed via temporal join, not `org:resource` experience at extraction); `resource_tenure_at_event` recomputed from HR join on `time:timestamp`, not `data_extract_time`.
- Calendar/cohort: `cases_started_last_7d_by_channel`, `rejection_rate_last_30d` (pre- only), `policy_era = [pre/post 2025-07-01]`, `migration_era` flags. These capture selection-pressure drift without using post- outcomes.

Distinction enforced: static = no temporal aggregation; prefix-derived = aggregation over `events  `; historical-reference = aggregation over *other cases* with `time:timestamp < `.

## 6. Leakage audit

| Feature or data element | Leakage or bias risk | Valid availability rule | Keep/repair/drop | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `final_limit` | High: populated only after final credit decision; encodes acceptance + risk tier | Available only after `Accept` event timestamp | **Drop** from early prefix model; may be used only in secondary model where   decision time with explicit label | Using it at k=3 would give perfect separation of Activated vs Rejected and leak future selection. |
| `decision_reason` | High: populated at/after Accept/Reject | Available only at termination | **Drop** for early prediction | Directly reveals competing outcome; future information. |
| `collection_status` | Critical: updated after activation, may verbatim say `Default Recorded` | Only after activation; never   | **Drop** | Target leakage (is the label). |
| Future events: `Contract Signed`, `Account Activated`, `Collections Contact`, `Default Recorded` | Critical | `time:timestamp  ` only | **Drop** any event > ; enforce prefix filtering | Post-prefix events define outcome; would inflate AUROC to ~1. |
| `documents_total` (nightly snapshot) | Medium: snapshot includes docs uploaded later same calendar day than nominal  | Value valid only if `snapshot_time  ` (strict); else use last snapshot strictly before  | **Repair**: recompute as `docs_uploaded where upload_time  `; discard same-day future docs | Otherwise lookahead bias on rework feature. |
| `risk_score` updated at several events | Medium: later updates post- leak evolving underwriting info | Use only `risk_score` values with `time:timestamp  `; derive `first/last/delta  ` | **Repair**: version by event time; drop post- updates | Late score may already incorporate final decision. |
| Resource experience (`org:resource` tenure) | Medium: stored as experience at extraction 2026-02-15, not event time | Recompute tenure as `event_date - hire_date` per event | **Repair**: join HR table as-of event; drop raw extraction-time column | Otherwise overstates experience for early 2024 cases, biases resource features. |
| `default_90d` (repeated on events) | Critical | Label defined only if `label_available_at  C-35d` and `activation+90d  C-35d` | **Keep** as target *only* after embargo; **drop** as feature ever | Label leakage if used as input. |
| `label_available_at`, `data_extract_time`, `source_system` | Medium: availability indicates maturity/selection (e.g., partner feed missing) | Allowed as *censoring/maturity* indicator for cohort logic, never as default predictor directly | **Keep for cohort logic / missingness model, drop from default predictor unless debiased** | Using them as features would learn "when label is missing" not risk. |
| `final_limit`/`risk_score` imputed missing | Bias: rejected cases never have limit — impute leaks | Do not impute from post-decision distribution | **Drop/repair** with missing-indicator only | Selection bias. |

## 7. Validation design
**Primary split is temporal by `case:concept:name` start time, not random case split.** Random split mixes pre/post-policy and pre/post-migration cases, allows future cases to predict past, and overstates performance because `documents_total` distribution and selection probability shift on 2025-07-01.

Scheme with embargo (example cutoffs configurable):

- **Train:** `Application Started  [2024-01-01, 2025-03-31]` with history cutoff `C_train = 2025-09-30`. A case is training-labeled only if its `activation+90+35  C_train`; otherwise censored. This ensures 5 months maturity and isolates pre-policy regime.
- **Validation (pre-policy):** Start `[2025-04-01, 2025-06-30]`, `C_val = 2025-12-31`. Used for hyperparameter tuning, calibration (Platt/isotonic on validation time slice), and early stopping. Captures drift just before policy change.
- **Test1 — Post-policy:** Start `[2025-07-01, 2025-09-30]`, `C_test1 = 2026-02-15` (or +35d beyond). Measures performance under new thresholds. Report delta TrainTest1 as policy-shift degradation.
- **Test2 — Post-migration:** Start `[2025-10-15, 2025-12-31]`, `C_test2 = 2026-02-15` but limited maturity  many censored; evaluate competing-outcome accuracy and calibration with heavier weighting, flag duplicate-handling robustness. Alternatively use rolling-origin backtesting: expand training window monthly, refit censoring model, test on next month's starts.

`pm4py.split_train_test` mentioned only as baseline to show inflation: we report its optimistic AUROC vs temporal AUROC.

**Metrics appropriate to target + censoring:**
- For  (multi-state): polytomous Brier score / log-loss, one-vs-rest AUPRC (rare default ~5.6% joint), confusion for competing states, calibration curves per state (reliability diagram, ECE) and *censoring-adjusted* calibration via IPCW.
- For  (conditional): time-dependent AUROC, AUPRC, Brier, calibration slope/intercept, decision-curve net benefit at operating threshold (cost of false positive review vs missed default).
- Time-to-default: C-index (Harrell/ Uno IPCW), integrated Brier, cumulative incidence function (CIF) calibration.
- Uncertainty-aware: prediction interval coverage (conformal sets for multi-state), subgroup ECE by `case:channel`, `case:region`, `policy_era`, `partner_originated`, `migration_period`; report selective prediction performance when abstaining on high-uncertainty transfers.
- Distribution shift: PSI/K-S on prefix features TrainTest, plus dynamic calibration drift (monthly ECE).

**Why random split misleading:** (1) Uses knowledge of 2025 policy to predict 2024 cases — temporally impossible. (2) Leak of label maturity: random test includes mature 2024 cases while train includes immature 2025 cases with same prefix. (3) Hides migration artifact. Temporal backtest mimics production where model scores *future* applications whose labels are not yet mature.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary
**What we can claim:** Under the observed activation policy  (including 2025-07-01 threshold), an application with prefix p has estimated factual probability (p) of becoming an activated default loss within 90d, with calibrated uncertainty accounting for right-censoring and delayed feed. We can rank prefixes by factual risk and predict competing outcomes (reject/withdraw/transfer) under current operations. Association is predictive, not causal.

**What we cannot claim:** That a rejected application *would have* defaulted if activated. `(p)` is *selection-conditioned*; `*(p)` counterfactual requires untestable ignorability and a specified reference policy. We cannot promise performance if thresholds, applicant pool, or partner linkage changes, or if `risk_score` generation changes.

**Selection vs prediction:** High  may reflect (a) truly risky borrower, (b) being in a policy era that activates riskier borrowers, (c) being from a channel with low withdrawal. Model learns `P(default & activated | prefix)`, not `P(inherent creditworthiness)`. Threshold tuning on  changes realized default rate partly by changing *who gets activated*, not just by screening risk.

**Three ways deployment changes the DGP (feedback loops):**
1. **Suppression / selective labels:** If loan officers use  to reject high- prefixes, future training data will have fewer high-risk activated cases (positive feedback),  will appear to improve while true risk is censored — classic performative prediction and dataset shift.
2. **Strategic applicant/resource behavior:** Applicants learn document-request patterns and alter submission timing; resources game queue metrics; `documents_total` and waiting-time features drift, invalidating historical-reference features.
3. **Policy and system automation:** Auto-approval for low- or migration fix for duplicates changes the event log generation itself (activity names, loops) and the activation selection probability, breaking the factual  estimand and requiring refit with new policy_era indicator and re-embargo.

**Decision-use boundary:** Use  for *triage and workload prioritization* (e.g., enhanced review for high , fast-track low  competing-risk of acceptance) with human-in-the-loop and monthly calibration monitoring. Do **not** use  alone to auto-decline, auto-set `final_limit`, or report "default rate if we activated everyone" without a labeled policy assumption and uncertainty interval that includes MNAR bounds. Defer high-uncertainty transfers/partner-originated to manual review.
