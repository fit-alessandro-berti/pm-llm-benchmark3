## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

**(a) Upstream data-quality chain.** Everything downstream inherits classification and document quality. A mis-route at Classify (8% exception rate, sitting behind the 11,680-frequency RegisterClassify edge) silently flips: whether collateral valuation enters the path (71% vs 18% by product), whether the case hits weekly-cadence committee, and whether the four-eyes rule applies. Document completeness (A5) determines whether Normalize  Credit analysis (5,000-edge) is fed with reliable inputs; normalization quality then propagates into Committee packaging (an explicit convergence artifact) and Contract preparation. This is why A8 and A9 are unsafe before A5/A6-class foundations exist.

**(b) Legal/compliance controls as structural dependencies.** Three gates constrain sequencing: (1) KYC clearance must precede Customer signing — A10 is therefore designed not as a reminder bot but as a *state-enforcing* gate that reads authoritative KYC status before issuing any signature invitation; (2) Committee-decision-to-contract consistency — A9's rule checks depend on decision data being trustworthy, hence year two; (3) the four-eyes separation between Credit analysis and Committee packaging for High-exposure SME — until an assignment-aware tool (A8 era) exists, SoD must be enforced procedurally (roster design, month-end smoothing). Every recommender in the portfolio (A2 later, A3 shadow, A4 now) must log suggestion  override  final decision.

**(c) Human escalation capacity.** The bottleneck-sensitive pools are not symmetric: Compliance specialists = 3 FTE with workload concentrated on two seniors (handover-network evidence); Credit analysts = 9 FTE and central in the working-together network. A4 must cap concurrent escalations to the two seniors, add cross-trained second-line staff funded from recovered specialist hours, and A5/A6 must be sequenced so analysts absorb inflow gradually (WIP limits, staggered go-live) — otherwise operators will improvise workarounds, which is precisely how current month-end four-eyes violations (5.8% of High-exposure cases) arise.

**(d) External valuation.** The 58h wait cannot be automated away — the counterparty is external. Manageable levers are triage (start valuation earlier, potentially parallel to normalization rather than strictly after credit analysis), valuer routing/SLAs, and an alternate panel. The DFG leakage (2,350 waited, only 1,980 reaching packaging) should be tracked as an abandonment/rework signal.

**(e) Core-banking integration budget.** Two deep integrations in the first six months. Allocate deliberately: **slot #1  A6** (document/credit-data-layer write-back, because analyst throughput is the binding constraint) and **slot #2  A10** (authoritative KYC-state read and signing-state enforcement — rails that A9 and eventually A11 will reuse). A5 and A4 are deliberately designed as lightweight overlays so neither deep slot is spent on them; this reserves architectural runway for year-two A9.

**(f) Anomalies to investigate before scaling (P0).** Two DFG fragments warrant control attention regardless of automation: Resolve entity mismatch  Contract preparation (380 cases) appears to reach contracting without evidence of committee passage; and provisional signing indicates the KYC-before-signing invariant is currently soft.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

Each portfolio honors the four-module cap; only the third meets the compliance-first mandate.

**Portfolio Q — Quick-win (A1 + A2 + A5 + A10).** Optimizes visible throughput: automated registration (politically legible win on the 98%-stable position-2 activity), classification hygiene, doc-loop reduction, faster signing. Sacrifices: no analyst absorption (A6 absent)  the 20h normalize and 28h credit-analysis waits persist or grow; the 31h entity-mismatch tail and 58h valuation sync untouched; end-to-end improvement modest because it doesn't touch any true scarcity. Uses one deep-integration slot on A1, which has the weakest leverage-per-slot ratio in the list.

**Portfolio B — Strict bottleneck/synchronization (A4 + A5 + A6 + A7).** Optimizes the longest waits and largest rework loops of the middle mile: entity mismatch (31h), documentation churn (3,300-loop), normalization gate (20h), external valuation (58h). Strong relief for the 3-FTE specialist pool and analysts; disproportionate benefit to Equipment-finance variants. Sacrifices: the 44h signing wait and the provisional-signing problem remain; **fails the compliance mandate as written** (no control-strengthening module); also concentrates the two hardest AI builds (A6 + A4-quality NLP/matching) in one delivery year.

**Portfolio C — Compliance-first (A9 + A12 + A3-shadow + A10-gated).** Optimizes violation suppression: contract-vs-decision rule checks, a workbench for control remediation, supervised KYC hit triage, and the signing gate. Sacrifices: barely moves cycle time — the 58h, 31h, 28h, 20h, and 18h waits are all unaddressed; A9 arrives before the data foundation it depends on, so its control-rule checks partly automate inconsistent upstream data (GIGO); little visible delivery, sustaining political pressure for "just automate registration."

## 4. Expected process-level effects

Directional planning assumptions anchored to the measured medians; to be validated at each phase gate, not treated as committed ROI.

**Portfolio Q (quick-win).**
- *Cycle time:* Modest trim (~8–15% on simple Micro/SME paths); High-exposure essentially unchanged (committee-cadence bound).
- *Waiting:* Registration (1.5h) and doc-review loops improve; the dominant waits (44h signing partially via A10; 31h mismatch; 58h valuation) persist.
- *Rework:* 3,300-request loop partially reduced; misrouting reduced by A2.
- *Handovers:* Fewer back-office touches; back office becomes visibly "quiet" (it was already <65% utilized after 15:00 — cosmetic).
- *Resource demand:* Shifts toward Compliance ops and analysts as inflow accelerates without A6 — expect lengthening Normalize and Credit-analysis queues, the two worst internal waits (20h/28h).
- *Control violations:* Only the signing gate improves; four-eyes month-end risk unchanged and possibly worse as pace rises.

**Portfolio B (strict bottleneck).**
- *Cycle time:* Large gains on Equipment-finance and entity-mismatch cohorts; strongest structural relief of the three.
- *Waiting:* Material compression at mismatch (31h), doc review (18h), normalize (20h); valuation wait attacked via earlier triggers; signing wait (44h) untouched — a large omission for 7,300 cases.
- *Rework:* Big cuts in the 2,800-return doc loop and 38% mismatch rework.
- *Handovers:* Fewer KYC re-entries (1,160 edge shrinks); fewer analyst interruptions.
- *Resource demand:* Specialists unblocked; analysts shielded by A6; collateral coordination effort rises slightly.
- *Control violations:* Essentially unaddressed  does not satisfy the compliance constraint.

**Portfolio C (compliance-first).**
- *Cycle time:* Near-flat; constraint violated on efficiency goals.
- *Waiting:* Unchanged except signing.
- *Rework:* Contract remediation (650-loop; 14% renewals) drops sharply *if* upstream data is adequate — doubtful in year one.
- *Handovers:* More QA/second-check handovers introduced deliberately.
- *Resource demand:* Shifts toward assurance roles.
- *Control violations:* Largest reduction of the three; purchased at the cost of ignoring every major wait.

**Recommended portfolio B — "Flow & Integrity" (A5 + A6 + A4 + A10-gated).**
- *Cycle time:* Expect 20–35% reduction on Micro/SME working-capital paths; largest relative gains on the entity-mismatch cohort (31h median wait plus 55-minute handling plus 38% rework concentrated on 82%-critical-path occurrences); High-exposure SME improves mainly via arrival-readiness at the weekly committee (packaging waits shrink) rather than committee throughput itself.
- *Waiting:* Mismatch ~halved-to-thirds; signing wait compressed materially as e-sign adoption replaces the 21% reminder loop; doc review and normalize waits fall together (they are causally linked through the 3,300/2,800 loop and 5,050-edge into normalization); credit-analysis wait *stabilizes* rather than grows — an explicit design objective.
- *Rework:* Repeat doc requests down sharply (baseline 52% repeated); mismatch loops down; provisional-signing remediation ~eliminated on the automated path via the KYC gate; contract-decision mismatch loops **persist** in year one — compensated manually (see §5) because A9 is withheld until data foundations mature.
- *Handovers:* Fewer back-officeanalyst round-trips; fewer KYCspecialist oscillations; RMcustomer handovers become templated (addresses the social-network finding that RMs initiate defective instructions).
- *Resource demand:* Headcount-neutral by design; mix shifts from chasing and re-keying toward exception resolution and QA. Back-office slack is left as-is (that surplus is intentionally consumed, not automated away). Watchpoint: parallel extraction fallback staffing for low-quality PDFs.
- *Control violations:* Signing legality mechanically enforced (gate); four-eyes violations addressed by procedure plus monitoring in year one (spread month-end load, rotation of checkers, violation heatmap) — A8/A9-grade embedding deferred; model overrides fully logged per the audit-trail requirement.
- *Concurrency note:* Because doc review and KYC often overlap despite sequential event logs, A5+A6 gains on the two branches will partially coincide; monitoring must use reconstructed concurrency-aware timelines.

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

**Phase 0 — Instrumentation & governance (Months 0–2, no module budget consumed).**
Baseline per-variant cycle/waiting distributions; rebuild concurrency-aware timelines for doc review  KYC; stand up control dashboards (provisional signing, four-eyes heatmap, decision-to-contract variance, valuation leakage 2,3501,980); investigate the 380-case mismatchcontract path; DPIA/security review for document AI and e-sign; RM council and design authority chartered.
*No deep integration used.*

**Phase 1 — First wave, lightest modules (Months 2–4).**
Go-live: **A5** (pilot on Micro working-capital, then SME) and **A10 tier 1** (reminders + adoption campaign). Development starts on deep integration #1 (**A6** data-layer write-back) and deep integration #2 (**A10** authoritative KYC-state gate).

*Gate G1 (end M4):* repeat-request share down meaningfully from 52%; customer complaints flat; e-sign reminder loops shrinking; **zero** signed-before-KYC-clearance events on the new path; Normalize queue depth stable (if growing  throttle A5, accelerate A6).

**Phase 2 — Absorption and gate enforcement (Months 4–7).**
**A6** pilot with confidence bands and staffed fallback desk; scale behind gate. **A10 gate goes live** (signature invitations blocked until authoritative KYC clearance). **A4** assistant piloted with the two senior specialists as co-designers.
*Integration ledger: two deep integrations consumed — A6 (slot #1), A10 (slot #2).* Both A5 and A4 deliberately remain lightweight overlays.

*Gate G2 (end M7):* extraction accuracy above the agreed floor with fallback volume below threshold; per-case analyst minutes down without a rise in QA-flagged files; Normalize (20h) and Credit-analysis (28h) waits trending down, not up; mismatch median wait beginning to compress.

**Phase 3 — Scale and hardening (Months 7–12).**
A6 to all products; A4 general availability plus cross-training of a second line in compliance (funded from recovered specialist hours); A5 extended to Credit-line renewals with tailored instruction templates; month-end workload smoothing and checker rotation running; year-two preparation: seed the A9 rule library from classified remediation codes, negotiate valuer SLAs (A7 precursor), curate the A2 training corpus from corrected classifications, draft the A1 intake-revamp proposal.

## 7. Final recommendation

**Adopt the modified bottleneck portfolio: A5  A6  A10(with KYC gate)  A4**, deployed in the phased order above. The other two portfolio shapes fail at least one hard constraint: Portfolio Q lacks analyst absorption and leaves the deepest waits standing; strict Portfolio B fails the control-strengthening mandate; Portfolio C spends the year's budget on the smallest set of process-lever improvements and deploys A9 onto unreliable data.

**Why the highest-frequency activity should not be automated first.** Senior management's instinct optimizes the wrong variable. Frequency ranks *activity instances*; end-to-end performance is governed by *synchronization, rework, and scarcity*, and the evidence separates these cleanly:

1. **Visible queues are not scarce resources.** Registration is enormous (11,880 cases, 8 minutes each) and	Register's position-2 placement is 98% stable — the perfect-looking automation target. But the median wait before it is 1.5h, and the owning back office runs below 65% utilization after 15:00. Freeing that capacity converts into almost no lead time; it produces an impressive dashboard number while the binding constraints sit elsewhere.
2. **Low-frequency activities dominate the clock and the tail.** Resolve entity mismatch occurs 1,750 times yet carries a 31-hour median wait, sits on the critical path in 82% of its occurrences, blocks both KYC and signing, loops back into KYC 1,160 times, and lands on a 3-FTE pool carried by two seniors. Wait for external valuation occurs 2,350 times and costs 58 hours. Customer signing affects 7,300 cases at 44 hours with a 21% reminder loop. Automation spend should buy down the largest totals, and none of those totals is proportional to activity frequency.
3. **Resource topology punishes naive throughput gains.** Accelerating clean cases (A1, even A5 alone) pushes load into the exact pools the social network shows are central and thin — the two senior specialists and nine analysts — reproducing the conditions under which the current violations (month-end four-eyes at 5.8%, 6.5% provisional signings) already emerge. Hence A6 is sequenced as the absorber, and A5 throttles against the normalize queue.

**Why the portfolio is still politically saleable.** It delivers visible, customer-facing wins early (checklist clarity, nudgeable missing documents, dramatically faster legal signing) without spending the irreplaceable deep-integration slots on the weakest-leverage module. It satisfies compliance's mandate structurally: the KYC-clearance signing gate is a real control, not a reporting feature, and every model in the wave ships with mandatory suggest/override/final audit logging. It leaves fraud-severe territory (KYC hit triage, automated activation) in supervised or deferred modes.

**Trade-offs accepted, explicitly.** The 58-hour valuation wait is addressed organizationally this year (triage, earlier triggers, SLAs) rather than by a module, because partner-dependent automation cannot be safely rushed and A6's slot was the better use of scarce integration capacity. Contract mismatch loops continue at their current rate, bridged by a targeted manual double-check on Credit-line renewals, because automated contract generation on today's upstream data would industrialize defects — exactly the failure mode the constraints prohibit. Both items anchor a concrete, gate-conditioned year-two plan: A9, A2, and A7 next, then A1 as part of a redesigned intake, with A3 in shadow mode throughout, and A8/A11 admitted only once the upstream control chain proves reliable.
