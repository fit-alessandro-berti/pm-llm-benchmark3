- KM: appropriate for  (any-terminal survival) per stratum; or KM of resolution treating competing events as censoring  estimates net survival / "resolution in absence of competing events" — hypothetical, generally not the operational estimand; also with informative transfer-censoring it's biased even for the hypothetical. - Aalen-Johansen CIF: primary. Gives  = 1  F_res(72),  decomposition,  = S(72). Nonparametric, handles competing events without independence assumptions among event types (only censoring independence needed), works with small n (wide CIs). Stratify by cohort and priority (small cells; report CIs via Aalen variance or bootstrap).
- Cause-specific hazards (Cox per cause, treating other causes as censored): for understanding which covariates drive resolution hazard vs withdrawal hazard etc.; effect sizes are hazard ratios — useful for staffing/policy levers; but 32 cases with ~13 resolutions  at most 1–2 covariates, penalization, or just descriptive. - Subdistribution hazards (Fine-Gray): directly models CIF — useful for individual risk prediction of eventual non-resolution; but interpretation subtleties and small-sample issues. Secondary.
- Restricted mean time in state (RMST/rMST) up to 72h (or up to max follow-up): area under survival/CIF curves  expected hours in "active" state by 72, expected resolutions by 72 etc. Communicable to ops ("expected active-hours per case before 72h"). Now think about "long event gaps make censoring informative" more carefully. Two distinct issues: (a) Administrative censoring at a common cutoff is typically assumed independent — but here censoring time = age at cutoff depends on Submit date, which may correlate with case mix/workload (cases submitted just before cutoff are censored young).
