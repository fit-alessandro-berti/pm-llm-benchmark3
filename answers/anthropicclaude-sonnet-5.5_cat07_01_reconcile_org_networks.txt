# 2. Competing role hypotheses

The hypotheses are not mutually exclusive, and the evidence does not rank them decisively.

### H1. Formal coordinator / exception desk
- **Mechanism:** Bot_Rules deliberately routes flagged cases to her. She triages, requests information, reopens, escalates and dispatches them, and specialists return answers to her.
- **Supporting evidence:**
  - Consistent 3.5-minute entry handovers.
  - Her 64% and 71% shares of reopens and complaints.
  - Temp_1 and Temp_2 escalations land on her.
  - Adjusters say she knows where hard cases go.
  - Routine cases are unaffected by her absence.
- **Contradicting or limiting evidence:**
  - Whether this is a documented role is unknown. "Assign Specialist" is not in the activity list.
  - Some specialist handovers do not pass through her (about 34% of Omar's cases are not explained by her edges).
  - It does not explain the absence-related delay unless no backup was designated.
- **Predictions:**
  - Stable routing by case_type and exception category.
  - Consistent time-to-first-touch.
  - A written or implicit rule set that explains most of her routing.
  - Similar behavior across weeks.

### H2. Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency
- **Mechanism:** Exceptions can only progress after her decision, and there is no trained deputy. Queue age and variability grow when she is unavailable, is interrupted, or has tacit knowledge that others lack.
- **Supporting evidence:**
  - Extreme handover and subcontracting betweenness.
  - A p90 jump of about the absence length.
  - A unique activity profile.
  - "High variance" contribution.
  - Specialists depend on her dispatches.
- **Contradicting or limiting evidence:**
  - Only about 67 logged hours in 12 weeks, so there is no apparent saturation of effort.
  - Mean handover time to her is short (3.5 min).
  - The absence is a single uncontrolled window.
  - Routine flow is unaffected.
  - She may be a single point of failure without being a bottleneck in normal operation.
- **Predictions:**
  - Exception queue age and waiting time correlate with her availability, not with her daily volume.
  - Time-of-day or day-of-week patterns.
  - Cases handled while she is away show longer tails.
  - Wait before her first touch grows with her WIP. If it does not, the issue is continuity, not capacity.

### H3. Organizational boundary spanner / triage broker
- **Mechanism:** She connects North and South teams, specialists, temporary staff and bots, so she holds information that no team lead holds. Her brokerage position produces high betweenness and low embeddedness.
- **Supporting evidence:**
  - Fan-out to both teams' adjusters.
  - Specialist answers return to her.
  - Low co-working with team leads but above-chance co-working with adjusters.
  - Cross-team reassignments (MiraCarla/Diego/Elise/Farid).
  - Informal reputation as the person who "knows where hard cases should go".
- **Contradicting or limiting evidence:**
  - Brokerage can also mean fragmentation. Low tie with team leads may reflect bypass and weak oversight.
  - Knowledge and skill claims are qualitative only.
  - There is no outcome evidence that her routing improves results.
- **Predictions:**
  - Her routing choices reduce rework, reopen or specialist bounce rates compared with matched cases routed otherwise.
  - Cases she routes across team boundaries have better or at least equal outcomes.
  - Removing her edges would create new direct ties from team leads to specialists or more formal escalations.

### H4. Specialist scheduler and discretionary gatekeeper
- **Mechanism:** She both identifies problem cases and selects the next specialist or action, which in effect allocates scarce specialist capacity and shapes who does what.
- **Supporting evidence:**
  - The compliance concern.
  - MiraOmar (210) and MiraPriya (180) handovers.
  - Specialist exception shares of 0.62 and 0.71 with "high" or "very high" cycle contribution.
  - The Bot_RulesMiraBot_Rules fraud screen update (62).
  - 71% of complaints, 64% of reopens.
- **Contradicting or limiting evidence:**
  - Nothing shows the discretion is misused, inconsistent or unreviewed. Discretion in a coordinator role is normal.
  - Possible policies or checklists are not visible in the log.
  - Specialists' overload might reflect case complexity.
- **Predictions:**
  - If routing is rule-consistent, specialist assignment is predictable from case attributes (classifier accuracy is high).
  - If discretionary, there is a residual pattern by person or time.
  - Assignment concentration, overrides of Bot_Rules outputs, and outcome differences should be checked and explained by case features.

### H5. Artifact of rules design and logging
- **Mechanism:** Her centrality reflects how Bot_Rules and the workflow tool are configured. Rules dump unresolved cases to a single resource, and administrative events (assignment, reopen, routing) may be logged under her name even if done by a system or shared queue.
- **Supporting evidence:**
  - Bot_Rules has betweenness 0.41 and sends 920 cases to her.
  - "Assign Specialist" is not in the catalogue.
  - Handover counts exceed event counts.
  - Timestamps look complete-only.
  - The exception flag may be set by routing itself (circularity).
  - Close Claim appears only under Temp_2 (470 of 4,100 cases), so the log may be incomplete.
- **Contradicting or limiting evidence:**
  - Human-described roles (adjusters, process owner) and the absence effect are independent of log artifacts.
  - The behavioral pattern (short events, then disappearing) fits a real human dispatch function.
- **Predictions:**
  - Changing routing rules or logging configuration moves her centrality without changing outcomes.
  - Events attributed to her have system-like timestamps or clustering, and show regularities by rule version.
  - Audit of the source system shows other actors or automations behind some events.

**Discriminating tests:** the "absence only affects exceptions" result is compatible with H1, H2 and H3. Outcome-matched routing quality separates H3 from H4. A routing-rule audit and event-source check test H5. Queue-before-her versus continuity-when-absent tests H2's mechanism.

  - "Assign Specialist" is missing from the activity list.

  - Close Claim appears under one resource only.

- Do not formalize her as a team lead, or split detection from selection, until routing quality and discretion patterns are assessed. A pilot of a four-eyes check on a sample is acceptable.
- Do not replace her routing with automated rules until rule-consistency analysis shows that her decisions are reproducible.
- Do not reallocate work among adjusters, specialists or temps on the basis of centrality alone.
- Do not treat the "queue cleaner" versus "knows where hard cases go" disagreement as settled.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira_Coordinator is the point where difficult claims are sorted and sent onward. She sits at the crossroads of the handover network and often sends work to specialists and takes it back, but she rarely works alongside the same people, because her role is brief sorting, assigning and reopening rather than team work. High centrality is not good or bad in itself. When she was absent for six days, routine claims were unaffected, yet the slowest 10% of exception cases took about five and a half days longer. That points to real dependence on her, and to a missing backup, rather than proof of overload. Because she both spots problems and chooses the next specialist, routing discretion should be checked, though nothing in the data shows misuse. Some data gaps also need fixing. First steps: name and train a deputy, log reasons for routing decisions, and track exception queue age. Do not remove or restructure her role until outcomes are compared.
