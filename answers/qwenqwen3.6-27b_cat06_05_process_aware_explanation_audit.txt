## 2. Process-model consistency checks

A robust validation suite for `t0` explanations should enforce the following checks before serving SHAP or pattern attributions:

1. **Prefix-time availability audit**: Traverse the feature lineage graph. Any feature whose computation depends on an event with `timestamp > t0` or `extraction_date > t0` is rejected. This catches batch-joined aggregates, nightly snapshots, and post-closure warehouse fields.
2. **Activity-order feasibility (Declarative & DFG)**: 
   - *Declarative checks*: Enforce hard constraints like `precedes(Coverage Check, Damage Assessment)`, `response(Start, End)`, and `not(precedes(Claim Closed, Damage Assessment))`. Violations trigger immediate flagging.
   - *DFG checks*: Frequent directed edges in historical traces can suggest typical flows, but **frequent  normative**. A DFG edge must be validated against the declarative model before being used in explanations. Limitations: DFGs cannot express cardinality or state-dependent enabling.
3. **Same-timestamp ambiguity resolution**: Events sharing a timestamp must carry a reliable sequence index. If missing, treat them as an unordered set. Exclude from sequential pattern mining until resolved, as arbitrary ordering creates phantom patterns (e.g., `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment`).
4. **Complete-trace information filter**: Regex/metadata scan for feature names containing `*_total`, `*_final`, `*_anywhere`, `full_trace`, or `last_*`. These are automatically quarantined for `t0` predictors unless explicitly versioned with `as_of` timestamps.
5. **State-enabled vs. impossible vs. future-possible mapping**: At `t0` (post-Coverage), the process state enables `Damage Assessment` or `Documents Requested`. `Fraud Investigation` is future-possible but not yet enabled. `Appeal`, `Settlement Decision`, and `Claim Closed` are impossible at `t0`. Explanations referencing impossible activities are rejected; future-possible activities may be referenced only if framed as risk probabilities, not observed facts.
6. **Repeated Settlement Decision validation**: A second `Settlement Decision Recorded` is semantically legitimate only if preceded by an `Appeal` in the prefix. The checker verifies the state machine transition `Partial/Deny -> Appeal -> Settlement Decision`. If no appeal exists, the duplicate is flagged as a logging error or misaligned timestamp.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

Predictive importance does not imply causality or actionability. Surviving or repairable signals fall into these classes:

- **Defensible predictive signal**: `initial_damage_complexity`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, `initial_reserve`, `claimant_channel`. 
  - *What may be claimed*: These features correlate with breach risk given the claim's intrinsic characteristics and time already consumed. They are stable, non-sensitive, and available at `t0`.
  - *What may not be claimed*: They do not cause delays. High complexity correlates with rework probability, but the model cannot predict which specific steps will fail.
- **Process-state or congestion signal**: `queue_length_at_t0`.
  - *What may be claimed*: Reflects systemic capacity constraints at prediction time. High queue length predicts breach due to resource contention, not claim complexity.
  - *Actionability*: Potentially actionable via dynamic routing or capacity scaling, but only if operational policies allow real-time reassignment.
- **Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review**: `claimant_region`.
  - *What may be claimed*: Captures unobserved heterogeneity (repair network density, language barriers, channel preferences, socioeconomic factors). Predictively valid but ethically sensitive.
  - *Standards*: Must undergo disparate impact analysis and counterfactual fairness testing. Cannot be used to justify differential treatment. Should be monitored for proxy discrimination.
- **Drift/calendar proxy**: `calendar_week`.
  - *What may be claimed*: Encodes operational rhythms, holiday closures, and policy shifts. Useful for calibration and batch-level risk adjustment.
  - *Standards*: Purely descriptive. Not actionable at the case level. Requires continuous drift monitoring; sudden shifts indicate process or policy changes needing investigation.
- **Unusable until lineage is repaired**: `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `risk_score_current`.
  - *What may be claimed*: Currently contaminated by post-`t0` data or extraction-time joins. Predictive value is illusory.
  - *Remediation*: Must be rebuilt with event-time semantics, `as_of` joins, or streaming aggregation before reintegration.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Feature-store design & historical timestamps**: Implement a time-travel capable feature store (e.g., Feast, Tecton) with strict `as_of` semantics. Every feature must carry an event-time or snapshot-time timestamp. Batch joins using `extraction_date` or nightly refreshes must be replaced with streaming or point-in-time correct joins.
2. **Prefix-only recomputation**: Retrain the model using only features provably available at `t0`. Expect a performance drop; this is the cost of eliminating leakage. Compare calibration curves before/after to quantify how much predictive power was illusory.
3. **Process-conformance tests in CI/CD**: Integrate declarative model checks and prefix availability audits into the feature pipeline. Reject any feature that references future activities, complete-trace aggregates, or impossible sequences. Use alignment-based conformance scoring to flag traces that deviate from the normative model.
4. **Calibration after feature removal**: Leakage removal often shifts probability distributions. Apply isotonic or Platt scaling to recalibrate outputs. Validate calibration using reliability diagrams and Brier scores on a held-out `t0` test set.
5. **Explanation stability monitoring**: Track SHAP/feature importance distributions over rolling windows. Leakage features typically dominate and destabilize explanations; their removal should increase stability. Alert on sudden shifts in top drivers, which may indicate process drift or feature pipeline breaks.
6. **Proxy/fairness analysis**: Conduct formal fairness audits for `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, and `calendar_week`. Use counterfactual fairness, disparate impact ratios, and proxy detection algorithms. Document that these features are retained for predictive validity but are not causally actionable or ethically justifiable for differential treatment.
7. **Monitoring for schema or process drift**: Deploy drift detectors for feature distributions, process variant frequencies, and prediction outputs. Monitor for new activities, policy changes, or timestamp alignment failures. Implement automated retraining triggers when drift exceeds thresholds, ensuring explanations remain aligned with the current operational reality.
