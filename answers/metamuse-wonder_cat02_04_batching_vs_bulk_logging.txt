# 1. Cluster diagnoses

# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

Direction of bias assumes the analyst naively uses `start` as service start and `complete` as service end for a single resource. “Waiting” = `start  queue_arrival`; “service” = `complete  start`.

| Cluster | Waiting-time bias | Service-time bias | Utilisation bias | Bottleneck-detection bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | Approximately unbiased for batch waiting if the batch start is the service start; but per-item waiting is identical by construction | Per-item service (3 min) is the *batch cycle time*, not item processing time; dividing by 4 without a batch model understates or overstates item cost depending on convention | Counting 4 × 3 min on one fixture overstates busy time 4-fold; correct busy time is 3 min at capacity 4 | Correctly a high-throughput cell; naive per-case analysis may falsely flag it as overloaded because utilisation >100% when overlaps are summed |
| G2 | Waiting to group start is real, but item-level waiting *inside* the group (10:00 to actual attention) is hidden, so later items’ true waiting is understated if 10:00 is taken as their service start | Service times (8, 14, 21, 27 min) are inflated for later-finishing items because they include time spent on earlier items after the common start; mean service overstated, variance overstated | Summed overlapping intervals give >100% utilisation for T-B; busy time double-counts the shared start period | May falsely identify T-B as a saturated bottleneck with long services, when the real constraint may be group-start policy or serial attention within the group |
| G3 | Waiting (`start  arrival`) is the least contaminated measure if starts are genuine; waiting computed to the common end would be inflated | Service times (28, 20, 13, 6 min) are distorted: early starters appear slow, late starters fast, purely because all ends are pinned to submission at 11:30; negative correlation between start and duration is an artifact signature | Busy time summed to a common end overstates overlap near 11:30 and creates a false utilisation spike | A phantom bottleneck / deadline spike at 11:30; throughput appears as a burst of 4 completions in one minute, distorting completion-rate and conformance timing checks |
| G4 | Unbiased, assuming scanner times are accurate; waiting grows modestly (7, 10, 13, 16, 19 min) as queue drains serially — genuine queueing signal | Unbiased serial service 4 min each; this is the reference pattern against which batch artifacts can be recognised | Correctly ~100% during 12:05–12:25 for a single server; no inflation | Correctly identifies a steadily loaded serial resource; a batch detector labelling this “sequential batching” must not be read as parallel capacity |
| G5 | Case-level waiting is computable, but attributing it to one analyst is wrong; pool-level waiting is meaningful | Individual service times are plausible per case, but assigning all to one worker creates impossible overlap; per-analyst service distribution is unidentified | Single-resource utilisation is grossly overstated (summed overlap 100%); true pool utilisation requires dividing by up to 3 analysts and knowing how many were present | False conclusion that one analyst is a multitasking bottleneck, or missed conclusion that staffing of the pool is the constraint; capacity is confounded with headcount |
| G6 | **Inflated**: every case appears to wait until 17:00 (e.g. C01 waits ~175 min from 14:05), because the bulk-entry time masquerades as service start; the queue appears to be ignored all afternoon and then served instantly | **Near-zero / exactly zero**: start = complete = 17:00 gives 0-min service for all seven cases — not fast work but missing work timestamps; any average including G6 drags mean service toward zero and destroys service-time distributions | Simultaneously absurd in both directions: busy time = 0 (resource looks idle) while completions = 7 at 17:00 (resource looks infinitely productive); utilisation is 0% with a vertical throughput spike | Creates a phantom end-of-day bottleneck (huge waits) *and* phantom infinite capacity (zero service) in the same rows; bottleneck rank, queue-length reconstruction, and conformance (activity duration = 0 may violate model constraints) are all corrupted |
| G7 | Waiting computed from rounded start (14:00) can be biased either way by up to the rounding window; M01 arrival 13:58 to start 14:00 looks like 2 min but true start is interval-censored | All services read exactly 15 min — variance is destroyed and true durations are censored into one grid cell; direction of bias per case unknown due to undocumented down/up rounding | Utilisation is quantised: either 0 or full 15-min blocks; overlapping identical intervals sum to 5×15 min on one resource | Bottleneck signals are grid artifacts; cannot distinguish a real batch, serial work within 14:00–14:15, or coarsening — exclude from fine-grained bottleneck ranking |
| G8 | Waiting is genuinely large for early cases (Q01 38 min) if clocks align, reflecting unavailability; but cross-clock offset between safety and queue systems adds uncertainty to the *cause*, not to the observed arrival-to-start gap on the scanner clock | Service 7 min each after 15:38 is credible serial work; unbiased if scanner clock is internally consistent | Utilisation is 0% 15:00–15:38 then ~100%; averaging over 15:00–16:13 understates loaded utilisation and overstates idle capacity unless the outage window is modelled | Correctly signals a capacity-loss episode followed by a saturated serial resource; risk is misattributing downtime to worker slowness or, if clocks differ, mis-dating the bottleneck window |

**Why G6 creates both inflated waiting and near-zero service (explicit mechanism):** bulk entry substitutes one session timestamp (17:00) for *both* lifecycle fields of every case. Waiting, defined as start  arrival, therefore inherits the full afternoon delay between each real arrival (14:05–16:25) and the 17:00 entry session — inflating waiting by hours. Service, defined as complete  start, subtracts the *same* substituted timestamp from itself and is identically zero — not because work was instantaneous, but because no within-day start/completion information survived entry. The two biases are coupled: the later the bulk entry relative to arrivals, the larger the fake waiting and the more completely real service duration is erased. Including G6 in queueing estimation would simultaneously suggest an enormous queue and a zero-service-time server, which violates any coherent queueing model (e.g. Little’s law consistency checks would fail).

def summarise_pm4py(pm):
    """Map PM4Py batch output back to clusters as defensively as the API allows."""
    if pm.empty:
        return {}
    out = {}
    type_col = next((c for c in pm.columns
                     if "batch" in c.lower() and ("type" in c.lower() or "categor" in c.lower())), None)
    case_col = next((c for c in pm.columns if "case" in c.lower()), None)
    case2cluster = dict(zip(df["case"], df["cluster"]))
    for _, row in pm.iterrows():
        label = str(row[type_col]) if type_col else "batch_detected"
        cases = row[case_col] if case_col else []
        if isinstance(cases, str):
            cases = [c.strip() for c in cases.strip("[]() ").split(",") if c.strip()]
        try:
            iterator = list(cases)
        except TypeError:
            iterator = [cases]
        for c in iterator:
            cl = case2cluster.get(str(c).strip(), None)
            if cl:
                out.setdefault(cl, []).append(
                    f"{label}@md={row.get('merge_distance')},mbs={row.get('min_batch_size')}")
    return {k: "; ".join(sorted(set(v))) for k, v in out.items()}

def overlap_rate(g):
    """Fraction of case pairs whose [start, complete] intervals overlap."""
    iv = list(zip(g["start_ts"], g["complete_ts"]))
    pairs = hits = 0
    for (s1, e1), (s2, e2) in itertools.combinations(iv, 2):
        if pd.isna(s1) or pd.isna(s2):
            continue
        pairs += 1
        hits += int(s1 < e2 and s2 < e1)  # strict overlap; adjacency is not overlap
    return hits / pairs if pairs else np.nan

def adjacency_rate(g):
    """Fraction of consecutive (by start) pairs with next.start == prev.complete."""
    g = g.sort_values("start_ts")
    nxt = g["start_ts"].iloc[1:].to_numpy()
    prev_end = g["complete_ts"].iloc[:-1].to_numpy()
    return float(np.mean(nxt == prev_end)) if len(g) > 1 else np.nan

        "zero_seconds_share": float(((g["start_ts"].dt.second == 0)

    })

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

**Resource IDs trusted vs treated as shared accounts.** If every resource ID is trusted as one worker: G5 shows one analyst running up to four overlapping analyses (utilisation >100%, extreme multitasking), G2 shows one inspector holding four overlapping inspections, and G1 shows one “resource” doing four labels at once (only credible because RBT-7 is a machine). If IDs are treated as potentially shared: G5’s overlap is fully explained by up to three analysts under `analyst_pool` and single-worker utilisation becomes unidentified (bounded between overlap-based minimum staffing and the observed pool); G2 remains a single named tablet user (T-B), so its common start is better explained by the group-start UI than by sharing; G6’s T-F bulk entry could have been typed by one person for work done by others, further weakening any person-level claim. Conclusions that survive both assumptions: G1 batch (machine capacity), G4 serial (no overlap to explain away), G6 logging artifact (zero durations under any identity assumption). Conclusions that flip: all person-level utilisation, multitasking, and bottleneck claims for G5, and any conformance check that assumes one active case per resource.

**Timestamps exact vs interval-censored by stated precision.** Assumption A (labelled): timestamps are exact as displayed. Assumption B: each timestamp is only known within its documented precision (1 sec, 1 min, or 15 min), with G7 additionally subject to an undocumented rounding direction. Under B: G1 (1 sec) is essentially unchanged — simultaneity survives. G4/G8 adjacency at 1-min displayed lifecycle fields vs 1-sec recorded times remains order-stable. G2/G3/G5 (1 min) gain ±1 min uncertainty: small gaps/overlaps near boundaries are not decisive, but the structural patterns (one start/many ends; many starts/one end) are far larger than 1 min and survive. G7 collapses: every start is only known within a 15-min window and every completion likewise, so “simultaneous batch”, “serial work inside 14:00–14:15”, and “pure coarsening of unrelated work” are observationally equivalent. G6 is *not* rescued by interval censoring at 1-min precision: censoring explains ±1 min, not seven zero-duration rows pinned to a single 17:00/17:01 entry session hours after arrival.

**Three cases where one temporal pattern supports multiple physical explanations:**

1. **G2 — common start, staggered ends.** (a) Logging batch: one “begin group” press timestamps all four items, then the inspector works serially — supported by the UI context and by completions spaced 6–7 min apart, compatible with serial attention. (b) True concurrent inspection: four items genuinely under inspection from 10:00 with unequal durations. (c) Hybrid: common physical setup, then serial finishing. Timestamps alone cannot separate (a)–(c); the completion spacing weakly favours serial finishing after a group start.
2. **G5 — staggered overlapping intervals.** (a) Three analysts working concurrently under one login; (b) one analyst multitasking/interleaving four analyses; (c) a mix. The overlap graph is identical in all three; only badge identity or pause/resume events can separate them. Any per-person performance claim is therefore a hypothesis, not an observation.
3. **G7 — identical 14:00–14:15 for five cases.** (a) A genuine 15-min simultaneous batch; (b) serial work inside one quarter-hour, rounded to the same boundaries; (c) unrelated work-times coarsened onto the grid. The finer `recorded_at` sequence (14:16–14:20, one per row) proves the *records* were created separately, which undermines a single bulk entry but says nothing about when the *work* happened.
4. **G1 — identical start and end (supporting case).** Even here, “four items processed simultaneously on one fixture” and “one fixture cycle logged once per item” are the same log pattern; the fixture-capacity context, PLC precision, and per-row `recorded_at` seconds are what elevate the physical-batch reading — illustrating that the extra-logical evidence, not the timestamps, does the adjudicating.

**Sensitivity summary:** shrinking `merge_distance` toward 0 should leave G1 simultaneous and G4/G8 sequential, leave G2/G3 start/end-batching intact (their equality is exact, not tolerance-driven), but must not be applied below 15 min to G7 or interpreted physically for G6. Raising `min_batch_size` to 4 drops genuine small patterns (none here below size 4 except none) — all clusters have n  4, so the present design is not size-sensitive, a limitation to state rather than hide.

# 6. Data-collection redesign

Priority order reflects how much inferential damage each fix removes, per the clusters above.

| Priority | Recommendation | Failure it prevents (clusters) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Record execution `start`/`complete` at the point of work, separately from `recorded_at`/submission time; forbid form submission from overwriting lifecycle fields; add an explicit `submitted_at` and `entry_mode` (live vs retrospective bulk) | G6 zero-duration bulk entry; G3 submission masquerading as completion |
| 2 | Add a group/batch identifier (`batch_id`, `fixture_id`, group-start event ID) plus batch role (setup, member, completion) so operational batches are declared, not inferred from timestamp equality | G1 vs G2 ambiguity; PM4Py labels becoming the only batch evidence |
| 3 | Retain badge-level resource identity alongside any shared login (`login_id` + `badge_id`), and record pool size / staffing count per shift for pooled resources | G5 shared-account artifact; impossible >100% individual utilisation |
| 4 | Record workstation capacity and parallelism model (fixture capacity, servers, whether multitasking is permitted) as master data joined to the log | Misreading machine batches (G1) as human multitasking and vice versa |
| 5 | Synchronise source clocks (NTP), store UTC offset and clock-source per timestamp, and log cross-system offset estimates where systems interact | G8 safety-clock vs queue-clock ambiguity in attributing the 15:00–15:38 gap |
| 6 | Emit pause/resume and availability events (downtime, safety checks, breaks) as resource-state intervals in the same log/clock domain | Confusing queue accumulation after capacity loss (G8) with slow service or worker idleness |
| 7 | Store timestamp precision *and rounding rule* (floor/ceil/nearest, grid size) as per-field metadata; never round lifecycle fields coarser than the analysis requires; keep raw unrounded values | G7 quarter-hour snapping with undocumented rule destroying duration variance |
| 8 | Preserve insertion order (`sequence_no`), per-row `recorded_at` at full precision, and an audit trail for edits/backfills, so retrospective entry is detectable by construction | Undetectable end-of-shift reconstruction (G6); loss of the G7-style recorded-at counter-evidence |

# 7. Final adjudication

**Interpretable with high confidence (use, with the stated modelling correction):**
- **G1** — genuine simultaneous batch on a capacity-4 automated fixture (confidence 90). Model as one batch cycle, not four independent services.
- **G4** — clean sequential single-server processing (confidence 92). Service, waiting, and utilisation are usable as the reference pattern.
- **G6** — high confidence (95) in the *negative* conclusion: the lifecycle fields are bulk-entry artifacts. It is interpretable only as “work arrived during the afternoon and was recorded at 17:01”; all duration-based quantities must be excluded.

**Exclude or interval-censor in queue analysis:**
- **G6** — exclude from waiting/service/utilisation estimation entirely; at most, bound true completion within (arrival, 17:01]. Including it mechanically produces inflated waiting with zero service and corrupts any queueing or bottleneck model.
- **G7** — interval-censor start and completion to their 15-min rounding windows; exclude from any analysis requiring finer resolution, simultaneity testing, or duration variance.
- **G3** — treat the common 11:30 end as submission-censored (true completion in (start, 11:30]); waiting-to-start may be retained cautiously, service times and completion throughput must not be used at face value.
- **G2** — treat item-level starts as group-censored: item service times are upper bounds contaminated by within-group queueing; pool-level (group) analysis only.

**Require operational validation before any performance conclusion:**
- **G5** — no person-level conclusion (multitasking, utilisation, bottleneck) is defensible until badge identity or staffing counts are recovered; pool-level concurrency is plausible but unattributable.
- **G8** — the queue-accumulation-after-capacity-loss reading (confidence 70) depends on aligning the safety-system clock (15:00–15:35) with the scanner/queue clock (first start 15:38); validate clock offset and confirm the safety check actually removed T-H capacity before attributing the 38-min initial wait to downtime rather than to scheduling or staffing.
- **G2 (secondary)** — confirm with T-B whether post-“begin group” work is serial or genuinely parallel before converting the staggered completions into individual service times.

**Overall adjudication principle:** PM4Py batch categories correctly detect *where timestamps coincide, abut, or overlap*; they are silent on *why*. In this evidence set, the decisive adjudicators were non-PM4Py facts — source and precision (PLC/scanner vs bulk form vs 15-min MES), `recorded_at` behaviour, resource-identity semantics, declared capacities, and UI/downtime context. Any performance conclusion that skips those diagnostics risks reporting logging behaviour as physical behaviour — most extremely in G6, where the log simultaneously fabricates an all-afternoon queue and instantaneous service.
