# 1. Model scope and state variables

Use a calendar-time, discrete-event simulation of the municipal permit portfolio. The unit of demand is an intended application. Record when the applicant starts preparing, submits, receives a first decision, and reaches final disposition after any appeal. Keep the official submission-to-decision clock and the applicant's end-to-end clock. Otherwise a checker that moves five days of work before submission can look like a five-day improvement. Applications already in progress at a policy cutover keep their age, completed work and pending appointments. Apply each new rule at its specified effective date and stage.

| Entity | State needed to make the next decision |
|---|---|
| Application | ID, type, district, architect, related project, value, indicators of required reviews, verified violation status or unknown, urgency, submission cohort and applicable policy version. Record stage, current document version, review results and their validity, outstanding prerequisites, deadlines, queue-entry times, appointments, rework history and outcome. |
| Applicant, architect and neighborhood | Document readiness, response timers, architect submission batches, linked projects, and a shared neighborhood/notice-period condition that can extend several notices. Keep observed attributes distinct from imputed ones. |
| Work item and resource | One work item per required task and version, with release time, skill requirement, priority, remaining active work, assigned person and interruptions. Track each pool's queue, roster, on-duty/busy status, next opening, and future bookings. Inspectors also have a location and a visit itinerary. |
| System | Local date and time, holiday and council calendars, demand regime, future-event list, open inventory by stage and age, and audit events for releases, starts, pauses, completions, waivers and decisions. |

After intake, a clerk can request documents and release the case to an applicant timer. Receipt enables a fresh completeness check. On clearance, fork Planning, Fire and Environmental work for the reviews that apply. Allow a review to wait for a planning input when that dependency is documented. Join the valid results before the subsequent notice/inspection sequence in the supplied model. Notice is a minimum-duration timer with possible extensions. Scheduling books a field visit on a feasible itinerary. Drafting requires valid results from every applicable review, notice and inspection, or a recorded lawful waiver. An optional final legal check can return the draft. A planning correction or failed inspection reopens only affected work, invalidates stale results and rechecks the join. A decision can open an appeal linked to the original case. Log both decisions without counting a second arrival. The tree gives the core control flow. Reasons, version invalidation, appeals and cross-case events require additional state.

Use skill-specific ready queues. The initial dispatch rule is oldest eligible first within documented urgency classes, with appointments honored and ties broken reproducibly. Five clerks work weekdays 08:00–16:00, with four available for permit work on Monday. Eight planners work weekdays 09:00–17:00, two restricted to Simple renovation. Planning and drafting draw on the same eligible people. Fire capacity is stated as two FTE equivalent with work on Tuesday–Thursday. Reconcile actual hours and simultaneous staff with the roster. Three environmental reviewers work weekdays with 40% reserved for other duties. Four inspectors do fieldwork Tuesday–Friday. Both travel and failed visits consume field slots. Two legal officers have council-week closures with an urgent exception. Use the responsible roster for scheduling and human decisions, confirming shared duties. Portal and automated system events have effectively unlimited parallel capacity. Staffed work crossing a shift boundary resumes in available time. Book non-interruptible visits only in slots that accommodate travel and work.

Generate related submissions together, let neighborhood events affect overlapping notices, and let eligible related cases share an inspection trip. The first eligible case can wait for a companion. Travel consumes inspector time once per actual route, and each site still consumes visit time. All applications also interact through finite queues, including cases with no architect or neighborhood link.

# 2. Parameter-estimation plan

**Establish the accounting units first.** Reconcile arrivals, tasks actually worked, first/final decisions and opening/closing inventory over the same dates. As a feasibility check, 31,200 cases in about 78 weeks is about 400 per week. If a comparable volume receives Planning review each week and its 3.5-hour median denotes individual active work, at least half those reviews take 3.5 hours or more. That alone requires about 700 planner-hours per week against 320 scheduled, before drafting. Check whether times include elapsed holds, reviews are shared across cases, staffing or case coverage differs, or the backlog really grew. Resolve this with rosters, task counts and sampled work records. If the mismatch persists, retain alternate capacity/work interpretations and report conditional projections. A fit to cycle times alone cannot resolve it.

1. **Prepare the log and model.** Normalize IDs, time zones, activity names and lifecycle transitions. Pair starts and completions by activity instance, including repeats. Audit duplicates, ties, reopened IDs, extraction boundaries and missing events. Preserve architect/district links and separate appointment, project and council records where available. In PM4Py, format the event frame, extract variants, frequency and performance directly-follows graphs (DFGs), and convert the parsed tree to a Petri net for token replay or alignments. Use trace deviations to investigate alternate routes and recording gaps. Completion order among parallel reviews provides neither a precedence rule nor a service duration.
2. **Estimate intended demand and submissions.** Use portal submissions as the baseline observable. Estimate counts by local week, weekday and hour with exposure offsets, a seasonal term, holidays and a trend/change point where justified. Fit a marked cluster model with parent submissions/batches, architect-specific batch sizes, related districts/types and within-week offsets. Allow residual weekly overdispersion. Fit parent intensity and batch size jointly so their product yields total demand. Calibrate the March–May and September–November to January–February ratio of 1.7, Monday to Wednesday ratio of 2.2, top-20 architect share of 31%, and the 45/28/17/10% type mix. Check their joint patterns and estimate the other months from records. Intake intentions before submission, non-submitters and voluntary appointment uptake require pilot data or scenarios.
3. **Separate service, queues and external holds.** Use paired timestamps and work segments to estimate active minutes conditional on activity, type, complexity and repeat number. With only start/end, subtract known off-shift periods and investigate interruptions before treating the residual as effort. Use partial pooling for sparse groups. A lognormal starting fit from median \(m\) and p90 \(q\) has \(\mu=\log m\) and \(\sigma=\log(q/m)/1.2816\) in consistent units. Compare gamma, mixture and empirical fits on the observed tail. Keep correlated task difficulty within a case. Fit site work separately from district travel, and environmental staff effort separately from waiting for a consultant. Fit request-to-receipt in calendar time, including pending requests as censored. A zero-service receipt releases work instantly even after a long response delay.
4. **Identify waiting.** Derive enablement from the last applicable prerequisite, legal release or external response. Compare enablement-to-start, time to next roster opening, ready-but-unassigned time and appointment lead time. Compare simulated waits with the reported medians, including six days for Planning and eight before Site inspection. Queue waits arise from dispatch, capacity and the release process. Sampling the observed wait again on top of a simulated queue would double count congestion. For completion-only events infer latent starts jointly with rosters, workload and audited paired cases. Carry a range of service/wait splits where these constraints are weak. Analyze missing starts by pool and case difficulty to test selection bias.
5. **Estimate routes, loops and shared events.** Apply known requirements first. Estimate remaining routes from conditional counts or hierarchical regressions on type, value, district, flags, date and history. Use permit files to estimate missing area/sensitivity and retain a residual probability of a required review even with a negative flag. Estimate planning, inspection and legal returns, document loops, appeal probability, return destination and repeat effort from version histories, repeated activities and a manually coded sample. An apparent repeat could be duplicate logging. Model repeat risk conditional on previous failures. Fit notice as a statutory floor plus administrative/calendar effects and a latent extension mixture. The observed long duration, including the 60-day summary, is evidence about total elapsed time. It cannot identify objection incidence and objection delay separately. Use sampled objection registers or vary both quantities and the degree of neighborhood correlation. Estimate bundling eligibility, trip time savings, no-shows and applicant readiness from appointment/route records. Architect plus district is evidence for a link, with uncertain precision.
6. **Estimate capacity and dispatch.** Reconcile HR shifts, leave, holidays, meetings, other duties and skills against observed throughput, appointments, busy-time samples and out-of-hours postings. Estimate utilization on permit-available hours. Distinguish the number of people available at a particular hour from weekly FTE totals. Use observed order among simultaneously eligible jobs to infer priorities. Calibrate on several targets together, including WIP (work in progress), queue ages, visit counts and decision throughput. Version input estimates by date and policy regime.

# 3. Assumptions

| Topic | Working assumption and check |
|---|---|
| Arrivals | The central forecast preserves the estimated number of intended applications and their joint attribute distribution. D1 and D7 can shift the time of actual portal submission. Test abandonment, displacement into later weeks and unequal uptake separately. |
| Service times | Active effort accrues only while the required worker is serving the case. Complexity can lengthen several tasks in one case. Test alternative tails and shared work across files, especially for Planning. |
| Routing | Positive flags and verified statutory requirements override estimated branch probabilities. Complex variance always receives notice. Unknown eligibility for the D2 waiver goes to the ordinary inspection path. Confirm legal requirements and effective-date rules with the municipality. |
| Resources/calendars | Baseline uses actual local rosters and eligible skills. Represent reserved environmental time as blocks when known. Urgent legal exceptions use real officer capacity. Baseline overtime, weekend work and inspector batching need evidence. Treat remaining calendar ambiguities as alternatives. |
| Rework | A return has a reason category or unknown, a destination, and a set of invalidated results. Resubmission, failed visit and appeal have different delays and repeat risks. An unresolved case stays open at the horizon. |
| Applicant/third-party response | Missing documents, site readiness and consultant reports use elapsed-time distributions outside municipal staff pools. D1 may add preparation effort even for a case that would originally have passed. The notice floor is 14 days under the stated baseline. Apply the verified legal counting rule for weekends/holidays and the actual meeting date for a required council action. |
| Cross-case dependencies | Architect batches, shared neighborhood effects and shared travel each have a distinct mechanism. Use project IDs where available and vary linkage error, cluster size and shared-event strength where flattened records obscure them. |
| Non-stationary demand | The fitted seasonal and weekday pattern recurs in the central projection, conditional on a stated trend and policy regime. Eighteen months give limited replication of a particular season. Include sustained growth, peak shocks and uncertain case mix. |
| Missing data | Missing starts, objections, violations and rework reasons remain unknown until supported by another source. Propagate alternative imputations and missingness mechanisms. Reintroduce omitted emergency workload as a sensitivity range, including its priority and council-week impact. |

# 5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment

- **Initialization.** For a dated rollout, start B and every alternative from the same reconstructed snapshot immediately before rollout. Include case ages, work in service, residual applicant/notice delays conditional on time already elapsed, inspector bookings and unfinished appeals. Reconcile starting WIP with the inventory register. Measure transition effects immediately. For recurring annual comparisons without a reliable snapshot, run preceding whole seasonal cycles under B, then switch policy on the same calendar date. Try one, two and three years, comparing stage WIP and age quantiles at the same week of the year from empty and busy starts. Discard the burn-in only when initialization effects are small relative to the policy differences of interest. A growing year-over-year backlog calls for finite-horizon analysis from a stated inventory, with the growth rate reported.
- **Horizon and cohorts.** Measure two full calendar years including two peak seasons of each kind. Use fixed calendar windows for resource costs, completions and WIP. Use entry cohorts for durations and SLA compliance, tagging carry-in separately. Follow those cohorts for at least another year with continuing arrivals and competition. Extend follow-up if needed. At a cutoff, right-censor open cases, show numbers at risk and identify quantiles that the follow-up cannot estimate. Track both first decision and final resolution.
- **Monte Carlo error.** Start with 50 independent paired replications per policy–stress comparison. Keep the master seed, calendar, policy settings, initial snapshot and input-model version. For a given replication share intended applicants, latent difficulty, potential task efforts, potential responses, neighborhood shocks and absences across arms. Key draws by case or cluster, task and occurrence so skipping a visit does not shift the next case's draws. Different replications use independent streams. Compute a p90 per replication and a paired policy-minus-B difference, or bootstrap whole paired replications for an explicitly pooled cohort estimand. Report the estimand, effect in days or percentage points and a 95% interval. Use fixed additional batches of replications until, for example, the p90 difference has half-width at most two days and the SLA difference one percentage point, or report the attained precision at the simulation budget. Use simultaneous intervals or fresh confirmatory seeds for shortlisted comparisons.
- **Input and structural uncertainty.** Outside the replication loop, draw parameter sets from a hierarchical posterior or refits to blocks of weeks with architect/project clusters preserved. Keep fitted parameters common to competing arms within a draw. Distinguish stochastic simulation intervals conditional on a fit from intervals across plausible fits. Elicit ranges for causal intervention effects and take-up. Vary missing-start splits, calendar capacity, linkage errors, required-review imputations, travel, repeat probabilities, shared objections, trend and quality risks jointly. Report break-even values such as the additional hold at which D3 loses its p90 advantage. A stress scenario with no known probability gets a separate result.
- **Heavy tails.** Retain long observations, distinguish open from completed cases and fit survival/mixture models to response, objection and appeal delays. Check upper quantiles against alternative tails and calendar shocks. Report p90/p95, the share still open at fixed ages and restricted mean time to a common horizon when the far tail is weakly identified. Use replication or block intervals that preserve clustering. Avoid precision claims for small district/type tails.

| Out-of-sample prediction | For example, use months 1–12 to fit, 13–15 to tune, then freeze and predict months 16–18. Use earlier rolling origins to cover a peak and a trough. Agree tolerances before looking at holdout results, for example ±10% on aggregate monthly throughput and median duration and ±15% on well-populated p90s, together with observed peak backlog inside predictive bands and no statutory-rule violations. Diagnose errors by stage and cohort. Baseline validation cannot establish the causal effect of a new checker or triage policy. Use a phased pilot or audited trial to measure uptake, actual work saved, displacement, safety and applicant effort before scaling.

# 7. Decision criteria

Use the following as proposed decision thresholds pending municipal sign-off. Compute effects against the matched B arm with uncertainty intervals. Report approvals, rejections, unresolved cases and abandonment by cohort so a faster rejection or a shifted submission date cannot silently improve the score.

| Criterion | Measure and proposed gate |
|---|---|
| Timeliness and SLA | Show median, p90 and p95 calendar days from submission to first decision and final resolution, plus intention to disposition. Seek at least a 10% reduction in overall p90, with no increase in overall median and no type's p90 worsening by more than 5%. Illustrative first-decision SLA targets are 30/90/120/240 days for Simple/New residential/Commercial/Complex. Target 90% on time within each type and flag any drop over 2 percentage points versus B. Replace these clocks and targets with adopted rules, including lawful clock stops. |
| District and architect fairness | Publish raw and case-mix-standardized waits, p90s and on-time shares by district, top-20 versus other architects, and low-volume submitters. Use the same type/value/requirements mix for standardization. Limit any widening of a comparable group's on-time gap to 2 percentage points. Report intervals and effective group size. A sparse subgroup with large uncertainty requires more evidence. |
| Capacity and backlog | Report busy/permit-available hours by skill and week, visits and travel hours, overtime, cost, peak and year-end WIP, and oldest-case age. Flag utilization above 90% for four consecutive staffed weeks and seek a return to pre-peak age-adjusted backlog within eight weeks. Require no sustained growth across equivalent annual dates under the central demand forecast. |
| Rework, burden and quality | Measure loops and repeat work-hours per 100 intended cases, failed visits, appeals and reversals, errors on audited waived/triaged cases, and applicant hours including pre-submission. Require zero simulated prohibited waivers or early notice closures. Agree a harm margin before a pilot, for example an upper 95% bound on excess material errors below 1 percentage point. Simulation alone cannot clear this gate where outcomes are missing. |
| Cost and robustness | Include checker operation, triage, alerts, verification, reserved/temporary staff, travel savings and applicant time. Compare incremental cost per additional on-time case and show cost versus tail improvement for alternatives within budget. Prefer a policy meeting gates in at least 90% of plausible input draws, with the direction of its p90 benefit retained in at least three of four named stresses. Show the size of the worst loss and retain statutory and safety constraints in every stress. |

Use paired intervals to distinguish a material improvement from simulation noise. Where several feasible policies overlap, show the cost, fairness and tail-duration trade-offs. Where key gates are unresolved, the decision can be a targeted pilot or retention of current practice. Policy ranking awaits calibration, validation and evidence about intervention effects.

# 8. Python skeleton

The first block loads the actual excerpt from the question file. It assumes offset-free local timestamps on input and takes the municipal time zone as an argument. The repository path can be replaced with a CSV export and `pd.read_csv` in a production loader. This block uses the PM4Py 2.x high-level API.

```python
from io import StringIO
from pathlib import Path

import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CID, ACT, END = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
START, READY = "start_timestamp", "enabled_at"
TREE = """->(
 'Submit application',
 *(->('Completeness check',X(tau,->('Request missing documents',
 'Receive missing documents'))),'Completeness rework'),
 +('Planning review',X(tau,'Fire-safety review'),X(tau,'Environmental review')),
 X(tau,'Public-notice period'),
 *(->('Inspection scheduling','Site inspection'),'Inspection rework'),
 'Decision drafting',X(tau,'Legal check'),X('Issue permit','Reject application'))"""


def load_excerpt():
    path = Path("questions/cat08_06_digital_twin_experiments.txt")
    body = path.read_text().split("Small illustrative event-log excerpt:", 1)[1]
    return pd.read_csv(StringIO(body.strip().split("\n\n", 1)[0]))


def describe(raw, local_tz):
    # Input row = one activity instance, with optional paired start and release.
    df = raw.copy()
    for col in (END, START, READY):
        if col in df:
            local = pd.to_datetime(df[col], errors="raise").dt.tz_localize(
                local_tz, ambiguous="raise", nonexistent="raise"
            )
            df[col] = local.dt.tz_convert("UTC")
        else:
            df[col] = pd.Series(pd.NaT, index=df.index, dtype="datetime64[ns, UTC]")
    elapsed = (df[END] - df[START]).dt.total_seconds()
    wait = (df[START] - df[READY]).dt.total_seconds()
    df["timing_order_error"] = elapsed.lt(0) | wait.lt(0)
    df["service_elapsed_s"] = elapsed.where(elapsed.ge(0))  # includes any pauses
    df["wait_to_start_s"] = wait.where(wait.ge(0))
    # active_s is the sum of audited busy segments, if available.
    df["service_active_s"] = df.get("active_s", float("nan"))

    # Completion projection gives comparable DFGs even with uneven start coverage.
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(df[[CID, ACT, END]].copy(), case_id=CID,
                                 activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=END)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants_as_tuples(log)
    dfg, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)
    submitted = df.loc[df[ACT].eq("Submit application")].groupby(CID)[END].min()
    arrival_gap_s = submitted.sort_values().diff().dt.total_seconds().mean()
    tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(" ".join(TREE.split()))
    net, initial, final = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)
    return df, dict(variants=variants, dfg=dfg, starts=starts, ends=ends,
                    performance_dfg=perf_dfg, case_arrival_average_s=arrival_gap_s,
                    replay_model=(net, initial, final))

# Example: timed_rows, observed = describe(load_excerpt(), local_tz="UTC")
# UTC here is an illustrative clock convention for the tiny excerpt.
```

For the two example cases the mean gap between submissions is 33 h 9 min. Each has a different variant, and scheduling directly precedes site inspection twice. Both timing columns inferred from starts are missing throughout. A performance DFG edge here measures elapsed time between consecutive recorded completions. For the full log, obtain `enabled_at` from verified releases or reconstruction of applicable prerequisites. Keep a provenance field for inferred times. In the parsed tree `+` is parallel, `X` is choice, `*` has a do and redo branch, and `tau` is silent. A visible abstract rework label may lack a same-named event in the log. Record the mapping used in replay.

The second block defines the boundary of a separate discrete-event engine. `fit`, `roster`, `study` and `make_engine` are injected components. `fit` supplies calibrated conditional distributions and a latent requirements draw per case. `roster` supplies named workers, skills and dated permit-available intervals. The engine implements the event transitions in section 1, logs actual service segments, and releases a resource whenever work pauses. Policy settings come from the experiment matrix. Expand D10 to the switches D1, D2, D3 and D4.

```python
import numpy as np


def random_sources(master_seed, replication):
    # Integer keys are stable across arms, including task and occurrence IDs.
    def source(family, *keys):
        seed = np.random.SeedSequence([master_seed, replication, family, *keys])
        return np.random.default_rng(seed)
    return source


def generate_intentions(mondays, fit, source):
    for w, monday in enumerate(mondays):
        rng = source(1, w)
        rate = fit.mean_parent_count(monday) * fit.week_multiplier(monday, rng)
        for batch in range(rng.poisson(rate)):
            # A parent can generate a singleton. Children share an architect and
            # project context. Each carries a time, district, type and value.
            children = fit.draw_related_applications(monday, source(2, w, batch))
            for child_index, intention in enumerate(children):
                yield (w, batch, child_index), intention


def required_results(app, hidden, switches):
    # app flags are normalized booleans. hidden is drawn once per case.
    tasks = {"Planning review"}
    if app.requires_fire or hidden.area_requires_fire:
        tasks.add("Fire-safety review")
    if app.requires_environment or hidden.sensitive_district:
        tasks.add("Environmental review")
    if app.case_type == "Complex variance" or app.requires_public_notice or hidden.notice:
        tasks.add("Public-notice period")
    waive = ("D2" in switches and app.case_type == "Simple renovation"
             and app.value_eur is not None and app.value_eur < 50_000
             and app.violation_status == "verified_clean")
    if not waive:
        tasks.add("Site inspection")  # requires scheduling and a booked field slot
    return tasks, waive


def drafting_ready(needed_versions, completed_versions):
    # Keys in needed_versions are precisely the results required on this route.
    return all(completed_versions.get(task) == version
               for task, version in needed_versions.items())


def install_resources(engine, roster, calendar, arm, stress):
    shifts = roster.permit_shifts(calendar, stress,
                                  peak_inspector=("D4" in arm.switches))
    for worker in shifts:
        # Intervals already exclude other duties, leave and routine closures.
        # Skill tags can restrict task, case type and urgency per interval.
        engine.add_worker(worker.id, worker.skill_intervals)
    engine.set_dispatch(priority=arm.priority, aging_rule=arm.aging_rule,
                        bundle_limit=arm.bundle_limit, max_hold=arm.max_hold)


def run_experiments(make_engine, fit_for, roster, study, arms, stresses, reps=50):
    records = []
    for stress in stresses:
        fit = fit_for(stress)  # same fitted parameter draw throughout this comparison
        for rep in range(reps):
            source = random_sources(study.master_seed, rep)
            for arm in arms:
                engine = make_engine(calendar=study.calendar, fit=fit, policy=arm,
                                     random_source=source, route=required_results,
                                     draft_join=drafting_ready)
                install_resources(engine, roster, study.calendar, arm, stress)
                engine.restore(study.opening_snapshot)  # fresh state for every arm
                for case_key, intention in generate_intentions(study.mondays, fit, source):
                    engine.schedule_intention(case_key, intention)
                # Queue processes run in event-time order. D1/D7 act before intake.
                # Shared shocks use neighborhood and period keys. Repeated work uses
                # case, task and repeat keys. Visits reserve actual route time.
                engine.run(until=study.followup_end)
                measures = engine.metrics(entry_window=study.entry_window,
                                          accounting_window=study.accounting_window)
                records.append(dict(stress=stress.name, arm=arm.name,
                                    replication=rep, **measures))
    return pd.DataFrame(records)
```

`study.mondays` covers arrival generation through follow-up. For example, a positive extension draw for a neighborhood can schedule one event affecting every open notice in that neighborhood's specified exposure window. At a visit release the dispatcher can hold a compatible job until the batch limit or the oldest job's deadline, then reserve travel and visit intervals for a single inspector. These state changes affect later cases through the event queue. The simulation exports its own event records to the same PM4Py analysis pipeline for comparison with the observed log.
