## 1. Interaction-graph description

**Node types.** Job (J1–J6), Component (C1–C6), ComponentLot (L7, L8), Machine (CNC-2, OVEN-1, TEST-1), Tool (FIX-A, RACK-7, CAL-3), Batch (B1, B2), Shipment (S1–S3). Events are best treated as hyperedges. For example, the 12:05 `Batch Cure` = {B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1–J3, C1–C3}. The interaction graph is the pairwise projection of these hyperedges.

**Edge types.**
- *Observed O2O:* `requires` (Job→Component), `contains` (Lot→Component), `batches` (Batch→Job), `consolidates` (Shipment→Job).
- *Observed E2O co-participation:* Batch Cure; `Quarantine/Release Lot` = {L7, C2, C5, J2, J5}; J4 `Machine Process` = {J4, C4, CNC-2, FIX-A}; `Final Test` = {Job, TEST-1, CAL-3}.
- *Inferred:* `holds` (Job→Tool, from acquire/release); `waits-for` (never logged); `state-carryover` (B1→RACK-7→B2); Job–Lot (via requires∘contains⁻¹, or only in the alternative pipeline); Job–Job coupling (co-batched, co-shipped, co-lot, tool-sequenced).

**Higher-order motifs.**
1. *Batch barrier:* {J1, J2, J3}–B1–OVEN-1–RACK-7, an AND-join over three jobs and two resources.
2. *Lot fan-out across batches:* L7–{C2, C5}–{J2, J5}–{B1, B2}. One quality decision reaches both oven batches.
3. *Tool-mediated cross-batch link:* J3–FIX-A–CNC-2–J4, with J3∈B1 and J4∈B2.
4. *Resource-state carryover:* B1–RACK-7 (cleaning/quarantine)–B2–OVEN-1.
5. *Misalignment cycle:* J1–B1–J3–FIX-A–J4–S1–J1. One delay at J3 can reach J1 twice: through B1, and through J4 via S1.
6. *Tool–test–shipment:* CAL-3–TEST-1–{J5, J6}–S3.

All six jobs form **one connected component** (via B1, FIX-A, B2, S3/CAL-3, L7, L8). J6 has no batch relation: either it needs no cure or the relation is missing.

```
L7 -contains-> C2,C5 ; L8 -contains-> C1,C3            [O]

L7 -quarantine/release-> J2, J5                        [A]
J2 -override-into-> B1                                 [O, outside OCEL]
L8 -> J1, J3                                           [I, no lot events]
```

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

All figures are counterfactual accounting under the stated assumption, not causal proof.

**(1) B1 synchronization.** J1 waited 08:10→12:05 = 235 min, J2 08:25→12:05 = 220 min, and J3 10 min, for 465 min in total.
- *Assumption A:* OVEN-1 and RACK-7 were free from 08:25, so {J1, J2} could have cured then and J3 could have gone later. J1 would wait 15 min and J2 0, so about **440 min** is avoidable.
- *Assumption B (quarantine respected):* J2 was ineligible from 10:20 unless overridden. Only 08:25→10:20 = **115 min** of J2's wait is then synchronization; the remaining 105 min overlaps a policy hold.
- Oven and rack availability before 12:05 is not recorded.

**(2) B2 is overdetermined.** J4 waited 13:10→14:50 = 100 min and J5 10 min, while OVEN-1 was idle for 115 min. RACK-7 became free at 14:35 and J5 ready at 14:40. Assuming about 10 min of loading:
- removing only the rack constraint → start about 14:50, saving 0 min;
- removing only J5's lateness → start about 14:45, saving 5 min;
- removing both → start about 13:20, saving **90 min**.

Attributing the delay to either factor alone fails, because it comes from the two together.

**(3) FIX-A and J4.** J4 arrived at CNC-2 at 09:50 and started at 11:45, a 115-min wait.
- *Upper bound:* if FIX-A was the only missing prerequisite and the 5-min changeover is intrinsic, J4 could have started at 09:55, so up to **110 min** is fixture-associated.
- *Lower bound:* if CNC-2 was legitimately busy outside its 10:05–10:47 idle window, only that **42 min** window was usable.

J3 held FIX-A for 160 min; whether it actually used the fixture throughout is not recorded.

**(4) S1 imposed on J1.** J1 was packed at 13:30 and S1 dispatched at 19:25, a 355-min wait.
- If J1 had shipped alone with J4's 15-min pack-to-dispatch time, it would have left at 13:45, so consolidation imposed **340 min**.
- Using the job-DFG median (66 min) instead gives **289 min**.

**(5) CAL-3 and J6.** J6 was ready at 16:05 and tested at 17:25, an 80-min wait. Without the recalibration (or with a second calibrated standard), J6 would have started at 16:35 after 30 min of ordinary queueing behind J5. That leaves about **50 min** CAL-3-associated: 45 min of recalibration plus 5 min of handover. TEST-1 was idle for those 50 min. If recalibration is mandatory every N tests, this time is displaced rather than removed.

**(6) Consolidation hidden by the median.** Pack→Dispatch has six values with a median of 66 min. Four are known: 15 min (J4), 25 min (last S2 job), 35 min (last S3 job) and 355 min (J1). For the median (the mean of the 3rd and 4th values) to be 66, the smaller of the two early-packed S2/S3 jobs must have waited 2×66−35 = **97 min**, and the other at least that. So three of six jobs waited more than 1.5 h.

**(7) J1's exposure.** From readiness (08:10) to dispatch (19:25) is 675 min. Of that, 235 + 355 = 590 min, or **87%**, was spent waiting on other objects.

**(8) Outlier screen.** With n = 6, the largest attainable sample z-score is (n−1)/√n = **2.04**. "No job above 1.8 SD" therefore excludes almost nothing.

**(9) Gap contradictions.** OVEN-1 was idle 115 min against a reported 73-min maximum gap, and TEST-1 50 min against 39. Auxiliary events (rack handling, `Calibrate`) probably fill the gaps, so machine gaps understate idle time during which jobs waited. Separately, L7's 210-min hold (10:20→13:50) upper-bounds J5's lot delay.

**Alternative ranking (dispatch-critical path).** J1's B1 wait is absorbed by S1, because its partner J4 was packed only at 19:10. J2's B1 wait is absorbed by S2, because its partner J3 cured in the same batch. B1 therefore probably changed dispatch times by close to zero. Dispatch is set by the *last partner*:
- S1: J4, delayed by FIX-A, by the B2 rack/J5/L7 conjunction, and by an unexplained 260 min from 14:50 to 19:10;
- S3: probably J6, delayed by CAL-3.

For delivery, the order becomes: consolidation amplifying tool contention, then lot coupling, then batch synchronization. The main ranking measures WIP waiting instead.

## 4. What flattening misses

**Job.**
- *Visible:* control flow and rework (2 of 6 jobs).
- *Hidden:* waits for partners, tools and lots. Batch Cure and Final Test are absent from the DFG despite relating to jobs, and lot events were extracted away.
- *Duplicated:* three dispatches become six job-level Dispatch events, and the one Cure event would become three.
- *Misleading:* "Pack→Dispatch 1.1 h" hides J1's 355 min. The edge medians sum to 3.9 h, which cannot be reconciled with J1's 675-min span.

**Machine.**
- *Visible:* utilization and activity sequences.
- *Hidden:* which jobs waited during idle periods, and why.
- *Duplicated:* auxiliary Calibrate or rack events inflate activity.
- *Misleading:* "OVEN-1 at 44%, spare capacity." That capacity could not be used while RACK-7 was quarantined.

**Batch.**
- *Visible:* composition, start and fill.
- *Hidden:* individual readiness, rack state, and later divergence into different shipments.
- *Duplicated:* member attributes are copied per batch.
- *Misleading:* "only 2 batches, so minor," yet B1 imposed 465 job-minutes.

**Tool.**
- *Visible:* hold intervals and recalibration.
- *Hidden:* unmet requests (there is no `Wait for Tool` activity).
- *Duplicated:* J4's Machine Process appears in both the CNC-2 and the FIX-A views.
- *Misleading:* FIX-A "busy 160 min" reads as productive use by J3.

**ComponentLot.**
- *Visible:* the L7 hold on C2 and C5.
- *Hidden:* the J2 override, J5's downstream lag, and L8's latent J1–J3 coupling (L8 has no events).
- *Duplicated:* the alternative pipeline copies each lot event to two jobs.
- *Misleading:* either "the quarantine did not affect B1" (true only because of an unlogged override) or "J2 violated the quarantine" (the override was authorized).

**Shipment.**
- *Visible:* Create→Load 0.8 h and Load→Dispatch 0.3 h.
- *Hidden:* packed jobs waiting for partners.
- *Misleading:* "no shipment bottleneck" while J1 waited 5 h 55 min.

**Low utilization with long waiting.** Jobs need *conjunctions* of resources (oven + rack + batch partners; CNC-2 + FIX-A; TEST-1 + CAL-3). Utilization measures each resource on its own, whereas waiting depends on joint availability. OVEN-1 was idle 115 min while J4 waited, CNC-2 42 min while J4 lacked FIX-A, and TEST-1 50 min while J6 lacked CAL-3. Batching idles machines deliberately, and consolidation waiting happens after machining ends.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**N1 — FIX-A → J3 → B1 → J1/J2 → S1/S2** (Tool, Job, Machine, Batch, Shipment)
- *Temporal:* J3 held FIX-A 09:00–11:40; J3 ready 11:55; Cure 12:05; J1 packed 13:30.
- *Feasibility:* J4's 11:45 process needed FIX-A. B1 needed OVEN-1 and RACK-7.
- *Policy:* B1 membership appears fixed, since the oven launched 10 min after J3 was ready (an inferred fill or recipe rule).
- *Speculation:* J3's fixture hold is a common upstream cause of J4's CNC wait and of B1's late launch. Via J4 and S1, it may also reach J1.

**N2 — L7 → C2/C5 → J2 → B1/RACK-7 → B2 → J4/J5 → S1/S3** (Lot, Component, Job, Batch, Tool, Machine, Shipment)
- *Temporal:* quarantine 10:20; J2 overridden into B1; Cure 12:05; L7 released 13:50; RACK-7 blocked until 14:35; J5 ready 14:40; B2 starts 14:50.
- *Feasibility:* B2 needed both RACK-7 and J5.
- *Policy:* the lot hold blocked J5, the override exempted J2, and a rack-quarantine rule applied.
- *Speculation:* RACK-7's quarantine may have been caused by curing quarantined C2 on it. It follows B1 and ends 45 min after the lot release. If so, the override turned J2's hold into delay for J4, which is not an L7 job, and through S1 into delay for J1. This is unverified.

**N3 — L7 → J5 → B2 → TEST-1/CAL-3 → J6 → S3** (Lot, Job, Batch, Machine, Tool, Shipment)
- *Temporal:* J5 ready for test 16:00 and J6 at 16:05; J5 tested 16:10–16:35; recalibration 16:35–17:20; J6 tested 17:25–17:50; S3 dispatched 35 min after the last pack.
- *Feasibility:* there is one CAL-3, and every Final Test requires it.
- *Policy:* the recalibration trigger is unknown.
- *Speculation:* the lot delay put J5 in the test queue alongside J6, creating the contention. The recalibration then delayed J6, and through S3 also J5, so each job delayed the other via different object types.

**N4 — the misalignment cycle.** J1's 19:25 dispatch is fixed by J4's path, several objects away from J1's own trace, so temporal succession within J1's events points to no cause. J4's 14:50→19:10 span remains unexplained.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

**I1 — Time-bounded partial batching (synchronization).** Launch a batch when the oven and a rack are free and either the batch is full or the oldest ready job has waited ≥60 min.
- *Benefit:* up to about 440 WIP-minutes in a B1-type case.
- *Helped:* J1, J2.
- *Harmed:* J3 (next batch); later batches, via more RACK-7 turnovers.
- *Cost/risk:* energy per job; quality at partial load.
- *Perverse metric:* "average batch wait" invites delayed readiness logging.
- *Evaluation:* shift-level switchback on per-Job readiness→Cure waits (E2O), partner dispatch times (to detect absorption), and energy per Batch.

**I2 — Fixture and rack discipline (tools).** Release fixtures on machine exit or add a second FIX-A, sequence tool-aware (J4 before a long J3 hold), and pool racks or use quick-change cleaning for RACK-7.
- *Benefit:* J4 42–110 min. The rack fix alone saves B2 only 0–5 min unless J5 is also addressed.
- *Helped:* J4, and via S1, J1.
- *Harmed:* J3 if resequenced; other CNC-2 work.
- *Cost/risk:* capital and fixture qualification.
- *Perverse metric:* "tool utilization" rewards long holds.
- *Evaluation:* interrupted time series on wait-for-tool intervals, checking whether waiting shifts to the tool's graph neighbors.

**I3 — CAL-3 recalibration in predicted idle windows (tools).** Use test-queue look-ahead to place recalibration, or add a second standard.
- *Benefit:* about 50 min for J6, and potentially for J5 via S3.
- *Harmed:* jobs arriving during the moved slot.
- *Risk:* testing with a drifting calibration if triggers are stretched.
- *Perverse metric:* "tests per calibration."
- *Evaluation:* before/after test waits for jobs arriving near recalibrations, stratified by last-partner status.

**I4 — Consolidation with a hold cap (shipment policy).** Split a shipment when a partner's predicted pack time is more than 2 h away, and form shipments after batch composition, as S2/B1 already align.
- *Benefit:* J1 289–340 min; early S2/S3 jobs about 60 min or more.
- *Harmed:* freight cost, the dock, and customers receiving partial deliveries.
- *Perverse metric:* "per-job dispatch lead time" encourages splitting everything, while "truck fill" encourages holding.
- *Evaluation:* randomize eligible shipments; measure per-Job pack→dispatch via `consolidates` alongside per-Shipment cost.

**I5 — Lot-aware batch formation.** Propagate lot holds to the batch planner, exclude held components instead of overriding, and reserve a rack for suspect material.
- *Benefit:* if N2 is true, protects B2 (J4, J5).
- *Harmed:* J2, which would wait until at least 13:50.
- *Risk:* informal workarounds.
- *Perverse metric:* "override count."
- *Evaluation:* rack-quarantine incidence after batches with vs. without lot-affected members.

**I6 — Logging redesign (logging only).** Add `Request Tool`/`Wait for Tool`, `Ready for Batch` with source and entry time, rack state changes with reason codes, lot-hold E2O links derived through requires∘contains, imported overrides, and calibration logged as tool maintenance.
- *Benefit:* inferred edges become observed ones.
- *Harmed:* operator time.
- *Risk:* bulk entry.
- *Perverse metric:* "logged waiting minutes" invites under-reporting.
- *Evaluation:* run the old and new pipelines in parallel and compare the share of job time explained by typed waits.

## 7. PM4Py analysis outline

```python
import pandas as pd
import networkx as nx
import pm4py

ACT = {"ready_batch": "Ready for Batch", "cure": "Batch Cure", "acquire": "Acquire Tool",
       "release_tool": "Release Tool", "arrive": "Arrive at Machine", "process": "Machine Process",
       "pack": "Pack", "dispatch": "Dispatch", "quarantine": "Quarantine Lot", "release_lot": "Release Lot"}
rel, o2o = ocel.relations.copy(), ocel.o2o.copy()

# 1. Interaction structure: two different frequency notions
inter = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)
pair_events = (inter.groupby(["ocel:type", "ocel:type_2"])["ocel:eid"].nunique()
               .rename("shared_events"))
pair_objects = (inter.assign(pair=inter[["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2"]].apply(lambda r: tuple(sorted(r)), axis=1))
                .groupby(["ocel:type", "ocel:type_2"])["pair"].nunique().rename("distinct_object_pairs"))
print(pd.concat([pair_events, pair_objects], axis=1).sort_values("shared_events", ascending=False))

interaction_edges = pm4py.discover_objects_graph(ocel, graph_type="object_interaction")
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)      # object-type graph: interaction/creation/co-death relations
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)    # activity <-> object-type frequencies
print(otg); print(etot)

# 2. OC-DFG: compare event-couple vs unique-object vs total-object frequencies per type
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
rows = []
for ot, edges in ocdfg["edges"]["event_couples"].items():
    for edge, couples in edges.items():
        rows.append({"type": ot, "edge": edge, "event_couples": len(couples),
                     "unique_objects": len(ocdfg["edges"]["unique_objects"][ot][edge]),
                     "total_objects": len(ocdfg["edges"]["total_objects"][ot][edge])})
freq = pd.DataFrame(rows)
freq["convergence_ratio"] = freq["total_objects"] / freq["event_couples"]  # >1: one event serves many objects
print(freq.sort_values("convergence_ratio", ascending=False))

# 3. Flattenings for contrast (these are the misleading views)
perf = {}
for ot in ["Job", "Machine", "Shipment", "Batch", "Tool", "ComponentLot"]:
    flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, ot)
    if len(flat):
        dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(flat)
        perf[ot] = {e: (v["median"], v["max"]) for e, v in dfg.items()}   # median vs max exposes tails

# 4. Typed waiting from object relations (not from machine gaps)
def times(act):
    return rel[rel["ocel:activity"] == act]

# 4a. Batch synchronization: job readiness -> Batch Cure sharing the same event
cure = times(ACT["cure"]); ready = times(ACT["ready_batch"])
cj = cure[cure["ocel:type"] == "Job"][["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:timestamp"]]
rj = ready[ready["ocel:type"] == "Job"].groupby("ocel:oid")["ocel:timestamp"].max()
cj = cj.assign(ready=cj["ocel:oid"].map(rj))
cj["batch_wait"] = cj["ocel:timestamp"] - cj["ready"]

# 4b. Tool wait: arrival at machine -> process start, intersected with other jobs' holds of the same tool
proc = times(ACT["process"])
proc_tool = proc[proc["ocel:type"] == "Tool"][["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid"]].rename(columns={"ocel:oid": "tool"})
proc_job = proc[proc["ocel:type"] == "Job"][["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:timestamp"]].merge(proc_tool, on="ocel:eid")
arrive = times(ACT["arrive"]).query("`ocel:type` == 'Job'").groupby("ocel:oid")["ocel:timestamp"].min()
proc_job["arrive"] = proc_job["ocel:oid"].map(arrive)

acq = times(ACT["acquire"]); rel_t = times(ACT["release_tool"])
def holds(df):
    j = df[df["ocel:type"] == "Job"][["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid"]]
    t = df[df["ocel:type"] == "Tool"][["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:timestamp"]]
    return j.merge(t, on="ocel:eid", suffixes=("_job", "_tool"))
h = holds(acq).merge(holds(rel_t), on=["ocel:oid_job", "ocel:oid_tool"], suffixes=("_start", "_end"))

def tool_overlap(r):
    other = h[(h["ocel:oid_tool"] == r["tool"]) & (h["ocel:oid_job"] != r["ocel:oid"])]
    s = other["ocel:timestamp_start"].clip(lower=r["arrive"]); e = other["ocel:timestamp_end"].clip(upper=r["ocel:timestamp"])
    return (e - s).clip(lower=pd.Timedelta(0)).sum()
proc_job["tool_wait_upper_bound"] = proc_job.apply(tool_overlap, axis=1)

# 4c. Lot holds linked two ways: direct E2O vs O2O requires∘contains (tests the inconsistent linkage)
req = o2o[o2o["ocel:qualifier"] == "requires"].rename(columns={"ocel:oid": "job", "ocel:oid_2": "comp"})
con = o2o[o2o["ocel:qualifier"] == "contains"].rename(columns={"ocel:oid": "lot", "ocel:oid_2": "comp"})
job_lot = req.merge(con, on="comp")[["job", "lot"]]
q = times(ACT["quarantine"]).query("`ocel:type` == 'ComponentLot'")
direct = set(times(ACT["quarantine"]).query("`ocel:type` == 'Job'")["ocel:oid"])
derived = set(job_lot[job_lot["lot"].isin(q["ocel:oid"])]["job"])
print("lot-hold jobs only via O2O:", derived - direct, "| only via E2O:", direct - derived)

# 4d. Shipment consolidation wait: pack -> shipment dispatch via 'consolidates'
cons = o2o[o2o["ocel:qualifier"] == "consolidates"].rename(columns={"ocel:oid": "shipment", "ocel:oid_2": "job"})
pack = times(ACT["pack"]).query("`ocel:type` == 'Job'").groupby("ocel:oid")["ocel:timestamp"].max()
disp = times(ACT["dispatch"]).query("`ocel:type` == 'Shipment'").groupby("ocel:oid")["ocel:timestamp"].max()
cons["pack"] = cons["job"].map(pack); cons["dispatch"] = cons["shipment"].map(disp)
cons["partner_wait"] = cons["dispatch"] - cons["pack"]
cons["last_partner"] = cons.groupby("shipment")["pack"].transform("max") == cons["pack"]

# 5. Features and coupling graph
feat = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel, "Job", enable_object_lifecycle_paths=True,
                                   enable_object_work_in_progress=True, include_obj_id=True)
G = pm4py.convert_ocel_to_networkx(ocel)
jobs = set(ocel.objects.query("`ocel:type` == 'Job'")["ocel:oid"])
J = nx.Graph()
J.add_edges_from((a, b) for a, b in interaction_edges if a in jobs and b in jobs)
for _, grp in cons.groupby("shipment"):
    js = list(grp["job"]); J.add_edges_from((x, y) for i, x in enumerate(js) for y in js[i + 1:])
print("job coupling components:", list(nx.connected_components(J)))
print(feat.describe())
print(cj[["ocel:oid", "batch_wait"]], proc_job[["ocel:oid", "tool", "tool_wait_upper_bound"]],
      cons[["job", "shipment", "partner_wait", "last_partner"]], sep="\n")
```

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations that would weaken batch synchronization as the top explanation:**
1. OVEN-1 or RACK-7 turns out to have been unavailable 08:25–12:05 (maintenance, preheat, another load).
2. J1/J2 readiness timestamps were bulk or retroactive entries, or a mandatory rest time before cure exists.
3. The cure specification forbids loads below three jobs, making the wait technically necessary.
4. Over a longer log, batch-wait length does not predict dispatch lateness, because consolidation absorbs it.
5. Periods with partial-batch launching show unchanged cycle and dispatch times.
6. B1 membership was mandated for traceability (co-cured parts for S2).
7. B2-type delays persist even with a free rack and ready members.

**Additional fields or events that would discriminate among the hypotheses:**
1. `Request Tool`/`Wait for Tool` events with job, tool and machine.
2. The readiness definition, with entry time vs. event time (logging lag).
3. RACK-7 state changes with reason codes (routine cleaning vs. contamination from L7).
4. Lot-hold links for every derived job, and override events with authorizer and reason.
5. Batch-formation decisions: planned members, fill threshold, recipe, minimum load.
6. Promised ship dates, consolidation rule, and freight cost per shipment.
7. The CAL-3 recalibration trigger (count, time, drift) and its results.
8. CNC-2 job attribution for 09:50–10:05 and 10:47–11:45, rework job identifiers, and J4's events between 14:50 and 19:10.
