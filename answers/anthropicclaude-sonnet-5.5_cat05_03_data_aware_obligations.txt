## 1. Temporal Data Semantics

**Event order.** Within a case, events are totally ordered by `(time:timestamp, event_index)`. I write `e  f` when `t_e < t_f`, or `t_e = t_f` and `i_e < i_f`. All "before" and "after" relations below are strict under ``. Equal timestamps are never treated as simultaneous. If `event_index` were missing for a tie, the result would be UNKNOWN.

**State reconstruction.**
- State is built only from state-setting events: `Create_Transaction`, `Update_Amount`, `Risk_Reclassify`, `Update_Jurisdiction` and `Update_Customer_Type`.
- The value of attribute `a` at event `e` is the latest non-blank value written by a state-setting event `u  e`. If none exists, the value is  (unknown).
- A blank means "no update". It is never a reset to null, and nothing is ever filled backward.
- Missing values are never defaulted to LOW, 0 or DE (R6).

**Columns that are not case state.**
- The `amount` on `Settlement_Confirmed` is an observation at settlement time (`settlement_amount`).
- The `jurisdiction` on `Sanctions_List_Update` is the listed jurisdiction, not a change of the case jurisdiction.
- The `exemption_id` on `Release_Funds` is a claim that an exemption was relied on, not proof.

**Final attributes.** The final case state is never used to evaluate an earlier event.

**Four times.**
- *Event time* is when the log says something happened. A state update is effective at its event time unless explicit evidence says otherwise.
- *Effective time* is the start of validity. For exemptions, the displayed `effective_from` is only a claim about validity scope.
- *Record time* is when a row was inserted. `Record_Exemption` at 11:00 is record time.
- *Evidence time* is when supporting evidence was created, for example a signature timestamp or certificate creation.
- A late record can prove earlier approval only if independent evidence time is at or before the trigger. A backdated display field alone does not.

**Material change, relative to the review snapshot.**
- Amount: an increase of more than 20% (strict) over the amount known at the latest review. I compare against the reviewed amount, not the previous update, so creeping increments cannot hide. Decreases are not material. If the baseline or new amount is , the change is unclassifiable.
- Jurisdiction, risk and customer type: a change is material if it expands a trigger. That means a new non-DE jurisdiction, risk becoming HIGH, or customer type becoming PEP. Moves out of scope (for example  DE) do not invalidate a review, but can un-trigger a rule at release.
- Thresholds: "at least 100,000" is inclusive and "more than 20%" is strict.

**Tie-breaks.** They matter only when they change which state a review saw, or whether a trigger exists at release. Each case states the consequence explicitly (TX-311 is the only real tie).

**Coverage caveat.** The rules contain no initial sanctions-screening obligation. `Sanctions_Screen` therefore matters only for R3.

## 4. Ambiguities and Alternative Readings

**TX-302 (COMPLIANT).**
- The exemption is unnecessary because the ER alone satisfies R1.
- If someone read "unless exemption" as requiring the exemption path, EX-77 still qualifies: signature 09:05  trigger 09:40, hash valid, outage documented. The result would be JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION or COMPLIANT, never a violation.
- The late 11:00 database row is record time, not approval time.
- What would change it: proof that the ER did not occur (for example a forged timestamp) would leave only the exemption. The exemption's strong evidence would then give JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.

**TX-305 (INDETERMINATE).**
- The amount at release is unknown.
- If an immutable release payload showed under 100k, R1 is not triggered and the case is COMPLIANT.
- If the payload showed 100k or more, and the ER record showed the reviewed amount, the case is COMPLIANT.
- If the ER did not know the amount, it is unproven as an enhanced review of a large transaction. That would push the case towards NONCOMPLIANT.
- Treating the settlement amount (150k) as the release amount is an inference. Fees, FX or a later change could differ. Even so, no amount change is logged between creation and release. So on the log evidence an ER precedes release either way, and the residual doubt is coverage.
- R6 forbids defaulting to a low amount. I also avoid defaulting to a high one. I lean "probably compliant in form, unproven in substance".

**TX-310 (NONCOMPLIANT, defeasible).**
- Confirmed approval evidence dated at or before 10:00 would give JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. Examples: signing-service or HSM logs, an email or workflow record from Head_Compliance, or a contemporaneous record from someone other than the requester.
- Confirmation that approval occurred only at 10:30 or later (ratification) would confirm NONCOMPLIANT. After-the-fact ratification cannot satisfy a release-time condition.
- A reasonable alternative is INDETERMINATE, because the evidence is conflicting: oral approval at 08:45 vs. displayed 08:50 vs. certificate at 10:30.
- I chose NONCOMPLIANT because R5 requires that evidence *supports* approval existing, and the burden sits on the exemption. The requester-witness is interested, and the PDF display field alone is "not conclusive".

**TX-311 (COMPLIANT).**
- The verdict is robust to the tie order and to the ER. Even if the DE update were wrong and the jurisdiction stayed US, the ER at 16:20 is after the amount increase at 16:10 and before release, so R1 would still be satisfied.
- It would be noncompliant only if all of the following held: the DE update was invalid, the jurisdiction change were treated as a material change after the ER, and R1 were evaluated on the US state. Those premises cut against each other.
- A control point remains. A jurisdiction change to DE at the same timestamp as the review, 10 minutes before release, needs provenance.

**TX-307 (NONCOMPLIANT).** A different interpretation, namely that the CA over a standard packet is a substitute for an ER, could be defended. It would need an explicit policy statement or evidence that the packet was actually an enhanced review. I do not adopt it: R2 says "both required", and the facts state a CA on a standard packet does not cure it.

**TX-308 (COMPLIANT).** If policy treated any amount change close to release as material, or suspected structuring to the threshold, re-review would be required. Under R4, a decrease is not material and the ER already covered the larger amount.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 1-2. load + pm4py formatting

df["event_index"] = df["event_index"].astype(int)
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS)
# Re-impose the R7 order explicitly (stable sort; event_index is the tie-breaker).
df = df.sort_values([CASE, TS, "event_index"], kind="mergesort").reset_index(drop=True)
df["seq"] = df.groupby(CASE).cumcount()          # position in the total order (ts, event_index)
g = df[CASE]

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 3. forward-fill state (never backward)

              "Update_Jurisdiction", "Update_Customer_Type"]
is_state = df[ACT].isin(STATE_ACTS)
for a in ["amount", "risk_level", "jurisdiction", "customer_type"]:
    df[a + "_after"] = df[a].where(is_state).groupby(g).ffill()          # state after the event
    df[a + "_before"] = df[a + "_after"].groupby(g).shift(1)             # state strictly before the event
# Non-state uses of shared columns are kept separate to avoid contamination.
df["settlement_amount"] = df["amount"].where(df[ACT].eq("Settlement_Confirmed"))
df["listed_jurisdiction"] = df["jurisdiction"].where(df[ACT].eq("Sanctions_List_Update"))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 4-5. trackers (latest strictly-earlier occurrence)
def prev_latest(s):
    return s.groupby(g).ffill().groupby(g).shift(1)

def value_at(col, seq_col):
    """Value of df[col] at the row whose in-case position is df[seq_col] (NaN if none)."""
    lookup = {c: grp[col].to_numpy() for c, grp in df.groupby(CASE)}
    return pd.Series([lookup[c][int(s)] if pd.notna(s) else np.nan
                      for c, s in zip(df[CASE], df[seq_col])], index=df.index)

         "SS": "Sanctions_Screen", "SLU": "Sanctions_List_Update"}
for k, act in TRACK.items():
    m = df[ACT].eq(act)
    df[k + "_seq"] = prev_latest(df["seq"].where(m))
    df[k + "_ts"] = prev_latest(df[TS].where(m))

df["REV_seq"] = prev_latest(df["seq"].where(is_REV))

df["amt_at_ER"] = value_at("amount_after", "ER_seq")      # NaN if the amount was unknown at the review
df["amt_at_REV"] = value_at("amount_after", "REV_seq")
df["juris_at_ER"] = value_at("jurisdiction_after", "ER_seq")
df["listed_at_SLU"] = value_at("listed_jurisdiction", "SLU_seq")

    (df[ACT].eq("Risk_Reclassify") & df["risk_level_after"].eq("HIGH") & df["risk_level_before"].ne("HIGH")) |
    (df[ACT].eq("Update_Customer_Type") & df["customer_type_after"].eq("PEP") & df["customer_type_before"].ne("PEP")) |
    (df[ACT].eq("Update_Jurisdiction") & df["jurisdiction_after"].notna() &
     df["jurisdiction_after"].ne("DE") & df["jurisdiction_after"].ne(df["jurisdiction_before"]))
)
df["material_any"] = df["amt_mat_ER"] | df["amt_mat_REV"] | df["attr_mat"]
df["MC_ER_seq"] = prev_latest(df["seq"].where(df["amt_mat_ER"] | df["attr_mat"]))
df["MC_attr_seq"] = prev_latest(df["seq"].where(df["attr_mat"]))
df["MC_REV_seq"] = prev_latest(df["seq"].where(df["amt_mat_REV"]))
df["MC_any_seq"] = prev_latest(df["seq"].where(df["material_any"]))
df["MC_any_ts"] = prev_latest(df[TS].where(df["material_any"]))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 6. release snapshot + exemption register
rel = df[df[ACT].eq("Release_Funds")].copy()
rel["ER_fresh"] = rel["ER_seq"].notna() & (rel["MC_ER_seq"].isna() | (rel["MC_ER_seq"] < rel["ER_seq"]))
rel["ER_fresh_attr"] = rel["ER_seq"].notna() & (rel["MC_attr_seq"].isna() | (rel["MC_attr_seq"] < rel["ER_seq"]))

rel["r4_review_invalidated"] = (rel["REV_seq"].notna() & rel["MC_REV_seq"].notna()

       .rename("exemption_record_ts").reset_index())        # record time only

                      (rel["ex_customer_type"] == rel["customer_type_before"]) &
                      (rel["amount_before"] <= rel["ex_max_amount"]))

                                               rel["customer_type_before"].isna() |
                                               rel["amount_before"].isna())

def ex_status(r):
    if not r.ex_present:
        return "NONE"
    if not (r.ex_approver_ok and r.ex_effective_ok and r.ex_unexpired and r.ex_scope_ok):
        return "TIME_SCOPE_OR_APPROVER_FAIL_OR_UNKNOWN"

def r1_trigger(r):
    j, a = r.jurisdiction_before, r.amount_before
    if pd.notna(j) and j == "DE": return "FALSE"
    if pd.notna(a) and a < 100000: return "FALSE"
    if pd.notna(j) and pd.notna(a): return "TRUE"
    return "UNKNOWN"

def r2_trigger(r):
    if r.risk_level_before == "HIGH" or r.customer_type_before == "PEP": return "TRUE"
    if pd.notna(r.risk_level_before) and pd.notna(r.customer_type_before): return "FALSE"
    return "UNKNOWN"

def r1_status(r):
    t = r1_trigger(r)
    if t == "FALSE": return "NOT_TRIGGERED"
    if t == "UNKNOWN": return "INDETERMINATE"
    if r.ER_fresh and pd.notna(r.amt_at_ER): return "SATISFIED_BY_REVIEW"
    if r.ex_status == "EFFECTIVE_CANDIDATE" and "R1" in str(r.ex_waives): return "SATISFIED_BY_EXEMPTION"
    if r.ex_status == "EVIDENCE_DISPUTED_REVIEW_REQUIRED": return "REVIEW_REQUIRED"
    if pd.notna(r.ER_seq) and pd.isna(r.amt_at_ER): return "INDETERMINATE"   # review amount unknown
    return "VIOLATED"

def r2_status(r):
    t = r2_trigger(r)
    if t == "FALSE": return "NOT_TRIGGERED"
    if t == "UNKNOWN": return "INDETERMINATE"
    return "SATISFIED" if (r.ER_fresh_attr and r.CA_after_ER) else "VIOLATED"

def r3_status(r):
    if pd.isna(r.SLU_seq): return "NOT_TRIGGERED"
    if pd.isna(r.jurisdiction_before): return "INDETERMINATE"
    if r.listed_at_SLU != r.jurisdiction_before: return "NOT_TRIGGERED"
    # latest screen must come strictly after the list update (no screen at all = violation)
    return "SATISFIED" if (pd.notna(r.SS_seq) and r.SS_seq > r.SLU_seq) else "VIOLATED"

rel["R1_trigger"] = rel.apply(r1_trigger, axis=1)
rel["R1"], rel["R2"], rel["R3"] = (rel.apply(r1_status, axis=1), rel.apply(r2_status, axis=1),
                                   rel.apply(r3_status, axis=1))

    if pd.isna(r.amount_before) and pd.notna(r.settlement_amount_evidence) and pd.notna(r.jurisdiction_before):
        return "TRUE" if (r.jurisdiction_before != "DE" and r.settlement_amount_evidence >= 100000) else "FALSE"
    return "n/a"

    if "VIOLATED" in s: return "FLAGGED_VIOLATION"
    if any(x in ("INDETERMINATE", "REVIEW_REQUIRED") for x in s): return "REVIEW_REQUIRED"
    return "NO_FLAG"

**Why the code's DECLARE check cannot be faithful.** Whether an activity is required depends on attribute values at release (jurisdiction, amount, risk, customer type) as they stood at that time. Whether a prior activity still counts depends on later material data changes. Whether an exemption excuses it depends on out-of-log evidence. DECLARE templates see only activity order. The table above shows it cannot separate the correct failures (TX-309) from false alarms and missed cases.

## 6. Why Control-Flow-Only Conformance Is Insufficient

1. **Final-state leakage.** A trace-level check that reads final attributes evaluates early events with the wrong facts. TX-301 would look like a 125k case from creation. TX-303 would look like HIGH from creation. TX-311 would look like DE when the review ran (it saw US). Using creation values instead would miss all of TX-301 and TX-303.
2. **Post-hoc exemptions.** A `Record_Exemption` event appears after release in both TX-302 and TX-310. Order alone cannot distinguish strong evidence (TX-302: signed at 09:05, outage documented) from weak evidence (TX-310: certificate created after release, interested witness).
3. **Material changes after review.** TX-309 has an ER before release, which passes any "ER precedes release" template. The ER was invalidated by a 22.9% increase (R4). Control flow sees no violation.
4. **Conditional activation.** Whether ER or CA is required depends on jurisdiction, amount, risk and customer type at release. Unconditional precedence templates produce false positives and false negatives. For example, TX-304 lacks an ER and R1 is not triggered, and TX-311 has an ER that was not needed.
5. **Missing data.** In TX-305 the trigger itself is undetermined. Control flow either passes it (an ER exists) or fails it, but cannot say "unknown".
6. **Same-timestamp ordering.** In TX-311, ER and jurisdiction change share 16:20. Without `event_index`, both orders are possible and the state the review saw is ambiguous. Sorting by timestamp alone makes the answer depend on an arbitrary sort.
7. **Value thresholds and ratios.** The ">20% since the review" and "100,000" tests are arithmetic over attribute history. Control-flow models have no notion of reviewed amount.
8. **Approval content.** In TX-307 a CA exists after the PEP change, yet it approved a standard packet. The activity label `Compliance_Approve` does not encode what was approved, so control flow counts it as compliant.
9. **Changes to the sanctions list are outside the case.** R3 depends on list-update timing and on the case's jurisdiction at that time. A list-update row with a jurisdiction column can be mistaken for a case update. Only the combination of the list update and the case's jurisdiction makes the screen stale.

---

## 7. Recommended Logging and Control Improvements

- **Immutable release payload.** At release, log a hash-linked payload with amount, currency, risk, jurisdiction, customer type, exemption references and rule version. This would have resolved TX-305 and removed any need for the settlement inference.
- **Separate timestamps.** Keep `event_time`, `effective_time`, `record_time` and `evidence/signature_time` as distinct fields for every state change and exemption. Require the digital-signature time and certificate time for exemptions, and verify signatures at approval.
- **Rule version and decision record.** Log the rule version in force and the computed trigger state at release. Log the snapshot and material-change baseline each review used, including the reviewed amount, jurisdiction, risk and customer type.
- **Structured exemption scope.** Store approver role, scope (jurisdiction, customer type, amount cap), expiry and waived rules as data. Flag any claimed exemption whose approval evidence time is later than the release.
- **Change provenance.** For every state change, log who changed it, from what source, from which prior value, and why (for example the DE change in TX-311). Distinguish case-state updates from external list updates, and use distinct attribute names (`listed_jurisdiction` rather than `jurisdiction`).
- **Stable event ordering.** Use a monotonically increasing source sequence number or logical clock. Require it in the export, and reject ties without one.
- **Review content.** Require reviews and approvals to record review type (standard or enhanced), the packet reviewed, the reviewed amount and the reviewer's role. A CA must reference the ER it follows.
- **Block-on-unknown control.** Prevent `Release_Funds` when amount, risk, jurisdiction or customer type is unknown. Require re-screening after a sanctions-list update for the case's jurisdiction before release. Block release when a review has been invalidated by a material change.
- **Unknown-value markers.** Distinguish "no update" from "unknown" with an explicit marker, so absent values are not silently treated as unchanged or defaulted.
