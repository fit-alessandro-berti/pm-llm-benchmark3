

# 1. Current-process diagnosis

The current process exhibits a stark contradiction between operational efficiency and service integrity. The most frequent path (Intake  Auto eligibility  Fast approval  Dispatch) processes 2,550 cases efficiently (median ~1 hour post-intake) but generates disproportionate downstream costs. Specifically, 14.1% of Fast approval cases result in Customer complaint/reopen, compared to only 3.3% for cases following the Repair  Approval path. This suggests the Fast path bypasses critical validation, leading to incorrect replacement selection.

Compliance is systematically violated on the high-volume path. Conformance findings highlight that Fast approval often occurs before evidence completeness (R2 violation) and for claims above EUR 400 without Manual or Fraud review (R1 violation). These deviations contribute to a token-based replay fitness of only 0.91 and a precision issue where low-frequency risk paths mix with auto-approvals.

Customer outcomes are segmented unevenly. Premium High-Value device claims on the Fast path suffer a 22% complaint/reopen rate, whereas the reviewed/repair path reduces this to 5%. Despite this, the cycle time for High-Value reviewed/repair path is 96 hours median, creating a tension between speed and quality. Finally, the "Unclear defect / missing evidence" group (22% of cases) suffers a 140-hour median cycle time, with 26% of cases requiring repeated evidence requests, indicating poor initial data capture.

Key process-mining artifacts informing this diagnosis include:
1.  **DFG Frequency:** Fast approval  Dispatch replacement (2,550 cases) is the dominant path.
2.  **Rework Statistics:** 14.1% complaint rate for Fast approval vs. 3.3% for Repair quote path.
3.  **Conformance Deviations:** "Fast approval before Evidence completeness" is a frequent move-on-log deviation.
4.  **Performance DFG:** Manual eligibility review  Repair quote takes 22 hours mean (too slow for Premium SLAs).
5.  **Customer Segment Outcomes:** Premium High-Value Fast path complaints (22%) vs. reviewed path (5%).

# 2. Redesign A: Segment-Aware Risk Routing

**Description:**
This redesign changes the intake routing logic. Instead of a blanket "Auto eligibility screen" for all 3,600 auto-eligible cases, the system will segment claims immediately based on device value and customer tier. High-Value devices (EUR >400) and Premium customers will be forced into Manual eligibility or Fraud/risk review before approval. Low-Value Standard cases remain on the Fast path.

**Target:**
High-Value Premium and Standard cases (35% of total volume).

**Mechanism:**
By enforcing normative rule R1 upstream, the redesign prevents Fast approval on risky claims. This aligns with the finding that the reviewed/repair path has significantly lower complaint rates (5% vs 22% for Premium High-Value).

**Sacrifice/Unresolved:**
This intentionally increases cycle time for high-value segments (currently 20h fast vs 96h reviewed). It may increase resource pressure on the Manual review team, potentially hitting the 82% utilization bottleneck faster. It does not solve the evidence quality issue for unclear defect cases.

# 3. Redesign B: Pre-Dispatch Verification Gate

**Description:**
This redesign retains the Fast path for speed but inserts a lightweight quality check immediately after Fast approval and before Dispatch replacement. A technician verifies part selection against the claim data before shipping. This is distinct from the current "Quality/compliance close" which happens post-dispatch.

**Target:**
All Fast approval cases (2,550 cases/year), particularly High-Value devices.

**Mechanism:**
This targets the root cause of the 14.1% Fast path complaint rate. Evidence suggests "Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen" (mean 96 hours) is linked to incorrect part selection. Adding a 6-18 hour check prevents EUR 410 wrong replacement costs for high-value devices.

**Sacrifice/Unresolved:**
Adds latency to the Fast path, potentially breaching 24h Premium SLAs. Requires reallocation of technician resources (currently 3 technicians for repair quotes). It does not address the high rate of missing evidence at intake.

# 4. Redesign C: Proactive Evidence Portal

**Description:**
This redesign focuses on the intake and evidence collection phase. Upon claim submission, customers are routed to a guided portal that validates image/attachment quality against the defect type before the claim enters the eligibility workflow. Repeated evidence requests are minimized through automated feedback loops.

**Target:**
The "Unclear defect / missing evidence" segment (22% of cases) and cases requesting missing evidence.

**Mechanism:**
The current process sees 26% of cases requesting evidence request evidence, leading to 34-hour loops. By shifting evidence validation to the customer-facing entry point, we reduce back-channel requests. This targets the 140-hour cycle time for unclear defect cases.

**Sacrifice/Unresolved:**
May increase abandonment for customers with low digital literacy (risk of "Claim abandoned" rising above current 320). Does not fix technical decision errors on valid claims.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A (Risk Routing):**
1.  **Failure:** Manual reviewers cannot handle the 35% high-value surge, queue times spike beyond 50 hours for Premium customers, damaging satisfaction more than the original Fast path.
2.  **Failure:** Rigid rule triggers force low-risk high-value claims into manual review (e.g., known defect types), increasing cost without value.
3.  **Displaced bottleneck:** Fraud/risk review team (Mondays after campaigns).

**Redesign B (Pre-Dispatch Gate):**
1.  **Failure:** The gate becomes a choke point during volume surges, delaying dispatch beyond SLA limits (124h for Premium).
2.  **Failure:** Gate staff lack authority to approve substitutions, causing decision loops back to engineers.
3.  **Displaced bottleneck:** Repair technicians (now required for pre-dispatch checks).

**Redesign C (Evidence Portal):**
1.  **Failure:** Low digital literacy customers abandon claims at portal upload, increasing abandonment rate beyond current 320 cases.
2.  **Failure:** Automated rejection of borderline quality images triggers manual follow-up, negating time savings.
3.  **Displaced bottleneck:** Customer support desk (handling portal queries).

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**First Pilot:** Redesign A (Segment-Aware Risk Routing) on High-Value devices only (20% of volume).
*   **Logic:** High-risk, high-cost impact; limited volume compared to total.
*   **Metrics to monitor:** Complaint/reopen rate, Premium High-Value; Cycle time for High-Value cases; Manual review queue utilization.
*   **Stop/Go thresholds:** Proceed if complaint rate drops below 10% and High-Value cycle time stays under 72h median. Stop if manual queue utilization > 90%.
*   **Sequencing logic:** Validate the trade-off between speed and quality before committing to portal infrastructure (Redesign C) or process gates (Redesign B).
*   **Learning mechanism:** Compare complaints in the pilot group against a control group of High-Value cases remaining on the Fast path.

**Second Pilot:** Redesign C (Evidence Portal) on Standard Low-Value claims (25% of volume).
*   **Metrics to monitor:** Evidence request rate, Cycle time (unclear defect), Abandonment rate.
*   **Stop/Go thresholds:** Proceed if evidence requests drop by 30% and abandonment does not increase by >5%.
*   **Learning mechanism:** Track distinct claim IDs to ensure improvements in cycle time are not caused by increased abandonment (skewed data).
**Third Pilot:** Redesign B (Pre-Dispatch Gate) on Premium High-Value cases after Redesign A is stable.
*   **Metrics to monitor:** Wrong-part cost savings, Gate wait time.
*   **Stop/Go thresholds:** Proceed if wrong-part cost savings exceed gate labor cost by 2x.
*   **Learning mechanism:** Audit dispatch logs to ensure gate is catching errors not caught by previous reviews.
