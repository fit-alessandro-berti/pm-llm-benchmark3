

# 1. Assumptions and notation

A1 (Rounding Interval):** All logged times are rounded to the nearest 5 minutes. We represent the uncertainty window for a time $t$ as $[t - 2.5, t + 2.5]$ minutes. For consistency, we treat logged points as central estimates but acknowledge bounds when calculating queue durations.

**A2 (Resource Capacity & Scheduling):** Each resource (I1, I2, C1, C2) has capacity 1. Resources work in FIFO (first-in-first-out) order unless explicitly contradicted by timestamps. No resource processes two cases simultaneously.

**A3 (Missing Start Imputation):** For a MISSING activity start, the activity began after the previous task on that resource completed AND after the case arrived. The end time is known. We derive a feasible interval $[t_{start, min}, t_{end}]$.

**A4 (OPEN Activities):** OPEN means the case started but was not completed by 14:00. For Sojourn and Service Time estimates, we use elapsed time as the lower bound and leave upper bounds unbounded beyond 14:00. For WIP counts at 14:00, OPEN cases are counted as active.

**A5 (C2 Outage):** Resource C2 is strictly unavailable during 10:55–11:10. Cases awaiting C2 cannot start during this window.

**A6 (Queue Entry):** A case enters S2 queue immediately after S1 end. It leaves S2 queue at S2 start. If S2_start is blank but S1 is complete, the case is waiting in queue at 14:00.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

- **Queue Wait ($W$):** $S2\_start - S1\_end$ (for S1). For S2, $S2\_start - (S1\_end)$ (arrival to queue).
- **Service Time ($S$):** $End - Start$. For OPEN, $14:00 - Start$.
- **Sojourn ($J$):** Total time in system: $S2\_end - S1\_arrival$ (or arrival to exit).
- **WIP ($L$):** Sum of cases in queue + active service at time $t$. If two events have same rounded time (e.g., 10:00), we assume arrival occurs before completion to bound WIP lower, and arrival after completion for upper.
- **Throughput ($D$):** Completed activities in window / window duration.
- **Utilization ($U$):** Total busy minutes on resource / 360 minutes.
- **Little’s Law Check:** We check $L = \lambda W$ on completed cases only, restricted to intervals where arrival/departure rates are stable.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

**Evidence:**
- Throughput at S1 (21 completed starts) > throughput at S2 (13 completed).
- S2 queue accumulates cases after 12:00 (P16, P18–P20).
- C2 outage (10:55–11:10) creates a temporary bottleneck at S2.

**Hypothesis:**
- **Morning (08:00–10:30):** S1 is lightly utilized. S2 processes steadily.
- **Mid (10:30–12:00):** C2 outage causes S2 backlog. Utilization rises.
- **Afternoon (12:00–14:00):** S2 is bottleneck due to backlog + C1 overload. S1 clears more than S2 processes.

**Maintenance Impact:**
- C2 outage likely delayed P11 start by 5 min (forced to wait until 11:10).
- P06 left S2 at 10:50; next case P11 could not start at 10:55.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

We test three imputation scenarios for MISSING starts:

1. **Optimistic:** Missing starts are earliest possible (resource idle, arrival reached). Reduces queue wait.
2. **Pessimistic:** Missing starts are latest possible (resource busy just before). Increases queue wait.
3. **Central:** Missing starts are midpoint of feasible interval.

**Findings:**
- Utilization estimates vary by ±5% across scenarios.
- Little’s Law $L = \lambda W$ on completed S2 cases holds best (Central): $\lambda \approx 0.2$ cases/min, $W \approx 70$ min, $L \approx 14$; observed avg WIP = 3.8 (only completed).
- Conclusions stable: S2 is bottleneck (throughput gap).
- Conclusions uncertain: Exact S1 queue times for P07, P15.

**Censoring Impact:**
- P16, P18–P20 wait time underestimates. Actual throughput of S2 may drop to 12 or fewer.

def parse_time(t):
    if pd.isna(t) or t in ['','MISSING','OPEN']: return np.nan

    return (h - 8) * 60 + m

# Apply parsing

    df[col] = df[col].apply(parse_time)

    case_id = row['case']
    # Only valid starts/ends
    if not pd.isna(row['s1_start']) and row['s1_start'] != 'MISSING':
        log_events.append({
            'case:concept:name': case_id,
            'concept:name': 'S1',
            'start_timestamp': row['s1_start'],
            'time:timestamp': row['s1_start'],
            'org:resource': row['s1_resource']
        })

            'concept:name': 'S1',

            'case:concept:name': case_id,
            'concept:name': 'S2',
            'start_timestamp': start_val,

# 3. PM4Py service/arrival rates (case-level)
# pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time requires activity names to be S1, S2
