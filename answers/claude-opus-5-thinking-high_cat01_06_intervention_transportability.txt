Shared mechanisms. Two mechanisms plausibly generalize. First, deleting an unnecessary control-flow prerequisite converts sequential service into overlapping service, so the verification span falls from the sum of the two service times toward their maximum. Second, span compression reduces waiting before `Assessment`, which is the largest identified component of Site A's median reduction (18h to 12h). Both are structural, not site-specific.

Site-specific mechanisms. Site A's gain also flowed through channels Site B cannot inherit: a registry API upgrade, two additional document reviewers, cross-trained reviewers who self-balance load, an inbound mix shifted toward simpler digital claims, daily huddles, and a case system that natively supports two active owners. None of these is a property of the `+` operator; each hangs off its own selection node.

Effect modifiers. `originals_required`, `digital_documents`, `case_complexity`, `role_specialization`, `IT_case_lock`, `assessment_capacity` and `legal_capacity` modify both the sign and the size of the effect. Where originals are legally required, `Document Check` cannot start, so parallelization is void. Where roles are separate and the case is locked, apparent overlap is a recording artifact. Where downstream pools are saturated, faster verification relocates the queue rather than removing it.

Why an identical diagram does not guarantee transport. A process tree constrains ordering only; it is silent on which orderings are legally attainable, who may execute concurrent tasks, whether the IT layer permits two open owners, and where capacity binds. Two sites can share a partial order and differ entirely in the data-and-resource layer that determines realized concurrency. Transport requires both a structural precondition (a genuinely removable prerequisite) and an operational one (capacity, roles, IT, measurement). Site A satisfies both; Site B satisfies the structural one only for a minority segment.

Reasoning that maps the evidence to these ranges.

- Span arithmetic. At B, sequential verification consumes roughly 3.4 + 8.1 = 11.5h of elapsed activity time plus at least one handoff wait; ideal overlap gives max(3.4, 8.1) = 8.1h. The theoretical ceiling from pure overlap is therefore about 3.4h of critical path, plus whatever handoff/queue time the handoff itself created. Site A's own service medians (3.5 and 7.2h before) imply an identical ceiling of ~3.5h, yet the reported median fell 9h — direct evidence that most of A's improvement came from waiting reduction and co-interventions, not from the overlap identity. This is why no scenario claims a 9h transfer.
- Segment position in the distribution matters more than segment size. The digital, no-originals cases are already the fast cohort (no 18h receipt wait, and the smaller `Identity Check -> Document Check` arc count of 1,160 shows they already run leaner). Improving cases that sit below the median compresses the left side of the distribution and barely moves the median: hence S2's 4-8h segment gain translates to only 0.5-2h overall. Conversely, the originals cohort sits above the median and in the tail, so S3's smaller per-case gain moves both the overall median and p90 substantially.
- Where the 1,020 `Identity Check -> Originals Receipt` cases come from. Roughly 30% of all cases, and about half of the originals-required cases, currently start receipt only after identity finishes. Eliminating that handoff removes identity's 3.4h elapsed duration plus its queue time from the receipt clock; with a median receipt delay of 18h and a heavy tail, moving the start of that clock earlier is the highest-leverage available change and is precisely the mechanism Site A demonstrated — applied to a different arc.
- Downstream capacity check. Throughput implies about 131 cases/week. Assessment: (3,300 + ~345) / 26 weeks / 3 assessors ≈ 47 per assessor-week, about 9 per day. Legal: 1,360 / 26 / 2 ≈ 26 per reviewer-week, about 5 per day on a 2-person pool. Document: (2,108 + 1,160 + 345) / 26 / 7 ≈ 20 per reviewer-week. The scarcest pools are downstream of the change, and the 2-person legal pool has the least variability absorption. Compressing upstream span without release control therefore relocates queueing — the basis for S4's median-improves/p90-worsens signature and for S6's larger tail damage.
- Quality direction. At A, rework rose 0.6pp and appeal 0.3pp *while the mix got easier*, so the true quality effect is likely mildly negative. At B the exposure is larger: 30% of cases are rejected and 10.6% (360/3,400) enter a supplement loop, so document work performed before identity and legal triage is more often wasted or invalidated. That justifies positive rework in S1/S4/S5/S6, and negative rework in S3/S7 only because those variants add a completeness triage and legal pre-screen that attack the loop directly.
- Fidelity discount. A's overlap jump from 8% to 71% is acknowledged to be partly a recording artifact, and B's lock plus late event entry makes the same bias worse. S5 exists to quantify the most likely real-world outcome if the lock exemption is refused: a large measured effect with no real one.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

Name: conditional concurrency with statutory gating, front-loaded originals, and pull-based downstream release.

Eligibility rules.
1. Wave 1 (immediate): all cases where originals are **not** legally required and `Legal Review` is not triggered (~23% of volume; 38% × 60%). Full identity ∥ document concurrency.
2. Wave 1b (immediate, different mechanism): all cases where originals **are** legally required (62%). Identity ∥ `Originals Receipt` ∥ digital completeness triage; statutory gate preserved.
3. Wave 2 (after a clean safety read): no-originals cases that require `Legal Review` (~15%), adding a concurrent legal pre-screen.
4. Wave 3 (only if waves 1-2 show no rework increase): high-complexity originals cases with legal review.
5. Permanent exclusions from full concurrency: cases flagged high identity-risk (full `Document Check` waits for identity outcome; triage still runs), and cases already in a supplement loop (loop iterations run sequentially to avoid duplicated document effort).

Tasks that may be created or executed concurrently.
- `Identity Check` ∥ `Originals Receipt` — always, for every case where originals are required. `Originals Receipt` is requested at `Register`, never after `Identity Check`.
- `Identity Check` ∥ `Document Check` — only when originals are not legally required (or already held at registration).
- `Identity Check` ∥ `Document Intake Triage` (new, non-statutory: legibility, completeness, page count, obvious mismatch on digital copies) — always permitted.
- `Legal Pre-Screen` (new, non-binding scoping by a legal reviewer) ∥ verification — for legal-eligible cases in wave 2+.
- `Assessment` never runs concurrently with either verification task.

Statutory prerequisites that remain mandatory.
- `Register` precedes everything.
- `Document Check` cannot start (and certainly cannot complete) before `Originals Receipt` when originals are legally required. Triage is not `Document Check` and cannot satisfy rule 4.
- `Assessment` starts only when both `Identity Check` and `Document Check` are complete.
- `Legal Review` is mandatory for public-sector/high-value cases and precedes the final decision for those cases.
- `Request Supplement` may force a repeat of `Document Check` and `Assessment`; the repeat re-inherits the originals gate.
- `Notify` follows `Approve`/`Reject`.

Queue and WIP controls.
- Per-reviewer cap of 6 open verification tasks (ported from Site A) for both identity and document pools.
- Case-level cap: at most 2 concurrently active verification tasks; the "awaiting originals" state is a non-work state and does not count toward document WIP or appear in the document work queue.
- Pull-based release into `Assessment`: a system-wide cap of 12 cases in the assessment-ready queue (4 per assessor); when the cap binds, verification completion is still recorded but release is FIFO with oldest-first override above 48h.
- Legal WIP cap of 8 (4 per legal reviewer); legal pre-screen items are capped separately at 4 so pre-screening never starves final review.
- Daily 15-minute huddle covering dual-owner cases, cases awaiting originals beyond 24h, and the oldest three items in each queue. Rolled out to **all** arms so it cannot confound the concurrency contrast.

Resource responsibilities.
- Registration clerk: creates the case, sets the `originals_required` and `legal_required` flags, issues the originals request and reminder schedule at `Register`, and creates both verification tasks in the correct variant.
- Identity pool (4): owns `Identity Check`; must start within 4h of `Register` for treated cases; publishes the identity outcome as an event immediately (it is a gate for high identity-risk cases).
- Document pool (7): owns `Document Intake Triage` and `Document Check`; triage within 4h of `Register`; `Document Check` starts within 2h of `Originals Receipt`.
- Assessors (3): pull only from the released queue; may not pull unreleased cases.
- Legal pool (2): owns `Legal Pre-Screen` and `Legal Review`; a supplement request must specify exactly which documents are missing so the loop is single-pass.
- A named process owner holds the lock-exemption register and the fidelity dashboard.

Handling legal review and supplement loops.
- `Legal Pre-Screen` runs concurrently with verification and produces a documented supplement risk list; `Document Check` must clear that list before being marked complete, which is the primary mechanism for reducing the 360 supplement requests.
- On `Request Supplement`: the case re-enters `Originals Receipt` if the supplement is a physical original, otherwise `Document Check` directly; the loop iteration runs sequentially; `Assessment` is repeated; the loop is capped at 2 iterations before mandatory escalation.
- Loop iterations are excluded from concurrency treatment so that measured effects are not driven by repeat handling.

Logging requirements.
- Separate `start` and `complete` events for every activity, with resource, timestamp, **entry timestamp**, and entry source (UI, batch, back-entry).
- Explicit events: `Originals Requested`, `Originals Received`, `Assessment Released`, `Legal Pre-Screen Started/Completed`, `Identity Outcome`.
- Case attributes: `originals_required`, `legal_required`, complexity, arm, wave, treatment version, lock-exemption flag.
- Validated overlap = overlap computed only from intervals whose start events were entered within 15 minutes of occurrence. Both raw and validated overlap are reported.

Partial-order relation set (POWL-style, one admissible representation).

```text
NODES
  R    = Register
  IC   = Identity Check
  ORCV = XOR( Originals Receipt , tau )        # tau taken iff originals not legally required (data guard, not encodable)
  TRI  = Document Intake Triage
  DC   = Document Check
  ASM  = Assessment
  LEG  = XOR( LOOP( Legal Review ,
                    PO( Request Supplement -> Document Check' -> Assessment' ) ) ,
              tau )                            # tau taken iff not public-sector/high-value
  DEC  = XOR( Approve , Reject )
  NOT  = Notify

STRICT PARTIAL ORDER (top level), edges = "must precede"
  R    -> IC
  R    -> ORCV
  R    -> TRI
  ORCV -> DC
  TRI  -> DC
  IC   -> ASM
  DC   -> ASM
  ASM  -> LEG
  LEG  -> DEC
  DEC  -> NOT

UNORDERED (concurrent) PAIRS, implied by absence of edges
  IC || ORCV      IC || TRI      IC || DC      ORCV || TRI
  IC || (Legal Pre-Screen, if modelled as a node parallel to IC/DC)

TRANSITIVE CLOSURE OBLIGATIONS (preserved)
  R -> DC, R -> ASM, R -> LEG, R -> DEC, R -> NOT
  ORCV -> ASM, TRI -> ASM, IC -> DEC, DC -> DEC
  IC -> NOT, DC -> NOT, ASM -> NOT

INSIDE LOOP redo path (total order)
  Request Supplement -> Document Check' -> Assessment'
```

The edges `IC -> ASM` and `DC -> ASM` jointly encode rule 5 (both checks complete before assessment). The edge `ORCV -> DC` encodes rule 4 whenever the XOR resolves to the real `Originals Receipt`. The absence of any edge between `IC` and `DC` is exactly the intervention; the presence of `ORCV -> DC` is exactly what stops the intervention from being illegal.

How the pilot separates mechanism failure from implementation failure (≤220 words). The design makes concurrency a *measured* variable rather than an assumed one. Every treated case carries a fidelity score built from event data: were both tasks created at `Register`, did both start within their SLA, was the lock exemption used, were events entered contemporaneously, was the originals request issued at registration. That yields a two-by-two reading. High fidelity with no improvement in waiting before `Assessment` is mechanism failure: the prerequisite was genuinely removed, cases genuinely overlapped, and the critical path still did not shorten — typically because the binding constraint is elsewhere (originals delay, three assessors, two legal reviewers). Low fidelity with no improvement is implementation failure: the intervention was never executed, so the mechanism is untested and the correct response is to fix the lock, the roster or the huddle, not to abandon the idea. High fidelity with improvement in the mechanism-proximal outcome but not end-to-end is a bottleneck shift, diagnosable directly from queue-length and waiting metrics at `Assessment` and `Legal Review`. Low fidelity with apparent improvement is the measurement trap: the raw-versus-validated overlap comparison and the event entry-lag distribution expose it, since back-entered intervals inflate raw overlap without moving validated overlap. Stepped-wedge period effects and the negative-control outcome guard against attributing a site-wide trend to either cause.

## 8. PM4Py model-construction code

```python
"""
Transportability modelling: Site A before/after process trees, and a Site B
POWL candidate with conditional concurrency, statutory gating and a
legal-review / supplement loop.

Tested against the PM4Py simplified interface (pm4py.parse_process_tree,
pm4py.convert_to_petri_net, pm4py.check_is_workflow_net, pm4py.check_soundness,
pm4py.play_out) plus the POWL object model in pm4py.objects.powl.obj.
"""

import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Site A: before and after process trees
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

SITE_A_BEFORE_STR = (
    "-> ( 'Register', 'Identity Check', 'Document Check', 'Assessment', "
    "X ( 'Approve', 'Reject' ), 'Notify' )"
)

SITE_A_AFTER_STR = (
    "-> ( 'Register', + ( 'Identity Check', 'Document Check' ), 'Assessment', "
    "X ( 'Approve', 'Reject' ), 'Notify' )"
)


def build_site_a():
    tree_before = pm4py.parse_process_tree(SITE_A_BEFORE_STR)
    tree_after = pm4py.parse_process_tree(SITE_A_AFTER_STR)
    return tree_before, tree_after


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3./4. Site B candidate as a POWL model
#
#   Register
#     |-> Identity Check                    (concurrent branch 1)
#     |-> XOR(Originals Receipt, tau)       (concurrent branch 2, data-guarded)
#     |-> Document Intake Triage            (concurrent branch 3, non-statutory)
#   XOR(Originals Receipt, tau) -> Document Check     (statutory gate, rule 4)
#   Document Intake Triage      -> Document Check
#   Identity Check -> Assessment  AND  Document Check -> Assessment   (rule 5)
#   Assessment -> XOR( LOOP(Legal Review, [Request Supplement ; Document Check' ;
#                                          Assessment']), tau )       (rule 6/7)
#             -> XOR(Approve, Reject) -> Notify                       (rule 8)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

def _chain(*nodes):
    """Total order over `nodes`: a StrictPartialOrder with a single chain."""
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for source, target in zip(nodes, nodes[1:]):
        po.order.add_edge(source, target)
    return po


def build_site_b_powl():
    register = Transition(label="Register")
    identity_check = Transition(label="Identity Check")
    originals_receipt = Transition(label="Originals Receipt")
    intake_triage = Transition(label="Document Intake Triage")
    document_check = Transition(label="Document Check")
    assessment = Transition(label="Assessment")
    approve = Transition(label="Approve")
    reject = Transition(label="Reject")
    notify = Transition(label="Notify")

    # Data-dependent statutory gate: originals are legally required for 62% of
    # cases. POWL can only offer the *choice*; the guard itself is not encodable.
    originals_block = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[originals_receipt, SilentTransition()],
    )

    # Legal review with the supplement loop. LOOP(A, B) executes A, then
    # optionally B followed by A again, i.e. A (B A)*. Fresh Transition objects
    # are used for the repeated activities: a POWL node may occur only once.
    legal_review = Transition(label="Legal Review")
    request_supplement = Transition(label="Request Supplement")
    document_check_again = Transition(label="Document Check")
    assessment_again = Transition(label="Assessment")

    redo_path = _chain(request_supplement, document_check_again, assessment_again)
    legal_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[legal_review, redo_path])

    # Legal review is mandatory only for public-sector / high-value cases (40%).
    legal_block = OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.XOR,
        children=[legal_loop, SilentTransition()],
    )

    decision = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[approve, reject])

    root = StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[
            register,
            identity_check,
            originals_block,
            intake_triage,
            document_check,
            assessment,
            legal_block,
            decision,
            notify,
        ]
    )

    # Register precedes all three verification-side branches.
    root.order.add_edge(register, identity_check)
    root.order.add_edge(register, originals_block)
    root.order.add_edge(register, intake_triage)

    # Statutory gate (rule 4) and the non-statutory triage prerequisite.
    root.order.add_edge(originals_block, document_check)
    root.order.add_edge(intake_triage, document_check)

    # (4) Assessment waits for BOTH identity and document verification (rule 5).
    root.order.add_edge(identity_check, assessment)
    root.order.add_edge(document_check, assessment)

    # Legal review, decision, notification (rules 6-8).
    root.order.add_edge(assessment, legal_block)
    root.order.add_edge(legal_block, decision)
    root.order.add_edge(decision, notify)

    # NOTE: no edge between identity_check and document_check. That omission IS
    # the intervention: the two checks are unordered and may overlap. The edge
    # originals_block -> document_check is what keeps the model lawful.
    return root


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2./5. Conversions to accepting Petri nets
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

def to_accepting_petri_net(model):
    """Convert a ProcessTree or a POWL model to (net, im, fm)."""
    try:
        return pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    except Exception:
        # Fallback for PM4Py builds whose generic converter does not dispatch POWL.
        from pm4py.objects.conversion.powl.variants.to_petri_net import (
            apply as powl_to_petri_net,
        )

        return powl_to_petri_net(model)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6./7. Structural checks and playout
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

def describe(name, model):
    net, im, fm = to_accepting_petri_net(model)
    print(f"\n=== {name} ===")
    print(f"places={len(net.places)} transitions={len(net.transitions)} arcs={len(net.arcs)}")

    try:
        print("is_workflow_net:", pm4py.check_is_workflow_net(net))
    except Exception as exc:
        print("is_workflow_net check unavailable:", exc)

    try:
        soundness = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm)
        # Recent PM4Py returns (bool, diagnostics); older versions return bool.
        if isinstance(soundness, tuple):
            print("easy_soundness/soundness:", soundness[0])
        else:
            print("soundness:", soundness)
    except Exception as exc:
        print("soundness check unavailable:", exc)

    try:
        log = pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm)
        print("sample traces:")
        for trace in list(log)[:5]:
            print("  ", " -> ".join(event["concept:name"] for event in trace))
    except Exception as exc:
        print("playout unavailable:", exc)

    return net, im, fm


def main():
    tree_before, tree_after = build_site_a()
    print("Site A before:", tree_before)
    print("Site A after :", tree_after)

    describe("Site A BEFORE (sequential verification)", tree_before)
    describe("Site A AFTER (parallel verification)", tree_after)

    site_b = build_site_b_powl()
    print("\nSite B POWL candidate:", site_b)
    describe("Site B CANDIDATE (conditional concurrency + legal loop)", site_b)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 8. What these models cannot encode
#
# - Data-dependent legal requirements. Whether `Originals Receipt` is legally
#   mandatory (62% of cases) and whether `Legal Review` applies (40%) are
#   attribute guards. Both appear here as free XOR choices, so the Petri net
#   happily produces a trace that skips originals for a case that legally needs
#   them. Only a DMN/guard layer or a data Petri net can forbid that; conformance
#   checking against this model will therefore not flag statutory violations.
# - Resource capacity and roles. Nothing expresses 4 identity reviewers, 7
#   document reviewers, 3 assessors and 2 legal reviewers, and nothing expresses
#   that the pools are non-substitutable. The parallel construct promises
#   concurrency the organisation may not be able to staff, and the bottleneck
#   shift toward Assessment and Legal Review is invisible here. A queueing or
#   simulation model is required to predict cycle-time effects.
# - Case locking and WIP limits. The legacy one-active-owner lock and the
#   6-open-tasks rule are execution constraints on the *state* of the system, not
#   on the ordering of activities. An unordered pair in POWL means "may overlap",
#   never "will overlap": the lock can reduce true overlap to zero while the log
#   still conforms perfectly.
# - Durations, waiting, and distributions. The 18h median originals delay and its
#   heavy tail, the 3.4h/8.1h service medians, and the p90 behaviour that drives
#   the business case are all outside the model. Control flow cannot distinguish
#   a 1h wait from a 100h wait.
# - Loop multiplicity and economics. The LOOP allows unbounded supplement
#   iterations; the real policy caps them at two and the cost of each iteration
#   (re-consuming document, assessment and legal capacity) is not represented.
# - Measurement quality. Back-entered timestamps, which are the main threat to
#   the overlap evidence at both sites, are a property of the log, not of the
#   model; the model cannot tell recorded concurrency from real concurrency.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
```

## 9. Recommendation

`conditional go`

Transfer the *mechanism*, not the model edit. Site A showed that a verification prerequisite existing only in the model — not in law or data — can be deleted, and that the resulting span compression appears as reduced waiting before `Assessment` (18h to 12h). That reasoning transfers, along with the companions that made it work: the six-open-task cap, the daily huddle, and disciplined start/complete logging. At Site B the highest-value application is not the one proposed. For the 62% originals cohort the removable serial edge is `Identity Check -> Originals Receipt`: about 1,020 cases begin originals collection only after identity finishes, in front of an 18h heavy-tailed delay. Requesting originals at `Register` plus a lawful digital completeness triage attacks both median and tail.

Do not copy the literal proposal of creating both checks simultaneously for every case. For the 62% with a statutory originals requirement that creates a document task which cannot lawfully start, inflating WIP and manufacturing fake overlap. Nor copy A's 9h median gain as an expectation: it is confounded by +11% FTE (per-FTE completions fell from 266.7 to 260.0), an 8-point favourable mix shift, a registry API upgrade, huddles, and better timestamp recording. Pure overlap arithmetic at B caps the critical-path saving near 3.4h.

Entering first: the no-originals, no-legal cohort (~23%) for full concurrency, plus the originals cohort (62%) for front-loading. High-complexity legal cases enter last.

Expected benefit from the staged package is median 44h to roughly 36-40h and p90 110h to roughly 95-105h, with rework falling 1-3 points if pre-screening reduces the 360 supplement requests. Risks are a bottleneck shift onto three assessors and two legal reviewers (median improves while p90 worsens 5-15h), wasted document effort on the ~30% of cases ending in rejection, and the case lock reducing everything to back-entered timestamps.

Expand on: validated overlap above 50%, waiting before `Assessment` down at least 20% against matched controls, no deterioration in rework, appeals or queue aging. Modify if the mechanism-proximal outcome improves without end-to-end gain (add release control where the queue moved) or if fidelity is below threshold. Abandon on high fidelity with no waiting reduction across two periods, or a persistent stop-rule breach.

## 10. Assumption register

1. Statutory ordering is genuinely binding for the 62% of cases requiring originals — why needed: it determines that identity ∥ `Document Check` is void for the majority segment and that only identity ∥ `Originals Receipt` is available — how to test: legal review of the requirement text plus an audit of whether any case lawfully completed `Document Check` before receipt; check the DFG for `Register -> Document Check` arcs among flagged cases — consequence if false: the addressable segment grows from 38% to near 100% and the optimistic scenario becomes credible, so the pilot's eligibility rules should be widened.

2. `Originals Receipt` can be initiated at `Register` for essentially all originals-required cases — why needed: the whole S3/S7 gain rests on moving the start of an 18h heavy-tailed clock earlier — how to test: sample the 1,020 `Identity Check -> Originals Receipt` cases and ask why receipt started late; run a two-week non-randomised trial of request-at-registration and measure receipt-delay percentiles — consequence if false: the largest expected median and p90 improvements disappear and the package reduces to the modest digital-only effect, which would not justify the change-management cost alone.

3. Site A's 9h median reduction is materially confounded, with a parallelization-only residual of roughly 3-5h — why needed: it sets the ceiling for every Site B forecast and prevents over-promising — how to test: reconstruct A's log and estimate a mix- and capacity-adjusted effect (stratify by complexity, condition on FTE-period, use the API deployment date as a break) — consequence if false: if the effect really is ~9h and structural, expectations at B should rise, though the statutory and capacity constraints at B still cap the transfer.

4. A pilot-scoped exemption from the one-active-owner case lock is obtainable, or a sanctioned dual-owner workaround with contemporaneous event entry is — why needed: without it, the mechanism cannot be executed and the pilot measures only recording behaviour (scenario S5) — how to test: ask IT for a scoped configuration change; measure the share of events entered within 15 minutes and compare raw against validated overlap in a two-week dry run — consequence if false: the concurrency arms are untestable; restrict the pilot to originals front-loading, triage, WIP caps and huddles, which do not require dual ownership.

5. Reported service medians (identity 3.4h, document 8.1h) are elapsed activity durations that contain substantial intra-task idleness rather than touch time — why needed: the arithmetic showing that 3,400 identity checks cannot fit into 4 reviewers' hours implies utilisation and capacity conclusions that drive the bottleneck analysis — how to test: time-and-motion sample or system-active-time telemetry on 50 cases per pool — consequence if false: if these are true touch times, all four pools are catastrophically over-subscribed, queueing dominates everything, and the correct intervention is capacity, not concurrency.

6. `Assessment` (3 assessors) and `Legal Review` (2 reviewers) are the binding downstream constraints — why needed: it drives the bottleneck-shift scenario, the pull-based release design, and the tail-risk stop rules — how to test: compute per-pool utilisation and queue lengths from event data; check waiting time immediately before `Assessment` and before `Legal Review`; run a short queueing simulation with compressed verification span — consequence if false: if the assessors have slack, the tail-worsening scenario is over-weighted, the release cap is unnecessary friction, and expected gains should be revised upward.

7. Identity and document evidence are substantially independent at Site B, as they are at Site A — why needed: independence is what makes concurrent execution non-wasteful and keeps rework flat — how to test: measure the conditional rate at which document findings change after an identity failure, and the share of document work on cases later rejected on identity grounds; compare complex and simple strata — consequence if false: parallel document work is systematically wasted or must be redone, rework rises, and the design must gate full `Document Check` on the identity outcome for far more cases than just the flagged cohort.

8. Intake streams can be defined so that stepped-wedge assignment is feasible and cross-arm resource interference is containable — why needed: it is the basis for identification; without it the pilot repeats Site A's uncontrolled before/after design — how to test: check baseline comparability of candidate streams on originals, legal, complexity and cycle-time distributions; verify from resource-level analysis that reviewers can be rostered to one arm per shift — consequence if false: fall back to a within-stratum, time-randomised design with explicit period effects and a negative-control outcome, accept lower precision, and state plainly that the resulting transport estimate is weaker.
