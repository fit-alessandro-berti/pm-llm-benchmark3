# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1. **Tail latency can worsen while the mean improves.** Simple cases may close faster due to automation and standardized approvals, pulling the average down, while high-complexity or Legal/Risk cases wait much longer in central queues. P90 and P95 cycle time matter more than average cycle time for this risk.

2. **High-complexity cases can be sacrificed by pooling.** The proposal explicitly routes all high-complexity work to Technical Shared Service. If that queue is loaded with technical advice, legal/risk triage, security, and difficult Quality Check, complex cases may experience longer waits even if ordinary cases improve.

3. **East can improve while North/South deteriorate.** East currently has slower cycle times, higher rework, higher SLA misses, and E_Tech utilization of 82%. Pooling may relieve East. But North and South currently have strong local expertise and stable loops; they may lose more than they gain. Aggregated results can hide this team-level redistribution.

4. **Loss of local expertise may appear as later rework, not immediate delay.** Local technical advice currently prevents unnecessary referrals and reduces rework in complex regional cases. After centralization, the first visible symptom may be more Quality Check failures, more Rework events, more Local Customer Contact loops, or more missing-context handovers—not necessarily a higher average cycle time.

5. **Fairness can worsen under uniform routing rules.** Equal access to a shared queue is not the same as equal service. Customers needing language, regional accommodation, or segment-specific handling may face extra clarification loops. Track fairness by `customer_segment`, `location`, `local_expertise_flag`, `priority`, and `case_complexity`.

6. **Learning erosion is slow and easy to miss.** Local staff may stop developing technical skill if all hard cases are routed away. Short-term throughput may improve while the organization loses future resilience. This should be monitored through resource similarity, role discovery, and the share of technical assessment work performed locally.

7. **Accountability can degrade even when handovers look cleaner.** A formal shared queue can make ownership less visible. Cases may wait because local staff assume shared services own the next step, while shared services assume local caseworkers own follow-up. Rising `reassignment_count`, advice pickup delay, and handover reasons such as “clarification” or “missing information” are key signals.

8. **Exception handling may become brittle.** The current local model has redundant embedded knowledge. The proposed model reduces local redundancy but concentrates specialist dependencies. Absence, illness, weekly peaks, or urgent legal/risk spikes can create organization-wide queues.

9. **Higher utilization can be mistaken for higher efficiency.** A 15% utilization increase sounds attractive, but specialists already near 90%+ utilization have little slack. In queueing terms, when variability exists, very high utilization often produces rapidly increasing waits.

10. **Nominal handover reduction may be a measurement artifact.** Cross-team handovers may fall, but shared-service handovers may rise. If central queues are represented by generic resources or activities, the model may undercount the true coordination load.

# 5. Pilot design

**Pilot scope.** Do not move all shared functions at once. Start with a controlled pilot for **Technical Shared Service on selected high-complexity cases**, preferably including East because East has the clearest bottleneck-relief hypothesis. Keep Legal/Risk capacity protected and avoid adding difficult Quality Check to the same queue in the first phase unless separate capacity is assigned. Shared approval should be piloted as a shadow or limited-scope queue for medium/high-value cases rather than a full supervisor removal from day one.

**Comparison group.** Use a staggered or matched design:

* East high-complexity cases routed through Technical Shared Service as the main pilot group.
* Matched North/South high-complexity cases retained under local embedded handling as comparison.
* A small randomized or rules-based North/South subset can be included only if volumes are safe, so the effect is not confounded with East’s baseline overload.
* Keep low-complexity cases mostly unchanged to preserve a stable baseline.

**Pre/post observation window.**

* Use at least **8–12 weeks pre-change** to establish baseline handovers, waits, variants, rework, and P90/P95 cycle time.
* Treat the first **2–4 weeks after launch** as a ramp-in period for leading indicators but avoid overinterpreting mature outcomes.
* Use **8–12 weeks post-ramp** for lagging indicators such as rework, SLA misses, and customer satisfaction.
* Review leading indicators weekly from the first week.

**Required segmentation.**

Every dashboard should be sliced by:

* `team`: North, South, East.
* `location`.
* `case_complexity`: low, medium, high.
* `required_specialty`: technical, legal, risk, security, none/other.
* `customer_segment`.
* `priority`.
* `local_expertise_flag`.
* Exposure to shared queue: no shared service, Technical Shared Service only, Legal/Risk shared queue, shared approval queue, multiple shared queues.

**Guardrail metrics.**

Minimum guardrails should include:

* Technical Shared Service median and P90 wait after handover.
* Legal/Risk median and P90 wait.
* Shared approval median and P90 wait.
* Queue age and backlog growth by queue.
* P90/P95 cycle time by team and complexity.
* SLA miss rate by team, complexity, priority, and customer segment.
* Rework rate by activity, especially after Technical Assessment, Specialist Review, Quality Check, and Approval.
* `reassignment_count` and handover reasons indicating missing context, wrong queue, or unclear ownership.
* Customer satisfaction by location/customer segment.
* Local learning proxy: share of technical work still performed by local caseworkers.

**Staff feedback collection.**

Combine log-based analysis with weekly structured feedback:

* Local caseworkers: whether advice was timely, actionable, and context-aware.
* Shared specialists: whether incoming notes are complete and correctly routed.
* Supervisors/approvers: whether accountability is clear.
* Customer-facing staff: whether regional or language accommodations are being lost.
* Short coded feedback should be mapped to log fields such as `handover_reason`, `local_expertise_flag`, and `rework_flag`.

**Rollback or adjustment triggers.**

Trigger adjustment, capacity increase, or partial rollback if any of the following persist for two review cycles:

* Technical Shared Service median wait exceeds management estimate by more than about 50%, or P90 queue age keeps growing.
* Legal/Risk backlog grows week over week, especially because those specialists are already near capacity.
* High-complexity P90 cycle time worsens by more than 10–15% versus matched baseline.
* SLA miss rate rises by more than 1–2 percentage points in any team or protected customer segment.
* Rework rises by more than 2–3 percentage points after shared-service advice or Quality Check.
* North/South performance deteriorates while aggregate performance appears stable.
* `reassignment_count` or missing-context handover reasons rise materially.
* Staff feedback repeatedly identifies unclear ownership or loss of local customer knowledge.

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: pilot first, with modifications before rollout.** The proposal is plausible but not safe for immediate full deployment. It has a strong case for helping East and for standardizing specialist decisions, but it also concentrates work into queues that may already be capacity-constrained, especially Legal/Risk. The management estimate should be treated as a hypothesis, not as a forecast guarantee.

The safest version is a phased model:

* Pool technical capacity first where the benefit is clearest, especially East and clearly high-complexity cases.
* Keep Legal/Risk and difficult Quality Check from being absorbed into the same undifferentiated bottleneck unless capacity and triage rules are explicit.
* Retain some local technical consultation capacity for North/South, at least during the pilot.
* Define case ownership explicitly: local caseworker owns the case; shared service owns advice by a target time; approval queue owns decision quality and turnaround.
* Standardize case notes before scaling, because inconsistent notes are already known to make cross-team cover perform poorly.
* Monitor P90/P95, queue aging, rework, fairness, and learning—not only average cycle time.

Management should **not** infer success from early average cycle-time improvement alone. Early averages may improve because simple cases close faster, because incomplete post-change cases have not yet matured, or because hard cases are still sitting in queues. Management also should not interpret fewer cross-team handovers as proof of less coordination burden; coordination may simply have moved into shared-service queues.

Proceed only if the pilot shows that shared-service waits remain controlled, East improves without material North/South harm, high-complexity tails do not worsen, rework does not rise, and fairness gaps across customer segments remain acceptable.

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

```python
"""
PM4Py-style sketch for comparing pre/post restructuring.

Assumptions:
- raw_df contains the event-log columns listed in the prompt.
- change_ts is the restructuring start timestamp.
- If SLA deadline is not in the event log, join a case-level deadline table or
  derive SLA miss from outcome if the organization encodes it there.
"""

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

CASE = "case:concept:name"
ACT = "concept:name"
TIME = "time:timestamp"
RES = "org:resource"

SEGMENT_COLS = [
    "team",
    "location",
    "case_complexity",
    "required_specialty",
    "priority",
    "customer_segment",
    "local_expertise_flag",
]

def prepare_log(raw_df: pd.DataFrame, change_ts) -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = raw_df.copy()
    df[TIME] = pd.to_datetime(df[TIME], utc=True, errors="coerce")
    df = df.dropna(subset=[CASE, ACT, TIME, RES])
    df = df.sort_values([CASE, TIME])

    change_ts = pd.Timestamp(change_ts)
    if change_ts.tzinfo is None:
        change_ts = change_ts.tz_localize("UTC")

    df["period"] = np.where(df[TIME] < change_ts, "pre", "post")

    # PM4Py formatting. Depending on PM4Py version, the original column names may
    # be retained and standardized columns may be added.
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id=CASE,
        activity_key=ACT,
        timestamp_key=TIME,
    )
    return df


def to_event_log(df: pd.DataFrame):
    return pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)


def discover_networks(df: pd.DataFrame) -> dict:
    """
    Run core organizational and process discovery for a segment.
    """
    log = to_event_log(df)

    result = {}
    result["handover_of_work"] = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log)
    result["working_together"] = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)
    result["subcontracting"] = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(log)

    result["resource_similarity"] = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log)
    result["organizational_roles"] = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log)

    result["dfg"] = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    result["performance_dfg"] = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)

    result["variants"] = pm4py.get_variants(log)
    return result


def add_next_event_handover_features(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Directly compute handover edges and waiting times from the event table.
    This complements PM4Py network discovery and makes segment-level summaries easy.
    """
    x = df.sort_values([CASE, TIME]).copy()

    for col in [TIME, ACT, RES, "team", "location"]:
        x[f"next_{col}"] = x.groupby(CASE)[col].shift(-1)

    x["next_time"] = x[f"next_{TIME}"]
    x["next_activity"] = x[f"next_{ACT}"]
    x["next_resource"] = x[f"next_{RES}"]

    x["wait_to_next_event_h"] = (
        (x["next_time"] - x[TIME]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600.0
    )

    x["is_handover"] = (
        x["next_resource"].notna()
        & x[RES].notna()
        & (x[RES] != x["next_resource"])
    )

    x["resource_handover"] = x[RES].astype(str) + " -> " + x["next_resource"].astype(str)
    x["activity_handover"] = x[ACT].astype(str) + " -> " + x["next_activity"].astype(str)

    # Useful organizational categories; adapt names to real post-change labels.
    shared_terms = (
        "Technical Shared Service|Specialist Review|Legal|Risk|Security|"
        "shared approval|Shared Approval|Quality Check"
    )
    x["next_is_shared_queue"] = x["next_activity"].astype(str).str.contains(
        shared_terms, case=False, regex=True, na=False
    )

    # Future rework after each event, as a handover-level quality proxy.
    is_rework_event = (
        x[ACT].astype(str).str.contains("Rework", case=False, na=False)
        | x.get("rework_flag", False).astype(str).str.lower().isin(["true", "1", "yes"])
    )
    x["is_rework_event"] = is_rework_event

    def future_rework(s: pd.Series) -> pd.Series:
        # True if any later event in the same case is rework.
        future = s.iloc[::-1].cummax().iloc[::-1]
        return future.shift(-1, fill_value=False)

    x["future_rework_after_event"] = (
        x.groupby(CASE)["is_rework_event"].transform(future_rework).astype(bool)
    )

    return x


def handover_summary(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    x = add_next_event_handover_features(df)
    h = x[x["is_handover"]].copy()

    # Count cases for per-100 normalization.
    case_counts = (
        df.groupby(["period"])[CASE]
        .nunique()
        .rename("cases")
        .reset_index()
    )

    summary = (
        h.groupby(["period", "activity_handover"], dropna=False)
        .agg(
            handovers=("activity_handover", "size"),
            median_wait_h=("wait_to_next_event_h", "median"),
            p90_wait_h=("wait_to_next_event_h", lambda s: s.quantile(0.90)),
            rework_after_handover_rate=("future_rework_after_event", "mean"),
            mean_reassignment_count=("reassignment_count", "mean"),
        )
        .reset_index()
        .merge(case_counts, on="period", how="left")
    )

    summary["handovers_per_100_cases"] = 100.0 * summary["handovers"] / summary["cases"]
    return summary.sort_values(["period", "handovers_per_100_cases"], ascending=[True, False])


def segmented_handover_summary(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    x = add_next_event_handover_features(df)
    h = x[x["is_handover"]].copy()

    group_cols = ["period", "team", "case_complexity", "required_specialty", "customer_segment",
                  "activity_handover"]

    summary = (
        h.groupby(group_cols, dropna=False)
        .agg(
            handovers=("activity_handover", "size"),
            median_wait_h=("wait_to_next_event_h", "median"),
            p90_wait_h=("wait_to_next_event_h", lambda s: s.quantile(0.90)),
            rework_after_handover_rate=("future_rework_after_event", "mean"),
            mean_reassignment_count=("reassignment_count", "mean"),
        )
        .reset_index()
    )
    return summary


def case_level_metrics(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    x = df.sort_values([CASE, TIME]).copy()

    first = x.groupby(CASE).first()
    last = x.groupby(CASE).last()

    cases = pd.DataFrame(index=first.index)
    cases["period"] = first["period"]
    cases["start_time"] = first[TIME]
    cases["end_time"] = last[TIME]
    cases["cycle_time_d"] = (
        (cases["end_time"] - cases["start_time"]).dt.total_seconds() / 86400.0
    )

    # Carry common case attributes from the first event.
    for col in SEGMENT_COLS:
        if col in x.columns:
            cases[col] = first[col]

    # Rework at any point in the case.
    if "rework_flag" in x.columns:
        cases["has_rework"] = (
            x.groupby(CASE)["rework_flag"]
            .apply(lambda s: s.astype(str).str.lower().isin(["true", "1", "yes"]).any())
        )
    else:
        cases["has_rework"] = x.groupby(CASE)[ACT].apply(
            lambda s: s.astype(str).str.contains("Rework", case=False, na=False).any()
        )

    # Outcome.
    if "outcome" in x.columns:
        cases["outcome"] = last["outcome"]

    # SLA miss. Prefer explicit deadline; otherwise fall back to outcome coding if used.
    if "sla_deadline" in x.columns:
        deadline = pd.to_datetime(last["sla_deadline"], utc=True, errors="coerce")
        cases["sla_miss"] = cases["end_time"] > deadline
    elif "outcome" in x.columns:
        cases["sla_miss"] = (
            cases["outcome"]
            .astype(str)
            .str.contains("sla_miss|breach|late|miss", case=False, regex=True, na=False)
        )
    else:
        cases["sla_miss"] = np.nan

    return cases.reset_index()


def case_performance_summary(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    cases = case_level_metrics(df)

    group_cols = ["period", "team", "case_complexity", "required_specialty", "customer_segment"]

    summary = (
        cases.groupby(group_cols, dropna=False)
        .agg(
            cases=(CASE, "nunique"),
            median_cycle_d=("cycle_time_d", "median"),
            p90_cycle_d=("cycle_time_d", lambda s: s.quantile(0.90)),
            p95_cycle_d=("cycle_time_d", lambda s: s.quantile(0.95)),
            rework_rate=("has_rework", "mean"),
            sla_miss_rate=("sla_miss", "mean"),
        )
        .reset_index()
    )
    return summary


def rework_by_activity(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """
    PM4Py rework measure plus a simple fallback from variants.
    """
    log = to_event_log(df)

    try:
        rework_pm4py = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(log)
    except Exception:
        rework_pm4py = None

    # Fallback: count cases where an activity occurs more than once.
    counts = (
        df.groupby([CASE, ACT])
        .size()
        .rename("activity_count")
        .reset_index()
    )
    repeated = counts[counts["activity_count"] > 1]
    fallback = (
        repeated.groupby(ACT)[CASE]
        .nunique()
        .rename("cases_with_rework_like_repeat")
        .reset_index()
    )

    return rework_pm4py, fallback


def filter_segment_pm4py_style(df: pd.DataFrame,
                               team=None,
                               complexity=None,
                               specialty=None,
                               customer_segment=None):
    """
    Use pandas for transparent segmentation; PM4Py filters can also be used
    when working directly with event logs.
    """
    f = df.copy()

    if team is not None:
        f = f[f["team"].isin(np.atleast_1d(team))]
    if complexity is not None:
        f = f[f["case_complexity"].isin(np.atleast_1d(complexity))]
    if specialty is not None:
        f = f[f["required_specialty"].isin(np.atleast_1d(specialty))]
    if customer_segment is not None:
        f = f[f["customer_segment"].isin(np.atleast_1d(customer_segment))]

    return f


def variant_and_dfg_analysis(df: pd.DataFrame) -> dict:
    """
    Compare variants and DFG/performance DFG before and after.
    """
    out = {}
    for period, seg in df.groupby("period"):
        log = to_event_log(seg)

        variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)
        dfg = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
        perf_dfg = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)

        # Example: filter dominant variants if needed.
        # top_variants = sorted(variants.items(), key=lambda kv: len(kv[1]), reverse=True)[:10]
        # filtered_log = pm4py.filter_variants(log, [v for v, cases in top_variants])

        out[period] = {
            "variants": variants,
            "dfg": dfg,
            "performance_dfg": perf_dfg,
        }
    return out


def service_waiting_time_analysis(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """
    PM4Py service/waiting-time analysis plus custom next-event waits.
    """
    log = to_event_log(df)

    # PM4Py waiting/service time enrichment.
    try:
        enriched = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log)
    except Exception:
        enriched = None

    # Activity service times.
    service_times = {}
    for act in sorted(df[ACT].dropna().unique()):
        try:
            service_times[act] = pm4py.get_service_time(log, activity=act)
        except Exception:
            pass

    # Custom waiting-time proxy after each completed event.
    wait_proxy = add_next_event_handover_features(df)

    wait_summary = (
        wait_proxy.groupby(["period", ACT], dropna=False)
        .agg(
            median_wait_to_next_h=("wait_to_next_event_h", "median"),
            p90_wait_to_next_h=("wait_to_next_event_h", lambda s: s.quantile(0.90)),
        )
        .reset_index()
    )

    return enriched, service_times, wait_summary


def temporal_feature_analysis(df: pd.DataFrame):
    """
    Detect day-of-week/hour bottlenecks, queue peaks, and absence sensitivity.
    """
    try:
        temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(df)
    except Exception:
        temporal = df.copy()
        temporal["hour"] = temporal[TIME].dt.hour
        temporal["dayofweek"] = temporal[TIME].dt.dayofweek

    return temporal


def full_pre_post_analysis(raw_df: pd.DataFrame, change_ts) -> dict:
    df = prepare_log(raw_df, change_ts)

    results = {}

    # Overall pre/post networks.
    results["networks"] = {
        period: discover_networks(seg)
        for period, seg in df.groupby("period")
    }

    # Team-specific networks show whether East benefits differently from North/South.
    results["team_networks"] = {}
    for (period, team), seg in df.groupby(["period", "team"]):
        results["team_networks"][(period, team)] = discover_networks(seg)

    # Handover and queue measures.
    results["handover_summary"] = handover_summary(df)
    results["segmented_handover_summary"] = segmented_handover_summary(df)

    # Variants and performance DFG.
    results["variants_dfg"] = variant_and_dfg_analysis(df)

    # Service/waiting times.
    results["service_waiting"] = service_waiting_time_analysis(df)

    # Rework and SLA/cycle-time outcomes.
    results["case_metrics"] = case_level_metrics(df)
    results["case_performance_summary"] = case_performance_summary(df)
    results["rework_by_activity"] = rework_by_activity(df)

    # Temporal features for weekly peaks, queue aging, and staffing effects.
    results["temporal_features"] = temporal_feature_analysis(df)

    # Example focused slices.
    results["east_high_complexity"] = case_performance_summary(
        filter_segment_pm4py_style(df, team="East", complexity="high")
    )
    results["north_south_high_complexity"] = case_performance_summary(
        filter_segment_pm4py_style(df, team=["North", "South"], complexity="high")
    )
    results["legal_risk_security"] = case_performance_summary(
        filter_segment_pm4py_style(df, specialty=["legal", "risk", "security"])
    )

    return results
```
