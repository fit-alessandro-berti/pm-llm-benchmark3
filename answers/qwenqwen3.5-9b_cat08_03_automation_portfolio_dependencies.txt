# 2. Dependency and Prerequisite Map

The automation candidates are not independent; they form a chain where upstream data quality dictates downstream automation success.

*   **Upstream Data Quality Dependencies:**
    *   **A1 (Registration)** feeds **A2 (Classification)**. If A1 errors, A2 misroutes cases, increasing **A5 (Missing Docs)** and **A6 (Normalization)** workload.
    *   **A5 (Missing Doc Nudge)** is a prerequisite for **A6 (Normalization)**. Financial statements cannot be normalized if the customer does not upload the documents.
    *   **A6 (Normalization)** is a prerequisite for **A2 (Classification)** and **A8 (Committee Packaging)**. Credit analysts cannot analyze without normalized data.
    *   **A12 (Remediation)** is a prerequisite for **A9 (Contract Generation)**. Contracts cannot be generated safely if upstream control issues (e.g., KYC mismatch) are unresolved.

*   **Legal/Compliance Controls:**
    *   **A9 (Contract)** must enforce the Four-Eyes principle (Credit Analysis vs. Committee Packaging). If **A12** is not active, **A9** risks generating contracts that violate High-exposure SME rules (5.8% violation rate).
    *   **A3 (KYC)** must precede **A10 (E-sign)**. **A10** cannot legally occur before **A3** clearance.

*   **Human Escalation Capacity:**
    *   **A3 (KYC)** and **A6 (Normalization)** increase the *quality* of inflow to Credit Analysts (9 FTE). If upstream data is poor, analysts must spend time re-checking, negating the benefit of automation.
    *   **A4 (Entity Mismatch)** requires Compliance Specialists (3 FTE). Automation must explicitly flag exceptions for these 3 specialists rather than bypassing them entirely.

*   **External Valuation Dependency:**
    *   **A7 (Collateral Triage)** depends on external valuers. It cannot reduce the 58h wait for **Wait for external valuation** significantly without triaging cases to lower-risk products (where valuation is not required).

*   **Core-Banking Integration Constraints:**
    *   **A1 (Registration)**, **A5 (Nudge)**, **A10 (E-sign)** are low-dependency (Portal/External) and can be integrated quickly.
    *   **A6 (Norm)**, **A9 (Contract)**, **A11 (Activation)** require deep integration with Core Banking for document storage, contract enforcement, and ledger activation. This counts toward the 2-module limit in the first six months.

# 7. Final Recommendation

**Portfolio:** **Hybrid Bottleneck & Compliance (A5, A12, A6, A9)**

**Why:**
Management's initial desire to automate the highest-frequency activity (**A1. Register Application**) is a trap. While **A1** has "Very High" feasibility and high frequency, process-mining data shows it sits at the top of a queue where the *real* delays occur downstream (KYC, Docs, Valuation). Automating **A1** without fixing **A5** (Missing Docs) or **A6** (Normalization) simply moves the bottleneck from Registration to the Back Office, increasing the load on the 12 FTE Back Office team who are already underutilized after 15:00.

**The Trade-Off Argument:**
We are sacrificing the immediate political "win" of **A1** (Volume) for the operational "win" of **A5/A6** (Quality/Speed).
1.  **Resource Demand:** Automating **A6** (Normalization) directly supports the 9 FTE Credit Analysts, who are the central resource network. If we automate **A1** but not **A6**, we increase the volume of *low-quality* work for analysts, causing burnout.
2.  **Control Risks:** Compliance mandates a control-strengthening module. **A12 (Control Remediation)** is the only candidate that directly addresses the 14% contract-remediation and 5.8% Four-Eyes violations. Automating **A9 (Contract Gen)** without **A12** creates legal risk.
3.  **Integration:** **A6** and **A9** are the only modules requiring deep core integration. By scheduling them in Phase 2 (Months 7-12), we adhere to the IT constraint of two deep integrations in the first six months, ensuring stability for the initial rollout.

**Conclusion:**
Prioritizing **A5, A12, A6, A9** ensures that when the automation goes live, it processes *cleaner* cases, respects *control* boundaries, and reduces the *longest* waits (Valuation/Docs) rather than just counting transactions. This approach aligns with the process-mining evidence that frequency is misleading and that synchronization delays dominate end-to-end performance.
