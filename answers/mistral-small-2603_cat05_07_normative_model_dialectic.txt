## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### Stipulated Facts
1. **Normative Model (M0)**: Defines a strict sequential flow with concurrent Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review, exclusive decision between approved (Manual_Procurement_Approval  Activate_Supplier) and Reject_Supplier, followed by Notify_Requester and Close_Case.
2. **Compliance Standard (CS-9)**: Requires Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review before activation, Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier, and distinct requester/approver humans. Does not address rejected leads or reactivations.
3. **Pilot Decision (PD-3)**: Temporary (2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31) allowing Auto_Approve for LOW-risk DE suppliers in approved corporate groups, with Sanctions_Check before activation and Quality_Review within 2 business days post-activation.
4. **Emergency Directive (FD-7)**: Temporary (2026-07-10 to 2026-07-12) allowing provisional activation before Quality_Review/Manual_Procurement_Approval for critical suppliers, with Sanctions_Check before activation and retrospective approval required.
5. **Data-Collection Change (N4)**: Notify_Requester moved to CRM on 2026-07-01; 70% of notifications are mirrored into onboarding logs with mutable text-field links for older cases.
6. **Stakeholder Practice (N5)**: Operations frequently uses Request_More_Info loops; Compliance considers this desirable but M0 lacks a loop construct. Operations also desires permanent Auto_Approve for low-risk suppliers.
7. **Observed Cases**: 15 cases show deviations from M0, including pilot/emergency usage, missing notifications, resource conflicts, reactivations, and label changes. Aggregate data shows 312 Auto_Approve cases (289 eligible, 14 ineligible, 9 missing eligibility), 47 Request_More_Info cases, and 29% notification missingness post-CRM migration.

### Disputed Propositions
1. **Scope of M0**:
   - *Prosecution*: M0 applies to all supplier onboarding cases, including rejected leads and reactivations.
   - *Defense*: M0 only applies to cases after pre-screen acceptance; rejected leads and reactivations are out of scope.
2. **Authority of Exceptions**:
   - *Prosecution*: PD-3 and FD-7 are temporary and do not amend CS-9; deviations from M0/CS-9 are noncompliant.
   - *Defense*: PD-3 and FD-7 are valid operational overlays; M0 is obsolete and should accommodate them.
3. **Resource Rules**:
   - *Prosecution*: M0’s lack of resource constraints (e.g., requester/approver identity) means cases like SUP-712 and SUP-715 violate CS-9.
   - *Defense*: CS-9’s resource rule is not represented in M0; its absence does not imply noncompliance.
4. **Data Quality**:
   - *Prosecution*: Missing notifications (e.g., SUP-706) and mutable text fields (e.g., SUP-702) undermine evidence of compliance.
   - *Defense*: CRM mirroring and stable notification IDs (where present) suffice for compliance; mutable fields are a logging issue, not a process violation.
5. **Label Equivalence**:
   - *Prosecution*: Renaming Quality_Review to QA_Clearance (SUP-713) violates CS-9’s requirement for Quality_Review.
   - *Defense*: The mapping was approved; the activity is functionally equivalent.
6. **Permanent Normalization**:
   - *Prosecution*: Post-pilot Auto_Approve usage (e.g., SUP-714) is noncompliant as no permanent policy amendment exists.
   - *Defense*: Operations has normalized the pilot flow; Compliance’s inaction implies acceptance.

### Burden of Proof
- **Noncompliance**: The prosecution must prove that a deviation from M0/CS-9 is not justified by a valid exception (pilot/emergency), scope exclusion, or data/logging defect. Evidence must show:
  - The case falls within M0/CS-9’s scope.
  - The deviation is not covered by PD-3/FD-7 or other formal overlays.
  - The deviation poses material risk (e.g., sanctions bypass, resource conflicts).
- **Model/Scoping Defect**: The defense must prove that M0’s structure or scope is inconsistent with:
  - Higher-authority documents (CS-9, PD-3, FD-7).
  - Observed operational needs (Request_More_Info, Auto_Approve).
  - Data limitations (CRM migration, mutable fields).
  - Scope ambiguities (rejected leads, reactivations).

## 2. Prosecution Brief

### Core Argument
The operation is **materially noncompliant** with CS-9 and M0 due to systemic deviations, unauthorized exceptions, and unaddressed risks. M0’s control-flow model is the normative standard, and CS-9’s requirements are unambiguous. Temporary directives (PD-3, FD-7) do not amend CS-9; they are exceptions requiring strict adherence to their terms and immediate reversion afterward. Stakeholder practices (e.g., Request_More_Info, permanent Auto_Approve) lack formal authorization and normalize noncompliance.

### Key Violations
1. **Unauthorized Auto_Approve**:
   - **SUP-702, SUP-703, SUP-714**: PD-3 expired on 2026-07-31, yet Auto_Approve was used in August (SUP-714). PD-3 explicitly states it is a monitored exception; its expiry should have reverted operations to M0/CS-9.
   - **SUP-703**: Used Auto_Approve for a HIGH-risk supplier ineligible under PD-3 (spend EUR 180,000 vs. EUR 20,000 cap). This is a severe sanctions and financial risk.
   - **Aggregate**: 14 ineligible Auto_Approve cases and 9 with missing eligibility attributes demonstrate systemic noncompliance. Operations’ normalization of the pilot flow ignores CS-9’s resource and risk requirements.

2. **Emergency Directive Violations**:
   - **FD-7 Scope**: SUP-705 occurred after FD-7’s expiry (2026-07-13) but Operations claims the flood emergency "factually existed." No written extension exists; this is a post-hoc justification. The spend cap (EUR 20,000) was exceeded in SUP-704 (EUR 18,000) and SUP-705 (EUR 12,000), but the directive required retrospective approval within two business days. No evidence of retrospective approval is provided.
   - **SUP-708**: Activated before Sanctions_Check (40 minutes) with no emergency/pilot justification. This violates CS-9’s requirement for Sanctions_Check before activation.

3. **Missing Notifications**:
   - **SUP-706**: No Notify_Requester event in the onboarding log. CRM contains a message, but the link is a mutable text field entered two days later. This undermines evidence of compliance with CS-9’s notification requirement.
   - **Aggregate**: Notification missingness rose to 29% post-CRM migration, indicating systemic data-quality failures that obscure compliance.

4. **Resource and Conflict Violations**:
   - **SUP-712**: Requester and manual approver are the same human (via aliases). CS-9 explicitly requires distinct humans.
   - **SUP-715**: Quality reviewer and manual approver are spouses in the same reporting line. No conflict-of-interest disclosure was recorded, violating CS-9’s policy.

5. **Scope Violations**:
   - **SUP-709**: Rejected lead with no Quality_Review. Model owner argues M0 begins after pre-screen acceptance, but CS-9 states "every supplier onboarding requires Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review before activation." Rejected leads are part of onboarding and must comply.
   - **SUP-710**: Reactivation lacks Register_Supplier and Quality_Review. CS-9 has no explicit reactivation procedure, but the event log’s omission of required steps violates the spirit of CS-9.

6. **Label and Data Integrity**:
   - **SUP-713**: Renamed Quality_Review to QA_Clearance. CS-9 mandates Quality_Review; renaming without formal equivalence proof (despite post-hoc mapping approval) risks masking noncompliance.
   - **SUP-711**: Duplicate Register_Supplier events with the same source transaction ID. This corrupts the event log and obscures the true process flow.

### Why Desirable Outcomes Do Not Amend Policy
- **Request_More_Info**: While desirable, M0’s lack of a loop construct is not a justification for ignoring CS-9. Operations’ informal adoption of loops does not retroactively amend the normative model.
- **Permanent Auto_Approve**: Operations’ belief in imminent permanent approval (SUP-714) is speculative. CS-9’s Manual_Procurement_Approval requirement is not optional.
- **CRM Migration**: Data-quality issues (e.g., mutable fields, missing notifications) are operational failures, not evidence that M0 is obsolete. The model’s requirements remain valid.

### Material Risk
- **Sanctions Bypass**: SUP-703 and SUP-708 risk sanctions violations by activating suppliers without proper checks.
- **Financial Risk**: Auto_Approve for ineligible suppliers (e.g., SUP-703’s EUR 180,000 spend) exceeds policy caps.
- **Resource Conflicts**: SUP-712 and SUP-715 risk approvals by unqualified or conflicted actors.
- **Evidence Gaps**: Missing notifications (SUP-706) and duplicate events (SUP-711) undermine auditability.

### Conclusion
The operation’s deviations are not isolated incidents but systemic failures to adhere to CS-9 and M0. Temporary directives do not justify permanent normalization of exceptions, and stakeholder desires do not amend normative requirements. M0 is fit for purpose; the issue is operational noncompliance and data-quality defects.

## 3. Defense Brief

### Core Argument
M0 is **obsolete, incomplete, and incorrectly scoped**, failing to reflect operational reality, higher-authority exceptions, and data limitations. CS-9 is rigid and does not account for:
- Temporary operational needs (pilots, emergencies).
- Desirable practices (Request_More_Info, permanent Auto_Approve).
- Data-quality constraints (CRM migration, mutable fields).
- Scope ambiguities (rejected leads, reactivations).

M0’s control-flow model is overly restrictive and cannot express concurrency, loops, or overlays without becoming unwieldy. The observed deviations are not noncompliance but evidence that the model must evolve.

### Key Deficiencies in M0
1. **Concurrency and Overlays**:
   - PD-3 and FD-7 explicitly allow deviations from M0’s sequential flow. PD-3 permits Quality_Review after activation for low-risk suppliers; FD-7 allows provisional activation before Quality_Review/Manual_Procurement_Approval. M0’s strict order cannot represent these overlays without modification.
   - **SUP-702, SUP-704, SUP-705**: These cases comply with PD-3/FD-7 but violate M0’s sequential assumptions. The model must accommodate temporary exceptions.

2. **Missing Loops**:
   - Operations’ use of Request_More_Info (SUP-707) is desirable and reduces risk by ensuring complete evidence before approval. M0’s lack of a loop construct forces deviations that are not misconduct.
   - **Aggregate**: 47 cases used Request_More_Info, all of which activated only after requested evidence arrived. This is a process improvement, not a violation.

3. **Data-Quality Constraints**:
   - **N4 (CRM Migration)**: Notify_Requester moved to CRM, but only 70% of notifications are mirrored into onboarding logs. Mutable text fields for older links (e.g., SUP-702) are a logging issue, not a process violation. The model should not fail cases due to data-quality defects.
   - **SUP-706**: Missing notification in the log is a data issue; CRM evidence exists. The model should not require perfect logging to deem a case compliant.

4. **Scope Ambiguities**:
   - **Rejected Leads (SUP-709)**: CS-9 states "every supplier onboarding requires Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review," but the scope document is ambiguous on whether rejected leads are included. Operations treats them as intake cases; the model owner’s interpretation is not authoritative.
   - **Reactivations (SUP-710)**: CS-9 has no explicit reactivation procedure. The event log’s omission of Register_Supplier/Quality_Review may reflect a separate reactivation process not covered by M0.

5. **Label Equivalence**:
   - **SUP-713**: Quality_Review was renamed to QA_Clearance, with a post-hoc mapping approval. The functional equivalence is documented; the model should not fail cases due to label changes.

6. **Resource Rules**:
   - CS-9’s resource rule (distinct requester/approver) is not represented in M0. **SUP-712** and **SUP-715** may violate CS-9, but the model’s silence on this point does not make the cases compliant. The issue is CS-9’s incompleteness, not M0’s.

7. **Permanent Normalization**:
   - Operations’ normalization of Auto_Approve (e.g., SUP-714) reflects a de facto policy change. Compliance’s inaction (no enforcement of CS-9’s Manual_Procurement_Approval) suggests acceptance of the new norm. The model should evolve to reflect this.

### Why M0 Must Change
- **Operational Reality**: PD-3 and FD-7 are not "exceptions" but part of the operational fabric. M0 must incorporate them as first-class constructs.
- **Desirable Practices**: Request_More_Info and permanent Auto_Approve are improvements that reduce risk and improve efficiency. The model should enable them.
- **Data Limitations**: The CRM migration and mutable fields are real-world constraints. The model should not fail cases due to logging defects.
- **Scope Clarity**: CS-9’s ambiguity on rejected leads and reactivations requires explicit scope rules in the model.

### Conclusion
M0 is a relic of a bygone era. Its rigid control-flow model cannot accommodate temporary directives, desirable practices, or data-quality constraints. The observed deviations are not noncompliance but evidence that the model must be revised to reflect reality. CS-9’s rigidity is the problem, not the solution.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

### Layered Architecture
| Layer | Components | Purpose | Example Rules |
|-------|------------|---------|---------------|
| **Control-Flow (POWL)** | Revised POWL model | Enforce standard, pilot, emergency, and rejection flows. | Concurrent Sanctions_Check/Quality_Review; Auto_Approve branch; provisional activation under FD-7. |
| **DECLARE/Log-Skeleton Constraints** | Declarative rules | Enforce data-aware constraints (risk, amount, time, eligibility). | "If risk = LOW and group_approved = True, then Auto_Approve allowed." "Spend  EUR 20,000 under FD-7." |
| **Data Checks (Pandas)** | Policy overlays, eligibility snapshots, actor aliases | Validate data quality and policy adherence. | "Notify_Requester must have stable notification_id." "Requester  Approver." |
| **Organizational Controls** | Conflict-of-interest policy, emergency/pilot governance | Enforce non-control-flow rules and governance. | "Disclosure required for spousal relationships in approval chain." "PD-3/FD-7 require steering-committee approval." |

### Revised Components
1. **Control-Flow Model (POWL)**:
   - Standard route: Register  (Sanctions_Check || Quality_Review)  (Manual_Procurement_Approval  Activate_Supplier)  Reject_Supplier  Notify  Close.
   - Pilot route: Register  Sanctions_Check  Auto_Approve  Activate_Supplier  Quality_Review (within 2 days)  Notify  Close.
   - Emergency route: Register  Sanctions_Check  Provisional_Activate_Supplier  Quality_Review/Manual_Approval (within 2 days)  Notify  Close.
   - Rejection route: Register  Sanctions_Check  Reject_Supplier  Notify  Close.
   - Request_More_Info: Loop construct (e.g., SilentTransition back to Register or a dedicated Request_More_Info activity).

2. **Data-Aware Rules (DECLARE/Log-Skeleton)**:
   - Risk-based eligibility: LOW-risk suppliers in approved corporate groups may use Auto_Approve.
   - Spend caps: EUR 20,000 under FD-7; no cap otherwise.
   - Time constraints: Quality_Review within 2 business days of activation (pilot/emergency).
   - Resource constraints: Requester  Approver; conflict-of-interest disclosure required.

3. **Data Checks (Pandas)**:
   - Stable notification IDs for CRM-mirrored events.
   - Actor alias resolution to prevent same-human approvals.
   - Eligibility snapshots at time of activation (e.g., risk level, group approval status).
   - Immutable effective/record timestamps for policy overlays.

4. **Organizational Controls**:
   - Formal governance for pilots/emergencies (steering-committee approval, expiry tracking).
   - Conflict-of-interest policy enforcement (disclosure, approval chain restrictions).
   - Scope rules for rejected leads and reactivations (separate sub-processes).
