### Analytical comparison of pandas diagnostics vs. PM4Py batch categories

Evaluating PM4Py's `discover_batches` interval rules against the exact cluster timestamps yields two critical insights:

1. **Sensitivity to `merge_distance` vs. `min_batch_size`**:
   - Within every cluster G1–G8, consecutive events either overlap (`G1`, `G2`, `G3`, `G5`, `G7`), coincide at a single instant (`G6`), or touch with zero gap (`G4`, `G8`: `start[i+1] == complete[i]`), and each cluster maps to a distinct `(activity, resource)` pair. Consequently, increasing `merge_distance` from `0` s to `900` s does **not** alter cluster membership here.
   - By contrast, increasing `min_batch_size` from `2` to `5` acts as a sharp threshold: `G1`, `G2`, `G3`, and `G5` (all size 4) drop out (`not_detected`), leaving only `G4` (5), `G6` (7), `G7` (5), and `G8` (5).

2. **Why PM4Py batch categories must be treated only as geometric pattern detections**:
   - **False equivalence under `Simultaneous`**: PM4Py groups **G1** (true 4-slot fixture batch), **G6** (zero-duration `17:00` bulk form entry), and **G7** (`15 min` coarsened `legacy_MES` grid with 4 negative-wait causal inversions) under the same `Simultaneous` label. Only pandas diagnostics (`zero_dur_n=7` for G6; `all_15m_grid=True` and `neg_wait_n=4` for G7) separate physical simultaneity from logging artifacts.
   - **False equivalence under `Sequential`**: PM4Py detects both **G4** and **G8** as sequential chains (`overlap_rate=0.0`, contiguous intervals), even though **G8** was caused by a 35-min safety outage (`15:00–15:35`) rather than deliberate batching.
   - **Blindness to shared accounts and UI triggers**: PM4Py labels **G5** as `Concurrent` (`max_conc=4`, `naive_util_pct=233.3%`) because it trusts `org:resource="analyst_pool"` as one resource, and labels **G2** (`n_uniq_start=1`) and **G3** (`n_uniq_end=1`) as start/end batching without knowing whether the underlying physical work was sequential (`G2` tablet button, `G3` folder submission).

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

### A. Sensitivity to resource identity assumptions (trusted vs. shared accounts)
- **If `analyst_pool` (G5) is trusted as 1 resource**: Utilization becomes $233.3\%$ and conformance analysis reports illegal 4-way multitasking. Treating it as a shared account of up to 3 analysts drops utilization into a plausible band ($77.8\%$ if 3 worked throughout), though 4-way overlap during `13:12–13:20` still proves either minor multitasking by at least one analyst or open-ticket idle time.
- **If `T-B` (G2), `T-C` (G3), or `T-G` (G7) were undisclosed shared accounts**: Apparent start batching (`G2`), end batching (`G3`), or `Simultaneous` grid collapse (`G7`) could instead mask multiple workers completing separate items in parallel under one terminal login.
- **For `T-D` (G4) and `T-H` (G8)**: Because intervals are strictly non-overlapping (`max_conc = 1`), treating them as shared accounts would not create false concurrency—only handoff attribution across shifts/operators would change.

### B. Sensitivity to timestamp precision assumptions (exact vs. interval-censored)
- **`1 sec` sources (`G1`, `G4`, `G8`)**: Note that `recorded_at` carries `:SS`, whereas `start`/`complete` appear at `HH:MM` granularity. If `start`/`complete` were rounded/truncated to whole minutes in the extract, G1's 4 items could have staggered sub-minute starts/completions inside `[08:59:30, 09:00:59]` and `[09:02:00, 09:03:03]`, and G4/G8 could have sub-minute handoff gaps or brief overlaps ($<60$ s) rather than exact $0$-second adjacency.
- **`1 min` sources (`G2`, `G3`, `G5`, `G6`)**: Every duration and waiting time carries up to $\pm 1$ min censoring error (or up to $2$ min for `complete - start`). In G6, `17:00`–`17:00` is compatible with $<60$ s of rapid bulk clicking within the `17:00` minute rather than literal $0.000$ s execution.
- **`15 min` source (`G7`)**: Under undocumented quarter-hour rounding, `start=14:00` and `complete=14:15` only bound true execution inside an interval of width $\ge 15$ min (e.g., if `start` floors to `:00`, true starts for M01–M05 lie in $[\max(14:00, \text{queue\_arrival}), 14:15)$ if causal ordering holds, and true completions could extend up to `recorded_at` (`14:16`–`14:20`) if rounded down to `14:15`). Wait times become interval-censored rather than negative.

### C. Observational equivalence: cases where one temporal pattern fits multiple physical realities
1. **G6 (`start = complete = 17:00`, `recorded_at = 17:01`)**: Identical timestamps match both *(i) end-of-shift deferred data entry* (work performed individually between `14:05` and `17:00`, entered all at once at `17:00`) and *(ii) end-of-shift bulk execution* (tickets ignored until `17:00`, then closed via one bulk action).
2. **G3 (staggered starts `11:02–11:24`, common complete `11:30`)**: Matches both *(i) sequential work with deferred folder submission* (true completions ~`11:10`, `11:17`, `11:24`, `11:30`) and *(ii) genuine parallel open-folder work* where all 4 cases are cross-checked and finalized simultaneously at `11:30`.
3. **G2 (common start `10:00`, staggered completions `10:08–10:27`)**: Matches both *(i) UI "begin group" start batching with sequential inspection* (~6–8 min each) and *(ii) common physical setup followed by concurrent/interleaved inspection* of all 4 items starting at `10:00`.
4. **G4 / G8 (contiguous sequential chain `end[i] == start[i+1]`)**: Matches both *(i) intentional sequential batching* (holding work until $k$ items accumulate) and *(ii) FIFO queue draining after an arrival burst or resource downtime* (as evidenced in G8 by the `15:00–15:35` safety check).

# 6. Data-collection redesign

Prioritized by impact on process-mining validity (highest impact first):

1. **Execution vs. recording timestamp separation (Priority 1)**: Capture immutable system-generated physical `work_start` and `work_complete` events from automated triggers (scan, tool save, PLC sensor) separately from `recorded_at` (DB insert time), and prohibit `manual_bulk_form` from defaulting `start = complete = submit_time`.
2. **Timestamp precision metadata & full-resolution retention (Priority 1)**: Store all timestamps in ISO-8601 UTC with at least `1 sec` (or ms) resolution, stop truncating `:SS` in downstream extracts (`G1`, `G4`, `G8`), and attach explicit metadata (`precision_sec`, `rounding_rule = none | floor | ceil | nearest`) so analysts can distinguish exact timestamps from `15 min` censored grids (`G7`).
3. **Source clock synchronization (Priority 1)**: Enforce NTP/PTP clock synchronization across upstream queue, PLC, scanners, tablets, `legacy_MES`, and the safety-check system (`G8`), and log periodic clock-skew diagnostics so causal inversions (`G7`) or outage boundaries (`G8`) are unambiguous.
4. **Resource and badge identity (Priority 2)**: Replace shared logins (`analyst_pool` in `G5`) with individual SSO/badge authentication per lifecycle event; where team assignment is needed, store both `pool_id` and `worker_id`.
