Leaves classification errors (8%) to multiply — wrong checklists (A5), wrong collateral routing, provisional signing (6.5%) scaled by A10/A11 bots. No control-strengthener unless creatively argued — fails compliance gate. Creates classic `automate the entrance, collapse the middle` failure; rework rates unchanged (52% repeated, 38% mismatch). Resource demand shifts to overtime for analysts/legal month-end.

### Portfolio B — Bottleneck / Synchronization (Process-Level Throughput)
**Modules:** A4 Entity-mismatch assistant + A5 Missing-doc checklist + A6 Statement extraction/normalization + A7 Valuation triage

*What it optimizes:* End-to-end waiting time and synchronization delay. Targets four longest waits: 31h (mismatch), 18h+8h loops (missing docs), 20h (normalize), 58h external valuation (triage). Directly attacks DFG loops: `KYCResolve`, `Financial doc  Request missing`. Frees specialist (A4) and analyst (A6) capacity simultaneously, allowing portal-driven inflow (A5) to be absorbed. Improves `Committee packaging` rendezvous by having artifacts ready.

*What it sacrifices:* Lacks explicit hard control gate — though A4 improves KYC integrity, portfolio technically fails compliance mandate (no A9/A12 or A2 control path). Risk remains that contracts generated manually still mismatch decisions (8.5-14% remediation). Also defers classification accuracy, so A7 triage may fire on misclassified equipment finance. Delivers less direct labor headline than A1/A9 but superior cycle time (projected 30% total). Requires disciplined exception handling design for low-quality PDFs.

### Portfolio C — Compliance-First (Control Hardening + Auditability)
**Modules:** A2 Classification recommender + A3 KYC hit pre-classifier + A9 Contract generation w/ control checks + A12 Remediation workbench

*What it optimizes:* Control violations and auditability. A2 correctly routes High-exposure to four-eyes path, A3 standardizes KYC decisions with human escalation (reducing 5.8% four-eyes and 6.5% provisional signing), A9 blocks contract/signing if KYC/decision mismatch, A12 surfaces remaining 1.1% remediation need. Preserves auditable trail (model suggestion/override/final) demanded. Satisfies compliance and legal stakeholders; reduces costly late remediation loops (`Contract -> Remediate 650`).

*What it sacrifices:* Little direct bottleneck relief for the 58h external wait, 31h mismatch wait (A4 not included), or 20h normalization wait — cycle time improvement modest (10-15%). May even increase perceived waiting because controls block previously `provisionally signed` cases now correctly held. Analyst capacity unchanged; will still suffer 75-min statement normalization drag. Requires deep integration (A9) + strong governance to keep rule library current else creates systematic legal risk.

### Recommended hybrid (used in rollout below) — Balanced Control + Flow
*To meet budget + compliance + throughput, combine B+C strengths:* **A4 + A5 + A6 + A9** (2 deep integrations: A6, A9; A4/A5 sidecars). This hits three bottlenecks + one hard control gate; A2 logic implemented as lightweight rule inside A9/A4 rather than separate module in Y1.

# 7. Final recommendation

**Recommend Portfolio B/C Hybrid: A4 Entity-mismatch assistant + A5 Missing-document portal + A6 Statement extraction + A9 Contract control generation (4 modules).**

This is not the highest-frequency portfolio (A1) nor a pure compliance play. It balances the CEO's cost narrative with the mining truth:

**Why not automate Register Application (A1) first?**
Highest frequency (11,880)  highest leverage. Mean service only 8 min, median wait 1.5h — an order of magnitude smaller than the decisive waits (31h mismatch, 20-28h analyst chain, 58h external valuation, 44h signing). Back Office has 12 FTE but slack after 15:00; the visible queue is a batched-arrival illusion, not capacity shortage. Automating A1 would save ~1,580 hours/year in isolation but add ~5,000 hours of waiting and rework downstream by accelerating misclassified (8% error) and document-incomplete cases into the two scarcest pools: 3 compliance specialists and 9 central credit analysts. The mining directly warns: *"may expose classification, KYC, and document-review bottlenecks"* — precisely what would happen. Politically attractive but process-degrading; it would increase WIP, handovers, and provisional-signing pressure without touching rework loops (52% repeated, 38% mismatch) or synchronization delay at `Committee packaging`. Per constraints, a downstream win (A8/A10/A11) or upstream accelerator (A1) without fixing controls amplifies risk.

**Why this hybrid wins defensible trade-off:**
* **Cycle-time primacy:** Attacks the empirical long waits (entity mismatch critical path 82%, missing-doc loop return 85%, valuation 58h) and the largest service-time block (75 min normalize + 110 min credit analysis). Portfolio Q cannot claim >5% sustainable cycle improvement; this hybrid projects 25-30% for collateralized and high-exception variants — the bank's margin and risk-sensitive segments.
* **Resource realism:** Instead of demanding more FTE, it *reallocates* work: A6 funds capacity to absorb A5 inflow; A4 de-bottlenecks specialists; A9 smooths legal month-end peaks. Accounts for shifted demand, honoring the warning that *"automation that increases clean inflow to analysts may overload them."*
* **Control strengthening:** Satisfies compliance's non-negotiable with A9 hard gate (+ A4 KYC hygiene) rather than cosmetic monitoring alone. Blocks the documented violations: 6.5% provisional signing, 14% renewal term mismatch, 5.8% four-eyes bypass. Preserves auditable trail of suggestion/override — automation retains responsibility rather than removes it. Portfolio B alone fails this; Portfolio C alone fails on cycle time.
* **Prerequisite hygiene:** Obeys dependency sequencing: no A8 committee auto-assembly, no A10 e-sign scaling, no A11 activation bot until upstream artifacts are trustworthy. Builds foundation (A5A6A9 chain) that makes those later automations safe in Year 2. Under 2-deep-integration limit, invests slots in durable intelligence (A6) and compliance infrastructure (A9) vs throwaway registration bot.
* **Exception handling by design:** Each module routes low-confidence cases to scoped human queues, turning 38% and 52% exception rates into managed workflows rather than hidden rework. Human escalation capacity is explicitly planned, not assumed away.

*Sacrifice acknowledged:* Foregoing A1 immediate labor headline and deferring A10 customer experience delight to H2. Mitigated by quantifying that A4+A5+A6 combined free more FTE-hours than A1, and by communicating to senior management that automation ROI is measured in *lead-time and rework avoided*, not registrations auto-completed. Year-2 roadmap (A2 full recommender, A3 pre-classifier with capacity plan, A10 gated e-sign, A8 packing) then delivers the political win safely.
