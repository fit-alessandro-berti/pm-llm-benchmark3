## 1. Decision rule

Use a two-step rule: first decide whether a swarm can still help, then allocate slots by robust marginal portfolio value.

- **Eligibility:** A case must be before `Diagnosis Complete`, have at least 60 minutes before its SLA deadline (the 45-minute swarm plus a small execution buffer), and have a plausible diagnostic action the swarm can take. I-07, at 90 minutes to deadline, qualifies but needs immediate handling. A case inside the 60-minute window is escalated to the incident owner for other recovery actions, not given a swarm whose effect cannot arrive in time. A completed swarm bars another one on that incident unless a materially new event creates a new, actionable diagnostic problem.
- **Evidence and alert persistence:** A new alert prompts review, not an automatic slot. Re-alert after review only for a material new event, a calibrated risk increase of at least 0.05, crossing into a more urgent deadline band, or a newly actionable blocker. Smaller refresh-to-refresh changes are suppressed. Human-confirm material, actionable evidence before committing a slot when a salient feature is missing or a segment estimate is especially uncertain.
- **Calibration:** For ranking urgency, carry a sensitivity interval rather than treating `p_now` as truth. As a provisional adjustment, add 0.04–0.09 to SME risks and subtract 0.05–0.12 from Strategic Enterprise risks, clipping at 0 and 1. Leave Public Sector estimates broadly centered on the model value but retain wider uncertainty than for a well-sampled segment. Do not apply a fabricated correction to Non-Native-Language Support: use a wide interval and a consistent human evidence review. These are prioritization adjustments pending recalibration, not causal estimates of swarm benefit. Keep the supplied risk-reduction ranges separate from the observational breach probabilities.
- **Allocation:** For each eligible case, estimate a conservative direct contribution as (lower plausible absolute risk reduction × value of avoiding breach) − €450 swarm cost − false-alarm burden. Adjust this for evidence quality, time remaining, customer or regulatory obligations, and the queue harm caused by another slot. Use deadline bands (under 3 hours, 3–6 hours, over 6 hours) to break close ties in favor of cases that will soon lose their intervention window. Require a plausible positive contribution, except that a documented public-service or contractual duty can justify a bounded exception. Recompute after each slot because spillover and congestion are state-dependent. Keep one of four slots in reserve for the possible regulatory incident through 15:00.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

- Suppress identical refresh alerts for 60 minutes after a human review. A 30-minute model refresh alone is not new evidence. Continue passive monitoring during suppression.
- Reopen review for a material event, a calibrated risk increase of at least 0.05, movement into a more urgent deadline band, a stage change that affects eligibility, or new evidence of an actionable blocker. Log the reason for every re-entry.
- After a swarm, lock out another swarm on that incident for the remainder of the case by default. An exception requires a materially new event after the first swarm, a distinct diagnostic task, confirmation that the case is still pre-`Diagnosis Complete`, and an independent senior approval that incremental benefit exceeds new queue harm. A persistent high score alone is not enough.
- Escalate a suppressed but persistently high-risk case to its case owner for non-swarm options; do not convert repeated notifications into repeated interventions. Expire alerts when the stage makes the swarm ineffective or the remaining time is too short.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

A purely monetary rule can repeatedly favor high-value enterprise cases and starve lower-value customers, SMEs, or public-service users. Keep contractual and public-service duties as explicit constraints, report allocation and outcomes by value band as well as segment, and review cases rejected by the monetary score. The regulatory reserve is one concrete way to retain capacity for an obligation whose value is not captured by the listed breach amounts. Do not allow an unbounded “strategic value” override to silently consume every slot.

Non-Native-Language Support has sparse calibration and effect evidence. Treating uncertainty as zero benefit would systematically deny access; treating the point estimate as certain could waste slots and burden customers. Use a standardized, language-accessible case review, preserve an uncertainty interval, include these cases in controlled effect estimation, and compare time to review and slot access with other segments. Do not require more documentation from these customers than from comparable cases.

The SME and Strategic Enterprise errors run in opposite directions. Apply provisional segment corrections only as sensitivity ranges, publish segment-level calibration and alert rates, and replace them with locally validated estimates as data accrue. For Public Sector, retain uncertainty intervals despite broadly acceptable aggregate calibration; moderate sample size can hide subgroup errors.

Intervened cases produce outcomes under treatment. If a swarmed case avoids breach, labeling it simply as an ordinary negative example can teach the model that its original warning was false. Record alert score, policy version, assignment, treatment timing, compliance, stage, and outcome. Train and evaluate with treatment-aware methods and randomized or otherwise identified comparison data; do not mix treated outcomes with untreated labels without adjustment.

Swarm spillovers may fall disproportionately on groups that are not selected, especially if their cases share the diagnosis queue but have lower listed values. Track queue delay, stage progression, breach rates, and missed deadlines among untreated cases by segment and value band. Report the externality as part of the selected case’s marginal cost. Cap simultaneous swarms or pause new dispatch when measured queue delay exceeds the policy’s stress range; rotate or cluster-assign evaluation periods so the same groups do not repeatedly absorb the delay.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

- **Operations and capacity:** Record dispatch and completion times, whether the case remained pre-`Diagnosis Complete`, specialist hours displaced, diagnosis-queue delay for treated and untreated cases, slot utilization, reserve use, false alarms, and missed intervention windows. Compare observed delays with the 8–20-minute base and 15–30-minute adverse ranges. Stop or slow dispatch if congestion exceeds the adverse range or produces material untreated-case harm.
- **Prediction quality:** Once breach labels mature, monitor calibration, Brier score, and alert precision by segment, stage, deadline horizon, language-support status, and data completeness. Show uncertainty intervals and sample sizes. Check the SME and Strategic Enterprise residuals separately; investigate I-06-like missing-feature jumps. Refit corrections on current workflow data rather than carrying fixed offsets forward indefinitely.
- **Intervention effect:** Estimate intent-to-treat effects and effects among compliers from a small randomized or randomized wait-list rollout among eligible cases in genuine equipoise. Stratify by stage, segment, and evidence type where sample size permits. Log assignment even when a swarm is declined or not completed. Because cases interfere through the shared queue, randomize or rotate at queue/shift level as well as case level, and estimate both direct effects and effects on untreated peers. Do not claim case-level causal estimates from raw treated-versus-untreated comparisons.
- **Fairness and feedback:** At each review, compare eligibility, alerts, review latency, interventions, queue delay, breaches, and false-alarm burden across segments and value bands. Audit who receives spillover harm. Keep treatment and policy metadata with training labels, use experimental comparisons or appropriate treatment/selection adjustment, and test for drift after workflow changes.
- **Rule changes:** Review weekly during rollout and at least monthly after stabilization. Reconsider calibration offsets, evidence thresholds, and allocation weights when segment calibration error persists, a segment’s access or untreated harm diverges, intervention benefit no longer covers direct and queue costs, or capacity delay enters the adverse range. Require adequate sample size or report the uncertainty explicitly; use a monitored pilot before broad changes. Lower a threshold only when more eligible cases have positive conservative portfolio contribution and spare capacity exists. Raise it, reduce dispatch, or change the allocation rule when marginal direct gains are outweighed by spillover harm or when a segment’s error is driving repeated misallocation.
