**1. Executive memo**

To: Telecom Fiber Installation Leadership  
From: Process Decision Team  
Date: 6 August 2026  
Subject: Decision on Redesign R after pilot – Recommended hybrid path

Management must decide next steps for Redesign R after a 6-week, 9,200-case pilot across four regions. The redesign delivered clear median-cycle-time reduction (14.2 → 10.8 days) and a sharp drop in multi-handover cases (34 % → 12 %), driven by single-coordinator ownership and merged validation/scheduling for simpler work. Visible cost before complaint handling also fell (EUR 184 → 161).  

However, the same pilot produced material deterioration on the right-hand tail (p90 31 → 38.5 days; p95 45 → 62 days), first-time-right (82 % → 76 %), 30-day complaints (6.1 % → 9.8 %), second visits (11 % → 18 %), manual activations (7 % → 15 %), and overall SLA breaches. Process-mining diagnostics show high fitness (0.94) but very low precision (0.51): the discovered model fits the simplified coordinator path well yet permits many deviant combinations of skipped surveys, repeated visits without survey, billing before successful activation, and missing permit checks. Alignments, DFG shifts, and temporal profiles confirm these paths, especially for apartments and older buildings. Resource data reveal coordinator utilisation at 93 % in two regions, second-visit teams overloaded (66 % → 92 %), and a more centralised working-together network that reduces resilience when coordinators are absent.  

Segment results are decisive. Simple low-risk houses (34 % of pilot volume) show large, consistent gains: median –6 days, p90 –8 days, FTR +3 pp, complaints –1 pp. Apartments (medium risk) and older permit-sensitive buildings show the opposite: large tail increases, FTR drops of 8–11 pp, and complaint increases of 5–7 pp. Pilot regions already contained more apartments and older stock than the national average, so aggregate negatives are not solely a composition artefact. Cost estimates that attempt to include downstream complaint and revisit handling already reverse the visible saving (≈ EUR 198 → 207), yet the data remain incomplete after week 4.  

Full national scaling of R (alternative A) is therefore unjustified: it would export the tail, rework, and compliance-like activation problems. Pure rollback (B) would forfeit proven gains on a substantial and operationally attractive segment. Scaling only coordinator ownership while suspending the two highest-risk shortcuts (C) is safer but still leaves capacity and resilience issues unaddressed and forgoes selective use of a potentially valuable skip rule once validated. A pure controlled experiment (D) delays value capture for the simple-house population that already benefits.  

The recommended course is therefore the hybrid (E):  

- Immediately deploy coordinator ownership plus the merged validate/schedule task only for confirmed simple low-risk houses.  
- Suspend survey-skipping and any post-billing activation for all other cases; enforce mandatory field survey + permit check for medium/high-risk and older buildings; require successful activation test before billing start.  
- Add capacity and explicit backup/context-sharing protocols for coordinators so utilisation falls below 85 % and absence does not stall cases.  
- Embed high-value measurement (especially validation of the building-risk rule, full 60-day downstream cost, structured reason codes, and real-time conformance on the critical alignments) inside the hybrid regions.  

This path captures the evidence-based gains where they are robust, contains the documented harms, restores process discipline (higher precision, fewer dangerous paths), and generates the information needed for a later, data-driven decision on wider rollout. Thresholds for broader expansion are stated in the final recommendation section. The redesign is not uniformly successful; the evidence is incomplete and conflicting; a segmented, controlled next step is the only defensible move.

**3. Explanation of metric conflicts**

High fitness but low precision arises because the majority of pilot cases followed the new simplified coordinator path (producing high fitness of 0.94). The discovered model, however, is overly permissive (precision 0.51): it allows many combinations of skipped survey, repeated installation without survey, manual activation and billing-before-activation that appear in the event log as alignments and elevated DFG edges. The model therefore “fits” the happy path while failing to constrain the costly deviant paths.

Improved median but worse tail duration is a classic mixture effect. Simple low-risk houses (34 % of volume) experienced large reductions in both median and p90, pulling the overall median down. Apartments, older buildings and exception cases experienced large increases in p90/p95 because of wrong skips, missing permits, overloaded second-visit teams and delayed recovery under high coordinator utilisation. The right-hand tail therefore lengthened even while the centre improved.

Fewer handovers but lower resilience follows from the change in social networks. The handover-of-work network simplified under single-coordinator ownership, cutting multi-handover cases. The working-together network became more centralised around coordinators and activation specialists. When a coordinator was absent or at 93 % utilisation, context was not shared, cases stalled, and Failed-visit → Second-visit delays increased. Distributed knowledge that previously provided resilience was removed.

Lower visible cost but higher rework/downstream cost occurs because the measured cost excludes complaint handling after week 4. Fewer surveys and handovers reduced the visible figure (EUR 184 → 161). Simultaneously second visits, manual activations and post-billing complaints rose, shifting cost downstream. The incomplete estimate already shows a net increase (≈ EUR 198 → 207); fuller measurement is required to confirm the true economic picture.

Improvement for simple houses but deterioration for apartments/old buildings is the direct consequence of applying a uniform redesign to heterogeneous work. Merged validation/scheduling and coordinator ownership suit low-complexity houses. The same skip rule and activation-sequence change misfire on medium-risk apartments and older permit-sensitive buildings, producing the observed FTR drops, complaint spikes and tail lengthening. Pilot composition overweight these harder segments, amplifying the aggregate negatives, yet the segment-level contrast remains large and consistent.

**4. Decision alternatives**

**A. Scale Redesign R nationally**  
Advantages: locks in median and handover gains plus the strong simple-house segment benefits; potential accountability improvement from ownership.  
Risks: exports large tail, rework, complaint and compliance-like (billing-before-activation, missing permits) problems; coordinator utilisation already unsustainable; low precision indicates uncontrolled variants will proliferate in a more heterogeneous national mix; incomplete cost data already point to higher total cost.  
Reasonable only if national segment mix is overwhelmingly simple low-risk *and* simultaneous, proven fixes to the skip rule, activation sequence and capacity are in place—conditions not met by current evidence.

**B. Roll back to the baseline process**  
Advantages: rapidly restores better tails, FTR, complaints, precision/generalisation and outcomes for apartments/old buildings; restores distributed resilience.  
Risks: forfeits clear, large gains on a substantial simple-house population; higher visible cost and handovers; loss of organisational learning on the coordinator model.  
Reasonable if risk appetite is very low, full downstream costs prove heavily negative, or service-quality recovery is the overriding short-term priority. Segment benefits argue against pure rollback as the first move.

**C. Scale only the coordinator-ownership part, suspend survey skipping and post-billing activation**  
Advantages: retains handover reduction and accountability while removing the two highest-risk process changes; preserves most median gains for simple cases.  
Risks: still leaves high coordinator utilisation and resilience problems unaddressed; forgoes any selective value of a validated skip rule; does not automatically fix permit gaps.  
Reasonable as an interim or fallback if dual-process operational complexity is judged too high in the short term, or if validation of the skip rule fails.

**D. Run a controlled regional experiment with better segment/risk measurement**  
Advantages: highest causal rigor; fills critical data gaps (attributes, costs, start timestamps, rule accuracy); minimises irreversible national exposure.  
Risks: delays value capture for the already-proven simple-house segment; opportunity cost and potential management impatience after a 6-week pilot.  
Reasonable when uncertainty is judged high enough that a wrong A or B decision is very costly, or when organisational preference strongly favours learning before any further scale. Best used *inside* a hybrid rather than as a pure pause.

**E. Hybrid: deploy R only for simple low-risk houses, add controls for apartments/old buildings, and run measurement before wider scale**  
Advantages: harvests the strongest evidence-based gains while protecting the segments that currently deteriorate; restores process discipline via mandatory controls on the dangerous paths identified by alignments; enables embedded high-value measurement; directly addresses the metric conflicts by applying different process variants where they work.  
Risks: requires reliable real-time segment/risk classification (attributes currently incomplete); operational complexity of dual pathways; residual capacity/resilience issues if coordinator headcount and backup protocols are not addressed.  
Reasonable under the present incomplete and conflicting evidence; it is the only alternative that simultaneously captures value, contains harm and generates the information needed for the next decision.

Preferability ranking given the evidence and constraints: E > C ≈ D > B > A.

**5. Value-of-information analysis**

Ranked by expected decision value (highest first):

1. **M6 – Validate the building-risk predictive rule against field-survey outcomes**  
   Resolves the central uncertainty about whether the skip rule is accurate or systematically wrong for medium-risk addresses. Directly changes the scope of hybrid (keep restricted skip, expand, or permanently suspend). Highest leverage on the design of E versus C.

2. **M4 – Measure full downstream cost for 60 days after Billing start**  
   Resolves whether the visible cost saving is real or illusory. A confirmed net cost increase would strengthen the case for tighter controls or partial rollback; a neutral or favourable result would support careful expansion of hybrid.

3. **M1 – Randomized pilot of survey skipping by building-risk stratum**  
   Provides causal evidence free of the current confounding by region mix and incomplete early attributes. Strongest basis for deciding whether any skipping can safely remain in the hybrid design.

4. **M3 – Structured reason codes for Failed visit, Manual activation, Complaint escalation and Second visit**  
   Diagnoses root causes of the rework spike. Informs targeted process fixes and whether the coordinator model itself contributes to the problems.

5. **M7 – Conformance monitoring for Billing start before Activation test and Permit-check absence**  
   Enables real-time operational control of the two most dangerous alignments during any hybrid deployment. Converts diagnostic insight into prevention without requiring full rollback.

6. **M5 – Compare coordinator ownership with shared-team ownership under coordinator absence**  
   Tests the resilience hypothesis. Determines whether pure ownership must be modified with backup protocols or shared-context mechanisms before wider use.

7. **M8 – Collect customer experience separately for “speed to appointment” versus “working service after installation”**  
   Clarifies whether the NPS decline is an acceptable speed-quality trade-off or signals a quality failure that customers weight more heavily. Useful for prioritising metrics but less likely to reverse the core process decision.

8. **M2 – Instrument start timestamps for Installation visit, Activation test and Manual activation**  
   Improves future bottleneck and waiting-time diagnosis. Valuable for continuous improvement but lowest immediate impact on the go / no-go / hybrid decision given existing completion timestamps and larger uncertainties.

The top five items can and should be embedded inside the hybrid deployment so that learning occurs while value is already being captured on the simple-house segment.

**6. Final recommendation**

**Recommended decision: Alternative E – Hybrid deployment with controls and accelerated measurement.**

- **Immediate action**:  
  Implement reliable segment/risk classification as fast as possible. Apply Redesign R (single coordinator ownership + merged address-validation/appointment-scheduling) exclusively to confirmed simple low-risk houses. For all apartments, older buildings, medium/high-risk addresses and any case whose attributes are incomplete, enforce tightened baseline controls: mandatory field survey + permit check where historically required; successful Activation test *before* Billing start; no survey skipping and no post-billing activation.

- **What to pause**:  
  The predictive survey-skip rule (pending M6 validation). Any activation checks performed after billing start. Further expansion of coordinator ownership beyond the simple-house population until capacity and resilience protocols are in place.

- **What to scale**:  
  Coordinator ownership and the merged validate/schedule task *only* for the simple low-risk segment, accompanied by immediate capacity planning (target utilisation ≤ 80–85 %) and explicit backup/shared-context protocols so that absence does not stall cases.

- **What to measure next** (embed in the hybrid regions, priority order):  
  M6 (rule validation), M4 (full 60-day downstream cost), M3 (structured reason codes), M1 (randomised skip by stratum, carefully scoped), M7 (real-time conformance on critical alignments), followed by M5 and M8. M2 can proceed in parallel for longer-term diagnostic quality.

- **Threshold / evidence that would justify broader rollout**:  
  After a minimum of 8–12 weeks of hybrid operation with complete segment attributes and cost data:  
  - Simple-house gains sustained or improved (median ≤ 11 days, FTR ≥ 85 %).  
  - Non-simple segments: FTR ≥ 80 %, 30-day complaints ≤ 7 %, p90 ≤ 35 days (or no worse than baseline + 5 days).  
  - Full downstream cost (M4) ≤ baseline estimate of EUR 198.  
  - Skip-rule validation (M6) achieves ≥ 85–90 % accuracy by stratum if any re-introduction is proposed.  
  - Discovered-model precision ≥ 0.70 and critical alignment rates (billing-before-activation, missing permits) held below 2–3 % under M7.  
  - Coordinator utilisation stable below 85 % with demonstrated resilience under absence (M5).  
  - Separated customer-experience scores show no further decline on “working service after installation.”  

If these thresholds are met, expand the hybrid carefully to the next risk strata. If they are not met, fall back to a refined version of C (coordinator ownership only, permanent suspension of skip and early billing) or further controlled experimentation.  

This course of action is the only one that respects the conflicting evidence, the segment heterogeneity, the process-mining diagnostics, and the incomplete measurement, while still capturing the clear value that already exists.
