# 1. Queue-by-queue diagnosis

**Method and conventions (apply throughout).** A "decision point" is every moment a resource becomes free or a running job is interrupted; at each point I enumerate the jobs that were plausibly waiting and eligible, then score each candidate discipline by the job it would have picked. Conventions and labeled assumptions:

- **Assumption A1 (decision timing):** decision instants are taken as segment-start timestamps; the two-minute scan/dispatch latency is treated as noise around them.
- **Assumption A2 (arrival scans):** a recorded arrival is an *upper bound* on true arrival (scans are delayed, never advanced). Eligible sets derived from recorded arrivals are therefore conservative — if a job is eligible under recorded data it was eligible in reality.
- **Assumption A3 (appointment rule):** the appointment discipline selects a job whose appointment time has arrived; a "strict" variant idles until then, a "backfill" variant works on other jobs meanwhile. Tolerance is unknown (collection need, §6).
- **Assumption A4 (estimates):** `est_min` is the dispatch-visible duration. Note the data-quality flag that realized duration equals `est_min` exactly for **all 33 jobs** (§5, item 4).
- Skill-based routing is degenerate for QA/QB/QC (single resource, uniform `general` skill); it is evaluated only for QD, where it is folded into eligibility.
