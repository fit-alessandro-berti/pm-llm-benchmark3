## 1. Conformance semantics

I distinguish the following classes:

- **Forbidden behavior:** an observed interaction is explicitly disallowed within the norm’s stated identity, event, and object scope. High frequency is not relevant: one occurrence is enough. A data-quality explanation can suspend the *business-behavior* conclusion, but does not silently repeal the prohibition.
- **Missing mandatory behavior:** an applicable mandatory relation is absent from a correctly scoped, sufficiently complete observation. This establishes an observed-graph deficit, not necessarily omitted real-world activity. Missing logging, missing object bindings, and an overly strict graph abstraction must be distinguished.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior:** an observation lies outside the norm’s coverage. It may be perfectly legitimate; absence from the displayed normative graph does not establish noncompliance.
- **Typical-pattern absence:** a common, desirable pattern is absent. This is not a mandatory violation, even when the pattern’s frequency is low.
- **Likely data-quality error:** the observation may not represent actual object interaction—for example, an invalid identifier join. It should be investigated without rewriting the original evidence.
- **Abstraction mismatch:** real activity may be consistent with the business rule while the graph representation loses intermediate events, timing, object identity, or event-to-object correspondence.
- **Insufficient evidence:** scope, completeness, identity, timing, or provenance cannot be established from the summary.

The norm is closed-world only for explicit prohibitions and in-scope mandatory relations. Typical and unspecified relations remain open-world. Thus, neither the 98% validation count nor the 92% payment count creates a numerical tolerance; no such bands are specified. Conversely, a mandatory graph gap cannot be dismissed as statistically acceptable.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

The OC-DFG establishes observed directly-follows relations under its aggregation and activity-alignment rules. It does not generally establish eventual completion, every intermediate event, or real-world execution independently of logging.

- **N1:** The summary explicitly approximates an eventual-validation rule with direct follows. Treating that approximation as the rule would turn an abstraction limitation into false noncompliance. The two apparent gaps require complete Order histories and a validation deadline. The three review paths cannot be assigned to those two gaps without Order identities.
- **N2:** 83 of 100 direct closes are typical behavior. The 17 without a direct close are not necessarily never closed, especially because 72 validation-to-invoice paths may coexist with later closure. Invoice issuance is an allowed intermediate.
- **N4:** The 198/200 result is an in-scope mandatory deficit in the observed abstraction, but it does not alone prove that two physical packing activities never occurred.
- **N5:** Payment, write-off, and no-successor counts describe different patterns. N5 is typical, explicitly recognizes exceptions, and sets no minimum frequency. Four write-offs are recognized; the other fourteen require context rather than automatic correction.
- **N3:** Unlike typical patterns, two same-Order post-cancellation dispatches are expressly forbidden. Neither a high percentage nor rarity would excuse them.

### OTG

The OTG establishes typed object associations, co-births, and co-deaths as represented. A present object edge does not establish event-level participation by itself; a missing event edge does not necessarily mean the object edge is absent.

T1’s Order–Item weight of 1,450 appears encouraging, but E2 reports only 1,448 Order bindings. If the weights are like-for-like event incidences, this suggests two candidates; otherwise the exact deficit is unresolved. T2 shows 200 Package–Delivery object relations while E6 shows only 198 per-event bindings, potentially because of Delivery multiplicity or aggregation.

T3’s 92 Invoice–Payment relations are typical, not an obligation to attach every payment to an Invoice. The one untyped unapplied-cash possibility can fit T3 while still failing E8.

T4 and T5 are explicitly unspecified. The 100 Order–Customer and three Order–RiskCase edges therefore need not correspond to T1 or another mandatory column. The seven codeaths are ungoverned and semantically different from T6’s births. Likewise, N6’s one reopening-to-close path is not a violation.

### ET–OT

ET–OT is best suited to checking which object types participate in each event, provided the weights and event IDs are comparable. Aggregate equality for E1, E3, E4, E5, and E7 is reassuring but is not independent per-event certification.

E2, E6, and E8 have apparent participation deficits. E8’s explicit exception is not triggered by finance staff’s explanation alone: the event is not typed as unapplied cash. Review–Customer has no normative ET–OT requirement; E9 covers RiskCase, which is unspecified anyway.

Conforming at one abstraction can be suspicious at another. E5 attaches Package to all 200 Dispatch Package events, yet N4 lacks PackDispatch for two Packages. E1 covers all 100 creations, yet two lack the direct validation edge. Neither fact proves noncompliance by itself.

**The central abstraction danger is equating “eventually expected” with “directly follows next.”** A direct-follows diagnostic can screen candidates, but only a complete temporal cohort and a defined completion horizon can establish eventual non-validation.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

- **Mandatory behavior:** E1, E3, E4, E5, and E7 match their stated aggregate counts. T1 and T2 appear covered at object-graph level but still need event-level confirmation. Apparent deficits are N1 **2**, N4 **2**, E2 **2**, E6 **2**, and E8 **1**. If an Add Item has an Order object but no qualifying interaction binding, E2 can fail without necessarily proving T1 absent; aggregate 1,450 cannot decide this.

- **Forbidden behavior:** N3 has **2** observations; T6 has **1**; E10 has **1 conditional** observation. If T6 represents coalesced rather than genuinely shared birth, or E10 is solely the invalid join, those become data-quality findings. Genuine occurrences remain forbidden.

- **Typical behavior:** N2 has **83** direct closes; N5 has **92** payment paths, **4** recognized write-offs, and **14** unresolved no-successor cases; T3 has **92** Invoice–Payment edges. These patterns do not impose 100% coverage. The one payment becomes an E8 exception only if explicitly typed as unapplied cash under a clarified compatible rule.

- **Unregulated behavior:** N6 **1**, T4 **100**, T5 **3**, E9 RiskCase **3**, unlisted Review–Customer **3**, codeath **7**, CreateReview **3**, and ValidateIssue **72** are not violations. This classification changes if a future norm expressly governs those relations; it does not change merely because the behavior is uncommon.

## 5. PM4Py code sketch

```python
import pm4py

# Assumed supplied by the caller:
# ocel: observed object-centric event log
# norm_ocdfg: bare normative OC-DFG dictionary
# norm_otg: bare normative OTG tuple
# norm_etot: bare normative ET-OT tuple

# Preserve the discovered structures separately from conformance diagnostics.
raw_discovered = {
    "ocdfg": pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel),
    "otg": pm4py.discover_otg(ocel),
    "etot": pm4py.discover_etot(ocel),
}

# Preserve each complete returned diagnostic object; do not overwrite it.
raw_diagnostics = {
    "ocdfg": pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(
        raw_discovered["ocdfg"], norm_ocdfg, diagnostics=True
    ),
    "otg": pm4py.conformance_otg(
        raw_discovered["otg"], norm_otg, diagnostics=True
    ),
    "etot": pm4py.conformance_etot(
        raw_discovered["etot"], norm_etot, diagnostics=True
    ),
}

# REQUIRED SIDECAR METADATA:
# Bare graphs do not encode status, eventual timing, object identity rules,
# exception types, or whether an edge is per event, per object, or deduplicated.
# In particular, N1 is eventually mandatory, not a literal next-event rule.
POLICY = {
    "N1": ("mandatory", "new standard Order; eventual validation"),
    "N2": ("typical", "validated Order; intermediate events allowed"),
    "N3": ("forbidden", "dispatch after cancellation of the same Order"),
    "N4": ("mandatory", "Package intended for external shipment"),
    "N5": ("typical", "issued Invoice; legitimate exceptions"),
    "N6": ("unspecified", "reopened Order"),
    "T1": ("mandatory", "each Add Item interaction"),
    "T2": ("mandatory", "each Dispatch Package interaction"),
    "T3": ("typical", "Invoice; unapplied cash may remain unassociated"),
    "T4": ("unspecified", "Order-Customer association"),
    "T5": ("unspecified", "Order-RiskCase association"),
    "T6": ("forbidden", "actual simultaneous Order and Payment births"),
    "E1": ("mandatory", "every Create Order event"),
    "E2": ("mandatory", "every Add Item event includes its Order"),
    "E3": ("mandatory", "every Add Item event includes its Item"),
    "E4": ("mandatory", "every Pack Package event includes Package"),
    "E5": ("mandatory", "every Dispatch Package event includes Package"),
    "E6": ("mandatory", "every Dispatch Package event includes Delivery"),
    "E7": ("mandatory", "every Record Payment event includes Payment"),
    "E8": ("mandatory", "Invoice unless explicitly typed unapplied cash"),
    "E9": ("unspecified", "Manual Compliance Review-RiskCase"),
    "E10": ("forbidden", "Sensor Heartbeat-Invoice binding"),
}

def interpret(nid, *, applies, checked, conforms,
             context_ok=True, suspected_dq=False):
    """Separate business interpretation from PM4Py diagnostics.

    checked means scope, identity, and per-event/object coverage were checked
    using sidecar evidence. Aggregate graph equality alone is insufficient.
    """
    status, scope = POLICY[nid]

    if applies is None:
        cls = "insufficient_evidence"
    elif applies is False:
        cls = "outside_stated_scope"
    elif status == "unspecified":
        cls = "unexpected_but_unregulated"
    elif status == "forbidden":
        if not (checked and context_ok and conforms is not None):
            cls = "insufficient_evidence"
        elif not conforms and suspected_dq:
            cls = "likely_data_quality_error"
        elif not conforms:
            cls = "forbidden_behavior"
        else:
            cls = "conforming"
    elif status == "mandatory":
        if not (checked and context_ok and conforms is not None):
            cls = "insufficient_evidence"
        elif not conforms:
            cls = "missing_mandatory_behavior_in_observed_graph"
        else:
            cls = "conforming"
    else:  # typical
        cls = ("typical_pattern_present" if conforms is True
               else "typical_pattern_absence")

    return {
        "norm_id": nid,
        "norm_status": status,
        "scope": scope,
        "semantic_class": cls,
        "source_verification_required": bool(suspected_dq),
    }

# Illustrative evaluations using the supplied context, not graph-only claims.
# These flags must ultimately come from event/object bindings, identity rules,
# exception labels, temporal horizons, and data-lineage checks.
semantic_findings = [
    interpret("N1", applies=True, checked=False, conforms=False),
    interpret("N3", applies=True, checked=True, conforms=False),
    interpret("N4", applies=True, checked=True, conforms=False),
    interpret("T6", applies=True, checked=True, conforms=False),
    interpret("E2", applies=True, checked=False, conforms=False),
    interpret("E8", applies=True, checked=True, conforms=False),
    interpret("E10", applies=True, checked=True, conforms=False,
              suspected_dq=True),
    interpret("N5", applies=True, checked=False, conforms=False),
    interpret("T4", applies=True, checked=False, conforms=True),
]
```

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 completion horizon and standard-Order scope.** Risk: prematurely declaring Orders noncompliant before late validation. Clarify cohort inclusion, terminal status, and the deadline after which validation is overdue. **Mandatory eventual behavior; literal direct adjacency explicitly unspecified as the compliance test.**

2. **Counting and identity semantics.** Risk: treating 1,448 versus 1,450, or OTG 200 versus E6 198, as exact event gaps. Specify per-event binding, object reuse, and multiplicity. **Mandatory for claimed per-event coverage; alternative graph representations explicitly unspecified.**

3. **Packing and Delivery identity.** Risk: inconsistent shipment audits when carrier references substitute for Delivery objects. Define external-shipment scope, split/return deliveries, and whether a carrier reference alone satisfies the rule. **Mandatory Delivery binding per Dispatch; multiplicity details initially explicitly unspecified.**

4. **E8 versus T3 unapplied cash.** Risk: either penalizing legitimate cash or using T3 to waive E8 silently. Define a typed unapplied-cash exception and require allocation or review. **Mandatory Payment binding and explicitly typed conditional Invoice binding; T3 remains typical.**

5. **Same-Order and simultaneous-birth semantics.** Risk: confusing reopened Order incarnations or timestamp proximity with actual identity and birth. Define lifecycle identity and birth-event lineage. **Forbidden for the same actual Order/Payment birth event; reincarnation representation explicitly unspecified.**

6. **Identifier qualification and referential integrity.** Risk: creating the Sensor–Invoice edge or missing valid relations through reused IDs. Require qualified keys and validated bindings. **E10 remains forbidden; mandatory integrity checks must operate before business interpretation.**

7. **Ownership of unspecified relations.** Risk: inconsistent treatment of Customer, RiskCase, Review–Customer, reopening, and codeath. Assign data ownership without inventing obligations. **Explicitly unspecified under the current norm.**

8. **Intermediate events, exception evidence, and numerical thresholds.** Risk: rejecting unusual paths, deleting the 14 no-successor cases, or treating 98/100 as an allowed error rate. Allow additional intermediates and retain reasons for claimed exceptions. **Relations explicitly unspecified; exception evidence mandatory if relied upon. Mandatory/forbidden relations receive no percentage tolerance; typical-pattern frequencies remain nonbinding.**

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

### Immediate containment

Prioritize **N3’s two post-cancellation dispatches**: stop further release where possible, establish authorization, and assess customer or recovery actions. Next prioritize **T6’s one co-birth edge**, preserving evidence and verifying actual birth lineage rather than deleting it. Immediately reconcile the two missing Delivery event bindings and packing histories.

### Data repair

Repair qualified identifiers, event-to-object mappings, and the suspected Sensor join using traceable corrections. Classify the one untyped payment and investigate the two apparent Add Item deficits. Preserve original graphs and diagnostics; do not fabricate direct validation edges or Delivery relations from carrier references alone.

### Process investigation

Review the cancellation-release controls behind N3 and the event-lineage process behind T6. Examine the individual Orders behind N1 and Invoices behind the 14 unresolved no-successor cases. Do not launch broad remediation merely because a typical pattern is uncommon.

### Norm revision

Address the N1 horizon, per-event counting rules, unapplied-cash typing, Delivery identity, and birth semantics from section 6. Keep unspecified relations unspecified until a policy owner supplies an actual obligation or prohibition.

### Monitoring

Monitor explicit forbidden relations and in-scope mandatory coverage, but report percentages as diagnostics—not tolerated deviations. Add controls for qualified identifiers, untyped unapplied cash, and event-level object bindings. Retain separate dashboards for direct-follows approximations and eventual business outcomes.
