- **Contract terms must match committee decision.** A9 (contract generation) with rulebased checks directly addresses this control finding. The rule library must be maintained, and all overrides must be auditable.
- **Auditable trail (control finding4)** applies to any automated recommendation that involves human override. A2 (classifier), A3 (KYC preclassifier), A6 (normalization) and A9 (contract generation) all require persistent logs of model output, human decision, justification, and final state.

## Human escalation capacity
- **Compliance specialists (3FTE)** are the primary bottleneck for entitymismatch resolution (A4) and complex KYC hits (A3). Automating routine mismatch checks (A4) can free their capacity for truly complex cases, but the handover network shows high dependency from KYC ops to two senior specialists. Any KYC automation (A3) must escalate only ambiguous hits, avoiding overwhelming the specialists with falsepositive flags.
- **Credit analysts (9FTE)** sit at the centre of the workingtogether network. Automating upstream steps (A1, A2, A5, A6, even A7) will accelerate the inflow of “clean” cases to credit analysis, potentially overloading the team unless their own work is made more efficient by A6 (normalization) or unless capacity is explicitly planned. The processmining data warns that improving inflow without normalization can create a creditanalysis queue.
- **Relationship managers (RMs)** are implicated in missingdocument loops (sending incomplete instructions). A5 portal must reduce RMdriven friction, not add to it; otherwise RM resistance will undermine adoption.
- **Legal ops (4FTE)** are underutilised when KYC is slow but overloaded at monthend. Faster KYC and contract automation (A9) would smooth their workload across the month, reducing the peak. This is a positive side effect but must be monitored.

## External valuation dependency
- Collateral valuation (A7) relies on external valuers whose turnaround (58h wait) is largely outside direct control. Triage (A7) can redirect simple valuations to quicker partners or parallelise, but it cannot eliminate the external dependency. Any automation that accelerates internal steps will still be gated by this wait for Equipmentfinance cases (71% of that product). The DFG reveals that 1980 cases flow from external valuation wait directly to committee packaging, so this is a genuine blocking point for that segment.

## Corebanking integration constraints
- Only two modules can be integrated deeply with the core banking system in the first six months. Deep integration is required for:
  - **A2 (classification)** – needs productmaster, customer risk profiles, exposurelimit data.
  - **A9 (contract generation)** – must pull committee decision terms and push contract data to core.
  - **A11 (loan activation)** – must validate control completeness against core records (but this is lower priority in Year1).
- Other modules (A3, A4, A5, A6, A10, A12) can function with lighter integration through APIs or documentmanagement layers in early phases, as long as data exchange is well defined.

# 3. Portfolio alternatives

## Portfolio1: Quickwin
**Modules selected**: A1 (autoregistration), A2 (classification recommender), A5 (missingdocument portal), A9 (contract generation with controlrule checks)

**What it optimises**:
- Political attractiveness and visible labour savings from highfrequency activities (A1, A9).
- Reduces misrouting (A2) and documentloop rework (A5).
- A9 strengthens control on contract/decision alignment, satisfying the compliance requirement.

**What it sacrifices**:
- Does not address the longtail delays: entity mismatch (31h), financial statement normalisation (20h), collateral valuation (58h). Endtoend cycle time may not drop as senior management hopes because these blocking points persist.
- Pushes more load into credit analysis without improving analyst productivity; risk of analyst overload and longer queues downstream.
- Ignores the KYCsigning control gap (A10 automation without gate could worsen provisional signing).
- A1, while highfrequency, adds volume to classify and document review without first stabilising them.

## Portfolio2: Bottleneck / synchronisation
**Modules selected**: A4 (entitymismatch resolution assistant), A5 (missingdocument portal), A6 (financialstatement normalisation), A7 (collateral valuation triage and routing)

**What it optimises**:
- Directly attacks the largest waitingtime hotspots: entity mismatch (31h), missingdoc loops (8h+), financial normalisation (20h), collateral valuation (58h). Endtoend timeline for the most delayed cases is substantially cut.
- Frees scarce compliance specialists (A4) and credit analysts (A6), balancing resource demand.
- Complements concurrent KYC/documentreview paths; A4 and A5/A6 work on both branches.

**What it sacrifices**:
- No highfrequency automation like registration or contract prep; labour savings appear modest in headcount terms, which may disappoint stakeholders seeking “quick wins”.
- Does not directly address the contractremediation control finding (8.5% loops) or the foureyes violations; controlstrengthening is indirect.
- Committee packaging (A8) still manual; although upstream artifacts become more reliable, the packaging step itself remains unimproved.
- A7 impact is limited to a small subset of cases (Equipment finance and some Workingcapital) and depends on external partners.

## Portfolio3: Compliancefirst
**Modules selected**: A3 (KYC preclassifier with human escalation), A4 (entitymismatch resolution assistant), A9 (contract generation with controlrule checks), A12 (control remediation workbench)

**What it optimises**:
- Maximum controlrisk reduction: tackles KYC integrity (A3, A4), contractdecision mismatch (A9), and overall control exception management (A12).
- Addresses the most severe regulatory findings and builds foundational audit trails.
- A4 notably cuts the compliancespecialist bottleneck, and A3 could improve KYC throughput while preserving human oversight for suspicious cases.

**What it sacrifices**:
- No focus on throughput and cycletime reduction for the majority of cases; waiting times in financial document review, normalisation, credit analysis and customer signing remain largely untouched.
- A3 carries high falsenegative risk and demands rigorous validation, potentially delaying benefits. External stakeholder confidence may be slow to build.
- Resource demand on credit analysts and legal ops may become more erratic because upstream volumes are not smoothed.
- Political pressure for a “simple highfrequency win” is unmet; the portfolio lacks visible laboursaving headlines.

# 5. Risks and compensating controls

## Risks introduced by automation

1. **False negatives in KYC preclassification (A3)** – missed sanctions or PEP hits  severe regulatory fines, reputation damage.  
   *Compensating control:* All hits screened by algorithm are reviewed by compliance specialist; confidence scores gate escalation; audit trail captures every override. Maintain a “safe list” only after multilevel approval.

2. **Misclassification of product/exposure (A2)** – highexposure SME routed as micro avoids committee  breaks foureyes and regulatory exposure limits.  
   *Compensating control:* Mandatory human confirmation for borderline scores; monthly sampling of overrides; ability to reroute ‘micro’ cases that exceed thresholds postfactum.

3. **Model drift in financial normalisation (A6)** – extraction accuracy degrades with new document formats or lowquality PDFs  errors feed credit models, causing incorrect decisions.  
   *Compensating control:* Continuous performance monitoring (accuracy, rejection rate) with thresholdbased retraining; human review of all lowconfidence outputs; fallback to manual entry.

4. **Outdated contract rule library (A9)** – a missing or wrong rule generates noncompliant contracts  legal disputes and regulatory penalty.  
   *Compensating control:* Rule library under version control, gated by legal signoff; all generated contracts logged with rule set version; sampling audit by legal ops.

5. **Automation of customerfacing nudging (A5) creates friction** – rigid templated requests may alienate SMEs and increase dropouts.  
   *Compensating control:* Portal built with UX flexibility (naturallanguage guidance, chat escalation); RM override to customise checklists; track abandonment rates and NPS feedback.

6. **Esignature enabled without KYC clearance (A10)** – if signing automation does not check KYC flag, it will increase provisional signing and later remediation.  
   *Compensating control:* Technical lock in signing workflow – esignature button disabled until system receives KYCcleared flag from core. Audit of all signing events with KYC timestamp.

7. **Foureyes violation increased by faster flow (A8, A2)** – if work speeds up, same analyst may rush to complete both analysis and packaging, especially at monthend.  
   *Compensating control:* Workbench enforces role segregation; work assignment engine prevents the same user from claiming both tasks on the same case; escalation if violated.

8. **Corebanking integration bottleneck** – with only two deep integrations in first six months, modules that need realtime data (A2, A9) must be prioritised; lighter integrations may rely on batch feeds.  
   *Compensating control:* Design all other modules to work with asynchronous APIs or filebased updates initially, with data reconciliation monitors.

9. **Unrealised dependency on external valuers (A7)** – triage may not materially reduce wait if thirdparty capacity remains fixed.  
   *Compensating control:* Formal SLAs with valuers; build internal capacity for standard valuations; monitor turnaround time and fallback to internal resources when exceeded.

10. **Human escalation overload** – if automation reduces the bulk of easy work, the remaining specialist workload may consist entirely of complex, higheffort cases, potentially increasing burnout.  
    *Compensating control:* Workload monitoring, flexible assignment, and regular capacity assessment. Possibly redistribute some tasks across the team.

## Auditability and responsibility

All automated modules that make suggestions (A2, A3, A6, A9) must log: unique model version, confidence score (where applicable), timestamp, output, human decision, outofpattern override reason, and final data committed. The control remediation workbench (A12) must provide a unified view of all logged decisions to auditors. Human signoffs remain legally responsible; automation is advisory or rulebased execution only where policy is deterministic.

# 6. Recommended phased rollout

I recommend a **blended portfolio** that balances processlevel benefit, control strengthening, and practical implementation constraints. The chosen modules are **A2 (classification recommender), A5 (missingdocument portal), A6 (financialstatement normalisation), A9 (contract generation with controlrule checks)**. This portfolio is elaborated in Section7.

Below is the phased plan respecting the twodeepintegration limit in the first six months.

## Phase1 (Months1–6): Deep integration modules
**Modules**: A2 (classification recommender) and A9 (contract generation)  
**Rationale**: Both require corebanking data – product/xposure master, customer risk profiles, committeeapproved terms, legal rules. Deep integration establishes the backbone for clean classification and compliant contract output.

**Monitoring metrics**:
- Classification accuracy (postoverride) and misrouting rate (target: drop from 8% to <3%)
- Contractremediation loop rate (target: drop from 8.5% to <4%)
- Number of contractrelated control violations per 1000 cases
- Legal ops utilisation spread (monthend peak vs midmonth)
- Audit trail completeness (100% of overrides logged)

**Gate criteria to proceed to Phase2**:
- Classification accuracy 95% on sampled overridden cases, with no highexposure misroutings in last month
- Contractremediation rate reduced by 40% and no reported legal issues from automated clauses
- Audit trail fully operational and reviewed by internal audit
- User satisfaction (credit analysts, legal ops) above acceptable threshold

## Phase2 (Months7–12): Lightintegration modules
**Modules**: A5 (missingdocument portal) and A6 (financialstatement extraction/normalisation)  
**Integration**: A5 needs customer portal integration (not core banking) and documentmanagement APIs. A6 ingests documents and outputs structured data to the credit system, which can be done through an API layer without deep corebanking hooks.

**Monitoring metrics**:
- Documentchecklist completion rate (% of cases with no missingdoc loop) – target >70% from ~50%
- Normalisation accuracy and rejection rate due to lowquality PDFs
- Creditanalyst utilisation and queue wait before “Normalize financial statements” (target: reduce median wait from 20h to <8h)
- Rework in financial document review (target: <15% from 24%)
- Customer friction feedback (portal abandonment, NPS)

**Gate criteria to consider future phases (Year2)**:
- No increase in provisional signing or foureyes violations after A5/A6 rollout
- Normalisation accuracy 90% with effective human fallback
- Credit analyst workload within planned capacity (overtime below threshold)
- Positive feedback from customers and RMs

Year2 candidates would then include A4 (entitymismatch assistant), A10 (esignature with KYC gate), and A12 (control workbench) depending on the remaining bottleneck profile.

# 7. Final recommendation

I recommend **Portfolio Blend: A2+A5+A6+A9** for Year1, implemented in the phased rollout above.

## Why not the highestfrequency activity (A1) first
Automating registration (A1) would feel like a quick win, but frequency alone is misleading. Processmining evidence shows that:

- The **waiting time** for registration is only **1.5hours** – not a dominant contributor to endtoend delay. The real cycletime killers are **entity mismatch (31h), financialstatement normalisation (20h), collateral valuation (58h), missingdocument loops, and contract/signing waits.**
- **Downstream bottleneck risk is severe**: A1 would push cases faster into classification (already 8% rework), KYC (11% rework, compliance bottleneck), and financial document review (24% rework). The credit analyst team (9FTE) would face a wave of unnormalised files, lengthening the very delays that matter.
- Politically attractive labour savings in the backoffice would mask the degradation in creditdecision latency and risk quality. The bank would be optimising a step that is already relatively frictionless in time terms, while leaving the real constraints untouched.

## Portfolio logic
This portfolio **attacks the three highestleverage areas simultaneously**:
1. **Data quality at source** (A2) – prevents misrouting and keeps highexposure cases on the correct control path.
2. **Document completeness and loops** (A5) – removes the largest driver of rework in the earlystage path, improving analyst and RM experience without heavy customer friction.
3. **Analyst capacity and pace** (A6) – normalisation is the bridge between document receipt and credit analysis; automating it both reduces waiting time and ensures that analysts are not inundated with unprocessed work, avoiding the overload that processmining flagged.
4. **Controlstrengthening with substantial labour savings** (A9) – directly resolves the contract/decision mismatch control finding, reduces legal ops monthend strain, and gives management a visible, highfrequency efficiency story (7600 contracts/year) while satisfying the compliance mandate.

## Tradeoff justification
- We **sacrifice immediate attention to the entitymismatch tail** (A4) and **collateral valuation wait** (A7). Those remain for Year2, but by giving the bank a stable, compliant core (classification, documentation, normalisation, contract), we build the prerequisite for those later enhancements. Introducing A4 without clean entity data from A2 would risk limited effectiveness.
- We **defer esignature (A10) and activation bot (A11)** because they are unsafe until KYCsigning gate is enforced, which can be done as a control fix alongside A9 rather than via a full automation module. This avoids the risk of increased provisional signing.
- The portfolio respects the budget (exactly four modules), the integration constraint (two deep integrations first), and the compliance requirement (A9 as the controlstrengthening element).

## Expected outcome
Endtoend cycle time will drop for the broad middle of cases because rework and normalisation wait are reduced. Control violations on contract/decision alignment and foureyes will be measurably lower. Credit analysts will receive structured, complete inputs, reducing their manual cleanup work and allowing them to focus on judgment. When Year2 brings mismatch assistance and esignature, the full process will accelerate further without having created a fragile, bottleneckridden frontend.
