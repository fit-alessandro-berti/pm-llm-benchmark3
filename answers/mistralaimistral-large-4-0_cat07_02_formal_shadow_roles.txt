# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

**Automation Boundary (R14_Nora).** Core A1/A2 are high-frequency (760/610) but low complexity; no peripheral activities. Authority: none; system. Substitution: none. Naïve frequency would overstate human front-office capacity.

**Front Office Routine Agent (R02_Ben).** Core A1/A2/A4/A12 are high-frequency; peripheral counts are low. Complexity is low/medium. Authority: entitlement only. Substitution: not observed. Peripheral work is occasional, not a second role.

**Front Office Generalist (R01_Amira).** Core A1/A2/A4/A12 are high-frequency; peripheral A3/A5/A7/A8/A10/A11/A13/A14 are moderate. Complexity is slightly higher than Ben’s. Authority: entitlement; no formal approval. Substitution: may absorb rework and medium/high cases.

**Temporary Front Office Support (R11_Kai, R12_Lina).** Core A1/A2/A4/A12 are high-frequency; peripheral activities are minimal. Complexity is low only. Authority: none. Substitution: seasonal; weeks 8–10 closures caused 17 reopens due incomplete A2 notes; this is not evidence of permanent competence.

**Senior Front Office / Commercial Shadow (R07_Giorgio).** Core front-office A1/A2/A4/A12 are high-frequency; peripheral A7/A8/A9 are substantial. Complexity is medium/high. Authority: pricing certification but informal; during Fatima’s absence pricing duration doubled. Substitution: covered Fatima weeks 3–4; not equivalent to formal commercial authority.

**Technical Resolution Specialist — Standard (R04_Dina).** Core A3/A5/A10 are high-frequency; peripheral activities are low. Complexity is medium. Authority: configuration. Substitution: none.

**Technical Resolution Specialist — Complex/Senior (R03_Chen).** Core A3/A5/A10 are high-frequency; peripheral A6 security and others are moderate. Complexity is high/critical. Authority: configuration; late shift. Substitution: none.

**Security Specialist (R05_Erik).** Core A6 is high-frequency for this resource; peripheral A3/A5/A10 etc. are moderate. Complexity is high/critical. Authority: security review. Substitution: Mateo filtered low-risk referrals weeks 7–8, causing two critical cases to wait >2 days; rare critical.

**Commercial Specialist (R06_Fatima).** Core A7/A8 are high-frequency; peripheral A2/A9 etc. are moderate. Complexity is medium/high. Authority: pricing. Substitution: Giorgio covered, but duration rose; not equivalent.

**Approval Authority (R08_Hana).** Core A9 is high-frequency; peripheral A8/A13/A11 etc. are moderate. Complexity is high/critical. Authority: approval. Substitution: Mateo’s preliminary approvals were rechecked 13/21; shows authority boundary.

**Quality Gatekeeper / KB Steward (R09_Ivan, R10_Julia).** Core A11/A14 are high-frequency; peripheral A10/A12/A3/A5 are moderate. Complexity is medium. Authority: quality. Substitution: none; shift coverage differs.

**Cross-functional Escalation Broker (R13_Mateo).** Core is coordination across A13/A9/A12/A2/A4, not a single dominant activity; peripheral activities touch all others. Complexity is high/critical. Authority: no formal decision authority; preliminary approvals only. Substitution: covered Hana and filtered security; risks exist.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

**R07_Giorgio — commercial shadow role.**  
Evidence: formal title is Senior Service Agent in Front Office, but certifications include entitlement and pricing. He performs 72 A7 Pricing Exception Review and 88 A8 Customer Negotiation events, plus 20 A9 Supervisor Approval events. Similarity to Fatima is 0.71, and similarity to Amira is 0.84. During weeks 3–4, when Fatima was absent, Giorgio performed 31 A7 and 18 A8 events, but median pricing exception duration rose from 38 to 74 minutes.  
Benefits: provides resilience, continuity, and development opportunities.  
Risks: informal authority may bypass commercial controls; slower pricing decisions; shadow role may become normalized without formal authority.  
This is not inherently improper, but it needs governance: dual control for pricing exceptions, clear approval rights, and monitoring of exception duration.

**R13_Mateo — cross-functional escalation broker.**  
Evidence: touches all 14 activities but has no single dominant activity. Fragments 3 and 5 show Mateo coordinating after security review and after reopen. During week 5, he performed 21 preliminary A9 Supervisor Approval events, but 13 were rechecked by Hana. During weeks 7–8, he filtered low-risk security referrals, reducing false escalation complaints, but two critical cases waited >2 days.  
Benefits: bridges silos, reduces false escalations, coordinates reopen work, and improves flow.  
Risks: may accumulate de facto authority; filtering can delay critical cases; preliminary approvals may be mistaken for final authority.  
Not improper, but requires clear authority boundaries, audit of preliminary approvals, and escalation thresholds.

**R05_Erik — rare but critical specialist.**  
Evidence: 96 A6 Security Review events, median complexity 0.86, with 82 high-complexity and 55 critical cases. During weeks 7–8, Erik performed fewer A6 events because Mateo filtered low-risk referrals; two critical cases waited >2 days.  
Benefits: deep security expertise, critical intervention capability, risk reduction.  
Risks: single point of failure, burnout, delay when referrals are filtered or when Erik is unavailable.  
Not improper, but needs backup coverage, on-call arrangements, and clear criteria for security referral.

**Additional boundary roles.**  
- **R14_Nora — automation boundary role.** Handles high-volume low-complexity A1/A2, but cannot handle exceptions. Risk: over-reliance and deskilling of human agents.  
- **R11_Kai / R12_Lina — temporary workforce bridge.** Provide seasonal capacity for low-complexity cases, but weeks 8–10 closures led to 17 reopens due incomplete Validate Entitlement notes. Risk: treating temporary substitution as permanent competence.  
- **R09_Ivan / R10_Julia — quality gatekeeping bridge.** Core A11/A14, but also perform technical work. Risk: if clustered only by activity, shift coverage and quality authority may be missed.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
import networkx as nx

# 1. Load and format the event log
df = pd.read_csv("order_to_resolution_event_log.csv")
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    resource_key="org:resource"
)

# Ensure case-level attributes are present
case_attributes = ["case_complexity", "customer_segment", "product_line",
                   "temporary_substitution", "escalation_level"]
for attr in case_attributes:
    if attr not in log.columns:
        raise ValueError(f"Missing case attribute: {attr}")

# 2. Discover organizational roles (activity-based resource clustering)
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(
    log,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
    clustering=True
)
print("Organizational roles:", roles)

# Normalize role output to a resource -> role label mapping.
# PM4Py may return a list of resource sets or a dict; inspect before using.
resource_to_role = {}
if isinstance(roles, list):
    for i, role_members in enumerate(roles):
        for member in role_members:
            resource_to_role[member] = f"mined_role_{i}"
elif isinstance(roles, dict):
    for role_id, members in roles.items():
        for member in members:
            resource_to_role[member] = str(role_id)

# 3. Activity-based resource similarity
similarity_df = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
    log,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
    similarity_metric="cosine"
)
print(similarity_df)

# 4. Handover-of-work and working-together networks
handover_net = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
    log,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
    threshold=0.0,
    include_start_activities=False,
    include_complete_activities=False
)
# handover_net is a networkx DiGraph: nodes are activities, edges are handovers
for u, v, data in handover_net.edges(data=True):
    print(f"Handover: {u} -> {v} weight={data.get('weight', data)}")

working_together_net = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(
    log,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
    threshold=0.0
)
# working_together_net is a networkx Graph: nodes are resources
for u, v, data in working_together_net.edges(data=True):
    print(f"Working together: {u} -- {v} weight={data.get('weight', data)}")

subcontracting_net = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(
    log,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
    threshold=0.0
)
# subcontracting_net is a networkx DiGraph: edges show delegation/subcontracting
for u, v, data in subcontracting_net.edges(data=True):
    print(f"Subcontracting: {u} -> {v} weight={data.get('weight', data)}")

# 5. Build case-level attributes and join with resource role/similarity
case_attrs = (
    log.groupby("case:concept:name")
       .agg(
           case_complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
           customer_segment=("customer_segment", "first"),
           product_line=("product_line", "first"),
           temporary_substitution=("temporary_substitution", "first"),
           escalation_level=("escalation_level", "first")
       )
       .reset_index()
)

resource_summary = (
    log.groupby("org:resource")
       .agg(
           event_count=("concept:name", "count"),
           distinct_activities=("concept:name", "nunique"),
           median_case_complexity=("case_complexity", "median")
       )
       .reset_index()
)

complexity_dist = (
    log.groupby(["org:resource", "case_complexity"])
       .size()
       .unstack(fill_value=0)
       .add_prefix("complexity_")
       .reset_index()
)

resource_summary = resource_summary.merge(complexity_dist, on="org:resource", how="left")
resource_summary["mined_role"] = resource_summary["org:resource"].map(resource_to_role)

# Join case attributes back to events for case-level analysis
log = log.merge(case_attrs, on="case:concept:name", how="left")

# 6. Time-window analysis to detect temporary substitution effects
log = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
    log,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
)

# Example: compare pricing exception duration by week and resource
pricing_events = log[log["concept:name"] == "A7 Pricing Exception Review"].copy()
pricing_events["week"] = pricing_events["time:timestamp"].dt.isocalendar().week

waiting_cols = [c for c in pricing_events.columns if "waiting_time" in c]
if waiting_cols:
    weekly_pricing = (
        pricing_events.groupby(["week", "org:resource"])
                    .agg(median_waiting_time=(waiting_cols[0], "median"),
                         count=("concept:name", "count"))
                    .reset_index()
    )
    print(weekly_pricing)

# 7. Optional: trace variants to see routine process paths
variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)
print("Variants:", variants)

# 8. Caution: do not treat mined roles as formal authority.
# Validate with formal attributes: title, team, certifications, contract, shift.
# Use handover/working-together networks to see actual collaboration.
# Use case complexity and temporary_substitution to avoid over-generalizing.
```

# 6. Operational interpretation

**Staffing.**  
Use the role structure to align shifts and complexity. Day shift: front-office routine (Ben, Amira, temps), commercial (Fatima), approval (Hana), quality (Julia). Late shift: technical complex (Chen), quality (Ivan), temp (Lina). 24/7 automation (Nora) handles low-complexity intake. Ensure security specialist (Erik) has on-call coverage for high/critical cases. Cross-train backups but do not replace specialists.

**Training.**  
Use core/peripheral profiles to design career paths: temps  permanent front office; front office  technical/commercial; quality analysts  KB stewardship. Validate with complexity and outcomes, not just activity counts.

**Risk control.**  
Monitor shadow roles: Giorgio’s pricing approvals require dual control; Mateo’s preliminary approvals require Hana recheck; Erik is a single point of failure; temp closures require validation completeness checks. Track reopen rates and pricing exception durations.

**Resilience.**  
Maintain backup coverage for rare specialists (Erik) and commercial (Fatima) with supervised shadowing; use automation for low complexity but keep a human exception path; ensure late-shift coverage for technical and quality.

**Cautions about changing staffing based only on mined roles.**  
1. Do not change staffing based only on mined activity-frequency clusters: Giorgio’s A7/A8 counts during Fatima’s absence reflect temporary substitution, not permanent competence; pricing duration doubled.  
2. Do not assume similar activity profiles imply similar authority or risk: Chen and Dina are similar at 0.94, but Chen handles critical/security-adjacent cases; Ivan and Julia are similar at 0.97, but shift coverage differs.  
3. Do not treat the automation bot as human capacity: Nora’s high A1/A2 counts are system throughput, not staffing.  
4. Do not ignore case complexity: temps handle low complexity; assigning them medium/high cases would increase reopen risk.
