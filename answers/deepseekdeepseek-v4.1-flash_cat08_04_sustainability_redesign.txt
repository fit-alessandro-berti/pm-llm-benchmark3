# 3. Intervention options

**S1. Green shipping recommender**
- Target: shipments with route slack, especially Standard-stock Service-contract and non-critical MTO.
- Mechanism: use promised_lead_time_hours, route reliability, customer_type, expedite_flag, and transport_kgco2e to choose ground/consolidated truck when SP1’s 85% service-probability condition is met.
- Service/quality/resilience: protects service by slack, not blanket air reduction; risk if route reliability is misestimated. Keep R1 alternative route available.
- Evidence to monitor: SP1 conformance, recommendation vs actual route, late delivery by slack bin, air share, transport kgCO2e, override reasons.

**S2. Dynamic batching for CNC and heat treatment**
- Target: Made-to-order paths with Batch wait -> CNC/heat treatment.
- Mechanism: batch toward CNC_A 12–20 and heat-treatment >10 where energy/item falls, but enforce maximum waiting-time caps by segment and due-date slack.
- Service/quality/resilience: energy/item down, but late risk and obsolescence up if caps fail; heat-treatment batch waiting already has 1.7x late-delivery risk.
- Evidence: batch_size, batch wait, heat wait, energy/item, late delivery, 14-day obsolescence, rework by machine_group.

**S3. Shift Made-to-order from CNC_B to CNC_A when slack allows**
- Target: MTO cases routed to CNC_B or repeatedly entering rework.
- Mechanism: CNC_A has 9% rework vs CNC_B 18%; fewer rework loops reduce hidden material waste and rework energy.
- Service/quality/resilience: quality and scrap improve if CNC_A queue allows; otherwise cycle time and late risk rise. Keep CNC_B for recovery/expedite.
- Evidence: machine_group, queue time, due-date slack, rework_flag, scrap, energy, late delivery.

**S4. Replace air for Standard-stock Service-contract with priority pick + ground**
- Target: Standard-stock Service-contract air shipments where SP1 is violated or slack is sufficient.
- Mechanism: air emits about 4–6x more kgCO2e than ground; priority picking can offset some transport time while preserving promise.
- Service/quality/resilience: ground adds transport time; use only when promised lead time still has >85% success probability. Keep air for genuinely insufficient slack and maintain alternative route.
- Evidence: promised_lead_time_hours, SP1 violation rate, route_mode, late delivery, transport kgCO2e, priority-pick time.

**S5. Consolidate truck departures twice daily for non-urgent stock**
- Target: non-urgent Standard-stock ground parcels.
- Mechanism: consolidated trucks reduce transport kgCO2e by about 35–55% vs individual ground parcels, at cost of 12–24h consolidation wait.
- Service/quality/resilience: acceptable only for non-urgent stock with slack; avoid Critical and tight Service-contract promises. Consolidation can reduce route flexibility, so preserve alternative carrier/route.
- Evidence: consolidation wait, route_mode, late delivery, transport kgCO2e, service by segment.

**S6. Improve packaging-route compatibility checks**
- Target: Package standard/reinforced/rework packaging before shipment.
- Mechanism: prevent incompatible packaging and rework loops; enforce Q1 for MTO >8 kg unless inspection certifies low damage risk; avoid unnecessary reinforced packaging when risk is certified low.
- Service/quality/resilience: fewer packaging rework loops, fewer damage returns, shorter cycle time; reinforced packaging can double material, so use where justified.
- Evidence: packaging rework rate, Q1 violations, package_material_kg, damage returns, route_mode/part geometry.

**S7. Add inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations**
- Target: inspection_cell, CNC_B, and high-rework MTO combinations.
- Mechanism: catch defects before packaging/customer shipment, reducing Inspection -> Rework production -> CNC loops and downstream returns.
- Service/quality/resilience: lowers scrap/rework energy but may add inspection queue; resource the gate to avoid rushed inspections. Improves quality and resilience if capacity is maintained.
- Evidence: inspection queue, rework production loops, scrap, returns, rework by machine/part.

**S8. Replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes**
- Target: Critical-downtime cases that are not true downtime, or where shared lanes can still meet SLA.
- Mechanism: reduce dedicated-van empty returns and low-load trips; true Critical-downtime keeps dedicated vans/air.
- Service/quality/resilience: shared lanes may add small wait and create single-point dependency; retain dedicated capacity for true downtime and R1 alternative routes.
- Evidence: SP2 justification, true-vs-precautionary flag, response time, transport kgCO2e, late delivery, empty-return data if added.

**S9. Split Critical-downtime into true downtime and precautionary urgent**
- Target: Critical-downtime Service-contract segment.
- Mechanism: true downtime uses emergency production/dedicated van/air; precautionary urgent can use stock/ground/consolidated when service allows.
- Service/quality/resilience: protects true-critical service; risk is misclassification. Frees dedicated capacity and reduces transport emissions for precautionary cases.
- Evidence: new urgency tier, SP2 justification, dedicated-van share, late delivery by tier, reclassification/upgrade rates.

**S10. Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular made-to-order**
- Target: Made-to-order Regular customers.
- Mechanism: offer longer promised lead time in exchange for batching/ground routing, reducing air/expedite and improving batch energy per item.
- Service/quality/resilience: service can be maintained if promises are realistic; risk of cancellations or customers choosing faster options. Do not apply to true Critical or Service-contract without agreement.
- Evidence: promised_lead_time_hours, accepted/declined offers, cancellations, route/batch choice, late delivery, transport, energy.

**S11. Reduce partial shipments by changing availability-check rules**
- Target: Pick from stock -> Partial shipment -> Backorder wait/cancel path.
- Mechanism: improve ATP/reservation/allocation and combine line items when complete-order waiting still meets segment SLA; avoid second shipment and backorder loops.
- Service/quality/resilience: cuts transport, packaging, scrap, and cycle time; but waiting for complete order can delay available items. Use caps and allow split for Critical/tight service.
- Evidence: partial shipment rate, backorder wait, cancellations, transport/order, package material, late delivery, availability-check rules.

**S12. Sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides**
- Target: policy overrides across shipping, vans, packaging.
- Mechanism: require structured justification for SP1/SP2/Q1 exceptions; reduces silent nonconformance and enables targeted process correction.
- Service/quality/resilience: protects service by allowing justified exceptions while making them visible; improves resilience planning and Q1 traceability.
- Evidence: exception rate, reason codes, SP1/SP2/Q1 violation rates, override outcomes, late/rework, transport/energy.

# 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching rebound:** Larger CNC/heat-treatment batches reduce energy/item, but increase batch wait and late-delivery risk; if forecasts fail, obsolescence rises and 6% of batch-produced items may not ship within 14 days, displacing energy savings into wasted material.
2. **Transport modal shift rebound:** Moving Service-contract air to ground or consolidation lowers transport kgCO2e, but if lead-time slack is misjudged, late orders may trigger expedite, emergency production, or air recovery that outweighs savings.
3. **Packaging displacement:** Reinforced packaging cuts damage returns by 40% for heavy MTO, but doubles package material. Overuse increases material impact; underuse increases returns, rework, and reverse logistics.
4. **Rework displacement:** An inspection gate can reduce rework production loops, but if inspection_cell queues grow, rushed inspections may miss defects and create downstream packaging/customer returns.
5. **Dedicated-van reduction:** Replacing vans with shared courier lanes lowers emissions for precautionary urgency, but true downtime misclassification can cause unacceptable service failure and emergency recovery emissions.
6. **Partial-shipment consolidation:** Waiting to ship complete orders reduces second shipments, but may delay available parts; customer pressure can then trigger express shipments or cancellations, offsetting transport savings.
7. **Customer behavior:** Carbon-aware promise-date negotiation may shift Regular MTO to slower green routes, but some customers may choose faster competitors or demand expedites, creating rebound through lost/rewritten orders.
8. **CNC routing displacement:** Shifting MTO from CNC_B to CNC_A reduces rework, but can bottleneck CNC_A and heat treatment, causing emergency production, overtime, or expedited transport.
9. **Exception-log erosion:** If SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides become routine, governance weakens and unjustified air/dedicated-van use returns under the label of exceptions.

# 6. Balanced recommendation

Use a segment-tiered, slack-based redesign that treats quality and rework reduction as the first environmental lever, then optimizes transport and batching within service caps.

Recommended package:

1. **Tier urgency and customer promises first.** Implement S9 to split true Critical-downtime from precautionary urgent, and S12 for SP1/SP2/Q1 exception governance. True downtime keeps dedicated van/emergency/air; precautionary urgent can use stock/ground/consolidated if SLA permits.
2. **Fix quality before pushing green routing.** Deploy S7 for high-rework CNC_B/MTO combinations and S6 for packaging-route compatibility and Q1 enforcement. This reduces hidden scrap, rework energy, packaging loops, and damage returns.
3. **Use slack-based transport decisions.** Implement S1, S4, S5, and S10. Replace Standard-stock Service-contract air with priority pick + ground only when promised lead time still has >85% success probability. Consolidate non-urgent stock twice daily. Offer Regular MTO carbon-aware promise dates, but keep air/dedicated options for tight Service-contract and Critical cases.
4. **Batch with caps, not maximization.** Use S2 to capture CNC_A 12–20 and heat-treatment >10 energy savings only when batch wait caps by segment are respected. Use S3 to move MTO from CNC_B to CNC_A when queue and due-date slack allow, preserving CNC_B for recovery.
5. **Reduce avoidable order fragmentation.** Implement S11 to reduce partial shipments through better availability-check allocation, while allowing splits when complete-order waiting would violate Critical or Service-contract promises.
6. **Use shared emergency lanes selectively.** Apply S8 only to non-true-downtime Critical cases or shared lanes that meet SLA, with dedicated capacity reserved for true downtime. Maintain R1 alternative routes for Service-contract customers.

Why this balances: it targets the largest observed impact paths—air transport at 18.5 kgCO2e/order, dedicated vans at 52 kgCO2e/order, MTO rework at 32 kWh and 1.8 kg scrap, and partial-shipment extra transport—while protecting service through slack rules, caps, and true-critical exceptions. It does not force the lowest-emission option where service or resilience would fail.

# 7. Validation and governance plan

Validate with pre/post event-log analysis at segment and path level, not only global averages. Add decision attributes to the log: recommended_route, chosen_route, SP1_probability, SP2_justification, Q1_certification, urgency_tier, wait_cap, override_reason, and carbon_aware_offer.

Conformance monitoring:
- SP1: % of ground/consolidated choices meeting >85% success probability; % of air/dedicated choices with insufficient slack or justified exception.
- SP2: % of dedicated-van cases with explicit true-Critical justification; missing-justification rate.
- Q1: reinforced-packaging compliance for MTO >8 kg; Q1 override outcomes and return/rework correlation.

Segment-level service and sustainability monitoring:
- Energy: energy_kwh/order and kWh/item by machine_group, batch_size, and segment.
- Transport: transport_kgCO2e/order by route_mode, expedite_flag, and customer_type; air share; dedicated-van share; consolidated share.
- Materials: material_scrap_kg/order, package_material_kg/order, reinforced-packaging share, 14-day obsolescence.
- Quality: rework_flag rate, Inspection -> Rework -> CNC loops, packaging rework, damage returns, Q1 violations.
- Service: median/p90 cycle time, late delivery, partial shipment/backorder/cancellation, promised-lead-time slack.
- Resilience: alternative-route availability during carrier disruption, true-Critical dedicated-van coverage, carrier concentration.

Exception governance:
- Review SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides weekly/monthly by reason code, segment, and outcome.
- Require manager/engineering sign-off for repeated SP1 and Q1 overrides; missing SP2 justification is a nonconformance.
- Trigger corrective action if batching increases late delivery/obsolescence, if ground/consolidation causes expedite rebounds, if inspection queues raise rework, or if shared courier lanes reduce true-Critical service.

Because the discovered model precision is low for emergency/partial combinations, validate by comparing stable path variants and by phased rollout or difference-in-differences where possible. Treat service thresholds as guardrails derived from current segment baselines, especially for Critical-downtime and Service-contract customers.
