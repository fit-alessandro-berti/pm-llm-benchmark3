## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### Stipulated facts

The following are established by the supplied materials, subject only to ordinary event-time and identity-record reliability:

- **CS-9 (N1)** is formally approved, controlled, and effective from 2025-01-01 onward.
  - It requires `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` before activation.
  - It requires `Manual_Procurement_Approval` before activation.
  - It requires requester and manual approver to be different humans.
- **M0** represents only some CS-9 obligations:
  - It models sanctions and quality as concurrent prerequisites to the approval/rejection decision.
  - It requires manual approval before activation on the approved branch.
  - It requires notification and closure after decision.
  - It does not represent separation of duties, conflict-of-interest controls, reactivation, intake/pre-screen scope, repeated information requests, or notification-evidence quality.
- **PD-3 (N2)** was an operational pilot decision, not a CS-9 amendment and not incorporated into M0.
  - It was time-limited: 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31.
  - It applied only to low-risk DE suppliers already in approved corporate groups.
  - It allowed `Auto_Approve` instead of manual procurement approval.
  - It allowed quality review after provisional activation, within two business days.
  - It still required sanctions and automated master-data validation before activation.
- **FD-7 (N3)** was an emergency directive effective only from 2026-07-10 through 2026-07-12.
  - It permitted provisional activation before quality review and manual approval for qualifying critical relief suppliers.
  - It still required sanctions before activation.
  - It imposed a EUR 20,000 cap until retrospective approval, daily monitoring, and quality review within two business days.
- **CRM migration (N4)** materially reduced onboarding-log notification completeness after 2026-07-01, while CRM may contain independently linkable notification evidence of varying reliability.
- **Request_More_Info** cycles are operationally common and are regarded by both Operations and Compliance as substantively desirable, but M0 does not model them.
- The observed traces and listed case contexts are evidence, but not all have equally strong provenance. In particular:
  - notification linkage in some CRM records is mutable;
  - actor identity can be obscured by aliases;
  - labels can change;
  - case scope is disputed for rejected leads and reactivations.

### Disputed propositions

| Proposition | Status | Why disputed |
|---|---|---|
| PD-3 validly overrode CS-9 for its stated pilot scope | Disputed | Steering-committee approval and monitored-exception language support operational authorization; lack of formal CS-9 amendment and Compliance observer’s later statement limit authority. |
| FD-7 justified SUP-704 | Supported, conditional | Date, sequence, spend, monitoring, and retrospective activity align; qualification as a “critical relief supplier needed to prevent shutdown” must still be evidenced. |
| FD-7 justified SUP-705 after 2026-07-12 | Not supported on current evidence | The directive expired automatically; factual continuation of flood recovery is not a written extension or delegated emergency authority. |
| Rejected leads such as SUP-709 are in M0 scope | Disputed | CS-9 permits abbreviated pre-screen for rejected leads, but does not define whether such leads enter the M0-controlled onboarding process. |
| Reactivations such as SUP-710 require a fresh register and quality review | Unproved | CS-9 contains no explicit reactivation procedure; validity of prior certification and reactivation scope are not governed clearly. |
| CRM notification evidence proves notification for SUP-706 | Indeterminate | A CRM message exists, but linkage is a mutable text field entered two days later, weakening evidential integrity. |
| `QA_Clearance` is equivalent to `Quality_Review` in SUP-713 | Substantially supported | Audit records say content was equivalent and mapping was later approved; retrospective mapping creates a governance timing issue but not necessarily a substantive control failure. |
| Operations’ normalized post-pilot auto-approval practice is policy | Not supported | No permanent policy amendment existed; expectation of approval is not authority. |
| Quality reviewer/approver spouse relationship is prohibited | Not established under CS-9 alone | No explicit rule forbids it, but undisclosed relationship may violate a separate conflict-of-interest policy. |

### Burden-of-proof approach

**For declaring noncompliance**

1. A finding of noncompliance should require:
   - a binding rule applicable at the event time;
   - a case within the rule’s scope;
   - reliable evidence of a violation of that rule; and
   - no established exception, override, or alternative control authorized for that case.
2. A trace mismatch with M0 alone is insufficient where:
   - M0 omits a known process element;
   - event collection is incomplete or semantically altered;
   - the activity is label-equivalent;
   - scope is unresolved; or
   - a time-bound overlay may apply.
3. Conversely, where a binding control is clear and a case defeats it—especially sanctions before activation, time-limited authority, or separation of duties—the absence of financial loss does not cure noncompliance.

**For accepting an exception**

An exception should require affirmative evidence, not merely plausible operational narrative:

- identifiable authority and authority level;
- effective and expiry timestamps;
- qualifying scope and eligibility snapshot;
- required compensating controls;
- immutable links to the case and underlying events;
- post-event review where the overlay requires it.

For an emergency exception, “the emergency continued” is insufficient absent authority to continue or extend the directive. For a pilot exception, operational practice cannot expand eligibility, duration, or conditions beyond the pilot’s written terms.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The central prosecution position is that CS-9 remains the controlling norm. M0 may be incomplete, but incompleteness does not negate the controls it does express, and it certainly does not nullify controls in CS-9 that M0 omitted. PD-3 and FD-7 may create narrow, time-bounded exceptions, but neither establishes a general operational power to amend the compliance standard.

### 1. Formal authority matters

CS-9 is the only source described as formally approved and controlled. PD-3 was a steering-committee decision recorded in minutes and email, but was not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. FD-7 was an emergency directive with a precise, automatic expiry.

The Compliance observer’s statement that the pilot “may proceed as a monitored exception” supports a limited exception program; her later statement that she had not approved weakening CS-9 prevents treating PD-3 as a permanent revision of the standard. At most, PD-3 created a constrained operational derogation. It did not authorize:

- high-risk suppliers;
- foreign or non-DE suppliers;
- suppliers outside approved groups;
- use after 2026-07-31;
- ignoring sanctions;
- abandoning master-data validation;
- permanent normalization of auto-approval.

### 2. M0 understates the compliance problem rather than overstates it

M0 has defects, but those defects hide important nonconformance:

- It does not enforce requester/manual-approver separation, so a trace can conform while violating CS-9. SUP-712 demonstrates this.
- It does not encode conflict-of-interest disclosure, relevant to SUP-715.
- It does not encode risk, jurisdiction, approved-group membership, pilot dates, emergency dates, spend caps, monitoring, or timing conditions.
- It treats quality and sanctions as a concurrent block before the decision, but actual compliance requirements include strict pre-activation requirements in the standard route.
- It does not distinguish between a formally authorized exception and an operator’s use of an exception label.

Thus a high apparent M0 conformance rate would be false reassurance. The model is not a safe surrogate for the policy corpus.

### 3. High-risk and unauthorized deviations are material

**SUP-703** is materially noncompliant under every plausible interpretation. It involved a high-risk new foreign supplier, yet the operator selected the pilot path. PD-3 was unavailable because eligibility was plainly absent. `Auto_Approve` replaced the mandatory manual approval, and quality occurred after activation. The first purchase was EUR 180,000. This is not a harmless trace deviation or a model issue; it is an unauthorized weakening of controls for a high-risk exposure.

**SUP-708** is also materially noncompliant. The supplier was activated 40 minutes before sanctions clearance. Neither PD-3 nor FD-7 applied. That no transactions occurred before clearance reduces realized harm but does not satisfy CS-9’s preventive control. Sanctions screening is designed to stop prohibited relationships before activation, not merely before first spend.

**SUP-705** cannot be justified by an expired directive. FD-7 ended at 23:59 on 2026-07-12. The case occurred 2026-07-13. The initial spend of EUR 12,000 was beneath the former cap, but the cap is only one condition of an authority that had expired. Factual flood recovery does not itself extend legal or policy authority. Treating continuing conditions as self-extending emergencies is exactly how exceptional controls become normalized.

**SUP-714** similarly used auto-approval after PD-3 expired. The supplier may have been substantively low risk and otherwise eligible, but the policy basis had ended. Belief that permanent approval was imminent is not approval. The aggregate evidence shows six post-pilot auto-approval cases in the first August week, indicating that this was not an isolated mistake but a normalized unauthorized practice.

### 4. Exceptions appear to be drifting into a shadow process

The aggregate evidence is concerning:

- 312 cases used `Auto_Approve` in June and July.
- 14 were clearly ineligible.
- 9 lacked eligibility attributes, so eligibility cannot be demonstrated.
- Six more auto-approval cases occurred immediately after pilot expiry.

Even if 289 cases were substantively eligible, repeated use of an informal or insufficiently governed exception creates a control-environment problem. Eligibility evidence missing in nine cases should not be presumed favorable. In compliance terms, absence of proof of qualification generally means the exception is not evidenced.

The critical distinction is between:

- a pilot that permits a monitored, bounded exception; and
- an operationally normalized alternative process that continues after the pilot without formal adoption.

The evidence supports the latter risk.

### 5. Desirable behavior does not retroactively amend policy

`Request_More_Info` cycles in SUP-707 and the 47 aggregate cases may improve decision quality. They do not weaken required controls, and the case did not activate before evidence arrived. But desirability does not automatically make an activity part of the normative process. The correct result is to revise the process model prospectively, not to declare M0 irrelevant retrospectively.

The same reasoning applies to a permanent low-risk auto-approval branch. Operations may have a sound business case, but no permanent amendment existed. An operational desire is evidence for redesign, not evidence of current authorization.

### 6. Data defects should be corrected, not used to excuse known violations

The CRM migration clearly caused notification logging defects. That can make SUP-706 indeterminate rather than noncompliant. The duplicate `Register_Supplier` in SUP-711 is plainly a migration artifact and should not be counted as substantive noncompliance. `QA_Clearance` in SUP-713 may be semantically equivalent to `Quality_Review`.

But such genuine data and model defects do not explain:

- SUP-703’s ineligible pilot use;
- SUP-708’s activation before sanctions;
- SUP-705’s use of expired emergency authority;
- SUP-714’s post-expiry auto-approval;
- SUP-712’s same-human requester and approver.

The prosecution therefore accepts that M0 is an inadequate compliance model, while maintaining that the operational record contains serious, independently evidenced violations of binding controls.

## 3. Defense Brief

The defense position is not that every observed deviation is acceptable. It is that M0 is an impoverished control-flow abstraction being used as if it were a complete and timeless compliance specification. Many “deviations” are caused by omitted scope, omitted loops, temporal overlays, label changes, identity/data defects, and an event log that became incomplete after a system migration.

### 1. M0 is incomplete by design and cannot be the sole conformance oracle

M0 does not represent several material process realities:

- the repeatable `Request_More_Info` loop;
- auto-approval pilot behavior;
- emergency provisional activation;
- automated master-data validation;
- time windows and eligibility predicates;
- spend caps and daily monitoring;
- requester/approver identity rules;
- conflict disclosures;
- rejected-lead pre-screening;
- reactivation;
- withdrawals;
- notification-system migration and cross-system evidence;
- semantic activity mapping.

Accordingly, an M0 replay result is not equivalent to a policy conclusion. It is merely evidence about fit to one narrow control-flow model.

M0 also enforces quality and sanctions before the XOR decision, including rejection. Yet CS-9 expressly says rejected leads *may* use abbreviated pre-screening and does not say whether they are governed by M0. This means M0 may be over-scoped for rejected leads.

### 2. Concurrency should not be mistaken for a fixed trace order

Within `checks`, sanctions and quality are concurrent. A valid standard-route execution may record either ordering, provided both complete before subsequent decisions and activation. Conformance analysis must therefore be partial-order aware rather than treating a linearized trace as the only execution semantics.

SUP-701 is a clean standard-route case. More broadly, the existence of concurrent checks means that simple sequence matching can misclassify legitimate ordering variations.

### 3. Pilot and emergency overlays are better explanations than “process breakdown” for some cases

**SUP-702** is the strongest example. It was low-risk, DE, in an approved corporate group, occurred inside the PD-3 window, had complete eligibility fields, completed sanctions before activation, and conducted quality review the next business day. Notification exists in CRM with a stable `notification_id`. Its apparent M0 deviation is exactly the kind of controlled alternate route that PD-3 contemplated.

The prosecution is right that PD-3’s formal status is weaker than CS-9. But the steering committee did decide on the pilot, and the Compliance observer explicitly permitted it as a monitored exception. Operational governance need not be identical to a permanent policy amendment to authorize a bounded experiment. The proper classification is a justified exception, subject to confirming master-data validation and pilot-authority evidence.

**SUP-704** similarly aligns closely with FD-7. It occurred during the directive’s effective period, involved a critical pump supplier, had pre-activation sanctions, initial spend below EUR 20,000, daily monitoring, and retrospective quality and approval the next business day. M0 cannot express this legitimate emergency pattern. Treating it as ordinary noncompliance would erase the point of an emergency directive.

### 4. The log is not the whole record

The notification defect after 2026-07-01 is measurable, not speculative: notification missingness rose from 2% to 29% after CRM migration. A missing onboarding-log notification is therefore weak evidence that no notification occurred.

For **SUP-706**, a CRM message exists at +3 hours. The mutable case-link field entered two days later makes proof weaker than a stable identifier, but it does not prove non-notification. The appropriate result is data-quality indeterminate, not a finding that a notification control failed.

Similarly:

- **SUP-711** has duplicate register rows sharing the same source transaction ID. The proper interpretation is a deduplication/data-ingestion defect, not two registrations or a behavioral violation.
- **SUP-713** uses `QA_Clearance`, with audit documentation showing equivalent content and a mapping approved shortly afterward. If the substantive content is equivalent, a label mismatch should not be treated as a missed quality review merely because the taxonomy update lagged practice.

### 5. Some cases reveal scope defects rather than behavioral failure

**SUP-709** rejected a lead at sanctions pre-screen and never proceeded into full onboarding. CS-9 expressly permits abbreviated pre-screening for rejected leads, while the scope document is ambiguous and the model owner says M0 starts after pre-screen acceptance. If that interpretation is accepted, quality review was never required because this was not an approved onboarding case. The model needs an explicit intake/pre-screen process, not a blanket conclusion of noncompliance.

**SUP-710** is a reactivation, not necessarily a new onboarding. The supplier had been closed six months earlier, and prior quality certification remained valid. CS-9 says nothing about reactivation. Requiring a new registration and quality review may be sensible, but it cannot be inferred conclusively from silence. The defect is policy and model scope. The prudent answer is not “compliant,” but “model or scope defect pending a reactivation rule.”

### 6. Trace deviation is not identical to business misconduct

A case can deviate from M0 without being misconduct:

- SUP-702 may be a properly governed pilot case.
- SUP-704 may be a properly governed emergency case.
- SUP-707 may be a more careful process than M0 depicts.
- SUP-711 may be a duplicated data event.
- SUP-713 may be a valid semantic equivalent.
- SUP-706 may have complete notification evidence outside the onboarding log.
- SUP-709 and SUP-710 may fall outside the model’s intended lifecycle.

Conversely, a trace can fit M0 while still violating broader controls, as SUP-712 demonstrates. This proves the defense’s central methodological point: M0 conformance is neither necessary nor sufficient for compliance.

### 7. Limits of the defense

The defense cannot plausibly justify SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-705, or SUP-714 merely by invoking obsolescence:

- PD-3 did not cover SUP-703’s risk and jurisdiction.
- no overlay allowed activation before sanctions in SUP-708;
- FD-7 expired before SUP-705;
- PD-3 expired before SUP-714.

Nor can missing eligibility attributes be presumed to establish eligibility. The defense position is therefore that the system is poorly modeled and poorly instrumented, not that informal practice can self-authorize.

### 3. System-level judgment

**Operational compliance judgment:** materially noncompliant, though not uniformly so.

The operation contains legitimate or plausibly legitimate alternate routes, including the PD-3-style case in SUP-702 and FD-7-style case in SUP-704. It also contains material violations: SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, and SUP-714. These are not explained away by model obsolescence or logging weakness. The aggregate auto-approval data also indicates weak exception governance:

- 14 clearly ineligible pilot uses;
- 9 uses with insufficient eligibility data;
- six post-pilot uses immediately after expiry;
- normalization of an exception route without a formal permanent amendment.

That pattern is a system-control failure, not merely isolated user error.

**Model-validity judgment:** M0 is materially incomplete and should not remain the sole normative or analytical model.

M0 is a reasonable representation of the original standard approved route, but it is inadequate as the full conformance architecture. It lacks:

- authority-aware exception routes;
- iterative information gathering;
- reactivation and pre-screen scope;
- data and time conditions;
- identity and conflict rules;
- robust cross-system notification evidence;
- semantic label mapping.

Therefore, M0 should be retained only as one layer of a revised architecture, not treated as the exhaustive norm.

### 4. Where each side is rejected

**The prosecution is rejected where it equates every M0 mismatch with misconduct.**  
SUP-702, SUP-704, SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-711, SUP-713, and potentially SUP-706 demonstrate why that equation fails. A process model that omits governed exceptions, loops, scope boundaries, semantic mappings, and cross-system evidence cannot conclusively classify all deviations as operational breaches.

**The defense is rejected where it treats practical desirability, factual emergency, lack of harm, or expected future policy as authority.**  
SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, and SUP-714 remain noncompliant. A “monitored exception” cannot become a permanent shadow policy. An expired emergency directive cannot be extended by operational belief. No transactions before sanctions clearance reduces impact but does not satisfy a preventive sanctions control.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The revised architecture should separate process structure from conditions, organizational obligations, authority, and data-quality evidence. No single POWL should be expected to encode all of these faithfully.

### A. Revised control-flow model: POWL

Use POWL for stable lifecycle structure:

1. **Entry and information-gathering**
   - `Register_Supplier`
   - zero-or-more `Request_More_Info` cycles
   - return to assessment after each cycle.

2. **Standard route**
   - concurrent `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review`;
   - `Manual_Procurement_Approval`;
   - `Activate_Supplier`;
   - `Notify_Requester`;
   - `Close_Case`.

3. **Low-risk pilot/permanent auto route**
   - `Sanctions_Check`;
   - `Automated_Master_Data_Validation`;
   - `Auto_Approve`;
   - provisional activation;
   - quality review allowed after activation only where the active overlay permits it;
   - notification and closure.

4. **Emergency route**
   - `Sanctions_Check`;
   - `Emergency_Authorize_Provisional_Activation`;
   - `Activate_Supplier`;
   - retrospective `Quality_Review` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval`;
   - notification and closure.

5. **Rejection**
   - an explicit rejection route after intake/pre-screen or after full review, depending on scope classification;
   - notification and closure.

The POWL should express ordering, branching, concurrency, and a bounded or approximate loop. It should not pretend to enforce risk, dates, amounts, or human identity.

### B. DECLARE/log-skeleton-style constraints

Use declarative constraints for obligations spanning multiple routes or sensitive to repetition:

- `Activate_Supplier` requires prior `Sanctions_Check` in all activation routes.
- Standard activation requires prior `Quality_Review` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval`, unless a valid overlay route is attached.
- `Notify_Requester` must occur before `Close_Case`.
- `Request_More_Info` may repeat; after each request, evidence receipt or reassessment must occur before final approval/activation.
- Emergency provisional activation requires eventual `Quality_Review` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval`.
- If `Reject_Supplier` occurs, `Activate_Supplier` must not occur in that lifecycle unless a new/reopened case is explicitly created.
- A case must have at most one terminal outcome for a given lifecycle version: rejection, withdrawal, or completed activation/closure.
- For standard cases, activation must not occur before both required checks.
- For auto and emergency routes, delayed quality must meet the overlay deadline.

These constraints are more suitable than POWL for “eventually,” “never together,” “within a deadline,” repeatable cycles, and lifecycle consistency.

### C. Pandas/data-quality and eligibility checks

Use data checks for conditions dependent on attributes, dates, amounts, identities, and timestamps:

- pilot eligibility:
  - risk = LOW;
  - country = DE;
  - approved corporate group = true;
  - active overlay = PD-3 or successor;
  - automated master-data validation passed;
  - event time within effective interval.
- emergency eligibility:
  - emergency directive active;
  - emergency class and critical-supplier rationale present;
  - activation spend at or below cap;
  - daily monitoring present;
  - retrospective approval and quality within deadline.
- timestamp checks:
  - effective time versus record time;
  - business-day deadlines;
  - event ordering based on reliable event times;
  - late-entered evidence flagged separately from late-occurring activity.
- data integrity:
  - duplicate source transaction IDs;
  - immutable event IDs;
  - stable notification IDs;
  - activity-label mappings with version/effective dates;
  - actor alias resolution confidence.
- notification reconciliation:
  - join onboarding and CRM records by immutable `notification_id`;
  - distinguish “not logged in onboarding” from “not sent.”
- reactivation:
  - prior certification validity;
  - elapsed time;
  - risk changes;
  - sanctions re-screening;
  - explicit reactivation policy version.

### D. Resource and conflict-of-interest rules

Use organizational controls, identity-master data, and workflow authorization checks:

- requester and manual approver must resolve to different canonical human identities;
- delegation must be documented, authorized, time-bound, and non-self-approving;
- quality reviewer and approver relationships must be checked against conflict-of-interest declarations;
- related-party or reporting-line risks require disclosure, recusal, or documented mitigation;
- approval authority must be validated against role, threshold, jurisdiction, and effective date;
- aliases must map to immutable person IDs, with historical identity mappings retained.

These are not primarily POWL problems. They require identity governance and organizational-policy enforcement.

### E. Policy-overlay governance

Create an explicit overlay register for pilots, emergencies, waivers, and successor policies.

Every overlay should include:

- unique `overlay_id` and policy/version ID;
- authority type and signatory;
- legal/compliance review status;
- effective timestamp and expiry timestamp;
- covered population and eligibility rules;
- overridden baseline controls;
- compensating controls;
- spend cap and currency where relevant;
- required evidence;
- monitoring requirements;
- retrospective review requirements;
- extension procedure;
- revocation status;
- linkage from every case to the overlay invocation.

The register should prohibit “implicit continuation.” Any use after expiry must fail validation unless an extension record is present and effective.

### F. Explicit scope rules

Define lifecycle boundaries rather than leaving them to model-owner interpretation:

1. **Rejected leads**
   - classify as either:
     - pre-screen-only intake case, governed by abbreviated controls; or
     - full onboarding case, governed by full checks.
   - Require a `case_scope` field at registration and an immutable reason for rejection.

2. **Reactivations**
   - define whether reactivation is:
     - a new onboarding;
     - a shortened reactivation process; or
     - a status restoration.
   - Specify validity periods for prior quality certification, sanctions re-screening, approval requirements, and risk-change triggers.

3. **Withdrawals**
   - include `Withdraw_Request` / `Close_Withdrawn` or equivalent terminal state.
   - prohibit activation after withdrawal unless explicitly reopened with a new lifecycle identifier.

4. **Reopened cases**
   - retain parent-case linkage and create a new lifecycle/version so that event histories are not silently merged.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The following POWL is a deliberately conservative control-flow approximation. It represents the main routes and uses a repeatable `Request_More_Info` loop before routing. The loop is an approximation: POWL expresses structure, but the model cannot enforce that each request is answered with sufficient evidence, nor can it determine whether a case qualifies for auto or emergency routing.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Atomic activities
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")

request_more_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
receive_more_info = Transition(label="Receive_Additional_Evidence")

sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
master_data = Transition(label="Automated_Master_Data_Validation")

manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")

emergency_authorize = Transition(
    label="Emergency_Authorize_Provisional_Activation"
)

activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")

notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

# Silent transitions make the information-request loop optional.
tau_skip_info = SilentTransition()
tau_exit_info = SilentTransition()


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Repeatable Request_More_Info approximation
#
# Each iteration is:
# Request_More_Info -> Receive_Additional_Evidence
#
# POWL loop semantics express zero-or-more iterations. This does not
# prove that evidence was sufficient; that is a data/DECLARE control.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

info_iteration = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[request_more_info, receive_more_info]
)
info_iteration.order.add_edge(request_more_info, receive_more_info)

info_loop = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[
        info_iteration,     # do body
        tau_exit_info       # redo/exit approximation, depending on PM4Py loop semantics
    ]
)

optional_info_loop = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[info_loop, tau_skip_info]
)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Standard route:
# sanctions and quality are concurrent, then manual approval, then activate.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

standard_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])

standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[standard_checks, manual_approval, activate]
)
standard_route.order.add_edge(standard_checks, manual_approval)
standard_route.order.add_edge(manual_approval, activate)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Low-risk auto-approval route:
# sanctions and master-data validation are concurrent prerequisites.
# Quality is deliberately after provisional activation here because the
# route represents a PD-3-style overlay. Eligibility, dates, and the
# two-business-day deadline are enforced outside POWL.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

auto_preconditions = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, master_data])

low_risk_auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[auto_preconditions, auto_approve, activate, quality]
)
low_risk_auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_preconditions, auto_approve)
low_risk_auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_approve, activate)
low_risk_auto_route.order.add_edge(activate, quality)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Emergency/provisional route:
# sanctions must precede emergency authorization and activation.
# Quality review and manual approval follow activation and can occur
# concurrently as retrospective controls.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

retrospective_controls = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[quality, manual_approval]
)

emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[sanctions, emergency_authorize, activate, retrospective_controls]
)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(sanctions, emergency_authorize)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(emergency_authorize, activate)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(activate, retrospective_controls)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Decision routes:
# Standard, low-risk auto, emergency, or rejection.
#
# Route eligibility cannot be enforced by POWL. A case must be linked
# to a valid active policy overlay and eligibility snapshot outside this
# model before auto/emergency replay is accepted.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

decision = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        standard_route,
        low_risk_auto_route,
        emergency_route,
        reject
    ]
)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Whole lifecycle:
# Register -> optional/repeatable information requests -> decision ->
# Notify_Requester -> Close_Case
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------

M1_revised = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[register, optional_info_loop, decision, notify, close]
)

M1_revised.order.add_edge(register, optional_info_loop)
M1_revised.order.add_edge(optional_info_loop, decision)
M1_revised.order.add_edge(decision, notify)
M1_revised.order.add_edge(notify, close)

print(M1_revised)
```

### POWL limitations and why the approximation is defensible

1. **Route eligibility is not structural.**  
   The model permits auto and emergency routes structurally, but it cannot decide whether the supplier is low-risk, German, in an approved group, below an emergency spend cap, or inside an effective date window. Allowing these branches in POWL is safe only if replay acceptance is coupled to policy-overlay validation.

2. **The information loop is semantic, not evidential.**  
   The loop captures repeatable requests, but does not prove that evidence was received, complete, authentic, or sufficient. The explicit `Receive_Additional_Evidence` event is therefore useful, but evidence quality must be checked in data and document controls.

3. **Business-day deadlines require temporal logic/data checks.**  
   “Quality within two business days” is not reliably encoded in ordinary POWL control-flow. It belongs in event-time and calendar-aware validation.

4. **Emergency caps and monitoring are non-control-flow rules.**  
   Spend, daily transaction monitoring, and emergency rationale require data fields and monitoring records.

5. **Reactivation and pre-screen rejection should be separate lifecycle models or explicit scope classes.**  
   They should not be silently replayed against the ordinary onboarding lifecycle.

### Normalized policy-overlay table

Maintain a controlled table such as `policy_overlay`:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `overlay_id` | Immutable identifier, e.g., `PD-3`, `FD-7`, `PA-2026-08-01` |
| `overlay_version` | Version and amendment number |
| `overlay_type` | `PILOT`, `EMERGENCY`, `WAIVER`, `PERMANENT_POLICY` |
| `authority_id` | Steering committee, compliance authority, emergency officer, etc. |
| `authority_document_hash` | Immutable hash/link to signed source |
| `approval_status` | Draft, approved, expired, revoked, superseded |
| `effective_ts_utc` | Effective timestamp |
| `expiry_ts_utc` | Expiry timestamp; mandatory for temporary overlays |
| `scope_definition` | Jurisdiction, supplier type, event class, business unit |
| `eligibility_rule_version` | Versioned reference to executable eligibility rules |
| `baseline_controls_overridden` | Controlled list, e.g., quality-before-activation |
| `compensating_controls` | Monitoring, caps, retrospective approval, review deadline |
| `spend_cap_amount` / `currency` | Monetary limit where applicable |
| `quality_deadline_business_days` | Post-activation review deadline |
| `manual_approval_deadline_business_days` | Retrospective approval deadline |
| `extension_overlay_id` | Explicit extension link; blank means no extension |
| `created_record_ts` | Immutable system record timestamp |
| `revoked_ts` | If revoked |
| `owner` | Named accountable policy owner |

A case-level `case_overlay_invocation` table should include:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `case_id` | Supplier lifecycle case |
| `overlay_id` / `overlay_version` | Exact invoked authority |
| `invocation_event_id` | Event asserting use of overlay |
| `eligibility_snapshot_id` | Immutable snapshot used at decision time |
| `authority_evidence_id` | Approval/delegation/emergency reference |
| `effective_ts_at_evaluation` | Used to test applicability |
| `validation_result` | Pass/fail plus rule-level reasons |
| `reviewer_id` | Person/system validating invocation |
| `recorded_ts` | Immutable record time |

### Event and data-collection proposal

Use an append-only event store plus controlled reference tables.

#### Core event table

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| `event_id` | Immutable UUID |
| `case_id` | Stable lifecycle ID |
| `parent_case_id` | Required for reactivation/reopen relationships |
| `event_type` | Canonical activity code, not display label only |
| `display_label` | Original UI text, e.g., `QA_Clearance` |
| `activity_mapping_version` | Mapping used to derive canonical code |
| `event_ts_utc` | When activity occurred |
| `record_ts_utc` | When system recorded it; immutable |
| `source_system` | Onboarding, CRM, ERP, sanctions service, workflow engine |
| `source_event_id` | Immutable source identifier |
| `source_transaction_id` | Used for deduplication and lineage |
| `actor_principal_id` | Immutable canonical identity |
| `actor_alias_id` | Source-system alias, retained for audit |
| `delegation_id` | Link to valid delegation evidence where applicable |
| `rule_version_id` | Policy/model/rule set evaluated at event time |
| `overlay_id` | Applicable overlay if any |
| `eligibility_snapshot_id` | Immutable point-in-time eligibility record |
| `evidence_hash` | Hash/link to document or service evidence |
| `correction_of_event_id` | Corrections create new records; do not overwrite history |

#### Eligibility snapshot table

Capture the facts known at the moment of routing:

- risk rating and source;
- supplier country and legal entity;
- approved corporate-group status;
- sanctions result and timestamp;
- master-data validation result and timestamp;
- supplier criticality and emergency rationale;
- expected and actual initial spend;
- policy/overlay version evaluated;
- eligibility rule result;
- evaluator identity or service version;
- event time and immutable record time.

This prevents later changes to supplier attributes from rewriting the historical basis for an exception.

#### Notification evidence

Require a stable, immutable `notification_id` generated once and carried across onboarding and CRM systems:

- onboarding case emits `notification_id`;
- CRM message records the same `notification_id`;
- delivery status and recipient are retained;
- cross-system link uses immutable IDs, not editable text;
- mutable text links may be retained as ancillary evidence but must not be the primary correlation key.

#### Identity, delegation, and conflict controls

Maintain:

- canonical `person_id`;
- historical alias-to-person mappings with effective dates;
- organizational hierarchy snapshots;
- delegation records with grantor, delegate, scope, amount threshold, effective dates, and approval;
- conflict-of-interest declarations, disclosures, recusals, and mitigation approvals;
- reviewer/approver relationship checks at decision time.

This would have detected SUP-712 reliably and would permit a properly evidenced conclusion in SUP-715.

#### Analytics and conformance outputs

Report separate dimensions rather than a single “conformant/nonconformant” score:

1. **Structural POWL fit** — does the route fit M1?
2. **Overlay validity** — did a valid authority apply at event time?
3. **Eligibility validity** — did the case meet route-specific predicates?
4. **Temporal compliance** — deadlines, expiry, business-day windows.
5. **Resource compliance** — separation of duties, delegation, conflicts.
6. **Evidence integrity** — stable IDs, immutable timestamps, linkage confidence.
7. **Scope classification** — new onboarding, pre-screen rejection, reactivation, withdrawal, reopen.

That separation prevents two opposite errors: treating every model mismatch as misconduct, and treating a plausible operational narrative as proof that a binding control did not apply.
