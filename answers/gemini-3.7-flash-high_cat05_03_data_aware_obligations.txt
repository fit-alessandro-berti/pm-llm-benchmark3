## 1. Temporal Data Semantics

Assessing compliance in data-aware, evolving business processes requires rigorous temporal and epistemic modeling. The core semantic principles applied across this assessment are:

1. **State Reconstruction via Last-Value-Carried-Forward (LVCF):**
   - An event log represents discrete state mutations. The state of a case at any arbitrary point in time \( t \) (or event index \( i \)) is reconstructed by forward-filling known case attributes (`amount`, `risk_level`, `jurisdiction`, `customer_type`) from the most recent prior event that updated that attribute.
   - **Handling of Blanks:** A blank (empty/NaN) cell in an event record denotes *absence of update* ("no change at this event"), preserving the prior active state. It must *never* be interpreted as an intentional erasure, reset, or transition to `null`.

2. **Deterministic Tie-Breaking for Equal Timestamps:**
   - When multiple events share an identical timestamp, temporal ordering is strictly established by the integer `event_index`, reflecting the source system's monotonic ingestion/execution sequence.
   - *Consequence:* An event with `(timestamp T, event_index 3)` strictly precedes `(timestamp T, event_index 4)`. A review executed at index 3 occurs under the state prevailing at index 3 and precedes any state mutation introduced at index 4, preventing false concurrency assumptions.

3. **Identification of Material Data Changes (Invalidation Semantics):**
   - A prior review (`Standard_Review` or `Enhanced_Review`) validates the transaction *only for the state known at the time of that review*.
   - Under Policy Rule R4, any subsequent amount increase strictly exceeding \( +20\% \) (\( \Delta \text{amount} / \text{amount}_{\text{reviewed}} > 0.20 \)) automatically invalidates prior reviews for amount-dependent obligations.
   - An amount decrease does *not* invalidate a prior review, as it reduces financial exposure.
   - Non-amount material changes (e.g., risk escalation from `LOW`/`MEDIUM` to `HIGH`, customer reclassification to `PEP`, or jurisdiction reclassification) invalidate reviews that did not assess those risk factors.

4. **Strict Rejection of Final-State Back-Propagation (No Retroactive Leakage):**
   - In standard process mining, case attributes are often flattened into static trace attributes taken from the final case state. This is forbidden. Every obligation trigger and guard condition must be evaluated against the *event-time snapshot* active at the exact moment of the trigger event (e.g., `Release_Funds`).

5. **Multi-Dimensional Time Model:**
   To evaluate exemptions, backdating, and regulatory obligations, four distinct temporal dimensions must be separated:
   - **Event Time (\( T_{\text{event}} \)):** The timestamp when the business action occurred in the operational environment.
   - **Effective Time (\( T_{\text{effective}} \)):** The point in time from which an authority, rule, or exemption legally or operationally applies (`effective_from`).
   - **Record Time (\( T_{\text{record}} \)):** The timestamp when the transaction, update, or exemption was committed to the physical database. A lag between event time and record time may indicate a system outage or uncommitted state.
   - **Evidence / Assertion Time (\( T_{\text{evidence}} \)):** The timestamp established by non-repudiable cryptographic proof (e.g., qualified digital signature timestamp, certificate issuance timestamp, tamper-proof blockchain/audit log). A displayed document date is an unverified assertion; it is legally binding only if corroborated by \( T_{\text{evidence}} \le T_{\text{trigger}} \).

### Formal Pseudo-Logic (R1–R7)

Let \( E = \langle e_1, e_2, \dots, e_n \rangle \) be a sequence of events for a case, totally ordered by the relation \( \prec \):
\[
e_i \prec e_j \iff \big( T(e_i) < T(e_j) \big) \lor \big( T(e_i) = T(e_j) \land \text{idx}(e_i) < \text{idx}(e_j) \big)
\]
Let \( S(e) = (\text{amt}(e), \text{risk}(e), \text{jur}(e), \text{cust}(e)) \) represent the forward-filled state at event \( e \).

- **R1 (Foreign High-Value Enhanced Review):**
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
  \forall r \in E \text{ with } \text{act}(r) = \text{Release\_Funds}: \\
  & \big( \text{jur}(r) \neq \text{'DE'} \land \text{amt}(r) \ge 100000 \big) \implies \\
  & \Big( \text{ExemptionValid}(r, \text{'R1'}) \lor \exists u \in E \text{ s.t. } \text{act}(u) = \text{Enhanced\_Review} \land u \prec r \\
  & \quad \land \neg \exists m \in E \big( \text{MaterialChange}(m) \land u \prec m \prec r \big) \Big)
  \end{aligned}
  \]
- **R2 (High-Risk or PEP Approval):**
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
  \forall r \in E \text{ with } \text{act}(r) = \text{Release\_Funds}: \\
  & \big( \text{risk}(r) = \text{'HIGH'} \lor \text{cust}(r) = \text{'PEP'} \big) \implies \\
  & \Big( \exists u, a \in E \text{ s.t. } \text{act}(u) = \text{Enhanced\_Review} \land \text{act}(a) = \text{Compliance\_Approve} \\
  & \quad \land u \prec a \prec r \land \text{ApprovesPacket}(a, u) \Big)
  \end{aligned}
  \]
- **R3 (Sanctions-List Update):**
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
  \forall r \in E \text{ with } \text{act}(r) = \text{Release\_Funds}, \forall s_{\text{up}} \in E \text{ with } \text{act}(s_{\text{up}}) = \text{Sanctions\_List\_Update}(\text{jur}(r)): \\
  & \big( s_{\text{up}} \prec r \big) \implies \exists s_{\text{scr}} \in E \text{ s.t. } \text{act}(s_{\text{scr}}) = \text{Sanctions\_Screen} \land s_{\text{up}} \prec s_{\text{scr}} \prec r
  \end{aligned}
  \]
- **R4 (Material Amount Change):**
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
  \forall v \in E \text{ with } \text{act}(v) \in \{\text{Standard\_Review}, \text{Enhanced\_Review}\}, \forall u \in E \text{ with } \text{act}(u) = \text{Update\_Amount}: \\
  & \big( v \prec u \land \text{amt}(u) > 1.20 \times \text{amt}(v) \big) \implies \text{Invalidates}(u, v)
  \end{aligned}
  \]
- **R5 (Exemption Validity):**
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
  \text{ExemptionValid}(r, \text{rule}) \iff & \exists x \in \text{Exemptions}: x.\text{id} = \text{ex\_id}(r) \land x.\text{approver} = \text{'Head\_Compliance'} \\
  & \land x.\text{waives} \ni \text{rule} \land x.\text{scope\_jur} \ni \text{jur}(r) \land x.\text{scope\_cust} \ni \text{cust}(r) \\
  & \land \text{amt}(r) \le x.\text{max\_amount} \land x.T_{\text{effective}} \le T(r) \le x.T_{\text{expires}} \\
  & \land x.T_{\text{evidence}} \le T(r)
  \end{aligned}
  \]
- **R6 (Missing Data Semantics):**
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
  \forall r \in E \text{ with } \text{act}(r) = \text{Release\_Funds}: \\
  & \big( \text{amt}(r) = \bot \lor \text{risk}(r) = \bot \lor \text{jur}(r) = \bot \lor \text{cust}(r) = \bot \big) \implies \text{Status}(r) = \text{INDETERMINATE}
  \end{aligned}
  \]
- **R7 (Event-Time State Evaluation):**
  \[
  \forall e_k \in E, \quad S(e_k) = \text{foldl}(\text{apply\_update}, S_0, \langle e_1, \dots, e_k \rangle)
  \]

## 4. Ambiguities and Alternative Readings

### TX-305 (Indeterminate Case)
- **Primary Reading (INDETERMINATE):**
  At creation, review, and release, the amount attribute was unpopulated due to a data feed failure (`amount = NaN`). Under Rule R6, missing data cannot be defaulted to low-risk values, and under Rule R4, an increase from an unknown amount cannot be safely classified. Although settlement occurs at EUR 150,000 on the following day (2026-04-07), there is no immutable release payload tying that settlement amount to the release instruction at 11:30:00Z. Because whether R1 was triggered at 11:30:00Z is epistemically undecidable from event-time evidence, the case is strictly `INDETERMINATE`.
- **Alternative Reading 1 (NONCOMPLIANT — Strict Operational Control):**
  If internal banking control policy mandates that releasing funds without a verified, populated amount payload is an automatic control failure, the release itself constitutes an unauthorized operational breach. Furthermore, if the settlement record (150,000) is admitted via backward inference as circumstantial proof of the released amount, the `Enhanced_Review` was defective because the review document explicitly noted "no amount."
- **Alternative Reading 2 (COMPLIANT — Form-over-Substance):**
  If an auditor assumes that `Enhanced_Review` was conducted as a general procedural gate and that absence of recorded amount implies the transaction defaulted to standard limits at release time, one might argue for compliance. However, this directly violates Rule R6 and cannot be defended.
- **Decisive Evidence Needed to Resolve:**
  An immutable payment dispatch payload containing the signed transaction amount at 11:30:00Z, or an audit log from the payment gateway establishing the exact instruction value at event time.

### TX-302 (Exemption vs Direct Compliance)
- **Primary Reading (JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION / COMPLIANT):**
  The case satisfies policy on two independent grounds: (1) An `Enhanced_Review` was completed at 09:20:00Z prior to release at 09:40:00Z; and (2) Exemption **EX-77** was approved with a qualified digital signature timestamped at 09:05:00Z (prior to release). The database insertion at 11:00:00Z is fully corroborated by a documented system outage log, satisfying Rule R5.
- **Alternative Reading (NONCOMPLIANT — Strict Database Record Time):**
  If an enterprise compliance policy enforces a "system-of-record gating" rule—requiring that an exemption must be queryable in the centralized database prior to release regardless of external cryptographic validity—releasing at 09:40:00Z when the DB record was created at 11:00:00Z would be flagged as a procedural defect. However, because the operational `Enhanced_Review` was also executed at 09:20:00Z, substantive compliance with R1 is preserved.
- **Decisive Evidence Needed to Resolve:**
  Clarification of organizational policy regarding whether offline cryptographically signed approvals authorize release during IT outages.

### TX-310 (Evidentiary Fraud / Backdated Exemption)
- **Primary Reading (NONCOMPLIANT):**
  The transaction was released at 10:00:00Z without an `Enhanced_Review`, relying on exemption **EX-88**. While the PDF text displayed approval at 08:50:00Z, digital forensics reveal the qualified signing certificate was created at 10:30:00Z (30 minutes after release). The only evidence of pre-release approval is oral testimony from the transaction requester (a conflicted party). Under Rule R5, displayed backdated fields alone are not conclusive, and evidence fails to support that valid approval existed at release time.
- **Alternative Reading (INDETERMINATE — Pending Compliance Officer Testimony):**
  If oral approval by the Head of Compliance is legally binding under institutional bylaws, and the requester's statement is treated as prima facie evidence pending formal interview of `Head_Compliance`, the case could be held as `INDETERMINATE` pending forensic deposition.
- **Decisive Evidence Needed to Resolve:**
  A formal affidavit, recorded telephone/turret audio, or pre-release electronic communication (e.g., email/ticket timestamped prior to 10:00:00Z) from `Head_Compliance` confirming oral authorization at 08:45:00Z.

### TX-311 (Sub-Second / Same-Timestamp Ordering)
- **Primary Reading (COMPLIANT):**
  At timestamp 16:20:00Z, two events occurred: `Enhanced_Review` (`event_index=3`) and `Update_Jurisdiction` to DE (`event_index=4`). Under Rule R7, `event_index` establishes that `Enhanced_Review` occurred while the jurisdiction was US. Then jurisdiction changed to DE. At `Release_Funds` (16:30:00Z), the prevailing jurisdiction is DE. Because DE is domestic, R1 is not triggered at release. Moreover, even if evaluated under US rules, an `Enhanced_Review` was already executed.
- **Alternative Reading (NONCOMPLIANT — Jurisdiction Mutation Invalidation):**
  If an auditor takes the position that changing jurisdiction from US to DE at index 4 is a "material data change" occurring *after* the `Enhanced_Review` at index 3, and that any post-review material change invalidates the review regardless of direction, release without re-review would be noncompliant. However, since the change relaxed rather than heightened the jurisdictional risk (DE is exempt from R1), invalidation is legally and logically unsound.
- **Decisive Evidence Needed to Resolve:**
  Auditor consensus on whether risk-reducing attribute mutations constitute "material data changes" requiring review re-triggering.

## 6. Why Control-Flow-Only Conformance Is Insufficient

Pure control-flow process mining formalisms (standard Petri nets, process trees, DECLARE constraints, and Transition Systems) evaluate compliance strictly based on strings of activity labels. In data-intensive, risk-governed environments, this approach fails catastrophically. The six decisive failure modes are:

1. **Final-State Leakage (Retroactive Contamination):**
   - Control-flow miners typically evaluate data by attaching trace-level attributes taken from the final database state.
   - In cases like **TX-301**, the transaction was created at EUR 80,000 (where standard review sufficed), reviewed, and subsequently increased to EUR 125,000. If evaluated against the final state alone, a control-flow check will either misread the initial review as being compliant or fail to recognize that the initial review was conducted under completely different financial parameters.

2. **Post-Hoc and Backdated Exemptions (Evidentiary Misalignment):**
   - Control flow checks only if an activity or exemption label (e.g., `exemption_id = EX-88`) is present in the case payload. It cannot verify whether the *authorization existed at the time of release*.
   - In **TX-310**, the activity sequence `[Create_Transaction -> Standard_Review -> Release_Funds -> Record_Exemption]` looks identical to **TX-302**. Only multi-temporal evidentiary analysis reveals that EX-88's digital signature certificate was created *after* release (10:30 vs 10:00), making it fraudulent/backdated, whereas EX-77 in TX-302 was cryptographically signed *before* release (09:05 vs 09:40).

3. **Material Data Changes After Review (Control-Flow Invalidation):**
   - Control flow treats an activity execution as permanently satisfying a requirement. Once `Enhanced_Review` appears in a trace, any downstream `precedence(Enhanced_Review, Release_Funds)` rule evaluates to `TRUE`.
   - In **TX-309**, `Enhanced_Review` occurred at 15:25:00Z for EUR 118,000. When amount was updated to EUR 145,000 (+22.88%) at 15:28:00Z, the review was invalidated. Pure control-flow conformance completely misses that the prior review was legally nullified before release.

4. **Conditional Activation and Scope-Dependent Branching:**
   - Compliance obligations are rarely global; they are guarded by complex, multi-variable predicates (e.g., R1 triggers only if `jurisdiction != DE` AND `amount >= 100,000`).
   - In **TX-308**, the initial amount was EUR 140,000 (triggering R1), but decreased to EUR 100,000, preserving review validity. In **TX-307**, the customer was reclassified to `PEP`, triggering R2. Control-flow models cannot dynamically activate or deactivate obligations based on evolving state vectors without an unmanageable combinatorial explosion of artificial activity labels.

5. **Epistemic Uncertainty and Missing Data Handling:**
   - Control-flow models operate under a closed-world, two-valued logic (compliant vs noncompliant).
   - In **TX-305**, the amount feed was down (`amount = null`). A standard system either crashes on missing data or defaults `amount` to 0, falsely marking the case compliant. A data-aware temporal model correctly identifies that the trigger condition cannot be evaluated and outputs `INDETERMINATE`.

6. **Sub-Second and Same-Timestamp Ordering (Concurrency & Tie-Breaking):**
   - Distributed logs frequently contain events sharing the exact same millisecond timestamp.
   - In **TX-311**, `Enhanced_Review` and `Update_Jurisdiction` share timestamp `16:20:00Z`. Without utilizing `event_index` to break ties, a control-flow engine treats them as concurrent or arbitrarily ordered. Using `event_index`, we establish that the review occurred at index 3 while the jurisdiction was US, and index 4 subsequently set jurisdiction to DE, rendering the release at 16:30:00Z fully compliant.

## 7. Recommended Logging and Control Improvements

To eliminate compliance ambiguity, prevent backdating fraud, and enable automated data-aware verification, organizations should implement the following logging and architecture standards:

1. **Immutable Cryptographic Release Payloads:**
   - The `Release_Funds` event must record a cryptographically hashed, immutable snapshot of the exact state vector governing the release (e.g., `released_amount`, `effective_risk`, `effective_jurisdiction`, `effective_customer_type`, `rule_set_version`). This prevents reliance on downstream settlement feeds or unverified post-hoc edits (resolving ambiguities like **TX-305**).

2. **Multi-Temporal Logging Schema:**
   Every event log record must explicitly distinguish and capture four distinct temporal attributes:
   - `event_timestamp`: Physical occurrence time in UTC (ISO 8601 with nanosecond precision).
   - `record_timestamp`: Transaction ingestion time into the immutable audit database.
   - `effective_from`: Business/legal applicability timestamp.
   - `evidence_timestamp`: Digital signature timestamp from a trusted Qualified Trust Service Provider (QTSP) / hardware security module (HSM).

3. **Strict Policy Engine & Rule Versioning:**
   - Every approval, review, and release event must log `policy_version_id` and the specific mathematical evaluation tree used. This ensures that retroactive changes to thresholds (e.g., 20% material change limit) do not distort historical audits.

4. **Cryptographically Scoped Exemption Tokens:**
   - Replace unstructured `exemption_id` text fields with signed JSON Web Tokens (JWT) or Verifiable Credentials. The token must embed:
     - Authorized scope (`jurisdictions`, `customer_types`, `max_amount`, `waived_rules`);
     - Digital signature of `Head_Compliance`;
     - Token issuance time and expiration timestamp;
   - The payment gateway must reject any transaction where the release timestamp falls outside token validity or exceeds scoped parameters.

5. **Full State Provenance & Mutation Diffs:**
   - All state-altering events (`Update_Amount`, `Risk_Reclassify`, `Update_Jurisdiction`) must log `attribute_name`, `previous_value`, `new_value`, `change_reason`, and `authorizing_principal_id`.
   - Invalidation flags must be automatically broadcast to the process instance when a material change threshold is breached.

6. **Monotonic Sequence Ordering and Distributed Causal Tracing:**
   - Implement Lamport timestamps or distributed monotonic sequence IDs (`event_index`, `vector_clock`) alongside wall-clock time. This guarantees deterministic ordering across microservices when multiple events are emitted within the same clock tick.
