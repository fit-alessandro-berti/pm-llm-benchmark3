## 1. Modeling assumptions

### Object types and capitalization:
- Object types: `Order`, `Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, `Payment`.

### E2O qualifier vocabulary:
- `created_for`: Relating Orders to the Order creation event.
- `contains`: Relating Orders to Items, Packages to Items.
- `reserved_for`: Relating Orders to the joint stock reservation event.
- `packed_with`: Relating Orders and Packages to the packing event.
- `dispatched_in`: Relating Deliveries to Orders and Packages.
- `billed_with`: Relating Invoices to Orders.
- `partially_settles`: Relating Payments to Invoices.
- `confirms_for`: Relating Deliveries to the confirmation event.
- `closes`: Relating Orders to their closing event.

### O2O direction conventions:
- `Order contains Item`
- `Package packs Item`
- `Delivery carries Package`
- `Invoice bills Order`
- `Payment partially_settles/complete_settlement Invoice`

### Logistics and financial event relations:
- Logistics and financial events directly relate to key objects upstream (Orders, Items) when causality is explicit (e.g., payment events to Invoices, delivery confirmation to Deliveries). This avoids excessive propagation, keeping analytical reconstruction for indirect causal chains separate.

### Avoid confusion between propagation and source facts:
- Explicit qualifying relationships (`qualifiers`) minimize accidental inference of object interactions from downstream events by including only system-recorded links.

### Version-dependent PM4Py assumption:
- Validate compatibility of `pm4py` APIs. Adjustments for OCEL constructor are isolated and version-safe.
