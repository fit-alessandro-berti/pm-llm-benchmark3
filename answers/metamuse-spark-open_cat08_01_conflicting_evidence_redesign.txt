# 1. Current-process diagnosis

**Core problem is not cycle time but mis-routed speed.** The organization has optimized the entry of the process for throughput, at the expense of containment and correctness downstream.

**Performance problem: Auto-screen fast lane dominates but creates rework loops.** 
From the Frequency DFG: `Intake -> Auto eligibility screen (3600/5000 = 72%) -> Fast approval (2550/3600 = 71%) -> Dispatch replacement (2300/2550 = 90%)` is the most frequent path. Performance DFG shows why it is attractive: `Intake->Auto (median 0.5h) + Auto->Fast approval (median 0.4h) + Fast->Dispatch (median 1.8h) + Dispatch->Close (median 3.2h)  6h` ideal path. By contrast, `Intake->Manual (median 5.0h) -> Request missing evidence (median 10.0h) -> Manual again (median 26.0h loopback)` explains why the *Unclear defect* segment has median 140h. `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review (890)` with a 34h mean loopback, and occurring 2 times in 26% of cases that enter it, is the major cycle-time inflator, not the repair work itself.

**Quality / Rework problem: Fast = cheap before rework, barely cheap after.** Rework statistics show Fast approval cases are 31% cheaper *before* rework but only 9% cheaper *after* accounting for complaints, reshipments and credits. This is explained by `Customer complaint/reopen` rate: 14.1% for Fast approval vs 3.3% for `Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device`. Frequency DFG confirms the coupling: `Dispatch -> Complaint (260)` and `Fast approval -> Complaint (140)` together feed `Complaint -> Manual review (310) -> Escalation board (120)` which adds Performance DFG delays of 40h and 54h mean respectively. `Repair device -> Quality check -> Rework repair (45)` is contained (12.5% for high-value, 4.0% low-risk) compared to open-ended customer-facing rework.

**Compliance problem: Fitness masks systematic violations.** Token-based replay shows `log fitness 0.91` but `fitting traces only 68%` - a classic sign of many traces with small deviations that still replay. Alignment diagnostics name them: `Fast approval before Evidence completeness`, `Dispatch without risk review for high-value devices`, `Complaint after close`. These map directly to normative rules: **R1 violated** (claims >EUR 400 must have Manual or Fraud/risk review before Fast approval) - yet Frequency DFG shows `Auto -> Fast approval 2550` far exceeds `Fraud/risk review -> Fast approval 240`, implying high-value devices are bypassing review. **R2 violated** - `Fast approval -> Dispatch` without evidence completeness, except Premium low-risk accessory is allowed. **R3 at risk** - reopened claims should pass Manual review before close/denial, but `Escalation board -> Dispatch (65)` and `-> Claim denied (10)` suggest shortcuts. Precision is low because the discovered model allows `auto approval + risk review + repair` combinations not allowed by policy - i.e., workarounds have become normalized.

**Customer-experience problem: Same process punishes opposite segments.**
Segment outcomes make the trade-off explicit:
*   **Premium low-risk accessory (18% share):** Fast path 8h median vs manual 46h. Complaint 6% vs 3%. Service-credit exposure if >24h. Here speed *is* quality. R2 explicitly allows the exception for them.
*   **Premium high-value device (14%):** Fast path 20h median with 22% complaint vs reviewed/repair 96h with 5% complaint. Compliance risk high if R1/R2 violated. Cost of wrong replacement EUR 410.
*   **Standard high-value (21%):** Similar pattern 28h/18% complaint vs 120h/6% complaint.
*   **Standard low-risk accessory (25%):** Tolerates 72h if visibility is good; 13h fast vs 52h manual, 8% vs 4% complaint. Experience is driven by status, not pure speed.
*   **Unclear defect (22%):** 140h median, highest abandonment (`Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned 320` and `Customer approval -> Abandoned 70`, total 390 abandoned), repeated requests drive `Manual -> Request missing evidence 780`.

**Resource and temporal dynamics:** Manual eligibility review (7 reviewers, EUR 34/h) queue explodes >82% utilization; Fraud/risk review (2 specialists, EUR 52/h) is bottleneck Mondays/post-campaign (`Auto->Fraud mean 11h, median 8h, p90 28h`). Repair quote delay is waiting for part-price confirmation (`Manual->Repair quote mean 22h, p90 60h` and `Repair quote->Customer approval mean 30h, p90 80h`). Escalation board (45min daily) cannot scale to handle 9.2% overall complaint rate (510 reopens / 5000 cases).

**The Contradiction Synthesized:** The frequency DFG rewards the fast path - it handles volume with near-zero marginal cost (Auto screen EUR 0.20, unlimited capacity) and best observed median times. Management sees average cycle time improve as more cases flow `Auto->Fast->Dispatch`. But performance DFG long-tail (`Fast->Complaint p90 150h`, `Dispatch->Complaint p90 180h`), rework statistics (9% net saving), and conformance (32% non-fitting, R1/R2 violations) reveal that speed is borrowed from quality and compliance. The slow path *looks* expensive (`Manual 7.5h mean`, `Repair` labor) but has contained rework (3.3% complaint) and avoids EUR 410 error cost for high-value. Averaging across segments hides this: fast is dominant and correct for Premium low-risk, but destructive for high-value.

# 2. Redesign A: Segment-Aware Smart Gate - Fix the Auto Rules and Catch Errors Before Shipment

**Redesigned process flow:**
`Intake claim -> Enriched Auto-Triage (new rules + segment/risk check)` branches into three lanes:
1. **Green Lane (Premium + Standard low-risk accessory, clear defect, <EUR 400):** `Auto eligibility -> Fast approval -> Lightweight Pre-Dispatch Check (new, 6-8h, sampling) -> Dispatch -> Close`. R2 exception is preserved and formalized.
2. **Yellow Lane (High-value devices, any Premium high-value, Standard high-value >EUR 400, or fraud score medium):** Mandatory `Fraud/risk review OR Manual eligibility review` before any `Fast approval`. `Auto -> Fast approval` is technically blocked by workflow control. If approved, still passes Pre-Dispatch Check (100% for high-value).
3. **Red Lane (Unclear defect / missing evidence):** `Auto -> Request missing evidence` triggers structured evidence portal invite + deadline, not immediate Manual review. Only after one portal attempt does it go to `Manual review`.

New controls: Updated auto-screen rules deployed (2-week effort) and a lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate staffed by cross-trained Quality inspectors (currently underused) to verify part-number vs defect-type before `Dispatch replacement`.

**Targets:** The 2550 `Auto -> Fast approval` edge and its 2300 downstream dispatches; specifically the ~35% of that flow that is high-value (14%+21% = 35% of cases) currently violating R1/R2. Also targets `Dispatch -> Complaint (260)` long-tail due to wrong part.

**Mechanism of improvement:** Shifts cost from downstream external failure (complaint, reshipment EUR 95/410, credit) to upstream prevention (EUR 0.20 rule update + 6-18h gate). Performance DFG shows `Fast->Dispatch 3.0h mean` is fast because unchecked; adding 6-8h gate still keeps Green Lane <24h (protecting Premium SLA), but catches mis-selection that currently costs 96h mean for `Dispatch->Complaint`. Conformance: enforces R1/R2 by design, raising fitting traces from 68% toward >85% without adding Manual headcount for low-risk. Uses existing slack: Quality inspectors (2 inspectors, underused) absorb gate, avoiding new hires.

**Deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved:** Accepts 15-35% increase in Manual/Fraud inflow as auto-rules tighten - will push Manual utilization toward 82%+ and increase median time for high-value fast-path cases from 20-28h to ~40-50h. Does not fix the `Request missing evidence -> Manual` loop (26% repeat rate) - only reduces its volume slightly by better initial routing. Intentionally leaves customer waiting experience untouched for Standard low-risk; visibility problem remains.

# 3. Redesign B: Evidence-First Repair-Forward - Eliminate the Fast-Path for Complex Claims

**Redesigned process flow:**
Fundamentally different logic: instead of gating the fast lane, it *removes* eligibility for fast lane for two segments.

`Intake -> Complexity Classifier`: If `Unclear defect OR high-value device with ambiguous defect` -> **Repair-Forward Path**: `Auto screen (fraud only) -> Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device -> Quality check -> Close`. `Fast approval -> Dispatch` is disabled for these cases. `Request missing evidence` is replaced by a **Synchronous Evidence Completion** step: customer uploads via portal + assisted by 2 dedicated Evidence Concierges (reallocated from Manual pool) before any eligibility decision, with SLA 4h. `Fraud/risk review` is embedded in parallel to Manual review, not sequential (`Manual->Fraud mean 18h` eliminated by parallelization).

For low-risk accessories (43% share: 18% Premium + 25% Standard), fast path remains untouched.

High-value but clear defect keeps a *conditional* fast path only if evidence complete and fraud score low.

**Targets:** The `Request missing evidence (650+780=1430 entries)` cycle and its 34h loopback; the 22% Unclear defect segment with 140h median and highest abandonment (320+70); and the 22% complaint rate for Premium high-value on fast path. Also targets `Rework repair 45` for high-value (12.5%) by routing through `Quality check` mandatorily.

**Mechanism of improvement:** Trades upfront delay for elimination of customer-facing rework. Rework stats show `Repair quote -> Approval -> Repair -> Quality check` yields 3.3% complaint vs 14.1% fast, and even with rework, total external failure is lower. By forcing evidence completeness *before* decision, it addresses alignment finding `Fast approval before Evidence completeness` and R2 at source. Cost effect: loses 31% pre-rework saving but captures near-full post-rework saving because EUR 410 wrong-replacement cost and escalation board time are avoided. Temporal profile: `Manual->Repair quote 22h mean` dominated by waiting for part-price; redesign pre-negotiates part prices for top 20 defect types and gives technicians price authority up to EUR 500, cutting that edge to ~10h. Customer approval wait (`30h mean, p90 80h`) is shortened by offering instant credit vs repair choice in portal.

**Deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved:** Explicitly sacrifices speed and short-term cost for high-value: cycle time rises from 20-28h to ~70-90h for that cohort, violating Premium expectation of fast replacement. Will increase Manual and Repair technician pressure (3 technicians already constrained) and requires new portal adoption. Deliberately leaves Premium low-risk SLA risk unmanaged if portal abandonment increases (noted risk for low digital literacy). Compliance for R1 improves, but abandons attempt to make fast path compliant - simply bypasses it.

# 4. Redesign C: Choice-Architecture & Decoupled Resolution - Let Customers Self-Select Speed vs Certainty

**Redesigned process flow:**
Introduces front-end differentiation and back-end decoupling, not a rule change.

`Intake -> Instant Segment Offer` (presented in portal/IVR):
*   **Option 1: Rapid Swap (24h SLA):** Available only to low-risk accessory + Premium high-value customers who accept *like-for-like* replacement with waiver of exact part-match guarantee. Flow: `Auto -> Fast approval -> Dispatch -> Close` with **no** pre-dispatch gate, but with proactive tracking and instant credit if complaint. Explicitly documented R2 exception with customer consent.
*   **Option 2: Verified Repair (5-day SLA):** Default for Standard high-value and Unclear defect. Flow: `Intake -> Evidence portal (self-serve, 30-45% repeat reduction) -> Manual/Risk review (batched) -> Repair`. 
*   **Option 3: Assisted Claim (for low digital literacy):** `Request missing evidence` goes to human callback, not portal loop, to avoid abandonment.

Back-end: `Customer complaint/reopen -> Manual review (310)` and `-> Escalation board (120)` are decoupled. New **Tier-1 Resolution Pool** (2 senior reviewers, asynchronous, not daily 45min board) handles 80% of complaints within 24h; Escalation board reserved for fraud/compliance exceptions. `Quality/compliance close -> END 3015` becomes two-stage: operational close (customer satisfied) + async compliance check within 72h to avoid blocking shipment.

**Targets:** Standard low-risk accessory tolerance for 72h if visibility good; Premium low-risk credit risk if >24h; Unclear defect abandonment; and Escalation board scalability. Targets Performance DFG edges `Customer complaint -> Escalation 54h mean` and `Complaint -> Manual 40h mean` by adding parallel capacity. Targets `Request missing evidence -> Abandoned 320` by offering assisted path.

**Mechanism of improvement:** Uses *customer experience* as control lever rather than process control. Instead of forcing compliance via system block (A) or forcing repair (B), it makes trade-off explicit and consented, reducing non-compliant hidden workarounds (low precision cause). For Standard low-risk, visibility + choice reduces anxiety and complaints without speeding up actual work. For Unclear defect, portal reduces repeat requests by 30-45% (given), while assisted path contains abandonment risk for low digital literacy. Decoupling close from compliance check removes compliance from critical path, preserving speed for Premium while still catching R3 violations async (reopened must pass Manual before final compliance close, but not before shipment).

**Deliberately sacrifices / leaves unresolved:** Sacrifices first-time quality and compliance certainty for Rapid Swap opt-ins: accepts 6-8% complaint rate on that lane as cost of speed, and creates documented compliance risk that must be priced as service credits. Does not improve `Repair device -> Quality check -> Rework` technical quality; still allows unchecked `Fast->Dispatch` for those who choose speed. Leaves resource pressure on Manual largely unchanged - relies on self-selection to balance load rather than enforcing it, so fraud/risk Monday bottleneck may persist if many choose Rapid Swap.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A: Smart Gate**
1. **Gate itself becomes delay beyond SLA:** If Quality inspectors (2) staff gate 100% for high-value, `6-18h` becomes 24-36h when campaign spikes (Fraud bottleneck Mondays propagates). Premium low-risk then exceeds 24h credit threshold, turning quality gain into service-credit loss.
2. **Rule update over-routes to Manual, causing queue collapse:** 15-35% inflow increase pushes Manual utilization >85%, Performance DFG `Manual->Request evidence 14h mean` balloons to 30h+, triggering abandonment (currently 320) instead of prevention.
*Displaced bottleneck:* Fraud/risk review (2 specialists). Blocking `Auto->Fast` forces all high-value through 2-person pool; Monday after marketing, p90 28h becomes p90 50h+ and starves Yellow lane.

**Redesign B: Repair-Forward**
1. **Portal abandonment spike for vulnerable customers:** Evidence portal reduces repeats 30-45% for digitally literate, but Unclear defect segment (22% share) includes low digital literacy; synchronous completion requirement increases `Request evidence -> Abandoned 320` to higher, losing legitimate claims and NPS.
2. **Repair capacity starvation:** Forcing high-value into `Repair quote (3 techs)` + `Repair device` drives tech utilization >90%; part-price authority helps but supplier lead time remains. `Repair device->Quality check 9h mean` queues, and `Rework repair 12.5%` for high-value rises with rushed repairs.
*Displaced bottleneck:* Repair technicians and Quality check. Current Quality underused except after campaigns; under B, 2 inspectors face 100% of high-value repairs (360+ cases) plus rework loops, becoming new constraint.

**Redesign C: Choice & Decoupled**
1. **Adverse selection - everyone picks Rapid Swap:** If customers overweight speed, 60-70% of Standard high-value opt for Rapid Swap despite EUR 410 error cost, re-creating current `Dispatch->Complaint 260` volume but now with documented waiver - compliance not improved, costs rise, R1 effectively nullified by consent loophole.
2. **Tier-1 pool normalizes poor quality:** Decoupling complaints from Escalation board (45min daily) with async Tier-1 resolves fast but may auto-approve credits without root-cause feedback; `Customer complaint -> Manual 40h` loop persists upstream, so defects are compensated not prevented, eroding learning.
*Displaced bottleneck:* Evidence portal and assisted callback. Choice architecture pushes 43% low-risk + 22% unclear into self-serve; portal support tickets and callback queue for low-literacy option become new front-door bottleneck, replicating `Intake->Manual 7.5h` delay at entry.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**Staged logic: Test gating cheapest, then structural alternatives, never optimize average cycle time alone. All pilots use segment-stratified control groups.**

**First Pilot (Weeks 1-4): Redesign A - Lightweight Pre-Dispatch Gate for High-Value Only**
*Why first:* Implementation complexity Low, deployable in 2 weeks (rule update + gate SOP), uses existing Quality slack, directly tests contradiction hypothesis (does catching wrong part reduce long-tail rework?). No portal needed, limits abandonment risk.
*Scope:* Randomize 50% of high-value claims (Premium high-value 14% + Standard high-value 21% = 35% of volume, ~1750 cases) that would have gone `Fast->Dispatch` to mandatory gate (6-8h checks by 1 inspector, sampling part-number vs defect-type). Low-risk accessories stay as control (current flow).
*Metrics to monitor (segment-split, not average):*
- Primary: `Dispatch -> Complaint` rate for high-value gated vs ungated; `Fast->Complaint` rate; Compliance fitting traces % via alignment (R1/R2 violations per 100 cases)
- Guardrails: Gate median time, Manual/Fraud queue length and utilization, Premium SLA breach (>24h) and credit exposure, Rework repair rate
- Downstream shift check: `Complaint -> Manual` and `Complaint -> Escalation` volumes, `Quality/compliance close` cycle time, cost per closed claim *including* reshipment/credit (not just pre-rework cost)

*Stop/go thresholds (qualitative ranges, not false precision):*
- **Go to scale A:** Gated high-value complaint  30% (e.g., 18-22%  12-14%), fitting traces  to 80%, gate added time stays 12h median, Manual util stays <85% for 3 weeks. Then expand gate to 100% high-value and test rule tightening (+10% manual inflow).
- **Stop/rollback A:** Gate adds >18h median *or* Premium high-value SLA breaches increase >50% vs control *or* Manual queue p90 doubles with abandonment increase >15%. Indicates gate resource insufficient or rule over-triggering.

**Second Experiment (Weeks 5-10): Conditional branch based on A**

*If A succeeded:* Pilot **Redesign B-lite: Evidence-First for Unclear Defect only** (not all high-value). Target worst segment (22% unclear, 140h median, highest abandonment). Deploy portal invite + 1 Evidence Concierge callback vs control (current `Request missing evidence -> Manual` loop). Parallelize `Manual + Fraud` for this cohort. Goal: test if eliminating 34h loopback reduces total cycle time *for that segment* despite added upfront step.
*If A failed due to Manual overload:* Pilot **Redesign C-lite: Choice + Visibility for Standard low-risk** (25% share). Offer explicit `Rapid (24h) vs Verified (72h with tracking)` choice, no workflow blocks. Test if visibility reduces complaints without adding review cost, and if self-selection balances Manual load naturally.

*Metrics:* Segment median + p90 (not mean), `Request missing evidence` repeat rate, abandonment rate, `Complaint rate` for pilot segment, cost-after-rework, portal completion vs abandonment split by digital-literacy proxy, Tier-1 resolution time if C.

*Stop/go:*
- **Go B:** Repeat evidence  to <15% (from 26% with 2 requests), Unclear median 140h  <100h, abandonment not worse than control (+<5%). Then consider extending repair-forward to Standard high-value.
- **Go C:** Standard low-risk complaint  25% with stable cost, Rapid Swap opt-in <40% of high-value (avoiding adverse selection), portal abandonment for low-literacy not elevated vs assisted arm. Then decouple Escalation board fully.
- **Stop:** Any pilot where segment complaint rises vs control or downstream `Rework repair` or `Escalation board` load increases >20% without upstream gain  indicates shifting, not solving.

**Sequencing Logic & Learning vs Shifting:**
1. **Always segment-report:** Never report global average cycle time; report Premium low-risk, Standard low-risk, high-value, unclear separately. Improvement is real only if *target segment* improves without degrading another beyond tolerance (e.g., Premium SLA).
2. **Trace the loop, not the edge:** Use process mining monthly: recompute Frequency DFG and Performance DFG for pilot cohorts. True improvement shows `Request missing evidence -> Manual` edge thinning and `Dispatch -> Complaint` thinning; shift shows same edges reappear as `Complaint -> Manual` or `Quality check -> Rework`.
3. **Conformance as guardrail:** Re-run alignments weekly; if fitting traces drop while speed improves, gains are compliance-borrowed.
4. **Holdout and washout:** Keep 20% holdout current process throughout. Measure closed-loop cost (including credits/reshipments 30 days post-close) to catch downstream displacement (`Fast approval -> Complaint mean 72h, Dispatch->Complaint 96h` lag).
5. **No exact Pareto dominance claim:** After 10 weeks, characterize each redesign's position on speed/cost/quality frontier by segment, then let sponsor choose trade-off (e.g., protect Premium SLA vs minimize compliance risk) rather than declaring one optimal.
