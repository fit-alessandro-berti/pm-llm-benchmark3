# 1. Data audit and estimands

**Opening-hours convention.** The service center is open 07:00–19:00, i.e. 12 hours = 720 minutes per day. All `arrivals` and `completed` counts are restricted to these opening windows.

**Queueing quantities and estimands.**
- **Arrival intensity** \(\lambda\): cases entering per minute (or per hour) during opening. Estimand from data: \(\lambda = \text{arrivals}/720\).
- **Completion intensity** \(\mu\): cases leaving per minute during opening. Estimand: \(\mu = \text{completed}/720\).
- **Service time** \(S\): active handling time excluding waiting. We only observe the **median** \(m\) and **p90** \(p\). The mean \(\mathbb{E}[S]\) is not supplied; therefore any load estimate using \(m\) is a **lower bound** because \(\mathbb{E}[S] \ge m\) for right-skewed service.
- **WIP** \(L\): `mean_wip` is the mean number of cases in the system from 15-minute snapshots. This is an **open-hours-only** average (48 snapshots inside 07:00–19:00). Overnight carry-over is not sampled.
- **Capacity** \(c\): `staff_fte` is mean concurrently available full-time-equivalent handlers. I assume \(c\) equals the number of parallel servers for a single pooled queue.
- **Capacity pressure**: \(\rho_{\text{med}} = \lambda \cdot m / c\) is a defensible **lower-bound** traffic-intensity indicator; \(\rho_{p90} = \lambda \cdot p / c\) is a tail-pressure indicator, not a steady-state \(\rho\).
- **Flow time** \(W\): mean time from arrival to completion. Not directly observed. Little’s Law would give \(W = L/\lambda\) or \(L/\mu\) only under strict conditions (see §6).

**Key limitations of the aggregates.**
- `median_service_min` and `p90_service_min` are not mean service times; substituting them for \(\mathbb{E}[S]\) or for flow time is invalid.
- `mean_wip` excludes the closed period, so cases waiting overnight are missing from the WIP average.
- Backlog at 07:00 is not given; completed cases need not have arrived the same day.
- Daily rows are not independent: weekday/weekend cycles, trends, and the outage create serial dependence.
- `complex_pct` may reflect a real case-mix shift, a coding change, or both (labelled **Hypothesis H1** below).

**Threats to naive inference (5).**
1. **Open-hours WIP boundary mismatch.** Little’s Law using `mean_wip` ignores overnight backlog, violating boundary stability.
2. **Median/p90 instead of mean.** Using median service time as if it were mean service or mean flow time understates true load and waiting.
3. **Non-independence of daily rows.** Weekend/weekday alternation and the Apr-20 step change induce autocorrelation and trend.
4. **Outage–recovery nonstationarity.** Apr-15 has 180 min outage; Apr-16/17 are catch-up days. Pooling them with normal days biases completion intensity.
5. **Complexity ambiguity (H1).** The Apr-20 jump in `complex_pct` may be a coding change, so treating it as genuine demand growth may overstate required capacity.
6. **Peak-share column inconsistency (Observation O1).** The table defines `peak_08_10_pct` as 08:00–09:59, but the representative hourly vectors imply the reported percentages align with bins 09:00–10:59 (indices 2–3). This must be treated as a data-definition threat.

- It pools the Apr-15 outage and Apr-16/17 recovery with ordinary baseline days, hiding a severe capacity-removal event.
- It ignores that weekend capacity (2.5 FTE) is structurally different from weekday capacity, even within the same complexity epoch.
- If H1 is true (coding change rather than real mix shift), this segmentation attributes capacity need to a possibly phantom demand change.

### Additional alternative (mentioned, not primary)
An **intraday burstiness segmentation** based on the four representative vectors (ordinary, outage, bursty, weekend) would tell a story where arrival-process variance, not mean rate, drives peak WIP. This is materially different but is weakly supported because only four days are represented.

# 4. Pooled versus segmented statistics

**Pooled naive view vs segmented reveal.**

| View | Pooled / naive summary | Segmented reveal | Why pooling obscures |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekday/weekend composition | Mean daily arrivals  90.0 over all 28 days | Weekday mean  108.0/day; weekend mean  44.9/day (factor ~2.4) | A single mean is representative of neither a weekday nor a weekend |
| Complex-case mix | Simple mean of `complex_pct`  32% | Pre-Apr-20 arrival-weighted complex share  21%; post-Apr-20  43% | Pooling hides a step-change that doubles complex load |
| Outage / intervention effect on completion | Overall completed/arrivals = 2409/2519  0.956 | R1  0.994; R3 = 0.552; R4 = 1.143; R7 = 1.041 | Outage under-completion and recovery over-completion cancel in the pooled ratio |
| Service-time behavior | Mean of daily medians  31.0 min | R1 median 25; R5 median 35; R2 median 29 | A single “average service time” hides bimodal regimes |
| WIP vs service time | Across all days, higher median service tends to coincide with higher WIP | Weekend (med 29, WIP 13.8) has higher median than R1 (med 25, WIP 20.3) but lower WIP | Simpson-like confounding: arrival rate and capacity differ by calendar |

**Robust takeaway.** Pooled means are dominated by weekday weight and by the post-Apr-20 complex epoch. They should not be used to infer a single \(\lambda\), \(S\), or \(\rho\).

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

**Representative hourly vectors — dispersion indicators.**  
Using the 12 hourly bins (07–08 … 18–19), with \(k=12\):

| Rep day | Total arr | Mean/hr | Population variance | Fano factor (Var/Mean) | CV (SD/Mean) | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 04-07 ordinary | 101 | 8.42 | 24.9 | 2.96 | 0.59 | Overdispersed vs Poisson (Fano=1) |
| 04-15 outage | 105 | 8.75 | 33.2 | 3.79 | 0.66 | Overdispersed; completions show midday zeros |
| 04-21 bursty | 115 | 9.58 | 88.8 | 9.27 | 0.98 | Strong burstiness; two peaks (10–11 and 13–14) |
| 04-25 weekend | 51 | 4.25 | 4.98 | 1.17 | 0.52 | Near-Poisson, only mild overdispersion |

**Observation O2.** The outage-day completion vector contains zeros around the 11:00–14:00 window, confirming nonstationarity inside the day.  
**Observation O3.** The bursty day has a similar *total* arrival count to ordinary weekdays but a Fano factor ~3× larger, so mean-arrival models would underestimate peak congestion.

**Daily sequence and serial dependence.**
- **Weekly seasonality:** weekend arrivals drop sharply, so lag-1 autocorrelation of raw daily arrivals is weakened by the FriSat and SunMon transitions, whereas lag-7 autocorrelation is strongly positive (confirmed in §8 output).
- **Carry-over dependence:** net daily inflow (`arrivals``completed`) is positively associated with next-day `mean_wip`, indicating overnight backlog carry-over.
- **Change points:** clear discontinuities at Apr-15 (outage), Apr-20 (`complex_pct` step from ~25 to >40 and median service jump), and Apr-29 (staff increase). The complexity jump is sustained for 9 days, so it is a regime change, not noise.

**Why four representative days are insufficient.** They are selected examples, not a random sample; they lack interarrival distributions, case-level identifiers, service-start/end events, and coverage of all 28 days. No full stochastic model (e.g., MAP/PH or time-varying M(t)/G(t)/c) can be identified from four hourly profiles plus daily aggregates.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

**Convention.** Implied flow time is computed only where explicitly chosen as \(W_{\text{impl}} = \text{mean\_wip} / (\text{completed}/720)\) minutes, using completions as the departure proxy. This is an **open-hours mean residence** and is not the same as true end-to-end flow time if overnight backlog exists.

| Window / regime | Boundary stability | Throughput proxy | WIP comparability | Carry-over risk | Stationarity assessment | Implied flow time (if computed) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Overall pooled 28 days | Poor (mix of regimes) | Ambiguous ( overall) | Low (regimes differ) | High | None | Not computed | Not defensible |
| R1 Baseline weekday | Stable across 7 days | Completions  arrivals (0.994) | Open-only, low backlog | Low | Approx stationary | \(20.3/(99.6/720)\approx147\) min | Approximate only |
| R3 Outage | Unstable (midday outage) | Completions depressed | Open-only, large hidden start backlog | High | None | \(47/(58/720)\approx583\) min | Not defensible |
| R4 Recovery | Post-outage, improving | Completions > arrivals (catch-up) | Open-only, inherits R3 backlog | High | Transition | Not reported as steady | Not defensible |
| R5 Complex weekday | Stable within epoch | Completions < arrivals (net +94) | Open-only, backlog building | High | Approx stationary but accumulating | \(44.3/(100.6/720)\approx317\) min | Approximate only |
| R7a Intervention weekday | Staffing changed; exact time unlogged | Completions > arrivals | Open-only, inherited backlog | Medium | Short window, declining WIP | \(37.0/(121/720)\approx220\) min | Approximate only |
| R2 Baseline weekend | Stable | Completions  arrivals | Open-only, overnight carry likely | Low–Medium | Approx stationary | \(13.8/(41.5/720)\approx239\) min | Approximate only |

**Key caveat.** In no row is median service time substituted for mean flow time. The implied \(W\) values are larger than median service (25–35 min), confirming that waiting dominates and that active service time alone is a poor proxy for flow time.

# 7. Operational implications

**Staffing.**
- Baseline weekends (R2) are safely staffed at 2.5 FTE, but complex weekends (R6) show \(\rho_{\text{med}}\approx0.93\) at 2.5 FTE; the intervention’s move to 3.0 FTE is directionally correct.
- Complex weekdays (R5) operate near saturation on median service and are severely tail-loaded (\(\rho_{p90}>2\)). The Apr-29 weekday staffing increase to 7.0 FTE reduces pressure but does not eliminate tail overload.

**Backlog recovery.**
- R3R4 shows that one 180-minute outage required more than one day of elevated staffing to bring WIP down. A explicit recovery plan (e.g., temporary +1 FTE for 48h after any outage) is warranted.

**Peak smoothing.**
- The bursty representative day (Apr-21) has ordinary mean arrivals but Fano  9.3. Appointment buffering, staggered complex-case intake, or intraday cross-training would reduce peak WIP more efficiently than average-capacity increases.

**Data-collection recommendations.**
1. Log 07:00 starting backlog explicitly.
2. Capture case-level event timestamps to compute true flow time and mean service, not just med/p90.
3. Record exact intervention start times and distinguish coding changes from real mix shifts (H1).
4. Correct the `peak_08_10_pct` definition inconsistency (O1).

**Policy that looks beneficial under pooled averages but risky under segmentation.**
A pooled view shows post-intervention average WIP  30 and overall completed/arrivals > 1, which could justify “return staffing to pre-intervention levels (6.0 weekday / 2.5 weekend) to save cost.” Under the segmented view, R5/R6 complex regimes still exhibit \(\rho_{\text{med}}\approx0.93\) and tail \(\rho_{p90}>2\); cutting staff would rebuild backlog precisely where complex demand persists. The pooled saving is therefore a Simpson’s-paradox artifact of mixing the high-complex surge with the later recovery window.
