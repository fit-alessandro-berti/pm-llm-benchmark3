## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### 1.1 Stipulated facts (high-confidence)
- **Normative baseline CS-9 and M0**
  - **CS-9 authority**: CS-9 is a formally approved compliance standard effective from 2025-01-01 and remains in force throughout all cases. It requires that every supplier onboarding includes `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` before activation, and it requires `Manual_Procurement_Approval` before `Activate_Supplier`. It also requires strict separation of requester and manual approver as different humans.
  - **M0 semantics**: M0 encodes a control-flow where:
    - `Register_Supplier` precedes `checks`.
    - `checks` is a partial order containing concurrent `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review`.
    - After checks, an XOR `decision` chooses between `approved_branch` and `Reject_Supplier`.
    - The approved branch enforces `Manual_Procurement_Approval` before `Activate_Supplier`.
    - `Notify_Requester` follows the decision; `Close_Case` follows notification.
  - **Scope encoded in M0**: M0 does not encode requester/approver separation, conflict-of-interest rules, risk/amount limits, eligibility conditions for pilots/emergencies, or reactivation-specific behavior.

- **Pilot PD-3 (N2)**
  - PD-3 is a steering-committee pilot decision, documented but not incorporated into CS-9 or M0.
  - Effective **only** from 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31.
  - For eligible LOW-risk DE suppliers in approved corporate groups:
    - `Auto_Approve` may replace `Manual_Procurement_Approval`.
    - `Sanctions_Check` and automated master-data validation must precede activation.
    - `Quality_Review` may occur up to two business days **after** provisional activation.
  - Compliance observer called it a “monitored exception” and later stated she did not intend to weaken CS-9 permanently.

- **Emergency directive FD-7 (N3)**
  - FD-7 is an emergency directive, effective **only** from 2026-07-10 00:00 through 2026-07-12 23:59.
  - Scope is limited to critical relief suppliers needed to prevent shutdown after flooding.
  - It allows **provisional activation** to occur before `Quality_Review` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval`, but still requires `Sanctions_Check` before activation, caps spend at EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval, and requires daily monitoring plus quality review within two business days.
  - FD-7 expired automatically; there is no written extension.

- **Data and practice**
  - From 2026-07-01, `Notify_Requester` migrated to CRM; only ~70% of notifications are mirrored in the onboarding log; CRM notifications have stable `notification_id`, but legacy linking is via mutable text fields.
  - Operations frequently performs `Request_More_Info` cycles before decision; stakeholders consider this desirable but M0 cannot represent loops or a dedicated `Request_More_Info` activity.
  - Operations wants a permanent low-risk auto-approval branch; no permanent amendment to CS-9 exists.

- **Aggregate evidence**
  - 312 cases used `Auto_Approve` in June–July: 289 appear PD-3-eligible, 14 clearly ineligible, 9 missing eligibility attributes.
  - 47 cases contain `Request_More_Info`; none activated before the requested evidence arrived.
  - Notification missingness in the onboarding log increased from 2% to 29% after CRM migration.
  - Six post-pilot cases used `Auto_Approve` in the first week of August.
  - Internal Audit identified SUP-703 and SUP-708 as potentially severe.

### 1.2 Disputed or assumption-dependent propositions
- **Scope of M0**
  - Whether **rejected leads** like SUP-709 are governed by M0 is unclear: CS-9 allows “abbreviated pre-screen” for rejected leads and does not state if these leads are part of the onboarding model’s scope. Process owner treats them as intake only; model owner indicates M0 starts after pre-screen acceptance.
  - **Reactivations** (SUP-710) are not addressed by CS-9; it is ambiguous whether they are “new onboardings” that must replay the full flow or a separate reactivation process.

- **Pilot and emergency as policy vs exception**
  - It is disputed whether PD-3 should be treated as:
    - a narrow, temporary exception to CS-9; or
    - a de facto amendment indicating evolving policy that M0/CS-9 failed to absorb.
  - Similarly, whether continuing FD-7-like behavior after expiry (SUP-705) is:
    - clear noncompliance; or
    - a reasonable continuation of risk-managed emergency measures in the absence of prompt formal renewal.

- **Data reliability**
  - Missing `Notify_Requester` events (e.g., SUP-706) may indicate:
    - True non-notification; or
    - Data-collection defects due to CRM migration.
  - Identity resolution (e.g., SUP-712 aliases, SUP-715 spouses) depends on master-data quality; we assume those mappings are correct but acknowledge potential error.

- **Risk materialization**
  - For SUP-708, where `Activate_Supplier` preceded `Sanctions_Check` but no transactions occurred before sanctions clearance, it is disputed whether we face:
    - a severe control failure regardless of realized loss; or
    - a near-miss with mitigated practical risk.

### 1.3 Burden-of-proof framework
- **Standard for declaring NONCOMPLIANT**
  - **Normative source**: CS-9 and FD-7 are formal, controlled documents; PD-3 is a documented but non-codified pilot.
  - I adopt a **“preponderance of evidence”** standard:
    - Where the log and contextual information, interpreted under CS-9 plus any valid overlay (PD-3 or FD-7 within scope and validity window), show that required controls were absent or improperly sequenced, the case is **NONCOMPLIANT**.
    - Ambiguity about scope (e.g., reactivations, rejected leads) shifts classification toward **MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT** if operations behave consistently with their documented understanding and no clear contrary rule exists.
    - Data gaps that plausibly arise from known logging changes (e.g., CRM migration) shift classification toward **DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE**, not automatic noncompliance.

- **Standard for accepting exceptions (JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION)**
  - Exceptions must be supported by:
    - A formally documented overlay (FD-7, PD-3) in force at the time; and
    - Evidence that the case meets that overlay’s eligibility conditions (risk, jurisdiction, corporate group, emergency scope, spend caps, deadlines, etc.).
  - Where behavior matches an overlay **in intent and conditions**, but documentation is weak or the overlay’s legal status is ambiguous, I still treat the case as **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION** but mark system-level governance as deficient.

- **Treatment of model vs reality**
  - M0 is not itself law; it is a **normative POWL representation** intended to reflect CS-9. When M0 conflicts with CS-9 or with well-documented overlays, CS-9 and valid overlays prevail; M0’s gap is a **MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT**.
  - Conversely, desirable behavior not yet codified (e.g., permanent low-risk auto-approval) does **not** retroactively change CS-9; such behavior may still be **NONCOMPLIANT** while simultaneously showing that CS-9/M0 are obsolete.


## 2. Prosecution Brief

### 2.1 Normative authority and expiry
- **CS-9 remains the binding standard** for all onboardings during the observed period. Neither PD-3 nor FD-7 purports to amend CS-9 textually; they are overlays that must be read as **exceptions**, not as replacements.
- **PD-3 is limited in time and scope**:
  - Effective only 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31.
  - Applicable only to LOW-risk DE suppliers already in approved corporate groups.
  - It allows quality review after activation, but only in this narrow context and only under monitoring.
  - The compliance observer’s subsequent clarification that she did **not** approve weakening CS-9 reinforces that PD-3 should be interpreted as a tightly controlled pilot, not a policy amendment.
- **FD-7 is a narrow emergency carve-out**:
  - Applicable only 2026-07-10 to 2026-07-12 and only for critical relief suppliers needed to prevent shutdown.
  - It never permits skipping `Sanctions_Check`; it only relaxes the timing of `Quality_Review` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval` and caps spend until retrospective approval.
  - The lack of written extension means any FD-7-style behavior after 2026-07-12 is **unauthorized**, regardless of factual flood conditions.

### 2.2 M0 and CS-9 as enforcement baselines
- **M0 lawfully encodes key CS-9 requirements**:
  - Both `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` must complete before the XOR decision branch is taken; the approved branch mandates `Manual_Procurement_Approval` before `Activate_Supplier`.
  - `Notify_Requester` and `Close_Case` ensure traceable closure.
  - While M0 omits some dimensions (e.g., resource separation, pilots, emergencies), it faithfully captures the core **control-flow** obligations of CS-9: checks before approval, approval before activation, decision before notification and closure.
- Therefore, **any trace that activates a supplier without completing sanctions, quality, and manual approval in the required order is prima facie noncompliant**, unless an overlay applies.

### 2.3 Deviations and normalization of exceptions
- **Auto-approval misuse and drift**
  - 312 cases used `Auto_Approve` during June–July; 289 appear PD-3-eligible, but **14 are clearly ineligible and 9 are missing eligibility attributes**. This already demonstrates that PD-3 was not reliably gated by the stipulated conditions.
  - SUP-703 is a high-profile example:
    - High-risk new foreign supplier, explicitly ineligible, processed on 2026-06-20 via `Auto_Approve`.
    - Activation occurred before quality review; first purchase was EUR 180,000.
    - This violates CS-9 (no manual approval, quality after activation), exceeds what PD-3 allows (ineligible risk profile), and introduces substantial financial and sanctions risk.
  - After PD-3’s expiry on 2026-07-31, **six post-pilot cases** still used `Auto_Approve` in early August, including SUP-714 on 2026-08-02.
    - SUP-714 repeats the pilot pattern without legal basis: low-risk and operationally sensible, but undertaken **after PD-3 expired** and without a permanent amendment.
    - The continuation of the pilot path beyond its window shows **normalization of an exception into de facto policy** without governance.

- **Emergency behavior beyond FD-7**
  - SUP-704 (2026-07-11) is within FD-7 and shows how the emergency path should work: `Sanctions_Check` before activation, limited spend (EUR 18,000), daily monitoring, and retrospective quality and manual approval next business day.
  - SUP-705 (2026-07-13) replicates the FD-7 pattern **after FD-7 expired**:
    - Operations claim the factual flood emergency persisted, but no written extension exists.
    - Spend (EUR 12,000) remained below the FD-7 cap, but that does not compensate for the lack of authority.
  - The pattern demonstrates that emergency provisions, once introduced, were **continued without reauthorization**, eroding the boundary between governed emergency exceptions and opportunistic shortcuts.

### 2.4 Hidden risks not captured by M0 alone
- **Resource and conflict-of-interest violations**
  - CS-9 mandates that the requester and manual approver be different humans; M0 does not enforce this, but it remains a normative requirement.
  - SUP-712: the trace fits M0 perfectly, but identity resolution reveals the requester and approver are the same human via aliases. This is a **clear SoD violation** and therefore noncompliant even though control-flow conformance is perfect.
  - SUP-715: again the trace fits M0, but the quality reviewer and manual approver are spouses in the same reporting line, without a recorded conflict-of-interest disclosure as required by policy. This is a **noncompliant lack of disclosure** and an unmitigated conflict-of-interest risk.
  - These cases show that relying solely on M0 conformance **underestimates actual compliance risk**; control-flow-centric metrics could falsely classify them as compliant.

- **Sanctions-before-activation as a non-negotiable rule**
  - CS-9 and FD-7 both insist that `Sanctions_Check` occur **before activation**; neither PD-3 nor FD-7 authorizes activation before sanctions.
  - SUP-708 violates this core requirement:
    - `Activate_Supplier` occurs 40 minutes before `Sanctions_Check` for a high-risk supplier without any applicable pilot or emergency.
    - Even though no transactions occurred before sanctions clearance, this is a **severe control failure**: the organization exposed itself to sanctioning risk and would have been unable to block transactions if they had occurred earlier.
  - This case, flagged by Internal Audit, exemplifies why **model deviations correlate with serious risk**, not just technicalities.

### 2.5 Desirability does not retroactively amend policy
- **Pilot success does not equal policy change**
  - Operations argue that the high volume and largely positive outcomes of PD-3 auto-approvals (289 seemingly eligible cases with timely post-activation quality reviews) indicate that the pilot “ought” to be permanent.
  - However, neither CS-9 nor any follow-up decision codified this; **normative change requires formal approval, not operational consensus**.
  - Post-pilot auto-approvals (e.g., SUP-714 and the other August cases) are therefore **unauthorized deviations**, irrespective of their low observed loss.

- **Desirable `Request_More_Info` loops vs formal model**
  - SUP-707 and the 47 cases with `Request_More_Info` show mature, risk-averse behavior: additional information is collected and no activation happens before evidence is received.
  - Nonetheless, the absence of explicit modeling of this loop in M0 means that conformance metrics will mark these traces as deviating. This is a **model limitation**, but it does not excuse other deviations.
  - Crucially, the existence of benign deviations does **not** negate the presence of severe ones (SUP-703, SUP-708); the latter still warrant enforcement.

### 2.6 Case-level prosecution conclusions
- **Clearly NONCOMPLIANT and high severity**:
  - SUP-703 (unauthorized auto-approval for a high-risk foreign supplier with large spend and late quality review).
  - SUP-708 (activation before sanctions for a high-risk supplier without emergency/pilot).
  - SUP-705 (emergency-style activation after FD-7 expiry).
  - SUP-712 (SoD violation via aliases).
  - SUP-715 (undisclosed conflict of interest).
- **Formally noncompliant but mitigated / low-to-moderate severity**:
  - SUP-702 and SUP-714 (auto-approval pattern where PD-3 either barely applies or has expired, with apparently low risk and good documentation).
  - SUP-710 (reactivation without explicit procedure; arguably noncompliant because full CS-9 controls are not replayed).
- **Data-related but potentially noncompliant**:
  - SUP-706 (missing notification in onboarding log with weakly linked CRM evidence).
  - SUP-711 (duplicate registrations indicate log integrity issues that could mask other problems).

On this view, **operations are materially noncompliant** relative to CS-9 and M0: exceptions have been normalized, critical controls (sanctions-first, manual approval, SoD, conflict-of-interest disclosures) are sometimes breached, and logging problems obscure traceability.


## 3. Defense Brief

### 3.1 M0 obsolescence and incompleteness
- **Missing pilot and emergency overlays**
  - M0 encodes only the “steady-state” CS-9 flow and is blind to PD-3 and FD-7, both of which were formally documented by steering and emergency authorities.
  - Cases like SUP-702 and SUP-704 therefore appear nonconformant to M0 even though they **faithfully follow** their respective overlays (PD-3 and FD-7) with proper eligibility, monitoring, and timing.
  - The failure to update M0 or to layer it with declarative constraints for pilots/emergencies is a **model governance issue**, not an operational failure.

- **Lack of loops and concurrency expressiveness**
  - M0 cannot represent:
    - Repeated `Request_More_Info` cycles.
    - Pilot semantics where `Quality_Review` may occur after provisional activation for low-risk cases.
    - Emergency semantics where provisional activation is allowed before quality review and manual approval, with retrospective controls.
  - Because of these limitations, cases like SUP-707 (good practice) appear as deviations, and emergency/pilot cases cannot be naturally expressed. This inflates deviation rates and creates a misleading picture of noncompliance.

### 3.2 Scope and lifecycle gaps
- **Rejected leads (SUP-709)**
  - CS-9 explicitly acknowledges that rejected leads may use an “abbreviated pre-screen process” and does not clearly state that they are within the scope of the onboarding model M0.
  - Process owners treat rejected leads as **intake cases**; model owners claim M0 begins only after pre-screen acceptance. This split indicates **uncertain scope**, not clear noncompliance.
  - Under an intake-centric view, SUP-709 is **out of M0’s scope** and reasonably omits `Quality_Review` for an early rejection, so control-flow “nonconformance” is an artifact of mis-scoping.

- **Reactivations (SUP-710)**
  - CS-9 lacks a reactivation procedure; yet reactivation is a real business need.
  - SUP-710 applies `Sanctions_Check`, `Manual_Procurement_Approval`, and activation while relying on still-valid historical quality certification.
  - Whether this is noncompliant depends on an unstated policy choice: does reactivation require a full re-onboarding, or is referencing recent certification acceptable?
  - In the absence of explicit rules, classifying SUP-710 as outright noncompliant is **overreaching**; it is more accurate to see this as a **MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT** where policy has not kept pace with lifecycle realities.

### 3.3 Data-collection and logging defects
- **Notification migration (SUP-706 and aggregate missingness)**
  - N4 confirms that from 2026-07-01, `Notify_Requester` moved to CRM and that only about 70% of notifications are mirrored into the onboarding log.
  - SUP-706’s missing notification in the log is therefore plausibly a **data-collection problem**, not necessarily a process failure:
    - CRM shows a notification at +3 hours, consistent in timing and content with expectations.
    - The case link is via a mutable text field added two days later; this is weak evidence but consistent with known migration issues.
  - The sharp rise in missing notifications from 2% to 29% after migration strongly suggests that **logging defects dominate** any true process deviation rate for notification.

- **Event duplication (SUP-711)**
  - SUP-711’s duplicate `Register_Supplier` events share the same source transaction ID and were attributed to migration.
  - The subsequent control flow (sanctions, quality, manual approval, activation, notification, closure) is perfectly aligned with CS-9 and M0.
  - Treating this as noncompliance conflates **ETL noise** with operational behavior; a more appropriate classification is that the case is compliant while the event data have quality issues that must be fixed analytically.

### 3.4 Distinguishing control-flow deviation from misconduct and risk
- **Emergency case SUP-704**
  - SUP-704, occurring during FD-7’s validity window, follows FD-7’s requirements:
    - `Sanctions_Check` before activation.
    - Spend limited to EUR 18,000 (below the EUR 20,000 cap).
    - Daily monitoring and retrospective quality review and manual approval the next business day.
  - Although M0 and CS-9 alone would deem the activation timing irregular, FD-7 is a duly authorized emergency overlay. This case is better classified as a **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**, not noncompliance.

- **Pilot case SUP-702**
  - SUP-702 is a low-risk DE supplier in an approved corporate group, on 2026-06-15 within PD-3’s window.
  - It uses `Auto_Approve`, performs sanctions and master-data validation before activation, and completes `Quality_Review` the next business day. CRM provides a stable `notification_id`.
  - From an operational and risk perspective, this is a **well-governed pilot-compliant exception**; the fact that M0 lacks an auto-approval branch does not make the underlying behavior wrongful.

- **Near-miss SUP-708**
  - SUP-708 is indeed a serious control deviation (activation before sanctions); however:
    - No transactions occurred before sanctions clearance, so the realized risk is lower than the potential risk.
    - The fact that there was no emergency or pilot overlay suggests an isolated operator error rather than normalized policy drift.
  - Defense concedes that the control breach is real but argues that **systemic condemnation** of operations based on a handful of severe near-misses overstates systemic noncompliance, especially when compared to hundreds of correctly handled high- and low-risk cases.

### 3.5 Pilot normalization as evidence of model obsolescence
- **High volume of seemingly appropriate PD-3 usage**
  - 289 of 312 June–July auto-approval cases appear PD-3-eligible; 47 `Request_More_Info` cases show careful information gathering before activation.
  - This suggests that frontline teams consistently internalized the **risk-based intent** of PD-3 (low-risk, DE, approved corporate groups) and applied it mostly correctly, with a relatively small set of clear misuses.
  - SUP-714 and the other six August auto-approvals signal that PD-3-style behavior had become the **de facto operating model**: risk-based auto-approval with post-activation quality review for low-risk suppliers.
  - The failure to amend CS-9 and M0 to reflect this new equilibrium is therefore a **policy lag**, not simply operational defiance.

### 3.6 Defense conclusions
- Defense acknowledges:
  - SUP-703 and SUP-708 as genuinely severe cases.
  - SUP-712 and SUP-715 as problematic SoD and conflict-of-interest cases.
  - Weak governance around pilot expiry and emergency-extension documentation.
- But defense maintains that:
  - **Many apparent M0 deviations** (SUP-702, SUP-704, SUP-707, large portions of the 312 auto-approvals, notification gaps, reactivations, rejected leads) are better explained by **model obsolescence, incomplete scope, or data defects** than by material noncompliance.
  - System-level judgment should therefore be **mixed**: some noncompliance exists, but the current normative model and data capture are not adequate bases for blanket condemnation.

## 5. Final Judgment

### 5.1 Case classifications, severity, and confidence

I use qualitative severity levels (LOW, MODERATE, HIGH) and confidence levels (LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH).

- **SUP-701**: **COMPLIANT**, severity **LOW**, confidence **HIGH**. Classic CS-9 and M0-conformant case with complete evidence and proper SoD.
- **SUP-702**: **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**, severity **LOW–MODERATE**, confidence **HIGH**. PD-3-eligible, well-documented pilot case; the legal formality of PD-3 is debatable, but given its explicit steering-committee approval and monitoring, I treat it as a justified exception; underlying model remains obsolete.
- **SUP-703**: **NONCOMPLIANT**, severity **HIGH**, confidence **HIGH**. Ineligible for PD-3, lacks manual approval before activation, quality after activation, large spend; this is a textbook serious breach.
- **SUP-704**: **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**, severity **MODERATE**, confidence **HIGH**. Clearly within FD-7; emergency behavior is authorized and properly bounded by spend and retrospective controls.
- **SUP-705**: **NONCOMPLIANT**, severity **MODERATE**, confidence **MEDIUM–HIGH**. FD-7 expired; although factual emergency persisted and controls mirrored FD-7, absence of written extension makes this unauthorized emergency behavior.
- **SUP-706**: **DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE**, severity **LOW–MODERATE**, confidence **MEDIUM**. Likely compliant notification with poor linkage due to CRM migration; cannot conclusively prove or disprove notification without better data.
- **SUP-707**: **MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT**, severity **LOW**, confidence **HIGH**. Behavior is desirable and risk-reducing; deviation is due solely to M0’s inability to represent `Request_More_Info` loops.
- **SUP-708**: **NONCOMPLIANT**, severity **HIGH**, confidence **HIGH**. Activation before sanctions for a high-risk supplier with no overlay; even as a near-miss, it reflects a fundamental control failure.
- **SUP-709**: **MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT**, severity **LOW**, confidence **MEDIUM**. Either out-of-scope intake or legitimately using abbreviated pre-screen; risk is limited because the supplier is rejected; main issue is ambiguous scope.
- **SUP-710**: **MIXED** (dominant reason: **MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT with residual control weakness**), severity **MODERATE**, confidence **MEDIUM**. Reactivation process is ungoverned; sanctions and manual approval mitigate risk, but lack of explicit rules for reusing quality certification and missing modeling of reactivation make this chiefly a model/policy gap with some noncompliant flavor.
- **SUP-711**: **COMPLIANT** (with data issues), severity **LOW**, confidence **HIGH**. Control flow is correct; duplicate registration is a migration artifact; I treat the process as compliant and flag the data separately.
- **SUP-712**: **NONCOMPLIANT**, severity **MODERATE–HIGH**, confidence **HIGH**. Clear SoD breach: requester and approver are the same person; risk of fraud or biased approval is real even if no loss is yet observed.
- **SUP-713**: **MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT**, severity **LOW**, confidence **HIGH**. Label rename (`QA_Clearance`) with later-approved equivalence; operationally compliant, but model and mapping lag behind reality.
- **SUP-714**: **NONCOMPLIANT**, severity **LOW–MODERATE**, confidence **HIGH**. Low-risk and operationally sensible but uses auto-approval after PD-3 expiry; this is unauthorized normalization of pilot behavior.
- **SUP-715**: **NONCOMPLIANT**, severity **MODERATE**, confidence **MEDIUM–HIGH**. Trace fits M0, but undisclosed conflict-of-interest violates policy; risk is less acute than sanctions failures but not negligible.

### 5.2 System-level judgment on operational compliance
- **Operationally**, the system shows a **mixed picture**:
  - A substantial share of cases (e.g., SUP-701, SUP-707, SUP-711, many PD-3-eligible auto-approvals) exhibit sound risk-based practice.
  - However, the presence of multiple NONCOMPLIANT cases (SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, SUP-715) shows that **material noncompliance exists**, not just technical deviations.
  - The normalization of pilot and emergency behavior beyond their authorized windows (SUP-705, SUP-714 and the other August cases) weakens the culture of formal adherence.
- Overall, I judge **operational compliance** as **PARTIALLY NONCOMPLIANT**: the system is not in collapse, but serious vulnerabilities and governance gaps persist that warrant remediation.

### 5.3 System-level judgment on model and data validity
- **M0 validity**:
  - M0 correctly captures the **standard** CS-9 control-flow but is **obsolete and incomplete** for the actual operating environment.
  - It lacks constructs for:
    - Request-more-info loops.
    - Low-risk auto-approval.
    - Emergency/provisional activation.
    - Reactivations and out-of-scope rejects.
  - Consequently, M0’s raw deviation rates conflate genuine noncompliance with justified exceptions and model gaps.
- **Data validity**:
  - Logging of `Notify_Requester` after CRM migration is significantly degraded (missingness from 2% to 29%); duplicate registrations and label changes further complicate analysis.
  - Event data are **insufficient as a single source of truth** for compliance; cross-system IDs, immutable links, and rule-versioning are needed.
- System-level, I judge the **model/data stack** as **INSUFFICIENT FOR FAIR ENFORCEMENT**: it must be revised before M0-based metrics can be relied upon as the primary compliance oracle.

### 5.4 Where each side’s argument is rejected
- I **reject the prosecution’s strongest claim** that “high M0 deviation rates alone prove widespread noncompliance”:
  - Many deviations are clearly due to missing loops, pilots, emergencies, and scope definitions.
  - Some deviations (SUP-707, SUP-711, SUP-713) reflect good practice or benign data issues rather than wrongdoing.
- I **reject the defense’s strongest claim** that “model obsolescence and data issues mostly exonerate operations”:
  - SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, SUP-715 remain genuinely noncompliant under any reasonable reading of CS-9 and known overlays.
  - The normalization of exceptions without formal governance is itself a material compliance problem.
- The balanced judgment is that **both** sides are partly right:
  - The **prosecution** is right that there is material noncompliance and that some deviations are severe.
  - The **defense** is right that M0 and the logging setup are not fit for purpose and must be overhauled for fair, automated conformance checking.


## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

### 6.1 Layered architecture overview
- **Layer 1 – Core control-flow (POWL)**
  - Represents the **standard onboarding route**:
    - `Register_Supplier` → concurrent `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` (with optional `Request_More_Info` loop) → decision among:
      - Standard manual approval and activation.
      - Low-risk auto-approval and activation.
      - Emergency/provisional activation followed by retrospective quality and approval and final activation.
      - Rejection and closure.
    - `Notify_Requester` always before `Close_Case`.

- **Layer 2 – Declarative constraints (DECLARE/log-skeleton-style)**
  - Capture **data-aware and temporal rules** not naturally expressed in POWL:
    - Sanctions must happen before any activation or provisional activation.
    - Quality must happen before final activation except where an overlay explicitly allows post-activation quality within strict deadlines.
    - Amount/risk/time constraints: spend caps under FD-7, deadlines for retrospective quality and approval, allowed windows for PD-3.
    - Separation-of-duties and conflict-of-interest constraints parameterized by identity, role, and relationship.

- **Layer 3 – Data validation (pandas/data-checks)**
  - Enforce **data-quality and eligibility checks**:
    - Presence and integrity of eligibility attributes (risk level, jurisdiction, corporate group, PD-3 eligibility flags).
    - Stable, immutable cross-system IDs (e.g., `notification_id`, `case_id`, `supplier_id`).
    - Correct event ordering and uniqueness (no unexplained duplicates like SUP-711).
    - Structured storage of overlays in effect for each case (which rule-version applied at each decision).

- **Layer 4 – Organizational and governance controls**
  - Codify **who may create/approve overlays** and how:
    - Clear authority model for pilots (PD-* rules) and emergencies (FD-* rules).
    - Mandatory documentation of effective dates/times, scope, risk limits, and expiry.
    - Required ex-post evaluation of pilots before normalization.
    - Formal reactivation and rejected-lead procedures.

### 6.2 Scope rules for special case types
- **Rejected leads**
  - Define a separate **“Intake / Pre-screen” scope** for early rejection cases:
    - Activities: `Register_Lead`, `Sanctions_PreScreen`, optional limited `Quality_PreScreen`, `Reject_Lead`, `Notify_Requester`, `Close_Intake`.
    - Only leads that pass pre-screen and become candidates for onboarding enter the main onboarding model.
  - The POWL model for onboarding should therefore **exclude** pure intake-only cases; these should be evaluated against a simpler pre-screen model.

- **Reactivations**
  - Introduce a **“Reactivation” sub-process**:
    - Entry from an existing supplier record with a still-valid or expiring quality certification.
    - Mandatory `Sanctions_Check`, confirmation that quality certification is within its validity horizon, and fresh `Manual_Procurement_Approval` (or justified auto-approval if allowed).
    - Optional shorter quality check (e.g., `Quality_Refresh`) defined explicitly.
  - Scoping rules must state when a reactivation requires a **full onboarding** vs a **reactivation shortcut**; this avoids ambiguity like in SUP-710.

- **Withdrawals and provisional activations**
  - For FD-7-like emergencies, define:
    - `Provisional_Activate_Supplier` within an emergency overlay.
    - Rules on when provisional activation must be **withdrawn** (e.g., if retrospective quality or approval fails) and how the case is closed or reverted.
  - These semantics belong partly in POWL (additional activation states) and partly in declarative rules (deadlines, caps, monitoring frequency).

### 6.3 Where each element should live
- **POWL model (control-flow)**
  - Good for:
    - The **standard order** of major activities (register → checks/loops → decision → notify → close).
    - **Concurrency** of sanctions and quality checks in the standard route.
    - Structural branching between manual, auto, emergency/provisional, and reject routes.
    - A simplified representation of `Request_More_Info` as an optional or summarized transition within the checking phase.

- **DECLARE/log-skeleton constraints**
  - Good for:
    - **Temporal and conditional constraints** that depend on timestamps and risk data:
      - “Sanctions must precede any activation.”
      - “If route == emergency, then quality and manual approval must occur within 2 business days of provisional activation.”
      - “If route == PD-3 auto-approval, then risk = LOW and jurisdiction = DE and corporate_group_approved = TRUE.”
    - **Loop properties** such as “Request_More_Info can repeat arbitrarily, but activation cannot occur before the last requested evidence has been received.”
    - **Resource constraints**:
      - Requester ≠ manual approver.
      - No first-degree relatives or same-reporting-line relationships without explicit mitigation.

- **Pandas/data checks and data models**
  - Good for:
    - Enforcing the presence and quality of **eligibility snapshots**:
      - Capturing risk, jurisdiction, corporate group membership, and due-diligence status at the time of decision.
    - Implementing **amount caps** and monitoring thresholds (e.g., EUR 20,000 under FD-7).
    - Maintaining **actor alias mappings** and delegation chains to evaluate SoD and CoI rules.
    - Verifying **notification link integrity** via stable IDs rather than free-text fields.

- **Organizational controls**
  - Good for:
    - Defining who can authorize pilots and emergencies and under which circumstances.
    - Ensuring overlays are registered in a **policy registry** with rule/version IDs, effective windows, and required follow-up reviews.
    - Training operations staff so that pilot/emergency routes are turned off or formalized on time; preventing further post-expiry drift as seen in SUP-705 and SUP-714.


## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

### 7.1 Revised POWL model (executable code)

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# Base transitions
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")  # QA_Clearance is mapped here at data level
request_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")  # summarizes one or more info cycles
manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
provisional_activate = Transition(label="Provisional_Activate_Supplier")
final_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

# 1) Standard concurrent checks: sanctions and quality may occur in parallel
checks_parallel = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])
# No order between sanctions and quality -> true concurrency

# 2) Checks with a Request_More_Info step (approximation of loop)
checks_with_rmi = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality, request_info])
checks_with_rmi.order.add_edge(sanctions, request_info)
checks_with_rmi.order.add_edge(request_info, quality)

# XOR between "simple checks" and "checks with extra info"
checks_choice = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[checks_parallel, checks_with_rmi],
)

# 3) Standard manual-approval route
standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[checks_choice, manual_approval, final_activate]
)
standard_route.order.add_edge(checks_choice, manual_approval)
standard_route.order.add_edge(manual_approval, final_activate)

# 4) Low-risk auto-approval route
# NOTE: Data/DECLARE constraints ensure it is only used for eligible low-risk cases.
low_risk_auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[checks_choice, auto_approve, final_activate]
)
low_risk_auto_route.order.add_edge(checks_choice, auto_approve)
low_risk_auto_route.order.add_edge(auto_approve, final_activate)

# 5) Emergency/provisional route
# - Sanctions must still precede any activation.
# - Provisional activation can occur after sanctions.
# - Quality and manual approval must both complete before final activation.
emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[sanctions, provisional_activate, quality, manual_approval, final_activate]
)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(sanctions, provisional_activate)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(provisional_activate, quality)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(provisional_activate, manual_approval)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(quality, final_activate)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(manual_approval, final_activate)

# 6) Rejection route (after basic checks)
reject_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])
reject_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[reject_checks, reject])
reject_route.order.add_edge(reject_checks, reject)

# 7) Decision between approval routes and rejection
decision = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[standard_route, low_risk_auto_route, emergency_route, reject_route],
)

# 8) Top-level model: register -> decision -> notify -> close
M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, decision, notify, close])
M1.order.add_edge(register, decision)
M1.order.add_edge(decision, notify)
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)
```

**Limitations and approximations**
- The `Request_More_Info` loop is approximated as a **single summarized transition** with an XOR choice between “with RMI” and “without RMI.” This does not model unbounded cycles but captures the presence or absence of additional information gathering before quality review.
- The emergency route models **provisional** and **final** activation as distinct transitions; data and declarative rules must enforce spend caps, monitoring, and deadlines.
- The rejection route assumes basic sanctions and quality checks before rejection; a separate pre-screen model can be defined for early rejects that never enter this onboarding POWL.

### 7.2 Policy-overlay registry (normalized table)

With these schemas in place:
- POWL `M1` governs the **shape of traces**.
- DECLARE/log-skeleton rules, parameterized by `rule_version_id`, `eligibility_snapshot_id`, and actor relationships, govern **temporal, data, and resource constraints**.
- Pandas/data checks ensure that **overlay conditions, spend caps, deadlines, and SoD/CoI constraints** are applied correctly per case.

This layered architecture allows:
- Fair assessment of compliance (distinguishing true NONCOMPLIANT cases from JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT, and DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE).
- Future policy evolution (e.g., codifying a permanent low-risk auto-approval route) while preserving clear audit trails of when and how the rules changed.
