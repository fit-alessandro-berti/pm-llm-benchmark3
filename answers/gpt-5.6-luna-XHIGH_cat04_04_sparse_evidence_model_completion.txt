# 1. Evidence Ledger

I use the symbols `R`, `C`, `E`, `S`, `X`, `A`, `P`, and `H` for Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Expedite Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, and Archive Case, respectively. The model language is about visible completion order, not about start order or hidden execution intervals.

| Evidence ID | What it supports | What it does not prove | Reliability | Modeling implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1: 12 x `R C E S A P H` | A common route has `C` before `E`, includes `S`, and ends in `A P H`. | It does not prove that `C` must precede `E`, that `S` is mandatory, or that every adjacent pair is a causal dependency. | High for the fact that these completions occurred; medium for causal interpretation. | Include this route in the conservative language; use it as evidence for, but not proof of, the corresponding order. |
| V2: 7 x `R E C S A P H` | The reverse principal-check completion order also occurs frequently, with `S` before approval. | It does not prove that `E` must precede `C`, nor that the two checks are concurrent. | High for occurrence; medium for interpretation. | A model with only `C -> E` is contradicted by the observed log unless it explicitly models logging distortion or noncompliance. |
| V3: 4 x `R C E A P H` | `S` is absent from some cases, and a route without `S` is observed. | Absence does not prove that supplier work was not required or did not happen; the case could be stocked or affected by missing integration data. | High for the recorded trace; medium for interpreting absence. | Provide an absent-`S` branch, but document that it conflates true skip and missing event. |
| V4: 3 x `R E C A P H` | The reverse principal-check order also occurs when `S` is absent. | It does not distinguish true concurrency from two sequential scheduling alternatives. | High for occurrence; medium for interpretation. | Do not impose a single observed order for `C` and `E` in the balanced model. |
| V5: 2 x `R C S E A P H` | `S` can complete after `C` but before completion of `E`; `S` need not visibly wait for the completion of both checks. | It does not prove that `S` started after `C`, because only completions are logged; it does not prove that `C` and `E` ran in parallel. | High for completion order; low-to-medium for start/dependency interpretation. | A partial-order branch may constrain `C < S` while leaving `E` and `S` unordered. |
| V6: 1 x `R C E S X A P H` | `X` occurred after both principal checks and after `S` in one case. | It does not prove that `X` always follows `S`, or that `X` is required. | High for the one occurrence; low for generalization. | Include an optional `X` branch and avoid imposing `S < X` globally. |
| V7: 1 x `R E C X S A P H` | `X` occurred after both principal checks but before `S` in one case. | It does not prove that `X` and `S` are generally unordered or that the same order is valid for every case. | High for the one occurrence; low for generalization. | The balanced model leaves `X` and `S` unordered when both are present. |
| V8: all 30 cases start with `R` and end with `H`; all 30 have the suffix `A P H`. | `R` is a stable visible start, `H` is a stable visible end, and `P` follows `A` in every case. | It does not by itself prove that no hidden work occurs before `R` or after `H`, or that `P` is the only activity between approval and archive. | High for the observed log; D1 and D2 provide independent support. | Make `R` the source and `H` the sink; enforce `A < P < H` and `A < P` in all candidates. |
| DFG: `C -> E` has frequency 17 and `E -> C` has frequency 11 | Both completion orders occur, and neither order is a rare one-off. | Two opposite directly-follows arcs do not prove concurrency. They can result from XOR routing, sequential cases with different scheduling choices, overlapping activities observed by completion, or a mixture of those explanations. | High as a derived count; lower as causal evidence because the DFG is based on completion order. | Use the bidirectional DFG as evidence against a single fixed sequence, not as a proof of a parallel gateway. |
| D1 | `R` is the intended start and `H` the intended end. | It does not establish the entire internal control flow. | High. | Treat `R` and `H` as hard boundary constraints. |
| D2 | `P` requires completed `A`. | It does not say whether `A` has one or several prerequisites beyond the other rules. | High. | Enforce `A < P < H`. |
| D3 | Both `C` and `E` should be completed before `A`. | “Should” is not an absolute guarantee, and it does not say whether `C` and `E` are sequential or overlapping. | Medium-to-high. | Keep `C < A` and `E < A` in all three models; regard this as a soft business rule that would merit exception logging. |
| D4 | When supplier confirmation is required, it must precede `A`. | It does not establish how often confirmation is required or whether an absent event means stocked, missing, or waived. | Medium. | Any recorded `S` must be before `A`; an absent-`S` branch is allowed only as an explicit missing/stocked abstraction. |
| D5 | `S` may begin after either principal check has produced enough information; it need not wait for both. | It does not prove actual start-time order, and “may” does not quantify whether this is common or exceptional. | Low-to-medium. | The balanced model uses a `C < S` or `E < S` partial-order alternative. The permissive model additionally tests an early-procurement interpretation. |
| D6 | `X` is optional, occurs at most once, needs both `C` and `E`, and is before `A`. | It does not prove how often `X` occurs, or whether supplier confirmation must precede it. | Medium. | Use an absent/present XOR for `X`; in the present branch enforce `C < X`, `E < X`, and `X < A`, with no general `S`/`X` order. |
| D7 | Completion timestamps do not identify concurrency, sequencing, or overlap. | It does not tell us which interpretation is true. | High as a data-quality limitation. | Treat concurrency as a hypothesis represented by an unordered partial order, not as a discovered fact. |
| S1 | Finance claims `C` must finish before `E` starts. | It does not establish that the statement applies to all orders, that it is enforced, or that the log preserves the relevant start/completion relation. It conflicts with the observed `E`-then-`C` traces if read literally for all cases. | Medium stakeholder evidence; internally inconsistent with part of the log. | Keep a strict `C < E` model as a compliance sensitivity interpretation, but do not make it the balanced interpretation without scope information. |
| S2 | Engineering claims the checks normally run in parallel and whichever finishes first appears first. | It does not prove that every case is parallel, that “normally” applies to this population, or that completion order reflects start overlap. | Medium stakeholder evidence. | Supports an unordered `C`/`E` partial order in the balanced candidate. |
| S3 | Supplier confirmation is mandatory for every external component. | The external/stocked attribute is not present, so it cannot establish that `S` is mandatory for every observed case. | Medium. | Make `S` conditional on an unobserved attribute; do not infer unconditional mandatory `S`. |
| S4 | Stocked components legitimately skip supplier confirmation. | It does not establish how many cases are stocked or rule out missing events among the absent cases. | Medium. | Supports a genuine no-`S` route in addition to the missing-event explanation. |
| S5 | The external/stocked attribute was not exported, and about 5–10% of `S` events may be missing. | It does not identify which cases are affected or explain all seven traces without `S`; the percentage is approximate. | Medium data-quality evidence. | Treat no observed `S` as “no recorded completion,” not as definitive business optionality; collect the attribute and event-source status. |
| S6 | Compliance permits `X` after both checks and either before or after `S`, but before approval. | It does not establish the frequency of `X` or settle whether “after” refers to completion or start. | Medium-to-high for the rule. | Use `C/E < X < A` and leave `S` and `X` unordered when both are recorded. |
| S7 | Only completion timestamps are available; same-timestamp order is ingestion order. | It does not provide a reliable total order for simultaneously completed work and cannot reconstruct intervals. | High. | Do not interpret adjacency, same-time order, or opposite DFG arcs as proof of causality. |

The opposite DFG arcs are therefore evidence of completion-order variability, not evidence of a parallel gateway. For example, a process with an XOR choice between `C`-then-`E` and `E`-then-`C` produces both arcs without concurrency; a process with overlapping checks can also produce both arcs. The data lacks the lifecycle intervals needed to distinguish these hypotheses.

# 2. Shared Assumptions

All three models use the following common abstraction.

- Each visible label represents one completion event per case. Starts, durations, retries, and hidden work are not modeled. The trace order is the timestamp/ingestion order supplied by the log.
- `R` is the visible source and `H` is the visible sink. `A` must precede `P`, and `P` must precede `H`.
- `C` and `E` each occur at most once in this abstraction and both must be completed before `A`, reflecting D3. This is a modeling boundary, not a claim that real checks cannot be retried.
- `X` is optional and at most once. If present, it is after both `C` and `E` and before `A`, reflecting D6 and S6.
- The `S`-absent alternative means “no recorded `S` completion.” It combines at least two latent states: stocked/waived supplier work and a missing integration event. The models do not pretend to identify which latent state occurred.
- In the balanced and permissive models, optionality is represented by an XOR between a branch containing `S` and one without `S`; `X` is handled similarly. This is control-flow abstraction for missing/conditional data, not a probability estimate.
- Event labels are assumed to be unique within a case. If retries or duplicate labels matter, they need lifecycle/retry attributes or distinct labels such as `Credit Check (attempt 1)`; otherwise an ordinary trace-language model will collapse those histories.
- The 30 counts are used as evidence and for validation. The candidate models are unweighted languages; they do not claim that the 12-case route has probability 12/30 in the future.

# 3. Conservative Candidate

The conservative candidate has a low-generalization, high-precision inductive bias: it admits exactly the seven distinct visible variants that were observed, while retaining the hard boundary rules. It deliberately treats the observed completion orders as the only safe control-flow alternatives. This protects against inventing concurrency or optionality from sparse evidence, but it is expected to overfit the sample.

Let `⊕` denote XOR and juxtaposition denote sequence. The compact formal expression is

```text
M_conservative =
  (R C E S A P H)
⊕ (R E C S A P H)
⊕ (R C E A P H)
⊕ (R E C A P H)
⊕ (R C S E A P H)
⊕ (R C E S X A P H)
⊕ (R E C X S A P H)
```

The following is self-contained ProcessTree construction code. Counts are intentionally not repeated in the tree because a ProcessTree encodes behavior, not trace frequency.

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

R = "Register Order"
C = "Credit Check"
E = "Engineering Review"
S = "Supplier Confirmation"
X = "Expedite Review"
A = "Approve Configuration"
P = "Release Production"
H = "Archive Case"

def activity(label):
    return ProcessTree(label=label)

def sequence(*labels):
    node = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE)
    node.children = [activity(label) for label in labels]
    return node

def xor(*trees):
    node = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR)
    node.children = list(trees)
    return node

model_conservative = xor(
    sequence(R, C, E, S, A, P, H),
    sequence(R, E, C, S, A, P, H),
    sequence(R, C, E, A, P, H),
    sequence(R, E, C, A, P, H),
    sequence(R, C, S, E, A, P, H),
    sequence(R, C, E, S, X, A, P, H),
    sequence(R, E, C, X, S, A, P, H),
)
```

Behavior deliberately excluded:

- Every complete trace not equal to one of the seven listed variants, including new `C`/`E` interleavings and new placements of `S` or `X`.
- `R E S C A P H`, even though D5 and S2 make it plausible.
- `R C E X S A P H`, even though V7 shows the reverse `S`/`X` order in another principal-check order.
- `R C E S X A P H` with a different or absent principal-check order, and any other unseen `X` placement.
- Duplicate checks, retries, hidden activities, and any event after `H`.

Known underfitting risks:

- It converts completion-order alternatives into hard sequences and therefore rejects valid executions with overlapping checks.
- It treats the exact seven variants as exhaustive, so it cannot generalize from `R C S E` to `R E S C` or from the two `S`/`X` orders to other valid interleavings.
- It cannot distinguish true supplier optionality from missing `S` events and may misclassify legitimate external cases with an omitted event.
- It overfits the two observed `X` cases and provides no principled behavior for a third valid expedite scenario.
- An XOR enumeration scales with the number of distinct variants. With duplicate labels, different control-flow histories can become indistinguishable as visible traces; with retries or many combinations, the number of branches can grow combinatorially and make the tree difficult to maintain.

# 4. Balanced Candidate

The balanced candidate uses a moderate-generalization bias. It treats the principal checks as unordered at the completion-order level, preserves the high-confidence approval/release constraints, and models `S` and `X` as conditional optional work. It does not assert that `C` and `E` were physically concurrent; it chooses a partial-order hypothesis because it explains both observed orders without inventing a fixed sequence.

For a set of activities and precedence relation `≺`, let `PO(activities, ≺)` mean the language of all linear extensions of that partial order. Define `B(s,x,g)` as follows, where `s` says whether `S` is present, `x` says whether `X` is present, and `g` is the supplier gate:

```text
base: R<C, R<E, C<A, E<A, A<P, P<H
if s: R<S, g<S, S<A       where g is C or E
if x: C<X, E<X, X<A
```

There is no edge between `C` and `E`; there is no edge between `S` and `X`. The formal model is

```text
M_balanced =
  ⊕ B(0, 0, -)
  ⊕ B(0, 1, -)
  ⊕ B(1, 0, C)
  ⊕ B(1, 0, E)
  ⊕ B(1, 1, C)
  ⊕ B(1, 1, E)
```

This uses an XOR over partial-order alternatives because ordinary conjunction cannot express the disjunctive statement “`S` may be released after `C` or after `E`” without incorrectly requiring both. The following POWL construction implements that expression.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

R = "Register Order"
C = "Credit Check"
E = "Engineering Review"
S = "Supplier Confirmation"
X = "Expedite Review"
A = "Approve Configuration"
P = "Release Production"
H = "Archive Case"

def partial_order(include_supplier=False, include_expedite=False,
                  supplier_gate=None):
    labels = [R, C, E]
    if include_supplier:
        labels.append(S)
    if include_expedite:
        labels.append(X)
    labels.extend([A, P, H])

    transitions = {label: Transition(label=label) for label in labels}
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=set(transitions.values()))

    def edge(before, after):
        po.order.add_edge(transitions[before], transitions[after])

    edge(R, C)
    edge(R, E)
    edge(C, A)
    edge(E, A)
    edge(A, P)
    edge(P, H)

    if include_supplier:
        edge(R, S)
        if supplier_gate in (C, E):
            edge(supplier_gate, S)
        edge(S, A)

    if include_expedite:
        edge(C, X)
        edge(E, X)
        edge(X, A)

    return po

balanced_branches = []
for has_supplier in (False, True):
    gates = (None,) if not has_supplier else (C, E)
    for has_expedite in (False, True):
        for gate in gates:
            balanced_branches.append(
                partial_order(has_supplier, has_expedite, gate)
            )

model_balanced = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=balanced_branches,
)
```

The exact decisions are:

- `R`, `A`, `P`, and `H` are sequential boundary/join activities. `R` precedes the work; `A` is the join; `P` and `H` are a strict suffix.
- `C` and `E` are represented as an unordered pair in a strict partial order. This admits either completion order and is the smallest structural commitment consistent with V1, V2, S1, and S2 after discounting S1 as a universal rule. It means observable completion order is exchangeable; it does not prove simultaneous execution.
- If present, `S` has a partial-order edge from either `C` or `E`, selected by an XOR branch, and an edge to `A`. It has no edge from the other principal check, so `R C S E A` is admitted. The other check still must precede `A`.
- If present, `X` has edges from both `C` and `E` and an edge to `A`. There is no `S < X` or `X < S` edge, exactly reflecting V6, V7, and S6.
- `S` and `X` are each absent/present alternatives. The missing-data interpretation is disclosed rather than silently converted into a claim that supplier work is business-optional.

Behavior deliberately excluded:

- `S` before both principal checks; this would violate the chosen reading of D5 that at least one check must have produced sufficient information before supplier confirmation.
- `X` before completion of either `C` or `E`, `X` after `A`, or more than one `X`.
- `A` before completion of either principal check, or `S` after `A` when an `S` event is recorded.
- Any trace that does not begin with `R`, end with `H`, or preserve `A < P < H`.
- Retries and duplicate visible labels, because they are outside the shared abstraction.

Known underfitting risks:

- The model may still be too strict about D5: supplier work could be triggered directly from order data, which would permit `S` before both check completions.
- Treating `C` and `E` as unordered can underfit a true policy that chooses one fixed sequence for a subset of orders, especially if the log's ingestion order is not a completion order.
- The `S`-absent branch may hide both missing integration events and legitimate stocked cases, so it cannot support an audit conclusion about supplier compliance.
- The model keeps D3 and D6 as hard constraints in the language even though they are not absolute, so it will reject exceptional approvals or expedite actions unless those are separately modeled.
- The disjunctive supplier gate is a compact uncertainty representation, not a probability model; without attributes it cannot learn which gate or route is appropriate for an individual case.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

The permissive candidate is an explicit upper-bound sensitivity model. It retains D1, D2, D3, and D6 as guardrails, but it relaxes the low-to-medium-confidence timing interpretation of D5. It allows supplier confirmation to be triggered after registration alone, as well as after either principal check. This is reasonable if procurement can work from order information or if the phrase “after either check” is permissive rather than a minimum prerequisite; it is intentionally not the recommended compliance interpretation.

Let `B(s,x,g)` use the balanced constraints, except that for `g=R` the supplier constraints are, precisely, `R<S` and `S<A`, with no edge from `C` or `E` to `S`. The formal expression is

```text
M_permissive =
  ⊕ B(0, x, -)                         for x in {0,1}
  ⊕ B(1, x, g)                         for x in {0,1}, g in {R,C,E}
```

The `g=R` branch is the only behavior that is not in the balanced candidate. It permits, for example, `R S C E A P H`. The boundary remains strict: the candidate does not permit `A` before both principal checks, `X` before both principal checks, `S` after approval, or any violation of the start/end and release rules.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

R = "Register Order"
C = "Credit Check"
E = "Engineering Review"
S = "Supplier Confirmation"
X = "Expedite Review"
A = "Approve Configuration"
P = "Release Production"
H = "Archive Case"

def partial_order(include_supplier=False, include_expedite=False,
                  supplier_gate=None):
    labels = [R, C, E]
    if include_supplier:
        labels.append(S)
    if include_expedite:
        labels.append(X)
    labels.extend([A, P, H])

    transitions = {label: Transition(label=label) for label in labels}
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=set(transitions.values()))

    def edge(before, after):
        po.order.add_edge(transitions[before], transitions[after])

    edge(R, C)
    edge(R, E)
    edge(C, A)
    edge(E, A)
    edge(A, P)
    edge(P, H)

    if include_supplier:
        edge(R, S)
        if supplier_gate in (C, E):
            edge(supplier_gate, S)
        # supplier_gate == R intentionally has no additional C/E edge
        edge(S, A)

    if include_expedite:
        edge(C, X)
        edge(E, X)
        edge(X, A)

    return po

permissive_branches = []
for has_supplier in (False, True):
    gates = (None,) if not has_supplier else (R, C, E)
    for has_expedite in (False, True):
        for gate in gates:
            permissive_branches.append(
                partial_order(has_supplier, has_expedite, gate)
            )

model_permissive = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=permissive_branches,
)
```

Behavior deliberately excluded:

- Approval after only one principal check, because D3 remains a medium-to-high guardrail in this upper-bound model.
- Expedite before both checks or after approval, because D6 and S6 remain constraints.
- Supplier confirmation after approval, after release, or after archive.
- A case not beginning with `R`, not ending with `H`, or with `P` before `A`.
- More than one `X`, duplicate check attempts, or arbitrary hidden activities.

Known underfitting risks:

- Even this model may underfit real retry/rework behavior and exceptional approvals.
- The early-`S` branch may be too permissive if D5 is in fact a mandatory minimum prerequisite.
- It may mask a data integration defect by accepting an early supplier event without requiring an explicit trigger or external-component flag.
- It does not distinguish an `S` event that was genuinely absent from one that was present but lost, and therefore is unsuitable as a direct compliance verdict.
- Its partial orders still describe completion-order language; they do not establish actual parallel execution or resource independence.

# 6. Unobserved Behavior Audit

The conservative model has no unobserved complete trace under the stated bound of at most one occurrence of each visible activity: its language is exactly the seven observed distinct variants. The balanced and permissive models admit additional linear extensions and optional combinations.

| Model | Unobserved trace | Why admitted | Plausible, risky, or clearly undesirable | Evidence used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | None within the bound `<= 1` occurrence per visible label | The tree is an XOR enumeration of exactly the seven observed variants. | Not applicable; this is deliberate overfitting. | V1–V7. |
| Balanced | `R E S C A P H` | `C` and `E` are unordered; the supplier branch uses `E < S`; both checks and `S` precede `A`. | Plausible, but not demonstrated. | V2, V5, D3, D5, S2. |
| Balanced | `R C S E X A P H` | The supplier branch uses `C < S`; `X` waits for both checks; no edge orders `S` and `X`. | Plausible and useful for testing the `S`/`X` ordering hypothesis. | V5, V6, V7, D6, S6. |
| Balanced | `R E C S X A P H` | Both checks precede `X`; `S` can follow either check; `S` and `X` are unordered. | Plausible, but its frequency is unknown. | V2, V6, V7, D5, D6, S6. |
| Permissive | `R S C E A P H` | The exploratory early-supplier branch requires only `R < S`; `C` and `E` still precede `A`. | Risky; reasonable only if procurement can start from order data. | D5 interpreted as permissive, S3–S5, D3. |
| Permissive | `R S E C A P H` | Same early-supplier branch plus the alternative `E`-then-`C` completion order. | Risky and maximally informative for testing the early-trigger hypothesis. | S2, S3–S5, D5, D7. |
| Permissive | `R S C E X A P H` | Early `S`; both checks then enable optional `X`; all precede approval. | Risky but consistent with the permissive sensitivity boundary. | D3, D6, S6, relaxed D5. |

The following traces are intentionally excluded. The earliest violation is the first point at which the model cannot explain the next completion under its stated constraints.

| Model | Excluded trace | Earliest violated constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Conservative | `R E S C A P H` | After `R E`, the only matching conservative branches require `C`, not `S`; the third completion diverges. |
| Conservative | `R C E X A P H` | After `R C E`, the conservative branches require `S` or `A`; `X` at position 4 is unsupported. |
| Balanced | `R C A P H` | `A` occurs before `E`, violating `E < A`. |
| Balanced | `R C E A S P H` | `S` occurs after `A`, violating `S < A` whenever `S` is recorded. |
| Balanced | `R X C E A P H` | `X` occurs before `C` and `E`, violating `C < X` and `E < X`. |
| Permissive | `R S C A P H` | `A` occurs before `E`, violating the retained D3 guardrail. |
| Permissive | `R X C E A P H` | `X` occurs before both principal checks, violating retained D6/S6. |
| Permissive | `R C E A S P H` | `S` occurs after approval, violating D4. |

# 7. Comparative Model Matrix

| Criterion | Conservative | Balanced | Permissive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expected fitness on the 30 observed cases | Very high, effectively complete for the seven variants | Very high, because every observed variant satisfies the partial-order constraints | Very high, because it contains the balanced language and the observed variants |
| Expected precision risk | Lowest: little unobserved behavior is admitted | Medium: admits unseen interleavings and optional combinations | Highest: additionally admits early supplier completion and therefore more behavior |
| Generalization potential | Very low | Medium-to-high | High, but partly speculative |
| Structural simplicity | Low-to-medium: short branches but seven explicit XOR alternatives and poor scalability | Medium: six partial-order alternatives make uncertainty explicit | Medium-to-low: eight alternatives and one low-confidence relaxation |
| Explainability | High for “these exact traces occurred”; low for explaining unseen valid behavior | High: each edge and XOR has a domain interpretation | Medium: the early-`S` branch is easy to explain but hard to defend operationally |
| Robustness to missing `S` events | Low: it treats recorded variants as exhaustive and cannot identify missingness | Medium-to-high: it accepts absence but discloses that absence conflates stocked and missing | High as a language envelope, but it can hide defects more readily |
| Handling of unseen interleavings | Poor | Good for `C`/`E`, `S`, and `X` within the stated prerequisites | Very good, including early supplier interleavings |
| Suitability for compliance versus exploration | Strong as a conservative audit baseline; weak as a production model | Suitable for immediate operational modeling after documenting the uncertainty | Best for exploration/sensitivity analysis; unsafe as a compliance gate |

# 8. Validation Code

The following script reconstructs the log, rebuilds all three candidates, converts them to accepting Petri nets, runs the available PM4Py checks, performs bounded playout, and tests positive/negative/boundary traces. It follows the `parse_event_log_string(list_of_trace_strings)` interface specified in the task. PM4Py releases differ in the exact return type and optional parameters of some metrics, so the script prints an explicit “unavailable” result rather than treating an API mismatch as a modeling result.

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition

R = "Register Order"
C = "Credit Check"
E = "Engineering Review"
S = "Supplier Confirmation"
X = "Expedite Review"
A = "Approve Configuration"
P = "Release Production"
H = "Archive Case"

# 1. Reconstruct the 30-case event log.
variant_specs = [
    (12, [R, C, E, S, A, P, H]),
    (7,  [R, E, C, S, A, P, H]),
    (4,  [R, C, E, A, P, H]),
    (3,  [R, E, C, A, P, H]),
    (2,  [R, C, S, E, A, P, H]),
    (1,  [R, C, E, S, X, A, P, H]),
    (1,  [R, E, C, X, S, A, P, H]),
]
trace_strings = [
    ",".join(labels)
    for count, labels in variant_specs
    for _ in range(count)
]
log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(trace_strings)

# 2. Candidate constructors.
def activity(label):
    return ProcessTree(label=label)

def sequence(*labels):
    node = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE)
    node.children = [activity(label) for label in labels]
    return node

def tree_xor(*trees):
    node = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR)
    node.children = list(trees)
    return node

model_conservative = tree_xor(
    sequence(R, C, E, S, A, P, H),
    sequence(R, E, C, S, A, P, H),
    sequence(R, C, E, A, P, H),
    sequence(R, E, C, A, P, H),
    sequence(R, C, S, E, A, P, H),
    sequence(R, C, E, S, X, A, P, H),
    sequence(R, E, C, X, S, A, P, H),
)

def partial_order(include_supplier=False, include_expedite=False,
                  supplier_gate=None):
    labels = [R, C, E]
    if include_supplier:
        labels.append(S)
    if include_expedite:
        labels.append(X)
    labels.extend([A, P, H])
    transitions = {label: Transition(label=label) for label in labels}
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=set(transitions.values()))

    def edge(before, after):
        po.order.add_edge(transitions[before], transitions[after])

    edge(R, C)
    edge(R, E)
    edge(C, A)
    edge(E, A)
    edge(A, P)
    edge(P, H)

    if include_supplier:
        edge(R, S)
        if supplier_gate in (C, E):
            edge(supplier_gate, S)
        edge(S, A)

    if include_expedite:
        edge(C, X)
        edge(E, X)
        edge(X, A)
    return po

balanced_branches = []
for has_supplier in (False, True):
    gates = (None,) if not has_supplier else (C, E)
    for has_expedite in (False, True):
        for gate in gates:
            balanced_branches.append(
                partial_order(has_supplier, has_expedite, gate)
            )
model_balanced = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR, children=balanced_branches
)

permissive_branches = []
for has_supplier in (False, True):
    gates = (None,) if not has_supplier else (R, C, E)
    for has_expedite in (False, True):
        for gate in gates:
            permissive_branches.append(
                partial_order(has_supplier, has_expedite, gate)
            )
model_permissive = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR, children=permissive_branches
)

models = {
    "conservative": model_conservative,
    "balanced": model_balanced,
    "permissive": model_permissive,
}

# 3. Convert to accepting Petri nets and run checks/metrics.
converted = {}
for name, model in models.items():
    net, initial_marking, final_marking = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    converted[name] = (net, initial_marking, final_marking)

    try:
        sound = pm4py.check_soundness(
            net, initial_marking, final_marking, print_diagnostics=True
        )
    except Exception as exc:
        sound = "unavailable: " + repr(exc)
    print(name, "soundness:", sound)

    metric_calls = {
        "fitness": lambda: pm4py.fitness_alignments(
            log, net, initial_marking, final_marking
        ),
        "precision": lambda: pm4py.precision_alignments(
            log, net, initial_marking, final_marking
        ),
        "generalization": lambda: pm4py.generalization_tbr(
            log, net, initial_marking, final_marking
        ),
        "simplicity": lambda: pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(
            net, initial_marking, final_marking
        ),
    }
    for metric_name, call in metric_calls.items():
        try:
            print(name, metric_name + ":", call())
        except Exception as exc:
            print(name, metric_name + ": unavailable:", repr(exc))

    # Bounded playout: this is a sample capped at 100 traces, not an
    # exhaustive language comparison.
    try:
        sample = pm4py.play_out(
            net, initial_marking, final_marking,
            parameters={"no_traces": 100},
        )
        print(name, "playout sample size:", len(sample))
    except Exception as exc:
        print(name, "playout unavailable:", repr(exc))

# 4. Manually defined conformance suite.
positive_traces = [
    [R, C, E, S, A, P, H],       # observed
    [R, E, C, A, P, H],          # observed
    [R, C, S, E, A, P, H],       # observed
    [R, E, S, C, A, P, H],       # plausible balanced generalization
    [R, C, E, S, X, A, P, H],    # observed

def one_trace_log(labels):

def trace_fitness(net, initial_marking, final_marking, labels):
```

The log has a finite observed sample, but a playout sample is not an exhaustive proof of the model language. Precision and generalization are also estimator/API-dependent metrics, not proofs that one causal interpretation is true. If a local PM4Py release uses a different parser or POWL conversion signature, the model definitions remain the specification and the compatibility adjustment should be limited to that API boundary.

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

Use `model_balanced` for immediate operational modeling, with its `S`-absence and unordered-check assumptions shown explicitly in the model documentation. It fits all observed variants while avoiding the unsupported claim that one of `C` or `E` must always precede the other. Use `model_conservative` as the compliance/sensitivity baseline: any case outside its exact observed language is a flag for review, not automatic proof of noncompliance. Retain `model_permissive` for exploratory stress testing and threshold analysis, not for approval or compliance decisions.

Collect the following on every future case:

- A case identifier, order/component identifier, and an explicit `external_component`, `stocked_component`, and `supplier_confirmation_required` decision, including the rule or user that produced it.
- For `C`, `E`, `S`, `X`, and `A`, separate start/request, completion, and cancellation timestamps; source-system event time; ingestion time; and a synchronized clock or sequence identifier. Same-timestamp ingestion order must remain a separate field.
- Trigger and dependency fields: which preceding event released each activity, whether the activity was queued or running concurrently, and resource/work-queue identifiers.
- Supplier event provenance, retry/attempt number, integration delivery status, and an explicit “event missing by export” indicator. This separates true absence from lost telemetry.
- Expedite reason, authorization, and whether the review was initiated before or after supplier work at the lifecycle level; approval exceptions and waived checks should be logged as decisions rather than inferred from traces.

The most informative future cases are deliberately selected to distinguish the competing hypotheses:

- To separate a fixed sequence from concurrency, collect repeated cases with both `C`-then-`E` and `E`-then-`C` completion orders while recording start intervals. Non-overlapping starts with stable route metadata support XOR/sequential alternatives; overlapping intervals support a parallel or overlapping interpretation.
- To test the supplier gate, obtain cases with `R C S E A P H` and `R E S C A P H`, each labeled with the external/stocked attribute. The first is already observed but should be repeated; the second distinguishes the balanced `E < S` branch from a strict “wait for both” model.
- To test the permissive early-supplier branch, look for `R S C E A P H` and `R S E C A P H` with lifecycle timestamps showing whether `S` was actually triggered after registration alone.
- To distinguish `S`/`X` ordering, collect both `R C E S X A P H` and `R C E X S A P H`, with complete timestamps. The observed pair already gives one example of each relative order, but repeated cases and trigger fields are needed to show whether the order is unconstrained or merely an incidental route split.
- To validate optionality, compare the same component class under recorded `supplier_confirmation_required=true/false` and audit the export for missing supplier events. This directly tests whether the seven no-`S` traces are stocked cases, missing events, or both.
- To test D3 and D6 rather than merely assume them, capture any exception in which approval or expedite occurs before a principal check and record the waiver or failure reason. Such traces should be rare and explicitly labeled if they are to be admitted in a future model.

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

The confidence levels below concern the modeling decisions, not just the fact that a trace was observed.

- `R` as the visible start and `H` as the visible end: high confidence, approximately 0.95 or higher, based on all 30 cases and D1.
- `A < P < H` and `P` requiring `A`: high confidence, approximately 0.95 or higher, based on all 30 cases and D2.
- Both `C` and `E` before `A`: medium-to-high confidence, roughly 0.75–0.90. It is directly supported by every observed case and D3, but D3 is not stated as absolute.
- No universal `C < E` or `E < C` sequence: medium confidence, roughly 0.60–0.75. Both orders are observed and S2 supports parallelism, but S1 is a credible conflicting stakeholder statement and D7 prevents resolution.
- A balanced unordered/partial-order representation for `C` and `E`: medium confidence, roughly 0.55–0.70 as a useful abstraction, with low confidence that it proves physical concurrency.
- Conditional or missing `S`: medium confidence, roughly 0.60–0.75. S3, S4, and S5 jointly explain why `S` cannot be treated as unconditionally mandatory, but the external/stocked field is absent and the missing-event rate is only approximate.
- At least one principal check before `S`: low-to-medium confidence, roughly 0.50–0.65. It follows the natural reading of D5 and is preserved by the balanced model, but start times are unavailable and supplier triggering may use order information.
- Optional, at-most-once `X` after both checks and before approval: medium confidence, roughly 0.65–0.80, supported by D6, S6, and the two observed cases, although the sample is small.
- The permissive early-`S` branch: low confidence, roughly 0.25–0.40. It is included to make the alternative hypothesis explicit, not because the observed traces establish it.
- The exact conservative language as the true future language: low confidence despite perfect sample fitness. Its confidence is high only as a lower-bound description of what has definitely been seen.

A materially different interpretation is that S1 is a universally enforced policy and the `E`-then-`C` records reflect an export/ingestion artifact or a population that is outside the finance rule. Under that interpretation, a compliance model should use `C < E` (or a conditional sequence) and treat the reverse traces as data-quality exceptions. Conversely, if lifecycle timestamps confirm overlapping principal checks and the external/stocked attribute explains the no-`S` cases, the balanced model becomes substantially stronger and the conservative model is clearly underfitting. The current completion-only evidence cannot choose between these interpretations.
