# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions
## Time origin and horizon
- **Time origin (t=0)**: The moment a case is `Submit`.
- **Observation horizon**: 72 hours, which is the Service Level Agreement (SLA) deadline.
- **Event(s)**: Achieving any terminal status (`Resolve`, `Reject`, `Withdraw`, or being `Transferred`).  
- **Censoring**: All cases that have not reached any terminal status by the observation cutoff (i.e., still `open`) are rightcensored at the last recorded age (`duration_or_age_h`).

## Contribution of example cases
| Case | Observed status | Duration (h) | Contribution under each estimand |
|------|-----------------|--------------|----------------------------------|
| **S03** (resolved at 76h) | Resolved | 76 | *Not* observed within SLA  contributes to breach probability. |
| **S09** (open at 92h) | Open | 92 (age at cutoff) | Still unclosed  censored; contributes to the risk set until 72h. |
| **S06** (withdrawn at 27h) | Withdrawn | 27 | Within SLA  counts as **ontime** but **not resolution**; influences cumulative incidence of resolution, but withdrawal is a competing outcome. |
| **S08** (transferred at 68h) | Transferred | 68 | Within SLA  counts as **ontime** but **not resolution**; treatment of transfer varies (see §2). |

### Two useful estimands
1. **Prob(p) = P(T > 72)** – *Probability of **not** being resolved (or otherwise terminated) by 72h.*  
   - Captures overall SLA tail risk across all terminal outcomes.  
   - Operational decision: triggers an **alert** if p exceeds a risk threshold, prompting proactive case routing.

2. **Cumulative incidence of **resolution** after 72h, CI_R(t)** – *P(case is resolved after the deadline | event is resolution).*  
   - Allows separate monitoring of **late resolutions** (e.g., “latefix alerts”).  
   - Decision: distinguishes whether the delay stems from true backlog (rework, escalation) vs. unavoidable transfers.

Both estimands treat **censoring** (open cases) as rightcensoring and **competitors** (`Withdraw`, `Reject`, `Transferred`) as competing events.

# 2. Event and censoring encoding
| Observed status | Event indicator(s) | Competingrisk code | Censoring rule | Transfer treatment options |
|-----------------|--------------------|---------------------|----------------|----------------------------|
| **Resolved** | `event_res = 1` | `competing = 0` (none) | — | N/A |
| **Rejected** | `event_rej = 1` | `competing = 0` | — | N/A |
| **Withdrawn** | `event_with = 1` | `competing = 1` (withdrawal) | — | N/A |
| **Transferred** | `event_trans = 1` | `competing = 2` (transfer) | — | **Option A** – treat as competing event (no further internal monitoring).<br>**Option B** – treat as right censor at 72h (ignore external outcome).<br>**Option C** – treat as informative censoring: include a covariate indicating “transfer flag” and possibly weight by known external throughput (if available). |
| **Open** (72h) | — | — | Rightcensor at `duration_or_age_h` (72h) | — |

*Note*: `last_event_gap_h` is always 0 for terminal rows; for open rows it records the time since the last internal activity up to the cutoff, useful for attrition modeling.

# 3. Why naive completedcase analysis is biased
A **naïve analysis** that only includes cases already resolved (or terminated) omits all open cases, inducing **survivor bias** and **informative censoring**:

- **Case S09** (open, 92h) is censored at 72h. If we exclude it, we ignore the fact that it was still processing at the deadline, potentially underestimating breach probability.
- **Competing risks**: Withdrawing or rejecting early removes cases from the resolution pathway. Excluding open cases also discards the risk of future withdrawal, leading to biased estimates of “resolutiononly” survival.
- **Cohort imbalance**: Cohort **B** (new policy) contains many transfers (e.g., S21, S32) and open cases with large `last_event_gap_h` (e.g., S26). Ignoring these yields distorted cohortspecific SLA performance.
- **Immortaltime bias**: Activities like `Escalate` or `Rework` appear after the submit time. Treating them as baseline covariates without timevarying modeling would allocate “immortal time” (time where the case cannot have the event yet) incorrectly, inflating survival probabilities.
- **Transfer informativeness**: Cases transferred early (e.g., S08 at 68h) may have lower internal latency but higher external handling time, making censoring at 72h partially informative unless recoded appropriately.

Thus, a completedcase only analysis would **overstate ontime compliance** for lowrisk paths and **understate tail risk** for highrisk paths.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan
| Method | Suitability for this data |
|--------|---------------------------|
| **Kaplan–Meier (KM)** | Provides overall survival (p(T>72)) but cannot separate competing outcomes; inappropriate for resolutionspecific risk when withdrawals/transfers coexist. |
| **AalenJohansen / CumulativeIncidence Functions (CIF)** | Naturally handles competing risks (withdrawal, reject, transfer) and yields causespecific CIFs for each terminal status, ideal for the two estimands. |
| **Causespecific Cox hazards** | Requires proportional hazards; competing events can bias estimates if not properly stratified. |
| **Subdistribution hazards (FineGray)** | Handles competing risks for a chosen *hazard of interest* but does not give the full CIF picture; useful for a single “resolution” marginal risk. |
| **Restricted Mean Time (RMT)** | Gives average time to event up to 72h, simpler to interpret for SLA compliance metrics, but less flexible for competing outcomes. |
| **Landmarking** | Allows inclusion of later timevarying covariates (e.g., Rework after 24h) while avoiding immortaltime bias. |
| **Multistate model** | Explicitly models transitions: **Submit  Review  (Escalate)  (Rework)  Resolve / Reject / Withdraw / Transfer**; captures timing of each activity and allows separate transition hazards, perfect for processaware analysis. |

**Recommendation**  
- **Primary approach**: **AalenJohansen CIFs** stratified by cohort and priority, with separate CIFs for `Resolve`, `Reject`, `Withdraw`, and `Transfer`. This directly answers both defined estimands and accommodates competing risks.  
- **Secondary approach**: **Processaware multistate model** fitted via PM4Py (or a similar statetransition framework). It incorporates the prefixderived stages (e.g., presence of `Rework`, timing of `Escalate`) as hazard covariates, providing richer insight while respecting the observed ordering.

KM could be used as a quick baseline but would be insufficient for causalstyle interpretation.

# 5. Processaware features and stage hazards
### Derived features from `prefix`
| Feature | Type | Rationale |
|---------|------|-----------|
| **Stage** | Baseline (categorical) | First activity after `Submit` (`Screen`, `AutoCheck` for cohortB). Indicates frontend routing. |
| **HasEscalate** | Timevarying binary (set after `Submit`) | `Escalate` appears  hazard for later resolution may increase (higher priority). |
| **HasRework** | Timevarying binary (set after `Submit`) | Presence of `Rework` may lengthen time to resolution; flagged when `Rework` appears. |
| **ReviewCount** | Count (timevarying) | Number of `Review` transitions; more reviews  higher latency risk. |
| **TransferFlag** | Binary (set at `Transfer`) | Indicates external handoff; treated as a causespecific event. |
| **InactivityGap** | Numeric (last_event_gap_h) | Time since last internal activity; large gaps may signal processing delay. |

### Potential leakage & immortaltime
- **Baseline covariates** (e.g., `HasEscalate`) are safe because they are set at `Submit`.  
- **Timevarying covariates** must be introduced **after** the relevant event time; otherwise immortal time appears. E.g., `HasRework` should become `1` at the time the `Rework` activity occurs, not at `Submit`.  
- **Cohort indicator** can be a baseline covariate; ensure any timedependent policy change (e.g., automated screening) is captured as a **timeoffset** variable rather than a static flag.

# 6. Sensitivity analysis
| Scenario | How transfers are treated | Expected impact on breach probability |
|----------|---------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **A – Competing event** | `Transferred` counted as a competing terminal outcome (no further internal monitoring). | **Reduces** apparent breach risk (fewer unresolved cases), likely **overoptimistic** SLA compliance. |
| **B – Right censor at 72h** | Cases transferred before 72h are censored at 72h (ignore external outcome). | **Inflates** breach risk for cohortB, as external handling time may be longer but is omitted. |
| **C – Informative censoring (weighting)** | Include a covariate `transfer_flag` and weight by a proxy external throughput (e.g., average external resolution time = 48h). | Balances between A and B; may **shift** the breach probability modestly higher for B, reflecting uncertainty about external performance. |

Other sensitivity checks:
- **Excluding highgap open cases (e.g., S26 with gap=72h)** to test if long internal inactivity drives censoring.  
- **Stratifying by priority** (`P1`, `P2`, `P3`) to see if SLA pressure differs.  
- **Policycompliance flag** (e.g., `B` cohort postpolicy) to assess rollout effect.

Qualitative conclusions could reverse if transfers are assumed informative (ScenarioC) versus purely competing (ScenarioA); the **identified tail risk** for cohortB would rise under C.

# 7. Operational interpretation
- **Alert thresholds**: Set an SLAbreach alert when the **probability of not being resolved by 72h (p(T>72))** exceeds a corporate tolerance (e.g., 15%).  
- **Workload actions**: For cases with **high ReworkCount** or **long InactivityGap**, trigger **priority escalation** or **automation** of next steps.  
- **Governance dashboards**: Display **causespecific CIFs** for each terminal status (Resolve, Reject, Withdraw, Transfer) sidebyside, allowing managers to see where delays originate.  

**Calibration & fairness**  
- Stratify the survival curves by **priority** and **cohort** to ensure that highpriority cases (P1) meet tighter SLA targets and that cohortB does not experience systematic over or underpenalisation.  
- Use **pseudoobservations** or **crossvalidated Cindex** to verify that the model predicts ordering consistently across subpopulations.

# 9. Limitations and additional data
- **Missing timestamps** for intermediate activities (e.g., exact time of `Rework`). Without them, hazard rates rely on coarsened timing (e.g., “has Rework?”) which could miss subtle delay patterns.  
- **External outcome after Transfer**: We lack the true resolution time posttransfer, making the treatment of `transfer` highly uncertain (see §6).  
- **Cutoff date & case start times**: The observation cutoff is given as a fixed age at observation, but the absolute date is unknown; this limits exact “timesincesubmit” calculations if cases span multiple days.  
- **Policy rollout compliance**: The nonrandom rollout of the new screening policy for cohortB introduces potential unmeasured confounding; a richer dataset would record when each case entered the altered workflow.  
- **Workload dynamics**: Variations in staff availability or casevolume spikes are not captured, which could bias hazard estimates.  
- **Additional covariates**: Prior case backlog, geographic region, or customertype features could improve the model but are absent.  

Collecting **eventlevel timestamps for each activity**, **external resolution outcomes for transferred cases**, and **policyassignment timestamps** would substantially strengthen causal inference and reduce uncertainty in the SLAtail risk estimates.
