## 1. Executive causal assessment
**Provisional verdict: `mainly confounded` — Confidence: 70/100.**

The crude 6.7h gap — Orion mean 21.96h vs Nova 15.24h, median 18.15h vs 10.60h — reverses within every complexity stratum: Low Orion 7.50h vs Nova 9.64h (-2.14h), Medium 17.17h vs 19.38h (-2.21h), High 31.57h vs 35.00h (-3.43h). The same reversal holds for mean wait (Orion lower in each stratum) and rework rate. The strongest evidence for confounding is case-mix imbalance: Orion is 42.9% High vs Nova 12.5% High, 64.3% compliance-required vs 25.0%, 92.9% night vs 25.0% night, High arrival load. Routing policy explicitly selects difficult cases to Orion, so total-effect comparison without adjustment confounds resource-group label with complexity, compliance burden, and night/load operating conditions.

Evidence against pure confounding is that stratification does not identify causation: High-Nova n=2 and Low-Orion n=2 give poor overlap/positivity, within-stratum residual selection on unobserved risk likely remains, and only completed cases are observed. Review service time and queue wait cannot be cleanly separated, and 30-day appeal is too sparse (1-1) to judge quality.

Blindly moving 50% of Orion work to Nova may fail because Nova would inherit complex, compliance-heavy night work it is not staffed for, increasing queueing and disrupting learning. It may succeed only if Nova has spare day capacity for Low cases. It may backfire by overloading Nova queues, raising rework/appeals, and destroying the informative routing signal without testing mechanisms.

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| 2 | Queueing / compliance capacity intervention, not label change | queue_wait and compliance_performed bottleneck | shift-slot / compliance desk (add day compliance reviewer, night triage buffer) | No change to group labels; roster limits, handoff protocol; monitor both groups | Before-after wait_before_review_h, compliance queue time, directly-follows Classify->Compliance->Review frequency and sojourn, resource utilization | Front-door test of H4: if wait falls equally for Orion and Nova, effect is capacity-mediated, not label-caused; informs staffing ROI | Stop if compliance wait not -15% in 4 weeks or service time rises (speed-quality tradeoff); revert roster |

**Final recommendation (158 words):** Do not execute the blind 50% reassignment. The crude Orion penalty is an aggregation artefact of routing difficult, compliance-heavy, night high-load work to Orion, with a full reversal favouring Orion within each complexity level on cycle, wait and rework. Moving half of that load to Nova would transfer bottleneck work to day capacity without evidence Nova is faster on comparable work, risking longer Nova queues, new rework and appeal risk, while destroying the signal that currently matches skill to difficulty. Instead freeze the proposal, complete Phase 0 instrumentation to expose censoring and queue states, run a narrow randomized pilot only where overlap exists — Low, day, no-compliance — to estimate a credible direct effect, and in parallel expand compliance and night-queue capacity which the variant and wait decomposition implicate as the true driver. Scale only if the pilot shows Nova parity without queue inflation and capacity expansion fails to close the residual gap.

## 8. Residual uncertainty
1. Within-complexity unobserved case risk beyond Low/Medium/High label — most valuable: prospective structured difficulty/risk score recorded at Classify before routing plus reviewer ID to enable matching and sensitivity analysis.
2. Queueing vs service vs compliance time conflation in wait_before_review_h — most valuable: event timestamps for every activity start/complete plus queue-entry and compliance start/end to reconstruct queue-state and true sojourns.
3. Selection from completed-only sampling with unknown open/abandoned tail — most valuable: full arrival cohort log including still-open, abandoned and censored times with arrival dates to estimate selection-corrected effects.
4. Shift/load capacity mechanism and reviewer skill bundle — most valuable: roster and staffing levels per shift plus reviewer certification/skill and workload at assignment to separate label effect from capacity effect.
5. Quality and rework mechanism under speed pressure — most valuable: granular rework reason, Request Info content, and risk-adjusted 30-day appeal/readmission with post-process controls plus randomized pilot data to test speed-quality tradeoffs.
