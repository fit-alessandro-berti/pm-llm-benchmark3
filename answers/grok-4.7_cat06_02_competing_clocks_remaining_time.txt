## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

Hours below are remaining time from Friday 18 Sep 2026, 15:30. Monday 21 Sep 08:00 is 64.5 h later. Tuesday 22 Sep 08:00 is 88.5 h later. The parallel join is always the later of the two usable branch results. Adding chemistry time to microbiology time would count the same weekend twice.

### Smooth path

Chemistry waiting and service. Both analysts are busy at 15:30. One frees about 15:54–16:24 and has about 1.6–2.1 h before 18:00. The other frees about 16:42–17:30 and has about 0.5–1.3 h. Under risk-priority, the first analyst takes the urgent sample. B-417 can be started by the second analyst, but that window is shorter than the 1.5 h minimum service, so either a fragment of about 0.5–1.3 h is done and paused, or the bench starts B-417 fresh on Monday. On Monday the second analyst can start at 08:00 while the first finishes the urgent sample. Service of 1.5 / 2.5 / 4.5 h then ends about Monday 09:30 / 10:30 / 12:30, or about an hour earlier if a Friday fragment was banked. Under first-in-first-out, Friday’s 2.1–3.4 new analyst-hours only start one or two of the four samples ahead. Monday still has on the order of 7–11 analyst-hours ahead of B-417. Two analysts put the start near Monday 11:30–15:30. Typical service then finishes Monday mid-to-late afternoon. A start after about 15:00 combined with a draw near 4.5 h pauses at 18:00 and finishes Tuesday 08:30–11:00.

Microbiology incubation and readout. The plate is technically ready Sunday 12:00–20:00. With authorization (0.25–0.45), readout finishes Sunday about 14:00–23:00. Without it, a microbiologist reads it Monday 08:30–09:30. There is no confirmation incubation on this path.

Parallel join. Usable microbiology is Sunday night or Monday about 09:00. Usable chemistry is Monday late morning under priority and Monday afternoon, occasionally Tuesday morning, under first-in-first-out. The join is that maximum. Most of the elapsed time is the incubation sitting through the weekend and the staffing delay onto Monday; chemistry decides whether the join is Monday morning, Monday afternoon, or Tuesday morning. Saturday QA work (09:00–13:00) and Friday evening QA (until 20:00) only reduce other batches’ backlog. They cannot start B-417.

QA waiting and service. A rough residual at Monday open is current work plus arrivals minus 8.5 pre-Monday reviewer-hours. With mean service near 2–2.5 h, five current batches are about 10–12 reviewer-hours and expected arrivals during those 8.5 open hours add several more, against 8.5 of capacity. Two Monday reviewers then take something like 1–6 h to reach a batch that joins Monday morning or afternoon; overdispersion allows a near-empty queue or an 8–16 h wait. B-417’s own review then adds a median 1.5 h and an 80th-percentile-like 3.5 h. A review that starts after about 16:30 often stops at 20:00 and resumes Tuesday 08:00.

Final step. Electronic release adds 0.25–0.75 h at any time of day after acceptance. Earliest alignment (priority, short service, Sunday or 08:30 Monday microbiology, empty QA queue, short review) is Monday about 10:30–13:00. The central smooth cases accept Monday late afternoon through Tuesday late morning and release immediately after that. FIFO plus a long review plus a thick QA queue reaches Tuesday afternoon or Wednesday morning without any scientific rework.

### Chemistry rerun

Primary chemistry follows the same queue and calendar as the smooth path, so a primary result is typically Monday 10:00–17:00. The rerun is priority and lasts about 1–3 h, with mean near 1.9 h, but it must fit entirely before 18:00. A primary result by about 15:00 often yields a Monday rerun finish around 16:00–18:00. A primary result after about 15:30–16:00 often cannot fit the rerun, so the whole rerun starts Tuesday 08:00 and finishes Tuesday about 09:00–11:00. That close rule is a cliff, not a small add-on.

Microbiology on this row is the clean readout, Sunday night or Monday about 09:00. The join is therefore the rerun result: late Monday or Tuesday morning. QA then adds the same 1–6 h typical wait and 1.5–3.5 h review. Monday acceptance is only plausible when the rerun finishes by mid-afternoon and the QA queue is short; otherwise release is Tuesday. If a query or documentation investigation is also opened, which is allowed and more likely when the excursion is confirmed, the terminal time moves to Wednesday–Friday. Those overlaps are why the rerun probability must not be read as a separate 10–20% slice of a partition.

### Delayed microbiology readout or confirmation

The routine delay is already inside the smooth path: a plate ready Sunday 12:00–20:00 waits until Monday 08:30–09:30 unless an on-call microbiologist is dispatched for 1–3 h. When chemistry finishes later on Monday, that staffing delay changes the join by little or nothing.

Confirmation is different. It is seen at the primary readout, then adds 20–30 h of continuous incubation and another 0.5–1.5 h readout. From a Monday 09:00 readout, 20 h is ready Tuesday 05:00 and is read Tuesday 08:30–09:30; 30 h is ready Tuesday 15:00 and is read Tuesday 15:30–16:30. From a Sunday evening on-call readout, the same addition is ready Monday afternoon to Tuesday early morning, and the read is Monday afternoon or Tuesday 08:30–09:30. Chemistry, even with a rerun, is usually finished before that second readout, so this join is a microbiology maximum. QA on Tuesday is often lighter than on Monday, because two reviewers have had a full Monday (12 h each) against arrivals of about 0.35 per hour. Central release is Tuesday afternoon through Wednesday. The same excursion that raises contamination probability also raises rerun and investigation probability, so the Thursday-to-next-Monday tail is a joint outcome, larger than the product of the marginals.

### QA query or investigation tail

Both usable results must exist before QA can open the file, so this clock starts at the join above, not at Friday 15:30. A query adds 4–12 QA working hours before review resumes, and review then needs another median-like 1.5 h. Opened Monday afternoon, 4 working hours consume the rest of Monday and Tuesday morning, with release around Tuesday midday to afternoon. Twelve working hours consume the rest of Monday and most of Tuesday, with release Tuesday evening or Wednesday. Saturday’s 4 h window is relevant only if the query is still open then; it is not available for a query first raised on Monday.

A documentation-only investigation adds 8–20 QA working hours and then a residual review. From a Monday or Tuesday opening, that is a Wednesday–Thursday release in the center, with an early edge on Tuesday evening when the investigation is short and the file stays with the same reviewer. A laboratory investigation adds 3–7 elapsed calendar days, interpreted as already containing extra lab work and any incubation inside it, then QA reviews again. Opened Monday, the early edge is Thursday 24 Sep; 7 calendar days reaches Monday 28 Sep, and a late opening plus re-review reaches Wednesday 30 Sep. Weekend days count on this branch. Two reviewers do not divide investigation duration; they only shorten the queue before it starts and the queue before the final review.

### How the excursion case combines the clocks

If the flag is later confirmed, the smooth-path probability falls and the rerun, confirmation, and investigation clocks become jointly more likely. The terminal time is still one maximum across chemistry (including any unsplittable rerun), microbiology (including any confirmation), and then a sum along the QA path only: queue wait, review, optional query or investigation, residual review, and release or rejection. The laboratory-investigation and rejection outcomes live in that conditional tail. A cleared flag does the opposite: rerun and contamination drop to the low conditionals, investigation drops toward 0.04–0.10, and the forecast concentrates on the smooth-path maximum of Monday microbiology staffing and the chemistry queue.

## 4. Forecast summary

Plausible earliest completion: Monday 21 September 2026, about 10:30–13:00, roughly 67–70 hours from Friday 15:30. That requires priority chemistry with service near 1.5 h, a microbiology result already in hand Sunday night or by 08:30 Monday, a nearly empty QA queue, a short review, immediate electronic release, and no rerun, query, or investigation.

Central / P50-like remaining-time range: about 75–100 hours, from Monday 21 September around 18:00 through Tuesday 22 September around 18:00. The mixture leans toward Tuesday daytime. Clean priority cases can release Monday evening; first-in-first-out chemistry, a Monday QA close at 20:00, and ordinary residual queueing put the middle of the distribution on Tuesday.

P80-like remaining-time range: about 115–150 hours, Wednesday 23 September around midday through Thursday 24 September evening. This is the documentation-query and documentation-investigation band, including a chemistry rerun or a confirmation incubation that does not also become a laboratory investigation.

P90-like and severe-tail range: about 160–290 hours, Friday 25 September through Wednesday 30 September. The driver is a laboratory investigation of 3–7 calendar days, often reached through a confirmed temperature excursion together with a rerun or a contamination indication, plus final QA review. Times beyond Thursday are a real part of the distribution, on the order of 0.05–0.15, not a negligible remainder.

Most plausible terminal event: Electronic Release. Batch Rejected is the secondary terminal event, roughly 0.02–0.08, and it is concentrated inside confirmed contamination and laboratory investigation. Rejection recording itself adds only 0.5–1.0 h after the defect is confirmed; the long wait is the science and the investigation, not the recording step.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. Temperature-excursion resolution. Dependence uncertainty and observation uncertainty. One sensor gap moves rerun probability, contamination probability, and investigation probability in the same direction, so the stacked tail is the main unknown in the shape of the forecast.

2. Investigation path, if one is opened. Path uncertainty. Documentation-only work adds about 8–20 QA working hours. Laboratory work adds 3–7 calendar days. That split dominates the distance between the P80 band and the severe tail. The 60–80% documentation-only share is itself uncertain.

3. Chemistry queue discipline and the true work content ahead of B-417. Queue uncertainty, with service-time uncertainty for the four samples whose durations were not observed. Priority versus first-in-first-out, plus the Friday fragment that may or may not be started, moves the clean join from Monday late morning to Tuesday morning.

4. QA backlog at the future join. Queue uncertainty. Today’s five batches, an arrival rate of 0.35 per open hour with substantial overdispersion, and an unknown service mean around a 1.5 h median can leave anything from a clear desk to more than a day of wait. The 20:00 cutoff then decides whether acceptance is Monday or Tuesday.

5. Contamination indication and weekend authorization. Path uncertainty interacting with calendar uncertainty. A clean plate makes weekend on-call almost irrelevant to the release time whenever chemistry finishes later on Monday. A positive plate makes both the indication and the Sunday-versus-Monday readout the binding maximum, and it raises the chance of investigation because of the excursion dependence.

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

The single most informative observation is resolution of the upstream temperature-excursion flag.

If it is cleared, borderline chemistry probability should be read near 0.05–0.12, contamination near 0.02–0.06, and investigation near 0.04–0.10 plus the separate documentation-query rate. Probability mass concentrates on electronic release from Monday evening through Tuesday afternoon, and the following-week tail becomes a thin remainder.

If it is confirmed, those three probabilities should be read together near 0.25–0.40, 0.12–0.25, and 0.30–0.55. The central mass shifts toward Wednesday–Thursday, and a laboratory investigation or a rejection becomes a routine part of the forecast rather than a remote tail. Because the three events move together, treating them as independent would understate that shift.

Secondary trigger: whether B-417 is actually in chemistry service by Friday 18:00, and whether the urgent sample took the first free analyst. A Friday start under priority pulls the smooth-path chemistry result toward Monday late morning and makes a Monday release plausible. No Friday start, with all four samples still ahead, pushes the join into Monday late afternoon or Tuesday morning and makes a Monday rerun likely to miss the 18:00 cutoff entirely.
