## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The raw-mapped fitness gap isolates conformance loss due to label and schema artifacts. Raw fitness collapses from 0.92-0.93 to 0.49-0.58 in March and stays low, while mapped fitness after merging Eligibility Screen to Validate Eligibility and removing duplicated Open Case falls only from 0.92 to 0.86-0.91. Recovery of 0.35-0.40 points means ~75-80% of the March fitness drop is logging drift, not business non-conformance, which does not imply the business process was unchanged. Mapping assumes semantic equivalence: it forces a syntactic match without testing whether eligibility screening logic, resource assignment or timing changed under the new system, and it hides provenance gaps. Duplicated technical events inflate distinct labels (9 to 11), add spurious Directly-Follows edges like Open Case->Register, distort DFG frequency and variant counts, and deflate fitness if not filtered; they leave performance metrics less biased but bias waiting-time reconstruction across the duplicate. Missing resource identifiers, concentrated in migrated activities, bias resource/organizational analyses, make staffing-driven drift estimates unreliable, and are not missing at random. Post-May patterns cannot be dismissed as logging drift: normalized rates show Assess->Request Info doubling from 12 to 24-29 per 100 and CollectDocs->Assess falling 95 to 89, median cycle rises 5-18h and p90 16-53h while mapped fitness only drops 0.03-0.07, and rework persists after supplier recovery. Primary monitoring population: **L2 Complexity-standard, non-Supplier Z, mapped log with duplicate removal** pooled as reference, with L1 supplier and high-complexity cohorts as contrast panels. Rationale: largest stable cell (baseline ~58-60% fast variant share, ~60% of cases), least affected by supplier-induced rework and seasonal Senior Assessment surge, directly reflects endogenous process capacity and rework institutionalization. It avoids overaggregation (global masks mechanisms) and tiny cells (four-way splits). Standard population's queue is most sensitive to staffing-driven performance drift and priority redistribution (standard queue should deteriorate if priority works), while high-complexity + Z cohorts serve as sentinels for population and external supplier drift. Report both absolute and case-mix-standardized metrics to avoid conditioning bias; use incident weighting to translate segment findings to global KPI.
