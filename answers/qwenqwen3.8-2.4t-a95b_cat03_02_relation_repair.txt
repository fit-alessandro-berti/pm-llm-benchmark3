## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

I treat the log as an incomplete observation, not as a source of hidden ground truth. The repair task is abductive: find explanations that make the largest number of cues coherent while creating the fewest side effects. I use the following rule classes.

**Hard invariants**

- **No invented objects or events.** Repairs may only add relations among objects and events already present.
- **Referential integrity.** Every added E2O relation must reference an existing event and object; every O2O relation must reference existing source and target objects.
- **Directionality of O2O relations.** `DEL-X carries PK-7` is not equivalent to `PK-7 carries DEL-X`; added O2O edges must preserve the intended source/target direction.
- **No silent deletion of observed relations.** I conserve the observed E2O and O2O relations unless there is an explicit contradiction; here there is no compelling reason to delete observed links.

**Strong defaults**

- `Create Order` normally relates to one Order.
- `Add Item` normally relates to one Order and one Item.
- `Pack Item` normally relates to one Item and one Package, but an item-only row is plausible during a scanner outage.
- `Dispatch Package` normally relates to one Package and one Delivery, but bulk dispatch is possible.
- `Issue Invoice` normally relates to one Invoice and at least one Order.
- `Record Payment` normally relates to one Payment and one Invoice, but unapplied cash is possible.
- Lifecycle ordering is expected: order creation before item addition, packing before dispatch, dispatch before delivery confirmation, invoice before payment. Temporal violation is evidence against a repair unless explained.

**Weak heuristics**

- Identifier similarity: `PK8-2` suggests `PK-8`; `N-421?` suggests `DEL-Y`; `N-42*` may match either `N-420` or `N-421`.
- Attribute similarity: same customer `C17`, same route `NORTH`, matching amounts, sequential bank references.
- Amount agreement: total `230` matching `130 + 100` is evidence, but not proof, because taxes, fees, discounts, or missing invoices are possible.
- Object hints: `order_hint` and `route_hint` are useful but defeasible.

**Evidence against a repair**

- Conflicting exact identifiers, e.g. assigning `ev10` with `external_ref=N-421?` to `DEL-X` whose carrier reference is `N-420`.
- Creating cross-order or many-to-many structures without independent support, e.g. packing an `ORD-B` item into `PK-8` solely because of a scanner prefix.
- Using arithmetic equality as the only reason to connect invoices and payments.
- Completing an object lifecycle only because incompleteness feels undesirable.

**Evidence-combination model**

A candidate relation is stronger when it is supported by several largely independent cues: identifier match, temporal plausibility, route/customer match, cardinality consistency, and existing O2O structure. Identifier similarity alone is medium evidence; amount agreement alone is weak. If an O2O edge is inferred only after first assuming an E2O edge, I discount the O2O edge as derived evidence, not independent confirmation. When two explanations are plausible, I prefer leaving the relation unresolved rather than forcing a complete lifecycle.

In the table below, `accept` means included in the selected conservative bundle, not asserted as true. `provisional` means plausible in an alternative bundle but not accepted conservatively. `unresolved` means deliberately left open. `reject` means an explicit non-addition is recommended because the balance of evidence is negative.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

### Bundle A: conservative / minimum-change repair

Bundle A adds only the best-supported dispatch relation and its directional O2O consequence:

- **ev08 and PK-8:** leave unresolved. The scanner reference `PK8-2` is suggestive, but the repair would create a cross-order package, `PK-8`, containing both `IT-A2` from `ORD-A` and `IT-B1` from `ORD-B`. Without explicit consolidation evidence, this is too aggressive.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** add `ev10 -> DEL-Y` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`. The identifier match `N-421?` to `N-421`, plus route consistency, is the strongest missing-link evidence in the log. I reject `ev10 -> DEL-X` because the carrier references conflict.
- **ev11 and the delivery objects:** leave unresolved. `N-42*` is ambiguous between `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y`, and the event observes only `ORD-A`. It may be a portal confirmation where carrier identifiers were lost.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** leave unresolved. The total `230` is compatible with both orders, but arithmetic alone is not enough to add `INV-44 bills ORD-B`.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** leave unresolved. `PAY-2` may settle `INV-44`, but it may also be unapplied cash or payment against a missing invoice.

Bundle A produces a coherent but incomplete log. It prevents `DEL-Y` from being entirely orphaned while preserving uncertainty around packing, confirmation, invoicing, and payment application. It does not force `ORD-B`, `IT-B1`, or `PAY-2` into complete lifecycles.

### Bundle B: integrated reconciliation repair

Bundle B treats the dataset as a single customer fulfillment and financial reconciliation episode. It includes Bundle A and promotes the provisional relations to provisional additions.

- **ev08 and PK-8:** add `ev08 -> PK-8` and `PK-8 packs IT-B1`. This assumes that the two items were consolidated into `PK-8`. This is plausible because the customer and route are the same, the pack events are close in time, and `PK-8` is later dispatched. It remains uncertain because cross-order packing is not otherwise evidenced.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** same as Bundle A: add `ev10 -> DEL-Y` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`.
- **ev11 and the delivery objects:** add both `ev11 -> DEL-X` and `ev11 -> DEL-Y`, not as a unique assignment. The wildcard `N-42*` can naturally cover both carrier references, and `ORD-A` has items in both packages under this bundle. A single-delivery interpretation is weaker because it must explain away the wildcard and the fact that `ORD-A` has goods in both dispatches. Still, this repair is provisional because the event may be only an order-level portal acknowledgement.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** add `ev12 -> ORD-B` and `INV-44 bills ORD-B`. This interprets `INV-44` as a consolidated invoice for both orders. The amount pattern and common customer support this, but do not prove it.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** add `ev14 -> INV-44` and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`. This makes the two payments jointly settle the invoice total. Again, this is plausible but not proven.

Bundle B yields more complete lifecycles. Both orders are created, items are added, packed, dispatched, and financially settled. However, it introduces several many-to-many structures: one package packs items from two orders, one invoice bills two orders, and one invoice is settled by two payments. These are not invalid, but they materially affect process mining results.

### Optional Bundle C: financial-only reconciliation

Bundle C is useful for sensitivity analysis. It accepts the dispatch repair from Bundle A and the financial repairs from Bundle B, but leaves packing and delivery confirmation unresolved.

- ev08 remains unresolved.
- ev10 connects to `DEL-Y`.
- ev11 remains unresolved.
- `INV-44` is linked to `ORD-B`.
- `PAY-2` is linked to `INV-44`.

This bundle is coherent if the financial records are trusted more than the logistics records. It creates a financially complete story while preserving logistical uncertainty.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

The audit below focuses on selected Bundle A, with notes where Bundle B differs.

**Order**

In Bundle A, `ORD-A` has a plausible partial lifecycle: creation, item additions, related packing through its items, dispatch through `PK-7` and `PK-8`, order-level confirmation at ev11, invoice link to `INV-44`, and one payment link through `PAY-1`. It is not complete in a payment sense because `PAY-2` is unresolved, but incomplete payment application is plausible.

`ORD-B` remains sparse: creation, item addition, and an item-only pack event. It has no confirmed package, dispatch, delivery, invoice, or payment relation in Bundle A. This is an intentional preservation of uncertainty. In Bundle B, `ORD-B` becomes much more complete because `IT-B1` is packed into `PK-8`, dispatched via `DEL-Y`, invoiced through `INV-44`, and paid through the combined payments. This is coherent but depends on several provisional assumptions.

No suspicious one-to-many Order relation is added in Bundle A. In Bundle B, `INV-44 bills ORD-B` creates an invoice-to-many-orders relation. It is allowed by the invoice rule, but it should be treated as a modeling choice with downstream effects.

**Item**

`IT-A1` has a complete observed logistics path: order containment, pack event with `PK-7`, and dispatch through `DEL-X`. It is supported independently by E2O and O2O evidence: ev06 and `PK-7 packs IT-A1` reinforce each other.

`IT-A2` has a similar path through `PK-8`. In Bundle A, its dispatch is strengthened by `DEL-Y carries PK-8`. Its delivery confirmation remains unresolved.

`IT-B1` has order creation, order containment, and an item-only pack event. Bundle A leaves its package unresolved. Bundle B adds `PK-8 packs IT-B1`, but this O2O edge is not independent of the assumed `ev08 -> PK-8` relation. If we first add the E2O edge and then derive the O2O edge, using the O2O edge as confirmation of the E2O edge would be circular. I therefore treat the pair as one weakly supported repair, not two independent pieces of evidence.

**Package**

`PK-7` is strongly supported: it packs `IT-A1`, is dispatched in ev09, and is carried by `DEL-X`. E2O and O2O evidence align.

`PK-8` is more uncertain. It certainly packs `IT-A2` and is present in ev10. Bundle A adds `DEL-Y carries PK-8`, making its dispatch lifecycle plausible. However, the O2O edge is largely derived from the E2O addition and should not be treated as independent proof. Bundle B additionally makes `PK-8` carry `IT-B1`, creating a cross-order package. This is the most structurally significant repair in the dataset.

**Delivery**

Before repair, `DEL-X` has dispatch and carry evidence, while `DEL-Y` is orphaned. Bundle A connects `DEL-Y` to ev10 and `PK-8`, creating a plausible dispatch lifecycle. Neither delivery has a confirmed delivery relation in Bundle A. This is acceptable: ev11 may be order-only confirmation, carrier identifiers may have been lost, or confirmation may be missing.

Bundle B adds confirmation relations to both deliveries. This creates a more complete delivery lifecycle but introduces a many-to-many confirmation pattern: one `Confirm Delivery` event relates to two deliveries. That is allowed by the domain rule, but it should not be treated as uniquely determined.

**Invoice**

`INV-44` is observed in ev12 and linked to `ORD-A` and `PAY-1`. Bundle A leaves its relation to `ORD-B` unresolved. The invoice total `230` remains unexplained by the linked order declared value `130`, but fees, taxes, discounts, or missing invoice objects are possible.

Bundle B adds `ORD-B` billing and `PAY-2` settlement. This makes the invoice total and payments arithmetically coherent. However, arithmetic coherence is not proof. The repair should be marked provisional and tested against downstream analysis.

**Payment**

`PAY-1` has a plausible lifecycle: event ev13 and O2O settlement of `INV-44`. `PAY-2` is payment-only in Bundle A. This can represent unapplied cash or a missing invoice. Bundle B links `PAY-2` to `INV-44`, producing a coherent two-payment settlement. This creates a many-to-one relation: two payments settle one invoice. It is plausible but should not be inferred from amount equality alone.

**Independent support and circularity**

Observed relations such as `PK-7 packs IT-A1`, `DEL-X carries PK-7`, `INV-44 bills ORD-A`, and `PAY-1 settles INV-44` are supported by both E2O and O2O cues. ## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**Unrepaired log**

In the unrepaired OCEL, the OC-DFG will show a coherent Order/Item/Pack fragment for `ORD-A` and a truncated fragment for `ORD-B`. There will be `Create Order -> Add Item` edges through orders, and `Add Item -> Pack Item` edges through items. Because ev08 has only `IT-B1`, the `ORD-B` pack event is not connected to a package-based dispatch path.

The `Dispatch Package` activity will connect to packages, but only `DEL-X` will be tied to dispatch through ev09. `DEL-Y` will be isolated or weakly represented. `Confirm Delivery` will appear connected to Order through ev11, not to Delivery. This can create a misleading order-centric confirmation fragment while hiding delivery confirmation behavior. `Issue Invoice` and one `Record Payment` will be connected through `INV-44`, but the second `Record Payment` will be isolated.

In the OTG, `DEL-Y` will be an orphan object instance or will only contribute to the Delivery type without meaningful edges. In the ET-OT graph, `Dispatch Package` will have an inconsistent object-type profile: some dispatch events relate to Delivery, while ev10 relates only to Package. `Confirm Delivery` will relate only to Order, not Delivery.

Flattening on Order will produce a rich trace for `ORD-A` and a short trace for `ORD-B`. Flattening on Delivery will produce a trace for `DEL-X` but not for `DEL-Y`. Payment flattening will show `PAY-2` as an orphan payment event.

**Bundle A**

Bundle A adds `ev10 -> DEL-Y` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`. The OC-DFG may not gain many new activity-activity edges if `DEL-Y` appears in only one event, but the object-type and ET-OT graphs become more consistent. `Dispatch Package` now relates to two Delivery instances in the ET-OT graph. The OTG gains a `DEL-Y` to `PK-8` edge.

The important absence remains deliberate: `Confirm Delivery` still does not connect to Delivery objects. Therefore, discovery will still show confirmation as order-related rather than delivery-related. This avoids falsely completing delivery lifecycles.

Order flattening changes little compared with the unrepaired log, except that package/delivery enrichment may indirectly expose `DEL-Y` in order-centric views if the flattening follows O2O links. Delivery flattening becomes more useful because `DEL-Y` now has a dispatch event. Invoice and payment flattenings remain conservative: `PAY-2` is still unapplied.

Activity frequencies based on event counts do not change, because no events are added. Frequencies based on unique related objects can change: `Dispatch Package` may now be associated with two unique deliveries instead of one. Frequencies based on total object relations also increase because two relations were added.

Equivalent-execution clustering around Order remains split. `ORD-A` has a relatively long lifecycle; `ORD-B` remains short. This preserves evidence that the log may contain missing logistics relations rather than assuming all orders followed the same path.

**Bundle B**

Bundle B substantially changes the process view. Adding `ev08 -> PK-8` and `PK-8 packs IT-B1` connects the `ORD-B` pack event to `PK-8`. In the OC-DFG, the `Pack Item -> Dispatch Package` fragment for `ORD-B` becomes visible. However, because `PK-8` also packs `IT-A2`, the package can induce cross-order behavior: events from `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` may appear connected through the same package. This may look like consolidation, interleaving, or concurrency, even if the underlying business process did not intend it.

Adding `ev11 -> DEL-X` and `ev11 -> DEL-Y` creates `Dispatch Package -> Confirm Delivery` edges through delivery objects. This is a major behavioral change: the unrepaired and Bundle A logs hide delivery confirmation, while Bundle B shows confirmed delivery for both dispatches. If the repair is wrong, discovery will overstate delivery confirmation rates and understate exceptional or unconfirmed shipments.

Adding `ev12 -> ORD-B`, `INV-44 bills ORD-B`, `ev14 -> INV-44`, and `PAY-2 settles INV-44` creates a consolidated financial process. The OC-DFG may show `Issue Invoice` connected to both order creation/addition fragments and both `Record Payment` events connected to the same invoice. This can produce duplicate-looking payment behavior in order flattenings: both `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` may inherit the same invoice and payment events if flattening follows shared objects.

In the OTG, Bundle B produces a dense network: Order to Item, Item to Package, Package to Delivery, Invoice to multiple Orders, Payment to Invoice. In the ET-OT graph, `Confirm Delivery` relates to Delivery, `Issue Invoice` relates to multiple Orders, and both `Record Payment` events relate to Invoice. These changes are plausible but should be presented as assumptions, not facts.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Forcing `ev08` onto `PK-8` can create false consolidation behavior.** The package would then contain items from `ORD-A` and `ORD-B`, causing order-centric flattenings and object graphs to connect two orders that may have been fulfilled separately.

2. **Assigning `ev11` to only one delivery can hide missing confirmation evidence.** If `DEL-Y` was not confirmed, a unique assignment to `DEL-X` would make delivery confirmation look complete and would suppress a genuine data-quality or exception signal.

3. **Linking `INV-44` to `ORD-B` mainly because amounts sum to `230` can create a false shared-invoice process.** This would make invoice and order flattenings duplicate invoice events across orders and could obscure a missing separate invoice.

4. **Linking `PAY-2` to `INV-44` mainly because amounts complement `PAY-1` can mask unapplied cash.** If `PAY-2` actually belongs to a missing invoice or is unreconciled, the repaired log will falsely show clean payment settlement.

5. **Treating derived O2O edges as independent evidence can create circular validation.** For example, adding `ev10 -> DEL-Y`, then deriving `DEL-Y carries PK-8`, and then citing the O2O edge as proof of the E2O edge is circular and overstates confidence.

6. **Completing every lifecycle can remove useful process variance.** The item-only `ev08`, order-only `ev11`, and payment-only `ev14` may represent scanner outages, portal failures, or unapplied cash. Repairing them away would make the process look more regular than it really is.
