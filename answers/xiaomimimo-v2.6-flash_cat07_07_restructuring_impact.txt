Then centralization function on the handover weight matrix:

```python
def centralization(how, top_n=5):
    W = how if isinstance(how, pd.DataFrame) else how.matrix
    W = pd.DataFrame(W).fillna(0)
    strength = W.sum(axis=0) + W.sum(axis=1) - np.diag(W)   # node strength
    share = strength / strength.sum()
    hhi = float((share ** 2).sum())                          # 0..1,1 = single hub
```

# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

An average can improve while the process gets worse. Nine specific traps:

1. **Tail latency (p90/p95) inflation.** Pooled queues with rising  lengthen the *tail* first. In an M/M/1-style queue the p90 wait  2.3× the mean, so a 2× rise in mean wait is a 2× rise in p90 — and p90 cycle time today already ranges 16.4–22.8 days. A portfolio median that moves 7.46  7.2d can coexist with p90 moving 18.6  22d. *Measure:* case-level p90/p95, edge-level p90 waits, survival curves rather than means.

2. **High-complexity cases concentrate in the bottleneck.** Rule 4 routes *all* high-complexity cases to one queue. Their handling time is longer (lower ), so their  is effectively higher than the portfolio . The median case improves because simple cases never touch the queue; complex cases degrade. *Measure:* cycle-time quantiles **conditioned on `case_complexity`**, not pooled.

3. **Loss of local knowledge.** Embedded tech did not only "do reviews" — they pre-empted unnecessary referrals and corrected intake errors informally. That work is invisible (it never becomes an event), so its disappearance shows up *later* as more referrals (3641), more rework, and more variants. *Measure:* `local_expertise_flag` share, referral rate per complexity class, activity-based resource similarity for technical activities.

4. **Fairness across teams.** The portfolio mean is a case-weighted blend (820/760/520). East improving by 1.2 days can numerically offset North and South each worsening by 0.5 days, producing a "better" average while two-thirds of staff and customers get a worse process. The 6.4-day inter-team p90 spread is the metric that matters. *Measure:* per-team quantiles and best-vs-worst spread; same for `customer_segment`.

5. **Learning and skill atrophy.** If local staff never handle complex or technical cases, `local_expertise_flag`  0 and activity similarity collapses. This is not visible in any near-term cycle-time chart but makes the current structure **irreversible in practice** — restoring local capability would take quarters. *Measure:* share of technical activities performed by local resources; per-resource activity entropy over rolling windows.

6. **Accountability and role clarity.** Shared approval means the approving `org:resource` is not the local supervisor. Decisions may be *faster on average* and still be contested, re-opened, or passed back. Symptom: `reassignment_count` **after** approval rises, and `Rework` follows `Supervisor Approval`. *Measure:* `discover_organizational_roles` role multiplicity; backward edges after approval in the DFG; `handover_reason` coded for ownership ambiguity.

7. **Exception and absence handling.** Today's cross-team handovers (6/100, 18% rework, 8.9h) are ugly but they are a **fallback**. Reducing them to 3/100 removes redundancy. On days when TSS staff are absent, high-complexity cases stall with no local substitute — visible only as day-level spikes, invisible in a monthly mean. *Measure:* `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` day/hour features; delay conditional on resource absence; `discover_subcontracting_network` out-volume.

8. **Priority inversions and segment-level queue fairness.** A shared queue serving multiple teams with mixed `priority` can systematically delay low-priority and language/regional-accommodation cases while fast-tracking high-value ones. Portfolio averages look fine because high-value cases dominate the volume. *Measure:* wait conditioned on `priority` × `customer_segment` × `team`; count of cases where a lower-priority case overtakes a higher-priority one.

9. **Utilization read as productivity.** "+15% specialist utilization" may simply mean *less slack*. Slack is what absorbs variability and absence. Utilization rising from 73%  85% while p90 wait rises 2× is a net loss misread as an efficiency gain. *Measure:* utilization **jointly** with wait distributions and absence-day performance — never alone.

# 5. Pilot design

## 5.1 Pilot scope
Two separable changes must be piloted **separately**, because they have different mechanisms and different risk profiles:

- **Stage A — Technical Shared Service (TSS):** merge the *technical review* function only. Routing rule: `case_complexity = high` **or** `required_specialty in {legal, risk, security}`  shared queue. **Do not** include difficult Quality Check in Stage A (it is the most likely hidden-bottleneck contributor). **Do not** change supervisor staffing in Stage A.
- **Stage B — Shared approval queue:** only after Stage A guardrails hold for 8 weeks. Requires a **named approver recorded in `org:resource` at every approval event** (this is the accountability fix, not an optional detail).
- **Excluded from pilot:** removal of local supervisory capacity permanently; elimination of all local technical capability (retain one part-time local technical role per team for early advice during the pilot).

Use a **staged rollout** so the design is evaluated against both a beneficiary and a likely-loser team:

| Stage | Teams routed to shared service | Duration | Control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | none | 12 weeks pre | — |
| A1 | **East only** (E_Tech 82% — strongest benefit hypothesis) | 8 weeks | South unchanged |
| A2 | East + **North** (E_Tech-free team with strong local loops — strongest risk hypothesis) | 8 weeks | South unchanged |
| A3 | East + North + **South** | 8 weeks | staggered difference-in-differences using earlier stages |
| B (gates) | Shared approval queue in A1–A2 teams | 8 weeks | Stage A period as control |

Running East (expected beneficiary) and North (expected loser) through the same rule is what makes the fairness and local-expertise hypotheses testable rather than asserted.

## 5.2 Comparison group
- **Primary control:** Team South during stages A1–A2 (unchanged process, same period  controls for seasonality, volume, automation changes).
- **Secondary control:** each team's own 12-week pre-window, matched on `case_complexity`, `priority`, `customer_segment` (pre/post within-team comparison).
- **Design:** staggered difference-in-differences; report **team-level effects separately**, never only portfolio aggregates.

## 5.3 Pre/post observation window
- **Pre:** 12 weeks minimum (covers seasonality, 4 cycle completions, stable p90 estimate).
- **Post per stage:** 8 weeks analysis window **excluding a 2-week ramp** (routing-rule tuning, staff training).
- **p90 caution:** with p90 cycle  19–23 days, right-censoring distorts the first 3–4 weeks (only fast cases have closed). Report p90 with survival adjustment or use only fully-closed cohorts; treat the first 4 weeks as **indicative only**.
- **Follow-up:** a 12-week lagging review after Stage B for learning/skill and absence-resilience effects, which will not appear earlier.

## 5.4 Segmentation (mandatory, not optional)
Every metric below is reported for **each** cell where volume permits, and always for:

- **Team:** North / South / East
- **Complexity:** `case_complexity` (low / medium / high) — high-complexity is the primary risk cell
- **Specialty:** `required_specialty` (none / legal / risk / security / technical)
- **Customer segment:** `customer_segment`, and specifically language/regional-accommodation segments
- **Priority:** `priority` (used to detect queue fairness / inversions)
- **Variant:** top-10 variants and "all other" (to detect new uncontrolled paths)

## 5.5 Guardrail metrics (rollback gates)
| Guardrail | Baseline | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| Handovers per 100 cases | 343 |  365 sustained 2 weeks |
| Handover-attributable wait per case | 5.88 h | > 7.0 h |
| TSS p90 wait | ~7 h (local tech) | > 12 h sustained 2 weeks |
| TSS utilization | 73% avg | > 85% sustained (queue instability) |
| p90 cycle, any team | N 16.4 / S 18.2 / E 22.8 d | +15% vs matched pre |
| High-complexity p90 cycle | measured in pre | +20% |
| Rework rate | 9.3% | +2.0pp |
| SLA miss | 7.6% | +2.0pp (or +3pp in any team) |

| Segment rework gap (worstbest) | pre-measured | +3.0 pp |

Any **two** gates breached in the same two-week period  automatic hold and review. Any **one** of: TSS p90 > 16h, high-complexity p90 +30%, rework +3pp, named-approver gap > 20%  **rollback that stage**.

## 5.6 Staff feedback collection
- **Weekly 5-minute pulse** (anonymous, 6 items): role clarity, queue fairness across teams, ownership of decisions, ability to get informal advice, confidence in routing rules. Scales 1–5 with a free-text field.
- **Biweekly structured debrief** with a stratified sample (2 staff per team, plus TSS members) using 6 fixed prompts: where did cases stall; where did you need local knowledge you no longer have; who owned a decision you were unsure about; which referrals were avoidable; what did you stop doing; what broke when someone was absent.
- **Handover-reason coding:** free-text `handover_reason` coded weekly into an agreed taxonomy (technical, legal/risk, absence, ownership unclear, customer request, rework) — this is the primary qualitative signal for mechanisms M4, M6, M7.
- **Informal-advice shadowing:** estimate (by observation + pulse) the number of quick local questions answered per day pre vs post — the mechanism most likely to be invisible in the log.
- **Fairness channel:** explicit monthly question on whether one team or customer segment is systematically disadvantaged, plus a non-punitive reporting route.

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: MODIFY the design, then PILOT FIRST with staged rollout and hard guardrails. Do not reject the direction; do not approve full rollout.**

## 6.1 Why "modify" before piloting
1. **The handover-reduction claim is not supported by management's own model** (343  359 per 100 cases, +4.7%). The proposal must be justified on *standardization and variance reduction*, not on handover count. Management should restate the benefit hypothesis accordingly, otherwise the pilot will be judged against a target that the design cannot meet.
2. **The utilization and wait-time numbers are mutually inconsistent.** +15% utilization (73%  ~85%) implies roughly 2× handover waiting time under any standard queueing model; the stated 3.2h  4.0h (+25%) implies only ~80%. Either staff the shared service with real slack or accept longer waits — and state which.
3. **Difficult Quality Check must be removed from the shared queue.** Adding a high-variability, high-value activity to an already-loaded queue (Legal 93%, Risk 91%) is the clearest single path to the adverse scenario.
4. **Named accountability must be designed in, not monitored in.** Shared approval without a recorded `org:resource` at the approval event destroys the ability to audit and destroys accountability simultaneously.
5. **Retain a part-time local technical role per team during the pilot.** This is cheap insurance against mechanism M5 (loss of early informal advice) and preserves the option to reverse.

## 6.2 Conditions for proceeding beyond the pilot
Proceed to the next stage **only if, over 8 weeks of post-ramp data**:
- Guardrails in §5.5 are unbreached;
- East shows real improvement (validates the pooling benefit);
- North and South show **no material degradation** in p90, rework, or SLA (validates that pooling did not cost local responsiveness);
- `local_expertise_flag` on complex cases is stable (validates that learning is not being destroyed);
- Staff pulse shows role clarity  pre-level and no ownership complaints trend;
- Segment-level fairness is unchanged or improved.

If East improves **and** North/South degrade, the correct response is not "roll out anyway" — it is to **restore embedded local capacity in North/South and centralize only for teams that are genuinely over-subscribed**. That is a different, better design than the one proposed.

## 6.3 What management must not infer from early averages
- **Not** that the process improved because the first 4 weeks look good: right-censoring means only fast-closing cases are counted, and backlog burn-down after a change flatters the numbers.
- **Not** that handovers fell: fewer *logged* handovers may just mean less precise logging in the shared queue; the count in management's own model goes **up**.
- **Not** that fairness improved because the inter-team spread narrowed: the spread narrows equally well by East improving *or by North/South degrading*. Only per-team trajectories distinguish these.
- **Not** that specialists became more productive because utilization rose: higher  with unchanged output is often just lost slack — the resource that used to absorb absence and variability.
- **Not** that quality held because rework is flat in aggregate: rework must be read by activity, complexity, and segment; a stable average is consistent with complex-case rework rising while simple-case rework falls.
- **Not** that the pilot generalizes: East is a special case (E_Tech at 82%); North and South are the tests of whether local expertise has value, and those answers take at least 8–12 weeks and a lagging 12-week follow-up for skill effects.
- **Not** that a good average means a good process: an average is a single number hiding p90, segments, priorities, exceptions, and who carries the risk when something breaks.

```python
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS)
    df["period"] = period                      # "pre" | "post"

pre  = load("events_pre_12w.csv",  "pre")

log  = pd.concat([pre, post], ignore_index=True)

def as_log(df):
    """Some PM4Py graph/role algorithms expect an EventLog, not a DataFrame."""
    return pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)

# ---------- 1. Handover, working-together, subcontracting networks -----
def networks(df, period):
    l = as_log(df)

                working   = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(l),
                subcontr  = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(l))

net = {p: networks(d, p) for p, d in [("pre", pre), ("post", post)]}

def net_matrix(obj):
    if isinstance(obj, pd.DataFrame):
        return obj.astype(float)
    m, labels = getattr(obj, "matrix", None), getattr(obj, "labels", None)
    if m is None:
        return pd.DataFrame()
    return pd.DataFrame(np.asarray(m), index=labels, columns=labels).astype(float)

def centralization(how_obj, top=5):
    """Herfindahl of node strength = how concentrated handovers are on one node."""
    W = net_matrix(how_obj)
    strength = W.sum(axis=0) + W.sum(axis=1) - np.diag(W)
    share = strength / strength.sum()
    return {"hhi": float((share ** 2).sum()),
            "top": share.sort_values(ascending=False).head(top)}

cent   = {p: centralization(net[p]["handover"]) for p in net}
Wt     = {p: net_matrix(net[p]["working"])  for p in net}
wt_dens = {p: float((Wt[p] > 0).sum().sum() / max(len(Wt[p]), 1)) for p in net}   # local co-work density
sub_out = {p: net_matrix(net[p]["subcontr"]).sum(axis=1) for p in net}            # work pushed out

# ---------- 2. Handover edge list with waiting times -------------------
def handover_edges(df, period):
    d = df.sort_values([CASE, TS]).copy()
    d["next_res"] = d.groupby(CASE)[RES].shift(-1)
    d["next_act"] = d.groupby(CASE)[ACT].shift(-1)
    d["gap_h"]    = d.groupby(CASE)[TS].shift(-1).sub(d[TS]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600

    ho = d.dropna(subset=["next_res"])
    ho = ho[ho["next_res"] != ho[RES]]                     # resource change = handover

    g = (ho.groupby([RES, "next_res", ACT, "next_act"])
           .agg(n        = ("gap_h", "size"),
                med_wait = ("gap_h", "median"),
                p90_wait = ("gap_h", lambda s: s.quantile(0.9)),
                rework   = ("rework_flag", "max"))
           .reset_index())
    g["per_100_cases"] = 100 * g["n"] / d[CASE].nunique()
    g["period"] = period
    return g

ho_pre, ho_post = handover_edges(pre, "pre"), handover_edges(post, "post")

summary = {
    "handovers_per_100": {"pre": ho_pre["n"].sum() / pre[CASE].nunique() * 100,
                          "post": ho_post["n"].sum() / post[CASE].nunique() * 100},
    "handover_wait_h_per_case": {"pre":  ho_pre["n"].mul(ho_pre["med_wait"]).sum() / pre[CASE].nunique(),
                                 "post": ho_post["n"].mul(ho_post["med_wait"]).sum() / post[CASE].nunique()},
}
print(pd.DataFrame(summary))

# ---------- 3. Service time & waiting time -----------------------------
def service_waiting(df, period):
    d = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df.copy())   # case-level waiting time column
    case_wait_h = d.groupby(CASE)["time:waiting_time"].max() / 3600

    d = d.sort_values([CASE, TS])
    d["gap_h"] = d.groupby(CASE)[TS].shift(-1).sub(d[TS]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    st_api  = pm4py.get_service_time(d)                     # activity-level service durations
    st_local = d.groupby(ACT)["gap_h"].agg(
        median="median", p90=lambda s: s.quantile(0.9))
    return case_wait_h.describe().rename(period), st_local, st_api

cw_pre,  st_pre,  api_pre  = service_waiting(pre,  "pre")
cw_post, st_post, api_post = service_waiting(post, "post")

# ---------- 4. Segmentation -------------------------------------------
def seg(df, team=None, complexity=None, specialty=None, segment=None, priority=None):
    out = df
    if team:       out = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(out, "team", team, level="cases")
    if complexity: out = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(out, "case_complexity", complexity, level="cases")
    if specialty:  out = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(out, "required_specialty", specialty, level="cases")
    if segment:    out = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(out, "customer_segment", segment, level="cases")
    if priority:   out = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(out, "priority", priority, level="cases")
    return out

cells = [(t, c, s, g)
         for t in ["North", "South", "East"]
         for c in ["low", "medium", "high"]
         for s in ["technical", "legal", "risk", "security", "none"]
         for g in ["standard", "regional", "language_accommodation"]]

def cell_report(df, period):
    rows = []
    for t, c, s, g in cells:
        f = seg(df, team=[t], complexity=[c], specialty=[s], segment=[g])
        if f[CASE].nunique() < 30:
            continue
        ct = f.groupby(CASE)[TS].agg(lambda x: (x.max() - x.min()).total_seconds() / 86400)
        rows.append(dict(period=period, team=t, complexity=c, specialty=s, segment=g,
                         cases=ct.size, median_d=ct.median(), p90_d=ct.quantile(0.9),
                         rework=f.groupby(CASE)["rework_flag"].max().mean(),
                         sla_miss=(ct > 7).mean(),
                         local_exp=f.groupby(CASE)["local_expertise_flag"].max().mean(),
                         reassign=f.groupby(CASE)["reassignment_count"].max().mean()))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

rep_pre, rep_post = cell_report(pre, "pre"), cell_report(post, "post")

# fairness: best-vs-worst team p90 spread (baseline 6.4 days)
fair = (rep_post.groupby("team")["p90_d"].max() - rep_post.groupby("team")["p90_d"].min())

# ---------- 5. Variants & DFG -----------------------------------------
def variants_dfg(df, period, top_k=30):

# ---------- 9. Distribution tests (means are not enough) ---------------
def case_ct(df):
    return df.groupby(CASE)[TS].agg(lambda x: (x.max() - x.min()).total_seconds() / 86400)
```

**How to read the output:** compare `cent` (centralization), `summary` (handover count and handover wait per case), `ho_pre` vs `ho_post` (per-edge frequency, median and p90 wait, rework after each handover), `rep_pre` vs `rep_post` (every guardrail **by team × complexity × specialty × segment**), `roles_df` (role multiplicity  accountability), `rework_cmp` (rework by activity), `variant_diversity` (loss of control over process paths), `wt_dens` (local co-working resilience), and `off_hours` (hidden off-peak load). The `ks` statistic and the `p90_d` rows are the checks that an average-cycle-time dashboard would miss.
