## 1. What is observed
Automation=1 records branch-month availability from April for Alpha and Beta and from July for Gamma; Delta and Epsilon were never treated through August. Availability is not use: automated fast path completion rose from a 4-6% baseline to 25-27% in adoption month and 46-50% by August for Alpha and Beta, and 25-32% for Gamma, with gradual learning. Alpha and Beta were selected for high volume and poor historical cycle times and began with the slowest medians, while Gamma entered after a readiness pilot; Delta was fastest and Epsilon intermediate, so branch comparisons mix selection with treatment. From March a centralized fraud desk served all branches, automated triage routed more borderline cases there, routing increased arrivals about 10% to Alpha and Beta from April and 5% to Gamma from July, FTE changed in Alpha, Beta, Gamma and Delta, and May increased high-complexity share in several branches. Outcomes are branch-month aggregates, not case records: medians and p90s hide case-level distributions and queue paths, rework and escalation percentages lack denominators and timing, FTE is average productive capacity, and CSAT means have no response counts despite 20-45% response, so satisfaction differences may reflect who responded.

Interference is intrinsic: branches share the centralized fraud desk and potentially specialist capacity, routing changes arrivals, and treated branches can affect control-branch queues. Treatment version is also unstable: thresholds, learning, local work practices and automated_fast_path_pct change after availability, so E1 estimates a bundle of system plus implementation, not a fixed software effect.

## 3. Identification strategy
| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary: Callaway-Sant'Anna group-time staggered DiD with event-study profile | Cohort by adoption month, April for Alpha and Beta, July for Gamma, with relative-time indicators | Not-yet-treated Gamma for Alpha and Beta before July, plus never-treated Delta and Epsilon | Calendar month effects, baseline branch level, pre-adoption high_complexity_pct and arrivals per FTE; do not condition on post-adoption fast-path use | Group-time ATT for E1, E3, E4, E5, E6 and dynamic E9 | Conditional parallel trends, no anticipation, limited spillover, stable measurement, treatment-version bundle interpreted as availability | Pre-trend event coefficients for January to March, placebo adoption dates, leave-one-branch-out, donor sensitivity, wild-cluster or randomization inference | Parallel trends fail because routing, staffing, case-mix and fraud-desk shocks are branch-specific; only five clusters |
| Matched DiD and synthetic control per adopter | Binary post-adoption for each adopter, with pre-period matching weights | Weighted never-treated Delta and Epsilon, and Gamma before July for Alpha and Beta | Pre-period median and p90 levels and trends, rework, escalation, CSAT, high_complexity_pct, arrivals per FTE | Adopter-specific ATT for Alpha, Beta and Gamma separately | Donor branches can reproduce adopter counterfactuals, no spillover, no unmeasured branch-specific shocks | Pre-period fit, in-time placebos, leave-one-donor-out, covariate balance, sensitivity to donor choice | Two never-treated donors are too few and differ in complexity and speed; excellent pre-fit can be coincidental |
| Comparative interrupted time series | Segmented level and slope change at adoption, with control series for common shocks | Never-treated and not-yet-treated branches as controls for calendar breaks | Month effects, March fraud-desk indicator, May case-mix indicator, lagged workload | Level and slope change in median, p90, rework, escalation and CSAT | Common shocks captured by controls and indicators, no unmodelled branch-specific break at adoption | Residual diagnostics, alternative break dates, placebo breaks in controls, stability across windows | Eight months are too short to estimate branch-specific slopes and multiple breaks; May and March breaks dominate |
| Treatment-intensity panel as sensitivity, not primary causal proof | Continuous automated_fast_path_pct, lagged where possible | Within-branch and cross-branch variation in intensity over time | Branch and month effects, high_complexity_pct, arrivals per FTE, escalation as process marker not a simple confounder | Association per 10 percentage points of fast-path use, E2 descriptive analogue | Intensity is exogenous conditional on controls, linear dose response, no selection into fast path by unobserved complexity | Nonlinear dose checks, lagged intensity, branch-by-branch slopes, falsification using pre-period intensity near 4% | Fast-path use is chosen by case mix, learning and local practice; month effects and intensity trend together |

The primary design is preferable because treatment is staggered, effects are likely dynamic, and intensity grows after adoption, so a single constant two-way-fixed-effects coefficient would average early low-use months with later mature use and can receive negative weights when later adopters are used as controls for earlier adopters. Group-time estimation for Alpha and Beta in April to August, using Gamma before July plus Delta and Epsilon as not-yet or never treated, and separately for Gamma in July to August, preserves that timing and lets the event-time profile show whether April disruption, May case-mix deterioration and August improvement are compatible with learning. It also targets the policy-relevant availability effect rather than conditioning on post-adoption fast-path use, which is selected by case complexity and would block part of the mechanism. Pre-trend and placebo event coefficients, donor sensitivity and aggregation to branch level can be inspected transparently. The design does not solve five-cluster inference, shared fraud-desk spillovers, or confounding by routing, staffing and case-mix changes; its advantage is that it makes those assumptions explicit and avoids imposing homogeneous, immediate effects on data that visibly violate them.

## 5. Quantitative evidence
**Arithmetic facts.** Define Pre as January to March 2026, Early as April to June, and Late as July to August. For Gamma, April to June is an immediate untreated pre-period. The table below is arithmetic only and is not a causal estimate.

| Adopter | Median pre | Median late | Median change | p90 pre | p90 late | p90 change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alpha | 48.9 | 45.6 | -3.3 | 98.5 | 105.4 | +6.9 |
| Beta | 45.9 | 46.8 | +0.9 | 92.9 | 102.4 | +9.5 |
| Gamma, versus January to March | 37.5 | 37.2 | -0.3 | 77.4 | 84.9 | +7.5 |
| Gamma, versus April to June | 43.7 | 37.2 | -6.5 | 94.2 | 84.9 | -9.3 |

Early post means show the dynamic path. Alpha in April to June had median 50.4 and p90 109.9, worse than Pre, before Late fell to 45.6 and 105.4. Beta early had median 48.4 and p90 103.0, also worse than Pre. Gamma early, while untreated, deteriorated to median 43.7 and p90 94.2 from January to March, consistent with the May case-mix change and workload rise in all branches. August alone was lower than July in every branch for median cycle time: Alpha 43.3 versus 47.9, Beta 45.0 versus 48.5, Gamma 36.0 versus 38.4, Delta 39.0 versus 39.5 and Epsilon 40.8 versus 44.8, while arrivals also fell in August in all branches.

Comparison with never-treated branches gives a different arithmetic picture. From January to March to July to August, Delta median rose 34.5 to 39.3, change +4.8, p90 +16.9, rework +2.2 percentage points, escalation +1.8 points and CSAT -0.07. Epsilon median rose 40.3 to 42.8, change +2.5, p90 +12.6, rework +2.7, escalation +2.1 and CSAT -0.05. The average never-treated change was therefore median +3.6 hours, p90 +14.7 hours, rework +2.5 points, escalation +2.0 points and CSAT -0.06. Subtracting that average change from adopters gives DiD-style contrasts of median -6.9 for Alpha, -2.8 for Beta and -3.9 for Gamma; p90 -7.8, -5.2 and -7.2; rework -1.2, -2.6 and -2.2 points; escalation +1.2, +0.6 and +0.9 points; CSAT +0.13, +0.06 and +0.09. Those are scenario calculations under a parallel-change assumption, not established causal effects.

Treatment intensity gives a weaker and less consistent pattern before common-time adjustment. Fast-path share rose by about 43 percentage points from Pre to Late for Alpha and 43 for Beta, and by 25 points for Gamma versus its April to June 4%. The corresponding median changes versus Pre were -3.3, +0.9 and -0.3 hours, implying naive slopes of -0.8, +0.2 and -0.1 hours per 10 points. After subtracting the average never-treated median change of +3.6, the implied slopes become about -1.6, -0.7 and -0.9 hours per 10 points. That consistency is interesting but depends on linearity, on the never-treated change being the correct counterfactual, and on ignoring the fact that fast-path cases are selected and that intensity, learning, arrivals and case mix move together.

Pre-trends are short but informative. January to February values are very stable, while March shows a common break: escalation rose exactly 2.0 points in every branch, and median rose by 2.5, 0.5, 3.1, 3.4 and 0.9 hours for Alpha, Beta, Gamma, Delta and Epsilon. That March fraud-desk break means a smooth parallel-trend assumption is already strained before adoption. Regression to the mean is not the main explanation for Alpha and Beta being slower, because their high medians persist across January to March rather than appearing as a single spike, and the selection was based on historical performance before January. However, regression to the mean or seasonal reversal remains plausible for Gamma after its May to June deterioration and for the common August fall in arrivals and median times. The data cannot distinguish a mature automation effect in August from a common late-summer workload decline.

Sensitivity is substantial for at least two outcomes. For median cycle time, the before-after Late minus Pre scenario gives -3.3, +0.9 and -0.3 hours, centred near zero. The DiD scenario using both never-treated branches gives -6.9 to -2.8 hours. Using Delta alone as donor gives -8.1, -3.9 and -5.1 hours; using Epsilon alone gives -5.8, -1.7 and -2.8 hours. A defensible scenario range is therefore roughly -7 to 0 hours for the late median contrast, with small positive harm not excluded. For p90, before-after gives +6.9, +9.5 and +7.5 hours, i.e. absolute worsening relative to January to March. DiD gives -7.8 to -5.2 hours because controls worsened more, and donor-specific DiD ranges roughly from -10 to -3 hours. The sign of any causal p90 effect is therefore not determined by these aggregates. For rework, before-after Late minus Pre is +1.3, -0.1 and +0.3 points, whereas DiD is about -1 to -3 points. For CSAT, before-after is +0.07, 0.00 and +0.03, whereas DiD is +0.06 to +0.13, well within plausible nonresponse and rounding variation.

**Causal interpretation, not established.** The most supportable reading is narrower than a single percentage claim: adopters did not experience the large absolute median improvement that a simple automation story predicts, early post-adoption performance worsened, late performance improved relative to never-treated branches, and p90 remained absolutely higher than pre-adoption despite being lower than the control-implied counterfactual. That pattern is compatible with a modest mature median benefit offset by case-mix, workload and specialist congestion, but it is also compatible with common seasonality, regression from May to June peaks, and branch-specific shocks.

## 6. Threats, tests, and decision consequences

|---|---|---|---|---|

## 8. Rollout recommendation
`expand with an embedded evaluation`

Expand to Delta first and to Epsilon only inside the same experimental protocol, not as an uncontrolled branch-wide switch. Delta is the lead branch because its high-complexity share is lower at 23%, its baseline cycles are faster, and it gained an FTE in June; Epsilon is the essential heterogeneity branch because 38% high complexity, higher rework and slower cycles make both benefit and harm more plausible there. Within each branch, randomly assign intake teams, or intake weeks if teams share queues too closely, stratified by registered complexity, to automated triage or manual triage for at least 16 weeks. Concurrent manual clusters sharing the same calendar period provide the main comparison, while the other branch and not-yet-converted clusters help estimate calendar and queue effects. Randomization at team or week level must be pre-registered, with allocation concealed until registration and complexity recorded before assignment.

Require at least eight prospective baseline weeks with frozen definitions after the May case-mix change, in addition to January to August history, and at least 16 post-assignment weeks covering normal staffing and routing. Collect case-level event logs with timestamps for registration, triage, specialist review, fraud-desk referral, rework loops, resolution and closure, plus variant, complexity, automated recommendation and confidence, human override, queue entry and exit, and waiting time by queue. Record daily arrivals, productive FTE hours, routing source, specialist and fraud-desk staffing, queue lengths, and survey denominator, response timing and case mix of respondents.

Monitor spillovers weekly: fraud-desk waiting time by originating branch and assigned arm, specialist queue length and age, control-cluster cycle times, and displaced manual work. Pause an arm for two consecutive weeks if its p90 exceeds the concurrent control by more than 10% or 10 hours, rework rises by more than three percentage points, fraud-desk or specialist waits breach the pre-set service level, or CSAT falls by more than 0.15 points with adequate response. Scale up branch-wide only if the pre-registered intention-to-treat analysis shows median improvement with p90 non-inferiority within 5%, no guardrail breach, no harm concentrated in high-complexity cases, and a stable or improving learning curve over the final eight weeks. Otherwise redesign thresholds, escalation rules or staffing before further expansion.

## 10. Final claim ladder
- Observed: Branch-month aggregates show fast-path use rising to about 50% in Alpha and Beta and 32% in Gamma, a common March escalation break, May case-mix deterioration across branches, higher late p90 than January to March for every adopter, and flat CSAT.
- Suggestive: Late median and p90 performance was better relative to never-treated Delta and Epsilon than in absolute before-after comparisons, with DiD-style median contrasts of roughly -3 to -7 hours, but this depends on the control counterfactual.
- Conditionally causal: If conditional parallel trends held, spillovers were negligible and co-occurring routing, staffing and case-mix changes were fully captured, availability would be associated with a late median ATT of roughly -3 to -7 hours, while the causal sign for p90, rework and CSAT would remain uncertain.
- Not supported: A single defensible statement that automation reduces cycle time by a fixed percentage for all cases, improves tail performance, reduces total rework, raises customer satisfaction, or will produce the same benefit when expanded uncontrolled to Delta and Epsilon.
