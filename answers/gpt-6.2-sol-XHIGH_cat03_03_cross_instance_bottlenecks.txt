## 1. Interaction-graph description

The graph has seven object types. Jobs require Components, ComponentLots contain Components, Batches group Jobs for curing, and Shipments consolidate Jobs for dispatch. Machines perform operations and Tools can constrain when those operations are feasible. Keep event IDs as separate nodes or hyperedges so a shared event retains its timestamp and each participant's role.

```text
Direct O2O
  requires:     Ji -> Ci (i = 1..6)
  contains:     L7 -> C2, C5; L8 -> C1, C3
  batches:      B1 -> J1, J2, J3; B2 -> J4, J5
  consolidates: S1 -> J1, J4; S2 -> J2, J3; S3 -> J5, J6

Timed event participation and resource records
  Cure 12:05     -> B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1, J2, J3, C1, C2, C3
  B2 start 14:50 -> B2, OVEN-1, RACK-7
  FIX-A hold     -> J3 [09:00, 11:40]
  Process 11:45  -> J4, C4, CNC-2, FIX-A
  Hold 10:20 and Release 13:50 -> L7, C2, C5 [J2, J5 via integration]
  Final Test     -> J5 or J6, TEST-1, CAL-3 [two distinct events]
  Dispatch 19:25 -> S1
```

B1 is a many-way synchronization motif joining Jobs and Components to a Batch, Machine and Tool in one cure. FIX-A connects J3's hold to J4's later operation on C4 at CNC-2, across B1 and B2. L7 connects J2 and J5 through their Components across two Batches, with different eligibility outcomes. S1 connects members of those Batches at a Shipment join, and S3 connects the two Jobs competing for the same test and calibration resources.

The O2O qualifiers and recorded event participation above are observations. The FIX-A interval is reconstructed from acquire and release. A directed claim that J3 blocked J4 additionally requires exclusivity, demand and a feasible CNC slot. The compared lot events pertain to J2 and J5 through their components, but the source Job extraction lacks those E2O links. Preserve the distinction between source and enriched links, and use the separately recorded override to qualify eligibility. Co-event projections identify possible interactions. The four Job–ComponentLot interactions and their reverse listing are two orientations of the same evidence.

## 4. What flattening misses

| Viewpoint | Visible | Hidden or duplicated and the misleading conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Individual routes, rework and recorded elapsed times | The projection can copy B1's cure into three cases, omit L7's gate and collapse another job's influence into a generic transition. Normal medians and similar total durations can coexist with substantial synchronization. |
| Machine | Operations, utilization and events on each machine | Job readiness, fixture demand and rack or calibration eligibility may be absent. An idle period can be mistaken for available capacity. Check the scopes of the 73-minute OVEN-1 inter-event maximum and the 12:55–14:50 idle interval. Idle time can contain logged events. |
| Batch | Membership and one lifecycle per cure, along with readiness if linked | Serializing several member arrivals can imply a sequential preparation task. A last-ready-to-cure DFG can make B1 look prompt while leaving J1's and J2's accrued waits invisible. |
| Tool | Reservations or holds where recorded, cleaning and calibration | Requests and ready demand may be missing. Long fixture occupation can be mistaken for continuous productive work, and a tool trace alone cannot tell whether an earlier machine slot was usable. |
| ComponentLot | Quarantine, release and affected components | Job-level permissions may disappear. A Quarantine → Release edge gives lot-state duration, which cannot be assigned as the same delay to J2 and J5. |
| Shipment | Creation, loading and dispatch | The case clock can start after an early job is packed. The short Load → Dispatch median can give an overly favorable account of packed-job dwell. A shared dispatch can also appear once per job in a Job projection. |

A job can be physically ready while the required tool, eligible kit, compatible batch or operator is unavailable. Machines then spend time idle even as work accumulates. Shift-level utilization and medians also smooth over brief windows in which several eligible jobs compete.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**J3, FIX-A, CNC-2, B2 and S1.** J3 releases FIX-A at 11:40, J4 processes C4 at 11:45 and is B2-ready at 13:10. J4 packs at 19:10, then S1 dispatches. An earlier CNC start requires simultaneous fixture and machine availability. A fixed B2 membership rule still requires J5 and an eligible rack, while S1 may require both packed members. An earlier fixture handoff could advance J4's CNC step and leave B2 and dispatch unchanged because they have separate gates.

**L7, C2/C5, J2/J5 and the two oven batches.** Quarantine begins at 10:20, B1 cures at 12:05, L7 clears at 13:50 and J5 is B2-ready at 14:40. Both cures use OVEN-1 and RACK-7. The hold is an eligibility gate, while simultaneous oven and rack availability is a resource constraint. The separate supervisor override allows J2's earlier path. A stricter lot policy could push B1 later and move its oven or rack demand into B2's window. Timing alone cannot show whether that conflict would occur.

**J5/J6, CAL-3, TEST-1 and S3.** J5's test ends at 16:35, CAL-3 clears at 17:20 and J6 finishes testing at 17:50. If valid calibration is required, an idle tester is insufficient. Test queue order determines who faces the reset, and consolidation can pass the later pack time to the other member. S3's 35-minute last-pack-to-dispatch lag leaves the identity of the last packer unknown. An earlier J6 test helps shipment timing only if it advances the relevant packing or carrier cutoff.

## 7. PM4Py analysis outline

Compare distinct consecutive event pairs with distinct contributing objects for each typed OC-DFG edge. Keep the explicit O2O relations alongside the discovered co-event graph. The B1 interval example requires a batch-linked readiness event. If it is missing, the join leaves that job unmeasured.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

views = {
    "interaction_summary": pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel),
    "co_event_graph": pm4py.discover_objects_graph(
        ocel, graph_type="object_interaction"
    ),
    "explicit_o2o": ocel.o2o,
    "otg": pm4py.discover_otg(ocel),
    "etot": pm4py.discover_etot(ocel),
}

ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
frequency_rows = []
for object_type in ("Job", "Batch", "Tool"):
    event_pairs = ocdfg["edges"]["event_couples"].get(object_type, {})
    objects = ocdfg["edges"]["unique_objects"].get(object_type, {})
    for edge in sorted(event_pairs.keys() | objects.keys()):
        frequency_rows.append({
            "object_type": object_type,
            "edge": edge,
            "event_pairs": len(event_pairs.get(edge, ())),
            "unique_objects": len(objects.get(edge, ())),
        })
edge_comparison = pd.DataFrame(frequency_rows)

performance_dfgs = {}
types_present = set(ocel.objects[ocel.object_type_column])
for object_type in ("Job", "Machine", "Batch", "Tool", "ComponentLot", "Shipment"):
    if object_type in types_present:
        flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type)
        if not flat.empty:
            performance_dfgs[object_type] = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(flat)
job_features = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel, "Job")

event_id = ocel.event_id_column
object_id = ocel.object_id_column
activity = ocel.event_activity
timestamp = ocel.event_timestamp
links = ocel.relations[[event_id, object_id]].drop_duplicates()
b1_events = links.loc[links[object_id].eq("B1"), event_id]
b1_job_links = links.loc[
    links[event_id].isin(b1_events) & links[object_id].isin(["J1", "J2", "J3"])
].merge(
    ocel.events[[event_id, activity, timestamp]],
    on=event_id,
    validate="many_to_one",
)
# Use the recorded label for effective readiness in the integrated log.
ready = b1_job_links.loc[
    b1_job_links[activity].eq("Ready for Batch"), [object_id, timestamp]
].rename(columns={timestamp: "ready_at"})
cure = b1_job_links.loc[
    b1_job_links[activity].eq("Batch Cure"), [object_id, timestamp]
].rename(columns={timestamp: "cure_at"})
b1_intervals = ready.merge(cure, on=object_id, validate="one_to_one")
elapsed = pd.to_datetime(b1_intervals["cure_at"], utc=True) - pd.to_datetime(
    b1_intervals["ready_at"], utc=True
)
b1_intervals["ready_to_cure_minutes"] = (
    elapsed.dt.total_seconds().div(60).where(elapsed.ge(pd.Timedelta(0)))
)
```

## 8. Falsification and additional data

The following observations would weaken batch synchronization as the leading explanation for recoverable waiting.

- J1's and J2's early timestamps mark staging, with effective eligibility only near noon.
- OVEN-1 has no executable slot before 12:05 for B1's recipe or cycle length.
- RACK-7 and all approved substitutes are unavailable through the earlier candidate starts.
- B1 routinely starts at the same reserved time even when all members are ready much earlier.
- J3 was assigned after B1's launch time was fixed, and earlier compatible capacity was available to neither early member.
- The authoritative B2 plan shows J5 joining after the start decision, with J4's timing controlled by another gate.
- A trial of flexible membership with feasible earlier slots leaves the ready-to-cure tail unchanged, with recorded lot or tool holds explaining the tail.

Collect effective readiness and revocation times per job and operation, with blocked reasons. Record machine state intervals, staffing and assignable slots. Capture tool requests, assignments, release, qualified substitutes and calibration or cleaning intervals. Retain batch membership revisions, lock times, recipes and minimum-load rules. Capture component genealogy, disposition effectiveness and override scope with source provenance. Record shipment membership changes, pack times, carrier bookings and actual departures. Use stable event IDs, explicit E2O roles and time-of-occurrence fields to distinguish genuine sequencing from late or duplicated integration.
