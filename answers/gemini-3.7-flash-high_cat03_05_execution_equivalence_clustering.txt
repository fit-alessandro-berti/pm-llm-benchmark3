## 1. Equivalence design space

To cluster object-centric executions centered on a lead object $\mathcal{O}_{lead}$ of type `Order`, we formalize four distinct equivalence relations across the hierarchy of abstraction from flat trace projections to qualified graph semantics.

Let an object-centric execution $X$ be represented as a tuple:
$$X = (E, O, \text{act}, \text{type}, \tau, \mathcal{R}_{E2O}, \mathcal{R}_{O2O}, \mathcal{Q})$$
where:
- $E$ is a finite set of event identifiers.
- $O = O_{loc} \uplus O_{ext}$ is a finite set of object identifiers partitioned into local objects and external objects (prefixed with `EXT`).
- $\text{act}: E \to \Sigma$ assigns an activity label to each event.
- $\text{type}: O \to \mathcal{T}$ assigns an object type ($\mathcal{T} = \{\text{Order}, \text{Item}, \text{Package}, \text{Delivery}, \text{Invoice}, \text{Payment}\}$).
- $\tau: E \to \mathbb{R}$ is the timestamp function defining a total (or weak) order over $E$.
- $\mathcal{R}_{E2O} \subseteq E \times O$ is the event-to-object incidence relation.
- $\mathcal{R}_{O2O} \subseteq O \times O$ is the object-to-object relation graph.
- $\mathcal{Q}: (E \times O) \cup (O \times O) \rightharpoonup \mathcal{S}_{qual}$ assigns semantic qualifiers (e.g., `removed_before_dispatch`, `replaces`, `partially_settles`).

---

### Relation 1: Activity-Sequence Equivalence ($\sim_{act}$)

- **Operational Definition**: Let $\pi_{act}(X) = \langle \text{act}(e_1), \text{act}(e_2), \dots, \text{act}(e_n) \rangle$ be the sequence of activity labels ordered by $\tau(e_1) \le \tau(e_2) \le \dots \le \tau(e_n)$. Two executions $X_1$ and $X_2$ are activity-sequence equivalent ($X_1 \sim_{act} X_2$) if and only if $\pi_{act}(X_1) = \pi_{act}(X_2)$ as words over the alphabet $\Sigma$.
- **Invariants Preserved**: Total sequence length, activity frequencies, and strict sequential ordering of activity labels.
- **Information Discarded**: All object identifiers, object types, multiplicities per activity, E2O associations, O2O topology, qualifiers, and external object boundaries.
- **Algebraic Properties**: $\sim_{act}$ is trivially an equivalence relation (reflexive, symmetric, transitive) because it is defined via equality of strings in $\Sigma^*$.
- **Concurrency & Timestamp Ties**: Under true concurrency or identical timestamps $\tau(e_i) = \tau(e_j)$, ties produce arbitrary interleavings unless events with equal timestamps are grouped into multisets (bags) of activities per timestamp step: $\langle B_1, B_2, \dots, B_k \rangle$ where $B_t = \{\!\{ \text{act}(e) \mid \tau(e) = t \}\!\}$; standard sequential flattening breaks ties arbitrarily, creating artificial distinctions.

---

### Relation 2: Typed Structural Equivalence under Local Identifier Renaming ($\sim_{struct}$)

- **Operational Definition**: $X_1 \sim_{struct} X_2$ if and only if there exists a pair of bijective functions $(f_E, f_O)$ where $f_E: E_1 \to E_2$ and $f_O: O_1 \to O_2$ such that:
  1. $\forall e \in E_1: \text{act}_2(f_E(e)) = \text{act}_1(e)$
  2. $\forall e, e' \in E_1: \tau_1(e) < \tau_1(e') \iff \tau_2(f_E(e)) < \tau_2(f_E(e'))$
  3. $\forall o \in O_1: \text{type}_2(f_O(o)) = \text{type}_1(o)$
  4. $\forall o \in O_1: o \in O_{1,ext} \iff f_O(o) \in O_{2,ext}$
  5. $\forall (e, o) \in E_1 \times O_1: (e, o) \in \mathcal{R}_{E2O, 1} \iff (f_E(e), f_O(o)) \in \mathcal{R}_{E2O, 2}$
  6. $\forall (o, o') \in O_1 \times O_1: (o, o') \in \mathcal{R}_{O2O, 1} \iff (f_O(o), f_O(o')) \in \mathcal{R}_{O2O, 2}$
- **Invariants Preserved**: Full bipartite E2O incidence topology, O2O graph topology, exact object multiplicities per event, object types, external boundary flags, and sequential event order.
- **Information Discarded**: Specific local identifier names (e.g., $O1$ vs $O2$, $P11$ vs $P21$), edge qualifiers $\mathcal{Q}$ not represented in the unlabeled graph structure, and absolute timestamps.
- **Algebraic Properties**: $\sim_{struct}$ is an equivalence relation:
  - *Reflexive*: Identity mappings $(id_E, id_O)$ satisfy all conditions.
  - *Symmetric*: Inverses $(f_E^{-1}, f_O^{-1})$ are valid bijections satisfying all biconditionals.
  - *Transitive*: Composition $(g_E \circ f_E, g_O \circ f_O)$ preserves all isomorphisms.
- **Concurrency & Timestamp Ties**: Handled by replacing the total order condition (2) with a causal partial order condition: $e \prec_1 e' \iff f_E(e) \prec_2 f_E(e')$, where $\prec$ is defined by object lifecycle transitions (transitive reduction of shared object lifecycles).

---

### Relation 3: Unqualified Object-Centric Topology & Multiplicity Equivalence ($\sim_{topo}$)

- **Operational Definition**: Two executions $X_1$ and $X_2$ are topologically equivalent ($X_1 \sim_{topo} X_2$) if they share the same object-type multiplicity vectors and event-to-object cardinality patterns across lifecycle phases, ignoring external object distinctions and fine-grained event interleavings of independent sub-processes. Formally, $X_1 \sim_{topo} X_2$ iff their quotient graphs of object-type interaction cardinalities (e.g., $|\text{Items}| : |\text{Packages}| : |\text{Deliveries}| : |\text{Invoices}| : |\text{Payments}|$) and lifecycle phase DAGs are isomorphic.
- **Invariants Preserved**: Macro-level object counts per type, fulfillment structure (e.g., $N$ items to $M$ packages to $K$ deliveries), and settlement structure (e.g., 1 invoice to $P$ payments).
- **Information Discarded**: External object boundaries (treating $EXT\text{-}OA$ as a local order), intra-phase event re-orderings, item-level replacement/removal qualifiers.
- **Algebraic Properties**: Reflexive, symmetric, and transitive via equivalence of quotient feature graphs.
- **Concurrency & Timestamp Ties**: Fully robust to interleavings because parallel object lifecycles (e.g., packing item 1 vs item 2) are aggregated into structural cardinalities.

---

### Relation 4: Business-Semantic Equivalence ($\sim_{sem}$, Preferred Relation)

- **Operational Definition**: Two executions $X_1$ and $X_2$ are business-semantic equivalent ($X_1 \sim_{sem} X_2$) if and only if they evaluate to identical values across a normalized 5-tuple of business process lifecycle motifs $\Phi(X) = (\mu_{fulf}, \mu_{settle}, \mu_{iso}, \mu_{qual}, \mu_{rev})$:
  1. $\mu_{fulf} \in \{\text{SinglePackageDirect}, \text{SplitPackConsolidatedDelivery}, \text{StaggeredSplitDelivery}, \text{CoPackedDelivery}\}$: Logistics consolidation strategy.
  2. $\mu_{settle} \in \{\text{SinglePaymentFull}, \text{InstallmentMultiPayment}, \text{CrossOrderJointBilling}\}$: Financial settlement mechanism.
  3. $\mu_{iso} \in \{\text{Isolated}, \text{CrossOrderLogistics}, \text{CrossOrderFinance}\}$: Operational boundary integrity.
  4. $\mu_{qual} \in \{\text{Nominal}, \text{ItemRemovedOrSubstituted}, \text{PackageReplaced}\}$: Execution integrity qualifiers.
  5. $\mu_{rev} \in \{\text{ForwardOnly}, \text{ReturnRepackRework}\}$: Reverse logistics and rework loops.
- **Invariants Preserved**: High-level fulfillment strategy, settlement policy, cross-order liability/cost sharing, physical fulfillment discrepancies (item removal/substitution), and rework/return lifecycles.
- **Information Discarded**: Purely synthetic identifiers, permutation of symmetric item additions, transient timestamp differences, and inconsequential parallel interleavings.
- **Algebraic Properties**: Reflexive ($\Phi(X) = \Phi(X)$), Symmetric ($\Phi(X_1) = \Phi(X_2) \implies \Phi(X_2) = \Phi(X_1)$), Transitive ($\Phi(X_1) = \Phi(X_2) \land \Phi(X_2) = \Phi(X_3) \implies \Phi(X_1) = \Phi(X_3)$).
- **Concurrency & Timestamp Ties**: Invariant to timestamp perturbations; motif extraction evaluates the causal graph and relational qualifiers, making it immune to logging jitter or concurrent event serialization artifacts.

---

## 2. Canonical signatures

To operationalize the preferred business-semantic relation ($\sim_{sem}$), we construct a deterministic canonical signature algorithm. The algorithm abstracts local identifiers into structural roles, extracts topological motifs (bipartite degree sequences between `Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, and `Payment`), parses qualification annotations, and flags cross-boundary objects.

```python
from collections import defaultdict
from typing import Dict, List, Set, Tuple, Any

class OCELCanonicalizer:
    def __init__(self, lead_type: str = "Order"):
        self.lead_type = lead_type

    def extract_signature(self, execution: Dict[str, Any]) -> str:
        """
        Computes a reproducible, identifier-invariant canonical signature
        preserving object types, multiplicities, boundary flags, qualifiers,
        and causal lifecycle motifs.
        """
        events: List[Dict[str, Any]] = execution["events"]
        o2o: List[Dict[str, Any]] = execution.get("o2o", [])
        
        # 1. Classify Object Boundaries
        all_objects = set()
        external_objects = set()
        for ev in events:
            for otype, oids in ev["e2o"].items():
                for oid in oids:
                    all_objects.add(oid)
                    if oid.startswith("EXT") or "EXT" in oid:
                        external_objects.add(oid)
        
        # 2. Extract Isolation Motif
        has_ext_logistics = any(
            oid in external_objects for ev in events 
            if ev["activity"] in {"Pack Package", "Dispatch"}
            for oids in ev["e2o"].values() for oid in oids
        )
        has_ext_finance = any(
            oid in external_objects for ev in events 
            if ev["activity"] in {"Issue Invoice", "Record Payment"}
            for oids in ev["e2o"].values() for oid in oids
        )
        
        if has_ext_logistics:
            iso_motif = "EXT_LOGISTICS"
        elif has_ext_finance:
            iso_motif = "EXT_FINANCE"
        else:
            iso_motif = "LOCAL_ISOLATED"

        # 3. Analyze Logistics & Fulfillment Topology
        pack_events = [e for e in events if e["activity"] == "Pack Package"]
        dispatch_events = [e for e in events if e["activity"] == "Dispatch"]
        
        num_packages = len({oid for e in pack_events for oid in e["e2o"].get("Package", [])})
        num_deliveries = len({oid for e in dispatch_events for oid in e["e2o"].get("Delivery", [])})
        
        # Interleaving check: are pack and dispatch staggered?
        act_seq = [e["activity"] for e in events]
        is_staggered = (
            act_seq.count("Pack Package") > 1 and 
            act_seq.count("Dispatch") > 1 and
            act_seq.index("Dispatch") < (len(act_seq) - 1 - act_seq[::-1].index("Pack Package"))
        )
        
        if iso_motif == "EXT_LOGISTICS":
            fulf_motif = f"COPACK_{num_packages}P_{num_deliveries}D"
        elif num_packages == 1 and num_deliveries == 1:
            fulf_motif = "1P_1D_DIRECT"
        elif num_packages > 1 and num_deliveries == 1:
            fulf_motif = f"{num_packages}P_1D_CONSOLIDATED"
        elif num_packages > 1 and num_deliveries > 1 and is_staggered:
            fulf_motif = f"{num_packages}P_{num_deliveries}D_STAGGERED"
        else:
            fulf_motif = f"{num_packages}P_{num_deliveries}D_MULTI"

        # 4. Analyze Financial Settlement Topology
        invoice_events = [e for e in events if e["activity"] == "Issue Invoice"]
        payment_events = [e for e in events if e["activity"] == "Record Payment"]
        num_invoices = len({oid for e in invoice_events for oid in e["e2o"].get("Invoice", [])})
        num_payments = len({oid for e in payment_events for oid in e["e2o"].get("Payment", [])})
        
        has_partial_settlement = any(
            rel.get("qualifier") in {"partially_settles", "completes_settlement"}
            for rel in o2o
        ) or num_payments > num_invoices

        if iso_motif == "EXT_FINANCE":
            settle_motif = f"{num_invoices}V_{num_payments}M_SHARED_JOINT"
        elif num_payments > 1 or has_partial_settlement:
            settle_motif = f"{num_invoices}V_{num_payments}M_INSTALLMENT"
        else:
            settle_motif = f"{num_invoices}V_{num_payments}M_FULL"

        # 5. Extract Edge Qualifiers & Relational Exceptions
        qualifiers = set()
        for rel in o2o:
            if "qualifier" in rel and rel["qualifier"]:
                qualifiers.add(rel["qualifier"])
        # Check if dispatch restricts item set (e.g. X7)
        for ev in dispatch_events:
            if "Item" in ev["e2o"] and len(ev["e2o"]["Item"]) > 0:
                qualifiers.add("ITEM_SUBSET_DISPATCH")
        
        if "removed_before_dispatch" in qualifiers or "ITEM_SUBSET_DISPATCH" in qualifiers:
            qual_motif = "ITEM_SUBSTITUTION_REMOVAL"
        elif "replaces" in qualifiers:
            qual_motif = "OBJECT_REPLACEMENT"
        else:
            qual_motif = "NOMINAL"

        # 6. Extract Reverse Logistics & Lifecycle Loops
        has_return = any(e["activity"] == "Return Item" for e in events)
        has_inspect = any(e["activity"] == "Inspect Return" for e in events)
        
        if has_return or has_inspect:
            rev_motif = "RETURN_INSPECT_REPACK_LOOP"
        else:
            rev_motif = "FORWARD_ONLY"

        # Construct Canonical Signature String
        signature = (
            f"SIG[Fulf={fulf_motif}|"
            f"Settle={settle_motif}|"
            f"Iso={iso_motif}|"
            f"Qual={qual_motif}|"
            f"Rev={rev_motif}]"
        )
        return signature
```

## 4. Cluster interpretations

### Cluster 1: `SEM_BASELINE_DIRECT` (Executions X1, X2)
- **Concise Signature**: `Fulf=1P_1D_DIRECT | Settle=1V_1M_FULL | Iso=LOCAL_ISOLATED | Qual=NOMINAL | Rev=FORWARD_ONLY`
- **Business Behavior**: Standard, frictionless "happy-path" order fulfillment. All items are packed into a single container, shipped in a single delivery, billed once, and settled in full by a single transaction.
- **Operational Risks & Opportunities**: Highest operational efficiency; lowest fulfillment overhead. Opportunity for full automated straight-through processing (STP). Risk is minimal, serving as the benchmark cost profile.
- **Flattening Failure Mode**: Standard single-case flattening (e.g., Order-centric) works adequately here because cardinality across all object types is 1:1:1:1:1 relative to the order.
- **Stability under Data Repair**: Extremely stable. Minor timestamp jitter or item sequence permutation does not perturb the signature.

---

### Cluster 2: `SEM_SPLIT_PACK_CONSOL` (Execution X3)
- **Concise Signature**: `Fulf=2P_1D_CONSOLIDATED | Settle=1V_1M_FULL | Iso=LOCAL_ISOLATED | Qual=NOMINAL | Rev=FORWARD_ONLY`
- **Business Behavior**: Split packaging with consolidated freight delivery. Items require separate physical packaging (e.g., hazmat separation, differing dimensions, or fragile goods) but are consolidated at the dispatch dock into a single outbound transport carrier.
- **Operational Risks & Opportunities**: Packaging material and labor costs are double baseline, but freight carrier costs are optimized via consolidated dispatch. Risk of dock-side staging delays while waiting for all packages of the order to assemble before dispatch.
- **Flattening Failure Mode**: 
  - *Order-centric flattening* collapses two `Pack Package` events into arbitrary duplicates or drops package-level lineage.
  - *Item-centric flattening* creates two divergent traces that artificially duplicate the shared `Dispatch`, `Issue Invoice`, and `Record Payment` events, inflating billing volumes.
- **Stability under Data Repair**: Stable if package entities are logged; if package IDs are missing, heuristic event-clustering might incorrectly merge the two pack events into one.

---

### Cluster 3: `SEM_STAGGERED_PARTIAL` (Execution X4)
- **Concise Signature**: `Fulf=2P_2D_STAGGERED | Settle=1V_2M_INSTALLMENT | Iso=LOCAL_ISOLATED | Qual=NOMINAL | Rev=FORWARD_ONLY`
- **Business Behavior**: Staggered multi-shipment fulfillment (e.g., backorder or multi-warehouse dispatch) paired with milestone-based installment payments. Items are packed and dispatched asynchronously as they become available.
- **Operational Risks & Opportunities**: High logistics friction (multiple carrier pick-up fees, multiple tracking numbers) and high accounts receivable (AR) overhead. Risk of cash-flow delay and customer dissatisfaction due to partial delivery.
- **Flattening Failure Mode**:
  - *Delivery-centric flattening* splits the single invoice across two independent case traces, losing the overall AR reconciliation picture.
  - *Order-centric flattening* produces an interleaved sequence (`Pack -> Dispatch -> Pack -> Dispatch`) that appears as an inefficient looping process rather than two parallel fulfillment pipelines.
- **Stability under Data Repair**: Moderate stability. Sensitive to payment reconciliation log quality; if partial payments are aggregated by daily banking batches, the installment motif might collapse into a single net settlement.

---

### Cluster 4: `SEM_COPACK_CROSS_ORDER` (Execution X5)
- **Concise Signature**: `Fulf=COPACK_1P_1D | Settle=1V_1M_FULL | Iso=EXT_LOGISTICS | Qual=NOMINAL | Rev=FORWARD_ONLY`
- **Business Behavior**: Cross-order logistics consolidation / Co-packing. Items from multiple independent customer orders ($O5$ and $EXT\text{-}OA$) going to the same destination (e.g., B2B distribution hub or joint household) are packed into a single container and shared delivery vehicle.
- **Operational Risks & Opportunities**: Significant transportation cost savings and reduced environmental footprint. Operational risk involves complex packing coordination, cross-contamination of goods, and delivery liability if the package is lost or damaged in transit.
- **Flattening Failure Mode**: Flattening onto either order completely severs the logistical dependency, making it impossible to calculate true package utilization, container weight, or shared delivery cost attribution.
- **Stability under Data Repair**: Fragile under naive case extraction. If external objects are stripped during data ETL (treating the log as single-order siloed traces), X5 identically collapses into X1.

---

### Cluster 5: `SEM_SHARED_BILLING` (Execution X6)
- **Concise Signature**: `Fulf=1P_1D_DIRECT | Settle=1V_1M_SHARED_JOINT | Iso=EXT_FINANCE | Qual=NOMINAL | Rev=FORWARD_ONLY`
- **Business Behavior**: Centralized corporate billing / Consolidated invoicing across multiple orders. Fulfillment occurs independently at the local order level, but billing and cash settlement are aggregated at a parent account or master contract level ($O6$ and $EXT\text{-}OB$).
- **Operational Risks & Opportunities**: Highly convenient for enterprise buyers; streamlined credit control. Serious financial audit risk: unallocated payments ($M61$) make aging and bad-debt attribution per order difficult without secondary ERP allocation rules.
- **Flattening Failure Mode**: Traditional order-centric process mining treats $V61$ and $M61$ as belonging to $O6$, duplicating them when analyzing $EXT\text{-}OB$, leading to double-counting of recognized revenue and cash receipts.
- **Stability under Data Repair**: Highly sensitive to boundary isolation. Stripping external order tags turns this into a false standard order, obscuring AR consolidation.

---

### Cluster 6: `SEM_ITEM_SUBSTITUTION` (Execution X7)
- **Concise Signature**: `Fulf=1P_1D_DIRECT | Settle=1V_1M_FULL | Iso=LOCAL_ISOLATED | Qual=ITEM_SUBSTITUTION_REMOVAL | Rev=FORWARD_ONLY`
- **Business Behavior**: Pre-dispatch discrepancy and item substitution/cancellation. Both items were initially packed into $P71$, but quality inspection or customer change caused item $I71$ to be removed prior to dispatch, with $I72$ serving as a replacement or the sole surviving line item.
- **Operational Risks & Opportunities**: Severe discrepancy risk: customer was invoiced ($V71$) potentially for the original order while physical delivery ($D71$) contained only $I72$, risking invoice disputes, returns, and margin erosion.
- **Flattening Failure Mode**: Activity-based flat mining sees the exact sequence of X1 (`Create -> Add -> Add -> Pack -> Dispatch -> Invoice -> Payment`) and classifies it as 100% compliant, completely missing the physical cargo alteration.
- **Stability under Data Repair**: Low stability unless relational qualifiers (`removed_before_dispatch`) and fine-grained E2O event payloads are strictly maintained.

---

### Cluster 7: `SEM_RETURN_REWORK_LOOP` (Execution X8)
- **Concise Signature**: `Fulf=2P_2D_MULTI | Settle=1V_1M_FULL | Iso=LOCAL_ISOLATED | Qual=OBJECT_REPLACEMENT | Rev=RETURN_INSPECT_REPACK_LOOP`
- **Business Behavior**: Closed-loop reverse logistics and replacement fulfillment. Item $I82$ was delivered, rejected/returned by the customer, received and inspected, repacked into package $P82$, and redelivered via $D82$ before the order was invoiced and paid.
- **Operational Risks & Opportunities**: High operational cost and lead-time extension. Highlights quality defect or shipping damage patterns. Opportunity to isolate defective suppliers or optimize return triage.
- **Flattening Failure Mode**: Flattening to Order shows a messy rework loop with duplicate pack and dispatch events. Item-centric flattening creates two asymmetric lifecycles ($I81$ clean; $I82$ looped) but loses the order-level hold on invoicing until return resolution.
- **Stability under Data Repair**: Highly stable because dedicated activity labels (`Return Item`, `Inspect Return`) make the rework motif evident even without deep O2O graph mining.

## 5. Borderline cases

### 1. X1 versus X2 (Nominal Symmetry vs String Divergence)
- **Equivalence Disagreement**:
  - *Divergence Under Literal Trace Comparison*: If events are compared as exact strings including raw identifiers (e.g., `Add Item{Order:O1; Item:I11}` vs `Add Item{Order:O2; Item:I22}`), X1 and X2 are strictly non-equivalent.
  - *Agreement Under Typed Isomorphism and Semantic Equivalence*: Under $\sim_{struct}$ and $\sim_{sem}$, X1 and X2 are perfectly identical. The permutation of item creation order (`I11, then I12` vs `I22, then I21`) is an artifact of symmetric set insertion into an unordered collection ($O.items$).
- **Defensible Stance**: In object-centric process mining, local identifier names have zero operational semantics. Equivalence must abstract away raw IDs.

---

### 2. X1 versus X5 (Process Boundary vs Label Identity)
- **Equivalence Disagreement**:
  - *Agreement Under Activity Sequence ($\sim_{act}$)*: Both traces execute the exact 7-step sequence: `Create Order -> Add Item -> Add Item -> Pack Package -> Dispatch -> Issue Invoice -> Record Payment`.
  - *Divergence Under Boundary-Aware Structural and Semantic Relations ($\sim_{struct}, \sim_{sem}$)*: In X5, package $P51$ is a shared multi-order container packing $EXT\text{-}IA$ from $EXT\text{-}OA$.
- **Defensible Stance**: While an isolated view of the order suggests a standard trace, logistics cost and warehouse capacity modeling treat X5 as a co-loading batch operation. Grouping X1 and X5 is valid only for high-level customer milestone reporting, but invalid for operational logistics.

---

### 3. X1 versus X6 (Financial Coupling vs Activity Sequence)
- **Equivalence Disagreement**:
  - *Agreement Under Activity Sequence ($\sim_{act}$) and Logistics Topology*: The physical movement of goods in X6 is 100% identical to X1 (1 local package, 1 local delivery).
  - *Divergence Under Semantic Settlement Equivalence ($\sim_{sem}$)*: X6 shares invoice $V61$ and payment $M61$ with $EXT\text{-}OB$.
- **Defensible Stance**: An operations manager analyzing warehouse pick-pack-ship would cluster X1 and X6 together. A financial auditor or credit risk manager must strictly separate them because payment $M61$ cannot be settled without cross-order clearing.

---

### 4. X1 versus X7 (Hidden Discrepancy vs Activity Sequence)
- **Equivalence Disagreement**:
  - *Agreement Under Activity Sequence ($\sim_{act}$)*: Both exhibit the exact identical sequence of activity labels.
  - *Divergence Under Qualified Semantic Equivalence ($\sim_{sem}$)*: X7 has an explicit qualifier `removed_before_dispatch` on $I71$ and restricted E2O on `Dispatch` ($I72$ only).
- **Defensible Stance**: At the control-flow level, X7 looks completely healthy. At the physical audit level, X7 represents an exception (partial shipment / item loss / substitution). Grouping X1 and X7 masks fulfillment defects.

---

### 5. X3 versus X4 (Consolidated Transport vs Staggered Execution)
- **Equivalence Disagreement**:
  - *Agreement Under Coarse Object Counting*: Both executions involve 1 Order, 2 Items, 2 Packages, and 1 Invoice.
  - *Divergence Under Logistics Topology and Cash Flow*:
    - X3 packs both items into separate boxes ($P31, P32$) but dispatches them together in a single consolidated delivery ($D31$) and settles with 1 payment ($M31$).
    - X4 performs staggered, asynchronous fulfillment ($P41 \to D41$, then $P42 \to D42$) and splits payment into two installments ($M41, M42$).
- **Defensible Stance**: A freight cost model must separate X3 (single drop-off charge) from X4 (multiple courier dispatches and multiple transaction processing fees).

---

### 6. X4 versus X8 (Planned Multi-Shipment vs Unplanned Rework Loop)
- **Equivalence Disagreement**:
  - *Agreement Under Macro-Multiplicities*: Both executions feature 2 Packages, 2 Deliveries, 2 Pack events, and 2 Dispatch events.
  - *Divergence Under Causal Semantics and Quality Lifecycle*:
    - In X4, the 2 packages and 2 dispatches represent planned split forward shipments for distinct items ($I41$ in $P41$; $I42$ in $P42$).
    - In X8, the second package and delivery ($P82, D82$) represent an unplanned reverse-logistics rework cycle for the *same* item ($I82$) following customer return and inspection.
- **Defensible Stance**: X4 is a forward capacity/availability pattern; X8 is a quality failure and rework loop. Equivalence criteria that only count event frequencies would conflate customer backorders with product defects.

## 6. Relation to `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel`

### Analysis of PM4Py Equivalence Mechanism

1. **What PM4Py Key Structure Preserves**:
   `pm4py.algo.clustering.ocel.cluster_equivalent_ocel` constructs equivalence variants for connected components or object-centric subgraphs. By default, it computes a hash key representing:
   - The sequence or multiset of executed activity labels.
   - The set of object types involved in each event.
   - The relative structural connections (E2O type-cardinalities) within the connected object component.

2. **Why Raw Object Identifiers Make Exact Clusters Too Fine**:
   If raw object identifiers ($O1, O2, P11, P21, \dots$) are retained during variant hashing, every single execution forms an isolated singleton cluster ($|Clusters| = |Executions| = 8$). Canonicalization requires quotienting out concrete IDs while preserving isomorphic graph topology and object type roles.

3. **How to Canonicalize / Post-Process Returned Clusters**:
   - *Weisfeiler-Lehman (WL) Graph Hashing*: Convert the bipartite E2O / O2O graph into a colored DAG where initial colors represent `(activity_label)` for events and `(object_type, is_external)` for objects. Perform 2–3 iterations of neighborhood color aggregation and hash the resulting multiset.
   - *Topological Quotienting*: Map local identifiers to deterministic canonical indices based on their appearance order in the topological sort of the execution DAG (e.g., first item $\to I_1$, second item $\to I_2$).

4. **When Exact Equivalence is Preferable to Coarser Business Clustering**:
   - *Regulatory Compliance & Audit*: Verifying strict execution conformance against binding legal protocols (e.g., verifying that no unauthorized external objects touched the transaction).
   - *Automated Regression Testing*: Validating that software-driven workflow engines or ERP automations produce strictly deterministic execution graphs down to the exact E2O incidence structure.

5. **Impact of `max_objs` and Large Connected Executions**:
   In enterprise logs, shared objects (e.g., a shared delivery truck carrying 500 orders, or a shared corporate invoice) trigger the "object explosion / giant component" problem, where thousands of orders become transitively connected into a single massive graph. 
   - Without bounding parameters like `max_objs` or depth-bounded ego-graph filtering around the lead object, graph isomorphism checks become computationally intractable (NP-hard subgraph isomorphism).
   - `max_objs` truncates large components, but arbitrary truncation risks severing cross-order relations (e.g., cutting $EXT\text{-}OA$ in X5), leading to inconsistent cluster assignments.

### Executable Python Implementation

The following script loads an OCEL, extracts subgraphs centered on lead `Order` objects, canonicalizes local identifiers, executes structural equivalence clustering, and maps clusters to business semantics.

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd
from collections import defaultdict

def canonicalize_order_execution(ocel: pm4py.OCEL, lead_order_id: str) -> str:
    """
    Extracts the connected execution graph for a lead Order,
    anonymizes local IDs, preserves external flags, qualifiers,
    and returns a deterministic canonical string key.
    """
    # 1. Filter events related to lead order and its immediate objects
    events_df = ocel.events
    relations_df = ocel.relations
    
    # Get all objects linked to this order
    order_relations = relations_df[relations_df["ocel:oid"] == lead_order_id]
    linked_event_ids = set(order_relations["ocel:eid"].unique())
    
    sub_relations = relations_df[relations_df["ocel:eid"].isin(linked_event_ids)]
    connected_objects = set(sub_relations["ocel:oid"].unique())
    
    # Partition into local vs external
    ext_objects = {oid for oid in connected_objects if str(oid).startswith("EXT")}
    
    # Sort events by timestamp
    sub_events = events_df[events_df["ocel:eid"].isin(linked_event_ids)].sort_values(by="ocel:timestamp")
    
    # 2. Build Canonical Event-Object Signature
    event_signatures = []
    obj_role_map = {}
    type_counters = defaultdict(int)
    
    for _, ev in sub_events.iterrows():
        eid = ev["ocel:eid"]
        act = ev["ocel:activity"]
        
        # Get participating objects for this event
        ev_objs = sub_relations[sub_relations["ocel:eid"] == eid]
        
        typed_roles = []
        for _, row in ev_objs.iterrows():
            oid = row["ocel:oid"]
            otype = row["ocel:type"]
            is_ext = oid in ext_objects
            
            if is_ext:
                canon_id = f"EXT_{otype}"
            else:
                if oid not in obj_role_map:
                    type_counters[otype] += 1
                    obj_role_map[oid] = f"{otype}_{type_counters[otype]}"
                canon_id = obj_role_map[oid]
            
            # Capture qualifiers if present in relations schema
            qual = row.get("ocel:qualifier", "none")
            typed_roles.append(f"{otype}:{canon_id}({qual})")
            
        typed_roles.sort()
        ev_sig = f"{act}[{','.join(typed_roles)}]"
        event_signatures.append(ev_sig)
        
    return " -> ".join(event_signatures)

def cluster_order_executions(ocel: pm4py.OCEL) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Groups all orders in the OCEL into canonical equivalence clusters.
    """
    orders = ocel.objects[ocel.objects["ocel:type"] == "Order"]["ocel:oid"].unique()
    
    cluster_records = []
    for order_id in orders:
        canon_sig = canonicalize_order_execution(ocel, order_id)
        cluster_records.append({
            "order_id": order_id,
            "canonical_signature": canon_sig
        })
        
    df_clusters = pd.DataFrame(cluster_records)
    # Assign cluster IDs based on unique signature hashing
    sig_to_cluster = {sig: f"CLUSTER_{i+1}" for i, sig in enumerate(df_clusters["canonical_signature"].unique())}
    df_clusters["cluster_id"] = df_clusters["canonical_signature"].map(sig_to_cluster)
    
    return df_clusters
```

## 7. Robustness analysis

We evaluate the stability of cluster assignments under five systematic data perturbations:

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    SUMMARY OF PERTURBATION COLLAPSE DYNAMICS                                |
+-----------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Perturbation          | Resulting Cluster Merges & Semantic Information Lost                                |
+-----------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. Ignore Qualifiers  | X7 merges into {X1, X2}. Physical item removal / discrepancy is lost.               |
| 2. Ignore External    | X5 merges into {X1, X2} (logistics); X6 merges into {X1, X2} (finance).             |
| 3. Collapse Repeats   | X3 merges into {X1, X2}; X4 and X8 collapse into simplified single-cycle templates. |
| 4. Partial Order      | X4 merges concurrent pack-dispatch paths; invariant to thread interleaving jitter.  |
| 5. Amount Attribute   | X4 payment leg collapses to single settlement; clusters purely by logistics.        |
+-----------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. Ignore All O2O Qualifiers
- **Impact on Assignments**: 
  - Execution $X7$ loses its `removed_before_dispatch` qualifier and `replaces` semantic tag. Because its activity sequence and raw object types match baseline, **$X7$ collapses completely into the baseline cluster $\{X1, X2\}$**.
  - In $X8$, the relation indicating $P82$ `replaces` $P81$ for item $I82$ is discarded. However, $X8$ remains isolated from baseline due to its distinct activity labels (`Return Item`, `Inspect Return`).
- **Semantic Loss**: Inability to detect silent physical fulfillment discrepancies, phantom inventory dispatches, and warranty item swaps.

### 2. Ignore External Objects (Erase `EXT-*` Boundaries)
- **Impact on Assignments**:
  - In $X5$, stripping $EXT\text{-}OA$ and $EXT\text{-}IA$ leaves only $O5, I51, I52, P51, D51, V51, M51$. Consequently, **$X5$ collapses directly into $\{X1, X2\}$**.
  - In $X6$, removing $EXT\text{-}OB$ makes $V61$ and $M61$ appear as purely local 1:1 billing objects, causing **$X6$ to collapse directly into $\{X1, X2\}$**.
- **Semantic Loss**: Total blindness to shared logistical capacity, co-loading freight efficiency, joint credit liabilities, and multi-party financial risk.

### 3. Collapse Repeated Same-Label Events (Set/Idempotent Abstraction)
- **Impact on Assignments**:
  - In $X3$, collapsing adjacent `Pack Package` events ($\langle \text{Pack}, \text{Pack} \rangle \to \langle \text{Pack} \rangle$) and multiple `Add Item` events makes the trace $\langle \text{Create}, \text{Add}, \text{Pack}, \text{Dispatch}, \text{Invoice}, \text{Payment} \rangle$. **$X3$ collapses into $\{X1, X2\}$**.
  - In $X4$, collapsing repeated `Pack`, `Dispatch`, and `Record Payment` events reduces $X4$ to a simple single-stage fulfillment and settlement sequence.
  - In $X8$, the second `Pack` and `Dispatch` events collapse into the first, leaving $\langle \text{Create}, \text{Add}, \text{Pack}, \text{Dispatch}, \text{Return}, \text{Inspect}, \text{Invoice}, \text{Payment} \rangle$.
- **Semantic Loss**: Erases all capacity planning insights, split packaging tracking, parcel count metrics, installment billing monitoring, and repeat rework counters.

### 4. Treat Pack and Dispatch as a Causal Partial Order (Poset Equivalence)
- **Impact on Assignments**:
  - In $X4$, the total sequence logs an interleaved execution: $\text{Pack}(P41) \to \text{Dispatch}(P41) \to \text{Pack}(P42) \to \text{Dispatch}(P42)$.
  - Under causal partial order semantics ($e_1 \prec e_2 \iff e_1, e_2 \text{ share an object}$), the lifecycles of $P41$ and $P42$ are two completely independent concurrent branches:
    $$\text{Branch 1}: \text{Pack}(P41) \prec \text{Dispatch}(P41)$$
    $$\text{Branch 2}: \text{Pack}(P42) \prec \text{Dispatch}(P42)$$
  - Any arbitrary logging interleaving (e.g., $\text{Pack}(P41) \to \text{Pack}(P42) \to \text{Dispatch}(P41) \to \text{Dispatch}(P42)$) evaluates to the exact same partial order DAG.
- **Semantic Gain/Stability**: Guarantees robust clustering invariant to CPU scheduling, network logging latency, or independent warehouse operator timing.

### 5. Treat Payment Multiplicity as an Amount Attribute (Financial Aggregation)
- **Impact on Assignments**:
  - In $X4$, the two `Record Payment` events ($M41, M42$) are aggregated into a single net settlement attribute ($\sum \text{amount} = \text{total}$).
  - $X4$ shifts from a "Multi-Installment" cluster to a "Split-Shipment / Net-Settled" cluster, isolating fulfillment fragmentation as its sole distinguishing factor.
- **Semantic Trade-Off**: Simplifies financial auditing to balance sheet reconciliation while sacrificing cash-flow velocity and customer installment delay analytics.

## 8. Limitations and recommendation

### Recommended Equivalence Levels by Operational Purpose

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                RECOMMENDED EQUIVALENCE NOTIONS BY USE CASE                                  |
+--------------------------+------------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
| Use Case                 | Recommended Equivalence Level            | Primary Driver                        |
+--------------------------+------------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
| Compliance Monitoring    | Qualified Structural Equivalence         | Boundary integrity & edge qualifiers  |
| Operational Segmentation | Multi-Aspect Business-Semantic Equivalence| Actionable macro-motifs (co-pack/split)|
| Root-Cause Analysis      | Fine-Grained Qualified Execution Graph   | Discrepancy & rework localization     |
| Descriptive Reporting    | Milestone Lifecycle Equivalence          | Noise-tolerant throughput tracking    |
+--------------------------+------------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
```

1. **For Compliance Monitoring: *Qualified Structural Equivalence under Local Identifier Renaming***
   - *Rationale*: Compliance mandates zero-tolerance verification of process boundaries, segregation of duties, and data integrity. It must detect when an order shares an invoice with an external entity (X6), when items are removed/substituted without authorization (X7), or when unauthorized external cargo enters a package (X5).
   - *Required Features*: Strict preservation of $O_{ext}$ boundary flags, O2O qualifiers, and E2O incidence.

2. **For Operational Segmentation: *Multi-Aspect Business-Semantic Equivalence (Our Preferred Relation)***
   - *Rationale*: Operations leaders need actionable cohort segmentation to manage cost-to-serve, freight carrier contracts, warehouse packing line balancing, and working capital optimization.
   - *Required Features*: Partitions the space cleanly into meaningful operational strategies: Baseline ($X1, X2$), Split-Pack Consolidated ($X3$), Staggered Multi-Shipment ($X4$), Shared Co-Packing ($X5$), Joint Billing ($X6$), Physical Discrepancy ($X7$), and Reverse Logistics Rework ($X8$).

3. **For Root-Cause Analysis: *Fine-Grained Qualified Topology & Execution Graph Equivalence***
   - *Rationale*: Investigating why an order missed its SLA or incurred margin loss requires pinpointing the exact failure mechanism—differentiating whether an extra package was caused by item dimensional constraints (X3), multi-location routing (X4), pre-dispatch damage removal (X7), or post-delivery return rework (X8).
   - *Required Features*: Causal partial order graphs, explicit failure/replacement qualifiers, and object lifecycle state transitions.

4. **For Descriptive Reporting: *Milestone / Activity-Lifecycle Equivalence (Coarse Quotient)***
   - *Rationale*: Executive dashboards and high-level KPI tracking require noise-free summaries of order throughput time from placement to cash. Overly fine graph clusterings overwhelm management with dozens of minor technical variants.
   - *Required Features*: Aggregates $\{X1, X2, X5, X6, X7\}$ into a single "Linear Standard Order" bucket, highlighting only structural exceptions like Split Shipments ($X3, X4$) and Returns ($X8$).

### Why No Single Equivalence Notion Suffices

A single universal equivalence notion is fundamentally unattainable in object-centric process mining due to the inherent trade-offs across four competing dimensions:

1. **The Granularity vs Interpretability Dilemma**:
   Strict graph isomorphism preserves every structural detail but fragments enterprise logs into thousands of micro-variants (e.g., separating $X1$ from $X5$, $X6$, and $X7$), making executive reporting impossible. Conversely, coarse activity-sequence projection groups $X1, X5, X6,$ and $X7$ together, hiding critical financial and physical fulfillment risks.

2. **Boundary Sensitivity vs Local Autonomy**:
   Real-world business processes operate in open ecosystems where objects interact across organizational boundaries. An equivalence relation that strictly includes external objects risks the *giant connected component* problem (where one shared invoice links thousands of unrelated orders). An equivalence relation that discards external objects treats shared logistics and joint financing as standard isolated orders, masking systemic supply chain and credit dependencies.

3. **Interleaving Artifacts vs Causal Concurrency**:
   Sequencing events along a 1D timestamp axis introduces artificial orderings between independent parallel operations (e.g., adding item 1 vs item 2, or packing package 1 vs package 2). Total order relations overfit to logging noise, while fully unordered bag abstractions lose the critical causal sequence required to distinguish forward multi-shipment (X4) from reverse rework loops (X8).

4. **Data Quality and Qualifier Dependence**:
   Advanced semantic clustering relies heavily on the richness of O2O qualifiers (`removed_before_dispatch`, `replaces`, `partially_settles`). In mature ERP logs, these qualifiers provide powerful diagnostic precision; in poorly instrumented logs lacking relational metadata, strict reliance on qualifiers fails, necessitating robust topological fallbacks.

Therefore, an effective object-centric process mining architecture must implement a **parameterized multi-level equivalence hierarchy**, allowing practitioners to dynamically select the abstraction level that matches their specific analytical, operational, or regulatory mandate.
