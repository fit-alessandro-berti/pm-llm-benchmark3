## 1. System boundary and assumptions

### Environmental Scope
The system boundary encompasses the internal industrial spare-parts distribution operations, bounded upstream by the **Receive order** event and downstream by the **Confirm delivery** / **Close** events. 

*   **Inside the boundary (Direct & Semi-Direct Operations):**
    *   **Energy:** Electricity and thermal energy consumed during production and heat treatment across machine groups (`CNC_A`, `CNC_B`, `heat_treat`, `inspection_cell`, emergency production).
    *   **Transport:** Scope 3 upstream/downstream distribution emissions (`transport_kgco2e`) mapped to route modes (`parcel_ground`, `parcel_air`, `dedicated_van`, `consolidated_truck`).
    *   **Materials:** Raw material scrap generated during production (`material_scrap_kg`), packaging material weight (`package_material_kg`), and packaging rework loops.
    *   **Quality/Rework:** Material waste, energy, and transport overhead caused by internal rework loops (`rework_flag`), inspection failures, and packaging mismatches.
    *   **Obsolescence:** Material waste resulting from over-produced parts in large batches that sit in stock for >14 days without shipping.

*   **Outside the boundary (Excluded / Exogenous):**
    *   Tier-1 through Tier-N upstream raw material extraction and manufacturing of the base spare parts (cradle-to-gate material creation).
    *   End-of-life disposal or recycling of distributed spare parts.
    *   General facility lighting, HVAC, and administrative building footprints.

### Key Assumptions & Remaining Uncertainty
1.  **Demand Stability:** Incoming order volume (12,000 cases/period) and case-type distributions remain relatively stable, though seasonal or macroeconomic demand shocks could distort batching wait times.
2.  **Attribution of Multi-Item Orders:** The event log is flattened by order. It is assumed that order-level metrics (e.g., `material_scrap_kg`, `energy_kwh`) accurately scale or reflect line-item complexities, particularly for Made-to-Order (MTO) and Critical-downtime segments.
3.  **Uncertainty in Service-Contract Elasticity:** It remains uncertain how strictly Service-contract customers will accept lead-time extensions resulting from green routing policies (e.g., substituting air with ground freight when slack permits).

## 2. Sustainability-related process indicators

To continuously measure trade-offs across operations, we define a multi-criteria indicator set mapped directly to event attributes:

| Dimension | Indicator Name | Event Attribute / Formula | Target / Guardrail |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Energy** | Specific Energy Consumption per MTO Order | `SUM(energy_kwh)` for `case_type = Made-to-order` | $\le 16.0$ kWh/order (optimizing batch-to-wait ratio) |
| **Transport** | Carbon Intensity per Shipment | `SUM(transport_kgco2e) / COUNT(cases)` by `route_mode` | Minimize share of `parcel_air` for non-critical stock |
| **Materials** | Scrap Intensity | `SUM(material_scrap_kg) / COUNT(cases)` | $< 0.10$ kg/order for Standard-stock; $< 0.80$ kg for MTO |
| **Packaging** | Packaging Material Efficiency | `SUM(package_material_kg)` normalized by part weight/route | Balance damage reduction vs. package mass |
| **Rework** | Rework Loop Rate | `COUNT(rework_flag == 1) / COUNT(cases)` | $< 5\%$ across all segments |
| **Batching** | Batch Obsolescence Ratio | Cases un-shipped at 14 days / Total batch-produced cases | $\le 2\%$ |
| **Service** | On-Time Delivery Rate (OTD) | `1 - (COUNT(late_delivery) / COUNT(cases))` | $\ge 90\%$ for Regular; $\ge 95\%$ for Service-contract |
| **Quality** | Post-Packaging Return/Damage Rate | Frequency of `Package standard/reinforced -> Rework packaging` | $< 2\%$ |
| **Resilience** | Alternative Route Availability | Count of active routes per customer segment during carrier disruption | $\ge 1$ viable alternative per R1 policy |

## 3. Intervention options

### S1. Green shipping recommender using promised lead time, route reliability, and customer segment
*   **Target path or segment:** Standard-stock Service-contract and Regular orders currently using `parcel_air` despite high schedule slack (`promised_lead_time_hours`).
*   **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Reroutes eligible shipments from carbon-intensive `parcel_air` to `parcel_ground` or `consolidated_truck` when slack exceeds safety thresholds, eliminating 4–6x emissions per order.
*   **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Zero service deterioration if slack is respected; maintains resilience by preserving air for true low-slack exceptions.
*   **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Joint distribution of `promised_lead_time_hours`, actual cycle time, `route_mode`, and late delivery flags by customer segment.

### S2. Dynamic batching for CNC and heat treatment with maximum waiting-time caps
*   **Target path or segment:** Made-to-order paths utilizing `CNC_A` and `heat_treat` (currently facing up to 38h batch waits).
*   **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Caps maximum batch wait times to prevent over-accumulation while retaining energy efficiency sweet spots (batch sizes $\ge 12$), curbing obsolescence-driven material waste.
*   **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Reduces median and p90 cycle times for MTO cases, lowering late delivery rates from 18% to $<12\%$, with a negligible increase in per-item energy consumption.
*   **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Correlation between `batch_size`, `energy_kwh`, batch wait duration, and 14-day obsolescence/scrap flags.

### S3. Shift Made-to-order production from CNC_B to CNC_A when queue and due-date slack allow
*   **Target path or segment:** MTO cases currently routed to `CNC_B` (which suffers from an 18% rework rate vs. 9% on `CNC_A`).
*   **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Eliminates downstream rework loops (`Rework production -> CNC production`), directly saving the energy and material scrap ($1.8$ kg/order) associated with failed parts.
*   **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Improves quality and reduces p90 cycle times by avoiding rework bottlenecks; requires queue balancing to prevent overloading `CNC_A`.
*   **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Machine group transition frequencies (`CNC_B` vs `CNC_A`), `rework_flag`, and `material_scrap_kg` per case type.

### S4. Replace air for Standard-stock Service-contract orders with priority pick + ground
*   **Target path or segment:** Standard-stock Service-contract orders (where SP1 policy is currently violated in 22% of cases).
*   **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Exploits internal process acceleration (priority picking) to absorb transit time differences, shifting high-emission air parcels to ground or consolidated lanes.
*   **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Preserves contractually mandated service levels while slashing transport emissions (`transport_kgco2e`) for the 16% segment share.
*   **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Conformance rates against policy SP1 and customer segment late-delivery tracking.

### S5. Consolidate truck departures twice daily for non-urgent stock orders
*   **Target path or segment:** Stock -> standard pack -> ground/truck paths (non-urgent Standard-stock Regular orders).
*   **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Replaces individual low-capacity ground parcels with consolidated truck runs, cutting transport kgCO2e by 35–55%.
*   **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Adds a 12–24h consolidation wait, slightly increasing median cycle time without breaching promised lead times for regular stock.
*   **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `route_mode` transitions, consolidation wait times, and delivery confirmation timestamps.

### S6. Improve packaging-route compatibility checks before shipment
*   **Target path or segment:** Paths resulting in `Rework packaging` (occurring in 260 standard and 360 reinforced packaging cases).
*   **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Eliminates redundant packaging loops and wasted packaging materials (`package_material_kg`) caused by route-incompatible packaging choices.
*   **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Eliminates internal packaging loops, improving process flow velocity and reducing damage-related returns.
*   **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Frequency of `Rework packaging` events and `package_material_kg` consumption per order.

### S7. Add inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations
*   **Target path or segment:** MTO production paths flowing through `inspection_cell` before packaging.
*   **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Catches defects early before heavy reinforced packaging is applied and before parts enter downstream rework loops, reducing material waste and wasted packaging.
*   **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Shortens overall MTO cycle times by preventing late-stage defect discovery; enhances overall product quality.
*   **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Transition frequencies from `Inspection` to `Rework production`, scrap weight, and inspection cell queue lengths.

### S8. Replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes
*   **Target path or segment:** Critical-downtime Service-contract orders utilizing `dedicated_van` (where SP2 justification is missing in 31% of cases).
*   **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Mitigates the high emissions and empty-return inefficiencies of dedicated vans by partnering with shared, certified emergency courier networks.
*   **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Maintains strict SLA performance for critical downtime while improving vehicle utilization and reducing emissions.
*   **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** SP2 compliance logs, `route_mode` distribution, and Critical-downtime late-delivery rates.

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1.  **Batching Rebound (Obsolescence vs. Energy):** Enforcing strict maximum waiting-time caps (S2) to curb inventory obsolescence may reduce batch sizes on CNC machines, slightly increasing energy consumption per item (`energy_kwh`), trading off direct machine energy efficiency for reduced material waste.
2.  **Transport Displacement (Consolidation vs. Expedited Air):** Consolidating truck shipments twice daily (S5) reduces transport emissions per parcel, but if local transit legs miss the consolidation window, customers may demand emergency re-shipments via air, resulting in a net negative emissions outcome.
3.  **Packaging Displacement (Material Weight vs. Damage Returns):** Over-correcting packaging policies to minimize `package_material_kg` can reduce container robustness, inadvertently triggering higher transit damage rates that require complete re-manufacture and heavy transport replacement.
4.  **Rework Displacement (Speed vs. Quality):** Rushing inspection cells to clear backlogs and improve cycle times increases defect escape rates, shifting defects downstream into `Rework packaging` or customer-site failures that demand carbon-intensive emergency returns and replacements.
5.  **Customer Behavior Rebound (Service-Contract Gaming):** If Service-contract customers perceive that green routing (S1/S4) prioritizes ground over air, they may preemptively flag all orders as "Critical-downtime" or abuse emergency exceptions to force dedicated transport, bypassing sustainability controls.
6.  **Resilience Displacement (Single-Machine Bottlenecks):** Shifting all MTO production from `CNC_B` to `CNC_A` (S3) to eliminate rework successfully reduces scrap, but concentrates load on `CNC_A`, increasing vulnerability to machine breakdowns and eroding system resilience.

## 6. Balanced recommendation

We recommend a phased, coherent redesign package named **"Lean-Green Flow Optimization"**, structured around three synchronized pillars:

1.  **Precision Routing & Policy Conformance (S1, S4, S8):** 
    Enforce sustainability policies SP1 and SP2 programmatically. Implement the green shipping recommender to systematically divert Standard-stock Service-contract and Regular orders from `parcel_air` to ground/consolidated lanes when slack allows. Replace un-justified dedicated vans with shared certified emergency courier lanes for critical parts, preserving SLA resilience without maintaining empty-return footprints.
2.  **Upstream Process & Quality Stabilization (S3, S6, S7):** 
    Rebalance MTO workload away from high-rework `CNC_B` toward `CNC_A` to eradicate the primary source of material scrap (saving ~1.8 kg/order). Install rigorous inspection quality gates before packaging and automate packaging-route compatibility checks to eliminate `Rework packaging` loops and material waste.
3.  **Controlled Dynamic Batching (S2, S5):** 
    Implement dynamic batching with strict waiting-time caps for CNC and heat treatment operations. This captures energy efficiency sweet spots while eliminating the 14-day obsolescence trap that wastes 6% of batch-produced items. Pair this with twice-daily truck consolidation for non-urgent stock orders.

### Why it Balances Trade-offs:
*   **Environmental:** Slashes transport carbon emissions (by 30–50%) and material scrap (by 20–35%) without relying on blunt, service-destroying bans.
*   **Service & Quality:** Protects Service-contract and Critical-downtime SLAs by utilizing internal process speed (priority picking) to absorb transit shifts, while dropping MTO late-delivery rates from 18% to below 12%.
*   **Resilience:** Retains air freight and dedicated transport strictly as governed exceptions for genuine low-slack/critical events, ensuring the distribution network maintains robust fail-safes against carrier disruptions (satisfying R1).

## 7. Validation and governance plan

Post-implementation, the industrial distributor will govern and validate the redesign using event-log mining and strict policy oversight:

*   **Conformance Monitoring for Policies (SP1, SP2, Q1):**
    *   Continuously mine event logs to track SP1 violation rates (target: $< 2\%$ for Standard-stock Service-contract orders).
    *   Monitor SP2 compliance by automatically flagging any `dedicated_van` event lacking a preceding valid Critical-downtime justification event (target: 0% un-justified occurrences).
    *   Track Q1 compliance by measuring Made-to-order heavy parts (>8 kg) utilizing reinforced packaging against inspection clearance flags.
*   **Segment-Level Service Monitoring:**
    *   Track median and p90 cycle times alongside late delivery rates weekly across all five customer-segment combinations. Any segment exhibiting a >5% degradation in OTD triggers an automated review of shipping recommender slack thresholds.
*   **Exception Governance & Sustainability Exception Log (S12):**
    *   Institute a formal exception log where operators or customers must log justifications for overriding green shipping recommendations (SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides). 
    *   Process-mine this exception log monthly to identify recurring operational bottlenecks, customer friction points, or systemic forecasting errors that force carbon-intensive workarounds.
