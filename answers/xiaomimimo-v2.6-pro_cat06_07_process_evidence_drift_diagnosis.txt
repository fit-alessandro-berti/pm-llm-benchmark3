**T3 — Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source and month.** Report AUROC, Brier, ECE, and **bias (mean predicted  matured rate)** per month × variant × source, on matured labels only, with bootstrap intervals. *Under H-concept*, V3 shows a growing negative bias from Jul (e.g. 20 pp in Aug–Sep) with AUROC collapsing **within** V3, while V1/V2 remain unbiased and at baseline AUROC. *Under H-mix/H-pipe*, the bias is uniform across variants and disappears after feature repair. *Under H-pop*, the residual bias is concentrated in the Partner Z / rural / old-device cells **after controlling for variant**, and non-Z V3 is fine.

**T4 — Variant and DFG / performance-DFG comparison.** Compare monthly `get_variants` distributions (variant share, entropy, new/unmapped variant count), `discover_dfg` (edge existence, start/end activities) and `discover_performance_dfg` medians per edge, split by month and by source. *Under H-mix*, the **edge inventory is unchanged** and only weights move (Jun: `DiagnosisRemote Fix` weight up), while per-edge performance is flat. *Under H-concept/supplier*, the **`Order PartPart Received` performance edge** degrades monotonically (354265130148155 h) and V3's total cycle-time distribution right-shifts; the remote edge stays 3.8–4.6 h. *Under H-pipe*, April shows spurious edges/`UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` nodes and inflated path lengths that vanish after normalization.

**T5 — Conditional-outcome stability: did P(Y|X, path) change?** On matched strata (variant × part-required × device-age band × rural band × supplier), estimate the breach rate per stratum in a reference window (Jan–Mar, Apr–May, Jun–Jul) versus Aug–Sep, with a chi-square / logistic model including period indicators and interactions. *Under H-concept*, the period effect is significant **within the same stratum** for V3/part-required (odds ratio  1, e.g. +0.5–1.0 on log-odds) and 1 for V1/V2  the same at-diagnosis X now yields a different outcome. *Under H-mix + H-pop only*, the period coefficient vanishes once variant and source are controlled. *Under H-pipe*, a significant period effect appears for Apr–May but is removed by feature recomputation.

**T6 — Population reweighting / transport check for Partner Z.** Build weights so the Aug (pre-Z) population matches the Sep–Oct population on variant × rurality × device age × volume; recompute Sep–Oct metrics under those weights, and conversely transport Jan–Mar-trained evaluation onto the Z covariate support (importance weighting / overlap weights, report effective sample size and max weight). *Under H-pop*, reweighting removes most of the Sep aggregate bias and the Partner Z vs non-Z gap shrinks to near zero **within variant**. *Under H-concept*, reweighting barely helps because V3 breaches regardless of source (non-Z V3 also at 0.6). *Under H-pipe*, the Z/non-Z gap is driven by label-delay differences and disappears under fixed-vintage (T1) evaluation.

**T7 — Feature missingness and lineage audit.** Per month, per feature: null rate, fill/derivation code version, upstream source table, event-label lineage, staleness of caches, and the recomputation timestamp; produce a lineage hash diff between "as-scored" and "as-rebuilt." *Under H-pipe*, April/May show a lineage break exactly at the 1 Apr migration and 6 May patch, and the cached event-count feature's as-scored  as-rebuilt for April, with the delta correlating with duplicate count per case. *Under H-concept/H-pop*, lineage is clean for Jul–Oct and feature statistics are stable except for the new source attributes (which are structurally absent).

**T8 — Prediction-coverage / prefix-construction audit.** Count per month the share of cases with a successfully built prediction at `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, the index of the event at which scoring actually fired, and the share scored after the true decision point. *Under H-pipe*, April shows a coverage collapse and a shifted scoring index (scoring at `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` or after `Dispatch Technician`), which induces selection and look-ahead effects on AUROC. *Under H-concept*, coverage and scoring index are normal for Jul–Sep. A coverage shift alone can depress AUROC through range restriction and must be separated from model degradation.

**T9 — Outcome-API / label-observation audit.** Measure label-arrival latency distribution by variant, source and path length; test whether missingness of labels is predictable from path length (logistic test of "label present at cutoff" on V3, cycle time, Partner Z). *Under H-cens*, label presence is strongly predicted by path length and variant  MNAR; October's labelled subset is disproportionately V2 (consistent with 0.16 being unattainable under the true mix). *Under "labels missing at random"*, the labelled subset's variant mix matches the full month's mix and the 0.16 is credible — which the mixture arithmetic strongly contradicts.

**T10 — Capacity/throughput hypothesis (competing explanation).** Compare case arrival rate, work-in-progress, technician utilisation and dispatch-to-repair latency against part lead-time. *Under a capacity/saturation story*, dispatchrepair and triage latencies degrade in parallel with the part edge and volume spikes; *under H-concept (supplier)*, only the part edge degrades while dispatchrepair stays flat — the supplied evidence (remote edge flat, part edge 4–5×) currently favours the supplier story. **[H]**

**T11 — Intervention confound check.** Overlay deployment/policy dates (1 Apr migration, 6 May patch, 1 Jun routing policy, promise-date or dispatch-rule changes) on every metric series. *Under "remediation-induced change"*, metric inflections coincide with interventions rather than with supplier/partner events; *under genuine drift*, inflections track the 10 Jul supplier event and the 1 Sep onboarding.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Stage 0 — Immediate containment (now).**
- **Quarantine October** (and flag Aug–Sep as provisional) in all performance dashboards; show matured-only and maturity-adjusted metrics side by side with the maturity percentage always adjacent.
- **Freeze global recalibration and retraining** while the pipeline/label states are red (per §5 dependency rules). Calibrating now would encode April's feature corruption and October's censoring into the production model.
- **Deterministic safety net for V3/parts-dependent cases**: since the model under-predicts exactly this segment (Aug–Sep V3 matured breach 0.61–0.64 vs model 0.19–0.21 mean), apply a segment override — escalate all `part-required` cases, revise promised resolution dates for parts cases, and enforce supplier SLA escalation. This is proportionate: it is a business rule, reversible, and does not bake in unverified assumptions.
- Add an ingestion guard: if the canonical activity mapping misses an activity or `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` share exceeds threshold, **block scoring and emit no prediction** rather than scoring on corrupted prefixes (April's real damage came from silently scoring).

**Stage 1 — Short-term recalibration (1–3 weeks, after T2/T7 pass).**
- Rebuild Apr–May features (T2) and validate recovery to baseline. Months safe for evaluation/training after repair: **Jan–Mar (safe now); Apr (safe only after full recomputation — treat as safe only if T2 restores baseline); May (safe after backfill of the cached event count); Jun–Jul (safe and the most valuable recent data, but must be handled as post-policy mix).**
- If T3 shows the residual bias is **variant-shaped and stable within variant**, a **variant-aware recalibration** is justified and sufficient: per-segment intercept/recalibration map (or isotonic on matured data) keyed on the **`part-required` flag captured at diagnosis** plus supplier history. A single global recalibration must be rejected — it would average the V3 shift (real) with the V2 stability and leave the segment that is actually breaching under-predicted.
- June's error is a calibration/prior problem and should be closed by recalibration alone, **not** retraining.

**Stage 2 — Feature repair and augmentation (2–6 weeks).**
- Retroactive dedupe on both sources; canonical activity registry as code, with a lineage/derivation-version column on every derived feature and an "as-scored vs as-rebuilt" hash diff (T7).
- Add the missing predictive content that the drift exposed: at-diagnosis **`part-required`/expected route**, **supplier ID and rolling on-time delivery rate**, **expected part lead time**, **rural travel distance**, **device age**, **Partner Z/source**, **service-volume/queue pressure**. These are the variables that make the post-diagnosis hazard partially predictable at scoring time; without them any retraining will be "correct but uninformative" and will fail again at the next supplier shock.

**Stage 3 — Full retraining (after label closure, realistically mid-Dec 2026 + tail).**
- Train only on **label-mature cohorts**: Jan–Jul fully safe now; **Aug–Sep usable only after T1 fixed-vintage/IPCW validation** (they are needed — they contain the only examples of the new regime — so do not discard them, but weight by maturity); **Oct excluded entirely** until its labels close, then used as a hold-out *test* set rather than training data.
- Because the drift is concentrated in one segment, prefer **one model with explicit variant/part-required interactions and a V3-specific sub-model (or two-head: V2/remote vs parts-dependent)** over six independent segment models. **Caveat that governs this choice:** at scoring time the *realized* variant is not yet known (V3 is only defined once `Order Part` occurs) — gating on the realized variant would leak post-decision information. Gating must use the at-diagnosis `part-required`/planned-route flag. If that flag is unreliable or unavailable, **do not** build variant-gated models; build one model with interactions and accept a small calibration loss.
- Evaluate with a **segment-first scorecard** (calibration and bias per variant/source/month) and require per-segment non-degradation versus the incumbent before release; deploy as shadow  canary with automatic rollback triggered by segment-level calibration alarms.

**Which months are safe/unsafe (summary).** Safe for training now: **Jan, Feb, Mar**. Safe after feature recomputation: **Apr, May**. Safe with variant interactions and post-policy caveats: **Jun, Jul**. Use with maturity weighting and only after T1/T5: **Aug, Sep**. **Unsafe/unusable until labels close: Oct** (use as future hold-out).

## 5. Monitoring architecture

**Monitors (layers).**

| Layer | Monitors | Core signals | Primary owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 Data quality / pipeline (gating) | activity-label registry match rate, `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` share, duplicate rate per source, feature null rate, lineage hash diff (as-scored vs as-rebuilt), prediction-coverage %, scoring-index distribution, schema diff vs canonical registry | unknown %, missing %, unmapped %, prefix-construction failure rate | Data engineering |
| L1 Label / outcome process | label-maturity curve, median/quantile label delay, matured-only vs observed gap, outcome-API queue depth, missingness predictability by path length | maturity %, delay days | Data ops / outcome API owner |
| L2 Predictive | AUROC/PR-AUC/Brier + bootstrap CI, evaluated on matured cohorts only and on fixed vintages |  vs 3-month baseline, CI overlap | Model owner |
| L3 Calibration | ECE, reliability diagram, **bias = mean predicted  matured rate**, per variant/source/month | segment-level bias | Model owner |
| L4 Process-variant | variant share, entropy, new/unmapped variant count, start/end activities (`discover_dfg`) | mix shift beyond control limits | Process owner |
| L5 Performance-DFG | per-edge median/quantile latency (`discover_performance_dfg`), especially `Order PartPart Received` and `DiagnosisRemote Fix` | supplier SLA breach | Supply chain / ops |
| L6 Population | source mix, Partner Z share, PSI/KD on covariates incl. new attributes, OOD/support-coverage score | PSI > threshold, new-source influx | Model owner + business |
| L7 Intervention / deployment | deploy/patch/policy events (1 Apr, 6 May, 1 Jun), promise-date policy, dispatch rules, model version, feature-code version | any change event becomes a mandatory annotation on every other series | MLOps |

**Alert dependency rules (so a label-delay alarm does not cause retraining).**

1. **Gating precedence:** if L0 is red (activity-registry mismatch, unknown share over threshold, lineage hash diff, prediction-coverage collapse), then **L2/L3 alarms are downgraded to "suppressed — suspected pipeline"** and both **recalibration and retraining tickets are blocked**; only a data-engineering incident opens. Rationale: April's real damage.
2. **Maturity gate:** if L1 reports maturity < 90% or median label delay > evaluation window, then L2/L3 readings are marked **provisional**; they may open an **outcome-API/label-latency incident**, and *may not* open a retraining ticket. A retraining ticket requires **two consecutive vintages that have reached  95% maturity** to show the same degradation (or a T1 fixed-vintage test confirming it).
3. **Censoring compensation:** performance and calibration alarms are only evaluated on matured cohorts or with IPCW/fixed-vintage adjustment; the raw "observed breach rate" is a *descriptive* series and is never a trigger.
4. **Process/performance routing:** an L4 mix alert or an L5 performance-edge alert routes to **operations/supply chain first**. It opens a *model* ticket only if the **conditional-outcome test (T5)** also fails within the affected segment. Rationale: June is a routing change, not a model defect.
5. **Population routing:** an L6 alert routes to a **feature/segmentation workstream** (add attributes, run transport checks) and explicitly not to recalibration or to a fairness/debiasing intervention.
6. **Deployment overlay:** every metric series carries L7 annotations; any metric change within ±2 monitoring windows of a deployment/policy event is auto-tagged **"confounded"** and cannot alone trigger model action.
7. **Segment-first escalation:** single-segment degradation (e.g. V3-only bias) escalates as a **segment hypothesis** with a temporary business-rule override, not as a global model failure.

```
L0 data-quality (red)> suppress L2/L3 > data-eng incident only
                  (green)
L1 label maturity (delay/low maturity)> outcome-API incident only
                  (mature & persistent)> may open L2/L3 ticket > T1/T5 gate > recalibrate or retrain
L4 variant + L5 perf-DFG > ops / supply-chain incident (+ T5 fail)> model feature work
L6 population > feature & transport workstream (no recalibration, no fairness action yet)
L7 deployment > annotates & gates all of the above
```

**Monthly process-evidence extraction (audit-side, no model fitting).**

```python
# monthly_process_evidence.py -- auditable process evidence, no model fitting.
# NOTE: pm4py exposes no validated top-level drift detector; drift attribution is done
# outside PM4Py via stratified comparison + PSI/KS/chi-square on these summaries.

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE, ACT, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"

# --- 0. Canonical activity registry (post 1-Apr-2026 migration) --------------
CANON = {
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed",
    "initial_diagnosis_completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed",
    "Initial Diagnosis Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed",
}
DIAG = "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
PART_EDGE = ("Order Part", "Part Received")


def load_events(path="field_service_events.csv") -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = pd.read_csv(path)
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(           # adds case:concept:name / concept:name / time:timestamp
        df, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp"
    )
    # 1) NORMALIZE the renamed diagnosis label BEFORE any feature/prefix work.
    df["activity_raw"] = df[ACT]
    df[ACT] = df[ACT].map(lambda a: CANON.get(str(a).strip(), str(a).strip()))
    # UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY is kept visible and reported, never silently merged into DIAG.
    return df.sort_values([CASE, TS]).reset_index(drop=True)


def deduplicate(df: pd.DataFrame, window_s: int = 60) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Conservative, auditable de-duplication.
    (a) drop byte-identical re-emitted events (same source event id, else same key);
    (b) drop only *near-identical* repeats: same case+activity within `window_s` with the
        same resource -> source re-emission. Genuine repeated attempts (different
        timestamps/resources) are RETAINED. Counts are returned for audit."""
    key = [CASE, ACT, TS] + (["resource"] if "resource" in df.columns else [])
    n0 = len(df)
    out = df.drop_duplicates(subset=key, keep="first")
    n1 = len(out)
    out = out.sort_values([CASE, TS])
    dup_mask = (
        (out[CASE] == out[CASE].shift())
        & (out[ACT] == out[ACT].shift())
        & ((out[TS] - out[TS].shift()).dt.total_seconds().abs() <= window_s)
    )
    out = out[~dup_mask]
    print(f"[dedupe] exact={n0 - n1} near={n1 - len(out)} kept={len(out)}")
    return out.reset_index(drop=True)


def month_windows(lo="2026-01-01", hi="2026-11-01"):
    for t0 in pd.date_range(lo, hi, freq="MS"):
        yield t0.to_pydatetime(), (t0 + pd.offsets.MonthBegin()).to_pydatetime()


def edge_median(pdfg, a, b):
    v = pdfg.get((a, b), None)          # seconds; may be list (raw) or scalar (aggregated)
    if v is None:
        return np.nan
    return float(np.median(v if isinstance(v, (list, tuple, np.ndarray)) else [v])) / 3600.0


def n_of(v):                            # get_variants returns counts or case-id lists
    return len(v) if isinstance(v, (list, tuple, set)) else int(v)


def monthly_summaries(dfm: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    rows = []
    for t0, t1 in month_windows():
        # COHORT A - event-calendar: all events that occurred in the window. Mixes cases;
        #   good for process/throughput health, WRONG for outcome rates.
        ev = pm4py.filter_time_range(dfm, t0, t1, mode="events", timestamp_column=TS)
        # COHORT B - case-start: cases whose FIRST event falls in the window. The correct
        #   cohort for breach-rate / label-maturity / model metrics. (filter_time_range's
        #   mode="cases" is containment-in-window, not case-start -> filter explicitly.)
        starts = dfm.groupby(CASE)[TS].min()
        ids = starts[(starts >= pd.Timestamp(t0)) & (starts < pd.Timestamp(t1))].index
        cs = dfm[dfm[CASE].isin(ids)]

        for cohort, sub in (("event_calendar", ev), ("case_start", cs)):
            if sub.empty:
                continue
            dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_dfg(
                sub, case_id_key=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS
            )
            pdfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
                sub, case_id_key=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS
            )
            var = pm4py.get_variants(sub, case_id_key=CASE, activity_key=ACT)
            total = sum(n_of(v) for v in var.values()) or 1
            v3 = sum(n_of(v) for k, v in var.items() if "Order Part" in k)
            rows.append(
                dict(
                    month=t0.strftime("%Y-%m"),
                    cohort=cohort,
                    n_cases=sub[CASE].nunique(),
                    n_events=len(sub),
                    unknown_share=float((sub[ACT] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean()),
                    unmapped_diagnosis_share=float(
                        (sub["activity_raw"] != sub[ACT]).mean()
                    ),
                    n_variants=len(var),
                    v3_share=v3 / total,
                    part_edge_h=edge_median(pdfg, *PART_EDGE),
                    remote_edge_h=edge_median(pdfg, DIAG, "Remote Fix"),
                    n_edges=len(dfg),
                    start_activities="|".join(map(str, sa)),
                    end_activities="|".join(map(str, ea)),
                )
            )

        # Process-health time series aggregated on the calendar (interpretation:
        # event-calendar, NOT case-level labels; do not use for breach-rate metrics).
        tf = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
            sub, grouper_freq="MS", activity_column=ACT,
            case_id_column=CASE, timestamp_column=TS,
        )
        rows.append(dict(month=t0.strftime("%Y-%m"), cohort="temporal_features",
                         notes=tf.round(3).to_dict(orient="records")))

    out = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    out.to_csv("monthly_process_evidence.csv", index=False)   # auditable artefact
    return out


if __name__ == "__main__":
    dfm = deduplicate(load_events())
    summary = monthly_summaries(dfm)
    print(summary.drop(columns=["notes"], errors="ignore").to_string(index=False))
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

**1. Retraining or recalibrating on immature/censored labels (highest risk).** October's 55% maturity with path-length-dependent missingness means the labelled subset is enriched in short/V2 cases (its 0.16 is arithmetically incompatible with a V3 share of 41% at rates of 0.6). Fitting on it teaches the model "breach risk is falling," shrinking predicted risk precisely as the supplier crisis peaks — the model would be confidently wrong through November–December, when the labels finally mature. Aug–Sep carry the same risk at lower dose. *Mitigation:* quarantine Oct from fitting; use fixed-vintage/IPCW evaluation (T1) and maturity-weighted training; require two matured vintages before opening a retraining ticket; back-test the candidate on closed historical vintages before deployment.

**2. Calibrating around a pipeline bug.** April's +9 pp over-prediction invites an obvious "fix": a global recalibration lowering predicted risk. That would (a) hide the renamed-activity/duplicate/cached-feature bug, (b) encode duplicate-inflated "events before diagnosis" into the calibration map, and (c) push scores *down* just as the real Aug–Sep under-prediction emerges — compounding both errors. Similarly, "fixing" May's AUROC of 0.78 by retraining on May data would teach the model from a corrupted cached feature. *Mitigation:* the L0 pipeline latch must **block both recalibration and retraining** while red (§5 rule 1); any deployed calibration must be validated against a **rebuilt** feature set with an as-scored/as-rebuilt hash diff; add code review and versioning to the activity registry.

**3. Treating Aug–Sep as "just mix shift" and ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift.** If June is correctly diagnosed as a mix change, there is a real temptation to over-generalize and explain August the same way. The decomposition says otherwise: JulAug is 61% conditional-rate effect. A pure recalibration would leave V3 cases at 0.6+ breach probability under-predicted by ~40 pp, so dispatch escalation, promise-date setting and spare-parts planning stay wrong — and, worse, the **operations signal is lost**: the supplier SLA failure (42155 h) would never be escalated as a business incident because "the model is being fixed." *Mitigation:* variant-stratified bias (T3) plus performance-DFG monitors (L5) with an independent routing path to supply chain; only the conditional-outcome test (T5) may classify a change as concept drift; keep the temporary deterministic V3 override until a features-updated model is validated.

**4. Attributing Partner Z's shift to unfair model behaviour without stratified evidence.** The 0.45 vs 0.27 September gap is presented alongside immature labels, a 58% V3 rate and new rural/device-age covariates absent from the model. Declaring bias now risks debiasing/parity constraints that trade away accuracy globally and mask the fact that Partner Z cases are simply routed through the path that is failing for **everyone** (non-Z V3 breaches at a similar rate). The opposite error is equally damaging: dismissing a real coverage gap as "supplier noise" when Partner Z/rural cases have systematically absent features and worse calibration. *Mitigation:* stratified calibration within variant × rurality × device-age × maturity (T3/T6) before any fairness action; report effective sample size under reweighting; add the missing features first and re-measure; scope any parity constraint to the segment where differential calibration survives controls.

**5. Using the wrong cohort and drawing the wrong conclusion from the numbers.** Event-calendar windows mix cases in flight and cannot support breach-rate or calibration claims; case-start cohorts can under-count late-arriving events and, if the reporting cutoff is short relative to the 52 d label delay, silently censor. October's "improvement" is exactly the artefact this produces. *Mitigation:* dual-cohort reporting with explicit interpretation (as in the code above: `event_calendar` = process health, `case_start` = outcomes/metrics); enforce a minimum "label-closure lag" (e.g. 2× median label delay + tail) before any month enters a decision.

**6. Deploying variant-gated/segment-specific models with look-ahead leakage.** V3 is defined by `Order Part  Part Received`, i.e. only after the prediction point, and the 1 Jun policy makes realized route a *decision* variable. Gating a V3-specific model on the realized variant leaks future information into scoring and will show inflated offline AUROC that cannot be reproduced in production. *Mitigation:* gate only on information available at `Initial Diagnosis Completed` (`part-required`/planned route, supplier history); if that flag is unreliable, use a single model with interactions; add a leakage audit to the deployment checklist.

**7. Blaming capacity saturation (or dismissing it) without testing.** Volume rose 64% (3,2005,250) over the period. If queueing saturation is a co-cause of Aug–Sep delay, then model retraining alone will not fix the breaches and the remediation budget is misallocated; if it is not, adding a queue-pressure feature is wasted effort. *Mitigation:* run T10 (arrival rate, WIP, dispatchrepair vs part edge) and treat the result as an operations capacity question with its own owner and SLA. **[H]**
