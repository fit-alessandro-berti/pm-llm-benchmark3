## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

### Causal framework choice
I use **separable effects** (Robins, Richardson, & Shpitser) paired with **interventional direct/indirect effects** (VanderWeele, Vansteelandt, & Robins) and **policy-regime (cluster-level) contrasts** rather than classical Natural Direct/Indirect Effects (NDE/NIE). Three structural features of this process invalidate NDE/NIE:
1. **Compound treatment:** “Setting `flag = 0`” is ill-defined unless decomposed into manipulable policy components—display visibility (`P1`), triage prioritization (`P2`), mandatory manual review (`P2`), manual-review capacity (`P3`), and escalation criteria/capacity (`P4`).
2. **Exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounding (and cross-world violations):** Priority triage shifts *when* a case reaches the manual-review queue, which alters queue congestion and reviewer assignment (`M1`/`M2`/`M3`)—post-treatment variables that confound downstream waiting, escalation, and cycle time. Cross-world independence required for NDE/NIE is neither verifiable nor plausible here.
3. **Shared-queue interference:** One case’s routing changes waiting times for concurrent cases (violating SUTVA). Interventional analogues (evaluating stochastic draws from mediator distributions under specified policies) and system-level policy contrasts remain well-defined without cross-world counterfactuals.

**Interference, exposure-induced confounding, and feedback:**
Interference arises through shared queues: diverting flagged cases into a priority triage lane (`T1`/`T2`) accelerates their arrival into downstream manual review while potentially starving unflagged triage (`T3`/`T4`), and routing 100% of flagged cases to manual review inflates `U_queue_state_manual` (and subsequently `U_queue_state_esc`) for all concurrent cases (`U_flag_cohort_volume`). Exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounding occurs because `flag` alters both triage completion timing and reviewer assignment (`U_reviewer_assignment`, e.g., High-complexity cases routed to `M2`), which in turn jointly affect `manual_queue_wait`, `escalation`, and `cycle_time`; conditioning on `manual_review` or `escalation` also induces collider bias between `flag` and `U_underlying_risk` (unflagged cases enter review only when unobserved risk is high). Treatment–confounder feedback operates dynamically across arrivals: earlier flagged cases accumulate work-in-process in `M1`–`M3` and senior escalation, altering the queue state faced by later-arriving cases in the same shift.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. What is the local causal effect of the flag bundle near the threshold? | Sharp Regression Discontinuity Design (RDD) around the `risk_score` cutoff (~0.62–0.63) using expanded historical logs ($n \ge 500$ near bandwidth) | Local polynomial in `risk_score` within narrow bandwidth (e.g., $\pm 0.05$), stratified by `complexity` and shift | Continuity of `U_underlying_risk`, `complexity`, and arrival rate across the cutoff; no score manipulation by intake staff | McCrary density test of `risk_score` at cutoff; covariate balance test for `complexity`, `arrival_load`, and arrival hour across cutoff | A sharp jump in `manual_queue_wait` and `cycle_time` *without* any jump in audited error/rejection rate proves policy-induced delay; conversely, no discontinuity in waiting implicates smooth risk confounding |
| 2. How much delay is driven by dynamic queue congestion vs. flag-specific gating? | Queue-state-aware survival/duration regression using reconstructed real-time WIP and server occupancy at each stage entry (`triage_done`, `manual_done`) | Real-time WIP in `M1`/`M2`/`M3` and senior queues, hour-of-day/shift, `complexity`, `risk_score`, `review_resource` | Timestamps accurately record queue entry and active work start; conditional ignorability of `flag` given local `risk_score` and exact WIP | Check FIFO compliance (order of `triage_done` vs. `manual_start` within each reviewer pool); test whether `flag` still predicts `manual_queue_wait` at zero WIP | If `flag` still adds 4–5h of delay when `WIP = 0`, the mechanism is non-queue gating/batching/hold policy rather than shared-queue congestion |
| 3. What is the causal effect of removing mandatory manual review (`P2`) or adding capacity (`P3`)? | Randomized encouragement or cluster/time-block policy trial (assigning `P2` vs. `P0` across shifts/days for Medium-complexity flagged cases) | Stratified by shift type (day/night), historical `arrival_load`, and `complexity` | Randomization validity; stable compliance/adherence; well-defined cluster boundaries to contain short-horizon queue carryover | Balance checks across randomized shifts; first-stage compliance check on manual-review rate; washout check between consecutive blocks | If removing mandatory review cuts Medium-flagged cycle time by >4h with no rise in audited error rate, `P2` dominates; if triage officers still refer >85% manually, behavioral anchoring dominates |
| 4. How much of the flag effect is mediated by review occurrence vs. waiting vs. escalation? | Interventional indirect effect / g-formula sequential mediation decomposition (estimated on RDD sample or multi-arm `P0`/`P2`/`P3`/`P4` pilot) | Baseline `risk_score`, `complexity`, shift, plus time-varying post-triage queue state `WIP(t)` and `review_resource` | Sequential ignorability of mediators given observed baseline covariates and time-varying `WIP(t)` (no cross-world independence needed) | Test whether mediator-shift predictions from g-formula match observed stage-level changes in a pilot arm (`P2` or `P3`) | If interventional indirect effect through `manual_queue_wait` is small once `WIP(t)` is held constant while occurrence/escalation dominates, shift focus from capacity (`P3`) to routing (`P2`/`P4`) |
| 5. Are observed wait differences artifacts of unmeasured confounding, timestamps, or shift calendars? | Negative-control outcome (`residual-to-close` if automated, or pre-triage intake lag) and negative-control exposure (placebo `risk_score` cutoffs at 0.45 and 0.80) | Same covariates as primary RDD/regression (`risk_score`, `complexity`, shift) | Negative-control outcome is unaffected by `flag` directly; placebo cutoffs trigger no policy change | Estimate discontinuities at placebo cutoffs (0.45, 0.80) and test `flag` effect on `residual-to-close` (already ~1.12h vs. 1.19h in observed data) | A large discontinuity at a placebo cutoff or a large `flag` effect on an automated closing step would reveal misspecification or unmeasured calendar/batch artifacts |
| 6. Could omitted risk (`U_underlying_risk`) explain the remaining within-complexity gap? | Quantitative bias / tipping-point sensitivity analysis (e.g., Cinelli–Hazlett partial $R^2$ or E-value bounds calibrated against `complexity`) | Benchmark confounder strength against `complexity` and `review_resource` in regressions of `manual_queue_wait` and `escalation` | Plausible bounds on how strongly `U_underlying_risk` can predict both `flag` (near cutoff) and queue wait (rather than service time) | Compare sensitivity of `manual_service` (which directly reflects case difficulty) vs. `manual_queue_wait` (which should not depend strongly on hidden risk under FIFO) | If `U_underlying_risk` would need an implausibly large direct effect on *waiting* (while leaving `manual_service` nearly unchanged: 2.70h vs. 2.75h) to erase the 5h gap, pure risk confounding is refuted |
| 7. How large are cross-case queue spillovers onto unflagged and concurrent flagged cases? | Two-stage or cluster-level randomized saturation design (varying the fraction of flagged cases subject to mandatory review across shifts/days: e.g., 100%, 50%, 0% for Medium cases) | Shift-level arrival volume, staffing headcount, case mix (`complexity` distribution) | Partial interference confined within shift/day clusters (or modeled via decay kernel across adjacent shifts) | Compare unflagged cases’ `triage_wait` and `manual_queue_wait` across high- vs. low-saturation clusters holding staffing fixed | Large reduction in unflagged waiting under low-saturation shifts proves queue interference is first-order and requires system-level (not case-level) optimization |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P0 (status quo) | Fast priority triage for flagged (0.84h), slow for unflagged (3.43h) | High manual-review wait (6.50h flagged) and heavy escalation load (60% flagged) | Long cycle time for flagged (16.51h), especially High (22.40h) and Medium (10.62h) | Baseline screening; potential over-processing of Medium flagged (0/5 rejected) and under-prioritization of unflagged High (2/3 rejected) | Starves unflagged triage under High load; congests shared `M1`–`M3` and senior queues | Moderate descriptive (n=20); weak causal | Baseline comparator; do not keep unchanged |

### Limited Pilot Design: Targeted Policy P2
- **Eligibility:** Flagged cases (`flag = 1`) with `complexity = Medium` and moderate `risk_score` (e.g., `0.63–0.80`). High-complexity cases and `risk_score > 0.80` remain under `P0` mandatory review to protect tail risk.
- **Assignment rule:** Cluster-randomized by **workday/shift block** (50% `P2` / 50% `P0`, stratified by weekday and expected `arrival_load`) over 6–8 weeks (minimum ~300 eligible cases across $\ge 40$ shifts), with a short queue-drain washout between shift transitions to bound interference. In `P2` shifts, priority triage (`T1`/`T2`) continues, but triage officers use structured discretion to approve or refer to manual review rather than facing an automatic mandate.
- **Queue-capacity guardrail:** Keep total `T1`–`T4` and `M1`–`M3` staffing fixed across arms; cap unreviewed closures if `M1`–`M3` backlog drops below minimum utilization, and auto-route any case with anomalous intake alerts directly to manual review.
- **Primary and secondary outcomes:** Primary: (1) total cycle time of eligible cases, and (2) blind post-close quality/compliance error rate (auditing 100% of bypassed approvals and a 25% random sample of `P0` approvals). Secondary: `manual_review` rate, `manual_queue_wait`, escalation rate, and spillover waiting times for High-complexity and unflagged cases.
- **Minimum process data needed:** Exact UTC timestamps for intake, queue entry, start/complete, and pause/resume holds; real-time WIP and active headcount per pool; resource IDs; exact `risk_score` and intake features; blind audit outcomes.
- **Stopping conditions:** Halt `P2` immediately if blind audits show a $>2$ percentage-point increase in severe compliance errors (or one-sided $p < 0.05$ harm signal), if triage referral rates exceed 85% (futility/anchoring), or if unflagged `triage_wait` rises $>25\%$.

    "arrival",
    "triage_start",
    "triage_done",
    "manual_start",
    "manual_done",
    "escalation_start",
    "escalation_done",
    "close",
]

    cases_df[col] = pd.to_datetime(cases_df[col], format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M", errors="coerce")

# 2. Reshape into an event-level dataframe without fabricating absent stages

    base_attrs = {
        "case:concept:name": row.case,
        "case:risk_score": row.risk_score,
        "case:flag": int(row.flag),
        "case:complexity": row.complexity,
        "case:arrival_load": row.arrival_load,
        "case:outcome": row.outcome,
    }
    # Register (instantaneous milestone)
    event_rows.append({
        **base_attrs,
        "concept:name": "Register",
        "start_timestamp": row.arrival,
        "time:timestamp": row.arrival,
        "org:resource": "SYSTEM",
    })
    # Triage (observed for all cases)
    if pd.notna(row.triage_start) and pd.notna(row.triage_done):
        event_rows.append({
            **base_attrs,
            "concept:name": "Triage",
            "start_timestamp": row.triage_start,
            "time:timestamp": row.triage_done,
            "org:resource": row.triage_resource,
        })
    # Manual Review (only emitted when stage actually occurred)
    if pd.notna(row.manual_start) and pd.notna(row.manual_done):
        event_rows.append({
            **base_attrs,
            "concept:name": "Manual Review",
            "start_timestamp": row.manual_start,
            "time:timestamp": row.manual_done,
            "org:resource": row.review_resource,
        })
    # Escalation (only emitted when stage actually occurred)
    if pd.notna(row.escalation_start) and pd.notna(row.escalation_done):

            "concept:name": "Escalation",
            "start_timestamp": row.escalation_start,
            "time:timestamp": row.escalation_done,
            "org:resource": "SENIOR_TEAM",
        })

    event_rows.append({
        **base_attrs,
        "concept:name": "Close",
        "start_timestamp": row.close,
        "time:timestamp": row.close,
        "org:resource": "SYSTEM",

    events_raw,
    case_id="case:concept:name",

cases_df["arr_to_triage_h"] = hours_diff(cases_df["triage_start"], cases_df["arrival"])

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk (`U_underlying_risk`):** If post-close quality audits reveal that Medium-complexity flagged cases (`R02`, `R06`, `R08`, `R12`, `R15`) actually carried latent compliance hazards that manual reviewers resolved via conditional approvals (explaining the 0/5 rejection rate), then removing mandatory review (`P2`) would cause unacceptable compliance failures and we should switch to adding net-new review capacity (`P3`).
2. **Queue interference and non-FIFO gating:** If real-time queue logs show that `manual_queue_wait` for flagged cases occurs even when `M1`–`M3` are idle—because of a mandatory cooling-off period, external document verification hold, or batch release—then the downstream bottleneck is not shared-queue congestion, reversing capacity-focused remedies in favor of redesigning the pre-review hold.
3. **Decision quality asymmetry:** If the higher rejection rate among unflagged High-complexity cases (`2/3`) reflects severe missed risk among unflagged cases that bypassed manual review (false approvals in `R04`, `R07`, `R11`, `R14`, `R16`, `R19`), the risk model itself is miscalibrated and the priority intervention should be recalibrating the scoring/flag threshold rather than relaxing manual review (`P2`).
4. **Timestamp semantics and shift calendars:** If local clock timestamps span unlogged off-shift hours or if `M2`’s overlapping review intervals (`R01`/`R09`/`R03`) represent passive “case-assigned-to-desk” windows rather than active service, then apparent 5–10h manual and escalation waits may be largely calendar/logging artifacts rather than true queue congestion.
5. **Positivity and threshold discontinuity failure:** If a larger dataset with overlap around the `risk_score` cutoff ($\approx 0.62–0.63$) shows that `manual_queue_wait` and `cycle_time` increase smoothly with `risk_score` with *no* jump at the flag threshold, then the observed 8.34h gap is primarily case-mix confounding rather than a flag-policy effect, overturning any flag-policy redesign.
6. **Reviewer and triage behavior change:** If removing mandatory manual review (`P2`) causes triage officers to either rubber-stamp manual referrals defensively (preserving ~95% referral rates and zero speed gain) or over-escalate complex cases to senior staff out of liability concerns, `P2` would fail to reduce cycle time and could worsen the escalation bottleneck.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed:** In these 20 cases, flagged cases complete triage 2.59 hours faster than unflagged cases (0.84h vs. 3.43h) and avoid triage slowdowns under high arrival load, yet experience total cycle times 8.34 hours longer (16.51h vs. 8.17h). This reversal coincides with 100% manual-review entry (vs. 40% unflagged), 60% escalation (vs. 20%), and substantially longer manual-review waiting (6.50h vs. 1.88h overall; 7.80h vs. 2.17h even within High complexity), while active manual and escalation service times remain nearly equal across groups. Medium-complexity flagged cases face 100% review and 5.20h manual waits despite 0/5 rejections.

**Plausible mechanism:** The flag operates as a policy bundle whose priority triage lane (`T1`/`T2`) accelerates early flow while mandatory manual review floods shared or gated downstream queues (`M1`–`M3`) and funnels cases into senior escalation (especially via `M2`, which escalates 7/7 cases), creating congestion and potential cross-case spillovers.

**What remains unidentified:** With $n=20$, complete `risk_score` separation ($\le 0.61$ vs. $\ge 0.64$), exclusive triage resource allocation, unobserved queue states/shift calendars, and no ground-truth quality audits, we cannot separate policy-induced queueing from `U_underlying_risk` confounding, pre-review holds, or shared-queue interference, nor identify natural direct/indirect effects.

**Most informative next intervention:** A shift-randomized pilot of **targeted `P2`** (retaining priority triage while replacing automatic mandatory review with structured triage discretion for Medium-complexity, moderate-risk flagged cases, paired with blind quality audits and queue-state logging) delivers the highest joint operational and scientific value—testing occurrence mediation, measuring queue spillovers, and protecting high-risk quality simultaneously.
