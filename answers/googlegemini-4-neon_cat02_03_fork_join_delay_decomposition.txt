# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions

Let $c \in \{\text{F01}, \dots, \text{F12}\}$ index cases and $i \in \{A, B, C\}$ index the three parallel branches. Denote observed timestamps by $t^{\text{arr}}_c$ (`arrival`), $t^{\text{fork}}_c$ (`fork_release`), $t^{\text{start}}_{c,i}$ (`{i}_start`), $t^{\text{end}}_{c,i}$ (`{i}_end`), $t^{\text{start}}_{c,F}$ (`final_start`), and $t^{\text{end}}_{c,F}$ (`final_end`). All durations are measured in minutes.

### Core model assumptions (labeled for scrutiny)
- **Assumption A1 (Simultaneous fork enablement):** All three branches become eligible to start at $t^{\text{fork}}_c$, with no unlogged branch-specific release lag.
- **Assumption A2 (Strict AND-join rule):** Activity $F$ becomes eligible at the latest branch completion time, $t^{\text{join}}_c$.
- **Assumption A3 (No unlogged preemption):** Each interval $[t^{\text{start}}_{c,i}, t^{\text{end}}_{c,i}]$ represents continuous branch service; pauses or multitasking are unobserved.
- **Assumption A4 (Downstream queueing interpretation):** $t^{\text{start}}_{c,F} - t^{\text{join}}_c$ is treated as downstream queueing only provisionally; Section 4 and Section 7 test whether it reflects true server contention versus a release/routing policy.

### Non-overlapping stage decomposition
For each case $c$ and branch $i \in \{A, B, C\}$:

1. **Upstream elapsed time before fork release:**
   $$U_c = t^{\text{fork}}_c - t^{\text{arr}}_c$$

2. **Branch-specific queue waiting (under A1):**
   $$Q_{c,i} = t^{\text{start}}_{c,i} - t^{\text{fork}}_c$$

3. **Branch active service (elapsed execution span under A3):**
   $$S_{c,i} = t^{\text{end}}_{c,i} - t^{\text{start}}_{c,i}$$

4. **Branch-specific elapsed time from fork release:**
   $$E_{c,i} = t^{\text{end}}_{c,i} - t^{\text{fork}}_c = Q_{c,i} + S_{c,i}$$

5. **Join-ready timestamp (under A2):**
   $$t^{\text{join}}_c = \max_{i \in \{A, B, C\}} t^{\text{end}}_{c,i}$$

6. **Elapsed fork-join stage time:**
   $$FJ_c = t^{\text{join}}_c - t^{\text{fork}}_c = \max_{i \in \{A, B, C\}} E_{c,i} = \max_{i \in \{A, B, C\}} (Q_{c,i} + S_{c,i})$$

7. **Critical branch set:**
   $$i^*(c) = \arg\max_{i \in \{A, B, C\}} t^{\text{end}}_{c,i} = \arg\max_{i \in \{A, B, C\}} E_{c,i}$$
   (When a unique branch attains the maximum, $i^*(c)$ is a single branch; ties are reported explicitly, e.g., `A+B`.)

8. **Branch-specific synchronization waiting after completion:**
   $$W^{\text{sync}}_{c,i} = t^{\text{join}}_c - t^{\text{end}}_{c,i} = FJ_c - E_{c,i} \ge 0$$
   Note that $W^{\text{sync}}_{c,i} = 0$ if and only if $i \in i^*(c)$, and for **every** branch $i$ individually:
   $$FJ_c = Q_{c,i} + S_{c,i} + W^{\text{sync}}_{c,i}$$

9. **Downstream queueing / pre-final waiting (under A2 and A4):**
   $$D_c = t^{\text{start}}_{c,F} - t^{\text{join}}_c$$

10. **Final service:**
    $$S_{c,F} = t^{\text{end}}_{c,F} - t^{\text{start}}_{c,F}$$

11. **Total case flow time (exact additive partition without double counting):**
    $$T_c = t^{\text{end}}_{c,F} - t^{\text{arr}}_c = U_c + FJ_c + D_c + S_{c,F}$$
    Equivalently, substituting any single branch $i$—or specifically the critical branch $i^*(c)$ where $W^{\text{sync}}_{c,i^*} = 0$:
    $$T_c = U_c + \underbrace{\left(Q_{c,i^*} + S_{c,i^*}\right)}_{FJ_c} + D_c + S_{c,F}$$

### Why summing all branch synchronization waits double counts elapsed time
Because branches $A, B, C$ execute concurrently during the single wall-clock interval $[t^{\text{fork}}_c, t^{\text{join}}_c]$ of length $FJ_c$:

1. **Adding $W^{\text{sync}}_{c,i}$ on top of $FJ_c$ double counts:** For any non-critical branch $i$, its synchronization wait $[t^{\text{end}}_{c,i}, t^{\text{join}}_c]$ occurs *inside* $[t^{\text{fork}}_c, t^{\text{join}}_c]$, simultaneously with the critical branch's ongoing queue or service. Thus $FJ_c$ already contains every branch's synchronization wait.
2. **Summing across branches multiplies wall-clock time:** Because $Q_{c,i} + S_{c,i} + W^{\text{sync}}_{c,i} = FJ_c$ for each branch $i \in \{A, B, C\}$, summing over all three branches yields:
   $$\sum_{i \in \{A,B,C\}} \left(Q_{c,i} + S_{c,i} + W^{\text{sync}}_{c,i}\right) = 3 \cdot FJ_c$$
   Even summing synchronization waits alone, $\sum_i W^{\text{sync}}_{c,i}$, aggregates overlapping wall-clock intervals across non-critical branches. For example, in case F07 ($FJ_{\text{F07}} = 75$ min), branch $A$ waits 25 min (`10:30–10:55`) and branch $C$ waits 52 min (`10:03–10:55`). Their sum is $25 + 0 + 52 = 77$ min—which exceeds the entire 75-minute fork-join elapsed time because the 25-minute window `10:30–10:55` is counted once for $A$ and again for $C$, while branch $B$ is actively running during the same clock minutes.

Synchronization wait is therefore a **branch-level diagnostic** (measuring idle WIP holding time and slack relative to the join barrier), not an additive component to be summed across branches into $T_c$.

### Reasoned influence ranking (and honest treatment of ties/near-ties)

1. **Rank 1 — Branch B (Machine Stress Test) is unmistakably the dominant controller of completion time.**
   - *Why service time alone misleads:* Branch A has a 33% higher mean service time than B (50.00 vs 37.50 min). Judging by service time alone would wrongly pick A as the bottleneck.
   - *Queue + service governs completion:* Branch B's substantial queue delay (mean 19.17 min, median 20.0 min, max 30 min) pushes its branch elapsed time to 56.67 min mean (60.0 min median), making B the last-completing branch in **8 of 12 cases (66.7%)** and giving it the lowest mean synchronization wait (4.33 min).
   - *Average and tail dominance:* Branch B generates **120 of the 147 total exclusive-excess minutes (81.6%)**, adding an average of 10.00 min/case above the runner-up. Crucially, B drives **100% of the upper tail**: every case with $FJ_c > 57$ min (F02, F03, F05, F06, F07, F09, F12), including both 75-minute maxima (F07 and F12), is B-critical. Notice also a strong empirical association inside B: longer stress tests (35–45 min) coincide with longer queues (20–30 min, except F01), whereas 30-min tests queue only 5–10 min—a hypothesis pointing toward heterogeneous test rigs or priority scheduling.

2. **Rank 2 — Branch A (Laboratory Assay), ahead of C despite a frequency tie.**
   - *Honest reporting of the A vs C tie/near-tie:* On raw critical-branch frequency, A and C are **exactly tied** (2/12 = 16.7% each: A on F01, F11; C on F04, F08). On exclusive critical excess above the runner-up, they are a **near-tie** (15 min / 1.25 min per case for A vs 12 min / 1.00 min per case for C). With $n = 12$, that 3-minute difference is statistically indistinguishable from noise.
   - *Why A still ranks 2nd in structural influence:* Branch A is deterministic at 50 min (0 queue, 50 service) and is the **runner-up in all 10 cases where it is not critical** (83.3%). Consequently, A establishes a hard **50-minute structural floor** on $FJ_c$. Its mean synchronization wait is only 11.00 min (versus 30.42 min for C), and—as shown in Section 4—the moment Branch B's queue is reduced, Branch A immediately clips B's gains and jumps from 2 to 6 critical/tied cases.

3. **Rank 3 — Branch C (Documentation Review) is normally non-binding, with occasional exception spikes.**
   - In 10 of 12 cases, C completes in 23–35 min (median 25.0 min), never even finishing as runner-up, and waits a mean of 30.42 min (up to 52 min) at the join.
   - Its two critical appearances (F04 and F08) stem from sharp queue spikes (35 and 25 min, plus 22–30 min service) that occur *while no other logged cases are occupying C* and while later cases (F05, F09) start earlier—strongly suggesting episodic administrative holds/exceptions rather than continuous capacity overload.

4. **Downstream interaction check (does Station F congestion mask branch gains?):**
   - Inspecting the actual busy windows of F1 (handling odd cases F01–F11) and F2 (handling even cases F02–F12) reveals a critical nuance: F1 and F2 each run 6 cases $\times 20$ min $= 120$ min over a ~180-minute span (~66–67% utilization, well below saturation).
   - Across the 176 total minutes of `downstream_queue`, only **25 minutes (14.2%)** occur while *both* F1 and F2 are simultaneously busy on logged cases (F05: 10 min; F09: 10 min; F10: 5 min), and **103 minutes (58.5%)** occur while even the *assigned* resource is idle on logged cases (F01, F02, F03, F04 after `10:00`, F06, F07, F10 after `11:25`, F12).
   - Therefore, under pure FCFS queueing, Station F rarely masks branch improvements—*except* on localized clusters like F11 (where F1 is busy with F09 from `11:30–11:50`). However, if the unexplained idle gaps reflect a **rigid clock-time release policy** or **unlogged background workload**, those policies *could* absorb earlier `join_ready` times unless addressed concurrently.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis

Unless noted otherwise, direct savings first assume **ceteris paribus pass-through** (constant `downstream_queue` $D_c$ and unchanged other branches) to isolate fork-join mechanics, and then explicitly evaluate **downstream queueing interaction** under observed F1/F2 schedules.

### Summary comparison of counterfactuals ($n = 12$; baseline mean $T_c = 115.25$ min, max $= 130.0$ min, P90 $= 124.5$ min)

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **CF1: 20% A service** | Direct pass-through (constant $D_c$) | 15.0 | 1.25 (1.1%) | 114.00 | 130.0 / 124.5 | 2 / 10 |
| *CF1 (with F1 masking)* | *F11 held until F1 frees at 11:50* | *5.0* | *0.42 (0.4%)* | *114.83* | *130.0 / 124.5* | *1 / 11* |
| **CF2: 50% B queue** | Direct pass-through (constant $D_c$) | **92.5** | **7.71 (6.7%)** | **107.54** | **115.0 / 114.5** | **8 / 4** |
| **CF3a: Add F3 only** | Removes only dual-busy overlap (25 min) | 25.0 | 2.08 (1.8%) | 113.17 | 130.0 / 124.5 | 3 / 9 |

### Counterfactual 1: Reducing A service time by 20% ($50 \to 40$ min)
- **Direct benefits:** Although total Branch A service time drops by 120 minutes across the 12 cases (10 min/case), total `join_ready` improves by only **15 minutes (1.25 min/case)**—a **12.5% pass-through rate**. Only **F01** saves 5 min (clipped from 10 to 5 min because Branch B finishes at 45 min and becomes critical) and **F11** saves 10 min (creating an `A+B` tie at 40 min).
- **Cases with zero benefit:** **10 of 12 cases (83.3%)** see zero improvement (F02, F03, F05, F06, F07, F09, F10, F12 remain B-critical; F04, F08 remain C-critical). The flow-time tail (max 130 min, P90 124.5 min) is completely untouched.
- **Secondary effects & downstream masking:**
  - *Downstream masking on F11:* F11's `join_ready` moves from `11:30` to `11:20`. Under the observed odd-case-to-F1 assignment, F1 is busy with F07 (`11:10–11:30`) and F09 (`11:30–11:50`), so F11 still cannot start on F1 until `11:50`—completely masking its 10-minute gain and shrinking net CF1 savings to **5 min total (0.42 min/case)**.
  - *Synchronization inflation:* Branch A's mean synchronization wait jumps from 11.00 to **19.75 min**, increasing completed-assay WIP sitting idle before the join.
  - *Hidden strategic value:* CF1's true payoff is *latent*: it lowers the fork-join floor from 50 to 40 min, which only pays off once Branch B is decongested (combining CF1 + CF2 saves 117.5 min vs $15 + 92.5 = 107.5$ min separately—a **+10 min super-additive synergy** unlocked on F01, F05, and F10).

### Counterfactual 2: Reducing B queue delay by 50% ($Q_{c,B} \to 0.5\,Q_{c,B}$)
- **Direct benefits:** Cutting B's queue by 115 total minutes yields **92.5 minutes** of direct `join_ready` reduction (**7.71 min/case**)—an **80.4% pass-through rate** (over 6× more effective per minute invested than CF1). Eight cases benefit: F02 (+10), F03 (+10), F05 (+10), F06 (+15), F07 (+15), F09 (+12.5), F10 (+5), and F12 (+15). (Note: 0.5-min fractions reflect exact arithmetic on 1-min-rounded inputs; rounding to nearest integer shifts total savings by at most $\pm 1$ min.)
- **Tail compression:** Maximum $FJ_c$ drops from 75 to **60 min** (20%); maximum $T_c$ drops from 130 to **115 min** (15 min), and P90 $T_c$ drops from 124.5 to **114.5 min** (10.0 min).
- **Cases with zero or clipped benefit:**
  - **4 cases get zero benefit:** F01 and F11 (A remains critical) and F04 and F08 (C remains critical).
  - **2 cases get partially clipped by Branch A's 50-min floor:** F05 drops B from 60 to 47.5 min (12.5), but A=50 caps the gain at 10 min (2.5 min clipped); F10 drops B from 55 to 45 min (10), but A=50 caps the gain at 5 min (5 min clipped).
- **Secondary effects:**
  - *Bottleneck shift to Branch A:* Criticality shifts dramatically from `B: 8, A: 2, C: 2` to `B: 4, A: 4, A+B tie: 2, C: 2`. Branch A becomes co-dominant (critical or tied in half of all cases), reducing A's mean sync wait from 11.00 to 3.29 min while raising B's from 4.33 to 7.96 min.
  - *Beneficial downstream de-bunching:* Under CF2, consecutive same-resource `join_ready` gaps widen to $\ge 22.5$ min on F1 and $\ge 23.0$ min on F2—strictly exceeding the 20-min final service time! Thus, under FCFS dispatching, CF2 **eliminates** the logged-case arrival bunching at Station F rather than worsening downstream congestion. However, if Station F operates on fixed clock-time release slots, earlier `join_ready` times could be partially absorbed into longer pre-release waits.

### Counterfactual 3: Adding final-station capacity or reducing downstream queueing
Because Station F sits **in series** after the AND-join, any true reduction in $D_c$ translates 1-for-1 into total flow time with **zero masking by parallel branches**. However, the mechanism matters enormously:

- **CF3a — Adding a third server (F3) alone, without fixing non-contention lag (conservative capacity-only bound):**
  - Only **3 cases** (F05: 10 min, F09: 10 min, F10: 5 min) experience `join_ready` while *both* F1 and F2 are busy on logged cases. Removing only that dual-server overlap saves **25 min total (2.08 min/case)**, leaving **9 cases with zero benefit** and the 130-min tail unchanged.
  - Even worse, simulating a **zero-lag 2-server pooled FCFS** queue on the baseline `join_ready` timestamps produces only **5 minutes of total queueing across all 12 cases** (solely F11 waiting `11:30–11:35`). Therefore, *if* no hidden external workload is saturating F1/F2, adding F3 buys at most 5 minutes of true capacity relief while lowering final-station utilization from ~66% to ~44%!
- **CF3b — Halving downstream waiting (50% reduction in $D_c$):**
  - Achievable either by halving a handover/batching delay or by relieving congestion if unlogged workload truly drives $D_c$. Saves **88.0 min total (7.33 min/case)**; **12 of 12 cases benefit** (0 cases with zero benefit); max $T_c$ falls from 130 to 122.5 min and P90 from 124.5 to 117.5 min.
- **CF3c & CF3d — Eliminating non-contention lag via pooled 2-server FCFS (CF3c) vs full elimination with F3 (CF3d):**
  - Simply pooling F1 and F2 and starting immediately upon `join_ready` (CF3c, **zero added headcount**) saves **171.0 min total (14.25 min/case)**—capturing **97.2%** of the theoretical 176-minute upper bound (CF3d) and lowering mean $T_c$ from 115.25 to **101.00 min** (max 115 min, P90 110 min). Every single case benefits (12/12).
- **Secondary effects:** Cutting downstream waiting does not alter fork-join synchronization waits, branch criticality, or upstream throughput (since 2 servers at 20 min/case already support 6 cases/hr vs the observed 4 cases/hr arrival rate). If unlogged external cases *do* share F1/F2, pooling without adding capacity could degrade external case wait times—making workload measurement mandatory before hiring F3.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks

### Recommendation 1: Decongest Branch B (Machine Stress Test) queue, with staged readiness for Branch A's 50-minute floor
**Target mechanism:** Reduce Branch B's 19.2-minute average queue (e.g., via cross-training/load-balancing across stress-test rigs, removing setup/changeover delays, reviewing the non-FIFO dispatching observed on F07/F08, or adding flexible rig capacity during peak morning hours), rather than starting with Branch A service-time reduction.

- **Throughput:** Observed arrival rate is ~4 cases/hr (one every 15 min after F01). Branch B averages 37.5 min service and runs up to 4 cases concurrently, suggesting steady-state capacity is not permanently below demand—yet queues climb to 30 min on 35–45 min tests. Eliminating internal rig fragmentation or hidden setup loss increases effective peak throughput and prevents queue build-up during longer-test clusters.
- **Flow-time tail:** Highest single-branch leverage on tail risk. Cutting B's queue by 50% alone slashes max flow time from 130 to 115 min (15 min) and P90 from 124.5 to 114.5 min (10 min).
- **Resource utilization:** If queue reduction comes from better scheduling/pooling across heterogeneous rigs or reduced setup time, rig utilization becomes more balanced across machines; if achieved by adding a dedicated stress-test rig, average rig utilization will drop (increasing fixed equipment cost).
- **Possible new bottlenecks:** **Immediate bottleneck shift to Branch A** (which jumps from 2 to 6 critical/tied cases under CF2). Once B's queue is roughly halved, further investment in B yields sharply diminishing returns until A's 50-minute assay time is shortened (at which point CF1 + CF2 unlocks +10 min of super-additive synergy). Additionally, C's sporadic 25–35 min exception spikes (F04, F08) become the sole remaining source of >55-min fork-join outliers once B is stabilized.
- **Synchronization effects:** Strongly positive overall. Mean `A_sync_wait` falls from 11.00 to 3.29 min and mean `C_sync_wait` falls from 30.42 to 22.71 min (less completed lab/doc WIP waiting at the join), while `B_sync_wait` rises modestly from 4.33 to 7.96 min.
- **What to pilot and measure first:**
  1. Log **rig/resource IDs**, test-protocol classes, and changeover/setup timestamps in Branch B to test whether long queues on 35–45 min jobs reflect a dedicated sub-pool of machines or priority overtaking (F08 passing F07).
  2. Measure whether the 5-minute floor on `B_queue` (even on F01) is physical sample transport/intake rather than true queueing.
  3. Pilot pooled/SPT-aware or FIFO-bounded dispatching on B for 2 weeks before purchasing hardware.

### Recommendation 2: Diagnose and remove non-contention downstream waiting at Station F via dynamic F1/F2 pooling and real-time join notification *before* adding a third resource F3
**Target mechanism:** Replace apparent static odd/even routing (F1=odd, F2=even) and any manual handoff, polling interval, or batch-release lag with automated `join_ready` alerting and a shared FCFS queue across F1 and F2.

- **Throughput:** Two resources at 20 min/case provide 6 cases/hr capacity against a 4 cases/hr arrival rate (~66.7% logged utilization). Removing idle gaps between `join_ready` and `final_start` does not expand maximum theoretical throughput (still 6 cases/hr), unless those idle gaps are currently caused by unlogged administrative overhead consuming F1/F2 time—in which case eliminating overhead frees genuine capacity.
- **Flow-time tail:** Large, immediate, and broad-based impact if non-contention lag is policy/handover-driven: zero-lag pooled 2-server FCFS reduces mean flow time by 14.25 min/case (115.25  101.00 min) and max flow time from 130 to 115 min, benefiting all 12 cases. Even a 50% reduction saves 7.33 min/case across every case.
- **Resource utilization:** Logged utilization of F1 and F2 stays near ~66–67% (avoiding the over-staffing risk of adding F3, which would push logged utilization down to ~44%). Pooling smooths load across F1 and F2 when case completion order swaps (e.g., F08 finishing before F07, or F11 arriving while F1 is busy and F2 is idle).
- **Possible new bottlenecks:** If F1 and F2 are actually serving **unlogged external case types** during the observed idle windows, forcing immediate priority start for F-cases will create a new bottleneck (queueing spike) for those external cases—only in *that* scenario is adding F3 justified. Also, eliminating a deliberate cooling/QA staging buffer could increase decision errors if the 8–15 min gap served an unlogged verification purpose.
- **Synchronization effects:** Neutral on upstream branch synchronization (`A_sync`, `B_sync`, `C_sync` are unchanged), **but** eliminating downstream release lag prevents downstream policies from *masking* future upstream gains from Recommendation 1 (and prevents completed case packages from stalling after all three branches have already synchronized).
- **What to pilot and measure first:**
  1. Audit F1 and F2 calendars/event logs over 1–2 weeks to capture **all work** (including unlogged case types, breaks, and meetings) and measure true total utilization.
  2. Instrument exact timestamps for *join completion notification*, *case arrival at F*, and *decision start* to separate physical transfer, IT notification lag, release-slot batching, and true queueing.
  3. Run a controlled 1-week pilot of automated join alerts + pooled F1/F2 dispatch (no headcount change), tracking both F-case downstream wait and any spillover delay on other F1/F2 workloads; escalate to adding F3 only if audited total utilization exceeds ~80–85%.

# 7. Limits of inference

The supplied timestamp table supports exact accounting of elapsed intervals under explicit assumptions, but several fundamental quantities remain **unidentifiable** from these timestamps alone:

1. **Causality versus association:**
   - Timestamps establish temporal ordering, not causal mechanisms. For instance, Branch B exhibits longer queues on cases with longer service times (20–30 min queues on 35–45 min tests vs 5–10 min queues on 30-min tests), and Branch C exhibits sharp queue spikes on F04 (35 min) and F08 (25 min) while later cases overtake them. We can *hypothesize* heterogeneous test rigs, priority rules, or missing-document holds, but timestamps alone cannot prove which mechanism causes the delay—nor guarantee that cutting a branch queue will leave service times or error rates unchanged.

2. **True branch resource capacity, staffing, and unlogged workload:**
   - Overlapping execution intervals reveal only **lower bounds** on concurrent capacity: at least 4 concurrent slots in A, at least 4 in B (at `10:52`: F07, F09, F10, F11), and at least 3 in C (at `09:48`: F04, F06, F07). True installed capacity, shift patterns, breaks, equipment downtime, and *external unlogged work* sharing A, B, C, or F are completely unobservable. Without resource IDs on A, B, and C, we cannot even tell whether A is a 4-station manual lab or an automated multi-slot assay analyzer.

3. **Work preemption, pauses, and multitasking:**
   - Because only `start` and `end` timestamps are logged per activity (no suspend/resume lifecycle events), $S_{c,i} = t^{\text{end}}_{c,i} - t^{\text{start}}_{c,i}$ measures **elapsed execution span**, not guaranteed uninterrupted effort. A 45-minute stress test or 30-minute document review could include mid-activity waiting, preemption by higher-priority jobs, or an operator multitasking across concurrent cases.

4. **Downstream waiting: true queueing versus release policy, routing, or handover lag:**
   - Although $D_c = t^{\text{start}}_{c,F} - t^{\text{join}}_c$ is provisionally labeled `downstream_queue`, our F1/F2 schedule audit shows that **103 of 176 minutes (58.5%)** occur while the *assigned* final resource is idle on logged cases, and only **25 minutes (14.2%)** occur while both F1 and F2 are busy. Timestamps alone cannot distinguish among four competing explanations: (a) unlogged external work keeping F1/F2 occupied, (b) a rigid clock-slot release policy or batching rule (11 of 12 `final_start` times fall on 5-minute multiples), (c) mandatory physical transfer, notification, or QA staging latency, or (d) strict static odd/even resource assignment preventing F1/F2 load sharing. Each explanation implies a very different operational fix.

5. **Fork-release semantics and minimum branch lags:**
   - The `arrival` to `fork_release` window (15–20 min) is an unsegmented black box. Moreover, `A_queue` is always 0 min, whereas `B_queue` and `C_queue` are *never* below 5 min—even on F01, the first case of the morning (`B_queue = 5`, `C_queue = 10`). Thus, we cannot separate true resource queueing from a fixed ~5-minute physical transport/intake lag or pre-08:00 warm-up work-in-process.

6. **Small-sample uncertainty, warm-up bias, and timestamp granularity:**
   - With only $n = 12$ cases on a single morning (`2026-05-19`), estimates of tail behavior (P90, max) and rare events (such as C's 2/12 spike rate or the A vs C frequency tie) carry wide sampling uncertainty and may reflect transient morning warm-up rather than steady-state operation. Finally, 1-minute timestamp rounding introduces up to $\pm 1$ min quantization error per duration, which is minor for broad rankings but relevant when interpreting exact ties or half-minute counterfactual estimates.
