# 2. Integrated explanation

The data describe a *time-varying performance surface* shaped by at least four overlapping forces, none of which can be read off the raw sequence-position curve in isolation.

**1. Selection/case-mix dominates the first hour.** Bins 1–5 and the first hour of every shift inherit the previous shift's residual. The complexity score is 0.72 and high-priority share 41% — by far the hardest mix of the day. Any naive "warm-up" estimate that ignores this will attribute the difficulty of the work to the worker, not the queue.

**2. Apparent learning is partly queue drainage and case drainage.** As the shift progresses, the queue shortens (46  31) and the mix becomes easier (0.72  0.50). Both effects mechanically lower service time and error rate. Within-resource comparisons (Seniors 15.9  11.8 min) suggest a real warm-up component, but the effect size shrinks once complexity is held constant.

**3. Speedup in the second half is largely queue pressure, not skill.** From bin 31 onward, mean service time continues to fall (12.9  10.8 min) but error rate reverses (2.7%  4.6%  7.9%). This is the classical speed–accuracy tradeoff under WIP: workers cut corners because the queue is visible. The WIP×complexity table confirms: at high WIP, even *low*-complexity cases see error rates rise from 2.1% to 5.8%, which complexity alone cannot explain.

**4. Fatigue and circadian effects exist but are conditional.** Day shift's last-2h error rate (7.5%) is the worst of any shift, consistent with end-of-day fatigue — but only on top of high WIP and lower-complexity cases being rushed. Night staff are slower (15.2 min) yet their error rate is mid-range (5.1%), because they handle fewer but more urgent exceptions; their workload profile is different, not their biology per se.

**5. Why service time falls while errors rise only in some conditions.** Service time and error rate decouple when the bottleneck shifts from *cognitive difficulty* (early shift, complex backlog) to *throughput pressure* (late shift, high WIP). Under difficulty, both rise together. Under pressure, workers trade accuracy for speed. The WIP×complexity table shows this clearly: at low WIP, complexity adds time *and* errors proportionally; at high WIP, errors explode (10.9%) while time does not — the protective "slow down on hard cases" is gone.

**6. Batching and automation are silent confounders.** Document Review batching and end-of-shift Quality Check bursts inflate "errors late in shift" measures that are really *catch-up* behavior, not fatigue. Bot_Risk shifts the error burden downstream to human correctors, especially when WIP is high — so the system looks more accurate than it is at the activity level and less accurate at the case level.

**Net interpretation:** The pattern is a *queue-pressure-dominant regime* with a *case-mix artifact* in the first hour, a *learning* component that is real but smaller than it looks, a *fatigue/circadian* overlay that is conditional on pressure, and *batching/automation* effects that distort the metrics unless explicitly detected.

# 3. Proposed analyses

### 3.1 Within-resource comparisons (hold the worker constant)
For each `org:resource`, compare first-20 events of shift vs later events, stratified by complexity band and WIP band. Use `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` to subset.

```python
import pm4py

log = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id='case:concept:name',
                             activity_key='concept:name',
                             timestamp_key='time:timestamp',
                             start_timestamp_key='start_timestamp')

# isolate one resource
sub = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(log, 'org:resource', ['Senior_A'])
# compare first 20 vs later within shift
sub['pos_bin'] = sub['sequence_position_within_shift'].apply(
    lambda p: 'first20' if p <= 20 else 'later')
```

### 3.2 Within-shift position effects (hold position, vary worker)
Re-bin by `sequence_position_within_shift` and compute service time / error rate per bin per resource group. Apply `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` to separate waiting from service.

### 3.3 WIP / queue stratification
Stratify by `wip_at_start` and `queue_length_at_start` quantiles. Use `pm4py.filter_case_performance` to keep only cases whose service time is in a plausible range, avoiding batch outliers.

```python
log = pm4py.filter_case_performance(log, min_performance=0, max_performance=3600)
```

### 3.4 Complexity adjustment
Add `case_complexity_score` and `priority` as covariates. Compute complexity-adjusted service time per bin:

```python
log['adj_service'] = log.groupby('activity_family')['service_time'].transform(
    lambda x: x / x.mean())
```

Or use a regression: `service_time ~ sequence_position + complexity + wip + priority + resource_group + shift_id`.

### 3.5 Shift and time-of-day controls
Use `pm4py.filter_time_range` to restrict to specific shifts, then re-derive the bin table. Dotted chart to visualize:

```python
pm4py.save_vis_dotted_chart(log, 'dotted.png')
```

### 3.6 Batching detection
```python
batches = pm4py.discover_batches(log, resource_key='org:resource',
                                 case_id_key='case:concept:name')
pm4py.save_vis_events_distribution_graph(log, 'dist.png')
```
Flag clusters: same resource, 9 consecutive events, service time < 30% of resource median, same `queue_length_at_start`.

### 3.7 Resource-experience interactions
Test whether `resource_experience_months` moderates the WIP effect. Expected: high-experience resources show smaller error-rate increase under high WIP (Seniors 4.9% vs Juniors 12.4%).

### 3.8 Rework / error downstream analysis
```python
rework = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(log, activity_key='concept:name')
```
Trace rework back to the original activity, resource, and WIP at original event. This is essential because late-shift "errors" often appear as next-shift rework.

### 3.9 Performance DFG
```python

```
Compare median service time per edge across bins to see which transitions degrade.

### 3.10 Temporal features
```python

```
Use hour-of-day, day-of-week features as circadian controls.

**Strongest evidence-based candidates:** #3 (queue-pressure alert) and #5 (micro-breaks), with #7 as a complementary capacity intervention. #8 is the highest-risk option and should not be deployed on raw metrics.

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

**Selection bias dominates naive worker comparisons.** The first hour of every shift carries the previous shift's complex backlog. A worker who happens to start Early inherits complexity 0.72 and 41% high-priority share; a Day-shift starter sees 0.61 / 34%. Penalizing Early-shift staff for higher error rates is penalizing them for *inherited work*, not skill.

**Case-mix confounding makes "fatigue" claims fragile.** The late-shift error rate is high partly because WIP is high *and* because workers stop slowing down on hard cases. Without complexity and WIP controls, a "tired worker" is indistinguishable from a "pressured worker." Fatigue may be real, but the data do not isolate it.

**Measurement error: error_flag vs rework_flag.** A case is "errored" if flagged during the activity, but most corrections only appear in the next shift's Quality Check burst or as 7-day rework. Late-shift "low error rate" can simply mean *no one has looked yet*. Comparing error rates across shifts without accounting for this lag is misleading.

**Queue pressure is a management variable, not a worker variable.** WIP and queue length are largely determined by arrival patterns, handover policies, and Bot_Risk throughput. Holding workers accountable for outcomes that are 60–70% driven by upstream conditions is a fairness failure and will not improve performance.

**Automation interaction: Bot_Risk is not free.** Its "instant" service time hides downstream correction work. If the dashboard reports Bot_Risk as having 0% error rate, the system rewards the bot and blames the human correctors. Quality measurement must be at the *case* level, not the *activity* level.

**Punitive use of error rates is doubly dangerous.** First, the metrics are confounded. Second, once workers know errors are punished, they will under-report, defer, or transfer hard cases. This degrades the data needed to manage the system, on top of harming the workers. The instruction in the question — do not punish on raw late-shift error rates — should be treated as a hard constraint.

**Batching is a coping behavior, not a defect.** Workers batch because the system rewards throughput. Removing batching without addressing its cause simply pushes the same effort into less visible forms (e.g., skipping Quality Check, escalating to rework). Any intervention should be paired with a workload change.

# 6. Monitoring dashboard specification

**Indicator 1 — Complexity-adjusted service time (CAST) per activity and resource group.**
Definition: service time ÷ expected service time given complexity and activity. Lets you compare workers fairly.

**Indicator 2 — WIP-stratified error rate.**
Definition: error rate within WIP bands (<30, 30–70, >70) × complexity bands. Surfaces the real risk zones (high WIP × high complexity at 10.9%) without conflating with shift effects.

**Indicator 3 — Queue pressure index.**
Definition: rolling mean of `wip_at_start` and `queue_length_at_start` over the last 60 minutes, with a traffic-light threshold (green <30, amber 30–70, red >70). Tied to the proposed alert.

**Indicator 4 — First-hour case mix.**
Definition: mean `case_complexity_score` and high-priority share in the first 60 minutes of each shift. Tracks whether handover is fair and whether the "warm-up penalty" is real.

**Indicator 5 — Late-shift error rate, complexity- and WIP-adjusted.**
Definition: residual from a regression of error on WIP, complexity, priority, and resource experience, evaluated at the last 2 hours. The only fair fatigue proxy.

**Indicator 6 — Batching indicator.**
Definition: count per shift of (same resource, same queue batch, 9 consecutive events, service time < 30% of resource median). Flags the Document Review batching pattern.

**Indicator 7 — End-of-shift Quality Check backlog.**
Definition: count of Quality Check events performed in the last 45 minutes of shift vs distributed across the shift. High values indicate deferred-quality behavior.

**Indicator 8 — Rework loop length and lag.**
Definition: median days from original error event to rework event, stratified by shift of origin. Surfaces "invisible" errors that only show in the next shift's Quality Check.

**Indicator 9 — Bot_Risk correction rate by WIP.**
Definition: share of Bot_Risk-classified cases subsequently corrected by a human, split by WIP. Detects automation degradation under load.

**Indicator 10 — Worker-load fairness (Gini over complexity-weighted WIP per resource per day).**
Definition: each worker's daily load weighted by `case_complexity_score`, then Gini coefficient across the team. High Gini means a few workers carry the hard cases.

**Indicator 11 — Handover integrity.**
Definition: share of high-complexity cases opened within 30 minutes of a shift handover. Combined with the next-shift rework rate, measures whether handovers lose information.

**Indicator 12 — Throughput vs quality scatter by shift hour.**
Definition: hourly service time (x) vs hourly error rate (y), per shift. The curve bending upward at high WIP is the diagnostic for the speed–accuracy tradeoff and should be the headline chart.

These twelve indicators together cover the five mechanisms (learning, fatigue, circadian, queue pressure, case-mix), the two workaround patterns (batching, deferred Quality Check), and the automation interaction — without ever requiring a worker-level judgment on raw error rate.
