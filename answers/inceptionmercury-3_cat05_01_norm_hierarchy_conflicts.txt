## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts I treat as established:** GPIP-4 is a board-approved global policy. B-AAC-PROC-17 Rev.6 is a controlled local SOP. ECD-2 is an incident directive signed by the COO and Incident Commander. The CFO email is an ordinary, uncontrolled email. The ERP outage and clock-error facts are evidence facts, not normative exceptions.

**Assumptions I use for this assessment:**

1. **“Commit_to_Supplier” is the operative commitment event.** Pre-commitment controls are measured against the actual time of supplier commitment, not against the time the ERP record was entered.
2. **Authority is not based on date or wording strength.** GPIP-4 is the board-level baseline. It can be suspended only through its own exception mechanism, GPIP-4 A7: a written, scoped, time-limited exception issued by the Chief Risk Officer. ECD-2 is not such an exception because it was not signed or ratified by the CRO.
3. **ECD-2 is an operational overlay, not a GPIP override.** It can alter local workflow steps under B7, such as PO timing and approved-supplier registration, but it cannot silently displace GPIP-4 A1–A3.
4. **The CFO email is advisory and Finance-interpretive at most.** It cannot waive Compliance controls or GPIP-4 segregation, and its later timing does not make it controlling.
5. **Burden of proof:** the party asserting compliance or an exception must show the facts supporting it. If a required approval is absent, that is a substantive deviation. If only a recording is late but the act is corroborated by better evidence, that is a logging defect, not necessarily a substantive breach.
6. **Post-hoc approval is mitigation, not automatic cure.** A6 is applied literally.
7. **Scope of ECD-2:** I use a functional reading of “critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the current contamination event.” Directly needed items for the affected line are covered. Backup-line spares with three days of stock and unrelated cafeteria repairs are not covered by ECD-2.
8. **Timing:** rules are applied at the time of the act. An action after ECD-2 expiry is governed by the normal GPIP/SOP framework unless a valid written extension exists.

**Issue-specific hierarchy:**

- **PO timing:** B1 normally requires PO before commitment. B4 emergency allows commitment before PO. ECD-2 C1 also allows commitment before PO within its scope.
- **Supplier onboarding:** B3 requires approved suppliers. ECD-2 C2 overrides B3 within its scope. GPIP-4 A3 still requires Compliance clearance before commitment for any new supplier.
- **Financial approvals:** GPIP-4 A2 applies at €50,000 and requires an independent budget owner and Finance Controller before commitment. B2 applies at €25,000 and adds Plant approval. ECD-2 does not waive these.
- **Compliance clearance:** GPIP-4 A3 applies above €100,000 or for new suppliers. ECD-2 does not waive it.
- **Segregation of duties:** GPIP-4 A1 is not waived by ECD-2 or the CFO email.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

### P-501 alternative: treat B2 as a pre-commitment gate

If B2 is interpreted as requiring Plant and Finance approval before supplier commitment, then P-501 becomes NONCOMPLIANT because Plant_Approve and Finance_Approve occurred hours after commitment.

- Why credible: A5 allows local procedures to impose stricter controls, and A6 warns that later approval does not automatically cure a missing pre-commitment approval.
- Why weak: B2 does not contain the phrase “before supplier commitment,” while GPIP A2 does. B4 separately carves out emergency commitment-before-PO timing. I therefore do not adopt this reading as primary.

### P-502 alternative: treat ECD-2 as a full GPIP exception

If ECD-2 were treated as a valid CRO A7 exception, and if C4 were read to waive Compliance clearance and finance approval, P-502 could be JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.

- Why credible: ECD-2 has broad wording: “takes precedence over ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding requirements.”
- Why weak: C5 says ECD-2 does not mention Compliance, Finance, segregation, or the €100,000 threshold. The CRO did not sign or ratify it. The CFO email is not controlled policy. This alternative is the least defensible for GPIP controls.

### P-503 alternative: accept Operations’ backup-line scope and waive A1/A2/A3

If backup-line spare pumps are treated as critical sterilization-line lots, and if the joint IC authorization is treated as satisfying both segregation and financial approvals, P-503 could become JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.

- Why credible: Operations explicitly labeled the pumps “critical contingency”; the incident directive’s scope is undefined.
- Why weak: even under that reading, A3 Compliance clearance for €140,000 is absent, and PROC-3’s multiple roles undermine the independent-approver purpose of A1. This alternative would require reading ECD-2 as a CRO-level override of GPIP, which the record does not support.

### P-505 alternative: accept the CFO email as a Finance-control override

If the CFO email is treated as a valid Finance-control interpretation, and if joint IC authorization is accepted as satisfying the independent budget owner and Finance Controller requirements for amounts up to €150,000, P-505 could be JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.

- Why credible: the CFO owns the Finance control framework and the email explicitly covers the ECD-2 window.
- Why weak: D3 and D4 preserve integrity controls and do not authorize waiver of GPIP segregation or Compliance; the email is not in the controlled policy repository. I do not give it board-policy effect.

### P-507 alternative: same CFO-email premise

If the CFO email is accepted as binding, P-507 would also become JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION despite the absence of a separate Finance event.

- Why credible: the amount is below €150,000, joint IC authorization occurred before commitment, and Compliance clearance was timely.
- Why weak: same reasons as P-505; the absence of Finance is not explained by the document-system outage. The outage explains the late ERP entry, not the missing financial control.

### P-508 alternative: treat Legal clearance as replacing Finance approval

If D2 is read as “for amounts above €150,000, Legal clearance is the complete substitute for all other approvals,” P-508 could be JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.

- Why credible: the CFO’s email explicitly addresses the above-€150,000 category.
- Why weak: D2 says “obtain Legal clearance as well,” not “instead of.” GPIP A2 still requires an independent budget owner and Finance Controller. This alternative is weaker than the P-505/P-507 alternative.

## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

**Policy breaches versus logging defects.**

- Substantive policy breaches exist in P-502, P-503, P-504, P-506, P-507, and P-508. P-505 is also a substantive breach under my hierarchy because Finance approval followed commitment.
- Logging defects are present in:
  - P-502: ERP entry after verbal authorization; corroborated by telephone recording.
  - P-505: ERP clock error; supplier acknowledgment is better evidence.
  - P-507: authorization appeared at 14:30 but a signed PDF existed at 12:40.

**Late evidence of a timely act.**

- P-502: commitment at 10:15 is supported by supplier recording/acknowledgment, despite ERP delay.
- P-505: commitment before ECD-2 expiry is supported by the call at 17:57 and supplier acknowledgment at 17:59:31.
- P-507: authorization within four hours is supported by the signed PDF created at 12:40.

**Inability to prove compliance.**

- P-501: compliance is provable only under my interpretation that B2 is not an explicit pre-commitment gate.
- P-502, P-503, P-505, P-506, P-507, P-508: the absence of independent budget owner evidence is not a mere record gap; it is a missing required control.
- P-505, P-507, P-508: the absence of Finance approval is substantive, not a documentation artifact.

**Immediate case-level remediation.**

1. **P-501:** preserve the emergency form and engineering forecast. Confirm the SOP interpretation of B2 timing. If the organization later determines B2 is pre-commitment, treat as noncompliant and require compensating review.
2. **P-502 and P-507:** conduct an urgent supplier risk assessment for the new suppliers; obtain Compliance and Finance approval now as a mitigating record; escalate to the CRO and executive committee for possible ratification, but do not characterize ratification as a cure.
3. **P-503:** immediately segregate duties; remove requesters/committers from Incident Command authorizer roles. Perform a full Compliance and Finance review for the €140,000 purchase. Consider whether the purchase can be cancelled or returned.
4. **P-504:** stop using the unapproved supplier unless a normal Compliance clearance is obtained; remove the “site continuity” emergency label; process as a standard purchase.
5. **P-505 and P-508:** obtain late Finance and independent budget owner approval and document it as post-commitment mitigation. Add them to a post-commitment approval register.
6. **P-506:** require a written CRO extension if contamination-recovery purchases are still necessary. If no extension is possible, treat all future purchases as normal and do not rely on verbal Incident Command authority.

**Monitoring actions.**

- Require a single controlled time source and reconcile ERP clock offsets.
- Maintain a “commitments without pre-approval” register, reviewed weekly by Internal Audit.
- Add automated controls that block supplier commitment when:
  - amount  €50,000 and Finance/independent budget owner approval is missing;
  - amount > €100,000 or new supplier and Compliance approval is missing;
  - requester/committer is the final approver.
- Audit every emergency designation against the B4/ECD-2 definitions within 30 days.
- Track all ECD-2-window boundary cases, especially commitments within 30 minutes of expiry.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Proposed replacement hierarchy/exception clause:**

> Only the Chief Risk Officer, or a deputy designated in writing by the Chief Risk Officer, may suspend GPIP-4. Any suspension must be in writing, must identify the specific GPIP-4 clauses suspended, must name the affected scope, suppliers, and amounts, and must expire no later than 72 hours unless renewed by a successor written CRO instrument.
>
> An incident directive signed by the COO and Incident Commander is an operational directive, not a GPIP-4 exception. It may vary local SOP workflow sequence, including PO timing and approved-supplier registration, but it may not waive GPIP-4 A1 segregation, A2 pre-commitment financial approval, or A3 pre-commitment Compliance clearance.
>
> For purchases of €50,000 or more, an independent budget owner and Finance Controller approval are required before supplier commitment. For purchases above €100,000 or from a new supplier, Compliance clearance is required before supplier commitment. Joint Incident Command authorization cannot substitute for these approvals unless a CRO suspension expressly says otherwise.
>
> Verbal emergency authorizations must be recorded within four hours using the best available evidence. A later ERP timestamp will not override a signed PDF, call log, or vendor acknowledgment that establishes a different actual time.
>
> Emergency designations must be supported by a written statement of the specific emergency basis: imminent safety harm, production stoppage expected within six hours, or a named critical sterilization-line lot required for the current contamination event. Repetitive labels such as “site continuity” or “backup contingency” are not sufficient.
>
> Expiry is hard. Any extension must meet the same formality as the original exception. Approval after commitment mitigates consequences but does not cure a missing pre-commitment approval.
>
> Safeguard against emergency normalization: Internal Audit shall review all emergency procurements monthly and report the number, value, and basis of emergency labels. Any emergency designation that lacks contemporaneous objective evidence shall be reclassified as nonemergency and treated as a process failure.
