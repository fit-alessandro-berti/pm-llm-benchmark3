**Explanation (255 words).** *Shared mechanisms:* at both sites Assessment is gated on two completed verifications; when the two checks draw on independent data, replacing a serial sum by a maximum removes one activity and its queue wait from the critical path. That saving first appears as shorter pre-Assessment waiting and then as shorter cycle time, while rework and appeal are only weakly coupled (`true_overlap  waiting_before_assessment  cycle_time`; `rework  cycle_time  appeal`).

*Site-specific mechanisms:* at Site B a statutory Originals Receipt gate (62% of cases, 18h median) binds before Document Check, so for the majority the serial verification order is not the binding constraint — Identity Check (3.4h) normally finishes long before originals arrive; the parallelisable gap exists only for the ~34–38% whose Document Check follows Identity directly. Specialised pools, the single-owner IT lock, a 40% legal branch with a 10% supplement loop and a heavier tail turn parallelisation into a redistribution of WIP across pools (`treatment  document/assessment capacity`) rather than its removal.

*Effect modifiers:* `originals_required`, `digital_documents`, `IT_case_lock`, `role_specialization`, the four pool capacities (document and legal most binding), `case_complexity`, plus `queue_huddles` and `API_latency` as correlated co-interventions.

A similar control-flow graph guarantees only ordering equivalence. It says nothing about which gate binds, whether the IT can host two active owners, whether resources can absorb concurrent WIP, or how tail quantiles respond; transportability therefore depends on structural and resource context, not on diagram shape.

**Reasoning behind the ranges.**
*Critical-path arithmetic.* For eligible (no-originals) cases the removed segment is the identity path: 3.4h plus its queue  3–6h per treated case. For originals-required cases identity (3.4h) finishes well before originals (18h), so the marginal saving is 0–1h — only cases whose identity queue exceeds ~18h benefit. The serialparallel arithmetic at Site A gives (3.5 + 7.2)  max(3.1, 6.5) = 10.7  6.5 = **4.2h  47% of A's observed 9h**, i.e. even at A the structural change explains less than half the reported effect.
*Eligible share.* Rule-based: 100  62 = 38%. DFG-based: Identity Check  Document Check = 1,160/3,400 = **34.1%**; reconciliation 2,108 + 1,160 + 345 = 3,613  Document Check occurrences 3,260 + 345 = 3,605 (8-case tolerance).
*Population quantile argument.* Treated cases avoid the 18h originals delay, so they sit **below** Site B's 44h median. With p = 0.35,   4h and a density ratio f_treated/f_population of 0.5–1.5, the median shift is  p··ratio = 0.35 × 4 × (0.5–1.5)  **0.7–2.1h**, rounded to 0.5 to 2.5h. Near p90 = 110h the treated density is negligible, so the structural contribution to p90 is  0–3h; p90 moves only through downstream queue effects (S3, S5).
*Little's-law reconciliation.* Site A: 5,200 × (3.1 + 6.5)  49,900 reviewer-hours vs 20 FTE × 130d × 8h = 20,800h  **240%**. Site B document: 3,605 × 8.1  29,200h vs 7 × 130 × 8 = 7,280h  **401%**; identity: 3,070 × 3.4  10,440h vs 4 × 130 × 8 = 4,160h  **251%**. The reported "service" medians therefore bundle queueing. Consequence: part of A's service-time drop is queue relief from added reviewers, and at B much of the 8.1h document time is queueing that parallelisation *redistributes* rather than removes — the direct source of S3 and S5.
*Tail.* A's p90 18h coincided with complexity 48  40 and +2 FTE. Site B holds complexity at 48% with a heavier tail, 62% originals delay (18h) and 40% legal review, so a structural change alone cannot be expected to move p90 downward.

## 6. Candidate Site B intervention

**Eligibility rules**
- **E1 (primary treatment):** originals *not* legally required (38%, flag known at Register)  create `Identity Check` and `Document Check` concurrently at Register.
- **E2 (statutory path):** originals required (62%)  create `Identity Check` and `Originals Receipt` concurrently (already permitted); additionally create `Document Check` at Register in a **blocked/pending-originals** state that starts automatically when `Originals Receipt` completes. Completion before originals remains impossible.
- **E3 (loop):** after `Request Supplement`, recreate `Document Check` only (identity remains valid) unless the supplement explicitly invalidates identity; in-loop instances are excluded from first-pass treatment metrics.
- **E4:** legal-review cases are treated upstream but analysed as a separate stratum; cases with active manual overrides are excluded.

**Concurrent tasks** — maximum two active verification tasks per case: `Identity Check  Document Check` (E1) or `Identity Check  Originals Receipt` (E2). `Assessment` is never concurrent with verification; `Legal Review` never concurrent with `Assessment`.

**Statutory prerequisites preserved** — `Register  Originals Receipt  Document Check (completion, 62%)`; `Identity Check  Assessment` and `Document Check  Assessment`; `Assessment  Legal Review  Decision` for the 40%; `Decision  Notify`.

**Queue and WIP controls** — per-reviewer cap on open verification tasks (start at 4, Site A used 6); pool-level throttle: if the document pool has >8 open items or >85% utilisation, new Document tasks are created in serial mode (after Identity) for the next batch; ramped daily cap of parallel activations (start 20/day  3× eligible daily volume of 6.4, then to full eligible volume); automatic fallback to serial mode if the lock blocks dual ownership.

**Resource responsibilities** — identity reviewers own `Identity Check`; document reviewers own `Document Check` and originals follow-up; assessors and legal reviewers unchanged; exactly one owner per task (respects the lock); no cross-training assumed in phase 1 (optional phase 2).

**Legal-review and supplement handling** — legal review and its supplement loop are untouched; loop-triggered document re-checks are logged with a `parent_instance_id` and counted as rework, not as treatment failures.

**Logging requirements** — per task instance: `task_created`, `task_started`, `task_completed`, `source  {system, manual, back_entry}`, owner, pool; per case: eligibility flag, originals flag, arm, complexity, lock state at creation; daily snapshots: WIP per reviewer, queue length per pool, throttle events; treatment fidelity flag `true_concurrency_achieved`.

**Separating mechanism failure from implementation failure (175 words).** The pilot separates the two by measuring *fidelity* and *mediators before outcomes*. **Implementation failure** appears as low fidelity: tasks created late or not at all, dual-active-task rate near zero because of the case lock, the WIP throttle engaging constantly, or high back-entry rates. In those cases mechanism indicators (true overlap from `source=system` events, pre-Assessment waiting) should not move, and a null result says only that Site B did not deliver the treatment. **Mechanism failure** appears as high fidelity with moving mediators but static outcomes: dual-task execution is genuinely achieved and pre-Assessment waiting falls, yet median and p90 do not improve — meaning the removed serial segment was not the binding constraint (originals delay, assessment or legal queues dominate) or that new waiting is created downstream. Contamination between the two is handled by classifying every treated case as *delivered-and-benefited*, *delivered-and-neutral*, or *not-delivered* using task timestamps, source tags, throttle events and pool snapshots; only the first two classes inform transportability, the third informs implementation.

```python
def is_acyclic(nodes, order):
    """A strict partial order must be a DAG."""
    succ = {n: [] for n in nodes}
    for a, b in order:
        succ[a].append(b)
    colour = {n: 0 for n in nodes}                  # 0 white, 1 grey, 2 black

    def dfs(u):
        colour[u] = 1
        for v in succ[u]:
            if colour[v] == 1:
                return False
            if colour[v] == 0 and not dfs(v):
                return False
        colour[u] = 2
        return True

    return all(dfs(n) for n in nodes if colour[n] == 0)


def build_powl(nodes, order):
    """POWL constructor signatures differ between PM4Py versions."""
    assert is_acyclic(nodes, order), "POWL order must be a strict partial order"
    try:
        return POWL(nodes=nodes, order=order)
    except TypeError:
        m = POWL()
        for n in nodes:
            m.add_node(n)
        for a, b in order:
            try:
                m.add_order(a, b)
            except Exception:
                m.order.append((a, b))
        return m

# --- Optional legal branch (40%) with ONE unrolled supplement loop ---
# NB: a *strict* partial order is acyclic, so (a) a loop must be unrolled and
# (b) optionality (XOR) cannot be expressed inside one POWL node set: every node
# appears in every linearisation.  We therefore build the legal variant as a
# separate model and, where the API exposes an exclusive-choice operator, try to
# combine them below.
leg, sup = T("Legal Review"), T("Request Supplement")
doc2, ass2 = T("Document Check (2nd pass)"), T("Assessment (2nd pass)")

order_L = [
    (reg, idn), (reg, doc),
    (idn, ass), (doc, ass),
    (ass, leg),                 # legal branch reached after Assessment
    (leg, sup),                 # legal may issue a supplement
    (sup, doc2),                # loop re-entry into document checking
    (doc2, ass2), (idn, ass2),  # identity stays valid; document re-checked
    (ass2, appr), (ass2, rej),
    (appr, notf), (rej, notf),
]
assert (idn, ass) in order_L and (doc, ass) in order_L
nodes_L = [reg, idn, doc, ass, leg, sup, doc2, ass2, appr, rej, notf]
powl_L = build_powl(nodes_L, order_L)

# Optional XOR combination, only if the installed POWL API provides it.
try:
    from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import ExclusiveChoice          # e.g. XOR(node, node)
    powl_opt = ExclusiveChoice(powl_E, powl_L)
    print("Optional legal branch combined via ExclusiveChoice.")
except Exception:
    powl_opt = None
    print("No XOR operator in this POWL API: legal optionality is exported as "
          "two separate models (powl_E / powl_L) plus an XOR at net level.")


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4/5/6) convert, then check workflow-net and soundness properties
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
for name, model in (("Site B stratum E (parallel doc)", powl_E),
                    ("Site B stratum O (statutory gate)", powl_O),
                    ("Site B legal + supplement (unrolled)", powl_L)):
    net, im, fm = powl_to_pn(model)
    report(name, net, im, fm)
    sample_traces(name, net, im, fm)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7) what these models CANNOT encode
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# * Data-dependent legal requirements: whether originals are legally required
#   (62%) and whether Legal Review applies (40%) are case-attribute predicates,
#   not control-flow structure.  A single POWL/PN must be instantiated per
#   stratum; optionality (XOR) is not representable inside a strict partial
#   order, and a supplement loop is acyclic only when unrolled.
# * Resource capacity: the four specialised pools (4/7/3/2 FTE), the 6-task WIP
#   cap, pool utilisation and bottleneck shifts are invisible here; the net says
#   nothing about whether two tasks can execute *simultaneously* in real time.
# * Case locks: the legacy single-active-owner lock is an IT constraint outside
#   the model; the net happily fires two transitions that the system would refuse.
# * Durations and queueing: no timestamps, no waiting time, no 18h originals
#   delay, no p90 tail.  Cycle-time claims require simulation on top of this
#   structure with empirical inter-arrival and service distributions.
# * Measurement artefacts: timestamp back-entry and true overlap cannot be
#   distinguished by the model.
```

## 9. Recommendation

`conditional go`

**What should be transferred.** The structural idea, not Site A's number: concurrent verification *where and only where* no statutory gate binds. Concretely — (i) the partial-order change for the ~34–38% of Site B cases whose Document Check has no originals prerequisite (DFG support: 1,160/3,400 = 34%); (ii) concurrent creation of Identity Check and Originals Receipt for the 62%, now logged so that genuine overlap is measurable; (iii) Site A's WIP discipline (open-task cap, queue visibility, daily huddle) as a separately tracked co-intervention; (iv) task-level logging with source-tagged timestamps.

**What should not be copied.** Immediate Document Check creation for originals-required cases — illegal for 62%; Site A's assumed cross-training; Site A's 71% overlap (partly a timestamp artefact); Site A's 9h median and 18h p90 as expected effects (confounded by the API upgrade, +2 FTE, complexity 48  40 and huddles — arithmetic attributes only 4.2h, 47%, to the structural change); and any change to Assessment or Legal Review ordering.

**First subgroup.** Originals-not-required, non-loop, digitally registered cases (~1,150–1,300 per six months), entering only after a technical precondition is met: the case system supports two active owners, or audit-grade virtual task creation.

**Expected benefits and risks.** Benefits: 3–6h off the critical path within the eligible stratum; population median 0.5 to 2.5h (1–6%) under the quantile argument, because eligible cases sit below the 44h median; little or no p90 movement. Risks: WIP inflation on the 7-document and 3-assessor pools (p90 +3 to +10h), lock workarounds corrupting overlap measurement, and faster upstream arrivals bursting into the 2-FTE legal pool (p90 +5 to +15h, rework +1 to +3pp).

**Evidence triggers.** *Expand* when fidelity 80% dual-active rate, pre-Assessment waiting falls 15% in treated cases, p90 non-inferior (+5%), rework +0.5pp, and the direction replicates across complexity strata. *Modify* when mediators move but the tail worsens — add the pool throttle, cap saturation below 60%, or add document capacity first. *Abandon* when true concurrency cannot be achieved under the lock, p90 exceeds +10% for two consecutive weeks, or rework rises >2pp without an offsetting cycle-time gain.

## 10. Assumption register

1. **Reported "service" medians at both sites include queueing, not pure touch time** — why needed: Little's-law otherwise implies impossible utilisation (Site A: 5,200 × 9.6h  49,900h vs 20 FTE × 130d × 8h = 20,800h  240%; Site B document: 3,605 × 8.1h  29,200h vs 7 × 130 × 8 = 7,280h  401%) — how to test: instrument touch vs elapsed time per task for one month and reconcile against pool hours — consequence if false: parallelisation would remove more critical-path time than modelled (identity 3.4h fully off the path), raising expected gains, and pool sizing must be re-derived from scratch.
2. **The originals-required flag (62%) is known at registration and immutable for the case** — why needed: eligibility must be decidable at Register without creating an illegal Document Check task — how to test: cross-check the case attribute against the legal rule and run DFG/ILP conformance on a 200-case sample — consequence if false: eligibility misclassification and statutory breach; the pilot must be re-scoped around a later decision point.
3. **The legacy single-active-owner lock can be relaxed or bypassed with audit-grade virtual tasks for treated cases** — why needed: without it there is no true concurrency at all — how to test: IT technical spike with a sprint estimate and a dual-ownership test case — consequence if false: the intervention collapses into a logging change (scenario S4), true overlap stays 5%, and the expected effect is 0.
4. **Site A's 9h median and 18h p90 are bundle effects, not parallelisation effects** — why needed: avoid transporting a confounded magnitude — how to test: no design at A can resolve this (no control, no negative process); instead compare A's own arithmetic ((3.5+7.2)  max(3.1,6.5)  4.2h) with the observed 9h and estimate the effect de novo at B — consequence if false (i.e. the structural effect really is ~9h): B's gains could be 2–3× our ranges and the pilot should be powered for a larger effect.
5. **No-originals cases are systematically faster than Site B's median (they avoid the 18h originals delay)** — why needed: it underpins the quantile argument that the population median barely moves (0.5 to 2.5h) — how to test: compare cycle-time distributions by originals flag before randomisation — consequence if false: eligible cases straddle the 44h median, so the population median shift could be much larger (or the eligible stratum may be the hard cases, reversing expectations).
6. **Document (7 FTE) and legal (2 FTE) pools are not already saturated** — why needed: concurrency creates value only if spare capacity exists; otherwise it creates queueing — how to test: eight weeks of queue length, utilisation and time-in-pool per pool — consequence if false: scenarios S3/S5 dominate (median up, p90 +3 to +15h) and capacity must be added or a throttle imposed before any rollout.
7. **Site A's overlap jump (8%  71%) is materially inflated by improved timestamp capture** — why needed: to discount the mechanism indicator when judging how much of A's "mechanism" is real — how to test: audit A's timestamp provenance (auto vs manual) and examine a negative-control event whose rules did not change — consequence if false: A's true concurrency gain is genuine, raising confidence in the mechanism — though eligibility gating at B still caps achievable overlap at ~34–38%.
8. **Site B's Assessment genuinely requires both verifications and Legal Review genuinely applies to 40%** — why needed: these facts define the order edges, the strata and the downstream gate — how to test: written legal opinion plus DFG/ILP conformance checking over the 3,400 cases (Document Check  Assessment 3,260; Assessment  Legal Review 1,360) — consequence if false: either the eligible set is larger (more cases can be parallelised) or hidden prerequisites exist that invalidate the pilot strata and the POWL structure.
