## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation
The 0.38 fitness collapse in March between raw (0.55) and mapped (0.90) model fitness implies an acute semantic and logging shift rather than behavioral divergence. The normative model expects `Validate Eligibility`; substituting `Eligibility Screen` generates massive alignment penalties on token replay, yet process execution remains unchanged. This gap does not imply process failure or compliance breakdown, nor does recovering conformance via label mapping prove the underlying business process remained identical. A relabeling mapping merely harmonizes vocabulary; it cannot detect whether screening criteria were relaxed, automated, or outsourced behind the new label.

Duplicated technical events and resource missingness affect analytical dimensions differently. The duplicated `Open Case` events (4–6%) corrupt start-activity distributions, artificially inflate case length, and violate log skeleton uniqueness constraints (`is-exactly-1`), but token replay easily skips them with minor silent transitions. Conversely, missing resource identifiers (jumping from 1.0% to 6.5%) severely undermine organizational mining, handover-of-work sociograms, and capacity modeling, though leaving control-flow conformance intact.

Critically, post-May patterns cannot be dismissed as logging drift alone. From May onward, mapped fitness itself declines (dropping to 0.86), while operational metrics deteriorate dramatically: rework variants more than double (12% to 29%), median cycle times increase by 56% (31.2h to 49.0h), and tail latency expands from 68h to 121h. Crucially, the normalized DFG reveals a doubling of `Assess -> Request Info` loops (12 to 29 per 100 cases). These symptoms reflect genuine behavioral churn, supplier defects, and queueing bottlenecks that logging transformations cannot account for.

### 2. Normalized DFG-Distance Analysis
The distance is computed on the 7-dimensional normalized relation vector $v = [r_1, \dots, r_7]$ per 100 cases, measuring relative transition rates:
- **Distance Metric:** Normalized Manhattan ($L_1$) distance $d_{L1}(v_t, v_{\text{base}}) = \sum_{k=1}^7 |v_t(k) - v_{\text{base}}(k)|$, Euclidean ($L_2$) distance $d_{L2}(v_t, v_{\text{base}}) = \sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^7 (v_t(k) - v_{\text{base}}(k))^2}$, and month-to-month shift $\Delta L_{1}(t) = \sum_{k=1}^7 |v_t(k) - v_{t-1}(k)|$, evaluated against the January baseline vector $v_{\text{base}} = [100, 98, 95, 12, 11, 4, 83]$.

**Assumptions about omitted edges:** The supplied relation rates capture core routing decisions (rework looping and senior assessment). Omitted edges (e.g., `Decision -> Close`, `Open Case -> Register`, parallel synchronization arcs) are assumed to remain structurally stationary or scale strictly proportionally with upstream throughput. Thus, this 7-relation distance represents an exact lower bound on the full graph edit distance.

### 3. Decomposition of Cycle-Time Deterioration
From the Jan–Feb baseline (Median: 31.15h, P90: 68.0h) to peak summer (July: Median 44.2h, P90 104h; $\Delta$Med = +13.05h, $\Delta$P90 = +36h) and peak autumn (November: Median 49.0h, P90 121h; $\Delta$Med = +17.85h, $\Delta$P90 = +53h):

1. **Service Time Component (4%–6% of median increase; ~0.6h to 0.8h):**
   - Direct median service time for `Assess` increased modestly from 4.8h to 5.4h (+0.6h). In July, this accounts for ~0.6h of the 13.05h median cycle-time increase (~4.6%). In November, service creep contributes ~0.6h–0.8h (~4.5%).
2. **Control-Flow / Rework Component (6%–8% of median increase; ~0.8h to 1.0h direct):**
   - The rework variant rate rose from 11.5% to 29.0% (+17.5 percentage points). Each rework loop adds a repeat assessment (5.4h) plus document re-collection. Across the entire case population, additional direct processing work from rework loops contributes $\approx 0.175 \times 4.8\text{h} \approx 0.84\text{h}$ (~6.4% in July; ~0.85h in November).
3. **Case-Mix Component (2%–9% of median increase; ~0.2h in July, ~1.4h in November):**
   - In July, high-complexity cases rose modestly from 20.5% to 28.0% (+7.5 pp). With an estimated 2.0h processing differential, case mix directly accounts for $\approx 0.075 \times 2.0\text{h} \approx 0.15\text{h}$ (~1.2%).
   - In November, high-complexity cases surged to 45.0% (+24.5 pp), and Senior Assessments tripled to 22 per 100 cases. Senior reviews require dedicated dual-assessor evaluation (~6.0h extra work), adding $\approx 0.245 \times 6.0\text{h} \approx 1.47\text{h}$ directly to the median (~8.2%).
4. **Queueing and Congestion Component (78%–88% of median increase; >85% of P90 increase):**
   - In July, queueing accounts for $\approx 13.05 - (0.60 + 0.84 + 0.15) = 11.46\text{h}$ (~87.8% of median growth, and over 30h of the P90 expansion). This was triggered by the June 15 loss of 4 assessors without replacement, reducing processing capacity while rework loops reinjected cases into the assessment queue, pushing server utilization close to saturation ($\rho \approx 0.95$).
   - In November, queueing accounts for $\approx 17.85 - (0.75 + 0.85 + 1.47) = 14.78\text{h}$ (~82.8% of median growth, and over 44h of P90 growth), driven by total case arrivals surging to 790 (+29% volume) against static depleted staffing.

### 4. Candidate Change Points by Dimension
- **Logging / Semantic Drift:**
  1. *2026-03-01 (Primary):* Raw fitness collapses from 0.93 to 0.55; label `Eligibility Screen` appears; technical `Open Case` duplication jumps from 0% to 4%; missing resource IDs jump from 1.0% to 4.8%.
  2. *2026-07-01 (Secondary):* Peak instrumentation missingness; missing resource IDs reach 6.5% and duplicate `Open Case` events reach 6.0%.
- **Behavioral / Control-Flow Drift:**
  1. *2026-05-05 (Primary):* Supplier Z disruption onset; rework variants jump from 13% to 24%; normalized `Assess -> Request Info` doubles from 12 to 24 per 100 cases; fast variant drops from 55% to 45%.
  2. *2026-10-01 (Secondary):* Annual renewal onset; `Assess -> SeniorAssessment` jumps from 7 to 22 per 100 cases; senior decision volume jumps to 160 cases.
- **Performance / Latency Drift:**
  1. *2026-05-01 to 2026-06-01 (Initial):* Median cycle time increases from 32.0h to 36.5h, and P90 increases from 71h to 84h, driven by rework loop execution.
  2. *2026-06-15 to 2026-07-01 (Acute):* Median cycle time leaps from 40.8h to 44.2h, and P90 leaps from 95h to 104h and 110h, triggered by the departure of 4 assessors and queue prioritization.

### 5. Single Global vs. Multiple Mechanism-Specific Change Points
The data decisively refute a single global change point. A single change point (e.g., placing a change point at March 1, 2026) conflates a pure semantic schema shift with subsequent operational breakdown, failing to explain why cycle times in March (31.2h) remained completely unchanged from baseline (31.15h). Conversely, placing a single change point in May or June ignores the logging rupture in March and masks the distinct October renewal shock. The empirical evidence demonstrates asynchronous, mechanism-specific change points: logging drift at March 1, supplier-induced rework drift at May 5, staffing-induced queueing drift at June 15, priority-induced tail dispersion at July 1, seasonal population drift at October 1, and intervention-driven abatement at December 5.

### Primary Monitoring Population
**Primary Population Definition:** `Supplier == Non-Supplier Z` $\cap$ `Complexity == Standard Complexity` $\cap$ `Duplicate Open Case == False` $\cap$ `Resource != Null`.

**Justification:** This homogeneous sub-population serves as the unpolluted "control group" for the organization. By filtering out Supplier Z, it removes the external document-defect confounder; by restricting to standard complexity, it eliminates senior assessment routing noise and priority dispatch bias; and by removing duplicate events and unassigned resources, it shields downstream analytics from known logging glitches. Any detected drift in this reference population reliably reflects true organizational changes, baseline assessor productivity shifts, or fundamental IT schema evolutions.

### Handling Seasonality
1. **Seasonally Stratified Baselines:** Rather than comparing Q4 (October–December) renewal periods against immediate Q3 summer windows, compare Q4 metrics against historical renewal baselines (e.g., October–November 2025).
2. **Dynamic Decomposition:** Implement STL (Seasonal and Trend decomposition using Loess) on daily volume and complexity metrics, generating dynamic alert thresholds ($\pm 3$ seasonal standard deviations) that adjust automatically for expected annual cycles.

### Handling Multiple Testing
1. **False Discovery Rate (FDR) Control:** Apply the Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) procedure across the family of statistical monitor tests run concurrently each monitoring cycle ($\alpha = 0.05$).
2. **Hierarchical Gating:** Evaluate monitors hierarchically: if the Schema/Logging monitor fires, downstream Conformance and DFG alerts are flagged as "suspected logging artifacts" rather than operational crises, preventing alert storms.

def format_event_log(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    case_col: str = "case_id",
    act_col: str = "activity",
    time_col: str = "timestamp",
    resource_col: Optional[str] = "resource"
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Format and standardize an incoming pandas dataframe to PM4Py event log conventions."""
    formatted_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id=case_col,
        activity_key=act_col,
        timestamp_key=time_col
    )
    if resource_col and resource_col in df.columns:
        formatted_df["org:resource"] = df[resource_col]
    return formatted_df

def slice_log_window(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    start_time: str,
    end_time: str,
    mode: str = "events"
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Slice an event log to a defined time window using pm4py.filter_time_range."""
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(
        df,
        dt1=start_time,
        dt2=end_time,
        mode=mode,
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name"
    )

def discover_monthly_dfg(
    df: pd.DataFrame
) -> Tuple[Dict[Tuple[str, str], int], Dict[str, int], Dict[str, int]]:

        df,

def discover_declarative_constraints(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    noise_threshold: float = 0.05
) -> Tuple[Dict[str, Any], Optional[Dict[str, Any]]]:
    """Discover log skeletons and DECLARE constraint models for structural conformance."""
    log_skeleton = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(
        df,
        noise_threshold=noise_threshold,
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        case_id_key="case:concept:name"
    )
    try:
        declare_model = pm4py.discover_declare(
            df,
            activity_key="concept:name",
            timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
            case_id_key="case:concept:name"
        )
    except Exception:
        declare_model = None
    return log_skeleton, declare_model

def compare_raw_and_normalized_views(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    label_mapping: Optional[Dict[str, str]] = None,
    remove_duplicates: Optional[List[str]] = None
) -> Tuple[pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame]:
    """Produce normalized event log view alongside immutable raw log for non-destructive comparison."""
    raw_view = df.copy(deep=True)
    normalized_view = df.copy(deep=True)

    if label_mapping:
        normalized_view["concept:name"] = normalized_view["concept:name"].replace(label_mapping)

    if remove_duplicates:
        for activity_name in remove_duplicates:
            is_target_act = normalized_view["concept:name"] == activity_name
            is_duplicate = normalized_view.duplicated(subset=["case:concept:name", "concept:name"])
            normalized_view = normalized_view[~(is_target_act & is_duplicate)]

    return raw_view, normalized_view

## 9. Provisional attribution
The observed drifts across the document-eligibility process stem from multiple asynchronous, interacting mechanisms rather than a single event.

Our strongest conclusions involve the March logging disruption and the May supplier shock (Very High Confidence, 95% and 90%). The March plunge in raw conformance (0.93 to 0.55) alongside unchanged cycle times (31.2h) and restored mapped fitness (0.90) establishes a pure logging/semantic shift caused by system migration relabeling and technical artifact duplication. Similarly, the May inflection—where Supplier Z cases surged from 18% to 43% and rework variants jumped from 13% to 24%—is definitively driven by Supplier Z’s bundle defects, as verified by the immediate doubling of normalized `Assess -> Request Info` transitions (12 to 24 per 100). The October–November escalation (volume reaching 790, high complexity 45%, senior assessments surging to 22 per 100) is strongly attributed to recurring seasonal case-mix drift (High Confidence, 90%).

Conclusions regarding queueing amplification and the July priority dispatch rule remain plausible but less direct (Medium-High Confidence, 75–85%). While the June 15 departure of four assessors logically explains why median cycle times climbed from 40.8h to 45.0h despite stable Assess service times (4.8h to 5.4h), aggregate reporting obscures queue dynamics. The July 1 priority rule plausibly accounts for the severe expansion in p90 tail latency (reaching 110h in August), but without sub-cohort wait times, we cannot definitively separate priority-induced starvation of standard cases from the lingering Supplier Z backlog.

Critically, mediation and multi-mechanism interaction prevent clean univariate attribution between June and August. Supplier Z’s rework loops injected re-entrant demand into the assessment stage precisely as assessor staffing contracted, compounding queue utilization non-linearly via Kingman’s equation.

This attribution would be revised if trace-level log inspection revealed: (1) that `Eligibility Screen` introduced subtle workflow branching or execution skips not captured by mapped replay; (2) that internal assessor policy revisions, rather than Supplier Z defect bundles, caused the May surge in info requests; or (3) that seasonal renewal cases exhibited higher internal error rates independent of case complexity.
