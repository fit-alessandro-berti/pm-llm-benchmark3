# 1. System boundary and assumptions

## 1.1 Scope definition (ordertodelivery, cradletogate + inuse distribution + failure/obsolescence)

I use a **"sparepart ordertodelivery" boundary** that covers everything the enriched event log can observe: inhouse production energy and scrap, packaging, outbound distribution, delivery performance, and the failure loops (rework, packaging rework, returns, partial/second shipments, cancellation, obsolescence).

**Inside the boundary (measured with event attributes):**

| Impact source | Attribute(s) | Boundary note |
|---|---|---|
| Inhouse machining/heat/inspection energy | `energy_kwh`, `machine_group` (CNC_A, CNC_B, heat_treat, inspection_cell) | Batchshared operations must be allocated per item in `batch_id`/`batch_size`; includes setup and idle where the logger captures it |
| Conversion loss / scrap | `material_scrap_kg`, `rework_flag` | Includes scrap created *inside* rework loops (Inspection  Rework production  CNC production) and postrework discard |
| Packaging | `package_material_kg`, Package standard / Package reinforced / Rework packaging | Includes the packagingrework loop ( 620 reworkpackaging events,  6.5 % of the  9,550 shipment attempts) |
| Outbound transport | `transport_kgco2e`, `route_mode` | All four modes, **including the second shipment** created by the partialshipment/backorder path |
| Failuredriven deadweight | Cancelduetodelay cases (530 = 4.4 %) | All energy, scrap and packaging consumed for an order that never delivered counts as pure loss |
| Obsolescence | `batch_id` + ship timestamp, 14day rule | 6 % of batchproduced items are not shipped within 14 days; if scrapped, the *entire* embedded energy and material is loss |
| Service benefit | `promised_lead_time_hours`, late delivery, `expedite_flag` | Held as a **constraint**, not converted to COe (see below) |

**Outside the boundary (declared, not counted):**

1. **Upstream** rawmaterial extraction, melt and inbound logistics for bar/blank stock. Directionally proxied by `material_scrap_kg` (more scrap  more upstream input), not quantified.
2. **Customer usephase and endoflife of the part**, plus the customer machine's own LCA — standard cutoff for a sparepart distributor.
3. **Capital goods and facilities**: carrier vehicle manufacture, warehouse build, racking, MHE.
4. **IT estate and the intervention stack itself** (processmining platform, the S1 recommender, the S12 exception log). Compared with the inscope  89,000 kWh of order energy (7.4 kWh/order × 12,000), this is immaterial — but it is declared, because S1/S12 add compute and administrative work.
5. **Avoided customer downtime emissions** (idle customer plant, rescheduling, secondary logistics). This is a *real* environmental credit of rapid Criticaldowntime fulfilment. I deliberately **do not monetise it** — instead it is the reason air and dedicatedvan transport are retained for the truedowntime lane. Converting it to COe with a proxy factor would create false precision and would dominate every other number.

**Why this matters for the answer:** spare parts exist to prevent customer production loss. A "lowestemission" policy that delivers a Critical part 24 h late does not reduce system impact — it displaces impact onto the customer's plant while destroying the service contract. The boundary therefore treats *service and resilience as constraints*, and *impact as the objective to be minimised subject to those constraints*.

## 1.2 Assumptions

- **Unit of analysis is the order, not the line item.** The log is flattened by order although orders contain 1+ line items. Any "per order" improvement can be faked by splitting orders into smaller ones (or by shipping fewer items per order), so all intensity indicators are paired with an ordersize/composition indicator.
- **Path averages are cohort averages, not marginal rates.** The 3.6 kgCOe (ground path) vs 18.5 kgCOe (air path) values are mixadjusted. Moving a cohort from air to ground will not realise the full difference.
- **Dedicated van "return empty"** is stated as evidence but has **no attribute** in the log. Fillrate/returnload must be proxied (see §2, Rindicators).
- **Heattreatment and inspection energy is batchshared**, so batching decisions move both the numerator (`energy_kwh`) and the waiting time; the allocation rule is an assumption, not an observation.
- **SP1's "probability > 85 %"** requires a leadtime *distribution* per mode and lane. The log has `promised_lead_time_hours` and observed cycle times per path; perlane reliability is only partially derivable — a perlane leadtime table must be built from the log before S1 can be switched on.
- **Triage evidence for S9** (true downtime vs precautionary) is not currently in the log; it must be added as an attribute before the split is executed.

## 1.3 Residual uncertainty and dataquality flags

- **Counts do not reconcile exactly** (ship events  9,550; Confirm delivery  Close = 11,200; cancellations 530;  270 orders neither closed nor cancelled at extraction). This is consistent with multishipment orders (partial + backorder second shipment) and latearriving confirmations. All percentages in this answer are **per order** unless stated; loop counts are per event and can exceed 100 % of orders.
- **Reworkproduction exits are untraced** (470 enter, 390 loop back to CNC;  80 cases unaccounted) — likely scrapandredo or a logging gap. Must be closed before rework impact is claimed.
- **The discovered model has low precision** because emergency paths and partial shipments combine combinatorially. Conclusions are therefore stratified by the five segments, never asserted globally.
- **Embodied carbon of packaging is not in the log** (only kg). "Doubling package material" is reported in kg, not COe.

### S1. Green shipping recommender (slack + reliability + segment aware)

Substitutes transport emissions for a picking/booking decision (no energy or material change).
- **Service/quality/resilience:** service protected by the 85 % reliability test rather than by averages; main risk is **recommendation error on perlane reliability** (tail late deliveries) and silent planner override. - **Evidence to monitor:** van share, R3 fill/emptyreturn proxy, Critical late % (4 % baseline), C2 justification coverage, sharedlane wait vs `promised_lead_time_hours`.

### S9. Split Criticaldowntime into true downtime and precautionary urgent
- **Target path/segment:** Availability check  Emergency production (760)  CNC  Package reinforced  Dedicated van (the 8 % variant: 7.0 kWh, 52.0 kgCOe); plus the existing 3 % "Critical  stock/ground exception" (1.2 kWh, 4.8 kgCOe).
- **Mechanism:** the log already shows a lowimpact Critical path exists (3 % of cases). By triaging at Availability check on *evidence of a customer machine stopped* (downtime ticket / machinedown flag — a new required attribute), the premium lane is reserved for true downtime and precautionary cases move to stock/ground or a shared lane. This removes the largest single transport hot spot without touching genuine downtime protection.
- **Service/quality/resilience:** transport , energy . The dominant risk is **mistriage** (a truedowntime case routed to ground) which is a severe service failure  require hard evidence to enter the premium lane and a samehour escalation override.
- **Evidence to monitor:** subcategory share, late % by subcategory, mistriage escalations, `expedite_flag` inflation, SP2 justification quality.

### S10. Carbonaware promisedate negotiation for Regular madetoorder customers
- **Target path/segment:** Plan production  Batch wait (Regular MTO, 2,640 orders, 22 % of volume, 18 % late).
- **Mechanism:** converts *hidden* batching slack into *explicit, customeragreed* slack: offer a later `promised_lead_time_hours` at a small discount so the order can join a 12–20 CNC_A batch (2.8  1.3 kWh/item) and a ground/consolidated departure. Accepting a longer promise is the cheapest way to buy batching and consolidation benefits without service loss, because the promise itself is rebaselined.
- **Service/quality/resilience:** energy , transport , cycle time  *by agreement*, late % can actually **fall** if the promise is set with a buffer. Obsolescence risk  if the batch is built ahead of confirmed acceptance  batch only accepted orders. Resilience: avoid creating a single "green lane" as the only lane.
- **Evidence to monitor:** offer accept/decline rate by customer, promised vs actual (S5n), B1/B2, O1, segment late % and p90.

### S11. Reduce partial shipments by changing availabilitycheck rules
- **Target path/segment:** Pick from stock  Partial shipment (1,010 = 14.3 % of stock picks)  Backorder wait (220)  second Pick/second shipment, and the 60 cancellations (27 % of backorder waits).
- **Mechanism:** the partial path is the worst path in the table (79 h median, 24 % late, 1.4 kWh, 7.2 kgCOe  2× the singleshipment ground path, 0.08 kg scrap): it duplicates packaging and transport. A short "completeship window" (ship complete if the missing line is expected within N hours) removes the second shipment.
- **Service/quality/resilience:** transport , material . But **this is the classic tradeoff trap**: holding the order delays a part the customer may need now, and the observed 27 % backordercancel rate shows customers sometimes walk away entirely (a cancelled order is 100 % waste). Therefore apply only to Standardstock Regular (and to Stock with slack  agreed window); never to Critical or tightslack Servicecontract.
- **Evidence to monitor:** S2n partial rate, T4 second shipments, S3n backordercancel, S6n customer contact load, segment late %.

### S12. Sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides (crosscutting enabler)
- **Target/segment:** all override points (mode downgrades/upgrades, packaging optouts, van justification, batchcap breaches).
- **Mechanism:** no direct environmental effect — it is the **measurement instrument** for everything else. Mandatory reason code + named approver + timestamp on every override turns "silent nonconformance" (SP1 22 %, SP2 31 % missing justification) into analysable data, and lets SP1's 85 % threshold be recalibrated against observed ontime rates.
- **Service/quality/resilience:** quality/resilience  (traceability, faster audits); slight administrative cycle  at the decision point. Without it, S1–S11 cannot be validated and hidden tradeoffs migrate into unlogged manual behaviour.
- **Evidence to monitor:** C4 override rate per 1,000 orders, reasoncode completeness, override outcome (was the delivery actually saved?), and the correlation of overrides with late deliveries.

# 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching rebound (S2, S10).** The energy case for big batches (2.8  1.3 kWh/item; heat treatment lowest > batch 10) is real, but the log shows the same lever raises batch wait from 4 h to 38 h, raises late risk 1.7× when a case waits for a heat batch, and puts **6 % of batch items past 14 days unsold**. If stale stock is scrapped, the "saved" 1.5 kWh/item is swamped by full embedded material + full incurred energy on an item that never delivers service. Net effect can invert. *Mitigation:* batch quantity bounded by confirmed/accepted demand (tie to S10 acceptance), hard wait caps by segment, O1/O2 killswitch.

2. **Transportsubstitution rebound (S4, S5).** Mode shift is not the same as shipment elimination. Consolidation adds 12–24 h; if a case then breaches its promise, the response is air or a dedicated van at 4–6× or worse — a *single* missed consolidation window can undo a week of ground shipments. Similarly, "priority pick + ground" adds picking events that can push the pick queue and trigger more expedites. *Mitigation:* R5 slackexposure monitoring; never route a case whose entire slack is consumed by one departure window.

3. **Packaging tradeoff displacement (S6, Q1).** Reinforced packaging cuts damagerelated returns 40 % but doubles `package_material_kg`. Two failure directions: (a) pushing reinforced everywhere to satisfy Q1 raises material impact on every order including light stock parts; (b) thinning packaging to save material raises damage returns, which generate return transport, remanufacture or scrap (1.8 kg on the rework path) and customercontact events. The packagingrework loop (620 events) shows a third mode: the "fix" moves waste from material into handling labour/energy and dwell time. *Mitigation:* a geometry × route × weight matrix with inspectioncertified optdown, monitored on M1 **and** Q3n **and** return rate together.

4. **Reworkqueue displacement (S3, S7).** Shifting work to CNC_A halves rework but lengthens CNC_A and inspection_cell queues — and the log states explicitly that **queues at inspection_cell cause the rushed inspections that generate rework loops**. The intervention can therefore reproduce the defect mechanism it targets. Longer inspection dwell also delays delivery, which invites air usage (transport rebound). *Mitigation:* minimum inspection dwell rule (protect the gate from queue pressure), queuedepth cap on S3 routing, keep CNC_B warm as a surge lane (resilience + load levelling).

5. **Cancellation and deadweight displacement (S11).** Suppressing partial shipments holds orders longer. On the partial path the observed outcome is 24 % late and **27 % of backorder waits end in cancellation** (60/220). A cancelled order has already consumed energy, scrap and packaging and delivers zero service — so a "greener" completeship rule can *increase* total impact per unit of service delivered. *Mitigation:* restrict S11 to Stock Regular, cap the completeship window by `promised_lead_time_hours`, and count cancelled orders' full impacts as loss in every KPI.

6. **Customerbehaviour rebound (S10, S9).** Cheaper, slower "green" dates invite earlier ordering and larger safetystock orders from customers — precisely the demand lumpiness that drives big batches and the 6 % obsolescence. Conversely, if "true downtime" unlocks the premium lane, Servicecontract customers learn to claim downtime: `expedite_flag` inflation, SP2 justifications that are theatre (31 % already missing), and van/air use rising again. *Mitigation:* triage on external evidence (machinedown ticket), audit a sample of justifications monthly, monitor `expedite_flag` rate as a leading rebound indicator.

7. **Selection / casemix displacement.** Perorder intensities can fall while absolute totals rise if "green" policies make slow cases unattractive and the surviving mix is expediteheavy. Because the log is flattened **by order**, a customer who splits orders to stay under a consolidation or packaging threshold lowers "per order" impact while raising perpart impact. *Mitigation:* G1 and G2 reported together; add lineitemsperorder and orderspercustomer as mix indicators.

8. **Resilience displacement (S4, S8).** Removing air for stockservice cases and vans for precautionary urgent leaves both modes underexercised. During a carrier disruption, R1 ("at least one alternative route for Servicecontract") is then violated *silently*, and the lastresort override is bought at unlimited cost and emissions. *Mitigation:* R1n routediversity floor per rolling 30 days, mandatory quarterly "dry run" of the backup mode on lowvolume lanes, van retained as documented override.

9. **Machineenergy allocation displacement (S2, heat treatment).** Heattreatment peritem energy is lowest at batch > 10 because fixed energy is amortised. If low utilisation or idle time is inside `energy_kwh`, batching gains can be partly an accounting artefact; if batching raises peak demand or idle runs, the real energy gain is smaller than the logged one. *Mitigation:* split logged energy into run/idle/setup before claiming S2 savings.

10. **Digital/administrative rebound (S1, S12).** The recommender and exception log add compute and planner workload. Immaterial against  89,000 kWh inscope energy, but it must be declared in the boundary (§1) so that it is not silently ignored when the stack grows.

# 6. Balanced recommendation

## 6.1 The package: "Promiseaware, qualityfirst, modedisciplined fulfilration"

Not a menu of green ideas — a fourlayer redesign in which every layer is instrumented before the next is switched on.

**Layer 0 — Instrumentation (S12 + §2 indicators), weeks 0–4.**
Nothing else can be trusted until SP2's 31 % missing justifications and SP1's silent 22 % violations are visible as data. Deliver: segmentstratified baselines for G1/G2, the exception log with reason codes, and the perlane leadtime/reliability table that S1 needs.

**Layer 1 — Demand and promise (S9 + S10 + S11 + S1).**
One triage decision at Availability check, evidenced:
- **Lane A — Critical, machine stopped (hard evidence):** no batching wait, reinforced packaging, fastest available mode (dedicated van or air), emergency production allowed. *This lane keeps the highestemission options and that is correct.*
- **Lane B — Critical precautionaryurgent and Servicecontract with slack  30 h:** shared emergency lane or consolidated/ground + priority pick; van only as documented override (S8/S9).
- **Lane C — Stock and Regular MTO with generous slack:** carbonaware promise negotiation (S10)  batchfriendly dates and consolidated departures (S5); completeship window instead of partial shipments (S11).
- The **S1 recommender** governs the final mode choice in Lanes B/C using the SP1 85 % test and always presenting  2 modes.

**Layer 2 — Production (S2 + S3 + S7).**
- Dynamic batching with **hard** wait caps (Critical 0 h, Servicecontract MTO  +12 h, Regular MTO  +24 h), batch size pushed to the 12–20 band **only** for confirmed/accepted demand, with a 14day staleness killswitch (O1).
- Routing preference to CNC_A when slack and queue allow, but with a load cap so CNC_A/inspection_cell do not become the new single point of failure and so inspection queues do not recreate rushed inspections.
- **Inspection quality gate first**: minimum dwell + second check for CNC_B parts and > 8 kg geometry. S7 leads the package because it is the only lever that lowers energy, scrap, transport *and* rework at once — and rework is explicitly identified as "hidden material waste".

**Layer 3 — Packaging and logistics (S6 + Q1 + S4 + S5 + S8).**
- Packaging × route × geometry compatibility matrix at packaging close, with Q1 enforced (reinforced for MTO > 8 kg unless inspection certifies low damage risk) — *not* "reinforced everywhere".
- Air for Standardstock Servicecontract replaced by priority pick + ground **only** where the SP1 test passes (target: the 22 %  < 5 %); air retained for slack < 24 h.
- Twicedaily consolidation for Stock Regular and Lane B/C nonurgent volumes; shared emergency lanes for Lane B; **dedicated van retained** for Lane A and as the documented backup.

## 6.2 Options deliberately rejected (constraint compliance)

| Rejected idea | Why |
|---|---|
| Blanket ban on air / dedicated van | Violates service on truedowntime cases (the 8 % path protects customer uptime) and breaks R1 by atrophying the alternative route; also ignores tightslack Servicecontract cases |
| Always largest batch ( 20) | 6 % obsolescence > 14 days and 1.7× heatbatch late risk invert the net effect; energy gain is only 1.3 vs 2.8 kWh/item at 12–20 |
| Always consolidate all shipments | 12–24 h wait destroys Critical (15 h median cycle) and tightslack cases; a missed window triggers air |
| Always reinforced packaging | `package_material_kg` doubles on 6,800 standardpack events, including 0.05 kg light stock parts |
| Mandatory "no partial shipments" | The 79 h / 24 %-late backorder path is bad, but 27 % of backorder waits end in cancellation — a cancelled order is total waste; and some customers genuinely need the first line now |
| Retire CNC_B entirely | Removes surge capacity and creates a machinegroup single point of failure (R4); S3 handles the quality problem at its source |

## 6.3 Why this is balanced

- **Impact is targeted where the data puts it.** Madetoorder is 34 % of orders but  60 % of energy and  85 % of scrap (derived); Criticaldowntime is 11 % of orders but  41 % of transport; Standardstock Servicetract is 16 % of orders and overindexed on transport (12 % of total at 7.8 vs 4.2 kgCOe/order) *because of avoidable SP1 violations*. S2/S3/S7 hit the first, S9/S8 the second, S4 the third.
- **Service is protected by tests, not averages.** The 85 % SP1 reliability test and hard persegment wait caps mean no green decision is taken on a case that is likely to go late. Premium modes survive exactly where speed has genuine service (and therefore customerdowntime) value.
- **Quality leads because it is multibeneficial.** Rework/returns are treated as an environmental impact class, not just a quality KPI: a rework loop consumes 32 kWh and 1.8 kg scrap against 16 kWh and 0.70 kg first pass.
- **Resilience is bought, not assumed.** Air, van, CNC_B and sameday ground are retained as underexercised but *exercised* backups (R1n diversity floor), because a policy that minimises today's emissions by deleting all redundancy will buy its savings back at unlimited cost during the next carrier disruption.
- **Rebound is priced in up front** (batch caps tied to confirmed demand, ordersplitting and `expedite_flag` inflation monitored, cancellation waste counted), not discovered later.

# 7. Validation and governance plan

## 7.1 Study design (avoiding a false before/after)

- **Phased, segmentstratified rollout**: Regular MTO first (lowest service criticality, biggest energy pool)  Standardstock (S4/S5)  Critical (S9/S8) last, after triage evidence exists.
- **Matched comparison, not global before/after**: match cases on `case_type`, `customer_type`, `promised_lead_time_hours` band and order composition. The discovered model has low precision and case mix shifts seasonally, so a global aggregate would attribute mix change to the intervention.
- **Randomised holdout (~5 %)** where the planner (not the recommender) chooses the mode, to detect silent service decay and selection effects in S1.
- **Counterfactual replay** on the enriched log: recompute `transport_kgco2e` if SP1 had been followed, and `energy_kwh`/scrap if rework loops had been avoided — this separates *policy* effect from *volume* effect.

## 7.2 Postimplementation measurement on the event log

Fourweek pre/post windows per segment, reporting **G1 (kgCOe per ontime, firstpass delivery) and G2 (absolute totals)** side by side:

|---|---|---|
| Energy | E1, E2, E3 by `machine_group` and batch band | E1  in MTO without E2 worsening; batching gain confirmed after splitting run/idle/setup |
| Transport | T1, T2, T3, T4 | T1  and T2  (not just T1); secondshipment rate  |
| Material | M1, M2, M3 | scrap per delivered order , packaging per order  or  **and** damage returns  |
| Rework/quality | Q1n (88 % baseline), Q2n, Q3n ( 5–6 % loop rate) | FPY , loop rate , no offsetting rise in packaging rework |
| Batching/obsolescence | B1, B2, B3, O1 (6 % baseline), O2 | batch band moves to 12–20 **and** stale14day share does not rise |
| Service | S1–S6n per segment | see guardrails below |
| Resilience | R1n–R5n | diversity floor held; no new single point of failure |

Conformance dashboards are **segmentlevel**, never global, and every violation links to its case so that the exception can be audited.

## 7.4 Segmentlevel service guardrails (killswitches)

Any breach in **two consecutive windows** pauses the intervention in that segment and triggers a rollback review:

| Segment | Late % | p90 cycle | Other guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standardstock Regular |  7 % + 1 pp |  62 h + 10 % | partialshipment rate not ; cancellation  4.4 % |
| Standardstock Servicecontract |  5 % + 1 pp |  41 h + 10 % | S5n promise reliability; air retained where slack < 24 h |
| Madetoorder Regular |  18 % (target ) |  180 h + 10 % | promisenegotiation acceptance rate; O1  6 % |
| Madetoorder Servicecontract |  13 % + 1 pp |  156 h + 10 % | no batchcap breach; servicecontact event count |
| **Criticaldowntime Servicecontract** | ** 4 % (no tolerance)** | ** 30 h (no tolerance)** | zero mistriage of true downtime; escalation time < 1 h |

Additional crosscutting guardrails: backorder  cancellation rate (27 % baseline) must not rise; customercontact events per order must not rise; cancelled orders' full energy/scrap counts against the programme.

## 7.5 Exception governance (S12 in operation)

- **Mandatory reason codes:** customer commitment · machinestopped evidence · safety/damage risk (Q1) · carrier disruption · other (with free text). Missing reason code = nonconformance.
- **Named approver per lane/segment** (Lane A: duty service manager; Lanes B/C: logistics planner) and a servicecontract rule: no Lane B/C case may be downgraded without slack evidence logged.
- **Weekly review** of the top10 override orders by (transport + energy + scrap) impact and of all Lane A entries without evidence.
- **Monthly recalibration of SP1**: compare the modelled 85 % ontime probability with observed ontime rates of the chosen mode per lane; if the model is miscalibrated, fix the model before tightening the policy. This is what stops "green shipping" from becoming "systematic lateness".
- **Quarterly override outcome audit**: did each override actually save the delivery? Unjustified overrides that did *not* save the delivery tighten the rule; justified ones loosen it. This closes the loop between sustainability targets and real service.
- **Variant stratification instead of one process model:** because the discovered model has low precision (emergency paths × partial shipments combinatorics), validate every change within segment/variant clusters and report exception variants separately — never claim a global improvement from a lowprecision model.

## 7.6 Reporting cadence and decision rights

- **Weekly (operations):** service guardrails, SP1/SP2/Q1 compliance, queue and batchwait caps.
- **Monthly (programme board):** G1/G2, energy/transport/scrap per delivered order, O1 obsolescence, rebound indicators (`expedite_flag` rate, ordersplitting, second shipments, air share) — **rebound indicators reviewed first**, so that an intensity gain cannot hide a totalimpact loss.
- **Quarterly (governance):** resilience test (routediversity floor, backup mode dry run, CNC_A/CNC_B load balance), boundary review (§1: add van returnload and upstream material data when they become available), and a restatement of the balance: impact reduced, service/quality/resilience within guardrails — or the package is rescoped.
