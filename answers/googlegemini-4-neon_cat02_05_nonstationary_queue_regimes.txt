# 1. Data audit and estimands

## Queueing quantities, units, and opening-hours convention
The supplied dataset aggregates a single service queue across 28 consecutive days (`2026-04-06` Monday through `2026-05-03` Sunday, four full weeks) plus four representative hourly arrival/completion vectors. Opening hours are `07:00` to `19:00` (**$H = 12$ opening hours = 720 opening minutes per day**), with no active service while closed, although unfinished cases may carry over overnight.

| Quantity | Operational definition & units | Supplied evidence / proxy |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival rate ($\lambda_A$) | Cases entering the queue per opening hour (or per day) | `arrivals / 12` (cases/opening-hr); daily `arrivals` (cases/day) |
| Completion rate / throughput ($\lambda_C$) | Cases completed per opening hour (or per day) | `completed / 12` (cases/opening-hr); daily `completed` (cases/day) |
| Net daily flow ($\Delta B_t$) | Daily imbalance `arrivals - completed` (cases/day) | Observed proxy for daily change in backlog *only under strict flow conservation* |
| Active service time ($S$) | Active handling duration per case, excluding waiting (minutes) | Daily `median_service_min` ($q_{50}$) and `p90_service_min` ($q_{90}$); **mean $E[S]$ is not supplied** |
| Mean work-in-process ($L$) | Time-average number of cases in system during opening hours (cases) | `mean_wip` from 15-minute snapshots (~48–49 snapshots/day) |
| Effective capacity ($C$) | Available server-time per day (staff-minutes or FTE-hours) | `staff_fte * 720` staff-min/day (adjusted for `outage_min` on outage days) |
| Traffic intensity / pressure ($\rho$) | Ratio of arrival workload to available server-time ($\lambda_A E[S] / c$) | **Not directly observed**; must be bounded or estimated from $q_{50}, q_{90}$ via stated assumptions |
| Case-flow time ($W$) | Mean total time a case spends in system (waiting + active service) | **Not supplied**; may only be approximated as $L/\lambda$ (in *opening hours*) where Little's Law holds |

## Explicit assumptions needed for audit and capacity bounding
- **Assumption A1 (Opening-hours clock for WIP):** `mean_wip` snapshots are taken exclusively during `07:00–19:00`. *Why needed:* Defines the time base so that $L/\lambda$ yields flow time in *opening hours* rather than 24-hour calendar hours.
- **Assumption A2 (Right-skewed service-time mean bounds):** Because service times are non-negative and strongly right-skewed ($q_{90}/q_{50}$ ranges from 1.80 to 2.44), $E[S] > q_{50}$. We use $q_{50}$ as a conservative **active-service lower benchmark** and a lognormal parameterization ($\sigma = \ln(q_{90}/q_{50})/1.28155$, $E[S] \approx q_{50} e^{\sigma^2/2}$) as an **upper-range parametric estimate**, cross-checked against empirical FTE-minutes per completed case on saturated days. *Why needed:* Mean service time is required for traffic intensity $\rho$ but is omitted from the table.

## Six critical threats to naive inference (data audit findings)
1. **Unresolved flow-conservation contradiction (likely unlogged exits or WIP-scope mismatch) [Observation + Hypothesis]:** Across all 28 days, total arrivals are **2,519** and total completions are **2,409**, yielding a cumulative net surplus of **+110 cases** (including **+88** by Fri `2026-04-24` and **+128** by Tue `2026-04-28`). Under lossless flow conservation, backlog and WIP would have to grow by ~110 cases. Instead, `mean_wip` remains bounded between **12 and 52**, drops from **43** (Fri `04-24`, net +10) to **23** (Sat `04-25`, net +8), drops from **35** (Fri `04-17`, cumulative net +22) to **16** (Sat `04-18`), and finishes at **19** on Sun `05-03` (almost identical to Week 1's 18–20).
   - *Defensible hypotheses:* (a) unlogged abandonments, cancellations, or transfers out of the queue during high congestion; (b) `mean_wip` only counts *active/in-queue* cases while excluding parked/on-hold or weekday-only backlog queues over weekends; or (c) unlogged off-hours administrative closures/purges. Without case-level exit logs, we cannot distinguish these, which directly threatens Little's Law and backlog accounting.
2. **Reconciliation mismatch between daily `peak_08_10_pct` and hourly arrival vectors [Observation]:** Bins are labeled `07-08, 08-09, ..., 18-19`, so `08:00–09:59` corresponds to indices 1 and 2 (`08-09` + `09-10`). On all four representative days, those two bins account for far less than the daily `peak_08_10_pct` column:

   - `2026-04-15`: `(6+13)/105 = 18.1%` vs. table `32%` (whereas `09-11` is `35/105 = 33.3%`).
   - `2026-04-25`: `(2+4)/51 = 11.8%` vs. table `22%` (whereas `09-11` is `11/51 = 21.6%`).
   - `2026-04-21`: `(3+8)/115 = 9.6%` vs. table `47%` (even `09-11` is only `42/115 = 36.5%`).

3. **Missing mean service time and distribution shape [Observation]:** Only daily medians and p90s of active handling are supplied. Daily medians cannot be averaged to obtain a pooled median, and $q_{50}$ cannot be substituted for either $E[S]$ or mean flow time $W$.
4. **Unknown boundary backlogs and overnight carry-over [Observation]:** Opening backlog $B(07{:}00)$ is never supplied, and completed cases need not have arrived on the same day. Thus daily `completed` reflects both same-day arrivals and carried-over backlog, while overnight closed hours (12 hours/night) are invisible to `mean_wip`.
5. **Confounded structural shifts and unlogged intervention onset [Observation]:** Multiple mechanisms shift simultaneously—on `2026-04-20`, `complex_pct`, service times, arrivals, and intraday concentration all jump together (raising the question of real mix shift vs. coding change), while the temporary staffing intervention began *during* `2026-04-29` at an unlogged hour (making `04-29` a mixed transition day, even though its daily average `staff_fte` is already 7.0).
6. **Non-independence and multi-scale non-stationarity [Observation]:** Daily rows are serially dependent (via backlog carry-over, weekly calendar cycles, and persistent structural shifts), and hourly flows are non-stationary within days (diurnal waves, 3-hour batch spikes, and an acute 3-hour outage). Naive i.i.d. or stationary M/M/c summaries will severely misestimate variance and tail waiting times.

1. **Weekday/weekend composition creates a "phantom average" operating point [Observation]:** Pooled `arrivals` (90.0/d) and `staff_fte` (5.14) lie inside an empty gap that **never occurs on any day** (zero days have 54–91 arrivals or 3.1–5.4 FTE). 2. **Simpson's-paradox reversal: higher staffing appears to *increase* WIP [Observation + Causal explanation]:**
   - *Pooled across all 28 days:* Mean WIP rises monotonically with `staff_fte`—from **16.3** at 2.5 FTE ($n=6$), **20.5** at 3.0 FTE ($n=2$), **31.5** at 5.5 FTE ($n=2$), **33.5** at 6.0 FTE ($n=13$) and 6.5 FTE ($n=2$), up to **43.7** at 7.0 FTE ($n=3$). 4. **Cancellation of outage crash, chronic overload, and recovery surges [Observation]:** Pooled net flow (+3.9 cases/d, a 4.4% gap) looks like mild slippage. # 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

## Intraday analysis of the four representative hourly vectors

### Key stochastic findings
- **Distinguishing deterministic diurnal variation from true burstiness:** A Fano factor above 1 across hourly bins does *not* alone prove stochastic burstiness, because a smooth non-homogeneous Poisson process (NHPP) with a deterministic diurnal curve produces $I > 1$ together with strongly positive lag-1 autocorrelation—precisely what we see on `04-07` ($I=3.23, r_1=+0.68$), `04-15` ($I=4.14, r_1=+0.71$), and `04-25` ($I=1.29, r_1=+0.80$).
- **Evidence of batch arrivals on `2026-04-21`:** In stark contrast, `04-21` triples the Fano factor to **$I = 10.12$**, pushes **$CV = 1.03 > 1$**, and flips lag-1 autocorrelation **negative ($r_1 = -0.14$)** because arrivals surge sharply every 3 hours (`34` at `10-11`, `24` at `13-14`, `14` at `16-17`) separated by troughs of 3–8/hr. Meanwhile, completions flatten to a capacity ceiling ($I=0.98, CV=0.34$), proving the queue is acting as a saturated buffer absorbing batch shocks. *Hypothesis:* Upstream scheduled batch releases or periodic system syncs began around `04-20`, compounding the queueing penalty of longer complex service times (in G/G/c queues, waiting time scales approximately with $(CV_a^2 + CV_s^2)/2$).

## Daily-level serial dependence and change-point evidence
- **Multi-lag autocorrelation:** Across all 28 days, `arrivals` exhibits strong **lag-7 periodicity** (weekly weekday/weekend cycle), while `mean_wip`, `complex_pct`, `median_service_min`, and `p90_service_min` show strong **positive lag-1 autocorrelation** driven by two distinct mechanisms: (a) **physical backlog persistence** (e.g., WIP trajectories `47 -> 52 -> 35` after the outage and `37 -> 41 -> 46 -> 44 -> 43 -> 49 -> 50 -> 43 -> 36 -> 32` across Weeks 3–4), and (b) **non-stationary step shifts** (specifically the `04-20` jump in `complex_pct` from $\le 27\%$ to $\ge 39\%$ and the `04-29` staffing step).
- **Change-point evidence (transparent, non-definitive):** Even within weekdays alone ($n=20$, removing the weekend cycle), a standardized two-sample mean-shift scan over `[complex_pct, median_service_min, p90_service_min, peak_08_10_pct]` (excluding the `04-15` outage) overwhelmingly peaks at **`2026-04-20`**, and a secondary split on post-`04-20` weekdays over `[staff_fte, net_flow, mean_wip, p90_service_min]` peaks at **`2026-04-29`**. However, the mild pre-shift drift in Week 2 (`04-13..14` arrivals 103–107, `complex_pct` 22–24% vs. Week 1's 98.2 and 20.0%) shows that a pure step model is only an approximation.

## Why four representative days cannot establish a full stochastic model
1. **Selection bias and $n=1$ per archetype:** Having a single day per archetype provides zero information on day-to-day variance in intraday shapes (e.g., we cannot verify whether 3-hour spikes occurred on *every* post-mix weekday or only on Tuesdays/`04-21`).
2. **Hour-level aggregation hides sub-hour micro-bursts:** Hourly bins cannot distinguish 34 cases arriving uniformly across `10:00–10:59` from a simultaneous batch of 34 arriving at `10:00:01`—which have drastically different waiting-time distributions.
3. **Missing service-time and arrival-service correlation structure:** Without case-level timestamps, we cannot estimate inter-arrival distributions, service-time $CV_s^2$, whether complex cases cluster inside the 3-hour bursts, or whether arrival rates and handling times are state-dependent (e.g., fatigue or speedup under congestion).

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little's Law ($L = \lambda W$, hence implied mean flow time $W = L / \lambda$) requires: (i) **flow conservation** (no unlogged abandonments/transfers and identical case populations for $L$ and $\lambda$), (ii) **boundary stability** ($B(0) \approx B(T)$ or boundary error negligible relative to total throughput), and (iii) **consistent time units** (here, $\lambda$ in cases per *opening hour* yields $W$ in *opening hours*, excluding 12-hour overnight closures). We never substitute `median_service_min` for $W$.

| Candidate window / regime | Boundary stability | Throughput proxy ($\lambda$) | WIP comparability ($L$) | Carry-over risk | Stationarity assessment | Implied flow time $W = L/\lambda$ (if defensible) | Verdict |

# 7. Operational implications

## 1. Staffing strategy (permanent re-baselining vs. surge pool)
- **Retain the `04-29` staffing uplift unless the mix shift reverses [Recommendation]:** Under post-mix demand (~114–115 weekday arrivals, 44% complex, ~35 min median / ~80+ min p90 handling), standard staffing (6.0 Mon–Thu, 5.5 Fri, 2.5 Sat–Sun) is structurally below capacity ($\rho > 1.07$). The intervention level (**7.0 FTE Mon–Thu, 6.5 FTE Fri, 3.0 FTE Sat–Sun**) restores $\rho < 1$, but still leaves tight utilization (~0.85–0.95). If the mix shift is permanent, make this uplift the new baseline—and consider an additional **+0.5 FTE buffer** on peak weekdays (Mon–Wed) to protect against volatility.
- **Avoid judging staffing effectiveness by raw WIP correlation:** Because reactive staffing created a positive pooled correlation between FTE and WIP, evaluate staffing changes via **net flow ($\Delta B_t$)** and **WIP slope ($dL/dt$)** within matched regimes, not cross-sectional WIP levels.

## 2. Backlog recovery playbook
- **Recalibrate post-outage recovery capacity for the new mix [Estimate + Recommendation]:** During pre-mix conditions, a 3-hour outage (`04-15`, +47 net deficit) was largely absorbed by 2 days at +1.0 FTE (-15/day net drawdown). Under post-mix demand, +1.0 FTE yields only **-6.0/day** net drawdown (R7), meaning an equivalent 3-hour outage would require **~8 weekdays** to clear—or even longer if another shock occurs. Pre-authorize a deeper temporary surge (+1.5 to +2.0 FTE, or cross-trained overflow) and fast-track triage for simple cases whenever WIP crosses a regime-adjusted threshold (e.g., weekday WIP > 35 or two consecutive days with $\Delta B_t \ge +5$).

## 3. Peak smoothing and intraday batch mitigation
- **Audit and stagger the 3-hour arrival bursts [Hypothesis + Recommendation]:** The `04-21` spikes (`34` at `10-11`, `24` at `13-14`, `14` at `16-17`; Fano = 10.12) and jump in `peak_08_10_pct` (40–50%) strongly point to upstream batch releases or scheduled intake syncs. Smoothing these releases into continuous/hourly feeds would reduce intraday queueing delay **without adding headcount**.
- **Align shift overlays to intraday demand:** Protect peak-window server availability (`09:00–14:00`) by shifting breaks, meetings, and administrative work to `07:00–08:30` and `16:30–19:00`.

## 4. Data-collection and instrumentation priorities
- **Close the +110 flow-conservation gap:** Log case-level exit dispositions (`completed`, `abandoned`, `cancelled`, `transferred`, `parked/on-hold`) and exact `07:00` / `19:00` open-backlog counts.
- **Resolve metric definitions & coding ambiguity:** Reconcile the ~1-hour/definition mismatch between `peak_08_10_pct` and hourly arrival bins; add an explicit **variant-definition version flag** to separate true case-mix shifts from taxonomy/coding changes; and mandate timestamped logs for all staffing interventions (avoiding the `04-29` gap) and IT outages.
- **Move from daily quantiles to event-level logs:** Record case ID, intake timestamp, service-start, service-end, active-handling segments, and resource ID so true mean flow time $W$, mean active service $E[S]$, $CV_s^2$, and overnight carry-over durations can be measured directly.

## 5. A policy that looks beneficial under pooled averages but is risky under a segmented view
- **Policy: Rolling back the temporary `04-29` staffing increase (or "right-sizing" staffing to the 28-day pooled average of 90 arrivals/day, ~5.5–6.0 FTE on weekdays).**
  - *Why it looks attractive under pooled averages:* Across the 28-day table, pooled arrivals (90.0/d) and completions (86.0/d) appear nearly balanced, average FTE is 5.14, and naive pooled utilization using median service time ($90 \times 31 / (5.14 \times 720) = 75\%$) suggests ample slack—making a return from 7.0 to 6.0 weekday FTE look like an easy ~14% payroll saving.
  - *Why it is dangerously risky under a segmented view:* In the active post-mix weekday regime (R5), 6.0 FTE (5.5 Fri) operates at **$\rho \approx 1.07\text{–}1.17$**, guaranteeing a chronic **+13.4 cases/day** deficit, rapidly deteriorating waiting times, severe vulnerability to bursty 3-hour spikes, and potential hidden customer abandonment (the likely driver of the +110 conservation discrepancy). Ending the staffing uplift without first reversing the case-mix/handling-time increase would immediately return the center to unstable overload.

def best_mean_shift_splits(df_sub, feature_cols, min_seg=3):
    """Ward-style standardized 1-split score across ordered rows."""
    X = df_sub[feature_cols].astype(float)
    Z = ((X - X.mean()) / X.std(ddof=0).replace(0, np.nan)).fillna(0.0).to_numpy()
    n = len(Z)
    records = []
    for k in range(min_seg, n - min_seg + 1):
        m1, m2 = Z[:k].mean(axis=0), Z[k:].mean(axis=0)
        score = (k * (n - k) / n) * float(np.sum((m1 - m2) ** 2))
        records.append({"split_start_date": df_sub["date"].iloc[k], "shift_score": score})
    return pd.DataFrame(records).sort_values("shift_score", ascending=False)
