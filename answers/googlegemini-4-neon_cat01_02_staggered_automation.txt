## 1. What is observed

This panel tracks five branches across eight months (January–August 2026; 40 branch-month observations). System availability (`automation=1`) began in April for Alpha and Beta and in July for Gamma, while Delta and Epsilon remained non-adopters through August. Availability differs markedly from actual use: even when `automation=0`, baseline `automated_fast_path_pct` stood at 4% (6% in Beta), and post-launch usage rose gradually from 25–27% in the adoption month to 48–50% in Alpha and Beta by August and 32% in Gamma.

Branch selection into rollout was non-random: Alpha and Beta entered first with the highest baseline volumes (874–1,100 arrivals/month) and slowest historical cycle times (45.4–50.4 h median), whereas Gamma adopted after a readiness pilot. Several operational changes coincided with the rollout window: a centralized fraud desk opened in March alongside a 2.0 percentage-point increase in `escalation_pct` across all branches; routing campaigns directed roughly 10% more volume to Alpha and Beta from April and 5% more to Gamma from July; staffing shifted across branches (Alpha 2 FTE in May, Beta +1 in April, Gamma 1 in June, Delta +1 in June); and a May product change coincided with `high_complexity_pct` rising 5–8 percentage points across branches.

Finally, outcome measurement is aggregated and incomplete: `median_cycle_h` and `p90_cycle_h` summarize branch-months without case-level timestamps, fraud-desk wait times and local managerial changes are unrecorded, and `csat_1to5` reports survey means with missing response counts and 20–45% response rates.

**Clarification on Interference and Treatment Versions:**
- **Interference (SUTVA violation):** Two structural spillover channels violate no-interference across branches. First, the centralized fraud desk serves all five branches; because automated triage refers more borderline cases there, extra referrals from Alpha, Beta, and Gamma can lengthen fraud-desk queues for Delta and Epsilon, contaminating untreated controls. Second, the routing campaigns (+10% to Alpha/Beta, +5% to Gamma) alter network-level load distribution if volume was shifted across branches rather than newly generated. Thus, observed contrasts blend *direct* automation effects with *indirect* network congestion effects.
- **Multiple treatment versions (Consistency violation):** `automation=1` is not a single well-defined intervention. It bundles software availability, gradually rising fast-path usage (25%50%), evolving staff learning, local managerial work-practice adjustments, and differing baseline automation (4% vs. 6%). Without case-level assignment records, branch-level estimands recover a composite "rollout-bundle" contrast rather than the pure algorithmic triage effect.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary: Cohort-Specific / Stacked Staggered DiD (Event-Study)** | Separate cohort stacks (Apr-2026 cohort: Alpha, Beta; Jul-2026 cohort: Gamma) with relative-time indicators $e \in \{-3,\dots,+4\}$ and stack-by-branch / stack-by-month FE | Clean controls only: never-treated (Delta, Epsilon) in both stacks, plus not-yet-treated Gamma (Jan–Jun) in the Apr stack; never uses already-treated Alpha/Beta as controls for Gamma | `arrivals_per_fte` (arrivals/FTE), `high_complexity_pct`; sensitivity checks excluding potentially endogenous workload | **E1, E4, E6** (cohort-specific and dynamic ATT of rollout availability) | Conditional parallel trends within each stack; no anticipation; no cross-branch fraud-desk spillover; linear additive covariate adjustment | Inspect pre-treatment event-time leads ($e=-3,-2$ vs. $-1$); compare Apr stack vs. Jul stack estimates; leave-one-control-out (Delta vs. Epsilon); placebo timing tests | Unobserved fraud-desk spillovers into Delta/Epsilon; only 2–3 pre-months for Apr cohort (with Mar already affected by fraud desk); bundled routing/management changes; $G=5$ invalidates standard cluster inference |
| **Alternative 1: Synthetic Control / Synthetic DiD (SDID)** | Cohort-specific adoption date (`2026-04` for Alpha/Beta; `2026-07` for Gamma) | Non-negative weighted combination of never-treated branches (Delta, Epsilon) plus intercept shift (demaned SC / SDID) | Pre-treatment outcome paths, `arrivals_per_fte`, `high_complexity_pct` | **E1, E3, E4** (branch-specific ATT for each adopter) | Treated branch's counterfactual lies in the affine/convex span of control branches' trends; stable factor loadings across shocks | Pre-treatment RMSPE fit; check whether weights collapse to a single donor (Epsilon for Alpha); in-space placebo using Delta vs. Epsilon | Only 2 never-treated donors (Delta, Epsilon) and 3 pre-months for Alpha/Beta; Alpha/Beta lie outside the convex hull of donors in baseline volume and cycle time, forcing heavy extrapolation |
| **Alternative 2: Continuous-Treatment Dose-Response Panel (Intensity FE / First-Differences)** | Continuous `automated_fast_path_pct` (change above baseline 4–6%) rather than binary `automation` | Within-branch variation over time relative to calendar-month shocks in non-adopters (branch FE + month FE) | `arrivals_per_fte`, `high_complexity_pct`, plus binary `automation` to separate launch step-change from intensity slope | **E2** (marginal effect per +10 pp increase in fast-path share) | Strong parallel trends across dosage levels; no time-varying unobservables correlated with uptake ramp-up (e.g., management effort, team learning, routing) | Plot residuals vs. `automated_fast_path_pct`; test nonlinearity (launch jump vs. post-launch slope); compare within-adopter AprAug slope against cross-branch DiD | Endogenous uptake: `automated_fast_path_pct` rises monotonically with time since launch, making genuine fast-path dosage nearly collinear with learning curves and seasonal recovery |
| **Alternative 3: Controlled Interrupted Time Series (Complexity-Matched Pairs)** | Level and slope shift at adoption month (`2026-04` for Alpha vs. Epsilon; `2026-07` for Gamma vs. Delta) | Single closest structural match (Alpha paired with Epsilon, which shares May +5 pp and Jun +3 pp complexity jumps; Gamma paired with Delta) | Explicit adjustment for month-of-shock dummies (Mar fraud desk, May/Jun product change) and FTE changes | Branch-specific **E1, E4** | Matched pair experiences identical unobserved common shocks and parallel baseline slopes | Visual pre-trend alignment; verify shared complexity and escalation jumps in Mar/May/Jun; check sensitivity to switching partner branch | Single-pair fragility: any idiosyncratic shock to Epsilon or Delta (such as Delta's +1 FTE in June) directly biases the estimate; zero formal statistical power ($N=2$ branches per pair) |

**Why the Primary Strategy Is Preferable:**
A naïve two-way fixed-effects (TWFE) regression of outcomes on binary `automation` with branch and month fixed effects is inappropriate here because adoption is staggered and treatment intensity ramps up over time (25%50%). Under dynamic, heterogeneous treatment effects, naïve TWFE implicitly uses already-treated Alpha and Beta as comparison units for Gamma in July–August; because Alpha and Beta are still evolving along their learning and ramp-up curves, these "forbidden comparisons" introduce bias and potentially negative weights. Cohort-specific stacked DiD eliminates forbidden comparisons by matching each adoption cohort strictly to clean never-treated (and, prior to July, not-yet-treated) controls while preserving transparent event-time dynamics.

Compared with the alternatives, stacked DiD best fits this 5-branch, 8-month panel. Synthetic control is severely under-identified with only two never-treated donors and three pre-periods for Alpha and Beta, which sit above all donors in baseline volume and cycle time. A pure dose-response panel conflates fast-path intensity with time-varying learning and unobserved management changes, whereas single-pair CITS is overly fragile to one control branch's idiosyncratic staffing shift. Stacked DiD transparently separates calendar-wide shocks (March fraud desk, May product complexity jump, summer volume peak) from staggered adoption, while accommodating intensity regressions as a secondary mechanistic check.

Thus, median cycle time and tail cycle time do **not** behave identically under staffing stress.

### 5.3 Treatment-intensity analysis (`automated_fast_path_pct`)

Scaling the DiD estimates by average post-adoption fast-path uptake (+34.0 pp in Alpha/Beta; +24.5 pp in Gamma) yields two contrasting patterns:
- **Continuous scaling on `median_cycle_h`:** Dividing complexity-matched and pooled DiD shifts by uptake yields roughly **1.0 to 1.7 hours per +10 pp increase in `automated_fast_path_pct`** (Alpha vs. Epsilon: 4.84 h / 3.4 = 1.42 h per 10 pp; Beta vs. avg controls: 3.36 h / 3.4 = 0.99 h; Gamma vs. avg controls: 4.0 h / 2.45 = 1.63 h). Moreover, Alpha's median gap versus Epsilon narrows progressively as fast-path share climbs: 2.4 h in Apr (+21 pp uptake), 4.5 h in Jun (+35 pp), and 6.1 h in Aug (+44 pp).
- **Binary regime shift on `rework_pct` and `escalation_pct`:** Both metrics shift immediately at `automation=1` (Alpha Apr: 2.0 pp rework, +1.0 pp escalation; Gamma Jul: 2.0 pp rework, +1.0 pp escalation) and show **zero further drift** as `automated_fast_path_pct` expands from 25% to 48–50% (holding flat in Jul–Aug when complexity is constant). This pattern is consistent with binary triage-rule activation rather than continuous fast-path volume dosage.

### 5.4 Pre-trends and regression to the mean (RTM)

Pre-trend diagnostics are severely constrained: Alpha and Beta have only **three pre-treatment months** (Jan–Mar), and March already contains the centralized fraud-desk launch. Over Jan–Mar, `median_cycle_h` slopes are heterogeneous: Alpha is noisy/flat (50.4  46.9  49.4 h), Beta rises gently (45.4  45.9  46.4 h), Epsilon dips and recovers (41.4  39.3  40.2 h), while Gamma (36.4  36.5  39.6 h) and Delta (34.4  32.8  36.2 h) jump in March.

Could the apparent median gains reflect **regression to the mean** because Alpha and Beta were selected for historically poor cycle times? Pure RTM is unlikely to explain the entire pattern for three reasons: (1) Alpha and Beta were persistently elevated across all three pre-months rather than experiencing a single transient Q1 spike; (2) **Gamma** was not a poor historical performer (36.4–36.5 h in Jan–Feb) yet showed an equally sharp 3.9 to 4.2 h DiD drop in July–August; and (3) Alpha and Beta absorbed *positive* demand shocks (+10% routing) that should push cycle times up, not down. Nonetheless, because Alpha and Beta operated closer to capacity limits, *non-parallel responses* to common shocks remain a live threat.

### 5.5 Sensitivity analysis and bounded conclusions

*Arithmetic facts above are distinct from causal estimates, which depend on untestable assumptions. Bounding key threats:*
1. **`median_cycle_h`:** Across control choices (Delta, Epsilon, Gamma pre-Jul), DiD spans **3.4 to 5.6 h**. Adjusting for the +10% routing campaign and Alpha's 2 FTE shock (which bias naive DiD *against* automation) would widen the implied benefit toward **5 to 8 h**. Conversely, if automated escalations congested the shared fraud desk and slowed Delta/Epsilon by 1–3 h (SUTVA bias *favoring* automation), the true branch-level benefit shrinks to **1.5 to 4.5 h**. Plausible bounded range: **2 to 7 hours (~4%–15%)**, conditional on no major unobserved managerial confounder.
2. **`p90_cycle_h`:** Raw pre/post shows **+9.7 to +9.8 h** worsening in Alpha/Beta; DiD shows **4.9 to 7.7 h** relative improvement; and Alpha's May–Aug staffing strain adds **+5 h** of excess tail widening relative to its median. Allowing for ±3–5 h of unmeasured fraud-desk spillover leaves a wide, directionally uncertain interval: **8 h (helpful under adequate staffing) to +4 h (harmful under high utilization/escalation load)**.
3. **`csat_1to5`:** DiD shows **+0.07 to +0.11 points**. However, with response rates varying between 20% and 45%, suppose satisfied fast-path customers (mean ~4.3) increase their share of respondents by just 10 percentage points relative to dissatisfied escalated customers (mean ~3.3): compositional shift alone generates **+0.10 CSAT points** with zero true satisfaction gain. Under plausible nonresponse bounds, the true causal effect spans **0.05 to +0.15 points** (includes zero and mild harm).

## 8. Rollout recommendation

`expand with an embedded evaluation`

Leadership should **not** issue a blanket “automation reduces cycle time by X%” statement or expand blindly to both remaining branches. Instead, deploy next to **Epsilon** while holding **Delta** as a concurrent untreated benchmark for at least one quarter. Epsilon is the highest-value operational and scientific target: it shares Alpha’s high-complexity mix (38% vs. 36%), elevated rework (16.2%), and slow cycle times, directly testing whether automation protects or amplifies right-tail delays under high complexity when staffing is held stable.

Because two remaining branches cannot power a branch-level experiment, embed a **within-branch case-level randomized controlled trial** inside Epsilon. Begin with a **4-week instrumented pre-period baseline** (supplementing the eight historical months) plus a 2-week silent shadow-mode calibration to verify triage rules without altering live routing. Freeze FTE transfers, routing campaigns, and non-essential policy changes across Epsilon and Delta during evaluation. Then randomly assign eligible incoming Epsilon cases—stratified by registration risk class and arrival hour—50/50 to Automated Triage versus Manual Triage for **8 to 12 weeks** (capturing ramp-up and steady-state learning). If case-level randomization creates operational friction, use stratified alternating-day assignment.

Collect case-level process-mining event logs (`case_id`, `activity`, `start_timestamp`, `complete_timestamp`, `resource_role`, `triage_mode`, `risk_class`, `rework_flag`, `escalation_flag`), real-time queue WIP and wait times at Manual Triage, Specialist Review, and the centralized Fraud Desk, and case-linked CSAT survey invitations and response indicators to correct nonresponse bias. Actively monitor **spillovers** by tracking centralized fraud-desk arrival rates, backlog, and p50/p90 wait times across all five branches—especially Delta and Epsilon’s manual control arm.

Enforce hard **customer-harm guardrails**: throttle or pause automated escalation rules if Epsilon’s weekly `p90_cycle_h` exceeds Delta-adjusted baseline by >10%, centralized fraud-desk median wait rises >25%, escalations rise >2.0 pp without verified fraud-detection yield gains, or response-weighted CSAT drops >0.15 points. **Scale-up decision rule:** expand to Delta only if the 8–12 week evaluation demonstrates 10% (4 h) median cycle-time reduction for treated versus control cases, non-inferior p90 performance (5% deterioration after spillover adjustment), stable or lower true rework, manageable fraud-desk load, and non-inferior inverse-probability-weighted CSAT.

## 10. Final claim ladder

- `Observed:` Across the three adopting branches, `automated_fast_path_pct` ramped from 4–6% to 32–50%, `escalation_pct` jumped immediately by ~0.7–1.1 percentage points relative to non-adopting peers, `median_cycle_h` outperformed non-adopters by ~3.4–5.6 hours in difference-in-differences despite higher routed volume, and `p90_cycle_h` rose ~10% in raw pre/post terms in Alpha and Beta alongside an extra ~5-hour tail widening in Alpha after its May staffing loss.
- `Conditionally causal:` Provided conditional parallel trends hold, concurrent managerial changes did not drive the gains, and centralized fraud-desk congestion did not materially slow non-adopting controls, deploying automated triage reduced branch `median_cycle_h` by roughly 2–7 hours (~4%–15%), lowered immediate `rework_pct` by ~1.5–2.0 percentage points, and increased `escalation_pct` by ~0.5–1.5 percentage points.
- `Suggestive:` Relative `p90_cycle_h` reductions in adequately staffed adopters (particularly Gamma) and +0.07 to +0.11 point relative `csat_1to5` shifts suggest automation may cushion delays without harming customer sentiment when staffing is stable, yet unmeasured fraud-desk queues, Alpha's staffing-linked tail surge, and 20–45% varying survey response rates leave both outcomes weakly identified.
- `Not supported:` A blanket executive statement that “automation reduces cycle time by X%” across the organization—or that it reliably improves customer satisfaction and protects tail (p90) delays regardless of staffing levels, case complexity, and centralized fraud-desk capacity—is not defensible from these data.
