## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

M0 requires registration, completion of both checks, exactly one decision branch, notification, and closure in that order. Sanctions and quality are unordered relative to each other. On the approved branch, manual approval precedes activation. On the rejection branch, M0 still requires both checks before rejection. CS-9 expressly requires both checks and manual approval before activation, and separately requires requester–approver separation by human identity. It permits abbreviated pre-screening of rejected leads while leaving the boundary with M0 ambiguous.

The recorded sequences show which events appear in the onboarding log and in what order. The case materials also establish specific facts beyond that log, including the pilot flag on an ineligible case, the FD-7 dates, the duplicated migration row, the identity match in SUP-712, and evidence that QA_Clearance performed the quality-review function. A missing log event establishes a trace mismatch where M0 expects that event. Whether the activity occurred depends on the coverage and reliability of the relevant evidence.

PD-3 establishes the existence, dates, and stated conditions of the pilot. Its authority to displace the controlled CS-9 standard is disputed. The minutes, follow-up email, and Compliance observer's contemporaneous statement support an exception reading. Her later qualification and the absence of a demonstrated delegation leave that reading unproved. FD-7 is presented as an operative, time-limited directive and supplies affirmative evidence for its specified departures. Whether every continuing control was satisfied remains a case-specific evidence question. The intake boundary, the treatment of valid prior quality work on reactivation, and the applicability and evidence of disclosure in SUP-715 require separate determinations.

I would record a substantive noncompliance finding when the applicable rule, scope, and breach are supported by the balance of reliable evidence. Serious findings warrant corroboration of decisive facts such as timestamps, identities, and authority. An exception claimant must establish contemporaneous authority covering the particular departures, case eligibility, and performance of the applicable conditions. Unproved authority leaves CS-9 as the enforcement baseline. Unverified safeguards prevent certification of full exception compliance. Where the only evidence of a breach is absence from a known-incomplete source, the finding remains open unless other evidence resolves it. An inability to certify a case and affirmative evidence of misconduct carry different evidentiary weight.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

CS-9 was formally approved and remained effective throughout. Its preventive gates have a clear purpose. An activation before sanctions clearance creates exposure at the moment the system grants supplier status, even if purchasing happens later. SUP-708 activated a HIGH-risk supplier 40 minutes before the sanctions check and also activated before manual approval. The absence of transactions limits realized exposure. The two prior-control failures still occurred. SUP-703 used automated approval for a HIGH-risk new foreign supplier and deferred quality. Its ineligibility defeats any PD-3 defense even if the committee had full power to authorize the pilot. The EUR 180,000 first purchase demonstrates financial exposure and supplies a reason to prioritize review. The record establishes neither a prohibited counterparty nor an actual loss.

The exception defense depends on the precise scope and duration of authority. The pilot record evidences institutional awareness and may explain reliance in SUP-702, but the ability to run a pilot and the authority to waive controlled gates need affirmative support. Of 312 June–July automated approvals, 14 were clearly ineligible and nine lacked attributes needed to decide eligibility. The 289 apparently eligible cases still require verification of authority and other conditions. Six cases used Auto_Approve in the first week of August. SUP-714 illustrates how a tolerated workflow can persist after expiry even for a supplier with eligible attributes. SUP-704 has a dated emergency directive and evidence of emergency safeguards. SUP-705 used the same sequencing after expiry. Continued flooding and lower initial spend support the operator's account of necessity and mitigation, but neither conferred authority on July 13.

M0 also misses affirmative policy breaches. SUP-712 passes the trace test while two aliases conceal that the requester and approver are the same human. Operations' argument from desirable behavior has force for changing process design. The 47 information-request cases support such a change. Those cases supply no authority for a permanent automated approval branch or retrospective approval of unauthorized activations.

A sound enforcement case must concede the CRM coverage loss, migration duplicate, meaningful label equivalence, missing information-request behavior, and disputed scope. The case for enforcement rests on applicable, evidenced controls, including the resource rule outside M0.

## 3. Defense Brief

M0 is a useful reference for the standard route, with important limits. Its parallel block accepts quality before sanctions as readily as sanctions before quality. Both must finish before the decision. A conformance implementation that requires the printed sequence creates false positives. Conversely, concurrency offers no defense for SUP-708 because activation crosses both the incomplete checks block and the manual-approval gate.

For SUP-702, the best defense is a specific, time-limited authorization argument. A steering committee recorded a decision with bounded eligibility and safeguards, and a Compliance representative contemporaneously described it as a monitored exception. A legitimate exception could coexist with an unchanged general standard if the committee held the necessary delegated power. The supplier qualifies on the stated attributes and the quality review met the stated timing. The CRM record strongly supports notification. The dedicated master-data validation and the power to override CS-9 remain unproved. For SUP-704, the directive expressly allows provisional activation before quality review and approval. Sanctions preceded activation, initial spend was under the cap, monitoring evidence exists, and the deferred checks followed next business day. Matching it against the unmodified standard path obscures the controls that applied.

CRM migration explains a material part of apparent trace deviation. Missing notifications rose from 2% to 29%, consistent with roughly 70% mirroring from July 1. SUP-702 has case-specific CRM evidence with a stable identifier and predates that change. SUP-706 has a plausible message with a late, mutable case link. It deserves corroboration rather than either automatic acceptance or a definitive finding of skipped notification. SUP-711's duplicate is a migration artifact on the supplied evidence. In SUP-713, audit documents establish equivalent quality work despite the delayed mapping approval. A dated semantic mapping can recognize that work while preserving the date on which the mapping was formally approved.

The evidence also supports changing what the model describes. SUP-707 obtained missing certificates through repeated requests. Across the 47 observed cases with requests, activation followed receipt of the requested evidence. A loop or interleavable request subprocess makes that practice visible for assurance. For SUP-709, the owner disagreement concerns whether a failed pre-screen ever entered M0's population. CS-9 permits abbreviation for rejected leads. For SUP-710, a prior valid quality certification and an existing supplier identity make a fresh-registration trace a poor default comparator. Whether prior certification satisfies the current release criterion depends on the policy's treatment of reactivation and applicability of that certification to the current supplier and circumstances.

These arguments establish defects in the deviation measure. They do not establish that those defects account for most deviations, since applicable populations, overlap between defects, and outcomes after evidence reconciliation are unavailable. The defense also has no authority argument for SUP-703, SUP-705, or SUP-714 on the current record. Operational necessity, status before purchase, good intentions, and incomplete data do little to rebut an independently established early activation or identity conflict.

I reject the prosecution's use of overall M0 deviation as a reliable measure of policy breach. M0 remains valid as a standard-route control-flow reference, with scope specified and evidence reconciled. It is inadequate as the sole compliance specification or a common denominator across all case types and policy versions.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

**Scope and control flow.** Record the scope decision and its rule version at an explicit admission point. Give intake a linked case ID and keep pre-screen rejection in that population. If a supplier is admitted, apply the onboarding model, including a rejection path after the standard checks. Preserve screening evidence across the boundary with source links. Define reactivation as a new activation episode for an existing supplier, linked to earlier registration and qualifying evidence. Require fresh sanctions and approval under the proposed standard reactivation profile. Permit quality reuse only under an approved rule with identity, certificate applicability, validity and material-change checks at reactivation time. Record the evidence used on the new episode. Track withdrawal as an explicit disposition. Intake withdrawals end intake. Admitted pre-activation withdrawals follow a separate termination profile with notice, closure, and a prohibition on activation in that episode. Once activation is effective, supplier termination or deactivation requires its own lifecycle treatment.

**POWL and temporal constraints.** Use POWL for admitted-case route structure, mandatory activities, concurrency, choice, repeatable requests, and order. Select the applicable route from independently verified scope, eligibility, and authority before interpreting alignment. Where route selection remains disputed, report the standard comparison and the conditional overlay comparison. DECLARE or log-skeleton-style constraints can supplement the model with profile-specific precedence, existence, cardinality, and exclusion rules. Examples include sanctions preceding every activation in the same episode, a qualifying notification preceding closure, manual approval preceding standard activation, and rejection or pre-activation withdrawal excluding activation within the episode. Evaluate absence and precedence on canonical events with explicit completeness and timestamp status. For deployments with automatic controls, block activation until the required events and successful outcomes are verified.

**Data and resources.** Use keyed, time-aware checks in pandas or equivalent for the operative policy version, snapshot-based eligibility, activation status, successful master-data validation, business-day deadlines, cumulative spend up to retrospective approval, and monitoring coverage. Apply the governance-defined business calendar and time zone for deadlines. Aggregate spend across the episode using a consistent accounting definition and ordered ledger events. Enforce the cap at transaction authorization and reconcile it against ledger evidence. Resolve requester and actual approver to distinct humans on standard and emergency manual approvals. Test delegation scope and date, and capture disclosure, independent review and any recusal for declared conflicts. If an approved auto route is enabled, record its authorized service principal and the rule version exercised. Assess conflicts using the applicable policy and facts about the relationship.

**Authority.** Maintain a controlled register that ties each overlay to a duly authorized decision, delegated powers, the baseline rules affected, scope, effective interval, safeguards and evidence requirements. Approval to launch a pilot and approval to override CS-9 require explicit treatment. Use the activated episode's governing version to evaluate obligations that continue after expiry. Block new overlay activations at expiry unless a valid successor applies. Preserve amendments and extensions as independently approved versions with recorded and effective times. A permanent automated branch requires formal approval and enforceable eligibility checks. Pending that approval, the structural path can be used to analyze historical behavior and test controls.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The model below represents admitted initial onboarding. Its XOR describes three structural possibilities. Route-specific policy checks determine which can be credited in a given case. Both provisional paths use the existing activation label with a mandatory provisional status in the data, and permit notification and deferred work to proceed independently after activation. Closure follows all required activities in the chosen branch. The proposed closure gate and controls on how notices are qualified should be adopted explicitly. An intake disposition, withdrawal, or reactivation uses the scope-specific profile described above.

In PM4Py's two-child LOOP, the first child executes once and each repetition executes the second child followed by the first. A silent first child permits zero or more visible requests. The request subprocess interleaves with the required pre-decision or pre-activation work. It records request occurrences, with request–receipt correspondence and evidence-use timing verified in the data. This approximation omits separate post-activation request cycles. It also leaves any timing relationship between emergency retrospective approval and quality review open, as FD-7 states no order between them.

```python
from itertools import combinations

from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    OperatorPOWL,
    SilentTransition,
    StrictPartialOrder,
    Transition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def sequence(*nodes):
    model = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for earlier, later in combinations(nodes, 2):
        model.order.add_edge(earlier, later)
    return model


def exclusive_choice(*branches):
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=list(branches))


def information_requests():
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.LOOP,
        children=[SilentTransition(), Transition(label="Request_More_Info")],
    )


def provisional_route(pre_activation, *follow_up):
    return sequence(
        pre_activation,
        Transition(label="Activate_Supplier"),
        StrictPartialOrder(
            nodes=[*follow_up, Transition(label="Notify_Requester")]
        ),
    )


standard_checks = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[
        Transition(label="Sanctions_Check"),
        Transition(label="Quality_Review"),
        information_requests(),
    ]
)
standard_decision = exclusive_choice(
    sequence(
        Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval"),
        Transition(label="Activate_Supplier"),
    ),
    Transition(label="Reject_Supplier"),
)
standard_route = sequence(
    standard_checks, standard_decision, Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
)

pilot_route = provisional_route(
    StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[
            Transition(label="Sanctions_Check"),
            Transition(label="Master_Data_Validation"),
            Transition(label="Auto_Approve"),
            information_requests(),
        ]
    ),
    Transition(label="Quality_Review"),
)

emergency_route = provisional_route(
    StrictPartialOrder(
        nodes=[Transition(label="Sanctions_Check"), information_requests()]
    ),
    Transition(label="Quality_Review"),
    Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval"),
)

revised_powl = sequence(
    Transition(label="Register_Supplier"),
    exclusive_choice(standard_route, pilot_route, emergency_route),
    Transition(label="Close_Case"),
)
```
