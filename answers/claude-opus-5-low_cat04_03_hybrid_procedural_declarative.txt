# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

## Material assumptions

A1. **Case notion** = one vendor onboarding request. All eighteen labels are visible; no invisible/duplicate labels except silent transitions used for optionality and loops in the procedural model.

A2. **Timestamps only.** The log gives `activity`, `timestamp`, `resource` per event and nothing else. There is no `case:emergency` flag, no `joint_authorization` attribute, no risk-class attribute, and no lifecycle transition. Consequently "low-risk route" vs "high-risk route" is *inferred from which approval activity occurred*, not from data. The procedural model therefore encodes a free XOR after `Classify Risk`; it cannot check that the chosen branch matches the actual risk class. This is a permanent blind spot, not an oversight.

A3. **Concurrency semantics.** The three checks are truly concurrent (unordered), so any interleaving in the log is acceptable. `Classify Risk` requires all three to have completed.

A4. **Clarification is orthogonal to the checks.** R3 does not say *when* clarification may start, only that each request is answered before the next request and before any final outcome. I model the clarification loop as concurrent with the check block and constrained to complete before `Classify Risk` and before terminal activities.

A5. **"Final outcome"** in R3 = `Reject Vendor`, `Activate Vendor`, `Close Onboarding`.

A6. **"Applicable approval"** in R9 = `Manager Approval` on the low-risk route, `Compliance Approval` on the high-risk route.

A7. **R12 pairs are elapsed times between the *matched* occurrences** of the two activities within a case (first-to-first for the singleton pairs; per-cycle pairing for clarification). Deviation is flagged when `|observed - mean| > zeta * stdev` with `zeta = 2.0`.

A8. **Post-activation behaviour (R11) is deliberately excluded from the procedural backbone** and enforced declaratively. Including `Change Bank Details` / `Suspend Vendor` / a second `Verify Bank Account` in a block-structured tree would require a loop around the activation region that would also permit a second `Activate Vendor`, contradicting R10.

A9. **Support/confidence are descriptive statistics, not norms.** I set `support = 1.0` on every rule as required by the output format, and use `confidence` to express *how strongly I would act on a violation* (1.0 = hard rule, <1.0 = advisory). This is a local convention and is stated explicitly because PM4Py's DECLARE conformance treats any listed constraint as checkable regardless of these numbers.

A10. **R2's "should"** is read as a hard rule for accepted cases (exactly one approval) and a hard prohibition for rejected cases, because it is a segregation/authorization rule, not a preference.

## The R5/R6 conflict

R5 (Legal) is a hard, unconditional ordering: `Sanctions Escalation` ⇒ `Obtain Legal Opinion` **precedes** `Activate Vendor`.
R6 (Operations) explicitly permits the opposite order under a condition — joint authorization — that **is not present in the event log**, plus a 24-hour repair deadline.

These are logically incompatible over the observable alphabet: R5 forbids exactly the traces R6 permits, and no activity-only formalism can separate the permitted subset from the forbidden one, because the discriminating fact (joint authorization) is not recorded. Any single-layer encoding therefore either over-rejects legitimate emergencies or under-detects genuine violations.

### Resolution A — *Legal-strict* (R5 authoritative, R6 unrecordable ⇒ inadmissible)

Encode `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` as a hard constraint in both the procedural backbone and DECLARE. Every emergency activation is a **fail**. R6 is only re-admitted by a manual, out-of-band waiver registered in a separate exception register keyed by case id.

*Compliance risk:* zero false negatives on the sanctions-ordering control, but a stream of false positives for operationally legitimate emergency activations. Analysts learn to dismiss the finding, which erodes trust in the control (alert fatigue), and the real waiver decision lives outside the process-mining stack where it is not audited.

### Resolution B — *Two-tier with conservative default* (implemented)

Split the requirement into an **observable core** and a **conditional exception**:

- Hard, in DECLARE and Log Skeleton: `responded_existence`/`response`(`Sanctions Escalation` → `Obtain Legal Opinion`) and `always_after(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` — R4 is unconditional and uncontested.
- Hard, in the procedural backbone: opinion precedes activation (the backbone is the *normal* path).
- Soft, in a dedicated **emergency-exception check** in the resource/data layer: a trace with `Activate Vendor` before `Obtain Legal Opinion` is not silently accepted. It is raised as an `EMERGENCY_ACTIVATION` finding that requires (i) an `emergency` / `joint_authorization` case attribute and (ii) `Obtain Legal Opinion` within 24 h of `Activate Vendor`. **If the attribute is missing — which is always, in the current log — the check resolves to `fail` (conservative default), not to `pass`.**

*Compliance risk:* the verdict is honest about *why* it is a violation (missing evidence of authorization) rather than pretending the order itself is always fatal, and it produces an actionable remediation: instrument the log. The residual risk is that once the attribute is added, the control's strength depends entirely on the integrity of that attribute — a single boolean field becomes the gate on a sanctions control, and it is set by the same operations department that benefits from setting it. Mitigation: require the authorizing resource ids to be recorded and apply a four-eyes check to *them* too.

**Implemented choice: Resolution B.** The procedural and DECLARE layers behave like Resolution A (so the ordering deviation is always surfaced), but the final verdict is assembled by the external layer, which distinguishes "forbidden order" from "forbidden order without recorded authorization". I do **not** relax the DECLARE precedence to `responded_existence` only; that would silently merge the two policies, which the task forbids.

# 3. Procedural Backbone

## 3.1 Compact formal description

Let the root be a strict partial order `PO` over the nodes

```
r  = Receive Onboarding Request
C  = { Capture Master Data , Run Sanctions Screening , Verify Bank Account }   (mutually unordered)
L  = *( tau , ( Request Clarification -> Receive Clarification ) )             (loop, 0..n cycles)
E  = X( tau , ( Sanctions Escalation -> Obtain Legal Opinion ) )               (optional escalation)
k  = Classify Risk
O  = X( Reject Vendor ,
        ( Manager Approval -> Activate Vendor -> Release First Payment ) ,
        ( Enhanced Due Diligence -> Compliance Approval -> Activate Vendor -> Release First Payment ) )
z  = Close Onboarding
```

with the explicit edge set

```
r -> Capture Master Data
r -> Run Sanctions Screening
r -> Verify Bank Account
r -> L
Capture Master Data     -> k
Run Sanctions Screening -> k
Verify Bank Account     -> k
Run Sanctions Screening -> E          (R7: escalation only after screening)
L -> k                                (R3: clarification closed before classification)
k -> O
E -> O                                (R5: legal opinion, if any, precedes the outcome block)
O -> z
```

Everything not connected by an edge is concurrent. The three checks are pairwise unordered; `L` is unordered w.r.t. the checks; `E` is unordered w.r.t. `Capture Master Data`, `Verify Bank Account`, `L` and `Classify Risk`.

## 3.2 PM4Py code

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# --- leaves -----------------------------------------------------------------
receive   = Transition(label="Receive Onboarding Request")
capture   = Transition(label="Capture Master Data")
screening = Transition(label="Run Sanctions Screening")
verify    = Transition(label="Verify Bank Account")
classify  = Transition(label="Classify Risk")

req_clar  = Transition(label="Request Clarification")
rec_clar  = Transition(label="Receive Clarification")

escalate  = Transition(label="Sanctions Escalation")
opinion   = Transition(label="Obtain Legal Opinion")

edd       = Transition(label="Enhanced Due Diligence")
mgr_appr  = Transition(label="Manager Approval")
comp_appr = Transition(label="Compliance Approval")

reject    = Transition(label="Reject Vendor")
activate  = Transition(label="Activate Vendor")
payment   = Transition(label="Release First Payment")
close     = Transition(label="Close Onboarding")

# --- clarification loop: 0..n matched request/response cycles ----------------
clar_body = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[req_clar, rec_clar])
clar_body.order.add_edge(req_clar, rec_clar)          # response always follows its request
clar_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP,
                         children=[SilentTransition(), clar_body])

# --- optional sanctions escalation, opinion strictly after escalation --------
esc_body = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[escalate, opinion])
esc_body.order.add_edge(escalate, opinion)            # R4 + ordering
esc_block = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                         children=[SilentTransition(), esc_body])

# --- terminal outcome: reject XOR low-risk route XOR high-risk route ---------
low_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[mgr_appr, activate, payment])
low_route.order.add_edge(mgr_appr, activate)
low_route.order.add_edge(activate, payment)

high_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[edd, comp_appr, activate_h := Transition(label="Activate Vendor"),
                                       payment_h := Transition(label="Release First Payment")])
high_route.order.add_edge(edd, comp_appr)
high_route.order.add_edge(comp_appr, activate_h)
high_route.order.add_edge(activate_h, payment_h)

outcome = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                       children=[reject, low_route, high_route])

# --- root partial order -----------------------------------------------------
procedural_model = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[receive, capture, screening, verify, clar_loop, esc_block, classify, outcome, close]
)
po = procedural_model.order
po.add_edge(receive, capture)
po.add_edge(receive, screening)
po.add_edge(receive, verify)
po.add_edge(receive, clar_loop)
po.add_edge(capture,   classify)
po.add_edge(screening, classify)
po.add_edge(verify,    classify)
po.add_edge(screening, esc_block)      # R7
po.add_edge(clar_loop, classify)       # R3
po.add_edge(classify,  outcome)
po.add_edge(esc_block, outcome)        # R5 on the normal path
po.add_edge(outcome,   close)

# optional: obtain an accepting Petri net for alignment-based conformance
net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(procedural_model)
```

Note on the duplicated `Activate Vendor` / `Release First Payment` leaves: POWL/Petri-net conversion requires distinct node objects per branch. They carry the *same visible label*, so the language is unaffected; only the internal identity differs. R10 (at most one activation per case) is guaranteed here because the two copies live in mutually exclusive XOR branches, and enforced independently by the Log Skeleton for logs replayed outside this model.

## 3.3 What the backbone does and does not model

**Concurrency.** `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, `Verify Bank Account` are unordered nodes of the root partial order, so all six interleavings replay. T1 (capture→screen→verify) and T2 (screen→verify→capture) and T3 (capture→verify→screen) are all accepted, which is the intended reading of backbone step 2. The three edges into `Classify Risk` implement the synchronization of step 3 — classification cannot fire until all three have completed. A process tree would express the same thing as `+( a, b, c )` inside a sequence, but the partial order also lets me put `clar_loop` and `esc_block` concurrently with the checks *without* making them concurrent with `Classify Risk`, which a block-structured tree cannot do without either over-serializing or over-generalizing. That is the reason for choosing POWL over a ProcessTree here.

**Choices.** The post-classification XOR has exactly three branches, so R2 and R8 hold structurally: no trace of the model contains both approvals, or EDD together with `Manager Approval`, or an activation together with a rejection. The model cannot check that the branch *matches the risk class* (assumption A2) — that would need a case attribute.

**Clarification loops.** `LOOP(tau, [Request Clarification → Receive Clarification])` yields zero or more complete request/response cycles and can never emit a request without its response, nor two requests back to back. The `clar_loop → Classify Risk` edge closes all cycles before classification, which is stronger than R3 (R3 only demands closure before the *final outcome*). I accept the stronger reading because every test case with clarification resolves it pre-classification; a looser variant would drop that edge and rely on DECLARE `altresponse` alone. **Approximation disclosed:** because the loop is a single node in the partial order, the model cannot express "a clarification request issued *between* two checks and answered *after* a third check" as a distinct behaviour — all interleavings of the loop's internal events with the check events are permitted, which is a relaxation, not a restriction.

**Terminal outcomes.** `Close Onboarding` follows the outcome block on every branch, satisfying step 5 for both rejection and payment.

**Deliberately excluded.** `Change Bank Details` and `Suspend Vendor` do not appear in the backbone at all. Modelling R11 procedurally would require a loop enclosing the activation region, which would re-admit multiple `Activate Vendor` and multiple `Release First Payment` occurrences and thus break R10. Their absence means any trace containing them will be *procedurally* non-fitting; that is intentional, and the DECLARE/skeleton layers are authoritative for them. In deployment they should be split into a separate "vendor master change" case notion (see §10).

## Rule table

| Template | Instance | Direction / semantics | Requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| `init` | `Receive Onboarding Request` | The first event of every trace carries this label | Backbone 1 |
| `existence` | `Classify Risk`, `Close Onboarding`, the three checks | The activity occurs at least once | Backbone 2–5 |
| `exactly_one` | `Receive Onboarding Request`, `Classify Risk`, `Close Onboarding` | Occurs exactly once — stronger than `existence`, used only where re-execution is meaningless | Backbone |
| `responded_existence` | (Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion) | If the source occurs, the target occurs **somewhere** in the trace (before *or* after) | R4, weak form |
| `response` | (Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion) | If the source occurs, the target occurs **strictly later**. Strictly stronger than `responded_existence`; both are listed so that an ordering-only violation is distinguishable from a total absence | R4 |
| `response` | (Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor) | Every bank-detail change is eventually followed by a suspension | R11 |
| `response` | (Activate Vendor / Reject Vendor, Close Onboarding) | Every terminal outcome is eventually followed by closure | Backbone 5 |
| `precedence` | (Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation) | The **target** may occur only if the **source** occurred earlier. Note the asymmetry with `response`: precedence puts no obligation on the source, so screening without escalation is fine | R7 |
| `precedence` | (Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor) | `Activate Vendor` requires a prior `Obtain Legal Opinion` | R5 — **over-approximation**, see below |
| `precedence` | (Verify Bank Account, Release First Payment) | No payment without a prior bank verification | R11 second clause, weak form |
| `precedence` | (Classify Risk, {approvals, Reject Vendor}) | No outcome activity before classification | Backbone 4 |
| `succession` | (Enhanced Due Diligence, Compliance Approval) | Conjunction of `response` and `precedence`: EDD ⇒ later CA, **and** CA ⇒ earlier EDD. Used because the high-risk route is bidirectionally coupled | Backbone 4 |
| `altresponse` | (Request Clarification, Receive Clarification) | After each source occurrence the target occurs **before any further source occurrence**. This is exactly "answered before the next request"; plain `response` would accept `RC, RC, ReC` | R3 |
| `altprecedence` | (Receive Clarification, Request Clarification) | Mirror: each response is preceded by a request with no intervening unmatched response — forbids `ReC` without a pending `RC` | R3 |
| `chainprecedence` | (Activate Vendor, Change Bank Details) | The event **immediately** preceding `Change Bank Details` is `Activate Vendor`. `chain*` = directly-follows adjacency; `alt*` = no repetition of the source in between; plain response/precedence = eventually. Confidence 0.6 because in practice other events intervene — advisory only | R11 scoping |
| `noncoexistence` | (Activate Vendor, Reject Vendor) etc. | The two activities never both appear in a trace, **in any order** | R1, R2, R8 |
| `nonsuccession` | (Activate Vendor, Manager Approval) | The source is never followed — at any distance — by the target. Weaker than `noncoexistence`: it permits `Manager Approval … Activate Vendor` (the legal order) while forbidding approval *after* activation | Backbone 5 |
| `absence` | Suspend Vendor | Should not occur at all in a clean onboarding; confidence 0.3 marks it as an expectation, not a rule | R11 context |

**Disclosed over-approximation on R5.** `precedence("Obtain Legal Opinion", "Activate Vendor")` is unconditional: it forbids activation in cases where no escalation ever happened and hence no opinion was needed. That makes T1, T2, T4, T5 fail this single constraint although R5 does not apply to them. DECLARE has no guarded precedence. Two honest options: (a) keep it and post-filter findings to cases containing `Sanctions Escalation`; (b) drop it from DECLARE and move R5 entirely into the external layer. **I keep it and post-filter** — the constraint is retained for documentation value, and the runner in §7 suppresses the finding for traces without `Sanctions Escalation`. In the verdict matrix of §8, the DECLARE column reports the *post-filtered* result and this is stated explicitly.

## Orientation and interaction with the other layers

- **`always_before(A, B)` reads "whenever A occurs, B has occurred strictly earlier."** The pair is written *(later, earlier)*. E.g. `("Obtain Legal Opinion", "Sanctions Escalation")` means an opinion is only ever seen after an escalation — the skeleton counterpart of DECLARE `precedence(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)`.
- **`always_after(A, B)` reads "whenever A occurs, B occurs strictly later."** The pair is written *(earlier, later)*. `("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion")` mirrors DECLARE `response`.
- Several pairs appear in both sets with swapped orientation — e.g. escalation/opinion and EDD/compliance approval. That is the skeleton's way of writing `succession`, and it is deliberate, not a duplication error.
- **`equivalence(A, B)`** means equal occurrence counts. `(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` is the cardinality half of R3 that `altresponse` does not give (altresponse alone would accept a trailing unanswered request only if nothing follows; equivalence rejects it outright). `(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` encodes "a payment iff an activation", consistent with `activ_freq` capping both at 1.
- **`never_together`** is unordered and is the authoritative encoding of R1 and R8. It duplicates DECLARE `noncoexistence` exactly; the skeleton is authoritative because its violation report is per-case and does not depend on constraint-activation semantics.
- **`activ_freq["Verify Bank Account"] = {1,2,3}`** is the one place where the skeleton is deliberately *looser* than the procedural model, which allows exactly one verification. This is the R11 re-verification allowance. The disagreement is intentional and documented: the procedural model is authoritative for the onboarding case; the skeleton tolerates the post-activation re-check.
- **`directly_follows` is left empty.** With three genuinely concurrent checks, a clarification loop that may interleave anywhere, and optional escalation, there is no activity pair that is *always* adjacent — not even `Reject Vendor → Close Onboarding` (a suspension or a late opinion could intervene). Asserting any directly-follows pair here would encode an artefact of the eight sample traces rather than a policy. I prefer an empty set to a rule I cannot defend. The adjacency intuition that does exist (`Activate Vendor` immediately before `Change Bank Details`) is expressed as a low-confidence DECLARE `chainprecedence` instead, where its advisory status is visible.

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
HOUR = 3600

temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (48 * HOUR, 24 * HOUR),   # 172800, 86400
    ("Sanctions Escalation",  "Obtain Legal Opinion"):  (12 * HOUR,  6 * HOUR),   #  43200, 21600
    ("Activate Vendor",       "Release First Payment"): (72 * HOUR, 36 * HOUR),   # 259200, 129600
}

zeta = 2.0
# pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(log, temporal_profile, zeta=zeta)
```

Acceptance bands (`|x − μ| ≤ ζσ`), in hours:

| Pair | μ | σ | Accepted range | Observed in tests |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Request Clarification → Receive Clarification | 48 | 24 | 0 – 96 | T2: 26 h → within band |
| Sanctions Escalation → Obtain Legal Opinion | 12 | 6 | 0 – 24 | T6: 17 h → within band; T7: 34 h → 3.67σ, **warning** |
| Activate Vendor → Release First Payment | 72 | 36 | 0 – 144 | T1 71 h, T2 84 h, T4 61 h, T6 77 h → within band; T7 2 h → 1.94σ, within band (narrowly) |

T7 is the interesting case: the legal opinion is 34 hours after escalation (a clear temporal deviation) while the activation-to-payment gap of 2 hours is *just* inside the band at 1.944σ. A slightly tighter ζ, or a one-sided "too fast is also suspicious" reading, would have flagged the second one as well. I keep the two-sided symmetric test the task specifies and report only the SE→OLO deviation, but note that "unusually fast payment after a temporally troubled sanctions case" is precisely the pattern a risk analyst would want surfaced — a good argument for a separate lower-bound rule rather than for tampering with ζ.

## Why temporal deviations are warnings, not violations

1. **Different modality.** R12 is stated by Compliance as "normative enough to flag but not automatically reject". The design must not silently promote it to a hard control.
2. **Statistical, not logical.** The bands are derived from historical means and standard deviations. With ζ = 2 and roughly normal data, about 5 % of perfectly compliant cases fall outside by construction. A layer with a built-in ~5 % false-positive rate cannot be a gate.
3. **Duration is confounded.** Calendars, weekends, vendor-side response latency, and time-zone effects all inflate elapsed time without any process deviation. The profile sees none of this.
4. **No control-flow content.** A slow legal opinion is still a legal opinion in the right position. The ordering constraint (R5) and the timing expectation (R12) are genuinely independent dimensions, and collapsing them would make it impossible to tell a *sequencing* failure from a *service-level* failure — which is exactly the distinction between T6 and T7.
5. **The one place timing *is* normative** — R6's 24-hour repair deadline — is deliberately **not** in the temporal profile. It is a hard conditional deadline attached to an authorization, evaluated in §7, because the profile has no notion of "only when attribute X is set" and its two-sided band would also flag opinions that arrive *too fast*.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

## Required attributes

| Check | Attributes | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| R9 four-eyes | `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `org:resource` | **Abstain** (`not evaluated`) and raise a data-quality finding. Do **not** pass: an unrecorded resource is not evidence of segregation. |
| R6 emergency | `case:emergency` (bool), `case:joint_authorization` (list/str of ≥2 authorizer ids), `time:timestamp` | **Fail** (conservative default of Resolution B). Absence of recorded authorization means the emergency activation is unauthorized as far as the audit trail is concerned. |
| R2 route/risk match | `case:risk_class` | `not evaluated`; the approval activity is the only proxy and it cannot falsify itself. |

## Code

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE, ACT, RES, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "org:resource", "time:timestamp"


def _first_ts(events, activity):
    hits = events[events[ACT] == activity]
    return None if hits.empty else hits[TS].iloc[0]


def _resources(events, activity):
    return set(events[events[ACT] == activity][RES].dropna())


# --- R9a / R9b : segregation of duties --------------------------------------
def check_segregation_of_duties(df):
    """Verify Bank Account != applicable approval; Capture Master Data != Activate Vendor."""
    findings = []
    if RES not in df.columns:
        return [{"case": None, "rule": "R9", "verdict": "not evaluated",
                 "detail": "org:resource attribute absent from the log"}]

    for case_id, ev in df.sort_values(TS).groupby(CASE):
        # which approval is the applicable one for this case?
        acts = set(ev[ACT])
        approval = ("Compliance Approval" if "Compliance Approval" in acts
                    else "Manager Approval" if "Manager Approval" in acts
                    else None)

        if approval is None:
            findings.append({"case": case_id, "rule": "R9a", "verdict": "not evaluated",
                             "detail": "no approval in case (rejected or incomplete)"})
        else:
            verifiers = _resources(ev, "Verify Bank Account")
            approvers = _resources(ev, approval)
            if not verifiers or not approvers:
                findings.append({"case": case_id, "rule": "R9a", "verdict": "not evaluated",
                                 "detail": "missing resource on one of the two events"})
            elif verifiers & approvers:
                findings.append({"case": case_id, "rule": "R9a", "verdict": "fail",
                                 "detail": f"{sorted(verifiers & approvers)} both verified the bank "
                                           f"account and performed {approval}"})
            else:
                findings.append({"case": case_id, "rule": "R9a", "verdict": "pass"})

        capt, actv = _resources(ev, "Capture Master Data"), _resources(ev, "Activate Vendor")
        if not capt or not actv:
            findings.append({"case": case_id, "rule": "R9b", "verdict": "not evaluated"})
        elif capt & actv:
            findings.append({"case": case_id, "rule": "R9b", "verdict": "fail",
                             "detail": f"{sorted(capt & actv)} captured master data and activated"})
        else:
            findings.append({"case": case_id, "rule": "R9b", "verdict": "pass"})
    return findings


# Equivalent library-level screen (returns the *violating* cases only).
# Run it once per activity pair; note it has no notion of "applicable" approval,
# so the Compliance-Approval variant must be run separately and the irrelevant
# hits filtered out afterwards.
def four_eyes_screen(log):
    return {
        "verify_vs_manager":    pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
            log, "Verify Bank Account", "Manager Approval"),
        "verify_vs_compliance": pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
            log, "Verify Bank Account", "Compliance Approval"),
        "capture_vs_activate":  pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
            log, "Capture Master Data", "Activate Vendor"),
    }


# --- R5 / R6 : emergency activation ----------------------------------------
EMERGENCY_WINDOW = pd.Timedelta(hours=24)

def check_emergency_activation(df, resolution="B"):
    """R5 says the legal opinion must precede activation after an escalation.
       R6 permits the reverse order under joint authorization + 24h repair.
       Only reachable when the case actually contains Sanctions Escalation."""
    findings = []
    for case_id, ev in df.sort_values(TS).groupby(CASE):
        esc = _first_ts(ev, "Sanctions Escalation")
        if esc is None:
            findings.append({"case": case_id, "rule": "R5/R6", "verdict": "pass",
                             "detail": "vacuous: no sanctions escalation"})
            continue

        opinion = _first_ts(ev, "Obtain Legal Opinion")
        activation = _first_ts(ev, "Activate Vendor")

        if opinion is None and activation is not None:
            findings.append({"case": case_id, "rule": "R4", "verdict": "fail",
                             "detail": "escalated and activated, legal opinion never obtained"})
            continue
        if activation is None or opinion < activation:
            findings.append({"case": case_id, "rule": "R5", "verdict": "pass"})
            continue

        # --- opinion came AFTER activation: the contested region -------------
        if resolution == "A":                       # Legal-strict
            findings.append({"case": case_id, "rule": "R5", "verdict": "fail",
                             "detail": "activation before legal opinion; R6 not admissible"})
            continue

        emergency = ev.attrs.get("case:emergency") if hasattr(ev, "attrs") else None
        if emergency is None and "case:emergency" in ev.columns:
            emergency = bool(ev["case:emergency"].iloc[0])
        authorizers = None
        if "case:joint_authorization" in ev.columns:
            raw = ev["case:joint_authorization"].iloc[0]
            authorizers = set(raw if isinstance(raw, (list, set)) else str(raw).split(";"))

        within = (opinion - activation) <= EMERGENCY_WINDOW

        if emergency is None or authorizers is None:
            findings.append({"case": case_id, "rule": "R6", "verdict": "fail",
                             "detail": "EMERGENCY_ACTIVATION: activation preceded the legal "
                                       "opinion and the log carries no emergency flag / joint "
                                       "authorization; conservative default = violation. "
                                       f"Repair delay {(opinion - activation)} "
                                       f"({'within' if within else 'outside'} the 24h window). "
                                       "Remediation: instrument the log or file a manual waiver."})
        elif not emergency or len(authorizers) < 2:
            findings.append({"case": case_id, "rule": "R6", "verdict": "fail",
                             "detail": "not flagged as emergency, or fewer than two authorizers"})
        elif not within:
            findings.append({"case": case_id, "rule": "R6", "verdict": "fail",
                             "detail": f"authorized emergency, but the opinion arrived after "
                                       f"{opinion - activation} (> 24h)"})
        else:
            findings.append({"case": case_id, "rule": "R6", "verdict": "warning",
                             "detail": "authorized emergency activation, opinion within 24h; "
                                       "log for periodic Legal review"})
    return findings


# --- R11 : re-verification before a later payment ---------------------------
def check_post_activation_bank_change(df):
    findings = []
    for case_id, ev in df.sort_values(TS).groupby(CASE):
        ev = ev.reset_index(drop=True)
        activation = _first_ts(ev, "Activate Vendor")
        changes = ev[(ev[ACT] == "Change Bank Details")]
        if activation is None or changes.empty:
            continue
        for _, ch in changes.iterrows():
            if ch[TS] < activation:
                continue
            after = ev[ev[TS] > ch[TS]]
            if "Suspend Vendor" not in set(after[ACT]):
                findings.append({"case": case_id, "rule": "R11a", "verdict": "fail",
                                 "detail": "post-activation bank change never followed by suspension"})
            pays = after[after[ACT] == "Release First Payment"]
            for _, p in pays.iterrows():
                between = ev[(ev[TS] > ch[TS]) & (ev[TS] < p[TS]) &
                             (ev[ACT] == "Verify Bank Account")]
                if between.empty:
                    findings.append({"case": case_id, "rule": "R11b", "verdict": "fail",
                                     "detail": "payment after a bank change with no re-verification"})
    return findings
```

Note on `filter_four_eyes_principle`: it screens the whole log for cases where the same resource performed both named activities and is the fastest first pass, but it is unaware of which approval is *applicable*, so a case that legitimately has no `Compliance Approval` simply never matches. The per-case function above is the authoritative version because it selects the applicable approval first and distinguishes `fail` from `not evaluated`.

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

**Duplicated enforcement.** R1 is enforced three times (procedural XOR, `noncoexistence`, `never_together`); R8 likewise; R7 twice (POWL edge, `precedence`, plus `always_before`). Duplication is defensible only when the authoritative layer is named — otherwise a single trace yields three findings for one root cause and inflates the apparent violation count. The mapping table in §2 names one authoritative layer per requirement, and the deployment runner in §10 deduplicates by (case, requirement) rather than by (case, constraint). The cost of redundancy is real: it also means relaxing a policy requires edits in three places, and a partial edit produces a model that silently disagrees with itself.

**Constraints that break under loops and post-activation change.** The sharpest one is **R10 vs R11**: R10 caps `Release First Payment` at one occurrence, but R11 speaks of "any *later* `Release First Payment`" after a bank change. Under R10 that later payment can never exist, so R11's second clause is permanently vacuous — it can never fire, and therefore can never catch anything. Two readings: either R10 is scoped to the onboarding case and post-activation payments belong to a different case notion, or R11 anticipates a payment-run process that is out of scope. I take the first reading; the consequence is that `activ_freq["Verify Bank Account"] = {1,2,3}` is the only trace of R11 that has any operational effect in this model, and the real fix is a second case notion (§10). Similarly, the clarification loop interacts badly with `exactly_one("Classify Risk")` if a clarification is ever raised *after* classification — the model forbids re-classification, so a late clarification has nowhere to lead. And `chainprecedence(Activate Vendor, Change Bank Details)` becomes false the moment `Release First Payment` intervenes, which is why it carries confidence 0.6.

**Vacuity.** `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` is vacuously satisfied in T1–T5 and T8, where no escalation occurs — six of eight test cases "pass" a sanctions control that was never activated. Likewise `response(Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor)` and `precedence(Verify Bank Account, Release First Payment)` in rejected cases. A conformance report that counts vacuous satisfactions as evidence of compliance is actively misleading. Any deployment must report **activated** vs **satisfied** counts separately, and a control whose activation count is zero across the whole log should be surfaced as untested, not as green.

**Missing case attributes.** No `risk_class` (so the model cannot check that the high-risk route was taken by a high-risk vendor — the single largest blind spot, since a mis-routed high-risk vendor through `Manager Approval` is indistinguishable from a legitimate low-risk case); no `emergency` / `joint_authorization` (so R6 is unevaluable); no vendor country/sector (so screening adequacy is unassessable); no amount on `Release First Payment`. Three of twelve requirements are partly or wholly outside the log's expressive reach.

**Timestamp-only limitations.** Events are instantaneous points, with no start/complete lifecycle, so "elapsed time" conflates queueing with processing — a 34-hour legal opinion in T7 may be 33 hours of waiting and one hour of work, or the reverse, and the profile cannot tell. Hour-granularity timestamps make concurrent events at the same hour unorderable, and the whole model's ordering semantics rest on total timestamp order; ties, clock skew between systems, or batch-written events would silently produce or hide ordering violations. Nothing here distinguishes "the opinion was written late" from "the opinion was logged late".

**Support/confidence as normative truth.** Every rule carries `support = 1.0` because the output format demands it — that number is an *assertion about this model*, not a measurement of any log, and it must never be read as "this rule holds in 100 % of observed cases". Confidence values here encode *my* prescriptive weighting (1.0 = hard, 0.6 = advisory), which is the opposite of the discovery-time meaning, where confidence is an estimated frequency. Mixing the two is a genuine hazard: a rule mined at confidence 0.97 from a log full of non-compliant behaviour describes what people *do*, not what they *should* do, and promoting mined metadata into a compliance gate launders historical misconduct into policy. Discovered and hand-authored constraints must be kept in separate files with separate provenance, and only the hand-authored set may produce `fail` verdicts.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

**Canonical procedural representation.** Keep the POWL model of §3 as the authored artefact — it is the only notation among the three that expresses the concurrent check block, the concurrently-scheduled clarification loop, and the escalation-before-outcome edge without either over-serializing or losing the synchronization at `Classify Risk`. Convert it to an accepting Petri net (`pm4py.convert_to_petri_net`) for execution, because alignment-based conformance gives per-move diagnostics and fitness numbers that a POWL replay does not, and the net is the interchange format for the rest of the stack. Version both, and treat the POWL as source and the net as build output.

**Authoritative policy layer.** The **procedural model is authoritative for the happy-path control flow** (backbone steps 1–5: ordering, synchronization, route exclusivity). **DECLARE is authoritative for the cross-cutting obligations that survive loops and optional activities** (R3, R4, R7, R11). **The Log Skeleton is authoritative for cardinality and co-occurrence** (R1, R8, R10). **The resource/data layer is authoritative for the final verdict on R5/R6 and for R9**, and it holds veto power: a case that passes every activity-only layer can still be rejected there (T4 is exactly this). **The temporal profile is authoritative for nothing** — it only annotates.

**Order of checks.** Run cheapest-and-most-decisive first, and stop escalating detail once a hard violation is found, but always run every layer for reporting completeness:

1. **Data quality gate** — required attributes present, timestamps monotone, no unordered ties. Failures here downgrade downstream layers to `not evaluated` rather than letting them pass by default.
2. **Log Skeleton** (`pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton`) — O(n) per trace, catches the catastrophic cases (T8's double outcome) before any alignment is computed.
3. **DECLARE** (`pm4py.conformance_declare`) — catches obligation failures (T5), with vacuity post-processing and the R5 post-filter from §4.
4. **Procedural alignment** against the accepting Petri net — the expensive step, run for precise diagnostics and to localize the deviation (T5, T6).
5. **Resource/data layer** (§7) — segregation of duties and the emergency-activation check. Runs regardless of upstream verdicts, since it is the only layer that can see T4.
6. **Temporal profile** (`pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile`, ζ = 2.0) — last, annotating already-classified cases.

**Surfacing contradictory findings.** Never collapse layers into a single boolean. Each case gets a record of the form:

- **Overall verdict** = `fail` if any layer with hard authority fails; `warning` if the only findings are temporal or advisory-confidence; `data-blocked` when a hard check abstained or hit the conservative default (T6 today); `pass` otherwise.
- **Earliest decisive violation** — one line, with the event index and the requirement id, so the analyst reads a cause rather than a list of symptoms.
- **All findings**, deduplicated by (case, requirement) not by (case, constraint), each labelled with its layer and whether the constraint was *activated* or *vacuously* satisfied.
- **Layer disagreements shown explicitly, never resolved silently.** T6's row must display "Log Skeleton: pass / Procedural: fail" side by side, with the note that the skeleton has no constraint relating the opinion to the activation. T7 must display "control flow pass / temporal warning". Hiding either would give a false impression of consensus.
- **The R5/R6 conflict rendered as a named, standing policy conflict** in the report header — not as an ordinary constraint violation. Every `EMERGENCY_ACTIVATION` finding carries both the Legal reading and the Operations reading, the implemented resolution (B), the conservative default that produced the verdict, and the concrete remediation: add `case:emergency` and `case:joint_authorization` (with authorizer ids, subject to their own four-eyes check) to the event log. Until that instrumentation exists, the report should state plainly that R6 cannot be evaluated and that the current verdict reflects missing evidence rather than proven misconduct.
