## 1. Conformance semantics

The norm here is deliberately partial, so the first analytical act is not measuring deviation but deciding *whether a deviation is even definable* for the observation at hand. I use seven mutually informed classes.

**Forbidden behavior.** An observation that instantiates a relation explicitly marked `forbidden` (N3, T6, E10). Here, and only here, the norm is closed-world: a single occurrence is a violation by definition, independent of frequency. Severity is then modulated by whether the occurrence is *real* behavior or an *artifact*, but the norm-level verdict is fixed. Two Orders showing `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` (N3) and one `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` edge (T6) and one `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` edge (E10) are the only three candidates in this snapshot.

**Missing mandatory behavior.** A relation marked `mandatory` whose *stated scope* is instantiated in the data but whose expected counterpart is absent. Mandatory statements are closed-world only *within their scope columns*: N1 covers "newly created standard Orders", N4 covers "packages intended for external shipment", E1–E8 cover "every event of the named type". So the arithmetic gaps — 2/100 Orders without `Create Order -> Validate Order` (N1), 2/200 packages without `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` (N4), 2 `Dispatch Package` events without a Delivery (E6/T2), 2 `Add Item` events without an Order (E2), 1 `Record Payment` without an Invoice (E8) — are all *prima facie* mandatory gaps. Prima facie, because each one must survive two defeaters: scope (was the case actually inside the stated scope?) and abstraction (is the encoding of the rule faithful to the rule?).

**Unexpected but unregulated behavior.** Behavior present in the log and absent from the norm, where the norm has *not* claimed coverage. Under the open-world default this is not a deviation at all. `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` (72 Orders), `Issue Invoice -> Write Off` (4), `Reopen Order -> Close Order` (N6, explicitly unspecified, 1 Order), `(Order, object_interaction, Customer)` (T4, 100), `(Order, object_interaction, RiskCase)` (T5, 3), `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)` (7), `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer` (3) and `Manual Compliance Review -> RiskCase` (E9, 3) all fall here. The correct output for these is "out of scope — candidate for norm extension", not "violation". Treating them as violations would be a closed-world error: it would silently promote the norm from a partial specification to a complete process model, which the norm itself disclaims.

**Typical-pattern absence.** A relation marked `typical` (N2, N5, T3) that is absent in some cases. Absence is *expected at some rate* because the norm names legitimate exceptions. Since the norm sets no tolerance band, the only defensible statement is descriptive: 83/100 Orders show `Validate Order -> Close Order` (N2), 92/110 Invoices show `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` (N5), and the 92-weight `(Invoice, object_interaction, Payment)` edge (T3) is consistent with that. Deviation from a `typical` relation is a *monitoring signal*, never a compliance finding, unless a rate threshold is later added to the norm.

**Likely data-quality error.** An observation whose most parsimonious explanation is a defect in extraction, joining, or labelling rather than in the real process. The `Sensor Heartbeat`–Invoice edge is the archetype: a join on a reused numeric identifier. Crucially, a data-quality explanation does *not* dissolve the finding; it relocates it. An E10-forbidden edge that is an artifact is a **critical data-integrity incident** (a financial object was spuriously linked to a telemetry stream) even though it is a **zero-severity behavioral finding**. Keeping these two verdicts separate is the discipline the norm's structure demands.

**Abstraction mismatch.** The norm says "should *eventually* enter validation" (N1) but the summary encodes it as a *directly-follows* count. If an unrecorded or filtered review event sits between `Create Order` and `Validate Order`, the DF edge vanishes while the actual rule holds perfectly. The observation is then an artifact of the abstraction, not of the process. This is the single most common false-positive generator in OC-DFG conformance and it applies directly to the 2 missing N1 edges.

**Insufficient evidence.** The observation is compatible with both a conforming and a nonconforming world and the summary cannot discriminate. The one `Record Payment` with a Payment but no Invoice is exactly this: E8 exempts unapplied cash, finance confirms unapplied cash exists, but the event is not *typed* as unapplied cash, and E8's scope clause says "none are so typed in this snapshot". So either the event is a genuine E8 violation, or it is unapplied cash with a missing label — a data-quality issue that would make it exempt. One record-level inspection resolves it; no amount of graph aggregation will.

**Open vs. closed world, summarized.** Closed-world reasoning applies to (a) forbidden edges — presence is sufficient for violation; (b) mandatory edges *within stated scope* — absence is sufficient for a finding, subject to scope and abstraction defeaters. Everything else is open-world: absence from the norm carries no information, and observed-but-unmodeled relations are evidence about the *norm's* incompleteness rather than about the process's compliance. Confusing these two regimes produces the two symmetric failure modes: over-reporting (every unmodeled edge becomes a violation) and under-reporting (every mandatory gap is excused as "probably just modeling").

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

**What it establishes.** Per-object-type control-flow adjacency and its frequency: that 98 of 100 Orders had validation directly after creation, that 198 of 200 external packages went pack→dispatch, that 2 Orders show a cancel→dispatch adjacency. It is good at localizing *where in a lifecycle* an expected step is missing, and it makes the forbidden N3 pattern directly visible.

**What it cannot establish.** Causality, intent, simultaneity across object types, or anything about events it does not contain. It cannot tell you whether the two missing N1 edges reflect an unvalidated Order or merely an intervening event. It cannot see the *content* of a dispatch (was anything physically shipped?). And it cannot distinguish a truly absent activity from one filtered out during extraction.

**Counts and scope.** Every count must be read against its denominator *and* the scope clause. "98 of 100 newly created Orders" is a scoped ratio; "2 Orders" for `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` has no denominator at all, and needs none — under a closed-world forbidden rule, n≥1 is the finding. Conversely, "83 Orders" for the typical N2 has a denominator but no threshold, so it supports description only. The asymmetry is deliberate: forbidden rules are count-insensitive, mandatory rules are ratio-sensitive within scope, typical rules are rate-descriptive.

**Same relation, two abstractions.** `Validate Order -> Close Order` at 83/100 looks like a 17% shortfall at the directly-follows abstraction and probably like 100% conformance at the eventually-follows abstraction, because N2 itself says intermediate invoice, review and fulfilment events occur. The relation is conforming at the level the rule actually speaks about and suspicious only at the level the summary happens to use.

**The eventually/directly danger.** N1 states "should *eventually* enter validation" and is then "approximated" by a directly-follows expectation. That approximation is not conservative — it is strictly stronger than the rule. Any Order with a legitimate intervening step (a hold, a credit check, an unrecorded review) fails the DF test while satisfying the norm. Reporting those two Orders as N1 violations would manufacture a control finding out of a modelling shortcut. The correct discipline: never escalate a DF gap against an eventuality rule until the eventually-follows relation has been checked on the raw traces. The same caution applies in reverse for forbidden rules — N3 as a *directly-follows* test would *miss* a dispatch that occurs two events after cancellation, so for forbidden eventuality-style rules the DF abstraction under-reports. Abstraction error has a direction, and it flips between mandatory and forbidden rules.

### OTG

**What it establishes.** Which object types are related, by which relationship kind, and how often — including relationship kinds control-flow models cannot express, such as `object_cobirth` and `object_codeath`.

**What it cannot establish.** Temporal ordering, event-level attribution, or the business meaning of a relationship kind. It also cannot tell you whether an edge's weight is an aggregate over many legitimate cases or a single artifact: `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` weight 1 is a forbidden edge under T6, but the graph alone gives no way to distinguish a genuine control bypass from two objects sharing a creation timestamp inside one batch import.

**Counts and scope.** `(Package, object_interaction, Delivery)` at 200 matches the 200 packages, yet the ET–OT view shows only 198 `Dispatch Package`–Delivery links. The two are not contradictory: interaction may arise from a non-dispatch event. That is precisely a case where an edge is *conforming at the OTG abstraction* (T2 satisfied, weight 200) and *suspicious at the ET–OT abstraction* (E6 short by two). Reading only the OTG would have concealed the gap. Similarly `(Invoice, object_interaction, Payment)` at 92 is fine under T3 regardless of the 18 unpaid invoices, because T3 is typical and explicitly permits unapplied cash.

**Unregulated edges.** T4 (Customer, 100) and T5 (RiskCase, 3) are marked unspecified; the codeath edges (7) are simply not mentioned. Under the open-world default all three are non-findings. The 7 codeaths in particular are a *modelling* observation — an artifact of one confirmation event terminating two lifecycles — and their proper destination is the norm-revision backlog.

### ET–OT

**What it establishes.** Whether each event type carries the object types the norm requires. It is the sharpest instrument for E1–E10 because the mandatory statements are phrased per-event ("every Add Item event"), so the arithmetic is direct: 1450 Add Item–Item vs 1448 Add Item–Order gives exactly two E2 gaps; 200 vs 198 gives two E6 gaps; 93 vs 92 gives one E8 candidate.

**What it cannot establish.** Ordering, case identity, or whether an absent link is absent in reality or only in the extraction. It also cannot read exemption clauses: E8's unapplied-cash carve-out depends on an event *label* that the summary says is not applied to any event, so the graph produces a gap that only record-level inspection can adjudicate.

**Counts and scope.** The near-miss counts here (2, 2, 1) are the most trustworthy signals in the whole summary because their denominators are exact and their scope is "every event". They also illustrate the value of small absolute numbers: each gap is individually inspectable, which is why the recommended action for ranks 3–5 is record retrieval rather than statistical analysis.

**Cross-abstraction tension.** `Sensor Heartbeat`–Invoice at weight 1 is an unambiguous E10 breach in the ET–OT view. In the OTG view it may not even appear as a notable edge. And in the OC-DFG view it is invisible entirely, since heartbeats have no place in the Order or Invoice lifecycles. A relation can thus be undetectable, benign, and forbidden depending on which projection you look through — which is the strongest argument for running all three families and reconciling them, rather than trusting any single diagnostic.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

**Mandatory (in scope; gaps require action or explicit scope/abstraction defeat)**
- N1 `Create Order -> Validate Order`, 98/100 — 2 gaps, *conditional*: reclassify as abstraction mismatch if an eventually-follows check succeeds, or as out-of-scope if the two Orders are not "standard".
- N4 `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package`, 198/200 — 2 gaps, *conditional* on whether both packages were truly intended for external shipment.
- E2 `Add Item`–Order, 1448/1450 — 2 gaps, no plausible scope defeater; genuine finding pending record inspection.
- E6 / T2 `Dispatch Package`–Delivery, 198/200 — 2 gaps; carrier attribute makes a data-quality explanation strong but does not remove the gap.
- E8 `Record Payment`–Invoice, 92/93 — 1 gap, *conditional*: exempt if the event is genuinely unapplied cash and merely unlabelled.
- E1 (100/100), E3 (1450), E4 (200), E5 (200), E7 (93), T1 (1450) — satisfied.

**Forbidden (closed-world; any occurrence is a finding)**
- N3 `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`, 2 Orders — behavioral violation unless the cancellation timestamps prove the dispatch preceded cancellation.
- E10 `Sensor Heartbeat`–Invoice, 1 — violation of the edge rule; *condition*: if confirmed as an identifier-collision artifact it becomes a data-integrity defect with no behavioral content, but it must still be removed, not excused.
- T6 `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)`, 1 — violation; *condition*: reclassify as a modelling artifact if cobirth is inferred from equal timestamps within a batch load rather than from a shared business event.

**Typical (absence is a signal, never a violation without a threshold)**
- N2 `Validate Order -> Close Order`, 83/100 — absence anticipated by the norm's own text.
- N5 `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment`, 92/110 — 4 write-offs and 14 open invoices are named legitimate exceptions.
- T3 `(Invoice, object_interaction, Payment)`, 92 — consistent with N5; no finding.
- *Condition for all three:* they become findings only if the norm is amended with an explicit rate band or aging rule.

**Unregulated (open-world; not violations)**
- `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, 72 — unmodeled, desirable.
- `Issue Invoice -> Write Off`, 4 — explicitly anticipated by N5's exception clause.
- N6 `Reopen Order -> Close Order`, 1 — declared unspecified.
- T4 `(Order, object_interaction, Customer)`, 100 — governed elsewhere.
- T5 `(Order, object_interaction, RiskCase)`, 3, and E9 `Manual Compliance Review`–RiskCase, 3 — declared unspecified.
- `Manual Compliance Review`–Customer, 3, and `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review`, 3 — unmodeled, plausible.
- `(Package, object_codeath, Delivery)`, 7 — codeath entirely outside the norm.
- *Condition for all:* any of these becomes regulated only by an explicit norm amendment; none should be escalated on rarity alone.

## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch

```python
import pm4py

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Discovery on the observed log. Raw PM4Py outputs are never mutated.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
obs_ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
obs_otg   = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
obs_etot  = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Structural conformance against the normative structures.
#    norm_ocdfg / norm_otg / norm_etot carry ONLY graph structure.
#    PM4Py answers "present / absent", not "allowed / required".
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
diag_ocdfg = pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(norm_ocdfg, obs_ocdfg)
diag_otg   = pm4py.conformance_otg(norm_otg,   obs_otg)
diag_etot  = pm4py.conformance_etot(norm_etot, obs_etot)

RAW_DIAGNOSTICS = {          # preserved verbatim for audit / replay
    "ocdfg": diag_ocdfg,
    "otg":   diag_otg,
    "etot":  diag_etot,
}

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. SEMANTIC METADATA LAYER.
#    This is the part PM4Py cannot hold: status, scope, exemptions, tolerance.
#    It MUST live outside the bare graph structures, keyed by the same edges,
#    and be version-controlled alongside the written norm document.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
NORM_STATUS = {
    # OC-DFG: (object_type, act_from, act_to) -> metadata
    ("Order",   "Create Order",   "Validate Order"):
        dict(id="N1", status="mandatory", scope="standard orders",
             semantics="eventually",   # <-- rule is an eventuality, not a DF edge
             note="DF encoding is STRICTER than the rule; recheck gaps with EF"),
    ("Order",   "Validate Order", "Close Order"):
        dict(id="N2", status="typical", tolerance=None),
    ("Order",   "Cancel Order",   "Dispatch Package"):
        dict(id="N3", status="forbidden", semantics="eventually",
             note="DF encoding UNDER-reports; scan full traces after cancellation"),
    ("Package", "Pack Package",   "Dispatch Package"):
        dict(id="N4", status="mandatory", scope="external shipment"),
    ("Invoice", "Issue Invoice",  "Record Payment"):
        dict(id="N5", status="typical", tolerance=None),
    ("Order",   "Reopen Order",   "Close Order"):
        dict(id="N6", status="unspecified"),
    # OTG: (type1, relationship, type2)
    ("Order",   "object_interaction", "Item"):     dict(id="T1", status="mandatory"),
    ("Package", "object_interaction", "Delivery"): dict(id="T2", status="mandatory"),
    ("Invoice", "object_interaction", "Payment"):  dict(id="T3", status="typical"),
    ("Order",   "object_interaction", "Customer"): dict(id="T4", status="unspecified"),
    ("Order",   "object_interaction", "RiskCase"): dict(id="T5", status="unspecified"),
    ("Order",   "object_cobirth",     "Payment"):  dict(id="T6", status="forbidden"),
    # ET-OT: (event_type, object_type)
    ("Create Order", "Order"):             dict(id="E1", status="mandatory"),
    ("Add Item", "Order"):                 dict(id="E2", status="mandatory"),
    ("Add Item", "Item"):                  dict(id="E3", status="mandatory"),
    ("Pack Package", "Package"):           dict(id="E4", status="mandatory"),
    ("Dispatch Package", "Package"):       dict(id="E5", status="mandatory"),
    ("Dispatch Package", "Delivery"):      dict(id="E6", status="mandatory"),
    ("Record Payment", "Payment"):         dict(id="E7", status="mandatory"),
    ("Record Payment", "Invoice"):
        dict(id="E8", status="mandatory", exemption="event typed as unapplied cash"),
    ("Manual Compliance Review", "RiskCase"): dict(id="E9", status="unspecified"),
    ("Sensor Heartbeat", "Invoice"):       dict(id="E10", status="forbidden"),
}

# Known extraction defects. Kept separate so a behavioral verdict is never
# silently downgraded -- the finding is relocated, not deleted.
SUSPECTED_DQ = {
    ("Sensor Heartbeat", "Invoice"): "join on reused numeric identifier",
    ("Dispatch Package", "Delivery"): "carrier reference present in attribute",
}

def classify(key, kind, observed_present):
    """Map a raw PM4Py present/absent fact to a semantic deviation class."""
    meta = NORM_STATUS.get(key)
    if meta is None:
        # OPEN WORLD: silence in the norm is not prohibition.
        return dict(cls="unregulated", severity="informational",
                    action="candidate for norm extension")
    status = meta["status"]
    if status == "forbidden" and observed_present:
        cls = "forbidden_behavior"
        if key in SUSPECTED_DQ:
            cls = "forbidden_edge_suspected_data_quality"
        return dict(cls=cls, severity="high", norm=meta["id"],
                    dq_hypothesis=SUSPECTED_DQ.get(key))
    if status == "mandatory" and not observed_present:
        if meta.get("semantics") == "eventually":
            # Do NOT report yet: the DF abstraction is stronger than the rule.
            return dict(cls="abstraction_mismatch_recheck_EF", severity="deferred",
                        norm=meta["id"])
        if meta.get("exemption"):
            return dict(cls="insufficient_evidence", severity="medium",
                        norm=meta["id"], resolve_by="inspect event label")
        return dict(cls="missing_mandatory", severity="medium", norm=meta["id"],
                    caveat="verify scope membership before escalation")
    if status == "typical" and not observed_present:
        return dict(cls="typical_absence", severity="low", norm=meta["id"],
                    note="no tolerance band defined in the norm")
    if status == "unspecified":
        return dict(cls="unregulated", severity="informational", norm=meta["id"])
    return dict(cls="conforming", severity="none", norm=meta.get("id"))

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Deferred eventually-follows recheck for rules whose semantics are
#    "eventually" (N1, N3). This needs the RAW LOG, not the graph summary.
#    pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Order") -> temporal / LTL check per case.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# n.b. Every "deferred" verdict above must be resolved here before any
#      finding is published. A DF gap is a hypothesis, not a violation.

SEMANTIC_FINDINGS = {  # sits BESIDE RAW_DIAGNOSTICS, never replaces it
    family: {k: classify(k, family, present)
             for k, present in enumerate_edges(diag)}   # project-specific adapter
    for family, diag in RAW_DIAGNOSTICS.items()
}
```

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1's "eventually" is encoded as directly-follows.** *Risk:* systematic false positives — the 2/100 gap would be escalated as a validation-control failure when a benign intervening event explains it; conversely analysts learn to distrust the report. *Clarification:* state that N1 is an eventuality constraint over the Order lifecycle and specify the permitted intervening activities (hold, credit check, review). *Status:* **mandatory** (eventuality form).

2. **N3 is not given a temporal reach.** *Risk:* far more serious than (1) — as a directly-follows rule it only catches dispatch *immediately* after cancellation, so a dispatch two events later escapes entirely. The 2 detected Orders may be the visible part of a larger set. *Clarification:* "no `Dispatch Package` may occur at any point after `Cancel Order` for the same Order, unless the cancellation is itself reversed by a documented `Reopen Order`." *Status:* **forbidden** (eventuality form).

3. **No rate bands for `typical` relations.** *Risk:* N2 at 83% and N5 at 84% cannot be judged. A drop to 50% would be equally "typical" under the current text, so genuine degradation is invisible. *Clarification:* define an expected range plus an alerting threshold per typical relation (e.g. N5 ≥ 80% within 60 days of issue). *Status:* **typical**, with an explicit numeric band.

4. **Scope terms are undefined.** *Risk:* "standard Order" (N1) and "intended for external shipment" (N4) are the exact defeaters invoked for the 2+2 gaps, yet neither is operationalized, so scope becomes an unfalsifiable excuse for any mandatory gap. *Clarification:* bind each scope to a named attribute and value set in the log. *Status:* **mandatory** (definitional).

5. **Unapplied cash is exempted but not identifiable.** *Risk:* E8's carve-out is unusable — the single 93-vs-92 gap cannot be adjudicated because no event carries the label, leaving a permanent "insufficient evidence" class. *Clarification:* require an explicit payment-application status attribute on every `Record Payment` event; absence of the attribute counts as a violation, not as an exemption. *Status:* **mandatory**.

6. **Cobirth/codeath semantics are undefined.** *Risk:* T6 forbids one cobirth pair while codeath is unmentioned, and the 7 Package–Delivery codeaths plus the 1 Order–Payment cobirth cannot be interpreted without knowing whether these relations are derived from shared *business events* or from coincident *timestamps*. A timestamp-derived T6 breach is meaningless; an event-derived one is a control bypass. *Clarification:* define cobirth/codeath as "created/terminated by the same event identifier", and state explicitly that codeath between Package and Delivery is permitted. *Status:* definitional plus **explicitly unspecified** for codeath (or `typical` if the confirmation pattern is endorsed).

7. **No policy on unmodeled activities.** *Risk:* `Issue Invoice`, `Write Off`, `Manual Compliance Review` and `Reopen Order` all appear in the log with no normative statement, so each analyst decides ad hoc whether they matter — the exact inconsistency this exercise is guarding against. *Clarification:* state the open-world default in the norm document itself and maintain a register of known-but-unregulated activities. *Status:* **explicitly unspecified**, with a review cadence.

8. **No observation-window or truncation rule.** *Risk:* the 14 invoices "with no observed successor" and the 17 Orders without a close may be right-censored rather than deviant, inflating typical-absence counts. *Clarification:* define the window and exclude cases whose expected completion falls outside it. *Status:* **mandatory** (measurement rule).

9. **No cross-policy reference for T4.** *Risk:* T4 defers Customer relations to "another policy" that is not named, so nobody can confirm coverage — the 100 Order–Customer edges and the 3 Review–Customer edges sit in a governance gap. *Clarification:* cite the owning policy by identifier. *Status:* **explicitly unspecified**, with pointer.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Immediate containment.** Two findings drive this, and only two. First, N3 (`Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`, 2 Orders): place both Orders under hold, ask warehouse and carrier whether physical goods left the site, and if so initiate recall or re-billing. Because N3's directly-follows encoding under-reports, run an *eventually-follows* scan over all cancelled Orders the same day — the true population may exceed 2. Second, E10 (`Sensor Heartbeat`–Invoice): quarantine the edge from all conformance and financial reporting immediately and check whether the reused-identifier join contaminates other object types; a single spurious link into the invoice graph implies the join key is unsafe generally. Nothing else in this snapshot warrants containment. In particular the 3 Manual Compliance Reviews, the 1 Reopen, and the 7 codeaths are rare *and* unregulated, and rarity alone is not a reason to escalate.

**Data repair.** Backfill the 2 missing Delivery objects from the carrier references on the `Dispatch Package` events (E6/T2), and fix the extractor so a carrier reference always materializes a Delivery. Repair the 2 `Add Item` events missing an Order link (E2) once the intended Order is identified. Add the payment-application status attribute so E8's exemption becomes testable, and re-type the one 93-vs-92 payment accordingly. Fix the identifier-collision join behind E10 and re-run discovery afterwards — several counts will change.

**Process investigation.** Beyond the N3 cases: determine why 2 Orders show no creation-to-validation adjacency (N1) — first by an eventually-follows test, then, only if that also fails, as a control review. Same two-step for the 2 packages under N4: establish whether they were dispatched without packing (a real control gap) or packed without dispatch (an inventory question) or simply not external (out of scope). Investigate the single T6 cobirth event to determine whether cobirth here means "same business event" or "same timestamp". Route the 14 unpaid and 4 written-off invoices to normal AR aging review; they are not conformance work.

**Norm revision.** Adopt the nine clarifications in section 6, prioritizing (2) N3's temporal reach — it changes what the *highest-severity* rule detects — then (1) N1's eventuality form, (4) scope definitions, and (5) the unapplied-cash label. Add positive statements for the observed-but-unmodeled relations: `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` and `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer` as `typical`, Package–Delivery codeath as permitted, `Issue Invoice -> Write Off` as a controlled exception with approval requirements.

**Monitoring.** Track as *rates*, not incidents: N2 and N5 conformance percentages with the new bands, write-off rate, manual-review rate. Track as *zero-tolerance alerts*: any N3, T6 or E10 occurrence. Track as *data-quality KPIs*: dispatch-without-Delivery count, add-item-without-Order count, payment-without-Invoice count, and join-collision detections. Keep the raw PM4Py diagnostics and the semantic classification layer as separate artifacts so that a change in the norm can be replayed against unchanged evidence.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

**Robust.** The arithmetic gaps are certain: 2 missing `Add Item`–Order links, 2 missing `Dispatch Package`–Delivery links, 1 `Record Payment` without an Invoice, 2 Orders with a cancel→dispatch adjacency, 1 cobirth edge, 1 heartbeat–invoice edge. That N3, T6 and E10 are the only closed-world forbidden rules, and that `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer`, Package–Delivery codeath, and `Reopen Order -> Close Order` are *not* violations, is robust and follows directly from the norm's own status assignments.

**Dependent on the norm's incompleteness.** Whether the N2 (83/100) and N5 (92/110) rates are acceptable is undecidable without tolerance bands. Whether the 2 N1 gaps and 2 N4 gaps are in scope depends on undefined terms ("standard Order", "intended for external shipment"). Whether the T6 cobirth is a control bypass depends on an undefined cobirth semantics. Whether the 14 successor-less invoices are deviant or merely censored depends on an unstated observation window.

**Dependent on suspected data quality.** The severity of the E10 edge hinges entirely on the identifier-collision hypothesis: artifact means a data-integrity incident, genuine means a serious control breach — the graph cannot tell them apart. The E6 gaps are probably extraction defects given the carrier attributes, but that is an inference from context, not from the log. The E8 gap is unresolvable until payment-application labelling exists.

**Undecidable from graph summaries alone.** Whether goods physically moved on the two cancelled Orders. Whether the two unvalidated Orders were actually unvalidated or merely non-adjacent. Any timestamp ordering, actor, amount, or approval. Whether absent relations are absent in the world or only in the extraction. And, most generally, whether the norm's silence on a relation reflects a considered decision or an oversight — that question is answered by talking to the norm's owners, not by re-running discovery.
