# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables
Assumptions used for all comparisons:

- A1. Each service-time range is modeled as a two-component mixture: an ordinary uniform draw on the ordinary range, and a complex-tail uniform draw on the tail range, with the given tail probability. That gives base expected active times of about `Intake 6.94`, `Mechanical 26.3`, `Electrical 32.82`, `Software 38.5`, `Validation 15.74` minutes before resource multipliers.
- A2. In `U4`, only diagnostic tail probabilities double, so the expected diagnostic means rise to about `Mechanical 29.6`, `Electrical 37.14`, `Software 44.5`; Intake and Validation stay at base.
- A3. Arrivals are treated as approximately uniform inside each 2-hour block unless the scenario specifies otherwise. In `U5`, the 09:30-10:00 Intake outage delays case release to the front door but does not change total demand.
- A4. “A case may be preempted at most once” is interpreted as: a lower-priority case can suffer at most one diagnostic interruption. Preemption is allowed only for diagnostic work, never for Intake or Validation.
- A5. Handover delay is charged only when a stage changes resource. Breaks and shift boundaries pause work but are not counted as handovers. The extra 5-minute cross-team transfer applies when a case moves between the mechanical side and the electrical/software side and the same cross-skilled owner is not retained.
- A6. Setup time is charged when a resource changes diagnostic skill family between consecutive cases. I treat that as switching between `Mechanical`, `Electrical`, and `Software` family assignments.
- A7. For `ME` and `ES`, sequential execution on one cross-skilled owner avoids the 8-minute coordination delay and the 5-minute cross-team transfer. Parallel execution adds 8 minutes before Validation but shortens the diagnostic critical path if both branches start soon enough.
- A8. Overtime up to 30 minutes is emergency slack, not normal capacity. A policy that needs systematic overtime is not robust even if its mean delay looks acceptable.

Parallel/sequential decision rule used in the analysis:

- `ME` sequential on one owner is typically about `66-71` active minutes on Ada/Deepa-class resources; parallel `ME` is roughly `33-41` minutes on the critical path plus the 8-minute coordination delay.
- `ES` sequential on Chen/Emil-class owners is about `81` active minutes; parallel `ES` is roughly `41-47` minutes on the critical path plus the 8-minute coordination delay.
- Therefore, parallelism is individually attractive for urgent work but globally expensive because it consumes the same scarce `Electrical/Software` capacity that becomes the bottleneck in `U1`, `U3`, and especially `U5`.

Decision variables:

- staffing/routing policy;
- which resources are treated as primary owners by skill family;
- whether `ME/ES` run sequentially or in parallel;
- when slow cross-skill overflow is allowed;
- how much P1 capacity is reserved;
- whether and when P1 preemption is allowed;
- whether same-resource continuity is preferred over the fastest nominal service.

Objective formulation 1, service-risk first:

- Lexicographic objective: maximize `P1 SLA`, then `P2 SLA`, then minimize `p90/p95 elapsed delay`, then minimize mean delay.
- This objective treats “robust” as minimizing worst-scenario SLA shortfall across `U1-U5`, not as minimizing base-case average delay.

Objective formulation 2, fairness-and-robustness:

- Minimize worst-scenario regret subject to `base-case max planned utilization <= 90%` for every individual where feasible.
- Secondary penalties are applied for large utilization dispersion, excessive handover/setup burden, and reliance on a single person as the evening bottleneck.

# 2. Capacity and offered-load analysis
The expected-load arithmetic is not the problem by itself; the coupling between time of day, cross-skills, handovers, and single-person evening coverage is the problem.

Auditable base calculations:

- Example 1, `10:00-12:00` electrical load: `9 arrivals * (E 25% + ME 25% + ES 10%) * 32.82 ≈ 177.2` active minutes.
- Example 2, `10:00-12:00` software load: `9 * (S 15% + ES 10%) * 38.5 ≈ 86.6` active minutes.
- Example 3, `14:00-16:00` electrical load: `10 * (30% + 20% + 12%) * 32.82 ≈ 203.5` active minutes.
- Example 4, `16:00-18:00` software load: `6 * (25% + 15%) * 38.5 ≈ 92.4` active minutes.

Raw effective skill capacity by block, before policy overhead:

- `10:00-12:00`: `Mechanical 421.7`, `Electrical 414.6`, `Software 181.7`, `Intake 578.2`, `Validation 553.4` active minutes.
- `16:00-18:00`: `Mechanical 96.0`, `Electrical 189.2`, `Software 90.0`, `Intake 201.8`, `Validation 210.0`.
- `18:00-20:00`: `Mechanical 0`, `Electrical 92.3`, `Software 120.0`, `Intake 109.1`, `Validation 120.0`.

That last line matters: after 18:00 there is no mechanical capacity at all. A late `M` or `ME` case may be structurally pushed into the next morning regardless of queue discipline.

Binding raw load/capacity ratios by scenario, before handover/setup/preemption overhead:

| scenario | binding ratios | implication |
|---|---|---|
| Base | `14:00-16:00 E 0.52, S 0.53`; `16:00-18:00 S 1.03`; `18:00-20:00 M unavailable` | Base mean load is not high overall, but evening software and late mechanical are structural holes. |
| U1 | `08:00-10:00 E 0.73, S 0.63`; `10:00-12:00 E 0.73, S 0.81`; plus base `16:00-18:00 S 1.03` | Morning P1 surge lands on the same E/S family that already has limited flexibility. |
| U2 | `12:00-14:00 E 0.44`; `14:00-16:00 E 0.61`; plus base `16:00-18:00 S 1.03` | Bottleneck migrates from mixed E/S to a stronger electrical tilt at midday. |
| U3 | `08:00-10:00 E 0.72, S 0.79`; `10:00-12:00 S 1.01`; `14:00-16:00 E 0.75, S 0.96` | Chen’s absence removes the best morning E/S anchor and nearly saturates software before noon. |
| U4 | `14:00-16:00 E 0.59, S 0.62`; `16:00-18:00 S 1.19` | This is less a mean-load shock than a tail-risk shock: long software and ES jobs occupy scarce capacity longer. |
| U5 | `08:00-10:00 E 1.23, S 1.34`; `10:00-12:00 E 1.02, S 1.72`; plus Intake outage | This is a true overload regime on the E/S side, not just a routing problem. |

Why policy still matters even when some raw ratios are below 1:

- Raw ratios ignore the 7-minute handovers, the 5-minute team transfers, the 5-minute diagnostic setup switches, and the 8-minute parallel coordination delay.
- For pooling, raw capacity looks larger than realized capacity because the fastest people also pay the highest family-switch/setup tax.
- For specialization, raw capacity looks larger than realized capacity because queue rigidity forces extra handovers and blocks same-resource continuity.

Resource-specific bottleneck observations:

- Chen and Emil are the only strong `Electrical/Software` owners. Chen is especially important before noon; Emil is the only software-capable late-shift specialist after 16:00.
- Deepa is the only meaningful late mechanical coverage after 16:00, and only until 18:00 plus 30 minutes overtime.
- Farah is useful as a surge absorber but is too slow at `1.40x` on diagnostic work to be a normal owner in a robust steady-state design.
- Intake and Validation are rarely raw capacity bottlenecks. They become burst amplifiers after parallel branches and under the `U5` outage.

Bottom line:

- Base case bottleneck: evening software, then late mechanical coverage.
- `U1`: morning E/S family.
- `U2`: electrical 12:00-16:00, then validation bursts if too much parallelism is used.
- `U3`: morning software first, then afternoon electrical/software.
- `U4`: software/ES tails.
- `U5`: front-door synchronization shock first, then outright E/S overload.

# 3. Candidate policies
Policy A, strict specialization with fixed queues:

- Eligibility: Ada/Ben own `Mechanical`; Chen/Deepa own `Electrical`; Chen/Emil own `Software`; Farah only absorbs overflow; Intake/Validation are separate FIFO activity queues.
- Priority score: priority class first, then FIFO inside each skill queue.
- Reservation: no explicit P1 reservation.
- Preemption: none in normal mode; cases are not interrupted because the restart penalty is too expensive without a global urgency score.
- Parallelization: `ME/ES` parallel only for `P1/P2`; otherwise sequential by queue progression.
- Handover minimization: minimal within a skill family, poor across stages because Intake, diagnostic, and Validation are usually different queues.
- Overflow trigger: only when the primary queue is empty or the designated owner is off shift.

Policy B, full pooling with transparent urgency dispatch:

- Eligibility: any qualified resource may take any ready task.
- Priority score: smallest predicted slack first, where `slack = SLA target - elapsed so far - expected remaining active time`; ties go to shorter expected remaining processing time.
- Reservation: no hard reservation, but the urgency score naturally prefers P1 work.
- Preemption: one diagnostic preemption allowed when a P1 case has negative predicted slack and no eligible resource is free; do not preempt a task with less than 20% remaining.
- Parallelization: run `ME/ES` in parallel whenever the parallel critical path beats sequential by more than 12 minutes and the current E/S bottleneck signal is below about 0.95.
- Handover minimization: prefer same-resource continuity as a tiebreaker, but only after urgency and expected remaining time.
- Overflow trigger: always on by design.

Policy C, hybrid owner lanes with P1 reservation and controlled overflow:

- Eligibility: owner lanes are Ada/Ben for mechanical, Chen/Emil for E/S, Deepa as bridge, Farah as 08:00-12:00 surge buffer.
- Priority score: owner-lane FIFO by default, but cases are promoted by slack-to-SLA once the oldest wait in a skill family exceeds threshold.
- Reservation: reserve one E/S-capable slot for P1-sensitive work in `08:00-12:00`; reserve one general diagnostic slot whenever any P1 diagnostic backlog exists.
- Preemption: allow one diagnostic preemption only when `P1 slack < 30 minutes`, no reserved slot is free, and the interrupted case still has more than 20% active work remaining.
- Parallelization: `ME/ES` parallel only for P1, or for rare cases where both branches can start within 10 minutes and the saved critical path is above 20 minutes. Otherwise run sequentially on one owner if possible: Ada/Deepa for `ME`, Chen/Emil for `ES`.
- Handover minimization: keep the same owner across Intake-diagnostic-Validation when feasible for simple cases; prefer sequential same-owner `ES` and `ME` for P2/P3 to avoid coordination.
- Overflow trigger: open slow cross-skill overflow only when the oldest skill-family wait exceeds 20 minutes or projected current-block utilization exceeds roughly 0.85.

Why I treat Policy C as distinct rather than “specialization plus exceptions”:

- It uses explicit owner lanes, so it is operationally explainable.
- It still exploits pooling when the queue-state proves that specialization is failing.
- It deliberately uses different rules in normal mode and stress mode instead of pretending one dispatch score is optimal in all scenarios.

Interpretation:

- Full pooling wins small base-case mean-delay contests, but it fails the fairness/fragility objective because it pushes the same few fast cross-skilled people above the “unfair” zone.
- Strict specialization is simple but brittle. It loses most badly whenever bottlenecks migrate across skill families or when one key person is absent.
- Hybrid is not always the best on base mean delay, but it has the lowest regret across `U1-U5` while also keeping base-case individual utilization below the fairness threshold.

# 5. Robust recommendation
Primary recommendation: Policy C, the hybrid owner-lane policy with explicit P1 reservation, selective parallelization, and threshold-based overflow.

Why it dominates on the stated objective:

- It preserves the interpretability and continuity benefits of specialization.
- It captures most of the SLA benefit of pooling for P1 work.
- It reduces base-case individual overload relative to pooling, so it is less fragile and more defensible operationally.
- It degrades more gracefully than the other two policies under `U1`, `U3`, and `U5`, which are the scenarios that really test robustness rather than simple efficiency.

Normal-mode dispatch rules I would deploy:

- Keep owner lanes by default: Ada/Ben on M, Chen/Emil on E/S, Deepa as bridge, Farah as 08:00-12:00 shock absorber.
- Reserve one E/S-capable slot for P1-sensitive work in the morning.
- Run `ES` and `ME` sequentially on one owner for `P2/P3` unless the saved critical path from parallelism is clearly worth the coordination cost.
- Use same-resource continuity across Intake-diagnostic-Validation whenever it avoids a handover and does not consume the only scarce late-shift specialist.

Stress-mode fallback:

- Switch into stress mode when any of the following is true:
- oldest P1 wait exceeds 15 minutes;
- projected `Electrical` or `Software` load ratio for the current/next block exceeds 0.90;
- Chen is absent;
- the Intake outage lasts more than 15 minutes;
- `Software` queued work after 15:00 exceeds about 45 active minutes;
- `Mechanical` queued work after 16:30 exceeds about 35 active minutes.

Stress-mode actions:

- Parallelize only P1 `ME/ES`, and only if both branches can start within 5-10 minutes.
- Freeze nonessential cross-family bouncing. In particular, do not spend Emil on medium-priority electrical work if there is any software backlog heading into 16:00-18:00.
- Protect Deepa’s late window for mechanical spillover and Emil’s late window for software spillover.
- Use Farah on Intake/Validation surge before assigning her to long diagnostics.
- Permit one P1 diagnostic preemption only when the predicted P1 miss is within 20-30 minutes and the interrupted task still has substantial remaining work.

Cases where another policy is preferable:

- If the objective is pure base-case mean delay and management accepts planned unfairness on a few people, full pooling is defensible.
- If the objective is ultra-simple governance with minimal rule change and the organization is willing to accept worse shock performance, strict specialization is defensible.
- If `U5`-class conditions are expected often rather than rarely, none of the three policies is enough; that is a staffing-gap problem, not just a routing problem.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks
- Gaming of estimates: any score using expected remaining time can be gamed if operators manually enter optimistic estimates. Use coarse time classes by case type and complexity flag, not free-text self-estimates.
- P3 starvation: reserved P1 capacity and selective preemption can quietly starve P3 work. Add aging: once P3 wait exceeds 90 minutes, it becomes overflow-eligible even if thresholds are not crossed.
- Skill erosion: a very rigid owner-lane design can let secondary skills decay. Track minimum monthly repetitions by secondary skill and rotate bridge work deliberately.
- Excessive preemption: because the penalty is 10% of remaining active time, repeated or late preemption can erase the benefit. Hard-cap to one interruption per case and audit restart penalty minutes explicitly.
- Coordination overhead: indiscriminate parallel `ME/ES` creates hidden work before Validation and can move the bottleneck into Validation. That is why the recommendation does not “always parallelize.”
- Validation bursts: parallel branches release cases in bunches. Ben/Deepa can become short-lived validation bottlenecks even when their daily average utilization looks safe.
- Fairness and burnout: pooling will look efficient in averages while quietly burning out Chen/Emil/Deepa. A fairness threshold is not cosmetic here; it is a resilience control.
- Human interpretability: operators need visible reason codes such as `P1 reserve`, `owner-lane`, `overflow due to 23 min oldest wait`, or `parallel ES because 31 min critical-path gain`. A black-box score will not be auditable enough.

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design
Deployment indicators:

- SLA attainment by priority and by case type.
- Oldest wait, average wait, and queued active work minutes by skill family and time block.
- Individual utilization, overtime minutes, and fairness gap.
- Handover count per case and cross-team transfer count per case.
- Setup minutes per resource per shift.
- Parallelization rate for `ME` and `ES`.
- Preemption count, victim priority mix, and restart-penalty minutes.
- Validation wait after parallel branches.
- Share of cases with same-resource continuity from Intake through Validation.

Event attributes to log:

- `case:concept:name`
- `concept:name`
- `start_timestamp`
- `time:timestamp`
- `org:resource`
- queue-entry timestamp
- assignment-decision timestamp
- eligible-resource set
- requested skill and actual skill used
- case priority and case type
- planned base service and actual active service
- speed multiplier
- pause/resume flags and remaining work at interruption
- handover minutes and setup minutes
- branch mode (`seq` or `parallel`)
- coordination delay before Validation
- policy version
- scenario/shock flag
- outage flag
- SLA clock target and slack at assignment

PM4Py analyses that matter:

- `pm4py.format_dataframe` to normalize the interval log.
- `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` to get case-level `@@service_time`, `@@sojourn_time`, and `@@waiting_time`.
- `pm4py.get_service_time` to track activity-level service drift, especially rising `Software` or `Electrical+Software(seq)` durations.
- `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` to see whether the hybrid policy actually reduces unnecessary handovers compared with pooling.

Important labeling point:

- PM4Py’s `@@service_time` and `@@waiting_time` are case-level outputs once inserted at the case level. They are not stage-level queue metrics by themselves.
- For queue-specific or stage-specific waiting, log an explicit queue-entry timestamp and compute `start_timestamp - queue_entry_timestamp` by stage after filtering the event log to the relevant activity set.

How to detect drift and bottleneck migration:

- Compare activity service times by week and by scenario flag to identify service-time inflation.
- Compare handover network density and cross-team edge weights by policy version to quantify coordination burden.
- Compare stage-specific waiting by block to see bottleneck migration from `Software 16:00-18:00` in base to `Electrical/Software 08:00-12:00` in `U1/U3/U5`.
- Compare the proportion of `ME/ES` handled in parallel versus sequential and correlate it with Validation waiting and P1 attainment.

def weighted_choice(options, rng):
    total = sum(weight for _, weight in options)
    x = rng.random() * total
    upto = 0.0
    for value, weight in options:
        upto += weight
        if x <= upto:
            return value
    return options[-1][0]

        if br:
            break_start = day * 1440 + br[0]
            break_end = day * 1440 + br[1]
            if break_start <= minute < break_end:
                minute = break_end
        if shift_start <= minute < shift_end:
            return minute
        day += 1
        minute = day * 1440 + resource['shift'][0]


def capacity_minutes(skill, start, end, resources):
    total = 0.0
    for resource in resources.values():
        if skill not in resource['skills']:
            continue
        cur = start
        while cur < end:
            active_start = next_available(cur, resource)
            if active_start >= end:
                break
            day = math.floor(active_start / 1440)
            seg_end = min(day * 1440 + resource['shift'][1] + 30, end)
            br = resource['break']
            if br:
                break_start = day * 1440 + br[0]
                break_end = day * 1440 + br[1]
                if active_start < break_start < seg_end:
                    seg_end = break_start
                elif break_start <= active_start < break_end:
                    cur = break_end
                    continue
            total += max(0.0, seg_end - active_start) / resource['skills'][skill]
            cur = seg_end + 1e-6
    return total


def apply_scenario(name):
    blocks = [dict(row) for row in DEMAND]
    resources = {k: {'skills': dict(v['skills']), 'shift': v['shift'], 'break': v['break']} for k, v in RESOURCES.items()}
    service = {k: dict(v) for k, v in SERVICE.items()}
    outage = None
    if name in {'U1', 'U5'}:
        for block in blocks[:2]:
            block['expected_arrivals'] *= 1.7
            p1 = block['P1'] + 8
            rem = 100 - p1
            old = block['P2'] + block['P3']
            block['P1'] = p1
            block['P2'] = block['P2'] / old * rem
            block['P3'] = block['P3'] / old * rem
    if name == 'U2':
        for block in blocks[2:4]:
            mix = {k: block[k] for k in ['M', 'E', 'S', 'ME', 'ES']}
            mix['E'] *= 2
            mix['ES'] *= 2
            total = sum(mix.values())
            for k, v in mix.items():
                block[k] = v / total * 100
    if name in {'U3', 'U5'}:
        resources.pop('Chen')
    if name == 'U4':
        for activity in ['Mechanical', 'Electrical', 'Software']:
            service[activity]['tail_p'] = min(1.0, service[activity]['tail_p'] * 2)
    if name == 'U5':
        outage = (9 * 60 + 30, 10 * 60)
    return blocks, resources, service, outage


def util_table(policy_name, blocks, resources, service):
    params = POLICIES[policy_name]
    out = {}
    for block in blocks:
        arrivals = block['expected_arrivals']
        raw_load = {
            'Intake': arrivals * expected_service('Intake', service),
            'Mechanical': arrivals * (block['M'] + block['ME']) / 100 * expected_service('Mechanical', service),
            'Electrical': arrivals * (block['E'] + block['ME'] + block['ES']) / 100 * expected_service('Electrical', service),
            'Software': arrivals * (block['S'] + block['ES']) / 100 * expected_service('Software', service),
            'Validation': arrivals * expected_service('Validation', service),
        }
        block_util = {}
        for skill, load in raw_load.items():
            cap = capacity_minutes(skill, block['start'], block['end'], resources)
            haircut = params['scarce_haircut'] if skill in {'Electrical', 'Software'} else params['cap_haircut']
            block_util[skill] = load / (cap * haircut) if cap else float('inf')
        out[block['block']] = block_util
    return out


def wait_proxy(rho, priority, policy_name):
    if rho <= 0.45:
        wait = 4
    elif rho <= 0.65:
        wait = 4 + (rho - 0.45) / 0.20 * 10
    elif rho <= 0.80:
        wait = 14 + (rho - 0.65) / 0.15 * 18
    elif rho <= 0.95:
        wait = 32 + (rho - 0.80) / 0.15 * 35
    elif rho <= 1.10:
        wait = 67 + (rho - 0.95) / 0.15 * 75
    else:
        wait = 142 + (rho - 1.10) * 160
    if priority == 'P1':
        wait *= POLICIES[policy_name]['p1_wait_factor']
    elif priority == 'P3':
        wait *= 1.15
    return wait


def choose_parallel(case_type, priority, policy_name, service_draws, block_util):
    if case_type not in {'ME', 'ES'}:
        return False
    if case_type == 'ME':
        seq = service_draws['Mechanical'] + service_draws['Electrical']
        par = max(service_draws['Mechanical'], service_draws['Electrical']) + 8
        scarce_gate = max(block_util['Mechanical'], block_util['Electrical'])
    else:
        seq = service_draws['Electrical'] + service_draws['Software']
        par = max(service_draws['Electrical'], service_draws['Software']) + 8
        scarce_gate = max(block_util['Electrical'], block_util['Software'])
    save = seq - par
    rule = POLICIES[policy_name]['parallel_rule']
    if rule == 'P1P2':
        return priority in {'P1', 'P2'} and save > 15
    if rule == 'save12':
        return save > 12 and scarce_gate < 0.95
    return priority == 'P1' and save > 20 and scarce_gate < 0.90


def generate_cases(blocks, service, outage, rng):
    cases = []
    case_id = 1
    for block in blocks:
        n = math.floor(block['expected_arrivals']) + (1 if rng.random() < (block['expected_arrivals'] % 1) else 0)
        for _ in range(n):
            arrival = rng.uniform(block['start'], block['end'])
            if outage and outage[0] <= arrival < outage[1]:
                arrival = outage[1]
            priority = weighted_choice([('P1', block['P1']), ('P2', block['P2']), ('P3', block['P3'])], rng)
            case_type = weighted_choice([('M', block['M']), ('E', block['E']), ('S', block['S']), ('ME', block['ME']), ('ES', block['ES'])], rng)
            draws = {activity: sample_service(activity, service, rng) for activity in SERVICE}
            cases.append({'case_id': f'C{case_id:04d}', 'arrival': arrival, 'block': block['block'], 'priority': priority, 'type': case_type, 'draws': draws})
            case_id += 1
    return cases


def approximate_metrics(policy_name, scenario_name, replications=200, seed=7):
    blocks, resources, service, outage = apply_scenario(scenario_name)
    params = POLICIES[policy_name]
    block_utils = util_table(policy_name, blocks, resources, service)
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    delays = []
    by_priority = {'P1': [], 'P2': [], 'P3': []}
    handover_setup = []
    for _ in range(replications):
        for case in generate_cases(blocks, service, outage, rng):
            priority = case['priority']
            case_type = case['type']
            draws = case['draws']
            arrival = case['arrival']
            util = block_utils[case['block']]
            parallel = choose_parallel(case_type, priority, policy_name, draws, util)
            if case_type == 'M':
                critical = draws['Mechanical']
                diag_rho = util['Mechanical']
                handover = params['handover_simple']
            elif case_type == 'E':
                critical = draws['Electrical']
                diag_rho = util['Electrical']
                handover = params['handover_simple']
            elif case_type == 'S':
                critical = draws['Software']
                diag_rho = util['Software']
                handover = params['handover_simple']
            elif case_type == 'ME':
                if parallel:
                    critical = max(draws['Mechanical'], draws['Electrical']) + 8
                    diag_rho = max(util['Mechanical'], util['Electrical'])
                    handover = params['handover_parallel']
                else:
                    critical = draws['Mechanical'] + draws['Electrical']
                    diag_rho = (util['Mechanical'] + util['Electrical']) / 2
                    handover = params['handover_seq']
            else:
                if parallel:
                    critical = max(draws['Electrical'], draws['Software']) + 8
                    diag_rho = max(util['Electrical'], util['Software'])
                    handover = params['handover_parallel']
                else:
                    critical = draws['Electrical'] + draws['Software']
                    diag_rho = max((util['Electrical'] + util['Software']) / 2, util['Software'])
                    handover = params['handover_seq']

            wait = (
                wait_proxy(util['Intake'], priority, policy_name)
                + wait_proxy(diag_rho, priority, policy_name)
                + wait_proxy(util['Validation'], priority, policy_name)
            )

            # Explicit preemption assumption:
            # a P1 case may pull forward one diagnostic slot if slack is tight and the policy permits it.
            if priority == 'P1' and params['p1_preempt_credit'] and diag_rho > 0.90:
                wait = max(0.0, wait - params['p1_preempt_credit'])

            # Explicit structural late-coverage assumptions:
            if case_type in {'M', 'ME'} and arrival >= 18 * 60:
                wait += (24 * 60 + 8 * 60 - arrival)  # next mechanical coverage next day at 08:00
            elif case_type in {'M', 'ME'} and 16 * 60 <= arrival < 18 * 60 and (arrival + draws['Intake'] + critical + handover + 20) > 18 * 60 + 30:
                wait += (24 * 60 + 8 * 60 - max(arrival, 18 * 60 + 30))

            # Explicit Chen-absence morning software fragility assumption:
            if case_type in {'S', 'ES'} and scenario_name in {'U3', 'U5'} and case['block'] in {'08:00-10:00', '10:00-12:00'} and diag_rho > 0.95:
                wait += 12

            total = draws['Intake'] + critical + draws['Validation'] + params['setup'] + handover + wait
            delays.append(total)
            by_priority[priority].append(total)
            handover_setup.append(handover + params['setup'])

    max_skill_rho = max(max(skill_map.values()) for skill_map in block_utils.values())
    max_res_util_est = min(1.12, 0.55 + 0.25 * max_skill_rho + params['resource_bias'])
    fairness_gap_est = min(0.70, params['fairness_gap'] + max(0.0, max_skill_rho - 1.0) * 0.12)

    return {
        'mean_delay': statistics.mean(delays),
        'p90_delay': sorted(delays)[int(0.90 * len(delays)) - 1],
        'P1_SLA': 100 * sum(d <= TARGETS['P1'] for d in by_priority['P1']) / max(1, len(by_priority['P1'])),
        'P2_SLA': 100 * sum(d <= TARGETS['P2'] for d in by_priority['P2']) / max(1, len(by_priority['P2'])),
        'max_resource_utilization_est': 100 * max_res_util_est,
        'utilization_fairness_gap_est': 100 * fairness_gap_est,
        'handover_setup_burden_est': statistics.mean(handover_setup),
        'block_utils': block_utils,
    }


def choose_resource_for_activity(activity, available_resources):
    for name in OWNER_MAP[activity]:
        if name in available_resources and activity in available_resources[name]['skills']:
            return name
    for name, row in available_resources.items():
        if activity in row['skills']:
            return name
    raise ValueError(f'No resource for {activity}')

# 9. Limits and validation plan
What cannot be concluded from the prompt alone:

- Exact SLA attainment depends on true arrival randomness, not just expected arrivals.
- Priority may correlate with complexity and case type; the prompt does not tell us whether P1 work is also systematically longer.
- We do not know whether real staff can pause/resume across breaks cleanly, or whether some tasks are effectively non-preemptive.
- We do not know the real cost of keeping the same owner across stages versus giving work to the nominally fastest specialist.
- A one-day capacity view understates multi-day backlog effects, especially for late mechanical spillovers.

Pilot / backtest design:

- Step 1. Shadow mode for two weeks: compute all three policy decisions on historical or replayed arrivals, but do not change live routing yet.
- Step 2. Live pilot only on `08:00-16:00` and only for `P1`, `ME`, and `ES` cases, because that is where the policy differences matter most and where the safety case is strongest.
- Step 3. Keep a human override for any predicted P1 miss, and disallow live preemption during the first week of the pilot.
- Step 4. Promote from shadow to live overflow only after showing that owner-lane hybrid reduces handovers without pushing any individual above 95% realized utilization.

Pilot stop conditions:

- P1 attainment drops below 90% for two consecutive days.
- Any one person exceeds 95% realized utilization for two consecutive 2-hour blocks.
- Preemption count or restart-penalty minutes exceed the agreed safety threshold.
- Validation waiting rises materially after enabling parallel `ME/ES`.

Best validation experiment:

- A stepped-wedge rollout by shift block is better than a one-shot full deployment.
- Compare current practice versus hybrid in matched blocks, stratified by case type and priority.
- Keep `U3/U5`-style contingency rules configured from day one, because the robust policy is valuable precisely when the day is abnormal.
