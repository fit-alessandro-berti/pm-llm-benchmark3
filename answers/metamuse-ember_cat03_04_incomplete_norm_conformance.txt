## 1. Conformance semantics

A deviation is classified only after three questions are answered: does the norm apply to this object, event, and time window; what is its status; and can the graph establish a breach once abstraction, censoring, and data provenance are considered?

- **Forbidden behavior:** an explicitly forbidden relation occurs for the same object with the required temporal or lifecycle relationship. Example: N3, T6, and E10. If object identity or the producing join is wrong, this becomes a data-quality issue rather than proven real-world forbidden behavior, but it cannot be downgraded merely because it is rare.
- **Missing mandatory behavior:** a mandatory relation is within scope, no recognized exception applies, and the expected occurrence is absent. This requires scrutiny of scope and abstraction. An eventually-required relation is not proven missing merely because a rarely occurring intermediate event breaks a directly-follows edge.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior:** the relation is observed but is unspecified or absent from the norm. Under an open-world interpretation, this is not a violation. It may be legitimate, innovative, or risky, but the norm gives no basis for calling it noncompliant.
- **Typical-pattern absence:** a typical relation is absent. Typical means common and desirable, not obligatory. Absence invites explanation—such as write-off, credit terms, or an intermediate process step—but is not itself a breach.
- **Likely data-quality error:** the pattern is better explained by identifier reuse, a faulty join, missing labels, dropped links, or source-system mismatch than by business behavior. The sensor–invoice link is the clearest candidate.
- **Abstraction mismatch:** the summary’s representation is stricter or coarser than the norm. The principal case is translating “eventually enter validation” in N1 into a directly-follows requirement.
- **Insufficient evidence:** the denominator, event subtype, in-flight status, object identity, or timing is unknown. Classification should be deferred rather than forced.

The norm is **closed-world only for explicit forbidden relations and for mandatory relations within their stated scopes**. It is otherwise open-world. Therefore, absence from a normative edge set does not imply prohibition, and presence in the observed graph does not automatically imply business significance. Conversely, open-world status does not excuse an explicit forbidden occurrence or a genuinely missing mandatory link.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An OC-DFG establishes directly-follows sequences for each object type and, if transitive closure is computed, eventual reachability. It can show that the same Order object has Cancel Order before Dispatch Package, which is exactly the evidence needed for N3. It can also reveal branching, repeated transitions, and lifecycle variants.

It cannot establish cross-object synchronization, the event-object links responsible for a transition, causality, or whether an unrecorded event intervened. Its counts are transition counts, not necessarily percentages of objects. Thus, Validate Order -> Issue Invoice at 72 and Validate Order -> Close Order at 83 cannot simply be added or treated as complementary outcomes: validation may repeat, orders may branch, and the denominator for direct closure is not given.

The danger of converting “eventually” into “directly follows” is central. N1 says every newly created standard Order should eventually enter validation, while the summary approximates this with a directly-follows expectation. If a review event occurs between creation and validation, the direct edge disappears even though the business rule is satisfied. The context explicitly allows this possibility for the two absent direct relations. The same caution applies, with less explicit textual support, to N4 and N2. A sound OC-DFG check should therefore evaluate reachability and allowed intermediates separately from adjacency.

### OTG

An OTG establishes lifecycle-level object relations: interaction, co-birth, co-death, and related associations. It is useful for deciding whether two objects ever interact or are born or die together. The single Order–Payment co-birth edge is therefore highly relevant to T6, whereas the seven Package–Delivery co-death edges are merely unregulated because the norm says nothing about co-death.

An OTG cannot establish sequence, timing, causation, or which event produced the relation. Most importantly, aggregate presence can mask per-event incompleteness. Package–Delivery interaction has weight 200, apparently satisfying T2, but Dispatch Package -> Delivery is present for only 198 dispatch events under E6. The lifecycle-level relation may arise through another event or through the other 198 dispatches. Conversely, the aggregated Order–Item weight of 1,450 does not cure the two missing Add Item -> Order ET–OT links. OTG and ET–OT conformance must therefore be reported at their own abstractions.

### ET–OT

ET–OT relations are the closest representation of per-event mandatory requirements such as E1–E8. Count mismatches are consequently strong log-level evidence: 1,450 Add Item–Item links but only 1,448 Add Item–Order links indicate two events without the mandatory Order link, assuming the event population is the same. Similarly, Record Payment–Payment 93 versus Record Payment–Invoice 92 suggests one payment event missing its Invoice link.

ET–OT cannot establish lifecycle order, object-object interaction, business outcome, or the meaning of event attributes. A carrier reference stored as an attribute is not an ET–OT Delivery link, even if a real delivery exists. An unlabelled unapplied-cash event is not indistinguishable from an applied payment with a missing link. Nor can ET–OT alone prove that Cancel Order and Dispatch Package concern the same Order; that identity and sequence come from the OC-DFG. Finally, a Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice ET–OT edge proves that the log contains the link, not that a real sensor was linked to a real invoice; the suspected identifier join changes the interpretation.

## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch

The following preserves PM4Py’s raw diagnostic results and adds a separate semantic layer. The lifecycle status and scope metadata are maintained outside the bare graph structures because PM4Py graphs alone do not encode mandatory, typical, forbidden, or unspecified meaning.

```python
import pm4py

# Metadata external to the graph structures.
NORM_STATUS = {
    ("Order", "Create Order", "Validate Order"): "mandatory_eventual",
    ("Order", "Cancel Order", "Dispatch Package"): "forbidden",
    ("Order", "Reopen Order", "Close Order"): "unspecified",
    ("Package", "Pack Package", "Dispatch Package"): "mandatory",
    ("Invoice", "Issue Invoice", "Record Payment"): "typical",
}
FORBIDDEN_OTG = {("Order", "object_cobirth", "Payment")}
FORBIDDEN_ETOT = {("Sensor Heartbeat", "Invoice")}
MANDATORY_ETOT = {
    ("Add Item", "Order"),
    ("Dispatch Package", "Delivery"),
    ("Record Payment", "Invoice"),
}
# Scope predicates, exceptions, timestamps, provenance, and identity
# checks must also be maintained outside these bare structures.

def semantic_layer(raw_result, status_index):
    """Return an interpretation without mutating or replacing raw output."""
    return {
        "raw_result": raw_result,
        "status_index": status_index,
        "note": "Interpretation requires scope, timing, identity, "
                "exception, and provenance checks.",
    }

observed_ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
observed_otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
observed_etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# Raw PM4Py diagnostics are retained unchanged.
raw_ocdfg = pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(ocel, norm_ocdfg)
raw_otg = pm4py.conformance_otg(ocel, norm_otg)
raw_etot = pm4py.conformance_etot(ocel, norm_etot)

interpreted = {
    "ocdfg": semantic_layer(raw_ocdfg, NORM_STATUS),
    "otg": semantic_layer(raw_otg, FORBIDDEN_OTG),
    "etot": semantic_layer(raw_etot, MANDATORY_ETOT | FORBIDDEN_ETOT),
}
```

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **Eventuality versus direct succession.** N1 uses “eventually,” but the summary applies directly-follows expectations. Leaving this unresolved creates false missing-mandatory findings. Clarify that validation is mandatory eventually, with permitted intermediate event types explicitly unspecified.

2. **Scope and snapshot censoring.** “Standard Order,” “newly created,” and “externally shipped Package” need operational predicates. Without them, in-flight orders and packages can be misclassified. Define mandatory scope using order type, shipment intent, creation time, and extraction time.

3. **Unapplied cash.** E8 depends on a subtype that is absent from the snapshot. Clarify that an event explicitly typed as unapplied cash is an explicitly unspecified Invoice-link case; otherwise, the Invoice link is mandatory. Require a machine-readable subtype rather than relying on staff recollection.

4. **Customer, RiskCase, and Manual Compliance Review.** T4, T5, and E9 leave important relationships to another policy, while Manual Compliance Review -> Customer is not represented. This creates recurring false alarms. Mark these relations explicitly unspecified in this norm, or move them to typical or mandatory status if the external policy is incorporated.

5. **Co-birth and co-death.** T6 forbids Order–Payment co-birth, but co-death is silent. The seven Package–Delivery co-death edges cannot be judged by analogy. Retain co-birth as forbidden and classify co-death as explicitly unspecified unless shared lifecycle termination is governed elsewhere.

6. **Invoice and validation branches.** Write-offs, credit notes, open terms, Issue Invoice after validation, and direct closure lack a complete branch model. Clarify that payment after issue is typical, Write Off is an explicitly unspecified legitimate successor, and Validate -> Issue Invoice is either typical or explicitly unspecified.

7. **Frequencies and tolerances.** The norm supplies no minimum rates or tolerance bands, so 98/100, 92/110, and 198/200 cannot be compared as equivalent deviations. Add typical expected ranges or monitoring thresholds without converting them into hard mandatory counts.

8. **Identifier and attribute linkage.** The norm does not state that a carrier attribute can substitute for a Delivery object or prohibit cross-domain numeric identifier reuse. Make namespaced identifiers and valid object linkage mandatory for conformance counting; treat unmapped attributes as data-quality evidence, not automatic business compliance.
