**100-case weekly rollout.** Reserving C1 capacity for 100 high-risk cases would demand ~200–300h of preliminary C1 work, far exceeding C1's weekly availability (~40–50h). Compliance would become a hard bottleneck; urgent non-target cases would be displaced, and some might miss regulatory deadlines. The K-104 intervention is the least transportable: it is locally beneficial but systemically harmful because it locks the scarcest single-resource pool.

Substituting D2 for D1 across 100 standard-handwriting cases would offload D1 (beneficial for complex corporate cases) but load D2. Because D2's service time is longer and clarification probability is higher, the expected rework load grows nonlinearly: if 20–30% of cases trigger a rework loop, the net Assessment demand rises, and the bottleneck migrates from D1 to D2 + A2. Weekend and external-response effects amplify this: rework requests issued late Friday may not get responses until Monday, converting a 4h delay into a 60h+ delay.

Suppressing the second rework loop (K-337) at scale would substantially reduce DocumentCheck and Assessment demand — potentially the largest throughput gain of the four. But the quality cost is deferred and hard to observe: appeals, reversals, and loss exposure may surface weeks later. The intervention is a risk transfer, not a cost elimination. Pilot measurement must track downstream outcomes, not just cycle time.

Parallelizing verification (K-411) at scale is capacity-neutral but shifts arrival patterns into the Assessment queue earlier and more clustered. If 100 cases arrive Tuesday, parallelization compresses their verification completion into a narrower window, spiking A2 demand Tuesday evening. Real-time timestamping is essential: batch-entered timestamps would create false concurrency and make the workaround unauditable. Weekend effects are limited because this case type is digital and standard, but the timestamp/logging discipline must be enforced programmatically.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-411 (parallelize verification). It is capacity-neutral, locally beneficial under nearly all queue assumptions, reversible, and carries bounded, observable risks (A2 congestion, logging audit). Its main limitation — that it does not create net capacity — is also its safety: it cannot starve other cases of a scarce resource.

The most dangerous intervention despite potential speed is K-337 (suppress the second rework loop). It offers the largest cycle-time gain (~3 days) but is a decision-policy change, not a timing optimization. The missing confirmation may be material; suppressing it transfers risk to appeals, reversals, and loss exposure that surface long after the decision. It is also the least reversible: once a conditional or negative decision is issued, undoing it cleanly is hard.

The local counterfactual least transportable to a system-wide policy is K-104. Locally, reserving a C1 slot and doing preliminary compliance work saves a few hours on one high-risk case. At 100 cases, the reservation demand exceeds C1's weekly capacity by a factor of 4–6, displacing urgent compliance cases and potentially creating regulatory exposure. The single-resource constraint on C1 makes the intervention non-scalable by construction.

The most valuable additional data are: (a) C1 actual utilization and reservation no-show rates, to test whether preliminary work genuinely shortens final review; (b) D2 clarification rates by document type, especially handwritten standard; (c) real-time timestamp accuracy vs system-log gaps; and (d) downstream appeal/reversal rates for conditionally approved high-risk cases. Without these, locally favorable counterfactuals will be mistaken for system-wide improvements.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. S1's advisory work can overlap with other resource activities without consuming their capacity. — **resource**
2. Preliminary C1 reading genuinely shortens the final Compliance Review by 1–3h rather than requiring fresh re-reading. — **quality**
3. D2's service time on standard handwritten documents falls in the 4–8h range and does not systematically exceed it. — **temporal**
4. The case lock in K-411 was a legacy artifact, not a data-integrity safeguard. — **structural**
5. The unresolved discrepancy in K-337 is not strictly material to the final decision (i.e., conditional approval is defensible). — **quality**
6. Queue snapshots are unbiased forecasts; actual start times may deviate but not systematically by more than ~30%. — **temporal**
7. Applying an intervention to 100 similar cases does not change the arrival distribution or mix of case types. — **interference**
8. Four-eyes separation is preserved because A1 and C1 are distinct individuals in all relevant cases. — **behavioral**
