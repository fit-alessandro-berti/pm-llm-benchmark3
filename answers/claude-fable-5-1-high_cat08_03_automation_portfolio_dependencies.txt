# Automation portfolio for small-business loan origination

Working assumptions used throughout: ratings are qualitative; where numbers are quoted they are derived directly from the table and DFG (frequency x service time for labor, frequency x median wait for queue burden, loop ratios from directly-follows counts). Loop and wait figures are indicative, not ROI claims.

Three facts from the process-mining evidence frame every judgment below:

- **Where the labor really sits.** Register application is the highest-frequency activity but represents roughly 11,880 x 8 min = ~1,600 hours of work per year, in a 12-FTE back-office pool that runs below 65% utilization after 15:00. Financial document review, normalization and credit analysis together represent roughly 6,900 + 6,500 + 16,300 = ~30,000 hours on a 9-FTE analyst pool. Resolve entity mismatch alone consumes ~1,600 hours of a 3-FTE specialist pool (about a third of it), with a 38% rework rate and dependency on two senior people.
- **Where the waiting really sits.** Median wait before Register application is 1.5h. Median waits before Financial document review (18h), Normalize financial statements (20h), Credit analysis (28h), Committee packaging (26h), Contract preparation (22h), Wait for external valuation (58h), Resolve entity mismatch (31h, on the critical path in 82% of its cases) and Customer signing (44h) are one to two orders of magnitude larger. Registration is a conveyor, not a constraint.
- **Where the loops are.** Financial document review -> Request missing documents (3,300 of 9,200 reviews, ~36%) with 2,800 flowing straight back (85% loop-back, 52% repeated requests); KYC check -> Resolve entity mismatch (1,750, ~15% of KYC checks) with 1,160 looping back to KYC; Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue (650, 8.5%; 14% for Credit-line renewal) with 510 looping back; Customer signing 21% reminder loop; provisional signing before KYC clearance in 6.5% of cases.

Automating the front of a queue that feeds constrained analysts and specialists increases work-in-progress in front of them; it does not shorten the case. The portfolio must therefore be built around the constraints, the synchronization points and the controls, and the high-frequency win should be sequenced, not skipped.

## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

**Upstream data quality dependencies**

- A6 depends on A5. Extraction fails on low-quality or incomplete documents; A5's checklists reduce the 52% repeated requests and produce standardized uploads that A6 can parse. Conversely A5 without A6 accelerates clean inflow into a 9-FTE analyst pool that already carries ~30,000 hours of service work; the resource note says this explicitly.
- A8 depends on A4, A6 and A7. Committee packaging waits on KYC status, normalized statements, valuation and credit notes; automating assembly before these are reliable produces inconsistent packs faster.
- A9 depends on structured KYC clearance status (A3/A4 quality) and on Committee decision data being captured as fields, not prose. The variant note "Resolve entity mismatch -> Contract preparation: 380" shows contracts being prepared straight out of mismatch resolution, so KYC status must be machine-readable at that point.
- A2 depends on corrected classification history; its 8% misroute rate is itself an upstream data-quality risk for every downstream control path.
- A1 depends on nothing technically but on everything organizationally: it raises arrival rate into Classify -> KYC (7,250) and Classify -> FDR (3,940) without adding capacity there.

**Legal / compliance controls**

- KYC clearance must precede Customer signing. Any automation that speeds contract issuance or signing (A9, A10, A11) must carry a hard gate, otherwise it turns the 6.5% provisional-signing pattern into a systematic violation. A9 is where the gate belongs because it generates the document that goes to signature.
- Contract terms must match the Committee decision. A9's rule checks address the 8.5% / 14% remediation loop; A12 manages the residue.
- Four-eyes in High-exposure SME cases (5.8% violations at month end). This is a role-separation rule best enforced in workflow at Committee packaging (A8, or a configuration rule if A8 is deferred). A2 misclassification can silently bypass it by labelling a High-exposure case as SME, so A2 must require human confirmation when exposure class changes.
- Auditable trail of suggestion, override and final decision applies to A2, A3, A4, A6, A9: every module must write these three events with actor and timestamp; this is an integration requirement to be designed once and reused.

**Human escalation capacity**

- A3 escalates KYC hits to 3 compliance specialists who already spend about a third of their capacity on entity mismatches with 38% rework, and the handover network concentrates on two seniors. A3 before A4 would break the specialist pool; A4 before A3 creates the capacity A3 needs.
- A6 shifts analysts from data entry to reviewing extraction output; that review capacity must be planned, and High-exposure cases (judgment-heavy) should remain fully human.
- A5 shifts document chasing from analysts and RMs to the portal and back office. Back office has slack after 15:00, so freed capacity from any later A1 rollout has a natural destination in exception handling for A5 and A2.
- A9 shifts legal ops from drafting to exception review; month-end peaks remain because signing and activation cluster there.

**External valuation dependency**

- The DFG shows Credit analysis -> Collateral valuation request (2,400) and Wait -> Committee packaging (1,980). Valuation currently starts only after credit analysis and then imposes a 58h median wait (19% late) before packaging. The main lever is sequencing: raise the request at classification for Equipment finance (71% need it) and flagged Working-capital cases (18%), so the external wait runs in parallel with the 18h + 20h + 28h internal waits. A7 makes this operational (triage, routing, chasing); without re-sequencing A7 only trims variability.

**Core-banking integration constraints**

- Deep integrations: A1 (origination master data), A3 (screening systems), A6 (document management plus credit-model input), A9 (decision data, KYC status, contract templates in core), A11 (activation in core). Light or peripheral: A2 (advisory recommender), A4 (specialist tool with registry look-ups), A5 (portal), A7 (partner routing), A10 (e-sign vendor), A12 (case workbench), A8 (document assembly from existing systems, medium).
- Only two deep integrations fit in months 0-6. Choosing A1 as one of them would spend a scarce slot on a non-constraint and leave either the analyst bottleneck (A6) or the control layer (A9) unaddressed until the second half of the year.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

**Portfolio Q: Quick-win (A1, A2, A10, A9)**

Optimizes visible labor savings and headcount narrative: the two highest-frequency activities plus the largest single queue (signing) and one control module (A9) to satisfy compliance. Deep slots: A1 and A9. Sacrifices: does nothing for the FDR missing-document loop, statement normalization, entity mismatch or external valuation. It accelerates arrivals into the analyst and specialist pools without raising their capacity, so the 18h/20h/28h/31h waits grow. A10 without A4 leaves the KYC clearance queue as the real gate to signing.

**Portfolio B: Bottleneck / synchronization (A5, A6, A4, A7)**

Optimizes end-to-end cycle time by attacking the analyst constraint (A6), the largest rework loop (A5), the scarcest pool and critical-path blocker (A4) and the longest external wait (A7, with re-sequencing). Deep slot: A6 only; A5, A4, A7 are light-to-medium. Control credential: A4 strengthens KYC clearance quality with an evidence trail and reduces the pressure that produces provisional signing, but compliance may consider this too indirect. Sacrifices: little visible labor saving in the back office, no direct fix for the contract remediation loop or four-eyes violations, no high-frequency headline for management.

**Portfolio C: Compliance-first (A3, A9, A12, A4)**

Optimizes control violations: KYC decisions with audit trail (A3), KYC-before-signing and decision-consistency gates (A9), a managed exception queue (A12), and specialist capacity for the escalations A3 creates (A4). Deep slots: A3 and A9. Sacrifices: no relief for analysts or the document loop, so cycle time on the FDR -> Normalize -> Credit analysis path is unchanged and may worsen as KYC clears faster and the KYC -> Credit analysis join (4,200) arrives sooner. A3's false-positive escalations are a real load risk even with A4.

**Portfolio R (recommended): Constraint-and-control (A5, A6, A9, A4)**

Optimizes the critical path and the control layer at the same time: A6 and A5 unblock the analyst constraint and its main rework loop; A9 is the compliance-mandated control module and fixes the 8.5% / 14% contract loop; A4 relieves the specialist pool and the 31h critical-path blocker. Deep slots: A6 and A9 (A5 and A4 are light). Sacrifices: A7 and A10 are deferred to year 2 (the collateral re-sequencing and an e-sign adoption drive can still be done in year 1 as process changes without a module); A1 is deferred deliberately; no automated four-eyes enforcement beyond a workflow configuration rule.

## 4. Expected process-level effects

**Portfolio Q (A1, A2, A10, A9)**

- Cycle time: modest reduction at the end (Customer signing 44h partly reduced by A10 reminders; Contract preparation 22h partly reduced by A9) and negligible at the start (1.5h + 2.0h before Register and Classify). For cases on the FDR or entity-mismatch paths, no change or a slight worsening.
- Waiting time: waits shift, not disappear. Faster Register -> Classify -> KYC/FDR (11,680 / 7,250 / 3,940) raises queue length before Financial document review (18h) and KYC (4h), and through the joins before Credit analysis (28h).
- Rework: contract remediation loop (650 -> 510) reduced by A9's checks; missing-document loop (3,300 / 2,800) and entity-mismatch loop (1,750 / 1,160) untouched; A2 misclassification (8%) may fall with human confirmation or rise in impact if auto-accepted.
- Handovers: fewer back-office touches; RM-to-customer signing touches fall; handovers into specialists and analysts unchanged in count and heavier in volume.
- Resource demand: back office freed (~1,600 h + part of ~2,400 h) in a pool that already has slack; analysts and compliance specialists see higher arrival rates with the same capacity; legal ops relieved but month-end peak persists.
- Control violations: A9 gates reduce provisional signing (6.5%) and contract-decision mismatches, provided KYC status is reliable; A10 without a KYC gate would increase provisional signing; four-eyes (5.8%) unchanged.

**Portfolio B (A5, A6, A4, A7)**

- Cycle time: largest reduction of the three. The FDR -> Request -> FDR loop shrinks (each avoided loop removes roughly 8h + customer response + 18h re-queue); Normalize wait (20h) and analyst service (75 min) fall; entity-mismatch cases lose most of the 31h critical-path wait; Equipment-finance cases lose most of the 58h external wait through parallelization.
- Waiting time: reduced at the exact points where the process waits longest (18h, 20h, 31h, 58h); Credit analysis wait (28h) falls as analysts recover hours from normalization and re-reviews.
- Rework: missing-document repeat requests (52%) and loop-back (85%) fall; entity-mismatch rework (38%) falls with assisted matching; late valuations (19%) fall with routing and chasing. Contract loop unchanged.
- Handovers: fewer analyst <-> back office <-> customer handovers for documents; fewer KYC ops -> senior specialist handovers; fewer collateral-team -> valuer chasers. Committee packaging receives inputs earlier and more consistently.
- Resource demand: analysts shift from normalization to review and to Credit analysis; specialists recover roughly a third of their capacity and the dependency on two seniors weakens; back office does more first-line document follow-up (using its slack); RMs must adopt structured instructions.
- Control violations: indirect improvement. Faster and cleaner KYC clearance removes the operational pressure behind provisional signing; no hard gate is added, so the 6.5% pattern is reduced but not eliminated; four-eyes and contract-decision mismatch unchanged.

**Portfolio C (A3, A9, A12, A4)**

- Cycle time: moderate improvement on the KYC path (4h before KYC, 31h mismatch) and contract path (22h, remediation loops); no improvement on the document / normalization / credit-analysis path, which carries the largest waits.
- Waiting time: KYC and mismatch waits fall; the KYC -> Credit analysis join (4,200) arrives sooner, lengthening the Credit analysis queue (28h) while FDR/Normalize remain unchanged.
- Rework: entity-mismatch rework falls (A4); contract remediation loop falls (A9) and its residue is managed (A12); missing-document loop unchanged.
- Handovers: KYC ops -> specialist handovers become structured escalations with a trail; legal ops <-> compliance remediation handovers are reduced and tracked; document handovers unchanged.
- Resource demand: specialists face A3 false positives offset by A4 efficiency; the net is uncertain and must be monitored; legal ops relieved; analysts unchanged or slightly worse; back office unchanged.
- Control violations: strongest reduction: provisional signing and contract mismatch gated by A9; KYC decisions fully auditable; A12 gives a measured exception process; four-eyes still needs a workflow rule.

**Portfolio R (A5, A6, A9, A4)**

- Cycle time: near Portfolio B's improvement on the document / normalization / credit-analysis path and on the mismatch path, plus Portfolio C's improvement on the contract path. Equipment-finance cases keep the external-valuation wait unless the request is re-sequenced by process change (recommended in parallel).
- Waiting time: reductions before FDR (18h), Normalize (20h), Credit analysis (28h), Resolve entity mismatch (31h) and Contract preparation (22h). Signing wait (44h) unchanged in year 1 except through cleaner, earlier KYC status.
- Rework: missing-document loop, mismatch loop and contract remediation loop all reduced; the three largest loops in the DFG are covered.
- Handovers: fewer document and KYC escalation handovers; legal ops <-> compliance loops reduced; Committee packaging receives more consistent normalized statements and KYC status even without A8.
- Resource demand: analysts gain capacity before inflow increases; specialists recover capacity; legal ops shifts to exception review; back office absorbs document follow-up; RMs adopt structured instructions. No pool sees increased inflow without a matching capacity gain.
- Control violations: provisional signing and contract-decision mismatch gated by A9; KYC clearance quality improved and evidenced by A4; four-eyes handled by a workflow configuration rule with month-end monitoring.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Risk | Where it arises | Compensating controls |
|---|---|---|
| Loss of auditability | A2, A3, A4, A6, A9: model suggestion silently becomes the decision | Every module writes three immutable events (suggestion with confidence and version, human override with reason, final decision with actor); process-mining conformance checks run on these events monthly; no "auto-accept" without a logged actor |
| False negatives in KYC screening | A3 (year 2); A4 if a wrong entity match is confirmed | A3 only pre-classifies, never clears; conservative thresholds with periodic random sampling of auto-low-risk hits by a specialist; A4 requires human confirmation with evidence attached; dual control on merges of legal entities |
| False positives overloading humans | A3 escalations to 3 specialists; A6 low-confidence fields to analysts; A9 rule hits to legal ops | Capacity gates before go-live (A4 live before A3); escalation SLAs and queue-length alerts; threshold tuning reviewed with volume; exception queues staffed explicitly rather than absorbed |
| Model and rule drift | A2 (new products), A3 (sanctions lists), A6 (new statement formats, document quality), A9 (policy or legal changes) | Versioned rule library owned by legal with change control and a review calendar; monthly monitoring of override rate, precision/recall on sampled cases, remediation rate by product; champion-challenger or shadow mode before threshold changes; drift triggers a fall-back to manual for the affected segment |
| Exception handling degrades | Any module: exceptions land in an unowned inbox | Named exception owner per module; exception rate and age as first-class KPIs; A12 as the consolidated workbench in year 2; process-mining variant monitoring to detect new workaround variants |
| Four-eyes violations | Month-end rush in High-exposure SME cases (5.8%); A2 misclassification masking exposure | Role-separation rule enforced in workflow at Committee packaging; A2 changes to exposure class require confirmation by a second person; month-end capacity smoothing for analysts and legal ops; monthly conformance report by segment |
| KYC-before-signing bypass | A9, A10, A11 speeding the tail while KYC status is stale or provisional | Hard gate in A9 on machine-readable KYC clearance; no send-to-sign without it; provisional signing becomes an exception requiring compliance approval; A11 not deployed until the gate has run clean for a defined period |
| Contract-decision mismatch | A9 template or rule errors | Rule checks compare generated terms to decision fields and block on mismatch; legal sampling of generated contracts; remediation loop rate tracked overall and for Credit-line renewal (14%) |
| Customer friction and RM resistance | A5 rigid checklists; A10 reminder cadence | Checklists by segment and product co-designed with RMs; soft nudges before escalation; RM dashboard showing loop reduction; Micro segment gets the simplest checklist |
| Upstream exposure of bottlenecks | A1, A2 raising arrival rates | Sequence A1 after A6/A5; monitor queue length before FDR, Normalize and Credit analysis; throttle or hold auto-registered cases if analyst WIP exceeds a threshold |
| External partner dependency | A7 routing; A10 e-sign vendor | Valuer independence rules (no routing by relationship); SLA monitoring with late rate (19% baseline); vendor identity-assurance standards for e-sign |
| Policy ambiguity exposed | A9 rule library, A12 | Treat as a benefit: compliance owns a decision log; ambiguous rules default to human review |
| Shifted workload not planned | Back office slack, analysts' review load, specialists' escalations, legal ops month-end | Workload model per pool updated each phase; redeploy freed back-office capacity to document follow-up and exception handling; no headcount action until post-go-live utilization is measured |
| Log-quality blind spots | KYC and FDR recorded sequentially although concurrent; provisional signing hidden as normal signing | Fix timestamps and status fields in phase 0 so the effects of A4/A5/A6 can be measured; add explicit "provisional" flag |
| Compliance responsibility assumed to move to the machine | All modules | Accountability remains with compliance ops and specialists; automation is documented as decision support in the control framework; periodic independent review |

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

**Phase 0 (months 0-1): baseline and instrumentation.** No module. Fix event logging so KYC check and Financial document review record true start and end times (concurrency is real but recorded sequentially), add a provisional-signing flag, define the metric set below and freeze a 12-month baseline by segment (Micro / SME / High-exposure) and product. Design the shared audit-event schema (suggestion, override, final) once. Agree with RMs on segment-specific document checklists. Decide the collateral re-sequencing rule (raise valuation request at classification for Equipment finance and flagged Working-capital cases); this is a process change, not a module, and can go live in Phase 1.

**Phase 1 (months 1-6): A5 then A6, and A9 in parallel. Deep integrations: A6 and A9 (the two slots).**

- Month 1-3: A5 live on the portal (light integration). Start A6 build with document-management and credit-model integration. Start A9 rule-library refresh with legal and compliance, and decision-data field capture. Collateral re-sequencing goes live as a workflow change.
- Month 3-6: A6 in shadow mode (analysts see extraction but still key manually) then assisted mode for Micro and SME; High-exposure stays manual. A9 in parallel run: generated contracts compared to manually prepared ones; hard gate on KYC status tested in shadow.
- Gate to Phase 2: A5 has reduced the missing-document request rate (baseline 3,300 / 9,200 = 36%) and the repeat rate (52%) with no rise in customer complaints or RM escalations; A6 field-level accuracy on the validation sample meets the agreed threshold with override rate falling month over month and no increase in Credit analysis rework (9%); A9 parallel run shows zero unreconciled contract-decision mismatches over the agreed sample and the rule library has legal sign-off; audit events present for 100% of automated suggestions.

**Phase 2 (months 6-9): A4 and A9 live.**

- A9 moves from parallel run to production with the KYC-before-send-to-sign gate active; provisional signing becomes a compliance-approved exception.
- A4 live for specialists (light integration to registries); knowledge from the two senior specialists codified into match rules and evidence templates; junior specialists handle assisted cases.
- Four-eyes role separation configured as a workflow rule at Committee packaging.
- Gate to Phase 3: median wait before Resolve entity mismatch falling from 31h with critical-path share falling from 82%; mismatch rework falling from 38%; contract remediation rate falling from 8.5% overall and 14% for Credit-line renewal; provisional signing falling from 6.5%; four-eyes violations falling from 5.8% at month end; specialist utilization and handover concentration on the two seniors measurably lower.

**Phase 3 (months 9-12): stabilize, extend A6 cautiously, prepare year 2.**

- Extend A6 to selected High-exposure statement types only if override rate and accuracy hold; keep judgment steps human.
- E-sign adoption drive using the existing tool, now safe behind the A9 gate (no new module).
- Readiness assessment for year 2: A7 (if re-sequencing shows residual valuation lateness), A10 (reminder automation), A3 (now that A4 exists), A8 (now that A4/A6 provide reconciled artifacts), A1 and A2 (once analyst and specialist queues have absorbed the improvement), A12, and last A11 once the control gates have run clean for two consecutive quarters.

**Monitoring metrics (all by segment and product, monthly):** end-to-end cycle time (median and 90th percentile); median wait before Financial document review, Normalize financial statements, Credit analysis, Resolve entity mismatch, Committee packaging, Contract preparation, Customer signing; loop counts and ratios for FDR -> Request -> FDR, KYC -> Mismatch -> KYC, Contract -> Remediate -> Contract; repeat-request rate; late valuation rate; utilization and queue length per pool (back office, compliance ops, specialists, analysts, legal ops); handover concentration on the two senior specialists; override rate, accuracy on sampled cases and exception age per module; provisional-signing rate, contract-decision mismatch rate, four-eyes violation rate; audit-event completeness; customer complaint and RM escalation counts.

## 7. Final recommendation

Recommend Portfolio R: A5 (missing-document checklist and nudging), A6 (financial-statement extraction and normalization), A9 (contract generation with control-rule checks) and A4 (entity-mismatch resolution assistant), with the collateral re-sequencing and four-eyes workflow rule as zero-module process changes in the same year. It respects the four-module budget, uses the two deep-integration slots on the two modules whose value depends on core data (A6, A9), satisfies compliance's requirement with A9 (and strengthens KYC clearance quality with A4), and keeps RMs on side because A5 is co-designed and A9 removes the remediation rework they experience with customers.

The highest-frequency activity should not be automated first, for reasons grounded in the artifacts rather than in principle:

- Register application accounts for about 1,600 labor hours per year in a 12-FTE pool that is below 65% utilized after 15:00; it has a 1.5h median wait, 4% rework and sits in position 2 in 98% of variants. It is neither a labor constraint nor a time constraint nor a rework source.
- The process is constrained by a 9-FTE analyst pool carrying roughly 30,000 hours of service work across Financial document review, Normalize and Credit analysis, by a 3-FTE specialist pool whose two seniors are the hub of the handover network, by customer and external waits (44h signing, 58h valuation), and by three rework loops that the DFG makes explicit (2,800, 1,160 and 510 loop-back arcs). Every hour saved at registration arrives as additional work-in-progress in front of those pools; the candidate list itself states this risk for A1.
- The politically attractive win therefore produces a visible but process-irrelevant saving and can make cycle time worse. Sequenced after A6 and A5 have raised analyst absorption capacity, A1 becomes a sensible year-2 module whose duplicate-detection feature has genuine control value; its labor saving can then be redeployed to document follow-up and exception handling, which is where the freed back-office hours are actually needed.

Portfolio B would deliver slightly better cycle time (A7 instead of A9) but leaves the contract-decision and KYC-before-signing controls to good intentions, which the 6.5% provisional-signing and 8.5% / 14% remediation figures show is not enough. Portfolio C would deliver the cleanest control posture but leaves the analyst constraint untouched and risks overloading specialists with A3 escalations. Portfolio R takes the constraint-relief core of B and the control core of C, and defers the two modules (A7, A10) whose main effect can be partly achieved in year 1 by process changes without integration budget.
