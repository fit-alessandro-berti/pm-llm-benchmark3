# 1. Model scope and state variables

### Digital Twin Boundary
The digital twin covers the entire lifecycle of a building-permit application from portal submission to final decision (permit or rejection). It operates at the municipality level, encompassing all relevant departments (Clerk, Planning, Fire, Environment, Inspection, Legal) and external stakeholders (Applicants, Third-party consultants).

### Level of Detail
- **Granularity:** Discrete Event Simulation (DES) at the event level (activity start/end).
- **Aggregation:** Resources are aggregated by role (e.g., "Planner") but distinguished by specialization (e.g., "Simple Renovation" vs "All").
- **Dependencies:** Explicit modeling of resource contention (queues) and cross-case dependencies (architect bundles, inspection batching, neighborhood objections).

### Entities
1.  **Cases:** Individual permit applications.
2.  **Resources:** Human staff (Clerk, Planner, Fire, Env, Inspector, Legal) and System.
3.  **Bundles:** Groups of cases sharing an `architect_id` and `district` for inspection scheduling.
4.  **Queues:** Waiting lines for specific activities (e.g., Planning Review Queue).
5.  **Events:** Activity starts, ends, rework triggers, decision outcomes.

### State Variables
- **Case State:** Current activity, status (pending, rework, closed), timestamps (arrival, completion).
- **Resource State:** Current busy resource, queue length, calendar availability.
- **System State:** Current backlog count, active bundles, policy flags (e.g., D1 active).
- **Global Clock:** Simulation time.

### Resources & Calendars
- **Clerks:** 5 FTE, Monday-Friday 08:00-16:00.
- **Planners:** 8 FTE, Mon-Fri 09:00-17:00 (2 specialized).
- **Fire/Env:** Specialized pools with specific working days.
- **Inspectors:** 4 FTE + potential D4 temporary pool, travel times based on district.
- **Legal:** 2 FTE, blocked during council meetings.

### Cross-Case Dependencies
- **Architect Bundling:** Multiple cases from the same `architect_id` in the same `district` are grouped for inspection scheduling to reduce travel time but increase waiting time for the bundle.
- **Objection Propagation:** Public-notice duration is extended stochastically based on district/case type proxies for objections.
- **Rework Chains:** Rework loops (e.g., Completeness) depend on applicant response times, modeled separately from municipal service.

# 2. Parameter-estimation plan

4.  **DFG Analysis:** Use `pm4py.get_dfg` and `pm4py.get_performance_dfg` to extract activity frequencies and bottlenecks.

### Parameter Estimation
- **Arrivals:** Fit a Piecewise Constant function or Poisson process with seasonality factors (1.7x in peak months, 2.2x Monday vs Wednesday).
- **Service Times:** Fit distributions (e.g., Log-Normal or Gamma) to empirical summaries.
- **Waiting Times:** Estimate from gaps between activity timestamps in the log; handle missing start times by inferring from empirical "Median waiting before activity".
- **Branching Probabilities:** Calculate from event log frequencies (e.g., % of cases requiring Fire Safety).
- **Rework:** Estimate from log (e.g., count of "Request missing documents" vs "Completeness check" re-occurrences).
- **Dependencies:** Calculate from `architect_id` and `district` co-occurrence matrices.

### Handling Data Gaps
- **Missing Start Times:** We cannot derive exact service/waiting split from logs alone. We will use the empirical summaries provided (e.g., "Median waiting before activity") to initialize the simulation distributions.
- **Objections:** Since specific objection events are missing, we will treat `Public-notice period` duration > 14 days as a latent "Objection" indicator with a probability derived from the p90 duration (60d) vs median (14d).

# 3. Assumptions

### Arrivals
- **Non-stationary:** Demand follows a weekly pattern (Monday spikes) and monthly seasonality (Peak months).
- **Bursts:** Large architects submit in bursts; modeled by clustering arrivals by `architect_id` and `district`.

### Service Times
- **Distributions:** Service times are stochastic (Log-Normal) to capture heavy tails (e.g., Environmental review).
- **Independence:** Service times for one case are independent of others, except for resource contention.

### Routing
- **Conditional:** Routing branches (e.g., Fire Safety) depend on case attributes (`requires_fire`, `district`).
- **Rework:** Rework loops are probabilistic based on historical frequency.

### Resources/Calendars
- **Availability:** Resources are blocked during holidays and council meetings.
- **Capacity:** FTE counts are hard constraints; overtime is not modeled (backlog increases instead).

### Rework
- **Trigger:** Rework is triggered by resource decisions (e.g., "Request missing documents").
- **Delay:** Rework delay is dominated by applicant response time, not municipal processing.

### Applicant/Third-party Response
- **Independence:** Applicant response times are external to municipal service times.
- **Heaviness:** "Receive missing documents" waiting time is heavily tailed.

### Cross-case Dependencies
- **Bundling:** Inspection scheduling is batched by `architect_id` + `district`.
- **Objections:** Public-notice duration is a proxy for objections; we assume objections occur with probability $P = \frac{\text{p90} - \text{median}}{\text{max\_duration}}$.

### Non-stationary Demand
- **Seasonality:** 1.7x demand in March-May and Sept-Nov.
- **Weekday:** 2.2x Monday submissions.

### Missing Data
- **Objections:** We do not treat missing objections as observed. Instead, we model them as a latent variable driving the duration of "Public-notice period".
- **Emergency Decisions:** Rare emergency decisions are excluded from the base model but added as a low-probability exception branch.

# 5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment

### Warm-up
- **Approach:** Running Average (Batch Means) or Visual Inspection of metrics (e.g., Queue Length).
- **Duration:** Discard first 5% of simulation time (approx. 1 month equivalent) to reach steady-state behavior given the non-stationary demand.
- **Justification:** To avoid bias from initial startup effects.

### Replications
- **Count:** 20 independent replications per experiment configuration.
- **Random Seeds:** Fixed seeds for each configuration to ensure reproducibility; varied within replications.
- **Confidence Intervals:** 95% Confidence Intervals (CI) for mean and median duration.

### Uncertainty Treatment
- **Heavy Tails:** Use Log-Normal distributions for waiting times (e.g., "Receive missing documents") to capture outliers without skewing mean estimates excessively.
- **Sensitivity Analysis:** Vary key parameters (e.g., Planner FTE, Objection Probability) by ±10% to assess impact on total duration.
- **Credible Intervals:** Bayesian inference not used; frequentist CIs applied to simulation outputs.

### Handling Non-Stationarity
- **Time-Varying Inputs:** Arrival rates and resource calendars are functions of simulation time (e.g., `if month in [3,4,5]: arrival_rate *= 1.7`).
- **Replication:** Replications run over the full 18-month equivalent horizon to capture seasonal effects.

# 6. Validation strategy

### Face Validation
- **Stakeholder Review:** Present model logic and assumptions to process owners (e.g., City Manager).
- **Goal:** Ensure the model reflects operational reality (e.g., "Do planners really work 09:00-17:00?").

### Event-Log Replay Validation
- **Comparison:** Run simulation with "Base" settings and compare generated metrics (cycle time, queue lengths) against empirical summaries (median, p90).
- **Metric:** Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) < 10% for key metrics.

### Distributional Validation
- **Tests:** Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) test between simulated and empirical service time distributions.
- **Goal:** Ensure stochastic distributions are adequately fitted.

### Resource-Utilization Validation
- **Comparison:** Compare simulated resource utilization (e.g., Planner % busy) against HR data (implied utilization from FTE/Workload).
- **Goal:** Verify resource constraints are realistic.

### Bottleneck Validation
- **Analysis:** Identify bottlenecks in simulation (e.g., Legal Check). Compare with empirical summaries (e.g., "Legal check waiting time").
- **Goal:** Ensure the model identifies the correct bottlenecks.

### Out-of-Sample Validation
- **Data:** Hold out the last 3 months of the log (not used for parameter estimation).
- **Test:** Run simulation with calibrated parameters and compare against actual outcomes for the holdout period.

# 7. Decision criteria

### Decision Metrics
1.  **Median Total Duration:** Target < 60 days for Simple, < 100 days for New Residential.
2.  **p90/Tail Duration:** Target < 180 days for all case types (to protect against long delays).
3.  **SLA Compliance:** % of cases completed within 90 days (Target > 80%).
4.  **Backlog:** Average queue length per resource (Target < 20 cases).
5.  **Rework Rate:** % of cases requiring rework (Target < 15%).
6.  **Fairness:** Variance in duration across districts (Target Coefficient of Variation < 0.2).
7.  **Resource Utilization:** Target 70-85% utilization (avoid under/over-utilization).

### Thresholds & Selection
- **Intervention Selection:** Choose the intervention that minimizes Median Duration without increasing p90 Duration by > 5% compared to Baseline.
- **Fairness Constraint:** Reject any intervention that increases the duration variance across districts by > 10%.
- **Robustness:** Select interventions that perform within CI bounds under Stress Scenarios (S1-S3).
