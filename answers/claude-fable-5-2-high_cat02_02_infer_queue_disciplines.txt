Confidence is the estimated probability that the stated primary rule plus hybrid component is the rule actually in force, not how well it fits the sample. Each queue has at most eight informative decisions.

**Assumptions (labelled, used throughout)**

- **AS1, arrival uncertainty.** True arrival lies in [scan - 2 min, scan]. A job scanned up to 2 min after a decision is "possibly present" and marked `?`. This is needed because scans can be late but not early.
- **AS2, decision time.** The decision is taken at the service start or stop instant. This is needed because no dispatch timestamp exists; if plans are made earlier, eligible sets shrink.
- **AS3, static attributes.** Priority, `est_min`, deadline and appointment are the values dispatch saw at arrival. This is needed because the tables show one value per job.
- **AS4, eligibility from arrival.** A job is eligible from its arrival, with no hidden readiness condition. Section 5 relaxes this.
- **AS5, resource availability.** Resources are available except for the recorded exceptions. Section 5 relaxes this.
- **AS6, remaining time.** A suspended job's remaining estimate is `est_min` minus work already done.
- **AS7, combined skills.** A job requiring "a+b" needs one resource holding both skills.

**Observation relevant to every queue.** For all 32 jobs the realized service time (sum of segments) equals `est_min` exactly, including B01 (14 + 17 + 14 = 45). Two consequences follow:

- Noisy or strategic estimates cannot be tested with these data. Estimates may even have been back-filled from actual durations.
- No resumption or changeover penalty is visible in service minutes.

# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

Rule definitions used for the predictions:

- **FIFO:** earliest scanned arrival among eligible waiting jobs. A suspended job keeps its original arrival.
- **PRIO:** lowest priority number, ties broken by FIFO.
- **SPT:** smallest estimate (remaining estimate for a suspended job), ties broken by FIFO.
- **APPT:** a job whose appointment time has been reached goes first. If none is due, the rule is silent ("none due"), and a slot-protecting variant is noted where it differs.
- **SKILL:** restricts the eligible set. In QA, QB and QC there is one general resource and all jobs are general, so it is non-binding.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

**QA: FIFO plus appointment head-of-line**

- **Support.** A02, A05 and A07 are each chosen while a P1 job waits. A02, A05 and A06 are each chosen while a shorter job waits. A04 (P1) waits 45 min and A08 (P1) waits 61 min.
- **Calibration.** The 08:52-08:58 calibration explains A04's late start. It must not be read as a decision.
- **Challenge.** A03 is the only FIFO break. Scan delay cannot explain it: A02's true arrival is at most 08:04 and A03's at least 08:06. The recorded appointment at exactly 08:20 is the simplest explanation, so no undocumented exception is needed.
- **A06 is not appointment evidence.** Its late start is not a violation of "head of line when due at the next completion". It does rule out slot protection: A05 was started at 09:05 knowing it would overrun 09:15. It also rules out an early window of 10 min or more, since A06 was present and not taken at 09:05.
- **Unexplained gaps.** A1 was idle 08:00-08:02 and 08:30-08:32. This could be setup or logging granularity, and it does not affect any ordering.

**QB: P1 interrupt plus FIFO, with one override**

- **Support.** B02's preemption is recorded as "urgent arrival". B05 is chosen over suspended B01 at 10:00. P2 jobs B03 and B06 do not interrupt and are not preferred over P3 work at 09:31, 10:14 or 10:28. B04 and B07 are served in arrival order. B07's appointment has no effect.
- **Challenge.** B03 at 09:48 needs an undocumented exception, and the log itself labels it "dispatcher reassignment", not "urgent arrival". The stop occurs exactly two minutes after B05's scan, the same lag as B02 (09:14 to 09:16). That suggests B05 triggered the stop. But B03 receives the capacity and B05 waits 14 min. Three explanations are possible:
  1. Dispatcher discretion.
  2. A wait-limit escalation for P2 at about 30 min. B03 had waited 28-30 min given scan delay.
  3. B05's priority was upgraded after arrival and the log stores the final value.
- **Limit on the aging explanation.** B06 (P2) started after 31 min, exactly when B04 finished. That is consistent with a 30-minute limit but does not test it.
- **Preemption cost.** B01's flow time is 88 min for 45 min of work, and it was interrupted a second time with only 14 min left. A cost-aware preemption rule would not do that for a P2 job. This supports "override" over "policy".

**QC: SPT core with escalation and aging**

- **Support.** C02 and C03 are uniquely SPT choices. C06 at 11:34 is SPT. C05 (P1) never preempts C02, C03 or C04, so preemptive priority is refuted.
- **Challenge 1, C04 over C06 at 10:32.** This is not explained by any recorded constraint. Candidate explanations:
  - A sequence planned once at 10:10 for the backlog, with later arrivals appended.
  - C06 not yet eligible at 10:32, which violates AS4.
  - Aging-adjusted SPT. This is my hypothesis, not supplied in the prompt. The response ratio (wait + est) / est is 2.14 for C04 and 2.17 for C06, a tie that flips if C04's scan was one minute late.
- **Challenge 2, C05 at 10:54.** This is a switch away from SPT. It happened at the first completion after the 10:50 deadline had passed. It did not happen when the deadline became unreachable, which was already true at 10:18. So the trigger looks reactive (breach or wait threshold), not slack-based. The note "deadline already at risk" understates this: the deadline was already missed when C05 was selected.
- **Challenge 3, C01 at 11:40.** This fits neither SPT nor deadline protection. It is explained only by a starvation guard: C01 had waited 100 min and been bypassed five times. It caused C07's avoidable 34-minute miss.
- **Net.** Two undocumented exceptions and one unexplained choice in six decisions. The "SPT" label describes the first 22 minutes far better than the whole log.

**QD: skill routing plus per-resource FIFO**

- **Support.** D04 before D08 (P1) on D-M. D07 before D10 (P1) on D-X. D-M is idle from 12:03 while D05 and D10 wait, which is pure skill ineligibility.
- **Apparent FIFO violations.** These are fully explained by known constraints:
  - D05 is overtaken by five jobs because D-F is the only qualified resource. A 30-minute job could not finish between 11:36 and the 12:00 meeting.
  - D07 is overtaken by D08 and D09 because D-X is the only qualified resource and D08 and D09 ran on D-M.
- **D08's "priority jump".** D08 (P1) starting before D05 and D07 looks like priority. It is not: D08 was simply the FIFO head of the mechanical pool at 11:35.
- **Challenge 1, D-X idle 11:20-11:30.** Explained if D06's appointment is a hard exact-time reservation with no gap filling, since D06 started exactly at 11:30 although present since 11:10. Otherwise it requires unrecorded unavailability.
- **Challenge 2, D-F idle 11:36-12:00 with D10 eligible.** No recorded constraint explains this. The note on D10 explains only why D-F could not take it at 12:16. It needs either early binding of D10 to D-X's queue (head-of-line blocking behind D05 on D-F) or an unrecorded D-F absence.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked from most to least plausible.

1. **Unrecorded resource unavailability or reservation.** Examples are D-F at 11:36-12:00, D-X at 11:20-11:30 and A1 at 08:30-08:32. The prompt explicitly warns that missing reasons prove nothing. If true, both QD anomalies disappear and "skill plus FIFO" strengthens, but the evidence for a hard appointment hold on D-X weakens.
2. **Manual dispatcher override.** B03 at 09:48 is recorded as "dispatcher reassignment". If true, QB is cleanly "P1 interrupts, otherwise FIFO" and the anomaly carries no policy information. Confidence in QB would rise to about 65.
3. **SLA or aging-triggered regime switch.** Candidates are C05 after its deadline passed, C01 after 100 min, and possibly B03 at about 30 min. If true, QC is "SPT with breach and starvation guardrails". But C07 was endangered at 11:40 and was not rescued, so any such switch is reactive or limited to P1, not deadline protection.
4. **Hidden readiness or eligibility (arrival is not queue entry).** C06 may not have been dispatchable at 10:32, and D10 may not have been ready for D-F at 11:36. If true, dynamic SPT in QC survives 10:32 and SPT hits rise to 4/6.
5. **Frozen plan or early binding (decision time is not start time).** This violates AS2. Examples are the QC sequence fixed at 10:10 and QD jobs bound to a resource queue at arrival. It changes QC to "batch SPT" and QD to "per-resource FIFO after skill-based assignment". Service orders stay the same but behaviour under load differs.
6. **Arrival-scan delay (2 min or less).** It matters materially in one place only: C05 at 10:10. If C05 was physically present, starting it then would have met 10:50 exactly, and ignoring it is stronger evidence against priority and EDD. If not, its deadline was infeasible on arrival (10:11 + 40 = 10:51), which is itself a data or commitment problem. Scan delay also touches the C04/C06 response-ratio tie and the `?` jobs A05 and D09. It cannot reverse any FIFO verdict used above, because each verdict rests on a pair scanned at least 3 min apart. The closest overtaking pair, D07 and D08 at 2 min, sits on different resources and is explained by skill.
7. **Attribute overwrite or back-filled estimates.** B05's priority may have been upgraded after arrival. `est_min` equals actual duration in 32 of 32 jobs. If estimates were back-filled, QC's "SPT" may really be "dispatcher judges short jobs by sight" and strategic estimation is untestable. If B05 was not P1 at 09:48, QB's main anomaly disappears.
8. **Work-in-progress-first or preemption-cost avoidance in QB.** This is observationally identical to FIFO by original arrival for B01. It would matter only if a suspended job were younger than a waiting one.

# 6. Additional event data needed

In priority order:

1. **Dispatch-decision event.** Timestamp, deciding actor (system or dispatcher), chosen job, and the eligibility set shown at that moment. This resolves QC 10:32, QD 11:36, and frozen plan versus dynamic choice.
2. **Preemption record.** Triggering job ID, reason code, who authorized it, and remaining estimated time of the interrupted job at the stop. This resolves QB 09:48 (B05 trigger, B03 aging or discretion) and preemption-cost rules.
3. **Resource state log.** Login, idle, setup, break, reserved-for-job and meeting-preparation states with timestamps. This resolves the D-F and D-X idle periods, the A1 two-minute gaps, and the true end of C1's prior job.
4. **Queue-entry and ready time, separate from the arrival scan.** Include the scan-delay flag and physical arrival time. This resolves C05 at 10:10, C06 at 10:32, D10, and the `?` cases.
5. **Appointment metadata.** Tolerance window (early and late), whether the appointment is bound to a named resource, and whether it reserves capacity. This resolves:
   - A03: exact time versus coincidence.
   - A06: a 5-minute delay without consequence.
   - B07: a 26-minute delay.
   - D06: the hold on D-X.
6. **Assignment or binding time of job to resource in QD, and the skill matrix version.** Include primary versus secondary skills. This resolves early versus late binding and why electrical jobs D03 and D10 went to different resources.
7. **Attribute history.** Priority changes, estimate at arrival versus current estimated remaining time, estimate author, and deadline creation time. This resolves B05's priority at 09:48, back-filled estimates, and C05's deadline that was infeasible on arrival.
8. **SLA timers and escalation events.** Breach-warning and breach timestamps, wait-limit configuration per priority, and starvation or aging thresholds. This resolves C05 (breach versus P1 versus wait of about 43 min), C01 (100 min), and B03 (about 30 min).
9. **Policy version or configuration ID per queue and time, plus override flags.** This resolves whether QC changed policy at 10:54 or applied one composite rule.
10. **Changeover and resumption effort.** Setup minutes on resume and rework. These are invisible today because segments sum exactly to the estimate.
11. **More volume.** Several days per queue, especially with these cases:
    - Appointments falling due behind older walk-ins.
    - P1 arrivals during P2 work.
    - Short jobs arriving mid-backlog.
    - Deadlines that contradict priority order.

# 7. Final synthesis

- **Most identifiable: QA.** It has one resource, a recorded calibration, and six informative decisions that FIFO plus appointment fits completely, while priority fails in five of them and SPT in four. Only the exact form of the appointment clause is open.
- **QD is second.** Its orderings are clean per resource, but the multi-resource idle periods are unexplained.
- **Least identifiable: QC.** Three behaviours (SPT, expedite after breach, aging release) appear in six informative decisions. Priority and deadline are perfectly collinear. The one SPT violation at 10:32 depends on unobserved plan timing or eligibility. Calling it a hybrid is honest, but any precise rule is over-fitted.
- **Most dangerous inference: "QB is a preemptive priority queue".** It rests on one labelled episode (B02). The other evidence points the other way:
  - The second P1 arrival (B05) did not receive the freed capacity.
  - P2 never outranked P3 at 09:31, 10:14 or 10:28.
