def scope_matches(auth_scope, case_scope):
    if auth_scope == "*": return True
    return str(auth_scope).lower() == str(case_scope).lower()

def authority_candidates(resource, t, case_scope, allowed_roles):
    out=[]
    for _, a in authority_df.iterrows():
        if a["human"] != resource: continue
        if not interval_contains(a["interval"], t): continue
        if not a["valid"]: continue
        if a["authority"] not in allowed_roles: continue
        if not scope_matches(a["scope"], case_scope): continue
        absence_status = "NA"
        if a["requires_principal_absence"]:
            absence_status = "MET" if interval_contains(asha_leave, t) else "NOT_MET_OR_UNKNOWN"
        out.append({
            "delegation_id": a["delegation_id"],
            "authority": a["authority"],
            "absence_status": absence_status,
            "incident_condition": a["incident_condition"],
            "evidence_strength": a["evidence_strength"],
        })
    return out

def invalid_delegation_matches(resource, t, case_scope):
    out=[]

        if a["valid"]: continue
        if not interval_contains(a["interval"], t): continue

## 2. Rule Interpretation

### RR1 — Drafter/approver separation

Formal test:

```text
RR1 violation if:
    normalized_resource(Draft_Change_Request)
    == normalized_resource(Approve_Change)
    within the same case
```

- Applies to all cases, regardless of risk level.
- Aliases must be normalized first.
- If either activity is performed by `svc_qms`, the event is technically nonhuman. It cannot satisfy a human four-eyes control unless reliable session evidence maps it to an authorized human.
- If reliable attribution maps the service account to the same human who performed the other activity, RR1 is violated substantively.
- If attribution is unreliable, the result is a data/identity problem and the SoD check is indeterminate or suspicious rather than cleanly passed.

### RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation

Formal test:

```text
RR2 violation if:
    normalized_resource(Implement_Change)
    == normalized_resource(Verify_Implementation)
    within the same case
```

- Applies to all cases.
- If `Verify_Implementation` is performed by `svc_qms`, the event does not satisfy the human verifier requirement unless reliable session evidence maps it to an authorized human.
- Weak attribution means the event is a data/identity problem and may also be suspicious if the likely human is the implementer.
- Strong attribution to a human other than the implementer can satisfy the substantive separation, but the service-account authentication weakness remains.

### RR3 — High-risk approval authority

For HIGH-risk cases, `Approve_Change` must be performed by:

1. A standing QM at event time; or
2. A valid QM delegate.

A QM delegation is valid only if all of the following are true at the approval timestamp:

- The delegation interval covers the event timestamp.
- The delegation scope covers the case scope and any incident/change-type limitation.
- The principal is absent or formally unavailable if the delegation requires absence.
- No prohibited subdelegation occurred.
- The delegate is the named delegate.
- The evidence is sufficient to show the delegation existed at event time.

A later database entry cannot, by itself, prove that a delegation existed earlier. A contemporaneous signed artifact, such as a PDF with a trustworthy timestamp before the event, is much stronger. A later migration record may explain why the QMS showed the delegation later, but it is weak evidence for event-time authority unless corroborated.

### RR4 — Emergency substitution

For HIGH-risk emergency changes:

- Implementation may proceed after approval by the Incident Commander or a valid IC delegate.
- The emergency flag must be credible, and a safety rationale should be available.
- A QM or valid QM delegate must perform `Retrospective_QM_Review` within two business days.

Business-day counting used here:

- Business days are Monday through Friday, excluding site holidays.
- The site calendar had no holidays in the relevant period.
- The day of the triggering approval is treated as day 0.
- The retrospective review is due by the end of the second following business day.
- Weekend days do not count as business days.

Examples:

- CHG-505 emergency approval: Tuesday 2026-08-11 13:00 UTC.  
  Business day 1: Wednesday 2026-08-12.  
  Business day 2: Thursday 2026-08-13.  
  Due: end of Thursday 2026-08-13.  
  Retrospective on Friday 2026-08-14 16:00 is late.
- CHG-510 emergency approval: Tuesday 2026-08-11 14:20 UTC.  
  Retrospective on Wednesday 2026-08-12 10:00 is within two business days, but the reviewer still must have valid QM authority for the relevant scope.

### Treatment of a principal acting while on leave

A principal’s standing authority is not automatically extinguished by leave if the principal genuinely acts using their own authenticated identity. Therefore, if Asha Rao personally approved CHG-504 using her own credentials, the approval can satisfy RR3 despite her leave status.

However, leave creates a control-context problem:

- The principal was expected to be absent.
- Delegation conditions may be affected.
- Credential sharing or management override becomes a plausible alternative explanation.
- If security cannot exclude credential sharing, the event should be treated as at least partially an identity/data problem, even if the event-time role appears valid.

### RR5 — Identity evidence

- A service account is not a human identity.
- Aliases belonging to the same human must be normalized before SoD checks.
- Service-account actions can support conformance only if reliable session evidence maps the action to an authorized human.
- Weak session evidence produces an indeterminate or data/identity finding, not a clean pass.

### RR6 — Suspicion versus proof

Repeated pairs, reciprocal approvals, shared workstations, concentrated handovers, and absence anomalies are indicators. They are not proof of collusion. Each indicator requires benign and adverse explanations and discriminating evidence before misconduct is inferred.

## 5. PM4Py and pandas Checking Code

Service-account attribution is represented in the code as follows:

- `svc_qms` remains `svc_qms` in `org:resource` for PM4Py checks, because it is not automatically treated as a human.
- Manual attribution evidence is stored in `svc_attributed_human` and `svc_attribution_strength`.
- The code produces flags such as `OK_IF_STRONG_ATTRIBUTION`, `INDETERMINATE_WEAK_ATTRIBUTION`, or `NONHUMAN_VERIFIER`, but it does not automatically declare misconduct or collusion.
- Judgments about whether weak attribution is enough, whether a service-account verification is acceptable operationally, and whether network patterns indicate collusion remain manual investigative judgments.

## 6. Control Improvements

### Delegation creation and effective-time recording

- Require delegations to be created, signed, and registered in the QMS before their effective start time.
- Store immutable created-by, created-at, effective-from, effective-to, scope, and subdelegation-permission fields.
- Flag migrated or backfilled records as administrative reconstructions, not as primary evidence of event-time authority.
- Prevent activation of delegations with past effective dates unless a formal exception is approved and documented.

### Principal availability

- Integrate HR leave, on-call status, and formal unavailability records with the QMS delegation engine.
- If a principal is recorded as absent but authenticates to the QMS, prompt for confirmation of whether they are resuming duty or handling a single exception.
- If a principal resumes work during leave, require explicit documentation and consider temporarily suspending or annotating active delegations.

### Alias management

- Maintain a single authoritative human identity identifier.
- Normalize all known aliases before SoD checks.
- Deprecate or monitor legacy aliases such as `b.koch` when a primary ID such as `u_bk` exists.
- Alert when two aliases for the same human appear in the same case in approval-like activities.

### Individual authentication and service accounts

- Prohibit service accounts from performing human approval, verification, or retrospective review unless there is a robust session-attribution mechanism.
- Require individual re-authentication on shared terminals.
- Use MFA/FIDO tokens for HIGH-risk approvals and verifications.
- Separate system integration accounts from human workflow accounts.
- If a service account must post a human decision, store the human’s authenticated approval artifact separately.

### Emergency review controls

- Require emergency changes to be linked to an incident ID, such as CI-44, at creation.
- Require a credible safety rationale and incident commander confirmation for emergency approval exceptions.
- Automatically start the RR4 retrospective clock at emergency approval.
- Escalate if no valid QM or QM delegate is available for retrospective review within the business-day window.
- Ensure retrospective QM review delegations cover the actual change scope, not only a related production cell.

### Pair rotation and segregation monitoring

- Introduce rotation or secondary-review requirements for HIGH-risk drafting/approval pairs.
- Monitor repeated drafter-approver pairs, but treat repeated collaboration as an indicator, not proof.
- Require supervisory review when the same pair handles a high proportion of HIGH-risk changes.
- Avoid assigning the same person as drafter and approver even for LOW-risk changes.

### Network and pattern monitoring

- Use PM4Py handover and working-together networks to detect concentration, reciprocal approvals, and unusual clusters.
- Combine network indicators with case outcomes, defect rates, absence records, and authentication evidence.
- Do not automatically infer collusion from repeated collaboration.

### Evidential retention

- Retain badge logs, CCTV where available, VPN logs, IP addresses, workstation identifiers, MFA/FIDO events, session start/end times, delegation artifacts, HR leave records, incident tickets, and safety rationales.
- Preserve evidence for the full change-control retention period.
- Ensure shared-workstation events are individually attributable or explicitly flagged as nonattributable.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

The apparent nonconformance is mixed. It is not primarily explained by one cause.

- Genuine authority and segregation failures are present in about half of the cases. CHG-502, CHG-505, CHG-507, CHG-508, and CHG-510 contain clear rule failures: self-approval, non-QM HIGH-risk approval, invalid emergency subdelegation, and retrospective QM review without proper scope or timing.
- Identity and data-quality problems materially affect the interpretation of several cases. CHG-503, CHG-504, CHG-506, CHG-508, and CHG-509 involve alias normalization, service-account attribution, leave/VPN ambiguity, disputed sick-leave account use, or principal-absence uncertainty.
- Organizational-pattern risk is real but not proof of collusion. The Clara-Ben drafting/approval concentration, reciprocal LOW-risk training approvals, shared workstations, and absence anomalies justify monitoring and process redesign, but they do not by themselves establish misconduct.

Overall, the strongest systemic judgment is:

- Approximately 40–50% of the apparent deviations are genuine authority or SoD failures.
- Approximately 30–40% are substantially complicated by identity, authentication, delegation administration, or master-data quality issues.
- A persistent organizational-pattern risk exists due to repeated pairs, shared accounts, weak delegation controls, and lack of rotation, but collusion is not proven by the available evidence.

The three highest-priority investigations are:

1. **CHG-510** — invalid emergency approval through prohibited subdelegation, drafter self-approval, and retrospective QM review without valid cooling-system QM authority.
2. **CHG-506** — HIGH-risk production approval using Ben Koch’s account during certified sick leave, with disputed identity and possible credential misuse.
3. **CHG-503 and the broader `svc_qms`/shared-workstation pattern** — service-account verification with weak attribution to the implementer’s workstation, creating a high risk that implementer/verifier separation was bypassed or cannot be proven.
