# 2. Benign alternative explanations

### **Four-Eyes Violations**
1. **Emergency Handling**:
   - **Explanation**: Violations may reflect legitimate emergency overrides (e.g., medical supplies, weekend repairs) where speed is critical and retrospective justification is delayed due to operational pressure.
   - **Supporting Evidence**: High `emergency_flag` rates (88% for Carla/Gita, 79% for Dan/Hugo) and documented emergency protocols.
   - **Refuting Evidence**: Lack of retrospective justification within 3 days, identical timestamps for batch-entered justifications, or approvals for non-emergency cases.

2. **Informal Specialization**:
   - **Explanation**: Specialized buyers (e.g., Carla for medical supplies, Dan for repairs) may work closely with dedicated approvers (e.g., Gita, Hugo) due to subject-matter expertise, leading to repeated resource pairs.
   - **Supporting Evidence**: Low vendor concentration, routine case attributes (e.g., `amount_band=low/medium` for Aline/Elena), and alignment with documented roles.
   - **Refuting Evidence**: Evidence of approvals for cases outside the resource’s specialization (e.g., medical supplies approved by a non-specialist) or lack of documented delegation.

### **Unusually Fast Approvals**
1. **Legitimate Urgency**:
   - **Explanation**: Fast approvals for emergency medical supplies or weekend repairs may reflect genuine time-sensitive needs (e.g., patient care, facility safety).
   - **Supporting Evidence**: High `emergency_flag` rates, alignment with documented emergency procedures, and justification in case notes.
   - **Refuting Evidence**: Approvals for non-emergency cases, lack of retrospective justification, or approvals outside business hours without documented urgency.

2. **Automation or Workflow Optimization**:
   - **Explanation**: Some fast approvals may involve automated bots (e.g., `Bot_PO`, `Bot_Match`) or pre-approved low-risk cases (e.g., routine purchases by Aline/Elena).
   - **Supporting Evidence**: Cases with `source_system="automated"` or low `amount_band` values.
   - **Refuting Evidence**: Manual overrides for low-risk cases or approvals bypassing standard automation rules.

### **Subcontracting Loops**
1. **Operational Workflow**:
   - **Explanation**: Loops like `Approver_Gita  Buyer_Carla  Approver_Gita` may reflect legitimate clarification cycles for complex emergency cases.
   - **Supporting Evidence**: Case notes documenting clarifications, alignment with emergency protocols, and no evidence of approval changes.
   - **Refuting Evidence**: Repeated loops without substantive changes, approvals for trivial cases, or loops involving non-emergency items.

2. **Data-Quality Artifacts**:
   - **Explanation**: Loops may result from event-log errors (e.g., shared-account mappings, timestamp misalignments) rather than actual workflows.
   - **Supporting Evidence**: Evidence of `org:resource` mapping errors (e.g., `legacy_erp` issues) or duplicate events.
   - **Refuting Evidence**: Consistent loops in the detailed audit trail (e.g., email chains, approval comments).

### **Vendor Concentration**
1. **Legitimate Vendor Relationships**:
   - **Explanation**: High concentration on vendor family V-77x may reflect a strategic supplier relationship or industry-standard practices (e.g., bulk medical supplies).
   - **Supporting Evidence**: Contracts or procurement policies favoring V-77x, competitive pricing documentation, or industry benchmarks.
   - **Refuting Evidence**: Lack of competitive bidding, approvals for non-standard terms, or vendor-specific kickbacks (e.g., unexplained bank detail changes).

2. **Data-Quality Issues**:
   - **Explanation**: Concentration may be an artifact of vendor master data migration or manual_upload errors.
   - **Supporting Evidence**: Missing linkages between procurement cases and vendor maintenance tickets, or timestamp inconsistencies.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

**Internal Audit Report: Procurement-to-Payment Process Review**

This review analyzed procurement-to-payment event logs to assess adherence to four-eyes principles, approval timeliness, and potential risks of collusion or procedural shortcuts. While no direct evidence of wrongdoing was identified, several control exceptions and data-quality issues warrant attention.

**Key Findings:**
1. **Four-Eyes Violations**: A small subset of cases (1.8% of total) violated normative four-eyes rules, primarily involving emergency medical supplies and vendor bank detail changes. Most violations occurred in high-priority, time-sensitive scenarios (e.g., `emergency_flag=True` in 88% of Buyer_Carla/Approver_Gita cases). Retrospective justification delays (18% late) and batch-entered timestamps raise questions about oversight rigor.
2. **Vendor Concentration**: High activity on vendor family V-77x (72% of VendorMaster_Lena/AP_Kim cases) warrants review, particularly given the reliance on manual uploads (83%) and four-eyes violations. The legitimacy of bank detail changes and payment approvals should be verified.
3. **Data-Quality Issues**: Event-log artifacts—such as shared-account mappings, `legacy_erp` bot-human confusion, and missing linkages between vendor maintenance and procurement cases—obscure accountability and may mask procedural gaps.
4. **Emergency Handling**: Fast approvals for genuine emergencies (e.g., medical supplies, weekend repairs) appear operationally necessary but should align with documented protocols. The most concerning subgroup is **Buyer_Carla/Approver_Gita**, given the volume of violations, high emergency rates, and retrospective justification risks.

**Recommendations:**
- **Targeted Review**: Prioritize cases involving Buyer_Carla/Approver_Gita (126 cases), VendorMaster_Lena/AP_Kim (44 cases), and Manager_Hugo/Buyer_Dan (104 cases) for substantive documentation (e.g., approval emails, case notes, vendor contracts). Focus on:
  - Justification for emergency overrides and retrospective timeliness.
  - Vendor concentration risks (V-77x family) and bank detail change legitimacy.
  - Evidence of independent review in approval chains.
- **Data-Quality Remediation**: Correct `org:resource` mapping errors in `legacy_erp`, link vendor maintenance tickets to procurement cases, and audit `manual_upload` access logs.
- **Policy Clarification**: Explicitly document emergency approval procedures, including retrospective justification requirements and delegation limits. Train resources on four-eyes principles and data-entry best practices.
- **Monitoring**: Implement automated alerts for:
  - Four-eyes violations outside documented exceptions.
  - Unusually fast approvals for non-emergency cases.
  - Manual uploads or shared-account usage.

**Limitations:**
This analysis relied on event-log data, which may contain inaccuracies (e.g., timestamp misalignments, missing attributes). Findings should be validated with detailed audit trails, approval emails, and vendor documentation. The absence of direct evidence of wrongdoing does not preclude localized risks, particularly where procedural shortcuts or data-quality gaps persist.

**Next Steps:**
- Conduct spot-checks on high-priority case clusters within 30 days.
- Partner with IT to resolve data-quality issues (e.g., `legacy_erp` mappings, vendor linkages).
- Update procurement policies to address identified control gaps.
