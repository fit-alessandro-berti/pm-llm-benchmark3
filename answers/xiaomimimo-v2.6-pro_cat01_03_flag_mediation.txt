**Framework used.** I use **interventional (randomized-analogue) indirect effects**, **stochastic queue/mediator interventions**, **separable effects** where the bundle can be split into a triage-priority component and a review-routing component, **controlled direct effects** only for components that can literally be fixed (E9), and a **policy/saturation estimand** to carry interference. I deliberately do **not** use natural direct/indirect effects: (i) "flag=1 with the mediator set to its flag=0 value" is a cross-world quantity that is not even coherent when one case's flag changes another case's queue; (ii) reviewer identity and review findings are mediator–outcome confounders *affected by* the flag (recanting-witness problem for any split of "through review" from "through escalation"); (iii) waiting-time mediators are **undefined when the stage does not occur**. Finally, "set the flag to zero" is not one intervention: suppressing display (P1), moving the threshold (changes case mix), and deleting the routing mandate (P2) are three different policies with three different effects.

Three structural features break textbook mediation. (1) Interference is built in: review and senior capacity are shared, so one case's routing changes other cases' `manual_queue_wait` and `escalation_queue_wait`; the own-case effect and the saturation effect are different estimands and standard unit-level mediation silently mixes them. (2) Exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounding: reviewer identity and the review finding are caused by the flag/routing rule and then cause escalation, cycle time and `decision_outcome`, so natural direct/indirect effects are not identified, and reviewer identity is a recanting witness on any path splitting "through review" from "through escalation". (3) Treatment–confounder feedback over time: a case's completion moves the queue state that confounds the next case's wait. Waiting-time mediators are additionally undefined when the stage never occurs (truncation), so waiting-mediated effects require stochastic queue interventions, not cross-world "set the mediator" statements.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification

| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| What is the local effect of the flag *bundle* at the margin? | sharp/fuzzy **regression discontinuity** in `risk_score` at the estimated cutoff (~0.62–0.64), local-linear, IK bandwidth, plus a donut RD; identifies the total bundle effect for marginal cases (E1, E2) | running variable only; report separately by `complexity`, `arrival_load`, hour-of-day; never adjust for post-flag variables | `risk_score` not precisely manipulable at intake; continuity of potential outcomes and of complexity/load/clock at the cutoff; no second policy discontinuity at the same cutoff | density test on `risk_score` near the cutoff (intake gaming); continuity of `complexity`, `arrival_load`, arrival clock, T1/T2 share; placebo cutoffs at 0.50 and 0.75; bandwidth sensitivity | discontinuity in `complexity`/arrival clock at the cutoff  the "flag" proxies another rule; only 2 cases lie near the cutoff in this extract  the design needs much more data before it says anything |
| Is the flagged wait gap "the case" or "the queue"? | reconstruct WIP at stage entry from the event stream, fit AFT/quantile models of `manual_queue_wait` and `escalation_queue_wait` with time-varying congestion, reviewer, hour-of-day spline; compare the flag coefficient with and without queue state | WIP at entry, `arrival_load`, hour-of-day, reviewer ID, `complexity`, `risk_score` (descriptive only) | timestamps are real work times; WIP measured without error; no unmeasured time-varying confounder (shift change, batch window) | **placebo**: future inflow (cases entering review *after* me) must not predict my wait once WIP is controlled; check whether waits cluster at fixed clock times (batching); residual autocorrelation by arrival hour | if the flag gap vanishes after WIP control  congestion, not case-specific deprioritization; if future inflow predicts my wait  priority inversion or bad timestamps |
| Does the *information* in the badge matter, apart from routing? | randomized encouragement / policy change: **shadow-flag** design — randomly suppress the badge from the triage officer for a subset of flagged cases (stratified by day × `complexity`), or a stepped-wedge rollout of protected capacity across teams; identifies E1, E2, E9 | block by day/hour and `complexity`; ITT analysis; report non-compliance | randomization respected; **no contamination** — staff cannot reconstruct the flag from the visible score | manipulation check (does triage start move only where the badge is hidden?); covariate balance; pre-trends for the stepped wedge | if hidden-badge cases are still triaged fast  the acceleration comes from the score/routing rule or staffing, not the flag display (E9 shrinks to ~0) |
| Which mediator carries the delay: occurrence or waiting? | parametric g-computation / Monte-Carlo for **interventional indirect effects** through (a) review occurrence (logistic), (b) review wait *given* occurrence (log-normal/quantile), (c) the escalation channel; re-draw one mediator block from the P1 distribution and keep the rest at P0 (E3, E4, E5, E6); fit a separable-effects model if priority and routing can be randomized separately | pre-flag L = `risk_score`, `complexity`, `arrival_load`, arrival clock; reviewer ID and review finding *simulated jointly* as exposure-induced confounders, never conditioned on as if pre-treatment | no unmeasured M–Y confounding given L and simulated history (dubious w.r.t. `U_risk`); correct mediator models; well-defined stochastic queue intervention | posterior-predictive check: the fitted model must reproduce the observed 3.5–10 h review-wait band and escalation rates 6/10 vs 2/10; compare natural vs interventional estimates as a stress test | failure to reproduce the wait distribution, or a large natural-vs-interventional gap  exposure-induced confounding is material and the decomposition should not be reported as identified |
| Is any of the association pure confounding/documentation? | **negative controls**: NC outcome = `residual-to-close` (a stage the flag should not touch); NC exposure = flag share of the *following* block (can affect me only via queues) | same L set; same model code | NC outcome genuinely unaffected by the flag (documentation burden would violate this — which is itself informative) | flag coefficient on `residual-to-close`  0 (data: 1.12 vs 1.19 h — reassuring); future-saturation coefficient on *triage service*  0 while non-zero on waits | if the flag predicts the NC outcome  hidden confounding or extra documentation work  re-label part of the "delay" as added work, not queueing |
| How much hidden risk would it take to explain this? | sensitivity/bounding: bias-function and E-value style analysis of the occurrence-mediated contrast under a latent `U_risk` affecting both review occurrence and cycle time; Manski bounds for decision quality given only Approve/Reject | `risk_score` + `complexity` + `arrival_load` as *imperfect* proxies; also residualize `risk_score` on `complexity` | bounded effects of `U_risk` (state them explicitly) | report the tipping point ("a hidden factor that doubled both review probability and cycle time would erase the effect"); vary (U, score) | if a modest `U_risk` erases the mediated effect  treat the mechanism as hypothesis, not evidence; act only on the queue-side pilot |
| What do flagged cases do to *other* cases? | **randomized saturation** design: block-level Z  {0%, 50%, 100%} of eligible flagged cases get a fast-track review slot; exposure mapping = fast-track share + WIP at my entry; estimate direct and spillover effects (E8) | cluster at block/shift; separate estimates for flagged vs non-lottery flagged vs unflagged | partial interference within blocks (plausible within a shift, doubtful across a day) | test whether spillover decays with temporal distance (if cases 6 h apart affect each other, the block is mis-specified); estimate the queue multiplier | spillover  0 with large own-case effects  review capacity is not actually shared  P3's externality argument is wrong |
| Does reviewer judgment, not case mix, drive escalation? | use scheduled roster rotation (which reviewer is on duty at review completion) as an instrument for reviewer identity (M1/M2/M3) | arrival clock, day; stratify by `complexity` | roster as-good-as-random given clock time; exclusion (roster acts only through the reviewer) | balance of `risk_score`/`complexity` across rosters; reduced form on escalation and cycle time | if M2's 6/7 escalation rate collapses to the pooled rate after adjustment  "reviewer effect" is complexity routing  P4 should target written criteria, not staff |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 keep current system | fast for flagged (0.2 h wait) | heavy: 100% of flagged into review; senior team scarce; 39% of flagged case-hours are review-queue wait | flagged  16.5 h mean; gap  +8.3 h vs flag0 | review coverage maximal, but escalation/review dependency and hurried triage remain | large: flagged cases inflate shared queues for everyone | descriptive only (n = 20; no counterfactual) | keep as the baseline condition for experiments |
| P1 suppress flag display | lose the acceleration (~+2.2 h triage wait), staffing pool may persist | unchanged if the routing engine still reads the flag; reduced if staff routing drives it | slightly worse (roughly +2 h for flagged) unless documentation effort falls | lower automation bias; possibly worse triage routing | unchanged | weak (no data on hidden-badge behaviour) | use as an *information* experiment (E1, E9), not as a performance lever |
| P2 keep priority, remove mandatory review | unchanged (still fast) | large relief: review demand falls toward the unflagged judgment rate; escalation follows | largest cycle reduction (up to ~5–9 h per flagged case) | **highest** risk: the two lowest-scored Rejects surfaced only via review + escalation; unreviewed Approve quality unknown | strongly positive | indirect (mechanism unproven; occurrence-vs-waiting not separated) | only after a non-inferiority quality pilot; not first |
| P3 keep routing, add protected review capacity | unchanged | targeted relief at the dominant mediator (6.5 h  target 2 h); escalation starts earlier, so senior queue may get hit earlier | moderate reduction (~4–5 h per flagged case) without touching occurrence | low risk, but reviewer-mix dilution if capacity is bought cheaply | mixed: displaces unflagged reviews unless capacity is truly added | moderate (waiting share is large and the queue mechanism is standard) | **pilot now** (see below) |
| P4 keep review, change escalation criteria/capacity | unchanged | relief at the second queue (25% of flagged case-hours are reviewescalation latency) | moderate, concentrated in escalated cases (~5–6 h for 30–60% of flagged) | medium-high: under-escalation of true risk unless criteria are validated against findings | positive if capacity added | weak (decision latency and queueing are not separated in the timestamps) | second pilot, after review-finding data exist |

**Pilot (P3 as a randomized fast-track lottery).** *Eligibility*: every flag=1 case arriving in a 4-hour block whose reconstructed review WIP  4 cases (or predicted review wait  4 h) and not yet past triage. *Assignment*: within-block seeded 50/50 lottery — fast-track (next protected reviewer) vs standard queue; logged as an instrument for realized `manual_queue_wait`; unflagged cases untouched. *Guardrail*: protected capacity  20% of reviewer hours and *added* (overtime/agency), never taken from the standing pool; auto-release back to the shared queue if unflagged P90 review wait exceeds its 2-week baseline by > 30 min or overtime > 15%. *Primary*: flagged `manual_queue_wait`, co-primary total cycle. *Secondary*: unflagged cycle time (spillover), escalation rate, senior-queue wait, decision concordance on a 10% blinded senior re-review, staff minutes. *Minimum data*: start/complete for triage, review, escalation, close; WIP snapshots at stage entry; reviewer ID; review finding; `risk_score`, `complexity`, `arrival_load`, arrival clock. *Stopping*: concordance < 90%; unflagged P90 wait > 4 h; overtime > 15%; 200 flagged cases accrued.

def _edge_value(obj):
    """PM4Py versions differ: a performance-DFG edge is a float or a metrics dict."""
    if isinstance(obj, dict):

parts = dur[IV_NAMES + ["residual-to-close"]].sum(axis=1, skipna=True)   # absent stages = NA, not 0
gap = (parts - dur["total cycle"]).abs().max()
print(f"identity check  sum(observed intervals) == arrival->close : "
      f"max |diff| = {gap:.3e} h  -> {'OK (no invented events)' if gap < 1e-9 else 'CHECK'}")

print("\ncase durations (hours), sorted by total cycle:")
print(dur.sort_values("total cycle")[["case", "flag", "complexity", "has_review",
                                     "has_escalation", "total cycle"]]
        .to_string(index=False, float_format=lambda x: f"{x:6.2f}"))

# ------------------------- 5/6. stage summaries + support counts per subgroup
def _cell(s):
    s = s.dropna()
    if s.empty:
        return "NA (n=0)"                       # stage structurally absent
    return f"{s.mean():.2f}/{s.median():.2f} (n={len(s)})"


GROUPS = [
    ("all cases",                            pd.Series(True, index=df.index)),
    ("flag=0",                               df["flag"].eq(0)),
    ("flag=1",                               df["flag"].eq(1)),
    ("complexity=Low",                       df["complexity"].eq("Low")),
    ("complexity=Medium",                    df["complexity"].eq("Medium")),
    ("complexity=High",                      df["complexity"].eq("High")),
    ("flag=1 & load=High",                   df["flag"].eq(1) & df["arrival_load"].eq("High")),
    ("flag=1 & load=Normal",                 df["flag"].eq(1) & df["arrival_load"].eq("Normal")),
    ("flag=1 & escalated",                   df["flag"].eq(1) & df["escalation_start"].notna()),
    ("flag=1 & not escalated",               df["flag"].eq(1) & df["escalation_start"].isna()),
    ("flag=0 & reviewed",                    df["flag"].eq(0) & df["manual_start"].notna()),
    ("flag=0 & not reviewed",                df["flag"].eq(0) & df["manual_start"].isna()),
    ("risk_score 0.55-0.75 (cutoff band)",   df["risk_score"].between(0.55, 0.75)),
]
COLS = IV_NAMES + ["residual-to-close", "total cycle"]

table, supports = [], []
for name, mask in GROUPS:
    sub = dur[mask]
    row = {"subgroup": name, "n": int(mask.sum())}
    row.update({c: _cell(sub[c]) for c in COLS})
    table.append(row)
    supports.append({"subgroup": name, "n": int(mask.sum()),
                     **{c: int(sub[c].notna().sum()) for c in COLS}})
    if mask.sum() < 5:
        warnings.warn(f"SMALL SUBGROUP '{name}': n={int(mask.sum())} -- estimates are "
                      "unstable; do not read stage contrasts as effects.")

print("\nstage decomposition (hours) -- cells = mean/median (support n)")
print(pd.DataFrame(table).to_string(index=False))
print("\nsupport counts (number of cases in which the stage interval exists)")
print(pd.DataFrame(supports).to_string(index=False))

print("\nFINAL WARNINGS")
print(" * Stage intervals bundle queueing with decision latency (e.g. "
      "'manual-done-to-escalation-start' is not pure queueing).")
print(" * Register/Close are instantaneous anchors; their 0.00 service is an artifact.")
print(" * Subgroups above differ in risk_score/complexity BY CONSTRUCTION (flag = "

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk.** If a chart audit shows `risk_score` omits a large, systematic risk dimension that reviewers detect (e.g. `U_risk` predicts both review occurrence and Reject after conditioning on score and complexity), then the "review adds delay" reading is case mix and the two lowest-scored Rejects (0.58, 0.61) become evidence that review carries real signal — I would reverse the drift toward P2 and keep review mandatory (P0/P3), treating cycle time as the secondary objective.
2. **Queue interference.** If reconstructed WIP shows the 3.5–10 h flagged review waits are actually batch/shift windows (waits cluster at fixed clock times, and future inflow predicts my wait), then adding capacity (P3) will not shorten `manual_queue_wait`; I would reverse the recommendation to scheduling reform (continuous coverage, staggered shifts, capped batch sizes) instead.
3. **Decision quality.** If blinded senior re-adjudication finds  10% discordance among unreviewed closes, or appeal/rework rates rise where review is skipped, I would reverse any endorsement of P2 and keep P0/P3 as the operating point regardless of cycle-time gains
