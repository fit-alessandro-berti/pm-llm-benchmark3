# 3. Risk assessment

**Bottleneck risk.** Calibrated assessment: possible but mainly for exception cases. Mira’s betweenness and the absence-period exception-tail increase are warning signs. However, routine claims were unaffected, and her event service time is short. She may be a necessary buffer rather than the bottleneck itself. Monitor waiting time before her events, queue length, and exception arrival rate.

**Coordination risk.** If Mira is the de facto router, losing her may scatter exception cases, cause inconsistent specialist selection, and increase formal escalations. The operational manager’s concern that removal would increase escalations and specialist overload is plausible. The coordination function should be preserved, not simply deleted.

**Key-person risk.** High betweenness, specialized knowledge, and low working-together co-occurrence suggest concentrated tacit knowledge. If she is absent or leaves, the exception process may slow. Cross-training and documented routing criteria are appropriate.

**Compliance/control risk.** The compliance reviewer’s concern is reasonable but not proof of wrongdoing. If Mira both detects problems and selects the next specialist, there is potential for uncontrolled discretion, inconsistent treatment, or bias. Regulated claims processes usually require segregation of duties, reason codes, audit trails, and periodic review. The log alone cannot show intent or misconduct.

**Workload fairness risk.** Mira has a 48% exception share and handles many short routing events. This may be invisible coordination labor that is under-recognized or unevenly distributed. Temps and vendors have lower exception shares, while specialists have high. Monitor case mix, reassignment rates, and after-hours activity if available.

**Data-quality risk.** The activity “Assign Specialist” appears in Mira’s profile but not in the supplied activity list. Working-together may understate her influence because it counts case co-occurrence, not interaction quality. Vendor waiting times are large and should not be attributed to Mira. Resource IDs may mask shared accounts, shifts, or role aliases.
