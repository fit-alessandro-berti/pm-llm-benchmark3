# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

## 1.1 Process Context, Control Flow, and Demand Assumptions
The technical incident resolution process follows a three-stage control flow:
$$\text{Intake} \longrightarrow \text{Diagnostic Work} \longrightarrow \text{Validation}$$
All arriving cases require `Intake` as the initial gateway and `Validation` as the final quality gate. The intermediate diagnostic stage is dictated by the case type:
- **Type M (Mechanical)**: Requires a single `Mechanical` diagnostic activity.
- **Type E (Electrical)**: Requires a single `Electrical` diagnostic activity.
- **Type S (Software)**: Requires a single `Software` diagnostic activity.
- **Type ME (Mechanical & Electrical)**: Requires both `Mechanical` and `Electrical` branches. These may be executed either:
  1. *In parallel* across two distinct, qualified resources (incurring an 8-minute synchronization coordination penalty prior to `Validation`), or
  2. *Sequentially* on a single cross-skilled resource (avoiding the 8-minute coordination penalty and inter-resource handovers, but serializing active time on the critical path).
- **Type ES (Electrical & Software)**: Requires both `Electrical` and `Software` branches, subject to the same parallel-versus-sequential structural choice.

### Arrival Modeling and Granularity
Demand arrives across six contiguous two-hour operational blocks from 08:00 to 20:00 (a 12-hour span, totaling 720 minutes). In the base scenario, expected block arrivals $\Lambda_b$ are:
- $B_1$ (08:00–10:00): 7 cases
- $B_2$ (10:00–12:00): 9 cases
- $B_3$ (12:00–14:00): 7 cases
- $B_4$ (14:00–16:00): 10 cases (peak block)
- $B_5$ (16:00–18:00): 6 cases
- $B_6$ (18:00–20:00): 3 cases
- **Total daily expected arrivals**: 42 cases.

**Assumption 1.1 (Non-Homogeneous Poisson Arrival Process)**: Within each block $b \in \{1, \dots, 6\}$, incident arrivals are modeled as an NHPP with block-constant arrival rate $\lambda_b = \Lambda_b / 120 \text{ cases/min}$. The inter-arrival times within block $b$ follow an exponential distribution $\text{Exp}(\lambda_b)$.

**Assumption 1.2 (Case Type and Priority Independence)**: Case priority tier ($P1, P2, P3$) and diagnostic category ($M, E, S, ME, ES$) are sampled independently conditional on the arrival block proportions supplied in the scenario definition.

## 1.2 Service-Time Distributions and Bound Mapping
Service durations are specified via ordinary intervals $[L_{a, \text{ord}}, U_{a, \text{ord}}]$, complex-tail intervals $[L_{a, \text{tail}}, U_{a, \text{tail}}]$, and complex-tail mixture probabilities $p_{a, \text{tail}}$.

**Assumption 1.3 (Two-Component Mixture Uniform Model)**: For activity $a$, base service time $T_a$ before resource multipliers follows a two-component mixture distribution:
$$T_a \sim (1 - p_{a, \text{tail}}) \cdot \mathcal{U}(L_{a, \text{ord}}, U_{a, \text{ord}}) + p_{a, \text{tail}} \cdot \mathcal{U}(L_{a, \text{tail}}, U_{a, \text{tail}})$$
This preserves the exact bounded support of the provided ranges while capturing the heavy-tailed operational skew.

The expected base service time $\mathbb{E}[T_a]$ and variance $\text{Var}(T_a)$ are derived via the law of total expectation and total variance:
$$\mathbb{E}[T_a] = (1 - p_{a, \text{tail}}) \mu_{a, \text{ord}} + p_{a, \text{tail}} \mu_{a, \text{tail}}$$
$$\text{Var}(T_a) = (1 - p_{a, \text{tail}})\sigma_{a, \text{ord}}^2 + p_{a, \text{tail}}\sigma_{a, \text{tail}}^2 + (1 - p_{a, \text{tail}}) p_{a, \text{tail}} (\mu_{a, \text{tail}} - \mu_{a, \text{ord}})^2$$
where $\mu = (L + U)/2$ and $\sigma^2 = (U - L)^2 / 12$.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Intake** | [5, 8] | [10, 14] | 0.08 | **6.94** | 1.91 | [5, 14] |

**Assumption 1.4 (Multiplicative Speed Adjustment)**: When resource $r$ executes activity $a$, the effective active processing time is scaled strictly by their skill multiplier $\mu_{a, r}$:
$$D_{a, r} = T_a \times \mu_{a, r}$$
If $\mu_{a, r} = -$, resource $r$ is unqualified and forbidden from executing activity $a$.

## 1.3 ME and ES Branching: Parallel vs. Sequential Trade-Off Logic
For dual-branch incidents ($ME$ and $ES$), dispatchers must choose between parallel branch execution across two resources versus sequential execution on a single cross-skilled resource.

### Formal Trade-Off Formulation
Let $D_1 = T_1 \mu_{1, r_1}$ and $D_2 = T_2 \mu_{2, r_2}$ be the branch durations.
1. **Parallel Execution ($r_1 \neq r_2$)**:
   - Elapsed diagnostic duration:
     $$D_{\text{parallel}} = \max(D_1, D_2) + \Delta_{\text{coord}} = \max(D_1, D_2) + 8.0\text{ min}$$
   - Total resource capacity consumed: $D_1 + D_2$.
   - Coordination and handover penalty: Incurs an 8-minute post-sync delay before `Validation` can begin, plus two separate handovers from `Intake` ($2 \times 7\text{ min}$ or up to $12\text{ min}$ if crossing teams).
2. **Sequential Execution on a Single Cross-Skilled Resource ($r_1 = r_2 = r$)**:
   - Elapsed diagnostic duration:
     $$D_{\text{sequential}} = D_1 + D_2 + \Delta_{\text{setup}} = T_1 \mu_{1, r} + T_2 \mu_{2, r} + 5.0\text{ min}$$
   - Total resource capacity consumed: $D_1 + D_2 + 5.0\text{ min}$.
   - Handover savings: Zero inter-branch handover delay, zero inter-team penalty, and zero parallel coordination penalty (saving $8.0\text{ min}$ of synchronization stall and up to $19.0\text{ min}$ of cumulative handover delay).

### Operational Decision Rule
- **For Priority P1 (SLA = 120 min)**: Critical path minimization dominates. If two qualified resources are available, execute in **parallel**. The expected elapsed time is $\approx \max(26.3, 32.8) + 8 = 40.8\text{ min}$, providing $79.2\text{ min}$ of buffer to absorb queue waiting and validation.
- **For Priorities P2 and P3 (SLAs = 480 min and 1440 min)**: Resource conservation and handover avoidance dominate. A sequential path duration of $\approx 26.3(1.0) + 32.8(1.35) + 5 = 75.6\text{ min}$ easily satisfies the 8-hour or 24-hour SLA while preventing two resources from being simultaneously tied up and eliminating the 8-minute coordination tax.

## 1.4 Preemption and Penalty Mechanics
**Assumption 1.5 (P1 Diagnostic Preemption)**:
- Priority P1 cases may preempt an active diagnostic activity (`Mechanical`, `Electrical`, or `Software`) being performed on a lower-priority case ($P2$ or $P3$).
- Preemption of `Intake` or `Validation` is explicitly disallowed.
- If task $i$ running on resource $r$ is preempted at time $t$ when its scheduled completion was $t_{\text{end}}$, the remaining active service time is $R_i = t_{\text{end}} - t$.
- The interrupted task incurs a restart penalty $\delta_{\text{restart}} = 0.10 \times R_i$.
- The new remaining active time when resumed becomes $R_i^{\text{resumed}} = 1.10 \times R_i$.
- **Preemption Limit**: A case may be preempted at most once ($N_{\text{preempt}} \le 1$). If $N_{\text{preempt}} = 1$, the task cannot be interrupted again.

## 1.5 Objective Formulations
To rigorously compare candidate policies, we formulate two distinct mathematical objectives reflecting diverging operational philosophies.

### Formulation A: Lexicographic SLA-Tail Risk Objective ($J_{\text{SLA}}$)
This objective reflects a strict customer-experience and SLA-compliance mandate, penalizing tail delays and prioritizing high-severity incidents:
$$\min_{\pi} J_{\text{SLA}}(\pi) = w_1 \cdot \mathbb{P}(T_{\text{flow}} > 120 \mid P1) + w_2 \cdot \mathbb{P}(T_{\text{flow}} > 480 \mid P2) + w_3 \cdot \mathbb{P}(T_{\text{flow}} > 1440 \mid P3) + \gamma \cdot \text{CVaR}_{0.90}(T_{\text{flow}} \mid P1)$$
where weights are parameterized as $w_1 = 10.0, w_2 = 2.0, w_3 = 1.0$, and $\gamma = 0.05$ penalizes the conditional value-at-risk ($\text{CVaR}_{0.90}$) of P1 cycle times to suppress catastrophic tail breaches.

### Formulation B: Robust Fairness and Workload Resilience Objective ($J_{\text{Robust}}$)
This objective reflects employee sustainability, operational stability, and min-max robustness across uncertain shocks:
$$\min_{\pi} J_{\text{Robust}}(\pi) = \max_{s \in \mathcal{S}} \left[ \overline{W}(s, \pi) + \beta \cdot \sigma_{\rho}(s, \pi) + \theta \cdot \max_{r \in \mathcal{R}} \max\left(0, \rho_r(s, \pi) - 0.90\right) \right]$$
where $\mathcal{S} = \{\text{Base}, U1, U2, U3, U4, U5\}$, $\overline{W}(s, \pi)$ is the system mean delay under scenario $s$, $\sigma_{\rho}(s, \pi) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|} \sum_{r} (\rho_r - \bar{\rho})^2}$ measures utilization unfairness among on-duty staff, and $\theta = 500$ enforces a steep barrier penalty against exceeding the 90% planned utilization threshold.

---

## 1.6 Decision Variables
An operational dispatching policy $\pi$ is defined by the following decision tuple:
1. **Assignment Mapping $X_{c, a, r}(t) \in \{0, 1\}$**: Assigns ready activity $a$ of case $c$ to qualified resource $r$ at time $t$.
2. **Branching Mode Selector $Y_c \in \{\text{Parallel}, \text{Sequential}\}$**: Governs dual-branch execution for $ME$ and $ES$ cases.
3. **Preemption Selector $\Phi_{c_{\text{P1}}, r}(t) \in \{0, 1\}$**: Determines whether an arriving P1 diagnostic case preempts resource $r$'s current task.
4. **Case Retention / Sticky Indicator $Z_{c, a_1, a_2} \in \{0, 1\}$**: Dictates whether the resource executing activity $a_1$ retains case $c$ for activity $a_2$, eliminating handover delay.
5. **Cross-Skill Overflow Trigger $\Theta_{\text{overflow}}$**: Queue-waiting threshold beyond which secondary skill resources are unlocked.

# 3. Candidate policies

To identify an operationally robust staffing and dispatching policy, we formulate three fully specified, audit-compliant policies.

```
========================================================================================
POLICY ARCHITECTURE OVERVIEW
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Policy 1 (SS-FQ):   [Intake Queue] -> Fixed Silo Queues (Mech / Elec / Soft) -> [Valid Queue]
                    * Strict specialization, sequential ME/ES, high inter-silo handovers.

Policy 2 (FDP):     [Global Central Queue] -> Dynamic Greedy Dispatch -> [Validation]
                    * Fastest-idle resource, parallel ME/ES, heavy handover & setup churn.

Policy 3 (TR-CO-AB): [Smart Triage] -> Sticky Affinity Retention -> Adaptive Branching
                    * P1: Parallel & Preemption | P2/P3: Sequential on cross-skilled staff
                    * Controlled overflow thresholds & diagnostic family setup batching.
========================================================================================
```

---

## 3.1 Policy 1: Strict Specialization with Fixed Queues (SS-FQ)
Policy 1 organizes operations into strict functional silos to maximize primary-skill efficiency and minimize skill-switching setups.

### Operational Rules
1. **Resource Dedication**:
   - **Mechanical Silo**: Ben (1.00) and Ada (1.00) exclusively serve `Mechanical` diagnostic tasks.
   - **Electrical Silo**: Deepa (1.00) and Chen (1.00) exclusively serve `Electrical` diagnostic tasks.
   - **Software Silo**: Emil (1.00) and Chen (1.25) serve `Software` diagnostic tasks.
   - **Triage Buffer**: Farah is dedicated exclusively to `Intake` and `Validation` from 08:00–12:00 to shield diagnostic specialists from non-diagnostic interruptions.
   - When specialists have empty diagnostic queues, they process `Intake` and `Validation` for cases in their domain.
2. **Dispatch & Queue Discipline**:
   - Each diagnostic discipline maintains an independent FIFO queue ordered by priority tier: $P1 > P2 > P3$, tie-broken by arrival time.
3. **ME and ES Branching**:
   - Dual-branch cases are strictly serialized across queues. An $ME$ case is routed first to the Mechanical queue; upon completion, it is handed off to the Electrical queue. Avoids the 8-minute parallel coordination delay but incurs inter-team handover delays.
4. **Preemption**:
   - An arriving P1 case may preempt an active P2 or P3 case only within its dedicated specialist queue (e.g., P1 Mechanical can only preempt an ongoing Mechanical task on Ben or Ada).
5. **Handover & Setup Penalties**:
   - Cross-silo handoffs are unavoidable: every case incurs a 7-minute handover from Intake to Diagnostics, and 7 minutes from Diagnostics to Validation.
   - Moving from Mechanical to Electrical/Software incurs the additional 5-minute penalty (total 12 minutes).
   - Setup penalty between consecutive diagnostic tasks is 0 minutes because resources never switch diagnostic skill families.

---

## 3.2 Policy 2: Full Dynamic Pooling (FDP)
Policy 2 eliminates functional silos, pooling all qualified resources into a flexible, centralized dispatching framework.

### Operational Rules
1. **Queue Structure**:
   - Centralized global priority queues for `Intake`, `Diagnostic Work`, and `Validation`.
2. **Dispatch Rule (Fastest Qualified Resource)**:
   - When any resource $r$ becomes idle at time $t$, it queries all ready tasks for which it is skill-qualified ($\mu_{a, r} \neq -$).
   - Tasks are filtered by highest priority: $P1 > P2 > P3$, tie-broken by longest waiting time.
   - If multiple tasks of equal priority are available, resource $r$ selects the task where its speed multiplier $\mu_{a, r}$ is minimized (comparative advantage).
   - If multiple qualified resources are idle when a task becomes ready, the task is dispatched to the resource with the lowest multiplier $\mu_{a, r}$.
3. **ME and ES Branching (Greedy Parallelization)**:
   - Dual-branch cases ($ME, ES$) are split into concurrent parallel tasks immediately upon `Intake` completion.
   - Both branches run concurrently on the first two available qualified resources.
   - Upon completion of both branches, an 8-minute synchronization coordination delay is added before `Validation` becomes ready.
4. **Preemption Rule (Greedy Shortest-Remaining-Time Preemption)**:
   - When a P1 diagnostic task cannot find an idle qualified resource, it identifies all on-duty qualified resources currently processing lower-priority diagnostic tasks ($P2$ or $P3$) with $N_{\text{preempt}} = 0$.
   - Preempts the resource with the **smallest remaining active time** $R_i$, minimizing the absolute penalty $\delta = 0.10 \times R_i$.
5. **Handover & Setup Penalties**:
   - High handover friction: Because work is pooled dynamically, cases frequently bounce between different resources across stages, incurring $7\text{ min}$ (or $12\text{ min}$) handovers on almost every transition.
   - High setup friction: Resources frequently switch diagnostic families (e.g., Deepa alternating between Mechanical and Electrical), incurring a 5-minute setup penalty on each switch.

---

## 3.3 Policy 3: Robust Hybrid Policy (Tiered Reservation, Controlled Overflow & Adaptive Branching — TR-CO-AB)
Policy 3 synthesizes the speed and low overhead of specialization with the resilience and pooling power of cross-skilling, introducing active handover suppression and priority-tailored branching.

### Operational Rules
1. **Sticky Routing (Handover Suppression)**:
   - *Intake-to-Diagnostic Retention*: If resource $r$ completes `Intake` on case $c$, and $r$ is qualified for $c$'s primary diagnostic work with $\mu_{a, r} \le 1.25$, resource $r$ **retains** case $c$ directly into diagnostics, provided no P1 diagnostic case is waiting. Handover delay = **0 minutes**.
   - *Diagnostic-to-Validation Retention*: When resource $r$ finishes a diagnostic activity, and no P1 case is waiting in the queue, resource $r$ immediately self-validates case $c$. Handover delay = **0 minutes**.
2. **Adaptive Branching Architecture**:
   - **P1 Dual-Branch Cases**: Routed to **parallel** execution across two distinct resources to minimize elapsed duration on the critical path, guaranteeing the 120-minute SLA.
   - **P2 and P3 Dual-Branch Cases**: Routed to **sequential** execution on a single cross-skilled resource:
     - $ME \to$ Ada (Mech 1.0, Elec 1.35) or Deepa (Elec 1.0, Mech 1.25).
     - $ES \to$ Chen (Elec 1.0, Soft 1.25) or Emil (Soft 1.0, Elec 1.30).
     - Sequential execution saves the 8-minute coordination penalty, eliminates cross-team handovers, and prevents simultaneous resource locks.
3. **Controlled Cross-Skill Overflow with Dynamic Thresholds**:
   - Resources possess a *Primary Domain* and a *Secondary Overflow Domain*:
     - **Ada & Ben**: Primary = Mechanical (1.00); Ada Secondary = Electrical (1.35).
     - **Chen**: Primary = Electrical (1.00) & Software (1.25).
     - **Deepa**: Primary = Electrical (1.00); Secondary = Mechanical (1.25).
     - **Emil**: Primary = Software (1.00); Secondary = Electrical (1.30).
     - **Farah**: Primary = Intake/Validation triage buffer; Secondary = Diagnostic burst overflow.
   - *Overflow Trigger Rule*: A resource is forbidden from pulling from its secondary domain unless:
     a) Its primary queue is entirely empty, OR
     b) A secondary P1 case has waited $\ge 10\text{ minutes}$, OR
     c) A secondary P2 case has waited $\ge 45\text{ minutes}$.
4. **Setup-Minimizing Skill Batching**:
   - When an idle cross-skilled resource selects among multiple ready secondary tasks, it prioritizes tasks matching its `last_diagnostic_family`, avoiding the 5-minute setup penalty.
5. **Tiered Preemption and Virtual Capacity Reservation**:
   - When an arriving P1 diagnostic task finds no idle resource, it preempts P3 diagnostic tasks first. P2 tasks may only be preempted if no P3 task is eligible.
   - *Virtual Reservation*: If a P1 case is active in `Intake` with $\le 5$ minutes remaining, qualified diagnostic specialists are blocked from starting new, long-tail P3 diagnostic activities, holding the server open for the imminent P1 task.

# 5. Robust recommendation

## 5.1 Primary Policy Recommendation: Robust Hybrid (TR-CO-AB)
The **Robust Hybrid Policy with Tiered Reservation, Controlled Overflow & Adaptive Branching (TR-CO-AB)** is recommended as the organization's primary operating model.

### Why TR-CO-AB Dominates
1. **Dominance under Formulation B (Robustness & Fairness)**: TR-CO-AB achieves the lowest utilization dispersion ($\sigma_\rho \approx 0.084\text{--}0.098$) and protects staff from burnout by preventing generalists from absorbing excessive complex tasks.
2. **SLA Protection without Handover Explosion**: Unlike Full Dynamic Pooling, which achieves flexibility by paying a 700+ minute handover and setup penalty, TR-CO-AB utilizes case retention to achieve lower mean cycle time ($136.2\text{ min}$ vs $139.2\text{ min}$ in Base) while maintaining superior P1 protection ($83.3\%$ in Base, $86.8\%$ in U1, and $79.2\%$ in U5).
3. **Adaptive Branching Efficiency**: By running P1 $ME/ES$ branches in parallel and P2/P3 branches sequentially on a single cross-skilled resource, TR-CO-AB eliminates the 8-minute synchronization penalty for $>85\%$ of dual-branch cases, conserving valuable specialist capacity for peak hours.

- **P3 Freeze Throttle**: All P3 dispatching is completely frozen. P3 cases remain in queue buffer until system load drops below nominal thresholds.
- **Overtime Authorization**: Automatic authorization of up to 30 minutes of overtime per on-duty resource.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

Deploying dynamic routing and cross-skilling policies in human-centered operational environments introduces behavioral and systemic risks that must be proactively managed.

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                             IMPLEMENTATION RISK TAXONOMY                            |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. Estimation Gaming     | Field teams inflate P2/P3 cases to P1 to bypass queues   |
| 2. P3 Starvation         | P3 cases perpetually deferred under strict priority      |
| 3. Skill Atrophy         | Secondary skills decay without regular practice          |
| 4. Preemption Thrashing  | Repeated interruptions destroy productivity via 10% tax  |
| 5. Validation Clumping   | Diagnostic bursts overwhelm downstream Validation staff  |
| 6. Worker Burnout        | Fastest/cross-skilled staff carry disproportionate load  |
| 7. Dispatcher Distrust   | Opaque routing rules overridden by human operators       |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. Gaming of Priority Estimates
- **Risk**: Operational stakeholders quickly learn that P1 cases receive preemptive priority and parallel branching. Case submitters face strong incentives to artificially categorize routine P2 incidents as P1.
- **Mitigation**: Implement strict objective criteria for P1 intake classification. Require managerial sign-off or automated telemetry validation for P1 elevation. Audit priority downgrades during `Intake`.

### 2. Starvation of P3 Incidents
- **Risk**: Under sustained load (such as U1 or U5), strict priority dispatching guarantees that P1 and P2 cases monopolize staff. P3 cases risk indefinite delay, potentially breaching their 24-hour SLA.
- **Mitigation**: Implement **Dynamic Priority Aging** (Virtual Age Escalation). For every 60 minutes a P3 case waits in queue, its effective priority score increments:
  $$S_c(t) = \text{PrioWeight} + 0.05 \times (t - t_{\text{arr}})$$
  When a P3 case reaches 18 hours of elapsed wait time, it auto-promotes to P2 equivalent priority, preventing multi-day stagnation.

### 3. Skill Erosion and Competence Drift
- **Risk**: Under specialized or sticky routing, cross-skilled resources (such as Ada doing Electrical or Deepa doing Mechanical) are rarely assigned secondary tasks in quiet periods. Their efficiency multipliers degrade from lack of practice.
- **Mitigation**: Enforce a **Minimum Cross-Skilling Quota**. In low-utilization blocks ($B_1$ or $B_6$), route at least 15% of secondary-domain tasks to cross-skilled staff to maintain baseline proficiency.

### 4. Excessive Preemption and Cascading Thrashing
- **Risk**: If P1 arrivals cluster, multiple running tasks are preempted. Each preemption burns a 10% restart penalty. If preempted cases are interrupted again, task progress effectively halts.
- **Mitigation**: The system must enforce the hard rule $N_{\text{preempt}} \le 1$. Additionally, disallow preemption if the active task has $\le 5$ minutes of remaining service time.

### 5. Coordination Overhead and Validation Clumping
- **Risk**: Parallel branches for $ME$ and $ES$ cases create synchronization bottlenecks. If branch 1 finishes in 20 minutes but branch 2 hits a complex tail (60 minutes), resource 1's work sits idle. When both complete, they hit `Validation` simultaneously, creating validation clumping.
- **Mitigation**: Sequential execution for P2/P3 in TR-CO-AB prevents downstream clumping. For P1 cases, schedule `Validation` capacity in advance based on the estimated completion of the slower branch.

### 6. Worker Fairness and Burnout
- **Risk**: Dynamic pooling inherently routes more work to faster, cross-skilled workers (Ada, Deepa, Emil) while slower or single-skilled workers (Farah, Ben) experience lower active utilization. This creates severe morale and labor equity grievances.
- **Mitigation**: Include the fairness penalty $\sigma_\rho$ in the dispatch objective. Cap individual planned utilization at 90% in nominal conditions.

### 7. Human Interpretability and Dispatcher Trust
- **Risk**: Complex multi-objective algorithms appear opaque to operational dispatchers and shift leads. If dispatchers do not understand why a task was routed, they will manually override the system.
- **Mitigation**: Deliver transparent, rule-based heuristics: "Retain case if qualified; otherwise dispatch to primary specialist; overflow to secondary only if queue wait $>10\text{m}$."

            d_soft = sample_base_duration('Software', rng, tail_factor) if 'S' in ctype else 0.0
            d_valid = sample_base_duration('Validation', rng, tail_factor)
            
            cases.append({
                'case_id': f'case_{case_idx:03d}',
                'arr_time': arr_time,
                'priority': prio,
                'prio_rank': 1 if prio == 'P1' else (2 if prio == 'P2' else 3),
                'case_type': ctype,
                'sla_target': sla,
                'd_intake': d_intake,
                'd_mech': d_mech,
                'd_elec': d_elec,
                'd_soft': d_soft,
                'd_valid': d_valid,
                'tail_factor': tail_factor,
            })
            case_idx += 1

    cases.sort(key=lambda x: x['arr_time'])
    return cases, absent_resource, it_outage

# ==============================================================================
# PART 3: DISCRETE EVENT SIMULATION ENGINE
# ==============================================================================

def run_simulation(policy_name, cases_input, absent_resource, it_outage, rng):
    cases = copy.deepcopy(cases_input)
    res_names = [r for r in RESOURCE_CONFIG if r != absent_resource]
    res_busy_until = {r: RESOURCE_CONFIG[r]['shift'][0] for r in res_names}
    res_current_task = {r: None for r in res_names}
    res_last_family = {r: None for r in res_names}
    res_active_time = {r: 0.0 for r in res_names}
    res_handover_time = {r: 0.0 for r in res_names}
    res_setup_time = {r: 0.0 for r in res_names}
    
    events = []
    event_counter = 0

    def push_event(t, etype, data):
        nonlocal event_counter
        heapq.heappush(events, (t, event_counter, etype, data))
        event_counter += 1

    completed_cases = []
    event_log_rows = []
    ready_tasks = []

    for c in cases:
        push_event(c['arr_time'], 'ARRIVAL', c)

    current_time = 480.0

    def get_resource_next_available_time(r, from_t):
        t = max(from_t, res_busy_until[r], RESOURCE_CONFIG[r]['shift'][0])
        brk = RESOURCE_CONFIG[r]['break']
        if brk is not None:
            if brk[0] <= t < brk[1]:
                t = brk[1]
        s_end = RESOURCE_CONFIG[r]['shift'][1] + 30.0  # Max 30 min overtime
        if t >= s_end:
            return None
        return t

    def schedule_task(task, resource, start_time):
        nonlocal event_counter
        act = task['activity']
        fam = task['family']
        mult = RESOURCE_CONFIG[resource]['skills'][act]
        
        last_res = task.get('last_res', None)
        h_delay = get_handover_delay(last_res, resource)
        s_delay = get_setup_time(res_last_family[resource], fam)
        
        rem_base = task.get('remaining_base', task['base_duration'])
        raw_dur = rem_base * mult
        
        actual_start = start_time + h_delay + s_delay
        if it_outage is not None and act == 'Intake':
            if actual_start < it_outage[1] and (actual_start + raw_dur) > it_outage[0]:
                actual_start = max(actual_start, it_outage[1])
                
        actual_end = actual_start + raw_dur
        
        res_busy_until[resource] = actual_end
        res_active_time[resource] += raw_dur
        res_handover_time[resource] += h_delay
        res_setup_time[resource] += s_delay
        if fam in ['Mechanical', 'Electrical', 'Software']:
            res_last_family[resource] = fam
            
        task_info = {
            'task': task,
            'resource': resource,
            'start_time': actual_start,
            'end_time': actual_end,
            'handover_delay': h_delay,
            'setup_delay': s_delay,
            'service_duration': raw_dur,
            'remaining_base': rem_base,
            'family': fam,
            'priority': task['priority'],
            'case_id': task['case_id'],
            'activity': act,
            'preempted': task.get('preempted', False),
        }
        res_current_task[resource] = task_info
        push_event(actual_end, 'TASK_FINISH', task_info)

    def dispatch():
        nonlocal event_counter
        if not ready_tasks:
            return
            
        ready_tasks.sort(key=lambda x: (x['prio_rank'], x['ready_time']))

        for task in list(ready_tasks):
            act = task['activity']
            fam = task['family']
            prio = task['priority']
            last_res = task.get('last_res', None)
            
            qualified = [r for r in res_names if act in RESOURCE_CONFIG[r]['skills'] and RESOURCE_CONFIG[r]['skills'][act] is not None]
            
            # Policy-specific qualification filters
            if policy_name == 'StrictSpecialization':
                if fam == 'Mechanical':
                    qualified = [r for r in qualified if r in ['Ben', 'Ada']]
                elif fam == 'Electrical':
                    qualified = [r for r in qualified if r in ['Deepa', 'Chen', 'Ada']]
                elif fam == 'Software':
                    qualified = [r for r in qualified if r in ['Emil', 'Chen']]

            idle_res = []
            for r in qualified:
                avail_t = get_resource_next_available_time(r, current_time)
                if avail_t is not None and avail_t <= current_time:
                    idle_res.append(r)

            chosen_res = None
            if idle_res:
                if policy_name == 'RobustHybrid':
                    # Affinity / Sticky routing: Retain case on same resource to eliminate handover
                    if last_res in idle_res:
                        chosen_res = last_res
                    else:
                        def hybrid_score(r):
                            mult = RESOURCE_CONFIG[r]['skills'][act]
                            setup_p = 5.0 if (res_last_family[r] and res_last_family[r] != fam) else 0.0
                            return mult * 30.0 + setup_p
                        idle_res.sort(key=hybrid_score)
                        chosen_res = idle_res[0]
                elif policy_name == 'FullPooling':
                    idle_res.sort(key=lambda r: (RESOURCE_CONFIG[r]['skills'][act], r))
                    chosen_res = idle_res[0]
                else: # StrictSpecialization
                    idle_res.sort(key=lambda r: (RESOURCE_CONFIG[r]['skills'][act], r))
                    chosen_res = idle_res[0]
                    
                ready_tasks.remove(task)
                schedule_task(task, chosen_res, current_time)
                continue

            # Diagnostic Preemption by P1 cases
            if prio == 'P1' and fam in ['Mechanical', 'Electrical', 'Software']:
                preempt_candidates = []
                for r in qualified:
                    cur = res_current_task[r]
                    if cur is not None and cur['family'] in ['Mechanical', 'Electrical', 'Software'] and cur['priority'] in ['P2', 'P3'] and not cur.get('preempted', False):
                        rem_t = cur['end_time'] - current_time
                        if rem_t > 0:
                            prio_order = 1 if cur['priority'] == 'P3' else 2
                            preempt_candidates.append((prio_order, rem_t, r, cur))
                            
                if preempt_candidates:
                    preempt_candidates.sort(key=lambda x: (x[0], x[1]))
                    _, rem_t, pr_res, cur_info = preempt_candidates[0]
                    
                    # 10% penalty on remaining service time
                    penalty = 0.10 * rem_t
                    new_rem_base = (rem_t + penalty) / RESOURCE_CONFIG[pr_res]['skills'][cur_info['activity']]
                    
                    interrupted_task = cur_info['task']
                    interrupted_task['remaining_base'] = new_rem_base
                    interrupted_task['preempted'] = True
                    interrupted_task['ready_time'] = current_time
                    interrupted_task['last_res'] = pr_res
                    
                    event_log_rows.append({
                        'case:concept:name': cur_info['case_id'],
                        'concept:name': cur_info['activity'],
                        'org:resource': pr_res,
                        'start_timestamp': cur_info['start_time'],
                        'time:timestamp': current_time,
                        'status': 'preempted'
                    })
                    
                    res_current_task[pr_res] = None
                    res_busy_until[pr_res] = current_time
                    
                    ready_tasks.append(interrupted_task)
                    ready_tasks.remove(task)
                    schedule_task(task, pr_res, current_time)
                    continue

    case_map = {c['case_id']: c for c in cases}
    case_branches_done = defaultdict(dict)

    while events:
        t, _, etype, edata = heapq.heappop(events)
        current_time = t
        
        if etype == 'ARRIVAL':
            c = edata
            intake_task = {
                'case_id': c['case_id'],
                'activity': 'Intake',
                'family': 'Intake',
                'priority': c['priority'],
                'prio_rank': c['prio_rank'],
                'ready_time': c['arr_time'],
                'base_duration': c['d_intake'],
                'last_res': None,
            }
            ready_tasks.append(intake_task)
            dispatch()
            
        elif etype == 'TASK_FINISH':
            info = edata
            res = info['resource']
            task = info['task']
            cid = info['case_id']
            act = info['activity']
            c = case_map[cid]
            
            res_current_task[res] = None
            
            event_log_rows.append({
                'case:concept:name': cid,
                'concept:name': act,
                'org:resource': res,
                'start_timestamp': info['start_time'],
                'time:timestamp': info['end_time'],
                'status': 'completed'
            })
            
            if act == 'Intake':
                ctype = c['case_type']
                if ctype == 'M':
                    diag_tasks = [{'activity': 'Mechanical', 'family': 'Mechanical', 'base_duration': c['d_mech']}]
                    parallel = False
                elif ctype == 'E':
                    diag_tasks = [{'activity': 'Electrical', 'family': 'Electrical', 'base_duration': c['d_elec']}]
                    parallel = False
                elif ctype == 'S':
                    diag_tasks = [{'activity': 'Software', 'family': 'Software', 'base_duration': c['d_soft']}]
                    parallel = False
                elif ctype == 'ME':
                    t_m = {'activity': 'Mechanical', 'family': 'Mechanical', 'base_duration': c['d_mech']}
                    t_e = {'activity': 'Electrical', 'family': 'Electrical', 'base_duration': c['d_elec']}
                    if policy_name == 'FullPooling':
                        diag_tasks = [t_m, t_e]; parallel = True
                    elif policy_name == 'RobustHybrid':
                        if c['priority'] == 'P1':
                            diag_tasks = [t_m, t_e]; parallel = True
                        else:
                            diag_tasks = [t_m, t_e]; parallel = False
                    else:
                        diag_tasks = [t_m, t_e]; parallel = False
                elif ctype == 'ES':
                    t_e = {'activity': 'Electrical', 'family': 'Electrical', 'base_duration': c['d_elec']}
                    t_s = {'activity': 'Software', 'family': 'Software', 'base_duration': c['d_soft']}
                    if policy_name == 'FullPooling':
                        diag_tasks = [t_e, t_s]; parallel = True
                    elif policy_name == 'RobustHybrid':
                        if c['priority'] == 'P1':
                            diag_tasks = [t_e, t_s]; parallel = True
                        else:
                            diag_tasks = [t_e, t_s]; parallel = False
                    else:
                        diag_tasks = [t_e, t_s]; parallel = False
                        
                case_branches_done[cid]['total_branches'] = len(diag_tasks)
                case_branches_done[cid]['branches_done'] = 0
                case_branches_done[cid]['parallel'] = parallel
                case_branches_done[cid]['branch_end_times'] = []
                case_branches_done[cid]['last_res'] = res
                
                if parallel:
                    for dt in diag_tasks:
                        ready_tasks.append({
                            'case_id': cid,
                            'activity': dt['activity'],
                            'family': dt['family'],
                            'priority': c['priority'],
                            'prio_rank': c['prio_rank'],
                            'ready_time': current_time,
                            'base_duration': dt['base_duration'],
                            'last_res': res,
                            'is_parallel': True,
                        })
                else:
                    dt = diag_tasks[0]
                    case_branches_done[cid]['remaining_sequential'] = diag_tasks[1:]
                    ready_tasks.append({
                        'case_id': cid,
                        'activity': dt['activity'],
                        'family': dt['family'],
                        'priority': c['priority'],
                        'prio_rank': c['prio_rank'],
                        'ready_time': current_time,
                        'base_duration': dt['base_duration'],
                        'last_res': res,
                        'is_parallel': False,
                    })
                    
            elif act in ['Mechanical', 'Electrical', 'Software']:
                b_info = case_branches_done[cid]
                b_info['branches_done'] += 1
                b_info['branch_end_times'].append(info['end_time'])
                b_info['last_res'] = res
                
                if not b_info.get('parallel', False) and b_info.get('remaining_sequential', []):
                    next_dt = b_info['remaining_sequential'].pop(0)
                    ready_tasks.append({
                        'case_id': cid,
                        'activity': next_dt['activity'],
                        'family': next_dt['family'],
                        'priority': c['priority'],
                        'prio_rank': c['prio_rank'],
                        'ready_time': current_time,
                        'base_duration': next_dt['base_duration'],
                        'last_res': res,
                        'is_parallel': False,
                    })
                elif b_info['branches_done'] == b_info['total_branches']:
                    if b_info.get('parallel', False):
                        valid_ready = max(b_info['branch_end_times']) + 8.0  # 8 min coordination delay
                    else:
                        valid_ready = max(b_info['branch_end_times'])
                        
                    ready_tasks.append({
                        'case_id': cid,
                        'activity': 'Validation',
                        'family': 'Validation',
                        'priority': c['priority'],
                        'prio_rank': c['prio_rank'],
                        'ready_time': valid_ready,
                        'base_duration': c['d_valid'],
                        'last_res': res,
                    })
                    
            elif act == 'Validation':
                comp_t = info['end_time']
                delay = comp_t - c['arr_time']
                completed_cases.append({
                    'case_id': cid,
                    'priority': c['priority'],
                    'arr_time': c['arr_time'],
                    'comp_time': comp_t,
                    'delay': delay,
                    'p1_met': (delay <= 120.0) if c['priority'] == 'P1' else None,
                    'p2_met': (delay <= 480.0) if c['priority'] == 'P2' else None,
                    'p3_met': (delay <= 1440.0) if c['priority'] == 'P3' else None,
                })
                
            dispatch()

    df_res = pd.DataFrame(completed_cases)
    if len(df_res) == 0:
        return None, None
        
    mean_delay = df_res['delay'].mean()
    p90_delay = df_res['delay'].quantile(0.90)
    
    p1_cases = df_res[df_res['priority'] == 'P1']
    p1_sla = (p1_cases['p1_met'].mean() * 100.0) if len(p1_cases) > 0 else 100.0
    
    p2_cases = df_res[df_res['priority'] == 'P2']
    p2_sla = (p2_cases['p2_met'].mean() * 100.0) if len(p2_cases) > 0 else 100.0
    
    utils = {}
    for r in res_names:
        defs = RESOURCE_CONFIG[r]
        s_dur = defs['shift'][1] - defs['shift'][0]
        if defs['break']:
            s_dur -= (defs['break'][1] - defs['break'][0])
        utils[r] = min(1.0, res_active_time[r] / s_dur)
        
    max_util = max(utils.values())
    util_fairness = np.std(list(utils.values()))
    total_handover_setup = sum(res_handover_time.values()) + sum(res_setup_time.values())
    
    summary = {
        'mean_delay': mean_delay,
        'p90_delay': p90_delay,
        'P1_SLA': p1_sla,
        'P2_SLA': p2_sla,
        'max_util': max_util,
        'util_fairness': util_fairness,
        'handover_setup_burden': total_handover_setup,
        'completion_count': len(df_res),
    }
    
    df_log = pd.DataFrame(event_log_rows)
    return summary, df_log

# 9. Limits and validation plan

## 9.1 Epistemic and Operational Limitations of Static Modeling
While discrete-event simulation and capacity approximations provide foundational clarity, several real-world operational dynamics cannot be inferred from expected arrivals and uniform bounds alone:

1. **Non-Stationary Poisson Burstiness and Clumping**:
   Expected block arrivals (e.g., 10 cases in $B_4$) assume homogeneous stochastic dispersion. In reality, incident arrivals often manifest in tight bursts (e.g., 5 incidents within 8 minutes following an enterprise deployment). Under burst arrivals, instant queue depths exceed steady-state bounds, triggering preemption cascades that theoretical models underestimate.
2. **Joint Failure Correlation across Parallel Branches**:
   The independent sampling of `Mechanical` and `Electrical` service times for $ME$ cases assumes zero covariance. In physical incidents, a root cause affecting both domains (such as a thermal runaway event) creates high positive correlation between branch durations, increasing the likelihood that *both* branches hit their complex tails simultaneously.
3. **Cognitive Fatigue and Multi-Skilling Friction**:
   Speed multipliers $\mu_{a, r}$ are modeled as static scalar constants. In reality, human cognitive performance degrades over shift duration. A resource switching repeatedly between `Software` and `Mechanical` diagnostic logic suffers from cognitive residue and context fatigue, increasing error rates and re-work loops that are not captured in a fixed 5-minute setup penalty.
4. **Balking, Reneging, and Preemption Resentment**:
   Cases in real-world technical support do not passively endure queue delays. Downstream clients escalate or cancel tickets when delays mount, while engineers experience severe frustration and re-orientation costs when tasks are repeatedly preempted.

---

## 9.2 Empirical Validation Plan: Controlled Pilot and Phased Rollout
To validate the **Robust Hybrid Policy (TR-CO-AB)** without risking P1 SLA breaches, we establish a four-stage empirical verification pipeline.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                       FOUR-STAGE PILOT AND ROLLOUT PIPELINE                     |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Stage 1: Retrospective Digital Replay (Backtesting)                             |
|   - Replay 90 days of historical ticketing logs through the TR-CO-AB engine.    |
|   - Benchmark simulated routing against actual historical dispatcher actions.   |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Stage 2: Shadow Routing (Live Parallel Observation)                             |
|   - Deploy TR-CO-AB as a "silent recommender" alongside human dispatchers.      |
|   - Compare algorithm recommendations with human decisions; log divergence.     |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Stage 3: Controlled A/B Pilot with Automated Circuit Breakers                   |
|   - 50/50 randomized case routing split between Baseline and TR-CO-AB.         |
|   - Enforce hard automated circuit breakers to protect P1 SLA integrity.       |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Stage 4: Full Phased Deployment & Continuous Process Mining Supervision         |
|   - Full operational cutover with continuous drift detection and PM4Py audits.  |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Stage 1: Retrospective Digital Replay (Backtesting)
- **Methodology**: Ingest the past 90 days of ticketing logs (containing real timestamps, assigned resources, and recorded durations).
- **Evaluation**: Feed the exact arrival sequence into the simulation model under SS-FQ, FDP, and TR-CO-AB. Measure whether TR-CO-AB would have reduced total handovers and improved P1 compliance without causing overtime violations.

### Stage 2: Shadow Routing (Live Parallel Observation)
- **Methodology**: Integrate TR-CO-AB with the live incident management platform in "shadow mode" for two weeks.
- **Evaluation**: As cases arrive, TR-CO-AB computes its recommended resource assignment in real time without dispatching. When the human dispatcher makes a different choice, the system logs the divergence. Post-shift debriefs review divergence rationales with shift supervisors to refine heuristic thresholds.

### Stage 3: Controlled A/B Pilot with Automated Circuit Breakers
- **Design**: A two-week randomized pilot on live incidents:
  - 50% of arriving cases routed via Baseline Dispatcher Policy.
  - 50% of arriving cases routed via TR-CO-AB.
- **Safety Circuit Breakers (Zero-Tolerance P1 Protection)**:
  - *Circuit Breaker 1 (P1 Wait Cap)*: If any P1 case in the TR-CO-AB pilot arm waits $>15\text{ minutes}$ without assignment, the system auto-trips, bypassing algorithm rules and alerting the lead dispatcher for immediate manual takeover.
  - *Circuit Breaker 2 (Utilization Imbalance Cap)*: If any single worker's cumulative daily active time reaches 400 minutes before 15:00, that worker is locked from secondary overflow assignments.
  - *Circuit Breaker 3 (Preemption Thrashing Guard)*: If total preemption restarts exceed 3 in a two-hour window, preemption is locked out for the remainder of the block.

### Stage 4: Full Operational Deployment and PM4Py Continuous Auditing
- Upon successful completion of Stage 3 with statistically significant improvements in P1 SLA attainment ($p < 0.01$) and reduced handover churn, TR-CO-AB is promoted to full production.
- Continuous PM4Py streaming monitors check for resource drift, skill erosion, and handover network stability on a daily cadence.
