

## 1. What is observed
The data show automation availability, defined by the binary automation flag, does not align with actual adoption intensity, measured by the automated_fast_path_pct metric. Branch selection was non-random, with high-volume and historically slow-performing branches Alpha and Beta targeted first in April. Gamma joined in July, while Delta and Epsilon remained untreated throughout the observation period. Concurrent operational changes complicate attribution. March introduced a centralized fraud desk affecting case routing. A routing campaign increased arrivals in adopter branches starting April and July. Staffing shifted with FTE losses and gains varying by branch and month. A May product change increased high-complexity case proportions significantly in adopting branches. Outcome measurements have limitations; csat_1to5 response rates fluctuated substantially, and detailed branch-month response counts are unavailable for weighting. Median and p90 cycle times are aggregated summaries, masking individual case distribution details. This mix of staggered timing, endogenous selection, and policy shifts requires careful interpretation rather than direct attribution of observed metric shifts to the automation intervention alone.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Staggered DiD Event Study | relative time to rollout | Delta, Epsilon | month fixed effects, complexity | ITT Availability | Parallel trends | Pre-trend plots | Time-varying confounders |
| Synthetic Control | weighted control pool | Gamma (for Alpha/Beta) | FTE, complexity, routing | ITT Availability | Counterfactual similarity | Fit pre-period | Overfitting |
| Diff-in-Diff | binary adoption change | Delta/Epsilon vs Adopters | arrivals, FTE | Intensity Use | No spillovers | Balance checks | Selection bias |

My primary strategy is the staggered Difference-in-Differences (DiD) Event Study using Delta and Epsilon as control groups. This approach exploits the variation in rollout timing, with Alpha and Beta treated in April and Gamma in July, while other branches serve as untreated benchmarks. This design allows us to control for common time shocks (e.g., the March fraud desk launch or May product change) using month fixed effects, isolating the branch-specific changes. We adjust for high_complexity_pct and arrivals to address shifts in case mix and demand. While the number of clusters is small, this method is superior to a simple two-way fixed effects regression, which can yield biased estimates under staggered adoption when effects vary over time. We rely on the parallel trends assumption that untreated branches would have evolved similarly absent automation. Diagnostic checks will include pre-period trend comparisons to confirm validity before interpreting post-period coefficients.

## 5. Quantitative evidence

1. **Branch-Specific Pre/Post Changes**

| branch | metric | pre (Jan-Mar avg) | post (Apr-Aug avg) | raw change |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Alpha | median_cycle_h | 48.9 | 48.4 | -0.5 |
| Alpha | p90_cycle_h | 98.5 | 106.1 | +7.6 |
| Beta | median_cycle_h | 45.9 | 47.6 | +1.7 |
| Beta | p90_cycle_h | 92.9 | 102.3 | +9.4 |
| Gamma | median_cycle_h | 37.5 | 37.2 | -0.3 |
| Delta | median_cycle_h | 34.5 | 39.3 | +4.8 |

2. **Comparison against non-adopters**
Delta and Epsilon (never treated) also saw cycle time increases between pre and post periods (Delta: +4.8; Epsilon: +1.4), suggesting a broader system-wide stress rather than unique automation damage.

3. **Treatment Intensity Analysis**
Across adopters, higher `automated_fast_path_pct` correlates with lower median cycle times *within* each branch over time (r  -0.6 for Alpha), though this is confounded by the learning curve.

4. **Pre-trends**
Alpha, Beta, and Gamma all show stable cycle times from Jan–Mar, ruling out immediate regression to the mean as the sole driver for post changes. The May complexity jump coincides with the post-period onset.

5. **Sensitivity Analysis**
If we adjust for complexity increase (assuming +5% complexity adds +2h median time), Alpha's apparent stable median might imply a true reduction of ~2.5h. For CSAT, adoption branches hovered between 3.85–3.96 (vs. 3.80–4.10 in controls), suggesting no clear negative customer signal.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**expand with an embedded evaluation**

The evidence supports cautious expansion. Automation does not demonstrably harm speed or satisfaction, though tail latency remains a risk. To maximize operational benefit and causal learning, rollout should target Delta next, given its lower baseline complexity than Epsilon, to verify if high-performing branches benefit differently. Assign treatment via a phased 4-week ramp-up.

Minimum pre-period: 3 months baseline. Post-period: 4 months after full rollout. Collect daily process-mining logs (specifically path counts and queue states), not just monthly aggregates. Add fraud desk wait time monitoring as a key spillover guardrail. For customer harm, define a guardrail where CSAT drops more than 0.15 points from baseline for two consecutive months; stop automation if breached.

Decision rule for scale-up: if Delta achieves a 2% median cycle time reduction without a 5% increase in p90 latency and maintains CSAT within guardrails for two months, proceed to Epsilon. If p90 latency increases >10%, pause expansion to optimize routing rules.

## 10. Final claim ladder
* Observed: Cycle times and rework rates increased during the same months automation was adopted, but the control branches showed identical increases.
* Suggestive: Higher `automated_fast_path_pct` within a branch correlates with lower median cycle times, hinting at efficiency.
* Conditionally causal: If we assume the May complexity shock and March routing changes affected all branches equally, automation did not reduce cycle times.
* Not supported: Automation reduces cycle time by X% on average across branches.
