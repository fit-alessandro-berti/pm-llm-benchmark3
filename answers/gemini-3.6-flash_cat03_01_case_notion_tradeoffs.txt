## 1. Analytical assumptions

To analyze the provided OCEL 2.0 snapshot, we establish the following formal analytical definitions and assumptions:

*   **Case Notion**: A projection function $\pi_T: \mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{O}_T)$ that maps events to instances of a chosen target object type $T \in \{\text{Order}, \text{Item}, \text{Package}, \text{Delivery}, \text{Invoice}, \text{Payment}\}$. A "case" for an object $o \in \mathcal{O}_T$ is the sequence of events directly related to $o$, ordered chronologically by timestamp.
*   **Convergence**: Occurs when an event $e \in \mathcal{E}$ is related to multiple distinct objects of the same chosen lead object type $T$ (e.g., $e13$ related to both $O2$ and $O3$ when $T = \text{Order}$). In traditional flattening, convergence forces event $e$ to be replicated across multiple case traces, introducing duplicate event counts and artificial cross-case synchronization points.
*   **Divergence**: Occurs when a single parent object of type $T$ relates to multiple downstream child objects that undergo concurrent or branching lifecycles (e.g., $O1$ splitting into $I11$ and $I12$, packed into $P1$ and $P2$, and dispatched via $D1$ and $D2$). In traditional flattening on $T = \text{Order}$, divergence forces parallel downstream activities into a single linear sequence, generating false precedence edges and spurious Direct-Follows Graph (DFG) loops.
*   **Treatment of Copied Shared Events**: When an event relates to $k$ objects of type $T$, flattening replicates that event $k$ times across $k$ traces. We treat these copied shared events as **both** empirical evidence of operational coupling/batching and a source of statistical distortion. They reveal cross-object dependencies but artificially inflate activity frequencies, skew throughput times, and corrupt performance aggregations if analyzed naively.
*   **Analytical Transformations**:
    1.  *Direct E2O Flattening (Baseline)*: Includes only events explicitly tied to object $o$ in the event-to-object (E2O) relation set.
    2.  *Explicit Propagated Relation Flattening*: Evaluates the impact of transitively inheriting E2O links through object-to-object (O2O) relations (e.g., propagating $I11 \xrightarrow{\text{contains}} O1 \xrightarrow{\text{bills}} V1$ so that $I11$ inherits Invoice events $e18$ and $e21$).
    3.  *Event Fractional Downweighting*: Adjusts event counts in aggregated metrics by applying a weight $w(e) = \frac{1}{|\pi_T(e)|}$ to eliminate frequency inflation.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

### Distortion 1: Cross-Order Package Packaging ($P3$ in $e13$)
*   **Affected Flattening**: Order Flattening.
*   **Cites**: Objects $O2$, $O3$, $P3$, $I21$, $I31$; Event $e13$ (`Pack Package` at 10:12).
*   **Description**: $P3$ packs item $I21$ (from $O2$) and item $I31$ (from $O3$). Event $e13$ directly links $\{O2, O3, I21, I31, P3\}$. In Order flattening, $e13$ is replicated in Case $O2$ and Case $O3$. In the discovered DFG, this duplicates the activity count of `Pack Package` for $P3$ and creates artificial direct-follows edges: $e07 (\text{Add Item } I21) \to e13$ in $O2$, and $e08 (\text{Add Item } I31) \to e13$ in $O3$.
*   **Classification**: **Harmful** for capacity planning because it overcounts warehouse packaging operations by 100% (counting 1 physical packing operation as 2). **Informative** for identifying multi-order batching dependencies.

### Distortion 2: Cross-Order Package Packaging ($P4$ in $e14$)
*   **Affected Flattening**: Item Flattening.
*   **Cites**: Objects $I32$ (from $O3$), $I41$ (from $O4$), $P4$; Event $e14$ (`Pack Package` at 10:20).
*   **Description**: $P4$ packs $I32$ and $I41$. In Item flattening, $e14$ appears in Case $I32$ and Case $I41$. The duration between $e10$ ($\text{Add Item } I41$ at 09:25) and $e14$ (10:20) is 55 minutes, while for $I32$ ($e09$ at 09:23) it is 57 minutes. When aggregated, the DFG edge $\text{Add Item} \to \text{Pack Package}$ gets a frequency of 2.
*   **Performance Bias**: This masks the fact that item $I41$ sat idle in the packing area waiting for item $I32$ from a completely different order ($O3$) to arrive before $P4$ could be sealed.
*   **Classification**: **Harmful**. It conceals cross-order operational dependencies and masks hold-up times caused by cross-order item synchronization.

### Distortion 3: Consolidated Delivery Trip ($D2$ in $e16$ and $e24$)
*   **Affected Flattening**: Order Flattening.
*   **Cites**: Objects $O1$, $O2$, $O3$, $P2$, $P3$, $D2$; Events $e16$ (`Dispatch` at 11:05) and $e24$ (`Confirm Delivery` at 16:00).
*   **Description**: $D2$ carries $P2$ (belonging to $O1$) and $P3$ (belonging to $O2$ and $O3$). Events $e16$ and $e24$ relate to $\{O1, O2, O3, I12, I21, I31, P2, P3, D2\}$. Replicating $e16$ and $e24$ across $O1$, $O2$, and $O3$ causes $O1$ to contain *two* dispatch events ($e15$ for $D1$ at 11:00, $e16$ for $D2$ at 11:05) and *two* delivery confirmation events ($e23$ at 12:30, $e24$ at 16:00).
*   **DFG Edge & Variant Distortion**: Forces $O1$ into an artificial sequential trace containing $Dispatch \to Dispatch$ and $Confirm \to Confirm$ self-loops, or false concurrency paths. It inflates $D2$'s dispatch frequency from 1 actual truck departure to 3 event executions.
*   **Classification**: **Harmful** for fleet efficiency and transport capacity evaluation; **Informative** for customer-facing order tracking.

### Distortion 4: Consolidated Invoicing ($V2$ in $e19$)
*   **Affected Flattening**: Order Flattening.
*   **Cites**: Objects $O2$, $O3$, $V2$; Event $e19$ (`Issue Invoice` at 12:05).
*   **Description**: Invoice $V2$ bills $O2$ and $O3$ jointly. In Order flattening, $e19$ is injected into both $O2$ and $O3$.
*   **Performance Bias**: The calculated billing delay for $O2$ is 3h 03m ($e02$ at 09:02 to $e19$ at 12:05), and for $O3$ is 3h 01m ($e03$ at 09:04 to $e19$ at 12:05). The DFG presents these as two independent invoicing actions. This hides the managerial decision to hold back billing for $O2$ until $O3$ was ready, skewing individual order billing SLA metrics.
*   **Classification**: **Context-dependent**. Useful for tracking AR debt per order, but harmful for measuring administrative billing workload.

### Distortion 5: Installment Payments ($M2$ and $M3$ for $V2$)
*   **Affected Flattening**: Order Flattening vs. Invoice Flattening.
*   **Cites**: Objects $O2$, $O3$, $V2$, $M2$, $M3$; Events $e22$ (`Record Payment` at 12:40) and $e25$ (`Record Payment` at 09:00 next day).
*   **Description**: $V2$ is partially paid by $M2$ ($e22$) and fully settled by $M3$ ($e25$). In Order flattening, *both* $e22$ and $e25$ are duplicated into Case $O2$ and Case $O3$, producing 4 payment event references across the two orders.
*   **DFG Variant Distortion**: Introduces an explicit loop edge $Record\ Payment \to Record\ Payment$ in both $O2$ and $O3$. Conversely, in Invoice flattening (Case $V2$), $e22$ and $e25$ naturally capture the linear two-step installment lifecycle: $Issue\ Invoice (e19) \to Record\ Payment (e22) \to Record\ Payment (e25)$.
*   **Classification**: **Harmful** in Order flattening (multiplies transaction counts and distorts payment velocity); **Informative** in Invoice flattening.

### Distortion 6: Payment Near-Singleton / Singleton Lifecycle
*   **Affected Flattening**: Payment Flattening.
*   **Cites**: Objects $M1$, $M2$, $M3$, $M4$; Events $e21$, $e22$, $e25$, $e26$.
*   **Description**: Flattening on Payment yields four cases ($M1$, $M2$, $M3$, $M4$), each containing exactly one event (`Record Payment`).
*   **DFG Distortion**: Complete structural breakdown. The process model collapses into a single node with no incoming or outgoing edges and zero transition flow. It truncates 100% of the preceding fulfillment flow (creation, picking, packing, dispatch, delivery, invoicing).
*   **Classification**: **Harmful** for process discovery and behavioral modeling.

### Distortion 7: Delivery Sub-process Lifecycle Truncation
*   **Affected Flattening**: Delivery Flattening.
*   **Cites**: Objects $D1$, $D2$, $D3$; Events $e15$, $e16$, $e17$, $e23$, $e24$, $e27$.
*   **Description**: Flattening on Delivery produces three isolated traces: $D1=[e15, e23]$, $D2=[e16, e24]$, and $D3=[e17, e27]$.
*   **Lifecycle Truncation**: Completely severs the delivery leg from order placement ($e01$–$e04$), packaging ($e11$–$e14$), and financial settlement ($e18$–$e26$).
*   **Performance Distortion**: $D2$'s transit duration (11:05 to 16:00 = 4h 55m) is isolated from why $D2$ departed at 11:05. It conceals that $D2$ was waiting for $P3$ (which depended on $O3$'s item $I31$) and $P2$ (from $O1$).
*   **Classification**: **Harmful** for root-cause latency analysis; **Informative** strictly for carrier transit SLA analysis.

### Distortion 8: Direct E2O Item Lifecycle Truncation (Missing Financial Events)
*   **Affected Flattening**: Item Flattening (under Direct E2O rules).
*   **Cites**: Objects $I11$, $I12$, $I21$, $I31$, $I32$, $I41$; Events $e18$–$e22$, $e25$, $e26$.
*   **Description**: The E2O relation table directly links invoicing ($e18$–$e20$) and payment ($e21$, $e22$, $e25$, $e26$) events to `Order`, `Invoice`, and `Payment` objects, but *not* to `Item` objects.
*   **Lifecycle Truncation**: Under strict direct E2O flattening, an Item trace (e.g., $I11 = [e05, e11, e15, e23]$) terminates at `Confirm Delivery`. The financial lifecycle is entirely missing.
*   **Interpretive Trap**: Analyzing Item flattening alone makes the business appear to deliver goods without ever billing or collecting revenue from customers.
*   **Classification**: **Harmful** for holistic Order-to-Cash analysis unless explicit O2O relation propagation is applied.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

### Recommended Flattened Logs & Safe Scope
1.  **Flattened Order Log (Commercial SLA View)**:
    *   *Safe Questions*: What is the end-to-end customer order duration ($e01 \to e28$)? What proportion of orders meet delivery deadlines? What is the status of order completion?
    *   *Unsafe Questions*: What is the physical workload of the warehouse packaging team? What is the actual transit efficiency of delivery trucks?
2.  **Flattened Item Log (Physical Fulfillment View)**:
    *   *Safe Questions*: How long does an item spend in picking, packing, and transit? Where do physical item bottlenecks occur during warehouse handling?
    *   *Unsafe Questions*: What is the billing and payment status of an order? What are total order processing costs?
3.  **Flattened Invoice Log (Accounts Receivable View)**:
    *   *Safe Questions*: What is the mean duration between invoice issuance and full settlement? How many payment installments are typical per invoice ($V2$ pattern)?
    *   *Unsafe Questions*: Did physical delivery complete prior to billing? What caused shipping delays?

### Questions Requiring Object-Centric Views (OCPM)
*   **Cross-Object Bottleneck Root-Cause Analysis**: Identifying why $D2$ departed at 11:05 requires an **Event-Type Object-Type (ET-OT) graph** or **OC-DFG** to reveal that $D2$ was synchronized with $P2$ ($O1$) and $P3$ ($O2, O3$).
*   **True Workload & Operational Capacity Assessment**: Determining actual physical resource consumption requires **OC-DFG frequency metrics calculated on unique events**, preventing the 200%–300% inflation present in Order-flattened logs.
*   **Structural Consolidation & Batching Discovery**: Uncovering how orders share packaging ($P3, P4$), deliveries ($D2$), and invoices ($V2$) requires an **Object Type Graph (OTG)** and **Object Interaction Summary**.
*   **Execution Pattern Segmentation**: Grouping complex multi-object interactions into distinct behavioral variants requires **Equivalent-Execution Clustering** (`pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel`).

### Reconciliation Strategy for Contradictory Findings
When flattened views conflict with object-centric models:
1.  **Frequency Reconciliation**: Apply fractional reweighting $w(e) = \frac{1}{|\pi_T(e)|}$ to flattened event counts. If Order flattening reports 3 `Dispatch` executions for $D2$, divide by the E2O cardinality ($|\{O1, O2, O3\}| = 3$), reducing the reconciled count to 1 real-world execution.
2.  **Lead-Time Decomposition**: Disambiguate flattened trace duration by splitting total lead time into *Active Execution Time* (derived from single-object item/package logs) and *Synchronization Queue Time* (derived from OCPM interaction graphs showing multi-object convergence wait times).

## 5. Lead-object decision

### Position Choice
**C. Reject a single lead object for this process.**

### Structured Argument

#### Decision Criteria
1.  **Process Coverage Completeness**: The selected case notion must encompass both physical fulfillment (picking, packing, delivery) and commercial/financial settlement (invoicing, payments) without dropping entire lifecycle phases.
2.  **Structural Distortion Minimization**: The case notion must not introduce severe artificial loops, false concurrency, or false direct-follows relationships through convergence or divergence.
3.  **Metric Integrity**: Activity frequencies and throughput times derived from the case notion must accurately reflect physical and administrative capacity without multi-counting.

#### Strongest Supporting Evidence
No single object type satisfies these criteria across the provided OCEL snapshot:
*   Choosing **Order** violates Criterion 2 and 3: Convergence on $D2$ ($e16$, $e24$), $P3$ ($e13$), $P4$ ($e14$), and $V2$ ($e19$, $e22$, $e25$) creates artificial loop edges ($Dispatch \to Dispatch$, $Record\ Payment \to Record\ Payment$) and inflates physical event counts by up to 300%.
*   Choosing **Item** violates Criterion 1: Under direct E2O relations, items completely omit invoicing ($e18$–$e20$) and payment ($e21$, $e22$, $e25$, $e26$) events, truncating the Order-to-Cash process into a purely physical fulfillment process.
*   Choosing **Package**, **Delivery**, or **Payment** severely violates Criterion 1: $Payment$ produces single-event traces ($M1$–$M4$), $Delivery$ produces 2-event traces ($D1$–$D3$), and $Package$ omits order placement and financial settlement entirely.

Because every candidate object type either truncates critical lifecycle phases or severely corrupts process topology and metrics, forcing a single lead object is analytically unviable.

#### Strongest Counterargument
*Argument*: "Order is the primary commercial construct of the business. Item-level split deliveries and invoice consolidations are merely operational execution details that can be represented as attribute annotations on an Order trace."

*Rebuttal*: Treating cross-order consolidations as order-level attributes fails when events are shared across *multiple* orders. For example, $D2$ carries packages for $O1$, $O2$, and $O3$. In an Order-flattened log, $e16$ ($Dispatch\ D2$) must either be duplicated across $O1$, $O2$, and $O3$ (corrupting activity frequencies and creating artificial precedence loops) or arbitrarily assigned to a single order (corrupting process completeness for the others). Neither outcome is analytically acceptable.

#### Residual Risk
Rejecting a single lead object increases analytical complexity. It requires multi-dimensional object-centric metrics that cannot be reduced to a single standard DFG. Stakeholders accustomed to simple single-trace flowcharts may find OCPM visualizations harder to interpret.

#### Conditions for Decision Change
We would adopt **Position A (Single Lead Object: Order)** if and only if the operational reality satisfied a strict 1-to-1-to-1 isomorphism: every Order contains exactly one Item, is packed into exactly one Package, is transported in a dedicated Delivery, billed on a dedicated Invoice, and settled via a single Payment. Under such conditions, convergence and divergence vanish, making single-lead-object flattening mathematically equivalent to OCPM.

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

### 1. Shared Event Fractional Down-weighting Check
*   **Method**: Compute activity frequencies and DFG edge weights in flattened logs using fractional event weights:
    $$w(e, T) = \frac{1}{|\pi_T(e)|}$$
    where $|\pi_T(e)|$ is the number of objects of type $T$ related to event $e$.
*   **Validation Goal**: Compare raw flattened Order frequencies against down-weighted Order frequencies. For event $e16$ (`Dispatch` D2 related to $O1, O2, O3$), raw flattening yields a frequency count of 3. Down-weighting assigns $w(e16, \text{Order}) = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3} = 1.0$.
*   **Expected Finding**: Fractional down-weighting aligns flattened activity totals with actual OCEL event counts, confirming that raw flattening overcounts operational workload by $200\%$ on consolidated dispatches ($e16$) and $100\%$ on cross-order packaging ($e13, e14$).

### 2. Direct E2O vs. O2O Propagated Relation Sensitivity Check
*   **Method**: Generate two flattened Item event logs:
    1.  *Log A (Direct E2O)*: Contains only events directly linked to Item instances ($e05$–$e17$, $e23$, $e24$, $e27$).
    2.  *Log B (Propagated O2O)*: Inherits E2O links transitively via O2O relations ($\text{Item} \xrightarrow{\text{contains}} \text{Order} \xrightarrow{\text{bills}} \text{Invoice} \xrightarrow{\text{settles}} \text{Payment}$).
*   **Validation Goal**: Quantify trace length expansion, activity inclusion, and DFG edge generation between Log A and Log B.
*   **Expected Finding**: Log A truncates at delivery confirmation, completely hiding financial settlement. Log B reintroduces financial events ($e18$–$e22$, $e25$, $e26$) into Item cases, but multiplies event duplication and introduces false parallel branches between item delivery and order invoicing.

### 3. Lead Object Perturbation Analysis
*   **Method**: Compute process throughput times across four alternative lead object flattenings: Order ($O1$–$O4$), Item ($I11$–$I41$), Package ($P1$–$P4$), and Invoice ($V1$–$V3$). Specifically measure the transit step duration:
    $$\Delta t_{\text{transit}} = t(\text{Confirm Delivery}) - t(\text{Dispatch})$$
*   **Validation Goal**: Measure variance in calculated transit lead times across object perspectives for trip $D2$.
*   **Expected Finding**:
    *   In Delivery flattening ($D2$), $\Delta t_{\text{transit}} = t(e24) - t(e16) = 16:00 - 11:05 = 4\text{h } 55\text{m}$.
    *   In Order flattening ($O1$), $O1$ exhibits two dispatches ($e15$ at 11:00, $e16$ at 11:05) and two deliveries ($e23$ at 12:30, $e24$ at 16:00). Depending on DFG edge pairing ($e15 \to e24$ vs $e16 \to e23$), lead time skews between $1\text{h } 25\text{m}$ and $5\text{h } 00\text{m}$.
    *   This confirms that single-lead-object choice introduces arbitrary variance into basic SLA throughput metrics.

### 4. OC-DFG Metric Triangulation Check
*   **Method**: Calculate edge frequencies in the Object-Centric Direct-Follows Graph (`pm4py.discover_ocdfg`) under three distinct aggregation metrics:
    1.  *Event Count Metric*: Number of unique events causing the state transition.
    2.  *Unique Object Instance Metric*: Number of distinct object instances traversing the edge.
    3.  *Total Interaction Metric*: Total object-to-object interaction pairs participating in the transition.
*   **Validation Goal**: Compare edge frequencies between `Pack Package` and `Dispatch` for package $P3$ and $P4$.
*   **Expected Finding**: Metric (1) reports 1 event for $e13 \to e16$. Metric (2) reports 1 Package ($P3$), 2 Items ($I21, I31$), and 2 Orders ($O2, O3$). Metric (3) reports 4 order-item interaction pairs. Triangulating these three metrics separates true physical workload (Metric 1) from container volume (Metric 2) and commercial complexity (Metric 3), validating that OCPM eliminates the false trade-offs forced by traditional log flattening.
