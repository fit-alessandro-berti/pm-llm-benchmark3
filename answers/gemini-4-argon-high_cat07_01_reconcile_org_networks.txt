# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

## Handover-of-work network
- **What it implies about `Mira_Coordinator`**: In `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network`, directed edges capture direct succession ($r_i \to r_j$) between consecutive events in a case. `Mira_Coordinator` exhibits the highest handover out-degree centrality (`0.82`), in-degree centrality (`0.51`), and betweenness centrality (`0.77`) in the entire process. Structurally, she operates as a cross-functional **routing switchboard** connecting four otherwise loosely coupled tiers:
  1. **Automated triage**: Receiving `920` exception handovers from `Bot_Rules` (mean elapsed time `3.5 min`), outnumbering routine routing from `Bot_Rules` to `Alice_TeamLead` (`610`) or `Bruno_TeamLead` (`590`).
  2. **Temporary front-line staff**: Absorbing missing-information and closure escalations from `Temp_1` (`190` edges, `9 min`) and `Temp_2` (`170` edges, `8 min`).
  3. **Domain specialists**: Dispatching medical and legal exceptions to `Omar_Medical` (`210` edges, `42 min`) and `Priya_Legal` (`180` edges, `51 min`) and receiving their completed evaluations back (`160` and `140` edges, `11–12 min`).
  4. **Both regional adjuster teams**: Fanning out reassigned and reworked claims across Team North (`Carla_Adjuster` `350`, `Elise_Adjuster` `290`) and Team South (`Diego_Adjuster` `330`, `Farid_Adjuster` `280`).
- **What it does not imply**:
  - **Direct succession is not substantive execution or line authority**: High handover centrality proves temporal adjacency in the trace, not that `Mira_Coordinator` performs core claims adjudication (`Assess Damage`, `Negotiate Settlement`) or exercises formal approval authority (`Approve Settlement`, `Issue Payment` via `Vendor_Pay`).
  - **High betweenness does not mean she lies on the critical path of routine claims**: She participates in only `22%` of all claims (`905 / 4,100`). The `78%` majority of routine claims flow through `Bot_Ingest -> Bot_Rules -> Alice/Bruno_TeamLead -> Adjusters -> TeamLead -> Vendor_Pay` and `Vendor_Verify` without ever touching her.
  - **Raw edge frequencies cannot be equated one-to-one with distinct case touches**: Table 1 reports `960` events across `905` distinct cases for `Mira_Coordinator`, yet her listed incoming and outgoing handover edges sum to `1,580` and `1,640` respectively. This discrepancy implies that the handover graph was either computed over uncollapsed `lifecycle:transition` events (e.g., `assign`, `start`, `complete`), included administrative sub-events (`Assign Specialist`, which is absent from the canonical 14-activity list), or used a multi-step causal window rather than strict single-event completion counts.

## Working-together network
- **What it implies about `Mira_Coordinator`**: In `pm4py.discover_working_together_network`, undirected edge weights represent the share of cases in which two resources co-occur. Here `Mira_Coordinator` has a low weighted degree (`0.16`) and small pairwise co-case rates with adjusters (`6%–8%`) and team leads (`4%–5%`), contrasting sharply with tightly coupled regional pairs (`Alice_TeamLead — Carla_Adjuster` at `27%`, `Bruno_TeamLead — Diego_Adjuster` at `26%`, and within-team senior/junior pairs at `19%–24%`). This implies she is **not embedded in a dedicated regional delivery pod** and does not co-own a fixed portfolio of cases with one stable partner.
- **What it does not imply (Reconciling the apparent contradiction)**:
  - **Low co-case percentages do not mean she works in isolation or has weak operational impact**: Her apparent peripherality in the working-together graph is a mathematical artifact of three structural mechanisms:
    1. *Log-wide denominator ceiling*: Because `Mira_Coordinator` touches only `905` of the `4,100` total cases (`22%`), her global co-occurrence rate with *any* resource in the log cannot exceed `22%`.
    2. *High cross-team fan-out dilution*: Whereas `Alice_TeamLead` concentrates her workload inside Team North (`Carla_Adjuster`, `Elise_Adjuster`) and `Bruno_TeamLead` inside Team South (`Diego_Adjuster`, `Farid_Adjuster`), `Mira_Coordinator` distributes her `905` exception cases nearly evenly across all four adjusters (`Carla` `350`, `Diego` `330`, `Elise` `290`, `Farid` `280`). Splitting `22%` of log volume across four adjusters mechanically yields `6%–8%` log-wide co-occurrence per adjuster (`~250–330 / 4,100`), even though almost every case she handles involves an adjuster.
    3. *Triage substitution for Team Leads*: When `Bot_Rules` routes an exception directly to `Mira_Coordinator` (`920` edges) and she dispatches it straight to an adjuster, `Alice_TeamLead` and `Bruno_TeamLead` are bypassed during initial triage—and if the claim is closed without settlement or remains in exception handling, the Team Lead never enters the trace. This explains why `Mira_Coordinator — Alice_TeamLead` (`5%`) and `Mira_Coordinator — Bruno_TeamLead` (`4%`) are so low.

## Subcontracting network
- **What it implies about `Mira_Coordinator`**: In `pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network` (with $n=2$), edges reflect $A \to B \to A$ delegation-and-return motifs within two subsequent events. `Mira_Coordinator` has the highest subcontracting betweenness (`0.69`), participating in two structurally distinct roles:
  1. **As orchestrator/delegator ($A = \text{Mira\_Coordinator}$)**: She wraps specialist consultations (`Mira_Coordinator -> Omar_Medical -> Mira_Coordinator`, $f=126$, `Request Missing Information -> Medical Review -> Assign Specialist/Reopen`; and `Mira_Coordinator -> Priya_Legal -> Mira_Coordinator`, $f=98$, `Escalate Complaint -> Legal Review -> Reopen/Assign Specialist`). Rather than specialists handing cases directly to adjusters, findings return to `Mira_Coordinator` for re-triage.
  2. **As human-in-the-loop subcontractor ($B = \text{Mira\_Coordinator}$)**: In `Bot_Rules -> Mira_Coordinator -> Bot_Rules` ($f=62$, `Fraud Screen -> manual exception -> Fraud Screen update`), the automated rules engine delegates ambiguous fraud flags to her and resumes automated processing once she clears or updates the exception.
  - This differs fundamentally from Team Lead subcontracting (`Alice -> Carla -> Alice` [$86$] and `Bruno -> Diego -> Bruno` [$82$], which are iterative settlement negotiation/approval loops) and clerical external verification (`Temp_1 -> Vendor_Verify -> Temp_1` [$68$]).
- **What it does not imply**:
  - **Subcontracting motifs do not imply supervisory authority or domain review**: Wrapping `Omar_Medical` and `Priya_Legal` does not mean `Mira_Coordinator` evaluates medical or legal correctness; she likely manages intake completeness and post-review routing.
  - **Not all $A \to B \to A$ patterns are intentional delegation**: Some $n=2$ loops can arise from interleaved `lifecycle:transition` logging (e.g., `Mira [start] -> Specialist [complete] -> Mira [complete]`) or repetitive rework ping-pong when missing information is only partially resolved.

## Performance/time statistics
- **What they imply about `Mira_Coordinator`**:
  - **Fast unit execution, high systemic leverage over waiting time**: Her median service time per event is just `4.2 min`—faster than `Alice_TeamLead` (`6.5 min`), `Bruno_TeamLead` (`6.8 min`), `Temp_1`/`Temp_2` (`8–9 min`), adjusters (`17–24 min`), specialists (`37–51 min`), and external vendors (`Vendor_Verify` `2.1 hours`, `Vendor_Pay` `1.3 hours`). Handover pickup times into and out of her queue are similarly short (`3.5 min` from `Bot_Rules`, `8–9 min` from Temps, `11–12 min` from Specialists, `16–19 min` to Adjusters).
  - **Critical buffer against exception queue accumulation**: During her 6-day absence, median cycle time for ordinary low-value claims was unchanged, but `exception_flag=True` cases accumulated in queues and their 90th-percentile cycle time rose by `65%` (from `8.4` to `13.9` days). Thus, her primary time impact lies in **preventing inter-step waiting time** on exception pathways.
- **What they do not imply**:
  - **"High variance" in average case cycle-time contribution does not mean `Mira_Coordinator` works inconsistently or slowly**: The variance is driven by **selection bias (case-mix confounding)**. Nearly half of her cases carry `exception_flag=True` (`0.48`), she touches `64%` of reopened claims and `71%` of complaints, and she triggers external customer wait states (`Request Missing Information`) and long specialist reviews (`Omar_Medical` `42 min` elapsed + `37 min` service; `Priya_Legal` `51 min` elapsed + `51 min` service).
  - **She is not the main source of baseline waiting time in routine claims**: High waiting-time components on routine cases are dominated by external providers (`Vendor_Verify` across `1,500` cases at `2.1 hours`; `Vendor_Pay` across `2,840` cases at `1.3 hours`), where `Mira_Coordinator` has virtually no direct handovers.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

## Hypothesis 1: Formal coordinator / structured exception desk
- **Mechanism**: `Mira_Coordinator` operates as the designated Tier-2 exception and escalation desk designed to keep high-volume automated intake (`Bot_Ingest`, `Bot_Rules`) and regional delivery pods (Team North and Team South) insulated from non-standard routing, complaints, reopenings, and specialist scheduling.
- **Evidence supporting it**:
  - `Bot_Rules` has a dedicated, high-volume routing rule sending `920` cases directly to `Mira_Coordinator` (`3.5 min` mean elapsed time), alongside structured escalations from `Temp_1` (`190`) and `Temp_2` (`170`).
  - Her activity set (`Request Missing Information`, `Reopen Claim`, `Escalate Complaint`, `Assign Specialist`) aligns with formal exception-desk duties, and she handles `64%` of reopened claims and `71%` of complaints at a rapid `4.2 min` median execution time.
  - She strictly respects financial authority boundaries: she never performs `Approve Settlement` (reserved for `Alice_TeamLead` and `Bruno_TeamLead`) or `Issue Payment` (`Vendor_Pay`).
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it**:
  - The process owner dismisses her as *"not a real team lead, just a queue cleaner,"* and there was no effective backup protocol during her 6-day absence—whereas a formally engineered exception desk in a regulated process would typically have documented SOPs, SLA coverage, and cross-trained backups.
  - The activity `Assign Specialist` appears in her resource summary and subcontracting traces but is missing from the official 14-activity process specification, suggesting parts of her workflow operate outside formal process documentation.
- **Observable predictions in future event logs**:
  - Cases routed `Bot_Rules -> Mira_Coordinator` will correlate deterministically with explicit rule triggers (`exception_flag=True`, specific `case_type`, `channel`, or `Fraud Screen` outcomes).
  - Her downstream assignments to `Carla`/`Elise` vs. `Diego`/`Farid` will strictly follow formal `region` (North vs. South) and `amount_band`/`priority` tiering rules.

## Hypothesis 2: Hidden bottleneck and tacit key-person dependency
- **Mechanism**: Over time, ambiguous exceptions from automation (`Bot_Rules`), under-trained temporary staff (`Temp_1`, `Temp_2`), and external specialists (`Omar_Medical`, `Priya_Legal`) have converged on a single individual who relies on undocumented institutional memory (*"the person who knows where hard cases should go"*), creating an unacknowledged single point of failure.
- **Evidence supporting it**:
  - During her 6-day absence, `exception_flag=True` cases accumulated in queues and their 90th-percentile cycle time surged from `8.4` to `13.9` days, proving that neither `Alice_TeamLead`, `Bruno_TeamLead`, nor `Bot_Rules` could seamlessly absorb her coordination load.
  - Adjusters explicitly credit her tacit routing judgment rather than formal routing tables, while the process owner severely underestimates her systemic criticality.
  - She has the highest handover betweenness (`0.77`) and subcontracting betweenness (`0.69`), acting as the sole bridge between specialists (`Omar_Medical`, `Priya_Legal`), temporary staff, and adjusters.
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it**:
  - Under normal operations, she is **not** a capacity bottleneck: her median service time is only `4.2 min`, and incoming handover delays into her queue are very low (`3.5 min` from `Bot_Rules`, `8–12 min` from Temps and Specialists).
  - `78%` of all claims bypass her completely, and ordinary low-value claims experienced zero median cycle-time degradation during her 6-day absence.
- **Observable predictions in future event logs**:
  - During intraday peaks, shift gaps, or leave periods for `Mira_Coordinator`, `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` will show sharp spikes in waiting time specifically on edges entering her typical activities (`Bot_Rules -> Request Missing Information / Assign Specialist`, `Temp_1/Temp_2 -> Escalate Complaint`, and post-`Medical Review`/`Legal Review` handoffs), while her active service time remains ~`4 min`.
  - If another resource temporarily covers her queue during absences, downstream rework loops (`Adjuster -> Mira/Lead` or repeated `Request Missing Information`) will increase due to loss of her tacit case-matching knowledge.

## Hypothesis 3: Organizational boundary spanner and cross-team triage broker
- **Mechanism**: While `Alice_TeamLead` and `Bruno_TeamLead` manage vertical execution and settlement approvals within regional silos (Team North and Team South), `Mira_Coordinator` acts as a horizontal broker bridging internal functional silos (automation, temp intake, regional adjuster teams) and specialist silos (Medical, Legal), translating incomplete or cross-cutting claims into actionable adjuster work packages.
- **Evidence supporting it**:
  - Explains the exact SNA signature: extreme handover out-degree (`0.82`) and subcontracting betweenness (`0.69`) paired with low working-together co-occurrence (`4%–8%`) because a boundary spanner touches many teams briefly (`1.06` events/case in Table 1) rather than staying embedded in a single team's case lifecycle.
  - She buffers scarce, high-cost specialists (`Omar_Medical` at `37 min` median service time; `Priya_Legal` at `51 min`) by pre-requesting missing information (`Mira -> Omar -> Mira`, $f=126$) and pre-triaging complaints (`Mira -> Priya -> Mira`, $f=98$), supporting the operations manager's warning that removing her would trigger specialist overload.
  - She balances exception assignments across all four adjusters (`350`, `330`, `290`, `280`), acting as a load-leveling valve across both teams.
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it**:
  - Her working-together co-occurrence with `Alice_TeamLead` (`5%`) and `Bruno_TeamLead` (`4%`) is remarkably low; if she were coordinating closely with team leadership on capacity or complex claims, we would expect more frequent trace co-participation or direct handovers between `Mira_Coordinator` and the Team Leads.
  - Two major external boundaries—`Vendor_Verify` (`1,500` cases) and `Vendor_Pay` (`2,840` cases)—are bridged by `Temp_1` (`Temp_1 -> Vendor_Verify -> Temp_1`, $f=68$) and the Team Leads (`Alice/Bruno -> Vendor_Pay`, `510` and `480` edges), not by `Mira_Coordinator`.
- **Observable predictions in future event logs**:
  - Cases containing `Medical Review` or `Legal Review` that are pre-brokered by `Mira_Coordinator` will exhibit fewer specialist rejections or repeat specialist consultations than the remaining ~`30%–40%` of specialist cases routed without her involvement (`316` total medical cases vs. `210` from Mira; `251` total legal cases vs. `180` from Mira).
  - When one region faces a surge in complex claims, `Mira_Coordinator`'s handover edges will dynamically shift across teams (`Carla`/`Elise` vs. `Diego`/`Farid`) more flexibly than `Bot_Rules`'s static routing.

## Hypothesis 4: Human-in-the-loop automation & front-line escalation buffer
- **Mechanism**: Imperfections in automated rules (`Bot_Rules`) and limited authority/training among temporary staff (`Temp_1`, `Temp_2`) generate a high volume of partial exceptions—such as inconclusive `Fraud Screen` flags, failed coverage validations after `Vendor_Verify`, or customer pushback at closure—that require rapid human triage (`4.2 min`) to clean up and re-inject into standard workflows.
- **Evidence supporting it**:
  - The subcontracting motif `Bot_Rules -> Mira_Coordinator -> Bot_Rules` ($f=62$, `Fraud Screen -> manual exception -> Fraud Screen update`) directly shows her functioning as a human-in-the-loop exception handler for the rules engine.
  - `Temp_1` (`510` events) and `Temp_2` (`480` events) have very low exception shares (`0.14` and `0.13`) precisely because whenever they encounter missing-info escalations (`190` edges from `Temp_1`) or closure exceptions (`170` edges from `Temp_2`), they hand the case off to `Mira_Coordinator` (`8–9 min` elapsed time)—consuming roughly `35%` of Temps' cases!
  - Aligns with the process owner's observation that she acts as a *"queue cleaner"* clearing stalled tickets so downstream adjusters can work on clean files.
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it**:
  - A purely clerical automation/temp cleaner would not be entrusted with `71%` of formal `Escalate Complaint` cases, `64%` of `Reopen Claim` events, or direct routing to high-cost legal and medical specialists (`Priya_Legal`, `Omar_Medical`).
- **Observable predictions in future event logs**:
  - Changes to `Bot_Rules` fraud thresholds or `Vendor_Verify` document rejection rates will immediately and proportionally shift `Bot_Rules -> Mira_Coordinator` and `Temp_1 -> Mira_Coordinator` handover frequencies.
  - Cases following `Temp_1 -> Vendor_Verify -> Temp_1` ($f=68$) where verification fails will disproportionately transition next to `Mira_Coordinator` via `Temp_1 -> Mira_Coordinator`.

## Hypothesis 5: Workflow-logging and shared-queue attribution artifact
- **Mechanism**: Part of `Mira_Coordinator`'s extreme handover (`0.82` out-degree) and subcontracting (`0.69` betweenness) centrality is inflated by how the IT system logs queue transitions, `lifecycle:transition` states (`assign`, `start`, `complete`), or shared exception-desk inbox actions under `Mira_Coordinator`'s user ID.
- **Evidence supporting it**:
  - **Mathematical discrepancy across tables**: Table 1 records `960` events across `905` distinct cases for `Mira_Coordinator` (at most `55` repeat events if each row in Table 1 counts all logged events), yet Table 2 lists `1,580` incoming and `1,640` outgoing handover edges for her, and Table 4 records `224` $A \to B \to A$ subcontracting loops where she appears at least twice (`126` with `Omar_Medical`, `98` with `Priya_Legal`). This is only mathematically possible if:
    1. Table 1 counts only `complete` transitions (or primary activities) while SNA tables include `assign`/`start`/`suspend`/`resume` transitions or unlisted system events like `Assign Specialist`; or
    2. Automated dispatcher triggers (e.g., auto-routing upon specialist completion) stamp `org:resource = Mira_Coordinator`.
  - Additionally, `Bot_Ingest` (`4,100` cases) shows a `0.19` share of `exception_flag=True` while `Bot_Rules` (`4,090` cases) shows `0.31`, proving that event/case attributes or sub-events are dynamically modified mid-trace.
- **Evidence contradicting or limiting it**:
  - Her role cannot be *purely* a logging artifact: her 6-day physical absence caused a real operational backlog and a `5.5-day` increase in 90th-percentile cycle time for exception cases, and human colleagues (`Adjusters`, `Process Owner`, `Compliance Reviewer`, `Operations Manager`) directly observe her daily interventions.
- **Observable predictions in future event logs**:
  - Filtering the log to `lifecycle:transition == 'complete'` and removing automated `Assign Specialist` state transitions will substantially reduce `Mira_Coordinator`'s raw handover edge counts and $n=2$ subcontracting frequency while preserving her qualitative bridge position.
  - Sub-second or zero-duration service times (`pm4py.get_service_time`) will cluster on a subset of `Mira_Coordinator` events (such as `Assign Specialist` or `Fraud Screen update`), separating system-triggered transitions from her ~`4.2 min` human triage actions.

# 3. Risk assessment

- **Bottleneck risk**:
  - *Execution bottleneck risk is low; queueing bottleneck risk on exception paths is high.* At `4.2 min` median service time per event across `960` events in 12 weeks (~`80` events/week, or ~`5.6 hours/week` of direct logged touch time), `Mira_Coordinator` is not capacity-saturated during normal operations. However, because `920` `Bot_Rules` exceptions, `360` temporary-staff escalations, and `300` specialist returns funnel through a single node, any unavailability immediately stalls exception throughput—as demonstrated when p90 cycle time for `exception_flag=True` claims jumped from `8.4` to `13.9` days during her 6-day absence. By contrast, routine claims face waiting-time bottlenecks at external vendors (`Vendor_Verify` `2.1 hours`, `Vendor_Pay` `1.3 hours`), not at `Mira_Coordinator`.
- **Coordination risk**:
  - *High risk of silo decoupling and context loss.* `Mira_Coordinator` assigns `1,250` handover edges directly to senior and junior adjusters (`Carla` `350`, `Diego` `330`, `Elise` `290`, `Farid` `280`), while co-occurring with `Alice_TeamLead` and `Bruno_TeamLead` in only `5%` and `4%` of cases. Because she bypasses Team Leads when dispatching exception rework and post-specialist claims to adjusters, Team Leads lack visibility into complex exception workloads entering their teams, creating risks of conflicting priorities between routine assignments (`Alice -> Carla` `320`, `Bruno -> Diego` `310`) and Mira's exception reassignments.
- **Key-person risk**:
  - *Severe (bus factor = 1).* `Mira_Coordinator` occupies a singleton role in `pm4py.discover_organizational_roles`, handles `64%` of reopened claims and `71%` of complaints, and holds uncodified routing knowledge (*"knows where hard cases should go"*). While `Alice_TeamLead` performs some `Reopen Claim` events (`0.26` exception share), neither Team Lead replicated Mira's specialist-orchestration or temp-escalation triage during her 6-day absence.
- **Compliance/control risk**:
  - *Moderate structural separation-of-duties (SoD) risk; no direct evidence of wrongdoing.* The compliance reviewer notes that `Mira_Coordinator` both diagnoses/flags problems (`Escalate Complaint`, `Reopen Claim`, manual `Fraud Screen` exception review in `Bot_Rules -> Mira -> Bot_Rules`) and selects the downstream handler (`Assign Specialist` or adjuster reassignment). In a regulated claims environment, unlogged discretion over which claims receive `Medical Review`, `Legal Review`, or reopening—and which adjuster receives a reopened file—creates an **auditability gap** and potential for inconsistent claimant treatment. However, this risk must stay calibrated: there is **no evidence of improper conduct or financial self-dealing**, and critical financial controls remain segregated (`Approve Settlement` is performed exclusively by `Alice_TeamLead` and `Bruno_TeamLead`, and `Issue Payment` by `Vendor_Pay`).
- **Workload fairness risk**:
  - *Moderate risk for both adjuster tiers and `Mira_Coordinator` herself.* First, look at adjuster case complexity: `Mira_Coordinator` routes nearly as many rework/missing-info handovers to Junior Adjusters (`Elise_Adjuster` `290`, `Farid_Adjuster` `280`) as to Senior Adjusters (`Carla_Adjuster` `350`, `Diego_Adjuster` `330`). Consequently, Junior Adjusters actually carry a slightly **higher** share of `exception_flag=True` cases (`0.36` and `0.35`) than Senior Adjusters (`0.33` and `0.29`), despite handling fewer total cases (`590–610` vs. `820–870`) and not performing `Negotiate Settlement`. Second, `Mira_Coordinator`'s high-cognitive-load coordination work (`0.48` exception share) is undervalued by leadership as *"just a queue cleaner"* because her individual events are brief (`4.2 min`).
- **Data-quality risk**:
  - *High risk of misinterpreting uncleaned SNA metrics.* The mismatch between Table 1 (`960` events / `905` cases) and Tables 2 & 4 (`1,580` incoming / `1,640` outgoing handovers; `224` $A \to B \to A$ loops), the presence of the undocumented `Assign Specialist` activity, and the shift in `exception_flag=True` share between `Bot_Ingest` (`0.19` on `4,100` cases) and `Bot_Rules` (`0.31` on `4,090` cases) indicate that `lifecycle:transition` granularity, system-generated assignment events, and mid-case attribute updates can distort naïve centrality rankings if not normalized prior to management decisions.

This directly tests Hypothesis 5 by checking how much of the discrepancy between `960` events and `1,640` outgoing handovers is caused by multi-transition logging or `Assign Specialist` sub-events.
   - Run `pm4py.discover_network_analysis` keyed by `(org:resource, concept:name)` and stratified by `region`, `amount_band`, and `priority` (visualized via `pm4py.save_vis_sna`) to verify whether `Mira_Coordinator`'s assignments to `Carla`/`Elise` (North) vs. `Diego`/`Farid` (South) respect regional boundaries or cross-level capacity across teams.

# 5. Recommendation

## What should be changed immediately
1. **Eliminate the single-point-of-failure (bus factor = 1) through cross-training and backup routing**:
   - Cross-train `Alice_TeamLead` and `Bruno_TeamLead` (plus one designated senior adjuster deputy per team, `Carla_Adjuster` and `Diego_Adjuster`) on `Mira_Coordinator`'s exception-triage, specialist-dispatch (`Assign Specialist`), and complaint/reopen workflows (`Bruno_TeamLead` currently handles zero `Reopen Claim` events, whereas `Alice_TeamLead` already handles `Reopen Claim`).
   - Configure automatic overflow/absence routing in `Bot_Rules` and the ticketing system so that when `Mira_Coordinator` is absent or her queue wait exceeds a threshold (e.g., `30 min`), regional exceptions route to the respective Team Lead rather than accumulating in an unattended queue.
2. **Codify tacit triage rules and add lightweight compliance reason codes**:
   - Conduct a structured walkthrough with `Mira_Coordinator`, `Omar_Medical`, `Priya_Legal`, and the Senior Adjusters to document her routing criteria (*"where hard cases should go"*).
   - Address the compliance reviewer's separation-of-duties concern without slowing down her `4.2 min` triage speed by requiring a standardized **reason code** whenever `Mira_Coordinator` executes `Assign Specialist`, `Reopen Claim`, `Escalate Complaint`, or manual `Fraud Screen` overrides (`Bot_Rules -> Mira -> Bot_Rules`), accompanied by periodic sample audits by Team Leads while keeping `Approve Settlement` strictly limited to `Alice_TeamLead` and `Bruno_TeamLead`.
3. **Standardize event-log taxonomy**:
   - Formally register `Assign Specialist` in the process schema, ensure `lifecycle:transition` states (`assign`, `start`, `complete`) are logged consistently across human and bot resources, and document whether `exception_flag` is an event-level or mutable case-level attribute.

## What should be monitored
1. **Exception-path waiting times and tail SLAs**:
   - Track median and 90th-percentile waiting times (`pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time`) on the five critical exception handoffs: `Bot_Rules -> Mira_Coordinator`, `Temp_1/Temp_2 -> Mira_Coordinator`, `Mira_Coordinator -> Omar_Medical / Priya_Legal`, and `Omar/Priya -> Mira_Coordinator`.
2. **Junior vs. Senior Adjuster exception load and rework loops**:
   - Monitor why Junior Adjusters (`Elise_Adjuster` `0.36`, `Farid_Adjuster` `0.35`) have a higher share of `exception_flag=True` cases than Senior Adjusters (`Carla_Adjuster` `0.33`, `Diego_Adjuster` `0.29`), and track whether `Mira_Coordinator -> Elise/Farid_Adjuster` handovers (`290` and `280` edges) represent appropriate missing-info chase tasks or overloaded complex claims that bounce back for rework.
3. **Automation and vendor friction rates**:
   - Monitor the frequency of `Bot_Rules -> Mira_Coordinator -> Bot_Rules` ($f=62$) fraud-screen loops, `Temp_1 -> Vendor_Verify -> Temp_1` ($f=68$) document-verification loops, and external vendor turnaround times (`Vendor_Verify` `2.1 hours`, `Vendor_Pay` `1.3 hours`).

## What should not be changed until more evidence is available
1. **Do NOT remove `Mira_Coordinator`'s role or dismiss her as "just a queue cleaner"**:
   - Removing her without a validated replacement mechanism for cross-team exception triage, temporary-staff escalation handling (`360` edges), and specialist packaging (`390` outgoing, `300` incoming edges) would recreate the 6-day absence failure mode (`90th percentile` exception cycle time spiking from `8.4` to `13.9+` days) and flood `Omar_Medical`, `Priya_Legal`, `Alice_TeamLead`, and `Bruno_TeamLead` with unscreened escalations.
2. **Do NOT blindly automate her routing edges inside `Bot_Rules`**:
   - Until process-mining variant and waiting-time analyses clarify what triggers the `920` `Bot_Rules -> Mira_Coordinator` handoffs and the `62` `Bot_Rules -> Mira -> Bot_Rules` loops—and how often she intercepts missing information *before* dispatching to `Omar_Medical` (`126` loops)—replacing her judgment with rigid routing rules risks sending incomplete files directly to scarce specialists (`37–51 min` per review).
3. **Do NOT impose synchronous dual-approval gates on every routing action**:
   - Requiring a Team Lead to co-sign every `Assign Specialist` or `Request Missing Information` event before establishing whether any inappropriate routing occurs would add multi-minute queueing delays to a `4.2 min` coordination task and create a new bottleneck.

# 6. Short executive summary

`Mira_Coordinator` is neither a passive "queue cleaner" nor a conventional team lead; she acts as the claims process's central exception switchboard. Although she touches only 22% of all claims—making her appear peripheral in team co-working charts because her work is spread across both regional teams rather than tied to one adjuster group—she handles 64% of reopened claims, 71% of complaints, and the primary handoffs between automated rules, temporary staff, adjusters, and medical/legal specialists.

Her individual actions take only 4.2 minutes, yet her 6-day absence caused complex claim turnaround at the 90th percentile to jump from 8.4 to 13.9 days. Removing her without replacing this triage function would stall exception queues and overload specialists and team leads.

Management should retain her role while immediately reducing single-person dependency: document her routing criteria, cross-train the two team leads as backups, and add lightweight reason-code logging for compliance oversight. Before automating her routing rules or restructuring teams, analyze queue waiting times and event logs to separate system logging noise from true coordination value.
