Ranked by plausibility (1=most plausible given documented constraints; 4=least but still defensible). Each states impact on diagnosis if accepted.

# 7. Final synthesis

Cross-queue comparison of identifiability and risk.

*Most identifiable: QA.* Observation `n=8` with 5 independent FIFO-vs-priority/SPT discriminations (08:30,09:05,09:32 plus appointment-forced choices) and explicit calibration window that is separable. FIFO predicts 5/6 work-conserving starts; secondary appointment window predicts the sole violation at 08:20 and the controlled lateness at 09:20. Both predictions rely on hard timestamps not on correlated est/deadline/priority, and scan-delay bounds preserve ordering. Residual uncertainty is window tolerance and the 2-min idle gap, not primary discipline.

*Least identifiable: QC.* `n=7` but three independent policies (SPT, priority, EDD) correlate on initial backlog and all predict similar order if slack filtered differently. Any single policy is falsified by at least two decisions (SPT by C04 at 10:32 and C01 at 11:40; priority by C02 at 10:10 and C06 at 11:34; EDD by C02 at 10:10). The data force a at-least-two-phase hybrid (feasible-SPT then SLA-risk switch), but phase boundary and rule (Moore skip vs early commitment vs manual) cannot be discriminated without decision-time snapshots (P2) and slack calculations (P7). This queue is where `estimates may be strategically inaccurate` and `deadlines and priorities can produce observationally equivalent choices` are maximally confounding.

*Most dangerous to infer from these observations alone: QB preemptive priority as a global, always-preemptive rule, and QC global SPT/EDD.* 
- Generalizing QB's single `urgent arrival` preemption at 09:16 to all priorities would license unbounded preemptions. The observed resume pattern (B01 09:31-09:48, then forced reassignment) already shows starvation control is required; naive implementation would increase work-in-process, cause repeated resume overhead, and miss B07 appointment entirely. Because SPT coincides at the preemption, one could also mis-infer SPT and preempt long jobs routinely, worsening tardiness.
- Generalizing QC as pure SPT or pure priority/EDD from 2-3 early decisions ignores the documented SLA-risk switch; deploying pure SPT would starve p1 C05 indefinitely, deploying pure priority would have delayed short jobs C02/C03 and increased mean flow time without improving on-time count. In both queues, small-n start-order inference mistakes *every FIFO violation for priority scheduling* - the exact error the benchmark warns against.

Operational recommendation bounds: Do not codify any single-queue discipline without P1-P3 eligibility logging. For QA, a non-preemptive FIFO+earliest-start appointment is safe to prototype with explicit idle-for-appointment; for QD, skill-hard-routing + FIFO-within-skill is safe. For QB and QC, treat current logs as evidence of **undocumented hybrid with manual overrides** and require phase-aware policy versioning before automation.
