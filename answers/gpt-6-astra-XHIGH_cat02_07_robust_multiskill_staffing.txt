# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

**Recommendation in brief:** use controlled pooling with explicit protection for urgent work, but treat staffing and late coverage as part of the decision. Under the distributional assumptions below, the existing roster cannot sustainably absorb even base demand while keeping every resource's planned utilization at or below 90%. No routing policy should be presented as a demonstrated solution to all three SLAs.

The process, eligibility matrix, calendars, penalties and arrival-block expectations are supplied facts. The following are modeling assumptions, not additional observations.

- **Demand:** independent Poisson counts in each two-hour block; conditional arrival times are uniform within the block. Priority and case type are independently sampled from that block's proportions. This is a reproducible starting point; incident clustering and a concentration of P1 cases in ES work could make it optimistic.
- **Service:** independently draw the ordinary/tail indicator for each activity, then draw uniformly within the applicable range. Multiply active service by the assigned resource's multiplier. Thus an ME case requires both branches' labor even when they overlap. Correlation of complexity across branches is unknown and needs sensitivity analysis. The experiment supplies scenario-specific service means to dispatch estimates; immediate recognition of U4 is therefore assumed, and delayed detection must be tested.
- **Scenario interpretation:** U1 increases morning P1 shares from 10%/15% to 18%/23%; the remaining probability is split between P2 and P3 in their original ratio. U2 keeps arrival totals fixed and doubles the E and ES *type weights* before renormalizing. U4 changes only diagnostic tail probabilities, to 0.30, 0.36 and 0.40. An alternative interpretation of U2 that increases total arrivals is considered below.
- **Calendars and SLA clock:** elapsed time starts at arrival, includes nights, breaks, outages and interruptions, and ends at Validation completion. The numerical experiment starts empty, admits one day's cohort, then drains it without further arrivals on repeating daily calendars. Chen returns the next day in U3/U5. This is an isolated-shock experiment, not a steady-state forecast. Overtime is zero in the experiment, within the permitted 30-minute limit; its potential capacity is assessed separately.
- **Resource use:** one worker performs at most one activity or setup at a time. Breaks pause active work without a restart surcharge. At shift end unfinished work can requeue and migrate; an intentionally sequential two-branch case retains its selected cross-skilled worker. A P1 interruption, unlike a calendar pause, adds 10% of the interrupted activity's remaining base work. This is equivalent to adding 10% of remaining active time when the worker is unchanged; another worker processes the augmented remaining work at their own speed. The interruption limit is enforced across the whole victim case.
- **Transfers:** a changed-resource dependency incurs seven elapsed minutes. A move between known mechanical and electrical/software teams adds five; the same worker avoids both charges. Ada/Ben are treated as mechanical, Chen/Emil as electrical/software, and Deepa/Farah as neutral for Intake/Validation; diagnostic work itself establishes its skill family. This team interpretation is necessary because team membership is not separately supplied. For a parallel join, simultaneous incoming transfer delays combine by their maximum, while all transfer edges count toward burden. Coordination adds eight elapsed minutes if branches actually overlap. Transfers and coordination do not consume productive labor in the model. Assignment holds the receiving worker during transfer, a conservative scheduling simplification requiring sensitivity testing.
- **Setup:** five worker-minutes when a resource's diagnostic family changes between different cases; intervening Intake/Validation does not reset the last diagnostic family. Switching between the two branches of the same case does not incur this particular penalty, following the wording “between consecutive cases.” Treating that switch as chargeable is a sensitivity case.

The decision variables are resource assignment, queue order, time and destination of overflow, branch mode and ownership, reservation of capacity, interruption and resumption, overtime, and any additional staffing or shift coverage. Branch decisions compare estimated calendar-aware completion times, transfer/setup costs and the capacity displaced from other cases. Estimates use service means and observed work already performed, not advance knowledge of simulated service draws.

Two useful objectives give different policy rankings:

1. **Efficiency objective:** minimize expected arrival-to-completion time plus priced setup, transfer and overtime burden. Impose skill/calendar constraints and separately report SLA misses. This can favor broad pooling even when the shortest mean comes at the expense of P3 or occasional P1 cases.
2. **Robust service objective:** first seek feasibility of all three 90% targets, non-growing backlog under repeated demand, and base planned individual utilization at most 90%. Among feasible alternatives, minimize worst-scenario P1 shortfall, then worst-scenario normalized tail lateness, with utilization dispersion and overtime as additional costs. For case \(i\), define \(Z_i=\max(0,T_i/d_{p_i}-1)\); use CVaR at 90%, the average of the worst tenth of \(Z_i\), rather than just the average delay. A penalized version is \(\max_s[1000v_{1s}+100v_{2s}+10v_{3s}+\mathrm{CVaR}_{.90,s}(Z)]+100F_0+\lambda O\), where \(v_{ps}=(.90-\Pr_s[T\le d_p])_+\), \(F_0\) is base utilization dispersion and \(O\) is overtime. These weights express a proposed service preference, not an estimated financial valuation.

Without probabilities for U1–U5, a weighted “average scenario” is unjustified. I use the second objective to identify a deployable design and explicitly report infeasibility with the current roster; the illustrative simulations are insufficient to establish dominance between closely performing policies.

# 3. Candidate policies

All three policies use the same arrivals and service draws, enforce qualifications and calendars, run Intake before diagnostics and Validation after all required branches, and retain the arrival-based SLA clock. Operators can audit a lexicographic dispatch key: earlier components take precedence; task ID and resource name resolve ties deterministically.

**A. Strict specialization with fixed queues.** Mechanical goes only to Ada/Ben, Electrical only to Chen/Deepa, and Software only to Emil. Intake and Validation have the fixed eligible set Ben/Deepa/Emil/Farah. These are fixed queue memberships, including shared support work, rather than dedicated single-task workers. Dispatch by original priority, arrival time, then estimated assignment completion. There is no reserved capacity and no cross-skill overflow. Multi-skill branches are released separately; actual overlap incurs coordination. Minimize transfers only among the fixed eligible choices. This deliberately strict, auditable baseline exposes the price of refusing Chen/Farah's morning Software skill and Deepa's late Mechanical skill. A better preplanned specialization roster is a legitimate additional comparator, not evidence that all specialization policies behave identically.

**B. Full qualified pooling.** Every qualified worker can take every ready activity. Dispatch by original priority, absolute deadline, then estimated local completion including multiplier, transfer and setup. Use case ownership when it yields the best completion estimate; do not route solely by the number of people in each queue. There is no reservation and no overflow threshold: eligible capacity is always pooled. At Intake completion compare the best common-worker sequential estimate with the best distinct-worker parallel estimate. Choose parallel routing when its predicted completion is earlier; otherwise reserve ownership of both sequential branches to the selected common worker. Actual queues and calendars can still prevent planned branches from overlapping.

**C. Hybrid with a virtual P1 reserve and controlled overflow.** Initially admit diagnostic assignments with multiplier at most 1.25. Permit slower qualified assignments for P1 immediately, or for other cases when that diagnostic queue has waited 30 minutes or nominal case slack is at most 30 minutes. Sequential case ownership is an explicit exception, evaluated as a whole two-branch plan. Nominal slack is deadline minus current time minus expected fastest remaining critical-path work; it is an estimate, not a guaranteed bound.

For each live P1 with nominal slack below 30 minutes, protect the fastest currently available qualified worker for its waiting/current stage; if Intake is unfinished, also protect upcoming diagnostic skills. The reserve prohibits starting new lower-priority diagnostic work on those workers. Intake/Validation may backfill, so this is a **virtual diagnostic reserve**, not a guarantee of an immediately idle worker. With no threatened P1, the restriction releases immediately. A stricter forecast-based 15-minute reserve is a pilot variant, not part of the reported experiment.

Hybrid dispatch uses effective priority, deadline, and then:
\[
\text{estimated local completion}+60\max(0,u_r-0.80)+15\,\mathbf1\{\text{software queued and Chen/Emil used for I/M/V}\}.
\]
The two additions are minute-valued policy penalties. They encourage workload balance and preserve scarce Software capacity without overriding a higher-priority case. P3 cases older than four hours are promoted to P2's dispatch rank but retain their true deadlines; this improves rank without providing a guaranteed P3 service share. Select parallel routing only for at least 15 minutes of predicted gain, or when it changes a P1 forecast from missing to meeting its deadline. Otherwise keep the two branches on one qualified owner. Both resource continuity and setup avoidance are included directly in completion estimates.

**Common interruption rule.** Consider interruption only for a waiting P1 with nominal slack below 30 minutes, no idle eligible worker, and an eligible worker actively performing a lower-priority diagnostic activity. The victim case must have no previous P1 interruption. Interrupt only if estimated remaining service exceeds the new task's transfer/setup costs plus the estimated restart penalty; select the least estimated restart cost. Increase the victim's remaining work by 10%, requeue it, and preserve its original deadline. This is a transparent local test, not a claim of global optimality. Intake, Validation and setup are not preempted; the IT outage suspends Intake without creating a P1-preemption penalty.

None of these rules creates the missing capacity. The utilization penalty is a tie-breaking preference, not enforcement of a feasible 90% staffing plan.

The code also prints 10th–90th percentile ranges **across seed-level metrics**. Those are simulation-day variability ranges, not confidence intervals for a population parameter. With only roughly a hundred P1 cases per ordinary scenario, a few successes can change the ranking; incident-day dependence makes naive binomial precision optimistic. Do not interpret rounded SLA percentages as evidence that a policy attains 90% reliably.

The main numerical trade-off is visible rather than assumed. Pooling has the lowest mean delay in every scenario in this experiment; in Base it averages about 599 minutes versus 612 for Hybrid and 796 for Specialist. The severe overnight penalties are consistent with empty-start cohorts spilling beyond the staffed day. Hybrid's worst-scenario P1 attainment is about 71.7%, versus 65.4% for Pool and 44.0% for Specialist, so its point estimates favor the proposed worst-scenario P1 objective. Pool has better base P1 attainment, however, and the hybrid's fairness penalty does not yield lower utilization dispersion: Pool is more even in this sample. Hybrid usually reduces setup burden relative to Pool but can add handovers and waits.

The Base 10th–90th percentile range of seed-level mean delay is approximately 502–725 minutes for Pool and 501–745 for Hybrid. There are 866 simulated cases, including 128 P1 cases, in each ordinary-demand scenario across the twenty seeds; U1/U5 have 1,090 cases including 191 P1. Their sample arrival averages differ from the expected 42/53.2 per day. These common samples support a paired comparison, but neither small policy differences nor the ranking should be treated as operationally established. **No row achieves all three SLAs, and none demonstrates stable recurring-day service.**

Low realized utilization alongside large queues is not spare interchangeable capacity: shifts, qualifications, held transfer assignments, case ownership and the priority rule can prevent useful work. Conversely, a favorable utilization dispersion can simply mean that everyone is overloaded. The offered-load calculation, busy utilization, transfer-reservation time, backlog and SLA outcomes must be read together.

# 5. Robust recommendation

**Adopt the hybrid as the operational design to pilot, conditional on adding or rearranging sufficient capacity and validating its P1 protection; do not declare the current roster robust.** It makes scarce-skill protection, overflow, continuity and fairness visible to operators. Full pooling remains a serious alternative where its measured tail/P1 results are better. The recommendation is a trade-off under the robust service objective, not a claim that the sample table proves hybrid dominance.

Separate two decisions. First, repair the capacity and coverage problem. Under the midpoint assumptions the base roster needs at least 532.9 additional available minutes at 90% utilization before assignment losses; U5 would need at least 1,784.6 additional available minutes relative to its 2,040-minute roster if its load recurred daily. These are optimistic aggregate lower bounds, not a request for exactly that many workers. Place new or shifted coverage where it can actually help: morning Software/Electrical backup, recovery capacity through 16–20, and a mechanical-qualified option after 18:00. Moving an existing shift later fixes part of the timing mismatch but adds no daily labor, and must not leave the morning surge uncovered. A late case's required Intake and Validation also need coverage.

Second, introduce the hybrid dispatch controls and tune them against an explicitly approved P1/tail/fairness objective. Use the following initial, auditable operating triggers, treated as design thresholds rather than fitted optima:

- **Overflow:** diagnostic queue age at least 30 minutes, nominal slack at most 30 minutes, or any ready P1. Allow the slower assignment only after comparing its completion estimate with waiting for the faster specialist and recording the expected gain.
- **P1 reserve:** start the virtual reservation when nominal slack drops below 30 minutes; prioritize the pending Intake/Validation gate as well as diagnostic skill. Escalate when the best qualified calendar-aware forecast is already beyond the P1 deadline. Preemption is useful only where a suitable lower-priority diagnostic job exists and is eligible for its one interruption.
- **Stress mode:** trigger on Chen's absence, an Intake outage, observed arrivals exceeding 1.4 times the block expectation, or forecast two-hour work exceeding 90% of qualified available capacity. The last trigger will often fire in the supplied base scenario; that is a staffing signal, not a threshold to relax until the dashboard turns green. Review forecasts at least every 15 minutes.
- **Stress response:** release ordinary overflow restrictions where doing so improves deadline completion, preserve threatened P1 specialists, finish near-complete cases through Validation, and activate a prearranged qualified backup or late coverage plan. Allow at most 30 minutes of overtime per resource as a short recovery action. An external backup is a proposed contingency requiring actual availability; none is assumed in the comparison table.
- **Recovery:** return to normal restrictions only after two consecutive 30-minute reviews show no threatened P1, no diagnostic queue over 30 minutes, and forecast qualified load below 85%. Continue draining aged P3 work; do not clear the dashboard by reclassifying or resetting cases.

Before operational acceptance, require base planned utilization at most 90% per worker with a non-increasing backlog over repeated days, and credible evidence for the priority targets across all supplied scenarios. If the present staffing budget is fixed, report the service targets as unproven and the recurring-load plan as infeasible under the chosen means. Explicit prioritization and escalation remain useful, but cannot turn that into an SLA guarantee.

Prefer full pooling if a sufficiently staffed pilot finds better worst-scenario P1 attainment or tail delay without excessive churn. Prefer fixed specialty queues when demand is lighter and predictable, ownership/setup savings are large, and the roster explicitly covers every required skill throughout its demand window. Neither preference should be inferred from the policy label alone.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

- **Gaming and estimate bias:** deadline-based routing creates incentives to overstate priority or understate job size. Freeze original priority and deadlines in the audit log, require reason codes for changes, and compare estimate error by type, resource, priority and policy. Do not penalize an operator merely for accurately forecasting a difficult case.
- **P3 starvation:** promotion into P2 rank is insufficient when the promoted case still has a much later deadline and the system is overloaded. Track oldest P3 age and a protected P3 work share in the pilot; activate recovery staffing before the 24-hour limit becomes unreachable. A proposed share, such as 10% of non-P1 capacity whenever aged P3 exists, must be tested against P2 and is not implemented in the numerical experiment.
- **Skill erosion and fatigue:** always routing Software to the fastest worker can erode backup competence and overburden that individual. Schedule supervised cross-skill work during genuine slack, then re-estimate multipliers. Equal job counts are not fairness: minutes, complexity, interruptions, overtime and undesirable shifts matter.
- **Interruption escalation:** the one-interruption limit prevents repeated P1 suspension of the same case, but many distinct victims can still incur restart work and fatigue. Monitor the number of interrupted cases and actual lost work. Prefer a soon-free qualified worker if the P1 deadline remains safe. Observe nonpreemptible Intake/Validation blocking separately.
- **Coordination and transfer overhead:** apparent parallel speedups may disappear through booking delays, resource changes and synchronization. Record branch-level transfer edges and join readiness. Test whether transfer/coordination requires sender or receiver labor; the assumed elapsed-only cost can otherwise understate staffing needs.
- **Validation migration:** faster branch completion can release a burst into Validation. Its base 661.1 minutes are substantial. A protected P1 diagnostic slot is ineffective if the case then waits for Validation or arrives there after its validator's shift.
- **Fairness and interpretability:** the soft fairness penalty does not enforce the 90% planning constraint. Publish the qualification decision, priority/deadline, continuity cost, predicted completion, reservation reason and overflow trigger for each assignment. Permit a logged supervisor override. Keep estimates and policy versions stable long enough to distinguish changed demand from changed behavior.

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design

Measure arrival-to-completion SLA attainment by priority, type and arrival block, with confidence intervals and all unfinished cases visible. Report mean, p90/p95 and normalized tail lateness; aged work in progress; work entering versus leaving each skill queue; oldest waiting case; and forecast completion against deadline. Use cohort-based SLA denominators: do not improve attainment artificially by excluding unfinished or overnight cases. Live cohorts require censoring-aware reporting; completed-case-only averages can be severely biased.

For each worker, monitor busy service, setup, transfer reservation, available time after breaks, overtime, P1 interruptions, and rolling planned utilization. Track utilization dispersion alongside the absolute maximum and burden of difficult work. For resilience, track time to recover baseline backlog, excess backlog after a shock, and net backlog growth under consecutive shock or ordinary days, not just time to empty after arrivals stop.

The production event schema should include:

| Record | Required attributes and purpose |
|---|---|
| Case and SLA | Stable case ID; type; original/current priority; arrival; original/current due time; elapsed SLA clock; completion/censoring; shock/scenario flag; policy version |
| Activity instance | Activity-instance ID; case ID; branch ID; predecessor IDs; join/split mode; skill used; queue-entry/eligibility time; resource; planned and actual active service; estimate version; start and completion |
| Assignment decision | Decision timestamp; full qualified set; currently available set; reservation/overflow exclusions; estimates and dispatch scores; selected resource; reason; override and author |
| Pause/resume | Activity-instance ID; pause and resume timestamps; reason: P1, break, shift end or IT outage; remaining-work estimate; restart work; victim-case interruption count; resuming worker |
| Handover/setup | Source/destination activity and resource; team transition; start/end; elapsed delay versus worker effort; skill-family change; planned/actual setup |
| Capacity context | Roster, absence, break and overtime intervals; queue state and predicted work at decision time; outage intervals; staffing or policy changes |

Store **active intervals separately** from transfer, setup and suspended intervals. A diagnostic activity that resumes has the same activity-instance ID across multiple active segments. A parallel branch needs explicit predecessor and join identities; a timestamp sort is not causal evidence.

Use PM4Py as follows:

1. Format the interval event data with explicit start and completion keys. `get_service_time(..., start_timestamp_key='start_timestamp')` on active segments estimates *segment* duration. Aggregate segments by activity-instance ID first to obtain total active labor per activity; creating one first-start/last-end interval would wrongly include interruptions. Compare service distributions by type, skill, resource and scenario, controlling for changing case mix before calling a change “drift.”
2. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` supplies case-level aggregates, not stage queue measurements. In the implementation checked for this answer, service is the **sum of event-interval durations**, sojourn is last completion minus first activity start, and “waiting” is their difference. Overlapping branches can make that difference misleading or negative; initial Intake queueing is also absent from the span. Compute arrival-to-completion separately and subtract the **union** of active intervals for elapsed time with no active service. Even that residual contains nights, transfers, setup, outages and coordination, not just queueing.
3. Measure each stage's queue-entry to assignment, assignment to first active start, total active service, and suspension separately from explicit records. Queue times on two parallel branches must not simply be added and called case delay. Inspect time-series queue workload and waiting quantiles by skill and shift boundary to expose migration from Electrical/Software to Intake or Validation.
4. `discover_handover_of_work_network` gives a resource succession network based on event order. It is useful for screening changing collaboration patterns, but parallel completions, split active segments and shared joins can create false “handovers.” Validate against explicit causal predecessor/source/destination edges, deduplicate resumes, and attach measured transfer costs rather than treating network edge counts as elapsed delay.
5. Discover or inspect performance directly-follows graphs for common-stage delays and variants, then validate the known workflow with a Petri net containing optional AND splits/joins for ME/ES. Use conformance checking for missing branches, premature Validation, ineligible execution and policy deviations, with calendars and interruption limits checked from attributes. Avoid interpreting a completion-ordered flattened trace as a proof of sequential execution. Compare weekly variants and parameter estimates with stable historical windows and flag drift for investigation rather than assuming its cause.

# 8. Reproducible Python

The following self-contained experiment requires NumPy, pandas and PM4Py; it was run with NumPy 2.5.0, pandas 3.0.4 and PM4Py 2.7.23.8. The grouped-apply call requires pandas 2.2 or later. It encodes all supplied tables, applies all six scenarios to three policies, uses common demand/service draws across policies, emits an in-memory interval log and assignment log, and prints compact comparisons with repeated-seed spread. It was executed for the estimates above without obtaining external data.

It is a **one-minute event simulation scaffold**, not an optimized scheduler. Each minute processes arrivals, calendar changes, priority interruptions, assignments, setup/service and completions. Durations are rounded by at most one minute per uninterrupted completion segment. Explicit simplifications are: no overtime; no future-day arrivals; approximate look-ahead that moves predicted starts into availability but does not fully propagate predicted completion through future breaks, shift ends or queued contenders; fixed order of sequential branches; no optimization of coordinated resource booking; recipient held during elapsed transfer; no sender labor for transfer or coordinator labor; neutral support-team membership for Deepa/Farah; a virtual rather than forecast-based P1 reserve; soft fairness; and no guaranteed P3 quota. The dispatch estimate observes consumed work but not its hidden sampled total. Execution does enforce calendars, so a sequential owner near shift end can take much longer than its branch-selection estimate predicts. All these omissions must be addressed or sensitivity-tested before forecasting production performance.

The generated active intervals preserve task IDs across interruptions; their gaps allow suspension reconstruction. The small scaffold logs assignments and active/setup intervals, but does not emit every production pause reason or rejected alternative estimate specified in Section 7. Such instrumentation is a deployment requirement, not a claim about the sample log. Cohorts must drain within seven calendar days or the code raises rather than silently dropping censored cases.

```python
import math
from itertools import permutations
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

ACT = ['Intake', 'Mechanical', 'Electrical', 'Software', 'Validation']
DIAG = ACT[1:4]
SERVICE = pd.DataFrame([
    [5,8,10,14,.08], [18,28,35,55,.15], [22,35,40,65,.18],
    [25,40,45,80,.20], [10,18,22,35,.12]], index=ACT,
    columns=['lo','hi','tail_lo','tail_hi','p'])
SKILL = pd.DataFrame([
    [1,1,1.35,np.nan,1.15], [1.1,1,np.nan,np.nan,1],
    [1.1,np.nan,1,1.25,1.1], [1,1.25,1,np.nan,1],
    [1.1,np.nan,1.3,1,1], [1,1.4,1.4,1.4,1.2]],
    index=['Ada','Ben','Chen','Deepa','Emil','Farah'], columns=ACT)
# Minutes since 08:00; repeated every 1440 elapsed minutes.
SHIFT = pd.DataFrame([[0,480,255,285],[0,480,240,270],
    [0,480,270,300],[120,600,360,390],[240,720,480,510],
    [0,240,0,0]], index=SKILL.index,
    columns=['start','end','break_start','break_end'])
DEMAND = pd.DataFrame([
    [7,10,55,35,30,25,15,20,10], [9,15,55,30,25,25,15,25,10],
    [7,12,58,30,22,28,18,20,12], [10,18,52,30,20,30,18,20,12],
    [6,10,50,40,20,25,25,15,15], [3,5,45,50,25,20,30,10,15]],
    columns=['n','P1','P2','P3','M','E','S','ME','ES'], dtype=float)
SCENARIOS = pd.DataFrame([
    ['Base',1,0,1,False,1,False], ['U1',1.7,.08,1,False,1,False],
    ['U2',1,0,2,False,1,False], ['U3',1,0,1,True,1,False],
    ['U4',1,0,1,False,2,False], ['U5',1.7,.08,1,True,1,True]],
    columns=['scenario','morning_factor','p1_add','E_ES_weight',
             'chen_absent','diagnostic_tail_factor','intake_outage']).set_index('scenario')
TYPES = {'M':['Mechanical'], 'E':['Electrical'], 'S':['Software'],
         'ME':['Mechanical','Electrical'], 'ES':['Electrical','Software']}
HOME = {'Intake':['Ben','Deepa','Emil','Farah'],
        'Mechanical':['Ada','Ben'], 'Electrical':['Chen','Deepa'],
        'Software':['Emil'], 'Validation':['Ben','Deepa','Emil','Farah']}
TEAM = {'Ada':'M','Ben':'M','Chen':'ES','Deepa':'neutral',
        'Emil':'ES','Farah':'neutral'}
POLICIES = ['Specialist','Pool','Hybrid']
TARGET = {1:120,2:480,3:1440}
HANDOVER, TEAM_MOVE, SETUP, COORDINATION, RESTART = 7,5,5,8,.10
OVERFLOW_WAIT, URGENT_SLACK, PARALLEL_GAIN = 30,30,15
OT_LIMIT = 30
OT_USED = 0  # Conservative experiment: no overtime; 0 <= 30 for every resource.
FAIR_PLAN_LIMIT = .90  # A reported constraint, not a claim that routing makes it feasible.


def scenario_data(name):
    s = SCENARIOS.loc[name]
    d = DEMAND.copy()
    d.loc[:1,'n'] *= s.morning_factor
    for b in [0,1]:
        old = d.at[b,'P1']/100
        new = old + s.p1_add
        d.loc[b,['P2','P3']] *= (1-new)/(1-old)
        d.at[b,'P1'] = 100*new
    d.loc[2:3,['E','ES']] *= s.E_ES_weight
    types = list(TYPES)
    d.loc[:,types] = d[types].div(d[types].sum(axis=1),axis=0)*100
    v = SERVICE.copy()
    v.loc[DIAG,'p'] *= s.diagnostic_tail_factor
    mu = (1-v.p)*(v.lo+v.hi)/2 + v.p*(v.tail_lo+v.tail_hi)/2
    return s,d,v,mu.to_dict()


def capacity_table():
    out=[]
    for sc in SCENARIOS.index:
        s,d,v,mu=scenario_data(sc)
        for b,row in d.iterrows():
            loads={a:row.n*mu[a]*(1 if a in ['Intake','Validation'] else
                sum(row[k]/100 for k,acts in TYPES.items() if a in acts)) for a in ACT}
            cap=0
            for r,z in SHIFT.iterrows():
                if s.chen_absent and r=='Chen': continue
                lo,hi=max(120*b,z.start),min(120*(b+1),z.end)
                cap+=max(0,hi-lo)-max(0,min(hi,z.break_end)-max(lo,z.break_start))
            out.append(dict(scenario=sc,block=b,**loads,capacity=cap,
                            optimistic_load=sum(loads.values())/cap))
    return pd.DataFrame(out)


def draw_cases(sc,seed):
    s,d,v,mu=scenario_data(sc)
    rng=np.random.default_rng(seed)
    cases=[]
    # All case draws precede dispatch: same sample path across policies.
    for b,row in d.iterrows():
        for _ in range(rng.poisson(row.n)):
            p=int(rng.choice([1,2,3],p=row[['P1','P2','P3']].to_numpy()/100))
            k=str(rng.choice(list(TYPES),p=row[list(TYPES)].to_numpy()/100))
            work={}
            for a,z in v.iterrows():
                tail=rng.random()<z.p
                work[a]=rng.uniform(z.tail_lo if tail else z.lo,
                                    z.tail_hi if tail else z.hi)
            arrival=int(math.floor(120*b+rng.uniform(0,120)))
            cases.append(dict(id=len(cases),arrival=arrival,p=p,type=k,work=work,
                              deadline=arrival+TARGET[p],preemptions=0,done=None))
    return cases


class Model:
    def __init__(self,sc,policy,seed,keep_log=False):
        self.sc,self.policy,self.seed=sc,policy,seed
        self.s,self.d,_,self.mu=scenario_data(sc)
        self.cases=draw_cases(sc,seed)
        self.speed=SKILL.to_dict('index')
        self.cal=SHIFT.to_dict('index')
        self.tasks=[]; self.busy={r:None for r in SKILL.index}
        self.last_diag={r:None for r in SKILL.index}
        self.used={r:0 for r in SKILL.index}
        self.den={r:int(z.end-z.start-z.break_end+z.break_start)
                  for r,z in SHIFT.iterrows() if not(self.s.chen_absent and r=='Chen')}
        self.logs=[]; self.last_segment={}; self.decisions=[]
        self.keep=keep_log; self.handover_edges=0; self.handover_minutes=0
        self.setup_minutes=0; self.restart_base=0; self.backlog20=0
        self.t=0

    def on(self,r,t):
        day,m=divmod(t,1440); z=self.cal[r]
        return not(day==0 and self.s.chen_absent and r=='Chen') and \
            z['start']<=m<z['end'] and not(z['break_start']<=m<z['break_end'])

    def nominal_remaining(self,c):
        # Dispatch uses expected unstarted work, never the hidden sampled draw.
        done={q['a'] for q in self.tasks if q['c'] is c and q['status']=='done'}
        diag=[self.mu[a] for a in TYPES[c['type']] if a not in done]
        return (0 if 'Intake' in done else self.mu['Intake']) + \
            (max(diag) if diag else 0) + (0 if 'Validation' in done else self.mu['Validation'])

    def slack(self,c):
        return c['deadline']-self.t-self.nominal_remaining(c)

    def qualified(self,a):
        return [r for r in SKILL.index if not math.isnan(self.speed[r][a])]

    def eligible(self,q):
        candidates=self.qualified(q['a'])
        if q.get('pin'): candidates=[q['pin']]
        if self.policy=='Specialist':
            candidates=[r for r in candidates if r in HOME[q['a']]]
        if self.policy=='Hybrid' and q['a'] in DIAG and not q.get('pin'):
            if q['c']['p']!=1 and self.t-q['ready']<OVERFLOW_WAIT and self.slack(q['c'])>URGENT_SLACK:
                candidates=[r for r in candidates if self.speed[r][q['a']]<=1.25]
        return candidates

    def new_task(self,c,a,parents,ready,pin=None):
        q=dict(id=len(self.tasks),c=c,a=a,parents=parents,ready=ready,qentry=ready,
               status='queue',remaining=c['work'][a],last_r=None,pin=pin,
               first_start=None,queue_wait=0,service=0,setup=0,
               observed_base=0,restart_estimate=0)
        self.tasks.append(q)
        return q

    def transfer(self,q,r):
        # Incoming transfers can occur concurrently: elapsed gate=max; burden=sum.
        src=[(q['last_r'],q['a'])] if q['last_r'] else \
            [(p['last_r'],p['a']) for p in q['parents']]
        edges=[]
        def team(worker,a):
            return 'M' if a=='Mechanical' else ('ES' if a in DIAG else TEAM[worker])
        for old,a in src:
            if old is not None and old!=r:
                cross={team(old,a),team(r,q['a'])}=={'M','ES'}
                edges.append(HANDOVER+TEAM_MOVE*cross)
        return max(edges,default=0),edges

    def setup(self,q,r):
        last=self.last_diag[r]
        return SETUP if q['a'] in DIAG and last and \
            last[0]!=q['a'] and last[1]!=q['c']['id'] else 0

    def expected_work(self,q,r):
        # Estimate remaining active work from observable effort already expended.
        base_done=q.get('observed_base',0)
        return max(5,self.mu[q['a']]+q.get('restart_estimate',0)-base_done)*self.speed[r][q['a']]

    def eta(self,q,r):
        t=self.t
        # Approximate calendar-aware look-ahead; final simulation uses exact calendars.
        if self.busy[r] is not None:
            old=self.busy[r]
            t+=math.ceil(self.expected_work(old,r)+old.get('setup_left',0))
        while not self.on(r,t): t+=1
        return t+self.transfer(q,r)[0]+self.setup(q,r)+self.expected_work(q,r)

    def diagnostic_plan(self,c,parent):
        acts=TYPES[c['type']]
        if len(acts)==1:
            self.new_task(c,acts[0],[parent],self.t); c['mode']='single'; return
        probes=[dict(a=a,c=c,parents=[parent],last_r=None,
                     remaining=c['work'][a],ready=self.t) for a in acts]
        a,b=probes
        qa=self.qualified(a['a']); qb=self.qualified(b['a'])
        common=set(qa)&set(qb)
        seq=[(self.eta(a,r)+self.mu[b['a']]*self.speed[r][b['a']],r) for r in common]
        seqbest=min(seq,default=(float('inf'),None))
        par=[(max(self.eta(a,r),self.eta(b,u))+COORDINATION,r,u)
             for r,u in permutations(SKILL.index,2) if r in qa and u in qb]
        parbest=min(par,default=(float('inf'),None,None))
        threshold=0 if self.policy=='Pool' else PARALLEL_GAIN
        parallel=self.policy=='Specialist' or seqbest[1] is None or \
            seqbest[0]-parbest[0]>=threshold or \
            (c['p']==1 and parbest[0]<=c['deadline']-self.mu['Validation']<seqbest[0])
        c['mode']='parallel' if parallel else 'sequential'
        if parallel:
            for act in acts: self.new_task(c,act,[parent],self.t)
        else:
            q=self.new_task(c,acts[0],[parent],self.t,pin=seqbest[1])
            q['next_act']=acts[1]

    def finish(self,q,r):
        q['status']='done'; q['end']=self.t; self.busy[r]=None
        c=q['c']
        if q['a']=='Intake': self.diagnostic_plan(c,q)
        elif q['a']=='Validation': c['done']=self.t
        elif 'next_act' in q:
            self.new_task(c,q['next_act'],[q],self.t,pin=r)
        else:
            diag=[p for p in self.tasks if p['c'] is c and p['a'] in DIAG]
            if len(diag)==len(TYPES[c['type']]) and all(p['status']=='done' for p in diag):
                self.new_task(c,'Validation',diag,
                              self.t+(COORDINATION if c.get('actual_parallel',False) else 0))

    def preempt(self):
        # At most one P1 interruption per victim case; calendars are not P1 preemptions.
        waiting=[q for q in self.tasks if q['status']=='queue' and q['ready']<=self.t and q['c']['p']==1]
        for q in sorted(waiting,key=lambda q:q['c']['deadline']):
            if self.slack(q['c'])>=URGENT_SLACK: continue
            if self.s.intake_outage and 90<=self.t<120 and q['a']=='Intake': continue
            elig=[r for r in self.eligible(q) if self.on(r,self.t)]
            if any(self.busy[r] is None for r in elig): continue
            victims=[]
            for r in elig:
                old=self.busy[r]
                if old and old['a'] in DIAG and old['c']['p']>1 and \
                        old['c']['preemptions']==0 and old['first_start'] is not None and \
                        self.t>=old['transfer_until'] and old['setup_left']==0:
                    cost=RESTART*self.expected_work(old,r)
                    if self.expected_work(old,r)>self.transfer(q,r)[0]+self.setup(q,r)+cost:
                        victims.append((cost,r,old))
            if victims:
                _,r,old=min(victims,key=lambda x:(x[0],x[1]))
                old['restart_estimate']+=RESTART*self.expected_work(old,r)/self.speed[r][old['a']]
                penalty=RESTART*old['remaining']; old['remaining']+=penalty
                self.restart_base+=penalty; old['c']['preemptions']+=1
                old.update(status='queue',qentry=self.t)
                self.busy[r]=None

    def reserved(self):
        # A virtual reservation for known P1 work approaching diagnostics.
        if self.policy!='Hybrid': return set()
        reserve=set()
        for c in self.cases:
            if c['arrival']>self.t or c['done'] is not None or c['p']!=1: continue
            if self.slack(c)>URGENT_SLACK: continue
            live=[q for q in self.tasks if q['c'] is c and q['status']!='done']
            acts=[q['a'] for q in live]
            if 'Intake' in acts: acts+=TYPES[c['type']]
            for a in acts:
                possible=[r for r in self.qualified(a) if self.on(r,self.t)]
                if possible: reserve.add(min(possible,key=lambda r:(self.speed[r][a],r)))
        return reserve

    def assign(self,q,r,score,eligible):
        h,edges=self.transfer(q,r); setup=self.setup(q,r)
        q['queue_wait']+=self.t-q['qentry']
        q.update(status='active',last_r=r,transfer_until=self.t+h,setup_left=setup)
        self.busy[r]=q
        self.handover_edges+=len(edges); self.handover_minutes+=sum(edges)
        if self.keep:
            self.decisions.append(dict(case=c_id(q),task=q['id'],activity=q['a'],
                queue_entry=q['qentry'],assignment=self.t,resource=r,
                eligible=','.join(eligible),score=str(score),handover_delay=h,
                handover_edges=len(edges),setup_planned=setup,policy=self.policy,
                scenario=self.sc,deadline=q['c']['deadline'],
                planned_active_service=self.expected_work(q,r),
                remaining_base_work=q['remaining']))

    def dispatch(self):
        reserve=self.reserved()
        while True:
            options=[]
            for q in self.tasks:
                if q['status']!='queue' or q['ready']>self.t: continue
                c=q['c']; rank=c['p']
                if self.policy=='Hybrid' and rank==3 and self.t-c['arrival']>=240: rank=2
                elig=self.eligible(q)
                for r in elig:
                    if not self.on(r,self.t) or self.busy[r] is not None: continue
                    if self.policy=='Hybrid' and r in reserve and c['p']>1 and q['a'] in DIAG: continue
                    if self.s.intake_outage and 90<=self.t<120 and q['a']=='Intake': continue
                    h,_=self.transfer(q,r); effort=self.expected_work(q,r)+self.setup(q,r)
                    finish=h+effort
                    if self.policy=='Specialist': score=(rank,c['arrival'],finish,q['id'],r)
                    elif self.policy=='Pool': score=(rank,c['deadline'],finish,q['id'],r)
                    else:
                        util=self.used[r]/self.den[r] if self.t<720 and r in self.den else 0
                        fairness=60*max(0,util-.80)
                        # Preserve an available software worker when software is queued.
                        software_wait=any(x['status']=='queue' and x['a']=='Software' for x in self.tasks)
                        scarce=15 if software_wait and r in ['Chen','Emil'] and q['a'] in ['Intake','Mechanical','Validation'] else 0
                        score=(rank,c['deadline'],finish+fairness+scarce,q['id'],r)
                    options.append((score,q,r,elig))
            if not options: break
            score,q,r,eligible=min(options,key=lambda x:x[0])
            self.assign(q,r,score,eligible)

    def tick_log(self,q,r,kind):
        if not self.keep: return
        prev=self.last_segment.get(r)
        key=(q['id'],kind)
        if prev and prev['_key']==key and prev['end']==self.t:
            prev['end']=self.t+1; return
        row=dict(_key=key,case=c_id(q),activity=q['a'],resource=r,
                 start=self.t,end=self.t+1,kind=kind,task=q['id'],
                 priority=q['c']['p'],case_type=q['c']['type'],
                 arrival=q['c']['arrival'],ready=q['ready'],
                 deadline=q['c']['deadline'],policy=self.policy,scenario=self.sc)
        self.logs.append(row); self.last_segment[r]=row

    def run(self):
        arrivals={}
        for c in self.cases: arrivals.setdefault(c['arrival'],[]).append(c)
        for t in range(7*1440):
            self.t=t
            if t==720: self.backlog20=sum(c['done'] is None for c in self.cases)
            for c in arrivals.get(t,[]): self.new_task(c,'Intake',[],t)
            # Shift-end migration is allowed; breaks retain the assignment.
            for r,q in list(self.busy.items()):
                if q is not None and t%1440==self.cal[r]['end']:
                    q.update(status='queue',qentry=t); self.busy[r]=None
            if t>=720 and all(c['done'] is not None for c in self.cases): break
            if not any(self.on(r,t) for r in SKILL.index): continue
            self.preempt(); self.dispatch()
            finished=[]; diagnostic_ticks={}
            for r,q in list(self.busy.items()):
                if q is None or not self.on(r,t) or t<q['transfer_until']: continue
                if self.s.intake_outage and 90<=t<120 and q['a']=='Intake': continue
                if t<720: self.used[r]+=1
                if q['setup_left']>0:
                    q['setup_left']-=1; q['setup']+=1; self.setup_minutes+=1
                    self.tick_log(q,r,'setup'); continue
                if q['first_start'] is None: q['first_start']=t
                if q['a'] in DIAG:
                    self.last_diag[r]=(q['a'],q['c']['id'])
                    cid=q['c']['id']; diagnostic_ticks[cid]=diagnostic_ticks.get(cid,0)+1
                    if diagnostic_ticks[cid]>1: q['c']['actual_parallel']=True
                q['remaining']-=1/self.speed[r][q['a']]; q['service']+=1
                q['observed_base']+=1/self.speed[r][q['a']]
                self.tick_log(q,r,'service')
                if q['remaining']<=1e-9: finished.append((q,r))
            self.t=t+1
            for q,r in finished: self.finish(q,r)
            if t>=720 and all(c['done'] is not None for c in self.cases): break
        assert all(c['done'] is not None for c in self.cases), 'Drain horizon censored; extend it.'
        n=len(self.cases)
        delay=np.array([c['done']-c['arrival'] for c in self.cases],dtype=float)
        u=np.array([self.used[r]/den for r,den in self.den.items()])
        out=dict(scenario=self.sc,policy=self.policy,seed=self.seed,n=n,
                 mean_delay=float(delay.mean()) if n else np.nan,
                 p95_delay=float(np.quantile(delay,.95)) if n else np.nan,
                 max_util=float(u.max()),util_sd=float(u.std()),
                 handover_per_case=self.handover_edges/max(1,n),
                 transfer_edge_min_per_case=self.handover_minutes/max(1,n),
                 setup_min_per_case=self.setup_minutes/max(1,n),
                 restart_base_minutes=self.restart_base,backlog20=self.backlog20,
                 clearance_hours=max((c['done'] for c in self.cases),default=720)/60-12,
                 preemptions=sum(c['preemptions'] for c in self.cases),
                 base_fairness_violation=bool(self.sc=='Base' and u.max()>.90))
        for p in [1,2,3]:
            group=[c for c in self.cases if c['p']==p]
            out[f'P{p}_n']=len(group)
            out[f'P{p}_ok']=sum(c['done']<=c['deadline'] for c in group)
        case_df=pd.DataFrame([dict(case=c['id'],arrival=c['arrival'],finish=c['done'],
                                  priority=c['p'],delay=c['done']-c['arrival']) for c in self.cases])
        task_df=pd.DataFrame([dict(case=c_id(q),task=q['id'],activity=q['a'],
                                  ready=q['ready'],first_start=q['first_start'],
                                  end=q['end'],queue_wait=q['queue_wait'],
                                  active_service=q['service'],setup=q['setup']) for q in self.tasks])
        return out,case_df,task_df
```

# 9. Limits and validation plan

Expected arrivals and service ranges alone do not determine queue lengths, SLA attainment, the optimal amount of pooling, or a robust staffing level. Missing inputs include interarrival clustering, cross-stage complexity dependence, type–priority association, opening backlog, multi-day persistence of shocks, shift-calendar details, actual progress information available to dispatchers, team membership, coordination labor, and the operational meaning of a partially completed activity at shift end. Simulation randomness does not capture this model uncertainty. The transfer-reservation assumption and strict queue memberships can particularly affect the magnitude of the policy differences.

The scenario set also does not establish protection against arbitrary combinations: U2 with U4, consecutive U5 days, a late mechanical emergency, or correlated complex P1 ES cases require additional stress tests. The seven-day drain horizon and return of Chen are explicit experiment choices. Recovery without new arrivals is more favorable than normal operation, so the simulation cannot demonstrate long-run stability. The offered-load bounds already warn against that interpretation.

Use a staged validation plan:

1. **Instrument and reconcile.** Capture the events in Section 7, reconcile activity labor with rosters, check that parallel work is not double-counted as elapsed time, and verify handover/setup definitions with operators. Estimate distributions and residual correlations by activity, type, priority and resource. Validate the assumptions about breaks, restarts and actual overtime use.
2. **Verify the model.** Test invariants: no unqualified assignment; no resource performing overlapping active work/setup; no work during breaks or the Intake outage; all required branches complete before Validation; exactly one arrival clock per case; no more than one P1 interruption per victim case; and no overtime over 30 minutes. Check workload conservation in base-work units, including restart increments and minute rounding. Test isolated M/ME/ES cases, both sides of each shift boundary, a P1 interrupting either parallel branch, and a parallel join with two transfers.
3. **Backtest and stress-test offline.** Fit service and arrival models on one period and validate on another. Compare all three dispatch policies on common random sample paths, retaining uncertainty in fitted parameters. Replay multi-day arrivals with opening backlog and repeated shocks; an overloaded scenario should exhibit backlog growth rather than being “stabilized” by deleting late cases. Vary within-range distributions, positive tail correlation, type–priority dependence, literal-volume U2, handover labor, branch booking, P3 quotas and 0–30 minutes of overtime. Historical replay alone cannot identify unobserved counterfactual worker speeds or changed interference; simulation and prospective evidence are both needed.
4. **Run shadow dispatch first.** Show recommendations without changing assignments, record disagreement and predicted deadline risk, and test the proposed staffing/late-coverage plan. Calibrate the nominal 30-minute slack trigger; conditional remaining time for a job that has already run long should replace a simplistic mean-minus-progress estimate when enough data exist.
5. **Pilot on a protected scale.** Use matched shifts or day-level crossover blocks to limit queue-interference contamination, with comparable opening backlog and explicit washout/recovery. Start with P2/P3 routing while retaining an experienced supervisor and qualified P1 backup. Then test P1 recommendations under supervision, with immediate override for threatened deadlines. Avoid assigning individual cases to independent policies while they compete invisibly for the same workers.
6. **Use predefined acceptance and stopping criteria.** Stop or revert on a forecast unavoidable P1 miss without an available contingency, failure of the interruption/calendar rules, unacceptable workload concentration, or accumulating aged backlog. Evaluate P1/P2/P3 attainment and tail delay with day-clustered uncertainty, alongside base planned utilization at most 90%, overtime, handover effort and P3 age. Accumulate enough independent P1-bearing periods that the lower confidence bound can support the 90% target; twenty simulated days cannot do that. Move to broader deployment only after sustained demand and the supplied shocks are supported by the actual staffed system.
