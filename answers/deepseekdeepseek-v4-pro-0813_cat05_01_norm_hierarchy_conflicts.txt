## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

I treat the sources as issue-specific rather than applying a single “higher document always wins” rule.

**Facts treated as fixed**
- The dates, times, amount thresholds, and event logs supplied are real data for the assessment.
- ECD-2 has an explicit effective window: **2026-07-14 09:00 CEST through 2026-07-18 18:00 CEST**.
- The ERP outage may affect timestamp reliability, so non-ERP evidence may be better evidence of real action time.
- The CFO email is not entered into the controlled policy repository.

**Interpretive assumptions**
1. **Source authority is issue-specific**: GPIP-4 is the strongest source for cross-entity integrity, segregation of duties, Compliance clearance, and high-value financial controls. ECD-2 is strongest for emergency operational timing and supplier registration within its own window and scope. The CFO email has persuasive force for finance interpretation, but not for compliance or segregation rules.
2. **ECD-2 is not an A7 CRO exception**: The Chief Risk Officer did not issue or ratify it. I therefore treat ECD-2 as an incident directive that may displace ordinary local purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding rules, but not GPIP-4 segregation, Compliance clearance, or the CRO-only exception power.
3. **Finance interpretation up to EUR 150,000**: I treat the CFO email as a valid temporary interpretation that joint Incident Command authorization may substitute for routine operational/financial approval routing during ECD-2 up to EUR 150,000. This is defeasible: if GPIP-4 A4/A7 is read strictly as reserving all non-competition deviations to the CRO, then the CFO email cannot waive GPIP-4 A2.
4. **Compliance and segregation are non-negotiable**: ECD-2 and the CFO email cannot waive GPIP-4 A1 or A3. New-supplier or above-threshold purchases still require Compliance_Clearance before commitment unless a valid CRO exception exists.
5. **Time of act controls**: I apply rules at the moment of the relevant act, especially supplier commitment, not at case completion.
6. **Late approval is not cure**: Under GPIP-4 A6, later approval can mitigate but does not automatically cure missing pre-commitment approval.
7. **Evidence hierarchy for time**: Contemporaneous telephone recordings, signed PDFs, access logs, and supplier acknowledgments are better than bare ERP timestamps when the ERP clock or document system is unreliable.
8. **Burden of proof**: Where a claimed exception is not evidenced in writing or in a contemporaneous record, I classify the case as noncompliant or indeterminate, not as justified.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

### 5.1 P-505 — strict GPIP-4 A4/A7 reading
**Alternative classification:** NONCOMPLIANT  
**Changed premise:** The CFO email cannot waive GPIP-4 A2 because GPIP-4 A4 says emergency status waives competitive tendering only, and A7 reserves exceptions to the CRO.  
**Why credible:** Board policy is explicit that non-competition approvals remain unless CRO exception exists.  
**Why weak here:** The CFO has explicit authority to interpret Finance controls, and D1 is emergency-scoped and time-limited up to EUR 150,000.

### 5.2 P-507 — strict GPIP-4 financial control or PDF-not-recorded reading
**Alternative classification:** NONCOMPLIANT  
**Changed premise:** Either D1 is ineffective for GPIP-4 A2, or a queued signed PDF is not “recorded” until entered into the system of record.  
**Why credible:** If “recorded” means available in the ERP control system, then ERP entry at 14:30 is more than four hours after 08:55.  
**Why weak here:** The signed PDF was created at 12:40 and the ERP outage/delay is a system evidence problem, not a real control failure.

### 5.3 P-502 — broad reading of ECD-2 “supplier-onboarding requirements”
**Alternative classification:** COMPLIANT or JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION  
**Changed premise:** ECD-2 C4 says it takes precedence over “supplier-onboarding requirements,” and this phrase could include Compliance_Clearance for new suppliers.  
**Why credible:** A broad incident-priority reading would minimize delay in emergency procurement.  
**Why weak here:** ECD-2 C2 specifically permits unapproved supplier use but does not mention Compliance_Clearance; GPIP-4 A3 is an integrity control, and A7 reserves exceptions to CRO.

### 5.4 P-506 — oral extension accepted
**Alternative classification:** JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION or COMPLIANT  
**Changed premise:** Incident Command’s verbal extension of ECD-2 overnight is accepted as continuity of the emergency.  
**Why credible:** Operational commanders may believe they have authority to extend during ongoing recovery.  
**Why weak here:** ECD-2 has an express expiry; no written extension exists; post-expiry emergency normalization is exactly the risk the controls seek to prevent.

### 5.5 P-501 — local B2 approvals are not pre-commitment
**Alternative classification:** COMPLIANT or LOW-SEVERITY  
**Changed premise:** SOP B2 only requires that Plant_Approve and Finance_Approve exist, not that they occur before commitment.  
**Why credible:** Some local approval frameworks separate approval routing from commitment timing.  
**Why weak here:** The purchasing scheme is built around GPIP-4 A2 and A6, and PR/commitment controls are generally pre-commitment by purpose.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Replacement hierarchy/exception clause**

> **Incident Exception Hierarchy.**  
> An incident directive may temporarily override only:  
> (i) ordinary purchasing timing, including the PO-before-commitment sequence;  
> (ii) approved-supplier onboarding/register requirements; and  
> (iii) routine operational approval routing up to a stated amount cap.  
>  
> It may not override, suspend, or dilute:  
> (a) GPIP-4 segregation of duties;  
> (b) GPIP-4 Compliance_Clearance for purchases above EUR 100,000 or from new suppliers;  
> (c) Financial Controller approval required by GPIP-4, except where the CFO issues a written, scoped, time-limited financial authorization; or  
> (d) the Chief Risk Officer’s formal exception power.  
>  
> **Conditions for emergency approval**:  
> - Two distinct Incident Command members must authorize before supplier commitment.  
> - The committer or requester may not be one of the two authorizers if the authorization is used as the final operational approval.  
> - Verbal authorization must be recorded in a durable medium within four hours.  
> - For amounts above EUR 150,000, Legal_Clearance must be obtained before commitment, and ordinary finance approval is not waived.  
>  
> **Expiry and evidence**:  
> - Emergency authorization expires automatically at the stated end time.  
> - Acts after expiry require a new written extension or ordinary controls.  
> - ERP timestamps are rebuttable. Telephone recordings, signed PDFs, access logs, and supplier acknowledgments may prove timely action.  
>  
> **Safeguard against emergency normalization**:  
> - Every use of this clause must be reported to the CRO within five business days.  
> - The CRO must ratify or reject any non-competitive emergency deviation.  
> - No standing “emergency” label may be used outside an active, written, time-limited incident directive.  
> - Procurement, finance, and incident command must conduct an after-action review of emergency purchases before month-end.
