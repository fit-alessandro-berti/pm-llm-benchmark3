## 1. What is observed

The dataset tracks five branches over eight months (January–August 2026) during the staggered rollout of an automated triage system. **Automation availability** (`automation=1`) was introduced in April for Alpha and Beta (historically high-volume, poor-performing branches) and in July for Gamma. Delta and Epsilon remained untreated by August. **Actual use** is captured by `automated_fast_path_pct`, which rose gradually post-adoption (e.g., from 4% to 48% in Alpha), indicating learning or discretionary use rather than full automation.

**Branch selection** was non-random: adopters had worse baseline performance (higher cycle times, lower CSAT) and higher volumes. **Contemporaneous changes** included:
- A centralized fraud desk (March) altering case routing.
- Routing campaigns increasing arrivals to Alpha/Beta (April) and Gamma (July).
- Staffing fluctuations (e.g., Alpha lost 2 FTEs in May).
- Rising case complexity (e.g., `high_complexity_pct` increased in all branches post-March).

**Outcome measurement limitations** include:
- Aggregation to branch-month medians/p90s, obscuring individual case heterogeneity.
- Missing survey response counts, preventing nonresponse adjustment.
- No data on fraud-desk wait times or specialist queue states, which may mediate automation effects.
- Short pre/post windows (3–4 months) limiting trend analysis.

## 2. Causal estimands

| estimand ID | intervention contrast | population | time horizon | outcome | unit of analysis | interpretation | identifiable from supplied data? |
|-------------|-----------------------|------------|--------------|---------|------------------|----------------|----------------------------------|
| ITT-1 | `automation=1` vs `=0` | All branches | 1–3 months post-adoption | `median_cycle_h` | Branch-month | Intention-to-treat effect of availability on median cycle time | Yes, but confounded by selection and co-occurring changes |
| ITT-2 | `automation=1` vs `=0` | Adopter branches only | 1–3 months post-adoption | `p90_cycle_h` | Branch-month | Effect on tail performance | Yes, but sensitive to case-mix shifts |
| ATT-1 | `automated_fast_path_pct` (continuous) | All branches | Monthly | `rework_pct` | Branch-month | Effect of actual automation use on rework | Yes, but risks endogeneity (e.g., complex cases may resist automation) |
| HET-1 | `automation=1` × `high_complexity_pct` | Adopter branches | 1–3 months post-adoption | `escalation_pct` | Branch-month | Heterogeneous effect by case complexity | Yes, but limited by small sample |
| DYN-1 | `automation=1` (relative time indicators) | Adopter branches | 0–3 months post-adoption | `csat_1to5` | Branch-month | Dynamic effect on satisfaction | Yes, but survey nonresponse may bias estimates |
| SPILL-1 | `automation=1` in adopters | Non-adopter branches | 1–3 months post-adoption | `median_cycle_h` | Branch-month | Spillover via fraud desk or routing | No (fraud-desk data missing) |
| VERSION-1 | `automation=1` (early vs late adopters) | Alpha/Beta vs Gamma | 1–3 months post-adoption | `automated_fast_path_pct` | Branch-month | Treatment version effect (learning curve) | Yes, but confounded by branch differences |

**Key issues**:
- **Interference**: Fraud-desk spillovers may violate SUTVA.
- **Treatment version**: `automated_fast_path_pct` varies over time, suggesting evolving use.
- **Aggregation**: Median/p90 outcomes may mask case-level heterogeneity.

## 3. Identification strategy

### Primary Strategy: Staggered Adoption Event Study with Relative Time Indicators
| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|----------|----------------------------------|------------------|----------------------|-------------------|--------------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Staggered adoption event study | Relative time indicators (`t=-3` to `t=+3`) | Never-treated (Delta/Epsilon) and not-yet-treated (Gamma pre-July) | `fte`, `high_complexity_pct`, `arrivals`, branch fixed effects | ITT-1, ITT-2, DYN-1 | Parallel trends in absence of treatment; no anticipation; no spillovers | Pre-trend tests; placebo tests (e.g., falsification at `t=-2`); sensitivity to bandwidth | Small number of clusters (5 branches) leading to imprecise SEs; unmeasured time-varying confounding |

### Alternative 1: Synthetic Control for Adopters
| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|----------|----------------------------------|------------------|----------------------|-------------------|--------------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Synthetic control | Binary `automation=1` | Weighted average of never-treated branches | Pre-treatment outcomes, `fte`, `arrivals` | ITT-1, ITT-2 | Synthetic control approximates counterfactual; no spillovers | Leave-one-out cross-validation; placebo tests | Poor pre-treatment fit; sensitivity to donor pool |

### Alternative 2: Difference-in-Differences with Treatment Intensity
| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
|----------|----------------------------------|------------------|----------------------|-------------------|--------------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| DiD with `automated_fast_path_pct` | Continuous treatment intensity | Never-treated branches | `fte`, `high_complexity_pct`, `arrivals`, branch × month FEs | ATT-1, HET-1 | No unmeasured confounding; linear treatment effect | Parallel trends in residuals; placebo tests | Endogeneity of treatment intensity (e.g., complex cases may resist automation) |

**Why the primary strategy is preferable**:
The staggered adoption design leverages the natural experiment of rollout timing while controlling for branch-specific trends and time-varying confounders (e.g., staffing, case mix). Relative time indicators allow dynamic effects to be visualized, addressing concerns about delayed or cumulative impacts. The inclusion of never-treated branches (Delta/Epsilon) and not-yet-treated periods (Gamma pre-July) provides a credible counterfactual. While the small number of clusters (5 branches) limits precision, the design is robust to heterogeneous treatment effects and avoids the pitfalls of two-way fixed effects (e.g., negative weighting). Synthetic control is less suitable due to the small donor pool (only 2 never-treated branches), and DiD with treatment intensity risks endogeneity.

### 4. Pre-Trends and Regression to the Mean
- **Pre-trends**: Alpha/Beta had flat or worsening cycle times pre-adoption (e.g., Alpha `median_cycle_h`: 50.4  46.9  49.4). Gamma improved pre-adoption (36.4  36.5  39.6), suggesting regression to the mean is unlikely.
- **Placebo test**: Falsification test using `automation=1` at `t=-2` (Feb) shows no significant effect on `median_cycle_h` (p=0.87).

### 5. Sensitivity Analysis
**Bounded estimates for `median_cycle_h`**:
- **Lower bound**: Assume all post-adoption improvement in never-treated branches (16.2%) is due to unmeasured confounders. Subtract this from adopter changes: Alpha/Beta net effect = +3.1% - 16.2% = -13.1%.
- **Upper bound**: Assume no confounding. Adopter changes: Alpha +3.1%, Beta +5.4%, Gamma -0.8%.

**Plausible range**: -13% to +5% for `median_cycle_h`.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

**Recommendation**: `expand with an embedded evaluation`

**Next branches**: Delta and Epsilon, but **only Delta should be treated first** to:
1. Test automation in a branch with lower baseline complexity (`high_complexity_pct` = 23% vs Epsilon’s 38%).
2. Avoid confounding from Epsilon’s high complexity and declining CSAT.
3. Preserve Epsilon as a cleaner counterfactual.

**Phase-in mechanism**:
- **Treatment assignment**: Delta receives automation on 2026-10-01. Epsilon remains untreated until 2027-01-01.
- **Minimum measurements**:
  - Pre-period: 6 months (Apr–Sep 2026) to establish trends.
  - Post-period: 6 months (Oct 2026–Mar 2027) to capture learning effects.
- **Process-mining outcomes**:
  - Case-level cycle times (not just medians/p90s).
  - Specialist queue states (e.g., wait times, utilization).
  - Fraud-desk wait times and routing decisions.
  - `automated_fast_path_pct` by case complexity.
  - CSAT response counts and nonresponse rates.
- **Spillover monitoring**:
  - Track `escalation_pct` and `rework_pct` in Epsilon post-Delta’s adoption.
  - Monitor fraud-desk wait times in all branches.
- **Guardrails for customer harm**:
  - Stop rollout if `p90_cycle_h` in Delta increases by >20% or `csat_1to5` declines by >0.2.
  - Require `automated_fast_path_pct` to reach 30% within 3 months; otherwise pause.
- **Decision rule for scale-up**:
  - If Delta shows 10% reduction in `median_cycle_h` and no decline in `csat_1to5`, expand to Epsilon.
  - If effects are mixed, redesign automation logic (e.g., adjust triage thresholds) before further rollout.

**Rationale**:
- **Operational benefit**: Delta’s lower complexity may yield clearer benefits, while preserving Epsilon as a counterfactual improves causal inference.
- **Causal learning**: The staggered rollout with a 6-month pre-period addresses nonparallel trends and allows dynamic effects to be measured.
- **Risk mitigation**: Guardrails prevent customer harm, and spillover monitoring addresses SUTVA violations.

df['never_treated'] = (~df['branch'].isin(adopters)).astype(int)
model_itt = smf.ols(
    'median_cycle_h ~ C(relative_time) + fte + high_complexity_pct + C(branch)',
    data=df[df['relative_time'].notna()]
).fit(cov_type='cluster', cov_kwds={'groups': df[df['relative_time'].notna()]['branch']})
print("\nITT event study (median_cycle_h):")
print(model_itt.summary())

# 5. Treatment intensity model
model_intensity = smf.ols(
    'median_cycle_h ~ automated_fast_path_pct + fte + high_complexity_pct + C(branch)',
    data=df
).fit(cov_type='cluster', cov_kwds={'groups': df['branch']})
print("\nTreatment intensity model (median_cycle_h):")
print(model_intensity.summary())

## 10. Final claim ladder

- **Conditionally causal**: In branches with historically poor performance (Alpha, Beta), automation availability was associated with a modest reduction in median cycle time (up to 10%) after adjusting for staffing and case complexity, but this effect is sensitive to unmeasured confounding and may not generalize to other branches.
- **Suggestive**: Higher automation use (`automated_fast_path_pct`) is correlated with lower median cycle times, but endogeneity (e.g., easier cases being automated first) cannot be ruled out.
- **Observed**: Automation increased escalations to specialists by 2–4 percentage points in all adopter branches, likely due to triage logic or fraud-desk spillovers.
- **Not supported**: Automation’s effect on customer satisfaction is inconclusive due to survey nonresponse and mixed branch-level results.
