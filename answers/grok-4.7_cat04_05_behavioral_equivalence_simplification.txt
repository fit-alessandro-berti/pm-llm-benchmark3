# 1. Equivalence Notions

Abbreviations used below: **Open**, **Analyze**, **Check**, **Approve**, **Rework**, **Close**. The hidden label is `tau`. A complete run starts in the initial marking and ends in the final marking. The binary loop executes its first child once, then either exits or executes its second child and the first child again, repeating that choice.

Duplicate visible labels are the same label at different **occurrence positions** in a word. They are not distinct identities. Multiplicity and order are observable unless a stated abstraction collapses them. P2 is the only view here that collapses selected labels.

**Visible trace equivalence after hiding `tau`.** Delete every silent step. Keep the word of visible labels. Models are equivalent when those sets of complete words are equal. Partial runs, timestamps, resources, and data are outside this boundary. Under that definition the languages are:

- Let \(D_A = \{\texttt{Analyze Check},\; \texttt{Check Analyze},\; \texttt{Analyze Check Approve},\; \texttt{Check Analyze Approve}\}\).
- \(L_A = \{\texttt{Open}\cdot d_1\cdot(\texttt{Rework}\cdot d_2)\cdots(\texttt{Rework}\cdot d_n)\cdot\texttt{Close} \mid n\ge 1,\; d_i\in D_A\}\).
- Let \(W\) range over \(\texttt{Analyze}\,\texttt{Approve}?\,(\texttt{Rework}\,\texttt{Analyze}\,\texttt{Approve}?)^*\). \(L_B\) is **Open**, then any word obtained by inserting **exactly one** **Check** into a slot of \(W\) (before, between, or after its events), then **Close**.

Thus each iteration of A contributes one **Analyze** and one **Check**, in either order, then an optional **Approve**. B contributes one **Check** for the whole loop, concurrent with every iteration. Neither language contains the other.

**Bounded visible-language equivalence under P3.** Same words as above, restricted to complete visible traces with length at most 12 and at most three **Analyze** occurrences. Equality of those finite sets is a test, not a proof of \(L_A = L_B\). A counterexample inside the bound does prove both bounded and unbounded inequality.

**Business-relevant equivalence under P1.** The observation keeps every visible label, including every **Approve** and its position. `tau` is the unobserved auto-approval branch, so it is deleted and does not identify the **Approve** branch with the auto-approval branch. Two models are P1-equivalent when their visible languages are equal and every admitted word satisfies all four rules: each **Analyze** has its own **Check** before the next **Rework** or before **Close**; **Rework** separates analysis attempts; **Approve** is optional; the number and positions of **Approve** stay visible. \(L_A\) is exactly the set of words meeting those rules if **Check** and **Analyze** of one attempt may finish in either order and **Approve**, when present, follows both. \(L_B\) contains words that break the per-analysis check rule and words in which **Approve** precedes **Check**.

**Abstracted dashboard equivalence under P2.** From a complete visible word, drop **Approve** and `tau`. Record only \((\mathit{n_A},\; \mathit{hasRework},\; \mathit{hasCheck})\), where \(\mathit{n_A}\) is the number of **Analyze** occurrences, \(\mathit{hasRework}\) means at least one **Rework**, and \(\mathit{hasCheck}\) means at least one **Check**, and require **Open** first and **Close** last. Positions of **Check** among **Analyze** are not recorded. Check multiplicity above one is not recorded. Models are P2-equivalent when these records coincide.

A sharper projection already identifies the two languages: erase **Check** and **Approve**, and both languages become \(\{\texttt{Open Analyze}(\texttt{Rework Analyze})^*\texttt{Close}\}\). P2 is coarser than that projection, so dashboard equality follows. It does not preserve P1.

# 2. Structural Comparison

| Feature | Model A | Model B | Semantic consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Loop scope | The loop body is the parallel pair **Analyze** \(\parallel\) **Check**, followed by optional **Approve**. **Rework** is the redo child. | The loop body is only **Analyze** followed by optional **Approve**. **Check** sits outside the loop. | In A, every new attempt repeats a check. In B, further attempts repeat analysis and optional approval only. |
| Minimum **Check** | 1, because the loop body runs at least once and contains one **Check**. | 1, because the parallel branch is a single **Check** and must complete. | Both supply the dashboard’s “a check occurred” bit. |
| Maximum **Check** | Unbounded, and exactly equal to the number of **Analyze** occurrences. | Exactly 1, for any number of analyses. | As soon as two analyses occur, no word of A is a word of B. |
| Placement of **Check** | Inside the attempt: concurrent with that attempt’s **Analyze**, and both before that attempt’s optional **Approve**, before the next **Rework**, and before **Close**. | Anywhere in the parallel region: before the first **Analyze**, between **Analyze** and **Approve**, after **Approve**, between **Rework** and a later **Analyze**, or after the last loop event, but before the join that enables **Close**. | A can put **Rework** only after that attempt’s check. B can put **Rework** before the only check. B can put **Approve** immediately before **Check**. |
| Optional approval | Per attempt, either **Approve** or `tau`, after that attempt’s **Analyze** and **Check** have both finished. Zero or one **Approve** per attempt. | Per attempt, either **Approve** or `tau`, immediately after that attempt’s **Analyze**. The single **Check** may sit on either side of that **Approve**. | Auto-approval adds no visible event. Explicit approval remains a visible event. The word **Analyze Approve Check** is possible only in B. |
| Concurrency | **Analyze** \(\parallel\) **Check** inside one attempt. No concurrency across **Rework**. | The single **Check** is concurrent with the entire loop, including every **Rework** and every **Approve**. | Both orders **Analyze Check** and **Check Analyze** occur in both models, but they mean different control scopes. Footprints that only record “both orders occur” miss that difference. |
| Join before **Close** | Sequence join. The loop’s silent exit fires only after the last attempt has joined its own **Analyze** and **Check** and resolved approval. **Close** follows that exit. | AND-join of the one **Check** branch and the finished loop. **Close** waits for both. | **Close** is last in every complete word of both models. Joining in B does not restore a missing per-attempt check. |
| **Rework** | Redo only, so in every complete word the number of **Rework** events is \(\mathit{n_A}-1\), and **Rework** sits on an attempt boundary. | Same loop arithmetic: number of **Rework** events is \(\mathit{n_A}-1\). | “Any rework occurred” is true exactly when \(\mathit{n_A}\ge 2\) in both languages. That shared fact is why P2 does not separate them. |
| Silent behavior | One tree `tau` (auto-approve), plus routing silents in the net (parallel split, parallel join, loop exit). No silent cycle. | One tree `tau`, plus routing silents (parallel split, parallel join, loop exit). No silent cycle. | Hiding `tau` removes auto-approval from the word. It does not remove **Approve**, and it does not create a livelock. |

Slice theorem, used by the later verdicts. Let \(L^n\) be the complete visible words with exactly \(n\) **Analyze** events.

- Every word in \(L_A^n\) has exactly \(n\) **Check** events. Every word in \(L_B^n\) has exactly one.
- Therefore \(L_A^n \cap L_B^n = \emptyset\) for every \(n\ge 2\).
- For \(n=1\), \(L_A^1\) is the four words **Open**·{**Analyze Check**, **Check Analyze**, **Analyze Check Approve**, **Check Analyze Approve**}·**Close**, and \(L_B^1 = L_A^1 \cup \{\texttt{Open Analyze Approve Check Close}\}\).

# 4. Counterexample Traces

`tau` is not written in the words. Choosing `tau` rather than **Approve** yields the same visible word as skipping approval. Choosing **Approve** inserts that visible label. The two branches stay distinct after hiding `tau`.

| Trace ID | Trace | Model A | Model B | First decisive semantic difference | Relevant view |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A-1 | Open Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Rework Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case | yes | no | Two attempts, two checks, no approval. B’s parallel region contains one **Check** transition. | P1, P3, visible language |
| A-2 | Open Case, Check Revision, Analyze Revision, Approve Revision, Rework Case, Check Revision, Analyze Revision, Close Case | yes | no | First attempt is **Check** then **Analyze** then **Approve**; second attempt has its own **Check**. B cannot emit the second **Check**. | P1, P3, visible language |
| B-1 | Open Case, Analyze Revision, Approve Revision, Check Revision, Close Case | no | yes | **Approve** immediately precedes **Check**. In A the approval choice is enabled only after **Analyze** and **Check** have joined. | P1, P3, visible language |
| B-2 | Open Case, Analyze Revision, Rework Case, Analyze Revision, Check Revision, Close Case | no | yes | Two analyses, one check, and the first **Rework** occurs before any **Check**. A’s redo is enabled only after the attempt’s check. | P1, P3, visible language |

A-1 and B-2 each contain two analysis attempts, and neither contains **Approve**. Hiding approvals, or restricting attention to the auto-approval branch, still separates the models. B-1 is the shortest separator and is the extra one-revision word: the one-revision slice of A is a proper subset of the one-revision slice of B, and the only extra word is B-1. From the second attempt onward the slices are disjoint because check counts differ.

Effect of optional **Approve** and hidden `tau` on the verdicts:

- P1 and visible trace equivalence stay negative on the approval-free witnesses A-1 and B-2. B-1 is an additional, independent separator, and P1 requires that it remain observable.
- P2 hides **Approve** and `tau`. A-1, B-2, and the one-revision words then fall together whenever they share \(\mathit{n_A}\) and the rework bit. That is why P2 equivalence can hold while P1 fails.
- P3 keeps **Approve** and only hides `tau`. All four witnesses are inside the cap, so the bounded visible languages are unequal whether or not approvals are used.

The overlap that does exist is exactly the four one-revision words in which any **Approve** follows **Check**: both orders of **Analyze** and **Check**, with and without a trailing **Approve**. Those four words are why a single short playout can look like agreement.

# 5. Bounded Comparison Code

The exploration below is breadth-first over markings of the translated nets. A state is the pair (marking, visible word). Silent transitions extend the marking and leave the word unchanged, so `tau` is hidden. Revisiting the same pair cuts silent cycles. A word is accepted only when the marking equals the final marking, the visible length is at most 12, and **Analyze** occurs at most three times. That search is exhaustive for the finite P3 fragment. `pm4py.play_out` is included only as a labeled non-authority: it draws a finite random sample of executions, so a missing word in that sample is not evidence of absence.

Hand case-split on the same semantics, for checking the program’s prints: partition by \(n\le 3\) and by which attempts approve. \(|L_A|\) inside P3 is \(4+16+56 = 76\). \(|L_B|\) inside P3 is \(5+20+60 = 85\). The intersection is the four shared one-revision words, so 72 words are unique to A and 81 are unique to B. The three-approval, three-analysis words of A have length 13 and are outside the cap. This case-split is not a substitute for the search; it is the expected count if the net translation preserves the tree language.

```python
"""Exhaustive P3 comparison of Model A and Model B. Requires pm4py."""

from collections import deque

import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import Marking
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.semantics import execute

TREE_A = """
->(
  'Open Case',
  *(
    ->(
      +('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'),
      X('Approve Revision', tau)
    ),
    'Rework Case'
  ),
  'Close Case'
)
"""

TREE_B = """
->(
  'Open Case',
  +(
    'Check Revision',
    *(
      ->(
        'Analyze Revision',
        X('Approve Revision', tau)
      ),
      'Rework Case'
    )
  ),
  'Close Case'
)
"""

MAX_VISIBLE = 12
MAX_ANALYZE = 3
ANALYZE = "Analyze Revision"


def marking_key(net, marking):
    return tuple(
        sorted(
            (place.name, marking[place])
            for place in net.places
            if marking[place] > 0
        )
    )


def same_marking(net, left, right):
    return all(
        (left[place] if place in left else 0) == (right[place] if place in right else 0)
        for place in net.places
    )


def bounded_visible_language(net, initial, final):
    """Exhaustive BFS of complete visible words inside the P3 bound."""
    start = (marking_key(net, initial), tuple())
    queue = deque([(initial, tuple())])
    visited = {start}
    accepted = set()
    while queue:
        marking, word = queue.popleft()
        if (
            same_marking(net, marking, final)
            and len(word) <= MAX_VISIBLE
            and word.count(ANALYZE) <= MAX_ANALYZE
        ):
            accepted.add(word)
        for transition in pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, marking):
            label = transition.label
            if label is None:
                new_word = word
            else:
                if len(word) >= MAX_VISIBLE:
                    continue
                new_word = word + (label,)
                if new_word.count(ANALYZE) > MAX_ANALYZE:
                    continue
            new_marking = execute(transition, net, Marking(marking))
            state = (marking_key(net, new_marking), new_word)
            if state in visited:
                continue
            visited.add(state)
            queue.append((new_marking, new_word))
    return accepted


def load(tree_string):
    tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_string)
    net, initial, final = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)
    sound = pm4py.check_soundness(net, initial, final)
    return tree, net, initial, final, sound


def show(title, words):
    print(f"--- {title}: {len(words)} ---")
    for word in sorted(words):
        print(" | ".join(word))


def main():
    _, net_a, im_a, fm_a, sound_a = load(TREE_A)
    _, net_b, im_b, fm_b, sound_b = load(TREE_B)
    print("sound_A", sound_a)
    print("sound_B", sound_b)
    print("exploration = exhaustive BFS within P3 (visible length <= 12, Analyze <= 3)")
    print("play_out is not used for the verdict; a sample cannot witness absence")

    lang_a = bounded_visible_language(net_a, im_a, fm_a)
    lang_b = bounded_visible_language(net_b, im_b, fm_b)
    only_a = lang_a - lang_b
    only_b = lang_b - lang_a
    both = lang_a & lang_b
    print("count_A", len(lang_a), "count_B", len(lang_b))
    print("count_only_A", len(only_a), "count_only_B", len(only_b), "count_both", len(both))
    show("unique to A", only_a)
    show("unique to B", only_b)

    a1 = (
        "Open Case", "Analyze Revision", "Check Revision", "Rework Case",
        "Analyze Revision", "Check Revision", "Close Case",
    )
    a2 = (
        "Open Case", "Check Revision", "Analyze Revision", "Approve Revision",
        "Rework Case", "Check Revision", "Analyze Revision", "Close Case",
    )
    b1 = (
        "Open Case", "Analyze Revision", "Approve Revision",
        "Check Revision", "Close Case",
    )
    b2 = (
        "Open Case", "Analyze Revision", "Rework Case",
        "Analyze Revision", "Check Revision", "Close Case",
    )
    assert a1 in only_a and a2 in only_a
    assert b1 in only_b and b2 in only_b


def non_exhaustive_sample(tree_string, n_traces=20):
    """Random playout. Not a language equality test."""
    tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_string)
    log = pm4py.play_out(tree, parameters={"no_traces": n_traces, "max_trace_length": MAX_VISIBLE})
    print("NON-EXHAUSTIVE play_out sample size", len(log))
    print("Missing a word in this sample does not mean the model rejects it.")
    return log


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

# 6. Stochastic and Footprint Comparison

**Directly-follows footprints.** On visible words, skipping silent steps, the direct-succession relation of \(L_A\) is:

- **Open** before **Analyze** or **Check**
- **Analyze** before **Check**, **Approve**, **Rework**, or **Close**
- **Check** before **Analyze**, **Approve**, **Rework**, or **Close**
- **Approve** before **Rework** or **Close**
- **Rework** before **Analyze** or **Check**

\(L_B\) has every one of those pairs and one more: **Approve** immediately before **Check** (witness B-1). In the usual footprint matrix that changes the **Check**/**Approve** cell from “**Check** precedes **Approve**” in A to “both direct orders occur” in B. So `pm4py.discover_footprints(...)` on these two models, if it reports visible direct successions, should be unequal, and that inequality is valid evidence against trace equivalence.

It is still not a decision procedure. The matrix does not count occurrences. A-1’s two checks are never adjacent, because **Rework** or another visible event sits between them, so the illegal-for-B word A-1 only uses succession pairs that also occur somewhere in \(L_B\). Erasing **Approve** from both languages removes the single differing pair, and the remaining directly-follows relations coincide, while words with two checks remain outside \(L_B\). Equal footprints would therefore not establish trace equivalence. The relation “both orders occur” also collapses A’s intra-attempt parallelism and B’s loop-wide parallelism of **Analyze** and **Check** into the same cell.

**Stochastic language and Earth Mover’s Distance.** Neither tree declares transition probabilities. A number returned by `pm4py.get_stochastic_language(...)` or `pm4py.compute_emd(...)` is meaningful only after a distribution is declared. One declared illustration, not a fitted model, is: at every marking, choose uniformly among enabled transitions, silent ones included; condition on the P3 bound and renormalize. Under that declaration every P3 word of a model receives positive probability, the two conditioned distributions have different supports, and EMD is strictly positive for any ground distance that is zero only on identical words.

A low EMD still would not establish trace equivalence. Mass can sit on the four shared one-revision words, with a small declared probability on multi-attempt words. EMD can then be arbitrarily small while A-1 stays impossible in B and B-2 stays impossible in A. EMD equal to zero, under a ground metric that separates words and under a declaration that gives every enabled choice positive probability, would force the positive supports inside the truncation to agree. That is a statement about the declared distribution on a finite fragment, not a reading of the unmarked trees, and it is not the situation here.

```python
"""Footprints, plus EMD under a DECLARED uniform-enabled policy. Requires pm4py."""

import pm4py

# Reuse TREE_A, TREE_B, and bounded_visible_language from the previous block
# if both snippets are run in one process. This block is otherwise self-contained
# once `langs` has been computed by that exhaustive search.

def footprint_delta(tree_a_string, tree_b_string):
    tree_a = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_a_string)
    tree_b = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_b_string)
    foot_a = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_a)
    foot_b = pm4py.discover_footprints(tree_b)
    print("footprints_equal", foot_a == foot_b)
    return foot_a, foot_b


def declared_uniform_language(net, initial, final, bounded_language):
    """DECLARED policy: uniform among enabled transitions, including silent.

    Probability of a visible word aggregates the firing sequences that hide tau.
    The mass is then conditioned on the P3 language and renormalized.
    These weights are not data and are not properties of the unmarked net.
    """
    from collections import deque
    from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import Marking
    from pm4py.objects.petri_net.semantics import execute

    # Path-sum implementation sketch: BFS carrying path probability, aggregating
    # by visible word when the final marking is reached inside the P3 bound.
    # Silent cycles are cut by state (marking, word, probability is path-local).
    mass = {word: 0.0 for word in bounded_language}
    queue = deque([(initial, tuple(), 1.0)])
    seen_budget = 0
    while queue:
        marking, word, prob = queue.popleft()
        seen_budget += 1
        if seen_budget > 1000000:
            raise RuntimeError("safety cap; nets are small and should finish earlier")
        enabled = list(pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, marking))
        if not enabled:
            if word in mass and same_marking_local(net, marking, final):
                mass[word] += prob
            continue
        share = prob / len(enabled)
        for transition in enabled:
            label = transition.label
            if label is None:
                new_word = word
            else:
                if len(word) >= 12:
                    continue
                new_word = word + (label,)
                if new_word.count("Analyze Revision") > 3:
                    continue
            new_marking = execute(transition, net, Marking(marking))
            if label is None and same_marking_local(net, new_marking, marking):
                continue
            queue.append((new_marking, new_word, share))
    total = sum(mass.values())
    if total <= 0:
        raise RuntimeError("declared policy put no mass on the P3 language")
    return {word: value / total for word, value in mass.items()}


def same_marking_local(net, left, right):
    return all(
        (left[place] if place in left else 0) == (right[place] if place in right else 0)
        for place in net.places
    )


def emd_under_declared_policy(lang_a, lang_b):
    # Keys must be the words; values must sum to 1 in each argument.
    # compute_emd does not upgrade a small distance into language equality.
    distance = pm4py.compute_emd(lang_a, lang_b)
    print("declared_policy_emd", distance)
    return distance
```

The queue in `declared_uniform_language` can recombine paths that reach the same visible word through different silent routes; summing on acceptance is what the declared policy requires. It is an illustration of the API contract, not a claim that those weights were measured.

# 7. Complexity Comparison

Counting convention: every operator and every leaf is a node. Depth is the number of edges on a longest root-to-leaf path. Visible leaves have a business label. The silent leaf is the explicit `tau`. Duplicate-label count is (visible leaves) minus (distinct visible labels), so runtime repetition caused by the loop is not counted as a duplicated leaf. Both originals have duplicate-label count 0.

The Petri-net figures use the standard inductive translation: a sequence inserts intermediate places; an exclusive choice shares the entry and exit places; a parallel region uses a silent split and a silent join; a loop runs the body from the entry place into a middle place, runs the redo child back to the entry place, and leaves through a silent exit. Under that translation both originals are the same size. Arc-degree simplicity is the usual score \(\bigl(1+\max(\bar d-2,0)\bigr)^{-1}\), where \(\bar d\) is the mean of \(|{}^\bullet x|+|x^\bullet|\) over places and transitions.

| Measure | Model A | Model B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tree nodes | 12 | 12 |
| Operator nodes | 5 (`->`, `*`, `->`, `+`, `X`) | 5 (`->`, `+`, `*`, `->`, `X`) |
| Depth | 4 | 5 |
| Visible leaves | 6 | 6 |
| Silent leaves | 1 | 1 |
| Duplicate-label count | 0 | 0 |
| Net places | 10 | 10 |
| Net transitions | 10, of which 4 are silent | 10, of which 4 are silent |
| Arcs | 22 | 22 |
| Mean arc degree | 2.2 | 2.2 |
| Arc-degree simplicity | 5/6 | 5/6 |

A’s extra silent transitions are the parallel split, the parallel join, and the loop exit, on top of the explicit auto-approval `tau`. B’s are the same three routing silents plus its own auto-approval `tau`. Equal simplicity does not imply equal language: the parallel operator is nested in a different place, which is exactly the check-cardinality gap.

```python
def tree_metrics(tree):
    labels = []
    silent_leaves = 0
    nodes = 0
    depth = 0

    def walk(node, edges):
        nonlocal nodes, silent_leaves, depth
        nodes += 1
        depth = max(depth, edges)
        if node.operator is None:
            if node.label is None:
                silent_leaves += 1
            else:
                labels.append(node.label)
            return
        for child in node.children:
            walk(child, edges + 1)

    walk(tree, 0)
    visible = len(labels)
    return {
        "nodes": nodes,
        "depth": depth,
        "visible_leaves": visible,
        "silent_leaves": silent_leaves,
        "duplicate_label_count": visible - len(set(labels)),
    }


def net_metrics(tree_string):
    tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_string)
    net, initial, final = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)
    print(tree_metrics(tree))
    print("simplicity", pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, initial, final))
    print("places", len(net.places), "transitions", len(net.transitions))
```

The table is derived from the trees and from the inductive translation, not from an executed call. `net_metrics` prints the library simplicity score for the installed translation, which can be compared with 5/6.

# 8. Simplified Model

Target semantics: **P1-Seq**, a compromise aimed at the operational compliance view. Each attempt is **Analyze**, then **Check**, then optional **Approve** or `tau`. **Rework** separates attempts. **Open** is first and **Close** is last. This keeps every P1 bullet that both originals were scored on, and it fixes the intra-attempt order that P1’s bullets leave free. It is the appropriate simplification because P2 erases the per-attempt check, and P3 is a test bound rather than a business language. One expression cannot preserve P1’s full concurrent language, P2’s collapsed dashboard, and P3 equality with both originals at once.

```text
->(
  'Open Case',
  *(
    ->(
      'Analyze Revision',
      'Check Revision',
      X('Approve Revision', tau)
    ),
    'Rework Case'
  ),
```

Structure against both originals, same conventions as section 7. The simplified tree has 11 nodes, depth 4, 4 operators, 6 visible leaves, 1 silent leaf, and duplicate-label count 0. Its inductive net has 7 places, 8 transitions of which 2 are silent (auto-approval and loop exit), and 16 arcs. Mean arc degree is \(32/15\), so arc-degree simplicity is \(15/17\). Against A and B that is one fewer tree node, one fewer operator, the removal of the parallel operator, 3 fewer places, 2 fewer transitions, 6 fewer arcs, and simplicity \(15/17 > 5/6\). Depth stays at A’s depth and is below B’s. Those inequalities are the sense in which the model is simpler.

Visible language of the simplified model: blocks \(D_S = \{\texttt{Analyze Check},\; \texttt{Analyze Check Approve}\}\), then **Open**·\(D_S\)·(**Rework**·\(D_S\))^*·**Close**. This is a proper subset of \(L_A\) and is disjoint from the \(n\ge 2\) slice of \(L_B\).

# 9. Final Recommendation

Use **Model A** for the operational compliance view. Its visible language is the P1 rule set with both intra-attempt orders of **Analyze** and **Check**. Use **Model B** only when the business truly wants a single check concurrent with the whole revision loop. B is the wrong system of record for P1: A-1 and B-2 are short, approval-free compliance failures that B accepts.

For the executive dashboard, either original can feed the same records. The dashboard must stay a projection of the operational log. Replacing the operational model by a one-check process because the dashboard cannot see the difference would make N-2 look healthy.

For bounded testing, treat A and B as different. The inequality is already inside length 8 and two analyses. A playout that happens to emit only the four shared one-revision words is a sampling accident.

Adopt the simplified model in place of A only together with the procedure “analyze, then check, then optionally approve.” It preserves that procedure, the per-attempt check, visible approvals, and the P2 image, and it is structurally simpler than both originals. Keep A in place of the simplified model when a check may complete before its analysis. Do not adopt the simplified model in place of B in order to retain B’s behavior: the simplification deletes B’s behavior on purpose. No single simplification preserves full P1 concurrency, the P2 collapse, and P3 equality with both originals.

Bounded comparison is a finite test. Finding A-1 or B-1 inside the cap proves the languages differ at every larger bound. Finding no difference would not have proved unbounded equivalence. The stated cap also omits genuine words: A’s three-analysis word with three approvals has length 13, and B can still complete four analyses in 10 visible events and five analyses in 12, each with a single check. Those omitted words are further separators, and they sit outside P3 by construction of the cap.
