# 1. Data audit and estimands

**Queueing Quantities and Units**
*   **Time:** All timestamps refer to the service center opening window, 07:00 to 19:00 (12 hours). Daily aggregates cover this window.
*   **Arrivals ($\lambda_{in}$):** Count of new cases entering the queue per day.
*   **Completions ($\lambda_{out}$):** Count of cases leaving the system per day.
*   **Work in Progress ($L$):** `mean_wip` represents the time-average number of cases in the system (queue + service) based on 15-minute snapshots during opening hours.
*   **Service Time ($S$):** `median_service_min` and `p90_service_min` represent *active handling time* only. They exclude waiting time.
*   **Capacity ($c$):** `staff_fte` is the mean concurrently available capacity.
*   **Case Mix:** `complex_pct` indicates the proportion of cases requiring a multi-step variant.

**Limitations of Aggregates**
*   **Boundary Conditions:** Backlog at 07:00 is unknown. Cases carry over overnight, meaning daily `arrivals` $\neq$ daily `completed` does not necessarily imply instability; it may reflect backlog digestion or accumulation.
*   **Service vs. Flow Time:** The data provides service duration, not total flow time (waiting + service). Little's Law ($L = \lambda W$) estimates flow time ($W$), not service time.
*   **Sampling:** `mean_wip` is derived from 15-minute snapshots. Rapid fluctuations between snapshots are missed.
*   **Intervention Opacity:** The staffing intervention starting 2026-04-29 lacks an exact hour, making intraday capacity modeling for that day approximate.

**Threats to Naive Inference**
1.  **Non-Stationarity:** The system undergoes at least two major shifts (Outage on Apr 15, Complexity Shift on Apr 20). Pooling data violates the steady-state assumption required for standard queueing formulas.
2.  **Aggregation Bias:** Daily averages hide intraday burstiness (e.g., the 08:00–10:00 peak). Staffing is constant within a day but demand is not, leading to temporary queues even if daily capacity suffices.
3.  **Confounding Variables:** The increase in WIP after Apr 20 correlates with both `complex_pct` doubling and a slight rise in arrivals. Attributing WIP growth solely to volume is incorrect.
4.  **Outage Recovery:** Data from Apr 15–17 reflects a transient recovery state, not a stable operating regime. Including this in capacity planning inflates perceived demand or service time requirements.
5.  **Weekend/Weekday Heterogeneity:** Weekend staffing (2.5–3.0 FTE) and volume (~45 arrivals) differ radically from weekdays (6.0–7.0 FTE, ~110 arrivals). Pooling them creates a "phantom" average regime that matches neither reality.
