## 1. Decision rule

Use a three-step rule: eligibility gate, evidence gate, and capacity-adjusted prioritization.

First, a case is eligible only while it is in Triage or Diagnosis Started, can receive the full 45-minute swarm before Diagnosis Complete, and has enough time for the swarm plus a handoff buffer. I would use 60 minutes as the normal minimum time to deadline. A regulatory, safety, or similarly exceptional case can use an emergency override with named human approval, but the override must be recorded. A case in Diagnosis Complete is ineligible for this intervention regardless of its breach probability.

Second, maintain one consolidated alert per case rather than creating a new alert on every refresh. For a normal case, an intervention candidate needs either:

- a materially new event or feature, such as a diagnostic log, severity change, queue-state change, or customer-impact signal; or
- a calibrated risk increase of at least 0.08 in one refresh, with complete and plausible inputs, or two consecutive refreshes in the high-risk/action band.

A jump of at least 0.15 with complete data may be acted on after one human validation. The alert must still pass the effect, burden, and capacity tests below. A refresh with no new information can keep an alert visible but does not by itself authorize another page or swarm.

The risk used for gating is a no-intervention baseline, not a treatment prediction. I would maintain an uncertainty interval around p_now. As an initial sensitivity adjustment, SME cases are evaluated at p_now + 0.04 to p_now + 0.09, Strategic Enterprise cases at p_now - 0.12 to p_now - 0.05, and Public Sector cases at approximately p_now with a sampling interval. For Non-Native-Language Support, I would not make a strong point correction from the sparse sample; I would widen the interval and require human validation. These are backtest-informed ranges, not assumed case-level truths.

For each eligible case, estimate a lower, central, and upper causal effect delta_i from the supplied range. Do not infer delta_i from p_now. The routine acceptance test is:

    robust direct net_i = lower(delta_i) x value_i - EUR450 - false-alarm burden_i
                         - shadow price of the queue spillover

The lower bound must normally be positive, and the central estimate must remain positive after the current queue shadow price. Rank qualifying cases by central net value, with a strong urgency multiplier for a short time to deadline and an explicit check that the opportunity to intervene will not disappear. The shadow price is six times the current per-case spillover probability times the average value of untreated diagnosis-stage cases; if those values are unavailable, use breach-equivalents and require an operations review rather than silently treating spillover as zero.

This produces risk/effect/value tiers rather than one breach-probability threshold. A documented non-monetary obligation, such as regulatory exposure or severe customer harm, may break a close tie or invoke the emergency override. It must not be used to hide an otherwise negative intervention or queue-impact calculation.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

Refresh predictions every 30 minutes, but store an alert as a state machine: new, persistent, dispatched, cooldown, suppressed, or re-entered. A new refresh updates the state and the risk interval; it does not create another customer-facing alert or consume capacity by itself.

After a swarm, impose a two-hour cooldown and normally prohibit a second swarm on that case. Re-entry requires a materially new event, a new stage/queue condition, or an evidence-backed calibrated risk change of at least 0.10 from the post-intervention baseline. A safety or regulatory event can break the cooldown only with senior approval. The second swarm must have a distinct hypothesized mechanism; repeating the same diagnostic action is not enough.

For a case that has not yet received a swarm, a persistent alert can be considered once it survives two refreshes or has a validated material event. A third unchanged refresh is not an escalation. If the case has already been reviewed and rejected, it returns to the portfolio queue rather than to an endless page loop. Cases near the deadline can move up because their opportunity window is closing, but urgency does not waive the effect and spillover checks.

I-05 and I-07 therefore receive different treatment. I-05 has a third alert with no new event and only a small change from 0.55 to 0.57: consolidate and suppress it from automatic escalation, while retaining it for a human sweep because its SME-adjusted risk and effect range are not negligible. I-07 has a new customer diagnostic log, an adjusted risk around 0.76-0.81, and only 1.5 hours left: the new evidence satisfies re-entry/dispatch criteria even though its point risk fell slightly. It is an urgent first swarm, not a repeated swarm based on an unchanged refresh.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

Value-based ranking can systematically starve cases with lower contractual value, including SME or Public Sector cases, even when their risk reduction is large and their burden is small. The policy should therefore publish both the value-ranked list and a risk/effect-ranked list, impose a minimum service floor for qualifying lower-value cases, and reserve part of daily capacity for an urgent, robustly positive case outside the highest-value segment. I-07 is an example of that guardrail. The guardrail is not a blank check: the case still needs stage eligibility, credible evidence, and a non-negative conservative net after queue harm.

Non-Native-Language Support has sparse calibration and effect data. Do not use uncertainty as a reason to deny service or to automatically give priority. Widen the confidence interval, provide a language-capable human review, separately monitor alert and intervention rates, and oversample this context in controlled effect estimation. Compare outcomes at matched risk and stage, not only in raw breach counts.

Segment corrections must remain segment-specific and provisional. SME underprediction and Strategic overprediction should be checked for slope as well as intercept; a single additive correction can be wrong at different risk levels. Use hierarchical or shrinkage calibration for small groups, maintain an out-of-time validation set, and avoid applying a noisy correction as if it were a precise individual truth.

Selective intervention contaminates future labels. A treated case may not breach because it received the swarm, so a future model trained on the observed label can learn that its pre-intervention risk was low. Persist treatment, timing, stage, reason code, model version, queue state, and compliance in the training data. Maintain randomized or otherwise defensible controls, use treatment propensity in estimation, and evaluate the baseline model on untreated or corrected counterfactual data rather than simply mixing treated outcomes into ordinary labels.

Spillover can fall disproportionately on untreated groups: for example, a low-value group may be left in a congested diagnosis queue while high-value cases consume specialist time. Record the queue and untreated case identifiers affected by each swarm, estimate their incremental delay and breach risk, and report spillover by segment, language context, value band, and queue. Cap concurrent swarms, require a live shadow price, and pause allocation when the harm to untreated cases exceeds the direct benefit. A portfolio can be individually fair to selected cases while being unfair at the queue level, so both views are necessary.

Customer disruption and the burden of a false alarm are also distributional. Audit whether particular segments receive more disruptive interventions for the same expected benefit, and offer a less disruptive information-gathering path when a swarm is not justified.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

Operate a near-real-time dashboard for stage at dispatch, time from alert to swarm, time from swarm to Diagnosis Complete, slots used, specialist interruption, queue length, queue wait, per-case untreated delay, reserve activation, false-alarm burden, repeat-alert volume, and breaches. The hard operational stop is reached if a case has passed Diagnosis Complete, the 45-minute service cannot be completed safely, or observed queue delay is outside the current capacity envelope.

For predictive monitoring, evaluate mature labels by week and by segment, stage, risk band, language context, and missingness pattern. Track calibration-in-the-large, calibration slope/reliability curves, Brier score, alert precision, false-negative rate, missing-feature rates, and drift in p_prev to p_now. Treat the supplied SME and Strategic corrections as control limits to be tested, not permanent constants.

Estimate intervention effects causally. Among cases meeting the eligibility gate, randomize the order or assignment of close-score cases within queue, stage, segment, and congestion strata, while preserving the emergency path. Keep a holdout or stepped-wedge control. Estimate intent-to-treat and treatment-on-treated effects with confidence intervals for breach, time to Diagnosis Complete, final resolution time, and customer burden. Estimate spillovers by comparing untreated cases in exposed queues or time blocks with untreated cases in unexposed randomized blocks. Report heterogeneity by stage, segment, language context, and congestion rather than relying on one pooled effect.

Capacity monitoring should estimate the actual per-slot relationship between specialist interruption, queue delay, and untreated breach hazard. Refit the spillover probability from observed queue features and measure whether the 8-20 minute base range or the 15-30 minute adverse range is still credible. Keep the one-slot reserve until the regulatory-arrival window closes and measure the opportunity cost of both reserving and releasing it.

Fairness monitoring should include alerting, eligibility, selection, intervention benefit, false-alarm burden, and spillover harm by segment and relevant language/customer context. Compare conditional metrics at similar calibrated risk, stage, deadline, and queue load. Use confidence intervals and minimum sample sizes; do not declare a group difference from a small sparse cell. Investigate a persistent unexplained gap of about 10 percentage points, a selection-rate ratio below 0.8, or materially higher untreated spillover in one group.

To detect policy-induced drift, maintain a treatment-aware feature and label pipeline, compare the live population with the randomized control population, monitor changes in customer or operator behavior caused by the alerts, and preserve periodic policy-off or shadow-scored evaluations. Retraining should not use post-alert features as if they were pre-alert features, and it should not infer baseline risk from treated outcomes without adjustment.

Review allocation weekly and after every material workflow change. Trigger an emergency review when two consecutive mature windows have segment calibration error above 0.05, when the lower confidence bound of a segment's effect makes its conservative net negative, when realized per-case spillover exceeds 0.03 or queue delay exceeds 20 minutes repeatedly, or when a fairness control limit is breached. Change thresholds or tiers only after out-of-time validation and a capacity simulation using the updated effect and spillover estimates. If effects remain uncertain, use a smaller randomized pilot or fewer simultaneous slots rather than increasing the intervention threshold solely to make historical metrics look better.
