# 1. Stress scenarios

Averages across the 8-week normal period (2,300 cases/week) mask a critical structural reality revealed by the 3-week peak log (3,300 cases/week): **Routine weekly volume barely changes between normal and peak periods (~1,403/week vs. ~1,386/week).** Nearly 100% of the +1,000 cases/week surge comes from Urgent (+131%, from 414 to 957/week), Complex-panel (+121%, from 299 to 660/week), and Repeat-after-failure (+61%, from 184 to 297/week). Furthermore, even in "normal" operations, Urgent p90 cycle time (19h) already breaches the 18h contractual penalty threshold.

To avoid designing for a misleading historical average, we evaluate the process against four distinct stress scenarios:

*   **Scenario 1: Winter Infection Wave (Demand Volume + High-Acuity Mix Shift)**
    *   *Profile:* Weekly volume rises +40–45%, driven almost entirely by Urgent (29% share) and Complex-panel (20% share) orders, while Routine volume stays flat in absolute terms.
    *   *Stress mechanism:* Simultaneous overload of physical processing and clinical sign-off. Urgent cases demand batch-wait bypasses (fragmenting machine setups), while Complex-panel cases require multiple assays per order, 3.1× more Medical Validation, and 2.4× more Specialist Review. Unfinished work-in-process (WIP) balloons (completions drop from 89.3% of starts in normal logs to 86.7% in peak logs).
*   **Scenario 2: Assay Machine M1 Degradation or Outage (Equipment & Rework Disruption)**
    *   *Profile:* Normal or moderate total volume, but reliable machine M1 suffers unplanned downtime or throughput degradation, forcing heavy reliance on faster, higher-failure M2 across day and night shifts.
    *   *Stress mechanism:* Assay run -> Repeat assay jumps from the 6.7% normal baseline (1,140 / 17,030 runs) toward or above the 14.8% peak rate (1,260 / 8,500 runs), clustered on M2 and night shifts. Because Repeat-after-failure cases consume assay capacity *twice* (with Repeat -> Assay median wait surging from 10h to 26h), parasitic rework consumes effective machine capacity and triggers a self-reinforcing queue spiral.
*   **Scenario 3: Clinical Sign-Off Deficit (Medical Validator / Specialist Absence)**
    *   *Profile:* Normal intake and assay capacity, combined with moderate Complex-panel/Urgent demand, coinciding with the bi-weekly Friday specialist absence plus unexpected illness/leave in the Medical Validator pool.
    *   *Stress mechanism:* Upstream physical stations flow smoothly, pushing validated runs into a tail-end wall. Technical validation -> Hold for specialist review (7.5% of peak tech validations vs. 1.8% normal) and Hold -> Medical validation (18h normal -> 48h peak median) stall Complex-panel cases, while Urgent cases breach the 18h SLA waiting in the Technical -> Medical validation queue (8h normal -> 24h peak median).
*   **Scenario 4: Courier Arrival Compression & Intake Error Cascade (Logistics Disruption)**
    *   *Profile:* Weather or traffic delays compress the 10:00 and 17:00 courier spikes into late-afternoon waves, forcing Registration clerks into >9h overtime shifts.
    *   *Stress mechanism:* Clerk fatigue pushes registration errors past the 9h shift cliff. Pre-analytical check -> Manual accession correction jumps (2.8% normal -> 4.3% peak), overwhelming the 2 accession specialists (91% peak utilization, 14h median queue). Worse, Pre-analytical -> Request recollection doubles (4.2% normal -> 8.0% peak), and failed manual corrections spilling into recollection rise from 14% (70/500) to 26% (110/420). Recollections not only cause 42–60h delays and 35–42% order cancellations, but also consume clerk phone time explaining errors to physicians—further starving intake capacity.

# 2. Bottleneck-migration analysis

Because several stations operate near saturation (71–76% normal, 89–101% peak), local optimizations routinely shift rather than remove bottlenecks:

### Scenario 1: Winter Infection Wave
1.  **Primary Bottlenecks (Technical Validation at 101% & Assay M1/M2 at 98%):** As Urgent and Complex-panel orders surge, Assay queues jump from 4.0h to 18.0h. Urgent cases attempt to bypass Batch wait, but bypass success falls from 74% to 58% as prep benches saturate (89% utilization, 11h queue). Multi-assay Complex-panel cases and rising repeats (14.8%) saturate M1/M2, feeding **Technical Validation (101% utilization, 16h queue)**—the true mathematical throughput limit where arrival rate exceeds service rate.
2.  **Migration under Naive Fixes:**
    *   If management automates registration (**I8**) or speeds up Pre-analytical check (96% peak), samples simply flood Preparation and Assay faster, lengthening the 18h Assay queue.
    *   If management pulls 2 Technical Validators to fix the 14h Accession Correction queue (**I4**), Technical Validation utilization spikes well above 105%, halting *all* result releases.
    *   If Technical Validation is relieved, the flood immediately hits **Medical Validation (93% utilization, 22h queue)** and **Specialist Review (87% utilization, 36h queue)** because Tech Validation routes 49.8% of peak output to clinical sign-off (3,420 to Med Val + 610 to Specialist, out of 8,080 exits) compared to only 26.4% in normal periods.

### Scenario 2: Assay Machine M1 Degradation / Outage
1.  **Primary Bottleneck (Assay M2 Effective Capacity):** Shifting load to M2 leverages its faster runtime initially, hiding the capacity loss for a few hours.
2.  **Migration to Rework Loop & Downstream Starvation/Burstiness:** Faster runtime on M2 (especially overnight) generates a wave of Repeat assay loops. Each repeat requires a second run (10–26h loop time), starving first-pass Routine and Urgent orders of machine slots. If management applies **I3** (dedicating surviving M1 capacity to Urgent/Complex-panel and dumping all Routine onto M2), Routine repeat rates surge, filling Preparation/Assay queues with Repeat-after-failure WIP (p90 cycle time 240h). Furthermore, the DFG shows a direct **Repeat assay -> Medical validation** edge (80 normal, 120 peak); rising assay failures push complex troubleshooting directly onto Medical Validators.

### Scenario 3: Clinical Sign-Off Deficit
1.  **Primary Bottleneck (Specialist Review [36h peak queue] & Medical Validation [22h peak queue]):** Even when Assay and Technical Validation run at normal medians, Complex-panel orders stall at Hold for specialist review (48h peak edge) and Urgent orders fail their 18h SLA sitting in the Technical -> Medical validation queue.
2.  **Migration Upstream via Priority Distortion:** When Medical Validation backs up, Urgent cases consume almost all available Urgent/Complex-panel sign-off slots to avoid contractual penalties. Complex-panel WIP stagnates for days, triggering physician inquiry calls to Registration/Intake clerks and manual status checks by Technical Validators—degrading upstream efficiency without any surge in physical tube arrivals.

### Scenario 4: Courier Compression & Intake Error Cascade
1.  **Primary Bottleneck (Courier Intake [7.5h peak queue] -> Registration Overtime -> Accession Correction [14h peak queue]):** Compressed arrivals create a lump at Receive sample -> Pre-analytical check (median jumps 2h -> 9h). Clerks work >9h overtime to clear the dock, spiking metadata errors.
2.  **Migration to Pre-Analytical & Assay Wave:** Pre-analytical check saturates (96% utilization, 9.5h queue) sorting good tubes from defective ones, while the 2 Accession Correction specialists drown. If Accession Correction is suddenly cleared (e.g., via cross-trained staff releasing 300+ held orders at once) or if automated registration (**I8**) dumps an unpaced digital wave straight to the floor, **Preparation benches** and **Assay machines** receive a synchronized shockwave, breaking Urgent batch-bypass discipline and spiking night-shift M2 usage (triggering Scenario 2's repeat loop).

# 3. Robust redesign package

These four structural interventions stay active across all periods. They reduce parasitic rework and schedule variability rather than blindly adding headcount, and each includes a built-in safeguard against overloading the next station.

### R1. Courier Slot Smoothing + Paced Digital Registration + Hard 9h Clerk Shift Cap (Modified I7 + Paced I8)
*   **Design:** Shift malleable clinic courier pickups away from the 10:00 and 17:00 spikes (**I7**) and automate registration for orders with complete digital metadata (**I8**). Pair automated registration with an **electronic release throttle** (releasing non-urgent digital orders to Pre-analytical check at a capped hourly rate aligned with bench capacity), and enforce a **strict 9-hour shift cap** for Registration clerks.
*   **Why it is robust:** Eliminates self-inflicted registration errors from clerk fatigue, cutting volume into the fragile 2-person Accession Correction pool and reducing recollection phone-call drag on intake.
*   **Downstream overload protection:** Without pacing, I8 floods Pre-analytical check (96% peak utilization). The electronic throttle holds excess *Routine* digital registrations in a virtual buffer while letting Urgent tubes pass immediately.
*   **Assumptions & failure modes:** Assumes 40–50% of orders arrive with clean digital metadata and regional courier contracts permit staggered windows. *Fails if* clinics transmit systematically malformed digital templates that pass automated syntax checks but fail at Pre-analytical check.

### R2. 2-Hour Pre-Analytical Defect Triage + Directional Cross-Training (I6 + Re-engineered I4)
*   **Design:** Install rapid visual/barcode defect triage at intake to reject poor samples within 2 hours (**I6**) via automated digital physician alerts (removing clerk phone explanations). **Reject manager proposal I4 as written** (never pull Technical Validators at 101% peak utilization to do Accession Correction). Instead, implement **directional cross-training**:
    1.  Cross-train *Registration clerks freed up by R1's automation* to assist the 2 Accession Correction specialists.
    2.  Use the 4-week training window noted in resource specs to cross-train *3–4 Preparation bench technicians* upward into **Technical Validation**.
*   **Why it is robust:** Failing bad samples within 2h cuts the 42–60h recollection cycle early and avoids wasting Prep capacity. Directional cross-training reinforces the two sharpest choke points—Accession Correction (2 staff) and Technical Validation (101% peak utilization)—using capacity freed upstream rather than cannibalizing a saturated resource.
*   **Downstream overload protection:** Clearing Accession Correction faster sends more valid tubes to Preparation and Assay; however, drawing flex Technical Validators from Prep benches naturally self-throttles Preparation output whenever Technical Validation is backed up.
*   **Assumptions & failure modes:** Assumes Prep technicians meet regulatory qualifications for 4-week Technical Validator cross-training, and physicians act on automated recollection alerts without calling the lab. *Fails if* staff turnover erodes cross-trained currency before peak waves hit.

### R3. Time-Windowed Urgent Micro-Batching + Routine Batch-24 (Balanced I1 + I2)
*   **Design:** Replace ad-hoc Urgent queue-jumping (and reject strict continuous Urgent-first **I1**) with **scheduled Urgent micro-batch windows every 90 minutes** at Preparation and Assay. Simultaneously, expand Routine preparation batch sizes from 12 to 24 (**I2**), with Complex-panel cases allocated dedicated mid-tier slots (max queue cap of 8h at Prep).
*   **Why it is robust:** Continuous Urgent-first (**I1**) fragments M1/M2 schedules and starves Routine/Complex-panel orders; conversely, peak congestion dropped Urgent batch-bypass from 74% to 58%, pushing Urgent p90 to 33h. A 90-minute micro-batch rhythm guarantees Urgent bypass predictability (<1.5h wait at Prep) while preserving uninterrupted machine blocks where Batch-24 improves setup efficiency for the ~1,390 weekly Routine orders.
*   **Downstream overload protection:** Batch-24 releases Routine runs in larger lumps to Technical Validation. To prevent Technical Validation spikes, Routine batches are staggered across M1/M2 completion windows, and Technical Validation prioritizes single-assay Routine sign-offs only between Urgent/Complex-panel micro-batches.
*   **Assumptions & failure modes:** Assumes Urgent orders still meet the 18h SLA with a worst-case 1.5h micro-batch wait (viable when downstream queues are controlled). *Fails if* Urgent arrivals exceed the capacity of the 90-minute windows themselves during extreme shocks.

### R4. Reliability-Gated M1/M2 Routing & Night-Shift QA Protocol (Modified I3)
*   **Design:** Reject rigid **I3** (dumping all Routine on M2 causes runaway repeats under high load). Instead, route **Complex-panel and Repeat-after-failure** orders exclusively to high-reliability M1 (preventing multi-assay failures and third-pass repeats). Allow M2 to run Urgent and Routine batches during day/evening shifts under full tech supervision, but **restrict night-shift M2 usage** to calibrated Urgent runs plus scheduled preventive maintenance/calibration.
*   **Why it is robust:** Directly attacks the root cause of Repeat-after-failure clustering (night shifts + M2 usage), reclaiming the 6.7%–14.8% machine capacity currently burned on rework.
*   **Downstream overload protection:** Fewer assay repeats directly reduce parasitic load on Technical Validation and eliminate Repeat assay -> Medical validation escalations.
*   **Assumptions & failure modes:** Assumes M1 has sufficient daytime capacity for Complex-panel (~660/wk in peak) + Repeat orders, and night-shift Routine deferral (6–7 hours) fits comfortably inside the 72h Routine SLA. *Fails if* M1 suffers a multi-day mechanical outage (handled by Scenario Policy P2).

# 4. Scenario-specific policies

Because no static priority rule can protect Urgent (18h), Routine (72h), and Complex-panel cases simultaneously under severe stress, the following dynamic policies activate **only** when specific leading thresholds are crossed, with explicit deactivation rules to avoid policy thrashing.

### Policy P1: High-Acuity Mix-Shift Surge Mode (Targets Scenario 1)
*   **Trigger ON:** Rolling 48h (Urgent + Complex-panel) intake share > 38% **OR** Technical Validation queue > 6h **OR** Medical Validation queue > 6h.
*   **Actions:**
    1.  **Flex Technical Validators:** Shift cross-trained Prep techs (from R2) into Technical Validation for 50% of their shift.
    2.  **Evening Medical Validation Shift (I5):** Activate temporary evening Medical Validation coverage (17:00–23:00) focused strictly on Urgent and aging Complex-panel cases, preventing overnight queue buildup (where Tech -> Med Val hits 24h in peak).
    3.  **Conditional Routine Release Batching (Gated I9):** Hold Routine result releases for automated twice-daily batch reporting *only for Routine cases currently <48h elapsed time*, freeing validator focus for Urgent/Complex-panel sign-offs without breaching the 72h Routine target.
*   **Downstream risk control:** Evening Medical Validation accelerates final releases (minimal downstream load since Release result -> END is automated/clerical), while flexing Prep techs into Technical Validation naturally paces Preparation output so Assay is not overfed.
*   **Trigger OFF:** Rolling 48h high-acuity share < 33% **AND** both Technical and Medical Validation median queues < 3.5h for 2 consecutive days.

### Policy P2: Assay Degradation & Repeat Containment Mode (Targets Scenario 2)
*   **Trigger ON:** M1 unplanned downtime > 3 hours **OR** rolling 24h Assay -> Repeat rate > 9.5% **OR** Assay median queue > 10h.
*   **Actions:**
    1.  **Mandatory M2 Recalibration Pause:** If triggered by repeat rate >9.5%, halt non-urgent M2 runs for a 2h diagnostic/calibration block rather than compounding Repeat-after-failure WIP.
    2.  **Early Complex-Panel Outsourcing (I10):** Divert newly pre-analytically cleared Complex-panel orders to the partner lab (absorbing a predictable 12h transport delay to immediately shed multi-assay load from crippled local machines).
    3.  **Temporary Gated Urgent-First Dispatch (Capped I1):** Grant Urgent orders first-available machine slots on surviving reliable capacity, *subject to a Routine anti-starvation cap* (any Routine order reaching 48h total elapsed time is promoted to Urgent-equivalent dispatch priority).
*   **Downstream risk control:** Outsourcing Complex-panel orders bypasses local Assay, Technical Validation, *and* Specialist Review, preventing a rebound flood when M1 recovers; however, a dedicated clerk/tech reconciles returned partner results to contain traceability errors.
*   **Trigger OFF:** M1 operational for 24h **AND** 24h Repeat rate < 7.5% **AND** Assay median queue < 5h.

### Policy P3: Clinical Sign-Off & Specialist Relief Mode (Targets Scenario 3)
*   **Trigger ON:** Planned/unplanned Specialist absence (including every second Friday if Hold-for-specialist WIP > 15 cases) **OR** Hold-for-specialist median wait > 16h **OR** Medical Validation queue > 8h.
*   **Actions:**
    1.  **Targeted Complex-Panel Overflow Outsourcing (I10):** Route incoming Complex-panel orders to the partner lab. Trading a fixed 12h transport delay for avoiding Peak's 36h Specialist queue (48h Hold -> Med Val median edge) + 22h Medical Validation queue cuts peak Complex-panel cycle time by >30 hours.
    2.  **SLA-Slack Pull Triage at Medical Validation:** Sort the Medical Validation queue by *remaining SLA slack* (`Target SLA - Elapsed Case Time`) rather than FIFO, pulling any Urgent case with <5h remaining before 18h penalty immediately upon Technical Validation completion.
*   **Downstream risk control:** Prevents upstream stations from choking on status inquiries for stalled Complex-panel WIP, while SLA-slack sorting protects Urgent penalties without starving Complex-panel orders that are already outsourced.
*   **Trigger OFF:** Full Specialist/Medical Validator roster restored **AND** Specialist Hold queue < 8h **AND** Medical Validation queue < 4h.

### Policy P4: Courier Compression Shock Absorber (Targets Scenario 4)
*   **Trigger ON:** Delayed courier wave delivering >2× normal hourly tube volume **OR** Courier Intake queue > 3h **OR** Pre-analytical check queue > 5h.
*   **Actions:**
    1.  **Color-Coded Urgent Tote Unbundling:** At Courier Intake, physically pull Urgent/Complex-panel bags immediately to Pre-analytical check, holding Routine totes in climate-controlled staging to smooth intake over the next 8–12 hours.
    2.  **Activate Flex Accession Specialists:** Deploy R2 cross-trained registration clerks immediately to Accession Correction before the 2-person pool backs up past 4h.
*   **Downstream risk control:** Staging Routine totes at the dock intentionally adds 4–6h wait to Routine orders (easily absorbed within their 72h target) to keep Pre-analytical check below 90% utilization and prevent an unpaced wave from hitting Preparation and Assay.
*   **Trigger OFF:** Courier Intake queue < 1.5h **AND** Pre-analytical check queue < 2.5h.

# 6. Residual vulnerabilities

Even with this redesign, four structural vulnerabilities remain—including two introduced directly by the redesign itself:

1.  **Pre-Lab Transit Eats Up to 44% of the Urgent 18h Budget (Structural Limit):**
    In the Performance DFG, `Register order -> Receive sample` takes a median of **5h normal / 8h peak** before the lab physically touches the sample. If a clinic registers an Urgent order just after a courier pickup and transit takes 8–10h, the lab has **8h total** to complete Pre-analytical, Prep, Assay, Technical Validation, and Medical Validation. Even zero-queue internal flow cannot save Urgent p90 penalties when external courier transit exceeds 10h.
2.  **Compound Disruption + Partner Lab Saturation (S1 + S2 + S3 Co-occurrence):**
    During a regional winter infection wave (S1), the partner lab used for **I10** outsourcing is experiencing the exact same regional surge. If M1 fails (S2) or a specialist is out (S3) while the partner lab caps overflow intake or breaches its own turnaround promises, Complex-panel cases fall back onto degraded local resources, risking >150h cycle times.
3.  **Redesign-Induced Vulnerability #1 — "Silent" Digital Defects Bypassing Registration (Caused by R1 / I8):**
    Automating registration (**I8**) removes human clerk review on incoming orders. If an external clinic EHR update introduces a systematic mapping bug (e.g., wrong tube-type metadata or mismatched panel codes that are syntactically "complete"), R1 will wave hundreds of flawed orders straight into **Pre-analytical check** and **Preparation** before anyone notices, triggering a sudden spike in late-stage recollections and wasted prep reagents.
4.  **Redesign-Induced Vulnerability #2 — Split-Order Traceability & Threshold Chattering (Caused by P2/P3 Outsourcing I10 & Dynamic Triggers):**
    When a single patient encounter contains both an Urgent tube (kept in-house) and a Complex-panel tube (outsourced via **I10**), splitting the physical tubes across two labs creates specimen-splitting risk, asynchronous partial reporting, and manual LIMS reconciliation burden. Additionally, if queues hover right around policy trigger bands without strict adherence to the 24–48h hysteresis OFF-rules, staff will suffer "mode-switching fatigue" (opening and closing evening shifts or outsourcing lanes repeatedly).

# 7. Minimum monitoring dashboard

Relying on cycle time or overall rework rate during a peak wave is fatal: when Complex-panel p90 hits 210h (nearly 9 days), cycle time tells you about a bottleneck that formed **last week**. Operations should review **6 Leading Indicators** intraday/daily to trigger Policies P1–P4 *before* SLAs break, while tracking **4 Lagging Outcome Metrics** weekly to audit overall system health.

### Leading Indicators (Actionable Daily / Intraday)

| Leading Indicator | Normal Baseline | Amber Warning Band (Prepare / Pre-stage) | Red Action Threshold (Trigger Policy) | Operational Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Rolling 48h High-Acuity Mix Share** *(Urgent % + Complex-Panel % at Registration)* | ~31% (18% U + 13% CP) | 34% – 38% | **> 38%** *(Triggers P1)* | Detects a Scenario 1 case-mix shift *at order entry*—24 to 48 hours before those tubes overload Technical and Medical Validation. |
| **2. Courier Dock + Pre-Analytical Combined Queue** *(Median wait + unprocesed tote count)* | ~2.8h combined | 4.5h – 6.5h | **> 6.5h or Pre-An > 5h** *(Triggers P4)* | Early signal of Scenario 4 arrival compression before clerks cross the >9h overtime error cliff. |
| **3. Accession Correction WIP & Oldest Unresolved Case** | 3.0h median queue | 4.5h – 7.0h | **> 7.0h median** *(Deploys R2 flex clerks)* | Monitors the fragile 2-specialist pool so blocked samples don't accumulate and release as a destructive downstream wave. |
| **4. Rolling 24h Assay -> Repeat Rate (Split by M1 vs. M2 & Shift)** | ~6.7% overall | 8.0% – 9.5% (or M2 > 10%) | **> 9.5% overall or M1 down >3h** *(Triggers P2)* | Catches Scenario 2 machine drift/night-shift degradation immediately before Repeat-after-failure WIP consumes double assay slots. |
| **5. Technical Validation Utilization & Queue Age** | 76% util / 3.5h queue | 85–90% util / 4.5–6.0h queue | **> 90% util or > 6.0h queue** *(Triggers P1 Tech Val flex)* | Prevents Technical Validation from crossing 100% saturation (as seen at 101% in peak logs) where queues grow unboundedly. |
| **6. Urgent "Remaining SLA Slack" at Tech Val Exit + Specialist Hold WIP** | > 8h Urgent slack; < 10 Specialist WIP | 5h – 7h Urgent slack; 10 – 15 Specialist WIP | **< 5h Urgent slack or Med Val > 8h or Spec Hold > 16h** *(Triggers P3 / I5)* | Protects Urgent 18h contractual penalties at the final sign-off gate and triggers **I10** outsourcing before Specialist queues hit 36h. |

### Lagging Outcome Metrics (Reviewed Daily/Weekly for Calibration, Not Intraday Switching)
*   **Urgent SLA Breach Rate (% > 18h) & p90 Cycle Time:** Confirms contractual penalty exposure (target: p90 < 17.5h vs. 19h normal / 33h peak). Decompose breaches into *Pre-Receive transit time* vs. *In-Lab processing time* to separate courier failures from lab bottlenecks.
*   **Routine & Complex-Panel p90 Cycle Times (plus % Routine > 72h):** Verifies that Urgent micro-batching (R3), dock staging (P4), and batch release holds (P1) are not silently starving Routine orders past 72h, and verifies partner-lab **I10** turnaround performance.
*   **End-to-End Rework & Recollection Cancellation Rate:** Tracks total `Pre-analytical -> Request recollection` % (target < 4.5% vs. 8.0% peak) and `Request recollection -> Cancel order` loss (35–42% of recollections) to audit clinic digital metadata quality (guarding against Redesign Vulnerability #1).
*   **Net Weekly WIP Balance (`Registered Orders - [Released + Canceled]`):** Confirms the lab is draining its queues over rolling 7-day windows rather than hiding congestion in unfinished in-flight inventory.
