## 1. Equivalence design space

Let an object-centric execution centered on a lead object $o_{\text{lead}}$ of type `Order` be a tuple
$$X = (E, \tau, \text{act}, O, \text{type}, \text{scope}, I_{E2O}, R_{O2O}, o_{\text{lead}})$$
where $E$ is a finite set of events with timestamp map $\tau: E \to \mathbb{R}$ and activity label $\text{act}: E \to \Sigma_{\text{act}}$; $O$ is a finite set of objects with type map $\text{type}: O \to \Sigma_{\text{type}}$ ($\Sigma_{\text{type}} = \{\texttt{Order}, \texttt{Item}, \texttt{Package}, \texttt{Delivery}, \texttt{Invoice}, \texttt{Payment}\}$) and boundary-scope flag $\text{scope}: O \to \{\texttt{LOC}, \texttt{EXT}\}$ ($\texttt{EXT}$ iff the identifier carries the prefix `EXT-`, signifying a lifecycle centered on another `Order`, and $\texttt{LOC}$ otherwise); $I_{E2O} \subseteq E \times O$ is the event-to-object incidence relation; and $R_{O2O} \subseteq O \times O \times \Sigma_{\text{qual}}$ is the directed, qualifier-labeled object-to-object relation.

We define four equivalence relations spanning a strict hierarchy of structural and semantic abstraction.

### 1.1 Activity-sequence equivalence ($\sim_{\text{act}}$)

- **Formal definition**: Given total timestamp orderings $E = \langle e_1, \dots, e_n \rangle$ and $E' = \langle e'_1, \dots, e'_m \rangle$, $X \sim_{\text{act}} X'$ iff $n = m$ and $\forall i \in \{1, \dots, n\}: \text{act}(e_i) = \text{act}(e'_i)$.
- **Invariants preserved**: Total event count, activity multiset, and sequential order of activity labels.
- **Information discarded**: All object identifiers, object types, object multiplicities, `LOC`/`EXT` boundary flags, $I_{E2O}$ links, and $R_{O2O}$ edges and qualifiers.
- **Equivalence properties**: Reflexive, symmetric, and transitive because $\sim_{\text{act}}$ is the kernel of the deterministic projection $f_{\text{act}}(X) = \langle \text{act}(e_1), \dots, \text{act}(e_n) \rangle$ ($X \sim_{\text{act}} X' \iff f_{\text{act}}(X) = f_{\text{act}}(X')$).
- **Concurrency and same-time events**: If multiple events share a timestamp $\tau(e_i) = \tau(e_j)$, raw log order is arbitrary. To maintain well-definedness, simultaneous events at timestamp $t$ are grouped into a multiset $M_t \in \mathbb{N}^{\Sigma_{\text{act}}}$ (or canonically sorted lexicographically by activity name), yielding a sequence of multisets $\langle M_{t_1}, \dots, M_{t_k} \rangle$.

### 1.2 Typed structural equivalence under identifier renaming ($\sim_{\text{struct}}$)

- **Formal definition**: $X \sim_{\text{struct}} X'$ iff there exist bijections $\psi: E \to E'$ and $\phi: O \to O'$ such that:
  1. $\forall e_1, e_2 \in E: \tau(e_1) < \tau(e_2) \iff \tau'(\psi(e_1)) < \tau'(\psi(e_2))$, and $\forall e \in E: \text{act}(\psi(e)) = \text{act}(e)$;
  2. $\forall o \in O: \text{type}(\phi(o)) = \text{type}(o)$, $\text{scope}(\phi(o)) = \text{scope}(o)$, and $\phi(o_{\text{lead}}) = o'_{\text{lead}}$;
  3. $\forall (e, o) \in E \times O: (e, o) \in I_{E2O} \iff (\psi(e), \phi(o)) \in I'_{E2O}$;
  4. $\forall (o_1, o_2) \in O \times O: \big(\exists q: (o_1, o_2, q) \in R_{O2O}\big) \iff \big(\exists q': (\phi(o_1), \phi(o_2), q') \in R'_{O2O}\big)$.
- **Invariants preserved**: Event count, sequential event order, object counts partitioned by $(\text{type}, \text{scope})$, exact bipartite $I_{E2O}$ incidence topology, and unqualified $R_{O2O}$ graph topology.
- **Information discarded**: Concrete local and external identifier strings (e.g., `O1` vs. `O2`, `I11/I12` vs. `I22/I21`) and specific O2O qualifier strings (`packs` vs. `removed_before_dispatch`).
- **Equivalence properties**: Reflexive ($\psi = \text{id}_E, \phi = \text{id}_O$), symmetric ($\psi^{-1}, \phi^{-1}$), and transitive ($\psi' \circ \psi, \phi' \circ \phi$) by closure of typed graph isomorphisms under inversion and composition.
- **Concurrency and same-time events**: Same-timestamp events form an ordered partition of $E$. Within any simultaneous stratum $E_t$, $\psi$ may permute events freely provided their incident object neighborhoods under $\phi$ are preserved (e.g., simultaneous `Add Item` events).

### 1.3 Operational business-semantic equivalence ($\sim_{\text{sem}}$, preferred relation)

- **Formal definition**: $X \sim_{\text{sem}} X'$ iff $\sigma_{\text{sem}}(X) = \sigma_{\text{sem}}(X')$, where $\sigma_{\text{sem}}$ maps $X$ to a canonical tuple comprising:
  1. **Role-scoped multiplicity vector**: exact counts of $(\text{type}, \text{scope})$ pairs;
  2. **Causal partial-order DAG ($<_P$)**: $e_1 <_P e_2$ iff $\tau(e_1) < \tau(e_2)$ and either (a) $e_1, e_2$ share at least one non-lead object $o \in (I_{E2O}(e_1) \cap I_{E2O}(e_2)) \setminus \{o_{\text{lead}}\}$, or (b) $e_1, e_2$ belong to distinct macro-stages (`Order/Item Setup` $\prec$ `Fulfillment/Return` $\prec$ `Invoicing` $\prec$ `Settlement`) or reorder non-commutative rework steps;
  3. **Canonicalized $I_{E2O}$ event signatures**: each event labeled by $(\text{act}(e), \{\!\{\text{role}(o) \mid o \in I_{E2O}(e)\}\!\})$ where $\text{role}(o)$ is $o$'s canonical structural-semantic role index;
  4. **Qualified $R_{O2O}$ semantic relation set**: canonicalized triples $(\text{role}(o_1), q, \text{role}(o_2))$ preserving all domain qualifiers (`contains`, `packs`, `repacks`, `carries`, `bills`, `settles`, `partially_settles`, `completes_settlement`, `replaces`, `removed_before_dispatch`).
- **Invariants preserved**: Local and external object multiplicities, cross-order coupling in logistics versus finance, O2O qualifiers encoding substitutions (`replaces`, `removed_before_dispatch`) and installment states (`partially_settles`, `completes_settlement`), reverse-logistics rework loops, and object-level causal dependencies.
- **Information discarded**: Raw object IDs, arbitrary ordering of independent `Add Item` events, and non-causal timestamp interleavings between independent sub-object flows.
- **Equivalence properties**: Reflexive, symmetric, and transitive as the kernel of the deterministic canonical map $\sigma_{\text{sem}}$. (By contrast, heuristic similarity thresholds or greedy uncanonicalized matching fail transitivity.)
- **Concurrency and same-time events**: Handled natively via the causal poset $(E, <_P)$ (Mazurkiewicz trace semantics). Concurrent events on disjoint non-lead objects are unordered in $<_P$ and serialized deterministically by canonical role labels.

### 1.4 Macro-archetype lifecycle equivalence ($\sim_{\text{motif}}$)

- **Formal definition**: $X \sim_{\text{motif}} X'$ iff $f_{\text{motif}}(X) = f_{\text{motif}}(X')$, where $f_{\text{motif}}(X)$ classifies $X$ by three orthogonal business deviation flags relative to an isolated single-flow order:
  $$\text{SplitLogistics} = \mathbb{I}(|O_{\texttt{Package}}^{\texttt{LOC}}| > 1 \lor |O_{\texttt{Delivery}}^{\texttt{LOC}}| > 1), \quad \text{CrossOrder} = \mathbb{I}(|O^{\texttt{EXT}}| > 0),$$
  $$\text{ExceptionRework} = \mathbb{I}\big(\exists (o_1, o_2, q) \in R_{O2O}: q \in \{\texttt{replaces}, \texttt{removed\_before\_dispatch}, \texttt{repacks}\} \;\lor\; \exists e \in E: \text{act}(e) \in \{\texttt{Return Item}, \texttt{Inspect Return}\}\big).$$
- **Invariants preserved**: High-level executive process regime: `M1` Standard Isolated (`0,0,0`: `{X1, X2}`), `M2` Multi-Package Split Fulfillment (`1,0,0`: `{X3, X4}`), `M3` Cross-Order Shared Execution (`0,1,0`: `{X5, X6}`), and `M4` Exception & Replacement Remediation (`*,0,1`: `{X7, X8}`).
- **Information discarded**: Sub-type of cross-order sharing (warehouse vs. billing), pre-dispatch substitution vs. post-dispatch return, and single vs. split delivery/payment within split-package orders.
- **Equivalence properties**: Reflexive, symmetric, and transitive (kernel of $f_{\text{motif}}$).
- **Concurrency and same-time events**: Fully invariant to event ordering because all predicates depend only on set-level and graph-level existence quantifiers.

## 2. Canonical signatures

The following Python implementation computes the reproducible canonical signature $\sigma_{\text{sem}}(X)$ for the preferred operational business-semantic equivalence relation. It assigns canonical object role identifiers via Weisfeiler-Lehman (WL) structural-semantic refinement (incorporating object type, `LOC`/`EXT` scope, incident activity profiles, and qualified O2O roles) before breaking remaining pure symmetries (such as interchangeable items `I11, I12` in `X1` and `I22, I21` in `X2`) by causal appearance rank.

```python
from collections import Counter, defaultdict
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Dict, List, Set, Tuple
import hashlib

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Event:
    idx: int
    activity: str
    objects: Tuple[Tuple[str, str], ...]  # ((obj_type, obj_id), ...)

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class O2OEdge:
    src: str
    qualifier: str
    dst: str

@dataclass
class Execution:
    exec_id: str
    lead_order: str
    events: List[Event]
    o2o: List[O2OEdge]

STAGE_RANK = {
    "Create Order": 1,
    "Add Item": 2,
    "Pack Package": 3,
    "Dispatch": 3,
    "Return Item": 3,
    "Inspect Return": 3,
    "Issue Invoice": 4,
    "Record Payment": 5,
}

def object_scope(oid: str) -> str:
    return "EXT" if oid.startswith("EXT") else "LOC"

def compute_canonical_signature(ex: Execution) -> Dict[str, object]:
    # 1. Collect object metadata (type, scope, lead flag)
    obj_type: Dict[str, str] = {}
    for ev in ex.events:
        for otype, oid in ev.objects:
            obj_type[oid] = otype

    # 2. Build intrinsic per-object semantic-structural fingerprint
    ev_profile: Dict[str, List[Tuple[str, Tuple[Tuple[str, str, int], ...]]]] = defaultdict(list)
    for ev in ex.events:
        type_scope_counts = Counter((ot, object_scope(oid)) for ot, oid in ev.objects)
        co_context = tuple(sorted((ot, sc, cnt) for (ot, sc), cnt in type_scope_counts.items()))
        for _, oid in ev.objects:
            ev_profile[oid].append((ev.activity, co_context))

    out_o2o: Dict[str, List[Tuple[str, str, str]]] = defaultdict(list)
    in_o2o: Dict[str, List[Tuple[str, str, str]]] = defaultdict(list)
    for edge in ex.o2o:
        out_o2o[edge.src].append((edge.qualifier, obj_type[edge.dst], object_scope(edge.dst)))
        in_o2o[edge.dst].append((edge.qualifier, obj_type[edge.src], object_scope(edge.src)))

    # Note: for pure item creation ("Add Item"), multiple Add Item events are unordered
    # unless distinguished by downstream E2O/O2O roles (e.g. I72 vs I71 in X7).
    base_color: Dict[str, Tuple] = {}
    for oid, otype in obj_type.items():
        non_add_seq = tuple(item for item in ev_profile[oid] if item[0] != "Add Item")
        add_count = sum(1 for item in ev_profile[oid] if item[0] == "Add Item")
        base_color[oid] = (
            otype,
            object_scope(oid),
            oid == ex.lead_order,
            add_count,
            non_add_seq,
            tuple(sorted(out_o2o[oid])),
            tuple(sorted(in_o2o[oid])),
        )

    # 3. 1-step Weisfeiler-Lehman refinement over qualified O2O and E2O neighborhoods
    refined_color: Dict[str, Tuple] = {}
    for oid in obj_type:
        o2o_nbrs = tuple(sorted(
            (edge.qualifier, "OUT", base_color[edge.dst])
            for edge in ex.o2o if edge.src == oid
        )) + tuple(sorted(
            (edge.qualifier, "IN", base_color[edge.src])
            for edge in ex.o2o if edge.dst == oid
        ))
        refined_color[oid] = (base_color[oid], o2o_nbrs)

    # 4. Assign canonical role IDs per (type, scope)
    # Break exact structural symmetry by first appearance in non-Add events, then Add events
    first_non_add = {
        oid: min((ev.idx for ev in ex.events if any(o == oid for _, o in ev.objects) and ev.activity != "Add Item"), default=999)
        for oid in obj_type
    }
    first_any = {
        oid: min(ev.idx for ev in ex.events if any(o == oid for _, o in ev.objects))
        for oid in obj_type
    }

    canonical_id: Dict[str, str] = {}
    grouped_objs: Dict[Tuple[str, str], List[str]] = defaultdict(list)
    for oid, otype in obj_type.items():
        grouped_objs[(otype, object_scope(oid))].append(oid)

    for (otype, sc), oids in sorted(grouped_objs.items()):
        oids_sorted = sorted(
            oids,
            key=lambda o: (refined_color[o], first_non_add[o], first_any[o], o),
        )
        for rank, oid in enumerate(oids_sorted, start=1):
            canonical_id[oid] = f"{otype}:{sc}#{rank}"

    # 5. Build canonical multiplicity profile
    multiplicity_sig = tuple(
        (otype, sc, len(oids))
        for (otype, sc), oids in sorted(grouped_objs.items())
    )

    # 6. Build causal partial-order event trace
    # Events sharing non-lead objects or crossing macro-stages preserve causal order;
    # concurrent symmetric events (e.g., Add Item) sort by canonical E2O target tuples.
    canon_events = []
    for ev in ex.events:
        c_objs = tuple(sorted(canonical_id[oid] for _, oid in ev.objects))
        non_lead_objs = frozenset(
            canonical_id[oid] for _, oid in ev.objects if oid != ex.lead_order
        )
        canon_events.append({
            "orig_idx": ev.idx,
            "stage": STAGE_RANK.get(ev.activity, 99),
            "activity": ev.activity,
            "c_objs": c_objs,
            "non_lead": non_lead_objs,
        })

    # Topological sort respecting stage order and shared non-lead object order
    n = len(canon_events)
    preds: Dict[int, Set[int]] = {i: set() for i in range(n)}
    for i in range(n):
        for j in range(i + 1, n):
            shares_sub_obj = bool(canon_events[i]["non_lead"] & canon_events[j]["non_lead"])
            crosses_stage = canon_events[i]["stage"] != canon_events[j]["stage"]
            same_act_rework = (
                canon_events[i]["activity"] == canon_events[j]["activity"]
                and canon_events[i]["activity"] != "Add Item"
            )
            if shares_sub_obj or crosses_stage or same_act_rework:
                preds[j].add(i)

    placed: Set[int] = set()
    ordered_trace: List[Tuple[str, Tuple[str, ...]]] = []
    while len(placed) < n:
        ready = [i for i in range(n) if i not in placed and preds[i].issubset(placed)]
        ready.sort(key=lambda i: (canon_events[i]["stage"], canon_events[i]["activity"], canon_events[i]["c_objs"]))
        chosen = ready[0]
        placed.add(chosen)
        ordered_trace.append((canon_events[chosen]["activity"], canon_events[chosen]["c_objs"]))

    # 7. Canonicalize qualified O2O edges
    canon_o2o = tuple(sorted(
        (canonical_id[edge.src], edge.qualifier, canonical_id[edge.dst])
        for edge in ex.o2o
    ))

    full_tuple = (multiplicity_sig, tuple(ordered_trace), canon_o2o)
    digest = hashlib.sha256(repr(full_tuple).encode("utf-8")).hexdigest()[:10]
    return {
        "multiplicity": multiplicity_sig,
        "event_trace": tuple(ordered_trace),
        "o2o": canon_o2o,
        "hash": digest,
    }
```

## 4. Cluster interpretations

### Cluster `C1` (`{X1, X2}`) — Isolated Single-Flow Order
- **Concise signature**: `LOC[O:1,I:2,P:1,D:1,V:1,M:1] | EXT[0] | 1P->1D, 1V->1M | Clean`
- **Business behavior**: Straight-through order-to-cash execution. Both line items are picked and packed into a single container, shipped in one delivery, billed on one dedicated invoice, and settled in a single full payment.
- **Operational risks or opportunities**: Lowest unit handling cost and cleanest audit trail; serves as the golden benchmark for cycle-time SLA tracking and touchless automation.
- **Poorly represented by flattening**: Flattening on `Order` duplicates `Add Item` (divergence), while flattening on `Item` duplicates `Pack Package`, `Dispatch`, `Issue Invoice`, and `Record Payment` across both items (convergence, inflating shipment and invoice counts by $2\times$).
- **Stability under data repair**: Highly stable; unaffected by identifier normalization or timestamp tie-breaking.

### Cluster `C2` (`{X3}`) — Split-Package Consolidated Delivery
- **Concise signature**: `LOC[O:1,I:2,P:2,D:1,V:1,M:1] | EXT[0] | 2P->1D, 1V->1M | Clean`
- **Business behavior**: Items are packed into separate boxes (`P31`, `P32`)—typically due to size, fragility, or multi-zone warehouse picking—but staged at the shipping dock to depart on a single consolidated delivery (`D31`).
- **Operational risks or opportunities**: Opportunity to save carrier line-haul fees via dock consolidation; operational risk of dock-staging delays while `P31` waits for `P32` to finish packing.
- **Poorly represented by flattening**: Flattening on `Order` or `Delivery` hides which item sits in which package; flattening on `Package` splits the order into two traces that duplicate `Dispatch`, `Issue Invoice`, and `Record Payment` (convergence).
- **Stability under data repair**: Moderately stable, but vulnerable if an event-cleaning script deduplicates consecutive `Pack Package` events as scanner double-reads when package IDs are missing.

### Cluster `C3` (`{X4}`) — Multi-Wave Fulfillment & Installment Settlement
- **Concise signature**: `LOC[O:1,I:2,P:2,D:2,V:1,M:2] | EXT[0] | 2x(1P->1D), 1V->2M(partial,complete)`
- **Business behavior**: Partial/backorder fulfillment where the first available item (`I41`) is packed and dispatched immediately (`P41 -> D41`) before the second item (`I42`) is packed and dispatched (`P42 -> D42`), followed by a single invoice settled across two installment payments (`M41`, `M42`).
- **Operational risks or opportunities**: Higher shipping cost ($2\times$ deliveries) and elevated credit/collections risk from partial payment (`M41`), balanced against faster first-item customer delivery.
- **Poorly represented by flattening**: Flattening on `Order` creates an apparent rework loop (`Pack -> Dispatch -> Pack -> Dispatch`) and repeated `Record Payment`, misclassifying parallel sub-object progress as process thrashing (divergence). Flattening on `Delivery` misses or duplicates the two payments ($2 \times 2 = 4$ payment edges).
- **Stability under data repair**: Sensitive to ERP ledger aggregation (which often collapses installment records `M41, M42` into one paid status) and partial-order trace normalization.

### Cluster `C4` (`{X5}`) — Cross-Order Logistics Consolidation
- **Concise signature**: `LOC[O:1,I:2,P:1,D:1,V:1,M:1] | EXT[O:1,I:1] | Shared(P,D), Isolated(V,M)`
- **Business behavior**: Multi-order warehouse co-packing and co-shipping. Items from `O5` and an external order `EXT-OA` (likely for the same customer ship-to address) are packed into one shared box `P51` and shipped on one shared delivery `D51`, while invoicing (`V51`) remains strictly per-order.
- **Operational risks or opportunities**: Major freight and packaging cost savings, but introduces cross-order fate sharing: a customs hold, stockout, or damage on `EXT-IA` delays `O5` as well, and freight cost allocation across orders becomes non-trivial.
- **Poorly represented by flattening**: Flattening on `Order` (`O5`) produces an event sequence indistinguishable from `X1` (deficiency of cross-order context). Flattening on `Package` (`P51`) merges two separate orders' lifecycles and interleaves their separate invoices.
- **Stability under data repair**: Fragile under subgraph truncation; any repair or extraction step that strips `EXT-*` objects collapses `C4` into `C1`.

### Cluster `C5` (`{X6}`) — Cross-Order Collective Invoicing & Settlement
- **Concise signature**: `LOC[O:1,I:2,P:1,D:1,V:1,M:1] | EXT[O:1] | Isolated(P,D), Shared(V,M)`
- **Business behavior**: Dedicated physical fulfillment per order (`P61`, `D61`), followed by consolidated periodic/collective billing (`V61` bills both `O6` and `EXT-OB`) and a single unallocated lump-sum payment (`M61`).
- **Operational risks or opportunities**: Reduces accounts-receivable transaction overhead for B2B buyers, but creates cash-application and dispute contagion: a dispute on `EXT-OB` can hold up settlement of `V61`, and `M61` lacks order-level allocation.
- **Poorly represented by flattening**: Flattening on `Order` makes `V61` and `M61` appear dedicated to `O6`, double-counting invoice value across `O6` and `EXT-OB` (convergence). Flattening on `Invoice` mixes independent warehouse executions from multiple orders.
- **Stability under data repair**: Sensitive to financial ERP post-processing; synthetic invoice-splitting repairs merge `C5` into `C1`, whereas keeping raw unallocated payment links preserves `C5`.

### Cluster `C6` (`{X7}`) — Pre-Dispatch Item Substitution
- **Concise signature**: `LOC[O:1,I:2,P:1,D:1,V:1,M:1] | EXT[0] | Disp(I2 only), O2O:replaces+removed_before_dispatch`
- **Business behavior**: An exception occurs between packing and shipping: `I71` and `I72` are initially associated with `P71`, but `I72` substitutes for `I71` (`I72 replaces I71`), `I71` is pulled (`removed_before_dispatch`), and only `I72` is dispatched with `P71`/`D71`.
- **Operational risks or opportunities**: High compliance and billing risk: `V71` bills `O7` after `I71` was removed before dispatch, creating a risk of overbilling if `I71` and `I72` were both charged or if inventory for `I71` was not properly restocked.
- **Poorly represented by flattening**: Flattening on `Order`, `Package`, `Delivery`, or `Invoice` yields the **exact 7-event trace of `X1`**, completely hiding the substitution and pre-dispatch removal.
- **Stability under data repair**: Highly fragile. Automated E2O imputation that assumes all packed items ship with their package (or drops qualifiers during schema simplification) destroys the signal and merges `C6` into `C1`.

### Cluster `C7` (`{X8}`) — Post-Dispatch Return & Repack
- **Concise signature**: `LOC[O:1,I:2,P:2,D:2,V:1,M:1] | EXT[0] | Return+Inspect(I2)->Repack(P2)->Disp(D2), P2_replaces_P1`
- **Business behavior**: After initial full shipment (`P81`, `D81`), item `I82` is returned (`Return Item`), inspected (`Inspect Return`), repacked into replacement package `P82` (`P82 repacks I82`, `P82 replaces P81`), and reshipped (`D82`) prior to invoicing (`V81`) and settlement (`M81`).
- **Operational risks or opportunities**: High reverse-logistics cost and extended order-to-cash cycle time (invoicing is delayed until after reshipment); signals potential picking errors, transit damage, or product quality defects on `I82`.
- **Poorly represented by flattening**: Flattening on `Order` loses the fact that only `I82` (not `I81`) was returned and reshipped; flattening on `Package` fractures the causal chain across `P81` and `P82`, making `P82` look like an orphan shipment without `Create Order` or `Return Item`.
- **Stability under data repair**: Very stable; explicit `Return Item` and `Inspect Return` events keep `C7` separated even if O2O qualifiers are dropped.

## 5. Borderline cases

### 5.1 `X1` versus `X2`
- **How definitions disagree**: A **raw-identifier or lexicographic-ID-indexed equivalence** splits `X1` and `X2` because `X2` adds `I22` before `I21` (so sorting item IDs alphabetically maps `I21 -> #1, I22 -> #2`, yielding `Add(#2) -> Add(#1)` in `X2` versus `Add(#1) -> Add(#2)` in `X1`). Conversely, **typed structural equivalence ($\sim_{\text{struct}}$)** and **business-semantic equivalence ($\sim_{\text{sem}}$)** merge them because the bijection $\phi(I11)=I22, \phi(I12)=I21$ preserves all E2O and O2O relations (`Pack Package` takes the unordered set $\{I21, I22\}$).

### 5.2 `X1` versus `X5`
- **How definitions disagree**: **Activity-sequence equivalence ($\sim_{\text{act}}$)** and **lead-order-local projection equivalence** (which filters out `EXT-*` objects before comparison) merge `X1` and `X5` because `O5`'s local lifecycle has the same 7 activities and 1:2:1:1:1:1 local object counts as `O1`. **Typed structural ($\sim_{\text{struct}}$)** and **business-semantic equivalence ($\sim_{\text{sem}}$)** split them because `P51` and `D51` are shared with `EXT-OA` and `EXT-IA`, creating cross-order logistics coupling.

### 5.3 `X1` versus `X6`
- **How definitions disagree**: **Activity-sequence equivalence ($\sim_{\text{act}}$)** and **warehouse/logistics-scoped equivalence** merge `X1` and `X6` because physical fulfillment (`O6, I61, I62, P61, D61`) is identical to `X1`. **Typed structural ($\sim_{\text{struct}}$)** and **business-semantic equivalence ($\sim_{\text{sem}}$)** split them because `V61` and `M61` are co-linked to external order `EXT-OB`, changing financial settlement from dedicated 1:1 billing to unallocated collective billing.

### 5.4 `X1` versus `X7`
- **How definitions disagree**: **Activity-sequence equivalence ($\sim_{\text{act}}$)** and **coarse multiplicity equivalence** (comparing only activity traces and per-type object counts `(O:1, I:2, P:1, D:1, V:1, M:1)`) merge `X1` and `X7`. **Typed structural ($\sim_{\text{struct}}$)** and **business-semantic equivalence ($\sim_{\text{sem}}$)** split them on two grounds: (1) E2O incidence at `Dispatch` includes `Item:I72` in `X7` (degree 4 vs. degree 3 in `X1`), and (2) `X7` carries the `I72 replaces I71` and `removed_before_dispatch` O2O qualifiers showing only one item shipped.

### 5.5 `X3` versus `X4`
- **How definitions disagree**: **Macro-archetype equivalence ($\sim_{\text{motif}}$)** (or a warehouse-centric relation that treats `Pack`/`Dispatch` as a partial order, ignores delivery consolidation, and treats payment multiplicity as a continuous amount attribute) merges `X3` and `X4` into a single *Multi-Package Split Fulfillment* cluster (`M2`). **Operational business-semantic equivalence ($\sim_{\text{sem}}$)** and **typed structural equivalence ($\sim_{\text{struct}}$)** split them because `X3` consolidates two packages into 1 delivery (`D31`) and 1 full payment (`M31`), whereas `X4` dispatches 2 separate deliveries (`D41, D42`) and collects 2 installment payments (`M41, M42`).

### 5.6 `X4` versus `X8`
- **How definitions disagree**: A **coarse logistics-multiplicity projection** (clustering by local counts `(Order:1, Item:2, Package:2, Delivery:2, Invoice:1)` or by the existence of two `Pack Package -> Dispatch` waves while ignoring rework activities and payment counts) groups `X4` and `X8` together as two-shipment orders. **Activity-sequence ($\sim_{\text{act}}$)**, **typed structural ($\sim_{\text{struct}}$)**, **business-semantic ($\sim_{\text{sem}}$)**, and **macro-archetype ($\sim_{\text{motif}}$)** equivalences all split them: `X4` is forward split fulfillment of two *different* items (`I41` in `P41/D41`, `I42` in `P42/D42`) with split payments, whereas `X8` is a reverse-logistics return (`Return Item`, `Inspect Return`) and reshipment of the *same* item `I82` (`P82 replaces P81`) with a single payment.

## 6. Relation to `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel`

### 6.1 How `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel` works and what its key preserves
In PM4Py, `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, object_type="Order", max_objs=...)` first extracts one sub-OCEL per lead object of type `Order` via `split_ocel_ancestors_descendants(ocel, object_type, max_objs=max_objs)` and then groups those sub-OCELs by a structural hash/tuple key derived from the sub-OCEL's events, E2O relations, and O2O relations:
- **What the PM4Py key structure preserves**: It preserves the ordered sequence of event activities, the typed E2O incidence structure linking events to objects, and the O2O relationship structure within each extracted sub-OCEL.
- **Why raw object identifiers make exact clusters too fine**: If a sub-OCEL key retains raw object IDs (`O1, I11` vs. `O2, I22`)—or assigns positional indices by sorting raw object strings lexicographically (`I21 < I22` even when `I22` appeared first in `X2`)—then every order or every ID-permuted isomorphic execution lands in a separate cluster (`X1` and `X2` split). Conversely, if only activity sequences and coarse type counts are hashed without `EXT` scope flags or O2O qualifiers, `X7` (and `X5, X6` when truncated) over-merge with `X1`.
- **Canonicalization and post-processing**: We treat `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel` as a fast first-pass structural pre-partition, extract the sub-OCELs from its returned dictionary, strip raw identifiers via WL + causal-partial-order canonicalization (`compute_canonical_signature`), and re-aggregate sub-OCELs into operational clusters (`C1`–`C7`) and macro-archetypes (`M1`–`M4`).
- **When exact equivalence is preferable to coarser business clustering**: Exact equivalence is preferable for ETL regression testing, digital-twin replay caching, deterministic conformance checking (where identical sub-OCELs share one alignment computation), and strict forensic auditing.
- **Impact of `max_objs` and large connected executions**: Shared objects (`P51` in `X5`, `V61` in `X6`, or hub objects like consolidated monthly invoices and carrier manifests) create giant connected components ("hairball sub-OCELs"). With `max_objs=None`, ancestor-descendant expansion traverses from `O6 -> V61 -> EXT-OB` and pulls in `EXT-OB`'s entire upstream execution, making every shared-resource order structurally unique. Conversely, setting `max_objs` too small (e.g., `max_objs=7`, the local size of `X1`) truncates `EXT-OA`/`EXT-IA`/`EXT-OB`, either deterministically erasing cross-order sharing (`X5, X6` collapse into `X1`) or non-deterministically dropping local objects depending on graph traversal tie-breaking.

### 6.2 Executable Python outline using `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel`

```python
import pm4py
from collections import defaultdict
from typing import Dict, List, Tuple

def canonicalize_sub_ocel(sub_ocel, lead_order_id: str) -> Tuple:
    """
    Post-processes a PM4Py sub-OCEL centered on `lead_order_id` into a
    renaming-invariant business-semantic signature and macro-archetype key.
    """
    ev_df = sub_ocel.events.sort_values(sub_ocel.event_timestamp)
    rel_df = sub_ocel.relations
    o2o_df = sub_ocel.o2o

    # Identify local vs. external objects relative to lead_order_id
    obj_df = sub_ocel.objects
    obj_types = dict(zip(obj_df[sub_ocel.object_id_column], obj_df[sub_ocel.object_type_column]))

    def get_scope(oid: str, otype: str) -> str:
        if oid.startswith("EXT") or (otype == "Order" and oid != lead_order_id):
            return "EXT"
        return "LOC"

    # Build event-level E2O profiles and O2O qualifier signatures per object
    e2o_by_event = defaultdict(list)
    obj_event_acts = defaultdict(list)
    for _, row in rel_df.iterrows():
        eid = row[sub_ocel.event_id_column]
        oid = row[sub_ocel.object_id_column]
        otype = row[sub_ocel.object_type_column]
        e2o_by_event[eid].append((otype, oid))
        obj_event_acts[oid].append(row[sub_ocel.event_activity])

    o2o_out = defaultdict(list)
    o2o_in = defaultdict(list)
    for _, row in o2o_df.iterrows():
        src, dst, qual = row[sub_ocel.object_id_column], row[sub_ocel.object_id_column + "_2"], row["ocel:qualifier"]
        o2o_out[src].append((qual, obj_types.get(dst, "UNK"), get_scope(dst, obj_types.get(dst, "UNK"))))
        o2o_in[dst].append((qual, obj_types.get(src, "UNK"), get_scope(src, obj_types.get(src, "UNK"))))

    # Canonical role indexing per (object_type, scope)
    grouped = defaultdict(list)
    for oid, otype in obj_types.items():
        grouped[(otype, get_scope(oid, otype))].append(oid)

    canon_map = {}
    for (otype, sc), oids in sorted(grouped.items()):
        oids_sorted = sorted(
            oids,
            key=lambda o: (
                tuple(a for a in obj_event_acts[o] if a != "Add Item"),
                obj_event_acts[o].count("Add Item"),
                tuple(sorted(o2o_out[o])),
                tuple(sorted(o2o_in[o])),
            ),
        )
        for rank, oid in enumerate(oids_sorted, start=1):
            canon_map[oid] = f"{otype}:{sc}#{rank}"

    # Build canonical event sequence (sorting concurrent Add Item steps canonically)
    canon_events = []
    for _, ev in ev_df.iterrows():
        eid = ev[sub_ocel.event_id_column]
        act = ev[sub_ocel.event_activity]
        c_targets = tuple(sorted(canon_map[oid] for _, oid in e2o_by_event[eid]))
        canon_events.append((act, c_targets))

    # Normalize adjacent symmetric Add Item order
    i = 0
    while i < len(canon_events) - 1:
        if canon_events[i][0] == "Add Item" and canon_events[i + 1][0] == "Add Item":
            if canon_events[i] > canon_events[i + 1]:
                canon_events[i], canon_events[i + 1] = canon_events[i + 1], canon_events[i]
        i += 1

    canon_o2o = tuple(sorted(
        (canon_map[row[sub_ocel.object_id_column]], row["ocel:qualifier"], canon_map[row[sub_ocel.object_id_column + "_2"]])
        for _, row in o2o_df.iterrows()
    ))
    mult_sig = tuple((ot, sc, len(oids)) for (ot, sc), oids in sorted(grouped.items()))
    return (mult_sig, tuple(canon_events), canon_o2o)

def cluster_orders_semantically(ocel, max_objs: int = 15) -> Dict[str, List]:
    # Step 1: Run PM4Py's built-in execution clustering centered on lead object type "Order"
    raw_pm4py_clusters = pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(
        ocel,
        object_type="Order",
        max_objs=max_objs,
    )

    # Step 2: Canonicalize and re-aggregate over-segmented clusters
    semantic_clusters = defaultdict(list)
    for _, sub_ocel_list in raw_pm4py_clusters.items():
        for sub_ocel in sub_ocel_list:
            orders = sub_ocel.objects[
                sub_ocel.objects[sub_ocel.object_type_column] == "Order"
            ][sub_ocel.object_id_column].tolist()
            lead_orders = [o for o in orders if not o.startswith("EXT")]
            lead_order_id = lead_orders[0] if lead_orders else orders[0]

            sem_key = canonicalize_sub_ocel(sub_ocel, lead_order_id)
            semantic_clusters[sem_key].append((lead_order_id, sub_ocel))

    return dict(semantic_clusters)
```

## 7. Robustness analysis

The table below summarizes how the preferred semantic partition (`C1`–`C7`) responds to five perturbations:

| Perturbation | Resulting Partition of `X1`–`X8` | Clusters | Key Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Baseline ($\sim_{\text{sem}}$)** | `{X1,X2}, {X3}, {X4}, {X5}, {X6}, {X7}, {X8}` | 7 | Preserves multiplicities, `EXT` scope, causal partial order, E2O, and O2O qualifiers. |
| **P1: Ignore all O2O qualifiers** | `{X1,X2}, {X3}, {X4}, {X5}, {X6}, {X7}*, {X8}` *(or `{X1,X2,X7}` if `Dispatch->I72` is dropped)* | 7 (or 6) | `X7` loses `replaces` and `removed_before_dispatch`; only `Dispatch{Item:I72}` separates `X7` from `{X1,X2}`. |
| **P2: Ignore external objects (`EXT-*`)** | `{X1,X2,X5,X6}, {X3}, {X4}, {X7}, {X8}` | 5 | Stripping `EXT-OA`, `EXT-IA`, and `EXT-OB` makes `X5` and `X6` isomorphic to `X1` and `X2`. |
| **P3: Collapse repeated same-label events** | `{X1,X2,X3}*, {X4}, {X5}, {X6}, {X7}, {X8}` *(when `P` multiplicity is also abstracted)* | 6 (or 7) | Consecutive `Add Item+`, `Pack Package+` (`X3`), and `Record Payment+` (`X4`) collapse; `X3` merges into `{X1,X2}` if `Package` count is abstracted. |
| **P4: `Pack`/`Dispatch` as partial order** | `{X1,X2}, {X3}, {X4}, {X5}, {X6}, {X7}, {X8}` | 7 | `X4`'s interleaved `Pack->Disp->Pack->Disp` matches `Pack->Pack->Disp->Disp` poset-wise, but `D:2` and `M:2` still separate `X4` from `X3`. |
| **P5: Payment multiplicity as attribute (+ P4)** | `{X1,X2}, {X3}, {X4}, {X5}, {X6}, {X7}, {X8}` *(or `{X3,X4}` if delivery split is abstracted)* | 7 (or 6) | Folding `M41, M42` into `Payment#1[installments=2]` removes the financial split between `X4` and `X3`, leaving only `1 Delivery` vs. `2 Deliveries`. |

### Detailed perturbation analysis

1. **Ignoring all O2O qualifiers (P1)**: In `X7`, the substitution (`I72 replaces I71`) and pre-dispatch removal (`removed_before_dispatch`) reside in O2O qualifiers plus the E2O link `Dispatch{Item:I72}`. Dropping O2O qualifiers reduces `X7` to an unlabeled O2O graph where `P71` links to both `I71` and `I72` just like `X1` (plus an unlabeled `(I72, I71)` link). If an ETL schema only retains standard E2O types per activity (`Order, Package, Delivery` for `Dispatch`), `X7` collapses completely into `{X1, X2}`. In `X4` and `X8`, dropping `partially_settles`/`completes_settlement` and `repacks`/`replaces` loses semantic nuance, but their event sequences and object multiplicities keep them distinct.
2. **Ignoring external objects (P2)**: Filtering out `EXT-OA`, `EXT-IA`, and `EXT-OB` projects each execution onto objects owned solely by the lead order. Under this projection, `X5`'s shared package/delivery and `X6`'s shared invoice/payment become indistinguishable from dedicated 1:1 resources, merging `C1 ({X1, X2})`, `C4 ({X5})`, and `C5 ({X6})` into `{X1, X2, X5, X6}`.
3. **Collapsing repeated same-label events (P3)**: Stuttering reduction collapses adjacent `Add Item` events across all executions, `Pack Package, Pack Package` in `X3`, and `Record Payment, Record Payment` in `X4`. Because `Pack Package` and `Dispatch` in `X4` (`Pack -> Dispatch -> Pack -> Dispatch`) and `X8` (`Pack -> Dispatch -> Return -> Inspect -> Pack -> Dispatch`) are non-adjacent, they do not collapse. Thus `X3`'s collapsed activity trace matches `X1/X2`, and `X3` merges into `{X1, X2}` unless `Package:2` multiplicity is still enforced.
4. **Treating `Pack` and `Dispatch` order as a partial order (P4)**: Because `(P41, D41, I41)` and `(P42, D42, I42)` in `X4` share only the lead order `O4`, their causal partial order forms two independent branches `(Pack P41 -> Dispatch D41) || (Pack P42 -> Dispatch D42)`. Under partial-order equivalence, `X4` is invariant to whether `Pack(P42)` occurs before or after `Dispatch(D41)`. However, `X3` remains distinct because its single `Dispatch(D31)` is a synchronization join requiring both `Pack(P31)` and `Pack(P32)`.
5. **Treating payment multiplicity as an amount attribute rather than structure (P5)**: Replacing `M41` and `M42` in `X4` with a single `Payment` object carrying an attribute `installments=2` eliminates the financial structural difference between `X4` and `X3`. Combined with P4 and delivery-count abstraction, `X3` and `X4` merge into `M2 ({X3, X4})`.

## 8. Limitations and recommendation

### Recommended equivalence level by analytical purpose

1. **Compliance monitoring $\rightarrow$ Qualified Operational Business-Semantic Equivalence ($\sim_{\text{sem}}$, supplemented by $\sim_{\text{struct}}$ for strict audit ordering)**:
   - *Justification*: Compliance rules must detect unbilled shipments, billed-but-unshipped items (`X7`'s `removed_before_dispatch`), unallocated cross-order payments (`X6`'s `EXT-OB`), and return inspections before reshipment (`X8`). Coarser relations ($\sim_{\text{act}}$, $\sim_{\text{motif}}$) hide `X7`, `X5`, and `X6` inside the compliant baseline `X1`.
2. **Operational segmentation $\rightarrow$ Operational Business-Semantic Equivalence ($\sim_{\text{sem}}$, 7 clusters `C1`–`C7`)**:
   - *Justification*: Warehouse and finance operations teams need each distinct operational regime separated (`C2` dock consolidation vs. `C3` split-wave backorder vs. `C4` co-packing vs. `C5` collective billing) while ignoring noise from local ID naming (`X1` vs. `X2`) and concurrent item-entry order.
3. **Root-cause analysis $\rightarrow$ Fine-Grained Typed Structural + Qualified Semantic Equivalence ($\sim_{\text{struct}} \cap \sim_{\text{sem}}$)**:
   - *Justification*: Diagnosing SLA breaches or invoice disputes requires preserving exact timestamp interleavings (e.g., whether `Pack(P42)` was delayed until after `Dispatch(D41)` in `X4`), exact E2O bindings (`Dispatch` binding `I72` in `X7`), and external object neighborhoods (`EXT-OA`, `EXT-OB`).
4. **Descriptive reporting $\rightarrow$ Macro-Archetype Lifecycle Equivalence ($\sim_{\text{motif}}$, 4 clusters `M1`–`M4`)**:
   - *Justification*: Executive dashboards become unreadable when hundreds of minor multiplicity and interleaving variants fragment the log into a long tail of micro-clusters. Grouping into four high-level business motifs (`M1` Standard Isolated 25%, `M2` Split Fulfillment 25%, `M3` Cross-Order Shared 25%, `M4` Exception/Replacement 25%) communicates portfolio health clearly.

### Why no single equivalence notion is best for all four purposes

Every equivalence relation trades **discriminative fidelity (low false-merge rate)** against **statistical concentration (low false-split / fragmentation rate)** across incompatible stakeholder dimensions:
- A relation fine enough for **compliance monitoring and root-cause analysis** ($\sim_{\text{sem}}$ or $\sim_{\text{struct}}$) must split executions whenever an O2O qualifier (`removed_before_dispatch`), an external boundary link (`EXT-OB`), or a split delivery/payment occurs. In real ERP logs with variable item counts ($1\dots 50$ items), partial shipments, and collective invoices, this produces hundreds of sparse clusters that defeat **descriptive reporting**.
- Conversely, a relation coarse enough for **descriptive reporting** ($\sim_{\text{motif}}$ or $\sim_{\text{act}}$) compresses the variant space into a few interpretable buckets, but necessarily conflates pre-dispatch warehouse substitutions (`X7`) with post-dispatch customer returns (`X8`), or worse, hides cross-order leakage (`X5, X6`) and unshipped items (`X7`) inside the happy path (`X1`).

Consequently, production object-centric process mining systems should compute the **canonical fine-grained semantic signature ($\sigma_{\text{sem}}$) once**, store it as a base lattice, and project upward to $\sim_{\text{motif}}$ for executive reporting or downward to $\sim_{\text{struct}}$ for forensic drill-down.
