## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The gap between `raw_model_fitness` and `mapped_model_fitness` is large immediately after the March migration (0.92  0.55 vs. 0.90) and persists through mid-year, indicating that the raw log’s labels and duplicated events severely distort conformance checking. Mapping `Eligibility Screen` to `Validate Eligibility` and removing duplicated `Open Case` events recovers fitness to ~0.88–0.91, suggesting that the *semantic drift* in labels and *logging drift* from duplicated technical events explain most of the early collapse. However, this recovery does **not** imply the business process was unchanged: the post-migration DFG shows reduced `Eligibility->CollectDocs` rates (98  96) and increased `Assess->RequestInfo` (12  24), indicating real behavioral changes in routing and rework. Duplicated technical events inflate activity counts and distort edge frequencies, while missing resource identifiers introduce noise in resource-aware analyses (e.g., team throughput). Post-May patterns—rising cycle times, rework, and priority-induced redistribution—cannot be dismissed as logging drift alone because they persist after label normalization and align with documented supplier disruption, staffing cuts, and policy changes. The residual drift in mapped fitness (~0.86–0.89) and sustained performance degradation indicate real process changes beyond provenance issues.

**Primary monitoring population**: *Non-Supplier Z, standard complexity, pre-renewal season (Jan–Sep 2026)*.
**Why**: Minimizes confounding from supplier disruption, seasonal complexity surge, and priority-induced redistribution; provides stable baseline for detecting new drift.

- Use 12-month rolling baselines for seasonal monitors; apply STL decomposition to remove seasonal component.

## 9. Provisional attribution

**Strongest conclusions**:
- **March 2026**: The collapse in `raw_model_fitness` and appearance of `Eligibility Screen` and duplicated `Open Case` events are **primarily logging/semantic drift** (confidence ~95%). The recovery after mapping confirms provenance issues; no evidence suggests real process change at this exact moment.
- **May–September 2026**: Supplier Z disruption caused **supplier-driven rework and queueing**, evidenced by doubled `rework_variant_pct`, elevated `Assess->RequestInfo` loops, and sustained cycle-time deterioration (confidence ~90%). Staffing cuts in June exacerbated queueing, but supplier disruption is the dominant driver.

**Plausible conclusions**:
- **July 2026**: The priority rule likely **redistributed delay from high-complexity to standard cases**, increasing p90 cycle time disproportionately (confidence ~80%). Direct observation of priority class is missing, so this is inferred from routing changes.
- **October 2026**: The **seasonal renewal surge** in cases and high-complexity share is the primary driver of cycle-time peaks (confidence ~95%). Interventions in December reduced cycle times, supporting a seasonal explanation.

**Where mediation prevents clean attribution**:
- The **interaction among supplier disruption, staffing cuts, and priority rule** (Jul–Sep) makes it hard to isolate individual effects on queueing and rework. A counterfactual simulation removing each mechanism sequentially would be needed.
- **Measurement missingness** (resource identifiers) is concentrated in migrated activities, but it does not explain cycle-time rises; however, it may mask true resource constraints.

**What would revise the account**:
- If supplier disruption logs showed no rework loops despite high `rework_variant_pct`, the behavioral attribution would weaken.
- If priority class data showed no delay redistribution, the priority-induced mechanism would be downgraded.
- If staffing data showed full replacement of lost assessors by September, the staffing attribution would weaken.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. **IF** `raw_model_fitness` drops >20% in a month **THEN** trigger a logging/semantic drift investigation **BECAUSE** sudden label or event duplication changes are the most likely cause.

2. **IF** `rework_variant_pct` increases >50% vs. 3-month baseline **THEN** escalate supplier disruption alert and segment by `supplier_z_pct` **BECAUSE** supplier-driven rework is the dominant mechanism for rework surges.

3. **IF** median waiting time before `Assess` rises >50% and `fast_variant_pct` falls >20% **THEN** initiate staffing and queueing review **BECAUSE** capacity constraints and priority-induced redistribution are the most plausible causes.

4. **IF** `high_complexity_pct` jumps >15% in a month **THEN** activate seasonal case-mix monitoring and compare to prior years **BECAUSE** renewal season is the primary driver of complexity surges.

5. **IF** `mapped_model_fitness` falls below 0.88 for two consecutive months **THEN** investigate behavioral adaptation and DFG edge changes **BECAUSE** persistent conformance drift indicates real process changes beyond provenance issues.

6. **IF** `missing_resource_pct` exceeds 3% in migrated activities **THEN** inspect team logs and compare to HR staffing data **BECAUSE** logging drift in resource identifiers may mask true resource constraints.

7. **IF** p90 cycle time rises >40% and median cycle time rises <20% **THEN** check priority rule implementation and complexity routing **BECAUSE** priority-induced redistribution disproportionately affects tail latency.
