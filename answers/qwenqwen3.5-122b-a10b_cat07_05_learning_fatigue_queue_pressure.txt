# 2. Integrated explanation

The observed performance patterns cannot be attributed to a single factor like fatigue or learning. Instead, they represent a dynamic interplay between **case selection**, **operational pressure**, and **human adaptation**.

**Early Shift (Sequence 1–30): The Complexity & Learning Phase**
Service times are high initially (18.8 min) primarily due to **case-mix selection**. Managers and seniors prioritize complex, high-risk cases early in the shift to clear backlog (evidenced by 0.72 complexity score and 41% high-priority share). As workers process these, they enter a **learning/warm-up** phase, reducing service time to 12.9 min by position 30. Errors are moderate because the work is complex but the queue pressure is manageable (WIP 31–46).

**Mid Shift (Sequence 31–50): The Efficiency Phase**
This is the "sweet spot." Complexity has dropped (0.50), queue pressure is lowest (WIP 31), and workers are warmed up. Service time bottoms out at 12.9 min with the lowest error rate (2.5%). This is driven by **learning** and favorable **case-mix**, not pressure.

**Late Shift (Sequence 51+): The Pressure Phase**
Service time continues to drop (10.8 min), but error rates spike (7.9%). This divergence is the critical signal. If this were pure learning, errors should remain low. If this were pure fatigue, service time should rise. The simultaneous drop in time and rise in errors indicates **queue pressure** and **throughput prioritization**.
*   **Mechanism:** As `queue_length_at_start` rises (33  88), workers accelerate processing to clear the backlog.
*   **Consequence:** Under high WIP, workers skip verification steps or rely too heavily on automation (Bot_Risk corrections rise). This is confirmed by the WIP table: High WIP + Low Complexity yields a 5.8% error rate (vs 2.1% baseline), while High WIP + High Complexity yields 10.9%.
*   **Resource Variance:** Seniors buffer this pressure (4.9% error at high WIP) due to experience. Juniors crash (12.4% error), suggesting the pressure exceeds their cognitive capacity.

**Batching Effects:**
Specific behaviors, such as Junior_H/I batching Document Review late in the Day shift, artificially lower service time metrics but likely contribute to the late-shift error spike by deferring cognitive load or bypassing individual case checks.

**Conclusion:** The pattern is a **U-shaped error curve** driven by an **inverted U-shaped queue pressure curve**. Early errors are complexity-driven; late errors are pressure-driven. Speed-up is a response to WIP accumulation, not just skill acquisition.

# 5. Ethical and operational cautions

**Selection Bias and Fairness**
Workers are not assigned cases randomly. Seniors take complex cases early (0.72 complexity), while juniors inherit simpler cases later (0.43 complexity). Comparing raw error rates between seniors and juniors without complexity adjustment is unfair. A junior processing a 0.43 complexity case with 7.9% error is performing worse than a senior processing 0.72 complexity with 3.4% error, but the raw numbers mask the difficulty differential.

**Management-Induced Pressure**
The rise in queue length (WIP) late in the shift is often a result of demand peaks or upstream bottlenecks, not just worker slowness. Penalizing workers for errors that occur when `queue_length_at_start` > 70 attributes a systemic capacity problem to individual performance. This creates a "blame the worker" culture that discourages reporting issues.

**Automation Quality**
Bot_Risk performs instant classification, but human correction rates rise when WIP is high. This suggests the automation is being rushed or the humans are not verifying bot output carefully due to pressure. Blaming humans for "errors" when the root cause is automation quality under load is misleading.

**Measurement Error**
Service time is measured by system timestamps. If a worker leaves the screen to think or consult a document, the system records this as "service time." Conversely, if they batch events (Document Review), the system records multiple events as one short duration, artificially inflating efficiency metrics while hiding quality risks.

**Punitive Use of Data**
Using error rates for punishment (Intervention 8) ignores the context of WIP and complexity. It incentivizes workers to reject complex cases or manipulate queue data to avoid high-WIP periods, worsening the overall flow.
