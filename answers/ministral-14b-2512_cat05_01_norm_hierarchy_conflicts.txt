### **1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method**

#### **Assumptions (Explicit Choices)**
1. **Source Authority Hierarchy**:
   - **Board-approved policies (GPIP-4)** are the *default* baseline, but **incident directives (ECD-2)** and **local SOPs (DE-AAC-PROC-17)** can override them *only if explicitly authorized* or where GPIP-4 permits local stricter controls (A5) or emergency waivers (A4/A7).
   - **Email communications (D1–D4)** are *not* normative sources unless they reflect pre-existing authority (e.g., CFO’s interpretive role under Finance controls). They may provide *evidential support* but cannot override controlled documents.
   - **Incident directives (ECD-2)** are treated as *temporary policy overlays* only if they:
     - Are jointly signed by the COO and Incident Commander (implying executive delegation).
     - Explicitly reference a normative exception mechanism (e.g., A7 or A4).
     - Do not silently weaken *non-waivable* controls (e.g., segregation of duties in A1, Compliance clearance in A3).
   - **Retrospective evidence** (e.g., late-recorded verbal authorizations) is admissible *only if* it can be tied to contemporaneous corroboration (e.g., supplier call logs, access logs). ERP timestamps are *not* conclusive if other evidence exists.

2. **Specificity Trumps Generality**:
   - More specific rules (e.g., ECD-2’s EUR 150k threshold in D1) override broader ones (e.g., GPIP-4’s EUR 100k Compliance requirement in A3) *only within their stated scope and time window*.
   - **Emergency necessity** (B4, C1–C4) waives *procedural timing* (e.g., PO before commitment) but does not waive *substantive controls* (e.g., Finance approval, segregation of duties) unless explicitly stated.

3. **Temporal Application**:
   - Rules apply *at the time of the act* (e.g., Commit_to_Supplier). Post-hoc approvals (e.g., Finance_Approve after commitment) do not cure pre-commitment violations unless the policy explicitly permits it (A6).
   - **Emergency directives expire** at their stated end time (C6). Acts after expiry revert to baseline policies unless a new directive is issued.

4. **Burden of Proof**:
   - **Compliance**: The organization must demonstrate adherence to the *most restrictive applicable rule* at the time of each act.
   - **Exceptions**: The party invoking an exception (e.g., Incident Command) bears the burden of proving:
     - The exception was *properly authorized* (e.g., COO/Incident Commander joint signature for ECD-2).
     - The exception was *scoped* to the act (e.g., "critical sterilization-line lots" must be narrowly defined).
     - The exception did not weaken *non-waivable* controls.

5. **Process Purpose**:
   - **Segregation of duties (A1)**: Prevents conflicts of interest in supplier commitment/approval.
   - **Finance approval (A2/B2)**: Ensures budgetary control.
   - **Compliance clearance (A3)**: Mitigates supply-chain risks (especially for new suppliers).
   - **Approved-supplier rule (B3)**: Ensures quality/integrity.
   - **Emergency waivers**: Balance speed with residual controls (e.g., Incident Command authorization).

#### **Facts (Non-Disputable)**
- GPIP-4 reserves formal exceptions to the **Chief Risk Officer (CRO)** (A7), but ECD-2 was not ratified by the CRO.
- **Incident Command members** lack explicit budget-owner authority (A2) or Compliance authority (A3).
- **ECD-2’s scope** ("critical sterilization-line lots") is contested (Operations vs. Internal Audit).
- **ERP outage** (July 14 08:30–13:00) affects timestamp reliability; alternative evidence (call logs, PDFs) is preferred.
- **CFO email (D1–D4)** does not mention Compliance or segregation of duties, implying they remain in force.

### **7. Recommended Policy Clarification**

**Proposed Amendment to GPIP-4 (A7) and DE-AAC-PROC-17 (B7):**

> **A7. Emergency Exceptions**
> 1. The Chief Risk Officer (CRO) or, in their absence, the **jointly signed directive by the COO and Incident Commander** may issue time-limited exceptions to:
>    - Competitive tendering (A4),
>    - PO timing (B1),
>    - Approved-supplier rule (B3),
>    - **Finance approval thresholds** (up to EUR 150k for operational continuity, per CFO guidance).
> 2. **Non-waivable controls** remain in force unless explicitly stated in the directive:
>    - Segregation of duties (A1),
>    - Compliance clearance for new suppliers (A3),
>    - Legal clearance for purchases >EUR 150k (D2).
> 3. **Scope**: Directives must define:
>    - "Critical" assets/lots (aligned with Operations/Internal Audit),
>    - Authorized spend limits,
>    - Required residual controls (e.g., Incident Command authorization).
> 4. **Recording**: Verbal authorizations must be documented within **4 hours** in the ERP or equivalent system. Failure to record does not invalidate the act but creates evidential risk.
> 5. **Expiry**: Directives auto-expire at the stated end time. Acts after expiry revert to baseline policies unless a new directive is issued.

> **B7. Emergency Normalization Safeguard**
> 1. After **72 hours** of an emergency directive, all purchases must revert to baseline controls (GPIP-4) unless extended by the CRO.
> 2. Incident Command may request a **one-time 24-hour extension**, requiring CRO ratification within 12 hours of the original expiry.

**Safeguards Against Emergency Normalization**:
- **Spend Limits**: Cap emergency waivers at EUR 150k without Legal clearance.
- **CRO Ratification**: Require CRO approval for extensions beyond 72 hours.
- **Scope Narrowing**: Define "critical" in terms of **risk to patient safety or production stoppage**, not operational convenience.

---
**Rationale**:
- Clarifies that **ECD-2-like directives** can override *procedural* but not *substantive* controls.
- Explicitly reserves **Compliance** and **SOD** from emergency waivers.
- Adds **automatic expiry** and **CRO oversight** to prevent normalization.
- Aligns with **CFO guidance (D1–D4)** while preserving non-Finance controls.
