# 1. Current-process diagnosis

Keep a fast route for straightforward claims. The largest branch out of auto screening is Fast approval (2,550 transitions), and the median waits from intake to auto screen, auto screen to fast approval, and fast approval to dispatch are just 0.5, 0.4, and 1.8 hours. But 14.1% of fast-approval cases generate a complaint or reopen, compared with 3.3% on the quote–approval–repair route. The fast route's observed cost advantage shrinks from 31% before rework to 9% after complaints, reshipments, and credits. Dispatch-to-complaint takes 70 hours at the median and 180 hours at p90, often following incorrect part selection. A dashboard ending at dispatch or first close would reward some failures as quick successes.

The trade-off changes by segment. Premium accessory claims take a median 8 hours fast versus 46 hours manual, with complaints of 6% versus 3%. Universal review would expose many to the 24-hour credit threshold. Standard accessories have more room for a check if customers can see progress (13 versus 52 hours, with tolerance up to 72 hours). For Premium high-value devices, fast handling buys a 20-hour median versus 96 hours reviewed, but complaints reach 22% versus 5%. For Standard high-value devices the figures are 28 versus 120 hours and 18% versus 6%. A wrong high-value replacement costs an expected EUR 410 versus EUR 95 for an accessory. These are observed route comparisons. Defect severity and the reasons cases were selected for review could explain part of each gap.

Two other failures call for different interventions.

- **Evidence collection consumes customer time and reviewer capacity.** Among claims entering Request missing evidence, 26% enter it at least twice. The DFG records 890 returns from that activity to manual review and 320 transitions to abandonment. The return takes 26 hours at the median and 88 at p90. Unclear-defect or missing-evidence cases have a 140-hour median. On the repair route, manual-review-to-quote reaches 60 hours at p90, with Premium cases particularly affected, and quote-to-customer-approval reaches 80 hours at p90. Speeding up the technician alone would leave much of that wait intact.
- **The discovered process permits routes policy excludes.** Token replay gives 0.91 fitness, yet only 68% of traces fit fully. Alignments flag approval before evidence completion, high-value dispatch without review, and complaints after close. Low precision means the discovered model also allows unsupported combinations of approval, risk review, and repair. Reconstruct ordered case histories to locate the bypass and check how a reopen was resolved. An after-close complaint needs handling, and its occurrence alone does not establish an R3 violation. DFG edges count transitions, including repeats. The listed END edges total 3,625 against 5,000 starts, so reconcile observation windows and case status before estimating completed-path volumes or end-to-end times.

All three designs enforce the same minimum controls. Above EUR 400, require a recorded manual eligibility or fraud/risk review before fast approval (R1). Require confirmed evidence before dispatch unless the claim qualifies for the recorded Premium low-risk accessory exception (R2). Each reopen creates a fresh manual-review obligation before final close or denial, including board dispositions (R3). Preserve the expected evidence and decision sequence and send high-value risk flags to the required review. Premium priority changes queue order, never the scope of those controls.

# 2. Redesign A: Route eligibility decisions by segment and risk

**Flow.** At intake, the EUR 0.20 auto screen checks claim value, tier, item class, evidence status, and defect/risk flags. Complete, unambiguous low-risk accessories with a policy-permitted fast decision go straight to fulfillment. Apply the Premium evidence exception only when eligible and record its use. Send missing items to evidence completion and resume the screen on receipt. Route value or eligibility exceptions to a manual reviewer and fraud indicators to a risk specialist. For mid-risk devices, use defect type and confidence to select the lane, with uncertain classifications going to review. The reviewer records the technical disposition or sends the case for diagnosis and quote. Prioritize Premium cases within the appropriate lane.

**Target.** Preserve short accessory lead times while intercepting unsafe fast approvals, especially high-value claims. Standard accessory customers get a visible status and expected completion time when held.

**Mechanism.** The screen acts before an irreversible dispatch. One qualifying review can satisfy R1, so ordinary value checks need not consume one of the two fraud specialists. Version the routing rules and record the facts and decision at each branch so that conformance can be checked on the actual trace.

**Trade-off.** The rule update can deploy in two weeks but may increase manual inflow by 15–35%. Reviewed cases may wait longer. A correctly routed and approved claim can still ship with the wrong part, and a customer can still receive repeated evidence requests.

# 3. Redesign B: Verify the remedy before committing stock or repair

**Flow.** After eligibility and evidence checks, a technician uses the serial number, model revision, and defect to determine whether a compatible replacement is known or repair is appropriate. For high-value or ambiguous replacements, reserve the matched part and have an inspector verify the selected SKU against the device before dispatch. When the match is uncertain, hold shipment for diagnosis. For a repairable defect, obtain part price and availability in parallel with diagnosis, issue a priced quote, obtain customer approval, repair, and test the reported fault before close. Send a failed test back with its failure reason. Confirmed low-risk accessory matches keep their existing fulfillment route.

**Target.** High-value replacements, ambiguous mid-risk part selections, and repairable devices. Give Premium cases an expedited quote and a dated update when price confirmation is outstanding.

**Mechanism.** Catch the selection error while the part is still in stock. At comparable check cost, preventing a wrong shipment is worth more on a EUR 410 error than on a EUR 95 one. Assess the gate using the measured reduction in wrong shipments times the relevant error cost, weighed against inspection, delay, and credit costs. Only a subset of complaints are wrong shipments. Earlier price confirmation addresses a known quote delay, and defect-specific testing addresses the 12.5% rework rate on repaired high-value devices versus 4.0% on repaired low-risk devices.

**Trade-off.** A pre-dispatch gate adds 6–18 hours and cannot eliminate failures arising in use. Repair also requires customer consent and can tie up a device for days. Applying the full gate to an 8-hour Premium accessory route would risk consuming its service margin for little avoided loss.

# 4. Redesign C: Give incomplete claims one evidence plan and one owner

**Flow.** At the first sign of missing evidence or an unclear defect, assign an owner who checks existing records, consults a technician if the defect is ambiguous, and sends one consolidated, claim-specific request. Let the customer upload documents or complete the same checklist with a person by phone or callback. Show accepted items, outstanding items, the next update date, and who is waiting on whom. Validate each submission against the checklist, retain accepted items, and resume at the outstanding eligibility or technical decision. A new discrepancy goes to the owner for a targeted follow-up. Reopened claims retain the history and receive the required fresh manual review.

**Target.** The 22% unclear-defect or missing-evidence group, including customers who struggle with digital submission. Give Premium cases expedited contact and Standard customers visible progress.

**Mechanism.** Consolidation reduces repeat requests, queue re-entry, and customers sending the same evidence twice. The portal's estimated 30–45% reduction in repeated requests is a hypothesis. With comparable entrants and a relative reduction of that size, the current 26% repeat rate would fall to roughly 14–18%. Track all claims flagged at intake as well as those who actually submit, so customers who disengage remain visible.

**Trade-off.** Staff spend time on the first contact and assisted submissions. Better retention may leave more claims to adjudicate and fulfill. Clear claims gain little directly, and evidence completeness alone cannot verify the replacement SKU.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

- **A.** Stale values or defect codes could fast-track ineligible claims. Default missing classifications to review and audit exceptions. Over-sensitive rules could instead swamp manual review. Seven reviewers supply 252 productive hours weekly, with queues growing sharply above about 207 loaded hours (82%). Measure touch hours and peak demand before ramping. A spillover to fraud review on Mondays or after campaigns could delay genuinely suspicious claims. Reserve specialist capacity for cases that need it.
- **B.** Incorrect compatibility data could make the selector and inspector endorse the same wrong SKU. Verify against a device-specific source and audit delivered parts. Supplier prices may expire, forcing requotes and renewed customer consent. Track price freshness and quote acceptance. Work can migrate from reshipments to the two inspectors during repair campaigns or to the three quoting technicians awaiting supplier responses. Monitor both queues and cap pilot volume at available capacity.
- **C.** A portal-only rollout could reduce recorded repeats by driving customers away. Offer assisted intake from the first request and measure channel-specific abandonment. An overbroad checklist could create new work even for simple defects. Audit requested items for necessity. The queue can migrate from repeat manual reviews to owner callbacks or to technical decisions as more customers finish. Track callback age and downstream workload before expanding.

# 7. Recommended experiment sequence

1. **Establish a comparable, compliant baseline.** Instrument evidence confirmation and exception reason, amount, risk flags, each review and reopen, part selected and shipped, queue entry, work start, and customer response. Link reshipments, credits, and complaints to the original claim. Reconcile post-close events and unresolved cases. Apply mandatory controls to every arm immediately. Use the historical process for context and a concurrent policy-compliant workflow to estimate each redesign's incremental effect.
2. **Pilot A first on a capped intake cohort.** Shadow the new rules across segments during the two-week update and estimate added reviewer hours from measured touch times. Then pilot clear accessory cases across both tiers and a bounded cohort near the EUR 400 boundary, including cases requiring review. Compare within tier, value band, risk, and defect type, allocating eligible cases at intake. Proceed if audited routing satisfies every required guard, Premium accessory completion within 24 hours holds or improves, and reopen rates hold or fall without a growing review tail. Pause expansion if manual load is sustained near or above 82%, p90 wait grows, or on-time service deteriorates. Contain any confirmed policy bypass immediately.
3. **Test B on high-value claims and C on incomplete claims.** Once review queues stabilize, compare B with compliant usual fulfillment for comparable device/defect classes. Expand if verified wrong shipments, repeat repairs, or reshipment costs fall enough to justify the gate and Premium completion and quote tails remain within the agreed service tolerance. Test C with assisted access available, comparing cohorts at the first evidence flag. Seek a repeat-rate reduction toward 14–18% among comparable evidence entrants, shorter p90 time to resolution, and stable or lower abandonment in each service channel. Stop or revise if fewer repeats accompany rising abandonment. For high-value incomplete claims, assign one intervention at a time during these tests. Test the combination separately after estimating their individual effects.
4. **Decide on mature case outcomes.** For each arm report intake-to-first-decision and intake-to-durable-resolution p50/p90, Premium accessory 24-hour misses and credits, Standard accessory 72-hour completion and status visibility, and satisfaction by tier. Track R1–R3 violations per applicable case, repeated requests, abandonment, denials, wrong parts, reopens, rework, and total cost per intake including labor, parts, repeat shipping, and credits. Follow linked cases through a common post-close window, initially 30 days, and check for later events before rollout. Keep open cases visible and report abandonment separately from successful resolution. Compare treatment and control over the same campaign periods, monitor untreated queues sharing staff, and extend the pilot when uncertainty covers a material service loss. Scale by segment only where the durable outcome improves at an acceptable cost and the receiving queues can absorb the work.
