# 1. Fairness dimensions

Procedural fairness cannot be reduced to approval-rate parity. The following dimensions must be assessed jointly, distinguishing raw gaps from gaps that remain after conditioning on legitimate complexity:

**Outcome parity (unadjusted).** Raw gaps are large: approval 78% (A) vs 63% (B), 69% (C), 58% (D); median cycle time 8.1d (A) vs 13.7d (B), 15.9d (C), 12.1d (D); 90th percentile spreads even wider; appeal 6% (A) vs 13-16% (B,D); rework 11% (A) vs 20-24% others. This signals *disparity* but not discrimination. Groups differ sharply in measured case mix: high-risk share 18% (A) vs 51% (D), incomplete documentation 21% (A) vs 46% (B), language need 57% (B) vs 2% (A), accommodation 62% (C) vs 4% (A), prior-case high 49% (D) vs 12% (A). No causal or fairness conclusion can be drawn without conditioning.

**Adjusted outcome disparity.** After controlling for risk_score_band, documentation_completeness, channel, household_size_band, prior_case_count, region, month, gaps shrink but do not disappear: approval -5.5pp (B), -2.2pp (C), -3.1pp (D) vs A; cycle time +2.6d (B), +4.1d (C), +0.9d (D); Prob(Supervisor Review) +8.0pp (B), +5.5pp (C); Prob(Request Missing Docs) +9.5pp (B); Prob(Appeal) +4.4pp (B), +5.7pp (D). Residual gaps are *concerning* but their interpretation depends on whether controls are legitimate and unbiased - see Limitation 5.

**Procedural burden.** Number and type of steps, handovers, and rework loops. V1 straight-through 42% (A) vs 18-22% (B,C,D); V2 with Request Docs 34% (B) vs 24% (A); V4 language route 24% (B) vs 1% (A); V5 accommodation route 31% (C) vs 2% (A); V3 fraud route 28% (D) vs 9% (A); rework rate 22-24% (B,C) vs 11% (A). Even after controls, B and C face more steps and loops. Burden matters even if outcome is eventually approved.

**Timeliness / waiting-time decomposition.** Median cycle and waits show rights-preserving steps are bottlenecks: Language Support  Eligibility 3.8d (B only), Accommodation Review  Eligibility 4.7d (C only), Completeness  Request Missing Docs 1.6d (B) vs 0.4d (A), Supervisor Review  Decision Draft 1.9d (B) vs 1.0d (A). Policy states support steps are rights-preserving but no separate service-level target exists, so queuing cost is externalized to applicants using those rights.

**Consistency of routing (conditional on same triggers).** Legitimate routing expects: high risk  Fraud Review, language need  Language Support, accommodation request  Accommodation Review. Inconsistency would be: same risk_score_band but different Prob(Fraud/Supervisor) by group, or same documentation completeness but different Prob(Request Missing Docs). Adjusted +8.0pp Supervisor Review for B and +9.5pp Request Missing Docs for B after controls suggest inconsistency beyond measured risk/completeness. For D, by contrast, adjusted Supervisor +2.0pp suggests routing largely consistent with higher risk.

**Quality / accessibility of support.** Not just presence of Language Support / Accommodation Review, but its resourcing and experience. Only 7 bilingual caseworkers serve 57% language-need in B; 5 accommodation specialists (2 double as supervisors) serve 62% accommodation need in C; concentration in few overloaded workers vs 52 caseworkers for A predicts lower quality, less explanation, more errors.

**Appeal / rework burden.** Appeal rate adjusted +4.4pp (B) and +5.7pp (D) after controls; V6 appeal path 13% (D), 8% (B) vs 4% (A). High appeal with longer 30.7d median signals decision quality, explainability, or trust issues, not just stricter decisions. Elevated rework also imposes dignity/time costs not captured in approval.

**Resource-allocation fairness.** Who does work and with what load: Gini-like concentration for B and C, regional overload (2 regions backlog), handovers general  bilingual  general (B) and general  specialist  supervisor (C). Even if workload assignment is unintentional, it creates inequitable queues and handover errors.

What can be inferred: where adjusted gaps persist after defensible controls, there is evidence of *inequitable procedure* warranting investigation. What cannot be inferred: intent, unlawful discrimination, or that any single attribute (risk score) is causal - especially given measurement bias concerns.

# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy

Goal: separate four explanations: (a) legitimate complexity/routing, (b) unlawful/disparate treatment, (c) structural under-resourcing of support steps, (d) uncertainty / confounding.

**Matching / stratification variables:**
*Primary defensible set:* household_size_band, region, month, channel (with caveat channel is partly choice/ access-constrained), prior_case_count (lagged). These are plausibly pre-determined and less prone to bias.
*Potentially biased covariates - use for sensitivity, not base conclusion:* risk_score_band (may encode historic disparities) and documentation_completeness (may be outcome of language accessibility). Base model should be estimated with and without them.
*Approach:* Coarsened exact matching or propensity-score stratification within region × channel × risk_band cells; exact match on language_preference and disability_accommodation_requested when comparing *within* need-group. Estimate adjusted differences via doubly-robust regression (outcome model + weighting) to reduce model-dependence. Report both unadjusted, fully-adjusted, and partially-adjusted (excluding risk/doc completeness).

**Path / variant comparison:**
Compare conditional path probabilities rather than population shares. E.g., P(V4 | needs language support=1) across B vs A speakers needing support; P(V5 | accommodation requested=1) across C vs others; P(Request Missing Docs | documentation_completeness=incomplete vs complete) to test communication failure; P(Supervisor Review | risk_band strata). Use `pm4py.get_variants` and `filter_variants` to isolate canonical paths (V1-V6) and `discover_log_skeleton` / DECLARE to test if constraints (e.g., Response[Accommodation Request, Accommodation Review]) are applied consistently.

**Resource-assignment comparison:**
Measure caseworker concentration (Gini / Herfindahl), caseload per active day, handovers per case, and `discover_handover_of_work_network`, `discover_working_together_network`, `discover_organizational_roles` and `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` to contrast networks for B/C vs A. Test if same region and same shift, B/C cases wait longer at same activity.

**Waiting-time decomposition:**
Use `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` to split cycle time into service time vs waiting time per transition. Compare `get_service_time` vs waiting at: SubmitVerify, CompletenessRequest Docs, LanguageEligibility, AccommodationEligibility, SupervisorDecision. If service time similar but wait longer, bottleneck is queuing/resource, not intrinsic complexity. Use `get_all_case_durations` and `filter_case_performance` / `filter_paths_performance` to test quantile differences (median and 90th percentile), not just means.

**Sensitivity analysis for possibly biased covariates:**
1. Omit risk_score_band from adjustment: does B penalty shrink/grow? If -5.5pp with risk, -8pp without, suggests risk masks part of disparity (or vice versa).
2. Omit documentation_completeness: if Request Missing Docs disparity falls when conditioning on completeness, it suggests mediation via accessibility.
3. Negative control: test outcome that should not be affected by group (e.g., time to automated Identity Verification) - if disparity persists there, suggests systemic bias.
4. Rosenbaum bounds / E-value for unmeasured confounding; alternative risk proxy (external validation) if available.
5. Auditing risk_score_band calibration by group: does same score predict same future fraud finding?

**Subgroup checks by region and channel:**
Stratify by region (especially the two backlog regions) and channel (online vs in-person). In-person applications wait longer at SubmitVerify; if B's in-person share is 38% vs 9% (A), compare B-in-person vs A-in-person within region. Similarly language-need within online only. If disparities disappear outside the 2 overloaded regions or 7 bilingual workers, mechanism is localized resourcing, not universal bias. Also test intersection: B + in-person + language need vs B + online + language need.

All contrasts should report uncertainty (CI, n) given smaller C (n=820) and D (n=980) vs A (3600).

# 3. Path and resource disparities

Most important procedural disparities, distinguishing justified vs concerning residual:

**V1 straight-through deficit:** 42% A vs 19% B, 22% C, 18% D. Raw 20+pp gap partly justified: B has 46% incomplete doc vs 21% A, D has 51% high risk vs 18% A, C has 62% accommodation need requiring extra step. However after adjustment cycle time still +2.6d (B) and +4.1d (C) and Prob(Request Docs) +9.5pp (B), so not fully explained. Concerning: even complete-doc, low-risk B cases less likely to stay on fast path.

**V4 Language Support Contact route:** 24% B vs 1% A. Expected given 57% language need vs 2% (legitimate rights-preserving routing). Not itself unfair, but its implementation is: LanguageEligibility wait 3.8d median (vs no wait for A), CompletenessRequest Docs 1.6d vs 0.4d (language-support cases wait for bilingual staff), and +9.5pp adjusted Request Missing Docs for B. The extra steps are legitimate; the 3-4 day queue and communication loop are concerning and represent burden transferred to rights-holders. Handover chain generalbilingualgeneral in two regions compounds delay/error.

**V5 Accommodation route:** 31% C vs 2% A, justified by 62% vs 4% accommodation requests. Yet AccommodationEligibility 4.7d median, longest of any support transition, and SupervisorDecision 1.6d vs 1.0d for A, plus Supervisor Review +5.5pp adjusted. Specialized team queue (5 staff, 2 double-hatting as supervisors) is a capacity bottleneck, not case complexity. Median cycle 21.6d for this variant vs 5.8d for V1. Policy treats accommodation as mandatory but has no timeliness SLA - structural inequity.

**V3 Risk/Fraud route:** 28% D vs 9% A vs 11% B, 8% C. Largely justified by risk composition (51% high-risk D). Adjusted Supervisor Review only +2.0pp for D (small), and FraudEligibility wait difference modest (2.8d vs 2.2d). Suggests risk-based routing operates relatively consistently. Concern remains elsewhere: V6 appeal path 13% D vs 4% A and adjusted appeal +5.7pp for D despite similar post-risk timeliness — points to decision quality/explanation or cumulative burden of fraud scrutiny, not queue time. Also risk_score_band itself may be biased (see §5).

**V2 Request Missing Docs:** 34% B vs 24% A, 29% C, 26% D. After adjustment B still +9.5pp vs A, C +4.2pp. Incomplete share explains part (46% B vs 21% A) but not the adjusted excess. Indicates communication/accessibility failure (forms, instructions, language-concordant notification) rather than applicant behavior alone.

**Resource concentration:** 7 bilingual workers handle most B cases and 3 overloaded regional officers; 52 workers for A. This concentration predicts the SupervisorDecision 1.9d (B) vs 1.0d (A) backlog in two regions and higher rework 22% B vs 11% A. Even if assignment is meant to match skill to need, load imbalance creates inequitable waits and more handovers (error-prone, dignity-reducing).

**Bottom line on justified vs concerning:** V3 excess for D is most explainable by legitimate risk routing (small residual). V4/V5 excess routing is legitimate in *whether* the step occurs, but the associated waits (3.8d, 4.7d) and extra Request-Docs loop for B are not justified by complexity — they reflect under-capacity and accessibility gaps. The adjusted Supervisor Review elevation for B (+8.0pp) and C (+5.5pp) and appeal elevations for B and D are the strongest signals of inconsistency after controls.

# 4. Plausible mechanisms

**1. Legitimate risk-based scrutiny (justified).** D's higher prior-case count and risk_score_band triggers Fraud Review and Supervisor Review per policy. Explains V3 28% share, longer RiskFraud wait, and lower raw approval. If risk scores were unbiased and threshold applied evenly, longer, more intensive path for D would be procedurally fair, even if outcome disparity persists. Evidence supporting: small adjusted Supervisor gap (+2pp) for D suggests most routing disparity tracks measured risk.

**2. Legitimately required but under-resourced rights-preserving support (legitimate intent  concerning outcome).** Language Support Contact and Accommodation Review are mandated when need is flagged. With only 7 bilingual and 5 accommodation specialists, queues form (3.8d, 4.7d). Mechanism: insufficient capacity + lack of SLA for support steps translates a right into waiting-time penalty. Groups exercising rights (B, C) bear extra procedural burden, extra handovers, and higher rework, even though the steps themselves protect substantive fairness.

**3. Communication and administrative burden amplified by language / accessibility mismatch (concerning).** Forms, checklists, and Request Missing Documents notices not fully translated or plain-language adapted; documentation_completeness coded as applicant deficit rather than system barrier. Generates higher adjusted Prob(Request Missing Docs) for B (+9.5pp) and rework (22% B vs 11% A), with CompletenessRequest wait 1.6d vs 0.4d while awaiting bilingual staff to clarify. May also affect documentation_completeness measurement itself.

**4. Resource concentration, handover friction, and regional overload (concerning, structural).** Assignment logic clusters B cases to few bilingual workers and 3 overloaded regional officers, and C cases to specialists who also supervise. Creates sequential handovers (generalbilingualgeneral; generalspecialistsupervisor), each adding wait, coordination cost, and information loss. Explains SupervisorDecision delay for B (1.9d) and C (1.6d) concentrated in two regions, and higher rework/appeal due to fragmented ownership.

**5. Historical bias embedded in risk_score_band and prior_case_count feedback loop (concerning, potentially circular).** If risk model trained on historical approvals/ fraud flags that themselves reflected prior disparities, then high risk share for D (51%) and B (29%) partly reproduces past bias. Over-routing to Fraud/Supervisor Review increases scrutiny, increases chance of minor inconsistencies being flagged, which then raises future risk scores. Adjusted models that control for risk_score_band will understate true disparity; models that omit it may overstate. Either way, neutrality cannot be assumed.

**6. Discretionary extra scrutiny / implicit bias in Supervisor Review triggering (concerning, discretionary).** Even after conditioning on risk, doc completeness, channel etc., B still +8.0pp and C +5.5pp more likely to get Supervisor Review. Threshold for escalation may be lower for minority-language or accommodated cases (e.g., uncertainty, unfamiliar documentation interpreted as risk). This creates both delay and a second, potentially more stringent decision layer, feeding higher appeal rates.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

**Measurement bias in risk_score_band and documentation_completeness.** Risk_score_band is observed, not ground truth; if model uses zip code, prior case count, or language-correlated features, it imports historic inequity. Documentation_completeness is assessed by caseworkers after communication attempts; incomplete may reflect language barrier, unclear instructions, or staff availability rather than applicant effort. Conditioning on these as if neutral may mask discrimination (over-adjustment).

**Missing applicant needs and barriers.** No measures of literacy, digital access, transportation, childcare, interpreter quality, or trust. Channel (online vs in-person) is an imperfect proxy — B's 38% in-person share may reflect need for help, not preference. Without these, we cannot tell if longer waits cause harm differentially (e.g., hourly workers unable to respond quickly to Request Docs).

**Unobserved resource workload.** Workload observed only at daily granularity. Cannot see intra-day queues, concurrent cases per worker, bilingual worker absences, or time-on-task vs wait. Handover counts derived from `org:resource` may miss informal consultations. Performance DFG waiting times therefore conflate queuing, batching, and actual service slowdown.

**Appeal and rework quality not captured.** `appeal_flag` and reopen/rework rates record occurrence, not grounds, outcome, explanation quality, or whether appeal succeeded. `case_outcome` (approved/denied) does not capture partial approvals, benefit amount, or dignity of treatment. High appeal for D (+5.7pp adjusted) could reflect either meritorious correction or frustration with opaque decisions — log cannot distinguish.

**Historical bias and sample composition.** Prior_case_count high for D (49%) may reflect earlier barriers to stable eligibility rather than risk. Region confounds: two regions with backlog coincide with B clustering; without staff roster history, cannot separate place bias from group bias. Temporal confounding by month limited if policy changed mid-period.

**Construct validity of procedural steps.** Log records activity labels but not whether Language Support Contact was interpretation vs translated form, or whether Accommodation Review was substantive vs checkbox. Service-level targets missing for support steps means no benchmark for timeliness fairness.

Result: inference about discrimination vs legitimate routing is partially identified. We can flag adjusted disparities and bottlenecks, but cannot quantify causal discrimination without auditing risk score generation, documentation assessment process, and collecting applicant-experience data.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects

Do not remove Language Support Contact or Accommodation Review to save time — they are rights-preserving. Mitigations should reduce burden while preserving the steps.

**Adding capacity for language support and accommodation review.** Hire / train more bilingual caseworkers (beyond 7) and accommodation specialists (beyond 5), separate accommodation specialist from supervisor role, create float pool for peak demand.
*Trade-offs:* Cost, recruitment lag (6-12 months), training needed to maintain quality; diluting specialist expertise if generalists do accommodation reviews poorly; may need to re-validate language proficiency.

**Service-level targets (SLAs) for support steps, with monitoring.** Introduce timeliness thresholds for Language Support  Eligibility and Accommodation  Eligibility (e.g., 1 business day) and Completeness  Request Docs, tracked separately and included in regional performance dashboards.
*Trade-offs:* Can induce rush or gaming (premature closure, superficial reviews); may divert capacity from other queues unless paired with staffing increase; requires precise timestamp and workload data to avoid perverse incentives.

**Routing redesign to reduce handovers.** Implement single-owner / case-holding model: bilingual caseworker retains ownership end-to-end for language-need cases; accommodation specialist co-works without full handover; use paired consultation rather than transfer; reduce generalbilingualgeneral loops in the two overloaded regions.
*Trade-offs:* Needs cross-training (bilingual workers need eligibility expertise, generalists need cultural competence); may reduce benefits of specialization; if caseload not rebalanced, single-owner bilingual workers may burn out; requires reconfiguration of DFG and role definitions.

**Decision-explanation improvements to reduce appeal/rework.** Provide translated templates, plain-language denial reasons, checklist of missing docs in preferred language, and mandatory explanation quality review before notification; offer pre-decision clarification call for incomplete cases.
*Trade-offs:* Increases Decision Draft  Notification service time modestly (currently small 0.5-0.8d gap) and drafting workload; may temporarily lengthen cycle time but expected to reduce V2 loops and V6 appeals (30.7d) net-positive; requires quality audit and language QA.

**Monitoring adjusted disparities, not just raw rates.** Dashboard of adjusted approval, cycle time, Supervisor Review, Request Docs, appeal by group using stratified or doubly-robust estimates, updated monthly by region/channel; alert if adjusted Supervisor Review >3pp or Request Docs >5pp or Language/Accommodation wait >2d vs baseline.
*Trade-offs:* Risk of metric fixation (Goodhart); staff may avoid flagging Supervisor Review to meet target; needs statistical governance and transparency about uncertainty; should supplement with qualitative applicant feedback.

**Auditing risk_score_band and documentation_completeness for bias.** Validate risk model calibration and error rates by group, retrain without proxies for language/region if needed; audit manual documentation completeness coding via bilingual re-review of sampled cases; consider blinding group in risk scoring where possible; document feature provenance.
*Trade-offs:* Audit may reveal model must be rebuilt, causing temporary routing uncertainty and need for manual overrides; dropping biased features may reduce predictive accuracy for true fraud, requiring compensating controls; prior_case_count adjustment may affect D's routing and needs policy debate; audit is resource-intensive and requires data outside log.

Additional supportive actions: publish waiting-time breakdowns, collect applicant-reported burden/dignity measures, and pilot in the two backlog regions first.

# 8. Bottom-line assessment

The process is **not procedurally fair as currently operated, but the evidence does not support a finding of intentional discrimination - the pattern is best described as structural inequity with indeterminate causal attribution.**

*What is supported:* (1) Much of the raw outcome gap (approval 15-20pp, median 5-8 days) tracks legitimate case-mix differences - higher incomplete documentation for B, higher risk/prior cases for D, and higher need for mandated support steps for B and C. Fraud routing for D appears largely consistent with measured risk. (2) **Material procedural burden remains after adjustment** and is concentrated where rights-preserving steps are required: B faces +9.5pp adjusted excess Request Missing Documents, +8.0pp excess Supervisor Review, +2.6 days cycle time and 3.8-day LanguageEligibility queue; C faces +4.1 days cycle time and 4.7-day AccommodationEligibility queue with rework 24% vs 11% for A, driven by severe capacity concentration (7 bilingual, 5 accommodation staff, 2 double-hatted) and extra handovers in two regions. These waits are not explained by complexity and effectively penalize exercising a right. (3) D shows a distinct appeal burden (+5.7pp adjusted, V6 13% vs 4%) not explained by waits, suggesting decision quality/explainability issues under intensive scrutiny. Raw approval gaps alone would overstate unfairness; adjusted gaps with potentially biased controls may understate it.

*What is not supported / indeterminate:* We cannot conclude that higher raw denial rates for B/C/D equal discrimination, nor that B/C delays are justified solely because those groups have more complex cases. We also cannot treat risk_score_band or documentation_completeness as neutral arbiters - both likely encode systemic barriers - so adjusted estimates represent a *lower bound* on disparity if those covariates are biased. Intent or individual caseworker bias cannot be inferred from aggregated log data, daily workload granularity, and absence of explanation-quality measures.

*Calibrated judgment:* **Indeterminate to moderately unfair on procedural dimensions.** The system legitimately routes more B cases through language support and C cases through accommodation review, and more D cases through fraud review, but it fails to resource those routes equitably, creating predictable, group-correlated delays, handovers, and rework. The residual excess Supervisor Review and Request-Docs for B after controls, and the sustained waits for support steps with no SLA, warrant prompt mitigation and audit before any stronger conclusion about discrimination can be drawn. Priority is capacity, handover redesign, and risk/doc-completeness auditing, with ongoing adjusted monitoring by region and channel.
