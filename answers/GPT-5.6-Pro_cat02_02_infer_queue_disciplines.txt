# 2. Reconstructed dispatch decisions

## Interpretation conventions

1. A recorded arrival at time (t) may correspond to physical arrival between (t-2) and (t). The recorded scan is nevertheless the latest plausible time at which the dispatcher became aware of the job. C05 at 10:10 is the main boundary case.
2. Standard SPT/SEPT is interpreted as non-preemptive. It chooses the shortest estimated job when a resource becomes available. A shortest-remaining-processing-time rule would be a different, preemptive policy.
3. For paused B01, “nominal remaining” is calculated as `est_min` minus recorded active service. The actual estimate visible to the dispatcher at resumption is not logged.
4. A compound skill requirement is treated as requiring all listed skills. Consequently, only D-F is fully qualified for D05, and only D-X is fully qualified for D07.
5. “Appointment” predictions distinguish a hard exact-slot or protected-window policy from a soft-window policy. The data do not provide configured early or late tolerances.
6. Skill-based routing by itself determines eligibility or resource fit; it does not necessarily rank two jobs that are both feasible on the same resource.

The likely QB logic is not unrestricted preemptive priority. A more defensible reconstruction is:

* selected urgent jobs, probably some priority-1 jobs, may preempt low-priority work;
* once a job has started or been committed, it receives substantial protection;
* paused B01 receives resume preference;
* ordinary unstarted jobs are largely FIFO;
* the dispatcher can manually lock or reassign the next job.

The unexplained part is the 09:48 choice. B05 had the highest priority, yet B03 was started. A plausible explanation is that B03 had been committed before B05 arrived, but that commitment is not in the event data.

The first four initial-backlog jobs are ranked by estimate as:

[
C02(8) < C03(14) < C04(22) < C01(36).
]

Their observed relative order is exactly that order, even though later arrivals are interposed. That is stronger evidence for a precomputed SPT sequence than for fully dynamic SPT.

The likely reconstruction is therefore:

1. A shortlist or sequence is generated using SEPT/SPT.
2. New arrivals do not necessarily displace already committed jobs.
3. C05 receives a priority/deadline override at 10:54.
4. C01 later receives either an age-based override or its previously committed place in the initial sequence.

A continuous deadline-risk policy is not supported. At 11:40, C07 has only 20 minutes until its deadline and an 18-minute estimate, yet 36-minute C01 is selected first. If estimates are trusted, this selection makes C07’s deadline unattainable.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

## QA

**Supporting cases**

A03 is the strongest case in the entire QA log. At 08:20:

* A02 is older;
* A04 has higher priority;
* A04 is shorter;
* A03 alone has the appointment due at that time.

Thus appointment scheduling is the only listed simple mechanism that independently predicts A03.

A02, A05, and A07 support FIFO as the fallback:

* A02 is selected at 08:32 despite A04 being both higher priority and shorter.
* A05 is selected at 09:05 despite A08 being higher priority and A07 being shorter.
* A07 is selected at 09:32 despite A08 being higher priority.

A06 is consistent with appointment service because it starts five minutes after its scheduled time and ahead of A07 and A08. It is not decisive, however, because A06 is also the oldest waiting job.

**Contradicting or qualifying cases**

A05 challenges a hard exact-time appointment policy. Starting A05 at 09:05 makes A06’s 09:15 start impossible. A07 could instead have been completed by approximately 09:14, allowing A06 to start on time.

This apparent violation can be explained without an undocumented exception if appointments have a soft window extending at least five minutes late, or if the dispatcher does not perform appointment look-ahead. Under a strict exact-slot interpretation, it is an exception.

The 08:52–08:58 idle interval is not a policy violation because A1 is in calibration.

## QB

**Supporting cases**

B02 provides direct evidence of preemptive priority. B01 is actively interrupted, B02 is priority 1, and the recorded reason is “urgent arrival.”

B05 provides weaker priority evidence. At 10:00 it is selected before older B04 and paused B01. It is also not the shortest job, because B04 has an eight-minute estimate.

B01’s resumptions at 09:31 and 10:14 support a continuity or resume-first component. A policy may rationally protect resumed work when interruption and cognitive reload costs are material, even if those costs are not represented as active service minutes.

B04 followed by B06 and B07 is broadly FIFO among the ordinary unstarted jobs remaining after B01 finishes.

**Contradicting cases**

At 09:48, B05 is already a recorded priority-1 arrival, yet B03 is selected. Neither strict priority nor SPT predicts B03:

* priority predicts B05;
* SPT predicts B04;
* FIFO among unstarted jobs predicts B03.

The explicit “dispatcher reassignment” reason establishes that an override occurred, but it does not explain why B03 rather than B05 was chosen. A prior commitment of B03 is plausible but undocumented.

At 09:31, B01 resumes ahead of higher-priority and shorter B03. At 10:14, it resumes ahead of higher-priority B06 and shorter B04 and B07. These are genuine violations of strict priority and dynamic SPT, but they are consistent with a documented or undocumented resume preference.

B07’s 10:30 appointment is not honored: B04 is started at 10:28, and B06 is selected at 10:36 while B07 is already late. A broad appointment window could technically accommodate this, but the note itself states that the appointment was not honored on time. Appointment scheduling is therefore not a credible primary QB rule.

## QC

**Supporting cases**

C02, C03, and C04 strongly support SEPT/SPT. Their estimates are 8, 14, and 22 minutes, and they are selected in exactly that order despite conflicting arrival order, priorities, and deadlines.

C06 is also strong SPT evidence. At 11:34 it is selected with an estimate of six minutes, ahead of:

* older C01, estimated at 36 minutes;
* higher-priority and earlier-deadline C07, estimated at 18 minutes.

C01’s eventual position is also consistent with the initial backlog having been sorted by estimate: it is the longest initial job and is executed after the three shorter initial jobs.

**Contradicting cases**

C04 over C06 at 10:32 violates dynamic SPT. C06 had been recorded for seven minutes and had an estimate of only six minutes. The most plausible explanation is that C04 was assigned or locked before C06 arrived. Without an assignment timestamp, this remains an undocumented exception.

C05 over C06 at 10:54 violates SPT but is readily explained by an override. C05 is both priority 1 and already beyond its listed 10:50 deadline. The data cannot identify whether the trigger was:

* priority 1;
* deadline crossing;
* predicted SLA breach;
* manual escalation.

C01 over C07 at 11:40 contradicts SPT, priority, and deadline ordering simultaneously. It supports either:

* anti-starvation or aging;
* resumption of the initial precomputed sequence;
* a prior commitment to C01.

Unlike C05, there is no explicit field explaining this exception.

The timing of C05 also weakens a simple “intervene when SLA becomes endangered” interpretation. By 10:18, its 40-minute estimate already exceeded the 32 minutes remaining to the listed deadline. Nevertheless, C03 and C04 were selected before it. The effective trigger may have been actual deadline crossing rather than prospective risk, or those jobs may already have been committed.

## QD

**Supporting cases**

D05 is the clearest skill constraint. It requires firmware and electrical skills, and D-F is explicitly the only fully qualified resource. Its delay until D-F returns from the meeting is therefore not evidence of neglect by D-M or D-X.

D07 likewise requires mechanical and electrical skills, which only D-X possesses. Selecting D07 on D-X is necessary under the stated skill matrix.

D06 supports appointment reservation. D-X becomes available at 11:20 but does not start another job; D06 begins exactly at its 11:30 appointment. The idle interval is more informative than the selection itself, because D06 was also old enough to win a FIFO comparison at 11:30.

D04 and D07 support FIFO or prior assignment:

* D04 is selected before shorter, priority-1 D08 on D-M.
* D07 is selected before shorter, priority-1 D10 on D-X.

**Contradicting or qualifying cases**

At 11:36, D-F could apparently process D10 in 12 minutes and finish before its 12:00 meeting. It instead remains idle. The meeting explains why a 30-minute non-preemptive D05 cannot safely start, but not why D10 is not opportunistically reassigned.

This requires at least one undocumented condition:

* D10 had already been locked to D-X;
* cross-resource reassignment was disallowed;
* D-F required unlogged meeting preparation or setup;
* an eligibility detail is missing.

D-X’s 11:20–11:30 idle period similarly contradicts a fully pooled, work-conserving policy. It could have completed eight-minute D08 before the appointment, based on the supplied estimates. Appointment reservation or static assignment explains the choice, but neither is directly recorded.

D-F’s selection of D03 at 11:21 rather than protecting capacity for D05 also challenges a simplistic “most constrained job first” skill rule. It becomes coherent only when the global plan is considered: D-X is reserved for D06, D03 needs an electrical resource immediately, and D05 cannot be completed before the meeting once D03 finishes.

The highest-value record is a dispatch-decision event containing the eligibility set and policy version. Without it, start timestamps conflate three different moments: arrival, assignment, and actual service commencement.

# 7. Final synthesis

**QA is the most identifiable queue.** A03’s 08:20 selection isolates appointment scheduling because FIFO, priority, and SPT all predict different jobs. The remaining sequence is best explained by FIFO fallback with soft appointment timing.

**QC is the least identifiable as a single discipline.** Its initial backlog is convincingly SPT-ordered, but later decisions require some combination of sequence locking, priority or deadline escalation, and aging. C05’s priority and deadline cannot be separated, and the missing assignment times prevent distinguishing dynamic SPT from a precomputed sequence.

QD’s structural skill routing is clear, but the optimization objective behind that routing is less clear. It appears to use resource-specific commitments, appointments, and FIFO rather than fully pooled dynamic assignment.

The most dangerous claim to infer from these observations alone is **pure preemptive priority**. Only QB’s B02 episode directly demonstrates an urgent priority-triggered preemption. The same queue later starts B03 ahead of priority-1 B05 and repeatedly resumes B01 ahead of higher-priority work. QA and QD contain explicit cases where lower-priority jobs are intentionally selected first, while QC confounds priority with deadline escalation. The defensible conclusion is therefore a set of heterogeneous hybrids, not a common priority rule across all four queues.
