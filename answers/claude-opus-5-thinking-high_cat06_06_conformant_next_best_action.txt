No re-screen, no report, and no forecast removes it.

(ii) The observed prefix ends at Evidence Validated. Under rule 5 the conformant successor is Analyst Review (R17's post-evidence re-review), and only then Second Officer Review by a person other than R17 (rule 7). So every conformant plan below implicitly contains: R17 closes the evidence cycle -> independent second officer -> Approve/Reject/hold -> (if approved) Payment Executed (rule 9).

(iii) Clock: T+0 = now, customer deadline T+90.

## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

Four harm classes are not commensurable, and I deliberately refuse to trade them off on a single scale:

1. **Deadline loss on P-884: €20k–€35k, bounded, monetary, recoverable.** The baseline breach probability is already 0.48–0.64, so at most roughly half of this value is actually in play for any action. Even a perfect intervention buys something like 0.3–0.5 x €27k ≈ €8k–€14k in expectation.
2. **False rejection or prolonged hold: €15k–€80k, bounded, monetary, partially reversible** (the payment can be re-submitted; the relationship damage is the sticky part).
3. **Unlawful execution: €100k–€500k plus non-monetary consequences, and irreversible.** Cross-border funds cannot be recalled, and the non-monetary component (regulatory finding, licence conditions, individual accountability, possible sanctions breach) has no defensible price.
4. **Spillover to P-901: +0.12–0.28 on a comparable high-value payment's breach probability**, i.e. roughly €2k–€28k in expectation depending on P-901's own exposure.

**Dominance argument 1 (why A and B are not merely low-ranked but excluded).** Class 3 stochastically dominates class 1 by an order of magnitude on the downside and is irreversible. Under any risk attitude that is not risk-seeking, and under any plausible probability that the 0.46 fuzzy match is a true positive, staking a €100k–€500k+ irreversible tail with non-monetary components to capture at most €35k is inadmissible. But the ranking does not actually rest on that arithmetic: A and B are excluded because they breach mandatory controls, and the arithmetic is offered only to show that even the operational case for them fails.

**Dominance argument 2 (conformant options among themselves).** Every conformant path shares the same protection against class 3, so they differ only in classes 1, 2 and 4. P1 dominates D standalone because it obtains identical information off the critical path. P1 weakly dominates C at T+0 because it defers the certain P-901 damage until it is known whether that damage buys anything: interrupting P-901 and then missing P-884's deadline anyway is a pure loss, and it occurs in a substantial part of the 35–75 minute review band. P1 dominates E as an immediate action because E forfeits class 1 in full without addressing the binding constraint (the missing second review), though E remains the right continuation if the second officer asks for more evidence.

**On F versus E.** F converts an uncertain class 3 exposure into a near-certain class 2 cost. That is a reasonable trade only when second-review capacity is genuinely unobtainable within a defensible window. At T+0 it is premature: the approval probability after a conformant review is 0.55–0.75, so rejecting now is more likely than not to be the wrong decision, and a documented **hold** achieves nearly all of F's protection while retaining optionality. F ranks above E only because it is faster and terminal; if the customer grants even a modest deadline extension, E overtakes F, because E preserves the possibility of a correct approval while F destroys it.

**Ranges, not point estimates.** The 0.55–0.75 approval figure and the 0.48–0.64 breach figure are observational. They were measured on cases that actually went through the process. Under an intervention that removes the review, they simply do not identify the post-intervention outcome, so no numerical improvement in them can license a shortcut. The alternate screening is valuable precisely because it changes the underlying evidence rather than the forecast conditioned on that evidence.

## 4. Conformance constraints

Action-by-action mapping to the mandatory rules:

- **A (execute now)** — violates rule 6 (second-officer gate active on both triggers: score 72 >= 70 and an unresolved fuzzy match at 0.46) and rule 9 (Payment Executed is conformant only after a valid approval path). Rule 3's Auto Clear route is unavailable: it requires no alert and a score below 40. `CURRENTLY NONCONFORMANT`.
- **B (R17 self-certifies)** — violates rule 7 (the first-review analyst and the second officer must be different people) and therefore also rule 6, since a self-certified review is not a Second Officer Review; the subsequent execution violates rule 9. `CURRENTLY NONCONFORMANT`.
- **C (S2 after interrupting P-901)** — satisfies rules 5–7 provided S2 is genuinely independent of R17 and actually reviews the validated evidence rather than rubber-stamping it. Rule 8's decision set (Approve / Reject / hold with SAR) then applies. `CONFORMANT IF STATED CONDITION HOLDS`.
- **D (alternate screening, then second review)** — conformant as an information-gathering step. Rule 6 is not satisfied by re-screening: only an authorized determination that the fuzzy match is resolved, together with a refreshed score below 70, could deactivate both triggers, and a vendor result is an input to that determination, not the determination itself. `CONFORMANT NOW`.
- **E (more customer evidence)** — expressly permitted by rule 5, which allows the Evidence Requested -> Received -> Validated -> Analyst Review cycle to repeat. Care is needed in what is requested and how it is framed, because rule 11 forbids disclosing a Suspicious Activity Report to the customer; the request must be phrased as ordinary due diligence. `CONFORMANT NOW`.
- **F (reject now)** — rule 8 lists Rejected among the permitted terminal decisions, and rule 6 gates only Approve and Payment Executed, not rejection. The conditionality is therefore twofold: the documented evidence threshold for rejection must actually be satisfied, and rule 8's phrase "after the required review(s)" must not be read as requiring the second-officer review before rejection as well. `CONFORMANT IF STATED CONDITION HOLDS`.
- **G (emergency override)** — rule 10 requires both a Compliance Director authorization and a valid recorded incident identifier; the prompt states neither condition is satisfied. `EXCEPTION PATH NOT YET ENABLED`.
- **Cross-cutting**: rule 8 also means that filing a Suspicious Activity Report never authorizes execution, so a SAR is a parallel obligation, not an alternative gate; and rule 11 constrains customer communication in every branch, including the deadline-extension conversation.

**Why A and B cannot be rescued by a favorable numerical forecast.** Three independent reasons, each sufficient:

1. *Deontic, not utilitarian.* Rules 6, 7 and 9 are mandatory controls. They are constraints on the feasible set, not penalty terms in an objective function. An action outside the feasible set has no rank on operational grounds; it can only be re-entered by satisfying the rule (obtain the review) or by a properly enabled exception (rule 10, currently unavailable). Ranking a nonconformant action highly because its expected loss is lower is exactly the error the task forbids.
2. *Identification failure.* The "0.55–0.75 probability of ultimate approval after a conformant review" is conditional on the review having happened, and it was estimated observationally. Using it to argue that the review is unnecessary is circular: it predicts the outcome of the very mechanism being deleted, and it inherits the selection effect that reviews themselves filter the bad cases. Under the intervention "execute without review", the relevant quantity — the probability that this payment is unlawful — is not estimated by that number at all, and the 0.46 fuzzy match is precisely the unresolved evidence about it. The prompt's own caveat that estimates "may not transport unchanged under intervention" is decisive here.
3. *Non-monetary and irreversible harm.* The downside includes regulatory findings, licence consequences, and individual accountability, which no €20k–€35k deadline benefit offsets, and the settlement cannot be reversed. Additionally, B does not merely omit a control; it creates a false record that a control was performed, which is misconduct in its own right, undermines every subsequent audit of R17's work, and removes the detection path that would allow the error to be corrected. That is why B ranks below A.

**Evidence required to make F valid.** (a) The institution's documented rejection standard for an unresolved fuzzy sanctions match, and a showing that the validated evidence meets it; (b) R17's recorded post-evidence Analyst Review with an explicit conclusion that the match cannot be resolved on the available evidence; (c) the alternate-screening result, or a documented reason it was not obtained, so that the rejection is not shown to have been avoidable by 15–30 minutes of work; (d) a refreshed, non-stale risk score; (e) a determination on whether a SAR is required, filed separately and not disclosed to the customer; (f) if rule 8 is read strictly, the completed second-officer review. Absent (a) and (b), the correct action is a documented hold, not a rejection.

**Evidence required to make G valid.** (a) An identified, authenticated Compliance Director actually authorizing this specific override, recorded in the case file; (b) a genuine incident identifier corresponding to a real incident, created and justified on its own merits. A commercial deadline is not an incident. Creating an incident identifier in order to unlock the override would satisfy the letter of rule 10 while defeating its purpose, and is a falsification comparable to B. Note also that even a valid override does not retroactively make A's execution conformant; it would create a separate, documented exception path.

## 5. Resource spillovers

**S2 versus S4.** S2 is available now but only by taking capacity from P-901, whose deadline (70 minutes) is tighter than P-884's and whose breach probability rises by 0.12–0.28 for a 45-minute interruption. S4 costs nothing in spillover but has a 30-minute-wide arrival window that straddles feasibility. The decision-relevant comparison is between P-884's marginal in-window value (at most ~€8k–€14k in expectation, since roughly half of the €20k–€35k is already lost to the 0.48–0.64 baseline breach risk, and it must be further multiplied by the 0.55–0.75 approval probability, giving roughly €5k–€11k) and the spillover cost (0.12–0.28 times P-901's own breach cost). If P-901's breach cost is comparable to P-884's €20k–€35k, the spillover is €2k–€10k and interrupting is roughly break-even and defensible. If P-901's exposure is materially larger — plausible for a high-value payment with a shorter deadline — the spillover reaches €12k–€28k and interrupting is dominated: it destroys more value than it can create. This is why the primary plan pre-briefs S2 but does not interrupt P-901 until S4's ETA is known, and why the interruption, if it happens, should be taken at a natural checkpoint in P-901 and be kept as far under 45 minutes as possible.

**Alternate-screening API quota.** Three expedited calls remain for the whole day. Spending one here has high marginal value: it is off the critical path, it can resolve or corroborate the 0.46 match, it shortens the second officer's work, and in the corroborating branch it prevents a wrong approval outright. Spending a second call on P-884 would not be justified — a repeat query on the same names yields little new information and depletes a resource that other cases may need before the day ends. One call, once, and no re-query on a disappointing result.

**Stale score.** Refreshing the 12-minute-old score is nearly free and must be done, but its decision value is limited: because the unresolved fuzzy match is an independent trigger, a refreshed score below 70 does not open the Auto Clear or single-review path. A refreshed score above 70, or an increase, would strengthen the case for a hold or rejection.

**Time consumed while waiting for an exception.** This is the specific trap in Action G. A 20–60-minute wait for the Compliance Director, on a 90-minute clock, most likely ends with no valid override and with S4 unbooked and P-901 undisturbed but P-884 unreviewable in time. G therefore has a negative option value under time pressure: it does not merely fail, it forecloses the conformant paths that would otherwise still have fit. The same logic applies, more weakly, to sequential D: information bought on the critical path costs more than the information is worth.

**How the ranking changes with S4's arrival time.**

- *S4 available at T+35 (fast end).* The S4 branch is genuinely competitive: screening completes by T+30, R17's review is already recorded, S4 reviews from T+35 with the evidence pre-digested (plausibly 25–50 minutes rather than 35–75), decision T+60–85, execution T+70–95. That is roughly a coin flip on the deadline with zero spillover. Ranking: P1 stays first, **D standalone rises above C**, and C drops to fourth behind D because its certain P-901 damage buys almost no incremental speed. Do not interrupt P-901.
- *S4 available at T+65 (slow end).* Waiting for S4 cannot meet the deadline: T+65 plus 25–50 minutes of review plus 5–15 minutes to record and execute lands at T+95–130. The choice narrows to (i) interrupt P-901 for S2, or (ii) accept the SLA breach, stay conformant, and negotiate an extension. C rises to a clear second and becomes the primary only if P-901's own exposure is demonstrably smaller than P-884's; otherwise the correct move is to accept the breach — a €20k–€35k bounded, monetary, recoverable loss on P-884 is preferable to shifting comparable-or-greater expected loss onto P-901 while still probably breaching. In that world, a documented hold plus Action E overtakes a defensive Action F, since the deadline is lost either way and the extra time should be used to resolve the match properly rather than to reject in haste.
- *Either way, the deadline-extension request at T+0 is the highest-leverage cheap move*: it is free, conformant, and it converts the S4-slow branch from a forced choice into a comfortable one. It must be worded as ordinary additional compliance checks, with no reference to any report (rule 11).

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence

**Primary recommendation — P1.** Immediately and in parallel: have R17 record the post-evidence Analyst Review that closes the rule-5 evidence cycle; launch the alternate sanctions screening using exactly one expedited API call; refresh the stale risk score; page S4 and demand a committed ETA; pre-brief S2 on P-884 without interrupting P-901; and ask the customer for a deadline extension, framed as additional compliance checks with no mention of any report. At T+20–25, gate on S4's confirmed ETA: if it is T+40 or earlier, take the second review from S4 and leave P-901 alone; if it is later or unconfirmed, escalate to Action C, interrupting P-901 at a natural checkpoint and for as short a period as possible, and only if P-901's own exposure is not clearly larger than P-884's. Execute only after a recorded, independent second-officer approval (rules 6, 7, 9).

**Fallback.** If no eligible second officer can complete a review within the window, place P-884 on a documented hold that explicitly names the unresolved fuzzy match and the missing second review as the blocking conditions, accept the SLA breach, and pursue Action E (further customer evidence targeted at name/ownership discrimination) with the second review to follow. Escalate to Action F only once the documented rejection threshold is demonstrably met. If the alternate screening corroborates the match, close the execution track immediately, move to hold plus SAR assessment, and disclose nothing to the customer.

**Confidence.** High (0.9+) on the conformance ordering: A and B are excluded outright and G is unavailable, and no forecast can change that. Medium-High (~0.7) that P1 is the best operational implementation. Medium (~0.5–0.6) that any conformant path executes inside the 90-minute deadline. Low-Medium on the point estimates themselves, which is precisely why the plan is built around a decision gate rather than a single committed sequence.

**Triggers for immediate reranking.**
1. S4's confirmed ETA — the single binding variable; 35 versus 65 minutes flips the S2/S4 choice.
2. The alternate-screening result: match shown false (speeds and favors approval), still unresolved (no change to the gate), or corroborated (execution track closes at once, hold plus SAR).
3. Any authorized determination that formally resolves the fuzzy match, combined with a refreshed score — the only legitimate way the rule-6 gate could deactivate.
4. A refreshed risk score that moves materially, especially upward.
5. Grant or refusal of a customer deadline extension.
6. A change in P-901's state — its own reviewer freeing up, its clearing, or its deadline moving — which re-prices the interruption.
7. Any new adverse-media, ownership, or counterparty information touching the sanctioned-border destination.
8. Unsolicited Compliance Director contact together with a genuine pre-existing incident identifier, which is the only condition under which G re-enters consideration at all.
9. Elapsed time itself: at T+45 with no reviewer secured, switch from optimizing the deadline to optimizing a defensible, documented hold.
