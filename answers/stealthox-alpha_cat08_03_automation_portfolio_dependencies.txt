# 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

**Upstream data quality chain.** Registration quality (A1) feeds classification accuracy (A2); classification determines the control path — whether collateral valuation is required (71% of Equipment finance vs 18% of Working-capital), which committee cadence applies, and whether four-eyes rules attach. A6 output must be model-ready before credit analysis; A8 is downstream of *four* artifacts (KYC status, normalized statements, valuation, credit notes) — automating it before those are reliable just produces garbage packs faster. A9 requires a complete committee decision record and KYC clearance; A10 requires a trustworthy KYC-cleared flag; A11 requires *all* of the above provable at activation time.

**Legal / compliance controls.** Three hard rules shape the design: (1) KYC clearance before signing — A10 must implement this as a system-enforced block, eliminating the 6.5% provisional-signing practice; (2) contract terms = committee decision — A9's rule checks address the 8.5%/14% mismatch; (3) four-eyes for High-exposure SME — currently violated in 5.8% of month-end cases; any future A8 must enforce assembler  credit analyst. Every model-suggestion module (A2, A3, A6, A9, A10) must log **suggestion  human override  final decision** in an immutable audit trail. Note the DFG edge *Resolve entity mismatch  Contract preparation* (380 cases): mismatches surfacing at contract stage indicate late discovery or deferral — A9 must hard-block contracts where KYC is not cleared rather than process them.

**Human escalation capacity.** The 3-FTE compliance pool (dependent on two seniors) is the escalation sink for A3/A4 — escalation volume must be budgeted, or automation converts a waiting problem into a specialist overload problem. A6 requires analyst QA capacity for low-confidence extractions. Committee cadence (weekly for High-exposure) is a fixed floor no module can change; automation can only avoid wasting slots via better packs (6% decision rework).

**External valuation dependency.** A7 can triage and parallelize but not control valuers. Real gains require triggering the request *earlier* — which depends on A2 surfacing the collateral requirement at classification instead of after credit analysis (current DFG: Credit analysis  Collateral valuation request, 2,400). Monitor the ~370 cases (2,350  1,980) that do not flow from the external wait into committee packaging.

**Core-banking integration constraint.** Only two deep integrations in the first six months. Allocation: **A6** (credit-data hand-off) and **A10** (KYC-status gate read) — both needed early. **A9's** deep integration lands in months 6–9, outside the cap. A5 is portal-side (no core slot); A1 and A11 are deferred partly for this reason. A8/A3 wait for year two slots.

**Concurrency caveat.** KYC and financial document review run in parallel in practice though recorded sequentially (Classify  KYC 7,250 vs Classify  Doc review 3,940). A5's gains are therefore partly independent of the KYC lane — and naive DFG cycle-time measurement will understate them. Phase 0 must reconstruct concurrency from timestamps.

# 3. Portfolio alternatives

**P1 — Quick-win portfolio: A1 + A5 + A9 + A10.**
Optimizes: visible throughput, headline labor savings, customer-facing latency; politically marketable. Sacrifices: the 31h entity-mismatch wait, the 58h valuation wait, and analyst capacity (A6 excluded) — so A1's accelerated inflow piles onto the analysts, the exact overload the resource network warns against. A1's marginal value is thin (72% already digital; back office has slack below 65% utilization after 15:00). Integration-strained: A1 and A9 both want deep core slots.

**P2 — Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4 + A5 + A6 + A7.**
Optimizes: structural cycle time — attacks the 31h mismatch wait (82% critical path), the 58h external wait, and the 85% doc-review loop; relieves the two scarcest pools (3 specialists, 9 analysts). Sacrifices: no fix for contract-decision mismatch (8.5%/14%) or provisional signing (6.5%); no headline win (political risk); the control-strengthening requirement is only weakly met — compliance would likely reject it as constituted.

**P3 — Compliance-first portfolio: A3 + A9 + A10 + A12.**
Optimizes: control integrity — all three control findings directly addressed; auditability by design. Sacrifices: cycle time (the 31h and 58h waits persist; A3 reduces mismatch *inflow* but not resolution speed), the analyst normalization bottleneck (20h wait, 75 min service untouched), and the doc-review loop (85% loop-back persists). Heavy audit investment needed for A3's false-negative risk.

**P4 — Balanced portfolio (recommended): A5 + A6 + A9 + A10.**
Optimizes: the three largest *population-weighted, internally controllable* delays (signing 44h × 7,300; doc loops 3,300/2,800; contract remediation 650 loops), the capacity of the central resource (analysts), and two hard control gates. Fits the integration budget (A6 + A10 deep in H1; A9 deep in H2) and the RM-friction constraint (A5 co-designed, A10 *reduces* RM chasing). Sacrifices: the 31h mismatch wait persists in year one (interim mitigations below), the 58h valuation wait persists (year two, with A2), and there is no high-frequency headline (A1 deferred — defended in §7).

# 4. Expected process-level effects

**P1 (quick-win)**
- *Cycle time:* modest front-end gain (1.5h registration wait); real tail gains from A9/A10.
- *Waiting:* signing wait (44h) and contract starvation (22h) improve; mismatch (31h) and valuation (58h) untouched.
- *Rework:* contract remediation (650 loops) and signing reminders (21%) down; registration duplicates down.
- *Handovers:* fewer back-office touches, but more cases pushed into the Classify  KYC (7,250) / Classify  Doc review (3,940) split faster than those lanes can absorb.
- *Resource demand:* back office falls (it has slack anyway); **analyst load rises** — the key failure mode; legal ops relieved.
- *Controls:* provisional signing and contract mismatch improve; four-eyes month-end risk may *worsen* as volume accelerates.

**P2 (bottleneck/synchronization)**
- *Cycle time:* largest structural cut — 31h mismatch wait, compressed 58h valuation wait via earlier triggering/parallelization with credit analysis.
- *Waiting:* mismatch, external valuation, and doc re-review (18h per loop iteration) all fall.
- *Rework:* mismatch 38%, doc review 24%, repeated requests 52% all down; fewer Resolve  KYC re-entries (1,160).
- *Handovers:* less specialist ping-pong; key-person dependency on two seniors reduced.
- *Resource demand:* specialists and analysts relieved — but A4's limited coverage (1,750) caps the specialist gain.
- *Controls:* the 380 late mismatch resolutions surface earlier (indirect gain); provisional signing and contract mismatch **persist** — the compliance gap.

**P3 (compliance-first)**
- *Cycle time:* smallest improvement; signing gate may lengthen apparent signing waits for KYC-stuck cases — *correct* behavior that moves the wait to where the control gap is.
- *Waiting:* KYC-related waits fall somewhat; doc/normalization waits unchanged.
- *Rework:* remediation loops and KYC hit rework down.
- *Handovers:* remediation consolidated in A12; fewer ad-hoc fixes.
- *Resource demand:* compliance work becomes structured but specialist capacity is still the ceiling.
- *Controls:* strongest — all three findings addressed, full audit trails; explicit sacrifice of speed.

**P4 (balanced — recommended)**
- *Cycle time:* material end-to-end reduction: signing wait 44h  high-20s; doc-loop iterations cut (85% loop-back  <60% gate); normalization service 75  ~25–30 min on standard statements; contract remediation loops 650  <300.
- *Waiting:* signing (44h), doc re-review re-entry (18h/loop), normalization (20h), and contract starvation (22h — legal ops is underutilized *because* upstream is slow, so the wait collapses as upstream cleans up) all fall. Mismatch (31h) and valuation (58h) deliberately deferred.
- *Rework:* doc review 24% , contract 12%  (8.5%  <4% target; Credit-line renewal 14%  <7%), signing reminder loop 21% .
- *Handovers:* fewer RM  back-office  customer loops (RMs currently trigger many missing-doc loops — templated instructions fix the source); month-end legal overload smoothed; cleaner analyst handovers.
- *Resource demand:* analyst capacity up ~2–3 FTE-equivalent — **governed by WIP limits, not converted 1:1 into inflow**; back-office A5 exceptions absorbed in post-15:00 slack; compliance specialists unchanged (residual hotspot, monitored).
- *Controls:* provisional signing hard-blocked (6.5%  ~0; 1.1% remediation  ~0); contract-decision mismatch down with versioned rule checks; four-eyes month-end violations *not solved* — monitored with WIP caps, A12 as the year-two contingency.

# 7. Final recommendation

**Recommend P4 — Balanced portfolio: A5, A6, A9, A10**, with A4 + A2 + A7 as the pre-committed year-two wave.

**Why not the highest-frequency activity first.** Receive/Register is the frequency trap the process-mining evidence exposes: it is already digital for 72% of cases, takes 3 minutes, and has a 0.2h wait — the marginal automation target is 28% of 3-minute tasks. Its resource pool (back office, 12 FTE) runs below 65% utilization after 15:00, so the ~0.8 FTE saving releases capacity in a pool that is *not* the binding constraint. Worse, A1's own risk profile is that it accelerates inflow into classification, KYC, and document review — and the resource network explicitly warns that analysts (9 FTE, central in the working-together network) will overload unless normalization improves first. Frequency is a numerator; leverage is frequency × wait contribution × critical-path position × control exposure. By that measure, the 44h signing wait across 7,300 cases, the 85% doc-review loop with 52% repeated requests, and the 8.5%/14% contract-decision mismatch dominate registration.

**Why these four.** A5 and A6 are mutual prerequisites: A5 cuts loop inflow, A6 expands analyst capacity — together they unlock flow without overload. A10 delivers the single largest population-weighted wait reduction *and* hard-enforces KYC-before-signing, satisfying the compliance mandate in the first wave. A9 converts a 4-FTE legal pool's biggest rework source into a controlled, rule-checked process and directly attacks the 650-loop remediation path. All four fit the integration budget and reduce rather than raise RM friction.

**What we consciously sacrifice, and why it is defensible.** The 31h entity-mismatch wait (1,750 cases, 82% critical path) and the 58h external valuation wait persist for a year — mitigated by interim triage rules, specialist cross-training to reduce the two-senior key-person risk, monitoring of the 380 late-resolution edge, and a pre-committed year-two wave. The high-frequency quick win is forgone for political reasons we accept, and mitigate by publishing the process-mining evidence (weighted waits, utilization, loop rates) that justifies the sequence. Four-eyes month-end violations are *monitored and capped*, not solved — with A12 as the explicit trigger-pull if they persist.

**Compliance responsibility is retained throughout:** every module logs suggestion, override, and final decision; credit judgment and committee decisions remain human; and no downstream bot (A8, A11) is activated until the upstream controls it depends on are demonstrably reliable.
