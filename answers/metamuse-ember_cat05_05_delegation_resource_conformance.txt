## 2. Rule Interpretation

**RR1 — Drafter/approver separation.** For each case, `human(Draft_Change_Request)  human(Approve_Change)` after alias normalisation. It applies irrespective of risk. A LOW-risk case still requires a different approver unless a documented policy exemption exists; none is supplied. An unresolved service account counts as unproven, not as distinct.

**RR2 — Implementer/verifier separation.** `human(Implement_Change)  human(Verify_Implementation)` after normalisation, on the same basis.

**RR3 — High-risk approval authority.** A HIGH-risk approval is technically valid only if the approver is the principal QM at the event timestamp, or a delegate whose delegation simultaneously satisfies: effective interval covers the event; scope covers the logged scope category; any principal-absence/formal-unavailability condition is met at that timestamp; and no prohibited subdelegation occurred. Scope categories are taken as logged: `production_equipment`, `cooling_system`, and `training_document` are distinct unless a documented taxonomy proves inclusion. A principal acting personally while on leave remains the QM in title; leave is an availability/HR status, not a role revocation. The identity question — did the principal personally act, or was the credential used by another — is separate from title.

**RR4 — Emergency substitution and retrospective review.** A HIGH emergency may use IC or valid IC-delegate approval, but it is a justified exception only if the emergency flag and safety rationale are credible and a QM or valid QM delegate performs Retrospective_QM_Review within two business days. Business days are counted here as Monday–Friday excluding weekends and site holidays, with the event date as Day 0 and the next business day as Day 1. Thus Tuesday 2026-08-11  Wednesday = 1, Thursday = 2, Friday 2026-08-14 = 3; the deadline is end of Thursday 13th. No holiday is recorded. Under an inclusive count the deadline would be earlier, so CHG-505 on Friday is late on either counting. CHG-510 on Wednesday 12th is timely on timing but fails on QM-delegate scope.

**RR5 — Identity evidence.** Account use is evidence of account use, not conclusive evidence of the named human. Aliases of one human must be collapsed. Service-account actions require session evidence tying the session to an authorised human at that time.

**RR6 — Suspicion versus proof.** Pair frequency, reciprocity, shared workstations, and concentrated handovers are risk indicators. They do not prove collusion, self-approval, or credential compromise without discriminating evidence.

**Later database entry.** The time a record was created in a database is not its effective interval. D-QM-01's DB record created 2026-08-12 09:15 during migration does not invalidate the signed PDF timestamped 2026-08-09 18:00, which is contemporaneous evidence of the instrument. Conversely, a post-hoc DB entry alone, without a signed or otherwise anchored contemporaneous instrument, would be weak evidence and vulnerable to backdating. D-QM-02 is stronger evidentially because its controlled DB record was created before its interval.

**Severity and confidence.** Severity: High = HIGH-risk safety/authority control failure or unproven critical verification; Medium = SoD or identity failure with lower immediate case risk, or HIGH case with material identity uncertainty; Low = residual control weakness where the technical rules are met; None = no deviation found. Confidence: High/Medium/Low reflects strength of identity, scope, time, and absence evidence, not seriousness.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

No indicator below meets the RR6 threshold for collusion. Each is assessed as a risk signal requiring discriminating evidence.

**Repeated Clara–Ben pair: Clara drafted and Ben approved 18 of 23 HIGH production changes in 60 days.**
Benign explanation: Clara is the frequent drafter, Ben is the available specialist/delegate, both sit in the same production cell, and no pair-rotation policy exists, so concentration is the expected outcome of resourcing. Adverse explanation: routinised rubber-stamping, mutual protection, or deliberate routing around Asha, normalising weak scrutiny. Discriminating evidence: approval turnaround and comment depth compared with Asha-approved cases; Ben's rejection/challenge rate for Clara versus others; defects found in retrospective QM sampling; whether Ben ever rejected or materially amended Clara drafts; whether approvals cluster at times when Asha was demonstrably available.

**Reciprocal pattern: Ben drafted four LOW training changes that Clara approved.**
Benign explanation: workload balancing and subject-matter convenience on LOW-risk work, with no known financial benefit. Adverse explanation: a favour bank in which each party approves the other's work to preserve goodwill. Discriminating evidence: whether Clara and Ben normally draft/approve these categories anyway; timing and reciprocity gaps; rejection rates and substantive comments; whether any reciprocal approval coincided with a personal benefit, overtime, or appraisal outcome.

**Shared workstations and service account: CHG-503 (WS-DM-04, Diego) and CHG-509 (WS-IV-02, Eva).**
Benign explanation: shared verification terminals and a service account designed for system verification; Diego's badge shows room entry only, and in CHG-509 FIDO strongly identifies Eva. Adverse explanation: Diego used svc_qms to self-verify in CHG-503, and Eva used it to verify her own approval in CHG-509, disguising the actor behind the service account. Discriminating evidence: FIDO/session binding for CHG-503, terminal logon and keystroke/session records, CCTV, badge door versus terminal use correlation, authorship of verification artefacts, and service-account checkout logs.

**Absence conflicts: Asha on leave with a VPN approval (CHG-504); Ben on sick leave with approvals/reviews (CHG-506, and the CHG-505 retrospective on 14th).**
Benign explanation: Asha genuinely interrupted leave for an urgent case; Ben worked while sick or a colleague innocently used his logged-in office session. Adverse explanation: credential sharing or impersonation, potentially by a delegate who preferred the principal's authority or by an unknown office user. Discriminating evidence: MFA and device-certificate binding, VPN endpoint fingerprint, workstation logon identity, badge/CCTV, writing and workflow patterns, Asha's urgent-case documentation, and Ben's sick-certification and whereabouts records. The repeated use of Ben's account during certified sick leave is the single strongest identity-risk signal in the set.

**Concentrated handover network: Clara drafts, Diego implements, Eva verifies in almost every case.**
Benign explanation: this is the designed specialisation — Clara is stated to be the frequent drafter, Diego the implementer/emergency authority, Eva the verifier. High edge frequency in a handover network is therefore expected. Adverse explanation: a closed loop with familiarity bias, eroded independence, and concentration of implementation plus emergency authority in Diego, who is also IC principal and workstation owner in CHG-503. Discriminating evidence: comparison against rostered baselines, independent audit sampling of verified work, defect and rework rates by pair, and whether Diego's emergency authority was ever used to approve his own implementation chain.

**Invalid subdelegation and self-approval in CHG-510 (Farid  Clara by email).**
Benign explanation: urgent cooling emergency, Farid as a valid delegate believed he could pass authority on, and Clara acted in good faith. Adverse explanation: a knowing bypass of the QM using a flimsy email trail, with Farid's prohibition ignored. Discriminating evidence: the email wording and Farid's awareness of the prohibition, CI-44 urgency timeline, whether Asha or Ben were contacted, and prior training/attestation on delegation limits.

**Approver-as-verifier concentration in CHG-509.**
Benign explanation: Saturday skeleton cover, HIGH training-document case, Eva either approved as delegate or verified as FIDO-identified user, not necessarily both in a prohibited sense. Adverse explanation: Eva approved and then self-verified, defeating independent review. Discriminating evidence: whether policy prohibits approver-as-verifier, the full FIDO session record, and whether a second person reviewed the verification output.

## 6. Control Improvements

**Delegation creation.** Replace email and ad-hoc instruments with a single delegation registry that enforces principal, delegate, scope enum, interval, no-subdelegation flag, and approval workflow. Subdelegation should be technically impossible where prohibited, not merely contractually prohibited. Emergency delegations need a same-day registration path so urgency does not drive informal workarounds.

**Effective-time recording.** Separate *effective interval*, *signed instrument time*, and *database record creation time* in the data model. Migration loads must preserve original instrument timestamps and hashes, and the system must show whether authority existed at the event time. D-QM-01's signed PDF versus late DB record should be the design test case.

**Principal availability.** Integrate HR leave/sick-leave feeds with the QMS so that, at approval time, the system displays principal availability and delegate availability. A delegate on certified sick leave should trigger a block or a documented override with re-authentication; a principal on leave who acts should require an explicit urgent-case justification. Weekend coverage rosters must be recorded where a delegation depends on absence.

**Alias management.** Enforce one person, one identity in the identity provider; merge `u_bk`/`b.koch`-type aliases, log the merging, and test SoD on the person ID. Legacy role accounts should be retired or visibly mapped to a named human.

**Individual authentication.** Require phishing-resistant MFA/FIDO for HIGH approvals, retrospective reviews, and any service-account checkout. Service accounts should not be usable for Verify_Implementation without a bound individual session; shared terminals need per-user re-authentication. Workstation, badge, and session events should be correlatable and retained.

**Emergency review.** Build an RR4 timer that counts business days from IC approval, alerts the QM/delegate pool, and escalates before the two-day deadline. The retrospective reviewer must be checked for QM/delegate scope (cooling versus production equipment) at assignment, not after the fact. Emergency rationale and incident linkage (e.g., CI-44) should be mandatory fields.

**Pair rotation.** Introduce soft caps and rotation for drafter–approver pairs on HIGH cases, with documented exceptions when the pool is genuinely small. Track rejection and amendment rates by pair so rotation decisions rest on scrutiny quality, not only frequency.

**Network monitoring.** Use handover and working-together networks as monitoring dashboards with baselines and thresholds for review, never as automatic misconduct flags. Pair concentration should prompt sampling of approval quality. Reports should be reviewed by Quality independent of the pairs monitored.

**Evidential retention.** Retain signed delegations with hashes, session/FIDO logs, VPN device data, badge data, and CCTV where available, with retention aligned to investigation needs. Shared-terminal and service-account evidence gaps (CHG-503) should be treated as control failures in their own right and closed by design.

## 7. Executive Conclusion

The apparent nonconformance is a mixture of three distinct problems. First, **genuine authority failure** is clear in CHG-502 (self-approval), CHG-507 (Eva approving without QM authority), CHG-508 (alias-masked self-approval), CHG-510 (self-approval plus a prohibited subdelegation), and the retrospective stage of CHG-505 (reviewer outside delegation scope and late). Second, **identity and data-quality failure** accounts for the most consequential uncertainties: the svc_qms verification in CHG-503, the unknown approver in CHG-506, the credential-sharing question in CHG-504, Ben's account use during sick leave on 13th–14th, and the aliasing in CHG-508. Third, **organisational-pattern risk** — Clara–Ben concentration, reciprocity, and the Clara–Diego–Eva closed loop — is real as a control weakness but is not, on current evidence, proof of collusion; much of it reflects stated role specialisation and the absence of a rotation policy.

Uncertainty is concentrated where identity is ambiguous: CHG-503, CHG-504, and CHG-506 cannot be resolved to a named actor on the supplied evidence, and CHG-509 depends on an inferred weekend absence. The three highest-priority investigations are: (1) the credential/identity investigation covering CHG-506, the CHG-505 retrospective, and CHG-504 — who used Ben's and Asha's accounts during leave, and whether a pattern of credential sharing exists; (2) the service-account investigation covering CHG-503 and CHG-509 — who operated svc_qms, whether Diego self-verified, and whether Eva verified her own approval; and (3) the emergency-governance review covering CHG-505 and CHG-510 — the FaridClara subdelegation, the repeated use of a production-equipment-scoped delegate for cooling cases, and the missed retrospective deadline, as a systemic test of whether the emergency pathway can bypass QM control.
