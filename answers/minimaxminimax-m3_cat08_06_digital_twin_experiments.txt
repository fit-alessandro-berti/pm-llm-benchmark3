# Simulation-Based Digital Twin for the Building-Permit Process

## 1. Model scope and state variables

### 1.1 Boundary and level of detail

The digital twin models the **end-to-end municipal building-permit process** from portal submission to final decision (issue or reject). It is a **discrete-event simulation (DES)** with an overlaid **case-arrival model** and **dependency layer**. The twin is *not* a generative AI controller; it is a calibrated stochastic simulator used to compare interventions under controlled conditions.

Scope boundaries:

| In scope | Out of scope |
|---|---|
| Municipal activities in the process tree | Pre-application conversations, technical drawing production, physical construction |
| Resource calendars (clerks, planners, fire/environment reviewers, inspectors, legal officers) | External agencies (e.g., regional fire authority) treated as exogenous unless they appear in the log |
| Applicant and third-party response delays (modeled as exogenous stochastic processes) | Real-time prediction of who will object to a given application |
| Cross-case interactions (architect bursts, district batching, neighborhood objections, legal pre-check) | Long-term policy adoption costs; political acceptance |
| 12–24 month horizon per experiment | Multi-year strategic workforce planning beyond horizon |

Level of detail: **activity-level with explicit queues and resources**, not task-level. Each process-tree activity is realized as one or more service stations; rework loops are explicit transitions; missing-document loops are explicit.

### 1.2 Entities

- **Case** (application): attributes `case_id`, `case_type`, `district`, `architect_id`, `application_value`, `requires_fire`, `requires_environment`, `requires_public_notice`, `submitted_at`, `current_activity`, `state` (open/waiting/in_service/done), `case_birth_cohort` (week), and dynamic fields `planning_result`, `fire_result`, `environment_result`, `inspection_result`, `legal_result`, `rework_count`, `objections_observed`.
- **Resource**: clerks, planners, fire reviewers, environmental reviewers, schedulers, inspectors, legal officers, system (unlimited).
- **Calendar exception**: holiday, council meeting week, partial-staffing day, district-specific road closure.
- **Bundle**: a grouping of cases scheduled for a shared site-inspection trip (key: district + inspector + day).
- **Objection event**: exogenous process affecting public-notice duration for related cases in a district.

### 1.3 State variables per case

`waiting_clock`, `service_clock`, `sojourn_since_arrival`, `rework_count`, `bottleneck_history`, `last_activity_completed_at`, `precedence_fan_in` (number of pending parallel results), `appeal_open`.

### 1.4 Resources, queues, calendars

Resources are organized as **pools with calendars and queues**:

| Pool | FTE | Calendar | Queue discipline |
|---|---|---|---|
| `clerk_pool` | 5 (1 portal-Monday) | Mon–Fri 08:00–16:00 | FIFO with portal spike priority on Mondays |
| `planner_pool` | 8 (2 Simple-only) | Mon–Fri 09:00–17:00 | FIFO by submission timestamp + aging rule (D6) |
| `fire_pool` | 2 FTE equivalent | Tue–Thu only | FIFO; capacity = effective daily hours |
| `env_pool` | 3 (40% reserved) | Mon–Fri, 60% effective | FIFO with triage priority (D8) |
| `scheduler_pool` | shared with clerk_pool | same | FIFO |
| `inspector_pool` | 4 (D4 adds temp) | Tue–Fri field; office days for routing | District batched (D3) |
| `legal_pool` | 2 | Mon–Fri, paused in council weeks except urgent | Priority for pre-checked (D5) |
| `system` | unlimited | 24/7 | n/a |

Queues expose both **length** and **age-of-oldest-case**, used for SLA monitoring and the aging-based intervention (D6).

### 1.5 Cross-case dependencies (modeled explicitly)

1. **Architect burst dependency.** Arrivals from the same `architect_id` are correlated: when an architect submits, additional applications from that architect within a 7-day window are more likely (top-20 architects account for 31% of cases). Modeled as a two-component arrival: a Poisson baseline plus an architect-specific batch process (e.g., a mixture or Hawkes-like excitation).
2. **District batching.** Cases entering `Inspection scheduling` from the same district on the same day may be bundled by the scheduler, reducing travel overhead but increasing waiting for the first case in the bundle (compromise effect).
3. **Neighborhood-objection dependency.** Exogenous `objection_event(district, t)` extends the public-notice period for all open cases in that district whose public notice is in progress. Modeled as a public-notice "objection clock" that pauses/resumes notice days.
4. **Legal pre-check dependency (D5).** When Commercial/Complex-variance cases run a parallel legal pre-check, the legal pool sees extra work; this affects shared queue dynamics for everyone.

## 2. Parameter-estimation plan

PM4Py is used for log and model inspection; the simulation engine (e.g., SimPy) is a separate component that consumes parameters produced from PM4Py outputs. PM4Py is **not** used as the simulator.

### 2.1 Arrival process

1. Per-case `arrival_ts = min(time:timestamp)` per `case:concept:name` from PM4Py-formatted log.
2. Estimate base rate: `_base = N_cases / T_observed` over the 18-month window.
3. Estimate **seasonal multiplier** `s(m)`: monthly arrival counts normalized to mean; observed 1.7x in March–May and September–November vs. baseline. Fit piecewise-constant or Fourier basis.
4. Estimate **day-of-week multiplier** `dow(d)`: from `arrival_ts.dt.dayofweek`; observed Monday  2.2x Wednesday. Fit a 7-level factor.
5. **Architect burst**: compute within-architect inter-arrival times for the top-20 architects vs. tail; estimate a per-architect burst factor or a mixture (e.g., 80% Poisson, 20% bursty on Mondays).
6. Optional: estimate via `statsmodels.tsa` (e.g., SARIMAX with exogenous day-of-week) or directly via grouped rate ratios.

### 2.2 Service times

Because start timestamps are missing for many human activities, the log primarily gives **completion timestamps**. Two complementary approaches:

- **Where both timestamps exist** (system activities, occasional human ones): activity duration = `end - start`.
- **Where only completion timestamps exist**: the duration is `completion - previous_completion`; this conflates waiting and service. We acknowledge this as a limitation. We **sample service time from a fitted lognormal** (per activity) and treat the residual as waiting. Service-time distributions are fit by case type × activity where sample sizes permit.

PM4Py: use `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg` for median/p90 inter-activity times; supplement with `pandas` groupbys on activity duration for distribution fitting (lognormal, gamma, or empirical).

### 2.3 Waiting times

Estimated indirectly as `inter_event_gap - sampled_service_time` with sensitivity bounds; or by replaying the log through a queue model (event-simulation replay) that fits a queue discipline. Validate waiting estimates against the published median "waiting before activity" column.

### 2.4 Branching probabilities

Per activity, fit `p(branch | case_attrs)` using PM4Py variant statistics (`pm4py.get_variants_count`, `pm4py.get_variants`) merged with `pandas` filtering on `case_type`, `requires_fire`, etc. For example:
- `p(fire_required | requires_fire)  1`
- `p(missing_docs_requested | case_type)` from variant frequencies.

### 2.5 Rework

Unstructured text fields are noisy. We approximate rework by detecting repeated activities in the same case using PM4Py variant statistics (`pm4py.get_variants`). Each variant with repeated activities yields one or more rework types (completeness rework, planning rework, inspection rework, legal rework, appeal-driven rework).

### 2.6 Resource calendars

HR data is the primary source; cross-validate against log activity timestamps per resource (e.g., work-hour frequency of `org:resource` events).

### 2.7 Dependencies

Architect burst: from grouped arrival analysis. District batching: compare inter-arrival-to-inspection time by district and architect; co-scheduling patterns are visible via shared `scheduler_*` resources and overlapping `Inspection scheduling` timestamps in the same district.

### 2.8 PM4Py usage summary

- `pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id='case:concept:name', activity_key='concept:name', timestamp_key='time:timestamp')`
- `pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)`
- `pm4py.get_variants`, `pm4py.get_variants_count`
- `pm4py.discover_dfg`, `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg`
- `pm4py.discover_tree_inductive` as a sanity check on the supplied tree
- `pm4py.parse_process_tree` to load the supplied tree string (with caveats; see §8)
- `pm4py.organize_logs` utilities for filtering by `case_type`, `district`

## 3. Assumptions

Each assumption is stated, motivated, and labeled with the data that would validate it. We do not assume a single ground truth.

### 3.1 Arrivals

- **Non-stationary Poisson** with multiplicative seasonal and day-of-week factors. *Validation*: KS test on simulated vs. empirical inter-arrival times, by hour and by month.
- Architect burst modeled as an **additive batch component**: when a top-20 architect arrives on a Monday, additional arrivals from that architect within 7 days follow a higher conditional rate. *Validation*: comparison of observed top-20 burst share (31%) to simulated share.
- Submissions outside working hours are absorbed into the next working day's arrival bucket (matches observed portal patterns).

### 3.2 Service times

- Service times are **lognormal** with parameters estimated per activity and case type (heavier tails than exponential, consistent with empirical p90/median ratios of ~3–7).
- When only completion timestamps exist, we **sample** service time and treat the remainder as waiting; we report sensitivity to this split in §5.
- System activities (`Submit application`, `Issue permit`, `Reject application`, `Public-notice period`) are **deterministic or calendar-driven**, not sampled.

### 3.3 Routing

- The supplied process tree is **structurally correct** but operator-specific probabilities (e.g., missing-doc-loop firing rate, rework triggers) are **estimated from variant frequencies**, not asserted.
- Conditional rules use the attributes `requires_fire`, `requires_environment`, `requires_public_notice` directly; missing building-area and district-sensitivity data mean some rules are approximated probabilistically (we report this as a data-gap correction in §3.9).

### 3.4 Resources and calendars

- HR calendars are authoritative for **capacity and availability**; log timestamps are used only to detect deviations (e.g., overtime).
- Inspector **travel time** is district-specific (D3 and D5 longer); modeled as additive service time drawn from a fitted distribution.
- Council-meeting weeks suspend legal review of non-urgent cases; we do not assume which weeks are council weeks but treat them as an exogenous calendar exception list.

### 3.5 Rework

- Rework is **bounded** per case (e.g., max 3 attempts per activity) to prevent runaway loops in the simulation; we calibrate the cap to match empirical p99 loop counts.
- Rework probability is conditioned on **prior activity outcome**, not random; when outcome is not observable (unstructured text), we fall back to variant-derived rates.

### 3.6 Applicant and third-party response

- **Applicant response** to "Request missing documents" is an exogenous stochastic delay with heavy tail (median 5d, p90 much longer). This is **not a municipal resource** and is sampled separately.
- **Objections** are an exogenous process with rate depending on district and case type; we do not infer objections from the log because the log does not reliably capture them.

### 3.7 Cross-case dependencies

- Architect bursts are **conditional**, not triggered; we assume the additional applications are independent conditional on the burst.
- District batching is **opportunistic**, not mandatory: the scheduler bundles if at least 2 cases from the same district are pending at scheduling time.
- Objection events affect **all open cases in the district with public notice in progress**; we do not model which specific cases are objecting.

### 3.8 Non-stationary demand

- We **do not assume** the demand pattern is stationary across replications. Each replication uses a randomly shifted seasonal calendar but preserves the same multipliers, so the average behavior is stationary but individual replications are not.

### 3.9 Missing data

- **Objection events**: not in the log; we use **public-notice duration as a proxy** and explicitly model the proxy as `notice_min = 14d + _objections` where `_objections` is an exogenous district-specific random variable. We **never** treat individual objection events as observed.
- **Start timestamps**: missing for many human activities. We impute via inter-event gaps and service-time sampling, and we report a sensitivity bound.
- **Rare emergency decisions**: removed from the log; we ignore them rather than infer their rates (they are <0.5% of cases; their omission will bias tail durations downward; we flag this).
- **Rework reasons**: unstructured; we use repeat-activity detection in PM4Py variants.
- **Building area / district sensitivity**: missing for some routing rules; we use probabilistic fallbacks with explicit uncertainty.

| **D10** | Combined policy D1+D2+D3+D4 | All four interventions simultaneously |  median and tail for most case types,  complexity, dependency risk |
| **S1** | Stress: demand surge | Seasonal multipliers scaled by 1.5 for one year | Tests capacity headroom |
| **S2** | Stress: inspector outage | One inspector unavailable for 4 consecutive weeks | Tests robustness of D3/D4 interaction |
| **S3** | Stress: new environmental rule | `requires_environment` probability +50% for Commercial cases | Tests env pool saturation under policy shift |
| **S4** | Stress: extended objection period | `_objections` distribution shifted to add 30d median | Tests public-notice bottlenecks |

Each experiment is a **full-factorial run with replications** (see §5). Combinations other than D10 (e.g., D1+D7, D3+D4) are run as exploratory cells if needed.

## 5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment

### 5.1 Warm-up

- **Warm-up period**: 90 days (one full seasonal quarter). KPIs are computed only after warm-up. Rationale: covers one seasonal cycle and one weekend pattern.
- Detection: the first 90 days of each replication are removed from KPI computation; initial queue and resource states are seeded from the long-run equilibrium estimated via a short pre-run (e.g., 60 days of no-stat warm-up).

### 5.2 Run length and replications

- **Run length per replication**: 24 months (2 full seasonal cycles).
- **Replications per experiment**: 50 (target), minimum 30 if compute is constrained. More replications for tail-sensitive KPIs (e.g., p95 cycle time, backlog).
- **Random seeds**: seeds are stored per experiment so replications are reproducible; master seed for experiment, replication index for run-level seed.

### 5.3 Confidence intervals

- 95% confidence intervals on **median** cycle time per case type using bootstrap (B = 1000) on per-replication medians.
- 95% CIs on **p90/p95** using the same bootstrap over per-replication quantiles.
- 95% CIs on **resource utilization** using t-intervals (utilization is approximately normal over replications).
- For **SLA compliance rates**, Wilson intervals.

### 5.4 Sensitivity analysis

A **global sensitivity analysis** is performed on the baseline (B0) to identify which parameters most affect KPIs:

- Method: Morris screening first (low-cost), then a small Sobol' analysis on the top 5–10 parameters.
- Parameters: mean inter-arrival time, service-time means per activity, rework probabilities, resource FTE counts, objection delay scale.
- Output: tornado plots and Sobol' indices for median and p90 cycle time.

### 5.5 Heavy-tailed durations

- Use **lognormal** (not exponential) for service times and applicant responses.
- For p90/p95/p99 KPIs, do not rely on t-intervals; use bootstrap.
- Detect heavy tails by fitting and reporting **tail index** (Hill estimator) on the log-derived service times.
- Variance reduction: common random numbers across experiments for shared noise components (e.g., arrival process), but only where variance decomposition justifies it.

### 5.6 Handling of missing-data uncertainty

- Run a **data-gap sensitivity variant** of each experiment: shift assumed parameters (e.g., service-time mean, objection delay mean) by ±20% to bound KPI uncertainty attributable to the data gaps listed in §3.9.

## 6. Validation strategy

A multi-tier validation mirrors industry practice for process digital twins.

### 6.1 Face validation

- Process experts walk through animated runs (e.g., via Plotly/SimPy animation) and compare observed behavior to their intuition.
- Calendar exceptions (council weeks, holidays) are visually confirmed to suppress legal/public-notice activity as expected.
- Acceptance criterion: experts sign off on animation and KPI ranges.

### 6.2 Event-log replay validation

- Replay the historical event log through the simulation in a **deterministic mode** (timestamps fixed, no randomness), and compare simulated queue lengths and resource utilization at each event to log-derived values.
- Because start timestamps are missing, replay validation is partial: it validates the queue/calendar logic, not the service/waiting split.

### 6.3 Distributional validation

- Compare simulated vs. empirical distributions of:
  - Inter-arrival times (by hour, by day-of-week)
  - Activity cycle times (per case type)
  - Number of rework loops per case
  - Case-type share
- Tests: two-sample KS (with Bonferroni correction), Q–Q plots, summary statistics.
- Acceptance: p > 0.05 for KS on at least 80% of tested distributions.

### 6.4 Resource-utilization validation

- Compute resource-level busy fraction in the log (estimated from event gaps) and compare to simulation.
- Acceptance: simulated utilization within ±15% of log-derived estimates for non-system resources.

### 6.5 Bottleneck validation

- Rank bottlenecks in the log by median waiting (Planning 6d, Inspection 8d, Legal 7d, Env 8d, Public-notice 14d+).
- Rank bottlenecks in the baseline simulation.
- Acceptance: top-3 bottlenecks match in rank order.

### 6.6 Out-of-sample validation

- Train parameters on the **first 12 months** of the log; validate KPIs on the **last 6 months**.
- Acceptance: simulated KPIs (median cycle time, p90, SLA rate) within ±10% of held-out log KPIs for at least 80% of case types.

### 6.7 Stress-against-log validation

- Where the log contains natural stress periods (e.g., a particular peak month), compare the simulation's response to that period; calibrate seasonal multipliers if deviation is large.

We **do not** claim calibration success until all six tiers pass; we report partial passes explicitly.

## 7. Decision criteria

### 7.1 Metrics

| Metric | Definition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `median_cycle_time[type]` | 50th percentile sojourn per case type | Standard SLA proxy |
| `p90_cycle_time[type]` | 90th percentile sojourn per case type | Tail pressure |
| `p95_cycle_time[type]` | 95th percentile sojourn per case type | Worst-case responsiveness |
| `sla_compliance[type, target]` | Fraction of cases finishing within type-specific target | Operational KPI |
| `rework_rate[type]` | Fraction of cases with at least one rework event | Quality indicator |
| `backlog_open` | Open cases at end of period | Sustainability |
| `utilization[resource]` | Busy fraction per resource | Capacity signal |
| `fairness_district` | Coefficient of variation of median cycle time across districts | Equity |
| `fairness_architect` | Coefficient of variation of median cycle time across top-N architects | Equity |
| `robustness_score` | KPI stability under stress scenarios (S1–S4) | Policy resilience |

### 7.2 Default targets (initial thresholds; tune with stakeholders)

| Case type | Median target | p90 target | SLA window |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple renovation |  14 d |  30 d | 30 d |
| New residential |  45 d |  95 d | 90 d |
| Commercial |  65 d |  130 d | 120 d |
| Complex variance |  130 d |  260 d | 240 d |

### 7.3 Selection rules

- An intervention is **promising** if it improves `median_cycle_time[type]` for at least 3 of 4 case types and does not worsen `p90_cycle_time[type]` by more than 5%.
- An intervention is **acceptable** if it improves `sla_compliance` by  5 percentage points and `robustness_score` does not decrease by more than 10%.
- An intervention is **rejected** if it increases `fairness_district` (worsens equity) by more than 15% or if any case type's p95 worsens by more than 10%.
- Combined policies are subject to interaction checks (e.g., D2 + D4 should not mask a fairness regression in D3/D5 combinations).

### 7.4 Decision reporting

We report a **Pareto front** across (median cycle time, p90 cycle time, robustness, cost proxy) rather than a single "best" intervention; the municipality chooses based on weights it sets.

### Notes on the skeleton

- The PM4Py-based block (sections 1–4) produces **estimates**; the simulation engine (sections 5–7) **consumes** them. This separation prevents confusion between descriptive mining and predictive simulation.
- `pm4py.read_csv` is intentionally not used; PM4Py expects a DataFrame to be formatted first.
- `pm4py.parse_process_tree` is attempted on the simplified string; if it fails (operator precedence, named `tau` leaves, or whitespace quirks), we fall back to inductive discovery and log the substitution. The simulation's routing does not depend exclusively on the parsed tree — it depends on calibrated conditional probabilities from the variant statistics.
- Service/waiting-time separation is **not** claimed from `activity_duration_min`; that column is a distributional input only. The simulator's queue model is the authoritative source of waiting vs. service.
- Missing objection events are **never** treated as observed. They enter the simulation as exogenous district-specific random variables on top of the 14-day fixed minimum.
