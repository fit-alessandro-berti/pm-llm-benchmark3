## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

### Facts, interpretations, and assumptions

**Facts relied on**
- The effective dates and texts of GPIP-4, SOP DE-AAC-PROC-17, ECD-2, and the CFO email are treated as given.
- The case timestamps, amounts, supplier statuses, and role facts are treated as recorded facts, subject to the ON-44 evidential caveat.
- The ERP outage occurred 2026-07-14 08:30–13:00 CEST and may make ERP timestamps up to 12 minutes late relative to real actions.
- The Chief Risk Officer was unavailable and did not later ratify ECD-2.

**Interpretations**
- **Commit_to_Supplier** is interpreted as the moment the company became practically or legally bound to the supplier, not merely the moment an ERP entry was made. Supplier acknowledgments, telephone recordings, signed PDFs, and access logs may therefore fix the real time better than ERP timestamps.
- **Final business approver** includes any person or group whose approval is treated as the decisive business authorization to proceed, including Plant_Approve, Finance_Approve, or Incident Command authorization when used as an approval mechanism.
- **ECD-2 scope** is ambiguous. “Critical sterilization-line lots” is construed narrowly for exception purposes, but Incident Command’s contemporaneous labeling is given some evidential weight if supported by operational necessity.
- The **CFO email** is treated as a contemporaneous executive interpretation of Finance and operational approval controls, not as a controlled policy exception. It can inform how Finance controls were to be operated during ECD-2, but it does not clearly waive Compliance controls or GPIP-4 segregation-of-duties controls.

**Assumptions**
- No hidden Chief Risk Officer exception exists.
- An “unapproved” supplier is treated as “new” for GPIP-4 A3 only where the case says “new supplier” or where there is no evidence of an existing approved relationship. Where supplier status is ambiguous, that ambiguity is identified as missing evidence.
- Local Plant_Approve and Finance_Approve are interpreted as required **before supplier commitment**, because their control purpose is to prevent unreviewed commitments.
- The CFO email applies prospectively from its sending time, 2026-07-15 07:20 CEST, unless a document says otherwise. It is not treated as retroactively authorizing 2026-07-14 actions.

### Treatment of source authority

- **GPIP-4** is board-approved and global. It presumptively controls integrity, segregation of duties, Compliance clearance, Finance dual approval, and exception authority.
- **Aachen SOP** may impose stricter local controls and define operational evidence, but may not silently weaken GPIP-4.
- **ECD-2** is a time- and scope-limited incident directive. It can override ordinary local workflow and supplier-register timing within its window and scope, especially where B7 allows corporate incident directives to supersede ordinary local steps. It is not automatically a GPIP-4 A7 Chief Risk Officer exception.
- **CFO email** is weaker than controlled policy. It may explain how Finance and operational approvals should be handled during ECD-2, but it does not clearly override GPIP-4 integrity controls.

### Later-in-time and specificity

Later-in-time does not automatically prevail. ECD-2 is later than GPIP-4 and the SOP, but its authority is limited by its emergency scope and by GPIP-4’s reservation of formal exceptions to the Chief Risk Officer. Specificity matters: ECD-2’s specific requirements—two distinct Incident Command authorizations and recording within four hours—are treated as mandatory conditions for relying on the emergency overlay.

### Emergency necessity

Emergency necessity is not treated as a standalone normative exception. It can:
- activate an existing emergency procedure;
- support a justified exception where a valid overlay applies;
- mitigate severity;
- explain why contemporaneous records are imperfect.

But necessity does not automatically cure missing pre-commitment controls, especially where GPIP-4 says emergency waives competitive tendering only.

### Retrospective evidence and burden of proof

- Verbal Incident Command authorization is allowed under ECD-2 only if recorded within four hours. A late ERP entry is not conclusive where stronger contemporaneous evidence exists.
- Post-hoc approvals are mitigating evidence but are not automatically curative.
- The party asserting an exception bears the burden of proving:
  1. temporal applicability;
  2. scope applicability;
  3. proper authorization;
  4. required contemporaneous record;
  5. preservation of non-waivable controls.

Where a rule breach is clear and no valid exception is proven, the classification is **NONCOMPLIANT**. Where the decisive issue is unresolved because of genuine temporal, authoritative, or evidential ambiguity, the classification is **INDETERMINATE**. Where the deviation is covered by a valid, evidenced emergency overlay and core integrity controls remain intact, the classification is **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**.

**General distinction**
- A **policy breach** is a failure to satisfy a rule that applied at the time of commitment and for which no valid exception is proven.
- A **logging defect** is a failure to record an act timely or accurately, where the underlying act may still have been authorized.
- **Late evidence of a timely act** exists where contemporaneous evidence shows the act occurred within the required time, even if the system entry is late.
- **Inability to prove compliance** exists where the evidence is insufficient to establish either compliance or a valid exception.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Replacement Emergency Procurement Exception Clause**

1. **Authority and activation**  
   A procurement exception may be issued only by the Chief Risk Officer. If the Chief Risk Officer is unavailable, the COO may issue a temporary Incident Procurement Overlay jointly signed by the COO, Incident Commander, and CFO delegate. The overlay must be entered into the controlled policy repository and must be ratified by the Chief Risk Officer within 24 hours. If not ratified, it expires automatically.

2. **Scope definition**  
   “Critical sterilization-line lots” means goods or services required to prevent or remedy imminent safety harm or sterilization-line stoppage within 24 hours. Backup-line components are covered only if a documented risk assessment by the Plant Manager and Incident Commander shows a credible stoppage or contamination-control failure within a defined period and no reasonable alternative exists.

3. **Norm hierarchy**  
   GPIP-4 segregation of duties and Compliance clearance controls remain non-waivable except by written Chief Risk Officer exception. An emergency overlay may waive competitive tendering, PO-before-commitment sequencing, and the approved-supplier register requirement, but only where the conditions in this clause are met.

4. **New suppliers and Compliance**  
   For any new supplier or purchase above EUR 100,000, Compliance_Clearance is required before commitment. If pre-commitment clearance is operationally impossible, the purchase may proceed only with a written Chief Risk Officer exception or with documented on-call Compliance pre-approval followed by full Compliance clearance within four hours.

5. **Thresholds and approvals**  
   - Below EUR 25,000: Incident Command authorization and Plant/Shift approval.  
   - EUR 25,000 to EUR 49,999: Finance Controller or delegate approval.  
   - EUR 50,000 to EUR 150,000: two distinct Incident Command authorizations plus either an independent budget owner or Finance Controller approval. One authorizer may not be the requester or committer.  
   - Above EUR 150,000: all of the above plus Legal clearance and written notification to the Chief Risk Officer.

6. **Segregation of duties**  
   The requester or person committing the company may not be one of the required authorizers or final approvers. If unavoidable due to life-safety or immediate containment risk, a second independent reviewer must document the conflict and mitigating controls within four hours.

7. **Retrospective records**  
   Verbal emergency authorizations are valid only if recorded in the controlled system within four hours. During a system outage, a signed PDF, telephone recording, access log, or supplier acknowledgment created within four hours is acceptable temporary evidence and must be entered into the system as soon as recovered. ERP timestamps are rebuttable by stronger contemporaneous evidence.

8. **Expiry and anti-normalization safeguard**  
   An emergency overlay expires automatically at its stated end time. Verbal extensions are invalid. Any emergency purchase above EUR 50,000 or involving a new supplier is automatically flagged for post-incident audit within five business days. If emergency procurement is used repeatedly for the same category of spend, the overlay is suspended and escalated to the Chief Risk Officer.
