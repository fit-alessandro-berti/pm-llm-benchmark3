# 1. Data audit and estimands

An opening day is 12 hours, 07:00–19:00. Arrival and completion intensities below are counts divided by open hours; they are time averages, not instantaneous rates. WIP is the number in the system, including waiting and handling, averaged over opening-hours snapshots. Available FTE is a capacity input, not a measured completion rate or utilization. Service statistics are daily medians and 90th percentiles of active handling time, not mean service time or mean case flow time. Averages below weight days equally; because nominal opening durations are equal, the average count divided by 12 also equals the pooled count divided by open hours.

The 28 days have 2,519 arrivals and 2,409 completions. Their difference, +110, is an inventory change only if all entries and exits, including any outside opening hours, are captured and the system boundaries match. Opening and closing inventories are not supplied.

Threats to naive inference include:

- Cases carry over overnight and completions may be older than the day's arrivals. Completions reflect inherited backlog as well as available capacity; daily observations are dependent.
- Daily quantiles cannot be averaged into a pooled case-level median or p90, or used to recover the mean. Complex percentages refer to arrivals, while the population behind daily service quantiles is unspecified; same-day figures need not describe the same cases.
- All four hourly vectors match their corresponding daily arrival and completion totals, but conflict with the reported 08:00–09:59 percentages. In date order the vectors imply 16.8%, 18.1%, 9.6%, and 11.8%, versus 31%, 32%, 47%, and 22% in the daily table. This exceeds rounding error. Timestamp, bin, and aggregation definitions need reconciliation; neither source can be silently preferred.
- Mean FTE hides intraday coverage, productive time, skills, concurrency and outage availability. Neither utilization nor theoretical capacity is identifiable without mean work per case and productive capacity.
- Mix, timing, staffing, outages and backlog vary together. The complex increase could be coding, a true mix change, or both. The intervention started at an unknown time on April 29, so attribution is confounded.
- Snapshot WIP can miss within-interval peaks and its averaging and population boundaries may differ from the count boundaries. Weekday/weekend composition also changes pooled comparisons.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

A materially different approach segments by operating **state**: normal operation, outage, backlog building, and backlog draining, using weekday, mix, and staffing as covariates. For example, split April 15 into outage and other hours; treat April 16–17 and the end of April as candidate drain periods without assuming the same mechanism produced them. The April 21 vector itself alternates between build and drain within one day. Actual interval-level WIP changes would define state more reliably than arrivals minus completions, which requires complete flow accounting.

This approach emphasizes transitions and can guide intraday response. Its weaknesses are that only four hourly examples are given, within-bin ordering and opening backlog are unknown, and net flow can reflect noise or uncounted movements rather than a stable state. The primary day-level segmentation is reproducible for all 28 days and supports calendar comparisons, but averages away intraday state changes and combines some carry-over-affected days.

# 4. Pooled versus segmented statistics

- **Calendar:** The pooled rate is 7.50 arrivals and 7.17 completions per open hour, with mean WIP 29.9. The 20 weekdays average 9.00 and 8.60/hour and WIP 34.9; the eight weekend days average 3.74 and 3.60/hour and WIP 17.4. A plan based on pooled demand is poorly matched to either day type.
- **Mix and handling:** Early weekdays average 20.9% recorded complex arrivals, daily median handling 24–26 minutes, and daily p90 44–50. April 20–28 weekdays average 44.6% complex, median 34–36, and p90 78–88. Arrival rate rises from 8.35 to 9.50/hour while mean FTE remains about 5.93. This is consistent with increased workload pressure but does not establish that complexity caused longer handling or quantify mean workload. The unweighted all-day mean complex percentage, 32.9%, is not the pooled case-weighted share.
- **Shock and recovery:** April 15 has net +47, followed by −30 over April 16–17; combining the three days gives +17 and hides both the outage and the recovery. Recovery completions average 10.04/hour versus 8.30/hour on early weekdays; treating the former as normal capacity ignores accumulated work and extra staffing.
- **Intervention:** The seven high-mix weekdays before April 29 have net +94 and WIP 44.3, versus −13 and WIP 34 on April 30–May 1. Mean FTE rises from 5.93 to 6.75, but there are only two later weekdays, April 29 has unknown onset, and the counterfactual and backlog boundaries are unobserved. This supports a hypothesis of improved balance, not a causal effect estimate.

Composition can produce Simpson's-paradox-like interpretations: changing the weekday share can move a pooled rate even if within-day-type rates stay unchanged. Comparing early weekends with later weekdays also conflates calendar and period. No actual reversal is established here; demonstrating one requires an explicit outcome, weights, and consistent comparisons within common strata.

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

The Fano factor below is sample variance of the 12 hourly arrival counts divided by their mean; CV is sample standard deviation divided by mean. Both describe variation **across clock hours of a selected day**, not stochastic variance across replicated days after removing time-of-day effects.

| Day | Mean arrivals/hour | Maximum hourly arrivals | Fano factor | Hourly CV | Maximum cumulative arrivals minus completions relative to 07:00 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Apr 7 | 8.42 | 20 | 3.23 | 0.62 | +6 |
| Apr 15 | 8.75 | 22 | 4.14 | 0.69 | +55 |
| Apr 21 | 9.58 | 34 | 10.12 | 1.03 | +27 |
| Apr 25 | 4.25 | 8 | 1.29 | 0.55 | +8 |

April 21 has 34 arrivals in 10:00–11:00 and 24 in 13:00–14:00, despite averaging below 10/hour. Its cumulative net reaches +27 by 14:00 and ends +14. Actual WIP equals opening WIP plus cumulative net only under complete accounting. April 15's maximum +55 precedes closing net +47. Hourly bins conceal within-hour order and shorter peaks, and the conflicting daily peak field cannot corroborate these shapes.

Fano factors above one can result from a deterministic changing hourly intensity, a random burst process, or both; these selected days do not establish a homogeneous Poisson process or measure stochastic overdispersion within a regime. Compare many aligned days within strata and model their time-of-day means to distinguish these effects.

The 28-day WIP series has descriptive calendar-day lag-one correlation about 0.61 and lag-seven correlation about 0.63, from 27 and 21 pairs respectively. These combine carry-over, weekday/weekend seasonality, trend, and shocks; they do not establish a stationary autoregressive process. Net counts indicate build-up during April 20–28, while weekday WIP rises and weekend WIP falls. A two-day-before versus two-day-after standardized-jump heuristic on complex percentage, median service, and WIP selects a cut before April 20. The weekend contrast, coding issue, chosen features and tiny sample make this a candidate, not a confirmed change point. The outage and intervention are separate externally suggested boundaries. Four selected vectors cannot establish a full arrival or service distribution, a causal mechanism, or a queueing model.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

Little's Law relates time-average in-system WIP to throughput times mean **total** flow time under compatible boundaries and stable long-run conditions. A finite-window ratio is not automatically the mean flow time of cases completed in that window: cases crossing either boundary cause censoring and residual-work effects. Here WIP is sampled only while open and throughput is per open hour, so a ratio is in *open-hours in system*, not calendar elapsed hours. This interpretation additionally assumes consistent snapshot coverage, matching case boundaries, no relevant uncounted entries or exits outside counted windows, and appropriate open-clock accounting of overnight cases. These conditions are not fully verified.

| Candidate window / regime | Boundary stability | Throughput proxy | WIP comparability | Carry-over risk | Stationarity assessment | Implied flow-time calculation | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Apr 6–14, contiguous opening periods including weekend | Recorded net +5 over nine days; opening and closing inventories unknown | 776 completions / 108 open hours = 7.19/hour; observed departures are slightly below arrivals | Mean daily snapshot WIP 18.67, assuming consistent sampling and matching system boundaries | Present at both ends and overnight; balance does not establish similar boundary age distributions | Weekday/weekend cycle, not homogeneous; a repeating-cycle approximation is unverified | Diagnostic 18.67 / 7.19 = **2.60 open hours**, not a measured case mean or calendar duration | approximate only |
| Apr 15 outage | Recorded net +47; inventory changing | 58 / 12 = 4.83/hour, affected by outage | Daily WIP 47 hides within-day trajectory | High and changing | Clearly nonstationary | None | not defensible |
| Apr 16–17 recovery | Recorded net −30; drawdown | 241 / 24 = 10.04/hour, unusually high | WIP 43.5 averages changing backlog | High; completions may be inherited cases | Transient | None | not defensible |
| Apr 20–28 high-mix period, including weekend | Recorded net +106; strong accumulation | Completions lag arrivals; neither establishes steady throughput | WIP mixes weekday and weekend levels | High and rising under complete accounting | Nonstationary with calendar variation | None | not defensible |
| Apr 30–May 3, after transition | Recorded net −13 over four days | Completions include inherited cases | WIP mixes weekdays and weekends | High | Short, mixed recovery period | None | not defensible |
| All 28 days | Recorded net +110; actual boundary inventories unknown | 2,409 / 336 = 7.17/hour | Pooled WIP combines distinct states | High | Regime shifts and transient shocks | None | not defensible |

The early-window diagnostic is assumption-dependent. It does not estimate waiting time: subtracting a daily median handling time would mix incompatible statistics and populations. Exact finite-window accounting requires case entry and exit times and the time each boundary-crossing case spends inside the window.

# 7. Operational implications

Forecast and schedule staff by weekday, time of day, skills and validated workload, with flexibility for peaks. Use the hourly vectors as examples of possible shapes only after resolving the conflicting peak field. Reserve capacity for complex or multi-step work if event-level validation confirms the mix shift. Estimate mean productive work and its variability by case type, availability, breaks and routing before converting demand to FTE.

Monitor opening and closing backlog, its age and priority, and arrivals, completions and active capacity in short intervals. After a disruption, set a temporary drawdown target using observed backlog and incoming demand, then reassess whether elevated completions persist after inherited work clears. Test peak smoothing through appointment slots or scheduled capacity where feasible, and check whether demand is displaced to another hour. Staffing all days for the pooled 7.50 arrivals per open hour could look sufficient in aggregate yet leave high-mix weekdays and bursts under-covered while misallocating weekend capacity; evaluate it against segmented service levels and backlog trajectories.

Collect case-level arrival, start, pause, resume, completion and exit timestamps; case and activity identifiers; case-type definitions and coding history; hourly staff availability and skills; actual intervention onset; outage start and end; and queue snapshots with explicit populations and sampling rules. Reconcile the peak percentages with the hourly bins and verify overnight entries and exits. With more weeks, compare aligned days and comparable pre/post strata, accounting for serial dependence and external changes before attributing effects to staffing or mix.

The script reproduces the descriptive figures and a deliberately simple change-point candidate. Its chosen finite-window ratio is a diagnostic, not an established case-level mean flow time. It requires pandas and NumPy.

d['weekend'] = d['day'].isin(['Sat', 'Sun'])

def regime(row):
    t = row['date']
    if t == pd.Timestamp('2026-04-15'):
        return 'outage'
    if t in [pd.Timestamp('2026-04-16'), pd.Timestamp('2026-04-17')]:
        return 'recovery weekdays'
    if t <= pd.Timestamp('2026-04-19'):
        return 'early weekend' if row['weekend'] else 'early weekdays'
    if t <= pd.Timestamp('2026-04-28'):
        return 'high-mix weekend' if row['weekend'] else 'high-mix weekdays'
    if t == pd.Timestamp('2026-04-29'):
        return 'intervention transition'
    return 'later weekend' if row['weekend'] else 'later weekdays'

d['regime'] = d.apply(regime, axis=1)
d['net'] = d['arrivals'] - d['completed']

def summarize(g):
    return pd.Series({'days': len(g), 'arr/h': g.arrivals.mean()/12,
