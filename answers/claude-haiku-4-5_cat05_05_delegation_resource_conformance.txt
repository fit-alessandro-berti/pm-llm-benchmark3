## 2. Rule Interpretation

### RR1 – Drafter/Approver Separation
- **Formalization**: For each case *c*, let *Drafter(c)* = human who performs `Draft_Change_Request` and *Approver(c)* = human who performs `Approve_Change`. The rule requires *Drafter(c)*  *Approver(c)*.
- **Aliases**: Before SoD check, normalize aliases to canonical human identity.
- **Application**: Cases where the same human (after normalization) appears in both roles violate RR1.

### RR2 – Implementer/Verifier Separation
- **Formalization**: For each case *c*, let *Implementer(c)* = human who performs `Implement_Change` and *Verifier(c)* = human who performs `Verify_Implementation`. The rule requires *Implementer(c)*  *Verifier(c)*.
- **Service accounts**: If `Verify_Implementation` is performed by svc_qms, attribute it to the session-identified human. If attribution is ambiguous or unattributable, flag as a data/identity problem rather than a direct violation.

### RR3 – High-Risk Approval Authority
- **Formalization**: For each case *c* with *risk = HIGH*, `Approve_Change` must be performed by:
  - The QM (Asha Rao), **or**
  - A valid QM delegate *D* under delegation *del* such that:
    1. *del.effective_start*  *event_time*  *del.effective_end*
    2. *del.scope* covers the change scope (e.g., "production-equipment"  case scope)
    3. Principal (Asha) is **absent or formally unavailable** at *event_time*, **or** the delegation explicitly permits the principal to also act
    4. *del* contains no prohibited subdelegation clause that was violated
- **Absence assessment**: "Recorded on leave" + no system logon  presumptively absent. VPN session during stated absence  credential-sharing risk; security cannot exclude it.
- **Scope matching**: Delegation scope must encompass the change scope. A production-equipment delegation does not cover training-document changes.

### RR4 – Emergency Substitution
- **Formalization**: If *risk = HIGH* **and** *emergency = true*, then:
  - `Approve_Change` may be performed by IC (Diego Martins) or valid IC delegate *D* (under conditions as RR3)
  - **Exception condition**: This is authorized only if a QM or valid QM delegate performs `Retrospective_QM_Review` within **two business days**.
  - **Business days**: Monday–Friday, excluding holidays. Weekend days (Saturday, Sunday) and recognized holidays do not count.
  - **Credibility**: Emergency flag and safety rationale must be credible (e.g., operational incident, system failure, imminent safety risk).
  
- **Business-day calculation for CHG-505 and CHG-510**:
  - CHG-505: `Approve_Change` 2026-08-11 (Monday); `Retrospective_QM_Review` 2026-08-14 (Thursday, Ben Koch).
    - Day 1 (Tue 2026-08-12), Day 2 (Wed 2026-08-13), reviewed Thu 2026-08-14  **within 2 business days**.
  - CHG-510: `Approve_Change` 2026-08-11 (Monday by Clara); `Retrospective_QM_Review` 2026-08-12 (Tuesday, Ben Koch).
    - Day 1 (Wed 2026-08-12)  **within 2 business days**.
  - However, CHG-510 approval by Clara (Process Engineer, not QM or valid IC delegate) is **unauthorized** regardless of retrospective review.

### RR5 – Identity Evidence
- **Service account mapping**: A service-account action is valid only if session logs, badge records, FIDO tokens, or cryptographic binding reliably attribute the action to an authorized human.
- **Strength hierarchy**: FIDO token > badge + session + activity window > badge only > workstation assignment > IP address alone.
- **Shared workstations**: If multiple authorized users can access a terminal, ambiguous session evidence cannot reliably prove segregation of duties.
- **Normalization**: Before SoD checks, replace all aliases of the same human with a canonical identity.

### RR6 – Suspicion versus Proof
- **Indicators (not proof)**:
  - Repeated resource pairs over time.
  - Unusual reciprocal approvals.
  - Shared workstations or co-location.
  - Concentrated handovers.
- **Proof requires**:
  - Direct evidence of collusion (e.g., communications, shared credentials, false reconciliation).
  - Elimination of benign alternative explanations (workload distribution, subject-matter expertise, absence of rotation policy).
- **Default**: Pattern flagged as SUSPICIOUS_PATTERN pending investigation; not automatically declared UNAUTHORIZED.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

### 4.1 Ben Koch & Clara Stein Repeated-Pair Pattern

**Indicators**:
- In prior 60 days, Clara drafted and Ben approved 18 of 23 HIGH-risk production changes (78% frequency).
- Both assigned to same production cell; no formal rotation policy.
- Four LOW-risk training changes show reciprocal approval (Ben drafts, Clara approves).
- No direct financial benefit known.

**Benign Explanations**:
1. **Subject-matter expertise allocation**: Clara is the production subject-matter expert (Process Engineer); Ben is the designated approver for production changes (Quality Engineer with QM delegation). High frequency reflects workload, not collusion.
2. **Absence of alternative approvers**: If Asha (QM) is frequently absent or burdened, Ben may be the de facto approver by delegation. Clara works with Ben systematically because he is available.
3. **Reciprocal LOW-risk approvals**: Training updates may be co-authored or related; they swap roles because the approver must differ by rule, but they collaborate on the same documents (common in small teams).
4. **No rotation policy**: Many organizations do not mandate approver rotation if segregation of duties is maintained and the approver is authorized. Absence of a policy does not prove collusion.

**Adverse Explanations**:
1. **Deliberate circumvention**: Clara and Ben coordinate to bypass QM approval by using Ben's delegation when Asha is away; Ben reciprocates by approving training documents Clara drafts, creating mutual obligation.
2. **Pre-coordinated rubberstamping**: Ben approves Clara's drafts without independent review, and Clara approves Ben's training documents without scrutiny, to accelerate cycle time.
3. **Shared credential misuse**: Ben and Clara share credentials or workstations; high frequency reflects account reuse, not two people working separately.

**Discriminating Evidence**:
- **Audit trail review**: Do approval comments or review notes show independent scrutiny (e.g., Ben's questions, revision requests)? Rubberstamping would show minimal commentary.
- **Anomaly detection**: Are Ben's approvals of Clara's drafts faster than Ben's approvals of drafts by other engineers? High speed suggests reduced review depth.
- **Workload analysis**: How many changes did Asha approve in the same period? If very low, it suggests delegation was necessary, not collusive.
- **Communication logs**: Email or chat history between Clara and Ben around approval times. Collusion would show pre-coordination; legitimate work would show technical discussion.
- **Multi-factor authentication**: Do Ben's approvals correlate with his workstation and FIDO token usage, or do they appear from shared terminals without re-auth? Collusion might show inconsistent device profiles.
- **Absence of other approvers**: Check if other Quality Engineers or acting QMs were available but bypassed.

**Current Assessment**: **SUSPICIOUS_PATTERN** pending investigation. Frequency is statistically notable but explainable by organizational structure and delegation scope. No direct evidence of collusion or fraud.

### 4.2 Ben Koch Account Misuse on 2026-08-13 13:00 (CHG-506)

**Indicators**:
- Ben certified sick leave 2026-08-13 12:00 onward.
- CHG-506 approval at 2026-08-13 13:00 (1 hour after leave began).
- Ben denies performing the action.
- Office workstation IP address (not home VPN or mobile).
- No multi-factor re-authentication log documented in case file.

**Benign Explanations**:
1. **Clock skew or backdating**: Approval timestamp may be incorrect (system clock skew, async logging, backdated by approver minutes before leaving office). Ben approved just before going on leave.
2. **Early departure confusion**: Ben may have worked part of the shift before recognizing illness and calling in sick. Approval at 13:00 was performed before submitting sick notice (submitted afterward). HR and QMS timestamps may not align.
3. **Workstation reuse**: Ben's colleague or another team member accessed the same workstation using Ben's cached login (Windows logon cache, single sign-on) without re-authentication. Common in shared-terminal environments.
4. **Technical error**: QMS incorrectly recorded Ben as approver when approval was queued or delegated.

**Adverse Explanations**:
1. **Credential compromise**: Ben's account was compromised (password theft, phishing) or shared with a colleague. An unauthorized user approved CHG-506 under Ben's identity.
2. **Collusion with Clara**: Clara and Ben pre-arranged for approval: Ben approves CHG-506 just before going sick, fulfilling his part of the pattern; Clara benefits from the approval.
3. **Malicious backdating**: A supervisor or administrator with QMS access retroactively inserted the approval using Ben's account to cover another approver's error.
4. **Third-party account use**: A team member (e.g., another Quality Engineer) used Ben's terminal login without consent, performing the approval under Ben's cached session.

**Discriminating Evidence**:
- **Multi-factor authentication logs**: If Ben entered a FIDO token or TOTP at 2026-08-13 13:00, he was likely present or actively authenticated. If MFA logs are absent, credential was likely cached/delegated.
- **Device location**: GPS or VPN gateway egress point at 2026-08-13 13:00. If office building network, consistent with either Ben onsite or terminal sharing. If home network, contradicts Ben's account (he says he was home sick). If completely different location, suggests credential misuse.
- **Video surveillance**: CCTV of workstation area at 2026-08-13 12:55–13:05. Shows who physically accessed the terminal.

    Count business days (Mon–Fri) between start_date and end_date (exclusive of start, inclusive of end).

    """
    business_days = 0
    current = start_date + timedelta(days=1)
    while current <= end_date:
        # Monday = 0, Sunday = 6
        if current.weekday() < 5:  # Mon–Fri
            business_days += 1
        current += timedelta(days=1)
    return business_days

                    'from_resource': current_resource,
                    'to_activity': next_activity,
                    'to_resource': next_resource,
                })
    
    handover_df = pd.DataFrame(handover_pairs)
    return handover_df
